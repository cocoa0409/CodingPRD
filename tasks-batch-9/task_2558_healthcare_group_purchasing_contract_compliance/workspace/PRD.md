# Healthcare Group Purchasing & Contract Compliance API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A group purchasing organization earns its keep by converting the aggregated buying power of many health systems into a tier ladder that individual hospitals could never negotiate alone. That promise only holds if the paperwork holds: a member has to elect a tier, sign a letter of participation, map its own item-master numbers onto the supplier's catalog, and then actually buy on contract. When any link in that chain slips — an unsigned letter, a missing cross-reference, a surgeon who keeps ordering a competing stent — the member drifts into off-contract leakage, the tier it was promised is no longer the tier it earned, and the administrative fee the GPO collects from the supplier is computed on spend that never should have counted.

This document specifies the API that runs that chain end to end for a Vizient-style purchasing organization. It covers nine coupled workflows: portfolio construction with an embedded tier ladder and an embedded fee-holdback ladder; clinical evidence review that gates a category conversion; member registration and item-master stewardship; commitment election and the letter-of-participation document cycle; item cross-referencing between the supplier catalog and the member item master; price activation on two tracks (a GPO-driven tier price and a member-requested local exception); distributor sales-tracing file intake with per-line classification; compliance measurement, tier requalification and the price cascade it fires; and administrative fee accrual, collection, member-share distribution and reversal.

Two design commitments run through everything that follows. First, **money is exact**: every amount is an integer number of cents, every rate is expressed in basis points, and the point at which a division is rounded is specified rather than left to the implementer — because rounding a fee per purchase line and rounding it on the aggregate produce different numbers, and only one of them is the contracted number. Second, **measurement is auditable**: the same compliance measurement row carries both a preliminary and a final result, computed over deliberately different eligible-file sets, so that a member can see exactly why the number that retiered it differs from the number it was shown mid-period. Every path in this API lives under the `/gpo` prefix.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contract-level rules that hold for every endpoint in the API: authentication, the response envelope, pagination, audit fields, monetary precision, concurrency, cascade discipline, and the order in which validations fire.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request carries two mandatory HTTP headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting user, and `X-User-Role` declares the operational capacity in which that user is acting. Six roles exist, spelled exactly as follows: `ContractManager`, `ClinicalReviewer`, `ComplianceAnalyst`, `MemberMaterialsManager`, `DistributorAgent`, and `FinanceAdministrator`. A request whose `X-User-ID` header is missing or empty is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401); the single exception is the logical-clock endpoint, which is a test-driving capability and requires no user identity. A request whose `X-User-Role` header is missing, is not one of the six role names above, or names a role that the requested operation does not admit is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

Five of the six roles are operator roles whose identity is opaque. When `X-User-Role` is `ContractManager`, `ClinicalReviewer`, `ComplianceAnalyst`, `DistributorAgent`, or `FinanceAdministrator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a workflow demands an audit trail — as `submitted_by_user_id` and `endorsed_by_user_id` on a clinical evidence review, as `approved_by_user_id` on a cross-reference, and as `endorsed_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` on a price activation — and it compares two such recorded identifiers against each other to enforce segregation of duties, but it never compares an opaque identifier to any entity's `id`.

`MemberMaterialsManager` is different: it is entity-backed. When `X-User-Role` is `MemberMaterialsManager`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Member`, and every ownership check on a resource that references a `Member` compares that resource's member foreign key to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a `MemberMaterialsManager` may only elect a commitment whose `member_id` equals its own `X-User-ID`, may only propose a cross-reference for its own `member_id`, may only sign the letter of participation of a commitment whose `member_id` equals its `X-User-ID`, may only request a local pricing exception on a commitment it owns, and may only read the leakage report of a commitment it owns. Any of these performed against another member's resource is rejected with `ERR_MEMBER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). Replacing a member's item master is scoped the same way, through the member identifier carried in the path.

Two segregation-of-duties gates are keyed on `X-User-ID` rather than on role. The `ClinicalReviewer` who submitted an evidence dossier may not be the `ClinicalReviewer` who endorses it, and the `ContractManager` who endorsed a local pricing exception may not be the `ContractManager` who approves it. Either violation returns `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 409). Read access is broad — all six roles may read every collection and every single resource — with one deliberate exception: the leakage report is a member-facing compliance artifact readable only by `ComplianceAnalyst`, `ContractManager`, and `MemberMaterialsManager`, and a `ClinicalReviewer`, `DistributorAgent`, or `FinanceAdministrator` asking for it receives `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints are paginated through two query parameters. `limit` sets the maximum number of rows returned; it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1, and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` sets the number of rows to skip and defaults to 0. Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, list results are ordered by the resource's creation timestamp, `created_at`, ascending — oldest first, newest last.

Every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` indicates whether rows remain beyond the current page.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Fields and the Logical Clock

Every resource in the system carries a server-minted `id` formatted as a UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp rendered as an ISO 8601 UTC string with exactly three fractional digits (for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`). Ten of the eleven resource types — contracts, contract items, members, clinical evidence reviews, commitments, item cross-references, price activations, distributor files, compliance measurements and administrative fee distributions — additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by one on every accepted mutation of that row, including mutations the server performs on its own initiative as part of a cascade.

Purchase lines are the exception. A purchase line is an append-only record created only as part of receiving a distributor file, never updated by any client request, and it therefore carries only `id` and `created_at`: it has no `updated_at` field and no `version` field at all. Its `classification`, `contract_units` and `matched_vendor_item_number` are written by the load cascade and cleared by the reversal compensation, but those writes do not manufacture a version counter that clients could pin against.

Time in this system is a **logical clock**, not wall-clock time. A test-driving capability sets the clock to an exact instant supplied as `current_utc` and echoes the value it stored back as `current_utc`; all six roles may invoke it, and it modifies no entity. Before that capability is called for the first time, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real wall-clock time. Every date-window comparison the server performs, the `loaded_at` stamp written when a distributor file is loaded, and the `effective_from` of any replacement price activation minted by a cascade all read this clock and nothing else.

### 2.5. Monetary Amounts, Basis Points and Rounding Discipline

All money in this API is an integer count of cents. There are no fractional cents anywhere in a stored field, a request body, or a response body. All rates are expressed in **basis points**, where one basis point is one hundredth of one percent, so a rate of 800 basis points is 8.00 percent and the denominator that converts basis points into a multiplier is 10000. Percentages that are stored rather than configured — a compliance percentage, a leakage percentage, an evidence score — carry exactly two decimal places.

Every division in this specification is performed in exact decimal arithmetic and rounded **half up** — a value exactly at the midpoint rounds away from zero, so 8508.5 cents becomes 8509 cents and 7.0050 becomes 7.01. Binary floating-point arithmetic is not an acceptable substitute, because it rounds 7.0050 down to 7.00 and thereby produces the wrong conversion recommendation. Amounts in cents are rounded half up to zero decimal places; percentages and evidence scores are rounded half up to two decimal places.

Where a rounding happens matters as much as how it rounds, and this specification pins it in every calculation. Three rules recur and must be read as absolute. The tier price of a catalog item is rounded once, immediately after the discount is applied, and every subsequent comparison — the local-exception ceiling, the contractual floor clamp — operates on whole cents that have already been rounded, so a discount is never rounded twice and a floor is never crossed by a rounding artifact. The administrative fee is rounded **once per purchase line** and the rounded line fees are then added as exact integers; rounding the aggregate instead is a different number and is wrong. The fee waterfall takes exactly two roundings, in a fixed order — the holdback off the gross pool first, then the member pool off the net pool — and one truncation, the per-member floor allocation, described in full in §3.9.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency on Parent Records

Two write operations require the client to declare which version of a parent record it was looking at when it composed the request. Both are cases where nothing in the state machine forbids the second write — the operation is legitimate on its own terms — but the second writer would be reasoning from a snapshot the first writer has already invalidated.

Opening a compliance measurement requires `expected_commitment_version` in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the parent `Commitment`. This field is mandatory, not optional. If it does not equal the commitment's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_COMMITMENT_VERSION` and HTTP status 412. Two measurements over different periods on the same commitment are both perfectly legal product operations, so no state precondition stops the second one; the hazard is that the second writer derived its period boundaries and its expected tier from a commitment snapshot that the first measurement has already moved, since creating a measurement itself increments `Commitment.version`, and finalizing one later rewrites `current_tier_code` and `requalification_round`. A stale declaration is refused rather than silently measured against a commitment the writer never saw. Every accepted measurement create increments the parent commitment's `version` by one.

Repricing a catalog item requires `expected_contract_version` in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the parent `Contract`. It too is mandatory. If it does not equal the contract's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CONTRACT_VERSION` and HTTP status 412. Successive base-price edits on the same item are each legitimate — a later edit supersedes an earlier one — so no state precondition rejects the second write; but the tier ladder and the floor semantics that give a base price meaning live on the contract, and every accepted edit advances `Contract.version`. A writer whose declared version is behind is repricing against a ladder that has already changed, so the write is refused.

No other endpoint in this API accepts a version field. In particular, activating a price, signing a letter of participation, loading a distributor file and distributing a fee pool take no expected-version input.

### 2.7. Cascade Discipline and Derived Counters

Several fields in this system are **derived**: the server recomputes them from other rows rather than accepting them from a client. Contracts carry `evidence_endorsed`, true exactly when at least one clinical evidence review naming that contract has reached `ENDORSED` status, recomputed on every endorse and every reject of an evidence review; and `active_commitment_count`, the number of commitments on that contract whose election status is `ACTIVE` or `REQUAL_REVIEW`, recomputed when a letter of participation is countersigned, when a letter is voided, and when a commitment is terminated. Catalog items carry `active_activation_count`, the number of price activations naming that item whose status is `ACTIVE`, counted across all commitments.

Two discipline rules govern these recomputations. First, **every recompute is a mutation of the row it lands on**: it refreshes that row's `updated_at` and increments that row's `version` by one, even though no client asked for a change to that row. Second, **a recompute fires at most once per triggering request per affected row**. When a single request supersedes four price activations belonging to two catalog items, each of those two items has `active_activation_count` recomputed exactly once and its `version` incremented by exactly one — not once per superseded activation.

Cascades in this system are **transitive rather than fan-out**, and the ordering is load-bearing because each hop is caused by the writes of the hop before it. Loading a distributor file classifies lines, and that classification write is what causes the member's year-to-date ledgers to move; the ledger write is what causes the leakage percentage to be recomputed; the recomputed percentage crossing the contract's threshold is what raises the commitment's leakage flag; and the flag rising from false to true is what puts the member on the watchlist. Finalizing a compliance measurement recomputes the measurement, and that recompute is what retiers the commitment; the retier — and only a retier that actually changed the tier — is what supersedes the commitment's live tier prices; each superseded tier price is what drags the local exception for the same catalog item with it; and those status writes together are what trigger the per-item counter recompute. §3.7 and §3.8 spell out both cascades hop by hop.

### 2.8. Path Resolution, Filtered Discovery and Relationship Shape

Identifiers appearing in a URL path are resolved **left to right**, and the first identifier that resolves to nothing terminates the request with the 404 that names that resource type: `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND` for an unknown `contract_id`, `ERR_CONTRACT_ITEM_NOT_FOUND` for `contract_item_id`, `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND` for `member_id`, `ERR_COMMITMENT_NOT_FOUND` for `commitment_id`, `ERR_XREF_NOT_FOUND` for `xref_id`, `ERR_ACTIVATION_NOT_FOUND` for `activation_id`, `ERR_FILE_NOT_FOUND` for `file_id`, `ERR_MEASUREMENT_NOT_FOUND` for `measurement_id`, `ERR_DISTRIBUTION_NOT_FOUND` for `distribution_id`, `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_FOUND` for `review_id`, and `ERR_PURCHASE_LINE_NOT_FOUND` for `purchase_line_id`. When several path identifiers appear, they are checked in that same order — the contract before the item it contains, for example — so a request naming a nonexistent contract and a nonexistent item reports the contract.

The resource graph is deliberately **flat**. Only one route in the whole API nests a child under its parent, namely adding an item to a contract's catalog. Every other write targets a top-level collection and names its parents inside the request body. Consequently, children are discovered by **filtering a top-level list**, not by reading a nested path: catalog items and evidence reviews and fee distributions are filtered by `contract_id`, compliance measurements by `commitment_id`, purchase lines by `distributor_file_id`, commitments by `member_id` and `contract_id`, cross-references by `member_id` and `contract_item_id`, distributor files by `member_id` and `contract_id`, and price activations by `commitment_id` and `contract_item_id`. Price activations may additionally be filtered by either end of a supersede link, `superseded_by_activation_id` or `replaces_activation_id`, which is the only discovery channel for replacement activations the server mints on its own during a requalification cascade.

Foreign keys named in a request body are resolved after the path identifiers, in the order the body declares them — `contract_id`, then `member_id`, then `contract_item_id`, then `commitment_id` — and an unresolvable one returns the same 404 error identifier as its path counterpart.

### 2.9. Validation Precedence

When a request violates more than one rule, exactly one error is returned: the one belonging to the **lowest-numbered rung** below. Rules are evaluated strictly in ascending order and evaluation stops at the first violation, so a later rung's error can never mask an earlier rung's error. The rungs are:

1. **Authentication** — `X-User-ID` present and non-empty on every request except `POST /gpo/_clock/set_current_utc`; otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Role admission** — `X-User-Role` present, one of the six declared roles, and permitted for the requested operation; otherwise `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Member scope** — for the entity-backed `MemberMaterialsManager`, `X-User-ID` must equal the member the operation touches: the body `member_id` on a create, or the `member_id` reachable from the path resource otherwise. Otherwise `ERR_MEMBER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (403). This rung guards exactly five operations: `POST /gpo/commitments`, `POST /gpo/item-cross-references`, `POST /gpo/price-activations` when `activation_type` is `LOCAL_EXCEPTION`, `POST /gpo/commitments/{commitment_id}/lop/sign`, and `GET /gpo/commitments/{commitment_id}/leakage-report`.
4. **Path existence** — every identifier in the path resolves, checked left to right in the order `contract_id`, `contract_item_id`, `member_id`, `commitment_id`, `xref_id`, `activation_id`, `file_id`, `measurement_id`, `distribution_id`, `review_id`, `purchase_line_id`; otherwise the matching 404 from §2.8. Applies to every path-parameterised endpoint.
5. **Body foreign-key existence** — every foreign key in the body resolves, checked in body-declaration order `contract_id`, `member_id`, `contract_item_id`, `commitment_id`; otherwise the matching 404. Applies to every create that carries foreign keys.
6. **Request shape** — the body parses, every required field is present, every enum value is a declared member of its enumeration, every integer sits inside its declared range, every date and timestamp is ISO 8601, and every decimal carries no more places than its declared scale; otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Applies to every endpoint. Two ladder-shape errors are evaluated with this rung and also carry HTTP 400: `ERR_INVALID_TIER_LADDER` for a malformed tier ladder and `ERR_INVALID_HOLDBACK_LADDER` for a malformed holdback ladder, both defined in §3.1.
7. **Parent contract state** — the parent contract is in the state the operation requires; otherwise `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE` (409). An `ACTIVE` contract is required to elect a commitment, to propose a cross-reference, to create a price activation, to activate a price activation, to receive a distributor file, and to accrue an administrative fee — that is, for `POST /gpo/commitments`, `POST /gpo/item-cross-references`, `POST /gpo/price-activations`, `POST /gpo/price-activations/{activation_id}/activate`, `POST /gpo/distributor-files`, and `POST /gpo/admin-fee-distributions`. For `PATCH /gpo/contracts/{contract_id}/tiers` the contract must be either `DRAFT` (ordinary setup) or `ACTIVE` (a category conversion); a `SUSPENDED` contract rejects the edit with the same error. Two sibling state errors sit on this rung: `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_ACTIVE` (409) when a `SUSPENDED` member is named by a commitment election or a distributor file, and `ERR_CONTRACT_ITEM_NOT_ACTIVE` (409) when a `DELISTED` catalog item is named by a cross-reference proposal.
8. **Path resource state** — the path resource is in a state from which the requested transition exists. This rung covers all eight state dimensions and returns whichever identifier matches: `ERR_INVALID_ACTIVATION_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_FILE_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_MEASUREMENT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_DISTRIBUTION_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_REVIEW_STATE`, `ERR_XREF_NOT_PROPOSED`, `ERR_COMMITMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`, `ERR_INVALID_LOP_STATE`, or `ERR_COMMITMENT_NOT_ELECTED` — all with HTTP 409. Applies to every action endpoint.
9. **Optimistic concurrency** — the declared `expected_commitment_version` or `expected_contract_version` equals the parent's current `version`; otherwise `ERR_STALE_COMMITMENT_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_CONTRACT_VERSION`, both HTTP 412. Applies to exactly two endpoints: `POST /gpo/compliance-measurements` and `PATCH /gpo/contract-items/{contract_item_id}/base-price`.
10. **Clinical evidence gate** — an item flagged as requiring evidence may only be activated when its contract carries an evidence review in `ENDORSED` status whose recommendation is `CONVERT`, and converting an `ACTIVE` contract's tier ladder requires the same; otherwise `ERR_CLINICAL_REVIEW_NOT_ENDORSED` (409). Applies to `POST /gpo/price-activations/{activation_id}/activate` and `PATCH /gpo/contracts/{contract_id}/tiers`.
11. **Segregation of duties** — the endorser of an evidence review is not its submitter, and the approver of a local pricing exception is not its endorser; otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (409). Applies to `POST /gpo/clinical-evidence-reviews/{review_id}/endorse` and `POST /gpo/price-activations/{activation_id}/approve`.
12. **Evidence thresholds and revision budget** — a review may not be endorsed while its weighted score is below 7.00, and may not be sent back for revision more than twice; the errors are `ERR_EVIDENCE_SCORE_BELOW_THRESHOLD` and `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED`, both 409. Applies to `POST /gpo/clinical-evidence-reviews/{review_id}/endorse` and `POST /gpo/clinical-evidence-reviews/{review_id}/request-revision`.
13. **Referential coherence** — the referenced rows belong together: a catalog item named by an activation belongs to the commitment's contract (`ERR_ITEM_CONTRACT_MISMATCH`), a member item number named by a cross-reference exists in that member's item master (`ERR_MEMBER_ITEM_NOT_IN_MASTER`), and a price may only be activated once an `APPROVED` cross-reference links the member to the item (`ERR_XREF_NOT_APPROVED`); all 409. Applies to `POST /gpo/price-activations`, `POST /gpo/item-cross-references`, and `POST /gpo/price-activations/{activation_id}/activate`.
14. **Requalification readiness** — a measurement may only be finalized while its commitment's election status is `REQUAL_REVIEW` (`ERR_COMMITMENT_NOT_IN_REQUAL_REVIEW`), and a local exception may be neither endorsed nor approved while its commitment is in `REQUAL_REVIEW` (`ERR_REQUALIFICATION_IN_PROGRESS`); both 409. Applies to `POST /gpo/compliance-measurements/{measurement_id}/finalize`, `POST /gpo/price-activations/{activation_id}/endorse`, and `POST /gpo/price-activations/{activation_id}/approve`.
15. **Uniqueness** — one non-terminated commitment per member-and-contract pair (`ERR_DUPLICATE_COMMITMENT`); one `APPROVED` cross-reference per member-and-catalog-item pair and per member-and-member-item-number pair (`ERR_DUPLICATE_XREF`); one non-superseded compliance measurement per commitment-and-period triple (`ERR_DUPLICATE_MEASUREMENT_PERIOD`); one non-reversed fee distribution per contract-and-period triple (`ERR_DUPLICATE_DISTRIBUTION_PERIOD`); and one occurrence of each tier code inside a submitted tier ladder (`ERR_DUPLICATE_TIER_CODE`); all 409. Applies to `POST /gpo/commitments`, `POST /gpo/item-cross-references`, `POST /gpo/item-cross-references/{xref_id}/approve`, `POST /gpo/compliance-measurements`, `POST /gpo/admin-fee-distributions`, and `PATCH /gpo/contracts/{contract_id}/tiers`.
16. **Known tier codes** — an elected tier code, and the tier code an activation must price against, appear in the contract's tier ladder, and the contract's derived default tier code appears there too; otherwise `ERR_TIER_CODE_UNKNOWN` (409). Applies to `POST /gpo/commitments`, `POST /gpo/price-activations`, `PATCH /gpo/contracts/{contract_id}/tiers`, and `POST /gpo/contracts/{contract_id}/activate`.
17. **Window orientation** — a price activation's `effective_to` is on or after its `effective_from`, and a distributor file's, measurement's, distribution's or leakage query's `period_end` is strictly after its `period_start`; otherwise `ERR_EFFECTIVE_WINDOW_INVALID` (409). Applies to `POST /gpo/price-activations`, `POST /gpo/distributor-files`, `POST /gpo/compliance-measurements`, `POST /gpo/admin-fee-distributions`, and `GET /gpo/commitments/{commitment_id}/leakage-report`.
18. **Containment in the contract term** — a price activation window lies inside its contract's own effective term; otherwise `ERR_PERIOD_OUTSIDE_CONTRACT_TERM` (409). Applies to `POST /gpo/price-activations`. The same error identifier is also used when a commitment's start date falls outside the contract term, as described in §3.4.
19. **Distributor file overlap** — no existing distributor file for the same member, contract and distributor code, in any status other than `REJECTED` or `REVERSED`, has a half-open period colliding with the new file's; abutting periods, where one ends exactly on the day the next begins, do not collide. Otherwise `ERR_FILE_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (409). Applies to `POST /gpo/distributor-files`.
20. **Activation overlap** — no existing price activation for the same commitment, catalog item and activation type, in status `DRAFT`, `ENDORSED`, `APPROVED` or `ACTIVE`, has a fully closed window colliding with the new one; sharing a single endpoint date is a collision. Otherwise `ERR_ACTIVATION_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (409). Applies to `POST /gpo/price-activations`.
21. **Price-shape coherence** — `requested_price_cents` is supplied for a `LOCAL_EXCEPTION` activation and omitted for a `TIER_PRICE` activation; the errors are `ERR_REQUESTED_PRICE_REQUIRED` and `ERR_REQUESTED_PRICE_NOT_ALLOWED`, both 409. Applies to `POST /gpo/price-activations`.
22. **Committed volume floor** — a commitment's `committed_volume_units` is at least the elected tier's minimum committed volume; otherwise `ERR_COMMITTED_VOLUME_BELOW_TIER` (409). Applies to `POST /gpo/commitments`.
23. **Base price floor** — a new base price is a positive integer that is not below the item's floor price; otherwise `ERR_BASE_PRICE_BELOW_FLOOR` (409). Applies to `PATCH /gpo/contract-items/{contract_item_id}/base-price`.
24. **Fee settlement coherence** — the collected fee does not exceed the accrued fee (`ERR_COLLECTION_EXCEEDS_ACCRUAL`), and the distribute action matches the contract's declared share basis (`ERR_DISTRIBUTION_BASIS_MISMATCH` or `ERR_DISTRIBUTION_BASIS_NOT_COLLECTABLE`); all 409. Applies to `POST /gpo/admin-fee-distributions/{distribution_id}/collect` and `POST /gpo/admin-fee-distributions/{distribution_id}/distribute`.

Three precedence outcomes are worth stating explicitly because they are the ones implementers most often get backwards.

Consider a commitment election that names a contract still in `DRAFT` **and** elects a rich tier with a committed volume far below that tier's floor. Both rung 7 and rung 22 are violated. The response is `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE` with HTTP 409, because a contract that has not been activated has no binding tier ladder to measure a commitment against, so the state check fires first and the fiscal floor is never reached.

Consider activating a `DRAFT` tier price on a catalog item that requires clinical evidence, where the contract's only evidence review is still `UNDER_REVIEW` **and** the member's cross-reference for that item is still `PROPOSED`. Both rung 10 and rung 13 are violated. The response is `ERR_CLINICAL_REVIEW_NOT_ENDORSED` with HTTP 409: clinical safety outranks catalog hygiene, because an unproven item must not be priced for anybody, and the missing evidence is reported even though the cross-reference is also unusable.

Consider creating a `TIER_PRICE` activation that wrongly carries a `requested_price_cents` value **and** whose `effective_from` equals the `effective_to` of an existing `ACTIVE` tier-price activation for the same commitment and catalog item. Both rung 20 and rung 21 are violated. The response is `ERR_ACTIVATION_PERIOD_OVERLAP` with HTTP 409, because the temporal ladder is what makes any price meaningful — two prices covering the same day cannot be reconciled at all — so the window conflict is reported before the body-shape complaint.

### 2.10. Half-Open Periods, Closed Windows, and Inclusive Dates

This system uses two different interval conventions on purpose, and confusing them changes results. All dates are ISO calendar dates in `YYYY-MM-DD` form.

**Half-open periods** apply to every reporting period: a distributor file's `period_start` and `period_end`, a compliance measurement's `period_start` and `period_end`, an administrative fee distribution's `period_start` and `period_end`, and a leakage query's `from_date` and `to_date`. The start day is included, the end day is excluded, and `period_end` must be strictly after `period_start`. This is why two distributor files can abut — one ending on the first of September, the next beginning on the first of September — without colliding.

**Closed windows** apply to contract terms and to prices: a contract's `effective_from` and `effective_to`, and a price activation's `effective_from` and `effective_to`. Both endpoint days are included. A contract's `effective_to` must be strictly after its `effective_from`; a price activation's `effective_to` must be on or after its `effective_from`, so a single-day price is legal. Because both endpoints are included, two activations sharing exactly one endpoint date do overlap, which is the opposite of the half-open file rule.

**Containment** always follows the convention of the thing being contained. A measurement includes a distributor file only when the file's half-open period lies wholly inside the measurement's half-open period. A price activation is inside its contract's term only when both of its inclusive endpoints fall inside the contract's inclusive term. A purchase line falls inside a price activation's window when its `purchase_date` is on or after the activation's `effective_from` and on or before its `effective_to`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Building the Contract Portfolio and Its Catalog

A `ContractManager` opens the portfolio by registering a supplier contract. The registration payload carries a `contract_code` of 1 to 40 characters that must be globally unique across the portfolio, compared as an exact case-sensitive string; a `supplier_name` of 1 to 120 characters naming the awarded supplier; a `category_code` of 1 to 40 characters naming the clinical spend category the contract governs, such as a cardiac stent category; and the contract term as `effective_from` and `effective_to`, both inclusive calendar dates, with `effective_to` required to be strictly after `effective_from`. Three optional switches round out the header: `requires_clinical_review`, a boolean defaulting to true; `share_basis`, an enumeration of `ACCRUAL` or `COLLECTED` defaulting to `COLLECTED`; and `leakage_alert_threshold_pct`, a percentage between 0.00 and 100.00 carrying two decimal places and defaulting to 15.00. Every new contract is born in `DRAFT` status with `evidence_endorsed` false and `active_commitment_count` zero.

Two fee rates are fixed at registration and never editable afterwards. `admin_fee_bps` is the administrative fee rate in basis points applied to each on-contract purchase line's extended cost, and it must be between 0 and 500 inclusive — at most five percent. `member_share_bps` is the share of the net fee pool distributed back to members in basis points, between 0 and 10000 inclusive, with whatever remains retained by the GPO.

The heart of the contract is its **tier ladder**, submitted as `tiers`: an array of 1 to 5 entries, each an object carrying a `tier_code`, a `min_committed_volume_units` integer, a `min_compliance_pct` percentage, and a `discount_bps` integer between 0 and 5000. The ladder must be well-formed in three respects. Exactly one entry must be the **default tier**, meaning its `min_committed_volume_units` is 0 and its `min_compliance_pct` is 0.00 — the rung nobody can fall below. When the entries are sorted by `min_committed_volume_units` ascending, `discount_bps` must be strictly increasing, so a richer volume commitment always buys a strictly deeper discount. And `tier_code` must be unique within the ladder. A ladder that is empty, exceeds five entries, lacks its default entry, or fails the strictly-increasing test is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_TIER_LADDER` at HTTP 400; a ladder that merely repeats a `tier_code` is instead rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_TIER_CODE` at HTTP 409, which is the uniqueness rung, not a shape complaint. On a well-formed submission the server derives `default_tier_code` as the `tier_code` of that single default entry.

Alongside the tier ladder sits the **holdback ladder**, submitted as `holdback_tiers`: an array of 1 to 4 entries, each an object carrying `min_gross_pool_cents` and `holdback_bps`. Every `min_gross_pool_cents` must be zero or greater and must be unique within the ladder; every `holdback_bps` must be between 0 and 1000 inclusive — at most ten percent; and when the entries are sorted by `min_gross_pool_cents` ascending, `holdback_bps` must be non-decreasing. Note the deliberate asymmetry with the tier ladder: discounts must be strictly increasing, holdbacks need only be non-decreasing, so two adjacent holdback rungs may carry the same rate. A malformed holdback ladder is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_HOLDBACK_LADDER` at HTTP 400.

Replacing the tier ladder is one operation with two meanings. On a `DRAFT` contract it is ordinary setup. On an `ACTIVE` contract it is a **category conversion**, and it is refused with `ERR_CLINICAL_REVIEW_NOT_ENDORSED` unless the contract carries an evidence review in `ENDORSED` status whose `conversion_recommendation` is `CONVERT`. A `SUSPENDED` contract refuses the edit outright with `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`. Whichever meaning applies, an accepted replacement overwrites `tiers` wholesale, re-derives `default_tier_code` from the new ladder — so the new ladder must itself contain exactly one default entry — and increments the contract's `version` by one. Already-existing price activations keep the prices they were minted with; the new ladder governs only what is minted after it.

Moving a contract from `DRAFT` to `ACTIVE` is what opens it for business: commitment elections, cross-reference proposals, price activations, distributor file receipts and fee accruals all require an `ACTIVE` contract. The transition is available only from `DRAFT`, and when `requires_clinical_review` is true it additionally demands that `evidence_endorsed` be true and that at least one evidence review naming this contract be in `ENDORSED` status with a `conversion_recommendation` of `CONVERT`; failing that, the attempt returns `ERR_CLINICAL_REVIEW_NOT_ENDORSED`. A contract created with `requires_clinical_review` false skips the gate entirely. Activation sets `status` to `ACTIVE` and increments `version` by one.

A `ComplianceAnalyst` — not the contract's own manager — may suspend an `ACTIVE` contract, optionally recording a `reason`, moving `status` to `SUSPENDED` and incrementing `version` by one. `SUSPENDED` is **terminal**: no reinstatement operation exists anywhere in this API. While a contract is suspended, no new commitment may be elected on it, no new distributor file may be received against it, and no new price activation may be created or activated for it — but activations that already reached `ACTIVE` keep pricing purchases, because the members who bought under them bought in good faith. Contracts may be listed with filters on `status` and `category_code` alongside the usual `limit` and `offset`.

The supplier's catalog is the one genuinely nested part of the API: a `ContractManager` adds an item directly under the contract that owns it. Each catalog line carries a `vendor_item_number` of 1 to 40 characters, unique among the items of that same contract; an `item_description` of 1 to 200 characters; a `uom` drawn from `EA`, `BX` or `CS` naming the contract unit of measure; a `units_per_uom` integer of at least 1 recording how many eaches a contract unit contains, which is descriptive only and is **never** used in any pricing or conversion arithmetic; a `base_price_cents` integer strictly greater than zero giving the undiscounted contract price per contract unit; a `floor_price_cents` integer that is zero or greater and no greater than `base_price_cents`, giving the hard contractual price floor below which no activation may ever price; and an optional `requires_evidence` boolean defaulting to false. A new catalog line is born with `status` `ACTIVE` and `active_activation_count` zero. Catalog lines may be listed filtered by `contract_id` and by `status`.

Repricing a catalog line replaces `base_price_cents` and is the only mutable field on it. The new price must be a positive integer that is not below the item's `floor_price_cents`; a price below the floor is rejected with `ERR_BASE_PRICE_BELOW_FLOOR`. The request must also declare `expected_contract_version` as described in §2.6. An accepted reprice increments both the contract's `version` and the item's `version` by one, and — importantly — leaves every already-created price activation holding the `computed_price_cents` it was minted with. Only activations minted after the reprice see the new base price.

Delisting a catalog line sets its `status` to `DELISTED`, optionally records a `reason`, and increments its `version` by one. A delisted line can no longer be named by a new cross-reference proposal — that attempt returns `ERR_CONTRACT_ITEM_NOT_ACTIVE` — and it can no longer be named by a new price activation. Activations already in `ACTIVE` status are untouched, and cross-references already `APPROVED` against it keep working.

### 3.2. Clinical Evidence and the Category-Conversion Gate

Before a purchasing organization moves a whole clinical category from one supplier to another, someone has to say in writing that the new product is at least as good. That is the job of the **clinical evidence dossier**, and a `ClinicalReviewer` owns it end to end. A submission names the `contract_id` whose category conversion is under review — the contract must exist and must be in `DRAFT` or `ACTIVE` status — and carries three evidence scores, each between 0.00 and 10.00 with at most two decimal places: `clinical_efficacy_score`, `safety_score`, and `supply_resilience_score`. An optional free-text `summary` of up to 500 characters, defaulting to the empty string, records the reviewer's narrative. The dossier is created in `UNDER_REVIEW` status with `revision_round` at zero, and its `submitted_by_user_id` is set to the submitting reviewer's `X-User-ID`. Dossiers may be listed filtered by `contract_id` and `status`.

The dossier's `weighted_score` is derived from the three scores by a fixed weighting: the efficacy score counts for forty percent of the total, the safety score for thirty-five percent, and the supply-resilience score for the remaining twenty-five percent. In arithmetic terms, the efficacy score is multiplied by 0.40, the safety score by 0.35, and the supply-resilience score by 0.25; those three products are added together **unrounded**, and the single resulting sum is then rounded half up to exactly two decimal places, once. Because the three weights add to 1.00, the weighted score occupies the same 0.00-to-10.00 range as its inputs. Worked through: scores of 8.00, 7.00 and 6.00 give 3.20 plus 2.45 plus 1.50, a weighted score of 7.15; scores of 6.50, 7.00 and 6.00 give 2.60 plus 2.45 plus 1.50, a weighted score of 6.55; and scores of 7.00, 7.00 and 7.02 give an unrounded 7.0050 that rounds half up to 7.01. No other review and no field of the contract participates in this computation.

From the weighted score the server derives `conversion_recommendation`: it is `CONVERT` when the weighted score is at least 7.00 — the threshold is **inclusive**, so a weighted score of exactly 7.00 recommends conversion — and `DO_NOT_CONVERT` otherwise. The recommendation is recomputed every time the scores change.

Scores can be revised in place while the dossier is `UNDER_REVIEW` or `REVISION_REQUESTED`: the revising reviewer submits all three of `clinical_efficacy_score`, `safety_score` and `supply_resilience_score`, which replace the stored values, and `weighted_score` and `conversion_recommendation` are recomputed under the same weighting. A score revision increments the dossier's `version` by one and does not change its `status`.

The dossier's lifecycle is a **bounded cycle**. From `UNDER_REVIEW`, a reviewer may send it back with a revision request, optionally recording a `reason`; the dossier moves `UNDER_REVIEW → REVISION_REQUESTED` and `revision_round` increments by one. A revision request is permitted only while `revision_round` is 0 or 1 — that is, at most 1 — so a dossier that has already been through two revision cycles and stands at `revision_round` 2 refuses a third with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED`. From `REVISION_REQUESTED`, a resubmission returns the dossier `REVISION_REQUESTED → UNDER_REVIEW`, overwrites the three score fields with the resubmitted values plus an optional replacement `summary`, recomputes `weighted_score` and then `conversion_recommendation` from it, and — this is the part that matters for segregation of duties — sets `submitted_by_user_id` to the resubmitting reviewer's `X-User-ID`, so the endorsement bar always applies to whoever submitted the dossier most recently. Resubmission does **not** increment `revision_round`; that counter is consumed only by the revision request. Attempting any of these transitions from a status where it does not exist returns `ERR_INVALID_REVIEW_STATE`.

Endorsement is the terminal act that opens the conversion gate, and it carries three preconditions evaluated in the order §2.9 fixes. The dossier must be `UNDER_REVIEW`, so `UNDER_REVIEW → ENDORSED` is the only endorsement path. The endorsing reviewer's `X-User-ID` must differ from `submitted_by_user_id`, or the request fails with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — the segregation rung outranks the score rung, so a self-endorsement of an under-threshold dossier reports the segregation violation. And the dossier's `weighted_score` must be at least 7.00, or the request fails with `ERR_EVIDENCE_SCORE_BELOW_THRESHOLD`. An accepted endorsement sets `status` to `ENDORSED`, records `endorsed_by_user_id` as the endorsing reviewer's `X-User-ID`, and then reaches across to the parent contract: `evidence_endorsed` is recomputed and the contract's `version` increments by one.

Rejection is the other terminal act, available from `UNDER_REVIEW` only, optionally recording a `reason`. It requires the same segregation check — the rejecting reviewer must not be the submitter — and returns `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` when it is. Rejection sets `status` to `REJECTED` and, exactly as endorsement does, recomputes the parent contract's `evidence_endorsed` and increments that contract's `version` by one. Both `ENDORSED` and `REJECTED` are terminal: neither can be revised, revisited, or cycled back into review.

### 3.3. Member Registration and Item-Master Stewardship

A `ContractManager` registers each participating health system as a **member**. Registration carries a `member_code` of 1 to 40 characters that must be globally unique, a `name` of 1 to 120 characters giving the legal entity name, an `aggregation_group_code` of 1 to 40 characters used purely for reporting rollups with no behavioural effect anywhere in this specification, and the member's `item_master`: an array of 1 to 50 entries, each an object carrying a `member_item_number`, a `description`, and a `units_per_uom` integer, with `member_item_number` unique inside the array. A new member is `ACTIVE`, with `watchlist_status` `NONE` and `fee_credit_balance_cents` zero. Members may be listed filtered by `status`, by `watchlist_status`, and by `aggregation_group_code`.

The item master is the member's own catalogue of the things it buys, and only the member curates it: a `MemberMaterialsManager` replaces its own `item_master` wholesale, which increments that member's `version` by one. Replacement is destructive to the list but not to history — cross-references already `APPROVED` against a `member_item_number` that the replacement removed keep working and keep classifying purchases, while any **new** cross-reference must name an entry that is present in the current list.

A `ComplianceAnalyst` may suspend a member, optionally recording a `reason`, which moves `status` to `SUSPENDED` and increments `version` by one. A suspended member may not be named by a new commitment election and may not be named by a new distributor file; both attempts return `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_ACTIVE`.

Two member fields are written only by machinery elsewhere in the system and never by a client. `watchlist_status` takes the value `WATCHLIST` when the leakage cascade of §3.7 raises the leakage flag on any of that member's commitments, and returns to `NONE` only when no commitment of that member carries a raised flag. `fee_credit_balance_cents` is the cumulative administrative-fee share credited to the member by the distribution waterfall of §3.9; it never goes below zero, and it is debited by an exact recorded amount when a distribution is reversed.

### 3.4. Electing a Commitment and Working the Letter of Participation

A commitment is a member's tier election on one contract, and it is the only entity in this system that carries **two independent state dimensions on the same row**. `election_status` is the commercial lifecycle and runs `ELECTED`, `ACTIVE`, `REQUAL_REVIEW`, `TERMINATED`, with a cycle back and forth between `ACTIVE` and `REQUAL_REVIEW`. `lop_status` is the letter-of-participation document lifecycle and runs `NOT_ISSUED`, `ISSUED`, `SIGNED`, `COUNTERSIGNED`, `VOID`, with a cycle from `ISSUED` or `SIGNED` out to `VOID` and back to `ISSUED`. The two dimensions move independently, with exactly one point of coupling: countersigning the letter is the same request that activates the election. No other letter transition touches `election_status`, and no election transition touches `lop_status`.

Electing is a `MemberMaterialsManager` action, and its payload names `member_id`, `contract_id`, `elected_tier_code`, `committed_volume_units`, and `effective_from`. Its preconditions are evaluated in this order: the member must exist and be `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_ACTIVE`; the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the body's `member_id`, else `ERR_MEMBER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`; the contract must exist and be `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`; the `elected_tier_code` must name an entry of that contract's tier ladder, else `ERR_TIER_CODE_UNKNOWN`; no commitment for the same member and contract may already sit in `ELECTED`, `ACTIVE` or `REQUAL_REVIEW` — a `TERMINATED` predecessor does not block a fresh election — else `ERR_DUPLICATE_COMMITMENT`; the `effective_from` date must fall inside the contract's inclusive term, else `ERR_PERIOD_OUTSIDE_CONTRACT_TERM`; and `committed_volume_units`, which must itself be strictly greater than zero, must be at least the elected tier's `min_committed_volume_units`, else `ERR_COMMITTED_VOLUME_BELOW_TIER`. That last check is the lowest-priority of the seven, which is why a `DRAFT` contract and an under-volume election together report the contract state rather than the volume shortfall.

A newly elected commitment is born with `election_status` `ELECTED`, `lop_status` `NOT_ISSUED`, `signer_name` null, `current_tier_code` null, `requalification_round` zero, `last_final_compliance_pct` null, all three year-to-date ledgers — `ytd_on_contract_spend_cents`, `ytd_total_category_spend_cents` and `ytd_measured_volume_units` — at zero, `ytd_leakage_pct` at 0.00, and `leakage_flag` false. Creation also reaches the parent contract: `active_commitment_count` is recomputed and the contract's `version` increments by one. Because an `ELECTED` commitment is not yet one of the counted states, the counter's value does not move, but the contract row is touched all the same. Commitments may be listed filtered by `member_id`, `contract_id`, `election_status` and `lop_status`.

The letter of participation is the paper trail that turns an election into a binding tier. A `ContractManager` issues it, driving `lop_status` from `NOT_ISSUED` to `ISSUED` and setting `signer_name` to null; issuance is permitted only while `election_status` is still `ELECTED`, and a commitment that has moved past `ELECTED` refuses it with `ERR_COMMITMENT_NOT_ELECTED`. The member's own `MemberMaterialsManager` then signs it, supplying `signer_name` in the request, which advances `lop_status` from `ISSUED` to `SIGNED` and stores that name; a manager whose `X-User-ID` is not the commitment's `member_id` is refused with `ERR_MEMBER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. A `ContractManager` finally countersigns it, which requires `lop_status` to be `SIGNED` — anything else returns `ERR_INVALID_LOP_STATE` — requires `election_status` to be `ELECTED`, and requires the contract to still be `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`.

Countersigning is the coupling point and does four things at once. It advances `lop_status` from `SIGNED` to `COUNTERSIGNED`. In the same request it drives `election_status` from `ELECTED` to `ACTIVE`. It sets `current_tier_code` to the commitment's `elected_tier_code`, which is the first moment the member has a tier actually in force rather than merely elected. And it recomputes the parent contract's `active_commitment_count` — which now genuinely rises, since `ACTIVE` is a counted state — incrementing that contract's `version` by one. The commitment's own `version` increments by one as well.

A letter can be unwound before it binds but not after. Voiding is available from `ISSUED` and from `SIGNED`, optionally recording a `reason`; it sets `lop_status` to `VOID`, resets `signer_name` to null, and leaves `election_status` completely untouched. From `VOID` the same `ContractManager` may issue the letter again, closing the document cycle `VOID → ISSUED` and again clearing `signer_name`, provided `election_status` is still `ELECTED` — otherwise `ERR_COMMITMENT_NOT_ELECTED`. Once `lop_status` reaches `COUNTERSIGNED`, however, voiding is refused with `ERR_INVALID_LOP_STATE`: an in-force letter is unwound by terminating the commitment, not by voiding the document. Issuing, signing, voiding and re-issuing each increment the commitment's own `version` by one and touch nothing else; because none of them can move `election_status`, none of them changes the parent contract's `active_commitment_count`, and none of them increments the contract's `version`. Apart from the initial election, countersigning and terminating are the only commitment operations that reach the contract row.

Opening a requalification is a `ComplianceAnalyst` action that moves `election_status` from `ACTIVE` to `REQUAL_REVIEW`, optionally recording a `reason`, and increments the commitment's `version` by one. It requires `election_status` to be `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_COMMITMENT_NOT_ACTIVE` — and it requires `lop_status` to be `COUNTERSIGNED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_LOP_STATE`. Opening a requalification is the **only** way to make a compliance measurement finalizable, and by itself it changes no price: it is a gate, not an action.

Terminating is also a `ComplianceAnalyst` action, optionally recording a `reason`, and it is available from all three non-terminal election states. `TERMINATED` is terminal in every case, and the commitment's own `version` increments by one on each branch. The branches differ in how far they reach:

- **From `ELECTED`.** The commitment has never had a tier in force and has never been counted on its contract, so terminating it sets `election_status` to `TERMINATED` and stops there. No price activation is superseded, no catalog item's activation counter is recomputed, and the contract's `active_commitment_count` and `version` are left alone.
- **From `ACTIVE`.** `election_status` becomes `TERMINATED`. Every price activation belonging to this commitment whose status is `DRAFT`, `ENDORSED`, `APPROVED` or `ACTIVE` moves to `SUPERSEDED` with its `superseded_by_activation_id` left null, because **no replacement is synthesized** — the member is leaving, not being repriced. For each catalog item touched by that supersede, `active_activation_count` is recomputed exactly once, incrementing that item's `version` by one. And the parent contract's `active_commitment_count` is recomputed, incrementing the contract's `version` by one.
- **From `REQUAL_REVIEW`.** Identical to the `ACTIVE` branch in every respect: `election_status` becomes `TERMINATED`, every `DRAFT`, `ENDORSED`, `APPROVED` or `ACTIVE` activation of the commitment becomes `SUPERSEDED` with a null `superseded_by_activation_id` and no replacement, each touched catalog item's `active_activation_count` is recomputed once with a `version` increment, and the contract's `active_commitment_count` is recomputed with a `version` increment. Abandoning a requalification mid-flight is exactly as destructive as terminating a settled commitment.

### 3.5. Cross-Referencing Member Items to the Contract Catalog

Distributor data arrives keyed by the **member's** item numbers, while contract prices are keyed by the **supplier's** vendor item numbers. The cross-reference is the junction that lets the two meet, and it also carries the unit conversion between them. A `MemberMaterialsManager` proposes one by naming its own `member_id`, the `contract_item_id` of the catalog line being mapped to, the `member_item_number` on its own side, a `match_type` drawn from `EXACT`, `EQUIVALENT` or `MANUAL` recording how good the mapping is, and a `conversion_factor` strictly greater than zero carrying at most four decimal places.

`conversion_factor` means: how many contract units one member-item unit delivers. A purchased quantity expressed in member-item units is multiplied by this factor to obtain contract units, which is the only place the number is ever used. Note that a catalog line's own `units_per_uom` and an item-master entry's `units_per_uom` play no part in this conversion; they are descriptive.

A proposal's preconditions run in this order: the member must exist and the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the body's `member_id`, else `ERR_MEMBER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`; the parent contract must be `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`; the catalog item must exist and be `ACTIVE` rather than `DELISTED`, else `ERR_CONTRACT_ITEM_NOT_ACTIVE`; the `member_item_number` must appear among the current `item_master` entries of that member, else `ERR_MEMBER_ITEM_NOT_IN_MASTER`; and no cross-reference already in `APPROVED` status may exist for the same member and catalog item, nor for the same member and member item number, else `ERR_DUPLICATE_XREF`. The mapping is created in `PROPOSED` status. Cross-references may be listed filtered by `member_id`, `contract_item_id` and `status`.

A `ContractManager` adjudicates the proposal. Approving it drives `PROPOSED → APPROVED`, records `approved_by_user_id` as the approver's `X-User-ID`, and increments the mapping's `version` by one; the two-sided uniqueness check is **re-evaluated at approval time**, so a second proposal that was legal when it was raised is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_XREF` if a sibling proposal reached `APPROVED` first. Rejecting drives `PROPOSED → REJECTED`, stores the optional `reason` as `reject_note` — null when the reason is omitted — and increments `version` by one. Both `APPROVED` and `REJECTED` are terminal, and either operation attempted on a mapping that is no longer `PROPOSED` returns `ERR_XREF_NOT_PROPOSED`.

Only an `APPROVED` cross-reference has effect. A `PROPOSED` or `REJECTED` mapping is invisible to the purchase-line classifier of §3.7 and does not satisfy the cross-reference precondition on price activation described in §3.6.

### 3.6. Pricing: Tier Activations and Local Exceptions

A **price activation** is an activated price for exactly one commitment-and-catalog-item pair over one closed date window, and it is the only thing that makes a purchase count as on-contract. Every activation names its `commitment_id` and its `contract_item_id`, declares an `activation_type` of either `TIER_PRICE` or `LOCAL_EXCEPTION`, and carries an inclusive window as `effective_from` and `effective_to`. A `LOCAL_EXCEPTION` additionally carries `requested_price_cents`, a positive integer naming the price the member is asking for; a `TIER_PRICE` must omit it. Supplying it on a tier price returns `ERR_REQUESTED_PRICE_NOT_ALLOWED`, and omitting it on a local exception returns `ERR_REQUESTED_PRICE_REQUIRED`.

The two tracks belong to different people. A `TIER_PRICE` activation is a GPO-driven contract price and may only be requested by a `ContractManager`. A `LOCAL_EXCEPTION` is a member-requested custom price and may only be requested by a `MemberMaterialsManager` whose `X-User-ID` equals the commitment's `member_id`. A role that requests the wrong track receives `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`; a member manager reaching for another member's commitment receives `ERR_MEMBER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`.

Requesting an activation is guarded by a chain of checks in this order. The commitment must exist and its `election_status` must be `ELECTED`, `ACTIVE` or `REQUAL_REVIEW` — a `TERMINATED` commitment can never be priced. The catalog item must exist and its own contract must be the same contract the commitment names, else `ERR_ITEM_CONTRACT_MISMATCH`. That contract must be `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`. The role-and-scope branch above is checked. The window must be oriented — `effective_to` on or after `effective_from`, else `ERR_EFFECTIVE_WINDOW_INVALID` — and must sit wholly inside the contract's inclusive term, else `ERR_PERIOD_OUTSIDE_CONTRACT_TERM`. No other activation for the **same commitment, same catalog item and same activation type** whose status is `DRAFT`, `ENDORSED`, `APPROVED` or `ACTIVE` may have a closed-interval collision with the new window; because both endpoints are inclusive, two windows that share even a single day collide, and the rejection is `ERR_ACTIVATION_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. Rows already in `SUPERSEDED` or `DENIED` are invisible to this guard, which is precisely what lets a cascade-minted replacement window start inside the window of the row it replaced. Finally the price-shape rule above is applied.

An accepted request creates the activation in `DRAFT` status with `origin` `REQUEST`, `endorsed_by_user_id`, `approved_by_user_id`, `deny_note`, `superseded_by_activation_id` and `replaces_activation_id` all null. The server then pins `tier_code_at_activation`: the commitment's `current_tier_code` if it has one, or the contract's `default_tier_code` when `current_tier_code` is still null — which is the case for any commitment whose letter has not yet been countersigned. From there it derives `computed_price_cents` and `price_clamped`.

**Deriving the price.** The derivation runs in four steps over a single catalog item, a single tier row, and — for a local exception — that activation's own requested price. No other activation and no purchase data participates.

1. Resolve the tier row: the entry of the contract's tier ladder whose `tier_code` equals the activation's `tier_code_at_activation`. If the ladder holds no such entry, the request fails with `ERR_TIER_CODE_UNKNOWN`.
2. Compute the tier price from the item's own `base_price_cents`. Take 10000 basis points, subtract the tier's `discount_bps`, multiply the base price by that difference, divide by 10000, and round the result half up to a whole number of cents. That value is the tier price. Worked over a `base_price_cents` of 10000 against a ladder whose rungs carry 0, 800 and 1500 basis points of discount, the tier prices are 10000, 9200 and 8500 cents respectively; over a `base_price_cents` of 10010 at 1500 basis points the exact result is 8508.5 cents, which rounds half up to 8509.
3. Apply the local-exception ceiling. When `activation_type` is `LOCAL_EXCEPTION`, the candidate price is the **lesser** of the activation's `requested_price_cents` and the tier price from step 2 — a local exception may only ever move *down* from the tier price, and asking for more than the tier price is silently ceilinged rather than rejected. When `activation_type` is `TIER_PRICE`, the candidate price is simply the tier price.
4. Clamp at the contractual floor. If the candidate price from step 3 is strictly below the item's `floor_price_cents`, then `computed_price_cents` becomes the floor price and `price_clamped` is set to true. Otherwise `computed_price_cents` is the candidate price and `price_clamped` is false. The floor outranks both the tier discount and the local exception.

The rounding boundary is fixed and singular: the only rounding in this derivation happens at step 2. Steps 3 and 4 compare and select among values that are already whole cents, so a discount is never rounded twice and the floor is never crossed by a rounding artifact. Two illustrations of the interaction: a requested 9000 cents against a zero-discount tier price of 10000 with a floor of 8200 yields a candidate of 9000, which is above the floor, so `computed_price_cents` is 9000 and `price_clamped` is false; a requested 8000 cents against a 1500-basis-point tier price of 8500 with a floor of 8200 yields a candidate of 8000, which is below the floor, so `computed_price_cents` becomes 8200 and `price_clamped` is true.

**The tier-price track is short.** A `ContractManager` activates the `DRAFT` row directly, moving `DRAFT → ACTIVE`. Four preconditions apply: the row must be in `DRAFT` and its `activation_type` must be `TIER_PRICE`, else `ERR_INVALID_ACTIVATION_STATE`; the contract must still be `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`; if the catalog item's `requires_evidence` flag is true, the parent contract must carry an evidence review in `ENDORSED` status whose `conversion_recommendation` is `CONVERT`, else `ERR_CLINICAL_REVIEW_NOT_ENDORSED`; and an `APPROVED` cross-reference must already link the commitment's member to this catalog item, else `ERR_XREF_NOT_APPROVED`. The evidence gate is checked before the cross-reference gate, so an unproven item with an unapproved mapping reports the missing evidence. Activation sets `status` to `ACTIVE`, increments the activation's own `version` by one, and recomputes the catalog item's `active_activation_count`, incrementing that item's `version` by one. There is no endorsement step on this track and attempting one returns `ERR_INVALID_ACTIVATION_STATE`.

**The local-exception track is long, and it is where segregation of duties bites.** A `ContractManager` first endorses the `DRAFT` row, moving `DRAFT → ENDORSED` and recording `endorsed_by_user_id` as the endorser's `X-User-ID`. Endorsement requires the row to be in `DRAFT` with `activation_type` `LOCAL_EXCEPTION`, else `ERR_INVALID_ACTIVATION_STATE`, and it requires the commitment's `election_status` **not** to be `REQUAL_REVIEW`, else `ERR_REQUALIFICATION_IN_PROGRESS` — a member being retiered is not a member whose custom prices can be moved. A **different** `ContractManager` then approves it, moving `ENDORSED → APPROVED` and recording `approved_by_user_id`; approval requires the row to be in `ENDORSED`, requires the approving `X-User-ID` to differ from `endorsed_by_user_id` (else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`), and applies the same `REQUAL_REVIEW` bar. Only then may a `ContractManager` activate it, moving `APPROVED → ACTIVE` under exactly the same four preconditions as the tier-price activation — `APPROVED` status with `activation_type` `LOCAL_EXCEPTION`, an `ACTIVE` contract, the evidence gate for an evidence-flagged item, and an `APPROVED` cross-reference — with exactly the same effects: `status` becomes `ACTIVE`, the activation's `version` increments by one, and the catalog item's `active_activation_count` is recomputed with a `version` increment on that item.

A local exception may be **denied** terminally from either `DRAFT` or `ENDORSED`, optionally recording a `reason` that is stored as `deny_note` and left null when omitted. `status` becomes `DENIED` and the activation's `version` increments by one. Denial is available only to `LOCAL_EXCEPTION` rows; a `TIER_PRICE` row, or a row already in `APPROVED`, `ACTIVE`, `DENIED` or `SUPERSEDED`, refuses it with `ERR_INVALID_ACTIVATION_STATE`.

`DENIED` and `SUPERSEDED` are the two terminal activation states. `SUPERSEDED` is never reached by a client request; it is written only by a commitment termination, as described in §3.4, or by the requalification cascade of §3.8. When the cascade mints a replacement, the replacement's `origin` is `REQUALIFICATION_CASCADE` rather than `REQUEST`, its `replaces_activation_id` points back at the row it replaced, and the replaced row's `superseded_by_activation_id` points forward at it. Because those replacements are created without a client request, the activation list — filterable by `commitment_id`, `contract_item_id`, `status`, `activation_type`, `origin`, and by either end of a supersede link through `superseded_by_activation_id` or `replaces_activation_id` — is the only channel through which a client can find them.

### 3.7. Distributor File Intake, Line Classification and the Leakage Ledger

Compliance is measured from what the distributor says the member actually bought. A `DistributorAgent` submits one **sales-tracing file** per member, contract, distributor and half-open period, naming `member_id`, `contract_id`, a `distributor_code` of 1 to 40 characters, `period_start` and `period_end`, and an array `lines` of at least one entry. Each entry of `lines` is an object carrying a `member_item_number` of 1 to 40 characters, a `quantity_units` integer of zero or more, a `unit_price_paid_cents` integer of zero or more giving the price actually paid per member-item unit, and a `purchase_date`.

Submission requires the member to exist and be `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_ACTIVE`; the contract to exist and be `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`; `period_end` to be strictly after `period_start`, else `ERR_EFFECTIVE_WINDOW_INVALID`; at least one entry in `lines`, else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; and no existing file for the same member, contract and `distributor_code`, in any status other than `REJECTED` or `REVERSED`, to have a half-open collision with the new period, else `ERR_FILE_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. Because the periods are half-open, a file ending on a given day and a file starting on that same day abut rather than collide.

An accepted submission creates the file in `RECEIVED` status and turns each entry of `lines` into exactly one **purchase line**, each carrying the file's identifier as `distributor_file_id`, the submitted `member_item_number`, `quantity_units`, `unit_price_paid_cents` and `purchase_date`, an `extended_cost_cents` computed immediately as `quantity_units` multiplied by `unit_price_paid_cents`, a `classification` of `UNCLASSIFIED`, a `reject_reason` of `NONE`, `contract_units` of 0, and `matched_vendor_item_number` null. The file's `line_count` is set to the number of purchase lines created, while `accepted_line_count` and `rejected_line_count` remain 0 and `loaded_at` and `reverse_note` remain null. A `member_item_number` reported by a distributor need **not** exist in the member's item master; an unknown number is not an error at intake, it is simply destined to classify as unmatched. Purchase lines are immutable append-only records: no request ever creates, updates or deletes one directly, and they are discovered only through the purchase-line list — filterable by `distributor_file_id`, `classification` and `reject_reason` — or through a single-line read. Files themselves may be listed filtered by `member_id`, `contract_id`, `distributor_code` and `status`.

The same `DistributorAgent` then validates the file, driving `RECEIVED → VALIDATING`; any other starting status returns `ERR_INVALID_FILE_STATE`. Validation stamps a `reject_reason` on each purchase line by a two-test cascade in a fixed order: the line takes `DATE_OUT_OF_PERIOD` when its `purchase_date` falls outside the file's half-open period; otherwise it takes `ZERO_QUANTITY` when its `quantity_units` is exactly 0; otherwise it keeps `NONE`. The order matters — a zero-quantity line dated outside the period records `DATE_OUT_OF_PERIOD`, not `ZERO_QUANTITY`. Validation then recomputes `accepted_line_count` as the number of lines whose `reject_reason` is `NONE` and `rejected_line_count` as the number whose `reject_reason` is anything else, and increments the file's `version` by one.

Loading is a `ComplianceAnalyst` action available only from `VALIDATING` — anything else returns `ERR_INVALID_FILE_STATE` — and it lands the file in one of three states according to the counters validation produced:

- **`VALIDATING → LOADED`** when `rejected_line_count` is 0 and `accepted_line_count` is greater than 0. Every accepted line is classified, the ledger cascade runs in full, `status` becomes `LOADED`, and `loaded_at` is stamped from the logical clock.
- **`VALIDATING → PARTIALLY_LOADED`** when `rejected_line_count` is greater than 0 and `accepted_line_count` is also greater than 0. Everything is identical to the `LOADED` landing — the same classification pass, the same four ledger hops, the same `loaded_at` stamp — and only the resulting `status` differs. A partially loaded file is a real load: its accepted lines are real purchases and they move the member's ledgers.
- **`VALIDATING → REJECTED`** when `accepted_line_count` is 0. `status` becomes `REJECTED`, no line is classified, and no commitment ledger field moves at all.

**Hop 1 — classification.** Each purchase line whose `reject_reason` is `NONE` is classified; rejected lines are skipped entirely and keep `classification` `UNCLASSIFIED`, `contract_units` 0 and `matched_vendor_item_number` null, and they never enter any sum anywhere in this system. For an accepted line the server looks for the cross-reference in `APPROVED` status whose member is the file's member, whose `member_item_number` equals the line's `member_item_number`, and whose catalog item belongs to the file's contract. If no such cross-reference exists, the line's `classification` becomes `UNMATCHED`, `matched_vendor_item_number` stays null, `contract_units` stays 0, and the line is finished. Otherwise the server takes the catalog item the cross-reference points at, copies that item's `vendor_item_number` into the line's `matched_vendor_item_number` — deliberately a value copy rather than a foreign key, because distributor data is keyed by item numbers rather than by identifiers — and computes `contract_units` as the line's `quantity_units` multiplied by the cross-reference's `conversion_factor`, rounded half up to a whole number of units.

The server then asks which activation governs the line. Only activations in `ACTIVE` status count, and only those belonging to the commitment for this member-and-contract pair, naming this catalog item, and whose closed window contains the line's `purchase_date` — that is, `effective_from` on or before the purchase date and `effective_to` on or after it. Activations in `DRAFT`, `ENDORSED`, `APPROVED`, `DENIED` or `SUPERSEDED` are invisible here, exactly as `PROPOSED` and `REJECTED` cross-references are. If no eligible activation exists, the line's `classification` becomes `OFF_CONTRACT_LEAKAGE`, even though `contract_units` and `matched_vendor_item_number` have already been written. If several are eligible, they are ranked to pick one governing row: a `LOCAL_EXCEPTION` always outranks a `TIER_PRICE` for the same pair; among rows of the same `activation_type` the later `effective_from` wins; and a tie on `effective_from` is broken by the earlier `created_at`. Against the governing row's `computed_price_cents`, the line classifies as `ON_CONTRACT` when its `unit_price_paid_cents` is less than or equal to that price, and as `OFF_CONTRACT_LEAKAGE` when it is greater. The comparison is an integer cent comparison and it is **inclusive at equality**: paying exactly the computed price is on contract, paying less than it is still on contract because the member merely beat the ceiling, and paying one cent more is leakage. This single-cent boundary is what the compliance percentage is most sensitive to.

**Hop 2 — the year-to-date ledgers**, caused by hop 1's classification writes. The server finds the commitment for this file's member-and-contract pair whose `election_status` is `ELECTED`, `ACTIVE` or `REQUAL_REVIEW`, and posts three running sums onto it: `ytd_on_contract_spend_cents` grows by the total `extended_cost_cents` of the file's `ON_CONTRACT` lines; `ytd_total_category_spend_cents` grows by the total `extended_cost_cents` of the file's `ON_CONTRACT`, `OFF_CONTRACT_LEAKAGE` and `UNMATCHED` lines together; and `ytd_measured_volume_units` grows by the total `contract_units` of the file's `ON_CONTRACT` lines. If no such commitment exists, this hop is simply a no-op — the file still loads and its lines still carry their classifications. The commitment's `version` increments by one when it is posted to.

**Hop 3 — the leakage rate**, caused by hop 2's ledger write. `ytd_leakage_pct` is recomputed as follows: subtract `ytd_on_contract_spend_cents` from `ytd_total_category_spend_cents`, multiply the difference by 100, divide by `ytd_total_category_spend_cents`, and round half up to two decimal places. When `ytd_total_category_spend_cents` is 0 the recomputed value is 0.00 rather than an error. If the recomputed `ytd_leakage_pct` is **strictly greater than** the contract's `leakage_alert_threshold_pct`, `leakage_flag` is set to true; a rate landing exactly on the threshold does not raise the flag. With the default threshold of 15.00, a member whose leakage stands at exactly 15.00 percent is not flagged and one at 15.01 percent is.

**Hop 4 — the watchlist**, caused by hop 3 raising the flag from false to true. The member's `watchlist_status` becomes `WATCHLIST` and that member's `version` increments by one.

Reversal unwinds a load completely. A `ComplianceAnalyst` reverses a file from `LOADED` or from `PARTIALLY_LOADED` — the two branches behave identically — optionally recording a `reason` stored as `reverse_note`; any other starting status returns `ERR_INVALID_FILE_STATE`. `status` becomes `REVERSED`, which is terminal, and the file's `version` increments by one. The compensation then runs in three hops of its own. First, the same commitment's `ytd_on_contract_spend_cents`, `ytd_total_category_spend_cents` and `ytd_measured_volume_units` are each decremented by **exactly the amounts this file added at load time** — not by a re-derivation from current data — and the commitment's `version` increments by one. Second, caused by that decrement, `ytd_leakage_pct` is recomputed by the same formula as hop 3, and if the recomputed rate is less than or equal to the contract's `leakage_alert_threshold_pct` — including the case where the denominator has fallen back to 0 and the rate is therefore 0.00 — then `leakage_flag` is set to false. Third, caused by that flag falling, the member's `watchlist_status` returns to `NONE` if and only if no commitment of that member still carries `leakage_flag` true, with a `version` increment on the member. Finally every purchase line of the reversed file has its `classification` reset to `UNCLASSIFIED`, its `contract_units` reset to 0 and its `matched_vendor_item_number` reset to null; `reject_reason` is left exactly as validation stamped it.

**Off-contract leakage reporting.** A `ComplianceAnalyst`, a `ContractManager`, or the owning `MemberMaterialsManager` may request a read-only leakage projection for one commitment over a half-open window supplied as `from_date` and `to_date`, with `to_date` required to be strictly after `from_date`, else `ERR_EFFECTIVE_WINDOW_INVALID`. The report considers purchase lines whose `reject_reason` is `NONE` belonging to files of this commitment's member-and-contract pair in status `LOADED` or `PARTIALLY_LOADED` whose own half-open period is contained in the requested window. It sums `extended_cost_cents` over the `OFF_CONTRACT_LEAKAGE` lines as `leakage_spend_cents`, over the `UNMATCHED` lines as `unmatched_spend_cents`, and over the `ON_CONTRACT` lines as `on_contract_spend_cents`. Its `denominator_cents` is the sum of all three of those figures — note that this denominator **always** includes unmatched spend, which is not true of the preliminary compliance variant in §3.8, because the report is a spend view rather than a settlement view. `leakage_pct` is `leakage_spend_cents` multiplied by 100, divided by `denominator_cents`, rounded half up to two decimal places, and it is 0.00 when the denominator is 0. The response also echoes `commitment_id`, `from_date` and `to_date`.

The report's `top_items` array names the worst offenders. The leakage lines are grouped by `member_item_number`, and each group reports that `member_item_number`, a `spend_cents` equal to the summed `extended_cost_cents` of the group, and a `quantity_units` equal to the summed `quantity_units` of the group. Groups are ordered by `spend_cents` descending, ties broken by `member_item_number` ascending — never by identifier, since a member item number is unique inside a member's item master — and the list is truncated to the top three groups.

### 3.8. Compliance Measurement, Requalification and the Retier Cascade

A **compliance measurement** answers one question for one commitment over one half-open purchase-date period: what fraction of the member's category spend actually landed on contract, and which tier has the member therefore earned? The same row carries both a preliminary answer and a final answer, computed over deliberately different eligible-file sets, so their divergence is directly observable.

A `ComplianceAnalyst` opens a measurement naming `commitment_id`, `period_start`, `period_end`, and `expected_commitment_version`. The commitment must exist and its `election_status` must be `ACTIVE` or `REQUAL_REVIEW`, else `ERR_COMMITMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`. The declared version must match, else `ERR_STALE_COMMITMENT_VERSION` at HTTP 412, as §2.6 describes. No measurement for the same commitment in `DRAFT`, `PRELIMINARY` or `FINAL` status may already cover the identical `period_start` and `period_end` pair, else `ERR_DUPLICATE_MEASUREMENT_PERIOD`; a measurement that has been abandoned into `SUPERSEDED` does not block a fresh one for the same period. And `period_end` must be strictly after `period_start`, else `ERR_EFFECTIVE_WINDOW_INVALID`. The new row is created in `DRAFT` with every money and volume field at 0, `compliance_pct` at 0.00, `preliminary_compliance_pct` null, `qualified_tier_code` null, `basis_empty` false, and `computed_variant` null; the parent commitment's `version` increments by one. Measurements may be listed filtered by `commitment_id` and `status`.

**The shared aggregation.** Both variants work the same way and differ in exactly two decisions. The server assembles the eligible distributor files: files belonging to the member-and-contract pair of the measurement's commitment, whose own half-open period lies wholly inside the measurement's half-open period, and whose `status` is in the variant's admitted set. Files still in `RECEIVED` or `VALIDATING`, and files in `REVERSED`, are excluded by both variants. From those files it takes every purchase line whose `reject_reason` is `NONE` and computes four sums: `on_contract_spend_cents` is the total `extended_cost_cents` of lines classified `ON_CONTRACT`; `leakage_spend_cents` is the total `extended_cost_cents` of lines classified `OFF_CONTRACT_LEAKAGE`; `unmatched_spend_cents` is the total `extended_cost_cents` of lines classified `UNMATCHED`; and `measured_volume_units` is the total `contract_units` of lines classified `ON_CONTRACT`.

`total_category_spend_cents` is then the denominator the variant actually used: always `on_contract_spend_cents` plus `leakage_spend_cents`, plus `unmatched_spend_cents` only if the variant counts unmatched spend. When `total_category_spend_cents` comes out at 0, `compliance_pct` is 0.00 and `basis_empty` is set to true; otherwise `compliance_pct` is `on_contract_spend_cents` multiplied by 100, divided by `total_category_spend_cents`, rounded half up to two decimal places, and `basis_empty` is false. All the sums are exact integer cents and the percentage is computed **once** from the unrounded integer sums — never by averaging per-file percentages.

**The preliminary variant** runs while the period may still be receiving files. It admits files in `LOADED` **and** `PARTIALLY_LOADED` status, because a partially loaded file's accepted lines are real purchases. It **excludes** `unmatched_spend_cents` from the denominator, on the reasoning that an unmatched line has not yet been proven to belong to the contract's category and charging it against the member early would understate compliance. The measure action drives `DRAFT → PRELIMINARY` — any other starting status returns `ERR_INVALID_MEASUREMENT_STATE` — writes the result to both `compliance_pct` and `preliminary_compliance_pct`, sets `computed_variant` to `PRELIMINARY`, computes `qualified_tier_code` from the preliminary figures, and increments the measurement's `version` by one. Crucially, **no commitment field moves**: a preliminary measurement never retiers anybody.

**The final variant** runs once the period is closed and every file has been either fully loaded or reversed. It admits files in `LOADED` status **only** — a `PARTIALLY_LOADED` file is dropped in its entirety, including its accepted lines, because a statement with rejected lines is not a settleable statement of the period. And it **includes** `unmatched_spend_cents` in the denominator, because at settlement an unmatched purchase is presumed in-category and therefore counts against the member. `preliminary_compliance_pct` is preserved unchanged as the audit trail while `compliance_pct` is overwritten with the final figure, and `computed_variant` becomes `FINAL`.

The divergence between the two is real and can run in either direction, which is why they cannot be collapsed into one number. Consider a period from the first of August 2026 up to but excluding the first of September 2026, in which a `LOADED` file contributes 5000 on-contract units at 9500 cents each, or 47,500,000 cents, plus 500 unmatched units at 6000 cents each, or 3,000,000 cents; and a `PARTIALLY_LOADED` file contributes 1500 leakage units at 10500 cents each, or 15,750,000 cents. The preliminary variant takes on-contract spend of 47,500,000 over a denominator of 47,500,000 plus 15,750,000, or 63,250,000, giving 75.10 percent. The final variant drops the partially loaded file entirely and adds the unmatched spend, taking 47,500,000 over 47,500,000 plus 3,000,000, or 50,500,000, giving 94.06 percent. The same stored data, 18.96 percentage points apart.

**Requalification: which tier has been earned.** Both variants finish by deriving `qualified_tier_code` from exactly one pair of numbers — the `compliance_pct` and the `measured_volume_units` just computed — against the contract's tier ladder. A ladder entry is *eligible* when the measured compliance percentage is at least that entry's `min_compliance_pct` **and** the measured volume is at least that entry's `min_committed_volume_units`. Both comparisons are inclusive, so landing exactly on a threshold qualifies; compliance is compared at two decimal places against a two-decimal minimum, and volume is an integer comparison. If no entry is eligible, `qualified_tier_code` falls back to the contract's `default_tier_code`. Otherwise the eligible entries are ordered by `discount_bps` descending, then by `min_committed_volume_units` descending, then by `tier_code` ascending, and the first entry's `tier_code` is the answer.

The two axes are evaluated jointly, not separately: a member may clear the volume floor of a rich tier and miss its compliance floor, or the reverse, and it then falls back to the best tier where **both** hold — not to the nearer of the two. Against a ladder of a default rung at 0 basis points, 0.00 percent and 0 units; a middle rung at 800 basis points, 80.00 percent and 2000 units; and a top rung at 1500 basis points, 90.00 percent and 5000 units — the preliminary figures above, 75.10 percent on 5000 units, qualify only the default rung, because 75.10 misses the 80.00 compliance floor even though 5000 units clears the top rung's volume floor. The final figures, 94.06 percent on 5000 units, sit exactly on the top rung's volume floor and clear its compliance floor, so they qualify the top rung.

**Finalizing, and the five-hop cascade it fires.** Finalizing requires the measurement to be in `PRELIMINARY` status, else `ERR_INVALID_MEASUREMENT_STATE`, and requires the commitment's `election_status` to be `REQUAL_REVIEW`, else `ERR_COMMITMENT_NOT_IN_REQUAL_REVIEW`. That second precondition also guarantees the letter of participation is countersigned, because `REQUAL_REVIEW` is reachable only by opening a requalification, which itself demands a `COUNTERSIGNED` letter, and no transition ever leaves `COUNTERSIGNED`. The hops then run in order, each caused by the writes of the one before it:

- **Hop 1.** Every money and volume field is recomputed under the final variant, `preliminary_compliance_pct` is preserved, `computed_variant` becomes `FINAL`, `qualified_tier_code` is derived from the final figures, `status` becomes `FINAL`, and the measurement's `version` increments by one.
- **Hop 2**, caused by hop 1. The commitment's `last_final_compliance_pct` is set to the final `compliance_pct`, its `current_tier_code` is overwritten with the measurement's `qualified_tier_code`, its `requalification_round` increments by one, and its `election_status` returns from `REQUAL_REVIEW` to `ACTIVE`, closing the requalification cycle. The commitment's `version` increments by one.
- **Hop 3**, caused by hop 2, and **only when `current_tier_code` actually changed**. Every price activation of this commitment whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `activation_type` is `TIER_PRICE` moves to `SUPERSEDED`. For each such row, if its `effective_to` is on or after the logical clock's current date, a replacement activation is minted: `origin` `REQUALIFICATION_CASCADE`, `activation_type` `TIER_PRICE`, `status` `ACTIVE` immediately, `effective_from` set to the logical clock's current date, `effective_to` copied from the superseded row, `tier_code_at_activation` set to the commitment's **new** `current_tier_code`, and `computed_price_cents` and `price_clamped` re-derived by the four-step derivation of §3.6 under that new tier. The replacement's `replaces_activation_id` points at the superseded row and the superseded row's `superseded_by_activation_id` points at the replacement. If instead the superseded row's `effective_to` has already passed the logical clock's current date, no replacement is minted and the superseded row's `superseded_by_activation_id` stays null. Note that the overlap guard of §3.6 ignores `SUPERSEDED` rows, which is exactly why a replacement window is allowed to begin inside the window of the row it replaced.
- **Hop 4**, caused by hop 3's supersede writes. For each commitment-and-catalog-item pair whose tier price was superseded in hop 3, an activation for that same pair whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `activation_type` is `LOCAL_EXCEPTION` — if one exists — is also moved to `SUPERSEDED`. If its `effective_to` is on or after the logical clock's current date, it too gets a replacement: `origin` `REQUALIFICATION_CASCADE`, `activation_type` `LOCAL_EXCEPTION`, `status` `ACTIVE`, `requested_price_cents` copied verbatim from the superseded row, `effective_from` set to the logical clock's current date, `effective_to` copied from the superseded row, `tier_code_at_activation` set to the commitment's new `current_tier_code`, and `computed_price_cents` and `price_clamped` re-derived under that new tier — which means the lesser-of ceiling in step 3 of the derivation moves, so a request that used to sit below the old tier price may now be ceilinged by the new one, or a request that used to be ceilinged may now stand. Again `replaces_activation_id` and `superseded_by_activation_id` are wired in both directions, and again a window whose `effective_to` is already behind the clock gets no replacement and keeps a null `superseded_by_activation_id`. A local exception is **never** re-derived on its own: it moves only because the tier price for the same catalog item moved first, which is what makes this cascade transitive rather than a fan-out.
- **Hop 5**, caused by the status writes of hops 3 and 4. For each catalog item touched anywhere in hops 3 and 4, `active_activation_count` is recomputed exactly once — once per item, not once per activation — incrementing that item's `version` by one.

When the derived `qualified_tier_code` equals the tier the commitment was already carrying, hops 3, 4 and 5 do not fire at all: no price activation is superseded, no replacement is minted, and no catalog item row is touched. Hops 1 and 2 still run, so `requalification_round` still increments and `election_status` still returns to `ACTIVE`.

A measurement can be abandoned instead of finalized. Superseding is available from `DRAFT` and from `PRELIMINARY`, optionally recording a `reason`, and it sets `status` to `SUPERSEDED` and increments the measurement's `version` by one while changing no commitment field whatsoever. A `FINAL` measurement can **never** be superseded — the attempt returns `ERR_INVALID_MEASUREMENT_STATE` — because the retier it caused is already reflected in the commitment and in the whole activation ladder beneath it.

### 3.9. Administrative Fee Accrual, Collection, Distribution and Reversal

The last workflow is how the GPO earns and shares its money. Every on-contract purchase carries an administrative fee that the supplier owes the GPO; the GPO holds back a slice, keeps a share, and rebates the rest to the members who actually bought on contract. An **administrative fee distribution** is one contract's fee settlement for one half-open period, and it moves through `ACCRUED`, then optionally `COLLECTED`, then `DISTRIBUTED`, with `REVERSED` as an unwind of a completed distribution.

A `FinanceAdministrator` opens a distribution naming `contract_id`, `period_start` and `period_end`. The contract must exist and be `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`. No distribution for the same contract in any status other than `REVERSED` may already cover the identical `period_start` and `period_end` pair, else `ERR_DUPLICATE_DISTRIBUTION_PERIOD` — a reversed settlement releases its period so it can be settled again. And `period_end` must be strictly after `period_start`, else `ERR_EFFECTIVE_WINDOW_INVALID`. The new row is created in `ACCRUED` status with `collected_fee_cents` at 0, `basis` null, `holdback_bps_applied`, `holdback_cents`, `gross_pool_cents`, `net_pool_cents`, `member_pool_cents`, `member_share_total_cents`, `undistributed_cents` and `gpo_retained_cents` all at 0, `member_shares` an empty array, and `reverse_note` null. Distributions may be listed filtered by `contract_id` and `status`.

**Accrual, computed once and never again.** `accrued_fee_cents` is computed at the moment of creation and is never recomputed by any later transition. The eligible lines are purchase lines whose `reject_reason` is `NONE` and whose `classification` is `ON_CONTRACT`, belonging to distributor files of this contract — across every member, since the fee is the contract's, not a member's — whose own half-open period lies wholly inside the distribution's half-open period, and whose `status` is `LOADED`. Files in `PARTIALLY_LOADED` status earn nothing, and neither do files that have been reversed; leakage lines and unmatched lines earn nothing, because a fee is owed only where the contract was actually used.

The lines are visited in order of `purchase_date` ascending and then `created_at` ascending, and each one's fee is `extended_cost_cents` multiplied by the contract's `admin_fee_bps`, divided by 10000, rounded half up to a whole cent. `accrued_fee_cents` is then the exact integer sum of those already-rounded per-line fees. The rounding happens per line and only per line — never on the summed spend — and the difference is real: two lines of 1150 cents at 300 basis points each yield 34.5 cents, rounded half up to 35, for a total of 70 cents, whereas rounding the aggregate would give 2300 times 300 divided by 10000, or 69 cents. The per-line figure is the one the supplier is invoiced for, so the per-line rounding is the correct one.

**Collection.** The collect action drives `ACCRUED → COLLECTED` and requires the row to be in `ACCRUED` status, else `ERR_INVALID_DISTRIBUTION_STATE`. It carries a `collected_fee_cents` body field, which must be an integer of at least 0, else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and must not exceed `accrued_fee_cents`, else `ERR_COLLECTION_EXCEEDS_ACCRUAL` — the GPO cannot collect more than it billed. A short payment is entirely legal and simply records the smaller figure. The row's `version` increments by one.

**Which basis a contract settles on.** Distribution is reachable by two mutually exclusive routes, and the contract's `share_basis` decides which one is legal. The `ACCRUED → DISTRIBUTED` route is legal only when `share_basis` is `ACCRUAL`, meaning the GPO shares what it billed and carries the collection risk itself; if `share_basis` is `COLLECTED`, distributing straight from `ACCRUED` returns `ERR_DISTRIBUTION_BASIS_NOT_COLLECTABLE`, since the cash has not been recorded yet. The `COLLECTED → DISTRIBUTED` route is legal only when `share_basis` is `COLLECTED`, meaning members share only cash actually received; if `share_basis` is `ACCRUAL`, distributing from `COLLECTED` returns `ERR_DISTRIBUTION_BASIS_MISMATCH`, because that contract's members are owed the billed figure and settling on the collected one would shortchange them. Exactly one of the two hops is available for any given contract, and any other starting status returns `ERR_INVALID_DISTRIBUTION_STATE`. Distributing pins `basis` to the contract's `share_basis` so the settlement records the rule it was computed under.

**The pool waterfall: gross, holdback, net, member pool.** `gross_pool_cents` is `accrued_fee_cents` when `basis` is `ACCRUAL` and `collected_fee_cents` when `basis` is `COLLECTED`. Once set, it is the single figure every later step and the conservation identity refer to; nothing downstream re-derives it from the accrual or the collection.

The holdback rate is read off the contract's holdback ladder keyed on the **gross** pool — never on the net pool and never on the member pool. The server takes the ladder entry with the greatest `min_gross_pool_cents` that is less than or equal to `gross_pool_cents` and writes its `holdback_bps` into `holdback_bps_applied`; if no entry qualifies, `holdback_bps_applied` is 0. Then `holdback_cents` is `gross_pool_cents` multiplied by `holdback_bps_applied`, divided by 10000, rounded half up; `net_pool_cents` is `gross_pool_cents` minus `holdback_cents`; and `member_pool_cents` is `net_pool_cents` multiplied by the contract's `member_share_bps`, divided by 10000, rounded half up. There are exactly two roundings in the waterfall and they happen in that fixed order — the holdback off the gross first, the member pool off the already-reduced net second. Rounding the member pool off the gross, or applying both rates in one multiplication, produces different cents.

Keying the ladder on the wrong pool changes the answer outright. With a holdback ladder of one entry at a 0 minimum and 0 basis points and a second at a 100,000-cent minimum and 250 basis points, and a `member_share_bps` of 6000, a gross pool of 100,200 cents selects the 250-basis-point rung, giving a `holdback_cents` of 2505, a `net_pool_cents` of 97,695 and a `member_pool_cents` of 58,617. Keying on the net pool instead would look up 97,695, fall to the 0-basis-point rung, hold back nothing, and hand the members 60,120 — the holdback would have erased itself by shrinking the pool below its own threshold. The same contract on the accrual basis with an `accrued_fee_cents` of 142,500 yields a `holdback_cents` of 3563, a `net_pool_cents` of 138,937 and a `member_pool_cents` of 83,362; on the collected basis with a `collected_fee_cents` of 100,200 it yields the 2505 / 97,695 / 58,617 figures above. Both are correct for their basis, which is why `basis` is recorded on the row.

**The member waterfall: who shares, and how the remainder lands.** The member pool is split among the contract's *eligible* commitments: those whose `election_status` is `ACTIVE`, whose `lop_status` is `COUNTERSIGNED`, whose `leakage_flag` is false, and whose `ytd_on_contract_spend_cents` is strictly greater than 0. A flagged member is excluded from the rebate entirely for that period — that is the commercial teeth behind the leakage threshold. The total weight is the sum of those commitments' `ytd_on_contract_spend_cents`.

When the eligible set is empty, or the total weight is 0, `member_shares` stays an empty array, `member_share_total_cents` is 0, and the whole `member_pool_cents` lands in `undistributed_cents`. Otherwise each eligible member's exact share is `member_pool_cents` multiplied by that commitment's `ytd_on_contract_spend_cents`, divided by the total weight. Each exact share is **floored** to a whole cent — floored, not rounded — and the shortfall, which is `member_pool_cents` minus the sum of the floors and is always strictly less than the number of eligible members, is then handed out one cent at a time: the member with the largest fractional part gets the first cent, the next largest the next, and ties are broken by the member's `created_at` ascending — never by identifier. This makes the allocation exhaustive and deterministic.

Each `member_shares` entry records `member_id`, `commitment_id`, `weight_cents` — that commitment's `ytd_on_contract_spend_cents`, the weight actually used — and `share_cents`, the final allocated amount including any remainder cent. The array is ordered by `share_cents` descending, then by the member's `created_at` ascending. `member_share_total_cents` is the sum of the `share_cents` values and, in this branch, equals `member_pool_cents` exactly, so `undistributed_cents` is 0.

Worked through: a `member_pool_cents` of 58,617 split between weights of 4,750,000 and 1,900,000, a total weight of 6,650,000, gives exact shares of 41,869.28… and 16,747.71…; the floors are 41,869 and 16,747, summing to 58,616, one cent short; the larger fractional part is the second member's .71 against the first member's .28, so the remainder cent goes there and the final shares are 41,869 and 16,748, summing to exactly 58,617.

The empty-basis branch is worth stating in the same numbers. A `gross_pool_cents` of 70 with a `holdback_bps_applied` of 0 and a `member_share_bps` of 6000 gives a `member_pool_cents` of 42. If no commitment is eligible, `member_shares` is empty, `member_share_total_cents` is 0, `undistributed_cents` is 42, and `gpo_retained_cents` is 28 — and 0 plus 42 plus 28 is 70, the gross pool.

**Completing the distribution.** `gpo_retained_cents` is `gross_pool_cents` minus `member_share_total_cents` minus `undistributed_cents`, which is exactly the holdback plus the non-member slice of the net pool. Every eligible member's `fee_credit_balance_cents` is then credited by that member's `share_cents`, incrementing each member's `version` by one, the distribution's `status` becomes `DISTRIBUTED`, and its `version` increments by one. The identity `member_share_total_cents` plus `undistributed_cents` plus `gpo_retained_cents` equals `gross_pool_cents` holds on every distributed row.

**Reversal.** A distribution in `DISTRIBUTED` status can be reversed, optionally carrying a `reason` that is stored as `reverse_note` and is null when omitted. Reversal debits each member listed in `member_shares` by exactly the `share_cents` recorded for that member, incrementing each member's `version` by one; the credits are read back off the stored array rather than re-derived, so a reversal returns precisely what was paid even if the underlying spend has since moved. Then `holdback_bps_applied`, `holdback_cents`, `net_pool_cents`, `member_pool_cents`, `member_share_total_cents` and `gpo_retained_cents` are all set to 0, `member_shares` is emptied, and `undistributed_cents` is set to `gross_pool_cents` — which keeps the conservation identity true on the reversed row, with the whole gross pool now sitting undistributed. `accrued_fee_cents`, `collected_fee_cents`, `gross_pool_cents` and `basis` are left untouched as the record of what was settled. `status` becomes `REVERSED` and the `version` increments by one. A row still in `ACCRUED` or `COLLECTED` has paid nobody and cannot be reversed: the attempt returns `ERR_INVALID_DISTRIBUTION_STATE`.
