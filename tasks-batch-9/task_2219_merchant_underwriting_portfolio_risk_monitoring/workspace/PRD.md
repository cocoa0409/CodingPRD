# Merchant Underwriting & Portfolio Risk Monitoring API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A payment facilitator carries the credit and fraud risk of every business it boards. Between the moment an applicant asks to process cards and the moment a merchant is either paid or terminated sits a chain of judgements — is this a real business, who owns it, is the category permitted, how much of each settlement must be withheld as reserve, is the daily transaction pattern consistent with legitimate commerce, and is the dispute experience inside network tolerance. Today those judgements live in spreadsheets, vendor portals and the memory of individual analysts. This API replaces that with a single risk-operations system of record.

The system spans four connected domains. **Underwriting** takes an applicant from a draft application through business verification and beneficial-ownership screening, prices the reserve requirement under one of two competing methodologies, and puts the approve-or-decline decision in front of a risk committee. **Portfolio monitoring** ingests one immutable row of processing activity per merchant per day, sweeps the book with four detectors, and raises alerts that an analyst triages. **Money movement** withholds reserve from each settlement period, funds chargeback losses through a fixed waterfall, defers or freezes payouts as the merchant's risk posture changes, and releases matured reserve tranches on a cooling schedule. **Enforcement and remedy** covers chargeback remediation programs, network monitoring escalation, termination under segregation of duties, and the merchant's right to appeal and be reinstated on re-priced terms.

Two design commitments run through every requirement below. First, **every number is reproducible**: money is integer cents, rates are integer basis points, and the order in which values are rounded, summed and clamped is part of the specification rather than an implementation detail. Second, **no single operator can act alone on an irreversible decision**: the user who proposes a termination cannot approve it, the analyst who opens a remediation program cannot activate it, and neither the proposer nor the approver of a termination may decide the merchant's appeal against it.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contracts that hold for every endpoint in the system. Where §3 describes what a workflow means, §2 describes how the request is authenticated, ordered, rounded and versioned.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which it acts. There are exactly six roles, spelled exactly as follows: `Applicant`, `MerchantUser`, `UnderwritingOfficer`, `RiskAnalyst`, `RiskCommitteeApprover` and `PortfolioController`. A request missing either header, or presenting an `X-User-Role` that is not one of those six strings, is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). A syntactically valid request from a role that is not listed for the target operation in §4 is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403).

Two of the six roles are entity-backed, meaning `X-User-ID` is a resource identifier rather than a staff identifier.

- When `X-User-Role` is `Applicant`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `MerchantApplication`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `MerchantApplication` compare the resource's application reference — the application `id` in the path, or the `application_id` supplied in the body — to `X-User-ID`. Application creation is the single exception, because no owning application exists yet: there the check is skipped unless `supersedes_application_id` is supplied, in which case `X-User-ID` must equal that superseded application's `id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `MerchantUser`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Merchant`; ownership checks compare the merchant `id` in the path, or the `merchant_id` supplied in the body, to `X-User-ID`.

A mismatch on either check is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403).

The remaining four roles are opaque operator identities. When `X-User-Role` is `UnderwritingOfficer`, `RiskAnalyst`, `RiskCommitteeApprover` or `PortfolioController`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it wherever a transition attributes an action to its actor — as `created_by_user_id` on a category rule, `verified_by_user_id` on a verification, `assessed_by_user_id` on a credit assessment, `decision_officer_user_id` on an application, `ingested_by_user_id` on an activity row, `reviewed_by_user_id` on an alert, `recorded_by_user_id` on a reserve ledger entry, `opened_by_user_id` and `activated_by_user_id` on a remediation program, `proposed_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` on a termination decision, `reviewer_user_id` on an appeal, and `generated_by_user_id` on a forecast — but it is never compared to any entity's `id`.

`Applicant` alone may create an application, attach a beneficial owner, submit and withdraw. `MerchantUser` alone may file an appeal and respond to an appeal information request. `UnderwritingOfficer` alone may publish a category rule, record a verification, screen an owner, advance an application to underwriting and create a credit assessment. `RiskAnalyst` alone may ingest day activity, run the monitoring sweep, review an alert, place a funding hold, open a remediation program, evaluate a remediation month and propose a termination. `RiskCommitteeApprover` alone may decide an application, release a funding hold, activate a remediation program, approve or reject a termination, and review, question and decide an appeal. `PortfolioController` alone may create, pay and reverse settlement batches, request a reserve release, execute a termination and generate a portfolio forecast. All six roles may set the logical clock.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and Collection Filters

List endpoints are paginated through two query parameters. `limit` is the maximum number of rows returned; it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` is the number of rows skipped; it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` outside 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is a schema violation and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Every list response reports `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope.

Unless a particular collection declares otherwise in §4, list results are sorted by `created_at` ascending. Several collections declare a domain-natural order instead — category rules by `mcc_code` ascending, an application's verification attempts by `attempt_index` ascending, an application's credit assessments by `assessment_sequence` ascending, a merchant's activity rows by `business_date` ascending, a merchant's settlement batches by `period_start_date` ascending, and a merchant's remediation programs by `window_start_date` ascending.

Collections also accept optional equality filters, all of which narrow the result set before pagination is applied: `disposition` on category rules; `state` and `mcc_code` on applications; `status` on an application's beneficial owners; `method` on an application's credit assessments; `account_state`, `funding_state` and `remediation_required` on merchants; `from_date` and `to_date` on a merchant's day activity, both bounds inclusive; `state` and `alert_type` on a merchant's alerts; `payout_state` on a merchant's settlement batches; `state` and `reserve_type` on reserve accounts; `entry_type` on a batch's ledger entries; `entry_type` and `reason_code` on a reserve account's ledger entries; `state` on remediation programs, termination decisions and appeals; and `method` on portfolio forecasts.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Fields and the Logical Clock

Every resource is addressed by an `id`, which is a server-minted UUID carrying no type prefix, and every path variable in this API is that `id`. Most resources additionally carry three system-managed audit fields: `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording creation; `updated_at`, the same format, refreshed on every mutation of the row; and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by one on every mutation, including mutations that only touch derived fields such as a recomputed trailing metric or a re-pinned reserve percentage.

Three record types are immutable ledger or reference rows and therefore carry only `id` and `created_at` — they have neither `updated_at` nor `version`: `ProhibitedCategoryRule`, `ReserveLedgerEntry` and `MerchantDayActivity`. A day of activity, once ingested, is never edited; a ledger entry, once appended, is never amended; a category rule, once published, is never revised.

All date and datetime rules in this system read a **logical clock** rather than wall-clock time. Reserve maturity cutoffs, the appeal window, payout due dates, activity-date checks and monitoring-run regression checks all measure against it. Before any caller moves it, the clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time. Any role may advance or rewind it by supplying a `now_utc` instant to the clock-setting capability; moving the clock recomputes nothing and mutates no entity.

### 2.5. Money, Basis Points and Rounding Discipline

Money is always an integer number of cents. Percentages and ratios are always integer basis points, where 10000 basis points is one hundred percent. Ownership percentages are the single exception: `ownership_pct` and the derived `owner_ownership_pct_total` are decimal percentages carried to two decimal places.

Rounding is **half up**, and it happens only where this document says it happens. Three consequences are load-bearing and are repeated in the workflows that depend on them. The reserve cap is produced by rounding the monthly hold once and then multiplying the rounded integer by the number of cap months — never by rounding after the multiplication. The reserve withheld from a settlement batch is produced by rounding each day line separately and then adding the rounded integers — never by adding the day volumes and rounding once. A per-merchant expected loss applies both percentage factors before a single rounding step, not one rounding per factor. Where a formula calls for rounding up to the next whole number or truncating down to a whole number, that is stated explicitly and half-up rounding does not apply.

A ratio whose denominator is zero evaluates to exactly 0. That is never an error condition: a merchant with no transactions in a window has a chargeback ratio of 0 basis points, and a remediation month with no transactions is compliant.

### 2.6. The Rejection Ladder: Validation Precedence

When a single request violates more than one rule, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fully determined. Checks are evaluated in the fixed precedence order below, and the first failure wins. This ordering is normative: it is what makes a mid-flight state change surface as a state conflict rather than being masked by a downstream fiscal check, and vice versa.

1. **Authentication** — headers present and role recognised: `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. **Authorization** — the acting role appears in the operation's role list in §4: `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403).
3. **Schema** — required body and query fields present, enum members recognised, numeric ranges and string formats respected, `limit` within 1 through 100, `offset` not negative, and every date well-formed as `YYYY-MM-DD`: `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400).
4. **Existence, in this exact sub-order.** Referenced applications first — including an `application_id` in a body and a `supersedes_application_id` — with `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_FOUND` (404). Then the beneficial owner named by an owner-screening request, with `ERR_OWNER_NOT_FOUND` (404). Then any merchant named in a path or body, with `ERR_MERCHANT_NOT_FOUND` (404). Then the reserve account named by a reserve-account fetch, a reserve-account ledger listing or a reserve release request, with `ERR_RESERVE_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND` (404). Then the settlement batch named by a batch fetch, a batch ledger listing, a payout or a reversal, with `ERR_BATCH_NOT_FOUND` (404). Then the alert named by an alert review, with `ERR_ALERT_NOT_FOUND` (404). Then the remediation program named by a program fetch, activation or month evaluation, with `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND` (404). Then the termination decision named by a decision fetch, approval, rejection, execution, its appeal listing, or the `termination_decision_id` of an appeal filing, with `ERR_TERMINATION_DECISION_NOT_FOUND` (404). Then the appeal named by an appeal fetch, review, information request, response or decision, with `ERR_APPEAL_NOT_FOUND` (404). Then the forecast named by a forecast fetch, with `ERR_FORECAST_NOT_FOUND` (404).
5. **Ownership** — for the two entity-backed roles, `X-User-ID` matches the resource being acted on: `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403).
6. **Optimistic concurrency** — `expected_application_version` on credit-assessment creation, then `expected_reserve_account_version` on a reserve release request: `ERR_STALE_APPLICATION_VERSION` (412) and `ERR_STALE_RESERVE_ACCOUNT_VERSION` (412) respectively.
7. **State guards, in this exact sub-order.** Application state, with `ERR_APPLICATION_STATE_INVALID` (409). Then merchant `account_state`, with `ERR_MERCHANT_ACCOUNT_STATE_INVALID` (409). Then merchant `funding_state`, with `ERR_FUNDING_STATE_INVALID` (409). Then reserve-account closure, with `ERR_RESERVE_ACCOUNT_CLOSED` (409). Then batch `payout_state`, with `ERR_BATCH_STATE_INVALID` (409). Then alert state, with `ERR_ALERT_STATE_INVALID` (409). Then remediation-program state, with `ERR_PROGRAM_STATE_INVALID` (409). Then termination-decision state, with `ERR_TERMINATION_STATE_INVALID` (409). Then appeal state, with `ERR_APPEAL_STATE_INVALID` (409).
8. **Underwriting-gate and duty-separation checks, in this exact sub-order.** Prohibited category, with `ERR_PROHIBITED_BUSINESS_CATEGORY` (422). Then a sanctioned beneficial owner, with `ERR_SANCTIONED_BENEFICIAL_OWNER` (422). Then incomplete owner screening, with `ERR_OWNER_SCREENING_INCOMPLETE` (409). Then segregation of duties, with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). Then ownership coverage, with `ERR_OWNERSHIP_COVERAGE_INSUFFICIENT` (422). Then the authoritative verification result, with `ERR_VERIFICATION_NOT_PASSED` (409).
9. **Referential business preconditions, in this exact sub-order**: `ERR_CREDIT_ASSESSMENT_REQUIRED` (409), then `ERR_BENEFICIAL_OWNERS_REQUIRED` (409), then `ERR_SUPERSEDED_APPLICATION_INVALID` (409), then `ERR_APPEAL_DECISION_NOT_EXECUTED` (409), then `ERR_REMEDIATION_NOT_REQUIRED` (409).
10. **Temporal rules, in this exact sub-order**: `ERR_ACTIVITY_DATE_IN_FUTURE` (422), then `ERR_MONITORING_RUN_DATE_REGRESSION` (409), then `ERR_REMEDIATION_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (409), then `ERR_RELEASE_TRANCHE_COOLING_VIOLATION` (409), then `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_EXPIRED` (409), then `ERR_SETTLEMENT_PERIOD_INVALID` (422), then `ERR_SETTLEMENT_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (409), then `ERR_PAYOUT_NOT_DUE` (409).
11. **Uniqueness, sequence and content rules, in this exact sub-order**: `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTIVITY_DATE` (409), then `ERR_DUPLICATE_CATEGORY_RULE` (409), then `ERR_DUPLICATE_APPEAL` (409), then `ERR_MONTH_INDEX_OUT_OF_SEQUENCE` (409), then `ERR_EMPTY_SETTLEMENT_PERIOD` (409), then `ERR_GROSS_VOLUME_MISMATCH` (422).
12. **Fiscal ceilings, in this exact sub-order**: `ERR_MONTHLY_VOLUME_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (422), then `ERR_NO_RELEASABLE_BALANCE` (409), then `ERR_RELEASE_AMOUNT_EXCEEDS_RELEASABLE` (422).

Two orderings inside this ladder deserve emphasis because two failing conditions frequently co-occur. On a reserve release request, the merchant's `funding_state` is checked before the tranche cooling buffer, and the cooling buffer is checked before the releasable balance, which is itself checked before the requested amount. On a settlement batch, the period's shape and overlap are checked before the period is required to be non-empty, the non-empty check precedes the gross-volume reconciliation, and the reconciliation precedes the trailing-volume ceiling.

### 2.7. Two Independent Merchant State Dimensions

A `Merchant` carries two orthogonal state dimensions, and confusing them is the most common source of incorrect behaviour in this domain. `account_state` is the **risk posture** and takes the values `ACTIVE`, `RESTRICTED` and `TERMINATED`; it gates activity ingest, settlement, reserve release and remediation. `funding_state` is the **payout posture** and takes the values `NORMAL`, `DELAYED` and `HELD`; it gates only the movement of money out to the merchant. A newly onboarded merchant begins at `account_state` `ACTIVE` and `funding_state` `NORMAL`.

A transition on one dimension never implies a mirror transition on the other except through the explicitly enumerated cascade links in §2.8 and §3. In particular, placing a funding hold on a merchant whose `account_state` is `ACTIVE` leaves `account_state` untouched, and lifting a funding hold on a `RESTRICTED` merchant returns `funding_state` to `DELAYED` rather than `NORMAL` — the two dimensions are resolved independently. The `delayed_payout_days` field is the deferral applied to new and cascaded batches: it is 0 whenever `funding_state` is `NORMAL` or `HELD`, and 7 whenever `funding_state` is `DELAYED`.

### 2.8. Named Cascades and Hierarchical Consistency

Six multi-link cascades exist. Each begins with one client-triggered transition and then propagates automatically, with no further client request, through a fixed chain of dependent transitions. Every entity touched by any link has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented, unless the entity is one of the three that carry no `version` at all. Whenever a cascade fans out over a merchant's settlement batches, the batches are processed in `period_end_date` ascending order, breaking ties by `created_at` ascending; whenever it fans out over a merchant's alerts, the alerts are processed in `created_at` ascending order.

- **CB-BREACH** — a monitoring sweep raises or re-raises a severe chargeback-threshold alert, which restricts the account, which delays the funding, which pushes out each scheduled batch.
- **ALERT-ESCALATION** — an analyst escalates a card-testing or laundering alert, which holds the funding, which freezes each unpaid batch. The account's risk posture is not touched.
- **REMEDIATION-PASS** — a remediation month evaluation passes the program, which lifts the account restriction, which returns funding to normal, which re-schedules each delayed batch and closes each escalated chargeback-threshold alert.
- **REMEDIATION-FAIL** — a remediation month evaluation fails the program on high volume, which escalates it to the network program tier, which holds the funding.
- **TERMINATION-EXEC** — a controller executes an approved termination, which terminates the account, which holds the funding, which freezes each unpaid batch and closes the reserve account, which in turn closes every non-terminal alert.
- **APPEAL-GRANT** — the committee grants an appeal, which reverses the executed termination decision, which reinstates the account as restricted, which converts the funding hold into a delay, which re-schedules each held batch as delayed and reopens the reserve account.

Denormalised mirrors are refreshed as part of the same operation that changes their source, never lazily. `Merchant.reserve_balance_cents` mirrors the reserve account's `held_balance_cents`; `pending_payout_cents` is the sum of `net_payout_cents` over the merchant's batches whose `payout_state` is `SCHEDULED`, `DELAYED` or `HELD`; `open_alert_count` counts the merchant's alerts whose state is `OPEN`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `ESCALATED`; and `lifetime_reversed_cents` accumulates over reversals as described in §3.8.

### 2.9. Optimistic Concurrency on Applications and Reserve Accounts

Exactly two write operations take a version pre-condition, because in both cases the operation is legitimate on state grounds and the only thing that can be wrong is the client's view of the parent record.

Creating a credit assessment requires `expected_application_version` as a non-optional body field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `MerchantApplication`. A new assessment legitimately supersedes the previous one — it re-pins the application's `current_reserve_pct_bps`, `current_reserve_cap_cents`, `current_reserve_type` and `current_assessment_method` — so it cannot be refused on state grounds. If the supplied value does not equal the application's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_APPLICATION_VERSION` and HTTP 412, which is what stops an officer from re-pinning reserve terms computed from a view of the application another writer has already replaced.

Requesting a reserve release requires `expected_reserve_account_version` as a non-optional body field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `ReserveAccount`. A second release against a still-releasable balance is also a legitimate operation, so it too cannot be refused on state grounds. If the supplied value does not equal the reserve account's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_RESERVE_ACCOUNT_VERSION` and HTTP 412, preventing a release sized against a superseded `held_balance_cents`.

No other operation accepts a version field, and supplying one elsewhere has no effect.

### 2.10. Reverse-Reachability Reads and Left-to-Right Path Validation

Entities in this system are peer collections linked by body-level foreign keys; no entity is addressed through a parent path segment. Nested paths exist only as **reverse-reachability reads** — a collection hanging off the record that owns it, such as an application's beneficial owners, verifications, credit assessments, superseding re-applications and onboarded merchant, or a merchant's activity rows, alerts, settlement batches, reserve account, remediation programs, termination decisions and appeals.

For any path carrying more than one identifier, and for any request that also names a resource in its body, identifiers are resolved left to right and body references are resolved after path references. The first identifier that does not resolve determines the error, following the sub-order in §2.6 step 4. A reverse-reachability read against a non-existent owner therefore returns that owner's not-found error rather than an empty list. The listing of the merchant onboarded from an application is the only channel through which a client learns the identifier of a merchant created by cascade, and it returns either zero or one row.

### 2.11. Segregation of Duties

Four operations enforce that the acting `X-User-ID` has not already taken a conflicting step on the same object. Approving a termination requires an `X-User-ID` different from the decision's `proposed_by_user_id`; rejecting one carries the same restriction. Activating a remediation program requires an `X-User-ID` different from the program's `opened_by_user_id`. Deciding an appeal requires an `X-User-ID` that is neither the `proposed_by_user_id` nor the `approved_by_user_id` of the appealed termination decision. Any of these four violations is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403), which sits above the referential, temporal and fiscal families in the ladder of §2.6 but below every state guard.

### 2.12. Reserve Ledger Invariants

A reserve account's balances are never edited in place; they are derived from an append-only ledger of `ReserveLedgerEntry` rows. Each entry carries a positive `amount_cents` magnitude, with direction expressed by `entry_type`: `HOLD` adds funds to the reserve, `RELEASE` returns funds to the merchant, and `OFFSET_LOSS` consumes reserve to cover a chargeback the settlement could not fund. `total_accrued_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` over the account's `HOLD` entries, `released_to_date_cents` the sum over its `RELEASE` entries, and `offset_to_date_cents` the sum over its `OFFSET_LOSS` entries; `held_balance_cents` is `total_accrued_cents` less `released_to_date_cents` less `offset_to_date_cents`.

The **conservation invariant** must therefore hold after every append: `total_accrued_cents` equals `held_balance_cents` plus `released_to_date_cents` plus `offset_to_date_cents`. A companion **per-batch invariant** must hold for every settlement batch at all times: `gross_volume_cents` equals `net_payout_cents` plus `reserve_hold_cents` plus `loss_offset_from_settlement_cents`.

Every entry also records why it exists, in `reason_code`, one of `SETTLEMENT_HOLD`, `SCHEDULED_RELEASE`, `BATCH_REVERSAL_RELEASE`, `LOSS_OFFSET` and `REINSTATEMENT_RELEASE`; when it exists, in `effective_date`, the date on which it takes economic effect; and, where the entry originated from settling or reversing a period, which batch produced it, in `settlement_batch_id` — a field that is null for scheduled release requests and for reinstatement releases. `tranche_start_date` and `tranche_end_date` are populated only on scheduled release entries and are null on every `HOLD` and `OFFSET_LOSS` entry. `recorded_by_user_id` holds the `X-User-ID` whose request produced the entry, and is null for entries written by an automated sweep rather than a request.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Category Screening Rules and Application Intake

Before any applicant arrives, an `UnderwritingOfficer` publishes the facilitator's category policy as a set of screening rules. Each rule names an `mcc_code` of exactly four digits, a human-readable `category` label such as `INBOUND_TELEMARKETING`, and a `disposition` of either `PROHIBITED` or `RESTRICTED_REVIEW`. A `PROHIBITED` category blocks underwriting outright; a `RESTRICTED_REVIEW` category permits it but adds `reserve_uplift_bps` — an integer from 0 through 2000, defaulting to 0 when omitted — to the reserve percentage computed later. Only one rule may exist per `mcc_code`; a second registration for the same code is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CATEGORY_RULE`. Publishing a rule is not purely additive: the server immediately recomputes `mcc_disposition` on every `MerchantApplication` still in `DRAFT` or `SUBMITTED` whose `mcc_code` matches the new rule, so a policy change reaches applications already in flight. The publishing officer is recorded as `created_by_user_id`.

An `Applicant` opens the funnel by creating an application, which begins life in `DRAFT`. The payload describes the business and its request: `legal_business_name`, a non-blank string of 1 through 200 characters; `tax_id`, an employer identification number written as two digits, a hyphen and seven digits; `mcc_code`, exactly four digits; `country_code`, either `US` or `CA`; `requested_monthly_volume_cents`, at least 100000 cents of monthly card volume; and `average_ticket_cents`, the applicant's declared average transaction size, at least 1 cent. Three further inputs exist purely to price risk and are discussed again in §3.4: `avg_delivery_lag_days`, the average number of days between authorization and delivery of the goods or service, from 0 through 365; `prior_processing_history_months`, months of verifiable prior card-processing history, from 0 through 600; `credit_score`, the principal's bureau score, from 300 through 850; and `declared_chargeback_ratio_bps`, the applicant's declared historical chargeback ratio in basis points, from 0 through 10000.

At creation the server sets `state` to `DRAFT`, `owner_ownership_pct_total` to 0.00, `outstanding_info_items` to an empty list, `verification_attempt_count` to 0, and derives `mcc_disposition` from the single screening rule whose `mcc_code` equals the application's — `PROHIBITED` or `RESTRICTED_REVIEW` from that rule's `disposition`, and `ALLOWED` when no rule matches. `decline_reason_code`, `current_assessment_method`, `current_reserve_pct_bps`, `current_reserve_cap_cents`, `current_reserve_type`, `decision_officer_user_id` and `decided_at` all start null.

A business that was previously terminated may come back. Such a re-application supplies `supersedes_application_id`, naming the prior application it replaces; the field defaults to null for a first-time applicant. The named application must itself be `APPROVED` and the `Merchant` onboarded from it must have `account_state` `TERMINATED`; anything else — a superseded application still under review, or one whose merchant is still trading — is rejected with `ERR_SUPERSEDED_APPLICATION_INVALID`. Because the applicant identity for a re-application is the superseded application, `X-User-ID` must equal that prior application's `id`, as described in §2.1. Underwriting staff can walk the link in the other direction to see every re-application that names a given prior application.

Beneficial owners are attached while the application is still in `DRAFT`; an attempt from any other state is rejected with `ERR_APPLICATION_STATE_INVALID`. Each owner carries the `application_id` it is disclosed on, a `full_name` of 1 through 200 non-blank characters, an `ownership_pct` from 0.01 through 100 expressed to two decimal places, a `date_of_birth` written as `YYYY-MM-DD`, a `government_id_last4` of exactly four digits, and a `country_code` of `US` or `CA`. A new owner starts with `status` `PENDING`, with `sanctions_hit`, `pep_flag` and `id_verified` all false and `screened_at` null. Attaching an owner immediately recomputes the application's `owner_ownership_pct_total` as the sum of `ownership_pct` across all owners of that application, carried to two decimal places.

Submission moves the application from `DRAFT` to `SUBMITTED`, and is also the re-entry point from `INFO_REQUESTED` back to `SUBMITTED` — an application may lap `SUBMITTED` to `IN_VERIFICATION` to `INFO_REQUESTED` and back to `SUBMITTED` any number of times. Only the owning applicant may submit, so `X-User-ID` must equal the application's `id`. At least one beneficial owner must exist, or the request is rejected with `ERR_BENEFICIAL_OWNERS_REQUIRED`. Each submission clears `outstanding_info_items` to an empty list and recomputes both `owner_ownership_pct_total` and `mcc_disposition`, so the figures the underwriter reads are the ones the applicant last asserted.

An applicant may abandon the application at any point before a decision. Withdrawal is accepted from `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `IN_VERIFICATION`, `INFO_REQUESTED` and `UNDERWRITING`, moves `state` to `WITHDRAWN`, and stamps `decided_at` with the logical clock; no `Merchant` is created. `WITHDRAWN`, `APPROVED` and `DECLINED` are the three terminal application states, and a request against a terminal application is rejected with `ERR_APPLICATION_STATE_INVALID`.

### 3.2. Verification, Owner Screening and the Information-Request Loop

Once an application reaches `SUBMITTED`, an `UnderwritingOfficer` records the outcome of a know-your-business vendor check. The recording payload names the `application_id` and four independent boolean match flags: `registry_match`, whether the legal name matched the state business registry; `tax_id_match`, whether the tax identifier matched the federal record; `bank_account_match`, whether ownership of the settlement bank account matched the legal entity; and `address_match`, whether the principal place of business matched the registry address. Recording is only declared from `SUBMITTED`; from any other state the request is rejected with `ERR_APPLICATION_STATE_INVALID`.

The server derives the rest. `mismatch_count` is how many of the four flags are false. `status` is `PASSED` when `mismatch_count` is zero — that is, when all four flags are true — and `FAILED` otherwise. `attempt_index` is a one-based ordinal assigned as the application's previous greatest `attempt_index` plus one, and the attempt bearing the greatest `attempt_index` is the authoritative one for every downstream check. The application's `verification_attempt_count` is incremented by one, `verified_by_user_id` records the acting officer, and the application advances `SUBMITTED → IN_VERIFICATION`.

A failing attempt does not stop there. The `FAILED` result itself drives the application onward from `IN_VERIFICATION` to `INFO_REQUESTED`, with `outstanding_info_items` set to the uppercase names of exactly those checks that came back false, listed in the fixed order `REGISTRY_MATCH`, `ADDRESS_MATCH`, `TAX_ID_MATCH`, `BANK_ACCOUNT_MATCH`. An officer may also request information by hand, supplying `outstanding_info_items` explicitly; that operation is declared from both `IN_VERIFICATION` and `UNDERWRITING`, and in both cases sets `outstanding_info_items` to the supplied labels and moves the application to `INFO_REQUESTED`. The applicant's next submission clears the list and starts the lap again.

Owner screening runs in parallel with verification. An officer applies a vendor result to one beneficial owner while that owner's application is in either `SUBMITTED` or `IN_VERIFICATION`; from any other application state the screen is rejected with `ERR_APPLICATION_STATE_INVALID`. The payload carries three booleans — `sanctions_hit`, `pep_flag` and `id_verified` — which are stored verbatim, along with `screened_at` set to the logical clock. The owner's `status` is then derived in this pinned order: `SANCTIONS_HIT` when `sanctions_hit` is true; otherwise `ID_UNVERIFIED` when `id_verified` is false; otherwise `FLAGGED_PEP` when `pep_flag` is true; otherwise `CLEARED`.

Screening is re-runnable, and this matters commercially: sanctions lists produce false positives. A later vendor result reporting no sanctions match, no politically-exposed-person match and a verified identity document moves an owner from `SANCTIONS_HIT`, from `ID_UNVERIFIED` or from `FLAGGED_PEP` to `CLEARED`, overwriting the three flags and `screened_at`, for as long as the application remains in `SUBMITTED` or `IN_VERIFICATION`. Note the asymmetry the gate in §3.3 depends on: `FLAGGED_PEP` is a disclosure outcome that does not block progress, whereas `SANCTIONS_HIT` does, and `PENDING` — an owner nobody has screened yet — blocks progress too.

### 3.3. The Underwriting Gate

Advancing an application from `IN_VERIFICATION` to `UNDERWRITING` is the single point at which the facilitator commits to pricing the risk, and it is guarded by five substantive checks in addition to the state guard. The operation is performed by an `UnderwritingOfficer` and accepts one optional field, `reviewer_note`, which is not persisted on the application.

The five checks are evaluated in this order, and the first failure is the response. First, the application's `mcc_disposition` must not be `PROHIBITED`; a prohibited category is rejected with `ERR_PROHIBITED_BUSINESS_CATEGORY`. Second, no beneficial owner of the application may have `status` `SANCTIONS_HIT`; otherwise `ERR_SANCTIONED_BENEFICIAL_OWNER`. Third, no beneficial owner may still have `status` `PENDING`, because an unscreened owner is an unknown, not a clean one; otherwise `ERR_OWNER_SCREENING_INCOMPLETE`. Fourth, `owner_ownership_pct_total` must be at least 75.00 percent, so that the facilitator has identified at least three quarters of the equity; otherwise `ERR_OWNERSHIP_COVERAGE_INSUFFICIENT`. Fifth, the `BusinessVerification` with the greatest `attempt_index` must exist and have `status` `PASSED`; a missing or failing authoritative attempt is rejected with `ERR_VERIFICATION_NOT_PASSED`. Placing the sanctions check ahead of the ownership-coverage check is deliberate: a sanctioned owner is a compliance stop, and it must surface as such even when the disclosed ownership is also thin.

An application that clears all five moves to `UNDERWRITING` and nothing else on the row changes beyond `state`, `updated_at` and `version`. Note that this gate reads sibling rows — the statuses of `BeneficialOwner` and `BusinessVerification` records — rather than any field of the application itself, so a screening result recorded a moment earlier changes the gate's answer immediately.

### 3.4. Reserve Requirement Determination

With the application in `UNDERWRITING`, an `UnderwritingOfficer` prices the reserve by creating a credit assessment. The request supplies `application_id`, a `method` of either `LAG_WEIGHTED` or `CHARGEBACK_WEIGHTED`, and `expected_application_version` as described in §2.9. The assessment may be run repeatedly, under either method, and each run receives an `assessment_sequence` equal to the application's previous greatest `assessment_sequence` plus one; the assessment with the greatest `assessment_sequence` is the authoritative one. `assessed_by_user_id` records the officer. The only inputs are the one application being assessed and, if one exists, the single screening rule whose `mcc_code` equals the application's; no other application's data participates.

**Step one, the credit band.** `credit_band` is `A` when `credit_score` is 720 or greater; otherwise `B` when it is 660 or greater; otherwise `C` when it is 600 or greater; otherwise `D`. `base_reserve_bps` follows from the band: 50 basis points for `A`, 500 for `B`, 900 for `C` and 1500 for `D`.

**Step two, the uplifts that both methods share.** `thin_file_uplift_bps` is 300 basis points when `prior_processing_history_months` is fewer than 6 — a thin file, in other words less than half a year of verifiable history — and 0 otherwise. `mcc_uplift_bps` is the `reserve_uplift_bps` of the screening rule whose `mcc_code` equals the application's `mcc_code`, but only when that rule's `disposition` is `RESTRICTED_REVIEW`; when no rule matches, or the matching rule is `PROHIBITED`, `mcc_uplift_bps` is 0.

**Step three, where the two methods diverge.** Both first compute one average day of requested volume: `requested_monthly_volume_cents` divided by 30, rounded half up to whole cents.

Under `LAG_WEIGHTED` — the forward-looking view, which prices exposure to goods not yet shipped — `lag_uplift_bps` is 150 basis points for every started week of delivery lag: take `avg_delivery_lag_days` divided by 7, round that quotient **up** to the next whole number, and multiply by 150. `chargeback_uplift_bps` is 0, and `declared_chargeback_ratio_bps` is ignored entirely. The raw exposure is `requested_monthly_volume_cents` multiplied by the lesser of `avg_delivery_lag_days` and 30, divided by 30, rounded half up — the projected in-flight share of one month's volume, with a lag beyond 30 days contributing no more than a full month.

Under `CHARGEBACK_WEIGHTED` — the historical view, which prices realised dispute experience — `chargeback_uplift_bps` is `declared_chargeback_ratio_bps` multiplied by 3, truncated **down** to a whole number. `lag_uplift_bps` is 0, and `avg_delivery_lag_days` is ignored entirely. The raw exposure is `requested_monthly_volume_cents` multiplied by `declared_chargeback_ratio_bps`, divided by 10000, multiplied by 4 for the four-period dispute horizon, and rounded half up once at the end of that chain.

Whichever method ran, `exposure_estimate_cents` is the greater of the raw exposure and the one-average-day figure, so estimated in-flight exposure never falls below a single day of requested volume. For identical application inputs the two methods return different values for `reserve_pct_bps`, `exposure_estimate_cents` and `reserve_cap_cents`, and frequently a different `reserve_type`.

**Step four, the clamp.** Add five components together: `base_reserve_bps`, `lag_uplift_bps`, `chargeback_uplift_bps`, `thin_file_uplift_bps` and `mcc_uplift_bps`. Take the greater of that sum and a floor of 100 basis points, then take the lesser of the result and a ceiling of 2500 basis points; the result is `reserve_pct_bps`. The floor is live in practice, not decorative: band `A`'s base of 50 basis points sits below it, so a pristine applicant in an unrestricted category with a thick file still reserves one percent.

**Step five, the cap.** The rounding order here is pinned. First compute the monthly hold: `requested_monthly_volume_cents` multiplied by `reserve_pct_bps`, divided by 10000, rounded half up **once** to whole cents. Then multiply that rounded integer by 6 cap months. `reserve_cap_cents` is the lesser of that six-month figure and twice `exposure_estimate_cents`. Rounding after the multiplication by 6, rather than before, is not the rule and yields a different number of cents.

**Step six, the mechanics.** `reserve_type` is `ROLLING` when `reserve_pct_bps` is below 1000 basis points, and `CAPPED` at 1000 or above. A rolling reserve accrues indefinitely and releases on maturity; a capped reserve stops accruing once its ceiling is reached.

Creating the assessment re-pins four fields on the application, so the committee always reads the terms of the latest run: `current_assessment_method`, `current_reserve_pct_bps`, `current_reserve_cap_cents` and `current_reserve_type` all take their values from the assessment with the greatest `assessment_sequence`. That re-pinning is a mutation of the application, so its `version` increments — which is precisely why the next assessment's `expected_application_version` must carry the new number.

### 3.5. The Committee Decision and Portfolio Onboarding

A `RiskCommitteeApprover` closes the application by supplying a `decision` of either `APPROVE` or `DECLINE`. The application must be in `UNDERWRITING`, and at least one `CreditAssessment` must exist for it; a decision on an unpriced application is rejected with `ERR_CREDIT_ASSESSMENT_REQUIRED`. Either outcome records `decision_officer_user_id` from `X-User-ID` and `decided_at` from the logical clock.

`DECLINE` moves the application to `DECLINED` and requires `decline_reason_code` in the body, one of `PROHIBITED_CATEGORY`, `INSUFFICIENT_CREDIT`, `VERIFICATION_FAILED` or `OWNERSHIP_UNVERIFIED`; the code is stored on the application and no `Merchant` is created.

`APPROVE` moves the application to `APPROVED` and cascades twice. First, a `Merchant` is created, holding the `application_id` it was onboarded from, with `legal_business_name` and `mcc_code` copied from the application, `monthly_volume_limit_cents` copied from `requested_monthly_volume_cents`, `account_state` `ACTIVE`, `funding_state` `NORMAL`, `delayed_payout_days` 0, `remediation_required` false, `funding_hold_reason_code` null, `terminated_at` and `reinstated_at` null, `metrics_as_of_date` and `last_monitoring_run_date` null, and every counter — `trailing_30d_txn_count`, `trailing_30d_volume_cents`, `trailing_30d_chargeback_count`, `trailing_30d_chargeback_amount_cents`, `rolling_chargeback_ratio_bps`, `amount_based_chargeback_ratio_bps`, `reserve_balance_cents`, `pending_payout_cents`, `lifetime_reversed_cents` and `open_alert_count` — at 0. The reserve terms are frozen onto the merchant from the authoritative assessment, the one with the greatest `assessment_sequence`: `pinned_reserve_pct_bps`, `pinned_reserve_cap_cents`, `pinned_reserve_type` and `pinned_assessment_method`. Once pinned, those terms change only through a granted appeal.

Second, that merchant creation itself triggers the creation of exactly one `ReserveAccount` for the merchant. The account copies `reserve_type`, `reserve_pct_bps` and `reserve_cap_cents` from the merchant's pinned values, sets `release_lag_days` to 90 days when `reserve_type` is `ROLLING` and 180 days when it is `CAPPED`, starts in state `ACCRUING` with `total_accrued_cents`, `released_to_date_cents`, `offset_to_date_cents` and `held_balance_cents` all 0, and `last_release_tranche_end_date` null. Because both the merchant and its reserve account are born by cascade rather than by direct request, the reverse-reachability reads described in §2.10 are how a client discovers their identifiers.

### 3.6. Activity Ingest and Trailing Portfolio Metrics

A `RiskAnalyst` feeds the risk engine one row per merchant per processing day. The row names its `merchant_id` and `business_date`, and reports the day's `txn_count` of approved transactions, `volume_cents` of approved volume, `distinct_card_count` of distinct card numbers presented, `decline_count` of declined authorizations, `cross_border_count` of transactions whose issuer country differs from the merchant's, `refund_cents` of refunds issued, `unique_bin_count` of distinct issuer bank identification numbers presented, `chargeback_count` of chargebacks received and `chargeback_amount_cents` of charged-back value. All nine counters must be zero or greater. `chargeback_network` names the card network that raised the day's chargebacks — one of `VISA`, `MASTERCARD`, `AMEX` or `DISCOVER` — and is required whenever `chargeback_count` is greater than zero; it defaults to null. The server computes `avg_ticket_cents` as `volume_cents` divided by `txn_count` rounded half up when `txn_count` is greater than zero, and 0 when the day had no transactions, and stamps `ingested_by_user_id` with the acting analyst.

Three rules guard ingest. The merchant's `account_state` must not be `TERMINATED`, or the row is rejected with `ERR_MERCHANT_ACCOUNT_STATE_INVALID`. `business_date` must not be later than the logical clock date, since a facilitator cannot observe the future; a forward-dated row is rejected with `ERR_ACTIVITY_DATE_IN_FUTURE`. And the pair of `merchant_id` and `business_date` must be unique across all activity rows — a day is ingested once and never restated — with a repeat rejected as `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTIVITY_DATE`.

Ingesting a row refreshes the merchant's trailing metrics, using the ingested row's `business_date` as the window end. The window is 30 days long and closed at both ends: it starts 29 days before the window end and includes both boundary dates. Every activity row of that merchant whose `business_date` falls inside the window contributes, and rows outside it are excluded even when they are the merchant's only other activity — no lifecycle filter applies, because activity rows have no lifecycle. `trailing_30d_txn_count`, `trailing_30d_volume_cents`, `trailing_30d_chargeback_count` and `trailing_30d_chargeback_amount_cents` are the sums of the corresponding day columns, and `metrics_as_of_date` records the window end.

From those sums come **two chargeback ratio variants, which are not interchangeable**. `rolling_chargeback_ratio_bps` is the count-based measure: `trailing_30d_chargeback_count` multiplied by 10000, divided by `trailing_30d_txn_count`, rounded half up, or exactly 0 when the trailing transaction count is 0. This is the network-standard measure, and it is the **only** variant that may restrict an account — the chargeback-threshold detector, the remediation month evaluation and every `account_state` cascade read it and nothing else. `amount_based_chargeback_ratio_bps` is the amount-based measure: `trailing_30d_chargeback_amount_cents` multiplied by 10000, divided by `trailing_30d_volume_cents`, rounded half up, or exactly 0 when the trailing volume is 0. It feeds loss forecasting only. Whenever the average charged-back ticket differs from the average approved ticket the two variants disagree, so a merchant can sit safely below the excessive threshold on the count-based measure while its amount-based value exceeds it.

A read-only risk summary assembles a merchant's position without persisting anything, recomputing from stored rows at read time. It reports `merchant_id`, `account_state`, `funding_state`, both ratio variants evaluated with the window ending at the merchant's `metrics_as_of_date` — both exactly 0 when `metrics_as_of_date` is null — and a `gating_ratio_band` derived solely from the count-based variant: `NORMAL` when `rolling_chargeback_ratio_bps` is below 65 basis points, `EARLY_WARNING` when it is 65 or more but below 90, and `EXCESSIVE` when it is 90 or more. It also reports `open_alert_count` as the count of the merchant's alerts in state `OPEN`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `ESCALATED`; `reserve_account_id`, `reserve_type` and `reserve_held_balance_cents` read from the merchant's single reserve account, or null, null and 0 when it has none; `reserve_releasable_cents`, the maturity-derived releasable balance of §3.9 evaluated against the logical clock date and reported as 0 when the account state is `CLOSED`; `unpaid_settlement_exposure_cents`, the sum of `net_payout_cents` over the merchant's batches whose `payout_state` is `SCHEDULED`, `DELAYED` or `HELD`, using the same filter the forecast of §3.12 applies per merchant; and `remediation_required`.

### 3.7. The Monitoring Sweep and Its Detectors

A `RiskAnalyst` sweeps the book by supplying a single `as_of_date`. The sweep evaluates every `Merchant` whose `account_state` is `ACTIVE` or `RESTRICTED`, in `created_at` ascending order; `TERMINATED` merchants are never evaluated and never receive alerts. `as_of_date` must not be later than the logical clock date, and it must not be earlier than the greatest `last_monitoring_run_date` across the whole portfolio — the sweep may stand still on a date but never walks backwards — with a violation rejected as `ERR_MONITORING_RUN_DATE_REGRESSION`. The operation returns the alerts it created or re-detected, ordered by merchant `created_at` ascending and then by detector order.

For each merchant the sweep first refreshes both trailing ratio variants with the window ending at `as_of_date`, exactly as §3.6 describes. It then reads two things: the merchant's activity row whose `business_date` equals `as_of_date`, called the day row, which may be absent; and a baseline of the merchant's activity rows from the 30 days ending the day before, that is, from 30 days before `as_of_date` through the day immediately before `as_of_date`, which deliberately excludes `as_of_date` itself. From the baseline come two averages, both of which divide by a fixed denominator rather than by the number of rows observed: `baseline_avg_daily_volume_cents` is the summed baseline `volume_cents` divided by the constant 30, rounded half up; and the baseline average ticket is the summed baseline `volume_cents` divided by the summed baseline `txn_count`, rounded half up, or 0 when the baseline transaction count is 0.

Four detectors then run **in this pinned order**, and each may raise at most one alert per merchant per sweep.

**One, `CHARGEBACK_THRESHOLD`.** This detector needs no day row. Let the observed ratio be the merchant's freshly computed `rolling_chargeback_ratio_bps`. When it is 65 basis points or more — the early-warning line — the detector raises, sets `observed_metric_bps` to that ratio and sets `severity_score` to 90 when the ratio is 90 basis points or more, and 60 otherwise. When the resulting `severity_score` is 90 or more and the merchant's `account_state` is `ACTIVE`, the CB-BREACH cascade fires, as described below.

**Two, `VELOCITY_SPIKE`.** Skipped entirely when there is no day row. It raises when `baseline_avg_daily_volume_cents` is greater than 0 and the day row's `volume_cents` is at least 3 times `baseline_avg_daily_volume_cents`. `observed_metric_bps` is the day's `volume_cents` multiplied by 10000, divided by `baseline_avg_daily_volume_cents`, rounded half up — the day expressed as basis points of a normal day. `severity_score` is the lesser of 100 and the quantity 20 plus `observed_metric_bps` divided by 1000 truncated down to a whole number.

**Three, `CARD_TESTING`.** Skipped entirely when there is no day row, and it requires all five of the following to hold together: the day's `txn_count` is greater than 0; the day's `decline_count` is at least 50; the decline ratio — `decline_count` multiplied by 10000, divided by the sum of `txn_count` and `decline_count`, rounded half up — is at least 4000 basis points; the card-spread ratio — `distinct_card_count` multiplied by 10000, divided by `txn_count`, rounded half up — is at least 8000 basis points; and the day's `avg_ticket_cents` is at most 500 cents, the micro-ticket signature of an enumeration attack. When all five hold, `observed_metric_bps` is the decline ratio just computed and `severity_score` is fixed at 85.

**Four, `LAUNDERING_PATTERN`.** An additive weighted score, again skipped when there is no day row. Start at 0 and add each of the following that applies: 30 points when the day's `txn_count` is greater than 0 and the cross-border ratio — `cross_border_count` multiplied by 10000, divided by `txn_count`, rounded half up — is at least 5000 basis points; 25 points when the day's `volume_cents` is greater than 0 and the refund ratio — `refund_cents` multiplied by 10000, divided by `volume_cents`, rounded half up — is at least 3000 basis points; 25 points when the day's `txn_count` is at least 100 and its `unique_bin_count` is at most 3, the narrow-issuer signature; and 20 points when the baseline average ticket is greater than 0 and the day's `avg_ticket_cents` is at least 5 times that baseline average ticket. The detector raises when the accumulated score is 55 or more, setting `severity_score` to the score itself and `observed_metric_bps` to the score multiplied by 100.

Raising an alert is idempotent per merchant and per `alert_type`. When an alert of the same `alert_type` for the same merchant already sits in state `OPEN` or `UNDER_REVIEW`, the sweep takes the re-detection path on that existing row instead of inserting: `recurrence_count` increments by one, and `severity_score`, `observed_metric_bps` and `last_detected_on_date` are overwritten with the current sweep's values. No new row is created. Otherwise a new `MonitoringAlert` is inserted with the given `merchant_id` and `alert_type`, state `OPEN`, `recurrence_count` 1, and both `detected_on_date` and `last_detected_on_date` set to `as_of_date`, with `reviewed_by_user_id` and `review_note` null. `severity_score` is always an integer from 0 through 100. After all four detectors have run for a merchant, the sweep sets that merchant's `last_monitoring_run_date` to `as_of_date` and recomputes `open_alert_count`.

**The CB-BREACH cascade.** When the chargeback-threshold detector raises or re-raises with `severity_score` of 90 or more against a merchant whose `account_state` is `ACTIVE`, four links fire in sequence. The alert is link one. Link two moves `account_state` `ACTIVE → RESTRICTED` and sets `remediation_required` to true, which is what makes a remediation program permissible. Link three moves `funding_state` `NORMAL → DELAYED` and sets `delayed_payout_days` to 7. Link four walks every settlement batch of that merchant whose `payout_state` is `SCHEDULED`, in `period_end_date` ascending order breaking ties by `created_at` ascending, moving each `SCHEDULED → DELAYED` with `scheduled_payout_date` set to the triggering sweep's `as_of_date` plus 7 days; `pending_payout_cents` is then recomputed. A merchant already `RESTRICTED` is not restricted twice, and a merchant whose funding is already `DELAYED` or `HELD` does not have its funding posture rewound by link three.

### 3.8. Alert Triage, Funding Holds and Funding Release

Alerts are worked by a `RiskAnalyst` through a `disposition`, accompanied by an optional `review_note`. `ACKNOWLEDGE` moves an alert `OPEN → UNDER_REVIEW` and records `reviewed_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID`. `DISMISS` moves it `UNDER_REVIEW → DISMISSED`, stores the note and recomputes the merchant's `open_alert_count`. `ESCALATE` moves it `UNDER_REVIEW → ESCALATED` and stores the note. A disposition the current state does not permit — acknowledging anything other than an `OPEN` alert, or dismissing or escalating anything other than an `UNDER_REVIEW` alert — is rejected with `ERR_ALERT_STATE_INVALID`. `DISMISSED` and `CLOSED` are terminal.

Escalation is where triage turns into money movement, and the two branches are deliberately asymmetric. When the escalated alert's `alert_type` is `CARD_TESTING` or `LAUNDERING_PATTERN` and the merchant's `funding_state` is `NORMAL` or `DELAYED`, the ALERT-ESCALATION cascade fires: link two moves `funding_state` to `HELD` with `delayed_payout_days` set to 0 and `funding_hold_reason_code` set to `CARD_TESTING` for a card-testing alert and `SUSPECTED_LAUNDERING` for a laundering alert, and link three moves every settlement batch of that merchant whose `payout_state` is `SCHEDULED` or `DELAYED` to `HELD` with `scheduled_payout_date` cleared to null, processed in `period_end_date` ascending order breaking ties by `created_at` ascending. The merchant's `account_state` is **not** modified by this cascade, and the cascade does not fire against a merchant whose `account_state` is `TERMINATED`. When the escalated alert's `alert_type` is `CHARGEBACK_THRESHOLD`, by contrast, **no funding change occurs at all**: the escalation records the review and nothing more, because a dispute-ratio problem is answered by the remediation program of §3.10 rather than by freezing the merchant's cash.

An analyst may also hold funding directly, supplying a `reason_code` from `SUSPECTED_LAUNDERING`, `CARD_TESTING`, `EXCESSIVE_CHARGEBACKS` and `TERMINATION_PENDING`, which is stored as `funding_hold_reason_code`. The merchant's `funding_state` must be `NORMAL` or `DELAYED`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_FUNDING_STATE_INVALID`, and the merchant's `account_state` must not be `TERMINATED`. Both the `NORMAL → HELD` and the `DELAYED → HELD` transitions behave identically: `delayed_payout_days` is set to 0, every settlement batch of the merchant whose `payout_state` is `SCHEDULED` or `DELAYED` moves to `HELD` with `scheduled_payout_date` cleared to null in `period_end_date` then `created_at` ascending order, and `pending_payout_cents` is recomputed — its value does not change, because held batches remain pending. `account_state` is untouched.

Lifting a hold is reserved to a `RiskCommitteeApprover` and takes no body. The merchant's `funding_state` must be `HELD`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_FUNDING_STATE_INVALID`. The outcome is decided by the merchant's risk posture, not by the caller. When `account_state` is `ACTIVE`, `funding_state` moves `HELD → NORMAL`, `funding_hold_reason_code` is cleared, `delayed_payout_days` is set to 0, and every batch of the merchant whose `payout_state` is `HELD` moves to `SCHEDULED` with `scheduled_payout_date` set to its own `period_end_date` plus 1 day. When `account_state` is `RESTRICTED`, `funding_state` moves `HELD → DELAYED` instead, `funding_hold_reason_code` is cleared, `delayed_payout_days` is set to 7, and every `HELD` batch moves to `DELAYED` with `scheduled_payout_date` set to the logical clock date plus 7 days. Release therefore cannot return a restricted merchant to normal funding; only the remediation pass of §3.10 can do that.

### 3.9. Settlement, the Loss Waterfall and the Payout Lifecycle

A `PortfolioController` settles a closed activity period by naming the `merchant_id`, the inclusive `period_start_date` and `period_end_date`, and the `gross_volume_cents` submitted for settlement, which must be at least 1 cent. Six checks guard the request, in the ladder order of §2.6. The merchant's `account_state` must not be `TERMINATED`, or `ERR_MERCHANT_ACCOUNT_STATE_INVALID`. The merchant's reserve account must not be in state `CLOSED`, or `ERR_RESERVE_ACCOUNT_CLOSED`. `period_start_date` must not be later than `period_end_date` and `period_end_date` must not be later than the logical clock date, or `ERR_SETTLEMENT_PERIOD_INVALID`. The closed period must not intersect the period of any other `SettlementBatch` of the same merchant whose `payout_state` is anything other than `REVERSED`; a single shared boundary date is already an intersection, and the rejection is `ERR_SETTLEMENT_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. At least one activity row must fall inside the period, or `ERR_EMPTY_SETTLEMENT_PERIOD`. `gross_volume_cents` must equal the summed `volume_cents` of the period's activity rows exactly, or `ERR_GROSS_VOLUME_MISMATCH`. Finally, the merchant's trailing 30-day volume measured with the window ending at `period_end_date` must be at most one and a quarter times `monthly_volume_limit_cents` — 12500 basis points of the contracted limit — or the batch is refused with `ERR_MONTHLY_VOLUME_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`.

The batch's line items are the merchant's activity rows whose `business_date` lies inside the inclusive period, taken in `business_date` ascending order; `day_line_count` is their number. Activity outside the period never contributes, even if it has never been settled.

**The reserve hold.** Let the reserve percentage be the `reserve_pct_bps` of the merchant's reserve account. For each day line, multiply that line's `volume_cents` by the reserve percentage, divide by 10000 and round half up to whole cents; then add those per-line integers together. Rounding per line and then summing is the rule; summing the volumes and rounding once is explicitly not, and the two differ whenever two or more lines round up. When the account's `reserve_type` is `CAPPED`, the remaining capacity is the greater of 0 and `reserve_cap_cents` less the current `held_balance_cents`, and `reserve_hold_cents` is the lesser of the per-line sum and that remaining capacity. When the `reserve_type` is `ROLLING`, `reserve_hold_cents` is the per-line sum with no ceiling.

**The loss waterfall.** The period's loss total is the summed `chargeback_amount_cents` of the day lines, and losses are funded from three buckets in a fixed order. The amount available to fund losses is the greater of 0 and `gross_volume_cents` less `reserve_hold_cents` — the reserve is taken off the top and is never reachable by the settlement bucket. `loss_offset_from_settlement_cents` is the lesser of the loss total and that available amount. Whatever remains unfunded is the deficit: the loss total less `loss_offset_from_settlement_cents`. `reserve_offset_cents` is the lesser of the deficit and the sum of the account's current `held_balance_cents` and this batch's `reserve_hold_cents`, so the hold just taken is itself available to absorb the period's disputes. `uncovered_loss_cents` is the deficit less `reserve_offset_cents` — the facilitator's own loss. `net_payout_cents`, the amount payable to the merchant, is the available amount less `loss_offset_from_settlement_cents`. The per-batch invariant of §2.12 holds by construction.

**The ledger appends, in pinned order.** First, when `reserve_hold_cents` is greater than 0, a `HOLD` entry is appended with `reason_code` `SETTLEMENT_HOLD`, `amount_cents` equal to `reserve_hold_cents`, `effective_date` equal to the batch's `period_end_date` — a hold matures from the economics of the period, not from the moment of bookkeeping — and `settlement_batch_id` naming this batch. Second, when `reserve_offset_cents` is greater than 0, an `OFFSET_LOSS` entry is appended with `reason_code` `LOSS_OFFSET`, `amount_cents` equal to `reserve_offset_cents`, `effective_date` equal to the logical clock date, and `settlement_batch_id` naming this batch. The account's `total_accrued_cents`, `offset_to_date_cents` and `held_balance_cents` are then recomputed, `Merchant.reserve_balance_cents` is set from the new `held_balance_cents`, and `net_payout_cents` is added to `Merchant.pending_payout_cents`.

Appending a hold can move the reserve account's own state. An account in `ACCRUING` whose `reserve_type` is `CAPPED` and whose `held_balance_cents` reaches or exceeds `reserve_cap_cents` after the hold moves `ACCRUING → CAP_REACHED`, after which subsequent batches withhold only the remaining capacity — which is 0 in that state. A `ROLLING` account never reaches `CAP_REACHED`. An account sitting in `RELEASING` returns `RELEASING → ACCRUING` whenever a `HOLD` entry is appended, with `total_accrued_cents` and `held_balance_cents` recomputed.

**The payout posture at creation** is dictated by the merchant's `funding_state`, not by the caller. A `NORMAL` merchant's batch is created in `payout_state` `SCHEDULED` with `scheduled_payout_date` set to `period_end_date` plus 1 day. A `DELAYED` merchant's batch is created and immediately taken to `DELAYED` with `scheduled_payout_date` set to `period_end_date` plus `delayed_payout_days` days. A `HELD` merchant's batch is created and immediately taken to `HELD` with `scheduled_payout_date` null; a held batch has no due date at all.

**Paying out.** A controller releases a payout on or after it falls due. The batch's `payout_state` must be `SCHEDULED` or `DELAYED`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_BATCH_STATE_INVALID`; the logical clock date must be on or after `scheduled_payout_date`, or `ERR_PAYOUT_NOT_DUE`; and the merchant's `funding_state` must not be `HELD`. On success the batch moves to `PAID`, `paid_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and `net_payout_cents` is subtracted from `Merchant.pending_payout_cents` as the batch leaves the pending set. `PAID` and `REVERSED` are the two terminal payout states.

**Reversing.** A compensating reversal unwinds an unpaid batch completely. The batch's `payout_state` must be `SCHEDULED`, `DELAYED` or `HELD` — a paid batch cannot be reversed, and the attempt yields `ERR_BATCH_STATE_INVALID` — and the merchant's reserve account must not be `CLOSED`, or `ERR_RESERVE_ACCOUNT_CLOSED`. The request carries a `reversal_reason_code` of `RISK_HOLD`, `FUNDING_ERROR`, `TERMINATION` or `APPEAL_REMEDY`, which is stored alongside `reversed_at` from the logical clock as the batch moves to `REVERSED`. Two compensating ledger entries restore the account: a `RELEASE` entry with `reason_code` `BATCH_REVERSAL_RELEASE` for `amount_cents` equal to this batch's `reserve_hold_cents`, with `settlement_batch_id` naming this batch, `effective_date` the logical clock date and both tranche dates null — skipped when the batch withheld nothing; and, when this batch's `reserve_offset_cents` is greater than 0, a `HOLD` entry with `reason_code` `SETTLEMENT_HOLD` for `amount_cents` equal to `reserve_offset_cents`, undoing the loss offset. The account's totals, `held_balance_cents` and state are recomputed, `Merchant.reserve_balance_cents` is refreshed, and `net_payout_cents` is subtracted from `Merchant.pending_payout_cents`, restoring its pre-batch value. `lifetime_reversed_cents` is the merchant's running record of value unwound by reversal: each reversal adds this batch's `uncovered_loss_cents`, `reserve_offset_cents` and `loss_offset_from_settlement_cents` to it.

### 3.10. Reserve Maturity, Tranche Releases and Cap Mechanics

Reserve is not released on request alone; it is released when it has matured. The maturity lag is the account's `release_lag_days` — 90 days for a `ROLLING` account and 180 days for a `CAPPED` one — and the cutoff is the logical clock date less that many days. The matured hold total is the summed `amount_cents` of the account's `HOLD` entries whose `effective_date` is on or before that cutoff, the boundary being inclusive; maturity is filtered by `effective_date` only and never by `created_at`. The releasable balance is then the greater of 0 and the lesser of two quantities: the account's current `held_balance_cents`, and the matured hold total less `released_to_date_cents` less `offset_to_date_cents` — so releases and loss offsets already taken are subtracted in full regardless of their own maturity.

A `PortfolioController` requests a release by supplying `amount_cents`, a closed tranche window given as `tranche_start_date` and `tranche_end_date`, and `expected_reserve_account_version`. Beyond the version pre-condition of §2.9, four guards apply. The merchant's `account_state` must be `ACTIVE` — a restricted or terminated merchant gets nothing back — or the request is rejected with `ERR_MERCHANT_ACCOUNT_STATE_INVALID`; the merchant's `funding_state` must be `NORMAL`, or `ERR_FUNDING_STATE_INVALID`; and the account's state must not be `CLOSED`, or `ERR_RESERVE_ACCOUNT_CLOSED`.

The tranche geometry is then checked as one rule reported through `ERR_RELEASE_TRANCHE_COOLING_VIOLATION`. `tranche_start_date` must not be later than `tranche_end_date`; `tranche_end_date` must not be later than the logical clock date; and a mandatory cooling buffer must separate this tranche from the previous scheduled one. The previous scheduled release is the account's `RELEASE` entry with `reason_code` `SCHEDULED_RELEASE` having the greatest `effective_date`, breaking a tie in favour of the entry with the greater `created_at`. When such an entry exists, `tranche_start_date` must be at least 31 days after that entry's `tranche_end_date` — a 30-day cooling period plus one day, so that consecutive tranches never touch. When no scheduled release has ever been recorded, the cooling condition is satisfied trivially.

Two fiscal checks close the sequence, in this order: the releasable balance must be greater than 0, or the request is rejected with `ERR_NO_RELEASABLE_BALANCE`; and the requested `amount_cents` must be no greater than the releasable balance, or `ERR_RELEASE_AMOUNT_EXCEEDS_RELEASABLE`.

On acceptance a `RELEASE` entry is appended with `reason_code` `SCHEDULED_RELEASE`, the supplied `amount_cents` and tranche dates, `effective_date` set to the logical clock date, `settlement_batch_id` null and `recorded_by_user_id` set to the acting controller. `released_to_date_cents` rises, `held_balance_cents` falls, `last_release_tranche_end_date` is set to this entry's `tranche_end_date`, `Merchant.reserve_balance_cents` is refreshed, and the account's `version` increments — which is exactly why a client intending a second release must re-read the account first.

The release also moves the account's own state. From `ACCRUING`, the account moves `ACCRUING → RELEASING` when, after the entry, `held_balance_cents` is greater than 0 and `released_to_date_cents` is greater than 0. From `CAP_REACHED`, it moves `CAP_REACHED → RELEASING` when, after the entry, `held_balance_cents` is greater than 0 and below `reserve_cap_cents`. A `CAP_REACHED` account also returns `CAP_REACHED → ACCRUING` when, after a `RELEASE` or `OFFSET_LOSS` entry, `held_balance_cents` is below `reserve_cap_cents` and either `released_to_date_cents` is 0 or `held_balance_cents` is 0 — reserve capacity reopens for future holds. `ACCRUING`, `CAP_REACHED` and `RELEASING` are all live states; `CLOSED`, reached only by termination, forbids any further `HOLD`, `RELEASE` or `OFFSET_LOSS` append.

### 3.11. Chargeback Remediation and Network Escalation

A restricted merchant earns its way back through a remediation program. A `RiskAnalyst` opens one by naming the `merchant_id`, the `window_start_date` and `window_end_date` of the monitoring window, a `target_ratio_bps` from 1 through 10000 that the merchant must stay below, and a `monitoring_month_count` from 1 through 6 of consecutive compliant months required to pass. The window is **half-open**: it includes `window_start_date` and excludes `window_end_date`. A program may only be opened while the merchant's `remediation_required` is true — the flag the CB-BREACH cascade sets — and an attempt against a merchant whose flag is false is rejected with `ERR_REMEDIATION_NOT_REQUIRED`; the merchant's `account_state` must also not be `TERMINATED`. The requested window must not overlap the window of any other program of the same merchant whose state is `DRAFT` or `ACTIVE`, with touching endpoints — a new window starting exactly where an existing one ends — explicitly not an overlap; a genuine overlap is rejected with `ERR_REMEDIATION_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. The program is created in state `DRAFT` with `months_elapsed` 0, `consecutive_compliant_months` 0, `last_observed_ratio_bps` null, `network_program_tier` `STANDARD`, `opened_by_user_id` set from `X-User-ID` and `activated_by_user_id` null.

Activation is the second signature. A `RiskCommitteeApprover` moves the program `DRAFT → ACTIVE`, which requires that the merchant's `account_state` be `RESTRICTED` and that the activating `X-User-ID` differ from `opened_by_user_id`; the same user opening and activating is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, and a program not in `DRAFT` with `ERR_PROGRAM_STATE_INVALID`. `activated_by_user_id` records the approver.

Months are then evaluated one at a time by a `RiskAnalyst`, who supplies a `month_index`. Months are fixed 30-day blocks measured from `window_start_date`, never calendar months: month one starts at `window_start_date`, and in general a month starts `month_index` less one, multiplied by 30, days after `window_start_date` and ends 30 days times `month_index`, less one day, after `window_start_date`, inclusive of both ends. Evaluation is strictly sequential: `month_index` must equal `months_elapsed` plus one, or the request is rejected with `ERR_MONTH_INDEX_OUT_OF_SEQUENCE`, and the program must be `ACTIVE`, or `ERR_PROGRAM_STATE_INVALID`.

The month's observed ratio is the count-based variant computed over that month's activity rows of the program's own merchant: summed `chargeback_count` multiplied by 10000, divided by summed `txn_count`, rounded half up, or exactly 0 when the month had no transactions. The month is **compliant** when that observed ratio is strictly below `target_ratio_bps`; equalling the target is not compliance, and a month with no transactions at all is compliant with a ratio of 0. In every case `months_elapsed` increments by one, `last_observed_ratio_bps` is set to the observed ratio, and `consecutive_compliant_months` becomes its previous value plus one when the month is compliant and is reset to 0 when it is not.

The outcome is then decided in this order. The program **fails** when the observed ratio is 180 basis points or more, or when the month is non-compliant and `months_elapsed` after this month has reached `monitoring_month_count` plus 2 grace months without the program having passed. Otherwise the program **passes** when `consecutive_compliant_months` after this month is at least `monitoring_month_count`. Otherwise the program stays `ACTIVE` and awaits the next month — a self-loop that may repeat as often as the window allows. `PASSED` and `NETWORK_ESCALATED` are terminal.

**The REMEDIATION-PASS cascade.** A pass fires three further links. `account_state` moves `RESTRICTED → ACTIVE` with `remediation_required` set back to false, provided the merchant has no `TerminationDecision` in state `PROPOSED` or `APPROVED` — a merchant already up for termination is not un-restricted by a passing program. That lift then moves `funding_state` `DELAYED → NORMAL` with `delayed_payout_days` reset to 0, which in turn moves every settlement batch of the merchant whose `payout_state` is `DELAYED` to `SCHEDULED` with `scheduled_payout_date` set to its own `period_end_date` plus 1 day, processed in `period_end_date` then `created_at` ascending order, and closes every alert of the merchant whose `alert_type` is `CHARGEBACK_THRESHOLD` and whose state is `ESCALATED`, moving each `ESCALATED → CLOSED` in `created_at` ascending order. `open_alert_count` and `pending_payout_cents` are recomputed.

**The REMEDIATION-FAIL cascade.** A failure escalates to the card networks only when the failing month was material: when the evaluated month's summed `chargeback_count` is at least 100, the program moves `FAILED → NETWORK_ESCALATED` and `network_program_tier` is set to `EXCESSIVE` when `last_observed_ratio_bps` is 180 basis points or more, and to `STANDARD` otherwise. That escalation then moves the merchant's `funding_state` to `HELD` with `funding_hold_reason_code` `EXCESSIVE_CHARGEBACKS`, and every non-terminal settlement batch of the merchant to `HELD`. A failing month below 100 chargebacks leaves the program in `FAILED` with no cascade, and `FAILED` is the state from which a termination proposal usually follows.

### 3.12. Termination, Appeal and Reinstatement

Termination is a three-signature sequence. A `RiskAnalyst` proposes it by naming the `merchant_id`, a `reason_code` of `EXCESSIVE_CHARGEBACKS`, `SUSPECTED_LAUNDERING`, `PROHIBITED_ACTIVITY` or `REMEDIATION_FAILURE`, and optionally `match_listing_flag`, which defaults to false and records whether the merchant is to be listed on the network terminated-merchant file when the decision is executed. The merchant's `account_state` must not be `TERMINATED`. The decision is created in state `PROPOSED` with `proposed_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID`, and with `approved_by_user_id`, `rejection_note`, `executed_at` and `reversed_at` all null.

A `RiskCommitteeApprover` then approves or rejects. Both operations require the decision to be in `PROPOSED`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_TERMINATION_STATE_INVALID`, and both require an `X-User-ID` different from `proposed_by_user_id`, or `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Approval additionally requires that the merchant's `account_state` not already be `TERMINATED`, moves the decision `PROPOSED → APPROVED` and records `approved_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID`. Rejection moves it `PROPOSED → REJECTED`, stores the supplied `rejection_note` and likewise records `approved_by_user_id` as the deciding user. `REJECTED` and `REVERSED` are terminal.

A `PortfolioController` executes an `APPROVED` decision. Execution requires the merchant's `account_state` to be `ACTIVE` or `RESTRICTED`, and requires that the merchant have no `RemediationProgram` in state `DRAFT` or `ACTIVE` — an open remediation programme must be resolved before the account is closed, and an attempt while one is open is rejected with `ERR_PROGRAM_STATE_INVALID`. On success `executed_at` is stamped from the logical clock, the decision moves `APPROVED → EXECUTED`, and the TERMINATION-EXEC cascade runs: `account_state` moves to `TERMINATED` with `terminated_at` set from the logical clock; that termination moves `funding_state` to `HELD` with `funding_hold_reason_code` `TERMINATION_PENDING` and `delayed_payout_days` 0; that hold moves every settlement batch whose `payout_state` is `SCHEDULED` or `DELAYED` to `HELD` with `scheduled_payout_date` cleared, in `period_end_date` then `created_at` ascending order, and moves the merchant's reserve account to `CLOSED` from whichever of `ACCRUING`, `CAP_REACHED` or `RELEASING` it occupied; and closing the reserve account moves every alert of the merchant whose state is `OPEN`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `ESCALATED` to `CLOSED`, processed in `created_at` ascending order, with `open_alert_count` recomputed.

A terminated merchant has 30 days to appeal. A `MerchantUser` files one by naming its own `merchant_id`, the `termination_decision_id` of the decision it contests, and an `evidence_summary` of 1 through 2000 non-blank characters. The named decision must be in state `EXECUTED` and must belong to the same merchant as the appeal, or the filing is rejected with `ERR_APPEAL_DECISION_NOT_EXECUTED`. The logical clock date must be at most 30 days after the date on which that decision's `executed_at` fell, or `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. And no appeal in state `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `INFO_REQUESTED` may already exist against the same `termination_decision_id`, or `ERR_DUPLICATE_APPEAL`. The appeal is created in state `SUBMITTED` with `filed_on_date` set to the logical clock date, `response_round_count` 0, `outstanding_info_items` empty, and `reviewer_user_id`, `reinstatement_reserve_pct_bps` and `decided_at` null.

Adjudication is a negotiation loop. A `RiskCommitteeApprover` takes up a `SUBMITTED` appeal, moving it `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW` and recording `reviewer_user_id` from `X-User-ID`. From `UNDER_REVIEW` the reviewer may request more evidence, supplying `outstanding_info_items` and moving the appeal `UNDER_REVIEW → INFO_REQUESTED`. The merchant answers with a `response_text`, which moves the appeal `INFO_REQUESTED → UNDER_REVIEW`, clears `outstanding_info_items` to an empty list and increments `response_round_count` by one; only the owning merchant may respond, so `X-User-ID` must equal the appeal's `merchant_id`. That lap may repeat without limit, and `response_round_count` counts the laps. Any operation attempted from a state that does not declare it — reviewing anything other than a `SUBMITTED` appeal, requesting information or deciding from anything other than `UNDER_REVIEW`, or responding from anything other than `INFO_REQUESTED` — is rejected with `ERR_APPEAL_STATE_INVALID`.

The decision carries an `outcome` of `GRANT` or `UPHOLD` and, optionally, `reinstatement_reserve_pct_bps`. Both outcomes require the appeal to be in `UNDER_REVIEW` and require that the deciding `X-User-ID` be neither the `proposed_by_user_id` nor the `approved_by_user_id` of the appealed decision, or the request is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; both stamp `decided_at` from the logical clock. `UPHOLD` moves the appeal `UNDER_REVIEW → UPHELD` and changes nothing else: the merchant remains `TERMINATED` and its reserve account remains `CLOSED`.

`GRANT` requires the appealed decision to be in state `EXECUTED`, moves the appeal `UNDER_REVIEW → GRANTED`, stores `reinstatement_reserve_pct_bps` when supplied, and runs the APPEAL-GRANT cascade through four further links. The termination decision moves `EXECUTED → REVERSED` with `reversed_at` stamped from the logical clock. That reversal moves `account_state` `TERMINATED → RESTRICTED`, setting `reinstated_at` from the logical clock, `remediation_required` to true and `terminated_at` back to null, and re-prices the reserve: `pinned_reserve_pct_bps` takes the appeal's `reinstatement_reserve_pct_bps` when one was supplied and is left unchanged otherwise, and the resulting value is mirrored onto the reserve account's `reserve_pct_bps`. That reinstatement then moves `funding_state` `HELD → DELAYED`, clearing `funding_hold_reason_code` to null and setting `delayed_payout_days` to 7. That funding change finally moves every settlement batch of the merchant whose `payout_state` is `HELD` to `DELAYED` with `scheduled_payout_date` set to the logical clock date plus 7 days, and moves the reserve account `CLOSED → ACCRUING`, re-pinning its `reserve_pct_bps` from the merchant's `pinned_reserve_pct_bps` and, when its `held_balance_cents` is greater than 0, appending a `RELEASE` entry with `reason_code` `REINSTATEMENT_RELEASE` for `amount_cents` equal to that whole `held_balance_cents`, `effective_date` the logical clock date and both tranche dates null. `released_to_date_cents`, `held_balance_cents`, `Merchant.reserve_balance_cents` and `pending_payout_cents` are all recomputed. A reinstated merchant thus comes back restricted, on delayed funding, with a fresh reserve account and its old balance returned.

### 3.13. Portfolio Loss Forecasting and Reserve Adequacy

A `PortfolioController` sizes the book's expected loss by generating a `PortfolioForecast` for an `as_of_date` under a `method` of either `EXPECTED_LOSS`, the base regulatory view, or `STRESSED`, the capital-planning view. `stress_multiplier_bps` accompanies the stressed variant, defaults to 25000 basis points, and must lie between 10000 and 100000 basis points inclusive; under `EXPECTED_LOSS` it is ignored even when supplied. The input set is every `Merchant` whose `account_state` is `ACTIVE` or `RESTRICTED`, taken in `created_at` ascending order; `TERMINATED` merchants are excluded even when they still hold reserve. `merchant_count` records how many merchants were included, and `generated_by_user_id` the acting controller.

Before anything is projected, the trailing metrics of every included merchant are refreshed with the window ending at `as_of_date`, exactly as §3.6 describes.

**Per merchant.** The probability of default in basis points is selected from the **amount-based** ratio variant, and only that variant: 100 basis points when `amount_based_chargeback_ratio_bps` is below 65; 300 when it is 65 or more but below 90; 900 when it is 90 or more but below 180; and 2000 when it is 180 or more. A merchant whose `account_state` is `RESTRICTED` carries a further 500 basis points of penalty on top of its tier. Under `STRESSED`, and only under `STRESSED`, the resulting figure is then multiplied by `stress_multiplier_bps`, divided by 10000, rounded half up, and capped at 10000 basis points — a certainty of default. That merchant's unpaid exposure is the sum of `net_payout_cents` over its settlement batches whose `payout_state` is `SCHEDULED`, `DELAYED` or `HELD`; paid and reversed batches are excluded. Its gross expected loss is that unpaid exposure multiplied by the probability of default, divided by 10000, multiplied by a loss-given-default of 6000 basis points, divided by 10000, with a **single** half-up rounding applied at the end of that chain rather than one per factor. Its net expected loss is the greater of 0 and its gross expected loss less the `held_balance_cents` of its reserve account — the floor is applied **per merchant**, so an over-reserved merchant contributes 0 rather than a negative offset to its peers.

**Portfolio roll-up.** `unpaid_exposure_cents`, `gross_expected_loss_cents`, `net_expected_loss_cents` and `total_reserve_held_cents` are the sums across included merchants of unpaid exposure, gross expected loss, net expected loss and reserve held balance respectively. `coverage_gap_cents` is the greater of 0 and `gross_expected_loss_cents` less `total_reserve_held_cents`.

**The frozen adequacy ratio.** `reserve_adequacy_ratio_bps` is `total_reserve_held_cents` multiplied by 10000, divided by `gross_expected_loss_cents`, rounded half up, and exactly 0 when `gross_expected_loss_cents` is 0. This value is then **frozen**: it is the sole input to the tier that sizes the additional reserve demanded, and it is never recomputed afterwards. `required_additional_reserve_cents` is 0 when `reserve_adequacy_ratio_bps` is 10000 basis points or more — the book is fully covered; otherwise it is `coverage_gap_cents` multiplied by 2500, divided by 10000, rounded half up when `reserve_adequacy_ratio_bps` is 7500 or more — a partial call for a quarter of the gap; otherwise it is the whole `coverage_gap_cents`.

Adding that reserve would of course improve the adequacy ratio, and the forecast reports the improvement separately rather than letting it feed back. `projected_adequacy_ratio_bps` is the sum of `total_reserve_held_cents` and `required_additional_reserve_cents`, multiplied by 10000, divided by `gross_expected_loss_cents`, rounded half up, and 0 when `gross_expected_loss_cents` is 0. Re-selecting the tier from this projected ratio, or iterating the two steps to a fixed point, is explicitly wrong. For identical portfolio data the two methods can differ on `gross_expected_loss_cents`, `net_expected_loss_cents`, `reserve_adequacy_ratio_bps`, `required_additional_reserve_cents` and the selected tier itself.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Operations that bring a new record into existence — a category rule, an application, a beneficial owner, a verification attempt, a credit assessment, a day of activity, a monitoring sweep, a reserve release request, a remediation program, a termination decision, an appeal or a forecast — return `201 Created`. Operations that modify an existing record, including every state transition, return `200 OK`, as do all reads and the logical-clock capability. Path variables are always the target resource's `id`.
