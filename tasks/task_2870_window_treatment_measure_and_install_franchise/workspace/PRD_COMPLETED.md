# Window Treatment Measure-and-Install Franchise API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A custom window-covering franchise sells a product that does not exist until somebody drives to a house and writes six numbers on a form. Between the homeowner's first phone call and the moment a motorized shade answers a voice command, the brand's money passes through a national call centre, a local franchise office, a designer's van, a fabric library, a manufacturer's production queue and an installer's ladder. Every one of those hand-offs is a place where a quarter of an inch, a missed cutoff hour or an uncounted sample kit turns a profitable job into a remake. This document specifies the backend service that holds that chain together: a single API, mounted under `/blinds`, through which brand headquarters charters territories, franchise owners run their books, designers measure and quote, manufacturers ship, and installers commission.

The service has two jobs that pull in opposite directions. The first is workflow orchestration — a lead must be routed to exactly one territory, an appointment must occupy exactly one slot on exactly one designer's calendar, an opening must be measured before it can be configured, configured before it can be quoted, quoted before it can be ordered, and received before it can be installed. The second is money: every retail price, every wholesale exposure, every royalty accrual and every chargeback must be reproducible to the cent from stored inputs, months after the fact, by an auditor who was not in the room. The two jobs collide constantly. A designer who mis-measures a nursery window costs the franchise a remake, the franchise a chargeback, the designer a liability point and headquarters a royalty reversal — one field mistake, four ledgers.

Three design commitments run through everything that follows. Time is a **logical clock** the service owns, not the wall clock, so that lead times, hold periods, quote validity and rolling liability windows are all testable. Money is always an integer number of cents, never a floating-point dollar amount, and the exact sequence in which amounts are rounded is part of the specification rather than an implementation detail. And nothing important happens invisibly: every cascade-created accrual lands as a row on a territory's ledger, which is the only place a reader can see what an install exception really cost.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Actors, Identity Headers and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the API — including the test-driving clock endpoint — must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting user and `X-User-Role` names the role that user is acting in. A request whose `X-User-ID` is absent, empty, or does not resolve to a known actor holding a role is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). A request whose resolved role is not among the roles the target endpoint permits is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403). An `X-User-Role` that is missing, or whose value is not exactly one of the four spellings below, resolves to no actor at all and is therefore `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` rather than `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`; and because the role-to-entity binding described below is part of resolution, an `X-User-ID` presented as a `Franchisee`, `Designer` or `VendorRep` that names no existing `Territory`, `Designer` or `Vendor` row is `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` too, never a not-found error. Authentication is always evaluated before authorization, and both are evaluated before anything else about the request is examined.

Four roles exist, and their names are spelled exactly `Franchisor`, `Franchisee`, `Designer` and `VendorRep`. They are deliberately **peer roles** rather than a privilege ladder: `Franchisor` is not a superset of the others. Brand headquarters charters territories, onboards manufacturers, drives the logical clock and reads every royalty ledger, but headquarters can never measure an opening, price a quote, install a blind or commission a motor. Conversely a `Designer` runs the entire in-home workflow but cannot charter a territory, hire a colleague, or place a manufacturing order.

Identity binds to resources differently for each role, and the binding is what makes ownership checks meaningful:

- When `X-User-Role` is `Franchisor`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it for audit purposes but never compares it to any entity's `id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `Franchisee`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Territory`. A franchise owner operates exactly one territory, so ownership checks on any row that references a territory compare that row's territory linkage to `X-User-ID`. A `Franchisee` who reads or writes a `Territory`, `Designer`, `SampleKit`, `Lead` or `VendorOrder` row belonging to another territory is rejected with `ERR_TERRITORY_OWNERSHIP_REQUIRED` (HTTP 403). An `InstallJob` belongs to that same set, reached through its order's quote; and placing a manufacturing order is checked the same way against the territory reached through the *named quote*, even though no `VendorOrder` row yet exists to own. A row whose territory linkage is null — an unrouted `QUEUED` or `UNROUTABLE` lead, for instance — references no territory and therefore belongs to no other territory, so this check does not withhold such a row from any acting franchise owner.
- When `X-User-Role` is `Designer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Designer`. Every designer write is gated on being the assigned designer: the consultation being closed out, the consultation behind a sample check-out, the consultation behind an opening, the consultation behind a quote, and the install job being commissioned or signed off must all name this designer. A designer acting on somebody else's work is rejected with `ERR_DESIGNER_OWNERSHIP_REQUIRED` (HTTP 403).
- When `X-User-Role` is `VendorRep`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Vendor`. A manufacturer's dispatcher holds exactly one write in the whole API — reporting one of its own orders shipped — and may report only orders whose manufacturing vendor is itself.

Ownership is checked **after** existence. Probing an id that belongs to another territory therefore yields HTTP 403, while probing an id that does not exist at all yields HTTP 404. This ordering is deliberate and observable.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`, followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the upper-case letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. It is the one instant in the whole API read from the host's real wall clock rather than from the logical clock of §2.4, so it does not move when `POST /_clock/set_current_utc` moves the logical clock.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.
- Failures the service does not itself raise are reported in this same envelope with the `error_id` `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and the transport status code rather than 400: a request to a path the service does not serve is 404 — a trailing slash is not such a case, since every path addresses the same endpoint with or without one — a method the addressed path does not accept is 405, a request the HTTP layer itself rejects as malformed is 400, and an unhandled server fault is 500. `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` is therefore the only identifier in §5 that can appear alongside a status other than the one tabulated for it.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query-string parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. The two failure modes carry different identifiers: a value that is not a base-ten integer is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400), while a value that parses as an integer but falls outside the domain just stated is `ERR_VALUE_OUT_OF_RANGE` (HTTP 422); either parameter supplied as an empty string is treated as absent and takes its default, and there is no upper bound on `offset` at all — an offset past the end of the collection is a legal request returning an empty `data` array with the true `total_count`. Both are echoed back in `meta`, alongside `total_count` (the number of rows matching the request's filters before pagination is applied) and `has_more` (true when rows remain beyond the current page).

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending — oldest row first. Because the logical clock stands still between clock-setting calls, `created_at` ties are the normal case rather than the exception, and they are broken by the order in which the service created the rows, earliest first; that creation sequence is a total order over every row in the store, is the final tiebreaker of every listing in the API, and is never itself exposed as a response field. The one documented exception to the primary key is the territory ledger listing, which is ordered by `occurred_at` ascending and falls back to `created_at` ascending for rows stamped at the same logical instant, and to the creation sequence when both stamps tie, because a single cascading request can write several ledger rows at one clock value and their relative order still has to be stable.

Filter parameters on list endpoints are query-string parameters and combine conjunctively: a request supplying both a territory filter and a status filter returns only rows matching both. A filter matches by exact equality between the supplied text and the stored value's own textual form; a filter supplied as an empty string is ignored as though it had been omitted, a query parameter the endpoint does not declare as a filter is ignored rather than rejected, and a row whose stored value for a filtered field is null matches no supplied filter value at all — there is no way to ask a listing for the rows where a reference is unset. A list endpoint is never narrowed any further by the identity of the caller: the rows it considers are the whole collection, reduced only by the filters the request itself supplies, so a `Franchisee` that omits the inversion filters of §2.10 sees rows belonging to every territory. The territory ledger listing is the one listing confined to a single territory, and it is confined by the `{territory_id}` in its path rather than by an implicit caller filter.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and the Logical Clock

Every resource the API mints carries an `id` that is a server-generated UUID string with no type prefix. Clients never supply an `id`. Every resource also carries a `created_at` timestamp, an ISO 8601 UTC instant rendered with exactly three fractional-second digits (for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`). Every stored instant the service stamps for itself is read from the logical clock described below rather than from real time — `created_at` and `updated_at`, a ledger row's `occurred_at`, and every `*_at` transition stamp alike — so two rows written without an intervening clock move carry byte-identical timestamps, and a stamp is always rendered at exactly millisecond width: any finer precision the clock was set with is retained internally for comparisons but truncated, not rounded, on the way out.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC instant in the same format that is refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by one on every mutation of the row. A `PATCH` that supplies no recognised field at all, or supplies only values identical to the ones already stored, still counts as a mutation: it refreshes `updated_at` and increments `version` exactly as a substantive change would. Recomputing a derived counter counts as a mutation: when a lead is routed and the winning territory's open-lead count changes, that territory's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` incremented, even though no client sent a field for it. The single exception is `LedgerEntry`. A ledger row is immutable once written, so it carries only `id` and `created_at` — it has neither `updated_at` nor `version`, and neither field appears in its response body.

Time is supplied by a **logical clock** owned by the server. Its startup default is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; before any clock-setting call the logical clock reads that instant, and it never falls back to real wall-clock time. Every deadline, hold window, lateness computation, lead-time date and rolling liability window in this specification reads the logical clock and nothing else. A `Franchisor` may move the clock to any supplied `current_utc` instant, forwards or backwards — no monotonicity is required — and that call is the one write in the API that returns no resource: it answers HTTP 200 with an empty object as its `data`. Moving the clock is a pure test-driving operation: it advances no state machine and re-runs no calculation. Quote expiry, remake windows and rolling liability windows are all evaluated **lazily**, by the next business request that touches the affected row.

### 2.5. Money, Inches and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts in this system are integer numbers of cents, carried in fields whose names end in `_cents`, and are represented in JSON as integers rather than strings or decimals. Wherever a monetary result is derived from a percentage, a rate or a fraction, the result is rounded **half up** to a whole cent — a value of exactly one half cent rounds away from zero.

The number of rounding steps and their position in the arithmetic are part of the contract, not a matter of taste, because moving a rounding step changes the answer by cents that then propagate into royalties. Two rules govern the whole domain. First, retail pricing rounds **per opening**: each opening's square footage is rounded up to its own billing unit and its own fabric charge is rounded to the cent before anything is summed. Second, wholesale cost rounds **once over the summed retail figure**: the cost of an order is derived from the total retail value of its openings and rounded a single time. These two conventions are deliberately asymmetric, and computing either one the other way round produces a different integer.

Linear measurements are inches expressed as decimal numbers with at most three decimal places. The spread that describes how far out of square an opening is, is rounded half up to three decimal places. Finished order dimensions are different: they are always exact multiples of one eighth of an inch, and the **direction** of that rounding is chosen by the mount decision rather than by proximity — an inside mount always rounds down to the next lower eighth so the blind can never bind in the pocket, and an outside mount always rounds up to the next higher eighth so the blind always covers the opening. Percentages such as a royalty rate or a discount fraction are decimal numbers and preserve the precision they were supplied with; a royalty rate carries at most four decimal places. Inches and percentages alike are rendered in JSON as bare numbers rather than as strings, and carry no significant trailing zeros: an `ordered_width_in` of 48.500 is emitted as `48.5` and a `discount_pct` of 0.05 as `0.05`.

Dates that describe manufacturing — the effective placement date and the promised ship date — are whole UTC calendar dates rendered as `YYYY-MM-DD` strings, and date arithmetic on them counts every calendar day, ignoring weekends and public holidays. Whole-day and whole-date arithmetic never truncates instants to midnight where an instant is called for: quote validity, for instance, is exact instant arithmetic.

### 2.6. Franchise Standing, Territory Exclusivity and the Ledger

A `Territory` is the franchised business unit and the account on which all money lands. Its `status` is one of `ACTIVE`, `PROBATION`, `SUSPENDED` or `TERMINATED`. Only an `ACTIVE` territory receives routed leads or permits new work; `PROBATION`, `SUSPENDED` and `TERMINATED` all block it identically, with `ERR_TERRITORY_NOT_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409). Closing a franchise to new work does not disturb rows already in flight — a suspension cancels nothing retroactively.

Territories hold **exclusive** postal-code coverage. No postal code may appear in the `postal_codes` set of two territories whose `status` is anything other than `TERMINATED`; a terminated territory's codes are released and may be re-issued. Because a released code may still be recorded on a terminated row, the lead router must be able to resolve a genuine multi-candidate pool deterministically rather than assuming the pool is always a single territory.

Every accrual against a franchise account is recorded as an immutable `LedgerEntry`. A ledger row names the `territory_id` it lands on, an `entry_type` drawn from `NET_SALES_ROYALTY`, `ROYALTY_REVERSAL`, `REMAKE_CHARGEBACK`, `REMEASURE_CHARGEBACK`, `SAMPLE_LATE_FEE` and `SAMPLE_REPLACEMENT`, a signed `amount_cents`, the `basis_cents` the amount was derived from (net sales for royalty rows, remake cost for chargeback rows, kit replacement value for sample rows), the `occurred_at` logical instant of the triggering transition, and a fixed machine-readable `memo` naming that transition — `quote_accepted`, `install_trueup`, `remake_replacement`, `remake_full_credit`, `sample_late`, `sample_lost` or `remeasure_chargeback`. A positive `amount_cents` increases what the territory owes; a negative one reverses a prior accrual. `ROYALTY_REVERSAL` rows never carry a positive amount and every other entry type never carries a negative one. The sign is strict for the four non-royalty types, but a royalty amount is a rounded product and is therefore zero whenever the territory's `royalty_rate_pct` is zero or the basis being reconciled has not moved; the acceptance row of §3.6 and the full-credit reversal of §3.8 are both written unconditionally and so may carry an `amount_cents` of exactly zero, whereas the install true-up of §3.9 is the one accrual in the API that a zero amount suppresses entirely.

Ledger rows point back at whatever caused them through a **polymorphic** pair of fields rather than a single foreign key: `source_entity_type` is one of `QUOTE`, `VENDOR_ORDER`, `INSTALL_JOB` or `SAMPLE_CHECKOUT`, and `source_entity_id` holds the id of the row of that kind. Because no write endpoint returns the ledger rows it created, the territory's ledger listing is the only channel through which cascade-created accruals are observable; it accepts optional `entry_type` and `source_entity_type` filters.

### 2.7. The Validation Ladder

When a single request violates several rules at once, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fully determined. Checks are evaluated in the following order, and the first failure decides the response:

1. **Authentication** — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`, on every endpoint including `POST /_clock/set_current_utc`.
2. **Authorization** — the actor's role must appear in the endpoint's permitted roles list; otherwise `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`, on every endpoint.
3. **Path existence** — every `{id}` path parameter must resolve to an existing row, validated left to right, with the first unresolvable segment deciding which of `ERR_TERRITORY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DESIGNER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SAMPLE_KIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LEAD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONSULTATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SAMPLE_CHECKOUT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OPENING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_QUOTE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VENDOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VENDOR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_INSTALL_JOB_NOT_FOUND` is returned (all HTTP 404). This applies to every endpoint carrying a path parameter.
4. **Body reference existence** — every foreign key carried in the request body must resolve, validated in the order the fields are declared for that endpoint, returning the matching not-found error. This applies to `POST /blinds/designers`, `POST /blinds/sample-kits`, `POST /blinds/consultations`, `POST /blinds/sample-checkouts`, `POST /blinds/openings`, `POST /blinds/quotes`, `POST /blinds/vendor-orders`, `POST /blinds/install-jobs` and `POST /blinds/install-jobs/{id}/exception`. Consultation booking, for example, resolves the lead before the designer, so a request naming two bad ids reports the lead. Only a value that is present and is a JSON string is resolved at this stage: a foreign key that is absent altogether, explicitly null, or carried as some other JSON type is not a resolution failure and is left for stage 7 to reject with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, so a create that omits its reference reports a malformed request rather than a not-found.
5. **Territory ownership** — `ERR_TERRITORY_OWNERSHIP_REQUIRED`, on all `Franchisee` operations against `Territory`, `Designer`, `SampleKit`, `Lead`, `VendorOrder` and `InstallJob` rows, and on the two creates that name a parent whose territory is the one that matters: `POST /blinds/vendor-orders` is checked against the territory of the named `Quote`, and `POST /blinds/install-jobs` against the territory of the named `VendorOrder` and then that of the named `Designer`.
6. **Designer assignment** — `ERR_DESIGNER_OWNERSHIP_REQUIRED`, on `POST /blinds/consultations/{id}/complete`, `POST /blinds/consultations/{id}/no-show`, `POST /blinds/sample-checkouts`, `POST /blinds/sample-checkouts/{id}/return`, `POST /blinds/openings`, `PATCH /blinds/openings/{id}/configuration`, `POST /blinds/quotes`, `POST /blinds/quotes/{id}/present`, `POST /blinds/quotes/{id}/revise`, `POST /blinds/quotes/{id}/accept`, `POST /blinds/install-jobs/{id}/commissioning/pair`, `POST /blinds/install-jobs/{id}/commissioning/verify`, `POST /blinds/install-jobs/{id}/exception` and `POST /blinds/install-jobs/{id}/complete`.
7. **Request shape** — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) on every write endpoint when a required field is missing, a field carries the wrong JSON type, an enum value is not a declared member, an instant is not a valid ISO 8601 UTC string — a client-supplied instant must place the literal `T` between the date and the time and must carry an explicit UTC designator, either a trailing `Z` or a zero `+00:00`/`-00:00` offset, so a space-separated instant, one with no designator at all, and one carrying a non-zero offset are all invalid here — or an array breaches its declared cardinality or contains duplicates, or a field declared as a postal code is not exactly five decimal digits. The trailing `Z` may be written in either upper or lower case. The body is parsed from the request's raw bytes whatever `Content-Type` the request declares. An absent request body and an empty one are both read as the empty JSON object, so an endpoint with no required fields accepts a bodyless call; a body that is not valid JSON, or is valid JSON but not an object, is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; and an explicit JSON `null` is indistinguishable from an omitted key, so a `null` on a required field is a missing field while a `null` on an optional field takes that field's documented default. A JSON boolean is never accepted where an integer or a number is declared. Fields the server owns — anything described in this document as derived, computed or system-stamped — are **silently ignored** when a client supplies them, never rejected.
8. **Numeric and interval domains** — `ERR_VALUE_OUT_OF_RANGE` (HTTP 422). Each of the six wall readings and the pocket depth must be strictly greater than zero, at most 300.000, and carry at most three decimal places. A royalty rate must be at least 0, at most 0.15, and carry at most four decimal places — the decimal-place limit is a domain failure here, not a shape failure at stage 7. A vendor cutoff hour must be between 0 and 23 inclusive. Every capacity and count input must be at least 1. A scheduled end instant must be strictly after its start instant; a hold end instant must be strictly after its hold start instant and no more than fourteen days after it; a hold start instant must be at or after the logical clock. A paired-channel count must be at least 0. This stage guards `POST /blinds/territories`, `PATCH /blinds/territories/{id}`, `POST /blinds/vendors`, `POST /blinds/consultations`, `POST /blinds/sample-checkouts`, `POST /blinds/openings`, `POST /blinds/install-jobs` and `POST /blinds/install-jobs/{id}/commissioning/pair`.
9. **Territory coherence of a linkage** — `ERR_CROSS_TERRITORY_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 422) on `POST /blinds/consultations`, `POST /blinds/sample-checkouts` and `POST /blinds/install-jobs`.
10. **Membership of a named opening** — `ERR_OPENING_NOT_ON_QUOTE` (HTTP 422) on `POST /blinds/vendor-orders`, `POST /blinds/install-jobs/{id}/exception` and `POST /blinds/openings`.
11. **Stale parent version** — `ERR_STALE_CONSULTATION_VERSION` on `POST /blinds/quotes` and `ERR_STALE_QUOTE_VERSION` on `POST /blinds/vendor-orders`, both HTTP 412. See §2.8.
12. **Franchise standing** — `ERR_TERRITORY_NOT_ACTIVE`, checked before any state precondition on the addressed row itself, so a suspended franchise blocks work even on correctly-staged rows. It guards `POST /blinds/designers`, `POST /blinds/sample-kits`, `POST /blinds/consultations`, `POST /blinds/sample-checkouts`, `POST /blinds/openings`, `POST /blinds/quotes`, `POST /blinds/quotes/{id}/present`, `POST /blinds/quotes/{id}/accept`, `POST /blinds/vendor-orders` and `POST /blinds/install-jobs`.
13. **Designer standing** — `ERR_DESIGNER_NOT_ACTIVE` on `POST /blinds/consultations` and `POST /blinds/install-jobs` when the named designer is `SUSPENDED` or `INACTIVE`.
14. **Quote expiry** — `ERR_QUOTE_EXPIRED`, evaluated before the per-endpoint status precondition so that an expired quote reports expiry rather than a status mismatch, on `POST /blinds/quotes/{id}/present`, `POST /blinds/quotes/{id}/revise`, `POST /blinds/quotes/{id}/accept` and `POST /blinds/vendor-orders`.
15. **Per-endpoint state preconditions** — the status checks described in §3, in the order given there: quote draft, presented and accepted checks, the revision cap, consultation status, kit availability, check-out openness, opening measure status, quote-has-openings, opening-already-ordered, the three vendor-order status checks, the install-job status check, and the three commissioning-status checks.
16. **Safety and compliance** — the child-safety cordless requirement, the inside-mount depth requirement, the motorization certification requirement and the hub-platform requirement. Safety deliberately sits **below** state: a request against a wrongly-staged resource is never evaluated for cord-hazard compliance at all, so when both are violated the caller sees the state error.
17. **Remaining constraints** — channel-count, commissioning-attempt, motor-option, remake-policy and remake-limit checks.
18. **Temporal conflicts** — the designer calendar check, the sample-kit hold check, the vendor capacity check and the vendor remake window check.
19. **Fiscal capacity** — `ERR_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, evaluated **last** of all, after vendor eligibility and after capacity, so an order that breaches both capacity and credit reports the capacity error.

### 2.8. Stale-Version Protection on Quote Creation and Order Placement

Two write operations in this API are guarded by optimistic concurrency control against a **parent** row's `version`. Both guards exist because the operation legitimately re-arranges work the client may not have observed, so the service forces the writer to prove it read the parent recently.

A `POST /blinds/quotes` request must carry `expected_consultation_version` as a non-optional field holding the client's last observed `version` of the named `Consultation`. If that value does not equal the consultation's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CONSULTATION_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and nothing is written. The reason is that quote creation supersedes any earlier draft or presented quote on the same consultation and re-attaches its openings: a create built on a stale view of the consultation would silently discard a price the homeowner has already seen, or would sweep in an opening set the writer never observed. Because recording an opening and patching an opening's configuration both increment the consultation's `version`, the value must be read back immediately before the create.

A `POST /blinds/vendor-orders` request must carry `expected_quote_version` as a non-optional field holding the client's last observed `version` of the named `Quote`. A mismatch against the quote's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_QUOTE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). An accepted quote may legitimately be split across several manufacturing orders, so a second placement against the same quote is a normal operation rather than a duplicate; it is refused only when the writer's view of which openings are still unplaced has gone out of date. Each successful placement increments the quote's `version`, so a second placement must carry the version read after the first one landed.

No other endpoint takes a version field. Endpoints not named above perform no version check, and supplying a version field to them has no effect.

### 2.9. Cascades, Derived Counters and Compensating Actions

Many transitions in this domain reach beyond the row the caller addressed. Three principles govern that reach.

**Derived counters are re-derived, never incremented in place.** Every count and running total described in this document as derived — a territory's open-lead count, its three recomputable money totals and its three kit counters, a kit's loan counters, a consultation's three opening and loan counters, a quote's opening counts, a vendor's open-order count, a designer's three incident counters — is recomputed from the rows it counts each time one of those rows changes state. The three recomputable territory totals are `royalty_accrued_cents` and `remake_chargeback_cents`, each summed from that territory's own ledger rows, and `open_order_exposure_cents`, summed from its live orders; `net_sales_cents` is the one figure on the territory that is *not* re-derived, being carried forward instead by the signed adjustments §3.6, §3.8 and §3.9 each describe. This matters most for the rolling designer liability window, where a row ageing out of the window silently raises the score on the next recompute, and for the sample library, where re-derivation makes drift across a check-out / return / check-out sequence impossible.

**Every entity a cascade touches is a mutation of that entity.** When a lead reaches a terminal status and its territory's open-lead count drops, the territory's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` incremented. When an install exception moves an opening, that opening is mutated; when the same exception re-places a vendor order, that order is mutated. For a territory, a sample kit, a consultation and a vendor this holds unconditionally: a transition that re-derives one of those rows bumps it even when every recomputed counter comes back identical to what it already held, so version numbers move for reasons no client sent a field for. Two rows behave differently and deliberately so. A designer is bumped only when a recompute actually changes one of its three counters or its `remeasure_liability_score`, or when that recompute suspends it — an idempotent re-score leaves the row's `updated_at` and `version` exactly as they were. And a quote's `opening_count` and `motorized_opening_count` are re-derived without any bump of their own, so a quote's `version` moves only for the transitions §3.6 through §3.9 describe as writing to the quote row itself — presentation, revision, acceptance, each successful manufacturing placement, a full-credit reversal and an installation true-up, and nothing else — which is what makes the guard of §2.8 usable at all. A caller who is holding a version number for a parent row must expect a cascade elsewhere in the system to have invalidated it, which is exactly the situation §2.8 protects against.

**Money-moving operations have exact compensating actions.** A territory's accrued royalty is always precisely the signed sum of its `NET_SALES_ROYALTY` and `ROYALTY_REVERSAL` ledger rows. Royalty is therefore always re-derived from a full basis and posted as the difference between two independently rounded royalty figures — never by rounding the difference between two bases. The consequence, which is a hard requirement rather than a happy accident, is that fully crediting a quote's entire opening set returns the territory's accrued royalty to exactly its pre-acceptance value, with no residual cent.

### 2.10. Addressing and Reverse Reachability

Resources in this domain form a **flat** collection space. Every write endpoint addresses one peer collection directly beneath `/blinds` and carries its references in the request body rather than in the path; the only nested paths in the API are the territory's ledger listing and the action sub-paths that operate on a single already-addressed resource. There is consequently no deep parent-child path validation to perform beyond the left-to-right existence rule of §2.7: when a path carries an identifier, that identifier is resolved before the request body is examined, and an unresolvable identifier produces the not-found error for the entity that segment addresses.

Because references travel in request bodies, the read side carries the burden of making relationships discoverable. Each entity has a single-resource read endpoint, and the list endpoints accept filter parameters that invert each stored reference: designers and sample kits and leads by `territory_id`, consultations by `lead_id` and `designer_id`, sample check-outs by `sample_kit_id` and `consultation_id`, openings by `consultation_id`, by `quote_id` and by the self-referential `replaces_opening_id`, quotes by `consultation_id`, vendor orders by `quote_id` and `vendor_id`, and install jobs by `vendor_order_id` and `designer_id`. Status filters are available alongside them — `status` on designers, sample kits, leads, consultations, sample check-outs, quotes and vendor orders, `measure_status` and `install_outcome` on openings, and `install_status` and `commissioning_status` on install jobs. An install job records the opening its exception named in `exception_opening_id`, but that field is not a discovery channel; the affected opening is found by listing openings filtered by quote.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Chartering the Franchise, Staffing It, and Routing National Demand

Brand headquarters brings a franchise into existence by chartering a territory. The charter carries a `legal_name` of between 1 and 120 characters, and the `postal_codes` the territory serves exclusively — between 1 and 40 entries, each exactly five digits, with no duplicates inside the set and no code that already appears on another territory whose `status` is not `TERMINATED`. Four commercial terms may be supplied and each has a default: `royalty_rate_pct`, the fraction of net sales owed to headquarters, defaults to 0.055 and may not be negative nor exceed 0.15, with at most four decimal places; `lead_capacity_per_week`, the number of live leads the territory may hold at once, defaults to 5 and must be at least 1; `credit_limit_cents`, the ceiling on the territory's open manufacturing exposure, defaults to twenty thousand dollars expressed as 2000000 cents and may not be negative; and `liability_window`, which is either `ROLLING_180D` or `LIFETIME` and defaults to `ROLLING_180D`. A negative `credit_limit_cents` is `ERR_VALUE_OUT_OF_RANGE` (HTTP 422) like the other domain breaches here. The exclusivity breach is not: a `postal_codes` entry already claimed by a territory whose `status` is not `TERMINATED` is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400), it is decided by the first offending entry in the order the request listed them, and — unlike the shape failures of the request-shape stage — it is evaluated **after** the numeric-domain stage rather than before it, as every uniqueness breach in this API is. A charter that both claims a taken code and carries an out-of-range royalty rate therefore reports the range error, and only a charter whose four commercial terms are all in domain ever reports the collision. A newly chartered territory is stamped `ACTIVE`, and every derived figure on it starts at zero — `open_leads_count`, `net_sales_cents`, `royalty_accrued_cents`, `remake_chargeback_cents`, `open_order_exposure_cents`, `available_kit_count`, `on_loan_kit_count` and `lost_kit_count`.

Headquarters may later re-state a territory's standing or its terms, supplying any subset of `status`, `royalty_rate_pct`, `lead_capacity_per_week`, `credit_limit_cents` and `liability_window`; the row's `version` increments. Those five are the whole of the patchable surface: `postal_codes` is fixed at charter time and a `postal_codes` value supplied here is ignored rather than rejected, so no re-statement can ever disturb the exclusivity map. Any of the four standings may be re-stated into any other, `TERMINATED` included in both directions — `TERMINATED` closes a franchise and releases its codes but is not terminal as a status, and re-activating such a territory is permitted even if its codes have since been re-issued elsewhere. Two consequences of such a re-statement are deliberately narrow. Raising `lead_capacity_per_week` does **not** promote any queued lead — only a lead of that territory actually reaching `CONVERTED` or `LOST` frees a slot and triggers a promotion. And changing `liability_window` changes which variant of the designer liability score applies at the **next** recompute for that territory's designers; nobody is re-scored retroactively.

A franchise owner hires designers into their own territory. A designer is created with a `territory_id`, a `full_name` of between 1 and 120 characters, a `home_postal_code` of exactly five digits, and an optional `certifications` array defaulting to empty, whose members are drawn without duplication from `MOTORIZATION`, `CHILD_SAFETY` and `COMMERCIAL`. The `home_postal_code` records where the designer starts the day; it plays no part in the calendar travel-buffer rule, which compares the postal codes of the two appointments being tested against each other. A new designer is stamped `ACTIVE` with `remeasure_liability_score` at the base value of 100 and all three incident counters — `remeasure_exception_count`, `late_sample_return_count` and `clean_install_count` — at zero. A designer's `status` is `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED` or `INACTIVE`, and only `ACTIVE` designers may be booked onto consultations or install jobs.

Re-stating a designer, which a franchise owner does by supplying `status` and/or `certifications`, is the only route back out of `SUSPENDED`: `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` is a deliberate franchisee override and leaves `remeasure_liability_score` exactly as it was, because suspension is sticky and a score that recovers on its own never reinstates anybody. Reinstatement also does not un-cancel the consultations and install jobs the suspension cancelled. The same operation drives `ACTIVE → INACTIVE`, which is terminal, and retiring a designer this way cancels that designer's future scheduled consultations and install jobs in precisely the same way a suspension does. That cancellation cascade is driven by the **transition**, not by the resulting status: it runs only when the request supplies a `status` that differs from the one already stored and that new status is `SUSPENDED` or `INACTIVE`, so re-stating an already-`SUSPENDED` designer as `SUSPENDED` again bumps the row and cancels nothing.

Homeowner demand enters through a lead, which either the national call centre or the local office may intake. Intake carries no ownership check of its own — there is no territory on the request to compare against — so a `Franchisee` may intake a lead whose `postal_code` belongs to somebody else's territory, and the created lead is returned to that caller already routed to the winning territory. A lead carries a `customer_name` of 1 to 120 characters, a `postal_code` of exactly five digits, a `source` that is one of `NATIONAL_CALL_CENTER`, `WEB_FORM`, `REFERRAL` or `WALK_IN`, a `requested_at` instant stating the earliest date the homeowner wants a visit, and an optional `child_occupied_dwelling` boolean defaulting to false. That boolean is the homeowner's declaration that children under nine live in or regularly visit the dwelling, and when it is true the child-safety cordless requirement of §3.6 applies to **every** opening of the job regardless of which rooms they sit in.

Routing happens inside the intake request itself, exactly once, before the response is written. A lead's `status` therefore begins at `NEW` but `NEW` is never observable in any response body — the created lead already reads `ROUTED`, `QUEUED` or `UNROUTABLE`. A territory is an eligible destination when three conditions all hold: its `status` is `ACTIVE`, so that `PROBATION`, `SUSPENDED` and `TERMINATED` franchises never receive leads; the lead's `postal_code` is a member of that territory's `postal_codes`; and its `open_leads_count` is strictly less than its `lead_capacity_per_week`. Among eligible territories the winner is chosen by comparing, in order, the largest remaining capacity — that is, `lead_capacity_per_week` minus `open_leads_count` — then the largest `royalty_rate_pct`, then the earliest `created_at`. The intent is to spread work to the emptiest book, then to the highest-yielding franchise, then to the longest-tenured one. A territory whose `open_leads_count` is one below its `lead_capacity_per_week` is still eligible and routes; one whose count has reached its capacity does not.

When routing succeeds, the lead moves `NEW → ROUTED`: its `territory_id` is set to the winning territory and its `routed_at` to the logical clock, and the winning territory's `open_leads_count` and `version` each increase by one. When the eligible pool is empty, the outcome depends on **why** it was empty, and the two outcomes are not interchangeable. If at least one `ACTIVE` territory does list the lead's `postal_code` but every such territory has an `open_leads_count` at or above its `lead_capacity_per_week`, the lead moves `NEW → QUEUED` with `territory_id` and `routed_at` left null — a retryable capacity outcome. If no `ACTIVE` territory lists the postal code at all, the lead moves `NEW → UNROUTABLE`, again with `territory_id` and `routed_at` null, and that is terminal: a lead outside every active territory is never revisited. Both outcomes still return HTTP 201 with the created lead, because a routing failure is a business state rather than a request error. Because routing runs inside the create, it is part of creating the lead rather than a later mutation of it: whichever of the three outcomes it reaches, the lead is returned with `version` 1 and with `updated_at` equal to `created_at`, even though its `status`, `territory_id` and `routed_at` were all settled after the row was minted. Only the lead's **later** transitions — promotion out of the queue, close-out, conversion, loss — bump its `version`. The winning territory, by contrast, is re-derived and bumped by the routing itself.

A territory's `open_leads_count` counts exactly those of its leads whose `status` is `ROUTED` or `CONSULT_COMPLETE`. When a lead leaves that pair — reaching `CONVERTED` because a quote for it was accepted, or `LOST` because its appointment was a no-show or its presented quote expired — the count drops by one and the territory's `version` increments, and that freed unit of capacity immediately promotes a queued lead. The promotion fires only when the freeing territory's `status` is `ACTIVE` and at least one `QUEUED` lead has a `postal_code` in that territory's `postal_codes`. Exactly one lead is promoted per unit of freed capacity: the `QUEUED` lead with the smallest `created_at`, tie-broken by the smallest `postal_code`, then by the smallest `customer_name`, and finally by the creation sequence of §2.3. The promotion does not re-test capacity: the freed slot is handed to the winning queued lead whether or not the re-derived `open_leads_count` is still strictly below `lead_capacity_per_week`. That lead moves `QUEUED → ROUTED` with its `territory_id` and `routed_at` stamped, and the territory's `open_leads_count` and `version` each rise by one again. A second queued lead stays queued.

Two further lead transitions carry no capacity consequence at all. A lead moves `ROUTED → CONSULT_COMPLETE` when its consultation reaches `COMPLETED`, and the territory's `open_leads_count` is unchanged because `CONSULT_COMPLETE` is still an open status. Acceptance of a quote converts the lead from whichever open status it holds — `CONSULT_COMPLETE → CONVERTED` normally, or `ROUTED → CONVERTED` when the quote is accepted while the appointment is still `SCHEDULED`. `UNROUTABLE`, `CONVERTED` and `LOST` are all terminal.

### 3.2. The Sample Library: Holds, Returns, Late Fees and Write-Offs

Fabric samples are the designer's only way of showing a homeowner what a shade will actually look like, and they are also the single most frequently lost asset in the business. Each franchise stocks its own library. A franchise owner adds a kit by naming the owning `territory_id`, a `kit_code` of between 1 and 40 characters that must be unique among that territory's kits whose `status` is not `LOST`, and a `replacement_value_cents` of at least 1 — the amount charged to the territory if the kit is written off, which also caps any late fee the kit can accumulate. Both of those constraints fail with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) rather than with the 422 of the numeric-domain stage, which does not guard this endpoint at all: a `replacement_value_cents` of 0 or below is a malformed request, and so is a `kit_code` that collides. Both are decided before the franchise-standing check, so a suspended territory adding a kit under a code it is already using reports the 400 and not `ERR_TERRITORY_NOT_ACTIVE`. The collision test is scoped to the one territory named on the request, so two territories may hold the same `kit_code`, and it skips that territory's `LOST` kits, so the code of a written-off kit is freely re-issuable to a fresh row. Adding a kit also re-derives the owning territory, raising its `available_kit_count` and its `version`. A new kit is stamped `AVAILABLE` with `loan_count` and `open_loan_count` at zero. A kit's `status` is `AVAILABLE`, `ON_LOAN` or `LOST`, and `LOST` is terminal.

Taking a kit out of the library is a designer's action and creates a check-out record naming the `sample_kit_id`, the `consultation_id` it is being taken for, and the closed hold interval the designer is reserving: `hold_start_at` and `hold_end_at`. The hold start must be at or after the logical clock, the hold end must be strictly after the hold start, and the two may be no more than fourteen days apart; a breach of any of those is `ERR_VALUE_OUT_OF_RANGE`. The kit must be `AVAILABLE` — `ON_LOAN` and `LOST` both yield `ERR_KIT_NOT_AVAILABLE` — and the appointment must still be `SCHEDULED`, since a closed file cannot take out samples, which is why `ERR_CONSULTATION_NOT_SCHEDULED` guards this operation as well as appointment close-out and no-show. The kit and the appointment must also belong to the same franchise: the kit's owning territory must equal the territory of the appointment's lead, and a mismatch is `ERR_CROSS_TERRITORY_FORBIDDEN`.

Holds are recorded, not merely observed. A requested interval is refused with `ERR_SAMPLE_KIT_HOLD_CONFLICT` when it overlaps the recorded hold interval of any check-out on the same kit whose `status` is `OPEN` **or** `RETURNED`. Both endpoints of both intervals are inclusive, so a new hold that starts exactly on an earlier hold's `hold_end_at` collides — and it collides even if that earlier kit came back early, because the library ledger records where the kit was promised to be, not only where it physically is.

A successful check-out is stamped `OPEN` with `checked_out_at` taken from the logical clock and `due_at` set equal to `hold_end_at`; `returned_at` stays null, and `days_late` and `late_fee_cents` are both zero while the loan is open. The kit moves `AVAILABLE → ON_LOAN`, its `loan_count` and `open_loan_count` each rise by one, and on the owning territory `available_kit_count` falls by one while `on_loan_kit_count` rises by one.

Those counters obey a conservation identity that holds at every observable moment. For any territory, `available_kit_count` plus `on_loan_kit_count` plus `lost_kit_count` equals the total number of kit rows the territory owns; `on_loan_kit_count` equals the number of check-outs in status `OPEN` whose kit belongs to that territory; and a kit's `open_loan_count` is always either zero or one, and is one exactly when the kit's `status` is `ON_LOAN`. `loan_count` records history rather than inventory: it counts every check-out ever created against the kit and never decreases. A check-out followed by an ordinary return is therefore an exact compensating pair on the inventory counters, while `loan_count` keeps its increment. Because a check-out whose kit belongs to a different territory is refused outright, cross-territory attempts are observable on the counters as well as on the status code.

Returning a kit is the moment lateness is priced, and everything about it is read from the logical clock at that instant. The check-out must still be `OPEN`; a record already `RETURNED` or `LOST` yields `ERR_CHECKOUT_NOT_OPEN`. If `returned_at` is at or before `due_at`, `days_late` is zero. Otherwise `days_late` is the elapsed time from `due_at` to `returned_at` divided by one day and rounded **up** to the next whole number, so one millisecond past the due instant is already one day late and a return exactly on the due instant is free.

What happens next is a threshold on that number. When `days_late` is at most fourteen, the kit is fined rather than written off: `late_fee_cents` is 750 cents per day late, capped at the kit's `replacement_value_cents` so that a fine can never exceed the asset. A kit worth 5000 cents therefore reaches its cap at seven days late, where seven times 750 is 5250, and stays at 5000 for eight through fourteen days late. Fourteen days late is the last fined return. The check-out moves `OPEN → RETURNED`, the kit moves `ON_LOAN → AVAILABLE`, `open_loan_count` falls by one, and the territory's `available_kit_count` and `on_loan_kit_count` return to their pre-checkout values. When `late_fee_cents` is greater than zero a ledger row of type `SAMPLE_LATE_FEE` is appended for that amount, with `basis_cents` equal to the kit's `replacement_value_cents`, `source_entity_type` `SAMPLE_CHECKOUT` and `memo` `sample_late`; a fee of zero writes no row.

When `days_late` is greater than fourteen the kit is written off instead of fined. `late_fee_cents` is set to the kit's full `replacement_value_cents`, the check-out moves `OPEN → LOST` and the kit moves `ON_LOAN → LOST`, `open_loan_count` falls by one, the territory's `on_loan_kit_count` falls by one and its `lost_kit_count` rises by one — and `available_kit_count` is **not** incremented, because the kit has left both live buckets permanently and `lost_kit_count` never decreases. A ledger row of type `SAMPLE_REPLACEMENT` is appended for the replacement value, with `basis_cents` also equal to that replacement value, `source_entity_type` `SAMPLE_CHECKOUT` and `memo` `sample_lost`.

Both return paths reach into the designer's record. Whenever `days_late` is greater than zero — which is always the case on the write-off path — the `late_sample_return_count` and `remeasure_liability_score` of the designer on the associated appointment are recomputed, and that recompute can suspend the designer outright, as §3.9 describes.

### 3.3. The In-Home Appointment and the Shared Designer Calendar

An in-home consultation is booked against a routed lead by either the franchise owner or a designer. The booking names the `lead_id`, the `designer_id` whose calendar it will occupy, the appointment window as `scheduled_start_at` and `scheduled_end_at`, and the `service_postal_code` of the dwelling. The end instant must be strictly after the start instant. The `service_postal_code` must equal the lead's own `postal_code`; a mismatch is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) and is decided **before** the end-after-start check, so a booking that both names the wrong postal code and inverts its window reports the 400 rather than the 422. The window itself is otherwise unconstrained in time — an appointment may be booked wholly in the past, and a lead may carry more than one appointment. The named designer's territory must be the same territory the lead was routed to, or the booking fails with `ERR_CROSS_TERRITORY_FORBIDDEN`. There is no precondition on the lead's own `status`: that territory-agreement test is the only thing standing between a booking and an unsuitable lead, so a `QUEUED` or `UNROUTABLE` lead — which is linked to no territory at all — is refused with `ERR_CROSS_TERRITORY_FORBIDDEN` rather than with any lead-status error, while a lead already `CONVERTED` or `LOST` inside the designer's own territory books exactly like an open one. The designer must be `ACTIVE`, since a `SUSPENDED` or `INACTIVE` designer cannot be booked at all, which is `ERR_DESIGNER_NOT_ACTIVE`.

A designer keeps **one** calendar, and both kinds of appointment share it: consultations that are `SCHEDULED` and install jobs whose `install_status` is `SCHEDULED` both occupy it, while rows that have reached `CANCELLED`, `COMPLETED`, `NO_SHOW` or `EXCEPTION_RAISED` free their slot. Occupation intervals are half-open — the start instant belongs to the appointment and the end instant does not — so two appointments that touch exactly, where the candidate starts at the very instant an existing one ends, do not collide **provided they are in the same postal code**.

Different postal codes require travel. The buffer applied to a pair of appointments is zero minutes when the existing appointment's `service_postal_code` equals the candidate's, and forty-five minutes otherwise. The candidate collides with an existing appointment when the candidate's start is strictly before the existing appointment's end plus the buffer **and** the existing appointment's start is strictly before the candidate's end plus the buffer. A booking is admissible only when it collides with no member of that designer's occupied set; otherwise it is rejected with `ERR_DESIGNER_CALENDAR_CONFLICT`. The test is strictly pairwise — no intervals are merged and nothing is counted — and the buffer is a property of the pair rather than of the candidate, so the very same candidate window can be legal against one neighbour and illegal against another. Across different postal codes a gap of exactly forty-five minutes is admissible while a gap of forty-four minutes and 59.999 seconds is not.

A booked appointment is stamped `SCHEDULED`, with `measured_opening_count`, `configured_opening_count` and `open_sample_loan_count` at zero and `completed_at` null. The first counter counts the appointment's openings whose `measure_status` is `MEASURED`, `CONFIGURED` or `QUOTED`; the second counts only those that are `CONFIGURED`; the third counts its check-outs still in status `OPEN`. An appointment's `status` is one of `SCHEDULED`, `COMPLETED`, `NO_SHOW` and `CANCELLED`, and the last three are all terminal.

Closing out a visit is the assigned designer's action and drives `SCHEDULED → COMPLETED`. It requires the appointment to still be `SCHEDULED`, the acting designer to be the one assigned, at least one measured opening on the record, and — crucially — `open_sample_loan_count` to be exactly zero. A designer cannot close the file while a kit is still in the van. All three of those business preconditions report the **same** identifier, `ERR_CONSULTATION_NOT_SCHEDULED` (HTTP 409), so the status of the appointment, the absence of any measured opening and an outstanding loan are indistinguishable from the error code alone; they are tested in that order, with the status test first, the measured-opening count second and the open-loan count last. The two counts are re-derived from the underlying rows before being tested, and a close-out that fails one of them writes nothing and leaves the appointment's `version` where it was. Close-out stamps `completed_at` from the logical clock, frees the calendar slot for that window, and drives the lead `ROUTED → CONSULT_COMPLETE`.

When the homeowner is not at home, the assigned designer records a no-show, driving `SCHEDULED → NO_SHOW`. This requires the appointment to be `SCHEDULED` and the logical clock to have reached or passed `scheduled_start_at` — nobody can be declared absent from an appointment that has not started. Both requirements report `ERR_CONSULTATION_NOT_SCHEDULED` (HTTP 409), the status test first and the clock test second, so an early no-show against a properly `SCHEDULED` appointment is indistinguishable by error code from a no-show against an appointment that has already been closed out. A no-show is terminal for the appointment and fatal for the lead: it drives the lead `ROUTED → LOST`, which decrements the territory's `open_leads_count`, which in turn promotes one queued lead. The calendar slot is freed.

The third exit is not a human action at all. When the assigned designer is auto-suspended or retired, every one of their consultations that is still `SCHEDULED` and whose `scheduled_start_at` is at or after the logical clock moves `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED`. Cancellation releases the calendar occupation so a replacement booking is accepted, and it deliberately leaves the lead in whatever status it holds — a cancelled appointment loses the appointment, not the lead. Appointments already in the past are left alone.

### 3.4. Measuring an Opening: Six Wall Readings to Ordered Dimensions

Walls are not square, and a custom blind that assumes they are will either bind in its pocket or leave a light gap. The measurement record is therefore not one width and one height but six readings plus a pocket depth, all recorded by the assigned designer against the appointment. An opening names its `consultation_id`, a human `label` of between 1 and 80 characters such as "Nursery west window", the `room_type` it sits in — one of `BEDROOM`, `NURSERY`, `LIVING_ROOM`, `KITCHEN`, `BATH` or `OFFICE` — and the `mount_type`, either `INSIDE` or `OUTSIDE`. The readings themselves are `width_top_in`, `width_middle_in` and `width_bottom_in` taken at the head, the mid-height and the sill, `height_left_in`, `height_center_in` and `height_right_in` taken at the left jamb, the centre and the right jamb, and `depth_in`, the usable pocket depth from the glass to the front of the jamb. All eight are inches. A re-measure trip additionally supplies `replaces_opening_id`, naming the opening it supersedes.

Measuring survives the end of the appointment. An opening may be recorded while the consultation is `SCHEDULED` or after it has reached `COMPLETED`, because a corrective trip and a later quote revision both legitimately attach to a closed-out visit; only a `CANCELLED` or `NO_SHOW` appointment closes the file for good, and recording against one of those is `ERR_CONSULTATION_CLOSED`. When `replaces_opening_id` is supplied it must resolve to an existing opening — an unresolvable id is `ERR_OPENING_NOT_FOUND` — and that opening must belong to the same consultation, or the request fails with `ERR_OPENING_NOT_ON_QUOTE`. A supersession is only meaningful against an opening whose `measure_status` is `REMEASURE_REQUIRED`, but that is guidance about when to use the field rather than a validation rule: same-consultation membership is the **only** thing tested about the superseded row, so naming an opening that is still `MEASURED`, `CONFIGURED` or `QUOTED` is accepted, the superseded row is left completely untouched by the supersession, and nothing stops two openings from naming the same predecessor. For a first measurement `replaces_opening_id` is simply omitted and stays null.

An inside mount is refused outright when the pocket is too shallow to hold the headrail: `depth_in` must be at least 2.500 inches, and anything less is `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_MOUNT_DEPTH`. An outside mount, which hangs on the face of the wall, carries no depth requirement at all.

The finished dimensions the vendor will cut to are **derived here and never supplied by the caller**. From the three width readings the service takes the narrowest and the widest; from the three height readings, the shortest and the tallest. The opening's `out_of_square_in` is the larger of the two spreads — widest width minus narrowest width, against tallest height minus shortest height — rounded half up to three decimal places. It is the number that later decides who pays for an out-of-square install failure.

The mount decision then chooses both the arithmetic and the rounding direction. For an `INSIDE` mount the service starts from the **narrowest** width and the **shortest** height, because the blind has to pass the tightest point of the pocket: it subtracts a width deduction of 0.500 inches from the narrowest width and a height deduction of 0.250 inches from the shortest height, then rounds each result **down** to the next lower exact eighth of an inch. For an `OUTSIDE` mount the service starts from the **widest** width and the **tallest** height, because the blind has to cover the largest point of the opening: it adds an overlap of 1.500 inches on each of the two sides — three inches of extra width in total — and adds to the tallest height a header allowance of 3.000 inches plus a sill overlap of 1.500 inches, then rounds each result **up** to the next higher exact eighth of an inch. The two results are stored as `ordered_width_in` and `ordered_height_in`, and both are always exact multiples of 0.125.

This calculation reads nothing but the eight numbers on the request for this one opening. No other opening and no prior measurement participates: an opening created with a `replaces_opening_id` recomputes its dimensions entirely from its own fresh readings. The derivation is also unguarded arithmetic — an inside-mount deduction applied to a very small reading may drive `ordered_width_in` or `ordered_height_in` to zero or below, and such a value is stored exactly as computed and refused nowhere, because it is the twelve-inch billing floor of §3.5 rather than a validation rule that keeps the opening priceable.

A newly recorded opening is stamped with `measure_status` `MEASURED`, `install_outcome` `PENDING`, `credited` false, `list_price_cents` zero and `billable_sqft` zero, and with `quote_id`, `fabric_code` and `lift_system` all null. `motorization_option` is the exception among the unconfigured fields: it is stamped with the enum member `NONE` from the start rather than left null, so an opening that has never been configured already reports a motor package of `NONE`. The parent appointment's `measured_opening_count` is re-derived and its `version` increments — which is precisely why the stale-version guard of §2.8 makes a quote writer re-read the consultation. An opening's `measure_status` runs `MEASURED`, `CONFIGURED`, `QUOTED` and `REMEASURE_REQUIRED`, of which `REMEASURE_REQUIRED` is terminal for that row: a re-measure is always a **new** opening pointing back at the old one, never an edit of it.

### 3.5. Choosing Fabric, Lift and Motor: Pricing One Opening

Configuration is the designer's second pass over an opening and the point at which it acquires a price. The configuration payload carries `fabric_code`, which must be one of `LT-SHEER`, `CL-LINEN`, `BO-THERMAL` or `WD-FAUX`; `lift_system`, one of `CORDED`, `CORDLESS`, `CONTINUOUS_LOOP` or `MOTORIZED`; and an optional `motorization_option` defaulting to `NONE`, whose other members are `BATTERY` and `HARDWIRED`. Motor packages and lift systems must agree: `motorization_option` must be `NONE` whenever `lift_system` is anything other than `MOTORIZED`, and must **not** be `NONE` when `lift_system` is `MOTORIZED`. Either mismatch is `ERR_MOTOR_OPTION_MISMATCH`.

The opening must be in `measure_status` `MEASURED` or `CONFIGURED`. A `QUOTED` opening fails with `ERR_OPENING_NOT_MEASURED` — the quote holding it must be superseded first so the opening detaches — and a `REMEASURE_REQUIRED` opening fails with the same error permanently. As with measuring, the appointment must be `SCHEDULED` or `COMPLETED`; a `CANCELLED` or `NO_SHOW` appointment yields `ERR_CONSULTATION_CLOSED`. A first configuration drives `MEASURED → CONFIGURED` and raises the appointment's `configured_opening_count` and its `version`. Re-configuration drives `CONFIGURED → CONFIGURED`: it overwrites `fabric_code`, `lift_system` and `motorization_option`, re-prices the opening from scratch rather than adjusting the previous figure, and raises the appointment's `version`. Re-configuration is unrestricted for as long as the opening is not attached to a quote. Note that the child-safety cordless requirement is deliberately **not** checked here: a hazardous configuration is perfectly storable, and is blocked only at the point of sale, as §3.6 describes.

Pricing one opening runs in a fixed order. First the billable dimensions are established as floors, not as the ordered dimensions themselves: the billable width is the larger of `ordered_width_in` and 12.000 inches, and the billable height is the larger of `ordered_height_in` and 12.000 inches, so no opening is ever priced as smaller than a foot in either direction. Their product divided by 144 gives the raw area in square feet, and `billable_sqft` is that raw area rounded **up** to the next whole quarter of a square foot — the trade's billing unit.

The fabric charge is `billable_sqft` multiplied by the rate for the chosen `fabric_code`, rounded half up to a whole cent. The rates per square foot are 620 cents for `LT-SHEER`, 940 cents for `CL-LINEN`, 1280 cents for `BO-THERMAL` and 1550 cents for `WD-FAUX`. To that rounded fabric charge the service adds the lift uplift for the chosen `lift_system` — nothing for `CORDED`, 1800 cents for `CORDLESS`, 2600 cents for `CONTINUOUS_LOOP` and nothing for `MOTORIZED`, whose cost lives in the motor package instead — and the motor price for the chosen `motorization_option`, which is nothing for `NONE`, 12500 cents for `BATTERY` and 19500 cents for `HARDWIRED`.

An oversize surcharge is then assessed on that combined figure. When the billable width is strictly greater than 72.000 inches the surcharge rate is 0.15; otherwise it is zero. The surcharge is the sum of fabric, lift and motor charges multiplied by that rate and rounded half up to a whole cent, and it is computed on that **combined** sum, never on the fabric charge alone. `list_price_cents` is the combined sum plus the surcharge.

There are exactly two rounding steps in this calculation and they occur in that order: the fabric charge first, the oversize surcharge second. Both are per-opening. This is the asymmetry §2.5 warns about: `billable_sqft` is rounded up to the quarter square foot for each opening individually and the fabric charge is rounded to the cent for each opening individually, and a quote's subtotal then sums those already-rounded integers. Summing raw areas across a consultation's openings and rounding once, or summing unrounded fabric charges and rounding once at the quote level, is forbidden and produces a different subtotal. Three identical openings whose raw area is 6.017795 square feet each price at 5875 cents apiece for a subtotal of 17625 cents, whereas the forbidden order gives an aggregate 18.25 square feet and a subtotal of 17155 cents.

### 3.6. The Quote: Assembly, the Waterfall Total, the Child-Safety Gate and Acceptance

A quote is assembled, not authored field by field. The assigned designer creates one by naming the `consultation_id` and the `expected_consultation_version` of §2.8, and the service sweeps in **every** opening on that consultation whose `measure_status` is `CONFIGURED`. If there is no such opening — every opening still merely `MEASURED`, say — the request fails with `ERR_QUOTE_HAS_NO_OPENINGS` (HTTP 422). The appointment must be `SCHEDULED` or `COMPLETED`, so quoting also lives behind `ERR_CONSULTATION_CLOSED`, and the owning franchise must be `ACTIVE`.

A new quote is stamped `DRAFT` with `revision_count` at zero, all of its monetary fields at zero, and `presented_at`, `expires_at` and `accepted_at` null. Its `lead_id` is a denormalized copy of the appointment's own lead reference, and the service keeps that copy equal to the appointment's value on every write. Each swept opening has its `quote_id` set to the new quote and moves `CONFIGURED → QUOTED`. Two counters describe the assembled set: `opening_count` is the number of openings carrying this quote's id, and `motorized_opening_count` is the number of those whose `motorization_option` is anything other than `NONE`. Assembly increments the appointment's `version`.

Assembly also **supersedes** the previous quote on the same appointment, and this is the reason the stale-version guard exists. Any earlier quote on that consultation still in `DRAFT` moves `DRAFT → SUPERSEDED`, and any earlier quote still in `PRESENTED` — provided the logical clock is strictly before its `expires_at` — moves `PRESENTED → SUPERSEDED` and has its `expires_at` cleared to null. In either case every opening carrying the superseded quote's id moves `QUOTED → CONFIGURED` with its `quote_id` cleared to null, which is exactly what makes those openings available for the superseding quote to sweep in. Three details of that sequence are pinned. First, the whole set the new quote will carry — the consultation's `CONFIGURED` openings together with those the supersession is about to release — is determined *before* anything at all is written, so `ERR_QUOTE_HAS_NO_OPENINGS` is decided against that combined set and a create that fails on it writes nothing whatever: the earlier quotes keep their status rather than being left `SUPERSEDED` behind a failed request. Second, an opening carrying the superseded quote's id whose `measure_status` is neither `QUOTED` nor `CONFIGURED` — one an install exception has already moved to `REMEASURE_REQUIRED` — still has its `quote_id` cleared to null but keeps its own `measure_status`, so it is released without being swept onto the new quote. Third, an earlier `PRESENTED` quote the logical clock has already reached or passed is left completely alone: assembly neither supersedes it nor flips it to `EXPIRED`, because assembly is not one of the four operations that drive the expiry flip described below, so its openings stay `QUOTED` and attached to it and are unavailable to the new quote. `SUPERSEDED` is terminal. A quote's `status` is one of `DRAFT`, `PRESENTED`, `ACCEPTED`, `EXPIRED` and `SUPERSEDED`, and the last three are all terminal.

Presenting a quote to the homeowner drives `DRAFT → PRESENTED` and is the moment the price is computed. The quote must be `DRAFT` — anything else is `ERR_QUOTE_NOT_DRAFT` — it must carry at least one attached opening — otherwise `ERR_QUOTE_HAS_NO_OPENINGS` again — and every attached opening must have a non-null `fabric_code` and a non-null `lift_system`. That last condition is guaranteed structurally rather than tested: only a `CONFIGURED` opening is ever swept onto a quote and configuration is precisely what sets those two fields, so the condition carries no error identifier of its own and cannot be reached. Every attached opening must also satisfy the child-safety cordless requirement described below. The owning franchise must be `ACTIVE`.

Pricing runs as a six-step waterfall, in exactly this order, once each. The order is pinned because the discount **tier** and the discount **base** are keyed on different quantities, which superficially reads as a circular dependency. Step one sums the already-rounded `list_price_cents` of every opening attached to the quote into `subtotal_cents`. Step two computes `install_fee_cents` as a base labour charge of 7500 cents, plus 2500 cents for each attached opening, plus a further 4500 cents for each attached opening that carries a motor package — that is, 2500 cents multiplied by `opening_count` plus 4500 cents multiplied by `motorized_opening_count`, added to the 7500-cent base. Step three sets `pre_discount_total_cents` to `subtotal_cents` plus `install_fee_cents`. Step four selects `discount_pct` from `pre_discount_total_cents` alone: 0.12 when that figure is at least 500000 cents, otherwise 0.08 when it is at least 250000 cents, otherwise 0.05 when it is at least 100000 cents, otherwise zero — every comparison inclusive of its threshold. Step five computes `discount_cents` as `subtotal_cents` multiplied by `discount_pct`, rounded half up to a whole cent; the base is the goods subtotal **only**, because install labour is discount-exempt. Step six sets `total_cents`, the customer-facing price, to `pre_discount_total_cents` minus `discount_cents`, and sets `net_sales_cents`, the royalty base, to `subtotal_cents` minus `discount_cents` — equivalently `total_cents` minus `install_fee_cents`.

Step five is the only rounding step in the whole waterfall. Selecting the tier from `subtotal_cents`, or applying `discount_pct` to `pre_discount_total_cents`, are both forbidden and both produce different money: for a subtotal of 99925 cents and an install fee of 19500 cents the pinned order yields a `discount_pct` of 0.05 and `discount_cents` of 4996, while selecting the tier from the subtotal yields a discount of zero and applying the rate to the pre-discount total yields 5971. The input set is every opening carrying this quote's id, whatever its install outcome; `opening_count` and `motorized_opening_count` are counts of that same set, and openings still `CONFIGURED` on the appointment but not attached to this quote are excluded.

Presentation stamps `presented_at` from the logical clock and sets `expires_at` to exactly fourteen days after `presented_at`, computed as exact instant arithmetic with no truncation to midnight. A quote is live only while the logical clock is **strictly** before `expires_at`; the boundary is closed on the expired side, so at the very instant the clock equals `expires_at` the quote is already dead. When any of presentation, revision, acceptance, or a manufacturing-order placement naming the quote is evaluated at a clock at or after `expires_at`, the service first flips the quote `PRESENTED → EXPIRED` — and **persists that flip** — and then rejects the triggering request with `ERR_QUOTE_EXPIRED`. The state change survives the failed request. Expiry also drives the lead `CONSULT_COMPLETE → LOST`, which decrements the territory's `open_leads_count` and promotes one queued lead.

Revision pulls a presented quote back for reconfiguration, driving `PRESENTED → DRAFT`. It requires the quote to be `PRESENTED` — otherwise `ERR_QUOTE_NOT_PRESENTED` — the logical clock to be strictly before `expires_at`, and `revision_count` to be strictly less than two, the cap on revisions. Since the counter records **completed** round trips, the first revision succeeds moving the count from zero to one, the second succeeds moving it from one to two, and the third fails with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED`. A revision increments `revision_count`, clears `expires_at` to null while leaving `presented_at` at its previous value, and clears the six monetary results — `subtotal_cents`, `install_fee_cents`, `pre_discount_total_cents`, `discount_cents`, `total_cents` and `net_sales_cents` — back to zero, with `discount_pct` returning to zero alongside them, because the next presentation re-derives all seven from scratch. The attached openings stay `QUOTED` and stay attached. The next presentation re-stamps both `presented_at` and `expires_at` from the clock at that later moment, so the validity window **restarts** rather than continuing where it left off; this is the revision cycle `PRESENTED → DRAFT → PRESENTED`, legal for up to two round trips.

The child-safety cordless requirement is the brand's hard compliance line and it is evaluated at the **point of sale** rather than at configuration time. For each opening attached to a quote, the requirement applies when that opening's `room_type` is `NURSERY` or `BEDROOM`, or when the quote's lead declared `child_occupied_dwelling` true — in which case it applies to every opening on the job whatever the room. Where the requirement applies, `lift_system` must be `CORDLESS` or `MOTORIZED`; both `CORDED` and `CONTINUOUS_LOOP` are refused with `ERR_CORD_HAZARD_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 422). The requirement gates exactly three operations — presentation, acceptance, and manufacturing-order placement — and in all three the rejection carries that same error. A quote may be freely built and freely revised carrying a hazardous configuration; it simply may never be shown to the homeowner, signed, or sent to a factory.

Acceptance drives `PRESENTED → ACCEPTED` and is the moment revenue is recognized. The quote must be `PRESENTED`, the logical clock must be strictly before `expires_at`, the owning franchise must be `ACTIVE`, and every attached opening must satisfy the cordless requirement. Acceptance stamps `accepted_at` from the logical clock and runs the **provisional** royalty recognition: the recognized basis is the quote's `net_sales_cents` — that is, the subtotal less the discount, over every attached opening whatever its install outcome, because install outcomes are not yet known and are deliberately ignored. That basis is copied into `recognized_net_sales_cents` and added to the territory's `net_sales_cents`. A ledger row of type `NET_SALES_ROYALTY` is appended whose `amount_cents` is the recognized basis multiplied by the territory's `royalty_rate_pct` and rounded half up to a whole cent, whose `basis_cents` is the recognized basis, whose `source_entity_type` is `QUOTE` and whose `memo` is `quote_accepted`; that amount raises the territory's `royalty_accrued_cents`. That row is appended unconditionally, so a territory whose `royalty_rate_pct` is zero still accrues a `NET_SALES_ROYALTY` row carrying an `amount_cents` of zero. Finally the lead converts — `CONSULT_COMPLETE → CONVERTED`, or `ROUTED → CONVERTED` when the appointment is still `SCHEDULED` — which frees a unit of weekly capacity and can promote one queued lead. The conversion is decided by the lead's own `status` alone and the appointment's status is not re-examined; a lead in any other status — already `CONVERTED`, or `LOST` because an earlier quote on the same appointment expired — is left untouched and frees no capacity, while the acceptance itself still succeeds. The quote's `credited_net_sales_cents` stays at zero until a full-credit remake reverses part or all of this revenue.

### 3.7. Vendor Selection, Lead Times and Production Capacity

Manufacturers are a brand-level asset, not a territory-level one: headquarters onboards them and every franchise draws from the same pool. A vendor record carries a `name` of between 1 and 120 characters that must be unique across all vendors; `base_lead_time_days`, the whole days from the effective placement date to the promised ship date before any adder, between 1 and 120; `motorization_extra_days`, defaulting to zero and capped at 60, added when the order carries any motorized opening; `cutoff_hour_utc`, defaulting to 17 and constrained to the range 0 through 23; a `remake_policy` of `FULL_VENDOR_90D`, `SHARED_50_50` or `NO_REMAKE`; `concurrent_capacity`, defaulting to 2 and at least 1, being the maximum number of the vendor's live orders whose production windows may contain any single calendar date; `supported_lift_systems`, a non-empty duplicate-free subset of `CORDED`, `CORDLESS`, `CONTINUOUS_LOOP` and `MOTORIZED`; and `supported_hub_platforms`, a duplicate-free subset of `ALEXA`, `GOOGLE_HOME`, `HOMEKIT` and `MATTER` that defaults to empty. A vendor's `status` is `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` and defaults to `ACTIVE`; a manufacturer may be created `SUSPENDED` to keep it on file without routing to it, and only `ACTIVE` vendors are eligible for routing. `open_order_count` starts at zero and counts the vendor's orders in status `PLACED`, `SHIPPED` or `REMAKE_REQUESTED`. `base_lead_time_days` is the one numeric term carrying no default and is therefore required; `motorization_extra_days` may not be negative; and `concurrent_capacity` has no upper bound at all. The name collision is not a domain failure: it is reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400), as every uniqueness breach in this API is, and it is tested only **after** all four numeric domains have passed, so a request that both duplicates an existing name and carries a `cutoff_hour_utc` of 24 reports `ERR_VALUE_OUT_OF_RANGE`.

The franchise owner places a manufacturing order for a **subset** of an accepted quote's openings, naming the `quote_id`, the `opening_ids` to manufacture — between 1 and 40 entries with no duplicates — the `expected_quote_version` of §2.8, and optionally a `preferred_vendor_id` to pin the manufacturer. The body-reference stage of §2.7 resolves those references in declared order: the `quote_id` first, then each entry of `opening_ids` that is a JSON string, in array order, reporting `ERR_OPENING_NOT_FOUND` on the first that names no row, and the `preferred_vendor_id` last. Because that resolution precedes the shape stage, an unknown opening id outranks the array's own cardinality and duplicate rules — forty-one entries one of which names no opening reports the not-found rather than `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Territory ownership is checked next, against the territory reached through the named quote, so a franchise owner placing an order on another territory's quote is refused with `ERR_TERRITORY_OWNERSHIP_REQUIRED` before the body's shape is examined at all. The quote must be `ACCEPTED`, or the request fails with `ERR_QUOTE_NOT_ACCEPTED`, and it must not have expired. Every named opening must carry that quote's id, or the request fails with `ERR_OPENING_NOT_ON_QUOTE`, and every named opening must still have `install_outcome` `PENDING`, whose breach carries the same `ERR_OPENING_ALREADY_ORDERED` as the next rule and is tested first of the two, so an opening that is both already resolved and already on a live order reports that identifier either way. No named opening may already sit on an order whose `status` is `PLACED`, `SHIPPED`, `RECEIVED` or `REMAKE_REQUESTED`; that is `ERR_OPENING_ALREADY_ORDERED`. A `CANCELLED` order releases its openings, so they may be re-ordered. The cordless requirement of §3.6 is enforced here for the third and last time, and the owning franchise must be `ACTIVE`.

When `preferred_vendor_id` is omitted the service selects a manufacturer itself, from every vendor row in the system. A candidate is eligible only when all four of the following hold: its `status` is `ACTIVE`; every opening on the order has a `lift_system` that is a member of the candidate's `supported_lift_systems`; if any opening on the order carries a `motorization_option` other than `NONE`, the candidate's `supported_hub_platforms` is non-empty; and placing this order on the candidate leaves every calendar date in the resulting production window at or below the candidate's `concurrent_capacity`. Among the eligible pool the winner is the candidate with, in order of precedence, the earliest promised ship date, then the smallest `open_order_count` — the least loaded partner next — then the earliest `created_at`, the longest-standing partner next. Should two vendors tie on all three, their ids provide a last-resort total ordering that expresses no business preference at all. Promised ship dates compare as `YYYY-MM-DD` strings and counts compare as integers.

If the eligible pool is empty the request fails with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_VENDOR` (HTTP 422) and **nothing** is written — no order row, no increment of the quote's `version`, no ledger row. When `preferred_vendor_id` **is** supplied the selector does not run at all: that one vendor is checked against the first three eligibility conditions, failing with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_VENDOR`, and separately against the capacity condition, failing with `ERR_VENDOR_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. The two paths therefore surface different errors for a capacity breach, which is intentional. A `preferred_vendor_id` that resolves to no vendor at all is `ERR_VENDOR_NOT_FOUND`. The chosen or pinned manufacturer is recorded on the order as its `vendor_id`.

The order's wholesale exposure, `order_cost_cents`, is 42 percent of the summed `list_price_cents` of the named openings, rounded half up to a whole cent **once**, over the summed retail figure. This is deliberately the opposite convention to retail pricing, which rounds per opening. Rounding each opening's wholesale share separately and then summing is forbidden: three openings at 5875 cents each total 17625 cents retail, and 42 percent of that is 7402.5, which rounds once to 7403 cents — whereas rounding 2467.5 three times and summing gives 7404.

Lead time is computed in four steps. First the cutoff roll: the hour component of the placement instant in UTC is compared against the vendor's `cutoff_hour_utc`, and when that hour is **strictly below** the cutoff the `effective_placement_date` is the UTC calendar date of the placement instant, otherwise it is the following calendar date. A vendor with a cutoff hour of 15 placed at 14:59:59.999Z keeps the same date; placed at 15:00:00.000Z it rolls to the next day. Second the adders: the raw day count is the vendor's `base_lead_time_days`, plus its `motorization_extra_days` when any opening on the order carries a `motorization_option` other than `NONE`. Third the expedite and the floor: when the order's `remake_count` is greater than zero, four days are subtracted from the raw count; `lead_time_days` is then the larger of the resulting figure and a floor of five days, and that floor is applied **unconditionally**, on a first placement exactly as on a remake — a vendor whose `base_lead_time_days` is 3 promises five days on its very first order, with no expedite anywhere in sight. A base of 8 with no motor adder expedites to 4 and floors up to 5, while a base of 9 expedites to exactly 5. Fourth, `promised_ship_date` is `effective_placement_date` advanced by `lead_time_days` calendar days. Both dates are whole UTC calendar dates rendered as `YYYY-MM-DD`, and the arithmetic counts every day including weekends and holidays. The computation reads only this one order, its vendor's parameters and its own openings' motor packages; a promised date is never pushed out by the vendor's existing backlog, because backlog is handled by the capacity rule, which refuses rather than delays.

Capacity is a **headcount on a single day**, not a pairwise relation. An order occupies its whole production window inclusively at both ends — from its `effective_placement_date` through its `promised_ship_date` — for as long as its `status` is `PLACED`, `SHIPPED` or `REMAKE_REQUESTED`; orders that have reached `RECEIVED` or `CANCELLED` release their slot. For the candidate placement the service walks every calendar date in the candidate's own window, counts how many occupying orders of that vendor contain that date, and admits the placement only when the largest such count plus one is at most the vendor's `concurrent_capacity`. Otherwise the placement is refused with `ERR_VENDOR_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Because the interval is closed at both ends, two orders that merely touch on one shared date do collide on that date — a candidate whose `promised_ship_date` equals an existing order's `effective_placement_date` shares exactly one loaded date and counts against capacity there. And because the breach is a headcount, three orders can be pairwise compatible and still breach a capacity of 2. This geometry is deliberately different from the designer calendar of §3.3, which is a pairwise test on half-open instant intervals with a conditional buffer.

The last check of all is fiscal. The territory's `open_order_exposure_cents` plus the new order's `order_cost_cents` must be at most its `credit_limit_cents`; a breach is `ERR_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. It is evaluated after vendor eligibility and after capacity, so an order that would breach both capacity and credit reports the capacity error. A successful placement stamps the order `PLACED` with `placed_at` from the logical clock, `remake_count` zero and `credit_scope` `NONE`, stores the three lead-time results, re-derives the vendor's `open_order_count` and the territory's `open_order_exposure_cents` — which sums `order_cost_cents` across the territory's orders in status `PLACED`, `SHIPPED` or `REMAKE_REQUESTED` — and increments the quote's `version`. The order's `shipped_at`, `received_at`, `remake_cost_cents` and three absorption buckets all start null or zero.

### 3.8. Order Settlement: Shipment, Receipt, Remakes and Chargebacks

An order's `status` runs `PLACED`, `SHIPPED`, `RECEIVED`, `REMAKE_REQUESTED` and `CANCELLED`, of which only `CANCELLED` is terminal. The happy path is three transitions long and involves three different actors.

The manufacturer's own dispatcher reports the goods shipped, driving `PLACED → SHIPPED` and stamping `shipped_at` from the logical clock. This is the `VendorRep` role's only write in the entire API, and the acting representative must be the order's own manufacturing vendor: a representative of a different manufacturer is refused with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403), and because that comparison is made only after the order has been found, an unknown order id still reports `ERR_VENDOR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`. The restriction binds the write alone — on the read side a `VendorRep` may read and list every order in the system whoever manufactures it. An order that is not `PLACED` yields `ERR_ORDER_NOT_SHIPPABLE`. A shipped order keeps occupying its production window, so shipping releases no capacity, and the transition re-derives no counter at all: neither the vendor row nor the territory row is touched or version-bumped by it.

The franchise owner records the goods arriving, driving `SHIPPED → RECEIVED` and stamping `received_at`. An order that is not `SHIPPED` yields `ERR_ORDER_NOT_SHIPPED`. Receipt is the point at which the vendor's production slot is released and the franchise's exposure falls: the vendor's `open_order_count` and the territory's `open_order_exposure_cents` are both re-derived downward, the latter by that order's `order_cost_cents`. Receipt also starts the clock on the vendor's remake window.

Being `RECEIVED` is the precondition for three quite different things — requesting a remake on the order, scheduling the install that fits it, and signing that install off — and all three report `ERR_ORDER_NOT_RECEIVED` when it does not hold. The re-check at sign-off is not redundant: an order can leave `RECEIVED` after its install job has been scheduled, because a remake re-places it, so completion re-examines the referenced order rather than trusting the check made at scheduling time.

A remake is requested by the franchise owner and carries a single field, `credit_scope`, which is either `REPLACEMENT` or `FULL_CREDIT`; the field is required, and being a shape rule it is tested before any state precondition, so a missing or undeclared scope on a `PLACED` order reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than `ERR_ORDER_NOT_RECEIVED`. Four checks then guard it, evaluated in the order stated here, and all four sit behind the `RECEIVED` precondition, so an order in any other status reports `ERR_ORDER_NOT_RECEIVED` however its vendor's policy reads. The vendor's `remake_policy` must not be `NO_REMAKE`, or the request fails with `ERR_REMAKE_NOT_OFFERED` — such a manufacturer refuses remakes altogether. The order's `remake_count` must be strictly less than two, the cap on remake cycles per order, or the request fails with `ERR_REMAKE_LIMIT_REACHED`. When the vendor's policy is `FULL_VENDOR_90D` the logical clock must be at most ninety days after the order's `received_at`, or the request fails with `ERR_REMAKE_WINDOW_CLOSED`; a `SHARED_50_50` policy has no window limit at all. And a `FULL_CREDIT` remake additionally requires every opening in the order's `opening_ids` to still have `install_outcome` `PENDING`, because revenue already trued up by a completed install cannot be reversed wholesale; a breach is `ERR_OPENINGS_ALREADY_RESOLVED`. A `REPLACEMENT` remake carries no such requirement.

Whichever scope is chosen, the cost of the remake is apportioned by a three-bucket waterfall. The amount being apportioned, `remake_cost_cents`, is the order's `order_cost_cents` — the wholesale value being redone. Targets are then chosen by the **origin** of the remake, and the two origins behave very differently.

When the origin is a franchise owner's direct request, the split follows the vendor's `remake_policy`. A `FULL_VENDOR_90D` vendor's target is the whole `remake_cost_cents` and the territory's target is zero. A `SHARED_50_50` vendor's target is half of `remake_cost_cents` rounded **down** to a whole cent, and the territory's target is whatever remains — so on an odd cost of 7403 cents the vendor takes 3701 and the territory 3702, the extra cent landing on the territory rather than on the manufacturer.

When the origin is the install-exception cascade of §3.9, the vendor's `remake_policy` is **ignored entirely** and the targets are driven by the liable party recorded on the driving install job. A liable party of `VENDOR` makes the vendor's target the whole cost and the territory's target zero. A liable party of `MEASURER` makes the territory's target the whole cost and the vendor's target zero. A liable party of `CUSTOMER` leaves both targets at zero, so the customer bucket absorbs everything. The consequence is that a measurer-liable exception moves the vendor's entire share onto the territory — capped, as below — and onto the customer, even when the manufacturer is a `FULL_VENDOR_90D` partner.

The buckets are then filled strictly in order. `vendor_absorbed_cents` is the smaller of the vendor's target and `remake_cost_cents`. `territory_absorbed_cents` is the smallest of the territory's target, a hard territory cap of 15000 cents, and whatever remains of `remake_cost_cents` after the vendor's bucket. `customer_absorbed_cents` is the pure remainder: `remake_cost_cents` less the vendor's bucket less the territory's bucket. The three buckets therefore always sum exactly to `remake_cost_cents`, an invariant that no rounding ever disturbs because every step is integer arithmetic. At the cap boundary, a measurer-liable exception on a 15000-cent order charges the territory the whole 15000 and the customer nothing, while a 15001-cent order charges the territory 15000 and the customer 1. Each remake is allocated on its own — prior remakes on the same order do not change the split — and the four stored fields hold the **most recent** allocation only. Whenever `territory_absorbed_cents` is greater than zero, a ledger row of type `REMAKE_CHARGEBACK` is appended for that amount, with `basis_cents` equal to `remake_cost_cents`, `source_entity_type` `VENDOR_ORDER` and `memo` `remake_replacement`, and the amount is added to the territory's `remake_chargeback_cents`.

A `REPLACEMENT` remake sets `credit_scope` to `REPLACEMENT`, increments `remake_count`, runs the waterfall, and then — **inside the very same request** — re-places the order. The observable outcome is therefore `RECEIVED → REMAKE_REQUESTED → PLACED` with `remake_count` one higher: `placed_at` is re-stamped from the logical clock, `shipped_at` and `received_at` are cleared to null, the lead-time calculation is re-run and now applies the four-day expedite before the five-day floor, storing a fresh `effective_placement_date`, `lead_time_days` and `promised_ship_date`, and the vendor's `open_order_count` and the territory's `open_order_exposure_cents` rise again by the order's cost. Because the cutoff roll is re-evaluated against the **new** placement instant, a remake can land on a later effective placement date and still promise an earlier ship date than the original. The re-placement is a re-stamping, not a fresh placement. It keeps the order's existing `vendor_id` without re-testing any of the four eligibility conditions of §3.7 — a manufacturer that has since gone `SUSPENDED`, or that no longer supports an opening's `lift_system`, still makes the remake — and it tests neither that vendor's `concurrent_capacity` nor the territory's `credit_limit_cents`, so a re-placement can never fail with `ERR_VENDOR_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` or `ERR_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` and may legitimately carry a vendor's load or a franchise's exposure past either ceiling. The whole `RECEIVED → REMAKE_REQUESTED → PLACED` sequence is one single mutation of the order row, so its `version` rises by exactly one and `REMAKE_REQUESTED` is never observable in any response body.

A `FULL_CREDIT` remake sets `credit_scope` to `FULL_CREDIT`, runs the same waterfall, and drives `RECEIVED → CANCELLED`, which is terminal. Every opening in the order's `opening_ids` has `credited` set to true, and credited openings are excluded from every royalty basis thereafter; their `install_outcome` is deliberately left `PENDING`, which is exactly what lets a cancelled order's openings be placed again. The credit branch of royalty recognition then unwinds the revenue those openings carried. The credited subtotal is the summed `list_price_cents` of the openings on the cancelled order; the credited discount is that subtotal multiplied by the quote's stored `discount_pct` and rounded half up to a whole cent; and the credited basis is the credited subtotal less the credited discount. The quote's `credited_net_sales_cents` rises by the credited basis, its `recognized_net_sales_cents` falls by the same amount, and the territory's `net_sales_cents` falls by the same amount. The royalty posted is the royalty on the **new** recognized figure minus the royalty on the **old** recognized figure, each computed independently as that figure multiplied by the territory's `royalty_rate_pct` and rounded half up to a whole cent — a negative number. It is appended as a ledger row of type `ROYALTY_REVERSAL` carrying that negative `amount_cents`, `basis_cents` equal to the credited basis, `source_entity_type` `VENDOR_ORDER` and `memo` `remake_full_credit`, and it lowers the territory's `royalty_accrued_cents`. That reversal row is appended unconditionally, even when the two independently rounded royalty figures come out equal and the amount is therefore exactly zero — the zero-delta suppression of §3.9's true-up does not apply here. When the waterfall also charged the territory, the `REMAKE_CHARGEBACK` row is written first and this `ROYALTY_REVERSAL` row second; both carry the same `occurred_at`, so that creation order is what the ledger ordering of §2.3 falls back to. The cancellation re-derives the territory but not the vendor, because a `RECEIVED` order already sat outside the live set that `open_order_count` and `open_order_exposure_cents` count. A full credit is the exact compensating action of an acceptance: crediting a quote's whole opening set must restore the territory's `royalty_accrued_cents` to precisely its pre-acceptance value.

### 3.9. Installation, Commissioning, Exceptions, the True-Up and Designer Liability

Installation is scheduled by either the franchise owner or a designer against a received order. The booking names the `vendor_order_id`, the `designer_id` who will install, the window as `scheduled_start_at` and `scheduled_end_at`, and the `service_postal_code` of the dwelling. The referenced order must be `RECEIVED`, the named designer must be `ACTIVE`, the designer's territory must be the same territory as the order's quote — a mismatch is `ERR_CROSS_TERRITORY_FORBIDDEN` — the owning franchise must be `ACTIVE`, and the requested window is tested against the designer's calendar exactly as a consultation is, against that designer's `SCHEDULED` consultations **and** `SCHEDULED` install jobs together, with the same conditional forty-five-minute travel buffer. The two body references resolve in declared order, `vendor_order_id` before `designer_id`, and territory ownership is then checked twice for a franchise owner — first against the order's territory, then against the named designer's. Nothing limits an order to a single install job: several may be scheduled against one `vendor_order_id`, and the only conflict test applied is the designer-calendar one.

An install job carries **two independent state dimensions on the same row**, and they advance separately. The first, `install_status`, is one of `SCHEDULED`, `COMPLETED`, `EXCEPTION_RAISED` and `CANCELLED`, with the last three terminal. The second, `commissioning_status`, is one of `NOT_REQUIRED`, `PENDING`, `PAIRED`, `VERIFIED` and `FAILED`, of which `NOT_REQUIRED` and `VERIFIED` are the resting states. A new job is stamped `install_status` `SCHEDULED`, and its `motorized_channel_count` is **frozen** at creation as the number of openings in the order's `opening_ids` whose `motorization_option` is anything other than `NONE`. The second dimension's nominal initial state is `PENDING`, and the frozen count decides at once whether the job stays there: when the count is zero the system itself drives `PENDING → NOT_REQUIRED` at creation, so the created job reads `commissioning_status` `NOT_REQUIRED` and already satisfies the completion guard while `install_status` is still `SCHEDULED`; when the count is one or more the job is created reading `PENDING`. The remaining commissioning and exception fields start empty — `paired_channel_count` zero, `hub_platform` `NONE`, `commissioning_attempt_count` zero, `exception_reason` `NONE`, `exception_opening_id` null, `liable_party` `NONE`, `remeasure_charge_cents` zero, and `exception_raised_at` and `completed_at` null.

Commissioning, exception-raising and sign-off all require `install_status` to be `SCHEDULED`, reported as `ERR_INSTALL_JOB_NOT_SCHEDULED`. That check is made on the **first** dimension before anything on the second is examined, so a job that has already completed reports the job state rather than a commissioning state.

Pairing motorized shades to the homeowner's hub is the assigned designer's action and reports two fields: `paired_channel_count`, how many channels actually answered, and `hub_platform`, the smart-home platform they answered on. Four gates apply beyond the job-state check. `commissioning_status` must be `PENDING` or `FAILED`; `NOT_REQUIRED` means the order carries no motorized channel at all, and `PAIRED` or `VERIFIED` mean pairing has already succeeded, so all three fail with `ERR_COMMISSIONING_NOT_APPLICABLE`. The acting designer's `certifications` must include `MOTORIZATION`, or the request is refused with `ERR_MOTORIZATION_CERT_REQUIRED` (HTTP 403); this pairing call is the **only** operation in the entire API that examines a designer's certifications at all, so configuring a motorized opening, quoting one, ordering one and signing its installation off are all open to an uncertified designer. The reported `hub_platform` must be a member of the manufacturing vendor's `supported_hub_platforms`, and since `NONE` is never a member of that set, reporting `NONE` always fails with `ERR_HUB_PLATFORM_UNSUPPORTED` (HTTP 422). And `commissioning_attempt_count` must be strictly less than three, the cap on pairing attempts, or the call fails with `ERR_COMMISSIONING_ATTEMPT_LIMIT`; the counter increments on **every** accepted pair call, including the ones that later fail verification.

The reported channel count must lie between zero and the job's frozen `motorized_channel_count`, both endpoints inclusive. A negative count is `ERR_VALUE_OUT_OF_RANGE`; a count above the frozen figure is `ERR_CHANNEL_COUNT_EXCEEDS_ORDER` (HTTP 422), because the order cannot contain more channels than it carries. That channel-count test is made **before** the attempt-cap test of the previous paragraph, so a call that breaches both reports the channel count. Reporting **fewer** channels than the order carries is entirely legal and is how a partial pairing gets recorded. An accepted pair call stores `hub_platform` and `paired_channel_count`, increments `commissioning_attempt_count`, and drives `PENDING → PAIRED` or `FAILED → PAIRED`. `install_status` is untouched: the two dimensions really do advance independently.

Verification is the assigned designer's second commissioning action and requires `commissioning_status` to be `PAIRED`, or it fails with `ERR_COMMISSIONING_NOT_PAIRED`. When `paired_channel_count` equals `motorized_channel_count` the job moves `PAIRED → VERIFIED`, which satisfies the completion guard on the first dimension without changing `install_status`. When `paired_channel_count` is less than `motorized_channel_count` the job moves `PAIRED → FAILED` and the call still returns HTTP 200 — a failed verification is a **recorded field observation**, not a request error. From `FAILED` the designer may pair again, giving the retry cycle `PAIRED → FAILED → PAIRED`, bounded by the three-attempt cap.

Signing off an installation drives `install_status` `SCHEDULED → COMPLETED` and is the join point of the two dimensions. It requires `install_status` to be `SCHEDULED`, the acting designer to be the assigned one, the referenced order to be `RECEIVED`, and `commissioning_status` to be either `NOT_REQUIRED` or `VERIFIED` — `PENDING`, `PAIRED` and `FAILED` all block completion with `ERR_COMMISSIONING_NOT_VERIFIED`. Sign-off stamps `completed_at` from the logical clock and sets `install_outcome` to `INSTALLED` on every opening in the order's `opening_ids` whose `install_outcome` is still `PENDING`.

Sign-off is also the moment provisional royalty is trued up to what was actually installed. The final basis is built only from openings that really landed: the installed subtotal is the summed `list_price_cents` of every opening carrying this quote's id whose `install_outcome` is `INSTALLED` **and** whose `credited` flag is false. The final discount is that installed subtotal multiplied by the quote's stored `discount_pct`, rounded half up to a whole cent, and the final basis is the installed subtotal less the final discount. The royalty delta is the royalty on the final basis minus the royalty on the quote's current `recognized_net_sales_cents`, each figure computed independently as its basis multiplied by the territory's `royalty_rate_pct` and rounded half up. The territory's `net_sales_cents` is adjusted by the difference between the final basis and the previously recognized figure, and `recognized_net_sales_cents` is then re-stated to the final basis — a mutation of the quote row that increments its `version`, while `credited_net_sales_cents` is left exactly as §3.8's credit branch left it. When the delta is positive a ledger row of type `NET_SALES_ROYALTY` is appended for it; when negative, a row of type `ROYALTY_REVERSAL` carrying the negative amount; and when the delta is exactly zero **no ledger row is written at all**. Either row carries `source_entity_type` `INSTALL_JOB` and `memo` `install_trueup`. Openings left `NOT_INSTALLED` by an exception are what make the provisional and final figures diverge in the first place. Sign-off finally recomputes the designer's `clean_install_count` and `remeasure_liability_score`.

When the installer simply cannot fit an opening, they raise an exception naming the `opening_id` and an `exception_reason` of `OUT_OF_SQUARE`, `OBSTRUCTION` or `DAMAGED_GOODS`. The named opening must appear in the job's order's `opening_ids`, or the request fails with `ERR_OPENING_NOT_ON_QUOTE`, and it must still have `install_outcome` `PENDING`, whose breach carries that very same `ERR_OPENING_NOT_ON_QUOTE`. The two tests sit on opposite sides of the job-state check: membership is the stage-10 test of §2.7 and precedes `ERR_INSTALL_JOB_NOT_SCHEDULED`, while the already-resolved test follows it — so on a job that has already completed, an exception naming a foreign opening reports the opening and one naming an already-resolved opening reports the job. Raising an exception carries no precondition on the order's own `status` at all: unlike a remake, a scheduling or a sign-off, it is never guarded by `ERR_ORDER_NOT_RECEIVED`. The reason, the opening id and the logical instant are stored as `exception_reason`, `exception_opening_id` and `exception_raised_at`.

Liability is then apportioned from the reason plus three stored facts about that one opening — its `mount_type`, its `out_of_square_in` and its `depth_in` — and nothing else. Damaged goods are always the manufacturer's fault, so `DAMAGED_GOODS` yields a `liable_party` of `VENDOR`. An out-of-square failure yields `MEASURER` when the opening is an `INSIDE` mount **and** its `out_of_square_in` is strictly greater than 0.375 inches, because an inside mount on a visibly racked opening was the measurer's call to refuse; otherwise — an outside mount, or a spread within tolerance — it yields `CUSTOMER`, an unforeseeable wall condition. An obstruction failure yields `MEASURER` when the opening is an `INSIDE` mount **and** its `depth_in` is at least 2.500 inches, because a pocket the measurer certified as deep enough was a pocket they inspected; otherwise it yields `CUSTOMER`. The boundaries are exact: an `out_of_square_in` of exactly 0.375 on an inside mount is `CUSTOMER` while 0.376 is `MEASURER`, and a `depth_in` of exactly 2.500 on an inside mount is `MEASURER` while 2.499 is `CUSTOMER`. Neither the other openings on the job nor the designer's history participate — the designer's history is a downstream consequence, not an input.

When and only when `liable_party` is `MEASURER`, `remeasure_charge_cents` is 35 percent of the named opening's `list_price_cents`, rounded half up to a whole cent and **then** capped at 12000 cents — rounding before the cap, never after. A 34285-cent opening charges 11999.75, which rounds to exactly the 12000-cent cap; a 34290-cent opening charges 12001.5, which rounds to 12002 and is then clipped back to 12000. For any other liable party `remeasure_charge_cents` is zero. A measurer-liable exception appends a ledger row of type `REMEASURE_CHARGEBACK` for `remeasure_charge_cents`, with `basis_cents` equal to the named opening's `list_price_cents`, `source_entity_type` `INSTALL_JOB` and `memo` `remeasure_chargeback`, and adds that amount to the territory's `remake_chargeback_cents` — which is the running total of the territory's `REMAKE_CHARGEBACK` and `REMEASURE_CHARGEBACK` rows together.

Raising an exception moves the job `SCHEDULED → EXCEPTION_RAISED`, terminal for that job, and sets off a four-hop cascade that is the most far-reaching sequence in the domain. **Hop one**: the named opening moves `QUOTED → REMEASURE_REQUIRED` and its `install_outcome` becomes `NOT_INSTALLED`. **Hop two**: that `NOT_INSTALLED` outcome drives the order carrying the opening through `RECEIVED → REMAKE_REQUESTED → PLACED`, with the waterfall of §3.8 run on the install-exception origin and the lead time re-computed with the four-day expedite. This hop fires when and only when that order is still `RECEIVED` at the moment the exception lands; when it is not — a `REPLACEMENT` remake has already re-placed it, or a `FULL_CREDIT` has cancelled it — hop two is skipped silently, no waterfall runs, no `REMAKE_CHARGEBACK` row is written, and the exception itself still succeeds with HTTP 200. The re-placement this hop performs is the same re-stamping §3.8 describes and is likewise subject to none of the remake guards: the two-remake cap, the vendor's `remake_policy`, the ninety-day window, production capacity and the credit limit are all bypassed, so a cascade can legitimately carry an order's `remake_count` past two. **Hop three**: when the liable party was the measurer, the chargeback row raises that designer's `remeasure_exception_count`, forcing a recompute of `remeasure_liability_score`. The recompute itself is run on **every** accepted exception whatever the liable party — a `CUSTOMER`- or `VENDOR`-liable exception changes no counter of its own, but the recompute can still move the score, and so bump the designer row, by re-evaluating a rolling window that has meanwhile aged a clean install out of it. **Hop four**: a recomputed score strictly below the suspension threshold drives the designer `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`, which cancels that designer's other future consultations and install jobs. Two things the cascade deliberately does not reach. The exception's own consultation is never re-derived, so its `measured_opening_count` goes stale and keeps counting the opening the exception has just moved out of `QUOTED`, until some later write against that consultation recomputes it. And when one exception produces both a `REMEASURE_CHARGEBACK` row and, through hop two, a `REMAKE_CHARGEBACK` row, the two carry the same `occurred_at`, with the remeasure row written first and the remake row second. The corrective measurement is always a **new** opening whose `replaces_opening_id` points at the superseded one.

The designer's liability score is recomputed on exactly three occasions: a sample check-out reaching `RETURNED` or `LOST`, an install job reaching `EXCEPTION_RAISED`, and an install job reaching `COMPLETED`. Each recompute re-derives all three counters from scratch rather than incrementing them, so a row ageing out of a rolling window silently raises the score at the next recompute. Which rows count depends on the owning territory's `liability_window`. Under `ROLLING_180D` a row counts only when its own stamp is at or after the logical clock minus 180 days; under `LIFETIME` every row counts however old it is. `remeasure_exception_count` is the number of that designer's install jobs whose `install_status` is `EXCEPTION_RAISED`, whose `liable_party` is `MEASURER`, and whose `exception_raised_at` falls in the window. `late_sample_return_count` is the number of sample check-outs whose appointment belongs to that designer, whose `days_late` is greater than zero, and whose `returned_at` falls in the window. `clean_install_count` is the number of that designer's install jobs whose `install_status` is `COMPLETED`, whose `exception_reason` is `NONE`, and whose `completed_at` falls in the window.

The raw score starts at a base of 100, subtracts 22 points for each counted measurer-liable exception, subtracts 9 points for each counted late sample return, and adds 3 points for each counted clean installation. `remeasure_liability_score` is that raw figure clamped at both ends — never above the base of 100 and never below zero. All terms are integers, so the score is exact and no rounding is involved. A designer is auto-suspended, `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`, when and only when the recomputed score is **strictly** below 80; a score of exactly 80 does not suspend. One measurer-liable exception alone therefore gives 100 minus 22, or 78, and suspends; the same exception with one prior clean install gives 81 and does not. The window choice can be decisive on its own: three late sample returns of which two are older than 180 days score 73 under `LIFETIME` and suspend the designer, where `ROLLING_180D` scores 91 and does not.

Suspension is a cascade, not just a flag. Every consultation of that designer that is still `SCHEDULED` and whose `scheduled_start_at` is at or after the logical clock moves `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED`, and every install job of that designer that is still `SCHEDULED` and whose `scheduled_start_at` is at or after the logical clock moves `install_status` `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED`. A cancelled install job releases the designer's calendar occupation and deliberately leaves its referenced order in `RECEIVED`, so a replacement job can be scheduled with a different installer. Retiring a designer to `INACTIVE` drives exactly the same two cancellation cascades.
