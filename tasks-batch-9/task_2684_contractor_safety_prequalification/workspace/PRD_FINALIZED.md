# Contractor Safety Prequalification API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Owner-operators in heavy industry no longer accept a contractor's word about its safety record. Before a crew is allowed through a gate, the hiring client wants three things proven independently: that the contractor's injury and citation statistics are real, that its written safety programs and worker credentials match the hazards actually present on the site, and that its insurance would still respond after the losses it has already suffered this year. Today that proof is assembled by email, spreadsheet and PDF, and every client reinvents its own thresholds. The result is a market where a contractor can be "approved" by one client while another client, using tighter yardsticks against the same underlying data, would reject it outright.

This document specifies the API for a **contractor prequalification network** — a shared service that sits between many contractors and many hiring clients. Each client publishes its own numeric thresholds, its own weighting of the four graded metrics, its own grade bands, its own cure window generosity and its own tolerance for subcontracting depth. Each contractor registers once, declares its trades and hazard exposure, and then submits one prequalification per client per cycle year. Statistics and insurance certificates are verified by an independent analyst who is deliberately barred from doing both halves of the same contractor's file. The client grades what the analyst has verified, and the grade — not the contractor's self-assessment — is what opens or closes a site gate for an individual badged worker.

The service is built around a small number of hard commitments that pervade every endpoint. Time is a **logical clock** the operator controls, never the wall clock, so expiry, cure deadlines and credential currency are all reproducible. Posture fields such as a site's access gate, a certificate's lifecycle position and a contractor's open-deficiency count are maintained by the write path but are recomputed fresh by every gate that depends on them, because a certificate that silently lapsed against the clock must not be allowed to buy a badge. Counters that go up must come back down by exactly the same amount when the compensating action runs. And when an incident report lands, the cascade it triggers reaches all the way from the contractor's standing down to the individual worker's badge, inside the request that filed it.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

Every rule in this section applies to every endpoint unless a later section narrows it. Where this section and Section 3 describe the same behaviour, they are describing it from two angles — the two are reinforcing, never contradictory.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Authentication is header-based. Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that principal is acting in. If either header is absent, present but empty, or if `X-User-Role` is not exactly one of the four declared role names, the request is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). This check runs before anything else, including body parsing and path resolution.

Four roles exist, spelled exactly as follows: `NetworkAdmin`, `ContractorAdmin`, `ClientSafetyManager`, and `VerificationAnalyst`.

A `NetworkAdmin` operates the network itself. It owns the tenant registry — it is the only role that may register a `Client` or a `Contractor`, amend a contractor's registration, or move the logical clock. It deliberately cannot grade, verify or grant access, so that no single principal can both introduce a contractor and bless it. When `X-User-Role` is `NetworkAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server does not compare it to any entity's `id`, and no ownership check is performed against it.

A `ContractorAdmin` acts for exactly one contractor: it opens prequalification cycles, files statistics, tenders written programs, uploads insurance certificates, maintains the workforce roster, requests variances, builds subcontractor edges and requests site badges. When `X-User-Role` is `ContractorAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Contractor`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Contractor` compare the resource's contractor reference — reached directly or through its parent chain — to `X-User-ID`, and a mismatch is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). A `ContractorAdmin` may create a subcontractor edge only when its own contractor is the prime contractor of the referenced prequalification.

A `ClientSafetyManager` acts for exactly one client: it creates sites, reviews written programs, grades submissions, issues improvement plans, decides variance requests, files incident reports, closes incident reviews, revokes badges, records audit findings and executes the two periodic batch runs. When `X-User-Role` is `ClientSafetyManager`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Client`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Client` compare the resource's client reference — reached directly or through its parent chain — to `X-User-ID`, and a mismatch is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. A `ClientSafetyManager` can never verify a statistic or a certificate; verification is reserved for the independent analyst.

A `VerificationAnalyst` is the independent third-party verifier and the only role that can move a safety statistic or an insurance certificate out of its pending posture. When `X-User-Role` is `VerificationAnalyst`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque analyst identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it (as `verified_by_user_id` on the `SafetyStatistic` or `InsuranceCertificate` the analyst decides) but does not compare it to any entity's `id`. That recorded identity is what the segregation-of-duties rule in Section 2.11 later consults.

Once authentication succeeds, the acting role must appear in the authorized-role list of the endpoint being called, as tabulated in Section 4. If it does not, the request is refused with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403). Read endpoints are shared by all four roles, but the rows a caller sees are narrowed by identity: a `ContractorAdmin` sees only rows reachable from its own contractor, a `ClientSafetyManager` sees only rows reachable from its own client, and `NetworkAdmin` and `VerificationAnalyst` read without narrowing.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 when omitted and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is a field-value violation and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_FIELD_VALUE` (HTTP 422).

Unless a specific collection declares otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Four collections declare a domain-specific order because the caller needs the most decision-relevant row first or needs to walk a hierarchy top-down: safety statistics for one prequalification are ordered by `reporting_year` descending and then `created_at` descending, so the governing year appears first; incident reports are ordered by `occurred_at` descending and then `created_at` descending; subcontractor edges are ordered by `depth` ascending and then `created_at` ascending, so a cascade can be walked from the prime downwards; and the prequalification and site-access-grant collections are ordered by `created_at` ascending with `id` ascending as a stable tie-break.

For list responses the `meta` object carries the four pagination fields `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`. `total_count` is the number of rows matching the request's filters before the page window is applied, and `has_more` is true exactly when rows remain beyond the current window. Single-resource responses omit all four.

### 2.4. Record Identity, Timestamps and Versioning

Every persisted row carries an `id` that is a server-minted UUID. Identifiers carry no type prefix and are never derived from client input; a client may not propose an `id`. Every row also carries a `created_at` timestamp, an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits, recorded from the logical clock at the moment the row was created.

Most entities additionally carry an `updated_at` timestamp in the same ISO 8601 UTC format with three fractional digits, and a `version` integer. `version` starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row; `updated_at` is refreshed to the current logical clock on every mutation. This applies to `Client`, `Site`, `Contractor`, `Prequalification`, `SafetyStatistic`, `WrittenProgram`, `ImprovementPlan`, `InsuranceCertificate`, `SiteAccessGrant`, `IncidentReport`, `Variance` and `SubcontractorLink`.

Two entities are explicitly immutable and therefore carry neither field. A `Worker` row and a `WorkerQualification` row each expose only `id` and `created_at`: `updated_at` and `version` are absent from both, and no endpoint mutates either row. A worker's credentials are renewed by appending a new qualification row, never by editing an existing one.

When one request mutates several rows — and cascades in this domain routinely do — every touched row independently advances its own `version` and refreshes its own `updated_at`. A cascade that revokes six badges advances six grant versions plus the site's version, not one shared counter.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

The service reads a **logical clock**, never the host wall clock. At startup the logical clock is `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. It is moved only by the operator-only clock endpoint, which accepts a single field `current_utc` and returns both `current_utc` and the `previous_utc` it replaced. `current_utc` must be a valid ISO 8601 UTC instant, otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_FIELD_VALUE`; the clock may legitimately be moved backwards.

Setting the clock sweeps nothing. No row changes state merely because time moved. Clock-driven consequences surface in exactly two ways: lazily, when a read endpoint projects a derived value against the current clock, or eagerly, when a client's requalification run is executed and materializes expiry for that client's population. This separation is deliberate — it lets a client's population be advanced through several months of simulated time and then reconciled in one auditable batch.

Every temporal comparison in this specification — credential currency, coverage intervals, cure deadlines, incident lookback windows, access cooldowns, reporting-year windows — resolves against the logical clock at the instant the request is processed. Instants are compared at second granularity in UTC.

### 2.6. Path Resolution and Reference Checking

Path parameters are resolved strictly left to right, and the first segment that fails to resolve determines the error. Each segment has its own not-found code: an unresolvable client segment yields `ERR_CLIENT_NOT_FOUND`, a contractor segment `ERR_CONTRACTOR_NOT_FOUND`, a prequalification segment `ERR_PREQUALIFICATION_NOT_FOUND`, a site segment `ERR_SITE_NOT_FOUND`, a worker segment `ERR_WORKER_NOT_FOUND`, an insurance-certificate segment `ERR_INSURANCE_CERTIFICATE_NOT_FOUND`, and an improvement-plan segment `ERR_IMPROVEMENT_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`. Leaf rows without a dedicated code — a safety statistic, a written program, a worker qualification, a site access grant, an incident report, a variance or a subcontractor link — yield `ERR_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`. All eight codes carry HTTP 404. Left-to-right resolution matters when two segments are both wrong: a request naming a nonexistent contractor and a nonexistent certificate under it reports the contractor, not the certificate.

References carried in a request body are resolved after every path segment has resolved, and they are resolved in the order the endpoint declares its input fields. Four write operations carry such references — prequalification registration, site-access-grant creation, incident-report filing and subcontractor-edge creation — and each reports the appropriate dedicated not-found code for the first reference that fails, falling back to `ERR_RECORD_NOT_FOUND` where no dedicated code exists. A contractor reference that does not resolve reports `ERR_CONTRACTOR_NOT_FOUND` whether it arrived as a plain contractor reference or as the `parent_contractor_id` or `child_contractor_id` of a subcontractor edge.

### 2.7. Numeric Precision and Rounding Conventions

All rounding in this service is **half-up**: a digit sequence exactly on the midpoint rounds away from zero. Four precision regimes exist and they are not interchangeable.

Normalized incident rates — a statistic's `trir` and `dart_rate` — are rounded half-up to four decimal places. Effective client thresholds, after tightening and after any variance overlay, are likewise rounded half-up to four decimal places, and the conditional band ceiling derived from them is rounded half-up to four decimal places before any comparison is made against it.

Scores are two-decimal quantities. Each per-metric sub-score is rounded half-up to two decimal places, each weighted product is rounded half-up to two decimal places, and the composite total is rounded half-up to two decimal places. Grade bands are compared against these two-decimal values with no further rounding.

Citation points are exact integers with no rounding step whatsoever. Insurance figures are whole US dollars: certificate limits, the depleted available aggregate, and the required floors a certificate is judged against are all integers, and where a required floor is produced by multiplying a base requirement by a tier multiplier the product is rounded half-up to zero decimal places before the comparison.

Day counts are whole days obtained by truncating toward zero — the number of complete 86,400-second days between two instants, discarding any partial day.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency on Parent Records

Two write operations mutate a parent record's standing so profoundly that a writer holding a stale view of that parent must be stopped rather than merged. Both use an explicit expected-version field, and neither field is optional.

A request to `POST /prequalifications` must carry `expected_contractor_version`, holding the client's last observed `version` of the target `Contractor`. If that value does not equal the contractor's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This matters because registering a new cycle year supersedes the contractor's prior active standing with the same client and because filing an incident report also advances the contractor's version: a writer that read the contractor before either event would otherwise silently retire a prequalification it never observed.

A request to `POST /prequalifications/{id}/improvement-plans` must carry `expected_prequalification_version`, holding the manager's last observed `version` of the target `Prequalification`. If that value does not equal the prequalification's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A plan create legitimately supersedes any live plan already on the row and snapshots the row's deficiency set, so a plan written against a pre-grading snapshot would both discard a plan the writer never saw and carry the wrong deficiency list.

No other endpoint accepts an expected-version field. Supplying one where it is not declared is an undeclared field and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

### 2.9. Validation Precedence

Checks are evaluated strictly in the order below and the **first** failure wins; no endpoint reports two failures, and no later check may mask an earlier one. The thirty checks apply to the scopes named with each.

1. Header authentication, on every endpoint — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. Role authorization against the endpoint's authorized-role list, on every endpoint — `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403).
3. Request-shape validation on every creating or amending write: every required input field present, every field of the declared JSON type, and no undeclared field present — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400).
4. Field-value validation on every creating or amending write and on every list endpoint: enum membership, numeric range, ISO 8601 instant format, array-subset and no-duplicate rules, a client's four metric weights summing to exactly 1.00, `expiration_date` strictly after `effective_date` on a certificate, `aggregate_limit` at least `each_occurrence_limit`, `limit` between 1 and 100, and `offset` at least 0 — `ERR_INVALID_FIELD_VALUE` (422).
5. Path-parameter resolution, left to right, on every endpoint that has path parameters — the dedicated 404 code of the leftmost failing segment, per Section 2.6.
6. Body-reference resolution, in declared field order, on prequalification registration, site-access-grant creation, incident-report filing and subcontractor-edge creation — the dedicated 404 code, per Section 2.6.
7. Ownership, on every endpoint whose authorized roles include `ContractorAdmin` or `ClientSafetyManager` — `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403).
8. Segregation of verification duties, on insurance-certificate verification only — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (409).
9. Expected-version match, on prequalification registration and improvement-plan creation — `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412).
10. Transition legality against the row's current state, on every state-transition endpoint, including terminal-state lockdown and a repeated trigger on an already-advanced row — `ERR_TRANSITION_NOT_ALLOWED` (409).
11. Governing contractor is `ACTIVE`, on prequalification registration, prequalification submission, site-access-grant creation and subcontractor-edge creation — `ERR_CONTRACTOR_NOT_ACTIVE` (409).
12. Referenced prequalification is `APPROVED` or `CONDITIONAL`, on site-access-grant creation and subcontractor-edge creation — `ERR_PREQUAL_NOT_ACTIVE` (409).
13. Contractor's incident-review flag is clear, on prequalification grading and prequalification submission — `ERR_INCIDENT_REVIEW_ACTIVE` (409).
14. A governing safety statistic exists, on prequalification submission — `ERR_STATISTICS_UNVERIFIED` (409).
15. Every required written program is present, on prequalification submission — `ERR_REQUIRED_PROGRAM_MISSING` (422).
16. Target site's freshly recomputed access gate is not locked, on site-access-grant creation — `ERR_SITE_GATE_LOCKED` (409).
17. Site hazard classes covered by the contractor's hazard profile, on site-access-grant creation — `ERR_HAZARD_PROFILE_MISMATCH` (422).
18. Worker holds every currently required credential, on site-access-grant creation — `ERR_WORKER_QUALIFICATION_EXPIRED` (422).
19. Internal consistency of the reported figures, on safety-statistic creation — `ERR_STATISTICS_INCONSISTENT` (422).
20. Reporting year inside its window, on safety-statistic creation — `ERR_REPORTING_YEAR_OUT_OF_WINDOW` (422).
21. No live statistic already claims that reporting year, on safety-statistic creation — `ERR_DUPLICATE_REPORTING_YEAR` (409).
22. No conflicting prequalification exists, on prequalification registration — `ERR_DUPLICATE_PREQUALIFICATION` (409).
23. Subcontractor graph stays acyclic and the parent is connected to the prime, on subcontractor-edge creation — `ERR_SUBCONTRACTOR_CYCLE` (409).
24. Computed cascade depth within the client's limit, on subcontractor-edge creation — `ERR_CASCADE_DEPTH_EXCEEDED` (422).
25. No overlapping live coverage interval, on insurance-certificate creation — `ERR_COVERAGE_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (409).
26. Revocation cooldown elapsed, on site-access-grant creation — `ERR_ACCESS_COOLDOWN_ACTIVE` (409).
27. No overlapping granted access window, on site-access-grant creation — `ERR_ACCESS_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (409).
28. Every client-required coverage type backed by a verified and currently effective certificate, on prequalification grading and site-access-grant creation — `ERR_MISSING_VERIFIED_COVERAGE` (409).
29. Certificate limits clear the applicable adequacy test, on insurance-certificate verification, prequalification grading, site-access-grant creation and subcontractor-edge creation — `ERR_COVERAGE_LIMIT_INSUFFICIENT` (422).
30. Grade at or above the site's effective minimum, on site-access-grant creation — `ERR_GRADE_BELOW_SITE_MINIMUM` (422).

Two consequences of this ordering are worth stating outright. Because segregation of duties sits at position 8 and transition legality at position 10, an analyst who is barred from a certificate is told so even if the certificate has already left its pending posture. And because acyclicity sits at position 23 while coverage adequacy sits at position 29, a proposed subcontractor edge that is both cyclic and under-insured reports `ERR_SUBCONTRACTOR_CYCLE`, never the coverage code.

### 2.10. Cascade Discipline and Conserved Counters

Every cascade described in this document completes inside the request that triggered it. There is no deferred queue, no eventual reconciliation and no background worker: when an incident report suspends four prequalifications and revokes eleven badges, all fifteen rows have moved before the response is written. Where a cascade processes a set of rows, the processing order is always stated, and it is always deterministic — never dependent on identifier values, which are random.

Two materialized counters are **conserved quantities**, meaning the stored field must equal its defining query after every single request, with zero drift.

The first is a site's `granted_worker_count`, which must always equal the number of site access grants on that site whose `status` is exactly `GRANTED`. Revoked and expired grants are excluded. A grant followed by its compensating revocation must return the field to its exact pre-grant value, and a revocation never drives the field below 0.

The second is a contractor's `open_deficiency_count`, which must always equal the total number of deficiency codes summed across that contractor's prequalifications whose `status` is exactly `CONDITIONAL`, across all clients. Rows in any other status contribute zero even when their `deficiency_codes` array is still populated — a suspended, expired or rejected row stops counting the moment it leaves `CONDITIONAL`. Grading a row conditionally therefore raises the counter by exactly the number of codes it was given, and the compensating action — an accepted improvement plan that clears the codes and returns the row to review — must return the counter to its exact pre-grading value.

### 2.11. Segregation of Verification Duties

The network's credibility rests on two independent verifications per contractor file. The analyst identity that verified any safety statistic belonging to a contractor is permanently barred from verifying that same contractor's insurance certificates. Concretely: when a `VerificationAnalyst` attempts an insurance-certificate decision, the server searches every safety statistic on every prequalification of that certificate's contractor for a `verified_by_user_id` equal to the acting `X-User-ID`. If one is found, the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 409). The bar applies to both possible decisions — approving and refusing a certificate are equally blocked — and it is checked before the certificate's own transition legality. A second, distinct analyst identity must perform the other half, so a fully verified file always carries two different verifier identities. The bar is one-directional in scope: it is enforced on the certificate side only, so nothing prevents an analyst who has already decided a certificate from later verifying a statistic.

### 2.12. Materialized Posture Versus Fresh Recomputation

Several fields in this domain are **materialized**: the write path computes them and stores them so that reads are cheap. That creates a hazard, because the logical clock can move without any write occurring, leaving a stored value describing a world that no longer exists.

The rule is therefore split, and the split is normative. A certificate's `lifecycle_status` is written to storage at exactly three moments: when the certificate is created, when it is verified or refused, and when a requalification run is executed for a client the contractor is engaged with. After a bare clock advance the stored value is deliberately stale. Every gate that depends on lifecycle position — the coverage check at grading, the coverage check at badge creation, the flow-down coverage check on a subcontractor edge, and the recomputation of a contractor's `verified_coverage_types` — must recompute the lifecycle position fresh against the current logical clock rather than trusting the stored field. A certificate whose stored value still reads `CURRENT` but which has in fact lapsed against the clock blocks grading and blocks badging.

The same fresh-recomputation discipline governs read projections. A site's `granted_worker_count` and `access_gate_status` are recomputed for the response on every site read; a certificate's `lifecycle_status` and `available_aggregate` are recomputed for the response on every certificate read; a written program's `is_required` is recomputed for the response on every program read; and an improvement plan reads as `OVERDUE` whenever its `due_date` is at or before the logical clock, even though no write has yet materialized that status. A contractor's `base_hazard_tier`, `incident_review_flag`, `open_severe_incident_count`, `open_deficiency_count` and `verified_coverage_types` are, by contrast, reported as the materialized values the write path maintains.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up the Network: Clients, Sites, Contractors and Crews

A `NetworkAdmin` brings a hiring client onto the network by registering it with a `legal_name` of 1 to 200 characters that must be unique across all clients, and an `industry_code` that must be exactly six digits. Everything else about a client is optional at registration and falls back to a published default, because the whole point of the client record is that its yardsticks are visible: contractors are allowed to read a client's thresholds precisely so that a contractor can predict its own grade before submitting. The `grading_policy` selects which grade-band table applies and is either `STRICT` or `BALANCED`, defaulting to `BALANCED`. Four numeric ceilings are published: `max_trir` defaults to 3.00, `max_dart_rate` defaults to 1.50, `max_emr` defaults to 1.00 — each a positive number carried to two decimal places — and `max_citation_points` defaults to 6 and may not be negative. Four composite weights are published alongside them: `weight_trir` defaults to 0.40, `weight_dart` to 0.25, `weight_emr` to 0.25 and `weight_citation` to 0.10; each must be greater than 0 and at most 1, carried to two decimal places, and the four together must sum to exactly 1.00. Supplying a partial set that breaks that sum is refused with `ERR_INVALID_FIELD_VALUE`.

Five more client-level dials shape the rest of this document. `conditional_band_pct` defaults to 20 and must be between 0 and 100 inclusive; it is the percentage above a resolved threshold that still earns a conditional outcome instead of an outright refusal. `requalification_interval_months` defaults to 12 and must be between 1 and 36; it is how long a graded standing lives. `audit_sample_rate_pct` defaults to 25 and must be between 0 and 100 inclusive; it is the share of the eligible population drawn per audit run. `cascade_depth_limit` defaults to 2 and must be between 1 and 5; it caps how many subcontracting tiers the client tolerates below a prime. `required_coverage_types` defaults to general liability and workers' compensation — the array `["GL", "WC"]` — and must be a non-empty duplicate-free subset of `GL`, `WC`, `AUTO` and `UMBRELLA`. A client is created at `version` 1, and the `id` minted for it is the value a `ClientSafetyManager` must present as `X-User-ID` when acting for that client.

The client's own safety manager then creates the sites where work will actually happen. Each site carries a `site_code` of 1 to 40 characters that must be unique among that client's sites — the site record's `client_id` names the owning client — and a `required_hazard_classes` array that must be a non-empty subset of `GENERAL`, `HEIGHTS`, `CONFINED_SPACE`, `ELECTRICAL`, `HOT_WORK` and `EXCAVATION`. This array is the site's declaration of what its work involves, and it drives both the hazard-profile match and the worker-credential set demanded at the gate. An optional `min_grade`, one of `A`, `B` or `C` and defaulting to `C`, is the lowest prequalification grade the client will admit at that site. A new site begins with `granted_worker_count` at 0 and `access_gate_status` at `OPEN`.

Contractors are likewise registered by a `NetworkAdmin`, with a `legal_name` of 1 to 200 characters unique across all contractors, a `scope_of_work` array that must be a non-empty subset of `GENERAL_BUILDING`, `ELECTRICAL_TRADE`, `MECHANICAL_TRADE`, `SCAFFOLDING`, `ROOFING` and `EARTHWORK`, a `hazard_profile` array drawn from the same six hazard classes as a site's, and an `employee_count` of at least 1. Registration fixes several derived fields: the contractor begins at `version` 1 with `status` `ACTIVE`, `incident_review_flag` false, `open_severe_incident_count` 0, `open_deficiency_count` 0, and an empty `verified_coverage_types` array. Its `base_hazard_tier` is set to 2 when the declared `hazard_profile` includes at least one of the five high-hazard classes — `HEIGHTS`, `CONFINED_SPACE`, `ELECTRICAL`, `HOT_WORK` or `EXCAVATION` — and to 1 otherwise; a contractor whose profile is only `GENERAL` is a tier-1 contractor. The `id` minted for the contractor is the value a `ContractorAdmin` must present as `X-User-ID`.

Amending a contractor's registration is also reserved to a `NetworkAdmin`, which may supply any of `scope_of_work`, `hazard_profile`, `employee_count` or `status`. Each amendment advances the contractor's `version` by exactly 1, and changing `hazard_profile` recomputes `base_hazard_tier`, which in turn changes both the tightening factor the next grading will apply and the coverage multiplier every adequacy test will use. One narrowing is forbidden: the hazard profile may not be reduced below the union of the `required_hazard_classes` of every site where the contractor currently holds a granted badge, and an attempt to do so is refused with `ERR_HAZARD_PROFILE_MISMATCH`. Moving `status` to `SUSPENDED` or `DEBARRED` is not retroactive — existing prequalifications, badges and edges are left exactly as they were. It only closes the door on new activity, surfacing as `ERR_CONTRACTOR_NOT_ACTIVE` on the four operations that demand an active contractor: opening a prequalification cycle, submitting one for review, requesting a site badge, and creating a subcontractor edge.

A contractor's `ContractorAdmin` maintains the crew roster. Each worker is added with a `full_name` of 1 to 120 characters, an `employee_ref` of 1 to 40 characters that must be unique within that contractor — a duplicate is `ERR_INVALID_FIELD_VALUE` — and a `trade_code` drawn from the same six trades as the contractor's scope. The worker record names its employing contractor through `contractor_id`. Worker rows are immutable: nothing amends them, and they carry no version. Credentials are appended beneath a worker as qualification rows, each naming its `worker_id` and carrying a `qualification_code` drawn from `OSHA10`, `OSHA30`, `FALL_PROTECTION_COMPETENT`, `CONFINED_SPACE_ENTRANT`, `LOTO_AUTHORIZED`, `HOT_WORK_PERMIT` and `EXCAVATION_COMPETENT`, an `issued_date` at or before the logical clock, an `expiration_date` strictly after the `issued_date` (otherwise `ERR_INVALID_FIELD_VALUE`), a `training_provider` of 1 to 120 characters and an `evidence_ref` of 1 to 120 characters pointing at the uploaded certificate. Renewal is expressed by appending, never by editing: a second row for the same worker and the same `qualification_code` does not replace the first, both rows coexist permanently, and wherever currency is judged the row with the greatest `expiration_date` is the one that counts. Reading a worker's credentials returns every row including long-expired ones, filterable by `qualification_code`; the caller judges currency itself against the clock. A contractor's roster can likewise be filtered by `trade_code`.

### 3.2. Opening a Cycle and Retiring the Previous One

A prequalification is the junction between one contractor and one client for one cycle year, and it is the row everything else in this document hangs from. A `ContractorAdmin` opens one by naming the applying `contractor_id`, the grading `client_id`, the `cycle_year` being applied for, and `expected_contractor_version`. The cycle year must fall within a three-year window centred on the logical clock's own year: one year behind, the current year, or one year ahead. A year outside that window is `ERR_INVALID_FIELD_VALUE`.

Two distinct duplicate rules guard the pair, and both report `ERR_DUPLICATE_PREQUALIFICATION`. The first forbids a second in-flight application: no prequalification may already exist for the same contractor and client with a `status` of `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `UNDER_REVIEW`, regardless of cycle year. The second forbids re-applying for a year already settled: no prequalification may already exist for the same contractor, client **and** cycle year with a `status` of `APPROVED`, `CONDITIONAL` or `SUSPENDED`. Together they permit exactly one open conversation per contractor–client pair at a time, while still allowing a fresh year to be opened over an older active standing.

The eight positions a prequalification's `status` can occupy are `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `APPROVED`, `CONDITIONAL`, `REJECTED`, `SUSPENDED` and `EXPIRED`. A new row begins at `DRAFT`, with `statistics_verification_status` at `NOT_SUBMITTED`, `document_review_status` at `NOT_STARTED` and `audit_state` at `NOT_SELECTED`. `REJECTED` and `EXPIRED` are terminal: nothing leaves them, and any transition attempted from them is `ERR_TRANSITION_NOT_ALLOWED`. The scoring fields — `provisional_composite_score`, `final_composite_score`, `grade`, `effective_thresholds`, `graded_at`, `graded_by_user_id`, `expires_at`, `conditional_expires_at`, `audit_selected_at` — all begin null, `deficiency_codes` begins as an empty array, and `audit_findings_count` begins at 0.

Opening a newer cycle **supersedes** the contractor's older standing with that same client. Every prequalification for the same contractor and client whose `cycle_year` is strictly lower than the new one and whose `status` is `APPROVED`, `CONDITIONAL` or `SUSPENDED` is carried to `EXPIRED`, processed in ascending `cycle_year` order and, within a year, ascending `created_at` order. Each superseded row fires its own full expiry chain rather than merely changing a label: every site access grant on that row still in `GRANTED` is carried to `REVOKED` with `revocation_reason` set to `PREQUAL_EXPIRED` and `revoked_at` set to the logical clock, processed in ascending `created_at` order, each revocation decrementing that site's `granted_worker_count` by 1 and recomputing that site's `access_gate_status`; and the `flow_down_status` of every `ACTIVE` subcontractor edge hanging off the row is recomputed, which drives it to `DEFICIENT` because its prime has left active standing. A superseded row that was `CONDITIONAL` additionally triggers a recount of the contractor's `open_deficiency_count`, because its codes stop counting the instant it leaves `CONDITIONAL`. Finally, opening a cycle advances the contractor's own `version` by 1 and recomputes `open_deficiency_count` once the supersede pass has finished.

Prequalifications can be listed with any combination of the `contractor_id`, `client_id`, `status`, `cycle_year` and `audit_state` filters, which combine conjunctively — every supplied filter must match.

### 3.3. Self-Reported Statistics and Independent Verification

While a prequalification is still in `DRAFT` — and only then; any later status is `ERR_TRANSITION_NOT_ALLOWED` — its `ContractorAdmin` files one or more reporting years of safety statistics beneath it. Each statistic names its `prequalification_id` and carries a `reporting_year`, the `hours_worked` in that year, the `recordable_incidents` count, the `dart_cases` count of days-away/restricted/transfer cases, the `fatalities` count, the carrier-published `emr`, the three citation tallies `citation_count_willful`, `citation_count_serious` and `citation_count_other`, and a `source_document_ref` of 1 to 120 characters pointing at the uploaded loss run or OSHA summary. All counts must be at least 0, `hours_worked` must be at least 0, and `emr` must be strictly greater than 0 and carried to two decimal places.

The reporting year must sit inside a four-year window that ends at the logical clock's own year: the clock's year, or any of the three years before it. Anything outside is `ERR_REPORTING_YEAR_OUT_OF_WINDOW`. Three internal-consistency conditions are then checked together, and any failure among them is `ERR_STATISTICS_INCONSISTENT`: the `dart_cases` count may not exceed the `recordable_incidents` count, the `fatalities` count may not exceed the `recordable_incidents` count, and `hours_worked` must be strictly greater than 0 whenever `recordable_incidents` is greater than 0 — a year with zero recorded hours is admissible only if it also recorded zero incidents. Lastly, no statistic may already exist on that prequalification for the same `reporting_year` while holding a `verification_status` of `PENDING` or `VERIFIED`; if one does, the request is `ERR_DUPLICATE_REPORTING_YEAR`. A previously refused statistic does not block the year — refusal releases it.

Filing a statistic normalizes it immediately. The **total recordable incident rate**, `trir`, is the recordable incident count multiplied by 200,000 employee-hours — the OSHA basis representing one hundred full-time employees working two thousand hours each — divided by the hours actually worked, rounded half-up to four decimal places. The **DART rate**, `dart_rate`, is computed identically but from the days-away/restricted/transfer case count instead. When `hours_worked` is 0 both rates are 0.0000 rather than undefined. Citation history is collapsed into a single integer `citation_points` by weighting severity: each willful citation contributes 5 points, each serious citation contributes 2 points, and each other-than-serious citation contributes 1 point; the three products are added and the result is an exact integer with no rounding step at all. The three counts on the row being created are the only inputs — no other statistic row participates.

A new statistic is born at `verification_status` `PENDING` and immediately advances the parent prequalification's `statistics_verification_status`, the first of the two parallel review dimensions that live on the prequalification row. That dimension is derived, not stored independently: it reads `NOT_SUBMITTED` while no statistic row exists at all; otherwise it takes the `verification_status` of the **governing** statistic, meaning the row with the greatest `reporting_year` among those whose `verification_status` is `PENDING` or `VERIFIED`; and it reads `REJECTED` when statistic rows exist but none of them is `PENDING` or `VERIFIED`.

Only a `VerificationAnalyst` can move a statistic out of `PENDING`, by supplying a `decision` of either `VERIFY` or `REJECT`. Either decision requires the row to still be `PENDING`, otherwise `ERR_TRANSITION_NOT_ALLOWED`, and either decision records `verified_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID` and `verified_at` from the logical clock. `VERIFY` carries the row to `VERIFIED`, which lifts the parent's statistics dimension to `VERIFIED`. `REJECT` carries the row to `REJECTED` and recomputes the parent's dimension by the same derivation rule, which releases the `reporting_year` for a replacement filing while the prequalification is still in `DRAFT`. `VERIFIED` and `REJECTED` are both terminal for a statistic; the analyst identity recorded here is exactly what the segregation-of-duties bar consults when the same principal later reaches for that contractor's insurance. Statistics beneath a prequalification can be listed filtered by `verification_status`.

### 3.4. Written Programs, the Document Dimension, and Reaching Formal Review

The second review dimension is documentary. A `ContractorAdmin` tenders written safety programs beneath a prequalification while its `status` is either `DRAFT` or `SUBMITTED`; any other status is `ERR_TRANSITION_NOT_ALLOWED`. Each document names its `prequalification_id` and declares a `program_type` from `HAZCOM`, `DISCIPLINE_POLICY`, `FALL_PROTECTION`, `CONFINED_SPACE`, `LOTO`, `HOT_WORK` and `EXCAVATION`, together with a `revision_date` that must not be in the future relative to the logical clock and a `document_ref` of 1 to 120 characters. Only one row per program type may exist on a prequalification; tendering a second document of a type already present is refused with `ERR_INVALID_FIELD_VALUE`, and a future-dated `revision_date` is refused with the same code. A tendered document begins at `review_status` `SUBMITTED`; the four positions available to it are `SUBMITTED`, `ACCEPTED`, `DEFICIENT` and `WAIVED`, of which `ACCEPTED` and `WAIVED` are terminal. Two fields are never accepted at creation and are written only by the client's review decision: `reviewer_note` and `reviewed_by_user_id`.

Which programs a client actually demands is derived from the contractor's own hazard declaration, not from a client setting. Each hazard class in the contractor's `hazard_profile` contributes its mandated documents, and the required set is the union of those contributions: `GENERAL` demands both `HAZCOM` and `DISCIPLINE_POLICY`; `HEIGHTS` demands `FALL_PROTECTION`; `CONFINED_SPACE` demands a `CONFINED_SPACE` program; `ELECTRICAL` demands `LOTO`; `HOT_WORK` demands a `HOT_WORK` program; and `EXCAVATION` demands an `EXCAVATION` program. A document's `is_required` flag is true exactly when its `program_type` falls inside that union. A contractor may tender documents outside the union — they are accepted, reviewed and stored, but they carry `is_required` false and they cannot by themselves hold the dimension back.

The prequalification's `document_review_status` is derived from that required set and from every written-program row on the prequalification, with no status filter on the row set itself. The four clauses are evaluated in this exact order and the first match wins: it reads `DEFICIENT` if any required program type has a row whose `review_status` is `DEFICIENT`; otherwise `COMPLETE` if every required program type has a row whose `review_status` is either `ACCEPTED` or `WAIVED`; otherwise `NOT_STARTED` while the prequalification is still in `DRAFT`; otherwise `IN_PROGRESS`. Two consequences follow directly. A `DEFICIENT` verdict on a document that is not required leaves the dimension at `IN_PROGRESS` rather than dragging it to `DEFICIENT`. And tendering documents while the row is still in `DRAFT` leaves the dimension reading `NOT_STARTED` until either the row is submitted or the required set is fully satisfied.

Reviewing a document is the `ClientSafetyManager`'s prerogative and requires the row to be in `SUBMITTED`, otherwise `ERR_TRANSITION_NOT_ALLOWED`. The manager supplies a `decision` of `ACCEPT`, `WAIVE` or `DEFICIENT`, plus an optional `reviewer_note`; all three decisions record the note and the acting identity as `reviewed_by_user_id`. `ACCEPT` carries the row to `ACCEPTED`. `WAIVE` carries it to `WAIVED` and is the client's explicit forgiveness of a required program it does not want to insist on — a waived row counts as satisfied for the dimension exactly as an accepted one does. `DEFICIENT` carries it to `DEFICIENT`. Every decision then recomputes `document_review_status` by the four-clause rule above. A deficient document is not dead: its `ContractorAdmin` may re-tender it, supplying a fresh `document_ref` and `revision_date`, which returns the row from `DEFICIENT` to `SUBMITTED`, discards the previous `reviewer_note` and `reviewed_by_user_id`, and recomputes the dimension so that it climbs back out of `DEFICIENT` toward `IN_PROGRESS`. Re-tendering is permitted only from `DEFICIENT`; from any other position it is `ERR_TRANSITION_NOT_ALLOWED`. This revision loop is what makes the documentary dimension cyclic, and it deliberately avoids creating a second row of the same program type. Documents beneath a prequalification can be listed filtered by `review_status`.

Submitting a prequalification for review carries it from `DRAFT` to `SUBMITTED` and is performed by its `ContractorAdmin` with no request body at all. Four conditions guard it, evaluated in the network-wide precedence order: the contractor must be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_CONTRACTOR_NOT_ACTIVE`; the contractor's `incident_review_flag` must be false, otherwise `ERR_INCIDENT_REVIEW_ACTIVE`; a governing statistic must exist, meaning `statistics_verification_status` is `PENDING` or `VERIFIED`, otherwise `ERR_STATISTICS_UNVERIFIED`; and every program type in the required set must have a row on the prequalification — merely a row, in any review position — otherwise `ERR_REQUIRED_PROGRAM_MISSING`. Submission writes `provisional_composite_score` under the provisional scoring variant, recomputes `document_review_status` (which typically lifts it from `NOT_STARTED` to `IN_PROGRESS`, unless it already reads `COMPLETE` or `DEFICIENT`), and deliberately leaves `grade`, `final_composite_score` and `effective_thresholds` null. The provisional variant is the contractor's honest self-preview: it admits the governing statistic from either `PENDING` or `VERIFIED` rows, it uses the client's **published** thresholds with no hazard-tier tightening whatsoever, and it ignores variances entirely.

Both scoring variants share one normalization engine, and its rounding order is pinned. Exactly one statistic participates per evaluation — the governing row for that variant's admitted set. Four metric values are drawn from it: its `trir`, its `dart_rate`, its `emr` as published by the carrier, and its integer `citation_points`. Each value is scored against its own effective threshold, and because every client threshold is strictly positive the division below is always defined. When a value is at or below its threshold, the sub-score is one hundred minus fifty times the ratio of value to threshold, rounded half-up to two decimal places — so a value exactly equal to its threshold scores exactly 50.00 and a value of zero scores 100.00. When a value is above its threshold, the sub-score is fifty minus fifty times the ratio of the excess over the threshold to the threshold itself, rounded half-up to two decimal places, and then floored at 0.00 so that no sub-score is ever negative. The four sub-scores are then weighted: the TRIR sub-score by `weight_trir`, the DART sub-score by `weight_dart`, the EMR sub-score by `weight_emr` and the citation sub-score by `weight_citation`. **Each weighted product is rounded half-up to two decimal places before it enters the sum**, the four rounded products are added in the fixed metric order TRIR, then DART, then EMR, then citation points, and the total is rounded half-up to two decimal places again. Summing the unrounded products and rounding once at the end is forbidden and produces a different cent.

Reaching `UNDER_REVIEW` is a **fork-join** over the two dimensions, and no request is dedicated to it. The prequalification advances from `SUBMITTED` to `UNDER_REVIEW` exactly when `statistics_verification_status` reads `VERIFIED` and `document_review_status` reads `COMPLETE`; the transition writes no score of any kind. Whichever of the two dimensions completes last performs the join inside its own request — a statistic verification, a document review, or the submission itself if both dimensions were already satisfied before the row left `DRAFT`. Each of those three requests re-evaluates the join before responding.

### 3.5. Insurance: Two Independent Dimensions and Two Adequacy Tests

Insurance evidence lives on the contractor, not on any one prequalification, and it is consumed by every client that engages that contractor. A `ContractorAdmin` files a certificate beneath its own contractor — the row records the insured `contractor_id` — declaring a `coverage_type` of `GL`, `WC`, `AUTO` or `UMBRELLA`, a `carrier_name` of 1 to 120 characters, an `each_occurrence_limit` and an `aggregate_limit` both expressed in whole US dollars and neither negative, an `effective_date` and an `expiration_date`, and optionally the two endorsement flags `additional_insured` and `waiver_of_subrogation`, each defaulting to false. The `aggregate_limit` must be at least as large as the `each_occurrence_limit` and the `expiration_date` must be strictly after the `effective_date`; either violation is `ERR_INVALID_FIELD_VALUE`.

A certificate's coverage interval is **half-open**: it includes its `effective_date` and excludes its `expiration_date`. That choice makes seamless renewal legal and near-misses illegal. No new certificate may be filed whose interval intersects the interval of an existing certificate for the same contractor and the same coverage type while that existing certificate holds a `verification_status` of `PENDING` or `VERIFIED`; an intersection is `ERR_COVERAGE_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. Because the interval excludes its endpoint, a renewal whose `effective_date` is exactly the incumbent's `expiration_date` does not intersect and is accepted, while a renewal starting one second earlier does intersect and is refused.

Two state dimensions coexist on a single certificate row and neither gates the other. The first, `verification_status`, moves only by an analyst's decision and occupies `PENDING`, `VERIFIED` or `REJECTED`, of which `REJECTED` is terminal. The second, `lifecycle_status`, moves only with the logical clock and occupies `NOT_YET_EFFECTIVE`, `CURRENT`, `EXPIRING_SOON`, `LAPSED` or `SUPERSEDED`. A still-unverified certificate can perfectly well reach `LAPSED`, and a verified certificate remains `VERIFIED` after it lapses — the two dimensions simply describe different facts.

The lifecycle position is derived from the clock by five clauses evaluated in order, the first match winning. A certificate whose `verification_status` is `REJECTED` and over whose interval a later certificate has since been filed reads `SUPERSEDED`; this is the only way a row reaches `SUPERSEDED`, and it is written by the side effect described below rather than by the clock. Otherwise, a clock earlier than the `effective_date` gives `NOT_YET_EFFECTIVE`. A clock at or after the `expiration_date` gives `LAPSED` — at, not merely after, because the interval excludes its upper endpoint. Otherwise the number of complete days remaining is taken as the whole number of 86,400-second days between the clock and the `expiration_date`, discarding any partial day; thirty or fewer complete days remaining gives `EXPIRING_SOON`, and the boundary is inclusive so exactly thirty days out already reads `EXPIRING_SOON`. Anything further out reads `CURRENT`.

Filing a certificate sets `verification_status` to `PENDING`, materializes `lifecycle_status` against the clock, materializes `available_aggregate` under the depleted-aggregate basis, and then sweeps the contractor's history for that coverage type: every previously **refused** certificate whose interval intersects the new one has its `lifecycle_status` written to `SUPERSEDED`. Finally the contractor's `verified_coverage_types` array is recomputed. That array holds exactly those coverage types for which the contractor currently holds a certificate whose `verification_status` is `VERIFIED` and whose freshly recomputed lifecycle position is `CURRENT` or `EXPIRING_SOON` — a lapsed certificate drops its coverage type out of the array even though the row itself still reads `VERIFIED`.

A `VerificationAnalyst` decides a certificate by supplying a `decision` of `VERIFY` or `REJECT`. Three guards apply in the network's precedence order. First the segregation-of-duties bar of Section 2.11: an analyst who has already verified any safety statistic of this certificate's contractor is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, and this fires ahead of transition legality, so it is reported even for a certificate that has already been decided. Then the row must still be `PENDING`, otherwise `ERR_TRANSITION_NOT_ALLOWED`. Then, for `VERIFY` only, the certificate's per-occurrence limit must clear its floor, otherwise `ERR_COVERAGE_LIMIT_INSUFFICIENT`. `VERIFY` writes `verified_by_user_id` from the acting identity, carries `verification_status` to `VERIFIED`, re-materializes `lifecycle_status` and `available_aggregate`, and recomputes `verified_coverage_types`. `REJECT` also writes `verified_by_user_id`, carries the row to `REJECTED`, removes that coverage type from `verified_coverage_types`, and releases the coverage interval so that an overlapping replacement certificate may be filed.

Adequacy is judged two different ways, and the same certificate can pass one test and fail the other. Both tests scale the requirement by the contractor's `base_hazard_tier`: a tier-1 contractor's requirement is multiplied by 1.0 and a tier-2 contractor's by 1.5, and the resulting requirement is rounded half-up to whole dollars before any comparison. Both comparisons are inclusive — a limit exactly equal to its requirement is adequate.

The **per-occurrence basis** is used only at certificate verification. It judges `each_occurrence_limit` against a floor of 1,000,000 dollars for `GL`, 500,000 dollars for `WC`, 1,000,000 dollars for `AUTO` and 2,000,000 dollars for `UMBRELLA`, and it ignores incident depletion completely. A general-liability certificate carrying one million per occurrence and two million aggregate, held by a tier-1 contractor, is adequate on this basis.

The **depleted-aggregate basis** is used at prequalification grading and at site-access-grant creation. It first computes `available_aggregate` as the `aggregate_limit` reduced by 250,000 dollars for every open severe incident the contractor currently carries — that is, by 250,000 dollars multiplied by the contractor's `open_severe_incident_count`. It then judges that available figure against a floor of 2,000,000 dollars for `GL`, 1,000,000 dollars for `WC`, 1,000,000 dollars for `AUTO` and 4,000,000 dollars for `UMBRELLA`. There is no floor under the subtraction: `available_aggregate` may go negative, and a negative figure is simply inadequate. The consequence is deliberate — the same one-million-per-occurrence, two-million-aggregate general-liability certificate that passed verification becomes inadequate the moment its contractor carries a single open recordable incident, because 2,000,000 less 250,000 leaves 1,750,000 against a 2,000,000 floor, and `ERR_COVERAGE_LIMIT_INSUFFICIENT` then fires at grading and at the gate even though verification succeeded earlier. Certificates beneath a contractor can be listed filtered by `coverage_type`.

### 3.6. Grading: Resolving the Yardstick, Assigning the Letter, Choosing the Outcome

Grading is the `ClientSafetyManager`'s act and it is permitted only while the prequalification is in `UNDER_REVIEW`; from anywhere else it is `ERR_TRANSITION_NOT_ALLOWED`. It takes no request body — every input it needs is already on the row, on the contractor, on the client and in the incident record. Three gates stand in front of it, in precedence order. The contractor's `incident_review_flag` must be false, otherwise `ERR_INCIDENT_REVIEW_ACTIVE`: a contractor under active severe-incident review cannot be graded at all. Every coverage type in the client's `required_coverage_types` must be backed by a certificate whose `verification_status` is `VERIFIED` and whose **freshly recomputed** lifecycle position is `CURRENT` or `EXPIRING_SOON`, otherwise `ERR_MISSING_VERIFIED_COVERAGE` — a certificate that has silently lapsed against the clock blocks grading even though its stored lifecycle field still reads `CURRENT`. And each of those certificates must clear the depleted-aggregate adequacy test of Section 3.5, otherwise `ERR_COVERAGE_LIMIT_INSUFFICIENT`.

The yardstick is then resolved in five ordered steps, and the order is normative because the incident set consulted in the first step depends on badges that only exist because of a standing this very request is about to change. Steps one through four are computed **exactly once**, against the pre-transition snapshot in which the row is still `UNDER_REVIEW`, and nothing computed after step four may feed back into them.

The first step takes a client-scoped snapshot of incident severity. The relevant sites are those the contractor has ever been badged into through any prequalification it holds with this same client — every site access grant belonging to any such prequalification contributes its site, regardless of whether that grant is currently granted, revoked or expired. Among incident reports filed against this contractor at those sites, those whose `occurred_at` falls within the trailing 365 days of the logical clock are collected, with both endpoints inclusive and with both open and closed reviews counting equally. The severity snapshot is the highest severity present among them, ranked as `FIRST_AID` at 1, `RECORDABLE` at 2, `LOST_TIME` at 3 and `FATALITY` at 4, or 0 when no such incident exists.

The second step fixes the hazard tier. The base tier is the contractor's own: 2 when its `hazard_profile` contains at least one of the five high-hazard classes and 1 otherwise. When the severity snapshot has reached rank 3 or above — that is, when a lost-time or fatal incident sits in the window — the tier is raised by one and then capped at 3; otherwise the base tier stands unchanged. A tier-2 contractor with a fatality in the window therefore grades at tier 3, and a tier-1 contractor with the same fatality grades at tier 2.

The third step converts the tier into a tightening factor and applies it. Under the provisional variant the factor is forced to 1.00, which is what makes the provisional preview the client's published position. Under the final variant the factor is 1.00 at tier 1, 0.85 at tier 2 and 0.70 at tier 3. Each of the client's four published ceilings — `max_trir`, `max_dart_rate`, `max_emr` and `max_citation_points` — is multiplied by that factor and rounded half-up to four decimal places, producing the effective ceiling the corresponding metric will actually be measured against. Tightening only ever makes a ceiling harder to clear.

The fourth step exists only under the final variant; the provisional variant skips it entirely. Every variance on this prequalification whose `status` is `APPROVED`, whose `expires_at` is strictly after the logical clock, and whose `requirement_code` is one of `TRIR`, `DART`, `EMR` or `CITATION_POINTS` relaxes the matching effective ceiling: that ceiling becomes the greater of its already-tightened value and the variance's `requested_value` rounded half-up to four decimal places. Because at most one approved variance can exist per requirement code, the overlay is order-independent. A variance can therefore only loosen a ceiling that tightening has already narrowed; it can never re-tighten one, and an expired approval contributes nothing.

The fifth step is simply the consumption order: normalization first, then the letter grade, then the outcome decision, then the status write. The resolved ceilings are pinned onto the row as the `effective_thresholds` snapshot object, which carries `max_trir`, `max_dart_rate`, `max_emr`, `max_citation_points`, the `tightening` factor that was applied, and the `hazard_tier` it came from.

With `final_composite_score` in hand, the letter is assigned from the client's `grading_policy`. Under `BALANCED`, a score of at least 85.00 is an `A`, at least 70.00 a `B`, at least 55.00 a `C`, and anything lower an `F`. Under `STRICT`, a score of at least 90.00 is an `A`, at least 78.00 a `B`, at least 66.00 a `C`, and anything lower an `F`. Every comparison is inclusive at the band floor, so a score landing exactly on a floor takes the higher letter. Grades rank `F` below `C`, `C` below `B`, and `B` below `A`.

The outcome is then chosen by classifying each metric's breach severity. The client's `conditional_band_pct` defines a tolerance multiplier of one plus that percentage expressed as a fraction — a default of 20 yields a multiplier of 1.20 — and each metric's tolerance ceiling is its effective ceiling multiplied by that multiplier, rounded half-up to four decimal places. A metric is a **hard breach** when its value exceeds its tolerance ceiling. A metric is a **soft breach** when its value exceeds its effective ceiling but does not exceed its tolerance ceiling. Both comparisons against the effective ceiling itself are strict: a value exactly equal to its effective ceiling is not a breach at all.

Three outcomes follow, and exactly one grading transition fires. When at least one hard breach exists, or the letter is `F`, the row is carried from `UNDER_REVIEW` to `REJECTED`, and `deficiency_codes` is built by walking the metrics in the fixed order `TRIR`, `DART`, `EMR`, `CITATION_POINTS` and emitting the code of every metric that is either a hard or a soft breach, followed by `COMPOSITE_F` when the letter is `F`. Otherwise, when at least one soft breach exists or the letter is `C`, the row is carried to `CONDITIONAL`, and `deficiency_codes` holds the soft-breach metric codes in that same fixed order, followed by `COMPOSITE_C` when the letter is `C`. Otherwise the row is carried to `APPROVED` with an empty `deficiency_codes` array. The composite code, when present, is always last.

All three transitions store the `effective_thresholds` snapshot, write `final_composite_score` under the final variant, write `grade`, set `graded_at` to the logical clock and set `graded_by_user_id` to the acting `X-User-ID`. Beyond that they diverge. An approval sets `expires_at` and leaves `conditional_expires_at` null and `deficiency_codes` empty. A conditional grading sets `expires_at` identically, additionally sets `conditional_expires_at` to 90 days after `graded_at`, and raises the contractor's `open_deficiency_count` by exactly the number of codes it just wrote — recomputed, as always, from the conserved-quantity definition in Section 2.10. A refusal sets both `expires_at` and `conditional_expires_at` to null, and it revokes access: every site access grant on the row still in `GRANTED` is carried to `REVOKED` with `revocation_reason` set to `PREQUAL_REJECTED`, processed in ascending `created_at` order, each decrementing its site's `granted_worker_count` by 1 and recomputing that site's `access_gate_status`. All three outcomes recompute the contractor's `open_deficiency_count` and recompute `flow_down_status` on every `ACTIVE` subcontractor edge whose prime is this row; under a refusal that recomputation necessarily lands on `DEFICIENT`.

The annual deadline itself is calendar arithmetic, not day arithmetic. `expires_at` is `graded_at` advanced by the client's `requalification_interval_months` calendar months, keeping the same day of month; when the target month is too short to hold that day, the last day of the target month is used instead. Requalification comparisons are inclusive: a row is due to expire once the logical clock reaches `expires_at`, and a conditional row is due to be refused once the clock reaches `conditional_expires_at`.

A read-only scorecard projection lets any role inspect the whole grading picture in one response without mutating anything. It reports `prequalification_id`, `contractor_id`, `client_id`, `status`, `grade`, `provisional_composite_score`, `final_composite_score`, `governing_reporting_year`, `hazard_tier`, a `normalized_metrics` object holding the governing statistic's `trir`, `dart_rate`, `emr` and `citation_points`, the `effective_thresholds` object, `deficiency_codes`, `conditional_expires_at` and `expires_at`. Both scores are recomputed live from the currently governing statistic so the two variants can be compared side by side — the provisional figure against the published, untightened ceilings and the final figure against the tightened, variance-overlaid ones. Both read null until a statistic has been verified, and `grade` and `effective_thresholds` read null until the row has been graded.

### 3.7. Curing a Conditional Standing: Plans, Variances and Audit Findings

A conditional standing is real standing — the contractor may badge workers and may subcontract — but it carries a 90-day clock and a list of named defects. The client drives the cure by issuing an improvement plan against the row, which is permitted only while the prequalification's `status` is `CONDITIONAL`; from anywhere else it is `ERR_TRANSITION_NOT_ALLOWED`. The plan names its `prequalification_id` and carries a `due_date`, a `milestone_count` between 1 and 20 inclusive, and the `expected_prequalification_version` demanded by Section 2.8. The `due_date` must be strictly after the logical clock and at or before the row's `conditional_expires_at`; either violation is `ERR_INVALID_FIELD_VALUE`. A plan cannot outlive the cure window it exists to serve.

Creating a plan snapshots the row's current `deficiency_codes` into the plan's own `deficiency_codes` array, so the plan permanently records what it was meant to fix even after the row's list is cleared. The plan begins at `status` `OPEN`, with `evidence_ref`, `decided_by_user_id` and `closed_at` all null; `evidence_ref` is never accepted at creation. Any plan already on the row whose `status` is `OPEN` or `SUBMITTED` is superseded — carried to `REJECTED` with `closed_at` set to the logical clock — so that exactly one live plan governs the row. Creating a plan also advances the prequalification's `version` by 1, which is precisely why the expected-version guard exists. The five positions a plan can occupy are `OPEN`, `SUBMITTED`, `ACCEPTED`, `REJECTED` and `OVERDUE`, of which `ACCEPTED` and `OVERDUE` are terminal.

The contractor answers by submitting evidence: its `ContractorAdmin` supplies an `evidence_ref`, carrying the plan from `OPEN` to `SUBMITTED`. Two conditions guard it and both report `ERR_TRANSITION_NOT_ALLOWED` — the plan must currently be `OPEN`, and the logical clock must be strictly before the plan's `due_date`. Evidence tendered on or after the deadline is refused.

The client then decides, supplying a `decision` of `ACCEPT` or `REJECT`, and the plan must be in `SUBMITTED`, otherwise `ERR_TRANSITION_NOT_ALLOWED`. `REJECT` is the revert loop: the plan returns to `OPEN`, its `evidence_ref` is cleared, `decided_by_user_id` records the acting manager, and the prequalification itself is left completely untouched and remains `CONDITIONAL` — the contractor may gather better evidence and submit again, as long as the deadline has not arrived. `ACCEPT` is the compensating action for the conditional grading. It carries the plan to `ACCEPTED` with `closed_at` set to the logical clock and `decided_by_user_id` recorded, and it drags the prequalification back from `CONDITIONAL` to `UNDER_REVIEW`, emptying its `deficiency_codes`, nulling its `conditional_expires_at`, its `grade`, its `final_composite_score` and its `effective_thresholds`, while leaving `expires_at` in place. It then recomputes the contractor's `open_deficiency_count`, which by the conservation rule of Section 2.10 must land back on exactly the value it held before the conditional grading, with zero drift. Finally it recomputes `flow_down_status` on every `ACTIVE` subcontractor edge whose prime is that row, which typically lifts each edge from `DEFICIENT` back to `PENDING`. The row is now back in review and can be graded again — a second conditional, or an approval. Improvement plans beneath a prequalification can be listed filtered by `status`.

A **variance** is the other route to relief, and unlike a plan it changes the yardstick rather than the evidence. The contractor's admin requests one while the prequalification's `status` is `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `CONDITIONAL`; from `APPROVED`, `REJECTED`, `SUSPENDED` or `EXPIRED` it is `ERR_TRANSITION_NOT_ALLOWED`. The request names its `prequalification_id`, a `requirement_code` from `TRIR`, `DART`, `EMR`, `CITATION_POINTS` and `HAZARD_CLASS`, a `requested_value` strictly greater than 0 naming the ceiling it asks the client to accept, and a `justification` of 1 to 500 characters. At most one live request per requirement code may exist on a prequalification — live meaning a `status` of `REQUESTED` or `APPROVED` — and a duplicate is `ERR_TRANSITION_NOT_ALLOWED`. A new request sits at `REQUESTED` and changes nothing about grading until it is decided.

The client's manager decides a variance with a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `DENY`, and the request must still be `REQUESTED`, otherwise `ERR_TRANSITION_NOT_ALLOWED`. `APPROVE` additionally requires an `expires_at` to be supplied and to be strictly after the logical clock; a missing or non-future value is `ERR_INVALID_FIELD_VALUE`. Approval writes `expires_at` and `decided_by_user_id` and enters the variance into the fourth step of threshold resolution as a suppressed breach for as long as `expires_at` remains strictly after the clock. `DENY` carries the row to `DENIED`, leaves `expires_at` null, and releases the `requirement_code` so a fresh request may be filed. `APPROVED` and `DENIED` are both terminal, and `expires_at` is never accepted at creation — only the decision writes it. Variances can be listed filtered by `status`.

Audit findings close the third loop on a conditional or approved row. A prequalification carries a second, entirely independent dimension in `audit_state`, occupying `NOT_SELECTED`, `SELECTED`, `PASSED` or `FAILED`, of which `PASSED` and `FAILED` are terminal. This dimension never moves `status`, and `status` never moves it: an approved row and a conditional row are equally drawable, and a row that leaves active standing keeps whatever `audit_state` it had — it is simply never drawn again, because only a `NOT_SELECTED` row is eligible for a draw. Once a row has been drawn, the client's manager records the outcome by supplying a `findings_count` of at least 0; the row must be in `SELECTED`, otherwise `ERR_TRANSITION_NOT_ALLOWED`. A `findings_count` of 0 carries the dimension to `PASSED` and writes `audit_findings_count` as 0. A `findings_count` of 1 or more carries it to `FAILED`, writes that number into `audit_findings_count`, and appends the code `AUDIT_SAMPLING` to the prequalification's `deficiency_codes` if it is not already present — always after any metric and composite codes — which then recomputes the contractor's `open_deficiency_count`. The row's `status` is untouched by either outcome; a failed audit is cured through an improvement plan or simply superseded by the next cycle.

### 3.8. The Gate: Badging a Worker onto a Site

A site access grant is the three-way junction where everything else in this domain is cashed in: it binds one worker, one site and one prequalification, and it is the densest gate in the system. Its `ContractorAdmin` requests one by naming a `worker_id`, a `site_id`, a `prequalification_id` and the `expires_at` instant at which the badge should stop working. The requested `expires_at` must be strictly after the logical clock.

Before any posture check runs, the three references must actually describe one coherent situation: the named worker must be employed by the same contractor the named prequalification belongs to, and the named site must belong to the same client that prequalification was graded by. A mismatch on either axis is `ERR_INVALID_FIELD_VALUE` — one contractor cannot badge another's worker, and a prequalification held with one client cannot open a different client's gate.

Ten further checks then fire in strict precedence order, and the first failure is the only one reported. The employing contractor must be `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_CONTRACTOR_NOT_ACTIVE`. The prequalification must be in `APPROVED` or `CONDITIONAL` — conditional standing is sufficient — else `ERR_PREQUAL_NOT_ACTIVE`. The site's freshly recomputed gate must not be locked, else `ERR_SITE_GATE_LOCKED`. Every hazard class in the site's `required_hazard_classes` must appear in the contractor's `hazard_profile`, else `ERR_HAZARD_PROFILE_MISMATCH`; the contractor must be qualified for everything the site does, though it may be qualified for more. The worker must hold a current credential for every code the site's hazards demand, else `ERR_WORKER_QUALIFICATION_EXPIRED`. The revocation cooldown must have elapsed, else `ERR_ACCESS_COOLDOWN_ACTIVE`. The requested window must not collide with an existing granted window, else `ERR_ACCESS_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. Every coverage type in the client's `required_coverage_types` must be backed by a verified certificate whose freshly recomputed lifecycle position is `CURRENT` or `EXPIRING_SOON`, else `ERR_MISSING_VERIFIED_COVERAGE`. Each such certificate must clear the depleted-aggregate adequacy test, else `ERR_COVERAGE_LIMIT_INSUFFICIENT`. And the prequalification's `grade` must rank at or above the site's effective minimum, else `ERR_GRADE_BELOW_SITE_MINIMUM`.

Which credentials a site demands is derived from its hazard classes, one mapping per class, and the demand is the union across the classes present. `GENERAL` work demands `OSHA10`. Work at `HEIGHTS` demands both `OSHA30` and `FALL_PROTECTION_COMPETENT`. `CONFINED_SPACE` work demands `OSHA30` and `CONFINED_SPACE_ENTRANT`. `ELECTRICAL` work demands `OSHA30` and `LOTO_AUTHORIZED`. `HOT_WORK` demands `OSHA10` and `HOT_WORK_PERMIT`. `EXCAVATION` demands `OSHA30` and `EXCAVATION_COMPETENT`. A credential counts as current only when its `expiration_date` is strictly after the logical clock — a credential expiring at exactly this instant is not current. Every qualification row the worker has ever been given participates in the search, because the entity is append-only and has no status to filter on; where several rows share a code, the one with the latest `expiration_date` decides. When the grant is issued, the resolved demand set is frozen into the grant's `qualification_snapshot` array, sorted ascending by code name.

A site's `access_gate_status` is a posture derived from the open incident record at that site, counting incidents filed against **every** contractor working there, not just the one being badged. Three clauses are evaluated in order and the first match wins: if any incident report at the site whose `review_status` is `OPEN` carries a `severity` of `LOST_TIME` or `FATALITY`, the gate is `LOCKED`; otherwise, if any open report there carries a `severity` of `RECORDABLE`, the gate is `RESTRICTED`; otherwise the gate is `OPEN`. A `FIRST_AID` report never changes the posture at all.

The gate posture also raises the bar a grade must clear. The site's own `min_grade` sets a base rank, with `F` at 0, `C` at 1, `B` at 2 and `A` at 3. When the gate reads `RESTRICTED`, the required rank is that base rank plus one, capped at 3 — a site normally admitting `C` work demands `B` while a recordable incident sits open there, and a site already demanding `A` stays at `A`. When the gate reads `OPEN`, the base rank stands. The prequalification's grade is admitted when its own rank is greater than or equal to the required rank. A `LOCKED` gate never reaches this comparison, because the lock check refuses the request several steps earlier.

Access windows are **closed** intervals, including both endpoints, which makes them stricter than coverage intervals. The window being requested runs from the logical clock through the requested `expires_at`; an existing grant's window runs from its `granted_at` through its `expires_at`. If the requested window shares even a single instant with the window of any grant for the same worker and the same site that is still in `GRANTED`, the request is refused with `ERR_ACCESS_WINDOW_OVERLAP` — touching endpoints do collide here, unlike the half-open coverage rule. The revocation cooldown is the mirror-image rule: for the same worker and site, no previously revoked grant may have a `revoked_at` whose 24-hour cooldown has not yet elapsed. The test is strict at the far end, so a clock sitting exactly 24 hours after the revocation passes while one second earlier is `ERR_ACCESS_COOLDOWN_ACTIVE`.

A successful grant is created in `status` `GRANTED` with `granted_at` set to the logical clock, which is the inclusive start of its window, and with `revocation_reason` and `revoked_at` both null. It increments the site's `granted_worker_count` by exactly 1 and recomputes that site's `access_gate_status`. The three positions a grant occupies are `GRANTED`, `REVOKED` and `EXPIRED`, the latter two terminal.

Revocation is the client manager's lever. It requires the grant to be in `GRANTED`, else `ERR_TRANSITION_NOT_ALLOWED`, and it accepts an optional `revocation_reason` which defaults to `CLIENT_REVOKED` when omitted. `CLIENT_REVOKED` is the only value a caller may supply; the three cascade reasons `PREQUAL_SUSPENDED`, `PREQUAL_REJECTED` and `PREQUAL_EXPIRED` are written exclusively by the system and supplying any of them explicitly is `ERR_INVALID_FIELD_VALUE`. Revocation sets `revoked_at` to the logical clock, decrements the site's `granted_worker_count` by 1, recomputes the site's `access_gate_status`, and starts the 24-hour cooldown for that worker-and-site pair. Every cascade revocation described elsewhere in this document behaves identically, differing only in the reason it records.

Expiry is the one departure from `GRANTED` that leaves no cooldown behind. A grant whose `expires_at` is strictly before the logical clock is carried to `EXPIRED` when its client's requalification run is executed; that decrements the site's `granted_worker_count` by 1 and recomputes the gate posture, but `revoked_at` stays null and no cooldown window opens, so the worker may be re-badged immediately. Grants can be listed filtered by any combination of `worker_id`, `site_id`, `prequalification_id` and `status`.

### 3.9. Incidents: Filing, Freezing, and the Reversible Half of the Cascade

Incident reports are filed by the client, not the contractor — the party that owns the site records what happened on it. A `ClientSafetyManager` files one by naming the involved `contractor_id`, the `site_id` where it happened, the `occurred_at` instant, a `severity` of `FIRST_AID`, `RECORDABLE`, `LOST_TIME` or `FATALITY`, a `description` of 1 to 500 characters, and optionally the `osha_reportable` flag, which defaults to false. The `occurred_at` instant must be at or before the logical clock and no earlier than 365 days before it; either violation is `ERR_INVALID_FIELD_VALUE`. The named site must belong to the acting manager's own client, otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. A new report opens at `review_status` `OPEN`, with `findings_note` and `closed_at` null and `triggered_suspension_count` at 0; `findings_note` is never accepted at filing. `OPEN` and `CLOSED` are the only two positions, and `CLOSED` is terminal.

Filing a report has four consequences, and they compound. First, when the `severity` is `RECORDABLE`, `LOST_TIME` or `FATALITY`, the contractor's `open_severe_incident_count` rises by 1 — that field counts the contractor's reports at those three severities whose review is still open — and because insurance depletion is driven by that counter, `available_aggregate` is re-materialized on every one of that contractor's insurance certificates. A single recordable event can therefore push a previously adequate aggregate below its floor. Second, when the `severity` is `LOST_TIME` or `FATALITY`, the contractor's `incident_review_flag` is set true; the flag reads true whenever at least one of the contractor's lost-time or fatal reports still has an open review, and while it is true the contractor can neither submit nor be graded. Third, the contractor's `version` advances by 1, which is exactly why the expected-version guard on cycle registration matters. Fourth, the site's `access_gate_status` is recomputed, which locks the gate outright for a lost-time or fatal report and restricts it for a recordable one.

When the review flag flips from false to true, the freeze cascades down through the contractor's entire active standing. Every prequalification of that contractor whose `status` is `APPROVED` or `CONDITIONAL` — across all clients, not merely the client that filed the report — is carried to `SUSPENDED`, processed in ascending `created_at` order. Each such suspension revokes every site access grant on that prequalification still in `GRANTED`, writing `revocation_reason` as `PREQUAL_SUSPENDED` and `revoked_at` as the logical clock, itself processed in ascending `created_at` order, with each revocation decrementing its site's `granted_worker_count` and recomputing that site's gate posture; it recomputes `flow_down_status` on every `ACTIVE` subcontractor edge hanging off the row; it recomputes the contractor's `open_deficiency_count`; and it increments the filing report's `triggered_suspension_count` by 1, so the report itself records how many standings it froze. A row suspended out of `CONDITIONAL` keeps its `conditional_expires_at` unchanged — the cure clock keeps running while the row is frozen — and otherwise behaves identically to one suspended out of `APPROVED`.

Closing a review is the reverse lever, and it is deliberately only half reversible. The manager supplies a `findings_note`, and the report must be in `OPEN`, otherwise `ERR_TRANSITION_NOT_ALLOWED`. Closure writes the note, sets `closed_at` to the logical clock, carries `review_status` to `CLOSED`, and advances the contractor's `version` by 1. If the severity was `RECORDABLE`, `LOST_TIME` or `FATALITY`, `open_severe_incident_count` falls by 1 and `available_aggregate` is re-materialized on every certificate of the contractor, which can restore adequacy that the filing had destroyed. The site's `access_gate_status` is recomputed, which can lift a lock or a restriction. And `incident_review_flag` is recomputed: if no other open lost-time or fatal report remains and the flag therefore flips from true to false, every `SUSPENDED` prequalification of that contractor is carried back to `UNDER_REVIEW`, processed in ascending `created_at` order, with `grade`, `final_composite_score`, `effective_thresholds`, `deficiency_codes`, `conditional_expires_at` and `expires_at` all cleared, followed by a recomputation of `open_deficiency_count`.

What closure does **not** do is give the badges back. Grants revoked by the suspension stay revoked, and their cooldowns keep running. Restoring a worker to a site therefore requires a fresh grading — the row is back in review with its grade wiped, so it must be graded again — and then a fresh grant request that clears the whole gate afresh. That asymmetry is intentional: the cascade is lossy by design, because a crew that was pulled off a site after a fatality should re-enter through the front door. Note also that a closed report keeps participating in the client-scoped severity snapshot of threshold resolution for the full 365 days after it occurred, so closing a review does not erase the tier escalation it caused. The lookback window, however, governs only that snapshot: the review flag and the site lock depend purely on whether a review is still open, no matter how old the event is. Incident reports can be listed filtered by any combination of `contractor_id`, `site_id`, `review_status` and `severity`.

### 3.10. Subcontractor Cascades: Flowing Requirements Down the Tiers

A prime contractor rarely works alone, and a client's requirements must reach the crews the prime hires. That is expressed as a directed edge from a parent contractor to a child contractor, hung off the prime's prequalification so that the client's requirements have a definite owner. A `ContractorAdmin` creates one by naming the `parent_contractor_id`, the `child_contractor_id`, the `prequalification_id` of the prime standing whose requirements cascade down the edge, and a `scope_of_work` array naming the trades being subcontracted on that edge. That array must be non-empty, drawn from the same six trade codes, and must be a subset of the child contractor's own `scope_of_work`; a trade the child is not registered for is `ERR_INVALID_FIELD_VALUE`. An edge opens at `status` `ACTIVE`; `ACTIVE` and `TERMINATED` are its only positions and `TERMINATED` is terminal.

Five checks guard edge creation, in precedence order. The acting contractor must be `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_CONTRACTOR_NOT_ACTIVE`. The prime prequalification must be in `APPROVED` or `CONDITIONAL`, else `ERR_PREQUAL_NOT_ACTIVE`. The edge set must remain acyclic and connected, else `ERR_SUBCONTRACTOR_CYCLE`. The computed depth must stay within the client's `cascade_depth_limit`, else `ERR_CASCADE_DEPTH_EXCEEDED`. And the child must carry adequate flow-down coverage, else `ERR_COVERAGE_LIMIT_INSUFFICIENT`. Because acyclicity is checked before coverage, a proposed edge that is both cyclic and under-insured reports the cycle code, never the coverage code.

Acyclicity is judged over the `ACTIVE` edges of that one prequalification; terminated edges leave both the cycle graph and the depth chain entirely. Three conditions fail the check. A contractor may not point at itself, so the parent and child references must differ. The parent must not be reachable by following edges forward from the child, because such a walk from child back to parent would close a loop. And the parent must be connected to the prime: either the parent is the prequalification's own contractor, or some `ACTIVE` edge on the same prequalification already has that parent as its child. An edge whose parent hangs off nothing is refused with the same cycle code.

Depth follows directly from that chain. An edge whose parent is the prequalification's own contractor sits at `depth` 1. Any other edge takes one more than the depth of the unique `ACTIVE` edge whose child is this edge's parent; the edge set is a forest rooted at the prime, so that parent edge is unique when it exists. The computed depth is then compared against the client's `cascade_depth_limit` — a default limit of 2 permits a prime's subcontractor and that subcontractor's own subcontractor, and refuses a third tier.

Flow-down coverage is a narrower, cheaper test than the two adequacy bases of Section 3.5: the child contractor must hold a certificate whose `coverage_type` is `GL`, whose `verification_status` is `VERIFIED`, whose freshly recomputed lifecycle position is `CURRENT` or `EXPIRING_SOON`, and whose `each_occurrence_limit` is at least 500,000 dollars. A child that satisfies nothing else still cannot be engaged without that general-liability floor.

Whether the child actually satisfies the cascaded requirements is reported by the edge's `flow_down_status`, which occupies `PENDING`, `SATISFIED` or `DEFICIENT`. It is computed when the edge is created and recomputed on every state transition of the prime prequalification, from four clauses evaluated in order with the first match winning. If the prime's own `status` is `REJECTED`, `EXPIRED` or `SUSPENDED`, the edge is `DEFICIENT` regardless of anything the child has done. Otherwise, the child's prequalifications with the **same client** as the prime are examined: if any of them is `APPROVED` or `CONDITIONAL`, the edge is `SATISFIED`; otherwise, if any of them is `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `UNDER_REVIEW`, the edge is `PENDING`; otherwise the edge is `DEFICIENT`. A child with no relationship at all with the prime's client is therefore `DEFICIENT`, exactly like a child whose only application was refused.

Termination is the only way to shrink a cascade, and it is available to either the contractor's admin or the client's safety manager. The edge must be `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_TRANSITION_NOT_ALLOWED`, and no `ACTIVE` edge on the same prequalification may still hang off this edge's child — a middle tier cannot be cut out from under a tier below it, and attempting to do so is also `ERR_TRANSITION_NOT_ALLOWED`. Terminating carries the edge to `TERMINATED`, and every `ACTIVE` descendant edge reachable through it on the same prequalification is carried to `TERMINATED` as well, processed in ascending `depth` order and then ascending `created_at` order. Because a terminated edge no longer participates in the acyclicity or depth computations, an edge that was previously refused for depth or for closing a loop may become legal once the intervening edges are cut. Edges can be listed filtered by any combination of `parent_contractor_id`, `child_contractor_id`, `prequalification_id` and `status`.

### 3.11. The Two Periodic Runs

Because setting the logical clock deliberately sweeps nothing, a client reconciles its own population by executing a **requalification run**. It takes no request body, is performed by that client's `ClientSafetyManager`, and reports six counters: `expired_count` with the matching `expired_prequalification_ids` array, `conditional_rejected_count` with the matching `conditional_rejected_prequalification_ids` array, `overdue_plan_count`, and `lapsed_certificate_count`. Its four steps run in a fixed sequence and the sequence carries meaning.

The first step enforces cure deadlines. Every prequalification of this client whose `status` is `CONDITIONAL` and whose `conditional_expires_at` is at or before the logical clock, and on which no improvement plan has reached `ACCEPTED`, is carried to `REJECTED`. Rows are processed in ascending `conditional_expires_at` order and then ascending `created_at` order. The mechanism is the plan, not the prequalification: every improvement plan on the row whose `status` is `OPEN` or `SUBMITTED` is carried to `OVERDUE` with `closed_at` set to the logical clock, and that plan closure is what drags the row into `REJECTED`. The row's `deficiency_codes` are preserved on refusal — the record of what went uncured survives — while `expires_at` is nulled. Every site access grant on the row still in `GRANTED` is revoked with `revocation_reason` `PREQUAL_REJECTED`, decrementing each site's `granted_worker_count` and recomputing each site's `access_gate_status`, and the contractor's `open_deficiency_count` and the row's edge `flow_down_status` values are recomputed.

The second step enforces annual expiry. Every prequalification of this client whose `status` is `APPROVED`, `CONDITIONAL` or `SUSPENDED` and whose `expires_at` is at or before the logical clock is carried to `EXPIRED`, processed in ascending `expires_at` order and then ascending `created_at` order, with the same revocation-and-recount chain except that the reason recorded on each revoked grant is `PREQUAL_EXPIRED` and the recomputed `flow_down_status` of every `ACTIVE` edge on the row necessarily becomes `DEFICIENT`. A row expiring out of `CONDITIONAL` additionally recomputes the contractor's `open_deficiency_count`, because its codes stop counting once it leaves `CONDITIONAL`; a row expiring out of `SUSPENDED` has no grants left to revoke, since suspension already took them. In the same sweep, any site access grant still in `GRANTED` whose `expires_at` now lies strictly before the logical clock is carried to `EXPIRED`, decrementing its site's `granted_worker_count` and recomputing that site's gate posture without opening a cooldown.

Step one strictly precedes step two, and that ordering is the point. A conditional row whose cure deadline and whose annual expiry have both elapsed lands in `REJECTED`, not `EXPIRED`; running the steps the other way round would quietly forgive an uncured conditional by retiring it as merely stale.

The third step materializes overdue plans that were not caught by step one: every improvement plan belonging to this client whose `status` is `OPEN` or `SUBMITTED` and whose `due_date` is at or before the logical clock is written as `OVERDUE`. The fourth step refreshes insurance: `lifecycle_status` and `available_aggregate` are re-materialized on every insurance certificate held by every contractor that holds a prequalification with this client, and each such contractor's `verified_coverage_types` is recomputed. This run is the only writer that materializes clock-driven expiry, and it is idempotent by construction — executed a second time at the same logical clock, it reports zeros across all six counters.

The second periodic operation is an **audit-sampling run**, likewise body-less and likewise the client manager's own. It reports `selected_count` and `selected_prequalification_ids`. The eligible pool is every prequalification of this client whose `status` is `APPROVED` or `CONDITIONAL` and whose `audit_state` is `NOT_SELECTED`; rows already `SELECTED`, `PASSED` or `FAILED` are never drawn again, and rows outside active standing are simply not eligible rather than being an error. The pool is ranked by `final_composite_score` ascending, so the weakest scores are audited first, with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break — never the identifier, which is random. The draw size is the client's `audit_sample_rate_pct` expressed as a fraction, multiplied by the number of eligible rows, rounded **up** to the next whole row: at the default rate of 25 percent, a pool of five rows yields two draws, and a pool of one row yields one. The first that many rows in ranked order are drawn, each moving from `NOT_SELECTED` to `SELECTED` with `audit_selected_at` set to the logical clock, and `selected_prequalification_ids` is emitted in that same ranked order. An empty eligible pool is a perfectly normal result: the run reports a `selected_count` of 0 and an empty array with a 200 response, never an error.
