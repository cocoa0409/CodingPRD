# Medical Device Vendor Credentialing & OR Access API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Every implant that reaches an operating table arrives in the company of a vendor representative, a tray of loaner instruments, and a paper trail that a hospital is legally obliged to be able to reconstruct. Today that paper trail is assembled by hand: a credentialing clerk keeps a spreadsheet of influenza shots and background checks, an OR coordinator keeps a separate list of who is expected in which room, sterile processing keeps a whiteboard of which trays landed late, and the value analysis committee keeps minutes in a shared drive. Nothing reconciles. A representative whose bloodborne-pathogens training lapsed on Tuesday still badges in on Wednesday, and nobody discovers the gap until an auditor asks.

This document specifies the Medical Device Vendor Credentialing & OR Access API, a single system of record that closes those gaps by construction. All resources are mounted under the `/vendor-access` prefix. The API governs the full arc of vendor presence in a hospital: enrolling a vendor company and its representatives, collecting and verifying the four mandatory attestations, monitoring credential expiry and automatically suspending site access when an attestation lapses, issuing kiosk badges that are valid only inside a window derived from a specific scheduled case, enforcing department escort rules on restricted cases, gating implants behind clinical endorsement and value analysis approval, holding loaner instrument deliveries to a deadline derived from sterile processing turnaround, assessing and occasionally reversing late-delivery fees, adjudicating incidents of unapproved product use, scoring vendors on two deliberately different bases, and hearing appeals against access revocations.

Two design commitments run through the whole specification and should be read as constraints on every later section. First, **time is explicit**: the server keeps a logical clock that test harnesses and operators set directly, and every deadline, expiry, badge window and sweep decision reads that clock rather than wall-clock time. Second, **derived state is never stale**: coverage postures, readiness postures, credential postures, quota counters, fee ledgers and both compliance scores are recomputed as a side effect of the operations that disturb them, in the pinned order this document specifies, so that a client which reads a resource immediately after a write sees a fully reconciled picture.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Authentication is header-based. Every request must carry `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which that principal is acting. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not exactly one of the eight role names below, the request is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If both headers are well-formed but the declared role is not among the roles the target operation admits, the request is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The eight recognised roles are `VendorAdmin`, `VendorRepUser`, `CredentialingOfficer`, `ORCoordinator`, `ClinicalSponsor`, `ValueAnalysisApprover`, `SterileProcessingTech` and `ComplianceAdmin`.

Their responsibilities divide as follows. A `VendorAdmin` is a vendor company administrator: it registers its own representatives, attests their credentials, opens and submits product introduction requests, registers loaner tray sets and files revocation appeals. A `VendorRepUser` is an individual representative who uploads its own attestations, badges in and out at the kiosk, and may file its own appeal. A `CredentialingOfficer` staffs the hospital credentialing office, verifying or rejecting attestations, revoking site access, and deciding appeals it did not itself cause. An `ORCoordinator` owns the case schedule, decides representative authorizations, attaches escorts and reports incidents. A `ClinicalSponsor` is the surgeon or clinical champion who endorses or rejects a product introduction request. A `ValueAnalysisApprover` sits on the value analysis committee and approves, rejects or returns a request for changes. A `SterileProcessingTech` receives and releases loaner tray sets and reports processing incidents. A `ComplianceAdmin` administers the vendor compliance programme: it investigates and adjudicates incidents, decides appeals, and runs the compliance sweep.

Two of these roles are backed by persisted entities, and the remaining six are not. When `X-User-Role` is `VendorAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Vendor`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Vendor` compare the resource's vendor foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `VendorRepUser`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `VendorRep`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `VendorRep` compare the resource's representative foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `CredentialingOfficer`, `ORCoordinator`, `ClinicalSponsor`, `ValueAnalysisApprover`, `SterileProcessingTech` or `ComplianceAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity: the server records it on the records those operators touch — as `verified_by_user_id` on a `Credential`, as `revoked_by_user_id` on a `VendorRep`, as `requested_by_user_id`, `decided_by_user_id` or `escort_user_id` on a `CaseRepAuthorization`, as `clinical_sponsor_user_id` or `value_analysis_user_id` on a product introduction request, as `escort_user_id` on a kiosk check-in, as `reported_by_user_id` or `adjudicated_by_user_id` on an `Incident`, and as `decided_by_user_id` on an `AccessAppeal` — but it never compares that identifier to any entity's `id`.

Because the two entity-backed roles are scoped to their own rows, a stricter check applies to them. A `VendorAdmin` may act only on rows belonging to its own vendor, meaning `X-User-ID` must equal the `Vendor` identifier reached through the target resource's `vendor_id`; a `VendorRepUser` may act only on its own representative rows, meaning `X-User-ID` must equal the `VendorRep` identifier. A violation is refused with `ERR_VENDOR_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). This scope check guards exactly seven operations: opening a product introduction request, submitting one, requesting a case authorization, checking in at the kiosk, checking out at the kiosk, filing an access appeal, and reading a vendor scorecard. A `VendorAdmin` reading another vendor's scorecard therefore receives `ERR_VENDOR_SCOPE_VIOLATION`, whereas a `CredentialingOfficer` or a `ComplianceAdmin` may read any vendor's scorecard. Every other read endpoint in the system is open to all eight roles.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

---

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint is paginated through two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of rows returned; when the caller omits it the server uses 10, and a supplied value must be at least 1 and at most 100. `offset` is the number of rows to skip before the page begins; when omitted it is 0, and a supplied value must not be negative. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request rather than an empty page.

Unless a particular endpoint says otherwise, list results are ordered by the creation timestamp of each row, ascending. List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` indicates whether further rows follow the current page.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock

Nothing in this system reads the host machine's wall clock. Instead the server keeps a single **logical current UTC instant**, and every temporal decision — a credential's expiry, a badge window's admissibility, a loaner delivery deadline comparison, an appeal filing window, a sweep's observation point — is evaluated against it. Before any caller sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; that is the startup default, and the server never falls back to real time if the clock has not been set.

Any of the eight roles may advance or rewind the clock through an operator capability that accepts a single `current_utc` instant and echoes it back. Setting the clock performs no state transitions of its own: it neither expires credentials nor voids badges. Those consequences are realised only when a later business call or a compliance sweep observes the new instant.

### 2.5. Common Resource Properties

Almost every resource in the system carries four system-managed properties.

- **`id`** (string): a server-minted UUID, with no type prefix and no embedded meaning. Clients never supply it.
- **`created_at`** (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, recording when the row was first written.
- **`updated_at`** (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, rewritten on every mutation of the row, including mutations that only touch derived fields such as a coverage posture, a counter or a compliance score.
- **`version`** (integer): a system-managed integer that increments on every mutation of the row.

The `Facility`, `Vendor`, `VendorRep`, `Credential`, `SurgicalCase`, `CaseRepAuthorization`, `ProductIntroductionRequest`, `ApprovedProduct`, `LoanerSet`, `KioskCheckIn`, `Incident` and `AccessAppeal` resources all carry the full set of four. The `ComplianceSweep` record is the single exception: it is an immutable event log entry, so it carries only `id` and `created_at`. Both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from a sweep record entirely — a client must not expect either field there.

All datetime-valued fields anywhere in this specification, whether supplied by the caller or computed by the server, are ISO 8601 instants in UTC. Server-written instants are emitted with three fractional digits.

### 2.6. Quantities, Money, Minutes and Rounding

Money in this domain is always an integer number of **cents**; there are no fractional-currency fields and no decimal money strings. The two monetary fields a client will meet are `unit_cost_cents` on a product introduction request and its approved copy, and the late-delivery amounts `assessed_late_fee_cents` on a loaner set and `late_fee_balance_cents` on a vendor.

Wherever a rounding step is required, the method is **round half up**, and the number of decimal places is stated at the point of use. Two rounding steps exist in the whole system, and both are load-bearing. The per-tray late charge is rounded half up to a whole number of cents *before* it is multiplied by the tray count, never after; §3.7 sets out that order in full. The vendor utilization ratio is rounded half up to four decimal places; §3.9 sets out where it is taken.

Elapsed lateness is measured in **whole minutes, truncated toward zero**: a delivery that arrives fifty-nine seconds past its deadline is zero minutes late. Delivery deadlines are produced by subtracting a whole number of hours from a basis instant. Badge windows are produced by shifting a case instant by a whole number of minutes, and instants are compared at second resolution. Compliance scores are pure integer arithmetic.

### 2.7. Version Pinning and Optimistic Concurrency

Two write operations in this system author a child record against a snapshot of a parent's state, and both are therefore protected by optimistic concurrency control on that parent's `version`.

Opening a product introduction request requires the non-optional field `expected_vendor_version` in the request body, carrying the `version` of the `Vendor` that the caller last read. If that value does not equal the vendor's current `version`, the create is refused with `ERR_STALE_VENDOR_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The reason is that a request is authored against the vendor's compliance posture as the caller saw it — its `compliance_status`, its `late_fee_balance_cents`, its `open_pir_count` — and any intervening write that moved the vendor forward invalidates that reading: a substantiated incident that rescored it, a late loaner fee added to its balance, a completed sweep, or an earlier request that this one supersedes. A successful create itself increments the vendor's `version` by one, so a caller submitting two requests in a row must re-read the vendor in between.

Registering a loaner instrument set requires the non-optional field `expected_case_version` in the request body, carrying the `version` of the `SurgicalCase` that the caller last read. If that value does not equal the case's current `version`, the registration is refused with `ERR_STALE_CASE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Registration pins both delivery deadline variants off the case schedule, so a set registered against a pre-reschedule reading of the case would pin the wrong deadline. A reschedule increments the case `version`, and so does a successful registration, which means registering a second tray set for the same case requires re-reading the case first.

No other endpoint takes a version field. Approving an authorization, verifying a credential, adjudicating an incident, deciding an appeal, receiving or releasing a tray set, rescheduling or cancelling a case, and every review step on a product introduction request are all executed without an echoed version, and it is an error for an implementation to demand one.

### 2.8. Cascade Propagation and Derived-Field Recomputation

The graph of entities here has no single ownership tree: a case and a representative meet through an authorization, a loaner set spans a case and an approved product, and an incident spans a representative and a product. As a result a single decision routinely mutates several aggregates, and this specification treats those secondary mutations as part of the operation's contract rather than as eventual housekeeping. Every cascade named in §3 is immediate and observable on the very next read.

Three rules govern cascades everywhere they appear. First, whenever a cascade rewrites a row, that row's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` is incremented, exactly as a direct write would do — a `SurgicalCase` whose `approved_rep_count` moved because someone revoked an authorization has been mutated. Second, when a cascade fans out over a set of sibling rows, they are processed in ascending order of their creation timestamp; this matters for counters and for badge serial assignment, and it is stated again at each site. Third, counters that are decremented never go below zero: `authorizations_used_epoch`, `open_authorization_count` and `approved_rep_count` are each floored at 0 when a revocation decrements them.

Derived fields are recomputed from their inputs rather than incrementally patched. `credential_posture` is recomputed from the representative's whole credential collection, `vendor_coverage_status` and `implant_readiness_status` from the case's whole authorization and loaner-set collections, and both vendor compliance scores from the vendor's whole incident and loaner-set history. Where an ordering between recomputations is observable, this document pins it.

### 2.9. Path Resolution and the Validation Ladder

When a URL contains more than one resource identifier, identifiers are resolved strictly left to right, and the first identifier that does not name an existing row determines the 404 that is returned — the server does not look further right. Foreign keys supplied in a request body are resolved in the order in which the endpoint's input list declares them, so a body naming both a missing case and a missing product reports the case.

Many operations carry several checks that a single malformed or ill-timed request could trip at once. The order in which those checks are evaluated is normative, because it determines which error the caller sees. The ladder below is evaluated top to bottom, and the first failing rung produces the response.

1. **Authentication** — `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` are both present and the role is one of the eight declared names, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Authorization** — the authenticated role appears in the target endpoint's role list, else `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Request well-formedness** — every required body field is present, JSON types are correct, enum values fall inside their declared domain, integers fall inside their declared ranges, ISO-8601 UTC instants parse, `limit` is between 1 and 100, and `offset` is not negative; otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Applies to every endpoint.
4. **Instant ordering** — any pair of instants supplied together must be correctly ordered, else `ERR_INVALID_TIME_RANGE` (400). This guards three operations: scheduling a surgical case and rescheduling one, where the end must be strictly after the start, and attesting a credential, where `expires_at_utc` must be strictly after `issued_at_utc`.
5. **Path existence** — every resource named in the path exists, validated left to right, else the corresponding 404: `ERR_FACILITY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VENDOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VENDOR_REP_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CREDENTIAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SURGICAL_CASE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PIR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPROVED_PRODUCT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LOANER_SET_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CHECK_IN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_APPEAL_NOT_FOUND`. Applies to every endpoint that has a path parameter.
6. **Body foreign-key existence** — every foreign key in the request body references an existing row, validated in the endpoint's declared field order, else `ERR_FACILITY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VENDOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VENDOR_REP_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SURGICAL_CASE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PIR_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_APPROVED_PRODUCT_NOT_FOUND` as appropriate. Applies to every creation endpoint that carries a foreign key in its body, and a `supersedes_request_id` naming a request that does not exist yields `ERR_PIR_NOT_FOUND`.
7. **Vendor scoping** — the entity-backed role restriction of §2.1, else `ERR_VENDOR_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (403), on the seven operations named there.
8. **Vendor version match** — `expected_vendor_version` equals the current vendor `version`, else `ERR_STALE_VENDOR_VERSION` (412). Applies to opening a product introduction request.
9. **Case version match** — `expected_case_version` equals the current case `version`, else `ERR_STALE_CASE_VERSION` (412). Applies to registering a loaner set.
10. **Representative not revoked** — the acting or referenced representative's `access_status` is not `REVOKED`, else `ERR_REP_ACCESS_REVOKED` (409). Applies to four operations: requesting a case authorization, kiosk check-in, kiosk check-out, and attesting a credential.
11. **Representative not suspended** — the referenced representative's `access_status` is not `SUSPENDED`, else `ERR_REP_ACCESS_SUSPENDED` (409). Applies to two operations: deciding a case authorization and kiosk check-in.
12. **Credential pending** — the target credential is in `PENDING_VERIFICATION`, else `ERR_CREDENTIAL_NOT_PENDING` (409). Applies to the credential verify/reject decision.
13. **Authorization requested** — the target authorization is in `REQUESTED`, else `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_NOT_REQUESTED` (409). Applies to the authorization approve/deny decision.
14. **Authorization approved** — the target authorization is in `APPROVED`, else `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_NOT_APPROVED` (409). Applies to two operations: revoking an authorization and kiosk check-in.
15. **Case schedulable** — the case is in `SCHEDULED` or `RESCHEDULED`, else `ERR_CASE_NOT_SCHEDULED` (409). Applies to five operations: rescheduling a case, cancelling a case, requesting a case authorization, registering a loaner set and receiving a loaner set.
16. **Review-step state** — the product introduction request is in the state the requested step needs, else `ERR_PIR_INVALID_STATE` (409): `DRAFT` or `CHANGES_REQUESTED` for a submission, `CLINICAL_REVIEW` for a clinical review decision, `VALUE_ANALYSIS_REVIEW` for a value-analysis decision. Applies to those three operations.
17. **Loaner-set state** — receiving requires `EXPECTED`, and releasing requires `DELIVERED_ON_TIME` or `DELIVERED_LATE`, else `ERR_LOANER_SET_INVALID_STATE` (409). Applies to those two operations, so a `HELD` or `REJECTED_LATE` set cannot be released.
18. **Badge active** — the target check-in's `badge_status` is `ISSUED`, else `ERR_CHECK_IN_NOT_ACTIVE` (409). Applies to two operations: attaching an escort and checking out.
19. **Escort pending** — the target check-in's `escort_status` is `ESCORT_PENDING`, else `ERR_ESCORT_NOT_PENDING` (409). Applies to attaching an escort, so a check-in that is already attached, already released, or never required an escort refuses the attach.
20. **Incident state** — investigating requires `REPORTED`; adjudicating requires `REPORTED` or `UNDER_INVESTIGATION` for any outcome, and additionally admits `SUBSTANTIATED` when and only when the requested outcome is `UNSUBSTANTIATED`. A `CLOSED` incident, an already `UNSUBSTANTIATED` incident, and a second `SUBSTANTIATED` outcome are all refused with `ERR_INCIDENT_INVALID_STATE` (409). Applies to those two operations.
21. **Appeal state** — the target appeal is in `SUBMITTED`, else `ERR_APPEAL_INVALID_STATE` (409). Applies to deciding an appeal.
22. **Representative revoked** — filing an appeal requires the representative's `access_status` to be `REVOKED`, else `ERR_REP_NOT_REVOKED` (409).
23. **Segregation of duties** — the acting `X-User-ID` differs from the counterparty recorded on the same record, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). This rung guards four operations, described in §2.10.
24. **Credential posture** — the representative's recomputed `credential_posture` is `COMPLIANT` or `EXPIRING_SOON`, and is neither `INCOMPLETE` nor `LAPSED`, else `ERR_REP_CREDENTIALS_NOT_COMPLIANT` (422). Applies to two operations: deciding a case authorization and kiosk check-in.
25. **Escort custody** — when the authorization's `escort_required` is true, an escort must already be attached before the badge may be checked out, else `ERR_ESCORT_REQUIRED` (422). Applies to kiosk check-out.
26. **Badge window open** — the logical clock lies inside the authorization's badge window, both endpoints inclusive, else `ERR_BADGE_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN` (422). Applies to kiosk check-in.
27. **Appeal window open** — the appeal is filed no later than the deadline derived in §3.9, else `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_CLOSED` (422). Applies to filing an appeal.
28. **Badge window conflict** — the representative has no other `APPROVED` authorization whose badge window overlaps the one this approval would create, else `ERR_REP_BADGE_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409). Applies to deciding a case authorization.
29. **Bay capacity** — the number of existing loaner sets at the facility whose buffered sterile-processing occupation collides with the candidate's occupation is strictly fewer than the facility's `spd_bay_count`, else `ERR_SPD_BAY_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409). Applies to registering a loaner set.
30. **Duplicate authorization** — no authorization already exists for the same case-and-representative pair in `REQUESTED` or `APPROVED`, else `ERR_DUPLICATE_AUTHORIZATION` (409). Applies to requesting a case authorization.
31. **Duplicate credential** — no credential of the same type already exists for the representative in `PENDING_VERIFICATION`, `VERIFIED` or `EXPIRING_SOON`, else `ERR_DUPLICATE_CREDENTIAL` (409). Applies to attesting a credential.
32. **Natural-key uniqueness** — a facility `code`, a `vendor_code` and a `case_number` must each be unused, else `ERR_DUPLICATE_FACILITY_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_VENDOR_CODE` or `ERR_DUPLICATE_CASE_NUMBER` (409). Applies to registering a facility, enrolling a vendor and scheduling a case respectively.
33. **One live badge** — the authorization has no other check-in whose `badge_status` is `ISSUED`, else `ERR_CHECK_IN_ALREADY_ACTIVE` (409). Applies to kiosk check-in.
34. **One appeal per revocation** — the representative has no other appeal whose `filed_for_revoked_at_utc` equals the representative's current `revoked_at_utc`, else `ERR_APPEAL_ALREADY_FILED` (409). Applies to filing an appeal.
35. **Revision bound** — the request's `revision_round` is strictly below the two-round maximum before a change request or a re-submission is accepted, else `ERR_PIR_REVISION_LIMIT` (422). Applies to two operations: the value-analysis decision and the submission.
36. **Product still approved** — the referenced approved product is `ACTIVE`, and is neither `SUSPENDED` nor `EXPIRED`, else `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_APPROVED` (422). Applies to two operations: registering a loaner set and releasing one.
37. **Implant backing** — when the case's `implant_required` is true, the representative's vendor owns at least one approved product that is both `ACTIVE` and flagged as an implant, else `ERR_IMPLANT_REQUIRES_APPROVED_PRODUCT` (422). Applies to deciding a case authorization.
38. **Access quota** — the vendor's `authorizations_used_epoch` is strictly below the hard cap of §3.4, else `ERR_VENDOR_ACCESS_QUOTA_EXCEEDED` (422). Applies to deciding a case authorization.
39. **Fee hold** — the vendor's `late_fee_balance_cents` is strictly below the fee hold threshold of §3.7, else `ERR_VENDOR_FEE_HOLD` (402). Applies to two operations: deciding a case authorization and releasing a loaner set.

The shape of this ladder is deliberate. Authentication and authorization precede everything; well-formedness precedes existence; existence precedes concurrency; concurrency precedes state; state precedes business judgement; and inside business judgement, patient-safety and compliance gates are evaluated before temporal gates, temporal gates before uniqueness and loop bounds, uniqueness before referential product checks, and money and quota last of all. An approval attempt on an over-quota vendor whose representative is also uncredentialed therefore reports `ERR_REP_CREDENTIALS_NOT_COMPLIANT`, not `ERR_VENDOR_ACCESS_QUOTA_EXCEEDED`.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties

Four decisions in this system must be taken by somebody other than the person who set them up, and in all four cases the comparison is on `X-User-ID` equality rather than on role names — two different people holding the same role satisfy the rule, and one person holding two roles does not.

The value-analysis decision on a product introduction request may not be cast by the user recorded as that request's `clinical_sponsor_user_id`. The adjudication of an incident may not be performed by the user recorded as its `reported_by_user_id`. The decision on an access appeal may not be taken by the user recorded as the representative's `revoked_by_user_id` — the officer who revoked access does not get to rule on the appeal against it. And the approve-or-deny decision on a case authorization may not be taken by the user recorded as its `requested_by_user_id`, which is why an `ORCoordinator` who opens an authorization request must hand the decision to a colleague. All four violations surface as `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Facilities, Vendor Enrolment and the Representative Roster

Before anything else can happen, somebody from the credentialing office or the compliance programme registers the hospital sites. A `Facility` is described by its display `name` and its short `code` — one to sixteen characters drawn from uppercase letters, digits and hyphens, unique across all facilities, and doubling as the prefix of every badge serial the site's kiosk ever prints. Alongside the identity fields, the facility carries the four operational parameters that later formulas read. `spd_turnaround_hours` is the sterile processing department's base reprocessing turnaround expressed in whole hours and may be zero but not negative; it is a term of the loaner delivery lead calculation. `spd_bay_count` is the number of reprocessing bays and must be at least one; it is the aggregate ceiling on how many loaner-set occupations may overlap at the site. `badge_lead_minutes` is how many minutes before a case's scheduled start a kiosk badge becomes valid, defaulting to 60 when the caller omits it and never negative. `badge_grace_minutes` is how many minutes after a case's scheduled end a badge stops being valid, defaulting to 30 and likewise never negative. Finally, the boolean `restricted_escort_required` defaults to true and declares that on this site an approved authorization for a restricted case must name an escort.

Vendors are enrolled next. A `Vendor` needs only a `legal_name` and a `vendor_code` unique across all vendor rows, but a great deal of derived state comes into existence with it, and every one of those fields has a mandated starting value: `provisional_compliance_score` and `adjudicated_compliance_score` both start at 100, `scorecard_tier` starts at `TIER_PLATINUM`, `compliance_status` starts at `IN_GOOD_STANDING`, `access_quota_in_effect` starts at the baseline quota of four approved authorizations per epoch, `authorizations_used_epoch` starts at 0, `late_fee_balance_cents` starts at 0, `open_pir_count` starts at 0, and `last_sweep_epoch` starts at 0 because no sweep has yet observed this vendor. The `scorecard_tier` domain is `TIER_PLATINUM`, `TIER_STANDARD` and `TIER_PROBATION`; the `compliance_status` domain is `IN_GOOD_STANDING`, `WATCH`, `PROBATION` and `BARRED`. Both scores are integers constrained to the range 0 through 100, and `access_quota_in_effect` never drops below 1.

A vendor company then builds out its roster. Registering a `VendorRep` takes the employing `vendor_id`, which is immutable once written, the representative's `full_name`, and an `email` that must be unique across every representative in the system. A freshly registered representative is inert: `access_status` is `PENDING_CREDENTIALING`, `credential_posture` is `INCOMPLETE`, `ever_credentialed` is false, `open_authorization_count` is 0, `suspension_reason` is `NONE`, and the three audit instants `revoked_by_user_id`, `revoked_at_utc` and `reinstated_at_utc` are all absent. The `access_status` domain is `PENDING_CREDENTIALING`, `CREDENTIALED`, `SUSPENDED` and `REVOKED`, and none of those four is terminal — this lifecycle cycles, as §3.2 and §3.9 describe. The `suspension_reason` domain is `NONE`, `CREDENTIAL_LAPSED` and `INCIDENT_SUBSTANTIATED`, and it exists purely to record *why* a representative sits in `SUSPENDED`, because the two reasons are cleared by different remedies.

The roster is browsable. A representative listing may be narrowed by employing vendor through `vendor_id`, by lifecycle state through `access_status`, and by attestation posture through `credential_posture`, in addition to the usual `limit` and `offset`. Each representative also exposes two sub-resource listings — its credentials, narrowable by `credential_type` and `status`, and its revocation appeals, narrowable by `status` — which together are how a client discovers rows that are only ever created as children.

### 3.2. Attestation, Verification and Credential Posture

A `Credential` is a single attestation about one representative. It is authored with the `vendor_rep_id` it attests for, a `credential_type`, the `issued_at_utc` instant at which the attestation was made or the document issued, the `expires_at_utc` instant at which it lapses, and a `document_reference` — an opaque locator the vendor supplies and the hospital never interprets. The expiry must fall strictly after the issue instant. Six credential types exist: `IMMUNIZATION_INFLUENZA`, `IMMUNIZATION_MMR`, `TRAINING_BLOODBORNE_PATHOGENS`, `TRAINING_OR_PROTOCOL`, `BACKGROUND_CHECK` and `COI_GIFT_ATTESTATION`, the last of which is the combined conflict-of-interest and gift policy attestation. **Only four of the six gate site access**: `IMMUNIZATION_INFLUENZA`, `TRAINING_BLOODBORNE_PATHOGENS`, `BACKGROUND_CHECK` and `COI_GIFT_ATTESTATION`. `IMMUNIZATION_MMR` and `TRAINING_OR_PROTOCOL` are optional extras that a vendor may file and the hospital may read, but they never influence posture in either direction.

A newly filed credential lands in `PENDING_VERIFICATION`, and filing it changes nothing about the representative: a pending attestation covers nothing, so `credential_posture` is left exactly as it was. Duplicate filing is refused — a representative may not hold a second credential of the same `credential_type` while an existing one is in `PENDING_VERIFICATION`, `VERIFIED` or `EXPIRING_SOON` — but once an old credential has gone `EXPIRED` or `REJECTED`, a replacement of the same type is accepted. The full `status` domain for a credential is `PENDING_VERIFICATION`, `VERIFIED`, `EXPIRING_SOON`, `EXPIRED` and `REJECTED`, and `REJECTED` is terminal.

A `CredentialingOfficer` then rules on the attestation, supplying a `decision` of either `VERIFY` or `REJECT` together with an optional `rejection_reason`. A verification moves the credential `PENDING_VERIFICATION → VERIFIED` and stamps `verified_by_user_id` with the officer's `X-User-ID` and `verified_at_utc` with the logical clock's current instant. A rejection moves it `PENDING_VERIFICATION → REJECTED` and records the supplied `rejection_reason` as free text. Both outcomes then force a recomputation of the owning representative's `credential_posture`, because a rejected credential never covers its type any more than a pending one does.

**Credential posture** is a pure derivation over the representative's whole credential collection, and it is the hinge on which site access turns. Collect the set of credential types held by credentials of that representative whose `status` is either `VERIFIED` or `EXPIRING_SOON`; these are the *covered* types. Credentials in `PENDING_VERIFICATION`, `REJECTED` or `EXPIRED` never contribute to that set. Now compare the covered set against the four gating types. If even one of the four gating types is not covered, the posture is `LAPSED` when the representative's `ever_credentialed` flag is true, and `INCOMPLETE` when it is false. If all four gating types are covered and at least one credential *of a gating type* is in `EXPIRING_SOON`, the posture is `EXPIRING_SOON`. If all four gating types are covered and no gating-type credential is in `EXPIRING_SOON`, the posture is `COMPLIANT`. Two consequences of this ordering deserve spelling out. A type covered by two credentials at once — say one `VERIFIED` and one `EXPIRING_SOON` — still counts as covered, and the `EXPIRING_SOON` credential still forces the whole posture to `EXPIRING_SOON`. And a credential of a non-gating type sitting in `EXPIRING_SOON` does not drag the posture down, because the warning test looks only at the four gating types. The derivation is exact set arithmetic; nothing here is rounded.

The `ever_credentialed` flag is what lets the system tell a representative who has never been cleared from one who has fallen out of compliance. It is set true the first time the recomputed posture reaches `COMPLIANT` or `EXPIRING_SOON`, and once true it never returns to false, so from that moment on a missing gating type reads as `LAPSED` rather than `INCOMPLETE` forever after.

Verification is also the ordinary route into site access. When a verification leaves the representative with a posture of `COMPLIANT` or `EXPIRING_SOON`, `access_status` advances `PENDING_CREDENTIALING → CREDENTIALED`, `ever_credentialed` is set true, and `suspension_reason` is set to `NONE`. The same verification, applied to a representative already sitting in `SUSPENDED`, moves it `SUSPENDED → CREDENTIALED` and stamps `reinstated_at_utc` with the logical clock's current instant — but **only** when the existing `suspension_reason` is `CREDENTIAL_LAPSED`. A suspension whose reason is `INCIDENT_SUBSTANTIATED` is not cleared by credential work, however many replacement attestations the vendor files; that suspension is lifted only by the routes described in §3.8 and §3.9. Reinstatement is also deliberately non-restorative: returning to `CREDENTIALED` does not resurrect any authorization that was revoked during the suspension. Those authorizations stay `REVOKED`.

Revocation is the credentialing office's blunt instrument, available to a `CredentialingOfficer` or a `ComplianceAdmin` and taking a single `reason` in the body. It is admissible from `PENDING_CREDENTIALING`, from `CREDENTIALED` and from `SUSPENDED` — the only precondition is that the representative is not already `REVOKED` — and in every case it writes `revoked_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID` and `revoked_at_utc` from the logical clock. Those two fields matter later: `revoked_by_user_id` is the segregation-of-duties key that bars the revoking officer from deciding the appeal, and `revoked_at_utc` anchors the appeal window. Revoking a representative that was `CREDENTIALED` additionally triggers the access cascade of §3.4, because such a representative may be holding live authorizations; revocation from `PENDING_CREDENTIALING` or from `SUSPENDED` carries no cascade of its own, since the cascade has either never been possible or has already run.

### 3.3. The Case Schedule and the Ripples a Reschedule Sends

An `ORCoordinator` owns the surgical schedule. Scheduling a `SurgicalCase` takes the hosting `facility_id`, which is immutable thereafter, a `case_number` unique within that facility, a `procedure_name` describing the procedure, and the pair `scheduled_start_utc` and `scheduled_end_utc` where the end must fall strictly after the start. Two optional flags shape everything downstream. `restriction_level` is either `STANDARD` or `RESTRICTED` and defaults to `STANDARD`; a `RESTRICTED` case demands a named escort on every approved authorization whenever the hosting facility has `restricted_escort_required` set. `implant_required` is a boolean defaulting to false; when it is true the case needs implant support, which adds a 24-hour floor to the loaner delivery lead time and switches on both the uncovered-case gating of §3.4 and the implant-readiness gating of §3.7.

At creation the server also writes `original_scheduled_start_utc` from the supplied `scheduled_start_utc`. **That field is never rewritten again, not even by a reschedule.** It is the audit basis against which the pinned loaner deadline is computed, and its immutability is the whole point of having two deadline variants. The case's `status` starts at `SCHEDULED`, and the domain is `SCHEDULED`, `RESCHEDULED` and `CANCELLED`, the last of which is terminal. The three counters `approved_rep_count`, `revoked_authorization_count` and `reschedule_count` all start at 0. The two derived postures start according to the implant flag: `vendor_coverage_status` is `UNCOVERED` on an implant case and `NOT_REQUIRED` otherwise, while `implant_readiness_status` is `PENDING` on an implant case and `NOT_APPLICABLE` otherwise. Case listings may be narrowed by `facility_id`, by `status`, by `vendor_coverage_status` and by `implant_readiness_status`.

Rescheduling is the single most consequential operation in the system, and it is admissible only while the case is `SCHEDULED` or `RESCHEDULED`. The coordinator supplies `new_scheduled_start_utc` and `new_scheduled_end_utc`, again with the end strictly after the start. A first reschedule moves the case `SCHEDULED → RESCHEDULED`; a subsequent one is a legal self-loop `RESCHEDULED → RESCHEDULED` with an identical set of consequences, so repeated moves are permitted and each one re-derives the current basis afresh while leaving the pinned basis alone. The live `scheduled_start_utc` and `scheduled_end_utc` are overwritten, `reschedule_count` is incremented, and the case's `version` is incremented.

Everything hanging off the case is then reconciled, in the following order. For each authorization of the case that is `APPROVED`, `badge_window_start_utc` and `badge_window_end_utc` are recomputed from the new schedule. For each kiosk check-in belonging to those authorizations whose `badge_status` is still `ISSUED`, `valid_from_utc` and `valid_until_utc` are refreshed from the recomputed window, so a badge already in a representative's hand tracks the case it belongs to. For each loaner set of the case whose `status` is `EXPECTED`, `DELIVERED_ON_TIME`, `DELIVERED_LATE` or `HELD`, `required_delivery_deadline_utc` is recomputed and the sterile-processing occupation window is re-derived, while `pinned_deadline_utc` is left untouched. For each of those sets that already has a `delivered_at_utc`, `late_minutes_current_basis` is re-derived and the delivery branch is re-adjudicated: a set that has become on time flips to `DELIVERED_ON_TIME`, and if its `fee_status` was `ASSESSED` the status flips to `REVERSED` and the vendor's `late_fee_balance_cents` is debited by that set's `assessed_late_fee_cents` — with `assessed_late_fee_cents` itself retained verbatim as the historical assessment. Finally the case's `implant_readiness_status` and `vendor_coverage_status` are recomputed, and both compliance-score variants are recomputed for every vendor whose sets changed branch.

Cancellation is terminal and takes a `cancellation_reason`. It is likewise admissible only from `SCHEDULED` or `RESCHEDULED`, and the two source states produce an identical cascade. Because the case is gone, every authorization of the case that is still `REQUESTED` moves `REQUESTED → DENIED`, and every one that is `APPROVED` moves `APPROVED → REVOKED` with `revoked_reason` set to `CASE_CANCELLED`; both sweeps run over the authorizations in ascending creation order. Each approval that is revoked decrements the vendor's `authorizations_used_epoch`, decrements the representative's `open_authorization_count`, decrements the case's `approved_rep_count` and increments the case's `revoked_authorization_count`. Because those authorizations are now `REVOKED`, each of their kiosk check-ins whose `badge_status` is `ISSUED` moves to `VOIDED`. Then `vendor_coverage_status` is recomputed, which lands on `NOT_REQUIRED` or `UNCOVERED` once `approved_rep_count` has reached zero.

### 3.4. Authorizing a Representative onto a Case

A representative reaches an operating room only through a `CaseRepAuthorization`, the junction that resolves the many-to-many relationship between cases and representatives. Either a `VendorAdmin` or an `ORCoordinator` opens the request, supplying the `surgical_case_id`, the `vendor_rep_id` and a `requested_role` describing the scope of presence being asked for: `IMPLANT_SUPPORT`, `TECHNICAL_SUPPORT` or `OBSERVATION`. The request lands in `REQUESTED`, the server stamps `requested_by_user_id` with the opener's `X-User-ID`, and `escort_required`, both badge-window instants and `epoch_number` all stay empty until a decision is taken. The `status` domain is `REQUESTED`, `APPROVED`, `DENIED` and `REVOKED`, with `DENIED` and `REVOKED` both terminal. A duplicate request for a case-and-representative pair that already has one in `REQUESTED` or `APPROVED` is refused, but a pair whose previous authorization ended `DENIED` or `REVOKED` may be requested again. Authorization listings may be narrowed by `surgical_case_id`, by `vendor_rep_id` and by `status`, and each authorization exposes a sub-resource listing of its kiosk check-ins narrowable by `badge_status`.

The decision belongs exclusively to an `ORCoordinator`, who supplies a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `DENY` plus an optional `escort_user_id` naming the escort. A denial is cheap: it needs only that the authorization is `REQUESTED` and that the acting `X-User-ID` differs from `requested_by_user_id`, and it moves `REQUESTED → DENIED` while writing `decided_by_user_id` and `decided_at_utc` and moving no counters at all. An approval is where the entire safety, temporal, referential and fiscal ladder runs. Every one of the following must hold: the authorization is `REQUESTED`; the case is `SCHEDULED` or `RESCHEDULED`; the representative's `access_status` is exactly `CREDENTIALED`, so a `PENDING_CREDENTIALING`, `SUSPENDED` or `REVOKED` representative cannot be approved; the representative's recomputed `credential_posture` is `COMPLIANT` or `EXPIRING_SOON`; when the case's `implant_required` is true, the representative's employing vendor owns at least one approved product that is simultaneously `ACTIVE` and flagged `is_implant`; the vendor's `late_fee_balance_cents` is strictly below the fee hold threshold; the vendor's `authorizations_used_epoch` is strictly below the hard cap described below; the representative holds no other `APPROVED` authorization whose badge window overlaps the window this approval would create; and the deciding `X-User-ID` differs from `requested_by_user_id`.

When all of that holds, the authorization moves `REQUESTED → APPROVED`, `decided_by_user_id` takes the coordinator's `X-User-ID` and `decided_at_utc` the logical clock's current instant. The **badge validity window** is then computed and stored: `badge_window_start_utc` is the case's live `scheduled_start_utc` shifted earlier by the facility's `badge_lead_minutes` minutes, and `badge_window_end_utc` is the case's live `scheduled_end_utc` shifted later by the facility's `badge_grace_minutes` minutes. With the site defaults, that is one hour of lead before the knife and half an hour of grace after. The boolean `escort_required` is written as the conjunction of two facts observed at this moment: the case's `restriction_level` equals `RESTRICTED` **and** the facility's `restricted_escort_required` is true; if either is false, `escort_required` is false. Any supplied `escort_user_id` is stored on the authorization. The server stamps `epoch_number` with the vendor's current `last_sweep_epoch`, which scopes this row to the quota epoch it consumes. Then three counters each rise by one — the vendor's `authorizations_used_epoch`, the representative's `open_authorization_count` and the case's `approved_rep_count` — and the case's `vendor_coverage_status` is recomputed.

The **quota admission** gate deserves its own paragraph, because it is deliberately soft. The hard cap for an epoch is the vendor's `access_quota_in_effect` multiplied by one and a half, then rounded down to a whole number. An approval is refused with `ERR_VENDOR_ACCESS_QUOTA_EXCEEDED` when `authorizations_used_epoch` has already reached that cap; otherwise the approval proceeds and the counter rises by one. With the enrolment quota of four, the cap is six, so the seventh approval inside a single epoch is the first one refused. Because the cap sits above the nominal quota, `authorizations_used_epoch` can legitimately exceed `access_quota_in_effect` — and that is precisely what makes a utilization ratio above one reachable at sweep time, as §3.9 explains. Revocation decrements the counter, so a revoked authorization hands its slot back.

The **badge window conflict** test protects a representative from being promised to two theatres at once. Windows are compared as **half-open** intervals: two windows conflict when the first starts strictly before the second ends and the second starts strictly before the first ends. Adjacency is therefore legal — a window that ends at exactly the instant the next one begins does not conflict, and the approval is accepted. Only the representative's other `APPROVED` authorizations are considered, examined in ascending creation order; authorizations in `REQUESTED`, `DENIED` or `REVOKED` never conflict with anything.

The **vendor coverage posture** on a case is derived from its authorizations and evaluated strictly top to bottom, so an earlier branch wins over a later one. Take the case's authorizations whose `status` is `APPROVED`. If the case does not require an implant and there are no approved authorizations, the posture is `NOT_REQUIRED`. Otherwise, if the case does require an implant and there are no approved authorizations, the posture is `UNCOVERED`. Otherwise, if any approved authorization belongs to a representative whose `credential_posture` is `EXPIRING_SOON`, or if the case's `revoked_authorization_count` is at least one, the posture is `AT_RISK` — meaning a single historical revocation keeps a case flagged even after fresh cover has been approved. Otherwise the posture is `COVERED`. This derivation is refreshed whenever an authorization of the case is approved or revoked, whenever a covering representative's `credential_posture` changes, and during the sweep's revocation phase.

Manual revocation of an approved authorization is available to an `ORCoordinator` or a `CredentialingOfficer` and takes an optional `revoked_reason`; when the caller omits it the server records `MANUAL`. The enumerated reasons the field otherwise carries are `NONE` for an authorization that was never revoked, `REP_SUSPENDED`, `REP_REVOKED`, `CASE_CANCELLED` and `COORDINATOR_WITHDRAWN`. Revocation requires the authorization to be `APPROVED`, moves it `APPROVED → REVOKED`, leaves `decided_at_utc` as the approval left it, decrements the vendor's `authorizations_used_epoch`, the representative's `open_authorization_count` and the case's `approved_rep_count` — each floored at zero — increments the case's `revoked_authorization_count`, voids every kiosk check-in of this authorization whose `badge_status` is `ISSUED`, and recomputes the case's `vendor_coverage_status`.

The same `APPROVED → REVOKED` transition is also reached without any coordinator asking for it, in four systemic circumstances: the sweep's revocation phase, the revocation of a representative's site access, the substantiation of a critical unapproved-product-use incident, and the cancellation of the case. In the first three — the representative-driven paths — only authorizations whose case starts at or after the logical clock's current instant are revoked, and `revoked_reason` is written as `REP_SUSPENDED` or `REP_REVOKED` accordingly; authorizations for cases that have already started are deliberately left `APPROVED`, because historical access is a matter of record and is not rewritten. In the fourth, `revoked_reason` is `CASE_CANCELLED`. In all four the counter decrements, the badge voiding and the coverage recomputation are identical to the manual revoke, and the affected authorizations are processed in ascending creation order.

### 3.5. The Check-In Kiosk: Two Independent Dimensions

On the day of surgery the representative presents at the vendor kiosk, and the badge that comes out of it is the physical realisation of an approved authorization. Check-in is available to a `VendorRepUser` badging itself in or to an `ORCoordinator` doing it on their behalf, and the body carries nothing but the `case_rep_authorization_id`. Before a badge prints, the server insists that the referenced authorization is `APPROVED`, that the representative's `access_status` is neither `REVOKED` nor `SUSPENDED`, that the representative's `credential_posture` is `COMPLIANT` or `EXPIRING_SOON`, that the logical clock lies inside the authorization's badge window, and that the authorization does not already have a live badge.

The badge window test is **inclusive at both ends**: check-in is admissible when the current instant is at or after `badge_window_start_utc` and at or before `badge_window_end_utc`, so arriving at exactly the opening instant or exactly the closing instant both succeed, and anything outside is refused with `ERR_BADGE_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN`. Note the asymmetry with §3.4 that this creates and that is intentional: kiosk admissibility is inclusive on both endpoints, while the double-booking test between two of a representative's windows treats them as half-open so that adjacent cases are bookable.

The badge itself carries a deterministic, never-random serial. `badge_serial` is the hosting facility's `code`, then a hyphen, then a four-digit zero-padded counter that is scoped per facility, starts at `0001`, and is handed out in check-in order — so the third representative to badge in at a facility whose code is `STMARY` receives `STMARY-0003`. Alongside the serial, `issued_at_utc` records the logical clock's instant of check-in, and `valid_from_utc` and `valid_until_utc` are copied from the authorization's `badge_window_start_utc` and `badge_window_end_utc`. Those two copies are not frozen: as §3.3 sets out, a reschedule refreshes them on any check-in still holding an `ISSUED` badge.

A check-in carries **two state dimensions that move independently**, and this is the one place in the system where an implementation must resist the urge to collapse them into a single status. The first dimension is `badge_status`, the physical badge lifecycle, whose domain is `ISSUED`, `CHECKED_OUT`, `EXPIRED` and `VOIDED`; it begins at `ISSUED` and each of the other three is terminal. The second is `escort_status`, the department escort dimension, whose domain is `ESCORT_NOT_REQUIRED`, `ESCORT_PENDING`, `ESCORT_ATTACHED` and `ESCORT_RELEASED`; it is initialised from the authorization's `escort_required` at check-in — `ESCORT_PENDING` when an escort is required, and `ESCORT_NOT_REQUIRED`, which is already terminal, when it is not. `ESCORT_RELEASED` is the other terminal value.

An `ORCoordinator` attaches the escort, supplying an `escort_user_id` in the body. This moves the escort dimension `ESCORT_PENDING → ESCORT_ATTACHED`, writes `escort_user_id` from the body and `escort_attached_at_utc` from the logical clock, and — critically — leaves `badge_status` exactly where it was at `ISSUED`. The escort recorded here is the one who actually showed up and may legitimately differ from the `escort_user_id` the coordinator named on the authorization back at approval time. Attaching requires that `escort_status` is `ESCORT_PENDING` and that `badge_status` is `ISSUED`.

Check-out, performed by a `VendorRepUser` or an `ORCoordinator` with an empty body, moves `badge_status` `ISSUED → CHECKED_OUT` and stamps `checked_out_at_utc` from the logical clock. This is the **only** point at which the two dimensions interact: if `escort_status` is `ESCORT_ATTACHED` at that moment, it moves in the same operation to `ESCORT_RELEASED`, while an `escort_status` of `ESCORT_NOT_REQUIRED` is left exactly as it is. If `escort_status` is still `ESCORT_PENDING`, the check-out is refused with `ERR_ESCORT_REQUIRED` — a representative admitted to a restricted case may not walk out without the escort having ever been logged.

The badge dimension has two further exits, neither of which touches the escort dimension. The sweep's badge phase moves an `ISSUED` badge to `EXPIRED` once its `valid_until_utc` is at or before the sweep's observation instant, leaving `escort_status` exactly as it stood. And any of the revocation or cancellation cascades described in §3.3, §3.4, §3.8 and §3.9 moves an `ISSUED` badge of a newly revoked authorization to `VOIDED`, again leaving `escort_status` untouched.

### 3.6. Product Introduction: Endorsement, Value Analysis and Supersession

No representative may bring an implant into a theatre until the product behind it has cleared a two-stage review. A `ProductIntroductionRequest` is opened by a `VendorAdmin` for its own vendor and carries the `vendor_id`, the `facility_id` the product is being introduced into, the `product_name`, the `product_catalog_number` assigned by the manufacturer, the `manufacturer` name itself, the boolean `is_implant` marking whether the device is implantable, the quoted `unit_cost_cents` which may be zero but never negative, and the `expected_vendor_version` of §2.7. It may also carry `supersedes_request_id`, the self-referential edge naming an earlier request of the vendor's that this one replaces; the field is nullable and is simply absent on a first-of-its-kind request. The request lands in `DRAFT` with `revision_round` at 0, and the create increments the vendor's `version` by one.

The review `status` domain is `DRAFT`, `CLINICAL_REVIEW`, `VALUE_ANALYSIS_REVIEW`, `CHANGES_REQUESTED`, `APPROVED` and `REJECTED`, with the last two terminal. The vendor's `open_pir_count` is defined as the number of that vendor's requests currently sitting in `DRAFT`, `CLINICAL_REVIEW`, `VALUE_ANALYSIS_REVIEW` or `CHANGES_REQUESTED`, and it never goes below zero. Request listings may be narrowed by `vendor_id`, by `facility_id`, by `supersedes_request_id`, by `status` and by `is_implant`.

Submission is the `VendorAdmin`'s move and takes an empty body, but the acting vendor must be the request's own `vendor_id`. From `DRAFT` it advances `DRAFT → CLINICAL_REVIEW`, increments the vendor's `open_pir_count`, and increments the request's own `version`. From `CHANGES_REQUESTED` it advances `CHANGES_REQUESTED → CLINICAL_REVIEW`, increments `revision_round` and increments the request's `version` — but it does **not** touch `open_pir_count`, because a request in `CHANGES_REQUESTED` was already being counted as open. A re-submission is refused with `ERR_PIR_REVISION_LIMIT` once `revision_round` has already reached two.

The `ClinicalSponsor` rules first, supplying a `decision` of `ENDORSE` or `REJECT` plus an optional `review_note`. An endorsement moves the request `CLINICAL_REVIEW → VALUE_ANALYSIS_REVIEW` and records `clinical_sponsor_user_id` from the sponsor's `X-User-ID` together with `clinical_reviewed_at_utc` from the logical clock; that recorded identifier is the segregation-of-duties key that will bar the same human from casting the value-analysis vote. A clinical rejection moves the request `CLINICAL_REVIEW → REJECTED`, stamps `clinical_sponsor_user_id`, `clinical_reviewed_at_utc` and `decided_at_utc` all from the current instant, and decrements the vendor's `open_pir_count`.

The `ValueAnalysisApprover` then rules, supplying a `decision` of `APPROVE`, `REJECT` or `REQUEST_CHANGES` plus an optional `decision_note`. Whichever branch is taken, the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `clinical_sponsor_user_id` and `value_analysis_user_id` is written from the acting user.

An approval moves the request `VALUE_ANALYSIS_REVIEW → APPROVED`, writes `decided_at_utc` from the logical clock, decrements the vendor's `open_pir_count`, and mints exactly one `ApprovedProduct`. That new row copies `product_name`, `product_catalog_number`, `manufacturer`, `is_implant` and `unit_cost_cents` from the request as they stand at this instant and **never re-reads them afterwards**, so a later edit of the request would not propagate. It records `product_introduction_request_id` pointing back at the request, `pinned_pir_version` capturing the request's `version` observed at the moment of approval, `approved_at_utc` from the logical clock, and `approval_expires_at_utc` exactly 365 days after `approved_at_utc`. Its `status` starts at `ACTIVE` and its `suspended_reason` at `NONE`. The vendor and facility that own an approved product are those of the request that minted it. If the request carried a `supersedes_request_id`, one further cascade fires: the approved product belonging to that superseded predecessor, if it is currently `ACTIVE`, moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` with `suspended_reason` set to `SUPERSEDED`. That supersession path deliberately does **not** hold any loaner sets — only a substantiated incident does that, as §3.8 explains.

A value-analysis rejection moves the request `VALUE_ANALYSIS_REVIEW → REJECTED`, writes `decided_at_utc` and decrements `open_pir_count`, and mints nothing. A request for changes moves it `VALUE_ANALYSIS_REVIEW → CHANGES_REQUESTED` and clears both `clinical_sponsor_user_id` and `clinical_reviewed_at_utc`, so the next round genuinely needs a fresh clinical endorsement rather than inheriting the old one; `open_pir_count` is unchanged because the request is still open. A change request is admissible only while `revision_round` is strictly below two, and the third attempt is refused with `ERR_PIR_REVISION_LIMIT`, which is what terminates the `CLINICAL_REVIEW → CHANGES_REQUESTED → CLINICAL_REVIEW` cycle at a bounded number of rounds.

An approved product's own `status` domain is `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED` and `EXPIRED`, the last being terminal, and its `suspended_reason` domain is `NONE`, `INCIDENT_SUBSTANTIATED`, `APPROVAL_EXPIRED` and `SUPERSEDED`. Approved products are only ever born as a cascade of a value-analysis approval, so a direct read of one by identifier and a filtered listing — narrowable by `product_introduction_request_id`, by `status` and by `is_implant` — are their only discovery channels. Expiry of the approval, when the sweep detects that `approval_expires_at_utc` has been reached, touches no loaner set at all; it is felt later, at the moment somebody tries to release a tray set and the product is re-checked.

### 3.7. Loaner Instrument Sets: Deadlines, Bay Capacity, Delivery Branches and Fees

An implant case needs instruments as well as a representative, and those instruments arrive as a `LoanerSet` — a tray set that a vendor lends the hospital for one case and that sterile processing must reprocess before it can go anywhere near a patient. A `VendorAdmin` or a `SterileProcessingTech` registers the set, supplying the `surgical_case_id` it is staged for, the `approved_product_id` naming the value-analysis-approved product the set carries, the `tray_count` of instrument trays it contains, the `reprocessing_complexity` classification, and the `expected_case_version` of §2.7. A `tray_count` of zero is entirely legal — it produces a real deadline and waives every late fee. `reprocessing_complexity` is one of `SIMPLE`, `COMPLEX` or `IMPLANT_SPECIALTY`, and it drives both the reprocessing hours in the deadline and the multiplier in the fee. The set lands in `EXPECTED` with `fee_status` at `NONE` and `assessed_late_fee_cents` at 0, and the registration increments the case's `version` by one. Registration is refused unless the referenced approved product is `ACTIVE`. Loaner listings may be narrowed by `surgical_case_id`, by `approved_product_id`, by `status` and by `fee_status`.

The **delivery deadline** is the heart of this workflow, and it is computed twice from the same arithmetic against two different basis instants. Start with the tray surcharge: a set of at most four trays adds no surcharge hours, a set of five through twelve trays inclusive adds one hour, and a set of thirteen trays or more adds two hours. Add three terms to get the turnaround: the facility's `spd_turnaround_hours`, plus the reprocessing allowance for the set's complexity — two hours for `SIMPLE`, six hours for `COMPLEX`, ten hours for `IMPLANT_SPECIALTY` — plus the tray surcharge just derived. The lead time is then the greater of that turnaround and an implant floor: on a case whose `implant_required` is true the floor is 24 hours, and on any other case the floor is zero, so the lead is simply the turnaround. The deadline is the basis instant with the lead time subtracted, expressed as a whole number of hours before it, and stored as an ISO 8601 UTC instant.

The two variants differ **only** in which basis instant they subtract from. `pinned_deadline_utc` is computed once, at registration, from the case's `original_scheduled_start_utc` — the schedule as it stood when the case was first created. It is never recomputed; a reschedule leaves it untouched, and it remains the audit basis for `late_minutes_pinned_basis`. `required_delivery_deadline_utc` is computed at registration from the case's live `scheduled_start_utc` and is recomputed on every reschedule. It is the only variant that selects the delivery branch and the only one that feeds the late fee.

Registration also stakes out the set's claim on a reprocessing bay. `spd_occupation_start_utc` equals the set's `required_delivery_deadline_utc` and `spd_occupation_end_utc` equals the case's `scheduled_start_utc`, so a set occupies a bay from the moment it was due until the moment the knife goes in, as a **closed** interval inclusive of both endpoints. Admission is then a counting exercise against the existing sets at the same facility. Each existing set's own occupation interval is widened by 30 minutes at each end to produce its buffered interval; the buffer is applied to the **existing** set only, never to the candidate. The candidate collides with an existing set when the existing set's buffered start is at or before the candidate's occupation end **and** the candidate's occupation start is at or before the existing set's buffered end — inclusive at both ends, so intervals that merely touch do collide. This is deliberately stricter than half-open adjacency: two sets whose raw occupations barely graze one another are rejected once the buffer is applied. Only sets whose `status` is `EXPECTED`, `DELIVERED_ON_TIME`, `DELIVERED_LATE` or `HELD` are counted, examined in ascending creation order; a set in `RELEASED_TO_OR` or `REJECTED_LATE` has left the bay and never counts. If the number of colliding sets reaches the facility's `spd_bay_count`, the registration is refused with `ERR_SPD_BAY_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`; a strictly smaller count admits the set.

Receipt at the dock is a `SterileProcessingTech`'s call and takes an optional `delivered_at_utc`; when the field is omitted the server uses the logical clock's current instant. Receipt requires the set to be `EXPECTED` and the case to be `SCHEDULED` or `RESCHEDULED`. **Lateness** is then measured twice, against each deadline variant, as the number of whole minutes from that deadline to `delivered_at_utc`, truncated toward zero and never allowed below zero: `late_minutes_pinned_basis` is measured against `pinned_deadline_utc` and `late_minutes_current_basis` against `required_delivery_deadline_utc`. A delivery at exactly the deadline is zero minutes late, and a delivery fifty-nine seconds past it is still zero minutes late.

The same receipt operation then **forks to one of three states purely on the current-basis figure**. When `late_minutes_current_basis` is zero, the set moves `EXPECTED → DELIVERED_ON_TIME`, `assessed_late_fee_cents` is set to 0 and `fee_status` to `NONE`, and the case's `implant_readiness_status` is recomputed. When `late_minutes_current_basis` is greater than zero and at most 120 — the two-hour hard-reject grace — the set moves `EXPECTED → DELIVERED_LATE`, the late fee below is assessed, that fee is added to the vendor's `late_fee_balance_cents`, and both vendor compliance-score variants and the case's `implant_readiness_status` are recomputed. When `late_minutes_current_basis` exceeds 120, the set moves `EXPECTED → REJECTED_LATE`, the late fee is assessed and added to the vendor's balance exactly as on the `DELIVERED_LATE` branch, both score variants are recomputed, and the case's `implant_readiness_status` is recomputed — a `REJECTED_LATE` set forces the case to `BLOCKED`. In all three branches `delivered_at_utc` and both late-minute fields are written.

The **late fee** is a tiered per-tray charge, and its evaluation order is pinned because rounding at the wrong step yields a different integer for most inputs. First compute the per-tray charge: multiply `late_minutes_current_basis` by the base rate of 2.5 cents per tray per minute, then by the complexity multiplier — one for `SIMPLE`, one and thirty-five hundredths for `COMPLEX`, one and seventy-five hundredths for `IMPLANT_SPECIALTY` — and **round that per-tray figure half up to a whole number of cents before going any further**. Second, multiply the rounded per-tray charge by `tray_count` to get the raw fee. Third, take the lesser of the raw fee and the cap of 250,000 cents, and store the result in `assessed_late_fee_cents`. Rounding after the multiplication rather than before it is wrong. The accompanying `fee_status` is decided in this order: `NONE` when `late_minutes_current_basis` is zero, otherwise `WAIVED` when `tray_count` is zero, otherwise `ASSESSED`. Its full domain is `NONE`, `WAIVED`, `ASSESSED` and `REVERSED`, and it is the ledger participation flag: **only sets whose `fee_status` is `ASSESSED` contribute to the vendor's `late_fee_balance_cents`**, which is defined as the sum of `assessed_late_fee_cents` over that vendor's sets in that state. A `NONE` means no fee is owed, either because the set was never delivered late or because it was delivered on time.

Rescheduling a case can move a set backwards across those branches, and the two backward transitions have distinct consequences. A set in `DELIVERED_LATE` whose recomputed `late_minutes_current_basis` has fallen to zero moves `DELIVERED_LATE → DELIVERED_ON_TIME`: `late_minutes_current_basis` becomes zero while `late_minutes_pinned_basis` is retained unchanged, and if `fee_status` was `ASSESSED` it becomes `REVERSED` and the vendor's `late_fee_balance_cents` is debited by that set's `assessed_late_fee_cents` — the amount itself being retained as the historical assessment — after which both vendor score variants and the case's `implant_readiness_status` are recomputed. A set in `REJECTED_LATE` whose recomputed `late_minutes_current_basis` has fallen to something greater than zero but at most 120 moves `REJECTED_LATE → DELIVERED_LATE`: `assessed_late_fee_cents` is re-derived from the new late minutes and the vendor's `late_fee_balance_cents` is adjusted by the difference between the new and old amounts, then the case's `implant_readiness_status` is recomputed.

Release to the operating room is the join point of the delivery fork and is likewise a `SterileProcessingTech`'s call with an empty body. Both `DELIVERED_ON_TIME → RELEASED_TO_OR` and `DELIVERED_LATE → RELEASED_TO_OR` are admissible and carry the same two additional preconditions: the referenced approved product must still be `ACTIVE`, else the release is refused with `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_APPROVED`, and the vendor's `late_fee_balance_cents` must be strictly below the fee hold threshold of 100,000 cents, else it is refused with `ERR_VENDOR_FEE_HOLD`. A set in `REJECTED_LATE` can never converge on `RELEASED_TO_OR`, and neither can a set in `HELD`. Releasing recomputes the case's `implant_readiness_status`, and the released set leaves the bay so it stops counting toward the facility's capacity. `RELEASED_TO_OR` is terminal.

The same fee hold threshold guards the front door as well as the back: an authorization approval is refused with `ERR_VENDOR_FEE_HOLD` when the vendor's `late_fee_balance_cents` has reached 100,000 cents. A vendor that lets a thousand dollars of late-delivery fees accumulate loses both the ability to put new representatives on cases and the ability to get already-delivered trays into theatres.

`HELD` is the out-of-band detour, and it is entered only from a substantiated incident against the set's product, as §3.8 describes. A set may enter `HELD` from `EXPECTED`, from `DELIVERED_ON_TIME` or from `DELIVERED_LATE`, and each of those three entries recomputes the owning case's `implant_readiness_status`, which a held set forces to `BLOCKED`. When the suspension is later released, the set returns to whichever branch its own history implies: `HELD → EXPECTED` when it has no `delivered_at_utc` at all, `HELD → DELIVERED_LATE` when it has a `delivered_at_utc` and `late_minutes_current_basis` is greater than zero, and `HELD → DELIVERED_ON_TIME` when it has a `delivered_at_utc` and `late_minutes_current_basis` is zero. Each of those three exits likewise recomputes the case's `implant_readiness_status`. The full `status` domain for a loaner set is therefore `EXPECTED`, `DELIVERED_ON_TIME`, `DELIVERED_LATE`, `REJECTED_LATE`, `HELD` and `RELEASED_TO_OR`.

The case's **implant readiness posture** is derived from all of that case's loaner sets regardless of their state, and the branches are evaluated strictly in this order. If the case's `implant_required` is false, the posture is `NOT_APPLICABLE`. Otherwise, if any set of the case is `HELD` or `REJECTED_LATE`, the posture is `BLOCKED`. Otherwise, if the case has at least one set and every one of them is `RELEASED_TO_OR`, the posture is `READY`. Otherwise the posture is `PENDING`. Because the blocked test precedes the ready test, a single held tray blocks a case whose every other set has been released; and because the ready test demands at least one set, an implant case with no sets registered at all is `PENDING`, never `READY`. The posture is refreshed on loaner registration, on receipt, on release, on incident adjudication and on case reschedule.

### 3.8. Incidents, the Deepest Cascade in the Domain, and Two Ways of Scoring a Vendor

An `Incident` records something that went wrong while a vendor was on site. An `ORCoordinator`, a `SterileProcessingTech` or a `ComplianceAdmin` may report one, supplying the `surgical_case_id` during which it occurred, the `vendor_rep_id` of the representative implicated, an `incident_type`, a `severity`, and a free-text `narrative` giving the reporter's account. The optional `approved_product_id` names the product implicated and is simply omitted when the incident is not product-specific. Four types exist: `UNAPPROVED_PRODUCT_USE`, `UNBADGED_ACCESS`, `LOANER_LATE_DELIVERY` and `GIFT_POLICY_VIOLATION`. Three severities exist: `MINOR`, `MAJOR` and `CRITICAL`.

Reporting stamps `status` at `REPORTED`, `adjudicated_outcome` at `NONE`, `reported_by_user_id` from the reporter's `X-User-ID` and `reported_at_utc` from the logical clock, and freezes `score_weight` from the severity at creation: three points for `MINOR`, eight for `MAJOR`, twenty for `CRITICAL`. The report immediately depresses the vendor's provisional score by that weight while leaving the adjudicated score exactly where it was. The `status` domain is `REPORTED`, `UNDER_INVESTIGATION`, `SUBSTANTIATED`, `UNSUBSTANTIATED` and `CLOSED`, with `CLOSED` terminal, and the `adjudicated_outcome` domain is `NONE`, `SUBSTANTIATED` and `UNSUBSTANTIATED`. Incident listings may be narrowed by `surgical_case_id`, by `vendor_rep_id`, by `approved_product_id`, by `status` and by `severity`.

A `ComplianceAdmin` may open an investigation on a `REPORTED` incident, moving it `REPORTED → UNDER_INVESTIGATION`. This step is deliberately score-neutral in both variants, because the provisional variant already counts a `REPORTED` incident at full weight and the adjudicated variant counts neither.

Adjudication is the same `ComplianceAdmin`'s judgement, supplied as an `outcome` of `SUBSTANTIATED` or `UNSUBSTANTIATED` plus an optional `adjudication_note`, and it may not be performed by the incident's own reporter. Whichever outcome is chosen, `adjudicated_outcome` takes that value, `adjudicated_by_user_id` takes the adjudicator's `X-User-ID`, `adjudicated_at_utc` takes the logical clock's current instant — which is also the anchor from which the incident's eventual closure is measured — and both vendor compliance-score variants are recomputed. Three source states are admissible, and they are not symmetric. An incident in `REPORTED` or `UNDER_INVESTIGATION` may go to either outcome, so `REPORTED → SUBSTANTIATED`, `UNDER_INVESTIGATION → SUBSTANTIATED`, `REPORTED → UNSUBSTANTIATED` and `UNDER_INVESTIGATION → UNSUBSTANTIATED` are all legal and the two substantiating transitions carry identical consequences to one another, as do the two unsubstantiating ones. An incident already in `SUBSTANTIATED` may be re-adjudicated **only** to `UNSUBSTANTIATED`, giving the reversal transition `SUBSTANTIATED → UNSUBSTANTIATED`. A second `SUBSTANTIATED` outcome on an already-substantiated incident, any adjudication of an already `UNSUBSTANTIATED` incident, and any adjudication of a `CLOSED` incident are all refused with `ERR_INCIDENT_INVALID_STATE`.

Substantiation is the deepest cascade in this domain, and each hop exists because the previous one happened. Because the incident is now substantiated, the adjudicated score variant starts counting its weight. Because the incident names an `approved_product_id`, that approved product moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` with `suspended_reason` set to `INCIDENT_SUBSTANTIATED`. Because that product is now suspended, every loaner set referencing it whose `status` is `EXPECTED`, `DELIVERED_ON_TIME` or `DELIVERED_LATE` moves to `HELD`, processed in ascending creation order. Because those sets are now held, each owning surgical case recomputes `implant_readiness_status` to `BLOCKED`. And separately, because the incident is of type `UNAPPROVED_PRODUCT_USE` at `CRITICAL` severity, the implicated representative moves `CREDENTIALED → SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` set to `INCIDENT_SUBSTANTIATED` — which in turn runs the representative cascade of §3.4, revoking that representative's `APPROVED` authorizations for cases starting at or after the current instant with `revoked_reason` of `REP_SUSPENDED`, adjusting every counter, voiding those authorizations' `ISSUED` badges, and recomputing each affected case's `vendor_coverage_status`. That is the only incident-driven route into representative suspension; a substantiated `MAJOR` unapproved-product-use incident, or a substantiated `CRITICAL` gift-policy violation, does not suspend the representative.

Unsubstantiation unwinds what substantiation built, but not everything. The provisional variant drops the incident's weight entirely, so the provisional score **rises** when an investigation clears — which is the intended behaviour of a pessimistic number. A product that this incident suspended returns `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` with `suspended_reason` reset to `NONE`, admissible only when the existing `suspended_reason` is `INCIDENT_SUBSTANTIATED`, so a product suspended by supersession is not reinstated by an unrelated acquittal. Each of that product's loaner sets in `HELD` returns to the delivery branch its own late minutes imply, per the three exit transitions of §3.7, and each owning case recomputes `implant_readiness_status`. A representative that this incident suspended, however, is **not** reinstated here. Reinstatement of a representative stays with credential verification, with the sweep's posture phase, and with an overturned appeal. On the reversal path specifically, the adjudicated variant drops the weight it had been carrying, while the provisional variant never carried it once the incident had been adjudicated at all.

The **vendor compliance score** is a weighted deduction from a base of 100, and its two variants differ in exactly one respect: which incidents they count. Four penalties are computed and then subtracted. The incident penalty is the sum, over the counted incidents, of three points for each `MINOR`, eight for each `MAJOR` and twenty for each `CRITICAL`. The late-delivery penalty is five points for each of that vendor's loaner sets whose `status` is `DELIVERED_LATE` or `REJECTED_LATE` **and** whose `fee_status` is not `REVERSED`. The lapsed-representative penalty is six points for each of the vendor's representatives counted as lapsed at the last sweep. The over-utilization penalty is a flat twelve points when the utilization ratio taken at the last sweep was strictly greater than one, and zero otherwise. Subtract all four penalties from 100 and **then** clamp the result into the range 0 through 100 — the clamp is applied once, after every penalty has been subtracted, never per term. The arithmetic is integer throughout. The incident set considered is those incidents whose `vendor_rep_id` belongs to this vendor. The lapsed-representative count and the utilization ratio are refreshed **only** inside the sweep's rescoring phase and are held constant between sweeps, which is what keeps a vendor's `version` stable during ordinary credentialing work.

`provisional_compliance_score` is the pessimistic variant, and it is the number a coordinator sees while investigations are still open. It counts an incident when that incident's `status` is `REPORTED` or `UNDER_INVESTIGATION` — that is, unadjudicated, at full severity weight — **or** when its `adjudicated_outcome` is `SUBSTANTIATED`. An incident adjudicated `UNSUBSTANTIATED` drops out of the provisional variant entirely.

`adjudicated_compliance_score` is the conservative variant, and it is the number that drives `scorecard_tier`, `access_quota_in_effect` and `compliance_status` at each sweep. It counts an incident **only** when its `adjudicated_outcome` is `SUBSTANTIATED`, regardless of whether that incident is still open or has already been moved to `CLOSED` — closure never discounts a substantiated finding. A freshly reported incident contributes nothing to the adjudicated variant, which is why, for identical data, the adjudicated score is always at least the provisional score.

Both variants are recomputed whenever an incident is reported, whenever an incident is adjudicated, whenever a loaner set is received, whenever a reschedule moves a set between delivery branches, and during the sweep's rescoring phase. A dedicated scorecard read — restricted to a `CredentialingOfficer`, a `ComplianceAdmin`, or the vendor itself — returns `vendor_id`, `provisional_compliance_score`, `adjudicated_compliance_score`, `scorecard_tier`, `compliance_status`, `access_quota_in_effect`, `authorizations_used_epoch`, `utilization_ratio`, `late_fee_balance_cents`, `lapsed_rep_count`, `substantiated_incident_count`, `open_incident_count`, `late_delivery_count` and `last_sweep_epoch`. It is entirely read-only, and `utilization_ratio` together with `lapsed_rep_count` are derived live from the current counters and representative postures — so they may legitimately differ from the frozen values that went into the score at the last sweep.

### 3.9. Appealing a Revocation, and the Nine Pinned Phases of a Compliance Sweep

A revocation is not the end of the conversation. A `VendorAdmin` or a `VendorRepUser` may file an `AccessAppeal` by naming the `vendor_rep_id` of the revoked representative and writing out the `grounds` on which the revocation is contested. Filing is admissible only while three things hold. The representative's `access_status` must currently be `REVOKED`; a suspended or credentialed representative has nothing to appeal, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_REP_NOT_REVOKED`. The filing must fall no later than the representative's `revoked_at_utc` plus fourteen days, with filing at exactly that instant still accepted and anything past it refused with `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_CLOSED`. And the representative must not already have an appeal on file for this same revocation, which is tested by comparing each existing appeal's `filed_for_revoked_at_utc` against the representative's current `revoked_at_utc`; a match is refused with `ERR_APPEAL_ALREADY_FILED`.

That comparison is the whole reason `filed_for_revoked_at_utc` exists as a stored copy rather than a live lookup. It pins the appeal to one specific revocation event. A representative who is reinstated, works for a while, and is then revoked a second time has a fresh `revoked_at_utc`, so the earlier appeal's frozen copy no longer matches and a second appeal becomes legal — one appeal per revocation, not one appeal per lifetime. Filing stamps `status` at `SUBMITTED`, `filed_at_utc` from the logical clock, and `appeal_deadline_utc` at the representative's `revoked_at_utc` plus fourteen days, so the deadline the appeal carries is the same boundary the window check applied.

A `ComplianceAdmin` or a `CredentialingOfficer` decides the appeal by supplying a `decision` of `UPHOLD` or `OVERTURN` together with a `decision_rationale`. The decider may not be the officer who performed the revocation, which is checked by comparing the acting `X-User-ID` against the representative's `revoked_by_user_id` and refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Only a `SUBMITTED` appeal may be decided; both `UPHELD` and `OVERTURNED` are terminal, and a second decision on an already-decided appeal is refused with `ERR_APPEAL_INVALID_STATE`. Either decision records `decided_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`, `decided_at_utc` from the logical clock, and the supplied `decision_rationale`.

`SUBMITTED → UPHELD` is the quiet branch: the revocation stands, the representative's `access_status` remains `REVOKED`, and nothing else in the system moves. `SUBMITTED → OVERTURNED` is the branch that reaches back into the representative record, and it does not blindly restore access. The representative's `credential_posture` is recomputed first, against the credentials as they stand at this instant — which may be weeks after the revocation, long enough for a credential that was valid then to have expired since. When the recomputed posture is `COMPLIANT` or `EXPIRING_SOON`, the representative goes `REVOKED → CREDENTIALED`. When it is anything else, the representative goes `REVOKED → SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` set to `CREDENTIAL_LAPSED`, because an overturned revocation cannot manufacture credentials the representative does not hold. Both branches set `reinstated_at_utc` to the current logical instant, and neither restores any authorization or badge that the revocation destroyed — as established in §3.2, reinstatement is forward-looking only, so the vendor must have fresh authorizations approved. Appeals are readable individually by every role, and a representative's appeals are reachable as a sub-resource listing of that representative.

A **compliance sweep** is the system's scheduled reckoning: one call by a `ComplianceAdmin` that walks the entire estate against the current logical instant and writes down what it changed. It runs in **nine pinned phases**, and the order is normative because each phase consumes what the previous one produced. Every comparison in every phase uses the single value `swept_at_utc`, captured once at the start — not a per-phase re-read of the clock — so a sweep is a coherent snapshot even though it performs many writes.

**Phase 1** opens the epoch. `epoch_number` is set to the previous maximum epoch plus one, so the first sweep ever run is epoch 1; `previous_epoch_number` is the epoch this sweep supersedes, which is 0 for that first sweep; and `swept_at_utc` is the logical clock's current instant.

**Phase 2** ages credentials. Every `VERIFIED` credential whose `expires_at_utc` is at or before `swept_at_utc` moves to `EXPIRED` — equality expires, the boundary is inclusive — and so does every `EXPIRING_SOON` credential past the same boundary; each such move increments `credentials_expired_count`. Every `VERIFIED` credential that has not yet expired but whose `expires_at_utc` falls at or before `swept_at_utc` plus fourteen days moves `VERIFIED → EXPIRING_SOON` and increments `credentials_warned_count`. A credential can therefore be warned by one sweep and expired by a later one, and it is counted once in each.

**Phase 3** refreshes posture and moves representatives. Every representative's `credential_posture` is recomputed from the credentials as phase 2 left them, and the two posture-driven access transitions are applied: a `CREDENTIALED` representative whose posture is now `LAPSED` moves `CREDENTIALED → SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` set to `CREDENTIAL_LAPSED`, incrementing `reps_suspended_count`; a `SUSPENDED` representative whose `suspension_reason` is `CREDENTIAL_LAPSED` and whose posture is now `COMPLIANT` or `EXPIRING_SOON` moves `SUSPENDED → CREDENTIALED` with `suspension_reason` reset to `NONE` and `reinstated_at_utc` stamped, incrementing `reps_reinstated_count`. A representative suspended for `INCIDENT_SUBSTANTIATED` is not reinstated by this phase no matter how good its credentials look. A `REVOKED` representative is untouched by this phase entirely.

**Phase 4** propagates those suspensions forward in time. For each representative newly suspended in phase 3, every `APPROVED` authorization of that representative whose case's `scheduled_start_utc` is at or after `swept_at_utc` moves `APPROVED → REVOKED` with `revoked_reason` set to `REP_SUSPENDED`, incrementing `authorizations_revoked_count`. Past cases are left alone; the sweep does not rewrite history. Each such revocation runs the full authorization-revocation cascade of §3.4 — decrementing the case's `approved_authorization_count`, incrementing its `revoked_authorization_count`, decrementing the vendor's `authorizations_used_epoch`, voiding any `ISSUED` badge on that authorization, and recomputing the case's `vendor_coverage_status`. Every badge voided as a consequence of a phase-4 revocation increments `badges_voided_count`, which is why that counter is scoped to voids that happened inside a sweep rather than to voids in general.

**Phase 5** expires badges on their own terms. Every `ISSUED` check-in whose `valid_until_utc` is at or before `swept_at_utc` moves `ISSUED → EXPIRED` and increments `badges_expired_count`. A badge voided in phase 4 is already out of `ISSUED` and cannot also be expired here, so the two badge counters never double-count the same row.

**Phase 6** expires product approvals. Every `ACTIVE` approved product whose `approval_expires_at_utc` is at or before `swept_at_utc` moves `ACTIVE → EXPIRED` — equality expires — and so does every `SUSPENDED` approved product past the same boundary; each increments `approvals_expired_count`. Expiry places **no hold** on any loaner set, exactly as in §3.7: a set already registered against a product whose approval lapses stays where it is, while a *new* release attempt against that product is refused.

**Phase 7** closes old findings. Every adjudicated incident — whether its `status` is `SUBSTANTIATED` or `UNSUBSTANTIATED` — whose `adjudicated_at_utc` plus thirty days falls at or before `swept_at_utc` moves to `CLOSED` and increments `incidents_closed_count`. Closure is bookkeeping only: `adjudicated_outcome` is retained precisely so that the adjudicated compliance score keeps counting a substantiated finding after the incident itself has been closed.

**Phase 8** rescores every vendor. Every vendor row is rescored on every sweep, processed in ascending `created_at` order, and `vendors_rescored_count` therefore always equals the total number of vendors. This is the **only** place in the system where `scorecard_tier`, `access_quota_in_effect`, `authorizations_used_epoch` and `compliance_status` may change; no ordinary endpoint may write them. The quota is both an input to and an output of this computation, so the following eight steps are normative and any other order yields a different observable score.

First, take `quota_in_effect` as the vendor's `access_quota_in_effect` **as read before this sweep** — the pre-sweep value, never the value step six is about to write. Second, compute `utilization_ratio` as `authorizations_used_epoch` divided by `quota_in_effect`, rounded half up to four decimal places. Third, set the over-utilization penalty to twelve when that ratio is strictly greater than one and to zero otherwise. Fourth, compute the adjudicated compliance score by the §3.8 formula, using the penalty from step three and taking the lapsed-representative count as the number of this vendor's representatives whose `credential_posture` is `LAPSED` **after** phase 3 has run. Fifth, derive `scorecard_tier`: `TIER_PLATINUM` when that score is 90 or above, `TIER_STANDARD` when it is 70 or above, and `TIER_PROBATION` otherwise. Sixth, write the new `access_quota_in_effect` as the larger of 1 and the floor of `quota_in_effect` multiplied by the step for the tier just derived — 1.5 for `TIER_PLATINUM`, 1.0 for `TIER_STANDARD`, 0.4 for `TIER_PROBATION` — so a platinum vendor's allowance grows, a standard vendor's holds, and a probation vendor's shrinks toward but never reaches zero. Seventh, and only now, reset `authorizations_used_epoch` to 0: the epoch reset happens **after** the ratio in step two has already been taken from it, so resetting earlier would make every ratio zero. Eighth, derive `compliance_status` from the same adjudicated score: `BARRED` below 40, `PROBATION` below 70, `WATCH` below 85, and `IN_GOOD_STANDING` at 85 or above; and set `last_sweep_epoch` to this sweep's `epoch_number`, which is what re-scopes the vendor's authorization counter to the new epoch.

The relationship between the quota and the admission gate of §3.4 is what makes over-utilization observable at all. The gate refuses a new approval only once `authorizations_used_epoch` has reached the floor of the quota multiplied by 1.5, so the counter can legitimately climb above `access_quota_in_effect` itself; a vendor at quota 4 may accumulate up to 6 approvals in an epoch, giving a `utilization_ratio` of 1.5 and drawing the twelve-point penalty at the next sweep.

**Phase 9** writes the record. Every counter field on the sweep row is persisted: `credentials_expired_count`, `credentials_warned_count`, `reps_suspended_count`, `reps_reinstated_count`, `authorizations_revoked_count`, `badges_voided_count`, `badges_expired_count`, `approvals_expired_count`, `incidents_closed_count` and `vendors_rescored_count`. The sweep is an event record rather than an editable resource: as noted in §2.5 it carries only `id` and `created_at` from the common properties, has no `updated_at` and no `version`, and is never updated once written. Running a second sweep at the same logical instant is legal and will simply find nothing left to do — every counter comes back zero except `vendors_rescored_count`, and the quota walk of phase 8 runs a second time against the already-reset counters.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Every endpoint below sits under the `/vendor-access` prefix and speaks the envelope of §2.2. Creation endpoints — those that mint a brand-new resource — return **201**; every read, every list, and every action that transitions an existing resource returns **200**, including the action endpoints that produce cascading changes elsewhere in the system. The one exception worth calling out is the compliance sweep, which returns **201** because it creates a new sweep event record rather than modifying an existing one. Action endpoints return the resource they acted upon in `data`, after all side effects and all derived-field recomputations have been applied, so a caller never has to re-read to see the consequences of its own call.

In the Authorized Role(s) column, **All roles** is shorthand for the complete set of eight roles named in §2.1 — `CredentialingOfficer`, `ComplianceAdmin`, `ORCoordinator`, `SterileProcessingTech`, `VendorAdmin`, `VendorRepUser`, `ClinicalSponsor` and `ValueAnalysisApprover` — and is used only for reads that carry no vendor-scoping restriction of their own. Where a narrower set is listed, any other role is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`, and where a vendor-scoped operation is listed for `VendorAdmin` or `VendorRepUser`, the scope check of §2.1 applies in addition.
