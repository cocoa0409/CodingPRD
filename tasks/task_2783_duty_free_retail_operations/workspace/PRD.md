# Duty-Free Retail Operations API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

An airport duty-free operator sits at the intersection of retail and customs law. Every basket it rings up is a fiscal event: goods leave a bonded warehouse under duty suspension, and whether that suspension becomes a permanent exemption depends on facts that are only knowable from the traveller's itinerary — is the departure genuinely international, has the boarding pass been verified, which country will the passenger actually arrive in, and how many units of that merchandise category may be imported there duty-free. When the operator gets this wrong, the consequence is not a mis-priced receipt; it is an unaccounted-goods duty liability assessed by a customs authority against the operator itself.

This document specifies the API that runs those operations end to end. It governs the commercial estate (terminals, supplier brands, category exclusivity deals, and the arrival-allowance rules published per destination country), the bonded stock ledger that tracks duty-suspended inventory movement by movement, the till workflow from basket assembly through eligibility clearance to gate handover, the sealed-bag regime that lets a connecting passenger carry liquids through a transfer airport, the pre-order and pick-up-on-return programme that reserves bonded units ahead of travel, the customs stock-count audit cycle that reconciles physical counts against the ledger and prices shrinkage, and the concession settlement that converts turnover into rent under a percentage-of-sales deal with a minimum guarantee.

Two design commitments run through the whole specification. First, quantities and money are conserved: every change to bonded inventory is an append-only ledger entry, and every compensating action (a voided sale, a reversed receipt, a cancelled reservation) is expressed as an equal-and-opposite entry rather than as an in-place correction, so that a customs auditor can always reconstruct how a balance came to be. Second, the accounting dimension of a sale is deliberately kept independent of its till dimension: a basket can be cleared for sale and still be in duty suspension, and a tampered seal can make a basket duty-liable without un-selling it. Implementers should treat these two dimensions as separate state machines that happen to share a row.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contract-level rules that hold for every endpoint in the API: authentication, the response envelope, pagination and filtering, the logical clock, resource identity and versioning, monetary precision, stale-version guards, and the order in which the server rejects a bad request.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which they act. If either header is missing or empty, or if `X-User-Role` names something outside the recognised set, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before anything else is examined. When the headers are well formed but the named role is not authorised for the operation being called, the rejection is `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

Five roles are recognised, spelled exactly as follows:

- `TerminalAdmin` — the commercial administrator of the duty-free estate. Registers terminals, supplier brands, products, arrival-allowance rules and exclusivity grants; governs which products are admitted into the assortment; drafts, activates and settles concession agreements; and owns the logical clock.
- `SalesAssociate` — the till operator in the store. Records passenger itineraries, scans boarding passes, amends routings, builds baskets, clears sales eligibility, issues and verifies sealed bags, hands goods over at pickup, and voids baskets.
- `StockController` — the bonded warehouse controller. Opens stock ledgers, posts receipts and audit adjustments, reverses its own manual movements, prepares customs stock-count audits and submits physical counts, and converts pre-orders into baskets.
- `CustomsAuditor` — the independent adjudicator. Returns a submitted count for more information and closes audits, assessing unaccounted-goods duty.
- `Passenger` — the traveller. Places and cancels their own pre-order reservations and reads only records that trace back to their own itinerary.

Four of these five identities are operator identities rather than persisted records. When `X-User-Role` is `TerminalAdmin`, `SalesAssociate`, `StockController` or `CustomsAuditor`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any stored entity; the server records it where a workflow demands an audit trail — as `posted_by_user_id` on a bonded ledger movement, as `preparer_user_id` on a customs audit at creation, and as `auditor_user_id` on that audit when it is closed — but never compares it against any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `Passenger`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `PassengerItinerary`: ownership checks on any resource that references a `PassengerItinerary` compare the resource's itinerary reference to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a `Passenger` placing a reservation must supply an `itinerary_id` equal to their own `X-User-ID`, and a `Passenger` cancelling a reservation must own the reservation's itinerary; either mismatch is rejected with `ERR_NOT_ITINERARY_OWNER` (HTTP 403). The same rule scopes a passenger's reads: itinerary-scoped records — the itinerary itself, its sales, its sale lines, its sealed bags and its pre-orders — are visible to a `Passenger` only when they trace back to that passenger's own itinerary, and a request for someone else's record is rejected with `ERR_NOT_ITINERARY_OWNER`. Reference data that is the same for every traveller (arrival-allowance rules, products and bonded stock items) is readable by a `Passenger` without any ownership restriction.

Segregation of duties is enforced inside the customs-audit workflow rather than by role alone: preparing an audit and adjudicating it are separate acts, and the operator identifier that prepared a count may not be the one that requests more information about it or closes it, even when that operator legitimately holds the `CustomsAuditor` role.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and Collection Filters

List endpoints are paginated with two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of records returned; when omitted it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` is the number of records to skip and defaults to 0. Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, every list is sorted by `created_at` in ascending order, so the oldest record appears first. List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope, where `total_count` counts every record matching the filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` states whether records remain beyond the returned page.

Each collection also accepts a small set of equality filters, all of them optional and combinable, which exist so that records synthesised by a cascade remain reachable even though they never appear in a creation response. The terminals collection filters on `country_code`. Brands filter on `category` and `status`. Arrival-allowance rules filter on `destination_country`, `category` and `status`. Exclusivity grants filter on `brand_id`, `terminal_id`, `category`, `scope` and `status`. Products filter on `brand_id`, `category` and `assortment_status`. Bonded stock items filter on `terminal_id` and `product_id`. The bonded ledger — the only discovery channel for movements the server writes on a client's behalf — filters on `stock_item_id`, `movement_type`, `reversal_of_movement_id`, `agreement_period_code`, `source_ref_type` and `source_ref_id`. Itineraries filter on `departure_terminal_id`, `nationality_code` and `scan_status`. Sales filter on `itinerary_id`, `terminal_id`, `agreement_id`, `fulfillment_status` and `duty_status`. Sale lines filter on `sale_id` and `product_id`. Sealed bags filter on `sale_id`, `terminal_id` and `status`. Pre-orders filter on `itinerary_id`, `stock_item_id`, `converted_sale_id`, `program` and `status`. Concession agreements filter on `terminal_id`, `period_code` and `status`. Customs audits filter on `terminal_id`, `stock_item_id`, `period_code` and `status`.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock

Nothing in this system reads the host machine's wall clock. Every deadline, seal-validity window, reservation window, nationality-mix lookback and settlement-period boundary is evaluated against a server-held **logical clock**, which starts at exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` when the service boots and only ever moves when a `TerminalAdmin` drives it. The clock-setting capability accepts a single field, `to_iso8601`, and answers with the new clock value under the key `current_utc`. The clock is monotonic: a `to_iso8601` earlier than the current logical time is rejected as a malformed request with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422). Setting the clock to exactly the current logical instant is accepted.

Wherever this document says "the current instant", "now", or stamps a timestamp field, the value written is the logical clock at the moment the request is processed. Instants are compared at millisecond resolution.

### 2.5. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Versioning

Every resource carries an `id`, which is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. Most resources additionally carry an `updated_at` with the same format, refreshed on every mutation, and a `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the resource is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation — including mutations the server performs on its own initiative as part of a cascade, and including mutations that only touch derived fields.

The one exception is the bonded ledger movement. A movement is an immutable accounting entry: it carries `id` and `created_at` only, and has neither `updated_at` nor `version`. The single field on a movement that ever changes after it is written is its `reversed` flag, which the server flips when a compensating reversal is appended; because the entry has no `version` and no `updated_at`, that flip leaves no other trace on the row.

Cascades are pervasive in this domain, and each entity a cascade touches is mutated in its own right: a pickup that issues bonded stock and rolls turnover into a concession agreement increments the `version` of the sale, of every bonded stock item it draws from, and of the agreement. Where a subsection below states that a cascade "bumps" or "increments" a version, that increment is by 1 per affected resource per request, and the resource's `updated_at` is refreshed at the same time.

### 2.6. Money, Basis Points and Rounding Discipline

All money in this system is an integer number of **cents**; no monetary field is ever fractional. All duty rates, tax rates, rent percentages, nationality shares and statutory tolerances are expressed in **basis points**, where 10000 basis points is one whole — so a `duty_rate_bps` of 500 means five percent, and a `rent_pct_bps` of 1200 means twelve percent of turnover.

Whenever a rate is applied to an amount, the product is divided by 10000 and the result is rounded **half up** to a whole number of cents. Two rounding-order rules are pinned and must not be reorganised:

- **Duty and tax on a basket are rounded per line, then summed.** The combined levy on each sale line is rounded half up to whole cents on that line, and the sale's totals are the sums of the already-rounded line figures. Summing unrounded line products and rounding once at the end produces a different — and incorrect — result.
- **Unaccounted-goods duty rounds twice.** The per-unit duty on the audited product is rounded half up to whole cents first, and the assessed total (that per-unit figure multiplied by the assessable quantity and by any penalty multiplier) is rounded half up second.

Where a quantity rather than an amount is derived from a rate — the statutory shrinkage tolerance, a nationality share, the number of whole days in a settlement period — the result is **floored** to a whole number rather than rounded. Nationality shares are therefore floored to whole basis points and need not sum to exactly 10000.

### 2.7. Stale-Version Guards on Reservation and Basket Writes

Two write surfaces are protected by optimistic concurrency control, because in both cases a repeat of the same request is perfectly legitimate under the product semantics and therefore no state precondition can distinguish a deliberate second write from a client working off an out-of-date snapshot.

- `POST /pre-orders` requires a non-optional `expected_stock_item_version` field carrying the `version` of the target `BondedStockItem` as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the stock item's current `version`, the reservation is rejected with `ERR_STALE_STOCK_ITEM_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A second reservation against the same item may well be admissible on capacity grounds, so without this guard a client that had not seen an intervening receipt, write-off or reservation would silently reserve against a quantity that no longer exists.
- `POST /sales/{sale_id}/lines` requires a non-optional `expected_sale_version` field carrying the `version` of the parent `Sale` as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the sale's current `version`, the line append is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Appending a second line to a basket is always legal while the basket is open, so the stale-version check is the only deterministic protection for the recomputed basket totals.

No other endpoint accepts a version field. Everywhere else, ordinary state preconditions are sufficient, and a client that sends an extra version field on such an endpoint gains nothing from it.

### 2.8. Rejection Precedence

When a request violates several rules at once, the response must name the violation the server reaches first. Checks are evaluated in the following order, and the first failure ends the request:

1. **Authentication.** Headers present and `X-User-Role` recognised, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. **Role authorisation.** The acting role appears among the roles authorised for the called operation, else `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403).
3. **Passenger ownership.** For the two passenger-facing writes — placing a reservation and cancelling one — `X-User-ID` must equal the itinerary the request targets: the `itinerary_id` in the body when placing a reservation, and the reservation's own itinerary when cancelling. Otherwise `ERR_NOT_ITINERARY_OWNER` (403).
4. **Existence.** Every identifier in the URL path is resolved first, left to right, and only then are identifiers carried in the request body resolved. Each identifier has its own 404: `terminal_id` yields `ERR_TERMINAL_NOT_FOUND`, `brand_id` yields `ERR_BRAND_NOT_FOUND`, `product_id` yields `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_FOUND`, `grant_id` yields `ERR_GRANT_NOT_FOUND`, `allowance_rule_id` yields `ERR_ALLOWANCE_RULE_NOT_FOUND`, `stock_item_id` yields `ERR_STOCK_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, `movement_id` yields `ERR_MOVEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `itinerary_id` yields `ERR_ITINERARY_NOT_FOUND`, `sale_id` yields `ERR_SALE_NOT_FOUND`, `sale_line_id` yields `ERR_SALE_LINE_NOT_FOUND`, `sealed_bag_id` yields `ERR_SEALED_BAG_NOT_FOUND`, `pre_order_id` yields `ERR_PRE_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `agreement_id` yields `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND` and `audit_id` yields `ERR_AUDIT_NOT_FOUND`. When several body identifiers are unresolvable, they are resolved in the order listed here, which is the order the identifiers were introduced above.
5. **Request well-formedness.** On every write, all required fields must be present, every enum value must fall inside its declared set, quantities and basis-point figures must lie inside their declared ranges, a quantity field declared positive must be greater than zero, `liquid_ml` must be supplied and greater than zero whenever `is_liquid` is true, `effective_to` must be strictly later than `effective_from`, `period_end_utc` strictly later than `period_start_utc`, `reservation_window_end` strictly later than `reservation_window_start`, and `to_iso8601` no earlier than the current logical clock. Any breach is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422).
6. **Uniqueness.** Duplicate business keys are rejected next, each with its own code: `ERR_DUPLICATE_SKU`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_STOCK_ITEM`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_BOARDING_PASS`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_AGREEMENT_PERIOD` and `ERR_DUPLICATE_SEAL_NUMBER`, all HTTP 409.
7. **Terminality of the basket.** Before any other state check, a basket that has already been voided rejects every further till action — adding a line, clearing eligibility, pickup and a second void alike — with `ERR_SALE_ALREADY_TERMINAL` (409). This tier sits ahead of the remaining state checks precisely so that a voided basket reports its terminality rather than the narrower "not in cart" or "not cleared" complaint.
8. **State preconditions.** The state of the targeted resource and of the resources it depends on: `ERR_SALE_NOT_IN_CART`, `ERR_SALE_NOT_CLEARED`, `ERR_ITINERARY_NOT_SCANNED`, `ERR_ITINERARY_SCAN_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_PENDING`, `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_LISTED`, `ERR_GRANT_NOT_ACTIVE`, `ERR_BRAND_ALREADY_SUSPENDED`, `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`, `ERR_AGREEMENT_ALREADY_ACTIVE`, `ERR_AUDIT_NOT_SUBMITTED`, `ERR_PREORDER_NOT_RESERVED`, `ERR_BAG_NOT_ISSUED` and `ERR_MOVEMENT_NOT_REVERSIBLE`. Within this tier, the resource named in the path is checked before the resources it references: a basket's own status precedes its itinerary's scan status, which precedes the product's assortment status.
9. **Stale-version guards.** `ERR_STALE_STOCK_ITEM_VERSION` and `ERR_STALE_SALE_VERSION`, as described in §2.7. A stale version is reported only once the target and its dependencies are in an otherwise admissible state.
10. **Transport safety.** The sealed-bag liquid limit (`ERR_LIQUID_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`) and the requirement that a sale needing a sealed bag actually has a verified one (`ERR_SEALED_BAG_REQUIRED`).
11. **Referential coherence.** `ERR_EXCLUSIVITY_CONFLICT`, `ERR_PRODUCT_CATEGORY_MISMATCH`, `ERR_BRAND_SUSPENDED` and `ERR_TERMINAL_MISMATCH`, followed by the availability of arrival-allowance rules for the categories in play (`ERR_ALLOWANCE_RULE_MISSING`).
12. **Temporal windows.** `ERR_SEAL_WINDOW_EXPIRED`, `ERR_DEPARTURE_PASSED`, `ERR_RESERVATION_WINDOW_INVALID` and `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_ENDED`, followed by the exclusivity-window overlap check (`ERR_EXCLUSIVITY_WINDOW_OVERLAP`).
13. **Assortment and governance constraints.** `ERR_NATIONALITY_MIX_BELOW_THRESHOLD`, `ERR_ITINERARY_NOT_INTERNATIONAL`, `ERR_BOARDING_PASS_MISMATCH` and `ERR_EMPTY_BASKET`, followed by the audit-governance trio `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, `ERR_AUDIT_PERIOD_NOT_CLOSED` and `ERR_COUNTED_QTY_EXCEEDS_EXPECTED`.
14. **Fiscal and capacity checks, evaluated last.** `ERR_BONDED_STOCK_INSUFFICIENT`, `ERR_RESERVATION_CAPACITY_OVERLAP` and `ERR_VOID_AFTER_PERIOD_CLOSE`. These are the most expensive checks and the ones most likely to be masked by a cheaper failure, so they run only when everything else about the request is sound.

### 2.9. The Append-Only Bonded Ledger

Bonded inventory is not stored as a mutable number. Each bonded stock item is the head of an append-only ledger of movements, and its quantities are always derived from that ledger: `on_hand_qty` is the sum of the signed `qty_delta` of every movement of the item whose `affects` dimension is `ON_HAND`, `reserved_qty` is the same sum restricted to movements whose `affects` is `RESERVED`, and `available_qty` is `on_hand_qty` less `reserved_qty`. No status filter is applied when summing — a reversed movement stays in the sum, and its effect is undone by the compensating entry rather than by exclusion.

Which dimension a movement affects is derived, not supplied: a `RESERVATION_HOLD` or `RESERVATION_RELEASE` affects `RESERVED`; a `REVERSAL` copies the `affects` of the movement it reverses; every other movement type affects `ON_HAND`. Each entry also records where it came from: `source_ref_type` is the polymorphic discriminator naming the aggregate responsible — one of `SALE`, `PRE_ORDER`, `CUSTOMS_AUDIT`, `MANUAL` or `REVERSAL` — and `source_ref_id` is that aggregate's identifier, left null when the type is `MANUAL`. `posted_by_user_id` records the operator identifier whose request produced the entry, and `agreement_period_code` records the `period_code` of the concession agreement of the item's terminal that was active when the entry was posted, or null when the terminal had no active agreement at that moment.

Every appended movement re-derives the item's `on_hand_qty`, `reserved_qty` and `available_qty`, and then re-values the duty under suspension: `duty_suspended_value_cents` is the item's `on_hand_qty` multiplied by the product's `unit_price_cents`, multiplied by the sum of the product's `duty_rate_bps` and `vat_rate_bps`, divided by 10000 and rounded half up to whole cents. The stock item's `version` increments once per appending request.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties and Period Sealing

Two governance invariants cut across the audit and settlement workflows and are worth stating once, in general terms, before the narratives that use them.

The first is separation of preparer and adjudicator. A customs audit stores the operator identifier that opened it in `preparer_user_id`; the identifier that later requests more information about the count, or closes it, is compared against that value and must differ, otherwise the request fails with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (409). The closing identifier is retained in `auditor_user_id`, so every closed audit names two distinct people.

The second is that a settled period is sealed against retroactive change. Once a concession agreement's period has been closed, the turnover figure that produced its rent is final: a basket that was handed over inside that period can no longer be voided, and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_VOID_AFTER_PERIOD_CLOSE` (409). The reverse dependency also holds — a customs audit may only be closed once the agreement covering its terminal and period has itself been closed — so shrinkage assessment always happens against a period whose commercial figures have stopped moving.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Commercial Estate: Terminals, Brands, Exclusivity and Arrival Allowances

Everything begins with a terminal. A `TerminalAdmin` registers each airport terminal the operator holds concession space in, supplying the `iata_code` of the airport (exactly three uppercase letters), an operator-facing `name`, and the `country_code` of the country the terminal physically sits in (exactly two uppercase letters, ISO 3166-1 alpha-2). Two further inputs are optional and describe how the terminal treats connecting passengers: `sealed_bag_regime`, either `ICAO_STEB` or `NONE`, which defaults to `ICAO_STEB`; and `liquid_limit_ml`, the maximum aggregate millilitres of liquid goods one sealed bag may hold, which must not be negative and defaults to 1000 — a full litre, the volume of a typical single-bottle purchase plus a fragrance. A freshly registered terminal has an empty `traveler_nationality_mix` object, a `scanned_itinerary_count_30d` of 0 and an `unaccounted_duty_liability_cents` of 0; all three are maintained by the server and never supplied by a client. That last field is the operator's running exposure at the terminal: the sum of the duty assessed by every customs audit of the terminal that closed with an assessment.

Supplier brands are registered next, each with a trading `name` and exactly one merchandise `category` drawn from `LIQUOR`, `TOBACCO`, `FRAGRANCE`, `CONFECTIONERY` and `ELECTRONICS`. A brand's `status` starts at `ACTIVE` with `suspended_at` null. When a supply failure or a compliance breach makes a brand unsellable, a `TerminalAdmin` suspends it, moving the brand `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` and stamping `suspended_at` with the logical clock; suspending a brand that is already `SUSPENDED` is refused with `ERR_BRAND_ALREADY_SUSPENDED`. Suspension is not a quiet flag flip: **every** product of that brand whose assortment standing is `LISTED` is delisted as part of the same request, processed in the products' `created_at` ascending order, each moving `LISTED → DELISTED` with its `listed_at` cleared back to null. The brand's `version` increments by 1 and so does the `version` of each product the cascade touched. Because `SUSPENDED` is terminal for a brand and `DELISTED` is terminal for a product, this is a one-way door: neither a suspended brand nor a delisted product is ever restored. Brand trading names are expected to be unique across brands as an operational convention, but no dedicated rejection code is defined for a repeat.

Exclusivity is how brands buy shelf dominance. A grant names a `brand_id`, the `terminal_id` it applies at, the `category` it covers, and a `scope`: `TERMINAL_EXCLUSIVE`, which blocks products of every other brand in that category from being admitted to the assortment at that terminal, or `CATEGORY_PREFERRED`, which records the commercial deal without blocking anybody. Its validity is the half-open interval running from `effective_from` inclusive to `effective_to` exclusive, and `effective_to` must be strictly later than `effective_from`. Two `ACTIVE` grants for the same terminal and category may not have intersecting intervals: an intersection exists when the stored grant's `effective_from` is earlier than the new grant's `effective_to` **and** the new grant's `effective_from` is earlier than the stored grant's `effective_to`. A new grant whose `effective_from` is exactly the stored grant's `effective_to` therefore abuts it without intersecting and is accepted, while any true overlap is refused with `ERR_EXCLUSIVITY_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. Grants already in `REVOKED` standing are ignored by this check entirely. A grant's `status` starts `ACTIVE` with `revoked_at` null; a `TerminalAdmin` may move it `ACTIVE → REVOKED`, which stamps `revoked_at` with the logical clock, increments the grant's `version`, and withdraws the grant from both the overlap check and the assortment block, freeing the terminal-and-category window for another brand. Revoking a grant that is not `ACTIVE` is refused with `ERR_GRANT_NOT_ACTIVE`.

The last piece of reference data is the arrival allowance. For each pairing of an arrival `destination_country` and a merchandise `category`, a `TerminalAdmin` publishes the number of units a single passenger may import duty-free — `allowance_units`, which may be zero but never negative — together with the `advisory_text` that must be shown verbatim to the traveller. Only one rule is operative per pairing: publishing a new rule flips any existing `ACTIVE` rule for the same `destination_country` and `category` to `SUPERSEDED`, stamps that rule's `superseded_at` with the logical clock, and — since this is a mutation of a stored resource like any other — refreshes its `updated_at` and increments its `version`. The new rule is created `ACTIVE` with `superseded_at` null. Superseded rules are retained for audit but are invisible to every exemption calculation; only `ACTIVE` rules are ever consulted.

### 3.2. Admitting Products into the Assortment

A product is created under a brand and is not sellable when it is created. The `TerminalAdmin` supplies the owning `brand_id`, a `sku` code, the merchandise `category`, the shelf price of one unit as `unit_price_cents` (strictly greater than zero), and the two levies that apply to any unit that falls outside a traveller's allowance: `duty_rate_bps`, the import duty rate, and `vat_rate_bps`, the value-added tax rate, each between 0 and 10000 basis points inclusive. Also required is `target_nationality_code`, the two-uppercase-letter nationality the SKU is assorted for. Optionally the admin may declare the product a liquid by setting `is_liquid` to true — it defaults to false — in which case `liquid_ml`, the millilitres of liquid in one unit, must be supplied and greater than zero; for a non-liquid product `liquid_ml` stays null. Also optional is `required_nationality_share_bps`, the minimum share of the terminal's traveller mix the target nationality must represent before the SKU may be sold, which defaults to 1500 basis points — fifteen percent of recently scanned travellers — and must lie between 0 and 10000. A repeat `sku` is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_SKU`, and a `category` that differs from the owning brand's own `category` is refused with `ERR_PRODUCT_CATEGORY_MISMATCH`. Every new product starts with `assortment_status` of `PENDING` and `listed_at` null.

Admission into the assortment is a governed act with four gates, and the `TerminalAdmin` who requests it is asserting that all four hold. The product must still be `PENDING`, otherwise `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_PENDING`. Its owning brand must be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_BRAND_SUSPENDED`. No exclusivity may bar it: if any terminal that holds a bonded stock ledger for this product is covered, at the current logical instant, by a grant that is `ACTIVE`, whose `scope` is `TERMINAL_EXCLUSIVE`, whose `category` equals the product's `category`, and whose `brand_id` is some brand other than the product's own — where "covers the current instant" means the grant's `effective_from` is at or before the logical clock and its `effective_to` is strictly after it — the request is refused with `ERR_EXCLUSIVITY_CONFLICT`. Finally, the traveller mix must justify the SKU: at **every** terminal that holds a bonded stock ledger for this product, the share recorded in that terminal's `traveler_nationality_mix` for the product's `target_nationality_code` must be at least `required_nationality_share_bps`. A nationality absent from the mix map counts as 0 basis points, and the comparison is inclusive, so a share exactly equal to the requirement passes. A terminal that falls short refuses the admission with `ERR_NATIONALITY_MIX_BELOW_THRESHOLD`. When all four gates hold, the product moves `PENDING → LISTED`, `listed_at` is stamped with the logical clock and the product's `version` increments by 1.

The traveller mix that gates admission is not configured; it is observed. It is recomputed for a terminal every time a boarding pass is successfully scanned there, over a rolling window of the last 30 days measured from the current logical clock. The pool is every passenger itinerary whose `departure_terminal_id` is that terminal, whose `scan_status` is either `SCANNED` or `AMENDED`, and whose `scanned_at` falls at or after the instant 30 days before the clock and at or before the clock itself — both ends of the window are inclusive. The terminal's `scanned_itinerary_count_30d` is the size of that pool. When the pool is empty, `traveler_nationality_mix` is an empty object. Otherwise it maps each distinct `nationality_code` appearing in the pool to that nationality's share in basis points, computed as the count of pool members with that nationality multiplied by 10000, divided by the pool size, and floored to a whole number of basis points. Never-scanned itineraries are excluded from the pool by construction, so an unscanned traveller cannot influence an assortment decision. A `TerminalAdmin` can also read the mix on demand for a terminal; that read recomputes the figures at the current logical clock without mutating anything and answers with `window_days` (the 30-day lookback), `scanned_itinerary_count` (the pool size) and `mix_bps` (the nationality-to-share map).

### 3.3. Bonded Stock Under Duty Suspension

Goods held for duty-free sale sit in a bonded warehouse under suspension of duty and tax, and the operator's licence depends on being able to account for every unit. A `StockController` opens one bonded ledger per pairing of a `terminal_id` and a `product_id`; a second ledger for a pairing that already has one is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_STOCK_ITEM`. A newly opened ledger has `on_hand_qty`, `reserved_qty`, `available_qty` and `duty_suspended_value_cents` all at 0, `last_reconciled_at` null, and a `version` of 1.

Only two kinds of entry may be posted by hand, and both are posted by a `StockController` who names the `stock_item_id`, the `movement_type` and the signed `qty_delta`. A `RECEIPT` records goods arriving into bond and must carry a positive `qty_delta`. An `AUDIT_ADJUSTMENT` records a controller-initiated correction and may carry either sign. Every other movement type — `SALE_ISSUE`, `RESERVATION_HOLD`, `RESERVATION_RELEASE`, `SHRINKAGE_WRITE_OFF` and `REVERSAL` — is synthesised by the aggregate that owns it and may never be posted directly; an attempt to do so, like a `qty_delta` of zero or a non-positive `RECEIPT`, is refused as a malformed request with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A manual entry that would drive the item's `on_hand_qty` below zero is refused with `ERR_BONDED_STOCK_INSUFFICIENT`. An accepted entry is stamped with `affects` of `ON_HAND`, `posted_by_user_id` taken from the acting operator identifier, `source_ref_type` of `MANUAL` and `source_ref_id` of null, and it carries the `agreement_period_code` of whichever concession agreement of the item's terminal was active at that instant — null if none was. The stock item's derived quantities and its `duty_suspended_value_cents` are then re-derived from the whole ledger and its `version` increments by 1.

A manual entry is never edited or deleted. When a controller needs to undo one, they compensate it: the server appends a fresh entry of type `REVERSAL` whose `qty_delta` is the exact negation of the original's, whose `affects` copies the original's dimension, whose `reversal_of_movement_id` points at the original entry, and whose `source_ref_type` is `MANUAL` with a null `source_ref_id`. The original entry's `reversed` flag flips to true, which is the only mutation an entry ever undergoes, and the stock item's derived quantities, `duty_suspended_value_cents` and `version` are updated exactly as for any other appended entry. Compensation is confined to entries the controller could have posted in the first place: only a `RECEIPT` or an `AUDIT_ADJUSTMENT` that has not already been reversed may be compensated this way, and anything else — a `SALE_ISSUE`, a `RESERVATION_HOLD`, a `RESERVATION_RELEASE`, a `SHRINKAGE_WRITE_OFF`, a `REVERSAL`, or an entry whose `reversed` flag is already true — is refused with `ERR_MOVEMENT_NOT_REVERSIBLE`. Movements owned by another aggregate are undone only by that aggregate's own compensating action, described in the workflows below. A compensation that would drive `on_hand_qty` below zero is likewise refused with `ERR_BONDED_STOCK_INSUFFICIENT`. Because a ledger entry is created rather than modified, a compensation is reported with the 201 status of a new resource.

### 3.4. Passenger Itineraries and Boarding-Pass Verification

Sales eligibility is a property of the journey, not of the shopper, so the till workflow starts by recording the journey. A `SalesAssociate` registers an itinerary with the operator's own `passenger_ref` for the traveller, the `nationality_code` shown on the travel document, the `boarding_pass_barcode` payload printed on the pass, the `departure_terminal_id` the traveller departs from, the scheduled `departure_utc` of the first leg, the `destination_country` the first leg arrives in, and the `final_destination_country` the journey ultimately ends in. When the traveller connects onward, `has_connection` is set to true — it defaults to false — and the `connection_country` in which the onward leg is taken and the scheduled `connection_departure_utc` of that leg are supplied; both stay null for a non-connecting journey, and when `has_connection` is false the `final_destination_country` must equal the `destination_country`, a discrepancy being refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Both the `boarding_pass_barcode` and the `passenger_ref` must be unique across itineraries; a repeat of either is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_BOARDING_PASS`. The server derives `is_international` as true exactly when the `destination_country` differs from the `country_code` of the departure terminal, and a new itinerary starts with `scan_status` of `UNSCANNED`, `scanned_at` null and `amend_count` at 0.

Verification is the act of scanning the pass at the till. The associate submits the `boarding_pass_barcode` read off the physical pass and the server compares it to the recorded value; a mismatch is refused with `ERR_BOARDING_PASS_MISMATCH`. A scan is admissible only from `UNSCANNED` or from `AMENDED` — scanning an itinerary that is already `SCANNED` is refused with `ERR_ITINERARY_SCAN_STATE_INVALID` — the journey must be international, else `ERR_ITINERARY_NOT_INTERNATIONAL`, and the logical clock must be strictly before `departure_utc`, else `ERR_DEPARTURE_PASSED`. A successful scan moves the itinerary `UNSCANNED → SCANNED` on a first pass or `AMENDED → SCANNED` on a re-verification, stamps `scanned_at` with the logical clock, recomputes the departure terminal's `traveler_nationality_mix` and `scanned_itinerary_count_30d` as described in §3.2, and increments the itinerary's `version` and the terminal's `version` by 1 each.

Routings change, and an amendment is deliberately expensive. An amendment is admissible only from `SCANNED` — attempting it from `UNSCANNED` or `AMENDED` is refused with `ERR_ITINERARY_SCAN_STATE_INVALID` — and only while the logical clock is strictly before `departure_utc`, else `ERR_DEPARTURE_PASSED`. The associate supplies the new `destination_country` and `final_destination_country`, and may additionally supply `has_connection`, `connection_country`, `connection_departure_utc` and a revised `departure_utc`; each supplied value overwrites the stored one. The server then recomputes `is_international` from the new `destination_country`, increments `amend_count` by 1, clears `scanned_at` back to null, moves the itinerary `SCANNED → AMENDED`, and increments its `version` by 1. It also refreshes the departure terminal's `traveler_nationality_mix` and `scanned_itinerary_count_30d` and increments the terminal's `version` by 1; because the amended itinerary's `scanned_at` is now null, that itinerary drops out of the mix pool until it is scanned again. Re-scanning closes the cycle `SCANNED → AMENDED → SCANNED`, and `amend_count` is never reset, so the number of amendments over a journey is permanently visible.

The expensive part is the compensation. Every basket of that itinerary that had already been cleared for sale is unwound in the same request: each such sale reverts `ELIGIBILITY_CLEARED → CART` on its till dimension, its `exempt_amount_cents` and `duty_liability_cents` return to 0, every one of its lines has `allowance_excess_qty`, `line_exempt_cents` and `line_duty_cents` reset to 0, and `eligibility_variant`, `effective_destination_country` and `cleared_at` are cleared to null, `arrival_advisory` back to an empty list and `sealed_bag_required` back to false. Each affected sale's `version` increments by 1. The sale's duty dimension is deliberately untouched by this compensation: `duty_status` stays exactly where it was, at `SUSPENDED`. Baskets still in `CART` are unaffected, and a basket already handed over or voided is beyond compensation and is left alone.

### 3.5. Building the Basket and Pricing the Exemption

A basket is opened by a `SalesAssociate` against a recorded itinerary, naming the `itinerary_id` and the `channel` the basket originated in — `IN_STORE` for a walk-up purchase or `PRE_ORDER` for one that came out of a reservation. Two conditions gate the opening: the journey must be international, else `ERR_ITINERARY_NOT_INTERNATIONAL`, and the departure terminal must have a concession agreement in `ACTIVE` standing, else `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE` — without a live agreement there is nothing for the turnover to accrue against. The server copies `terminal_id` from the itinerary's `departure_terminal_id` and `agreement_id` from that terminal's one `ACTIVE` agreement. A new basket carries `fulfillment_status` of `CART` on its till dimension and `duty_status` of `SUSPENDED` on its customs dimension, with `gross_amount_cents`, `exempt_amount_cents` and `duty_liability_cents` at 0, `effective_destination_country`, `eligibility_variant`, `cleared_at`, `picked_up_at` and `voided_at` all null, `arrival_advisory` an empty list and `sealed_bag_required` false.

Lines are appended one product at a time. The associate supplies the `product_id`, the `qty` of units (strictly positive) and the `expected_sale_version` guard described in §2.7. The basket must not already be voided, else `ERR_SALE_ALREADY_TERMINAL`, and must still be in `CART`, else `ERR_SALE_NOT_IN_CART`. The product must be in `LISTED` standing, else `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_LISTED` — a `PENDING` SKU has never been admitted and a `DELISTED` one has been withdrawn, and neither may be sold. A bonded ledger for that product must exist at the basket's own terminal, else `ERR_TERMINAL_MISMATCH`; the operator cannot sell from a warehouse it is not standing in. That ledger's `available_qty` must be at least the requested `qty`, else `ERR_BONDED_STOCK_INSUFFICIENT`. An accepted line snapshots `unit_price_cents` from the product at the moment of creation — a later price change never rewrites a line already in a basket — sets `line_total_cents` to the `qty` multiplied by that snapshotted price, and starts `allowance_excess_qty`, `line_exempt_cents` and `line_duty_cents` at 0. The basket's `gross_amount_cents` is then re-derived as the sum of its lines' `line_total_cents`, and the basket's `version` increments by 1. Appending a line does **not** move any bonded stock: it reads `available_qty` but writes no ledger entry, so nothing is issued and nothing is reserved until the goods are handed over.

Clearing eligibility is the moment the till commits to a duty position. It is admissible only on a basket that has not been voided (`ERR_SALE_ALREADY_TERMINAL`) and is still in `CART` (`ERR_SALE_NOT_IN_CART`), that has at least one line (`ERR_EMPTY_BASKET`), whose itinerary has been verified — `scan_status` of `SCANNED` or `AMENDED`, else `ERR_ITINERARY_NOT_SCANNED` — whose journey is still international, else `ERR_ITINERARY_NOT_INTERNATIONAL`, and whose logical clock is still strictly before the itinerary's `departure_utc`, else `ERR_DEPARTURE_PASSED`. Finally an `ACTIVE` arrival-allowance rule must exist for the effective destination and for **every** distinct product category present in the basket; a single category without a rule refuses the whole request with `ERR_ALLOWANCE_RULE_MISSING`. On success the basket moves `CART → ELIGIBILITY_CLEARED`, `cleared_at` is stamped with the logical clock, and the basket's `version` increments by 1. Its `duty_status` is deliberately left at `SUSPENDED`: this transition advances the till dimension only, and the goods remain under duty suspension until they physically leave the store.

The pricing itself is a **per-category allowance waterfall**, and it is evaluated twice over a basket's life with a single difference between the two runs — which arrival country the allowance is looked up for. Under the `SUSPENDED_ESTIMATE` variant, run when eligibility is cleared, the effective destination is the itinerary's `destination_country`, the arrival country of the first leg, and any onward connection is ignored entirely. Under the `EXEMPT_CONFIRMED_FINAL` variant, run at handover, the effective destination is the itinerary's `final_destination_country` when `has_connection` is true and its `destination_country` otherwise. Because a connecting traveller's two countries can carry different allowances, the identical basket can move from partly dutiable to fully exempt, or the reverse, between the two runs with no input field having changed.

Within a run, the basket's lines are processed strictly in ascending `created_at` order — never in identifier order — and allowance units are consumed by the basket's own lines as they go:

1. For the line's product, find the arrival-allowance rule in `ACTIVE` standing whose `destination_country` equals the effective destination and whose `category` equals the product's `category`. At most one such rule exists, since publishing a new one supersedes the old; rules in `SUPERSEDED` standing are never eligible. If no such rule exists, the whole run fails with `ERR_ALLOWANCE_RULE_MISSING`.
2. Determine how much of that category's allowance the earlier lines already consumed: the sum of `qty` over the lines of the same basket whose product shares this product's `category` and whose `created_at` is strictly earlier than this line's.
3. Add this line's own `qty` to that already-consumed figure. If the result does not exceed the rule's `allowance_units`, the line's `allowance_excess_qty` is 0. Otherwise the excess is the amount by which the result exceeds `allowance_units`, capped at this line's own `qty` — a line can never contribute more excess units than it contains.
4. Set the line's `line_total_cents` to its `qty` multiplied by its snapshotted `unit_price_cents`, and its `line_exempt_cents` to the units that stayed inside the allowance — the `qty` less the excess — multiplied by that same snapshotted price.
5. Set the line's `line_duty_cents` to the excess units multiplied by the snapshotted `unit_price_cents`, multiplied by the sum of the product's `duty_rate_bps` and `vat_rate_bps`, divided by 10000 and rounded half up to whole cents. This rounding happens here, on the line, and is not deferred.

Once every line has been priced, the basket's `gross_amount_cents` is the sum of the lines' `line_total_cents`, its `exempt_amount_cents` the sum of their `line_exempt_cents`, and its `duty_liability_cents` the sum of their **already-rounded** `line_duty_cents`. The basket's `effective_destination_country` records the arrival country the run keyed on, and `eligibility_variant` records which of `SUSPENDED_ESTIMATE` and `EXEMPT_CONFIRMED_FINAL` produced the figures now stored. The basket's `arrival_advisory` is rebuilt as one entry per distinct product `category` present in the basket, each entry the `advisory_text` of that category's applied rule, ordered by category name in ascending alphabetical order.

Clearing eligibility also decides whether the traveller will need a tamper-evident bag. `sealed_bag_required` is set to true when three things hold together: the departure terminal's `sealed_bag_regime` is `ICAO_STEB`, the itinerary's `has_connection` is true, and at least one line's product has `is_liquid` true. If any of the three fails — the terminal's regime is `NONE`, the journey is direct, or nothing in the basket is a liquid — `sealed_bag_required` is false and no bag is needed at handover.

### 3.6. Sealed Bags for Connecting Passengers

A traveller who buys a litre of spirits in one airport and changes planes in another must satisfy the transfer airport's security screening, and the international answer is the tamper-evident bag: the goods are sealed at the point of sale, the seal is inspected at the connection, and an intact seal means the liquids may travel on. A `SalesAssociate` issues one by naming the `sale_id` and the printed `seal_number`; the seal serial must be unique across all bags, and a repeat is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_SEAL_NUMBER`. A bag may only be issued for a basket whose till dimension is `ELIGIBILITY_CLEARED`, else `ERR_SALE_NOT_CLEARED`.

Before the compliance checks run, the server computes the bag's contents and its validity window. `total_liquid_ml` is the sum, over the basket's lines whose product has `is_liquid` true, of the line's `qty` multiplied by that product's `liquid_ml`; lines whose product is not a liquid contribute nothing. `expires_at` is the itinerary's `departure_utc` advanced by the seal validity period of 96 hours — four full days, which is the window the operator's seals are certified for. Two checks then apply, both of them inclusive at the boundary. The volume must not exceed the departure terminal's `liquid_limit_ml`; a bag whose `total_liquid_ml` is exactly equal to the limit is accepted, and one over it is refused with `ERR_LIQUID_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. And when the itinerary's `has_connection` is true, the onward leg must depart no later than the seal's expiry: a `connection_departure_utc` at or before `expires_at` is accepted, and one strictly after it is refused with `ERR_SEAL_WINDOW_EXPIRED`, because the seal would already have lapsed by the time the transfer screening looked at it. A direct journey skips the onward-leg check altogether. An accepted bag copies `terminal_id` from the basket, stamps `issued_at` with the logical clock, starts in `ISSUED` standing with `verified_at` null, and is reported with the 201 status of a new resource.

Inspection is recorded with a single `outcome` field, and it forks the bag's lifecycle. Either outcome requires the bag to still be in `ISSUED` standing — a bag already verified, tampered or voided is refused with `ERR_BAG_NOT_ISSUED` — requires the owning basket's till dimension to be `ELIGIBILITY_CLEARED`, else `ERR_SALE_NOT_CLEARED`, and requires the logical clock to be at or before the bag's `expires_at`, the boundary again being inclusive, else `ERR_SEAL_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. An `outcome` of `INTACT` moves the bag `ISSUED → INTACT_VERIFIED`, stamps `verified_at` with the logical clock and increments the bag's `version` by 1; nothing else in the system changes, and the basket is now clear to be handed over. An `outcome` of `TAMPERED` moves the bag `ISSUED → TAMPERED`, stamps `verified_at` with the logical clock and increments the bag's `version` by 1 — and then forfeits the traveller's allowance.

The forfeiture is the sharpest illustration of the two dimensions being independent. When the basket's `duty_status` is `SUSPENDED`, a tampered seal moves that dimension `SUSPENDED → DUTY_LIABLE` while leaving the till dimension exactly where it was, at `ELIGIBILITY_CLEARED`. Every line of the basket has its `allowance_excess_qty` set to the line's full `qty`, its `line_exempt_cents` set to 0, and its `line_duty_cents` recomputed as the full `qty` multiplied by the snapshotted `unit_price_cents`, multiplied by the sum of the product's `duty_rate_bps` and `vat_rate_bps`, divided by 10000 and rounded half up to whole cents. The basket's `exempt_amount_cents` becomes 0 and its `duty_liability_cents` becomes the sum of the recomputed per-line figures. The basket's `version` increments by 1 alongside the bag's. Nothing about the basket's till standing has changed — but because a tampered bag is not an `INTACT_VERIFIED` one, a basket that required a bag can no longer be handed over, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_SEALED_BAG_REQUIRED`.

Bags do not survive their basket. When a basket is voided, every bag of that basket standing in `ISSUED`, `INTACT_VERIFIED` or `TAMPERED` moves to `VOIDED` and has its `version` incremented by 1; a bag that was still merely `ISSUED` also has `verified_at` stamped with the logical clock, since it was null, while a bag already inspected keeps the `verified_at` it earned. `INTACT_VERIFIED`, `TAMPERED` and `VOIDED` are all terminal for a bag; only a fresh bag, with a fresh `seal_number`, can put a basket back in a position to be handed over.

### 3.7. Handover at the Gate, and Compensating a Void

Handover is where duty suspension ends and the operator's commercial position is struck. The basket must not be voided (`ERR_SALE_ALREADY_TERMINAL`), its till dimension must be `ELIGIBILITY_CLEARED` (`ERR_SALE_NOT_CLEARED`), its itinerary must still be verified with a `scan_status` of `SCANNED` or `AMENDED` (`ERR_ITINERARY_NOT_SCANNED`), and the logical clock must still be strictly before `departure_utc` (`ERR_DEPARTURE_PASSED`) — goods cannot be handed over after the aircraft has left. When the basket's `sealed_bag_required` is true, a bag of that basket in `INTACT_VERIFIED` standing must exist, else `ERR_SEALED_BAG_REQUIRED`. An `ACTIVE` arrival-allowance rule must exist for the final effective destination and every distinct category in the basket, else `ERR_ALLOWANCE_RULE_MISSING` — and note that this is a different lookup from the one that passed at eligibility clearance, because a connecting traveller's allowance is now keyed on the final leg. Lastly, every line must still be coverable: for each line, the bonded ledger of that product at the basket's terminal must have an `available_qty` of at least the line's `qty`, else `ERR_BONDED_STOCK_INSUFFICIENT`.

A successful handover runs a four-level cascade, and each level exists because of the one above it. **First**, the accounting is finalised: `effective_destination_country` is set to the itinerary's `final_destination_country` when `has_connection` is true and to its `destination_country` otherwise, the allowance waterfall of §3.5 is re-run under the `EXEMPT_CONFIRMED_FINAL` variant — overwriting every line's `allowance_excess_qty`, `line_exempt_cents` and `line_duty_cents` and the basket's `exempt_amount_cents` and `duty_liability_cents` — `eligibility_variant` becomes `EXEMPT_CONFIRMED_FINAL`, `picked_up_at` is stamped with the logical clock, and the till dimension moves `ELIGIBILITY_CLEARED → PICKED_UP`. The duty dimension resolves at the same moment: `duty_status` becomes `EXEMPT_CONFIRMED` when the recomputed `duty_liability_cents` is exactly 0, and `DUTY_LIABLE` when any duty remains. **Second**, because the duty dimension has left `SUSPENDED`, the goods must leave bond: one `SALE_ISSUE` ledger entry is appended per line, processed in the lines' ascending `created_at` order, each with a `qty_delta` of the negated line `qty`, an `affects` of `ON_HAND`, a `source_ref_type` of `SALE`, a `source_ref_id` of the basket's identifier and an `agreement_period_code` of the `period_code` of the terminal's `ACTIVE` agreement. Each affected bonded item's `on_hand_qty` falls accordingly, its `available_qty` and `duty_suspended_value_cents` are re-derived, and its `version` increments by 1. **Third**, because those entries carry an `agreement_period_code`, the concession ledger moves: the agreement's `gross_sales_cents` increases by the basket's `gross_amount_cents` and its `percentage_rent_cents` is recomputed. **Fourth**, because `percentage_rent_cents` changed, the agreement's `rent_basis` is re-derived, becoming `PERCENTAGE` when `percentage_rent_cents` is greater than or equal to `minimum_guarantee_cents` and `MINIMUM_GUARANTEE` otherwise. The basket's `version`, each touched bonded item's `version` and the agreement's `version` each increment by 1.

Voiding is available from all three non-terminal till states and always produces the same visible outcome: the till dimension moves to `VOIDED` — from `CART`, from `ELIGIBILITY_CLEARED`, or from `PICKED_UP` — the duty dimension moves to `REVERSED`, `voided_at` is stamped with the logical clock, `exempt_amount_cents` and `duty_liability_cents` are both set to 0, and every sealed bag of the basket is voided as described in §3.6. `gross_amount_cents` is deliberately left intact, so a voided basket still shows what it would have been worth. The basket's `version` increments by 1. A second void of an already-voided basket is refused with `ERR_SALE_ALREADY_TERMINAL`.

What differs is how much has to be given back. Voiding a basket still in `CART` or in `ELIGIBILITY_CLEARED` touches nothing outside the basket and its bags: no bonded stock had been issued, and no turnover had accrued. Voiding a basket that had already been handed over is a full compensation, and it is only permitted while the basket's concession agreement is still `ACTIVE`; once that period has been closed the settled rent may not be disturbed and the void is refused with `ERR_VOID_AFTER_PERIOD_CLOSE`. When it is permitted, every `SALE_ISSUE` entry whose `source_ref_id` is this basket and whose `reversed` flag is still false is compensated, processed in the originals' ascending `created_at` order: each produces a `REVERSAL` entry whose `qty_delta` is the exact negation of the original's, whose `affects` is `ON_HAND`, whose `reversal_of_movement_id` names the original, and whose `source_ref_type` is `SALE` with a `source_ref_id` of the basket's identifier; the original's `reversed` flag flips to true. Each affected bonded item's `on_hand_qty` is restored, its `available_qty` and `duty_suspended_value_cents` re-derived, and its `version` incremented by 1. Because those compensating entries carry the same `agreement_period_code` as the originals, the agreement's `gross_sales_cents` decreases by the basket's `gross_amount_cents`, its `percentage_rent_cents` is recomputed from the reduced turnover, its `rent_basis` is re-derived from that new figure, and its `version` increments by 1. Both conserved quantities — bonded units on hand and accrued turnover — end exactly where they stood before the handover.

### 3.8. Pre-Orders and the Pick-Up-on-Return Programme

Travellers who know what they want book it before they fly, and the operator holds the units for them. A reservation is placed by the `Passenger` themselves, naming their own `itinerary_id`, the `stock_item_id` of the bonded ledger the units come out of, the `qty` of units wanted, the `program` — `PICKUP_ON_DEPARTURE` for collection on the outbound journey, `PICKUP_ON_RETURN` for collection when the traveller comes back — the closed collection window bounded by `reservation_window_start` and `reservation_window_end` inclusive at both ends, and the `expected_stock_item_version` guard of §2.7. The reserved product must be in `LISTED` standing, else `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_LISTED`, and the bonded ledger must sit at the traveller's own departure terminal — the stock item's `terminal_id` must equal the itinerary's `departure_terminal_id` — else `ERR_TERMINAL_MISMATCH`.

Windows are policed in two ways. The window must be structurally sound: `reservation_window_end` strictly later than `reservation_window_start`, checked as part of request well-formedness. It must also be positioned sensibly relative to the flight, and here the `program` decides: the window may not start in the logical past, so `reservation_window_start` must be at or after the current logical clock; a `PICKUP_ON_DEPARTURE` reservation must be collectable before the aircraft leaves, so its `reservation_window_end` must be at or before the itinerary's `departure_utc`; and a `PICKUP_ON_RETURN` reservation is by definition collected after the outbound flight, so its `reservation_window_start` must be at or after `departure_utc`. Any of these three failing is refused with `ERR_RESERVATION_WINDOW_INVALID`.

Capacity is then checked over a **buffered** set of windows rather than the requested window alone, because warehouse staff need slack around a collection slot to pick and stage the goods. The conflicting reservations are those on the same bonded item that are still in `RESERVED` standing and whose window comes within one day of the requested one: precisely, those whose `reservation_window_start` is at or before the requested `reservation_window_end` advanced by 24 hours, and whose `reservation_window_end` is at or after the requested `reservation_window_start` set back by 24 hours. The sum of the `qty` of those conflicting reservations plus the requested `qty` must not exceed the bonded item's `on_hand_qty`; note that the comparison is against units physically in bond, not against `available_qty`. Exceeding it is refused with `ERR_RESERVATION_CAPACITY_OVERLAP`.

An accepted reservation is created in `RESERVED` standing with `converted_sale_id` and `closed_at` null, and it takes a real hold on the ledger: a `RESERVATION_HOLD` entry is appended with a `qty_delta` of the positive `qty`, an `affects` of `RESERVED`, a `source_ref_type` of `PRE_ORDER` and a `source_ref_id` of the reservation's own identifier. The bonded item's `reserved_qty` rises by that quantity, its `available_qty` and `duty_suspended_value_cents` are re-derived, and its `version` increments by 1 — which is exactly why the next client to reserve against that item needs a fresh `version` to quote.

A reservation leaves `RESERVED` in one of three ways, and every one of them releases the hold. **Conversion** is performed by a `StockController` at collection time and turns the reservation into a real basket. It requires the reservation to be `RESERVED` (`ERR_PREORDER_NOT_RESERVED`), the traveller's itinerary to be verified with a `scan_status` of `SCANNED` or `AMENDED` (`ERR_ITINERARY_NOT_SCANNED`), the logical clock to fall inside the collection window with both bounds inclusive (`ERR_RESERVATION_WINDOW_INVALID`), the terminal to have a concession agreement in `ACTIVE` standing (`ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`) and the reserved product to still be `LISTED` (`ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_LISTED`). The server then creates one basket with a `channel` of `PRE_ORDER`, the reservation's `itinerary_id`, a `terminal_id` taken from the bonded item's terminal, an `agreement_id` of that terminal's `ACTIVE` agreement, a `fulfillment_status` of `CART` and a `duty_status` of `SUSPENDED`; adds one line to it for the reserved product and `qty`, snapshotting `unit_price_cents` and setting `line_total_cents`; sets the basket's `gross_amount_cents` to that line total; appends a `RESERVATION_RELEASE` entry with a `qty_delta` of the negated `qty`, an `affects` of `RESERVED`, a `source_ref_type` of `PRE_ORDER` and a `source_ref_id` of the reservation; and moves the reservation `RESERVED → CONVERTED`, recording the new basket in `converted_sale_id` and stamping `closed_at` with the logical clock. The hold is released precisely so the units are issued once — by the basket at handover — and never counted twice. The reservation's `version` and the bonded item's `version` each increment by 1.

**Cancellation** is the traveller's own act: a `Passenger` may cancel a reservation that traces back to their own itinerary, and only while it is still `RESERVED`. It moves the reservation `RESERVED → CANCELLED`, stamps `closed_at` with the logical clock, and appends a `RESERVATION_RELEASE` entry with a `qty_delta` of the negated `qty` and an `affects` of `RESERVED`, restoring `reserved_qty` to exactly its pre-reservation value and re-deriving `available_qty`. The reservation's `version` and the bonded item's `version` each increment by 1. **Release** is the involuntary third way: when a customs write-off leaves fewer units in bond than are spoken for, the operator releases reservations until the books balance again, as described in §3.9. `CONVERTED`, `CANCELLED` and `RELEASED` are all terminal, and converting or cancelling a reservation in any of them is refused with `ERR_PREORDER_NOT_RESERVED`.

### 3.9. Customs Audits, Shrinkage and Unaccounted-Goods Duty

Periodically the customs authority requires the operator to prove that the units it claims to hold under suspension are physically there. A `StockController` opens an audit against one bonded item, naming the `terminal_id` under audit, the `stock_item_id` being counted and the `period_code` of the settlement period the count reconciles, in `YYYY-MM` form. The server stamps `preparer_user_id` with the acting operator identifier and creates the audit in `DRAFT` standing with `counted_qty` and `expected_qty_snapshot` null, `shrinkage_qty`, `assessable_qty` and `assessed_duty_cents` at 0, `round_count` at 1, `auditor_user_id`, `info_request_note` and `closed_at` all null.

Submitting the count moves the audit into review. The controller declares `counted_qty`, the physical count, which may not be negative and may not exceed the audited item's current `on_hand_qty` — a count above the book figure is not shrinkage but a bookkeeping error, and it is refused with `ERR_COUNTED_QTY_EXCEEDS_EXPECTED`. A submit is admissible from `DRAFT` and from `INFO_REQUESTED` only; from anywhere else it is refused with `ERR_AUDIT_NOT_SUBMITTED`. The submit stores the declared `counted_qty` and freezes `expected_qty_snapshot` at the item's `on_hand_qty` **as of that instant**. On a first submit the audit moves `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` and `round_count` stays at 1; on a re-submit after an information request it moves `INFO_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED`, re-takes the snapshot from the item's current `on_hand_qty`, and increments `round_count` by 1. The audit's `version` increments by 1 either way.

The review cycle can repeat. A `CustomsAuditor` who is not the preparer may return a submitted count for more information, which stores the auditor's `info_request_note` and moves the audit `SUBMITTED → INFO_REQUESTED`, incrementing its `version` by 1; the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` when the acting operator identifier equals `preparer_user_id`, and with `ERR_AUDIT_NOT_SUBMITTED` when the audit is not currently `SUBMITTED`. The cycle `SUBMITTED → INFO_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED` may run any number of times, and `round_count` is the loop counter that survives it — which matters, because a finding that took three rounds to establish is priced differently from one accepted at the first look.

Closing the audit is the adjudication, and it forks. It requires the audit to be `SUBMITTED` (`ERR_AUDIT_NOT_SUBMITTED`), an operator identifier different from `preparer_user_id` (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`), and the concession agreement covering the audit's own `terminal_id` and `period_code` to exist and to be in `CLOSED` standing (`ERR_AUDIT_PERIOD_NOT_CLOSED`). The assessment is then computed in a pinned order, from the frozen snapshot rather than from live inventory, so that the write-off this close is about to perform cannot feed back into its own input:

1. The shrinkage is the frozen `expected_qty_snapshot` less the declared `counted_qty` when that difference is positive, and 0 when the count met or exceeded the snapshot. This is stored as `shrinkage_qty`.
2. The statutory tolerance is 2 percent of the frozen snapshot — the snapshot multiplied by 200 basis points, divided by 10000 and floored to a whole number of units. Breakage and evaporation inside this tolerance are not chargeable.
3. `assessable_qty` is the shrinkage in excess of that tolerance when positive, and 0 otherwise.
4. The per-unit levy on the audited product is its `unit_price_cents` multiplied by the sum of its `duty_rate_bps` and `vat_rate_bps`, divided by 10000 and rounded half up to whole cents.
5. A repeat-finding penalty applies when the count needed three or more rounds: the multiplier is 2 when the audit's `round_count` is at least 3, and 1 otherwise.
6. `assessed_duty_cents` is the `assessable_qty` multiplied by that per-unit levy, multiplied by the penalty multiplier, rounded half up to whole cents.

When `assessable_qty` comes out at 0 the audit closes clean: it moves `SUBMITTED → CLOSED_CLEAN`, records the computed `shrinkage_qty` and an `assessable_qty` of 0, leaves `assessed_duty_cents` at 0, sets `auditor_user_id` to the acting operator identifier and `closed_at` to the logical clock, and sets the audited item's `last_reconciled_at` to the logical clock. The audit's `version` and the bonded item's `version` each increment by 1. Nothing is written off and no duty is owed.

When `assessable_qty` is greater than 0 the audit closes with an assessment, and the side-effect set is strictly larger. The audit moves `SUBMITTED → CLOSED_ASSESSED`, recording `shrinkage_qty`, `assessable_qty` and `assessed_duty_cents`, setting `auditor_user_id` and `closed_at`, and the audited terminal's `unaccounted_duty_liability_cents` increases by the `assessed_duty_cents` just assessed. Because the goods are gone, they leave the books: a `SHRINKAGE_WRITE_OFF` entry is appended with a `qty_delta` of the negated **`shrinkage_qty`** — the whole missing quantity, not merely the assessable part — an `affects` of `ON_HAND`, a `source_ref_type` of `CUSTOMS_AUDIT` and a `source_ref_id` of the audit's identifier; the item's `on_hand_qty` falls, its `available_qty` and `duty_suspended_value_cents` are re-derived, and its `last_reconciled_at` is stamped with the logical clock. And because the write-off can leave more units reserved than physically held, reservations are released until the books balance: while the item's `reserved_qty` exceeds its `on_hand_qty`, the `RESERVED` pre-orders on that item are taken in ascending `created_at` order — the reservation's identifier plays no part in the ordering — and each is moved `RESERVED → RELEASED` with `closed_at` stamped from the logical clock and a `RESERVATION_RELEASE` entry appended carrying a `qty_delta` of that reservation's negated `qty` and an `affects` of `RESERVED`. The loop stops as soon as `reserved_qty` is less than or equal to `on_hand_qty`, so no more reservations are sacrificed than the shortfall requires. The audit's `version`, the terminal's `version` and the bonded item's `version` each increment by 1, as does the `version` of every released reservation. `CLOSED_CLEAN` and `CLOSED_ASSESSED` are both terminal for an audit.

### 3.10. Concession Settlement: Percentage Rent, Minimum Guarantees and Net Rent

Airport landlords do not let retail space for a flat fee; they take a percentage of turnover against a floor. A `TerminalAdmin` drafts one agreement per settlement period per terminal, supplying the `terminal_id`, the `period_code` label in `YYYY-MM` form, the inclusive `period_start_utc` and the exclusive `period_end_utc` (strictly later than the start), the `rent_pct_bps` percentage-of-sales rate and the `minimum_guarantee_cents` floor for the full period, which may be zero but never negative. A second agreement for a terminal and period that already has one is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_AGREEMENT_PERIOD`. A drafted agreement starts in `DRAFT` standing with `gross_sales_cents` and `percentage_rent_cents` at 0, `final_rent_cents`, `marketing_credit_cents`, `net_rent_cents` and `rent_basis` all null, and `closed_at` null.

Activation makes the agreement the one that turnover accrues against. It requires the agreement to be in `DRAFT` standing, else `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`, and it requires that no other agreement of the same terminal is already `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_AGREEMENT_ALREADY_ACTIVE` — at most one live agreement per terminal, which is why a basket can copy its `agreement_id` unambiguously. Activation moves the agreement `DRAFT → ACTIVE`, derives `rent_basis` as `PERCENTAGE` when `percentage_rent_cents` is greater than or equal to `minimum_guarantee_cents` and as `MINIMUM_GUARANTEE` otherwise, and increments the agreement's `version` by 1.

The turnover ledger is narrow by design. `gross_sales_cents` counts the `gross_amount_cents` of exactly those baskets whose `agreement_id` is this agreement and whose till dimension is `PICKED_UP`. Baskets still in `CART`, baskets merely `ELIGIBILITY_CLEARED`, and baskets that were voided are all excluded — only goods that physically left the store are concessionable turnover. `percentage_rent_cents` is that ledger multiplied by `rent_pct_bps`, divided by 10000 and rounded half up to whole cents, and it is re-derived every time the ledger moves.

Mid-period, a `TerminalAdmin` can read a **projection** of where the rent currently stands. The projection is computed at the current logical clock and mutates nothing. It first establishes the period's length in whole days: the difference between `period_end_utc` and `period_start_utc` in seconds, divided by 86400 seconds — one day — and floored, which is reported as `period_days`. It then establishes how much of the period has run: the difference between the logical clock and `period_start_utc` in seconds, divided by 86400 and floored, plus 1, so that the opening day counts as one elapsed day; that figure is raised to a minimum of 1 and then capped at `period_days`, and is reported as `elapsed_days`. The minimum guarantee is prorated across that fraction of the period — `minimum_guarantee_cents` multiplied by `elapsed_days`, divided by `period_days`, rounded half up to whole cents — and reported as `prorated_minimum_guarantee_cents`. The projected rent, reported as `provisional_rent_cents`, is the greater of `percentage_rent_cents` and `prorated_minimum_guarantee_cents`, and the accompanying `rent_basis` is `PERCENTAGE` when `percentage_rent_cents` is greater than or equal to `prorated_minimum_guarantee_cents` and `MINIMUM_GUARANTEE` otherwise. The read also echoes `gross_sales_cents` so the reader can see the turnover the projection was struck from.

Closing the period is the settlement, and it is where the proration disappears. A close requires the agreement to be `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`, and requires the logical clock to have reached or passed `period_end_utc`, else `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_ENDED`. The final rent, written to `final_rent_cents`, is the greater of `percentage_rent_cents` and the **full** `minimum_guarantee_cents`, with `rent_basis` re-derived as `PERCENTAGE` when `percentage_rent_cents` is greater than or equal to the full `minimum_guarantee_cents` and `MINIMUM_GUARANTEE` otherwise. An agreement that reported a `PERCENTAGE` basis all through the period can therefore flip to `MINIMUM_GUARANTEE` at close on identical turnover, and the rent rises accordingly — the projection is a forecast, not a promise.

Settlement then nets off the brand marketing contribution, in a strictly pinned order of evaluation. The tier is keyed on the **gross** figure — the `final_rent_cents` just written — and never on the net figure that comes out at the end: a final rent of at least 5,000,000 cents (50,000 in major currency units) earns 400 basis points, a final rent of at least 2,000,000 cents (20,000 in major currency units) but below the higher floor earns 250 basis points, and anything below that earns 100 basis points, each tier boundary being inclusive. `marketing_credit_cents` is `final_rent_cents` multiplied by the tier rate, divided by 10000 and rounded half up to whole cents. `net_rent_cents` is `final_rent_cents` less `marketing_credit_cents`. Re-evaluating the tier against `net_rent_cents` after the subtraction is forbidden, however circular the dependency looks from the outside. The close stamps `closed_at` with the logical clock, moves the agreement `ACTIVE → CLOSED`, and increments its `version` by 1. `CLOSED` is terminal: from that point the period's figures are frozen, which is what makes a handed-over basket inside it unvoidable and what allows a customs audit for that period to be adjudicated.
