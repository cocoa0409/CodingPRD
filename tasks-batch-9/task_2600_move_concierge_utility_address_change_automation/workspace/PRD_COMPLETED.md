# Move Concierge Utility Automation API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Moving house is the moment at which a household's entire service footprint has to be torn down and rebuilt inside a few days: electricity and gas transferred, water opened at the new address and closed at the old one, internet installed, mail redirected through the postal service, and a handful of commercial decisions (broadband plan, renter's insurance, moving crew) made under time pressure. Property managers and moving companies sit on the earliest reliable signal that a move is happening, but that signal arrives as an unstructured feed, frequently duplicated, occasionally cancelled and re-booked, and almost never accompanied by the identity evidence a utility or the postal service requires before it will act on an address change. The Move Concierge platform exists to turn that raw feed into a governed, auditable workflow.

This document specifies the API behind that platform. The system ingests move records from property-manager and mover integrations, screens them for duplicates and for cancelled predecessors worth reactivating, verifies the mover's identity before any address change leaves the building, resolves the destination postal code to the utility providers that actually serve it, orchestrates start and stop service orders through a credit-check and deposit handoff, files the postal change-of-address and waits for mail-forwarding confirmation, and places partner referrals whose commissions are attributed, revenue-shared back to the originating feed and settled or clawed back on an immutable ledger. Every one of those legs is reversible: a resident who withdraws data-sharing consent, a provider that reports a provisioning failure, or an operator who merges a duplicate must all be able to unwind the partially-completed work without leaving orphaned occupancy windows, live credit exposure or unearned revenue accruals behind.

Two design commitments shape almost every rule that follows. The first is that the resident's consent is a live gate rather than a checkbox captured once at intake — revoking it mid-flight suspends downstream provisioning and voids marketing placements, and restoring it reopens the work without silently resurrecting anything that was already terminal. The second is that identity and postal filing are subject to segregation of duties: the compliance reviewer who waves an identity check through by hand is barred from being the person who then files the postal redirect. Engineers implementing this API should read the ordered validation ladder in §2.7 as the arbiter whenever two rules could both plausibly reject the same request; the benchmark for correctness is not merely that a bad request fails, but that it fails with the specific error the ladder nominates.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contracts, envelopes and cross-cutting principles that hold for every endpoint in the system. All resource paths are mounted at the server root, so a move record is addressed directly at `/moves/{id}` and a service order at `/utility-orders/{id}`, even where ownership is logically nested.

### 2.1. Actors, Headers and Role-Based Access Control

Every request — read or write — must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting principal and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity in which it is acting. Exactly eight roles are recognised, spelled precisely as follows: `PlatformAdmin`, `FeedIntegrator`, `MoveOpsAgent`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `Resident`, `ProviderAgent`, `PartnerAgent`, and `ExternalServiceGateway`. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is any string outside that set of eight, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401); a header that is present but empty, or that holds nothing but whitespace, counts as absent for this test. Both header values are trimmed of surrounding whitespace before use, so a padded role name is still recognised and every ownership comparison below is made against the trimmed `X-User-ID`, compared as text rather than as a typed key. If the headers are well-formed but the declared role is not authorised for the endpoint being addressed — or the endpoint is one that a role holds exclusively and the caller is not among its holders — the rejection is `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

Four of the eight roles are **entity-backed**, meaning `X-User-ID` is not an opaque operator string but the primary key of a persisted row, and ownership checks compare a resource's foreign key against it:

- When `X-User-Role` is `FeedIntegrator`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `FeedSource`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `FeedSource` compare the resource's `feed_source_id` to `X-User-ID`. A `FeedIntegrator` may therefore ingest a move only when the `feed_source_id` in the body equals its own identifier, and may cancel only moves already carrying it.
- When `X-User-Role` is `Resident`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Resident`; ownership checks compare the addressed resident's `id`, or a consent record's `resident_id`, to `X-User-ID`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `ProviderAgent`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `UtilityProvider`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `UtilityProvider` compare the resource's `provider_id` to `X-User-ID`. A `ProviderAgent` may act on a service order only when that order's `provider_id` equals its own identifier, and may declare a territory only when the submitted `provider_id` equals it.
- When `X-User-Role` is `PartnerAgent`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `ReferralPartner`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `ReferralPartner` compare the resource's `partner_id` to `X-User-ID`.

Any attempt by one of those four roles to address a resource it does not own is rejected with `ERR_OWNERSHIP_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403).

The remaining four roles are **opaque operator identities** not tied to any persisted entity. When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier; the platform administrator configures feeds, providers, territories and partners, settles referral revenue, drives the logical clock, and is additionally permitted on every other operation in the system, but its identifier is never compared to an entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `MoveOpsAgent`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier; the concierge operations agent drives a move through identity, mapping, scheduling, filing and referral work, and the server records it (for example as `submitted_by_user_id` on a `CreditCheckHandoff` or on an `AddressChangeFiling`) without comparing it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `ComplianceOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier; the server records it as `reviewer_user_id` on any manually overridden `IdentityVerification` and later compares that recorded value against the identifier submitting a postal filing, but it is never matched against an entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `ExternalServiceGateway`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier belonging to the inbound callback channel for knowledge-based-authentication vendor results, credit bureau scores and postal filing responses; it is never compared to any entity's `id`.

Certain operations are reserved. Feed, provider and partner registration and their status toggles, referral settlement, and the logical-clock control are reserved to `PlatformAdmin`. Provider mapping, attention clearance, move completion, order scheduling, credit-check dispatch, order waiver and referral placement are reserved to `MoveOpsAgent` and `PlatformAdmin`. Recording an identity verification with `method` `MANUAL_OVERRIDE` and cancelling a postal filing are reserved to `ComplianceOfficer` and `PlatformAdmin` — which means a `MoveOpsAgent` that is entirely entitled to open a `KBA` verification attempt on the very same path is refused the override with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`. Order activation and provision-failure reporting are reserved to `ProviderAgent` and `PlatformAdmin`; placement acceptance, decline and conversion are reserved to `PartnerAgent` and `PlatformAdmin`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`, followed by exactly twenty randomly chosen characters drawn from the lower-case letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints and appear on no other response: a single-resource read, a create and every state-advancing write carry `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp` alone.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every collection endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` outside the inclusive range 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422), and so is a value that is not a whole number at all; a leading `+` is tolerated, so `limit=+5` reads as 5, and either parameter supplied with an empty value is treated as absent and falls back to its default.

List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending unless a specific endpoint states otherwise; the revenue-share ledger listing follows the same `created_at` ascending order, which is what makes the ledger replayable in posting sequence. Because the logical clock frequently does not advance between two writes, rows sharing an identical `created_at` are ordered by the server-side sequence in which they were written, earliest write first; that sequence counter is internal bookkeeping and appears in no response body. The same composite key settles every "newest" or "latest child" selection the sections below rely on — the move's latest identity verification, its newest non-`CANCELLED` filing, the first `GRANTED` consent record of a scope — so none of those selections is ever a genuine tie. For every list response the `meta` object carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` (the number of rows matching the filters before the page window is applied) and `has_more` (true when rows remain after the current window). Filter parameters on list endpoints are combined conjunctively: a listing that supplies both a status filter and a foreign-key filter returns only rows satisfying both. Each filter is compared as text against the stringified field value, so the literals `true` and `false` match a boolean field; a filter supplied with an empty value is treated as absent, a query parameter the endpoint does not declare is ignored rather than rejected, and a row whose filtered field is null is matched only by the literal text `None`.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock

Nothing in this system reads the host machine's wall clock. Every deadline, expiry, lead-time floor, attribution window and date comparison is evaluated against a server-held **logical clock**. Before any call to the clock-control endpoint the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; that startup default is mandatory and the server never falls back to real time. A `PlatformAdmin` may move the clock to any instant — forwards or backwards — by supplying `to_iso8601` as an ISO 8601 timestamp string that carries a `T` date/time separator; a value that is not a string, or that omits the separator, or that will not parse, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The response body carries the single field `current_utc`, and it reports the *normalised* instant rather than echoing the submitted text verbatim: the value is re-rendered in UTC with exactly three fractional digits and a trailing `Z`, so `2026-09-05T08:00:00+02:00` comes back as `2026-09-05T06:00:00.000Z`. Setting the clock mutates no entity — it changes only the instant against which subsequent requests evaluate their temporal rules.

Where a rule speaks of a *date* rather than an instant (the move date horizon, service lead times, forwarding start dates, move completion), the comparison uses the calendar date of the logical clock in UTC. Where a rule speaks of an *instant* (verification expiry, attribution windows, activation windows), the comparison uses the full logical-clock timestamp. Any timestamp the server writes — `verified_at`, `decided_at`, `granted_at`, `revoked_at`, `opted_out_at`, `responded_at`, `placed_at`, `accepted_at`, `converted_at`, `mail_forwarding_confirmed_at`, `posted_at` — is the logical-clock instant at which the write happened, never a real-time reading.

### 2.5. Common Resource Properties

Every persisted resource carries a server-minted `id` in UUID form, with no type prefix and no client control over its value, and a `created_at` timestamp in ISO 8601 UTC form with three fractional digits. Nearly every resource additionally carries `updated_at`, in the same timestamp format, refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by one on every mutation of that row. Thirteen of the fourteen resource types behave exactly this way: `FeedSource`, `Resident`, `ServiceAddress`, `UtilityProvider`, `ProviderServiceTerritory`, `MoveRecord`, `IdentityVerification`, `UtilityOrder`, `CreditCheckHandoff`, `AddressChangeFiling`, `ReferralPartner`, `ReferralPlacement` and `ConsentRecord`.

The one exception is the `RevenueShareLedgerEntry`. Ledger entries are append-only and immutable: once posted they are never rewritten, so they carry only `id` and `created_at`. A ledger entry has **no** `updated_at` field and **no** `version` field, and neither key appears in its response body.

### 2.6. Path Resolution and Reference Validation

Path identifiers are resolved strictly left to right, and the first identifier that fails to resolve decides the error — the server does not evaluate a deeper segment, or any business rule, once a shallower one has missed. A missing move yields `ERR_MOVE_NOT_FOUND`, a missing resident `ERR_RESIDENT_NOT_FOUND`, a missing service order `ERR_UTILITY_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, a missing credit-check handoff `ERR_HANDOFF_NOT_FOUND`, a missing postal filing `ERR_FILING_NOT_FOUND`, a missing referral placement `ERR_PLACEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, a missing consent record `ERR_CONSENT_NOT_FOUND`, and a missing identity verification `ERR_VERIFICATION_NOT_FOUND`. All eight carry HTTP 404. The configuration entities have no dedicated not-found code of their own: a path identifier that fails to resolve to a `FeedSource`, a `ServiceAddress`, a `UtilityProvider`, a `ProviderServiceTerritory`, a `ReferralPartner` or a `RevenueShareLedgerEntry` yields `ERR_REFERENCE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404), the same code a body reference miss yields. All of the above presuppose that the request matched a declared route in the first place. A request whose path matches no declared route at all is a different failure and is answered with `ERR_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) rather than with any entity-specific code; a request whose path matches a declared route but whose HTTP method that route does not serve is answered with `ERR_METHOD_NOT_ALLOWED` (HTTP 405); and an unhandled server-side fault is answered with `ERR_INTERNAL` (HTTP 500). All three carry the standard error envelope of §2.2.

Identifiers supplied inside a request body are a separate concern and are checked one step later. When a body carries `resident_id`, `feed_source_id`, `origin_address_id`, `destination_address_id`, `provider_id`, `partner_id`, `territory_id`, `survivor_move_id` or `move_id` and any of them fails to resolve to an existing row, the request is rejected with `ERR_REFERENCE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404). This applies to move ingestion, territory declaration, referral placement creation, duplicate resolution and consent creation.

### 2.7. Validation Precedence

When more than one rule could reject the same request, the outcome is not a matter of taste. The server evaluates checks in the fixed order below and returns the error of the **first** rule that fails; later rules are not evaluated at all. Read this ladder as authoritative: a request that violates rule 27 and rule 32 simultaneously must surface rule 27's error, never rule 32's. The ordering runs from cheap transport-level checks through existence, ownership and schema, then through state gates, concurrency, identity safety, consent and referential integrity, and finally through temporal, structural and fiscal business rules.

1. Both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` are present and `X-User-Role` is one of the eight declared roles — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. The acting role is authorised for the target endpoint, including the reserved-operation restrictions of §2.1 — otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. Every identifier in the request path resolves, left to right, to an existing row — otherwise the matching not-found error of §2.6 (404). Applies to every endpoint carrying a path identifier.
4. Every identifier supplied in the request body resolves to an existing row — otherwise `ERR_REFERENCE_NOT_FOUND` (404). Applies to `POST /moves`, `POST /provider-service-territories`, `POST /moves/{id}/referral-placements`, `POST /moves/{id}/duplicate-resolution` and `POST /residents/{id}/consent-records`.
5. For an entity-backed role, `X-User-ID` owns the addressed resource per §2.1 — otherwise `ERR_OWNERSHIP_VIOLATION` (403). Applies to every endpoint reachable by `FeedIntegrator`, `Resident`, `ProviderAgent` or `PartnerAgent`.
6. The body conforms to the endpoint's input schema: every required field present and non-null, no unknown enum member, integers inside their declared ranges (`priority_rank` from 1 through 999, `rev_share_pct` from 0.0000 through 0.9000, `monthly_placement_cap` from 1 through 1000, `attribution_window_days` from 1 through 90, `gross_revenue_cents` at least 1, both bureau credit scores and KBA vendor scores from 300 through 850), dates written as `YYYY-MM-DD`, timestamps written in ISO 8601 UTC form, and a consent `expires_at` strictly later than the logical clock — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422). Applies to every write endpoint. An absent or empty request body is read as an empty JSON object, so an endpoint whose every field is optional accepts it, while a body that parses to anything other than a JSON object, or that will not parse as JSON at all, draws the same `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422). A field supplied explicitly as `null` counts as absent: a required field so supplied is rejected as missing, an optional one falls back to its stated default. Fields the endpoint does not declare are ignored rather than rejected, and the type tests are strict — a boolean is never accepted where an integer is required, and neither is a numeric string, though a monetary or percentage field accepts an integer, a real number or a numeric string alike.
7. The addressed move's `lifecycle_status` permits the requested operation — otherwise `ERR_MOVE_NOT_ACTIONABLE` (409). Applies to every write under `/moves/{id}` and to most child writes that resolve back to a move: mapping, scheduling, credit-check dispatch, waiver, deposit payment, activation, provision-failure reporting, closure, filing creation, filing submission, placement creation, attention clearance, completion and cancellation. It does **not** apply to the three inbound gateway callbacks, to filing withdrawal, to consent creation or revocation, or to placement acceptance, decline, conversion, settlement and reversal — none of those writes consults the parent move's `lifecycle_status` at all.
8. The addressed service order's `provision_status` permits the operation — scheduling from `DRAFT`, credit-check dispatch from `DRAFT` and then only against a `PENDING_CHECK` credit state, deposit payment from `DRAFT` or `SCHEDULED` and then only against a `DEPOSIT_DUE` credit state, activation from `SCHEDULED`, provision-failure reporting from `DRAFT` or `SCHEDULED`, closure from `ACTIVE_SERVICE` and only once the clock has passed `scheduled_window_end`, waiver from `DRAFT` — otherwise `ERR_ORDER_NOT_SCHEDULABLE` (409). Applies to all seven order write operations: schedule, credit-checks, deposit-payments, activation, provision-failure, closure and waiver.
9. The addressed filing's `status` permits the operation — submission from `DRAFT` or `REJECTED`, a postal response only against `SUBMITTED`, cancellation only from a non-terminal status — otherwise `ERR_FILING_NOT_SUBMITTABLE` (409). Applies to filing submission, postal response and filing cancellation. Filing creation raises the same error when the move already holds a filing in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `CONFIRMED`, but from its own position after rung 27 rather than from this rung. Attention clearance raises the same error for a move still carrying a `REJECTED` filing, but from its own position in the §3.9 sequence rather than from this rung.
10. The addressed placement's `placement_status` permits the operation — acceptance from `PLACED`, decline from `ACCEPTED`, conversion from `ACCEPTED`, settlement from `CONVERTED`, reversal from `CONVERTED` — otherwise `ERR_PLACEMENT_NOT_CONVERTIBLE` (409). Applies to placement acceptance, decline, conversion, settlement and reversal.
11. The addressed consent record is still in state `GRANTED`; a `SUPERSEDED`, `REVOKED` or `EXPIRED` record cannot be revoked again — otherwise `ERR_CONSENT_ALREADY_TERMINAL` (409). Applies to consent revocation.
12. The addressed identity verification is still `PENDING` — otherwise `ERR_VERIFICATION_ALREADY_DECIDED` (409). Applies to the vendor result callback.
13. The addressed credit-check handoff is still `SUBMITTED` when a bureau response arrives — otherwise `ERR_HANDOFF_NOT_OPEN` (409). Applies to the bureau response callback.
14. `expected_resident_version` equals the addressed resident's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_RESIDENT_VERSION` (412). Applies to consent creation.
15. `expected_move_version` equals the addressed move's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_MOVE_VERSION` (412). Applies to referral placement creation and to duplicate resolution.
16. The move has a passed identity verification, evidenced both by a non-null `verified_at` and by the move's latest verification under §2.3's ordering standing at `PASSED`, before any address-change or service-provisioning write — otherwise `ERR_IDENTITY_NOT_VERIFIED` (422). Applies to exactly three operations: order scheduling, filing creation and filing submission.
17. That passed identity is still inside its validity window, meaning the logical clock is at or before `verified_at` plus 30 days — otherwise `ERR_IDENTITY_VERIFICATION_EXPIRED` (422). Applies to exactly two operations: order scheduling and filing submission.
18. `identity_attempt_count` on the move is below 3 before a new verification attempt is opened — otherwise `ERR_VERIFICATION_ATTEMPTS_EXHAUSTED` (409). Applies to opening an identity verification.
19. The submitting `X-User-ID` differs from the `reviewer_user_id` of every passed `MANUAL_OVERRIDE` verification on the same move — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). Applies to filing submission.
20. A consent record in state `GRANTED` exists for the resident carrying the scope the operation needs, with its `move_id` either null or equal to this move — `UTILITY_SETUP` for provisioning work, `USPS_FILING` for filing submission, `PARTNER_MARKETING` for placement creation — otherwise `ERR_CONSENT_MISSING` (422). Applies to four operations: provider mapping, order scheduling, filing submission and referral placement creation.
21. The move's `consent_gate` is `OPEN` — otherwise `ERR_CONSENT_REVOKED` (409). Applies to eight operations: provider mapping, order scheduling, order activation, filing submission, placement acceptance, placement conversion, attention clearance and move completion.
22. The resident's `marketing_opt_out` is false — otherwise `ERR_MARKETING_OPT_OUT` (422). Applies to two operations: referral placement creation and placement conversion.
23. For each requested utility type, at least one `ACTIVE` provider has a territory row covering the service address `postal_code` and the move's `move_date` — otherwise `ERR_NO_PROVIDER_COVERAGE` (422). Applies to provider mapping.
24. The submitted `account_data` object supplies every key listed in the winning provider's `required_account_fields`, each with a non-empty value — otherwise `ERR_MISSING_ACCOUNT_DATA` (422). Applies to order scheduling.
25. The provider behind the order still has `provider_status` `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_PROVIDER_INACTIVE` (409). Applies to two operations: order scheduling and order activation.
26. The `FeedSource` named by `feed_source_id` has `feed_status` `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_FEED_SUSPENDED` (409). Applies to move ingestion.
27. The move's `duplicate_flag` is not `SUSPECTED`; a suspected duplicate must be resolved first — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_MOVE_UNRESOLVED` (409). Applies to four operations: opening an identity verification, provider mapping, filing creation and referral placement creation.
28. The destination address has `usps_deliverable` true — otherwise `ERR_ADDRESS_NOT_DELIVERABLE` (422). Applies to filing submission.
29. `move_date` falls inside the inclusive window running from 30 days before the logical clock's date through 180 days after it — otherwise `ERR_MOVE_DATE_OUT_OF_RANGE` (422). Applies to move ingestion.
30. `scheduled_window_start` is strictly earlier than `scheduled_window_end`, both fall on the calendar date given by `requested_service_date`, and the span between them is at most 8 hours — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_SERVICE_WINDOW` (422). Applies to order scheduling.
31. For an order whose `order_kind` is `START`, `requested_service_date` is at or after the logical clock's date plus the provider's `scheduling_lead_days`; for `STOP` the identical floor applies unless the provider has `same_day_stop_allowed` true, in which case the floor is waived entirely — otherwise `ERR_LEAD_TIME_VIOLATION` (422). Applies to order scheduling.
32. The requested occupancy window does not intersect the window of any other order for the same `service_address_id` and `utility_type` whose `provision_status` is `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE_SERVICE` — otherwise `ERR_SERVICE_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409). Applies to order scheduling.
33. A new default-carrier territory row starts at least one full day after the inclusive `effective_to` of every existing default-carrier row for the same postal code and utility type — otherwise `ERR_TERRITORY_OVERLAP` (409). Applies to territory declaration.
34. `forward_start_date` is at or after the logical clock's date at submission time — otherwise `ERR_FORWARD_DATE_INVALID` (422). Applies to filing submission.
35. The resident has no other filing in status `SUBMITTED` or `CONFIRMED` whose forwarding period intersects this one — otherwise `ERR_FORWARDING_PERIOD_CONFLICT` (409). Applies to filing submission.
36. The logical clock is at or before the attribution anchor plus the partner's `attribution_window_days` — otherwise `ERR_ATTRIBUTION_WINDOW_EXPIRED` (422). Applies to placement conversion.
37. The logical clock is at or after `scheduled_window_start` for the order being activated — otherwise `ERR_ACTIVATION_TOO_EARLY` (409). Applies to order activation.
38. The logical clock's date has reached or passed the move's `move_date` — otherwise `ERR_MOVE_DATE_NOT_REACHED` (409). Applies to move completion.
39. No order already exists on this move for the same combination of `utility_type` and `order_kind` in `provision_status` `DRAFT`, `SCHEDULED`, `ACTIVE_SERVICE` or `SUSPENDED` — otherwise `ERR_TYPE_ALREADY_MAPPED` (409). Applies to provider mapping.
40. The order has no credit-check handoff in status `SUBMITTED` — otherwise `ERR_HANDOFF_ALREADY_OPEN` (409). Applies to credit-check dispatch.
41. The move has no other placement in the partner's `vertical` whose `placement_status` is `PLACED`, `ACCEPTED`, `CONVERTED` or `SETTLED` — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_VERTICAL_PLACEMENT` (409). Applies to referral placement creation.
42. `resubmit_count` on the filing is below 2 when a `REJECTED` filing is resubmitted — otherwise `ERR_RESUBMIT_LIMIT_REACHED` (409). Applies to filing submission.
43. `survivor_move_id` differs from the move identified in the path — otherwise `ERR_SELF_MERGE_FORBIDDEN` (422). Applies to duplicate resolution.
44. For a merge resolution the survivor move is non-terminal and shares either the `resident_id` or the destination `address_hash` of the move being merged — otherwise `ERR_SURVIVOR_MOVE_INVALID` (422). Applies to duplicate resolution.
45. Every order of the move that is `PROVISION_FAILED`, or whose `credit_status` is `DECLINED`, has a replacement order for the same `utility_type` and `order_kind` in `DRAFT`, `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE_SERVICE` — otherwise `ERR_UNRESOLVED_PROVISION_FAILURE` (409). Applies to attention clearance.
46. When `deposit_policy` is `FLAT`, `flat_deposit_cents` does not exceed 50000 — otherwise `ERR_DEPOSIT_CAP_EXCEEDED` (422). Applies to provider registration.
47. The paid `amount_cents` equals the order's `deposit_amount_cents` exactly, with neither partial nor over-payment accepted — otherwise `ERR_DEPOSIT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH` (422). Applies to deposit payment.
48. The partner's `partner_status` is `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_PARTNER_PAUSED` (409). Applies to referral placement creation.
49. The count of the partner's placements whose `placed_at` falls in the logical clock's calendar month is below `monthly_placement_cap` — otherwise `ERR_PARTNER_CAP_REACHED` (409). Applies to referral placement creation.
50. `settled_gross_revenue_cents` is at least 1 and does not exceed twice the recorded `gross_revenue_cents` — otherwise `ERR_SETTLEMENT_AMOUNT_INVALID` (422). Applies to placement settlement.

One further gate sits outside the numbered ladder because it belongs to a single operation. Activation additionally requires the order's `credit_status` to be one of `NOT_REQUIRED`, `CLEARED` or `DEPOSIT_PAID`; when it is instead `PENDING_CHECK`, `DEPOSIT_DUE`, `DECLINED` or `VOIDED`, activation is refused with `ERR_CREDIT_NOT_CLEARED` (409). Although it is unnumbered, its position in the evaluation sequence is fixed: it is checked immediately after rung 8's order-state check and therefore ahead of the consent-gate check of rung 21, the provider-status check of rung 25 and the window-timing check of rung 37, so an activation that violates the credit gate and one of those three simultaneously surfaces `ERR_CREDIT_NOT_CLEARED`. This is the single point in the entire system at which the credit dimension of a service order constrains its provisioning dimension.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency on Residents and Moves

Three write operations are guarded by optimistic concurrency control, because each of them supersedes state that the client must have observed before deciding.

Recording a data-sharing consent for a resident requires a non-optional `expected_resident_version` field in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of that `Resident`. If that value does not match the resident's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_RESIDENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The reason is substantive rather than ceremonial: the new record legitimately supersedes the resident's prior `GRANTED` record for the same `scope`, so a writer holding a stale view of the resident would silently discard a consent decision it never saw.

Placing a partner referral against a move requires a non-optional `expected_move_version` field carrying the client's last observed `version` of that `MoveRecord`; a mismatch with the move's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_MOVE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Placement eligibility is derived entirely from move-level state — lifecycle, consent gate, and which verticals still have a free slot — so a writer working from a stale version may be placing against a move that has since been merged, cancelled or gated.

Resolving a suspected duplicate likewise requires a non-optional `expected_move_version` matching the addressed move's current `version`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_MOVE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A merge supersedes the entire move aggregate, so the decision must be taken against the exact version whose `duplicate_match_score` the reviewer inspected.

No other endpoint accepts a version field. Scheduling an order, submitting a filing, activating a service, converting a placement and every other write in the system take no `expected_..._version` input and are never rejected for concurrency reasons.

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline and Conserved Quantities

Most interesting writes in this system touch more than one row. A cascade is specified as an ordered sequence of hops: the first hop mutates the addressed resource, and each subsequent hop is *caused by* the hop above it. Implementations must apply every hop of a cascade, and every row a hop touches has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by one, exactly as any direct mutation would — with one systematic exception for the hops that merely re-derive a value. A move's derived-field commit compares before against after across the fields the move actually tracks (`lifecycle_status`, `consent_gate`, `blocked_reason`, `readiness_score`, `mapped_order_count`, `unmapped_utility_types`, `verified_at`, `identity_attempt_count`, `cancellation_reason`, `move_date`, `external_reference`, `duplicate_flag`, `duplicate_match_score`, `duplicate_candidate_move_id`, `duplicate_of_move_id` and `reactivation_candidate_move_id`) and advances `version` and `updated_at` only when at least one of them differs, so a recomputation that lands every derived field back on the value it already held leaves the move at the `version` and `updated_at` it already had. A feed source's `accrued_share_cents` roll-up behaves the same way, touching the feed row only when the recomputed total differs from the stored one. A handful of writes deliberately force the bump regardless of what changed — duplicate resolution in all three of its forms, attention clearance, move completion and move cancellation, and placement creation's bump of the parent move — and wherever §3 pins an unconditional increment for a named operation, that pinning governs.

Four quantities are conserved across the cascades, meaning that after a compensating unwind they return to the values they held before the action being unwound:

- **The occupancy window count** for a pair of `service_address_id` and `utility_type` — the number of service orders holding a window on that pair. Scheduling adds one; closure, provision failure, suspension and cancellation each release one. An order in `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE_SERVICE` counts as holding its window; an order in any other provisioning state does not.
- **The move's open deposit liability**, expressed in cents — the sum of `deposit_amount_cents` across the move's orders whose `credit_status` is `DEPOSIT_DUE`. A bureau response that lands in the deposit band raises it; paying the deposit and voiding the credit dimension each drop it back.
- **The placement's ledger net**, expressed in cents — the sum of `amount_cents` across every ledger entry whose `placement_id` is that placement. At all times it equals the placement's current `attributed_share_cents` minus its `feed_source_share_cents`, and once a placement has been reversed it equals exactly 0.
- **The feed source's accrued share**, held on `accrued_share_cents` — recomputed rather than incrementally patched whenever a placement's economics change.

### 2.10. Precision, Rounding and Score Ranges

All monetary amounts in this system are integers denominated in cents; there are no fractional-cent representations anywhere in the API. Every monetary computation is performed with exact decimal arithmetic and rounded to whole cents — that is, to 0 decimal places — using round-half-up. Where a formula composes several factors, the product is computed exactly and rounded **once**, at the point the formula specifies; intermediate factors are never pre-rounded and no floating-point residue is retained between steps.

Two ratio-shaped scores use different precision. The `duplicate_match_score` is a decimal ratio between 0.00 and 1.00 rounded to exactly 2 decimal places with round-half-up, and every threshold comparison against it is performed on the already-rounded value. The `readiness_score` and each of its four components are integers: components are rounded to 0 decimal places with round-half-up **before** weighting, the weighted sum is rounded to 0 decimal places with round-half-up **after** summing, and the result is clamped into the inclusive range 0 through 100.

Percentage-shaped configuration fields — `referral_revenue_share_pct` on a feed source and `rev_share_pct` on a partner — are decimal fractions carried at 4 decimal places, so a thirty-percent revenue share is stored as 0.3000. Their permitted ranges differ: `referral_revenue_share_pct` runs from 0.0000 through 0.5000 inclusive, `rev_share_pct` from 0.0000 through 0.9000 inclusive. A submitted value carrying more than four decimal places is rounded half-up to four rather than refused. Every decimal-valued field in the system travels as a JSON number rather than as a string, and trailing zeros are not preserved on the wire: a stored 0.3000 reads back as 0.3, and a `duplicate_match_score` of 0.90 reads back as 0.9.

### 2.11. Segregation of Duties and the Audit Trail

Identity and postal filing are deliberately split between two hands. Whenever a `ComplianceOfficer` (or a `PlatformAdmin` acting in that capacity) records an identity verification by manual override rather than through the vendor, the server stamps the acting `X-User-ID` onto that verification as `reviewer_user_id`. Later, when a postal filing on that same move is submitted, the submitting `X-User-ID` is compared against the `reviewer_user_id` of every passed manual-override verification on the move; a match means the same human is both waving identity through and filing the redirect, and the submission is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). The bar is per-move and per-identifier: a different compliance officer may submit the filing freely, and a move whose identity passed through the vendor path carries no `reviewer_user_id` at all and is therefore unconstrained.

Several other fields exist purely to make after-the-fact reconstruction possible and are never used as gates. `territory_id` pins which territory row won the provider selection. `submitted_by_user_id` on a credit-check handoff records who dispatched it. `readiness_snapshot` records the readiness figure the deposit formula was allowed to read. `deposit_amount_cents` survives a void so the historical exposure remains legible even though it no longer counts toward the move's liability. `scheduled_window_start` and `scheduled_window_end` survive a closure for the same reason, even though the window is no longer occupied.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Configuring the Concierge Network

Before a single move can be ingested, a `PlatformAdmin` populates the reference data the workflow reads. **Feed sources** represent the property-manager and mover integrations entitled to push move records. Registering one requires a display `name` of 1 to 120 characters, a `source_type` of either `PROPERTY_MANAGER` or `MOVER`, and a `referral_revenue_share_pct` — the fraction of platform referral commission owed back to this integration, carried at four decimal places anywhere from 0.0000 through 0.5000. Two further inputs are optional. `dedupe_window_days` sets the half-width, in days, of the move-date proximity window that duplicate detection will later use; it accepts 1 through 120 and defaults to 30 when omitted. `trust_tier` accepts `TIER_A` or `TIER_B` and defaults to `TIER_B`; the difference bites at intake, where a `TIER_A` feed has its below-threshold duplicate candidates auto-dismissed while a `TIER_B` feed leaves them parked for a human. A newly registered feed starts with `accrued_share_cents` at 0 and `feed_status` at `ACTIVE`. The admin may later toggle `feed_status` between `ACTIVE` and `SUSPENDED`; a suspended feed is refused at move intake with `ERR_FEED_SUSPENDED`, but moves it already produced are untouched by the toggle. All three status patches in this section — a feed source's `feed_status`, a utility provider's `provider_status` and a referral partner's `partner_status` — take the new status as a required field, write it unconditionally without first comparing it to the stored one, and touch nothing else, so re-asserting the status a row already holds is accepted and still refreshes that row's `updated_at` and increments its `version` by one.

A **resident** is the natural person moving, and the row holds the identity attributes the knowledge-based-authentication vendor consumes. Creation takes `full_name` (1 to 120 characters, normalised by trimming and case-folding when duplicate matching compares it, with internal whitespace left exactly as submitted so `Ada  Lovelace` and `Ada Lovelace` do **not** compare equal), `email` (3 to 200 characters, which must contain exactly one at-sign, and is trimmed and case-folded before any comparison), `phone_last4` (exactly four digits of the contact telephone number), `date_of_birth` as an ISO 8601 calendar date in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, and `ssn_last4` (exactly four digits of the national identifier, which the vendor requires). An optional `marketing_opt_out` boolean defaults to false. A fresh resident starts at `version` 1 with `opted_out_at` null.

Marketing preference is a first-class, resident-controlled switch rather than a profile edit. A `Resident` may change its own preference, and a `ComplianceOfficer` or `PlatformAdmin` may change it on the resident's behalf, by submitting a new `marketing_opt_out` value. When the value flips from false to true, the server sets `opted_out_at` to the logical-clock instant and voids every referral placement belonging to the resident's non-terminal moves whose `placement_status` is `PLACED` or `ACCEPTED`. When the value flips from true back to false, `opted_out_at` is cleared to null — but placements already voided are **not** resurrected; a fresh placement must be created. Either direction increments the resident's `version` by one, which matters because that same `version` is the concurrency token for consent creation. The server applies the submitted value unconditionally rather than only on an observed change, so re-submitting `true` against an already-opted-out resident re-stamps `opted_out_at` with the current instant, re-runs the void hop and increments `version` again, and re-submitting `false` against an already-opted-in resident likewise increments `version`.

**Service addresses** are created independently and then referenced by moves as origin and destination. The input is `line1` (the street line, 1 to 160 characters, lower-cased verbatim into the match key derived below — neither trimmed nor whitespace-collapsed), `city` (1 to 120 characters), `state_code` (exactly two upper-case letters), `postal_code` (exactly five digits), and an optional `unit` designator of 1 to 40 characters that defaults to null for single-family addresses. The server derives two fields. `address_hash` is a deterministic digest formed by concatenating the lower-cased `line1`, a vertical-bar separator, the lower-cased `unit` (an empty string when `unit` is null), another vertical bar, and the `postal_code`; it is the exact-match key duplicate detection uses. `usps_deliverable` is derived as false when the `postal_code` begins with the non-deliverable prefix `000`, and true otherwise; a false value later blocks postal filing submission with `ERR_ADDRESS_NOT_DELIVERABLE`. The `postal_code` doubles as the join key against the territory table.

**Utility providers** are registered with the operational terms that will govern every order routed to them. Required inputs are the provider trade `name` (1 to 120 characters), the `utility_type` it supplies — one of `ELECTRIC`, `GAS`, `WATER` or `INTERNET` — a `required_account_fields` array, a `deposit_policy`, a `credit_check_required` boolean, and a `scheduling_lead_days` integer from 0 through 30. The `required_account_fields` array holds between one and six entries drawn from the set `account_holder_name`, `service_start_date`, `ssn_last4`, `prior_account_number` and `unit_designator`, stored in the order submitted and not deduplicated, so a repeated entry is accepted rather than rejected; every entry named here must later appear as a populated key of an order's account payload before that order can be scheduled. The `deposit_policy` is `NONE`, `FLAT` or `CREDIT_SCORE_BASED`, and determines how a deposit base is derived once a credit bureau demands one. `flat_deposit_cents` is optional, defaults to 0, must not be negative, is used as the deposit base only under a `FLAT` policy and is ignored otherwise; registering a `FLAT` provider whose `flat_deposit_cents` exceeds the platform deposit ceiling of 50000 cents is refused with `ERR_DEPOSIT_CAP_EXCEEDED`. `credit_check_required` decides the starting credit posture of every order routed to this provider: true means the order opens at `PENDING_CHECK`, false means it opens at `NOT_REQUIRED`. `scheduling_lead_days` is the minimum number of whole days that must separate the logical-clock date from a requested service date on a start order. An optional `same_day_stop_allowed` boolean defaults to false; when true, stop orders are exempt from that lead-day floor entirely. Providers begin at `provider_status` `ACTIVE`, and an admin may toggle that to `INACTIVE` and back; an `INACTIVE` provider is ineligible as a mapping candidate and is refused at scheduling and activation with `ERR_PROVIDER_INACTIVE`, though existing orders keep their `provider_id` unchanged.

Coverage itself lives in **provider service territories**, a junction row that binds one provider to one postal code over a closed date interval. Either a `PlatformAdmin` or the provider's own `ProviderAgent` may declare one; when a `ProviderAgent` declares it, the submitted `provider_id` must equal the agent's own `X-User-ID`. The row carries `provider_id`, a five-digit `postal_code`, a two-letter `state_code` that must equal the state code of any address it serves, an `effective_from` date that is the first date inclusive on which the row may serve a move date, an optional `effective_to` date that is the last date inclusive and means open-ended when null (and, when present, must be on or after `effective_from`), a `priority_rank` from 1 through 999 that is the primary ascending ranking key among eligible candidates, and an optional `is_default_carrier` boolean defaulting to false. Default-carrier rows are the ones subject to a spacing rule: for any one pairing of postal code and utility type, a new default-carrier row must begin at least one full day after the inclusive `effective_to` of every existing default-carrier row for that pairing. Because both intervals are closed at each end, a new row starting on the day immediately after an existing row's `effective_to` is **still** a conflict and is refused with `ERR_TERRITORY_OVERLAP`; a clear day must separate them. An existing default-carrier row whose `effective_to` is null is open-ended and therefore has no date to clear, so any new default-carrier row for that pairing is refused outright. The test reads only the new row's `effective_from` against each existing row's `effective_to`, which means a new default-carrier row backdated to start *before* an existing one is refused just as a late-starting one is; the new row's own `effective_to` is not consulted, and the row's `state_code` plays no part in the pairing. Non-default rows carry no such restriction and may overlap each other freely. Territory rows are listable and filterable by `provider_id`, `postal_code`, `state_code` and `is_default_carrier`; the listing is not ownership-scoped, so a `ProviderAgent` calling it sees every provider's rows and must filter by its own `provider_id` to narrow them.

**Referral partners** complete the configuration surface. A partner is registered with a trade `name`, a `vertical` of `INTERNET`, `INSURANCE` or `MOVERS`, a `payout_model`, an `attribution_window_days` integer from 1 through 90, an `attribution_basis`, and a `monthly_placement_cap` from 1 through 1000. The `payout_model` is either `FLAT_BOUNTY`, which pays a fixed commission per conversion and therefore requires `bounty_cents`, or `REV_SHARE_PCT`, which pays a fraction of reported gross revenue and therefore requires `rev_share_pct`; `bounty_cents` defaults to 0 and must not be negative, `rev_share_pct` defaults to 0.0000 and is carried at four decimal places from 0.0000 through 0.9000, and each is ignored under the other payout model. The `attribution_basis` is `PLACEMENT_CLICK`, which anchors the attribution window at the instant the placement was created, or `PLACEMENT_ACCEPTANCE`, which anchors it at the instant the partner accepted. `attribution_window_days` is a whole-day span measured from that anchor, and the boundary is inclusive: a conversion arriving at exactly the anchor plus the full window is still attributable. `monthly_placement_cap` is the maximum number of placements the partner may receive within the calendar month of the logical clock. Partners begin at `partner_status` `ACTIVE`; an admin may move them to `PAUSED` and back. A `PAUSED` partner cannot receive new placements — attempts are refused with `ERR_PARTNER_PAUSED` — but placements already in `PLACED` or `ACCEPTED` continue on to settle normally.

### 3.2. Move Intake and Duplicate Detection

A move enters the system as a **move record**, ingested either by the `FeedIntegrator` whose system produced it or by a `MoveOpsAgent` keying it in. The payload names the mover through `resident_id`, the originating integration through `feed_source_id`, the address being vacated through `origin_address_id`, the address being occupied through `destination_address_id`, and the physical `move_date`. An optional `external_reference` of 1 to 200 characters carries the feed's own identifier so it can be echoed back to the property manager; it defaults to null. When a `FeedIntegrator` ingests, the submitted `feed_source_id` must equal its own `X-User-ID`; a `MoveOpsAgent` and a `PlatformAdmin` are exempt from that comparison. The feed named must have `feed_status` `ACTIVE`. The `move_date` must fall inside the inclusive window running from 30 days before the logical clock's date through 180 days after it — a 30-day past grace so that a feed reporting last week's move is still accepted, and a 180-day forward horizon so speculative bookings are not. Origin and destination must differ; supplying the same address identifier for both is a malformed request and yields `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

A newly ingested move lands in `lifecycle_status` `INTAKE` with `verified_at` null, `identity_attempt_count` at 0, `mapped_order_count` at 0, `unmapped_utility_types` empty, `duplicate_of_move_id` null, `reactivation_candidate_move_id` null, `duplicate_candidate_move_id` null, `cancellation_reason` null and `blocked_reason` `NONE`. Its `consent_gate` is derived immediately from the resident's existing consent records, and its `readiness_score` is computed under the pre-verification form described in §3.9. Both duplicate-detection passes and that first derivation run before the row is ever serialized and are treated as part of the creation itself rather than as mutations of it, so the response reports `version` 1 with `created_at` equal to `updated_at` even for a move that came out of intake already flagged `SUSPECTED`.

**Duplicate detection runs immediately after the row is written**, as two ordered passes over the moves that already existed. Both passes share one candidate-selection rule. A move already in the store is a candidate when all of the following hold: it is not the new move itself; it was written before the new move — either a strictly earlier `created_at`, or the same `created_at` with an earlier write sequence in the sense of §2.3, which is what keeps two moves ingested at one and the same logical-clock instant strictly ordered rather than mutually invisible — so scoring is a pure function of rows that existed before this ingestion; the absolute difference in whole days between its `move_date` and the new move's `move_date` is at most the `dedupe_window_days` of the new move's feed source; and it shares at least one of three identity signals with the new move — the same `resident_id`, the same destination `address_hash`, or the same `email` on the resident once both addresses are case-folded. Moves already flagged `CONFIRMED_DUPLICATE` are excluded from both passes, because they are represented by their survivor.

Each surviving candidate is scored on four components, all of which are exact two-decimal decimal values. The **name component** is 1.00 when the two residents' `full_name` values are equal after trimming and case-folding, 0.60 when they are not equal but share an identical final token (a surname match), and 0.00 otherwise. The **email component** is 1.00 when the two case-folded `email` values are equal and 0.00 otherwise. The **address component** is 1.00 when the two destination addresses have the same `address_hash`, 0.50 when they do not but share both the same `postal_code` and the same `state_code`, and 0.00 otherwise. The **recency component** decays linearly across the dedupe window: take the feed source's `dedupe_window_days` as the window, take the absolute difference in whole days between the two `move_date` values as the gap, subtract the gap from the window, divide by the window, and use the larger of that quotient and 0.00 — so a same-day match scores 1.00 and a match exactly at the edge of the window scores 0.00.

The four components are then weighted: the name component is multiplied by 0.35, the email component by 0.25, the address component by 0.25, and the recency component by 0.15. Those four products are summed and the sum is rounded **once** to two decimal places with round-half-up; that rounded figure is the candidate's score. Candidates are ranked deterministically by score descending, then by `created_at` ascending, then by `move_date` ascending, with the internal write sequence of §2.3 settling any residual tie — the ranking never reads `id`, so ties resolve identically on every implementation. The best-ranked candidate's score is written to `duplicate_match_score`. If the pass finds no candidate at all, `duplicate_match_score` is 0.00, `duplicate_flag` stays `NONE`, and the candidate foreign key is left null. The suspicion threshold is 0.80, compared with a greater-than-or-equal test against the already-rounded score.

The **first pass scans only open moves** — those whose `lifecycle_status` is `INTAKE`, `IDENTITY_PENDING`, `IDENTITY_FAILED`, `VERIFIED`, `SCHEDULING`, `ATTENTION_REQUIRED` or `IN_SERVICE`. It writes `duplicate_candidate_move_id` with the winning candidate's identifier, and that foreign key is retained even when the score sits below the threshold, so an operator can always see who the near-miss was. When the score reaches 0.80 or above, `duplicate_flag` becomes `SUSPECTED` and `blocked_reason` becomes `DUPLICATE_SUSPECTED`, and the move is held in `INTAKE` until a duplicate resolution decides its fate — while `duplicate_flag` is `SUSPECTED`, opening an identity verification, running provider mapping, drafting a postal filing and creating a referral placement are all refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_MOVE_UNRESOLVED`. When the score sits below 0.80, the feed's `trust_tier` decides the outcome: a `TIER_A` feed has `duplicate_flag` set to `DISMISSED` automatically, while a `TIER_B` feed leaves `duplicate_flag` at `NONE` so the near-miss stays visible for review.

The **second pass scans only closed moves** — those whose `lifecycle_status` is `CANCELLED` or `DUPLICATE_MERGED` — and it runs on the same ingestion only when the first pass produced no `SUSPECTED` match. Its purpose is different: a cancelled predecessor is a re-book signal, not a live collision. It computes exactly the same four-component weighted score by exactly the same arithmetic, and when that score reaches 0.80 or above it writes the winner's identifier to `reactivation_candidate_move_id`. It also writes `duplicate_match_score` — but only when the first pass found no candidate at all, so that the observable score is never silently overwritten with a weaker number. This pass never sets `duplicate_flag`, never sets `duplicate_candidate_move_id`, and never blocks the move. Identical resident, address and date data therefore produces the same numeric score in both passes but a materially different observable outcome, and implementers should treat that asymmetry as intentional.

Resolving a suspected duplicate is an explicit decision taken by a `MoveOpsAgent` or a `PlatformAdmin`, and it always carries `expected_move_version` as described in §2.8. The `resolution` field selects one of three behaviours. A `DISMISS` resolution leaves `lifecycle_status` entirely untouched, sets `duplicate_flag` to `DISMISSED`, clears `blocked_reason` back to `NONE` when it had been `DUPLICATE_SUSPECTED`, and increments the move's `version` by one. A `RESCORE` resolution re-runs the first pass only — the open-move pass, by exactly the same candidate rule and arithmetic — and may either clear or preserve the `SUSPECTED` flag depending on the new score, likewise incrementing `version` by one; it rewrites `duplicate_match_score` and `duplicate_candidate_move_id` from that fresh pass, resetting them to 0.00 and null when no candidate survives, re-applies the `TIER_A`/`TIER_B` split to a below-threshold score, clears a `blocked_reason` of `DUPLICATE_SUSPECTED` whenever the new score no longer suspects, and never revisits `reactivation_candidate_move_id`. A `MERGE_INTO` resolution additionally requires `survivor_move_id`, and folds this move into that survivor. `DISMISS` and `RESCORE` are reachable from any of the seven non-terminal lifecycle states, so a `COMPLETED`, `CANCELLED` or `DUPLICATE_MERGED` move cannot be dismissed or rescored and is refused with `ERR_MOVE_NOT_ACTIONABLE`; `MERGE_INTO` carries the narrower gate described below. All three resolutions increment `version` unconditionally, even a `DISMISS` against a move that was already `DISMISSED` and so changed nothing. The self-merge bar is not confined to merges either: whenever `survivor_move_id` is supplied at all — including alongside a `DISMISS` or `RESCORE`, where it is otherwise ignored — naming the addressed move itself is refused with `ERR_SELF_MERGE_FORBIDDEN`.

The merge is the deepest cascade in the system and is reachable only from `INTAKE`, `IDENTITY_PENDING`, `IDENTITY_FAILED`, `VERIFIED`, `SCHEDULING` or `ATTENTION_REQUIRED`; a move already `IN_SERVICE` may not be merged away. The nominated survivor must not be the move itself, must itself be non-terminal — meaning its `lifecycle_status` is one of `INTAKE`, `IDENTITY_PENDING`, `IDENTITY_FAILED`, `VERIFIED`, `SCHEDULING`, `ATTENTION_REQUIRED` or `IN_SERVICE` — and must share either the same `resident_id` or the same destination `address_hash` as the move being merged. A survivor that is terminal, or that shares neither signal, is refused with `ERR_SURVIVOR_MOVE_INVALID`; naming the move itself is refused earlier, with `ERR_SELF_MERGE_FORBIDDEN`. When the merge proceeds, the first hop sets `duplicate_flag` to `CONFIRMED_DUPLICATE`, writes `duplicate_of_move_id` with the survivor's identifier, drives `lifecycle_status` to the terminal `DUPLICATE_MERGED`, and increments `version` by one. Because that first hop reached a terminal state, three further hops fire. Every service order of this move whose `provision_status` is `DRAFT`, `SCHEDULED`, `ACTIVE_SERVICE` or `SUSPENDED` moves to `CANCELLED` with `cancellation_reason` set to `DUPLICATE_MERGED` and both `scheduled_window_start` and `scheduled_window_end` cleared, releasing any occupancy window it held. Each such cancelled order whose `credit_status` was `PENDING_CHECK` or `DEPOSIT_DUE` then moves to `VOIDED`, and that order's credit-check handoff in `SUBMITTED` or `RESPONDED` moves to `VOIDED` alongside it. In parallel, every postal filing of this move in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `REJECTED` moves to `CANCELLED` with `mail_forwarding_confirmed_at` cleared, and every referral placement of this move in `PLACED` or `ACCEPTED` moves to `VOIDED`.

### 3.3. Verifying the Mover's Identity

No address change and no service provisioning may proceed until the platform has satisfied itself that the person named on the move record is the person requesting it. That evidence is an **identity verification**, and each attempt is a child row of the move. Opening one takes a `method` of either `KBA` or `MANUAL_OVERRIDE`, plus an `override_reason` of 1 to 500 characters that is required and must be non-empty when the method is `MANUAL_OVERRIDE` and is otherwise omitted — though an `override_reason` volunteered alongside a `KBA` method is accepted rather than rejected, and is simply carried on the row. An attempt may be opened only while the move is in `INTAKE`, `IDENTITY_PENDING` or `IDENTITY_FAILED`; every other lifecycle state, `VERIFIED` included, is refused with `ERR_MOVE_NOT_ACTIONABLE`, so a second attempt may be stacked on a move whose earlier attempt is still `PENDING` but no attempt may be opened once identity has already passed. The server copies `move_id` and `resident_id` onto the new row from the move, and computes `attempt_no` as the move's current `identity_attempt_count` plus one, so attempts are numbered 1, 2 and 3.

Three attempts is the ceiling. Before a new attempt is opened the move's `identity_attempt_count` must be strictly below 3, and a fourth attempt is refused with `ERR_VERIFICATION_ATTEMPTS_EXHAUSTED`. Every successfully opened attempt, of either method, increments `identity_attempt_count` by one.

The `KBA` path hands the resident to an external knowledge-based-authentication vendor. Opening a `KBA` attempt requires the resident to have both `ssn_last4` and `date_of_birth` populated, because the vendor consumes them. The new row lands in `status` `PENDING` with `expires_at` set to the creation instant plus 72 hours — three full days for the vendor to answer — and with `kba_score`, `reviewer_user_id` and `decided_at` all null. The move advances from `INTAKE` to `IDENTITY_PENDING`, and its readiness is recomputed under the pre-verification form. The same operation is how a failed move retries: a move sitting in `IDENTITY_FAILED` returns to `IDENTITY_PENDING` when a fresh `KBA` attempt is opened, which is what closes the `IDENTITY_PENDING` ↔ `IDENTITY_FAILED` cycle. Opening a fresh attempt also sweeps the past: any pre-existing verification still in `PENDING` whose `expires_at` is already behind the logical clock moves to `EXPIRED` with `decided_at` set to the current instant, and the newly created attempt becomes the move's latest verification. An `EXPIRED` verification is treated exactly like a `FAILED` one for lifecycle purposes but, unlike a real failure, it does not itself consume a further attempt.

The vendor answers through the inbound callback channel reserved to `ExternalServiceGateway` and `PlatformAdmin`, supplying an `outcome` of either `PASSED` or `FAILED` and a `score`. The addressed verification must still be `PENDING`; a `PASSED`, `FAILED` or `EXPIRED` row cannot receive a result and the callback is refused with `ERR_VERIFICATION_ALREADY_DECIDED`. The passing bar is 700: the verification moves to `PASSED` only when `outcome` is `PASSED` **and** `score` is at least 700, and reaching `PASSED` additionally requires the logical clock to be at or before the row's `expires_at`, so a favourable answer arriving after the vendor window has lapsed cannot be credited. A lapsed row is settled as `EXPIRED` rather than `FAILED`, whatever the `outcome` and `score` say; only where the row is still inside its window does a non-passing answer settle as `FAILED` — including the deliberately counter-intuitive case where `outcome` is `PASSED` but `score` falls below 700. Either way the server writes `kba_score` from the supplied `score` (an integer from 300 through 850, a value outside that range being refused as a malformed request) and stamps `decided_at` with the logical-clock instant. A pass sets the move's `verified_at` to that same instant and drives `lifecycle_status` to `VERIFIED` from whatever state the move was in, recomputing readiness under the post-verification form; a non-pass drives `lifecycle_status` to `IDENTITY_FAILED` only when the move is currently in `IDENTITY_PENDING` or `INTAKE`, leaving every other lifecycle state — including a move already `IDENTITY_FAILED`, `VERIFIED` or further along — untouched, and recomputes readiness under whichever form that lifecycle then implies.

The `MANUAL_OVERRIDE` path is the compliance escape hatch, reserved to `ComplianceOfficer` and `PlatformAdmin`. It carries no vendor round trip: the row is created directly in `status` `PASSED` with `decided_at` set to the current instant, `kba_score` left null, `expires_at` still stamped with the creation instant plus 72 hours exactly as a `KBA` row's is — the field is minted uniformly and is simply never consulted on an already-decided row — and `reviewer_user_id` stamped with the acting `X-User-ID` — the value that §2.11's segregation-of-duties bar will read later. The move's `verified_at` is set to the current instant and `lifecycle_status` jumps straight to `VERIFIED`, whether it was sitting in `INTAKE` or in `IDENTITY_FAILED`, with readiness recomputed under the post-verification form. An override submitted without a non-empty `override_reason` is refused as a malformed request.

A passed identity does not stay good forever. **Identity validity runs 30 days from `verified_at`, inclusive of the boundary instant**: an operation guarded by the validity window succeeds while the logical clock is at or before `verified_at` plus 30 days and is refused with `ERR_IDENTITY_VERIFICATION_EXPIRED` after that. Two operations carry that guard — scheduling a service order and submitting a postal filing — and both additionally require the move's latest verification to be `PASSED`. An operation attempted on a move whose `verified_at` is still null is refused one rung earlier, with `ERR_IDENTITY_NOT_VERIFIED`; that earlier rung guards three operations, adding postal filing creation to the two above. Verification attempts are individually readable and are listable with filters on `move_id`, `resident_id`, `status` and `method`.

### 3.4. Data-Sharing Consent and the Consent Gate

The platform shares resident data with utilities, with the postal service and with commercial partners, and each of those three disclosures is separately consented. A **consent record** is created against a resident and carries a `scope` of `UTILITY_SETUP`, `USPS_FILING` or `PARTNER_MARKETING`; a `source_channel` recording how the agreement was captured, one of `PM_FEED`, `WEB_PORTAL` or `PHONE_ATTESTATION`; and a `consent_text_version` of 1 to 16 characters naming the version tag of the disclosure text the resident agreed to. An optional `move_id` scopes the consent to a single move, and when supplied it must resolve to a move — a miss being `ERR_REFERENCE_NOT_FOUND` at rung 4 — whose `resident_id` equals the resident in the path, a move belonging to some other resident being refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` at rung 6; leaving it null makes the consent resident-wide and therefore applicable to every move that resident has. An optional `expires_at` instant makes the consent lapse; it must be strictly later than the logical clock at creation time, and a null value means the consent does not lapse at all. The record is created in `state` `GRANTED` with `granted_at` set to the current instant and with `revoked_at` and `revocation_reason` both null. A `Resident` may record its own consents and a `MoveOpsAgent`, `ComplianceOfficer` or `PlatformAdmin` may record them on the resident's behalf; when the actor is a `Resident`, `X-User-ID` must equal the resident identified in the path.

Creation is guarded by `expected_resident_version` as §2.8 describes, and the reason is visible in the side effects. Any prior consent record for the **same resident and the same `scope`** that is still `GRANTED` moves to `SUPERSEDED`. A superseded record deliberately does **not** get a `revoked_at` value, and a `SUPERSEDED` record never blocks the consent gate — superseding is a refresh, not a withdrawal. The resident's `version` is then incremented by one, and every non-terminal move of that resident has its `consent_gate` and `readiness_score` recomputed. Where that recomputation flips a gate from blocked back to open, the restoration cascade described below fires.

The **consent gate** is a derived, two-valued field on each move. It is evaluated one gate scope at a time: a scope blocks when some consent record linked to that move in that `scope` stands in `state` `REVOKED` or `EXPIRED` **and** no record linked in that same scope is still `GRANTED`, so a fresh grant neutralises the withdrawn one rather than merely sitting beside it. `consent_gate` is `BLOCKED` when either `UTILITY_SETUP` or `USPS_FILING` blocks under that test, and `OPEN` otherwise. A consent record counts as linked to a move when its `move_id` equals that move, or when its `move_id` is null and its `resident_id` matches the move's resident. Note that `PARTNER_MARKETING` is deliberately **not** a gate scope: withdrawing marketing consent stops referrals without suspending the household's electricity order. A move whose gate is `BLOCKED` is refused on eight operations — provider mapping, order scheduling, order activation, postal filing submission, placement acceptance, placement conversion, attention clearance and move completion — each time with `ERR_CONSENT_REVOKED`. Separately, an operation that needs a scope for which the resident has **no** `GRANTED` record at all is refused with `ERR_CONSENT_MISSING`: provisioning work needs `UTILITY_SETUP`, filing submission needs `USPS_FILING`, and placement creation needs `PARTNER_MARKETING`.

Revocation is available to the `Resident` who owns the record, to a `ComplianceOfficer` and to a `PlatformAdmin`, and it accepts an optional free-text `revocation_reason` of 1 to 500 characters, stored as null when omitted. Only a `GRANTED` record may be revoked; a `SUPERSEDED`, `REVOKED` or `EXPIRED` record is refused with `ERR_CONSENT_ALREADY_TERMINAL`. The first hop writes `revoked_at` with the logical-clock instant and stores `revocation_reason`, moving `state` from `GRANTED` to `REVOKED`. What happens next depends entirely on the scope, and the two branches are disjoint.

For scope `UTILITY_SETUP` or `USPS_FILING`, the second hop reaches every linked non-terminal move and recomputes its `consent_gate` by the per-scope test above; a non-terminal move the record is not linked to — because the record named a different `move_id` — is not visited at all. Where the recomputation yields `BLOCKED`, which is the ordinary case and is the case whenever no other `GRANTED` record still covers the revoked scope, `blocked_reason` is set to `CONSENT_REVOKED`, readiness is recomputed with the consent component at 0, and a move sitting in `SCHEDULING` or `IN_SERVICE` is driven to `ATTENTION_REQUIRED`. Where it still yields `OPEN` the move keeps its `lifecycle_status`, its `blocked_reason` and its orders, and its row advances only if some derived field genuinely changed. A third hop, caused by that gate flip, moves every service order of the gated moves whose `provision_status` is `DRAFT`, `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE_SERVICE` to `SUSPENDED`, clearing `requested_service_date`, `scheduled_window_start` and `scheduled_window_end` and thereby releasing any occupancy window the order held. A fourth hop, caused by each suspension, moves that order's `credit_status` from `PENDING_CHECK` or `DEPOSIT_DUE` to `VOIDED` and moves its credit-check handoff to `VOIDED` as well.

For scope `PARTNER_MARKETING` the cascade takes an entirely different route: the second hop sets the resident's `marketing_opt_out` to true and `opted_out_at` to the current instant — applied unconditionally, so a resident already carrying `marketing_opt_out` true has `opted_out_at` re-stamped and its `version` incremented all the same — and a third hop voids every referral placement of that resident's non-terminal moves whose `placement_status` is `PLACED` or `ACCEPTED`. No move is gated, no order is suspended and no credit exposure is voided by this branch.

Restoring consent reverses only what can safely be reversed. Recording a fresh `GRANTED` consent for a previously revoked gate scope flips the affected moves' `consent_gate` back to `OPEN`, and each of that move's suspended service orders returns from `SUSPENDED` to `DRAFT`. That return is deliberately incomplete: `requested_service_date`, `scheduled_window_start` and `scheduled_window_end` all stay null, so the order must be scheduled again from scratch rather than silently reclaiming its old window — this is what closes the `SCHEDULED` → `SUSPENDED` → `DRAFT` recovery cycle. A `credit_status` of `VOIDED` likewise stays `VOIDED`; where the provider demands a check, a fresh credit-check handoff must be dispatched. A `blocked_reason` of `CONSENT_REVOKED` is reset to `NONE` by the same flip, but `lifecycle_status` is not: a move the revocation drove to `ATTENTION_REQUIRED` stays parked there until an explicit attention clearance returns it to `SCHEDULING`. The `PARTNER_MARKETING` scope has no restoration path at all — a fresh `PARTNER_MARKETING` grant leaves the resident's `marketing_opt_out` true and `opted_out_at` populated, so placement creation and conversion stay refused with `ERR_MARKETING_OPT_OUT` until the marketing-preference patch of §3.1 flips the switch back.

Consents can also lapse rather than be withdrawn, and lapsing is discovered by an operations checkpoint rather than by a background timer. A `MoveOpsAgent`, `ComplianceOfficer` or `PlatformAdmin` may run a consent evaluation against a move that is in one of the seven non-terminal lifecycle states — a `COMPLETED`, `CANCELLED` or `DUPLICATE_MERGED` move is refused with `ERR_MOVE_NOT_ACTIONABLE` — and the checkpoint takes no body of its own. It moves every `GRANTED` consent record of that resident whose `expires_at` is non-null and strictly behind the logical clock to `EXPIRED`, working through them in `created_at` order, and fires exactly the same cascade hops as a revocation with `blocked_reason` set to `CONSENT_REVOKED`; the sweep is resident-wide, so a lapsed consent scoped to a *different* move of the same resident is expired by this call too and cascades onto that other move. The same checkpoint sweeps identity, but only for the addressed move: every `PENDING` verification of that move past its `expires_at` moves to `EXPIRED` without consuming a further attempt. It closes by recomputing the addressed move's `consent_gate`, `blocked_reason` and `readiness_score` and returning that move. Because the checkpoint is idempotent, the addressed move's own `version` advances only when one of its derived or lifecycle fields actually changed: a checkpoint that finds nothing to expire returns the move untouched, at the same `version` and `updated_at` it already had. Consent records are individually readable and listable with filters on `resident_id`, `move_id`, `scope`, `state` and `source_channel`.

### 3.5. Mapping Providers and Scheduling Service Windows

Once a move is `VERIFIED`, a `MoveOpsAgent` runs **provider mapping**, the step that turns "this household is moving to this postal code" into concrete service orders against named providers. The request names the commodities wanted through a `utility_types` array — at least one entry, each drawn from `ELECTRIC`, `GAS`, `WATER` and `INTERNET`, with a repeated entry accepted and then collapsed so each distinct type is resolved exactly once in the order it first appeared — and may set `include_stop_orders`, an optional boolean defaulting to false, to request that service at the old address be closed as well as opened at the new one. Mapping requires the move's `consent_gate` to be `OPEN`, requires a `GRANTED` consent record with scope `UTILITY_SETUP` for this resident (with its `move_id` null or equal to this move), requires `duplicate_flag` to be anything other than `SUSPECTED`, and requires at least one requested utility type to have a non-empty pool of eligible territory rows. Success drives `lifecycle_status` from `VERIFIED` to `SCHEDULING`.

Eligibility is a many-to-many resolution against the territory table rather than a walk down a hierarchy. For a given requested utility type and a given service address, a territory row is eligible when its provider's `provider_status` is `ACTIVE`, that provider's `utility_type` is the requested one, the row's `postal_code` equals the address's `postal_code`, the row's `state_code` equals the address's `state_code`, and the move's `move_date` falls inside the row's closed effective interval — at or after `effective_from`, and at or before `effective_to` when that end date is present, with a null `effective_to` meaning the interval never ends. Among the eligible rows the winner is chosen by four ordered keys: default-carrier rows first (`is_default_carrier` true ahead of false), then the lowest `priority_rank`, then the latest `effective_from`, then the earliest `created_at`. When **no** requested utility type has any eligible row at all, mapping is refused with `ERR_NO_PROVIDER_COVERAGE`. That coverage test is taken over the whole set of pairings the run would produce, stop orders included: a run whose destination is uncovered for every requested type but whose origin is covered, with `include_stop_orders` true, still has work to do and so is **not** refused — it creates the stop orders and reports the types as unmapped instead.

Mapping creates one service order per covered pairing of utility type and direction. The destination address always receives an order whose `order_kind` is `START`, with `service_address_id` pointing at `destination_address_id`. The origin address receives an order whose `order_kind` is `STOP`, with `service_address_id` pointing at `origin_address_id`, but only when `include_stop_orders` is true **and** the origin's `postal_code` also has coverage for that utility type. Each new order records `move_id`, the winning provider's identifier as `provider_id`, the winning territory row's identifier as `territory_id` (pinned purely for audit), and a `utility_type` copied from the selected provider. It opens with `provision_status` `DRAFT` and with `credit_status` set from the winning provider's terms — `PENDING_CHECK` when that provider has `credit_check_required` true, and `NOT_REQUIRED` when it does not. Its `account_data` starts as an empty object; `requested_service_date`, `scheduled_window_start`, `scheduled_window_end`, `confirmation_code`, `cancellation_reason`, `failure_reason` and `waiver_reason` all start null; `readiness_snapshot` starts null; and `deposit_amount_cents` and `deposit_paid_cents` both start at 0.

`deposit_estimate_cents`, by contrast, is populated at creation with a deliberately pessimistic figure so that operations can quote a worst case before any bureau has spoken. The estimate takes the provider's deposit base — `flat_deposit_cents` under a `FLAT` policy, a platform standard base of 15000 cents under a `CREDIT_SCORE_BASED` policy, and nothing at all under a `NONE` policy, which yields an estimate of 0 — multiplies it by the worst score multiplier of 1.50, multiplies that by a neutral readiness multiplier of 1.00, rounds the product once to whole cents with round-half-up, and caps the result at the platform deposit ceiling of 50000 cents. A `CREDIT_SCORE_BASED` provider therefore always estimates 22500 cents. The estimate is written to `deposit_estimate_cents` and leaves `deposit_amount_cents` at 0; the two fields never converge by accident, and §3.6 explains why the final figure routinely lands lower.

Requested types with an empty candidate pool are not silently discarded. Mapping writes them into `unmapped_utility_types` on the move, so an operator can see which commodities were asked for and could not be routed. That list tracks the destination `START` leg alone: a type whose destination is covered but whose origin has no coverage simply produces no stop order and is **not** recorded as unmapped. The list is also rewritten wholesale on every run rather than accumulated, so a re-run that now finds coverage empties it again. Mapping returns the updated move record — not the orders it created, which must be read back from the order listing — and closes by recomputing `mapped_order_count` — the count of every service order whose `move_id` is this move, regardless of what state that order is in — and recomputing `readiness_score` under the post-verification form.

Mapping is re-runnable, and the re-run is what makes recovery from a provider failure possible. Running mapping again while the move is already in `SCHEDULING` leaves it in `SCHEDULING` (a self-loop), and running it while the move is parked in `ATTENTION_REQUIRED` leaves it in `ATTENTION_REQUIRED` (a second self-loop); both require `consent_gate` to be `OPEN` and require at least one requested utility type to have no order in `DRAFT`, `SCHEDULED`, `ACTIVE_SERVICE` or `SUSPENDED`. A re-run creates the missing orders exactly as the first run did and skips utility types that already have a live order. An order that is `CANCELLED`, `CLOSED`, `NOT_APPLICABLE` or `PROVISION_FAILED` does **not** block a replacement — that is precisely the exemption that lets a move parked after a provider failure be given a fresh order and then cleared. When every requested pairing of `utility_type` and `order_kind` already has an order in `DRAFT`, `SCHEDULED`, `ACTIVE_SERVICE` or `SUSPENDED`, the re-run has nothing to do and is refused with `ERR_TYPE_ALREADY_MAPPED`.

**Scheduling** an order is the step that commits a real appointment. The request carries a `requested_service_date`, a `scheduled_window_start` and `scheduled_window_end` bounding the technician window, and the `account_data` object the provider demands. The order must be in `DRAFT`; its parent move must be in `SCHEDULING`; the move's `consent_gate` must be `OPEN`; the move's latest identity verification must be `PASSED` and still inside its 30-day validity window; and the provider must still be `ACTIVE`. Every entry listed in that provider's `required_account_fields` must appear as a key of `account_data` carrying a non-empty value, and an omission or an empty value is refused with `ERR_MISSING_ACCOUNT_DATA`. A key present with `null`, or with a string of nothing but whitespace, counts as empty; a non-string value such as a number or a boolean counts as populated; and keys beyond the ones the provider demands are stored on the order untouched rather than rejected.

Three temporal rules then apply. The **lead-time floor**: for an order whose `order_kind` is `START`, `requested_service_date` must be at or after the logical clock's date plus the provider's `scheduling_lead_days`; the identical floor applies to a `STOP` order unless that provider has `same_day_stop_allowed` true, in which case the floor is waived outright and a stop can be booked for today. A violation is `ERR_LEAD_TIME_VIOLATION`. The **window shape**: `scheduled_window_start` must be strictly earlier than `scheduled_window_end`, both must fall on the calendar date named by `requested_service_date`, and the span between them must be at most 8 hours; a violation is `ERR_INVALID_SERVICE_WINDOW`. The **occupancy rule**: the interval from `scheduled_window_start` up to but excluding `scheduled_window_end` must not intersect the corresponding interval of any other service order sharing this order's `service_address_id` and `utility_type` whose `provision_status` is `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE_SERVICE`. Because the interval is half-open, a window that begins at exactly the instant another window ends does **not** collide and is accepted; genuine overlap is refused with `ERR_SERVICE_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Scheduling performs no deposit arithmetic of its own: the order's `deposit_amount_cents` is never consulted here, and the 50000-cent ceiling binds only the two places a deposit figure is actually computed.

What scheduling deliberately does **not** check is credit. An order may be scheduled while its `credit_status` is `NOT_REQUIRED`, `PENDING_CHECK`, `CLEARED`, `DEPOSIT_DUE` or `DEPOSIT_PAID` — the credit dimension gates activation and nothing else, which is exactly what makes the two dimensions on a single order row independent of one another. A successful schedule writes `requested_service_date`, `scheduled_window_start`, `scheduled_window_end` and `account_data`, drives `provision_status` from `DRAFT` to `SCHEDULED`, adds one to the occupancy count for that pairing of `service_address_id` and `utility_type`, and lifts the parent move's readiness utility component.

Some commodities simply do not apply — water included in the rent, internet already wired by the landlord. A `MoveOpsAgent` may **waive** an order sitting in `DRAFT`, supplying a `waiver_reason` of 1 to 500 characters, provided the parent move is in `SCHEDULING` or `ATTENTION_REQUIRED`. The order's `provision_status` becomes `NOT_APPLICABLE`. A waived order whose `credit_status` was `PENDING_CHECK` or `DEPOSIT_DUE` moves to `VOIDED`, and any credit-check handoff attached to it moves to `VOIDED` as well, releasing the move's open deposit liability. For readiness purposes a `NOT_APPLICABLE` order counts as done rather than as outstanding, so waiving an unroutable commodity moves the move toward completion instead of stalling it.

Service orders are individually readable, exposing both state dimensions and both deposit figures, and are listable with filters on `move_id`, `provider_id`, `service_address_id`, `territory_id`, `provision_status`, `credit_status`, `order_kind` and `utility_type`. The single-order read is ownership-scoped for a `ProviderAgent`, whose `X-User-ID` must equal the order's `provider_id`; the listing is **not**, so the same agent sees every provider's orders and must pass its own `provider_id` as a filter to narrow them.

### 3.6. Credit Handoff, Deposits, Activation and Failure

Providers that demand a credit decision have their orders opened at `credit_status` `PENDING_CHECK`, and clearing that posture is a two-message exchange with a bureau. A `MoveOpsAgent` dispatches a **credit-check handoff** naming the `bureau` — one of `EXPERIAN`, `EQUIFAX` or `TRANSUNION`. Dispatch requires the parent move to be in `SCHEDULING` or `ATTENTION_REQUIRED`, requires its `provision_status` to be `DRAFT`, then requires the order's `credit_status` to be `PENDING_CHECK` — any other credit posture is refused with `ERR_ORDER_NOT_SCHEDULABLE` and never with `ERR_CREDIT_NOT_CLEARED`, because the unnumbered credit gate of §2.7 belongs to activation alone — requires `consent_gate` to be `OPEN`, and requires that no other handoff for this order is currently `SUBMITTED`; a second concurrent handoff is refused with `ERR_HANDOFF_ALREADY_OPEN`. The new row records `utility_order_id`, lands in `handoff_status` `SUBMITTED` with `score`, `result` and `responded_at` all null, and stamps `submitted_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`.

Dispatch has one further side effect that is easy to overlook and central to the deposit arithmetic: it writes `readiness_snapshot` on the order, freezing the parent move's `readiness_score` **as of that instant**. The evaluation order here is pinned and observable. First, dispatch freezes the snapshot. Second, the bureau response computes the deposit from that frozen snapshot and never from the live `readiness_score`. Third, only after the deposit has been written is the move's `readiness_score` recomputed. The consequence is that a readiness change landing between the dispatch and the bureau's answer does **not** move the deposit, while the same change landing before the dispatch does.

The bureau answers through the inbound callback channel with a `score` between 300 and 850 inclusive, against a handoff that must still be `SUBMITTED`. The response writes `score`, sets `responded_at` to the current instant, moves `handoff_status` from `SUBMITTED` to `RESPONDED`, and derives `result` from the score using two thresholds; the response body is the handoff record itself rather than the order whose credit posture it re-decided. The `score` is validated before the handoff-state check, so an out-of-range score against an already-`RESPONDED` handoff surfaces `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than `ERR_HANDOFF_NOT_OPEN`. A score of 640 or above is a `CLEAR` result. A score from 580 through 639 is a `DEPOSIT_REQUIRED` result. A score of 579 or below is a `DECLINED` result. Each band drives the order's credit dimension differently, and the three branches are genuinely disjoint.

A `CLEAR` result moves `credit_status` from `PENDING_CHECK` to `CLEARED` and leaves `deposit_amount_cents` at 0. A `DEPOSIT_REQUIRED` result moves `credit_status` to `DEPOSIT_DUE`, writes the computed deposit into `deposit_amount_cents`, and raises the move's open deposit liability by that amount. A `DECLINED` result moves `credit_status` to `DECLINED`, leaves `deposit_amount_cents` at 0, and triggers a two-hop cascade: the order's `provision_status` moves from `DRAFT` **or** `SCHEDULED` to `PROVISION_FAILED` with `failure_reason` recorded as `CREDIT_DECLINED` and both window fields cleared, an order found in any other provisioning state keeping that state and merely having its row touched; and then the parent move moves from `SCHEDULING` to `ATTENTION_REQUIRED` with `blocked_reason` set to `CREDIT_DECLINED`. That second hop fires whenever the move is in `SCHEDULING` or `IN_SERVICE`, merely re-stamps `blocked_reason` when the move is already `ATTENTION_REQUIRED`, and is skipped in every other lifecycle state — and it fires on the strength of the decline alone, whether or not the first hop actually found an order to fail. Unlike the provider-reported failure below, this branch deliberately does **not** void the credit dimension: `credit_status` settles at `DECLINED` rather than travelling on to `VOIDED`, and the handoff that carried the decline stays `RESPONDED`.

The deposit itself is a product of three factors, each read from a specific place. The **base** is the provider's `flat_deposit_cents` when `deposit_policy` is `FLAT`, the platform standard base of 15000 cents when `deposit_policy` is `CREDIT_SCORE_BASED`, and 0 under a `NONE` policy — so a `NONE`-policy provider that nevertheless demanded a credit check and drew a deposit band still lands the order at `DEPOSIT_DUE`, with `deposit_amount_cents` 0, settled by a payment of exactly 0 cents. The **score multiplier** reads the bureau score: 1.50 for a score from 580 through 599, 1.25 for a score from 600 through 619, and 1.00 for a score from 620 through 639. The **readiness multiplier** reads the order's frozen `readiness_snapshot` and nothing else: 1.20 when the snapshot is below 50, 1.00 when the snapshot is from 50 through 79 inclusive, and 0.90 when the snapshot is 80 or above. The three factors are multiplied together as exact decimals, the product is rounded once to whole cents with round-half-up, and the result is capped at 50000 cents before being written to `deposit_amount_cents`. A weak household therefore pays a fifth more and a well-prepared one a tenth less. Concretely, a `CREDIT_SCORE_BASED` provider with a bureau score of 639 and a frozen snapshot of 40 yields 18000 cents against the 22500-cent estimate written at mapping, and the same score with a snapshot of 85 yields 13500 cents — identical provider terms, three different numbers, decided by which variant is running and what the snapshot froze.

Paying a deposit is an exact-match operation open to a `MoveOpsAgent`, a `PlatformAdmin` or the `Resident` itself — and for a `Resident` caller the ownership comparison is against the parent *move's* `resident_id` rather than anything on the order, an outsider being refused with `ERR_OWNERSHIP_VIOLATION` before the body is read at all. The request supplies `amount_cents`, a non-negative integer which must equal the order's `deposit_amount_cents` precisely; neither a partial payment nor an over-payment is accepted, and any mismatch is refused with `ERR_DEPOSIT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`. Payment additionally requires the parent move to sit in one of the seven non-terminal lifecycle states, a terminal parent being refused with `ERR_MOVE_NOT_ACTIONABLE`, and requires the order's `provision_status` to be `DRAFT` or `SCHEDULED` — that provisioning test running ahead of the `DEPOSIT_DUE` credit test, though both surface `ERR_ORDER_NOT_SCHEDULABLE`. A successful payment writes `deposit_paid_cents` with the paid amount, moves `credit_status` from `DEPOSIT_DUE` to `DEPOSIT_PAID`, and drops the move's open deposit liability by `deposit_amount_cents`. Handoffs are readable individually and listable with filters on `utility_order_id`, `handoff_status` and `result`.

**Activation** is the only moment at which the credit dimension constrains provisioning. The provider's own `ProviderAgent`, or a `PlatformAdmin`, reports the service live by supplying a `confirmation_code` of 1 to 64 characters. The parent move must sit in one of the seven non-terminal lifecycle states, a terminal parent being refused with `ERR_MOVE_NOT_ACTIONABLE` ahead of every gate below; the order must be `SCHEDULED`; when the caller is a `ProviderAgent` its `X-User-ID` must equal the order's `provider_id`; the provider must still be `ACTIVE`; the parent move's `consent_gate` must be `OPEN`; the logical clock must have reached or passed `scheduled_window_start`, an earlier attempt being refused with `ERR_ACTIVATION_TOO_EARLY`; and the order's `credit_status` must be `NOT_REQUIRED`, `CLEARED` or `DEPOSIT_PAID`, any of `PENDING_CHECK`, `DEPOSIT_DUE`, `DECLINED` or `VOIDED` being refused with `ERR_CREDIT_NOT_CLEARED`. Activation writes `confirmation_code`, moves `provision_status` from `SCHEDULED` to `ACTIVE_SERVICE`, keeps the occupancy window occupied (an `ACTIVE_SERVICE` order still counts against the address), recomputes the parent move's `readiness_score`, and may then bubble the move from `SCHEDULING` up to `IN_SERVICE` if that transition's own preconditions have become satisfied.

**Closure** ends a live service. A `ProviderAgent` owning the order, a `MoveOpsAgent` or a `PlatformAdmin` may close an order in `ACTIVE_SERVICE`, optionally supplying a `final_read_note` — accepted and validated as a string of 1 to 500 characters when it is present, but not retained as a field of the service order resource, which carries no `final_read_note` attribute — provided the logical clock has reached or passed `scheduled_window_end`, an early attempt being refused with `ERR_ORDER_NOT_SCHEDULABLE`. Closure is the one order write whose parent-move test is merely the completion lock rather than the full non-terminal test: only a `COMPLETED` parent refuses it, with `ERR_MOVE_NOT_ACTIONABLE`. Closure moves `provision_status` to `CLOSED`, releases the occupancy window so the count for that address and utility type drops by one, and keeps both window fields populated for audit. The parent move's readiness utility component is recomputed.

**Provision failure** is the provider's way of saying the order cannot be fulfilled. A `ProviderAgent` owning the order, or a `PlatformAdmin`, reports it against an order in `DRAFT` **or** `SCHEDULED` — a provider may condemn a pairing before any window was ever booked, so the report is not confined to scheduled work — with a `failure_reason` of 1 to 500 characters, and against a parent move sitting in one of the seven non-terminal lifecycle states. The first hop writes `failure_reason`, moves `provision_status` to `PROVISION_FAILED`, and clears both `scheduled_window_start` and `scheduled_window_end`, releasing the occupancy window. A second hop, caused by that failure, moves the order's `credit_status` from `PENDING_CHECK` or `DEPOSIT_DUE` to `VOIDED` and moves any credit-check handoff in `SUBMITTED` or `RESPONDED` to `VOIDED`, restoring the move's open deposit liability to its pre-handoff value; `deposit_amount_cents` is retained for audit but no longer counted. A third hop moves the parent move from `SCHEDULING` to `ATTENTION_REQUIRED` with `blocked_reason` set to `PROVISION_FAILED`, or from `IN_SERVICE` to `ATTENTION_REQUIRED` when the failure struck a move whose service had already gone live; a move already `ATTENTION_REQUIRED` keeps that state and simply has `blocked_reason` re-stamped, and a move in any other lifecycle state is left untouched by this hop.

Once a handoff has reached `VOIDED`, no bureau response is accepted against it — a late answer is refused with `ERR_HANDOFF_NOT_OPEN`, exactly as an answer to an already-`RESPONDED` handoff is.

### 3.7. Redirecting the Mail: Postal Change-of-Address Filings

A move is not finished when the lights come on; the household's mail has to follow it. A `MoveOpsAgent` or `ComplianceOfficer` drafts a postal change-of-address filing against the move, naming a `filing_type` of `INDIVIDUAL`, `FAMILY` or `BUSINESS` and a `forward_start_date` — the first date on which mail is redirected. The system copies `resident_id` down from the move and derives `forward_end_date` as `forward_start_date` plus the standard 365-day forwarding term minus one day, so the period is inclusive at both ends: a `forward_start_date` of 2026-08-20 yields a `forward_end_date` of 2027-08-19. The filing opens in `status` `DRAFT` with `submitted_by_user_id`, `usps_confirmation_code`, `reject_reason`, `mail_forwarding_confirmed_at` and `cancellation_reason` all null and `resubmit_count` at 0.

The lifecycle gate on drafting is deliberately loose: the parent move may be in `IDENTITY_PENDING`, `IDENTITY_FAILED`, `VERIFIED`, `SCHEDULING`, `ATTENTION_REQUIRED` or `IN_SERVICE`. That breadth is intentional, because the rule meant to stop a premature filing is the identity rule rather than the coarser lifecycle rule — drafting separately requires the move's `verified_at` to be non-null, so a filing prepared for a household that has not yet cleared identity is refused with `ERR_IDENTITY_NOT_VERIFIED` rather than with `ERR_MOVE_NOT_ACTIONABLE`. Drafting also requires the move's `duplicate_flag` to be anything other than `SUSPECTED`, and requires that no other filing on this move is currently in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `CONFIRMED` — a second active filing being refused with `ERR_FILING_NOT_SUBMITTABLE`, tested after the duplicate rung. Nothing about `forward_start_date` is checked at draft time; the clock comparison waits for submission. While the filing sits in `DRAFT` the move's readiness filing component stays at 0: preparing paperwork earns nothing.

**Submission** is the step that actually reaches the postal service, and it carries the densest precondition set in the system. It takes no body of its own. The filing must be in `DRAFT` (or in `REJECTED`, discussed below); the parent move must be in `VERIFIED`, `SCHEDULING`, `ATTENTION_REQUIRED` or `IN_SERVICE`; the move's latest identity verification must be `PASSED` and the logical clock must still be within 30 days of `verified_at`, a lapsed window surfacing `ERR_IDENTITY_VERIFICATION_EXPIRED`; a `GRANTED` consent record with scope `USPS_FILING` must exist for the resident, with its `move_id` null or equal to this move; the destination address must have `usps_deliverable` true, an undeliverable destination being refused with `ERR_ADDRESS_NOT_DELIVERABLE`; and `forward_start_date` must be at or after the logical clock's date, a backdated start being refused with `ERR_FORWARD_DATE_INVALID`. Submitting a filing that is already `SUBMITTED` or `CONFIRMED` — or posting a postal answer against one still in `DRAFT`, or trying to withdraw one already `CONFIRMED` — is refused with `ERR_FILING_NOT_SUBMITTABLE`.

Two further rules make submission interesting. The first is **segregation of duties**: the acting `X-User-ID` must not match the `reviewer_user_id` of any `PASSED` `MANUAL_OVERRIDE` identity verification on this move. A compliance reviewer who personally vouched for a mover's identity by hand may not also be the person who files that mover's mail redirection; the attempt is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. A different reviewer, or a `MoveOpsAgent` who granted nothing, submits without obstruction, and the bar applies only within the move whose override that user granted. The second is the **one-active-forwarding-period rule**: the resident may have no other filing in `SUBMITTED` or `CONFIRMED` whose closed period from `forward_start_date` through `forward_end_date` intersects the period being submitted. Because each period spans a full year and the aggregate capacity per resident is exactly one, a second overlapping redirection is refused with `ERR_FORWARDING_PERIOD_CONFLICT`; a period that begins after a prior period's inclusive end is accepted. Successful submission moves `status` to `SUBMITTED`, writes `submitted_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`, and lifts the move's readiness filing component to 60.

The postal service answers through the inbound gateway with an `outcome`, against a filing that must be `SUBMITTED`. An `outcome` of `CONFIRMED` requires a `usps_confirmation_code` of 1 to 64 characters, its absence being refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` at the schema rung and therefore ahead of the filing-state check; it moves `status` to `CONFIRMED`, writes that code and sets `mail_forwarding_confirmed_at` to the current instant, raises the readiness filing component to 100, and may then bubble the parent move from `SCHEDULING` up to `IN_SERVICE` when that transition's own conditions have become satisfied. An `outcome` of `REJECTED` requires a `reject_reason` of 1 to 500 characters, likewise refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` when absent; it writes that reason, moves `status` to `REJECTED`, and in a second hop moves the parent move from `SCHEDULING` to `ATTENTION_REQUIRED` with `blocked_reason` set to `FILING_REJECTED` — the same hop also reaching a move in `IN_SERVICE`, merely re-stamping `blocked_reason` on a move already `ATTENTION_REQUIRED`, and leaving any other lifecycle state alone.

A rejection is recoverable but not indefinitely. A `REJECTED` filing may be resubmitted, which returns `status` to `SUBMITTED`, adds one to `resubmit_count`, clears `reject_reason`, rewrites `submitted_by_user_id` with the current actor, and restores the readiness filing component to 60. Every submission precondition is re-evaluated at resubmission time — a consent revoked in the interim, or an identity window that has since lapsed, blocks the retry exactly as it would block a first attempt. Resubmission additionally requires `resubmit_count` to be below the platform limit of 2, so a filing may be submitted once and retried at most twice; the third retry is refused with `ERR_RESUBMIT_LIMIT_REACHED`.

Withdrawal is available to a `ComplianceOfficer` or `PlatformAdmin` for a filing in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `REJECTED`, recording a **required** `cancellation_reason` of 1 to 500 characters; an omitted reason is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Cancellation moves `status` to `CANCELLED`, stores that reason and clears `mail_forwarding_confirmed_at`, which is what releases the resident's forwarding-period slot and lets a corrected filing be submitted for an overlapping period; `usps_confirmation_code` and `reject_reason` are left exactly as they stood. The move's readiness filing component drops back to 0 whenever the parent move is still non-terminal. Withdrawal and the postal-response callback are the two filing writes that carry no parent-move lifecycle gate at all, so each remains accepted against a filing whose move has since reached `COMPLETED`, `CANCELLED` or `DUPLICATE_MERGED` — where the parent is terminal, withdrawal simply leaves the move's derived fields alone. A filing that reaches `CANCELLED` through a cascade rather than through this endpoint — the move-cancellation unwind of §3.9 and the duplicate merge of §3.2 — has no reason threaded through the hop, so its `cancellation_reason` stays null even though the service orders cancelled alongside it do carry one. Filings are readable individually — the `Resident` may read its own — and listable with filters on `move_id`, `resident_id`, `status` and `filing_type`.

### 3.8. Referral Placements, Attribution and Revenue Share

Movers buy things: internet service, renters insurance, moving labour. The platform monetises that by placing partner referrals against a move, and the money that results is split three ways — the partner pays a commission, the feed source that supplied the lead takes a contractual cut of it, and the platform keeps the remainder.

A `MoveOpsAgent` creates a placement by naming a `partner_id` — required, but resolved at rung 4, so an absent or null value surfaces `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` while a value naming no partner surfaces `ERR_REFERENCE_NOT_FOUND` — and supplying `expected_move_version` for optimistic concurrency. The parent move must be in `VERIFIED`, `SCHEDULING`, `ATTENTION_REQUIRED` or `IN_SERVICE`; the resident's `marketing_opt_out` must be false, an opted-out household being refused with `ERR_MARKETING_OPT_OUT`; a `GRANTED` consent record with scope `PARTNER_MARKETING` must exist for that resident; the partner's `partner_status` must be `ACTIVE`, a paused partner being refused with `ERR_PARTNER_PAUSED`; no other placement on this move may share the partner's `vertical` while sitting in `PLACED`, `ACCEPTED`, `CONVERTED` or `SETTLED`, a second live placement in the same vertical being refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_VERTICAL_PLACEMENT`; and the count of that partner's placements whose `placed_at` falls in the logical clock's calendar month — every placement of that partner, across every move and every resident, counted regardless of its `placement_status`, so a row that has since gone `DECLINED`, `VOIDED` or `REVERSED` still consumes quota for that month — must be below the partner's `monthly_placement_cap`, an exhausted quota being refused with `ERR_PARTNER_CAP_REACHED`. The new row copies `vertical` from the partner, records `placed_at` as the current instant, opens in `placement_status` `PLACED` with `accepted_at`, `converted_at`, `decline_reason` and `reversal_reason` null and with `gross_revenue_cents`, `settled_gross_revenue_cents`, `attributed_share_cents` and `feed_source_share_cents` all 0, and adds one to the parent move's `version`.

The partner that owns the placement — its `X-User-ID` matching `partner_id` — accepts it while it is `PLACED` and while the move's `consent_gate` is `OPEN`; acceptance takes no body of its own, and the move's `lifecycle_status` is not consulted. Acceptance moves `placement_status` to `ACCEPTED` and stamps `accepted_at`, touching no row but the placement, which matters because that instant is the attribution anchor for partners configured with an `attribution_basis` of `PLACEMENT_ACCEPTANCE`.

From `ACCEPTED` the placement forks, and the two branches are exactly as consequential as one another. **Conversion** is the revenue branch. The owning partner reports a `gross_revenue_cents` of at least 1, and the logical clock must be at or before the attribution anchor plus the partner's `attribution_window_days`, the boundary day itself still qualifying — a `PLACEMENT_CLICK` partner anchors on `placed_at`, a `PLACEMENT_ACCEPTANCE` partner on `accepted_at`, and a late report is refused with `ERR_ATTRIBUTION_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. The resident's `marketing_opt_out` must still be false. Conversion writes `gross_revenue_cents` and `converted_at`, moves `placement_status` to `CONVERTED`, computes both share figures, and posts two ledger entries. **Decline** is the mirror branch: the owning partner supplies a `decline_reason` of 1 to 500 characters and, unlike acceptance and conversion, is asked for no `consent_gate` check of its own, `placement_status` moves to `DECLINED`, and the placement posts no ledger entry at all, leaves `attributed_share_cents` at 0, and leaves the feed source's `accrued_share_cents` untouched. Two structurally identical requests against the same `ACCEPTED` row therefore diverge into "two ledger rows and a changed feed-source balance" versus "no ledger rows and an unchanged balance"; a placement that has reached `DECLINED` is terminal and cannot later convert, an attempt being refused with `ERR_PLACEMENT_NOT_CONVERTIBLE`.

The waterfall that turns gross revenue into shares runs in a fixed order. First the **commission** the platform is owed by the partner: under a `FLAT_BOUNTY` payout model the commission is simply the partner's `bounty_cents`, independent of the reported revenue. Under a `REV_SHARE_PCT` model the commission at conversion is provisional, because the gross figure a partner reports on the day of sale is not yet final — it is `gross_revenue_cents` multiplied by the partner's `rev_share_pct` and then by 0.90, holding back a tenth against later adjustment, rounded once to whole cents. Second the **feed source's cut**: the commission multiplied by the originating feed source's `referral_revenue_share_pct`, rounded once to whole cents — taken regardless of that feed source's `feed_status`, since the suspension rung of §2.7 binds move intake alone and a `SUSPENDED` feed keeps earning on the leads it already supplied. Third the **platform remainder**: the commission minus the feed source's cut, taken as an exact subtraction and never rounded a second time, so the three parts always add back to the commission. The placement stores the commission in `attributed_share_cents` and the feed source's cut in `feed_source_share_cents`. Worked through: a gross of 100000 cents against a 0.3000 revenue share yields a provisional commission of 27000 cents; a feed source on a 0.1750 share takes 4725 cents of it; the platform keeps 22275 cents.

Every movement of money is journalled. Conversion posts an `ACCRUAL` entry whose `amount_cents` is the positive commission and a `FEED_SOURCE_SHARE` entry whose `amount_cents` is the negative of the feed source's cut. **Settlement**, available to a `PlatformAdmin` on a `CONVERTED` placement, takes a `settled_gross_revenue_cents` that must be at least 1 and must not exceed twice the recorded `gross_revenue_cents`. A value of 0, or one above that ceiling, is refused with `ERR_SETTLEMENT_AMOUNT_INVALID`; a *negative* value never reaches that rule, failing the schema's non-negative check first and surfacing `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Settlement recomputes both shares without the holdback — the commission becomes `settled_gross_revenue_cents` multiplied by `rev_share_pct`, rounded once — overwrites `attributed_share_cents` and `feed_source_share_cents`, moves `placement_status` to `SETTLED`, and posts **only the deltas**: an `ADJUSTMENT` entry for the settled commission minus the provisional commission, and a `FEED_SOURCE_SHARE_ADJUSTMENT` entry for the negative of the settled feed share minus the provisional feed share. Continuing the example, a settled gross of 100000 cents lifts the commission to 30000 and the feed share to 5250, so the ledger receives an `ADJUSTMENT` of positive 3000 and a `FEED_SOURCE_SHARE_ADJUSTMENT` of negative 525 — never a restatement of the original figures. Both delta entries are posted unconditionally, even when a delta works out to 0, which is exactly what settling a `FLAT_BOUNTY` placement produces: the bounty commission is identical before and after, so that settlement still journals an `ADJUSTMENT` of 0 and a `FEED_SOURCE_SHARE_ADJUSTMENT` of 0 rather than nothing at all.

**Reversal** handles the chargeback. A `PlatformAdmin`, or the owning partner, supplies a `reversal_reason` of 1 to 500 characters against a `CONVERTED` placement; `gross_revenue_cents` is retained on the row for audit even though both share fields are zeroed. Reversal posts a `REVERSAL` entry for the negative of the current `attributed_share_cents` and a `FEED_SOURCE_SHARE_REVERSAL` entry for the positive of the current `feed_source_share_cents`, then resets both share fields to 0 and moves `placement_status` to `REVERSED`. The invariant this protects is arithmetic: the sum of `amount_cents` over a placement's ledger entries always equals that placement's `attributed_share_cents` minus its `feed_source_share_cents`, and once the placement is `REVERSED` that sum is exactly 0. A settled placement is terminal and is not reversible.

A placement that never reached `CONVERTED` can still be voided — by a consent revocation, by a marketing opt-out, by the move being cancelled, or by the move being merged away as a duplicate. Voiding requires precisely that the placement never converted, so there is no ledger entry to unwind; it posts nothing, leaves `attributed_share_cents` at 0, moves `placement_status` to `VOIDED`, and frees the pairing of `move_id` and `vertical` so a fresh placement can be made in that vertical later.

The feed source's running balance is an aggregate with a pinned evaluation order. `accrued_share_cents` on a `FeedSource` is the sum of the already-rounded per-placement `feed_source_share_cents` over that source's placements in `CONVERTED` or `SETTLED` — each placement's share is rounded first, then the rounded figures are added. Summing raw products and rounding once at the end gives a different answer, and the platform does not do that: two placements each with a gross of 3715 cents, one on a 0.3000 revenue share and one on a 0.1750 share, each round to 176 cents, so the balance is 352 cents rather than the 351 a single terminal rounding would produce. The balance is recomputed after every conversion, settlement and reversal, and the feed source's own `updated_at` and `version` advance only when that recomputation actually changes the stored total.

Ledger entries themselves are append-only records rather than mutable resources. Each carries `placement_id`, `move_id` and `feed_source_id` denormalised for reporting, an `entry_type` from `ACCRUAL`, `FEED_SOURCE_SHARE`, `ADJUSTMENT`, `FEED_SOURCE_SHARE_ADJUSTMENT`, `REVERSAL` and `FEED_SOURCE_SHARE_REVERSAL`, a signed `amount_cents` in which a positive value is revenue owed to the platform and a negative value is an obligation to a feed source, and a `posted_at` instant taken from the logical clock. Because they are never edited, they carry only an `id` and a `created_at` — no `updated_at` and no `version`. They are readable individually by a `PlatformAdmin` or `ComplianceOfficer` and listable with filters on `placement_id`, `move_id`, `feed_source_id` and `entry_type`, in `created_at` order; a `FeedIntegrator` and a `PartnerAgent` may read the list to reconcile their own statements, but that listing is not ownership-scoped for them: it returns every entry in the system and has to be narrowed with the `feed_source_id` or `placement_id` filter. Placements are readable individually — a `PartnerAgent` only for a placement whose `partner_id` equals its own `X-User-ID` — and listable with filters on `move_id`, `partner_id`, `placement_status` and `vertical`, that listing likewise unscoped, so the same agent sees every partner's placements unless it filters by its own `partner_id`.

### 3.9. Readiness Scoring, Attention Clearance, Completion and Cancellation

`readiness_score` is the single number operations watch, and it is recomputed at the end of every write that touches a move's identity, its service orders, its filing or its consent state. It is a weighted blend of four components, each an integer from 0 through 100 computed and rounded to whole numbers **before** weighting, with the weighted sum rounded once to a whole number **after** summing and then clamped to the inclusive range 0 through 100. The inputs are strictly the move's own children: its identity verifications, its non-`CANCELLED` service orders, its newest non-`CANCELLED` filing, and the `GRANTED` consent records of its resident whose `move_id` is null or equal to this move.

The `identity_component` is 100 when the move's latest identity verification by `created_at` descending is `PASSED` and the logical clock is still within 30 days of `verified_at`, 50 when that latest verification is `PENDING`, and 0 in every other case — including a `PASSED` verification whose validity window has lapsed. The `utility_component` divides done by mapped: mapped counts the move's service orders whose `provision_status` is anything other than `CANCELLED`, done counts those in `SCHEDULED`, `ACTIVE_SERVICE`, `CLOSED` or `NOT_APPLICABLE`, and the component is 100 times done divided by mapped rounded to a whole number, or 0 when mapped is 0. A cancelled order therefore leaves the ratio alone rather than dragging it down, while a waived order counts as progress. The `filing_component` is 100 when the newest non-`CANCELLED` filing is `CONFIRMED`, 60 when it is `SUBMITTED`, and 0 otherwise — a draft, a rejection, no filing at all, or nothing but cancelled filings. The `consent_component` is 0 outright whenever `consent_gate` is `BLOCKED`; with the gate `OPEN` it is 100 when `GRANTED` consents exist for both the `UTILITY_SETUP` and `USPS_FILING` scopes, 50 when exactly one of those two scopes is covered, and 0 when neither is.

Two variants then combine those components, and which one runs is decided by the move's own lifecycle rather than by the caller. The **pre-verification** form applies while the move is in `INTAKE`, `IDENTITY_PENDING` or `IDENTITY_FAILED`: it drops the identity term entirely and renormalises the remaining three weights — utility at 0.30, filing at 0.20, consent at 0.20 — by dividing the weighted sum by 0.70, so a household that has not yet cleared identity is scored only on what it can control. The **post-verification** form applies in every other lifecycle state, including the terminal `COMPLETED`, `CANCELLED` and `DUPLICATE_MERGED` states, so a cancelled or merged move still reports a defined score: it applies all four weights — identity 0.30, utility 0.30, filing 0.20, consent 0.20 — with no renormalisation. One snapshot of children yields three different numbers depending on variant and identity state. With a `utility_component` of 100, a `filing_component` of 0 and a `consent_component` of 50, the pre-verification form reports 57; the post-verification form reports 70 when the `identity_component` is 100 and 40 when it is 0.

The computed read exposes that arithmetic to operations. It returns the `move_id`, the `variant` that was used, all four of `identity_component`, `utility_component`, `filing_component` and `consent_component`, and the resulting `readiness_score`. A `variant` query parameter accepting `pre_verification` or `post_verification` overrides the lifecycle-implied choice, which is how an agent can ask "what would this move score once identity clears" without waiting for it to clear. Omitting the parameter, or supplying it empty, falls back to the lifecycle-implied variant; supplying any other value is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The read is pure: it recomputes the figures on demand and writes nothing back, so the move's own stored `readiness_score`, `updated_at` and `version` are untouched by it and an overriding `variant` never persists.

**Attention clearance** closes the loop between `SCHEDULING` and `ATTENTION_REQUIRED`. A move lands in `ATTENTION_REQUIRED` from `SCHEDULING` in three distinct ways, each stamping a different `blocked_reason`: a provider failure or a declined bureau response parks it with `PROVISION_FAILED` or `CREDIT_DECLINED` respectively, a consent revocation parks it with `CONSENT_REVOKED` and simultaneously drives `consent_gate` to `BLOCKED`, and a postal rejection parks it with `FILING_REJECTED`. A move already `IN_SERVICE` is parked the same way when one of its live children leaves `ACTIVE_SERVICE` or `CONFIRMED`, with the `blocked_reason` set by whichever cascade fired.

A `MoveOpsAgent` clears the parking, optionally recording a `clearance_note` of 1 to 500 characters, and the clearance is refused unless every blocker actually has a remedy. The three remedies are checked in a fixed order after the lifecycle gate. First, `consent_gate` must be back to `OPEN`, which means a fresh consent must have been granted if a revocation caused the parking; a still-blocked gate is refused with `ERR_CONSENT_REVOKED`. Second, no filing of the move may still be in `REJECTED`, so a rejected filing must have been resubmitted or withdrawn; a lingering rejection is refused with `ERR_FILING_NOT_SUBMITTABLE`, and because it is checked here rather than at rung 9's position, a move that is both gated and carrying a rejected filing surfaces `ERR_CONSENT_REVOKED`. Third, every service order of the move whose `provision_status` is `PROVISION_FAILED` or whose `credit_status` is `DECLINED` must have a replacement order covering the same pairing of `utility_type` and `order_kind` in `DRAFT`, `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE_SERVICE` — which is exactly why re-running provider mapping while parked is permitted, since that re-run is what manufactures the replacement. A clearance attempted while a failed or declined order still has no replacement is refused with `ERR_UNRESOLVED_PROVISION_FAILURE`. Success returns `lifecycle_status` to `SCHEDULING`, resets `blocked_reason` to `NONE`, and recomputes `readiness_score`.

The upward path is not driven by an operator at all. A move rises from `SCHEDULING` to `IN_SERVICE` as a bubble-up on the last qualifying child mutation — the activation that leaves no order outstanding, or the postal confirmation that completes the paperwork. Four conditions must hold simultaneously: `mapped_order_count` must be at least 1, every service order of the move must be in `ACTIVE_SERVICE`, `CLOSED`, `CANCELLED` or `NOT_APPLICABLE`, at least one of them must actually be in `ACTIVE_SERVICE`, and exactly one filing of the move must be `CONFIRMED`. A move with every order waived and none live does not qualify, and neither does one whose mail is still unconfirmed.

**Completion** is the operator's final act. A `MoveOpsAgent` completes a move in `IN_SERVICE` provided the logical clock's date has reached or passed `move_date` — a move cannot be declared finished before the household has actually moved, an early attempt being refused with `ERR_MOVE_DATE_NOT_REACHED` — provided every service order is in `ACTIVE_SERVICE`, `CLOSED`, `CANCELLED` or `NOT_APPLICABLE`, and provided `consent_gate` is `OPEN`. It takes no body of its own, and its gates run in a pinned order: the `IN_SERVICE` lifecycle check first, then the consent gate, then the move-date floor, and the order-settlement sweep last — so a move that is simultaneously gated and short of its move date surfaces `ERR_CONSENT_REVOKED`, and one that clears both but still holds an order in `DRAFT`, `SCHEDULED`, `SUSPENDED` or `PROVISION_FAILED` is refused with `ERR_MOVE_NOT_ACTIONABLE`. Completion moves `lifecycle_status` to `COMPLETED`, recomputes `readiness_score` a final time, and locks the move down: no further mutation of the move or of any of its children is accepted, and any attempt is refused with `ERR_MOVE_NOT_ACTIONABLE`.

**Cancellation** is the compensating unwind, available to a `MoveOpsAgent`, a `PlatformAdmin` or the originating `FeedIntegrator` from any of the seven non-terminal lifecycle states — `INTAKE`, `IDENTITY_PENDING`, `IDENTITY_FAILED`, `VERIFIED`, `SCHEDULING`, `ATTENTION_REQUIRED` and `IN_SERVICE` — with a required `cancellation_reason` of 1 to 500 characters recorded on the move. Everything the move opened is closed in one pass. Every service order in `DRAFT`, `SCHEDULED`, `ACTIVE_SERVICE` or `SUSPENDED` moves to `CANCELLED` with `cancellation_reason` `MOVE_CANCELLED` and both window fields cleared, which releases each occupied service window. Each such cancelled order whose `credit_status` was `PENDING_CHECK` or `DEPOSIT_DUE` moves to `VOIDED`, and any of its handoffs in `SUBMITTED` or `RESPONDED` moves to `VOIDED`, so the move's open deposit liability returns to zero. Every filing in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `REJECTED` moves to `CANCELLED` with `mail_forwarding_confirmed_at` cleared, releasing the resident's forwarding slot. Every placement in `PLACED` or `ACCEPTED` moves to `VOIDED`, posting no ledger entry. Finally `blocked_reason` is reset to `NONE` and `readiness_score` is recomputed under the post-verification form. The cancelled move does not vanish from the system's memory: it becomes eligible as a reactivation candidate for a later intake, so a household that cancels in March and re-books in April is recognised as the same household rather than treated as a fresh lead.

Moves are readable individually, exposing every derived field — `lifecycle_status`, `duplicate_flag`, `duplicate_match_score`, `consent_gate`, `blocked_reason`, `readiness_score`, `mapped_order_count`, `unmapped_utility_types`, `verified_at`, `identity_attempt_count` and all three of the cross-move pointers. The move list supports every foreign key as a filter — `resident_id`, `feed_source_id`, `origin_address_id`, `destination_address_id`, `duplicate_of_move_id`, `reactivation_candidate_move_id` and `duplicate_candidate_move_id` — alongside `lifecycle_status` and `duplicate_flag`, all combined conjunctively. The listing is not ownership-scoped: a `FeedIntegrator` calling it sees every feed's moves and must pass its own `feed_source_id` as a filter to narrow them, and the same holds for the identity-verification listing, which a caller narrows with `move_id` or `resident_id` rather than having it narrowed for them.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Every path below is mounted directly at the server root, with no version or service prefix. The twelve operations that bring a brand-new resource into existence respond with HTTP 201 Created: registering a feed source, a resident, a service address, a utility provider, a provider service territory or a referral partner; ingesting a move record; opening an identity verification attempt; dispatching a credit-check handoff; drafting an address-change filing; recording a consent record; and creating a referral placement. Every other operation in the table, including all reads, all state-advancing sub-resource writes such as scheduling, activation, submission, conversion and cancellation, and all status patches, responds with HTTP 200 OK. The Authorized Role(s) column lists the roles permitted to call the operation at all; several operations additionally require the caller to own the addressed record, and those ownership rules are stated in section 2.1 and in the relevant part of section 3.
