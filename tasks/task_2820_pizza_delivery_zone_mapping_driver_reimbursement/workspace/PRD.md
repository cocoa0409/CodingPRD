# Pizza Delivery Zone Reimbursement API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A pizza franchise lives and dies on the boundary of its delivery map. Draw the polygon too tightly and the store hands paying customers to a competitor two blocks away; draw it too loosely and drivers burn an hour on a run that earns the store four dollars, the pizza arrives cold, and the franchise pays a remake plus a goodwill credit out of the same ticket. Every store-level decision that follows from that map — whether to take the order at all, which driver to send, how many stops to stack in the same trip, what the customer is charged for delivery, and what the driver is reimbursed for the miles — is today made on paper, in a back-office spreadsheet, or in a manager's head. The result is inconsistent: two stores in the same market decline different addresses, two drivers with identical odometer readings receive different cheques, and nobody can prove after the fact that the cash a driver collected at the door ever reached the safe.

This document specifies the **Store Operations API**, a single backend service that turns those decisions into recorded, replayable, auditable operations. The service owns the delivery-area geometry and its version history, the drive-time model that classifies a street address as inside the area, at its edge, or outside it, the dispute process by which a customer on the edge can be granted a time-limited exception, the order intake and pricing pipeline, driver qualification and insurance compliance, route construction with hot-bag and stacking capacity limits, the two-tier mileage reimbursement schedule, the order-to-door stopwatch, the remake and complaint remedy policies, and the cash-on-hand and safe-drop procedure that keeps money conserved between the driver's pouch and the store safe.

Two design commitments run through the whole specification and are worth stating up front. First, **every decision is reproducible**: all geometry is integer micro-degree arithmetic, all money is integer cents, all mileage carries exactly two decimal places, and no computation anywhere consults the host wall clock — time comes from a logical clock the service itself owns. Second, **every number the service reports is either derived from stored facts on every read or explicitly snapshotted at a named moment**. A store manager may raise the advertised promise time this afternoon, but yesterday's order is still judged against the promise it was taken under; a manager may publish a more generous mileage schedule tonight, but a route already on the road is paid at the schedule it was locked against. The whole API is mounted under `/store-ops`.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Actors, Identity Headers and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint — including all read endpoints and including the logical-clock endpoint — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capability set that principal is exercising. A request whose `X-User-ID` is absent or empty is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_USER_ID` (HTTP 401). A request whose `X-User-Role` is absent, empty, or is any string other than the five role names enumerated below is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_ROLE` (HTTP 401) — an unrecognised role is treated as a failure to authenticate, not as a failure to authorize. Once both headers are accepted, the presented role must appear in the target endpoint's authorized-role list, which §4 tabulates; otherwise the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). That role gate is evaluated before any resource named in the path is loaded, so a role that is not permitted to call an endpoint learns nothing about whether the identifiers in the path exist.

The five roles are `StoreManager`, `Dispatcher`, `Driver`, `ComplianceOfficer` and `CustomerCareAgent`.

A **`StoreManager`** is the franchisee or general manager of exactly one store. The manager owns delivery-area policy, cash policy, the remake decision, route settlement and complaint resolution. When `X-User-Role` is `StoreManager`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Store`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Store` compare the resource's store reference to `X-User-ID`. The comparison is transitive: a delivery-area version, a service address, a driver, a vehicle cost model and an order each carry their own store reference; a dispute resolves through its service address; a dispatch and a safe drop resolve through their driver; a dispatch stop resolves through its dispatch and that dispatch's driver; a complaint resolves through its order; a reimbursement ledger line resolves through its driver. A `StoreManager` whose `X-User-ID` is not the owning store is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_STORE_SCOPE` (HTTP 403), so the manager of one store can never read or write another store's resources. Store registration is the one unavoidable exception: at the moment a store is created there is no target resource to scope against, so any non-empty `X-User-ID` is accepted on that single call, and the identifier the call returns becomes the `X-User-ID` that every later `StoreManager` request for that store must present.

A **`Driver`** is the person in the car, and owns exactly the events only that person can attest to: departure, the handoff at each door with its odometer reading and cash, the check-in on return, and dropping cash into the safe. When `X-User-Role` is `Driver`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Driver`; ownership checks compare the driver reference on the target dispatch, dispatch stop, safe drop or reimbursement ledger line — and, for a driver record read directly, that record's own `id` — to `X-User-ID`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_DRIVER_SCOPE` (HTTP 403). One driver may not depart another driver's route, may not record a door arrival on it, may not check it back in, and may not drop cash against another driver's pouch.

A **`Dispatcher`** is the inside expediter: taking orders, dispositioning them against the delivery area, running the make line, and building routes. A **`ComplianceOfficer`** is the back-office risk function: pulling motor-vehicle records, filing certificates of insurance, and signing qualification decisions. A **`CustomerCareAgent`** takes phone and web orders, makes the fee-versus-tip disclosure, files and withdraws edge-address disputes, and proposes complaint remedies — but never approves one, which is the manager's decision alone. For all three of these roles, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity: the server records it where a workflow calls for attribution — as `recorded_by_user_id` on a motor-vehicle-record pull, as `qualified_by_user_id` on a driver, as `reviewer_user_id` on a dispute, as `logged_by_user_id` on a complaint — but never compares it to any entity's `id`, and no store-scope or driver-scope check applies to it.

One endpoint splits authorization by the action requested rather than by the endpoint alone: the dispute review action. The `START_REVIEW`, `REQUEST_INFO`, `GRANT` and `DENY` actions are reserved to `StoreManager`; the `WITHDRAW` action is reserved to `CustomerCareAgent`. Both roles may reach the endpoint, but a role that supplies an action reserved for the other is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and Collection Reads

Every collection endpoint accepts two optional query-string parameters. `limit` controls page size: its default is 10, its minimum accepted value is 1, and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position within the result set: its default is 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. A supplied `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a supplied `offset` below 0, is a schema violation and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending unless a specific endpoint's description states otherwise. The `meta` object of every list response carries `limit` (the effective page size applied), `offset` (the effective starting position), `total_count` (the number of records matching the filters before pagination), and `has_more` (true exactly when records remain beyond the current page).

Filters are additive and each collection publishes its own set. Delivery-area versions may be filtered by `store_id`, by `supersedes_zone_version_id` (which walks the supersession lineage), and by `status`. Service addresses accept `store_id` and `zone_state`. Disputes accept `service_address_id` and `status`. Drivers accept `store_id`, `qualification_status` and `vehicle_class`. Motor-vehicle-record pulls accept `driver_id` and `result`, and are the one collection returned newest first, ordered by `created_at` descending. Insurance verifications accept `driver_id` and `status`. Vehicle cost models accept `store_id`, `vehicle_class` and `status`. Safe drops accept `driver_id` and `status`. Orders accept `store_id`, `service_address_id`, `fulfillment_status` and `settlement_status`, so either state dimension can be queried independently. Routes accept `driver_id`, `resolved_cost_model_id` and `status`. Route stops accept `dispatch_id`, `order_id` and `status` — filtering by `dispatch_id` walks one route in `sequence` ascending order, while filtering by `order_id` walks the full history of one order across every route it has ever been placed on. Reimbursement ledger lines accept `dispatch_id`, `driver_id`, `order_id`, `line_type` and `status`. Complaints accept `order_id`, `status` and `category`.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties, Timestamps and the Logical Clock

Every stored resource carries a server-minted `id`: a UUID string with no type prefix and no embedded meaning. Clients never supply it. Every resource also carries `created_at`, the instant of creation, formatted as an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with exactly three fractional-second digits (for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`). All datetime fields in requests and responses use that same format.

Almost every resource additionally carries `updated_at`, refreshed to the current instant on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that resource. The single exception is the motor-vehicle-record pull, which is an append-only, immutable audit record: it carries `id` and `created_at` only. It has no `updated_at`, no `version`, and no update endpoint of any kind. Every other resource in the system — stores, delivery-area versions, service addresses, disputes, drivers, vehicle cost models, insurance verifications, orders, routes, route stops, reimbursement ledger lines, safe drops and complaints — carries all four common properties.

"Now" in this system never means the host wall clock. Every instant the service stamps or compares against — `placed_at`, `ready_at`, `door_arrival_at`, `activated_at`, `evaluated_at`, `decided_at`, `departed_at`, `returned_at`, `voided_at`, `resolved_at`, `fee_disclosed_at`, the peak-hour surcharge test, every insurance-coverage and delivery-area effective-window containment test, and the safe-drop void window — reads a single server-owned **logical clock**. At server startup the logical clock is `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. A test-driving capability, available to every role, sets the clock to a supplied `current_utc` instant and echoes back the value now in force. Setting the clock never retroactively alters any stored timestamp; it does, however, change what re-derived fields report on their next read, because moving the clock can expire an address override, move a driver's insurance in or out of coverage, change which delivery-area version is in force for a store, and make a recorded safe drop cross out of its void window.

### 2.5. Path Resolution and Nested Reads

Path parameters are resolved strictly left to right along the URL template, and the first identifier that fails to resolve determines the error. The path variables in play are `store_id`, `zone_version_id`, `service_address_id`, `address_dispute_id`, `driver_id`, `mvr_check_id`, `insurance_verification_id`, `vehicle_cost_model_id`, `order_id`, `dispatch_id`, `dispatch_stop_id`, `safe_drop_id`, `complaint_id` and `reimbursement_line_id`. Each maps to its own not-found error: `ERR_STORE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ZONE_VERSION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SERVICE_ADDRESS_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ADDRESS_DISPUTE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DRIVER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MVR_CHECK_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INSURANCE_VERIFICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VEHICLE_COST_MODEL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DISPATCH_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DISPATCH_STOP_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SAFE_DROP_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COMPLAINT_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_REIMBURSEMENT_LINE_NOT_FOUND`, all returned with HTTP 404. Left-to-right ordering is observable: a request that names a non-existent route and a non-existent stop within it reports the route as missing, never the stop. A stop identifier that does exist but belongs to a different route than the one named earlier in the same path is treated as not found under this rule — it is not reported as a mismatch of any other kind.

Foreign keys supplied inside a request body are resolved only after every path parameter has resolved. A request that names both a bad path identifier and a bad body reference always reports the path identifier. Body-level references are checked on delivery-area version creation, service-address creation, driver creation, vehicle-cost-model creation, order creation, dispute creation, route-stop insertion and complaint creation, and they report the same not-found error identifiers listed above. One body reference carries an extra ownership condition: an order's `service_address_id` must name an address belonging to the same store as the order's `store_id`, and an address belonging to a different store is reported as `ERR_SERVICE_ADDRESS_NOT_FOUND` rather than as a scope error.

Resources created under a parent path are read back at their own flat collection URL. Motor-vehicle-record pulls, insurance verifications, safe drops and route stops are all created beneath their parent, but each is retrieved individually and listed at its own canonical top-level collection.

### 2.6. Arithmetic Discipline: Cents, Micro-Degrees, Miles and Minutes

Money is never a decimal in this system. Every monetary field is an integer count of cents, and every field name that ends in `_cents` is to be read that way. Where a monetary computation produces a fractional intermediate value, that value is rounded **half up** to a whole number of cents — a value ending in exactly one half rounds away from zero, so 199.5 becomes 200 and 274.5 becomes 275. The specification always states *where* in a formula the rounding happens, and that placement is part of the contract: rounding earlier or later than specified produces different money.

Geography is likewise integer-only. Latitude and longitude are carried as **micro-degrees**: signed integers equal to degrees multiplied by one million. `latitude_micro` must lie between -90000000 and 90000000 inclusive; `longitude_micro` must lie between -180000000 and 180000000 inclusive. No trigonometry, no floating-point geometry, and no epsilon tolerance appears anywhere in the delivery-area logic, so every geometric verdict is bit-reproducible.

Mileage is a number carried to exactly two decimal places, produced by rounding half up. Durations are always whole minutes. Where a duration is derived from mileage the specification says explicitly whether the fractional minute is rounded **up** to the next whole minute (the drive-time and route-duration models both do this, and both do it per leg rather than on a total) or **truncated downward** to the whole minute already elapsed (the order-to-door stopwatch and the two hold accumulators do this, on the principle that a partly elapsed minute is not yet late). Elapsed-minute figures are computed from whole seconds of difference between two instants, then truncated downward.

### 2.7. Publishing Against a Parent Record That May Have Moved

Two write operations mutate the meaning of a parent record so profoundly that they are not permitted to proceed on a stale view of that parent. Both are guarded by an explicit expected-version field, and in both cases the check is placed high in the refusal ladder described in §2.8 — before any state or business rule — so that a client holding a stale parent view is told its view is stale and is told nothing at all about the state it was trying to reach.

A `POST /store-ops/zone-versions` request must carry `expected_store_version`, a non-optional field holding the `version` of the parent `Store` as the client last read it. If that value does not equal the store's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_STORE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The guard exists because activating a delivery-area version legitimately supersedes the prior one and bumps the store's `version`, so a writer working from an outdated store view would otherwise publish a polygon derived from an outdated delivery fee, service posture and area lineage. A client obtains a fresh value by reading the store.

A `POST /store-ops/drivers/{driver_id}/insurance-verifications` request must carry `expected_driver_version`, a non-optional field holding the `version` of the parent `Driver` as the client last read it. If that value does not equal the driver's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_DRIVER_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Filing a verification legitimately supersedes the driver's previous verified certificate and rewrites the driver's derived insurance standing, so a writer holding a stale driver view would supersede a policy it never saw. A client obtains a fresh value by reading the driver record, which is also the read that re-derives every computed driver figure.

No other endpoint accepts a version field of any kind. Endpoints not named in this subsection take no expected-version input, and supplying one has no defined effect.

### 2.8. The Order of Refusal

When a single request violates more than one rule, exactly one error is reported, and which one is fully determined. Rules are evaluated in the strict ascending order below; the first violated rule short-circuits the request, no later rule is evaluated, and **no side effect of any kind is applied**. Where a rule's description in a later section appears to disagree with this ordering, this ordering wins.

1. `X-User-ID` present and non-empty, else `ERR_MISSING_USER_ID` (401) — on every endpoint.
2. `X-User-Role` present and one of the five role names, else `ERR_INVALID_ROLE` (401) — on every endpoint.
3. The presented role is authorized for the endpoint (and for the specific dispute review action supplied), else `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403) — on every endpoint.
4. A `StoreManager`'s `X-User-ID` owns the target resource transitively, else `ERR_FORBIDDEN_STORE_SCOPE` (403) — on every `StoreManager` operation except store registration.
5. A `Driver`'s `X-User-ID` owns the target resource, else `ERR_FORBIDDEN_DRIVER_SCOPE` (403) — on every `Driver` operation.
6. Every path identifier resolves, left to right, else the corresponding not-found error (404) — on every endpoint that has path parameters.
7. Every foreign key in the request body resolves, else the corresponding not-found error (404) — on delivery-area version creation, service-address creation, driver creation, vehicle-cost-model creation, order creation, dispute creation, route-stop insertion and complaint creation.
8. Generic schema validity: required fields present, declared types honoured, enum members in range, integers within their stated bounds, datetimes well-formed ISO 8601 UTC instants — else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400), on every endpoint that takes a body or a query string. This rule also covers the action-conditional requirements: `granted_until` on the dispute `GRANT` action, `reason` on the `DISQUALIFY` qualification decision, and a cash door arrival whose `cash_collected_cents` does not equal the order's `total_charge_cents`. Note that `actual_miles` is deliberately optional at the door: omitting it there is legal and is caught later, at settlement, by rule 33.
9. Domain-shape rules on freshly supplied structures — a `polygon` with fewer than three vertices yields `ERR_INVALID_POLYGON` (422); an `effective_to` that is not strictly after `effective_from` yields `ERR_INVALID_ZONE_WINDOW` (422); a `coverage_to` that is not strictly after `coverage_from` yields `ERR_INVALID_COVERAGE_WINDOW` (422). These apply to delivery-area version creation and insurance-verification filing, and are evaluated after generic parsing but before any uniqueness or concurrency check.
10. Uniqueness: `store_number` is globally unique across stores and `license_number` is unique within its store, yielding `ERR_STORE_NUMBER_TAKEN` or `ERR_LICENSE_NUMBER_TAKEN` (both 409) — on store registration, store policy edits and driver creation.
11. The supplied expected-parent-version matches, else `ERR_STALE_STORE_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_DRIVER_VERSION` (both 412) — on delivery-area version creation and insurance-verification filing.
12. The target entity is in a state from which the requested transition is declared, else the per-entity state error (409): `ERR_INVALID_ZONE_VERSION_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_DISPUTE_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_DRIVER_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_DISPATCH_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_STOP_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_COMPLAINT_STATE` or `ERR_INVALID_SAFE_DROP_STATE`. This is one rule at one priority with per-entity error identifiers. The entity named by the **last** path parameter has its state checked here, and a violation on that entity pre-empts every business rule below it, including every fiscal rule.
13. The driver is `QUALIFIED`, else `ERR_DRIVER_NOT_QUALIFIED` (422) — on route lock and on departure. This sits deliberately above every capacity and constraint rule, so a stop-free lock attempted by a suspended driver reports the qualification failure rather than the empty route.
14. The driver's newest verified insurance certificate covers the relevant instant, else `ERR_INSURANCE_NOT_ACTIVE` (422) — on route lock, on departure, and on a qualification decision. The instant tested differs by operation and that difference is observable: lock tests the route's `planned_depart_at`, while departure and qualification test the logical clock.
15. The driver's latest recorded violation-point total is at most 5, else `ERR_MVR_POINTS_EXCEEDED` (422) — on a qualification decision, specifically the `QUALIFY` and `REINSTATE` decisions. Exactly 5 points passes; 6 fails.
16. The acting officer is not the officer who recorded the driver's newest motor-vehicle-record pull, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (409) — on a qualification decision, specifically `QUALIFY` and `REINSTATE`.
17. A delivery-area version with status `ACTIVE` whose effective window contains the logical clock exists for the store, else `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_ZONE_VERSION` (409) — on service-address creation and on the order zone decision.
18. The order's address reads an effective zone state of `IN_ZONE`, else `ERR_ADDRESS_OUT_OF_AREA` (422) — on the order zone decision with action `CLEAR`. This covers both the geometrically-outside case and the edge case whose dispute was denied or whose granted override has expired.
19. The order named in a route-stop insertion belongs to the same store as the route's driver, else `ERR_ORDER_STORE_MISMATCH` (409) — on route-stop insertion. Both entities exist, so this is not a 404; the association itself is illegal.
20. The cost-model selector resolves at least one eligible schedule, else `ERR_NO_COST_MODEL` (422) — on route lock.
21. The new delivery-area window does not intersect the window of any existing `DRAFT` or `ACTIVE` version of the same store, else `ERR_ZONE_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (409) — on delivery-area version creation.
22. The route's driver-occupation interval does not intersect that of another `LOCKED` or `EN_ROUTE` route of the same driver, else `ERR_DRIVER_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409) — on route lock.
23. Committed hot-bag slots across the store's window-overlapping `LOCKED` and `EN_ROUTE` routes, plus this route's own commitment, do not exceed the store's bag pool, else `ERR_HOT_BAG_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409) — on route lock.
24. Inserting the stop keeps the route's stop count within the store's stacking cap, else `ERR_STACK_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (409) — on route-stop insertion.
25. The route has at least one counted stop, else `ERR_DISPATCH_HAS_NO_STOPS` (422) — on route lock.
26. The order named in a route-stop insertion has fulfillment status `READY`, else `ERR_ORDER_NOT_READY` (409) — on route-stop insertion. The route's own state was checked at rule 12; the referenced order's state is a business rule and is checked here.
27. No stop with status `PENDING` already references that order, else `ERR_ORDER_ALREADY_STACKED` (409) — on route-stop insertion.
28. The dispute's review-round count is below 2, else `ERR_DISPUTE_ROUND_LIMIT` (409) — on the dispute review action `REQUEST_INFO`.
29. Every counted stop's order has settlement status `DISCLOSED` or `CHARGED`, else `ERR_FEE_NOT_DISCLOSED` (422) — on route lock.
30. The order's remake count is below 1, else `ERR_REMAKE_LIMIT_REACHED` (409) — on the remake action.
31. When the remake `reason` is `LATE`, the order's gross order-to-door minutes strictly exceed its snapshotted promise plus a 5-minute grace, else `ERR_REMAKE_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (422) — on the remake action.
32. Every stop of the route has status `DELIVERED`, `RELEASED` or `REMADE`, else `ERR_STOPS_OUTSTANDING` (409) — on route check-in.
33. Every `DELIVERED` or `REMADE` stop of the route has an `actual_miles` reading on file, else `ERR_ODOMETER_MISSING` (422) — on route settlement.
34. The store's service posture is `OPEN`, else `ERR_STORE_NOT_ACCEPTING_DELIVERY` (409) — on the order zone decision with action `CLEAR`.
35. The address reads an effective zone state of `EDGE` or `OUT_OF_ZONE`, else `ERR_ZONE_HOLD_NOT_APPLICABLE` (422) — on the order zone decision with action `HOLD`.
36. The address reads an effective zone state of `EDGE` or `OUT_OF_ZONE`, else `ERR_DECLINE_NOT_APPLICABLE` (422) — on the order zone decision with actions `DECLINE` and `REDIRECT_CARRYOUT`.
37. When `driver_chargeable` is true, the route that settled the complaint's order has status `SETTLED`, else `ERR_CLAWBACK_NOT_SETTLED` (409) — on complaint resolution with action `APPROVE`.
38. The driver's cash on hand is strictly below the store's cash limit, else `ERR_CASH_LIMIT_REACHED` (HTTP 402) — on departure. Because this rule sits below every state check, attempting to depart an already-checked-in route reports the route state, not the cash.
39. The drop amount does not exceed the driver's cash on hand, else `ERR_SAFE_DROP_EXCEEDS_CASH` (422) — on safe-drop creation.
40. The safe drop is still inside its 60-minute void window, else `ERR_SAFE_DROP_NOT_VOIDABLE` (409) — on the safe-drop void action.
41. The proposed remedy does not exceed the order's remaining refundable balance, else `ERR_REMEDY_EXCEEDS_TOTAL` (422) — on complaint resolution with action `APPROVE`.

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline and Derived Values

Many operations in this system touch more than one record. Whenever an operation mutates a record, that record's `updated_at` is refreshed to the logical clock and its `version` increments by exactly 1 — and that applies to records reached by cascade just as much as to the record the request named. Where a cascade fans out across a set of sibling records, the set is processed in `created_at` ascending order of the affected records, so the observable end state is deterministic.

A second family of fields is not stored-and-mutated at all but **re-derived on every read**. A store's `cash_collected_total_cents`, `safe_deposit_total_cents`, `unassigned_order_count` and `active_zone_version_number` are all recomputed when the store is read. A driver's `insurance_status`, `mvr_points_latest`, `cash_on_hand_cents`, `cash_posture` and `reimbursement_accrued_cents` are all recomputed when the driver is read. A service address's `effective_zone_state` is recomputed against the logical clock on every read. Recomputation on read is not a mutation: it does not bump `version` and does not refresh `updated_at`. Consequently, moving the logical clock can change what a read reports without any write having occurred.

A store's `unassigned_order_count` is the count of that store's orders whose fulfillment status is `ZONE_CLEARED`, `IN_PRODUCTION`, `READY` or `REMAKE_QUEUED` — the orders that need a route. Because the definition is a filter over live order state, any operation that moves an order into or out of that set changes the count the next store read reports, and the specification names those movements explicitly wherever they occur.

### 2.10. Window and Interval Conventions

Several rules in this specification compare time intervals, and the inclusivity of each endpoint of each interval is part of the contract, never an implementation choice.

A delivery-area version's effective window runs from `effective_from` inclusive to `effective_to` exclusive. An instant is "in force" for that version when it is at or after `effective_from` and strictly before `effective_to`. Overlap between two such windows is tested on the same half-open basis, so two versions that merely touch — where the earlier version's `effective_to` equals the later version's `effective_from` — are adjacent, not overlapping, and both may be stored.

An insurance certificate's coverage window runs from `coverage_from` inclusive to `coverage_to` exclusive, and coverage of an instant means the instant is at or after `coverage_from` and strictly before `coverage_to`.

An address override runs until `override_until` exclusive: the override is in force while `override_until` is strictly after the logical clock, and has lapsed the moment the clock reaches it.

Route timing uses two different intervals with deliberately different endpoints. The **hot-bag reservation window** of a route is half-open: from `planned_depart_at` inclusive to `expected_return_at` exclusive. Two routes whose windows merely touch share no bags. The **driver-occupation interval** of a route is closed at both ends: from `planned_depart_at` through `expected_return_at` plus the store's post-return reset buffer, both endpoints included. Back-to-back routes for the same driver must therefore leave the reset buffer plus at least one whole minute between them. The contrast between these two conventions is intentional and observable.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up a Store and Publishing a Delivery Area

A franchise store is the root of everything else in the system, and registering one is the only operation whose actor is not yet scoped to an existing store. Registration requires a `store_number` — the franchise's own store identifier, between 1 and 8 characters, globally unique across all stores, with a collision reported as `ERR_STORE_NUMBER_TAKEN` — a display `name`, and the store's own origin coordinates as `latitude_micro` and `longitude_micro`. Every remaining field is policy, and every one has a default the store may accept silently: the advertised order-to-door `promise_minutes` defaults to 30 whole minutes and must be at least 1; the base customer `delivery_fee_cents` defaults to 399 and must be at least 0; the `cash_limit_cents` ceiling on how much cash a driver may carry defaults to 5000 (fifty dollars) and must be at least 0; `hot_bag_count`, the number of insulated bag slots the store physically owns, defaults to 4 and must be at least 1; `max_stacked_stops`, the ceiling on stops in one trip, defaults to 3 and must be at least 1; `handoff_minutes_per_stop`, the flat curbside allowance the route model charges at each door, defaults to 6 and must be at least 0; and `dispatch_reset_buffer_minutes`, the recovery time appended to a driver's occupied interval after a route comes back, defaults to 10 and must be at least 0. Finally, `service_posture` gates order intake and defaults to `OPEN`, its other legal values being `CARRYOUT_ONLY` and `CLOSED`. A freshly registered store reports `cash_collected_total_cents`, `safe_deposit_total_cents` and `unassigned_order_count` all at 0, and `active_zone_version_number` as null because no delivery area is in force yet.

Store policy can be edited later, and every field except the coordinates is editable: `store_number`, `name`, `promise_minutes`, `delivery_fee_cents`, `cash_limit_cents`, `hot_bag_count`, `max_stacked_stops`, `handoff_minutes_per_stop`, `dispatch_reset_buffer_minutes` and `service_posture` are each optional on the edit, and the uniqueness rule on `store_number` applies to the edit exactly as it applies to registration. An edit increments the store's `version` by 1. Two consequences of editing deserve emphasis. Raising or lowering `promise_minutes` never retro-changes an order already on the books, because every order freezes the store's promise into its own `promise_minutes_snapshot` at the moment it is taken. Lowering `cash_limit_cents`, on the other hand, is felt immediately: a driver already holding cash at or above the new limit will read as `DROP_REQUIRED` on the next driver read, and will be unable to leave the store again until the pouch is lightened.

A separate manager-only computed read proves the store's cash books balance at the current instant. It returns the store's `store_id`, its `cash_collected_total_cents`, its `safe_deposit_total_cents`, the summed `driver_cash_on_hand_total_cents` across the store's drivers, and a `reconciled` flag that is true exactly when the collected total equals the safe-deposit total plus the drivers' combined cash on hand.

The delivery area itself is versioned rather than edited. Publishing a new version is a manager operation and requires the parent store's `store_id`, the ring geometry as `polygon`, the window bounds `effective_from` and `effective_to`, and the `expected_store_version` guard described in §2.7. The `polygon` is an ordered array of vertices, each vertex an object carrying its own `latitude_micro` and `longitude_micro`; the ring closes implicitly from the last vertex back to the first, so a triangle is expressed as three vertices, not four. Fewer than three vertices is rejected as `ERR_INVALID_POLYGON`, and an `effective_to` that is not strictly later than `effective_from` is rejected as `ERR_INVALID_ZONE_WINDOW`. Five further fields tune the drive-time model and each has a default: `max_drive_time_minutes`, the inclusive drive-time ceiling separating an accepted address from an edge address, defaults to 25 and must be at least 1; `base_drive_minutes`, the fixed oven-rack-to-road allowance charged on every estimate, defaults to 6 and must be at least 0; `minutes_per_mile`, the minutes charged for each grid mile, defaults to 3 and must be at least 1; `peak_surcharge_minutes` defaults to 7 and must be at least 0; and `barrier_surcharge_minutes` defaults to 4 and must be at least 0.

A newly published version is stamped with a `version_number` equal to the count of that store's pre-existing delivery-area versions plus 1, so the first is 1 and numbering is per store rather than global. It begins in `DRAFT` status — the other statuses being `ACTIVE` and the terminal `SUPERSEDED` — with `supersedes_zone_version_id` and `activated_at` both null. Publication also increments the parent store's `version` by 1, which is precisely why the next publication needs a freshly-read `expected_store_version`. Before the version is stored, its window is tested against every existing version of the same store whose status is `DRAFT` or `ACTIVE`: if the two windows intersect on the half-open basis of §2.10, the publication is rejected with `ERR_ZONE_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. Versions already in `SUPERSEDED` status are historical and are excluded from that test entirely, which is what allows a store to re-use a window a superseded version once occupied.

Putting a version in force is a separate manager action that advances it `DRAFT → ACTIVE`. It requires the version to be in `DRAFT` — anything else is `ERR_INVALID_ZONE_VERSION_STATE` — requires the acting manager to own the version's store, and re-confirms that the `polygon` still carries at least three vertices. On success the version's status becomes `ACTIVE` and `activated_at` is stamped from the logical clock. The service then looks for the version this activation displaces: among the same store's versions that are currently `ACTIVE` and whose `effective_to` is at or before the newly activated version's `effective_from`, it takes the one with the greatest `effective_to`, breaking a tie by the later `created_at`. That version's `id` is written onto the new version's `supersedes_zone_version_id`, and that version's own status becomes `SUPERSEDED`. When no version satisfies those conditions, `supersedes_zone_version_id` stays null and nothing is superseded. Either way the parent store's `version` increments by 1, and the store's `active_zone_version_number` — re-derived on read as the `version_number` of whichever `ACTIVE` version's window contains the logical clock, and null when none does — begins reporting the new area.

### 3.2. Classifying a Street Address: Grid Miles, Drive Time and Zone State

Before a store can quote an address it must classify it, and classification happens exactly once, at the moment the address is filed. Filing an address is open to a `Dispatcher`, a `CustomerCareAgent` or a `StoreManager`, and requires the owning `store_id`, the street line as `line1`, and the geocoded `latitude_micro` and `longitude_micro`. One optional flag, `crosses_barrier`, defaults to false and is set true when the driving route to the address crosses a mapped obstacle — a river, a rail corridor, or a limited-access highway with sparse crossings. Filing fails with `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_ZONE_VERSION` when the store has no `ACTIVE` delivery-area version whose window contains the logical clock, because there is nothing to classify against. On success the address records `evaluated_zone_version_number`, the `version_number` of the version that did the classifying, and `evaluated_at`, the logical-clock instant of the evaluation. Its `override_state` and `override_until` both begin null, so at birth an address's `effective_zone_state` is identical to its `zone_state`.

**Grid mileage.** Pizza mileage is reimbursed on street distance, and the store models its streets as an axis-aligned grid, so the two coordinate deltas are added rather than combined as the hypotenuse of a triangle. Take the absolute difference between the address's `latitude_micro` and the store's `latitude_micro`, take the absolute difference between the address's `longitude_micro` and the store's `longitude_micro`, add those two differences together, and divide the sum by 15000 micro-degrees per mile. The quotient, rounded half up to two decimal places, is the address's `grid_miles`. An address 30000 micro-degrees north and 15000 micro-degrees west of its store is 3.00 grid miles away; an address 94950 micro-degrees due north is 6.33 grid miles, while one 95100 micro-degrees due north is 6.34; and an address 400000 micro-degrees due north works out to 26.666… miles and is recorded as 26.67.

**Drive time.** The modelled minutes from the oven rack to the customer's door, stored as `drive_time_estimate_minutes`, is the sum of three terms drawn from the delivery-area version in force. The first is the flat `base_drive_minutes` allowance. The second is the mileage term: `grid_miles` multiplied by `minutes_per_mile`, then rounded **up** to the next whole minute — the rounding-up applies to this term alone and never to the total. The third is a single surcharge, and this is the step most easily got wrong: the peak-hour component is `peak_surcharge_minutes` when the UTC hour of the logical clock is 17, 18, 19 or 20, and zero otherwise; the barrier component is `barrier_surcharge_minutes` when the address's `crosses_barrier` flag is true, and zero otherwise; the surcharge actually applied is the **greater of the two components**, not their sum, because a driver crossing a river during the dinner rush is delayed once rather than twice. With a 6-minute base and 3 minutes per mile, a 3.00-mile address off-peak with no barrier models at 15 minutes, and a 9.00-mile address at 33. A 6.33-mile address models at 6 plus 19, which is 25; a 6.34-mile address models at 6 plus 20, which is 26. That same 3.00-mile address, evaluated at 18:00 UTC with `crosses_barrier` true, a 7-minute peak surcharge and a 4-minute barrier surcharge, models at 6 plus 9 plus 7, which is 22.

**Zone state.** Geometry is decided first and drive time second, and the resulting `zone_state` is one of `IN_ZONE`, `EDGE` or `OUT_OF_ZONE`. The geometric test asks whether the address point lies inside the in-force `polygon`, using integer ray casting with no floating point and no tolerance band. Treat each vertex's `longitude_micro` as its horizontal coordinate and its `latitude_micro` as its vertical coordinate, and walk every edge of the ring — remembering that the ring closes from the last vertex back to the first. If the point lies exactly on an edge, meaning it is collinear with that edge's two endpoints and lies within the rectangle those endpoints bound, the point counts as **inside** and the walk stops there; a point sitting precisely on a boundary or on a vertex is never outside. Otherwise an edge participates in the crossing count only when exactly one of its two endpoints lies strictly above the point's vertical coordinate. For each participating edge, form two integer products: the first multiplies the point's horizontal offset from the edge's first endpoint by the edge's vertical span, taken as the second endpoint's vertical coordinate minus the first's; the second multiplies the edge's horizontal span, taken as the second endpoint's horizontal coordinate minus the first's, by the point's vertical offset from the first endpoint. When the edge rises — its second endpoint above its first — the crossing counts when the first product is less than the second; when the edge falls, the crossing counts when the first product is greater than the second. Begin with a verdict of outside and flip it on each counted crossing; the verdict after the whole ring is the answer, and no division is ever performed.

Once the geometric verdict is in, the drive-time ceiling decides the rest. A point that is not inside the ring is `OUT_OF_ZONE` however short its modelled drive may be. A point inside the ring whose `drive_time_estimate_minutes` is **at or below** the in-force `max_drive_time_minutes` is `IN_ZONE` — the ceiling is inclusive, so a 25-minute estimate against a 25-minute ceiling is accepted. A point inside the ring whose estimate exceeds the ceiling is `EDGE`: too far to promise, but close enough to argue about, and therefore eligible for the dispute process, for a delivery hold, and for a carryout redirect. An address sitting exactly on the southern boundary of the ring with a 27-minute estimate against a 25-minute ceiling is `EDGE`, never `OUT_OF_ZONE`, because the on-boundary point counted as inside.

The address's `effective_zone_state` is what every downstream rule actually consults, and it is re-derived on every read rather than stored: when `override_state` is not null and `override_until` is strictly later than the logical clock, the effective state is the `override_state`; in every other case — no override recorded, or an override whose expiry the clock has reached — the effective state falls back to the stored `zone_state`.

### 3.3. Arguing the Edge: Disputes and the Override Window

Customers on the fringe of the map do not accept a drive-time model as an answer, and the franchise does not want them to. A `CustomerCareAgent` or a `StoreManager` may therefore open a dispute on behalf of a customer who insists the address is really inside the delivery area. Opening one requires the `service_address_id` of the address in question, a `claimed_state` — whose only legal value is `IN_ZONE`, since nobody disputes their way out of the delivery area — and an `evidence_note` carrying the customer's or the agent's free-text case. A new dispute begins in `SUBMITTED` status with `review_round_count` at 0, `resolution` null, `decided_at` null and `reviewer_user_id` null.

Every subsequent movement on a dispute goes through a single review action that carries an `action` naming what is being done, an optional `granted_until`, and an optional free-text `note`. The dispute's statuses are `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `INFO_REQUESTED`, and the three terminal outcomes `GRANTED`, `DENIED` and `WITHDRAWN`. Requesting an action that is not declared from the dispute's current status is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_DISPUTE_STATE`.

A manager picks up a submitted dispute with `START_REVIEW`, advancing it `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW` and stamping `reviewer_user_id` with the acting manager's `X-User-ID`. From `UNDER_REVIEW` the manager may bounce it back to the customer for more evidence with `REQUEST_INFO`, advancing it `UNDER_REVIEW → INFO_REQUESTED`, incrementing `review_round_count` by 1 and re-stamping `reviewer_user_id`. The same `START_REVIEW` action closes that loop from the other side, advancing `INFO_REQUESTED → UNDER_REVIEW` and re-stamping `reviewer_user_id`; note that returning to review does **not** decrement `review_round_count`, which only ever climbs. The loop is bounded at two rounds: a `REQUEST_INFO` attempted when `review_round_count` has already reached 2 is rejected with `ERR_DISPUTE_ROUND_LIMIT`, so the second request for information is the last one and a third is refused.

Two terminal decisions are available from `UNDER_REVIEW`. `DENY` advances the dispute `UNDER_REVIEW → DENIED`, sets its `resolution` to `DENIED` and stamps `decided_at` from the logical clock; the address's `override_state` and `override_until` are left null, so any orders parked in `ZONE_HELD` on that address stay parked. `GRANT` advances the dispute `UNDER_REVIEW → GRANTED`, sets `resolution` to `GRANTED` and stamps `decided_at`, but it additionally requires `granted_until` to be supplied and to be strictly later than the logical clock — an absent or already-elapsed `granted_until` is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Separately, a `CustomerCareAgent` may retire a dispute the customer no longer wants to pursue with `WITHDRAW`, advancing it `SUBMITTED → WITHDRAWN`; withdrawal frees the address for a fresh dispute later.

A grant is the widest-reaching cascade in the disputes area of the system, and every hop of it is observable. First, the address's `override_state` is set to `IN_ZONE` and its `override_until` is set to the `granted_until` value supplied on the grant, which by the rule of §3.2 flips that address's `effective_zone_state` to `IN_ZONE` for as long as the clock stays strictly before `override_until`. Second, because the address now reads as in-zone, every order on that address whose fulfillment status is `ZONE_HELD` is automatically advanced `ZONE_HELD → ZONE_CLEARED`, processed in `created_at` ascending order across those orders; each such order accrues into its `zone_hold_minutes` the whole minutes it spent parked, computed as the difference between the logical clock and its `zone_held_at` in whole seconds, divided by 60 and truncated downward. Third, because those orders have left `ZONE_HELD` and entered a status the unassigned filter counts, the owning store's `unassigned_order_count` re-derives upward by exactly the number of orders cleared in the previous hop. Each record the cascade touches has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented.

### 3.4. Taking the Order, Pricing the Ticket, and Disclosing the Fee

A `Dispatcher` or a `CustomerCareAgent` takes a delivery order. The ticket requires the fulfilling `store_id`, the destination `service_address_id`, the `customer_name` and `customer_phone` on the ticket, the `food_subtotal_cents` for food and beverage with taxes excluded, and the `payment_method`, which is either `CASH` or `CARD`. A pre-declared `tip_cents` is optional and defaults to 0. Both money inputs must be at least 0. At creation the order stamps `placed_at` from the logical clock — this is the start of the order-to-door stopwatch — and copies the store's current `promise_minutes` into its own `promise_minutes_snapshot`, permanently insulating this ticket from later policy edits.

An order carries **two independent state dimensions** that advance on entirely different triggers. Its `fulfillment_status` tracks production and delivery and begins at `PLACED`, its full set of values being `PLACED`, `ZONE_HELD`, `ZONE_CLEARED`, `IN_PRODUCTION`, `READY`, `ASSIGNED`, `EN_ROUTE`, `DELIVERED`, `REMAKE_QUEUED`, and the three terminal states `CLOSED`, `DECLINED_OUT_OF_AREA` and `CARRYOUT_REDIRECTED`. Its `settlement_status` tracks money and begins at `UNDISCLOSED`, its full set of values being `UNDISCLOSED`, `DISCLOSED`, `CHARGED`, `ADJUSTED`, and the two terminal states `CLOSED_SETTLED` and `VOIDED`. Neither dimension implies the other: an order can be fully priced and disclosed while still in the oven, and an order can be void on the money side while its fulfillment side is terminal for an entirely different reason. Any order action requested from a status that does not declare it is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATE`.

One posture short-circuits intake entirely. When the store's `service_posture` is `CARRYOUT_ONLY` at the moment the order is created, the order is born already redirected: its fulfillment dimension goes `PLACED → CARRYOUT_REDIRECTED`, its `declined_reason` is set to `STORE_CARRYOUT_ONLY`, and its settlement dimension is set to `VOIDED`. The delivery pricing fields are nonetheless computed in full, so the ticket can still show the customer what delivery would have cost had the store been taking deliveries.

**Pricing the ticket.** Three independent inputs are consulted in a pinned order, and the order matters, because the drive-time tier raises the fee and the food-subtotal waiver then reduces the *raised* fee. The first step reads the destination address's `drive_time_estimate_minutes` and picks the `fee_tier`: an estimate of 15 minutes or less is `BASE`; an estimate above 15 but at most 25 minutes is `MID`; anything above 25 minutes is `FAR`. The second step computes `base_delivery_fee_cents` as the store's `delivery_fee_cents` plus that tier's surcharge, where the surcharge is 0 cents for `BASE`, 150 cents for `MID` and 350 cents for `FAR`. The third step picks `fee_waiver_pct` from `food_subtotal_cents` **alone** — the tip never counts toward a waiver threshold: a food subtotal of at least 3000 cents earns a waiver of 100, a subtotal of at least 2000 but under 3000 earns 50, and anything below 2000 earns 0. The fourth step computes the `delivery_fee_cents` actually charged by multiplying `base_delivery_fee_cents` by the complement of the waiver percentage — that is, by 100 minus `fee_waiver_pct` — dividing by 100, and rounding the result half up to a whole cent. The fifth and final step sets `total_charge_cents` to the sum of `food_subtotal_cents`, the charged `delivery_fee_cents`, and `tip_cents`.

The pinned order of those steps is visible in the numbers. A 15-minute address at a store charging 399 cents, with 2200 cents of food and a 500-cent tip, lands in `BASE` with a 399-cent surcharged base, a waiver of 50, a charged fee of 200 cents (399 halved is 199.5, which rounds half up to 200) and a total of 2900 cents. Drop the food to 1900 cents and the waiver becomes 0, the fee stays 399 and the total is 2299. A 33-minute address with 2600 cents of food and a 300-cent tip lands in `FAR`, so the surcharged base is 749, the waiver is 50, the fee is 375 (374.5 rounded half up) and the total is 3275. A 25-minute address with 3000 cents of food and a 700-cent tip lands in `MID` with a base of 549, a waiver of 100, a charged fee of 0 and a total of 3700; the same 25-minute address with exactly 2000 cents of food and no tip lands on a waiver of 50 and a fee of 275 (274.5 rounded half up), for a total of 2275. A 15-minute address with only 100 cents of food takes no waiver at all: fee 399, total 499.

**Disclosing fee versus tip.** Consumer-protection policy requires the customer to be told, before the food is on the road, what the delivery fee is, what the tip is, and that the delivery fee is not the driver's tip. A `CustomerCareAgent` or a `Dispatcher` records that conversation, supplying the `disclosed_fee_cents` and `disclosed_tip_cents` that were actually quoted to the customer and the boolean `driver_tip_notice_given` stating whether the fee-is-not-a-tip notice was given. The action advances the settlement dimension `UNDISCLOSED → DISCLOSED` and requires the settlement dimension to be exactly `UNDISCLOSED`; it also requires the fulfillment dimension to be none of `DECLINED_OUT_OF_AREA`, `CARRYOUT_REDIRECTED` or `CLOSED`. Because the two dimensions are independent, disclosure is legal while the order is `PLACED`, `ZONE_CLEARED`, `IN_PRODUCTION` or `READY`. On success `fee_disclosed_at` is stamped from the logical clock and `fee_disclosure_accurate` is computed: it is true only when `disclosed_fee_cents` equals the order's computed `delivery_fee_cents`, `disclosed_tip_cents` equals the order's `tip_cents`, and `driver_tip_notice_given` is true; it is false when any of those three fails, and it is null before disclosure has happened at all. A mis-quote is deliberately **recorded rather than rejected** — the request succeeds — because the record of the inaccuracy is what a later fee-disclosure complaint is priced against.

### 3.5. Dispositioning the Order Against the Delivery Area, and the Make Line

Once a ticket exists, somebody has to decide whether the store will actually drive to it. A `Dispatcher` or a `StoreManager` makes that call through a single zone-decision action carrying an `action` and an optional `note`. Four actions are available, and which ones are legal depends jointly on the order's fulfillment status and on the destination address's `effective_zone_state`.

`CLEAR` accepts the order for delivery, advancing it `PLACED → ZONE_CLEARED` or, for an order already parked, `ZONE_HELD → ZONE_CLEARED`. Three conditions guard it beyond the state check. The store's `service_posture` must be `OPEN`; a `CLOSED` or `CARRYOUT_ONLY` store clears nothing and is refused with `ERR_STORE_NOT_ACCEPTING_DELIVERY`, a case that arises when the posture was changed after the order was taken. The store must have an `ACTIVE` delivery-area version whose window contains the logical clock, or the request fails with `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_ZONE_VERSION`. And the destination's `effective_zone_state` must read `IN_ZONE`, or the request fails with `ERR_ADDRESS_OUT_OF_AREA` — which for a parked edge address means the clearance can only succeed while a granted dispute's `override_until` is still strictly ahead of the clock. Clearing an order out of `ZONE_HELD` accrues into `zone_hold_minutes` the whole minutes since `zone_held_at`, taken as the whole-second difference from the logical clock divided by 60 and truncated downward. Because a cleared order joins the set the unassigned filter counts, the store's `unassigned_order_count` reflects it on the next store read.

`HOLD` parks an order pending an argument about the map, advancing it `PLACED → ZONE_HELD` and stamping `zone_held_at` from the logical clock. It requires the address's `effective_zone_state` to be `EDGE` or `OUT_OF_ZONE`; an address the model already accepts cannot be parked for review and yields `ERR_ZONE_HOLD_NOT_APPLICABLE` — the exact mirror image of the clearance rule.

`DECLINE` turns the customer away, advancing the order to the terminal `DECLINED_OUT_OF_AREA` from either `PLACED` or `ZONE_HELD`. It likewise requires an `effective_zone_state` of `EDGE` or `OUT_OF_ZONE`, refusing an in-zone address with `ERR_DECLINE_NOT_APPLICABLE`, since the store owes an in-zone address a delivery. When `zone_held_at` is not null the accrual into `zone_hold_minutes` happens exactly as it does on clearance. The `declined_reason` recorded distinguishes the two ways an address can fail: it is `OUT_OF_AREA` when the address's stored `zone_state` is `OUT_OF_ZONE`, and `DRIVE_TIME_EXCEEDED` otherwise — that is, for an address inside the ring that simply models too far. Declining also drives the settlement dimension to the terminal `VOIDED`, which is legal from `UNDISCLOSED` or `DISCLOSED`; no money moves and the ticket closes out unbilled.

`REDIRECT_CARRYOUT` offers the customer the counter instead of the car, advancing the order to the terminal `CARRYOUT_REDIRECTED` from either `PLACED` or `ZONE_HELD` under the same `EDGE`-or-`OUT_OF_ZONE` requirement and the same `ERR_DECLINE_NOT_APPLICABLE` refusal. It accrues `zone_hold_minutes` on the same terms when `zone_held_at` is not null, records `declined_reason` as `DRIVE_TIME_EXCEEDED`, and drives the settlement dimension to `VOIDED`.

Cleared orders then move down the make line, which a `Dispatcher` drives with an `action` of either `START` or `READY`. `START` advances the order into production, and it is declared from two statuses: `ZONE_CLEARED → IN_PRODUCTION` for the ordinary first pass, and `REMAKE_QUEUED → IN_PRODUCTION` for the second pass that closes the remake cycle described in §3.12. `READY` advances `IN_PRODUCTION → READY` and stamps `ready_at` from the logical clock. An order in `READY` is exactly what a route builder is looking for.

### 3.6. Putting a Driver on the Road: Records, Insurance and Segregation of Duties

Hiring is a manager act and it deliberately confers nothing. A new driver requires the employing `store_id`, the driver's legal `full_name`, a `license_number` that must be unique among the drivers of that same store — a collision within the store is `ERR_LICENSE_NUMBER_TAKEN` — and a `vehicle_class`, which is `CAR`, `SUV` or `SCOOTER` and later selects the reimbursement schedule. A freshly hired driver reads back with `qualification_status` `APPLICANT`, `insurance_status` `NONE`, `mvr_points_latest` 0, `cash_on_hand_cents` 0, `cash_posture` `CLEAR`, `reimbursement_accrued_cents` 0, and both `qualified_by_user_id` and `disqualified_reason` null. The `qualification_status` dimension runs `APPLICANT`, `PENDING_CHECKS`, `QUALIFIED`, `SUSPENDED` and the terminal `DISQUALIFIED`; a decision that is not declared from the driver's current status — including any decision at all on a driver already `DISQUALIFIED` — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_DRIVER_STATE`.

A `ComplianceOfficer` pulls the motor-vehicle record. Each pull records the `result` of the pull, which is `CLEAR`, `MINOR_VIOLATIONS` or `FAILED`; the `violation_points` on the record at pull time, an integer from 0 through 20 inclusive; and `checked_on`, the instant the vendor produced the record. The service stamps `recorded_by_user_id` with the acting officer's `X-User-ID` — the field the segregation-of-duties rule reads later. A pull is an immutable audit record: it has an `id` and a `created_at` and nothing else in the way of common properties, no `updated_at`, no `version`, and no way to amend it. Recording a pull re-derives the driver's `mvr_points_latest` as the `violation_points` of the newest pull on file, where "newest" means the greatest `created_at`, breaking ties by the greater `checked_on`, and 0 when the driver has no pulls at all; the driver's `version` increments by 1. A pull whose `result` is `CLEAR` or `MINOR_VIOLATIONS` also advances an applicant `APPLICANT → PENDING_CHECKS`.

A pull whose `result` is `FAILED` does something far more consequential, and it does it without any officer signing anything. The driver is driven straight to `DISQUALIFIED` from whichever of `APPLICANT`, `PENDING_CHECKS`, `QUALIFIED` or `SUSPENDED` they occupied, with `disqualified_reason` recorded as `MVR_FAILED`, and a four-hop cascade unwinds everything that driver was in the middle of. Because the driver is now disqualified, every route of theirs whose status is `STACKED`, `LOCKED` or `EN_ROUTE` is driven to `RECALLED` with `recalled_reason` recorded as `DRIVER_DISQUALIFIED`, processed in `created_at` ascending order across those routes. Because those routes are recalled, each of their stops still in `PENDING` becomes `RELEASED`, and each released stop's order reverts on the fulfillment dimension from `ASSIGNED` or `EN_ROUTE` back to `READY`, while every provisional reimbursement ledger line belonging to those routes becomes `VOIDED`. Because those orders are back in `READY` they re-enter the unassigned filter, so the store's `unassigned_order_count` re-derives upward by the number of orders released. The driver's own `version` increments by 1 as well.

Insurance is filed as a certificate, again by a `ComplianceOfficer`, and again against the parent driver's `expected_driver_version` as §2.7 requires. A certificate carries the carrier's `policy_number`, the coverage window bounds `coverage_from` and `coverage_to`, and the combined single-limit `liability_limit_cents`, which must be at least 0. A `coverage_to` that is not strictly later than `coverage_from` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_COVERAGE_WINDOW`. The filing itself is never rejected for being inadequate; instead the certificate's own `status` records the verdict. A certificate whose `liability_limit_cents` is at least ten million cents — one hundred thousand dollars of combined single-limit liability — is stored `VERIFIED`; anything below that is stored `REJECTED` with a `rejected_reason` populated, and a verified certificate leaves `rejected_reason` null. Filing a new certificate moves any previous certificate of that driver to `SUPERSEDED`. The driver's `insurance_status` is then re-derived on read: it is `VERIFIED` when the driver's newest certificate is `VERIFIED`, `REJECTED` when the newest is `REJECTED`, and `NONE` when the driver has no certificate at all. The driver's `version` increments by 1, which is exactly why an OCC client must re-read the driver — the driver read is the endpoint that hands back a usable `expected_driver_version` along with every re-derived driver figure.

Qualification itself is a signed decision, carrying a `decision` of `QUALIFY`, `SUSPEND`, `REINSTATE` or `DISQUALIFY` plus an optional `reason`. `QUALIFY` advances `PENDING_CHECKS → QUALIFIED` and demands four things beyond the state check. The driver's newest motor-vehicle-record pull must have a `result` of `CLEAR` or `MINOR_VIOLATIONS`. The driver's `mvr_points_latest` must be at most 5 — exactly 5 passes, 6 is refused with `ERR_MVR_POINTS_EXCEEDED`. The driver's `insurance_status` must be `VERIFIED` and that newest verified certificate must actually cover the logical clock, meaning the clock is at or after its `coverage_from` and strictly before its `coverage_to`; otherwise the decision is refused with `ERR_INSURANCE_NOT_ACTIVE`. And the acting officer's `X-User-ID` must differ from the `recorded_by_user_id` on that newest pull: the officer who pulled the record may not sign the decision that relies on it, and an attempt to do so is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. On success `qualification_status` becomes `QUALIFIED`, `qualified_by_user_id` records the signing officer, and the driver's `version` increments by 1.

`SUSPEND` moves a driver `QUALIFIED → SUSPENDED` and bumps the driver's `version`, and it deliberately does **not** recall anything: existing routes stay exactly where they are, and the consequence of suspension is felt only when someone next tries to commit or depart a route. `REINSTATE` moves a driver `SUSPENDED → QUALIFIED` and re-applies the same three substantive checks as qualification — points at most 5, a verified certificate covering the logical clock, and an acting officer who is not the recorder of the newest pull — again stamping `qualified_by_user_id` and bumping `version`. The suspend-and-reinstate cycle may repeat any number of times over a driver's career. `DISQUALIFY` moves a driver to the terminal `DISQUALIFIED` from `PENDING_CHECKS`, `QUALIFIED` or `SUSPENDED`; it requires the `reason` field, which is recorded verbatim as `disqualified_reason`, and it drives exactly the same three cascade hops as the failed-record path above — route recall, stop release with order reversion and provisional-line voiding, and upward re-derivation of the store's unassigned count — plus the increment of the driver's own `version`.

### 3.7. Publishing Reimbursement Schedules and Building a Route

Before any route can be priced, the store must have published a reimbursement schedule for the vehicle class the driver drives. A `StoreManager` publishes one, naming the owning `store_id`, the `vehicle_class` it prices, the flat `per_delivery_cents` paid for every stop, the cumulative `tier1_cap_miles` after which mileage prices at the second rate, the `tier1_per_mile_cents` rate inside that cumulative band, the `tier2_per_mile_cents` rate beyond it, the `min_reimbursement_per_stop_cents` floor applied to each individual stop, and the `effective_from` instant from which the schedule may be selected. The cap must be strictly greater than 0 and carries two decimal places; the three cent amounts and the floor must each be at least 0. A published schedule is stored `ACTIVE` with `retired_at` null, and publishing it retires the store's earlier work: any schedule for the same store and the same `vehicle_class` that is currently `ACTIVE` and whose `effective_from` is earlier than the new one's becomes `RETIRED`, with `retired_at` stamped from the logical clock. Routes that were already committed are untouched — each keeps the schedule identifier it snapshotted and is never re-priced by a later publication.

Route construction begins with an empty shell. A `Dispatcher` opens one by naming the `driver_id` who will run it and the `planned_depart_at` instant the driver is expected to leave — an instant that serves both as the inclusive start of that driver's occupied interval and as the inclusive start of the store's hot-bag reservation window. A new route begins in `DRAFT` with `stop_count` 0, `committed_hot_bag_slots` 0, and `route_minutes`, `expected_return_at`, `resolved_cost_model_id` and `estimated_reimbursement_cents` all null; `departed_at`, `returned_at`, `recalled_reason`, `actual_reimbursement_cents` and `reimbursement_variance_cents` are likewise null. A route's `status` moves among `DRAFT`, `STACKED`, `LOCKED`, `EN_ROUTE`, `RETURNED`, and the three terminal states `SETTLED`, `RECALLED` and `CANCELLED`; a route action requested from a status that does not declare it is refused with `ERR_INVALID_DISPATCH_STATE`.

Stacking an order onto a route is the operation that ties the many-to-many relationship together, and it is where most of the routing constraints bite. The `Dispatcher` supplies the `order_id` to be carried and, optionally, `hot_bag_slots` — the number of insulated bag slots this particular stop occupies, defaulting to 1 and constrained to 1 or 2, since a large multi-box order needs two bags. The route must be in `DRAFT` or `STACKED`. Inserting must not push the route's `stop_count` beyond the store's `max_stacked_stops`, or the insertion is refused with `ERR_STACK_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. The referenced order must belong to the same store as the route's driver, or the insertion is refused with `ERR_ORDER_STORE_MISMATCH` — both records exist, so this is a refusal of the association, not a not-found. The referenced order's fulfillment status must be exactly `READY`, or the insertion is refused with `ERR_ORDER_NOT_READY`; that order must also not be voided on the settlement dimension, a condition the `READY` requirement subsumes in practice, since a voided ticket has a terminal fulfillment status. And no stop with status `PENDING` may already reference that order, or the insertion is refused with `ERR_ORDER_ALREADY_STACKED`: an order may be re-stacked after being released or recalled, but it may never sit on two live routes at once.

A successful insertion creates a stop in `PENDING` status — the other stop statuses being `DELIVERED`, `RELEASED` and `REMADE` — with `arrived_at` null, `actual_miles` null, and `cash_collected_cents` at 0. Its `sequence` is assigned as the count of **all** stops ever created on that route, in any status whatsoever, plus 1. Existing stops are never renumbered and a released stop's ordinal is never reused, so the insertion that follows a release receives a fresh higher number and a route's live stops may carry non-contiguous ordinals. The stop also snapshots two figures that later pricing depends on: `planned_miles` copies the `grid_miles` of the order's service address as it stands at insertion, and `planned_drive_minutes` is `planned_miles` multiplied by the in-force delivery-area version's `minutes_per_mile`, rounded up to the next whole minute. Insertion raises the route's `committed_hot_bag_slots` by the stop's `hot_bag_slots` and raises `stop_count`, moves the route `DRAFT → STACKED` when it was the first stop, and advances the carried order `READY → ASSIGNED`.

Un-stacking is the exact compensating inverse. Removing a stop requires the route to be `STACKED` and the stop itself to be `PENDING`; a stop in any other status is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STOP_STATE`. The stop's status becomes `RELEASED`, its order reverts `ASSIGNED → READY`, and the route's `committed_hot_bag_slots` and `stop_count` both fall back to their pre-insertion values — the hot-bag pool must return to exactly the total it had before, which is why released stops stop being counted. When the removal drops `stop_count` to 0 the route returns `STACKED → DRAFT`. Surviving stops keep the `sequence` values they were born with.

### 3.8. Committing the Route: Capacity, Route Duration and the Mileage Waterfall

Committing a route is the heaviest gate in the system, because it is the moment the store reserves scarce physical resources — a qualified insured driver and a finite number of insulated bags — and freezes a price. A `Dispatcher` commits a route that is in `DRAFT` or `STACKED`, advancing it `STACKED → LOCKED`. Seven substantive conditions must hold, and §2.8 fixes the order in which they are reported.

The route's driver must be `QUALIFIED`; a `SUSPENDED` or `DISQUALIFIED` driver is refused with `ERR_DRIVER_NOT_QUALIFIED`. The driver's newest `VERIFIED` insurance certificate must cover the route's `planned_depart_at` — at or after `coverage_from`, strictly before `coverage_to` — and not merely cover the present moment; a certificate that covers today but lapses before the planned departure is refused with `ERR_INSURANCE_NOT_ACTIVE`. Every counted stop's order must have a settlement status of `DISCLOSED` or `CHARGED`, or the commitment is refused with `ERR_FEE_NOT_DISCLOSED`: fee-versus-tip disclosure is a gate on dispatch rather than on delivery, so nothing rolls until every ticket on the route has been quoted to its customer. The driver's occupied interval for this route — from `planned_depart_at` through `expected_return_at` plus the store's `dispatch_reset_buffer_minutes`, closed at both ends — must not intersect the same interval of any other route of the **same driver** whose status is `LOCKED` or `EN_ROUTE`, or the commitment is refused with `ERR_DRIVER_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. The store's bag pool must accommodate the route: taking every route of the same store whose status is `LOCKED` or `EN_ROUTE` and whose half-open reservation window from `planned_depart_at` inclusive to `expected_return_at` exclusive intersects this route's window, the sum of their `committed_hot_bag_slots` plus this route's own `committed_hot_bag_slots` must not exceed the store's `hot_bag_count`, or the commitment is refused with `ERR_HOT_BAG_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. This is an aggregate, store-wide constraint spanning different drivers: each route can be individually legal while the collective demand exceeds the bags on the shelf, and two routes whose windows merely touch share no bags at all. The schedule selector must resolve exactly one schedule, or the commitment is refused with `ERR_NO_COST_MODEL`. And the route must carry at least one counted stop, or the commitment is refused with `ERR_DISPATCH_HAS_NO_STOPS` — an empty route has no schedule to resolve, no last stop to drive home from, and nothing to reimburse.

**Selecting the schedule.** The selector considers every vehicle cost model whose `store_id` matches the driver's store, whose `vehicle_class` matches the driver's `vehicle_class`, whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, and whose `effective_from` is at or before the route's `planned_depart_at`. If that set is empty, the commitment fails as described above. Otherwise the set is ranked by `effective_from` descending and, among schedules sharing an `effective_from`, by `created_at` descending; the first schedule in that ranking wins, and its identifier is snapshotted onto the route's `resolved_cost_model_id`. Ties are broken by creation time and never by identifier. Because the snapshot is taken here, a schedule published tonight cannot retro-price a route locked this afternoon.

**Modelling the round trip.** The route's `route_minutes` covers the whole journey: out to each door in `sequence` ascending order, a flat handoff allowance at each door, and the drive home from the last door. Counted stops are those whose status is `PENDING`, `DELIVERED` or `REMADE`. The outbound component is the sum, over counted stops in `sequence` ascending order, of each stop's `planned_miles` multiplied by the in-force delivery-area version's `minutes_per_mile` and rounded up to the next whole minute — the rounding-up is applied to each leg individually, before the legs are added together, never to the sum. The handoff component is the store's `handoff_minutes_per_stop` multiplied by the number of counted stops. The homeward component is modelled as a repeat of the last counted stop's outbound leg: that stop's `planned_miles` multiplied by `minutes_per_mile` and rounded up to the next whole minute. `route_minutes` is the sum of those three components, and `expected_return_at` is `planned_depart_at` advanced by that many minutes. Because the last stop contributes both an outbound and a homeward leg, removing the final stop shortens the modelled route twice over. With 3 minutes per mile and a 6-minute handoff allowance, a single 3.00-mile stop models at 9 plus 6 plus 9, which is 24 minutes; two 3.00-mile stops model at 9 plus 9, plus 12 of handoff, plus a 9-minute home leg, for 39; and three stops of 3.00, 3.00 and 9.00 miles model at 9 plus 9 plus 27, plus 18 of handoff, plus a 27-minute home leg, for 90.

**The mileage waterfall.** What the store owes a driver for a route is computed stop by stop, in `sequence` ascending order, and the order is observable because the tier-1 mileage allowance is consumed **cumulatively across the whole route** rather than reset at each stop. Start a running cumulative-mileage counter at zero and a running total at zero, then process each stop in turn. For the stop in hand, the remaining tier-1 room is the schedule's `tier1_cap_miles` minus the cumulative mileage consumed so far, or zero if that difference would be negative. The stop's tier-1 miles are the lesser of the stop's own mileage and that remaining room; its tier-2 miles are whatever the stop's mileage has left over. The stop's raw mileage money is its tier-1 miles multiplied by `tier1_per_mile_cents` plus its tier-2 miles multiplied by `tier2_per_mile_cents`, computed exactly and left fractional at this point. Add the schedule's `per_delivery_cents` to that figure to get the stop's raw subtotal. **Round that subtotal half up to a whole number of cents, then raise it to `min_reimbursement_per_stop_cents` if it falls below that floor, and only then add it to the running total** — the rounding happens once per stop, the floor is applied after the rounding, and the summation happens after both. Finally, add the stop's mileage to the cumulative counter before moving to the next stop.

The consequences of that ordering are worth spelling out. Take a car schedule paying 150 cents per delivery, with a 5.00-mile tier-1 cap, 62 cents per tier-1 mile, 41 cents per tier-2 mile and a 100-cent per-stop floor. For stops of 3.00, 3.00 and 9.00 miles, the first stop draws 3.00 miles entirely from tier 1 for 186 cents of mileage, giving 336 cents; the second stop finds only 2.00 miles of tier-1 room left, so it prices 2.00 miles at 62 and 1.00 mile at 41 for 165 cents of mileage, giving 315; the third stop is entirely tier 2, 9.00 miles at 41 for 369 cents, giving 519; the route totals 1170 cents. The same schedule against a single 3.00-mile stop pays 336; against two 3.00-mile stops, 651; against stops of 3.00 and 9.00 miles, 336 plus 561, which is 897. The per-stop rounding is not cosmetic: two stops of 0.25 miles each price at 150 plus 15.5, which is 165.5, rounding half up to 166 per stop for a route total of 332 — summing the raw subtotals first would have given 331. And the floor genuinely bites: a scooter schedule paying 90 cents per delivery with a 3.00-mile cap, 35 cents per tier-1 mile and a 250-cent per-stop floor pays 250 cents for a single 1.00-mile stop, because the raw 125 cents falls short of the floor.

The waterfall runs in two variants over two different input sets. The **estimated** variant counts stops whose status is `PENDING`, `DELIVERED` or `REMADE` and uses each stop's `planned_miles`; it is what fills the route's `estimated_reimbursement_cents` at commitment time and what prices each provisional ledger line written at a door. The **actual** variant counts only stops whose status is `DELIVERED` or `REMADE` — a released stop earns nothing — and uses each stop's `actual_miles`; it is what fills `actual_reimbursement_cents` at settlement. Both variants apply the identical per-stop rounding, per-stop floor and cumulative tier consumption described above.

Committing a route therefore writes four fields at once: `route_minutes` and `expected_return_at` from the round-trip model, `resolved_cost_model_id` from the selector, and `estimated_reimbursement_cents` from the estimated variant of the waterfall. It also reserves the driver's occupied interval and the store's hot-bag slots, which is what makes the next route's commitment harder. Releasing a commitment reverses all of it: a `Dispatcher` may move a route `LOCKED → STACKED`, which returns `route_minutes`, `expected_return_at`, `resolved_cost_model_id` and `estimated_reimbursement_cents` all to null and frees both the driver's occupied interval and the store's bag reservation, so that stops can be added or removed and the route committed again. A route may cycle between committed and stacked as many times as the dispatcher needs.

### 3.9. Out of the Door: Departure, the Handoff, and the Order-to-Door Stopwatch

Departure is a driver's own act and nobody else's. The acting `Driver`'s `X-User-ID` must be the route's own driver, the route must be `LOCKED`, the driver must still be `QUALIFIED` at this moment — a suspension signed after the route was committed bites here, with `ERR_DRIVER_NOT_QUALIFIED` — and the driver's newest verified certificate must cover the logical clock now, not merely the planned departure instant, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_INSURANCE_NOT_ACTIVE`. One further gate is fiscal: the driver's `cash_on_hand_cents` must be **strictly below** the store's `cash_limit_cents`, equivalently the driver's `cash_posture` must read `CLEAR`. A driver holding exactly the limit is already blocked and must visit the safe first; the refusal is `ERR_CASH_LIMIT_REACHED` and it carries HTTP 402. On success the route advances `LOCKED → EN_ROUTE`, `departed_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and every order sitting behind a `PENDING` stop on the route advances `ASSIGNED → EN_ROUTE`.

At each door the driver records the handoff. The route must be `EN_ROUTE`, the stop must be `PENDING`, and the stop's order must be `EN_ROUTE`. Two optional fields are accepted. `actual_miles` is the odometer-derived mileage the driver actually covered to reach this stop, at least 0 and carrying two decimal places; it is genuinely optional at the door, because a driver who forgets to read the odometer should still be able to record the handoff — the omission is caught later, at settlement. `cash_collected_cents` is the money taken at this door, and its rules are exact: for an order whose `payment_method` is `CASH` it is **required** and must equal that order's `total_charge_cents` — any other value, or its absence, is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; for an order whose `payment_method` is `CARD` it must be omitted or supplied as 0.

Recording the handoff sets the stop's `arrived_at` from the logical clock and moves the stop's status to `DELIVERED`, then unwinds a four-hop cascade. First, the order's fulfillment dimension advances `EN_ROUTE → DELIVERED`, its `door_arrival_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and both order-to-door figures are computed. Second, because the order has reached `DELIVERED`, its settlement dimension advances `DISCLOSED → CHARGED` and the order's own `cash_collected_cents` is fixed — equal to `total_charge_cents` for a cash order, 0 for a card order. Third, because the order is now charged and its `payment_method` is `CASH`, the driver's `cash_on_hand_cents` and the store's `cash_collected_total_cents` each rise by that order's `total_charge_cents`, and the driver's `version` and the store's `version` each increment by 1. Fourth, because the driver's cash on hand has risen, the driver's `cash_posture` is re-derived and flips to `DROP_REQUIRED` the moment `cash_on_hand_cents` reaches or exceeds the store's `cash_limit_cents` — which is exactly what blocks that driver's next departure. Alongside the cascade, one provisional reimbursement ledger line is written for this stop: its `line_type` is `PROVISIONAL_STOP`, its `basis` is `ESTIMATED`, its `status` is `PROVISIONAL`, its `sequence_snapshot` records the stop's `sequence`, and its `amount_cents` is this stop's stop-total under the estimated variant of the mileage waterfall.

**The stopwatch, reported two ways.** `order_to_door_gross_minutes` is what the customer experienced: the difference between the order's `door_arrival_at` and its `placed_at`, taken in whole seconds, divided by 60 and truncated downward, so a partly elapsed minute is not yet counted. `order_to_door_net_minutes` removes the delays the franchise is not judged on: it is the gross figure minus the order's accumulated `zone_hold_minutes` and minus its accumulated `remake_hold_minutes`, clamped so that it never falls below 0. An order placed at noon and handed over at 12:36 reads 36 gross and, with no holds, 36 net; the same order with 8 accumulated hold minutes reads 36 gross and 28 net; with 40 accumulated hold minutes it reads 36 gross and 0 net rather than a negative figure. The two variants are never interchangeable: both the remake-eligibility test and the late-complaint tier read the **gross** figure only, while the net figure exists purely for franchise service reporting.

Checking the route back in is again the driver's act, advancing it `EN_ROUTE → RETURNED`. Every stop on the route must have reached a terminal stop status — `DELIVERED`, `RELEASED` or `REMADE` — or the check-in is refused with `ERR_STOPS_OUTSTANDING`, because a driver cannot come back while a pizza is still in the bag. Check-in stamps `returned_at` from the logical clock and releases the store's hot-bag reservation for this route, so the route's `committed_hot_bag_slots` re-derives to 0 and the bags become available to whoever locks next.

### 3.10. Cash on Hand and the Safe-Drop Procedure

Money collected at doors either sits on the driver or sits in the store safe. It never exists in both places and it never disappears, and that conservation identity is the whole point of the cash sub-system. A driver's `cash_on_hand_cents` is the sum of `cash_collected_cents` over every stop of that driver's routes whose status is `DELIVERED` or `REMADE`, minus the sum of `amount_cents` over every safe drop of that driver whose status is `RECORDED`. The driver's `cash_posture` is `DROP_REQUIRED` when `cash_on_hand_cents` is greater than or equal to the owning store's `cash_limit_cents`, and `CLEAR` otherwise. At store level, `cash_collected_total_cents` is the sum of the collected component across the store's drivers and `safe_deposit_total_cents` is the sum of the recorded-drop component across the same drivers. All four figures are exact integer cents with no rounding anywhere, and all four are re-derived on read. The identity the manager's reconciliation read proves is that a store's `cash_collected_total_cents` always equals its `safe_deposit_total_cents` plus the combined cash on hand of its drivers.

Dropping cash is available to the `Driver` themselves or to a `StoreManager` acting on their behalf. A drop names the `amount_cents` moved into the safe, which must be at least 1, and the `envelope_number` of the pre-printed drop envelope it went into. The drop may not exceed what the ledger says is in the pouch: an `amount_cents` greater than the driver's `cash_on_hand_cents` is refused with `ERR_SAFE_DROP_EXCEEDS_CASH`, because a drop must never break the conservation identity. A recorded drop begins with `status` `RECORDED`, `voided_reason` null and `voided_at` null. It lowers the driver's `cash_on_hand_cents` and raises the store's `safe_deposit_total_cents` by `amount_cents`, re-derives the driver's `cash_posture` — which is how a `DROP_REQUIRED` driver becomes `CLEAR` again and is unblocked to depart — and increments both the driver's `version` and the store's `version` by 1 each.

Mis-keyed drops happen, so a `StoreManager` may reverse one, but only briefly. Voiding requires a `voided_reason` and advances the drop `RECORDED → VOIDED`, its only transition and its terminal state; voiding a drop that is already `VOIDED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_SAFE_DROP_STATE`. The window is strict: the difference between the logical clock and the drop's `created_at`, measured in whole seconds, must be **less than** 60 minutes. At exactly 3600 seconds the envelope is in the safe for good and the attempt is refused with `ERR_SAFE_DROP_NOT_VOIDABLE`. A successful void stamps `voided_at` from the logical clock, records the `voided_reason`, and is the exact compensating inverse of the drop: the driver's `cash_on_hand_cents` rises by `amount_cents`, the store's `safe_deposit_total_cents` falls by the same figure, and both the driver's `version` and the store's `version` increment by 1. Consider the whole cycle: a driver collects 2900 cents at one door, so the driver holds 2900, the store has collected 2900 and the safe holds 0, and 2900 equals 0 plus 2900. The driver drops all 2900, so the driver holds 0 and the safe holds 2900, and 2900 equals 2900 plus 0. The manager voids that drop inside the window, so the driver holds 2900 again and the safe holds 0. The identity holds at every instant.

### 3.11. Closing the Route: Settlement, Recall, and the Reimbursement Ledger

The reimbursement ledger is an append-only record of what the store owes its drivers, and every line on it carries the route that produced it, the payee driver, the order it is attributable to, a `line_type`, a `basis`, a signed `amount_cents`, a `sequence_snapshot` and a `status`. `line_type` is `PROVISIONAL_STOP` for a line written at a door on the estimated basis, `ACTUAL_STOP` for a line written at settlement on the actual basis, or `CLAWBACK` for a line written when a complaint remedy is charged back to a driver. `basis` records which variant of the waterfall produced the amount and is `ESTIMATED`, `ACTUAL` or `NONE`. `status` is `PROVISIONAL` while the route is unsettled, `ACCRUED` once the money is genuinely payable, `SUPERSEDED` once an actual line has displaced a provisional one, and `VOIDED` when the route was recalled. `sequence_snapshot` carries the `sequence` of the stop the line was computed for, and is 0 on a clawback line, which belongs to no stop. `amount_cents` is signed and is negative only on a clawback line.

What a driver is actually owed on the next payroll run — the driver's `reimbursement_accrued_cents` — is the sum of `amount_cents` across every ledger line of that driver whose `status` is `ACCRUED`, and nothing else. `PROVISIONAL`, `SUPERSEDED` and `VOIDED` lines contribute nothing whatsoever, and a clawback line reduces the total naturally because its amount is negative. The consequence is that a driver who has locked a three-stop route and knocked on all three doors still shows 0 accrued, because every line written at a door is provisional; the same driver shows 1203 once that route settles for 1203 cents; and shows 842 after an approved chargeable complaint claws back the 361-cent line for one of those stops.

Settlement is the manager's payroll act on a route that has been checked in, advancing it `RETURNED → SETTLED`. The acting manager must own the driver's store. Every stop whose status is `DELIVERED` or `REMADE` must have an `actual_miles` reading on file, or settlement is refused with `ERR_ODOMETER_MISSING` — the actual variant of the waterfall is undefined without every odometer reading, and the service refuses rather than silently falling back to planned mileage. Settlement then does five things. It computes `actual_reimbursement_cents` from the actual variant of the waterfall, over stops in `DELIVERED` or `REMADE` status using their `actual_miles`, and sets `reimbursement_variance_cents` to the actual figure minus the frozen `estimated_reimbursement_cents`. It moves every `PROVISIONAL_STOP` line of the route to `SUPERSEDED`. It writes one `ACTUAL_STOP` line per `DELIVERED` or `REMADE` stop, each with `basis` `ACTUAL` and `status` `ACCRUED`. Because those accrued lines now exist, the driver's `reimbursement_accrued_cents` re-derives upward by the route's `actual_reimbursement_cents` and the driver's `version` increments by 1. And every order on the route that is `DELIVERED` advances on the fulfillment dimension `DELIVERED → CLOSED` and on the settlement dimension from `CHARGED` or `ADJUSTED` to the terminal `CLOSED_SETTLED`. To see the variance concretely: the three-stop route that estimated 1170 cents on planned mileage of 3.00, 3.00 and 9.00 miles settles against odometer readings of 3.40, 2.80 and 9.60 miles as 361 plus 298 plus 544, which is 1203, giving a `reimbursement_variance_cents` of positive 33.

Recall is the opposite ending, available to a `StoreManager` or a `Dispatcher`, and it pays nothing. A route in `STACKED`, `LOCKED` or `EN_ROUTE` status can be recalled with a supplied `recalled_reason`, advancing it to the terminal `RECALLED`. Recall records the reason, frees the driver's occupied interval and the store's hot-bag reservation, releases every `PENDING` stop — each released stop's order reverting on the fulfillment dimension from `ASSIGNED` or `EN_ROUTE` back to `READY`, which raises the store's `unassigned_order_count` on its next read — and moves every `PROVISIONAL_STOP` line of the route to `VOIDED`. Stops already `DELIVERED` keep their status and the cash already collected at those doors stays on the driver's pouch, exactly where the conservation identity says it is. No `ACTUAL_STOP` line is ever written for a recalled route, so a recall leaves the driver's `reimbursement_accrued_cents` untouched. The divergence from check-in is deliberate: checking a route in preserves its stop commitments so that settlement can pay them, while recalling a route reverses them and pays nothing. One special case sits alongside: recalling a route that is still `DRAFT` with a `stop_count` of 0 records the `recalled_reason` and drives the route to the terminal `CANCELLED` instead of `RECALLED`, since there is nothing to release.

### 3.12. Service Recovery: Remakes, Complaints and Clawbacks

When a delivery goes wrong the franchise has two instruments, and they are separate. A remake replaces the food; a complaint remedy refunds money. A `StoreManager` queues a remake on an order whose fulfillment status is `DELIVERED`, supplying a `reason`. Two constraints govern it. The order's `remake_count` must still be below 1 — one remake per order, and the second is refused with `ERR_REMAKE_LIMIT_REACHED` even when the order runs late a second time. And when the `reason` given is `LATE`, the order's `order_to_door_gross_minutes` must be **strictly greater** than its `promise_minutes_snapshot` plus a 5-minute grace allowance, or the request is refused with `ERR_REMAKE_NOT_ELIGIBLE`; against a 30-minute promise, exactly 35 gross minutes is inside the grace and is refused, and only 36 or more qualifies. This test reads the gross figure and never the net one.

Queueing a remake advances the order `DELIVERED → REMAKE_QUEUED` and rewinds the stopwatch. The whole minutes between the recorded `door_arrival_at` and the logical clock — the whole-second difference divided by 60 and truncated downward — accrue into the order's `remake_hold_minutes`; `door_arrival_at` is then set back to null and both `order_to_door_gross_minutes` and `order_to_door_net_minutes` are cleared, because the delivery that produced them has been disowned. The order's `remake_count` increments by 1 and its `remake_credit_cents` rises by its `food_subtotal_cents`, recording the food cost the store is absorbing. The stop that delivered the order moves from `DELIVERED` to `REMADE`, keeping the cash it collected and staying counted for reimbursement. And the settlement dimension advances `CHARGED → ADJUSTED`. The order then re-enters the make line through the production start action, closing the cycle `REMAKE_QUEUED → IN_PRODUCTION` and travelling the delivery path again.

Complaints are logged by a `CustomerCareAgent` or a `StoreManager` against an order, naming the `order_id`, a `category` and the customer's verbatim `description`. The `category` is one of `LATE`, `FEE_DISCLOSURE`, `QUALITY` or `DRIVER_CONDUCT`, and it selects the remedy formula. A new complaint stamps `logged_by_user_id` with the logging user's `X-User-ID` and begins in `OPEN` status with `proposed_remedy_cents`, `remedy_basis`, `resolved_by_user_id` and `resolved_at` all null, `driver_chargeable` false and `clawback_cents` 0. Complaint statuses are `OPEN`, `REMEDY_PROPOSED`, and the terminal `REMEDY_ISSUED` and `DISMISSED`; an action not declared from the current status is refused with `ERR_INVALID_COMPLAINT_STATE`.

A `CustomerCareAgent` sizes the credit, advancing the complaint `OPEN → REMEDY_PROPOSED`. The complaint's order must have a fulfillment status of `DELIVERED`, `REMAKE_QUEUED` or `CLOSED`. **The remedy formula** reads a different basis per category, and every branch is capped at the end. For a `LATE` complaint, compute how many minutes the order ran over by subtracting the order's `promise_minutes_snapshot` from its `order_to_door_gross_minutes`; if that overage is 20 minutes or more, the food percentage is 100 and the whole `delivery_fee_cents` is additionally refunded; if the overage is at least 10 but under 20, the percentage is 50 and no fee is refunded; if the overage is at least 1 but under 10, the percentage is 20 and no fee is refunded; and if the order was not late at all the percentage is 0 with no fee refund. The raw remedy is the order's `food_subtotal_cents` multiplied by that percentage, divided by 100, rounded half up to a whole cent, plus any fee refund, and the `remedy_basis` recorded is `LATE_TIER`. For a `FEE_DISCLOSURE` complaint the raw remedy is the order's `delivery_fee_cents` plus a flat 500-cent penalty when the order's `fee_disclosure_accurate` is false, with no penalty when it is true, and the `remedy_basis` is `FEE_REFUND`. For a `QUALITY` complaint the raw remedy is half of `food_subtotal_cents` — the subtotal multiplied by 50, divided by 100, rounded half up — and the `remedy_basis` is `QUALITY_HALF`. For a `DRIVER_CONDUCT` complaint the raw remedy is 0 and the `remedy_basis` is `CONDUCT_ZERO`; conduct is handled as a personnel matter, not with the customer's money. Finally, and applied last in every branch, `proposed_remedy_cents` is the lesser of the raw remedy and the order's `total_charge_cents`: the store never proposes to refund more than the ticket. A late order with 2200 cents of food, 36 gross minutes and a 30-minute promise is 6 minutes over, so it proposes 440; the same order at 50 gross minutes is 20 minutes over, so it proposes the whole 2200 plus the 200-cent fee, which is 2400. A quality complaint on 2200 cents of food proposes 1100. A fee-disclosure complaint on a 200-cent fee proposes 700 when the disclosure was inaccurate and 200 when it was accurate. And the cap genuinely binds: an inaccurate fee-disclosure complaint on an order with 100 cents of food, a 399-cent fee and no tip has a raw remedy of 899 against a ticket total of 499, so it proposes 499.

Only a `StoreManager` resolves a complaint, supplying an `action` and, optionally, `driver_chargeable`. `REJECT` sends a proposal back for reconsideration, advancing the complaint `REMEDY_PROPOSED → OPEN` and clearing `proposed_remedy_cents`, `remedy_basis` and `resolved_by_user_id` all back to null; the agent may re-propose afterwards, any number of times. `DISMISS` advances an `OPEN` complaint to the terminal `DISMISSED`, stamping `resolved_at` and `resolved_by_user_id`, and moves no money at all. `APPROVE` advances a `REMEDY_PROPOSED` complaint to the terminal `REMEDY_ISSUED` and is guarded twice. First, the acting manager must own the order's store. Second, `proposed_remedy_cents` must be no greater than the order's `total_charge_cents` minus its already-issued `remedy_issued_cents` — the **remaining** refundable balance at approval time — or the approval is refused with `ERR_REMEDY_EXCEEDS_TOTAL`. That is a stricter test than the proposal-time cap, and deliberately so: proposals are each capped at the full ticket, but approvals are cumulative, so a second complaint on the same order may only draw down whatever the first one left behind. Third, when `driver_chargeable` is true, the route that settled the complaint's order must have reached `SETTLED`, or the approval is refused with `ERR_CLAWBACK_NOT_SETTLED` — there is no actual-basis line to claw back until the route has settled.

An approval stamps `resolved_at` from the logical clock and `resolved_by_user_id` with the approving manager, raises the order's `remedy_issued_cents` by `proposed_remedy_cents`, and advances the order's settlement dimension to `ADJUSTED` from whichever of `CHARGED`, `ADJUSTED` or `CLOSED_SETTLED` it occupied. Re-entering `ADJUSTED` from `ADJUSTED` is a legal self-loop: a second approved complaint re-adjusts an already-adjusted ticket, drawing down the remaining refundable balance. When `driver_chargeable` is true the approval additionally writes a ledger line with `line_type` `CLAWBACK`, `basis` `NONE`, `status` `ACCRUED`, `sequence_snapshot` 0, and an `amount_cents` equal to the negation of the `ACTUAL_STOP` line amount recorded for this order; the complaint's `clawback_cents` records the absolute value of that amount, and because the new line is accrued and negative, the driver's `reimbursement_accrued_cents` re-derives downward by that same figure while the driver's `version` increments by 1. When `driver_chargeable` is false or omitted, no ledger line is written and `clawback_cents` stays 0.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Every path below is mounted beneath the `/store-ops` prefix. An endpoint that brings a brand-new resource into existence answers with HTTP 201 and returns that new resource in the `data` field; this includes the action endpoints that create a record as their principal effect, such as filing a motor-vehicle-record pull, filing an insurance certificate, signing a qualification decision, stacking a stop onto a route, or recording a safe drop. Every other successful call — a read, a list, an edit, a state transition, an un-stack, a void, and the clock setter — answers with HTTP 200. A successful un-stack returns the parent route rather than the removed stop, since the stop no longer exists. Requests are refused with the status codes catalogued in §5: 400 for a malformed or incomplete request body, 401 for a missing or unrecognised caller identity, 402 for the cash-on-hand limit that blocks a departure, 403 for a caller whose role or ownership does not permit the operation, 404 for a referenced resource that does not exist, 409 for a state conflict or a uniqueness, capacity or window collision, 412 for a stale parent version on the two version-guarded writes, and 422 for a semantically invalid value or an unmet business precondition.
