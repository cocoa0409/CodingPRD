# Aviation Fuel Farm & Into-Plane Fueling API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

An airport fuel consortium operates the jet fuel supply chain for a single airport: it receives parcels of product by pipeline and by road tanker, holds them in a farm of bulk storage tanks, subjects every parcel to a laboratory quality-control chain before a single liter is allowed to touch the ledger, and then meters that product into aircraft wings either through a pressurised hydrant loop or from mobile truck refuellers. Today this work is coordinated across a patchwork of paper meter tickets, laboratory worksheets, spreadsheet stock reconciliations, handwritten fuel release codes read over the radio, and month-end billing runs assembled by hand. The consequences of that fragmentation are expensive and occasionally dangerous: quarantined product finds its way into a wing, a tank is overfilled past its safe fill line, a discharge permit's daily throughput ceiling is breached without anyone noticing, an airline flies on credit it does not have, and the physical stock at month end disagrees with the book stock by a volume nobody can explain.

This document specifies the API for a single system of record that closes those gaps. Four commitments shape the design. First, **the ledger is sacred**: a liter enters a tank's book volume only when a quality inspector signs a release verdict, and it leaves only when an into-plane uplift ticket is posted or a reconciliation adjustment is written — every movement is attributable to a document. Second, **posture gates operations**: the farm continuously derives an operational posture from the state of its tanks, refuelling assets, open spills and environmental permit, and that posture can restrict or entirely freeze downstream fuelling activity without an operator having to remember to stop it. Third, **segregation of duties is structural, not procedural**: the inspector who draws a sample cannot sign its release verdict, and the billing clerk who issues an invoice cannot resolve its dispute. Fourth, **concurrency is explicit**: where two operators can plausibly be looking at the same fuelling order or airline account from two different vehicles, writes carry the version the client last observed and are rejected rather than silently overwritten.

The reader should treat the fuel farm as a ledger system wearing safety equipment. Volumetric arithmetic — temperature correction to 15 degrees Celsius, water deduction capping, gauge-versus-meter-ticket variance, tiered contract pricing, taxes and fees — must be reproducible to the cent and to two decimal places of a liter, because these numbers are the basis of both regulatory reporting and airline invoicing.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that user is acting in. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not exactly one of the seven role names below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). When the headers are well formed but the named role is not among the roles authorised for the requested endpoint, the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The seven roles are `ConsortiumAdmin`, `FarmOperator`, `QualityInspector`, `IntoPlaneAgent`, `AirlineRep`, `EnvironmentalOfficer`, and `BillingClerk`.

- A `ConsortiumAdmin` is the consortium's operations manager. This role registers fuel farms, environmental permits, storage tanks, fuelling assets, hydrant pits and airline accounts, posts reconciliation runs, and drives the system's logical clock. It shares tank service-status changes and asset status changes with `FarmOperator`, shares flight creation with `AirlineRep`, and may read every collection in the system.
- A `FarmOperator` runs the farm floor: recording incoming fuel receipts, taking gauge measurements, isolating and returning tanks to service, rejecting quarantined parcels back to the supplier, opening reconciliation runs and posting their measurements.
- A `QualityInspector` owns the product-quality chain: drawing samples from receipts, tanks and post-filter-change membranes, posting laboratory results, signing receipt release and quarantine verdicts, releasing and quarantining tanks on the quality dimension, and performing filter element changes.
- An `IntoPlaneAgent` works the ramp: authorising, assigning, unassigning, starting, aborting and completing fuelling orders, posting uplift tickets, and reporting spills observed during fuelling. Order creation and order cancellation are shared with `AirlineRep`, and spill reporting is shared with `EnvironmentalOfficer`.
- An `AirlineRep` is an airline fuel-desk representative acting on behalf of exactly one airline account. It may create flights, request fuel authorizations (shared with `BillingClerk`), raise and cancel fuelling orders, dispute invoices, and read its own account's flights, authorizations, orders, uplift tickets and invoices.
- An `EnvironmentalOfficer` is the compliance authority: suspending and reinstating environmental permits, and driving the spill response chain through escalation, containment, remediation and closure. Spill reporting is shared with `IntoPlaneAgent`.
- A `BillingClerk` handles the money: issuing fuel authorizations (shared with `AirlineRep`), voiding authorizations, sweeping uplift tickets into draft invoices, issuing, disputing-resolution, voiding and settling invoices, and recording payments.

**Identity semantics.** When `X-User-Role` is `AirlineRep`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `AirlineAccount`; ownership checks on any resource that references an `AirlineAccount` compare the resource's airline-account reference to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, an `AirlineRep` acting on a flight, a fuel authorization, a fuelling order or an invoice whose owning account is not the account named by `X-User-ID` is rejected with `ERR_ACCOUNT_OWNERSHIP_REQUIRED` (HTTP 403); for a fuelling order or an uplift ticket the owning account is reached through the order's flight. For the other six roles — `ConsortiumAdmin`, `FarmOperator`, `QualityInspector`, `IntoPlaneAgent`, `EnvironmentalOfficer` and `BillingClerk` — `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a workflow demands an audit trail (for example as `sampled_by_user_id` and `released_by_user_id` on a fuel receipt, `drawn_by_user_id` and `tested_by_user_id` on a quality sample, `changed_by_user_id` on a filter change, `posted_by_user_id` on a reconciliation run, and `issued_by_user_id` and `dispute_resolved_by_user_id` on an invoice) but never compares it to any entity's `id`.

**Segregation of duties.** Three workflow steps compare the acting `X-User-ID` against the identifier recorded at the preceding step of the same workflow, and reject a match with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403): an inspector may not sign a receipt verdict when the acting identifier equals the receipt's `sampled_by_user_id`; an inspector may not release a tank on the quality dimension when the acting identifier equals the `drawn_by_user_id` of the governing periodic tank sample; and a billing clerk may not resolve a dispute on an invoice whose `issued_by_user_id` equals the acting identifier.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1, and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` or `offset` outside those bounds, or one that is not parseable as an integer at all, is a field-constraint violation and is rejected with `ERR_FIELD_OUT_OF_RANGE`. Those two parameters are the only query parameters a list endpoint validates. The filter parameters named in the §4 descriptions are matched literally and conjunctively against the rows and are never validated: a filter value that is not a member of the field's enumeration, or that names no existing row, simply matches nothing and yields an empty page — it is **not** `ERR_FIELD_OUT_OF_RANGE` and **not** `ERR_REFERENCED_ENTITY_NOT_FOUND`, since the enumeration and existence constraints of §2.8 and §2.10 govern request payloads rather than query strings. A filter parameter that is absent, or present with an empty value, is not applied; an unrecognised parameter name is ignored.

List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending unless a subsection of this document explicitly states another ordering for a particular collection. The `meta` object on a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` (the total number of rows matching the query before pagination) and `has_more` (true when rows remain beyond the current page).

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Versioning

Every resource carries a server-minted `id` that is a UUID string with no type prefix; clients never supply an `id`. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording the moment of creation.

Every ISO 8601 instant the server puts on a resource uses that one rendering — `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SS.mmmZ`, normalised to UTC, exactly three fractional digits, never more and never fewer — and that includes instants the client itself supplied in a payload. A submitted `scheduled_departure_utc`, `valid_from_utc`, `valid_until_utc`, `window_start_utc`, `window_end_utc`, `isolation_window_start_utc`, `isolation_window_end_utc`, `period_start_utc` or `period_end_utc` is parsed to an instant, converted to UTC and re-rendered in that canonical shape rather than echoed back verbatim, so a client that sends `2026-08-01T12:00:00Z` reads back `2026-08-01T12:00:00.000Z`, and one that sends an offset such as `2026-08-01T14:00:00+02:00` reads back `2026-08-01T12:00:00.000Z`. Server-minted instants (`released_at_utc`, `last_gauge_at_utc`, `changed_at_utc`, `last_filter_change_at_utc`, `reported_at_utc`, `received_at_utc`, `expires_at_utc`, `created_at`, `updated_at`) are rendered identically. Equality of two timestamps is therefore equality of the instant to the millisecond, never of the literal string as submitted.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at` — same ISO 8601 UTC format with three fractional digits, refreshed on every mutation of that row — and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on each mutation of that row. The one exception is the filter-change record: a `FilterChange` is an immutable audit document, so it carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has neither `updated_at` nor `version`, and nothing in the system ever mutates it after creation.

Derived figures are also refreshed on read, so a client never sees a stale computed value. Reading a farm recomputes its posture, permit utilisation, in-service asset count and open spill count before responding. Reading a tank recomputes its book, reserved and available volumes. Reading an asset recomputes its element life. Reading an airline account recomputes both of its exposure figures. Reading a fuel authorization — individually or in a list — re-evaluates whether it has expired against the logical clock. These read-time refreshes follow exactly the same formulas as the write-time ones described in §3.

Version increments are not confined to the row the client addressed. Fuel farm operations are deeply interlinked, and a single write frequently advances the `version` of several rows — the addressed row, its parent farm, the source tank, the assigned asset, the airline account, the authorization. Each affected row's `version` rises by exactly 1 per request, however many derived fields on it were recomputed, and each affected row's `updated_at` is refreshed to the logical clock instant of the request. The per-workflow lists of which rows are touched appear in §3.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

Time in this system is not wall-clock time. A single logical clock, settable only by a `ConsortiumAdmin`, supplies the instant referred to throughout this document as the current logical clock. Every timestamp the server writes — a receipt's `released_at_utc`, a gauge's `last_gauge_at_utc`, a filter change's `changed_at_utc`, a spill's `reported_at_utc`, the `created_at` and `updated_at` of every row — is taken from that clock. Every time-based comparison — permit validity, authorization expiry, fuelling window membership, reconciliation period elapse, filter element age — is evaluated against it.

Advancing the clock is therefore a first-class business event, not a test convenience. Two lifecycle transitions are purely time-driven and are evaluated lazily: a fuel authorization moves from `ISSUED` to `EXPIRED` once the clock has reached or passed its `expires_at_utc`, and an environmental permit moves from `ACTIVE` to `EXPIRED` once the clock has reached or passed its `valid_until_utc`. Both are evaluated whenever the row (or, for the permit, its farm) is read or referenced, and whenever a child mutation recomputes the farm's posture, so a clock advance followed by a read is enough to observe the transition. Each such auto-transition raises the affected row's `version` by 1, and a permit expiry additionally recomputes the farm's posture — which will land on `FROZEN`, because a farm without an active permit covering the clock is frozen by definition.

Time windows in this domain are consistently **half-open** — start inclusive, end exclusive — with exactly one deliberate exception. A permit covers the clock when the clock is at or after `valid_from_utc` and strictly before `valid_until_utc`. A fuelling window admits work when the clock is at or after `window_start_utc` and strictly before `window_end_utc`. A reconciliation period, a billing period, and the permit-day throughput window all follow the same convention. The exception is asset scheduling: an asset's occupied interval is treated as **closed** at both ends and extended by its turnaround buffer, which is described in §3.

### 2.6. Volumetric and Monetary Precision

All volumes in liters are carried and compared at 2 decimal places, rounded half up. All volume correction factors are carried at 5 decimal places, rounded half up, and — this matters — the correction factor is rounded to 5 decimals **before** it is used as a multiplier, never afterwards. All variance percentages on reconciliation runs are carried at 4 decimal places, rounded half up, as are the three legs of the filter element life computation.

Money is carried exclusively in integer cents. Rates are quoted in cents per liter and may have fractional parts, but every monetary component is rounded half up to a whole number of cents at the point the specification says it is rounded, and only rounded components are ever summed. The distinction is load-bearing: summing unrounded components and rounding the sum once produces a different answer and is explicitly not the rule. Where a computation pins an evaluation order across steps, that order is part of the contract even when the steps look independent.

### 2.7. Farm Operational Posture

Each fuel farm continuously derives a `posture`, one of `OPEN`, `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN`, and that posture gates the farm's child operations. Posture is never set by a client; it is recomputed by the server on every mutation of a child storage tank, fuelling asset, spill incident or environmental permit, on every fuel receipt release, and on every advance of the logical clock.

The recomputation runs in a fixed order. First, the **governing permit** is identified: among the farm's environmental permits, the one with the latest `valid_from_utc` governs; if two permits share the same `valid_from_utc`, the tie is broken by `created_at` ascending, so the earliest-created of the tied permits wins. The permit is considered satisfactory when it exists, its `status` is `ACTIVE`, and the logical clock is at or after its `valid_from_utc` and strictly before its `valid_until_utc`. Second, the farm is **frozen** if either the governing permit is not satisfactory or at least one spill incident at the farm has `status` `ESCALATED`. Third, the farm is **restricted** if any of four conditions holds: at least one storage tank at the farm has `quality_status` `QUARANTINED`; at least one spill incident at the farm has `status` `REPORTED`; the farm's `active_permit_throughput_used_liters` has reached or exceeded 90 percent of the governing permit's `max_daily_throughput_liters`; or the farm's `in_service_asset_count` is zero. The resulting posture is `FROZEN` when the frozen condition holds, otherwise `RESTRICTED` when any restricted condition holds, otherwise `OPEN`. Note the ordering consequence: a frozen farm stays `FROZEN` regardless of how many restricted conditions also hold, and the 90-percent utilisation comparison is inclusive — a farm sitting at exactly 90.00 percent of its permitted daily throughput is `RESTRICTED`, not `OPEN`.

Alongside the posture the same pass refreshes two counters on the farm: `open_spill_count`, the number of spill incidents at the farm whose `status` is `REPORTED` or `ESCALATED`, and `in_service_asset_count`, the number of fuelling assets at the farm whose `status` is `IN_SERVICE`. The two postures gate differently. A `FROZEN` farm blocks the whole set of operations enumerated in §2.10. A `RESTRICTED` farm is narrower: it blocks the assignment of an asset to an order and the start of fuelling for those orders that draw on a tank whose `quality_status` is `QUARANTINED`, while leaving orders on clean tanks to proceed normally. Operations that a `FROZEN` posture blocks are rejected with `ERR_FARM_FROZEN`, except that on the four operations which also carry the active-permit guard listed in §2.10 the absence of a satisfactory governing permit is reported as `ERR_PERMIT_NOT_ACTIVE` instead.

### 2.8. Nested Path Resolution and Referential Integrity

Several collections are addressed as children of a parent in the URL — permits, tanks, assets, pits and reconciliation runs under a farm; samples, gauge measurements and status changes under a tank; samples and verdicts under a receipt; filter changes under an asset; uplift tickets under a fuelling order; payments under an invoice. Path parameters are validated strictly left to right. If the outer identifier does not resolve to an existing row, the request fails with `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` before the inner identifier is looked at; if the outer resolves but the inner does not, or the inner row exists but is not a child of the named outer row, the result is likewise `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`.

Identifiers supplied inside a request body are a separate concern. A foreign-key identifier in a payload that does not resolve to any existing row yields `ERR_REFERENCED_ENTITY_NOT_FOUND`. A payload whose identifiers each resolve but do not agree — a tank, asset, pit, flight and authorization that are not all anchored to the same farm, or an authorization that does not belong to the flight named on the same order — yields `ERR_CROSS_FARM_REFERENCE`. Payload shape problems come first: a missing required field or a field of the wrong type is `ERR_INVALID_PAYLOAD`, and a value that resolves and is well typed but breaks a declared numeric or enumeration constraint is `ERR_FIELD_OUT_OF_RANGE`. Uniqueness collisions are their own class: a farm's `iata_code`, a permit's `permit_number`, a receipt's `meter_ticket_number`, an uplift ticket's `ticket_number`, an airline account's `airline_iata_code`, a tank's `tank_code` within its farm, an asset's `asset_code` within its farm and a pit's `pit_code` within its farm must all be free, and a collision is `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY`.

### 2.9. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Two write operations in this system can legitimately be repeated against the same parent, which means no state precondition can distinguish a deliberate repetition from one written against an out-of-date view. Both therefore require the client to declare the parent version it last observed.

Posting an uplift ticket onto a fuelling order requires the non-optional field `expected_fueling_order_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of that order. A second ticket on the same order is legitimate product behaviour — a top-up after a first partial ticket, each with its own `ticket_number` — so the request cannot be refused on state grounds. If `expected_fueling_order_version` does not equal the order's current `version`, the post is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ORDER_VERSION` (HTTP 412), which is what prevents a ticket from being written against an out-of-date view of the order's posted volume and the tank reservation behind it.

Creating a draft invoice requires the non-optional field `expected_airline_account_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the billed airline account. Creating a second draft over an overlapping period is legitimate, since the later draft supersedes the earlier sweep, so nothing about invoice state refuses it. If `expected_airline_account_version` does not equal the account's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ACCOUNT_VERSION` (HTTP 412), catching the case where the account's exposure figures moved — a completed order, an issued invoice, a voided invoice — after the clerk computed the sweep.

No other endpoint in this system accepts a version field. Requests to endpoints outside these two must not be expected to supply one, and supplying one has no effect.

### 2.10. Validation Precedence

When several checks could reject the same request, the order in which they are evaluated is part of the contract, because it determines which error identifier the caller sees. The system evaluates checks in the following bands, and within each band in the order written:

1. **Authentication and authorisation.** Header presence and role validity (`ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`) is checked first, then endpoint role membership (`ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`), then — for the five operations an `AirlineRep` may reach, namely flight creation, fuel authorization creation, fuelling order creation, fuelling order cancellation and invoice dispute — account ownership (`ERR_ACCOUNT_OWNERSHIP_REQUIRED`).
2. **Existence.** Path identifiers are resolved left to right (`ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`) on every endpoint carrying a path parameter, then body foreign keys are resolved in the order the fields are listed in the endpoint's input (`ERR_REFERENCED_ENTITY_NOT_FOUND`), then aggregate agreement is checked (`ERR_CROSS_FARM_REFERENCE`) on fuelling order creation and order assignment — specifically that the quoted authorization's flight matches the order's flight, that the hydrant pit and the storage tank belong to the same farm, and that the fuelling asset and the storage tank belong to the same farm.
3. **Payload shape and static constraints.** Required-field presence and type correctness (`ERR_INVALID_PAYLOAD`) precede declared value constraints (`ERR_FIELD_OUT_OF_RANGE`), which cover every numeric range and enumeration membership declared in this document as well as the relational constraints — a receipt's closing gauge must exceed its opening gauge, an uplift's meter end reading must exceed its meter start reading, a fuelling window's end must be later than its start, a reconciliation or billing period's end must be later than its start, and a tank's safe fill volume must not exceed its shell capacity. Scoped uniqueness (`ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY`) is checked next, on farm creation, permit creation, tank creation, asset creation, pit creation, receipt creation, airline account creation and uplift ticket creation. Wherever this document requires a string to be non-empty, the test is literal and nothing is trimmed: only the empty string `""` fails it, so a value made up entirely of whitespace counts as non-empty, passes the check and is stored exactly as supplied. Which identifier an empty value reports is fixed by the rule that demands the field, not by this band.
4. **Concurrency and state.** The two optimistic-concurrency checks come next — `ERR_STALE_ORDER_VERSION` on uplift posting and `ERR_STALE_ACCOUNT_VERSION` on invoice creation — followed by the terminal-state guard (`ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`), which applies to every state-transition endpoint and fires when the target row already sits in a terminal state for its machine: a fuel receipt in `RELEASED` or `REJECTED`, a fuelling order in `COMPLETED`, `CANCELLED` or `ABORTED`, a fuel authorization in `REDEEMED`, `VOIDED` or `EXPIRED`, a spill incident in `CLOSED`, an invoice in `PAID` or `VOIDED`, or a reconciliation run in `POSTED`. Only then are the per-machine from-state guards evaluated: `ERR_INVALID_RECEIPT_STATE` on receipt sampling, verdict and rejection; `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATE` on order authorize, assign, unassign, start, uplift posting, complete, abort and cancel; `ERR_INVALID_TANK_STATE` on tank service-status changes, quality release, quarantine and tank sampling; `ERR_INVALID_ASSET_STATE` on asset status changes and filter changes; `ERR_INVALID_SPILL_STATE` on spill escalate, contain, remediate and close; `ERR_INVALID_INVOICE_STATE` on invoice issue, dispute, dispute resolution, void and payment; and `ERR_INVALID_RUN_STATE` on reconciliation measure and post.
5. **Safety and compliance.** The frozen-farm guard (`ERR_FARM_FROZEN`) is evaluated before anything else in this band, so a mid-flight freeze surfaces as a freeze rather than being masked by a later check. It guards eleven operations: receipt creation, receipt sampling, receipt verdict, tank sampling, fuelling order creation, order authorization, order assignment, fuelling start, uplift posting, filter changes, and asset status changes. The active-permit guard (`ERR_PERMIT_NOT_ACTIVE`) follows on four operations: receipt creation, receipt verdict, order authorization and uplift posting. On exactly those four operations the two guards are fused and the permit condition is inspected first, because an unsatisfactory governing permit is itself a freeze cause and the caller is better served by the specific diagnosis: if the governing permit is not satisfactory the rejection is `ERR_PERMIT_NOT_ACTIVE`, and only a farm that is `FROZEN` for some other reason (an escalated spill) while its permit is satisfactory reports `ERR_FARM_FROZEN`. On the other seven operations, which carry the frozen-farm guard alone, a `FROZEN` farm always reports `ERR_FARM_FROZEN` whatever the cause of the freeze. Segregation of duties (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`) comes next on its three operations — receipt verdict, tank quality release and invoice dispute resolution. Then the source-tank quality guard (`ERR_TANK_QUARANTINED`) on order authorization, fuelling start and uplift posting; then the sample-verdict guard (`ERR_SAMPLE_VERDICT_FAIL`) on receipt verdicts; then the water-bottom guard (`ERR_WATER_BOTTOM_EXCEEDED`) on tank quality release and tank service-status changes; then the re-test ceiling (`ERR_RETEST_LIMIT_REACHED`) on receipt sampling; then asset serviceability (`ERR_ASSET_NOT_SERVICEABLE`) on order assignment, fuelling start and uplift posting; then spill reportability (`ERR_SPILL_NOT_REPORTABLE`) on spill escalation; then the empty-result guard (`ERR_EMPTY_TEST_RESULT_SET`) on posting laboratory results.
6. **Referential business checks.** The quoted authorization code must match character for character (`ERR_AUTH_CODE_MISMATCH`) — checked on both fuelling order creation and order authorization — and then the authorization must still be redeemable (`ERR_AUTHORIZATION_NOT_REDEEMABLE`) on order authorization.
7. **Mode, temporal and capacity checks.** Delivery-mode coherence (`ERR_DELIVERY_MODE_MISMATCH`) is checked on order creation and order assignment. Then the two scheduling conflicts, pit first and asset second: `ERR_PIT_WINDOW_CONFLICT` then `ERR_ASSET_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, both only on order assignment. Then tank isolation conflict (`ERR_TANK_ISOLATION_CONFLICT`) on tank service-status changes; then fuelling-window membership (`ERR_OUTSIDE_FUELING_WINDOW`) on fuelling start and uplift posting; then reconciliation period elapse (`ERR_PERIOD_NOT_ELAPSED`) followed by measurement-set completeness (`ERR_MEASUREMENT_SET_INCOMPLETE`), both on posting measurements; then the variance override requirement (`ERR_VARIANCE_OVERRIDE_REQUIRED`) on posting a run; then the at-least-one-ticket rule (`ERR_NO_UPLIFT_RECORDED`) on order completion; then the empty-sweep rule (`ERR_NOTHING_TO_SWEEP`) on invoice creation; then the dispute ceiling (`ERR_DISPUTE_LIMIT_REACHED`) on raising a dispute.
8. **Fiscal and ledger checks, evaluated last.** Safe fill (`ERR_SAFE_FILL_EXCEEDED`) on the receipt verdict; then permit daily throughput (`ERR_PERMIT_THROUGHPUT_EXCEEDED`) on the receipt verdict and on uplift posting; then tank volume sufficiency (`ERR_INSUFFICIENT_TANK_VOLUME`) on order authorization, on uplift posting, and on order assignment for the `TRUCK` supply check of §3.6; then the credit check (`ERR_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`) on order authorization; then the authorization ceiling (`ERR_UPLIFT_EXCEEDS_AUTHORIZATION`) on uplift posting and order completion; and finally the payment bounds check (`ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_BALANCE`) on recording a payment.

The practical reading is that structural problems always beat business problems, business-state problems beat safety problems, and safety problems beat money problems. A request that is both unauthorised and fiscally impossible reports the authorisation failure.

### 2.11. Cascade Depth and Ledger Conservation

Two events in this system fire cascades three hops deep, and both are specified hop by hop in §3: a quality quarantine landing on a tank, and a farm transitioning into `FROZEN` posture. In both cases hop 1 changes a status, hop 2 is caused by hop 1 and reaches the fuelling orders drawing on the affected resource, and hop 3 is caused by hop 2 and reaches those orders' uplift tickets, tank reservations, hydrant pit occupancy, fuel authorizations and airline exposure figures. Cascades run to completion within the request that triggered them; there is no deferred or eventual settlement.

Three conservation invariants must hold after every cascade, and they are the fastest way to check an implementation. **Fuel volume conservation**: when a truck-delivered order is aborted, the voided tickets leave the posted set and the truck's `onboard_volume_liters` recomputes to zero, so the sum of the source tank's `book_volume_liters` and the asset's `onboard_volume_liters` returns to exactly the value it held before fuelling started. **Permit throughput conservation**: a rejected parcel never touched the ledger, so rejecting a quarantined receipt leaves the farm's `active_permit_throughput_used_liters` exactly as it was — only receipts that reach `RELEASED` and uplift tickets that are `POSTED` or `BILLED` count toward the permit day. **Airline exposure conservation**: voiding an issued invoice returns the account's `accrual_exposure_cents` to precisely its pre-issue value, because the swept tickets go back to `POSTED` and re-enter the uncollected term at the same amount they left it.

Note one deliberate asymmetry in the freeze cascade: assets driven to `DOWN_MAINTENANCE` by a farm freeze are **not** automatically restored when the farm unfreezes. A `ConsortiumAdmin` must return each one to `IN_SERVICE` individually.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up a Consortium Farm

Before any fuel moves, a `ConsortiumAdmin` builds out the static picture of the airport. Registering a farm requires the airport's `iata_code` — exactly three uppercase letters, unique across all farms — and a consortium `name`. Two optional inputs shape the farm's later arithmetic: `hydrant_system_available`, a boolean defaulting to false that determines whether the farm can serve hydrant deliveries at all, and `unpumpable_bottom_liters`, the volume in each tank that sits below the suction point and can never be issued, which must be zero or greater and defaults to 500.0 liters. A newly registered farm's `posture` is `FROZEN`, because a farm with no active permit covering the logical clock is frozen by definition, and its `active_permit_throughput_used_liters`, `in_service_asset_count` and `open_spill_count` all start at zero.

An environmental permit is what thaws the farm. Issuing one requires a regulator-issued `permit_number` unique across all permits, an inclusive `valid_from_utc`, an exclusive `valid_until_utc`, and a `max_daily_throughput_liters` strictly greater than zero expressing the permitted net movement — released receipt volume plus posted uplift volume — for a single UTC day. The optional `max_reportable_spill_liters` sets the volume at or above which a release must be reported to the regulator and defaults to 20.0 liters. A permit begins life with `status` `ACTIVE`, and issuing it recomputes the farm's posture, which typically leaves `FROZEN` at that moment. An `EnvironmentalOfficer` may later drive `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`, which requires a non-empty `suspension_reason` recorded on the permit and immediately drives the farm to `FROZEN` because it no longer has an active permit — the full freeze cascade described in §3.7 fires on that same request. The reverse, `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE`, is permitted only while the logical clock is still strictly before the permit's `valid_until_utc`, and it recomputes posture. Both moves raise the permit's `version` and the farm's `version` by one each.

Tank registration establishes the ledger baseline. The `tank_code` must be unique within the farm; `capacity_liters` is the nominal shell capacity and must be greater than zero; `safe_fill_liters` is the ceiling on book volume after any receipt release and must be greater than zero and no greater than `capacity_liters`; `density_kg_per_m3` is the certificated product density at 15 degrees Celsius for the tank's parcel and must be at least 750 and at most 850. The optional `opening_volume_liters` is the net volume at 15 degrees Celsius present when the tank is registered, defaulting to 0.0, required to be zero or greater and no greater than `safe_fill_liters`; it is the immovable baseline of every subsequent ledger computation. A fresh tank's `service_status` is `IN_SERVICE` and its `quality_status` is `ON_TEST`, so it is mechanically available but not yet permitted as a source of product. Its `isolation_window_start_utc`, `isolation_window_end_utc`, `last_gauge_volume_liters` and `last_gauge_at_utc` are all null, and `water_bottom_mm` starts at 0.0.

Fuelling assets come in two shapes, declared by `asset_type`: a `TRUCK` refueller carries product on board, while a `HYDRANT_CART` only meters product drawn from the pressurised loop. The `asset_code` must be unique within the farm. `tank_capacity_liters` describes the on-board tank, defaults to 0.0, must be zero or greater, and must be zero for a `HYDRANT_CART`, which by construction carries nothing. `turnaround_buffer_minutes` is the mandatory gap after a booked fuelling window before the asset may take its next order; it defaults to 15 minutes and must be zero or greater. An optional `differential_pressure_kpa` records the filter differential pressure at registration and defaults to 0.0. On registration the asset's `last_filter_change_at_utc` is stamped with the current logical clock, `liters_since_filter_change` starts at 0.0, `element_life_pct` is computed from those counters, `reliability_score` starts at 100, `onboard_volume_liters` is 0.0, and `status` is `IN_SERVICE`.

Hydrant pits may only be registered at a farm whose `hydrant_system_available` is true. Each pit needs a `pit_code` unique within the farm, a `stand_code` naming the aircraft stand it serves, and a `source_storage_tank_id` identifying the tank whose product the loop feeds through it. A pit's `status` starts `AVAILABLE`; the only other value, `LOCKED_OUT`, is reserved for administrative lockout when the pit's source tank is taken out of service, and is never used to represent momentary occupancy by an order.

On the commercial side, an airline account is opened with an `airline_iata_code` of exactly two uppercase alphanumeric characters, unique across all accounts, a `legal_name`, and a `contract_type`. A `CONTRACT_FUEL` airline buys product at the consortium contract price; an `INTO_PLANE_ONLY` airline supplies its own fuel and pays handling only, so its `base_price_cents_per_liter` is zero. That field is required in the payload, expressed in cents per liter, and must be zero or greater. `credit_limit_cents` caps the account's permitted accrual-basis exposure and must be zero or greater. The optional `tax_exempt` flag, defaulting to false, records that a valid federal excise and state sales tax exemption certificate is on file. A new account's `status` is `ACTIVE` — the alternative being `CREDIT_HOLD` — and both `invoiced_exposure_cents` and `accrual_exposure_cents` start at zero. Registering a flight to be fuelled requires an `airline_account_id`, a `flight_number` unique for that account and scheduled departure, a `tail_number`, a `scheduled_departure_utc` which anchors both the authorization code and the fuelling window, and a `stand_code`. A flight begins `SCHEDULED` and may later be `DEPARTED` or `CANCELLED`.

The logical clock itself is driven by an admin-only capability that accepts a single `to_iso8601` instant. The startup default is exactly 2026-08-01T00:00:00Z, and the server never falls back to real wall-clock time. Advancing the clock re-evaluates authorization expiry, permit expiry, every asset's element life, and every farm's posture in that same request. That list is closed: beyond those re-evaluations and the status moves and `version` bumps they themselves entail, the clock advance writes nothing. In particular, when a permit expiry on a clock advance lands a farm on `FROZEN`, the freeze cascade of §3.8 does **not** fire on that request — no order is cancelled or aborted, no asset is driven to `DOWN_MAINTENANCE`, no uplift ticket is voided and no authorization is voided by the clock advance itself. The freeze cascade fires on the child mutations that recompute posture, not on the clock.

### 3.2. Receiving a Parcel and Correcting Its Volume

A `FarmOperator` records an inbound parcel the moment it lands, and the arithmetic performed at that instant is frozen forever: the volumes computed on creation are never recomputed, and they are exactly what a later release verdict adds to the tank ledger. The payload names the destination tank with a `storage_tank_id`, declares how the parcel arrived through `delivery_mode` (either `PIPELINE` or `TRUCK`), and carries a `supplier_name`, a `meter_ticket_number` unique across all receipts, the `meter_ticket_gross_liters` printed on the supplier's ticket (greater than zero), the `tank_opening_gauge_liters` read before the parcel landed (zero or greater), the `tank_closing_gauge_liters` read afterwards (strictly greater than the opening reading), the observed `temperature_c` in Celsius (at least minus 20 and at most 60), and the `certificate_density_kg_per_m3` from the supplier's refinery certificate of quality (at least 750 and at most 850). An optional `measured_water_liters`, defaulting to 0.0 and required to be zero or greater, records the free water drained from the parcel before capping.

The correction runs in six pinned steps. **First**, a thermal expansion coefficient is selected by density band from the certificate density: 0.000850 when the certificate density is at least 800.0 kilograms per cubic metre, otherwise 0.000900 when it is at least 780.0, otherwise 0.000950. **Second**, the volume correction factor is one minus the coefficient multiplied by the parcel temperature less the 15-degree reference temperature, and this factor is rounded half up to five decimal places *before* it multiplies anything. **Third**, two gross bases are derived: the gauge delta, being the closing gauge reading less the opening gauge reading rounded half up to two decimals, and the meter gross, being the supplier ticket figure as printed. Delivery mode alone decides which governs. For a `TRUCK` parcel the `governing_gross_liters` is the supplier meter ticket and the `advisory_gross_liters` is the gauge delta; for a `PIPELINE` parcel it is the other way round — the gauge delta governs and the meter ticket becomes advisory. Two otherwise identical receipts differing only in delivery mode therefore write different volumes into the same tank ledger, which is deliberate: road tankers are metered warm and gauged after settling, so the two bases genuinely disagree, and the consortium's contract says the meter ticket rules for road and the tank rules for pipeline. **Fourth**, the corrected volume is the governing gross multiplied by the correction factor, rounded half up to two decimals. **Fifth**, the water deduction cap is one half of one percent of the *governing gross* — not of the corrected volume — rounded half up to two decimals, and the deduction actually applied is the smaller of the measured water volume and that cap. **Sixth**, `net_volume_liters` is the corrected volume less the applied deduction, rounded half up to two decimals again. The `advisory_net_volume_liters` repeats the fourth, fifth and sixth steps on the advisory gross basis, using the same correction factor and the same measured water figure against a cap of one half of one percent of the advisory gross; it exists purely for the reconciliation audit trail and never enters the ledger.

A newly recorded receipt sits in `RECORDED` and has added nothing to any tank. Its `sampled_by_user_id`, `released_by_user_id` and `released_at_utc` are null and its `retest_round` is zero. Recording a receipt is blocked when the farm's posture is `FROZEN` (`ERR_FARM_FROZEN`) or when the farm has no active permit covering the clock (`ERR_PERMIT_NOT_ACTIVE`).

The quality chain then advances the receipt through four steps. Drawing the receipt QC sample moves it `RECORDED → SAMPLED`; this is a `QualityInspector` action and requires that the farm of the receipt's tank is not `FROZEN` and that the destination tank's `service_status` is not `OUT_OF_SERVICE`. It creates a quality sample whose `sample_purpose` is `RECEIPT_QC`, whose `status` is `DRAWN`, and whose `fuel_receipt_id` points back at this receipt, stamps the receipt's `sampled_by_user_id` with the acting identifier, and raises the receipt's `version` by one.

Posting laboratory results against a drawn sample moves the receipt `SAMPLED → UNDER_TEST`. The precondition is that the referenced sample is still `DRAWN` and belongs to this receipt. Five metrics may be posted, each optional individually but with at least one required overall — a payload in which every metric is null is rejected with `ERR_EMPTY_TEST_RESULT_SET`. The metrics are `water_reaction_rating`, the ASTM D1094 interface rating, an integer of at least 1 and at most 3; `particulate_mg_per_l`, membrane particulate contamination, zero or greater; `conductivity_ps_per_m`, static dissipator conductivity, zero or greater; `observed_density_kg_per_m3`, the density at 15 degrees Celsius measured on the sample; and `flash_point_c`, the flash point in Celsius. Posting results sets the sample's `status` to `TESTED`, records `tested_by_user_id` from the acting identifier, and raises the receipt's `version` by one.

The verdict computation is a strict all-pass gate over the metrics that were actually posted; a null metric is skipped entirely and cannot fail. The water reaction rating fails when it exceeds 1. The particulate figure fails when it exceeds 1.0 milligrams per litre, so exactly 1.0 passes. The conductivity fails when it falls outside the inclusive band from 50 to 600 picosiemens per metre, so both 50 and 600 pass. The observed density fails when its absolute difference from the comparison density exceeds 3.0 kilograms per cubic metre, where the comparison density is the parent receipt's `certificate_density_kg_per_m3` for a `RECEIPT_QC` sample and the parent tank's `density_kg_per_m3` otherwise. The flash point fails when it is below 38.0 degrees Celsius, so exactly 38.0 passes. The names of the failing metrics are collected into `failed_metrics` in a fixed order — water reaction rating first, then particulate, then conductivity, then observed density, then flash point — regardless of the order they appeared in the payload. The `verdict` is `PASS` when nothing failed and `FAIL` otherwise. A sample's own lifecycle has just two values, `DRAWN` and `TESTED`, and its polymorphic parentage is expressed by exactly one of `fuel_receipt_id`, `storage_tank_id` or `fueling_asset_id` being set, with `drawn_by_user_id` always recording who took it.

### 3.3. Signing the Verdict: Release, Quarantine, Re-test and Rejection

A tested receipt awaits a signature, and the signature carries a `decision` of either `RELEASE` or `QUARANTINE`. Both branches are `QualityInspector` actions and both refuse a signer whose identifier equals the receipt's `sampled_by_user_id` — the inspector who drew the sample may not sign its verdict, and an attempt is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Both branches also require that a `TESTED` sample exists for the receipt at all; if none does, or if the decision is `RELEASE` and the latest tested sample's verdict is `FAIL`, the request is rejected with `ERR_SAMPLE_VERDICT_FAIL`.

The `UNDER_TEST → RELEASED` branch is the only path by which product enters the ledger, and it carries the heaviest set of preconditions. The latest tested sample must have verdict `PASS`. The destination tank's current `book_volume_liters` plus the receipt's `net_volume_liters` must not exceed the tank's `safe_fill_liters`, or the release is refused with `ERR_SAFE_FILL_EXCEEDED`. The farm's `active_permit_throughput_used_liters` plus the receipt's net volume must not exceed the governing permit's `max_daily_throughput_liters`, or the release is refused with `ERR_PERMIT_THROUGHPUT_EXCEEDED`. And the farm must have an `ACTIVE` permit covering the current logical clock. On success the receipt's `released_at_utc` is stamped from the logical clock and its `released_by_user_id` from the acting identifier; the receipt's net volume enters the tank ledger, which recomputes the tank's `book_volume_liters`, `reserved_volume_liters` and `available_volume_liters`; the farm's permit-day throughput counter is recomputed; and the farm's posture is recomputed, which may land on `RESTRICTED` the moment permit utilisation reaches the 90 percent boundary. The tank's `version` and the farm's `version` each rise by one, and the receipt's own `version` rises by one. `RELEASED` is terminal for the receipt.

The `UNDER_TEST → QUARANTINED` branch is the sibling with a wholly disjoint effect set, and it is the deeper of the two: it is the depth-three quality cascade. Its only preconditions are that a `TESTED` sample exists and that the signer differs from the sampler; no volume, safe-fill or permit check applies, because nothing is being added. **Hop one** sets the destination tank's `quality_status` to `QUARANTINED`, which is the tank's own quality-dimension move from either `RELEASED` or `ON_TEST` into `QUARANTINED`. **Hop two**, caused by hop one, reaches every fuelling order drawing on that tank: each order in `ASSIGNED` is driven `ASSIGNED → AUTHORIZED`, releasing its `fueling_asset_id` and `hydrant_pit_id` and raising its `unassign_count` by one, and each order in `FUELING` is driven `FUELING → ABORTED` with `abort_reason` set to `QUALITY_HOLD`. **Hop three**, caused by hop two, settles the consequences: each unassigned or aborted order's hydrant pit occupancy ends; every `POSTED` uplift ticket on an aborted order becomes `VOIDED` with `voided_reason` `ORDER_ABORTED` and its `net_liters` are credited back to the tank's `book_volume_liters`; each affected fuel authorization is driven to `VOIDED`; and the farm's posture is recomputed to `RESTRICTED`. The receipt's `released_by_user_id` is stamped with the acting identifier even on this branch, and the receipt's, the tank's and the farm's `version` each rise by one.

From `QUARANTINED` a receipt has exactly two futures. A `QualityInspector` may open a re-test round, moving `QUARANTINED → SAMPLED`, which is permitted only while the receipt's `retest_round` is at most 1 — so a parcel may be re-sampled at most twice, and a third attempt on a receipt already at a `retest_round` of 2 is rejected with `ERR_RETEST_LIMIT_REACHED` — and only while the farm is not `FROZEN`. A re-sample raises `retest_round` by one, creates a fresh `RECEIPT_QC` sample in `DRAWN`, re-stamps `sampled_by_user_id`, and raises the receipt's `version` by one. The revision cycle is therefore `QUARANTINED → SAMPLED → UNDER_TEST → RELEASED` or back to `QUARANTINED`.

Alternatively a `FarmOperator` may give up on the parcel and return it to the supplier, moving `QUARANTINED → REJECTED` with an optional `reason`. Rejection is the conservation case: no ledger volume is added and the farm's `active_permit_throughput_used_liters` is left exactly as it was, because only receipts that actually reached `RELEASED` ever counted toward the permit day. Rejection also has a healing effect on the tank — if no other receipt targeting that tank is still `QUARANTINED` and the tank's latest periodic tank QA sample carries verdict `PASS`, the tank's `quality_status` returns to `RELEASED`. Posture is recomputed and the receipt's and tank's `version` each rise by one. `REJECTED` is terminal.

### 3.4. Tank Custody: Two Independent Dimensions

A storage tank carries two status dimensions that evolve on the same row without constraining each other's shape. The mechanical dimension, `service_status`, moves among `IN_SERVICE`, `ISOLATED` and `OUT_OF_SERVICE`. The product dimension, `quality_status`, moves among `ON_TEST`, `RELEASED` and `QUARANTINED`. A tank may perfectly well be `RELEASED` on quality while `ISOLATED` on service, and the two dimensions must be tracked and reported separately. Service-status changes are requested by a `FarmOperator` or `ConsortiumAdmin` with a `to_status` naming the destination.

Isolating a tank, `IN_SERVICE → ISOLATED`, requires an isolation window supplied as `isolation_window_start_utc` and `isolation_window_end_utc`, and it competes for a scarce resource: **a farm may have at most one tank isolated over any given instant.** No order drawing on the tank may be `ASSIGNED` or `FUELING`, and the requested half-open window must not intersect the isolation window of any other tank at the same farm whose `service_status` is already `ISOLATED`; a conflict is `ERR_TANK_ISOLATION_CONFLICT`. The window is stored on the tank, the tank's `available_volume_liters` is forced to 0.0 for the duration of the isolation, posture is recomputed, and the tank's and farm's `version` each rise by one. Returning `ISOLATED → IN_SERVICE` requires that the tank's `quality_status` is not `QUARANTINED` and that its `water_bottom_mm` is strictly below 25.0 millimetres — at 25.0 or above the return is refused with `ERR_WATER_BOTTOM_EXCEEDED`. The return clears both isolation window fields to null and recomputes availability and posture, again raising both versions. Retiring a tank, `ISOLATED → OUT_OF_SERVICE`, is a `ConsortiumAdmin` action permitted only once the tank has been drained down to its unpumpable heel: the tank's `book_volume_liters` must not exceed the farm's `unpumpable_bottom_liters`. Retirement additionally locks out every hydrant pit whose `source_storage_tank_id` names this tank, setting each pit's `status` to `LOCKED_OUT`, then recomputes posture and raises both versions. `OUT_OF_SERVICE` is terminal for the service dimension.

On the quality dimension, a fresh tank starts `ON_TEST` and cannot yet source an order. Signing a tank's quality release, `ON_TEST → RELEASED`, is a `QualityInspector` action requiring three things: a periodic tank QA sample exists for this tank whose `sample_purpose` is `PERIODIC_TANK_QA`, whose `status` is `TESTED`, whose `verdict` is `PASS`, and whose `created_at` is the latest of any sample for this tank; the acting identifier differs from that sample's `drawn_by_user_id`, since an inspector may not release a tank on the strength of a sample they drew themselves; and the tank's `water_bottom_mm` is strictly below 25.0. Release makes the tank eligible as an order source, recomputes posture, and raises the tank's and farm's `version` by one each.

Quarantining a tank, `RELEASED → QUARANTINED`, arrives by three different routes: a direct request by a `QualityInspector` carrying an optional `reason`, the cascade hop from a receipt quarantine described above, or a gauge measurement reporting free water at or above 25.0 millimetres. Whichever route drives it, the same hop two and hop three fire: every order on the tank in `ASSIGNED` is unassigned back to `AUTHORIZED` and every order in `FUELING` is aborted with `abort_reason` `QUALITY_HOLD`; then the freed orders' pit occupancy ends, the aborted orders' `POSTED` uplift tickets become `VOIDED` with their `net_liters` credited back to `book_volume_liters`, their authorizations are voided, and the farm's posture becomes `RESTRICTED`. The tank's and farm's `version` each rise by one. Recovery runs the other way: drawing a fresh periodic tank QA sample moves `QUARANTINED → ON_TEST`, which is permitted only when no receipt targeting the tank is still `QUARANTINED`. That request creates a `DRAWN` sample for the tank, recomputes posture, and raises both versions, completing the quality cycle `ON_TEST → RELEASED → QUARANTINED → ON_TEST`.

Manual gauging is how physical reality enters the tank record. A `FarmOperator` posts a `measured_volume_liters` (the net volume at 15 degrees Celsius from the dip), a `temperature_c`, and a `water_bottom_mm`. The measurement writes `last_gauge_volume_liters`, stamps `last_gauge_at_utc` from the logical clock, and stores the water depth. When the reported water depth reaches 25.0 millimetres **and the tank's `quality_status` is `RELEASED`**, the gauge itself forces the quality quarantine and its two downstream hops; this route, like the direct request, is exactly the `RELEASED → QUARANTINED` transition, so a tank sitting on `ON_TEST` or already `QUARANTINED` merely records the water depth and keeps the quality status it had. Posture is recomputed in every case, and the gauge is never refused on water-bottom grounds.

The tank ledger is never maintained incrementally. On each of its triggers — a receipt release, an uplift ticket being posted, an uplift ticket being voided, an order abort, and a reconciliation run being posted — the tank's figures are recomputed in full from the source rows. The `book_volume_liters` is the tank's `opening_volume_liters`, plus the sum of `net_volume_liters` over every receipt destined for this tank whose `status` is `RELEASED`, minus the sum of `net_liters` over every uplift ticket drawn from this tank whose `status` is `POSTED` or `BILLED`, plus the sum of `adjustment_liters` written for this tank by every reconciliation run whose `status` is `POSTED`, the whole rounded half up to two decimals. Receipts in `RECORDED`, `SAMPLED`, `UNDER_TEST`, `QUARANTINED` or `REJECTED` contribute nothing; `VOIDED` uplift tickets contribute nothing. The `reserved_volume_liters` is the sum of `requested_uplift_liters` over every order on this tank whose `status` is `AUTHORIZED`, `ASSIGNED` or `FUELING`, rounded half up to two decimals. The `available_volume_liters` is the book volume less the reserved volume less the farm's `unpumpable_bottom_liters`, rounded half up to two decimals and then floored at 0.0 so it can never go negative.

### 3.5. Fuel Release Authorizations and Airline Credit

Nothing may be uplifted into a wing without a release authorization, and the authorization's code is the token the into-plane agent quotes on the ramp. Issuing one is available to both an `AirlineRep` acting for its own account and to a `BillingClerk`, and the payload names the `airline_account_id` to be billed, the `flight_id` the authorization may be redeemed for — exactly one flight, never a bearer credit — and a `max_authorized_liters` strictly greater than zero that ceilings the total posted uplift volume redeemable under it.

The `authorization_code` is minted deterministically, with no random component, so it is fully reproducible from the account, the flight and the issuance order. It is assembled as fourteen characters in three parts plus a check. The first two characters are the account's `airline_iata_code`. The next six are the flight's `scheduled_departure_utc` rendered in UTC as a two-digit year, then a two-digit month, then a two-digit day. The next four are the `day_sequence` zero-padded to four digits, where the day sequence is one more than the number of authorizations already existing for the same airline account whose flight departs on the same UTC calendar day, counted in `created_at` ascending order and counted regardless of status — voided and expired authorizations still consume their sequence number, so a number is never reused. Those twelve characters form the body. The final two characters are a check value: the ASCII code points of the twelve body characters are summed, the remainder of that sum on division by 97 is taken, and that remainder is zero-padded to two digits. The `expires_at_utc` is set to the flight's scheduled departure plus 2 hours, and the authorization opens in `ISSUED` with `voided_reason` null.

The lazy expiry evaluation applies to the creation response as well as to later reads: issuing an authorization for a flight whose departure plus two hours already lies at or before the current logical clock is not refused, but the row is expired on the spot and comes back with `status` `EXPIRED` and `version` 2 — created at `ISSUED` with `version` 1, then advanced one step by the immediate expiry check. Such an authorization can never authorise an order, so a flight must be scheduled ahead of the clock for its authorizations to be usable.

An authorization leaves `ISSUED` by one of three doors, all terminal. It is `REDEEMED` by the system when an order completes against it, subject to that order's `posted_net_liters` not exceeding the authorization's `max_authorized_liters`. It is `VOIDED` — manually by a `BillingClerk`, or by the system when the order that quoted it is cancelled or aborted, or by the freeze cascade — and the `voided_reason` records which: `MANUAL` for the clerk's own action, `ORDER_CANCELLED` when an order cancellation or abort drove it, and `FARM_FROZEN` when the farm freeze cascade drove it. A manual void additionally requires that no order referencing the authorization is currently `ASSIGNED` or `FUELING`. It becomes `EXPIRED` by the system once the logical clock has reached or passed its `expires_at_utc`; this is evaluated lazily whenever the authorization is read or referenced after a clock advance, and an expired authorization can no longer authorise an order. Every one of these moves raises the authorization's `version` by one.

Airline credit is measured on two bases that answer different questions, and the difference between them is the point of the design. The **invoiced basis** counts only what has actually been billed: `invoiced_exposure_cents` is the sum, over the account's invoices whose `status` is `ISSUED` or `DISPUTED`, of that invoice's `total_cents` less its `amount_paid_cents`. Draft, paid and voided invoices contribute nothing. The **accrual basis** adds two further terms to that figure: the sum of `billed_line_cents` over the account's uplift tickets whose `status` is `POSTED` (tickets already `BILLED` sit inside an issued invoice and would double-count; `VOIDED` tickets are excluded), plus the sum of `reserved_amount_cents` over the account's orders whose `status` is `AUTHORIZED`, `ASSIGNED` or `FUELING`. The result is `accrual_exposure_cents`. For the same account at the same instant the accrual figure is strictly larger than the invoiced figure whenever any posted ticket or open reservation exists, which is why an airline that has never received an invoice can still be refused authorization. Both figures are recomputed on order authorization, order completion, order cancellation, order abort, invoice issuance, invoice voiding and payment, and both are reported side by side when the account is read.

The accrual basis is the one that gates fuelling. The reservation term for a single order is computed once, at authorization time, and is the sum of two components: the requested volume multiplied by the account's `base_price_cents_per_liter` plus the applicable tier differential, rounded half up to whole cents; plus an into-plane handling component that is the larger of 2,500 cents and the requested volume multiplied by 0.45 cents per liter rounded half up to whole cents. The tier differential is selected exactly as it is for a real uplift line, described in §3.6, except that the cumulative volume it is keyed on is the account's month-to-date net liters *plus* this order's `requested_uplift_liters`.

### 3.6. Into-Plane Fuelling: From Order to Uplift Ticket

An into-plane order is the five-way join at the centre of ramp operations, and it may be raised by either an `IntoPlaneAgent` or the `AirlineRep` that owns the flight. The payload names the `flight_id` to be fuelled, the `fuel_authorization_id` being quoted, the `storage_tank_id` the product will be drawn from, a `delivery_mode` of either `HYDRANT` or `TRUCK`, a `requested_uplift_liters` strictly greater than zero expressing the net volume at 15 degrees Celsius the airline wants, a `window_start_utc` and a `window_end_utc` strictly later than the start bounding the booked fuelling window, and a `quoted_authorization_code`. That quoted code must equal the authorization's minted `authorization_code` character for character, and any discrepancy is rejected with `ERR_AUTH_CODE_MISMATCH` — checked both when the order is raised and again when it is authorised. Delivery-mode coherence is likewise checked at creation and again at assignment: `HYDRANT` demands a farm whose `hydrant_system_available` is true, and `TRUCK` demands that no `hydrant_pit_id` be supplied; violations are `ERR_DELIVERY_MODE_MISMATCH`. A new order opens in `REQUESTED` with `fueling_asset_id` and `hydrant_pit_id` null, `reserved_amount_cents` zero, `posted_net_liters` 0.0, `unassign_count` zero and `abort_reason` null, and — importantly — **no reservation is taken against the tank yet.**

Authorising an order, `REQUESTED → AUTHORIZED`, is an `IntoPlaneAgent` action and the single densest gate in the system. The quoted authorization must be `ISSUED` and the logical clock must be strictly before its `expires_at_utc`, or the request fails with `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_NOT_REDEEMABLE`. The quoted code must still match. The airline account's `status` must be `ACTIVE` and the accrual-basis exposure, including this order's freshly computed `reserved_amount_cents`, must not exceed the account's `credit_limit_cents`; failure either way is `ERR_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 402), which is also how a `CREDIT_HOLD` account is refused. The source tank must be `RELEASED` on quality and `IN_SERVICE` on service — anything else is `ERR_TANK_QUARANTINED` — and its `available_volume_liters` must be at least the order's `requested_uplift_liters`, or `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_TANK_VOLUME`. The farm must not be `FROZEN`. On success the order's reservation amount is computed, the requested volume is added to the tank's `reserved_volume_liters` and its `available_volume_liters` drops by the same amount, the account's accrual exposure is recomputed, and the order's, tank's and account's `version` each rise by one.

Assignment, `AUTHORIZED → ASSIGNED`, is where the two operating modes diverge sharply, and the two branches carry equal weight. The payload supplies a `fueling_asset_id` and, for hydrant work, a `hydrant_pit_id`. **For `TRUCK` delivery** the asset's `asset_type` must be `TRUCK`, no `hydrant_pit_id` may be present, and the truck must be able to supply the volume — either its own `onboard_volume_liters` is at least the requested volume, or its source tank's `available_volume_liters` is at least the requested volume; failing both is `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_TANK_VOLUME`, and because `available_volume_liters` is already net of this same order's reservation taken at authorization, a truck order that must draw from the tank rather than from on-board stock needs the tank to have carried roughly twice the requested volume of free product before it was authorised. This supply check is evaluated last, after the scheduling conflicts of §2.10 band 7. **For `HYDRANT` delivery** the asset's `asset_type` must be `HYDRANT_CART`, a `hydrant_pit_id` must be supplied, that pit's `status` must be `AVAILABLE`, and the pit's `stand_code` must equal the flight's `stand_code` — a cart cannot reach a stand the pit does not serve. Both branches require the asset's `status` to be `IN_SERVICE` (otherwise `ERR_ASSET_NOT_SERVICEABLE`) and the farm not to be `FROZEN`.

The two scheduling geometries are deliberately different, and getting them backwards is the classic error. **A hydrant pit is booked on a half-open interval**: the order's window from `window_start_utc` inclusive to `window_end_utc` exclusive must not intersect the window of any other order on the same pit whose `status` is `ASSIGNED` or `FUELING`. Back-to-back windows that merely touch at an endpoint — one ending at exactly the instant the next begins — do **not** intersect and are both permitted; a real overlap is `ERR_PIT_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. **A fuelling asset is booked on a closed interval extended by its turnaround buffer**: the occupied interval runs from `window_start_utc` through `window_end_utc` plus the asset's `turnaround_buffer_minutes`, with both endpoints included, and it must not intersect the correspondingly extended interval of any other order on the same asset whose `status` is `ASSIGNED` or `FUELING`. Here touching endpoints **do** intersect, and the buffer is part of the occupied interval, so a default 15-minute buffer means a quarter of an hour of dead time after every job. A conflict is `ERR_ASSET_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Assignment stores the `fueling_asset_id` and, for hydrant work, the `hydrant_pit_id`, and records the order's pit occupancy for its window — note that the pit's own `status` is *not* changed to `LOCKED_OUT` by an assignment, because lockout means administrative unavailability, not momentary use. The order's and asset's `version` each rise by one, and for hydrant work the pit's `version` also rises.

Assignment is reversible. Unassigning, `ASSIGNED → AUTHORIZED`, clears both the `fueling_asset_id` and the `hydrant_pit_id` to null, raises `unassign_count` by one, and ends the order's pit occupancy for its window — but it deliberately leaves the tank's `reserved_volume_liters` untouched, because the volume reservation belongs to the authorised state, not to the assignment. The order's and asset's `version` each rise by one, and the order may then be re-assigned to a different asset, so the cycle `AUTHORIZED → ASSIGNED → AUTHORIZED` may repeat.

Starting fuelling, `ASSIGNED → FUELING`, is gated on time and fitness. The logical clock must be at or after `window_start_utc` and strictly before `window_end_utc`, or the start is refused with `ERR_OUTSIDE_FUELING_WINDOW`. The asset must be `IN_SERVICE` with its `element_life_pct` strictly below 100.0. The source tank must be `RELEASED` on quality. The flight must still be `SCHEDULED`. And the farm's posture must be `OPEN`, or `RESTRICTED` provided the order's tank is not itself quarantined — a `RESTRICTED` farm still fuels from clean tanks. Starting raises only the order's `version`.

Posting an uplift ticket is the moment product leaves the ledger. The payload carries a `ticket_number` unique across all uplift tickets, a `meter_start_liters` totaliser reading zero or greater, a `meter_end_liters` reading strictly greater than the start, the product `temperature_c` at the wing (at least minus 20 and at most 60), and the mandatory `expected_fueling_order_version` described in §2.9. The ticket's `gross_liters` is the end reading less the start reading, rounded half up to two decimals. Its `net_liters` applies the same volume-correction machinery as a receipt but with two differences: the density band is taken from the **source tank's** `density_kg_per_m3` rather than from any certificate, and no water deduction applies at the wing. Concretely, the coefficient is 0.000850 when the tank's density is at least 800.0, otherwise 0.000900 when it is at least 780.0, otherwise 0.000950; the correction factor is one minus that coefficient multiplied by the wing temperature less 15 degrees, rounded half up to five decimals; and `net_liters` is the gross volume multiplied by that factor, rounded half up to two decimals. A ticket opens in `POSTED` with `voided_reason` and `invoice_id` null, and it copies the order's `storage_tank_id` onto itself so the ledger hop is explicit. Posting is gated on the fuelling window, on the source tank still being `RELEASED`, on the asset still being serviceable, on the farm not being `FROZEN` and having an active permit, on the tank's `book_volume_liters` less the farm's `unpumpable_bottom_liters` being at least the ticket's `net_liters` (`ERR_INSUFFICIENT_TANK_VOLUME`), on the farm's permit-day throughput including this volume staying within `max_daily_throughput_liters` (`ERR_PERMIT_THROUGHPUT_EXCEEDED`), and on the order's `posted_net_liters` after this ticket not exceeding **105 percent** of the authorization's `max_authorized_liters` — a deliberate five percent posting tolerance, breach of which is `ERR_UPLIFT_EXCEEDS_AUTHORIZATION`. Posting debits the tank's book volume, raises the asset's `liters_since_filter_change` and recomputes its `element_life_pct`, recomputes the farm's permit-day throughput and posture, recomputes the account's accrual exposure, and raises the order's `version` by one.

Each posted ticket is also priced immediately, provisionally, using the tiered contract formula. The **cumulative volume** driving the tier is the account's month-to-date net liters: the sum of `net_liters` over that airline account's uplift tickets whose `status` is `POSTED` or `BILLED` and whose `created_at` falls between the first instant of the calendar UTC month containing this ticket's `created_at` and this ticket's own `created_at` inclusive, and it **includes this ticket itself**. Ordering is by `created_at` ascending, ties broken by `created_at` and then by the order in which tickets were posted; no identifier is consulted. Voided tickets are excluded from the cumulative, which means a void can move a later ticket into a cheaper tier. From that cumulative a whole-line differential rate `volume_tier_diff_cents_per_liter` is selected — 1.60 cents per liter once the cumulative reaches 500,000 liters, otherwise 2.40 cents per liter once it reaches 100,000 liters, otherwise 3.20 cents per liter — and the rate applies to the entire line, not marginally. Five components are then computed, each rounded half up to a whole number of cents **before** anything is summed: `fuel_component_cents` is the ticket's net liters multiplied by the sum of the account's `base_price_cents_per_liter` and the selected differential; the into-plane fee is the larger of 2,500 cents and net liters multiplied by 0.45 cents per liter; the flowage fee is net liters multiplied by 0.12 cents per liter; the federal excise is net liters multiplied by 1.14 cents per liter; and the state sales tax is 7.25 percent of the sum of the fuel component and the into-plane fee. When the account's `tax_exempt` flag is true both the excise and the sales tax are zero. The `billed_line_cents` is the sum of those five already-rounded components. Summing the components unrounded and rounding once at the end is explicitly not the rule and gives a different answer. For an `INTO_PLANE_ONLY` account the picture collapses: the account's base price is zero, no differential is applied, `fuel_component_cents` is zero, and `billed_line_cents` is just the into-plane fee plus the flowage fee.

Of that arithmetic the ticket row persists exactly three fields: `volume_tier_diff_cents_per_liter`, `fuel_component_cents` and `billed_line_cents`. The into-plane fee, the flowage fee, the federal excise and the state sales tax are formula intermediates and are **not** stored on the ticket, so nothing reads them back individually. `volume_tier_diff_cents_per_liter` always records the differential the cumulative volume selected — it is the rate the tier lookup returned, reported even for an `INTO_PLANE_ONLY` account whose line does not apply it.

An order closes in one of three ways. **Completion**, `FUELING → COMPLETED`, requires at least one `POSTED` ticket on the order (otherwise `ERR_NO_UPLIFT_RECORDED`) and requires the order's `posted_net_liters` to be at most the authorization's `max_authorized_liters` — with **no tolerance at all** at this gate, in contrast to the five percent allowed when posting. Completion drives the authorization `ISSUED → REDEEMED`; releases the reservation by subtracting `requested_uplift_liters` from the tank's `reserved_volume_liters` and recomputing `available_volume_liters`, the posted tickets having already debited book volume; ends the order's pit occupancy as the order leaves the assigned-or-fuelling set; leaves a truck asset `IN_SERVICE` unless its element life has reached 100.0, in which case the asset moves `IN_SERVICE → FILTER_DUE`; and recomputes the account's accrual exposure, where the reservation term drops out and the uncollected-ticket term enters. The order's, authorization's, tank's, asset's and account's `version` each rise by one. **Abort**, `FUELING → ABORTED`, carries an `abort_reason` of `SPILL`, `QUALITY_HOLD`, `AIRCRAFT_RELEASE` or `EQUIPMENT_FAILURE`, and compensates everything: every `POSTED` ticket on the order becomes `VOIDED` with `voided_reason` `ORDER_ABORTED` and its `net_liters` are credited back to the tank's `book_volume_liters`; the requested volume is subtracted from the tank's `reserved_volume_liters`; for truck delivery the asset's `onboard_volume_liters` recomputes to 0.0 as the voided tickets leave the posted set, so the sum of tank book volume and on-board volume returns to exactly its pre-fuelling value; the authorization is driven `ISSUED → VOIDED` with `voided_reason` `ORDER_CANCELLED`; pit occupancy ends; and the account's accrual exposure returns to its pre-authorization value. Five rows' versions rise by one: the order, the tank, the asset, the authorization and the account. **Cancellation** is available from three states and its effects grow with how far the order had progressed. From `REQUESTED → CANCELLED` only the authorization is voided with reason `ORDER_CANCELLED`, and the order's and authorization's versions rise. From `AUTHORIZED → CANCELLED` the requested volume is additionally subtracted from the tank's `reserved_volume_liters`, availability is restored, the account's accrual exposure returns to its pre-authorization value, and the order's, tank's, authorization's and account's versions rise; when the freeze cascade rather than a person drove this move, the authorization's `voided_reason` is `FARM_FROZEN` instead. From `ASSIGNED → CANCELLED` the pit occupancy additionally ends and `fueling_asset_id` is cleared, the reservation is released, the authorization is voided, exposure is recomputed, and five versions rise: the order, the tank, the hydrant pit, the authorization and the account. All three of `COMPLETED`, `CANCELLED` and `ABORTED` are terminal.

### 3.7. Keeping Fuelling Assets Fit

Filtration is the last barrier between the farm and an aircraft engine, so the system tracks element consumption on three independent legs and always believes the worst of them. The **throughput leg** compares the asset's `liters_since_filter_change` against a nominal element life of four million liters and expresses the result as a percentage, rounded half up to four decimals. The **age leg** takes the whole days elapsed from the asset's `last_filter_change_at_utc` to the current logical clock — floored to whole days, so a partial day does not count — compares that against a nominal element life of one year, taken as 365 days, and expresses the result as a percentage rounded half up to four decimals. The **pressure leg** compares the asset's `differential_pressure_kpa` against a maximum permitted differential of 103.0 kilopascals and expresses that as a percentage rounded half up to four decimals. The asset's `element_life_pct` is the largest of those three percentages. The counter that feeds the throughput leg accumulates the `net_liters` of `POSTED` and `BILLED` tickets metered by the asset since its last filter change, and a ticket that is later voided is subtracted back out. Element life is recomputed whenever a ticket is posted, whenever an order completes, whenever a filter change is recorded, and whenever the logical clock advances.

Reaching 100.0 percent element life moves the asset `IN_SERVICE → FILTER_DUE` automatically — the comparison is inclusive, so exactly 100.0 triggers it — provided the differential pressure leg has not itself passed the 103.0 kilopascal threshold. `FILTER_DUE` blocks any further assignment of that asset to an order, recomputes the farm's `in_service_asset_count`, and recomputes posture, which becomes `RESTRICTED` if the count has fallen to zero. When the differential pressure has reached 103.0 kilopascals the asset skips the advisory state and goes straight to `DOWN_FOR_FILTER`, regardless of what the other two legs say. A `FarmOperator` or `ConsortiumAdmin` may also drive an asset's `status` directly by supplying a `to_status`; moving to `DOWN_FOR_FILTER` from either `FILTER_DUE` or `IN_SERVICE` requires that no order on the asset is currently `ASSIGNED` or `FUELING`. That explicit endpoint admits exactly six moves and no others: `IN_SERVICE → FILTER_DUE`, `IN_SERVICE → DOWN_FOR_FILTER`, `FILTER_DUE → DOWN_FOR_FILTER`, `IN_SERVICE → DOWN_MAINTENANCE`, `FILTER_DUE → DOWN_MAINTENANCE` and `DOWN_MAINTENANCE → IN_SERVICE`. Anything outside that set is `ERR_INVALID_ASSET_STATE` — including a redundant restatement of the status the asset already holds, and including any attempt to leave `DOWN_FOR_FILTER`, which only a filter change can do. No asset status is terminal, so the `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` guard never applies to an asset. The automatic moves described above and the freeze cascade's push to `DOWN_MAINTENANCE` are not routed through this endpoint and are not bound by its set. Every one of these moves recomputes the farm's in-service asset count and posture and raises the asset's and farm's `version` by one each.

Recording a filter change is a `QualityInspector` action, permitted when the asset's `status` is either `DOWN_FOR_FILTER` or `FILTER_DUE` and the farm is not `FROZEN`, and it is what returns the asset to `IN_SERVICE`. The payload names the `element_lot_number`, an `element_count` of at least 1, and a `post_change_differential_pressure_kpa` read after the new elements were fitted. The change record captures the pre-change state for audit: `liters_at_change` is the value of the asset's `liters_since_filter_change` immediately before it was reset, and `differential_pressure_before_kpa` is the value of the asset's `differential_pressure_kpa` immediately before it was overwritten. The record also stamps `changed_by_user_id` from the acting identifier and `changed_at_utc` from the logical clock, and it references the asset it belongs to through its `fueling_asset_id`. As an immutable audit document a filter change record carries no `updated_at` and no `version`. On the asset itself, `liters_since_filter_change` resets to 0.0, `differential_pressure_kpa` is set to the posted post-change reading, `last_filter_change_at_utc` is set to the current logical clock, and `element_life_pct` is recomputed from the reset counters. The same request draws a fresh quality sample with `sample_purpose` `POST_FILTER_CHANGE` in `DRAWN` status against the asset, which is the membrane check on the new elements — its metrics are later posted and judged by the same all-pass verdict logic, with the comparison density taken from the parent tank rather than a receipt certificate. Finally the farm's in-service asset count and posture are recomputed and the asset's and farm's `version` each rise by one, closing the cycle `IN_SERVICE → FILTER_DUE → DOWN_FOR_FILTER → IN_SERVICE`.

A truck's on-board holding is tracked as product that has left the farm ledger but has not yet been closed out. The `onboard_volume_liters` of an asset is the sum of `net_liters` over the uplift tickets whose `status` is `POSTED`, whose parent order is currently `FUELING`, whose parent order's `delivery_mode` is `TRUCK`, and whose parent order names this asset. For a `HYDRANT_CART` the figure is always 0.0, because a cart meters product straight from the loop and holds nothing.

Reliability is the other axis of asset fitness, and it is driven entirely by spill attribution. Every asset starts with a `reliability_score` of 100. When a spill attributed to the asset reaches containment, a penalty is computed and subtracted. The severity component counts the spilled volume in whole 10-litre steps, rounding the number of steps up, and charges 5 points per step, with the severity component itself capped at 40 points **before** anything else happens. A surface multiplier of 2 then applies when the product landed on a `STORM_DRAIN` or on `UNSEALED_GROUND`, and of 1 for an `APRON` or a `TANK_BUND`. The product of severity and multiplier is capped at 40 again **after** the multiplication, and that capped figure is the penalty. The new score is the previous score less the penalty, floored at zero. All arithmetic is on whole numbers. Incidents are applied in `reported_at_utc` ascending order, with ties broken by `created_at` ascending, and only incidents that have actually reached containment are applied — meaning every incident whose `status` is `CONTAINED`, `REMEDIATED` or `CLOSED`, because remediation and closure sit downstream of containment and neither remits nor refunds the penalty, whereas an incident still in `REPORTED` or `ESCALATED` has had no effect on the score yet. Once the score falls **below 60** the asset is driven `IN_SERVICE → DOWN_MAINTENANCE`; that move is also reachable from `FILTER_DUE`, by an explicit status change, and by the farm freeze cascade. Returning `DOWN_MAINTENANCE → IN_SERVICE` is reserved to a `ConsortiumAdmin` and requires that the `reliability_score` has recovered to at least 60, that `element_life_pct` is strictly below 100.0, and that the farm is not `FROZEN`.

### 3.8. Spill Response and the Freeze Cascade

Reporting a release is open to both an `EnvironmentalOfficer` and the `IntoPlaneAgent` who witnessed it. The payload names the `fuel_farm_id` where the release occurred, a `spilled_volume_liters` strictly greater than zero, and the `surface` the product landed on — one of `APRON`, `TANK_BUND`, `UNSEALED_GROUND` or `STORM_DRAIN`. Attribution is optional and may name either a `fueling_asset_id`, a `storage_tank_id`, or neither. The incident opens in `REPORTED` with `contained_volume_liters` and `regulator_reference` null and `reported_at_utc` stamped from the logical clock. Reporting alone recomputes the farm's `open_spill_count` and posture, and an open incident makes the farm `RESTRICTED` — never `FROZEN`. Only escalation freezes a farm.

Escalation and containment are sibling branches out of `REPORTED` with genuinely disjoint effects, and which branch is lawful depends on the volume. **Escalation, `REPORTED → ESCALATED`,** is lawful only when the spilled volume has reached the governing permit's `max_reportable_spill_liters` — the comparison is inclusive, so a spill of exactly the reportable volume must be escalated — and only when a non-empty `regulator_reference` is supplied. A volume below the threshold, or a missing reference, is rejected with `ERR_SPILL_NOT_REPORTABLE`. Escalation fires the depth-three environmental cascade. **Hop one** recomputes the farm's posture, which becomes `FROZEN` because an escalated incident now exists at the farm. **Hop two**, caused by hop one, sweeps the farm: every fuelling order at the farm in `AUTHORIZED` is driven `AUTHORIZED → CANCELLED`, every order in `FUELING` is driven `FUELING → ABORTED` with `abort_reason` `SPILL`, and every fuelling asset at the farm whose `status` is `IN_SERVICE` or `FILTER_DUE` is driven to `DOWN_MAINTENANCE`. **Hop three**, caused by hop two, settles each affected order: its authorization is driven `ISSUED → VOIDED` with `voided_reason` `FARM_FROZEN`, its tank reservation is released, and the owning account's `accrual_exposure_cents` is recomputed; aborted orders additionally void their `POSTED` uplift tickets and credit those tickets' `net_liters` back to the source tank's `book_volume_liters`. The incident's and farm's `version` each rise by one.

**Containment, `REPORTED → CONTAINED`,** is the non-reportable path and skips escalation entirely: it is lawful only when the spilled volume is strictly below the governing permit's `max_reportable_spill_liters`, and it requires a `contained_volume_liters` no greater than the `spilled_volume_liters`. Its effects are correspondingly narrow — the reliability penalty is applied to the attributed asset when a `fueling_asset_id` was recorded, which may itself drive that asset `IN_SERVICE → DOWN_MAINTENANCE`; the farm's `open_spill_count` and posture are recomputed; and the incident's, the farm's and (when attributed) the asset's `version` each rise by one. An escalated incident reaches containment by the same action from a different origin: `ESCALATED → CONTAINED` requires only a `contained_volume_liters` no greater than the spilled volume, applies the same reliability penalty, and recomputes the open spill count and posture. That recomputation is how a farm thaws: the farm leaves `FROZEN` once no escalated incident remains at it and its permit is `ACTIVE` and unexpired, landing on `RESTRICTED` while any incident is still merely `REPORTED`. Assets that the freeze pushed to `DOWN_MAINTENANCE` stay there until an admin returns them individually.

The tail of the chain is short. `CONTAINED → REMEDIATED` requires only that the incident is `CONTAINED` and raises the incident's `version` by one; it touches nothing else. `REMEDIATED → CLOSED` requires that the incident is `REMEDIATED` and, when the `surface` was `STORM_DRAIN`, that a non-empty `regulator_reference` is on file — a drain release cannot be closed without a regulator reference, whereas a release on any other surface can. Closure recomputes the farm's posture and raises the incident's and farm's `version` by one each. `CLOSED` is terminal.

### 3.9. Period Close: Stock Reconciliation and Airline Invoicing

Both of the consortium's period-close activities sweep a half-open window, and both are reserved to different roles for good reason: the operator who measures the stock is not the one who posts the adjustment, and the clerk who bills the airline is not the one who resolved its last dispute.

Reconciliation begins when a `FarmOperator` opens a run over a `period_start_utc` and a `period_end_utc` strictly later than the start. The run opens in `OPEN` with `tank_measurements` an empty array, `receipts_net_liters`, `uplifts_net_liters` and `throughput_liters` at 0.0, `variance_liters`, `variance_pct_throughput_basis`, `variance_pct_opening_stock_basis` and `adjustment_liters` null, and `posted_by_user_id` null. Measurements may not be posted until the logical clock has reached or passed the run's `period_end_utc` — an early attempt is `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_ELAPSED`. The `tank_measurements` payload is an array of objects, each carrying a `storage_tank_id`, a `measured_volume_liters` and a `temperature_c`, and it must contain **exactly one entry for each storage tank at the farm whose `service_status` is `IN_SERVICE` or `ISOLATED`, and no entry for any other tank**. Tanks that are `OUT_OF_SERVICE` are excluded and contribute no variance. A set that is short, long, or contains a stranger is rejected with `ERR_MEASUREMENT_SET_INCOMPLETE`.

The variance computation processes the measured tanks in `created_at` ascending order. For each tank, the measured volume is temperature-corrected using that tank's own `density_kg_per_m3` to select the coefficient by the same three density bands used elsewhere, with the correction factor rounded half up to five decimals and the corrected measured volume rounded half up to two decimals. The per-tank book figure is the tank's `book_volume_liters` as the ledger stands at measure time. The per-tank variance is the corrected measured volume less the book volume, rounded half up to two decimals — a positive number is a gain, a negative number a loss — and the run's `variance_liters` is the sum of those per-tank variances, rounded half up to two decimals. Two movement totals are then gathered over the half-open period: `receipts_net_liters` is the sum of `net_volume_liters` over receipts at the farm whose `status` is `RELEASED` and whose `released_at_utc` falls at or after the period start and strictly before the period end, and `uplifts_net_liters` is the sum of `net_liters` over tickets drawn from the farm's tanks whose `status` is `POSTED` or `BILLED` and whose `created_at` falls in the same half-open window. Their sum, rounded half up to two decimals, is `throughput_liters`, the governing variance denominator. A derived opening stock is also computed as the sum of the measured tanks' book volumes, less the period's released receipt volume, plus the period's uplift volume, rounded half up to two decimals.

Two variance percentages are then reported side by side, each the absolute variance divided by its denominator and multiplied by one hundred, rounded half up to four decimals, and in each case the denominator is taken as the larger of the computed denominator and 1.0 so that a dead-quiet period cannot divide by zero. `variance_pct_throughput_basis` uses the throughput denominator and is the **governing** figure; `variance_pct_opening_stock_basis` uses the derived opening stock and is advisory, reported for the auditor. The distinction has teeth: for a busy farm the opening-stock denominator is far larger than throughput, so the same absolute variance can sit comfortably under tolerance on the advisory basis while breaching it on the governing basis, and conversely a quiet period with little movement makes even a tiny absolute variance breach tolerance. The run auto-transitions `MEASURED → VARIANCE_HOLD` on the same request that computed the variance when the governing percentage is **strictly greater than** 0.50 percent; a run sitting at exactly 0.5000 percent does not hold and posts clean. Measuring also writes `last_gauge_volume_liters` and `last_gauge_at_utc` onto each measured tank and raises the run's `version` and each measured tank's `version` by one; the hold transition raises the run's `version` again.

Posting a run is a `ConsortiumAdmin` action and requires that no storage tank at the farm has `quality_status` `QUARANTINED`. From `MEASURED` it needs nothing further; from `VARIANCE_HOLD` it needs a non-empty `variance_override_reason` in the payload, and posting a held run without one is rejected with `ERR_VARIANCE_OVERRIDE_REQUIRED`. Posting writes the signed per-tank variance into the ledger as that tank's `adjustment_liters`, with the effect that each measured tank's `book_volume_liters` afterwards equals its corrected measured volume exactly. It stamps `posted_by_user_id` from the acting identifier, recomputes each tank's `available_volume_liters` and the farm's posture, and raises the run's `version`, each tank's `version` and the farm's `version` by one. `POSTED` is terminal for a run.

Invoicing starts with a sweep. A `BillingClerk` creates a draft naming the `airline_account_id` to bill, a `period_start_utc`, a `period_end_utc` strictly later than the start, the mandatory `expected_airline_account_version`, and optionally a `replaces_invoice_id` when the draft is a reissue superseding a previously voided invoice. The sweep collects the uplift tickets belonging to that airline account — reached from each ticket's `fueling_order_id` through the order's flight to the account — whose `status` is `POSTED`, whose `invoice_id` is still null, and whose `created_at` falls at or after the period start and strictly before the period end, taken in `created_at` ascending order. Tickets already `BILLED` and tickets `VOIDED` are never swept, which is precisely why voiding an invoice and reissuing it re-sweeps exactly the same tickets. If the sweep finds nothing, the create is rejected with `ERR_NOTHING_TO_SWEEP`. Each swept ticket has its `invoice_id` set to the new draft.

The draft's totals are then computed in four strictly ordered steps, and the order matters because the rebate is keyed on volume while the admin fee is keyed on the post-rebate money total, which makes the two look mutually dependent when they are not. **Step one** sums the swept figures: `swept_net_liters` is the sum of the tickets' `net_liters` rounded half up to two decimals, `fuel_component_cents` is the sum of the tickets' own `fuel_component_cents`, and `subtotal_cents` is the sum of the tickets' `billed_line_cents`, each of which was already rounded to whole cents when the ticket was posted. **Step two** computes the volume rebate, which is keyed on liters and applied to the fuel component only: the rate is 1.5 percent once the swept volume reaches 250,000 liters, otherwise 0.75 percent once it reaches 100,000 liters, otherwise nothing at all, and `volume_rebate_cents` is the fuel component multiplied by that rate, rounded half up to whole cents. **Step three** computes the consortium administration fee on the post-rebate subtotal: `admin_fee_cents` is the smaller of 50,000 cents and four tenths of one percent of the subtotal less the rebate, rounded half up to whole cents. **Step four** sets `total_cents` to the subtotal, less the rebate, plus the admin fee. A draft opens with `status` `DRAFT`, `amount_paid_cents` zero, `dispute_round` zero, and `issued_by_user_id` and `dispute_resolved_by_user_id` null. Creating a draft raises the airline account's `version` by one, which is exactly why a second draft cannot be created from the same stale account version.

Issuing a draft, `DRAFT → ISSUED`, requires a `subtotal_cents` strictly greater than zero — that is, at least one ticket was actually swept — and requires that none of the swept tickets has since become `VOIDED`. Issuance flips every swept ticket from `POSTED` to `BILLED` and freezes its `billed_line_cents`, recomputing the invoice totals one final time in the same four steps; stamps `issued_by_user_id` from the acting identifier; and recomputes both of the account's exposure figures, where the uncollected-ticket term is replaced by the invoiced-invoice term, which additionally carries the admin fee and the rebate. The invoice's `version`, the account's `version` and each swept ticket's `version` each rise by one.

An `AirlineRep` owning the account may dispute an issued invoice, `ISSUED → DISPUTED`, provided the acting identifier is the invoice's `airline_account_id`, the invoice's `amount_paid_cents` is zero, and its `dispute_round` is at most 1 — so an invoice may be disputed at most twice, and a third attempt on an invoice already at a `dispute_round` of 2 is rejected with `ERR_DISPUTE_LIMIT_REACHED`. The dispute raises only the invoice's `version`. Resolution, `DISPUTED → ISSUED`, is a `BillingClerk` action refused when the acting identifier equals the invoice's `issued_by_user_id`; it raises `dispute_round` by one, stamps `dispute_resolved_by_user_id`, and raises the invoice's `version` by one, so the cycle `ISSUED → DISPUTED → ISSUED` is bounded at two rounds.

Payments are recorded against an issued invoice with an `amount_cents` that must be strictly positive and must not push the cumulative `amount_paid_cents` above the invoice's `total_cents`; a violation of either bound is `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_BALANCE`. Once the cumulative paid amount reaches the total the invoice moves `ISSUED → PAID`, both exposure figures fall by the invoice total, and the invoice's and account's `version` each rise by one. `PAID` is terminal. A payment is a resource in its own right, not merely a mutation of the invoice: recording one creates a payment row carrying the `invoice_id` it was applied to, the `amount_cents` recorded, a `received_at_utc` stamped from the logical clock, and the full identity, timestamp and version fields of §2.4 (`id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` and `version` starting at 1 — only the filter-change record is exempt from those last two). Accordingly the endpoint answers with HTTP 201 and returns **the created payment** in `data`, not the updated invoice; a client that needs the invoice's new `amount_paid_cents` or `status` re-reads the invoice.

Voiding is available from both `ISSUED` and `DISPUTED`, and the two branches behave identically: each requires an `amount_paid_cents` of zero, each flips every swept ticket from `BILLED` back to `POSTED` with its `invoice_id` cleared to null so a reissue can sweep it again — the ticket's `voided_reason` of `INVOICE_VOIDED` is recorded only when a ticket is retired for good rather than returned to the unbilled pool — and each recomputes both of the account's exposure figures, with the accrual figure returning to exactly its pre-issue value. In both branches the invoice's `version`, the account's `version` and each swept ticket's `version` rise by one. `VOIDED` is terminal.
