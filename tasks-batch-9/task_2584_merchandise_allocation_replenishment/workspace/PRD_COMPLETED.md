# Merchandise Allocation & Replenishment API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Retail allocation teams live with a permanent tension. Distribution centres receive finite quantities of merchandise, and every unit pushed to one store is a unit denied to another. The team that decides those splits works from store grades, historical sell-through, size curves, shelf capacity and a promotional calendar that keeps moving — and it works under a clock, because a receipt sitting unallocated at the DC earns nothing. Today most of that reasoning happens in spreadsheets that cannot see in-transit or on-order units, cannot tell a broken assortment from a healthy one, and cannot say whether shipping twelve units from one door to another is worth the freight. The consequences are familiar: overstock in the wrong grade, stockouts in the right one, and size-broken sets that quietly stop selling weeks before anyone notices.

This document specifies the backend service that replaces those spreadsheets. The service owns the whole chain: it registers the season's stores and shelf plans, holds the size curves and SKUs, tracks every Store-and-item pairing as a first-class **position** with its own stock and assortment health, books DC receipts, spreads them across stores under a selectable replenishment model and a selectable need basis, authorises and ships the resulting allocation lines, recommends store-to-store rebalancing transfers only when the margin recovered justifies the freight, detects size-outs and broken assortments and routes each one to either a consolidation or a markdown, and finally scores clusters on in-stock rate, sell-through and allocation accuracy. Two principles run through all of it. First, **units are conserved**: every unit received at the DC is either committed to a store or still in the open reserve, never both and never neither, and every compensating operation returns exactly what it took. Second, **nobody authorises their own work**: the operator who builds a spread cannot commit it, and the operator who proposes a transfer cannot approve it.

The API is a JSON-over-HTTP service mounted under `/allocation`, with a single test-driving endpoint for the logical clock mounted outside that prefix. It is a decision system, not merely a record store: most write endpoints run one or more pinned calculations whose rounding placement, ranking keys, tie-breaks and empty-set fallbacks are all observable in the response, and the specification below fixes each of them deliberately.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Operators, Roles and Store Scoping

Every request to the service must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting operator, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that operator is acting in. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the four role names below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` and HTTP 401 before anything else is examined. When both headers are well formed but the named role is not one of the roles authorised for the endpoint (see the Authorized Role(s) column in §4), the rejection is `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` with HTTP 403. Exactly one endpoint relaxes the header requirement: the out-of-prefix logical-clock endpoint of §2.5 accepts a request that carries **neither** header, treating it as the operator `system` acting as `PlanningAdmin`, because that endpoint carries no business data of its own. A clock request that carries either header is held to the ordinary rules — both headers must be present, the role must be recognised, and it must be `PlanningAdmin`.

The service recognises exactly four roles, spelled exactly as follows.

**`PlanningAdmin`** sets up the season. Programs, stores and their status changes, shelf plans, size curves, SKUs and SKU re-ticketing, the promotional calendar, and the logical clock all belong to this role. A `PlanningAdmin` never builds a spread and never authorises one. **`Allocator`** is the analyst: it opens allocation runs against receipts, proposes and revises the spread, and proposes rebalancing transfers. It cannot authorise its own work. **`AllocationManager`** authorises: it commits and recalls runs, releases lines from the DC, approves, rejects, dispatches and cancels transfers, executes or waives assortment remedies, and locks and unlocks the program. **`StoreOperator`** is the receiving store itself; it books physical arrivals and reads only its own store's data. Position creation and adjustment, receipt booking and receipt recounts are shared between `PlanningAdmin` and `Allocator`; run cancellation, the assortment detection sweep and cluster snapshot creation are shared between `Allocator` and `AllocationManager`; all three of those roles may perform every read except the two store-scoped reads described below.

Identity binding differs by role, and it matters. When `X-User-Role` is `PlanningAdmin`, `Allocator` or `AllocationManager`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a transition says so — as `proposed_by_user_id` and `committed_by_user_id` on an allocation run, and as `proposed_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` on a transfer recommendation — and it compares those recorded values against each other for segregation-of-duties purposes, but it never compares them to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `StoreOperator`, by contrast, `X-User-ID` **is** the `id` of the acting `Store`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Store` compare the resource's store-referencing field to `X-User-ID`.

Four operations enforce that store scoping, and all four reject with `ERR_STORE_SCOPE_VIOLATION` and HTTP 403 when the comparison fails. The comparison is only ever made once the target resource has been found, so a scoped operation naming an identifier that resolves to nothing reports that identifier's own not-found error rather than `ERR_STORE_SCOPE_VIOLATION`, even though rung 3 of §2.10 otherwise sits ahead of path resolution. Reading a single store requires that the store's own identifier equal `X-User-ID`. Reading a single position requires that the position's `store_id` equal `X-User-ID`. Confirming physical receipt of an allocation line requires that the line's `store_id` equal `X-User-ID`. Receiving a dispatched transfer requires that the recommendation's `to_store_id` equal `X-User-ID`. A `StoreOperator` has no other capabilities at all: list endpoints, program administration, allocation decisions and transfer decisions are all closed to it, and attempting them yields `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`, followed by exactly 20 characters drawn from the uppercase letters `A`–`Z` and the digits `0`–`9`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. It is the single value in the service taken from the host wall clock rather than from the logical clock of §2.5, so it moves between two calls made at the same logical instant and never equals a stored `created_at` merely because the clock has not been advanced.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position within the result set: it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0, with no upper bound. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and HTTP 400, as is a `limit` or `offset` that is not a base-10 integer.

Results are always returned in ascending `created_at` order unless an endpoint's description in §4 explicitly names a different ordering. Because the logical clock of §2.5 only moves when a client moves it, records created without an intervening clock advance share an identical `created_at`; such ties are broken by creation sequence, so the record created first is listed first, and that ordering is stable across repeated calls. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit` and `offset` echoing the values in effect, `total_count` giving the number of records matching the filters before pagination is applied, and `has_more`, which is true exactly when `offset` plus the number of records in `data` is less than `total_count`. Every filter query parameter documented for a list endpoint is optional and combines with the others conjunctively: supplying `program_id` and `status` together returns only records matching both. A filter that names an identifier no record carries — including an identifier that resolves to nothing at all — simply matches nothing: the response is HTTP 200 with an empty `data` array and `total_count` of 0, never a not-found error. An `offset` past the end of the result set behaves the same way. A filter parameter supplied as an empty string is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and query parameters other than the ones documented for the endpoint are ignored rather than rejected.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Version Numbers

Each stored resource is addressed by an `id`, which is a server-minted UUID carrying no type prefix of any kind — clients never supply it and must not attempt to derive meaning from its shape. Alongside `id`, most resources expose three common properties. `created_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, fixed at creation. `updated_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format, refreshed on every mutation of the resource. `version` is an integer that begins at 1 when the resource is created and increases by exactly 1 on each mutation.

Two resource kinds are deliberately leaner. A size profile is immutable once published, and a cluster performance snapshot is a frozen measurement; neither carries `updated_at` and neither carries `version`. For those two kinds, `id` and `created_at` are the only common properties present, and the absence of `updated_at` and `version` in their payloads is intended rather than an omission. Every other resource — programs, stores, shelf plans, SKUs, positions, receipts, allocation runs, allocation lines, promotional events, transfer recommendations and assortment actions — carries all four.

Version increments are part of the observable contract, not an implementation detail. Whenever a transition or side effect described in §3 says that a resource's version advances, the client can rely on reading a `version` exactly one higher than before, and on `updated_at` having moved to the current logical clock. This matters most where one operation touches several resources: creating an allocation run advances the parent receipt's `version`, and creating a transfer recommendation advances the donor position's `version`, because each of those creations stakes a claim against the parent that other writers must be able to detect.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

The service does not read wall-clock time for business decisions. It maintains a logical clock that, before any client sets it, reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it must never silently fall back to the host clock. An admin-only test-driving capability sets that clock to a supplied `current_utc` instant, which is a required field, is normalised to UTC on the way in, and may move the clock backwards as freely as forwards — a value earlier than the instant currently in effect is never refused, and no timestamp already stamped on a stored resource is rewritten when the clock moves. Shelf-plan effective intervals, promotional event windows, transfer expiry instants, the `detected_at` stamp on assortment actions, the `as_of_date` stamp on cluster snapshots, and the `locked_at` stamp on a program lock are all evaluated and written against this logical clock and nothing else.

### 2.6. Program Gating: What a Lock Freezes, and What It Does Not

Every child resource in the system belongs, directly or through its parent, to an allocation program, and the program's `status` gates writes. A program is created in `PLANNING`, becomes `ACTIVE` once its store list and SKU list are non-empty, may cycle through `LOCKED` and back, and ends in `CLOSED`. Only an `ACTIVE` program admits **decisions**: booking a receipt, correcting a receipt count, opening an allocation run, proposing, revising or committing a spread, creating, approving or dispatching a transfer, running the assortment detection sweep, executing an assortment remedy, and creating a cluster snapshot. Attempting any of those while the program is `PLANNING`, `LOCKED` or `CLOSED` is rejected with `ERR_PROGRAM_LOCKED` and HTTP 409. That list is exhaustive, and the complement matters as much as the list itself: **catalogue maintenance is not a decision and is never gated by program status**. Registering a store, driving a store's lifecycle, publishing a shelf plan, creating a size profile, creating a SKU and patching a SKU all remain permitted whatever the program's `status`, `CLOSED` included, and so does every read endpoint.

A lock is deliberately narrower than a freeze. **Physical logistics are exempt**: releasing an allocation line from the DC, a store confirming physical receipt of a line, and a store receiving a dispatched transfer all remain permitted while the program is `LOCKED`, because goods already in motion must be allowed to land. **Compensating and abandonment steps are exempt too**, on the same reasoning that undoing or walking away from a decision is not itself a decision: closing a receipt, recalling a `COMMITTED` run, cancelling a `DRAFT` or `PROPOSED` run, rejecting a `PROPOSED` recommendation, cancelling an `APPROVED` recommendation, the transfer-expiry sweep of §3.8 and waiving an assortment remedy are all permitted whatever the program's `status`. So are opening and patching a store position and the whole promotional calendar — creating an event and every one of its lifecycle transitions — none of which appears in the decision list above. That asymmetry is the point of having a lock at all rather than simply closing the program.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Creations That Supersede

Two creation endpoints do not merely add a row — they invalidate something the client may not have seen. Both therefore require the client to prove it was looking at a current parent, and both reject a stale view with HTTP 412.

A `POST /allocation/receipts/{receiptId}/allocation-runs` request — opening an allocation run against a receipt — requires `expected_receipt_version` in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of that receipt. This field is not optional. If it does not equal the receipt's current `version`, the creation is rejected with `ERR_STALE_RECEIPT_VERSION` and HTTP 412. The reason is that opening a run legitimately supersedes any `DRAFT` or `PROPOSED` sibling run on the same receipt: a client working from a stale receipt would silently discard a spread it never observed, so the version check converts that hazard into a deterministic refusal.

A `POST /allocation/transfer-recommendations` request — creating a transfer recommendation — requires `expected_from_position_version` in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the **donor** position — the position identified by the donor store and the item being moved. This field is not optional either. If it does not equal that donor position's current `version`, the creation is rejected with `ERR_STALE_POSITION_VERSION` and HTTP 412. Creation supersedes any `PROPOSED` recommendation on the same donor-store, receiving-store and item triple and sizes the move against donor stock, so a client working from a stale donor position would size a move against inventory that has already been claimed or already moved. Both fields are JSON integers of at least 0, and both are checked for shape at rung 4 ahead of everything else: a missing one, a non-integer one or a negative one is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` with HTTP 400 rather than the 412, which only ever answers a well-formed number that fails to match.

No other endpoint in the system takes a version field. Requests to endpoints outside these two must not be expected to supply one, and supplying one has no effect.

### 2.8. Basis Points, Money and Rounding Discipline

Ratios, indices, shares, lifts and rates in this system are carried as integers in **basis points**, where 10000 means exactly one times, or 100 percent. A store demand index of 13000 means 130 percent; a promotional lift of 12500 means 1.25 times; a minimum transfer benefit ratio of 15000 means the benefit must reach 1.50 times the cost. Wherever a basis-point value is the result of a division, it is produced as an integer, and the specification for each calculation in §3 states whether that integer comes from rounding half up or from truncating toward zero — the two are not interchangeable, and several calculations deliberately use one where the other would look plausible.

Monetary amounts — unit costs, retail prices, freight, handling fees and transfer benefits — are carried to exactly 2 decimal places, and every monetary rounding step in this system rounds **half up**. They are serialised in responses as JSON **numbers**, never as strings, after being quantised to two decimals, so an amount of 45.00 is rendered as the number `45.0` and an amount of 12.50 as `12.5`; a monetary field that carries no value is rendered as `null`. Where a monetary calculation has more than one rounding step, the steps are applied at the points the calculation specifies and nowhere else; rounding earlier or later than specified produces a different, wrong answer on many inputs. Unit quantities are always integers and are never rounded away from an exact value: where a quantity split must divide, the specification names either a floor and an explicit remainder-distribution loop or a single round-half-up, and the two are used in different places on purpose.

### 2.9. Unit Conservation

Two conservation invariants must hold after every operation that could disturb them, and both are directly observable.

The **receipt unit ledger** conserves the DC pool. For any receipt, `units_committed` is the sum of `units_allocated` over every allocation line whose owning run has reached `COMMITTED` and whose own `fulfillment_status` is `PENDING`, `SHIPPED` or `RECEIVED`; `units_unallocated` is `units_received` minus that committed total. After every run proposal, commit, recall or cancellation, and after every receipt closure or recount, `units_received` must equal `units_committed` plus `units_unallocated`, and `units_committed` must be at least 0 and at most `units_received`. A recall returns exactly the units its matching commit booked, so any number of commit-and-recall cycles leaves `units_unallocated` at its original value with no drift whatsoever. The same ledger rolls up to the program: `total_units_received` is the sum of `units_received` over the program's receipts whose `status` is `STAGED`, `ALLOCATING`, `ALLOCATED` or `CLOSED`; `total_units_committed` is the sum of `units_allocated` over lines of `COMMITTED` runs whose `fulfillment_status` is `PENDING`, `SHIPPED` or `RECEIVED`; and `open_reserve_units` is `total_units_received` minus `total_units_committed`, representing the DC-side units not yet committed to any store.

The **network on-hand invariant** conserves store-side stock across the transfer lifecycle. For a fixed SKU, take the sum over all of its positions of `on_hand_units` plus `in_transit_units`. Approving a transfer moves donor units from available into reserved and leaves that sum unchanged. Dispatching subtracts the units from the donor's `on_hand_units` and adds them to the receiver's `in_transit_units`, leaving the sum unchanged. Receiving moves them from the receiver's `in_transit_units` into its `on_hand_units`, again unchanged. Cancelling an approved transfer releases the reservation and is likewise neutral. Only the DC-to-store leg changes the sum: releasing an allocation line from the DC raises it by exactly that line's `units_allocated`, because units that were sitting at the DC become in-transit stock in the network, while the store's later confirmation merely converts in-transit units into on-hand units and changes nothing. Independently of all that, for every position the sum of the `units` entries in `size_on_hand` must equal `on_hand_units` after every mutation, with one deliberate exception that follows from the transfer size split of §3.8: because a candidate's floor share in that split is not capped by the donor's on-hand count for that size, and because the donor-side subtraction on dispatch clamps each size at zero, a dispatch whose split assigned a size more units than the donor held of it removes fewer size units than it removes from `on_hand_units` and so leaves the donor's `size_on_hand` summing **above** its `on_hand_units`, and the service does not reconcile the difference.

### 2.10. Path Resolution, Program Cohesion and the Validation Precedence Ladder

Path identifiers are resolved strictly left to right, and the first one that fails to resolve determines the response. The identifiers used in URLs are `programId`, `storeId`, `fixturePlanId`, `sizeProfileId`, `itemId`, `positionId`, `receiptId`, `runId`, `lineId`, `eventId`, `recommendationId`, `actionId` and `snapshotId`, and each has its own 404 error: `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STORE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FIXTURE_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SIZE_PROFILE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_POSITION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RECEIPT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RUN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LINE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROMO_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RECOMMENDATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSORTMENT_ACTION_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_SNAPSHOT_NOT_FOUND`. Foreign keys supplied in a request body resolve against the same catalogue and raise the same error for the referenced entity kind; a `positionId` that resolves to nothing and a transfer whose donor-store-and-item pair has no position both raise `ERR_POSITION_NOT_FOUND`. Failures that are not path resolution at all are answered differently, and the error identifier is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` in each case even though the status varies: a request to a path matching no endpoint of this service returns `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` with HTTP 404, a request to a declared path using a method that path does not offer returns `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` with HTTP 405, and a body the server cannot parse as JSON returns `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` with HTTP 400. Every path is matched with an optional trailing slash, so `/allocation/stores` and `/allocation/stores/` reach the same endpoint.

Resources may not be joined across programs. Every write that references more than one entity must find them all inside the same allocation program: a SKU's `size_profile_id` must name a size profile of the SKU's own program; a position's store and SKU must share a program; a receipt's SKU must belong to the receipt's `program_id`; a transfer's donor store, receiving store and SKU must all belong to its `program_id`; and a promotional event's `store_id` must belong to its `program_id`. A violation is rejected with `ERR_CROSS_PROGRAM_REFERENCE` and HTTP 422. The five writes that carry this check are SKU creation, position creation, receipt creation, transfer creation and promotional-event creation.

All validation in this service is **strictly ordered**. Checks are evaluated in ascending priority and the **first** violated check determines the response; later checks are never reached, so a request that violates three rules always reports the lowest-numbered one. The full ladder is:

1. Headers present and `X-User-Role` recognised — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`, 401.
2. Role authorised for the endpoint — `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`, 403.
3. `StoreOperator` acting within its own store, on the four scoped operations listed in §2.1 — `ERR_STORE_SCOPE_VIOLATION`, 403.
4. Request body and query shape: every required field present, every enum value inside its declared set, every integer inside its declared range, monetary values at 2 decimals, datetimes ISO 8601 UTC, `limit` between 1 and 100 inclusive and `offset` at least 0 — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, 400. This rung is deliberately strict about types and has no coercion anywhere. A field supplied explicitly as `null` is treated exactly as if it had been omitted, so a required field sent as `null` fails as missing and an optional field sent as `null` takes its documented default — the one exception is the SKU patch of §3.2, where an explicit `null` is meaningful. An integer field must be a JSON integer: a numeric string, a floating-point value and a boolean are all rejected. A monetary field may be a JSON number or a numeric string, must be finite, and may carry **at most** two decimal places — fewer is fine and is stored as the same amount at two decimals, more is rejected. A datetime field must be a non-empty ISO 8601 string; a trailing `Z` or `z` is accepted, an explicit numeric offset is accepted and normalised to UTC, and a value with no zone marker at all is read as already being UTC. A string field must be non-empty after trimming, but is stored and echoed back exactly as supplied, whitespace included. Any array-of-sizes field must be an array whose entries are each an object pairing a non-empty `size_code` string with a non-negative integer `units`; an array with no entries is well formed at this rung and is left to the reconciliation rungs below. An absent or empty request body is read as an empty JSON object, so an endpoint whose fields are all optional accepts a request with no body, while a body that parses to anything other than a JSON object — an array, a number, a bare string — is rejected here. Fields that no endpoint documents are ignored.
5. Every path identifier resolves, left to right — the matching not-found error, 404.
6. Every foreign key in the request body resolves — the matching not-found error for the referenced entity, 404.
7. Program cohesion across referenced entities — `ERR_CROSS_PROGRAM_REFERENCE`, 422.
8. The target resource is in a state that permits the requested transition — `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, 409. A commit requires a `PROPOSED` run; a recall requires a `COMMITTED` run; a dispatch requires an `APPROVED` recommendation; a transfer receive requires a `DISPATCHED` recommendation; a line receipt confirmation requires a `SHIPPED` line; an assortment remedy execution requires a `PENDING` action; and a resource in a terminal state accepts nothing at all.
9. The program is `ACTIVE`, for the decision endpoints listed in §2.6 — `ERR_PROGRAM_LOCKED`, 409.
10. The SKU's `lifecycle_state` permits executing an assortment remedy — `ERR_ITEM_LIFECYCLE_BLOCKED`, 409.
11. The supplied parent version matches the parent's current `version`, on the two creations described in §2.7 — `ERR_STALE_RECEIPT_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_POSITION_VERSION`, 412.
12. Segregation of duties on the two authorisation steps — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, 409.
13. Brand presentation minimum on every line of a run being committed — `ERR_PRESENTATION_MIN_NOT_MET`, 409.
14. Fixture capacity on a run commit, a transfer approval or a consolidation execution — `ERR_FIXTURE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, 409.
15. Uniqueness of business keys — `ERR_DUPLICATE_STORE_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_SKU_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_EVENT_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_POSITION` or `ERR_DUPLICATE_PROFILE_CODE`, all 409.
16. Size-curve wellformedness on profile creation — `ERR_SIZE_CURVE_INVALID`, 422.
17. Size arrays reconcile with their unit totals — `ERR_SIZE_UNITS_MISMATCH`, 422.
18. Every size code in a size array exists in the SKU's curve — `ERR_SIZE_CODE_UNKNOWN`, 422.
19. A SKU's merchandise class matches its size profile's class — `ERR_ITEM_CLASS_MISMATCH`, 422.
20. A run's declared method corresponds to the SKU's resolved replenishment model — `ERR_METHOD_MODEL_MISMATCH`, 422.
21. A transfer's donor and receiver differ — `ERR_SAME_STORE_TRANSFER`, 422.
22. Shelf-plan intervals do not overlap — `ERR_FIXTURE_PLAN_OVERLAP`, 409.
23. Promotional windows do not conflict — `ERR_PROMO_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, 409.
24. A recommendation being approved has not expired — `ERR_TRANSFER_EXPIRED`, 409.
25. A shelf plan for the SKU's class is in effect at the store — `ERR_NO_FIXTURE_PLAN_IN_EFFECT`, 422.
26. Store status eligibility for transfers and allocation — `ERR_STORE_NOT_ELIGIBLE`, 409.
27. A store reaching `CLOSED` holds no stock — `ERR_STORE_HAS_INVENTORY`, 409.
28. A run being recalled has no line beyond `PENDING` — `ERR_LINE_ALREADY_SHIPPED`, 409.
29. The transfer size split found at least one candidate size — `ERR_TRANSFER_SIZE_INFEASIBLE`, 422.
30. At least one store survives the proration candidate filter — `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_STORES`, 422.
31. Total network need is above zero and the receipt still has unallocated units — `ERR_ZERO_NETWORK_NEED`, 409.
32. A cluster snapshot has at least one included position — `ERR_NO_CLUSTER_POSITIONS`, 422.
33. The donor position holds enough uncommitted stock — `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_DONOR_UNITS`, 409.
34. Freight worthiness of a rebalancing transfer — `ERR_BENEFIT_RATIO_BELOW_THRESHOLD`, 409.
35. The receipt pool ceiling on both sides of the ledger — `ERR_RECEIPT_UNITS_EXCEEDED`, 409.

Two consequences of this ladder are worth stating explicitly, because they are easy to get backwards. Because the program-active check sits at rung 9 and behind the state check at rung 8, a commit attempted on a `DRAFT` run inside a `LOCKED` program reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, not `ERR_PROGRAM_LOCKED`. And because segregation of duties sits at rung 12, ahead of both the presentation minimum at rung 13 and fixture capacity at rung 14, an operator committing its own proposal always sees `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` even when the spread would also have breached a shelf capacity. A third consequence runs the other way: the numbering above is the general rule, and where a workflow in §3 spells out its own sequence explicitly that sequence governs. The transfer approval of §3.8 is the case that matters — there the expiry check runs **ahead** of segregation of duties, the receiving store's shelf-plan check comes next, and the donor-stock check runs **ahead** of fixture capacity, so an expired recommendation approved by its own proposer reports `ERR_TRANSFER_EXPIRED` and a donor short of stock reports `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_DONOR_UNITS` even when the receiver is also out of headroom.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up a Season: Programs, Stores and Shelf Plans

An allocation season begins when a `PlanningAdmin` registers an **allocation program**, supplying a human-readable `name` such as "FA26 Core Tops", a `season_code` such as "FA26", and a `banner_code` naming the retail banner or division the program serves. Two policy dials may be supplied at the same time, and both have defaults. `transfer_min_ratio_bp` is the minimum benefit-to-landed-cost ratio, in basis points, that a rebalancing transfer must clear before it may be approved; it must be at least 0, and when omitted it defaults to 15000, meaning the recovered margin must reach one and a half times the cost of moving the goods. `scorecard_basis` is the default variant used for cluster scorecards when a snapshot request does not name one, and when omitted it defaults to `UNIT_WEIGHTED`; the only other accepted value is `SIMPLE_AVERAGE`. A new program opens in `PLANNING` with `total_units_received`, `total_units_committed` and `open_reserve_units` all at 0 and with `locked_at` and `lock_reason` both null.

Activation is gated on the program having something to allocate to and something to allocate. Moving a program from `PLANNING` to `ACTIVE` requires that at least one store belonging to the program exists with `status` of `ACTIVE`, and that at least one SKU belonging to the program exists — any SKU counts, whatever its `lifecycle_state`; the program's `version` advances by one. Both failure modes report the same thing: activating a program that is not in `PLANNING`, and activating a `PLANNING` program that has no `ACTIVE` store or no SKU, are each refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` and HTTP 409. Once `ACTIVE`, the program admits receipts, runs and transfers. An `AllocationManager` may take the program `ACTIVE → LOCKED`, supplying a `reason` — a required, non-empty string — that is stored as `lock_reason` while `locked_at` records the current logical clock; the reverse transition `LOCKED → ACTIVE` needs no body at all and clears both fields back to null. Locking is not terminal and may be repeated any number of times.

Closing is terminal, and it is deliberately hard to do by accident. A `PlanningAdmin` may take a program `ACTIVE → CLOSED` or `LOCKED → CLOSED`, but only when no allocation run belonging to any receipt of the program sits in `DRAFT` or `PROPOSED`, and no transfer recommendation of the program sits in `PROPOSED`, `APPROVED` or `DISPATCHED`. In other words, every decision in flight must first be resolved, and a close attempted with any such decision still open — or attempted from `PLANNING` or from `CLOSED` — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` and HTTP 409. When the transition does succeed it cascades: every receipt of the program whose `status` is `STAGED`, `ALLOCATING` or `ALLOCATED` is driven to `CLOSED`, every assortment action whose `status` is `PENDING` and whose SKU belongs to the program is driven to `CANCELLED`, and the program's `version` advances by one. Each receipt and each action the cascade touches also advances its own `version` by exactly one and has its `updated_at` moved to the current logical clock; resources the cascade leaves alone keep the `version` they had. Both closing paths behave identically — the precondition set, the cascade set and the version bump are the same whether the program was `ACTIVE` or `LOCKED` when it closed.

Stores are registered against the program by `PlanningAdmin`, each carrying its owning `program_id`, a `store_code` that must be unique within that program, a `grade` of `A`, `B`, `C` or `D`, a `grade_index_bp` demand index of at least 1, the `cluster_code` the store rolls up to on scorecards, a `freight_flat_usd` amount of at least 0 charged whenever the store acts as a transfer donor, and a `historical_sell_through_bp` trailing-season sell-through between 0 and 10000 inclusive. All seven of those fields are required; none of them has a default. Three of these are load-bearing far from the point of entry. Grade carries a **pinned rank** — `A` ranks 1, `B` ranks 2, `C` ranks 3 and `D` ranks 4 — and that rank is the tie-break when allocation remainders and consolidation targets are ordered. The demand index scales trailing sales under one of the two need bases. The trailing sell-through does double duty: it is the realisation rate applied to recovered margin when a transfer's benefit is computed, and it is the primary ranking key when a consolidation target is chosen. A store is created with `status` of `ACTIVE`. Attempting a second store with a `store_code` already used inside the same program is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_STORE_CODE`.

The store lifecycle is driven by a `PlanningAdmin` supplying a `target_status`, and only four moves are declared. `ACTIVE → REMODEL` excludes the store from every subsequent allocation proration candidate set and from every consolidation-target candidate set, and advances `version` by one; allocation lines already `PENDING` or `SHIPPED` and transfers already `APPROVED` are untouched, because only future decisions change. `REMODEL → ACTIVE` returns the store to eligibility and advances `version` by one — the cycle may be repeated, so a store may re-enter allocation after a remodel. `ACTIVE → CLOSING` excludes the store from allocation proration and bars it from being a transfer **receiver**, while leaving it fully eligible as a transfer **donor**, and advances `version` by one; that asymmetry exists so that a closing door's stock can be swept back into the chain. `CLOSING → CLOSED` is permitted only when every position of the store holds `on_hand_units` of 0, and is otherwise refused with `ERR_STORE_HAS_INVENTORY`; when it succeeds it sets `assortment_status` to `EXITED` on every position of the store, recomputes `broken_store_count` and `size_out_store_count` on every affected SKU and re-derives each of those SKUs' `lifecycle_state`, and advances `version` by one — the store's own `version`, and only the store's: the positions and SKUs whose derived health this closure changes keep the `version` and `updated_at` they already had, because that health is derived rather than stored. The transition is checked as a **pair**: the request is honoured only when the store's current `status` and the supplied `target_status` together form one of the four declared moves, so an attempt to jump from `ACTIVE` straight to `CLOSED`, any move out of `CLOSED`, and equally a request whose `target_status` merely repeats the status the store is already in, are all refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. That state check precedes the stock check, so a `CLOSING` store holding stock asked to go to `REMODEL` reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` rather than `ERR_STORE_HAS_INVENTORY`.

Shelf capacity is expressed as a **shelf plan** published by `PlanningAdmin` for exactly one merchandise class in exactly one store over a bounded interval. Each plan names its `store_id` and its `item_class`, which is one of `APPAREL_TOP`, `APPAREL_BOTTOM`, `FOOTWEAR` or `ACCESSORY`, and describes the physical fixture: `fixture_count` fixtures of at least one, `facings_per_fixture` facings of at least one, and `units_per_facing` units of at least one that physically fit on a facing. From those three the service computes `capacity_units` as the fixture count multiplied by the facings per fixture multiplied by the units per facing. The plan also carries `presentation_min_units`, the minimum number of units the brand considers a presentable set of that class in that store; it must be at least 0 and may not exceed the computed `capacity_units`, and a value above capacity is a request-shape failure reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` with HTTP 400 at rung 4 — that is, ahead of resolving `store_id`, so a request that both names an unknown store and breaches capacity reports the malformed body rather than `ERR_STORE_NOT_FOUND`. The plan's effective interval runs from `effective_from` inclusive to `effective_to` exclusive, and an omitted or null `effective_to` means the plan runs forever; the service does not require `effective_to` to fall after `effective_from`, so a reversed or empty interval is stored exactly as supplied and simply never contains the clock.

Plan intervals are **half-open with no buffer**. Two plans for the same store and the same merchandise class conflict only when their half-open intervals actually intersect; a new plan whose `effective_from` falls exactly on an existing plan's `effective_to` is adjacent, not overlapping, and is accepted with zero gap between them. An overlap is refused with `ERR_FIXTURE_PLAN_OVERLAP`. Downstream, four operations need a plan to be in effect for the SKU's class at the store, meaning the plan whose half-open interval contains the current logical clock — and, should more than one plan for that store and class somehow qualify, the earliest-created one, ties broken by creation sequence: proposing a spread, committing a spread, approving a transfer, and running the assortment detection sweep. If no such plan exists, the operation is refused with `ERR_NO_FIXTURE_PLAN_IN_EFFECT`.

### 3.2. The Merchandise Catalogue: Size Curves, SKUs and Replenishment Models

A **size profile** is the curve along which units are split into sizes, and once published it is immutable. A `PlanningAdmin` creates one against a `program_id` with a `profile_code` label such as "TOP-4SZ", an `item_class` the curve applies to, and a `size_curve`: an ordered array of at least one entry, each entry pairing a `size_code` string with a `share_bp` integer share in basis points. The `size_code` values must be distinct and the `share_bp` values must sum to **exactly** 10000; a repeated size code or any other sum is refused with `ERR_SIZE_CURVE_INVALID`. A `profile_code` already used within the same program is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PROFILE_CODE`. A `size_curve` that is missing, is not an array, is an array of no entries at all, or contains an entry that is not an object pairing a non-empty `size_code` string with a non-negative integer `share_bp`, never reaches the wellformedness test: it fails at rung 4 as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` with HTTP 400.

The profile also fixes what counts as a **CORE size**, through `core_size_min_share_bp`, which defaults to 1500 when omitted. A size is CORE when its `share_bp` is greater than or equal to that threshold — the comparison is inclusive, so a size sitting exactly on the threshold is CORE. Non-core sizes never contribute to broken-assortment detection. Two counts are computed and exposed: `size_count`, the number of entries in the curve, and `core_size_count`, the number of entries whose `share_bp` reaches the threshold, which may legitimately be 0. That degenerate case has a consequence worth internalising: a curve of eight sizes at 1250 basis points each, against the default threshold of 1500, has no CORE sizes at all, so positions of SKUs on that curve can never be detected as size-broken no matter how empty they run. Because a size profile is immutable, it carries only `id` and `created_at` and has neither `updated_at` nor `version`.

SKUs are the allocable unit. A `PlanningAdmin` creates one against a `program_id` with a `sku_code` unique within that program, a `style_code` the SKU rolls up to, an `item_class`, an `item_type`, a `size_profile_id` naming the curve to use, a `unit_cost_usd` landed cost and a `retail_price_usd` ticket price, both at least 0 and both to two decimals. The SKU's `item_class` must equal the referenced profile's `item_class`; a mismatch is refused with `ERR_ITEM_CLASS_MISMATCH`. A duplicate `sku_code` inside the program is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_SKU_CODE`. A new SKU opens with `lifecycle_state` of `ACTIVE` and with `broken_store_count` and `size_out_store_count` both at 0.

The **replenishment model** is what makes a SKU behave one way rather than another, and it is resolved rather than stored directly. `item_type` is one of `BASIC`, `SEASONAL` or `FASHION`. When `replenishment_model_override` is null — its default — the resolved `replenishment_model` follows the type: a `BASIC` SKU resolves to `MIN_MAX`, a `SEASONAL` SKU to `DEMAND_DRIVEN`, and a `FASHION` SKU to `PUSH`. When `replenishment_model_override` is supplied and non-null, that value becomes the resolved `replenishment_model` outright and the SKU's type is disregarded; the accepted override values are `MIN_MAX`, `DEMAND_DRIVEN` and `PUSH`. Resolution runs when the SKU is created, whenever the SKU is patched, and again whenever an allocation run is opened against a receipt for that SKU — that last re-resolution rewrites the stored `replenishment_model` in place and advances neither the SKU's `version` nor its `updated_at`, because opening a run is a write against the receipt and not against the SKU. A `PlanningAdmin` may patch a SKU to change `replenishment_model_override`, to re-ticket `retail_price_usd`, or both; the patch re-runs resolution and advances the SKU's `version` by one. This is the one place where an explicit `null` is meaningful rather than equivalent to omission: sending `replenishment_model_override` as `null` clears the override, so the resolved `replenishment_model` falls back to the one implied by `item_type`, whereas leaving the key out altogether preserves whatever override was already stored. No other field of a SKU may be patched, a patch is accepted whatever the SKU's `lifecycle_state` — the terminal `MARKDOWN` included, since re-ticketing a marked-down SKU is exactly what a markdown is for — and the `version` advances by one even when the body carries neither field.

Because a spread must be built the way the SKU says it should be built, the run's declared method and the SKU's resolved model must correspond one-for-one: `MIN_MAX` pairs with `MIN_MAX_TOPUP`, `DEMAND_DRIVEN` pairs with `DEMAND_DRIVEN`, and `PUSH` pairs with `PUSH_SIZE_PROFILE`. Any other pairing is refused with `ERR_METHOD_MODEL_MISMATCH` when the run is opened. A SKU's `lifecycle_state` moves through `ACTIVE`, `SIZE_OUT_WATCH`, `BROKEN`, `CONSOLIDATION_PENDING` and `MARKDOWN`; how it moves is described in §3.3 and §3.9, and `MARKDOWN` is terminal.

### 3.3. Store Positions: Two Independent Health Dimensions

The pairing of one store with one SKU is materialised as a **store position**, and it is the busiest object in the system: every allocation line lands on one, every transfer moves stock between two, and every health signal is derived from one. Either a `PlanningAdmin` or an `Allocator` opens a position by supplying its `store_id` and `item_id` together with its opening stock and its replenishment policy. Opening stock is given twice and must agree: `on_hand_units` is the total physical count, and `size_on_hand` is an array of entries each pairing a `size_code` with a `units` count. The sum of the `units` entries must equal `on_hand_units` exactly, or the request is refused with `ERR_SIZE_UNITS_MISMATCH`, and every `size_code` used must already exist in the SKU's size curve, or the request is refused with `ERR_SIZE_CODE_UNKNOWN`. Only one position may exist for a given store-and-SKU pair; a second attempt is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_POSITION`. Creation itself runs the full health derivation described later in this subsection, so a brand-new position is created with `broken_size_count`, `stock_status` and `assortment_status` already populated, and it immediately recomputes the SKU's `broken_store_count` and `size_out_store_count` and re-derives the SKU's `lifecycle_state` — a season can therefore open with a SKU already `BROKEN` if its opening size mixes are thin.

Policy is supplied alongside stock. `min_units` is the min-max model floor and must be at least 0; `max_units` is the ceiling and must be greater than or equal to `min_units`, and that comparison is a request-shape failure reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` with HTTP 400 at rung 4 — ahead of resolving `store_id` and `item_id`, so a creation that both names an unknown store and inverts the min-max pair reports the malformed body. `weekly_forecast_units` is the baseline weekly unit forecast, which promotional lift multiplies under one of the two need bases. `trailing_4wk_sales_units` is the units sold in the trailing four weeks, and it is the sole demand source under the other. `on_order_units` records units sitting on an open purchase order that have not yet reached the DC; it defaults to 0 when omitted, and it is visible to exactly one of the two need bases — a distinction §3.6 makes precise. Both quantities must be at least 0. `on_order_units` is the only optional field on the creation: `store_id`, `item_id`, `on_hand_units`, `size_on_hand`, `min_units`, `max_units`, `weekly_forecast_units` and `trailing_4wk_sales_units` are all required and none of them has a default. A position is created with `consolidated_out` set to false. A `PlanningAdmin` or `Allocator` may later patch `min_units`, `max_units`, `weekly_forecast_units`, `trailing_4wk_sales_units` or `on_order_units`; such a patch re-derives the position's stock dimension, leaves its assortment dimension entirely alone because the two are independent, and advances `version` by one. On a patch every one of those five fields is optional, so a body carrying none of them is accepted and still advances `version` by one, and the max-versus-min comparison is applied to the **merged** pair — the supplied value where one was supplied and the stored value where it was omitted — which places it after `positionId` has resolved rather than ahead of it.

Three quantities on a position are never supplied by a client and are always derived. `in_transit_units` is the sum of `units_allocated` over allocation lines destined for this store whose `fulfillment_status` is `SHIPPED` and whose SKU is this position's SKU, plus the `units` of every transfer recommendation for this SKU whose `to_store_id` is this store and whose `status` is `DISPATCHED`. `reserved_out_units` is the sum of `units` over transfer recommendations for this SKU whose `from_store_id` is this store and whose `status` is `APPROVED` or `DISPATCHED` — stock the store still physically holds but has already promised away. `available_units` is `on_hand_units` minus `reserved_out_units`, and it, not the raw on-hand count, is what every downstream decision reads; the subtraction is unguarded, so `available_units` is negative whenever a position's reservations exceed the stock it still holds. All three are recomputed from the current allocation-line and recommendation rows on every read rather than kept as stored counters, so an operation that moves one of them — the DC releasing a line, for instance — leaves the position's own `version` and `updated_at` exactly where they were unless that same operation also writes a stored field of the position.

A position carries **two health dimensions that move completely independently of one another**, and it is essential not to collapse them. The first is `stock_status`, derived purely from `available_units` against the min-max policy, evaluated in this order: when `available_units` is exactly 0 the position is `STOCKOUT`; otherwise, when `available_units` is strictly less than `min_units` it is `LOW` — the comparison is signed and carries no lower guard, so an `available_units` driven **negative** (a donor whose dispatched-but-unreceived transfer still reserves more units than the donor now holds) is `LOW`, not `STOCKOUT`, for every `min_units` of 0 or more; otherwise, when `available_units` is greater than or equal to `min_units` and less than or equal to `max_units` it is `IN_STOCK`; otherwise, `available_units` exceeds `max_units` and the position is `OVERSTOCK`. This dimension never gates the other one, and it is re-derived after every mutation that changes `available_units`.

The second dimension is `assortment_status`, and it rests on `broken_size_count`: the number of the SKU's **CORE** sizes for which the position's `size_on_hand` shows zero units, where a size code entirely absent from `size_on_hand` counts as zero. Non-core sizes are never counted. Evaluation order for the status itself is pinned and the first matching branch wins: if the store's `status` is `CLOSED` **or** the position's `consolidated_out` is true, the position is `EXITED`; otherwise, if `broken_size_count` is 0 the position is `COMPLETE`, which includes the degenerate case of a SKU whose curve has no CORE sizes at all; otherwise, if `broken_size_count` is exactly 1 the position is `SIZE_OUT_RISK`; otherwise `broken_size_count` is 2 or more and the position is `BROKEN`. Because the dimensions are orthogonal, a position may perfectly legitimately be `OVERSTOCK` and `BROKEN` at the same time — drowning in the wrong sizes.

Both dimensions are re-derived after every mutation that changes a position's `size_on_hand`, `on_hand_units`, `reserved_out_units` or `consolidated_out`, and after a store reaches `CLOSED`. The re-derivation then propagates upward to the SKU. `broken_store_count` becomes the number of that SKU's positions whose `assortment_status` is `BROKEN`, and `size_out_store_count` becomes the number whose `assortment_status` is `SIZE_OUT_RISK`. From those two counts the SKU's `lifecycle_state` is re-derived in a **pinned order** that must be applied exactly as written. First, if the SKU is already `MARKDOWN`, it is frozen and nothing further happens — no later branch can move it. Second, if the SKU is `CONSOLIDATION_PENDING`, it returns to `ACTIVE` only when `broken_store_count` has reached 0, and otherwise stays where it is. Third, in every other case: if `broken_store_count` is at least 1 the SKU becomes `BROKEN`; otherwise, if `size_out_store_count` is at least 1 the SKU becomes `SIZE_OUT_WATCH`; otherwise the SKU becomes `ACTIVE`. Both counts are computed on read from the SKU's positions rather than stored, and the re-derived `lifecycle_state`, though it is stored, is rewritten in place: **a propagation that moves a SKU's `lifecycle_state` never advances that SKU's `version` and never moves its `updated_at`**, whichever operation triggered the propagation — position creation or patch, a line confirmation, a transfer dispatch or receipt, or a store closure. Only the SKU patch of §3.2 and the remedy executions of §3.9 advance a SKU's `version`.

That derivation is cyclic on purpose, and every leg of the cycle is reachable. A SKU with no degraded positions sits at `ACTIVE`; when a single position slips to `SIZE_OUT_RISK` while no position is broken, the SKU moves `ACTIVE → SIZE_OUT_WATCH`, and when the last such position recovers it moves `SIZE_OUT_WATCH → ACTIVE`. When any position becomes `BROKEN`, the SKU moves to `BROKEN` — from `ACTIVE` or from `SIZE_OUT_WATCH` alike — and when the broken count returns to 0 with no size-out positions remaining, it moves `BROKEN → ACTIVE`. The remaining two legs, the move into `CONSOLIDATION_PENDING` — reachable from `BROKEN` and equally from `SIZE_OUT_WATCH` — and the return `CONSOLIDATION_PENDING → ACTIVE`, plus the terminal moves into `MARKDOWN` from either `BROKEN` or `SIZE_OUT_WATCH`, belong to the remedy workflow in §3.9. One more knock-on: whenever a re-derivation leaves a position's `assortment_status` at `COMPLETE` — the test is on the resulting status alone, with no comparison against the status the position held beforehand, so a position that was already `COMPLETE` supersedes just as one that has just recovered does — any assortment action still sitting at `PENDING` for that same SKU-and-store pair is driven to `SUPERSEDED` and its `version` advances by one; actions already `EXECUTED` or `CANCELLED` are left untouched.

Reading positions is how the rest of the system is discovered. A single position may be read by any of the three planning roles, and by the owning `StoreOperator` for its own store only. The list view filters on `store_id`, `item_id`, `stock_status` and `assortment_status` — either dimension independently — and is closed to `StoreOperator`.

### 3.4. DC Receipts and the Open Reserve

Merchandise enters the system as a **receipt** booked at the distribution centre by a `PlanningAdmin` or an `Allocator`. A receipt names its owning `program_id` and the `item_id` of the SKU that landed, carries a `reference` string for the ASN or receipt document, a `units_received` count of at least 1, a `size_received` array of `size_code` and `units` entries, and a `receipt_date` giving the instant the units landed at the DC. As with a position's opening stock, the sum of the `units` entries in `size_received` must equal `units_received` exactly or the booking is refused with `ERR_SIZE_UNITS_MISMATCH`, and every `size_code` must exist in the SKU's size curve or it is refused with `ERR_SIZE_CODE_UNKNOWN`. A newly booked receipt opens in `STAGED` with `units_committed` at 0 and `units_unallocated` equal to `units_received`, and the program's `total_units_received` and `open_reserve_units` are recomputed to take it in.

The receipt's own `status` narrates the spread. Opening the first allocation run against a `STAGED` receipt drives it `STAGED → ALLOCATING` and advances its `version` by one; the program must be `ACTIVE` for that to be possible at all. A commit that leaves `units_unallocated` at 0 once the committed lines are booked drives the receipt `ALLOCATING → ALLOCATED` and advances its `version` by one, while a commit that leaves any unallocated units behind keeps the receipt in `ALLOCATING`. Recalling a committed run puts units back into the pool and therefore drives the receipt `ALLOCATED → ALLOCATING`, again advancing `version` — the cycle between those two states may repeat indefinitely. An `AllocationManager` may close a receipt from either `ALLOCATING` or `ALLOCATED`, which abandons any open spread: every allocation run of the receipt whose `status` is `DRAFT` or `PROPOSED` is driven to `CANCELLED`, every allocation line of those runs is driven to `RECALLED` with its `units_allocated` reset to 0, and the receipt's `version` advances by one. Each run and each line the cascade touches advances its own `version` by exactly one as well and has its `updated_at` moved to the current logical clock. A close attempted from `STAGED` is not one of the two declared moves and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. `CLOSED` is terminal for a receipt.

DC counts are wrong often enough that correcting them is a first-class operation. A `PlanningAdmin` or `Allocator` may **recount** a receipt that is still `STAGED` or `ALLOCATING`, supplying a corrected `units_received`, a corrected `size_received` array, and an `adjustment_reason` explaining the change — a recount, a shrinkage write-off, or a mis-ship. All three fields are required, and the corrected `units_received` is held to the same floor of at least 1 as the original booking, so a receipt can never be corrected down to zero units. The two size validations apply exactly as they do at booking. The reason is retained on the receipt but is not part of the receipt payload, so it is not echoed back in the response. The receipt's `status` itself never changes as a result of a recount. What does change is the ledger: `units_unallocated` is re-derived as the corrected `units_received` minus the unchanged `units_committed`, and the program's `total_units_received` and `open_reserve_units` are recomputed. A correction that would leave `units_committed` above the corrected `units_received` is refused outright with `ERR_RECEIPT_UNITS_EXCEEDED` — the ledger may never go negative. On success the receipt's `version` advances by one, which is also what makes a recount visible to the stale-version check on run creation. Note the interaction this creates: a downward recount can strand a spread that has already been proposed, because that spread's lines were sized against the older, larger pool; the correction itself is accepted, and it is the pool ceiling at commit time that ultimately refuses the spread.

### 3.5. The Promotional and Event Calendar

Baseline forecasts are wrong during a promotion, so the program carries a calendar of **promotional events**, each of which multiplies the baseline for the stores and classes it covers. A `PlanningAdmin` creates one against a `program_id` with an `event_code` unique within that program — a duplicate is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_EVENT_CODE` — a `scope`, a window running from `start_date` to `end_date` where the end must not precede the start, and a `lift_factor_bp` multiplier in basis points that must be at least 10000, so an event can amplify a forecast but never suppress it. An event is created in `DRAFT`.

Scope determines both what the event covers and how it competes with its peers. A `scope` of `PROGRAM` covers every store in the program, and both `cluster_code` and `store_id` must be null. A `scope` of `CLUSTER` requires `cluster_code` to be supplied naming the target cluster, and requires `store_id` to be null. A `scope` of `STORE` requires `store_id` to be supplied naming the target store, and requires `cluster_code` to be null. Independently of scope, an optional `item_class` restricts the event to a single merchandise class; leaving it null applies the event to every class. Each of those scope-and-target rules, and equally the requirement that `end_date` not precede `start_date`, is a request-shape failure enforced at rung 4 as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` with HTTP 400 — ahead of resolving `program_id` and `store_id`, so a `STORE`-scope event that also carries a `cluster_code` while naming an unknown store reports the malformed body rather than `ERR_STORE_NOT_FOUND`. Once both resolve, a `STORE`-scope event's `store_id` must name a store of that same program, or the request is refused with `ERR_CROSS_PROGRAM_REFERENCE`; that test precedes the `event_code` uniqueness test, which in turn precedes the window-conflict test. Scope also carries a **pinned specificity rank**: `PROGRAM` ranks 1, `CLUSTER` ranks 2 and `STORE` ranks 3, with the higher rank being the more specific.

Two events may not fight over the same ground. Promotional windows are treated as **closed intervals** — both `start_date` and `end_date` are inside the window — and before any overlap test each window is **expanded by one buffer day on each end**. Two events conflict only when all three of the following hold: they share the same `scope` and the same target, meaning the same `store_id` for `STORE` scope, the same `cluster_code` for `CLUSTER` scope, and program-wide for `PROGRAM` scope; their merchandise classes overlap, meaning their `item_class` values are equal or at least one of them is null; and their buffered windows intersect. Only an incumbent whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE` can be conflicted with — `DRAFT`, `ENDED` and `CANCELLED` events are invisible to the test. The check runs at creation **and** runs again when the event is scheduled, because peers may have been scheduled in between. A conflict is refused with `ERR_PROMO_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.

The event lifecycle is `PlanningAdmin`-driven throughout, and each transition advances the event's `version` by one. `DRAFT → SCHEDULED` re-runs the buffered conflict test against every `SCHEDULED` or `ACTIVE` peer. `SCHEDULED → ACTIVE` is the moment the lift becomes real: only from here does the event's `lift_factor_bp` become visible to need calculation, and a `DRAFT` or `SCHEDULED` event never contributes lift no matter how perfectly its window straddles the clock. `ACTIVE → ENDED` is terminal and stops the event contributing lift to any subsequent calculation. Cancellation is reachable from `DRAFT`, from `SCHEDULED` and from `ACTIVE`, is terminal, and likewise removes the event from any future lift calculation.

Selecting the lift for one store is a **selector policy with a pinned ranking and an explicit fallback**, and it runs once per candidate store while need is being computed under the forward-looking basis. The candidate set is every promotional event of the SKU's program whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, whose window contains the current logical clock — inclusive at both `start_date` and `end_date` — whose `item_class` either equals the SKU's `item_class` or is null, and whose scope matches the store: a `PROGRAM`-scope event always matches, a `CLUSTER`-scope event matches when its `cluster_code` equals the store's `cluster_code`, and a `STORE`-scope event matches when its `store_id` is the store itself. Candidates are then ordered by three keys applied strictly in sequence: specificity rank descending first, so a `STORE`-scope event outranks a `CLUSTER`-scope event which outranks a `PROGRAM`-scope event; then `lift_factor_bp` descending; and finally `created_at` ascending as the last tie-break, with an identical `created_at` broken by creation sequence so that the event created first is selected. **Exactly one** event is selected — the first in that order — and its `lift_factor_bp` is the lift in effect; lifts never compound, never sum and never multiply together. If the candidate set is empty, the lift in effect is 10000 basis points, which is to say the baseline passes through unchanged. Specificity outranks magnitude, and that is the intended behaviour: a store-scoped event at 11000 basis points beats a cluster-scoped event at 20000 basis points for the stores it covers.

### 3.6. Building a Spread: Need, Proration and the Size Breakdown

An **allocation run** is one attempt at spreading one receipt across the network. An `Allocator` opens a run against a receipt by declaring the `method` to be used — `MIN_MAX_TOPUP`, `DEMAND_DRIVEN` or `PUSH_SIZE_PROFILE`, which must correspond to the SKU's resolved replenishment model as described in §3.2 — and the `need_basis` to be used, either `FORWARD_LOOKING` or `HISTORICAL_SELL_THROUGH`, alongside the `expected_receipt_version` demanded by §2.7. The run opens in `DRAFT`, records the acting `X-User-ID` as `proposed_by_user_id`, and advances the receipt's `version` by one. If any sibling run on the same receipt is sitting in `DRAFT` or `PROPOSED`, that sibling is driven to `CANCELLED` and its own `version` advances by one, the new run's `supersedes_run_id` is set to the superseded sibling's identifier, and every allocation line of the superseded run is driven to `RECALLED` with its `units_allocated` reset to 0 and its `version` advanced by one. The consequence is a useful invariant: **a receipt carries at most one non-terminal run at any moment**, because opening a new one always retires the old one. Opening the first run against a `STAGED` receipt also drives the receipt into `ALLOCATING`; a receipt already in `ALLOCATING` or `ALLOCATED` keeps the status it has, and the receipt's `version` advances on every run creation either way. Only a `CLOSED` receipt refuses the creation, with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, and that state check runs ahead of both the program gate and the `expected_receipt_version` match.

Proposing the run takes it `DRAFT → PROPOSED` and is where all the arithmetic happens. Its preconditions are guarded in a fixed order. The program must be `ACTIVE`, or the request is refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_LOCKED`. Next, at least one store must survive the candidate filter, or the request is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_STORES` — and when the filter admits nobody at all, no shelf plan is ever looked for. Every store that did survive must then have a shelf plan in effect for the SKU's class, or the request is refused with `ERR_NO_FIXTURE_PLAN_IN_EFFECT`; the plans are resolved for the whole surviving set before any need is computed, so a missing plan surfaces ahead of both of the emptiness refusals that follow. Only then is need computed per store: at least one surviving store must come out with a need above zero, or the refusal is again `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_STORES`. And last, the receipt must still have unallocated units and the total need across that set must be above zero, or the request is refused with `ERR_ZERO_NETWORK_NEED` — which means an exhausted pool is reported as `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_STORES` whenever no store also has a need.

The **candidate filter** is narrow and worth stating plainly. A store enters the candidate set only when its `status` is `ACTIVE` — a `REMODEL`, `CLOSING` or `CLOSED` store never does — only when a store position already exists for that store and the receipt's SKU, and only when the SKU's `lifecycle_state` is anything other than `MARKDOWN`, since a marked-down SKU is permanently out of allocation. For each surviving store the shelf plan used is the one whose `item_class` equals the SKU's `item_class` and whose half-open effective interval contains the current logical clock; if the store has no such plan the proposal is refused with `ERR_NO_FIXTURE_PLAN_IN_EFFECT`.

**Need** is computed once per candidate store, and it is always the target for that store less what is already coming to it, floored at zero: `need_units` is the greater of zero and the target minus the pipeline. The target depends on the run's `method`, while the pipeline depends on the run's `need_basis` **alone** and is assembled identically for every method — a `MIN_MAX_TOPUP` and a `PUSH_SIZE_PROFILE` run see `on_order_units` under `FORWARD_LOOKING` and ignore them under `HISTORICAL_SELL_THROUGH` exactly as a `DEMAND_DRIVEN` run does. Under `MIN_MAX_TOPUP` the target is simply the position's `max_units`, which makes it demand-independent — the promotional calendar and the trailing sales history are both irrelevant to a min-max top-up. Under `PUSH_SIZE_PROFILE` the target is the in-effect shelf plan's `capacity_units` multiplied by 80 percent, truncated down to a whole number of units; the push fill rate is fixed at 8000 basis points. Under `DEMAND_DRIVEN` the target is built from a weekly demand figure carried out to a **cover horizon of four weeks**, and both the demand figure and the placement of its rounding depend on the run's `need_basis`.

Under a `need_basis` of `FORWARD_LOOKING`, weekly demand is the position's `weekly_forecast_units` multiplied by the lift in effect for that store from §3.5 expressed as a fraction of 10000, **rounded half up to a whole number of units first**, and only then multiplied by the four-week cover horizon to give the demand-driven target. The pipeline under this basis is the position's `available_units` plus its `in_transit_units` plus its `on_order_units` — units still at the vendor **are** visible to this basis, because a forward-looking view of supply should not double-order what is already on a purchase order.

Under a `need_basis` of `HISTORICAL_SELL_THROUGH`, weekly demand is the position's `trailing_4wk_sales_units` scaled by the store's `grade_index_bp` as a fraction of 10000 and then divided by four to convert four weeks of sales into one week, and that figure is kept as an **exact unrounded value**. It is multiplied by the four-week cover horizon and only that final product is rounded half up to a whole number of units, giving the demand-driven target under a **single** rounding step applied after the cover multiply rather than before it. The pipeline under this basis is the position's `available_units` plus its `in_transit_units` and **nothing else** — `on_order_units` are deliberately ignored, because this basis trusts realised sales and stock already committed to the store, not vendor promises. Promotional lift is never applied under this basis at all; the store's `grade_index_bp` takes its place as the demand modifier.

Once every candidate store has a need, the receipt pool is spread by a **four-step waterfall** whose steps run strictly in order. The pool is the receipt's `units_unallocated`. The proration candidate set is the candidate stores whose `need_units` is greater than zero; if that set is empty the proposal is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_STORES`, and if the total need summed across the set is zero the proposal is refused with `ERR_ZERO_NETWORK_NEED`. **Step one** gives each store a proportional floor share: the pool multiplied by that store's need, divided by the total need across the set, truncated down to a whole number of units. Truncation rather than rounding is deliberate here — the remainder loop in step four is the only mechanism by which a store gains a unit above its floor share. **Step two** applies the need ceiling, taking the lesser of the floor share and the store's own need. **Step three** applies the fixture capacity ceiling: the store's headroom is the greater of zero and the in-effect shelf plan's `capacity_units` less the position's `available_units` less its `in_transit_units`, and the store's allocation becomes the lesser of its step-two figure and that headroom. The units lost specifically to capacity at this step — the step-two figure minus the step-three figure — are recorded on the line as `capacity_capped_units`, so an allocator can see why a store was short-changed.

**Step four** distributes what remains one unit at a time. The remaining pool is the original pool less the sum of the step-three allocations. While units remain, the eligible set is every store whose current allocation is still strictly below the lesser of its need and its headroom; if that set is empty the loop stops immediately and the leftover units stay unplaced. Otherwise the set is ordered by three keys applied strictly in sequence: residual need descending, where residual need is the store's need less its current allocation; then grade rank ascending, so an `A` store beats a `B` store beats a `C` store beats a `D` store; and finally `store_code` ascending in lexicographic order. The first store in that order receives one unit, the remaining pool drops by one, and the loop re-evaluates from scratch. Because the eligible test caps each store at the lesser of its need and its headroom, capacity can never be breached by the remainder loop even for a store that step three had already clipped. Whatever is still unplaced when the loop finishes becomes the run's `unallocated_remainder_units` — units the proration could not place because every eligible store had hit either its need or its capacity ceiling.

An allocation line is materialised for each store whose final allocation is greater than zero; **a store allocated zero units gets no line at all**. The lines are created in the candidate order, which is the order the surviving stores were registered in the program, and because they all share the proposal's `created_at` that same order is what a later list of the run's lines returns. Each line records its owning `run_id` and destination `store_id`, the `need_units` computed for that store, the `units_proposed` the waterfall placed there, the `capacity_capped_units` from step three, and a `presentation_gap_units` figure computed as the greater of zero and the store's in-effect `presentation_min_units` less the sum of the position's `available_units`, its `in_transit_units` and the line's `units_proposed`. That gap is **recorded at proposal time but not enforced there** — a proposal may legally sit below a presentation minimum, and it is the commit that refuses it. A line's `units_allocated` is 0 while the run is merely `PROPOSED`, becomes equal to its `units_proposed` once the run is `COMMITTED`, and returns to 0 if the run is later recalled. Every line opens with `fulfillment_status` of `PENDING`.

Each line also carries a `size_breakdown`, an array of `size_code` and `units` entries produced by splitting the line's `units_proposed` along the SKU's size curve. The split gives each size a floor share — the line's proposed units multiplied by that size's `share_bp`, divided by 10000, truncated down to a whole number — and then distributes the leftover units, being the proposed units less the sum of the floor shares, one at a time in passes. Within a pass, sizes are ordered by `share_bp` descending and then by `size_code` ascending lexicographically, and **each size may receive at most one leftover unit per pass**; passes repeat until nothing is left. The sum of the `units` entries in `size_breakdown` must equal the line's `units_proposed` exactly. **Every size of the curve appears in `size_breakdown`, listed in the curve's own order, including a size that ends the split at zero units** — the array is the whole curve and is never filtered down to the sizes that received something. Note that this split follows the size curve **only** and is deliberately not constrained by the receipt's `size_received` mix, because the DC substitutes sizes at pick time.

Proposing also sets three roll-ups on the run itself: `units_proposed` is the sum of the lines' proposed units and is 0 while the run is `DRAFT`; `eligible_store_count` is the number of stores that entered the proration candidate set at proposal time; and `units_committed` is the sum of the lines' allocated units, which stays 0 unless the run has reached `COMMITTED`. The run's `version` advances by one.

An `Allocator` may take a `PROPOSED` run back to `DRAFT` to change its inputs and try again — the program must be `ACTIVE` or the request is refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_LOCKED`. Revising **deletes every allocation line of the run** and resets `units_proposed`, `unallocated_remainder_units` and `eligible_store_count` to 0, then advances the run's `version` by one. The propose-and-revise cycle may loop any number of times while the receipt remains in `ALLOCATING`. Alternatively the spread may be abandoned entirely: either an `Allocator` or an `AllocationManager` may cancel a run from `DRAFT` or from `PROPOSED`, which is terminal and books nothing. Cancelling a `DRAFT` run has no effect beyond advancing its `version`; cancelling a `PROPOSED` run additionally sets `fulfillment_status` to `RECALLED` on every one of its lines, each of those lines advancing its own `version` by one while its `units_allocated` — already 0 on a run that was never committed — is left exactly where it is, before advancing `version`.

Every run exposes the `receipt_id` of the receipt it spreads, and the filtered list view over runs accepts `receipt_id`, `supersedes_run_id` and `status` as filters — which is how the supersede chain on a busy receipt is reconstructed after the fact.

### 3.7. Authorising, Releasing and Landing a Spread

Committing is the moment a proposal becomes a commitment against the DC ledger, and it belongs exclusively to an `AllocationManager`. Five conditions are checked, and because the ladder in §2.10 is strict, they surface **in this order**. First the program must be `ACTIVE`, or the request is refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_LOCKED`. Second the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the run's `proposed_by_user_id`, or the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — an operator may never commit its own spread, and this check precedes the two merchandising checks so that a self-commit is reported as such even when the spread would also have broken a shelf rule. Third, every line of the run must satisfy the brand presentation standard: for each line, the destination position's `available_units` plus its `in_transit_units` plus the line's `units_proposed` must be greater than or equal to the store's in-effect `presentation_min_units` for the SKU's class, and any line that falls short refuses the whole commit with `ERR_PRESENTATION_MIN_NOT_MET`. No store may be left below a presentable set. Fourth, every line must satisfy fixture capacity: the same three quantities summed must be less than or equal to the in-effect shelf plan's `capacity_units`, and a breach refuses the commit with `ERR_FIXTURE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Both of those merchandising tests silently skip any line whose destination store has no shelf plan in effect for the SKU's class at all, and likewise any line whose destination position has meanwhile ceased to exist — such a line blocks nothing and is simply passed over: the missing plan is reported as `ERR_NO_FIXTURE_PLAN_IN_EFFECT` only once every line that does have a plan has cleared both tests, so on a run whose stores are a mixture the merchandising breach surfaces first and the missing plan only afterwards. Fifth, the total across the run of `units_proposed` must not exceed the receipt's current `units_unallocated`, or the commit is refused with `ERR_RECEIPT_UNITS_EXCEEDED` — this is the rung that catches a spread stranded by a downward recount.

When all five pass, the run moves `PROPOSED → COMMITTED` and the cascade is broad. Every line's `units_allocated` is set equal to its `units_proposed` and its `fulfillment_status` is set to `PENDING`, and each line advances its own `version` by one. The run's `committed_by_user_id` is set to the acting `X-User-ID`. The receipt's `units_committed` and `units_unallocated` are recomputed, and if `units_unallocated` reaches 0 the receipt is driven `ALLOCATING → ALLOCATED`; because those two ledger figures are derived rather than stored, the receipt's `version` advances **only** when that status transition actually happens and is left exactly where it was by a commit that leaves units unallocated behind. The program's `total_units_committed` and `open_reserve_units` are recomputed. The run's `version` advances by one.

Recalling reverses a commit exactly, and it is the compensating operation the ledger relies on. An `AllocationManager` may recall a `COMMITTED` run only while **every** one of its lines is still `PENDING`; if even one line has already been released from the DC or landed at a store, the recall is refused with `ERR_LINE_ALREADY_SHIPPED`. On success the run moves `COMMITTED → RECALLED`, which is terminal, and every line has its `units_allocated` reset to 0, its `fulfillment_status` set to `RECALLED` and its own `version` advanced by one. The receipt's `units_committed` and `units_unallocated` are recomputed and the receipt is driven `ALLOCATED → ALLOCATING` if it was `ALLOCATED` — and, exactly as on commit, the receipt's `version` advances only on that status change and not merely because the derived ledger moved; the program's `total_units_committed` and `open_reserve_units` are recomputed; and the run's `version` advances by one. Because a recall returns exactly the units its commit booked, repeated commit-and-recall cycles leave the receipt's `units_unallocated` where it started with zero drift.

Physical movement begins when an `AllocationManager` releases a line from the DC, taking it `PENDING → SHIPPED`. Two conditions apply: the owning run must be in `COMMITTED` — a line whose run is not committed cannot move, and the refusal is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` — and the line's `units_allocated` must be greater than zero, a line carrying zero allocated units being refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` as well, checked after the line's own `PENDING` status and its run's `COMMITTED` status. The release adds the line's `units_allocated` to the destination position's `in_transit_units` and re-derives that position's `stock_status` — a movement that follows from the line's new `SHIPPED` status rather than from any write to the position, so the position's own `version` and `updated_at` do not move and the line is the only resource this transition touches; the line's `version` advances by one. This is one of the three operations explicitly permitted while the program is `LOCKED`, because a lock freezes decisions and not trucks.

Landing the goods belongs to the store alone. A `StoreOperator` confirms physical receipt of a line, taking it `SHIPPED → RECEIVED`, and the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the line's `store_id` or the request is refused with `ERR_STORE_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. Confirmation runs the deepest cascade on the inbound side: the line's `units_allocated` are added to the position's `on_hand_units` and the line's `size_breakdown` is merged into the position's `size_on_hand` — merging means each entry of more than zero units is added to the matching `size_on_hand` entry, or appended after the existing entries when that size code is not yet present, while **an entry of zero units contributes nothing and never creates a zero-unit entry**; the same quantity is removed from the position's `in_transit_units`; if `on_hand_units` becomes greater than zero, the position's `consolidated_out` flag is cleared back to false; the position's `broken_size_count`, `stock_status` and `assortment_status` are all re-derived; the SKU's `broken_store_count` and `size_out_store_count` are recomputed and its `lifecycle_state` is re-derived under the pinned order in §3.3; the destination position's own `version` advances by one, because this is one of the transitions that writes its stored `on_hand_units` and `size_on_hand`, while the SKU's `version` stays where it was; and the line's `version` advances by one. If the destination position has meanwhile ceased to exist the confirmation still succeeds: the line reaches `RECEIVED` and advances its `version`, and the entire inbound cascade above is simply skipped rather than being refused. `RECEIVED` is terminal for a line, as is `RECALLED`.

Allocation lines are created by the proposal, never by a client, so the filtered list view is the only way to discover them: it filters on `run_id`, on `store_id` and on `fulfillment_status`, and a single line may also be read directly for its size breakdown and its capacity and presentation diagnostics.

### 3.8. Rebalancing Between Stores: Freight Economics and the Transfer Lifecycle

Allocation is never perfect, so the network needs a way to move stock sideways — out of a door drowning in units and into one that is out of them. That instrument is the **transfer recommendation**, proposed by an `Allocator` who supplies the owning `program_id`, the donor `from_store_id`, the receiving `to_store_id`, the `item_id` being moved, a `units` count of at least 1, and the `expected_from_position_version` demanded by §2.7. **Both** ends must already have a store position for the SKU: the donor's is the one the version check is made against, and a receiving store with no position for that SKU is refused with `ERR_POSITION_NOT_FOUND` exactly as a donor without one would be. Those two position lookups happen **after** the program gate, so a creation attempted while the program is not `ACTIVE` reports `ERR_PROGRAM_LOCKED` even when neither position exists. Donor and receiver must differ, or the request is refused with `ERR_SAME_STORE_TRANSFER`. The receiver must be an `ACTIVE` store at creation time — a `REMODEL`, `CLOSING` or `CLOSED` store may not receive — and the refusal is `ERR_STORE_NOT_ELIGIBLE`; the donor's own `status` is never examined at all, so a `REMODEL` or `CLOSING` store may donate freely and even a `CLOSED` one is never refused on status grounds — it simply has no stock left to clear the donor-stock test. The donor position must hold enough uncommitted stock, meaning its `available_units` must be at least the requested `units`, or the request is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_DONOR_UNITS`. A recommendation created this way opens in `PROPOSED` with `source_reason` of `REBALANCE`, records the acting `X-User-ID` as `proposed_by_user_id`, and advances the donor position's `version` by one to record the new claim staked against it. If any recommendation on the identical donor-store, receiving-store and SKU triple is already sitting in `PROPOSED`, that peer is driven to `CANCELLED` with its `reject_reason` set to the string `superseded`, its `version` advances by one, and the new recommendation's `supersedes_recommendation_id` points at it.

Creation also freezes the economics, and none of the four figures below is ever recomputed afterwards. The **size split** comes first, as an array of `size_code` and `units` entries stored as `size_split`, and it answers a different question depending on `source_reason`. For a `REBALANCE` transfer the goal is to fill holes: the candidate sizes are the SKU's **CORE** sizes for which the receiving position shows zero units on hand and the donor position shows more than zero, and each candidate's weight is its `share_bp` from the size curve. For a `CONSOLIDATION` transfer, described in §3.9, the goal is to sweep a door out: the candidate sizes are every size the donor holds more than zero units of, whatever those sizes happen to be, and each candidate's weight is the donor's own on-hand unit count for that size. In either mode, if the candidate set is empty the operation is refused with `ERR_TRANSFER_SIZE_INFEASIBLE`. The split itself gives each candidate a floor share — the transfer's `units` multiplied by that candidate's weight, divided by the sum of all candidate weights, truncated down to a whole number — and then distributes the leftover units, being the transfer's `units` less the sum of the floor shares, one at a time, ordering candidates by weight descending and then by `size_code` ascending, and capping every size at the donor's on-hand units for that size. That cap governs the **leftover distribution only**: a floor share is never trimmed to the donor's holding, so a narrow candidate set — one core size the receiver is missing, say — can be assigned more units of that size than the donor physically has, which is the exception §2.9 records against the size-array invariant. The leftover loop is also not a pass loop: each remaining unit goes to the **first** candidate in that order still below the donor's on-hand count for it, and the loop then restarts from the top of the order, so one size may absorb several leftover units in succession; if no candidate is still below its cap the loop stops and the split is left short. `size_split` therefore lists only candidate sizes that ended with more than zero units, ordered as they appear in the SKU's size curve — unlike a line's `size_breakdown` it is never padded out to the whole curve — and its `units` entries sum to the transfer's `units` whenever the donor's candidate holdings allow.

**Landed cost** comes second, in three pinned steps whose order is directly observable. Step one is freight: the donor store's `freight_flat_usd` plus 85 cents per unit moved, that sum rounded half up to two decimals, and the result taken as the greater of itself and a floor of 45 dollars — so a tiny transfer from a cheap donor still costs 45.00 — and stored as `freight_cost_usd`. Step two is the handling fee, whose rate tier is selected **on the freight figure and never on the landed figure**: a `freight_cost_usd` below 50.00 attracts 1200 basis points, a figure from 50.00 inclusive up to but not including 150.00 attracts 900 basis points, and a figure of 150.00 or above attracts 600 basis points. The chosen rate is applied to `freight_cost_usd` as a fraction of 10000 and rounded half up to two decimals, giving `handling_fee_usd`. Step three is `landed_cost_usd`, the exact sum of the two-decimal `freight_cost_usd` and the two-decimal `handling_fee_usd`, with no further rounding. Keying the tier on landed cost instead of freight is the classic wrong answer: a freight of 45.00 lands at 50.40, which would flip the tier and change the fee.

**Benefit** comes third and accrues where the units land. The unit margin is the SKU's `retail_price_usd` multiplied by a net margin of 5500 basis points, kept exact and unrounded. That margin is then multiplied by the **receiving** store's `historical_sell_through_bp` as a fraction of 10000 — the donor's sell-through is never used — and again kept exact and unrounded, giving a realised per-unit margin. Rounding happens **per size line**: for each entry of `size_split`, that entry's `units` multiplied by the realised per-unit margin is rounded half up to two decimals, and `benefit_usd` is the sum of those rounded per-size figures. Rounding the total once instead of once per size line differs by a cent on many inputs, and the per-line order is the specified behaviour. Fourth and last, `benefit_ratio_bp` is `benefit_usd` divided by `landed_cost_usd`, multiplied by 10000, rounded half up to a whole number of basis points.

Creation also stamps `expires_at` as the current logical clock plus a transfer time-to-live of seven days. A `PROPOSED` recommendation may be approved only while the logical clock is **strictly before** `expires_at`; at or past that instant it is no longer approvable and an approval attempt is refused with `ERR_TRANSFER_EXPIRED`. An `AllocationManager` may also sweep the program, which drives every `PROPOSED` recommendation whose `expires_at` is at or before the logical clock to `EXPIRED` and advances each one's `version` by one; `APPROVED` and `DISPATCHED` recommendations are never expired by that sweep. The sweep names its target in the request body as a required `program_id`, and its response is an array of exactly the recommendations it has just expired, in ascending `created_at` order, carrying no pagination block in `meta` and coming back empty when nothing was due.

Approval is an `AllocationManager` step and enforces its conditions in exactly the order given here, which is the order §2.10's ladder yields to: the recommendation must be `PROPOSED` or the refusal is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`; the program must be `ACTIVE` or the refusal is `ERR_PROGRAM_LOCKED`; the clock must be strictly before `expires_at` or the refusal is `ERR_TRANSFER_EXPIRED`; the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `proposed_by_user_id` or the refusal is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; both the donor and the receiving position must still resolve, or the refusal is `ERR_POSITION_NOT_FOUND`, and the receiving store must have a shelf plan in effect for the SKU's class — the very plan the capacity condition below reads — or the refusal is `ERR_NO_FIXTURE_PLAN_IN_EFFECT`; the freight-worthiness gate must pass; the donor position's `available_units` must still be at least the transfer's `units` or the refusal is `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_DONOR_UNITS`; and the receiving position's headroom, being the greater of zero and its in-effect `capacity_units` less its `available_units` less its `in_transit_units`, must be at least the transfer's `units` or the refusal is `ERR_FIXTURE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. The **freight-worthiness gate** is conditional on `source_reason`: a `CONSOLIDATION` transfer skips it entirely, because a mandated sweep-up is exempt by design, while a `REBALANCE` transfer requires `benefit_ratio_bp` to be greater than or equal to the program's `transfer_min_ratio_bp`, with equality passing, and otherwise is refused with `ERR_BENEFIT_RATIO_BELOW_THRESHOLD`. On success the recommendation moves `PROPOSED → APPROVED`, `approved_by_user_id` is set to the acting `X-User-ID`, the transfer's `units` are added to the donor position's `reserved_out_units` — which lowers that position's `available_units` and re-derives its `stock_status` — and the recommendation's `version` advances by one. That reservation follows from the recommendation's new `status` rather than from any write to the position, so the donor position's own `version` and `updated_at` do not move; the approval does nevertheless re-run the position-to-SKU propagation of §3.3 over the donor, which is how an approval can drive a `PENDING` assortment action on the donor's pair to `SUPERSEDED` and can rewrite the SKU's `lifecycle_state` in place. Segregation of duties is structurally unbreakable for `CONSOLIDATION` transfers, whose `proposed_by_user_id` is the literal string `system`.

A `PROPOSED` recommendation reaches exactly one of four outcomes, and three of them are terminal endings. It may be **rejected** by an `AllocationManager`, which takes it `PROPOSED → REJECTED`, stores the supplied `reason` as `reject_reason` and advances `version` by one; `reason` is a required, non-empty string both there and on the cancellation below, and it is validated at rung 4 ahead of resolving `recommendationId`. It may **expire** through the sweep above. It may be **cancelled** implicitly by a newer recommendation on the same triple, as described earlier. Or it may be approved. From `APPROVED` there are two onward paths. An `AllocationManager` may **cancel** an approved recommendation, taking it `APPROVED → CANCELLED`, which exactly reverses the approval: the transfer's `units` are released from the donor position's `reserved_out_units`, restoring its `available_units` and re-deriving its `stock_status`, the supplied `reason` is stored as `reject_reason`, and `version` advances by one — the donor position's own `version` staying put here too, while the SKU's `lifecycle_state` is re-derived in place exactly as it is on approval. Or the goods may move.

**Dispatch** takes the recommendation `APPROVED → DISPATCHED` and requires that the program be `ACTIVE`, or the refusal is `ERR_PROGRAM_LOCKED`, and that the receiving store's `status` still be `ACTIVE`, or the refusal is `ERR_STORE_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. The donor position must still resolve, or the refusal is `ERR_POSITION_NOT_FOUND`, whereas a receiving position that has gone missing is tolerated and simply skipped. It subtracts the transfer's `units` from the donor position's `on_hand_units` — the subtraction is **floored at zero**, so a donor whose on-hand count has meanwhile fallen below the transfer's `units` lands on 0 rather than going negative, and the units that floor swallows are never restored to anyone — and removes the `size_split` quantities from the donor's `size_on_hand` — removal is per size code and is **clamped at zero**, so a size never goes negative, an entry drained to nothing stays in the array carrying `units` of 0 rather than being deleted, and a `size_split` code the donor's array does not carry is silently ignored. Dispatch **does not release the donor's reservation**: a `DISPATCHED` recommendation is still inside the `APPROVED`-or-`DISPATCHED` set that §3.3 sums, so the donor's `reserved_out_units` goes on carrying the transfer's `units` — and the donor's `available_units` stays lower than its `on_hand_units` by them — until the receiving store confirms receipt and the recommendation reaches `RECEIVED`. Dispatch also adds the `units` to the receiving position's `in_transit_units`; it re-derives `broken_size_count`, `stock_status` and `assortment_status` on **both** positions; it recomputes the SKU's `broken_store_count` and `size_out_store_count` and re-derives its `lifecycle_state`; and it advances the recommendation's `version` by one. Of the two positions only the **donor's** own `version` advances alongside it, because dispatch writes the donor's stored `on_hand_units` and `size_on_hand`; the receiving position's `version` and `updated_at` are left exactly where they were, its `in_transit_units` following from the recommendation's new `status` rather than from any write of its own.

**Receiving** is the store's own act and produces the deepest cascade in the whole model. A `StoreOperator` whose `X-User-ID` equals the recommendation's `to_store_id` — any other operator is refused with `ERR_STORE_SCOPE_VIOLATION` — takes the recommendation `DISPATCHED → RECEIVED`, which is terminal. The transfer's `units` move out of the receiving position's `in_transit_units` and into its `on_hand_units`, and the `size_split` entries are merged into its `size_on_hand` under the same merge rule the line confirmation of §3.7 uses; if that leaves the receiving position's `on_hand_units` above zero its own `consolidated_out` flag is cleared back to false, and its `version` advances by one because those are stored fields. If the donor position's `on_hand_units` has reached 0 and the transfer's `source_reason` is `CONSOLIDATION`, the donor position's `consolidated_out` flag is set to true, which by the pinned evaluation order in §3.3 immediately makes that position `EXITED`; that flag is the only stored field this step ever writes on the donor, so the donor position's `version` advances by one exactly when the flag is set and stays where it was on every other receive. Both positions have `broken_size_count`, `stock_status` and `assortment_status` re-derived. The SKU's `broken_store_count` and `size_out_store_count` are recomputed and its `lifecycle_state` is re-derived, which is how a SKU returns from `CONSOLIDATION_PENDING` to `ACTIVE` once `broken_store_count` reaches 0. Because §3.3's supersede test runs once for each position that is re-derived, any assortment action still `PENDING` for **either** position's SKU-and-store pair is driven to `SUPERSEDED` when that position's re-derived `assortment_status` comes out `COMPLETE` — which for the donor of a consolidation it never does, that position having landed on `EXITED`. And the recommendation's `version` advances by one. Recommendations are discovered through their filtered list view, which filters on `program_id`, `from_store_id`, `to_store_id`, `item_id`, `supersedes_recommendation_id`, `assortment_action_id` and `status`; that list is the only channel through which a `CONSOLIDATION` transfer created by a remedy execution can be found.

### 3.9. Broken Assortments, Remedies and Cluster Measurement

A size-broken set stops selling long before anyone reports it, so the service raises the alarm itself. Either an `Allocator` or an `AllocationManager` may run a detection sweep over one program, supplying the `program_id`; the program must be `ACTIVE`, or the sweep is refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_LOCKED`. The sweep scans every store position whose **SKU** belongs to the given program — the linkage tested is the SKU's `program_id`, not the store's — looking for degraded assortment health, and raises exactly one `PENDING` **assortment action** per newly degraded position; a position whose store or SKU record no longer resolves at all is passed over in silence. Four exclusions apply. A position that already has a `PENDING` assortment action for the same SKU-and-store pair is skipped, so re-running the sweep never duplicates work. Only positions whose `assortment_status` is `SIZE_OUT_RISK` or `BROKEN` are in scope at all, so a `COMPLETE` position, having nothing wrong with it, and an `EXITED` position, having nothing left to fix, are both skipped. Positions in stores whose `status` is neither `ACTIVE` nor `REMODEL` are skipped. And positions of SKUs whose `lifecycle_state` is `MARKDOWN` are skipped, since a marked-down SKU is beyond remedy. The sweep needs a shelf plan in effect for the SKU's class at the store, and its absence is reported as `ERR_NO_FIXTURE_PLAN_IN_EFFECT`; that check is made for every position that survived the four exclusions, in the sweep's own order, **before any action is raised at all**, so a single store without a plan aborts the entire sweep and leaves nothing behind it. The actions the sweep returns are ordered by the store's `store_code` ascending and then by the SKU's `sku_code` ascending — this is one of the few places a list is not ordered by `created_at`. The response carries only the actions this sweep has just raised, never the ones it skipped, as an array with no pagination block in `meta`, and it is an empty array when nothing was newly degraded; `program_id` is a required field of the request body.

Each raised action records the `item_id` and `store_id` of the degraded position, a `detected_at` stamp holding the logical clock at sweep time, and a `trigger_reason` that is `SIZE_OUT` when the position's `assortment_status` was `SIZE_OUT_RISK` and `BROKEN_ASSORTMENT` when it was `BROKEN`. It snapshots two figures from the position at detection time and then never changes them: `broken_size_count`, copied from the position's own count, and `units_at_detection`, copied from the position's `available_units`, which later becomes the unit count of the consolidation transfer if one is created. Each action opens with `status` of `PENDING`.

The remedy is chosen by a **selector policy with an explicit empty-set fallback**, run once per degraded position. Given the degraded position, the candidate consolidation targets are the other positions of the **same SKU** that satisfy all of the following: they belong to a different store; their own `assortment_status` is `COMPLETE`; their store's `status` is `ACTIVE`; and their headroom — the greater of zero and their in-effect shelf plan's `capacity_units` less their `available_units` less their `in_transit_units` — is greater than or equal to the degraded position's `available_units`, so the target can physically absorb the whole move — a candidate store with no shelf plan in effect for the SKU's class has a headroom of 0 and is therefore simply dropped from the candidate set rather than raising `ERR_NO_FIXTURE_PLAN_IN_EFFECT`. One short-circuit applies before any of that: if the degraded position's `available_units` is 0 there is nothing to move, and the candidate set is empty by definition. Surviving candidates are ordered by three keys applied strictly in sequence: the candidate store's `historical_sell_through_bp` descending; then grade rank ascending, with `A` ahead of `B` ahead of `C` ahead of `D`; and finally the candidate store's `store_code` ascending. When at least one candidate survives, the action's `action_type` is `CONSOLIDATION` and its `consolidation_target_store_id` is the first candidate's store. When the candidate set is empty, the action's `action_type` is `MARKDOWN` and `consolidation_target_store_id` stays null. Because the eligibility filter runs before the ranking, the highest sell-through store in the network can still lose to a lower one simply for having no fixture headroom.

Only actions that resolved to `MARKDOWN` carry a depth, computed as a **tiered figure floored on a ratio**. If the SKU's size profile has a `core_size_count` of 0 — or the SKU has no size profile at all, which counts as the same thing — the broken ratio is taken as 0 basis points; otherwise the broken ratio is the position's `broken_size_count` multiplied by 10000, divided by `core_size_count`, truncated down to a whole number of basis points. The resulting `markdown_pct_bp` is then read off four tiers, each lower-inclusive and upper-exclusive: a broken ratio below 2500 gives 0, a ratio from 2500 up to but not including 5000 gives 1500, a ratio from 5000 up to but not including 7500 gives 3000, and a ratio of 7500 or above gives 4500. A `CONSOLIDATION` action leaves `markdown_pct_bp` null.

Executing an action belongs to an `AllocationManager` and takes it `PENDING → EXECUTED`. Three conditions apply, and they are tested in the order given here, after the action itself has been confirmed to be `PENDING`. The program must be `ACTIVE`, or the refusal is `ERR_PROGRAM_LOCKED`. The SKU's `lifecycle_state` must be either `SIZE_OUT_WATCH` or `BROKEN`, or the refusal is `ERR_ITEM_LIFECYCLE_BLOCKED` — an `ACTIVE` SKU has nothing wrong with it, a `CONSOLIDATION_PENDING` SKU already has a remedy in flight, and a `MARKDOWN` SKU is frozen. And for a `CONSOLIDATION` action, `units_at_detection` must be at least 1 and the target store's headroom must **still** cover `units_at_detection` at execution time, or the refusal is `ERR_FIXTURE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. That headroom is read against the shelf plan in effect for the SKU's class at the target store at execution time, and a target with no plan in effect then has a headroom of 0 and therefore fails this same check: the refusal is `ERR_FIXTURE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, never `ERR_NO_FIXTURE_PLAN_IN_EFFECT`. Ahead of it, the recorded `consolidation_target_store_id` must still resolve, or the refusal is `ERR_STORE_NOT_FOUND`, and both the degraded position and the target store's position for the SKU must still resolve, or the refusal is `ERR_POSITION_NOT_FOUND`.

What execution does depends on `action_type`, and the two branches are equally specified. A `CONSOLIDATION` action creates a transfer recommendation with `source_reason` of `CONSOLIDATION`, `from_store_id` set to the action's own `store_id`, `to_store_id` set to the action's `consolidation_target_store_id`, `units` set to `units_at_detection`, `assortment_action_id` set to the action's identifier, and `proposed_by_user_id` set to the literal string `system`; that recommendation's `size_split`, `freight_cost_usd`, `handling_fee_usd`, `landed_cost_usd`, `benefit_usd`, `benefit_ratio_bp` and `expires_at` are all computed exactly as described in §3.8, using the consolidation branch of the size split, and if that split finds no candidate size the execution is refused with `ERR_TRANSFER_SIZE_INFEASIBLE`. That creation is the same act §3.8 describes, so it also supersedes: any recommendation still `PROPOSED` on the same donor-store, receiving-store and SKU triple is driven to `CANCELLED` with `reject_reason` of `superseded` and its `version` advances, and the new recommendation records it in `supersedes_recommendation_id`. Unlike the operator creation of §3.8, though, a remedy-created recommendation does **not** advance the donor position's `version` — no client staked a version against it and no stored field of the donor is written here. The SKU is then driven to `CONSOLIDATION_PENDING` from whichever of `BROKEN` or `SIZE_OUT_WATCH` it was holding, since the lifecycle condition above admits both, and its `version` advances by one. A `MARKDOWN` action instead drives the SKU straight to `MARKDOWN`, which is terminal — reachable from `BROKEN` and equally from `SIZE_OUT_WATCH` — leaves `consolidation_target_store_id` null, advances the SKU's `version` by one, and permanently excludes the SKU from every future allocation proration candidate set and from every future consolidation-target candidate set. Once a SKU is `MARKDOWN`, no lifecycle re-derivation ever moves it again. In both branches the action's own `version` advances by one. An `AllocationManager` may instead waive the remedy, taking the action `PENDING → CANCELLED`, which is terminal and advances `version` by one. Actions are discovered through their filtered list view, which filters on `item_id`, `store_id`, `consolidation_target_store_id`, `action_type` and `status`, and that list is the only channel through which sweep-raised actions can be found.

Measurement closes the loop. Either an `Allocator` or an `AllocationManager` creates a **cluster performance snapshot** by supplying a `program_id` and a `cluster_code`, optionally naming a `basis` of `SIMPLE_AVERAGE` or `UNIT_WEIGHTED`; when `basis` is omitted it falls back to the program's `scorecard_basis`. The program must be `ACTIVE`. The snapshot records `as_of_date` from the logical clock and is immutable thereafter, which is why it carries neither `updated_at` nor `version`. The included population is every store position whose store belongs to the given `program_id`, whose store's `cluster_code` matches the requested cluster, whose store's `status` is either `ACTIVE` or `REMODEL`, and whose SKU's `lifecycle_state` is anything other than `MARKDOWN`. If that population is empty the request is refused with `ERR_NO_CLUSTER_POSITIONS`. Otherwise `store_count` is the number of distinct stores contributing at least one included position and `included_position_count` is the number of included positions.

Three per-position quantities feed the scorecard. A position's **covered** units are the lesser of its `available_units` and its `min_units`. Its **sold** units are its `trailing_4wk_sales_units`. Its **base** is its sold units plus its `available_units`. From these, `in_stock_pct_bp` and `sell_through_pct_bp` are computed, and the two bases differ in **where the division and the rounding happen** — a difference that produces materially different numbers on the same data. Neither figure is clamped under either basis: truncation is toward negative infinity, and because a position's `available_units` can be negative while a dispatched transfer is still outstanding, its covered units and its base can both be negative and can carry `in_stock_pct_bp` or `sell_through_pct_bp` below zero. Only `allocation_accuracy_bp` carries an explicit floor at 0.

Under `SIMPLE_AVERAGE`, each position is reduced to a ratio first and the ratios are then averaged. A position's in-stock ratio is 10000 basis points when its `min_units` is 0, and otherwise its covered units multiplied by 10000, divided by its `min_units`, truncated down to a whole number of basis points. A position's sell ratio is 0 basis points when its base is 0, and otherwise its sold units multiplied by 10000, divided by its base, truncated down. Then `in_stock_pct_bp` is the arithmetic mean of the in-stock ratios across the included positions, rounded half up to a whole number of basis points, and `sell_through_pct_bp` is the arithmetic mean of the sell ratios, rounded half up the same way. Every store weighs the same regardless of size under this basis: a two-unit door moves the cluster as much as a two-hundred-unit door.

Under `UNIT_WEIGHTED`, numerators and denominators are summed across the whole cluster first, divided once, and truncated once. `in_stock_pct_bp` is 10000 basis points when the sum of `min_units` across included positions is 0, and otherwise the sum of covered units multiplied by 10000, divided by the sum of `min_units`, truncated down to a whole number of basis points. `sell_through_pct_bp` is 0 basis points when the sum of the bases is 0, and otherwise the sum of sold units multiplied by 10000, divided by the sum of the bases, truncated down. There is a single division and a single truncation, so no per-position rounding error accumulates.

`allocation_accuracy_bp` is **identical under both bases**, because it is always unit-weighted. For each included position, the allocated figure is the sum of `units_allocated` over every allocation line whose `store_id` is that position's store, whose owning run's receipt names that position's SKU, and whose `fulfillment_status` is `PENDING`, `SHIPPED` or `RECEIVED`. The deviation is the sum, across included positions, of the absolute difference between that allocated figure and the position's sold units. The cluster's total sold is the sum of sold units across included positions. When total sold is 0, `allocation_accuracy_bp` is 0. Otherwise it is 10000 less the deviation multiplied by 10000 and divided by total sold with the quotient truncated down, and that result floored at 0 so the figure never goes negative. A cluster that allocated exactly what it sold scores 10000; one that allocated twice what it sold scores 0.
