# Disaster Public Assistance API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

When a governor requests and receives a federal major-disaster declaration, a state emergency management agency becomes the pass-through grantee for every dollar of Public Assistance money that flows to the local governments, tribes, state agencies and private nonprofits inside the declared area. The work that follows is not primarily engineering work — it is documentation work. Somebody has to prove that the damage was caused inside the incident period, that the applicant is a statutory class of applicant, that the facility was in active use and is the applicant's legal responsibility, that the labor rates and equipment rates charged against the grant are the ones the state actually publishes, that the contracts were competed rather than handed to a favored vendor, and that no insurer has already paid for the same repair. Getting any one of those wrong does not merely delay a payment; it produces a finding at audit and a demand for the money back, years later, from a community that has already spent it.

This API is the system of record for that documentation chain. It carries a declaration from the moment it is declared, through damage assessment data collection and per-capita indicator computation, applicant and facility eligibility screening, project formulation by work category with small- and large-project classification, cost eligibility review of force account labor, force account equipment, materials and contracted work, insurance requirements and duplication-of-benefits reduction, mitigation measure funding attached to repair projects, obligation of the federal share, cash drawdown and quarterly progress reporting, and finally closeout certification, deobligation and the two-level appeal that follows a deobligation determination. Every collection is mounted as a top-level path segment under `/public-assistance`.

Two design commitments shape everything below. First, **the ledger is always right now**: when an obligation is posted, a settlement is recorded, a report goes delinquent or an appeal is granted, every downstream aggregate — the project's basis, the applicant's cash position, the declaration's roll-ups, the applicant's three status dimensions — is recomputed inside the same request and is visible in that request's response. There is no reconciliation job to wait for. Second, **money is never approximate**: all amounts are integer cents, every rounding point is pinned to a specific step in a specific order, and where the specification says a product is rounded before it is added, adding first and rounding once is a defect even when the two answers agree by coincidence.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section fixes the contract-level behaviour that applies to every endpoint: authentication, response shape, pagination, resource identity, time, money, the order in which a request is validated, optimistic concurrency, and the cascade and segregation-of-duties principles that the workflows in §3 lean on.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request — including reads — must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting user and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity in which they are acting. If either header is absent, or if the value in `X-User-Role` is not one of the six roles defined below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). If the headers are well formed but the declared role is not among the roles authorized for the target endpoint in §4, the request is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403).

The six roles are:

- `PlatformAdmin` — the state program office administrator. Declares disasters, defines and extends incident periods, registers applicants, advances the reporting epoch for an applicant, and drives the logical clock.
- `ApplicantAgent` — the authorized agent of exactly one applicant. Registers facilities, force account resources, contracts and insurance policies; collects damage assessment observations; formulates and submits projects; answers requests for information; claims costs; requests and refunds cash; files progress reports; requests closeout; and files appeals.
- `ProgramSpecialist` — the state reviewer. Determines applicant eligibility, screens facilities, validates or rejects damage assessment sites, reviews procurement compliance, reviews cost eligibility of individual claimed lines, issues requests for information, and validates or denies projects at cost review.
- `GrantsOfficer` — the obligation and cash officer. Obligates validated projects, records insurance settlements, disburses or rejects drawdowns, and finalizes deobligation determinations. May also record a deobligation determination.
- `AppealsAdjudicator` — the independent adjudicator. Decides first- and second-level appeals.
- `StateAuditor` — the closeout auditor. Certifies project closeout, may record a deobligation determination, and reads every record.

Only one role is backed by a persisted entity. When `X-User-Role` is `ApplicantAgent`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Applicant`; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Applicant` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, an `ApplicantAgent` may act only where `Facility.applicant_id`, `Project.applicant_id`, `ForceAccountResource.applicant_id`, `Contract.applicant_id`, `Drawdown.applicant_id` and `ProgressReport.applicant_id` equal `X-User-ID`; where an `InsurancePolicy` is reached, the owning `Facility`'s applicant must equal `X-User-ID`; where a `CostLineItem` is reached, its `Project`'s applicant must equal `X-User-ID`; and where an `Appeal` is filed, the appealed `Deobligation`'s `Project` must belong to `X-User-ID`. Any violation of this rule is rejected with `ERR_APPLICANT_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (403).

When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `ProgramSpecialist`, `GrantsOfficer`, `AppealsAdjudicator` or `StateAuditor`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a workflow demands accountability — as `Project.cost_reviewed_by_user_id` when a `ProgramSpecialist` decides cost review, as `Project.obligated_by_user_id` when a `GrantsOfficer` obligates, as `Drawdown.disbursed_by_user_id` when a `GrantsOfficer` disburses, as `Deobligation.determined_by_user_id` when a determination is recorded (whether recorded manually or created by a system cascade in the same request), and as `Appeal.decided_by_user_id` when an `AppealsAdjudicator` decides — but it is never compared against any entity's `id`. It is, however, compared against those recorded operator identifiers; see §2.10.

Every `GET` endpoint in §4 is readable by all six roles.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (422).

Unless a subsection says otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before the page window is applied and `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond the current window. Filters combine conjunctively: supplying both `applicant_id` and `status` returns only rows matching both.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties

Thirteen resource collections make up this system: declarations, applicants, facilities, damage assessment sites, projects, cost line items, force account resources, contracts, insurance policies, drawdowns, progress reports, deobligation determinations and appeals. Every one of them, without exception, carries the same four system-managed properties:

- `id` — a server-minted UUID. It carries no type prefix and clients never supply it.
- `created_at` — an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, fixed at creation and never changed.
- `updated_at` — an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, refreshed on every mutation of the row, including mutations of purely derived fields.
- `version` — an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of the row.

There are no create-only resources here: no collection omits `updated_at` and none omits `version`. This matters because the cascades in §2.9 mutate rows the caller did not name. When obligating a project also moves the applicant's ledger and the declaration's roll-ups, all three rows have their `updated_at` refreshed and their `version` incremented in that one request, and the version of a row is the only handle a client has on whether its view of that row is current (§2.8).

Timestamps written from the logical clock — `screened_at`, `reviewed_at`, `submitted_at`, `settled_at`, `finalized_at`, `appeal_deadline_at`, `filed_at`, `decided_at`, `monitoring_scored_at`, `due_at`, `period_start_at` and `period_end_at` — use the same ISO 8601 UTC representation with three fractional digits.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock and the Temporal Contract

Nothing in this system reads the host machine's wall clock. Instead the server maintains a **logical clock** that an administrator sets explicitly, so that appeal windows, reporting grace periods and quarter derivations are reproducible. Before the clock is ever set, it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; that startup default never decays into real time. Setting the clock takes a single body field, `to_iso8601`, carrying the instant to move to, and answers with `{ current_utc: string }` — the only endpoint in the system whose response is not a resource envelope payload of an entity. Setting the clock mutates no entity row.

Every instant the server writes for itself reads this clock: `screened_at` and `reviewed_at` on screening and cost review, `submitted_at` on report filing, `finalized_at` and the derived `appeal_deadline_at` on finalization, `filed_at` on appeal filing, `decided_at` on appeal decision, `monitoring_scored_at` on monitoring scoring, and the quarter boundaries of a progress report. Instants supplied by clients — `incident_start_at`, `incident_end_at`, `damage_observed_at`, `work_start_at`, `work_end_at`, `awarded_at`, `effective_start_at`, `effective_end_at` and `settled_at` — must be valid ISO 8601 and are compared against stored instants and against the clock as written.

Two interval conventions coexist and must not be confused. The **incident period** of a declaration, `[incident_start_at, incident_end_at]`, and the **effective interval** of an insurance policy, `[effective_start_at, effective_end_at]`, are *closed*: an instant exactly equal to either endpoint is inside the interval. A declaration's **occupied interval** for the purposes of overlap exclusivity, and a progress report's **reporting period** `[period_start_at, period_end_at)`, are *half-open*: the start is inside and the end is outside. Because reporting periods are half-open, the quarter `2026-Q2` spans `2026-04-01T00:00:00Z` up to but not including `2026-07-01T00:00:00Z`, and `2026-Q3` starts exactly where `2026-Q2` ended; the two are adjacent and never overlap. Deadline comparisons are inclusive: a clock instant exactly equal to `due_at` is on time, and a clock instant exactly equal to `appeal_deadline_at` is inside the appeal window.

### 2.6. Monetary Amounts, Rates and Rounding Discipline

Every monetary field in this system is an integer number of cents and every field name that holds money ends in `_cents`. There are no fractional cents anywhere in storage or in a response body. Rates and percentages are the exception: `federal_cost_share_pct` (one of 0.75, 0.90 or 1.00), `fringe_benefit_pct` (a fraction from 0 through 1 inclusive), `work_completed_pct` and `pct_complete` (each a number from 0 through 100 inclusive) are stored as supplied.

Wherever a monetary amount is multiplied by a rate, the product is rounded **half up** to whole cents. Three rules govern *when* that rounding happens, and they are not interchangeable:

1. **Per-component rounding is pinned.** Where a specification says two or three components are each rounded and then summed, each component is rounded to whole cents on its own and the rounded values are added exactly. Summing the raw products and rounding the total once is a different rule and can differ by a cent. This applies to the three components of force account labor cost, to the repair and mitigation components of the obligated federal share, and to the same two components again at closeout certification.
2. **Caps and per-capita ratios floor.** Where a cap is computed as a percentage of a basis, or a ratio is reduced to an integer, the result is taken by `floor` — truncation downward — never by half-up rounding. A mitigation cap can therefore never round upward into a cent the program did not authorize, and the per-capita damage indicator can never round upward into meeting a threshold it does not meet.
3. **Pure integer arithmetic never rounds.** Ledger aggregation, waterfall allocation and variance computation are additions and subtractions of integers; no rounding step exists inside them.

The applicant risk score is the one non-monetary computed number with a precision rule: it is carried to exactly 2 decimal places, rounded half up.

### 2.7. Request Evaluation Order and Path Resolution

A single request can fail for many reasons at once, and which error a client sees is part of the contract, not an implementation detail. Checks are therefore evaluated in the following fixed order, and the **first** failing rung decides the response. A later rung never masks an earlier one, and an earlier rung never runs after a later one has already passed.

1. Header presence and role recognition — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. Role authorization for the target endpoint — `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403).
3. `ApplicantAgent` ownership scope over every applicant-scoped resource named in the path or the body — `ERR_APPLICANT_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (403).
4. Resolution of every identifier embedded in the URL path, validated left to right. The path identifiers are `disaster_id`, `applicant_id`, `facility_id`, `damage_site_id`, `project_id`, `cost_line_item_id`, `force_account_resource_id`, `contract_id`, `insurance_policy_id`, `drawdown_id`, `progress_report_id`, `deobligation_id` and `appeal_id`. The first identifier that does not resolve decides the error: `ERR_DISASTER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPLICANT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FACILITY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DAMAGE_SITE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COST_LINE_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_POLICY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DRAWDOWN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROGRESS_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DEOBLIGATION_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_APPEAL_NOT_FOUND` (all 404).
5. Request shape: every required body field present, every enum value a member of its declared set, every integer inside its declared range, every datetime valid ISO 8601, `work_end_at` strictly later than `work_start_at`, `quarter_label` of the form four digits, a hyphen, the letter `Q`, and a digit from 1 to 4, and the pairing between `cost_type` and its foreign key respected — `force_account_resource_id` required and `contract_id` null for `FORCE_ACCOUNT_LABOR` and `FORCE_ACCOUNT_EQUIPMENT`, `contract_id` required and `force_account_resource_id` null for `CONTRACT`, and both null for `MATERIALS`. Failures here yield `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422); a malformed page window yields `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (422).
6. Resolution of every foreign key supplied in the body — `disaster_id`, `applicant_id`, `facility_id`, `project_id`, `force_account_resource_id`, `contract_id`, `deobligation_id` and `prior_appeal_id` — with the same 404 error identifiers as rung 4.
7. Optimistic concurrency: `expected_applicant_version` on drawdown creation and `expected_project_version` on deobligation creation, as specified in §2.8 — `ERR_STALE_APPLICANT_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_PROJECT_VERSION` (412).
8. State admissibility of the target row, and — for drawdown creation and deobligation creation — of the referenced project: `ERR_PROJECT_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_DISASTER_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_DAMAGE_SITE_ALREADY_ADJUDICATED`, `ERR_CONTRACT_ALREADY_REVIEWED`, `ERR_COST_LINE_ITEM_LOCKED`, `ERR_FACILITY_AMENDMENT_LOCKED`, `ERR_DRAWDOWN_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_DEOBLIGATION_NOT_FINAL`, `ERR_APPEAL_ALREADY_DECIDED` or `ERR_REPORT_ALREADY_SUBMITTED` (all 409).
9. On cost review only: no cost line item on the project may still be awaiting review — `ERR_UNREVIEWED_COST_LINE_ITEMS` (409).
10. Incident-period containment of `damage_observed_at` on damage site collection, and of the whole charged interval on cost line item creation — `ERR_OUTSIDE_INCIDENT_PERIOD` (422).
11. Appeal filing inside the appeal window — `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_EXPIRED` (422).
12. Incident-period well-formedness at declaration and forward-only movement at amendment — `ERR_INCIDENT_PERIOD_INVALID` (422).
13. Effectiveness of a settled policy at the declaration's incident start — `ERR_POLICY_NOT_EFFECTIVE_AT_INCIDENT` (422).
14. Facility eligibility on project submission and on cost review — `ERR_FACILITY_INELIGIBLE` (409).
15. The private-nonprofit small-business-loan referral prerequisite at eligibility determination — `ERR_PNP_SBA_REFERRAL_REQUIRED` (409).
16. Contract compliance at cost line item creation — `ERR_PROCUREMENT_NONCOMPLIANT` (409).
17. Contract review completeness at cost line item review — `ERR_PROCUREMENT_REVIEW_PENDING` (409).
18. The obtain-and-maintain flood insurance bar at obligation — `ERR_INSURANCE_REQUIREMENT_UNMET` (409).
19. Applicant eligibility on project submission, cost line item creation and drawdown creation — `ERR_APPLICANT_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (409).
20. Declaration openness on project submission and obligation — `ERR_DISASTER_NOT_OPEN` (409).
21. Single-applicant containment of cross-aggregate references on cost line item creation and drawdown creation — `ERR_CROSS_APPLICANT_REFERENCE` (409).
22. Review completeness across the declaration when closing obligations — `ERR_PROJECT_REVIEWS_OPEN` (409).
23. Uniqueness of `declaration_number`, and of the pair of `applicant_id` and `quarter_label` — `ERR_DECLARATION_NUMBER_DUPLICATE` or `ERR_DUPLICATE_REPORT_PERIOD` (409).
24. Incident-period exclusivity across declarations of the same `state_code` and `incident_type` — `ERR_INCIDENT_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (409).
25. The labor rest-buffer collision rule on a labor resource — `ERR_LABOR_ENTRY_OVERLAP` (409).
26. The aggregate daily equipment-hours cap on an equipment resource — `ERR_EQUIPMENT_DAILY_HOURS_EXCEEDED` (409).
27. The request-for-information round cap — `ERR_RFI_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED` (409).
28. `mitigation_requested_cents` not above `estimated_repair_cost_cents` at project creation — `ERR_MITIGATION_EXCEEDS_REPAIR` (422).
29. Segregation of duties on disbursement and on appeal decisions — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
30. Appeal admissibility, level and duplication — `ERR_SECOND_APPEAL_REQUIRES_FIRST_DENIED`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_APPEAL` or `ERR_APPEAL_LEVEL_EXHAUSTED` (409).
31. Relief bounds on appeal filing and on a partial grant — `ERR_RELIEF_EXCEEDS_DEOBLIGATION` (422).
32. Settlement not above coverage — `ERR_SETTLEMENT_EXCEEDS_COVERAGE` (422).
33. Determination amount not above the project's remaining obligation — `ERR_DEOBLIGATION_EXCEEDS_OBLIGATION` (422).
34. Drawdown suspension — `ERR_DRAWDOWN_SUSPENDED` (409).
35. Drawdown availability — `ERR_DRAWDOWN_EXCEEDS_AVAILABLE` (402).
36. Refund bound — `ERR_REFUND_EXCEEDS_DRAWDOWN` (422).
37. Documented costs at closeout request — `ERR_NO_DOCUMENTED_COSTS` (409).

The substantive content of rungs 9 through 37 belongs to the workflows in §3, where each is described where the business reader would look for it. The ordering above is what pins which error a client actually receives when several would apply. Note in particular that rung 3 precedes rung 4: an `ApplicantAgent` reaching for a resource that does not belong to them learns about the scope violation and not about whether some deeper identifier resolves.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency on Ledger Writes

Two write operations create a child row whose amount was sized against a parent row's ledger, and for both of them a repeat request is legitimate business behaviour rather than a mistake. An applicant may draw cash more than once against the same obligation; a project may be reduced for both `DISALLOWED_COST` and `INSURANCE_DOB`. Because no state precondition can distinguish "the client meant to do this again" from "the client is acting on a stale reading", both operations carry an explicit version expectation.

Creating a drawdown requires a non-optional `expected_applicant_version` field in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Applicant`. If it does not equal that applicant's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_APPLICANT_VERSION` (412) and nothing is written. This is what protects a client whose view of `total_obligated_cents`, `cash_on_hand_cents` and `drawdown_status` predates an intervening ledger movement.

Creating a deobligation determination requires a non-optional `expected_project_version` field in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Project`. If it does not equal that project's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PROJECT_VERSION` (412) and nothing is written. This is what stops a determination whose `amount_cents` was sized against a superseded view of `federal_share_obligated_cents` and `deobligated_cents`.

No other endpoint in this system takes a version field, and no other endpoint performs a version check. Every other write is guarded by state preconditions alone.

### 2.9. Immediate Cascade Consistency and the Conserved Ledger Quantities

Cascades in this system are **immediate**: the full chain of consequences of an operation is applied inside the triggering request and is observable in that request's response. Cascades are also **causal chains, not flat lists** — each hop happens because the preceding hop happened, which is why a hop can be skipped when its cause does not fire. Two chains run four hops deep. Obligating a project moves the project's obligated share, which moves the applicant's obligation ledger, which may cross the single-audit threshold and escalate monitoring, which creates a progress report row, which refreshes the declaration's counters. Sweeping an applicant's reporting flags an overdue report delinquent, which moves the applicant's delinquent count, which suspends drawdowns, which refreshes the declaration's suspended count. Every row touched anywhere along such a chain has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by 1.

Two quantities are conserved, and the conservation is the acceptance criterion for the compensating actions:

- **The obligation ledger.** An applicant's `total_obligated_cents` is the sum, over that applicant's projects in status `OBLIGATED`, `CLOSEOUT_REQUESTED` or `CLOSED`, of `federal_share_obligated_cents` minus `deobligated_cents`. Obligating a project for some amount and then finalizing a determination for exactly that amount must leave `total_obligated_cents` — and the declaration's `total_obligated_cents` — exactly where they started. Equally, finalizing a determination for some amount and then granting an appeal that restores exactly that amount must leave both figures exactly where they started.
- **The cash ledger.** An applicant's `cash_on_hand_cents` is `total_drawn_cents` minus `total_refunded_cents`. A paid drawdown of some amount followed by a full refund of that amount must leave `cash_on_hand_cents`, `available_drawdown_cents` and the declaration's `total_drawn_cents` exactly where they started, while `total_drawn_cents` and `total_refunded_cents` each rise by that amount.

Two computed fields are **high-water marks** and never move backwards. `audit_requirement` becomes `SINGLE_AUDIT` once the applicant's `total_obligated_cents` reaches the single-audit threshold and never returns to `NONE`, even if a later deobligation drops the ledger below the threshold. `monitoring_status` escalates from `STANDARD` to `ENHANCED` on the same event and never returns to `STANDARD`. By contrast `drawdown_status` is deliberately reversible: it oscillates between `ACTIVE` and `SUSPENDED` for as long as the grant is open.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties

Two separations are enforced on `X-User-ID` within a single role, because in a small state office the same person plausibly holds both duties and the control has to be mechanical rather than organizational.

The first separation is between obligating and paying. The `GrantsOfficer` whose identifier was recorded in `Project.obligated_by_user_id` when the project was obligated may not be the officer who disburses a drawdown against that project. An attempt is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403); the disbursing officer's identifier is what lands in `Drawdown.disbursed_by_user_id`.

The second separation is between determining and adjudicating. The user whose identifier was recorded in `Deobligation.determined_by_user_id` may not decide an appeal against that determination, even when that user legitimately holds `AppealsAdjudicator` duty as well. The attempt is rejected with the same `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403), and a clean decision records the adjudicator in `Appeal.decided_by_user_id`. This separation bites hardest on determinations the system created by cascade rather than by hand: a settlement waterfall or a closeout certification stamps the acting officer's or auditor's identifier onto every determination it creates, so that officer or auditor is barred from adjudicating those appeals afterwards.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

The nine subsections below follow the grant chronologically, from the declaration that opens the program to the appeal that closes the last argument about it. Where a rule cuts across stages — insurance, segregation of duties, the applicant's three status dimensions — it is described at the point in the timeline where it first bites, and reinforced where it bites again.

### 3.1. Opening a Declaration and Registering Applicants

A program cycle begins when a `PlatformAdmin` declares a disaster. The declaration carries a `declaration_number` (a non-empty string that must be globally unique across every declaration; a repeat is rejected with `ERR_DECLARATION_NUMBER_DUPLICATE`), a `state_code` of exactly two uppercase letters identifying the state or territory that owns the declaration, an `incident_type` from `SEVERE_STORM`, `FLOOD`, `WILDFIRE`, `HURRICANE` and `EARTHQUAKE`, the inclusive incident period bounds `incident_start_at` and `incident_end_at`, and a `population` of at least 1 that serves as the denominator of the per-capita indicator. Two economics knobs are optional: `federal_cost_share_pct`, which defaults to 0.75 and must be one of 0.75, 0.90 or 1.00, and `small_project_threshold_cents`, which defaults to 4,000,000 cents — forty thousand dollars — and must be at least 1. The declaration is created in status `DECLARED` with every roll-up zeroed: `total_validated_loss_cents`, `per_capita_impact_cents`, `total_obligated_cents`, `total_drawn_cents`, `enhanced_monitoring_applicant_count` and `suspended_applicant_count` all start at 0 and `indicator_met` starts false.

Two temporal rules police the incident period. First, well-formedness: `incident_end_at` must be strictly later than `incident_start_at`, and a violation yields `ERR_INCIDENT_PERIOD_INVALID`. Second, exclusivity: for a given combination of `state_code` and `incident_type`, no two declarations may occupy overlapping periods, where the occupied interval is treated as half-open — the start instant is occupied and the end instant is not. An incoming period whose start is exactly equal to an existing period's end is therefore adjacent, not overlapping, and is accepted; any genuine intersection is rejected with `ERR_INCIDENT_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. Declarations of different incident types, or of different states, never collide with each other.

Incident periods are routinely extended as the meteorology becomes clearer, and a `PlatformAdmin` may amend one by supplying a new `incident_end_at`. The amendment moves the end **strictly forward only**: a value that is equal to or earlier than the current `incident_end_at` is rejected with `ERR_INCIDENT_PERIOD_INVALID`, and the half-open exclusivity check is re-run against every other declaration sharing the same `state_code` and `incident_type`, so a forward extension that would swallow a neighbouring declaration's start is refused with `ERR_INCIDENT_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. An amendment is admissible only while the declaration is still `DECLARED`; attempting it on a declaration that has already reached `OBLIGATION_CLOSED` yields `ERR_DISASTER_INVALID_STATE`. A successful amendment increments the declaration's `version` by 1.

The declaration's terminal transition, `DECLARED → OBLIGATION_CLOSED`, is a `PlatformAdmin` capability with a compound gate: it is blocked by the state of its grandchildren. No project anywhere under the declaration — that is, under any of its applicants — may still be in `UNDER_REVIEW` or `RFI_ISSUED`, because closing the declaration while a review is open would strand that review with nowhere to land. Any open review yields `ERR_PROJECT_REVIEWS_OPEN`. Note what is *not* required: a project sitting in `VALIDATED` and still waiting for money is not an open review, so the declaration may close over it. Closing sets `status` to `OBLIGATION_CLOSED` and increments the declaration's `version` by 1; a second attempt on an already-closed declaration yields `ERR_DISASTER_INVALID_STATE`. Once closed, both project submission and project obligation under that declaration fail with `ERR_DISASTER_NOT_OPEN` — a `VALIDATED` project that missed the window can no longer be funded.

Applicants are registered under a declaration by a `PlatformAdmin`, who supplies the `disaster_id` of the declaration, the applicant's non-empty legal `name`, its `applicant_type` from `LOCAL_GOVERNMENT`, `TRIBE`, `STATE_AGENCY` and `PRIVATE_NONPROFIT`, and a `population_served` of at least 0. Three private-nonprofit attributes are optional at registration: `pnp_service_type`, which is `CRITICAL`, `ESSENTIAL_NON_CRITICAL` or `NON_ELIGIBLE` for a nonprofit and null for a public applicant (defaulting to null); `pnp_sba_referral_completed`, defaulting to false, recording whether the small-business disaster-loan referral has been completed; and `tax_exempt_letter_on_file`, defaulting to false, recording whether the tax-exempt determination letter is on file. A new applicant starts with `eligibility_status` `PENDING`, `monitoring_status` `STANDARD`, `drawdown_status` `ACTIVE`, `audit_requirement` `NONE`, `eligibility_rationale` null, `risk_score_screening` 0, `risk_score_monitoring` null, `monitoring_scored_at` null, `risk_tier` `LOW`, and every ledger figure — `total_obligated_cents`, `total_drawn_cents`, `total_refunded_cents`, `cash_on_hand_cents`, `available_drawdown_cents`, `pending_deobligation_cents` and `delinquent_report_count` — at 0.

An applicant carries **three independent status dimensions on one row**, and they are genuinely orthogonal rather than three views of one lifecycle. `eligibility_status` records the statutory determination and, once made, is permanent. `monitoring_status` records oversight intensity and only escalates. `drawdown_status` records whether cash may flow right now and oscillates freely. A single applicant can quite properly be `ELIGIBLE`, `ENHANCED` and `SUSPENDED` at the same moment.

Eligibility is determined by a `ProgramSpecialist`, who supplies a `decision` of either `ELIGIBLE` or `INELIGIBLE` together with a `rationale` recorded verbatim into `eligibility_rationale`. The two available transitions are `ELIGIBILITY:PENDING → ELIGIBILITY:ELIGIBLE` and `ELIGIBILITY:PENDING → ELIGIBILITY:INELIGIBLE`; only an applicant still in `PENDING` may be determined, and the determination is one-way, with no path back to `PENDING` and no path between `ELIGIBLE` and `INELIGIBLE`. The `ELIGIBLE` branch carries one extra prerequisite: when the applicant's `applicant_type` is `PRIVATE_NONPROFIT` and its `pnp_service_type` is `ESSENTIAL_NON_CRITICAL`, its `pnp_sba_referral_completed` must be true, because an essential-but-non-critical nonprofit must be referred to the disaster-loan program before grant money is considered; a false flag yields `ERR_PNP_SBA_REFERRAL_REQUIRED`. Nonprofits whose `pnp_service_type` is `CRITICAL` face no such prerequisite. Both branches behave identically in every other respect: each writes `eligibility_status` and `eligibility_rationale`, each re-scores the `SCREENING` variant of the applicant risk score into `risk_score_screening` and refreshes `risk_tier`, and each increments the applicant's `version` by 1. What differs is only what happens afterwards — the `ELIGIBLE` branch opens project submission, cost claiming and cash drawdown, while the `INELIGIBLE` branch performs no ledger work at all and permanently blocks all three with `ERR_APPLICANT_NOT_ELIGIBLE`.

The applicant risk score exists in two variants that share one skeleton. Three point components are weighted and summed: the applicant-type component at 30 percent, a second component at 30 percent, and a third component at 40 percent, with the weighted sum rounded half up to two decimal places. The applicant-type component is a fixed point value read from the `applicant_type`: 20 points for `LOCAL_GOVERNMENT`, 30 for `TRIBE`, 10 for `STATE_AGENCY` and 60 for `PRIVATE_NONPROFIT`. The resulting score maps to a tier: at least 60 points is `HIGH`, at least 30 but below 60 is `MEDIUM`, and anything below 30 is `LOW`. Because two scores can coexist on one applicant, `risk_tier` is derived from whichever score is the more recent evidence — it is the tier of `risk_score_monitoring` whenever `monitoring_scored_at` is non-null, and the tier of `risk_score_screening` otherwise.

The `SCREENING` variant runs at eligibility determination, before any money has moved, and its two remaining components measure the applicant's exposure footprint. The second component awards 10 points for every facility registered to the applicant, counted regardless of each facility's own `eligibility_status`, and is then capped at 100 points — so an applicant with ten or more facilities scores the maximum. The third component is all-or-nothing: it is 100 points when the applicant owns at least one facility whose `flood_zone` is true and on which no insurance policy of `policy_type` `FLOOD` exists at all, and 0 points otherwise. A city with a single unflooded, uninsured facility therefore scores 0.30 times its type points plus 0.30 times 10 plus 0.40 times 0.

### 3.2. Facility Screening and the Damage Assessment Record

Facilities are the physical unit of eligibility, and an `ApplicantAgent` registers each one against its own `applicant_id` with a non-empty `name`, a `facility_type` from `ROAD_BRIDGE`, `WATER_CONTROL`, `BUILDING_EQUIPMENT`, `UTILITY`, `PARK_RECREATION` and `EMERGENCY_PROTECTIVE`, and an `ownership` form of `PUBLIC`, `PNP_OWNED` or `LEASED`. Four documentation flags are optional and default in the applicant's favour except where noted: `in_active_use` defaults to true, `legal_responsibility_documented` defaults to true, `flood_zone` defaults to false, and `prior_disaster_flood_damage` defaults to false. A newly registered facility sits in `eligibility_status` `PENDING` with `screening_failure_reason` null and `screened_at` null.

Screening is a `ProgramSpecialist` capability that drives the facility from `PENDING` to either `ELIGIBLE` or `INELIGIBLE`, and it is admissible only from `PENDING`. The rules are evaluated in exactly this order and the **first** failure decides both the outcome and the `screening_failure_reason` recorded on the row:

1. If `legal_responsibility_documented` is false, the facility is `INELIGIBLE` with reason `LEGAL_RESPONSIBILITY_NOT_DOCUMENTED`.
2. Otherwise, if `in_active_use` is false, the facility is `INELIGIBLE` with reason `NOT_IN_ACTIVE_USE`.
3. Otherwise, if the owning applicant's `applicant_type` is `PRIVATE_NONPROFIT`, three nonprofit-only rules run in their own fixed order: if `ownership` is anything other than `PNP_OWNED`, the facility is `INELIGIBLE` with reason `PNP_MUST_OWN_FACILITY`; else if the applicant's `tax_exempt_letter_on_file` is false, the facility is `INELIGIBLE` with reason `TAX_EXEMPT_DOCUMENTATION_MISSING`; else if the applicant's `pnp_service_type` is `NON_ELIGIBLE`, the facility is `INELIGIBLE` with reason `PNP_SERVICE_NOT_ELIGIBLE`.
4. Otherwise the facility is `ELIGIBLE` and `screening_failure_reason` is null.

Screening inspects only the facility's own attributes and three fields of the owning applicant — `applicant_type`, `tax_exempt_letter_on_file` and `pnp_service_type`. It never looks at projects or damage assessment records. Whichever branch fires, `screened_at` is stamped with the logical clock and the facility's `version` increments by 1; the `PENDING → ELIGIBLE` branch additionally clears `screening_failure_reason` to null, and the `PENDING → INELIGIBLE` branch sets it to the first failed rule's code.

A documentation defect is curable, and that is what makes the facility state machine cyclic. An `ApplicantAgent` may amend its own facility's `in_active_use`, `legal_responsibility_documented`, `ownership`, `flood_zone` and `prior_disaster_flood_damage`, and any such amendment — from `ELIGIBLE` or from `INELIGIBLE` alike — writes the supplied attributes, forces `eligibility_status` back to `PENDING`, clears `screening_failure_reason` to null and `screened_at` to null, and increments `version` by 1. The facility may then be re-screened, giving the `INELIGIBLE → PENDING → ELIGIBLE` path that cures a missing tax-exempt letter or a mis-recorded ownership form. Amendment is not free, however: once real money depends on the facility, the record is frozen. If any project on that facility is in `VALIDATED`, `OBLIGATED` or `CLOSEOUT_REQUESTED`, the amendment is refused with `ERR_FACILITY_AMENDMENT_LOCKED`. Amendments *are* still allowed while projects on the facility sit in `UNDER_REVIEW` or `RFI_ISSUED`, and this has a deliberate consequence: because the facility has just been knocked back to `PENDING`, every such project can no longer pass cost review and will be rejected with `ERR_FACILITY_INELIGIBLE` until the facility is re-screened `ELIGIBLE`.

Damage assessment data collection is the applicant's own record of what it found. An `ApplicantAgent` files each observation against a `facility_id` with the Public Assistance work `category` of the damage — one of `A`, `B`, `C`, `D`, `E`, `F` or `G` — a `reported_loss_cents` of at least 0, and a `damage_observed_at` instant. Optionally it may record `work_completed_pct`, the share of repair work already finished at the moment of observation, which defaults to 0 and must lie from 0 through 100 inclusive. The observation instant must fall inside the declaration's **inclusive** incident period, so an observation exactly at `incident_start_at` or exactly at `incident_end_at` is accepted while anything outside is rejected with `ERR_OUTSIDE_INCIDENT_PERIOD`. A new site is created in `validation_status` `REPORTED` with `validated_loss_cents` 0 and `rejection_reason` null.

A `ProgramSpecialist` adjudicates each reported site once, supplying a `decision` of either `VALIDATE` or `REJECT`. A site whose `validation_status` is already `VALIDATED` or `REJECTED` cannot be adjudicated again and yields `ERR_DAMAGE_SITE_ALREADY_ADJUDICATED` — both of those states are terminal. The two branches diverge sharply in what they touch. `REPORTED → VALIDATED` requires a supplied `validated_loss_cents` of at least 0, writes it onto the site, sets `validation_status` to `VALIDATED`, and then refreshes the declaration-level indicator, incrementing both the site's `version` and the declaration's `version` by 1. `REPORTED → REJECTED` requires a supplied `rejection_reason`, records it, forces `validated_loss_cents` to 0 — where it stays forever — sets `validation_status` to `REJECTED`, and increments only the site's `version` by 1: the declaration's aggregates are **not** recomputed and the declaration's `version` does not move.

The indicator refresh that only the validate branch performs works as follows. The system collects every damage assessment site whose facility belongs to an applicant registered under this declaration and whose `validation_status` is `VALIDATED`; sites still `REPORTED` and sites `REJECTED` are excluded, and no time window is applied. `total_validated_loss_cents` becomes the sum of `validated_loss_cents` across that set, which is 0 when the set is empty; because it is a plain sum, no ordering or tie-break matters. `per_capita_impact_cents` becomes `total_validated_loss_cents` divided by the declaration's `population`, truncated downward to a whole number of cents. `indicator_met` becomes true when `per_capita_impact_cents` is at least the statewide per-capita indicator of 189 cents — one dollar and eighty-nine cents of validated loss per resident — and false otherwise.

### 3.3. Project Formulation, Submission and the Request-for-Information Cycle

A **project** is the unit of scope, money and obligation: one facility, one work category, one scope of work. An `ApplicantAgent` formulates it by supplying its own `applicant_id`, the `facility_id` being repaired, the work `category` (again one of `A` through `G`), a non-empty `title`, a non-empty `scope_of_work` narrative, and an `estimated_repair_cost_cents` of at least 0. Mitigation funding — measures that reduce the risk of the same damage recurring — is requested at formulation time through the optional `mitigation_requested_cents`, which defaults to 0, must be at least 0, and may not exceed `estimated_repair_cost_cents`; a larger request is rejected outright with `ERR_MITIGATION_EXCEEDS_REPAIR`. The project is created in `DRAFT` with `size_class` null, `rfi_round_count` 0, `last_rfi_question` null, `work_type` not yet derived, and every basis and ledger field at its opening value: `repair_eligible_cents`, `anticipated_insurance_cents`, `settlement_allocated_cents`, `insurance_reduction_cents`, `mitigation_approved_cents`, `eligible_cost_provisional_cents`, `federal_share_obligated_cents`, `deobligated_cents` and `pending_deobligation_cents` at 0, and `eligible_cost_final_cents`, `federal_share_final_cents`, `closeout_variance_cents`, `cost_reviewed_by_user_id`, `obligated_by_user_id` and `denial_reason` all null.

Submission moves the project `DRAFT → UNDER_REVIEW` and is the moment the eligibility chain is enforced end to end. The acting agent must own the project, the project must actually be in `DRAFT`, the owning applicant's `eligibility_status` must be `ELIGIBLE` (otherwise `ERR_APPLICANT_NOT_ELIGIBLE`), the repaired facility's `eligibility_status` must be `ELIGIBLE` (otherwise `ERR_FACILITY_INELIGIBLE`), and the declaration must still be `DECLARED` (otherwise `ERR_DISASTER_NOT_OPEN`). Submission also derives `work_type` from the category once and for all: categories `A` and `B` are `EMERGENCY` work, and categories `C`, `D`, `E`, `F` and `G` are `PERMANENT` work. That single derived value goes on to govern which force account labor hours are eligible, which is why an emergency debris-removal project and a permanent road-rebuild project reviewed with identical timesheets reach different answers.

Submission then computes the **`PROVISIONAL`** cost variant, the estimate-based forward-looking view of what the project is worth before a single receipt has been examined. Its repair basis is the project's own `estimated_repair_cost_cents`. Its insurance offset is *anticipated*, never actual: the system gathers every insurance policy on the project's facility whose closed effective interval contains the declaration's `incident_start_at`, sums each such policy's `coverage_limit_cents` less its `deductible_cents` (treating any negative result as 0), and takes `anticipated_insurance_cents` to be the lesser of that sum and the repair basis. Size classification comes next and is a pure comparison of the repair basis against the declaration's `small_project_threshold_cents`: strictly below the threshold makes `size_class` `SMALL`, and at or above it makes `size_class` `LARGE`. The mitigation cap percentage follows from that class — 20 percent for a `SMALL` project, 15 percent for a `LARGE` one — and `mitigation_approved_cents` becomes the lesser of `mitigation_requested_cents` and the repair basis multiplied by the cap percentage, truncated downward to whole cents. Finally, `eligible_cost_provisional_cents` is the repair basis less the anticipated insurance offset, floored at 0 so an over-insured project never produces a negative figure, plus the approved mitigation component. Submission increments the project's `version` by 1.

Reviewers rarely accept a first submission untouched. A `ProgramSpecialist` may issue a request for information, supplying the `question` text; this drives `UNDER_REVIEW → RFI_ISSUED`, records the text in `last_rfi_question`, raises `rfi_round_count` by 1, and increments the project's `version` by 1. The round counter is hard-capped: a project may carry at most two rounds, so a request issued while `rfi_round_count` is already 2 is refused with `ERR_RFI_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`. The applicant closes the loop by answering, supplying the `response` narrative and optionally a `revised_estimate_cents`; this drives `RFI_ISSUED → UNDER_REVIEW`, and when `revised_estimate_cents` is supplied it **replaces** `estimated_repair_cost_cents` outright. Because the estimate may have changed, answering re-runs the whole `PROVISIONAL` variant — `size_class`, `anticipated_insurance_cents`, `mitigation_approved_cents` and `eligible_cost_provisional_cents` are all recomputed from the new basis — and increments the project's `version` by 1. A project can therefore travel `UNDER_REVIEW → RFI_ISSUED → UNDER_REVIEW` twice, and a revision that crosses `small_project_threshold_cents` flips `size_class` mid-review, which in turn flips the mitigation cap percentage from 20 percent to 15 percent or back.

### 3.4. Building the Cost Record: Force Account Resources, Procurement Awards and Claimed Lines

Two kinds of cost basis exist alongside plain materials purchases, and both have to be registered before any cost can be claimed against them. A **force account resource** is something the applicant already owns — an employee or a piece of equipment — and an `ApplicantAgent` registers it under its own `applicant_id` with a `resource_kind` of either `LABOR` or `EQUIPMENT` and a non-empty `label` giving the employee's name or the equipment description. Labor resources carry an `employee_type` of `PERMANENT` or `TEMPORARY`, which is required when `resource_kind` is `LABOR` and must be null otherwise, together with a `base_hourly_rate_cents` straight-time rate (defaulting to 0, never negative) and a `fringe_benefit_pct` expressed as a fraction from 0 through 1 inclusive (defaulting to 0). Equipment resources instead carry an `equipment_code` naming the published schedule entry, the applicant's own `published_hourly_rate_cents`, and the state schedule's `schedule_hourly_rate_cents` — both defaulting to 0 and neither negative. Every resource starts with `charged_hours_total` at 0; that field is the running total of `straight_time_hours` plus `overtime_hours` plus `equipment_hours` across every cost line item that references the resource, and it is refreshed whenever one of those lines is reviewed.

A **procurement award** is the other basis. An `ApplicantAgent` registers each contract under its own `applicant_id` with a non-empty `vendor_name`, the `contract_type` describing the procurement method used, an `award_amount_cents` of at least 0, and the `awarded_at` instant. Four compliance attributes are optional: `bids_received`, the count of responsive bids, defaulting to 0 and never negative; `advertised`, recording whether the solicitation was publicly advertised, defaulting to false; `sole_source_justification_on_file`, defaulting to false; and `debarment_check_completed`, recording whether the vendor-exclusion check was run, also defaulting to false. The six recognized methods are `SEALED_BID`, `COMPETITIVE_PROPOSAL`, `NONCOMPETITIVE`, `SMALL_PURCHASE`, `TIME_AND_MATERIALS` and `COST_PLUS_PERCENTAGE_OF_COST`. A new contract sits in `procurement_status` `PENDING_REVIEW` with `noncompliance_reason` null and `allowed_cost_total_cents` 0, the last of which accumulates the `eligible_cents` of every cost line item on the contract whose `review_status` is `ALLOWED` or `PARTIALLY_ALLOWED`.

Procurement compliance review is a `ProgramSpecialist` capability that drives the contract out of `PENDING_REVIEW` exactly once — a second attempt on a contract already `COMPLIANT` or `NONCOMPLIANT` is refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_ALREADY_REVIEWED`. It reads nothing but the contract row itself, and it evaluates five rules in exactly this order, the first failure deciding both `procurement_status` and `noncompliance_reason`:

1. A `contract_type` of `COST_PLUS_PERCENTAGE_OF_COST` is categorically forbidden: `NONCOMPLIANT` with reason `PROHIBITED_CONTRACT_TYPE`.
2. Otherwise, a `TIME_AND_MATERIALS` award whose `award_amount_cents` is **strictly greater** than the small-purchase ceiling of 2,500,000 cents — twenty-five thousand dollars — is `NONCOMPLIANT` with reason `TIME_AND_MATERIALS_CEILING_EXCEEDED`. An award of exactly twenty-five thousand dollars passes this rule.
3. Otherwise, if `award_amount_cents` is **at or above** the simplified acquisition threshold of 25,000,000 cents — two hundred fifty thousand dollars — two competition rules apply in their own order. A `NONCOMPETITIVE` award whose `sole_source_justification_on_file` is false is `NONCOMPLIANT` with reason `SOLE_SOURCE_UNJUSTIFIED`. Otherwise, a `SEALED_BID` or `COMPETITIVE_PROPOSAL` award that either was not `advertised` or received fewer than two responsive bids is `NONCOMPLIANT` with reason `INADEQUATE_COMPETITION`.
4. Otherwise, if `award_amount_cents` is **at or above** the small-purchase ceiling of twenty-five thousand dollars and `debarment_check_completed` is false, the contract is `NONCOMPLIANT` with reason `DEBARMENT_CHECK_MISSING`. Note that this boundary is inclusive while rule 2's is strict, so an award of exactly twenty-five thousand dollars escapes rule 2 but is caught by rule 4 when the exclusion check was skipped.
5. Otherwise the contract is `COMPLIANT` and `noncompliance_reason` is null.

Either outcome increments the contract's `version` by 1. A `NONCOMPLIANT` finding has a forward-looking consequence: from that moment on, any attempt to claim a `CONTRACT` cost against that contract is refused at creation time with `ERR_PROCUREMENT_NONCOMPLIANT`.

Costs themselves are claimed as **cost line items**, each filed by an `ApplicantAgent` against a `project_id` with a `cost_type` of `FORCE_ACCOUNT_LABOR`, `FORCE_ACCOUNT_EQUIPMENT`, `MATERIALS` or `CONTRACT`, a `claimed_cents` amount of at least 0, and the charged work interval bounded by `work_start_at` and `work_end_at`, where the end must be strictly later than the start. The claim class selects which foreign key is required: `force_account_resource_id` for both force account classes and `contract_id` for a contracted cost, with the unused key left null and both left null for materials — a mismatch is an invalid request. Three optional hour fields, each defaulting to 0 and never negative, carry the timesheet: `straight_time_hours` and `overtime_hours` for labor, and `equipment_hours` for equipment. A new line is created in `review_status` `SUBMITTED` with `eligible_cents` 0, `disallowance_reason` null and `reviewed_at` null.

Six guards stand between a claim and the record. The owning applicant must be `ELIGIBLE`, or the claim fails with `ERR_APPLICANT_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. The project must be open to cost accumulation, meaning its `status` is `DRAFT`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `RFI_ISSUED` or `OBLIGATED`; a project sitting in `VALIDATED`, `CLOSEOUT_REQUESTED`, `CLOSED` or `DENIED` has its cost record frozen and the claim is refused with `ERR_COST_LINE_ITEM_LOCKED`. The entire charged interval must lie inside the declaration's inclusive incident period — both endpoints included — and any part of it falling outside yields `ERR_OUTSIDE_INCIDENT_PERIOD`. Cross-aggregate references must stay inside one applicant: the referenced force account resource or contract must belong to the same applicant as the project, or the claim fails with `ERR_CROSS_APPLICANT_REFERENCE`. A contracted claim against a `NONCOMPLIANT` contract fails with `ERR_PROCUREMENT_NONCOMPLIANT`.

The last two guards are the physical-plausibility rules on force account resources, and they are deliberately different in shape because labor and equipment behave differently. A **labor** line may not overlap another labor line on the same resource, and the prohibition is widened by a thirty-minute rest buffer on each side: the new line's interval, extended half an hour earlier and half an hour later, must not intersect the closed interval of any existing labor line on that resource. In practice, two back-to-back shifts collide, two shifts exactly thirty minutes apart still collide, and a shift starting thirty-one minutes after the previous one ended is accepted. A collision yields `ERR_LABOR_ENTRY_OVERLAP`. An **equipment** line, by contrast, may overlap other equipment lines freely — a backhoe can be charged to two tasks in the same hour on paper — but the aggregate is capped per day: for every UTC calendar day that the new line's interval touches, the sum of `equipment_hours` over that resource's existing lines touching that same day, plus the new line's own `equipment_hours`, may not exceed sixteen hours. A total of exactly sixteen is accepted; anything above it yields `ERR_EQUIPMENT_DAILY_HOURS_EXCEEDED`.

### 3.5. Cost Eligibility Review of a Claimed Line

Reviewing one claimed line is a `ProgramSpecialist` capability and it is where the substantive cost-eligibility rules live. The line's project must still be open to cost accumulation — `DRAFT`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `RFI_ISSUED` or `OBLIGATED` — or the review is refused with `ERR_COST_LINE_ITEM_LOCKED`. A contracted line additionally may not be reviewed while its contract is still `PENDING_REVIEW`: the procurement question has to be settled first, and an attempt yields `ERR_PROCUREMENT_REVIEW_PENDING`. Review states are **not terminal**; a line may be reviewed again for as long as its project remains open, which matters for the contract ceiling described below.

A **force account labor** line is computed from the referenced resource's rates, and the very first decision is whether straight time is eligible at all. When the resource's `employee_type` is `PERMANENT` **and** the project's `work_type` is `EMERGENCY`, the billable straight-time hours are forced to zero — a permanent employee's regular salary is not a disaster cost for emergency work, only the premium for working extra hours is — and the reduction reason is pinned to `STRAIGHT_TIME_NOT_ELIGIBLE_FOR_EMERGENCY_WORK`. In every other combination (a `TEMPORARY` employee hired for the disaster, or a `PERMANENT` employee on `PERMANENT` work) the full `straight_time_hours` are billable and the reduction reason starts out as `CLAIM_EXCEEDS_COMPUTED_COST`. Overtime hours are always billable in full. Three cost components are then formed and — this is the pinned order — **each is rounded half up to whole cents before any of them are added**: the straight-time component is the billable straight hours multiplied by `base_hourly_rate_cents`; the overtime component is `overtime_hours` multiplied by `base_hourly_rate_cents` and then by the overtime multiplier of one and one half; and the fringe component is the sum of the two already-rounded wage components multiplied by the applied fringe rate. The applied fringe rate is the lesser of the resource's `fringe_benefit_pct` and the fringe cap of 40 percent, and whenever the resource's own `fringe_benefit_pct` exceeds that 40 percent cap the reduction reason is overwritten with `FRINGE_RATE_CAPPED`, superseding whatever it was before. The computed cost is the exact integer sum of the three rounded components; summing the three raw products and rounding once is not the rule.

A **force account equipment** line applies the lesser-of rule: the applied rate is the smaller of the resource's `published_hourly_rate_cents` and its `schedule_hourly_rate_cents`, and the computed cost is `equipment_hours` multiplied by that applied rate, rounded half up to whole cents. When the applicant's published rate is strictly greater than the state schedule rate — that is, when the lesser-of rule actually bit — the reduction reason is `LESSER_OF_RATE_APPLIED`; otherwise it is `CLAIM_EXCEEDS_COMPUTED_COST`.

A **materials** line is taken at face value: the computed cost is simply the line's own `claimed_cents`, and the reduction reason, should one ever be needed, is `CLAIM_EXCEEDS_COMPUTED_COST`.

A **contract** line is governed by a cumulative award ceiling shared by every line drawn on the same contract. If the contract's `procurement_status` is `NONCOMPLIANT`, the computed cost is 0 and the reduction reason is `PROCUREMENT_NONCOMPLIANT`. Otherwise the system totals the `eligible_cents` of the contract's *other* lines whose `review_status` is `ALLOWED` or `PARTIALLY_ALLOWED` **and whose `created_at` is strictly earlier than this line's `created_at`** — creation order, never identifier order, is what consumes the ceiling. The remaining headroom is the contract's `award_amount_cents` less that already-allowed total, floored at 0, and the computed cost is the lesser of the line's `claimed_cents` and that headroom. When the headroom is smaller than the claim the reduction reason is `CONTRACT_AWARD_CEILING_REACHED`; otherwise it is `CLAIM_EXCEEDS_COMPUTED_COST`. Because the ceiling counts only earlier-created siblings, reviewing lines out of creation order can allow a line that a later re-review of the same line must take back: allowing an earlier-created sibling after this line was already allowed drops this line's headroom to 0, and re-reviewing it then drives `ALLOWED → DISALLOWED` with `disallowance_reason` `CONTRACT_AWARD_CEILING_REACHED`.

Whichever branch ran, the outcome is settled the same way. The line's `eligible_cents` is the lesser of the computed cost and the line's `claimed_cents` — the program never pays above what was claimed. The `review_status` is then `ALLOWED` when `eligible_cents` equals `claimed_cents`, `DISALLOWED` when `eligible_cents` is 0, and `PARTIALLY_ALLOWED` in between; the equality test comes first, so a line claiming nothing and computing nothing lands in `ALLOWED`. `disallowance_reason` is null on an `ALLOWED` outcome and otherwise carries the reduction reason pinned by the branch. Review stamps `reviewed_at` with the logical clock, refreshes the referenced resource's `charged_hours_total` and, for a contracted line, the contract's `allowed_cost_total_cents`, and increments the line's `version` by 1.

### 3.6. Insurance, the Obtain-and-Maintain Bar and the Duplication-of-Benefits Waterfall

Insurance is the program's largest source of duplicated benefits, so policies are recorded as first-class rows. An `ApplicantAgent` registers each policy against a `facility_id` with a non-empty `insurer_name`, a `policy_type` of either `FLOOD` or `PROPERTY`, a `coverage_limit_cents` of at least 0, and the inclusive coverage bounds `effective_start_at` and `effective_end_at`. Optionally it records a `deductible_cents` (defaulting to 0, never negative) and an `is_nfip` flag (defaulting to false) noting that the policy was written through the national flood insurance program. A new policy carries `settlement_amount_cents` 0, `settled_at` null, `allocated_cents` 0 and `unallocated_cents` 0.

Policy type matters for one hard bar. A facility that both sits in a special flood hazard area, with `flood_zone` true, and has previously received flood assistance, with `prior_disaster_flood_damage` true, is under a statutory obligation to obtain and maintain flood coverage. Such a facility may not have a project obligated unless at least one insurance policy on it has `policy_type` `FLOOD` and an effective interval whose closed bounds contain the declaration's `incident_start_at`. A `PROPERTY` policy does not satisfy the bar however generous its limit, and `is_nfip` is recorded for reference but is not itself the test. A project failing this bar is refused at obligation with `ERR_INSURANCE_REQUIREMENT_UNMET`.

Recording actual proceeds is a `GrantsOfficer` capability that supplies the `settlement_amount_cents` received and the `settled_at` instant, and it is guarded twice. The settlement may not exceed the policy's `coverage_limit_cents`, or the request fails with `ERR_SETTLEMENT_EXCEEDS_COVERAGE`. And the policy must actually have been in force when the disaster struck: its closed effective interval must contain the declaration's `incident_start_at`, or the request fails with `ERR_POLICY_NOT_EFFECTIVE_AT_INCIDENT`.

Once accepted, the proceeds are pushed through a **waterfall** across the projects on the settled policy's facility. The candidate set is every project on that facility whose `status` is `UNDER_REVIEW`, `RFI_ISSUED`, `VALIDATED`, `OBLIGATED` or `CLOSEOUT_REQUESTED`; projects still in `DRAFT`, and projects already `CLOSED` or `DENIED`, never absorb proceeds. Candidates are visited in ascending work `category` order — `A` before `B` before `C` before `D` before `E` before `F` before `G` — and within a category in ascending `created_at` order; the row identifier is never an ordering key. The available balance starts at the full `settlement_amount_cents`. For each candidate in turn, the room remaining on that project is its `repair_eligible_cents` less whatever `settlement_allocated_cents` it has already absorbed, floored at 0; the amount taken is the lesser of the available balance and that room; the project's `settlement_allocated_cents` rises by the amount taken; the project's `insurance_reduction_cents` is reset to the greater of its `anticipated_insurance_cents` and its new `settlement_allocated_cents`; and the available balance falls by the amount taken. When the walk is finished, the policy's `allocated_cents` is the settlement amount less whatever balance remains, and its `unallocated_cents` is that remaining balance — proceeds with nowhere to go simply stay on the policy.

The waterfall has teeth for projects where money has already gone out. Whenever a candidate whose `status` is `OBLIGATED` or `CLOSEOUT_REQUESTED` absorbs a non-zero amount, the system recomputes what that project's federal share *would* be on its new insurance reduction, using exactly the obligation formula of §3.7, and compares it with what is actually still obligated. The shortfall is the project's `federal_share_obligated_cents` less its `deobligated_cents` less that recomputed share; when the shortfall is positive, the system immediately creates a deobligation determination on that project with `reason` `INSURANCE_DOB`, an `amount_cents` equal to the shortfall, a `status` of `PROPOSED`, and `determined_by_user_id` set to the acting officer's `X-User-ID`. That last detail has a consequence the officer should understand: having determined it, they are barred from later adjudicating any appeal against it. Each such determination raises the project's `pending_deobligation_cents` and, through the applicant's ledger, the applicant's `pending_deobligation_cents`, which drives `DRAWDOWN:ACTIVE → DRAWDOWN:SUSPENDED` on that applicant and refreshes the declaration's `suspended_applicant_count`. The policy's own `version` increments by 1, as does the `version` of every project, applicant and declaration the walk touched. Candidates in `UNDER_REVIEW`, `RFI_ISSUED` or `VALIDATED` absorb proceeds and have their `insurance_reduction_cents` raised, but generate no determination — there is nothing obligated yet to reduce.

### 3.7. Cost Review, the Mitigation Cap and Obligation of the Federal Share

Cost review is the hinge of the whole program: it is where an estimate becomes a basis. A `ProgramSpecialist` supplies a `decision` of `VALIDATE` or `DENY`, with a `denial_reason` required on the deny path. The project must be in `UNDER_REVIEW`. Two further checks guard the operation: no cost line item on the project may still be sitting in `review_status` `SUBMITTED`, because reviewing the project before its lines have been examined would fix a basis on unexamined claims (`ERR_UNREVIEWED_COST_LINE_ITEMS`), and the project's facility must be `ELIGIBLE` at this moment (`ERR_FACILITY_INELIGIBLE`) — which is precisely how a mid-review facility amendment blocks the review that was already in flight.

The `UNDER_REVIEW → VALIDATED` branch fixes the obligation basis in two ordered steps. **Step one** establishes the repair basis and the insurance picture. The system totals the `eligible_cents` of every cost line item on the project whose `review_status` is `ALLOWED` or `PARTIALLY_ALLOWED`, and `repair_eligible_cents` becomes that total. `DISALLOWED` lines contribute nothing, and no `SUBMITTED` line can exist at this point. If that reviewed set is empty — a project validated on its estimate alone, with no documented costs yet — `repair_eligible_cents` falls back to the project's `estimated_repair_cost_cents`; this fallback is the pinned behaviour, not an error condition. Next, `anticipated_insurance_cents` is recomputed on the new repair basis: the system sums, over every insurance policy on the project's facility whose closed effective interval contains the declaration's `incident_start_at`, that policy's `coverage_limit_cents` less its `deductible_cents` with any negative result treated as 0, and takes the lesser of that sum and `repair_eligible_cents`. `insurance_reduction_cents` — the figure that will actually be subtracted at obligation — is then the greater of `anticipated_insurance_cents` and the project's `settlement_allocated_cents`, so a settlement already pushed through the waterfall can never be undercut by a smaller anticipated estimate. Finally `size_class` is re-derived: `SMALL` when `repair_eligible_cents` is strictly below the declaration's `small_project_threshold_cents`, and `LARGE` when it is at or above it. This re-derivation is why the classification made provisionally at submission is only provisional — a project estimated at forty-five thousand dollars but documented at thirty-eight thousand becomes `SMALL` here.

**Step two** sizes the mitigation award, and its evaluation order is pinned precisely because the dependency looks circular and is not. The size class selects the cap percentage, the cap percentage sizes the mitigation award, and the mitigation award is exactly what can push the project's total cost across the small-project threshold. The order is: take `repair_eligible_cents` as step one fixed it; classify `size_class` from that repair basis **with mitigation excluded**; read the cap percentage from the class, which is 20 percent for `SMALL` and 15 percent for `LARGE`; and set `mitigation_approved_cents` to the lesser of the project's `mitigation_requested_cents` and the repair basis multiplied by the cap percentage, truncated downward to whole cents. The total project cost is then the repair basis plus the approved mitigation. That total may well exceed `small_project_threshold_cents`, and when it does the `size_class` from the earlier step **stands unchanged** — the classification is never re-derived from the total. Exceeding the cap is not an error either: a request above the cap is silently reduced to the cap, and the project is validated. Validation records `cost_reviewed_by_user_id` from the acting specialist's `X-User-ID`, sets `status` to `VALIDATED`, and increments the project's `version` by 1.

The `UNDER_REVIEW → DENIED` branch is deliberately inert. It records the supplied `denial_reason`, sets `status` to `DENIED`, records `cost_reviewed_by_user_id` from the acting specialist's `X-User-ID`, and increments the project's `version` by 1 — and that is all. `repair_eligible_cents`, `mitigation_approved_cents` and `insurance_reduction_cents` are left exactly as they were, neither recomputed nor cleared, and no ledger field on the applicant or the declaration moves by a single cent. `DENIED` is terminal.

Obligation is a `GrantsOfficer` capability driving `VALIDATED → OBLIGATED`, and it is where the federal government's money is committed. The project must be `VALIDATED`, the declaration must still be `DECLARED` (`ERR_DISASTER_NOT_OPEN`), and the obtain-and-maintain flood insurance bar of §3.6 must be satisfied (`ERR_INSURANCE_REQUIREMENT_UNMET`). The obligated amount is computed on the frozen basis with the same pinned per-component rounding as everywhere else money is shared. First, the net repair figure is `repair_eligible_cents` less `insurance_reduction_cents`, floored at 0 so a fully insured repair contributes nothing rather than a negative. Then the repair share is that net repair figure multiplied by the declaration's `federal_cost_share_pct` and rounded half up to whole cents, **and separately** the mitigation share is `mitigation_approved_cents` multiplied by the same `federal_cost_share_pct` and rounded half up to whole cents. `federal_share_obligated_cents` is the exact integer sum of those two independently rounded values; adding the two products first and rounding once is not the rule and can differ by a cent. The project's `status` becomes `OBLIGATED` and `obligated_by_user_id` records the acting officer's `X-User-ID`, which permanently bars that officer from disbursing this project's drawdowns.

Obligation then sets off the system's deepest cascade, and each hop happens **because** the previous hop happened. Because `federal_share_obligated_cents` moved, the applicant's grant ledger is recomputed, refreshing `total_obligated_cents` and `available_drawdown_cents`. Because `total_obligated_cents` moved, the single-audit test runs: when it now stands at or above 75,000,000 cents — seven hundred fifty thousand dollars of federal award — and the applicant's `monitoring_status` is still `STANDARD`, `audit_requirement` becomes `SINGLE_AUDIT` and `monitoring_status` escalates `MONITORING:STANDARD → MONITORING:ENHANCED`. Because `monitoring_status` became `ENHANCED`, the system creates one progress report row for this applicant in status `DUE`, for the quarter containing the current logical clock, with `quarter_label`, `period_start_at`, `period_end_at` and `due_at` all derived by the quarter rules of §3.8. Because that report row now exists, the declaration's `enhanced_monitoring_applicant_count` is recomputed; the declaration's `total_obligated_cents` is likewise recomputed from its applicants' ledgers. The project's `version`, the applicant's `version` and the declaration's `version` each increment by 1. An applicant already `ENHANCED` when a second project is obligated does not escalate again and no second report row appears — the escalation hop did not fire, so neither did the hops that depend on it.

### 3.8. Drawing Cash, Refunding It, and the Quarterly Reporting Regime

The applicant's grant ledger is a single derived picture recomputed inside every request that moves a project obligation, a determination's status, a drawdown's status or a progress report's status. `total_obligated_cents` is the sum, over the applicant's projects in status `OBLIGATED`, `CLOSEOUT_REQUESTED` or `CLOSED`, of `federal_share_obligated_cents` less `deobligated_cents`; projects in `DRAFT`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `RFI_ISSUED`, `VALIDATED` or `DENIED` contribute nothing. `total_drawn_cents` is the sum of `approved_cents` over the applicant's drawdowns in status `PAID` or `REFUNDED`. `total_refunded_cents` is the sum of `refunded_cents` over the applicant's drawdowns in status `REFUNDED`. `cash_on_hand_cents` is `total_drawn_cents` less `total_refunded_cents`, and `available_drawdown_cents` is `total_obligated_cents` less `cash_on_hand_cents`. `pending_deobligation_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` over determinations in status `PROPOSED` on any of the applicant's projects, and `delinquent_report_count` is the count of the applicant's progress reports in status `DELINQUENT`. `REQUESTED` and `REJECTED` drawdowns contribute nothing anywhere.

That ledger drives one bright-line switch. After **every** recomputation, `drawdown_status` is set to `SUSPENDED` whenever `delinquent_report_count` is greater than 0 or `pending_deobligation_cents` is greater than 0, and to `ACTIVE` whenever both are exactly 0. There is no manual override. Each crossing of that line is a transition in its own right — `DRAWDOWN:ACTIVE → DRAWDOWN:SUSPENDED` or `DRAWDOWN:SUSPENDED → DRAWDOWN:ACTIVE` — which increments the applicant's `version` by 1 and causes the declaration's `suspended_applicant_count` to be recomputed. The same recomputation also applies the high-water audit rule: `audit_requirement` stays `SINGLE_AUDIT` once it has ever been `SINGLE_AUDIT`, and otherwise becomes `SINGLE_AUDIT` when `total_obligated_cents` reaches the seven-hundred-fifty-thousand-dollar threshold.

Requesting cash is an `ApplicantAgent` action. The request carries the agent's own `applicant_id`, the `project_id` of the obligated project the cash is drawn against, a `requested_cents` amount of at least 1, and the mandatory `expected_applicant_version` described in §2.8. Five substantive checks follow the version check. The applicant must be `ELIGIBLE` (`ERR_APPLICANT_NOT_ELIGIBLE`); the referenced project must be `OBLIGATED` (`ERR_PROJECT_INVALID_STATE`); the project must belong to the same applicant as the drawdown (`ERR_CROSS_APPLICANT_REFERENCE`); the applicant's `drawdown_status` must be `ACTIVE` (`ERR_DRAWDOWN_SUSPENDED`); and `requested_cents` must not exceed `available_drawdown_cents`, where equality is expressly allowed — drawing the last available cent is legitimate — with an excess rejected as `ERR_DRAWDOWN_EXCEEDS_AVAILABLE` and the payment-required status 402. A created drawdown sits in `REQUESTED` with `approved_cents` 0, `refunded_cents` 0, `disbursed_by_user_id` null and `rejection_reason` null, and the create increments the applicant's `version` by 1 — which is why a client intending to file two drawdowns in a row must re-read the applicant between them.

Disbursement is a `GrantsOfficer` decision, supplied as a `decision` of `PAY` or `REJECT` with a `rejection_reason` required on the reject path. Only a drawdown in `REQUESTED` may be decided; anything else yields `ERR_DRAWDOWN_INVALID_STATE`. The `REQUESTED → PAID` branch additionally requires that the acting officer's `X-User-ID` differ from the project's `obligated_by_user_id` (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`) and that the applicant's `drawdown_status` still be `ACTIVE` at the moment of payment (`ERR_DRAWDOWN_SUSPENDED`) — a suspension that landed between request and disbursement stops the payment. Paying sets `approved_cents` to the full `requested_cents`, sets `status` to `PAID`, and records `disbursed_by_user_id`; because `approved_cents` moved, the applicant's `total_drawn_cents`, `cash_on_hand_cents` and `available_drawdown_cents` are recomputed, and because `cash_on_hand_cents` moved, the declaration's `total_drawn_cents` is recomputed; the drawdown's, the applicant's and the declaration's `version` each increment by 1. The `REQUESTED → REJECTED` branch is the mirror image in inertness: it records the `rejection_reason`, sets `status` to `REJECTED`, leaves `approved_cents` at 0, moves **no** ledger field on either the applicant or the declaration, and increments neither of their versions — only the drawdown's own `version` rises by 1.

Refunding is the compensating action for a payment and belongs to the `ApplicantAgent`. It carries a single `refund_cents` amount, which may not exceed the drawdown's `approved_cents` (`ERR_REFUND_EXCEEDS_DRAWDOWN`), and it is admissible only on a drawdown in `PAID`; a refund attempted against a `REQUESTED`, `REJECTED` or already-`REFUNDED` row yields `ERR_DRAWDOWN_INVALID_STATE`. The `PAID → REFUNDED` transition writes `refunded_cents` and sets `status` to `REFUNDED`; the applicant's `total_refunded_cents`, `cash_on_hand_cents` and `available_drawdown_cents` are recomputed, the declaration's `total_drawn_cents` follows, and the drawdown's, applicant's and declaration's `version` each increment by 1. Note that a refunded drawdown still counts toward `total_drawn_cents` — the pair of figures records that cash went out and came back, while `cash_on_hand_cents` records the net truth.

Quarterly progress reporting exists in two flavours that meet in the same table. A report row can be **created by the system** as part of the monitoring escalation of §3.7, in which case it is born in status `DUE` with `submitted_at` null and no reported data. Or it can be **filed by the applicant** for a quarter the system did not pre-create, in which case the `ApplicantAgent` supplies its own `applicant_id`, the `quarter_label`, and optionally `expenditures_to_date_cents` (defaulting to 0, never negative) and `pct_complete` (defaulting to 0, from 0 through 100 inclusive). Only one report may exist per applicant per quarter; a second attempt at the same pair of `applicant_id` and `quarter_label` is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_REPORT_PERIOD`.

The quarter derivation is purely a function of `quarter_label`, which must read as four digits, a hyphen, the letter `Q`, and a digit from 1 to 4. `period_start_at` is the first instant of that quarter of that year in UTC; `period_end_at` is the first instant of the following quarter, and because the period is half-open that end instant belongs to the next quarter rather than this one; and `due_at` is `period_end_at` plus the reporting grace period of thirty days. When an applicant files a report directly, its status is decided on the spot: `SUBMITTED` when the logical clock is at or before `due_at`, and `LATE` when the clock is past `due_at`, with `submitted_at` stamped from the clock either way.

Filing data against a row the system created follows the same clock test. Submission is admissible only against a row in `DUE` or `DELINQUENT`; a row already `SUBMITTED` or `LATE` yields `ERR_REPORT_ALREADY_SUBMITTED`. The submission writes `expenditures_to_date_cents` and `pct_complete`, stamps `submitted_at` from the clock, and drives `DUE → SUBMITTED` when the clock is at or before `due_at` or `DUE → LATE` when it is past. A `DELINQUENT → LATE` filing is the interesting one, because delinquency by definition means the deadline has already passed: it writes the same fields, and then, because the report left `DELINQUENT`, the applicant's `delinquent_report_count` is recomputed; because that count returned to 0 and `pending_deobligation_cents` is also 0, `drawdown_status` returns to `ACTIVE`; and because `drawdown_status` became `ACTIVE`, the declaration's `suspended_applicant_count` is recomputed. The report's `version` increments by 1, and the applicant's and declaration's versions increment by 1 as the ledger moves.

Delinquency itself is declared by a `PlatformAdmin` capability that advances the reporting epoch for one applicant. Every progress report of that applicant whose `status` is `DUE` and whose `due_at` is now behind the logical clock is driven `DUE → DELINQUENT`. Because a report became `DELINQUENT`, `delinquent_report_count` is recomputed; because that count is now above 0, `drawdown_status` becomes `SUSPENDED`; because it became `SUSPENDED`, the declaration's `suspended_applicant_count` is recomputed. The sweep also re-scores the applicant on the `MONITORING` variant, whatever it found: `risk_score_monitoring` is rewritten, `monitoring_scored_at` is stamped from the clock, and `risk_tier` is refreshed — and because `monitoring_scored_at` is now non-null, `risk_tier` follows the monitoring score from here on rather than the screening score. Each swept report's `version`, the applicant's `version` and the declaration's `version` increment by 1.

The `MONITORING` variant of the risk score shares the skeleton of §3.1 — 30 percent on the applicant-type points, 30 percent on the second component, 40 percent on the third, rounded half up to two decimal places, with the same tier boundaries of at least 60 for `HIGH` and at least 30 for `MEDIUM` — but its two behavioural components look at grant conduct rather than exposure. The second component awards 25 points for each of the applicant's `DELINQUENT` reports, capped at 100 points, so four delinquent reports max it out. The third component measures how much cash the applicant is sitting on relative to what it has been awarded: when `total_obligated_cents` is exactly 0 while `cash_on_hand_cents` is above 0 — cash held against no live obligation at all, the worst possible signal — the component is a flat 100 points; otherwise it is one hundred multiplied by `cash_on_hand_cents`, divided by `total_obligated_cents` (treating a zero denominator as 1), truncated downward, and then capped at 100.

### 3.9. Closeout, Deobligation and the Two-Level Appeal

When the work is done and the receipts are in, the `ApplicantAgent` requests closeout, driving the project `OBLIGATED → CLOSEOUT_REQUESTED`. At least one cost line item on the project must carry a `review_status` of `ALLOWED` or `PARTIALLY_ALLOWED`, because closeout is an accounting against documented actuals and there is nothing to account for otherwise; an undocumented project is refused with `ERR_NO_DOCUMENTED_COSTS`. The request computes the **`FINAL`** cost variant into `eligible_cost_final_cents`, and its three inputs differ from the `PROVISIONAL` variant in every respect. Its repair basis is the sum of `eligible_cents` over the project's `ALLOWED` and `PARTIALLY_ALLOWED` lines, with **no** estimate fallback — the empty case cannot arise because it was rejected upstream. Its insurance offset is the project's `settlement_allocated_cents`, that is, **actual proceeds only**; `anticipated_insurance_cents` is deliberately ignored here, which is the whole point of having two variants. And its mitigation component is `mitigation_approved_cents` exactly as frozen at cost review, never recomputed from the actual basis. `eligible_cost_final_cents` is therefore the documented repair basis less the actual settlement allocation, floored at 0, plus the frozen mitigation component. The project's `version` increments by 1. The two variants live side by side on the project and are expected to differ whenever documented actuals or recorded settlements differ from the estimate and the anticipated proceeds.

Certification belongs to the `StateAuditor`, who supplies an `audit_finding` and drives `CLOSEOUT_REQUESTED → CLOSED`. What happens next depends entirely on the size class fixed at cost review, and the two branches are genuinely different policies rather than two cases of one policy. A **`SMALL`** project is funded on its estimate and is never reconciled against actuals: `federal_share_final_cents` is set equal to `federal_share_obligated_cents`, `closeout_variance_cents` is set to 0, and **no** determination is created no matter how far the documented actuals fell below the estimate. That is the entire benefit of small-project status. A **`LARGE`** project is reconciled: the net final figure is `eligible_cost_final_cents` less `mitigation_approved_cents`, floored at 0; `federal_share_final_cents` is that net final figure multiplied by the declaration's `federal_cost_share_pct` and rounded half up to whole cents, **plus separately** `mitigation_approved_cents` multiplied by the same share and rounded half up to whole cents — the identical per-component rounding order used at obligation, so that an unchanged basis reconciles to exactly the obligated figure. `closeout_variance_cents` is `federal_share_final_cents` less `federal_share_obligated_cents`. A positive variance is recorded and nothing more: closeout never obligates additional money. A negative variance is an underrun, and the system immediately creates a determination on the project with `reason` `CLOSEOUT_UNDERRUN`, an `amount_cents` equal to the magnitude of that negative variance, `status` `PROPOSED`, and `determined_by_user_id` set to the certifying auditor's `X-User-ID`. Because that determination exists, the project's `pending_deobligation_cents` and the applicant's `pending_deobligation_cents` are recomputed; because the applicant's pending figure is now above 0, `drawdown_status` becomes `SUSPENDED` and the declaration's `suspended_applicant_count` is recomputed. The project's `version` increments by 1, and the applicant's `version` increments by 1 whenever the ledger moved.

Determinations can also be recorded by hand, by either a `GrantsOfficer` or a `StateAuditor`. Such a record carries the `project_id`, a `reason` from `CLOSEOUT_UNDERRUN`, `INSURANCE_DOB`, `DISALLOWED_COST`, `PROCUREMENT_NONCOMPLIANCE` and `SCOPE_REDUCTION`, an `amount_cents` of at least 1, and the mandatory `expected_project_version` of §2.8. The referenced project must be in `OBLIGATED`, `CLOSEOUT_REQUESTED` or `CLOSED` — there is no obligation to reduce otherwise — and a project in any other status yields `ERR_PROJECT_INVALID_STATE`. The amount is bounded by what is actually still reducible: it may not exceed `federal_share_obligated_cents` less `deobligated_cents` less `pending_deobligation_cents`, so two proposed determinations cannot between them exceed the obligation, and a breach yields `ERR_DEOBLIGATION_EXCEEDS_OBLIGATION`. A new determination sits in `PROPOSED` with `determined_by_user_id` recording the acting user, `finalized_at` null, `appeal_deadline_at` null and `reversed_cents` 0. Because it exists, the project's and the applicant's `pending_deobligation_cents` are recomputed, `drawdown_status` becomes `SUSPENDED` and the declaration's `suspended_applicant_count` is refreshed; the project's `version` and the applicant's `version` each increment by 1.

Finalization is a `GrantsOfficer` action driving `PROPOSED → FINAL`, admissible only while the project is `OBLIGATED`, `CLOSEOUT_REQUESTED` or `CLOSED`. It stamps `finalized_at` from the logical clock and sets `appeal_deadline_at` to that instant plus the sixty-day appeal window. Because the determination became final, the money actually leaves the obligation: the project's `deobligated_cents` rises by `amount_cents` and its `pending_deobligation_cents` falls by the same amount. Because `deobligated_cents` moved, the applicant's `total_obligated_cents`, `pending_deobligation_cents` and `available_drawdown_cents` are recomputed; because the applicant's pending figure has returned to 0 and `delinquent_report_count` is also 0, `drawdown_status` returns to `ACTIVE` and the declaration's `suspended_applicant_count` is recomputed; the declaration's `total_obligated_cents` is recomputed as well. The determination's, the project's, the applicant's and the declaration's `version` each increment by 1.

An applicant that disagrees files an appeal, supplying the `deobligation_id` under challenge, a non-empty `grounds` narrative, a `relief_requested_cents` of at least 1 that may not exceed the determination's `amount_cents` (`ERR_RELIEF_EXCEEDS_DEOBLIGATION`), and optionally a `prior_appeal_id`. The `level` is derived, not supplied: it is `FIRST` when `prior_appeal_id` is null and `SECOND` otherwise. Only a `FINAL` determination is appealable — a `PROPOSED` or `REVERSED` one yields `ERR_DEOBLIGATION_NOT_FINAL` — and `filed_at`, stamped from the logical clock, must be at or before the determination's `appeal_deadline_at`, with a clock instant exactly equal to the deadline still inside the window and anything later rejected as `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. Three admissibility rules then apply. A `SECOND`-level appeal's `prior_appeal_id` must reference an appeal on the *same* determination whose `status` is `DENIED` or `PARTIALLY_GRANTED`, since only a loss at the first level opens the second (`ERR_SECOND_APPEAL_REQUIRES_FIRST_DENIED`). No other appeal on that determination may still be `SUBMITTED`, because one live case at a time is the rule (`ERR_DUPLICATE_APPEAL`). And once a `SECOND`-level appeal exists on a determination, no further appeal may be filed against it at all (`ERR_APPEAL_LEVEL_EXHAUSTED`). A filed appeal sits in `SUBMITTED` with `relief_granted_cents` 0, `decided_at` null, `decided_by_user_id` null and `decision_rationale` null.

Deciding is an `AppealsAdjudicator` action carrying a `decision`, a `decision_rationale` recorded on the row, and — on a partial grant — a `relief_granted_cents`. The appeal must be in `SUBMITTED`, or the decision is refused with `ERR_APPEAL_ALREADY_DECIDED`, and the adjudicator's `X-User-ID` must differ from the determination's `determined_by_user_id`, or it is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. All four decisions stamp `decided_at` from the clock, record `decided_by_user_id`, and increment the appeal's `version` by 1; beyond that they diverge completely.

- **`SUBMITTED → GRANTED`** sets `relief_granted_cents` to the full `relief_requested_cents` and reverses the determination by that amount.
- **`SUBMITTED → PARTIALLY_GRANTED`** requires a supplied `relief_granted_cents` strictly greater than 0 and strictly less than `relief_requested_cents`; a value outside that open interval is rejected with `ERR_RELIEF_EXCEEDS_DEOBLIGATION`. It then reverses the determination by that partial amount.
- **`SUBMITTED → DENIED`** sets `relief_granted_cents` to 0 and moves **nothing** outside the appeal row — no determination, project, applicant or declaration field changes, and no version other than the appeal's increments. Its one forward consequence is that a `SECOND`-level appeal now becomes admissible.
- **`SUBMITTED → REMANDED`** sets `relief_granted_cents` to 0 and sends the determination back for re-work. Remand is available only when the deciding appeal's `level` is `FIRST`; the system does not remand on a `SECOND`-level appeal.

A grant or partial grant runs the determination's `FINAL → REVERSED` path, which requires the determination to be `FINAL` and the granted relief to be above 0. `reversed_cents` is set to `relief_granted_cents`; the determination's `status` becomes `REVERSED` when `reversed_cents` equals the full `amount_cents`, and **stays `FINAL`** when the reversal is partial, that is when `reversed_cents` is above 0 but below `amount_cents`. Because `reversed_cents` moved, the project's `deobligated_cents` falls by `relief_granted_cents`, which puts the money back on the obligation ledger; because `deobligated_cents` moved, the applicant's `total_obligated_cents` and `available_drawdown_cents` are recomputed and the declaration's `total_obligated_cents` follows. The determination's, project's, applicant's and declaration's `version` each increment by 1.

A remand runs the determination's `FINAL → PROPOSED` path instead, and it is the cyclic edge of this state machine. The determination's `status` returns to `PROPOSED`, `finalized_at` is cleared to null, `appeal_deadline_at` is cleared to null, and `reversed_cents` stays at 0 — nothing was granted. Because the determination is no longer final, the project's `deobligated_cents` falls by the full `amount_cents` and its `pending_deobligation_cents` rises by the same amount, so the money returns to the obligation ledger while remaining proposed for withdrawal. Because `deobligated_cents` moved, the applicant's `total_obligated_cents`, `pending_deobligation_cents` and `available_drawdown_cents` are recomputed; because the applicant's pending figure is above 0 again, `drawdown_status` becomes `SUSPENDED` and the declaration's `suspended_applicant_count` is recomputed; the declaration's `total_obligated_cents` is recomputed too. The determination's, project's, applicant's and declaration's `version` each increment by 1. The determination may then be finalized again, travelling `PROPOSED → FINAL → PROPOSED → FINAL`, and each fresh finalization opens a brand-new sixty-day appeal window measured from the new `finalized_at`.
