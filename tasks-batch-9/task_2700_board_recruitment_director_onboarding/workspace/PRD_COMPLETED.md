# Board Recruitment & Director Onboarding API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A public company's nominating and governance committee carries a workload that is at once deeply judgemental and highly procedural. The judgemental half — deciding which skills the board is short of, which candidate is worth an interview, whether a payment relationship is material enough to defeat independence — belongs to the committee. The procedural half is where boards get into trouble: a nominee slipped onto a proxy card while an interlocking-directorate limit was breached, a director seated before their orientation programme closed, a placement fee committed past the budget the committee approved, a slate approved on a ballot that a conflicted member should never have cast. This API exists to make the procedural half mechanical, auditable, and impossible to skip.

The service is mounted under `/governance` and models the full recruitment arc: a skills-matrix gap analysis against the seated board, candidate sourcing through retained search firms, network referrals, shareholder proposals and universal-proxy nominations, independence screening under one of two selectable standards, an interview and reference-check loop, conflict clearance covering interlocks, overboarding and retirement age, slate assembly with a search-fee ledger, committee balloting with quorum and recusal rules, proxy filing, the election record, and the post-election onboarding, committee-assignment, continuing-education and compensation programme for the directors who take their seats.

Two design commitments run through everything that follows. First, **every threshold in the system is policy data, not code**: retirement ages, tenure caps, overboarding limits, quorum sizes, approval percentages, retainer amounts and ownership multiples all live on a `GovernancePolicy` record that a corporate secretary adopts for a cycle year, and a request that needs a threshold when no policy is in force is refused rather than guessed at. Second, **derived state is never hand-maintained**: nominee counts, independence percentages, fee ledgers, committee seat counts, compliance postures and onboarding rollups are all recomputed from their source rows on every event that could move them, including events several hops away from where the client acted.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section fixes the contracts that hold for every endpoint: authentication, the response envelope, pagination, resource identity, path resolution, precision, concurrency control and the ordering in which the server refuses a bad request.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting user and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity in which they act. If either header is missing, or is present but empty or holds nothing but whitespace, the request is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before any other processing; surrounding whitespace is otherwise stripped from both header values before they are used, and the role name is then matched exactly and case-sensitively. If `X-User-Role` is not one of the six recognised role names, or is a recognised role that the target operation does not list, the request is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The six roles are `GovernanceStaff`, `CorporateSecretary`, `CommitteeChair`, `CommitteeMember`, `SeatedDirector` and `SearchFirmPartner`, spelled exactly that way.

- **`GovernanceStaff`** are the corporate-governance staff who run day-to-day sourcing, screening intake, interview logistics and slate assembly. When `X-User-Role` is `GovernanceStaff`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it in audit context but never compares it to any entity's `id`, and no ownership check is applied to a `GovernanceStaff` request.
- **`CorporateSecretary`** owns the official record: policy adoption, director records, committee charters and assignments, slate filing, election results, compensation awards, waivers and the logical clock. When `X-User-Role` is `CorporateSecretary`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity and is never compared to any entity's `id`. A `CorporateSecretary` may additionally perform every operation a `GovernanceStaff` user may perform.
- **`CommitteeChair`** chairs the nominating and governance committee and makes the independence determination, the conflict-of-interest determination, skill-rating validations, interview-round reopenings, onboarding waivers' sibling decisions and the slate decision. When `X-User-Role` is `CommitteeChair`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Director`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Director` compare the resource's director-referencing field to `X-User-ID`.
- **`CommitteeMember`** is a non-chair member of the nominating and governance committee who reviews the candidate file and casts a ballot on the slate. When `X-User-Role` is `CommitteeMember`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Director`, and the same director-to-`X-User-ID` comparison governs ownership.
- **`SeatedDirector`** is any director acting for themselves: recording their own skills self-assessment, completing their own onboarding and continuing-education items, scoring interviews they conducted, and reading their own compensation awards. When `X-User-Role` is `SeatedDirector`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Director`, and ownership checks compare the resource's `director_id` (or, for an interview, its `interviewer_director_id`) to `X-User-ID`.
- **`SearchFirmPartner`** is a partner at a retained executive-search firm who may read only their own firm record and its live placement-fee ledger. When `X-User-Role` is `SearchFirmPartner`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `SearchFirm`, and the ownership check compares the addressed firm's `id` to `X-User-ID`.

Ownership enforcement is a distinct, third check that runs after role authorisation succeeds. Four operations carry it: recording a director's skill rating, completing an onboarding item, casting a committee ballot, and reading a single search firm. In each case an entity-backed role acting on a record it does not own is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403) — a `SeatedDirector`, `CommitteeMember` or `CommitteeChair` whose `X-User-ID` does not equal the `Director.id` that owns the addressed resource, or a `SearchFirmPartner` whose `X-User-ID` does not equal the addressed `SearchFirm.id`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`, followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the uppercase letters `A` through `Z` and the digits `0` through `9`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints accept two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of rows returned; it defaults to 10 and is clamped into the range 1 through 100 rather than refused, so a `limit` below 1 is served as 1 and a `limit` above 100 is served as 100. `offset` is the number of rows to skip, defaults to 0, and is clamped up to 0 when negative. Either parameter that is absent, empty or not parseable as an integer falls back to its default; no pagination input ever produces an error, and the `limit` and `offset` reported in `meta` are the clamped values actually applied.

Unless a specific list endpoint states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Several lists override this because the governance file reads more naturally in another order: governance policies come back newest `effective_from` first, independence screens and onboarding items newest `created_at` first, skill categories and committees by `code` ascending, interview sessions by `start_at` ascending, slate nominees by `ballot_order` ascending, nomination slates by `cycle_year` descending, and compensation awards by `cycle_year` descending. Every one of these orderings is stable and ties on the ordering key are broken by the order in which the rows were created inside the process — earliest first under an ascending order, latest first under a descending one — and never by `id`.

Every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the envelope's `meta` object. The `total_count` is the number of rows that survive the endpoint's filters, counted before the pagination window is applied, and `has_more` is true exactly when `offset` plus the number of rows in `data` is less than `total_count`. Filtering happens before ordering and pagination: each filter query parameter is named exactly after the row field it matches, a parameter that is absent or supplied empty is ignored, an unrecognised query parameter is ignored rather than refused, a string filter compares for exact equality, a boolean filter reads only `true`, `1`, `yes` or `y` in any letter case as true and every other value as false, and an integer filter whose value is not an integer matches nothing and yields an empty page rather than an error.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties and the Logical Clock

All state lives in the server process's own memory: a freshly started process holds no rows at all, nothing is seeded, cached or written to disk, and every row that exists was created by an API caller. Most resources in this system are full audit-bearing records and expose four system-managed properties:

- **`id`** (string): a server-minted UUID. It carries no type prefix — the bare UUID form is the whole identifier.
- **`created_at`** (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, recorded when the row was first written.
- **`updated_at`** (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format, refreshed on every mutation of the row, including mutations the server performs on its own initiative while cascading a change from elsewhere.
- **`version`** (integer): an integer that starts at 1 and increments by one on every mutation of the row. Recomputation of a derived field counts as a mutation: when a nominee is added and the owning slate's `nominee_count` and `independent_nominee_pct` are re-derived, that slate's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` is incremented. Within one request a row's `version` advances at most once, however many separate writes that request makes to it: a nominee add that writes the slate and then re-derives the slate's rollups leaves that slate's `version` exactly one higher, not two, while `updated_at` is refreshed by each of those writes.

Two record types are deliberately leaner. A `SkillCategory` and an `ExternalCompany` are immutable reference data once created; they carry only `id` and `created_at`, and both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from their representations entirely. Every other record type — `GovernancePolicy`, `SearchFirm`, `Director`, `DirectorSkillRating`, `Candidate`, `ExternalDirectorship`, `IndependenceScreen`, `InterviewSession`, `Committee`, `CommitteeAssignment`, `NominationSlate`, `SlateNominee`, `CommitteeVote`, `OnboardingItem` and `DirectorCompensationAward` — carries all four.

Time in this system is a **logical clock**, not wall-clock time. Before any client sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to the host's real time. Every server-written instant (`submitted_at`, `determined_at`, `completed_at`, `activated_at`, `ended_at`, `voided_at`, `cast_at`, `filed_at`, `election_recorded_at`, `validated_at`, `finalized_at`, `retirement_notice_at`) is the logical clock instant at the moment of the write, carrying the clock's own reading verbatim: the literal text `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` until a client has moved the clock, and afterwards the exact `current_utc` string that the most recent accepted move supplied, character for character, rather than being re-serialised into the three-fractional-digit form used by `created_at`, `updated_at` and `api_request_timestamp`. Those three audit stamps are the one exception to the logical clock: `created_at`, `updated_at` and `api_request_timestamp` are read from the host's real clock at the moment of the write, so they advance on their own and are never rewritten when the logical clock moves. Every age, tenure, deadline and lookback comparison is measured against the logical clock's date. An administrative capability lets a corporate secretary move the clock forward by supplying a `current_utc` instant; the response `data` carries exactly two fields — the accepted `current_utc` echoed verbatim and `recomputed_director_count`, which is the total number of director rows held by the server, since `age_years` and `tenure_years` are refreshed for every director including those who are `RETIRED`, `RESIGNED` or `NOT_RENOMINATED`, and those three statuses are excluded only from the compliance recomputation. The clock is monotonic: a `current_utc` that is not strictly after the current logical clock instant is refused with `ERR_CLOCK_NOT_MONOTONIC` (HTTP 422).

### 2.5. Path Resolution Order for Nested Governance Records

Many operations address a child through its parent — a screen under a candidate, an assignment under a committee, a nominee or a ballot under a slate, an onboarding item or a compensation award under a director. For any such path, identifiers are resolved strictly left to right, and the first identifier that does not resolve produces the 404 for that identifier's own type before any later identifier is examined. A request for the onboarding items of a director that does not exist yields `ERR_DIRECTOR_NOT_FOUND`, never `ERR_ONBOARDING_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`.

The full set of 404 codes, each bound to one identifier type, is `ERR_POLICY_NOT_FOUND` for `policy_id`, `ERR_SKILL_CATEGORY_NOT_FOUND` for `skill_category_id`, `ERR_SEARCH_FIRM_NOT_FOUND` for `firm_id` or `search_firm_id`, `ERR_EXTERNAL_COMPANY_NOT_FOUND` for `external_company_id`, `ERR_DIRECTOR_NOT_FOUND` for `director_id`, `referring_director_id`, `interviewer_director_id`, `voting_director_id` or `incumbent_director_id`, `ERR_SKILL_RATING_NOT_FOUND` for `rating_id`, `ERR_CANDIDATE_NOT_FOUND` for `candidate_id`, `ERR_SCREEN_NOT_FOUND` for `screen_id`, `ERR_DIRECTORSHIP_NOT_FOUND` for `directorship_id`, `ERR_INTERVIEW_NOT_FOUND` for `interview_id`, `ERR_COMMITTEE_NOT_FOUND` for `committee_id`, `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND` for `assignment_id`, `ERR_SLATE_NOT_FOUND` for `slate_id`, `ERR_NOMINEE_NOT_FOUND` for `nominee_id` or for any member of `elected_nominee_ids`, `ERR_VOTE_NOT_FOUND` for `vote_id`, `ERR_ONBOARDING_ITEM_NOT_FOUND` for `item_id`, and `ERR_COMP_AWARD_NOT_FOUND` for `award_id`. Identifiers supplied in a request body rather than the path resolve to the same code set and the same HTTP 404.

### 2.6. The Governing Policy Record

Nearly every judgement in this system reads a threshold, and every threshold comes from exactly one `GovernancePolicy` row: the **governing policy**. Selection is deterministic. The eligible set is the `GovernancePolicy` rows whose `status` is `ACTIVE`; rows whose `status` is `SUPERSEDED` are never consulted for any purpose. The eligible rows are ranked by `effective_from` descending, and where two rows share an identical `effective_from` the tie is broken by `created_at` descending, and where those are identical too the tie is broken by insertion order with the row written later in the process ranking first. The highest-ranked row is the governing policy. Row identifiers are never used as a ranking key.

If the eligible set is empty — no policy has ever been adopted, or every policy has been superseded — then any request that needs a threshold is refused with `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_POLICY` (HTTP 409). Nine operations depend on a threshold and therefore carry this refusal: submitting an independence screen, running conflict clearance on a candidate, adding a nominee to a slate, submitting a slate for review, recording a slate decision, creating a compensation award, seating a director, running the skills gap analysis, and advancing the logical clock.

Adopting a new policy flips every prior row whose `status` was `ACTIVE` to `SUPERSEDED`, so in normal operation exactly one row is eligible. Superseding is forward-looking only: it never rewrites a frozen copy that an earlier request already took. A screen that was submitted under the `BRIGHT_LINE` standard keeps `standard_applied` at `BRIGHT_LINE` forever, and an award that captured `equity_value_cents` at creation keeps that figure, even after a policy with different values becomes governing.

### 2.7. Monetary Precision, Rounding and Percentage Discipline

Every money amount in this system is an integer number of **cents** — `retainer_fee_cents`, `committed_fee_cents`, `net_payable_cents`, `target_annual_comp_cents`, `placement_fee_cents`, `search_fee_budget_cents`, `committed_search_fee_cents`, `payments_to_company_cents`, `recipient_gross_revenue_cents`, `charitable_contributions_cents`, `company_gross_revenue_cents`, `gross_revenue_cents`, `share_price_cents`, `prorated_cash_retainer_cents`, `committee_fees_cents`, `equity_value_cents`, `pre_offset_total_cents`, `excess_offset_cents`, `total_cents`, `ownership_requirement_cents` and `ownership_actual_cents` all hold whole cents and are returned as JSON numbers, never as strings. Wherever a cent amount is derived from a percentage or a proration, the result is rounded to a whole cent using **round half up** — a value ending in exactly half a cent rounds away from zero.

Percentages and hour figures that the system computes rather than accepts — `independent_nominee_pct`, `approval_pct`, `readiness_score`, `onboarding_completion_pct`, `interview_score_avg`, `weighted_gap`, `board_gap_index` — are carried to exactly **two decimal places**, again using round half up.

Two rounding-order rules are load-bearing and are stated here because getting them backwards produces answers that are off by whole cents. First, **search-firm placement fees are rounded per nominee and then summed as integers**; summing the unrounded products and rounding once at the end is wrong. Second, **the excess-compensation offset tier is selected from the pre-offset total, never re-derived from the post-offset total**. Both rules are restated in the workflows that use them.

Where a comparison decides an outcome, the direction and inclusivity are fixed and never approximated. The slate independence test fails only when the computed percentage is *strictly below* the policy floor, so an exact match passes. The committee approval test passes when the approval percentage is *at or above* the threshold. The interlock test fails when the shared-board count is *at or above* the limit. The overboarding test fails when the count *strictly exceeds* the limit. The retirement test fails when the subject's age *reaches* the retirement age. The education test fails when credited hours are *strictly below* the requirement. The stock-ownership test passes when actual holdings are *at or above* the requirement.

### 2.8. Version Echo Requirements on Slate and Committee Writes

Two write operations are guarded by optimistic concurrency control, because in both cases the writer's decision depends on aggregate state that the writer can only have read beforehand.

**Adding a nominee to a nomination slate** requires a non-optional `expected_slate_version` field in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `NominationSlate`. The server compares `expected_slate_version` to the slate's current `version` and to nothing else. If they differ — because some other writer added a nominee, voided one, submitted the slate for review, or because a cascade elsewhere re-derived the slate's rollups and advanced its `version` — the request is refused with `ERR_STALE_SLATE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The reason this guard matters is that the nominee-add decision is taken against slate-level aggregates: `nominee_count` against `seat_count`, `independent_nominee_pct` against the independence floor, and `committed_search_fee_cents` against `search_fee_budget_cents`. A write built on a stale view of those aggregates would pass every product rule individually and still leave the slate outside policy.

**Proposing a committee assignment** requires a non-optional `expected_committee_version` field in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Committee`. The server compares `expected_committee_version` to the committee's current `version` and to nothing else; a mismatch is refused with `ERR_STALE_COMMITTEE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Seat capacity, the single-chair rule and the committee posture are all evaluated against the committee's own occupancy, so a proposal written against a stale view of the committee file would commit a seat the committee cannot honour.

No other endpoint takes a version field. Operations that are not listed above — activating or revoking an assignment, voiding a nominee, submitting a slate for review, deciding, filing, recording an election, and every read — perform no version comparison, and a version field supplied to them is simply an unknown field.

### 2.9. Cascading Recomputation and Derived-State Propagation

The governance record is a graph, not a tree, and a single client action routinely changes rows the client never named. The server performs those changes itself, immediately, within the same request — there is no deferred sweep and no eventual convergence. Three propagation families recur.

**Rollup propagation.** Whenever a child row enters or leaves a counted set, the parent's rollups are re-derived from the surviving children rather than adjusted incrementally. A nominee entering or leaving a slate's counted set re-derives that slate's `nominee_count`, `independent_nominee_count`, `independent_nominee_pct`, `independence_shortfall`, `committed_search_fee_cents`, `contested` and `universal_proxy_applicable`. An assignment entering or leaving a committee's active set re-derives that committee's `filled_seats` and its posture `status`. An onboarding item changing status re-derives its director's `onboarding_status`, `onboarding_completion_pct` and, for continuing-education items, `education_hours_cycle`. A ballot entering or leaving the cast set re-derives its slate's `votes_for`, `votes_against`, `votes_abstain`, `votes_recused`, `approval_pct` and `quorum_met`.

**Transitive cascade.** Some events propagate several hops. The deepest chain in the system runs five hops from a single determination: the screen itself changes state, the candidate is rejected, that candidate's live nominee rows are voided, each slate whose own nominee set changed re-derives its rollups and — if it was under committee review — returns to draft with its revision counter advanced, and finally every ballot on that returned slate is voided. A departure notice runs three hops: the director leaves seated service, that director's active committee assignments end, and each affected committee re-derives its seat count and posture. Recording an election runs five: the slate is marked recorded, each confirmed nominee becomes elected or not elected, each elected non-incumbent nominee mints a new director row, each new director row is seeded with its required orientation items, and each new director's onboarding rollup is derived from those seeded items. One hop of the nominee chain reaches wider than the slate that changed: because a search firm's fee ordering spans every slate it has placed onto, re-deriving that firm's ledger can move the `nominee_ordinal`, `fee_tier_applied` and `placement_fee_cents` of nominees sitting on *other* slates, and each of those slates then re-derives its own rollups as well. That fee-driven widening re-derives rollups only — a slate reached solely through a firm's re-tiering is never returned to draft, its revision counter never advances, and its ballots are left standing.

**Posture derivation.** A `Committee`'s `status` is not set by any client; it is a pure function of the committee's active assignments and those assignments' directors. Likewise a `Director` carries three *independent* state dimensions that evolve on separate schedules — `service_status`, `compliance_status` and `onboarding_status` — and the latter two are derived rather than commanded. A director can be seated and education-delinquent and onboarding-complete all at once; the three dimensions never gate each other except where a rule explicitly says so.

Every row a cascade actually moves is a mutated row: its `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` is incremented, exactly as if the client had written it directly. A recomputation that leaves every field of a row identical is not a mutation at all, so that row's `updated_at` and `version` stay exactly where they were.

### 2.10. Validation Ordering and Rejection Precedence

When one request violates several rules, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fully determined. Checks run in the following order, and the first failure wins:

1. **Header presence.** Both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` present and non-empty, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. **Role authorisation.** `X-User-Role` is one of the six declared roles and appears on the target operation's role list, else `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403).
3. **Resource ownership.** For the four ownership-bearing operations named in §2.1, the entity-backed role's `X-User-ID` matches the owning `Director.id` or `SearchFirm.id`, else `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403).
4. **Payload shape.** Every required input field is present and carries the declared JSON type; unknown fields are ignored rather than rejected. A missing or mistyped required field yields `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). An absent or empty request body is read as an empty JSON object, while a body that is present but is not a JSON object — malformed JSON, or a JSON array, string or number — yields `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). An explicit `null` counts as absent: it is a missing value for a required field and selects the stated default for an optional one. Declared types are checked without coercion — a field declared integer rejects `3.0` and every string form of a number, a field declared number accepts a plain integer, and `true` or `false` satisfies neither — and a date or instant string the server cannot parse is likewise refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than as an interval error — though that parse runs only after the enum and range checks of steps 5 and 6, so a body carrying both an out-of-vocabulary enum value or an out-of-range number and an unparseable date reports the enum or range code first. Where a calendar date is expected a longer ISO 8601 instant is accepted and only its date part is read.
5. **Enum membership.** Every enum-typed input value is a member of its declared vocabulary, else `ERR_INVALID_ENUM_VALUE` (400). This applies to `source_channel`, `fee_model`, `criticality`, `seat_role`, `ballot`, `notice_type`, `resolution`, `decision`, `category`, `variant`, `independence_standard` and `initial_service_status`, and is enforced when sourcing a candidate, onboarding a search firm, registering a skill category, proposing a committee assignment, casting a ballot, recording a departure notice, cancelling an interview, deciding a slate, adding an onboarding item, running the gap analysis, adopting a policy, and creating a director.
6. **Numeric range.** Numeric inputs lie inside their declared ranges — skill ratings from 1 through 5 inclusive, interview scores from 1 through 5 inclusive, `required_min_directors` at least 1, `strategic_weight` above 0.0 and at most 1.0, percentage fields from 0.0 through 100.0, and cent amounts and counts at least 0 — else `ERR_VALUE_OUT_OF_RANGE` (422). This range check guards recording a skill rating, validating a skill rating, completing an interview, registering a skill category, onboarding a search firm, adopting a policy, sourcing a candidate — whose `target_annual_comp_cents` may not be negative — chartering a committee, opening a nomination slate, adding a slate nominee, adding an onboarding item, and creating a compensation award. Cent amounts and counts that no listed operation guards are stored exactly as supplied, so the factual cent figures on an independence screen and a reference check's `reference_count` carry no lower bound of their own.
7. **Interval sanity.** Interview `end_at` strictly after `start_at`; assignment `term_end_on` on or after `term_start_on`; award `service_end_on` on or after `service_start_on`; external-directorship `end_on` on or after `start_on`. A violation yields `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL` (422), and it guards scheduling an interview, proposing a committee assignment, creating a compensation award, and ending an external directorship. The external-directorship comparison is the one exception to this position in the ladder, because the earlier endpoint of the interval is the stored `start_on` rather than a second input field: it is therefore evaluated only after the addressed directorship has been resolved, so an unknown `directorship_id` reports its own 404 first, but still ahead of that row's state precondition, so ending an already-`ENDED` seat with an `end_on` before its `start_on` reports `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL` rather than `ERR_DIRECTORSHIP_STATE_INVALID`.
8. **Subject exclusivity on an external directorship.** Exactly one of `director_id` or `candidate_id` is supplied; supplying both, or neither, yields `ERR_DIRECTORSHIP_SUBJECT_INVALID` (422).
9. **Source-channel coherence.** The candidate's `source_channel` agrees with the supplied source identifiers, and a slate nominee names exactly one subject; a violation yields `ERR_SOURCE_CHANNEL_MISMATCH` (422). This guards sourcing a candidate and adding a slate nominee.
10. **Path identifier existence**, left to right, per §2.5 (404).
11. **Body identifier existence.** Every identifier supplied in a request body resolves to an existing row — `search_firm_id`, `referring_director_id`, `skill_category_id`, `external_company_id`, `candidate_id`, `director_id`, `incumbent_director_id`, `interviewer_director_id`, `voting_director_id` and every member of `elected_nominee_ids` — with the same 404 code set. Where one request body carries several of them they are resolved in exactly the order just listed, so a request naming both an unknown `external_company_id` and an unknown `director_id` reports `ERR_EXTERNAL_COMPANY_NOT_FOUND`, and one naming both an unknown `candidate_id` and an unknown `interviewer_director_id` reports `ERR_CANDIDATE_NOT_FOUND`. This guards sourcing a candidate, recording a skill rating, recording an external directorship, scheduling an interview, proposing a committee assignment, adding a slate nominee, casting a ballot, and recording an election.
12. **Governing policy existence**, per §2.6 (`ERR_NO_ACTIVE_POLICY`, 409).
13. **Optimistic concurrency**, per §2.8 (`ERR_STALE_SLATE_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_COMMITTEE_VERSION`, 412).
14. **State precondition on the target row.** The addressed resource is in a state from which the requested transition is declared; every other originating state, terminal states included, is refused with the code for that resource type — `ERR_CANDIDATE_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_SCREEN_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_INTERVIEW_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_SLATE_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_NOMINEE_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_ONBOARDING_ITEM_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_COMP_AWARD_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_DIRECTOR_STATE_INVALID` or `ERR_DIRECTORSHIP_STATE_INVALID` — all at HTTP 409. This applies to every action operation.
15. **Cross-entity state dependencies**, all at HTTP 409: `ERR_DIRECTOR_NOT_SEATABLE` when an incumbent renomination names a director who is neither `SEATED` nor `RETIREMENT_NOTICED`, or when an assignment is activated for a director whose `service_status` is not exactly `SEATED` or while the committee is `SUSPENDED`; and `ERR_ONBOARDING_INCOMPLETE` when a director is seated while their onboarding dimension is `NOT_STARTED` or `IN_PROGRESS`.
16. **Segregation of duties**, `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403), on interview scheduling and ballot casting.
17. **Safety, compliance and regulatory guidelines**, all at HTTP 422: `ERR_TENURE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_RETIREMENT_AGE_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_INTERLOCK_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_OVERBOARDING_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` and `ERR_COMMITTEE_INDEPENDENCE_REQUIRED`.
18. **Uniqueness**, `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE` (409).
19. **Temporal rules**, in this order of introduction: `ERR_INTERVIEWER_DOUBLE_BOOKED` (409), `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_TERM_OVERLAP` (409), `ERR_CYCLE_YEAR_NOT_ADVANCED` (422) and `ERR_CLOCK_NOT_MONOTONIC` (422).
20. **Constraint satisfaction**, all at HTTP 422: `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_INTERVIEWS`, `ERR_READINESS_BELOW_THRESHOLD`, `ERR_QUORUM_NOT_MET`, `ERR_APPROVAL_THRESHOLD_NOT_MET`, `ERR_INDEPENDENCE_SHORTFALL`, `ERR_UNIVERSAL_PROXY_MINIMUM`, `ERR_COMMITTEE_SEATS_EXHAUSTED` and `ERR_SEAT_COUNT_EXCEEDED`.
21. **Fiscal limits, evaluated last of all.** `ERR_SEARCH_FEE_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` (422) is the final gate on a nominee add, so a nominee that also breaches a safety guideline surfaces the safety code, not the budget code.

Within the safety block at step 17, the nominee-add operation carries four of the five guidelines, and they are evaluated in the order the guidelines are declared: the tenure guideline first, then the retirement-age guideline, then the interlocking-directorate limit, then the overboarding limit. The standalone conflict-clearance evaluation sits outside this ladder — it reports its outcome on the candidate rather than as an error — and uses its own fixed order of interlocking directorate, then overboarding, then retirement age. Within step 20, the slate-decision operation evaluates quorum before the approval percentage, so a decision taken on too few ballots reports `ERR_QUORUM_NOT_MET` rather than being masked by the approval arithmetic; and the submit-for-review operation evaluates the independence floor before the universal-proxy minimum.

Uniqueness at step 18 covers fourteen distinct rules, all reported as `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`: one `SkillCategory` per `code`; one `SearchFirm` per `name`; one `DirectorSkillRating` per director-and-category pair; at most one open independence screen per candidate, counting screens whose `status` is `DRAFT` or `SUBMITTED`; one active `ExternalDirectorship` per subject-and-company pair; one `SlateNominee` per slate-and-subject pair among rows that are not `VOIDED`; one `SlateNominee` per slate-and-`ballot_order` among rows that are not `VOIDED`; one `CommitteeVote` per slate-and-voting-director pair among rows whose `status` is `CAST`; one `Committee` per `code`; one `Committee` per `name`; at most one `ACTIVE` assignment holding `seat_role` `CHAIR` per committee; one `NominationSlate` per `cycle_year` among rows whose `status` is not `ABANDONED`; one `OnboardingItem` per director-and-`module_code` among rows whose `status` is not `WAIVED`; and one `DirectorCompensationAward` per director-and-`cycle_year` pair among rows whose `status` is not `VOIDED`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Skills Matrix and the Board Gap Analysis

Board recruitment starts from a statement of what the board is short of. Governance staff register each dimension of the skills matrix as a **skill category**, giving it a stable `code` in uppercase snake case, a human-readable `name`, a `strategic_weight` strictly greater than 0.0 and no greater than 1.0 that expresses how much the category matters to the board-level index, a `required_min_directors` count of at least 1, and a `criticality` of `CORE`, `STRATEGIC` or `EMERGING` (defaulting to `STRATEGIC`). A category may also be flagged `risk_oversight`, which defaults to false; a category that serves risk oversight carries extra weight in the gap arithmetic. Categories are immutable reference data — once registered they are never edited, and duplicate `code` values — compared exactly, character for character and case-sensitively — are refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`.

Coverage of a category is evidenced by **skill ratings**. A rating names a `skill_category_id` and records a `self_rating` from 1 through 5 for one director; staff may record a rating for anyone, while a `SeatedDirector` may only record their own. Each rating is born with `validation_status` `UNVALIDATED`, `validated_rating` null and `validated_at` null, and its `effective_rating` therefore equals the `self_rating`. When the committee chair validates a rating, they supply a `validated_rating` from 1 through 5; the rating flips to `VALIDATED`, `validated_at` records the logical clock instant, and `effective_rating` becomes the `validated_rating`. A rating carries no state precondition of its own, so a rating that is already `VALIDATED` may be validated again: the new `validated_rating` overwrites the old one and `validated_at` is re-recorded. There is at most one rating per director-and-category pair; a second attempt is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`.

The gap analysis is a computed read, and it runs in one of two variants selected by the `variant` query parameter: `SELF_REPORTED`, which is what a `variant` that is omitted or supplied empty selects, or `VALIDATED_ONLY`. The population is the directors whose `service_status` is `SEATED` or `ONBOARDING`; directors who are `RETIREMENT_NOTICED`, `RETIRED`, `RESIGNED` or `NOT_RENOMINATED` are excluded entirely. For a given director and category, the rating used under `SELF_REPORTED` is that pair's `self_rating`, regardless of `validation_status`; under `VALIDATED_ONLY` it is the `validated_rating` when the row is `VALIDATED` and zero otherwise. Where no rating row exists for the pair at all, the rating is zero under either variant.

Categories are then processed in ascending `code` order, and for each one the analysis produces five figures. The `covered_director_count` is the number of directors in the population whose rating for that category is **at or above 3**. The `gap` is the `required_min_directors` less the `covered_director_count`, floored at zero so it is never negative. A risk multiplier of 1.5 applies when the category is flagged `risk_oversight` and 1.0 otherwise. The `weighted_gap` is the `gap` multiplied by the category's `strategic_weight` multiplied by that risk multiplier, rounded half up to two decimal places. The `severity` is `CRITICAL_GAP` when the gap is 2 or more, `MINOR_GAP` when the gap is exactly 1, and `COVERED` when the gap is zero.

Two board-level figures close the read. The `board_gap_index` is the sum of every category's `weighted_gap`, rounded half up to two decimal places. The `priority_category_code` is the `code` of the single most pressing category, chosen by sorting categories on `weighted_gap` descending, then on criticality rank ascending where `CORE` ranks ahead of `STRATEGIC` which ranks ahead of `EMERGING`, then on `code` ascending. When no skill category has been registered at all, `board_gap_index` is 0.00 and `priority_category_code` is null. The response reports the `variant` actually applied, the `seated_director_count`, the `category_count`, the `board_gap_index`, the `priority_category_code`, and a `categories` array whose elements each carry `skill_category_id`, `code`, `criticality`, `required_min_directors`, `covered_director_count`, `gap`, `weighted_gap` and `severity`. Because the two variants read different rating fields, the same board can come back fully covered under `SELF_REPORTED` and critically short under `VALIDATED_ONLY`, and the two variants can nominate different priority categories.

A committee may also declare that a committee needs a financial expert, using the `requires_financial_expert` flag on the committee charter; the flag records that at least one of the committee's `ACTIVE` members is expected to hold a validated rating of 3 or above in a skill category whose `criticality` is `CORE`.

### 3.2. Sourcing the Candidate Pipeline

Candidates reach the committee through four channels, and the channel determines what else must be supplied. A candidate is sourced with a `full_name`, an `email` that is recorded exactly as supplied and is **not** required to be unique across candidates — candidate email is not one of the uniqueness rules of §2.10 — a `birth_date` as an ISO 8601 calendar date in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, and a `source_channel`; all four are required. When the channel is `SEARCH_FIRM`, a `search_firm_id` is required and `referring_director_id` must be absent. When the channel is `NETWORK_REFERRAL`, a `referring_director_id` is required and `search_firm_id` must be absent — and that referring director will be required to recuse themselves when the committee ballots on any slate carrying this candidate. When the channel is `SHAREHOLDER_PROPOSAL` or `UNIVERSAL_PROXY_NOMINATION`, a `shareholder_proponent_name` is required and both identifier fields must be absent. On the two identifier-bearing channels the `shareholder_proponent_name` is itself unconstrained: it may be supplied or omitted, and whatever arrives is persisted verbatim, null when omitted. Any other combination is refused with `ERR_SOURCE_CHANNEL_MISMATCH`, and because that coherence test runs ahead of body-identifier resolution, a `SEARCH_FIRM` candidate that supplies a `referring_director_id` reports the mismatch even when neither identifier names an existing row. Two optional flags complete the intake: `is_sitting_ceo`, defaulting to false, which selects the stricter overboarding limit later on, and `target_annual_comp_cents`, defaulting to 0 and never negative, which becomes the basis for a search firm's placement fee if this candidate is ever nominated.

A newly sourced candidate begins at `SOURCED` with `independence_result` `PENDING`, `readiness_score` 0.00, `completed_interview_count` 0, `interview_score_avg` 0.00, `external_public_board_count` 0, `interlock_shared_board_count` 0, `reference_check_count` 0, `adverse_reference_found` false, `interview_round` 0, and both `rejection_reason_code` and `deferred_cycle_year` null.

Retained search firms are onboarded by the corporate secretary with a `name` unique across firms under exact, case-sensitive comparison, a `contact_email` for the engagement partner, and two required fee tiers: `placement_fee_pct`, applied to the firm's first two fee-bearing nominees, and `placement_fee_tier2_pct`, applied to the third and every later one. Both percentages must be strictly above 0.0 and no greater than 100.0. The `fee_model` is `RETAINER` by default or `CONTINGENT`; a `RETAINER` firm credits the `retainer_fee_cents` it has already been paid — zero by default, never negative — against the fees it earns, while a `CONTINGENT` firm bills placement fees only. Four ledger fields on the firm are maintained by the server and never written by a client: `placed_nominee_count`, `committed_fee_cents`, `retainer_credit_applied_cents` and `net_payable_cents`, all starting at 0. A search firm partner may read their own firm record to see this ledger live.

Outside boards are modelled by two records. An **external company** is registered with a `name` unique across companies under exact, case-sensitive comparison, a required `ticker` string that is the empty string when the company is not listed, an `is_public` flag defaulting to true, an `is_competitor` flag defaulting to false, and a `gross_revenue_cents` figure defaulting to 0 that serves as the recipient revenue basis in payment-materiality arithmetic. Only companies where `is_public` is true count toward overboarding and interlock arithmetic; a shared seat at a company where `is_competitor` is true is an automatic interlock violation regardless of how many boards are shared. External companies are immutable once created.

An **external directorship** records one subject's seat on one outside board. Exactly one of `director_id` or `candidate_id` must be supplied — both, or neither, is refused with `ERR_DIRECTORSHIP_SUBJECT_INVALID`. The row also names the `external_company_id`, a `start_on` calendar date, an optional `seat_role` of `DIRECTOR` (the default), `CHAIR` or `CEO_AND_DIRECTOR`, and an optional `is_audit_chair` flag defaulting to false that records, for the committee's time-commitment file, that the subject chairs that company's audit committee. The row is born `ACTIVE` with `end_on` null. Ending a seat supplies an `end_on` date that must be on or after `start_on`, flips `status` to `ENDED`, and records the `end_on` value; a seat that is already `ENDED` cannot be ended again and is refused with `ERR_DIRECTORSHIP_STATE_INVALID`. There may be only one `ACTIVE` directorship per subject-and-company pair, and a duplicate is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`.

Recording an outside seat for a **director** subject has immediate compliance consequences. The subject's `external_public_board_count` is re-derived as the number of their `ACTIVE` directorships whose company has `is_public` true, and their `compliance_status` is recomputed. The overboarding limit that applies to a director is `max_public_boards_sitting_ceo` when their `is_sitting_ceo_elsewhere` flag is true and `max_public_boards_default` otherwise, and the test counts *this* board as well as the outside ones: the count of outside public boards plus one must not strictly exceed the limit. With the shipped policy defaults, that means a director who is not a sitting chief executive may hold three outside public boards for a total of four, while a sitting chief executive may hold only one outside public board for a total of two. A new seat that takes a director past their limit moves `compliance_status` from `COMPLIANT` to `OVERBOARDED`, and every committee holding an `ACTIVE` assignment for that director re-derives its posture to `SUSPENDED`. Ending a seat runs the same arithmetic in reverse: once the row is `ENDED`, if the count plus one is at or below the limit and neither the ownership leg nor the education leg fails, the director returns from `OVERBOARDED` to `COMPLIANT`, and each committee that was `SUSPENDED` only because of that director re-derives its posture. Attempting to record a seat that would take a **director** subject past the applicable limit is separately refused with `ERR_OVERBOARDING_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, evaluated before the duplicate-seat rule. Because recording an outside seat is not one of the policy-dependent operations of §2.6, that refusal and the compliance recomputation are both skipped when no `ACTIVE` policy is in force: the seat is recorded rather than refused and the director's `compliance_status` is left exactly as it stood. Recording or ending a seat for a **candidate** subject re-derives only that candidate's `external_public_board_count`; no limit is enforced against a candidate here — the overboarding test reaches a candidate only at conflict clearance and at slate placement — and no compliance posture, committee posture or `readiness_score` moves.

### 3.3. Independence Screening and Determination

Independence is established by an **independence screen**, and a candidate may have only one screen open at a time: if a screen already exists for them in `DRAFT` or `SUBMITTED`, opening another is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`. Opening a screen for a candidate at `SOURCED` advances that candidate `SOURCED → SCREENING` and creates the screen at `DRAFT` with `candidate_id` taken from the path, `determination` `PENDING`, `standard_applied` null, `bright_line_fail_count` and `qualitative_fail_count` both 0, `disqualifying_factor_code` null, `submitted_at` null and `determined_at` null. Opening a screen for a candidate already at `NOMINATED` is a **supplemental re-screen**: new information surfacing during proxy season forces the committee to look again at a sitting nominee. The candidate stays at `NOMINATED` and keeps its existing `independence_result` until the new screen is determined; on that branch the candidate row is not written at all, so its `updated_at` and `version` do not move. Any other originating status is refused with `ERR_CANDIDATE_STATE_INVALID`, and that state precondition is evaluated before the one-open-screen rule, so a `SOURCED` candidate who somehow holds an open screen reports the duplicate while a `REJECTED` one reports the state.

The screen's payload is the factual record the determination will be made on, and every field is optional with a stated default. `payments_to_company_cents` (default 0) is the value of payments between the issuer and an organisation where the candidate is an executive; `recipient_gross_revenue_cents` (default 0) is that recipient organisation's gross revenue and forms the percentage leg of the materiality test. `prior_employment_end_on` is the calendar date the candidate's employment with the issuer or its auditor ended, and is null when they were never employed there. `family_member_employed` (default false) records that an immediate family member is an executive officer of the issuer. `auditor_affiliation` (default false) records that the candidate is a current partner or employee of the issuer's external auditor. `charitable_contributions_cents` (default 0) is what the issuer contributed to a charity where the candidate is an executive officer, and `company_gross_revenue_cents` (default 0) is the issuer's own gross revenue, the basis of the charitable materiality test.

**Submitting** a screen freezes the standard and computes the arithmetic. Three preconditions apply, and §2.10 fixes their order: a governing policy must exist, else `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_POLICY`; then the screen must be at `DRAFT`, else `ERR_SCREEN_STATE_INVALID`; then the owning candidate must be at `SCREENING` or `NOMINATED`, else `ERR_CANDIDATE_STATE_INVALID`. Submitting an already-`SUBMITTED` screen while no policy is in force therefore reports `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_POLICY`, and a `DRAFT` screen whose candidate has since moved on reports the candidate code. On success, `standard_applied` is set to the governing policy's `independence_standard`, `submitted_at` records the logical clock instant, and the screen moves `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`. A second submit of the same screen is refused with `ERR_SCREEN_STATE_INVALID`.

Five factors are evaluated at submit time, all against the governing policy and the logical clock's date.

- **Payment materiality.** The threshold is the greater of two figures: the policy's `bright_line_payment_floor_cents` as an absolute floor, and the policy's `bright_line_revenue_pct` taken as a percentage of the screen's `recipient_gross_revenue_cents`, that product rounded half up to whole cents. With the shipped defaults, the absolute floor is one million dollars and the percentage leg is two percent of the recipient's revenue, so the percentage leg only bites once the recipient's revenue passes fifty million dollars. The factor fails when `payments_to_company_cents` is **strictly greater** than that threshold; an amount exactly equal to the threshold does not fail.
- **Recent employment.** The factor is evaluated against a lookback expressed in years, and years are converted to days at 365 days per year. It does not fail at all when `prior_employment_end_on` is null. Otherwise it fails when the number of days from `prior_employment_end_on` to the logical clock's date is **strictly less** than the lookback in years multiplied by 365. This is the one factor whose answer depends on which standard is in force: the lookback is the policy's `bright_line_lookback_years` (three years by default, so 1095 days) under one standard and its `enhanced_lookback_years` (five years by default, so 1825 days) under the other.
- **Family employment.** Fails when `family_member_employed` is true.
- **Auditor affiliation.** Fails when `auditor_affiliation` is true.
- **Charitable materiality.** The threshold is the policy's `enhanced_charitable_pct` taken as a percentage of the screen's `company_gross_revenue_cents`, rounded half up to whole cents — two percent of issuer revenue with the shipped default. The factor fails when `charitable_contributions_cents` is **at or above** that threshold, so an amount exactly equal to the threshold does fail.

Both counts are recorded on every submitted screen, whichever standard governs. `bright_line_fail_count` is the number of failures among payment materiality, recent employment evaluated at the bright-line lookback, family employment and auditor affiliation — four factors, charitable materiality excluded. `qualitative_fail_count` is the number of failures among payment materiality, recent employment evaluated at the enhanced lookback, family employment, auditor affiliation and charitable materiality — five factors. The `disqualifying_factor_code` is the first factor that fails **under the applied standard**, searched in the fixed order `PAYMENT_MATERIALITY`, `RECENT_EMPLOYMENT`, `FAMILY_EMPLOYMENT`, `AUDITOR_AFFILIATION`, `CHARITABLE_MATERIALITY`, and null when none fails.

Which count decides the outcome is fixed by `standard_applied`. Under the **bright-line variant** — the governing policy's `independence_standard` was `BRIGHT_LINE` when the screen was submitted — the deciding count is `bright_line_fail_count`: the employment lookback is the three-year figure and charitable materiality is ignored entirely for the decision, although `qualitative_fail_count` is still recorded for the file. Under the **enhanced qualitative variant** — the standard was `ENHANCED` at submit — the deciding count is `qualitative_fail_count`: the employment lookback extends to five years and charitable materiality joins the decision. The two variants genuinely disagree on identical screen data whenever employment ended between three and five years before the logical clock's date, or whenever charitable contributions are at or above two percent of issuer revenue.

**Determination** is the committee chair's act, but the fork is decided by the screen data and never by the actor. The screen must be at `SUBMITTED`, else `ERR_SCREEN_STATE_INVALID`. If the deciding count is zero, the screen moves `SUBMITTED → DETERMINED_INDEPENDENT`, `determination` becomes `INDEPENDENT`, and `determined_at` records the logical clock instant. If the deciding count is 1 or more, the screen moves `SUBMITTED → DETERMINED_NOT_INDEPENDENT`, `determination` becomes `NOT_INDEPENDENT`, and `determined_at` is likewise recorded. Determination reads only the `standard_applied` and the two fail counts frozen onto the screen at submit, so it needs no governing policy and recomputes none of the screen's factual fields or counts. There is no separate notion of a governing screen: the candidate's `independence_result` simply carries the verdict of whichever of its screens was determined most recently, and `determined_at` is recorded for the file rather than consulted as a ranking key.

The favourable branch cascades one hop. The candidate's `independence_result` becomes `INDEPENDENT`; a candidate at `SCREENING` advances `SCREENING → SCREEN_PASSED`, while a candidate at `NOMINATED` under a supplemental re-screen stays `NOMINATED`; and the candidate's `readiness_score` is recomputed. The hop stops at the candidate: no nominee row's `is_independent` is rewritten and no slate rollup is re-derived, so a favourable re-screen of a sitting nominee leaves that slate's `independent_nominee_count` and `independent_nominee_pct` exactly as they were. A candidate who has left both of those statuses by the time the determination lands — a nominee whose row was voided, so that the candidate now sits at `DEFERRED` — keeps its own status and has only its `independence_result` and `readiness_score` rewritten.

The adverse branch is the deepest cascade in the system, and every hop must land.

1. The screen itself becomes `DETERMINED_NOT_INDEPENDENT` with `determination` `NOT_INDEPENDENT` and `determined_at` set.
2. Because the screen went adverse, the candidate's `independence_result` becomes `NOT_INDEPENDENT`, its `rejection_reason_code` becomes `INDEPENDENCE_FAILED`, and its status moves `SCREENING → REJECTED` on a first-pass screen or `NOMINATED → REJECTED` on a supplemental re-screen. On a first-pass rejection out of `SCREENING` every interview session of that candidate whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` also becomes `CANCELLED`, and because a candidate at `SCREENING` has no nominee row yet, the slate-side hops below do not fire. A candidate sitting at any status other than those two when the determination lands keeps that status and its existing `rejection_reason_code`, taking only the new `independence_result`, and its `SCHEDULED` interviews are left alone because the interview-cancelling hop fires only out of `SCREENING`; the nominee-voiding hop below still runs against whatever live nominee rows that candidate holds.
3. Because the candidate became `REJECTED`, every slate nominee row naming that candidate whose `status` is `PROPOSED` or `CONFIRMED` becomes `VOIDED` with `voided_at` set to the logical clock instant.
4. Because those nominee rows left the counted set, each affected slate re-derives `nominee_count`, `independent_nominee_count`, `independent_nominee_pct`, `independence_shortfall`, `committed_search_fee_cents`, `contested` and `universal_proxy_applicable`; each sourcing search firm re-derives `placed_nominee_count`, `committed_fee_cents`, `retainer_credit_applied_cents` and `net_payable_cents`; and any affected slate that was at `COMMITTEE_REVIEW` returns `COMMITTEE_REVIEW → DRAFT` with `revision_count` incremented by 1 and `version` incremented by 1, because its nominee set changed underneath the review.
5. Because such a slate returned to `DRAFT`, every ballot on it whose `status` is `CAST` becomes `VOIDED_BY_REVISION`, and that slate's `votes_for`, `votes_against`, `votes_abstain`, `votes_recused`, `approval_pct` and `quorum_met` are re-derived from the ballots that remain `CAST` — which is none, so the four tallies are 0, `approval_pct` is 0.00 and `quorum_met` is false.

A candidate may also step out voluntarily. Withdrawal is available from `SOURCED`, `SCREENING`, `SCREEN_PASSED`, `INTERVIEWING`, `REFERENCE_CHECK` and `SLATE_ELIGIBLE`, and moves the candidate to the terminal `WITHDRAWN`; a candidate already at `NOMINATED` must have their nominee row voided first, and any other originating status is refused with `ERR_CANDIDATE_STATE_INVALID`. Withdrawal voids every screen of that candidate whose `status` is `DRAFT` or `SUBMITTED` — each such screen moves to `VOIDED` with `determination` left at `PENDING` and `determined_at` left null — and cancels every interview session of that candidate whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`.

### 3.4. Interviews, Reference Checks and Candidate Readiness

Interviewing begins once a candidate's independence is established. Staff schedule an **interview session** naming the `candidate_id`, the `interviewer_director_id`, a `start_at` and `end_at` instant pair, and an optional `modality` of `IN_PERSON` or `VIDEO` (defaulting to `VIDEO`). The `end_at` must be strictly after the `start_at`, else `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`. The candidate must be at `SCREEN_PASSED` or `INTERVIEWING`; every other status — `SOURCED`, `SCREENING`, `REFERENCE_CHECK`, `SLATE_ELIGIBLE` and `NOMINATED` included — is refused with `ERR_CANDIDATE_STATE_INVALID`. The `interviewer_director_id` need only resolve to an existing director row: no `service_status` precondition applies, so a director who is `ONBOARDING`, `RETIREMENT_NOTICED`, `RETIRED`, `RESIGNED` or `NOT_RENOMINATED` may still be named as the interviewer.

The interviewer's calendar is protected. The requested window is treated as **half-open**, covering the `start_at` instant and every instant up to but not including the `end_at` instant, and it must not overlap any session of the same `interviewer_director_id` whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `COMPLETED`. Because the interval is half-open, a session whose `start_at` equals an existing session's `end_at` is permitted, and back-to-back interviews are therefore legal; any genuine overlap is refused with `ERR_INTERVIEWER_DOUBLE_BOOKED`. Sessions whose `status` is `CANCELLED` or `NO_SHOW` no longer occupy the calendar, so their windows may be reused freely.

Segregation of duties applies at scheduling, and both tests read the **named `interviewer_director_id`** rather than the acting staff user, whose `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier tied to no director. The first test compares the candidate's own `referring_director_id` to the interviewer: a director may not interview a candidate whose nomination they proposed, which is to say a candidate they referred in through the `NETWORK_REFERRAL` channel. The second test looks for a ballot: collect every slate carrying a nominee row for this candidate whose `status` is anything but `VOIDED`, and refuse when the interviewer holds a ballot on any of those slates whose `status` is `CAST` — a ballot already `VOIDED_BY_REVISION` no longer conflicts. Either overlap is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, the referral test first, and both are evaluated ahead of the double-booking check.

Scheduling the first session of a round moves the candidate from screen-passed into interviewing, and the session's `interview_round` is recorded as the candidate's `interview_round` plus 1 when the candidate was at `SCREEN_PASSED`, or as the candidate's current `interview_round` for a later session inside the same round. Opening a round this way also advances the candidate's own `interview_round` to that same new number in the same write — the counter moves alongside the move into `INTERVIEWING`, which is what makes a later session "inside the same round" land on the number just assigned; scheduling a session for a candidate already at `INTERVIEWING` leaves the candidate's `interview_round` as it stands. A session is born `SCHEDULED` with `score` null, `recommendation` null and `completed_at` null.

**Completing** a session records an integer `score` from 1 through 5 and a `recommendation` of `ADVANCE`, `HOLD` or `DECLINE`. The session must be `SCHEDULED`, else `ERR_INTERVIEW_STATE_INVALID`, and it moves to `COMPLETED` with `completed_at` set to the logical clock instant. Completing a session is not one of the four ownership-bearing operations of §2.1, so a `SeatedDirector` may complete any `SCHEDULED` session and not only one they conducted; nor is the candidate's own status a precondition, so a session may still be completed for a candidate who has since been rejected or withdrawn. The candidate's `completed_interview_count` is then re-derived as the number of that candidate's sessions whose `status` is `COMPLETED` — counted across every interview round and never reset by a reopening — the `interview_score_avg` as the mean `score` over exactly those sessions rounded half up to two decimal places (0.00 when there are none), and the `readiness_score` is recomputed. **Cancelling** a session supplies a `resolution` of `CANCELLED` or `NO_SHOW`; the session must likewise be `SCHEDULED`, else `ERR_INTERVIEW_STATE_INVALID`; its `status` becomes exactly the supplied `resolution`, releasing the calendar window, while `score`, `recommendation` and `completed_at` stay null. Neither outcome contributes to `completed_interview_count`, and the candidate row is not written at all, so a candidate at `INTERVIEWING` whose every session has been cancelled stays at `INTERVIEWING` with its interview rollups and `readiness_score` unchanged.

The **reference check** closes the interview stage. It records a `reference_count` and an `adverse_findings` flag defaulting to false. The candidate must be at `INTERVIEWING`, else `ERR_CANDIDATE_STATE_INVALID`, and must already hold at least the governing policy's `min_completed_interviews` sessions in `COMPLETED` status — counted over every round the candidate has been through and not merely the current one — else `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_INTERVIEWS`. The state precondition is evaluated first and the interview count second; and because a reference check is not one of the policy-dependent operations of §2.6, the count gate is skipped altogether when no `ACTIVE` policy is in force, so the check then succeeds on any number of completed sessions rather than reporting `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_POLICY`. On success `reference_check_count` is set from `reference_count` and `adverse_reference_found` from `adverse_findings`, and the branch is decided by the flag: false advances the candidate `INTERVIEWING → REFERENCE_CHECK`; true moves the candidate `INTERVIEWING → REJECTED` with `rejection_reason_code` `ADVERSE_REFERENCE`, and additionally voids every slate nominee row of that candidate whose `status` is `PROPOSED` or `CONFIRMED`, with the slate and search-firm rollups following as usual. Both branches recompute the `readiness_score`, and both return HTTP 200 — an adverse reference is a recorded outcome, not a client error.

The committee chair may send a candidate back for another look: reopening interviews moves the candidate `REFERENCE_CHECK → INTERVIEWING`, increments `interview_round` by 1 and resets `reference_check_count` to 0. The request carries no body, and nothing else on the candidate moves: `completed_interview_count`, `interview_score_avg`, `adverse_reference_found` and `readiness_score` are all left exactly as they stand. The loop is bounded — the candidate's `interview_round` must be strictly less than 3 at the time of the request, so the round counter can reach 3 but no reopening is permitted from there, and a further attempt is refused with `ERR_CANDIDATE_STATE_INVALID`.

The **readiness score** is a weighted composite recomputed whenever an interview completes, a reference check is recorded, conflict clearance runs, or a screen is determined. It is assembled from four components and one penalty.

- The **interview component** is 8.0 points per point of `interview_score_avg`, that product rounded half up to two decimal places, and then capped at 40.00.
- The **independence component** is 30.00 when the candidate's `independence_result` is exactly `INDEPENDENT`, and 0.00 otherwise — a `PENDING` candidate earns nothing here.
- The **channel component** is 20.00 for `SEARCH_FIRM`, 15.00 for `NETWORK_REFERRAL`, 10.00 for `SHAREHOLDER_PROPOSAL` and 5.00 for `UNIVERSAL_PROXY_NOMINATION`.
- The **board-capacity component** is 10.00 less 2.5 points for each of the candidate's outside public boards, that is, less 2.5 multiplied by `external_public_board_count`, floored at 0.00 so a candidate with four or more outside public boards scores 0.00 rather than a negative figure.

Those four components are summed and the sum is capped at 100.00. Only then is the **adverse-reference penalty** of 15.00 subtracted, and only when `adverse_reference_found` is true. The result, rounded half up to two decimal places, is the `readiness_score`. The order matters: a candidate whose four components sum above the ceiling is first brought down to 100.00 and then still loses the full 15.00, landing at 85.00 rather than at some higher figure derived from the uncapped sum. The penalty is also subtracted without a floor: the board-capacity component is clamped at 0.00 before the sum and the sum itself is capped at 100.00, but the figure that remains after the 15.00 is taken off is not clamped at zero — it is simply the capped sum less the penalty.

### 3.5. Conflict Clearance: Interlocks, Overboarding and Retirement Age

Conflict clearance is the chair's last look at a candidate before slate placement, and it is the one operation whose adverse outcome is a recorded business result rather than a rejected request: both the clean branch and the failed branch return HTTP 200 with the updated candidate. A governing policy must exist, else `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_POLICY`, and the candidate must be at `REFERENCE_CHECK`, else `ERR_CANDIDATE_STATE_INVALID`; the policy is demanded first per §2.10, so clearance attempted on a candidate at the wrong status while no policy is in force reports `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_POLICY`.

The evaluation looks only at outside seats that matter. The candidate's board set is the set of `external_company_id` values drawn from that candidate's directorships whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose company has `is_public` true; seats that are `ENDED`, and seats at companies that are not public, are excluded entirely. The comparison population is every director whose `service_status` is `ONBOARDING`, `SEATED` or `RETIREMENT_NOTICED`; directors who are `RETIRED`, `RESIGNED` or `NOT_RENOMINATED` are excluded. Each such director contributes their own board set on the same terms.

Three tests then run.

- **Interlocking directorate.** For each director in the population, count the companies that appear in both that director's board set and the candidate's. The `interlock_shared_board_count` is the largest of those per-director counts; when the population is empty the count is 0 rather than an error. The test fails when that count is **at or above** the policy's `interlock_max_shared_boards`, or when any company shared with any director in the population is flagged `is_competitor` — a single shared seat at a competitor fails regardless of the count.
- **Overboarding.** The applicable limit is the policy's `max_public_boards_sitting_ceo` when the candidate's `is_sitting_ceo` is true and its `max_public_boards_default` otherwise. The candidate's `external_public_board_count` is re-derived as the size of their board set, and the test fails when that count plus one — the plus one counting the seat now under consideration — is **strictly greater** than the limit. A count exactly equal to the limit passes.
- **Retirement age.** The candidate's age in whole years is the number of days from `birth_date` to the logical clock's date divided by 365, discarding any fraction. The test fails when that age is **at or above** the policy's `mandatory_retirement_age`.

The three tests are evaluated in the fixed order interlocking directorate, overboarding, retirement age, and the first one to fail supplies the outcome. A clean pass moves the candidate `REFERENCE_CHECK → SLATE_ELIGIBLE`; any failure moves them `REFERENCE_CHECK → REJECTED` with `rejection_reason_code` set to `INTERLOCK_LIMIT`, `OVERBOARDING` or `RETIREMENT_AGE` from that first failing test. Either way the recomputed `interlock_shared_board_count` and `external_public_board_count` are persisted and the `readiness_score` is refreshed.

The same three tests are re-evaluated when the candidate is actually placed on a slate, but there they are hard refusals rather than recorded outcomes, surfacing as `ERR_INTERLOCK_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_OVERBOARDING_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` and `ERR_RETIREMENT_AGE_EXCEEDED`. For an incumbent subject the same three tests read the director's own record in place of a candidate's: the board set is that director's `ACTIVE` seats at public companies, the overboarding limit is selected by their `is_sitting_ceo_elsewhere` flag, the age test reads their `birth_date`, and the director themselves is excluded from the interlock comparison population so their own seats are never counted as shared with themselves. Nothing is written onto the incumbent director row by the attempt, whether it succeeds or fails. A fourth guideline joins them for incumbents: the **tenure** guideline refuses the renomination of a sitting director whose completed years of service reach the policy's `max_tenure_years`, reported as `ERR_TENURE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. A director's `tenure_years` is the number of days from `appointed_on` to the logical clock's date divided by 365 with the fraction discarded, and their `age_years` is computed the same way from `birth_date`; both are refreshed for every director whenever the logical clock advances.

A candidate rejected at conflict clearance is terminal. There is no path back from `REJECTED`; the committee's only route to reconsidering that person is a fresh candidate record.

### 3.6. Assembling the Nomination Slate

A **nomination slate** is the committee's working proxy card for one cycle. It is opened with a `cycle_year` of at least 2000, a `seat_count` of at least 1 that states how many board seats stand for election, and an optional `search_fee_budget_cents` that defaults to 10000000000 — one hundred million dollars — and may not be negative. The new slate's `cycle_year` must be greater than or equal to the governing policy's `cycle_year`, else `ERR_CYCLE_YEAR_NOT_ADVANCED` — a comparison that is skipped entirely when no `ACTIVE` policy is in force, because opening a slate is not one of the policy-dependent operations of §2.6 and never reports `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_POLICY` — and only one slate may hold a given `cycle_year` among rows whose `status` is not `ABANDONED`, else `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`. The slate is born `DRAFT` with `nominee_count`, `independent_nominee_count`, `committed_search_fee_cents`, `revision_count`, `votes_for`, `votes_against`, `votes_abstain` and `votes_recused` all 0, `independent_nominee_pct` and `approval_pct` 0.00, `independence_shortfall`, `contested`, `universal_proxy_applicable` and `quorum_met` all false, `filed_at` and `election_recorded_at` null, and `version` 1.

A **slate nominee** row places one subject on the card. Exactly one of `candidate_id` or `incumbent_director_id` must be supplied — a cleared new candidate or a sitting director standing for renomination — and supplying both, or neither, is refused with `ERR_SOURCE_CHANNEL_MISMATCH`. The row also carries a `ballot_order` of at least 1, unique per slate among rows whose `status` is not `VOIDED`, and the mandatory `expected_slate_version` echo described in §2.8. A nominee may only be added while the slate is `DRAFT` or `COMMITTEE_REVIEW`, else `ERR_SLATE_STATE_INVALID`.

The gates on a nominee add are numerous, and §2.10 fixes their order. A candidate subject must be at `SLATE_ELIGIBLE`, else `ERR_CANDIDATE_STATE_INVALID`, and must carry a `readiness_score` at or above the policy's `readiness_min_score`, else `ERR_READINESS_BELOW_THRESHOLD` — a score exactly equal to the floor passes. An incumbent subject's `service_status` must be `SEATED` or `RETIREMENT_NOTICED`; a director who is `ONBOARDING`, `RETIRED`, `RESIGNED` or `NOT_RENOMINATED` cannot be renominated and the request is refused with `ERR_DIRECTOR_NOT_SEATABLE`. The same subject may appear only once per slate among non-`VOIDED` rows, else `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`. The count of non-`VOIDED` nominees on the slate may not exceed the policy's `authorized_seats`, else `ERR_SEAT_COUNT_EXCEEDED`. And the four safety guidelines of §3.5 apply. Where one add breaches several of these at once the ladder of §2.10 decides, and two orderings inside it are worth naming: the duplicate-subject rule is tested before the duplicate-`ballot_order` rule, and the readiness floor is tested before the `authorized_seats` ceiling.

On success the nominee is created `PROPOSED` with `slate_id` from the path and `voided_at` null, its `is_independent` copied from the subject — the incumbent director's `is_independent` for a renomination, or true only when the candidate's `independence_result` is exactly `INDEPENDENT` — and a candidate subject moves `SLATE_ELIGIBLE → NOMINATED` and has the two figures the safety evaluation produced written back onto it — its `interlock_shared_board_count` and `external_public_board_count` set to exactly the two figures §3.5 defines, recomputed against the record as it stands at the moment of the add. An incumbent subject's director row is not written at all. The slate's `version` is incremented by 1. Adding a nominee is not a revision: a slate at `COMMITTEE_REVIEW` stays at `COMMITTEE_REVIEW`, its `revision_count` does not move, and its `CAST` ballots and tally figures survive untouched — which is the one asymmetry between an add and the void that compensates it.

**The placement fee.** A nominee whose candidate was sourced by a search firm carries a fee; every other nominee has `placement_fee_cents` 0, `fee_tier_applied` null and `nominee_ordinal` 0. The fee is tiered by the nominee's position in the firm's own book of business. Take every nominee row, across all slates, whose candidate names this firm and whose `status` is `PROPOSED`, `CONFIRMED` or `ELECTED`; sort them by `created_at` ascending, for ties by `ballot_order` ascending, and where even those are equal by insertion order with the row written earlier ranking first — never by `id`. A nominee being added right now sorts last among any rows it ties with on both keys, so it takes the next free ordinal rather than displacing an existing one. The `nominee_ordinal` is this nominee's 1-based position in that ordering. The first two positions take the firm's `placement_fee_pct` and record `fee_tier_applied` `TIER1`; the third position and every later one take the firm's `placement_fee_tier2_pct` and record `TIER2`. The nominee's `placement_fee_cents` is that percentage of the candidate's `target_annual_comp_cents`, rounded half up to whole cents.

The rounding order is pinned and it is not interchangeable: **each nominee's fee is rounded to whole cents first, and the already-rounded integers are then summed.** Summing the raw products and rounding the total once is wrong and can differ by one or more cents. The firm's ledger follows from that summation: `placed_nominee_count` is the size of the ordered set, `committed_fee_cents` is the sum of the rounded per-nominee fees, `retainer_credit_applied_cents` is the lesser of the firm's `retainer_fee_cents` and its `committed_fee_cents` when the `fee_model` is `RETAINER` and is 0 when the `fee_model` is `CONTINGENT`, and `net_payable_cents` is the `committed_fee_cents` less the `retainer_credit_applied_cents`. The slate's `committed_search_fee_cents` is the sum of `placement_fee_cents` over that slate's own counted nominees. Because every stored fee is an exact integer, voiding a nominee releases precisely the cents it committed and the ledger returns to its prior value with no drift.

Evaluated last of all the gates, after every state, safety and uniqueness check has passed, the slate's `committed_search_fee_cents` including the new nominee may not exceed the slate's `search_fee_budget_cents`, else `ERR_SEARCH_FEE_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`.

**The independence percentage.** The counted nominee set for every slate rollup is the rows whose `status` is `PROPOSED`, `CONFIRMED` or `ELECTED`; rows that are `VOIDED` or `NOT_ELECTED` leave both the numerator and the denominator. The `nominee_count` is the size of that set and the `independent_nominee_count` is the number of those rows whose `is_independent` is true. The `independent_nominee_pct` is 0.00 when the `nominee_count` is 0, and otherwise 100 multiplied by the `independent_nominee_count` divided by the `nominee_count`, rounded half up to two decimal places. The `independence_shortfall` flag is true only when the `nominee_count` is greater than 0 **and** the `independent_nominee_pct` is **strictly below** the policy's `independence_min_pct`; a percentage exactly equal to the floor is not a shortfall, and an empty slate is never a shortfall. With no `ACTIVE` policy in force there is no floor to fall below, so the flag derives as false however few of the nominees are independent. The `contested` flag is true when any counted nominee's candidate has `source_channel` `UNIVERSAL_PROXY_NOMINATION`, and `universal_proxy_applicable` is simply equal to `contested`.

**Voiding a nominee** is the exact compensating operation for the add. The nominee must be `PROPOSED` or `CONFIRMED` and its slate must be `DRAFT` or `COMMITTEE_REVIEW`, else `ERR_NOMINEE_STATE_INVALID`. The row becomes `VOIDED` with `voided_at` set to the logical clock instant, and four consequences follow: a candidate subject moves `NOMINATED → DEFERRED` with `deferred_cycle_year` set to the slate's `cycle_year`; the sourcing firm's ledger is recomputed with every surviving nominee's `nominee_ordinal`, `fee_tier_applied` and `placement_fee_cents` re-derived from the new ordering, so a void can promote a later `TIER2` nominee into `TIER1`; the slate re-derives `nominee_count`, `independent_nominee_count`, `independent_nominee_pct`, `independence_shortfall`, `committed_search_fee_cents`, `contested` and `universal_proxy_applicable`; and a slate that was at `COMMITTEE_REVIEW` returns `COMMITTEE_REVIEW → DRAFT` with `revision_count` incremented while every ballot on it whose `status` is `CAST` becomes `VOIDED_BY_REVISION`. The slate's `version` is incremented by 1.

A deferred candidate is not finished. A later cycle may reopen their screening: the candidate moves `DEFERRED → SCREENING`, a fresh screen is created in `DRAFT`, `independence_result` resets to `PENDING` and `interview_round` resets to 0. Nothing else on the candidate is rewound: `reference_check_count`, `adverse_reference_found`, `completed_interview_count`, `interview_score_avg`, `readiness_score` and `rejection_reason_code` all stand as they were, and `deferred_cycle_year` keeps its recorded value. The request carries no body, so the fresh screen takes every factual field at its stated default — `payments_to_company_cents`, `recipient_gross_revenue_cents`, `charitable_contributions_cents` and `company_gross_revenue_cents` 0, `prior_employment_end_on` null, `family_member_employed` and `auditor_affiliation` false — and this reopening is not measured against the one-open-screen rule, so a screen left over from the earlier cycle in `DRAFT` or `SUBMITTED` stays where it is beside the new one. The gate is that the governing policy's `cycle_year` must be **strictly greater** than the candidate's `deferred_cycle_year`, so a candidate deferred in one cycle cannot be reopened inside that same cycle; a premature attempt is refused with `ERR_CANDIDATE_STATE_INVALID`, and so is an attempt made while no `ACTIVE` policy is in force or while the candidate carries no `deferred_cycle_year` at all — reopening screening is not one of the policy-dependent operations of §2.6 and never reports `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_POLICY`.

### 3.7. Committee Deliberation, Filing and the Election Record

A slate leaves the drafting stage by being submitted to the nominating committee, which moves it `DRAFT → COMMITTEE_REVIEW`. Four preconditions apply, and each is reported with its own distinct code. The slate must carry at least one nominee and its `independent_nominee_pct` must be at or above the policy's `independence_min_pct`, else `ERR_INDEPENDENCE_SHORTFALL` — the empty slate is caught by this same rule because a slate with no nominees has nothing to review. Then, when the slate is contested — that is, when `universal_proxy_applicable` is true because some counted nominee arrived through `UNIVERSAL_PROXY_NOMINATION` — the slate must nominate at least the policy's `authorized_seats` less one, so the company's universal proxy card offers shareholders a full alternative board; a shorter contested slate is refused with `ERR_UNIVERSAL_PROXY_MINIMUM`. Finally the slate's `committed_search_fee_cents` must remain within its `search_fee_budget_cents`, else `ERR_SEARCH_FEE_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`. A governing policy must exist for any of this to be measured, else `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_POLICY`. The slate re-derives `nominee_count`, `independent_nominee_count`, `independent_nominee_pct`, `independence_shortfall`, `committed_search_fee_cents`, `contested` and `universal_proxy_applicable` from the counted set *before* those three preconditions are weighed, so each of them is judged against freshly derived figures rather than against whatever the stored rollups happened to read; on success the `version` is incremented by 1. A slate in any state other than `DRAFT` cannot be submitted, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_SLATE_STATE_INVALID`.

**Balloting.** Each committee ballot names a `voting_director_id`, a `ballot` of `FOR`, `AGAINST`, `ABSTAIN` or `RECUSED`, and an optional `rationale` that defaults to the empty string and is minuted with the vote. The slate must be at `COMMITTEE_REVIEW`, else `ERR_SLATE_STATE_INVALID`. One ballot per slate-and-director pair is permitted among rows whose `status` is `CAST`, else `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`, and an entity-backed voter may only cast their own ballot, else `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. The server derives `recusal_required` from the record: it is true when the balloting director referred any counted nominee's candidate on this slate, or when that director is themselves a nominee on it. A director for whom recusal is required must ballot `RECUSED`; casting `FOR`, `AGAINST` or `ABSTAIN` instead is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, as is a ballot cast by a director who interviewed a candidate on the slate — meaning a director who holds an interview session, in `SCHEDULED` or `COMPLETED` status, against the candidate subject of any counted nominee of that slate; a session that was `CANCELLED` disqualifies nobody, and an incumbent nominee has no candidate subject to have interviewed. The interviewer test is applied after the recusal test and before the one-ballot-per-director rule, so a director who must recuse and has also interviewed reports the recusal failure, and a director who has already balloted and has since interviewed reports `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` rather than `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`. The ballot is created `CAST` with `slate_id` from the path and `cast_at` set to the logical clock instant.

Every ballot refreshes the slate's tally, computed over that slate's ballots whose `status` is `CAST` — ballots that are `VOIDED_BY_REVISION` are excluded from every figure. The `votes_for`, `votes_against`, `votes_abstain` and `votes_recused` are the counts of `CAST` ballots carrying each of the four values. The `quorum_met` flag is true when the sum of all four counts — recused ballots included — is **at or above** the policy's `quorum_size`; with no `ACTIVE` policy in force there is no quorum to meet and the flag derives as false however many ballots are in. The approval denominator, by contrast, excludes recusals: it is `votes_for` plus `votes_against` plus `votes_abstain`. The `approval_pct` is 0.00 when that denominator is zero, and otherwise 100 multiplied by `votes_for` divided by that denominator, rounded half up to two decimal places. Approval is met when `quorum_met` is true **and** the `approval_pct` is at or above the policy's `approval_threshold_pct`; an `approval_pct` of exactly 66.67 clears a floor of 66.67. Because recusals count toward quorum but are removed from the approval denominator, a three-member committee with one recusal needs both remaining ballots to be `FOR` in order to clear a two-thirds floor.

**The decision.** The chair records a `decision` of `APPROVE`, `REJECT` or `ABANDON` with an optional `rationale`. A governing policy must exist, else `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_POLICY`.

- `APPROVE` requires the slate to be at `COMMITTEE_REVIEW`, with `quorum_met` true, `approval_pct` at or above `approval_threshold_pct` and `independence_shortfall` false. Quorum is checked before the approval percentage, so a decision taken on too few ballots reports `ERR_QUORUM_NOT_MET`, and an adequately attended vote that falls short reports `ERR_APPROVAL_THRESHOLD_NOT_MET`. On success the slate moves `COMMITTEE_REVIEW → APPROVED` and every nominee whose `status` is `PROPOSED` becomes `CONFIRMED`; confirmed nominees remain inside the counted set, so no slate or firm aggregate moves.
- `REJECT` requires the slate to be at `COMMITTEE_REVIEW` with `quorum_met` true. The slate returns `COMMITTEE_REVIEW → DRAFT`, `revision_count` is incremented by 1, every `CAST` ballot becomes `VOIDED_BY_REVISION`, and the six tally fields are re-derived from the ballots that remain `CAST` — leaving all four counts at 0, `approval_pct` at 0.00 and `quorum_met` false. The nominees are untouched, so the committee can revise and resubmit the same card.
- `ABANDON` is available from `DRAFT` or `COMMITTEE_REVIEW` and terminates the cycle: the slate becomes `ABANDONED`, every nominee whose `status` is `PROPOSED` or `CONFIRMED` becomes `VOIDED` — which returns each `NOMINATED` candidate to `DEFERRED` with `deferred_cycle_year` recorded — every `CAST` ballot becomes `VOIDED_BY_REVISION`, and each sourcing firm re-derives `placed_nominee_count`, `committed_fee_cents`, `retainer_credit_applied_cents` and `net_payable_cents`. The slate itself then re-derives both families: its rollups collapse to an empty counted set — `nominee_count` and `independent_nominee_count` 0, `independent_nominee_pct` 0.00, `committed_search_fee_cents` 0, `independence_shortfall`, `contested` and `universal_proxy_applicable` all false — and its six tally figures fall to the four counts 0, `approval_pct` 0.00 and `quorum_met` false. Abandonment is not a revision, so `revision_count` is left exactly where it stood. Because `ABANDONED` is excluded from the per-`cycle_year` uniqueness rule, abandoning a slate frees that cycle year for a fresh one.

Every decision increments the slate's `version` by 1, and any decision attempted from `APPROVED`, `FILED`, `ELECTION_RECORDED` or `ABANDONED` is refused with `ERR_SLATE_STATE_INVALID`.

**Filing.** Filing moves the slate `APPROVED → FILED` and sets `filed_at` to the logical clock instant. One precondition attaches: when `universal_proxy_applicable` is true, the slate's `nominee_count` must be at or above its own `seat_count`, so a contested card is not filed with fewer nominees than there are seats standing for election, else `ERR_UNIVERSAL_PROXY_MINIMUM`. That test reads the stored `nominee_count` and `universal_proxy_applicable` as they already stand; filing re-derives no rollup of its own. The slate's `version` is incremented by 1.

**The election record.** Recording the shareholder-meeting result moves the slate `FILED → ELECTION_RECORDED` and takes an `elected_nominee_ids` array. Every id in that array must be a nominee of this slate whose `status` is `CONFIRMED`, and the number of ids may not exceed the slate's `seat_count`. Those three tests are reported in the ladder positions §2.10 gives them, which matters because they can fail together. An element that is not a string at all is a payload-shape failure, `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Each id is then resolved as a body identifier: one that names no nominee, and equally one that names a real nominee belonging to some *other* slate, reports `ERR_NOMINEE_NOT_FOUND`. Only then is the slate's own `FILED` state demanded, with `ERR_SLATE_STATE_INVALID`; so an election recorded against an `APPROVED` slate naming an unknown nominee reports the 404, while the same call naming a genuinely `PROPOSED` nominee of that slate reports the state error rather than `ERR_NOMINEE_STATE_INVALID`. The `CONFIRMED` test on the named nominees follows, and the `seat_count` ceiling — `ERR_SEAT_COUNT_EXCEEDED` — is weighed last of the four, so an over-long array of nominees that are not yet confirmed reports `ERR_NOMINEE_STATE_INVALID`. This is the second five-hop cascade in the system.

1. The slate becomes `ELECTION_RECORDED` with `election_recorded_at` set to the logical clock instant and `version` incremented by 1.
2. Each nominee whose `status` is `CONFIRMED` becomes `ELECTED` when its id appears in `elected_nominee_ids` and `NOT_ELECTED` when it does not. The slate's nominees are walked in ascending `ballot_order` — a total order over every nominee this hop can touch, since `ballot_order` is unique per slate among rows that are not `VOIDED` — so the directors minted in hop 3 are created in ballot order. Nominees already at `VOIDED`, `ELECTED` or `NOT_ELECTED` are passed over, and a duplicated id inside the array elects its nominee once.
3. Each nominee's candidate subject follows to `ELECTED` or `NOT_ELECTED`. For each newly `ELECTED` nominee whose `incumbent_director_id` is null, a new director row is minted in `service_status` `ONBOARDING` with `source_candidate_id` set to that candidate, `full_name`, `email` and `birth_date` copied from the candidate, `is_independent` copied from the nominee's `is_independent`, and `appointed_on` set to the logical clock's date. The rest of that row is server-chosen, since the cascade has no request body to read: `is_sitting_ceo_elsewhere` copied from the candidate's `is_sitting_ceo`, `is_management` false, `shares_held` 0, `compliance_status` `COMPLIANT`, `onboarding_status` `NOT_STARTED`, `onboarding_completion_pct` 0.00, `education_hours_cycle` 0.0, `retirement_notice_at` null, `tenure_years` 0 because the appointment is today, `age_years` computed from the copied `birth_date` against the logical clock, and `external_public_board_count` carried over as the size of the candidate's own outside public board set. For each `NOT_ELECTED` nominee whose `incumbent_director_id` is not null, that sitting director moves `SEATED → NOT_RENOMINATED`.
4. Because a director row was created in `ONBOARDING`, three required onboarding items are seeded for it: `module_code` `BOARD_ORIENTATION` in category `BUSINESS_ORIENTATION`, `module_code` `COMMITTEE_ONBOARD` in category `COMMITTEE_ONBOARDING`, and `module_code` `DOC_PORTAL` in category `DOCUMENT_ACCESS`. Each is created with `is_required` true, `credit_hours` 0.0, `status` `PENDING` and `due_on` set to the logical clock's date plus 90 days.
5. Because required items now exist for that director, its `onboarding_status` is derived as `NOT_STARTED` and its `onboarding_completion_pct` as 0.00.

Three further consequences complete the refreshment cycle. Every director whose `service_status` is `RETIREMENT_NOTICED` becomes `RETIRED`, and every director who leaves seated service through this cascade — whether `RETIRED` or `NOT_RENOMINATED` — has each of their `ACTIVE` committee assignments ended with `ended_reason_code` `RETIREMENT_NOTICE` and `ended_at` recorded, with each affected committee re-deriving its `filled_seats` and posture `status`. Last, because `NOT_ELECTED` leaves the counted nominee set while `ELECTED` stays inside it, every search firm that sourced a nominee of this slate re-derives its whole ledger, and the slate re-derives its own rollups from the survivors: `nominee_count`, `independent_nominee_count`, `independent_nominee_pct`, `independence_shortfall`, `committed_search_fee_cents`, `contested` and `universal_proxy_applicable` all settle onto the elected board rather than the card that was filed, and an unelected nominee's placement fee is released exactly as a void would have released it. Recording an election is not a revision either, so `revision_count` does not move.

### 3.8. Onboarding, Committee Seating and Continuing Education

**The onboarding programme.** An **onboarding item** is one module in a director's programme. It carries a `module_code` unique per director among rows whose `status` is not `WAIVED`, a `category` of `BUSINESS_ORIENTATION`, `COMMITTEE_ONBOARDING`, `DOCUMENT_ACCESS` or `CONTINUING_EDUCATION`, an `is_required` flag defaulting to true, a `credit_hours` figure defaulting to 0.0 and never negative, and an optional `due_on` date that is validated as an ISO 8601 calendar date when supplied and then stored and returned exactly as the client wrote it, defaulting to null when omitted. Items are created `PENDING` with `director_id` from the path, `completed_at` null and `waiver_reason` null, and adding one re-derives the owning director's onboarding dimension and nothing else — an item arriving in the programme never disturbs that director's `education_hours_cycle` or `compliance_status`, because a `PENDING` item contributes no credit and no posture. Adding an item is not one of the policy-dependent operations of §2.6, so a programme can be built while no `ACTIVE` policy is in force. Required items gate both the director's onboarding dimension and the seating transition; optional items do not.

Two closures are available from `PENDING`. **Completing** an item — which a `SeatedDirector` may do only for their own items, else `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` — moves it to `COMPLETED`. This is the one ownership test that cannot sit at its usual position in the ladder, because the `Director.id` it compares against is a stored field of the addressed item rather than a request input: the item must therefore be resolved first, so a `SeatedDirector` addressing an item id that does not exist receives that 404 rather than the ownership 403, while an item that does exist and belongs to somebody else reports `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` ahead of any state precondition. The closure sets `completed_at` to the logical clock instant. **Waiving** an item, reserved to the corporate secretary, requires a non-empty `waiver_reason` — a reason made only of whitespace counts as empty and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, tested as a payload-shape rule and therefore ahead of the addressed item's own existence and state — moves the item to `WAIVED` and records that reason; a waived required item counts as satisfied for the rollup but contributes no education credit. The two closures differ in their reach: completing an item re-derives the director's onboarding dimension, then their `education_hours_cycle`, then their `compliance_status`, while waiving one re-derives the onboarding dimension and the education total but deliberately leaves `compliance_status` alone, since a waiver can only ever reduce the hours a director has banked and is not the moment to re-post a delinquency. Either closure attempted on an item that is already `COMPLETED` or `WAIVED` is refused with `ERR_ONBOARDING_ITEM_STATE_INVALID`.

The director's onboarding dimension is a pure function of their items. Let the required set be the items whose `is_required` is true, and the satisfied set be those of them whose `status` is `COMPLETED` or `WAIVED`. When the required set is empty the `onboarding_status` is `NOT_STARTED` and the `onboarding_completion_pct` is 0.00. Otherwise, when every required item is satisfied the status is `COMPLETE`; when at least one item of that director — required or optional — is `COMPLETED` or `WAIVED` but some required item is still `PENDING`, the status is `IN_PROGRESS`; and when nothing has been touched at all the status is `NOT_STARTED`. The `onboarding_completion_pct` is 0.00 when nothing is required, and otherwise 100 multiplied by the size of the satisfied set divided by the size of the required set, rounded half up to two decimal places. Because the dimension is derived rather than latched, adding a new required item to a director whose programme was `COMPLETE` regresses that director to `IN_PROGRESS` and pulls the percentage back down.

**Seating a director.** Seating moves the service dimension `ONBOARDING → SEATED` and recomputes the compliance dimension. The director's `onboarding_status` must be exactly `COMPLETE`; a director whose programme is `NOT_STARTED` or `IN_PROGRESS` is refused with `ERR_ONBOARDING_INCOMPLETE`. The director being seated must fit inside the ceiling: when the count of directors already at `SEATED` plus one exceeds the policy's `authorized_seats` the request is refused with `ERR_SEAT_COUNT_EXCEEDED`, and a governing policy must exist. A director whose `service_status` is anything other than `ONBOARDING` cannot be seated, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_DIRECTOR_STATE_INVALID`. Note that a board may also be loaded directly: creating a director accepts an `initial_service_status` of `ONBOARDING` (the default) or `SEATED`, so an incumbent board can be recorded as seated without walking the programme, and every created director begins with `compliance_status` `COMPLIANT`, `onboarding_status` `NOT_STARTED`, and `age_years` and `tenure_years` computed from `birth_date` and `appointed_on` against the logical clock. That direct load is not measured against `authorized_seats` — the seat ceiling is enforced only by the seating operation, so a board loaded straight in as `SEATED` may exceed it — and a director's `email` is not required to be unique. Every created director further begins with `source_candidate_id` and `retirement_notice_at` null, `onboarding_completion_pct` 0.00, `external_public_board_count` 0, `education_hours_cycle` 0.0, and `shares_held` as supplied, defaulting to 0 and never negative.

**Departure.** A departure notice records a `notice_type` of `RETIREMENT` or `RESIGNATION` and takes an optional `effective_on` date, which is validated as an ISO 8601 calendar date when it is supplied but is not persisted on the director or echoed anywhere; the notice requires the director to be `SEATED`, else `ERR_DIRECTOR_STATE_INVALID`. `RETIREMENT` moves the director `SEATED → RETIREMENT_NOTICED`; `RESIGNATION` moves them `SEATED → RESIGNED`. Both set `retirement_notice_at` to the logical clock instant and cascade three hops: every committee assignment of that director whose `status` is `ACTIVE` becomes `ENDED` with `ended_reason_code` `RETIREMENT_NOTICE` and `ended_at` recorded, and each affected committee re-derives its `filled_seats` and posture `status`. A resignation additionally voids every compensation award of that director whose `status` is `DRAFT`, which releases that award's per-director per-cycle uniqueness slot. A director who is `RETIREMENT_NOTICED` remains renominatable — that is the mechanism by which a retiring director can stand one final time — and becomes `RETIRED` only when the next election is recorded.

**Committees.** A committee is chartered with a `code` of `AUDIT`, `COMPENSATION`, `NOMINATING_GOVERNANCE` or `RISK` that is unique across committees, a `name` that is also unique across committees, an `authorized_seats` count of at least 1, a `min_seats` figure defaulting to 3 and itself at least 1 — the two are not compared with each other, so a committee may be chartered whose `min_seats` exceeds its `authorized_seats`, and such a committee can never reach `AT_STRENGTH` — and the two independence flags `requires_all_independent` and `requires_financial_expert`, both defaulting to false. The committee is created with `filled_seats` 0, `status` `UNDER_STRENGTH` and `version` 1.

A **committee assignment** names a `director_id`, a `term_start_on` and `term_end_on` pair, an optional `seat_role` of `MEMBER` (the default) or `CHAIR`, and the mandatory `expected_committee_version` echo of §2.8. The `term_end_on` must be on or after the `term_start_on`, else `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`. Terms are **closed intervals**, covering both endpoint dates, and the requested term may not overlap any assignment of the same committee-and-director pair whose `status` is `PROPOSED` or `ACTIVE`; because both ends are inclusive, a term beginning exactly on the day a prior term ends *does* overlap and is refused with `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_TERM_OVERLAP`. When the committee's `requires_all_independent` is true, the assigned director's `is_independent` must be true, else `ERR_COMMITTEE_INDEPENDENCE_REQUIRED`; that independence guideline is weighed **before** the overlap test, per §2.10, so proposing an overlapping term for a non-independent director on an all-independent committee reports the independence code and not `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_TERM_OVERLAP`. The assignment is created `PROPOSED` with `committee_id` from the path, `activated_at` null, `ended_at` null and `ended_reason_code` null, and the committee's `version` is incremented by 1.

**Activating** an assignment moves it `PROPOSED → ACTIVE` and sets `activated_at` to the logical clock instant. The assigned director's `service_status` must be exactly `SEATED` — an `ONBOARDING`, `RETIREMENT_NOTICED`, `RETIRED`, `RESIGNED` or `NOT_RENOMINATED` director may not take a committee seat — and the committee must not be `SUSPENDED`; either failure is refused with `ERR_DIRECTOR_NOT_SEATABLE`, the director's own status being weighed before the committee's. The charter's independence requirement is then re-tested at activation rather than trusted from the proposal, so a director who was independent when the seat was proposed and has since ceased to be cannot be activated into an all-independent committee and is refused with `ERR_COMMITTEE_INDEPENDENCE_REQUIRED`. When the `seat_role` is `CHAIR`, no other assignment of that committee whose `status` is `ACTIVE` may already hold `seat_role` `CHAIR`, else `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`. Activation may not push `filled_seats` above the committee's `authorized_seats`, else `ERR_COMMITTEE_SEATS_EXHAUSTED` — and because uniqueness precedes constraint satisfaction in §2.10, activating a second chair into a committee that is also full reports the duplicate-chair code rather than the exhausted-seats one. **Revoking** an assignment is the compensating operation: available from `PROPOSED` or `ACTIVE`, it sets `status` to `REVOKED`, `ended_reason_code` to `REVOCATION` and `ended_at` to the logical clock instant. Both operations increment the committee's `version` by 1, and either operation attempted on an assignment that is already `ENDED` or `REVOKED` is refused with `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_STATE_INVALID`.

The committee's **seat ledger** is re-derived after every event that moves an assignment into or out of `ACTIVE`. The `filled_seats` is the count of that committee's assignments whose `status` is exactly `ACTIVE`; `PROPOSED`, `ENDED` and `REVOKED` rows never occupy a seat. The posture is then decided in a fixed order: the `status` is `SUSPENDED` when any `ACTIVE` assignment belongs to a director whose `compliance_status` is `OVERBOARDED`; failing that it is `AT_STRENGTH` when the `filled_seats` is at or above the committee's `min_seats` **and** at least one `ACTIVE` assignment holds `seat_role` `CHAIR`; and otherwise it is `UNDER_STRENGTH`. An activation followed by a revocation therefore returns `filled_seats` to exactly its prior value and re-derives the same posture from that count.

**Continuing education.** A director's `education_hours_cycle` is the sum of `credit_hours` over that director's items whose `category` is `CONTINUING_EDUCATION`, whose `status` is `COMPLETED`, and whose `completed_at` falls inside the governing policy's `cycle_year` — the comparison reads the four-digit year that opens the recorded instant. The sum is rounded half up to two decimal places, so a fractional credit-hour total is stored to the cent-like precision of the rest of the record. Waived items contribute nothing, items whose `completed_at` was never recorded contribute nothing, and items completed in a different cycle year contribute nothing. With no `ACTIVE` policy in force there is no cycle to measure against and the figure derives as 0.00 however many items have been completed, which is why the total can fall back to zero and then return when a policy is adopted. Completing a continuing-education item therefore refreshes both the onboarding rollup and the compliance dimension, and it is the route by which a director clears an `EDUCATION_DELINQUENT` posture.

### 3.9. Director Compensation, Compliance Monitoring and the Governance Policy Record

**Building an award.** A **compensation award** is built for one director and one `cycle_year` from a `service_start_on` and `service_end_on` date pair, a `share_price_cents` of at least 1 used to value the director's holdings, and a `shares_held` count that may not be negative. The `service_end_on` must be on or after the `service_start_on`, else `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`; the `cycle_year` must be at least 2000 and is unique per director among rows whose `status` is not `VOIDED`, else `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`; and a governing policy must exist, else `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_POLICY`. Those gates fall in the ladder order of §2.10, so the `cycle_year` floor is reached before the interval comparison, the addressed director is resolved before the policy is demanded, and the policy is demanded before the per-cycle uniqueness rule — a second award for a cycle year the director already holds, attempted while no policy is in force, therefore reports `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_POLICY` rather than `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`. Every money figure on the award is computed by the server at creation time and none of them may be supplied by the client.

The waterfall runs in a pinned order.

1. The `service_days` is the inclusive day span of the service window: the number of days from `service_start_on` to `service_end_on`, plus one, so that both endpoint dates are paid.
2. The `committee_member_seat_count` and `committee_chair_seat_count` are snapshotted from the director's committee assignments at award-creation time — the count of their assignments whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `seat_role` is `MEMBER`, and the count whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `seat_role` is `CHAIR`. Assignments that are `PROPOSED`, `ENDED` or `REVOKED` contribute nothing.
3. The `prorated_cash_retainer_cents` is the policy's `annual_cash_retainer_cents` multiplied by the `service_days` and divided by 365, rounded half up to whole cents.
4. The `committee_fees_cents` is the `committee_member_seat_count` multiplied by the policy's `committee_member_fee_cents`, plus the `committee_chair_seat_count` multiplied by the policy's `committee_chair_premium_cents`. A chair seat therefore pays the chair premium in place of the member fee, not in addition to it.
5. The `equity_value_cents` is a frozen copy of the policy's `equity_grant_value_cents` taken at award creation, so a later policy adoption never rewrites an existing award.
6. The `pre_offset_total_cents` is the `prorated_cash_retainer_cents` plus the `committee_fees_cents` plus the `equity_value_cents`.
7. The `excess_offset_pct` is selected **from the `pre_offset_total_cents` and never re-derived from the `total_cents`**: it is 0.0 when the `pre_offset_total_cents` is at or below 30000000 cents, 2.5 when it is above that and at or below 40000000 cents, and 5.0 when it is above 40000000 cents.
8. The `excess_offset_cents` is the `excess_offset_pct` taken as a percentage of the `pre_offset_total_cents`, rounded half up to whole cents.
9. The `total_cents` is the `pre_offset_total_cents` less the `excess_offset_cents`.

The tier-keying rule in step 7 is load-bearing. A `pre_offset_total_cents` above 40000000 attracts the 5.0 percent tier even when subtracting that offset leaves a `total_cents` back below 40000000 cents; re-deriving the tier from the net figure would select 2.5 percent and produce a different, wrong award.

The stock-ownership guideline is computed from **cash compensation only**. The `ownership_requirement_cents` is the policy's `ownership_multiple` applied to the sum of the `prorated_cash_retainer_cents` and the `committee_fees_cents`, rounded half up to whole cents — the `equity_value_cents` and the offset are both excluded from that base. The `ownership_actual_cents` is the award's `shares_held` multiplied by its `share_price_cents`, and `ownership_compliant` is true when the actual value is **at or above** the requirement, so an exact match complies. The award is created `DRAFT` with `finalized_at` null.

**Finalizing an award** moves it `DRAFT → FINALIZED` and sets `finalized_at` to the logical clock instant. The award must be `DRAFT`, else `ERR_COMP_AWARD_STATE_INVALID`, and the owning director's `service_status` must be `ONBOARDING`, `SEATED` or `RETIREMENT_NOTICED`, else `ERR_DIRECTOR_STATE_INVALID`. Finalizing copies the award's `shares_held` onto the director's own `shares_held` and recomputes that director's `compliance_status`. That copy is unconditional: the director row is written, and so has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented, even when the figure it already carried was identical — one of the few writes that stands outside the no-op rule of §2.9, because it is a direct write of a supplied figure rather than a recomputation. Awards in `DRAFT` never influence compliance; only a `FINALIZED` award does, and where a director holds several, the **governing award** is chosen by sorting their `FINALIZED` awards on `cycle_year` descending, then on `finalized_at` descending — an award still carrying a null `finalized_at` sorting as the empty string and therefore last among its cycle year — then on `created_at` descending, and finally, where even those are identical, on insertion order with the row written later in the process ranking first; identifiers never rank. The first row of that ordering is the governing award. An award may also become `VOIDED` — the resignation cascade voids every `DRAFT` award of the departing director — and a `VOIDED` award is outside both the uniqueness rule and the governing-award selection.

**The compliance dimension.** A director's `compliance_status` is derived, never commanded, and it is recomputed whenever an outside seat is recorded or ended, an award is finalized, an onboarding item is completed, a director is seated, or the logical clock advances. Every leg is stated in terms of a policy threshold, so with no `ACTIVE` policy in force the whole derivation is abandoned and the director keeps whatever `compliance_status` they were already carrying — the posture is never reset to `COMPLIANT` merely because the thresholds have gone away. Three legs are evaluated in a **fixed order, and the first match wins**.

1. **Overboarding.** The applicable limit is the policy's `max_public_boards_sitting_ceo` when the director's `is_sitting_ceo_elsewhere` is true and its `max_public_boards_default` otherwise. When the director's `external_public_board_count` plus one is **strictly greater** than that limit, the `compliance_status` is `OVERBOARDED`.
2. **Ownership.** Otherwise, when a governing award exists whose `ownership_compliant` is false **and** the logical clock's date is at or after the director's `appointed_on` advanced by the policy's `ownership_grace_years` years — advancing a calendar date by whole years keeps the same month and day, except that a 29 February landing in a non-leap year is clamped back to the 28th — the `compliance_status` is `OWNERSHIP_SHORTFALL`. Inside the grace window a shortfall is recorded on the award but does not move the director's posture.
3. **Education.** Otherwise, when the logical clock's date is at or after 31 December of the policy's `cycle_year` **and** the director's `education_hours_cycle` is **strictly less** than the policy's `education_hours_required`, the `compliance_status` is `EDUCATION_DELINQUENT`.
4. When no leg matches, the `compliance_status` is `COMPLIANT`.

Whenever this derivation actually moves a director's posture, the move propagates: every committee holding an `ACTIVE` assignment of that director re-derives its `filled_seats` and posture `status`, which is the mechanism by which a director tipping into `OVERBOARDED` suspends their committees and by which clearing that posture releases them again. A recomputation that leaves the posture where it was propagates nothing.

Because the legs are ordered, an overboarded director who also has an ownership shortfall reports `OVERBOARDED`, and the shortfall becomes visible only once the outside seat is ended. Advancing the logical clock recomputes `age_years` and `tenure_years` for every director and recomputes `compliance_status` for every director whose `service_status` is not `RETIRED`, `RESIGNED` or `NOT_RENOMINATED`, which is the only way an `EDUCATION_DELINQUENT` posture becomes reachable: the education deadline is a date, and the date only arrives when the clock is moved. Completing a continuing-education item that brings `education_hours_cycle` up to the requirement clears the posture back to `COMPLIANT`, provided the overboarding and ownership legs both pass.

Independence of a seated director is a separate matter from compliance. The `is_independent` flag on a director defaults to true, and a director whose `is_management` flag is true is never independent. That flag is what a committee's `requires_all_independent` charter tests against, and it is what a renomination copies into the nominee's `is_independent`, so a management director on a slate lowers that slate's `independent_nominee_pct` exactly as a non-independent candidate would.

**Adopting a governance policy.** The corporate secretary adopts a policy with a `cycle_year` of at least 2000 and an `effective_from` instant; every threshold field is optional and falls back to the default declared for it. The new `effective_from` must be **strictly after** the currently `ACTIVE` policy's `effective_from`, else `ERR_CYCLE_YEAR_NOT_ADVANCED`, and only one `ACTIVE` policy may hold a given `cycle_year` — an invariant that the supersede step below maintains on its own rather than a precondition on the request, so adopting a policy whose `cycle_year` an already-`ACTIVE` policy holds is accepted, and policy adoption is not one of the uniqueness rules of §2.10 and never reports `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`. Adoption is a two-part act: every prior policy whose `status` is `ACTIVE` becomes `SUPERSEDED`, and the new row is created `ACTIVE` and immediately becomes the governing policy under the selection rule of §2.6. The full set of adjustable thresholds, each shown with the default it falls back to when the request omits it, is `authorized_seats` 9, `independence_min_pct` 66.67, `independence_standard` `BRIGHT_LINE`, `bright_line_payment_floor_cents` 100000000, `bright_line_revenue_pct` 2.0, `bright_line_lookback_years` 3, `enhanced_lookback_years` 5, `enhanced_charitable_pct` 2.0, `max_public_boards_default` 4, `max_public_boards_sitting_ceo` 2, `interlock_max_shared_boards` 2, `mandatory_retirement_age` 75, `max_tenure_years` 15, `min_completed_interviews` 3, `quorum_size` 3, `approval_threshold_pct` 66.67, `annual_cash_retainer_cents` 12000000, `committee_member_fee_cents` 1500000, `committee_chair_premium_cents` 2500000, `equity_grant_value_cents` 20000000, `ownership_multiple` 5.0, `ownership_grace_years` 5, `education_hours_required` 8.0 and `readiness_min_score` 60.0. The four percentage thresholds — `independence_min_pct`, `bright_line_revenue_pct`, `enhanced_charitable_pct` and `approval_threshold_pct` — must lie from 0.0 through 100.0 inclusive, `authorized_seats` must be at least 1, and every other numeric threshold must be at least 0; a violation of any of those bounds, like a `cycle_year` below 2000, is refused with `ERR_VALUE_OUT_OF_RANGE`. The `effective_from` is required and is read as an ISO 8601 instant — a string that cannot be parsed as one is an `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and it is stored and returned verbatim as the client supplied it, while the ordering comparisons of §2.6 and the `ERR_CYCLE_YEAR_NOT_ADVANCED` test read the instant it denotes. That advancement test is made against the governing policy selected by §2.6 and is skipped entirely when no `ACTIVE` policy exists, so the very first adoption may carry any `effective_from`.

Adoption is prospective. Frozen copies already written onto existing rows — a screen's `standard_applied`, an award's `equity_value_cents` — are never rewritten, but every subsequent evaluation reads the new thresholds. A policy adopted with a stricter `independence_standard` therefore changes the outcome of screens submitted after it takes effect while leaving earlier determinations exactly as they were recorded.
