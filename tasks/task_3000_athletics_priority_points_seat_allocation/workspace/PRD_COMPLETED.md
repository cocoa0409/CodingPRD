# Athletics Priority Points & Seat Allocation API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A university athletics development office lives or dies by the credibility of one number. Every donor in the book is told, once a year, how many **priority points** they hold; that single figure decides who chooses a seat first at the season-ticket selection event, who gets into the postseason allocation at all, and whose relocation request into a better section is even considered. When the number is wrong — or merely looks wrong — the office does not lose an argument, it loses a donor. This document specifies the service that computes that number, defends it against disputes, and then spends it: ranking the donor base, handing out timed selection slots, pricing each seat against a per-section donation requirement, and re-seating an entire section when a renovation changes the seat map underneath everyone.

The service is deliberately built around a *two-figure* discipline. Every annual recalculation produces a **PROVISIONAL** score, which is the generous, pledge-inclusive figure quoted to the donor in the communication that opens the dispute window, and a **CERTIFIED** score, which admits only settled money and is the only figure any ranking, slotting, or eligibility decision is ever allowed to read. The gap between the two is where most donor conversations happen, so both are computed on every run, both are explained line-by-line in an append-only point ledger, and neither is ever silently overwritten. Certification is the moment the CERTIFIED figure becomes load-bearing: ranks are stamped, already-published selection slots that no longer reflect the donor's rank are invalidated, and every downstream request that depended on the old ranking is walked back to a safe state rather than left to execute against stale data.

Three structural constraints shape the API. First, the office is subject to segregation of duties: the officer who opens a recalculation may not be the person who certifies it, and the analyst who takes a dispute under review may not be the person who decides it. Second, seat inventory is contended — two donors picking from the same stale view of a section must not both walk away believing they hold the seat — so seat picks and a handful of other writes are guarded by explicit version stamps. Third, money and points are both exact quantities: giving is carried in integer cents, points to two decimal places, and every rounding step in every formula is pinned so that the office's spreadsheet and the service agree to the last cent. The API is mounted under `/athletics`, with a single test-driving endpoint outside that prefix for controlling the server's logical clock.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Callers, Identity and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the service — without exception, including the clock-control endpoint — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the caller, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capability set the caller is acting under. If either header is absent, or present but empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before anything else about the request is examined. If both headers are present but the value of `X-User-Role` is not one of the roles listed for the endpoint being called, the request is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403). There is no session, no token exchange, and no notion of a caller holding more than one role in a single request.

Six roles exist, and their names are matched exactly as written here:

- **`DevelopmentOfficer`** — development-office staff who maintain the donor book, record gifts, and drive the annual recalculation up to the point of communication. They also create and gate selection events, decide relocation requests, and advance seat assignments on both the occupancy and billing axes.
- **`PointsCertifier`** — the independent certifier who freezes an annual cycle and who moves, or reverses, approved point transfers.
- **`DisputeAnalyst`** — the analyst who works donor point disputes: taking them under review and requesting supporting information. An analyst never decides a dispute.
- **`PolicyCommittee`** — the committee that decides disputes and that grants or refuses the policy exceptions certain point transfers require.
- **`Donor`** — the donor acting on their own behalf: filing disputes, answering information requests, consuming a selection slot to take a seat, forfeiting a slot, and asking to relocate or upgrade.
- **`PlatformAdmin`** — the operator who owns venue inventory, section pricing and the renovation pipeline, and who suspends or reinstates donors. A `PlatformAdmin` stands alongside the `DevelopmentOfficer` on most development-office writes — the donor book, gifts, the recalculation up to communication, selection-event creation, publication and gating, both seat-assignment axes, and relocation decisions — and is additionally the only role that may write a `DELINQUENT` seat assignment off. Its reach is not total, however, and the role lists of §4 are the authority endpoint by endpoint. Beyond the writes reserved to the certifier, the analyst and the committee, three donor-facing writes admit the `Donor` and the `DevelopmentOfficer` and nobody else, a `PlatformAdmin` included: filing a point dispute, forfeiting a selection slot, and filing a relocation request. A `PlatformAdmin` attempting one of those three is refused with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` exactly as any other unlisted role would be.

Identity semantics differ by role, and the difference matters for ownership checks. When `X-User-Role` is `Donor`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting donor record; ownership checks on any resource that references a donor compare that resource's donor reference to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a `Donor` caller may only file a dispute whose `donor_id` is their own, only answer an information request on a dispute they own, only consume or forfeit a selection slot whose `donor_id` is their own, and only file a relocation request whose `donor_id` is their own. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). Those four are the whole of it: a seat assignment carries a `donor_id` but no ownership check ever fires on one, because neither of the assignment's two write axes admits a `Donor` caller at all — a donor calling the occupancy or billing endpoint is turned away at the door with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`, never with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. Nor does ownership narrow a read: the collection and single-row reads a `Donor` may make — seat assignments and relocation requests among them — return the same rows they would return for an officer, so a donor-scoped listing is obtained by passing `donor_id` as a query filter and never by the server inferring the caller's own rows. When `X-User-Role` is `DevelopmentOfficer`, `PointsCertifier`, `DisputeAnalyst`, `PolicyCommittee` or `PlatformAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity: the server records it where a workflow demands an audit trail — as `initiated_by_user_id` and `certified_by_user_id` on a recalculation, as `reviewed_by_user_id` and `decided_by_user_id` on a dispute, and as `exception_granted_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` on a point transfer — but never compares it to any entity's `id`.

Two of those recorded identifiers exist purely to enforce **segregation of duties**, and both comparisons are made on `X-User-ID` values, so they bite even when the two actors hold different roles. Certifying an annual recalculation requires that `X-User-ID` differ from the recalculation's `initiated_by_user_id`; deciding a dispute requires that `X-User-ID` differ from the dispute's `reviewed_by_user_id`. Either violation is reported as `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`, followed by 20 characters drawn from the uppercase letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, read from the logical clock of §2.4 rather than from wall-clock time, so two requests made without the clock moving in between carry the same value.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.
- A request whose path this service does not define, or whose method is not defined on a path that it does, is answered with this same error envelope carrying `error_id` `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and the transport's own status — 404 for an unknown path and 405 for an unsupported method — which is the one place an `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` body does not carry HTTP 400.

### 2.3. Pagination, Filtering and Ordering of Collections

Every list endpoint in the service accepts two pagination parameters on the query string. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, must be at least 1, and must not exceed 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and must be at least 0. Either parameter supplied with an empty value counts as omitted and falls back to its default. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100 inclusive, a negative `offset`, or either parameter supplied as something that is not an integer, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside `meta`, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` is true exactly when rows remain beyond the current page.

Unless a specific collection states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Because the logical clock moves only when a caller moves it, two rows written without an intervening clock move share a `created_at`; such a tie is broken by the order in which the rows were written, and a row's `id` is never an input to any ordering. One collection overrides this: the selection-slot list is ordered by `slot_index` ascending by default, because a slot's position in its event's window is the only order a reader of that collection cares about; two slots that share a `slot_index` — which happens as soon as the list spans more than one event — fall back to `created_at` ascending and then to write order, so the whole collection is deterministic without ever consulting an `id`. The donor list additionally accepts a `sort` parameter for callers who want a different ordering of the donor book, alongside its filters `donor_status`, `alumni_status`, `entity_type`, `household_partner_donor_id` and `min_certified_points`, the last of which admits a donor whose `certified_points` is at least the supplied figure. A `sort` value names one stored donor field, and the sortable set is exactly these eleven: `created_at`, `updated_at`, `full_name`, `email`, `donor_status`, `certified_points`, `provisional_points`, `cumulative_giving_cents`, `consecutive_giving_years`, `season_ticket_seasons_purchased` and `rank_position`. Descending order is asked for either by prefixing the field name with `-`, or by appending a direction to it after a colon, a space or a comma, or by appending the literal suffix `_desc`; `desc` and `descending` are the two direction words that select descending, and `_asc` or any other direction word leaves the order ascending — so `-certified_points`, `certified_points:desc` and `certified_points_desc` are three spellings of one request. A `sort` naming a field outside that set is **not** rejected: it is ignored and the collection falls back to `created_at` ascending, exactly as an omitted or empty `sort` does. The sort is stable, so donors who tie on the named field stay in `created_at` ascending order, and when the field is `rank_position` the donors whose rank is still null sort after every ranked donor ascending and before every ranked donor descending. Other collections offer filters drawn from their own fields: gifts by `gift_status`, `cycle_year` and `designation`; point transfers by `status` and `basis`; point-ledger entries by `donor_id`, `recalculation_id`, `entry_type` and `variant`; selection events by `event_type` and `status`; disputes by `donor_id`, `recalculation_id` and `status`; sections by `status`, `renovation_status`, `reseating_recalculation_id`, `pricing_posture` and `venue_code`; seats by `seat_status` and `row_label`; seat assignments by `donor_id`, `seat_id`, `selection_slot_id`, `occupancy_status`, `payment_status`, `eligibility_status` and `pricing_mode`; selection slots by `selection_event_id`, `donor_id` and `slot_status`; and relocation requests by `donor_id`, `current_seat_assignment_id`, `target_section_id`, `selection_event_id`, `granted_seat_assignment_id`, `request_type` and `status`. Every filter is optional, and supplying several narrows the result set by conjunction. A filter supplied with an empty value counts as absent; a filter value outside its field's enumeration, or a non-numeric value where the field is numeric, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; and a query parameter this specification does not define for the collection is ignored.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties and the Logical Clock

Each persisted resource carries a server-minted `id`: a UUID string with no type prefix and no embedded semantics. Clients never supply an `id`, and no `id` value is ever an input to a ranking, ordering or tie-break decision anywhere in this specification. Alongside `id`, every resource carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with exactly three fractional-second digits, recorded when the row was first written. Most resources also carry `updated_at`, in the same format, refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increases by exactly 1 on each mutation that the specification says advances it. There is one exception: a point-ledger entry is append-only and therefore carries only `id` and `created_at` — it has neither `updated_at` nor `version`, and no operation ever rewrites one. Donors, gifts, recalculations, disputes, transfers, sections, seats, seat assignments, selection events, selection slots and relocation requests all carry the full set.

The server keeps a **logical clock** rather than reading wall-clock time. Before any call is made the clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time. A test-driving endpoint moves it: the caller supplies `to_iso8601`, the instant the clock should read from that moment on, and every role may call it; the call answers 200 with an empty `data` object. `to_iso8601`, and every other instant a request body supplies, is accepted in any ordinary ISO 8601 form — a trailing `Z` or an explicit UTC offset, any number of fractional-second digits, a form carrying no zone at all (read as UTC), or a bare calendar date (read as midnight UTC) — and is normalised to UTC on the way in, while instants in responses are always rendered in the three-fractional-digit UTC form no matter how they were supplied. Because every window in the system — a slot's pick window, a recalculation's dispute window, a seat hold's expiry — is evaluated against this clock, moving it is what makes those windows open and close. Fields the specification describes as "the current logical clock instant" — `status_effective_date` after a status change, `settled_at`, `certified_at`, `decided_at`, `reversed_at`, `computed_at`, `hold_start` and `hold_end` — are all written from the logical clock and all use the three-fractional-digit ISO 8601 UTC form.

### 2.5. Version Stamps and the Rejection of Stale Writes

Several writes in this service are guarded by **optimistic concurrency control**: the caller must echo back the `version` of a named parent resource as it stood when the caller last read it, and the server refuses the write if that parent has moved on since. In every such case the echoed value is a required field of the request body, not an optional hint, and a mismatch against the parent's current `version` is reported as `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The guarded writes and their fields are: `expected_donor_version` against the target donor's `version` on a donor status change, on a household link, and on the creation of a relocation request; `expected_recalculation_version` against the recalculation's `version` at certification; `expected_source_donor_version` against the source donor's `version` when a point transfer is created; `expected_section_version` against the section's `version` on a reprice and on every renovation action; `expected_seat_version` against the seat's `version` when a selection slot is consumed to pick that seat; and `expected_seat_assignment_version` against the seat assignment's `version` when its occupancy axis is advanced. Endpoints not named in that list take no version field at all, and supplying one to them has no effect.

Two of these guards carry weight beyond bookkeeping, because in both cases no state precondition would otherwise reject the second writer. Creating a relocation request — `POST /athletics/relocation-requests`, whose parent for this purpose is the `Donor` — is one: a donor is explicitly permitted to file a replacement request that supersedes their own prior outstanding one, so the request's own state machine cannot tell a legitimate replacement apart from a client working from an outdated view. A create whose `expected_donor_version` does not match the donor's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412) — the stale stamp is the only signal that the writer's picture of the donor, including the request it is about to supersede, is out of date. Picking a seat — `POST /athletics/selection-slots/{id}/pick`, whose parent for this purpose is the `Seat` — is the other: once a prior hold's window has ended, a later picker's hold legitimately supersedes it, so again no state check fires. A pick whose `expected_seat_version` does not match the seat's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412), telling the picker that the seat was chosen from an inventory view that has since changed.

### 2.6. Exactness: Cents, Points and Rounding

Money is carried in integer cents throughout — there are no fractional-cent amounts anywhere, and every field whose name ends in `_cents` is an integer. Points are decimal quantities carried to exactly 2 decimal places. In JSON a `_cents` field is an integer number and a point, ratio or percentage-cap field is a decimal JSON number — never a string — whose value is exact to its stated number of decimal places; on the way in a numeric field may be supplied either as a JSON number or as a numeric string, a point value supplied with more than 2 decimal places is rounded half up to 2 places before any range check is applied to it rather than being rejected, and a value carrying a fractional part where an integer is required is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Every rounding operation described in this document rounds **half up**: a value exactly at the midpoint rounds away from zero. All point arithmetic is performed on exact decimal values, never on binary floating-point, and intermediate results are rounded only where this document says a rounding step occurs — inserting or omitting a rounding step changes the answer and is a defect.

Point results are floored at zero: a computation that would yield a negative score yields `0.00` instead. Where a formula produces a ratio rather than a currency or point amount, the ratio is rounded half up to 4 decimal places before it is compared with a threshold or returned. Where a formula produces integer cents from a multiplication, the product is rounded half up to whole cents at the point the formula says so, and the rounded figure — not the raw product — is what participates in any later comparison or sum.

### 2.7. Path Resolution and Referential Integrity

Path parameters are resolved strictly left to right, and the first one that fails to resolve determines the response. Each collection has its own not-found code: `ERR_DONOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_GIFT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RECALCULATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LEDGER_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DISPUTE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRANSFER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SECTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SEAT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SEAT_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SELECTION_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SELECTION_SLOT_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_RELOCATION_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, each carrying HTTP 404. So a request naming a seat inside a section reports the section's code first if the section id is unknown, and only reaches the seat check once the section resolves.

Identifiers supplied inside a request body are a separate concern and report a separate code. When a body names a row that does not exist — a partner donor on a household link, the donor or recalculation on a dispute, the source or recipient donor on a transfer, the reseating recalculation on a renovation, the seat on a pick, the current assignment, target section or selection event on a relocation request, or the target seat on a relocation decision — the response is `ERR_REFERENCED_ENTITY_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404).

### 2.8. The Order in Which Requests Are Checked

Because a single request can violate several rules at once, the order of evaluation is part of the contract. Checks run strictly in the sequence below; the **first** rule that a request violates determines the response, and no later rule is evaluated. A test that expects a 409 where the request also happens to be forbidden will see the 403, because the auth checks sit above the state checks.

1. Both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` are present and non-empty — on every endpoint, and a header whose value consists only of whitespace counts as empty. Violation: `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. `X-User-Role` is one of the roles listed for the endpoint — on every endpoint. Violation: `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403).
3. For a caller acting as `Donor`, `X-User-ID` equals the `donor_id` of the targeted dispute, selection slot or relocation request. This applies to exactly five operations: filing a point dispute, providing information on a dispute, picking a seat through a selection slot, forfeiting a selection slot, and creating a relocation request. Violation: `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). No seat-assignment operation appears in this step, because neither assignment axis admits a `Donor` caller at all — such a call is already refused at step 2. This step's position relative to step 5 is the one place where the ladder is not uniform, and the difference is observable. On the three operations that address an existing row through the path — providing information on a dispute, picking a seat, and forfeiting a slot — the path identifier is resolved *before* the owner is compared, so for those three this check effectively sits below step 5: a donor naming a dispute or slot that does not exist is answered with that collection's own 404 rather than with a 403, and the ownership comparison is reached only once the row is in hand. On the two operations that carry no path parameter — filing a dispute and filing a relocation request — the comparand is the `donor_id` in the request body, and the check runs exactly where it stands here: after the body has been confirmed to be a JSON object, but ahead of step 6's field validation and step 7's reference resolution, so a donor claiming somebody else's `donor_id` is refused before the rest of the body is judged and whether or not the donor named actually exists. On those two the comparison can only fire when `donor_id` is present and is a JSON string; omitted, sent as `null`, or sent as a number it cannot fire at all and evaluation falls through to step 6.
4. Segregation of duties: at certification `X-User-ID` differs from the recalculation's `initiated_by_user_id`, and at a dispute decision `X-User-ID` differs from the dispute's `reviewed_by_user_id`. This applies to exactly those two operations. Where the comparand is still null — a dispute nobody has taken under review yet carries no `reviewed_by_user_id` — the comparison cannot fire and evaluation falls through to the state check at step 9. Violation: `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
5. Every path identifier resolves, checked left to right — on every endpoint that has path parameters. Violation: the collection-specific 404 code from §2.7.
6. The body parses; required fields are present; enum values are recognised; numeric and string constraints hold; `limit` is within 1 through 100 and `offset` is at least 0 — on every endpoint. An absent or empty request body reads as an empty JSON object, so an endpoint with no required fields accepts a bodyless call, while a body that is not a JSON object is malformed; and a field present with an explicit JSON `null` is treated exactly as if it had been omitted, so a required field sent as `null` is reported as missing and an optional one falls back to its default. Violation: `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400).
7. Every identifier supplied in the body resolves to an existing row. This applies to the household link, dispute creation, transfer creation, the renovation action, a seat pick, relocation-request creation, and a relocation decision. Violation: `ERR_REFERENCED_ENTITY_NOT_FOUND` (404).
8. The supplied version stamp equals the parent's current `version`. This applies to a donor status change, a household link, certification, transfer creation, a section reprice, a renovation action, a seat pick, an occupancy change, and relocation-request creation. Violation: `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412).
9. The target resource's current state admits the requested transition — on every state-transition endpoint. Violation: `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409). Two situations are carved out of this rule and reported by a later, more specific check instead: approving a transfer that is still in `EXCEPTION_PENDING` is reported at step 13 as `ERR_TRANSFER_POLICY_EXCEPTION_REQUIRED`, and picking through a slot in `STALE` whose event has fallen to `SLOTS_STALE` is reported at step 12 as `ERR_EVENT_SLOTS_STALE`. A third situation keeps the 409 but loses its place in the ladder: on the occupancy confirmation, the refusal of a row whose `eligibility_status` has drifted to `UNDERWATER` is an `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409) like any other state refusal, yet it is weighed *after* the money check of step 25, so a row that is at once short of its bill and `UNDERWATER` answers the 402 shortfall rather than the 409. Every other state precondition on that endpoint — the requirement that the row stand in `HELD`, and the refusal of `HELD` as a destination — is weighed here at step 9 as usual, ahead of the money.
10. The recalculation the operation depends on is in `CERTIFIED`. This applies to publishing slots for a selection event, starting a section's reseating, and reading a recalculation's retention analysis. Violation: `ERR_RECALCULATION_NOT_CERTIFIED` (409).
11. The section holding the target seat is `OPEN` rather than `UNDER_RENOVATION` or `CLOSED`. This applies to a seat pick, a relocation decision, and adding a seat to a section. Violation: `ERR_SECTION_UNDER_RENOVATION` (409).
12. The selection event is `OPEN` for a pick, and either `SLOTS_PUBLISHED` or `OPEN` for a relocation request; an event in `SLOTS_STALE` accepts neither. A pick against a stale event reports `ERR_EVENT_SLOTS_STALE`, and a relocation request against an event in any other status reports `ERR_EVENT_NOT_OPEN_FOR_REQUESTS`; both carry HTTP 409. This applies to picks and to relocation-request creation.
13. A transfer whose basis precondition does not hold may not be approved until the policy committee has granted the exception. Approving a transfer still in `EXCEPTION_PENDING` yields `ERR_TRANSFER_POLICY_EXCEPTION_REQUIRED` (422). This applies to transfer approval only.
14. The current logical clock instant lies inside the slot's pick window, which includes `slot_start` and excludes `slot_end`. Violation: `ERR_OUTSIDE_SLOT_WINDOW` (422). This applies to seat picks only.
15. The current logical clock instant is strictly earlier than the parent recalculation's `dispute_window_end`. This applies to filing a dispute and to deciding a dispute. Violation: `ERR_DISPUTE_WINDOW_CLOSED` (422).
16. The event's window does not overlap the window of another event on the same recalculation that is in `SLOTS_PUBLISHED` or `OPEN`; touching at a shared boundary instant is permitted. This applies to the event gate operation. Violation: `ERR_SELECTION_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (409).
17. The target seat is neither already `ASSIGNED` nor `DECOMMISSIONED`. This applies to a seat pick and to a relocation decision. Violation: `ERR_SEAT_NOT_AVAILABLE` (409).
18. The number of the seat's hold windows whose closed intervals contain the pick instant is strictly below the seat's `max_concurrent_holds`. This applies to seat picks only. Violation: `ERR_SEAT_HOLD_CONFLICT` (409).
19. The dispute's `info_request_rounds` is strictly below the information-request round cap. This applies to requesting information on a dispute. Violation: `ERR_INFO_REQUEST_LIMIT_REACHED` (422).
20. The eligible donor pool for the event is non-empty. This applies to publishing slots. Violation: `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_DONORS` (422).
21. The last computed slot's `slot_end` does not fall after the event's `window_end`. This applies to publishing slots. Violation: `ERR_SLOT_WINDOW_OVERFLOW` (422).
22. A household link names a donor other than the acting one, both donors have `entity_type` `JOINT_HOUSEHOLD`, and neither is already linked to some third donor. This applies to the household link. Violation: `ERR_HOUSEHOLD_LINK_INVALID` (409).
23. A granted relocation supplies `target_seat_id`, and that seat belongs to `target_section_id`. This applies to a relocation decision. Violation: `ERR_RELOCATION_TARGET_INVALID` (422).
24. The donor's `certified_points` is at least the target section's `required_points_per_seat`. This applies to a seat pick and to a relocation decision. Violation: `ERR_SEAT_POINTS_SHORTFALL` (422).
25. The money covers the bill: the tendered `amount_cents` is at least `total_due_cents` when a seat assignment is marked paid, `amount_paid_cents` is at least `total_due_cents` when occupancy advances to `ASSIGNED`, and the donor's current assignment is fully paid when a relocation request is filed. This applies to seat-assignment billing, seat-assignment occupancy, and relocation-request creation. Violation: `ERR_SEAT_DONATION_SHORTFALL` (402).
26. `points_amount` is at most `max_transferable_points` and at most the source donor's current `certified_points`. This applies both to creating a transfer and to approving one. Violation: `ERR_TRANSFER_AMOUNT_EXCEEDS_BALANCE` (422).
27. Creating a seat would not push the section's `seat_row_count` above its `seat_capacity`. This applies to adding a seat to a section. Violation: `ERR_SECTION_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409).

### 2.9. Derived Counters, Cascades and Consistency

A great deal of this service's state is derived, and derived state is recomputed **immediately and within the same request** as the change that invalidated it — there is no deferred sweep, no eventual reconciliation, and no endpoint whose only purpose is to refresh a counter. When one request touches several resources, every touched resource has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` advanced by 1, and this document names each such resource explicitly at each transition rather than leaving cascades to be inferred. The converse holds too: recomputing a derived counter on a resource this document does not name at that transition leaves that resource's `version` and `updated_at` exactly as they stood, because the recomputation is not itself a mutation.

The section-level seat inventory is the clearest example. `seat_row_count` is the number of seat rows in the section; `seats_available` counts those whose `seat_status` is `AVAILABLE`; `seats_committed_count` counts those whose `seat_status` is either `HELD` or `ASSIGNED`; and `decommissioned_seat_count` counts those whose `seat_status` is `DECOMMISSIONED`. After every request, including every cascade, the sum of `seats_available`, `seats_committed_count` and `decommissioned_seat_count` must equal `seat_row_count`. Two further counters look at assignments rather than seats: `assigned_seat_count` is the number of seat assignments on the section's seats whose `occupancy_status` is `HELD` or `ASSIGNED`, and `underwater_seat_count` is that same set narrowed to rows whose `eligibility_status` is `UNDERWATER`. A seat assignment's `eligibility_status` is `UNDERWATER` whenever the donor's *current* `certified_points` is below that row's `required_points`, and `ELIGIBLE` otherwise — re-judged only on rows whose `occupancy_status` is `HELD` or `ASSIGNED`, so a `RELEASED` row keeps whatever `eligibility_status` it carried when it left the pool — the row's own frozen `points_at_pick` records what the donor had at the moment of the pick, but eligibility is always judged against the live figure. Whenever `underwater_seat_count`, `assigned_seat_count` or `seats_available` changes, the section's `pricing_posture` is recomputed from them.

Donor-level derived figures follow the same discipline. `certified_points` is recomputed whenever `certified_base_points`, `transferred_points_in`, `transferred_points_out` or `dispute_adjustment_points` changes; `pending_request_count` tracks the number of the donor's relocation requests currently in `AWAITING_SELECTION`; and `certified_points_sum` on a recalculation is recomputed whenever the `certified_points` of any donor it scored changes — where "the donors it scored" means exactly those donors carrying a `CERTIFIED` ledger generation for that recalculation, and where the recomputation fires for a recalculation already in `CERTIFIED` just as it does for one still being worked, so the sum is a live figure rather than one frozen at certification. `seats_available`, `scheduled_slot_count`, `pending_request_count` and every other counter named in this document is a stored, readable field, not a value the client is expected to compute.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Donor Book and the Donor Status Machine

Everything begins with a donor of record. Registering one is a development-office or platform-operator act and requires four values plus one optional anchor: `full_name`, the legal or entity name, between 1 and 120 characters and not blank; `email`, the contact address used for recalculation communications, which must be non-blank, must contain an at-sign, carries no upper length bound, and must be unique across the donor book compared case-insensitively — a duplicate address is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than as a state conflict; `alumni_status`, one of `ALUMNUS`, `FACULTY_STAFF`, `STUDENT` or `NON_ALUMNUS`; and `entity_type`, one of `INDIVIDUAL`, `JOINT_HOUSEHOLD`, `CORPORATION` or `FOUNDATION`. The optional `season_ticket_first_cycle_year`, when supplied, must be a year between 1900 and 2100 and serves as an informational anchor for tenure audits. A newly registered donor lands in `donor_status` `ACTIVE` with `status_effective_date` set to the current logical clock instant, and every point and counter field starts at zero: `season_ticket_seasons_purchased`, `cumulative_giving_cents`, `consecutive_giving_years`, `provisional_points`, `certified_base_points`, `certified_points`, `transferred_points_in`, `transferred_points_out`, `dispute_adjustment_points` and `pending_request_count` are all `0`, while `household_partner_donor_id` and `rank_position` are both null. `rank_position` stays null until the donor's first certification, and once set is a 1-based rank of at least 1.

The `donor_status` axis has five values — `ACTIVE`, `LAPSED`, `DECEASED`, `DISSOLVED_ENTITY` and `SUSPENDED` — and one terminal value, `DISSOLVED_ENTITY`. A status change carries `new_status`, an `effective_date`, a free-text `reason` that is required and must not be blank, and `expected_donor_version`; on success it writes `effective_date` into `status_effective_date` and advances the donor's `version` by 1. Whether the requested edge exists at all is always decided first, ahead of any constraint on `effective_date` or on `entity_type`. Lapsing a donor moves `ACTIVE → LAPSED` and requires both that the donor is currently `ACTIVE` and that `effective_date` is not later than the current logical clock instant — an `effective_date` in the future is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, and lapsing is the only edge on this machine that constrains `effective_date` at all; every other path accepts any instant, past or future. Reactivation runs the loop back the other way, `LAPSED → ACTIVE`, and requires only that the donor is currently `LAPSED`; because the alumni bonus is granted for `ACTIVE` and `LAPSED` alike, this round trip leaves the donor's scoring unchanged, and a `LAPSED` donor remains rankable with `certified_base_points` untouched.

Death is recorded from either of the two live statuses: `ACTIVE → DECEASED` and `LAPSED → DECEASED` both require that the donor is currently `ACTIVE` or `LAPSED`. Either path drops the alumni bonus from the *next* recalculation, because the bonus is granted only while `donor_status` is `ACTIVE` or `LAPSED`, and either path unlocks a `DEATH`-basis point transfer without needing a policy exception. Moving to `DECEASED` from `ACTIVE` additionally makes the donor ineligible for every selection-event candidate pool. Dissolving a corporate donor moves `ACTIVE → DISSOLVED_ENTITY` and is admitted only when the donor's current status is `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` **and** its `entity_type` is `CORPORATION` or `FOUNDATION` — an `INDIVIDUAL` or `JOINT_HOUSEHOLD` donor on an otherwise admissible edge is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; the effect is to unlock an `ENTITY_DISSOLUTION`-basis transfer without a policy exception. Suspension is a platform-operator lever: `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` requires the donor to be `ACTIVE` and removes them from every selection-event candidate pool, and `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` requires the donor to be `SUSPENDED` and puts them back. All four of these paths also write `status_effective_date` from `effective_date` and advance the donor's `version` by 1. A status change whose starting state does not admit the requested destination is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

Two donors who give as a couple are linked as **household partners**. The link operation names the counterpart as `partner_donor_id` and echoes `expected_donor_version`; when it succeeds, `household_partner_donor_id` is written reciprocally on *both* rows and *both* donors have their `version` advanced by 1, and the response body is the donor named in the path rather than the partner. Re-issuing the link for a pair already linked to each other is admitted rather than refused, and advances both versions again. A link is admitted only when the partner is a different donor from the one being linked, when both donors carry `entity_type` `JOINT_HOUSEHOLD`, and when neither donor is already linked to some third donor; otherwise the response is `ERR_HOUSEHOLD_LINK_INVALID`. This reciprocal link is exactly the factual precondition a `DIVORCE`-basis point transfer later relies on, which is why the household list filter on `household_partner_donor_id` exists as a way to walk the link from either side.

Season-ticket tenure is recorded one season at a time. The renewal operation carries a single `cycle_year`, which must be a year between 1900 and 2100, and each distinct `cycle_year` counts once: recording a year not already on file increments `season_ticket_seasons_purchased` by 1 and advances the donor's `version` by 1, and if `season_ticket_first_cycle_year` was previously unset it is filled in from this call. Recording a year that is already on file does not increment the count a second time; it is not a mutation at all, so it answers 200 with the donor exactly as it stood, `version` and `updated_at` included, and it does not fill in `season_ticket_first_cycle_year` either. `season_ticket_seasons_purchased` is what feeds the tenure component of the priority-point formula.

### 3.2. Gifts, Settlement and the Qualifying Input Set

A gift is recorded against a donor with three required values — `gift_date`, the instant the gift was made, whose UTC calendar year must fall between 1900 and 2100 or the request is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; `amount_cents`, which must be strictly greater than zero; and `designation`, one of `ATHLETICS_ANNUAL`, `CAPITAL` or `ENDOWMENT` — plus an optional `gift_status` that defaults to `PLEDGED` when omitted and is otherwise `RECEIVED` or `REFUNDED`. Two fields on the gift are derived and never accepted from the body. `cycle_year` is the UTC calendar year of `gift_date`, and it is the bucket the consecutive-year streak scan reads. `counts_toward_points` is true exactly when `designation` is `ATHLETICS_ANNUAL` or `CAPITAL`, and false for `ENDOWMENT`; a value supplied by the client is ignored. The gift's `donor_id` is taken from the path the gift was created under, never from the body. When a gift is recorded with `gift_status` already `RECEIVED`, its `amount_cents` is added to the donor's `cumulative_giving_cents` immediately and the donor's `version` advances by 1. `settled_at` remains null while the gift is `PLEDGED`; a gift recorded with `gift_status` already `RECEIVED` or already `REFUNDED` is born settled and carries `settled_at` stamped from the current logical clock instant.

Settlement drives the gift's own small state machine, whose initial state is `PLEDGED` and whose terminal state is `REFUNDED`; the call carries only `new_status`. Settling `PLEDGED → RECEIVED` requires the gift to be `PLEDGED`; it stamps `settled_at` with the current logical clock instant, adds `amount_cents` to the donor's `cumulative_giving_cents`, sets `counts_toward_points` to true when `designation` is `ATHLETICS_ANNUAL` or `CAPITAL`, makes the gift eligible for the CERTIFIED point variant in addition to the PROVISIONAL one, and advances both the gift's `version` and the donor's `version` by 1. Refunding an unsettled pledge, `PLEDGED → REFUNDED`, likewise requires the gift to be `PLEDGED`; it stamps `settled_at`, sets `counts_toward_points` to false, removes the gift from both the PROVISIONAL and the CERTIFIED input set, and advances only the gift's `version` by 1 — the donor's cumulative giving never included this money, so nothing is subtracted. Clawing back settled money, `RECEIVED → REFUNDED`, requires the gift to be `RECEIVED`; it subtracts `amount_cents` from the donor's `cumulative_giving_cents`, sets `counts_toward_points` to false, and advances both the gift's `version` and the donor's `version` by 1. Critically, a clawback leaves an already-certified `certified_base_points` untouched until the next recalculation run — the office does not retroactively rewrite a cycle it has already certified and communicated.

Which gifts a recalculation actually reads is a narrower question than which gifts exist. A gift enters a recalculation's input set only when it belongs to the donor being scored, its `gift_date` is at or before the recalculation's `cutoff_date`, and its `designation` is `ATHLETICS_ANNUAL` or `CAPITAL`. An `ENDOWMENT` gift never scores, no matter how large or how well settled. Within that set, the variant decides which `gift_status` values are admitted, which is the single mechanism by which the two annual figures diverge.

### 3.3. The Annual Recalculation and the Priority-Point Formula

An annual cycle is opened by a development officer or platform operator with three values: `cycle_year`, the athletics cycle year being certified, which must be between 1900 and 2100 and must be unique across all recalculations — a second recalculation naming a year already on file is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; `cutoff_date`, the instant after which gifts are excluded from both variants; and `dispute_window_end`, the last instant at which a dispute may be filed against this cycle, which must be strictly later than `cutoff_date`. The new recalculation starts in `DRAFT` with `donors_processed_count`, `communication_sent_count` and `certified_points_sum` at zero, `certified_by_user_id` and `certified_at` null, and `initiated_by_user_id` set to the caller's `X-User-ID` — the value that will later be compared against the certifier's identity. The lifecycle runs `DRAFT → COMPUTED → COMMUNICATED → CERTIFIED`, with `CERTIFIED` terminal and one backward edge from `COMMUNICATED` to `COMPUTED` described in §3.4.

Running the cycle moves `DRAFT → COMPUTED` and scores **every** donor twice — once under each variant — writing a full explanatory ledger generation as it goes. The donor book is walked in `created_at` ascending order, which is what fixes the order the ledger rows of a run are appended in. The score is assembled in the following order, and the order is load-bearing.

First, the **tiered giving component**. Sum `amount_cents` across the donor's gifts in this recalculation's input set to obtain the donor's qualifying giving. That total is then split across three cumulative bands: the portion from zero up to but not including $25,000 (2,500,000 cents), the portion from $25,000 up to but not including $100,000 (10,000,000 cents), and the portion at or above $100,000. Each band earns points **marginally**, at its own rate per $1,000 of giving (100,000 cents): 1.0 points per $1,000 in the first band, 1.5 points per $1,000 in the second, and 2.0 points per $1,000 in the third. The portion falling in each band is divided by 100,000 cents and multiplied by that band's rate, the three products are added together, and the sum is rounded half up to 2 decimal places. This rounded figure is the giving component. Because the bands are marginal rather than average, a donor $1 above a band boundary earns the higher rate only on that single dollar.

Second, the **loyalty multiplier**. When the donor's consecutive-giving streak is at least 5 years, the giving component is multiplied by 1.10; otherwise it is multiplied by 1.00. The multiplier is applied to the **already-rounded** giving component, not to the raw band products, and the product is itself rounded half up to 2 decimal places. This multiplied figure is what enters the assembly below.

Third, the **consecutive-year component**: the donor's consecutive-giving streak, or 20 if the streak exceeds 20, multiplied by 5 points per year. Fourth, the **season-ticket tenure component**: the donor's `season_ticket_seasons_purchased`, or 30 if that count exceeds 30, multiplied by 3 points per season. Fifth, the **alumni bonus**, which is granted only while `donor_status` is `ACTIVE` or `LAPSED` and is 0 otherwise: 25 points for `ALUMNUS`, 15 for `FACULTY_STAFF`, 5 for `STUDENT`, and 0 for `NON_ALUMNUS`. Sixth, the **net transfer component**: the donor's `transferred_points_in` minus their `transferred_points_out`.

The assembly then adds five terms together — the multiplied giving component, the consecutive-year component, the tenure component, the alumni bonus and the net transfer component — plus the donor's `dispute_adjustment_points`. That sum is rounded half up to 2 decimal places, and if the rounded result is below zero the score is `0.00` instead. The PROVISIONAL variant writes this final figure to `provisional_points`. The CERTIFIED variant writes the *gift-and-attribute part* — the multiplied giving component plus the consecutive-year, tenure and alumni components, before the net transfer term and before dispute adjustments — to `certified_base_points`, and writes the full final figure to `certified_points`. Every donor's `cumulative_giving_cents` and `consecutive_giving_years` are refreshed by the run to the values the CERTIFIED input set produced, so those two stored fields always describe the last completed run.

The **streak** itself is computed by walking backwards year by year. A year qualifies when the donor has at least one gift in the variant's input set whose `cycle_year` equals that year. Start at the recalculation's `cycle_year`; if that year does not qualify, step back exactly one year — this one-year grace is applied at most once, and never again later in the walk. From the year now under examination, count upward by one for each consecutive qualifying year while stepping back one year at a time, and stop at the first year that does not qualify. The resulting count is the streak. A donor whose most recent qualifying gift is two years old therefore has a streak of zero, because the single grace step is not enough to reach it.

Because a run writes both variants, it appends **two generations of ledger rows per donor**: exactly six rows per variant, one per formula component, written even where the component contributed `0.00`. Each row records `donor_id`, `recalculation_id`, the `entry_type` naming the component — `CUMULATIVE_GIVING`, `CONSECUTIVE_YEARS`, `SEASON_TICKET_TENURE`, `ALUMNI_STATUS`, `NET_TRANSFER` or `DISPUTE_ADJUSTMENT` — the `variant` (`PROVISIONAL` or `CERTIFIED`), the `points` that component contributed to 2 decimal places, a human-readable `basis_note` explaining the derivation such as the tier split, the streak span or the cap applied, and `computed_at`, the logical clock instant the row was written. For each donor the `PROVISIONAL` generation is appended first and the `CERTIFIED` generation second, and inside a generation the six rows are appended in exactly the order the components are listed above. The `points` on the `CUMULATIVE_GIVING` row is the giving component **after** the loyalty multiplier has been applied rather than the raw band sum — the multiplier earns no row of its own and is instead explained inside that row's `basis_note` — while the `NET_TRANSFER` and `DISPUTE_ADJUSTMENT` rows read the donor's stored fields rather than the variant's input set and therefore carry identical `points` in both generations. Ledger rows are never rewritten or deleted; a re-run appends a fresh generation alongside the old one. The run also recomputes each donor's `certified_points`, sets `donors_processed_count` to the number of donors scored, refreshes `certified_points_sum` to the sum of `certified_points` across the donors it scored — the run itself changes those figures, so the §2.9 recomputation fires inside the run rather than waiting for certification, and the aggregate no longer sits at its `DRAFT` zero once a run has completed — and advances the recalculation's `version` by 1 along with the `version` of every scored donor. Two further recomputations ride along on a run without being mutations of their own: because the run moves `certified_points`, it re-judges the `eligibility_status` of every seat assignment in `occupancy_status` `HELD` or `ASSIGNED` and recomputes the `assigned_seat_count`, `underwater_seat_count` and `pricing_posture` of the sections holding those seats — none of which advances an assignment's or a section's `version` or `updated_at` — and it refreshes `certified_points_sum` on *every* other recalculation as well, not merely on the one being run, since those cycles scored the same donors whose figures just moved.

One nuance about who gets scored: donors in `DECEASED`, `DISSOLVED_ENTITY` or `SUSPENDED` status are still scored, so that their balances remain transferable, but they forfeit the alumni bonus. And one nuance about derivation: `certified_points` is a **derived** figure, recomputed on demand rather than only during a run. Whenever `certified_base_points`, `transferred_points_in`, `transferred_points_out` or `dispute_adjustment_points` changes, `certified_points` is recomputed as `certified_base_points` plus `transferred_points_in` minus `transferred_points_out` plus `dispute_adjustment_points`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places and floored at `0.00`. This recomputation reads the single donor row and performs no gift scan. `certified_base_points` is never re-derived outside a recalculation run, which is precisely why moving a donor to `DECEASED` does not retroactively strip the alumni bonus out of a cycle that has already been certified.

### 3.4. Two Figures, One Communication, and the Dispute Loop

The two variants differ in exactly one respect, and both are written on every run. The **PROVISIONAL** variant admits gifts whose `gift_status` is `PLEDGED` as well as those whose `gift_status` is `RECEIVED`, so an unsettled pledge both raises the tiered giving component and is allowed to carry the consecutive-year streak; this is the generous figure quoted to the donor in the recalculation communication. The **CERTIFIED** variant admits gifts whose `gift_status` is `RECEIVED` only, so a pledged gift is excluded from the giving bands *and* its `cycle_year` does not qualify for the streak; this is the figure that drives ranking, slotting and seat eligibility, and it is frozen at certification. The consequence is deliberate and worth stating plainly: whenever a donor has any qualifying pledge still unsettled, the same underlying data yields a strictly lower CERTIFIED score than PROVISIONAL score, and the donor is told the higher number first.

Communicating the results moves the recalculation `COMPUTED → COMMUNICATED`, requires the recalculation to be in `COMPUTED`, sets `communication_sent_count` equal to `donors_processed_count`, opens the dispute window until `dispute_window_end`, and advances the recalculation's `version` by 1. From here the cycle can go forward to certification, or it can be pulled backwards for a second pass: re-running a communicated cycle moves `COMMUNICATED → COMPUTED`, and is admitted only when the recalculation is in `COMMUNICATED` **and** no dispute on it is currently in `UNDER_REVIEW` or `INFO_REQUESTED`. That second pass recomputes every donor's points, this time folding in the `dispute_adjustment_points` produced by disputes that were upheld, appends a fresh generation of ledger rows, resets `communication_sent_count` to 0 because the donors must be told again, and advances the recalculation's `version` by 1. This backward edge is what makes the recalculation machine non-linear: dispute outcomes force a re-score before the cycle can be frozen.

A donor who believes the figure is wrong files a dispute against the communicated cycle. The filing carries `donor_id`, `recalculation_id`, `claimed_points_delta` — the points the donor claims were omitted, which must be strictly greater than zero and is carried to 2 decimal places — and `basis_note`, the donor's stated basis, between 1 and 500 characters and not blank. Filing is admitted only while the current logical clock instant is strictly before the parent recalculation's `dispute_window_end`; at or after that instant the filing is rejected with `ERR_DISPUTE_WINDOW_CLOSED`. That window is the *only* gate on filing: the parent recalculation's own `status` is never examined, so a dispute may be filed against a cycle still in `DRAFT` or `COMPUTED`, or against one already `CERTIFIED`, as long as the window has not closed. A `Donor`-role filer may only supply their own `donor_id`, and where the body omits `donor_id` altogether or sends it as something other than a string the ownership comparison cannot fire and evaluation falls through to the body validation of §2.8 step 6. The new dispute lands in `SUBMITTED` with `info_request_rounds` at 0, and `reviewed_by_user_id`, `decided_by_user_id`, `granted_points_delta`, `decided_at` and `info_note` all null. Its mere existence blocks certification of the parent recalculation until it reaches a terminal state — `UPHELD` or `DENIED`.

Working the dispute is the analyst's job. Taking it under review moves `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW`, and requires both that the dispute is in `SUBMITTED` and that the parent recalculation is in `COMMUNICATED`; on the **first** review only, `reviewed_by_user_id` is stamped with the analyst's `X-User-ID`, and the dispute's `version` advances by 1. That first reviewer's identity is the comparand the eventual decision is measured against, which is why it is stamped once and never overwritten. If the analyst needs evidence, requesting information moves `UNDER_REVIEW → INFO_REQUESTED`, requires the dispute to be in `UNDER_REVIEW`, records the supplied `info_note` — which is required and must not be blank — on the dispute, and advances its `version` by 1 — but only while `info_request_rounds` is strictly below the round cap of 2. A third attempt, on a dispute that has already completed 2 rounds, is rejected with `ERR_INFO_REQUEST_LIMIT_REACHED`.

The donor answers with a `response_note`, which walks the dispute back `INFO_REQUESTED → UNDER_REVIEW`, requires the dispute to be in `INFO_REQUESTED` and the caller's `X-User-ID` to equal the dispute's `donor_id`, increments `info_request_rounds` by 1, and advances the dispute's `version` by 1. `response_note` is required and must not be blank, but it is not persisted anywhere on the dispute — the donor's answer moves the state and the round counter only, and `info_note` keeps the analyst's most recent request text untouched. This is a genuine cycle: the dispute can leave and re-enter `UNDER_REVIEW`, and the second completed loop exhausts the cap.

Deciding is reserved to the policy committee and carries a `decision` of `UPHOLD` or `DENY` plus an optional `granted_points_delta`, which is carried to 2 decimal places and, when present, must be at least zero; an `UPHOLD` that omits it is treated as granting `0.00` rather than being refused. Both outcomes require that the dispute is in `UNDER_REVIEW`, that the current logical clock instant is before the parent recalculation's `dispute_window_end`, and that the decider's `X-User-ID` differs from `reviewed_by_user_id`. Upholding it, `UNDER_REVIEW → UPHELD`, sets `granted_points_delta` from the payload and `decided_by_user_id` to the decider's `X-User-ID`, stamps `decided_at` with the current logical clock instant, adds `granted_points_delta` to the donor's `dispute_adjustment_points`, recomputes the donor's `certified_points` — and with it, exactly as an approved transfer does, the `eligibility_status` of the donor's `HELD` and `ASSIGNED` seat assignments, the `underwater_seat_count` and `pricing_posture` of the sections holding them, and the `certified_points_sum` of every recalculation that scored the donor, none of which advances a `version` of its own — and advances both the dispute's `version` and the donor's `version` by 1. Denying it, `UNDER_REVIEW → DENIED`, sets `granted_points_delta` to 0 and `decided_by_user_id` to the decider's `X-User-ID`, stamps `decided_at`, leaves the donor's `dispute_adjustment_points` unchanged, and advances only the dispute's `version` by 1. Both `UPHELD` and `DENIED` are terminal.

### 3.5. Certification, the Ranking, and the Staleness Cascade

Certification is the single most consequential act in the system, and it belongs to the `PointsCertifier` alone. Moving a cycle `COMMUNICATED → CERTIFIED` requires four things at once: the recalculation is in `COMMUNICATED`; the supplied `expected_recalculation_version` equals the recalculation's current `version`; the certifier's `X-User-ID` differs from `initiated_by_user_id`; and no dispute on this recalculation remains in `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `INFO_REQUESTED`. Note that the dispute gate at certification is wider than the one on the backward re-run edge — a merely `SUBMITTED` dispute nobody has looked at yet blocks certification, even though it does not block a re-score.

On success the recalculation records `certified_by_user_id` from the certifier's `X-User-ID` and `certified_at` from the current logical clock instant, sets `certified_points_sum` to the sum of `certified_points` across every donor it processed, and advances its own `version` by 1. It then stamps `rank_position` across the ranked donor pool — a restamp that advances neither the donor's `version` nor their `updated_at`, and that leaves a donor outside the pool carrying whatever `rank_position` they were last given rather than resetting it to null — and that stamping is what sets off a three-step cascade. Every selection slot still in `SCHEDULED` whose `rank_at_publish` no longer equals its donor's freshly assigned `rank_position` moves `SCHEDULED → STALE`, and each such slot decrements its event's `scheduled_slot_count` and has its own `version` advanced by 1; the decrement by itself does not advance the event's `version` or `updated_at`. Any selection event that just had at least one slot marked `STALE` and that retains no slot in `SCHEDULED` at all is itself moved to `SLOTS_STALE`, whether it was sitting in `SLOTS_PUBLISHED` or already `OPEN`, with its `version` advanced by 1. Finally, every relocation request in `AWAITING_SELECTION` that names one of those newly stale events moves `AWAITING_SELECTION → BLOCKED`, records the cascade reason in its `decision_note`, decrements its donor's `pending_request_count`, and has its `version` advanced by 1. The chain exists so that a rank shift can never let a donor pick out of turn: the published order is invalidated wholesale rather than partially honoured.

The ranking itself is a pure function of stored business fields. The candidate pool for a selection event admits a donor when the donor's `donor_status` is `ACTIVE` or `LAPSED`, when their `certified_points` is at least the event's `eligible_donor_min_points`, and — for a `POSTSEASON` or `AWAY_GAME` event — when the donor holds at least one seat assignment whose `occupancy_status` is `ASSIGNED`. For a `SEASON_TICKET` event that last requirement is dropped, so every `ACTIVE` or `LAPSED` donor above the points floor is admitted whether or not they currently hold a seat. Postseason and away-game events reuse the identical ordering but restrict the pool to existing seat holders, which is how a high-point donor with no assigned seat is excluded from an otherwise identical ranking. At certification the pool is every donor whose `donor_status` is `ACTIVE` or `LAPSED`.

The pool is ordered on four keys, applied in strict sequence: `certified_points` descending, then `cumulative_giving_cents` descending, then `consecutive_giving_years` descending, then `created_at` ascending. `rank_position` is the 1-based index in that order. Ties are impossible, because `created_at` settles any remaining ambiguity and, where two donors were registered without the logical clock moving in between and therefore share a `created_at`, the order in which the two rows were written settles it — a donor's `id` is **never** an input to the ordering. An empty pool is treated as an error rather than as an empty publication: publishing slots against an empty pool is rejected with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_DONORS`.

Once a cycle is certified, the office can read a retention picture off it. This computed read requires the recalculation to be in `CERTIFIED` and is rejected with `ERR_RECALCULATION_NOT_CERTIFIED` otherwise. It buckets the donors the cycle scored by the streak the cycle actually computed: `retained_donor_count` is the number of donors whose `consecutive_giving_years` is at least 2, `new_donor_count` is the number whose `consecutive_giving_years` equals exactly 1, and `lapsed_risk_donor_count` is the number whose `consecutive_giving_years` equals 0 while their `cumulative_giving_cents` is strictly greater than zero — a donor who has given historically but did not give in the streak window. The `retention_rate` is `retained_donor_count` divided by the sum of `retained_donor_count` and `lapsed_risk_donor_count`, except that the divisor is 1 whenever that sum is zero, and the quotient is rounded half up to 4 decimal places. The `average_certified_points` is the sum of `certified_points` across all scored donors divided by `donors_processed_count`, except that the divisor is 1 whenever `donors_processed_count` is zero, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. The response returns `cycle_year`, `donors_processed_count`, `retained_donor_count`, `new_donor_count`, `lapsed_risk_donor_count`, `retention_rate`, `average_certified_points` and `certified_points_sum` inline as a single object rather than as a stored resource.

### 3.6. Moving Points Between Donors

Points follow people, but only along four sanctioned paths. A transfer request names `source_donor_id`, the donor surrendering points; `recipient_donor_id`, the donor receiving them, which must be a different donor from the source; `basis`, one of `DEATH`, `DIVORCE`, `ENTITY_DISSOLUTION` or `ENTITY_GIVING_REALIGNMENT`; `points_amount`, strictly greater than zero and carried to 2 decimal places; and `expected_source_donor_version`, which must equal the source donor's current `version`. Creation is a development-office or platform-operator act, and it advances the source donor's `version` by 1. A body naming the same donor twice is malformed rather than a state conflict: a `recipient_donor_id` equal to `source_donor_id` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` at §2.8 step 6, before either identifier is resolved; the two are then resolved in the order the fields are listed above, so an unknown source is reported ahead of an unknown recipient. A brand-new transfer carries `exception_granted_by_user_id`, `approved_by_user_id`, `decision_note` and `reversed_at` all null.

The `basis` fixes how much of the source donor's balance may move. `transfer_pct_cap` is a fraction, carried to 2 decimal places and always greater than zero and at most 1: a `DEATH` basis permits the entire balance, 1.00; an `ENTITY_DISSOLUTION` basis likewise permits 1.00; a `DIVORCE` basis permits half, 0.50; and an `ENTITY_GIVING_REALIGNMENT` basis permits a quarter, 0.25. From it, `max_transferable_points` is computed once, at creation time, as the source donor's `certified_points` multiplied by `transfer_pct_cap`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. If `points_amount` exceeds either `max_transferable_points` or the source donor's current `certified_points`, the request is rejected with `ERR_TRANSFER_AMOUNT_EXCEEDS_BALANCE`; the same pair of ceilings is re-checked at approval time, because the source's balance can move between the two calls.

Each `basis` also carries a **factual precondition** on the source donor, evaluated at creation. A `DEATH` basis expects the source donor's `donor_status` to be `DECEASED`. A `DIVORCE` basis expects the source donor's `entity_type` to be `JOINT_HOUSEHOLD` **and** their `household_partner_donor_id` to equal `recipient_donor_id` — the reciprocal household link established earlier is precisely what satisfies this. An `ENTITY_DISSOLUTION` basis expects the source donor's `donor_status` to be `DISSOLVED_ENTITY`. An `ENTITY_GIVING_REALIGNMENT` basis expects the source donor's `entity_type` to be `CORPORATION` or `FOUNDATION`. When the precondition for the invoked basis holds, `exception_required` is false and the transfer is created in `REQUESTED`. When it does not hold, `exception_required` is true and the transfer is created directly in `EXCEPTION_PENDING` — the entry state of a brand-new transfer therefore depends on the source donor's own status and type, not on anything the requester supplies.

`EXCEPTION_PENDING` is a fork the policy committee resolves with a `decision` of `GRANT` or `REFUSE` plus a free-text `note` that is required and must not be blank. Granting the exception moves `EXCEPTION_PENDING → REQUESTED`, requires the transfer to be in `EXCEPTION_PENDING`, stamps `exception_granted_by_user_id` with the committee member's `X-User-ID`, records the note as `decision_note`, and advances the transfer's `version` by 1. Granting does not rewrite `exception_required`: that flag records the verdict reached at creation and stays true on a transfer whose exception was later granted. Refusing it moves `EXCEPTION_PENDING → DENIED`, requires the transfer to be in `EXCEPTION_PENDING`, records `decision_note`, and advances the `version` by 1. The committee may also refuse a transfer that never needed an exception at all: `REQUESTED → DENIED` requires the transfer to be in `REQUESTED`, records `decision_note`, and advances the `version` by 1. `DENIED` is terminal. Attempting approval while the transfer is still in `EXCEPTION_PENDING` is not reported as an ordinary state error; it is reported specifically as `ERR_TRANSFER_POLICY_EXCEPTION_REQUIRED`, so that the caller learns *what* is missing rather than merely that the state is wrong.

Approval belongs to the `PointsCertifier` and moves `REQUESTED → APPROVED`. It requires the transfer to be in `REQUESTED`, `points_amount` to be at most `max_transferable_points`, and `points_amount` to be at most the source donor's current `certified_points`. On success it adds `points_amount` to the recipient's `transferred_points_in` and the same amount to the source's `transferred_points_out`, recomputes `certified_points` for both donors, and stamps `approved_by_user_id` with the certifier's `X-User-ID`. Then it cascades into seating, and the re-judgement runs in both directions: every seat assignment of either donor in `HELD` or `ASSIGNED` whose donor's recomputed `certified_points` has fallen below that row's `required_points` flips its `eligibility_status` to `UNDERWATER`, while a row whose donor now meets that figure — typically the recipient's — flips back to `ELIGIBLE`. Every section holding one of those rows has its `assigned_seat_count`, `underwater_seat_count` and `pricing_posture` recomputed, and the `certified_points_sum` of every recalculation that scored either donor is refreshed; none of those recomputations advances a `version` or an `updated_at` of its own. The transfer's `version` and both donors' `version` values each advance by 1. This is the point at which a generous transfer out can silently put the source donor's own seats underwater.

Reversal is the compensating edge, also reserved to the `PointsCertifier`. Moving `APPROVED → REVERSED` requires the transfer to be in `APPROVED` and carries a `reason`, which is required and must not be blank. It subtracts `points_amount` from the recipient's `transferred_points_in` and from the source's `transferred_points_out`, recomputes `certified_points` for both donors — restoring the pre-transfer total across the pair **exactly**, which is the invariant the compensating design exists to guarantee — re-judges both donors' `HELD` and `ASSIGNED` rows in the same two directions the approval used, restoring `eligibility_status` to `ELIGIBLE` on every seat assignment whose donor once again meets that row's `required_points` and, symmetrically, flipping a recipient's row to `UNDERWATER` where handing the points back has left that donor short, recomputes `assigned_seat_count`, `underwater_seat_count` and `pricing_posture` on the affected sections and `certified_points_sum` on every recalculation that scored either donor, stamps `reversed_at` with the current logical clock instant, records the supplied reason as `decision_note`, and advances the transfer's `version` and both donors' `version` values by 1. `REVERSED` is terminal; a reversed transfer is history, not a request that can be re-approved.

### 3.7. Venue Inventory, Section Pricing, and the Renovation Pipeline

Seating inventory belongs to the `PlatformAdmin`. Creating a section takes `section_code`, a short code between 1 and 16 characters, not blank, and unique across all sections — compared exactly rather than case-insensitively, and a duplicate is a malformed request rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than a state conflict; `venue_code`, between 1 and 32 characters and not blank; `seat_capacity`, the maximum number of seat rows the section may hold, strictly greater than zero; `per_seat_requirement_cents`, the current market per-seat donation requirement, strictly greater than zero; and `required_points_per_seat`, the minimum `certified_points` a donor needs to occupy a seat here, at least zero and carried to 2 decimal places. The section opens with `status` `OPEN`, `renovation_status` `NONE`, `pricing_posture` `NORMAL`, every inventory counter at zero — `reseated_donor_count` and `reseating_invoice_total_cents` among them, both of which are integers that are never null — and `post_renovation_capacity`, `post_renovation_requirement_cents` and `reseating_recalculation_id` all null. It also opens `requirement_history` with a single entry pairing the opening `per_seat_requirement_cents` with a `cycle_year` taken from the UTC calendar year of the current logical clock instant. `requirement_history` is append-only, ordered by the order in which entries were appended, and it is the lookup source for every grandfathering decision later in this document.

A physical seat is added to a section with `row_label`, between 1 and 8 characters and not blank; `seat_number`, strictly greater than zero; and an optional `max_concurrent_holds`, which defaults to 1 and must be at least 1. The combination of the owning section, `row_label` and `seat_number` is unique; a duplicate is a malformed request rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` at §2.8 step 6, and is therefore reported ahead of both the section-status gate and the capacity gate described next. A seat's `section_id` comes from the path it was created under and is never read from the body. Seats may only be added while the section's `status` is `OPEN` — a section that is `UNDER_RENOVATION` or `CLOSED` rejects the addition with `ERR_SECTION_UNDER_RENOVATION` — and only while `seat_row_count` would remain within `seat_capacity`; the seat that would push it past capacity is refused with `ERR_SECTION_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. A new seat starts in `seat_status` `AVAILABLE`, increments the section's `seat_row_count` and `seats_available`, and advances the section's `version` by 1. The `seat_status` axis has four values — `AVAILABLE`, `HELD`, `ASSIGNED` and the terminal `DECOMMISSIONED`.

Outside any renovation, the market rate can be reset in place. A reprice supplies a new `per_seat_requirement_cents`, the `cycle_year` the new rate takes effect for — a year between 1900 and 2100 — and `expected_section_version`; it replaces `per_seat_requirement_cents`, appends the new pairing to `requirement_history`, and advances the section's `version` by 1. Despite the framing, the reprice imposes no state gate whatsoever: neither the section's `status` nor its `renovation_status` is examined, so a section sitting `UNDER_RENOVATION` may still be repriced in place, and a `cycle_year` already present in `requirement_history` is not rejected either — a second entry for that year is simply appended. The appended history entry is what lets an earlier occupant keep the rate they first came in at while a new occupant pays the new one, and it changes nothing on the seat assignments already standing in the section.

The renovation pipeline is a second, independent axis on the same section row, carried in `renovation_status` and driven by a `renovation_action` of `SURVEY`, `REPRICE`, `START_RESEATING`, `COMPLETE` or `ABANDON`. Every action requires `expected_section_version` to equal the section's current `version` and advances that `version` by 1. Its states run `NONE → SURVEYED → REPRICED → RESEATING → COMPLETED`, with `ABANDONED` reachable from either `SURVEYED` or `REPRICED` and terminal for the renovation dimension.

- **`SURVEY`** moves `NONE → SURVEYED` and requires `renovation_status` to be `NONE` and `status` to be `OPEN`. Its side effect is to set `status` to `UNDER_RENOVATION`, which blocks every pick into this section for the duration.
- **`REPRICE`** moves `SURVEYED → REPRICED` and requires `renovation_status` to be `SURVEYED` and both `post_renovation_capacity` and `post_renovation_requirement_cents` to be present in the payload. It stores both values on the section without applying either one yet; `post_renovation_capacity` and `post_renovation_requirement_cents` must each be strictly greater than zero when present, and both remain null until this action runs. Because the two fields are optional on the endpoint as a whole and only this one action needs them, their absence here is reported as `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409) rather than as a malformed body, and the `renovation_status` precondition is weighed first.
- **`START_RESEATING`** moves `REPRICED → RESEATING` and requires `renovation_status` to be `REPRICED` and the supplied `reseating_recalculation_id` to reference a recalculation in `CERTIFIED`; a recalculation in any other status is rejected with `ERR_RECALCULATION_NOT_CERTIFIED`. An omitted `reseating_recalculation_id` is, like the reprice fields above, reported as `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409) rather than as a missing field, and the `renovation_status` precondition is checked ahead of both it and the `CERTIFIED` gate. This is the heavy action, described in detail below.
- **`COMPLETE`** moves `RESEATING → COMPLETED` and requires `renovation_status` to be `RESEATING`. It sets `status` back to `OPEN`, unblocking picks, and recomputes `pricing_posture` from the post-reseating underwater ratio. That recomputation is not special to `COMPLETE`: every one of the five actions ends by recomputing the section's whole derived inventory block — `seat_row_count`, `seats_available`, `seats_committed_count`, `decommissioned_seat_count`, `assigned_seat_count`, `underwater_seat_count` and `pricing_posture`. `COMPLETE` also leaves `post_renovation_capacity` and `post_renovation_requirement_cents` standing on the row as a record of what was applied, and leaves `reseating_recalculation_id` in place.
- **`ABANDON`** moves `SURVEYED → ABANDONED` or `REPRICED → ABANDONED`, and in both cases requires `renovation_status` to be either `SURVEYED` or `REPRICED`. Both paths set `status` back to `OPEN` and clear `post_renovation_capacity` and `post_renovation_requirement_cents` back to null. The two axes really are independent: the renovation dimension rests in the terminal `ABANDONED` while the section's own `status` is a perfectly ordinary `OPEN`.

Those three actions are the *only* writes to a section's own `status` anywhere in this specification. A section is minted `OPEN`; `SURVEY` is the single edge that leaves it `UNDER_RENOVATION`; and `COMPLETE` and `ABANDON` are the only edges that restore `OPEN`. There is no endpoint that closes a section and no cascade that does so either, so in practice a section's `status` oscillates between exactly those two values for the whole of its life. `CLOSED` nonetheless belongs to the `status` vocabulary — the section listing's `status` filter accepts it as a recognised value and answers with an empty page rather than rejecting the query, and the seat-addition and pick gates above name it alongside `UNDER_RENOVATION` — but no transition described anywhere in this document ever writes it. Those gates name it defensively: a caller cannot manoeuvre a section into `CLOSED` in order to observe the refusal, and `UNDER_RENOVATION` is the only way to see either gate bite.

Starting the reseating is where a renovation reaches the donor base. The section's `seat_capacity` is replaced by `post_renovation_capacity` and its `per_seat_requirement_cents` by `post_renovation_requirement_cents`, and a new `requirement_history` entry is appended pairing the new requirement with the `cycle_year` of the reseating recalculation. Seats beyond the new capacity are retired: taking the section's seats in `created_at` ascending order — never in `id` order — every seat still in `seat_status` `AVAILABLE` whose ordinal position in that order exceeds `post_renovation_capacity` moves `AVAILABLE → DECOMMISSIONED`, shifting one seat out of `seats_available` and into `decommissioned_seat_count` and advancing that seat's `version` by 1.

Then every seat assignment on the section's seats whose `occupancy_status` is `HELD` or `ASSIGNED` is repriced under its own `pricing_mode`, and invoiced. The invoice is prorated for the remaining months of the cycle at seven twelfths, and **the order of operations is load-bearing**: visiting those assignments in `created_at` ascending order, each row is first repriced from scratch under its own `pricing_mode` against the section's **new** `per_seat_requirement_cents` — a `MARKET` row takes that new rate outright, while a `RENEWAL` row takes the smaller of its escalated `grandfathered_requirement_cents` and 85% of the new rate, with the years held measured from the reseating recalculation's `cycle_year` exactly as §3.8 describes — and it is that freshly computed figure, not the row's previous `requirement_cents`, that is then multiplied by 7, divided by 12, and rounded half up to whole cents; that rounded per-seat share is written back as the row's new `requirement_cents` and added to a running total. `reseating_invoice_total_cents` is that running total and `reseated_donor_count` is the number of rows visited. Summing the unrounded shares and rounding once at the end produces a different figure and is wrong. Every row the cascade touched has its `payment_status` moved to `INVOICED` unconditionally — whatever that status was before, whether or not the row's `requirement_cents` actually came out different, and including the otherwise terminal `WRITTEN_OFF`. That plainly includes rows that had already been paid: an assignment in `payment_status` `PAID` is dragged backwards `PAID → INVOICED`, with `total_due_cents` recomputed as the row's new `requirement_cents` plus its existing `handling_fee_cents` — the fee is carried over untouched rather than re-banded against the new figure — while `amount_paid_cents` keeps the prior payment, so the outstanding balance is visible. Each visited row's `version` advances by 1. The row's `occupancy_status`, `eligibility_status`, `required_points` and `points_at_pick` are untouched by any of this.

Two derived signals close out the section story. `pricing_posture` is computed from the section's own counters: the underwater ratio is `underwater_seat_count` divided by `assigned_seat_count`, except that the divisor is 1 whenever `assigned_seat_count` is zero, rounded half up to 4 decimal places. The branch order is pinned and `SOFT` is evaluated first and wins outright — when the ratio is at least 0.30 the posture is `SOFT`; otherwise, when the ratio is exactly zero **and** `seats_available` is zero, the posture is `PREMIUM`; otherwise it is `NORMAL`. A separate computed read reports pricing sensitivity for a section: it returns `section_code`, `per_seat_requirement_cents`, `required_points_per_seat`, `assigned_seat_count`, `underwater_seat_count`, the `underwater_ratio` computed the same way and rounded half up to 4 decimal places, the current `pricing_posture`, the whole `requirement_history`, and a `recommended_requirement_cents`. That recommendation is `per_seat_requirement_cents` multiplied by 0.90 and rounded half up to whole cents when the posture is `SOFT`, multiplied by 1.10 and rounded half up to whole cents when the posture is `PREMIUM`, and simply `per_seat_requirement_cents` unchanged when the posture is `NORMAL`.

### 3.8. Selection Events, Slot Geometry, and the Pick

A selection event hangs off a certified cycle and runs one allocation. Creating one supplies `event_type`, which is `SEASON_TICKET`, `POSTSEASON` or `AWAY_GAME`; `window_start` and `window_end`, the bounds of the event's occupation window on its recalculation, where `window_end` must be strictly later than `window_start`; `slot_minutes`, the length of each donor's individual selection slot, at least 1; an optional `slot_buffer_minutes`, the gap inserted between consecutive slots, defaulting to 0 and at least 0; and an optional `eligible_donor_min_points`, the points floor for inclusion in the candidate pool, defaulting to 0, at least 0 and carried to 2 decimal places. The event's `recalculation_id` comes from the path it was created under, never from the body. Creation imposes no gate at all on that recalculation's own `status`: an event may be drafted against a recalculation still in `DRAFT`, `COMPUTED` or `COMMUNICATED`, and it is publishing the slots — not creating the event — that demands `CERTIFIED`. Nor is the window checked against the logical clock, so an event may be drafted wholly in the past. It starts in `DRAFT` with `published_slot_count` and `scheduled_slot_count` both zero. The event lifecycle is `DRAFT → SLOTS_PUBLISHED → OPEN → CLOSED`, with `CLOSED` terminal and a cyclic detour through `SLOTS_STALE` described below.

Publishing the slots is the act that turns a ranking into a schedule. It is admitted when the event is in `DRAFT` **or** in `SLOTS_STALE`, when the parent recalculation is in `CERTIFIED`, when the eligible donor pool for this `event_type` is non-empty, and when the last slot's computed `slot_end` does not fall after the event's `window_end`. It ranks the eligible pool on the four keys of §3.5 and creates one slot per ranked donor, each carrying the donor's `donor_id`, its `selection_event_id`, a `slot_index`, a `slot_start`, a `slot_end`, and `rank_at_publish` — the donor's `rank_position` as observed at publication, which is the comparand the staleness check later reads. That snapshot is copied exactly as it stands rather than being required to exist, so a donor who has never been ranked — a pool admitted on points alone before any certification stamped a ranking — carries `rank_at_publish` null. The staleness check of §3.5 is a plain equality between that snapshot and the donor's `rank_position` as it stands after the new ranking is stamped, so a null snapshot against a freshly stamped rank counts as a change and stales the slot, while a null snapshot still matched by a null rank — a donor no longer in a rankable status, or one whose row has gone — counts as unchanged and leaves the slot alone. `published_slot_count` and `scheduled_slot_count` are both set to the pool size, every new slot starts in `slot_status` `SCHEDULED`, and the event's `version` advances by 1.

Slot geometry is arithmetic on the event window. Taking the ranked pool in order with a zero-based counter, each donor's `slot_index` is their position in that order; their `slot_start` is `window_start` advanced by the counter multiplied by the sum of `slot_minutes` and `slot_buffer_minutes`, expressed in minutes; and their `slot_end` is their `slot_start` advanced by `slot_minutes`. Each slot is **half-open**, including `slot_start` and excluding `slot_end`: a pick at exactly `slot_end` belongs to the next slot, never to this one, and is rejected against this slot with `ERR_OUTSIDE_SLOT_WINDOW`. If the last slot's `slot_end` would fall strictly after `window_end`, nothing at all is published and the request is rejected with `ERR_SLOT_WINDOW_OVERFLOW` — the schedule is all-or-nothing. Event windows on the same recalculation are half-open by the same convention, so two events may touch at a shared boundary instant without overlapping, while any genuine interior overlap is a conflict. Seat hold windows, by deliberate contrast, use the **opposite** convention: a hold's interval is closed at both ends, so a pick at exactly a prior hold's `hold_end` still collides with that hold.

Republication is the recovery edge that makes the event machine cyclic. When an event has fallen to `SLOTS_STALE`, publishing again moves `SLOTS_STALE → SLOTS_PUBLISHED`, discards the `STALE` slots, and lays down a fresh generation from the current ranking under the same preconditions as a first publication. Discarding is literal: a `STALE` slot is deleted outright rather than retired into a terminal status, so it leaves the selection-slot collection entirely and a later fetch of its identifier answers `ERR_SELECTION_SLOT_NOT_FOUND`. Only slots in `STALE` are removed — slots already `CONSUMED` or `FORFEITED` on the event survive republication untouched, and because `published_slot_count` is overwritten with the new pool size rather than accumulated, that counter describes the newest generation alone while the surviving older slots remain visible in the collection. The deletion happens only after every precondition has passed, so a republication rejected for a stale-slot overflow or an empty pool leaves the old `STALE` slots in place. It also repairs what the staleness cascade broke: every relocation request on this event sitting in `BLOCKED` moves `BLOCKED → AWAITING_SELECTION`, its donor's `pending_request_count` is incremented again, and the request's own `version` advances by 1 while the donor's `version` and `updated_at` stand still.

Gating the event is a separate call carrying `to_status`, and that field admits exactly two values, `OPEN` and `CLOSED`; any other status name — including `DRAFT`, `SLOTS_PUBLISHED` and `SLOTS_STALE`, which are reached by other means — is a malformed request rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than a state conflict. Opening it moves `SLOTS_PUBLISHED → OPEN`, requires the event to be in `SLOTS_PUBLISHED`, and additionally requires that no other event on the same recalculation whose status is `SLOTS_PUBLISHED` or `OPEN` has a window overlapping this event's window; an interior overlap is rejected with `ERR_SELECTION_WINDOW_OVERLAP`, while a shared boundary instant is permitted. Only the event's own recalculation is consulted, so two events on different recalculations may overlap freely, and events already in `DRAFT`, `SLOTS_STALE` or `CLOSED` are not obstacles. Opening never consults the logical clock: an event whose window has already elapsed, or has not yet begun, opens exactly the same way, and it is the per-slot window check at pick time that enforces timing. Once `OPEN`, the event accepts picks and relocation requests. Closing it moves `OPEN → CLOSED` and requires the event to be in `OPEN` — a `SLOTS_STALE` event therefore cannot be closed directly and must be republished and reopened first; it moves every slot still in `SCHEDULED` to `FORFEITED`, advancing each of those slots' `version` by 1, and drives `scheduled_slot_count` to zero while leaving `published_slot_count` standing as the record of what was published. It then denies every outstanding relocation request on the event. In both directions the event's `version` advances by 1.

An event does not only reach `SLOTS_STALE` from `SLOTS_PUBLISHED`; it also falls there from `OPEN`. Both `SLOTS_PUBLISHED → SLOTS_STALE` and `OPEN → SLOTS_STALE` are driven by the certification cascade of §3.5 and share identical preconditions — that certification marked at least one of this event's `SCHEDULED` slots as `STALE`, and that no slot on the event remains in `SCHEDULED` — and identical side effects: relocation requests on the event that were in `AWAITING_SELECTION` move to `BLOCKED`, and the event's `version` advances by 1. An event mid-selection is therefore just as vulnerable to a rank shift as one that has not yet opened.

A donor who does not want their turn gives it up with a `reason`. That `reason` is required and must not be blank, but it is used only as a discipline on the caller: no field on the slot records it and it appears nowhere in any later response. Forfeiting moves the slot `SCHEDULED → FORFEITED`, requires the slot to be in `SCHEDULED` and — for a `Donor`-role caller — the caller's `X-User-ID` to equal the slot's `donor_id`, decrements the event's `scheduled_slot_count`, and advances the slot's `version` by 1. The parent event's own `status` is never examined, so a slot on an event still in `SLOTS_PUBLISHED` is just as forfeitable as one on an `OPEN` event — a donor may give up a turn before selection has even been opened — and the slot's own window is not consulted either, so a forfeit is accepted before, during or after that window. In keeping with §2.9 the `scheduled_slot_count` decrement leaves the event's `version` and `updated_at` untouched, because the event is not the resource this transition names. A slot's four states are `SCHEDULED`, `STALE`, `CONSUMED` and `FORFEITED`, of which `CONSUMED` and `FORFEITED` are terminal.

Consuming a slot is the donor's own act and the only way a seat is claimed through an event. The pick names `seat_id`, the seat being taken, and `expected_seat_version`, which must equal that seat's current `version`. It moves the slot `SCHEDULED → CONSUMED` and requires all of the following: the slot is in `SCHEDULED`; the caller's `X-User-ID` equals the slot's `donor_id`; the parent event is in `OPEN`, where an event still in `DRAFT` or `SLOTS_PUBLISHED`, or already `CLOSED`, is reported as `ERR_INVALID_STATE` while an event in `SLOTS_STALE` is singled out as `ERR_EVENT_SLOTS_STALE`; the current logical clock instant lies inside the slot's half-open window, at or after `slot_start` and strictly before `slot_end`; the section holding the target seat has `status` `OPEN`; and the target seat is either in `seat_status` `AVAILABLE` or already in `HELD` with spare capacity under its `max_concurrent_holds`. A seat already `ASSIGNED`, or one that has been `DECOMMISSIONED`, is refused with `ERR_SEAT_NOT_AVAILABLE`. If the count of the seat's hold windows whose closed intervals contain the pick instant has already reached `max_concurrent_holds`, the pick is refused with `ERR_SEAT_HOLD_CONFLICT`. That count is taken across the seat's assignments still in `occupancy_status` `HELD` or `ASSIGNED` only: a row already `RELEASED` no longer collides however its window is drawn, since the hold it represented has been given up. And if the donor's `certified_points` is below the section's `required_points_per_seat`, the pick is refused with `ERR_SEAT_POINTS_SHORTFALL`.

The pick creates a seat assignment. Its `donor_id` is copied from the slot's `donor_id` and never read from the body, and its `selection_slot_id` records the slot it came through. The row starts with `occupancy_status` `HELD` and `payment_status` `UNBILLED`, `amount_paid_cents` at 0, `eligibility_status` `ELIGIBLE` and `release_reason` `NONE`. Its hold window is closed at both ends: `hold_start` is the current logical clock instant and `hold_end` is 72 hours later — a hold lasts 4,320 minutes. Two snapshots are frozen onto the row at creation and never move afterwards: `required_points` copies the section's `required_points_per_seat`, and `points_at_pick` copies the donor's `certified_points` as they stood at the moment of the pick. On success the seat moves `AVAILABLE → HELD`, one seat shifts out of the section's `seats_available` and into its `seats_committed_count`, the event's `scheduled_slot_count` decrements, and the `version` of the seat, the section and the slot each advance by 1. The event itself is not one of those three: its `version` and `updated_at` stand still across a pick even though its counter moved, exactly as §2.9 provides. The response is the newly created seat assignment, returned with 201.

A pick can also displace an earlier holder. When the seat already carries a hold whose closed interval has ended before the pick instant, that earlier assignment is moved `HELD → RELEASED` by the system with `release_reason` set to `SUPERSEDED_BY_NEW_HOLD` and its `version` advanced by 1 — and, unlike a donor-initiated release, the seat is **left in `HELD`** for the incoming assignment rather than returned to `AVAILABLE`, because the seat never actually became free. Only rows still in `HELD` are displaced this way, never an `ASSIGNED` one, and every such expired hold on the seat is displaced rather than just the oldest, taken in `created_at` ascending order. The supersede is a narrower act than a release in one further respect: the displaced row's own consuming slot is left standing at `CONSUMED` rather than being walked on to `FORFEITED`. The whole displacement happens only after every precondition above has passed, so a rejected pick leaves the expired holder in `HELD`. Note that this supersede path is precisely the case the seat's version stamp guards: no state precondition would have rejected the second pick, so a picker working from an outdated inventory view is caught only by `expected_seat_version` and reported as `ERR_STALE_VERSION`.

Pricing the new row happens in two steps, and which step applies depends on how long the donor has been in the section. `pricing_mode` is `RENEWAL` when the donor already holds a seat assignment in `occupancy_status` `ASSIGNED` in the same section from an earlier cycle year, in which case `first_occupied_cycle_year` is inherited from that prior assignment; otherwise `pricing_mode` is `MARKET` and `first_occupied_cycle_year` is the cycle year of the event's recalculation. "From an earlier cycle year" is decided on the prior row's own stored `first_occupied_cycle_year`, which must be strictly less than the cycle year now being priced — not on when that row was created, and not on any other date — and `occupancy_status` `ASSIGNED` is the only status that counts, so a row still merely `HELD`, or one already `RELEASED`, confers no tenure. Where the donor has several qualifying rows in the section, the one with the earliest `first_occupied_cycle_year` wins, ties among equal years being broken by `created_at` ascending and then by write order, so the longest tenure the donor can demonstrate is the one inherited. `grandfathered_requirement_cents` is the `per_seat_requirement_cents` recorded in the section's `requirement_history` for `first_occupied_cycle_year`; because a section may carry more than one history entry for a single `cycle_year` — repricing and reseating both append without replacing — it is the **earliest appended** entry for that year that supplies the figure, never the latest, so a later reprice within the same year cannot retroactively raise a renewing donor's grandfathered base. When no history entry exists for that year at all, the section's current market rate is used and `pricing_mode` is forced back to `MARKET` even though a prior tenure was found; `first_occupied_cycle_year`, however, keeps the inherited earlier year rather than reverting to the current cycle.

Under `MARKET` pricing, `requirement_cents` is simply the section's current `per_seat_requirement_cents`, with no escalator and no ceiling. Under `RENEWAL` pricing, let the years held be the cycle year of the recalculation that owns the event — or, during a reseating, the cycle year of the reseating recalculation — minus `first_occupied_cycle_year`. That difference is used exactly as it comes out and is never floored at zero or at one: a reseating driven by a recalculation whose `cycle_year` is the same as or earlier than the row's `first_occupied_cycle_year` yields a zero or negative exponent, which leaves the escalated figure at, or below, the grandfathered base. Two candidate figures are computed. The escalated figure is `grandfathered_requirement_cents` compounded at 1.03 raised to the power of the years held, rounded half up to whole cents. The ceiling figure is the section's current `per_seat_requirement_cents` multiplied by 0.85, rounded half up to whole cents. Both are rounded **before** they are compared, and `requirement_cents` is the smaller of the two. The practical effect is that a section which has just been repriced upward charges a renewing donor materially less than a brand-new donor, and never more than 85% of the current market rate.

The second step is the handling fee, which exists only for the two out-of-season allocations. For a `SEASON_TICKET` event, `handling_fee_cents` is 0 and `total_due_cents` equals `requirement_cents`. For a `POSTSEASON` or `AWAY_GAME` event, a handling rate is selected by band on `requirement_cents` — and the band is keyed on `requirement_cents` alone, never on the fee-inclusive total: below $500 (50,000 cents) the rate is 6%, at or above $500 but below $2,500 (250,000 cents) the rate is 4%, and at or above $2,500 the rate is 2%. `handling_fee_cents` is `requirement_cents` multiplied by that rate and rounded half up to whole cents, and `total_due_cents` is `requirement_cents` plus `handling_fee_cents`. Because the rate *decreases* as the base rises, keying the band off the fee-inclusive total would select the wrong band for a row sitting just under a boundary — which is exactly why the specification pins the input to the pre-fee figure.

### 3.9. The Two Axes of a Seat Assignment, and Moving Between Sections

A seat assignment carries **two independent status axes on the same row**, and neither constrains the other. `occupancy_status` tracks who physically holds the seat and runs `HELD → ASSIGNED → RELEASED`, with `RELEASED` terminal. `payment_status` tracks the money and runs across `UNBILLED`, `INVOICED`, `PAID`, `DELINQUENT` and the terminal `WRITTEN_OFF`. A row can be `RELEASED` while still `INVOICED`, or `PAID` while still merely `HELD`; each axis is advanced by its own call and neither call reads the other axis except where this document says so explicitly.

The occupancy call carries `to_status`, an optional `release_reason`, and `expected_seat_assignment_version`, which must equal the row's current `version`. Both fields are parsed before anything is decided, so an unrecognised `to_status` or an unrecognised `release_reason` is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` even on a call that would have ignored the reason. `to_status` accepts any of the three occupancy names, but `HELD` is not a destination: it parses cleanly and is then refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, since a hold is created by a pick or a grant and never re-entered. Confirming a hold moves the assignment `HELD → ASSIGNED` and requires four things, weighed in exactly this order: the version stamp matches; the row is in `HELD`; `amount_paid_cents` is at least `total_due_cents`; and `eligibility_status` is `ELIGIBLE`. A row that has not been paid up is refused with `ERR_SEAT_DONATION_SHORTFALL`, and a row that has drifted to `UNDERWATER` cannot be confirmed at all — that last refusal is `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409) rather than an eligibility-specific code, and because the money is weighed first a row that is simultaneously short and `UNDERWATER` reports the 402 shortfall. On success the seat itself moves to `ASSIGNED`, the section's `assigned_seat_count` increments, the seat's `version` advances by 1, and — if this assignment satisfies an outstanding relocation request — that request is set to `GRANTED` with its `granted_seat_assignment_id` pointed at this row. The assignment's own `version` advances by 1. The section, by contrast, is only recomputed here: unlike the release edges below, a confirmation leaves the section's `version` and `updated_at` exactly where they stood. At most one request is satisfied, and it is the donor's earliest `AWAITING_SELECTION` request targeting this seat's section, taken in `created_at` ascending order; its `version` advances by 1 and the donor's `pending_request_count` falls, but its `decision_note` is left null, its `current_seat_assignment_id` is not released — the donor's old seat stays exactly as it was — and the donor's own `version` and `updated_at` do not move. Any further `AWAITING_SELECTION` request the donor holds against the same section stays outstanding.

Releasing a hold moves `HELD → RELEASED` and requires the row to be in `HELD` with a matching version stamp. It sets `release_reason` from the payload, defaulting to `DONOR_RELEASE` when the caller supplies none; it returns the seat from `HELD` to `AVAILABLE` and restores the section's seat inventory counters to their pre-pick values, moving one seat from `seats_committed_count` back to `seats_available` and advancing both the seat's and the section's `version` by 1; and it walks the consuming slot from `CONSUMED` back to `FORFEITED`, advancing that slot's `version` by 1. That last step is worth dwelling on: `CONSUMED` looks terminal but is not a lockdown state, and a release moves the slot to the *other* terminal state rather than restoring the donor's turn.

Releasing a confirmed assignment moves `ASSIGNED → RELEASED` and requires the row to be in `ASSIGNED` with a matching version stamp. It sets `release_reason` from the payload, defaulting to `DONOR_RELEASE` on this edge exactly as on the one above, returns the seat from `ASSIGNED` to `AVAILABLE` — moving one seat from `seats_committed_count` back to `seats_available` and advancing the seat's and the section's `version` by 1 — decrements the section's `assigned_seat_count`, and, when the released row was `UNDERWATER`, decrements the section's `underwater_seat_count` and recomputes its `pricing_posture`. The consuming slot, however, is left exactly where it stands: only a release from `HELD` walks a `CONSUMED` slot on to `FORFEITED`, so a seat given up after confirmation leaves its slot recorded as `CONSUMED` forever. On both release edges the seat is returned to the pool only if it currently stands in `HELD` or `ASSIGNED`; a seat already `DECOMMISSIONED` by a reseating keeps that status and its `version` does not move, while the assignment is released regardless. The assignment's `version` advances by 1. The four `release_reason` values are `NONE`, `DONOR_RELEASE`, `SUPERSEDED_BY_NEW_HOLD` and `RESEATING`, and on either release edge the caller may name any of the four — the field is validated against that vocabulary and then written through unexamined, so nothing stops a release being recorded as `NONE` or as `SUPERSEDED_BY_NEW_HOLD`. What the service writes on its own initiative is narrower: `NONE` at creation, whether by a pick or by a relocation grant; `SUPERSEDED_BY_NEW_HOLD` when a fresh pick displaces an expired hold on the same seat; and `DONOR_RELEASE` as the default whenever a release supplies no reason and on the release a granted relocation performs on the donor's previous row. `RESEATING` is the one value the service never writes for itself: the section reseating of §3.7 moves only the money axis and does not touch `release_reason` — or `occupancy_status` — on any row it reprices, so a row carrying `RESEATING` got there because a caller asked for it.

The billing call carries `to_status` and an optional `amount_cents`, and — alone among the assignment writes — no version stamp at all, so a stale concurrent billing write is not guarded here. Invoicing moves `UNBILLED → INVOICED`, requires the row to be in `UNBILLED`, and freezes `requirement_cents`, `handling_fee_cents` and `total_due_cents` for the row — "freezes" meaning simply that invoicing recomputes nothing: the three figures the pick or the grant wrote stand as they are, and only a reseating (§3.7) ever rewrites them. Paying moves either `INVOICED → PAID` or `DELINQUENT → PAID` — both require the row to be in `INVOICED` or `DELINQUENT` and require the tendered `amount_cents` to be at least `total_due_cents`, and both set `amount_paid_cents` to the tendered `amount_cents`; a short tender is refused with `ERR_SEAT_DONATION_SHORTFALL`, and so is an *omitted* `amount_cents`, which is treated as a shortfall rather than as a missing field even though the field is optional on the other transitions. Marking a row delinquent moves `INVOICED → DELINQUENT` and requires the row to be in `INVOICED`. Writing a row off moves `DELINQUENT → WRITTEN_OFF`, is reserved to the `PlatformAdmin`, requires the row to be in `DELINQUENT`, and leaves `amount_paid_cents` exactly as it stood; that role restriction is evaluated inside the transition rather than at the door, so it is weighed after the path and body have been accepted but ahead of the `DELINQUENT` precondition — a non-admin naming a row that is not even `DELINQUENT` still gets `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`. `UNBILLED` is a legal value of `to_status` but never a legal destination: naming it parses cleanly and is then refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, because the money axis never runs backwards to unbilled. The cure edge from `DELINQUENT` back to `PAID` deliberately touches nothing on the occupancy axis. Every one of these billing transitions advances the assignment's `version` by 1.

A donor who wants a different seat files a relocation or upgrade request rather than picking again. The filing carries `donor_id`; `current_seat_assignment_id`, the existing assignment being moved; `target_section_id`, the section the donor wants; `selection_event_id`, the event the move would be executed through; `request_type`, either `RELOCATION` for a lateral move or `UPGRADE` for a higher-tier section; and `expected_donor_version`. The named event must be in `SLOTS_PUBLISHED` or `OPEN`; an event in any other status, `SLOTS_STALE` included, is refused with `ERR_EVENT_NOT_OPEN_FOR_REQUESTS`. The donor's current assignment must also be fully paid — filing while it still owes money is refused with `ERR_SEAT_DONATION_SHORTFALL`. Only that money is weighed: the row named by `current_seat_assignment_id` need not still be live, so a row already `RELEASED` or `WRITTEN_OFF` is an acceptable starting point provided `amount_paid_cents` covers `total_due_cents`, and it need not even belong to the filing donor. The target section's own `status` is likewise not examined at filing — a section under renovation may be requested, and it is the grant that insists on `OPEN` — and `request_type` is purely descriptive: choosing `UPGRADE` over `RELOCATION` changes no precondition, no pricing and no later behaviour. A `Donor`-role filer may only name their own `donor_id`; that ownership check runs before the body is validated, but it can only fire when `donor_id` is present and is a string, so a donor who omits `donor_id` altogether, or sends it as a number, falls through to the ordinary missing-field rejection with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` instead of `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. The four references are resolved in the order `donor_id`, `current_seat_assignment_id`, `target_section_id`, `selection_event_id`, so when several are unknown it is the first of them that is reported. The new request lands in `AWAITING_SELECTION` with `granted_seat_assignment_id` and `decision_note` null, and the donor's `version` advances by 1. The response is the newly created request, returned with 201.

Filing a second request replaces the first. The donor's prior request in `AWAITING_SELECTION` moves `AWAITING_SELECTION → SUPERSEDED`, requires that prior request to be in `AWAITING_SELECTION`, requires the new filing to carry the donor's current `version` in `expected_donor_version`, records the supersede reason in the superseded request's `decision_note`, overwriting whatever note that request carried, and advances that request's `version` by 1. Every one of the donor's outstanding requests is superseded, not merely the newest — a donor can be holding two at once after a republication revived a pair of `BLOCKED` rows — and they are visited in `created_at` ascending order. The sweep is indifferent to which event or section those requests name, and a donor with none simply files a first request. `BLOCKED` requests are not swept: only `AWAITING_SELECTION` is superseded, so a blocked request survives a fresh filing and can still be revived or denied on its own. Because `pending_request_count` is simply the number of the donor's requests currently in `AWAITING_SELECTION`, a donor's *first* request increments it while a *replacement* leaves it unchanged — the old row leaves the bucket exactly as the new one enters it. `SUPERSEDED` is terminal, alongside `GRANTED` and `DENIED`; the five states are `AWAITING_SELECTION`, `BLOCKED`, `GRANTED`, `DENIED` and `SUPERSEDED`.

Deciding a request is a development-office or platform-operator act carrying a `decision` of `GRANT` or `DENY`, an optional `target_seat_id`, and an optional `decision_note` which, on either decision, must not be blank when it is supplied at all. Granting moves `AWAITING_SELECTION → GRANTED` and requires the request to be in `AWAITING_SELECTION`, `target_seat_id` to name a takeable seat belonging to `target_section_id`, and the target section's `required_points_per_seat` to be at most the donor's `certified_points`. "Takeable" is the same test the pick applies: the seat must merely be neither `ASSIGNED` nor `DECOMMISSIONED`, so a seat standing in `HELD` under someone else's live hold **is** an admissible relocation target and keeps its `HELD` status as the second hold is laid on it — the grant does not consult `max_concurrent_holds` or any hold window, which is the one place in this service where two holds may be created on one seat without a capacity check. A seat that is `ASSIGNED` or `DECOMMISSIONED` is refused with `ERR_SEAT_NOT_AVAILABLE`; a missing `target_seat_id`, or one naming a seat outside `target_section_id`, with `ERR_RELOCATION_TARGET_INVALID`; a target section not in `status` `OPEN` with `ERR_SECTION_UNDER_RENOVATION`; and a points shortfall with `ERR_SEAT_POINTS_SHORTFALL`. Those four are weighed in exactly that reading order after the request's own status: section first, then the seat's availability, then whether the seat belongs to the section, then the points — so a dead seat in the *wrong* section reports `ERR_SEAT_NOT_AVAILABLE` rather than `ERR_RELOCATION_TARGET_INVALID`, while a `target_seat_id` naming nothing at all is an unresolved body reference and is reported as `ERR_REFERENCED_ENTITY_NOT_FOUND` before any of them. On success a new seat assignment is created on the target seat with `pricing_mode` `MARKET` — a relocation always pays the market rate, never a grandfathered one — and with `selection_slot_id` left null because no slot was consumed. Its `requirement_cents` is the target section's current `per_seat_requirement_cents`; its `handling_fee_cents` is 0 and its `total_due_cents` therefore equals `requirement_cents`, with no handling band applied even when the request's event is a `POSTSEASON` or `AWAY_GAME` one; `first_occupied_cycle_year` is the cycle year of that event's recalculation, so tenure starts afresh in the new section; `grandfathered_requirement_cents` is recorded for the record as the section's `requirement_history` figure for that year, falling back to the current market rate when the year has no entry, but it is never used to price this row; `amount_paid_cents` starts at 0 with `payment_status` `UNBILLED`, `eligibility_status` `ELIGIBLE` and `release_reason` `NONE`; `required_points` and `points_at_pick` are snapshotted from the section and the donor exactly as at a pick; and the row starts in `occupancy_status` `HELD` with the same closed 72-hour hold window measured from the current logical clock instant. The seat and the section each advance their `version` by 1 and the section's inventory counters are recomputed. `granted_seat_assignment_id` is pointed at the new row; the assignment named by `current_seat_assignment_id` is released with `release_reason` `DONOR_RELEASE`, but only if it is still in `HELD` or `ASSIGNED` — a row already `RELEASED` is left untouched, and a release from `HELD` walks its consuming slot on to `FORFEITED` just as an ordinary release would; the donor's `pending_request_count` decrements while the donor's own `version` and `updated_at` stand still; and the request's `version` advances by 1. The response is the updated relocation request, returned with 200, not the seat assignment the grant created.

Denying a request moves it to `DENIED` from either `AWAITING_SELECTION` or `BLOCKED` — the precondition admits both — records `decision_note`, decrements the donor's `pending_request_count`, and advances the request's `version` by 1. That note is written unconditionally, so denying with the field omitted writes null over whatever reason the row was already carrying — the blocking reason a rank shift left on it, for instance. A denial reaches none of the grant's other machinery: no seat, section or assignment is touched, and the donor's own `version` and `updated_at` stand still. The response is the updated request with 200. The same destination is reached without any operator action when the event closes: closing an event denies every request on it that was in `AWAITING_SELECTION` or `BLOCKED`, taken in `created_at` ascending order, recording the closure reason in `decision_note` — again overwriting any note already there — and advancing each request's `version` by 1, while each affected donor's `pending_request_count` falls without that donor's `version` or `updated_at` moving. A request that was `BLOCKED` by a rank shift therefore has three futures and nothing else — republication returns it to `AWAITING_SELECTION`, an operator denies it outright, or it is denied by the closing of its event — and the third of those is reachable only after the first, because an event in `SLOTS_STALE` cannot be gated closed and must be republished and reopened before it can close.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Request payloads, response bodies and side effects are **not** repeated here — they are specified in the §3 narratives, and an implementer must read §3 to reconstruct a complete request. Status codes follow one rule with no exceptions: an operation whose response body *is* a newly created resource returns **201 Created**, and every other successful operation — including every read and every state transition on an existing resource — returns **200 OK**.
