# Branch Network Optimization API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Retail banking distribution is no longer a growth story; it is a portfolio-management problem. A regional bank operating several hundred branches faces steadily migrating transaction volume, rising per-square-foot occupancy cost, and a regulatory regime that treats a branch closure as a community event rather than a real-estate decision. The Branch Network Optimization API is the system of record for that problem. It scores every branch on the four factors distribution committees actually argue about — deposits on book, transaction volume, digital adoption and operating efficiency — places each branch in a candidacy band, and then carries a proposed consolidation through the full institutional gauntlet: trade-area saturation analysis, deposit-retention modeling, community impact assessment, regulatory notice, workforce disposition, lease or owned-property exit, customer transition waves, and twelve months of post-closure deposit runoff surveillance against the very forecast that justified the closure.

Two pressures shape every requirement in this document. The first is **segregation of duties**. A closure that eliminates jobs and removes a branch from a low-or-moderate-income neighborhood cannot be authored and approved by the same person, and the regulatory filing cannot be produced by the strategist whose savings case depends on it. The second is **reversibility with arithmetic integrity**. Consolidation moves real deposit balances between branch ledgers and real headcount between staffing rosters; when a closure is unwound inside its rescission window, or a redeployment offer is rescinded, the network's totals must return to exactly where they were, to the cent and to the person. The API therefore treats deposits and headcount as conserved quantities and pins the evaluation order of every derived figure so that two implementations reading this document produce byte-identical numbers.

The system also serves the growth side of the ledger. De novo site plans are scored against trade-area demographics and competitor density, gated on market saturation, opened as live branches, and then tracked against a quadratic first-year ramp curve. Growth and contraction are deliberately coupled: a market that is currently remediating a runoff breach on an executed closure is barred from opening new branches until the breach resolves. All resources described here are mounted under the `/distribution` prefix, with a single administrative clock endpoint outside it.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contract-level rules that apply uniformly across every endpoint: authentication, response shape, pagination, resource identity, rejection precedence, version pinning, numeric discipline, clock semantics and cascade integrity.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting principal and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity in which that principal is acting. Seven roles are recognised, spelled exactly as follows: `NetworkStrategist`, `ConsolidationApprover`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `HRPartner`, `RealEstateManager`, `BranchManager` and `NetworkOpsAdmin`. If either header is absent or empty, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of these seven strings, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401); a header that is present but consists only of whitespace counts as empty for this test. The role is matched against the seven spellings exactly and case-sensitively with no trimming, so `networkstrategist` and ` NetworkStrategist` are both unrecognised and both yield the 401 — an unrecognised role is never reported as `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`. `X-User-ID` itself is carried through verbatim, untrimmed, into every identifier comparison the rules below make. If both headers are well formed but the declared role is not among the roles authorised for the requested operation in the endpoint table of section 4, the request is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

Six of the seven roles are operator identities. When `X-User-Role` is `NetworkStrategist`, `ConsolidationApprover`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `HRPartner`, `RealEstateManager` or `NetworkOpsAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — it stamps `created_by_user_id` on a consolidation case from the `NetworkStrategist` who opened the case, and stamps `decided_by_user_id` from the `ConsolidationApprover` who decides it — but it never compares such an identifier to any entity's `id`. Those two recorded identifiers are what the segregation-of-duties rules below are evaluated against.

`BranchManager` is the one entity-backed role. When `X-User-Role` is `BranchManager`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Branch`; ownership checks compare the branch identifier in the request path to `X-User-ID`. A `BranchManager` may record a performance period for their own branch and read their own branch's on-demand score, and attempting either of those two operations against a branch whose `id` differs from `X-User-ID` is rejected with `ERR_BRANCH_OWNERSHIP_REQUIRED` (HTTP 403). The ownership restriction reaches exactly those two operations and no others: listing a branch's snapshot history, reading a single snapshot, reading a branch row and every other read endpoint are open to `BranchManager` without an ownership restriction. The comparison is a plain string comparison between the header value and the branch identifier taken from the request path, made before that identifier is resolved, so a `BranchManager` naming a branch that does not exist at all still receives `ERR_BRANCH_OWNERSHIP_REQUIRED` rather than `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`; a role other than `BranchManager` is never subject to the check.

Two further identity rules govern the consolidation case itself. A `ConsolidationApprover` whose `X-User-ID` equals the case's `created_by_user_id` may not record a decision on that case; the attempt is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Conversely, submission and revision of a case are reserved to its author: a `NetworkStrategist` whose `X-User-ID` differs from the case's `created_by_user_id` is rejected with `ERR_CASE_AUTHOR_REQUIRED` (HTTP 403). Regulatory work — impact assessment, assessment finalisation, notice filing and withdrawal, transition-wave planning and launch, closure execution and closure unwind — belongs exclusively to `ComplianceOfficer`. Workforce disposition belongs exclusively to `HRPartner`, premises and ATM disposition exclusively to `RealEstateManager`, monitored-case close-out and the logical clock exclusively to `NetworkOpsAdmin`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`, followed by exactly 20 characters drawn from the uppercase letters `A`-`Z` and the digits `0`-`9`, giving a 24-character string in total. A fresh value is minted per request, and the same value appears in the envelope of a successful response and of an error response alike.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection endpoint is paginated by two query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10, accepts a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100, and a value outside that inclusive range is a malformed request. `offset` defaults to 0 and counts records to skip from the head of the ordered result; a negative `offset` is likewise a malformed request, and there is no upper bound on it — an `offset` at or beyond the end of the filtered set returns an empty `data` array while `total_count` still reports the full filtered size. Both parameters must be written as a plain base-10 integer literal; a value carrying a decimal point, a sign other than a leading `-`, or any non-digit character is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A parameter supplied with an empty string value is treated exactly as if it had been omitted, and so falls back to its default. Unless a specific listing states otherwise, collections are ordered by `created_at` ascending, with records sharing a `created_at` value ordered by the sequence in which the server wrote them, oldest write first. For list responses the `meta` block carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` (the number of records matching the filter before the window is applied) and `has_more`, which is true exactly when `offset` plus the number of records actually returned in this window is still less than `total_count`. Query filters follow the same discipline: a filter supplied with an empty string value is treated as absent and narrows nothing; a filter whose value must come from a declared enumeration — a `status`, a `saturation_tier`, a `performance_band`, a snapshot `band`, a `notice_type`, a `model_variant`, an `impact_tier`, a `disposition_state`, a `notification_state`, an `atm_disposition`, a `monitoring_posture`, a `ramp_status`, a `subject_type` or a `scoring_variant` — is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` when the supplied value is not a member of that enumeration, whereas a free-text or identifier-valued filter such as `region_code`, `market_area_id` or `consolidation_case_id` is matched literally and simply selects nothing when no record carries that value; the one boolean filter, `breach`, accepts `true`, `false`, `1` and `0` in any letter case and rejects anything else with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; and query parameters the endpoint does not declare are ignored entirely rather than rejected.

Three listings deliberately invert the default order because their readers care about the newest record: the performance history of a branch is returned newest reporting period first, and the retention forecasts, impact assessments and closure notices of a single consolidation case are each returned newest first.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Versioning

Every stored resource carries a server-minted `id` in UUID form with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp in ISO 8601 UTC with three fractional digits. Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, refreshed to the current instant on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that begins at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that resource — including mutations caused indirectly by a cascade from another resource, not only mutations requested directly by the client. The increment is once per resource per request, not once per write: when a single request touches the same resource at several points — as the multi-hop cascades of section 2.9 routinely do, recomputing a case's savings chain on the way through a closure that also stamps the case's own fields — that resource's `version` still advances by exactly 1 in total and its `updated_at` carries a single refreshed instant. A read never advances a version.

Two resource families are immutable append-only records and therefore carry neither field. A `PerformanceSnapshot` and a `DepositObservation` each expose only `id` and `created_at`; both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from their representations, because neither resource is ever modified after it is written.

Nested paths are resolved strictly left to right. When a request addresses a snapshot inside a branch, or an assessment inside a case, the outer identifier is resolved first and an unresolvable identifier at any position yields `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) naming that position — the server never skips ahead to test a later segment. Foreign keys supplied in a request body are a separate concern: a body identifier such as `market_area_id`, `closing_branch_id`, `receiving_branch_id`, `target_branch_id`, `consolidation_case_id` or `de_novo_plan_id` that does not resolve to a stored resource yields `ERR_REFERENCED_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404), which is a distinct code from the path-parameter failure. Body foreign-key resolution applies to branch registration, case creation and case revision, retention-forecast creation, closure-notice filing, staff-disposition creation, property-disposition creation, transition-campaign creation, de novo plan creation, and deposit-observation creation. Two routing-level failures share the path-parameter code: a request whose path matches no declared route at all, and a request that uses an HTTP method the matched path does not declare, both answer `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) rather than any method-specific status. Because no route body runs in either case, these two answers are produced without any authentication or authorisation check, so they precede even rank 1 of section 2.5 — an unauthenticated request to an undeclared path reads back 404, not 401. A trailing slash on an otherwise valid path is accepted and addresses the same route.

### 2.5. Request Rejection Precedence

Many operations in this system are guarded by several independent checks at once, and which error the client receives is itself part of the contract. The server evaluates guards in one fixed global order and returns the first failure it encounters; it never masks an earlier-ranked failure behind a later-ranked one. The order is as follows.

1. **Header presence and role validity** — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. **Role authorisation for the endpoint** — `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`.
3. **Segregation of duties on a case decision** — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, when the deciding approver's `X-User-ID` equals the case's `created_by_user_id`.
4. **Case authorship on submission and revision** — `ERR_CASE_AUTHOR_REQUIRED`, on case submit and case revise only.
5. **Branch ownership for a `BranchManager`** — `ERR_BRANCH_OWNERSHIP_REQUIRED`, on recording a performance period for a branch and on the on-demand performance score of a branch.
6. **Request well-formedness** — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Required fields must be present, enum fields must carry a declared member, integers must satisfy their declared ranges, strings must satisfy their declared length bounds and character patterns, dates must be ISO 8601, distance and mileage values must carry at most one decimal place, and `limit` must fall in 1 through 100. A field must also carry the declared JSON type: a value where an integer is declared must be a JSON integer, so a boolean and a number with a fractional part are both malformed; a value where a boolean is declared must be a JSON boolean, so the strings `"true"` and `"false"` are malformed; a value where a string is declared must be a JSON string. A field present with an explicit `null` is treated exactly as an absent field — malformed if the field is required, and otherwise falling back to the declared default. A request body that is absent or empty is read as the empty object, so a required field simply reports as missing; a body that is syntactically invalid JSON, or valid JSON that is not an object, is malformed. Every one of these conditions reports the same `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` at the same rank, so there is no observable precedence between a missing field, a wrongly typed field and an out-of-range field: whichever fails, the client sees one 400 with this identifier.
7. **Path-parameter resolution, left to right** — `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`, on every endpoint that has path parameters.
8. **Body foreign-key resolution** — `ERR_REFERENCED_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`.
9. **Branch version pinning on case creation** — `ERR_STALE_BRANCH_VERSION`.
10. **Case version pinning on retention-forecast creation and on case decision** — `ERR_STALE_CASE_VERSION`.
11. **Consolidation case from-state** — `ERR_CASE_STATE_INVALID`, guarding case submit, case decision, case revise, closure execution, closure unwind, case close-out, closure-notice filing, transition-wave launch, impact-assessment finalisation, the four staff-disposition transitions (notify, accept, rescind and sever) and premises contracting. On those last six operations the owning case's status is tested only *after* the addressed resource's own from-state, so a wrong assessment, disposition or premises from-state at rank 14, 15 or 16 is reported in preference to the case-status failure; on every other operation in the list this rank holds its stated position.
12. **Closing branch already has an open case** — `ERR_BRANCH_HAS_OPEN_CASE`, on case creation. This check deliberately outranks the plain not-active check at position 31, so that a branch tied up by an in-flight case reports the specific open-case diagnosis rather than the generic one.
13. **Closure notice is still open** — `ERR_NOTICE_NOT_OPEN`, on notice withdrawal and on closure execution.
14. **Staff disposition from-state, on either axis** — `ERR_DISPOSITION_STATE_INVALID`, guarding notify, accept, rescind and sever.
15. **Property disposition from-state and tenure fork match** — `ERR_PROPERTY_STATE_INVALID`, guarding contract and settle.
16. **Impact assessment from-state** — `ERR_ASSESSMENT_STATE_INVALID`, on finalisation.
17. **Transition campaign from-state** — `ERR_CAMPAIGN_STATE_INVALID`, on launch.
18. **De novo plan from-state** — `ERR_PLAN_STATE_INVALID`, guarding the site decision and the site opening.
19. **Receiving branch is `ACTIVE`** — `ERR_RECEIVING_BRANCH_NOT_ACTIVE`, checked at case submit, at closure execution and at redeployment acceptance.
20. **Impact assessment finalised** — `ERR_IMPACT_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FINALIZED`, on closure-notice filing.
21. **Notice lead time sufficient** — `ERR_NOTICE_PERIOD_INSUFFICIENT`, on closure-notice filing.
22. **Notice period elapsed** — `ERR_NOTICE_PERIOD_NOT_ELAPSED`, on closure execution.
23. **WARN period elapsed** — `ERR_WARN_PERIOD_NOT_ELAPSED`, on severance.
24. **A transition wave has launched** — `ERR_TRANSITION_CAMPAIGN_NOT_LAUNCHED`, on closure execution.
25. **Market notice window free** — `ERR_NOTICE_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, on closure-notice filing.
26. **Planned closure date not earlier than the effective date** — `ERR_PLANNED_DATE_BEFORE_EFFECTIVE`, on closure-notice filing.
27. **Wave dating and launch lead time** — `ERR_CAMPAIGN_LEAD_TIME_INSUFFICIENT`, on transition-wave creation and on transition-wave launch.
28. **Unwind window still open** — `ERR_UNWIND_WINDOW_CLOSED`, on closure unwind.
29. **Observation month sequence** — `ERR_OBSERVATION_OUT_OF_SEQUENCE`, on deposit-observation creation.
30. **Receiving-branch absorption capacity** — `ERR_ABSORPTION_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, on transition-wave creation.
31. **Closing branch is `ACTIVE`** — `ERR_BRANCH_NOT_ACTIVE`, on case creation.
32. **Receiving branch is a legal pairing** — `ERR_RECEIVING_BRANCH_INVALID`, on case creation and case revision.
33. **Uniqueness keys** — `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY`, on trade-area registration, branch registration, performance-period recording, staff-disposition creation, property-disposition creation, and de novo site opening.
34. **Segment shares sum exactly** — `ERR_SEGMENT_MIX_INVALID`, on retention-forecast creation.
35. **Revision limit** — `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, on a return decision.
36. **Observation subject coherence** — `ERR_OBSERVATION_SUBJECT_MISMATCH`, on deposit-observation creation.
37. **Site score minimum** — `ERR_SITE_SCORE_BELOW_MINIMUM`, on an approve decision for a de novo plan.
38. **Market not over-branched** — `ERR_MARKET_OVER_BRANCHED`, on an approve decision for a de novo plan.
39. **Market not under a de novo hold** — `ERR_MARKET_DE_NOVO_HOLD`, on a de novo approve decision and on a de novo site opening.
40. **Exactly one active retention forecast** — `ERR_RETENTION_FORECAST_MISSING`, on case submit.
41. **A premises disposition exists** — `ERR_PROPERTY_DISPOSITION_MISSING`, on closure execution.
42. **No staff disposition still pending** — `ERR_STAFF_DISPOSITIONS_PENDING`, on closure execution.
43. **Selector found a receiving branch** — `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_RECEIVING_BRANCH`, on case creation when the receiving branch was omitted.
44. **Performance history exists** — `ERR_NO_PERFORMANCE_HISTORY`, on the on-demand performance score of a branch.
45. **Disposition target coherence** — `ERR_DISPOSITION_TARGET_INVALID`, on staff-disposition creation and on redeployment acceptance.
46. **Net savings clear the governance floor** — `ERR_SAVINGS_BELOW_FLOOR`, on an approve decision for a case.
47. **Monitoring horizon complete** — `ERR_MONITORING_INCOMPLETE`, on case close-out.
48. **Premises terms complete for the tenure form** — `ERR_PROPERTY_TERMS_INCOMPLETE`, on property-disposition creation.

The practical consequence of this ordering is worth stating twice: a request that is simultaneously unauthorised and malformed reports the authorisation failure, a request that names a stale branch version and also targets a branch with an open case reports the stale version, and a de novo approval that fails both the score minimum and the saturation gate reports the score minimum.

### 2.6. Version Pinning on Case-Critical Writes

Two write operations in this system can silently destroy work that another operator has already committed, and both are therefore protected by optimistic concurrency control against the parent resource the write reinterprets. In each case the pinning field is a required, non-optional element of the request body; omitting it is a malformed request, and supplying a value that does not equal the parent's current `version` is a precondition failure rather than a conflict. The field is declared as an integer of at least 1, which is the lowest `version` any resource can carry, so a pin of 0 or a negative pin never reaches the comparison at all — it is caught at rank 6 as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) rather than reported as a stale version, whereas any integer of 1 or more that simply disagrees with the parent's current `version`, high or low, is the 412.

A `POST /distribution/consolidation-cases` request must carry `expected_branch_version` holding the client's last observed `version` of the branch named by `closing_branch_id`. If the branch's `version` has advanced since the client read it, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_BRANCH_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This matters because a prior create may already have produced a draft case against the same branch and moved that branch to `UNDER_REVIEW`; the product permits a newer case to supersede such a draft, so a client working from a stale branch reading holds a stale picture of both the branch's `status` and its outstanding case set, and must re-read before proceeding.

A `POST /distribution/retention-forecasts` request must carry `expected_case_version` holding the client's last observed `version` of the case named by `consolidation_case_id`. A mismatch against the case's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CASE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The reason is that a new forecast legitimately supersedes the case's prior active forecast and rewrites the case's retention and savings figures wholesale; a writer holding an out-of-date case version would overwrite a model it has never seen. The same pinning field and the same error code guard `POST /distribution/consolidation-cases/{caseId}/decision`, where the approver's decision must be taken against the exact case revision the approver read.

No other endpoint accepts a version field. In particular, submission, revision, closure execution, closure unwind, close-out, notice filing and withdrawal, all staff and property transitions, wave planning and launch, de novo decisions and openings, and deposit observations take no `expected_branch_version` and no `expected_case_version`.

### 2.7. Units, Basis Points and Rounding Discipline

All monetary quantities in this API are whole-cent integers; no field carries a decimal currency value. All rates, shares and scores expressed as proportions are whole **basis points**, where 10000 basis points is 100 percent — a `digital_adoption_bps` of 6250 means 62.5 percent of that branch's households are digitally active, and a `household_growth_pct_bps` of 180 means projected household growth of 1.80 percent over five years. Impact and siting scores are instead expressed as whole points on a 0-to-100 scale. Two input fields are genuine decimals: `distance_miles` on a retention forecast and `distance_to_nearest_remaining_miles` on an impact assessment, each accepting a value from 0.0 through 60.0 inclusive with at most one decimal place. Both must arrive as a JSON number — a bare integer such as `5` is accepted and read as five whole miles, while a quoted string such as `"5.0"` and a boolean are malformed — and both bounds are inclusive, so 0.0 and 60.0 are accepted while 60.1 is not; a second decimal place such as 3.25 is malformed even when it falls inside the range.

Wherever a derived figure must be reduced to a whole cent, a whole basis point, a whole point or a whole household, the reduction is **round half up** — a fractional part of exactly one half rounds away from zero to the larger magnitude. Rounding is never applied twice to the same quantity and never applied at an unnamed step: each calculation in section 3 names the exact points at which rounding occurs, and where a calculation pins an order (round each component first, then combine and round once) that order is part of the contract because the alternative order yields a materially different figure. Threshold comparisons are inclusive at the lower boundary unless the narrative says otherwise; a value sitting exactly on a floor satisfies that floor.

### 2.8. The Logical Clock

Notice periods, WARN waiting periods, unwind windows and lease holdover all turn on the passage of calendar days, which a test run cannot wait out. The server therefore keeps a **logical current UTC instant**, and every date the server stamps — `filed_on`, `finalized_on`, `notified_on`, `severed_on`, `executed_on`, `launched_on`, `opened_on`, `marketing_started_on`, `settled_on`, `observed_on` — is the calendar date of that logical instant, not of wall-clock time. Until the clock is set for the first time it reads `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`.

A `NetworkOpsAdmin` may move the clock by supplying a required `to_iso8601` instant, and the response reports the resulting `current_utc` alongside two counters describing what the move triggered: `notices_made_effective` and `properties_moved_to_holdover`. The instant is accepted in any ISO 8601 form, with a trailing `Z`, with an explicit numeric UTC offset, or with no offset at all — a value carrying an offset is converted to UTC before it is stored, and a value carrying none is read as already being UTC; a value that is not a string, or a string that does not parse as an ISO 8601 instant, is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The clock may be set to any instant, including one earlier than its current reading, and the move is unconditional; the two transitions described below are one-way, so a backward move never returns an `EFFECTIVE` notice to `FILED` or a `HOLDOVER` premises to `MARKETED`, and both counters simply report zero when the move triggers nothing. The `current_utc` field is rendered in the same form the default instant is written above — ISO 8601 UTC truncated to whole seconds with a trailing `Z` and **no** fractional digits, so a clock moved to the first instant of 2026-11-01 reads back as `2026-11-01T00:00:00Z`. The three-fractional-digit convention of sections 2.2 and 2.4 governs `api_request_timestamp`, `created_at` and `updated_at` only and does not reach `current_utc`. Advancing the clock is not a passive operation. Every closure notice in `FILED` status whose `effective_closure_date` is on or before the new logical date transitions to `EFFECTIVE`, and every leased premises in `MARKETED` status whose `lease_end_date` falls strictly before the new logical date transitions to `HOLDOVER` with its carrying cost recomputed and the owning case's savings chain refreshed. All calendar-day differences in this document are whole-day counts between calendar dates, computed with no timezone offset.

### 2.9. Cascade Integrity and the Two Conservation Invariants

Several operations here are multi-hop: one request mutates a chain of resources, and every mutated resource has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by one, including resources the client never named. Cascades are applied within the request that triggers them — this system has no deferred or eventual settlement — and a cascade that recomputes a figure recomputes it in full rather than adjusting it incrementally.

The network's deposits are a conserved quantity at the trade-area level. For any market, take the sum of `deposit_balance_cents` across every branch of that market in `ACTIVE`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `CLOSURE_SCHEDULED`, `CLOSED` or `DE_NOVO_RAMPING` status, and add that market's `cumulative_runoff_deposits_cents`. This total must be identical immediately before a closure is executed and immediately after that same closure is unwound. Execution moves the case's projected retained deposits from the closing branch to the receiving branch and moves the case's projected runoff from the closing branch into the market runoff ledger; unwinding moves exactly those two amounts back. Because no rounding takes place in either transfer, the invariant is exact and zero drift is tolerated.

Headcount is conserved in the same spirit but is a distinct quantity. For any case, take the `fte_count` of its closing branch plus the `fte_count` of its receiving branch plus the case's `severed_fte_count`. That total must be identical immediately before a redeployment offer is accepted and immediately after that same offer is rescinded, and the sum of the case's `redeployed_fte_count` and `pending_disposition_count` must likewise be identical across the pair. Acceptance debits the source branch by one and credits the target branch by one; rescission reverses exactly those two moves. Again, zero drift is permitted in either quantity.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Trade Areas, Saturation Tiers and the Branch Register

A distribution analysis begins with the trade area. A `NetworkStrategist` or a `NetworkOpsAdmin` registers a **MarketArea** by supplying its `name` of 1 to 120 characters, a `region_code` matching two uppercase letters, a hyphen and two digits, the resident `population` (at least 1000), the `median_household_income_cents` (at least 0), the total addressable `deposit_market_total_cents` (at least 0), the `competitor_branch_count` of rival-bank branches inside the area (at least 0), the projected five-year `household_growth_pct_bps` (from -2000 through 5000 basis points), and the `digital_penetration_bps` share of area households using digital banking (0 through 10000 basis points). The `name` must be unique within its `region_code`; a repeat is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY`.

At registration the server derives the area's competitive intensity. `competitor_density_index` is the `competitor_branch_count` multiplied by 100000 and divided by the `population`, rounded half up to a whole number — that is, rival branches per hundred thousand residents. It then derives the bank's own footprint: `own_branch_count` is the number of branches in the area whose `status` is one of `ACTIVE`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `CLOSURE_SCHEDULED` or `DE_NOVO_RAMPING`, which is zero for a newly registered area, and `branch_density_per_100k` is the sum of `own_branch_count` and `competitor_branch_count`, multiplied by 100000, divided by the `population` and rounded half up. Branches in `CLOSED` status are excluded from `own_branch_count` entirely.

The density figure classifies the market into a `saturation_tier`. A `branch_density_per_100k` of 30 or more makes the market `OVER_BRANCHED`; from 15 up to but not including 30 it is `SATURATED`; below 15 it is `UNDER_BRANCHED`. Both boundaries are inclusive at their lower edge, so a density of exactly 30 is `OVER_BRANCHED` and a density of exactly 15 is `SATURATED`. The tier is re-derived, together with `own_branch_count` and `branch_density_per_100k`, on every event that moves a branch into or out of those four counted statuses — branch registration, de novo opening, closure execution and closure unwind alike. One subtlety follows directly from the counted set: moving a branch from `ACTIVE` to `UNDER_REVIEW` when a consolidation case opens against it does **not** change `own_branch_count`, because both statuses are counted, so the tier does not move at that point in the lifecycle.

A trade area also carries four ledger and governance counters that begin at zero or false and are maintained by later workflow: `absorbed_deposits_cents` accumulates the deposits transferred into surviving branches of this market by executed closures; `cumulative_runoff_deposits_cents` accumulates the deposits forecast to leave the bank at closure execution and is the market-level runoff ledger; `flagged_case_count` counts the market's cases whose `governance_flag` reads `SAVINGS_WATCH`; `remediation_case_count` counts the market's cases whose `monitoring_posture` reads `REMEDIATION`; and `de_novo_hold` is a boolean that is true exactly while `remediation_case_count` is 1 or more. Reading a single trade area returns all of these, and the trade-area listing may be filtered by `region_code` and by `saturation_tier`.

Branch records are registered against a trade area by the same two roles. The creator supplies the `market_area_id`, a `branch_code` matching two uppercase letters, a hyphen and three digits and unique across the entire network, a `branch_type` of `FULL_SERVICE`, `LIMITED_SERVICE` or `DRIVE_UP`, a `tenure_class` of `LEASED` or `OWNED`, the current `deposit_balance_cents` on book (at least 0), the `monthly_transaction_count` covering teller plus drive-up activity (at least 0), the `digital_adoption_bps` share of the branch's households active in digital channels (0 through 10000 basis points), the `annual_noninterest_expense_cents` (at least 0), the `annual_revenue_cents` attributed to the branch (at least 0), the staffed `fte_count` (at least 0), the installed `atm_count` (at least 0), and the `opened_on` date in year-month-day form, which carries no calendar bound of its own beyond being a valid ISO 8601 date. Two further inputs are optional: `scoring_variant`, which defaults to `TRAILING_BLEND`, and `onboarding_capacity_households_per_day`, which defaults to 40 and must be at least 1. A duplicate `branch_code` is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY`.

A newly registered branch opens in `ACTIVE` status with `performance_band` set to `UNSCORED`, `snapshot_count` at zero, `transferred_in_deposits_cents` at zero, and `absorbing_branch_id`, `pre_close_deposits_cents` and `pre_close_fte_count` all null. Registration increments the owning market's `own_branch_count` by one and re-derives `branch_density_per_100k` and `saturation_tier`. Branch listings may be filtered by `market_area_id`, by `absorbing_branch_id` — which is how an analyst finds every branch a survivor has absorbed — by `status` and by `performance_band`.

### 3.2. Branch Performance Scoring and Candidacy Banding

Consolidation candidacy is evidence-driven, and the evidence is the branch's performance history. Recording a reporting period for a branch is available to a `NetworkStrategist` for any branch and to a `BranchManager` for their own branch only. The submitted period carries a `period_label` of 1 to 20 characters such as a quarter designation, unique within that branch and rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY` if repeated, a `period_end_date` closing the period, the average `deposits_cents` during the period (at least 0), the monthly average `transaction_count` (at least 0), the `digital_active_bps` household share (0 through 10000 basis points), and the annualised `revenue_cents` and `noninterest_expense_cents` (each at least 0). Recording a period is not gated on the branch's `status` at all: a period may be recorded against a branch standing in any status, `CLOSED` included. The resulting **PerformanceSnapshot** is immutable: it records the scored branch in its `branch_id`, is never edited or deleted, and never drops out of the branch's scoring history.

Each snapshot is scored on four factors, all expressed in basis points and all capped at 10000. The **deposit factor** is the period's `deposits_cents` multiplied by 10000 and divided by a network deposit target of 120 million dollars — twelve billion cents, written out as 12,000,000,000 — rounded half up, then capped at 10000. The **transaction factor** is the period's `transaction_count` multiplied by 10000 and divided by a target of 9000 monthly transactions, rounded half up, then capped at 10000. The **digital factor** is simply the period's `digital_active_bps`, capped at 10000. The **profitability factor** runs through an efficiency ratio: if `revenue_cents` for the period is zero the efficiency ratio is taken as 20000 basis points, otherwise it is the `noninterest_expense_cents` multiplied by 10000 and divided by `revenue_cents`, rounded half up. That ratio is then mapped onto the band between an efficiency ceiling of 9000 basis points (a 90 percent efficiency ratio, the worst tolerated) and an efficiency floor of 5000 basis points (a 50 percent ratio, best in class): subtract the efficiency ratio from 9000, multiply by 10000, divide by the 4000-basis-point spread between ceiling and floor, round half up, then clamp the result first to a maximum of 10000 and then to a minimum of 0. A branch running an efficiency ratio at or worse than the ceiling therefore scores zero on profitability, and one at or better than the floor scores the full 10000.

The four factors are blended into `composite_score_bps` at fixed weights — 35 percent deposits, 20 percent transactions, 15 percent digital adoption and 30 percent profitability. Concretely: multiply the deposit factor by 3500, the transaction factor by 2000, the digital factor by 1500 and the profitability factor by 3000, add the four products, divide the sum by 10000, and round half up. That single figure is persisted on the snapshot alongside its own `band`, derived from the thresholds below.

Recording a period does more than store a snapshot. It increments the branch's `snapshot_count` by one, refreshes the branch's `performance_score_bps` and `performance_band`, and increments the branch's `version` by one. The refresh consumes the branch's whole snapshot history, ordered by `period_end_date` descending and, for periods sharing an end date, by `created_at` descending — newest first on both keys — with a final tie between periods sharing both keys broken by the order in which the server wrote them, most recently written first. That same ordering is what the per-branch snapshot listing returns. Which of the ordered snapshots feed the score depends on the branch's `scoring_variant`, and the two variants are genuinely different instruments rather than presentation options. Under `POINT_IN_TIME` the branch's `performance_score_bps` is exactly the `composite_score_bps` of the single newest snapshot; the two prior periods are ignored entirely. Under `TRAILING_BLEND` the three newest snapshots are blended at weights of 5000, 3000 and 2000 basis points respectively, taken as a prefix of that weight list truncated to however many snapshots actually exist and then renormalised: multiply each available snapshot's `composite_score_bps` by its weight, sum those products, divide by the sum of the weights actually used, and round half up. With only two snapshots the weights used are 5000 and 3000 and the divisor is 8000; with one snapshot the blend degenerates to that snapshot's composite. Each snapshot composite entering the blend is **already** rounded from the four-factor step, and the blend rounds exactly once more — no other rounding order is permitted, and for identical snapshot histories the two variants routinely produce different scores and can produce different bands.

The score maps to a `performance_band` on four thresholds: 7000 basis points or above is `CORE`; from 4000 up to but not including 7000 is `STABLE`; from 2000 up to but not including 4000 is `WATCH`; anything below 2000 is `CONSOLIDATION_CANDIDATE`. A branch with no snapshots at all is a fifth case — its `performance_band` reads `UNSCORED` and its `performance_score_bps` is null, because both variants are undefined over an empty history.

Alongside the persisted figures, the system offers an on-demand scoring capability: a `NetworkStrategist`, `ConsolidationApprover`, `NetworkOpsAdmin` or the branch's own `BranchManager` may request a branch's score under a named `scoring_variant` without persisting anything. The variant is named by a `scoring_variant` query parameter; it is optional, and when it is omitted or supplied empty the branch's own stored `scoring_variant` is applied instead, while a value that is neither `POINT_IN_TIME` nor `TRAILING_BLEND` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` at rank 6 — that is, before the branch identifier is even resolved. The response reports `branch_id`, the `scoring_variant` actually applied, the resulting `performance_score_bps` and `performance_band`, and the `contributing_snapshot_count` — one under the point-in-time variant, and up to three under the trailing blend. The branch row is not mutated and its `version` does not move. Requesting this for a branch whose `snapshot_count` is zero is rejected with `ERR_NO_PERFORMANCE_HISTORY`, since there is nothing to blend. Snapshots may be listed per branch, newest period first, or across the whole network — in the default oldest-first order — filtered by `branch_id` and by `band`, where that `band` filter admits only the four scored bands `CORE`, `STABLE`, `WATCH` and `CONSOLIDATION_CANDIDATE`, since `UNSCORED` is a branch-level state no snapshot ever carries and supplying it is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; the branch listing's own `performance_band` filter, by contrast, does admit `UNSCORED`. Any single snapshot may be read on its own.

### 3.3. Opening a Consolidation Case, Pairing the Survivor and Modeling Retention

Only a `NetworkStrategist` may open a **ConsolidationCase**, and doing so is the moment a candidate branch stops being an analytical observation and becomes a live proposal. The creator names the `closing_branch_id`, a `rationale_code` of `LOW_PERFORMANCE`, `LEASE_EXPIRY`, `MARKET_OVERLAP` or `DIGITAL_MIGRATION`, a `planned_closure_date` in year-month-day form, and the `expected_branch_version` described in section 2.6. Two inputs are optional: `retention_model_variant`, which defaults to `DISTANCE_DECAY`, and `receiving_branch_id`. The case is created in `DRAFT` status with `created_by_user_id` stamped from the acting strategist's `X-User-ID`, `decided_by_user_id` null, `returned_count` at zero, `governance_flag` at `NONE`, `impact_tier` at `UNASSESSED`, `notice_required_days` at zero, `monitoring_posture` at `NOT_STARTED`, `last_observed_month_index` at zero, `cumulative_variance_bps` at zero, the closure stamps `pre_close_deposits_cents` and `executed_on` both null, and every savings, forecast and staffing counter at zero.

The closing branch must be in `ACTIVE` status. If it already carries a case in `SUBMITTED`, `APPROVED`, `NOTICE_FILED`, `CLOSURE_EXECUTED` or `POST_CLOSE_MONITORING` status the create is refused with `ERR_BRANCH_HAS_OPEN_CASE`; if it is non-active for any other reason — sitting at `UNDER_REVIEW` under an existing draft case, still `DE_NOVO_RAMPING`, or already `CLOSED` — the refusal is `ERR_BRANCH_NOT_ACTIVE`. When the create succeeds the branch transitions `ACTIVE → UNDER_REVIEW` and its `version` increments by one. That transition carries a supersession cascade: every case on the same closing branch still in `DRAFT` status transitions to `SUPERSEDED`, and for each such superseded case its `ACTIVE` retention forecast transitions to `SUPERSEDED` and its impact assessment in `DRAFT`, `FINALIZED` or `REOPENED` status likewise transitions to `SUPERSEDED`. A branch may therefore be re-proposed freely while nothing has been submitted, and only the newest draft survives.

When `receiving_branch_id` is supplied it must be a legal pairing: it may not equal `closing_branch_id`, and the named branch must belong to the closing branch's trade area. A violation of either condition is `ERR_RECEIVING_BRANCH_INVALID`. When `receiving_branch_id` is omitted, the server selects the survivor itself. The candidate set is every branch of the closing branch's trade area other than the closing branch itself, in `ACTIVE` status, of `FULL_SERVICE` or `LIMITED_SERVICE` format, and holding `deposit_balance_cents` of at least half the closing branch's `deposit_balance_cents` — the absorption floor is 5000 basis points of the closing balance. Branches in `UNDER_REVIEW`, `CLOSURE_SCHEDULED`, `CLOSED` or `DE_NOVO_RAMPING` status are never candidates, and neither is a `DRIVE_UP` format. Candidates are ranked by `deposit_balance_cents` descending, then by `monthly_transaction_count` descending, then by `created_at` ascending, and the first row under that ranking is selected; the final tie-break is the creation timestamp and never the identifier. An empty candidate set fails the create with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_RECEIVING_BRANCH`. That absorption floor filters selector candidates only — it never rejects a receiving branch the strategist named explicitly.

Every case must be backed by a deposit-retention model before it can be submitted. The same strategist creates a **RetentionForecast** naming the `consolidation_case_id`, a `model_variant` of `DISTANCE_DECAY` or `SEGMENT_MIX`, the driving `distance_miles` from the closing branch to the receiving branch, and the three customer-segment shares — `retail_share_bps`, `small_business_share_bps` and `wealth_share_bps` — each from 0 through 10000 basis points and together summing to exactly 10000. A sum other than exactly 10000 is rejected with `ERR_SEGMENT_MIX_INVALID`. Creation copies two figures off the closing branch and freezes them on the forecast: `baseline_deposits_cents` from the branch's `deposit_balance_cents`, and `digital_adoption_bps` from the branch's digital-channel share. The new forecast is written in `ACTIVE` status, and the case's prior `ACTIVE` forecast, if any, transitions to `SUPERSEDED`; only the single `ACTIVE` forecast ever feeds the case's fields. Forecast creation is not gated on the case's status: past the version pin, a forecast may be written against a case standing anywhere in its lifecycle — `DRAFT`, `RETURNED`, `SUBMITTED`, already decided, even already executed or terminal — and it supersedes the prior model and rewrites the case's retention and savings figures wherever the case stands.

The two variants model retention through different mechanics and, for identical inputs, return different results. Under `DISTANCE_DECAY`, distance is consumed by rounding **down** to a whole mile: subtract 1 from the floored mileage, take the larger of that and zero, and multiply by 400 basis points to obtain the decay. Subtract the decay from a base retention of 9200 basis points and take the larger of the result and a retention floor of 5000 basis points. Then add a digital uplift equal to 1500 basis points of the frozen `digital_adoption_bps` — that is, the adoption share multiplied by 1500 and divided by 10000, rounded half up — capped at 800 basis points. The sum of the decayed base and the uplift, capped at a retention ceiling of 9800 basis points, is `retention_bps`. Segment mix is ignored entirely under this variant — but only in the arithmetic: all three share fields remain required inputs of the request whichever variant is named, and their sum is still tested against exactly 10000, so a `DISTANCE_DECAY` forecast whose shares are absent is malformed and one whose shares sum to anything else is `ERR_SEGMENT_MIX_INVALID`.

Under `SEGMENT_MIX`, the segment shares drive a weighted base: multiply `retail_share_bps` by 8200, `small_business_share_bps` by 7000 and `wealth_share_bps` by 9100, add the three products, divide by 10000 and round half up. Distance then applies as a multiplicative penalty and is consumed by rounding **up** to a whole mile: subtract 1 from the ceiled mileage, take the larger of that and zero, multiply by 250 basis points, and cap the penalty at 2500 basis points. Multiply the weighted base by 10000 minus the penalty, divide by 10000, round half up, and cap the result at the same retention ceiling of 9800 basis points to obtain `retention_bps`. Digital adoption is ignored entirely under this variant.

From `retention_bps` the forecast derives the split. `retained_deposits_cents` is `baseline_deposits_cents` multiplied by `retention_bps`, divided by 10000 and rounded half up. `runoff_cents` is `baseline_deposits_cents` minus `retained_deposits_cents` — the exact complement, computed by subtraction and never rounded independently, so that no cent is created or lost in the split. The three figures are mirrored onto the case as `forecast_retention_bps`, `forecast_retained_deposits_cents` and `forecast_runoff_cents`, the case's savings chain is recomputed, and the case's `version` increments by one. Forecasts may be listed per case newest first, filtered by `status`, or across cases filtered by `consolidation_case_id`, `model_variant` and `status`, and any single forecast may be read on its own.

### 3.4. Governance Review: Submission, the Savings Chain and the Decision Branches

A case earns its way to the approver's desk through arithmetic. Submission is available only to the strategist who authored the case, and only from `DRAFT` status; a submission from any other status is `ERR_CASE_STATE_INVALID`, and a submission by any other `NetworkStrategist` is `ERR_CASE_AUTHOR_REQUIRED`. The case must by then have a `receiving_branch_id` on file, and that survivor must be in `ACTIVE` status — a survivor that has drifted out of `ACTIVE` in the meantime fails with `ERR_RECEIVING_BRANCH_NOT_ACTIVE`. The case must also have exactly one retention forecast in `ACTIVE` status; none, and the submission fails with `ERR_RETENTION_FORECAST_MISSING`. Submission carries no request body of its own and moves the case `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`, recomputing `severance_pool_cents` and the entire savings chain on the way through and incrementing the case's `version` by one.

The savings chain is the heart of the business case, and its evaluation order is pinned. The seven steps run exactly once each, top to bottom, with no iteration.

First, the **expense avoided** is the closing branch's `annual_noninterest_expense_cents`, taken as-is. Second, the **revenue attrition** is the deposits the bank expects to lose multiplied by the deposit spread the bank earns on them: take the case's `forecast_runoff_cents`, multiply by 225 basis points — a 2.25 percent net spread — divide by 10000, and round half up. Third, the **property carry** is the sum of `annual_carry_cents` across every premises disposition attached to the case, regardless of that disposition's status. Fourth, `gross_annual_savings_cents` is the expense avoided minus the revenue attrition minus the property carry; it may legitimately be negative. Fifth, `transition_incentive_rate_bps` is selected from a tier table keyed on that gross figure: 12 basis points when gross savings reach 1,000,000 dollars (one hundred million cents, 100,000,000) or more, 18 basis points when gross savings are at least 400,000 dollars (forty million cents, 40,000,000) but below 100,000,000 cents, and 25 basis points when gross savings fall below 40,000,000 cents. Sixth, `transition_incentive_cents` is the case's `forecast_retained_deposits_cents` multiplied by that selected rate, divided by 10000 and rounded half up — the bank pays to keep the deposits it expects to retain, and it pays a *higher* rate when the underlying savings are thinner. Seventh, `net_annual_savings_cents` is `gross_annual_savings_cents` minus `transition_incentive_cents`.

The tier in the fifth step is always selected from the fourth step's **gross** value and is never re-derived from the seventh step's **net** value, even though net is the figure the governance floor is tested against. The dependency looks circular — the incentive depends on the savings tier while the savings depend on the incentive — and it is resolved solely by this pinned order. Rounding occurs at exactly two points in the chain, the second step and the sixth, and nowhere else. The chain is recomputed in full whenever a retention forecast is created, whenever a case is submitted, whenever a staff disposition is created, and on every premises-disposition status change that moves `annual_carry_cents`.

Decisions belong exclusively to a `ConsolidationApprover`, who supplies a `decision` of `APPROVE`, `RETURN` or `REJECT`, the required `expected_case_version`, and an optional `reason_code` — a free-text string that must be non-empty when it is supplied and that the server validates and then discards, so no reason code appears anywhere on the returned case. On this endpoint and on revision the case identifier in the path is resolved before the request body is inspected at all, which is what allows the rank-3 segregation-of-duties test and the rank-4 authorship test to precede rank-6 well-formedness; the visible consequence is that a decision or a revision naming a case that does not exist answers `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` even when its body is also malformed. All three branches share three guards: the decision may be taken only from `SUBMITTED` status, the approver's `X-User-ID` may not equal the case's `created_by_user_id`, and the pinned case version must match. All three branches stamp `decided_by_user_id` from the deciding approver and increment the case's `version` by one. The branches then diverge, and each carries its own additional precondition and its own consequences.

An `APPROVE` decision requires that the case's `net_annual_savings_cents` be at least the governance floor of 150,000 dollars — fifteen million cents, written out as 15,000,000. A case below that floor is rejected with `ERR_SAVINGS_BELOW_FLOOR`, and the boundary is inclusive, so a case whose net savings equal exactly 15,000,000 cents is approvable. On success the case transitions `SUBMITTED → APPROVED` and nothing else moves; the closing branch remains at `UNDER_REVIEW`.

A `RETURN` decision requires that the case's `returned_count` still be below the revision limit of three. A fourth return attempt is rejected with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. On success the case transitions `SUBMITTED → RETURNED` and `returned_count` increments by one. The author may then revise the case, which moves it `RETURNED → DRAFT` and optionally applies a new `planned_closure_date` and a new `receiving_branch_id`; when a receiving branch is supplied on revision it must be in `ACTIVE` status and must not be the closing branch itself, on pain of `ERR_RECEIVING_BRANCH_INVALID`. Revision is likewise restricted to the case's author. The full `DRAFT → SUBMITTED → RETURNED → DRAFT` cycle may therefore run at most three times before the case is permanently stuck in review.

A `REJECT` decision has no extra precondition beyond the three shared guards, but it carries the widest cascade. The case transitions `SUBMITTED → REJECTED`, which is terminal. The closing branch transitions `UNDER_REVIEW → ACTIVE` and its `version` increments — released back into the network, provided the rejected case is the only case on that branch in `SUBMITTED`, `APPROVED` or `NOTICE_FILED` status. The case's `ACTIVE` retention forecast transitions to `SUPERSEDED`. The case's impact assessment, if it stands in `DRAFT`, `FINALIZED` or `REOPENED` status, transitions to `SUPERSEDED`, and as a direct consequence the case's `impact_tier` resets to `UNASSESSED` and its `notice_required_days` resets to zero. `REJECTED`, `SUPERSEDED`, `UNWOUND` and `CLOSED_OUT` are the four terminal statuses a case can reach; no operation moves a case out of any of them.

Cases may be read individually with every computed forecast, savings and monitoring figure exposed, or listed with filters on `closing_branch_id`, `receiving_branch_id`, `status` and `monitoring_posture`.

### 3.5. Community Impact, Regulatory Lead Time and Notice Filing

Closing a branch is a regulated act, and the regulator's first question is who loses access. A `ComplianceOfficer` — and nobody else — records a **CommunityImpactAssessment** against a case, supplying `lmi_tract` (true when the closing branch sits in a low-or-moderate-income census tract), the `distance_to_nearest_remaining_miles` to the nearest remaining bank branch, the `alternative_access_count` of offsite ATMs and partner sites within two miles (0 through 20), the `senior_household_pct_bps` share of trade-area households headed by someone over 65 (0 through 10000 basis points), and `public_transit_access` (true when a fixed-route transit stop serves the branch). At creation the server copies the closing branch's market `saturation_tier` onto the assessment as `market_saturation_tier_at_assessment`, freezing the conditional input of the impact formula at the moment of assessment rather than re-reading it later. Any prior assessment of the case that is not already `SUPERSEDED` transitions to `SUPERSEDED`, so a case has at most one live assessment; a superseded `FINALIZED` assessment also drags the case's `impact_tier` back to `UNASSESSED` and its `notice_required_days` back to zero until a newer assessment is finalised. Superseding a merely `DRAFT` or `REOPENED` assessment leaves those two case fields exactly as they stood, so the case is re-stamped — `updated_at` refreshed and `version` incremented — on an assessment creation **only** when a `FINALIZED` assessment was the one dragged out, while each superseded assessment increments its own `version` either way. Note that this is narrower than the reject cascade of section 3.4, where superseding an assessment in any of the three live statuses resets the case's two regulatory fields. The new assessment itself opens in `DRAFT` status with `finalized_on` null, and no case status gates its creation: an assessment may be recorded against a case standing in any status whatever.

The `impact_score` is built on a 0-to-100 point scale from a base of 20 points, with harms adding and mitigations subtracting. A low-or-moderate-income tract adds 25 points. Distance adds 6 points per mile of `distance_to_nearest_remaining_miles`, rounded half up, capped at 30 points. Senior concentration adds one point per 800 basis points of `senior_household_pct_bps` — that is, one point per 8 percent of senior-headed households — with the division rounded **down** to a whole point and the result capped at 15 points. Market saturation adjusts the score by a tier-dependent amount: an `UNDER_BRANCHED` market adds 10 points because the closure leaves a thin market thinner, an `OVER_BRANCHED` market subtracts 5 points, and a `SATURATED` market adjusts by zero. Against those, two reliefs subtract: alternative access relieves 5 points per access point up to a maximum of 20 points, and public transit access relieves a flat 3 points. Add the base, the low-or-moderate-income component, the distance component, the senior component and the tier adjustment, subtract the access relief and the transit relief, then clamp the total to a minimum of 0 and finally to a maximum of 100; the clamps are applied in that written order.

The score yields an `impact_tier`: 60 points or more is `HIGH`, from 35 up to but not including 60 is `MODERATE`, and below 35 is `LOW`. A separate flag, `requires_public_comment`, is true when either the tier is `HIGH` or `lmi_tract` is true — the two conditions are independent, so a low-scoring closure in a low-or-moderate-income tract still opens a comment period.

The assessment also fixes the regulatory clock. `notice_required_days` starts at 120 calendar days when the `impact_tier` is `HIGH` and at 90 days otherwise. An extension of 30 days applies when the closure is in a low-or-moderate-income tract **and** the distance to the nearest remaining branch is at least 5.0 miles; both conditions must hold, and the distance threshold is inclusive, so exactly 5.0 miles adds the extension while 4.9 miles does not. The base plus any extension is then capped at 180 days. The same figure is written onto the assessment and, on finalisation, mirrored onto the case.

Finalising an assessment is a `ComplianceOfficer` action available from `DRAFT` or from `REOPENED` status; a finalise request against an assessment already `FINALIZED`, or one already `SUPERSEDED`, is rejected with `ERR_ASSESSMENT_STATE_INVALID`. A first finalisation out of `DRAFT` is permitted while the case stands in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `APPROVED` status; a re-finalisation out of `REOPENED` requires the case to be back at `APPROVED`; a case standing outside the set permitted for that from-state is rejected with `ERR_CASE_STATE_INVALID`, and the assessment's own from-state is evaluated first, so an already-`FINALIZED` or already-`SUPERSEDED` assessment reports `ERR_ASSESSMENT_STATE_INVALID` even when the case status would also have failed. Either way `finalized_on` is stamped from the logical clock, the assessment transitions to `FINALIZED`, the case's `impact_tier` and `notice_required_days` are refreshed from the assessment, and the case's `version` increments by one. The `FINALIZED → REOPENED → FINALIZED` cycle is unbounded — an assessment may be reopened and re-finalised as many times as the regulatory dialogue requires. Assessments are listable per case newest first with a `status` filter, listable across cases filtered by `consolidation_case_id`, `impact_tier` and `status`, and individually readable.

With a finalised assessment in hand the `ComplianceOfficer` files a **ClosureNotice**, naming the `consolidation_case_id`, a `notice_type` of `REGULATOR`, `CUSTOMER` or `LOBBY_POSTING`, and the `effective_closure_date` on which the branch may stop serving customers. Filing stamps `filed_on` from the logical clock, copies the case's `notice_required_days` onto the notice as `required_lead_days` — the value the filing was actually validated against — computes `lead_days` as the whole calendar days from `filed_on` to `effective_closure_date` counting neither endpoint, sets `buffer_end_date` to the `effective_closure_date` plus a 30-day regulatory cooling buffer, and opens the notice in `FILED` status.

Four guards apply to a filing, all of them phrased in terms of the case's regulatory posture. The case's live, non-superseded assessment must exist and be `FINALIZED`, or the filing fails with `ERR_IMPACT_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FINALIZED`. The whole calendar days from the current logical date to the `effective_closure_date` must be at least the case's `notice_required_days`, or the filing fails with `ERR_NOTICE_PERIOD_INSUFFICIENT`. The case's `planned_closure_date` must not fall earlier than the `effective_closure_date`, or the filing fails with `ERR_PLANNED_DATE_BEFORE_EFFECTIVE`. And the new notice must not collide with another regulatory notice already occupying the same trade area's notice window — a test the server reaches before the planned-date test, following the ranks of section 2.5 rather than the order these four guards are narrated in.

That last guard is an interval test, and it is run only when the notice being filed is itself of type `REGULATOR`; a `CUSTOMER` or `LOBBY_POSTING` filing never tests the window and can therefore never raise this failure, however crowded the market's window already is. Each regulatory notice occupies the market from its `filed_on` through its `buffer_end_date` — the `effective_closure_date` plus the 30 buffer days — and both ends of that interval are **closed**. The candidate set is every other notice of type `REGULATOR` in `FILED` or `EFFECTIVE` status whose case's closing branch sits in the same trade area; withdrawn notices are ignored entirely. Two intervals collide when the new interval's start falls on or before the existing interval's end and the existing interval's start falls on or before the new interval's end. Because both intervals are closed, touching endpoints collide: a new filing whose `filed_on` equals an existing notice's `buffer_end_date` **is** a conflict. A collision is rejected with `ERR_NOTICE_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.

A `REGULATOR` filing is the transition that schedules the closure. It moves the case `APPROVED → NOTICE_FILED` and increments the case's `version`, and it moves the closing branch `UNDER_REVIEW → CLOSURE_SCHEDULED` with that branch's `version` incremented. `CUSTOMER` and `LOBBY_POSTING` notices are recorded for the file and move no state machine. Notices are listable per case newest first with `notice_type` and `status` filters, listable across cases with `consolidation_case_id`, `notice_type` and `status` filters, and individually readable including their lead days and buffered window end.

Withdrawal reverses a filing. A `ComplianceOfficer` withdraws a notice in `FILED` or `EFFECTIVE` status by supplying a `withdrawn_reason_code` of `REGULATOR_OBJECTION`, `PLAN_REVISION` or `COMMUNITY_PROTEST`; the field is null while the notice has not been withdrawn. A withdrawal aimed at a notice already `WITHDRAWN` is rejected with `ERR_NOTICE_NOT_OPEN`. The notice transitions to `WITHDRAWN`, releasing its market window for other filings. When the withdrawn notice is the case's `REGULATOR` notice and the case stands at `NOTICE_FILED`, the withdrawal cascades: the case transitions `NOTICE_FILED → APPROVED` with its `version` incremented, the closing branch transitions `CLOSURE_SCHEDULED → UNDER_REVIEW` with its `version` incremented, the case's impact assessment transitions `FINALIZED → REOPENED`, and every transition campaign of the case in `PLANNED` or `LAUNCHED` status transitions to `CANCELLED`, releasing its wave interval from the receiving branch's calendar. Withdrawal is thus the single lever that walks an in-flight closure all the way back to an approved-but-unscheduled plan.

### 3.6. Workforce Transition: Notification, Redeployment and Severance

Every employee of a closing branch gets an individual **StaffDisposition** record, created by an `HRPartner` against the case funding it. The creator supplies the `consolidation_case_id`, an opaque `employee_ref` of 1 to 40 characters that must be unique within the case (a repeat is `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY`), the `role_title` held at the closing branch (a non-empty string with no upper length bound), the completed `tenure_months` of service (at least 0), the `annual_salary_cents` of base pay (at least 0), and a `proposed_disposition` of either `REDEPLOYMENT` or `SEVERANCE`. A redeployment must name a `target_branch_id`, and that target may not be the closing branch itself; a severance must omit it. Supplying a redeployment without a target, naming the source branch as the target, or attaching a target to a severance are all rejected with `ERR_DISPOSITION_TARGET_INVALID`, and the same check applies again when a redeployment offer is accepted. The server copies `source_branch_id` from the case's `closing_branch_id`, opens the record with `disposition_state` at `PROPOSED` and `notification_state` at `PENDING`, increments the case's `pending_disposition_count` by one, recomputes the case's `severance_pool_cents` and savings chain, and increments the case's `version`.

Severance economics are computed per employee at creation. `service_years` is the `tenure_months` divided by 12 and rounded **down** to a whole year. `severance_weeks` is twice the service years, raised to a floor of 4 weeks and then capped at 26 weeks — the floor is applied before the cap, so an employee with under two years of service still receives 4 weeks and an employee with 20 years receives 26 rather than 40. `weekly_pay_cents` is the `annual_salary_cents` divided by 52 and rounded half up to whole cents. `severance_amount_cents` is the `severance_weeks` multiplied by that `weekly_pay_cents`, plus a flat outplacement stipend of 1,200 dollars (120,000 cents). Redeployment records carry `severance_weeks` of 0, `weekly_pay_cents` of 0 and `severance_amount_cents` of 0.

The case's `severance_pool_cents` is the sum of `severance_amount_cents` over the case's dispositions whose `proposed_disposition` is `SEVERANCE` and whose `disposition_state` is `PROPOSED` or `SEVERED`. Redeployment records contribute nothing, and so do records whose `disposition_state` has reached `OFFER_ACCEPTED` or `COMPLETED`. The rounding order is pinned: round each employee's weekly pay to whole cents **first**, multiply by that employee's weeks, then sum across employees. Summing annual salaries first and dividing the aggregate by 52 is explicitly not the rule and produces a different pool; the round-then-sum result is the one the system must reproduce.

A disposition carries **two independent state axes**, and this is the structural point of the record. The `disposition_state` axis tracks the employment outcome through `PROPOSED`, `OFFER_ACCEPTED`, `SEVERED` and `COMPLETED`, with `SEVERED` and `COMPLETED` terminal. The `notification_state` axis tracks the statutory WARN notification through `PENDING` and `NOTIFIED`. The two axes never move each other. Recording notification transitions `notification_state` from `PENDING → NOTIFIED` and stamps `notified_on` from the logical clock while leaving `disposition_state` exactly where it stood — notification is legal whether the outcome axis reads `PROPOSED` or `OFFER_ACCEPTED`. Notifying an already-notified record is rejected with `ERR_DISPOSITION_STATE_INVALID`, the same code that guards a wrong from-state on either axis.

All four `HRPartner` transition operations — notify, accept, rescind and sever — require the funding case to stand in `APPROVED` or `NOTICE_FILED` status, and a case standing anywhere else is rejected with `ERR_CASE_STATE_INVALID`; workforce moves are legal once the closure is authorised and remain legal while its notice is on file, but not before approval and not after the closure has been executed. Creating a disposition is deliberately not gated this way: a disposition may be proposed against a case in any status. On all four transitions the record's own two-axis from-state is tested before the case status, so a record on the wrong axis reports `ERR_DISPOSITION_STATE_INVALID` even when the case status would also have failed.

Accepting a redeployment offer requires that the record's `proposed_disposition` be `REDEPLOYMENT`, that its `notification_state` already read `NOTIFIED`, and that its `target_branch_id` name a branch in `ACTIVE` status — a target that has drifted out of `ACTIVE` yields `ERR_RECEIVING_BRANCH_NOT_ACTIVE`. On success the `disposition_state` moves `PROPOSED → OFFER_ACCEPTED`, the source branch's `fte_count` decreases by one and the target branch's `fte_count` increases by one with both branch `version` values incremented, the case's `redeployed_fte_count` increases by one and its `pending_disposition_count` decreases by one with the case `version` incremented, and the disposition's own `version` increments.

Rescinding an accepted offer is the exact inverse. The `HRPartner` supplies a `rescind_reason_code`, which is a required non-empty string that the server validates and then discards rather than persisting anywhere on the record; the `disposition_state` moves `OFFER_ACCEPTED → PROPOSED`, the source branch's `fte_count` increases by one and the target branch's `fte_count` decreases by one — restoring the pre-acceptance counts exactly — the case's `redeployed_fte_count` decreases by one and its `pending_disposition_count` increases by one, and every touched resource's `version` increments. The network headcount invariant of section 2.9 must hold across the accept-then-rescind pair with zero drift.

Severing an employee requires that the record's `proposed_disposition` be `SEVERANCE`, that its `notification_state` read `NOTIFIED`, and that the statutory waiting period have run: the current logical date must be at least 60 whole days after `notified_on`. An earlier attempt is rejected with `ERR_WARN_PERIOD_NOT_ELAPSED`. On success `severed_on` is stamped, the `disposition_state` moves `PROPOSED → SEVERED`, the source branch's `fte_count` decreases by one with that branch's `version` incremented, the case's `severed_fte_count` increases by one and its `pending_disposition_count` decreases by one, and the case and disposition `version` values increment. Note the asymmetry with acceptance: a severance removes a head from the network permanently, and the case's `severed_fte_count` is the third term of the headcount invariant precisely so that the removal is accounted for rather than lost.

The fourth outcome state is reached by the closure itself rather than by any `HRPartner` action: when a closure is executed, every disposition of the case sitting at `OFFER_ACCEPTED` transitions to `COMPLETED` with its `version` incremented, and the case's `redeployed_fte_count` does not change, because `COMPLETED` records are counted alongside `OFFER_ACCEPTED` records in that total. Dispositions may be read individually with both axes exposed, or listed with filters on `consolidation_case_id`, `source_branch_id`, `target_branch_id`, `disposition_state` and `notification_state`.

### 3.7. Premises Disposition and ATM Retention

Every closure must have somewhere for the building to go, and a `RealEstateManager` opens a **PropertyDisposition** for it. The creator names the `branch_id` of the premises and the `consolidation_case_id` that triggered the disposition, declares the fate of the cash fleet with an `atm_disposition` of `RETAIN_OFFSITE`, `RELOCATE` or `DECOMMISSION`, and supplies both the `atm_monthly_cost_cents` of keeping the fleet in place and the `atm_monthly_surcharge_revenue_cents` of surcharge and interchange income it generates, each of them an integer of at least 0. At most one disposition may exist for a given branch-and-case pair; a second is `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY`. The record opens in `PENDING` status and the server copies `tenure_class` from the branch, which selects the fork the rest of the record follows.

The tenure fork dictates which commercial terms are mandatory. A `LEASED` premises must supply `monthly_rent_cents`, the `lease_end_date` on which the lease expires, and the landlord's `buyout_offer_cents` ask. An `OWNED` premises must supply the `book_value_cents` carrying value and the `list_price_cents` asking price. All four of those monetary terms are optional integers of at least 0, and a term belonging to the other fork is neither required nor rejected — it is stored exactly as supplied, so a `LEASED` premises that also carries a `book_value_cents` keeps that figure on the record without it entering any calculation. A missing term on the applicable fork is rejected with `ERR_PROPERTY_TERMS_INCOMPLETE`, which is the lowest-ranked guard on the endpoint: a second disposition for the same branch and case reports `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY` even when its commercial terms are incomplete as well.

The ATM decision is adjudicated by the server rather than accepted at face value. The fleet's annual margin is twelve times the difference between `atm_monthly_surcharge_revenue_cents` and `atm_monthly_cost_cents`, and `atm_breakeven_met` is true when that annual margin reaches a retention margin floor of 6,000 dollars — 600,000 cents. The threshold is inclusive, so an annual margin of exactly 600,000 cents clears it. `atm_decision_final` then equals the requested `atm_disposition` in every case except one: a requested `RETAIN_OFFSITE` whose fleet fails breakeven is downgraded to `DECOMMISSION`. A `RELOCATE` or `DECOMMISSION` request is never altered, and a `RETAIN_OFFSITE` request that clears the floor is honoured. Once the premises reaches a contract, `atm_decision_final` is locked and is never re-adjudicated.

Carrying cost is the figure the savings chain consumes, and it moves with the premises status. The base carry is zero once the premises is `DISPOSED`; for a `LEASED` premises in any other status it is twelve times the `monthly_rent_cents`; for an `OWNED` premises in any other status it is a flat 18,000 dollars a year (1,800,000 cents) of taxes, insurance and maintenance. A holdover premium applies only in `HOLDOVER` status and adds 50 percent of the base carry — the base multiplied by 5000 basis points, divided by 10000 and rounded half up. A marketing charge of 24,000 dollars a year (2,400,000 cents) applies while the premises is in `MARKETED`, `HOLDOVER`, `ASSIGNMENT_PENDING` or `SALE_PENDING` status. `annual_carry_cents` is the base plus the holdover premium plus the marketing charge, and it is recomputed at creation and on every status change, each recomputation forcing a refresh of the owning case's `gross_annual_savings_cents`, `transition_incentive_rate_bps`, `transition_incentive_cents` and `net_annual_savings_cents`.

The premises status machine is a diamond that forks on tenure and rejoins at disposal. A premises leaves `PENDING` only as a cascade of closure execution: when the branch it belongs to becomes `CLOSED`, the disposition transitions `PENDING → MARKETED`, stamps `marketing_started_on` from the logical clock, and picks up the marketing charge. From `MARKETED`, a `LEASED` premises whose `lease_end_date` falls strictly before the logical date when the clock is advanced transitions `MARKETED → HOLDOVER`, picking up the holdover premium and forcing the case savings refresh; an `OWNED` premises never enters `HOLDOVER`.

Contracting is the fork. A `RealEstateManager` records a `counterparty_ref` — a required non-empty string with no upper length bound — together with an `agreed_amount_cents` and a `selling_costs_cents`, both required integers of at least 0, and the destination depends on tenure: a `LEASED` premises transitions `MARKETED → ASSIGNMENT_PENDING`, or `HOLDOVER → ASSIGNMENT_PENDING` if the lease has already run out, recording an assignment of the lease to the counterparty; an `OWNED` premises transitions `MARKETED → SALE_PENDING`, recording a sale. Contracting requires the owning case to stand in `NOTICE_FILED`, `CLOSURE_EXECUTED` or `POST_CLOSE_MONITORING` status, and a from-state or tenure-fork mismatch — attempting a sale contract on a leased premises, or contracting a premises still `PENDING` — is rejected with `ERR_PROPERTY_STATE_INVALID`. A case standing outside those three statuses is rejected with `ERR_CASE_STATE_INVALID`, and the premises from-state is tested first, so a premises still `PENDING` under a `DRAFT` case reports the premises failure.

Settlement is the join. It carries no request body and, unlike contracting, no case-status gate of its own — the premises from-state is its only precondition, and a premises in any status other than `ASSIGNMENT_PENDING` or `SALE_PENDING` is rejected with `ERR_PROPERTY_STATE_INVALID`. Both forks converge on `DISPOSED`, stamp `settled_on` from the logical clock, drop `annual_carry_cents` to zero and refresh the case savings chain — but they compute different money. On the lease fork, settling from `ASSIGNMENT_PENDING`, `net_disposition_cents` is the `agreed_amount_cents` minus the `buyout_offer_cents` the landlord demanded minus the `selling_costs_cents` of brokerage and legal work. On the owned fork, settling from `SALE_PENDING`, `net_disposition_cents` is the `agreed_amount_cents` minus the `selling_costs_cents` minus the `book_value_cents` carried on the books. Both are exact integer arithmetic with no rounding, and both are signed: a negative figure is the exit cost the bank absorbs, which is the normal outcome of buying one's way out of a long lease. The `list_price_cents` recorded for an owned premises is the asking price on file and does not enter the settlement arithmetic — the `agreed_amount_cents` does.

One further transition runs backwards. When an executed closure is unwound, every disposition of the closing branch sitting at `MARKETED` reverts `MARKETED → PENDING`, `marketing_started_on` is cleared, and `annual_carry_cents` is recomputed without the marketing charge. Dispositions may be read individually with their carry cost and ATM breakeven outcome, or listed with filters on `branch_id`, `consolidation_case_id`, `status` and `atm_disposition`.

### 3.8. Customer Transition Waves, Closure Execution and Unwind

Customers do not migrate instantly, and the receiving branch cannot absorb them all at once. A `ComplianceOfficer` plans a **CustomerTransitionCampaign** against a case by supplying the `consolidation_case_id`, the `wave_start_date`, the `wave_end_date`, the `inbound_household_count` of households to be onboarded during the wave (at least 1), and a `channel_mix` of `LETTER_ONLY`, `LETTER_EMAIL` or `FULL_OMNI` describing how those households are contacted. The server copies `receiving_branch_id` from the case — the receiving branch is the resource whose calendar the wave occupies — and opens the campaign in `PLANNED` status.

The wave interval is **half-open**: the `wave_start_date` is included and the `wave_end_date` is excluded. `wave_days` is accordingly the whole calendar days from `wave_start_date` to `wave_end_date`, and `daily_onboarding_load` is the `inbound_household_count` divided by `wave_days` and rounded half up — the per-day burden charged against the receiving branch. At creation the `wave_end_date` must fall strictly after the `wave_start_date`, or the plan is rejected with `ERR_CAMPAIGN_LEAD_TIME_INSUFFICIENT`.

Capacity is tested day by day and in aggregate. The candidate set is every campaign on the same receiving branch in `PLANNED` or `LAUNCHED` status; `COMPLETED` and `CANCELLED` campaigns release their interval and are not counted. Two half-open intervals overlap when the first starts strictly before the second ends and the second starts strictly before the first ends, so touching endpoints do **not** collide — a wave ending on the fifteenth and another starting on the fifteenth are independent. For every calendar day inside the new wave's interval, the load on that day is the new campaign's `daily_onboarding_load` plus the `daily_onboarding_load` of every candidate campaign whose half-open interval contains that day. If the highest such daily load exceeds the receiving branch's `onboarding_capacity_households_per_day`, the plan is rejected with `ERR_ABSORPTION_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. The test is a strict exceedance, so a peak day landing exactly on capacity is allowed. Two campaigns may share a receiving branch and may even overlap in time, provided their summed daily load never exceeds capacity on any single day.

Launching a wave is what makes it real, and it is gated on both the case and the calendar. The case must stand at `NOTICE_FILED`; the campaign must stand at `PLANNED`, and a launch against any other campaign status is `ERR_CAMPAIGN_STATE_INVALID`. The current logical date must fall strictly before the `wave_start_date` — a wave cannot be launched after it was due to begin — and the `wave_start_date` must fall at least 14 days after the `filed_on` date of the case's regulatory notice — meaning the newest `REGULATOR` notice of that case still standing in `FILED` or `EFFECTIVE` status, newest by `created_at` — so that customers receive their statutory notice before onboarding begins. Either dating failure is `ERR_CAMPAIGN_LEAD_TIME_INSUFFICIENT`. Should the case carry no open regulatory notice at all, the launch is rejected with `ERR_CASE_STATE_INVALID`, and that test sits between the two dating tests: after the wave-start-date test and before the 14-day lead test. On success the campaign transitions `PLANNED → LAUNCHED` and `launched_on` is stamped. Campaigns are readable individually with their computed daily load, and listable with filters on `consolidation_case_id`, `receiving_branch_id` and `status`.

**Closure execution** is the pivot of the entire system, and it is available only to a `ComplianceOfficer` on a case standing at `NOTICE_FILED`. Six preconditions guard it, and each has its own diagnosis. The case's regulatory notice must still be open — in `FILED` or `EFFECTIVE` status — or the request fails with `ERR_NOTICE_NOT_OPEN`. The current logical date must have reached the notice's `effective_closure_date`, or the request fails with `ERR_NOTICE_PERIOD_NOT_ELAPSED`. The receiving branch must be in `ACTIVE` status, or `ERR_RECEIVING_BRANCH_NOT_ACTIVE`. The case must have at least one campaign in `LAUNCHED` status, or `ERR_TRANSITION_CAMPAIGN_NOT_LAUNCHED`. The case's `pending_disposition_count` must be exactly zero — every employee's fate settled — or `ERR_STAFF_DISPOSITIONS_PENDING`. And at least one premises disposition must exist for the closing branch, or `ERR_PROPERTY_DISPOSITION_MISSING`.

Execution then runs a four-hop cascade in a single request. The case transitions `NOTICE_FILED → CLOSURE_EXECUTED`, stamping `executed_on` from the logical clock and `pre_close_deposits_cents` from the closing branch's current `deposit_balance_cents` — that stamp is the baseline every later runoff observation is measured against. In the **first hop** the closing branch transitions `CLOSURE_SCHEDULED → CLOSED`: its own `pre_close_deposits_cents` is stamped from its `deposit_balance_cents` and that balance is then set to zero, its `pre_close_fte_count` is stamped from its `fte_count` and that count is then set to zero, its `absorbing_branch_id` is set to the case's `receiving_branch_id`, and its `version` increments. In the same movement the receiving branch's `deposit_balance_cents` and `transferred_in_deposits_cents` each increase by the case's `forecast_retained_deposits_cents` with that branch's `version` incremented, while the trade area's `absorbed_deposits_cents` increases by the same retained amount and its `cumulative_runoff_deposits_cents` increases by the case's `forecast_runoff_cents`. Every disposition of the case at `OFFER_ACCEPTED` transitions to `COMPLETED`, and every campaign of the case at `LAUNCHED` transitions to `COMPLETED`.

The **second hop** follows from the branch now being `CLOSED`: each premises disposition of the closing branch sitting at `PENDING` transitions to `MARKETED` and stamps `marketing_started_on`. The **third hop** follows from that: because the premises has entered `MARKETED`, its `annual_carry_cents` is recomputed with the 24,000-dollar annual marketing charge now included, which forces recomputation of the case's `gross_annual_savings_cents`, `transition_incentive_rate_bps`, `transition_incentive_cents` and `net_annual_savings_cents`. The **fourth hop** follows from that: if the recomputed `net_annual_savings_cents` has fallen below the fifteen-million-cent governance floor, the case's `governance_flag` is set to `SAVINGS_WATCH` and the trade area's `flagged_case_count` increases by one. This is the intended consequence of the design — a case that cleared the floor at approval can slip beneath it at execution once marketing carry lands, and the flag records that the closure went ahead on economics the approver never saw. The case's `version` increments once for the whole request, and every other resource the cascade touched increments its own.

**Unwinding** an executed closure is a `ComplianceOfficer` rescission and is deliberately narrow. The case must stand at `CLOSURE_EXECUTED`, the case's `last_observed_month_index` must still be zero — once a single runoff observation has landed the closure is no longer reversible — and the current logical date must be no more than 30 whole days after `executed_on`. Either window failure is `ERR_UNWIND_WINDOW_CLOSED`, and the zero-observation test is applied before the calendar test. The requester supplies a `rescission_reason_code`, which is a required non-empty string that the server validates and then discards rather than persisting anywhere; as on the decision endpoint the case identifier is resolved before that body field is read, so an unwind naming a case that does not exist answers `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` rather than the 400.

Unwinding reverses every movement execution made. The case transitions `CLOSURE_EXECUTED → UNWOUND`, which is terminal. The closing branch transitions `CLOSED → ACTIVE`: its `deposit_balance_cents` is restored from `pre_close_deposits_cents` and that stamp is cleared to null, its `fte_count` is restored verbatim from `pre_close_fte_count` and that stamp is cleared to null, its `absorbing_branch_id` is cleared to null, and its `version` increments. The receiving branch's `deposit_balance_cents` and `transferred_in_deposits_cents` each decrease by the case's `forecast_retained_deposits_cents`, and the trade area's `absorbed_deposits_cents` decreases by the same retained amount while its `cumulative_runoff_deposits_cents` decreases by the case's `forecast_runoff_cents`. The case's regulatory notice transitions to `WITHDRAWN`. Every premises disposition of the closing branch at `MARKETED` reverts to `PENDING` with `marketing_started_on` cleared and `annual_carry_cents` recomputed without the marketing charge. And if the case's `governance_flag` reads `SAVINGS_WATCH` it resets to `NONE` while the trade area's `flagged_case_count` decreases by one. The reversal reaches exactly that far and no further. The notice is moved to `WITHDRAWN` in place, its `withdrawn_reason_code` left null and none of the withdrawal cascade of section 3.5 running — the case is not returned to `APPROVED`, the impact assessment stays exactly as it stood, and no transition campaign is cancelled. The staff dispositions and campaigns that execution advanced to `COMPLETED` stay `COMPLETED`. And the case keeps its own `executed_on` and `pre_close_deposits_cents` stamps as the historical record of the closure that was rescinded; only the closing branch's twin stamps are cleared. When the dust settles, the trade area's deposit total described in section 2.9 must read exactly what it read before execution.

### 3.9. Post-Closure Runoff Surveillance, Market Remediation and De Novo Growth

A closure is not finished when the doors shut; it is finished when the deposits the forecast promised to keep have actually stayed. Monthly surveillance is recorded as a **DepositObservation**, posted by a `NetworkOpsAdmin` or a `NetworkStrategist`. Each observation declares a `subject_type` of `CONSOLIDATION_CASE` or `DE_NOVO_PLAN`, a `month_index` from 1 through 12 counting months elapsed since closure execution or since a de novo opening, and the `observed_deposits_cents` actually on book for the tracked population (an integer of at least 0). Exactly one foreign key must accompany the subject type: `consolidation_case_id` for a consolidation case and `de_novo_plan_id` for a de novo plan, with the other **absent**. Both keys are declared as optional strings, so a non-string value where an identifier belongs — a number or a boolean — is the rank-6 `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than a subject mismatch, and a key present with an explicit `null` counts as absent. Both are also resolved as body foreign keys at rank 8, far ahead of the subject-coherence test at rank 36, so a key that is present but names no stored record answers `ERR_REFERENCED_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) — and that includes the *wrong* key of an incoherent request, whose unresolvable identifier is reported as the 404 rather than as the coherence 422. Supplying the wrong key, supplying both, supplying neither, or naming a subject that is not in a trackable state — a case must be in `CLOSURE_EXECUTED` or `POST_CLOSE_MONITORING` status, a plan in `OPENED`, `RAMP_TRACKING` or `RAMP_REMEDIATION` status — is rejected with `ERR_OBSERVATION_SUBJECT_MISMATCH`. Observations must also arrive in strictly increasing month order: the `month_index` must be strictly greater than the subject's highest recorded month, or the post is rejected with `ERR_OBSERVATION_OUT_OF_SEQUENCE`. Each observation stamps `observed_on` from the logical clock and, being an immutable reading, carries neither `updated_at` nor `version`.

For a consolidation case, the forecast the observation is measured against is a **runoff curve** that distributes the case's projected runoff across twelve months in declining monthly shares of 2500, 2000, 1500, 1000, 800, 600, 500, 400, 300, 200, 100 and 100 basis points — a curve that front-loads attrition, since a quarter of the expected runoff departs in the first month and the twelve shares together account for the whole 10000 basis points. For an observation at month index k, take the cumulative weight as the sum of the first k shares. `forecast_deposits_cents` is then the case's stamped `pre_close_deposits_cents` minus the case's `forecast_runoff_cents` multiplied by that cumulative weight, divided by 10000 and rounded half up. `variance_bps` is the difference between `observed_deposits_cents` and that forecast, multiplied by 10000, divided by the forecast and rounded half up — a signed figure where a negative value means deposits came in worse than modelled. Should `forecast_deposits_cents` work out to exactly zero for the month, the division is not attempted and `variance_bps` is recorded as 0, which in turn leaves `breach` false however far the observation sits from that zero forecast. The observation's `breach` flag is true when `variance_bps` is at or below -300 basis points; the threshold is inclusive, so exactly -300 basis points breaches while -299 does not.

Recording an observation against a case updates the case's `last_observed_month_index` to the observation's month and its `cumulative_variance_bps` to the observation's variance. Only the highest-indexed observation drives posture; earlier observations are never re-read. The first observation also transitions the case `CLOSURE_EXECUTED → POST_CLOSE_MONITORING` and increments its `version`. The case's `monitoring_posture` — an axis entirely independent of the case's `status`, running `NOT_STARTED`, `ON_TRACK`, `REMEDIATION` and `RESOLVED` — is then re-derived from the newest variance in a **first hop**: the posture becomes `REMEDIATION` if the observation breached; otherwise, if the posture was already `REMEDIATION` and the new `variance_bps` has recovered to at least -150 basis points, it becomes `RESOLVED`; otherwise it becomes `ON_TRACK`.

That posture change propagates outward, and this chain is where contraction constrains growth. In a **second hop**, when the posture enters `REMEDIATION` in this request the trade area's `remediation_case_count` increases by one, and when it leaves `REMEDIATION` for `RESOLVED` the count decreases by one. In a **third hop**, the trade area's `de_novo_hold` is re-derived within the same request as true whenever `remediation_case_count` is 1 or more and false otherwise. In a **fourth hop**, when `de_novo_hold` flips from false to true, every de novo site plan of that market standing at `APPROVED` transitions to `HELD`; when it flips from true to false, every plan standing at `HELD` returns to `APPROVED`. Each plan touched increments its own `version`. The third hop is unconditional: every observation against a case re-stamps the trade area — refreshing its `updated_at` and incrementing its `version` exactly once — even when neither `remediation_case_count` nor `de_novo_hold` ends up changing value, whereas an observation against a de novo plan never touches the trade area at all.

A monitored case is closed out by a `NetworkOpsAdmin` once surveillance is complete. The case's `last_observed_month_index` must have reached the full twelve-month monitoring horizon and its `monitoring_posture` must read `ON_TRACK` or `RESOLVED`; a case short of twelve months, or still in `REMEDIATION`, is rejected with `ERR_MONITORING_INCOMPLETE`. On success the case transitions `POST_CLOSE_MONITORING → CLOSED_OUT`, which is terminal, and its `version` increments. Observations are readable individually with their variance against forecast, and listable with filters on `subject_type`, `consolidation_case_id`, `de_novo_plan_id` and `breach`. That listing carries no ordering of its own: it follows the default `created_at`-ascending order of section 2.3, which for any single subject coincides with month order because months must arrive strictly increasing, but which across several subjects simply interleaves their months in the order the observations were written.

Growth runs on the same rails. A `NetworkStrategist` records a **DeNovoSitePlan** by naming the `market_area_id` under consideration, a `site_label` of 1 to 80 characters, the `proposed_branch_code` to be minted for the future branch (matching the same two-letters-hyphen-three-digits pattern, though its uniqueness against existing branch codes is **not** tested at plan creation — that test waits until the site is opened), the `target_year1_deposits_cents` that serve as the ramp curve's terminal value (at least 1), and the `capex_budget_cents` build-out budget (at least 0). The plan opens in `SITE_IDENTIFIED` status with `ramp_status` at `UNTRACKED`, `ramp_month_index` at zero, `ramp_attainment_bps` at zero, and `opened_branch_id` null.

`site_score` is computed at creation from the trade area as it stands at that moment, on a 0-to-100 point scale, in four factors each individually capped at 100 points. That cap is the only clamp anywhere in the calculation: no factor is floored at zero and neither is the blended result. Three of the four factors are non-negative by construction, but `household_growth_pct_bps` admits values down to -2000, and a market projecting shrinking households therefore contributes a *negative* growth factor and can drag `site_score` itself below zero, where it is recorded as the negative number it computes to and simply fails the siting minimum. The **growth factor** is the market's `household_growth_pct_bps` divided by 5 and rounded half up — one point per 5 basis points of projected household growth, so 1.00 percent growth earns 20 points. The **income factor** is the market's `median_household_income_cents` divided by 120,000 and rounded half up — one point per 1,200 dollars of median household income. The **opportunity factor** starts from the deposit dollars available per competing branch: divide the market's `deposit_market_total_cents` by one more than its `competitor_branch_count`, then express that as a percentage of a 150-million-dollar per-branch deposit target (fifteen billion cents, 15,000,000,000) by multiplying by 100, dividing by the target and rounding half up. The **competition factor** is the market's `competitor_density_index` multiplied by 4 and rounded half up, then capped at 100 — and it enters the blend *inverted*, as 100 minus the factor, because dense competition is a penalty. The four are blended at fixed weights of 3000, 2500, 2500 and 2000 basis points respectively: multiply growth by 3000, income by 2500, opportunity by 2500 and the inverted competition figure by 2000, sum the products, divide by 10000 and round half up. Each factor is rounded to whole points **first**, and the weighted blend rounds exactly once at the end.

A `ConsolidationApprover` decides an identified site with a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `REJECT` and an optional `reason_code` — a free-text string that must be non-empty when it is supplied and that the server validates and then discards, exactly as on the case decision, so no reason code appears anywhere on the returned plan; the decision is available only from `SITE_IDENTIFIED` status, and any other status is `ERR_PLAN_STATE_INVALID`. A `REJECT` moves the plan to `REJECTED`, which is terminal, and carries no further precondition. An `APPROVE` carries three: the plan's `site_score` must be at least the siting minimum of 55 points, or `ERR_SITE_SCORE_BELOW_MINIMUM`; the market's `saturation_tier` must not be `OVER_BRANCHED`, or `ERR_MARKET_OVER_BRANCHED`; and the market's `de_novo_hold` must be false, or `ERR_MARKET_DE_NOVO_HOLD`. On success the plan transitions `SITE_IDENTIFIED → APPROVED`. Both branches increment the plan's `version`.

Opening the site is the strategist's move, and it mints a branch. The plan must be `APPROVED`, the `proposed_branch_code` must still be unused by any branch (otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY`), and the market's `de_novo_hold` must still be false (otherwise `ERR_MARKET_DE_NOVO_HOLD`) — the code-uniqueness test is the earlier of those two, as the ranks of section 2.5 fix. The opener supplies the new branch's `branch_type` and `tenure_class`, drawn from the same two enumerations branch registration declares, and may supply an `onboarding_capacity_households_per_day`, which must be at least 1 and falls back to 40 when it is omitted. The server mints a branch in the plan's trade area with `branch_code` set to the `proposed_branch_code`, `deposit_balance_cents` of zero, `fte_count` of zero, `atm_count` of 1, `performance_band` of `UNSCORED` alongside a null `performance_score_bps`, `snapshot_count` and `transferred_in_deposits_cents` at zero, `monthly_transaction_count`, `digital_adoption_bps`, `annual_revenue_cents` and `annual_noninterest_expense_cents` all at zero, a `scoring_variant` of `TRAILING_BLEND`, its own `opened_on` stamped from the logical clock, and `absorbing_branch_id`, `pre_close_deposits_cents` and `pre_close_fte_count` all null; that branch is created directly in `DE_NOVO_RAMPING` status and never passes through `ACTIVE`; the plan's `opened_branch_id` is set to that branch and `opened_on` is stamped from the logical clock. The market's `own_branch_count` increases by one with `branch_density_per_100k` and `saturation_tier` recomputed — a de novo opening is one of the events that can push a market into a higher saturation tier. The plan itself transitions `APPROVED → OPENED` and immediately into `RAMP_TRACKING`, with `ramp_month_index` at zero and `ramp_status` set to `ON_TRACK`.

Ramp surveillance uses the same observation record with a `subject_type` of `DE_NOVO_PLAN` and a quadratic target curve. For an observation at month index k, the cumulative target is the plan's `target_year1_deposits_cents` multiplied by k, multiplied by k again, divided by 144 and rounded half up — a curve that starts slowly and reaches exactly the year-one target at month 12. That cumulative target is the observation's `forecast_deposits_cents`. The plan's `ramp_attainment_bps` is the `observed_deposits_cents` multiplied by 10000, divided by that cumulative target and rounded half up, and the observation's `variance_bps` is that attainment minus 10000, so an attainment exactly on curve records a variance of zero. The cumulative target is itself rounded to a whole cent, and for a very small `target_year1_deposits_cents` an early month can round to a target of exactly zero; in that case the division is not attempted and `ramp_attainment_bps` is recorded as 0, which lands the observation in `AT_RISK` on the tests below. The observation's `breach` flag is true when attainment falls below the at-risk threshold of 7000 basis points.

`ramp_status` is then derived in a fixed order of tests: `MET` when the month index is 12 and attainment is at least 9000 basis points; otherwise `AT_RISK` when attainment is below 7000 basis points; otherwise `ON_TRACK` when attainment is 10000 basis points or more; otherwise `BEHIND`. The plan's status follows: an observation against an `OPENED` plan moves it to `RAMP_TRACKING`; an attainment below 7000 basis points after refresh moves a `RAMP_TRACKING` plan to `RAMP_REMEDIATION` with `ramp_status` reading `AT_RISK`; a recovery to 7000 basis points or above moves a `RAMP_REMEDIATION` plan back to `RAMP_TRACKING`; and a month-12 observation attaining at least 9000 basis points moves a `RAMP_TRACKING` plan to `RAMP_MET`, which is terminal, sets `ramp_status` to `MET`, and graduates the branch itself from `DE_NOVO_RAMPING → ACTIVE` with that branch's `version` incremented. Each observation refreshes the plan's `ramp_month_index` and increments the plan's `version`. Plans are readable individually with their ramp attainment, and listable with filters on `market_area_id`, `opened_branch_id`, `status` and `ramp_status`.
