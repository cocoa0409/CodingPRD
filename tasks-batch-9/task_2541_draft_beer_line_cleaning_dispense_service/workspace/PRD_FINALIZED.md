# Draft Line Service Ops API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A draft systems service company lives and dies by two things nobody outside the trade ever sees: whether the caustic and acid cycles really alternated on every line, and whether the gas blend pushing beer up a forty-foot run actually matches the carbonation the brewer intended. Today that evidence lives on carbon-copy service tickets in a van. When a venue calls to complain that its flagship lager pours foam, nobody can say when the line was last circulated, at what solution strength, for how long, or at what temperature — and when a brewer asks why it was invoiced for a quality programme, nobody can reconstruct which lines were billable and which failed their compliance grade. This document specifies the service platform that replaces the ticket book.

The platform, mounted under the `/draft-service` namespace, holds the whole operating picture: the venue **service account** with the pre-open window in which a crew may be on the floor, the **contract** that fixes cleaning frequency and line count, the **draft lines** with their run geometry, the **chemical lots** drawn down cycle by cycle, the **route board** of visits, the caustic/acid **cleaning cycles** with their computed dosing and grade, the **gas balance proposals**, the **faucet, coupler and FOB inspections** with parts consumption, the **pour-quality complaints** with their yield-loss arithmetic, and the **glycol loops** whose bath health gates whether chemistry may be circulated at all. Money flows through the same records: a provisional charge frozen when the technician closes out field work, a settled charge computed again when the venue signs, and a brewer-funded waterfall that draws on a capped quality programme.

Two design commitments run through every requirement below. First, **every derived figure is reproducible**: dosing, contact-time tiers, equilibrium pressure, restriction, health scores, charges and yield loss are all computed from pinned formulas with pinned rounding, so two readers of the same records arrive at the same number to the cent and to the decimal place. Second, **the crew that performs the work never signs it off**: verification belongs to the venue, waiver belongs to the compliance office, and the auditor who escalates a complaint may never be the auditor who closes it. Where those two commitments collide with convenience, the specification favours auditability.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint — reads, writes, and the test-driving clock alike — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID` identifying the caller and `X-User-Role` naming the capacity in which they are acting. If either header is absent or empty, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the five role names below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If the headers are well formed but the named role is not one of the roles authorised for the endpoint being called (see the authorisation column of the endpoint table in §4), the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The five roles, spelled exactly as they must appear in `X-User-Role`, are:

- **`DispatchCoordinator`** — the back-office router. Owns venue registration, contracts, the line register, technician records, chemical and parts inventory, brewer programmes, and the route board.
- **`ServiceTechnician`** — the field crew. Starts and closes jobs, logs cleaning cycles, proposes and applies gas balance figures, inspects dispense hardware, and services glycol loops.
- **`VenueManager`** — the venue-side signatory. Signs or disputes service verification, files and reopens pour-quality complaints, and posts venue cooler readings.
- **`QualityAuditor`** — the compliance office. Suspends, reactivates and terminates accounts, quarantines chemistry, voids cycle records, rejects balance proposals, waives missing signatures, and runs complaint diagnosis and closure.
- **`BrewerRep`** — the brewer's field-quality representative. Funds and closes brewer quality programmes and reads the records those programmes pay for; never touches operational records.

Identity semantics differ per role, and the difference is what makes ownership checks meaningful. When `X-User-Role` is `ServiceTechnician`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Technician`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Technician` compare the resource's technician reference to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a technician may only start, close out or post child records against a visit whose `technician_id` equals their own `X-User-ID`, and may only apply a balance proposal whose `proposed_by_technician_id` equals their own `X-User-ID`; a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_ASSIGNED` (HTTP 403). That assignment check guards six operations: `POST /draft-service/service-visits/{visit_id}/start`, `POST /draft-service/service-visits/{visit_id}/complete-work`, `POST /draft-service/cleaning-cycles`, `POST /draft-service/balance-records`, `POST /draft-service/component-inspections`, and `POST /draft-service/glycol-loops/{loop_id}/maintenance`, and it also governs the apply branch of `POST /draft-service/balance-records/{record_id}/decision`.

When `X-User-Role` is `VenueManager`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `ServiceAccount`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `ServiceAccount` compare the resource's account reference to `X-User-ID`. The account reached depends on the endpoint: the visit's `account_id` when recording verification, the complaint's `account_id` when reopening, the `account_id` supplied in the body when filing a complaint, and the owning account of the `GlycolLoop` named by `glycol_loop_id` when posting a cooler reading. A venue manager acting outside its own account is rejected with `ERR_ACCOUNT_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403), which guards exactly four operations: `POST /draft-service/service-visits/{visit_id}/verification`, `POST /draft-service/quality-complaints`, `POST /draft-service/quality-complaints/{complaint_id}/reopen`, and `POST /draft-service/cooler-readings`.

When `X-User-Role` is `DispatchCoordinator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server never compares it to any entity's `id`. The same holds for `BrewerRep`. When `X-User-Role` is `QualityAuditor`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity, but the server does record it where a workflow demands accountability: it is stored as `escalated_by_user_id` when an auditor escalates a complaint and as `closed_by_user_id` when an auditor resolves one. Those two recorded identifiers drive the platform's segregation-of-duties rule: the `X-User-ID` closing a complaint as resolved must not equal the complaint's `escalated_by_user_id`, and an attempt to do so is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403) on `POST /draft-service/quality-complaints/{complaint_id}/closure`. Technician and venue-manager identifiers are similarly captured on the records they create — `logged_by_technician_id` on a cleaning cycle, `proposed_by_technician_id` on a balance proposal, `inspected_by_technician_id` on a component inspection, and `recorded_by_user_id` on a cooler reading — and a cooler reading additionally derives its `source` from the acting role.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: its default is 10, its minimum accepted value is 1, and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: its default is 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) rather than being silently clamped.

Unless a subsection below says otherwise, every list endpoint returns its rows sorted by `created_at` in ascending order, so the oldest record appears first and paging through a collection is stable. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the supplied filters before paging and `has_more` indicates whether rows remain beyond the current page. Single-resource reads carry no pagination fields in `meta`.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and the Version Counter

Every stored record is addressed by an `id` that the server mints as a UUID. Identifiers carry no type prefix and are never supplied by the client: a request that attempts to set `id` is a malformed request. Every record also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording the instant the row was created, taken from the logical clock described in §2.5.

Most resources are mutable and therefore additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp refreshed on every mutation that changes the row, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on each mutation of that row. This applies to `ServiceAccount`, `ServiceContract`, `BeerLine`, `Technician`, `ChemicalLot`, `PartCatalogItem`, `BrewerProgram`, `ServiceVisit`, `CleaningCycle`, `BalanceRecord`, `QualityComplaint` and `GlycolLoop`. Two record types are immutable event logs and are the exception: `ComponentInspection` and `CoolerReading` carry only `id` and `created_at` — both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from their payloads entirely, and neither has an update endpoint.

Because `version` participates in the concurrency guards of §2.7, the rules for when it moves are stated explicitly wherever a workflow moves it. Two of them are worth flagging up front because they are easy to miss: drafting a contract and activating a contract each increment the owning `ServiceAccount`'s `version`, and creating a balance proposal increments the target `BeerLine`'s `version` even though the proposal is a separate record.

### 2.5. The Logical Service Clock

Nothing in this system reads real wall-clock time. The server maintains a **logical clock**, and every "now" the specification refers to — a cycle's `logged_at`, a visit's `work_completed_at`, a signature's `signed_at`, a complaint's `reported_at` and `resolved_at`, a reading's `reading_at`, a loop's `last_serviced_at`, and every `created_at` and `updated_at` — is read from it. Before the clock has ever been set, it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; it must never fall back to the host's real time.

Any of the five roles may advance or rewind the clock by supplying a `current_utc` instant to the clock endpoint. Setting the clock is not a passive act: it re-derives every time-dependent projection in the system before the response is built. Specifically, each `BeerLine` has its `days_overdue` and `compliance_flag` recomputed, and each `GlycolLoop` has its `health_score` and `status` recomputed with the fresh service-interval penalty. The clock endpoint returns an empty `data` object.

### 2.6. Numeric Precision and Rounding

Unless a rule below names a different rule, every rounding in this specification is **round half up** — a value exactly on the midpoint rounds away from zero — and every rounding is applied at the point the formula names it, never deferred.

All money is stored and returned as integer cents; fractional cents are never stored anywhere in the system. Where a monetary intermediate needs rounding, the formula names whether it rounds half up or truncates downward toward zero, and the two are not interchangeable: the per-line cleaning amount rounds half up, whereas the waiver withholding and each brewer funding share truncate downward so that the rounding cent always favours the payer rather than the platform.

Non-monetary precision is fixed per field. Solution and concentrate volumes in the cleaning-cycle arithmetic are held to three decimal places for line holdup volume, whole liters for the solution batch, two decimal places for prescribed concentrate, and three decimal places for achieved active strength; chemical lot volumes are three decimal places. Pressures are two decimal places, gas-blend percentages one decimal place, glycol health scores and their component penalties two decimal places each, yield-loss volumes two decimal places, and averaged cooler temperature two decimal places. Elapsed-day counts are always whole days obtained by discarding any fractional day (that is, rounding down), and each such count has its own floor or cap stated where it is used.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Account and Line Writes

Two write operations sit on top of records that other writers legitimately mutate, and both are guarded by an optimistic-concurrency check against the parent record's `version`.

Drafting a service contract requires the request body to carry `expected_account_version`, a non-optional field holding the client's last observed `version` of the `ServiceAccount` named by `account_id`. If that value does not equal the account's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ACCOUNT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The guard exists because a second contract on the same account is not inherently invalid — activating a new contract legitimately supersedes the account's prior active one — so the stale version is the only signal that the coordinator drafted its commercial terms against an account picture that no longer holds.

Creating a gas balance proposal requires the request body to carry `expected_line_version`, a non-optional field holding the client's last observed `version` of the `BeerLine` named by `line_id`. If that value does not equal the line's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_LINE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Again, a new proposal legitimately supersedes the line's currently applied record, so the create itself is valid product behaviour; a stale version says the technician computed the proposal from restriction and applied-pressure figures the line no longer carries. Note the self-reinforcing consequence: because creating a proposal itself increments the line's `version`, a second create that reuses the version read before the first create is stale by construction.

No other endpoint in this system takes a version field. `POST /draft-service/contracts` and `POST /draft-service/balance-records` are the only two, and any version-like field submitted to any other endpoint is an unknown body field.

### 2.8. The Screening Ladder: Order of Rejection

When one request violates several rules at once, exactly one error is returned, and which one is not a matter of implementation convenience. Checks are evaluated in a strict order and the **first** violated check short-circuits the request; nothing after it runs, and no side effect is applied. The ladder, from first to last, is:

1. **Authentication.** Both headers present and non-empty and `X-User-Role` one of the five declared roles, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. **Authorisation.** The acting role appears among the endpoint's authorised roles, else `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
3. **Path resolution.** Every identifier embedded in the URL resolves to a stored row, checked left to right, with the per-parameter error mapping given in §2.9.
4. **Request shape.** No unknown body fields; every non-optional field of the endpoint present; types correct; enum values members of their declared sets; integers inside their declared ranges; datetimes valid ISO 8601 UTC; `limit` between 1 and 100 and `offset` at least 0; `access_window_end_minute` strictly greater than `access_window_start_minute`; `scheduled_end_minute` strictly greater than `scheduled_start_minute`; `signer_name`, `dispute_reason`, `void_reason`, `decision_note`, `waiver_note`, `reopen_reason` and `end_note` non-empty wherever they are required; and `measured_waste_liters` at least 0. Any breach is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
5. **Body reference resolution.** Every foreign key supplied in the body resolves to a stored row, checked in the order the fields are listed for that endpoint, with the mapping in §2.9.
6. **Venue ownership scope**, then **technician assignment scope**, then **segregation of duties** — the three authorisation checks of §2.1, in that relative order.
7. **Optimistic concurrency** — `expected_account_version` and `expected_line_version` as described in §2.7.
8. **Own-state preconditions** — whether the targeted record's own lifecycle state permits the operation.
9. **Cross-entity state preconditions** — whether the governing account, contract, technician, line, lot or programme is in a state that permits the operation.
10. **Referential coherence** — whether the referenced rows belong together.
11. **Temporal and scheduling rules** — date windows, lead times, access-window containment, and slot and route overlaps.
12. **Safety rules** — expiry, certification, and the glycol alarm gate.
13. **Business constraints** — alternation, uniqueness, aggregate caps, round and reopen limits, evidence-at-completion, tolerance bands.
14. **Fiscal and stock sufficiency** — remaining chemical volume and parts stock on hand.

Three orderings inside that ladder are deliberately counter-intuitive and are called out again where the workflows describe them. A request aimed at a non-active account that is *also* outside the venue's access window reports `ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_ACTIVE`, never `ERR_OUTSIDE_ACCESS_WINDOW`, because the account-state check sits above the access-window check. An inspection naming a part whose component type is wrong *and* whose stock is exhausted reports `ERR_PART_TYPE_MISMATCH`, never `ERR_PART_OUT_OF_STOCK`. And a caustic cycle logged by an uncertified technician that *also* breaks the caustic/acid alternation reports `ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_CAUSTIC_CERTIFIED`, never `ERR_CHEMICAL_PARITY_VIOLATION`.

### 2.9. Resolving References in Paths and Bodies

No entity in this system is nested inside another's URL; every collection is a peer under `/draft-service`, and ownership is expressed entirely through reference fields in request bodies and through filtered collection reads. Identifiers nevertheless appear in paths for single-resource reads and for action endpoints, and each one must resolve to a stored row before the endpoint's own logic runs. Where a path carries more than one identifier they are resolved left to right, and the first unresolvable one determines the error. All of these rejections carry HTTP 404 and each has its own code: `{account_id}` yields `ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND`, `{contract_id}` yields `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`, `{line_id}` yields `ERR_LINE_NOT_FOUND`, `{technician_id}` yields `ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_FOUND`, `{lot_id}` yields `ERR_CHEMICAL_LOT_NOT_FOUND`, `{part_id}` yields `ERR_PART_NOT_FOUND`, `{program_id}` yields `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`, `{visit_id}` yields `ERR_VISIT_NOT_FOUND`, `{cycle_id}` yields `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND`, `{record_id}` yields `ERR_BALANCE_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`, `{inspection_id}` yields `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_FOUND`, `{complaint_id}` yields `ERR_COMPLAINT_NOT_FOUND`, `{loop_id}` yields `ERR_GLYCOL_LOOP_NOT_FOUND`, and `{reading_id}` yields `ERR_READING_NOT_FOUND`. Written without their braces, the fourteen path identifiers this platform uses are `account_id`, `contract_id`, `line_id`, `technician_id`, `lot_id`, `part_id`, `program_id`, `visit_id`, `cycle_id`, `record_id`, `inspection_id`, `complaint_id`, `loop_id` and `reading_id`, and each must equal the `id` of a stored row of the corresponding type.

References supplied in a request body are resolved the same way, after the shape check of the ladder and in the order the fields are listed for the endpoint, using the same code per target type: `account_id` yields `ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND`, `line_id` yields `ERR_LINE_NOT_FOUND`, `technician_id` yields `ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_FOUND`, `chemical_lot_id` yields `ERR_CHEMICAL_LOT_NOT_FOUND`, `part_sku_id` yields `ERR_PART_NOT_FOUND`, `brewer_program_id` yields `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`, `visit_id` yields `ERR_VISIT_NOT_FOUND`, and `glycol_loop_id` yields `ERR_GLYCOL_LOOP_NOT_FOUND`.

Because nothing is nested, every parent-to-child relationship is navigated by filtering a collection read. Accounts may be filtered by `status` and `billing_mode`. Contracts may be filtered by `account_id`, `brewer_program_id`, `status` and `funding_status`. Draft lines may be filtered by `account_id`, `status` and `compliance_flag`. Technicians may be filtered by `status`, `certification_level` and `caustic_certified`. Chemical lots may be filtered by `chemical_type` and `status`. Catalogue parts may be filtered by `component_type` and `brewer_funded_eligible`. Brewer programmes may be filtered by `status` and `funded_scope`. Visits may be filtered by `account_id`, `technician_id`, `rescheduled_from_visit_id`, `work_status`, `verification_status`, `settlement_state` and `scheduled_date`. Cleaning cycles may be filtered by `visit_id`, `line_id`, `chemical_lot_id`, `record_status` and `compliance_result`. Balance records may be filtered by `line_id`, `visit_id`, `status` and `balance_state`. Component inspections may be filtered by `visit_id`, `line_id`, `part_sku_id`, `component_type` and `condition_grade`. Complaints may be filtered by `account_id`, `line_id`, `status` and `symptom`. Glycol loops may be filtered by `account_id` and `status`. Cooler readings may be filtered by `glycol_loop_id` and `source`. Every filter is optional; supplying several narrows the result to rows matching all of them.

### 2.10. Cascade Discipline and Derived Projections

Consistency in this platform is immediate. When an operation's side effects reach into other records, those records are updated inside the same request, before the response is built, and the response reflects the post-cascade world. A caller who suspends an account and then reads its contracts sees them already suspended; a caller who logs a failing cycle and then reads the account sees the raised out-of-service counter and, where the threshold is met, the suspension.

Cascades are also deterministic. Wherever a cascade touches a set of sibling rows, the rows are processed in a pinned order so that side effects that depend on iteration order are reproducible. Contracts belonging to an account are always processed in ascending `created_at` order. Balance records superseded on a line are processed in ascending `created_at` order. Visits abandoned by an account suspension are processed by ascending `scheduled_date`, then ascending `scheduled_start_minute`, then ascending `created_at`.

Every record a cascade actually changes has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by 1, exactly as if it had been mutated directly — with the single exception that the two immutable event types carry neither field. A record that a cascade evaluates but leaves unchanged is not touched: recomputing a derived counter to the same value it already held is not a mutation.

Several fields are pure projections that the server maintains and clients may never write. On an account, `lines_out_of_service_count` is the number of that account's lines currently out of service and `open_complaint_count` is the number of its complaints in an unfinished state, meaning any complaint whose status is `OPEN`, `DIAGNOSING`, `AWAITING_PARTS` or `ESCALATED`; a `RESOLVED` complaint drops out of that count while an `ESCALATED` one does not. On a line, `last_chemical_type`, `last_cleaned_at`, `next_due_at`, `days_overdue`, `compliance_flag`, `applied_psi` and `applied_co2_pct` are all projections replayed from surviving records. On a visit, `serviced_line_count`, `billable_line_count` and every money figure are projections. On a programme, `consumed_cents` and `remaining_cents` are projections of settlement activity. On a loop, `health_score`, `status` and `window_avg_cooler_temp_f` are projections of its readings and its maintenance history. Each projection's exact derivation is given with the workflow that moves it in §3.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Venue Onboarding: Accounts, Contracts and Brewer-Funded Programmes

A new customer enters the system when a `DispatchCoordinator` registers the venue. The registration payload carries the venue's trading name as `venue_name` (non-empty, at most 120 characters) and, crucially, the two minute-of-day bounds of the window in which a crew is allowed on the floor: `access_window_start_minute`, the first minute of the day at which the crew may be on site, which must be between 0 and 1439 inclusive, and `access_window_end_minute`, the last minute by which the crew must be off site, which must be between 1 and 1440 inclusive and strictly greater than the start. Most venues let crews in before opening, so a window of 360 through 660 — six in the morning until eleven — is typical. An optional `glycol_system_present` flag, defaulting to false, declares whether the venue runs a glycol-chilled long-draw system. The account is created in `ACTIVE` with `version` 1, `billing_mode` `DIRECT_BILL`, `lines_out_of_service_count` 0, `open_complaint_count` 0 and `suspended_reason` `NONE`; its `status` may later be `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED` or `TERMINATED`, and `suspended_reason` may later be `NONE`, `LINE_COMPLIANCE_THRESHOLD` or `MANUAL`.

Registration has one side effect that surprises first-time integrators. When `glycol_system_present` is true, the same request also creates exactly one glycol loop for the venue, seeded with `glycol_pct` 35.0, `pump_psi` 12.0, `bath_temp_f` 28.0, `last_serviced_at` equal to the loop's own `created_at`, `health_score` 100.0 and `status` `NORMAL`. That loop is **not** included in the account payload returned by registration; the only way to discover it is to list glycol loops filtered by the owning `account_id`. Registration does not otherwise disturb the account, which is why a freshly registered venue — with or without a loop — is at `version` 1 rather than 2.

Commercial terms live on a separate contract record, which a `DispatchCoordinator` drafts against the account. Its payload names the `account_id`, the contracted `cleaning_frequency_days` (which must be exactly 14, 21 or 28), the `contracted_line_count` the deal covers (at least 1), the `per_line_rate_cents` charged per line per visit before any multiplier, the flat `visit_base_fee_cents` truck-roll fee charged once per visit, an `effective_from` instant before which the contract may not be activated, the `expected_account_version` guard described in §2.7, and optionally a `brewer_program_id` when a brewer underwrites the venue's quality programme. Both money fields are integer cents and neither may be negative. The account must exist and be `ACTIVE`; otherwise the draft is refused. The contract lands in `DRAFT` — its `status` progresses through `DRAFT`, `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED` and `EXPIRED` — and drafting increments the owning account's `version` by 1.

Two computed fields are stamped at draft time. `funding_status` is `NONE` when no `brewer_program_id` was supplied, `BREWER_FUNDED` when the named programme's status is `ACTIVE`, and `SELF_FUNDED` when the named programme is already exhausted or ended. `frequency_multiplier` is a soil-load factor keyed purely on the contracted interval, held to two decimal places: a fortnightly contract at 14 days multiplies by 1.00, a three-weekly contract at 21 days by 1.10, and a four-weekly contract at 28 days by 1.25 — a line left three extra weeks between cleanings carries a quarter more soil and costs a quarter more to circulate.

Activating a contract advances it from `DRAFT` to `ACTIVE`, and a `DispatchCoordinator` drives it by supplying `to_status` on the contract lifecycle operation. Four conditions must hold, evaluated in the ladder order of §2.8: the contract must currently be `DRAFT`, the logical clock must have reached or passed the contract's `effective_from` (otherwise `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_YET_EFFECTIVE`), the owning account must be `ACTIVE` (otherwise `ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_ACTIVE`), and when the contract names a `brewer_program_id`, that programme's `status` must be `ACTIVE` — an exhausted or ended programme cannot back a newly activated contract, which is refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_ACTIVE`. Activation then does three things: any other contract on the same account that was `ACTIVE` is superseded to `EXPIRED`, processed in ascending `created_at` order, so an account has at most one active contract at any instant; the account's `billing_mode` becomes `PROGRAM_BILL` when this contract's `funding_status` is `BREWER_FUNDED` and `DIRECT_BILL` otherwise; and the account's `version` increments by 1 again.

The remaining contract edges are all driven by the same lifecycle operation with a different `to_status`, or by a cascade. Moving an `ACTIVE` contract to `SUSPENDED` requires it to be `ACTIVE` and sets the account's `billing_mode` to `DIRECT_BILL`. Moving a `SUSPENDED` contract back to `ACTIVE` requires it to be `SUSPENDED` and requires the owning account to be `ACTIVE` (otherwise `ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_ACTIVE`), and it restores `billing_mode` to `PROGRAM_BILL` when the contract's `funding_status` is `BREWER_FUNDED` and to `DIRECT_BILL` otherwise. Expiring an `ACTIVE` contract requires it to be `ACTIVE` and sets the account's `billing_mode` to `DIRECT_BILL` unless some other contract on that account is still `ACTIVE` with `funding_status` `BREWER_FUNDED`, in which case the programme billing survives. Expiring a `SUSPENDED` contract requires it to be `SUSPENDED` and has no further effect. A `DRAFT` contract can reach `EXPIRED` only through the account-termination cascade of §3.9, never through a direct request, and that edge likewise carries no further effect.

Brewer money is modelled as its own programme record, opened by a `DispatchCoordinator`. The payload carries the funding `brewer_name` (non-empty, at most 120 characters), the `funded_scope` naming which charge buckets the brewer will pay for — `CLEANING_ONLY`, `CLEANING_AND_PARTS` or `FULL` — the venue's `co_pay_bps` co-pay expressed in basis points between 0 and 10000 inclusive, and the lifetime `funding_cap_cents` ceiling, which must be greater than zero. A programme opens in `ACTIVE` with `consumed_cents` 0 and `remaining_cents` equal to `funding_cap_cents`; its `status` may later be `ACTIVE`, `EXHAUSTED` or `ENDED`. The co-pay is a haircut on the brewer's share rather than on the venue's bill: the programme funds the fraction of each eligible bucket given by 10000 minus `co_pay_bps`, divided by 10000, so a `co_pay_bps` of 2000 means the brewer covers eighty per cent of each eligible bucket and the venue the remaining fifth.

A `BrewerRep` can top a programme up at any time by supplying `additional_cents`, which is added to `funding_cap_cents`, after which `remaining_cents` is recomputed as the cap less `consumed_cents`. When the programme was `EXHAUSTED` and the top-up leaves `remaining_cents` greater than zero, the programme moves from `EXHAUSTED` back to `ACTIVE` — exhaustion is not terminal — and that revival cascades: every contract naming this programme whose status is `DRAFT`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` has its `funding_status` recomputed to `BREWER_FUNDED`, processed in ascending `created_at` order, and each such contract's account has its `billing_mode` recomputed to `PROGRAM_BILL` when that contract is itself `ACTIVE`. Closing a programme is the mirror image: a `BrewerRep` supplies an `end_note` and the programme moves from `ACTIVE` or from `EXHAUSTED` to `ENDED`, whereupon every contract naming it whose status is `DRAFT`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` has its `funding_status` recomputed to `SELF_FUNDED` in ascending `created_at` order and each such contract's account has its `billing_mode` recomputed to `DIRECT_BILL`. `ENDED` is terminal: no further settlement may draw on the programme, and a `DRAFT` contract naming it can no longer be activated. When the programme was already `EXHAUSTED`, its active and suspended contracts are already `SELF_FUNDED`, so in practice only its draft contracts change — but the recomputation is performed over the same set either way.

### 3.2. The Draft Line Register and the Cleaning-Due Projection

Each physical beer line at a venue is registered by a `DispatchCoordinator` as its own record, because almost every rule in this system is per-line rather than per-venue. The payload names the owning `account_id`, the operator-facing `line_number` (at least 1), and the geometry and product the balancing arithmetic later needs: `run_length_feet`, the horizontal run from cooler to faucet, which must be greater than zero and is held to one decimal place; `vertical_rise_feet`, the vertical rise from keg coupler to faucet, which may be zero and is likewise held to one decimal place; `inner_diameter_inches`, the beer tubing bore, which must be exactly 0.1875, 0.25 or 0.375 because those three values select both the per-foot restriction rate and the volume-per-foot constant; `beer_style_group`, the carbonation family of the product on the line, which must be `ALE`, `LAGER`, `NITRO` or `CIDER`; and `average_daily_throughput_liters`, the contracted average daily pour volume, which may be zero, is held to one decimal place, and feeds the provisional yield-loss estimate.

Three conditions gate registration and they are checked in the ladder order of §2.8. The owning account must be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_ACTIVE`. That account must have exactly one contract in `ACTIVE` status, otherwise `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_CONTRACT` — without an active contract there is no cleaning interval to project a due date from and no rate to price a visit with. And the register must stay inside the commercial deal: if the number of lines already registered on the account plus this one would exceed the active contract's `contracted_line_count`, the registration is refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_LINE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. That last rule is an aggregate one — every individual line in the payload is perfectly valid, and only the collective count breaches the contract. Separately, `line_number` must be unique among that account's lines counting **every** status, including lines currently out of service; a collision is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_LINE_NUMBER`.

A newly registered line opens in `IN_SERVICE` — the only other value `status` ever takes is `OUT_OF_SERVICE` — with `compliance_flag` `COMPLIANT`, `last_chemical_type` `NONE`, and `last_cleaned_at`, `next_due_at`, `applied_psi` and `applied_co2_pct` all null and `days_overdue` 0. Registration also recomputes the owning account's `lines_out_of_service_count`, which in practice leaves it unchanged because the new line is in service.

Everything else on a line is a replayed projection, and the replay is worth stating precisely because voiding a cycle record must be able to rewind it. Consider the surviving cycle records on a line — those whose `record_status` is `POSTED`, ignoring every voided one. `last_chemical_type` is the `chemical_type` of the newest such record, where "newest" means ordered by `logged_at` descending and, for ties, `created_at` descending; when no posted record exists, `last_chemical_type` reads `NONE`. `last_cleaned_at` is narrower: it is the `logged_at` of the newest posted record whose `compliance_result` is `PASS`, under the same ordering, and null when the line has never had a passing cycle. From there, `next_due_at` is `last_cleaned_at` advanced by the active contract's `cleaning_frequency_days` in days, and is null whenever `last_cleaned_at` is null. `days_overdue` is the number of whole days that have elapsed from `next_due_at` to the current logical clock, discarding any fractional day and floored at zero, and is 0 whenever `next_due_at` is null.

The `compliance_flag` carries two independent reasons to read `NON_COMPLIANT`. The first is grade-driven: once a posted cycle on the line records a `compliance_result` other than `PASS` and that record has not been voided, the line is non-compliant. The second is calendar-driven: a line is non-compliant once `days_overdue` is greater than the three-day grace allowance, so a line exactly three days past its due date is still `COMPLIANT` and one four days past it is not. Because the grace test is evaluated against the logical clock, moving the clock forward is enough to flip a line's flag, and the clock endpoint therefore re-derives `days_overdue` and `compliance_flag` for every line before it answers.

The gas figures on a line are projections too. `applied_psi` is the `proposed_applied_psi` of the newest balance record on the line whose status is `APPLIED`, ordered by `created_at` descending, and `applied_co2_pct` is the `proposed_co2_pct` of that same record; both read null when the line has no applied record.

A line leaves service in one of two ways, both described where they happen: a cleaning cycle that grades anything other than `PASS` takes the line from `IN_SERVICE` to `OUT_OF_SERVICE` and flips `compliance_flag` to `NON_COMPLIANT` (see §3.4), and a component inspection that grades a part `FAILED` without replacing it takes the line from `IN_SERVICE` to `OUT_OF_SERVICE` while leaving `compliance_flag` untouched (see §3.6). Coming back is a compliance decision, not a field decision: a `QualityAuditor` returns the line from `OUT_OF_SERVICE` to `IN_SERVICE`, and the line's newest posted cycle — again ordered by `logged_at` descending then `created_at` descending — must grade `PASS`, or the line must have no posted cycle at all. A line whose newest posted cycle still grades `FAIL_CONCENTRATION`, `FAIL_CONTACT_TIME` or `FAIL_TEMPERATURE` is refused with `ERR_LINE_STILL_NON_COMPLIANT`. A successful return sets `compliance_flag` back to `COMPLIANT`, recomputes the owning account's `lines_out_of_service_count`, and increments the line's `version` by 1. Note the asymmetry that follows from this: voiding the very cycle record that took a line out of service does **not** bring the line back automatically — the auditor must still return it explicitly.

### 3.3. Building the Route Board: Crews, Bookings and Arrival

Field staff are registered by a `DispatchCoordinator` with a `full_name` (non-empty, at most 120 characters), a `certification_level` from the draft guild's three tiers `APPRENTICE`, `CERTIFIED` and `MASTER`, an optional `caustic_certified` flag defaulting to false that records whether the technician may handle caustic solution at all, and an optional employment `status` defaulting to `ACTIVE`, whose only other value is `INACTIVE`. Registering a technician has no further consequences anywhere in the system; the record exists to be routed against.

Booking a stop is where the calendar gets interesting. A `DispatchCoordinator` supplies the `account_id` of the venue, the `technician_id` of the crew, the `visit_kind` — `ROUTINE_CLEANING`, `EMERGENCY_TROUBLESHOOT` or `GLYCOL_MAINTENANCE` — the `scheduled_date` as a calendar date, and the two minute-of-day bounds `scheduled_start_minute` (between 0 and 1439 inclusive) and `scheduled_end_minute` (between 1 and 1440 inclusive and strictly greater than the start). The booking is screened by nine business rules in a fixed order, and because several of them can fail at once, the order determines which code the coordinator sees.

First come the three state checks. The venue account must be `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_ACTIVE`. The account must have exactly one contract in `ACTIVE` status, else `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_CONTRACT`, since the visit cannot otherwise be priced. The named technician must be `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_TECHNICIAN_INACTIVE`.

Then come the temporal rules. The `scheduled_date` may not fall before the logical clock's current date — no back-dated visits — else `ERR_VISIT_DATE_IN_PAST`. Nor may it fall more than six weeks and three days ahead: the route board extends exactly 45 days beyond the current date and no further, else `ERR_SCHEDULE_LEAD_EXCEEDED`. The slot must sit wholly inside the venue's crew access window, meaning `scheduled_start_minute` is at or after the account's `access_window_start_minute` and `scheduled_end_minute` is at or before the account's `access_window_end_minute`; both bounds are inclusive, so a slot ending exactly at `access_window_end_minute` is allowed, and a breach is `ERR_OUTSIDE_ACCESS_WINDOW`. Because the account-state check sits above this one, a booking that is both outside the window and aimed at a suspended venue reports `ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_ACTIVE`, never the window error.

Next come the two overlap rules, which use deliberately opposite boundary conventions. **Venue slot exclusivity** compares the new slot against every existing visit on the *same* account and the *same* `scheduled_date` whose `work_status` is `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS`; visits already `ABANDONED` or `WORK_COMPLETE` are ignored entirely. The new booking is rejected with `ERR_VENUE_SLOT_CONFLICT` when its start is at or before the existing visit's end **and** its end is at or after the existing visit's start. Both comparisons are non-strict, so two back-to-back slots that merely touch — the new one starting on the exact minute the existing one ends — are a conflict: only one crew at a time works a venue floor. **Technician route feasibility** compares the new slot against every existing visit for the *same* `technician_id` on the *same* `scheduled_date`, again only those `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS`, and adds a half-hour travel allowance to each side. The booking is rejected with `ERR_TECHNICIAN_ROUTE_CONFLICT` when the new slot's start is strictly earlier than the existing visit's end plus 30 minutes **and** the existing visit's start is strictly earlier than the new slot's end plus 30 minutes. Both comparisons here are strict, so a gap of exactly 30 minutes between two jobs is accepted while a gap of 29 minutes is rejected — the opposite convention from the venue rule, on purpose, because a van needs half an hour to cross town but a floor cannot be shared for even a minute.

Last comes the glycol gate. A `GLYCOL_MAINTENANCE` booking is only meaningful at a venue that actually has a chiller loop, so it requires the account's `glycol_system_present` to be true and a glycol loop to exist for that account; otherwise the booking is refused with `ERR_NO_GLYCOL_SYSTEM`. Being the last check in the sequence, this error only ever surfaces on a booking that has already cleared every state and scheduling rule above.

A booked visit carries two independent state dimensions, and that independence is the single most important structural fact about it. The field-work dimension, `work_status`, runs `SCHEDULED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `WORK_COMPLETE` and `ABANDONED`, with the last two terminal. The customer-verification dimension, `verification_status`, runs `UNVERIFIED`, `SIGNED`, `WAIVED` and `DISPUTED`. Neither dimension reads the other except where a rule below says so explicitly. A third field, `settlement_state`, records whether the visit's charges have reached the brewer ledger and takes the values `UNSETTLED`, `SETTLED` and `REVERSED`. A freshly booked visit is `SCHEDULED`, `UNVERIFIED` and `UNSETTLED`, with `abandon_reason` `NONE`, `rescheduled_from_visit_id`, `signer_name`, `signed_at` and `work_completed_at` all null, and every count and money figure at zero.

Plans change, and a `DispatchCoordinator` can move a `SCHEDULED` visit by supplying a fresh `scheduled_date`, `scheduled_start_minute` and `scheduled_end_minute`, plus optionally a different `technician_id`. The original visit must still be `SCHEDULED`, otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_VISIT_NOT_SCHEDULED`, and the named technician — the replacement's if supplied, otherwise the original's — must be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_TECHNICIAN_INACTIVE`. The replacement slot is then re-screened against all five scheduling rules exactly as a fresh booking would be: past date, lead limit, access-window containment, venue slot exclusivity and technician route feasibility. On success the original visit advances `SCHEDULED → ABANDONED` with `abandon_reason` set to `RESCHEDULED`, and a brand-new visit is minted in `SCHEDULED` carrying `rescheduled_from_visit_id` equal to the original visit's `id`, the same `account_id` and the same `visit_kind`, and a `technician_id` that defaults to the original's when the payload omitted it. The newly minted replacement, not the abandoned original, is what the response returns.

On the day, the assigned technician opens the job, advancing `work_status` from `SCHEDULED` to `IN_PROGRESS`. Four conditions apply: the visit must be `SCHEDULED` (else `ERR_VISIT_NOT_SCHEDULED`), the caller's `X-User-ID` must equal the visit's `technician_id` (else `ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_ASSIGNED`), the logical clock's current date must have reached the visit's `scheduled_date` (else `ERR_VISIT_START_TOO_EARLY` — a crew cannot open tomorrow's job today), and the account must still be `ACTIVE` (else `ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_ACTIVE`).

Visits can also die without being worked. A `SCHEDULED` visit advances `SCHEDULED → ABANDONED` either because its account was suspended, in which case `abandon_reason` is `ACCOUNT_SUSPENDED`, or because it was rescheduled, in which case `abandon_reason` is `RESCHEDULED`. An `IN_PROGRESS` visit advances `IN_PROGRESS → ABANDONED` only through an account suspension, with `abandon_reason` `ACCOUNT_SUSPENDED`; this edge exists for the manually suspended venue where a crew is already on the floor. In both abandonment cases the verification dimension is left at `UNVERIFIED` and can never advance afterwards — an abandoned visit is never signed, never waived and never settled.

### 3.4. Chemistry Inventory and the Alternating Cleaning Cycle

Concentrate arrives by the drum and is received into inventory by a `DispatchCoordinator` as a lot. The payload declares the `chemical_type`, which is either `CAUSTIC` or `ACID`; the supplier's `lot_code`, which must be unique across every lot ever received regardless of that lot's current status, a collision being refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_LOT_CODE`; the `concentration_pct_stock` active-ingredient percentage of the undiluted concentrate, between 1.0 and 100.0 inclusive and held to one decimal place; the `liters_initial` volume received, which must be greater than zero and is held to three decimal places; and `expires_on`, the calendar expiry date. A received lot opens `AVAILABLE` with `liters_remaining` equal to `liters_initial`. Thereafter `liters_remaining` is a projection: it is `liters_initial` less the sum of `concentrate_liters_used` across every cycle record still `POSTED` that drew on the lot, rounded to three decimal places, and it never goes negative. A lot's `status` may be `AVAILABLE`, `DEPLETED`, `QUARANTINED` or `EXPIRED`, with `EXPIRED` terminal.

Two of those transitions are automatic and two directions of the remainder belong to the compliance office. Automatically, a lot drops `AVAILABLE → DEPLETED` the moment a draw leaves `liters_remaining` below the minimum usable volume of 0.05 liters — fifty millilitres of concentrate cannot dose anything — and a lot climbs `DEPLETED → AVAILABLE` when voiding a cycle record restores `liters_remaining` to at least 0.05. Manually, a `QualityAuditor` supplies a `to_status` to pull a lot for inspection (`AVAILABLE → QUARANTINED` or `DEPLETED → QUARANTINED`), to release an inspected lot (`QUARANTINED → AVAILABLE`), or to write a lot off (`AVAILABLE → EXPIRED` or `QUARANTINED → EXPIRED`); each of those requires the lot to be in the stated originating status. Releasing a quarantined lot additionally requires `liters_remaining` to be at least 0.05, otherwise the release is refused with `ERR_LOT_RELEASE_BLOCKED`. One consequence is deliberate and easy to trip over: because the automatic revive edge runs only from `DEPLETED`, a drained lot that was pulled into `QUARANTINED` stays quarantined even when a later void restores its volume — the auditor must release it explicitly.

Logging a circulation is the technician's central act. The payload names the `visit_id` the work belongs to, the `line_id` that was circulated, the `chemical_lot_id` the concentrate was drawn from, the `chemical_type` used this cycle, the measured `contact_minutes` of recirculation (a non-negative whole number), the measured `recirculation_temp_f` solution temperature in Fahrenheit held to one decimal place, and the `concentrate_liters_used` actually drawn from the lot, which must be greater than zero and is held to two decimal places. The server stamps `logged_at` from the logical clock and `logged_by_technician_id` from `X-User-ID`, and the record is created with `record_status` `POSTED` and `void_reason` null. Cycles are the most heavily guarded write in the system; the checks below are listed in the exact order they are evaluated, and the first one violated is the one reported:

1. The caller's `X-User-ID` must be the visit's `technician_id`, else `ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_ASSIGNED`.
2. The visit's `work_status` must be `IN_PROGRESS`, else `ERR_VISIT_NOT_IN_PROGRESS`.
3. The line must be `IN_SERVICE`, else `ERR_LINE_OUT_OF_SERVICE`.
4. The lot must be `AVAILABLE`, else `ERR_CHEMICAL_LOT_UNAVAILABLE` — a `DEPLETED`, `QUARANTINED` or `EXPIRED` lot is unusable.
5. The line's owning account must be the visit's account, else `ERR_LINE_ACCOUNT_MISMATCH`; a technician cannot log work on another venue's line from this visit.
6. The body's `chemical_type` must equal the lot's own `chemical_type`, else `ERR_CHEMICAL_TYPE_MISMATCH` — a caustic cycle cannot be logged against an acid lot.
7. The logical clock's current date must not be past the lot's `expires_on`, else `ERR_CHEMICAL_LOT_EXPIRED`; expired chemistry may never touch a line.
8. A `CAUSTIC` cycle requires the technician's `caustic_certified` to be true, else `ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_CAUSTIC_CERTIFIED`.
9. If the account has a glycol loop, that loop's `status` must not be `ALARM`, else `ERR_GLYCOL_ALARM_BLOCK` — chemistry cannot be circulated through a failing chiller bath.
10. The alternation rule must hold, else `ERR_CHEMICAL_PARITY_VIOLATION`: the body's `chemical_type` must differ from the line's `last_chemical_type`, and when `last_chemical_type` reads `NONE` the very first cycle in a line's life must be `CAUSTIC`. Two consecutive caustic cycles, two consecutive acid cycles, and an acid-first virgin line are therefore all rejected.
11. The early-cleaning guard must hold, else `ERR_CLEANING_TOO_EARLY`: when the line's `next_due_at` is not null, the date of `logged_at` must be on or after `next_due_at`'s date less two days, so a crew may pull a line forward by at most two days. A line with no passing cycle yet, and therefore a null `next_due_at`, is exempt — a virgin line can always be cleaned.
12. The lot must hold enough concentrate: `liters_remaining` must be at least `concentrate_liters_used`, else `ERR_CHEMICAL_INSUFFICIENT_VOLUME`.

Two of those orderings are load-bearing. Check 8 sits above check 10, so an uncertified technician logging a caustic cycle that *also* breaks the alternation is told about the certification, never the parity. And check 3 sits above check 5, so a line that is both out of service and belongs to another venue reports the service status.

Once a cycle clears every check, the platform grades it immediately, and the grading is frozen on the record for good. Five figures are derived in a strict chain, each feeding the next:

- **`line_volume_liters`** — the line's holdup volume, obtained by multiplying `run_length_feet` by the volume-per-foot constant selected by the line's bore, then rounding half up to three decimal places. The three bores hold 0.0173 liters per foot at 0.1875 inches, 0.0308 liters per foot at 0.25 inches, and 0.0692 liters per foot at 0.375 inches.
- **`total_solution_liters`** — the batch of made-up solution, in whole liters. Multiply `line_volume_liters` by the flush factor of 3.0 (a proper clean pushes three line volumes of solution), round that product **up** to the next whole liter, and then take whichever is larger, that figure or the four-liter minimum batch. Rounding up rather than to nearest matters: a holdup of 2.214 liters gives a product of 6.642, which becomes 7 liters, not 6.
- **`prescribed_concentrate_liters`** — how much concentrate the technician *should* have drawn: `total_solution_liters` multiplied by the target active percentage for the chemistry, then divided by the lot's `concentration_pct_stock`, rounded half up to two decimal places. The target active strength is 2.0 per cent for `CAUSTIC` and 1.0 per cent for `ACID`.
- **`effective_active_pct`** — what the technician *actually* achieved: `concentrate_liters_used` multiplied by the lot's `concentration_pct_stock`, divided by `total_solution_liters`, rounded half up to three decimal places. Note that the prescription reads the lot's stock strength and so does the achievement, which is why drawing the prescribed volume from a weaker drum yields a weaker bath.
- **`required_contact_minutes`** — the dwell time the achieved strength demands. This tier is selected from the **achieved** strength, never from the prescribed dose: a weaker solution must sit in the line longer. For a `CAUSTIC` cycle, an `effective_active_pct` of at least 2.0 requires 15 minutes, at least 1.5 requires 20 minutes, and anything weaker requires 30 minutes. For an `ACID` cycle, at least 1.0 requires 10 minutes, at least 0.75 requires 15 minutes, and anything weaker requires 25 minutes. Every tier comparison uses the already-rounded three-decimal `effective_active_pct`, never the unrounded intermediate, so an achievement of 1.9995 rounds to 2.000 and therefore earns the 15-minute tier.

The `compliance_result` grade is then assigned by walking three tests in this exact precedence and stopping at the first failure. If `recirculation_temp_f` is below the minimum recirculation temperature for the chemistry — 95.0 degrees Fahrenheit for `CAUSTIC`, 70.0 for `ACID` — the grade is `FAIL_TEMPERATURE`. Otherwise, if `effective_active_pct` is below the minimum effective strength for the chemistry — 1.5 per cent for `CAUSTIC`, 0.75 for `ACID` — the grade is `FAIL_CONCENTRATION`. Otherwise, if `contact_minutes` is below `required_contact_minutes`, the grade is `FAIL_CONTACT_TIME`. Otherwise the grade is `PASS`. A cold caustic bath that is also under-dosed and under-dwelt therefore reports the temperature failure alone.

Posting a cycle then moves a chain of other records. The lot's `liters_remaining` is reduced by `concentrate_liters_used`, held to three decimal places, and if the remainder falls below 0.05 liters the lot advances `AVAILABLE → DEPLETED`. The line's `last_chemical_type` is set to the cycle's `chemical_type` whatever the grade — a failed caustic cycle still counts as caustic for alternation purposes, so the next cycle on that line must be acid. On a `PASS`, the line's `last_cleaned_at` becomes the cycle's `logged_at` and `next_due_at` and `days_overdue` are re-projected accordingly.

On any grade other than `PASS` a three-hop cascade fires. Hop one takes the line `IN_SERVICE → OUT_OF_SERVICE` with `compliance_flag` set to `NON_COMPLIANT`, moves every balance record on that line whose status is `APPLIED` to `SUPERSEDED`, and increments the line's `version` by 1. Hop two recomputes the owning account's `lines_out_of_service_count` and applies the compliance threshold: when twice that count is greater than or equal to the active contract's `contracted_line_count`, the account advances `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` with `suspended_reason` set to `LINE_COMPLIANCE_THRESHOLD`. The doubling is integer arithmetic rather than a fractional ratio, so on a four-line contract two lines out of service suspends the venue while one does not. Hop three is the suspension's own cascade: every visit on that account still `SCHEDULED` advances to `ABANDONED` with `abandon_reason` `ACCOUNT_SUSPENDED`, processed by ascending `scheduled_date`, then ascending `scheduled_start_minute`, then ascending `created_at`, and every contract on that account still `ACTIVE` advances to `SUSPENDED`, processed in ascending `created_at` order. A single mis-dosed caustic circulation can therefore end with a suspended venue and an emptied route board, all inside one request.

Mistakes on the ticket are corrected by voiding, which only a `QualityAuditor` may do, supplying a non-empty `void_reason`. Two conditions apply in order: the record must still be `POSTED`, else `ERR_CYCLE_ALREADY_VOIDED`, and the cycle's visit must not yet have `settlement_state` `SETTLED`, else `ERR_VISIT_ALREADY_SETTLED` — once money has moved, the compliance record behind it is frozen. A void advances the record `POSTED → VOIDED`, stores the `void_reason`, and gives back the chemistry: the lot's `liters_remaining` is increased by `concentrate_liters_used`, held to three decimal places, and a `DEPLETED` lot climbs back to `AVAILABLE` when the restored volume reaches 0.05 liters. The line's `last_chemical_type`, `last_cleaned_at`, `next_due_at`, `days_overdue` and `compliance_flag` are then replayed from the cycles that remain `POSTED`, which can roll `last_cleaned_at` back to an earlier passing cycle or to null, pull `next_due_at` earlier, and push `days_overdue` past the three-day grace inside the very same request. The visit's `serviced_line_count` and `billable_line_count` are recomputed, but `provisional_charge_cents` is deliberately **not** recomputed: it stays frozen at the value it took when field work was closed out.

### 3.5. Balancing Gas Blend and Applied Pressure

Cleaning a line is half the job; pushing beer up it at the right pressure with the right blend is the other half. A technician on an open job proposes a setting by supplying the `visit_id`, the `line_id`, the `measured_temp_f` beer temperature taken at the faucet (held to one decimal place), the `proposed_applied_psi` regulator pressure they intend to set (greater than zero, held to two decimal places), the `proposed_co2_pct` carbon-dioxide share of the blend they intend to use (between 0.0 and 100.0 inclusive, held to one decimal place, with the balance of the blend being nitrogen), and the `expected_line_version` concurrency guard of §2.7. The proposal is screened in this order: the caller must be the visit's assigned technician (`ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_ASSIGNED`), the `expected_line_version` must match the line's current `version` (`ERR_STALE_LINE_VERSION`), the visit must be `IN_PROGRESS` (`ERR_VISIT_NOT_IN_PROGRESS`), the line must belong to the visit's account (`ERR_LINE_ACCOUNT_MISMATCH`), and only then are the two tolerance checks below applied.

Six figures are computed from the line's geometry and the measured temperature, and they are frozen on the record. Three independent selectors drive them — the product's style group, the temperature band, and the tubing bore — and each selector picks its own constant.

**Carbonation target.** A `NITRO` line is not carbonated up at all: its `target_co2_volumes` is the base carbonation of 1.2 volumes. For every other style group, the measured temperature is first placed in a band, with the upper bound of each band inclusive: at or below 38.0 degrees Fahrenheit the band is cold, above that and at or below 42.0 it is mild, and above 42.0 it is warm — so 38.0 is cold, 38.1 is mild, 42.0 is mild and 42.1 is warm. The style group and band together select the target: an `ALE` line targets 2.4 volumes cold, 2.5 mild and 2.6 warm; a `LAGER` line targets 2.6 cold, 2.7 mild and 2.8 warm; a `CIDER` line targets 2.8 cold, 2.9 mild and 3.0 warm.

**Equilibrium pressure.** The `equilibrium_psi` is the pressure needed to hold that carbonation at that temperature. Take `target_co2_volumes`, subtract the base 1.2 volumes, and multiply the difference by 5.5 psi per volume; separately take `measured_temp_f`, subtract the 38.0-degree reference temperature, and multiply that difference by 0.45 psi per degree; add the two products and round the sum half up to two decimal places. Warmer beer therefore needs more pressure, and a line held exactly at the reference temperature contributes nothing from the temperature term.

**Restriction.** The `total_restriction_psi` is what the plumbing itself eats. Multiply `run_length_feet` by the per-foot restriction of the bore — 2.2 psi per foot at 0.1875 inches, 0.85 at 0.25 inches and 0.30 at 0.375 inches — add `vertical_rise_feet` multiplied by 0.5 psi per foot of rise, add the flat faucet loss of 2.0 psi, and round the total half up to two decimal places. Narrow tubing is the dominant term: a fifty-foot run of quarter-inch line eats 42.5 psi before rise or faucet.

**What to apply, and in what blend.** The line must be pushed at whichever demand is larger, so `required_applied_psi` is the greater of `equilibrium_psi` and `total_restriction_psi`. The `required_co2_pct` is 25.0 for a `NITRO` line by fiat. For every other line it is the carbon-dioxide share of the applied pressure that preserves carbonation: divide `equilibrium_psi` by `required_applied_psi`, multiply by 100, round half up to one decimal place, and then — after rounding, never before — clamp the result to be at least 40.0 and at most 100.0, because no practical blend runs leaner than forty per cent carbon dioxide.

**Diagnosis.** The `balance_state` records what the numbers say about the installation. When the absolute difference between `total_restriction_psi` and `equilibrium_psi` is at most the 0.75 psi tolerance, the line is `BALANCED` — a gap of exactly 0.75 psi is balanced and 0.76 psi is not. Otherwise, when restriction exceeds equilibrium the state is `RESTRICTION_HIGH` (too much line for the carbonation, and the pour will be slow), and otherwise it is `PRESSURE_HIGH` (too little line to absorb the pressure the carbonation needs, and the pour will foam).

Only then are the technician's own numbers judged, and in this order. The absolute difference between `proposed_applied_psi` and `required_applied_psi` must be at most 0.5 psi, else the proposal is refused with `ERR_PRESSURE_OUT_OF_BALANCE`. Then, and only once the pressure tolerance has passed, the absolute difference between `proposed_co2_pct` and `required_co2_pct` must be at most 2.0 percentage points, else the proposal is refused with `ERR_GAS_BLEND_MISMATCH`. A surviving proposal is stored in `PROPOSED` with `proposed_by_technician_id` taken from `X-User-ID` and `decision_note` null, and — importantly — it increments the target line's `version` by 1. Each proposal is computed from the line's geometry as it stands at proposal time; records already `SUPERSEDED` or `REJECTED` on the same line feed no figure.

A proposal is decided separately, and the two branches belong to different roles. Either way the record must still be `PROPOSED`, else the decision is refused with `ERR_BALANCE_RECORD_NOT_PROPOSED`, and an optional `decision_note` may be attached. When `decision` is `APPLY`, the caller must be the technician who proposed it — the `X-User-ID` must equal `proposed_by_technician_id`, else `ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_ASSIGNED` — and the line must be `IN_SERVICE`, else `ERR_LINE_OUT_OF_SERVICE`. Applying advances the record `PROPOSED → APPLIED`, moves every other record on the same line that was `APPLIED` to `SUPERSEDED` in ascending `created_at` order, makes the line's `applied_psi` and `applied_co2_pct` resolve to this record's proposed figures, and increments the line's `version` by 1 again. When `decision` is `REJECT`, only a `QualityAuditor` may act; the record advances `PROPOSED → REJECTED`, the `decision_note` is stored, and the line's `applied_psi` is left exactly as it was. `REJECTED` and `SUPERSEDED` are both terminal, and a record can also reach `APPLIED → SUPERSEDED` without any sibling proposal at all — taking the line out of service supersedes whatever was applied to it. Closing out field work, by contrast, decides nothing: proposals still `PROPOSED` when the technician completes the visit stay `PROPOSED`.

### 3.6. Component Inspections and Parts Consumption

The van carries a stock catalogue, maintained by a `DispatchCoordinator`. Each catalogue entry declares its `sku`, which must be unique across the whole catalogue; the `component_type` of dispense hardware it replaces, being `FAUCET`, `COUPLER` or `FOB`; the `unit_price_cents` charged per replaced unit; the `stock_on_hand` count of units currently available on the van; and an optional `brewer_funded_eligible` flag, defaulting to false, which declares whether a brewer quality programme may pick the part up when its funded scope stretches to parts. Both the price and the stock count are non-negative integers.

During an open job, a technician records what they found on each piece of hardware. An inspection names the `visit_id` and the `line_id`, the `component_type` inspected, the `condition_grade` observed — `GOOD`, `WORN` or `FAILED` — and the `action_taken`, which is `NONE`, `CLEANED` or `REPLACED`. A `part_sku_id` accompanies the record when and only when the action was `REPLACED`; it is null otherwise. Inspections are immutable evidence: each carries an `id` and a `created_at` and nothing else in the way of common metadata — there is no `updated_at`, no `version`, and no way to amend one — and the server stamps `inspected_by_technician_id` from `X-User-ID`.

Inspections are screened in this order: the caller must be the visit's assigned technician (`ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_ASSIGNED`), the visit must be `IN_PROGRESS` (`ERR_VISIT_NOT_IN_PROGRESS`), the line must belong to the visit's account (`ERR_LINE_ACCOUNT_MISMATCH`), and then two part checks apply when the action was `REPLACED`. The referenced catalogue part's `component_type` must equal the `component_type` in the payload, else `ERR_PART_TYPE_MISMATCH` — a faucet inspection cannot consume a coupler sku. Only after that does the platform look at stock: the part's `stock_on_hand` must be at least 1, else `ERR_PART_OUT_OF_STOCK`. Because the type check sits above the stock check, a request naming a sku of the wrong component type that is *also* out of stock reports the mismatch and never the shortfall.

A replacement consumes a unit and generates a charge: `part_charge_cents` is stamped from the catalogue part's `unit_price_cents` and the part's `stock_on_hand` is reduced by 1. For any other action, `part_charge_cents` is 0 and no stock moves.

One combination has consequences beyond the record. When a component is graded `FAILED` and the technician did **not** replace it, the line advances `IN_SERVICE → OUT_OF_SERVICE` — a venue cannot keep pouring through a failed faucet. On this path, unlike the failed-cycle path of §3.4, the line's `compliance_flag` is left exactly as it was, because nothing about the line's cleaning record has changed. Everything else about the transition is shared: every balance record on the line that was `APPLIED` moves to `SUPERSEDED`, the line's `version` increments by 1, the owning account's `lines_out_of_service_count` is recomputed, and the compliance threshold is re-evaluated — so an unreplaced failed faucet can suspend the venue, suspend its active contract and abandon its scheduled visits exactly as a failed circulation can.

### 3.7. Closing Out a Visit: Provisional Charge, Signature, Waiver and Dispute

When the crew packs up, the assigned technician closes out field work, advancing `work_status` from `IN_PROGRESS` to `WORK_COMPLETE`. Three conditions apply in order: the caller must be the visit's `technician_id` (`ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_ASSIGNED`), the visit must be `IN_PROGRESS` (`ERR_VISIT_NOT_IN_PROGRESS`), and there must be evidence that work actually happened (`ERR_NO_WORK_RECORDED`). What counts as evidence depends on the `visit_kind`: a `ROUTINE_CLEANING` or `EMERGENCY_TROUBLESHOOT` visit requires at least one cycle record on the visit still `POSTED`, whereas a `GLYCOL_MAINTENANCE` visit requires at least one loop maintenance recorded during the visit, meaning the account's loop carries a `last_serviced_at` at or after the start of the visit's day. Closing out stamps `work_completed_at` from the logical clock, recomputes `serviced_line_count` and `billable_line_count`, and runs the **provisional** variant of the visit charge. It does not touch `settlement_state`, which stays `UNSETTLED`, moves nothing on any brewer ledger, and decides no balance proposal.

The visit charge is computed twice in a visit's life, from the same records but with two different inclusion sets and two different withholding rules. Both variants read the one contract on the visit's account whose status is `ACTIVE`.

The shared groundwork is this. The **per-line amount** is the contract's `per_line_rate_cents` multiplied by its `frequency_multiplier`, rounded half up to whole cents, computed once and then applied identically to every billable line. Rounding at the per-line level rather than at the subtotal level is pinned and the two are not interchangeable: a `per_line_rate_cents` of 4175 at a `frequency_multiplier` of 1.10 gives a per-line amount of 4593 cents, so three lines come to 13779 cents, whereas rounding the aggregate would have given 13778. `serviced_line_count` is the number of distinct lines that have a cycle record still `POSTED` on this visit. The passed-line count is the number of distinct lines whose **newest** posted cycle on this visit — ordered by `logged_at` descending, then `created_at` descending — graded `PASS`. `parts_charge_cents` is the sum of `part_charge_cents` across the visit's inspections whose `action_taken` was `REPLACED`, and is 0 when nothing was replaced. Voided cycles are excluded from both line counts, so voiding the only passing cycle on a line drops that line out of the settled inclusion set entirely.

The **provisional** variant, which runs when field work is closed out, sets `billable_line_count` to `serviced_line_count` — every line the crew touched is billed, whatever its grade, because the signature outcome is not yet known — and applies no waiver withholding at all. The **settled** variant, which runs at signature, at waiver and again on a re-signature after a dispute, sets `billable_line_count` to the passed-line count instead, so a line whose circulation failed its grade is not billed. On the waiver path only, a withholding is subtracted: take `billable_line_count` multiplied by the per-line amount, take ten per cent of that pre-withholding line total (a withholding of 1000 basis points), and truncate downward to whole cents so the rounding cent favours the venue. On every signing path, including the re-signature out of a dispute, the withholding is zero.

From there both variants converge. `cleaning_subtotal_cents` is `billable_line_count` multiplied by the per-line amount, less the waiver withholding. The charge is the contract's `visit_base_fee_cents` plus `cleaning_subtotal_cents` plus `parts_charge_cents`; the base fee is charged once per visit regardless of how many lines were billable, including when no line was billable at all. The provisional run stores the result in `provisional_charge_cents`; the settled run stores it in `settled_charge_cents` and then hands it to the brewer waterfall. To see the gap the two variants open up, consider a three-line visit priced as above where one line graded `FAIL_CONTACT_TIME`: the provisional cleaning subtotal is 13779 cents against a settled cleaning subtotal of 9186.

Verification is a separate dimension that moves on its own triggers and, being cyclic, may move more than once. The venue manager's decision carries a `decision` of either `SIGN` or `DISPUTE`, with `signer_name` and `dispute_reason` supplied as the branch requires. Whichever branch is taken, the platform first checks that the verification dimension permits the decision at all: a `SIGN` requires the visit to be `UNVERIFIED` or `DISPUTED`, a `DISPUTE` requires it to be `SIGNED`, and a waiver requires `UNVERIFIED`. Anything else is refused with `ERR_VERIFICATION_ALREADY_RECORDED`. Every one of these three operations is scoped: the venue branches demand that `X-User-ID` equal the visit's `account_id`, else `ERR_ACCOUNT_SCOPE_VIOLATION`.

A first signature moves `UNVERIFIED → SIGNED`. Field work must already be finished — the `work_status` must be `WORK_COMPLETE`, else `ERR_WORK_NOT_COMPLETE` — and a non-empty `signer_name` must be supplied, else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The `signer_name` is captured, `signed_at` is stamped from the clock, the settled charge variant runs with no withholding, `settlement_state` becomes `SETTLED`, and the brewer waterfall runs.

A dispute moves `SIGNED → DISPUTED` and is a genuine compensating reversal, not a flag. The venue has 168 hours from `signed_at` to lodge it; past that the platform answers `ERR_DISPUTE_WINDOW_CLOSED`. A non-empty `dispute_reason` is required. The brewer programme is credited back the exact `brewer_funded_cents` that this visit posted at settlement, its `consumed_cents` falls by that amount and its `remaining_cents` is recomputed; if that brings `consumed_cents` back below the `funding_cap_cents` and the programme was `EXHAUSTED`, it returns to `ACTIVE`, whereupon every contract naming it recomputes its `funding_status` to `BREWER_FUNDED` and each of those accounts recomputes its `billing_mode` to `PROGRAM_BILL` — the funding cascade of §3.1 run in reverse. The visit's `settlement_state` becomes `REVERSED` and its `settled_charge_cents`, `brewer_funded_cents`, `account_due_cents` and `cleaning_subtotal_cents` are all reset to 0. `signed_at` is deliberately left untouched, so the dispute window stays anchored to the original signature and a disputed visit cannot be re-disputed indefinitely on a rolling clock.

A re-signature moves `DISPUTED → SIGNED` and is where the two-sided cycle earns its keep. The `signer_name` is overwritten, `signed_at` is stamped afresh — restarting the dispute window this time — and the settled variant is re-run with no withholding **against the current compliance picture**, so a cycle voided while the visit sat in dispute changes the amount owed. `settlement_state` returns to `SETTLED` and the whole brewer ledger cascade runs again from scratch.

The waiver is the auditor's escape hatch for a venue that never signs. It moves `UNVERIFIED → WAIVED`, requires `work_status` to be `WORK_COMPLETE` (`ERR_WORK_NOT_COMPLETE`), and may not be exercised until 72 hours have passed since `work_completed_at` — the venue gets three days to sign — else `ERR_WAIVER_TOO_EARLY`. A `waiver_note` accompanies the request. The settled variant runs with the withholding applied, `settlement_state` becomes `SETTLED`, and the same brewer cascade follows. A waived visit's verification dimension never returns to `UNVERIFIED`, and it can be neither signed nor disputed afterwards.

Every settlement ends in the brewer funding waterfall, which splits `settled_charge_cents` between the brewer and the venue. It reads the `ACTIVE` contract on the visit's account and, through it, the programme. If the contract names no `brewer_program_id`, or its `funding_status` is anything other than `BREWER_FUNDED`, or the programme's status is anything other than `ACTIVE`, then `brewer_funded_cents` is 0, `account_due_cents` is the whole `settled_charge_cents`, and the waterfall stops there.

Otherwise three buckets are formed. The `CLEANING` bucket is the `cleaning_subtotal_cents`, already net of any waiver withholding. The `PARTS` bucket is the sum of `part_charge_cents` over the visit's `REPLACED` inspections whose catalogue part carries `brewer_funded_eligible` set true — a part that is not eligible is billed to the venue even under the widest scope, inflating `settled_charge_cents` while contributing nothing to this bucket. The `BASE_FEE` bucket is the contract's `visit_base_fee_cents`. The programme's `funded_scope` decides which buckets it will touch: `CLEANING_ONLY` reaches the cleaning bucket alone, `CLEANING_AND_PARTS` reaches cleaning and parts, and `FULL` reaches all three.

The buckets are then filled in the fixed order cleaning, parts, base fee, with the cap consumed as the fill proceeds. The headroom at the start is the programme's `funding_cap_cents` less its `consumed_cents`. For each bucket in turn, an ineligible bucket is skipped; otherwise the brewer's share of that bucket is the bucket amount multiplied by the eligible proportion — that is, ten thousand basis points less the programme's `co_pay_bps`, divided by ten thousand — truncated downward to whole cents so the brewer never over-funds a bucket by a rounding cent. That share is then clamped to whatever headroom is left after the buckets already filled, and never falls below zero. The clamp is applied per bucket against the **running** headroom, not once against the total, and the two are not the same: a cleaning bucket of 9186 and a parts bucket of 3195 at a `co_pay_bps` of 2000 fund 7348 and 2556 under a roomy cap, but under a starting headroom of 8000 the cleaning bucket takes its full 7348 while the parts bucket is truncated to 652. A bucket whose remaining headroom is zero contributes exactly zero and does not make the settlement fail.

The running total becomes the visit's `brewer_funded_cents`, and `account_due_cents` is `settled_charge_cents` less that total. The programme's ledger is then synchronised: `consumed_cents` rises by the granted amount and `remaining_cents` is recomputed as the cap less the consumed figure. When `remaining_cents` reaches exactly zero the programme moves `ACTIVE → EXHAUSTED`, and that exhaustion cascades as described in §3.1 — every contract naming the programme whose status is `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` recomputes its `funding_status` to `SELF_FUNDED`, processed in ascending `created_at` order, and each of those accounts recomputes its `billing_mode` to `DIRECT_BILL`. One venue's signature therefore changes the billing arrangement of every other venue on the same brewer's programme.

### 3.8. Pour-Quality Complaints, Diagnosis and Yield Loss

When the beer tastes wrong, the venue manager files a complaint naming its own `account_id`, the `line_id` at fault, and the `symptom` — `FOAMY_POUR`, `FLAT_POUR`, `OFF_FLAVOR`, `SLOW_POUR` or `CLOUDY`. Intake is screened in this order: the `X-User-ID` must equal the `account_id` in the body (`ERR_ACCOUNT_SCOPE_VIOLATION`), the account must be `ACTIVE` (`ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_ACTIVE`), and the line must belong to that account (`ERR_LINE_ACCOUNT_MISMATCH`). The complaint opens with `status` `OPEN`, `reported_at` stamped from the logical clock, `diagnostic_round` and `reopen_count` at 0, and `last_finding_code` at `NONE`. The account's `open_complaint_count`, which counts complaints in `OPEN`, `DIAGNOSING`, `AWAITING_PARTS` or `ESCALATED`, is recomputed.

Intake also fixes the exposure window. The `days_at_risk` figure counts whole elapsed days from the line's `last_cleaned_at` to `reported_at`, discarding any part-day; when the line has never recorded a passing cleaning, the count runs from the line's `created_at` instead, because a line that has never been cleaned has been exposed since the day it was plumbed. The figure is never negative and is capped at thirty, so a line last cleaned forty days before the report yields exactly 30, not 40.

Yield loss is then estimated twice over the life of the complaint, and the two figures sit side by side on the same record so the divergence stays visible.

The **provisional** variant runs at intake and is a forward-looking estimate driven by symptom severity. Each symptom carries a severity factor: `FOAMY_POUR` 0.35, `FLAT_POUR` 0.20, `OFF_FLAVOR` 0.50, `SLOW_POUR` 0.15 and `CLOUDY` 0.30. Multiply the symptom's factor by `days_at_risk` and by the line's `average_daily_throughput_liters`, and round half up to two decimal places to get `provisional_yield_loss_liters`. Multiply that by the wholesale value of 480 cents per liter and round half up to a whole number to get `provisional_yield_loss_cents`. A `FOAMY_POUR` on a line pouring 12.0 liters a day, reported seventeen days after its last cleaning, therefore estimates 71.40 liters and 34272 cents. This estimate is frozen on the record: a cleaning performed the day after the complaint was filed does not retroactively shrink it.

An auditor then works the problem one round at a time, each round carrying an `outcome` of either `ROOT_CAUSE_FOUND` or `PARTS_REQUIRED` together with a `finding_code` drawn from `LINE_SOIL`, `GAS_IMBALANCE`, `TEMP_EXCURSION`, `FAUCET_WEAR` and `KEG_DEFECT`. Rounds are only accepted while the complaint is `OPEN`, `DIAGNOSING` or `AWAITING_PARTS`; once it has been resolved or escalated the platform answers `ERR_COMPLAINT_ALREADY_CLOSED`. At most three rounds may ever be logged on one complaint — the `diagnostic_round` count must be below three, else `ERR_DIAGNOSTIC_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`, and the fourth round is refused with the instruction implicit that the complaint must now be closed one way or the other. From `OPEN`, finding the root cause moves the complaint `OPEN → DIAGNOSING` while needing parts moves it `OPEN → AWAITING_PARTS`. From `DIAGNOSING`, needing parts moves it `DIAGNOSING → AWAITING_PARTS`. From `AWAITING_PARTS`, finding the root cause moves it back `AWAITING_PARTS → DIAGNOSING`. Those last two are the revision loop: diagnosis and parts-waiting alternate, each round burning one of the three, until the cap forces a disposition. Every round increments `diagnostic_round` by 1 and overwrites `last_finding_code` with the round's `finding_code`, so the record always reflects the most recent theory rather than the first.

Closure carries a `disposition` of `RESOLVED` or `ESCALATED`. An escalation is the auditor handing the problem to the brewer: it is available from `DIAGNOSING` or `AWAITING_PARTS`, stamps `escalated_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`, and leaves the complaint counted in the account's `open_complaint_count`, because nothing has actually been fixed. A resolution is available from `OPEN`, `DIAGNOSING`, `AWAITING_PARTS` and also from `ESCALATED` — a complaint whose cause was obvious at intake can be disposed of without burning a round, and an escalated complaint can be brought back down once the brewer has answered. Every resolution requires a `measured_waste_liters` figure of zero or more, else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; requires that the acting `X-User-ID` differ from the `escalated_by_user_id` already on the record, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, since the auditor who escalated a complaint may never be the one who signs it off; and requires the complaint's line to be back `IN_SERVICE`, else `ERR_LINE_OUT_OF_SERVICE`, because a line still out of service has not been made good. A resolution stamps `resolved_at` from the clock, records `closed_by_user_id`, stores the `measured_waste_liters`, and recomputes the account's `open_complaint_count`; a resolved complaint drops out of that count while an escalated one does not.

The **settled** variant runs at closure and discards the severity estimate entirely in favour of what the technician actually dumped. First count the days the complaint was open: take the whole elapsed days from `reported_at` to `resolved_at`, discarding any part-day, and add one, so the report day itself counts. Then add to the `measured_waste_liters` a foam allowance of half a liter for each of those days, and round half up to two decimal places to get `settled_yield_loss_liters`; multiply by 480 cents per liter and round half up for `settled_yield_loss_cents`. The same complaint from the worked example above, closed with 18.5 measured liters after two days open, settles at 19.50 liters and 9360 cents — a downward delta of 24912 cents from the provisional figure, and both figures remain on the record.

A venue that is not satisfied may reopen a resolved complaint, moving it `RESOLVED → OPEN` with a `reopen_reason`. Only a `RESOLVED` complaint may be reopened, else `ERR_COMPLAINT_ALREADY_CLOSED`; the caller must be the complaint's own account (`ERR_ACCOUNT_SCOPE_VIOLATION`); the reopen must land within 336 hours of `resolved_at`, else `ERR_REOPEN_WINDOW_CLOSED`; and the `reopen_count` must be below two, else `ERR_REOPEN_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, so a complaint may be reopened twice and no more. Reopening raises `reopen_count` by 1, resets `diagnostic_round` to 0 so a fresh set of three rounds becomes available, and clears `resolved_at`, `closed_by_user_id`, `measured_waste_liters`, `settled_yield_loss_liters` and `settled_yield_loss_cents` back to null. What survives the loop is the intake snapshot: `reported_at`, `days_at_risk` and both provisional figures are never recomputed, so the estimate a reopened complaint carries is still the one struck the day it was first filed.

### 3.9. Glycol Loop Health, Cooler Monitoring and Account Enforcement

A long-draw venue chills its beer through a glycol loop, and the health of that loop decides whether the platform will let anyone circulate chemistry at all. Only venues that declared `glycol_system_present` have one, and the loop was created for them at registration; there is no endpoint that creates a loop directly. Three operations therefore share a common gate: booking a `GLYCOL_MAINTENANCE` visit, posting a cooler reading, and servicing a loop all require the governing account's `glycol_system_present` to be true and a loop to exist for that account, else `ERR_NO_GLYCOL_SYSTEM`. Sitting near the bottom of the ladder, that refusal only surfaces once every ownership, state and reference rule above it has passed.

Temperature evidence arrives as cooler readings, which either a `ServiceTechnician` or a `VenueManager` may post. A reading names the `glycol_loop_id` and carries two Fahrenheit figures to two decimal places: `cooler_temp_f`, the walk-in air temperature, and `glycol_supply_temp_f`, the temperature of the glycol supply line. The server stamps `reading_at` from the logical clock, records `recorded_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID`, and derives `source` from the acting role — `TECH_MANUAL` for a technician, `VENUE_MANUAL` for a venue manager. A venue manager may only post against a loop whose owning account is its own, else `ERR_ACCOUNT_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. Like inspections, readings are immutable event records: they carry an `id` and nothing else in the way of common metadata — no `updated_at`, no `version` — and there is no endpoint that amends or deletes one. A wrong reading is corrected only by posting a later one and letting it displace the old from the scoring window.

Servicing the bath is field work. During an open visit, the assigned technician posts the `visit_id` together with the three bath figures — `glycol_pct`, the glycol concentration in percent, `pump_psi`, the circulation pump's discharge pressure, and `bath_temp_f`, the chiller bath temperature — which overwrite whatever the loop carried before. The loop's `last_serviced_at` is reset to the current clock instant, which zeroes the service-interval penalty described below. Like every other child record posted against a visit, maintenance requires the caller to be the visit's assigned technician (`ERR_TECHNICIAN_NOT_ASSIGNED`) and the visit to be `IN_PROGRESS` (`ERR_VISIT_NOT_IN_PROGRESS`).

The loop's `health_score` is a weighted penalty score against a ceiling of 100.0, recomputed whenever a reading is posted, whenever the loop is serviced, and whenever the logical clock moves — the score decays with time even when nothing else happens.

The scoring window is the newest three readings on the loop, ordered by `reading_at` descending and then, for ties, by `created_at` descending, and never by identifier. When the window is empty, `window_avg_cooler_temp_f` is null and the temperature penalty is zero; otherwise `window_avg_cooler_temp_f` is the mean of the window's `cooler_temp_f` values rounded half up to two decimal places. Four penalties are then computed, each rounded half up to two decimal places on its own before any subtraction, so that penalties never accumulate unrounded drift:

- The **temperature penalty** is the amount by which `window_avg_cooler_temp_f` exceeds the target of 38.0 degrees, multiplied by 8.0 points per degree, and floored at zero — a cooler running colder than target earns no credit.
- The **glycol penalty** is the amount by which `glycol_pct` falls short of the 30.0 per cent minimum, multiplied by 2.5 points per percentage point, and floored at zero.
- The **pump penalty** is a flat 12.0 points when `pump_psi` is below 9.0, and zero otherwise. There is no gradation: 8.99 psi and 2.00 psi cost the same twelve points.
- The **service penalty** counts whole days from `last_serviced_at` to the current clock instant, discarding any part-day and never going below zero, subtracts the 180-day service interval, floors that difference at zero, and multiplies by 0.6 points per day overdue.

Subtract all four penalties from 100.0, clamp the result into the range 0.0 through 100.0, and round half up to two decimal places once more to obtain `health_score`. The loop's `status` is then a pure projection of that number, with both band edges inclusive on their lower bound: `NORMAL` at 75.00 and above, `WATCH` from 50.00 up to but not including 75.00, and `ALARM` below 50.00. A score of exactly 75.00 is `NORMAL` while 74.99 is `WATCH`; exactly 50.00 is `WATCH` while 49.99 is `ALARM`. A loop with no readings and its factory bath figures scores exactly 100.00. The `status` never moves on a trigger of its own — there is no endpoint that sets it — and an `ALARM` band immediately blocks new cleaning cycles anywhere on that account, as §3.4 describes.

Two properties of the window are worth spelling out because they surprise people. Because only the newest three readings count, a fourth reading pushes the oldest out, so the score can **improve** after a bad reading is displaced — nothing is averaged over history. And because the window is ordered by `reading_at` rather than by insertion, a back-dated reading enters the window only if its `reading_at` is among the three newest; creation order alone does not admit it. Readings from a technician and from a venue manager are weighted identically.

Enforcement at the account level is the `QualityAuditor`'s job, driven by a `to_status` on the account lifecycle operation. An account not in the starting state a transition requires is refused with `ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_ACTIVE`, and `TERMINATED` is terminal — nothing leaves it.

Suspending an `ACTIVE` account by request moves it `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` and sets `suspended_reason` to `MANUAL`, which is precisely how the manual path is distinguished afterwards from the automatic one that sets `LINE_COMPLIANCE_THRESHOLD`. A manual suspension additionally requires a quiet board: no visit on the account may be `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS`, else `ERR_ACCOUNT_HAS_OPEN_VISITS`. The suspension then cascades exactly as the automatic path does — every contract on the account still `ACTIVE` moves to `SUSPENDED` in ascending `created_at` order, and every visit still `SCHEDULED` moves to `ABANDONED` with `abandon_reason` `ACCOUNT_SUSPENDED`, processed by ascending `scheduled_date`, then ascending `scheduled_start_minute`, then ascending `created_at`.

Reactivating a `SUSPENDED` account moves it `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE`, but only once the compliance breach has actually been cleared: twice the account's `lines_out_of_service_count` must be strictly less than the `contracted_line_count` of the account's newest contract that is not `EXPIRED`, taking the newest by descending `created_at`, else `ERR_LINE_COMPLIANCE_UNRESOLVED`. Note that this reads the newest non-expired contract rather than the active one, because the suspension cascade has by then left the account with no active contract at all. Reactivation resets `suspended_reason` to `NONE` and moves every contract on the account still `SUSPENDED` back to `ACTIVE` in ascending `created_at` order, each of which recomputes the account's `billing_mode` as §3.1 describes. Visits that were abandoned by the suspension are **not** restored: the route board has to be rebuilt by booking fresh visits.

Terminating an account is available from either `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, moves it to `TERMINATED`, and likewise requires that no visit on the account be `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS`, else `ERR_ACCOUNT_HAS_OPEN_VISITS`. Termination expires the commercial relationship wholesale: every contract on the account whose status is `DRAFT`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` moves to `EXPIRED`, processed in ascending `created_at` order. This is the only path by which a `DRAFT` contract ever reaches `EXPIRED`.
