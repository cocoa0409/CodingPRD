# Children's Online Privacy Compliance API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Operators of child-directed online services carry an obligation that is unusual among compliance regimes: almost every duty they owe is conditional on a determination they must make about themselves. Whether a game, a video property, an education app, a social network or a general-interest portal is "directed to children" is not a label a regulator hands out — it is a conclusion the operator reaches from measured audience composition, from the subject matter it publishes, and from the design choices it makes about visual content, celebrities, language, music, advertising placement and interactivity. Everything downstream — whether an age screen is needed, whether a direct parental notice must be delivered before a single data element is collected, which verifiable-consent method is good enough, which advertising SDK may run in which surface — hangs off that one conclusion. Today that reasoning lives in spreadsheets, legal memos and email threads, which means it cannot be audited, cannot be re-run when new evidence arrives, and cannot be relied on when a regulator asks "on what basis, and as of when?"

This API turns that reasoning into a system of record. It manages the evidence an operator accumulates about each of its online services, runs the child-directed determination from that evidence on demand, and then enforces the consequences: neutral age screening that does not invite a child to misstate their age, a direct parental notice whose required content is complete before it is published, verifiable consent whose assurance standard rises with the sensitivity of the intended data use, collection limited to the data elements reasonably necessary for the activity the child is participating in, parental rights to review, delete and refuse further collection, an audit trail over every third-party advertising and analytics integration operating in a child-directed area, a retention schedule with a written purpose and an approved secure-disposal method for every element, and participation in a safe harbor program with an annual assessment that a second reviewer must certify.

Two design commitments run through the whole specification and should be understood before reading further. First, a service carries **two independent compliance dimensions at once** — what it has been determined to be, and how tightly its operations are currently constrained — and these two dimensions move on separate triggers. Second, the system is **deliberately explicit about time and ordering**: it runs on an explicit logical clock rather than wall-clock time, it never uses a resource identifier as a sort key, and secure disposal is a two-stage process that spans two separate compliance runs so that no record is destroyed in the same operation that first noticed it was due.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contract-level rules that hold across every endpoint: authentication, the response envelope, pagination, resource identity, the two compliance axes, concurrency control, numeric precision, and the order in which validations are evaluated.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two mandatory headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which it acts. The system recognises exactly five roles: `ComplianceOfficer`, `PrivacyEngineer`, `Parent`, `SafeHarborAssessor`, and `SafeHarborCertifier`. If either header is absent or present but empty, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of those five exact strings, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If both headers are well-formed but the acting role is not listed among the authorised roles for the endpoint in §4, the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The five roles implement a segregation of duties. A `ComplianceOfficer` owns determination, evidence, notices, activities, the consent lifecycle, integration audit decisions, retention policy, the posture override, the logical clock and safe harbor program registration — but may never record collected child data and may never author an age screen. A `PrivacyEngineer` builds the age screen and performs the actual collection, but may not approve its own screen and may not touch consent, notices or audits. A `Parent` exercises parental rights only. A `SafeHarborAssessor` prepares assessments; a `SafeHarborCertifier` reviews and certifies them, and must never be the same person who prepared the one being certified.

Four of the five roles carry an opaque identity. When `X-User-Role` is `ComplianceOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server does not compare it to any entity's `id`. The same holds when `X-User-Role` is `PrivacyEngineer`. When `X-User-Role` is `SafeHarborAssessor`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier, but the server does record it — it is stored as `preparer_user_id` on the assessment that assessor opens. When `X-User-Role` is `SafeHarborCertifier`, `X-User-ID` is again an opaque operator identifier, recorded as `certifier_user_id` at certification and compared only against `preparer_user_id`, never against any entity's `id`.

The `Parent` role is different, and this difference is load-bearing. When `X-User-Role` is `Parent`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `ConsentRequest` — the consent record that parent holds. Ownership checks therefore compare a resource's consent linkage to `X-User-ID` directly: a `Parent` may read or act on a `ConsentRequest` only when that request's own `id` equals `X-User-ID`, and may read or act on a `ParentalRightsRequest` only when that request's `consent_request_id` equals `X-User-ID`. A `Parent` who addresses somebody else's consent record or rights request is rejected with `ERR_NOT_CONSENT_HOLDER` (HTTP 403). This ownership rule guards four operations: reading a single consent request, reading a single parental rights request, creating a parental rights request, and driving a parental rights request's status.

Every status-transition endpoint in this API names its desired target state in a `to_status` body field, so the transition being requested is the pair formed by the resource's current `status` and that target. Role authorisation on an endpoint is necessary but not always sufficient. Several status endpoints are shared by two roles, and each individual transition within them names the one role permitted to make it. Where the acting role is authorised for the endpoint but not for the specific transition requested, the rejection is `ERR_TRANSITION_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403). This per-transition restriction applies to three endpoints: `POST /age-screens/{id}/status`, `POST /parental-rights-requests/{id}/status`, and `POST /safe-harbor-assessments/{id}/status`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits; it is taken from real wall-clock time, not from the logical clock of §2.4.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.
- A request body that is absent or empty behaves as an empty JSON object, so the missing fields surface through the ordinary body-shape checks; a body that is present but unparseable, or that parses to something other than a JSON object, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422).

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints are paginated through two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of items returned; when omitted it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` is the number of items to skip; when omitted it defaults to 0, and its minimum accepted value is 0. A parameter supplied as an empty string is treated exactly as if it had been omitted; a value that is not an integer, or an integer outside the bounds just given, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422).

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. There is exactly one documented exception: the reverse-lookup listing of a service's determination evidence is ordered by `evidence_sequence` ascending, because that counter — not creation time and never the row's identifier — is the authoritative ordering of the evidence record.

Where two rows carry the same value in the ordering key, the row inserted earlier wins the tie, so list order is stable. Every list response includes `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the envelope's `meta` object; `total_count` is the size of the filtered set before the page window is applied, and `has_more` is true exactly when `offset` plus the number of items actually returned is still less than `total_count`.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and the Logical Clock

Every resource the API returns carries an `id`: a server-minted UUID, with no type prefix and no domain-flavoured scheme. Every resource also carries a `created_at` timestamp, an ISO 8601 UTC string with three fractional digits. Like every other stored instant in this system — `updated_at` included — `created_at` is stamped from the logical clock described below rather than from wall-clock time, so a resource created while the clock sits at its startup value carries exactly that instant.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at` — an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp, refreshed on every accepted mutation of that resource — and `version`, an integer that begins at 1 when the resource is created and increments by exactly one on each accepted mutation. This applies to the online service, age screen, parental notice, activity definition, consent request, child data record, parental rights request, third-party integration, retention schedule, safe harbor program and safe harbor assessment resources.

Determination evidence is the sole exception, and deliberately so: evidence is append-only and is never updated after it is written. An evidence row carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has **no** `updated_at` field and **no** `version` field, and neither field appears in its response payload.

All time-dependent behaviour — deadlines, expiries, disposal due dates, effective ranges, certification validity — is evaluated against a server-side **logical clock**, never against real wall-clock time. The clock's startup value is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. Until an operator moves it, every comparison in this specification uses that instant. A `ComplianceOfficer` may reposition the clock by supplying `to_iso8601`; the response reports the new instant as `current_utc`. Because expiry and lapse conditions are clock-dependent, moving the clock recomputes `safe_harbor_status` and `compliance_posture` for every registered service.

### 2.5. The Two Compliance Axes of an Online Service

An `OnlineService` row carries two enumerated state fields that evolve independently of one another, on the same row, at the same time.

**Axis one is `determination_status`**, whose value is one of `NOT_ASSESSED`, `GENERAL_AUDIENCE`, `MIXED_AUDIENCE` or `CHILD_DIRECTED_PRIMARY`. A newly registered service starts at `NOT_ASSESSED`. This axis is written by exactly one operation — the determination run described in §3.2 — and by nothing else. A determination run may move the axis between any pair of its values, so a service that was `CHILD_DIRECTED_PRIMARY` can be re-determined down to `GENERAL_AUDIENCE` when fresh evidence supports it.

**Axis two is `compliance_posture`**, whose value is one of `OPEN`, `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN`, and which starts at `OPEN`. This axis is never set directly. It is always the output of a single ordered decision, re-evaluated after the service's derived counters have been refreshed within the same request. The clauses are evaluated in this order and the first one that matches wins:

1. If `posture_override` is not null, the posture is exactly that override value.
2. Otherwise, if `third_party_exposure_points` is 80 or more, the posture is `FROZEN`.
3. Otherwise, if `open_audit_count` is 1 or more, the posture is `RESTRICTED`.
4. Otherwise, if `third_party_exposure_points` is 40 or more, the posture is `RESTRICTED`.
5. Otherwise, if `safe_harbor_status` is `LAPSED`, the posture is `RESTRICTED`.
6. Otherwise, the posture is `OPEN`.

Both numeric boundaries are inclusive: an exposure total of exactly 80 freezes the service, and an exposure total of exactly 40 restricts it. Clause order matters independently of the thresholds — a service with 90 exposure points and one open audit is `FROZEN`, not `RESTRICTED`, because clause 2 is reached first. Recomputation is triggered by a change to `posture_override`, by any third-party integration status change, by a consent grant or revocation, by the creation or disposal of a child data record, by a safe harbor certification or failure, and by a compliance run.

`posture_override` is a manual regulator-style lock whose value is `OPEN`, `RESTRICTED`, `FROZEN`, or null. It defaults to null, meaning the computed posture governs. While it is non-null it wins over the computation outright.

The posture axis is a gate on three operations. When the posture is `FROZEN`, collecting a child data element, creating a consent request, and approving a third-party integration are all refused with `ERR_POSTURE_FROZEN` (HTTP 409). When the posture is `RESTRICTED`, collecting a child data element is refused with `ERR_POSTURE_RESTRICTED` (HTTP 409) unless the collection's `purpose_code` is `PARTICIPATION_CORE`; consent creation and integration approval are unaffected by `RESTRICTED`. Parental rights are honoured under every posture — no posture value blocks the verification or fulfilment of a parental rights request.

### 2.6. Version Stamping, Cascade Settlement and Optimistic Concurrency

Because so many derived counters live on the service row, almost every write in this API touches `OnlineService`. The rule is: an accepted request increments `OnlineService.version` by **exactly one**, applied after every cascade triggered by that request has settled, and refreshes the service's `updated_at`. A single request that moves a determination, forces three integrations back into audit, recomputes the exposure total and flips the posture still advances `version` by one, not by five. "Almost every" is literal, though, and the exceptions are fixed: a write that leaves the service row itself alone leaves both `OnlineService.version` and the service's `updated_at` untouched. Those exceptions are age-screen declaration and every age-screen status transition (including an activation that retires a sibling screen), activity definition, retention-schedule publication, parental-rights-request creation and every rights transition short of fulfilment, assessment submission, remediation and resubmission, the consent transitions §3.5 already lists as counter-neutral, safe harbor program registration, and movement of the logical clock. Every other accepted write named in §3 advances the service's `version` by exactly one. A cascade that mutates some *other* row — a superseded notice, a retired screen, a superseded or revoked consent, a disposed record, an integration forced back into audit — advances that row's own `version` by one as well, on the same rule as any direct mutation.

Two write endpoints are guarded by optimistic concurrency control against that service version.

`POST /parental-notices` requires `expected_service_version` as a non-optional body field carrying the client's last observed `OnlineService.version`. If it does not equal the target service's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SERVICE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The reason is concrete: another writer may already have drafted and published a newer notice revision, which legitimately supersedes the prior published revision, so the current writer's picture of the service — which notice is live, which vendors that notice discloses — is out of date.

`POST /consent-requests` likewise requires `expected_service_version` as a non-optional body field carrying the client's last observed `OnlineService.version`, and a mismatch with the service's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SERVICE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Here the stake is the assurance tier: a new request for the same parent and child supersedes the parent's prior in-flight request, and the required assurance tier is pinned from a snapshot of the service's approved integrations taken at creation. A stale service version therefore means the tier the client believes it is consenting to no longer matches the service. On both endpoints the field is read as a body-shape requirement before it is compared: an `expected_service_version` that is absent, null or not an integer is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422), and only a well-formed integer that disagrees with the service's current `version` produces the 412.

No other endpoint accepts a version field. Endpoints not named above perform no concurrency check and must not require, read or reject on any client-supplied version value.

### 2.7. Numeric Precision, Rounding and Ordering Keys

Two derived numbers on a service are decimal; everything else in the system is integer arithmetic. Both are carried in the payload as JSON numbers rather than as strings, so the declared precision governs the arithmetic and not the rendering — a `design_score` of `0.00` is emitted as `0.0` and an `audience_child_share` of `0.4000` as `0.4`.

`design_score` is carried to 2 decimal places using round-half-up. Its rounding order is pinned and is not interchangeable: each individual factor contribution is rounded half-up to 2 decimal places **first**, those rounded contributions are then summed, and the sum is rounded half-up to 2 decimal places again. Summing unrounded contributions and rounding once at the end is explicitly rejected as an implementation, because it produces different scores.

`audience_child_share` is carried to 4 decimal places using round-half-up. It is computed once, as a single ratio over a summed numerator and a summed denominator across all qualifying samples — never as an average of per-sample ratios.

Every other quantity — assurance tiers, exposure points, audit counts, record counts, revision and remediation rounds, retention days — is an integer.

Ordering keys are fixed and a resource `id` is **never** among them. The latest design-factor evidence for a factor is the row with the greatest `evidence_sequence`, a per-service monotonic integer in which ties are impossible. Compliance run phases are separated by `compliance_run_sequence`, another per-service monotonic integer. Records within a resolved deletion or disposal scope are processed in order of `collected_at` ascending, then `created_at` ascending. Among certified assessments, the governing one is the row with the greatest `assessment_year`, and if two rows share that year, the one with the later `certified_at`.

### 2.8. Reverse-Lookup Reads, Filtering and Identifier Resolution

Every business resource in this API is its own top-level collection; no business resource is nested inside another resource's URL path. Parent-to-child discovery happens in two ways: through read-only reverse-lookup sub-resources hanging off a service, and through foreign-key filters on the flat collections.

Where a path contains more than one identifier, identifiers are resolved strictly left to right and the request fails on the **first** one that does not resolve, with the 404 error belonging to that identifier: `ERR_SERVICE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AGE_SCREEN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_NOTICE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONSENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RIGHTS_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INTEGRATION_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FOUND`. Body foreign keys are resolved in a fixed declaration order — `service_id` first, then `notice_id`, then `consent_request_id`, then `activity_id`, then `program_id` — and the first unresolvable one determines the error: `ERR_SERVICE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_NOTICE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONSENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ACTIVITY_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`. A 404 is reserved for a key that is well-formed but points at nothing; a key that is missing altogether, null, empty or not a string cannot identify anything and is a body-shape problem, reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422) at this same stage. This body-key resolution guards eleven creating operations: `POST /determination-evidence`, `POST /age-screens`, `POST /parental-notices`, `POST /activities`, `POST /consent-requests`, `POST /child-data-records`, `POST /parental-rights-requests`, `POST /third-party-integrations`, `POST /retention-schedules`, `POST /safe-harbor-assessments` and `POST /compliance-runs`.

Resolution alone is not enough: references must also be coherent with the service named in the same body. A referenced consent request, a referenced activity definition and a referenced notice revision must all belong to the `service_id` given in the body; otherwise the request is rejected with `ERR_SERVICE_SCOPE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). This coherence check guards three creating operations: `POST /child-data-records` (over both `consent_request_id` and `activity_id`), `POST /parental-rights-requests` (over `consent_request_id`) and `POST /consent-requests` — where the reference actually checked is `notice_id`, since that is the only cross-resource key that body carries.

The available filters are as follows. The service list accepts `determination_status`, `compliance_posture` and `safe_harbor_status`. A service's evidence reverse-lookup accepts `evidence_kind` and `measurement_method`; its age-screen, notice and integration reverse-lookups accept `status`; its integration reverse-lookup additionally accepts `integration_type` and `placement_area`; its retention-schedule reverse-lookup accepts `data_element_code` and `purpose_code`; its activity reverse-lookup takes no filters beyond pagination. The consent-request list accepts `service_id`, `notice_id`, `parent_email`, `child_pseudonym` and `status`. The child-data-record list accepts `service_id`, `consent_request_id`, `activity_id`, `child_pseudonym` and `status`. The parental-rights-request list accepts `service_id`, `consent_request_id`, `right_type` and `status`. The assessment list accepts `service_id`, `program_id`, `assessment_year` and `status`. Every one of these list endpoints also accepts `limit` and `offset`. Every filter matches on exact equality against the stored value, and a filter value that is not a legal member of the field's enumeration — or an `assessment_year` that is not a number — is not an error: it simply matches nothing and yields an empty page with `total_count` 0. A filter supplied as an empty string, like an empty `limit`, is treated as not supplied at all. Unrecognised query parameters are ignored.

### 2.9. Validation Precedence

Many operations in this API are guarded by several checks at once, and which error a caller sees is itself part of the contract. Checks are evaluated in the following strict order, and the first failing check determines the response. A later check never masks an earlier one.

1. **Authentication.** Headers present and the role recognised, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. **Endpoint authorisation.** The acting role appears in the endpoint's authorised-role list in §4, else `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403).
3. **Parent ownership.** A `Parent` addresses only its own consent record and its own rights requests, else `ERR_NOT_CONSENT_HOLDER` (403). Where the check can be settled from the supplied identifier alone it genuinely precedes existence: reading a single consent request compares the path identifier to `X-User-ID`, and creating a parental rights request compares the body's raw `consent_request_id` to `X-User-ID`, so a `Parent` naming somebody else's identifier — or omitting it, since an absent `consent_request_id` is not the parent's own either — is refused 403 even when that identifier, or the `service_id` alongside it, resolves to nothing. Where the check needs the stored row, it cannot run until the row is in hand: reading or driving a parental rights request whose identifier resolves to nothing reports `ERR_RIGHTS_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND` (404) even to a `Parent`.
4. **Path identifier resolution**, left to right, with the 404 codes of §2.8.
5. **Body foreign-key resolution**, in declaration order, with the 404 codes of §2.8.
6. **Cross-parent coherence**, else `ERR_SERVICE_SCOPE_MISMATCH` (422).
7. **Service version freshness.** `expected_service_version` equals the current `OnlineService.version`, else `ERR_STALE_SERVICE_VERSION` (412).
8. **Body shape.** Required fields present, correctly typed, and within their declared enumerations, else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422); for evidence creation, a payload that does not match its declared evidence kind is rejected with `ERR_EVIDENCE_FIELDS_MISMATCH` (422).
9. **Transition legality.** The target resource's current `status` permits the requested transition, else one of `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_NOTICE_NOT_PUBLISHED`, `ERR_CONSENT_NOT_GRANTED`, `ERR_CONSENT_REVOKED` or `ERR_RIGHTS_REQUEST_NOT_VERIFIED` (all 409). This step guards every status-transition endpoint as well as notice publication, assessment certification and record collection. Where no more specific code applies the response is `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — publishing a notice that is not `DRAFT`, or certifying an assessment that is not `SUBMITTED`, both land here. Note the consequence for collection: attempting to collect against a revoked consent surfaces here as `ERR_CONSENT_REVOKED` (409), *before* the notice-delivery and consent-quality checks at step 13 have a chance to fire.
10. **Per-transition role restriction**, else `ERR_TRANSITION_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403).
11. **Posture gate**, else `ERR_POSTURE_FROZEN` or `ERR_POSTURE_RESTRICTED` (409).
12. **Determination and age-screening gates on collection**, in that order: first `ERR_DETERMINATION_NOT_MADE` or `ERR_SERVICE_NOT_CHILD_DIRECTED` (409), then `ERR_AGE_SCREEN_NOT_ACTIVE`, `ERR_NOT_CHILD_SUBJECT` or `ERR_AGE_SCREEN_NOT_PERMITTED` (422). Age-screen neutrality on activation, `ERR_AGE_SCREEN_NOT_NEUTRAL` (422), sits at the same stage.
13. **Direct-notice-before-collection and consent quality**, evaluated in this internal clause order: (a) the referenced consent's `notice_delivered_at` must be non-null, else `ERR_DIRECT_NOTICE_NOT_DELIVERED`; (b) the consent method's assurance tier must reach the pinned required tier, else `ERR_CONSENT_METHOD_TIER_INSUFFICIENT`; (c) an `EMAIL_PLUS` consent needs its secondary confirmation, else `ERR_EMAIL_PLUS_CONFIRMATION_MISSING`; (d) parental-rights verification must reach the consent method's tier, else `ERR_RIGHTS_VERIFICATION_TIER_INSUFFICIENT`; (e) `requester_email` must match the consent's `parent_email`, else `ERR_REQUESTER_EMAIL_MISMATCH`. All five are HTTP 422. This clause group guards three operations: `POST /child-data-records`, `POST /consent-requests/{id}/status` and `POST /parental-rights-requests/{id}/status`.
14. **Third-party integration audit gate**, else `ERR_BEHAVIORAL_AD_TIER_UNMET` (422).
15. **Audience-sample period rules**, else `ERR_SAMPLE_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (409) or `ERR_INVALID_PERIOD` (422).
16. **Retention-schedule range rules**, else `ERR_RETENTION_SCHEDULE_OVERLAP` (409).
17. **Consent verification window**, else `ERR_CONSENT_WINDOW_EXPIRED` (409).
18. **Integration disclosure**, else `ERR_INTEGRATION_NOT_DISCLOSED` (422).
19. **Assessment-year uniqueness**, else `ERR_ASSESSMENT_YEAR_DUPLICATE` (409).
20. **Data minimization**, else `ERR_ELEMENT_NOT_REASONABLY_NECESSARY` or `ERR_PURPOSE_NOT_CONSENTED` (422).
21. **Notice completeness and disposal-method admissibility**, else `ERR_NOTICE_CONTENT_INCOMPLETE`, `ERR_DISPOSAL_METHOD_NOT_PERMITTED` or `ERR_INVALID_PERIOD` (422).
22. **Loop bounds**, else `ERR_VERIFICATION_ATTEMPTS_EXHAUSTED`, `ERR_REMEDIATION_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED` or `ERR_SCREEN_REVISIONS_EXHAUSTED` (409).
23. **Safe harbor gates**, else `ERR_OPEN_AUDITS_PRESENT`, `ERR_OPEN_FINDINGS_PRESENT`, `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_REGULATOR_APPROVED` or `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_REQUIRED` (409).
24. **Segregation of duties**, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
25. **Retention-schedule availability at collection**, else `ERR_NO_RETENTION_SCHEDULE` (409).
26. **Evidence sufficiency for determination**, else `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE` (422).

Two consequences of this ordering deserve emphasis because they are easy to get backwards. Step 23 precedes step 24: a certification attempt that both leaves open findings and is made by the preparer reports `ERR_OPEN_FINDINGS_PRESENT` (409), never `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). And within a certification attempt the clause order is findings first, then regulator approval of the program, then segregation of duties.

### 2.10. Bounded Loops and Second-Pair-of-Eyes Rules

Three workflows in this system may cycle, and each is bounded so that a rejected artefact cannot be resubmitted indefinitely. Age-screen revision after a rejection is capped at 2 rounds. Parental-rights re-submission after a failed verification is capped at 3 attempts. Assessment remediation is capped at 2 rounds, and once that cap is reached the certifier's only remaining move is to fail the assessment outright. Each cap is compared against the counter the corresponding entity carries, with re-entry permitted only while the counter is strictly below the cap.

Certification of a safe harbor assessment is a second-pair-of-eyes operation. The `X-User-ID` that certifies must differ from the `preparer_user_id` recorded when the assessment was opened; if they are equal, the request is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). The same principle shapes the age screen: the `PrivacyEngineer` who builds and submits a screen is never the principal who activates it.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Registering a Service and Assembling the Determination Record

A `ComplianceOfficer` brings a property under compliance management by registering it with a public `name`, the legal `operator_organization` that will be shown to parents in notices, a `service_category` drawn from `GAME`, `VIDEO_PLATFORM`, `EDUCATION_APP`, `SOCIAL_NETWORK` or `GENERAL_INTEREST_PORTAL`, and a `subject_matter_code` drawn from `CHILD_ENTERTAINMENT`, `CHILD_EDUCATION`, `FAMILY_MIXED`, `TEEN_LIFESTYLE` or `ADULT_GENERAL`. Registration returns a fully initialised compliance record: `determination_status` is `NOT_ASSESSED`, `compliance_posture` is `OPEN`, `posture_override` is null, `determination_variant_applied` is null, `design_score` is `0.00`, `audience_child_share` is null, `determination_evaluated_at` is null, `safe_harbor_status` is `NOT_ENROLLED`, `safe_harbor_expires_at` is null, every counter — `active_consent_count`, `granted_disclosure_consent_count`, `live_child_data_element_count`, `lifetime_collected_element_count`, `third_party_exposure_points`, `open_audit_count`, `audit_decision_count`, `evidence_sequence_counter` and `compliance_run_sequence` — is 0, and `version` is 1.

Evidence is then appended to the service's record, one row at a time, by the same `ComplianceOfficer`. Evidence is append-only: a row is never edited or deleted once written, which is why it carries no modification timestamp and no version of its own. Each row names the `service_id` it supports, declares an `evidence_kind` of either `AUDIENCE_SAMPLE` or `DESIGN_FACTOR`, and may carry a free-text `evidence_note` recording provenance. Every accepted row advances the service's `evidence_sequence_counter` by one and is stamped with that new value as its `evidence_sequence`, starting at 1 for the first row. That stamp is the only ordering key the determination logic ever consults. An evidence row's payload always carries the union of both field groups: the group its `evidence_kind` does not use is present and null rather than omitted, so a design-factor row reports `measurement_method`, `period_start`, `period_end`, `total_users` and `users_under_13` as null and an audience sample reports `factor_code` and `rating` as null.

`evidence_kind` is a discriminator over two mutually exclusive field groups, and supplying the wrong group is a rejection, not a silently ignored extra. An `AUDIENCE_SAMPLE` row **requires** `measurement_method` — one of `PANEL`, `AGE_SCREEN_LOG` or `SELF_REPORTED` — together with `period_start`, `period_end`, `total_users` and `users_under_13`, and **must not** carry `factor_code` or `rating`. A `DESIGN_FACTOR` row **requires** `factor_code` and `rating`, and **must not** carry any of the five sample fields. Either violation is rejected with `ERR_EVIDENCE_FIELDS_MISMATCH`, and the whole group test — presence of the required group, absence of the forbidden one — runs before any individual field is checked for type or enumeration membership, so a row missing `total_users` reports the mismatch while a row carrying every sample field but a bogus `measurement_method` reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A field supplied explicitly as null counts as absent for the group test. `sample_verified` is a boolean marking whether the sample was independently verified and defaults to false; it is meaningful only on audience samples, and supplying it on a design-factor row is accepted and simply carried without effect rather than treated as a group violation. `total_users` must be at least 1, and `users_under_13` must be at least 0 and no greater than `total_users`; values outside those bounds are rejected as an invalid request, and that bounds check runs before the period rules below. On a design-factor row, `factor_code` is one of `SUBJECT_MATTER`, `VISUAL_CONTENT`, `CHILD_CELEBRITIES`, `LANGUAGE`, `MUSIC_AUDIO`, `ADVERTISING_PLACEMENT` or `INTERACTIVE_DESIGN`, and `rating` is one of `ABSENT`, `INCIDENTAL`, `MODERATE` or `STRONG`.

Audience windows are half-open: `period_start` is included in the measured window and `period_end` is excluded from it. `period_end` must be strictly later than `period_start`, otherwise the row is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PERIOD`; that well-formedness test comes first, so a degenerate window that would also collide with an existing one reports `ERR_INVALID_PERIOD` rather than the overlap error. Two windows measured by the same method on the same service may not overlap. Concretely, a new sample is refused with `ERR_SAMPLE_PERIOD_OVERLAP` when there exists an earlier row for the same `service_id` and the same `measurement_method` such that the new window's start falls strictly before the existing window's end *and* the existing window's start falls strictly before the new window's end. Because the interval is half-open, windows that merely touch are compatible: a new window whose `period_start` is exactly an existing window's `period_end` is accepted. Windows measured by different methods, or belonging to different services, never conflict with one another.

Appending evidence immediately refreshes the service's `design_score` and `audience_child_share` and advances the service's `version` by one — but it deliberately does **not** change `determination_status`. Evidence accumulation and determination are separate acts, so that an operator can assemble a complete record before drawing a conclusion from it.

`design_score` is the weighted picture of the service's design. Only the latest row per `factor_code` participates: for each factor code that appears at all among the service's `DESIGN_FACTOR` rows, the row with the greatest `evidence_sequence` is selected, and rows belonging to other services are excluded entirely. If no design-factor row exists for the service, `design_score` is `0.00`. Otherwise each of the seven factor codes carries a fixed weight — `SUBJECT_MATTER` 30 points, `VISUAL_CONTENT` 20, `CHILD_CELEBRITIES` 15, `LANGUAGE` 10, `MUSIC_AUDIO` 10, `ADVERTISING_PLACEMENT` 10 and `INTERACTIVE_DESIGN` 5 — and each rating carries a fixed multiplier: `ABSENT` contributes a multiplier of `0.00`, `INCIDENTAL` `0.25`, `MODERATE` `0.60` and `STRONG` `1.00`. First, sum the weights of only those factor codes that are present in the selected set; call that the present total. Note that a factor rated `ABSENT` still counts its weight into the present total even though it contributes nothing to the score — deliberately documenting that a factor was looked for and not found dilutes the score rather than leaving it out. Then, for each present factor, divide that factor's weight by the present total, multiply by its rating's multiplier, and round the result half-up to two decimal places. Sum those rounded per-factor contributions and round the sum half-up to two decimal places; that is `design_score`.

`audience_child_share` is the measured picture, and it is far pickier about its inputs. Only rows for this service with `evidence_kind` of `AUDIENCE_SAMPLE`, `measurement_method` of `PANEL`, and `sample_verified` true qualify. Rows measured by `AGE_SCREEN_LOG` or `SELF_REPORTED`, and unverified panel rows, are stored as evidence and are visible in the evidence listing, but they never enter this aggregate — an age-screen log is only as reliable as the screen that produced it, and an unverified sample is only as reliable as the party that supplied it. If no qualifying row exists, `audience_child_share` is null. Otherwise, sum `users_under_13` across all qualifying rows, sum `total_users` across the same rows, divide the first sum by the second, and round half-up to four decimal places.

### 3.2. Reaching a Child-Directed Determination and Managing Posture

Determination is an explicit act performed by a `ComplianceOfficer` against the service's evidence as it currently stands. It takes no request body: the whole point is that the conclusion is reproducible from the record. Before anything else, the service must have something to reason from — at least one `DESIGN_FACTOR` evidence row, or at least one `AUDIENCE_SAMPLE` row that was measured by `PANEL` and has `sample_verified` true. A service with neither is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE`, and its `determination_status` remains where it was.

A determination run first refreshes `design_score` and `audience_child_share` exactly as described in §3.1, then records which of two reasoning variants it used in `determination_variant_applied`, then writes `determination_status`, then stamps `determination_evaluated_at` with the current logical-clock instant.

Variant selection is decided purely by evidence availability, and the two variants reach different conclusions from identical design evidence. When a verified panel sample exists — that is, when `audience_child_share` is non-null — `determination_variant_applied` is `AUDIENCE_FIRST`: measured composition leads. A share of 0.4000 or more makes the service `CHILD_DIRECTED_PRIMARY`. Otherwise a share of 0.2000 or more makes it `MIXED_AUDIENCE`. Otherwise, if `design_score` is 0.65 or more, design evidence escalates the service to `MIXED_AUDIENCE`. Otherwise the service is `GENERAL_AUDIENCE`. Under this variant design evidence can only lift a service from `GENERAL_AUDIENCE` to `MIXED_AUDIENCE`; it can never reach `CHILD_DIRECTED_PRIMARY`, because measured composition has already spoken.

When no verified panel sample exists but at least one design-factor row does, `determination_variant_applied` is `DESIGN_FIRST` and design evidence decides alone. A `design_score` of 0.75 or more makes the service `CHILD_DIRECTED_PRIMARY`. Otherwise a `design_score` of 0.45 or more makes it `MIXED_AUDIENCE`. Otherwise the service is `GENERAL_AUDIENCE`. Unlike the audience-first variant, this one **can** reach `CHILD_DIRECTED_PRIMARY` on design evidence alone. Every threshold in both variants is inclusive: a share of exactly 0.4000 *is* primary, and a `design_score` of exactly 0.45 *is* mixed.

Escalation has consequences beyond the determination axis, and those consequences are scoped narrowly. When a determination run is made against a service whose `determination_status` is already one of `GENERAL_AUDIENCE`, `MIXED_AUDIENCE` or `CHILD_DIRECTED_PRIMARY`, and the run concludes `CHILD_DIRECTED_PRIMARY`, a chain of consequences follows. The determination axis is written to `CHILD_DIRECTED_PRIMARY`. Because of that, every third-party integration of this service that is currently `APPROVED` and whose `placement_area` is `CHILD_DIRECTED_AREA` is moved `APPROVED → AUDIT_PENDING`, and each such integration's `audit_note` is set to `re-audit forced by child-directed determination`. Because those integrations have left `APPROVED`, the service's `third_party_exposure_points` is recomputed and their points drop out of it, and `open_audit_count` is recomputed upward. Because `open_audit_count` is then at least 1, the posture computation of §2.5 reaches clause 3 and `compliance_posture` becomes `RESTRICTED` — unless a non-null `posture_override` or a frozen-level exposure total takes precedence at an earlier clause. The whole chain settles inside the one request, and `OnlineService.version` advances by exactly one.

The two sibling outcomes are treated differently, and the difference is intentional. A run on a service still at `NOT_ASSESSED` writes the determination axis, refreshes the computed figures and advances `version` by one, but does **not** force any integration re-audit — the first determination establishes a baseline rather than invalidating prior audit decisions. A re-determination out of an already-assessed status that concludes `GENERAL_AUDIENCE` or `MIXED_AUDIENCE` likewise refreshes `design_score`, `audience_child_share`, `determination_variant_applied` and `determination_evaluated_at` and advances `version` by one, with no integration re-audit cascade: only an escalation that lands on `CHILD_DIRECTED_PRIMARY` from an already-assessed status forces re-audit.

Separately from determination, a `ComplianceOfficer` may take manual control of the posture axis by submitting `posture_override` — one of `OPEN`, `RESTRICTED`, `FROZEN`, or null to hand control back to the computation. The key itself is mandatory: a body that omits `posture_override` entirely is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422), because an explicit null is how control is handed back and cannot be expressed by silence. The submitted value is stored, `compliance_posture` is then recomputed through the ordered clauses of §2.5, and `version` advances by one. One guard applies: overriding to `OPEN` while the service still has unfinished integration audits would paper over live risk, so an override of `OPEN` is refused with `ERR_OPEN_AUDITS_PRESENT` (HTTP 409) unless `open_audit_count` is exactly 0. Overrides to `RESTRICTED` and `FROZEN`, and clearing the override to null, carry no such guard.

### 3.3. Age Screening Built in a Neutral Manner

Age screening only counts if it is neutral — that is, if the screen does not nudge, prefill or re-ask its way toward a convenient answer. A `PrivacyEngineer` declares a screen against a `service_id`, choosing a `mechanism` of `NEUTRAL_DOB_ENTRY`, `AGE_RANGE_SELECT` or `ACCOUNT_AGE_INHERIT`, and configuring four behavioural switches. `default_prefilled_age` is the age value the form arrives pre-filled with; it defaults to null and **must** be null for the screen to be neutral. `retry_allowed_on_fail` says whether a visitor screened as under 13 may simply answer again; it defaults to false, and true encourages misstatement. `outcome_session_persisted` says whether the screen's outcome is remembered for the session; it defaults to true, and false lets a visitor re-answer freely. `inherited_from_verified_account` is meaningful only when `mechanism` is `ACCOUNT_AGE_INHERIT` and asserts that the source account was itself age-verified; it defaults to false.

Neutrality is computed, never asserted, and is published on the screen as `neutrality_violation_codes`. The set is derived from the screen's own field values at the moment of the call, and its members appear in this fixed order regardless of which are present: `PREFILLED_AGE` when `default_prefilled_age` is not null; `RETRY_ALLOWED` when `retry_allowed_on_fail` is true; `OUTCOME_NOT_PERSISTED` when `outcome_session_persisted` is false; and `UNVERIFIED_INHERIT` when `mechanism` is `ACCOUNT_AGE_INHERIT` and `inherited_from_verified_account` is false. A fully neutral screen carries an empty list. Note that `UNVERIFIED_INHERIT` cannot arise on a `NEUTRAL_DOB_ENTRY` or `AGE_RANGE_SELECT` screen no matter what `inherited_from_verified_account` says, because the mechanism condition is part of the test.

A new screen is born in `DRAFT` with `revision_round` at 0, `activated_at` null, and `neutrality_violation_codes` already computed from the values supplied. Submitting it for review moves it `DRAFT → IN_REVIEW`, and this transition is reserved to the `PrivacyEngineer`; any other role is refused with `ERR_TRANSITION_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`. Submission recomputes `neutrality_violation_codes` from the screen's current field values, so the reviewer always sees a fresh verdict rather than a stale one.

Approval authority belongs to the `ComplianceOfficer`, and this is where the segregation bites: the engineer who built the screen cannot wave it through. A `ComplianceOfficer` moves the screen `IN_REVIEW → ACTIVE`, and no other role may — again `ERR_TRANSITION_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`. Activation is refused with `ERR_AGE_SCREEN_NOT_NEUTRAL` (HTTP 422) while `neutrality_violation_codes` is non-empty; only an empty list permits activation. A successful activation stamps `activated_at` with the current logical-clock instant and, because a service may have at most one live screen, moves any other screen of the same service that was `ACTIVE` to `RETIRED`.

A `ComplianceOfficer` may instead send the screen back by moving it `IN_REVIEW → REJECTED`, optionally recording a `review_note`; this transition is also officer-only. `review_note` is a plain optional field on the shared status endpoint rather than a field of that one transition: it is stored on the screen, and published back on it, whenever it is supplied on any accepted transition, and a transition that omits it leaves whatever note the screen already carried in place. A screen born in `DRAFT` carries `review_note` null. A rejected screen is not dead. The `PrivacyEngineer` fixes the offending switches and moves it `REJECTED → DRAFT`, which advances `revision_round` by one. That loop is bounded: the transition is permitted only while `revision_round` is strictly below 2, so a screen may go around twice and no further. A third attempt is refused with `ERR_SCREEN_REVISIONS_EXHAUSTED` (HTTP 409). An `ACTIVE` screen may be moved `ACTIVE → RETIRED` by a `ComplianceOfficer` with no preconditions, and the system performs that same retirement automatically when a sibling screen of the service is activated. `RETIRED` is terminal.

### 3.4. The Direct Parental Notice and Its Disclosure Section

Before any consent can be sought, the operator must have a published direct notice to deliver. A `ComplianceOfficer` drafts a notice revision against a `service_id`, giving it an operator-facing `notice_label`, choosing a `delivery_channel` from `EMAIL`, `IN_APP_MODAL`, `POSTAL` or `SCHOOL_INTERMEDIARY`, and listing in `content_keys` the required content sections the notice body actually contains. `content_keys` is itself mandatory and must be an array, though the empty array is legal — that simply describes a notice that contains none of the seven sections — and every entry must be one of the seven canonical section names below, so an unrecognised section name is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than ignored. The draft also carries `disclosed_integration_vendors`, the list of vendor names the notice's third-party disclosure section names; it is an array of arbitrary strings and defaults to an empty list. Drafting is one of the two operations guarded by `expected_service_version` as described in §2.6, and it advances the service's `version` by one.

Seven content sections are required of a direct notice, and the system tracks them in a fixed canonical order: `OPERATOR_IDENTITY_CONTACT`, `ELEMENTS_COLLECTED`, `USE_PURPOSES`, `THIRD_PARTY_DISCLOSURE`, `PARENTAL_RIGHTS`, `NO_CONDITIONING_OF_PARTICIPATION` and `RETENTION_AND_DISPOSAL`. The notice's computed `missing_content_keys` is exactly the required sections that do not appear in `content_keys`, listed in that canonical order rather than in the order the client supplied — and duplicates within `content_keys` are ignored, so listing a section twice neither helps nor hurts. A complete notice has an empty `missing_content_keys`.

A notice begins in `DRAFT` with `published_at` null. Publication moves it `DRAFT → PUBLISHED` and is refused with `ERR_NOTICE_CONTENT_INCOMPLETE` (HTTP 422) while `missing_content_keys` is non-empty. A successful publication does three things beyond setting `status` and stamping `published_at` with the current logical-clock instant. It moves any other notice of the same service that was `PUBLISHED` to `SUPERSEDED`, because a service has at most one live notice at a time. It causes every third-party integration of the same service to recompute its `disclosed_in_notice` flag against this notice's `disclosed_integration_vendors` — so publishing a notice that names a vendor is what unlocks that vendor's audit approval, and publishing one that drops a vendor silently withdraws that unlock. And it advances the service's `version` by one.

Superseding a notice does not rewrite history. Consent requests that already reference a superseded notice keep their `notice_id` unchanged, because the revision that was actually delivered to a parent is a historical fact and not a pointer to be refreshed.

### 3.5. Verifiable Consent: Method Selection by Intended Use

The consent record is the spine of the child-data surface: nothing may be collected without one, and a parent's later rights are exercised through it. A `ComplianceOfficer` opens a request against a `service_id`, referencing the notice revision that will be delivered via `notice_id`, recording the `parent_email` the direct notice goes to and a `child_pseudonym` that is the operator-side identity key for the child. The request declares a non-empty `intended_uses` set drawn from `PARTICIPATION_CORE`, `INTERNAL_OPERATIONS`, `CONTESTS_PRIZES`, `PERSONALIZED_ADVERTISING`, `THIRD_PARTY_DISCLOSURE` and `PUBLIC_POSTING`, and a `consent_method` drawn from `EMAIL_PLUS`, `SCHOOL_AUTHORIZATION`, `KNOWLEDGE_BASED_AUTH`, `SIGNED_FORM_RETURN`, `PHONE_CALL_TRAINED_PERSONNEL`, `CREDIT_CARD_TRANSACTION`, `GOVT_ID_MATCH` or `VIDEO_CONFERENCE_REVIEW`. Creation is the second of the two operations guarded by `expected_service_version` per §2.6, is refused with `ERR_POSTURE_FROZEN` on a frozen service, and advances the service's `version` by one.

Consent quality is expressed as an integer assurance tier, and the requirement rises with what the operator intends to do with the data. Each intended use carries a tier: `PARTICIPATION_CORE` and `INTERNAL_OPERATIONS` are tier 1; `CONTESTS_PRIZES` is tier 2; and the three uses that put a child's data in front of somebody else — `PERSONALIZED_ADVERTISING`, `THIRD_PARTY_DISCLOSURE` and `PUBLIC_POSTING` — are tier 3. The base requirement is the highest tier among the request's `intended_uses`. To that base the system adds one if, at this moment, the service has at least one third-party integration whose `status` is `APPROVED`, whose `collects_persistent_identifier` is true, and whose `integration_type` is `BEHAVIORAL_AD_NETWORK`, `ANALYTICS` or `SOCIAL_PLUGIN`; otherwise it adds nothing. The sum is then capped at 3, and the result is stored as `required_tier_at_creation`.

The word "pinned" is load-bearing there. `required_tier_at_creation` is computed once, from the snapshot of approved integrations taken at the creation instant, and is frozen on the row. It is never re-derived at grant time — not even though approving another integration later would have raised the bump, and not even though approving that integration itself depends on how many disclosure consents the service has already granted. The reciprocal half of that circularity is pinned on the other side, in the integration audit gate of §3.7, which evaluates the approved set *excluding* the integration currently under audit. Alongside the requirement, the request stores `method_assurance_tier`, the tier of the method actually chosen: `EMAIL_PLUS` and `SCHOOL_AUTHORIZATION` are tier 1; `KNOWLEDGE_BASED_AUTH`, `SIGNED_FORM_RETURN` and `PHONE_CALL_TRAINED_PERSONNEL` are tier 2; and `CREDIT_CARD_TRANSACTION`, `GOVT_ID_MATCH` and `VIDEO_CONFERENCE_REVIEW` are tier 3.

A new request is born `INITIATED`, with `notice_delivered_at`, `verification_deadline_at`, `granted_at`, `revoked_at`, `revocation_reason` and `verification_evidence_code` all null and `secondary_confirmation_received` false. Opening a newer request for the same combination of `service_id`, `parent_email` and `child_pseudonym` supersedes the parent's earlier in-flight request: any prior request of that same triple whose `status` is `INITIATED`, `NOTICE_DELIVERED` or `PENDING_VERIFICATION` is moved to `SUPERSEDED` with `revocation_reason` set to `SUPERSEDED_BY_NEW_CONSENT`. Supersession is not a revocation: the superseded row's `revoked_at` stays null, as do the other lifecycle stamps it never earned.

Delivering the direct notice moves the request `INITIATED → NOTICE_DELIVERED` and is where the notice and the consent finally meet. The referenced notice must have `status` of `PUBLISHED`; a draft or superseded revision is refused with `ERR_NOTICE_NOT_PUBLISHED` (HTTP 409). And the channel must fit the method: when `consent_method` is `SCHOOL_AUTHORIZATION`, the referenced notice's `delivery_channel` must be `SCHOOL_INTERMEDIARY`, otherwise the delivery is refused with `ERR_NOTICE_CHANNEL_INCOMPATIBLE` (HTTP 422). A successful delivery stamps `notice_delivered_at` with the current logical-clock instant and sets `verification_deadline_at` to two weeks — fourteen days — after that instant.

Opening verification moves the request `NOTICE_DELIVERED → PENDING_VERIFICATION`, permitted only while the current logical-clock instant is strictly earlier than `verification_deadline_at`; at or past the deadline the transition is refused with `ERR_CONSENT_WINDOW_EXPIRED` (HTTP 409).

Granting consent moves the request `PENDING_VERIFICATION → GRANTED` and carries four preconditions. `method_assurance_tier` must be greater than or equal to `required_tier_at_creation`, otherwise `ERR_CONSENT_METHOD_TIER_INSUFFICIENT`. The current logical-clock instant must still be strictly earlier than `verification_deadline_at`, otherwise `ERR_CONSENT_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. A `verification_evidence_code` must be present in the request, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — this string is an evidence handle recorded at grant, and it is worth noting that a parent's later rights verification is compared against the consent method's tier, not against this string. And when `consent_method` is `EMAIL_PLUS`, a `secondary_confirmation_code` must be supplied, otherwise `ERR_EMAIL_PLUS_CONFIRMATION_MISSING`; email-plus is the one tier-1 method that relies on a second touch to be credible. Those four are listed in business order, not in evaluation order, which is the global order of §2.9: the `verification_evidence_code` requirement is a body-shape check at step 8 and therefore fires ahead of even transition legality, so a grant requested from `INITIATED` without one reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422) rather than `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; then comes the tier check, then the email-plus confirmation at step 13, and the verification window last at step 17 — so a grant that is both past its deadline and short of its tier reports `ERR_CONSENT_METHOD_TIER_INSUFFICIENT`, and one both past its deadline and missing its email-plus code reports `ERR_EMAIL_PLUS_CONFIRMATION_MISSING`. A successful grant stamps `granted_at`, stores the `verification_evidence_code`, and sets `secondary_confirmation_received` to true when a code was supplied. It then supersedes any other `GRANTED` request of the same `service_id`, `parent_email` and `child_pseudonym` triple, moving it `GRANTED → SUPERSEDED` with `revocation_reason` set to `SUPERSEDED_BY_NEW_CONSENT`. Finally it recomputes the service's `active_consent_count` — the number of that service's consent requests whose `status` is `GRANTED` — and `granted_disclosure_consent_count` — the number of granted requests whose `intended_uses` include at least one of `PERSONALIZED_ADVERTISING`, `THIRD_PARTY_DISCLOSURE` or `PUBLIC_POSTING` — and advances the service's `version` by one.

A `ComplianceOfficer` may instead move the request `PENDING_VERIFICATION → DENIED`, optionally recording a `denial_reason`. Denial has no preconditions and changes no counters, since the consent was never granted.

Revocation moves a granted request `GRANTED → REVOKED` and may be requested directly by a `ComplianceOfficer`, optionally with a `revocation_reason` in the body, or may arrive as a cascade from a parental deletion. Either way `revoked_at` is stamped with the current logical-clock instant, and `revocation_reason` is recorded as `PARENT_REFUSAL` for the direct call and `DELETION_FULFILLED` for the deletion cascade. The reason is therefore derived from how the revocation arrived, not from the caller: a `revocation_reason` supplied in the body is accepted without complaint and then ignored, and the stored value is `PARENT_REFUSAL` regardless. Revocation then propagates. First, `active_consent_count` and `granted_disclosure_consent_count` are recomputed. Because of that recomputation, if `granted_disclosure_consent_count` has now reached 0, every third-party integration of this service whose `status` is `APPROVED`, whose `placement_area` is `CHILD_DIRECTED_AREA` and whose `collects_persistent_identifier` is true is moved `APPROVED → AUDIT_PENDING` — the disclosure basis those SDKs were relying on is gone. Because those integrations left `APPROVED`, `third_party_exposure_points` and `open_audit_count` are recomputed and `compliance_posture` is then re-derived through the ordered clauses of §2.5. The service's `version` advances by one.

Across the consent lifecycle, the service's `version` advances by one only on those transitions that actually change one of its counters — a grant and a revocation do; delivering the direct notice, opening verification, denial, expiry and supersession do not.

Consent can also lapse without anyone acting. During a compliance run, any request whose `status` is `NOTICE_DELIVERED` or `PENDING_VERIFICATION` and whose `verification_deadline_at` has been reached or passed is moved to `EXPIRED`. No counters change, since the consent was never granted. `DENIED`, `EXPIRED`, `REVOKED` and `SUPERSEDED` are all terminal.

### 3.6. Participation Activities, Data Minimization and Collection

Data minimization in this system is not an aspiration; it is a whitelist. A `ComplianceOfficer` defines an activity against a `service_id` with an `activity_code` that is expected to be unique within that service — an operator-side naming discipline the server records but does not police, so a second activity reusing a code on the same service is accepted rather than refused, and collection always resolves the whitelist through the `activity_id` it was handed rather than through the code — and enumerates in `participation_required_elements` the data element codes that are genuinely necessary for a child to participate in that activity. That list is mandatory and must be an array whose entries are all legal data element codes, though it may be empty, in which case no core-participation collection can ever clear the whitelist. It is the enforcement surface for core-participation collection.

Collection itself is the `PrivacyEngineer`'s act, and it is the most heavily gated operation in the API. The engineer records one collected element at a time, naming the `service_id`, the `consent_request_id` the collection relies on, the `activity_id` the element was gathered for, a `data_element_code` from `PERSISTENT_IDENTIFIER`, `GEOLOCATION_PRECISE`, `PHOTO_VIDEO_AUDIO`, `VOICE_RECORDING`, `FULL_NAME`, `EMAIL`, `SCREEN_NAME`, `PHONE`, `POSTAL_ADDRESS` or `SCHOOL_NAME`, a `purpose_code` from `PARTICIPATION_CORE`, `INTERNAL_OPERATIONS`, `PERSONALIZED_ADS`, `CONTESTS` or `SUPPORT`, and the `age_screen_outcome` the collecting surface actually observed, which is either `UNDER_13` or `THIRTEEN_PLUS`.

The gates fire in the order laid out in §2.9, and it is worth walking them in business terms. The posture gate comes first among the domain checks: a `FROZEN` service refuses collection outright with `ERR_POSTURE_FROZEN`, and a `RESTRICTED` service refuses it with `ERR_POSTURE_RESTRICTED` unless the `purpose_code` is `PARTICIPATION_CORE` — under restriction, the operator may keep the lights on for participation but may not gather data for advertising, contests, support or internal operations.

Next comes the determination gate. A service whose `determination_status` is still `NOT_ASSESSED` has not established the basis for collecting from children at all, and collection is refused with `ERR_DETERMINATION_NOT_MADE`. A service determined `GENERAL_AUDIENCE` is outside this regime's child-data surface entirely, and collection is refused with `ERR_SERVICE_NOT_CHILD_DIRECTED`. Only `MIXED_AUDIENCE` and `CHILD_DIRECTED_PRIMARY` services may proceed.

Then the age-screening gate, which reads differently for the two permitted determinations. On a `MIXED_AUDIENCE` service, the population is genuinely mixed, so the screen must be real: the service must have an age screen whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, otherwise collection is refused with `ERR_AGE_SCREEN_NOT_ACTIVE`, and the `age_screen_outcome` presented must be `UNDER_13`, because an outcome of `THIRTEEN_PLUS` means the subject is outside the child-data surface and is refused with `ERR_NOT_CHILD_SUBJECT`. On a `CHILD_DIRECTED_PRIMARY` service the logic inverts: every visitor is treated as a child, so no active screen is demanded, but presenting an `age_screen_outcome` of `THIRTEEN_PLUS` is a category error and is refused outright with `ERR_AGE_SCREEN_NOT_PERMITTED`.

Then the consent gates. The referenced consent must be usable: a consent whose `status` is neither `GRANTED` nor `REVOKED` is refused with `ERR_CONSENT_NOT_GRANTED`, and a consent whose `status` is `REVOKED` is refused with `ERR_CONSENT_REVOKED`. Both of those are transition-legality failures and therefore precede the notice check, which means a revoked consent that was also never delivered reports `ERR_CONSENT_REVOKED`. Where the consent is usable, its `notice_delivered_at` must be non-null, otherwise collection is refused with `ERR_DIRECT_NOTICE_NOT_DELIVERED`: no data may be gathered before the parent has actually been given the direct notice.

Then minimization proper, which splits on `purpose_code`. When the purpose is `PARTICIPATION_CORE`, the `data_element_code` must appear in the referenced activity's `participation_required_elements`; anything else is not reasonably necessary for participation and is refused with `ERR_ELEMENT_NOT_REASONABLY_NECESSARY`. For every other purpose the test is instead whether the parent consented to that use, by mapping the purpose onto an intended use and requiring that the consent's `intended_uses` contains it: `INTERNAL_OPERATIONS` maps to the intended use `INTERNAL_OPERATIONS`, `SUPPORT` also maps to `INTERNAL_OPERATIONS`, `CONTESTS` maps to `CONTESTS_PRIZES`, `PERSONALIZED_ADS` maps to `PERSONALIZED_ADVERTISING`, and `PARTICIPATION_CORE` maps to `PARTICIPATION_CORE`. A purpose whose mapped use is absent from `intended_uses` is refused with `ERR_PURPOSE_NOT_CONSENTED`.

Finally, retention. A retention schedule must be in force for this exact combination of element and purpose at the moment of collection, as described in §3.9; if none is, collection is refused with `ERR_NO_RETENTION_SCHEDULE` (HTTP 409).

A record that clears every gate is created with `status` of `COLLECTED`, `scheduled_run_sequence` null and `disposed_at` null. Its `child_pseudonym` is copied from the referenced consent rather than supplied by the caller, which is what makes child-identity-scoped deletion possible later. `collected_at` is stamped with the current logical-clock instant. The governing schedule's `retention_days` and `disposal_method` are copied onto the record as `retention_days_applied` and `disposal_method_applied` — pinned copies, so that a later change to retention policy never retroactively alters the terms a record was collected under — and `disposal_due_at` is set to `collected_at` plus `retention_days_applied` days. The service's `live_child_data_element_count` and `lifetime_collected_element_count` are recomputed, `compliance_posture` is re-derived, and the service's `version` advances by one.

Those two counters differ in a way that matters. `live_child_data_element_count` counts the service's records whose `status` is `COLLECTED` or `SCHEDULED_FOR_DISPOSAL` — the data actually still held — and it rises and falls. `lifetime_collected_element_count` counts every record the service has ever created, including those already `DISPOSED`, and is monotonically non-decreasing.

### 3.7. Third-Party Integrations, Audit Decisions and Exposure Points

Advertising and analytics code running inside a child-directed surface is the sharpest exposure an operator carries, so every such component is declared and audited. A `ComplianceOfficer` declares an integration against a `service_id` with a `vendor_name`, an `integration_type` of `BEHAVIORAL_AD_NETWORK`, `CONTEXTUAL_AD_NETWORK`, `ANALYTICS`, `SOCIAL_PLUGIN` or `CDN`, a boolean `collects_persistent_identifier`, and a `placement_area` of either `CHILD_DIRECTED_AREA` or `GENERAL_AREA`. A newly declared integration has `status` of `DECLARED`, `last_audit_decision_at` null and `audit_note` null; declaration advances the service's `version` by one.

Each integration carries its own `exposure_points`, computed from its declaration: the integration type's base weight — `BEHAVIORAL_AD_NETWORK` 40, `SOCIAL_PLUGIN` 25, `ANALYTICS` 15, `CONTEXTUAL_AD_NETWORK` 5 and `CDN` 0 — doubled when `collects_persistent_identifier` is true and taken as-is when it is false. The service's `third_party_exposure_points` is the sum of `exposure_points` over only those of its integrations whose `status` is `APPROVED`. Integrations that are `DECLARED`, `AUDIT_PENDING` or `BLOCKED` contribute nothing, so a freshly declared integration raises the service's exposure total by zero no matter how heavy it is. That total drives clauses 2 and 4 of the posture computation: 40 or more restricts the service, 80 or more freezes it.

An integration also carries a computed `disclosed_in_notice` flag, true exactly when the service has a notice whose `status` is `PUBLISHED` and whose `disclosed_integration_vendors` contains this integration's `vendor_name`. The flag defaults to false, is computed at declaration time from whatever notice is published at that moment, and is recomputed for every integration of the service whenever a notice of that service is published.

Moving an integration into audit — `DECLARED → AUDIT_PENDING` or `APPROVED → AUDIT_PENDING` — has no preconditions and may be requested by a `ComplianceOfficer` with an optional `audit_note`, or arrive as a cascade from a determination escalation or from the loss of the service's last disclosure consent. `audit_note` is a plain optional field of the shared status endpoint rather than a field of the into-audit transitions: a note supplied on any accepted transition, approvals and blocks included, is stored on the integration, and a transition that omits it leaves the previous note in place. That is also why the disclosure-loss cascade leaves `audit_note` exactly as it was, while the determination-escalation cascade overwrites it with its own fixed string. Either way the service's `open_audit_count` — the number of its integrations whose `status` is `AUDIT_PENDING` — and `third_party_exposure_points` are recomputed, `compliance_posture` is re-derived, and the service's `version` advances by one.

Clearing an audit — `AUDIT_PENDING → APPROVED` — is where the substantive checks live, and there are three. They are described here in business order but evaluated in the global order of §2.9, which is the normative one: the posture gate (step 11) fires first, then the behavioural-advertising gate (step 14), then disclosure (step 18) — so an approval that is both undisclosed and frozen reports `ERR_POSTURE_FROZEN`, and one that is both undisclosed and short of a disclosure basis reports `ERR_BEHAVIORAL_AD_TIER_UNMET`. The integration must be disclosed to parents: `disclosed_in_notice` must be true, otherwise the approval is refused with `ERR_INTEGRATION_NOT_DISCLOSED` (HTTP 422). The service must not be frozen: a `compliance_posture` of `FROZEN` refuses the approval with `ERR_POSTURE_FROZEN` (HTTP 409). And the heaviest case needs a disclosure basis: when the `integration_type` is `BEHAVIORAL_AD_NETWORK` or `SOCIAL_PLUGIN`, and the `placement_area` is `CHILD_DIRECTED_AREA`, and the service's `determination_status` is `MIXED_AUDIENCE` or `CHILD_DIRECTED_PRIMARY`, the service's `granted_disclosure_consent_count` must be at least 1, otherwise the approval is refused with `ERR_BEHAVIORAL_AD_TIER_UNMET` (HTTP 422). That count is evaluated over the service's approved set **excluding the integration currently under audit**, which is what keeps the mutual dependency between consent tiers and integration approval from becoming circular. Integrations of other types, or placed in a `GENERAL_AREA`, or on a `NOT_ASSESSED` or `GENERAL_AUDIENCE` service, are not subject to this third check.

A successful approval stamps `last_audit_decision_at` with the current logical-clock instant, advances the service's `audit_decision_count` by one, adds this integration's `exposure_points` into the service's `third_party_exposure_points`, recomputes `open_audit_count`, re-derives `compliance_posture`, and advances the service's `version` by one.

Blocking an integration — permitted from `AUDIT_PENDING`, `APPROVED` or `DECLARED`, with no preconditions — is the symmetric decision and is recorded with the same bookkeeping: `last_audit_decision_at` is stamped, `audit_decision_count` advances by one, `third_party_exposure_points` is recomputed so that a previously approved integration's points drop out of the total, `open_audit_count` is recomputed, `compliance_posture` is re-derived, and the service's `version` advances by one. `audit_decision_count` therefore counts every accepted approval and every accepted block for the service and is monotonically non-decreasing; sending an integration back into audit does not count as a decision. `APPROVED` is not terminal: an integration may cycle `APPROVED → AUDIT_PENDING → APPROVED` any number of times. `BLOCKED`, however, is the end of the line — no transition leaves it, and an attempt to move a blocked integration anywhere, back into audit included, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). The six transitions the endpoint accepts are therefore exactly `DECLARED → AUDIT_PENDING`, `APPROVED → AUDIT_PENDING`, `AUDIT_PENDING → APPROVED`, and a block from any of `DECLARED`, `AUDIT_PENDING` or `APPROVED`; anything else is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

### 3.8. Parental Rights: Review, Deletion and Refusal of Further Collection

A parent exercises rights through the consent record they hold. Acting as `Parent`, they open a request naming the `service_id`, the `consent_request_id` — which must equal their own `X-User-ID` — a `right_type` of `REVIEW`, `DELETE` or `REFUSE_FURTHER_COLLECTION`, and the `requester_email` they assert. The request is born `SUBMITTED` with `verification_attempt_count` at 0 and `verification_method`, `scope_mode`, `in_scope_record_count` and `fulfilled_at` all null. Parental rights are honoured under every posture; no posture value blocks this workflow.

Verification is driven by a `ComplianceOfficer`. Moving the request `SUBMITTED → VERIFYING` requires a `verification_method` in the body — drawn from the same set of methods as consent — otherwise the call is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; the method is stored on the request. This transition is reserved to the `ComplianceOfficer`, and any other role attempting it is refused with `ERR_TRANSITION_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.

Moving the request `VERIFYING → VERIFIED` applies three cross-checks against the referenced consent, in this order. The consent's `notice_delivered_at` must be non-null, otherwise `ERR_DIRECT_NOTICE_NOT_DELIVERED`: a parent who has not yet been given the direct notice has no consent record to review, delete or refuse. The assurance tier of the request's `verification_method` must be greater than or equal to the tier of the consent's own `consent_method`, using the same tier assignment described in §3.5, otherwise `ERR_RIGHTS_VERIFICATION_TIER_INSUFFICIENT` — a parent may not be identified more loosely than they were when they consented. And `requester_email` must equal the consent's `parent_email`, otherwise `ERR_REQUESTER_EMAIL_MISMATCH`.

Verification may fail. A `ComplianceOfficer` moves the request `VERIFYING → VERIFICATION_FAILED` with no preconditions, which advances `verification_attempt_count` by one. From there the parent — and only the `Parent` role, else `ERR_TRANSITION_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` — may move it `VERIFICATION_FAILED → SUBMITTED` to try again, permitted only while `verification_attempt_count` is strictly below 3; a fourth attempt is refused with `ERR_VERIFICATION_ATTEMPTS_EXHAUSTED` (HTTP 409). Re-submission clears `verification_method` back to null, so a fresh method must be recorded on the next verification attempt rather than the failed one being reused. Alternatively, a `ComplianceOfficer` may close the matter by moving the request `VERIFICATION_FAILED → REJECTED`, with no preconditions and an optional `rejection_reason`. `REJECTED` and `FULFILLED` are both terminal.

Fulfilment moves a verified request `VERIFIED → FULFILLED` and requires a `scope_mode` in the body, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Attempting fulfilment from any status other than `VERIFIED` is refused with `ERR_RIGHTS_REQUEST_NOT_VERIFIED` (HTTP 409) — but only once the body has passed shape, because §2.9 puts step 8 ahead of step 9. A fulfilment attempt that omits `scope_mode` from a `SUBMITTED` request therefore reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422), not the 409, and the same holds for a `VERIFYING` transition that omits `verification_method`. The supplied `scope_mode` selects between two ways of drawing the boundary of the request, and the choice materially changes what happens. Under `PARENT_CONSENT_SCOPE`, the scope is the set of that service's child data records whose `consent_request_id` equals the referenced consent's own identifier and whose `status` is `COLLECTED` or `SCHEDULED_FOR_DISPOSAL` — only what was collected under the single consent this parent holds, so records gathered about the same child under a superseded consent or a school-authorization consent survive. Under `CHILD_IDENTITY_SCOPE`, the scope is the set of records belonging to the referenced consent's own service whose `child_pseudonym` equals the referenced consent's `child_pseudonym` and whose `status` is `COLLECTED` or `SCHEDULED_FOR_DISPOSAL` — every record about that child on the service, across all of its consent records, which is a strictly larger set whenever the child has more than one consent record. Records already `DISPOSED` are excluded from both variants. The cardinality of the resolved set is stored as `in_scope_record_count`, and where records are destroyed they are processed in order of `collected_at` ascending, then `created_at` ascending — an ordering that does not change the resulting set but does fix the sequence of the work.

Every accepted fulfilment stores the supplied `scope_mode` on the request, sets `in_scope_record_count`, stamps `fulfilled_at` with the current logical-clock instant, and advances the service's `version` by exactly one. Beyond that, what fulfilment does depends on `right_type`. For `REVIEW`, nothing is mutated: the resolved count itself is the deliverable, and `in_scope_record_count` tells the parent how many records the operator holds within the scope they chose. For `REFUSE_FURTHER_COLLECTION`, the referenced consent is moved `GRANTED → REVOKED` with `revocation_reason` set to `PARENT_REFUSAL`, and no record is destroyed — the parent is shutting off the tap, not emptying the tank. That revocation is conditional on the consent actually standing at `GRANTED`: where it has already lapsed, been revoked or been superseded, the fulfilment still succeeds and still stamps its own fields, it simply leaves the consent — and the disclosure cascade that would have followed — alone.

For a `right_type` of `DELETE`, a six-link chain runs inside the single request. First, the rights request's own `status` becomes `FULFILLED`. Because of that, every child data record in the resolved scope whose `status` is `COLLECTED` or `SCHEDULED_FOR_DISPOSAL` is moved to `DISPOSED`, with `disposed_at` stamped at the current logical-clock instant and destruction carried out by each record's pinned `disposal_method_applied` rather than by whatever policy is current. Because those records left the live set, the service's `live_child_data_element_count` is recomputed from the surviving records, while `lifetime_collected_element_count` is untouched. Because of that recomputation, if every child data record whose `consent_request_id` equals this rights request's `consent_request_id` now has `status` of `DISPOSED`, that consent is moved `GRANTED → REVOKED` with `revocation_reason` set to `DELETION_FULFILLED` — a consent with nothing left to authorise is spent. The test is over all of that consent's records regardless of the scope that was resolved, and it is satisfied vacuously: a consent that never had a record at all counts as having nothing left, so a `DELETE` fulfilment resolving an empty scope still revokes it. As with refusal, the revocation only happens while the consent stands at `GRANTED`. Because of that revocation, `granted_disclosure_consent_count` is recomputed, and if it has reached 0, every third-party integration of the service whose `status` is `APPROVED`, whose `placement_area` is `CHILD_DIRECTED_AREA` and whose `collects_persistent_identifier` is true is moved `APPROVED → AUDIT_PENDING`. And because those integrations left `APPROVED`, `third_party_exposure_points` and `open_audit_count` are recomputed and `compliance_posture` is re-derived through the ordered clauses of §2.5. `fulfilled_at` is stamped with the current logical-clock instant, and the service's `version` advances by exactly one across the whole chain.

### 3.9. Retention Policy, Compliance Runs and Secure Disposal

A retention schedule is the written answer to "why are you still holding this, and how will you destroy it?" A `ComplianceOfficer` publishes one against a `service_id` for exactly one combination of `data_element_code` and `purpose_code`, using the same element and purpose enumerations that collection uses. It declares `retention_days`, the number of days from collection until disposal is due, which must be at least 1; a `disposal_method` of `CRYPTO_ERASE`, `OVERWRITE`, `PHYSICAL_DESTRUCTION` or `ANONYMIZE`; an effective range from `effective_from` to `effective_to`; and a non-empty `defined_purpose_statement` recording in writing the purpose the retention period serves.

Not every disposal method is admissible for every element, because destroying a voice recording is not the same problem as destroying a screen name. A persistent identifier may be disposed of by `CRYPTO_ERASE`, `OVERWRITE` or `ANONYMIZE`. Precise geolocation permits only `CRYPTO_ERASE` or `OVERWRITE`, as do a full name, an email address and a phone number. Photo, video or audio material and voice recordings permit only `CRYPTO_ERASE` or `PHYSICAL_DESTRUCTION`. A screen name and a school name permit `CRYPTO_ERASE`, `OVERWRITE` or `ANONYMIZE`. A postal address permits `CRYPTO_ERASE`, `OVERWRITE` or `PHYSICAL_DESTRUCTION`. A method outside its element's permitted list is refused with `ERR_DISPOSAL_METHOD_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 422). A `retention_days` below 1, or an `effective_to` that is not strictly later than `effective_from`, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PERIOD` (HTTP 422). Within that shared stage the period checks come first — the range test, then the `retention_days` floor — so a schedule that is both badly ranged and badly methoded reports `ERR_INVALID_PERIOD`.

A schedule's effective range is **closed** at both ends: a schedule is in force on `effective_from` itself and on `effective_to` itself. Because of that closure, adjacency is itself treated as a conflict — schedules for the same `service_id`, `data_element_code` and `purpose_code` must be separated by at least one clear day of handover. A new schedule is accepted only when, against every existing schedule for that same triple, either its `effective_from` is at least one day after that schedule's `effective_to`, or its `effective_to` plus one day is at or before that schedule's `effective_from`. If neither separation holds against some existing schedule — including the same-day adjacency case where one range ends on the very day the next begins — the new schedule is refused with `ERR_RETENTION_SCHEDULE_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). Schedules for different elements, different purposes or different services never conflict.

At collection time, the governing schedule is the one for the record's service, `data_element_code` and `purpose_code` whose closed effective range contains the current logical-clock instant — that is, whose `effective_from` is at or before it and whose `effective_to` is at or after it. Its `retention_days` and `disposal_method` are pinned onto the record as described in §3.6, and `disposal_due_at` follows from `collected_at`. A record is due for disposal when the current logical-clock instant has reached or passed `disposal_due_at`; the boundary is inclusive, so a record whose `disposal_due_at` equals the current instant to the millisecond is due.

Nothing is destroyed spontaneously. Destruction happens inside a **compliance run**, which either a `ComplianceOfficer` or a `PrivacyEngineer` may trigger for one `service_id`. A run first advances the service's `compliance_run_sequence` by one, and that new value is the run's sequence number for everything that follows. It then executes four phases in order.

Phase one expires stale consents: every consent request of the service whose `status` is `NOTICE_DELIVERED` or `PENDING_VERIFICATION` and whose `verification_deadline_at` has been reached or passed becomes `EXPIRED`, with no counter changes.

Phase two schedules due records: every record of the service whose `status` is `COLLECTED` and whose `disposal_due_at` has been reached or passed is moved `COLLECTED → SCHEDULED_FOR_DISPOSAL` and has its `scheduled_run_sequence` set to this run's sequence number. Scheduling does not change `live_child_data_element_count`, because a record awaiting disposal is still held.

Phase three destroys records scheduled by an **earlier** run: a record whose `status` is `SCHEDULED_FOR_DISPOSAL` is moved to `DISPOSED` only when its `scheduled_run_sequence` is strictly less than this run's sequence number. A record that phase two scheduled moments ago in this very run therefore survives it, and is destroyed by the next run instead. Secure disposal always takes two runs: a record first becomes due in one run and is only destroyed in a subsequent one. Destruction stamps `disposed_at` with the current logical-clock instant and is carried out by the record's pinned `disposal_method_applied`, which is the method of record regardless of what the schedule says today. Each destruction lowers the service's `live_child_data_element_count` while leaving `lifetime_collected_element_count` unchanged. Within both phases, the service's records are processed in order of `collected_at` ascending, then `created_at` ascending, and records already `DISPOSED` are skipped.

Phase four refreshes every derived counter on the service and then re-derives `compliance_posture` through the ordered clauses of §2.5. The run advances the service's `version` by one and reports back a summary: the `service_id` it acted on, the `run_sequence` it used, an `expired_consent_count`, a `scheduled_count`, a `disposed_count`, the resulting `live_child_data_element_count_after`, and the resulting `compliance_posture_after`. Those seven fields are the whole of the response `data`: a run is a bookkeeping act rather than a stored resource, so it is never listed, never readable afterwards, and carries no `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` or `version` of its own.

### 3.10. Safe Harbor Participation and the Annual Assessment

Participation in an approved safe harbor program is the operator's demonstration that a third party is checking its work. A `ComplianceOfficer` registers a program with a `program_name` and a boolean `regulator_approved` recording whether the program itself carries regulator approval. Three further settings have defaults: `annual_assessment_required` defaults to true and states whether members owe a yearly assessment; `assessment_validity_days` defaults to 365 — a full year — and sets how long a certification remains valid; and `assessment_grace_days` defaults to 30 and sets how long after expiry a member is still treated as merely due rather than lapsed.

A `SafeHarborAssessor` opens an assessment against a `service_id` and a `program_id` for a given `assessment_year`, and at most one assessment may exist for any combination of those three; a duplicate is refused with `ERR_ASSESSMENT_YEAR_DUPLICATE` (HTTP 409). The new assessment is `DRAFT`, with `preparer_user_id` set to the acting assessor's `X-User-ID`, `certifier_user_id` null, `remediation_round` 0, `findings_open_count` 0, `certified_at` null and `expires_at` null. Opening it recomputes the service's `safe_harbor_status`, which moves the service off `NOT_ENROLLED`, and advances the service's `version` by one.

Submitting an assessment moves it `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` and is reserved to the `SafeHarborAssessor`; any other role is refused with `ERR_TRANSITION_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`. The submission must carry `findings_open_count` in the body as an integer, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and, being a body-shape check, that requirement fires ahead of transition legality, so a submission requested from a status that permits none reports the 422 rather than `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The program must also actually require an annual assessment: a program whose `annual_assessment_required` is false refuses the submission with `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_REQUIRED` (HTTP 409). The supplied count is stored on the assessment.

A `SafeHarborCertifier` reviewing a submitted assessment has three moves. It may send the assessment back by moving it `SUBMITTED → REMEDIATION_REQUIRED`, optionally recording a `remediation_note`; this transition is certifier-only, else `ERR_TRANSITION_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`, is permitted only while `remediation_round` is strictly below 2, else `ERR_REMEDIATION_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED` (HTTP 409), and advances `remediation_round` by one. From `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED` the assessor may resubmit, moving it `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED → SUBMITTED`, which again requires `findings_open_count` in the body — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and overwrites the stored count with the new value. Once `remediation_round` has reached 2 the loop is closed, and the certifier's remaining move from `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED` is to move the assessment to `FAILED`, permitted only when `remediation_round` is 2 or more; a failure attempted before the loop has closed is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409), since no more specific code covers a premature failure. Failure recomputes the service's `safe_harbor_status`, re-derives `compliance_posture`, and advances the service's `version` by one.

Certification is the third move, and the most guarded. Moving an assessment `SUBMITTED → CERTIFIED` requires, in this clause order: `findings_open_count` equal to 0, otherwise `ERR_OPEN_FINDINGS_PRESENT` (HTTP 409); the referenced program's `regulator_approved` to be true, otherwise `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_REGULATOR_APPROVED` (HTTP 409); and the acting `X-User-ID` to differ from the assessment's `preparer_user_id`, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). That order is normative: an attempt that both leaves findings open and is made by the preparer reports `ERR_OPEN_FINDINGS_PRESENT`, never `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. A successful certification records `certifier_user_id` as the acting `X-User-ID`, stamps `certified_at` with the current logical-clock instant, sets `expires_at` to `certified_at` plus the program's `assessment_validity_days` days, recomputes the service's `safe_harbor_status` and `safe_harbor_expires_at`, re-derives `compliance_posture`, and advances the service's `version` by one. `CERTIFIED` and `FAILED` are both terminal.

The service's `safe_harbor_status` is derived, never set, and is one of `NOT_ENROLLED`, `CERTIFIED`, `ASSESSMENT_DUE` or `LAPSED`. The governing assessment is the one for this service whose `status` is `CERTIFIED` with the greatest `assessment_year`; where two such rows share that year, the one with the later `certified_at` governs. If the service has no assessment at all, its `safe_harbor_status` is `NOT_ENROLLED`. If it has assessments but none certified, the status is `ASSESSMENT_DUE`. Otherwise, if the current logical-clock instant is strictly before the governing assessment's `expires_at`, the status is `CERTIFIED`. Otherwise, if the current instant is strictly before that `expires_at` plus the program's `assessment_grace_days` days, the status is `ASSESSMENT_DUE`. Otherwise the status is `LAPSED`, which by clause 5 of §2.5 pulls the service's posture to `RESTRICTED` unless an earlier clause has already decided it. Both clock comparisons are strict: at exactly `expires_at` the service is already `ASSESSMENT_DUE`, and at exactly `expires_at` plus the grace period it is already `LAPSED`. The service's `safe_harbor_expires_at` mirrors the governing assessment's `expires_at`, and is null when no certified assessment exists. Because these conclusions depend on the clock, they are re-derived whenever the logical clock moves, whenever an assessment is certified or failed, and in every compliance run.
