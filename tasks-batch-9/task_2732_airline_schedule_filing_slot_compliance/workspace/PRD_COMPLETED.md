# Airline Schedule Filing & Slot Compliance API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Network planning at a scheduled airline is the discipline of turning a commercial intention — "we want to fly Munich to Lisbon four times a week next winter" — into a legally operable, slot-backed, filed schedule. That translation is governed by a dense stack of external authorities that do not coordinate with one another: airport slot coordinators grant series of take-off and landing rights at fully coordinated airports and take them back when they are not used; national aeronautical authorities grant traffic rights on country pairs with weekly frequency ceilings; distribution systems accept schedule messages only inside published change windows; noise regulators impose night curfews measured in local time; and consumer-protection regulators impose notification and reaccommodation obligations on already-booked passengers whenever a published time moves. A planner who satisfies four of these five constraints has not produced a schedule — they have produced a violation.

This service is the system of record for that entire chain. It holds carriers, stations, seasons, tails, slot series, schedule versions and their legs, slot exchange and transfer negotiations, filing submissions and passenger notification obligations as twelve peer collections, each addressed at the root of the API with no shared prefix and no nesting in URL paths; every parent-to-child traversal is a filtered list. The system's defining behaviour is that it is *immediately consistent in the derived direction*: every utilization percentage, compliance counter, feasibility verdict, charge ledger and obligation count is recomputed inside the same request that mutated its source data, and the response body of that request already reflects the new values. A planner never has to poll for a score.

Two design commitments run through everything that follows. The first is **segregation of duties**: the person who validated a schedule version may not be the person who transmits its filing, and the person who proposed a slot transaction may not be the person who approves it — enforced against persisted user identifiers, so one human wearing two authorized hats is still refused. The second is **conservation**: the filing charge ledger, the attached-operations counter on a slot series, and the open-obligation counter on a leg are all quantities that must return exactly to their prior value when the action that moved them is compensated. A rejected filing must leave the accrued-charge ledger byte-identical to its pre-transmit state, with no drift.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Actors and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint — reads included — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which it acts. If either header is absent, empty, or blank, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before anything else about the request is inspected. If both headers are present but `X-User-Role` is not one of the six declared role names, or is a declared role that the target endpoint does not admit, the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The six roles are spelled exactly `NetworkPlanner`, `PartnerCarrier`, `SlotCoordinator`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `FilingAgent`, and `SystemAdmin`.

- A `NetworkPlanner` is a schedule planner employed by the home carrier. It registers tails, opens schedule versions, adds and retimes and cancels legs, assigns aircraft, attaches slots, returns slot series, and proposes and withdraws slot transactions. It cannot validate its own schedule, cannot approve its own slot transaction, and cannot transmit a filing.
- A `PartnerCarrier` is a counterparty airline. It answers slot exchange and transfer proposals aimed at it, and acknowledges codeshare schedule changes. It has no power over the home carrier's schedule.
- A `SlotCoordinator` is the independent airport slot coordinator. It allocates slot series, approves or denies exchanges and transfers, and runs the season's use-it-or-lose-it audit. It never touches a schedule version.
- A `ComplianceOfficer` runs the compliance gate on a schedule version and owns passenger notification duties.
- A `FilingAgent` files schedules to distribution systems and records their acknowledgements. It cannot validate a schedule, and cannot transmit a schedule it validated itself.
- A `SystemAdmin` administers reference data and the logical clock: carriers, stations, seasons and traffic rights, season transitions, carrier and authority suspensions, and clock advancement. It holds no planning, slot, compliance or filing power whatsoever.

Every role may call every read endpoint. Recording a booked-load snapshot on a leg is shared between `NetworkPlanner` and `ComplianceOfficer`; logging a slot utilization record is shared between `NetworkPlanner` and `SlotCoordinator`; recording a codeshare acknowledgement is shared between `FilingAgent` and `PartnerCarrier`. Everything else is exclusive to exactly one role, as the endpoint table in §4 states authoritatively.

**What `X-User-ID` means, role by role.** Two of the six roles are entity-backed and four are opaque, and the difference decides whether ownership is checked at all.

When `X-User-Role` is `NetworkPlanner`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Carrier` — the carrier the planner works for. Ownership checks on any resource that references a `Carrier` compare that resource's carrier foreign key to `X-User-ID`: the `carrier_id` of the schedule version for schedule and leg writes, the `carrier_id` of the slot series for slot writes, and the `initiating_carrier_id` of the transaction when a proposal is pulled back. When `X-User-Role` is `PartnerCarrier`, `X-User-ID` is likewise the `id` of the acting counterparty `Carrier`; answering a slot transaction compares it to that transaction's `counterparty_carrier_id`, so the initiator cannot answer on the counterparty's behalf even by re-sending the request under the `PartnerCarrier` role. Any mismatch in either case is `ERR_OWNERSHIP_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403), and it applies to every write against a schedule version, a flight leg, a slot holding, or a slot transaction.

When `X-User-Role` is `SlotCoordinator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it — as `recorded_by_user_id` on a slot utilization record, and as `approved_by_user_id` on a slot transaction — and compares it to `proposed_by_user_id` on the same transaction to enforce segregation of duties, but it never compares it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `ComplianceOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier; the server records it as `validated_by_user_id` on the schedule version it validates, and that stored value is what later locks the same person out of transmitting. When `X-User-Role` is `FilingAgent`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier; the server records it as `submitted_by_user_id` when a submission is queued and as `transmitted_by_user_id` when it is transmitted, and the transmit gate requires it to differ from the version's `validated_by_user_id`. When `X-User-Role` is `SystemAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is never compared to any entity's `id` and never persisted on a domain row.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`, followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the uppercase letters `A` through `Z` and the digits `0` through `9`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. It is read from real wall-clock time rather than from the logical clock of §2.5, so it does not move when the clock is set and bears no relation to the instant the request's business rules were evaluated against.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every collection read accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, may be no smaller than 1 and no larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may be no smaller than 0, with no upper bound. A `limit` outside the 1-to-100 band, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) rather than being silently clamped.

Unless a subsection says otherwise, every list endpoint returns its rows sorted by `created_at` in ascending order, so that repeated paging over an unchanged collection is stable. Because `created_at` carries only millisecond precision, several rows written inside the same millisecond would tie on it; that tie is broken by insertion order, so the effective ordering is always exactly the order in which the rows were created. Alongside the two echoed parameters, the `meta` object of a list response carries `total_count` — the number of rows matching the supplied filters before the page window is applied — and `has_more`, a boolean that is true exactly when rows matching the filters remain beyond the current window.

Because the topology is flat, list filters are the only reverse-reachability channel in the system: a foreign key that appears on no creation response is discovered by filtering the target collection on it. Every filter is an exact-equality comparison against the stored value and is case-sensitive; a query parameter the endpoint does not declare is ignored rather than rejected; a filter whose domain is an enumeration must carry a declared member of that enumeration, or the request is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; and a boolean filter accepts only `true`, `false`, `1` or `0`, in any letter case and with surrounding whitespace tolerated, rejecting anything else with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. This matters in three places in particular. The successor version minted when a schedule version is forked is discoverable only by listing schedule versions filtered on `supersedes_version_id`. The successor slot series minted by an executed transfer is discoverable only by listing slot holdings, or by reading the transaction's `successor_slot_holding_id`. And the notification obligations that the system raises as cascade side effects are discoverable only by listing notification obligations filtered on `flight_leg_id`.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Version Counters

Every resource in the system carries a server-minted `id`: a UUID string with no type prefix of any kind. Clients never supply an `id`.

Eleven of the twelve resource types also carry the full set of audit fields. `created_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, recording when the row was first written. `updated_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format, refreshed on every mutation of the row — including mutations that only touch derived fields such as a recomputed utilization percentage or compliance score. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row, again including derived-field-only mutations. When a single request cascades into several rows, every row it actually writes has its own `version` incremented and its own `updated_at` refreshed. Both audit timestamps are read from real wall-clock time rather than from the logical clock of §2.5. A *mutation* here means a write that genuinely changes a value: a re-derivation that recomputes a field to the value the row already holds is not a mutation, and leaves both `version` and `updated_at` untouched. And the increment is once per request, not once per assignment — a row that one request writes several times over, in the handler and again in the cascades and re-derivations that request fires, has its `version` incremented exactly once, and every row that request wrote carries one identical `updated_at` stamp. A creation response therefore always reports `version` 1, because the re-derivation that closes the creating request finds nothing on the new row left to change.

The one exception is the slot utilization record, which logs what actually happened on one dated operation of a slot series. It is immutable: there is no update endpoint for it, and it carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has neither an `updated_at` field nor a `version` field, and those two keys must be absent from its response body rather than present and null.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

Every deadline, expiry and change-window rule in this system is evaluated against a server-side **logical clock** rather than against wall-clock time, so that seasonal calendars behave deterministically regardless of when the service is run. Before any client has set the clock, it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it must never fall back to real wall-clock time. A `SystemAdmin` moves it with an administrative test-driving capability that takes a single field, `to_iso8601`, carrying the target instant; the capability answers with an object of the shape `{ current_utc: string }` reporting the instant now in force, and every subsequent read of the current instant across the whole system returns that value. `to_iso8601` is parsed exactly as any other timestamp field and echoed back canonicalised to three fractional digits. The capability is a setter, not an advance-only ratchet: it accepts an instant earlier than the one currently in force just as readily as a later one.

Advancing the clock has one derivation consequence: any route authority still in `ACTIVE` whose `granted_until_utc` now lies strictly in the past is immediately re-derived to `EXPIRED`, with its `version` incremented. An authority whose `granted_until_utc` falls exactly on the new instant survives, since the comparison is strict, and the re-derivation is one-way — setting the clock back to before an authority's `granted_until_utc` never restores it from `EXPIRED` to `ACTIVE`. Nothing else in the system is time-driven — no sweep cancels legs, no sweep closes seasons, no sweep runs the slot audit.

### 2.6. Precision, Rounding and Conserved Ledgers

There is exactly one rounding mode in this system: **round half up**, applied away from zero at the boundary. A banker's-rounding or round-half-to-even implementation produces observably different values and is wrong.

Percentages and scores — the two slot utilization percentages, the schedule compliance score, and the expedite rate percentage — are decimal numbers carried to exactly 2 decimal places and returned as JSON numbers, never as strings. Monetary values are integer counts of cents and are never fractional: whenever a monetary formula produces a fraction of a cent, that intermediate is rounded half up to a whole number of cents at the step the formula names, and never later. Durations are whole minutes throughout; a duration derived by subtracting two instants is expressed in whole minutes.

Three quantities in the system are **conserved ledgers**, meaning that their value is an invariant of other rows rather than an independently mutable counter, and that every action which moves them has a compensating action which returns them exactly to their prior value with no drift:

- `filing_charge_accrued_cents` on a schedule version always equals the sum of `total_charge_cents` over that version's filing submissions whose `status` is `TRANSMITTED` or `ACKNOWLEDGED`. Transmission adds a submission's total to the ledger; every route by which a submission leaves those two statuses subtracts exactly the same total back off.
- `attached_operations` on a slot series always equals the number of flight legs whose `status` is not `CANCELLED` and which reference that series through either `departure_slot_holding_id` or `arrival_slot_holding_id`. Attaching raises it; detaching, retiming and cancelling lower it back.
- `open_obligation_count` on a flight leg always equals the number of passenger notification obligations against that leg whose `status` is `PENDING`. Raising an obligation increments it; dispatching or waiving one decrements it.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Two writes in this system — and only two — require the caller to prove that it read the resource it is about to disturb. Both carry the expected version as a mandatory, non-optional field in the request body, and both reject a mismatch without mutating anything at all.

The first is **forking a schedule version**. Because a fork branches an entire plan, the request must carry `expected_schedule_version_version` holding the client's last observed `version` of the target schedule version. Every write anywhere in that version's subtree — creating a leg, retiming a leg, cancelling a leg, assigning a tail, attaching a slot, validating, transmitting, acknowledging, or any compliance re-derivation triggered by those — increments the schedule version's `version`. If the supplied value does not equal the current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SCHEDULE_VERSION` (HTTP 412): the target version does not move to `SUPERSEDED`, no successor row is created, and no counter changes. Re-reading the version and re-attempting with the fresh number succeeds.

The second is **proposing a slot transaction**. Proposing an exchange or transfer encumbers the offered slot series, which makes that series the one genuinely contended resource in the domain. The request must carry `expected_slot_holding_version` holding the client's last observed `version` of the offered slot holding. Any intervening write to that holding — a leg attaching to it, a leg detaching from it, a return, an audit outcome, or an earlier proposal that took the encumbrance — has already advanced its `version`, so the proposal is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SLOT_HOLDING` (HTTP 412): no transaction row is created and `pending_transaction_id` on the holding is left exactly as it was, owned by whichever proposal legitimately holds it.

No other endpoint in the system takes an expected-version field. Attaching a slot, retiming a leg, validating, transmitting and approving a transaction are all guarded by state preconditions rather than by version comparison, and supplying a version field to them has no effect.

### 2.8. Segregation of Duties

Two operations enforce that the *person* acting is not the person who performed the paired upstream step, even though the *role* is correct. Both are rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403), and in both cases a second user holding the same role but a different `X-User-ID` succeeds on the byte-identical request.

Transmitting a filing submission is refused when the acting `X-User-ID` equals the `validated_by_user_id` stored on the submission's schedule version at validation time. Approving or denying a slot transaction is refused when the acting `X-User-ID` equals that transaction's `proposed_by_user_id`. The second case matters precisely because `SlotCoordinator` and `NetworkPlanner` identifiers are drawn from the same opaque namespace: in a small deployment one person can plausibly hold both hats, and the check exists to refuse exactly that.

### 2.9. Rejection Precedence

Many requests in this system violate more than one rule at once, and which error a client sees is part of the contract rather than an implementation detail. Every request is evaluated against the rule bands below **in the order listed**, and the first band that produces an objection determines the response; no later band can mask an earlier one. Within a band, checks are evaluated in the order the band's own narrative gives.

1. **Authentication.** Both headers present and non-blank, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. **Role admission.** The role is one of the six and is admitted by this endpoint, else `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.
3. **Request shape.** Every required field present; every enum field carrying a declared member; every timestamp parsing as ISO 8601 with a `Z` suffix; every integer field an integer inside its declared range; every monetary and count field non-negative; `days_of_week` a non-empty string of distinct digits drawn from 1 through 7; `limit` between 1 and 100 and `offset` non-negative. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Four further shape conventions belong to this band. A request that sends no body at all, or an empty one, is read as an empty JSON object — so an endpoint that requires no fields accepts a bodyless request — while a body that will not parse as JSON, or parses to something other than a JSON object, is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. An explicit `null` counts as absence: in an optional field it selects that field's documented default, and in a required field it is the missing-field failure. A JSON boolean is never an acceptable integer, even where the underlying language would treat it as one. Fields the endpoint does not declare are ignored rather than rejected, so a body carrying an extra key is validated purely on the keys the endpoint reads. Timestamps must carry the literal `Z` suffix — a numeric offset such as `+00:00` is rejected outright — while the seconds and the fractional part are both optional, and whatever precision arrives is stored and re-emitted canonicalised to exactly three fractional digits. Finally, the global-uniqueness constraints on a carrier's `iata_code` and `icao_code`, a station's `iata_code`, a season's `season_code` and a tail's `registration` are enforced in this band as well, so a collision is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than any conflict status.
4. **Existence.** Path identifiers are resolved strictly left to right, and that resolution is the one part of this band that runs *ahead* of band 3 rather than after it: a request naming a path resource that does not exist reports its 404 even when the request body is malformed as well. Only the foreign keys named in the body sit in band-4 position proper, resolved after the body has passed its shape check. A carrier that does not resolve gives `ERR_CARRIER_NOT_FOUND`; a station, `ERR_STATION_NOT_FOUND`; a season, `ERR_SEASON_NOT_FOUND`; a tail, `ERR_AIRCRAFT_NOT_FOUND`; a schedule version, `ERR_SCHEDULE_VERSION_NOT_FOUND`; a flight leg — including one named as an upstream connection — `ERR_FLIGHT_LEG_NOT_FOUND`; a slot holding — including a counter-offered one — `ERR_SLOT_HOLDING_NOT_FOUND`; a utilization record, `ERR_SLOT_UTILIZATION_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`; a slot transaction, `ERR_SLOT_TRANSACTION_NOT_FOUND`; a filing submission, `ERR_FILING_SUBMISSION_NOT_FOUND`; a route authority, `ERR_ROUTE_AUTHORITY_NOT_FOUND`; a notification obligation, `ERR_NOTIFICATION_OBLIGATION_NOT_FOUND`. All of these are HTTP 404.
5. **Ownership.** For the two entity-backed roles, the acting `X-User-ID` must own the target, else `ERR_OWNERSHIP_MISMATCH`.
6. **Optimistic concurrency.** The expected-version field must match, else `ERR_STALE_SCHEDULE_VERSION` on a fork or `ERR_STALE_SLOT_HOLDING` on a proposal. This band sits *above* every state and deadline check, so a stale fork request against an already-superseded version reports the stale-version error rather than a state error.
7. **Season and carrier lifecycle.** `ERR_SEASON_NOT_OPEN`, then `ERR_SEASON_STATE_INVALID`, then `ERR_SLOT_AUDIT_ALREADY_RUN`, then `ERR_SEASON_AUDIT_PENDING`, then `ERR_CARRIER_SUSPENDED`.
8. **Target-resource state.** `ERR_SCHEDULE_VERSION_STATE_INVALID`, then `ERR_SCHEDULE_VERSION_NOT_READY`, then `ERR_SCHEDULE_BLOCKED`, then `ERR_FLIGHT_LEG_STATE_INVALID`, then `ERR_SLOT_HOLDING_STATE_INVALID`, then `ERR_SLOT_ENCUMBERED`, then `ERR_SLOT_TRANSACTION_STATE_INVALID`, then `ERR_FILING_SUBMISSION_STATE_INVALID`, then `ERR_NOTIFICATION_STATE_INVALID`, then `ERR_FILING_IN_FLIGHT`.
9. **Regulatory and operational safety.** `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, then `ERR_CURFEW_VIOLATION`, then `ERR_MAINTENANCE_OPPORTUNITY_INSUFFICIENT`, then `ERR_NOTIFICATION_CHANNEL_INSUFFICIENT`.
10. **Fleet, rotation, connection, gate and slot constraints.** In order: `ERR_AIRCRAFT_NOT_IN_SERVICE`, `ERR_FLEET_TYPE_MISMATCH`, `ERR_AIRCRAFT_CARRIER_MISMATCH`, `ERR_ROTATION_CONTINUITY_BROKEN`, `ERR_TURN_TIME_VIOLATION`, `ERR_DAILY_BLOCK_CAP_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_CONNECTION_STATION_MISMATCH`, `ERR_MIN_CONNECT_TIME_VIOLATION`, `ERR_GATE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_STATION_NOT_COORDINATED`, `ERR_SLOT_REQUIRED_AT_COORDINATED_STATION`, `ERR_SLOT_STATION_MISMATCH`, `ERR_SLOT_CARRIER_MISMATCH`, `ERR_SLOT_TIME_MISMATCH`, `ERR_SLOT_SERIES_OVERSUBSCRIBED`, `ERR_SLOT_SERIES_CONFLICT`, `ERR_SELF_TRANSACTION_NOT_ALLOWED`, `ERR_COUNTER_SLOT_REQUIRED`.
11. **Traffic rights, codeshare and precedence.** In order: `ERR_ROUTE_AUTHORITY_MISSING`, `ERR_ROUTE_AUTHORITY_INACTIVE`, `ERR_CABOTAGE_PROHIBITED`, `ERR_FREQUENCY_CAP_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_CODESHARE_NOT_SYNCHRONIZED`, `ERR_HISTORIC_PRECEDENCE_PROTECTED`.
12. **Windows and deadlines.** In order: `ERR_LEG_OUTSIDE_SEASON_WINDOW`, `ERR_SLOT_RETURN_DEADLINE_PASSED`, `ERR_SLOT_TRANSACTION_DEADLINE_PASSED`, `ERR_CHANGE_WINDOW_CLOSED`, `ERR_LATE_NOTIFICATION_UNREFERENCED`.
13. **Fiscal.** Last of all, `ERR_FILING_CHARGE_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`.

Three conflict pairs deserve to be called out explicitly, because a naive implementation gets each of them backwards.

A slot return attempted against a season that is no longer `OPEN` **and** whose slot return deadline has already passed must report `ERR_SEASON_NOT_OPEN`, not `ERR_SLOT_RETURN_DEADLINE_PASSED`: the season being shut is the more fundamental objection than the deadline being blown, which is why the season-lifecycle band sits far above the deadline band. A transmission that is both blocked on compliance **and** over the version's filing charge budget must report `ERR_SCHEDULE_BLOCKED`, not `ERR_FILING_CHARGE_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`: a schedule that must not be filed at all is never a budget question, which is why the fiscal band is last. And a leg that both lands inside a night curfew **and** has no traffic right at all must report `ERR_CURFEW_VIOLATION`, not `ERR_ROUTE_AUTHORITY_MISSING`: a noise-abatement breach is a physical and community objection and outranks a paperwork objection.

### 2.10. Immediate Re-derivation and Cascade Depth

Nothing in this system is derived lazily. When a request mutates a source row, every dependent quantity is recomputed inside that same request, in a fixed order, and the response body already reflects the result. Retiming one leg, for example, re-derives that leg's block duration, then re-walks its assigned tail's whole rotation, then re-derives the tail's feasibility verdict and both maintenance-opportunity figures, then re-derives every counter on the owning schedule version, then re-derives that version's compliance score, then re-derives its compliance posture, and then — if the posture moved to blocked — rejects the version's live filing submissions and reverses their charges. That is a five-link chain fired by a single field change, and every link is observable in the same response or in an immediately following read. Two things about *when* that recomputation runs are worth pinning. Every mutating request closes by re-deriving the whole world rather than only the subtree beneath the row it changed, driving the derived fields of every collection to a fixed point before the response is serialised — so a counter that had drifted anywhere is corrected by the next write of any kind, wherever that write landed. The fixed order that closing sweep visits collections in is: flight legs, then slot series, then tails, then filing submissions, then schedule versions, then carriers, then seasons, each collection walked in row creation order; and the entire sweep is repeated from the top until one whole pass changes nothing, which is what makes the result a genuine fixed point independent of where in the order the originating write landed. Read endpoints, by contrast, never re-derive anything at all: a GET returns exactly the values the last write left on the row and can never itself repair a stale figure.

Cascades that walk a set of sibling rows are **conditional**: they touch only the rows whose current state makes the transition meaningful, and leave the rest untouched rather than forcing them. Two instances are load-bearing. When a version returns from filed to validated, its legs that are currently filed move back to slot-backed while legs that have already fallen into refile-required are skipped entirely. And when a version's compliance posture drops to blocked, its filing submissions that are transmitted or acknowledged are rejected and their charges reversed, while a submission still merely queued is left exactly as it is — it never accrued against the charge ledger and never reached a distribution system, so there is nothing to compensate. The block surfaces for that queued submission later, when someone attempts to transmit it.

### 2.11. Enumerations

For reference, the complete set of enumerated values used in the system. A value outside these sets is a malformed request.

A carrier's `status` is `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`. A station's `coordination_level` is `LEVEL_1_NON_COORDINATED`, `LEVEL_2_FACILITATED`, or `LEVEL_3_COORDINATED`. A season's `status` is `DRAFT`, `OPEN`, `CLOSED`, or `ARCHIVED`. A tail's `status` is `IN_SERVICE`, `MAINTENANCE_GROUNDED`, or `RETIRED`, and its `rotation_feasibility_status` is `UNCHECKED`, `FEASIBLE`, `INFEASIBLE_CONTINUITY`, `INFEASIBLE_TURN`, or `INFEASIBLE_BLOCK_CAP`. A schedule version's `filing_status` is `DRAFT`, `VALIDATED`, `FILED`, `SUPERSEDED`, or `WITHDRAWN`, and its `compliance_state` is `CLEAN`, `WARNED`, or `BLOCKED`. A flight leg's `status` is `DRAFT`, `ASSIGNED`, `SLOT_BACKED`, `FILED`, `REFILE_REQUIRED`, or `CANCELLED`. A slot series' `movement_type` is `ARRIVAL` or `DEPARTURE`, and its `holding_status` is `HELD`, `RETURNED`, `TRANSFERRED_OUT`, `LOST`, or `EXPIRED`. A utilization record's `outcome` is `OPERATED`, `CANCELLED_CARRIER`, `CANCELLED_ATC`, `NOT_OPERATED`, or `RETURNED_IN_TIME`. A slot transaction's `kind` is `EXCHANGE` or `TRANSFER`, and its `status` is `PROPOSED`, `COUNTERPARTY_ACCEPTED`, `COUNTERPARTY_REJECTED`, `COORDINATOR_DENIED`, `EXECUTED`, or `WITHDRAWN`. A filing submission's `distribution_system` is `IATA_SSIM`, `OAG`, or `GDS_ATPCO`; its `change_window` is `STANDARD`, `EXPEDITED`, or `EMERGENCY`; its `status` is `QUEUED`, `TRANSMITTED`, `ACKNOWLEDGED`, or `REJECTED`; and its `codeshare_sync_status` is `NOT_REQUIRED`, `PENDING_ACK`, or `SYNCHRONIZED`. A route authority's `freedom_type` is `THIRD_FOURTH`, `FIFTH`, or `SEVENTH`, and its `status` is `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED`, or `EXPIRED`. A notification obligation's `trigger_kind` is `RETIME`, `CANCELLATION`, or `SLOT_LOSS`; its `obligation_tier` is `ADVISORY` or `MANDATORY_REACCOMMODATION`; its `status` is `PENDING`, `DISPATCHED`, or `WAIVED`; and its `dispatched_channel`, when present, is `EMAIL`, `SMS`, or `AGENCY_QUEUE`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Reference Data: Carriers, Stations and Traffic Rights

Before any planning can happen, a `SystemAdmin` populates the reference layer. A **carrier** is registered with its `iata_code` — exactly two uppercase alphanumeric characters, globally unique — its `icao_code` of exactly three uppercase letters, also globally unique, and the `country_code` of the state that issued its operating licence, exactly two uppercase letters. A collision on either designator is a request-shape objection reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The optional `is_home_carrier` flag marks the single carrier whose network this deployment plans and defaults to false when omitted; partner airlines that appear only as slot-transaction counterparties or codeshare marketing carriers are registered the same way with the flag left off. Every carrier is created in `status` `ACTIVE` with `held_series_count` at 0. That counter is derived, not stored by hand: it is always the number of slot series whose `carrier_id` is this carrier and whose `holding_status` is `HELD`, and it is recomputed whenever a series is created, returned, transferred away, or taken by the season audit.

Suspending a carrier's operating licence takes a `reason` of one to five hundred characters and drives its `status` from `ACTIVE` to `SUSPENDED`; the carrier must currently be `ACTIVE`, and a carrier already `SUSPENDED` is refused with `ERR_CARRIER_SUSPENDED`. The `reason` is validated but not persisted — a carrier row carries no suspension-reason field of any kind. Suspension is not merely a flag — it reaches into the carrier's planning work. Every schedule version belonging to that carrier whose `filing_status` is `DRAFT` or `VALIDATED` is driven to `WITHDRAWN`, which in turn cancels each of those versions' non-cancelled legs and detaches their slot series exactly as a voluntary withdrawal would. A version already in `FILED`, `SUPERSEDED` or `WITHDRAWN` is left alone. The carrier's own `version` increments by 1. From then on, any attempt to open a new schedule version, register a tail, allocate a slot series, or propose a slot transaction under that carrier is refused with `ERR_CARRIER_SUSPENDED`. `SUSPENDED` is terminal for a carrier; there is no reinstatement.

A **station** is an airport, and its record is dense because almost every operational constraint in the system reads from it. It is registered with an `iata_code` of exactly three uppercase letters, globally unique, the `country_code` it sits in, and its `coordination_level` under the IATA Worldwide Slot Guidelines: `LEVEL_1_NON_COORDINATED`, `LEVEL_2_FACILITATED`, or `LEVEL_3_COORDINATED`. Only the third of those has slots at all. A second station claiming an `iata_code` already registered is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and a station row carries no lifecycle status field of its own — there is no way to close or suspend a station. Local clock times are derived from `utc_offset_minutes`, a fixed offset from UTC in minutes that must be no less than -720 and no more than 840. Physical capacity is `gate_count`, the number of contact gates available for concurrent occupancy, which must be at least 1.

The night curfew is expressed as a pair of local minute-of-day values. `curfew_start_local_minutes` is the local minute at which the curfew opens and is treated as **inclusive**; `curfew_end_local_minutes` is the local minute at which it closes and is treated as **exclusive**. Both are integers from 0 through 1439 and both default to null, meaning the station has no curfew; when one is supplied the other must be supplied too. When the end value is numerically smaller than the start value the window wraps past local midnight — a station with a start of 1380 and an end of 330 is closed from 23:00 local until 05:30 local.

Three further station fields feed calculations elsewhere. `min_connect_domestic_minutes` is the published minimum connect time when neither leg of a connection is international, defaulting to 35 minutes; `min_connect_international_minutes` is the minimum when either leg is international, defaulting to 60 minutes; both must be non-negative. `min_turn_override_minutes` is a station-imposed floor on aircraft ground time between two consecutive legs, defaulting to 0 and never negative — it competes with the fleet's own minimum turn and the larger of the two wins. Finally `maintenance_capable` is a boolean, defaulting to false, that is true exactly when line maintenance can be performed on the ground at this station; only ground time at a maintenance-capable station counts toward a tail's check opportunity.

**Traffic rights** are held as route authorities, each granted by a `SystemAdmin` to one carrier through its `carrier_id`, over a directed country pair given as `origin_country_code` and `dest_country_code` — each exactly two uppercase letters, and the two must differ. The `freedom_type` conveyed is `THIRD_FOURTH` (carriage between the carrier's own state and the other state), `FIFTH` (carriage between two foreign states on a route touching the carrier's own), or `SEVENTH` (standalone carriage entirely outside the carrier's own state). The authority sets a `weekly_frequency_cap` of at least 1, being the maximum number of non-cancelled legs **the carrier as a whole** may place on that country pair inside one ISO week — the count is taken across every schedule version belonging to that carrier, whatever each version's own `filing_status`, and never within a single version alone — and a validity window from `granted_from_utc` through `granted_until_utc`, where the end instant must be strictly later than the start. Every authority is created `ACTIVE` with `suspension_reason` null.

An authority leaves `ACTIVE` by two routes. A `SystemAdmin` may suspend it with a `reason` of one to five hundred characters, recording that reason on `suspension_reason`; the resulting status is chosen from the clock rather than fixed, so the authority moves to `SUSPENDED` when its `granted_until_utc` is at or after the current logical instant and to `EXPIRED` when the clock has already passed it. That endpoint carries no state precondition whatsoever: an authority already `SUSPENDED`, or already `EXPIRED`, may be suspended again, which simply overwrites `suspension_reason`, re-derives the status the same way and increments the row's `version`. Alternatively, advancing the logical clock past `granted_until_utc` re-derives `status` from `ACTIVE` to `EXPIRED`. Neither route is a cascade: suspending or expiring an authority does not touch a single existing leg. It is a forward-looking guard — every *subsequent* leg creation on that country pair is refused with `ERR_ROUTE_AUTHORITY_INACTIVE`, while legs already in the plan stay exactly as they are.

### 3.2. The Season Calendar: Opening, Auditing and Archiving

A **season** is the scheduling period every other artefact hangs off, identified by a `season_code` of two to four characters such as "W26" or "S27", globally unique — any characters at all are accepted within that length, with no case or character-class constraint, and a collision is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. It runs from `start_date` to `end_date`, both ISO 8601 UTC instants with the end strictly later than the start. Two deadline-calendar instants sit *before* the season opens for traffic: `slot_return_deadline_utc`, the last moment at which a slot series may be handed back in time, and `filing_change_window_close_utc`, the moment after which standard-urgency filings are refused. Both must fall strictly before `start_date`, and the return deadline must be at or before the filing change-window close. A season is created in `status` `DRAFT`, with `slot_audit_completed_at_utc` and `audit_reference` both null and `held_series_count` at 0 — that last counter being, at any instant, the number of slot series whose `season_id` is this season and whose `holding_status` is `HELD`.

Advancing a season is a one-step-at-a-time affair, driven by a `SystemAdmin` supplying `to_status`. The only legal path is `DRAFT` to `OPEN` to `CLOSED` to `ARCHIVED`, and the requested target must be the **immediate** successor of the current status. Asking a `DRAFT` season to go straight to `CLOSED`, or asking a season to transition to the status it already holds, is refused with `ERR_SEASON_STATE_INVALID`. Each accepted transition increments the season's `version` by 1. `ARCHIVED` is terminal.

What each status unlocks is the substance of the season machine. `OPEN` is the working state: allocating a slot series, opening a schedule version, adding a leg, returning a series, proposing a slot transaction and approving one all require the governing season to be `OPEN`, and are otherwise refused with `ERR_SEASON_NOT_OPEN`. `CLOSED` is the audit state, and the *only* status in which the use-it-or-lose-it audit may run; it simultaneously freezes returns and slot transactions, since those need `OPEN`. `ARCHIVED` retires the season, and its transition carries the one genuinely consequential season cascade: every slot series of the season still in `HELD` whose `retains_historic_precedence` is true stays `HELD` and has its `historic_precedence` set to true, carrying the grandfather claim forward into the next season, while every series still in `HELD` whose `retains_historic_precedence` is false moves `HELD → EXPIRED` with `historic_precedence` set to false. A series whose `retains_historic_precedence` is still null — one no audit ever scored — is treated exactly as false and expires. Each series touched has its own `version` incremented. Because archival depends on the audit having produced those verdicts, a `CLOSED → ARCHIVED` request while `slot_audit_completed_at_utc` is still null is refused with `ERR_SEASON_AUDIT_PENDING`.

**The use-it-or-lose-it audit** is the enforcement moment of the whole slot regime, and it is run once per season by a `SlotCoordinator` who supplies the coordinator's own `audit_reference` string of one to one hundred and twenty characters. It requires the season to be `CLOSED` — otherwise `ERR_SEASON_STATE_INVALID` — and it requires `slot_audit_completed_at_utc` to still be null, because the sweep is deliberately **not** idempotent and a second run is refused with `ERR_SLOT_AUDIT_ALREADY_RUN`. It answers with a summary object of the shape `{ audited_count: integer, retained_count: integer, lost_count: integer, audit_reference: string, audited_slot_holding_ids: string[] }`, where `audited_count` is the number of `HELD` series of the season it examined, `retained_count` and `lost_count` partition that number, and `audited_slot_holding_ids` lists the identifiers it examined, in the order those series were created.

Every `HELD` series of the season is scored on `utilization_pct` — the as-operated variant described in §3.5, and never the as-filed variant. A series whose `utilization_pct` is at least 80.00 percent is retained: `retains_historic_precedence` is written true and the series stays `HELD`. A series strictly below 80.00 percent is taken away, moving `HELD → LOST` with both `historic_precedence` and `retains_historic_precedence` set to false. Losing a series cascades: every leg referencing it whose `status` is `SLOT_BACKED` or `FILED` moves to `REFILE_REQUIRED` with the referencing slot identifier nulled; the series' `at_risk_of_loss` is recomputed to false, because the risk has now been *realised* rather than remaining pending; every affected schedule version has its counters, `schedule_compliance_score` and `compliance_state` recomputed; each detached leg whose booked load across all three cabins is greater than zero raises a passenger notification obligation with `trigger_kind` `SLOT_LOSS`; and both the owning carrier's `held_series_count` and the season's `held_series_count` are recomputed. The audit stamps `slot_audit_completed_at_utc` with the logical clock and records the supplied `audit_reference` on the season.

### 3.3. Fleet Registration, Rotation Feasibility and Maintenance Opportunity

A `NetworkPlanner` registers each **tail** it intends to schedule. A tail belongs to a carrier through `carrier_id` and to a maintenance base through `base_station_id`. Its `registration` — a tail mark such as "D-AHXA" of four to eight characters — is globally unique, and its `fleet_type_code` is an equipment code of three or four uppercase alphanumeric characters such as "B738". `seats` records the total installed seat count and must be at least 1. Three operating parameters govern how the tail may be flown: `min_turn_minutes`, the fleet-standard minimum ground time between two consecutive legs, which must be non-negative; `max_daily_block_minutes`, the maximum summed block time the tail may fly inside one UTC calendar date, which must be at least 1; and `required_check_minutes`, the ground minutes a maintenance opportunity must provide for the tail's next scheduled check, which must be non-negative. The boolean `maintenance_check_required` defaults to false and decides whether that check requirement is merely reported or actually enforced. The tail's `status` describes its airworthiness and availability and defaults to `IN_SERVICE`; a tail may also be registered as `MAINTENANCE_GROUNDED` or `RETIRED`. On registration `assigned_leg_count` is 0, both `maintenance_opportunity_minutes` and `cumulative_ground_minutes` are 0, and `rotation_feasibility_status` is `UNCHECKED`. A `registration` duplicating an existing tail's is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and that duplicate check runs ahead of resolving either foreign key; `carrier_id` is then resolved before `base_station_id`, and only after both resolve is the carrier's being `SUSPENDED` grounds for `ERR_CARRIER_SUSPENDED`. Registering a tail is not an ownership-checked write: a `NetworkPlanner` may register a tail against any carrier that exists, not only the one its own `X-User-ID` names.

`assigned_leg_count` is always the number of legs whose `assigned_aircraft_id` is this tail and whose `status` is not `CANCELLED`. `rotation_feasibility_status` is `UNCHECKED` only while that count is 0; once the tail carries at least one leg it must hold one of `FEASIBLE`, `INFEASIBLE_CONTINUITY`, `INFEASIBLE_TURN` or `INFEASIBLE_BLOCK_CAP`.

**Assigning a tail to a leg** advances the leg from `DRAFT` to `ASSIGNED` and is the moment the whole rotation is re-walked. The request names a single `aircraft_id`. The leg must currently be in `DRAFT`, or the action is refused with `ERR_FLIGHT_LEG_STATE_INVALID`. Three tail-level checks then run in order: the tail's `status` must be `IN_SERVICE`, and a `MAINTENANCE_GROUNDED` or `RETIRED` tail is refused with `ERR_AIRCRAFT_NOT_IN_SERVICE`; the tail's `fleet_type_code` must equal the leg's own `fleet_type_code`, else `ERR_FLEET_TYPE_MISMATCH`; and the tail's `carrier_id` must equal the `carrier_id` of the leg's schedule version, else `ERR_AIRCRAFT_CARRIER_MISMATCH`. Only then is the rotation evaluated.

**The rotation walk.** Take every leg whose `assigned_aircraft_id` is this tail and whose `status` is not `CANCELLED`, including the leg now being assigned. That set is scoped by the tail alone and by nothing else: legs drawn from *any* schedule version the tail appears on walk together in one rotation, and a version's own `filing_status` is never consulted — so the legs of a version that has since been forked to `SUPERSEDED` still sit in the tail's rotation and can still make a new assignment infeasible, precisely because superseding a version does not cancel its legs. Sort that set by `dep_utc` ascending, breaking ties by `created_at` ascending — never by identifier. Partition the sorted set into groups by the UTC calendar date of each leg's `dep_utc`, and evaluate every check **within a single date group only**, so two legs on different UTC dates never form a pair at all: a 23:30 arrival followed by a 00:15 departure the next day is neither a turn violation nor a continuity break, and the discontinuity between the last leg of one date and the first leg of the next is simply not examined.

Each leg's `block_minutes` is the whole number of minutes from its `dep_utc` to its `arr_utc`. The required turn after an inbound leg is the larger of the tail's `min_turn_minutes` and the `min_turn_override_minutes` of the station the inbound leg arrives at, and then, when that inbound leg is international, 15 further minutes are added on top. A destination station whose `min_turn_override_minutes` is 75 therefore beats a tail whose `min_turn_minutes` is 45, giving a required turn of 75; and a 45-minute fleet turn after an international inbound sector at a station with no override becomes 60 minutes.

The three checks run in this pinned order and **short-circuit on the first failure**, recording the offending leg. The ordering is check-major rather than group-major: continuity is tested across *every* date group before any turn comparison is attempted anywhere, and every turn comparison across every date group is attempted before any block-cap sum is taken — so a tail whose earliest date group breaks its turn time while a later date group breaks continuity reports `INFEASIBLE_CONTINUITY`, not `INFEASIBLE_TURN`. The offending leg recorded is, for continuity and for turn, the **later** leg of the failing consecutive pair, and for the block cap the leg whose own `block_minutes` carried the running date-group total past the ceiling. First **continuity**: for every consecutive pair inside a date group, the earlier leg's `dest_station_id` must equal the later leg's `origin_station_id`; otherwise the tail would have to teleport, the verdict is `INFEASIBLE_CONTINUITY`, and the assignment is refused with `ERR_ROTATION_CONTINUITY_BROKEN`. Second **turn**: for every consecutive pair, the later leg's `dep_utc` must be no earlier than the earlier leg's `arr_utc` plus the required turn computed above — the comparison is inclusive, so a tail arriving at 09:00 with a required turn of 45 minutes may legally depart again at exactly 09:45, while 09:44 is refused with `ERR_TURN_TIME_VIOLATION` and the verdict is `INFEASIBLE_TURN`. Third **daily block cap**: within each date group the summed `block_minutes` must be no greater than the tail's `max_daily_block_minutes`, so ten 60-minute legs against a cap of 600 are feasible while an eleventh 60-minute leg on the same date reaches 660 and is refused with `ERR_DAILY_BLOCK_CAP_EXCEEDED` under the verdict `INFEASIBLE_BLOCK_CAP`. When every date group clears all three checks the verdict is `FEASIBLE`.

The walk is a fixed-point re-derivation rather than a one-off check. Assigning, retiming or cancelling a single leg re-derives that leg's `block_minutes`, then re-walks every date group of the tail, then re-derives `rotation_feasibility_status`, `maintenance_opportunity_minutes` and `cumulative_ground_minutes`, then re-derives every schedule-version counter that references the tail. Because the walk is per-date-group it always terminates in one pass and has no cyclic dependency.

**Maintenance opportunity** is computed from the same pinned leg ordering, and it has two variants that deliberately disagree. Within each UTC date group, walk consecutive leg pairs and form the ground interval that begins at the earlier leg's `arr_utc` and ends at the later leg's `dep_utc`, with the start instant included and the end instant excluded. An interval whose end instant is at or before its start instant — the later leg departing at, or even before, the moment the earlier one arrives — is not a window at all and is discarded before the maintenance-capability test is even reached. Keep the remaining interval only when the station where the aircraft is actually sitting — the earlier leg's `dest_station_id` — has `maintenance_capable` true; discard it otherwise. Kept intervals at the same station that overlap or merely touch are merged into one window before measurement, so two ground periods separated by a gap of exactly zero form one continuous window rather than two.

`maintenance_opportunity_minutes` is the **contiguous-window** variant: the length in whole minutes of the longest single merged kept interval across all date groups. `cumulative_ground_minutes` is the **cumulative-ground** variant: the sum in whole minutes of every merged kept interval across all date groups. Consider a tail flying five legs on one UTC date between a maintenance-capable base and a station that is not maintenance-capable, arriving at the non-capable station at 07:00 and departing again at 07:45, sitting at the capable base from 08:45 until 11:15, back at the non-capable station from 12:15 to 13:00, then at the capable base from 14:00 to 16:00. The two intervals at the non-capable station, of 45 minutes each, are discarded outright. The two at the capable base measure 150 minutes and 120 minutes. The contiguous-window figure is therefore 150 while the cumulative-ground figure is 270 — a divergence of 120 minutes on the same tail at the same instant. A tail with only one leg has no consecutive pair and reports 0 for both variants, and a tail whose entire ground time is spent at stations that are not maintenance-capable reports 0 for both even though it sat for hours.

That divergence is load-bearing, because only one variant gates anything. When a tail's `maintenance_check_required` is true, the schedule-version compliance gate refuses to validate unless `maintenance_opportunity_minutes` — the contiguous window — is at least `required_check_minutes`, rejecting otherwise with `ERR_MAINTENANCE_OPPORTUNITY_INSUFFICIENT`. A heavy check cannot be split across two separate ground periods, so `cumulative_ground_minutes` is never sufficient: with a `required_check_minutes` of 240, the tail above fails on its contiguous 150 even though its cumulative 270 would have cleared. When `maintenance_check_required` is false both figures are still computed and exposed for planners, but neither ever gates validation.

Assigning a tail has one further shortcut worth stating plainly. After the assignment lands and the leg reaches `ASSIGNED`, if the leg needs no slot at all — because neither its origin nor its destination station is `LEVEL_3_COORDINATED` — or if every slot it does need is already attached, the leg immediately auto-advances `ASSIGNED → SLOT_BACKED` inside the very same request. The assignment also recomputes `assigned_leg_count`, `rotation_feasibility_status`, `maintenance_opportunity_minutes` and `cumulative_ground_minutes` on the tail, recomputes the owning version's counters, `schedule_compliance_score` and `compliance_state`, and increments the leg's `version` by 1.

A read-only computed view is available for any tail, giving the planner the rotation walk as the server sees it: an object of the shape `{ rotation_feasibility_status: string, maintenance_opportunity_minutes: integer, cumulative_ground_minutes: integer, ordered_leg_ids: string[], first_violation_leg_id: string, required_turn_minutes: integer, total_block_minutes: integer }`. Here `ordered_leg_ids` is the pinned leg order described above, `first_violation_leg_id` is the offending leg when the verdict is infeasible and null when it is not, `required_turn_minutes` is the required turn computed at the first consecutive pair — meaning the pair formed by the first two legs of the earliest UTC date group that holds at least two legs, reported as 0 when the tail has no consecutive pair anywhere and so no turn was ever required — and `total_block_minutes` is the summed block time of the tail's non-cancelled legs.

### 3.4. Building a Schedule Version and Its Legs

A **schedule version** is one candidate plan for one carrier in one season. A `NetworkPlanner` opens it by naming the `carrier_id` and the `season_id` and giving it a planner-visible `version_label` of one to forty characters such as "W26-R1", unique within the pair of carrier and season. That uniqueness constraint is enforced in the request-shape band exactly as the other global uniqueness constraints of §2.9 are: a collision answers `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and because the constraint sits in band 3 it is evaluated *before* either `carrier_id` or `season_id` is resolved, so a duplicate label naming a carrier that does not exist reports the shape objection rather than the 404. Once the label clears, `carrier_id` is resolved before `season_id`. The optional `filing_charge_budget_cents` sets the ceiling on cumulative filing charges the version may accrue; it defaults to 100000 cents and must be non-negative. Opening a version requires the season to be `OPEN` and the carrier to be `ACTIVE`, in that order. The new row starts with `filing_status` `DRAFT`, `compliance_state` `CLEAN`, `schedule_compliance_score` at 100.00, `filing_charge_accrued_cents` at 0, `validated_by_user_id` null, `supersedes_version_id` null, and every counter at 0.

Seven derived counters live on the version and are re-derived on every mutation that could change them. `leg_count` is the number of legs whose `schedule_version_id` is this version **including cancelled ones** — a cancelled leg stays on the version's manifest, so this counter only ever rises. That is a deliberate exception: every other counter on the version excludes cancelled legs. `unslotted_leg_count` is the number of non-cancelled legs that are not fully slotted, meaning either the leg's `status` is `REFILE_REQUIRED` or some slot the leg requires has no holding attached; a leg whose origin and destination are both outside `LEVEL_3_COORDINATED` requires no slots at all and therefore never counts here unless it is `REFILE_REQUIRED`. `curfew_violation_count` is the number of non-cancelled legs whose `curfew_waiver_reference` is non-null. `mct_violation_count` is the number of non-cancelled legs that designate an upstream connection through `connects_from_leg_id` and whose `mct_shortfall_minutes` is greater than 0. `gate_overflow_count` is the number of station-and-UTC-date pairs whose peak gate occupancy, computed over this version's non-cancelled legs, exceeds that station's `gate_count`. `at_risk_slot_count` is the number of **distinct** slot series referenced by this version's non-cancelled legs, through either `departure_slot_holding_id` or `arrival_slot_holding_id`, whose `at_risk_of_loss` is true. And `schedule_compliance_score` is the weighted score described in §3.7.

**Forking a version.** When a plan needs to branch, a `NetworkPlanner` supersedes it. The request carries a new `version_label`, the mandatory `expected_schedule_version_version` described in §2.7, and optionally a fresh `filing_charge_budget_cents` for the successor — omitted, the successor inherits the parent's current budget rather than falling back to the 100000-cent default. The label's uniqueness is tested against every *other* version of the same carrier and season but explicitly **excludes the fork's own target**, so a successor may carry the label the parent already holds and the two rows then legitimately share it; any other version's label in that pair still collides with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, evaluated ahead of the ownership, concurrency and state checks. Forking is also the one schedule-version write that is gated on neither lifecycle: it does not require the governing season to still be `OPEN` and it does not require the carrier to still be `ACTIVE`. A version may be forked from `DRAFT`, from `VALIDATED` or from `FILED`; a version already `SUPERSEDED` or `WITHDRAWN` is refused with `ERR_SCHEDULE_VERSION_STATE_INVALID`. On success the target moves `DRAFT → SUPERSEDED`, `VALIDATED → SUPERSEDED` or `FILED → SUPERSEDED` as the case may be, its own `version` increments by 1, and a brand-new schedule version is created in `filing_status` `DRAFT` with `compliance_state` `CLEAN`, the same `carrier_id` and `season_id` as its parent, `supersedes_version_id` pointing back at the parent, and **zero legs** — a fork never copies the manifest. `SUPERSEDED` is terminal.

**Withdrawing a version** is how a planner abandons a plan that has not been filed. The request carries a `reason` of one to five hundred characters, which is validated but not persisted — a schedule version row carries no withdrawal-reason field, and the only reason the cascade records anywhere is the literal `SCHEDULE_WITHDRAWN` it writes onto each cancelled leg. It is permitted from `DRAFT` and from `VALIDATED` only, and it is refused with `ERR_FILING_IN_FLIGHT` while any filing submission of the version is still `QUEUED` or `TRANSMITTED` — a filing that is either awaiting despatch or awaiting an answer must be resolved first. Withdrawal drives `filing_status` to `WITHDRAWN`, which is terminal, and cascades in two steps: every non-cancelled leg of the version moves to `CANCELLED` with `cancellation_reason` set to the literal `SCHEDULE_WITHDRAWN`, and each of those legs detaches from both `departure_slot_holding_id` and `arrival_slot_holding_id`, which in turn recomputes each affected series' `attached_operations`, `utilization_pct_as_filed` and `at_risk_of_loss`. The version's `version` increments by 1. On the passenger side a withdrawal is a pure reversal rather than a disruption: the legs it cancels raise **no** `CANCELLATION` notification obligation at all, unlike a leg cancelled on its own, and instead every obligation already sitting in `PENDING` against each of those legs is waived with `waiver_reason` set to the literal `CHANGE_REVERTED`, driving each leg's `open_obligation_count` back to 0. The identical cascade is what fires when the owning carrier is suspended.

**Adding a leg.** A leg may only be added while the version's `filing_status` is `DRAFT`, and the governing season must be `OPEN`. The planner supplies the `schedule_version_id` it belongs to, a marketing `flight_number` of three to eight characters such as "HX412", the `origin_station_id` and `dest_station_id` — which must differ from each other — the scheduled off-block instant `dep_utc` and on-block instant `arr_utc` as ISO 8601 UTC timestamps with three fractional digits, and the `fleet_type_code` the leg is planned on, written in the same form as a tail's — three or four uppercase alphanumeric characters. The arrival must be strictly later than the departure and the elapsed time between them may not exceed 1080 minutes, which is eighteen hours. That 1080-minute ceiling is a request-shape constraint reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, as is naming the same station for both endpoints, and both are evaluated in band 3 ahead of resolving any identifier the body names. The body's own foreign keys then resolve in a pinned order: every element of `marketing_carrier_ids` first, in the order the array gives them, then `origin_station_id`, then `dest_station_id`, then `schedule_version_id`, and `connects_from_leg_id` last of all. Three optional fields complete the payload: `connects_from_leg_id`, naming the inbound leg of a designated passenger connection and defaulting to null when the leg starts an itinerary; `marketing_carrier_ids`, and `curfew_waiver_reference`, both described below. The leg is created in `status` `DRAFT` with `assigned_aircraft_id`, `departure_slot_holding_id` and `arrival_slot_holding_id` all null, all three booked-cabin counters at 0, `cancellation_reason` null, `open_obligation_count` at 0, and `block_minutes`, `is_international`, `connect_minutes` and `mct_shortfall_minutes` all computed. Creation increments the version's `leg_count` and re-derives `schedule_compliance_score` and `compliance_state`.

`block_minutes` is the whole number of minutes from `dep_utc` to `arr_utc`. `is_international` is true exactly when the `country_code` of the origin station differs from the `country_code` of the destination station. Both `dep_utc` and `arr_utc` must fall inside the season's window — the departure no earlier than `start_date` and the arrival no later than `end_date`, with the departure strictly before the arrival — and a breach in any of those three respects is `ERR_LEG_OUTSIDE_SEASON_WINDOW`.

`marketing_carrier_ids` is an **array** of carrier identifiers, not a single-valued foreign key: it lists the partner airlines that place their own designator on this leg as a codeshare. It defaults to an empty array, may hold at most four elements, may not contain duplicates, and every element must be an existing carrier other than the version's own `carrier_id` — an element naming the version's own carrier is a request-shape objection reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, raised once the version itself has resolved and therefore after any 404 the array or the version identifier would have produced. Every carrier listed anywhere on a version's legs must acknowledge a filing before that filing may be transmitted, which is the subject of §3.8.

**Curfew compliance** is evaluated at both endpoints of the leg, in the station's own local time. Take the leg's `dep_utc` at the origin station and its `arr_utc` at the destination station; convert each to a local minute-of-day by adding the station's `utc_offset_minutes` to the instant's UTC minute-of-day and reducing the result modulo 1440, so that offsets pushing past either end of the day wrap correctly. The instant's UTC minute-of-day is its hour times sixty plus its minute and nothing more — seconds and fractional seconds are discarded outright, so an arrival at 22:59:59.999Z and one at 22:59:00.000Z are indistinguishable to this test. That local minute falls inside the curfew when it is at or after `curfew_start_local_minutes` and strictly before `curfew_end_local_minutes`; when the end value is smaller than the start value the window wraps midnight and the minute falls inside it when it is at or after the start **or** strictly before the end. A station with both curfew fields null has no window and can never produce a violation, and neither can a station whose start and end are the *same* number: that is an empty half-open window, not a twenty-four-hour one. The check is also skipped entirely, at both endpoints, the moment a waiver reference is in force — the waiver is consulted before either station is examined. If either endpoint lands inside its station's curfew, the leg is refused with `ERR_CURFEW_VIOLATION` — unless the request supplies `curfew_waiver_reference`, a regulator waiver reference of four to thirty-two characters. Supplying the waiver does not make the leg clean; it converts a hard rejection into a compliance penalty. The check is skipped, the leg is accepted, and the leg counts toward the version's `curfew_violation_count` for the entire time it remains non-cancelled.

**Traffic rights** are checked next, and only for legs that need them. A leg whose origin and destination countries differ is international, and it must have a matching route authority granted to the acting carrier on that country pair, matched as an **unordered** pair so that one authority covers both directions; with no such authority the leg is refused with `ERR_ROUTE_AUTHORITY_MISSING`. The matching authority must further be in `status` `ACTIVE` and must cover the leg's departure, meaning `granted_from_utc` is at or before `dep_utc` and `dep_utc` is at or before `granted_until_utc`; a `SUSPENDED` or `EXPIRED` authority, or a departure outside the window, gives `ERR_ROUTE_AUTHORITY_INACTIVE`. A leg whose origin and destination countries are identical **and** both different from the operating carrier's own `country_code` is domestic carriage inside a foreign state — cabotage — and is prohibited unless the matching authority carries `freedom_type` `SEVENTH`; otherwise it is refused with `ERR_CABOTAGE_PROHIBITED`. Because a route authority's two country codes must always differ, no authority can literally name the degenerate pair such a leg forms, so cabotage matches differently: the candidates are every authority of the acting carrier that names that one country in **either** position, whatever its other country is, and those candidates then run the same `ACTIVE`-and-in-window filter before the `SEVENTH` test is applied to what survives. Where more than one authority survives every filter, the governing one — the single authority whose `weekly_frequency_cap` the next check reads — is the earliest-created of them. Finally the weekly ceiling: counting the acting carrier's non-cancelled legs on that unordered country pair whose departure falls inside the new leg's ISO week — from Monday at 00:00:00Z through the following Monday at 00:00:00Z, the later boundary excluded — the total including the new leg may not exceed the authority's `weekly_frequency_cap`, or the leg is refused with `ERR_FREQUENCY_CAP_EXCEEDED`. That last check is an aggregate one: each individual leg is fully authorised and only the weekly total breaches. Outside the cabotage case the authority's `freedom_type` is never consulted at all: any authority covering the unordered country pair authorises the leg whatever freedom it nominally conveys, so a `THIRD_FOURTH` grant will carry a leg between two states that are both foreign to the carrier just as a `FIFTH` one would. And a leg whose origin and destination countries are identical **and** equal to the carrier's own `country_code` — domestic carriage in the carrier's home state — skips this whole paragraph, requiring no authority of any kind.

**Minimum connect time.** When `connects_from_leg_id` is null, `connect_minutes` is null and `mct_shortfall_minutes` is 0, and nothing further is checked. When it is supplied, the upstream leg must belong to the *same* schedule version and its `dest_station_id` must equal this leg's `origin_station_id`; a mismatch on either count is `ERR_CONNECTION_STATION_MISMATCH`, and it is checked **before** any connect-time arithmetic is attempted. `connect_minutes` is then the whole number of minutes from the upstream leg's `arr_utc` to this leg's `dep_utc`, and may be negative when this leg departs before its inbound arrives. The applicable minimum comes from the connecting station — the station where the passenger changes aircraft: it is that station's `min_connect_international_minutes` when the upstream leg is international **or** this leg is international, and its `min_connect_domestic_minutes` only when neither is. A single international sector on either side of the connection therefore promotes the whole connection to the international minimum. `mct_shortfall_minutes` is the amount by which the connect time falls short of that minimum, floored at 0 — so a connection meeting or exceeding the minimum has a shortfall of 0, since the minimum is inclusive. Both figures continue to be re-derived from the upstream leg's stored `arr_utc` for as long as `connects_from_leg_id` points at it, whatever has since become of that upstream leg: **cancelling the inbound does not dissolve the connection**, so a downstream leg whose upstream has been cancelled keeps its `connect_minutes` and its shortfall and keeps counting toward the version's `mct_violation_count`. The only way to clear such a violation is to retime one of the two legs or to cancel the downstream one. At a station publishing a 35-minute domestic minimum, an inbound arriving at 10:00 and this leg departing at 10:35 gives a connect of 35 and a shortfall of 0; departing at 10:34 gives a connect of 34, a shortfall of 1, and rejection with `ERR_MIN_CONNECT_TIME_VIOLATION`. At a station publishing a 60-minute international minimum, an inbound international sector arriving at 10:00 and a domestic onward sector departing at 10:45 gives a connect of 45 against a required 60 — a shortfall of 15 and a rejection, even though 45 comfortably clears the domestic 35.

**Gate capacity** is the other aggregate check at creation, and it is computed per station per UTC calendar date across **every** carrier and every schedule version at that station, not just the acting one. Each leg contributes a half-open occupancy interval at each end: a departure occupies its origin station from 45 minutes before `dep_utc` up to but excluding `dep_utc`, and an arrival occupies its destination station from `arr_utc` up to but excluding 30 minutes after `arr_utc`. A leg that both arrives at and departs from the same station contributes both intervals independently. Which UTC calendar date an interval is weighed against is decided by the **movement instant itself**, never by the span the interval covers: a departure interval belongs to the date of `dep_utc` even when its 45 minutes of preparation begin on the previous UTC date, and an arrival interval belongs to the date of `arr_utc` even when its 30 minutes of release run over into the next. A consequence worth stating is that two intervals lying on opposite sides of midnight are never compared with each other at all, however much clock time they genuinely share. Peak concurrency is the maximum number of intervals simultaneously active, computed by sweeping the interval endpoints in ascending instant order and processing every ending before any beginning at the same instant — which is exactly what makes the intervals half-open, so two intervals that merely touch are never concurrent. The station is over capacity when peak concurrency is strictly greater than its `gate_count`. At a station with two gates, departures at 08:00, 08:20 and 08:40 produce intervals beginning at 07:15, 07:35 and 07:55; at 07:55 all three are active, peak concurrency is 3, and the third leg is refused with `ERR_GATE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Drop the last of those and peak concurrency is 2 — exactly at capacity, and accepted. A departure at 08:00 and another at 08:45 never overlap, because the first interval has already ended at the instant the second begins; but an arrival at 08:00 and a departure at 08:45 both occupy the instant 08:00 and do overlap, giving a peak of 2.

**Retiming a leg** is deliberately more permissive than creating one, and the asymmetry is the point: a planner must be able to move a leg into a congested bank and then *see* the penalty rather than being blocked from expressing the move. The request may carry any of `dep_utc`, `arr_utc`, `flight_number` and `curfew_waiver_reference`; each omitted field keeps the value the leg already holds, and because an explicit `null` counts as absence under §2.9 a retime can set or replace `curfew_waiver_reference` but can never **clear** one — a waiver, once on a leg, is only removable by cancelling the leg. The season-window check and the curfew check still apply in full — a retime outside the season is `ERR_LEG_OUTSIDE_SEASON_WINDOW` and a retime into a curfew without a waiver is `ERR_CURFEW_VIOLATION` — and so does the 1080-minute ceiling on the resulting elapsed time, reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The governing season's own lifecycle, by contrast, is not consulted: unlike adding a leg, retiming one is permitted while the season sits in `DRAFT`, `CLOSED` or `ARCHIVED` just as readily as while it is `OPEN`. But a retime that creates a gate overflow is **accepted** and increments the version's `gate_overflow_count`, and a retime that creates a connect-time shortfall is **accepted** and increments the version's `mct_violation_count`. A retime is permitted from any status except `CANCELLED`; on a cancelled leg it is refused with `ERR_FLIGHT_LEG_STATE_INVALID`.

What a retime does to the leg depends on where the leg was, and a change to either `dep_utc` or `arr_utc` triggers the full chain. A leg in `FILED` moves `FILED → REFILE_REQUIRED`. A leg in `SLOT_BACKED` moves `SLOT_BACKED → ASSIGNED` instead, because it has not been filed yet — but it is now missing a slot it requires, so the version's `unslotted_leg_count` still rises by 1 whenever the leg requires any slot at all; when the leg requires none, it immediately re-enters `SLOT_BACKED` inside the same request. A leg in `ASSIGNED` stays `ASSIGNED`, a leg in `DRAFT` stays `DRAFT`, and a leg in `REFILE_REQUIRED` stays `REFILE_REQUIRED`. In every one of those cases the retimed leg's `block_minutes` is recomputed and **both** `departure_slot_holding_id` and `arrival_slot_holding_id` are set to null — a retimed leg loses its slot backing outright, because a slot series is anchored to a time of day and a retimed movement no longer matches it.

That detachment starts a five-link chain. Because the slot identifiers were nulled, each detached series recomputes `attached_operations` and `utilization_pct_as_filed`. Because those fell, any such series still `HELD` with at least one remaining attached operation whose `utilization_pct_as_filed` is now below 80.00 percent flips `at_risk_of_loss` to true. Because that flipped, the version's `at_risk_slot_count` rises, `schedule_compliance_score` is recomputed and `compliance_state` moves to `BLOCKED`. And because the version is now blocked, every filing submission of that version whose `status` is `TRANSMITTED` or `ACKNOWLEDGED` moves to `REJECTED` with `rejection_reason` set to the literal `SCHEDULE_BLOCKED`, the version's `filing_charge_accrued_cents` is reduced by that submission's `total_charge_cents`, and the version's remaining `FILED` legs move back to `SLOT_BACKED`. Separately from that chain, a retime also fans out to the passenger side: when the leg's booked load across all three cabins is greater than zero and the magnitude of the retime reaches 30 minutes, a passenger notification obligation with `trigger_kind` `RETIME` is raised, and the leg's `open_obligation_count` increments. The retime also re-derives the assigned tail's `rotation_feasibility_status`, `maintenance_opportunity_minutes` and `cumulative_ground_minutes`, and increments the leg's `version` by 1. Note that a retime touching only `flight_number` or `curfew_waiver_reference` updates those fields and recomputes the version's counters without triggering the slot-detachment chain. The whole detachment-and-notification chain is keyed strictly on `dep_utc` or `arr_utc` actually differing from the stored value; supplying either field with the value the leg already carries is not a retime and fires none of it. The write itself, however, is unconditional: a `PATCH` that changes nothing at all — even one carrying an empty body — still counts as a write to the leg, so its `version` increments by 1 and its `updated_at` is restamped exactly as a substantive retime's would be.

**Cancelling a leg** takes a `reason` and is permitted from any status except `CANCELLED`; a second cancellation of an already-cancelled leg is refused with `ERR_FLIGHT_LEG_STATE_INVALID`. Whatever the starting status — `DRAFT`, `ASSIGNED`, `SLOT_BACKED`, `FILED` or `REFILE_REQUIRED` — the effects are identical: `status` becomes `CANCELLED`, which is terminal, `cancellation_reason` records the supplied reason, and both slot holdings are detached, while every other field the leg carries is left exactly as it stands — in particular a cancelled leg keeps its `assigned_aircraft_id` and its `connects_from_leg_id` on the row for the record, even though it has dropped out of every derivation those two fields feed, which recomputes each series' `attached_operations`, `utilization_pct_as_filed` and `at_risk_of_loss`. That detachment is the exact compensation of the attachment, returning `attached_operations` to its pre-attach value with no drift. The assigned tail's `assigned_leg_count`, `rotation_feasibility_status` and both maintenance-opportunity figures are re-derived, and so are the version's counters, `schedule_compliance_score` and `compliance_state`. If the cancelled leg had any booked passengers at all, a passenger notification obligation is raised with `trigger_kind` `CANCELLATION`, `change_magnitude_minutes` of 240 and `obligation_tier` `MANDATORY_REACCOMMODATION`. The leg's `version` increments by 1.

**Booked loads** reach the system through a snapshot action shared by `NetworkPlanner` and `ComplianceOfficer`, which overwrites `booked_first`, `booked_business` and `booked_economy` with the currently booked passenger count in each cabin. All three are **mandatory** on the request — the action is a full replacement of the cabin vector rather than a partial patch, so omitting any one of them, or sending it as `null`, is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and each must be a non-negative integer. All three fields are system-managed in the sense that no other request writes them and they start at 0. A snapshot is permitted on a leg in any status except `CANCELLED`. Taking a snapshot is never itself a triggering change: it raises no notification obligation however large the movement in booked load, and it waives none either. These three numbers are the passenger-impact input that every notification obligation copies at the instant it is created.

### 3.5. Slot Holdings at Coordinated Airports and Utilization Tracking

Only a `SlotCoordinator` may record a slot series, and only at a fully coordinated airport: if the named station's `coordination_level` is anything other than `LEVEL_3_COORDINATED` the request is refused with `ERR_STATION_NOT_COORDINATED`, because slots simply do not exist at schedules-facilitated or non-coordinated fields. The coordinator supplies the `station_id`, the `season_id`, the `carrier_id` that holds the series, the `movement_type` — either `ARRIVAL` or `DEPARTURE` — the anchor instant `slot_time_utc`, the day mask `days_of_week`, and the number of consecutive weeks `series_weeks` between 1 and 26 inclusive. Two optional fields carry grandfather rights forward: `historic_precedence`, a boolean defaulting to false that marks the series as carrying rights from a prior season, and `historic_predecessor_holding_id`, naming the series those rights were inherited from and defaulting to null for a newly allocated series. The four identifiers the body names are resolved in a fixed band-4 order that is not the order they are described in: `carrier_id` first, then `station_id`, then `season_id`, then `historic_predecessor_holding_id` when one is supplied — so a body naming two unknown entities reports the earlier failure in that order, and an unknown carrier alongside an unknown station is `ERR_CARRIER_NOT_FOUND`. Beyond resolving, the named predecessor is not constrained in any other way: it may be a series in any `holding_status`, held by any carrier, at any station and in any season. Two lifecycle preconditions then run ahead of the coordination-level and conflict checks below: the named season must be `OPEN`, or the request is refused with `ERR_SEASON_NOT_OPEN`, and the holding carrier must be `ACTIVE`, or it is refused with `ERR_CARRIER_SUSPENDED`.

`days_of_week` is a **string mask**, not an array: one to seven characters drawn from the digits "1" through "7" in strictly ascending order with no repeats, where 1 is Monday and 7 is Sunday, so a Monday-Wednesday-Friday series is written "135". `slot_time_utc` is stored as a full instant but only its **UTC time-of-day** is ever compared against anything; its date is informational. A new series is created in `holding_status` `HELD` with `planned_operations` equal to `series_weeks` multiplied by the number of digits in `days_of_week`, `attached_operations` at 0, `credited_operations` at 0, both `utilization_pct` and `utilization_pct_as_filed` at 0.00, `at_risk_of_loss` false, `pending_transaction_id` null, and `returned_reason`, `returned_before_deadline` and `retains_historic_precedence` all null. Creating a series recomputes the holding carrier's `held_series_count` and the season's `held_series_count`. A twenty-six week series on a five-day mask is 130 planned operations; a single-week Sunday-only series is 1.

Two `HELD` series may not collide. A new series is refused with `ERR_SLOT_SERIES_CONFLICT` when some other series already in `holding_status` `HELD` shares this one's `station_id`, `season_id`, `movement_type` **and** the UTC time-of-day of its `slot_time_utc` — that time-of-day compared to the full stored precision, hour, minute, second and fractional second alike, so two series thirty seconds apart at the same station never collide — while the digit sets of the two `days_of_week` masks intersect in at least one day. The `carrier_id` plays no part in the test: a carrier's new series collides with its own existing series on exactly the same terms as with a rival's. Masks that overlap on a single day conflict; masks that are disjoint — "135" against "246" at the same station, season, movement type and time-of-day — coexist happily. A series whose `holding_status` has already left `HELD` never blocks a new allocation, which is precisely how a coordinator re-allocates a returned or lost series to another airline.

**Attaching a slot to a leg** is the act that turns a planned movement into a slot-backed one. A `NetworkPlanner` names the `slot_holding_id` and the `movement_type` the holding is to back, and on success either `departure_slot_holding_id` or `arrival_slot_holding_id` is written on the leg — overwriting whatever series that movement was previously backed by, since the action carries no guard against a movement that is already slotted, and the displaced series then recomputes its own `attached_operations` downward. The leg itself must be in a status that can still take a slot: `DRAFT`, `ASSIGNED` or `REFILE_REQUIRED`, and a leg already `FILED` or `CANCELLED` is refused with `ERR_FLIGHT_LEG_STATE_INVALID` — a check that, being a leg-state objection, precedes all six of the series checks below under §2.9. Six substantive checks then apply, in the order given by §2.9. The series must still be `HELD`, or the request fails with `ERR_SLOT_HOLDING_STATE_INVALID`; a series `RETURNED`, `TRANSFERRED_OUT`, `LOST` or `EXPIRED` can never back a flight. The series must be unencumbered — `pending_transaction_id` null — or the request fails with `ERR_SLOT_ENCUMBERED`, because a series in the middle of an exchange or a transfer has already been committed elsewhere. The series' `station_id` must equal the leg's `origin_station_id` when the movement type is `DEPARTURE` and its `dest_station_id` when the movement type is `ARRIVAL`, or the request fails with `ERR_SLOT_STATION_MISMATCH`. The series' `carrier_id` must equal the `carrier_id` of the leg's schedule version, or the request fails with `ERR_SLOT_CARRIER_MISMATCH` — one airline cannot borrow another's slot. The series must match the movement in time: the UTC time-of-day of `slot_time_utc` must equal the UTC time-of-day of the leg's `dep_utc` for a `DEPARTURE` or its `arr_utc` for an `ARRIVAL` — and that equality is exact to the full stored precision, comparing hour, minute, second and fractional second alike rather than only the hour and minute, so a series anchored at 09:00:00.000Z does not match a departure at 09:00:30.000Z — and the ISO weekday of that same instant, numbered 1 for Monday through 7 for Sunday, must be one of the digits in `days_of_week`; a failure on either half is `ERR_SLOT_TIME_MISMATCH`. And the series must have headroom: `attached_operations` must be strictly less than `planned_operations`, or the request fails with `ERR_SLOT_SERIES_OVERSUBSCRIBED`, because a series cannot back more operations than it was granted. A ten-operation series with nine legs already attached accepts a tenth and refuses an eleventh.

A successful attach recomputes the series' `attached_operations`, `utilization_pct_as_filed` and `at_risk_of_loss`, and then the owning version's `unslotted_leg_count`, `at_risk_slot_count`, `schedule_compliance_score` and `compliance_state`. It also advances the leg, but only under two conditions: the leg must already have an aircraft — `assigned_aircraft_id` non-null — and **every** slot the leg requires must now have a holding attached. When both hold, the leg moves to `SLOT_BACKED`, whether it was sitting in `ASSIGNED` or in `REFILE_REQUIRED`. When either fails, the attach still succeeds and records the slot, and the leg stays exactly where it was: a leg in `DRAFT` with no aircraft yet stays `DRAFT` however many slots are attached to it, a leg in `ASSIGNED` that still has an unfilled required movement stays `ASSIGNED`, and a leg in `REFILE_REQUIRED` that is still short of a slot stays `REFILE_REQUIRED`. A leg departing a coordinated airport and arriving at a non-coordinated one requires only a departure slot and, once assigned a tail, reaches `SLOT_BACKED` on that single attach; a leg between two coordinated airports requires both and waits for the second attach to land. The step from `REFILE_REQUIRED` to `SLOT_BACKED` is the one that unwinds a block: the version's `unslotted_leg_count` decreases by 1, its `schedule_compliance_score` rises, and its `compliance_state` is re-derived, which can carry it from `BLOCKED` up to `WARNED` and on to `CLEAN`. Both the leg's `version` and the holding's `version` increment by 1 on every attach, and the whole `SLOT_BACKED` → `FILED` → `REFILE_REQUIRED` → `SLOT_BACKED` loop may be traversed any number of times within a single season as slots move around.

**The two utilization variants** are the heart of the use-it-or-lose-it regime, and they are computed from different inputs and consumed by different rules. Both share the same denominator, `planned_operations`, and both are rounded to two decimal places, half up.

The **as-operated** variant looks at what actually happened. `credited_operations` counts the utilization records filed against the series whose `outcome` is `OPERATED`, `CANCELLED_ATC` or `RETURNED_IN_TIME`; the airline gets credit when the flight went, when air traffic control caused the cancellation, and when the series was handed back in good time. An `outcome` of `CANCELLED_CARRIER` or `NOT_OPERATED` never earns credit. Then `utilization_pct` is `credited_operations` multiplied by 100, divided by `planned_operations`, rounded half up to two decimals. When `planned_operations` is 0 the result is 0.00. This is the **only** variant the season slot audit rules on, and a series whose `utilization_pct` is strictly below 80.00 percent at audit time loses its grandfather claim.

The **as-filed** variant looks at what is currently planned. `utilization_pct_as_filed` is `attached_operations` multiplied by 100, divided by `planned_operations`, rounded half up to two decimals, again 0.00 when the denominator is 0. This variant drives forward-looking risk: `at_risk_of_loss` is true exactly when the series is still `HELD`, `attached_operations` is at least 1, and `utilization_pct_as_filed` is strictly below 80.00. All three conjuncts matter. A series with five planned operations and no legs attached at all has an as-filed figure of 0.00, which is far below the threshold, yet `at_risk_of_loss` stays false because the second conjunct fails — a series nobody has begun planning against is dormant, not at risk. Attach a single leg and the figure becomes 20.00 with `at_risk_of_loss` now true.

The two variants routinely disagree, and both must be maintained independently. Take a series with five planned operations. Four legs are attached, so `attached_operations` is 4 and `utilization_pct_as_filed` is 4 times 100 divided by 5, which is exactly 80.00 — at the threshold, not below it, so `at_risk_of_loss` is false. Meanwhile four utilization records have been filed with outcomes `OPERATED`, `OPERATED`, `CANCELLED_ATC` and `CANCELLED_CARRIER`: three of those earn credit, so `credited_operations` is 3 and `utilization_pct` is 3 times 100 divided by 5, which is 60.00. The series looks healthy on paper and is failing in practice, and an audit run at that moment takes it away. Because both are exactly at 80.00 in the four-of-five case, the threshold's inclusiveness is decisive: 80.00 passes, 79.99 does not.

**Filing utilization records.** Either a `NetworkPlanner` or a `SlotCoordinator` may log what happened on one dated operation, naming the `slot_holding_id`, the `operation_date` and the `outcome`. The series must be `HELD`, and for a planner acting on their own behalf the series' `carrier_id` must be the acting carrier or the request is refused with `ERR_SLOT_CARRIER_MISMATCH`. The `operation_date` must fall inside the season's window, from `start_date` through `end_date` inclusive; it is a full ISO 8601 instant carrying the mandatory `Z` suffix like every other timestamp in the contract rather than a bare calendar date, and it is compared as an instant against the season's own stored `start_date` and `end_date` instants. A date outside that window is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and because the window test runs once the named series has been resolved but still ahead of the `HELD` test and the carrier test, an out-of-window date against a series that is also no longer `HELD` reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than the state error; when the series' season cannot be resolved at all the window test is skipped entirely and the record is accepted. An `outcome` of `RETURNED_IN_TIME` is **never accepted from a client** and a request supplying it is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` in the same band as any other undeclared enum member; nor is it ever written by anything else, because no cascade in the system synthesises a utilization record — the return of a series detaches its legs but files nothing against it — so `RETURNED_IN_TIME` earns credit wherever the enumeration admits it yet in practice never appears on a stored row, and this endpoint is the only writer of the collection. On success the record's `credited` flag is set to true exactly when the `outcome` is one of the three crediting values, and `recorded_by_user_id` is stamped with the acting `X-User-ID`, always and without exception. Filing a record recomputes `credited_operations` and `utilization_pct` and **only** those: `utilization_pct_as_filed` is untouched, because what happened on the day says nothing about what is currently attached.

Utilization records are immutable. There is no update or delete action, the entity carries neither a `version` counter nor an `updated_at` field — `created_at` is the only timestamp on the row, and the pair of §2.4 fields that every other entity exposes is simply absent from the serialised record — and the correct way to fix a mis-filed day is to file the real one — records may legitimately be posted out of chronological order and the derived figures must be identical whichever order they arrive in. Where an order is needed for a deterministic walk, records are processed ascending by `operation_date` with `created_at` breaking ties.

**Returning a series** is the one exit from `HELD` that protects the grandfather claim, and it belongs to the `NetworkPlanner` who holds it. The request carries a `reason`. Four preconditions apply, and their priority order is the one the bands of §2.9 dictate rather than the order it is natural to recite them in: first the season must be `OPEN` (`ERR_SEASON_NOT_OPEN`, band 7) — and a series whose `season_id` resolves to nothing at all fails here too, since an unresolvable season is treated as a season that is not open; then the series must be `HELD` (`ERR_SLOT_HOLDING_STATE_INVALID`, band 8); then the series must not be encumbered by an open transaction (`ERR_SLOT_ENCUMBERED`, band 8, immediately after the state test); and last the logical clock must not have passed the season's `slot_return_deadline_utc` (`ERR_SLOT_RETURN_DEADLINE_PASSED`, band 12). The deadline comparison is strict, so a return made at the deadline instant exactly still succeeds and only an instant strictly after it is refused. The ownership check of band 5 precedes all four. On success `holding_status` moves `HELD → RETURNED`, `returned_reason` records the supplied reason, `returned_before_deadline` is set to true and `retains_historic_precedence` is set to true. Every leg referencing the series whose `status` is `SLOT_BACKED` or `FILED` moves to `REFILE_REQUIRED` with the referencing slot identifier nulled; `attached_operations` consequently drops to 0, `utilization_pct_as_filed` is recomputed, and `at_risk_of_loss` becomes false — not because the risk was averted but because the series is no longer `HELD`. Each affected schedule version then recomputes `unslotted_leg_count`, `schedule_compliance_score` and `compliance_state`, both `held_series_count` counters are recomputed, and the series' `version` increments by 1. `RETURNED`, `TRANSFERRED_OUT`, `LOST` and `EXPIRED` are all terminal.

### 3.6. Slot Exchanges and Transfers: A Two-Gate Negotiation

Series move between airlines through a negotiation with two independent gates. The counterparty answers first; the coordinator answers second; **both** must clear before any slot actually moves. A `NetworkPlanner` opens the negotiation by declaring the `kind` — `EXCHANGE` when the two carriers swap series, `TRANSFER` when one hands a series over outright — the `counterparty_carrier_id` being asked to respond, the `offered_slot_holding_id` it is putting on the table, the `response_deadline_utc` by which an answer is due, the mandatory `expected_slot_holding_version` described in §2.7, and, for an exchange only, the `counter_slot_holding_id` the counterparty is expected to offer back.

Several things are checked before a proposal is accepted, and here too the priority order is the one §2.9 dictates rather than the order they read most naturally: the season must be `OPEN` first (`ERR_SEASON_NOT_OPEN`, band 7, and an unresolvable `season_id` fails here as well), then the offered series must be `HELD` (`ERR_SLOT_HOLDING_STATE_INVALID`), then it must not already be encumbered (`ERR_SLOT_ENCUMBERED`). `initiating_carrier_id` is not supplied by the client at all — it is copied from the offered holding's own `carrier_id` — and the declared `counterparty_carrier_id` must differ from it, or the proposal is refused with `ERR_SELF_TRANSACTION_NOT_ALLOWED`, since an airline shuffling series between its own holdings has nothing to negotiate. The counter-slot field is strictly keyed to the kind: it must be present for an `EXCHANGE`, must be absent for a `TRANSFER`, and when present must be a series belonging to `counterparty_carrier_id`; a breach of any of those three is `ERR_COUNTER_SLOT_REQUIRED`. The `response_deadline_utc` must fall at or before the `slot_return_deadline_utc` of the offered holding's season, because a negotiation cannot legitimately outlive the window in which its outcome could still be actioned; a breach of that is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and it is tested as soon as the counterparty carrier and the two holdings have been resolved — therefore **ahead** of the ownership check, the expected-version check, the season-lifecycle check and every state check, so a too-late deadline proposed by the wrong carrier against an already-encumbered series reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and it is skipped altogether when the offered holding's `season_id` resolves to nothing. The body's three foreign keys resolve in a fixed order too: `counterparty_carrier_id` first, then `offered_slot_holding_id`, then `counter_slot_holding_id` when one is supplied. Nothing further is required of the counter series or of the counterparty beyond that: the counter holding need not be `HELD`, need not be unencumbered, and need not sit at the same station, in the same season, or on the same movement type as the offered one, and the counterparty carrier need not be `ACTIVE` — it is enough that the carrier resolves and that the counter series belongs to it. And a series carrying grandfather rights is protected: when the offered holding's `historic_precedence` is true and the `kind` is `TRANSFER`, the proposal is refused with `ERR_HISTORIC_PRECEDENCE_PROTECTED`. A historic series may be exchanged — the rights follow the swap — but never disposed of outright.

A successful proposal is created in `status` `PROPOSED` with `proposed_by_user_id` stamped from the acting `X-User-ID`, `responded_by_user_id`, `approved_by_user_id`, `executed_at_utc`, `successor_slot_holding_id` and `decision_reason` all null, and it **encumbers** every involved holding by writing the transaction's own id into each holding's `pending_transaction_id`. An encumbered series can no longer be attached to a leg, returned to the coordinator, or offered into a competing proposal. A stale `expected_slot_holding_version` produces `ERR_STALE_SLOT_HOLDING` and encumbers nothing at all.

**The first gate.** Only a `PartnerCarrier` acting as `counterparty_carrier_id` may respond, and only while the transaction is `PROPOSED` — any other status gives `ERR_SLOT_TRANSACTION_STATE_INVALID`, and a mismatched principal gives `ERR_OWNERSHIP_MISMATCH`. The response must arrive at or before `response_deadline_utc` as read from the logical clock, or it is refused with `ERR_SLOT_TRANSACTION_DEADLINE_PASSED`. The request carries a `decision` and, when rejecting, a `rejection_reason`. A `decision` of `ACCEPT` moves `PROPOSED → COUNTERPARTY_ACCEPTED`, stamps `responded_by_user_id`, and leaves every encumbrance in place, because the slots are now closer to moving rather than further from it; a `rejection_reason` sent alongside an `ACCEPT` is still length-checked as an optional string but is then ignored, and `decision_reason` stays null. Neither this gate nor a withdrawal re-examines the season at all — only the coordinator's gate does — so a counterparty may accept, and an initiator may withdraw, in a season that has since left `OPEN`; and neither gate examines the state of the holdings themselves, which the encumbrance has already reserved. A `decision` of `REJECT` moves `PROPOSED → COUNTERPARTY_REJECTED`, which is terminal, stamps `responded_by_user_id` and records the reason in `decision_reason`, and **releases** the encumbrance by nulling `pending_transaction_id` on the offered holding and, for an exchange, on the counter holding too, incrementing each of those holdings' `version` by 1.

**The second gate** belongs to a `SlotCoordinator`, is permitted only from `COUNTERPARTY_ACCEPTED`, and is subject to segregation of duties: the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `proposed_by_user_id`, or the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The season must still be `OPEN`, and the historic-precedence protection is re-checked here as well as at proposal time, because a series' `historic_precedence` can have been set true by an intervening archival. The request carries a `decision` and, when denying, a `denial_reason`; a `denial_reason` sent alongside an `APPROVE` is length-checked and then ignored, so an executed transaction always carries a null `decision_reason`. A `decision` of `DENY` moves `COUNTERPARTY_ACCEPTED → COORDINATOR_DENIED`, which is terminal, stamps `approved_by_user_id`, records `decision_reason`, and releases the encumbrance on every involved holding with each holding's `version` incrementing by 1. Nothing else happens: no holding changes carrier and no leg is detached. Denial and rejection differ only in which gate they occurred at and which user field is stamped; their effect on the slots themselves is identical, and in both cases the series return to exactly the state they were in before the proposal.

A `decision` of `APPROVE` moves `COUNTERPARTY_ACCEPTED → EXECUTED`, which is terminal, stamps `approved_by_user_id` and sets `executed_at_utc` to the logical clock's `current_utc`. What happens to the slots then depends on the `kind`, and the two branches are deliberately asymmetric.

On a `TRANSFER`, the offered holding moves `HELD → TRANSFERRED_OUT` with its `retains_historic_precedence` set to false — the transferor loses the grandfather claim, which moves to the successor — and a **new** slot holding is minted for `counterparty_carrier_id` carrying the same `station_id`, `season_id`, `movement_type`, `slot_time_utc`, `days_of_week` and `series_weeks` as the original, `holding_status` `HELD`, `historic_precedence` copied from the offered holding — which, because a historic series can never be transferred in the first place, is always false on a successor — `historic_predecessor_holding_id` set to the offered holding's id, and every other field at exactly the default a freshly recorded series of §3.5 takes: `planned_operations` recomputed as `series_weeks` times the mask width, `attached_operations`, `credited_operations`, `utilization_pct` and `utilization_pct_as_filed` at 0 or 0.00, `at_risk_of_loss` false, `pending_transaction_id` null — the successor is never encumbered by the transaction that minted it — and `returned_reason`, `returned_before_deadline` and `retains_historic_precedence` all null, with `version` 1. The successor inherits the series but none of its flights, and it is minted without any re-run of the `ERR_SLOT_SERIES_CONFLICT` test, which it could not fail anyway now that the series it duplicates has left `HELD`. The new holding's id is written back into the transaction's `successor_slot_holding_id`.

On an `EXCHANGE`, no successor holding is minted and `successor_slot_holding_id` stays null. Instead the offered and counter holdings **swap** their `carrier_id` values in place; each row keeps its own `slot_time_utc` and `days_of_week`, and both remain `HELD`. The two airlines end up holding each other's times, which is exactly the point of an exchange.

Both branches then share the same downstream consequences. Every leg attached to a holding that changed carrier — and on an exchange that is legs on **both** sides — whose `status` is `SLOT_BACKED` or `FILED` moves to `REFILE_REQUIRED` with the referencing slot identifier nulled. Each affected schedule version's `unslotted_leg_count` rises, its `schedule_compliance_score` is recomputed and its `compliance_state` re-derived. Any filing submission of such a version that is already `ACKNOWLEDGED` has `resync_required` set to true, flagging that the distribution systems now hold a plan the airline no longer has the slots for. Finally `pending_transaction_id` is cleared on every holding involved, since the transaction that encumbered them has run to completion.

**Withdrawing a proposal** belongs to the initiating carrier's `NetworkPlanner`, verified against `initiating_carrier_id` rather than the counterparty, and it is permitted from `PROPOSED` **and** from `COUNTERPARTY_ACCEPTED` — an initiator may pull back even after the counterparty has said yes, right up until the coordinator rules. The request carries a `reason`, which lands in `decision_reason`. `WITHDRAWN` is terminal, and the encumbrance release is identical whichever status the withdrawal came from: `pending_transaction_id` is nulled on every involved holding. Attempting to withdraw a transaction that has already reached `COUNTERPARTY_REJECTED`, `COORDINATOR_DENIED`, `EXECUTED` or `WITHDRAWN` gives `ERR_SLOT_TRANSACTION_STATE_INVALID`.

The successor holding that a transfer mints is discoverable only by listing — either by filtering slot holdings on `historic_predecessor_holding_id` or by filtering transactions on `successor_slot_holding_id`. There is no endpoint that walks forward from a holding to its successor directly.

### 3.7. The Compliance Gate: Scoring, Warning and Blocking

Every schedule version carries a single number, `schedule_compliance_score`, that summarises how badly the plan breaches the rules it was allowed to breach. It starts from a base of 100.00 and subtracts a weighted penalty for each of five counters. Each curfew violation costs 12.00 points. Each minimum-connect-time violation costs 7.00 points. Each station-date gate overflow costs 9.00 points. Each unslotted leg costs 8.00 points. And each distinct at-risk slot series costs 15.00 points — the heaviest weight in the system, because a slot about to be lost threatens the airline's ability to fly the route at all next season, not merely this filing.

The raw penalty is the sum of `curfew_violation_count` times 12.00, `mct_violation_count` times 7.00, `gate_overflow_count` times 9.00, `unslotted_leg_count` times 8.00 and `at_risk_slot_count` times 15.00. That raw penalty is then **capped at 100.00** before it is subtracted, and the score is the greater of 0.00 and 100.00 minus the capped penalty, rounded half up to two decimals. The cap and the floor are two expressions of the same intent — a score never goes negative and never wraps — and because the cap is applied to the penalty rather than to the difference, the result is identical either way; a version with nine curfew violations has a raw penalty of 108.00, a capped penalty of 100.00 and a score of exactly 0.00. A pristine version scores 100.00. A version with one curfew violation and two unslotted legs has a raw penalty of 12.00 plus 16.00, so 28.00, and scores 72.00. A version with one at-risk slot alone has a raw penalty of 15.00 and scores 85.00.

From that score and one of the counters, the version's `compliance_state` is derived. It is `BLOCKED` when `at_risk_slot_count` is at least 1 **or** the score is strictly below 60.00. Otherwise it is `WARNED` when the score is strictly below 85.00. Otherwise it is `CLEAN`. The order of those three tests is what makes the at-risk rule bite: the version scoring exactly 85.00 because of a single at-risk slot would be `CLEAN` on score alone — 85.00 is not below 85.00 — yet it is `BLOCKED`, because a single at-risk series blocks a plan outright regardless of how good the rest of the score looks. That is the sharpest edge in the whole scoring model, and it is deliberate: an at-risk slot is not a quality problem to be warned about, it is a hard bar to filing. Conversely a version scoring 59.99 with no at-risk slots is `BLOCKED` on score, and one scoring exactly 60.00 with no at-risk slots is `WARNED`.

`compliance_state` is a **second, independent state dimension** on the same row as `filing_status`. It is re-derived after every single mutation that could change any of the five counters — creating, retiming, cancelling or slot-attaching a leg, assigning an aircraft, a slot detachment from any cause, a transaction execution, a season archival, an audit, and validation itself — and it moves freely in both directions, in the general case without `filing_status` changing at all — the one exception being the drop into `BLOCKED` described below, whose rejection cascade can step a `FILED` version back to `VALIDATED`. There are no terminal states: `CLEAN` and `WARNED` swap back and forth as the plan degrades and is repaired, `CLEAN` and `WARNED` both fall to `BLOCKED`, and `BLOCKED` recovers to `WARNED` when every at-risk slot has been cleared and the score has returned to the band from 60.00 up to but excluding 85.00, or all the way to `CLEAN` when every at-risk slot has been cleared and the score is at least 85.00.

Two of those movements carry side effects beyond the state field itself. Moving into `WARNED` from `CLEAN` sets `resync_required` to true on every filing submission of the version whose `status` is `ACKNOWLEDGED`; the submission itself **stays** `ACKNOWLEDGED`, because the distribution systems did accept what they were sent — it is simply no longer what the airline intends to fly. That flag is **not** cleared by recovery: a version that slips to `WARNED` and is then repaired back to `CLEAN` leaves `resync_required` true, and only a fresh filing submission discharges the planner's obligation to re-file. Moving into `BLOCKED` from either `CLEAN` or `WARNED` fires the rejection cascade set out in §2.10 and §3.8: every filing submission of the version whose `status` is `TRANSMITTED` or `ACKNOWLEDGED` moves to `REJECTED` with `rejection_reason` set to the literal `SCHEDULE_BLOCKED`, the version's `filing_charge_accrued_cents` is reduced by that submission's `total_charge_cents`, and the version's `FILED` legs move back to `SLOT_BACKED`. When that cascade has rejected at least one submission in this way and the version's own `filing_status` is `FILED`, the version steps back `FILED → VALIDATED` alongside those legs — the same return from filed to validated that a downstream rejection performs, and what leaves the plan re-fileable once the block is cleared without a second trip through validation; a version whose `filing_status` is anything other than `FILED` keeps it unchanged, and `validated_by_user_id` is left exactly as it stands either way. A submission still sitting in `QUEUED` is deliberately left alone by that cascade — it never reached a distribution system and never accrued a charge, so there is nothing to reverse; instead its later transmit attempt is what fails, with `ERR_SCHEDULE_BLOCKED`. In every one of these movements the version's `version` increments by 1.

**Validation** is the gate a `ComplianceOfficer` runs to promote a version from `DRAFT` to `VALIDATED`, and it takes no request body at all. It is permitted only from `DRAFT`; from `VALIDATED`, `FILED`, `SUPERSEDED` or `WITHDRAWN` it is refused with `ERR_SCHEDULE_VERSION_STATE_INVALID`. An empty version is not validatable — `leg_count` must be at least 1. Every non-cancelled leg of the version must be in `status` `SLOT_BACKED`, and any leg still in `DRAFT`, `ASSIGNED` or `REFILE_REQUIRED` makes the version not ready, reported as `ERR_SCHEDULE_VERSION_NOT_READY`. Immediately after that readiness sweep, and **ahead** of every remaining check, the version's `compliance_state` must not be `BLOCKED`, or validation fails with `ERR_SCHEDULE_BLOCKED`; a `WARNED` version validates perfectly well, which is the practical difference between a warning and a block. Three substantive checks then run, and they run in the order the bands of §2.9 dictate rather than in the order it is natural to describe them. First, every aircraft referenced by the version's non-cancelled legs whose `maintenance_check_required` is true must have `maintenance_opportunity_minutes` at least equal to its `required_check_minutes`, or validation fails with `ERR_MAINTENANCE_OPPORTUNITY_INSUFFICIENT`. Second, every such aircraft must have `rotation_feasibility_status` `FEASIBLE`, and when the walk is infeasible the specific reason surfaces — `ERR_ROTATION_CONTINUITY_BROKEN`, `ERR_TURN_TIME_VIOLATION` or `ERR_DAILY_BLOCK_CAP_EXCEEDED` as appropriate. Both of those sweeps visit the tails in the order the version's non-cancelled legs first reference them, which settles the case of two tails failing differently: the reason reported is the one belonging to the tail referenced by the earlier leg, not whichever identifier happens to sit higher in the band-10 list, so a version whose first-referenced tail is `INFEASIBLE_TURN` and whose second is `INFEASIBLE_CONTINUITY` reports `ERR_TURN_TIME_VIOLATION`. Third and last, slot coverage is checked explicitly and separately: every non-cancelled leg must have a non-null `departure_slot_holding_id` when its origin station is `LEVEL_3_COORDINATED` and a non-null `arrival_slot_holding_id` when its destination station is `LEVEL_3_COORDINATED`, or validation fails with `ERR_SLOT_REQUIRED_AT_COORDINATED_STATION`.

Validation is also a recomputation point, and it is the **first** thing the endpoint does once the target version has been resolved — ahead of the `DRAFT` precondition itself. It re-derives `leg_count`, `unslotted_leg_count`, `curfew_violation_count`, `mct_violation_count`, `gate_overflow_count` and `at_risk_slot_count` from scratch, recomputes `schedule_compliance_score` and re-derives `compliance_state` — so a version whose stored counters had drifted is corrected here, and a version that has silently become blockable is caught by its own gate, judged on the freshly settled figures rather than the stale ones. Because that re-derivation precedes the state check, even a validation attempt that is going to be refused for being out of `DRAFT` has already driven the whole world to its fixed point. On success `filing_status` moves `DRAFT → VALIDATED` and `validated_by_user_id` is stamped with the acting `X-User-ID`. That stamp is load-bearing: it is precisely what the transmit step later compares the transmitting user against for segregation of duties, so the officer who validates a plan can never be the agent who sends it.

### 3.8. Filing to Distribution Systems, Codeshare Sync and Charges

A **filing submission** is one attempt to publish one schedule version to one downstream distribution system. A `FilingAgent` queues it by naming the `schedule_version_id`, the `distribution_system` the SSIM message is addressed to — one of `IATA_SSIM`, `OAG` or `GDS_ATPCO` — and the `change_window` urgency band, one of `STANDARD`, `EXPEDITED` or `EMERGENCY`. Queuing is a recomputation point in exactly the sense §3.7 gives validation: the moment the target version has been resolved, and **before** its `filing_status` is examined, the whole world is re-derived to its fixed point, so both the status test and the charge freeze that follows are judged on freshly settled figures — and, as with validation, a queuing attempt that is then refused has already driven that re-derivation and whatever cascade it fired. The version must be in `filing_status` `VALIDATED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_SCHEDULE_VERSION_STATE_INVALID`; a `DRAFT` version has not been through the compliance gate and a `FILED` one has already been sent. That status is the **only** precondition on queuing: it is not gated on the season being `OPEN`, not gated on the carrier being `ACTIVE`, and not gated on the version's `compliance_state` at all, so a `BLOCKED` version accepts a queued submission perfectly well and the block surfaces only when someone tries to transmit it. Nor is a version limited to one submission — several may coexist against the same version, including several `QUEUED` ones addressed to the very same `distribution_system`, each with its own independently frozen charge. The submission is created in `status` `QUEUED` with `submitted_by_user_id` stamped from the acting `X-User-ID`, `codeshare_ack_carrier_ids` an empty array, `resync_required` false, and `transmitted_by_user_id`, `transmitted_at_utc`, `acknowledged_at_utc` and `rejection_reason` all null.

Creation is also the moment the price is **frozen**, and the freeze is the whole point of the design: later leg churn must never retroactively reprice a submission that has already been queued. At the instant of creation, `changed_leg_count` is set to the number of legs of that schedule version whose `status` is `SLOT_BACKED` or `CANCELLED` — the legs that represent a change the distribution system needs to hear about, whether an addition or a removal — and it is **never recomputed afterwards**. Legs still in `DRAFT`, `ASSIGNED` or `REFILE_REQUIRED` are not counted, since they are not yet ready to be published.

The charge is then built in five ordered steps, and the order matters at every join. First, the frozen `changed_leg_count`. Second, a raw amount equal to that count multiplied by 4225 cents per leg. Third, a volume surcharge chosen by the count: at 5 legs or fewer the surcharge is 0.00 percent, above 5 legs and up to 20 legs inclusive it is 12.50 percent, and above 20 legs it is 22.00 percent — and it is a **flat** surcharge on the whole raw amount, not a marginal band, so at 21 legs every one of the 21 legs is charged at 22.00 percent and none at 0.00. `base_charge_subtotal_cents` is the raw amount multiplied by 100 plus the surcharge percent, divided by 100, rounded half up to whole cents. Fourth, `expedite_rate_pct` is chosen by the `change_window`: 0.00 percent for `STANDARD`, 35.00 percent for `EXPEDITED` and 80.00 percent for `EMERGENCY`; and `expedite_fee_cents` is `base_charge_subtotal_cents` multiplied by `expedite_rate_pct`, divided by 100, rounded half up to whole cents. The expedite fee is keyed on the **post-surcharge subtotal**, never on the raw amount and never on a total that already contains the fee. Fifth, `total_charge_cents` is `base_charge_subtotal_cents` plus `expedite_fee_cents`.

Four filings make the arithmetic concrete. Four legs filed `EXPEDITED`: raw 16900, tier-one surcharge of 0.00 percent leaves the subtotal at 16900, the expedite fee is 35.00 percent of 16900 which is 5915, and the total is 22815. Seven legs filed `EMERGENCY`: raw 29575, the 12.50 percent surcharge gives 33271.875 which rounds half up to a subtotal of 33272, the 80.00 percent expedite fee on that subtotal is 26617.6 which rounds to 26618, and the total is 59890 — where keying the fee on the raw amount instead would have produced 23660 and a total of 56932, which is wrong. Twenty legs filed `STANDARD`: raw 84500, the 12.50 percent surcharge gives exactly 95062.5, which rounds **half up** to a subtotal of 95063; an implementation using banker's rounding returns 95062 and is incorrect. There is no expedite fee, so the total is 95063. Twenty-one legs filed `STANDARD`: raw 88725, now in the third tier, so 22.00 percent gives 108244.5 which rounds to a subtotal and total of 108245 — a figure that exceeds the default `filing_charge_budget_cents` of 100000 and will make transmission fail. At the tier edges: 5 legs is tier one, giving a subtotal of 21125, while 6 legs is tier two, raw 25350 becoming 28518.75 and rounding to 28519; 20 legs is tier two and 21 legs is tier three. A version with zero changed legs still produces a creatable submission, with a raw amount, subtotal, fee and total all of 0, and it still transmits.

**Codeshare synchronisation.** At creation, `codeshare_sync_status` is set to `PENDING_ACK` when any non-cancelled leg of the version carries a non-empty `marketing_carrier_ids`, and to `NOT_REQUIRED` when none does. A submission sitting in `PENDING_ACK` cannot be transmitted, because a partner airline that sells seats on the flight must be told before the schedule change reaches the distribution systems. Acknowledgement is recorded one carrier at a time, by either a `FilingAgent` or a `PartnerCarrier`, naming a single `marketing_carrier_id` which must resolve to a registered carrier (`ERR_CARRIER_NOT_FOUND`) but is otherwise entirely unconstrained: a `PartnerCarrier` is **not** required to name itself, no ownership check applies to this endpoint at all, and the named carrier need not be a marketing carrier of any leg of the version, so any partner may record any carrier's acknowledgement. That id is appended to `codeshare_ack_carrier_ids` — appended only if it is not already there, so re-acknowledging the same carrier leaves the list unchanged, though the submission is still counted as written by that request and its `version` and `updated_at` advance on the repeat call anyway — and `codeshare_sync_status` is then recomputed against the **distinct** marketing carriers of the version's non-cancelled legs. The status becomes `SYNCHRONIZED` when the acknowledgement list covers every one of them, and stays `PENDING_ACK` otherwise. Acknowledgements may only be recorded while the submission is `QUEUED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_FILING_SUBMISSION_STATE_INVALID`. Note that the acknowledgement list is compared against the marketing carriers currently on the version's legs, not against a list frozen at creation — so cancelling the only codeshared leg can move a submission from `PENDING_ACK` to `NOT_REQUIRED` without any acknowledgement being filed at all. `codeshare_sync_status` is in that sense a fully derived field rather than a stage of the submission's own lifecycle: it is re-settled by the closing sweep of §2.10 on **every** mutating request in the system, and on every submission regardless of that submission's own `status`, so a `TRANSMITTED`, `ACKNOWLEDGED` or even `REJECTED` submission's status keeps tracking the version's current legs long after the gate it once guarded has been passed. The required set is the distinct marketing carriers of the version's non-cancelled legs, taken in the order those legs first name them, and an acknowledgement list that covers all of them is `SYNCHRONIZED` even if it also carries carriers no leg names any more.

**Transmission** carries the densest gate stack in the system, it takes no request body fields at all, and its ordering is set out in §2.9 — which is not the order the checks are most naturally recited in, so the stack is given here in the order it actually runs. Like queuing and like validation, transmission first re-derives the whole world to its fixed point, before any gate below is evaluated, which means a transmit that is about to be refused has nonetheless already settled every counter and fired any cascade that settling triggered: a transmit refused with `ERR_SCHEDULE_BLOCKED` has, in that same request, already rejected the version's live submissions and reversed their charges under §3.7. Then, in order: the version's `compliance_state` must not be `BLOCKED`, or it fails with `ERR_SCHEDULE_BLOCKED` — this comes **first** among the state objections, ahead of the submission's own status, so an already-`TRANSMITTED` submission on a blocked version reports `ERR_SCHEDULE_BLOCKED` rather than `ERR_FILING_SUBMISSION_STATE_INVALID`. The submission must be `QUEUED`, or the request fails with `ERR_FILING_SUBMISSION_STATE_INVALID`. The acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the version's `validated_by_user_id`, or it fails with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The `codeshare_sync_status` must not be `PENDING_ACK`, or it fails with `ERR_CODESHARE_NOT_SYNCHRONIZED`. When the `change_window` is `STANDARD`, the logical clock must not have passed the season's `filing_change_window_close_utc`, or it fails with `ERR_CHANGE_WINDOW_CLOSED` — a comparison that is strict, so a `STANDARD` transmit made at the close instant exactly still succeeds, and one that is skipped altogether when the version's `season_id` resolves to nothing; `EXPEDITED` and `EMERGENCY` submissions are **exempt** from that close, which is exactly what their surcharge buys, and no gate here requires the season to be `OPEN`, so a filing may be transmitted into a `CLOSED` or `ARCHIVED` season. And the budget must hold: the version's `filing_charge_accrued_cents` plus this submission's `total_charge_cents` must be no greater than its `filing_charge_budget_cents`, or it fails with `ERR_FILING_CHARGE_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`, which is the one payment-related status in the catalogue. When a transmit is both blocked on compliance and over budget, the compliance objection is the one reported: a schedule that must not be filed at all is never a budget question.

A successful transmission moves the submission `QUEUED → TRANSMITTED`, stamps `transmitted_by_user_id` with the acting user and `transmitted_at_utc` with the logical clock, adds `total_charge_cents` to the version's `filing_charge_accrued_cents`, sets the version's `filing_status` to `FILED`, and moves every one of that version's `SLOT_BACKED` legs to `FILED`. That `filing_status` write is an unconditional assignment rather than a guarded `VALIDATED → FILED` transition, and nothing at this point re-examines what the version's status had become since the submission was queued — so a submission queued while its version was `VALIDATED` and transmitted after that version was forked to `SUPERSEDED` still drives the superseded version to `FILED`. It is the mirror-image asymmetry to the guarded reversal on rejection described below. Both the submission's `version` and the version's `version` increment by 1.

**Acknowledgement** records what the distribution system said back, and is permitted only from `TRANSMITTED` — the submission's own status being the sole precondition, since acknowledgement carries no segregation-of-duties rule (the very agent who transmitted may acknowledge), no compliance-state gate, no season gate and no ownership rule, and, unlike queuing and transmission, it does **not** re-derive the world before reading anything; it judges on the figures the previous write left in place. The request carries an `outcome` and, on a rejection, a `rejection_reason`; a `rejection_reason` sent alongside an `ACCEPT` is length-checked and then ignored. An `outcome` of `ACCEPT` moves `TRANSMITTED → ACKNOWLEDGED`, stamps `acknowledged_at_utc` from the logical clock, sets `resync_required` back to false, and leaves the ledger completely alone — an acknowledged submission still counts toward `filing_charge_accrued_cents`, because the filing was made and the fee was earned. An `outcome` of `REJECT` moves `TRANSMITTED → REJECTED`, which is terminal, records the supplied `rejection_reason`, stamps `acknowledged_at_utc`, **subtracts** `total_charge_cents` back off the version's `filing_charge_accrued_cents` so the ledger returns exactly to its pre-transmit value with no drift, moves the version `FILED → VALIDATED`, and moves every `FILED` leg of that version back to `SLOT_BACKED`. The charge reversal is unconditional, but that `FILED → VALIDATED` step is **guarded**: the version's `filing_status` is rewritten only when it is currently `FILED`, and a version that has meanwhile been superseded or withdrawn keeps the status it has while still having the charge taken back off its ledger. That leg sweep is the conditional kind of §2.10, moving only the legs that are actually `FILED` — and leaving legs already in `REFILE_REQUIRED` untouched, since those have lost their slot backing and cannot be returned to a slot-backed state by a filing rejection.

There is a second route into `REJECTED`, and it does not come from the distribution system at all: a submission already in `ACKNOWLEDGED` is rejected by the cascade that fires when its version enters `compliance_state` `BLOCKED`. In that case `rejection_reason` is set to the literal `SCHEDULE_BLOCKED`, distinguishing an internally-forced rejection from a downstream one, and `total_charge_cents` is subtracted back off `filing_charge_accrued_cents` exactly as on a downstream rejection. The invariant that ties the whole ledger together is simple to state and must hold after every one of these writes: a version's `filing_charge_accrued_cents` always equals the sum of `total_charge_cents` over its submissions whose `status` is `TRANSMITTED` or `ACKNOWLEDGED`, and no other.

### 3.9. Passenger Notification Obligations and Compensation

When a schedule change affects passengers who have already booked, the airline owes them notice and, depending on the size of the change, compensation. The system raises these obligations automatically on a retime, on a cancellation and on a slot loss at audit; a `ComplianceOfficer` may also raise one by hand for a change that arrived from outside the system, supplying the `flight_leg_id`, the `trigger_kind` — `RETIME`, `CANCELLATION` or `SLOT_LOSS` — and the `change_magnitude_minutes`, a non-negative whole number of minutes.

An obligation exists at all only when two conditions both hold: the leg's booked total across all three cabins is greater than zero, and `change_magnitude_minutes` is at least 30. When either fails, no row is persisted; on the manual endpoint the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, because there is no obligation to log, and in the automatic cascades the obligation is simply never created. That refusal is evaluated only once the named leg has been resolved, so a request naming a leg that does not exist reports `ERR_FLIGHT_LEG_NOT_FOUND` even when its magnitude would also have been too small. Nothing else about the leg is examined on the manual endpoint: its `status` is never consulted, so an obligation may be hand-raised against a leg in any status at all — `DRAFT`, `CANCELLED` or anything between — its owning carrier is never consulted either, so no ownership rule applies, and the supplied `change_magnitude_minutes` is taken verbatim whatever `trigger_kind` accompanies it, so a hand-raised `CANCELLATION` at 30 minutes is an `ADVISORY` obligation rather than being forced up to the 240-minute cancellation-equivalent. A leg with no bookings at all that is retimed by six hours produces nothing. A fully booked leg retimed by 29 minutes produces nothing either, while the same leg retimed by 30 minutes produces an obligation.

Where the system computes the magnitude itself, it does so per trigger. For a `RETIME` it is the greater of the absolute change in `dep_utc` and the absolute change in `arr_utc`, in whole minutes — so a leg whose departure slides 20 minutes and whose arrival slides 40 has a magnitude of 40. For a `CANCELLATION` and for a `SLOT_LOSS` it is the fixed cancellation-equivalent of 240 minutes, which always clears the 30-minute floor; losing the flight entirely is treated as the largest possible disruption.

`obligation_tier` follows from the magnitude: `ADVISORY` when the magnitude is at least 30 and strictly below 120, and `MANDATORY_REACCOMMODATION` when it is 120 or more. The threshold is inclusive on the mandatory side — 119 minutes is advisory and 120 minutes is mandatory. The conceptual tier `NONE`, for a booked total of zero or a magnitude below 30, is never persisted, which is why the stored field only ever holds one of the two real tiers. The tier selects a multiplier: 37.50 percent for `ADVISORY` and 100.00 percent for `MANDATORY_REACCOMMODATION`.

At creation the obligation **snapshots** `booked_first`, `booked_business` and `booked_economy` off the leg and never re-reads them; `booked_total` is their sum. Compensation is then computed from those three snapshots at fixed per-passenger rates: 61500 cents per first-class passenger, 40252 cents per business-class passenger and 25124 cents per economy passenger. The rounding order is **pinned**: each cabin amount is rounded to whole cents **first**, and only then are the three summed. For each cabin, take the booked count multiplied by that cabin's rate multiplied by the multiplier percent divided by 100, and round half up to a whole number of cents; `compensation_cents` is the sum of the three rounded figures.

The order is observable. Take a 45-minute retime of a leg holding 3 in first, 7 in business and 41 in economy. Since 45 is below 120 the tier is `ADVISORY` and the multiplier is 37.50 percent. First class is 3 times 61500, which is 184500, times 0.375, which is 69187.5, rounding to 69188. Business is 7 times 40252, which is 281764, times 0.375, which is 105661.5, rounding to 105662. Economy is 41 times 25124, which is 1030084, times 0.375, which is 386281.5, rounding to 386282. The sum is 69188 plus 105662 plus 386282, so `compensation_cents` is 561132, and `booked_total` is 51. Summing the unrounded cabin amounts instead — 69187.5 plus 105661.5 plus 386281.5, which is 561130.5, rounding to 561131 — is one cent low and is wrong. With the same snapshot at the mandatory tier the multiplier is 100.00 percent, giving 184500 plus 281764 plus 1030084, so 1496348; every cabin amount is already whole, so the rounding order happens to be invisible there. And a cancellation of a leg holding 2 in first, 5 in business and 30 in economy has magnitude 240, therefore tier `MANDATORY_REACCOMMODATION`, and compensation of 123000 plus 201260 plus 753720, so 1077980, with a `booked_total` of 37.

`notify_deadline_utc` is the logical clock's `current_utc` at creation plus 72 hours. It is **frozen** on the row and never recomputed, so advancing the clock afterwards moves the world past a fixed deadline rather than pushing the deadline along. Every new obligation starts in `status` `PENDING` with `dispatched_channel`, `regulator_reference` and `waiver_reason` null and `late_dispatch` false, and increments the leg's `open_obligation_count`.

**Dispatching the notice** is a `ComplianceOfficer` action, permitted only from `PENDING` — any other status gives `ERR_NOTIFICATION_STATE_INVALID`. The request names the `channel`, one of `EMAIL`, `SMS` or `AGENCY_QUEUE`, and may carry a `regulator_reference` of between 1 and 120 characters. Two rules constrain it. If the logical clock has passed `notify_deadline_utc`, the request **must** carry a `regulator_reference` justifying the late notice, or it is refused with `ERR_LATE_NOTIFICATION_UNREFERENCED`; a dispatch at or before the deadline needs no reference. And a `MANDATORY_REACCOMMODATION` obligation may not be dispatched by `SMS` at all — only `EMAIL` or `AGENCY_QUEUE` are sufficient for a notice that has to carry re-accommodation options — with a violation reported as `ERR_NOTIFICATION_CHANNEL_INSUFFICIENT`. An `ADVISORY` obligation may go out on any of the three channels. On success `status` moves `PENDING → DISPATCHED`, `dispatched_channel` and `regulator_reference` are recorded, `late_dispatch` is set to true exactly when `current_utc` is strictly after `notify_deadline_utc` — a dispatch at the deadline instant is on time — and the leg's `open_obligation_count` decreases by 1.

The other exit is a **waiver**, and it is never requested directly: it is the system's own reversal when the change the passenger was going to be told about is undone before the notice went out. A `PENDING` obligation is waived when the leg's `dep_utc` and `arr_utc` are restored to the values they held before the triggering change — a `REFILE_REQUIRED` leg re-slotted at its original times, or a cancelled leg whose version is withdrawn. Those pre-change times are captured on the obligation, invisibly and outside the serialised body, at the moment an automatic cascade raises it; a **hand-raised** obligation captures none, having no triggering change inside the system to record, and so the times-restored test can never fire for it however the leg is subsequently retimed. The one route by which a hand-raised obligation still reaches `WAIVED` is a withdrawal of its leg's schedule version, which waives every `PENDING` obligation of every leg it cancels outright without comparing any times at all. The obligation's `waiver_reason` is set to the literal `CHANGE_REVERTED` and the leg's `open_obligation_count` decreases by 1, returning that counter exactly to its pre-change value. Both `DISPATCHED` and `WAIVED` are terminal, and the counter invariant that governs both exits is the same: a leg's `open_obligation_count` always equals the number of its obligations still in `PENDING`. Because the system's automatic obligations are created as cascade side effects with no response of their own, listing obligations filtered by `flight_leg_id` is the only way to discover them.
