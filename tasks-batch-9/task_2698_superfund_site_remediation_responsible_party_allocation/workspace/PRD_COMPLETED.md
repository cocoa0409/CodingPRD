# Superfund Remediation Allocation API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Cleaning up a contaminated site under CERCLA is not one project but three that run on top of each other: an engineering project that delineates contamination and builds a remedy, a legal project that identifies who sent the waste and negotiates who pays, and an accounting project that reconciles every dollar the government advanced against every dollar it recovers. Today these three streams live in unrelated spreadsheets, and the seams between them are where the programme loses money. An allocation is negotiated against waste volumes that the evidentiary team later cuts in half. A consent decree is entered with an oversight cost cap that nobody bills against. A party goes insolvent after adoption and its orphan share silently vanishes from the recovery target instead of being written off on the books.

The Superfund Remediation Allocation API is the single system of record for all three streams. It carries a site from a proposed National Priorities List candidacy through remedial investigation and feasibility study, remedy selection and community involvement, allocation negotiation among potentially responsible parties, consent decree lodging and entry, construction procurement and field work, and finally into long-term operation and maintenance with statutory five-year reviews. Every cost-bearing operation is gated by a derived posture that reflects the site's current legal exposure, and every share of liability is derived — never hand-entered — from volumetric, toxicity and ownership-period evidence that the system itself holds.

Two design commitments run through the whole specification and should be read as non-negotiable. First, **the ledger always balances**: recovered cost, written-off orphan cost and unrecovered cost sum exactly to the site's total response cost after every write, including compensating writes such as voiding a collected settlement. Second, **no user reviews their own work**: the project manager who authored an investigation cannot review it, and the reviewer who approved it cannot sign the decision document that rests on it. Implementations that treat either commitment as advisory will fail acceptance.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contract-level rules that hold for every endpoint in the system: authentication, the response envelope, pagination, identifier and versioning conventions, the logical clock, validation precedence, numeric precision, and the two cross-cutting derived mechanisms (the cost posture gate and the response-cost ledger).

### 2.1. Actors, Headers and Role-Based Access Control

Every request — reads included — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which that user is acting. If either header is missing, empty or nothing but whitespace, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the six role names below — matched exactly and case-sensitively, with no trimming and no aliasing — the request is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). `X-User-ID` is likewise taken verbatim: it is recorded, and compared against a stored `id`, exactly as the client sent it. When both headers are well formed but the declared role is not authorised for the endpoint being called, the refusal is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The system recognises exactly six roles: `RemedialProjectManager`, `AllocationFacilitator`, `SettlementCounsel`, `PRPRepresentative`, `Contractor`, and `CommunityMember`.

A `RemedialProjectManager` is the EPA remedial project manager. This role owns site listing, party identification, waste-in verification, investigation authorship and review, decision documents, oversight cost posting, procurement, construction certification and five-year reviews. An `AllocationFacilitator` is the neutral facilitator who runs the allocation negotiation: opening rounds, moving them to balloting, and reopening them for renegotiation. This role can neither verify evidence nor settle claims. `SettlementCounsel` is government settlement counsel: drafting, lodging and entering consent decrees and administrative orders, recording compliance events and penalties, and issuing, collecting and voiding recovery actions — but never running the allocation rounds on whose outcome those demands rest. A `PRPRepresentative` is counsel for a single potentially responsible party. A `Contractor` is the remediation contractor performing field work. A `CommunityMember` is a member of the affected community whose only write capability is filing a public comment on a proposed plan.

Three of these roles are **opaque identities** and three are not, and the distinction determines how `X-User-ID` is interpreted:

- When `X-User-Role` is `RemedialProjectManager`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a transition calls for it — as `authored_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` on an investigation, as `signed_by_user_id` on a decision document, as `verified_by_user_id` on a waste-in record — but never compares it to any entity's `id`. The same holds for `AllocationFacilitator`, whose identifier is recorded as `facilitator_user_id` on a round it opens, for `SettlementCounsel`, and for `CommunityMember`; none of these three is compared against a stored `id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `PRPRepresentative`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `PotentiallyResponsibleParty`. Ownership checks on any resource that references a party compare that resource's party reference to `X-User-ID`: a representative may record an election only for itself, may cast a ballot only on its own allocation share, and may respond only to a recovery action whose respondent is itself.
- When `X-User-Role` is `Contractor`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `RemediationContract`. A contractor may therefore schedule field work only on the very contract it was awarded.

Any attempt by an entity-backed role to act on someone else's record is rejected with `ERR_ACTOR_IDENTITY_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403). Note the difference from `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`: the role is correct and the endpoint permits it, but the identity does not match the target.

Because two segregation-of-duties rules compare one `RemedialProjectManager` identifier against another, a deployment needs at least two distinct project-manager user identifiers to exercise the full workflow. All six roles may call every read endpoint, and all six may set the logical clock.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` followed by twenty upper-case alphanumeric characters.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, read from the logical clock of §2.5 rather than from wall-clock time — so it does not advance between requests unless the clock is moved, and two responses issued at the same clock value carry the same timestamp.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field. Wherever this specification describes a field as **empty** — an unstamped reference, an unset percentage, an unrecorded instant, a `max_toxicity_class` with no verified record behind it, an `oversight_cap_utilization_pct` with no governing instrument — that field is present in the body and carries JSON `null`; it is never rendered as an empty string and never silently replaced by 0.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination, Filtering and List Ordering

List endpoints are paginated through two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of records returned; it defaults to 10 when omitted, must be at least 1, and may not exceed 100. `offset` is the number of records to skip; it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` or `offset` outside those bounds is a malformed request and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422) rather than silently clamped, and a value that is not a bare integer literal is malformed for the same reason. Every list endpoint in the system reaches those defaults and bounds through this one path and none of them bypasses it — the zero-or-one selected-remedy read of §3.5 is paginated on exactly the same terms as the site list. Pagination and filter values are read and validated *ahead* of path resolution, so a malformed `limit` on a list nested under an identifier that does not resolve reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than the 404, exactly as the ladder of §2.6 requires. The single-resource reads and the cost-ledger projection of §2.10 are not lists, take no `limit` or `offset`, and carry no pagination fields in `meta` at all.

Every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope, where `total_count` is the number of records matching the filter before pagination is applied and `has_more` indicates whether records remain beyond the current page.

Unless a specific list states otherwise, records are returned sorted by `created_at` ascending. Because `created_at` is stamped from the logical clock of §2.5, records created without moving that clock all share an identical `created_at`, so ties are the normal case rather than the exception: **every ordering and every tie-break chain in this specification falls back last of all to server insertion order** — of two records that compare equal on the stated keys, the one the server created first sorts first — and never to `id`. That same fallback settles the residual-taking party of §3.6, the remedy selector's final tie of §3.4, and the enumeration order the allocation arithmetic walks. Several lists deliberately deviate from `created_at` ascending, and the deviation is part of the contract:

- The top-level site list is ordered by `created_at` **descending**, with `name` ascending as the tie-break; because the sense of the primary key is reversed, records tied on both `created_at` and `name` place the **later**-created record first.
- The site's chain of title is ordered by `start_date` ascending. That reversal belongs to the site-scoped list alone: a *party's* own ownership-period list states nothing and therefore keeps the default `created_at` ascending.
- A site's allocation rounds are ordered by `round_number` ascending, and superseded rounds are included rather than hidden.
- An investigation's analytical results are ordered by `collected_at` ascending.
- A contract's field-work windows are ordered by `start_at` ascending.
- The shares of an allocation round are ordered by the owning party's `created_at` ascending — the same enumeration order the allocation arithmetic itself uses. That key too is scoped to the round's own share list; a *party's* share list across every round of its site states nothing and therefore keeps the default `created_at` ascending read off the shares themselves, so it is not grouped or ordered by `round_number`.
- A site's party roster is ordered by `created_at` ascending, again matching the allocation enumeration order.

Lists also accept optional equality filters. Sites can be narrowed by `listing_status`, `epa_region` or `cost_posture`. Party rosters, waste-in lists (the party-scoped one as well as the site-scoped one), investigation lists, the site's decision-document list, allocation-round lists and the site's contract list accept `status`. Five nested lists deliberately accept no filter of their own and ignore any that is supplied: the two ownership-period lists, the alternatives of a feasibility study, the decision documents resting on an investigation and the contracts procured against a decision document. Consent decree lists accept `legal_status`. Share lists accept `disposition`. Sample lists accept `qa_status` and `medium`. Work-window lists accept `activity_type`. The recovery-action list is the one place where three foreign keys collapse into a single channel and accepts `site_id`, `respondent_prp_id`, `claimant_prp_id`, `action_type` and `status`. An unrecognised filter value for an enumerated field is a malformed request, as is an `epa_region` that is not an integer literal. The three identifier filters are by contrast not checked for existence and not validated in shape at all: an identifier that resolves to no record simply matches nothing and the call returns an empty page with `total_count` 0, never a 404. An identifier filter supplied with an entirely empty value is read as the filter not having been supplied at all, and because the match is a plain equality an action carrying no `claimant_prp_id` never matches a `claimant_prp_id` filter. Filtering always precedes ordering and ordering always precedes the pagination slice, so `total_count` counts the filtered set and `has_more` is true exactly when `offset` plus the number of records actually returned is still below `total_count`.

### 2.4. Identifiers, Timestamps and Record Versioning

Every resource carries a server-minted `id` formatted as a UUID, with no type prefix of any kind, and a `created_at` timestamp expressed as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits (for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`). Clients never supply an `id`.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp refreshed on every mutation and taken from the logical clock, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the resource is created and increases by exactly 1 on every subsequent mutation — including mutations that only touch derived fields. A recomputation of a site's oversight figures, a refresh of a party's volumetric share, or the stamping of a final percentage onto a share are all mutations in this sense, and each of them bumps `version` and refreshes `updated_at` on the resource that changed. The bump is settled before the response is rendered, so the resource a successful write returns in `data` already carries its **new** `version` and its refreshed `updated_at`, never the values the record held when the request arrived.

Three qualifications make that rule exact, and all three are observable. **The bump is per request, not per field or per recomputation**: however many of a request's hops touch the same record — a party whose volumes, share and eligibility are all rewritten by one verification — that record's `version` rises by exactly 1 for the whole request. **A recomputation that changes nothing is not a mutation**: when a re-derivation reaches the value the field already held, the record is left alone and neither `version` nor `updated_at` moves, so the parties of a site whose volumetric share happens not to shift are untouched by a verification elsewhere on the site. And **a refused request mutates nothing at all** — every gate of §2.6 is evaluated before any field is written, so a 4xx response never bumps a `version`, never refreshes an `updated_at` and never leaves a partial write behind. The two exceptions run the other way: opening an allocation round bumps the site's `version` and issuing a recovery action bumps the respondent party's `version` even though neither write changes a stored field on those records, because those two counters are precisely what the guards of §2.7 read.

Three record types are immutable once written and therefore carry **only** `id` and `created_at`: the ownership periods that make up a site's chain of title, the analytical results attached to an investigation, and the field-work windows scheduled against a contract. These three have no `updated_at` and no `version` field at all, and no operation in the system modifies them after creation.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

All time-dependent behaviour in the system reads a server-held **logical clock** rather than wall-clock time. The clock's startup value is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, it never falls back to real time, and any role may move it to an arbitrary instant by supplying a `current_utc` value to the clock-setting capability. The clock may be moved freely in either direction — backwards as readily as forwards, with no monotonicity requirement — and `current_utc` is parsed like every other client-supplied instant: an offset-bearing value is normalised to UTC, and a value with no time component at all is read as midnight UTC on that date. Setting the clock mutates no entity and increments no `version`; it answers HTTP 200 with a `data` object holding the single field `current_utc`, echoing the instant the clock now holds in the standard three-fractional-digit form.

Comment windows, penalty accrual windows, decree entry eligibility and five-year review due dates are all evaluated against this clock, which makes every deadline in the specification deterministic and reproducible.

### 2.6. Path Resolution and Validation Precedence

Requests are validated in a strictly ordered sequence, and the **first** failing check determines the response. A request that violates several rules at once always reports the earliest one in the order below; implementations that report a later error for such a request are wrong even though the request was correctly refused.

1. Authentication: both context headers present, role recognised — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. Authorisation: the acting role appears on the endpoint's authorised list — `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
3. Request shape: required fields present, types correct, enumerated values in range, integers within their declared bounds, monetary amounts supplied as integer counts of cents and never as decimal strings, decimal fields within their declared scale, arrays non-empty and free of duplicates, pagination within range — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Type checking is strict rather than coercive: a numeric field supplied as a string is malformed even when the string parses, a boolean is not accepted where an integer is declared and an integer is not accepted where a boolean is, and an explicit `null` counts as absent — so a `null` in a required field is reported as the field being missing, and a `null` in an optional field is simply the field not being supplied. A field the endpoint does not declare is ignored. An absent or entirely empty request body is read as an empty JSON object, so it is refused only by endpoints that have a required field to miss; a body that is not valid JSON, or that is valid JSON but not an object, is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422) — as is a body sent without a JSON content type.
4. Path existence, resolved **left to right**: the site before the round, the round before the share, the contract before the work window. The error reported is the one belonging to the first identifier in the path that does not resolve — `ERR_SITE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRP_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WASTE_IN_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REMEDIAL_INVESTIGATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RECORD_OF_DECISION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ALLOCATION_ROUND_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONSENT_DECREE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REMEDIATION_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_COST_RECOVERY_ACTION_NOT_FOUND`, all HTTP 404.
5. Body reference existence: every identifier carried inside a request body must resolve — `prp_id`, `remedial_investigation_id`, `record_of_decision_id`, `alternative_id`, `respondent_prp_id`, `claimant_prp_id` and each element of `party_prp_ids`. The applicable errors are `ERR_PRP_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REMEDIAL_INVESTIGATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RECORD_OF_DECISION_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_REMEDY_ALTERNATIVE_NOT_FOUND`. This check applies to ownership-period creation, waste-in submission, decision-document drafting, decision signature, consent decree drafting, contract solicitation and recovery-action issuance.
6. Actor identity for entity-backed roles — `ERR_ACTOR_IDENTITY_MISMATCH`. This guards party election recording, ballot casting, recovery-action responses and work-window scheduling.
7. Stale-version guards — `ERR_STALE_SITE_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_PRP_VERSION`, both HTTP 412 (see §2.7).
8. The site's cost posture is not frozen — `ERR_SITE_COST_FROZEN`.
9. The site's cost posture is not restricted — `ERR_SITE_COST_RESTRICTED`.
10. The target entity is in a state from which the requested transition is declared — `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. This includes every repeat of an already-applied transition: a second verification of an already-verified waste-in record, a second signature of an already-signed decision, an entry request against an already-entered decree, or a collection against an action that is still merely offered. Two endpoints depart from this position and both are called out where they are specified. The five-year review tests the determination-and-recommendation pair only after its phase gate and its due-date gate have both passed, so on that endpoint `ERR_FIVE_YEAR_REVIEW_NOT_DUE` outranks `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (§3.8). And a compliance event recorded against a settlement instrument tests the compliance dimension's own transition only after the site's phase gate and the entry gate have both passed, so on that endpoint both `ERR_SITE_PHASE_INVALID` and `ERR_DECREE_NOT_ENTERED` outrank `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`: an event of a type the instrument's current `compliance_status` does not admit, recorded against an instrument that has never been entered, reports `ERR_DECREE_NOT_ENTERED` (§3.7).
11. The site's listing phase permits the operation — `ERR_SITE_PHASE_INVALID`.
12. through 15. Referenced-document state gates: the cited investigation is approved before a comment period opens (`ERR_RIFS_NOT_APPROVED`), the cited decision is signed before a contract is solicited or awarded (`ERR_ROD_NOT_SIGNED`), the site has an adopted allocation before a decree is lodged or a recovery action is issued (`ERR_NO_ADOPTED_ALLOCATION`), and the decree is entered before any compliance event is recorded (`ERR_DECREE_NOT_ENTERED`).
16. through 21. Safety and integrity gates: the two segregation-of-duties checks (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, first on investigation review, then on decision signature), the listing eligibility score check (`ERR_HRS_SCORE_BELOW_THRESHOLD`), the valid-sample check (`ERR_NO_VALID_SAMPLES`), the community-involvement check (`ERR_COMMUNITY_INVOLVEMENT_INCOMPLETE`), and the remedy protectiveness check (`ERR_REMEDY_NOT_PROTECTIVE`).
22. through 24. Referential business gates: the named alternative belongs to the cited investigation (`ERR_ALTERNATIVE_NOT_FOR_SITE`), the party is in a participating posture (`ERR_PARTY_NOT_PARTICIPATING`), and the respondent actually carries an allocated share (`ERR_RESPONDENT_NOT_ALLOCATED`).
25. through 29. Temporal gates: `ERR_COMMENT_PERIOD_OPEN`, `ERR_COMMENT_PERIOD_CLOSED`, `ERR_DECREE_COMMENT_PERIOD_OPEN`, `ERR_FIVE_YEAR_REVIEW_NOT_DUE` and `ERR_OWNERSHIP_PERIOD_OVERLAP`, in that relative order.
30. through 36. Structural business constraints: `ERR_WORK_WINDOW_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_WEIGHTS_NOT_NORMALIZED`, `ERR_NO_ALLOCATION_BASIS`, `ERR_BALLOT_ALREADY_CAST`, `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_REMEDY`, `ERR_VERIFIED_VOLUME_EXCEEDS_CLAIM` and `ERR_NOT_DE_MINIMIS_ELIGIBLE`, in that relative order.
37. through 41. Fiscal constraints, evaluated last of all: `ERR_DE_MINIMIS_PREMIUM_INSUFFICIENT`, `ERR_BID_EXCEEDS_ESTIMATE`, `ERR_OVERSIGHT_CAP_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_OVERPAYMENT_NOT_ALLOWED` and `ERR_PENALTY_BALANCE_MISMATCH`, in that relative order.

The practical consequences of this ordering are worth spelling out. A frozen site rejects a cost-bearing request before the target entity's own state is examined, so a frozen site plus an already-adopted round reports the freeze, not the state conflict. Conversely, both posture checks come *after* the stale-version guard, so a client holding a stale view of the site learns that its view is stale before it learns that the site is frozen. And because every fiscal check sits at the very bottom, a bid that both exceeds the tolerance and targets an unsigned decision reports the unsigned decision.

Nested paths deserve one further note. When a path names more than one resource — for example an identifier under `/remediation-contracts/{id}/work-windows` — the parent is resolved first and its own phase and posture gates are applied before any child-level rule runs, so a request can never be judged against a child inside an invalid container.

### 2.7. Stale-Version Guards on Round and Action Creation

Two write operations in the system can silently discard work that the client has never seen, and both are therefore guarded by an explicit version check against the parent record. These are the only two endpoints in the system that take a version field; no other endpoint accepts one, and supplying one elsewhere has no effect. Both fields travel as plain non-negative integers, so a version supplied as a string, as a decimal, as a boolean or as a negative number is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` at the shape stage — decided before the path is resolved and therefore before the 412 could ever be reached.

Opening an allocation round for a site requires `expected_site_version` as a mandatory, non-optional field of the request. Its value must equal the site's current `version`. If any writer has advanced the site's `version` since the client last read the site, the create is refused with `ERR_STALE_SITE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The reason this guard is mandatory rather than advisory is that a new round legitimately supersedes whatever open round the site already had, so no state precondition would stop the create — a facilitator working from a stale view would quietly throw away a round it never observed. The guard forces the facilitator to re-read the site, see the round that now exists, and decide deliberately whether to reopen it or supersede it.

Issuing a recovery action against a party requires `expected_prp_version` as a mandatory, non-optional field, matching the **respondent party's** current `version` — not the site's. A mismatch is refused with `ERR_STALE_PRP_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Here too a new offer of the same instrument type legitimately supersedes the respondent's open offer, and worse, the offer's premium floor and the respondent's allocated exposure are both frozen from the party record at the instant of issuance. An offer built on a stale view of the party would be priced against figures that have already moved, so the author is forced to re-read the respondent first.

### 2.8. Money, Basis Points and Rounding Discipline

Money is never represented as a decimal in this system. Every monetary field is an **integer count of cents** — `oversight_cost_cap_cents`, `stipulated_penalty_per_day_cents`, `capital_cost_cents`, `annual_om_cost_cents`, `engineer_estimate_cents`, `awarded_amount_cents`, `demanded_cents`, `received_cents`, `incurred_cents`, `paid_penalty_cents` and every derived cents figure alike. A monetary value submitted as a decimal number or as a string is a malformed request.

Rates and weights are expressed in **basis points**, integers where 10,000 basis points is 100 percent. `discount_rate_bp`, `weight_volume_bp`, `weight_toxicity_bp`, `weight_ownership_bp` and the derived `oversight_indirect_rate_bp` all follow this convention; converting a basis-point figure to a fraction means dividing it by 10,000.

Non-monetary numbers travel in the JSON body as **plain JSON numbers, never as strings** — `hrs_score`, every `_pct`, `volume_tons`, `concentration_ug_per_l`, both risk figures and the hazard index alike. A field's declared scale therefore governs the rounding, not the rendering: a percentage rounded to `100.00` or `0.00` is emitted as the JSON number `100.0` or `0.0`, and a comparison against such a field is a numeric comparison rather than a textual one. Likewise a scale is a ceiling on precision and not a requirement of it — a field declared to two decimal places accepts a whole number or a one-decimal value and refuses only a third decimal.

The single rounding method used everywhere in the system is **round half up**: a value exactly halfway between two representable results rounds away from zero. Every rounding is performed on the exact value rather than on a binary floating-point approximation of it, so a term that is arithmetically a true half always rounds upward. Where a result is a whole-cent amount, it is rounded half up to zero decimal places. Percentages — allocation shares, share totals, oversight cap utilisation, volumetric shares — are rounded half up to 2 decimal places. Exposure point concentrations are rounded half up to 3 decimal places, excess lifetime cancer risks to 7 decimal places, and the hazard index to 2 decimal places. Day counts are whole days taken from the date part of the logical clock.

Two rounding disciplines matter enough to state as rules rather than as conventions. First, **where a calculation sums a series of rounded terms, each term is rounded before it enters the sum**, never once at the end; the two orders give different answers and only the per-term order is correct. Second, **every threshold comparison in this specification is inclusive at the stated boundary unless the text explicitly says otherwise** — a score exactly at the listing threshold qualifies, a subtotal exactly at a tier ceiling stays in that tier, a utilisation of exactly 90.00 percent restricts, and an acceptance weight exactly at the adoption threshold adopts.

### 2.9. The Cost Posture Gate

Beyond its listing phase, every site carries a second, derived state called `cost_posture`, whose value is one of `OPEN`, `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN`. It starts at `OPEN` when the site is proposed and is never set directly by any request; instead it is re-derived from scratch after every consent decree compliance event, every decree entry, every oversight cost posting, every change to a decision document's `status`, and every change to the site's `listing_status`. Those are the writes that can actually move it, but the posture is not reconciled in isolation: it travels with the rest of the site's derived block — `adopted_round_id`, the oversight figures of §3.7 and the cost ledger of §2.10 — and that whole block is re-reconciled together after any write that could touch any part of it. Re-deriving from scratch is what makes this safe to do liberally, since a reconciliation that arrives at the values the site already holds writes nothing and, per §2.4, bumps no `version`.

The derivation is a strict cascade evaluated in this order. If **any** consent decree of the site currently has a compliance posture of `PENALTY_ACCRUING`, the posture is `FROZEN`. Otherwise, the posture is `RESTRICTED` if **any** of four conditions holds: the site's `oversight_cap_utilization_pct` is at or above 90.00 percent; a consent decree of the site has a compliance posture of `IN_VIOLATION`; a decision document of the site has `status` of `AMENDMENT_REQUIRED`; or the site's `listing_status` is `REMEDY_REEVALUATION`. If neither the frozen condition nor any restricted condition holds, the posture is `OPEN`.

`FROZEN` strictly dominates `RESTRICTED`. A site that is simultaneously penalty-accruing and above 90 percent oversight utilisation reports `FROZEN`, and a request blocked by that site reports `ERR_SITE_COST_FROZEN`, never `ERR_SITE_COST_RESTRICTED`.

The two postures gate different, overlapping sets of operations. While a site is `FROZEN`, four operations are refused with `ERR_SITE_COST_FROZEN` (HTTP 409): posting oversight cost, opening an allocation round, issuing a recovery action, and scheduling a field-work window. While a site is `RESTRICTED`, three operations are refused with `ERR_SITE_COST_RESTRICTED` (HTTP 409): opening an allocation round, awarding a construction contract, and scheduling a field-work window — oversight postings and recovery actions remain permitted, because a restricted site is one whose exposure is elevated, not one whose books are closed.

The asymmetry is deliberate and worth reading twice. Opening an allocation round and scheduling a field-work window are the two operations blocked by *both* postures. Posting oversight cost and issuing a recovery action are blocked *only* by a freeze, so a merely restricted site can still bill oversight and still pursue money. Awarding a construction contract appears only in the restricted set, but its own transition precondition additionally requires the posture to be neither restricted nor frozen — so an award against a frozen site is also refused, reported as `ERR_SITE_COST_FROZEN` because that is the condition that actually holds. Note that the frozen condition is not a subset of the restricted conditions: a decree in `PENALTY_ACCRUING` freezes the site without necessarily satisfying any of the four restriction triggers, so the frozen case must be tested in its own right rather than inferred from the restricted case.

### 2.10. The Response-Cost Conservation Ledger

Each site maintains a four-way reconciliation of money advanced against money recovered, and the four figures are required to reconcile exactly after every single write, including compensating writes.

`total_response_cost_cents` is the site's `oversight_billed_cents` plus the `awarded_amount_cents` of every remediation contract of the site whose `status` is `AWARDED`, `CONSTRUCTION_COMPLETE` or `CLOSED`. A contract still in solicitation contributes nothing.

`recovered_cost_cents` is the sum of `received_cents` over the site's recovery actions whose `status` is `ACCEPTED` or `COLLECTED` — a partial receipt leaves its action in `ACCEPTED`, so money already booked on a partially paid action counts from the moment it arrives rather than only once the demand is satisfied. A voided action contributes nothing, because voiding zeroes that action's `received_cents`, and an action in any other status has never booked a receipt.

`orphan_write_off_cents` is `total_response_cost_cents` multiplied by the adopted round's `orphan_share_pct`, divided by 100 and rounded half up to whole cents. When the site has no adopted round, it is 0.

`unrecovered_cost_cents` is `total_response_cost_cents` less `recovered_cost_cents` less `orphan_write_off_cents`. **It may be negative and is never floored at zero** — an over-recovered site legitimately reports a negative unrecovered balance.

The conservation identity that must hold after every write is that `recovered_cost_cents` plus `orphan_write_off_cents` plus `unrecovered_cost_cents` equals `total_response_cost_cents` exactly. On the allocation side there is a matching identity: for an adopted round, `allocated_share_total_pct` plus `orphan_share_pct` plus `settled_share_pct` equals exactly 100.00. Neither identity is approximate, and neither is allowed to drift across a void-and-restore cycle.

Stipulated penalties sit deliberately outside this ledger. Money paid to cure a decree violation is punitive, not compensatory: it never increases `recovered_cost_cents` and never reduces `unrecovered_cost_cents`. The decree tracks it separately through `accrued_penalty_cents` and `paid_penalty_cents`.

A dedicated read exposes the whole reconciliation in a single call so that the identity can be checked without stitching several responses together. That read returns `total_response_cost_cents`, `recovered_cost_cents`, `orphan_write_off_cents`, `unrecovered_cost_cents`, `oversight_direct_cents`, `oversight_indirect_rate_bp`, `oversight_indirect_cents`, `oversight_billed_cents`, `oversight_cap_utilization_pct`, `allocated_share_total_pct`, `orphan_share_pct`, `settled_share_pct`, and a boolean `ledger_balanced` asserting that the conservation identity currently holds. It is a projection rather than a resource: `data` holds exactly those thirteen fields and nothing else — no `id`, no `created_at`, no `updated_at`, no `version` — every figure is read back from the site as already reconciled rather than recomputed for the read, and `ledger_balanced` is the literal arithmetic test on the four cent figures the same response reports. When the site has no adopted round the three share percentages all read `0.00` rather than empty.

### 2.11. Segregation of Duties and Facilitator Neutrality

Three independence rules are structural rather than procedural, and each has its own enforcement point.

Within an investigation, the user who authored it may not review it. The identifier stored as `authored_by_user_id` at creation is compared against the reviewer's `X-User-ID`, and a match is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 409). Within the decision document that rests on an approved investigation, the user recorded as `approved_by_user_id` on that investigation may not be the signer; a match is again refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Both rules compare project-manager identifiers, and both are evaluated in the order given in §2.6 — the review rule sits immediately ahead of the signature rule.

The third rule is enforced by the role model rather than by an error code: the facilitator who runs an allocation round has no capability to issue, collect or void a settlement, and settlement counsel has no capability to open, ballot or reopen a round. A user acting in the wrong capacity is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Listing a Site and Building the Party Roster

A remedial project manager opens the file on a contaminated site by proposing it, supplying the site's `name` as it will appear on the docket, the `epa_region` administering it as an integer from 1 through 10, and the `hrs_score` produced by the Hazard Ranking System screen as a number from 0.00 through 100.00 carrying at most two decimal places — a whole number such as `50` and a one-decimal value such as `28.5` are both well formed, and only a third decimal place is malformed. A newly proposed site begins in `PROPOSED` with its `cost_posture` at `OPEN`, its `listing_reference` empty, and every derived cost, volume and share figure at zero. The fields that are references or instants rather than figures begin empty instead: `oversight_cap_utilization_pct`, `adopted_round_id`, `construction_complete_at`, `five_year_review_due_at`, `last_five_year_review_at` and `last_protectiveness_determination` are all null on a freshly proposed site. Proposing a site returns the site at HTTP 201.

Listing the site on the National Priorities List advances `listing_status` from `PROPOSED` to `LISTED` and stamps the Federal Register docket reference the manager supplies as `listing_reference`, which is a required non-empty string on that request. Listing is permitted only from `PROPOSED`, and only when the site's `hrs_score` is at least 28.50 — a score of exactly 28.50 qualifies. A score below that threshold is refused with `ERR_HRS_SCORE_BELOW_THRESHOLD`; because the state precondition sits above the score check in §2.6, a site that is no longer `PROPOSED` reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` even when its score would also have failed. Listing returns the site, with its whole derived block re-reconciled, at HTTP 200.

From `LISTED` the site advances automatically rather than by explicit request. The first investigation accepted for the site moves it `LISTED → RI_FS_UNDERWAY`; a second investigation opened while the site is already `RI_FS_UNDERWAY` leaves `listing_status` untouched. Signing a decision document moves the site to `ROD_SIGNED`, awarding a construction contract moves it `ROD_SIGNED → CONSTRUCTION`, and certifying construction complete moves it `CONSTRUCTION → OM_PHASE`. From the operation-and-maintenance phase a five-year review either keeps the site in `OM_PHASE`, sends it to `REMEDY_REEVALUATION`, or retires it to `DELETED_FROM_NPL`; a reevaluated site returns to `ROD_SIGNED` when an amended decision is signed, closing the cycle `OM_PHASE → REMEDY_REEVALUATION → ROD_SIGNED → CONSTRUCTION → OM_PHASE`. `DELETED_FROM_NPL` is the site's only terminal phase, and while a site sits there every write scoped under it is refused with `ERR_SITE_PHASE_INVALID`.

That same phase error also guards the earlier stages of the lifecycle. Waste-in submissions, ownership periods and investigations all require the site to be `LISTED` or further along. Opening an allocation round requires the site to be `RI_FS_UNDERWAY` or further along, because there is nothing to allocate until an investigation exists. Recording a five-year review requires the site to be exactly in `OM_PHASE`. Each of those five operations reports `ERR_SITE_PHASE_INVALID` when the phase does not permit it.

Against the site, the manager names each potentially responsible party discovered by site-history research, supplying the party's `legal_name`, its `party_type` — one of `OWNER_OPERATOR`, `GENERATOR`, `TRANSPORTER` or `ARRANGER` — and its `viability_score`, an integer from 0 through 100 produced by the ability-to-pay screen. Each party record names the `site_id` it belongs to. A freshly named party begins with `status` `IDENTIFIED`, with `submitted_volume_tons`, `verified_volume_tons`, `ownership_days_total` and `volumetric_share_pct` all at zero, with `max_toxicity_class` empty, with `notice_reference` empty, with `de_minimis_eligible` false and with `contribution_protection` false, and the created party is what comes back at HTTP 201. Naming a party is deliberately **not** gated on the site's listing phase: unlike the five operations listed above it is accepted while the site is still merely `PROPOSED`, and the only phase that refuses it is the terminal `DELETED_FROM_NPL`, which refuses every write scoped under the site.

Issuing the general notice letter moves the party `IDENTIFIED → NOTIFIED` and stamps the reference the manager supplies as `notice_reference`, a required non-empty string. Notice is permitted only from `IDENTIFIED`, and only while the site is past proposal and not yet deleted from the docket — that is, `listing_status` is neither `PROPOSED` nor `DELETED_FROM_NPL`.

Once noticed, the party itself speaks. Its representative records an `election` of either `PARTICIPATE` or `DECLINE`, optionally accompanied by a `response_note` — the note is validated as a non-empty string when it is supplied but is not persisted anywhere, since the party record has no field for it. Electing to participate moves the party `NOTIFIED → PARTICIPATING`; declining moves it `NOTIFIED → RECALCITRANT`. Both directions remain open afterwards: a recalcitrant party may later elect to participate (`RECALCITRANT → PARTICIPATING`), and a participating party may later decline (`PARTICIPATING → RECALCITRANT`), so the re-election loop may run as many times as the negotiation requires. There is one brake on withdrawal: a participating party that has already been named as a signatory of a consent decree of this site whose `legal_status` is `LODGED` or `ENTERED` may not decline, because its commitment is already before the court — that brake reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, the same identifier a decline from an ineligible posture reports. Every one of these elections requires that the acting representative's `X-User-ID` be the party's own `id`, and both an election and a notice return the **party** at HTTP 200.

When a party turns out to be defunct or insolvent, the manager records that fact with a `defunct_reason` — a required non-empty string that, like the election note, is validated and then not persisted — and the party moves to `ORPHAN` from `NOTIFIED`, `PARTICIPATING` or `RECALCITRANT`; from any other posture, `IDENTIFIED` included, the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. The response returns the **party** at HTTP 200; the shares and rounds the cascade below touches are read back from their own endpoints. `ORPHAN` is the party's only truly terminal posture. `SETTLED` and `DE_MINIMIS_SETTLED` are quiescent rather than terminal — voiding the instrument that produced them returns the party to `PARTICIPATING`, and §3.9 describes that reversal in full.

Recording a party defunct fires a three-part cascade, and every part must land. In the site's adopted allocation round, that party's share captures its current `final_pct` into `pre_settlement_final_pct`, sets `final_pct` to 0.00, and moves its `disposition` from `ALLOCATED` to `ORPHANED`; the round's `orphan_share_pct` rises by exactly that amount while `allocated_share_total_pct` falls by the same amount. That first part is conditional on the share still being `ALLOCATED`: a share whose `disposition` has already moved to `SETTLED` by a de minimis buyout, or to `ORPHANED` already, is left exactly as it stands, and a site with no adopted round has nothing to convert. Rather than being adjusted by increments, the round's three percentage totals are always re-derived from the dispositions its shares now carry — which is what keeps them summing to exactly 100.00 however many times the composition shifts. In any round of the site that is still `DRAFTING` or `BALLOTING`, that party's share instead has its `ballot` stamped `ABSTAIN` — a conditional cascade that touches only rounds not yet adopted — and the round's `balloted_count` and `accept_share_pct` are recomputed accordingly. That stamping writes nothing else on the share: `ballot_cast_at` and `ballot_rationale` are left exactly as they were, so a share whose party never voted still reports both empty, and a share whose party had already voted keeps the instant and the rationale of that earlier ballot under the substituted `ABSTAIN`. Nor does the cascade resolve the round: an abstention it stamps neither adopts nor fails a round even when it brings `balloted_count` up to `share_count`, because only a ballot the party itself casts resolves a round, as §3.6 describes. Finally, the site's `orphan_write_off_cents` and `unrecovered_cost_cents` are recomputed, since an orphaned share is money that will never be recovered. The party's own `version` increases by 1, as does the `version` of each share and round the cascade touched.

### 3.2. The Evidentiary Record: Chain of Title and Waste-In Verification

Liability at a Superfund site is proved from two independent evidentiary families, and the system keeps them separate all the way through. The chain of title establishes who held the property and for how long; the waste-in records establish who sent what, in what quantity, and how toxic it was.

Each link in the chain of title is recorded by the project manager as an ownership period naming the holding party through `prp_id`, an inclusive `start_date`, an exclusive `end_date`, and the `acquisition_type` found in the title search — one of `PURCHASE`, `MERGER`, `FORECLOSURE` or `LEASE`. Every one of those is required except `end_date`, which is optional and stored empty when omitted. Both dates are plain ISO calendar dates with no time component. The named party must be a party **of this very site**: a `prp_id` that resolves to a party of some other site is reported as `ERR_PRP_NOT_FOUND`, exactly as an identifier that resolves to nothing at all is. An empty `end_date` means the party still holds title, and for all interval arithmetic such an open period is treated as ending on 9999-12-31. From the two dates the system derives `ownership_days` as the whole number of days on the half-open interval that runs from the start date up to but not including the end date; a period whose end date equals its start date therefore yields zero days. The two dates are not required to be in order — a period whose `end_date` falls before its `start_date` is accepted and yields a negative `ownership_days`, which then reduces the party's `ownership_days_total` accordingly.

The chain of title is a partition of time, so **overlapping ownership is a data error rather than a shared period** — and the rule is site-wide, not party-wide. A proposed period conflicts with an existing period of the same site when the proposed start date falls strictly before the existing end date **and** the existing start date falls strictly before the proposed end date; that test applies across any pair of parties, not merely two periods of the same party. Touching endpoints do not conflict: a period ending on 1978-06-01 and a period starting on 1978-06-01 are adjacent, and both are accepted. A genuine overlap is refused with `ERR_OWNERSHIP_PERIOD_OVERLAP`, which is tested only after the site's listing phase has been accepted, so a site that is not yet `LISTED` reports `ERR_SITE_PHASE_INVALID` even when the proposed dates also collide. Ownership periods are immutable once written; recording one refreshes the holding party's `ownership_days_total`, which is simply the sum of `ownership_days` over that party's periods for the site, and re-derives the provisional percentages of any round of the site that is currently drafting. Those two recomputations are the whole of the side effect — the site's own posture and cost ledger are not touched, because no ownership figure feeds them. The response returns the **ownership period** at HTTP 201, and being immutable it carries neither `updated_at` nor `version`.

Waste-in submissions arrive with the party they are attributed to, again through `prp_id`, the `volume_tons` claimed (a positive number with at most two decimal places), the `toxicity_class` of the waste stream (`I`, `II`, `III` or `IV`), the `evidence_grade` recording the documentary strength of the record (`A`, `B` or `C`), the `disposal_year` as a calendar year from 1940 through 2026, and optionally the `transporter_name` from the manifest, which is stored empty when omitted. Here too the `prp_id` must name a party of this very site, and a party of another site is `ERR_PRP_NOT_FOUND`. A newly filed record begins with `status` `SUBMITTED`, an `effective_volume_tons` equal to the claimed `volume_tons`, and an empty `verified_volume_tons` and `verified_by_user_id`; it immediately lifts the attributed party's `submitted_volume_tons`, and because the provisional variant of §3.6 counts submitted records at their claimed volume it also re-derives the provisional percentages of any round of the site still drafting. It leaves the party's `verified_volume_tons`, `max_toxicity_class`, `volumetric_share_pct` and `de_minimis_eligible` exactly where they stood, and the site's `verified_waste_volume_tons` with them, since all five read verified records only. The response returns the **waste-in record** at HTTP 201.

Verification is where a claim becomes evidence. A project manager records a `decision` of either `VERIFIED` or `REJECTED`, optionally with a `verification_note`, and the acting user's identifier is stamped onto the record as `verified_by_user_id` in both cases. A verification must carry `verified_volume_tons`, which must be strictly positive and no greater than the record's claimed `volume_tons` — verification may accept less volume than was claimed, but never more. A rejection must **not** carry `verified_volume_tons` at all. Violating either side of that pairing — a figure above the claim, a non-positive figure, a missing figure on a verification, or a figure supplied alongside a rejection — is refused with `ERR_VERIFIED_VOLUME_EXCEEDS_CLAIM` (HTTP 422). The only shape rule `verified_volume_tons` carries in its own right is the two-decimal scale, so a zero or negative figure is well formed as a request and is caught by that pairing rule rather than by `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Verification is available only from `SUBMITTED`; both `VERIFIED` and `REJECTED` are terminal for the record, so a second verification attempt is refused as an invalid transition — and because the pairing rule sits at the bottom of §2.6, an already-verified record reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` even when the figure supplied is also invalid. The response returns the **waste-in record** at HTTP 200.

Verification fires the deepest cascade in the system, and each hop is caused by the one before it. **The first hop** rewrites the record itself: `status` becomes `VERIFIED` with `verified_volume_tons` and `effective_volume_tons` both set to the accepted figure, or `status` becomes `REJECTED` with `effective_volume_tons` set to 0. Read as a rule, `effective_volume_tons` is the verified volume once the record is verified, the claimed volume while it is still submitted, and zero once it is rejected.

**The second hop** follows from the changed effective volume. The attributed party's `submitted_volume_tons` is re-derived as the sum of `effective_volume_tons` over its records that are either submitted or verified; its `verified_volume_tons` as the sum of `verified_volume_tons` over its verified records only; its `max_toxicity_class` as the most hazardous class across its verified records, where class `I` outranks `II`, which outranks `III`, which outranks `IV`, and which is left empty when the party holds no verified record at all. The site's `verified_waste_volume_tons` is re-derived as the sum of `verified_volume_tons` over every verified record of the site. Because that site total is the denominator of every party's `volumetric_share_pct`, **every** party of the site — not only the one whose record moved — has its `volumetric_share_pct` recomputed as its own `verified_volume_tons` divided by the site total, multiplied by 100 and rounded half up to two decimal places, reported as 0.00 when the site total is zero. And because eligibility for the small-contributor programme reads that share, every party of the site also has `de_minimis_eligible` recomputed.

A party qualifies as `de_minimis_eligible` only when all of the following hold together: its `volumetric_share_pct` is at most 1.00 percent, its `max_toxicity_class` is either `III` or `IV`, its `verified_volume_tons` is at most 110.00 tons, and its `max_toxicity_class` is not empty — which is to say a party holding no verified record is never eligible, however small its claim. Every one of these comparisons is inclusive at the ceiling, and critically **the share test is applied to the rounded figure**: a party whose unrounded share is 1.004 percent rounds to 1.00 and is eligible, while a party at 1.005 percent rounds to 1.01 and is not.

**The third hop** follows from the changed party volumes. Every share of the site's allocation round has its `provisional_pct` re-derived against the new data — but only while that round is `DRAFTING`. Rounds that have already moved to `BALLOTING`, `ADOPTED`, `FAILED` or `SUPERSEDED` are left entirely untouched, because provisional percentages freeze the moment a round leaves drafting and become the weights parties vote on. A rejection runs exactly the same second and third hops as a verification, with the rejected record contributing zero.

### 3.3. Remedial Investigation, Sampling and Risk Characterisation

Against a listed site a project manager opens a remedial investigation and feasibility study, supplying a `scope_summary` narrating the contaminant delineation extent and the `exposure_medium` the risk assessment will be driven from — one of `SOIL`, `GROUNDWATER`, `AIR` or `SEDIMENT`. Both fields are required. The investigation records the `site_id` it belongs to, begins with `status` `DRAFT`, and stamps the opening manager's identifier as `authored_by_user_id`; it starts with `approved_by_user_id` empty, `revision_round_count` and `valid_sample_count` at 0, both exposure-point concentrations, both risk figures and the `hazard_index` all empty, and `action_level_exceeded` false. The first investigation the site accepts also advances the site's own phase, as §3.1 describes. The response returns the **investigation** at HTTP 201 — the site's advanced phase is read back from the site.

Analytical results are attached to the investigation one at a time. Each carries the `remedial_investigation_id` it belongs to, the `medium` actually sampled, the `contaminant` analysed, the reported `concentration_ug_per_l` in micrograms per litre (a non-negative number with at most three decimal places), the laboratory's data-validation flag as `qa_status` — `VALID`, `ESTIMATED` or `REJECTED` — the field `collected_at` timestamp, and the `lab_reference` of the data package. Every one of those but the `remedial_investigation_id`, which comes from the path, is a required body field. Results are immutable once attached, and the accepted result is what comes back, at HTTP 201, carrying neither `updated_at` nor `version`. Attaching one is not gated on the investigation's own `status`: a result may be added while the study is in draft, under review, awaiting revision or already approved, and attaching it never moves that status. Each new result refreshes the investigation's `valid_sample_count`, which counts only those results whose `medium` equals the investigation's `exposure_medium` **and** whose `qa_status` is `VALID` — but it refreshes nothing else, so a result attached after submission leaves the frozen exposure, risk and `action_level_exceeded` figures exactly as the submission computed them until the study is submitted again.

Submitting the investigation for review moves it `DRAFT → UNDER_REVIEW`, and it is also the way a revision re-enters review (`REVISION_REQUESTED → UNDER_REVIEW`), closing the cycle `UNDER_REVIEW → REVISION_REQUESTED → UNDER_REVIEW` as many times as the review requires. The submission request declares no fields of its own, so an absent or empty body is well formed. Submission is permitted only when `valid_sample_count` is at least 1 — the count is re-derived from the results on file immediately before the test, rather than trusted as stored — and an investigation with no result of its own exposure medium carrying a valid quality flag cannot leave draft, the attempt being refused with `ERR_NO_VALID_SAMPLES`. The response returns the **investigation** at HTTP 200, carrying the figures the submission just froze.

Submission is also the moment the risk assessment is frozen against the current sample set, and it computes two deliberately different exposure figures from the same data. First the candidate set is narrowed to results of this investigation whose `medium` matches the investigation's `exposure_medium`; a result of any other medium is invisible here **even when its quality flag is valid**. Within that medium-filtered set, the central-tendency set is the results flagged `VALID`, while the reasonable-maximum-exposure set is the results flagged either `VALID` or `ESTIMATED`. Results flagged `REJECTED` belong to neither set.

The `central_tendency_epc_ug_per_l` is the arithmetic mean of `concentration_ug_per_l` over the central-tendency set, rounded half up to three decimal places. The `rme_epc_ug_per_l` is instead the single largest `concentration_ug_per_l` in the reasonable-maximum-exposure set, taken as reported rather than averaged. Because the second set is the wider one, whenever an estimated result happens to be the highest concentration on file the reasonable-maximum figure is strictly larger than it would have been from valid results alone — which is exactly the conservatism the two-variant design is there to expose.

From those two concentrations, three risk figures follow. The `central_tendency_risk` is the central-tendency concentration multiplied by the unit risk factor of 0.000005 excess lifetime cancers per microgram per litre, rounded half up to seven decimal places. The `rme_risk` is the reasonable-maximum concentration multiplied by the same unit risk factor, likewise rounded half up to seven decimal places. The `hazard_index` is the reasonable-maximum concentration divided by the reference concentration of 5.0 micrograms per litre, rounded half up to two decimal places.

The flag that everything downstream keys on is `action_level_exceeded`, and it is set to true when **either** the `rme_risk` is at least the action level of 0.0001 — one excess cancer per ten thousand exposed people — **or** the `hazard_index` is at least 1.00. Both comparisons are inclusive: an `rme_risk` of exactly 0.0001000 exceeds, and a `hazard_index` of exactly 1.00 exceeds. Note that only the reasonable-maximum variant drives this flag; the central-tendency figures are reported for the administrative record but never gate anything. Because the flag has just moved, submission also re-scores every remedial alternative already recorded under the investigation, as §3.4 explains.

Review is the segregation-of-duties gate. A project manager records a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `REQUEST_REVISION`, optionally with a `review_note`, but never on their own work: if the reviewer's `X-User-ID` equals the investigation's `authored_by_user_id`, the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. A revision request moves the investigation `UNDER_REVIEW → REVISION_REQUESTED` and increases `revision_round_count` by 1, counting how many times the document has been sent back. An approval moves it `UNDER_REVIEW → APPROVED` and stamps the reviewer's identifier as `approved_by_user_id`, leaving `revision_round_count` alone. The optional `review_note` is validated when supplied and then discarded; the investigation has no field for it. `APPROVED` is terminal for the investigation, and that stored approver identifier is precisely what the decision-document signature check later excludes. Either decision returns the **investigation** at HTTP 200.

### 3.4. Feasibility Alternatives, Present Value and Remedy Scoring

The feasibility half of the study screens candidate remedies. A project manager records each alternative under the investigation with a short `label` such as "Pump and Treat", the up-front `capital_cost_cents` and the recurring `annual_om_cost_cents` (both non-negative integer cent amounts), the number of `om_years` included in the present-value analysis (from 1 through 100), the real `discount_rate_bp` in basis points (from 0 through 2000, so at most 20 percent), and two nine-criteria scores as integers from 0 through 100: the `effectiveness_score` for long-term effectiveness and permanence, and the `implementability_score`. All seven of those fields are required. Each alternative names the `remedial_investigation_id` whose feasibility study screened it, and carries a `selected_by_rod_id` that stays empty until a signed decision document selects it. Recording one is not gated on the investigation's `status` — an alternative may be added to a study still in draft or to one already approved — and its `remedy_score` is computed against the investigation's `action_level_exceeded` flag exactly as that flag stands at the moment of creation, which for a study that has never been submitted is false. The response returns the **alternative** at HTTP 201, already carrying `npv_cents`, `cost_score` and `remedy_score`.

Present value is computed once, at creation, and is never recomputed afterwards because its cost inputs are immutable. The discount rate is the basis-point figure divided by 10,000. The `npv_cents` is the `capital_cost_cents` plus, for each operating year counted from 1 up to and including `om_years`, the `annual_om_cost_cents` divided by one plus the discount rate raised to the power of that year number — **with each annual term rounded half up to whole cents before it is added into the running total**. This order of operations is mandatory and observable: summing the undiscounted stream of discounted terms and rounding once at the end produces a different, wrong answer, off by a cent or more on realistic inputs.

From the present value comes a normalised `cost_score`. Take 100 and subtract the present value expressed in units of 50,000,000 cents — that is, in units of half a million dollars. Clamp the result to the closed range from 0 through 100, taking whichever of the raw value and 0 is larger, then whichever of that and 100 is smaller, and round half up to two decimal places. An alternative whose present value reaches five billion cents therefore scores 0.00, and a costless alternative scores 100.00.

The composite `remedy_score` weights the three criteria, and **which weighting applies depends on the parent investigation's `action_level_exceeded` flag**. When the action level has been exceeded, the score is 0.65 times the `effectiveness_score`, plus 0.20 times the `implementability_score`, plus 0.15 times the `cost_score`, rounded half up to two decimal places — permanence dominates, cost barely matters. When the action level has not been exceeded, the score is 0.45 times the `effectiveness_score`, plus 0.35 times the `implementability_score`, plus 0.20 times the `cost_score`, again rounded half up to two decimal places. The two branches are emphatically not a rescaling of one another: the same pair of alternatives can rank in opposite orders under the two weightings, which is why re-submitting an investigation re-scores every one of its alternatives rather than only the newly added one.

When a decision document is signed without naming an alternative explicitly, the system selects one. The candidate pool is the alternatives belonging to the investigation that the decision document cites — alternatives under any other investigation are simply invisible to the selection. If the cited investigation has `action_level_exceeded` true, the pool is first narrowed to alternatives whose `effectiveness_score` is at least 60, the minimum the agency will accept as protective. If the pool is empty after that filter — because every alternative fails the protectiveness bar, or because the investigation has no alternatives at all — the signature is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_REMEDY`.

The winner is the pool member with the greatest `remedy_score`, compared on the stored two-decimal values rather than on unrounded intermediates. When two alternatives tie on that score, the one with the lower `npv_cents` wins. When they tie on both, the one created earlier wins, ordered by `created_at` and never by `id`.

### 3.5. The Record of Decision: Community Involvement and Signature

The Record of Decision is the document that commits the agency to a remedy, and its lifecycle is built around the statutory requirement that the affected community be heard before the commitment is made. A project manager drafts one against the site, citing the `remedial_investigation_id` it rests on, publishing a `proposed_plan_summary` for community review, and fixing the length of the public comment window as `comment_period_days`, an integer from 30 through 90. All three fields are required, and the cited investigation must belong to this very site — one that exists under another site is reported as `ERR_REMEDIAL_INVESTIGATION_NOT_FOUND`. Drafting is deliberately **not** gated on the cited investigation's `status`: a document may be drafted against a study still in draft, under review or awaiting revision, and the approval requirement bites only when the comment period is opened. The document records the `site_id` it covers, begins with `status` `DRAFT`, and starts with `public_comments_count` at zero, an empty `responsiveness_summary`, an empty `selected_alternative_id`, an empty `comment_period_start` and `comment_period_end`, and an empty `signed_by_user_id` and `signed_at`. The response returns the **decision document** at HTTP 201.

Opening the comment period moves the document `DRAFT → PUBLIC_COMMENT`, but only if the cited investigation has reached `APPROVED`; a decision resting on an investigation still in draft, under review or awaiting revision is refused with `ERR_RIFS_NOT_APPROVED`. The request declares no fields of its own, so an absent or empty body is well formed. On acceptance the system stamps `comment_period_start` with the current logical clock and derives `comment_period_end` as that instant plus `comment_period_days` days. **The comment window is the closed interval running from the start instant through the end instant inclusive.** The response returns the **decision document** at HTTP 200.

While the window is open, a community member or a party's representative files a comment by supplying a `commenter_name` and the `comment_text`, both required non-empty strings. A representative filing here is **not** identity-checked: the decision document references no party, so any `PRPRepresentative` may comment and the acting `X-User-ID` need not resolve to a party of the site at all. Each accepted comment increases the document's `public_comments_count` by 1 and bumps the document's `version`; comment bodies themselves are not persisted and not separately addressable resources, and the response returns the **decision document** at HTTP 200 with only that counter moved. A comment filed after the window has closed — that is, when the logical clock has moved strictly past `comment_period_end` — or filed against a document that is not currently in `PUBLIC_COMMENT`, is refused with `ERR_COMMENT_PERIOD_CLOSED`.

Signature is the most heavily guarded operation in the system, and it takes only two inputs: the `responsiveness_summary` text that answers the comments received, and an optional `alternative_id` naming the remedy to adopt. Omitting `alternative_id` hands the choice to the selection logic of §3.4. Six conditions must all hold.

The document must be in `PUBLIC_COMMENT`. The comment window must have **closed**: the logical clock must be strictly past `comment_period_end`, and a signature attempted at or before that instant is refused with `ERR_COMMENT_PERIOD_OPEN` — note that this is the exact mirror image of the comment-filing rule, so there is no instant at which both filing and signing are legal. The community must actually have been heard: `public_comments_count` must be at least 1, and a document with no comments on file is refused with `ERR_COMMUNITY_INVOLVEMENT_INCOMPLETE`. The signer must not be the user who approved the cited investigation: if `X-User-ID` equals that investigation's `approved_by_user_id`, the signature is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. An explicitly named alternative must belong to the very investigation this document cites — an alternative that exists but was screened under a different investigation is refused with `ERR_ALTERNATIVE_NOT_FOR_SITE`, while an `alternative_id` that names no alternative at all anywhere in the system is refused with `ERR_REMEDY_ALTERNATIVE_NOT_FOUND`. Finally, when the cited investigation has `action_level_exceeded` true, an explicitly named alternative must have an `effectiveness_score` of at least 60; a less protective explicit choice is refused with `ERR_REMEDY_NOT_PROTECTIVE`. That bar is read against the flag of the investigation *this document* cites, not against the flag of whichever investigation screened the named alternative; and because the protectiveness test sits above the belonging test in the ladder of §2.6, an explicit alternative that both belongs to another investigation and scores below 60 reports `ERR_REMEDY_NOT_PROTECTIVE` rather than `ERR_ALTERNATIVE_NOT_FOR_SITE`. That protectiveness rule applies **only to an explicitly named alternative** — when the choice is delegated, the selection logic filters the pool instead and reports `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_REMEDY` if nothing survives.

On acceptance, the winning alternative's `selected_by_rod_id` is set to this document's `id`, and the document's own `selected_alternative_id` mirrors that alternative's `id` — the mirror is a derived convenience, and the authoritative link lives on the alternative. The document's `status` becomes `SIGNED`, `signed_by_user_id` records the signer, `signed_at` records the logical clock instant, and `responsiveness_summary` captures the supplied text. The site's phase advances at the same moment, from `RI_FS_UNDERWAY → ROD_SIGNED` for an original decision or from `REMEDY_REEVALUATION → ROD_SIGNED` for an amended one. Signature itself carries **no** phase gate beyond the terminal one: from any other phase — a site already in `ROD_SIGNED`, `CONSTRUCTION` or `OM_PHASE`, or one still merely `LISTED` — the signature is accepted and `listing_status` is left exactly as it was. The response returns the **decision document** at HTTP 200; the alternative's stamped `selected_by_rod_id` and the site's advanced phase are read back from their own endpoints.

An amended signature carries one further cascade. When the site was in `REMEDY_REEVALUATION`, every decision document of that site whose `status` is `AMENDMENT_REQUIRED` moves to `WITHDRAWN` — a strictly conditional cascade that touches documents awaiting amendment and never touches a document that is merely `SIGNED`. Because the amendment-required condition was one of the four triggers that held the site's `cost_posture` at `RESTRICTED`, withdrawing those documents re-derives the posture and can release the restriction.

Both `SIGNED` and `WITHDRAWN` are terminal for a decision document, with one exception in the reverse direction: a five-year review that finds the remedy not protective moves a `SIGNED` document to `AMENDMENT_REQUIRED`, reopening remedy selection. §3.8 describes that path. A read is available that returns the selected alternative as an array holding either exactly one element or none at all, so an unsigned document reports an empty array rather than an error.

### 3.6. Allocation Rounds: Gore-Factor Weighting, Balloting and Adoption

Allocation is a negotiation, not a calculation, but the negotiation runs on calculated numbers. The facilitator opens a round against the site and chooses how much each of the three Gore factors counts, supplying `weight_volume_bp`, `weight_toxicity_bp` and `weight_ownership_bp` in basis points. Each weight is an integer from 0 through 10,000, and **the three must sum to exactly 10,000** — that is, to exactly 100 percent. Any other total is refused with `ERR_WEIGHTS_NOT_NORMALIZED` (HTTP 422). The same request must carry `expected_site_version` as described in §2.7, and it is refused before anything else is examined if the site is frozen, or if the site is merely restricted, or if the site has not yet reached the investigation phase.

A new round begins with `status` `DRAFTING`, stamps the opening facilitator's identifier as `facilitator_user_id`, and takes a `round_number` of 1 for the site's first round and otherwise the site's previous highest round number plus 1 — **counting superseded and failed rounds**, so numbers are never reused. Its `accept_share_pct` and all three of its percentage totals begin at 0.00, its `share_count` and `balloted_count` at 0, and its `adopted_at` and `revision_note` empty. The create declares no state precondition of its own — a site whose every earlier round is already adopted or superseded may still be given another — and it answers with the **round** at HTTP 201, already carrying the share count and the provisional percentages the opening just derived. Opening a round increments the site's own `version`, and it supersedes whatever open round the site already had: a round in `DRAFTING`, `BALLOTING` or `FAILED` moves to `SUPERSEDED`. Both `ADOPTED` and `SUPERSEDED` are terminal.

Opening the round also mints one allocation share per candidate party. The candidate set is exactly those parties of the site whose `status` is anything other than `IDENTIFIED` — that is, `NOTIFIED`, `PARTICIPATING`, `RECALCITRANT`, `ORPHAN`, `SETTLED` or `DE_MINIMIS_SETTLED` — captured at the instant the round is opened, and enumerated in party `created_at` ascending order, never by `id`. A party that has never been noticed is not allocable and receives no share at all; a party noticed after the round was opened can only enter through a reopening. Each share records the `allocation_round_id` it belongs to and the `prp_id` it is computed for, and starts with `disposition` `PENDING`, an empty `ballot`, an empty `ballot_rationale`, an empty `ballot_cast_at`, an empty `final_pct` and an empty `pre_settlement_final_pct`. The round tracks `share_count` as the number of shares it holds and `balloted_count` as the number of those whose `ballot` has been cast.

**The share arithmetic.** For each candidate party the system computes three factor contributions. The volumetric contribution is the sum, over that party's waste-in records in the variant's record set, of the variant's volume field. The toxicity contribution is the sum, over the same records, of that same volume field multiplied by the toxicity multiplier of the record's `toxicity_class`, where class `I` multiplies by 8, class `II` by 4, class `III` by 2 and class `IV` by 1. The ownership contribution is simply the party's `ownership_days_total` for the site, and it is identical in both variants because ownership periods are immutable once written.

The three site-wide totals are the sums of those contributions across the candidate set. A factor whose site-wide total is zero has no basis, and its weight is dropped rather than distributed: the surviving weights are rescaled by their own sum, and **the dropped factor's weight is not redistributed pro rata across the survivors**. Concretely, the denominator is the sum of just those weights whose factor has a positive total. If all three totals are zero — no volume, no toxicity, no ownership days anywhere in the candidate set — there is no basis to allocate on at all, and the request is refused with `ERR_NO_ALLOCATION_BASIS`; this guard applies both when a round is opened and when it is moved to balloting, and to those two facilitator transitions only. There is one further degenerate case the rescaling admits, and it is not an error: the surviving weights can themselves sum to zero, when the only factors with a positive total are ones the facilitator weighted at 0. The denominator is then 0, no factor contributes anything, every party's raw share is 0.00, and the entire 100.00 residual consequently lands on the single residual-taking party identified below — so a round can legitimately allocate one party 100.00 percent and every other party 0.00.

A party's raw share is then the sum of up to three products: its volumetric weight divided by the denominator, multiplied by its volumetric contribution divided by the volumetric total, contributing nothing when the volumetric total is zero; plus its toxicity weight divided by the denominator, multiplied by its toxicity contribution divided by the toxicity total, contributing nothing when the toxicity total is zero; plus its ownership weight divided by the denominator, multiplied by its ownership contribution divided by the ownership total, contributing nothing when the ownership total is zero. That raw fraction is multiplied by 100 and **rounded half up to two decimal places per party, before any summation** — never summed first and rounded once.

Because each party is rounded independently, the rounded percentages will usually not total exactly 100.00. The residual, computed as 100.00 minus the sum of the rounded party percentages, is assigned in full to **one** party: the party with the largest volumetric contribution. Should two parties tie on that, the one with the larger ownership contribution takes the residual; should they still tie, the party created earliest takes it, ordered by `created_at` and never by `id`. The residual may be negative, in which case that party absorbs the shortfall.

**The two variants of the arithmetic use different record sets, and this is the crux of the negotiation.** The provisional variant counts every waste-in record of the site whose `status` is `SUBMITTED` or `VERIFIED`, valued at `effective_volume_tons` — so an unverified submission counts at its full claimed volume and a party whose claim is later cut keeps its full negotiating weight until then. The final variant counts only records whose `status` is `VERIFIED`, valued at `verified_volume_tons`; rejected and still-submitted records contribute nothing to either the volumetric or the toxicity factor. On identical data the two variants therefore produce different percentages, and a rejected or reduced waste-in shifts share onto the remaining parties. The provisional variant writes `provisional_pct`; the final variant writes `final_pct` and runs exactly once, at adoption. The `ERR_NO_ALLOCATION_BASIS` guard is not re-applied at that moment and there is no way to refuse an adoption the ballot has already decided, so if the final variant's three factor totals have all fallen to zero — every waste-in record rejected and no ownership day anywhere — adoption still completes, with the degenerate residual rule above handing 100.00 to one party and 0.00 to the rest.

Provisional percentages are re-derived whenever a round is opened, whenever it is reopened, and whenever a waste-in submission, a waste-in verification or an ownership-period creation changes the underlying data **while the round is still drafting** — a submission counts here because the provisional variant values submitted records at their claimed volume, so filing one shifts the provisional split immediately. Such an evidentiary re-derivation is never refused: if it finds that the basis has disappeared entirely — every factor total now zero — it writes `provisional_pct` 0.00 onto every share of the drafting round and leaves the round in place, rather than reporting `ERR_NO_ALLOCATION_BASIS` against a verification that has nothing to do with the facilitator. Moving the round to balloting freezes them: from that moment they are the voting weights and no later evidentiary change touches them. Balloting requires the round to be drafting and to hold at least 2 shares — a single-party allocation is not a negotiation — and both of those failures report the one `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. The request declares no fields of its own, so an absent or empty body is well formed; the transition itself neither re-derives nor re-rounds `provisional_pct`, which stays exactly as the last drafting-time derivation left it, and the response returns the **round** at HTTP 200.

Each party's representative then casts a `ballot` of `ACCEPT`, `REJECT` or `ABSTAIN`, optionally with a `ballot_rationale`, on that party's own share; the acting `X-User-ID` must be the balloting party's `id`. That identity test is performed by locating the share *this round* holds for the acting identifier, so a representative whose party holds no share in this round at all — one never noticed, or one that joined the roster after the round was opened and has not yet been let in by a reopening — is refused with the same `ERR_ACTOR_IDENTITY_MISMATCH` as a representative reaching for another party's share, and never with a 404. The optional `ballot_rationale` is validated as a non-empty string when supplied, is stored on the share exactly as given, and is stored empty when omitted. Only a party whose `status` is `PARTICIPATING` or `RECALCITRANT` may vote, and a ballot from a party in any other posture is refused with `ERR_PARTY_NOT_PARTICIPATING`. A party that has already voted on this round cannot vote again while the round remains open, and a second attempt is refused with `ERR_BALLOT_ALREADY_CAST`; reopening the round clears the record and restores the right to vote. Accepting the ballot stamps `ballot`, `ballot_rationale` and `ballot_cast_at` on the share, then recomputes the round's `balloted_count` and its `accept_share_pct`, the latter being the sum of `provisional_pct` over the round's shares whose `ballot` is `ACCEPT`. A cast ballot touches both the share and its round, and the resource it returns in `data` is the **allocation share** — never the round; the round's refreshed `balloted_count`, `accept_share_pct`, `status` and share totals are read back from `GET /allocation-rounds/{id}`.

The round resolves itself on the ballot that makes `balloted_count` equal `share_count`. If `accept_share_pct` is at least 66.67 — two thirds of the allocated liability, with exact equality adopting — the round moves `BALLOTING → ADOPTED`. Otherwise it moves `BALLOTING → FAILED`, no `final_pct` is stamped anywhere, and every share keeps its `disposition` at `PENDING`.

Adoption is a single atomic settlement of the whole round. The final variant of the share arithmetic runs, and the party's posture at that instant decides only which of two treatments its share receives. A share belonging to a party in `ORPHAN` captures its computed percentage into `pre_settlement_final_pct`, has `final_pct` set to 0.00, and takes `disposition` `ORPHANED`; a share belonging to a party in `DE_MINIMIS_SETTLED` receives exactly the same treatment with `disposition` `SETTLED`. **Every other share receives its `final_pct` with `disposition` `ALLOCATED`** — the ordinary cases `PARTICIPATING` and `RECALCITRANT`, and no differently a party still merely `NOTIFIED` that never elected at all, or a party already `SETTLED` by a collected cost-recovery demand. Only the two postures named above are carved out; there is no third treatment and no share is left `PENDING`. The round then stamps `allocated_share_total_pct` as the sum of `final_pct` over its allocated shares, `orphan_share_pct` as the sum of `pre_settlement_final_pct` over its orphaned shares, and `settled_share_pct` as the sum of `pre_settlement_final_pct` over its settled shares, such that the three total exactly 100.00. Before adoption, `allocated_share_total_pct` reads 0.00. Adoption also stamps `adopted_at` with the logical clock, mirrors the round's `id` into the site's `adopted_round_id` — empty until there is one — and recomputes the site's `orphan_write_off_cents` and `unrecovered_cost_cents`. Adoption is expected to happen once per site, but nothing structurally refuses a further round after it: an `ADOPTED` round is never superseded by a later opening, so if a second round also reaches adoption both stay `ADOPTED` and `adopted_round_id` resolves to the one with the highest `round_number`, which is the round every downstream gate and every ledger figure then reads.

Renegotiation runs backwards. The facilitator reopens a round that is `BALLOTING` or `FAILED`, supplying a `revision_note` — a required non-empty string that overwrites whatever an earlier reopening recorded — and the round returns to `DRAFTING`, closing the cycle `BALLOTING → DRAFTING → BALLOTING` as many times as the negotiation needs. Reopening clears `ballot`, `ballot_rationale` and `ballot_cast_at` on every share, resets `balloted_count` to 0 and `accept_share_pct` to 0.00, re-derives `provisional_pct` against current data, and mints shares for any party that has entered the candidate set since the round was opened — those late shares arriving with exactly the same empty ballot fields, empty percentages and `PENDING` disposition as a share minted at opening. Both reopening paths — from balloting and from failure — perform exactly the same clearing and recomputation. Reopening carries no posture gate and re-applies neither the weight-normalisation test nor the `ERR_NO_ALLOCATION_BASIS` test, so a round whose basis has since vanished reopens with every `provisional_pct` at 0.00; the response returns the **round** at HTTP 200.

### 3.7. Settlement Instruments, Oversight Billing and Stipulated Penalties

A settlement instrument is drafted by settlement counsel against the site. Its `scope_type` distinguishes the two families the programme uses: `RIFS_AOC` for an administrative order on consent covering investigation work, and `RD_RA_CONSENT_DECREE` for a judicial decree covering remedial design and construction. Counsel supplies the `scope_summary` describing the work the settling parties commit to, the list of signatories as `party_prp_ids`, the `oversight_cost_cap_cents` ceiling on reimbursable oversight cost, the `stipulated_penalty_per_day_cents` daily penalty rate, and the `performance_deadline` date for the committed work. Both cent amounts must be strictly positive.

The signatory list carries structural rules of its own. `party_prp_ids` must hold at least 1 and at most 20 elements, must contain no duplicates, and **every element must be the identifier of a party named for this very site**; an empty list, a list with repeats, or an element that resolves to no party at all is refused as described in §2.6. The list is a genuine multi-valued attribute rather than a single foreign key: one party may sign several instruments and one instrument binds several parties, so the relationship must be stored as a list on the instrument and cannot be collapsed into a single reference on either side.

An instrument begins with `legal_status` `NEGOTIATING` and, independently, `compliance_status` `COMPLIANT`. **These two dimensions move independently of one another**, and understanding that is essential: a decree that is deep in a penalty accrual is still `ENTERED` on the legal axis throughout, and the compliance cycle never rewinds the legal posture. At creation the system also derives `penalty_cap_cents` as 10 percent of the `oversight_cost_cap_cents` — the cap in basis points is 1,000 — rounded half up to whole cents. Every remaining field of a fresh instrument sits at its zero or its empty: `days_in_violation`, `accrued_penalty_cents`, `paid_penalty_cents` and `open_violation_count` at 0, `penalty_capped` false, and `violation_type`, `first_noncompliance_date`, `lodged_at`, `public_comment_close_at`, `court_docket_number`, `entered_at`, `last_assessment_at` and `terminated_at` all empty. Drafting is gated by nothing beyond the site's terminal phase — it requires no particular listing stage, no approved investigation and no adopted allocation, since those bite at lodging — and it returns the **instrument** at HTTP 201.

Lodging the instrument with the court moves it `NEGOTIATING → LODGED`. Two business preconditions apply. The site must already have an adopted allocation round, because a settlement whose payment shares have not been agreed has nothing to settle; a lodging attempted before adoption is refused with `ERR_NO_ADOPTED_ALLOCATION`. And every party named in `party_prp_ids` must currently have `status` `PARTICIPATING` — a signatory that has gone recalcitrant, orphan or already settled is refused with `ERR_PARTY_NOT_PARTICIPATING`. Lodging stamps `lodged_at` with the logical clock and derives `public_comment_close_at` as 30 days after that instant. The request declares no fields of its own, so an absent or empty body is well formed, and it returns the **instrument** at HTTP 200.

Entry moves the instrument `LODGED → ENTERED` and requires that the 30-day lodging comment window has fully elapsed: the logical clock must be at or after `public_comment_close_at`, with the closing instant itself qualifying. An earlier attempt is refused with `ERR_DECREE_COMMENT_PERIOD_OPEN`. Counsel supplies the `court_docket_number` — the one field this request declares, required and non-empty — which is stamped along with `entered_at`, and the instrument's `compliance_status` is set to `COMPLIANT` with `first_noncompliance_date` cleared and `penalty_cap_cents` re-derived from the oversight cap; entry leaves every penalty figure otherwise untouched and returns the **instrument** at HTTP 200. Entry matters far beyond the legal record, because **the entered instrument becomes the site's governing oversight cap**: the site's `oversight_cap_utilization_pct` and `cost_posture` are recomputed against `oversight_cost_cap_cents` at this point. A site is expected to have at most one entered instrument at a time, but entry declares no precondition to that effect and nothing refuses a second one: should two instruments of the site both read `ENTERED`, the governing cap is the **earlier-created** of them, and the later entry changes the site's utilisation not at all.

Termination is not requested directly. When construction is certified complete, an instrument that is `ENTERED`, currently `COMPLIANT`, and whose `accrued_penalty_cents` exactly equals its `paid_penalty_cents` moves `ENTERED → TERMINATED` and stamps `terminated_at`. An instrument with an outstanding penalty balance stays entered.

**Oversight billing.** A project manager posts each incurred oversight cost against the site with the `incurred_cents` amount — a required non-negative integer count of cents, so a posting of exactly 0 is accepted and only a negative amount is malformed — the `cost_category` it falls under, and the `incurred_on` date it was incurred, a plain ISO calendar date. All three are required. Posting is refused while the site's posture is frozen but remains available while it is merely restricted, and it is refused with `ERR_SITE_PHASE_INVALID` once the site has been deleted from the docket. The posting itself is **not** an addressable resource — no read returns it and it carries no `version` — so the resource the call returns in `data` is the **site**, at HTTP 200, with its whole derived block already reconciled: the accepted posting is observable only through the site's re-derived oversight figures. On each posting the site's `oversight_direct_cents` is re-derived as the sum of `incurred_cents` over every accepted posting for the site — recomputed from the postings, never incremented in place.

The indirect-cost rate is then selected from a three-tier schedule keyed on the direct subtotal. When `oversight_direct_cents` is at most 5,000,000 cents — fifty thousand dollars — the rate is 1,550 basis points, or 15.50 percent. When it is above that but at most 25,000,000 cents — a quarter of a million dollars — the rate is 1,275 basis points. Above that it is 1,050 basis points. Both tier ceilings are inclusive, so a subtotal of exactly 5,000,000 cents is still in the first tier. The selected rate is stored as `oversight_indirect_rate_bp`. The schedule is only entered once there is something to rate: a site that has taken no posting at all has **no** tier and reports `oversight_indirect_rate_bp` as 0 rather than the first-tier 1,550, which is what §3.1's promise that a newly proposed site begins with every derived cost figure at zero actually means for this field.

Two aspects of this schedule are load-bearing. First, **the tier is keyed on the pre-fee direct subtotal, never on the billed total** — keying it on the billed figure is wrong and observably so, because a subtotal just under a ceiling can produce a billed total just over it and would then be re-rated at the wrong tier. Second, **the indirect component is recomputed in full on every posting**: when a posting carries the subtotal across a tier ceiling, the entire accumulated subtotal is re-rated at the new rate. There is no marginal or blended tier arithmetic, so crossing a ceiling can make the billed total rise by less than the amount just posted.

The `oversight_indirect_cents` figure is therefore the whole `oversight_direct_cents` subtotal multiplied by the selected rate in basis points, divided by 10,000, rounded half up to whole cents; and `oversight_billed_cents` is the direct subtotal plus that indirect figure. When the site has a governing instrument — one whose `legal_status` is `ENTERED` — a posting that would drive `oversight_billed_cents` past that instrument's `oversight_cost_cap_cents` is refused with `ERR_OVERSIGHT_CAP_EXCEEDED`, and **every field is left exactly as it was**: the posting does not partially apply. Where a governing instrument exists, `oversight_cap_utilization_pct` is the billed total multiplied by 100, divided by the cap, rounded half up to two decimal places; where the site has no governing instrument it is reported as empty, and no cap check applies. "Governing" is a live condition re-evaluated on every recomputation rather than a one-time stamp: a site is governed by whichever of its instruments currently reads `legal_status` `ENTERED`, so before entry and again after that instrument terminates the site has no governing cap, `oversight_cap_utilization_pct` reverts to empty, the cap check stops applying, and the utilisation trigger of §2.9 can no longer restrict the site. Because billed oversight cost enters the response-cost basis, every accepted posting also recomputes `total_response_cost_cents`, the rest of the cost ledger, and the site's `cost_posture`.

**Compliance events and stipulated penalties.** Counsel records a compliance event against an instrument by supplying the `event_type` — `VIOLATION`, `PENALTY_ASSESSMENT` or `CURE` — the `effective_at` instant the event takes effect, which is an ISO 8601 UTC instant exactly like every other client-supplied `_at` field in the system rather than a bare calendar date (a value supplied without a time component is read as midnight UTC), and, depending on the type, a `violation_type` describing the nature of the noncompliance or a `paid_penalty_cents` amount. Those last two are **optional on every event type**, not conditionally required by the type: `violation_type` is validated as a non-empty string whenever it is supplied and `paid_penalty_cents` as a non-negative integer count of cents, whatever the `event_type`, and a value handed to an event type that has no use for it is validated and then discarded. No compliance event of any type may be recorded against an instrument whose `legal_status` is not `ENTERED`; an earlier attempt is refused with `ERR_DECREE_NOT_ENTERED`, and because this endpoint is one of the two that test the target's own transition last (§2.6), that identifier is what an event of an inadmissible type reports too when the instrument has never been entered. Every accepted event returns the **instrument** at HTTP 200.

A `VIOLATION` event moves the compliance dimension `COMPLIANT → IN_VIOLATION`. It stamps `first_noncompliance_date` from the event's `effective_at` — the date part of that instant, since `first_noncompliance_date` is a plain calendar date — records the supplied `violation_type` — left empty when the optional field was omitted, and overwriting whatever an earlier violation recorded — and increases `open_violation_count` by 1 — that counter reads 0 whenever the instrument is compliant. Because an open violation is one of the four restriction triggers, the site's `cost_posture` is recomputed and becomes `RESTRICTED`, which halts new contract awards and new work windows but leaves oversight billing and recovery actions untouched.

A `PENALTY_ASSESSMENT` event moves `IN_VIOLATION → PENALTY_ACCRUING` and requires that the date part of its `effective_at` be at or after the instrument's `first_noncompliance_date` — a comparison of calendar days, so any instant on the first day of noncompliance qualifies. Further assessments on the same open violation are permitted as a self-loop, `PENALTY_ACCRUING → PENALTY_ACCRUING`, and each requires an `effective_at` strictly later than the `last_assessment_at` already recorded — that one is a comparison of full instants, so a second assessment later on the same calendar day is permitted while an equal instant is not. Both of those temporal preconditions belong to the transition itself rather than to the temporal gates of §2.6, so a breach of either is reported as `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` — the same identifier an assessment against a still-`COMPLIANT` instrument reports, and the same one a cure against a still-`COMPLIANT` instrument reports. Every assessment stamps `last_assessment_at` with its own `effective_at`, whole instant included, so `last_assessment_at` is reported as a timestamp with three fractional digits while `first_noncompliance_date` is reported as a plain date. Because a penalty-accruing instrument is the sole freeze trigger, the site's `cost_posture` becomes `FROZEN` and stays frozen until the violation is cured — which is why an accruing penalty stops oversight postings, round openings, recovery actions and work windows all at once.

The accrual itself works as follows. The `days_in_violation` count is the number of days from `first_noncompliance_date` through the date part of the event's `effective_at` **inclusive of both endpoints**, so 1 March 2027 through 30 March 2027 is 30 days. The accrual window is therefore counted in whole calendar days and the time of day carried by `effective_at` never changes it. The first 30 of those days accrue at the flat `stipulated_penalty_per_day_cents` rate; specifically, the flat day count is whichever is smaller of `days_in_violation` and 30. Any days beyond the thirtieth accrue at an escalated rate — the escalated day count is `days_in_violation` less 30, or zero if that would be negative — and the escalated daily rate is `stipulated_penalty_per_day_cents` multiplied by 15,000 basis points, divided by 10,000, rounded half up to whole cents, which is one and a half times the flat rate. The gross accrual is the flat day count multiplied by the flat rate, plus the escalated day count multiplied by the escalated rate.

That gross figure is then clamped. The `penalty_cap_cents` ceiling is the instrument's `oversight_cost_cap_cents` multiplied by 1,000 basis points, divided by 10,000, rounded half up to whole cents — ten percent of the oversight cap. The `accrued_penalty_cents` is whichever is smaller of the gross accrual and that cap, and `penalty_capped` is set true precisely when the gross accrual exceeded the cap and was clamped down to it, and false otherwise.

**Repeat assessments recompute the whole accrual from `first_noncompliance_date`; they never add to the previous figure.** This is what keeps the thirty-day rate step from being applied twice: a second assessment covering a longer window replaces the earlier accrual outright, so repeated assessment is idempotent in aggregate rather than additive.

A `CURE` event moves the instrument from either `IN_VIOLATION` or `PENALTY_ACCRUING` back to `COMPLIANT`, and it must settle the books exactly. The supplied `paid_penalty_cents` must equal the outstanding balance — the instrument's `accrued_penalty_cents` less its already-recorded `paid_penalty_cents` — to the cent, and because the field is optional an omitted `paid_penalty_cents` is read here as 0, so a cure of a violation that never reached assessment carries nothing outstanding and may omit the field entirely. Any other amount, over or under, is refused with `ERR_PENALTY_BALANCE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). On acceptance, `paid_penalty_cents` is brought up to `accrued_penalty_cents`, `compliance_status` returns to `COMPLIANT`, `first_noncompliance_date` and `last_assessment_at` are cleared, `days_in_violation` is reset to 0, `open_violation_count` is decreased by 1 and never below 0, and the site's `cost_posture` is recomputed — typically releasing the freeze. What a cure does **not** touch is the accrual record itself: `accrued_penalty_cents`, `penalty_capped` and `violation_type` are left standing exactly as the last assessment left them, so the cured instrument still reports what it accrued and what it was cited for. The cycle `COMPLIANT → IN_VIOLATION → PENALTY_ACCRUING → COMPLIANT` may run any number of times. As §2.10 states, penalty money paid on a cure is punitive: it does **not** increase the site's `recovered_cost_cents` and does not reduce `unrecovered_cost_cents`.

### 3.8. Procurement, Field Work, Construction Completion and Long-Term Stewardship

Construction procurement begins once a remedy has been chosen. A project manager opens a solicitation against the site citing the `record_of_decision_id` whose selected remedy the work will build, together with the `solicitation_reference` procurement number and the `engineer_estimate_cents` independent government estimate, which must be strictly positive and is the yardstick every bid is measured against. Both `record_of_decision_id` and `solicitation_reference` are required non-empty strings, and the cited document must belong to **this very site** — one that exists under another site is reported as `ERR_RECORD_OF_DECISION_NOT_FOUND`, exactly as an identifier that resolves to nothing at all is. The cited decision document must be `SIGNED`; a solicitation opened against a draft, commenting, amendment-required or withdrawn document is refused with `ERR_ROD_NOT_SIGNED`. Beyond the site's terminal phase the solicitation carries no phase gate and no posture gate of its own — the signed decision it cites is the whole of its precondition. A new contract begins with `status` `SOLICITATION`, a `work_window_count` of 0, a `punch_list_items` of 0, an empty `contractor_name`, `awarded_amount_cents`, `awarded_at` and `completion_date`, and — this being the one derived-looking field that is not empty — a `max_concurrent_windows` already reading the default 2 from the moment of creation, so the field is never null and is only ever overwritten by an award that states a value. The response returns the **contract** at HTTP 201.

Award moves the contract `SOLICITATION → AWARDED`. The manager supplies the `contractor_name` — a required non-empty string — the `awarded_amount_cents` bid value, a required **non-negative** integer count of cents so that a bid of exactly 0 is well formed and only a negative one is malformed, and `max_concurrent_windows`, an optional integer from 1 through 5 declaring how many buffered field-work windows the winning crew can run at once; where a value is not stated the field is left alone rather than defaulted afresh, and since the contract was created carrying 2 the effective crew capacity is 2. The cited decision must still be `SIGNED`, and the site's posture must be neither frozen nor restricted — an award against a restricted site is refused with `ERR_SITE_COST_RESTRICTED`. Bid reasonableness is tested against a tolerance of 1,500 basis points over the engineer's estimate: the `awarded_amount_cents` may not exceed the `engineer_estimate_cents` multiplied by one plus 1,500 divided by 10,000 — that is, 115 percent of the estimate — and a bid exactly at the tolerance passes. A bid above it is refused with `ERR_BID_EXCEEDS_ESTIMATE` (HTTP 422). On acceptance the contract stamps `awarded_at` with the logical clock, the awarded amount enters the site's `total_response_cost_cents` and the whole cost ledger is recomputed, and the site's phase advances `ROD_SIGNED → CONSTRUCTION`. That advance is strictly conditional on the site actually standing in `ROD_SIGNED`: the award itself carries no phase gate beyond the terminal one, so a second contract awarded once the site has already moved on to `CONSTRUCTION` or `OM_PHASE` is accepted and leaves `listing_status` exactly as it was. The response returns the **contract** at HTTP 200.

Field work is scheduled by the contractor itself, and only on its own contract: the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the contract's `id`, and a contractor reaching for another contract's schedule is refused with `ERR_ACTOR_IDENTITY_MISMATCH`. Each window names the `remediation_contract_id` it belongs to, the `activity_type` it is reserved for — `CONSTRUCTION`, `LONG_TERM_MONITORING` or `MAINTENANCE_SHUTDOWN` — an inclusive `start_at` instant, an inclusive `end_at` instant that must fall strictly after the start, the `crew_size` of field personnel mobilised as an integer from 1 through 200, and optionally a `description` scope note, validated as a non-empty string when supplied and stored empty when omitted. The `end_at`-after-`start_at` rule is a shape rule rather than a business rule: a breach is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and, sitting at priority 3, it is decided before the contract identifier in the path is even resolved, so a reversed interval against a contract that does not exist reports the 422 and not the 404. Windows are immutable once scheduled, and scheduling one is refused while the site's posture is frozen or restricted. Scheduling declares **no precondition on the contract's own `status`**: a window may be booked against a contract still in solicitation, one already certified construction-complete, or one closed out by a protective five-year review, and it is only the identity check, the two posture gates, the site's terminal phase and the capacity test that can refuse it.

Crews do not appear and disappear instantaneously, so every window occupies more calendar than it nominally spans. The system derives `buffered_start_at` as the window's `start_at` moved 4 hours earlier and `buffered_end_at` as its `end_at` moved 4 hours later, and it is these mobilisation-buffered intervals — not the raw ones — that are tested for contention. The count of concurrent windows is the number of existing windows **on the same contract** whose buffered interval intersects the new window's buffered interval, treated as half-open: a window whose buffered end instant coincides exactly with another's buffered start instant does not intersect it. Windows on other contracts never interact. If that count plus the new window itself would exceed the contract's `max_concurrent_windows`, the request is refused with `ERR_WORK_WINDOW_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. The buffer is what makes this non-obvious: two windows six hours apart do not overlap on their raw intervals, but they do overlap once each is padded by four hours on both sides. Each accepted window increases the contract's `work_window_count` — re-derived as the number of windows now on file rather than incremented in place — and that counter is a stored field, so an accepted window bumps the **contract's** `version` even though the window itself, being immutable, carries no `version` of its own. The response returns the **work window** at HTTP 201, carrying its buffered interval and neither `updated_at` nor `version`.

Certifying construction complete moves the contract `AWARDED → CONSTRUCTION_COMPLETE` and requires that the contract hold at least one field-work window — a contract that certifies completion having never scheduled any work is refused as an invalid transition. The manager supplies the outstanding `punch_list_items` count — the one field this request declares, a required non-negative integer — which is recorded alongside a `completion_date` taken from the date part of the logical clock. Three things then happen beyond the contract. The site advances `CONSTRUCTION → OM_PHASE`, and its `construction_complete_at` is stamped as that same date at exactly midnight UTC. The site's `five_year_review_due_at` is set to `construction_complete_at` plus 1,826 days — five calendar years including one leap day — also at exactly midnight UTC. Only the phase advance is conditional: certification carries no phase gate beyond the terminal one, so a contract certified while its site stands in some other phase still stamps both of those instants and still terminates the eligible instruments, and only `listing_status` is left where it was. And any settlement instrument of the site that is entered, compliant and free of any outstanding penalty balance terminates, as §3.7 describes. The response returns the **contract** at HTTP 200; the site's stamped instants, its advanced phase and the terminated instruments are read back from their own endpoints.

**Long-term stewardship.** Once a site is in operation and maintenance, the statutory five-year review is the mechanism that keeps the remedy honest. A project manager records one by supplying the `protectiveness_determination` — `PROTECTIVE`, `PROTECTIVE_SHORT_TERM` or `NOT_PROTECTIVE` — a boolean `deletion_recommended`, and a `review_summary` narrative. All three fields are required; the narrative is validated as a non-empty string and then not persisted, since the site carries no field for it. A review is accepted only when the site is in `OM_PHASE` and only when the logical clock has reached `five_year_review_due_at`, with the due instant itself qualifying; an early review is refused with `ERR_FIVE_YEAR_REVIEW_NOT_DUE`, as is a review of a site whose `five_year_review_due_at` is still empty because construction was never certified complete. Every accepted review records the determination as the site's `last_protectiveness_determination` and stamps `last_five_year_review_at` with the review instant taken from the logical clock, and returns the **site** at HTTP 200.

The determination decides the branch, and the three branches are deliberately asymmetric. A determination of `PROTECTIVE` or `PROTECTIVE_SHORT_TERM` with `deletion_recommended` false keeps the site in `OM_PHASE` — a self-loop — and simply advances the review clock. A determination of `PROTECTIVE` with `deletion_recommended` true retires the site to `DELETED_FROM_NPL`, but only when the site's books are square: `unrecovered_cost_cents` must be at or below zero, meaning the government has recovered or written off everything it advanced, and a site still carrying a positive unrecovered balance is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. A determination of `NOT_PROTECTIVE` sends the site to `REMEDY_REEVALUATION` regardless of what was recommended. The fourth combination has no declared branch at all: `PROTECTIVE_SHORT_TERM` with `deletion_recommended` true is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, since a remedy protective only in the short term cannot support retirement from the docket. Both of those refusals are the last checks this endpoint performs rather than the first — on this endpoint, which is one of the two exceptions §2.6 records, the transition check runs *after* the phase gate and the due-date gate, so a site that is not yet due reports `ERR_FIVE_YEAR_REVIEW_NOT_DUE` even when the determination and recommendation it carries would also have been rejected.

For both the self-loop and the reevaluation branch, the next `five_year_review_due_at` is derived by adding 1,826 days **to the previous due date, never to the date the review was actually performed**. Chaining off the due date is what keeps the statutory cycle fixed: an early or late review shifts nothing, and a site reviewed two months late is still due again on the original five-year rhythm. A site retired from the docket has no further review obligation and its due date is not advanced.

The `NOT_PROTECTIVE` branch fires the system's second three-hop cascade. **The first hop** sets the site's `listing_status` to `REMEDY_REEVALUATION` and advances the review clock as just described. **The second hop follows from the first**: because the site has entered reevaluation, every decision document of the site whose `status` is `SIGNED` moves `SIGNED → AMENDMENT_REQUIRED`, reopening remedy selection — the hop is written over the whole roster of documents rather than over one, exactly as the withdrawal cascade of §3.5 is, and a document in any other status is untouched. **The third hop follows from the second**: because a decision document is now awaiting amendment, the site's `cost_posture` is re-derived and becomes `RESTRICTED`. Note that two independent restriction triggers are now live at once — the amendment-required document and the reevaluation phase itself — which is why signing the amended decision (which clears both) is what releases the restriction, as §3.5 describes.

A `PROTECTIVE` determination has a smaller cascade of its own: every contract of the site sitting in `CONSTRUCTION_COMPLETE` moves to `CLOSED`, with no other field changed — its `completion_date` was already stamped at certification. That cascade runs on any `PROTECTIVE` determination, whether or not deletion was recommended, and never on `PROTECTIVE_SHORT_TERM` or `NOT_PROTECTIVE`. Because a closed contract still contributes its `awarded_amount_cents` to `total_response_cost_cents`, closing one moves no ledger figure. `CLOSED` is terminal for a contract.

### 3.9. Cost Recovery, Contribution Claims and De Minimis Settlements

The final stream is money going the other way: the government recovering what it advanced, parties suing each other for contribution, and the smallest contributors buying their way out early. All three are the same record type, distinguished by `action_type`.

Settlement counsel opens an action against the site naming the `respondent_prp_id` — the party being asked to pay — the `action_type`, the `demanded_cents` amount, which must be strictly positive, and the `expected_prp_version` stale-version guard described in §2.7. `claimant_prp_id` is **optional on every action type**, `CONTRIBUTION_CLAIM` included. For a contribution claim it names the party pursuing the claim and is stored; omitting it is nonetheless accepted and leaves the field null. For a `COST_RECOVERY_DEMAND` and a `DE_MINIMIS_SETTLEMENT` no claimant is named, because the government is the claimant, and the field stays null even when one *was* supplied — a supplied claimant must still resolve to some party under the body-reference check of §2.6, and is then simply discarded. Unlike the respondent, the claimant is not required to be a party of this site and is not required to differ from the respondent. Every action starts at `status` `OFFERED` with `received_cents` at 0, no `received_at`, and no `void_reason`, and the created action is what comes back at HTTP 201; the response, the collection and the voiding endpoints each return the **action** at HTTP 200.

Several gates run in the order §2.6 lays out. A frozen site refuses all cost-recovery activity with `ERR_SITE_COST_FROZEN` — note this stream is refused only when the site is frozen, not when it is merely restricted, since recovering money is the one activity a cost-restricted site still wants to do. The site must have an adopted allocation round, since without an adopted allocation there is no share to sue over: an action opened against a site whose `adopted_round_id` is null is refused with `ERR_NO_ADOPTED_ALLOCATION`. The respondent must hold an `ALLOCATED` share in that adopted round; a respondent who never received a share, or whose share has already been settled or orphaned, is refused with `ERR_RESPONDENT_NOT_ALLOCATED`. That is also where a cross-site respondent lands: a `respondent_prp_id` that resolves to a real party of some *other* site satisfies the body-reference check of §2.6 and is then refused with `ERR_RESPONDENT_NOT_ALLOCATED`, not with `ERR_PRP_NOT_FOUND` — the only place in the specification where a wrong-site party reference is reported as something other than a 404. Opening any action increments the respondent's `version` by one, which is precisely what the `expected_prp_version` guard exists to detect: two settlement counsel racing to open actions against the same respondent will see the second attempt rejected with `ERR_STALE_PRP_VERSION`.

Opening an action also **supersedes** the respondent's existing offer of the same kind. If the respondent already holds an `OFFERED` action whose `action_type` matches the new one, that older action moves `OFFERED → SUPERSEDED`, which is terminal — the party is never left holding two live offers of the same kind. An `OFFERED` action of a *different* type is untouched, so a respondent can simultaneously hold a live de minimis offer and a live cost-recovery demand.

**The de minimis premium floor.** A de minimis settlement is a buyout: the party pays a premium above its arithmetic share in exchange for permanent protection from contribution claims, and the premium compensates the government for accepting the risk that the remedy costs more than projected. Two extra gates therefore apply to a `DE_MINIMIS_SETTLEMENT`. The respondent's `de_minimis_eligible` flag must be true — an ineligible party is refused with `ERR_NOT_DE_MINIMIS_ELIGIBLE` (HTTP 409) — and the `demanded_cents` must be at or above the computed `premium_floor_cents`, with an offer exactly at the floor accepted and anything below it refused with `ERR_DE_MINIMIS_PREMIUM_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 422).

The floor is derived at the moment of the offer and then frozen. The system first captures `respondent_share_pct_at_offer`, the respondent's `final_pct` in the adopted round — the *allocated* exposure, which is deliberately **not** the `volumetric_share_pct` that fed the allocation model, because the adopted allocation may have moved the party a long way from its raw volumetric arithmetic. The floor is then the site's `total_response_cost_cents` multiplied by that captured percentage divided by 100, multiplied by a premium multiplier of 2.0, rounded half up to whole cents. Both `respondent_share_pct_at_offer` and `premium_floor_cents` are stamped onto the action and are never re-derived: later cost postings that raise `total_response_cost_cents`, and later rounds that change the party's share, do not retroactively move the floor of an offer already on the table. For a non-de-minimis action `premium_floor_cents` is null — but `respondent_share_pct_at_offer` is **not**: every action of every type captures the respondent's allocated share at issuance, reading 0.00 when the allocated share carries no `final_pct` yet, so a cost-recovery demand and a contribution claim both record the exposure they were priced against even though neither is floored on it.

**Responding.** The respondent answers through its own representative — the acting `X-User-ID` must resolve to the action's `respondent_prp_id`, and any other party's representative is refused with `ERR_ACTOR_IDENTITY_MISMATCH`. The response carries a `decision` of `ACCEPT` or `REJECT` and an optional `response_note` — validated as a non-empty string when supplied and then not persisted, since the action carries no field for it. Accepting a `DE_MINIMIS_SETTLEMENT` re-tests both of the buyout's issuance gates against current data before anything is written: the respondent's `de_minimis_eligible` flag must *still* be true, or the acceptance is refused with `ERR_NOT_DE_MINIMIS_ELIGIBLE`, and the frozen `demanded_cents` must still be at or above the frozen `premium_floor_cents`, or it is refused with `ERR_DE_MINIMIS_PREMIUM_INSUFFICIENT`. So a party whose eligibility lapsed between the offer and the answer — a later verification having lifted its volumetric share past 1.00 percent — cannot close the buyout, and must be offered a fresh instrument. Neither re-test applies to a rejection, nor to either of the other two action types.

`REJECT` moves the action `OFFERED → REJECTED`, which is terminal. For a `COST_RECOVERY_DEMAND` specifically, rejection also moves the respondent's `status` to `RECALCITRANT` — the party has refused a government demand and is flagged accordingly. Rejecting a de minimis offer or a contribution claim leaves the respondent's participation status alone.

`ACCEPT` moves the action `OFFERED → ACCEPTED`. Acceptance is a promise to pay, not a payment: `received_cents` stays at 0 and the site's `recovered_cost_cents` does not move. For a `DE_MINIMIS_SETTLEMENT`, however, accepting immediately restructures the allocation, because the party has bought out of it. The respondent moves to `DE_MINIMIS_SETTLED` and its `contribution_protection` becomes true — unconditionally, those two being written whatever the state of the allocation. Its share in the adopted round moves `ALLOCATED → SETTLED`: the share's current `final_pct` is preserved into `pre_settlement_final_pct` and its `final_pct` is set to 0.00, and rather than being adjusted by increments the round's three totals are re-derived from the dispositions its shares now carry, which moves that same amount of percentage out of `allocated_share_total_pct` and into `settled_share_pct`. That share half of the settlement is conditional in the same way the defunct cascade of §3.1 is: if the site has lost its adopted round, or the respondent's share in it has meanwhile been orphaned or settled by another instrument, the share and the round totals are left exactly as they stand and only the party's own two fields move. The round's conservation identity from §3.6 therefore still holds — settled percentage plus allocated percentage plus orphan percentage still sums to 100 — but the composition has shifted, which is exactly what the identity is designed to make visible.

**Collection.** Settlement counsel records money actually received against an `ACCEPTED` or partially collected action — a partial receipt leaves the action in `ACCEPTED`, so the two are the same precondition — supplying the `received_cents` amount for this receipt as a required **non-negative** integer count of cents, so a receipt of exactly 0 is well formed and only a negative one is malformed, and an optional `receipt_reference` that is validated as a non-empty string and then not persisted, the action carrying no field for it. Overpayment is refused, never clamped: the receipt may not exceed the amount still outstanding, that is `demanded_cents` minus the `received_cents` already booked, and a larger receipt is rejected with `ERR_OVERPAYMENT_NOT_ALLOWED` (HTTP 422) with nothing recorded. An accepted receipt adds to the action's cumulative `received_cents` and stamps `received_at` with the logical clock. If the cumulative total now equals `demanded_cents` the action moves to `COLLECTED`; if it falls short the action stays `ACCEPTED` as a partial receipt, and further receipts are accepted against it until the demand is satisfied. Every receipt increases the site's `recovered_cost_cents` and re-derives `unrecovered_cost_cents`, so the ledger identity of §2.10 holds after every partial payment, not merely at the end.

A fully collected `COST_RECOVERY_DEMAND` closes the party out: the respondent moves from `PARTICIPATING` or `RECALCITRANT` to `SETTLED` and its `contribution_protection` becomes true. Note the asymmetry with de minimis — a demand settlement grants protection at the moment the money is *fully in hand*, whereas a de minimis settlement grants it at the moment the offer is *accepted*, because the premium buyout restructures the allocation up front. A collected demand does **not** zero the respondent's `final_pct` or touch the round's percentage totals; the party remains in the allocation, it has simply paid.

**Voiding.** An action that was accepted or collected can be unwound — a settlement rescinded, a payment reversed, a receipt discovered to be in error. Settlement counsel supplies a `void_reason` — the one field this request declares, required and non-empty, recorded on the action — and the action moves `ACCEPTED → VOIDED` or `COLLECTED → VOIDED`, which is terminal. Voiding is a full compensating reversal, and its correctness is measured by the ledger: the site's `recovered_cost_cents` is decreased by exactly the `received_cents` the action had booked, the action's `received_cents` is reset to 0, and `unrecovered_cost_cents` is re-derived, so the conservation identity of §2.10 is restored with no residual drift. Its `received_at`, `demanded_cents`, `premium_floor_cents` and `respondent_share_pct_at_offer` are by contrast left standing as the offer and the last receipt wrote them: the void reverses the money, not the record of what was asked and when it last arrived. An action that had collected nothing reverses nothing from the ledger.

The rest of the reversal mirrors whichever grant the action made. Voiding a `DE_MINIMIS_SETTLEMENT` returns the respondent from `DE_MINIMIS_SETTLED` to `PARTICIPATING`, sets its `contribution_protection` back to false, and re-derives its `de_minimis_eligible` flag against the current site totals — a party whose eligibility no longer holds does not get it back. Its share moves `SETTLED → ALLOCATED` with `final_pct` restored from `pre_settlement_final_pct` and `pre_settlement_final_pct` cleared, and the restored amount is moved back out of the round's `settled_share_pct` and into its `allocated_share_total_pct`. Voiding a collected `COST_RECOVERY_DEMAND` returns the respondent from `SETTLED` to `PARTICIPATING` and sets `contribution_protection` to false, but **leaves the share entirely alone** — there is nothing to restore, because a demand settlement never zeroed the `final_pct` in the first place. Reversing a share that was never modified would be the natural mistake here, and it would corrupt the round's percentage identity. Both of those restorations are conditional on the grant still standing: the party is returned to `PARTICIPATING` only if it is still in the posture that instrument put it in — `DE_MINIMIS_SETTLED` for a buyout, `SETTLED` for a demand — and the buyout's share is restored only if that share is still `SETTLED`, so a respondent since recorded defunct, or re-settled by a later instrument, keeps the posture and the disposition it now carries. Voiding a `CONTRIBUTION_CLAIM` mirrors no grant at all, because a claim makes none: it reverses the receipts on the ledger and touches neither the respondent's `status` nor its share.

## 5. Error Codes

Every error response carries one of the identifiers below in `data.error_id`, together with the HTTP status shown. When several conditions are simultaneously unsatisfied, the identifier returned is the one that appears earliest in the precedence ladder of §2.6. Every identifier is paired with exactly one HTTP status, so the status is a function of the identifier and never varies with the endpoint that raised it.

Two identifiers sit outside this catalogue because neither is reachable from a well-formed call against a declared endpoint, and both are nonetheless returned in the standard error envelope. A request whose path matches no declared route is answered with `ERR_NOT_FOUND` and HTTP 404, and a request whose path matches a route that does not declare that method is answered with the same `ERR_NOT_FOUND` identifier and HTTP 405. An unexpected server fault is answered with `ERR_INTERNAL` and HTTP 500.
