# Household Staff Placement API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A household staffing agency sells trust before it sells labour. When a principal household engages us to find an estate manager, a housekeeper, a chef or a chauffeur, it is handing a stranger the keys to its home, its calendar and its children, and it expects the agency to have done the work of verification before a single candidate crosses the threshold. This API is the operational spine of that promise. It governs the whole arc of a placement: intake of the household and countersignature of the confidentiality agreement, scoping of the role and its compensation, candidate registration and reference-chain verification, third-party background screening and its adjudication, coordination of paid trial days and unpaid working interviews, negotiation of live-in accommodation and working schedule, extension and confirmation of the offer, and finally the money — a placement fee expressed as a percentage of first-year compensation, billed against three milestones and posted to an append-only household receivable ledger.

Two commercial realities shape almost every rule that follows. The first is that the fee is priced twice. At scoping time the agency knows only a target compensation figure, so it stamps a **provisional fee**; at confirmed start it knows the household's actual package broken into base salary, guaranteed bonus, imputed accommodation value and budgeted overtime, so it re-prices from those four components and re-allocates the milestone schedule around whatever has already been invoiced or collected. The two computations deliberately do not agree to the cent, and the system must be honest about which one produced the number on the invoice. The second reality is that a placement carries a **replacement guarantee**: if the placed employee separates inside the guarantee window, the household is entitled to either a fresh search or a pro-rated refund net of a retained administrative fee, and the agency's exposure has to be computed from the contracted fee rather than from whatever it has managed to collect so far.

Around those two spines sit the agency's protective controls. A vetting desk that records a reference may not be the desk that verifies it, and a compliance officer who files a poaching allegation may not be the officer who resolves it. Candidates carry two independent state dimensions — how far their vetting has progressed, and whether they are actually free to be presented — because a fully cleared candidate on a cooling-off period after a terminated placement is simply not available, and conflating the two axes is how agencies double-book people. Households that solicit a candidate directly, in breach of the two-year non-solicitation term, are assessed a penalty on a regressive rate schedule with a repeat-offender multiplier, and are suspended from all placement activity until they settle. Every one of these controls is expressed below as a state machine, a calculation, or a numbered validation with a fixed error identifier, because in this business an ambiguous rule is a rule that will eventually be argued about in front of a client.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contract-level rules that hold across every endpoint: authentication, response shape, pagination, resource identity, the logical clock, monetary precision, concurrency control, cascade discipline, separation of duties, validation precedence, and the household ledger.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request other than the two logical-clock endpoints must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which it is acting. If either header is absent or present but empty, the request is refused with `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADER` (HTTP 401). This check runs before anything else at all — before path parameters are parsed, before the body is inspected — so an unauthenticated request naming a non-existent identifier still returns 401 rather than 404. The two clock endpoints (`POST /_clock/set_current_utc` and `GET /_clock`) are exempt from header checking; they are test-harness capabilities and accept any caller.

Seven roles are recognised, and the spellings are exact: `AgencyPrincipal`, `PlacementConsultant`, `VettingSpecialist`, `BillingAdministrator`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `Client`, and `Candidate`. A value of `X-User-Role` outside that set of seven is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ROLE` (HTTP 401). A recognised role that is not granted the requested operation by the permission matrix in §4 is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403), and this refusal precedes request-shape validation — a forbidden role that also sends a malformed body receives 403, not 400.

The five agency-side roles — `AgencyPrincipal`, `PlacementConsultant`, `VettingSpecialist`, `BillingAdministrator` and `ComplianceOfficer` — carry an **opaque** identity. When `X-User-Role` is any one of these five, `X-User-ID` is an operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it wherever a transition nominates an actor field — `created_by_user_id` on a `RoleRequisition` and on a `Placement`, `created_by_user_id` and `verified_by_user_id` on a `ReferenceCheck`, `recorded_by_user_id` and `adjudicated_by_user_id` on a `BackgroundVerification`, `invoiced_by_user_id` on a `FeeMilestone`, and `opened_by_user_id` and `resolved_by_user_id` on a `NonSolicitationCase` — but it never compares that value to any entity's `id`. Comparisons between an opaque `X-User-ID` and a stored operator field happen only in the two separation-of-duties rules described in §2.10.

The two counterparty roles are **entity-backed** by convention only. When `X-User-Role` is `Client`, `X-User-ID` is understood to be the `id` of the acting `Client` row, and when it is `Candidate`, the `id` of the acting `Candidate` row. No endpoint, however, compares that identifier against the resource it addresses: there is no ownership check anywhere in the system, so a `Client` caller may act on any household's resource and a `Candidate` caller on any candidate's, and the only comparisons ever made between an `X-User-ID` and a stored field are the two separation-of-duties rules described in §2.10.

One operation is further partitioned by the value in the body rather than by the endpoint. On the confidentiality-agreement endpoint, an `action` of `SIGN` is exclusive to the `Client` role and an `action` of `COUNTERSIGN` is exclusive to `AgencyPrincipal`; the wrong role for the supplied `action` is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`. That partition is decided on the literal body value, so it fires only when `action` is exactly `SIGN` or exactly `COUNTERSIGN`; either permitted role sending some other `action` string proceeds to request-shape validation and receives `ERR_INVALID_ENUM`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`, followed by exactly twenty uppercase alphanumeric characters.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, read from wall-clock time rather than from the logical clock of §2.6, so it is never affected by a clock-set call.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two pagination parameters. `limit` defaults to 10 when omitted, has a minimum of 1 and a permitted maximum of 100; `offset` defaults to 0 and has a minimum of 0. A `limit` value outside the accepted band of 1 through 200, or a negative `offset`, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_RANGE` (HTTP 400); a `limit` above the permitted maximum of 100 but still inside the accepted band is served with at most 100 rows. A `limit` or `offset` that is present but not a base-ten integer — a decimal, a word, a bare sign — is likewise refused with `ERR_INVALID_RANGE`, while a present-but-empty value for either is treated exactly as an omission and takes the default. Successful list responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside `meta`, where the reported `limit` is the **effective** one actually applied — that is, the smaller of the requested value and the permitted maximum of 100, so a request for 200 rows reports `limit` 100 — `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied, and `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond the current page.

Unless a specific collection pins its own order, list results are sorted by `created_at` ascending, with two rows sharing an identical `created_at` — which the logical clock makes commonplace — ordered by the sequence in which they were created. Two collections pin an explicit business order instead: a household's ledger is returned in ascending `sequence_no` order, and a placement's milestone rows are returned in ascending `sequence_no` order.

List filters are declared per collection and are all optional. Households may be filtered by `status`, `region_code` and `confidentiality_agreement_status`. Fee bands may be filtered by `role_type` and `status`. Requisitions may be filtered by `client_id`, `resolved_fee_schedule_id`, `role_type` and `status`. Candidates may be filtered by `primary_role_type`, `vetting_status` and `availability_status`. References may be filtered by `candidate_id`, `prior_reference_id`, `status` and `relationship_type`, and the per-candidate reference collection accepts `status` and `relationship_type`. The per-candidate background-verification collection accepts `status`. Trial engagements may be filtered by `requisition_id`, `candidate_id`, `client_id`, `status`, `iso_week_key` and `engagement_type`. Accommodation terms may be filtered by `requisition_id`, `candidate_id` and `status`. Placements may be filtered by `requisition_id`, `candidate_id`, `client_id`, `snapshot_fee_schedule_id`, `placement_status` and `billing_status`. A placement's milestone collection accepts `invoice_status` and `milestone_type`; its claim collection accepts `status` and `requested_remedy`; its payroll-handoff collection accepts `status`. A household's ledger accepts `entry_type` and `related_ref_type`. Enforcement cases may be filtered by `respondent_client_id`, `candidate_id`, `source_placement_id` and `status`. Any filter value outside its declared enumeration is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ENUM` (HTTP 400).

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Versioning

Every stored row is addressed by an `id` that the server mints as a UUID. Identifiers carry no type prefix — a placement identifier and a candidate identifier are indistinguishable by shape, which is precisely why the referential rules in §2.11 check that an identifier resolves to a row *of the expected entity* rather than merely to some row.

Most entities carry the full complement of housekeeping fields: `created_at`, `updated_at` and `version`. Both timestamps are ISO 8601 UTC strings with exactly three fractional digits. `version` is an integer that begins at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly 1 on the first mutation of that row within a request, including a mutation reached indirectly through a cascade. The increment is once per row per request, not once per hop: a row that several hops of the same request touch still advances by exactly 1, and a row created by the request itself stays at `version` 1 however many later hops of that request go on to touch it. A recomputation that leaves every stored figure of a row unchanged is not a mutation at all and moves neither `version` nor `updated_at`. Whenever a row's `version` is incremented, its `updated_at` is refreshed to the current logical clock instant. The entities that carry all four housekeeping fields are `Client`, `FeeSchedule`, `RoleRequisition`, `Candidate`, `ReferenceCheck`, `BackgroundVerification`, `TrialEngagement`, `AccommodationTerm`, `Placement`, `FeeMilestone`, `ReplacementClaim`, `PayrollHandoff` and `NonSolicitationCase`.

`LedgerEntry` is the deliberate exception. Ledger rows are immutable once appended, so they carry only `id` and `created_at`: a ledger row has **no** `updated_at` field and **no** `version` field, and neither appears in its response payload. A correction to the ledger is always a new row with the opposite sign, never an edit of an existing one.

### 2.5. Nested Read Paths and Path-Parameter Resolution

Most resources in this system are addressed at the top level and carry their relationships as identifier fields in the body — a trial engagement names its `requisition_id`, a milestone names its `placement_id`, a ledger row names its `client_id`. Only a handful of collections are addressed as children of their parent: a household's ledger entries, a candidate's reference checks and background verifications, and a placement's fee milestones, replacement claims and payroll handoffs. Write operations that must be anchored to a parent are likewise nested — a reference check, a background verification, a trial engagement, an accommodation term, a replacement claim, a payroll handoff and a manual ledger adjustment are all created beneath their parent.

Path parameters are resolved strictly left to right. The parent segment is resolved first, and if it does not name a stored row of the expected entity the request fails immediately with that entity's not-found error — the child segment is never examined. The not-found error is selected by the entity the failing segment addresses: `ERR_CLIENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FEE_SCHEDULE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REQUISITION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CANDIDATE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REFERENCE_CHECK_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BACKGROUND_VERIFICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRIAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ACCOMMODATION_TERM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PLACEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FEE_MILESTONE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REPLACEMENT_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PAYROLL_HANDOFF_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_NON_SOLICITATION_CASE_NOT_FOUND`, each with HTTP 404. The same catalogue is used when a foreign-key identifier supplied in a request *body* fails to resolve — a bad `candidate_id` in a trial booking is `ERR_CANDIDATE_NOT_FOUND`, a bad `source_placement_id` on an enforcement case is `ERR_PLACEMENT_NOT_FOUND`.

Resolving to a row of the right entity is not sufficient; the rows must also belong together. When a chained reference's `prior_reference_id` belongs to a different candidate, when the candidate named in an offer has no trial engagement on the named requisition, or when an enforcement case's `source_placement_id` names a placement whose `candidate_id` differs from the case's `candidate_id`, the request is refused with `ERR_ENTITY_RELATIONSHIP_MISMATCH` (HTTP 409).

### 2.6. The Logical Clock and the Ops Sweep

All temporal reasoning in this system reads a **logical clock** rather than wall-clock time. Before any caller sets it, the clock stands at `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. Advancing it is done through `POST /_clock/set_current_utc`, which takes a single `current_utc` timestamp; an absent or null `current_utc` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400), and a present value that is not an RFC 3339 UTC timestamp with a trailing `Z` fails with `ERR_INVALID_DATE_FORMAT` (HTTP 400). The clock is monotonic: a requested instant *earlier* than the currently stored clock is refused with `ERR_CLOCK_NOT_MONOTONIC` (HTTP 422), which guarantees that a sweep-driven transition can never be un-fired; an instant exactly equal to the stored clock is accepted and still runs the sweep. `GET /_clock` reads the stored instant back and returns it as `current_utc`. What is stored and echoed is the caller's literal string, preserved character for character rather than re-formatted: a clock set to `2026-09-01T00:00:00Z` reads back in exactly that form, and one set with fractional digits reads back with them.

Advancing the clock runs an **ops sweep** with two jobs. First, every `FeeMilestone` whose `invoice_status` is `INVOICED` and whose `due_date` falls strictly before the new clock date moves to `OVERDUE`; a clock landing exactly on `due_date` leaves the milestone `INVOICED`. Second, every `Placement` whose `placement_status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `guarantee_expires_on` is on or before the new clock date moves to `GUARANTEE_EXPIRED`. Each affected row has its `version` incremented. The clock-set response reports what the sweep did: `current_utc` echoes the new instant, `milestones_marked_overdue` counts the milestones aged in this call, and `placements_guarantee_expired` counts the placements whose guarantee lapsed in this call.

### 2.7. Money, Basis Points, Points and Rounding

Every monetary amount in this system is an integer number of cents, and every field holding one has a name ending in `_cents`. There are no fractional cents anywhere in the model, and no floating-point amounts appear in any request or response.

Percentage rates are expressed in **basis points** — hundredths of a percent — in fields whose names end in `_bps`. Converting a basis-point rate to an amount means multiplying by the rate and dividing by 10000. Wherever such a conversion cannot land on a whole cent, the result is rounded **half up**: a fractional part of exactly one half rounds away from zero to the larger magnitude. Every rounding step in this document is half-up, and each calculation in §3 states exactly how many rounding steps it performs and at which point, because the same inputs rounded at different points give different answers.

Scores and month counts are exact integers and are never rounded: vetting points are whole points, and reference coverage is measured in whole calendar months. Day counts are whole days. `outstanding_balance_cents` on a household is an exact integer sum and may legitimately be negative when the household is in credit.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency Control on Supersede Paths

Four write operations can legitimately overwrite or supersede work that another writer created, so each of them requires the client to prove that it has read the current state of the governing parent before it writes. In each case the version field is a **non-optional** member of the request body, and a mismatch against the parent's stored `version` is refused before any state or business rule is evaluated.

Creating a placement through `POST /placements` requires `expected_requisition_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `RoleRequisition` named by `requisition_id`. If the requisition's stored `version` differs, the create is refused with `ERR_STALE_REQUISITION_VERSION` (HTTP 412) — the writer's picture of which candidate holds the outstanding offer and which trials are still live is out of date, and a blind write would void a placement it never saw.

Proposing a new accommodation term through `POST /role-requisitions/{id}/accommodation-terms` likewise requires `expected_requisition_version` against the same parent, and a mismatch is refused with `ERR_STALE_REQUISITION_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A fresh proposal drives any open term on that requisition to `SUPERSEDED`, so a stale writer would silently discard a negotiation round it never observed.

Adjudicating a flagged background result through `POST /background-verifications/{id}/adjudicate` requires `expected_candidate_version`, carrying the last observed `version` of the subject `Candidate`. A mismatch is refused with `ERR_STALE_CANDIDATE_VERSION` (HTTP 412), because a failing disposition fans a multi-hop cascade across the candidate's trials and their requisitions and the adjudicator must have seen the vetting state, scores and trial set that the cascade is about to act on.

Filing a replacement claim through `POST /placements/{id}/replacement-claims` requires `expected_placement_version`, carrying the last observed `version` of the subject `Placement`. A mismatch is refused with `ERR_STALE_PLACEMENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412); re-filing after a denial legitimately supersedes the earlier claim, so a stale filer might not have seen the previous decision, the re-allocated milestone schedule or the current guarantee snapshot.

No other endpoint accepts a version field. An endpoint not named in this section neither requires nor honours an `expected_requisition_version`, `expected_candidate_version` or `expected_placement_version` member.

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline and Parallel State Dimensions

Several operations in this system mutate more than one row. The rule is uniform: every row touched by a cascade has its `version` incremented and its `updated_at` refreshed, and every hop of the cascade completes inside the same request. Consistency is immediate — a caller that reads any affected row after a successful response sees the fully settled state, never an intermediate one.

Two entities carry **two independent state dimensions** each, and neither dimension is derivable from the other. A `Candidate` carries `vetting_status` — moving through `UNVETTED`, `SCREENING`, `REFERENCES_VERIFIED`, `BACKGROUND_PENDING`, `CLEARED` and `REJECTED` — and, separately, `availability_status`, moving through `AVAILABLE`, `ON_TRIAL`, `OFFER_HELD`, `PLACED`, `COOLING_OFF` and `INACTIVE`. The two axes evolve on their own except for exactly one coupling, which runs in exactly one direction: when a candidate's `vetting_status` becomes `REJECTED`, its `availability_status` is driven from `ON_TRIAL` to `INACTIVE`. Nothing on the availability axis ever moves the vetting axis. A `Placement` similarly carries `placement_status` — `PENDING_ACCEPTANCE`, `ACTIVE`, `VOIDED`, `TERMINATED_NO_REMEDY`, `TERMINATED_REPLACED`, `TERMINATED_REFUNDED`, `GUARANTEE_EXPIRED` — alongside `billing_status`, which moves through `UNBILLED`, `PARTIALLY_BILLED`, `FULLY_BILLED`, `REVERSED` and `REFUNDED` under the control of its milestone rows.

Terminal states accept no further transitions. On the requisition machine the terminal states are `PLACED` and `WITHDRAWN`; on a fee band, `RETIRED`; on a reference check, `VERIFIED`, `UNREACHABLE` and `DISPUTED`; on a background verification, `ADJUDICATED_PASS` and `ADJUDICATED_FAIL`; on a trial engagement, `PASSED`, `FAILED` and `CANCELLED`; on an accommodation term, `ACCEPTED`, `REJECTED` and `SUPERSEDED`; on the placement dimension, `VOIDED`, `TERMINATED_REPLACED`, `TERMINATED_REFUNDED` and `GUARANTEE_EXPIRED`; on the billing dimension, `REFUNDED`; on a milestone, `PAID`, `REVERSED` and `VOIDED`; on a claim, `APPROVED_REPLACEMENT`, `APPROVED_REFUND`, `DENIED` and `SUPERSEDED`; on a payroll handoff, `ATTESTED`; and on an enforcement case, `UNSUBSTANTIATED` and `SETTLED`. Neither the household lifecycle (`PROSPECT`, `ONBOARDED`, `SUSPENDED`) nor either candidate dimension has any terminal state at all — households and candidates cycle indefinitely. An action aimed at a row already sitting in a terminal state of the machine it would drive is refused with `ERR_ENTITY_TERMINAL` (HTTP 409).

### 2.10. Separation of Duties on the Vetting and Compliance Desks

Two decisions in this system may not be taken by the person who set them up, even though both people hold the same role. When a `VettingSpecialist` records the outcome of contacting a reference, the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from that reference's stored `created_by_user_id`; when a `ComplianceOfficer` resolves an enforcement case, the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from that case's stored `opened_by_user_id`. A violation of either is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). The maker may not be the checker, and the rule is about the human identifier, not about the role — a second `VettingSpecialist` is exactly what the rule demands.

### 2.11. Validation Precedence

When a single request violates several rules at once, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fully determined by the ordering below. The ordering is a single global ladder, evaluated top to bottom; the first rule whose condition holds produces the response.

1. **Authentication and authorization, in three steps.** Missing or empty `X-User-ID` or `X-User-Role` yields `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADER`. Then an unrecognised role value yields `ERR_INVALID_ROLE`. Then a recognised role without permission for the operation yields `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
2. **Request shape, in six steps.** A field declared required by the endpoint that is absent or null yields `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; this same error also covers a string outside its declared length bounds and an email-shaped field that does not contain an `@` character. Then an enum-typed value outside its declared set yields `ERR_INVALID_ENUM`. Then a numeric field outside its accepted band yields `ERR_INVALID_RANGE`; the accepted bands are: no `_cents` field may be negative, `years_experience` must be 0 through 60, `weekly_hours` must be 1 through 80, `days_off_per_week` must be 0 through 7, `fee_percent_bps` must be 1 through 10000, `guarantee_window_days` must be 1 through 365, `scope_items` must be non-empty, `limit` must be 1 through 200 and `offset` must not be negative. These are the enforced request-shape boundaries; where an entity's descriptive band is narrower, the boundary enforced at the API surface is the one stated here. A value of the wrong JSON type is folded into this same ladder rather than reported separately: a field declared numeric that carries anything other than a whole number — a string, a boolean, a fractional number, an object — is treated as out of band and yields `ERR_INVALID_RANGE`, whereas a non-string in a string field, a non-boolean in a boolean field and a non-array in an array field yield `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Then a date that is not `YYYY-MM-DD`, a timestamp that is not RFC 3339 UTC with a trailing `Z`, or a month key that is not `YYYY-MM` yields `ERR_INVALID_DATE_FORMAT`. Then a mis-ordered interval yields `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`: an `end_at` at or before `start_at` when booking a trial, an `employment_end_month` earlier than `employment_start_month` when recording a reference, or a non-null `comp_band_ceiling_cents` at or below `comp_band_floor_cents` when publishing a fee band. Then a manual ledger movement whose `signed_amount_cents` is zero yields `ERR_INVALID_LEDGER_AMOUNT`.
3. **Referential resolution, in three steps.** An unresolvable path identifier yields that entity's not-found error; then an unresolvable body identifier yields the same per-entity catalogue; then rows that resolve but do not belong together yield `ERR_ENTITY_RELATIONSHIP_MISMATCH`.
4. **Optimistic concurrency.** A supplied `expected_requisition_version`, `expected_candidate_version` or `expected_placement_version` that differs from the stored parent `version` yields the matching stale-version error. This runs after the parent has been resolved but before any state or business rule, so a stale version on a request that is also invalid for business reasons still returns 412.
5. **Terminality, then state legality.** A row already sitting in a terminal state of the machine the request would drive yields `ERR_ENTITY_TERMINAL`, and this test is taken **first** on every endpoint that guards terminality at all, so the endpoint's specific state error only ever reports on non-terminal states. Ranked immediately below it, a subject row whose current non-terminal state offers no transition to the requested target yields the endpoint's specific state error — `ERR_REQUISITION_NOT_SCOPING`, `ERR_TRIAL_NOT_SCHEDULED`, `ERR_TRIAL_NOT_IN_PROGRESS`, `ERR_BACKGROUND_NOT_FLAGGED`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_UNDER_REVIEW`, `ERR_MILESTONE_NOT_INVOICED`, `ERR_PLACEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`, `ERR_CASE_NOT_INVESTIGATING`, or the generic `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` where the endpoint has no specific one. Because state legality outranks every temporal and business rule below, recording an outcome on a trial that is still `SCHEDULED` and whose window has not elapsed reports `ERR_TRIAL_NOT_IN_PROGRESS`, not the window error.
6. **Household eligibility, then search constraints.** `ERR_NDA_NOT_COUNTERSIGNED`, then `ERR_CLIENT_SUSPENDED`, then `ERR_SCOPING_ROUND_LIMIT`, then `ERR_REQUISITION_HAS_ACTIVE_TRIALS`, then `ERR_NO_FEE_SCHEDULE_MATCH`, then `ERR_FEE_BAND_OVERLAP`.
7. **Safety, vetting and scheduling, in this order.** `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, then `ERR_REFERENCE_CHAIN_MISMATCH`, then `ERR_CANDIDATE_NOT_VETTED`, then `ERR_CANDIDATE_UNAVAILABLE`, then `ERR_COOLING_OFF_ACTIVE`, then `ERR_TRIAL_NOT_STARTED`, then `ERR_TRIAL_WINDOW_NOT_ELAPSED`, then `ERR_TRIAL_OVERLAP`, then `ERR_TRIAL_WEEKLY_CAP_EXCEEDED`, then `ERR_CANDIDATE_SCORE_BELOW_THRESHOLD`, then `ERR_REFERENCE_CHAIN_TOO_SHORT`.
8. **Negotiation and offer preconditions.** `ERR_LIVE_IN_INCOMPATIBLE`, then `ERR_NEGOTIATION_ROUND_LIMIT`, then `ERR_RESPONDER_MUST_ALTERNATE`, then `ERR_NO_PASSED_TRIAL`, then `ERR_ACCOMMODATION_NOT_ACCEPTED`.
9. **Money and compliance gates.** `ERR_CLIENT_CREDIT_HOLD`, then `ERR_PAYROLL_HANDOFF_NOT_ATTESTED`, then `ERR_EIN_NOT_ISSUED`, then `ERR_PAYROLL_HANDOFF_EXISTS`, then `ERR_REFERENCE_PERIOD_OVERLAP`, then `ERR_PAYMENT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`, then `ERR_GUARANTEE_WINDOW_EXPIRED`, then `ERR_INVALID_SEPARATION_DATE`, then `ERR_OPEN_CLAIM_EXISTS`, then `ERR_REMEDY_MISMATCH`, then `ERR_NON_SOLICITATION_TERM_EXPIRED`, then `ERR_SETTLEMENT_EXCEEDS_PENALTY`, and finally `ERR_CLOCK_NOT_MONOTONIC`.

Three consequences of this ladder are worth calling out because they are easy to get backwards. A candidate who both scores below the presentation threshold and belongs to a household over its credit limit is reported as `ERR_CANDIDATE_SCORE_BELOW_THRESHOLD`, because a safety judgement is never traded against a receivable. A reference payload that both breaks the chain ordering and overlaps a stored employment period is reported as `ERR_REFERENCE_CHAIN_MISMATCH`, since chain continuity outranks period overlap. And a negative `settlement_amount_cents` is caught by the numeric-range step as `ERR_INVALID_RANGE` long before the settlement-versus-penalty comparison is reached.

### 2.12. The Household Receivable Ledger and the Credit Hold

Each household carries an append-only ledger. A `LedgerEntry` names its household through `client_id`, occupies a 1-based `sequence_no` that increments monotonically within that household, and records a `signed_amount_cents` movement, a human-readable `memo`, and a `balance_after_cents` snapshot stamped once at append time. Nothing in the ledger is ever updated or deleted.

The sign convention is fixed per `entry_type`, and there are seven kinds. `FEE_INVOICED` and `PENALTY_ASSESSED` are positive — they increase what the household owes. `FEE_PAYMENT`, `FEE_REVERSAL`, `REFUND_ISSUED` and `PENALTY_SETTLED` are negative — they reduce it. `MANUAL_ADJUSTMENT` may be either sign but never zero, and it is the only `entry_type` a caller may nominate directly; the other six are produced exclusively by the workflow transitions that own them. On five of those six the `memo` is composed by the server and names the object being settled — the milestone type on a `FEE_INVOICED`, `FEE_PAYMENT` or `FEE_REVERSAL`, the claim on a `REFUND_ISSUED`, the case on a `PENALTY_ASSESSED`. `PENALTY_SETTLED` is the exception: it carries through the optional `memo` supplied by the settling caller, and is null when none was supplied.

Each row also carries a polymorphic back-reference: `related_ref_type` is one of `NONE`, `PLACEMENT`, `FEE_MILESTONE`, `REPLACEMENT_CLAIM` or `NON_SOLICITATION_CASE`, defaulting to `NONE`, and `related_ref_id` holds the identifier of the object named by that tag. When `related_ref_type` is `NONE` — as it always is for a manual adjustment — `related_ref_id` is null. This is deliberately not a declared foreign key; the tag is what tells a reader which collection to look in.

A household's `outstanding_balance_cents` is the running fold of its ledger. Starting from zero, each row in ascending `sequence_no` order adds its `signed_amount_cents` to the running total, and the total after each row is what that row's `balance_after_cents` preserves; the household's `outstanding_balance_cents` is the total after the last row. The invariant is exact and holds after every append: the household's `outstanding_balance_cents` equals the sum of `signed_amount_cents` across its ledger, and equals the last row's `balance_after_cents`. Because an invoicing entry and its reversal carry identical magnitudes with opposite signs, invoicing a milestone and then reversing it returns the balance to precisely its prior value.

The balance drives one gate. When a household's `outstanding_balance_cents` is **strictly greater** than 5,000,000 cents — fifty thousand dollars — it is on **credit hold**, and neither a new trial booking nor a new offer may be made for it; both are refused with `ERR_CLIENT_CREDIT_HOLD` (HTTP 409). A balance of exactly 5,000,000 cents still transacts.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Household Intake, the Confidentiality Agreement and the Scoping Loop

A prospective household enters the system when an `AgencyPrincipal` or a `PlacementConsultant` records its intake. The payload carries the `household_name` that will appear on the engagement letter (a required string of 1 to 120 characters), the `primary_contact_email` of the household's point of contact (required, and it must contain an `@` character), and a `region_code` drawn from `NY`, `CA`, `FL`, `TX` and `MA` — the region is what later selects the state-specific payroll and tax guidance packet. The new household begins life with `status` `PROSPECT`, `confidentiality_agreement_status` `UNSIGNED`, a null `signatory_name`, a null `countersigned_at`, `outstanding_balance_cents` of 0, `placement_count` of 0, a null `non_solicitation_expires_on` and `version` 1.

Nothing else may happen for that household until the two-party confidentiality agreement is complete, and it completes in two ordered moves. The household itself signs first: acting as `Client`, it submits an `action` of `SIGN` together with the `signatory_name` to be captured, which advances `confidentiality_agreement_status` from `UNSIGNED` to `SIGNED` and stores the name. That name is **optional** — a string of 1 to 120 characters when supplied, and the field is simply left null when it is omitted — so a signature with no named signatory is accepted. The agency then countersigns: acting as `AgencyPrincipal`, it submits an `action` of `COUNTERSIGN`, which ignores any `signatory_name` in its body and is admissible only while `confidentiality_agreement_status` is `SIGNED`. Countersignature advances that field from `SIGNED` to `COUNTERSIGNED`, stamps `countersigned_at` with the current logical-clock instant, and drives the household lifecycle `PROSPECT → ONBOARDED` — the only path by which a household becomes eligible for role scoping — incrementing `Client.version` by 1. A countersignature attempted while the agreement is still `UNSIGNED`, or a second signature on an already-`COUNTERSIGNED` agreement, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409).

Two derived household figures are maintained continuously and are worth defining here because later rules read them. `placement_count` is the number of `Placement` rows belonging to this household whose `placement_status` is one of `ACTIVE`, `GUARANTEE_EXPIRED`, `TERMINATED_REPLACED`, `TERMINATED_REFUNDED` or `TERMINATED_NO_REMEDY` — offers still awaiting acceptance and voided offers do not count. `non_solicitation_expires_on` is an ISO date computed as the latest `start_date` among the household's placements whose `placement_status` is anything other than `VOIDED`, plus 730 days — a two-year term — and it is null whenever `placement_count` is 0.

Opening a search is done by a `PlacementConsultant` or by the household acting as `Client`. The request names the household through `client_id`, the `role_type` being searched (one of `ESTATE_MANAGER`, `HOUSEKEEPER`, `CHEF` or `CHAUFFEUR`), the `first_year_compensation_cents` being scoped (a figure the agency describes as at least 100,000 cents, though the only boundary enforced at the API surface is the `_cents` rule of §2.11 that it may not be negative), and the `schedule_pattern` from `FIVE_TWO`, `SIX_ONE`, `ROTA_TWO_TWO` and `ON_CALL`; it may also carry `live_in_required`, which defaults to false, and an opening `scope_note`, which is optional here and left null when omitted. Two household gates apply before the row is written: a household whose `confidentiality_agreement_status` is anything other than `COUNTERSIGNED` is refused with `ERR_NDA_NOT_COUNTERSIGNED` (HTTP 409) — the child's admissibility is decided by a field on its parent — and a household whose `status` is `SUSPENDED` is refused with `ERR_CLIENT_SUSPENDED` (HTTP 409). A successfully opened requisition sits in `status` `DRAFT` with `scoping_round` 0, `active_trial_count` 0, a null `resolved_fee_schedule_id`, `fee_percent_bps`, `guarantee_window_days` and `provisional_fee_cents`, `created_by_user_id` stamped from the acting `X-User-ID`, and `version` 1. `active_trial_count`, wherever it is read, is the number of `TrialEngagement` rows on this requisition whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS`.

Scoping is a **round-based loop**, and it is the first of this system's cycles. A scoping round may be run by a `PlacementConsultant` or by the household, and it moves the requisition either `DRAFT → SCOPING` on the first pass or `SOURCING → SCOPING` on a re-scope. A requisition in any other non-terminal state — `SCOPING` itself, or `OFFER_PENDING` — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, and a `PLACED` or `WITHDRAWN` requisition is refused with `ERR_ENTITY_TERMINAL` ahead of that check. Both paths require the household to be `ONBOARDED`, a requirement expressed through the two household errors of §2.11 in their ladder order — a household whose agreement is not `COUNTERSIGNED` reports `ERR_NDA_NOT_COUNTERSIGNED` and a `SUSPENDED` household reports `ERR_CLIENT_SUSPENDED` — and require `scoping_round` to be strictly below the ceiling of three rounds; the re-scope path additionally requires that the requisition has no live trials, meaning `active_trial_count` must be 0. A re-scope attempted while at least one trial is still `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS` is refused with `ERR_REQUISITION_HAS_ACTIVE_TRIALS` (HTTP 409), and any scoping attempt on a requisition whose `scoping_round` has already reached three is refused with `ERR_SCOPING_ROUND_LIMIT` (HTTP 409) — the third round is accepted and takes `scoping_round` from 2 to 3, and it is the fourth attempt that is refused. Each accepted round increments `scoping_round` by 1, overwrites `scope_note` with the supplied text — `scope_note` is **required** on a scoping round, unlike at open, and its absence is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and overwrites `first_year_compensation_cents`, `live_in_required` and `schedule_pattern` with whichever of those three values the payload supplies. On the re-scope path specifically, the previously resolved commercial terms are discarded: `resolved_fee_schedule_id`, `fee_percent_bps`, `guarantee_window_days` and `provisional_fee_cents` are all cleared back to null, so that the next finalisation re-resolves the fee band from scratch against the revised compensation. Both paths increment `RoleRequisition.version` by 1.

Closing a round is exclusive to the `PlacementConsultant` and transitions the requisition `SCOPING → SOURCING`. It accepts an optional `note`, which is not persisted anywhere. A requisition in any other **non-terminal** state is refused with `ERR_REQUISITION_NOT_SCOPING` (HTTP 409), while a `PLACED` or `WITHDRAWN` requisition is refused with `ERR_ENTITY_TERMINAL` instead, terminality being tested first; and a suspended household blocks the operation with `ERR_CLIENT_SUSPENDED`. On success the fee band is resolved and the provisional fee is stamped, exactly as described in §3.2, and `RoleRequisition.version` is incremented.

Abandoning a search is available to a `PlacementConsultant` or the household and accepts an optional `reason`, which is read but not persisted — the requisition has no field to hold it. Withdrawal is admissible from `DRAFT`, `SCOPING`, `SOURCING` and `TRIALING`, and takes the requisition to `WITHDRAWN`. It is **not** admissible from `OFFER_PENDING`, which is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`; and because `PLACED` and `WITHDRAWN` are terminal, an attempt on either is refused with `ERR_ENTITY_TERMINAL`. Withdrawal cascades in two directions: every `TrialEngagement` of the requisition whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS` moves to `CANCELLED` with `cancel_reason` `CLIENT_REQUEST`, each such cancellation restoring the affected candidate's `availability_status` from `ON_TRIAL` to `AVAILABLE`; and every `AccommodationTerm` of the requisition whose `status` is `PROPOSED` or `COUNTERED` moves to `REJECTED`. Every row touched has its `version` incremented, including the requisition itself.

Three further requisition transitions are driven by the system rather than by a caller, and all three land back in `SOURCING`. When the requisition's last non-terminal trial leaves the active set — through a failed outcome or a cancellation — and the recomputed `active_trial_count` reaches 0, the requisition falls back `TRIALING → SOURCING`, provided no placement on that requisition is sitting in `PENDING_ACCEPTANCE`. When the requisition's only `PENDING_ACCEPTANCE` placement becomes `VOIDED` and no replacement offer has taken its place — that is, no placement on the requisition is in `PENDING_ACCEPTANCE` or `ACTIVE` — the requisition falls back `OFFER_PENDING → SOURCING`. And when a granted replacement claim reopens a filled search, the requisition moves `PLACED → SOURCING`; notably this reopening does **not** consume a scoping round, so the household's three rounds remain available for the fresh search. Each of these system transitions increments `RoleRequisition.version` by 1.

### 3.2. Fee Bands and the Resolution of the Placement Fee

The agency prices placements from a published band table that only an `AgencyPrincipal` may write. Publishing a band names the `role_type` it prices, the inclusive `comp_band_floor_cents` at which the band begins, the `fee_percent_bps` charged inside it, the `guarantee_window_days` the band grants, and the `effective_from` ISO date from which the band may be selected; `comp_band_ceiling_cents` is optional, and omitting it publishes an unbounded top band; both band edges are `_cents` fields and so may not be negative. A new band is created with `status` `ACTIVE` and `version` 1. Bands for the same `role_type` sharing the same `effective_from` may not overlap: if an existing `ACTIVE` band for that role type and that same effective date covers any part of the proposed range, the publication is refused with `ERR_FEE_BAND_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). Overlap is judged on half-open ranges, so bands that merely touch — one band's ceiling exactly equal to the next band's floor — do not overlap and are accepted. A band may later be retired by the same role, moving it `ACTIVE → RETIRED` with an optional `reason` that is accepted but not persisted; retirement removes the row from every future resolution's candidate set but leaves already-stamped references on requisitions and placements untouched, which is what makes the snapshots on those rows genuinely pinned. Retirement increments `FeeSchedule.version`, and a second retirement attempt on an already-`RETIRED` band is refused with `ERR_ENTITY_TERMINAL`.

**Resolving a fee percentage** is a two-part act: assemble a candidate set, then pick a winner. The candidate set contains every `FeeSchedule` row whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, whose `role_type` equals the role type being priced, and whose band covers the subject compensation figure — meaning `comp_band_floor_cents` is less than or equal to that figure, and either `comp_band_ceiling_cents` is null or the figure is strictly less than `comp_band_ceiling_cents`. The bands are therefore **half-open**: a compensation figure landing exactly on a band's floor belongs to that band, while a figure landing exactly on a band's ceiling belongs to the next band up. The winner among the candidates is the row with the greatest `effective_from`; when two or more share that greatest `effective_from`, the tie is broken by `created_at` ascending, so the earliest-created of the tied rows wins — and where even `created_at` is identical, the row published first wins. The winner's identifier is stamped as the subject's fee-schedule reference, and its `fee_percent_bps` and `guarantee_window_days` are copied onto the subject. When the candidate set is empty the resolution has no result at all and the calling operation is refused with `ERR_NO_FEE_SCHEDULE_MATCH` (HTTP 422); the most common way to hit this is a compensation figure equal to the top band's ceiling when no unbounded band has been published above it. Basis points are handled as exact integers throughout the resolution — no rounding occurs in this step.

The same band table is consulted twice in the life of one placement, against two different compensation figures. At scope finalisation the subject compensation is the requisition's `first_year_compensation_cents`, and the resolved row is recorded as `resolved_fee_schedule_id` with its `fee_percent_bps` and `guarantee_window_days` copied onto the requisition. At confirmed start the subject compensation is the placement's `actual_first_year_compensation_cents`, and the resolved row is recorded as `snapshot_fee_schedule_id`. A household whose actual package crosses a band ceiling is therefore genuinely re-priced when the placement starts, and both resolutions read the requisition's `role_type`.

The **provisional fee** is computed at scope finalisation from a single aggregate figure. Take the requisition's `first_year_compensation_cents`, multiply it by the `fee_percent_bps` just resolved, divide by 10000, and round the result half up to a whole number of cents; that value is stamped as the requisition's `provisional_fee_cents`. There is exactly one rounding operation, and it is applied to the aggregate compensation figure. When an offer is later created it copies this same commercial snapshot, and its `fee_variant` is recorded as `PROVISIONAL` to make explicit which of the two computations produced `fee_cents`. The `FINAL` variant, and the way in which it deliberately diverges from `PROVISIONAL`, is described in §3.6.

### 3.3. Candidate Vetting: References, the Reference Chain and the Background Fork

A candidate is registered by a `VettingSpecialist` or a `PlacementConsultant` with a `full_name` (required, 1 to 120 characters), a `contact_email` containing an `@`, the `primary_role_type` they are represented for (one of `ESTATE_MANAGER`, `HOUSEKEEPER`, `CHEF`, `CHAUFFEUR`), whole `years_experience` of household service between 0 and 60, and optionally `live_in_willing`, which defaults to false. A fresh candidate begins at `vetting_status` `UNVETTED` and `availability_status` `AVAILABLE`, with `cooling_off_until` null, `verified_reference_count`, `reference_coverage_months`, `longest_contiguous_months`, `screening_score` and `placement_ready_score` all 0, and `version` 1.

References are recorded only by a `VettingSpecialist`, one row per employment period. Each row names the `principal_employer_name` that employed the candidate for that period (required, 1 to 120 characters), the `relationship_type` of the referee (`PRINCIPAL_EMPLOYER`, `AGENCY_SUPERVISOR` or `PEER`), the inclusive `employment_start_month` and `employment_end_month` as `YYYY-MM` month keys, and the referee's `contact_email`. It may also carry `prior_reference_id`, naming the immediately older link in this candidate's chain; the chain head leaves it null. The new row is created with `status` `PENDING`, a null `verification_note`, a null `verified_by_user_id`, and `created_by_user_id` stamped from the acting `X-User-ID`. Recording a reference also drives the vetting axis: a candidate at `UNVETTED` moves `UNVETTED → SCREENING` on its first reference, and — this is a genuine cycle, not a mistake — a candidate previously rejected moves `REJECTED → SCREENING`, so a rejected candidate can be re-vetted from scratch simply by adding a new reference. Either way `Candidate.version` is incremented, and both the coverage figures and both score variants are recomputed.

Two structural rules police the chain, and their relative order matters. **Chain continuity** comes first: when `prior_reference_id` is supplied, the referenced row's `employment_end_month` must not be later than the new row's `employment_start_month`; if it is later, the request is refused with `ERR_REFERENCE_CHAIN_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). **Period overlap** comes second: the proposed closed month interval from `employment_start_month` through `employment_end_month` must not intersect the interval of any existing reference for the same candidate whose `status` is anything other than `UNREACHABLE`, and an intersection is refused with `ERR_REFERENCE_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). Because these intervals are closed, sharing even a single month is an overlap, whereas one period ending in December and the next beginning the following January is adjacency and is accepted. A payload that violates both rules at once reports the chain mismatch, since continuity outranks overlap.

Verification is where separation of duties bites. A `VettingSpecialist` records an `outcome` of `VERIFIED`, `UNREACHABLE` or `DISPUTED` together with an optional `verification_note`, and the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the row's `created_by_user_id`, or the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). The row must be `PENDING`; since `VERIFIED`, `UNREACHABLE` and `DISPUTED` are all terminal, a second verification attempt is refused with `ERR_ENTITY_TERMINAL`. All three outcomes stamp `verified_by_user_id`, write `verification_note` only when the request actually supplied one — an omitted note leaves the stored field untouched rather than nulling it, and this is how every optional note field in the system behaves — and increment the reference row's `version`. Only `VERIFIED` contributes anything to the dossier: an `UNREACHABLE` or `DISPUTED` row contributes zero reference weight and zero coverage months. Recording a `VERIFIED` outcome recomputes the candidate's coverage figures and both score variants, and may advance the vetting axis `SCREENING → REFERENCES_VERIFIED`.

**Reference chain coverage** is computed over the candidate's `VERIFIED` reference rows only; `PENDING`, `UNREACHABLE` and `DISPUTED` rows contribute neither to the count nor to any month total. `verified_reference_count` is simply how many `VERIFIED` rows the candidate has. Each `VERIFIED` row contributes one interval running from its `employment_start_month` to its `employment_end_month`, closed and inclusive at both ends and measured in whole months — a single-month reference, where the start month equals the end month, counts as one month. The intervals are sorted by start month and then merged pairwise whenever two of them either overlap or are *adjacent*, where adjacency means the next interval's start month is the calendar month immediately following the previous interval's end month, leaving a gap of zero months. `reference_coverage_months` is the sum, over the merged intervals, of the inclusive month length of each; `longest_contiguous_months` is the greatest inclusive month length among the merged intervals, and is 0 when there are no merged intervals at all.

The gate out of `SCREENING` reads exactly those two figures. A candidate advances `SCREENING → REFERENCES_VERIFIED` when the recomputed `verified_reference_count` is at least two **and** the recomputed `longest_contiguous_months` is at least twelve — one unbroken verified year. Both comparisons are inclusive, so exactly two references spanning exactly twelve contiguous months passes.

Once the reference gate is met, a `VettingSpecialist` orders a background screen naming the vendor's `provider_reference` (required, 1 to 64 characters) and a non-empty `scope_items` array whose members are drawn from `IDENTITY`, `CRIMINAL`, `DMV`, `CREDENTIAL` and `SOCIAL_MEDIA`. The new screen is created with `status` `INITIATED`, `rerun_count` 0, and null `findings_note`, `recorded_by_user_id`, `adjudicated_by_user_id` and `waiver_note`; ordering it advances the candidate `REFERENCES_VERIFIED → BACKGROUND_PENDING` and increments `Candidate.version`. Ordering a screen for a candidate not sitting at `REFERENCES_VERIFIED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.

Recording the vendor's result is a **three-way fork**, and the three branches have deliberately different side effects. Each is recorded by a `VettingSpecialist` supplying a `result` and an optional `findings_note`, and each is admissible while the screen's `status` is `INITIATED` or `INCONCLUSIVE`. A `result` of `CLEARED` moves the screen to `CLEARED`, stamps `recorded_by_user_id` and — only when one was supplied — `findings_note`, and advances the candidate `BACKGROUND_PENDING → CLEARED`. A `result` of `FLAGGED` moves the screen to `FLAGGED` and stamps the same two fields, but leaves the candidate at `BACKGROUND_PENDING` pending adjudication — no candidate-side movement at all. A `result` of `INCONCLUSIVE` moves the screen to `INCONCLUSIVE`, stamps the same two fields, and has no candidate-side effect whatsoever. Vendors sometimes come back inconclusive repeatedly, so `INCONCLUSIVE → INCONCLUSIVE` is a permitted self-loop with no bound on how often it may be taken; `rerun_count` is incremented by exactly 1 each time an `INCONCLUSIVE` result is recorded on a screen that is *already* `INCONCLUSIVE`, and the re-runs are otherwise observable only through that counter. Every result recording increments `BackgroundVerification.version` and recomputes both of the candidate's score variants. A result recorded on a screen already in `CLEARED` or `FLAGGED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, and one aimed at an already-adjudicated screen is refused with `ERR_ENTITY_TERMINAL`.

A `FLAGGED` screen rejoins the main line only through adjudication by an `AgencyPrincipal`, who supplies a `disposition` of `PASS` or `FAIL`, the mandatory `expected_candidate_version`, and an optional `waiver_note`. The screen must be `FLAGGED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_BACKGROUND_NOT_FLAGGED` (HTTP 409); an already-adjudicated screen is refused with `ERR_ENTITY_TERMINAL`; and a stale `expected_candidate_version` is refused with `ERR_STALE_CANDIDATE_VERSION` (HTTP 412) before either of those checks would be reached. The two dispositions are as asymmetric as they look. `PASS` moves the screen `FLAGGED → ADJUDICATED_PASS`, stamps `adjudicated_by_user_id` and, when one was supplied, `waiver_note`, advances the candidate `BACKGROUND_PENDING → CLEARED`, and increments both `Candidate.version` and `BackgroundVerification.version` by 1. `FAIL` moves the screen `FLAGGED → ADJUDICATED_FAIL`, stamps those same two actor and note fields, and fans a four-hop cascade: first the candidate's `vetting_status` moves `BACKGROUND_PENDING → REJECTED`; second, because the candidate is now rejected, every `TrialEngagement` of that candidate whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS` moves to `CANCELLED` with `cancel_reason` `VETTING_FAILED`; third, because those trials have left the active set, every affected `RoleRequisition` sitting in `TRIALING` whose recomputed `active_trial_count` reaches 0 falls back to `SOURCING`; and fourth, the candidate's `availability_status` moves `ON_TRIAL → INACTIVE` with `cooling_off_until` cleared to null. `FAIL` likewise increments both `Candidate.version` and `BackgroundVerification.version`, and every row touched anywhere in the cascade has its own `version` incremented.

Two **vetting score variants** are maintained on every candidate, and they disagree on both their inputs and their thresholds. Both share three components. **Experience points** are the candidate's `years_experience` multiplied by three, capped at 36 points — so twelve years of service reaches the cap and further years add nothing. **Reference points** are the sum, over the candidate's `VERIFIED` reference rows, of the weight of each row's `relationship_type` — twelve points for a `PRINCIPAL_EMPLOYER`, eight for an `AGENCY_SUPERVISOR`, four for a `PEER` — capped at 24 points. **Coverage points** are the candidate's `reference_coverage_months`, capped at 20 points, so a candidate with twenty or more merged verified months is at the cap.

`screening_score` is the sum of those three components and nothing else: background work is not yet expected when a candidate is being put in front of a household, so background points contribute zero, and disputed references are ignored entirely at this stage. Its gate is a `screening_score` of at least 70 points, evaluated inclusively so that a score of exactly 70 passes.

`placement_ready_score` adds two further terms to the same three components. **Background points** are 20 when a `BackgroundVerification` of this candidate has reached `CLEARED`, 10 when the best status the candidate's screens have reached is `ADJUDICATED_PASS` — a waived flag is worth half of a clean result — and 0 otherwise. **The disputed penalty** is 15 points for every `ReferenceCheck` of this candidate whose `status` is `DISPUTED`, and it is subtracted. `placement_ready_score` is therefore experience points plus reference points plus coverage points plus background points minus the disputed penalty, floored at 0 so the field never goes negative. Its gate is a `placement_ready_score` of at least 80 points. Because the two variants weigh different evidence against different thresholds, the very same stored dossier can admit a candidate to a trial day and refuse them an offer, and that divergence is intentional rather than a defect. Both scores are integer point totals with no rounding and no fractional weights, and both are recomputed on every reference recording, every reference verification, every background result and every adjudication.

Finally, the availability axis has its own release path. A `VettingSpecialist` or `PlacementConsultant` may return a candidate to the pool, optionally supplying a `note` that is accepted but not persisted. From `COOLING_OFF` this is admissible only when the logical-clock date is on or after `cooling_off_until`, or when `cooling_off_until` is null and there is therefore nothing to wait for; an earlier date is refused with `ERR_COOLING_OFF_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409), so a clock landing exactly on `cooling_off_until` releases the candidate while one day earlier does not. A successful release from `COOLING_OFF` moves `availability_status` `COOLING_OFF → AVAILABLE` and clears `cooling_off_until` to null. From `INACTIVE` the same operation moves `INACTIVE → AVAILABLE`, but only for a candidate whose `vetting_status` is `CLEARED`; any other vetting state is refused with `ERR_CANDIDATE_NOT_VETTED` (HTTP 409). Release attempted from any other availability state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, and each successful release increments `Candidate.version`.

### 3.4. Presenting Candidates: Trial Days and Working Interviews

A candidate is put in front of a household by a `PlacementConsultant`, who books an engagement under the requisition. The booking names the `candidate_id`, the `engagement_type` — `TRIAL_DAY` for a paid day of work in the household, or `WORKING_INTERVIEW` for an unpaid period of observation — the inclusive `start_at` and the exclusive `end_at` of the window, and the `day_rate_cents` payable to the candidate. The new row is created with `status` `SCHEDULED`, a null `cancel_reason` and a null `outcome_note`. Two of its fields are derived rather than supplied: `client_id` is denormalised from the requisition, so that household-slot overlap can be checked without a join, and `iso_week_key` is derived from `start_at` as the ISO year and week formatted as four digits, a hyphen, the letter `W` and two digits, with each week beginning at Monday 00:00:00.000Z. Booking also moves the candidate `AVAILABLE → ON_TRIAL` on the availability axis, and moves the requisition `SOURCING → TRIALING`, or takes the `TRIALING → TRIALING` self-loop when another candidate is already on trial for the same search; either way `active_trial_count` is recomputed and `RoleRequisition.version` is incremented.

The booking gate is the densest in the system, and the order in which its conditions are evaluated is the order given in §2.11. Structurally, `end_at` must be strictly after `start_at`, and an `end_at` equal to or earlier than `start_at` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`; `day_rate_cents` must not be negative, and must be exactly 0 when `engagement_type` is `WORKING_INTERVIEW`, either violation being refused with `ERR_INVALID_RANGE`. The requisition must be in `SOURCING` or `TRIALING`, and any other state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`; the household must not be `SUSPENDED`, or the booking is refused with `ERR_CLIENT_SUSPENDED`. Then come the four candidate gates. The candidate's `vetting_status` must be one of `REFERENCES_VERIFIED`, `BACKGROUND_PENDING` or `CLEARED` — a trial may deliberately be run while the background screen is still outstanding — and anything else, including `UNVETTED`, `SCREENING` and `REJECTED`, is refused with `ERR_CANDIDATE_NOT_VETTED`. The candidate's `availability_status` must be exactly `AVAILABLE`, and any other value, including `ON_TRIAL` for a different household, is refused with `ERR_CANDIDATE_UNAVAILABLE`.

**Household slot overlap** is checked next. The household may not host two engagements at once, and it is entitled to a changeover buffer of 120 minutes — two hours — on each side of every booked window. Concretely: for every other `TrialEngagement` sharing this `client_id` whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS`, form its occupancy interval by taking its `start_at` less two hours as the inclusive lower bound and its `end_at` plus two hours as the exclusive upper bound; if that interval intersects the proposed window, taken as `start_at` inclusive to `end_at` exclusive, the booking is refused with `ERR_TRIAL_OVERLAP`. Because both intervals are half-open, the arithmetic is exact: after an engagement running 09:00 to 17:00 on a given day, the next admissible start for that same household is 19:00 exactly, and a start at 18:59 is refused.

**The candidate's weekly cap** is checked after overlap. A candidate may not work more than two trial engagements in one ISO week. Count the candidate's existing `TrialEngagement` rows whose `iso_week_key` equals the proposed row's `iso_week_key` and whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `PASSED` or `FAILED`; if that count has already reached two, the booking is refused with `ERR_TRIAL_WEEKLY_CAP_EXCEEDED`. Note which statuses are counted and which are not: an engagement that was `CANCELLED` does not consume a slot, while one that already `PASSED` or `FAILED` does. Each individual booking may be perfectly admissible on its own; it is the third one landing in the same ISO week that is refused.

**The presentation score gate** is checked after the cap: the candidate's `screening_score` must be at least 70 points, and a lower score is refused with `ERR_CANDIDATE_SCORE_BELOW_THRESHOLD` (HTTP 422). Only then is the household's receivable considered: a household whose `outstanding_balance_cents` exceeds the credit-hold limit is refused with `ERR_CLIENT_CREDIT_HOLD`. That ordering is deliberate and load-bearing — a household that is simultaneously over its credit limit and being shown an under-scoring candidate is told about the candidate, because a safety judgement about who enters a home is never traded against a receivable.

Once the clock has reached the window, a `PlacementConsultant` marks the engagement under way, optionally attaching a `note` that is accepted but not persisted. This moves `SCHEDULED → IN_PROGRESS`. The engagement must be `SCHEDULED`, and any other non-terminal state is refused with `ERR_TRIAL_NOT_SCHEDULED`; a `PASSED`, `FAILED` or `CANCELLED` engagement is refused with `ERR_ENTITY_TERMINAL` ahead of that. The candidate's `vetting_status` must not be `REJECTED`, or the start is refused with `ERR_CANDIDATE_NOT_VETTED`. Only then is the clock consulted: it must be at or after `start_at`, and an earlier clock is refused with `ERR_TRIAL_NOT_STARTED`, so a rejected candidate and an unreached window together report the vetting error.

When the window has closed, the same role records the household's verdict, supplying an `outcome` of `PASSED` or `FAILED` and an optional `outcome_note`, which is written to the row only when it is supplied. The engagement must be `IN_PROGRESS`, or the request is refused with `ERR_TRIAL_NOT_IN_PROGRESS`; the logical clock must be at or after `end_at`, or it is refused with `ERR_TRIAL_WINDOW_NOT_ELAPSED`. These two conditions interact in a way an implementer must get right: the state check ranks above the temporal one, so recording an outcome on a `SCHEDULED` engagement whose `end_at` has not been reached breaks both rules and must surface `ERR_TRIAL_NOT_IN_PROGRESS`, not `ERR_TRIAL_WINDOW_NOT_ELAPSED`.

The two outcomes differ only in their cascade. `PASSED` moves the engagement `IN_PROGRESS → PASSED`, recomputes the requisition's `active_trial_count`, and deliberately leaves the candidate at `ON_TRIAL` so that an offer can follow immediately — the candidate is not returned to the pool merely because the trial went well. `FAILED` moves the engagement `IN_PROGRESS → FAILED`, recomputes `active_trial_count`, returns the candidate `ON_TRIAL → AVAILABLE` provided the candidate holds no other engagement in `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS` **and** the candidate's `vetting_status` is not `REJECTED` — the same restoration proviso the cancellation path uses — and moves the requisition `TRIALING → SOURCING` when the recomputed `active_trial_count` reaches 0 and no placement on that requisition sits in `PENDING_ACCEPTANCE`. Both outcomes increment `TrialEngagement.version`.

Cancellation is available to a `PlacementConsultant` or to the household as `Client`, and requires an explicit `cancel_reason` from `CLIENT_REQUEST`, `CANDIDATE_WITHDRAWN`, `VETTING_FAILED` and `CLIENT_SUSPENDED`, plus an optional `note`. It is admissible from `SCHEDULED` and from `IN_PROGRESS`, both taking the engagement to `CANCELLED` with the supplied reason stamped; a terminal engagement is refused with `ERR_ENTITY_TERMINAL`. The optional `note` is accepted but not persisted on the engagement: `outcome_note` is written only by the record-outcome path, so a cancelled engagement's `outcome_note` stays null. Both paths carry the same side-effect set: the candidate is returned `ON_TRIAL → AVAILABLE` provided the candidate holds no other active engagement **and** the candidate's `vetting_status` is not `REJECTED`, and the requisition falls `TRIALING → SOURCING` when the recomputed `active_trial_count` reaches 0 and no placement on that requisition sits in `PENDING_ACCEPTANCE`. The system itself takes these same cancellation transitions in three places, each stamping a different reason: `VETTING_FAILED` on a failed background adjudication, `CLIENT_SUSPENDED` on a substantiated poaching case, and `CLIENT_REQUEST` on a withdrawn requisition.

### 3.5. Negotiating Live-in Accommodation and the Working Schedule

Live-in terms are negotiated as a three-cornered conversation between the agency, the household and the candidate. A `PlacementConsultant` or the household as `Client` opens the negotiation under the requisition, naming the `candidate_id` it concerns and the opening package: `live_in`, the contracted `weekly_hours`, the guaranteed `days_off_per_week`, whether `private_quarters` are provided, and the annual `imputed_accommodation_cents` value of any accommodation offered. It must also carry `expected_requisition_version`, and may carry a `note`, which is stored on the term row itself and is null when omitted. Opening a negotiation **supersedes** whatever was open before: every `AccommodationTerm` on the same `requisition_id` whose `status` is `PROPOSED` or `COUNTERED` moves to `SUPERSEDED` with its `version` incremented, and the new row is created with `status` `PROPOSED`, `round_no` 1 and `last_responder_role` `AGENCY`. `RoleRequisition.version` is incremented as well, which means the caller's `expected_requisition_version` must be refreshed before each successive proposal; a mismatch is refused with `ERR_STALE_REQUISITION_VERSION` (HTTP 412) before any business rule is consulted. The only state test applied to the parent is terminality — a `PLACED` or `WITHDRAWN` requisition is refused with `ERR_ENTITY_TERMINAL`, evaluated immediately after that version comparison — so a negotiation may be opened from `DRAFT`, `SCOPING`, `SOURCING`, `TRIALING` or `OFFER_PENDING` alike, and no household eligibility gate applies here at all: neither an uncountersigned agreement nor a `SUSPENDED` household blocks a proposal. `weekly_hours` and `days_off_per_week` are range-checked and `imputed_accommodation_cents` must not be negative and must be exactly 0 when `live_in` is false, each violation being refused with `ERR_INVALID_RANGE`. A term proposing `live_in` true for a candidate whose `live_in_willing` is false is refused with `ERR_LIVE_IN_INCOMPATIBLE` (HTTP 409).

The two counterparties then answer, and only they may: responding is exclusive to the `Client` and `Candidate` roles. A response carries a `response` of `COUNTER`, `ACCEPT` or `REJECT`, and may carry replacement values for `live_in`, `weekly_hours`, `days_off_per_week`, `private_quarters` and `imputed_accommodation_cents`, plus a `note`. The five package values are validated whatever the `response`, but only a `COUNTER` actually writes them: on an `ACCEPT` or a `REJECT` they are discarded, and the live-in judgement reads the term's **stored** `live_in` rather than anything the response supplied. The `note` is the exception — all three responses write it whenever one is supplied, overwriting whatever note the term already carried, and leave it untouched when it is omitted. Three rules govern who may speak and how often, and all three are applied to a `COUNTER` while a `REJECT` is exempt from every one of them: either party may reject at any time, in any round. **Alternation:** on a `COUNTER`, and on an `ACCEPT` taken out of `COUNTERED`, the party implied by `X-User-Role` must differ from `last_responder_role`, and a party attempting to answer its own move is refused with `ERR_RESPONDER_MUST_ALTERNATE` (HTTP 409) — a household cannot counter its own counter, and after the agency's opening proposal either counterparty may speak first, but then the other must reply. **The round cap:** a `COUNTER` is admissible only while `round_no` is strictly below four, so the fourth round is the last one that may be countered and a `COUNTER` arriving when `round_no` has already reached four is refused with `ERR_NEGOTIATION_ROUND_LIMIT` (HTTP 409). **Live-in compatibility** is re-checked on a `COUNTER` and on an `ACCEPT`, and never on a `REJECT`, so a counter that switches `live_in` to true against a candidate who is not `live_in_willing` is refused with `ERR_LIVE_IN_INCOMPATIBLE`. A response aimed at a term already in `ACCEPTED`, `REJECTED` or `SUPERSEDED` is refused with `ERR_ENTITY_TERMINAL`.

A `COUNTER` moves the term `PROPOSED → COUNTERED` on the first counter and then takes the `COUNTERED → COUNTERED` self-loop for each further round — the negotiation genuinely cycles between household and candidate until the cap is reached. Each counter increments `round_no` by 1, overwrites `live_in`, `weekly_hours`, `days_off_per_week`, `private_quarters` and `imputed_accommodation_cents` with the supplied counter values, sets `last_responder_role` to `CLIENT` or `CANDIDATE` according to the responding role, and increments `AccommodationTerm.version`.

An `ACCEPT` is admissible from `PROPOSED` and from `COUNTERED`. Accepting from `COUNTERED` additionally requires alternation — the accepting party must not be the party that made the last move — and both paths require that either `live_in` is false or the candidate's `live_in_willing` is true. Acceptance from `COUNTERED` also aligns the parent search to the agreed package: `RoleRequisition.live_in_required` is set to the accepted term's `live_in`, while `RoleRequisition.schedule_pattern` is deliberately left untouched, because the pattern describes the shape of the role rather than the negotiated accommodation. Either way `AccommodationTerm.version` is incremented, and the term becomes `ACCEPTED`, which is the precondition the placement confirmation will later look for. Neither an `ACCEPT` nor a `REJECT` alters `last_responder_role`: after the opening proposal stamps `AGENCY`, only a `COUNTER` ever rewrites that field, so a term settled out of a counter still records the party that made the last counter.

A `REJECT` is admissible from `PROPOSED` and from `COUNTERED`, takes the term to `REJECTED` and increments its `version`. The system also drives `PROPOSED → REJECTED` and `COUNTERED → REJECTED` when the parent requisition is withdrawn, and drives `PROPOSED → SUPERSEDED` and `COUNTERED → SUPERSEDED` when a newer term is opened on the same requisition. All three of `ACCEPTED`, `REJECTED` and `SUPERSEDED` are terminal.

### 3.6. The Offer, the Confirmed Start and the Final Re-pricing

An offer is extended by a `PlacementConsultant`. The request names the `requisition_id` and the `candidate_id`, the intended `start_date`, the four components of the household's package — `base_salary_cents`, `guaranteed_bonus_cents`, `accommodation_imputed_cents` and `overtime_allowance_cents` — and the mandatory `expected_requisition_version`. None of the four money components may be negative, or the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_RANGE`, and `start_date` must be a well-formed calendar date, or it is refused with `ERR_INVALID_DATE_FORMAT`. `actual_first_year_compensation_cents` is not supplied: it is computed as the exact sum of those four components and stored on the row.

The offer gate runs in this order. After the requisition and candidate have been resolved, the candidate must have at least one `TrialEngagement` on the named requisition at all, or the request is refused with `ERR_ENTITY_RELATIONSHIP_MISMATCH` (HTTP 409). The `expected_requisition_version` is then compared with the stored `RoleRequisition.version` and a mismatch is refused with `ERR_STALE_REQUISITION_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The requisition must be in `TRIALING`, or in `OFFER_PENDING` on the supersede path described below; any other state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. The household must not be `SUSPENDED`, or the offer is refused with `ERR_CLIENT_SUSPENDED`. The candidate's `vetting_status` must be exactly `CLEARED` — the looser trial-stage set does not apply to offers — and anything else is refused with `ERR_CANDIDATE_NOT_VETTED`. The candidate's `availability_status` must be `ON_TRIAL` or `OFFER_HELD`, and anything else is refused with `ERR_CANDIDATE_UNAVAILABLE`; `OFFER_HELD` is admitted only on the supersede path, meaning the candidate must be the `candidate_id` of the incumbent `PENDING_ACCEPTANCE` placement on the named requisition, so that a household may revise its own outstanding offer to the same candidate while a second household cannot poach a candidate who is already holding one. The candidate's `placement_ready_score` must be at least 80 points, or the offer is refused with `ERR_CANDIDATE_SCORE_BELOW_THRESHOLD` (HTTP 422).

**The role-conditional chain rule** is checked next, and it is the only rule in the system whose threshold depends on the role being filled. The candidate's `longest_contiguous_months` must be at least 24 — two unbroken verified years — when the requisition's `role_type` is `ESTATE_MANAGER` or `CHEF`, and at least 12 when it is `HOUSEKEEPER` or `CHAUFFEUR`; a shorter chain is refused with `ERR_REFERENCE_CHAIN_TOO_SHORT` (HTTP 422). The same stored reference chain therefore passes for a housekeeper and fails for a chef. After that, at least one `TrialEngagement` with the named `requisition_id` and `candidate_id` must have `status` `PASSED`, or the offer is refused with `ERR_NO_PASSED_TRIAL` (HTTP 409) — note the distinction from the relationship check above, which only asks whether any engagement exists at all. Then the household's credit position is tested and a balance over the limit is refused with `ERR_CLIENT_CREDIT_HOLD`. Last of all, the requisition must actually carry a resolved commercial snapshot: a null `fee_percent_bps` or a null `guarantee_window_days` — the state a re-scope deliberately leaves behind until the round is finalised again — is refused with `ERR_NO_FEE_SCHEDULE_MATCH` (HTTP 422), tested after the credit hold rather than before it.

Extending an offer does five things. First, if a `PENDING_ACCEPTANCE` placement already exists on the requisition, it is **superseded**: it moves to `VOIDED` with `void_reason` `SUPERSEDED_BY_NEW_OFFER`, and the supersede cascade described below runs against it. Second, the new placement is created with `placement_status` `PENDING_ACCEPTANCE`, `billing_status` `UNBILLED`, `fee_variant` `PROVISIONAL`, `client_id` taken from the requisition, `snapshot_fee_schedule_id`, `fee_percent_bps` and `guarantee_window_days` copied from the requisition's resolved snapshot, `guarantee_expires_on` computed as `start_date` plus `guarantee_window_days` days, a null `separation_date`, a null `void_reason`, `created_by_user_id` stamped from `X-User-ID`, and `fee_cents` computed by the `PROVISIONAL` variant described in §3.2. Third, three `FeeMilestone` rows are created in ascending `sequence_no` order and amounted by the allocation rule in §3.7. Fourth, the candidate moves `ON_TRIAL → OFFER_HELD`, or takes the `OFFER_HELD → OFFER_HELD` self-loop on the supersede path. Fifth, the requisition moves `TRIALING → OFFER_PENDING`, or takes the `OFFER_PENDING → OFFER_PENDING` self-loop on the supersede path, and its `version` is incremented.

**The supersede cascade** on a voided offer is four hops deep and touches three entities. Hop one voids the placement itself and stamps `void_reason`, which takes the value `SUPERSEDED_BY_NEW_OFFER` when a newer offer displaced this one and the value `CLIENT_WITHDRAWN` when the household abandoned the offer instead; those two are the only permitted values of the field, and it stays null on any placement that was never voided. Hop two settles its billing rows: every `FeeMilestone` of that placement whose `invoice_status` is `PENDING` moves to `VOIDED`, and every one whose `invoice_status` is `INVOICED` or `OVERDUE` moves to `REVERSED`. Hop three posts the money back: each milestone that moved to `REVERSED` appends a `LedgerEntry` of `entry_type` `FEE_REVERSAL` with `signed_amount_cents` equal to the negative of that milestone's `amount_cents` and `related_ref_type` `FEE_MILESTONE`. Hop four recomputes the household's `outstanding_balance_cents` from the extended ledger. Alongside those hops, the voided placement's `billing_status` becomes `REVERSED` when at least one milestone was actually reversed and otherwise stays `UNBILLED`, and the candidate moves `OFFER_HELD → AVAILABLE` — which on the supersede path is immediately overridden by the new offer taking the same candidate back to `OFFER_HELD`. Every row touched has its `version` incremented. When the requisition's only `PENDING_ACCEPTANCE` placement is voided and no placement on that requisition remains in `PENDING_ACCEPTANCE` or `ACTIVE`, the requisition itself falls back `OFFER_PENDING → SOURCING`.

**Confirming the start** is exclusive to the `AgencyPrincipal` and accepts only an optional `note`, which is not persisted. The placement must be `PENDING_ACCEPTANCE`, and a terminal placement is refused with `ERR_ENTITY_TERMINAL` while any other non-terminal state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. The fee band must then re-resolve against the actual package, and an empty candidate set is refused with `ERR_NO_FEE_SCHEDULE_MATCH` (HTTP 422). Only after that does the accommodation gate apply: an `AccommodationTerm` with the placement's `requisition_id` and `candidate_id` and `status` `ACCEPTED` must exist, or the confirmation is refused with `ERR_ACCOMMODATION_NOT_ACCEPTED` (HTTP 409). The band test therefore outranks the accommodation test, exactly as the ladder in §2.11 ranks them, so a placement that both prices out of band and lacks an accepted term reports the fee-schedule error.

Confirmation is where the placement is priced for the second and final time, and the sequence matters. First the band is re-resolved against `actual_first_year_compensation_cents` — the sum of the four stored components — and `snapshot_fee_schedule_id`, `fee_percent_bps` and `guarantee_window_days` are all overwritten with the newly resolved values, which may differ from the requisition-time snapshot if the actual package landed in a different band. Then the **`FINAL` fee** is computed: each of the four components is multiplied by the re-resolved `fee_percent_bps`, divided by 10000 and **rounded half up individually**, in the fixed order `base_salary_cents`, then `guaranteed_bonus_cents`, then `accommodation_imputed_cents`, then `overtime_allowance_cents`; the four rounded results are then summed, and that sum is stored as `fee_cents` with `fee_variant` set to `FINAL`. Rounding happens **before** the sum, four separate times, and this is the whole point of the two variants: the `FINAL` figure is not the `PROVISIONAL` figure recomputed, and on identical total compensation the two can differ by a cent or more. An implementer who sums the components first and rounds once will produce the `PROVISIONAL` answer under a `FINAL` label. Next, `guarantee_expires_on` is recomputed as `start_date` plus the re-resolved `guarantee_window_days` days, and the window it describes is half-open: it covers `start_date` inclusive up to but excluding `guarantee_expires_on`. Then the milestone schedule is re-allocated as described in §3.7, freezing whatever has already been invoiced, aged or paid and pushing the whole delta into the still-`PENDING` rows. Finally the cascade fans out: the placement moves `PENDING_ACCEPTANCE → ACTIVE`, the candidate moves `OFFER_HELD → PLACED`, the requisition moves `OFFER_PENDING → PLACED`, and on the household both `placement_count` and `non_solicitation_expires_on` are recomputed, the latter becoming this `start_date` plus 730 days when this is the household's latest placement. Every row touched has its `version` incremented.

Separation is recorded by a `PlacementConsultant` or by the household as `Client`, supplying the `separation_date` and an optional `reason` that is validated but not persisted. The placement must be `ACTIVE`; a terminal placement is refused with `ERR_ENTITY_TERMINAL` and any other non-terminal state with `ERR_PLACEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409). The `separation_date` must not be earlier than the placement's `start_date` and must not be later than the logical-clock date, either violation being refused with `ERR_INVALID_SEPARATION_DATE` (HTTP 422). Recording it moves the placement `ACTIVE → TERMINATED_NO_REMEDY`, stamps `separation_date`, and moves the candidate `PLACED → COOLING_OFF` with `cooling_off_until` set to the logical-clock date plus 30 days. Termination on its own grants the household **no remedy whatsoever**: to obtain a replacement search or a refund the household must file a claim inside the guarantee window, as described in §3.8.

### 3.7. Milestone Billing, the Payroll Handoff and the Household Ledger

The placement fee is never billed as one invoice. Every placement carries exactly three `FeeMilestone` rows, created when the offer is extended, each identified by its `placement_id`, its `milestone_type` and its `sequence_no`: `sequence_no` 1 is the `ENGAGEMENT` milestone weighted at 3000 basis points, `sequence_no` 2 is `TRIAL_CLEARED` weighted at 3000, and `sequence_no` 3 is `START_DATE` weighted at 4000 — thirty, thirty and forty percent of the fee. Each row carries its own `weight_bps` copied from that table, an `amount_cents` set by the allocation rule below, an `invoice_status` beginning at `PENDING`, and null `invoiced_on`, `due_date`, `paid_on`, `payment_reference` and `invoiced_by_user_id`.

**The waterfall allocation** is the rule that keeps the three amounts summing to the fee under re-pricing, and it is run twice: once when the offer is created and once when the start is confirmed. Take the three rows in ascending `sequence_no` order and partition them. The **frozen** partition holds every row whose `invoice_status` is `INVOICED`, `OVERDUE`, `PAID` or `REVERSED`; the **open** partition holds every row whose `invoice_status` is `PENDING`; rows whose `invoice_status` is `VOIDED` are excluded from both partitions and take no part in the allocation at all. Compute the remaining amount as the placement's `fee_cents` less the sum of the `amount_cents` of the frozen rows. Compute the open weight total as the sum of `weight_bps` over the open rows. Then, walking the open rows in ascending `sequence_no` order, every open row **except the last** receives the remaining amount multiplied by that row's `weight_bps`, divided by the open weight total, rounded half up; the **last** open row receives the exact arithmetic remainder — the remaining amount less the sum of whatever has just been assigned in this pass. Frozen rows keep their stored `amount_cents` untouched. The invariant this guarantees, and which must hold after every run: the sum of `amount_cents` over all of the placement's `FeeMilestone` rows whose `invoice_status` is not `VOIDED` equals the placement's `fee_cents` exactly. It is the last open row absorbing the remainder, rather than a fourth rounding, that makes re-allocation exact.

Invoicing a milestone is exclusive to the `BillingAdministrator` and accepts an optional `note` that is not persisted. The row must be `PENDING`, and a `PAID`, `REVERSED` or `VOIDED` row is refused with `ERR_ENTITY_TERMINAL` while an `INVOICED` or `OVERDUE` row is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. The parent placement's `placement_status` must be `PENDING_ACCEPTANCE` or `ACTIVE` when the `milestone_type` is `ENGAGEMENT` — the engagement fee is billable as soon as the offer is out — and must be exactly `ACTIVE` when the `milestone_type` is `TRIAL_CLEARED` or `START_DATE`; a placement outside the admissible set is refused with `ERR_PLACEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409). Additionally, for a `START_DATE` milestone, a `PayrollHandoff` of that placement must have reached `status` `ATTESTED`, or the invoice is refused with `ERR_PAYROLL_HANDOFF_NOT_ATTESTED` (HTTP 409) — a cross-arm gate in which the compliance desk blocks the billing desk. Invoicing moves the row `PENDING → INVOICED`, stamps `invoiced_on` with the logical-clock date, `due_date` as `invoiced_on` plus 14 days — net two weeks — and `invoiced_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID`; appends a `LedgerEntry` of `entry_type` `FEE_INVOICED` with `signed_amount_cents` equal to positive `amount_cents` and `related_ref_type` `FEE_MILESTONE` pointing at the milestone through `related_ref_id`; recomputes the household's `outstanding_balance_cents`; and advances the placement's `billing_status` `UNBILLED → PARTIALLY_BILLED`, which is admissible when at least one of the placement's milestones is now `INVOICED`, `OVERDUE` or `PAID` and at least one is still `PENDING`.

Payment is recorded by the same role and must be exact. The request carries `paid_amount_cents` and a `payment_reference`; both are required, and the reference is accepted as any string at all, including an empty one, its content never being examined. The row must be `INVOICED` or `OVERDUE`, and any other non-terminal state is refused with `ERR_MILESTONE_NOT_INVOICED` (HTTP 409) while a terminal row is refused with `ERR_ENTITY_TERMINAL`. `paid_amount_cents` must equal the row's `amount_cents` exactly — there are no partial payments, a household settles a milestone in full or not at all — and any other figure is refused with `ERR_PAYMENT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). Recording payment moves the row `INVOICED → PAID` or `OVERDUE → PAID`, stamps `paid_on` with the logical-clock date and `payment_reference`, appends a `LedgerEntry` of `entry_type` `FEE_PAYMENT` with `signed_amount_cents` equal to the negative of `amount_cents`, recomputes `outstanding_balance_cents`, and advances the placement's `billing_status` `PARTIALLY_BILLED → FULLY_BILLED` once every milestone of the placement has an `invoice_status` in `INVOICED`, `OVERDUE`, `PAID`, `VOIDED` or `REVERSED` — that is, none remains `PENDING` — and at least one is `PAID`.

Reversal is available to the `BillingAdministrator` with an optional `reason` that is not persisted, and is also driven by the system on the placement-void cascade. The row must be `INVOICED` or `OVERDUE`, or the request is refused with `ERR_MILESTONE_NOT_INVOICED`; a terminal row is refused with `ERR_ENTITY_TERMINAL`. Reversal moves the row to `REVERSED`, appends a `LedgerEntry` of `entry_type` `FEE_REVERSAL` with `signed_amount_cents` equal to the negative of `amount_cents` and `related_ref_type` `FEE_MILESTONE`, and recomputes `outstanding_balance_cents`. Because `FEE_INVOICED` and `FEE_REVERSAL` carry identical magnitudes with opposite signs, invoicing a milestone and then reversing it returns the household's balance to its exact pre-invoice value — the compensation is precise, not approximate. The placement's `billing_status` moves `PARTIALLY_BILLED → REVERSED` once none of its milestones is `INVOICED`, `OVERDUE` or `PAID` and at least one is `REVERSED`. Every recomputation of `billing_status` is judged **only from the value the field currently holds**: `UNBILLED` may become `PARTIALLY_BILLED`, and `PARTIALLY_BILLED` may become `FULLY_BILLED` or `REVERSED`, and no other starting value moves at all. A placement that has reached `FULLY_BILLED` therefore stays there when one of its milestones is subsequently reversed, and only the refund path of §3.8 ever writes `REFUNDED`.

Two further milestone transitions are driven by the system rather than by the billing desk. Advancing the logical clock ages every `INVOICED` row whose `due_date` is **strictly before** the new clock date to `OVERDUE`; a clock landing exactly on `due_date` leaves the row `INVOICED`. And whenever a placement is voided, replaced or refunded, each of its `PENDING` rows moves to `VOIDED`; a voided row keeps whatever `amount_cents` it was last allocated but leaves the allocation base permanently, which is why the allocation invariant is stated over non-`VOIDED` rows only. `PAID`, `REVERSED` and `VOIDED` are all terminal.

The `START_DATE` milestone is gated on the compliance arm, and that arm has its own small workflow. A `ComplianceOfficer` prepares the handoff under the placement, naming the `provider_name` of the payroll bureau (required, 1 to 120 characters) and the `guidance_packet_version` of the household-employer guidance being issued (required, 1 to 32 characters), and optionally an opening `ein_status`, which may be any of the three values and defaults to `NOT_APPLIED`; the row is created with `status` `PREPARED` and null `attested_by_name` and `attested_at`. The placement's own state is not examined at all, so a handoff may be prepared under a placement in any state. Only one handoff may exist per placement, and a second attempt is refused with `ERR_PAYROLL_HANDOFF_EXISTS` (HTTP 409) whatever state the first one has reached. The same role advances the employer identification number by supplying a new `ein_status`, which changes only that field and leaves the handoff's `status` alone; the supplied value must be `APPLIED` or `ISSUED`, and a request naming `NOT_APPLIED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ENUM` because the operation only advances. No ordering is enforced between the two admissible values, so `ISSUED` may be walked back to `APPLIED`; an already-`ATTESTED` handoff is refused with `ERR_ENTITY_TERMINAL`. Attestation may be performed by the household as `Client` or by a `ComplianceOfficer`, supplying a required `attested_by_name` whose content is not otherwise constrained. The handoff must be `PREPARED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_ENTITY_TERMINAL`, and its `ein_status` must be exactly `ISSUED`, or it is refused with `ERR_EIN_NOT_ISSUED` (HTTP 409). Attestation moves the handoff `PREPARED → ATTESTED`, stamps `attested_by_name` and `attested_at` from the logical clock, increments `PayrollHandoff.version`, and is the single act that unblocks the `START_DATE` milestone for invoicing. `ATTESTED` is terminal.

Manual movements on the household account are the `BillingAdministrator`'s alone. The request carries an `entry_type`, a `signed_amount_cents` and an optional `memo` of 1 to 200 characters; `entry_type` is restricted to `MANUAL_ADJUSTMENT` here, because every other `entry_type` is produced only by the workflow transition that owns it. `signed_amount_cents` may be positive or negative but must not be zero, and a zero amount is refused with `ERR_INVALID_LEDGER_AMOUNT` (HTTP 400). The append creates a `LedgerEntry` with the next `sequence_no` for that household, `related_ref_type` `NONE` and a null `related_ref_id`, stamps `balance_after_cents` with the running total as of that row, recomputes the household's `outstanding_balance_cents` and increments `Client.version`. The resource returned in `data` is the created `LedgerEntry`, not the household.

### 3.8. The Guarantee Window: Claims and the Pro-rated Refund

The guarantee is the agency's core promise and its principal financial exposure. It runs from the placement's `start_date` inclusive up to but excluding `guarantee_expires_on`, and a claim may be filed by the household as `Client` or on its behalf by a `PlacementConsultant`. The request carries the `requested_remedy` — `REPLACEMENT` for a fresh search or `REFUND` for money back — the `separation_date`, a `narrative` explaining the separation (required, 1 to 2000 characters), and the mandatory `expected_placement_version`.

Filing gates in this order. A mismatched `expected_placement_version` is refused with `ERR_STALE_PLACEMENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412) before any business rule runs. The window must still be open: a logical-clock date on or after `guarantee_expires_on`, or a placement whose `placement_status` is already `GUARANTEE_EXPIRED`, is refused with `ERR_GUARANTEE_WINDOW_EXPIRED` (HTTP 409). Because the window is half-open, a claim filed on the day immediately before `guarantee_expires_on` is admissible and yields exactly one unused day, while a claim filed on `guarantee_expires_on` itself is refused. Those two guarantee tests are the **only** gate on the subject placement: its `placement_status` is not otherwise examined and terminality is not guarded here, so a placement already recorded as `TERMINATED_NO_REMEDY`, `VOIDED`, `TERMINATED_REPLACED` or `TERMINATED_REFUNDED` may still be claimed against while its window remains open — which is exactly how a household that recorded a plain separation obtains a remedy afterwards. The `separation_date` must not be earlier than the placement's `start_date` and must not be later than the logical-clock date, either violation being refused with `ERR_INVALID_SEPARATION_DATE` (HTTP 422). And only one claim may be open at a time: if a claim on this placement has `status` `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `INFO_REQUESTED`, the filing is refused with `ERR_OPEN_CLAIM_EXISTS` (HTTP 409). Re-filing after a refusal is expressly allowed and **supersedes** it: an existing `DENIED` claim on the same placement moves `DENIED → SUPERSEDED` with its `version` incremented. The new claim is created with `status` `SUBMITTED`, `info_request_round` 0, and `elapsed_days`, `unused_days`, `gross_refund_cents`, `retained_admin_fee_cents`, `net_refund_cents` and `decision_note` all null; `Placement.version` is incremented.

Adjudication of a claim is exclusive to the `AgencyPrincipal`, who supplies an `action` and an optional `decision_note`. An `action` of `OPEN_REVIEW` moves the claim `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW`, and stamps `decision_note` when one was supplied — as does every other action, an omitted note always leaving the stored value alone. An `action` of `REQUEST_INFO` moves it `UNDER_REVIEW → INFO_REQUESTED` and increments `info_request_round` by 1. Both of those actions report a state violation with the same identifier the deciding actions use: `OPEN_REVIEW` on a claim that is not `SUBMITTED`, and `REQUEST_INFO` on a claim that is not `UNDER_REVIEW`, are refused with `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_UNDER_REVIEW`, so every non-terminal state violation on this endpoint carries that one error whichever action was asked for. The household answers by supplying a required `info_note`, which is exclusive to the `Client` role and moves the claim `INFO_REQUESTED → UNDER_REVIEW`, appending the supplied note to `decision_note` after a newline separator — or writing it as the whole value when `decision_note` was null or empty; a claim in any other state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, and a terminal claim with `ERR_ENTITY_TERMINAL`. This loop is genuinely cyclic and has no bound: review may request information and be answered any number of times, each round incrementing `info_request_round`. Every one of the three deciding actions — `APPROVE_REPLACEMENT`, `APPROVE_REFUND` and `DENY` — requires the claim to be `UNDER_REVIEW`, and a claim in `SUBMITTED` or `INFO_REQUESTED` is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_UNDER_REVIEW` (HTTP 409) while a claim already in `APPROVED_REPLACEMENT`, `APPROVED_REFUND`, `DENIED` or `SUPERSEDED` is refused with `ERR_ENTITY_TERMINAL`. The two approvals must also match what was asked for: approving a replacement on a claim whose `requested_remedy` is `REFUND`, or approving a refund on a claim whose `requested_remedy` is `REPLACEMENT`, is refused with `ERR_REMEDY_MISMATCH` (HTTP 409).

`APPROVE_REPLACEMENT` grants a fresh search rather than money. The claim moves `UNDER_REVIEW → APPROVED_REPLACEMENT`; the placement moves to `TERMINATED_REPLACED`, which is admissible whether it was still `ACTIVE` or had already been recorded as `TERMINATED_NO_REMEDY`; every `FeeMilestone` of that placement whose `invoice_status` is `PENDING` moves to `VOIDED`, with the fee already collected carried as search credit and **no ledger movement of any kind**; the requisition moves `PLACED → SOURCING` so the search reopens without consuming a scoping round; and the candidate moves `PLACED → COOLING_OFF` if still `PLACED`. Every row touched has its `version` incremented.

`APPROVE_REFUND` grants money and runs a four-hop cascade. Hop one computes the proration and stamps `elapsed_days`, `unused_days`, `gross_refund_cents`, `retained_admin_fee_cents` and `net_refund_cents` on the claim. Hop two moves the placement to `TERMINATED_REFUNDED`, sets its `billing_status` to `REFUNDED` — admissible from either `PARTIALLY_BILLED` or `FULLY_BILLED`, and independent of where `placement_status` sits — and moves every `PENDING` `FeeMilestone` to `VOIDED`. Hop three appends a `LedgerEntry` of `entry_type` `REFUND_ISSUED` with `signed_amount_cents` equal to the negative of `net_refund_cents` and `related_ref_type` `REPLACEMENT_CLAIM`; the row is appended even when `net_refund_cents` computes to 0 — as it does on a zero-fee placement, or when the prorated gross rounds down to nothing — so a zero-amount `REFUND_ISSUED` row is still recorded rather than skipped. Hop four recomputes the household's `outstanding_balance_cents` from the extended ledger. The candidate moves `PLACED → COOLING_OFF` if still `PLACED`, and every row touched has its `version` incremented.

**The refund proration** has five steps, evaluated in exactly this order, each consuming the previous step's already-rounded output. Its inputs are the placement's `fee_cents`, `start_date` and `guarantee_window_days`, plus the claim's `separation_date`; milestone payment state is deliberately **not** an input, because the household is refunded against the contracted fee rather than against whatever the agency has managed to collect. Step one: `elapsed_days` is the whole number of days from the placement's `start_date` to the claim's `separation_date`. Step two: `unused_days` is the placement's `guarantee_window_days` less `elapsed_days`, floored at 0, so a separation after the window has run yields no refund base at all. Step three: `gross_refund_cents` is the placement's `fee_cents` multiplied by `unused_days`, divided by `guarantee_window_days`, rounded half up. Step four: the administrative retention rate is selected by tier **keyed on `gross_refund_cents`** — not on `fee_cents` and not on the amount invoiced — at 500 basis points when `gross_refund_cents` is below 1,000,000, at 750 basis points when it is from 1,000,000 up to but excluding 3,000,000, and at 1000 basis points when it is 3,000,000 or more; `retained_admin_fee_cents` is `gross_refund_cents` multiplied by that rate, divided by 10000, rounded half up. Step five: `net_refund_cents` is `gross_refund_cents` less `retained_admin_fee_cents`, an exact subtraction with no rounding. Three plausible rearrangements all produce the wrong answer and are expressly forbidden: keying the retention tier on `fee_cents` instead of on the prorated gross, deducting the administrative fee from the full fee before prorating, and truncating instead of rounding half up at step three.

Two other paths close the guarantee. A `DENY` moves the claim `UNDER_REVIEW → DENIED` and leaves the placement's `placement_status` and `billing_status` exactly as they were — a denial changes nothing except the claim itself and the household's right to re-file. And the ops sweep moves any `ACTIVE` placement whose `guarantee_expires_on` is on or before the new logical-clock date to `GUARANTEE_EXPIRED`, after which no claim may be filed at all.

### 3.9. Non-solicitation Enforcement, the Penalty and Reinstatement

Every confirmed placement puts the household under a non-solicitation term running 730 days — two years — from the `start_date` of its most recent non-voided placement, recorded as the household's `non_solicitation_expires_on`. A `ComplianceOfficer` alleges a breach by filing a case naming the `respondent_client_id`, the `candidate_id` said to have been solicited, an `allegation_note` of 1 to 2000 characters, the `discovered_on` date, and optionally the `source_placement_id` of the placement the candidate came from. If `source_placement_id` is supplied and names a placement whose `candidate_id` differs from the case's own `candidate_id`, the filing is refused with `ERR_ENTITY_RELATIONSHIP_MISMATCH` (HTTP 409). The case is created with `status` `ALLEGED`, `prior_substantiated_count` 0, null `penalty_cents`, `settlement_amount_cents`, `investigation_opened_at`, `substantiated_at`, `resolved_by_user_id` and `findings_note`, and `opened_by_user_id` stamped from `X-User-ID`.

Opening an investigation is also a `ComplianceOfficer` act and accepts an optional `note` that is not persisted. The case must be `ALLEGED`, and any other non-terminal state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` while `UNSUBSTANTIATED` or `SETTLED` is refused with `ERR_ENTITY_TERMINAL`. The respondent household's term must actually cover the conduct: its `non_solicitation_expires_on` must not be null and the case's `discovered_on` must not be later than that date, or the investigation is refused with `ERR_NON_SOLICITATION_TERM_EXPIRED` (HTTP 422). Conduct discovered on the very last covered day is still actionable. Opening the investigation moves the case `ALLEGED → INVESTIGATING` and stamps `investigation_opened_at` from the logical clock.

Resolution is separated from filing by duty: it is performed by a `ComplianceOfficer` whose `X-User-ID` must differ from the case's `opened_by_user_id`, or the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). The case must be `INVESTIGATING`, and any other non-terminal state is refused with `ERR_CASE_NOT_INVESTIGATING` (HTTP 409). The resolver supplies a `disposition` of `SUBSTANTIATE` or `UNSUBSTANTIATE` and an optional `findings_note`.

`UNSUBSTANTIATE` is the quiet branch: the case moves `INVESTIGATING → UNSUBSTANTIATED`, `resolved_by_user_id` is stamped and `findings_note` with it when one was supplied, `penalty_cents` stays null, and no ledger row is appended and no household state changes at all.

`SUBSTANTIATE` is a five-hop cascade. Hop one freezes `prior_substantiated_count` and computes the penalty, stamping `penalty_cents`, `substantiated_at` from the logical clock and `resolved_by_user_id`. `prior_substantiated_count` is the number of **other** cases against the same `respondent_client_id` whose `status` is `SUBSTANTIATED` or `SETTLED` and whose `substantiated_at` is strictly before this case's `investigation_opened_at`; it is frozen at the moment of resolution and never recomputed thereafter, so a later case cannot retroactively change an earlier penalty. Hop two appends a `LedgerEntry` of `entry_type` `PENALTY_ASSESSED` with `signed_amount_cents` equal to positive `penalty_cents` and `related_ref_type` `NON_SOLICITATION_CASE`. Hop three recomputes the household's `outstanding_balance_cents`. Hop four moves the household `ONBOARDED → SUSPENDED`, which by itself blocks new searches, new scoping rounds, scope finalisation, new trial bookings and new offers. Hop five cancels the consequences of the suspension: every `SCHEDULED` `TrialEngagement` of that household moves to `CANCELLED` with `cancel_reason` `CLIENT_SUSPENDED`, and every `TRIALING` `RoleRequisition` of that household whose recomputed `active_trial_count` reaches 0, and which has no placement sitting in `PENDING_ACCEPTANCE`, falls back to `SOURCING`. Every row touched has its `version` incremented.

**The penalty** is computed in five ordered steps. Step one: the base amount is the `fee_cents` of the placement named by `source_placement_id` when that field is set, and otherwise the penalty floor of 750,000 cents — $7,500. Step two: the rate is selected by a deliberately **regressive** tier keyed on that base — 10000 basis points, a full 100 percent, when the base is below 2,000,000; 7500 basis points when the base is from 2,000,000 up to but excluding 5,000,000; and 5000 basis points when the base is 5,000,000 or more. Step three: the rated amount is the base multiplied by that rate, divided by 10000, rounded half up. Step four: the rated amount is multiplied by one plus `prior_substantiated_count`, so a first offence is taken once, a first repeat twice, a second repeat three times; this multiplication is exact with no rounding. Step five: the result is clamped, first raised to the floor of 750,000 cents and then trimmed to the cap of 7,500,000 cents — $75,000 — and stored as `penalty_cents`. The order is load-bearing in two ways: the tier is keyed on the pre-multiplier base rather than on the multiplied figure, and the floor and cap are applied only **after** the repeat-offender multiplier, never before it.

Reinstatement is the `BillingAdministrator`'s act. Settling a case supplies the `settlement_amount_cents` and an optional `memo` of 1 to 200 characters, which is carried onto the ledger row the settlement appends and is null there when the caller omitted it. The case must be `SUBSTANTIATED`, and any other non-terminal state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` while `UNSUBSTANTIATED` or `SETTLED` is refused with `ERR_ENTITY_TERMINAL`. A negative `settlement_amount_cents` is caught by the numeric range check and refused with `ERR_INVALID_RANGE` (HTTP 400), and a non-negative amount greater than the case's `penalty_cents` is refused with `ERR_SETTLEMENT_EXCEEDS_PENALTY` (HTTP 422) — the household may settle for less than the assessed penalty, or for exactly it, but never for more. Settlement moves the case `SUBSTANTIATED → SETTLED`, stamps `settlement_amount_cents`, appends a `LedgerEntry` of `entry_type` `PENALTY_SETTLED` with `signed_amount_cents` equal to the negative of `settlement_amount_cents`, recomputes the household's `outstanding_balance_cents`, and moves the household `SUSPENDED → ONBOARDED` — but only once no other case against that household remains in `SUBSTANTIATED`. A household with two substantiated cases must settle both before it is readmitted to placement activity. `UNSUBSTANTIATED` and `SETTLED` are the two terminal states of the enforcement machine.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

All paths are given in full and are served at the top level of the API — there is no versioning or naming prefix. Endpoints that create a new resource return HTTP 201 with the created resource in `data`; endpoints that modify an existing resource, including every action endpoint that drives a state machine, return HTTP 200 with the updated resource in `data`; read endpoints return HTTP 200. The request bodies, query parameters, side effects and refusal conditions of each endpoint are specified in §2 and §3; the table below fixes the surface and the role permissions. A role absent from an endpoint's authorized list receives `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403) even when its request would otherwise be valid. A path this table does not serve is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` at HTTP 404, and a served path addressed with a method it does not offer is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` at HTTP 405; neither refusal consults the authentication headers, and a trailing slash is ignored when matching a path.
