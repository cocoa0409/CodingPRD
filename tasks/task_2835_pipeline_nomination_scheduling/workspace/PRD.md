# Refined Products Pipeline Nomination & Scheduling API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A refined products pipeline is a shared highway. Gasoline, jet fuel and ultra-low-sulfur diesel from a dozen competing marketers travel down the same steel tube, one batch behind another, and the carrier that owns the line is a **common carrier**: it may not favour one shipper over another, it must publish its rates, and when demand for a shipping cycle exceeds what the line can physically move it must cut everybody back by a filed, non-discriminatory formula rather than by preference. This service is the system of record for that obligation. It collects shipper nominations cycle by cycle, prorates them against the capacity the line can actually offer, sequences the surviving volumes into batches whose grade-to-grade interfaces generate a predictable transmix cut, follows each batch through injection and delivery, and finally settles the volumetric imbalance and bills the tariff.

The operational pain the API removes is the gap between the commercial plan and the physical line. Capacity is not a constant: an integrity dig, a pigging run or a hydrotest derates the segment, sometimes to nothing, and a schedule that was published last week must be torn back down and re-prorated with every affected shipper notified. Product is not fungible: a barrel of diesel injected behind a batch of jet fuel contaminates the interface, and the slop cut that results has to be carved out of nominatable capacity before a single barrel is awarded. Measurement is not exact: what is injected at the origin terminal never arrives whole at the destination, and the shrinkage has to be allocated across shippers in proportion to what they actually took. Every one of those facts changes somebody's allocation, somebody's batch order, or somebody's invoice, and each change has to propagate deterministically rather than by an operator's memory.

Accordingly, this document specifies a strictly deterministic service. Every rule below — every threshold, every rounding decision, every ordering of checks — is a contract. All resources are mounted under the `/pipeline` prefix, with a single test-driving endpoint outside it. Time is never read from the host: the service keeps a **logical clock** that the test harness controls, and every window, notice period and lookback rule reads that clock. Where two plausible readings of a business rule exist, this document names the one the service implements.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the service must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that principal is acting in. If either header is missing or empty, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the four role names declared below, the request is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). Once the headers are accepted, the acting role must appear in the authorized-role list of the endpoint being called; a role that is not listed there is refused with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403). Role authorization is decided before any path identifier is resolved, so an unauthorized caller learns nothing about whether the resource it named exists.

There are exactly four roles, spelled here as they must appear in `X-User-Role`:

- **`Shipper`** — a common carrier customer that nominates volume into a cycle, revises that nomination when the carrier asks, withdraws it, and reads its own nominations, batch tickets and settlements alongside the public reference data (cycles, segments, product grades, tariffs and maintenance notices).
- **`PipelineScheduler`** — the carrier's scheduling desk. It opens and closes cycles, prorates capacity, builds and publishes the batch sequence, reschedules injection windows, plans and progresses maintenance, sends nominations back for revision, settles cycles and certifies the resulting settlements.
- **`TerminalOperator`** — the terminal-side operator. It books physical inventory adjustments, funds linefill, and executes injections and deliveries with the measured quality and measured volumes taken at the manifold.
- **`TariffAdministrator`** — the commercial administrator. It maintains master data (terminals, product grades, segments, shippers, historical movement records), sets shipper credit, drafts and activates tariffs, changes a segment's proration methodology, and invoices certified settlements.

The meaning of `X-User-ID` depends on the role. When `X-User-Role` is `Shipper`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Shipper` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Shipper` compare that resource's shipper foreign key to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a `Shipper` may act on or read a nomination only when the nomination's `shipper_id` equals `X-User-ID`; may read a batch ticket only when the ticket's nomination belongs to that shipper; may read a settlement only when the settlement's `shipper_id` equals `X-User-ID`; and, when submitting a nomination, must supply a body `shipper_id` equal to `X-User-ID`. Any of those mismatches is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403).

When `X-User-Role` is `PipelineScheduler`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where the specification says so — as `schedule_published_by_user_id` on a cycle whose schedule that scheduler published, and as `certified_by_user_id` on a settlement that scheduler certified — but it never compares it to any entity's `id`. The same holds for `TerminalOperator` and for `TariffAdministrator`: for both, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity, and while the server may record it for audit it does not compare it to any entity's `id`. Those recorded identifiers are what the segregation-of-duties rule in §2.10 compares against each other.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection endpoint accepts two optional query-string parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, may be no smaller than 1 and no larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may be no smaller than 0. A `limit` or `offset` outside those bounds, or one that is not an integer, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

List responses carry `response_type` of `"array"` and populate four pagination fields inside `meta`: `limit` (the effective page size), `offset` (the effective starting position), `total_count` (the number of rows matching the filters before the page window is applied) and `has_more` (true when rows remain beyond the current page). Single-resource reads carry `response_type` of `"object"` and do not populate those four fields.

Unless a collection declares a different default, rows are returned sorted by `created_at` ascending. The declared exceptions are: terminals sort by `terminal_code` ascending; product grades sort by `sequence_rank` ascending; segments sort by `segment_code` ascending; shippers sort by `shipper_code` ascending; historical movement records sort by `cycle_end_at` ascending and then by `created_at` ascending; tariffs sort by `effective_from` descending and then by `created_at` descending; nomination cycles sort by `window_open_at` ascending and then by `created_at` ascending; nominations sort by `created_at` ascending; batch tickets sort by `sequence_position` ascending and then by `created_at` ascending; maintenance events sort by `window_start` ascending and then by `created_at` ascending; and settlements sort by the billed shipper's `created_at` ascending and then by the settlement's own `created_at` ascending.

Each collection also accepts a set of equality filters, which narrow the result set before sorting and paging. Terminals may be filtered by `role_kind`. Product grades may be filtered by `is_transmix_grade`. Segments may be filtered by `origin_terminal_id`, `destination_terminal_id`, `linefill_grade_id` or `operational_status`. Shippers may be filtered by `status`. Historical movement records may be filtered by `shipper_id`, `segment_id` or `record_status`. Tariffs may be filtered by `segment_id`, `grade_id` or `status`. Cycles may be filtered by `segment_id` or `status`. Nominations may be filtered by `cycle_id`, `shipper_id`, `grade_id`, `origin_terminal_id`, `destination_terminal_id`, `supersedes_nomination_id`, `pinned_tariff_id` or `status`. Batch tickets may be filtered by `cycle_id`, `nomination_id`, `predecessor_batch_id`, `movement_status` or `quality_status`. Maintenance events may be filtered by `segment_id` or `status`. Settlements may be filtered by `cycle_id`, `shipper_id` or `status`.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and the Logical Clock

Every resource carries a server-minted `id`: a UUID string with no type prefix of any kind. Clients never supply an `id`; a client-supplied `id` in a request body is ignored. Every resource also carries `created_at`, the ISO 8601 UTC instant at which the row was created, rendered with exactly three fractional-second digits and a trailing `Z`. Every instant the service stores or returns uses that same rendering, and every instant a client supplies must parse as ISO 8601 UTC or the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, refreshed on every mutation of the row, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly one on every mutation of that row. The one exception is the historical movement record: it is an immutable audit row, it has no update operation, and it carries only `id` and `created_at` — both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from its representation. Terminals, product grades, segments, shippers, tariffs, nomination cycles, nominations, batch tickets, maintenance events and settlements all carry the full set.

The service reads time exclusively from a **logical clock**. Before any call in a session the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to the host's wall-clock time. A test-driving endpoint, available to all four roles, sets the clock to the instant supplied as `current_utc`; that call mutates no entity. Every rule in this document that speaks of "now" — the nomination window test, the 48-hour maintenance notice test, the one-year shipping-history lookback, the instants recorded in `schedule_published_at`, `notified_at` and `settled_at` — reads the logical clock at the moment the request is processed.

### 2.5. The Order in Which Requests Are Refused

More than one rule can be violated by the same request, and the service must always report the *first* violated rule in a fixed order of evaluation. The ladder below is exhaustive and is evaluated from top to bottom; the first rank whose condition fails determines both the `error_id` and the HTTP status, and no later rank is consulted.

1. **Authentication.** Both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` present and non-empty, and `X-User-Role` one of the four declared roles — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Role authorization.** The acting role appears in the called endpoint's authorized-role list — otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Resource ownership.** When the acting role is `Shipper`, `X-User-ID` matches the owning shipper of the target resource — otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). This rank guards exactly six operations: nomination submission (against the body `shipper_id`), nomination withdrawal, nomination resubmission, the single-nomination read, the single-batch-ticket read (through the ticket's nomination) and the single-settlement read.
4. **Path existence.** Every identifier embedded in the URL path is resolved left to right, before any identifier in the request body is looked at. A missing nomination cycle yields `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND` (404) on the single-cycle read and on every cycle action; a missing nomination yields `ERR_NOMINATION_NOT_FOUND` (404) on the single-nomination read and every nomination action; a missing batch ticket yields `ERR_BATCH_TICKET_NOT_FOUND` (404) on the single-ticket read and every ticket action; a missing maintenance event yields `ERR_MAINTENANCE_EVENT_NOT_FOUND` (404) on the single-event read and every event action; a missing settlement yields `ERR_SETTLEMENT_NOT_FOUND` (404) on the single-settlement read and every settlement action.
5. **Body reference resolution.** Every foreign key supplied in a request body resolves to an existing row — `shipper_id`, `grade_id`, `segment_id`, `origin_terminal_id`, `destination_terminal_id`, `linefill_grade_id`, `cycle_id`, `nomination_id`, `supersedes_nomination_id`, `tariff_id` and `terminal_id` alike. An unresolvable reference yields `ERR_REFERENCE_NOT_FOUND` (404). This rank shares priority with path existence and applies to every creating endpoint that accepts a foreign key in its body.
6. **Request shape.** Required body fields present; every enum value a member of its declared set; volumes and rates strictly greater than zero where positivity is required; `window_end` after `window_start`; `effective_to`, when supplied, after `effective_from`; `sequence_rank` between 1 and 9 inclusive; `capacity_derate_pct` inside the range 0 through 1 inclusive; and every supplied instant parsing as ISO 8601 UTC. Any failure yields `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Applies to every endpoint.
7. **From-state guards.** A lifecycle action must be called from a state the transition allows, and each family reports its own code. For cycles the required from-states are: closing requires `OPEN`, prorating requires `CLOSED` or `RESEQUENCING`, building a schedule requires `PRORATED`, publishing requires `SCHEDULED`, and settling requires `PUBLISHED`; a violation reports `ERR_CYCLE_INVALID_STATE` (409), nomination actions `ERR_NOMINATION_INVALID_STATE` (409), batch-ticket actions `ERR_TICKET_INVALID_STATE` (409), maintenance actions `ERR_MAINTENANCE_INVALID_STATE` (409), settlement actions `ERR_SETTLEMENT_INVALID_STATE` (409) and tariff activation `ERR_TARIFF_INVALID_STATE` (409). Nomination submission has its own code at this rank, `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_OPEN` (409), because the state being guarded is the cycle's rather than the nomination's.
8. **Stale-version guards.** The optimistic-concurrency fields described in §2.6, yielding `ERR_STALE_CYCLE_VERSION` (412) or `ERR_STALE_TERMINAL_VERSION` (412).
9. **Route serviceability.** The nominated origin and destination match the cycle's segment endpoints — otherwise `ERR_ROUTE_NOT_SERVED` (422).
10. **Tariff resolvability at close.** Every submitted nomination in a closing cycle resolves an effective tariff — otherwise `ERR_NO_EFFECTIVE_TARIFF` (422).
11. **Temporal windows.** The nomination window test (`ERR_NOMINATION_WINDOW_CLOSED`, 422), the window-ordering tests (`ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`, 422) and the maintenance notice period (`ERR_INSUFFICIENT_NOTICE`, 422).
12. **Safety and duty separation.** Product quality at the injection point (`ERR_QUALITY_SPEC_VIOLATION`, 422) and the segregation-of-duties test on certification and invoicing (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, 403). Product quality is deliberately enforced ahead of every commercial check.
13. **Operational constraints.** Maintenance window conflicts (`ERR_MAINTENANCE_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, 409), origin-terminal bay conflicts (`ERR_TERMINAL_BAY_CONFLICT`, 409), an unavailable segment (`ERR_SEGMENT_UNAVAILABLE`, 409), a locked nomination (`ERR_NOMINATION_LOCKED`, 409) and batches still in transit at settlement (`ERR_BATCHES_IN_TRANSIT`, 409).
14. **Shipper standing.** The acting or referenced shipper is in good standing — otherwise `ERR_SHIPPER_NOT_ACTIVE` (409).
15. **Tender minimum.** The nominated volume clears the applicable tender minimum — otherwise `ERR_TENDER_MINIMUM_NOT_MET` (422).
16. **Revision discipline.** A resubmission does not grow — otherwise `ERR_REVISION_VOLUME_INCREASE` (422).
17. **Transmix reserve.** The schedule's total interface transmix fits the reserve — otherwise `ERR_TRANSMIX_RESERVE_EXCEEDED` (422).
18. **Physical inventory limits.** The origin terminal keeps its operating floor (`ERR_INSUFFICIENT_ORIGIN_INVENTORY`, 422), the destination terminal keeps under its shell (`ERR_DESTINATION_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, 422), and the segment holds its linefill before a schedule is published (`ERR_LINEFILL_REQUIREMENT_NOT_MET`, 422).
19. **Fiscal capacity.** The shipper's credit limit — `ERR_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (402). Fiscal checks run last, after product quality and after every physical check.

Two consequences of this ladder are worth stating outright. First, because the cycle-status guard on injection sits at rank 7 in the *cycle* family, an injection attempted before its cycle has been published reports `ERR_CYCLE_INVALID_STATE` and never `ERR_TICKET_INVALID_STATE`, even though the endpoint acts on a ticket. Second, because quality precedes credit, a shipper over its credit limit presenting off-spec product hears about the specification failure, not the money.

### 2.6. Stale-View Guards on Repeatable Writes

Two write operations are guarded by optimistic concurrency control against a *parent* row's `version`. In both cases the guard exists because nothing in the target's own state would reject the write on its own, so a client working from an out-of-date read would otherwise destroy work it had never seen.

The first guard is on nomination submission. `POST /pipeline/nominations` requires a non-optional body field `expected_cycle_version` carrying the `version` of the target `NominationCycle` as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the cycle's current `version`, the submission is refused with `ERR_STALE_CYCLE_VERSION` and HTTP 412. The reason is that a new nomination for the same shipper, grade, origin and destination legitimately retires the shipper's earlier row for that combination; a submission carrying a stale cycle version would silently supersede a nomination the client never saw. A successful submission increments the cycle's `version` by one, so a client that submits twice must re-read the cycle between attempts.

The second guard is on terminal inventory adjustments. `POST /pipeline/terminals/{id}/inventory-adjustments` requires a non-optional body field `expected_terminal_version` carrying the `version` of the target `Terminal` as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the terminal's current `version`, the adjustment is refused with `ERR_STALE_TERMINAL_VERSION` and HTTP 412. Adjustments are legitimately repeatable — booking the same gauge correction twice is not intrinsically invalid — so the stale version is the *only* signal available that the operator's view of `inventory_bbl` predates an injection, a delivery, or a previous adjustment. A successful adjustment increments the terminal's `version` by one.

No other endpoint takes a version field. Cycle lifecycle actions, nomination withdrawal and resubmission, revision requests, injections, deliveries, cancellations, window reschedules, maintenance transitions, tariff activation, certification and invoicing all rely on their own from-state guards instead, and a client must not send `expected_cycle_version` or `expected_terminal_version` to them.

### 2.7. Volumetric Conservation Across the Terminal Book

Barrels are conserved. For any segment, the quantity the service treats as the system's total book volume is the sum of five accumulators: the origin terminal's `inventory_bbl`, the destination terminal's `inventory_bbl`, the segment's `in_transit_bbl`, the segment's `transmix_inventory_bbl` and the segment's `system_loss_bbl`. That total is asserted after every injection, every delivery and every batch cancellation, always at two-decimal precision. The only operation in the entire API permitted to change it is a terminal inventory adjustment — everything else merely moves volume between the five accumulators.

The sharpest consequence concerns compensation. An injection followed by a cancellation of the same batch ticket must leave the five-accumulator total bit-identical to its value before the injection: the cancellation returns the injected barrels *plus* the interface transmix cut to the origin terminal's `inventory_bbl`, and unwinds `in_transit_bbl` and `transmix_inventory_bbl` by exactly the amounts the injection added. Nothing is rounded on the way back, and nothing is booked to `system_loss_bbl` by a cancellation.

### 2.8. Capacity Conservation Within a Cycle

A parallel identity governs allocation rather than volume: for every nomination cycle, `allocated_total_bbl` plus `unallocated_capacity_bbl` must equal `effective_capacity_bbl` exactly, in whole barrels, with zero drift. That identity is re-asserted after every close, every proration, every schedule publication, every withdrawal, every revision request and every maintenance derate cascade.

Interface transmix never consumes this identity, because the volume set aside to absorb transmix is carved out of nominatable capacity up front, inside the effective-capacity computation described in §3.4. When a withdrawal or a revision request releases allocation, the released barrels land in `unallocated_capacity_bbl`, and after whatever re-proration that release triggers the identity must hold again exactly.

### 2.9. Measurement, Money and Rounding

Wherever this document says an amount is rounded, the method is **round half up** — a value exactly on the midpoint rounds away from zero. Rounding scales differ by quantity and are not interchangeable:

- Monetary amounts are carried to **2 decimal places**. That covers `credit_limit_usd`, `exposure_usd`, `tariff_charge_usd`, `alt_basis_charge_usd`, `basis_delta_usd`, `imbalance_charge_usd` and `total_billed_usd`.
- Tariff rates are carried to **3 decimal places**: `base_rate_usd_per_bbl`, `tier1_rate_usd_per_bbl` and `tier2_rate_usd_per_bbl`. The imbalance penalty rate, `imbalance_penalty_rate_usd_per_bbl`, must be zero or greater.
- Allocations and capacities are **whole barrels**. `effective_capacity_bbl`, `allocated_bbl`, `allocated_total_bbl`, `unallocated_capacity_bbl` and `transmix_bbl` are integers, and every fractional intermediate that produces them is truncated downward to the next lower whole barrel rather than rounded.
- Two ratios are carried to **6 decimal places**: a cycle's `proration_factor` and a settlement's `imbalance_pct`.
- A segment's `derate_pct` and a maintenance event's `capacity_derate_pct` are carried to **2 decimal places**.
- Measured and historical volumes — `injected_bbl`, `delivered_bbl`, `actual_shipped_bbl`, `history_base_bbl`, `imbalance_bbl`, `loss_allocation_bbl`, `scheduled_bbl`, `inventory_bbl`, `linefill_bbl`, `in_transit_bbl`, `transmix_inventory_bbl` and `system_loss_bbl` — are carried to **2 decimal places**.

One rounding rule is load-bearing enough to state separately: where a charge is built from several batch lines, each line is rounded to two decimal places *first* and the rounded lines are then summed exactly. The service never sums unrounded lines and rounds the total.

### 2.10. Common Carrier Obligations and Segregation of Duties

The carrier's non-discrimination obligation shapes three rules that recur throughout §3. Closing a nomination window never discriminates between shippers: the only thing that can reject a nomination at close is the tender minimum on the tariff pinned to it. Proration allocates by a filed formula and by nothing else, and a shipper with no shipping history still receives a share out of a reserved pool rather than nothing. And a request for revision is an invitation, not an obligation — a scheduler may re-prorate over nominations whose shippers never responded, using their original nominated volumes as the weight, precisely so that silence does not cost a shipper its allocation.

Duty separation is enforced on the settlement chain. A settlement may be certified only by a `PipelineScheduler` whose `X-User-ID` differs from the `schedule_published_by_user_id` recorded on the settled cycle; a certification attempt by the very operator who published that cycle's schedule is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). The same code and status guard invoicing: the `TariffAdministrator` whose `X-User-ID` appears in the settlement's `certified_by_user_id` may not invoice it.

### 2.11. Cascade Semantics and Version Propagation

Many operations in this domain touch more than one row. When they do, every row they touch has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by one, including rows the caller never named — the cycle whose totals a withdrawal recomputes, the segment whose derate a maintenance notice moves, the nominations a resequencing pushes back for revision, the batch tickets a resequencing cancels. Cascades are applied immediately and synchronously within the request that triggers them; there is no deferred or eventual settling of derived fields, and a read issued immediately after a mutating call sees every consequence of it.

Cascades in this system are deep. The maintenance notification cascade is five hops long: the event records its notification, the segment's derate and operational status are recomputed, affected published cycles recompute their effective capacity and may fall back into resequencing, over-allocated nominations are pushed to revision at a reduced allocation, and those nominations' planned batch tickets are cancelled. The second quality rejection of a batch is four hops long: the ticket's retest counter advances, the ticket is cancelled, its nomination is pushed to revision with its allocation released, and the cycle's allocation totals are recomputed. Each hop is specified in the workflow subsection where it belongs, and an implementation that stops at the first hop will leave the conservation identities of §2.7 and §2.8 broken.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up the Physical and Commercial Network

Before a single barrel can be nominated, a `TariffAdministrator` must describe the physical system. Registering a tank terminal takes a `terminal_code` that must be unique across all terminals, a `name`, a `role_kind` of `ORIGIN`, `DESTINATION` or `BOTH` declaring whether the terminal may originate movements, receive them, or do both, a `shell_capacity_bbl` strictly greater than zero giving total tankage, and a `min_operating_inventory_bbl` of zero or more giving the floor that must remain in tank after any injection debit. An optional `injection_bays` states how many batches may occupy overlapping injection windows at that terminal; it must be at least 1 and defaults to 1 when omitted. A newly registered terminal starts with `inventory_bbl` of 0 and `version` of 1.

Book inventory moves only through a `TerminalOperator`'s inventory adjustment, which is the one operation in the system allowed to change total book volume. The adjustment names an `adjustment_kind`, an `adjustment_bbl` delta and a free-text `reason`, and it carries the terminal stale-view guard described in §2.6. An `adjustment_kind` of `INVENTORY` adds `adjustment_bbl` to the terminal's `inventory_bbl`, and a negative `adjustment_bbl` therefore withdraws volume. An `adjustment_kind` of `LINEFILL` behaves differently: it requires an accompanying `segment_id`, it adds `adjustment_bbl` to that segment's `linefill_bbl`, and it leaves the terminal's `inventory_bbl` untouched. Either way the terminal's `version` increments by one, and the response is the updated terminal.

Product grades are the vocabulary of batch sequencing. Each grade carries a unique `grade_code` — `JET`, `ULSD`, `CBOB` and `TRANSMIX` are typical — a human-readable `name`, and a `sequence_rank` between 1 and 9 inclusive where rank 1 is the cleanest, lightest product and higher ranks are progressively dirtier. Two specification ceilings, `max_sulfur_ppm` and `max_rvp_psi`, must both be strictly greater than zero and are the numbers a shipper's product is measured against at the injection point. A boolean `is_transmix_grade`, defaulting to false, marks a grade as the interface slop cut rather than a shippable product.

A pipeline segment ties two terminals together with the commercial parameters that govern a cycle on it. Its `segment_code` must be unique. Its `origin_terminal_id` must name a terminal whose `role_kind` is `ORIGIN` or `BOTH`, and its `destination_terminal_id` must name a terminal whose `role_kind` is `DESTINATION` or `BOTH`. Its `linefill_grade_id` names the grade currently standing as linefill in the line, which matters because the first batch of every published schedule interfaces against that grade rather than against a predecessor batch. Four volumetric parameters follow: `capacity_bbl_per_cycle`, the nameplate throughput for one nomination cycle, strictly greater than zero; `transmix_reserve_bbl`, zero or more, the volume held back from nominatable capacity to absorb interface transmix; `transmix_cut_bbl`, zero or more, the slop generated per step of sequencing-rank gap at a grade interface; and `min_tender_bbl`, strictly greater than zero, the smallest volume the segment will physically batch. A `linefill_requirement_bbl` of zero or more states how much linefill the segment must hold before a schedule may be published, while `linefill_bbl` of zero or more records how much it actually holds. Finally, `proration_policy` selects the allocation methodology and must be either `HISTORY_WEIGHTED` or `VOLUME_WEIGHTED`. A new segment is `OPERATIONAL` in its `operational_status` with `derate_pct` of 0, and its `linefill_bbl`, `in_transit_bbl`, `transmix_inventory_bbl` and `system_loss_bbl` all start at 0.

Changing methodology is a filing, not a retroactive correction. A `TariffAdministrator` may replace a segment's `proration_policy` at any time; the new value governs every subsequent proration run and never rewrites the allocations of a cycle that has already been prorated.

Shippers are enrolled with a `shipper_code` that must be unique across all shippers and between 3 and 12 characters long, a legal `name`, and a `credit_limit_usd` of zero or more carried to two decimal places, expressing the maximum unsettled tariff exposure the carrier will extend. A new shipper's `status` is `ACTIVE`, its `suspended_reason` is null and its `exposure_usd` is 0.00. Suspension is a single administrative act that records a `suspended_reason` and moves `status` to `SUSPENDED`; the only two values `status` may take are `ACTIVE` and `SUSPENDED`. A suspended shipper may neither nominate, nor resubmit a revised nomination, nor have a batch injected on its behalf — all three are refused with `ERR_SHIPPER_NOT_ACTIVE`.

Historical movement records are the raw material of history-weighted proration, and they are deliberately immutable: there is no update operation for them and, as noted in §2.4, they carry neither `updated_at` nor `version`. Each row names the `shipper_id` whose movement it records, the `segment_id` the movement ran on, the `cycle_code` of the historical cycle it summarises, an `actual_shipped_bbl` of zero or more giving the barrels actually delivered for that shipper in that cycle, a `cycle_end_at` instant at which the historical cycle's shipping window ended, and a `record_status` of either `FINAL` or `PROVISIONAL`. Only `FINAL` rows ever enter the history base; a `PROVISIONAL` row is ignored by proration even when its `cycle_end_at` falls squarely inside the lookback window.

### 3.2. The Tariff Catalogue and How a Rate Is Pinned

A tariff prices one segment and grade pair. Drafting one takes the `segment_id` and `grade_id` it prices, a three-tier rate structure, a tender minimum, an imbalance penalty rate and an effectivity interval. The rate structure is a `base_rate_usd_per_bbl` that applies while billable volume is below `tier1_min_bbl`; a `tier1_rate_usd_per_bbl` that replaces it once billable volume reaches `tier1_min_bbl` and while it remains below `tier2_min_bbl`; and a `tier2_rate_usd_per_bbl` that applies once billable volume reaches `tier2_min_bbl`. Both breakpoints are inclusive, all three rates must be strictly greater than zero and are carried to three decimal places, `tier1_min_bbl` must be strictly greater than zero and `tier2_min_bbl` must be strictly greater than `tier1_min_bbl`. The `tender_minimum_bbl`, strictly greater than zero, is the common carrier tender minimum: nominations that fall below it are rejected when the cycle closes, and billing is floored at it. The `imbalance_penalty_rate_usd_per_bbl`, zero or more, is the per-barrel rate that a settlement's tier multiplier scales. The interval runs from `effective_from` inclusive to `effective_to` exclusive, where `effective_to` may be omitted or null to mean open-ended, and when supplied must be strictly later than `effective_from`.

A tariff's `status` may be `DRAFT`, `EFFECTIVE` or `SUPERSEDED`. It begins as `DRAFT`, and a `DRAFT` tariff is never a candidate for pinning. Activation by a `TariffAdministrator` moves `DRAFT → EFFECTIVE`; activation from any other status is refused with `ERR_TARIFF_INVALID_STATE`. Activation also has a cascade: every *other* tariff for the same `segment_id` and `grade_id` that is currently `EFFECTIVE` and whose effectivity interval overlaps the newly activated one moves `EFFECTIVE → SUPERSEDED`. That is the only way a tariff reaches `SUPERSEDED`, and it is a terminal state. Crucially, superseding a tariff does **not** rewrite `pinned_tariff_id` values that were already frozen on nominations of cycles that had closed — a rate, once pinned, is the rate that bills.

**Pinning** is the selector that decides which tariff bills a nomination, and it runs once per submitted nomination when the cycle closes. The candidate set is every tariff whose `segment_id` equals the cycle's segment, whose `grade_id` equals the nomination's grade, whose `status` is `EFFECTIVE`, whose `effective_from` is at or before the cycle's `window_close_at`, and whose `effective_to` is either null or strictly later than the cycle's `window_close_at`. `DRAFT` and `SUPERSEDED` rows are never candidates. Candidates are ranked by `effective_from` descending, and ties on that instant are broken by the tariff's own `created_at` descending, so the most recently filed rate that covers the closing instant wins. The winner's identifier is written to the nomination's `pinned_tariff_id` and is never rewritten afterwards. If the candidate set for any submitted nomination is empty, the entire close is refused with `ERR_NO_EFFECTIVE_TARIFF` (HTTP 422) and no nomination in the cycle is pinned, accepted or rejected.

### 3.3. Opening a Cycle and Collecting Nominations

A nomination cycle is the unit of allocation. A `PipelineScheduler` opens one on a segment by supplying a `cycle_code` that must be unique among that segment's cycles, the `segment_id` whose capacity the cycle allocates, and four instants: `window_open_at` and `window_close_at` bounding the nomination window, and `ship_start_at` and `ship_end_at` bounding the physical shipping window. Those four instants must be ordered so that `window_open_at` is strictly earlier than `window_close_at`, `window_close_at` is at or before `ship_start_at`, and `ship_start_at` is strictly earlier than `ship_end_at`; any other ordering is refused with `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW` (HTTP 422). The nomination window is half-open — `window_open_at` is inclusive and `window_close_at` is exclusive — and so is the shipping window, whose `ship_start_at` is inclusive and `ship_end_at` exclusive. A new cycle's `status` is `OPEN`, its `schedule_version` is 0, its `nominated_total_bbl`, `allocated_total_bbl`, `unallocated_capacity_bbl` and `total_transmix_bbl` are all 0, its `effective_capacity_bbl` and `derate_pct_applied` are 0, its `proration_factor`, `schedule_published_at`, `schedule_published_by_user_id` and `settled_at` are null, and its `version` is 1. A cycle's `status` may only ever be `OPEN`, `CLOSED`, `PRORATED`, `SCHEDULED`, `PUBLISHED`, `RESEQUENCING` or `SETTLED`, and `SETTLED` is terminal.

Submitting a nomination is the only write a `Shipper` initiates from nothing. The payload names the `cycle_id` the nomination competes in, the `shipper_id` doing the nominating — which, per §2.1, must equal `X-User-ID` — the `grade_id` of the product, the requested `origin_terminal_id` and `destination_terminal_id`, the `nominated_bbl` requested for the cycle (strictly greater than zero), and the `expected_cycle_version` guard of §2.6. A `supersedes_nomination_id` may optionally be supplied to point explicitly at the row this submission retires; when it is omitted the server derives the same reference from the twin-key rule below, and when it is supplied the referenced nomination is moved to `SUPERSEDED`. Five business gates then apply in the order of §2.5: the target cycle must be `OPEN`, or the request is refused with `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_OPEN`; the logical clock must be at or after `window_open_at` and strictly before `window_close_at`, or `ERR_NOMINATION_WINDOW_CLOSED`; the nominating shipper's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, or `ERR_SHIPPER_NOT_ACTIVE`; the supplied `origin_terminal_id` must equal the cycle's segment's `origin_terminal_id` and the supplied `destination_terminal_id` must equal that segment's `destination_terminal_id`, or `ERR_ROUTE_NOT_SERVED`; and `nominated_bbl` must be at least the segment's `min_tender_bbl`, or `ERR_TENDER_MINIMUM_NOT_MET`.

The **twin key** is the combination of `cycle_id`, `shipper_id`, `grade_id`, `origin_terminal_id` and `destination_terminal_id`. A shipper is free to replace its own nomination for a given twin key while the cycle is still collecting, but not once the carrier has committed volume to it. Concretely, every existing nomination sharing the twin key must be in `SUBMITTED`, `ACCEPTED`, `WITHDRAWN`, `SUPERSEDED` or `REJECTED`; if a twin exists in `PRORATED`, `SCHEDULED`, `REVISION_REQUESTED` or `COMPLETED`, the submission is refused with `ERR_NOMINATION_LOCKED` (HTTP 409). Where the submission is accepted, the newest surviving twin that is in `SUBMITTED` or in `ACCEPTED` transitions `SUBMITTED → SUPERSEDED` or `ACCEPTED → SUPERSEDED` and has its `allocated_bbl` set to 0, and the new row's `supersedes_nomination_id` points at it. A twin superseded out of `ACCEPTED` keeps its `pinned_tariff_id` for audit even though it will never bill, and superseding out of `ACCEPTED` requires the cycle to still be `OPEN`. Either way a `SUPERSEDED` nomination is terminal and is excluded from the cycle's `nominated_total_bbl`.

Every accepted submission has two consequences on the parent cycle. The cycle's `version` increments by one — which is exactly why the next submission must re-read it — and the cycle's `nominated_total_bbl`, `allocated_total_bbl` and `unallocated_capacity_bbl` are recomputed. The new nomination itself starts with `status` of `SUBMITTED`, `revision_count` of 0, `allocated_bbl` of 0, `history_base_bbl` of 0, `allocation_class` of `NEW_SHIPPER`, `pinned_tariff_id` of null and `rejection_reason` of null. A nomination's `status` may only ever be `SUBMITTED`, `ACCEPTED`, `PRORATED`, `SCHEDULED`, `COMPLETED`, `REVISION_REQUESTED`, `WITHDRAWN`, `SUPERSEDED` or `REJECTED`, of which `WITHDRAWN`, `SUPERSEDED`, `REJECTED` and `COMPLETED` are terminal. Its `rejection_reason`, which stays null unless `status` is `REJECTED`, may only be `BELOW_TENDER_MINIMUM` or `BELOW_TENDER_AFTER_PRORATION`.

Withdrawal is the shipper's own escape hatch, permitted only while the carrier has not yet built a schedule, and only by the owning shipper — a mismatch between `X-User-ID` and the nomination's `shipper_id` is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. Three from-states allow it and each carries its own cycle-status condition. A `SUBMITTED` nomination may be withdrawn while its cycle is `OPEN`, `CLOSED` or `PRORATED`. An `ACCEPTED` nomination may be withdrawn while its cycle is `CLOSED` or `PRORATED`. A `PRORATED` nomination may be withdrawn only while its cycle is exactly `PRORATED`. A withdrawal attempted with the cycle outside the state allowed for that from-state is refused with `ERR_NOMINATION_LOCKED`, and a withdrawal attempted from any other nomination status is refused with `ERR_NOMINATION_INVALID_STATE`. In every case — `SUBMITTED → WITHDRAWN`, `ACCEPTED → WITHDRAWN` or `PRORATED → WITHDRAWN` — the nomination transitions to `WITHDRAWN`, its `allocated_bbl` is set to 0, and the cycle's `nominated_total_bbl`, `allocated_total_bbl`, `unallocated_capacity_bbl` and `proration_factor` are recomputed.

Withdrawing after proration is a three-hop cascade rather than a bookkeeping tidy-up, and each hop matters. Hop one moves the nomination to `WITHDRAWN` and zeroes its `allocated_bbl`. Hop two recomputes the cycle's `nominated_total_bbl` and `proration_factor` over the surviving nominations. Hop three fires only because the cycle is in `PRORATED`: the whole proration is re-run and every remaining `PRORATED` nomination's `allocated_bbl` is rewritten, because the barrels the withdrawing shipper released are now available to shippers whose nominations were cut. The cycle's `allocated_total_bbl` and `unallocated_capacity_bbl` are then recomputed so that the capacity identity of §2.8 holds again exactly.

### 3.4. Closing the Window: Pinning Rates and Sizing the Line

Closing is the moment the commercial terms freeze. A `PipelineScheduler` closing a cycle drives it `OPEN → CLOSED`, and the action is refused with `ERR_CYCLE_INVALID_STATE` from any other status. Before anything is written, the pinning selector of §3.2 is evaluated for every nomination of the cycle still in `SUBMITTED`; if even one of them resolves an empty candidate set the whole close is refused with `ERR_NO_EFFECTIVE_TARIFF` and the cycle stays `OPEN`. That is a deliberate all-or-nothing gate: a carrier cannot half-close a cycle for which it has no filed rate.

Once every submitted nomination has a rate, each of them is judged against the tender minimum of its own pinned tariff — not against the segment's `min_tender_bbl`, which was already checked at submission. A nomination whose `nominated_bbl` is at least its pinned `tender_minimum_bbl` moves `SUBMITTED → ACCEPTED` with `pinned_tariff_id` frozen. A nomination whose `nominated_bbl` falls short moves `SUBMITTED → REJECTED`, has its `rejection_reason` set to `BELOW_TENDER_MINIMUM` and its `allocated_bbl` set to 0, and drops out of the cycle's `nominated_total_bbl`. Nothing else about a shipper — not its history, not its credit, not the order it nominated in — can cause a rejection at close; this is the common carrier obligation of §2.10 made concrete.

Closing also sizes the line, by computing the cycle's **effective capacity**: the barrels the cycle may actually award. The computation proceeds in a fixed order. First, the **linefill make-up** is the amount by which the segment's `linefill_requirement_bbl` exceeds its current `linefill_bbl`, or zero when linefill is already at or above requirement — never a negative number. Second, the segment's nameplate `capacity_bbl_per_cycle` is multiplied by one minus the segment's `derate_pct`, and that derated nameplate is truncated downward to the next lower whole barrel. Third, the linefill make-up is subtracted from that truncated figure, and then the segment's `transmix_reserve_bbl` is subtracted as well. The truncation happens on the derated nameplate *before* either subtraction, not on the final result. If the resulting figure is negative it is clamped to 0. The outcome is written to the cycle's `effective_capacity_bbl`, and the value of `derate_pct` used is snapshotted into the cycle's `derate_pct_applied`.

Three occasions recompute effective capacity: a close, a proration, and any change to the segment's `derate_pct` caused by a maintenance transition. The third occasion is scoped, and the scoping is easy to get wrong. When a maintenance transition changes `derate_pct`, only the cycles of that segment whose shipping window from `ship_start_at` inclusive to `ship_end_at` exclusive intersects the event's window from `window_start` through `window_end` inclusive recompute anything; a cycle whose shipping window does not intersect the maintenance window keeps its existing `effective_capacity_bbl` and `derate_pct_applied` untouched. The segment's own `derate_pct` is always the largest `capacity_derate_pct` among the maintenance events on that segment whose `status` is `NOTIFIED` or `ACTIVE`, and is 0 when no such event exists.

Finally the close refreshes the cycle's aggregates. `nominated_total_bbl` is the sum of `nominated_bbl` over the cycle's nominations whose `status` is one of `SUBMITTED`, `ACCEPTED`, `PRORATED`, `REVISION_REQUESTED`, `SCHEDULED` or `COMPLETED`; `allocated_total_bbl` is the sum of `allocated_bbl` over that same status set; `unallocated_capacity_bbl` is `effective_capacity_bbl` less `allocated_total_bbl`; and `proration_factor` is refreshed as described in §3.5. Withdrawn, superseded and rejected rows contribute to none of them. The close also computes each accepted nomination's shipping history base and `allocation_class` by the rule of §3.5, so that a scheduler can inspect the class split before committing to a proration run; those two fields are re-frozen at every subsequent proration run.

### 3.5. Prorating Capacity Against the Line

Proration is the heart of the system. A `PipelineScheduler` may prorate a cycle from exactly two states — `CLOSED → PRORATED` in the ordinary case, and `RESEQUENCING → PRORATED` after a maintenance derate has torn a published schedule apart — and any other from-state is refused with `ERR_CYCLE_INVALID_STATE`.

The **shipping history base** is computed first, per nomination in the input set. A historical movement record qualifies for a nomination when its `shipper_id` matches the nomination's shipper, its `segment_id` matches the cycle's segment, its `record_status` is `FINAL`, and its `cycle_end_at` falls at or after the instant exactly 365 days — one full year — before the cycle's `window_open_at` and strictly before `window_open_at` itself. `PROVISIONAL` rows never qualify. If no record qualifies, `history_base_bbl` is 0. Otherwise `history_base_bbl` is the sum of `actual_shipped_bbl` across the qualifying records divided by the *number of qualifying records* — never by a fixed number of cycles — rounded half up to two decimal places. The count of qualifying records also fixes `allocation_class`: a shipper with at least two qualifying records on the segment is `HISTORICAL`, and a shipper with fewer than two is `NEW_SHIPPER`.

The proration input set is the cycle's nominations whose `status` is `ACCEPTED`, `PRORATED` or `SCHEDULED`, plus those in `SUBMITTED` but only when the cycle's `status` is `RESEQUENCING`. Rows in `WITHDRAWN`, `SUPERSEDED`, `REJECTED` or `COMPLETED` are excluded from both the allocation pools and the redistribution pass. If the input set is empty, every allocation is 0, the cycle's `proration_factor` becomes exactly 1.000000, `unallocated_capacity_bbl` becomes the whole of `effective_capacity_bbl`, and the run stops there.

Otherwise the segment's `proration_policy` selects one of two genuinely different algorithms over the same nomination set, and the same nominations can therefore be awarded different volumes depending only on the filing in force.

Under **`HISTORY_WEIGHTED`**, capacity is split before it is shared. A **new-shipper reserve** equal to one tenth — ten percent — of `effective_capacity_bbl`, truncated downward to a whole barrel, is set aside; the **historical pool** is whatever remains of `effective_capacity_bbl` after that reserve is removed. The input set is then partitioned by `allocation_class`. For each nomination classed `HISTORICAL`, its weight is the smaller of its `nominated_bbl` and its `history_base_bbl`, and the total weight is the sum of those weights across the historical nominations. Each historical nomination's raw share is the historical pool multiplied by that nomination's weight divided by the total weight; where the total weight is zero, every historical raw share is zero instead. Each historical allocation is then the smaller of its `nominated_bbl` and its raw share truncated downward to a whole barrel. The historical leftover is the historical pool less the sum of the historical allocations. For nominations classed `NEW_SHIPPER`, the reserve is split equally: each one's raw share is the reserve divided by the number of new-shipper nominations, and each allocation is the smaller of its `nominated_bbl` and that raw share truncated downward to a whole barrel; the new-shipper leftover is the reserve less the sum of those allocations. When there are no new-shipper nominations at all, the entire reserve becomes new-shipper leftover and spills over into redistribution rather than being wasted. The leftover carried into redistribution is the historical leftover plus the new-shipper leftover. Because weights are capped at the history base, two identical nominations award more barrels to the shipper with the larger shipping history.

Under **`VOLUME_WEIGHTED`**, there is no class split, no reserve and no use of `history_base_bbl` or `allocation_class` at all. The total nominated volume is the sum of `nominated_bbl` across the input set, each nomination's allocation is the smaller of its `nominated_bbl` and `effective_capacity_bbl` multiplied by that nomination's `nominated_bbl` divided by the total nominated volume and truncated downward to a whole barrel, and the leftover carried into redistribution is `effective_capacity_bbl` less the sum of those allocations.

A single **redistribution pass** then runs, identically under both variants. Every nomination whose allocation is still strictly below its `nominated_bbl` is eligible, and the eligible nominations are ordered by unmet demand — `nominated_bbl` less current allocation — descending, with ties broken by the nomination's `created_at` ascending. Walking that order, each nomination receives the smaller of its own unmet demand and the leftover still available, the grant is added to its allocation and subtracted from the leftover. There is exactly one such pass: any leftover surviving it stays as the cycle's `unallocated_capacity_bbl` and is never redistributed a second time.

After allocations are written, a single **cleanup pass** enforces the tender minimum a second time. Any nomination whose freshly computed `allocated_bbl` is below the `tender_minimum_bbl` of its pinned tariff has its `allocated_bbl` set to 0, moves `PRORATED → REJECTED`, and has its `rejection_reason` set to `BELOW_TENDER_AFTER_PRORATION`. The barrels so released remain in `unallocated_capacity_bbl`; they are *not* handed to anybody else, because the cleanup pass runs exactly once and triggers no re-redistribution. Surviving nominations settle into `PRORATED` by one of three hops — `ACCEPTED → PRORATED` in the ordinary run, and `SUBMITTED → PRORATED` or `REVISION_REQUESTED → PRORATED` when the cycle was `RESEQUENCING` — and the cycle itself moves to `PRORATED`.

When the run starts from `RESEQUENCING` the input set is wider and one extra check applies up front: nominations in `SUBMITTED` (revisions the shipper did resubmit) and in `REVISION_REQUESTED` (revisions the shipper ignored) are re-tested against the `tender_minimum_bbl` of their pinned tariff, and any that fall short are rejected with `rejection_reason` of `BELOW_TENDER_MINIMUM`. Those that clear it are prorated at the derated `effective_capacity_bbl` alongside the rest. A nomination that was never resubmitted still competes on its original `nominated_bbl`, which is the mechanism by which a shipper's silence does not forfeit its allocation.

Every proration run closes by recomputing the cycle's `nominated_total_bbl` first, then `allocated_total_bbl` as the sum of the allocations, then `unallocated_capacity_bbl` as `effective_capacity_bbl` less `allocated_total_bbl`, and finally `proration_factor` as `allocated_total_bbl` divided by `nominated_total_bbl` rounded half up to six decimal places — or exactly 1.000000 when `nominated_total_bbl` is zero.

### 3.6. Sequencing Batches, Interfaces and the Published Schedule

A prorated cycle carries volumes but no order. Building the schedule turns it into a physical plan and drives the cycle `PRORATED → SCHEDULED`; from any other status the action is refused with `ERR_CYCLE_INVALID_STATE`. Re-running it out of `RESEQUENCING` — which is reached by way of a fresh proration, as §3.8 explains — rebuilds the order over whichever nominations survived, and it deletes nothing: earlier tickets that no longer belong to the plan were already cancelled by the cascade that produced the resequencing.

**Sequencing order.** The tickets are built from the cycle's nominations whose `status` is `PRORATED` and whose `allocated_bbl` is strictly greater than zero. Nominations in `REJECTED`, `WITHDRAWN` or `REVISION_REQUESTED` never receive a ticket. Those nominations are ordered by the `sequence_rank` of their product grade ascending — cleanest product first — then by `allocated_bbl` descending, then by the nomination's `created_at` ascending. Walking that order assigns `sequence_position` starting at 1, each ticket's `predecessor_batch_id` names the ticket at the position immediately before it, and the ticket at position 1 has a null `predecessor_batch_id`. Each ticket's `injection_window_start` is the cycle's `ship_start_at` advanced by one 24-hour slot for every position ahead of it — that is, by `sequence_position` minus one whole days — and its `injection_window_end` is 24 hours after its own start. Windows are half-open, inclusive of start and exclusive of end, and rendered as ISO 8601 UTC with three fractional digits. Each new ticket also takes its `cycle_id` from the cycle, its `nomination_id` from the nomination it lifts, and a `volume_bbl` equal to that nomination's `allocated_bbl` at build time.

**Interface transmix.** Every grade change in the line contaminates the interface, and the slop cut is computed per ticket against whatever stands immediately upstream of it. For the ticket at `sequence_position` 1 the upstream rank is the `sequence_rank` of the segment's linefill grade, named by the segment's `linefill_grade_id`; for every later ticket it is the `sequence_rank` of the grade on the predecessor ticket's nomination. The rank gap is the absolute difference between the ticket's own grade rank and that upstream rank. Where the gap is zero — same grade behind same grade — the ticket's `transmix_bbl` is 0 and its `requires_flush` is false. Where the ticket's own rank is *higher* than the upstream rank, meaning a dirtier product following a cleaner one, the cut is a **downgrade**: `transmix_bbl` is the segment's `transmix_cut_bbl` multiplied by the rank gap, and `requires_flush` stays false. Where the ticket's own rank is *lower* than the upstream rank, meaning a cleaner product following a dirtier one, the cut is an **upgrade** and costs twice as much: `transmix_bbl` is the segment's `transmix_cut_bbl` multiplied by the rank gap and then multiplied by two, and `requires_flush` is true when and only when the rank gap is two or more steps. All transmix volumes are whole barrels. The cycle's `total_transmix_bbl` is the sum of `transmix_bbl` across the cycle's tickets whose `movement_status` is anything other than `CANCELLED`.

The reserve is a hard gate on building. The sum of the transmix cuts across the sequence the build would produce must not exceed the segment's `transmix_reserve_bbl`; when it would, the build is refused with `ERR_TRANSMIX_RESERVE_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422) and no ticket is created. Because the same reserve was already subtracted from nominatable capacity when effective capacity was computed, transmix never eats into the capacity identity of §2.8 — it is paid for up front.

Every ticket is born with two independent status dimensions on the same row: `movement_status` of `PLANNED`, tracking the physical movement and taking only the values `PLANNED`, `INJECTED`, `DELIVERED` or `CANCELLED` (of which `DELIVERED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal), and `quality_status` of `PENDING`, tracking the specification dimension and taking only `PENDING`, `PASSED` or `REJECTED`. The two advance separately and must never be conflated. A new ticket's `measured_sulfur_ppm`, `measured_rvp_psi`, `injected_bbl`, `delivered_bbl` and `cancel_reason` are all null, and its `retest_count` is 0.

**Publication** is what makes the plan binding on shippers. It requires the cycle to be in `SCHEDULED` — from any other status it is refused with `ERR_CYCLE_INVALID_STATE` — and it has one physical precondition: the segment's `linefill_bbl` must be at least its `linefill_requirement_bbl`. A segment short of linefill cannot publish, is refused with `ERR_LINEFILL_REQUIREMENT_NOT_MET` (HTTP 422), and must be topped up through a linefill inventory adjustment first. Publication drives the cycle `SCHEDULED → PUBLISHED`, increments the cycle's `schedule_version` by one, stamps `schedule_published_at` with the logical clock and records the publishing operator's `X-User-ID` in `schedule_published_by_user_id`, and moves every nomination of the cycle still in `PRORATED` to `SCHEDULED`. Building a schedule does not itself advance `schedule_version`; only publication does. The recorded publisher is what the segregation-of-duties check of §2.10 will later compare a certifier against, so a cycle published and certified by the same operator identifier is unsettleable by design.

**Rescheduling an injection window.** A `PipelineScheduler` may move a planned batch's slot by supplying a replacement `injection_window_start` and `injection_window_end`. The ticket's `movement_status` must be `PLANNED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_TICKET_INVALID_STATE`; the requested window must lie inside the cycle's shipping window bounded by `ship_start_at` and `ship_end_at`, or `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`; and the segment carrying the ticket must not be shut, or `ERR_SEGMENT_UNAVAILABLE`. The last gate is **bay occupancy** at the origin terminal. The peers considered are every *other* batch ticket whose nomination shares the same `origin_terminal_id`, whose `movement_status` is `PLANNED`, and whose own half-open injection window intersects the requested half-open window — counted across every cycle, not just this one, because a bay is physical. Because the arithmetic is half-open, a peer whose window ends exactly when the requested window starts does not overlap it, and neither does a peer whose window starts exactly when the requested window ends; instants are compared at millisecond resolution. Tickets that are `INJECTED`, `DELIVERED` or `CANCELLED` free their bay and are not peers. If the number of overlapping peers is greater than or equal to the origin terminal's `injection_bays`, the reschedule is refused with `ERR_TERMINAL_BAY_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409); otherwise the two window fields are replaced.

### 3.7. Moving Product: Injection, Quality Refusal, Delivery and Compensation

Injection is where the commercial plan meets a manifold and a sample bottle. A `TerminalOperator` injecting a batch supplies three measurements: `measured_sulfur_ppm` and `measured_rvp_psi` taken from the product presented, and `injected_bbl`, the barrels actually pumped. Before any of them is honoured, five gates apply in the order of §2.5. The ticket's `movement_status` must be `PLANNED` and its `quality_status` must be `PENDING` or `REJECTED` with `retest_count` still below 2, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_TICKET_INVALID_STATE`. The ticket's cycle must be in `PUBLISHED` — an unpublished plan is not authority to move product — and an attempt made against a cycle in any other status is refused with `ERR_CYCLE_INVALID_STATE`, never with `ERR_TICKET_INVALID_STATE`. The segment must be usable: its `operational_status` must be `OPERATIONAL` or `DERATED`, and a segment that is `SHUT` refuses the injection with `ERR_SEGMENT_UNAVAILABLE`. The nominating shipper must still be `ACTIVE`, or `ERR_SHIPPER_NOT_ACTIVE`. And the product must be on specification.

**The quality dimension.** `measured_sulfur_ppm` must be at or below the `max_sulfur_ppm` of the ticket's grade and `measured_rvp_psi` must be at or below that grade's `max_rvp_psi`. If either ceiling is exceeded, the carrier exercises its rejection right at the injection point: the response is `ERR_QUALITY_SPEC_VIOLATION` with HTTP 422, and yet the state writes persist, because the product was physically presented and physically refused. Specifically, `quality_status` moves `PENDING → REJECTED`, both measured values are stored on the ticket, `retest_count` increments by one, `movement_status` stays `PLANNED`, and no volume moves anywhere. The shipper may present the batch again. A retest that comes back within specification while `movement_status` is `PLANNED` and `retest_count` is still below 2 moves `quality_status` `REJECTED → PASSED` and carries out the full injection below.

A second refusal, however, ends the batch. When `retest_count` reaches 2 the cascade is four hops deep. Hop one is the rejection itself, as described above. Hop two cancels the ticket: `movement_status` moves `PLANNED → CANCELLED` with `cancel_reason` recorded as `QUALITY_RETEST_LIMIT`. Hop three pushes the parent nomination `SCHEDULED → REVISION_REQUESTED`, increments its `revision_count` by one and sets its `allocated_bbl` to 0, releasing the refused barrels back into the cycle's `unallocated_capacity_bbl` so that allocated plus unallocated still equals effective capacity. Hop four recomputes the cycle's `allocated_total_bbl`, `unallocated_capacity_bbl` and `total_transmix_bbl`. An implementation that returns the 422 and stops at hop one leaves the cycle's books wrong.

**Physical and fiscal headroom.** A successful injection must not draw the origin tank below its floor: the origin terminal's `inventory_bbl` less the sum of the supplied `injected_bbl` and the ticket's own `transmix_bbl` must remain at or above that terminal's `min_operating_inventory_bbl`, and a shortfall is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_ORIGIN_INVENTORY` (HTTP 422). Note that the transmix cut is debited alongside the product — the slop has to come from somewhere. The final gate is credit, and it runs last of all, after quality and after inventory. A shipper's **exposure** is the sum, over every batch ticket belonging to that shipper whose `movement_status` is `INJECTED` or `DELIVERED` and whose cycle has not yet reached `SETTLED`, of that ticket's `volume_bbl` multiplied by the `base_rate_usd_per_bbl` of the tariff pinned on its own nomination, each such line rounded half up to two decimal places before the lines are summed. The gate asks whether that exposure, plus the candidate ticket's own line value computed the same way from its `volume_bbl` and its pinned base rate, remains at or below the shipper's `credit_limit_usd`; if it does not, the injection is refused with `ERR_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` and HTTP 402. Tickets on cycles that have reached `SETTLED` leave exposure entirely and become ordinary receivables, and `exposure_usd` is recomputed on every injection, delivery, batch cancellation, cycle settlement and settlement invoicing.

When every gate passes, the injection writes a lot at once. `quality_status` becomes `PASSED`, both measured values are stored, `injected_bbl` takes the supplied value, and `movement_status` moves `PLANNED → INJECTED`. The origin terminal's `inventory_bbl` is reduced by the sum of `injected_bbl` and `transmix_bbl`. The segment's `in_transit_bbl` is increased by `injected_bbl` and its `transmix_inventory_bbl` is increased by `transmix_bbl`. The shipper's `exposure_usd` is recomputed. The five-accumulator total of §2.7 is unchanged by all of this, which is the point.

**Delivery** closes the movement. The ticket's `movement_status` must be `INJECTED`, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_TICKET_INVALID_STATE`. The supplied `delivered_bbl` must be strictly greater than zero and no greater than the ticket's `injected_bbl`; anything else is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. And the destination tank must have room: its `inventory_bbl` plus the supplied `delivered_bbl` must not exceed its `shell_capacity_bbl`, or the delivery is refused with `ERR_DESTINATION_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). On success, `delivered_bbl` is stored and `movement_status` moves `INJECTED → DELIVERED`; the destination terminal's `inventory_bbl` increases by `delivered_bbl`; the segment's `in_transit_bbl` decreases by the ticket's `injected_bbl`; the segment's `system_loss_bbl` increases by the difference between `injected_bbl` and `delivered_bbl`, which is exactly where line shrinkage is booked; and the shipper's `exposure_usd` is recomputed. The conservation identity of §2.7 is re-asserted afterwards.

**Cancellation** is available to either a `PipelineScheduler` or a `TerminalOperator`, and takes a `cancel_reason`. It applies from two movement states and behaves differently in each. Cancelling a ticket whose `movement_status` is `PLANNED` moves it `PLANNED → CANCELLED`, stores the `cancel_reason`, moves no volume at all because nothing was injected, and recomputes the cycle's `total_transmix_bbl` — a cancelled ticket drops out of that sum. Cancelling a ticket whose `movement_status` is `INJECTED` moves it `INJECTED → CANCELLED` and is a full compensation: the origin terminal's `inventory_bbl` is increased by the sum of `injected_bbl` and `transmix_bbl`, the segment's `in_transit_bbl` is decreased by `injected_bbl`, and the segment's `transmix_inventory_bbl` is decreased by `transmix_bbl` — each by exactly the amount the injection moved, so the five-accumulator total of §2.7 is left unchanged and nothing is booked to `system_loss_bbl`. The `cancel_reason` is stored, and both the cycle's `total_transmix_bbl` and the shipper's `exposure_usd` are recomputed. Cancellation from `DELIVERED` or from `CANCELLED` is refused with `ERR_TICKET_INVALID_STATE`. Besides an operator's explicit cancellation, a planned ticket is also cancelled by the resequencing cascade of §3.8 and by the second quality refusal above; in both of those cases `cancel_reason` records which one it was.

### 3.8. Integrity Work, Derates and the Resequencing Loop

Pipelines need maintenance, and maintenance costs capacity. A `PipelineScheduler` plans the work by recording a `segment_id`, a `kind` that must be one of `PIGGING`, `INTEGRITY_DIG`, `HYDROTEST` or `VALVE_REPAIR`, a `window_start` and a `window_end` bounding the outage, a `capacity_derate_pct` stating the fraction of nameplate capacity the work removes, and an optional free-text `notes`. Unlike the cycle's windows, a maintenance window is **closed at both ends**: both `window_start` and `window_end` are inclusive. `window_end` must be strictly later than `window_start`, or the plan is refused with `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`. `capacity_derate_pct` is carried to two decimal places, must be strictly greater than zero and no greater than 1, and a value of exactly 1 means the line is shut outright rather than merely narrowed. A new event's `status` is `PLANNED`, its `notified_at` is null and its `notified_shipper_count` is 0. A `PLANNED` event derates nothing yet — planning work is not the same as telling shippers about it. An event's `status` may only be `PLANNED`, `NOTIFIED`, `ACTIVE` or `COMPLETED`, and `COMPLETED` is terminal.

Two outages cannot be crammed against each other. A new maintenance window must not overlap, within a buffer of one full day — 24 hours — on each side, any existing maintenance event on the same segment whose `status` is `PLANNED`, `NOTIFIED` or `ACTIVE`; the comparison treats both windows as closed at both ends, so windows that merely touch inside the buffer still conflict. A conflict is refused with `ERR_MAINTENANCE_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409).

**Notification** is the common carrier's notice obligation, and it is the single most consequential call in the API. The event must be in `PLANNED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_MAINTENANCE_INVALID_STATE`, and the notice must be timely: the logical clock advanced by two full days — 48 hours — must be at or before the event's `window_start`, or the request is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_NOTICE` (HTTP 422). Notification drives the event `PLANNED → NOTIFIED` and sets off a five-hop cascade.

Hop one records the notice. `notified_at` takes the logical clock, and `notified_shipper_count` takes the size of the **notification scope**: the number of distinct shippers the notice reaches. The affected cycles are those on the event's segment whose `status` is `PRORATED`, `SCHEDULED`, `PUBLISHED` or `RESEQUENCING` and whose shipping window, from `ship_start_at` inclusive to `ship_end_at` exclusive, intersects the event's closed window from `window_start` through `window_end`. Cycles in `OPEN`, `CLOSED` or `SETTLED` are never in scope. Within those cycles, the count is of distinct values of the nominations' `shipper_id` over nominations whose `status` is `PRORATED`, `SCHEDULED` or `REVISION_REQUESTED` — a shipper appearing on three such nominations is counted once, and a shipper whose only nomination was rejected or withdrawn is not counted at all.

Hop two derates the line. The segment's `derate_pct` is recomputed as the largest `capacity_derate_pct` among the maintenance events on that segment whose `status` is `NOTIFIED` or `ACTIVE`, and the segment's `operational_status` follows from it: `OPERATIONAL` when no such event applies and the governing derate is therefore 0, `DERATED` when the governing derate is strictly between 0 and 1, and `SHUT` when it is exactly 1.

Hop three re-sizes the affected cycles. Every cycle on the segment whose `status` is `PUBLISHED` and whose shipping window intersects the event's closed window recomputes its `effective_capacity_bbl` and `derate_pct_applied` at the new derate. Where the recomputed `effective_capacity_bbl` is still at least the cycle's `allocated_total_bbl`, the published plan survives untouched. Where the recomputed `effective_capacity_bbl` is strictly less than `allocated_total_bbl`, the cycle transitions `PUBLISHED → RESEQUENCING`, because the carrier can no longer move what it promised.

Hop four re-cuts the nominations of a resequencing cycle. Proration is re-run over the cycle's nominations in `SCHEDULED` at the derated capacity, and every nomination whose new allocation is below its current `allocated_bbl` has `allocated_bbl` overwritten with that new, lower allocation, transitions `SCHEDULED → REVISION_REQUESTED`, and has its `revision_count` incremented by one. A nomination whose allocation is unaffected stays in `SCHEDULED`.

Hop five clears the physical plan. Every batch ticket of that cycle whose `movement_status` is `PLANNED` and whose nomination just moved to `REVISION_REQUESTED` transitions `PLANNED → CANCELLED` with `cancel_reason` recorded as `RESEQUENCING`. The cycle's `allocated_total_bbl`, `unallocated_capacity_bbl`, `proration_factor` and `total_transmix_bbl` are then recomputed, and the capacity identity of §2.8 must hold again exactly.

Starting the work moves the event `NOTIFIED → ACTIVE` and requires the `NOTIFIED` status, otherwise `ERR_MAINTENANCE_INVALID_STATE`. The derate stays in force across that transition; the segment's `derate_pct` and `operational_status` are recomputed on it, and `operational_status` becomes `SHUT` when the governing derate is exactly 1. Completing the work moves the event `ACTIVE → COMPLETED`, again requiring the declared from-state, and restores capacity: the segment's `derate_pct` is recomputed over whatever `NOTIFIED` or `ACTIVE` events remain, and its `operational_status` returns to `OPERATIONAL` when none do. Completion has one deliberately partial effect on cycles: a cycle sitting in `RESEQUENCING` recomputes its `effective_capacity_bbl` and `unallocated_capacity_bbl` at the restored capacity but **keeps** its `status` of `RESEQUENCING`. It does not spring back to `PUBLISHED` on its own; a scheduler must prorate, rebuild and republish it, which is exactly the `PUBLISHED → RESEQUENCING → PRORATED → SCHEDULED → PUBLISHED` loop this domain runs on.

**The revision loop.** A scheduler may also send a nomination back for revision by hand, without any maintenance at all — after a quality failure, or on a shipper's request. The action requires the acting role `PipelineScheduler` and is allowed from two nomination states, giving `PRORATED → REVISION_REQUESTED` while the cycle is `PRORATED` or `RESEQUENCING`, and `SCHEDULED → REVISION_REQUESTED` while the cycle is `PUBLISHED` or `RESEQUENCING`. From any other nomination status it is refused with `ERR_NOMINATION_INVALID_STATE`, and it is refused with `ERR_NOMINATION_LOCKED` once any batch ticket of that nomination has left `PLANNED` — that is, once a ticket is `INJECTED` or `DELIVERED`, the volume is in the line and there is nothing to revise. On success the nomination moves to `REVISION_REQUESTED`, its `allocated_bbl` is set to 0, its `revision_count` increments by one, every batch ticket of that nomination still in `PLANNED` is cancelled, and the cycle's `allocated_total_bbl` and `unallocated_capacity_bbl` are recomputed.

Resubmission closes the loop and belongs to the shipper. Only the owning shipper may resubmit — otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` — the shipper's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, or `ERR_SHIPPER_NOT_ACTIVE`, and the nomination must be in `REVISION_REQUESTED`, or `ERR_NOMINATION_INVALID_STATE`. The body carries a single field, `nominated_bbl`, and it is bounded on both sides: it must not exceed the `nominated_bbl` the nomination held when revision was requested, or the resubmission is refused with `ERR_REVISION_VOLUME_INCREASE` (HTTP 422), and it must be at least the segment's `min_tender_bbl`, or `ERR_TENDER_MINIMUM_NOT_MET`. The route checks of §3.3 apply again — a resubmission whose nomination no longer matches the cycle's segment endpoints is refused with `ERR_ROUTE_NOT_SERVED` — and so does the nomination window test, so a resubmission attempted outside the half-open interval bounded by `window_open_at` and `window_close_at` is refused with `ERR_NOMINATION_WINDOW_CLOSED`. On success the nomination's `nominated_bbl` is replaced with the body value, its `allocated_bbl` is set to 0, it moves `REVISION_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED`, and the cycle's `nominated_total_bbl`, `allocated_total_bbl` and `unallocated_capacity_bbl` are recomputed. From there the scheduler's next proration run — which, per §3.5, accepts `SUBMITTED` rows only while the cycle is `RESEQUENCING` — carries it back to `PRORATED`, completing the `SCHEDULED → REVISION_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED → PRORATED` circuit.

### 3.9. Settlement: Loss Allocation, Tariff Billing and the Imbalance Penalty

Settlement is the end of a cycle's life and drives it `PUBLISHED → SETTLED`, a terminal state; from any other status the attempt is refused with `ERR_CYCLE_INVALID_STATE`. It has one physical precondition: every batch ticket of the cycle must have reached `DELIVERED` or `CANCELLED`. A single ticket still in `PLANNED` or still in `INJECTED` blocks the whole settlement with `ERR_BATCHES_IN_TRANSIT` (HTTP 409), because a cycle cannot be billed while product is still in the line. The `PipelineScheduler` settling the cycle supplies one field, `basis_mode`, which must be either `SCHEDULED` or `DELIVERED` and which decides how transportation is billed. Settlement stamps the cycle's `settled_at` with the logical clock, reconciles the segment's `system_loss_bbl`, moves every nomination of the cycle still in `SCHEDULED` whose tickets have all reached `DELIVERED` along the `SCHEDULED → COMPLETED` hop, and recomputes `exposure_usd` for every billed shipper — which, since the cycle is now `SETTLED`, drops its tickets out of exposure entirely.

Settlement raises one settlement row per **billed shipper**: each distinct shipper holding at least one batch ticket of the cycle whose `movement_status` is anything other than `CANCELLED`. The rows are created in ascending order of the shipper's own `created_at`, each with `status` of `DRAFT`, and each recording the `cycle_id` settled, the `shipper_id` billed, the `basis_mode` chosen and a null `invoice_number`. A settlement's `status` may only be `DRAFT`, `CERTIFIED` or `INVOICED`, of which `INVOICED` is terminal, and its `certified_by_user_id` is null while it is `DRAFT`.

**Loss allocation runs first**, before any charge is computed, because every later figure depends on it. The cycle's total injected volume is the sum of `injected_bbl` across the cycle's tickets whose `movement_status` is `DELIVERED`, and its total delivered volume is the sum of `delivered_bbl` across that same set. Cancelled tickets contribute nothing to either total, which is safe precisely because the settlement precondition guarantees that every non-delivered ticket is cancelled by then. The cycle's system loss is the total injected less the total delivered, rounded half up to two decimal places — and interface transmix is deliberately **not** part of it, since transmix was already accounted into the segment's slop inventory at injection. If the total delivered volume is zero, `loss_allocation_bbl` is 0 for every shipper and the allocation stops there. Otherwise each billed shipper, taken in ascending order of the shipper's `created_at`, receives a share equal to the system loss multiplied by that shipper's own delivered volume divided by the cycle's total delivered volume, rounded half up to two decimal places. Because per-shipper rounding will not generally add up, a remainder pass follows: the remainder is the system loss less the sum of the shares, rounded half up to two decimal places, and it is added to the share of the shipper with the largest delivered volume, with ties broken by the shipper's `created_at` ascending. After that pass the allocated shares sum to the system loss exactly.

**Transportation charges** are then computed per shipper, twice. The tickets that bill are the shipper's tickets in the cycle whose `movement_status` is `PLANNED`, `INJECTED` or `DELIVERED`; `CANCELLED` tickets are excluded from billing entirely. Each ticket prices against the tariff pinned on its *own* nomination, so a shipper moving two grades in one cycle prices against two tariffs. For a given ticket, the billable volume under the `DELIVERED` basis is the larger of the ticket's `delivered_bbl` — treated as zero when it is null — and the pinned tariff's `tender_minimum_bbl`; under the `SCHEDULED` basis it is the larger of the ticket's `volume_bbl` and that same `tender_minimum_bbl`. The rate that applies to that billable volume is the pinned tariff's `tier2_rate_usd_per_bbl` when the billable volume is at or above `tier2_min_bbl`, otherwise its `tier1_rate_usd_per_bbl` when the billable volume is at or above `tier1_min_bbl`, and otherwise its `base_rate_usd_per_bbl`. The ticket's line amount is the billable volume multiplied by that rate, rounded half up to two decimal places — **rounded per ticket, before any summation** — and the shipper's charge on a given basis is the exact sum of those rounded lines.

Both bases are always computed. The charge on the `basis_mode` the scheduler chose is written to `tariff_charge_usd`; the charge on the other basis is written to `alt_basis_charge_usd` as an explicit counterfactual; and `basis_delta_usd` is `alt_basis_charge_usd` less `tariff_charge_usd`, rounded half up to two decimal places. The economics differ by design: the `DELIVERED` basis bills the barrels that actually arrived, floored at the tender minimum, so a refused or undelivered batch bills only the tender minimum, while the `SCHEDULED` basis is ship-or-pay and bills the scheduled batch volume regardless of delivery — which means the same cycle yields a strictly larger charge on the `SCHEDULED` basis whenever any batch under-delivers.

**The imbalance penalty** is computed strictly after loss allocation, in a pinned eight-step order, because the tier depends on the imbalance, the imbalance depends on the loss allocation, and the loss allocation depends on cycle-wide delivered volume. Step one: the settlement's `scheduled_bbl` is the sum of `volume_bbl` over the shipper's tickets in the cycle whose `movement_status` is `PLANNED`, `INJECTED` or `DELIVERED`. Step two: its `delivered_bbl` is the sum of `delivered_bbl` over the shipper's tickets whose `movement_status` is `DELIVERED`. Step three: its `loss_allocation_bbl` is the share computed above. Step four: `imbalance_bbl` is the `delivered_bbl` less the `scheduled_bbl` plus the `loss_allocation_bbl`, rounded half up to two decimal places — note that it may legitimately be negative. Step five: `imbalance_pct` is 0 when `scheduled_bbl` is zero, and otherwise the absolute value of `imbalance_bbl` divided by `scheduled_bbl`, rounded half up to six decimal places. Step six sets `imbalance_tier` and its multiplier from that percentage: an `imbalance_pct` at or below 0.005 — half of one percent — is `TOLERANCE` and carries a multiplier of 0; an `imbalance_pct` above that but at or below 0.02 — two percent — is `STANDARD` and carries a multiplier of 1.0; anything larger is `PENALTY` and carries a multiplier of 2.5. Step seven computes `imbalance_charge_usd` as the absolute value of `imbalance_bbl` multiplied by the applicable `imbalance_penalty_rate_usd_per_bbl` and then by the tier multiplier, rounded half up to two decimal places; the penalty rate is taken from the tariff pinned on the nomination of that shipper's ticket with the lowest `sequence_position` in the cycle, with ties broken by the ticket's `created_at` ascending. Step eight computes `total_billed_usd` as `tariff_charge_usd` plus `imbalance_charge_usd`, rounded half up to two decimal places. A shipper inside tolerance therefore pays transportation only, because its multiplier zeroes the penalty term outright.

**Certification and invoicing** close the chain, and they are deliberately split between two roles and two people. A `PipelineScheduler` certifies a settlement, driving it `DRAFT → CERTIFIED`; certification from any other status is refused with `ERR_SETTLEMENT_INVALID_STATE`, and certification by the very operator whose `X-User-ID` was recorded in the cycle's `schedule_published_by_user_id` is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). A successful certification records the certifier's `X-User-ID` in `certified_by_user_id`. A `TariffAdministrator` then invoices it, driving it `CERTIFIED → INVOICED` and supplying the carrier's `invoice_number`; invoicing from any other status is refused with `ERR_SETTLEMENT_INVALID_STATE`, and invoicing by the operator whose `X-User-ID` appears in `certified_by_user_id` is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Invoicing stores the `invoice_number` and recomputes the billed shipper's `exposure_usd` one last time.

Creating a brand-new resource returns HTTP **201**; every other successful call — state transitions, adjustments, reschedules and reads alike — returns HTTP **200**.
