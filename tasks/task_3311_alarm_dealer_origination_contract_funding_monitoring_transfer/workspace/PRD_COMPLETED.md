# Alarm Dealer Origination and Contract Funding API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Residential security is sold through two origination routes. The company's own representatives write DIRECT contracts on company paper, while a network of independent dealers sells contracts that the company later purchases under a funding programme. The two routes differ sharply in economics: a dealer-originated contract only becomes company paper after a package of contracts has been reviewed, approved and funded inside a bounded funding window, and the dealer that sold it remains financially exposed to the outcome through a holdback reserve and a chargeback liability.

This document specifies the API that runs that pipeline end to end — dealer onboarding and standing, funding-package negotiation and funding, contract origination and the quality gates that a contract must clear before it may be activated, installation and central-station monitoring transfer, monthly attrition sweeps and delinquency handling, alarm verification and dispatch coordination, relocation and contract assumption, rescission, and dealer chargeback adjudication. Because money moves against a contract before the equipment is installed and before the monitoring account is provisioned, the specification pays close attention to the ordering of side effects, to the resumption of interrupted work, and to the serialisation of concurrent writers.

The principal users are the dealer channel organisation, the company's funding desk, a field service coordination team, the central monitoring station, a compliance function responsible for disclosure and rescission, and the subscriber. Each of these actors sees a different slice of the same contract, and several lifecycle steps are gated on a step owned by a different actor: a contract cannot be funded until a monitoring account has been provisioned by the monitoring station, and an installation cannot be completed until a monitoring transfer has been committed. The system must therefore keep every gate, hold and financial consequence consistent as a contract travels from submission to activation, through delinquency and service, to cancellation, assumption or refund.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the acting role. A request that omits either header, or that presents a role string that is not one of the declared roles, is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` and HTTP 401. A request that names a declared role the endpoint does not admit is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` and HTTP 403.

Eight roles exist and their names are spelled exactly as follows: `PlatformAdmin`, `ChannelManager`, `Dealer`, `FundingAnalyst`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `MonitoringOperator`, `ServiceCoordinator` and `Customer`. `PlatformAdmin` is the benchmark operator that owns the logical clock and the cross-tenant administrative reads; its identifier is opaque. `ChannelManager` is the dealer-channel operator that onboards dealers and originates direct contracts; its identifier is opaque. `FundingAnalyst` is the company funding-side analyst that approves and funds packages, runs attrition sweeps and adjudicates chargebacks; its identifier is opaque. `ComplianceOfficer` adjudicates rescissions and disclosures and enforces segregation of duties, and its identifier is opaque. `MonitoringOperator` is the central-station operator that provisions monitoring accounts, verifies alarms and coordinates dispatch; its identifier is opaque. `ServiceCoordinator` is the field operations coordinator that schedules installs and service visits and restores service; its identifier is opaque.

Two roles are entity-backed rather than opaque. When `X-User-Role` is `Dealer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Dealer`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Dealer` compare the resource's `dealer_id` field to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `Customer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Customer`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Customer` compare the resource's `customer_id` field to `X-User-ID`. For the six opaque roles the identifier is not tied to any persisted entity — the server may record it where a workflow captures an operator identifier, as with `RescissionRequest.operator_id`, `RescissionRequest.approved_by` and the waiver's `requested_by`, but it is never compared to any entity's `id`.

One authorization rule is stronger than role membership alone. The operator that approves a rescission must differ from the operator recorded as the requestor; when the approving `X-User-ID` equals the request's `operator_id`, the approval is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES_REQUIRED` and HTTP 403.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection read returns a page of results rather than the whole set. The page is selected with two query parameters: `limit` sets the maximum number of rows returned and defaults to 10, with a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100; `offset` sets the number of leading rows skipped and defaults to 0. The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`, where `total_count` is the size of the full filtered collection and `has_more` indicates whether further rows remain beyond the returned page. Unless a resource-specific rule says otherwise, list endpoints order rows by `created_at` ascending, so pages taken in sequence walk the collection from the oldest row forward.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock and Its Role in Time-Bounded Rules

The service does not read wall-clock time when it evaluates a temporal rule. A logical clock is fixed at `2026-09-01T00:00:00Z` when the server starts, and no operation falls back to real time before the clock is first set. A `PlatformAdmin` operator may move the clock by naming a destination instant in the `to_iso8601` field of the clock-setting capability, and the response reports the resulting `current_utc`. Every rule framed in days, months, windows or expiries is evaluated against this logical clock rather than against the host's clock.

That single decision shapes several workflows. Funding windows, rescue windows, trailing measurement windows, attrition cohort months and the false-alarm measurement window are all read off the logical clock, which lets a test drive the system forward months at a time without waiting. Where a rule speaks of a "month" it means a calendar month label, and where it speaks of a trailing window it means a count of whole calendar months or whole calendar days measured back from the logical clock's current instant. The clock-setting capability is exclusive to `PlatformAdmin` and changes nothing but the clock.

### 2.5. Common Resource Properties and the Aggregate Hierarchy

Every persisted resource carries a common block of server-managed properties. The `id` is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix — a dealer's `id` and an alarm event's `id` are indistinguishable in shape. `created_at` is set once, at creation, as an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. `updated_at` is refreshed whenever the row is mutated, and `version` is an integer that begins at its initial value and is incremented by 1 on every mutation of the row. The `version` field is what the optimistic-concurrency rules of §2.8 compare against, so any write that mutates a row must advance that row's `version`: an operation that touches several rows advances the `version` of each of them.

Resources form a hierarchy rather than a flat table. A `Dealer` owns `DealerPackage` rows, which in turn carry the child `Contract` rows declared on them. A `Contract` is the spine of the operational domain: the `Site` it protects, its `Installation`, its `MonitoringAccount`, its `ServiceVisit` records, its `ContractOwner` segments, its `AssumptionRequest` rows and its `RescissionRequest` rows all hang beneath it. `AlarmEvent` rows hang beneath a `MonitoringAccount`, and each `DispatchRequest` hangs beneath the `AlarmEvent` that prompted it; `AlarmReductionAction` rows also hang beneath the monitoring account. An `AttritionSweep` owns the `AttritionFlag` rows it raises, and each flag points back at the contract that entered attrition. Because the hierarchy is deep, a single business action can mutate rows at several levels; the cascades are described where each workflow is introduced in §3, and each level's `version` and `updated_at` are refreshed as part of the cascade.

Nested collection reads follow the same left-to-right resolution discipline as any other path-scoped operation: a parent identifier that does not resolve to a row fails before any child collection is consulted, and the failure is reported as `ERR_ENTITY_NOT_FOUND` with HTTP 404. A body-supplied foreign key that does not resolve fails separately, reported as `ERR_FK_INTEGRITY_VIOLATION` with HTTP 400.

### 2.6. The Origination Gate Configuration and the Channel Quality Matrix

Not every contract a dealer submits may be funded. A contract that reaches the activation step is judged against a fixed quality configuration, and a contract that fails any gate is frozen rather than activated. The configuration is worth stating once, because the same thresholds recur across origination, assumption and rescission.

A contract is credit-eligible when its `credit_score` is at least 620; a score strictly below 620 is a gate failure reported as `ERR_CREDIT_SCORE_BELOW_FLOOR`. A contract's term is eligible when its `contract_term_months` is at least 36; a shorter term is a gate failure reported as `ERR_TERM_BELOW_FLOOR`. The permitted equipment configuration depends on the origination channel recorded in `source_channel`: the DIRECT channel permits the configurations `BASE`, `PREMIUM` and `ELITE`, while the DEALER channel permits only `BASE` and `PREMIUM`. A configuration outside the channel's permitted set is a gate failure reported as `ERR_EQUIPMENT_CONFIG_NOT_PERMITTED`. Finally, a contract needs a monitoring account already in the `PROVISIONED` state, or an approved onsite-install waiver standing in for it; without either, the gate fails with `ERR_MONITORING_NOT_PROVISIONED`.

The permitted `equipment_config_code` values are exactly `BASE`, `PREMIUM` and `ELITE`, and every contract carries its chosen value through to its installation, where the installation's own `equipment_config_code` is copied from the contract at completion. The channel enumeration itself is exactly `DIRECT` and `DEALER`. A dealer-originated contract must additionally clear its package's funding window: the package's `expires_at` is the approval instant advanced by ten days, and a funding attempt evaluated on the logical clock after that instant is refused with `ERR_FUNDING_WINDOW_EXPIRED`.

Dealer data carries its own bounds. A dealer is registered with a `holdback_rate` expressed as a fraction between 0.0 and 0.5 inclusive; both ends of the range are registrable values, and a value outside it — a rate above 0.5 included — is refused by the ordinary declared-range check as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, so the dedicated `ERR_HOLDBACK_RATE_EXCEEDS_CEILING` identifier is catalogued but is not the one this registration check surfaces. `quality_tier` is exactly one of `TIER_1`, `TIER_2` or `TIER_3`, and it drives the dealer's funding points: a `TIER_1` dealer's packages are funded at 1.03 points, a `TIER_2` dealer's at 1.00 and a `TIER_3` dealer's at 0.97. A credit score is always an integer between 300 and 850 inclusive, and a contract term is always an integer between 12 and 84 months inclusive, regardless of the higher funding floors above.

### 2.7. Monetary Precision and Rounding Discipline

All money is carried as integer cents, never as a floating-point amount, and money-valued fields such as `capex_cents`, `holdback_cents`, `funding_amount_cents`, `equipment_financed_cents`, `equipment_payment_cents`, `billed_cents`, `dealer_sweep_cents`, `holdback_reserve_cents` and `chargeback_balance_due_cents` are whole cents. Where a rule multiplies cents by a fractional rate, the product is rounded to the nearest cent using the round-half-up rule — a value exactly halfway between two cents resolves upward — and the rounded integer cent value is what is stored and what later arithmetic consumes.

Two rules are meaningful only as percentages, and both are reported with 2 decimal places under the same round-half-up rule: the dealer chargeback rate and the false-alarm ratio. Their percent-valued outputs — the chargeback rate that drives a dealer's standing and the `ratio_percent` reported by the false-alarm reads — are rounded half-up to two decimal places. Where a formula states that rounding happens at a named intermediate step, that step's rounding is performed before the value feeds the next step; the equipment-financing split is the clearest case, because its per-installment amount is rounded to cents first and only then multiplied by the number of term months. Rounding the sum instead of the installment would produce a different cent total and is not what this specification requires.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency Control on Funding Writes

Three funding-desk writes serialise against a stale client view. Each of them requires the client to supply the version it last observed for the aggregate being mutated, and each of them rejects the write when that observed version no longer matches the aggregate's current `version`.

Approving a package requires an `expected_dealer_package_version` field carrying the client's last observed version of the target `DealerPackage`. When the package's `version` has advanced since the client read it, the approval is refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` and HTTP 412, so that a stale view cannot approve a package against a contract composition that has already been resubmitted. Funding a package likewise requires `expected_dealer_package_version`; a mismatch with the package's current `version` is refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` and HTTP 412, because funding legitimately supersedes the package posture the client observed. Funding a single direct-channel contract requires `expected_contract_version` carrying the client's last observed version of the target `Contract`; a mismatch with the contract's current `version` is refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` and HTTP 412, so that a stale view cannot fund a contract whose gates have already been re-evaluated.

The version guard is a property of these writes, not of the whole API. Ends not named above do not carry a version field. The guard is compared at the head of each of these writes, before the operation's own state and business guards, so a request that is both stale and out of state reports `ERR_STALE_VERSION` rather than the state error. The field is optional in the sense that a request which omits it entirely is not refused for staleness; a request that supplies it is refused when the supplied value does not equal the target's current `version`. Separately, the dealer chargeback-rate recompute is itself a version-guarded write that carries an `expected_dealer_version` field for the dealer it recalculates; it is the dealer-level analogue of the same discipline and is described with the dealer workflows in §3.

### 2.9. Dealer Financial Position and Standing Derivation

A dealer's financial relationship with the company is tracked in two running balances. The `holdback_reserve_cents` balance is the money the company withholds from what it pays the dealer at funding and has not yet consumed; the `chargeback_balance_due_cents` balance is money the dealer owes the company net of remittances and holdback applications. Both are non-negative integer-cents balances, and each dealer records the running `funded_package_count` of its packages that have reached `FUNDED`. Two further counters retain the numerator and denominator of the dealer's trailing chargeback rate: `chargeback_rate_12mo_sum` holds the numerator `L`, which counts the dealer's packages in state `FUNDED` whose `closed_at` falls inside the trailing twelve logical months, and `funding_package_count_12mo` holds the denominator `D`, which counts the dealer's contracts that are `CANCELLED` from `ACTIVE`, carry a non-null `activated_at`, have a `cancelled_at` inside that same window and whose active lifetime carried at least one valid inadvertent alarm. Because the trailing rate is the one that decides standing, `L` and `D` are defined in full together with that rate in §3.1, and those full definitions are the operative ones.

A dealer's `standing` is one of the three values `ACTIVE`, `WATCH` or `SUSPENDED`, and it is derived — never set directly except by an explicit administrative suspension. The derivation runs on any write that touches `chargeback_balance_due_cents`, `chargeback_rate_12mo_sum` or `funding_package_count_12mo`. When the dealer's `chargeback_balance_due_cents` exceeds 500000 cents, standing becomes `SUSPENDED`. Otherwise, when the denominator `funding_package_count_12mo` is greater than zero and the trailing rate computed from the two counters exceeds 12.00 percent, standing becomes `WATCH`. Otherwise standing is `ACTIVE`.

Standing gates origination, and the gate is enforced through distinct checks. A `SUSPENDED` dealer may not have a package approved — the approval is refused with `ERR_DEALER_STANDING_SUSPENDED`. An attempt to create or submit a package for a dealer whose standing is `SUSPENDED` is refused first, before any package row is written, with `ERR_DEALER_PACKAGE_SUSPENDED`. Separately, a package may not be created or submitted for a dealer whose trailing chargeback rate exceeds the cap of 12.00 percent, which is refused with `ERR_DEALER_CHARGEBACK_RATE_EXCEEDED`; this rate check consults the dealer's retained `L`/`D` counters, so it bites only once a recompute has left a non-zero denominator, and it is evaluated after the standing check on both paths. On package creation the natural-key check that a reference is unique within the dealer is evaluated after these two, so a suspended dealer with a duplicate reference reports `ERR_DEALER_PACKAGE_SUSPENDED` rather than `ERR_PACKAGE_REFERENCE_DUPLICATE`.

The two balances move only through defined movements. A holdback release moves the smaller of the requested `amount_cents` and the current reserve out of `holdback_reserve_cents` and into `chargeback_balance_due_cents`; a holdback reversal moves the smaller of the requested `amount_cents` and the current balance back out of `chargeback_balance_due_cents` and into `holdback_reserve_cents`. Both movements re-derive standing afterwards, and both accept the `amount_cents` they move as their sole input.

### 2.10. Validation Precedence and Rejection Ordering

Requests are validated in a fixed order, and the first check that fails determines the single `error_id` returned. Text order in this document is not the ordering; the ordering is the one stated here, and any implementer that evaluates checks in a different sequence will surface a different error for a request that violates two rules at once.

Authentication is evaluated first: the presence of both headers and the validity of the declared role, failing with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. Authorization follows: whether the acting role is admitted for the operation, failing with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`. Existence checks come next, and three distinct existence rules sit at this level — a path identifier that does not resolve yields `ERR_ENTITY_NOT_FOUND` before any state or business check runs; a body foreign key that does not resolve yields `ERR_FK_INTEGRITY_VIOLATION`; and a funding package that declares no child contract yields `ERR_MONITORING_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND` when an approval or a funding is attempted against it, so the package cannot be evaluated at all. On the two package operations that carry this third rule, the package's own status is in fact consulted before the empty-package rule, so a package whose status does not admit the operation is refused with its state error first and the empty-package rule bites only once the status admits the operation.

State checks follow, failing with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` when the target is not in a state from which the requested transition is declared. Three state errors are specialised because they carry their own identifiers: funding a package from a state other than `APPROVED` reports `ERR_PACKAGE_NOT_APPROVED`; funding a contract from a state other than `PENDING_VALIDATION` reports `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_PENDING`; approving a rescission for a request that is not `REQUESTED`, or after the rescission window has closed, reports `ERR_RESCISSION_WINDOW_CLOSED`; verifying or marking false an alarm that is not `PENDING` reports `ERR_ALARM_ALREADY_VERIFIED`; and any dispatch action attempted from a state that does not declare it reports `ERR_DISPATCH_NOT_REQUESTED`.

Temporal and scheduling checks come fourth, covering the funding window (`ERR_FUNDING_WINDOW_EXPIRED`), the alarm verification and abort windows (`ERR_ALARM_OUTSIDE_VERIFIED_WINDOW`), resource window collisions (`ERR_SERVICE_WINDOW_OVERLAP`) and the relocation radius (`ERR_RESIDENCE_OUTSIDE_SERVICE_RADIUS`). Safety checks come fifth: the suspended-dealer package refusal (`ERR_DEALER_PACKAGE_SUSPENDED`), segregation of duties on rescission (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES_REQUIRED`), the dispatch mass-cancel guard (`ERR_MASS_CANCEL_BLOCKED`) and the false-alarm watch blocking an assumption (`ERR_FALSE_ALARM_WATCH_ACTIVE`). Uniqueness and aggregation constraints come sixth: `ERR_PACKAGE_REFERENCE_DUPLICATE`, `ERR_SWEEP_MONTH_DUPLICATE`, `ERR_NATURAL_KEY_DUPLICATE` and `ERR_NO_FUNDABLE_CONTRACT`. Referential business checks come seventh (`ERR_ASSUMED_CONTRACT_MUST_HAVE_SURVIVING_CONTRACT`, `ERR_ALARM_NOT_FOR_ACCOUNT`), and the fiscal quality gates come eighth (`ERR_CREDIT_SCORE_BELOW_FLOOR`, `ERR_TERM_BELOW_FLOOR`, `ERR_EQUIPMENT_CONFIG_NOT_PERMITTED`, `ERR_MONITORING_NOT_PROVISIONED`, `ERR_DEALER_STANDING_SUSPENDED`, `ERR_DEALER_CHARGEBACK_RATE_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_HOLDBACK_RATE_EXCEEDS_CEILING`, `ERR_CHARGEBACK_WINDOW_CLOSED`, `ERR_DISCLOSURE_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`). The version and shape checks come last: a stale `expected_..._version` value reports `ERR_STALE_VERSION`, and a missing required body field or a value outside its declared type or range reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. On the version-guarded writes the observation is compared before the operation's own state guard, so a request that is both stale and out of state reports `ERR_STALE_VERSION` rather than the state error.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Dealer Onboarding, Standing and the Chargeback Rate

A `ChannelManager` brings a dealer onto the programme by registering it with a `dealer_code` used as the dealer's programme identity, the registered `legal_name`, the fraction of remittance the company will retain as `holdback_rate`, and a `quality_tier`. On registration the dealer's `standing` is initialised to `ACTIVE`, its `chargeback_balance_due_cents` and `holdback_reserve_cents` balances both begin at zero, and its `funded_package_count` begins at zero. The `dealer_code` is not merely a label: together with the package reference it forms the natural key of a funded package, so the same code reappears whenever the dealer's packages are identified.

Standing is largely derived rather than assigned, and the derivation is worth following because a forbidden value can be reached three separate ways. The ordinary path runs when a `FundingAnalyst` asks for the dealer's chargeback rate to be recomputed. First the analyst recomputes two quantities over the trailing twelve logical months ending at the current logical clock. The numerator `L` counts the dealer's packages in state `FUNDED` whose `closed_at` falls inside that window. The denominator `D` counts the dealer's contracts, across all packages, that are in contract state `CANCELLED` with a recorded `cancelled_from_status` of `ACTIVE` and a non-null `activated_at`, whose `cancelled_at` falls inside the same window, and whose active lifetime carried at least one valid inadvertent alarm — a contract is counted toward the denominator only if it has at least one alarm event with a non-null `verified_at`, an outcome of `VERIFIED` or `FALSE_ALARM`, and an `occurred_at` falling between its `activated_at` and its `cancelled_at` inclusive. With both quantities in hand the rate is the numerator scaled to a percent by the denominator, using the ratio of `L` to `D` times one hundred — and it is defined to be 0.00 percent when the denominator is zero. The rate is expressed in percent with two decimal places under round-half-up.

The recompute then writes `chargeback_rate_12mo_sum` to `L` and `funding_package_count_12mo` to `D`, and the standing derivation of §2.9 runs on those fresh counters. A dealer whose recomputed rate is strictly greater than 12.00 percent moves to `WATCH`; a dealer whose rate is at or below 12.00 percent moves back to `ACTIVE`. The recompute request carries an `expected_dealer_version` field, so a stale observation of the dealer is refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` rather than recomputed against a dealer row the client has not seen.

Administrative suspension is the second path and reaches `SUSPENDED` without any rate arithmetic: a `FundingAnalyst` may suspend a dealer that is `ACTIVE` or `WATCH`, and the operation requires nothing beyond authorization because it is an explicit operator act. The third path is automatic and is the one worth remembering — a dealer whose `chargeback_balance_due_cents` exceeds 500000 cents is derived to `SUSPENDED` by any write that touches its balances, without any operator deciding it. A suspended dealer blocks its own origination pipeline in the two ways already described in §2.9, and a dealer already in `WATCH` blocks nothing by standing alone; it is blocked only because the rate that produced `WATCH` also trips the rate cap at package creation and submission.

Reinstatement is the only route back from `SUSPENDED`, and it is a two-condition gate rather than a single one. The dealer's `chargeback_balance_due_cents` must be exactly zero, and the freshly recomputed chargeback rate must be at or below 12.00 percent — in which case the dealer returns to `ACTIVE` — or strictly greater than 12.00 percent, in which case the dealer returns to `WATCH`. A reinstatement attempted while the balance is non-zero has not cleared the first condition and cannot succeed: both that attempt and an attempt against a dealer that is not currently `SUSPENDED` are refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, and neither moves the row.

### 3.2. Opening and Negotiating a Funding Package

Dealer origination happens through packages. A `Dealer` or a `FundingAnalyst` opens one by naming a `package_reference` that is unique within the selling dealer, and may additionally name the owning `dealer_id`; when that field is supplied it designates the owning dealer, and when it is omitted the acting dealer itself owns the package. The natural key is the pair of the dealer and the `package_reference`, so a second package with a reference the dealer has already used is refused with `ERR_PACKAGE_REFERENCE_DUPLICATE`. A new package opens in `DRAFT` — the value its `package_status` field takes — with `funded_count` and `declined_count` both at zero and an empty `submitted_contract_count`. Its three aggregate rollups `package_capex_cents`, `package_holdback_cents` and `package_funding_amount_cents` stay at zero until children have funded, and the only way those rollups move is through a child contract that has reached `FUNDED`. Two safety refusals guard the very act of opening or submitting: a package may be created or submitted only when the dealer's chargeback rate does not exceed the cap, refused with `ERR_DEALER_CHARGEBACK_RATE_EXCEEDED`, and may not be created or submitted at all for a dealer whose standing is `SUSPENDED`, refused with `ERR_DEALER_PACKAGE_SUSPENDED` before any package row is written. The two are ordered — the standing refusal fires before the rate refusal on `SUSPENDED`/over-cap combinations — and both are evaluated before the reference-duplicate check. When creation names no `dealer_id` the acting `Dealer` must be the owning dealer; a create request that omits `dealer_id` when the acting role is not `Dealer` is a shape failure reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and one that is not carried by the acting `Dealer` because no such dealer row exists reports `ERR_ENTITY_NOT_FOUND`.

Declaring contracts onto the package is part of the same negotiation. A `Dealer` or a `FundingAnalyst` declares a contract by supplying the `customer_id` the contract is sold to, the `equipment_config_code`, the `credit_score`, the `contract_term_months`, the `monthly_monitoring_cents`, and optionally the `financed_charge_at_apr_cents`; the protected premises is supplied inline on the same request through `premises_address_line`, `premises_city`, `premises_state_code` and `premises_postal_code`, and the installation may be scheduled in the same breath by supplying `installation_scheduled_at`, `installation_duration_minutes` and `installation_engineer_id`. Declaration validates its payload through the same shared origination path as direct origination, evaluating field shape before any row is written; among the inline premises fields the `premises_state_code` must be exactly two characters. The declared contract is created in `PENDING_VALIDATION` on the DEALER channel, its monitoring account is created `UNPROVISIONED`, an installation is created `SCHEDULED` when a schedule instant was supplied, and the package's `submitted_contract_count` and `version` both advance. When the inline schedule names an engineer whose crew already holds an overlapping non-failed installation slot, the declaration is refused with `ERR_SERVICE_WINDOW_OVERLAP` before any row is written. Because the same payload shape creates a direct-channel contract in §3.4, the fields mean the same thing on both routes; the channel is what differs.

Submission moves a `DRAFT` package to `UNDER_REVIEW`. The `Dealer` alone may submit or withdraw — those two acts are the dealer's exclusive negotiation levers — and submission requires at least one child contract to exist on the package. Submitting stamps the package's `submitted_at` from the logical clock and advances its `version`. Withdrawal returns an `UNDER_REVIEW` package to `DRAFT`, permitted only while no child contract has reached a terminal contract state, and it clears `submitted_at` and advances the `version` again. The pairing is deliberately cyclic: `DRAFT` to `UNDER_REVIEW` to `DRAFT` may repeat without bound, and every submission re-runs the whole-package evaluation afresh, so a dealer that fixes a rejected contract and resubmits is re-evaluated from scratch rather than inheriting the previous verdict.

### 3.3. Package Approval, Holdback Ceiling and the Funding Posture

Approval is the funding desk's turn. A `FundingAnalyst` approves an `UNDER_REVIEW` package; the request carries `expected_dealer_package_version`, so an approval attempted against a package whose `version` has moved on is refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` and HTTP 412. Two business preconditions must also hold: the package reference must be unique within the dealer — the same natural key rule that guards creation — and the dealer's standing must not be `SUSPENDED`, refused with `ERR_DEALER_STANDING_SUSPENDED`. Approving then computes the holdback ceiling and the funding posture, stamps the package's funding deadline, and advances its `version`.

The holdback ceiling binds how much reserve the company will accept on the package. The ceiling is the smaller of two amounts: fifteen percent of the package's total capitalised equipment amount `package_capex_cents`, and the dealer's `holdback_rate` applied to that same package total — both rounded half-up to whole cents. The amount actually submitted by the children is the sum, over the package's child contracts, of the dealer's `holdback_rate` applied to each child's `capex_cents` and rounded half-up per child. Because a child's holdback is applied only once it has been computed, an approval first runs the funding fee against any child still carrying a zero `capex_cents`, so the ceiling and the submitted sum are taken over real capitalised amounts. When the submitted sum exceeds the ceiling, the approved holdback that is reported and recorded is the ceiling itself, not the submitted sum; otherwise it is the submitted sum. Approval records both quantities on the package — the ceiling as `allowed_holdback_rate_ceiling_cents` and the approved figure as `approved_holdback_cents` — and the posture pass writes the postural holdback into the package's `package_holdback_cents` alongside `package_posture`; the write of that posture into `package_holdback_cents` is what makes the package's recorded holdback the aggregate of its funded children rather than the per-child sum.

The funding posture is an aggregation over the package's children, and it is the pivot on which the whole-package verdict turns. It considers every child contract whose contract status is not `CANCELLED` as a funded child, and separately collects the children that are `CANCELLED` with a recorded `cancelled_from_status` of `PENDING_VALIDATION` as the declined children. If any declined child carries a `validation_failure_code` of `ERR_CREDIT_SCORE_BELOW_FLOOR`, the posture is `DECLINED` and the whole package is refused funding. Otherwise the posture is `FUNDED` when at least one funded child exists and `DECLINED` when none does. The aggregate holdback the posture returns is the sum of `holdback_cents` over the funded children, expressed as exact integer cents.

The two shapes this produces matter for what happens at funding time. In the partial case — at least one child survived the gates and no declined child froze on the credit floor — the posture is `FUNDED` even though `declined_count` is non-zero, and the package funds with a mixed population. In the whole-declined case — a declined child froze on `ERR_CREDIT_SCORE_BELOW_FLOOR` — the posture is `DECLINED`, and a funding attempt then freezes every remaining `PENDING_VALIDATION` child: the credit offender carries `ERR_CREDIT_SCORE_BELOW_FLOOR`, and each other child is frozen with `ERR_PACKAGE_DECLINED` because the whole-package posture declined it. A fund attempt in a `DECLINED` posture is refused outright with `ERR_NO_FUNDABLE_CONTRACT`.

Approval sets the package's `expires_at` to the approval instant advanced by `funding_window_days`, which is ten days, and this deadline is the package's funding window. A package that has been approved but not yet funded may be reopened by the funding desk, returning it to `UNDER_REVIEW`, but only while the package's `funded_count` is zero; reopening clears `expires_at` and advances the `version`. A package under review may instead be declined outright by the funding desk, which requires nothing beyond authorization: declining closes the package at the logical clock, sets its `closed_at`, and cascades a decline to every child contract still `PENDING_VALIDATION`, freezing each one. A package under review whose composition is empty is refused on an approval attempt with `ERR_MONITORING_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND` rather than being approved, and the same empty-composition refusal guards a funding attempt once the package has been approved.

One deadline is handled without an operator. A package left in `APPROVED` past its `expires_at` — that is, whose `expires_at` is strictly before the current logical clock — is observed to have expired opportunistically by the next funding write that touches it, with no wall-clock waiting: the check compares the package's `expires_at` against the logical clock at the moment of the request. When that happens the package moves to `EXPIRED`, closes at the sweep-observed expiry instant, advances its `version`, and cascades a deadline miss to every child contract still `PENDING_VALIDATION`, freezing each with `ERR_FUNDING_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. This is the second way a pending child can be frozen without anyone deciding to decline it.

### 3.4. Contract Origination and the Activation Gates

A direct-channel contract is originated by a `ChannelManager`, a `FundingAnalyst` or a `Customer` through its own origination act, with the same payload shape used to declare a contract onto a package: the `customer_id`, `equipment_config_code`, `credit_score`, `contract_term_months`, `monthly_monitoring_cents` and optional `financed_charge_at_apr_cents`, the inline premises through `premises_address_line`, `premises_city`, `premises_state_code` and `premises_postal_code`, and the optional scheduling triple of `installation_scheduled_at`, `installation_duration_minutes` and `installation_engineer_id`. The contract is created in `PENDING_VALIDATION` with `source_channel` set to `DIRECT`, a null `dealer_id` and a null `dealer_package_id` because neither is supplied on the direct route, its monitoring account is created `UNPROVISIONED`, and an installation is created `SCHEDULED` when a schedule was supplied. The premises becomes a `Site` row recorded against the contract through its `contract_id`, whose `address_line`, `city`, `state_code` and `postal_code` record the address and whose `occupancy_status` begins `OCCUPIED`; the `state_code` is a two-letter code. The customer may be registered first by a `ChannelManager`, a `FundingAnalyst` or a `PlatformAdmin` with a `full_name`, an `email` and a `phone`; a new subscriber begins in the status `PENDING_ACTIVATION` — the value its `status` field takes — and its `email` must be unique across all customers, a clash being refused with `ERR_NATURAL_KEY_DUPLICATE`. Every contract's server-side accounting surface is the same set of fields regardless of channel: the `contract_status` it currently holds, the `contract_number` the company stamps at funding, the `cancelled_from_status` and `cancelled_at` that recall how and when it ended, the `activated_at` that records when it reached `ACTIVE`, the `attrition_checked_at` of its last sweep and the `monitoring_hold_until` that exempts it from one, its `assumption_count`, the `funding_points` and `warranty_months` set at funding, the `validation_failure_code` a freeze leaves behind, the `chargeback_exempt` and `equipment_financing_disclosed` flags, and the `dealer_holdback_sweep_cents` and `holdback_remaining_cents` that track its post-warranty drawdown.

Activation of any contract is gated on equipment and on monitoring, and the gates are evaluated in the fixed order of §2.10. The contract quality configuration of §2.6 supplies four of them: a `credit_score` of at least 620, a `contract_term_months` of at least 36, an `equipment_config_code` on the channel's permitted set, and a monitoring account already `PROVISIONED` or an approved onsite-install waiver in its place. A dealer-channel contract carries a fifth gate, because it may only be activated while its parent package is `APPROVED` and the funding instant is at or before the package's `expires_at`; the funding window thus guards the contract as well as the package.

Activation is performed either as part of funding a whole package or, for a direct-channel contract, on its own. Funding a single direct-channel contract requires the contract's `expected_contract_version` and refuses a stale observation with `ERR_STALE_VERSION`; funding a package requires the package's `expected_dealer_package_version` for the same reason. A contract that funds successfully runs the equipment-financing split against its frozen credit band, which fixes its `equipment_financed_cents` and `equipment_payment_cents` and sets `equipment_financing_disclosed` to false; runs the funding fee, which fixes its `capex_cents`, its `holdback_cents`, its `holdback_remaining_cents` at the same amount, and its `funding_amount_cents`; sets its `funding_points` from the dealer's `quality_tier`; stamps its `contract_number` — the company's system identifier, minted from the logical clock's month label and a running sequence, and never supplied by the originator; moves its `contract_status` to `ACTIVE` and stamps `activated_at` from the logical clock; and moves the subscriber's status to `ACTIVE`. A contract that fails a gate instead moves to `CANCELLED`, with the failing gate's name frozen into its `validation_failure_code`, its `cancelled_from_status` recorded as `PENDING_VALIDATION`, and its `cancelled_at` stamped from the logical clock. On the single-contract fund route this freeze is committed first and the request is then answered with the standard error envelope of §2.2 — the response's `response_type` is `error`, its `data.error_id` is the failing gate's name so that the frozen `validation_failure_code` and the reported `error_id` agree, and its HTTP status is that gate's status in the §5 catalogue — rather than a success body carrying a validation-failure field.

The funding fee is worth stating in full because it decides what the dealer is paid. The contract's capitalised equipment amount `capex_cents` is looked up from its `equipment_config_code`: the `BASE` configuration carries 14900 cents, `PREMIUM` carries 24900 cents and `ELITE` carries 34900 cents. The `holdback_cents` retained by the company is the dealer's `holdback_rate` applied to `capex_cents`, rounded half-up to whole cents; `holdback_remaining_cents` starts equal to `holdback_cents`; and the `funding_amount_cents` remitted to the originating party is `capex_cents` minus `holdback_cents`. Funding also credits the dealer's running `holdback_reserve_cents` balance with the contract's `holdback_cents`, because that reserve is the money the company withholds from what it pays the dealer at funding; without that credit the reserve, and with it the post-warranty, chargeback and rescission waterworks that draw against it, would never move. The contract's `funding_points` is set from the dealer's tier map of §2.6 — 1.03, 1.00 or 0.97 — and is zero for a direct-channel contract. All of this arithmetic is in exact integer cents.

Freezing a contract out of `PENDING_VALIDATION` is a distinct event from cancelling a live one, and it is treated as a package-level decline rather than as attrition. When a child is frozen by a package fund or decline, the parent package's `declined_count` advances by one and the package's `version` advances as well. No dealer chargeback arises from a frozen contract, and the origination cascade leaves the dealer row untouched: a frozen child neither runs a dealer-side recompute nor moves the dealer's balances, so the freeze is observably a contract-and-package event only. The frozen child is identified by its `validation_failure_code`, whose permitted values are exactly `ERR_CREDIT_SCORE_BELOW_FLOOR`, `ERR_TERM_BELOW_FLOOR`, `ERR_EQUIPMENT_CONFIG_NOT_PERMITTED`, `ERR_MONITORING_NOT_PROVISIONED`, `ERR_FUNDING_WINDOW_EXPIRED` and `ERR_PACKAGE_DECLINED`. Funding a package moves the dealer too: it refreshes the dealer's `funded_package_count` to the running count of its `FUNDED` packages and advances the dealer's `version`. A direct-channel contract carries no dealer, so on the single-contract fund route only the contract and its subscriber move.

### 3.5. Installation, Monitoring Transfer and the Financing Disclosure

Equipment reaches the subscriber's premises through an `Installation` owned by the contract. A `ServiceCoordinator` schedules one by naming the `contract_id`, a `scheduled_at` instant, the `installation_engineer_id` whose crew will perform the work, and optionally a slot `duration_minutes`, which defaults to 180 minutes both here and when the same triple is supplied inline at origination; a `scheduled_at` supplied without its engineer identifier is a shape failure reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The crew is a resource whose windows may not collide: a newly scheduled installation must not overlap another non-cancelled slot of the same engineer, and the collision is reported with `ERR_SERVICE_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. The overlap test uses the half-open interval rule, so slots that merely abut do not collide, and it treats every slot that is not `FAILED` as occupying its engineer. An installation begins `SCHEDULED` and carries its `equipment_config_code`, copied from the contract when it completes.

Two of the installation's three terminal outcomes depend on the monitoring side of the business, because a contract may not be activated until its equipment is installed and a monitoring transfer may not be committed against nothing. A `MonitoringOperator` triggers the central-station transfer for the contract's monitoring account, which moves the account to `PROVISIONED` — the value its `provision_state` takes — sets its `activation_gate_met` flag true, stamps its `monitoring_account_number` — unique across all accounts, so a collision is refused with `ERR_NATURAL_KEY_DUPLICATE` — records the `central_station_host` provisioned for the account, and advances the account's `version`. The account itself is keyed back to the contract through its `contract_id`, and the transfer may proceed only once the contract has a current `OCCUPIED` site on file; a transfer attempted with no such site is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The transfer proceeds only from `UNPROVISIONED`, so a second trigger against an account that is already `PROVISIONED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` as well. The transfer is also what commits the contract's installation: it marks every still-`SCHEDULED` installation of the contract `activation_committed` true, and that flag is what later makes the installation's completion — and its failure requeue — safe. This provisioning is what satisfies the monitoring gate of §3.4.

When an installation completes, the commit is what makes it safe. A `ServiceCoordinator` completes a `SCHEDULED` installation only once the associated monitoring transfer has been committed — that is, once either the installation's own `activation_committed` flag or the account's `activation_gate_met` flag is set, both of which the transfer sets; a completion attempted before that is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The completion re-runs the equipment-financing split, persists the contract's `equipment_financed_cents` and `equipment_payment_cents` with `equipment_financing_disclosed` still false, moves the installation to `COMPLETED` and stamps the installation's `equipment_config_code` from the contract, refreshes the contract's `updated_at`, and advances both the installation's and the contract's `version`. Completion is also the moment the equipment-financing disclosure is issued: the contract's `equipment_financing_disclosed` becomes true, and the disclosure records the contract's `equipment_financed_cents` and `monthly_monitoring_cents` alongside `financed_payment_cents` and a per-installment-rounded `total_of_payments_cents`. The disclosure's amounts are snapped at issuance, and a later attempt to issue it when the contract's `equipment_financed_cents` or `monthly_monitoring_cents` no longer match the snapped values is refused with `ERR_DISCLOSURE_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`.

The split itself deserves care because the ordering of its rounding is pinned. The monitoring share of the contract is sixty percent of `capex_cents`, rounded half-up, plus a dealer onboarding fee of 149 cents. The financed equipment amount `equipment_financed_cents` is two percent of that monitoring share, rounded half-up. The monitored service amount is what remains of the monitoring share once the equipment amount is taken out of it, and the monthly `equipment_payment_cents` is `equipment_financed_cents` divided by `contract_term_months`, rounded half-up to whole cents. The disclosure's `total_of_payments_cents` is the per-installment amount multiplied by the term — the installment is rounded to cents first and only then multiplied, because rounding the sum instead produces a different cent total. Re-running the split at disclosure time for a contract whose term changed after funding keeps the funded `equipment_financed_cents` but recomputes `equipment_payment_cents` against the new term, which is why the two snapshots can disagree.

Failure is the third outcome, and it is where the commit matters most. A `ServiceCoordinator` reports an installation failure, optionally supplying a `reason` that is recorded on the installation, and the behaviour forks on the installation's own `activation_committed` flag — a flag the monitoring transfer sets, and one that, once this operation has failed an installation, stays true so that a later reschedule cannot silently discard committed work. A failure may be reported only against a `SCHEDULED` installation, and a report against any other state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. When `activation_committed` is false, the installation moves to `FAILED` and the parent contract's `version` advances by one through a task-bubble. When `activation_committed` is true, the same request requeues the installation rather than failing it: the installation's `install_status` stays `SCHEDULED` and only its own `version` advances, so work already committed to the central station is never silently discarded.

An installation may also be excused its provisioning requirement through a waiver. A `ServiceCoordinator` or a `ComplianceOfficer` requests an onsite-install waiver against an installation, supplying the `requested_by` operator identifier and an `onsite_install` flag; the flag must be a boolean, and a non-boolean is a shape failure reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. When `onsite_install` is true the contract's `chargeback_exempt` is set true and the contract's `version` advances; the waiver is what stands in for a provisioned account at the activation gate and what later exempts the contract from any chargeback. A waiver may be recorded while the installation is `SCHEDULED`, `FAILED` or `COMPLETED`, and one that is requested against an installation in any other state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. An installation requires a `contract_id`, a `scheduled_at`, an `installation_engineer_id` and optionally a `duration_minutes` of its own, and it occupies a crew window that third parties must avoid.

### 3.6. Attrition Sweeps, Delinquency and Service Restoration

A contract that has activated can still be pulled back into arrears by a monthly sweep. A `FundingAnalyst` runs the attrition sweep for a logical month by naming a `sweep_month` in `YYYY-MM` form. Each month may be swept at most once, so a second sweep for a month already swept is refused with `ERR_SWEEP_MONTH_DUPLICATE`. The sweep is a chronological pass over the whole book of contracts rather than over a single dealer's rows — direct-channel contracts are swept alongside dealer-originated ones — and its cohort rule is what decides who is eligible: a contract enters the sweep when its cohort month — the month of its `activated_at` — is at or before the sweep month.

The pass walks the contracts in order of `created_at` ascending, and visits only those whose status is `ACTIVE`, so a contract already `DELINQUENT`, `CANCELLED` or `ASSUMED` is not re-examined on the flagging side. For each `ACTIVE` contract it first skips any whose cohort month is after the sweep month, then applies a hold exemption: a contract whose `monitoring_hold_until` is set to an instant after the sweep instant is skipped, which is how a service or administrative hold keeps a contract out of an attrition pass it would otherwise enter. Each contract that survives both filters has its `id` appended to the sweep's `flagged_contract_ids` array in that same ascending order, the sweep's `delinquent_count` advances, the contract's `contract_status` moves to `DELINQUENT` and its `monitoring_hold_until` is set to the sweep instant. The append itself is not inert: each id appended is the trigger for a `SUSPEND` cascade on that contract's monitoring account, which moves the account from `PROVISIONED` to `SUSPENDED` — an account in any other provision state is left as it stands. Each append raises an `AttritionFlag` whose `attrition_sweep_id` names the sweep that raised it, whose `contract_id` names the contract entering attrition, whose `cohort_month` records the logical month of that contract's `activated_at` as `YYYY-MM`, and whose `flagged_at` is stamped from the logical clock. The contract's `attrition_checked_at` is stamped from the logical clock and its `version` advances.

The same pass then looks for recoveries. Walking the contracts in `created_at` ascending order again, any contract already in `DELINQUENT` whose monitoring account carries an open reduction action of type `SERVICE_REQUIRED` is returned to `ACTIVE`, and the sweep's `resumed_count` advances. The sweep closes by recording its `evaluated_count` as the number of contracts whose cohort month equals the sweep month, and it stamps its own `ran_at` and advances its `version`. A sweep thus both flags and unflags within one pass, which is why a month's sweep can leave the book smaller than the naive cohort count would suggest.

Delinquency is reversible by a service restore rather than by a second sweep. A `ServiceCoordinator` restores service on a `DELINQUENT` contract when an open reduction action of type `SERVICE_REQUIRED` exists on the contract's monitoring account, which resolves that action and stamps its `resolved_at`; a restore attempted against a contract that is not `DELINQUENT`, or one whose account carries no open `SERVICE_REQUIRED` action, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The same call reprovisions the monitoring account to `PROVISIONED` and advances the account's `version`, and it returns the contract to `ACTIVE` and advances the contract's `version`. Each delinquency also has a measurable age: while a contract is in `DELINQUENT`, its delinquency is reported as the whole calendar days between its `attrition_checked_at` and the current logical instant, and it reports as zero for any contract that is not delinquent. The age is read from the contract as an object carrying the contract's `contract_id` and a `delinquency_days` whole-day count, and the read itself mutates nothing.

### 3.7. Alarms, Verification and Dispatch Coordination

The monitoring station records signals against a provisioned account. A `MonitoringOperator` records an alarm by naming the `monitoring_account_id`, the `signal_source`, the `occurred_at` instant at which the signal was received, and optionally a client-supplied `entered_at_utc` marking when the alarm was logged; when that optional instant is omitted the server substitutes `occurred_at` as the entered instant and stores that substituted value in `entered_at_utc`, so an omitted `entered_at_utc` reads back as the `occurred_at` instant rather than as null. A new alarm begins in the verification state `PENDING` — the value its `verification_outcome` field takes — with `verified_at` null and both of its counting flags false except where the ratio window below applies. For the trailing-window arithmetic, an alarm is known to the window when its `occurred_at` falls inside the window from thirty days before the current logical instant through the current instant inclusive, and such an alarm is marked `counted_for_window_total` true so it can serve as the denominator immediately; an alarm that falls outside the window begins with that flag false.

Verification is bounded in time. A `MonitoringOperator` records the outcome of a `PENDING` alarm as either `VERIFIED` or `FALSE_ALARM`, supplying the result through the `outcome` field. Verification is permitted only while the alarm's `entered_at_utc` lies inside the inclusive verified window running from thirty seconds before `occurred_at` through one hundred and twenty seconds after it; an instant outside that window is refused with `ERR_ALARM_OUTSIDE_VERIFIED_WINDOW`. Verifying an alarm that is not `PENDING` is refused with `ERR_ALARM_ALREADY_VERIFIED`. A successful verification stamps `verified_at` from the logical clock, sets the alarm's `verification_outcome`, and sets `counted_for_window_total` true. The two outcomes then diverge on the second flag: a `VERIFIED` outcome sets `counted_for_false_alarm` true only if the account was `PROVISIONED` at the alarm's `occurred_at`, because the flag is what feeds the numerator of the false-alarm ratio; a `FALSE_ALARM` outcome leaves `counted_for_false_alarm` false, because a confirmed false alarm is not a verified signal.

The false-alarm ratio is a read against a trailing window and it drives a reduction measure. Over the window from thirty logical days back through the current instant, the account's known alarms are counted on `occurred_at`, and its verified signals are counted as those alarms in the window that have a non-null `verified_at`, an outcome of `VERIFIED` or `FALSE_ALARM`, and `counted_for_false_alarm` true. The ratio is the verified signal count as a percent of the known alarm count, and it is defined to be 0.00 percent when the known count is zero; like the dealer rate, it is carried in percent with two decimal places under round-half-up. The ratio is computed on verification and recomputed on each read, and the read reports it as `ratio_percent` alongside `known_alarm_count` and `verified_signal_count`. The cap on this ratio is 35.00 percent, but the ratio itself does not raise a service action: the service action belongs to the abuse path, not to the ratio path.

Alarms are also subject to a compliance overlay. A `ComplianceOfficer` may place a legal hold on a verified alarm, which sets `counted_for_false_alarm` false and advances the alarm's `version`, removing it from the verified signal count; releasing the hold restores the flag to true when the alarm had been counted for its window, and advances the `version` again. Both the hold and its release apply only to an alarm that has been verified — that is, whose outcome is `VERIFIED` or `FALSE_ALARM` — and a hold or a release requested against a still-`PENDING` alarm is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. An alarm already verified cannot be verified a second time, so the legal hold is the only lever that changes an alarm's counting after verification.

Dispatch is the operational response, and it can be raised either from an alarm's result or in one step. A `MonitoringOperator` requests dispatch for a pending alarm by naming its `alarm_event_id`; the request verifies the alarm as `VERIFIED`, stamps `verified_at`, sets its counting flags exactly as a verification would, and creates a `DispatchRequest` in `REQUESTED`. An alarm event named on a dispatch must belong to the account the dispatch is raised against, and a mismatch is refused with `ERR_ALARM_NOT_FOR_ACCOUNT`; a request against an alarm that is not `PENDING` is refused with `ERR_ALARM_ALREADY_VERIFIED`. The dispatch's `abort_reason` begins as an empty string and its `dispatched_at` as null. A `DispatchRequest` moves through a short machine whose current node is its `dispatch_status` and whose creation instant is its `requested_at`: a `MonitoringOperator` releases a `REQUESTED` dispatch to the central station, which is permitted only while the monitoring account is `PROVISIONED` — a release attempted while the account is not `PROVISIONED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — stamps `dispatched_at` and advances the `version`; a dispatched call may be completed, which advances the `version`; and either a `REQUESTED` or a `DISPATCHED` dispatch may be aborted or closed, recording the `abort_reason` and advancing the `version`. Completion is reachable only from `DISPATCHED`, and an action attempted from the wrong state is refused with `ERR_DISPATCH_NOT_REQUESTED`.

The abort path carries two clocks and one guard. An abort of a `DISPATCHED` call is permitted only strictly before the pre-arrival abort window closes, which is sixty seconds after the alarm's `entered_at_utc`; an abort after that instant is refused with `ERR_ALARM_OUTSIDE_VERIFIED_WINDOW`. Closing a `REQUESTED` dispatch has no such timing precondition — it requires nothing beyond authorization. Both paths, however, count toward the mass-cancel guard: once the count of `ABORTED` or `CLOSED` dispatches for the alarm's monitoring account at or after the alarm's `occurred_at` has already reached three, a further abort is refused with `ERR_MASS_CANCEL_BLOCKED`. the guard counts a closed requested dispatch exactly as it counts an aborted dispatched one: any dispatch whose `dispatch_status` is `ABORTED` or `CLOSED` counts, and the limit is three.

False alarms produce reduction measures of their own. A `ServiceCoordinator` or a `MonitoringOperator` may raise a reduction action against a monitoring account by naming the account and an `action_type` of `WATCH`, `TRAINING_REQUIRED` or `SERVICE_REQUIRED`; the action begins in the reduction-action state `OPEN` — the value its `action_status` field takes — with its `triggered_at` stamped, and it remains `OPEN` until its `resolved_at` is stamped. The station also raises one automatically when a verification pushes the recomputed false-alarm ratio past its cap: an `SERVICE_REQUIRED` action is created in `OPEN` on the affected account. This automatic raise is driven by the record-verification act itself, and it raises nothing when the account already carries an open `SERVICE_REQUIRED` action, so at most one such action stands open per account. An open `SERVICE_REQUIRED` action is a hard block on more than one workflow — it is the precondition for a service restore in §3.6, it participates in the post-warranty sweeps of §3.8, and it blocks an assumption in §3.9 with `ERR_FALSE_ALARM_WATCH_ACTIVE`.

### 3.8. Post-Warranty Service, Dealer Holdback Sweeps and the Warranty Line

Every activated contract carries a warranty window measured from funding: `warranty_months` defaults to six, and the window end is the contract's `activated_at` advanced by that many months. Work performed inside that line and work performed after it are billed to different parties, and the boundary is decided once, when a visit is scheduled — the visit's `warranty_status_at_visit` is true exactly when its `scheduled_at` is at or before the warranty window end. That derived flag, not the perform instant, is what routes the money.

A `ServiceCoordinator` requests a service or repair visit under a contract by naming the `contract_id`, the `scheduled_at` instant, the `work_type` of `REPAIR` or `WARRANTY_REPAIR`, the `technician_id` whose calendar will hold it, an optional `duration_minutes` and an optional `allowed_duration_minutes`. The visit begins in the visit state `REQUESTED`. Scheduling moves it to `SCHEDULED` — the value its `visit_status` field then holds — and is permitted only when the technician's service windows hold for the requested instant under the half-open overlap rule — a collision with another non-cancelled slot of the same technician is refused with `ERR_SERVICE_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. The slot a visit occupies for that overlap test is set by the `allowed_duration_minutes` field, which is a requested slot length used solely for that detection and defaults to 60 minutes; the visit's own `duration_minutes` is the work's notional length, defaults to 120 minutes, and does not size the occupied window. A scheduled visit may still be cancelled, but only strictly before its `scheduled_at`; a cancellation at or after that instant, like one attempted from a state other than `SCHEDULED`, is not permitted and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

Performing a scheduled visit derives both the billing route and the amount. The flat dealer visit fee is 89 cents, and it is recorded in the visit's `billed_cents` on either billing route. When the visit's `warranty_status_at_visit` is true the visit is inside warranty, so its `billing_route` is `CUSTOMER`, a flat after-hours fee is charged, and `dealer_sweep_cents` is zero because the dealer is not billed and no reserve sweep runs. When the flag is false the visit is post-warranty, so its `billing_route` is `DEALER_HOLDBACK`, `dealer_sweep_cents` equals the 89-cent visit fee, and the post-warranty waterfall below draws on the contract's own post-warranty base. Performing the visit stamps `performed_at` from the logical clock and advances both the visit's `version` and the dealer's `version`.

Post-warranty work reaches the dealer's reserve through a waterfall. On the post-warranty path the visit is billed against the contract's post-warranty base, `post_warranty_cents`, which is three times the contract's `capex_cents`. The draw against the dealer's reserve is twenty percent of that base, rounded half-up to whole cents, and it is applied only up to what the dealer's `holdback_reserve_cents` can cover: the applied amount is the smaller of the rounded draw and the reserve. The reserve is reduced by the applied amount, the contract's `holdback_remaining_cents` is set to the reserve as it now stands, and the contract's `dealer_holdback_sweep_cents` advances by the applied amount. If the draw exceeds what the reserve could cover, the remainder is added to the dealer's `chargeback_balance_due_cents` as a cash recovery case; otherwise the case is a holdback-only recovery. The dealer's `chargeback_balance_due_cents` having moved, the standing derivation of §2.9 runs on the new balances. A post-warranty visit also resolves, after the sweep, any open `SERVICE_REQUIRED` reduction action on the account, which is how a service reconnect in §3.6 becomes reachable.

The same sweep runs on a much larger base when a contract is cancelled. A contract whose `post_warranty_cents` is strictly greater than the sweep threshold of 15000 cents has the post-warranty service sweep run against the dealer's holdback reserve at cancellation time, using that contract's post-warranty base rather than a single visit's; a contract at or below that threshold is skipped entirely, and its sweep amount is zero. Because the post-warranty base is three times the contract's `capex_cents`, every contract that has funded clears this gate comfortably, so in practice the threshold admits every contract that carries a non-zero `capex_cents`. Applying that sweep also resolves any open `SERVICE_REQUIRED` reduction action on the account and re-derives the dealer's standing.

### 3.9. Relocation, Assumption and the Ownership Ledger

Contracts change hands when a subscriber relocates, and the transfer is recorded as a ledger of ownership segments rather than as an overwrite. Every contract carries a `ContractOwner` segment naming the `customer_id` that currently holds it, with a `started_at` from the logical clock, an `ownership_status` of `CURRENT` or `SUPERSEDED`, and an `ended_at` that stays null while the segment is current; a segment produced by an assumption names the originating `assumption_request_id`, and the original owner's segment leaves that field null. Segments are superseded, never deleted, so a contract that has been assumed three times carries four segments with exactly one in `CURRENT`.

A relocation opens with an assumption negotiation. A `Customer`, a `FundingAnalyst` or a `ServiceCoordinator` opens a request by naming the `contract_id`, the `incoming_customer_id` who would take the contract over, the incoming subscriber's `incoming_credit_score`, and a `residence_state_code` for their new residence. The request begins in the request state `PENDING_NEGOTIATION` — the value its `request_status` field takes — with a `rejection_count` of zero and no accepted or completed instants. Rejections are the funding desk's counter-offer. The reject act's only request-side refusal is an out-of-radius residence, refused with `ERR_RESIDENCE_OUTSIDE_SERVICE_RADIUS`; it consults no `incoming_credit_score` at all. A request in `PENDING_NEGOTIATION` whose residence lies inside the radius records a rejection regardless of its incoming credit score. A recorded rejection increments `rejection_count` and advances the request's `version`. Critically, the first two rejections loop the request back to `PENDING_NEGOTIATION` rather than terminating it — each is a cyclic negotiation round that leaves the request open for a new incoming subscriber — while the third rejection is terminal: a reject that finds `rejection_count` already at three or more sets the request to `REJECTED` and stamps `ended_at` on the current ownership segment.

Acceptance commits the funding side to the incoming subscriber. A `FundingAnalyst` accepts a request only when the incoming `incoming_credit_score` is at least 620, which stamps `accepted_at` and advances the `version`. Acceptance is not final: a counterparty that withdraws after acceptance is rejected back to `PENDING_NEGOTIATION`, which clears `accepted_at` and advances the `version`, and the terminal `REJECTED` state is reachable only from `PENDING_NEGOTIATION`, never directly from `ACCEPTED`. The withdrawal is the same reject act applied to an `ACCEPTED` request: it consults no residence radius and increments no `rejection_count`, so it is a pure return to negotiation rather than a counted rejection round.

Completion is the subscriber's act and the moment the ledger turns over. A `Customer` or a `FundingAnalyst` completes an accepted request, and completion is gated on four conditions, evaluated in the order given: the incoming `incoming_credit_score` is at least 620; the `residence_state_code` lies inside the relocation service radius of `TX`, `OK`, `NM`, `AR` and `LA`; the outgoing subscriber retains at least one other contract in status `ACTIVE` or `DELINQUENT`, refused otherwise with `ERR_ASSUMED_CONTRACT_MUST_HAVE_SURVIVING_CONTRACT`; and the contract carries no open `SERVICE_REQUIRED` reduction action, refused otherwise with `ERR_FALSE_ALARM_WATCH_ACTIVE`. When those hold, the completion supersedes the outgoing subscriber's current ownership segment and stamps its `ended_at`, sets the contract's `customer_id` to the incoming subscriber, moves the contract's `contract_status` to `ASSUMED`, and advances the contract's `assumption_count` by one; it opens a fresh ownership segment in `CURRENT` for the incoming subscriber; it sets the outgoing subscriber's status to `ASSUMED`; it reprovisions the monitoring account to `PROVISIONED` and vacates the old premises site as `VACATED`; and it stamps the request's `completed_at`. The contract's `version`, the incoming customer's `version` and the request's `version` all advance. `ASSUMED` is terminal for the outgoing subscriber's interest, but the contract itself remains monitored under the incoming subscriber.

Relocation also moves the protected premises. A site's `occupancy_status` moves from `OCCUPIED` to `VACATED` when a contract is cancelled, and it never moves back by a reverse transition. At completion the relocation vacates the outgoing subscriber's current occupied site — stamping it `VACATED` — and the assumption completion itself mints no replacement site row, so the contract is left with its vacated premises on file; a fresh premises would be recorded the same way any premises is, through the origination payload that creates the contract's `Site`.

### 3.10. Cancellation, Chargeback Adjudication and Rescission

A monitored contract ends in cancellation, and cancellation is where the dealer's exposure crystallises. A `Customer` or a `FundingAnalyst` cancels an `ACTIVE` contract, but the act is gated by a backlog test on the dealer channel: for an `ACTIVE` dealer-channel contract, the count of that dealer's contracts in status `ACTIVE` must be strictly less than the dealer backlog threshold of eight, and a cancellation attempted when the dealer is at or above that threshold does not pass the check and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The test does not apply to a direct-channel contract, whose `dealer_id` is null, and it does not apply to a contract that is already `DELINQUENT`, since such a contract is not in the `ACTIVE` population the test measures; a cancel attempted against a contract in any state other than `ACTIVE` or `DELINQUENT` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Cancellation remembers where the contract came from: `cancelled_from_status` records the status observed immediately before the cancellation — `ACTIVE` for a live contract, `DELINQUENT` for one in arrears, `PENDING_VALIDATION` for a frozen one — and `cancelled_at` is stamped from the logical clock. That remembered status is what makes the compliance disclosure floor and the chargeback window below computable after the fact.

Every cancellation cascades outward, and the cancellation is also where the dealer's chargeback is first taken. The contract's monitoring account closes and the closure cascades to every non-terminal dispatch of that account, that is, every dispatch in `REQUESTED` or `DISPATCHED`, so no call is left open against a dead account. The subscriber's status moves to `CANCELLED` if and only if the cancelled contract was the subscriber's last contract not in a terminal state, that is, its last contract that is neither `CANCELLED` nor `ASSUMED`. The contract's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` advances. When the contract's `post_warranty_cents` is strictly greater than the post-warranty sweep threshold of 15000 cents, and the contract has a dealer, the post-warranty service sweep of §3.8 runs against the dealer's holdback reserve, which also resolves any open `SERVICE_REQUIRED` reduction action on the account and re-derives the dealer's standing. A cancellation additionally runs the chargeback decision of the paragraph below when the contract has a dealer, so a cancelled dealer-channel contract that lies inside its chargeback window carries its chargeback the moment it is cancelled, and the dealer's `version` advances with it. The direct-channel contract carries no dealer, so its cancellation runs neither the sweep nor the chargeback.

Chargeback is the dealer's share of a cancellation, and it is adjudicated separately from the cancellation itself. A `FundingAnalyst` may adjudicate a chargeback for a cancelled contract, and the adjudication returns early — with no amount and the marker `EXEMPT` — when the contract's `chargeback_exempt` is true, which is the state an approved onsite-install install waiver leaves behind. It returns `NOT_IN_WINDOW` when the contract carries no dealer at all, when the contract's `cancelled_from_status` is anything other than `ACTIVE`, or when the `cancelled_at` instant is later than the `activated_at` instant advanced by the chargeback window of thirty days. An adjudication attempted for a cancellation outside the window, for an exempt contract, or for a contract with no dealer is refused with `ERR_CHARGEBACK_WINDOW_CLOSED`. Otherwise the amount is ten percent of the contract's `capex_cents`, rounded half-up to whole cents, and it is recovered in a waterfall: the portion the dealer's `holdback_reserve_cents` can cover is taken from the reserve, and whatever remains is added to the dealer's `chargeback_balance_due_cents`. The decision fires once per contract: the first of the cancellation itself and the explicit adjudication to reach it marks the contract adjudicated, and a later adjudication returns the same amount and marker without moving the balances a second time. The adjudication returns the amount and the marker `CHARGEBACK`, re-derives the dealer's standing from the moved balances, and advances the dealer's `version`. A read-only preview of the same decision is available so that a dealer or analyst can see the recovery, the holdback application and the amount due before committing to it; the preview reports `chargeback_recovery_cents`, `holdback_application_cents`, `amount_due_cents` and a `decision_code`, does not adjudicate or move any balance, and reaches the same `EXEMPT` and `NOT_IN_WINDOW` decisions as the committed path.

A cancellation may also be reversed at its source. A `ComplianceOfficer` adjudicates a rescission, and the approval path is tightly bounded. A rescission is opened by a `Customer` or a `ComplianceOfficer` naming the `contract_id` and an `operator_id` for the requestor, which creates the request in `REQUESTED` and computes the request's `days_since_funding` as the whole calendar days from the contract's funding instant to the request instant. A decision is recorded by a `ComplianceOfficer` through the `outcome` field as either `APPROVED` or `DECLINED`; a decline changes nothing but the request's status and its `decided_at`, and it is available with no precondition beyond authorization. Approval is where the guard rails sit. The request instant must be at or before the funding instant advanced by the rescission window of five days, and the request itself must still be in `REQUESTED`; a request outside that window, or no longer pending, is refused with `ERR_RESCISSION_WINDOW_CLOSED`. The approving operator's `X-User-ID` must differ from the request's recorded `operator_id`, and the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES_REQUIRED` when the same operator both raised and decided it; the window and status guards are evaluated before that segregation guard. Approval then records `approved_by` and `approved_at` and moves the request to `APPROVED`, cancels the contract through the ordinary cancellation path — so `cancelled_from_status` records the status the contract held, `ACTIVE` in the ordinary case of a contract still inside its five-day window — with `cancelled_at` stamped from the clock, closes the contract's monitoring account and cascades the closure to every non-terminal dispatch of that account, and — most consequentially — creates the equipment-and-rescission refund-floor adjudication, whose amount cascades into the dealer's `chargeback_balance_due_cents` and `holdback_reserve_cents`, advancing the dealer's `version` as it does so. Because the refund floor stands in for them, the rescission cancellation runs neither the post-warranty service sweep nor the ordinary contract chargeback decision. The contract's `version` and the request's `version` advance as well.

The refund floor is a floor rather than a simple refund, and it is computed from four quantities. The contract's equipment balance floor is the sum of its frozen `equipment_financed_cents` and its `financed_charge_at_apr_cents`, and it contributes zero when the contract has not yet been disclosed — that is, when `equipment_financing_disclosed` is not true. The deferred monitoring amount is ten percent of the contract's `capex_cents`, rounded half-up, plus the contract's `financed_charge_at_apr_cents`. The amortised capitalised-equipment recovery is the contract's `capex_cents` multiplied by the smaller of the whole calendar months since activation and three, divided by the contract term, rounded half-up to cents. The adjudication amount is the larger of the equipment balance floor and the sum of the deferred monitoring amount and the amortised recovery. The dealer's share of the recovery is the smaller of the dealer's remaining holdback on the contract and the adjudication amount, and what the dealer's reserve cannot cover is added to the dealer's `chargeback_balance_due_cents`; the company's net position is the deferred monitoring amount plus the amortised recovery, less the dealer's share. Two variants bend the arithmetic in opposite directions: inside the rescission window, the deferral is retained, so the adjudication total exceeds the equipment-balance floor in every non-degenerate case; but when the dealer's early-cancellation abuse flag is open for the contract, the deferred monitoring amount is forced to zero, so the adjudication total collapses to the amortised capitalised-equipment recovery and the company nets strictly less. That abuse flag is a parameter of the adjudication rather than a field any endpoint can set, so no request can open it and the collapse variant is not reachable through the API surface.

Two naming conventions close out the contract lifecycle. A contract's `contract_number` is stamped by the company at funding, and it is a system field rather than something the originator supplies. The full set of contract states a reader must distinguish is `PENDING_VALIDATION`, `ACTIVE`, `DELINQUENT`, `CANCELLED` and `ASSUMED`, and the cancellation outcomes a reader must distinguish are the ones just described: a live contract cancels with `ACTIVE` remembered, a delinquent one with `DELINQUENT` remembered, a rescinded one with `ACTIVE` remembered and the refund floor applied, and a frozen pending one with `PENDING_VALIDATION` remembered and no chargeback arising.
