# Fitness Franchise Membership & Territory Administration API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A fitness club franchise is two businesses stacked on top of each other. Underneath sits a network of independently owned gyms, each one selling monthly memberships to walk-in consumers, chasing late payments, and negotiating with the neighbouring club over whose member just used whose treadmill. On top sits the franchisor, which owns the brand, grants each operator an exclusive trade area, dictates what a membership contract may say, polices how deeply the price may be cut, inspects the equipment, and bills a royalty on the dues the network collects. Every dispute that reaches franchisor headquarters — an encroaching relocation, a membership sold below the brand floor, a member who cancelled inside the statutory cooling-off window, a franchisee who reported more members than the ledger can account for — is at bottom a disagreement about which of those two businesses owns a number.

This document specifies the administrative backbone that settles those disagreements before they happen. The service is the single system of record for franchise territories and their protected radii, for the standardized membership agreements sold inside them, for the dues ledger maintained jointly with a third-party billing and collections vendor, for cross-club guest usage and the money that moves between clubs because of it, for national and corporate account subsidies allocated back down to franchisees, for facility inspection scoring and the equipment refresh cycle it grades against, and for the monthly royalty report each franchisee files. Three properties are non-negotiable throughout: money is exact, never approximate; every derived counter is recomputed from its source records rather than incremented by guesswork; and a compliance defect anywhere in a franchisee's estate must propagate to that franchisee's standing in the same request that discovered it.

The API is mounted under the `/franchise` path prefix, with the single exception of the logical-clock control endpoint described in §2.4. Because virtually every rule in this domain is a date comparison — a cooling-off window, a notice period, a promotion window, a closed royalty month, a delinquency age — the service never reads wall-clock time and instead reads a server-held logical clock that the test harness drives explicitly.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to this API must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which it acts. If either header is absent or supplied as an empty string, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the six role names declared below (the comparison is exact and case-sensitive), the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If the headers are well formed but the named role is not among the roles authorized for the endpoint being called (see the table in §4), the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The six roles are `BrandAdministrator`, `FranchiseOwner`, `ClubManager`, `ComplianceAuditor`, `BillingPartner`, and `Member`.

A `BrandAdministrator` is franchisor headquarters staff: it owns brand standards, the plan-template library, territory grants, relocation adjudication, promotional-pricing governance, corporate and national accounts, and inter-club settlement posting. A `FranchiseOwner` is the franchisee legal entity itself, which opens clubs inside its granted territory, drafts and submits relocation requests, files promotional pricing for brand review, and self-reports membership counts for royalty. A `ClubManager` is the general manager of exactly one club, selling and servicing membership agreements there, recording the cross-club guest usage that club hosts, and handling memberships moving into and out of it. A `ComplianceAuditor` is an independent brand-standards inspector who schedules and scores facility inspections and may waive another auditor's remediation finding, but holds no pricing, billing, or territory authority. A `BillingPartner` is the third-party dues-billing and collections vendor, which appends immutable ledger entries and places delinquent accounts with collections but can never alter pricing, membership state directly, or franchise records. A `Member` is the natural person holding one or more membership agreements, and is the only role that may exercise the statutory cancellation right.

Three of the six roles are **entity-backed**, meaning `X-User-ID` is the `id` of a persisted record and ownership is checked against it:

- When `X-User-Role` is `FranchiseOwner`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Franchisee`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Franchisee` compare the resource's `franchisee_id` to `X-User-ID`. A `FranchiseOwner` may therefore write only against `Territory`, `Club`, `RelocationRequest`, `Promotion`, and `RoyaltyReport` records whose owning `franchisee_id` equals `X-User-ID` — and for a `RelocationRequest`, the owning franchisee is that of the club named by `club_id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `ClubManager`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Club`. A `ClubManager` may write against a `MembershipAgreement` only when its `home_club_id` equals `X-User-ID`, and against a `TransferRequest` only when its `from_club_id` equals `X-User-ID` at creation time and its `to_club_id` equals `X-User-ID` for the decide and complete steps. Recording a `GuestUsageEvent` is the one `ClubManager` write that is deliberately *not* ownership-scoped: any club manager may record a batch naming any `host_club_id`, its own club's or another operator's, and the acting `X-User-ID` is neither compared to the host club nor stored on the batch.
- When `X-User-Role` is `Member`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Member`; it may write against a `MembershipAgreement` only when that agreement's `member_id` equals `X-User-ID`.

The remaining three roles are **opaque**. When `X-User-Role` is `BrandAdministrator`, `ComplianceAuditor`, or `BillingPartner`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a transition demands an audit stamp — as `decided_by_user_id`, `reviewed_by_user_id`, `adjudicated_by_user_id`, `scored_by_user_id`, or `waived_by_user_id` — and compares two such stamps to each other when enforcing segregation of duties, but never compares an opaque identifier to any entity's `id`. Opaque roles are consequently never ownership-scoped.

An ownership violation by an entity-backed role on a write operation is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). Read operations are never ownership-scoped: any of the six roles may read any resource it can name, so the visibility of a franchisee's compliance posture or a competitor's territory is deliberately open across the network.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from uppercase letters and digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, read from the logical clock of §2.4 rather than from wall-clock time, so every response taken at one clock setting carries the identical timestamp.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.


### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be lower than 1, and may not be higher than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` outside the inclusive range 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) rather than silently clamped, as is a `limit` or `offset` that is not a base-ten integer at all. A `limit` or `offset` supplied as an empty string is treated as omitted and takes its default, and query parameters other than `limit`, `offset` and the filters named below are ignored rather than rejected.

List responses order their rows by `created_at` ascending — oldest record first — and this is the only ordering any list endpoint applies unless its own description in §4 names a different one. Ties are broken by creation sequence, oldest first, which matters constantly because the logical clock never advances on its own and a whole chain of records built at one clock setting therefore shares a single `created_at` value. The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` (the number of rows matching the filters before the page window is applied) and `has_more` (true when rows remain beyond the current window).

Filter parameters on list endpoints are named exactly after the field they filter on, and combine conjunctively. Each is compared for exact equality against the stored field, except that a boolean field accepts `true`, `1`, `false` or `0` in any letter case; a filter supplied as an empty string is treated as omitted; and a filter value that no row matches — including a value that is not even well formed for the field, such as a non-existent status name — yields an empty page rather than an error. Franchisees may be filtered by `status` and `national_account_eligible`; territories by `franchisee_id`, `state_code` and `status`; clubs by `territory_id`, `franchisee_id`, `state_code`, `status` and `equipment_compliance_state`; relocation requests by `club_id` and `status`; plan templates by `status`; promotions by `franchisee_id`, `plan_template_id` and `status`; members by `status` and `home_state_code`; corporate accounts by `status` and `allocation_method`; membership agreements by `member_id`, `home_club_id`, `plan_template_id`, `promotion_id`, `corporate_account_id`, `agreement_status` and `dues_status`; the per-agreement dues ledger by `event_type`; transfer requests by `agreement_id`, `from_club_id`, `to_club_id` and `status`; guest usage batches by `agreement_id`, `host_club_id`, `home_club_id_at_visit`, `settlement_id` and `period_month`; usage settlements by `home_club_id`, `host_club_id`, `period_month` and `status`; royalty reports by `franchisee_id`, `period_month` and `status`; and facility inspections by `club_id`, `status` and `variant_applied`.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock

Every date comparison in this domain — delinquency ageing, statutory cooling-off windows, contractual notice periods, promotion windows, royalty period closure, inspection dating, transfer effective dating, settlement posting dates — reads a single server-held logical UTC clock. The service never falls back to real wall-clock time. At server startup the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it continues to read that value until a `BrandAdministrator` moves it.

A test-driving capability lets a `BrandAdministrator` set the clock to a supplied `current_utc` instant, at `POST /_clock/set_current_utc`. This is the one endpoint that lives outside the `/franchise` prefix. The required `current_utc` is an ISO 8601 instant accepted either with or without a trailing `Z` and read as UTC when it carries no offset, and the `data` of the reply is not a resource but the single-key object `{"current_utc": …}` echoing the supplied string back exactly as it arrived. The clock may be advanced or rewound freely, and moving it re-derives nothing: no stored balance, counter, score, or status is recomputed as a side effect of a clock move. Ageing therefore only becomes visible the next time some business operation re-runs a projection or evaluates a window.

Wherever this document refers to "the logical clock date", it means the calendar date (year, month, day) of that instant in UTC. Day counts between two dates are whole-day differences on the calendar, and monthly period arithmetic uses a fixed 30-day month convention: a term of `term_months` months is exactly 30 multiplied by `term_months` days, and one paid month of dues covers exactly 30 days.

### 2.5. Common Resource Properties and Immutable Ledgers

Every resource carries an `id`, which is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix; clients never supply it. Most resources also carry three further common properties. `created_at` is the ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits at which the record was created, and like every other timestamp the server writes it is read from the logical clock of §2.4 rather than from wall-clock time — a record created before the clock is first moved therefore carries exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`. `updated_at` is the same format, reads the same clock, and initially equals `created_at`, and is refreshed on every mutation of the record. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the record is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that record — including mutations caused by a cascade from a different entity's operation, not merely by direct writes to the record itself.

Two entities are deliberate exceptions because they are append-only ledgers. A `BillingEvent` and a `GuestUsageEvent` each carry only `id` and `created_at`: neither has an `updated_at` field and neither has a `version` field, and there is no endpoint that updates either one. The only mutation a `GuestUsageEvent` row ever undergoes is the server stamping or clearing its `settlement_id`, which is a bookkeeping flag rather than a client-visible edit, and it does not create a `version` field where none exists.

### 2.6. Nested Retrieval and the Order of Validation

Path segments are resolved strictly left to right. When a path names a parent and then a child — the dues ledger of one agreement, for example — the parent identifier is resolved before the child scope is considered, and a parent that does not exist produces the parent's own not-found error rather than an empty list. Within a request body, referenced identifiers are validated in the order the endpoint declares its inputs, and all path identifiers are validated before any body identifier.

Every request is evaluated through the following fifteen precedence bands, in this exact order. The first band that rejects wins, and no later band is consulted; this matters because several bands can be simultaneously unsatisfied and the caller must receive the earlier code.

1. **Authentication.** Both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` are present, and `X-User-Role` is one of the six declared roles. Otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. **Role authorization.** The acting role appears in the called endpoint's authorized list. Otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403).
3. **Resource ownership.** For entity-backed roles on any non-GET operation whose path or body names a `Territory`, `Club`, `RelocationRequest`, `Promotion`, `MembershipAgreement`, `TransferRequest`, or `RoyaltyReport`, the ownership rules of §2.1 hold. Recording a `GuestUsageEvent` is not among them and is never refused in this band. Otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). GET operations are exempt, and so is any request whose named record does not exist: ownership cannot be evaluated against an absent record, so such a request is not refused here but falls through to the existence band and answers with that band's 404. Three writes are deliberate exceptions to that fall-through, because they compare a supplied identifier to `X-User-ID` as a plain string without looking the referenced record up at all: a `ClubManager` signing an agreement whose `home_club_id` is not its own identifier, a `ClubManager` opening a transfer request whose `from_club_id` is not its own identifier, and a `FranchiseOwner` filing a royalty report whose `franchisee_id` is not its own identifier, are each refused here with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` even when no record by that identifier exists anywhere. That plain-string comparison is made only when the field actually arrived as a string: a `franchisee_id`, `home_club_id` or `from_club_id` that is absent, `null` or of some other JSON type skips this band entirely and is answered by the schema band instead.
4. **Schema.** All required body fields are present with correct types; enum values fall inside their declared sets; dates are `YYYY-MM-DD` and every `period_month` is `YYYY-MM`; integer money fields are at least 0; `discount_pct` lies within 0 through 0.500 inclusive and `corporate_credit_pct` within 0 through 0.100 inclusive, so a value above its own ceiling is malformed in this band rather than a governance refusal later; `center_lat`, `requested_lat` and any other latitude lies within −90 through 90; `center_lng`, `requested_lng` and any other longitude lies within −180 through 180; `protected_radius_km` and `requested_radius_km` are greater than 0 and, more tightly, lie within 0.5 through 50.0 inclusive, so a radius of 0.4 is malformed here rather than merely commercially unacceptable; `visit_count` is at least 1; `limit` lies within 1 through 100; `offset` is at least 0. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Three body conventions apply throughout this band. A request body that is absent, empty or unparseable is treated as the empty JSON object, so an endpoint all of whose inputs are optional succeeds with no body at all, while a body that parses to anything other than a JSON object is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A field supplied explicitly as `null` is treated exactly as if it had been omitted — a missing-field rejection for a required field, and the declared default for an optional one. And body fields the endpoint does not declare are ignored rather than rejected.
5. **Existence.** Every referenced resource exists, checked left to right as described above. The 404 codes are grouped by family: `ERR_FRANCHISEE_NOT_FOUND` for a missing `Franchisee`; `ERR_TERRITORY_NOT_FOUND` for a missing `Territory`; `ERR_CLUB_NOT_FOUND` for any missing club reference, whether it arrived as `club_id`, `home_club_id`, `host_club_id`, `from_club_id` or `to_club_id`; `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND` for a missing `Member`; `ERR_PLAN_ASSET_NOT_FOUND` for a missing `MembershipPlanTemplate`, `Promotion` or `CorporateAccount`; `ERR_MEMBERSHIP_RECORD_NOT_FOUND` for a missing `MembershipAgreement`, `TransferRequest`, `GuestUsageEvent` or `BillingEvent`; and `ERR_OPS_RECORD_NOT_FOUND` for a missing `RelocationRequest`, `FacilityInspection`, `UsageSettlement` or `RoyaltyReport`.
6. **Optimistic concurrency.** Where an endpoint declares an expected-version input, it must match the parent's current `version`. See §2.7.
7. **Declared state transition.** The target record is in a state from which the requested transition exists. Only a `SCHEDULED` inspection may be scored; only a `DRAFT` or `CHANGES_REQUESTED` relocation request may be submitted; only an `OPEN` settlement may be posted; only a `PUBLISHED` plan template may back a new agreement; only an `OPEN` club may host a new agreement or receive a transfer; and only a `RESERVED` territory may take a new club shell, so a territory that has already reached `ACTIVE` because its club opened is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TERRITORY_STATE`. The codes in this band are `ERR_INVALID_TERRITORY_STATE`, `ERR_CLUB_NOT_OPERATIONAL`, `ERR_INVALID_RELOCATION_STATE`, `ERR_PLAN_NOT_PUBLISHED`, `ERR_INVALID_PLAN_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_PROMOTION_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_AGREEMENT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_DUES_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_TRANSFER_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_SETTLEMENT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_INSPECTION_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_ROYALTY_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_ALLOCATION_STATE` and `ERR_CORPORATE_ACCOUNT_SUSPENDED`, all HTTP 409.
8. **Segregation of duties.** The identifier that produced a record may not adjudicate it, yielding `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). This band guards exactly two operations: deciding a relocation request, and waiving an inspection's remediation.
9. **State statute gates.** `ERR_NOTICE_PERIOD_NOT_ELAPSED`, `ERR_STATUTE_NOTICE_LIMIT` and `ERR_CROSS_STATE_TRANSFER_PROHIBITED`, all 409. This band guards exactly three operations: signing an agreement, finalizing a notice-period cancellation, and opening a transfer request.
10. **Equipment and facility safety gates.** `ERR_CLUB_NOT_OPERATIONAL`, `ERR_REMEDIATION_NOT_REQUIRED` and `ERR_RELOCATION_PENDING`, all 409. This band guards exactly five operations: bringing a club into service, signing an agreement, opening a transfer request, deciding a transfer request — whose approval branch re-applies the destination club's safety gate — and waiving remediation.
11. **Cross-entity referential coherence.** `ERR_HOME_CLUB_MISMATCH`, `ERR_SAME_CLUB_TRANSFER`, `ERR_GUEST_VISIT_NOT_ALLOWED` and, for shape violations in this family, `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. This band guards exactly five operations: signing an agreement, opening a transfer request, completing a transfer request — which re-checks that the agreement's `home_club_id` is still the request's `from_club_id` — recording a guest usage batch, and creating a usage settlement.
12. **Territory protection and uniqueness.** `ERR_TERRITORY_ENCROACHMENT`, `ERR_TERRITORY_OCCUPIED`, `ERR_RELOCATION_PENDING`, `ERR_RELOCATION_REVISION_LIMIT`, `ERR_DISCOUNT_CAP_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_SETTLEMENT_PERIOD` and `ERR_DUPLICATE_ROYALTY_PERIOD`, all 409. This band guards exactly nine operations: creating a territory, creating a club shell, drafting a relocation request, submitting a relocation request — where the only code this band contributes is `ERR_RELOCATION_REVISION_LIMIT`, since a submission never checks territory protection — deciding a relocation request, filing a promotion, reviewing a promotion, whose approval branch re-applies the same `ERR_DISCOUNT_CAP_EXCEEDED` ceiling the filing faced, creating a usage settlement, and filing a royalty report.
13. **Brand-governance gates that depend on another entity's current state.** `ERR_FRANCHISEE_ON_PROBATION` and `ERR_PROMOTION_WINDOW_CLOSED`, both 409. This band guards exactly two operations: reviewing a promotion, and signing an agreement.
14. **Date-window coherence.** `ERR_PROMO_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, `ERR_REPORT_PERIOD_NOT_CLOSED` and, for ordering violations such as a `starts_on` later than its `ends_on`, `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. This band guards exactly five operations: filing a promotion, reviewing a promotion — whose approval branch re-applies the seven-day separation rule — recording a guest usage batch, filing a royalty report, and scheduling an inspection.
15. **Money gates, evaluated last.** `ERR_PRICE_BELOW_FLOOR`, `ERR_CANCELLATION_BLOCKED_BY_ARREARS`, `ERR_TRANSFER_BLOCKED_BY_ARREARS`, `ERR_COLLECTIONS_THRESHOLD_NOT_MET`, `ERR_INVALID_DUES_EVENT`, `ERR_NO_SETTLEABLE_USAGE` and `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_FRANCHISEE`, all 409. This band guards exactly nine operations: publishing a plan template, signing an agreement, requesting cancellation, placing an account in collections, appending a billing event, opening a transfer request, deciding a transfer request, creating a usage settlement, and allocating a corporate pool.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Two write operations in this system quote a parent record's posture at the moment the client read it, and both therefore demand that the client prove it is still looking at the current parent.

Signing a membership agreement requires the non-optional input field `expected_club_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the home `Club`. If that value does not equal the home club's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CLUB_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A club legitimately accepts any number of agreements, so nothing in the club's state blocks a second signing; the reason for the check is that the club's pricing posture (`guest_fee_cents`), its compliance posture (`equipment_compliance_state` and `status`) and its counters may all have moved between the read and the write, and an agreement quoted against a stale club snapshot must be refused rather than silently signed at the old posture.

Opening a transfer request requires the non-optional input field `expected_agreement_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `MembershipAgreement`. If that value does not equal the agreement's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_AGREEMENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). An agreement may carry many transfer requests over its life, so no state precondition blocks a second one; the reason for the check is that a billing event, a freeze, or an earlier completed transfer may have moved the agreement's `home_club_id` or its `dues_status` in the meantime, which would make both the `from_club_id` supplied on the new request and its arrears eligibility stale.

No other endpoint takes an expected-version field. Where a version field is absent from an endpoint's inputs, supplying one has no meaning and the endpoint performs no version comparison.

### 2.8. Money, Rounding and Conserved Quantities

All monetary amounts in this API are integers in cents; there are no decimal currency values anywhere in a request or a response. Wherever a monetary result requires rounding, the method is ROUND_HALF_UP — a half-cent rounds away from zero, upward. The individual calculations in §3 pin the exact *point* at which rounding is applied, and those pinned orders are part of the contract: rounding earlier or later than specified produces a different cent value and is wrong even when the arithmetic is otherwise correct.

Non-monetary numeric precision is likewise fixed. `center_lat` and `center_lng` carry 4 decimal places; `protected_radius_km` and `requested_radius_km` carry 1 decimal place; `discount_pct` and `corporate_credit_pct` carry 3 decimal places; `variance_pct` and the settlement `rebate_pct` of §3.7 each carry 2 decimal places, and both are server-derived rather than client-supplied, so each is quantized to exactly that width before it is stored and returned; and the kilometre distances and clearances computed for territory protection are rounded to 3 decimal places with ROUND_HALF_UP. For the client-supplied geometry fields those decimal counts describe what callers are expected to supply rather than a server-side rounding step: a `Territory` or a `RelocationRequest` persists and returns exactly the latitude, longitude and radius numbers it was handed, without re-quantizing them, and only the derived distances and clearances are rounded. The same holds for the two client-supplied percentages: a `Promotion` stores and returns exactly the `discount_pct` it was handed and a `CorporateAccount` exactly the `corporate_credit_pct` it was handed, neither re-quantized to 3 decimal places nor refused for carrying more of them, and any extra precision a caller supplies is carried at full precision into the discount arithmetic of §3.3 before that product is rounded to whole cents.

Two quantities are conserved and must remain exactly balanced at all times. First, the inter-club settlement ledger: summing `settlement_ledger_cents` across every `Club` in the system always yields exactly 0, because every posting debits one club and credits another by the identical amount, and every reversal undoes both legs exactly. Second, corporate account funding: for every `CorporateAccount`, `pool_balance_cents` plus `allocated_cents` always equals `lifetime_funded_cents`, where `lifetime_funded_cents` is monotonic and never decreases.

### 2.9. State Health-Club Statutes

Three jurisdictions are modelled, and the `state_code` enumeration is exactly `CA`, `TX`, `NY`. Each carries a statutory profile that governs consumer cancellation rights and cross-state portability:

| `state_code` | Cooling-off days | Maximum notice days | Prorates on cancellation | Cross-state transfer allowed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `CA` | 5 | 30 | yes | no |
| `TX` | 3 | 30 | yes | yes |
| `NY` | 3 | 15 | no | yes |

The governing statute for a membership is the one attached to the club where it was signed, not the one where the member happens to live: `home_state_code` on the `Member` record is captured for audit only. Once an agreement's `state_code` is pinned at signing it is never re-derived — neither a relocation that moves the club's geometry nor a transfer that moves the membership to a club in another state changes which statute governs the contract.

### 2.10. Cascade Discipline and Franchisee Standing

Derived counters in this system are never incremented on faith; they are recomputed from the records that define them, and the recomputation happens inside the same request that disturbed them. When a single operation therefore touches several entities, the response returns only the primary resource — the caller must re-read the other entities to observe the cascade. Re-deriving a record's figures and incrementing that record's `version` are nevertheless two separate acts, and the operation's own paragraph in §3 is what pins which of the records it disturbs actually bumps. Two records are habitually rewritten without one. A `CorporateAccount`'s `enrolled_agreement_count` never bumps its account, as §3.3 states. And the `Franchisee` is re-derived in place on every event that could move its standing — its three counters, its `compliance_score`, and where the fresh score demands it the demotion to `PROBATION` together with the withdrawal of its approved promotions, all written through in the same request — while its own `version` and `updated_at` stand still on most of those paths; §3.9 enumerates the short list of paths that do carry the increment. A withdrawn promotion, by contrast, always takes a `version` increment of its own.

The most consequential cascade is the franchisee compliance chain, and it always runs in the same direction: a facility, billing, or reporting event changes a club-level or report-level fact, that fact changes the owning `Franchisee`'s `compliance_score`, and a score that falls below the probation threshold demotes the franchisee from `ACTIVE` to `PROBATION`, which in turn withdraws every one of that franchisee's approved promotions. Each of those three links fires in the same request as the event that started it. The chain never runs backwards on its own: curing the underlying defect does not automatically restore `ACTIVE` standing, and restoring `ACTIVE` standing does not resurrect a withdrawn promotion.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Franchise Spine: Franchisees, Territories, Clubs and Relocation

Everything in this system hangs off a three-level ownership spine. Headquarters registers a franchise operator, grants it a protected trade area, and the operator then builds a club inside that area. Registration is a `BrandAdministrator` action and captures the operator's registered `legal_name`, which must be between 1 and 120 characters, and a primary `contact_email` for franchisor correspondence, which is required but is checked only for being a non-empty string — no address format is validated and no uniqueness is imposed across operators. Two commercial parameters may be supplied at registration and both have defaults: `royalty_rate_bps` expresses the royalty rate in basis points against the verified dues basis and defaults to 650 — that is, six and a half percent — with an accepted range of 0 through 1200 inclusive; and `national_account_eligible`, which defaults to true, determines whether the operator participates in corporate revenue sharing at all. A newly registered operator starts in `ACTIVE` standing with `compliance_score` at 100, `inspection_fail_streak` at 0, `collections_watch_club_count` at 0, `disputed_royalty_count` at 0 and `corporate_allocation_cents` at 0. The `status` of a `Franchisee` is only ever `ACTIVE` or `PROBATION`.

Granting a trade area is likewise reserved to headquarters, and it is where the brand's radius protection is enforced. A grant names the receiving `franchisee_id`, the protected circle's centre as `center_lat` and `center_lng`, its `protected_radius_km` — accepted between 0.5 and 50.0 kilometres inclusive — and the `state_code` whose health-club statute will govern agreements signed there. Before the grant is written, the server measures the proposed circle's clearance against the competition. The candidate set is every existing `Territory` that belongs to a *different* franchisee and whose `status` is `RESERVED` or `ACTIVE`; when the check is re-run for a relocation, the subject territory itself is additionally excluded. Two territories owned by the same franchisee may overlap as freely as their owner likes, and are never candidates. Neither the grant nor the club shell later built inside it is gated on the receiving operator's standing: a franchisee sitting in `PROBATION` may still be granted a fresh trade area and still build and open on it.

The measurement is a deliberately simple planar approximation with no trigonometry, using fixed degrees-to-kilometre factors so that every implementation lands on the same cent-level answer. For each candidate territory, the north-south separation in kilometres is the difference between the proposed latitude and the candidate's `center_lat`, multiplied by 111.0; the east-west separation in kilometres is the difference between the proposed longitude and the candidate's `center_lng`, multiplied by 85.0. The straight-line distance is the square root of the sum of the squares of those two separations, rounded half up to 3 decimal places. The required separation is the proposed radius plus the candidate's `protected_radius_km` plus a brand protection buffer of 0.5 kilometres. That candidate's clearance is the distance minus the required separation, again rounded half up to 3 decimal places. The proposal's overall clearance is the smallest clearance across all candidates, and it is null when the candidate set is empty. Rejection is strict: only a negative clearance is an encroachment, so two circles separated by precisely the sum of their radii plus the buffer produce a clearance of exactly 0.000 and are accepted, while a clearance of −0.001 is refused with `ERR_TERRITORY_ENCROACHMENT`. A granted territory is created `RESERVED` with `relocation_count` at 0, and it becomes `ACTIVE` only when its club actually opens. At grant time the clearance is a pre-write check only and is never persisted: a `Territory` record carries no clearance attribute of its own — not on creation, not when a later relocation rewrites its geometry — so no clearance value appears in a territory's created or retrieved representation. `encroachment_km` is a field of the `RelocationRequest` record alone.

A club shell may then be built inside the reserved area, either by headquarters or by the owning `FranchiseOwner`. The shell names its `territory_id`, its trading `name` — any non-empty string, with no upper length bound and no uniqueness requirement — and the `opened_on` date on which the location opened, written as a `YYYY-MM-DD` calendar date and never compared against the logical clock, so a future or long-past opening date is accepted as given; a per-visit `guest_fee_cents` charged to visiting members' home clubs may also be supplied, defaulting to 1250 cents and accepted between 0 and 5000 inclusive. Four fields are inherited rather than accepted: `franchisee_id`, `state_code`, `lat` and `lng` are copied from the parent territory at creation — `lat` and `lng` from the territory's centre — and any client-supplied value for them is ignored outright rather than rejected. The `franchisee_id` copied at creation is never rewritten afterwards, no matter what happens to the territory. A new shell starts with `status` `PRE_OPENING`, `equipment_compliance_state` `COMPLIANT`, `last_inspection_score` null, `active_agreement_count` 0, `delinquent_member_count` 0, `collections_watch` false and `settlement_ledger_cents` 0. Exactly one club may inhabit a territory, and the refusal depends on how the territory got occupied: a `RESERVED` territory that already holds a `PRE_OPENING` shell is refused with `ERR_TERRITORY_OCCUPIED`, whereas a territory that has already reached `ACTIVE` because its club opened is caught by the earlier state band and refused with `ERR_INVALID_TERRITORY_STATE`.

Bringing the location into service is the franchisee's own act, advancing the club from `PRE_OPENING` to `OPEN`, and it may carry an optional `note`, which is validated as a string when supplied but is not persisted anywhere on the club. Only the `FranchiseOwner` whose identifier matches the club's `franchisee_id` may do it, and only from `PRE_OPENING`. The operation is refused with `ERR_RELOCATION_PENDING` while any relocation request for that club sits in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `CHANGES_REQUESTED`, because the club's protected geometry is still in flux and the brand will not put a location into service on a footprint that may still move. Opening bubbles up: if the parent territory is still `RESERVED`, it flips `RESERVED → ACTIVE` and its `version` increments, alongside the club's own `version` increment. A territory holding only a `PRE_OPENING` club stays `RESERVED`. The third club state, `SUSPENDED`, is reachable only through the inspection cascade of §3.9 — no manual operation ever suspends a club, and a `SUSPENDED` club may not sign new agreements, host guest visits, or receive transfers.

Relocating an existing club is a governed, cyclic review. The `FranchiseOwner` drafts the request against its own club — whose own `status` is not gated at all here, so a `PRE_OPENING`, an `OPEN` and a `SUSPENDED` club are equally eligible subjects for a draft — naming the `club_id`, the proposed `requested_lat` and `requested_lng`, a `requested_radius_km` between 0.5 and 50.0 inclusive, and a free-text `justification` making the business case. The draft is created in `DRAFT` with `revision_count` at 0 and with `encroachment_km`, `submitted_by_user_id`, `decided_by_user_id` and `reviewer_note` all null, and only one request per club may be open at a time: a second draft is refused with `ERR_RELOCATION_PENDING` while an existing request is in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `CHANGES_REQUESTED`. Submitting the request for brand review moves it `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`, records `submitted_by_user_id` as the acting owner's identifier, and computes and stores the proposed circle's clearance into `encroachment_km` using the measurement above, run against other franchisees' territories. A submission may optionally restate `requested_lat`, `requested_lng` and `requested_radius_km`; whichever of the three are supplied overwrite the stored proposal, and `encroachment_km` is then recomputed from the new geometry. Submission merely records what it measures and never itself judges the geometry: a negative clearance is stored in `encroachment_km` exactly as computed and the request still moves to `SUBMITTED`, so submitting a proposal that sits inside another franchisee's protection buffer is not refused with `ERR_TERRITORY_ENCROACHMENT`. Territory protection is enforced against a relocation only at the approval gate described below.

Brand review may bounce the request back for rework, moving it `SUBMITTED → CHANGES_REQUESTED` and storing the mandatory `reviewer_note` explaining what must change. The franchisee then resubmits, which is the same submit action taken from `CHANGES_REQUESTED` and which additionally increments `revision_count` by 1 before recomputing the clearance. Resubmission leaves the bounce's `reviewer_note` in place rather than clearing it, and restamps `submitted_by_user_id` with whoever submitted this time. The cycle `SUBMITTED → CHANGES_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED` is capped at two revisions: a resubmission whose resulting `revision_count` would exceed 2 — a third round trip, which would set the counter to 3 — is refused with `ERR_RELOCATION_REVISION_LIMIT`, and the request stays where it was. Submitting from any state other than `DRAFT` or `CHANGES_REQUESTED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_RELOCATION_STATE`.

Deciding the request is a `BrandAdministrator` action carrying a `decision` of either `APPROVE` or `REJECT` and an optional `reviewer_note`, and it is available only from `SUBMITTED`. Segregation of duties bites here: the identifier stored as `submitted_by_user_id` may not decide its own request, even when it presents itself with the `BrandAdministrator` role, and an attempt is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The two branches diverge sharply in what they touch. On `APPROVE`, the clearance measurement is re-run against live territory geometry, and the request only proceeds when the clearance is at least 0 against every territory of a different franchisee whose `status` is `RESERVED` or `ACTIVE`; a negative result at this second gate is refused with `ERR_TERRITORY_ENCROACHMENT` even though the request passed at submission time. When it clears, the request moves `SUBMITTED → APPROVED`, `decided_by_user_id` is stamped with the deciding identifier, the parent territory's `center_lat`, `center_lng` and `protected_radius_km` are rewritten to the requested values with `relocation_count` incremented by 1, and because the territory moved, the club's own `lat` and `lng` are rewritten to the same requested values. The territory's `version` and the club's `version` each increment by 1. On `REJECT`, the request moves `SUBMITTED → REJECTED`, `decided_by_user_id` and `reviewer_note` are stored, and nothing else in the system changes at all: territory and club geometry are left exactly as they were, and only the request's own `version` increments. One thing neither branch ever touches is the governing statute of an already-signed membership — a relocation never moves an agreement's pinned `state_code`, even when the club's coordinates cross a state line. Nor is any `state_code` re-derived from the new geometry at all: the territory's own `state_code` and the club's inherited copy of it both stand exactly as granted, and the club's `status` and the territory's `status` are likewise left alone by an approval.

The franchisee may also abandon its own request, moving it `DRAFT → WITHDRAWN` or `CHANGES_REQUESTED → WITHDRAWN` with an optional `reason` — validated as a string when supplied, but not persisted on the record — which frees the club to draft a fresh one. A `SUBMITTED` request is under active brand review and may not be withdrawn; the attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_RELOCATION_STATE`. `APPROVED`, `REJECTED` and `WITHDRAWN` are all terminal.

### 3.2. Plan Standardization and Promotional Pricing Governance

Membership contracts in this network are not written locally. Headquarters maintains a library of standardized plan templates, and a franchisee may sell only what the library publishes. Creating a template is a `BrandAdministrator` action and fixes six commercial terms: a brand-standard `code`, which must be unique across all templates and consists of 2 to 24 characters drawn from uppercase letters, digits and the underscore; `monthly_dues_cents`, the list monthly dues before any discount, which must be at least 100; `initiation_fee_cents`, the one-time fee charged at signing, which must be at least 0; `term_months`, the contract term length, accepted between 1 and 36 inclusive; `cancellation_notice_days`, the contractual notice period, accepted between 0 and 60 inclusive; and `floor_price_cents`, the brand pricing floor below which no agreement may ever be signed, which must be at least 0. Both `code` rules are enforced in the schema band rather than as governance conflicts: a `code` carrying any character outside the permitted set, and a `code` an existing template already carries, are each refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). A template is created in `DRAFT`.

Publishing a template moves it `DRAFT → PUBLISHED` and may carry an optional `note`, which is validated as a string when supplied but is not persisted anywhere on the template. A template may only be published when its `floor_price_cents` is less than or equal to its `monthly_dues_cents` — a floor above list price would make the plan unsellable at any discount — and a template failing that test is refused with `ERR_PRICE_BELOW_FLOOR`. Publishing an already-`PUBLISHED` template is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PLAN_STATE`, since the transition no longer exists from that state; `PUBLISHED` is terminal and a template is never unpublished or edited afterwards. Only a `PUBLISHED` template may back a promotion or a membership agreement, and citing a `DRAFT` one is refused with `ERR_PLAN_NOT_PUBLISHED`.

Deep discounting is the brand's most jealously guarded lever, because a price cut in one trade area re-anchors what consumers in the next one will pay. A `FranchiseOwner` may therefore only *file* a promotional price for review, never activate one. The filing names its own `franchisee_id`, the `plan_template_id` whose dues the discount applies to, a quotable `code` — any non-empty string, with no charset rule, no upper length bound and no uniqueness requirement of any kind, so two live promotions may quote the same code — the `discount_pct` as a fraction of monthly dues carried to 3 decimal places and accepted in the range 0.0 through 0.500, and the closed window `starts_on` through `ends_on` in which the code may be quoted, both dates inclusive. Filing is not gated on the operator's own standing: a franchisee sitting in `PROBATION` may still file, because standing is consulted only at the approval gate below. The filing is created in `PENDING_REVIEW` with `reviewed_by_user_id` and `withdrawn_reason` both null. The schema band accepts a `discount_pct` as high as 0.500, but brand governance caps a franchise-level discount at 0.30 — thirty percent off list — and a filing above that cap is refused with `ERR_DISCOUNT_CAP_EXCEEDED`, while a value above 0.500 never gets that far and is refused as malformed with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A `starts_on` later than its `ends_on` is likewise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Promotions for the same `franchisee_id` and `plan_template_id` pair must also be kept apart in time, so that a franchisee cannot chain back-to-back discounts into a permanent price cut. When a new filing is compared against every other promotion for that same pair whose `status` is `PENDING_REVIEW` or `APPROVED`, the two closed windows must be separated by at least a full week — 7 whole days — on whichever side they meet: if the new window sits later, the gap from the existing promotion's `ends_on` to the new `starts_on` must be at least 7 days; if the new window sits earlier, the gap from the new `ends_on` to the existing promotion's `starts_on` must be at least 7 days. Overlapping windows and merely adjacent windows are both refused with `ERR_PROMO_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. The boundary is exact: a gap of precisely 7 whole days is accepted, and a gap of 6 is rejected.

Brand governance then reviews the filing with a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `REJECT` and an optional `note` — validated as a string when supplied and then discarded rather than persisted on the promotion — moving it `PENDING_REVIEW → APPROVED` or `PENDING_REVIEW → REJECTED`; either decision stamps `reviewed_by_user_id` with the reviewing identifier and increments the promotion's `version`. Reviewing a promotion that is not in `PENDING_REVIEW` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PROMOTION_STATE`. The two branches are not symmetric in what they check. Approval re-verifies that `discount_pct` does not exceed the 0.30 cap, that the owning franchisee's `status` is still `ACTIVE` — approval while the owner sits in `PROBATION` is refused with `ERR_FRANCHISEE_ON_PROBATION` — and that no other `PENDING_REVIEW` or `APPROVED` promotion for the same franchisee-and-template pair violates the seven-day separation rule. Rejection checks none of those things: an over-cap filing owned by a franchisee on probation can always be rejected, because rejection only requires that the promotion currently be in `PENDING_REVIEW`.

An approved promotion can also be taken away without anyone asking for it. When the owning franchisee is demoted from `ACTIVE` to `PROBATION` by the compliance cascade of §3.9, every promotion of that franchisee whose `status` is `APPROVED` moves `APPROVED → WITHDRAWN_BY_BRAND`, has its `withdrawn_reason` set to the string `FRANCHISEE_PROBATION`, and has its `version` incremented — one increment per withdrawn promotion. What the withdrawal deliberately does *not* do is disturb pricing already sold: no `MembershipAgreement` that quoted the promotion has its `effective_monthly_dues_cents` or its stored discount components recomputed, because signed pricing is pinned forever. `REJECTED` and `WITHDRAWN_BY_BRAND` are both terminal; a withdrawn promotion is never resurrected, and a franchisee returning to `ACTIVE` standing must file a fresh one.

### 3.3. Signing a Membership Agreement

Before anyone can sign, the natural person must exist as a member record. Either headquarters or a club manager may register one, supplying the member's `full_name`, an `email` that must be unique across all members, and the `home_state_code` in which they reside, drawn from `CA`, `TX` and `NY`. `full_name` is any non-empty string with no upper length bound, and the `email` is checked only for being a non-empty string and for uniqueness — no address format is validated — with a duplicate refused as malformed with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400) rather than as a governance conflict. A member is created with `status` `ACTIVE` and `agreement_count` 0. Residence is recorded for statute audit only and never decides which statute governs a contract, as §2.9 explains. A member's `status` tracks whether they currently hold anything: `agreement_count` counts the agreements of that member whose `agreement_status` is `PENDING`, `ACTIVE`, `FROZEN` or `CANCELLATION_PENDING`, and when the count falls to 0 after an agreement reaches `CANCELLED` the member flips `ACTIVE → INACTIVE` with a `version` increment. Signing a new agreement flips them back `INACTIVE → ACTIVE`, again with a `version` increment.

Signing is available to the `ClubManager` of the home club and to a `BrandAdministrator`, and returns 201 with the new agreement. The request names the `member_id` who is signing, the `home_club_id` that will bill them, the `plan_template_id` being sold, the `expected_club_version` demanded by §2.7, and `signed_on`. Three optional inputs shape the price: `multi_club_access`, which defaults to false and grants the member the right to be recorded as a guest at other clubs; `promotion_id`, which defaults to null and quotes a local discount; and `corporate_account_id`, which defaults to null and enrols the membership in a national account. Although `signed_on` is stated on the request, the server always pins it to the logical clock date at creation, so a value that disagrees with the clock is ignored rather than honoured.

Four terms are copied off their sources at signing and then frozen for the life of the contract. The agreement's `state_code` is taken from the home club's `state_code` and is never re-derived. `monthly_dues_base_cents` is a pinned copy of the template's `monthly_dues_cents`, and `initiation_fee_cents` a pinned copy of the template's `initiation_fee_cents`, so a later edit to the library cannot move a signed contract. `term_end_on` is the `signed_on` date plus 30 days multiplied by the template's `term_months`, using the 30-day month convention. The stored `cancellation_notice_days` is the lesser of the template's `cancellation_notice_days` and the maximum notice days the agreement's governing statute allows; and independently of that, a template whose notice period exceeds the statutory maximum for the home club's state may not be sold there at all, which is refused with `ERR_STATUTE_NOTICE_LIMIT`. A 30-day-notice template is therefore unsellable at an `NY` club, whose statute caps notice at 15 days.

Pricing is where the ordering of arithmetic operations becomes contractual. The base for every component is the template's list `monthly_dues_cents`. Each of the three discount components is computed and rounded half up to whole cents *independently*: `promo_discount_cents` is 0 when no `promotion_id` was quoted and otherwise the base multiplied by the promotion's `discount_pct`, rounded half up; `corporate_credit_cents` is 0 when no `corporate_account_id` was named and otherwise the base multiplied by the account's `corporate_credit_pct`, rounded half up; and `multi_club_credit_cents` is 0 when `multi_club_access` is false and otherwise the base multiplied by the multi-club credit rate of 0.015 — one and a half percent — rounded half up. Only then are the three *already-rounded* components added together to form the total discount, and the gross monthly dues are the base minus that total. The pinned order is round-each-component, then sum, then subtract, then apply the floor. Summing the three unrounded fractional components and rounding once at the end is the forbidden alternative: it yields a different cent value and is wrong.

The brand floor is applied last, and it is a hard gate rather than a clamp. If the gross monthly dues computed above fall below the template's `floor_price_cents`, the signing is refused with `ERR_PRICE_BELOW_FLOOR` and no agreement is created; nothing is quietly lifted up to the floor. When the gross figure is at or above the floor, it is stored verbatim as `effective_monthly_dues_cents`, and that figure — like every other pinned term — never moves again, not when the quoted promotion is later withdrawn by the brand, and not when the membership is transferred to another club.

The references supplied must cohere with each other and with the current state of the world. Exactly one plan template participates and it must be `PUBLISHED`, otherwise `ERR_PLAN_NOT_PUBLISHED`. At most one promotion participates: it must be in `APPROVED` status, or the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PROMOTION_STATE`; its closed `starts_on` through `ends_on` window must contain the logical clock date, or the request is refused with `ERR_PROMOTION_WINDOW_CLOSED`; and it must belong to the same franchisee that owns the home club and cite the same `plan_template_id` the agreement is using, or the request is refused with `ERR_HOME_CLUB_MISMATCH`. At most one corporate account participates and it must be `ACTIVE`; naming a `SUSPENDED` account is refused with `ERR_CORPORATE_ACCOUNT_SUSPENDED`. The home club must be `OPEN`, and its `equipment_compliance_state` must not be `NON_COMPLIANT`; either failure is refused with `ERR_CLUB_NOT_OPERATIONAL`. What is never consulted is the owning franchisee's standing: a club whose `Franchisee` sits in `PROBATION` keeps selling agreements exactly as it keeps opening clubs, and nothing about the member's own `status` is gated either — an `INACTIVE` member may sign, and a member may hold any number of concurrent agreements at one club or across several.

A freshly signed agreement carries two independent status dimensions, and both start at their initial values. The contract dimension, `agreement_status`, starts at `PENDING` and takes the values `PENDING`, `ACTIVE`, `FROZEN`, `CANCELLATION_PENDING` and `CANCELLED`. The collections dimension, `dues_status`, starts at `CURRENT` and takes the values `CURRENT`, `PAST_DUE`, `DELINQUENT`, `IN_COLLECTIONS` and `WRITTEN_OFF`. The two evolve independently of each other. Alongside them the agreement is created with `outstanding_balance_cents` 0, `dues_paid_cents` 0, `delinquency_days` 0 and `transfer_count` 0, and with `cancellation_requested_on`, `cancellation_effective_on`, `refund_cents`, `early_termination_fee_cents` and `net_settlement_cents` all null.

Signing also reaches outside the new record. The signing member's `agreement_count` increments by 1 and their `version` increments; if they were `INACTIVE`, their `status` returns to `ACTIVE`. When a `corporate_account_id` was supplied, that account's `enrolled_agreement_count` increments by 1 — the counter is otherwise defined as the number of agreements enrolled in the account whose `agreement_status` is `ACTIVE` or `CANCELLATION_PENDING`, and every subsequent freeze, unfreeze, cooling-off cancellation and cancellation finalization recomputes it from that definition, while a notice-period cancellation request recomputes nothing and leaves the stored figure exactly as it stands. That counter is the one derived figure in this system that moves without a version bump: neither the increment at signing nor any later recomputation of it touches the `CorporateAccount`'s own `version` or `updated_at`, so a caller watching the account sees `enrolled_agreement_count` change while the version stands still. The home club's `version` increments by 1, which is exactly why the next signing at the same club needs a freshly read `expected_club_version`. What signing does *not* do is move the club's `active_agreement_count`: a `PENDING` agreement is not yet an active membership, and the counter only moves when the first payment lands, as §3.4 describes.

### 3.4. The Dues Ledger, Delinquency and Collections

Dues are not billed by this service; they are *recorded* in it. The third-party billing vendor — and, for administrative correction, headquarters — appends immutable entries to a per-agreement ledger, and every derived money figure on the agreement is a projection over that ledger rather than a running total nudged up and down. Each entry names its `agreement_id`, an `event_type`, an `amount_cents` of at least 0, the `occurred_on` date on which the vendor processed it, and optionally a `due_on` date. Creation returns 201 and the entry is never updated or deleted: as §2.5 states, a ledger entry has no `version` and no `updated_at`.

Six event types exist. `DUES_POSTED`, `PAYMENT_RECEIVED`, `PAYMENT_FAILED` and `WRITE_OFF` may be appended through the ledger endpoint by the `BillingPartner` or a `BrandAdministrator`, and those entries are stamped with `source` `BILLING_PARTNER`. The other two, `TRANSFER_FEE_POSTED` and `COLLECTION_PLACED`, are system-generated only — no client may append them directly — and carry `source` `SYSTEM`. A `DUES_POSTED` entry requires `due_on`; every other event type must leave `due_on` null. `PAYMENT_FAILED` and `COLLECTION_PLACED` must carry `amount_cents` of exactly 0 and move no money. Three of those rules are schema-band rules answered with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400) before the referenced agreement is even looked up: naming `TRANSFER_FEE_POSTED` or `COLLECTION_PLACED` as the `event_type`, which lies outside the enumeration this endpoint accepts at all; supplying a `due_on` on any entry that is not `DUES_POSTED`; and supplying a non-zero `amount_cents` on a `PAYMENT_FAILED`. A `DUES_POSTED` that omits `due_on` is by contrast a money-gate failure, as the rest of this paragraph describes, and a zero `amount_cents` on a `DUES_POSTED`, `PAYMENT_RECEIVED` or `WRITE_OFF` entry is perfectly legal. Every appended entry also stores `resulting_outstanding_balance_cents`, a snapshot of the agreement's outstanding balance immediately after that entry was folded into the projection, so the ledger can be replayed for audit. A `PAYMENT_RECEIVED` or `WRITE_OFF` amount that exceeds the agreement's current outstanding balance, a `DUES_POSTED` entry that omits `due_on`, and any dues entry posted against an agreement whose `agreement_status` is `CANCELLED` are all refused with `ERR_INVALID_DUES_EVENT`.

The projection re-runs in full on every append, and its ordering rules are contractual. All of the agreement's entries are considered in ascending `occurred_on`, with ascending `created_at` breaking ties and creation sequence, oldest first, breaking whatever tie remains. Charges are the entries whose type is `DUES_POSTED` or `TRANSFER_FEE_POSTED`, and they are considered in ascending `due_on`, with ascending `created_at` and then the same creation-sequence tie-break behind it. That last tie-break decides most real cases rather than being a formality, because the logical clock never advances on its own and a whole ledger appended at one clock setting shares a single `created_at`. `dues_paid_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` over every `PAYMENT_RECEIVED` entry. The write-off total is the sum of `amount_cents` over every `WRITE_OFF` entry. `outstanding_balance_cents` is the sum of the charge amounts minus the payment total minus the write-off total, and it may legitimately go to zero or below.

Ageing is decided by first-in-first-out application, which is what identifies *which* charge is the oldest unsatisfied one. Available credit starts as the payment total plus the write-off total. Walking the charges in their strict due-date order, each charge whose full amount the remaining credit can cover consumes that amount from the credit and is treated as satisfied; the first charge the remaining credit cannot fully cover is the earliest unsatisfied charge, and the walk stops there. If `outstanding_balance_cents` is at or below zero, or if no unsatisfied charge was found, then `delinquency_days` is 0. Otherwise `delinquency_days` is the number of whole days from that earliest unsatisfied charge's `due_on` to the logical clock date, floored at 0 so that a charge not yet due never produces a negative age.

The collections dimension is then resolved in a strict cascade of conditions, and the order matters because several can hold at once. If `outstanding_balance_cents` is at or below zero, `dues_status` becomes `CURRENT`. Otherwise, if the prior `dues_status` was `IN_COLLECTIONS` or `WRITTEN_OFF`, it stays exactly where it was — those two states are sticky and a partial payment does not walk them back. Otherwise, if `delinquency_days` is at least 30, `dues_status` becomes `DELINQUENT`. Otherwise, if `delinquency_days` is at least 1, it becomes `PAST_DUE`. Otherwise it becomes `CURRENT`. The boundary is exact and inclusive on the delinquency side: an age of 29 days is `PAST_DUE`, and an age of 30 days is `DELINQUENT`. Paying an account down to a zero or negative balance is the *only* escape from the sticky `IN_COLLECTIONS` and `WRITTEN_OFF` states, and it resets `delinquency_days` to 0 at the same time.

Each of those transitions carries its own cascade. Moving `CURRENT → PAST_DUE` recomputes the home club's `delinquent_member_count` and `collections_watch`, which may well leave both unchanged, because `PAST_DUE` does not count as delinquent. Moving `PAST_DUE → DELINQUENT` does count: the home club's `delinquent_member_count` increases and `collections_watch` is recomputed, and because that flag may have flipped to true, the owning franchisee's `compliance_score` is recomputed and the franchisee is demoted to `PROBATION` if the score fell below 70 — one event, three entities, and a `version` increment on the agreement, the club and the franchisee. Moving `DELINQUENT → PAST_DUE`, which happens when a payment satisfies the oldest unpaid dues rows but leaves a balance aged between 1 and 29 days, decreases the club's `delinquent_member_count`, recomputes `collections_watch` and recomputes the franchisee's `compliance_score`, incrementing the agreement's, the club's and the franchisee's `version`. Moving to `CURRENT` from `PAST_DUE`, `DELINQUENT`, `IN_COLLECTIONS` or `WRITTEN_OFF` decreases the club's `delinquent_member_count` when the prior status was one of the three delinquent ones, recomputes `collections_watch` and the franchisee's `compliance_score`, and increments the agreement's, the club's and the franchisee's `version`. Those three increments are unconditional rather than change-driven: every entry appended through the ledger endpoint re-derives the home club's counters and the owning franchisee's standing figures and bumps the `version` of the agreement, of the club and of the franchisee even when nothing moved at all — a `DUES_POSTED` that leaves the account `CURRENT`, or a `PAYMENT_FAILED` that moves no money, still bumps all three.

The club-level share that drives all of this is recomputed from scratch on every dues-status or contract-status change and on every completed transfer. The denominator is the number of agreements whose `home_club_id` is that club and whose `agreement_status` is `ACTIVE`, `FROZEN` or `CANCELLATION_PENDING`; that figure is stored as `active_agreement_count`. The numerator is how many of those same agreements have a `dues_status` of `DELINQUENT`, `IN_COLLECTIONS` or `WRITTEN_OFF`; that figure is stored as `delinquent_member_count`. `collections_watch` is true when the denominator is greater than 0 and the numerator divided by the denominator is strictly greater than 0.15 — more than fifteen percent of live memberships in arrears. The comparison is exact and strict at the boundary: a share of exactly 0.15, such as three delinquent memberships out of twenty, does *not* raise the flag, while four out of twenty does.

The first payment on a new contract does double duty. When a `PAYMENT_RECEIVED` entry with an `amount_cents` greater than 0 is the first such entry on the agreement, the contract dimension advances `PENDING → ACTIVE` in the same request: the home club's `active_agreement_count` increases by 1, `collections_watch` is recomputed from the new share, the owning franchisee's `compliance_score` is recomputed, and the agreement's, the club's and the franchisee's `version` each increment. Until that payment arrives the membership is signed but not live, and it contributes to no club counter.

Placing an account with a collections agency is the `BillingPartner`'s exclusive act and requires an `agency_reference` identifying the placement at the vendor. It moves the collections dimension `DELINQUENT → IN_COLLECTIONS`, and both of its preconditions are checked: an account not currently in `DELINQUENT` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_DUES_STATE`, and an account whose `delinquency_days` is below 60 — two months of unpaid dues — is refused with `ERR_COLLECTIONS_THRESHOLD_NOT_MET`. Placement appends a system ledger entry of type `COLLECTION_PLACED` with `amount_cents` 0, `source` `SYSTEM`, `due_on` null, an `occurred_on` of the logical clock date and a `resulting_outstanding_balance_cents` that merely snapshots the balance as it already stood — placement re-runs no projection, so `outstanding_balance_cents` and `delinquency_days` are left exactly where they were — leaves the club's `delinquent_member_count` untouched because `DELINQUENT` and `IN_COLLECTIONS` both count as delinquent, and increments the agreement's `version` and only the agreement's: the home club's counters and the owning franchisee's standing figures are both re-derived in the same request, but neither of those two records takes a `version` increment here. The `agency_reference` is validated as a non-empty string and then discarded — it is persisted neither on the agreement nor on the ledger entry.

Writing an account off is a ledger act rather than a dedicated operation. A `WRITE_OFF` entry may only be folded in while the collections dimension is `IN_COLLECTIONS`, and its `amount_cents` may not exceed the agreement's current `outstanding_balance_cents`; the balance is then re-projected. If the re-projected `outstanding_balance_cents` is still above zero, `dues_status` becomes `WRITTEN_OFF`; if the write-off cleared the balance entirely, the agreement lands on `CURRENT` instead. Either way the agreement's `version` increments. Attempting a write-off against an account that is not `IN_COLLECTIONS` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_DUES_STATE`.

The full ledger of one agreement can be listed in its canonical ascending `occurred_on`, then ascending `created_at`, then creation-sequence order — the same ordering the projection walks — and that listing is the only place the system-generated `TRANSFER_FEE_POSTED` and `COLLECTION_PLACED` rows become visible, since no creation response ever returns them.

### 3.5. Freezes, Cancellation Rights and Settlement

A membership can be paused. The home club's `ClubManager` may put a live membership on hold, advancing the contract dimension `ACTIVE → FROZEN` with an optional `reason`, which is validated as a string when supplied and then discarded rather than persisted on the agreement. Three conditions must hold: the agreement must currently be `ACTIVE`, or the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_AGREEMENT_STATE`; the acting manager must be the home club, or the request is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`; and the collections dimension must be `CURRENT` or `PAST_DUE`, so a membership that has already reached `DELINQUENT` or worse cannot be frozen out of trouble and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_DUES_STATE`. Freezing recomputes the enrolled agreement count of the named corporate account when the agreement carries a `corporate_account_id`, and increments the agreement's `version`. It deliberately leaves the club's `active_agreement_count` alone: a frozen membership still counts as active in the collections-watch denominator, so freezing cannot be used to flatter a club's arrears share.

Returning a frozen membership to service advances it `FROZEN → ACTIVE` with an optional `note`, likewise validated and then discarded, and its only preconditions are that the agreement is `FROZEN` and that the acting manager is the home club. In particular, the collections dimension is irrelevant here — the `ACTIVE ↔ FROZEN` cycle runs entirely independently of `dues_status`, which keeps advancing from billing events all the way through a freeze, so a membership frozen while `CURRENT` may well come back as `DELINQUENT`, and it comes back regardless. Unfreezing likewise recomputes the corporate account's enrolled agreement count where one applies and increments the agreement's `version`.

Cancellation is the member's own right, exercised by the `Member` whose `member_id` the agreement names, with an optional `reason` that is validated as a string when supplied and then discarded rather than persisted. It is available only from `ACTIVE`, and it is barred while the collections dimension is `DELINQUENT`, `IN_COLLECTIONS` or `WRITTEN_OFF`: a member cannot walk away from arrears, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_CANCELLATION_BLOCKED_BY_ARREARS`. The request always stamps `cancellation_requested_on` with the logical clock date, and then splits into two branches on the statute of the agreement's own pinned `state_code` — the same endpoint, the same actor, and two disjoint sets of consequences.

**Branch A, the statutory cooling-off window.** When the number of whole days from `signed_on` to the logical clock date is at most the statute's cooling-off days — 5 for `CA`, 3 for `TX`, 3 for `NY` — the consumer is inside their statutory right of rescission and the contract unwinds immediately. `cancellation_effective_on` is set to the logical clock date itself, with no notice period at all. `refund_cents` is the sum of `initiation_fee_cents` and `dues_paid_cents`, which is every cent the club has collected. `early_termination_fee_cents` is 0, and `net_settlement_cents` therefore equals `refund_cents`. The contract dimension goes straight to `CANCELLED`, and because a live membership just disappeared, the cascade fires in full: the home club's `active_agreement_count` decreases by 1 and its `delinquent_member_count` and `collections_watch` are recomputed; because those club counters moved, the owning franchisee's `compliance_score` is recomputed; the member's `agreement_count` decreases by 1 and, if it has reached 0, the member flips to `INACTIVE`; the corporate account's enrolled agreement count is recomputed where a `corporate_account_id` applies; and the agreement's, the club's and the member's `version` each increment by 1 — the club's and the member's unconditionally, whether or not their recomputed figures actually moved. The `Franchisee` is the one record this unwind rewrites without bumping: its `compliance_score` and, where the score has fallen far enough, its `status` and the withdrawal of its approved promotions all happen in this same request, while the franchisee's own `version` and `updated_at` stand still.

**Branch B, the contractual notice period.** When more than the statutory cooling-off days have elapsed since `signed_on`, the member owes notice instead. `cancellation_effective_on` becomes the logical clock date plus the agreement's pinned `cancellation_notice_days`. The refund calculation asks how far the member has pre-paid: the number of fully paid periods is `dues_paid_cents` divided by `effective_monthly_dues_cents`, discarding any fraction, and the paid-through date is `signed_on` plus 30 days multiplied by that number of periods. The unused days are the whole days from `cancellation_effective_on` to the paid-through date. When the governing statute prorates on cancellation — `CA` and `TX` do, `NY` does not — and the unused days are greater than 0, `refund_cents` is `effective_monthly_dues_cents` multiplied by those unused days, divided by 30, rounded half up to whole cents. In every other case, including any `NY` agreement and any case where the paid-through date is on or before the effective date, `refund_cents` is 0.

The early-termination fee on that same branch charges for the unexpired term. The remaining months are the whole days from `cancellation_effective_on` to `term_end_on`, divided by 30 and rounded *up* to the next whole number, floored at 0 so that an effective date at or beyond `term_end_on` yields no remaining months. The monthly charge is `effective_monthly_dues_cents` multiplied by 0.5 — half a month's dues — rounded half up to whole cents, and that rounded monthly figure is then multiplied by the remaining months. Only after the multiplication is the cap applied: `early_termination_fee_cents` is the lesser of that product and 15000 cents. Rounding the product instead of the monthly charge, or applying the cap per month instead of to the total, produces a different figure and is wrong. `net_settlement_cents` is then `refund_cents` minus `early_termination_fee_cents`, and a negative result is meaningful: it means the member owes the club rather than the reverse.

Branch B leaves the contract dimension at `CANCELLATION_PENDING` and touches nothing else. The club's `active_agreement_count`, the member's `agreement_count`, the franchisee's `compliance_score` and the corporate enrolment count all stay exactly where they were, because the membership is still live through the notice period, and only the agreement's own `version` increments.

Closing out a notice-period cancellation is the home club's job, advancing `CANCELLATION_PENDING → CANCELLED` with an optional `note`. It requires that the logical clock date has reached `cancellation_effective_on`; earlier attempts are refused with `ERR_NOTICE_PERIOD_NOT_ELAPSED`, and the boundary is inclusive, so the operation succeeds on the effective date itself. Finalization fires exactly the same cascade the cooling-off branch fired: the club's `active_agreement_count` decreases by 1 with `delinquent_member_count` and `collections_watch` recomputed, the franchisee's `compliance_score` recomputed, the member's `agreement_count` decreased by 1 with a flip to `INACTIVE` at zero, the corporate account's enrolled agreement count recomputed where applicable, and a `version` increment on the agreement, the club and the member — but again not on the franchisee, whose standing figures are re-derived in place without a bump. The optional `note` is validated as a string and then discarded. `CANCELLED` is terminal for everything that gates on the contract dimension — a cancelled agreement can neither be frozen nor cancelled again, and no further dues event may be posted against it — but the transfer machinery of §3.6 gates on the collections dimension alone, so a cancelled agreement whose `dues_status` is clean may still have a transfer opened, approved and completed on it.

### 3.6. Moving a Membership Between Clubs

Members move house, change jobs, and expect their gym membership to follow them. Because the home club is the billing club, moving a membership moves revenue between two independent businesses, so the receiving side — not the releasing side — holds the decision. The releasing club's `ClubManager` opens the request, naming the `agreement_id` being moved, the `from_club_id` it is leaving, the `to_club_id` it is joining, the `expected_agreement_version` demanded by §2.7, and a member-supplied `reason`. The request is created in `REQUESTED` with `transfer_fee_cents`, `effective_on` and `decided_by_user_id` all null, and it increments the agreement's `version` by 1 — which is why two transfer attempts in a row need two separate reads of the agreement.

Opening the request is gated on five things beyond ownership and concurrency. The supplied `from_club_id` must equal the agreement's current `home_club_id`, otherwise `ERR_HOME_CLUB_MISMATCH`. The two clubs must differ, otherwise `ERR_SAME_CLUB_TRANSFER`. The destination club must be `OPEN` with an `equipment_compliance_state` other than `NON_COMPLIANT`, otherwise `ERR_CLUB_NOT_OPERATIONAL` — the brand will not push a member into a club it has suspended. A destination club whose `state_code` differs from the agreement's own pinned `state_code` is prohibited whenever the agreement's governing statute forbids cross-state portability, which is the case for `CA`, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_CROSS_STATE_TRANSFER_PROHIBITED`; `TX` and `NY` agreements travel freely. Finally, a membership in arrears cannot be handed off: a `dues_status` of `DELINQUENT`, `IN_COLLECTIONS` or `WRITTEN_OFF` is refused with `ERR_TRANSFER_BLOCKED_BY_ARREARS`. Two things are conspicuously not gated here. The releasing club's own posture is never examined at all — a `PRE_OPENING` or `SUSPENDED` club, or one sitting at `NON_COMPLIANT`, may still release a membership it holds — and neither is the agreement's contract dimension, so a `PENDING`, `ACTIVE`, `FROZEN`, `CANCELLATION_PENDING` or even `CANCELLED` agreement may have a transfer opened on it provided its collections dimension is clean.

The receiving club then decides, supplying a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `DENY` and an optional `note`. Only the manager of the club named as `to_club_id` may decide — an attempt by the releasing club is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` — and only from `REQUESTED`, with anything else refused as `ERR_INVALID_TRANSFER_STATE`. Approval re-checks the destination's posture at decision time, because weeks may have passed: the receiving club must still be `OPEN` and must not be `NON_COMPLIANT`, otherwise `ERR_CLUB_NOT_OPERATIONAL`, and the agreement's `dues_status` must still be `CURRENT` or `PAST_DUE`, otherwise `ERR_TRANSFER_BLOCKED_BY_ARREARS`. Approval moves the request `REQUESTED → APPROVED`, stamps `decided_by_user_id` with the deciding club's identifier, pins `transfer_fee_cents`, and increments the request's `version` — and, exactly like denial, it leaves the agreement itself untouched. Cross-state portability is not re-examined at this gate, having been settled when the request was opened, and the optional `note` is validated as a string and then discarded. Denial moves it `REQUESTED → DENIED`, stamps `decided_by_user_id`, and increments the request's `version` — and that is all it does: `transfer_fee_cents` stays null and the agreement is not touched in any way.

The fee itself distinguishes an internal reshuffle from a genuine handover of revenue. When the releasing club and the receiving club belong to the same franchisee — that is, both clubs carry the same `franchisee_id` — the move is intra-franchise and `transfer_fee_cents` is exactly 0. When the two clubs belong to different franchisees, the fee is a base of 2500 cents plus half of the agreement's pinned `effective_monthly_dues_cents`, where that percentage component is rounded half up to whole cents before the base is added.

Executing the approved move is also the receiving club's act, advancing the request `APPROVED → COMPLETED` with an optional `note`. It requires that the agreement's `home_club_id` still equals the request's `from_club_id`; if some other transfer got there first, the attempt is refused with `ERR_HOME_CLUB_MISMATCH`. Completion stamps `effective_on` with the logical clock date and then cascades through three levels. First, the agreement's `home_club_id` is rewritten to the `to_club_id` and its `transfer_count` increments by 1. Second, because the agreement's home club moved, both clubs' counters are recomputed from the share definition of §3.4 — the releasing club loses this agreement from its `active_agreement_count` and, where applicable, from its `delinquent_member_count`, the receiving club gains it, and `collections_watch` is recomputed on both. Third, because either club's `collections_watch` may have flipped, the `compliance_score` of *both* clubs' franchisees is recomputed, which can demote one franchisee and rescue nothing on the other side, since a score never recovers standing automatically.

When the pinned `transfer_fee_cents` is greater than 0, completion also appends a system ledger entry of type `TRANSFER_FEE_POSTED` carrying that amount, with `due_on` set to the logical clock date plus 15 days and `source` `SYSTEM`, and then re-runs the dues projection of §3.4 over the whole ledger — so a cross-franchise transfer can, by itself, push a previously `CURRENT` membership towards `PAST_DUE` once the clock advances past that due date. When the fee is 0, no ledger entry is created at all. Completion increments the `version` of the request, the agreement, and both clubs — the two clubs unconditionally, even when neither set of counters actually moved because the agreement was in neither club's live set — while the two franchisees have their standing figures re-derived without a `version` increment of their own. The optional `note` is validated as a string and then discarded, and the destination club's `status` and `equipment_compliance_state` are not re-examined at this final step. Two things stay exactly as signed: the agreement's `state_code` keeps the statute of the club where it was originally sold, and its `effective_monthly_dues_cents` keeps the price it was sold at, even when the receiving club's list pricing differs. Both `DENIED` and `COMPLETED` are terminal.

### 3.7. Cross-Club Guest Usage and Inter-Club Settlement

A member with multi-club access who trains at a club other than their own is consuming a facility their dues do not pay for, so the visited club is owed a usage fee by the visiting member's home club. Usage is recorded in batches rather than one visit at a time. The hosting club's `ClubManager` — or a `BrandAdministrator` — records a batch naming the `agreement_id` of the visiting member, the `host_club_id` that hosted them, the `period_month` the visits fall in as a `YYYY-MM` string, and the `visit_count` in the batch, which must be at least 1 and carries no upper bound of any kind. Recording is the one `ClubManager` write that is not ownership-scoped, as §2.1 notes: any club manager may record a batch naming any `host_club_id`, and the acting identifier is neither checked against it nor stored on the batch.

Three facts are snapshotted at recording time so that later history cannot rewrite them. `home_club_id_at_visit` captures the agreement's `home_club_id` as it stands at that instant, which means a later transfer never re-points historical usage at the new home club. `guest_fee_cents_at_visit` captures the host club's `guest_fee_cents` as it stands at that instant. And `usage_fee_cents` is `visit_count` multiplied by `guest_fee_cents_at_visit`. The batch is created unsettled, with `settlement_id` null, and it is immutable: it has no `version`, no `updated_at`, and no update operation.

Recording is refused in three circumstances. If the agreement does not carry `multi_club_access` as true, or if the supplied `host_club_id` equals the agreement's current `home_club_id` — you cannot be a guest at your own club — the request is refused with `ERR_GUEST_VISIT_NOT_ALLOWED`. If the host club is not `OPEN`, or its `equipment_compliance_state` is `NON_COMPLIANT`, the request is refused with `ERR_CLUB_NOT_OPERATIONAL`. And a `period_month` that falls before the calendar month of the agreement's `signed_on` date is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, since the membership did not exist to be used; that test is a lower bound only, so a `period_month` later than the logical clock's own month is accepted. The three gates are evaluated in the band order of §2.6 rather than in the order just narrated: the host club's operational state is tested first, so a batch naming a `SUSPENDED` or `NON_COMPLIANT` host club for an agreement that also lacks `multi_club_access` answers `ERR_CLUB_NOT_OPERATIONAL`, and the `period_month` test comes last of the three. What recording never consults is the standing of the membership itself or of its home club: the agreement's `agreement_status` and `dues_status` are both ignored, so a `PENDING`, a `FROZEN` or even an already-`CANCELLED` membership may still have usage recorded against it, and the home club is read only for its identifier — neither its `status` nor its `equipment_compliance_state` is examined, and it is not touched, so no `version` anywhere in the system moves when a batch is recorded.

Settlement is a headquarters function, and it operates on one club pair for one month at a time. Creating a settlement names the `period_month`, the `home_club_id` that will pay, and the `host_club_id` that will be credited. The two must differ, or the request is refused with `ERR_SAME_CLUB_TRANSFER`. Only one settlement may exist per `period_month`, `home_club_id` and `host_club_id` triple: a second attempt while an `OPEN` or `POSTED` settlement already covers that triple is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_SETTLEMENT_PERIOD`. The pair is ordered rather than symmetric, so the mirror triple that reverses payer and payee is a different triple entirely and may be settled for the same month alongside it; and a `REVERSED` settlement never blocks anything, since only `OPEN` and `POSTED` count. The sweep collects every guest usage batch whose `settlement_id` is null, whose `period_month` matches, whose `host_club_id` matches, and whose `home_club_id_at_visit` matches the settlement's `home_club_id`, considered in ascending `created_at` order with creation sequence, oldest first, breaking the ties a stationary logical clock makes routine. An empty sweep is refused with `ERR_NO_SETTLEABLE_USAGE`; there is no such thing as an *empty* settlement, though a zero-value one is possible when every swept batch carried a `guest_fee_cents_at_visit` of 0. Neither named club's own posture is consulted at any point in this operation: a `SUSPENDED`, a `PRE_OPENING` or a `NON_COMPLIANT` club settles exactly like an `OPEN` one, on either side of the pair, and no franchisee standing figure is re-derived. Both clubs must nevertheless exist, the `home_club_id` being resolved before the `host_club_id`.

The waterfall over the swept rows runs in a pinned order, because the rebate tier looks circular if the steps are taken in the wrong sequence. `event_count` is the number of swept rows and `visit_count` is the sum of their visit counts. `gross_fee_cents` is the sum of their `usage_fee_cents`. Step one, the brand's administration hold: `admin_hold_cents` is `gross_fee_cents` multiplied by 0.08 — eight percent — rounded half up to whole cents. Step two: `net_before_rebate_cents` is `gross_fee_cents` minus `admin_hold_cents`. Step three, tier selection, which is keyed on `net_before_rebate_cents` and on nothing else — never on `gross_fee_cents` and never on the final payable: `rebate_pct` is 0.00 when `net_before_rebate_cents` is below 50000 cents, 0.03 when it is at least 50000 but below 100000 cents, and 0.06 when it is at or above 100000 cents. Step four, and only now: `volume_rebate_cents` is `net_before_rebate_cents` multiplied by `rebate_pct`, rounded half up to whole cents. Finally `net_payable_cents` is `net_before_rebate_cents` minus `volume_rebate_cents`. Re-keying the tier on the final `net_payable_cents` creates a fixed-point disagreement and is forbidden. Creation stamps the new settlement's identifier into the `settlement_id` of every swept batch and leaves the settlement `OPEN`, with `posted_on` and `reversed_on` null. No money has moved yet.

Posting an `OPEN` settlement, with an optional `note` — validated as a string when supplied and then discarded rather than persisted — is what moves money, and it applies no amount gate of its own: `net_payable_cents` is non-negative by construction, and a zero-value settlement posts without complaint. The home club's `settlement_ledger_cents` decreases by `net_payable_cents` and the host club's increases by the same amount, `status` advances `OPEN → POSTED`, `posted_on` records the logical clock date, and the settlement's `version` plus both clubs' `version` values each increment by 1 — the two clubs unconditionally, even in that zero-value case where neither ledger figure actually moved. Nothing else in the system is disturbed: no club counter is recomputed, no franchisee standing figure is re-derived, and the swept batches keep the `settlement_id` creation already stamped on them. Because every posting is a matched pair of equal and opposite legs, the sum of `settlement_ledger_cents` across all clubs remains exactly 0, as §2.8 requires. Posting anything that is not `OPEN` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_SETTLEMENT_STATE`.

Reversal is the compensating action and requires a `reason`, validated as a non-empty string and then discarded rather than persisted. It advances `POSTED → REVERSED`, adds `net_payable_cents` back to the home club's `settlement_ledger_cents` and subtracts the same amount from the host club's, clears `settlement_id` back to null on every batch the settlement had swept — making those batches eligible for a later settlement of the same triple — records `reversed_on` as the logical clock date, and increments the settlement's `version` and both clubs' `version` values by 1 each. Both club ledgers and the sweep flags return to exactly their pre-posting values with zero drift, and, exactly as at posting, no club counter and no franchisee standing figure is re-derived. The batches released are identified by their stored `settlement_id` rather than by replaying the original sweep criteria, so a batch is released if and only if it still carries this settlement's identifier. Reversing anything that is not `POSTED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_SETTLEMENT_STATE`, and `REVERSED` is terminal — a reversed settlement is never re-posted, though the released batches may be swept into a fresh one.

### 3.8. National Accounts and Corporate Revenue Allocation

Large employers buy memberships for their workforces at a negotiated rate, and the resulting subsidy arrives centrally rather than club by club, which means headquarters holds money that belongs to franchisees. A corporate account is opened by a `BrandAdministrator` with the employer's `name`, the `corporate_credit_pct` granted off list dues to every agreement enrolled under it — a fraction carried to 3 decimal places and accepted in the range 0.0 through 0.100, so at most a ten percent credit — and an `allocation_method` of either `USAGE_SHARE` or `EQUAL_SPLIT`, which defaults to `USAGE_SHARE` when omitted. A `corporate_credit_pct` above 0.100 is malformed rather than merely over-generous and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400), and `name` is any non-empty string with no length bound and no uniqueness requirement. The account is created `ACTIVE` with `pool_balance_cents`, `allocated_cents`, `lifetime_funded_cents` and `enrolled_agreement_count` all at 0.

Funding wires subsidy money in: a funding call carries an `amount_cents` that increases both `pool_balance_cents` and `lifetime_funded_cents` by that amount and increments the account's `version`. `lifetime_funded_cents` is monotonic and never decreases, which is what makes the conservation invariant of §2.8 checkable at any moment. Funding is otherwise ungated: the account's `status` is never consulted, so a `SUSPENDED` account may still be funded, and an `amount_cents` of exactly 0 is accepted — it leaves both figures where they were while still incrementing the `version`.

Allocation distributes the *entire* undistributed pool downward and may carry an optional `note`, validated as a string when supplied and then discarded rather than persisted. Each franchisee's weight is the number of agreements enrolled in this account — that is, carrying this `corporate_account_id` — whose `agreement_status` is `ACTIVE` or `CANCELLATION_PENDING` and whose home club belongs to that franchisee. A franchisee is eligible when its `status` is `ACTIVE`, its `national_account_eligible` flag is true, and its weight is at least 1; a franchisee excluded by that flag is excluded from every candidate set no matter how many enrolled members it serves. If no franchisee is eligible, the allocation is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_FRANCHISEE`, and an allocation attempted while `pool_balance_cents` is 0 is refused earlier with `ERR_INVALID_ALLOCATION_STATE`.

When the method is `EQUAL_SPLIT`, every eligible franchisee's weight is replaced by 1 before the split, so the pool divides by head count of operators rather than by enrolled members; when it is `USAGE_SHARE`, the weights computed above are used as they stand. Each franchisee's base share is the pool multiplied by that franchisee's weight, divided by the total weight across all eligible franchisees, with any fraction discarded. The remainder is the pool minus the sum of all base shares, and it is strictly less than the number of eligible franchisees. That remainder is then distributed one cent at a time, in a fully deterministic order: eligible franchisees are sorted by descending weight, with ascending `created_at` breaking ties and creation sequence, oldest first, breaking whatever tie remains — and that last key decides most real cases, since franchisees registered at one clock setting all carry the same `created_at` — and the first however-many franchisees in that order each receive one extra cent. A resource `id` is never a tie-break input. Every eligible franchisee's `corporate_allocation_cents` increases by its final share and that franchisee's own `version` increments by 1, `allocated_cents` increases by the whole pool, and `pool_balance_cents` is set to 0. Because of the explicit remainder waterfall, the distributed shares sum to the pool exactly — not one cent more or fewer. The account's `version` increments.

Recalling an allocation is its inverse and requires a `reason`, validated as a non-empty string and then discarded rather than persisted. The recall does not replay the original allocation from stored history; it re-runs the very same weighting and remainder waterfall over the eligible set *as that set stands at recall time*, applied to the whole of `allocated_cents`, and decreases each franchisee in that freshly computed set by exactly its freshly computed share, incrementing that franchisee's `version`. When nothing has moved in between, that is the exact inverse of the allocation. When something has — an enrolment ended, a franchisee slipped into `PROBATION`, a member transferred to another operator's club — the debits land on the current eligible set rather than on the original recipients, and a franchisee that has since become ineligible simply keeps the credit it was given. If no franchisee is eligible any more at all, the recall still succeeds and debits nobody: `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_FRANCHISEE` guards allocation only. Either way `pool_balance_cents` increases by `allocated_cents`, `allocated_cents` returns to 0, and the account's `version` increments — restoring `pool_balance_cents` plus `allocated_cents` to equal `lifetime_funded_cents`. A recall attempted while `allocated_cents` is 0 is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ALLOCATION_STATE`, and the account's own `status` is never consulted, so a `SUSPENDED` account may be recalled as freely as an `ACTIVE` one.

Suspending an account, which requires a `reason` — validated as a non-empty string and then discarded rather than persisted — advances it `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` and increments its `version`. Suspending an account that has already reached `SUSPENDED` is refused with `ERR_CORPORATE_ACCOUNT_SUSPENDED` (409). Suspension is a gate on *new* business only: no further agreement may enrol in the account, and an attempt to sign one naming it is refused with `ERR_CORPORATE_ACCOUNT_SUSPENDED`. It deliberately leaves `pool_balance_cents`, `allocated_cents` and `lifetime_funded_cents` untouched, so money already funded into a suspended account may still be allocated to the franchisees that earned it. `SUSPENDED` is terminal; there is no un-suspend.

### 3.9. Facility Inspections, Franchisee Standing and Royalty Reporting

Brand standards are enforced by walk-through inspection, and a bad inspection is not merely a note in a file: it can suspend a club, demote its franchisee, and wipe out that franchisee's approved promotions. A `ComplianceAuditor` or a `BrandAdministrator` schedules an inspection naming the `club_id` and the `inspection_date` of the walk-through. The date may not be later than the logical clock date — inspections are recorded after they happen, never booked into the future — and a future date is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The inspection is created `SCHEDULED` with `equipment_condition_pts`, `cleanliness_pts`, `safety_pts`, `equipment_age_months_max`, `refresh_cycle_months_applied`, `refresh_pts`, `score`, `variant_applied`, `scored_by_user_id`, `waived_by_user_id` and `waiver_reason` all null. Scheduling consults nothing about the club beyond the fact that it exists: any `status` and any `equipment_compliance_state` accepts an inspection, the club itself is not written to and its `version` does not move, and any number of `SCHEDULED` inspections may stand open against one club at once. The date test is the last gate applied, so a future `inspection_date` against a club that does not exist answers `ERR_CLUB_NOT_FOUND` rather than the 400.

Scoring is the auditor's act and is available only from `SCHEDULED`; scoring anything else is refused with `ERR_INVALID_INSPECTION_STATE`. The auditor supplies four numbers, each of which is validated on its own scale: `equipment_condition_pts`, `cleanliness_pts` and `safety_pts` are integers from 0 through 100, and `equipment_age_months_max` — the age in months of the oldest cardio or strength asset on the floor — is an integer from 0 through 240. The inspected club must be in one of its three live states, `PRE_OPENING`, `OPEN` or `SUSPENDED`; a suspended club is inspected precisely so that it can recover, and a club in any other state is refused with `ERR_CLUB_NOT_OPERATIONAL`. That club gate is applied after the inspection's own state gate, so a non-`SCHEDULED` inspection at a non-live club still answers `ERR_INVALID_INSPECTION_STATE`.

The scoring order matters, because the refresh-cycle variant is chosen first and it changes the arithmetic that follows. The accelerated cycle applies when the club's `opened_on` date falls before 2020-01-01, or when the club's current `equipment_compliance_state` is `WATCH` — either condition alone is enough, and the check reads the club's compliance state as it stands *before* this inspection updates it. On the accelerated cycle, `refresh_cycle_months_applied` is 42 months and `variant_applied` is `ACCELERATED_CYCLE`; otherwise `refresh_cycle_months_applied` is the brand-standard 60 months and `variant_applied` is `STANDARD_CYCLE`.

Then `refresh_pts` is computed: multiply 100 by `equipment_age_months_max`, divide by `refresh_cycle_months_applied`, round that quotient half up to a whole number, subtract it from 100, and floor the result at 0, so an over-age floor earns zero refresh points rather than negative ones. This rounding to an integer happens *before* the sub-score is weighted, and skipping it changes the outcome. On the standard cycle a 54-month-old floor yields 100 minus 90, which is 10 refresh points; on the accelerated cycle the same 54 months yields 100 minus 129, floored to 0.

The `score` is then a single weighted sum, rounded half up to an integer exactly once, at the very end: `equipment_condition_pts` weighted at 0.40, plus `cleanliness_pts` at 0.25, plus `safety_pts` at 0.20, plus `refresh_pts` at 0.15. Rounding the individual weighted terms instead of the total changes the result and is wrong. Because the two cycles differ only in the refresh term, one identical set of four sub-scores can land on opposite sides of the pass mark depending purely on the club's opening date and prior compliance state.

Scoring writes `scored_by_user_id` from the `X-User-ID` header and then sets off the cascade described in §2.10, in order. The club's `last_inspection_score` is set to the new `score`, and its `equipment_compliance_state` is re-derived from that score alone: `COMPLIANT` at 85 or above, `WATCH` at 70 through 84, and `NON_COMPLIANT` below 70. A club that has just become `NON_COMPLIANT` and was `OPEN` moves to `SUSPENDED`; a club that was `SUSPENDED` and has just scored 70 or better moves back to `OPEN`. Those two are the only `status` moves scoring ever makes, and they are mutually exclusive: a club in any other state — a `PRE_OPENING` one, notably — keeps its `status` whatever the score, taking only the new `last_inspection_score` and `equipment_compliance_state`, and a `SUSPENDED` club that scores below 70 again simply stays `SUSPENDED`. The franchisee's `inspection_fail_streak` and `compliance_score` are then recomputed, which may demote the franchisee and withdraw its approved promotions. Finally the inspection auto-resolves, within this same request, to `CLOSED` when the `score` is 70 or higher and to `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED` when it is below 70; `SCORED` is therefore a transitional label that is never persisted and never returned, so no retrieval, listing or `status` filter ever shows an inspection sitting in it, and the inspection just scored is already counted by the `inspection_fail_streak` walk described below. The inspection's `version`, the club's `version` and the franchisee's `version` each increment by 1, the club's and the franchisee's unconditionally, whether or not the recomputation actually moved anything on either record.

Remediation may be waived, but not by the person who scored it. A waiver moves `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED → REMEDIATION_WAIVED`, requires a `reason` which is stored as `waiver_reason`, and records `waived_by_user_id` from the `X-User-ID` header. The acting `X-User-ID` may not equal `scored_by_user_id`; the same auditor attempting to forgive their own finding is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. An inspection that is not in `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED`, or whose `score` is null or at or above 70 and therefore never needed remediation, is refused with `ERR_REMEDIATION_NOT_REQUIRED`. The segregation test runs first, ahead of that gate, as the band order of §2.6 requires: the auditor who scored an inspection and then tries to waive it after it has already reached `REMEDIATION_WAIVED` or `CLOSED` receives `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, not `ERR_REMEDIATION_NOT_REQUIRED`. Against a still-`SCHEDULED` inspection the comparison has nothing to compare — `scored_by_user_id` is still null — so it passes and the remediation gate answers instead. The waiver sets the club's `equipment_compliance_state` to `WATCH` — leaving `last_inspection_score` untouched, because the failing score is a historical fact — and returns a `SUSPENDED` club to `OPEN`. It deliberately does *not* reset the franchisee's `inspection_fail_streak`; the franchisee's `compliance_score` is recomputed with the streak unchanged, so a waiver reopens the club without erasing the franchisee's record. The inspection's `version` and the club's `version` each increment by 1, and those two are the only increments the waiver makes: the franchisee's counters and `compliance_score` are re-derived in place — and, if the fresh score demands it, an `ACTIVE` franchisee is demoted to `PROBATION` and its approved promotions are withdrawn, each withdrawal bumping that promotion's own `version` — while the franchisee's own `version` and `updated_at` stand still.

A `BrandAdministrator` files an inspection away from either `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED` or `REMEDIATION_WAIVED` to `CLOSED`, optionally with a `note` that is validated as a string and then discarded rather than persisted, incrementing the inspection's `version`; closing from any other state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_INSPECTION_STATE`. Closing a `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED` inspection is purely administrative and does not lift the club's suspension — only a waiver or a passing re-inspection does that — and it is the one inspection transition that cascades nowhere at all: the club's posture is left exactly as it stands, the franchisee's standing figures are not even re-derived, and neither record takes a `version` increment. Because all four of `SCORED`, `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED`, `REMEDIATION_WAIVED` and `CLOSED` feed the fail-streak walk alike, closing an inspection never changes the streak either. `CLOSED` is terminal.

A franchisee's `compliance_score` is a derived figure, recomputed from scratch on every event that could move it: scoring an inspection, waiving remediation, appending a billing event, placing an agreement in collections, requesting or finalizing a cancellation, completing a transfer, submitting a royalty report, adjudicating one, and attempting a reinstatement. Only four of those recomputations increment the franchisee's own `version`, and this is the complete list, closing out the general rule of §2.10: scoring an inspection, appending a billing event, a royalty report resolving to `DISPUTED`, and adjudicating a disputed report. On every other path in that list — waiving remediation, placing an agreement in collections, either cancellation branch, completing a transfer, a royalty report resolving to `ACCEPTED`, and the recomputation that opens a reinstatement attempt — the fresh counters, the fresh `compliance_score` and any resulting demotion to `PROBATION` with its promotion withdrawals are all written through, while the franchisee's own `version` and `updated_at` do not move. Three counters feed it. `inspection_fail_streak` is found by walking every inspection of every club belonging to this franchisee whose `status` is `SCORED`, `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED`, `REMEDIATION_WAIVED` or `CLOSED` and whose `score` is not null, in ascending `inspection_date` order with ascending `created_at` and then creation sequence, oldest first, breaking ties, counting up by one for each inspection scoring below 70 and resetting to zero at each inspection scoring 70 or better — so the figure is the length of the *current trailing* run of failures, not the lifetime total. `collections_watch_club_count` is the number of that franchisee's clubs whose `collections_watch` flag is true. `disputed_royalty_count` is the number of that franchisee's royalty reports whose `status` is `DISPUTED`.

`compliance_score` then starts at 100 and subtracts 20 for each step of `inspection_fail_streak`, a flat 20 if `collections_watch_club_count` is greater than zero — one flat penalty no matter how many clubs are on watch, not 20 per club — and 10 for each disputed royalty report, with the result clamped into 0 through 100. If the franchisee is `ACTIVE` and the freshly recomputed score is strictly below 70, the franchisee is demoted `ACTIVE → PROBATION`, which withdraws every `APPROVED` promotion it owns; a score of exactly 70 keeps it `ACTIVE`. A `BrandAdministrator` reinstates a franchisee with an optional `note`, which is validated as a string but not stored: the score is recomputed first, and the move `PROBATION → ACTIVE` is allowed only when that fresh score is 70 or above, with a still-deficient franchisee refused as `ERR_FRANCHISEE_ON_PROBATION`. The same `ERR_FRANCHISEE_ON_PROBATION` answers an attempt to reinstate a franchisee that is not on probation in the first place. That opening recomputation is written through whichever way the call then goes: a refused reinstatement still leaves the freshly recomputed counters and `compliance_score` on the record, and still demotes an `ACTIVE` franchisee whose recomputed score is below 70 and withdraws its approved promotions — but the recomputation on its own never touches the franchisee's `version`, so only a successful reinstatement increments it. Reinstatement increments the franchisee's `version` and does not restore the withdrawn promotions — those are gone, and new ones must be filed and reviewed.

Royalties are self-reported and independently verified. A `FranchiseOwner` submits a report naming its own `franchisee_id`, the `period_month` as `YYYY-MM`, and the `reported_member_count`, an integer of 0 or more. The `franchisee_id` must be the acting `X-User-ID`, and that is a plain string comparison made before the identifier is looked up at all, so an owner naming another operator — or an operator that exists nowhere in the system — is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403) rather than with any 404, as §2.6 records. The period must be closed: a `period_month` equal to or later than the logical clock's own month is refused with `ERR_REPORT_PERIOD_NOT_CLOSED`. Only one report may exist per `franchisee_id` and `period_month` pair, and a second is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ROYALTY_PERIOD`; that uniqueness test looks at the pair alone, whatever status the existing report has reached, so an `ACCEPTED`, a `DISPUTED` and an `ADJUSTED` report each block a resubmission just as firmly. The uniqueness test also precedes the closed-period test in the band order of §2.6, so a duplicate filed for a period that is also still open answers `ERR_DUPLICATE_ROYALTY_PERIOD`.

The server then derives the truth from its own ledger rather than trusting the submission. The qualifying set is every membership agreement whose home club belongs to this franchisee, whose `agreement_status` is `ACTIVE` or `CANCELLATION_PENDING`, and whose `signed_on` date is on or before the last calendar day of the reported month; the agreement's status as it stands at submission time is used, with no historical replay, and `PENDING`, `FROZEN` and `CANCELLED` agreements are excluded outright. `verified_member_count` is the size of that set and `verified_dues_basis_cents` is the sum of the set's `effective_monthly_dues_cents` values — summed first and rounded once, never rounded per agreement.

Two royalty figures are always computed and stored. The reported basis, `reported_dues_basis_cents`, is `reported_member_count` multiplied by the brand list price of 4500 cents per member per month, which ignores every promotion, corporate credit and multi-club credit; `reported_royalty_cents` is that basis multiplied by the franchisee's `royalty_rate_bps` divided by 10000, rounded half up to whole cents once at the end of that single expression rather than after the multiplication. The verified basis produces `royalty_due_cents` the same way, from `verified_dues_basis_cents` and the same rate — the rate being whatever the `Franchisee` record carries at submission time, since it is read live rather than replayed for the reported month. `royalty_due_cents` is the only figure ever billed; `reported_royalty_cents` is stored purely for audit, and because the verified basis is net of every discount while the reported basis is at list price, the two disagree whenever any agreement carries a discount or the reported head count is wrong.

`variance_pct` measures the overstatement of the reported count against the verified count, carried to 2 decimal places: `reported_member_count` minus `verified_member_count`, multiplied by 100, divided by `verified_member_count`, rounded half up to two decimals. It is negative when the franchisee under-reports. When `verified_member_count` is 0 the division is undefined, so `variance_pct` is exactly 0.00 if `reported_member_count` is also 0 and exactly 100.00 otherwise. The report is created `SUBMITTED`, with `adjusted_royalty_cents` and `adjudicated_by_user_id` both null, and immediately auto-resolves on the absolute value of `variance_pct` against a tolerance of 5.00 percent: a variance whose magnitude exceeds 5.00 resolves to `DISPUTED`, and anything at or within 5.00 resolves to `ACCEPTED`. Like `SCORED` on an inspection, `SUBMITTED` is a transitional label the creation response never shows — the report handed back is already `DISPUTED` or `ACCEPTED`, so no report ever sits in `SUBMITTED` to be read or filtered. `ACCEPTED` is terminal. Resolving to `DISPUTED` increments the franchisee's `disputed_royalty_count` by 1, recomputes its `compliance_score` — demoting it to `PROBATION` if that drops it below 70 — and increments the franchisee's `version` by 1 on that path. Resolving to `ACCEPTED` still re-derives the franchisee's three counters and `compliance_score` in the same request, and still demotes an `ACTIVE` franchisee whose freshly computed score is below 70 and withdraws its approved promotions, but leaves the franchisee's own `version` and `updated_at` exactly where they were.

Brand finance settles a `DISPUTED` report by adjudicating it with an `adjusted_royalty_cents` figure and an optional `note`. That figure must lie between `royalty_due_cents` and `reported_royalty_cents` inclusive — either endpoint is acceptable, and which of the two is the lower bound depends on which way the franchisee erred — and a figure outside that band is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. That band comparison is made after the state gate rather than in the schema band, so a report that is not `DISPUTED` answers `ERR_INVALID_ROYALTY_STATE` even when the figure supplied also lies outside the band; what the schema band checks about the figure is only that it is present and is an integer of at least 0. The optional `note` is validated as a string and then discarded rather than persisted. Adjudicating a report that is not `DISPUTED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ROYALTY_STATE`. Adjudication moves the report `DISPUTED → ADJUSTED`, stores the figure in `adjusted_royalty_cents`, records `adjudicated_by_user_id` from the `X-User-ID` header, decrements the franchisee's `disputed_royalty_count` by 1 and recomputes its `compliance_score` — so clearing a dispute releases the 10-point penalty, though it does not by itself reinstate a franchisee already on probation — and increments the report's `version` and the franchisee's `version` by 1 each. `ADJUSTED` is terminal.

## 5. Error Codes

Every failed request answers with the error envelope of §2.2, carrying one of the identifiers below in `data.error_id` together with a human-readable `message`. When a single request violates several rules at once, the identifier reported is the one belonging to the earliest band in the precedence order of §2.6. Two transport-level refusals sit outside those bands and outside the table below, and neither one consults the authentication headers: a request to a path no endpoint serves answers HTTP 404 with the `error_id` `ERR_NOT_FOUND`, and a request that names a served path with an HTTP method that path does not accept answers HTTP 405 with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
