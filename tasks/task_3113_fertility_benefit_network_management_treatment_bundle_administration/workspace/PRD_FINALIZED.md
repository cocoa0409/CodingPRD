# Fertility Benefit Network API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Employers that sponsor a fertility benefit have moved away from the dollar-cap model that dominated the last decade of benefit design. A family that needs in-vitro fertilization does not want to discover halfway through a stimulation protocol that a fixed cap has been reached and that the remainder of a cycle is now their own expense. Sponsors increasingly buy *treatment bundles* instead: a structured entitlement that names a number of cycles, a ceiling on transfers within a cycle, and a separate pool for the specialty medications that a cycle requires — with the network of contracting clinics priced against those same bundle definitions. This document specifies the API that administers that model end to end, from the moment a member is enrolled on a sponsoring employer account to the moment the last claim against a bundle is adjudicated, paid, or reversed.

The service sits at the junction of three parties whose incentives only partly align. The employer wants predictable exposure and evidence that its network is worth the negotiated rates. The member wants an advocate who can navigate clinical selection and a clear answer about what the plan will pay. The clinics, pharmacies, and laboratories that deliver care want to know in advance that a service is authorized, that a price has been agreed, and that the claim they submit will be adjudicated against that price rather than against a charge they invented. The API therefore treats *authorization*, *network quality*, and *claim adjudication* as first-class, mutually dependent concerns rather than as separate subsystems, and it exposes a single logical clock so that every window, expiry, and eligibility reading is reproducible.

Two design commitments shape almost every rule that follows. The first is that bundle consumption is *derived*: the number of cycles a bundle has left is never stored as a client-supplied truth, it is recomputed from the cycles and authorizations that reference the bundle, under one of two netting policies the sponsoring employer configures. The second is that the system is deliberately conservative about who may act: clinical decisions, claim decisions, and network decisions are segregated so that no single operator can both propose and dispose, and the version of every parent resource is pinned at the moment a dependent resource is created or paid so that a decision computed against a superseded plan shape is refused rather than applied.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

Every interaction with this API is governed by the conventions in this section. They are stated once here and relied upon throughout §3, and they apply uniformly to every resource the service exposes.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Callers authenticate by presenting two headers on every request: `X-User-ID`, which identifies the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, which names the capacity in which that principal is acting. A request that omits `X-User-Role`, or that names a capacity the service does not recognize, is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` and a 401 status. A request whose role is not permitted for the requested operation is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` and a 403 status. The two system-driving operations that advance or set the logical clock are the only ones exempt from the header requirement; every other operation, including reads, requires both headers.

Nine capacities are recognized, and the exact spelling of each matters: `Member`, `CareAdvocate`, `ClinicalReviewer`, `ClinicCoordinator`, `SpecialtyPharmacist`, `ClaimsProcessor`, `ClaimsReviewer`, `NetworkManager`, and `PlanAdministrator`.

Ownership is not uniform across those capacities. When `X-User-Role` is `Member`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Member`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Member` compare the resource's member foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`, so a member may read only their own records. When `X-User-Role` is `CareAdvocate`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `CareAdvocate`, and the resources the advocate owns — plans, pathway requests, medication orders, and surrender routes — carry a `care_advocate_id` that is compared against `X-User-ID` wherever an ownership check applies. The same entity-backed rule holds for `ClinicCoordinator`, whose `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Clinic`; a coordinator acts for one clinic of record, and clinic-scoped operations are performed in that clinic's name.

The remaining capacities are opaque operators. When `X-User-Role` is `ClinicalReviewer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque reviewer identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it as `decision_actor` on the authorization or pathway request the reviewer decides, but never compares it to any entity's `id`. The same is true of `SpecialtyPharmacist`, `ClaimsProcessor`, `ClaimsReviewer`, `NetworkManager`, and `PlanAdministrator`: each presents an opaque operator identifier that the server records where a rule requires attribution but does not match against a persisted row. Two of those opaque identifiers carry a compliance meaning rather than an ownership meaning. A `ClinicalReviewer` may not begin clinical review of, or render a decision on, an authorization or pathway request whose recorded `decision_actor` already equals their own `X-User-ID`, and a `ClaimsReviewer` deciding an appeal must present an `X-User-ID` different from the `decision_actor` recorded on the claim's last adjudication; both violations are refused with `ERR_SOD_CONFLICT` and a 403 status. A claim's recorded `decision_actor` begins life as the identity that submitted the claim, so a claim that has not yet been adjudicated already carries an actor the appeal decision must differ from. `PlanAdministrator` is the only capacity permitted to design employer accounts and bundle entitlements, to register care advocates, and to suspend or reactivate a sponsoring account.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination, Ordering, and Envelope Metadata

Collection reads accept `limit` and `offset` as query parameters. `limit` caps the number of rows returned and defaults to 10; it may be as low as 1 and no higher than 100. `offset` skips that many leading rows and defaults to 0. Unless a narrative in §3 says otherwise, every collection is returned in ascending order of `created_at`.

List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope; `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before pagination and `has_more` reports whether further rows remain beyond the current window. Collection reads also accept the domain filters described alongside each resource in §3 — for example an account may be filtered by `account_status`, `plan_year`, or `allocation_method`, and a member list by `member_status`, `state_code`, `pathway_selected`, `clinic_id`, or `care_advocate_id`.

### 2.4. The Bundle Ledger Principle and Dual-Variant Netting

The unit of benefit in this system is not a dollar balance but a **bundle entitlement**: a named grant of cycles against a treatment bundle, held by one sponsoring employer account for one plan year. Every entitlement maintains three derived counters that the client never sets and always reads back: `cycles_consumed`, `cycles_committed`, and `cycles_remaining`, plus a parallel pair `pharmacy_consumed_cents` and `pharmacy_remaining_cents` for the specialty-pharmacy pool. These are ledgers in the strict sense — they are recomputed from the rows that reference the entitlement, never incremented blindly, so a reversal or a cancellation restores the allowance it consumed.

What makes the cycle ledger interesting is that the employer chooses, at account-creation time, how much of its future liability the ledger nets. An account configured `allocation_method` `SEQUENTIAL_BUNDLE` nets only cycles that have already been recorded as consumed, so authorizations that have been approved but not yet performed leave `cycles_remaining` untouched. An account configured `CONCURRENT_BUNDLE` nets every committed cycle — consumed cycles, approved-but-unperformed cycles, and authorizations still awaiting a clinical decision — so the same rows yield a strictly smaller `cycles_remaining`. The two policies therefore do not merely differ at the edges: with three authorized-but-unperformed cycles against a three-cycle bundle, concurrent netting reads zero remaining while sequential netting reads three. Every other rule in this document that speaks of "a remaining cycle" means a cycle measured under the sponsoring employer's own policy, and both the creation of an authorization and the decision on one are checked against that same reading.

A suspended employer account is a special case of the ledger principle: while `account_status` is `SUSPENDED`, its entitlements are read as `DRAFT` and compute no netting at all, and their remaining counters read zero. Reactivation restores the reading for any entitlement whose allowance is non-empty, which is possible because `EXHAUSTED` is a derived posture rather than a stored fact.

### 2.5. Common Resource Properties and Derived Fields

Every top-level resource in this API carries the same four system-managed fields. The `id` is a server-minted universally unique identifier with no type prefix and no embedded semantics. The `created_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits, set when the row is first written and never changed. The `updated_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC instant refreshed on every mutation. The `version` is a monotonically increasing integer that advances by one each time the resource changes; it exists to support the concurrency controls in §2.8 and to let a client detect that a dependent decision was computed against a superseded shape. All resources in this document are created with this full set of common fields; none is immutable after creation.

Derived counters are mutations like any other. When a recompute changes at least one field on a resource, the resource's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` advances by exactly one — a refresh that leaves every counter identical advances neither. This distinction matters for the cycle ledger, where a plan's mutation frequently triggers a bundle recompute that legitimately changes nothing; such a recompute must not be observable through the entitlement's `version`. Cascaded updates are not exempt: when one operation moves a parent and a child at the same time, each row updates its own `updated_at` and its own `version` independently, and the operation's response reflects the row named in the request path.

### 2.6. The Logical Clock and Time-Dependent Eligibility

The service does not read wall-clock time. It maintains a single logical clock which, at server startup, reads exactly the instant 2026-09-01T00:00:00Z and holds that value until a caller sets it. An administrative capability accepts a `current_utc` instant and adopts it; from that moment every time-dependent reading in the system — advocate credential validity, contract coverage windows, authorization validity windows, appeal windows, reversal windows, amendment windows, and the scheduled expiry sweeps — is evaluated against the new instant rather than against real time. A second administrative capability advances the clock to the earliest instant at which some pending scheduled transition is due, fires every transition whose deadline has by then been reached, and reports the instant it reached as `advanced_to` together with the number of transitions it fired as `transitions_fired`; if no transition is pending it returns without moving the clock at all.

Three scheduled transitions ride on the clock and nothing else moves them. An authorization that has been submitted and is still awaiting a clinical decision — in `SUBMITTED` or `CLINICAL_REVIEW` — expires with `denial_reason_code` `EXPIRED_NO_DECISION` once the clock reaches the instant it was submitted plus the configured submission-expiry interval. A cycle that has reached `TRANSFER` moves to `OUTCOME_PENDING` once the clock reaches the cycle's `transfer_date` plus the configured pregnancy-test interval. A claim that is still in `RECEIVED` moves to `PENDED` with `pended_reason` `SUBMITTED_LATE_REQUIRES_DOCUMENTATION` once the clock reaches the claim's date of service plus the configured late-submission interval.

Several further windows are measured against the clock but fire on an explicit request rather than on a sweep. The interval that governs how long values may be corrected after the fact is stated in days throughout this document: an approved authorization is valid for a fixed authorization window beginning at the logical clock at approval, a paid claim may be reversed only within the reversal window measured from the instant it was paid, an outcome record may be amended only within the amendment window measured from the instant it was recorded, a denied claim may be appealed only within the appeal window measured from the end of its authorization's validity, and a routed surrender route may be retired only once the storage retention period measured from the route's end has closed. Each of these windows is closed at its boundary: an operation requested at the exact instant a window closes is refused, and the refusal carries the window-specific error named in §3.

### 2.7. Monetary Precision, Percentages, and Rounding

All monetary amounts in this API are integer cents; no monetary field is ever a floating-point value, and none is ever returned as a string. Percentages that drive money — the member cost-share percentage, the specialty differential, the ancillary share slices, the ingredient share of a pharmacy fill, and the manager-fee percentage — are applied to cent amounts and rounded to whole cents; a rounding step that falls exactly halfway between two cents rounds upward, away from zero. Each rule that involves rounding says so explicitly where it is described, and where a rule applies more than one percentage it states the order in which the steps occur, because applying the same steps in a different order can move the result by a cent.

Quality rates and other basis-point readings are separate from money and follow their own instruction: they are integers computed as ten-thousandths and rounded half-up, with no fractional part retained. Counters — cycle counts, transfer counts, report counts, caseload counts — are plain integers and are never rounded.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Three write operations in this API carry a mandatory concurrency token. Each names, in its request body, the `version` the client last observed for the parent resource the operation depends on, and each is refused when that observation is stale.

A decision on a pre-authorization requires `expected_treatment_plan_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the plan whose cycle the authorization covers. If the plan's `version` has advanced since that observation — because another writer selected a different clinic, abandoned the plan, or moved it into planning revision — the decision is refused with `ERR_PLAN_VERSION_STALE` and a 412 status, because the decision was computed against a plan shape that no longer holds.

Paying a claim requires `expected_preauthorization_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the authorization the claim is drawn against. If the authorization's `version` has advanced since that observation — because it has been freshly consumed, released by a reversal, or otherwise touched — the payment is refused with `ERR_PREAUTH_VERSION_STALE` and a 412 status, because the adjudicated allowance no longer reflects the authorization the payment would settle.

Reporting an outcome from the clinic side requires `expected_treatment_cycle_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the cycle being reported. If the cycle's `version` has advanced since that observation — for example because the cycle has been cancelled — the report is refused with `ERR_CYCLE_VERSION_STALE` and a 412 status, because the reported outcome would no longer describe a live cycle.

No other operation in this API accepts a version token, and no operation treats one as optional: where a token is required, a request that omits it is refused before any business rule is evaluated.

### 2.9. Nested Resource Retrieval and Validation Order

Paths in this API nest a resource inside its parent — a contract under a clinic, an authorization under a plan, a cycle under a plan, a surrender route under a member — and the service validates identifiers along the path from left to right. The first identifier that does not resolve to an existing row determines the refusal, and the refusal carries the entity-specific identifier for that segment rather than a generic one: `ERR_EMPLOYER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ADVOCATE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLINIC_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BUNDLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PREAUTH_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND`, or `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, each with a 404 status. A foreign key supplied in a request body that does not resolve is a different matter: it is refused with the generic `ERR_NOT_FOUND` and a 404 status.

Validation runs as an ordered sequence rather than a set of independent checks, and the order is observable because the first failure that applies is the one reported. The sequence is: header presence and role validity first; then authorization of the role for this operation; then resolution of every path identifier left to right, with the entity-specific not-found code for the failing segment; then the segregation-of-duties rule; then the declared-state rule for the requested transition, including any sequence guard that requires a cycle or authorization to be filed at the plan's next position, which is reported as `ERR_SEQUENCE_OUT_OF_ORDER`; then body integrity — every foreign key in the body resolving, every required input present and inside its declared enumeration, and every pathway-specific reference present for its own enumeration member, reported as `ERR_SURROGACY_CARRIER_REQUIRED` for a surrogacy request without a carrier reference and `ERR_DONOR_AGENCY_REQUIRED` for a donor request without an agency reference; and only then the business rules, in the fixed order set out in §2.10. A caller that supplies both a missing required field and a bad foreign key therefore sees the state error before the body error, and a caller that violates two business rules sees only the earlier one. Nothing in the body-integrity step passes silently: a missing required input, a value outside its declared enumeration, or an interval whose end precedes its start is refused outright with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and a 400 status, and a caller cannot reach the business rules of the later steps with a malformed body.

### 2.10. Allowance and Network Governance

Two families of business rule stand between a caller and a write, and both families evaluate in a fixed order across every operation that they govern.

The first family concerns the *terms of coverage*. A covered clinical or pricing operation must find the clinic named by the plan — or, for a contract activation, the clinic whose contract is being activated — contracted: its `clinic_status` is `CONTRACTED`, it holds a contract that covers the logical clock, and that contract carries a negotiated rate for the member's bundle type; a contract may not be activated at all until its clinic has accumulated the required number of quality reports. A failure of any of those conditions is reported as `ERR_CLINIC_NOT_CONTRACTED` with a 422 status. Where the operation also derives an authorization window or reads a billed service instant, that window or instant must fall entirely inside the contract's coverage interval, and a window that would extend past the contract's end is refused with `ERR_PREAUTH_WINDOW_OUTSIDE_COVERAGE` rather than silently truncated. A clinic whose active contract has been frozen for a quality breach is unavailable for new clinical authorization and for new medication orders, refused with `ERR_CLINIC_CONTRACT_FROZEN`. The sponsoring employer account must be `ACTIVE`, and the bundle entitlement must be live, refused with `ERR_BENEFIT_INACTIVE` when either fails. Where the operation draws on the member's own travel through a preservation, donor, or surrogacy pathway, the acting advocate's credential must be unexpired and the advocate must not have reached their caseload ceiling — refused with `ERR_ADVOCATE_CREDENTIAL_LAPSED` — and the requested route must not disagree with an overlapping route already on file, refused with `ERR_SURRENDER_ROUTE_CONFLICT`. A new cycle must clear the plan's configured minimum inter-cycle spacing after the plan's most recent prior transfer, and must not occupy a window already reserved by a routed surrender route, refused with `ERR_CYCLE_SLOT_OVERLAP`.

The second family concerns the *allowance itself*. An action that draws on a bundle fails when the carried allowance cannot bear it; where both the cycle allowance and the specialty-pharmacy pool are empty the specific code `ERR_BUNDLE_EXHAUSTED` applies, and where the pool alone is empty while a specialty drug is being ordered the specific code `ERR_PHARMACY_POOL_EXHAUSTED` applies; the general code for any other shortfall against a cycle allowance, a pathway route allowance, or the pool is `ERR_NO_REMAINING_ALLOWANCE`. A clinical decision fails against the appropriateness floor with `ERR_CLINICAL_CRITERIA_NOT_MET`, and a priced operation fails when no bundle price has been pinned for the member's bundle type with `ERR_BUNDLE_PRICE_UNPINNED` or when the billed charge is not a positive amount. A correction after the fact fails outside its window with `ERR_REVERSAL_WINDOW_CLOSED` or `ERR_AMENDMENT_WINDOW_CLOSED`. Finally, four operations are idempotency-protected — reporting a cycle outcome, amending an outcome record, voiding an outcome record, and deciding an authorization — and a second attempt at an operation already carried out on the same resource is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_OPERATION` and a 409 status.

Ordered against one another, the coverage family precedes the allowance family, and within each family the rule stated earlier in the paragraphs above is evaluated earlier. Where two rules in the same operation would both fail, the earlier rule is the one reported.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Designing the Benefit: Sponsor Accounts and Bundle Grants

A `PlanAdministrator` opens the relationship by registering the sponsoring organization. The registration carries the sponsoring organization's `employer_name`, the `plan_year` the design is priced for, the `funding_type` under which the plan is funded, the `allocation_method` the sponsor has elected, and the `enrollment_limit` capping covered members. The `funding_type` is either `SELF_FUNDED` or `FULLY_INSURED`; the `allocation_method` is either `SEQUENTIAL_BUNDLE` or `CONCURRENT_BUNDLE` and decides, for the life of the account, how concurrent bundle liability is netted. A newly registered account begins with `account_status` `ACTIVE`, `active_member_count` of zero, and `entitlement_count` of zero, and both counters are recomputed whenever the population it sponsors changes — `active_member_count` tracks members whose `member_status` is `INTAKE`, `CARE_ACTIVE`, or `CARE_COMPLETE`, and `entitlement_count` tracks entitlements whose `bundle_status` is `ACTIVE` or `EXHAUSTED`.

Against an account the administrator defines one or more **bundle entitlements**. Each grant names the `bundle_type` it confers — one of `DIAGNOSTIC_IUI`, `IVF_RETRIEVAL_TRANSFER`, `PRESERVATION`, `DONOR_EGG`, `DONOR_SPERM`, `SURROGACY`, or `MEDICATION` — together with `lifetime_max_cycles`, the annual slice `max_cycles_per_year`, the `max_transfers_per_cycle` ceiling that a single cycle's embryo transfers may not exceed, the `pharmacy_pool_cents` reserved for specialty fertility medications, and the coverage interval expressed as an inclusive `effective_from` and an exclusive `effective_to`. Two further inputs shape pricing rather than consumption: `copay_max`, a boolean defaulting to false, which controls whether the grant applies the maximum copay reduction to the negotiated bundle price, and — implicitly through the sponsoring account — the netting policy that governs the counters.

A fresh grant is stored with `bundle_status` `DRAFT` and every counter at zero: `cycles_consumed`, `cycles_committed`, `cycles_remaining`, `pharmacy_consumed_cents`, and `pharmacy_remaining_cents` all begin empty so that a design cannot be consumed before it has been deliberately opened. Activation by the same administrator is what turns a draft into a live grant, and it is refused unless the grant's `effective_from` has already been reached by the logical clock and its sponsoring account is `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_BENEFIT_INACTIVE`. Activation recomputes the entitlement's counters under the account's netting policy and opens the pharmacy pool for draw. An administrator may also reinstate a grant that has been read as `EXHAUSTED`: because exhaustion is a derived posture rather than a stored terminal fact, a grant whose allowance has since been restored by a reversal, a cancellation, or a released order returns to `ACTIVE` on reinstatement, and the reinstatement is refused unless the sponsoring account is `ACTIVE`.

Suspension is the blunt instrument. When an administrator suspends a sponsoring account the account's `account_status` becomes `SUSPENDED`, its `active_member_count` and `entitlement_count` are recomputed, and every entitlement it sponsors is read as `DRAFT` with its `cycles_remaining` and `pharmacy_remaining_cents` reading zero, so the whole population's ability to draw on the benefit stops at once. Any authorization belonging to a member of that account that is still `SUBMITTED` and awaiting a first clinical decision is expired at the same moment, and it is recorded as expired for the reason `ACCOUNT_SUSPENDED` rather than for lapse of time. Reactivation by the same administrator reverses the reading: every entitlement whose allowance is non-empty reads as `ACTIVE` again, and its counters return to the values the account's netting policy implies. Accounts, like every resource here, expose `account_status`, `plan_year`, and `allocation_method` as filters on the collection read.

### 3.2. Enrolling a Member and Picking an Advocate

Enrollment is opened by a `CareAdvocate` or a `PlanAdministrator`, who supplies the `employer_account_id` of the sponsoring account, the sponsor's own `external_ref` for the person — unique within that account — the member's `full_name`, the `state_code` in which they reside, and the `required_track` their treatment calls for. That last field is the first thing the system does anything with: the required treatment track is one of `STANDARD_IVF`, `DIAGNOSTIC_IUI`, `PRESERVATION`, `DONOR_EGG`, `DONOR_SPERM`, or `SURROGACY`, and it is what the advocate selector must satisfy when a guide is later chosen. A new member starts life in `INTAKE`, with `care_advocate_id` empty, `clinic_id` empty, `pathway_selected` reading `NONE`, `completed_cycles` and `live_births_recorded` at zero, and `intake_completed_at` unset.

Advocates themselves are registered by a `PlanAdministrator`. An advocate record carries a `full_name`, the set of US state codes in `states_licensed` in which the advocate may guide members, the treatment bundles in `specializations` the advocate is qualified to handle, a `caseload_limit` capping concurrent members, and a `credentials_expire_on` instant at which the clinical credential lapses. A new advocate begins with `current_caseload` and `assignments_completed` at zero and an `advocate_status` of `ACTIVE`. That status is itself derived and is read continuously: an advocate reads `AT_CAPACITY` once their live caseload has reached their ceiling, `INACTIVE` once their credential has lapsed, and `ACTIVE` otherwise. The live caseload counts members whose `member_status` is `CARE_ACTIVE` or `CARE_COMPLETE`, and the completed-assignment count counts only members who have reached `CARE_COMPLETE`, so an advocate's `assignments_completed` trails their `current_caseload` until members genuinely finish.

Matching a member to a guide is a policy decision, not a lookup, and the policy is chosen by the caller. Assignment is performed by a `PlanAdministrator` or a `NetworkManager`, and the request may carry an `allocation_strategy`; when it does, that strategy governs the selection, and when it does not, the sponsoring account's own `allocation_method` governs. Under either source, the eligible pool is built by three filters applied together: the advocate must be licensed in the member's `state_code`, must be specialized in the member's `required_track`, and must read `ACTIVE` or `AT_CAPACITY` rather than `INACTIVE`. A pool that comes back empty is refused with `ERR_NO_ADVOCATE_CANDIDATE` and a 422 status rather than silently leaving the member unguided. When the pool is non-empty the effective strategy decides how the candidates are ordered against one another before the first is taken; the choice of strategy changes which eligible advocate is selected, never whether a member may be assigned at all. Candidates may also be listed in advance through the advocate collection read, which accepts `state_code`, `specialization`, `advocate_status`, and `member_id` as filters. Assigning a guide writes the chosen advocate into `care_advocate_id`, recomputes the previous advocate's `current_caseload`, and bumps the sponsoring account's `version`.

Intake is closed separately, and only by a `CareAdvocate` or a `PlanAdministrator`. Closing intake requires that the member is still in `INTAKE`, that a guide has actually been assigned — a member cannot complete intake before an advocate is on the record — that the assigned advocate reads `ACTIVE` or `AT_CAPACITY`, and that the logical clock is still before the advocate's `credentials_expire_on`. When those hold, the member's `member_status` becomes `CARE_ACTIVE`, `intake_completed_at` is stamped with the logical clock, and both the advocate's `current_caseload` and the sponsoring account's `active_member_count` are recomputed. A failure of the credential check is reported as `ERR_ADVOCATE_CREDENTIAL_LAPSED`; a failure of the account's standing is reported as `ERR_BENEFIT_INACTIVE`.

The care episode also ends explicitly. A member may be closed only when they are `CARE_ACTIVE` and when nothing is still running underneath them: no plan of theirs may be in `PLAN_DRAFT`, `CLINIC_SELECTED`, `AUTHORIZATION_PENDING`, `IN_PROGRESS`, or `PLANNING_REVISION`, and no pathway request of theirs may be in `DRAFT`, `CLINICAL_REVIEW`, `APPROVED`, or `ACTIVE`. When closure is permitted the member reaches `CARE_COMPLETE`, which is terminal, and the advocate's `current_caseload` and `assignments_completed` and the account's `active_member_count` are all recomputed.

### 3.3. Contracting the Clinic Network on Quality Evidence

A clinic enters the network as a candidate. A `NetworkManager` registers it with its `legal_name`, its ten-digit `npi` — which is globally unique across the service — the `state_code` in which it operates, the `network_tier` it is being proposed for, the `bundles_offered` it is able to deliver, and four quality parameters: `min_live_birth_rate_bps`, the floor below which its posture is demoted; `max_multiple_birth_rate_bps`, the ceiling above which the same happens; `required_quality_report_count`, the number of outcome records it must accumulate before a contract may be activated; and `maturity_report_threshold`, the number of reported cycles it must accumulate before its quality readings are allowed to drive its posture at all. A newly registered clinic reads `clinic_status` `IN_REVIEW`, with its two rate readings, its composite quality score, and its `cycles_reported` all at zero. The `network_tier` is one of `PREFERRED`, `STANDARD`, or `OUT_OF_NETWORK`. The three window lengths of §2.6 — the authorization window opened at approval, the reversal window opened at payment, and the amendment window opened at recording — are configured once for the service rather than per clinic; the fourth, the appeal window, is measured from the close of the authorization's own validity.

Pricing is agreed per clinic, in a **clinic contract**. The contract carries its own inclusive `effective_from` and exclusive `effective_to`, and a `bundle_rates` map from bundle type to cents — for example an entry of 1450000 cents for `IVF_RETRIEVAL_TRANSFER` and 220000 cents for `DIAGNOSTIC_IUI`. A contract is created in `IN_REVIEW` with `frozen_reason` empty. Two rules bind the rates to the benefit design: the map must contain an entry for the member's bundle type before a plan may select the clinic at all, and the contract's own interval, not the entitlement's, is what an authorization window must fit inside.

Activation is the gate that turns a contract from a proposal into a term of coverage, and it is deliberately the point at which quality evidence is weighed. Activation is performed by the same manager and names the clinic whose `version` the manager last observed through `expected_clinic_version`, so a contract cannot be activated against a clinic posture that has moved underneath the request. Approval is refused unless the contract is still `IN_REVIEW`, the clinic's own posture is still `IN_REVIEW`, the logical clock falls inside the contract's own `[effective_from, effective_to)` interval, and the clinic has accumulated at least three outcome reports against its `required_quality_report_count`. (The scaffold's precondition for contract activation is a floor of three reports; a clinic configured with a larger `required_quality_report_count` is held to the larger figure at the moment the clinic's own readiness is assessed.) A clinic that fails the contractedness test — not `CONTRACTED`, no covering contract, or no negotiated rate for the member's bundle type — is refused here too, with `ERR_CLINIC_NOT_CONTRACTED`. On success the contract becomes `ACTIVE` and the clinic's posture becomes `CONTRACTED`, and the clinic's own `version` advances alongside the contract's.

Every clinic posture is derived from evidence, and the evidence is the clinic's outcome records. The clinic-side clinician reports an outcome for a cycle that has already been recorded clinically — the report names the `treatment_cycle_id`, the `live_birth` flag, the `births_count`, the `multiple_birth` flag, and `gestational_weeks`, and it pins the cycle's version through `expected_treatment_cycle_version`, so a report against a cycle that has moved since it was read is refused with `ERR_CYCLE_VERSION_STALE`. A report is accepted only for a cycle whose `cycle_status` is already `OUTCOME_RECORDED`, and only when no live record already exists for that cycle; a second report for the same cycle is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_OPERATION`. The accepted report is created in `outcome_status` `RECORDED`, and it drives `cycles_reported` and the derived quality readings on the clinic.

The quality readings themselves are computed from the clinic's live workset — the records whose `outcome_status` is `RECORDED` or `AMENDED`, each counted once regardless of which of those two it is. Voided records leave both the numerator and the denominator, which is why voiding a non-live-birth record raises the live-birth rate while voiding a live-birth record lowers it. `cycles_reported` is the size of that workset. The `live_birth_rate_bps` reading is ten thousand times the number of live-birth records divided by the workset size, rounded half-up to a whole number of basis points, and it reads zero when the workset is empty rather than failing. The `multiple_birth_rate_bps` reading is ten thousand times the number of multiple-birth records divided by the number of live-birth records, again rounded half-up, and it too reads zero when its denominator is zero.

The composite score is a weighted reading over the same workset, published as `composite_quality_bps` and rounded half-up to whole basis points: 60 times `live_birth_rate_bps` capped at ten thousand, minus 45 times `multiple_birth_rate_bps`, plus 20 times `cycles_reported` capped at 5. A clinic's eligibility as a contracted participant is then determined by an ordered list of gates rather than by the composite alone, and the first gate that is satisfied decides. The first gate is satisfied when the workset has reached the clinic's `maturity_report_threshold` and the live-birth rate is at or above the clinic's `min_live_birth_rate_bps` and the multiple-birth rate is at or below the clinic's `max_multiple_birth_rate_bps`. If that gate fails, the second gate is satisfied when the workset is empty, which treats a clinic that has reported nothing at all as contracted by default. If that fails too, the third gate is satisfied when the workset has reached exactly one report fewer than the maturity threshold. A clinic that satisfies none of the three is never eligible. Because the gates are walked in order and not evaluated as an unordered set, there is no possibility of two gates tying.

Posture moves on the strength of those readings. A clinic in `CONTRACTED` posture whose workset has reached the maturity threshold and whose live-birth rate has fallen below the floor or whose multiple-birth rate has risen above the ceiling is moved to `PROBATION`, and every contract the clinic holds that was `ACTIVE` is frozen with `frozen_reason` `QUALITY_BREACH`. The same demotion is reachable directly through the clinic-quality recompute capability, which re-runs the calculation against the current workset without writing any new outcome data. Probation is not terminal: an amended record that brings the live-birth rate back to or above the floor, and the multiple-birth rate back to or below the ceiling, returns the clinic to `CONTRACTED` and unfreezes every contract that was frozen for `QUALITY_BREACH`, clearing `frozen_reason`. The recompute capability carries one further demotion of its own — a clinic whose current reading is eligible but whose latest `ACTIVE` contract window has already closed is moved to `SUSPENDED`, so that a stale agreement stops gating clinic selection.

### 3.4. Opening a Treatment Plan and Committing It to a Clinic

A plan is the bridge between a member's clinical intention and a bundle's allowance. A `CareAdvocate` opens one against a member and an entitlement by supplying the `member_id`, the `bundle_entitlement_id` the plan will draw on, the `pathway_type` the plan follows — one of `STANDARD_IVF`, `DIAGNOSTIC_IUI`, `PRESERVATION`, `DONOR_EGG`, `DONOR_SPERM`, or `SURROGACY` — the `planned_cycles` the plan intends to run, and optionally `min_inter_cycle_days`, the minimum number of whole days that must separate this plan's consecutive cycles. The advocate who opens the plan is snapshotted into the plan's own `care_advocate_id` at creation, so a later reassignment of the member's advocate does not retroactively change who guided this plan. The plan starts in `PLAN_DRAFT` with `clinic_id` empty, `selected_bundle_price_cents` at zero, `cycles_completed` at zero, and `plan_blocked_reason` empty.

Committing the plan to a clinic is the next step, and it is the moment pricing is fixed for the plan. The advocate supplies the target `clinic_id` and an `expected_member_version` recording the member's `version` as last observed; the member's version is required here because a plan change and a change to the member's pathway or surrender posture must not interleave silently, and a select-clinic request that arrives against a member who has since moved is refused as a stale-version conflict. The clinic named must read `CONTRACTED`, must hold a contract whose status is `ACTIVE` and whose interval covers the logical clock, and that contract must carry a negotiated rate for the member's bundle type — a clinic that fails any of those three is refused with `ERR_CLINIC_NOT_CONTRACTED`. On success the plan records the clinic, moves to `CLINIC_SELECTED`, snapshots the negotiated rate into `selected_bundle_price_cents` so that the plan's price is pinned at the rate that was in force when the clinic was chosen, and mirrors the same clinic onto the member's own `clinic_id`, advancing the member's `version`.

A plan may be blocked after it has been committed, and it is blocked by the network rather than by the advocate. Whenever a plan's selected clinic stops being `CONTRACTED`, the plan is moved into `PLANNING_REVISION` with `plan_blocked_reason` reading `CLINIC_NOT_CONTRACTED`. This cascade reaches plans in `CLINIC_SELECTED`, in `AUTHORIZATION_PENDING`, and in `IN_PROGRESS`, and on each of them it also denies every authorization that is still awaiting a decision — that is, every authorization in `SUBMITTED` or `CLINICAL_REVIEW`, which is recorded as denied for the reason `CLINIC_NOT_CONTRACTED`. A block is recoverable. Once the clinic returns to `CONTRACTED`, the advocate may resubmit the plan, moving it from `PLANNING_REVISION` back to `AUTHORIZATION_PENDING`, clearing `plan_blocked_reason`, and returning each authorization that had been denied for `CLINIC_NOT_CONTRACTED` to `CLINICAL_REVIEW` with its denial reason cleared. Resubmission carries `expected_treatment_plan_version` and requires that the plan's clinic is genuinely contracted again and that the resubmission does not change the plan's `clinic_id` — a resubmission that quietly points the plan at a different clinic is refused with `ERR_CLINIC_NOT_CONTRACTED`.

A plan that has not begun treatment may also be walked away from. Abandonment is available to the advocate while the plan is in `PLAN_DRAFT`, `CLINIC_SELECTED`, or `PLANNING_REVISION`, and it is refused once the plan is `IN_PROGRESS`. Abandoning expires every authorization of the plan that is in `SUBMITTED`, `CLINICAL_REVIEW`, or `APPROVED`, cancels every cycle of the plan that has not yet recorded an outcome, ensures each of those cancelled cycles carries the `UNCONSUMED` marker so it releases rather than consumes its bundle slot, recomputes the bundle counters, and leaves the plan terminal in `ABANDONED`. Both `COMPLETED` and `ABANDONED` are terminal for a plan.

Suspending or terminating the clinic itself compounds the same cascade. Suspending a clinic moves it to `SUSPENDED` from either `CONTRACTED` or `PROBATION`, terminates every contract it holds that is `ACTIVE` or `FROZEN`, blocks every plan committed to it that is in `CLINIC_SELECTED` or `AUTHORIZATION_PENDING` with `plan_blocked_reason` `CLINIC_NOT_CONTRACTED`, and denies the pending authorizations on those plans for the same reason. Terminating the clinic is available only from `SUSPENDED`, moves the clinic to `TERMINATED`, and is terminal: no contract and no plan may reference the clinic afterwards. A contract may also be terminated directly, which terminates the clinic along with it and blocks the dependent plans in exactly the same way. Read-side, clinics may be filtered by `state_code`, `network_tier`, `clinic_status`, `bundle_type`, and `min_composite_quality_bps`, and contracts by `clinic_id` and `contracting_status`.

### 3.5. Pre-Authorization: Clinical Appropriateness and the Sequencing Guard

Each cycle a plan intends to run needs its own authorization before the clinic may begin it. The advocate files one against the plan, supplying the `cycle_sequence_index` — the one-based position of the authorized cycle in the plan's sequence — a `criteria_inputs` object carrying the clinical inputs on which appropriateness will be scored, and a `member_cost_share_pct` between 0 and 100 recording the member's responsibility percentage for this bundle. A new authorization is stored in `review_status` `SUBMITTED` and, independently, in `consumption_status` `UNCONSUMED`, with `approved_cycles` and `consumed_cycles` at zero and `bundle_price_snapshot_cents` at zero until a decision pins it. Filing one moves the plan to `AUTHORIZATION_PENDING` and recomputes the bundle's committed and remaining counters.

Two gates stand in front of the filing. The first is sequencing: the new authorization's `cycle_sequence_index` must be strictly greater than the highest index already on the plan, so a plan's authorizations accumulate in order and a request filed at a position that is not the plan's next is refused with `ERR_SEQUENCE_OUT_OF_ORDER`. The second is allowance: the bundle the plan draws on must have a remaining cycle under the sponsoring account's own netting policy, and a filing that would exceed the carried allowance is refused with `ERR_NO_REMAINING_ALLOWANCE`.

The `criteria_inputs` keys are `age_years`, `amh_ng_ml`, `prior_cycles`, `prior_live_birth`, and `fsh_iu_l`. They are scored into `clinical_criteria_score`, an integer between 0 and 100 that starts at 100 and is then adjusted by five steps, after which the running total is clamped into range by lifting anything below 0 up to 0 and capping anything above 100 at 100. The steps, in the order they are applied, are: subtract an age penalty of 0 for an age under 35, 20 for an age from 35 through 37, 40 for an age from 38 through 40, and 60 for an age of 41 or over; subtract 20 when `amh_ng_ml` is below 0.5, 10 when it is below 1.0 but at least 0.5, and nothing otherwise; add 15 when the member has a prior live birth; subtract 25 when `prior_cycles` is 3 or more, 15 when it is exactly 2, and nothing otherwise; and subtract 10 when `fsh_iu_l` is above 12. The penalties are summed before the single clamp is applied, not clamped step by step, which matters exactly at the boundary: inputs that total 74 approve, and an implementation that clamps after every subtraction would land on 70 and also approve, but one step further down the inputs diverge at 70 versus 69. Missing keys are read as neutral values rather than rejected: an absent `age_years` reads 30, an absent `amh_ng_ml` reads 2.0, an absent `prior_cycles` reads 0, an absent `prior_live_birth` reads false, and an absent `fsh_iu_l` reads 6. Neutral inputs therefore score 100 and approve. In practical terms the score is a clinical-appropriateness gate whose approval floor is 70, inclusive.

A `ClinicalReviewer` conducts the review in two steps. Beginning clinical review moves the authorization from `SUBMITTED` into `CLINICAL_REVIEW` and computes the score, and it is refused where the authorization is not `SUBMITTED` or where the reviewer's own `X-User-ID` already matches the `decision_actor` recorded on the resource, which would violate the segregation-of-duties rule; that refusal carries `ERR_SOD_CONFLICT`. Rendering the decision is a separate act: the reviewer supplies a `decision` of approve or deny, together with `expected_treatment_plan_version` carrying the plan's `version` as the reviewer last observed it, and may supply a `denial_reason_code` when the decision is a denial — a decision offered against a plan that has moved since is refused with `ERR_PLAN_VERSION_STALE` and a 412 status. Approval requires that the authorization is in `CLINICAL_REVIEW`, that the score has reached the floor of 70, that the plan is currently `AUTHORIZATION_PENDING`, that the bundle still has at least one remaining cycle under the account's netting policy, and that no cycle of the plan already carries a `cycle_index` beyond the authorization's own position — a cycle has been created that belongs to a later position than the one being authorized, which is refused with `ERR_SEQUENCE_OUT_OF_ORDER`. A score below the floor produces a denial instead, recorded for the reason `CLINICAL_CRITERIA_NOT_MET`, and in either direction the reviewer's `X-User-ID` is written to `decision_actor`. Approval additionally pins `bundle_price_snapshot_cents` from the plan's `selected_bundle_price_cents`, sets `approved_cycles` to 1, computes `effective_from` as the logical clock and `effective_to` as that instant plus the configured authorization window, advances the plan to `IN_PROGRESS`, and recomputes the bundle counters. A second decision on an authorization that is no longer awaiting a decision — whether it reached its terminal reading by an explicit decision, an expiry, a cascade denial, or an abandonment — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` rather than the idempotency code, and an approval whose derived window would run past the end of the clinic contract's coverage is refused with `ERR_PREAUTH_WINDOW_OUTSIDE_COVERAGE` rather than having its window truncated.

A denial is not necessarily final. The advocate may revise the clinical inputs of a denied authorization by supplying a fresh `criteria_inputs` object, which returns it to `CLINICAL_REVIEW`, recomputes the score from the new inputs, and clears the denial reason, leaving `decision_actor` untouched — so the reviewer who denied it cannot be the one who now approves it. Revision is available only where the denial arose from the clinical criteria or from a bundle-exhausted cascade, and the recomputed counters make a retry against an allowance the requester has since earned admissible.

Three further events close an authorization out. An authorization awaiting a decision — in `SUBMITTED` or `CLINICAL_REVIEW` — expires once the logical clock reaches the instant it was submitted plus the configured submission-expiry interval, and it is recorded as expired with `denial_reason_code` `EXPIRED_NO_DECISION`; a plan stays where it is when that happens. An authorization in `APPROVED` state whose plan is abandoned expires as part of that abandonment. And an authorization whose plan's clinic leaves the network is denied by the cascade described in §3.4 for the reason `CLINIC_NOT_CONTRACTED`. Reading the authorization's own state, `review_status` moves `SUBMITTED → CLINICAL_REVIEW → APPROVED` or `SUBMITTED → CLINICAL_REVIEW → DENIED`, with the loop back `DENIED → CLINICAL_REVIEW`, and the terminal reading `EXPIRED`. The consumption dimension is independent of all of it and is described in §3.6.

### 3.6. Running a Cycle: Stimulation Through Outcome

A cycle belongs to a plan and is created under an approved authorization. A `ClinicCoordinator` opens one against the plan, supplying the `preauthorization_id` that admits it, the planned `retrieval_date` and `transfer_date`, and the `embryos_transferred` the cycle intends to transfer. The new cycle starts in `cycle_status` `SCHEDULED` with its `consumption_marker` `UNCONSUMED`, and its `cycle_index` is recomputed at creation as the one-based rank of the plan's cycles by `created_at` ascending. Two ordering rules govern the rank and are worth stating precisely, because a naive implementation diverges. Ties on `created_at` are broken first by the `X-User-ID` of the creating actor ascending and then by the cycle's own `retrieval_date`; the row's `id` is never used as a sort key. And the index is a dense ordinal over all cycles of the plan including cancelled ones, so a plan whose first cycle is cancelled and whose second cycle records an outcome reads `cycle_index` 2 on the recorded cycle while its `cycles_completed` reads 1.

Creation also has to clear the plan's spacing rule and the bundle's allowance. The cycle's requested service instant — the `retrieval_date` it is created under — must fall inside the authorization's `[effective_from, effective_to)` window, and the logical clock is deliberately not part of that coverage test at creation, so a cycle is frequently created for a date still ahead of the clock. The logical clock must be at least `min_inter_cycle_days` whole days after the plan's most recent prior transfer instant, and the cycle must not fall inside a retention window already reserved by a routed surrender route belonging to the same member; a violation is refused with `ERR_CYCLE_SLOT_OVERLAP` and a 409 status. Where the authorization's validity window does not cover the requested service instant, or where the clinic's contract has been frozen by a quality breach, or where the named authorization is not approved, the creation is refused under the coverage rules of §2.10.

The clinical pipeline then advances one explicit step at a time, each step performed by a `ClinicCoordinator` and each refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` when the cycle is not in the state that step departs from. Starting stimulation moves `SCHEDULED → STIMULATION` and requires that the cycle's authorization is `APPROVED`, that the logical clock lies inside the authorization's `[effective_from, effective_to)` window, and that no other cycle of the same plan is simultaneously in `STIMULATION`. Starting stimulation consumes nothing: the cycle was already counted against the bundle as committed when its authorization was approved. Recording retrieval moves `STIMULATION → RETRIEVAL` and requires that every other cycle of the plan with a smaller `cycle_index` has already reached either `OUTCOME_RECORDED` or `CANCELLED`, so cycles cannot overtake one another; a violation is refused with `ERR_SEQUENCE_OUT_OF_ORDER`. Recording fertilization moves `RETRIEVAL → FERTILIZATION` with no further precondition, and recording the transfer moves `FERTILIZATION → TRANSFER` provided the recorded `embryos_transferred` is at least 1 and does not exceed the bundle's `max_transfers_per_cycle`.

Two states beyond those are driven by the clock rather than by the clinic. Once the logical clock reaches the cycle's `transfer_date` plus the configured pregnancy-test interval, the cycle moves `TRANSFER → OUTCOME_PENDING` without anyone asking. Recording the outcome then moves `OUTCOME_PENDING → OUTCOME_RECORDED`, supplied by the coordinator with the `live_birth` flag, the `births_count`, the `multiple_birth` flag, and `gestational_weeks`. That single act is the point at which the cycle consumes its bundle slot: the cycle's `consumption_marker` becomes `CONSUMED`, the authorization's `consumed_cycles` is incremented and its consumption posture recomputed — moving from `UNCONSUMED` to `PARTIALLY_CONSUMED` and, on the compensation path that finds two cycles consumed against a single-cycle approval, on to `EXHAUSTED` — the bundle's `cycles_consumed` and `cycles_remaining` are recomputed, the member's `completed_cycles` is recomputed, and the plan's `cycles_completed` is recomputed with the plan moving to `COMPLETED` once `cycles_completed` has reached `planned_cycles`. A bundle whose cycle allowance and pharmacy pool both read zero at that point is read as `EXHAUSTED`. Notably, recording a clinical outcome does not create the clinic's quality record; only the clinic-side outcome report described in §3.3 does that.

A cycle may be cancelled instead, from any of `SCHEDULED`, `STIMULATION`, `RETRIEVAL`, `FERTILIZATION`, or `TRANSFER`. Cancellation moves the cycle to `CANCELLED`, which is terminal, leaves the `consumption_marker` `UNCONSUMED`, and recomputes the bundle's committed and remaining counters so that the slot the cycle had claimed is released back to the allowance. A cycle in `OUTCOME_PENDING` or already `OUTCOME_RECORDED` cannot be cancelled; the route back from a recorded outcome runs through a claim reversal, described in §3.9. Reading cycles back, the collection accepts `treatment_plan_id`, `preauthorization_id`, `member_id`, and `cycle_status` as filters, and authorizations accept `treatment_plan_id`, `review_status`, and `consumption_status`.

### 3.7. The Specialty Pharmacy Pathway and Its Pool

Fertility medications do not ride on the cycle allowance; they draw on the entitlement's own specialty-pharmacy pool, and the order that dispenses them is a resource in its own right. An order is placed by a `SpecialtyPharmacist` or a `CareAdvocate` against the member and an approved authorization, carrying the `medication_code`, the `specialty_drug` flag recording whether the drug must ship from the specialty pharmacy network, the `quantity` in the drug's own unit of measure, the `days_supply` the fill covers, the `acquisition_cost_cents` the specialty pharmacy paid, and the `dispensing_pharmacy` identifier. A new order reads `pharmacy_status` `PRESCRIBED`, with `plan_paid_cents`, `member_cost_cents`, and `dispensed_on` all empty.

Dispensing is performed by a `SpecialtyPharmacist` and is the point at which the pool is drawn. It is available only from `PRESCRIBED`, requires that the order's authorization is `APPROVED`, and requires that the bundle's pharmacy pool holds at least one cent. A pool that has reached exactly zero admits nothing and the dispensing is refused with `ERR_PHARMACY_POOL_EXHAUSTED`; a stopping condition that is genuinely a stop, where a pool holding a single cent still admits the next fill. Dispensing also pins the entitlement's version through `expected_bundle_entitlement_version`, so two fills that would draw against the same pool reading cannot both proceed against a stale one.

Dispensing is where two calculations happen. The first splits the amount the pool will bear between the drug and the service of dispensing it: the ingredient portion is 82 percent of the claimable amount rounded half-up to whole cents, and the dispensing fee is whatever remains, so the two always sum back to the claimable amount exactly and no cent is ever lost to drift. The second performs the allocation itself. The claimable amount is the order's `acquisition_cost_cents` when the pool can cover it in full and the pool's remaining balance otherwise — never more than the pool holds, so a shortfall lands on the member rather than driving the pool negative. The order's `plan_paid_cents` is set to that claimable amount, its `member_cost_cents` to whatever part of the acquisition cost the pool did not cover, and the entitlement's `pharmacy_consumed_cents` is increased by the claimable amount with `pharmacy_remaining_cents` recomputed as the pool less consumption, floored at zero. A pool that covers the fill entirely therefore leaves the member with nothing to pay, while a fill that outruns the pool leaves the pool at exactly zero and passes the excess to the member; a subsequent fill against that pool then reports nothing paid by the plan and the whole acquisition cost on the member. Dispensing also stamps `dispensed_on` with the logical clock and recomputes the entitlement.

Shipping and delivery follow. A `SpecialtyPharmacist` moves the order `DISPENSING → DISPENSED`, then marks it delivered from either `DISPENSED` or `SHIPPED`, which moves it to `DELIVERED` and raises a `MEDICATION` claim in `RECEIVED` status whose `billed_cents` equals the order's `member_cost_cents`, so that the member's own share enters the claim pipeline. `DELIVERED` and `CANCELLED` are the terminal states of an order. Cancellation is available while the order is `PRESCRIBED`, where it simply stops, and while it is `DISPENSING`, where it also gives back what it took: the entitlement's `pharmacy_consumed_cents` is recomputed downward by the cancelled order's `plan_paid_cents`, `pharmacy_remaining_cents` is recomputed, and the order's `plan_paid_cents` and `member_cost_cents` are reset to zero. Orders may be listed with `member_id`, `care_advocate_id`, `preauthorization_id`, and `pharmacy_status` as filters.

### 3.8. Life-Path Routes: Preservation, Donation, and Surrogacy

Not every member is pursuing a standard in-vitro course. Preservation of one's own gametes or embryos, the use of donated eggs or donated sperm, and surrogacy are all covered under this benefit, but each runs on its own administrative path with its own consent and its own coverage arithmetic, and none of them consumes the cycle allowance the way a treatment cycle does.

A **pathway request** is filed by a `CareAdvocate` against the member. It names the `pathway_type` — one of `PRESERVATION`, `DONOR_EGG`, `DONOR_SPERM`, or `SURROGACY` — together with `consent_signed`, the `consent_signed_on` instant at which the pathway-specific consent packet was executed, and a `coverage_pct` between 0 and 100 recording the plan's coverage for this pathway. Two pathway-specific references ride in the same body and are required only for their own enumeration member: `gestational_carrier_ref` for a surrogacy request, and `donor_agency_ref` for a donor-egg or donor-sperm request. Supplying a carrier reference on a donor request, or an agency reference on a surrogacy request, has no effect; omitting the reference its own pathway demands is refused, with `ERR_SURROGACY_CARRIER_REQUIRED` for surrogacy and `ERR_DONOR_AGENCY_REQUIRED` for the donor pathways. A new request starts in `pathway_status` `DRAFT` with `pathway_cost_cents` at zero, and the filing advocate is recorded as its `care_advocate_id`.

Clinical review of a pathway request begins only when the consent record is complete: the request must be in `DRAFT`, `consent_signed` must be true, and `consent_signed_on` must be set; a request whose consent is incomplete cannot be moved into review at all. A `ClinicalReviewer` moves it from `DRAFT` into `CLINICAL_REVIEW`, and from `DENIED` back into `CLINICAL_REVIEW` if it is resubmitted. The decision is rendered in review and it turns on the sponsoring account rather than on a score: the reviewer supplies a `decision` of approve or deny, and approval requires that the member's sponsoring account is `ACTIVE` and that the pathway is not barred by a surrogacy rule conflict, while a suspended account produces a denial instead of an approval. Approval computes the request's `pathway_cost_cents` from the pathway rule-set round over the member's `coverage_pct` and the pathway's own baseline, and it recomputes the member's `pathway_selected`. The reviewer's `X-User-ID` is recorded as `decision_actor` and may not equal its prior value, so the reviewer who previously touched the request cannot decide it.

An approved pathway is activated separately by the advocate, which moves it from `APPROVED` to `ACTIVE` and again requires the account to be `ACTIVE`; completion moves it on from `ACTIVE` to `COMPLETED`, which is terminal. Withdrawal is available from any of `DRAFT`, `CLINICAL_REVIEW`, `APPROVED`, or `ACTIVE` and is terminal in `WITHDRAWN`. Every one of approval, activation, completion, and withdrawal recomputes the member's `pathway_selected`, which reads as the `pathway_type` of the earliest-created pathway request that is currently `APPROVED` or `ACTIVE`, and reads `NONE` when no such request exists. The derived field therefore moves both forward and backward as requests are approved, completed, or withdrawn.

A **surrender route** is the mechanism by which a member's gametes, embryos, or the arrangement itself leave the active treatment pipeline and pass into the custody of someone else — the member themselves, a gestational carrier, or a frozen-asset custodian. It is filed by a `CareAdvocate` against the member with the `pathway_type` it belongs to, a `carrier_kind` of `MEMBER`, `GESTATIONAL_CARRIER`, or `CUSTODIAN`, an inclusive `route_from` and an exclusive `route_to` marking the occupancy window, and a `raw_cost_cents` recording the route's unreserved cost; the request also pins the member's version through `expected_member_version`, because entering custody changes the member's own posture. The route's `surrender_cost_cents` is derived by the same pathway rule-set round that prices pathway coverage, over the `raw_cost_cents` and the `carrier_kind`. Filing is refused when the acting advocate's credential has lapsed or the advocate has reached their caseload ceiling, reported as `ERR_ADVOCATE_CREDENTIAL_LAPSED`, and it is refused when the requested route disagrees with an overlapping route already on file for the same member, reported as `ERR_SURRENDER_ROUTE_CONFLICT` with a 409 status. A new route starts in `route_status` `SUBMITTED`.

The route's lifecycle is mostly driven rather than requested. Once the logical clock reaches the route's `route_from` minus the configured pre-route lead time, the route moves from `SUBMITTED` to `ROUTED`, and the member's entry into routed-pathway custody moves their `completed_cycles` and `live_births_recorded` into a read-only routed-custody reading; the member's `version` also advances at that moment, so any plan change still carrying a stale expected member version is refused. A route that has not yet routed may be withdrawn by the advocate, naming the target route through `route_id`, which moves it from `SUBMITTED` to `WITHDRAWN` and advances the member's `version`; and a routed route is retired by the advocate, also naming `route_id`, once the logical clock has reached the route's `route_to` plus the configured storage-retention interval, which moves it from `ROUTED` to `RETIRED` and restores the member's lifecycle to the read-write plan set. Both `RETIRED` and `WITHDRAWN` are terminal for a route. Routes belonging to one member may be listed beneath that member, and the full collection accepts `member_id`, `pathway_type`, `carrier_kind`, and `route_status` as filters. A survival note: a route that is currently `ROUTED` reserves the retention window it occupies, and a new treatment cycle that would fall inside that window is refused as a slot overlap under the rule in §3.6.

### 3.9. Claim Adjudication, Member Share, and the Reversal Route

A claim is how a delivered service turns into money. It is submitted by a `ClinicCoordinator`, a `ClaimsProcessor`, or a `SpecialtyPharmacist`, naming the `member_id` whose benefit pays, the `preauthorization_id` it is drawn against, the `treatment_cycle_id` the billed service belongs to where one applies, the `service_category` being billed, the `billed_cents` the provider charged, and the `service_date_iso` instant the service was rendered. The `service_category` is one of `CLINIC_BUNDLE`, `MEDICATION`, `LAB`, `STORAGE`, `DONOR_MATERIAL`, or `SURROGACY_FEE`. A new claim starts in `claim_status` `RECEIVED` with `allowed_cents` at zero, `adjudication_basis` and `pended_reason` empty, and its `member_cost_share_cents` set to the provisional reading described below. Its `member_accumulator_before_cents` is computed at the same time and `member_accumulator_after_cents` is left equal to that before value until adjudication moves it.

The allowance a claim may draw — `allowed_cents` — depends entirely on which bundle component is being billed, and it is never the billed charge itself when the billed charge exceeds what the bundle has agreed to bear. For a `MEDICATION` claim the allowance is simply the amount the pharmacy pool already settled on the corresponding order, and the adjudication basis reads `BUNDLE_COMPONENT_RULE`. For a `CLINIC_BUNDLE` claim the ceiling is the plan's pinned bundle price: the allowance is the billed charge when the bill is at or below that ceiling, and the ceiling itself when the bill exceeds it, with the adjudication basis reading `CONTRACT_RATE` in the first case and `BUNDLE_COMPONENT_RULE` in the second. The four ancillary categories are each capped at their own slice of the same ceiling, and those slices are computed independently rather than divided out of one another: a laboratory claim is capped at 12 percent of the ceiling, a storage claim at 4 percent, a donor-material claim at 18 percent, and a surrogacy-fee claim at 25 percent, each rounded half-up to whole cents, each compared against the billed charge with the smaller of the two becoming the allowance, and each carrying the `BUNDLE_COMPONENT_RULE` basis. Because the slices are not additive, four ancillary claims may each reach their own slice against the same ceiling. A claim against a plan whose bundle price has not been pinned, or a claim whose billed charge is not a positive amount, is refused with `ERR_BUNDLE_PRICE_UNPINNED`. A claim whose `service_date_iso` falls outside its authorization's `[effective_from, effective_to)` window is refused under the coverage rules of §2.10, and a claim naming an authorization that is not `APPROVED` likewise fails.

The member's own share is computed in five ordered steps, and the order is part of the specification because a different order moves the result. The starting point is the authorization's `member_cost_share_pct`, applied to the claim's `allowed_cents` — not to the billed charge, which would overstate the member's responsibility when the bill exceeded the ceiling — and rounded half-up to whole cents. That figure is then capped by what is left of the member's out-of-pocket maximum: the member may be charged the smaller of the percentage share and the amount remaining below 250000 cents after the running total they had already accumulated, which is floored at zero so that a member already past the maximum is charged nothing further. Third, for a `MEDICATION` or `DONOR_MATERIAL` claim only, a specialty differential of 12 percent is added on top of that capped figure, rounded half-up; for every other service category the differential is zero. Those three steps produce the share, and the last two steps depend on which reading is being taken. On the **provisional** reading — the one exposed when a claim is first created, before adjudication — the share is returned exactly as computed, with the specialty differential still inside it and no cap applied. On the **final** reading — the one taken at adjudication and at appeal decision — the cost-difference cap of 15000 cents is netted out of the share, floored at zero, which means a member share smaller than the cap collapses to zero and one larger than the cap is reduced by exactly 15000 cents. The two readings diverge substantially rather than marginally: a 20000-cent allowed medication claim at a 20 percent share reads 4480 cents provisionally and 0 cents finally, a spread wider than the cap itself.

Adjudication by a `ClaimsProcessor` requires the claim to be `RECEIVED` or, when a pended claim is being re-adjudicated, to carry the `service_date_iso` that resolves whatever the pending was about. It computes `allowed_cents` and `adjudication_basis` as above, recomputes `member_cost_share_cents` with the final variant, sets `plan_paid_cents` to the allowance less the member share — never negative — and advances `member_accumulator_after_cents` to `member_accumulator_before_cents` plus the member share. The `member_accumulator_before_cents` reading itself is the sum of `member_cost_share_cents` across the member's claims that are `ADJUDICATED` or `PAID`, taken in `paid_at` ascending order with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break; claims in `RECEIVED`, `PENDED`, `DENIED`, `APPEALED`, or `REVERSED` contribute nothing to it, so a claim does not raise the member's running total merely by being submitted.

A claim that sits too long before adjudication is pended by the clock: once the logical clock reaches the claim's service instant plus the configured late-submission interval, a claim still in `RECEIVED` moves to `PENDED` with `pended_reason` `SUBMITTED_LATE_REQUIRES_DOCUMENTATION`. A claim may also be denied outright by a `ClaimsProcessor` from either `RECEIVED` or `PENDED`, provided the logical clock has already reached the end of its authorization's validity — an authorization still open will not support a denial. Denial moves the claim to `DENIED` and recomputes both `plan_paid_cents` and `member_cost_share_cents` to zero, so a denied claim costs the member nothing, and it recomputes `monthly_claim_liability_cents` for the member. A denied claim may be appealed by the member's `CareAdvocate` within the configured appeal window measured from the end of the authorization's validity, moving it to `APPEALED`, and the appeal is decided by a `ClaimsReviewer` under the segregation-of-duties rule — the reviewer's `X-User-ID` must differ from the `decision_actor` recorded on the claim's last adjudication, and a match is refused with `ERR_SOD_CONFLICT`. A successful appeal re-adjudicates the claim, recomputing the allowance and the member share with the same rules and moving it back to `ADJUDICATED`.

Paying an adjudicated claim is the point at which the authorization's version is pinned. The `ClaimsProcessor` supplies `expected_preauthorization_version` carrying the authorization's `version` as last observed; a payment offered against an authorization that has since been consumed, released, or otherwise touched is refused with `ERR_PREAUTH_VERSION_STALE` and a 412 status. Payment moves the claim to `PAID`, recomputes `monthly_claim_liability_cents`, and — for a `MEDICATION` claim only — recomputes the entitlement's pharmacy counters, since payment is what confirms the pool draw the dispensing already made.

The last act available on a claim is reversal, and it is the compensation path that unwinds a bundle consumption. A `ClaimsProcessor` may reverse a `PAID` claim while the logical clock is still inside the configured reversal window measured from the claim's `paid_at` instant; outside that window the reversal is refused with `ERR_REVERSAL_WINDOW_CLOSED`. Reversal moves the claim to `REVERSED`, resets `member_accumulator_after_cents` back to `member_accumulator_before_cents`, returns the cycle the claim belonged to from the consumed reading to `UNCONSUMED` — the marker is not re-applied later unless a fresh clinic outcome report re-records the cycle — decrements the authorization's `consumed_cycles` and recomputes its consumption posture, recomputes the bundle's cycle counters and its pharmacy counters, and zeros both `plan_paid_cents` and `member_cost_share_cents` on the reversed claim itself. Because the cycle's consumption marker is released and the bundle recomputes from its rows, a bundle that had been read as exhausted can legitimately become available again, which is precisely why exhaustion is a derived posture rather than a terminal fact. `PAID`, `DENIED`, and `REVERSED` are the terminal states of a claim; claims may be read back with `member_id`, `preauthorization_id`, `treatment_cycle_id`, `service_category`, and `claim_status` as filters.

A warranty on the two ledger readings deserves its own paragraph, because almost every other rule in this document assumes it. The remaining-cycle count is recomputed on every mutation of a cycle or an authorization that belongs to the bundle, on every activation, and on any reinstatement; it is never adjusted incrementally from the client's side. The consumed count is the number of the bundle's cycles whose consumption marker reads `CONSUMED`. The committed count is the number of the bundle's authorizations that are either approved and still within their validity or still awaiting a decision, and it never counts an authorization that was denied by a bundle-exhausted cascade without any reviewer touching its score. The remaining count is therefore the bundle's lifetime allowance, reduced by the cycles already consumed in the current plan year, then reduced again by whatever the sponsoring employer's netting policy nets out, and floored at zero so that it can never read negative; where the annual slice is smaller than the lifetime allowance, the annual slice is the binding figure. Cancelled cycles and expired authorizations never reduce the reading at all.

### 3.10. Network Stewardship and the Consequences of Exhaustion

Network management is not a separate administrative chore bolted onto clinical operations; it is the same loop, driven by the outcomes the clinics themselves report. A `NetworkManager` curates the network by reading the quality postures described in §3.3, by amending the evidence on which those postures rest, and by suspending or terminating clinics that no longer meet their terms. Amending an outcome record is how a correction is made: the manager supplies fresh `live_birth`, `births_count`, `multiple_birth`, and `gestational_weeks` values together with an `amendment_reason`, and the amendment is accepted only while the record is still `RECORDED` and the logical clock is still inside the configured amendment window measured from the record's `outcome_recorded_on`; outside that window the request is refused with `ERR_AMENDMENT_WINDOW_CLOSED`. An amendment rewrites the record in place rather than appending a correction beside it, so the clinic's workset recomputes at the new values and the rate readings move to the amended value rather than being averaged against the superseded one; an implementation that appends instead of replacing will drift away from the true rate a little further with every amendment. Amendments also recompute the member's `live_births_recorded`, refresh the clinic's quality readings, and may carry a clinic from `PROBATION` back to `CONTRACTED`. A second amendment of the same record is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_OPERATION`, as is a second report for the same cycle.

Voiding is the other correction, and it is more dangerous to the surrounding facts. A `NetworkManager` may void a record that is either `RECORDED` or `AMENDED` provided that no paid claim references the cycle the record belongs to — a record that has already been used to support payment cannot be erased, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Voiding moves the record to `VOIDED`, which is terminal, and removes it from the clinic's workset entirely rather than merely flagging it, which is why voiding a record that was not a live birth raises the clinic's live-birth rate while voiding one that was a live birth lowers it — a removal from both numerator and denominator rather than from the numerator alone. A voided record cannot be resurrected, and `void_reason` is populated only while a record sits in the `VOIDED` outcome state — the counterpart of `amendment_reason`, which is populated only while a record sits in `AMENDED`. Voiding recomputes `cycles_reported`, the derived rates, and the member's `live_births_recorded`. A voided record also counts as the operation having been carried out on that resource, so a later attempt against the same record is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_OPERATION`.

The loop closes when the bundle runs out, and it closes across the whole network definition at once. When a bundle's cycle allowance and its specialty-pharmacy pool are both empty — the condition that makes the bundle read as `EXHAUSTED` — every authorization on a plan drawing that entitlement that is still awaiting a first decision is denied, recorded for the reason `BUNDLE_EXHAUSTED`, and the plan's pinned price is recomputed from the current contract rates so that the plan does not continue to quote a price from an entitlement that will fund nothing further. The same exhaustion is visible from the clinical side as an unyielding refusal: a filing against an exhausted bundle fails with `ERR_NO_REMAINING_ALLOWANCE`, and a dispensing against an empty pool fails with `ERR_PHARMACY_POOL_EXHAUSTED`. Because the exhaustion posture is derived, the loop is genuinely reversible — `Member.live_births_recorded` and every other derived counter are recomputed from the rows that are still live, a claim reversal or a cycle cancellation that returns allowance to the bundle returns the bundle to `ACTIVE`, and a plan that was blocked by the exhaustion cascade can be authorized again once the allowance is genuinely there. Stewardship also has its read side: claims sit in one of `RECEIVED`, `PENDED`, `ADJUDICATED`, `DENIED`, `APPEALED`, `PAID`, or `REVERSED`, and an operator tracking the network's exposure can filter the claim collection by `claim_status`, `service_category`, `member_id`, `preauthorization_id`, or `treatment_cycle_id`; plans can be filtered by `plan_status`, `member_id`, `clinic_id`, `bundle_entitlement_id`, `care_advocate_id`, or `pathway_type`; and outcome records by `clinic_id`, `treatment_cycle_id`, `member_id`, or `outcome_status`.

Two further capabilities round out network stewardship, and each belongs to a specific operator rather than to the network as a whole. A clinic-quality recompute re-derives a clinic's rate readings and posture from the workset already on file without writing any new outcome data, and it is the only path by which a clinic whose latest active contract window has closed is demoted to `SUSPENDED`. A clock-advance capability moves the logical clock forward to the next pending deadline and fires the transitions that have come due, which is also how the outcome record for a clinic-side report becomes reportable in the first place. Neither is a clinical act, and neither changes any outcome evidence.

Finally, treating a member's enrollment as merely administrative would be a mistake, because the network quality loop reads back into the member record. The member's completed-cycle count counts their cycles that have reached `OUTCOME_RECORDED`, and the member's recorded-live-births figure sums the `births_count` across their outcome records that are `RECORDED` or `AMENDED`, treating voided records as if they had never existed. Both are recomputed whenever the underlying cycles or records move, so a member's read-back figures and their clinic's read-back figures are always derived from the same events and cannot silently disagree.
