# Student Records Transfer API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A district student records office sits at the intersection of two pressures that pull in opposite directions. Families and receiving districts want a student's file to move quickly — a child who enrolls somewhere new on Monday should not wait a month for a transcript — while federal and state education-privacy law makes every page of that file a regulated disclosure with its own authorization, notification, and logging duties. The office that gets this wrong is either slow or unlawful, and usually both.

This document specifies the Student Records Transfer API, the system of record for a district's withdrawal processing, inter-district records requests, cumulative-file assembly, parent and eligible-student inspection, amendment challenges and hearings, directory-information opt-outs, subpoena handling, and retention and destruction scheduling. The API is mounted under `/records-office`. Its central object is the **cumulative file**: a per-student container assembled from four components — academic, health, discipline, and special education — whose collective certification state determines whether the district may lawfully snapshot and transmit a transfer packet. Around that container the system tracks statutory response windows measured in business days, disciplinary-record inclusion rules that differ from state to state, review appointments in physical rooms, hearings before assigned officers, and destruction dates that slide forward every time the record is touched.

Two design commitments run through everything that follows. First, **every deadline is derived, not asserted**: response windows, hearing windows, opt-out grace periods, and destruction eligibility are all computed by the server from statutory reference data plus a logical clock, and are never accepted from a client. Second, **derived state is reconciled immediately**: when a component is excluded, a statement of disagreement is filed, or a disclosure is logged, the parent file, the affected transfer packets, and the retention schedule are all brought back into agreement inside the same request, so no read ever observes a half-propagated cascade.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contract-level rules that apply across every endpoint: authentication and roles, the response envelope, pagination and filtering, timekeeping, identity, validation precedence, concurrency, and the conservation invariants the domain guarantees.

### 2.1. Rights-Holders, Operators, and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which they act. Eight roles exist, spelled exactly as follows: `SystemAdmin`, `RecordsClerk`, `RecordsOfficer`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `LegalAffairsOfficer`, `ReceivingRegistrar`, `Parent`, and `EligibleStudent`. When `X-User-Role` is absent, or names a role that is not listed among the authorized roles for the endpoint being called, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). Role authorization is the second gate a request passes, immediately after path resolution, and it is checked before any payload field is inspected.

The roles divide along duty lines. A `SystemAdmin` configures statutory reference data and the physical and human resources the office schedules against. A `RecordsClerk` works the front counter: enrolling students, opening withdrawals, managing exit holds, collecting file components, recording discipline incidents and directory disclosures, and taking fee payments. A `RecordsOfficer` is the certifying official: completing withdrawals, certifying and excluding components, driving the whole records-request lifecycle, booking review appointments, recording attendance, and deciding amendments. A `ComplianceOfficer` runs the periodic compliance sweep and executes certified destruction. A `LegalAffairsOfficer` handles subpoenas, law-enforcement requests, and ex parte orders, including the notification duty and production. A `ReceivingRegistrar` is the registrar of an enrolling district who files inter-district requests in that district's name. A `Parent` is a guardian whose child's rights have not yet transferred, and an `EligibleStudent` is a student aged eighteen or over to whom those rights have transferred; the two hold an identical operation set and are mutually exclusive on any given student at any given instant.

How `X-User-ID` is interpreted depends on the role. When `X-User-Role` is `ReceivingRegistrar`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `District`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `District` compare the resource's district reference to `X-User-ID`, which is why a records request filed by a registrar must name that same district as its requesting district. For the seven remaining roles — `SystemAdmin`, `RecordsClerk`, `RecordsOfficer`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `LegalAffairsOfficer`, `Parent`, and `EligibleStudent` — `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity, and the server never compares it to any entity's `id`. The server does record such identifiers on the rows they touch: `certified_by_user_id` on a file component, `logged_by_user_id` on a disclosure log entry, `requester_user_id` on an inspection request or amendment request, and `initial_decided_by_user_id` and `hearing_decided_by_user_id` on an amendment request. Two of those recorded values are load-bearing rather than decorative. A `Parent` may act only when `X-User-ID` equals the student's `guardian_user_id`, and an `EligibleStudent` may act only when `X-User-ID` equals the student's `student_user_id`; another family's guardian holds a permitted role but is still refused with `ERR_NOT_STUDENT_GUARDIAN`. And the officer who denied an amendment may never decide its hearing: a post-hearing decision whose `X-User-ID` equals the stored `initial_decided_by_user_id` is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection endpoint is paginated through two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of items returned; it defaults to 10, may be no smaller than 1, and may be no larger than 100. `offset` skips a number of leading items and defaults to 0. Both are echoed back in the `meta` object of the envelope alongside `total_count`, the number of items matching the query before pagination, and `has_more`, which is true whenever items remain beyond the current page.

Unless a specific collection states otherwise, list results are sorted by `created_at` in ascending order — oldest first, newest last. Two collections restate this ordering because operators depend on it: a student's disclosure log is append-only and read newest last, and a student's discipline incidents are returned in ascending order of `incident_date` with `created_at` breaking ties, which is the same order the discipline-inclusion pass walks them in.

### 2.4. Collection Filters

Filters narrow a collection before pagination is applied, and every filter is an exact-match query parameter on the field of the same name. The state-rule and cumulative-file collections take no filters beyond `limit` and `offset`. The district collection filters on `state_code`. Inspection rooms filter on `status`; hearing officers filter on `status` and `credential`. The student collection filters on `district_id`, `enrollment_status`, and `has_iep`. A student's withdrawals filter on `status`; a student's legal demands filter on `demand_type` and `status`; a student's inspection requests filter on `status` and `requester_role`; a student's discipline incidents filter on `action_type`, `incident_status`, and `transfer_inclusion_state`; and a student's disclosure log filters on `disclosure_type` and `source_type`. A cumulative file's components filter on `status` and `component_type`, and its retention schedules filter on `status`. The records-request collection is the single reverse-lookup channel for all three of that resource's references and filters on `student_id`, `requesting_district_id`, `responding_district_id`, `status`, and `is_overdue`. The review-appointment collection is likewise the only reverse channel for both of its references and filters on `inspection_request_id`, `room_id`, and `status`. The amendment-request collection filters on `student_id`, `contested_component_id`, `hearing_officer_id`, and `status`.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock and Statutory Time Arithmetic

All time-dependent behaviour in this domain reads a server-side **logical clock** rather than wall-clock time. Before any clock-setting call arrives, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it must never fall back to real time. A `SystemAdmin` may move the clock by supplying a `current_utc` instant; doing so changes what every subsequent request perceives as "now" and mutates no entity and runs no sweep. Every timestamp the server mints — the instant a request is received, a component is certified, a disclosure is logged, a demand is produced — is the logical clock's value at that moment.

Three distinct kinds of interval arithmetic appear in the rules, and they must not be conflated. **Calendar-day offsets** add whole days to an instant while preserving the time of day exactly to the millisecond; the directory opt-out grace period, the amendment hearing window, and the legal-demand notification waiting period all work this way. **Business-day offsets** advance a cursor one calendar day at a time from an anchor instant, counting only days that fall on Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, or Friday, until the required number of business days has been counted; the anchor's own calendar day is never counted even when it is itself a business day, and no holiday calendar exists, so Saturday and Sunday are the only skipped weekdays. The statutory response window for an inter-district records request is the only business-day computation in the system, and its result is expressed as the resolved UTC calendar date at `23:59:59.999Z`. **Whole-year offsets** add a number of years to an instant while preserving month, day, and time of day; when the resulting calendar date does not exist — a February 29 anchor projected into a non-leap year — the day is taken as the 28th of the same month and year instead. Retention destruction dates are the only whole-year computation.

Boundary inclusivity is specified individually for each deadline and is never assumed. Where a rule says a deadline is inclusive, an action occurring at exactly the deadline instant succeeds and one millisecond later fails; where a rule is expressed as a strict comparison, exact equality does not trip it.

### 2.6. Common Resource Properties

Every resource carries a server-minted `id`: a UUID string with no type prefix, generated by the server and never accepted from a client. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the row was first stored.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by one on every accepted mutation of that resource. Reconciliation of a derived field counts as a mutation for both purposes: when a component change re-derives a cumulative file's counters, the file's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` is incremented even though no client wrote to the file directly.

Two resources are exceptions and carry only `id` and `created_at`, with both `updated_at` and `version` absent. A **state records rule** is immutable once registered — there is no update operation for it, and it exposes neither `updated_at` nor `version`. A **disclosure log entry** is append-only: entries are written by the operations they document and are never edited, so they too expose neither `updated_at` nor `version`.

### 2.7. Statutory Reference Data

Before any student can be enrolled, the state whose law governs the district must be configured. A **state records rule** is keyed by `state_code`, which is one of `CA`, `TX`, or `NY` and is unique across the table; a second registration for a state that already has one is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_STATE_RULE`. The rule row carries ten statutory parameters. `transfer_response_days` is the business-day window for answering an inter-district request and must be at least 1 and at most 90. `discipline_inclusion_mode` is one of `ALL_INCIDENTS`, `SUSPENSIONS_AND_EXPULSIONS`, or `EXPULSIONS_ONLY` and decides which discipline incidents may leave the state. `discipline_min_days_removed` is the instructional-day threshold a suspension must meet under the middle mode, defaults to 10 when omitted, and is ignored by the other two modes. `include_pending_investigations` decides whether incidents still under investigation may transfer. `sped_requires_consent` decides both whether releasing a special-education component requires signed consent on file and whether the response window is extended for such a request. `amendment_hearing_days` is the calendar-day window within which a challenged amendment's hearing must start and must be at least 1 and at most 90. `destruction_min_age_years` is the number of whole years that must elapse past the retention anchor before destruction and must be at least 1 and at most 30. `directory_optout_grace_days` is the calendar-day window after a student's creation during which a directory opt-out is accepted and must be at least 1 and at most 120. `notification_wait_days` is the number of calendar days the district must wait after notifying a family before producing records under a legal demand, and must be at least 0 and at most 30.

A **district** is registered with a `name`, a `district_code`, and a `state_code`. The district code is globally unique — a duplicate is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_DISTRICT_CODE` — and must be between 2 and 12 characters of uppercase alphanumeric text. The state code must already have a rule row; registering a district for an unconfigured state is refused with `ERR_STATE_RULE_NOT_FOUND`. Each district also exposes `open_request_count`, a derived counter that starts at 0 and always equals the number of records requests whose responding district is this district and whose status is `RECEIVED`, `VALIDATED`, `ASSEMBLING`, or `READY_FOR_RELEASE`. That counter is recomputed on every records-request status change, including the automatic ones.

Two more configuration resources support scheduling. An **inspection room** carries a `room_code` that is globally unique across rooms — a duplicate is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ROOM_CODE` — and between 2 and 12 characters long, a `site_name` naming the building or office, a `seats` capacity of at least 1 and at most 40, and a `status` of `ACTIVE` or `CLOSED` that begins as `ACTIVE`. A `SystemAdmin` may close or reopen a room by supplying a new `status`; a `CLOSED` room accepts no new appointments and yields `ERR_ROOM_CLOSED`, but appointments already scheduled in it keep their occupancy interval and remain attendable. A **hearing officer** carries a `full_name` printed on the decision, a `credential` of `ADMINISTRATIVE_LAW_JUDGE`, `DISTRICT_DESIGNEE`, or `EXTERNAL_CONTRACTOR`, and a `status` of `ACTIVE` or `INACTIVE` that begins as `ACTIVE`. A `SystemAdmin` may deactivate or reactivate an officer by supplying a new `status`; an `INACTIVE` officer keeps hearings already on the calendar but cannot receive new ones, and an attempt to book one is refused with `ERR_OFFICER_INACTIVE`.

### 2.8. Nested Path Resolution and Reference Integrity

Paths in this API nest up to two levels — a cumulative file and one of its components, for example, where the file is addressed by `id` and the component by `component_id`. Every identifier in a path is resolved left to right, and the first one that does not resolve terminates the request with the 404 that names that resource: a bad file identifier yields `ERR_CUMULATIVE_FILE_NOT_FOUND` before the component is looked at, and a component identifier that resolves to a row belonging to a different file is treated as unresolved and yields `ERR_FILE_COMPONENT_NOT_FOUND`. The natural-key path segment `state_code` follows the same rule and yields `ERR_STATE_RULE_NOT_FOUND`. Each resource family has its own code: `ERR_DISTRICT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STATE_RULE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STUDENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WITHDRAWAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CUMULATIVE_FILE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FILE_COMPONENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DISCIPLINE_INCIDENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RECORDS_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INSPECTION_ROOM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INSPECTION_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REVIEW_APPOINTMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_HEARING_OFFICER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AMENDMENT_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LEGAL_DEMAND_NOT_FOUND`, and `ERR_RETENTION_SCHEDULE_NOT_FOUND`.

References supplied in a request body are resolved with the same codes and at the same precedence as path identifiers, before roles are considered against payload contents. This applies to `student_id`, `requesting_district_id`, and `responding_district_id` when a records request is filed, to `contested_component_id` when an amendment is filed, to `room_id` when a review appointment is booked, and to `hearing_officer_id` when a hearing is scheduled.

### 2.9. Validation Precedence

When one request violates several rules at once, the response is determined by a total precedence ordering over nine bands, and the error returned is always the one from the earliest band. The bands, in order, are: **path and body reference existence** (HTTP 404 — every identifier resolves, left to right); **authorization** (HTTP 403 — role permission, rights transfer, guardianship, district ownership, segregation of duties); **payload schema** (HTTP 400 — required fields present and non-null, enum membership, numeric ranges, parseable instants, interval sanity); **optimistic locking** (HTTP 412 — the supplied expected version matches the parent's current version); **entity state** (HTTP 409 — the target resource is in a state that permits the action); **cross-entity state** (HTTP 409 — a different resource is in the state the action requires); **temporal conflict** (HTTP 409 — interval overlap or capacity over a time window); **business rule** (HTTP 422 — statutory windows, consent, custody, conservation); and finally **uniqueness** (HTTP 409 — natural-key and one-active-per-scope collisions).

Two consequences of this ordering deserve emphasis, because they are counter-intuitive. Uniqueness is deliberately last: a duplicate is only reported once the payload is otherwise sound, so a malformed duplicate is reported as a payload error rather than as a collision. And optimistic locking precedes every state check: a stale expected-version token is reported even when the target resource is also in the wrong state for the action.

Within the authorization band the order is likewise fixed: role permission first, then rights transfer (a `Parent` acting on a student whose rights have transferred, then an `EligibleStudent` acting on a student whose rights have not), then guardianship identity, then segregation of duties, then a receiving registrar's district ownership.

### 2.10. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Exactly two write operations take an expected-version token, and both do so because the write folds another resource's current contents into a new row or snapshot.

Marking a records request ready for release requires `expected_cumulative_file_version` in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the student's cumulative file. It is not optional. If it does not equal that file's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CUMULATIVE_FILE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The reason is that marking ready snapshots the packet — its page count and its discipline-inclusion set — out of the file as it stands; a token that is behind the file's version means the client's view predates a component or statement mutation, so the snapshot it is asking for is not the snapshot it would receive. Re-snapshotting is itself legitimate product behaviour, so it is the stale-version check rather than a state precondition that distinguishes an intended re-snapshot from an accidental one.

Booking a review appointment requires `expected_inspection_request_version` in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the inspection request. It is likewise non-optional, and a value that does not equal the request's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_INSPECTION_REQUEST_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Booking supersedes any appointment already scheduled against that inspection request, which is legitimate rescheduling; the token ensures the client is displacing the appointment it actually saw.

No other operation accepts a version field, and none may be rejected for a version mismatch.

### 2.11. Conservation Invariants

Two quantities in this domain are conserved, and both are checked by reading the resource after any mutation rather than by trusting the mutation itself.

The **page-custody identity** holds on every cumulative file after every component mutation: `total_page_count` always equals `included_page_count` plus `excluded_page_count`. Pages are never created or destroyed by moving a component between statuses — they only change custody. `included_page_count` sums `page_count` over components whose status is `COLLECTED` or `CERTIFIED`; `excluded_page_count` sums `page_count` over components whose status is `EXCLUDED`; components still `PENDING` always hold a `page_count` of 0. `total_page_count` sums `page_count` over every component regardless of status and is the conserved quantity: excluding a component and then reinstating it must leave it bit-identical to its prior value. Separately, `disclosable_page_count` on the file sums `page_count` minus `redacted_page_count` over components whose status is `COLLECTED` or `CERTIFIED`, and each component exposes its own `disclosable_page_count` equal to its `page_count` minus its `redacted_page_count`.

The **fee-custody identity** holds on every inspection request after every fee transaction: `fee_assessed_cents` always equals `fee_collected_cents` plus `fee_waived_cents` plus `fee_outstanding_cents`. The assessed amount is pinned when the request is created and is never mutated afterwards by any operation; only the three-way split among collected, waived, and outstanding changes.

### 2.12. Precision and Rounding

Money is held in integer cents; no monetary field is ever a fractional value. Where a fee computation produces a fractional cent, it is rounded half up to a whole cent, and the point at which rounding happens is pinned by the rule that produces it rather than left to the implementation. Page counts, incident counts, day counts, and minute durations are whole integers. Completeness is the single non-integer derived value in the system: a cumulative file's `completeness_pct` is carried to one decimal place, rounded half up, so three of four required components certified reads 75.0, one of three reads 33.3, and two of three reads 66.7. Retention and window instants are millisecond-exact ISO 8601 UTC values, and where a computation carries an anchor's time of day forward it preserves that time of day exactly.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Enrollment, the Records Skeleton, and Directory-Information Rights

Enrollment is the moment the district takes custody of a record, and it is deliberately heavy: a single enrolling call mints the student plus the entire skeleton of the file that will follow them. A `RecordsClerk` (or a `SystemAdmin`) supplies the `district_id` of the enrolling district, the student's `first_name`, `last_name`, and `dob` as an ISO 8601 calendar date, a `grade_level` between 0 and 12 inclusive, the `guardian_user_id` of the parent or guardian who holds the family's rights, and optionally `has_iep` (defaulting to false) and `student_user_id` (defaulting to null, and filled in when the student is old enough to act for themselves). The date of birth must parse as a calendar date and must fall strictly before the logical clock's current date. The new student begins with `enrollment_status` set to `ENROLLED`.

Three derived values are established at that instant. `is_eligible_student` is computed from the date of birth against the logical clock, as described below. `directory_optout_deadline_at` is pinned to the student's `created_at` advanced by the governing state rule's `directory_optout_grace_days` calendar days, at the identical time of day, and is never recomputed afterwards even if the state rule changes. `directory_optout_effective` starts false, `directory_optout_received_at` starts null, and `directory_optout_categories` starts as an empty array. Both `exit_effective_date` and `records_disposition_date` start null: the first is filled from the withdrawal that ends the student's enrollment, the second mirrors a retention schedule that does not yet exist.

In the same transaction the student receives exactly one **cumulative file** whose `student_id` names them, whose `assembly_status` is `NOT_STARTED`, and whose `required_component_types` is pinned from the student's `has_iep` value: `ACADEMIC`, `HEALTH`, and `DISCIPLINE` always, plus `SPECIAL_EDUCATION` when an IEP is active. Every page counter on the file — `total_page_count`, `included_page_count`, `excluded_page_count`, `disclosable_page_count`, `certified_component_count` — begins at 0, `completeness_pct` begins at 0.0, and `last_packet_snapshot_at` begins null. Four **file components** are minted alongside it, one for each `component_type` in `ACADEMIC`, `HEALTH`, `DISCIPLINE`, and `SPECIAL_EDUCATION`, regardless of IEP status; each carries the file's identifier as its `cumulative_file_id`, a `status` of `PENDING`, a `page_count` and `redacted_page_count` of 0, an `amendment_count` of 0, `statement_of_disagreement_present` false, `exclusion_reason`, `certified_by_user_id`, and `certified_at` all null, and an `is_required` flag set true exactly when its type appears in the file's `required_component_types`. A component that is not required may still be collected, certified, and priced for copying — it simply does not gate the file's completeness. Component types are unique within a file, and because components are created by cascade rather than by a client call, the components listing is the only way to learn a component's identifier; it returns the four rows in the fixed order `ACADEMIC`, `HEALTH`, `DISCIPLINE`, `SPECIAL_EDUCATION`. No retention schedule exists yet; that arrives only when a withdrawal completes.

Whether a student or their parent holds the family's rights is a pure function of age and the clock, recomputed on every read of the student and on every mutation, and again on every authorization check for an endpoint that admits the `Parent` or `EligibleStudent` role. Take the difference between the logical clock's calendar year and the year of birth; if the clock's month-and-day pair falls before the month-and-day pair of the birth date, subtract one year. `is_eligible_student` is true when that whole-year count is at least 18, so eligibility begins on the birthday itself: a student born on 2008-08-01 is eligible from the instant `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z` and is not eligible at `2026-07-31T23:59:59.999Z`. The transfer of rights is total rather than shared. While `is_eligible_student` is true, a `Parent` is refused with `ERR_RIGHTS_TRANSFERRED` even though they remain the recorded guardian; while it is false, an `EligibleStudent` is refused with `ERR_RIGHTS_NOT_TRANSFERRED`. Both refusals, and the `ERR_NOT_STUDENT_GUARDIAN` identity check that follows them, guard the same five rights-holder operations: filing an inspection request, filing an amendment request, filing a directory opt-out, requesting a hearing on a denied amendment, and filing a statement of disagreement.

Directory information — the categories a district may publish without consent — is opted out of by the rights-holder, and the opt-out is only effective inside the grace window. The rights-holder supplies `directory_optout_categories`, a non-empty list drawn from `NAME`, `ADDRESS`, `PHONE`, `PHOTO`, `HONORS`, and `SPORTS_PARTICIPATION`; the server stores it in that fixed order regardless of the order supplied, sets `directory_optout_received_at` to the current logical clock instant, and sets `directory_optout_effective` true exactly when that instant is at or before `directory_optout_deadline_at`. The boundary is inclusive to the millisecond: at exactly the deadline the opt-out takes effect, and one millisecond later the filing is refused with `ERR_OPTOUT_WINDOW_CLOSED` and `directory_optout_effective` remains false.

Releasing directory information to a third party is a logged disclosure. A `RecordsClerk` or `RecordsOfficer` supplies the `recipient_name`, the `disclosed_categories` actually released, and optionally a `legitimate_interest_note` recording the recipient's legitimate educational interest or legal basis. The release is refused with `ERR_DIRECTORY_OPTOUT_ACTIVE` when the student's `directory_optout_effective` is true and every requested category appears in `directory_optout_categories`; a category that was not opted out may still be disclosed in the same call. An accepted release appends a **disclosure log entry** whose `student_id` names the student, whose `disclosure_type` is `DIRECTORY_INFORMATION` and `source_type` is `STUDENT_DIRECTORY`, whose `source_id` is the student's own identifier, whose `disclosed_categories` are stored in the fixed directory order `NAME`, `ADDRESS`, `PHONE`, `PHOTO`, `HONORS`, `SPORTS_PARTICIPATION`, whose `page_count` is 0 because no pages leave the office, whose `disclosed_at` is the current logical clock instant, and whose `logged_by_user_id` is the acting `X-User-ID`. Because a new disclosure entry exists, the student's retention schedule — when one exists — has its `last_activity_at` and `destruction_eligible_at` recomputed and the student's `records_disposition_date` re-mirrored. The `source_type` of an entry is what resolves its `source_id`, which points at a records request, a legal demand, an inspection request, or the student themselves depending on which family produced the entry; the four `source_type` values are `RECORDS_REQUEST`, `LEGAL_DEMAND`, `STUDENT_DIRECTORY`, and `INSPECTION_REQUEST`, and the four `disclosure_type` values are `INTERDISTRICT_TRANSFER`, `DIRECTORY_INFORMATION`, `LEGAL_DEMAND`, and `PARENT_INSPECTION`. Every entry in a student's log is created by the operation it documents; there is no operation that writes one directly.

### 3.2. Withdrawal Clearance and the Exit Cascade

A withdrawal is opened by a `RecordsClerk` on behalf of a leaving family. The clerk supplies the `effective_date` — the student's last day of enrollment, as an ISO 8601 calendar date — the `destination_district_code` naming the receiving district, and a `withdrawal_reason` of `TRANSFER_OUT`, `MOVED`, `HOMESCHOOL`, or `OTHER`. The destination code is a natural-key reference, conceptually resolved against a district's `district_code` rather than against any district identifier; it is stored as a free string, is not required to match any registered district, and may be null when the family did not name a receiving district. Opening the withdrawal advances the student from `ENROLLED` to `WITHDRAWAL_PENDING` and creates the withdrawal row with `status` `CLEARED`, `hold_count` 0, `active_hold_types` empty, and `completed_at` null.

Two guards protect that opening, and they are ordered. The student must be in `ENROLLED` or `WITHDRAWAL_PENDING`; a `WITHDRAWN` or `TRANSFERRED_OUT` student can never open another withdrawal and is refused with `ERR_STUDENT_NOT_ENROLLED`. A student already in `WITHDRAWAL_PENDING` passes that first check and is then caught by the sharper uniqueness rule, which refuses a second withdrawal row with `ERR_OPEN_WITHDRAWAL_EXISTS` whenever the student already has one whose status is anything other than `COMPLETED`. Because uniqueness sits in the last precedence band and entity state sits earlier, the state error surfaces for a genuinely departed student while the uniqueness error surfaces for one whose exit is already in flight.

Exit holds are how the office keeps a file from leaving before the student's obligations are settled. A `RecordsClerk` places a hold by supplying a `hold_type` of `UNRETURNED_PROPERTY`, `LIBRARY_FINE`, `MEAL_ACCOUNT`, or `SPED_EXIT_MEETING`. The type is appended to `active_hold_types`, which is kept sorted ascending by enum name, `hold_count` is set to the number of entries in that list, and the withdrawal moves `CLEARED → HOLD`. Placing a second, different hold is a `HOLD → HOLD` self-transition that leaves the status where it is while growing the list. A hold type already present in `active_hold_types` is refused with `ERR_HOLD_ALREADY_ACTIVE`. Releasing a hold names the same `hold_type`; a type that is not in `active_hold_types` is refused with `ERR_HOLD_NOT_ACTIVE`. A release while two or more holds are active removes that one entry and is another `HOLD → HOLD` transition, leaving `hold_count` at one or more; the final release — the one taken while `hold_count` is exactly 1 — empties `active_hold_types`, returns `hold_count` to 0, and moves the withdrawal `HOLD → CLEARED`. That cycle `CLEARED → HOLD → CLEARED` may run any number of times. Once a withdrawal is `COMPLETED`, none of these three operations is available: placing a hold, releasing a hold, and completing are all refused with `ERR_WITHDRAWAL_ALREADY_COMPLETED`.

Completion is the single heaviest cascade in the system, reserved to a `RecordsOfficer` and taking no payload at all. It requires the withdrawal to be `CLEARED` with `hold_count` at 0 and the student still in `WITHDRAWAL_PENDING`; a single outstanding hold blocks the student's exit even though the withdrawal row is otherwise ready, and either failure is refused with `ERR_WITHDRAWAL_NOT_CLEARED`. On success, five effects fire in order and all of them are visible to the very next read. First, the withdrawal moves `CLEARED → COMPLETED` and its `completed_at` is set to the current logical clock instant. Second, the student moves `WITHDRAWAL_PENDING → WITHDRAWN` and their `exit_effective_date` is copied from the withdrawal's `effective_date`. Third, because the student became `WITHDRAWN`, their cumulative file moves `NOT_STARTED → IN_ASSEMBLY`. Fourth, because the file entered `IN_ASSEMBLY` by that route, a **retention schedule** is minted for it with `cumulative_file_id` naming the file, `status` `ACTIVE`, `exit_anchor_at` set to the student's `exit_effective_date` at `00:00:00.000Z`, `destruction_min_age_years` copied from the governing state rule at that moment, and `last_activity_at`, `destruction_eligible_at`, and `exit_anchored_eligible_at` computed as described in the retention narrative below; `destruction_method`, `certification_note`, and `destroyed_at` all begin null. Fifth, because a retention schedule now exists, the student's `records_disposition_date` is set to mirror that schedule's `destruction_eligible_at`, and it is re-mirrored on every later recomputation of that date.

### 3.3. Building the Cumulative File

Assembly is driven component by component, and the entire lifecycle of a component runs through a single update that names a target `status`. A `RecordsClerk` normally drives collection; certification, exclusion, and reinstatement are `RecordsOfficer` work, and both roles may issue the update. Alongside the target status the caller may supply `page_count`, `redacted_page_count`, and `exclusion_reason`, each required only by the transition that consumes it.

Targeting `COLLECTED` against a `PENDING` component is collection: the `page_count` must be present in the body, must be at least 0 and at most 5000, and the `redacted_page_count` — pages withheld inside the component because they carry another student's information — defaults to 0 and may never exceed `page_count`. Both are stored, the component's `disclosable_page_count` becomes its `page_count` minus its `redacted_page_count`, and the component moves `PENDING → COLLECTED`. A `COLLECTED` target against an already-`COLLECTED` component is accepted as a re-collection that restates those page figures. Targeting `CERTIFIED` is certification, permitted only from `COLLECTED` and only when the component's `page_count` is at least 1; the component moves `COLLECTED → CERTIFIED`, its `certified_by_user_id` records the acting `X-User-ID`, and its `certified_at` records the current logical clock instant. Certification attempted against a component that is not `COLLECTED` — a `PENDING` one included — is refused with `ERR_COMPONENT_NOT_COLLECTED`, and certification attempted against a component that is already `CERTIFIED` is refused with `ERR_COMPONENT_ALREADY_CERTIFIED`.

Exclusion is how material is held back. Targeting `EXCLUDED` is permitted from either `COLLECTED` or `CERTIFIED`, and requires an `exclusion_reason` of `OTHER_STUDENT_INFO`, `SEALED_BY_COURT`, or `DUPLICATE_OF_TRANSCRIPT` in the body. From `COLLECTED` the effect is contained: the component moves `COLLECTED → EXCLUDED`, the reason is stored, and on the parent file the component's `page_count` moves out of `included_page_count` and into `excluded_page_count` while `total_page_count` stays exactly where it was. From `CERTIFIED` the effect propagates outward in four linked steps. The component moves `CERTIFIED → EXCLUDED`, the reason is stored, and both `certified_by_user_id` and `certified_at` are cleared to null. The same page transfer between `included_page_count` and `excluded_page_count` happens on the parent, again leaving `total_page_count` untouched. Because a required component has left `CERTIFIED`, the parent file bubbles `COMPLETE → IN_ASSEMBLY`. And because the parent left `COMPLETE`, every records request for that student sitting in `READY_FOR_RELEASE` reverts `READY_FOR_RELEASE → ASSEMBLING`, has its `reopen_count` incremented by one and its `packet_status` set to `VOID`; because those packets became void, their `packet_page_count`, `included_incident_count`, `withheld_incident_count`, and `receiving_state_inclusion_count` are all set to null and every discipline incident of that student returns to a `transfer_inclusion_state` of `NOT_EVALUATED`.

Reinstatement is the compensating action, and it is exact. Targeting `COLLECTED` against an `EXCLUDED` component moves it `EXCLUDED → COLLECTED`, clears `exclusion_reason` back to null, and leaves `page_count` and `redacted_page_count` unchanged unless the caller supplies replacements; on the parent, the component's pages move back out of `excluded_page_count` and into `included_page_count`, restoring the page-custody identity to precisely the figures it held before the exclusion. Reinstatement attempted against a component that is not `EXCLUDED` is refused with `ERR_COMPONENT_NOT_EXCLUDED`, and a `COLLECTED` target aimed at a `CERTIFIED` component — which is neither a valid collection nor a valid reinstatement — carries that same `ERR_COMPONENT_NOT_EXCLUDED`. One further route out of `CERTIFIED` exists and is never requested directly: when an amendment challenge against a component is granted, the system moves it `CERTIFIED → COLLECTED`, clears `certified_by_user_id` and `certified_at`, and increments the component's `amendment_count` by one.

Every accepted component update re-derives the parent file and increments the parent's `version` by one, and it is that version that a packet snapshot's expected-version token is taken against. The file's `certified_component_count` is the number of its components whose `component_type` appears in `required_component_types` and whose status is `CERTIFIED`. Its `completeness_pct` is 100 multiplied by that certified count, divided by the number of required component types, rounded half up to one decimal place — so a file with four required types and three certified reads 75.0, a three-type file with one certified reads 33.3, and the same file with two certified reads 66.7. Excluded components count toward neither the certified count nor the percentage. The file's `assembly_status` is a pure function of its children rather than a value anyone sets: it is `NOT_STARTED` while every component is still `PENDING` and the student is still `ENROLLED`; it is `COMPLETE` when the certified count equals the number of required types; and it is `IN_ASSEMBLY` in every other case. Two consequences follow. The first component to leave `PENDING` moves the file `NOT_STARTED → IN_ASSEMBLY` by itself, without waiting for a withdrawal. And the pair `IN_ASSEMBLY → COMPLETE` and `COMPLETE → IN_ASSEMBLY` forms a cycle the file may traverse repeatedly: certifying the last required component bubbles it up to `COMPLETE` and pins `completeness_pct` at 100.0, while excluding, reinstating, or amending any required component bubbles it straight back down and recomputes the percentage.

### 3.4. Discipline History and Cross-State Inclusion

Discipline is recorded as it happens, by a `RecordsClerk` or a `RecordsOfficer`, and only becomes a transfer question later. Recording an incident takes the `incident_date` as an ISO 8601 calendar date, an `action_type` of `SUSPENSION`, `EXPULSION`, `DETENTION`, or `BEHAVIOR_PLAN`, a `description` narrative that is filed into the discipline component, and three optional facts: `days_removed`, the number of instructional days the student was removed, which defaults to 0 and must be at least 0 and at most 365; `incident_status`, one of `SUSTAINED`, `PENDING_INVESTIGATION`, or `DISMISSED`, which defaults to `SUSTAINED`; and `adjudicated_by_court`, recording whether a court adjudicated the underlying conduct, which defaults to false. A newly recorded incident carries a `transfer_inclusion_state` of `NOT_EVALUATED`, and it keeps that value until a packet snapshot evaluates it.

Whether an incident may leave the state is decided by one function of the governing state rule and the incident, applied in a fixed order of tests. A `DISMISSED` incident never transfers. An incident whose `incident_status` is `PENDING_INVESTIGATION` never transfers when the governing rule's `include_pending_investigations` is false. Past those two gates, the rule's `discipline_inclusion_mode` decides. Under `ALL_INCIDENTS` every surviving incident transfers. Under `SUSPENSIONS_AND_EXPULSIONS` an `EXPULSION` always transfers, a `SUSPENSION` transfers exactly when its `days_removed` is greater than or equal to the rule's `discipline_min_days_removed` — a ten-day threshold means a ten-day suspension does transfer and a nine-day one does not — and a `DETENTION` or `BEHAVIOR_PLAN` does not transfer. Under `EXPULSIONS_ONLY` an incident transfers only when it is an `EXPULSION` and its `adjudicated_by_court` is true; a court-unadjudicated expulsion stays home.

That function is applied twice over the identical incident set, and the two passes exist because the sending and receiving districts may sit in states that disagree. The incident set is every discipline incident of the student regardless of `incident_date` — there is no lookback window — processed in ascending order of `incident_date` with `created_at` breaking ties. The **governing pass** applies the state rule of the responding district, the district that owns the record, and it is the only pass that writes anything to the incidents themselves: each incident's `transfer_inclusion_state` becomes `INCLUDED` or `WITHHELD` accordingly, the request's `included_incident_count` becomes the number of included incidents, and its `withheld_incident_count` becomes the total number of the student's incidents minus that count, so the two always sum to the student's incident count. The **advisory pass** applies the state rule of the requesting district — the receiving district — to the very same incident set and writes only the request's `receiving_state_inclusion_count`. That advisory number never filters a single page; it exists so the receiving registrar is told the size of the gap. For identical incidents the two counts diverge exactly when the two districts' states differ in `discipline_inclusion_mode` or `include_pending_investigations`, and a district in a state that would have accepted more records learns as much from the difference between the advisory count and the governing count.

### 3.5. Inter-District Request Intake and the Statutory Response Window

An inter-district request is filed either by the `ReceivingRegistrar` of the enrolling district or by a `RecordsOfficer` taking a mailed or faxed request in over the counter. The filing names the `student_id` whose records are demanded, the `requesting_district_id` of the district asking, and the `responding_district_id` of the district that owns the record; the responding district must be the district in which the student is enrolled, that is, the student's `district_id`. When the caller acts as `ReceivingRegistrar`, the `requesting_district_id` must equal the acting `X-User-ID`, because a registrar may only file in the name of their own district; a mismatch is refused with `ERR_DISTRICT_MISMATCH`. A `RecordsOfficer` filing on a family's behalf is exempt from that ownership check. Four further facts are optional at filing: `request_priority`, either `STANDARD` or `UNACCOMPANIED_YOUTH`, defaulting to `STANDARD`; `includes_special_education`, declaring whether the requesting district demands the special-education component, defaulting to false; `sped_consent_on_file`, declaring whether signed consent for special-education release is held, defaulting to false; and `authorization_reference`, the enrollment-authorization document reference supplied by the requesting district, defaulting to null.

An accepted filing lands in `RECEIVED` with `received_at` set to the current logical clock instant — the instant from which the statutory window is measured — `authorization_verified` false, `reopen_count` 0, `packet_status` `NONE`, `is_overdue` false, and `packet_page_count`, `included_incident_count`, `withheld_incident_count`, `receiving_state_inclusion_count`, `denial_reason`, `transmission_method`, and `released_at` all null. The responding district's `open_request_count` is recomputed. One request per district pair is allowed at a time: a student may not carry two requests from the same `requesting_district_id` while one of them is in `RECEIVED`, `VALIDATED`, `ASSEMBLING`, or `READY_FOR_RELEASE`, and a second filing is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTIVE_REQUEST`. A request that has been released or denied blocks nothing, so a receiving district may always file afresh after a denial.

The statutory response window is computed at filing and recomputed exactly once more, at validation, after which it is frozen for the rest of the request's life. Only one state rule participates: the rule of the **responding** district's state. The requesting district's rule is never consulted for the window. The computation has two steps. Step one picks the day budget. For an `UNACCOMPANIED_YOUTH` request the budget is the smaller of the responding state's `transfer_response_days` and five days, and the special-education extension is suppressed entirely — an unaccompanied-youth request against a ten-day state that demands the special-education component gets five days, not twenty. For a `STANDARD` request the budget starts at the responding state's `transfer_response_days` and is extended by a further ten days when both `includes_special_education` on the request and `sped_requires_consent` on the responding state's rule are true. Step two advances that many business days from the window's anchor instant, which is `received_at` on both computations — validating late never buys the district extra days, because the recompute at validation still measures from the original receipt instant rather than from the validation instant. The budget arrived at is stored as `statutory_days_allowed`, and `statutory_due_at` is the resolved calendar date at `23:59:59.999Z`.

Validation is a `RecordsOfficer`'s confirmation that the receiving district really is entitled to the record. It requires the request to be in `RECEIVED`; a request in any other status is refused with `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_RECEIVED`, and a request already `RELEASED` or `DENIED` is refused with `ERR_REQUEST_TERMINAL`. The officer supplies `authorization_verified` as true, and the request must already hold a non-null `authorization_reference`; a call with `authorization_verified` false, or against a request whose `authorization_reference` is null, is refused with `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_NOT_VERIFIED`, and the correct disposition of an unverifiable request is denial with the reason `NO_ENROLLMENT_AUTHORIZATION` rather than validation. On success `authorization_verified` is stored as true, the request moves `RECEIVED → VALIDATED`, and the window is recomputed and frozen. The request then bubbles `VALIDATED → ASSEMBLING` immediately, without a further call, whenever the student's cumulative file already sits in `IN_ASSEMBLY` or `COMPLETE`; if the file has not started assembling yet, the request waits in `VALIDATED` and makes the same automatic move as soon as the file enters `IN_ASSEMBLY`. Each time a request changes status, whether by operator action or by that automatic bubble, the responding district's `open_request_count` is recomputed.

Overdue tracking is not automatic. A `ComplianceOfficer` runs a compliance sweep, which takes no payload and is idempotent with respect to the logical clock: run twice at the same instant, the second run finds nothing left to change. The sweep sets `is_overdue` true on every request whose status is `RECEIVED`, `VALIDATED`, `ASSEMBLING`, or `READY_FOR_RELEASE` and whose `statutory_due_at` is strictly earlier than the current logical clock instant — at exactly the due instant the request is not yet overdue. The same sweep expires stale inspection requests and appointments and promotes retention schedules to destruction eligibility, as described in the sections that own those rules.

### 3.6. Packet Snapshot, Reopening, Release, and Denial

Snapshotting the transfer packet is the point at which a live, mutable file becomes a fixed set of pages, and it is guarded more tightly than any other operation. Only a `RecordsOfficer` may take it, and the body carries a single field: `expected_cumulative_file_version`, the `version` of the student's cumulative file as the officer last read it. The guards are evaluated in the precedence order of §2.9, which matters here because five of them can fire on the same call. First the expected-version token is compared: a value that does not equal the file's current `version` is refused with `ERR_STALE_CUMULATIVE_FILE_VERSION`, since any collection, certification, exclusion, or reinstatement since the officer's read has moved the file on and invalidated the snapshot they were expecting. Next the request's own status must be `ASSEMBLING`; anything else — including a request still parked in `VALIDATED` because the student's file has not started assembling — is refused with `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_ASSEMBLING`, and a `RELEASED` or `DENIED` request is refused with `ERR_REQUEST_TERMINAL`. Then the cross-entity condition: the student's cumulative file must be in `COMPLETE`, meaning every required component is `CERTIFIED`, and a file in `IN_ASSEMBLY` is refused with `ERR_FILE_NOT_COMPLETE`. Finally the consent condition: when the student's `has_iep` is true and the responding state's `sped_requires_consent` is true, the request's `sped_consent_on_file` must be true, and a request that fails this is refused with `ERR_SPED_CONSENT_REQUIRED`. Where the responding state's rule sets `sped_requires_consent` false, the consent flag is irrelevant and the identical payload passes. The file must also hold at least one component in `CERTIFIED` for a snapshot to mean anything.

A successful snapshot does five things. Both passes of the discipline-inclusion evaluation run, storing `included_incident_count`, `withheld_incident_count`, and `receiving_state_inclusion_count` on the request and stamping every one of the student's discipline incidents with `INCLUDED` or `WITHHELD`. The packet's page count is computed and stored as `packet_page_count`. The request's `packet_status` becomes `SNAPSHOT`. The cumulative file's `last_packet_snapshot_at` is set to the current logical clock instant and the file's `version` is incremented by one — which is why an officer who intends to take a second snapshot must re-read the file before doing so. And the request moves `ASSEMBLING → READY_FOR_RELEASE`.

The packet page count is deliberately not the file's disclosable total. Only `CERTIFIED` components contribute; `PENDING`, `COLLECTED`, and `EXCLUDED` components contribute nothing, which is precisely why excluding a certified component both reopens the request and changes the page count of the next snapshot. Sum, over the certified components of the student's file, each component's `page_count` minus its `redacted_page_count`; call that the raw page figure. Separately sum the same quantity over just the certified `DISCIPLINE` component. When the raw figure is strictly greater than 500 pages, the packet substitutes a summary for the disciplinary material: the packet page count becomes the raw figure minus the discipline pages, plus three pages of summary. Otherwise the packet page count is the raw figure unchanged. The substitution test is evaluated exactly once, against the raw figure, and the resulting packet page count is never re-tested — so a packet that falls back below the 500-page threshold after substitution keeps its substituted value.

Reopening is the counterpart cycle. Whenever the student's cumulative file leaves `COMPLETE` — by exclusion of a required component, by reinstatement that leaves a required type uncertified, or by an amendment that decertifies one — every records request for that student in `READY_FOR_RELEASE` moves `READY_FOR_RELEASE → ASSEMBLING`, its `reopen_count` grows by one, its `packet_status` becomes `VOID`, its `packet_page_count`, `included_incident_count`, `withheld_incident_count`, and `receiving_state_inclusion_count` are all nulled, and every discipline incident of the student returns to `NOT_EVALUATED`. The cycle `ASSEMBLING → READY_FOR_RELEASE → ASSEMBLING` may run any number of times, and each fresh snapshot supersedes the void one.

Release transmits what was snapshotted. A `RecordsOfficer` supplies the `transmission_method`, one of `SECURE_PORTAL`, `ENCRYPTED_EMAIL`, or `CERTIFIED_MAIL`. The request must be in `READY_FOR_RELEASE` — a request reopened to `ASSEMBLING` by a component change is refused with `ERR_REQUEST_NOT_READY` — and its `packet_status` must be `SNAPSHOT`. Two cross-entity holds then apply. An unexecuted ex parte order freezes the file against outbound transfer: while the student has any legal demand whose `demand_type` is `EX_PARTE_ORDER` and whose status is `RECEIVED` or `NOTIFIED`, release is refused with `ERR_EX_PARTE_HOLD_ACTIVE`, and producing that demand clears the block. Court-sealed material may not leave the district under a transfer request: when any component of the file is `EXCLUDED` with an `exclusion_reason` of `SEALED_BY_COURT` and the request's `denial_reason` was not set to `COURT_SEALED`, release is refused with `ERR_COURT_SEALED_COMPONENT`.

An accepted release moves the request `READY_FOR_RELEASE → RELEASED`, sets `released_at` to the current logical clock instant, and stores the transmission method. It then appends a disclosure log entry whose `disclosure_type` is `INTERDISTRICT_TRANSFER`, whose `source_type` is `RECORDS_REQUEST`, whose `source_id` is the request's own identifier, whose `page_count` is the request's `packet_page_count`, and whose `disclosed_categories` are the packet's component types in the fixed order `ACADEMIC`, `HEALTH`, `DISCIPLINE`, `SPECIAL_EDUCATION`. Because a new disclosure entry now exists, the student's retention schedule has its `last_activity_at` set to that entry's `disclosed_at`, its `destruction_eligible_at` recomputed, and the student's `records_disposition_date` re-mirrored from it. A student who is in `WITHDRAWN` at that moment advances `WITHDRAWN → TRANSFERRED_OUT`, the terminal enrollment state; releasing a packet for a student who is still `ENROLLED` is perfectly legal and leaves `enrollment_status` untouched. The responding district's `open_request_count` is recomputed.

Denial is available from `RECEIVED`, `VALIDATED`, and `ASSEMBLING` alike, and requires a `denial_reason` of `NO_ENROLLMENT_AUTHORIZATION`, `STUDENT_NOT_IN_DISTRICT`, `DUPLICATE_REQUEST`, or `COURT_SEALED`. The request moves to `DENIED`, the reason is stored, and the responding district's `open_request_count` is recomputed. No disclosure log entry is written, because a denial discloses nothing. `RELEASED` and `DENIED` are both terminal: every further action against such a request is refused with `ERR_REQUEST_TERMINAL`.

### 3.7. Inspection Requests, Copy Fees, and Review Appointments

A rights-holder — a `Parent` while rights have not transferred, an `EligibleStudent` once they have — may ask to inspect the record. The filing names `requested_categories`, a non-empty subset of `ACADEMIC`, `HEALTH`, `DISCIPLINE`, and `SPECIAL_EDUCATION`, and optionally `copies_requested`, defaulting to false. The server derives the rest: `requester_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID` and `requester_role` from the acting `X-User-Role`, recorded as `PARENT` or `ELIGIBLE_STUDENT`. The request begins in `SUBMITTED` with `inspection_completed_at` null, and its `statutory_inspection_due_at` is pinned to its `created_at` advanced by 45 calendar days at the identical time of day. A request left unscheduled past that deadline is expired by the compliance sweep: any request still in `SUBMITTED` whose `statutory_inspection_due_at` is strictly earlier than the current logical clock instant moves `SUBMITTED → EXPIRED`.

Copy fees are priced once, at filing, and frozen. When `copies_requested` is false, `base_copy_fee_cents` is 0. Otherwise the fee is built component by component over the components of the student's cumulative file whose status is `COLLECTED` or `CERTIFIED` and whose `component_type` appears in `requested_categories`, processed in the fixed order `ACADEMIC`, `HEALTH`, `DISCIPLINE`, `SPECIAL_EDUCATION`. Components that are still `PENDING` hold no pages and contribute nothing, and `EXCLUDED` components never contribute. For each qualifying component, take its `page_count` minus its `redacted_page_count` as the chargeable page figure, multiply that by seven and a half cents per page, and round half up to a whole number of cents; then apply the 8.25 percent copy surcharge by multiplying that whole-cent figure by one plus the surcharge rate and rounding half up again to whole cents. Add the twice-rounded per-component amounts together to get `base_copy_fee_cents`. The rounding order is pinned: rounding is applied per component, twice, and never once over the summed raw page charges — summing first and rounding at the end produces a different total and is wrong. The priced component set is frozen at filing, so later page changes never re-price an existing request.

A certification fee rides on top, keyed on the copy fee alone. When `base_copy_fee_cents` is below 2000 cents there is no certification fee. When it is at least 2000 cents but below 10000 cents, `certification_fee_cents` is 500 cents. When it is 10000 cents or more, `certification_fee_cents` is 1500 cents. Both tier floors are inclusive. `fee_assessed_cents` is then `base_copy_fee_cents` plus `certification_fee_cents`, and the tier is never re-evaluated against that total — even when adding the certification fee pushes the total across a tier boundary, the tier stays the one the copy fee alone selected. The request is created with `fee_collected_cents` and `fee_waived_cents` at 0 and `fee_outstanding_cents` equal to `fee_assessed_cents`.

Fees move through a single transaction operation available to a `RecordsClerk` or a `RecordsOfficer`, which names a `fee_transaction_type` and an `amount_cents` of at least 1. A `PAYMENT` adds its amount to `fee_collected_cents`; a `WAIVER` adds its amount to `fee_waived_cents`, recording a hardship waiver; a `REVERSAL` subtracts its amount from `fee_collected_cents`, so the collected total is always the sum of payments less the sum of reversals. After every transaction, `fee_outstanding_cents` is recomputed as `fee_assessed_cents` minus `fee_collected_cents` minus `fee_waived_cents`, `fee_assessed_cents` itself is left untouched, and the inspection request's `version` is incremented by one. No payment or waiver may exceed the current `fee_outstanding_cents`; one that would is refused with `ERR_FEE_OVERPAYMENT`, which is what keeps the fee-custody identity of §2.11 true at every instant.

Scheduling the review is `RecordsOfficer` work. The booking names the `room_id` of the room to be occupied, the `start_at` and `end_at` instants of the appointment as ISO 8601 UTC values, and the `expected_inspection_request_version` token described in §2.10. The end instant must be strictly after the start instant — equality is rejected as a payload error — and the resulting `duration_minutes` is the whole number of minutes between them. The inspection request must be in `SUBMITTED` or `SCHEDULED`; a `COMPLETED` one is refused with `ERR_INSPECTION_ALREADY_COMPLETED` and an `EXPIRED` one with `ERR_INSPECTION_EXPIRED`. The room must be `ACTIVE`, or the booking is refused with `ERR_ROOM_CLOSED`. Room occupancy is then checked with **closed, buffer-padded** semantics: the new interval and every existing `SCHEDULED` or `ATTENDED` appointment in the same room — including any still-`SCHEDULED` appointment of this very inspection request, whose interval is not released until this booking has been accepted — are each padded by fifteen minutes of turnover on both sides, and a collision exists whenever the new start is at or before the existing end plus fifteen minutes and the new end is at or after the existing start minus fifteen minutes. Touching the padded edge therefore collides, and the booking is refused with `ERR_ROOM_DOUBLE_BOOKED`. Finally the appointment must fall inside the statutory inspection window: `start_at` must be at or after the current logical clock instant and at or before the request's `statutory_inspection_due_at`, and a booking into the past or beyond the window is refused with `ERR_APPOINTMENT_OUTSIDE_STATUTORY_WINDOW`.

An accepted booking creates a **review appointment** whose `inspection_request_id` names the request it serves, in status `SCHEDULED` with `attended_at` null. Any appointment of that same request already in `SCHEDULED` moves `SCHEDULED → SUPERSEDED` and releases its room interval, so the superseded interval no longer participates in the overlap detection of any later booking. The inspection request itself moves to `SCHEDULED` and its `version` is incremented by one. Booking repeatedly is therefore the supported way to reschedule an inspection, and each rebooking requires a fresh expected-version token. An appointment nobody attends is cleaned up by the compliance sweep: any appointment still in `SCHEDULED` whose `end_at` is strictly earlier than the current logical clock instant moves `SCHEDULED → EXPIRED` and releases its room interval.

Attendance closes the loop. A `RecordsOfficer` records the `attended` flag as true against an appointment that is in `SCHEDULED`; a `SUPERSEDED`, `EXPIRED`, or already-`ATTENDED` appointment can never be attended and is refused with `ERR_APPOINTMENT_NOT_SCHEDULED`. Where the inspection request had `copies_requested` true, its `fee_outstanding_cents` must be exactly 0 before attendance may be recorded; an outstanding balance is refused with `ERR_FEE_OUTSTANDING`. On success the appointment moves `SCHEDULED → ATTENDED` with `attended_at` set to the current logical clock instant, and because it was attended the parent inspection request bubbles `SCHEDULED → COMPLETED` with `inspection_completed_at` set to the same instant. A disclosure log entry is appended with `disclosure_type` `PARENT_INSPECTION` and `source_type` `INSPECTION_REQUEST`, its `source_id` naming the inspection request. And because a new disclosure entry exists, the student's retention schedule — if one exists — has its `last_activity_at` and `destruction_eligible_at` recomputed and the student's `records_disposition_date` re-mirrored.

### 3.8. Amendment Challenges, Hearings, and Statements of Disagreement

When a rights-holder believes the record is inaccurate or misleading, they may challenge one component of it. The challenge names the `contested_component_id` of the component holding the disputed content, the `contested_field` naming the record element alleged to be wrong, the `requested_change` describing the correction sought, and a `rationale` stating the basis. The component must belong to the cumulative file of the student in question and must not be `EXCLUDED`; a component belonging to another student's file, or an excluded one, is refused with `ERR_COMPONENT_NOT_FOR_STUDENT`, while an identifier that resolves to nothing at all is refused earlier with `ERR_FILE_COMPONENT_NOT_FOUND`. An accepted challenge begins in `SUBMITTED` with `requester_user_id` taken from the acting `X-User-ID`, `hearing_officer_id` null, and `initial_decided_by_user_id`, `hearing_decided_by_user_id`, `denied_at`, `hearing_due_at`, `hearing_start_at`, `hearing_end_at`, `hearing_duration_minutes`, `statement_text`, and `statement_filed_at` all null.

A `RecordsOfficer` decides the challenge by supplying an `outcome` of `GRANT` or `DENY`, optionally with a `decision_note`. From `SUBMITTED` this is the initial decision and the acting `X-User-ID` is recorded as `initial_decided_by_user_id`. A grant moves the challenge `SUBMITTED → AMENDED` and reaches into the record: the contested component moves `CERTIFIED → COLLECTED` with its `amendment_count` incremented by one, which bubbles the parent cumulative file `COMPLETE → IN_ASSEMBLY` and increments its `version`, which in turn reverts every `READY_FOR_RELEASE` records request of that student to `ASSEMBLING` with `packet_status` `VOID` and the packet counters nulled. A denial moves the challenge `SUBMITTED → DENIED`, sets `denied_at` to the current logical clock instant, and computes `hearing_due_at` as that instant advanced by the `amendment_hearing_days` of the state rule governing the **student's** district, at the identical time of day. A decision attempted against a challenge in any status other than `SUBMITTED` or `HEARING_SCHEDULED` — a `DENIED`, `HEARING_REQUESTED`, or `UPHELD` one — is refused with `ERR_AMENDMENT_NOT_SUBMITTED`, and one attempted against a challenge that has reached `AMENDED` or `STATEMENT_FILED`, both terminal, is refused with `ERR_AMENDMENT_TERMINAL`.

A denial opens the hearing right, and the right is time-boxed. The rights-holder requests a hearing with no payload at all; the challenge must be in `DENIED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_AMENDMENT_NOT_DENIED`, and the current logical clock instant must be at or before `hearing_due_at`, the boundary being inclusive — a request filed after that instant is refused with `ERR_HEARING_REQUEST_WINDOW_CLOSED`. On success the challenge moves `DENIED → HEARING_REQUESTED`.

Scheduling the hearing is `RecordsOfficer` work and names the `hearing_officer_id` who will preside plus the `hearing_start_at` and `hearing_end_at` instants; `hearing_duration_minutes` is derived as the whole number of minutes between them, and the end instant must be strictly after the start instant. The challenge must be in `HEARING_REQUESTED` or `HEARING_SCHEDULED` — a call from a status outside that pair is refused with `ERR_HEARING_NOT_SCHEDULED` — so a second scheduling call is a legitimate reschedule rather than an error, and that self-loop on `HEARING_SCHEDULED` may run as often as the statutory window allows. The named officer must exist and must be `ACTIVE`, or the call is refused with `ERR_OFFICER_INACTIVE`. Three further conditions then apply, in this order. Officer occupancy is checked with **half-open** semantics and no buffer at all: the new window conflicts with another `HEARING_SCHEDULED` window for the same officer only when the two intervals overlap treating each as including its start instant and excluding its end instant, so a hearing that starts exactly when another ends is accepted. This is deliberately different from the padded, closed room rule of §3.7. A conflict is refused with `ERR_OFFICER_DOUBLE_BOOKED`. Next the officer's daily load: the sum of `hearing_duration_minutes` for that officer across one UTC calendar date, including the window being booked, may not exceed 240 minutes — four hours; exactly 240 is accepted and 241 is refused with `ERR_OFFICER_DAILY_CAP_EXCEEDED`. On a reschedule, the window being replaced is excluded from that sum, and the previously reserved interval is released before the new interval is tested, so rescheduling onto the identical slot with the identical officer is always allowed. Finally the statutory window: `hearing_start_at` must be at or before `hearing_due_at`, inclusive to the millisecond, and a later start is refused with `ERR_HEARING_WINDOW_EXCEEDED`. On success the officer and window are stored and the challenge stands in `HEARING_SCHEDULED`.

The post-hearing decision uses the same decision operation, and it carries the segregation-of-duties rule that gives this workflow its shape. The challenge must be in `HEARING_SCHEDULED`, the current logical clock instant must be at or after `hearing_end_at` — a decision issued before the hearing has actually been held is refused with `ERR_HEARING_NOT_HELD` — and the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the stored `initial_decided_by_user_id`, or the decision is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The official who denied the amendment may not decide its own hearing, which means the two decision calls on one challenge must come from two different operators. Whichever way it goes, the acting `X-User-ID` is recorded as `hearing_decided_by_user_id`. A grant moves the challenge `HEARING_SCHEDULED → AMENDED` and fires the identical component and packet cascade as the initial grant. A denial moves it `HEARING_SCHEDULED → UPHELD`.

An upheld record cannot be changed, but it can be annotated forever. The rights-holder files a statement of disagreement by supplying `statement_text`; the challenge must be in `UPHELD`, and a filing from any other status — including a second filing against a challenge already in `STATEMENT_FILED` — is refused with `ERR_STATEMENT_ALREADY_FILED`. On success the text is stored, `statement_filed_at` is set to the current logical clock instant, and the challenge moves `UPHELD → STATEMENT_FILED`, which is terminal. The statement then becomes part of the record: the contested component's `statement_of_disagreement_present` is set true and its `page_count` grows by exactly one page, the statement's own page. Because the component's page count changed, the parent cumulative file's `total_page_count`, `included_page_count`, and `disclosable_page_count` are recomputed and the file's `version` is incremented by one — so a statement filed after a packet snapshot invalidates the expected-version token any officer was holding.

### 3.9. Legal Demands, Retention Schedules, and Certified Destruction

Subpoenas and law-enforcement requests are docketed by a `LegalAffairsOfficer`. The docket entry names the `demand_type` — `JUDICIAL_SUBPOENA`, `GRAND_JURY_SUBPOENA`, `LAW_ENFORCEMENT_REQUEST`, or `EX_PARTE_ORDER` — the `issuing_authority` that issued the instrument, the `case_reference` docket or case number recorded in the disclosure log, and the `production_scope`, a non-empty subset of `ACADEMIC`, `HEALTH`, `DISCIPLINE`, and `SPECIAL_EDUCATION` naming the components the demand reaches. Two optional fields govern the notification duty: `notification_waived`, defaulting to false, and `waiver_basis`, one of `GRAND_JURY_ORDER`, `EX_PARTE_COURT_ORDER`, or `LAW_ENFORCEMENT_DIRECTIVE`. The pairing rules are strict and are checked as payload conditions: a waived demand must carry a `waiver_basis`, a non-waived demand must leave it absent or null, `GRAND_JURY_ORDER` is accepted only alongside a `GRAND_JURY_SUBPOENA`, and `EX_PARTE_COURT_ORDER` only alongside an `EX_PARTE_ORDER`. Any violation is refused with `ERR_WAIVER_BASIS_REQUIRED`. A docketed demand begins in `RECEIVED` with `received_at` at the current logical clock instant and `notified_party`, `notification_sent_at`, `production_allowed_at`, `produced_at`, and `produced_page_count` all null. From that moment an `EX_PARTE_ORDER` in `RECEIVED` or `NOTIFIED` blocks release of any of the student's records requests, as described in §3.6.

The notification duty is discharged before production. The officer records the `notified_party`, either `PARENT` or `ELIGIBLE_STUDENT`. The demand must be in `RECEIVED` — a `NOTIFIED` demand is refused with `ERR_DEMAND_NOT_RECEIVED` and a `PRODUCED` one with `ERR_DEMAND_ALREADY_PRODUCED` — and its `notification_waived` must be false, since notifying under a waived instrument is refused with `ERR_NOTIFICATION_WAIVED`. On success the party is recorded, `notification_sent_at` is set to the current logical clock instant, `production_allowed_at` is computed as that instant advanced by the `notification_wait_days` of the state rule governing the student's district at the identical time of day, and the demand moves `RECEIVED → NOTIFIED`.

Production forks into two branches out of the same starting point, and the two write different fields. The officer supplies the `produced_page_count`, the number of pages actually handed over. On the **notified branch**, the demand is in `NOTIFIED` and the current logical clock instant must be at or after `production_allowed_at`; the boundary is inclusive, so production at exactly that instant succeeds while production one millisecond earlier is refused with `ERR_NOTIFICATION_WAIT_NOT_ELAPSED`. On the **waived branch**, the demand is still in `RECEIVED` with `notification_waived` true and a non-null `waiver_basis`, and it may be produced immediately with no waiting period at all; this branch never sets `notification_sent_at` or `production_allowed_at`, both of which remain null on the produced row. Attempting to produce a demand that sits in `RECEIVED` with `notification_waived` false is refused with `ERR_NOTIFICATION_REQUIRED`, because such a demand owes a notification first, and attempting to produce an already-`PRODUCED` demand is refused with `ERR_DEMAND_ALREADY_PRODUCED`. Either branch moves the demand to `PRODUCED`, sets `produced_at` to the current logical clock instant, and stores the page count. Either branch then appends a disclosure log entry with `disclosure_type` `LEGAL_DEMAND` and `source_type` `LEGAL_DEMAND`, its `source_id` naming the demand. And because a new disclosure entry exists, the student's retention schedule has its `last_activity_at` set to that entry's `disclosed_at` and its `destruction_eligible_at` recomputed, and because that date changed, the student's `records_disposition_date` is re-mirrored from it.

Retention is anchored twice over, and the two anchors tell different stories about the same file. The schedule's `exit_anchor_at` is the student's `exit_effective_date` at `00:00:00.000Z`. Its `last_activity_at` is the latest of three candidate instants: the exit anchor itself, the latest `disclosed_at` across the student's disclosure log entries of every `disclosure_type` — directory disclosures very much included — and the latest `certified_at` across the components of the file that still hold one. A component whose `certified_at` was cleared by exclusion or by a granted amendment drops out of that comparison immediately, which can pull the latest-activity instant backwards. Where two candidate instants are identical the result is the same instant either way, so no tie-break is needed. The governing eligibility date, `destruction_eligible_at`, is `last_activity_at` advanced by `destruction_min_age_years` whole years, using the year arithmetic of §2.5 with its February 29 fallback to the 28th and its exact preservation of the anchor's time of day. The advisory date, `exit_anchored_eligible_at`, is `exit_anchor_at` advanced by the same number of whole years. The governing value moves forward with every later disclosure or certification, so an actively disclosed record is never destroyed on schedule; the advisory value freezes at the student's exit and ignores everything afterwards, which makes it always at or before the governing value and makes the gap between them a direct measure of post-exit activity. Both are recomputed when the schedule is minted and again after every disclosure log append and every component certification for that student.

Eligibility is declared by the compliance sweep, never by the operator who wants to destroy. The sweep moves a schedule `ACTIVE → DESTRUCTION_ELIGIBLE` when the current logical clock instant is at or after its `destruction_eligible_at`, the boundary being inclusive, and when the student has no legal demand in `RECEIVED` or `NOTIFIED`. Certified destruction is then executed by a `ComplianceOfficer`, who supplies the `destruction_method` — `CROSS_CUT_SHRED`, `INCINERATION`, or `CERTIFIED_DIGITAL_WIPE` — and a `certification_note` retained on the destruction certificate for audit. The schedule must be in `DESTRUCTION_ELIGIBLE`: one still in `ACTIVE` is refused with `ERR_RETENTION_NOT_ELIGIBLE` because the sweep has not promoted it, and one already in `DESTROYED` is refused with `ERR_ALREADY_DESTROYED`. Eligibility by date is necessary but never sufficient — destruction is additionally refused with `ERR_DESTRUCTION_HOLD_ACTIVE` while the student has any records request in `RECEIVED`, `VALIDATED`, `ASSEMBLING`, or `READY_FOR_RELEASE`, or any legal demand in `RECEIVED` or `NOTIFIED`, the latter being the litigation hold. On success the schedule moves `DESTRUCTION_ELIGIBLE → DESTROYED`, `destroyed_at` is set to the current logical clock instant, and the method and note are stored. Every component of the file then has its `page_count` and `redacted_page_count` set to 0, so the file's `total_page_count` becomes 0 and the page-custody identity holds trivially thereafter. No disclosure log entry is appended: destruction is not a disclosure.
