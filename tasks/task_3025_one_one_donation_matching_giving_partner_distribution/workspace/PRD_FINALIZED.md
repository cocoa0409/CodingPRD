# One For One Donation Matching Giving Partner Distribution API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

The One-for-One Donation Matching and Giving Partner Distribution platform is the control plane behind a pledge that every sold item is matched by a donated one. It exists because a marketing promise of that shape is easy to make and hard to keep honest: the organization has to accrue a donation obligation at the exact moment a sale becomes real, design goods that are fit for donation rather than merely unsold, place those goods with vetted charitable partners, prove the goods arrived, and then defend the published numbers in an annual audit. The API is the single system of record for all four of those jobs, and its output is what the public claim, the partner relationship and the audit opinion are all reconciled against.

Two organizing commitments shape every rule that follows. The first is that a donation obligation, once created by a sale, is never reversed — not by a refund, not by an exchange, not by a product retirement. A customer who returns a pair of socks keeps them out of the return ledger but does not take the donated pair back off a shelter's shelf. The second is that the donation obligation is tracked on two ledgers with deliberately different membership: a provisional ledger that counts obligations the moment an order is opened, and a final ledger that counts only settled, completed sales. The divergence between those two ledgers is not a bug to be reconciled away; it is the quantity a season's freeze snapshots and reports.

The work is distributed across eight operating roles whose duties are deliberately separated, so that no single actor can commit a donation, ship it, confirm its arrival and then grade the partner who received it. Lifecycle-wise, the system moves continuously between a design plane (products, variants and their donation suitability), a partner plane (vetting, capacity ceilings and standing), a logistics plane (allocation, shipment, receipt and reconciliation), a commercial plane (retail orders, payment, returns) and a governance plane (season freezes, claims and audits). Every business path is mounted under the top-level prefix `/impact-program`; the only exceptions are the clock and season-rolling capabilities and the liveness probe, which live outside the business surface.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the business surface must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the operating capacity in which that principal is acting. A request missing either header, or naming a role that the system does not recognize, is rejected before any path or body inspection with `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_CONTEXT` (HTTP 401). A request that is properly authenticated but asks for an operation its role may not perform is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

The system recognizes exactly eight roles. `GivingPartnerCoordinator` onboards and vets partner organizations, records vetting evidence, declares capacity ceilings and confirms partner receipts. `GivingPartnerOrg` is the partner organization acting as itself. `ProvisioningPartner` owns donation product design, the sold-versus-donated split and the operational plan behind the pledge. `AllocationPlanner` chooses beneficiaries, commits benefit allocations against partner capacity, reallocates and reverses supply, and carries unsubstantiated units forward. `FulfillmentOperator` ships committed units, settles donation lines out of the warehouse, and closes, writes off or voids distributions. `CustomerService` creates retail orders, takes payment, and processes returns and exchanges without ever touching partner allocation or audit state. `MarketingManager` publishes donation claims and holds the sole authority to substantiate, derive-build or revoke them. `ImpactAuditor` runs annual impact and partner performance audits, finalizes the season reporting claim, and suspends partners, but may never create allocations.

How `X-User-ID` relates to persisted data depends on the role. When `X-User-Role` is `GivingPartnerOrg`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `GivingPartner`; every ownership check on a resource that references a `GivingPartner` compares that resource's `giving_partner_id` to `X-User-ID`, so this role may confirm receipt only for distributions whose allocation is attributed to its own partner id, and may read only the roll-ups addressed to its own partner id. For the other seven roles — `GivingPartnerCoordinator`, `ProvisioningPartner`, `AllocationPlanner`, `FulfillmentOperator`, `CustomerService`, `MarketingManager` and `ImpactAuditor` — `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record such an identifier where a rule requires it (for example the auditor identity that guards audit finalization, discussed in §2.12), but it never compares an opaque operator identifier to any entity's `id`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints accept a `limit` query parameter that caps how many records are returned and an `offset` query parameter that skips that many leading records. When `limit` is omitted it defaults to 10; it must be at least 1 and at most 100, and any value outside that range is a malformed request. When `offset` is omitted it defaults to 0. All list endpoints order their results by `created_at` in ascending order unless something more specific is stated in §3, and the collection roll-ups that report partner attribution use their own documented tie-break order rather than `created_at`.

Every paginated response carries four fields inside the `meta` object of the envelope: `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`. `total_count` is the number of records matching the query before pagination; `has_more` indicates whether further pages exist beyond the current one.

### 2.4. The Adjudication Ladder: Which Rejection Wins

When more than one rule could reject a request, the system does not choose arbitrarily — it walks a fixed eighteen-rung ladder and reports the highest-priority failure only. Deployments of this API are graded on that ladder, so it is stated here in full order, from the earliest check to the last.

Authentication leads. The presence and validity of `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` is checked first (`ERR_MISSING_AUTH_CONTEXT`), so an unauthenticated request that names a product which does not exist reports the authentication failure rather than `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_FOUND`. Second, the acting role must be permitted for the operation (`ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`), and because this rung precedes all input-shape checks, a forbidden role that submits a body missing a required field reports the permission failure rather than `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Third comes existence: path identifiers are resolved left to right and the first identifier that does not resolve decides the response (`ERR_{RESOURCE}_NOT_FOUND`). Fourth, the request body must be well shaped — every required field present, every enumerated member declared, and every integer range respected (`ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`); a syntactically bad body against a non-existent identifier therefore still reports the not-found failure. Fifth, the target resource must be in a state from which the requested transition is declared (`ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`). Sixth, on the order's independent fulfillment dimension the requested `fulfillment_status` must be strictly later than the stored one (`ERR_FULFILLMENT_REGRESSION`) — this is a separate rung precisely because order `status` and `fulfillment_status` are independent state dimensions on the same row.

The seventh rung is the optimistic concurrency guard (`ERR_STALE_VERSION`, HTTP 412), detailed in §2.7; it runs after the state precondition so a stale write against a terminal resource reports the state failure, and before the fiscal checks so a stale commit never mutates capacity. Eighth, the naming partner's standing is checked (`ERR_PARTNER_SUSPENDED`); ninth, the stricter standing required for a benefit allocation is checked (`ERR_PARTNER_NOT_APPROVED`); both are described in §2.5. Tenth, uniqueness keys are enforced (`ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`): `order_number` must be unique across all sales orders, the pair of partner and `season_code` must be unique across capacity profiles, no two vetting rounds for the same partner may share a `round_number`, and at most one impact season may hold the `FROZEN` status at a time. Eleventh, exactly one capacity profile must resolve for a partner-and-season pair (`ERR_CAPACITY_PROFILE_AMBIGUOUS`); the inverse situation — a single commitment that spans several beneficiaries — is legal and is not an ambiguity. Twelfth, temporal eligibility (`ERR_SEASON_FROZEN`), described in §2.11. Thirteenth, the return window (`ERR_RETURN_WINDOW_CLOSED`, HTTP 422). Fourteenth, the add-on tier boundary rule (`ERR_ADDON_TIER_BOUNDARY`), described in §2.6. Fifteenth, the capacity ceiling (`ERR_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`); sixteenth, liability coverage (`ERR_LIABILITY_INSUFFICIENT`); seventeenth, the claim reference period (`ERR_CLAIM_PERIOD_UNSUPPORTED`); and eighteenth, referential identity agreement (`ERR_ENTITY_UNRESOLVED`).

The ordering is observable in three named conflicts. *Authentication precedes existence*: rung one beats rung three. *State precedes fiscal*: approving an allocation that is not `PENDING_ALLOCATION` reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` even when the same request also exceeds the partner's capacity ceiling. *Temporal precedes fiscal*: a post-freeze commit that is also over capacity reports `ERR_SEASON_FROZEN` rather than `ERR_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Two further pairs follow from the same principle: capacity is checked before liability, so an over-capacity commit against an under-covered liability reports `ERR_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, and the referential rung runs last of all, so a referential mismatch on a request that is also over capacity still reports `ERR_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.

### 2.5. Partner Standing and Allocation Eligibility

Two distinct standing checks guard the allocation surface, and they are not interchangeable. The first asks whether the naming partner is `APPROVED` or `SUSPENDED` at the instant of the write. A partner that is `SUSPENDED` may still receive goods, but only on an allocation whose `priority_tier` is `EMERGENCY`; any other priority tier produces `ERR_PARTNER_SUSPENDED` (HTTP 409). Because this check sits above the fiscal rungs, a `SUSPENDED` partner's over-capacity commit reports `ERR_PARTNER_SUSPENDED` and not `ERR_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.

The second and stricter check asks whether the partner is genuinely `APPROVED` — not merely `SUSPENDED` — and applies to benefit allocations. A partner whose status is neither `APPROVED` nor `SUSPENDED` — `INACTIVE`, `VETTING`, `CANDIDATE`, `REJECTED` or `REJECTED_FINAL` — is ineligible for a benefit allocation and produces `ERR_PARTNER_NOT_APPROVED` (HTTP 409). The stricter check does not, however, override the looser check's `EMERGENCY` carve-out: where the write carries a priority tier and that tier is `EMERGENCY`, a `SUSPENDED` partner is admitted through the stricter gate as well, and a non-`EMERGENCY` tier is the case that produces `ERR_PARTNER_SUSPENDED`. These two checks apply to committing an allocation plan, approving an allocation, substantiating a claim, and reversing a received allocation; the stricter benefit-only check applies to committing a plan, reversing an allocation and approving an allocation. A distorted capacity picture is the reason both exist: a suspension cascades into capacity profiles and claims (see §3.1), so an allocation computed against a partner's last-known headroom may be computed against a partner that is no longer entitled to receive it.

### 2.6. The Capacity Ledger, Its Ceilings and Its Tier Boundaries

Each partner declares, per season, a capacity ceiling in `max_allocatable_units`. Three figures are tracked against that ceiling and they are related, not interchangeable. `committed_units` is the sum of `allocated_units` over the partner's benefit allocations that are `APPROVED`, `SHIPPED`, `PARTIALLY_RECEIVED` or `RECEIVED`. `reserved_units` holds units forecast to return in a later season by reversal allocations. `impact_units_confirmed` is the count of units a partner has actually confirmed receiving. The live headroom — reported as `capacity_utilization_units` — is `committed_units` plus `reserved_units` minus the confirmed figure, and it is never reported below zero. The figure subtracted from the headroom is the running total of units actually confirmed receiving for the season's distributions, so each partial receipt already lowers the headroom as it lands; it is the partner-level `impact_units_total` reported alongside the partner's other roll-ups — a separate figure that counts only a distribution whose cumulative receipt has reached its shipment — that waits for a full receipt. It is surfaced both on the profile itself and as the `capacity_utilization_units` of the owning partner.

The governing fiscal rule of the surface is that `capacity_utilization_units` must not exceed `max_allocatable_units` for the resolved partner-and-season profile, and that a shortage of exactly zero units passes. A request that would breach the ceiling is rejected with `ERR_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409) rather than silently clamped, and the same ceiling is re-checked on approving an allocation, on committing a plan, and on reallocating a cancelled allocation. A profile whose committed figure plus reserved figure minus confirmed figure exactly fills the ceiling is not merely allowed but is transitioned to `EXHAUSTED` in the very request that commits the filling allocation; conversely a profile returns from `EXHAUSTED` to `ACTIVE` the moment a receipt is confirmed or a reversal allocation lands and headroom reappears. When a partner holds a locked or superseded profile for the season and nothing selectable remains, the attempt to draw against it produces `ERR_PARTNER_CAPACITY_LOCKED` (HTTP 409). When two selectable profiles resolve for the same partner and season the commitment is refused outright with `ERR_CAPACITY_PROFILE_AMBIGUOUS`, and when the covering liability has no available units behind it the commitment is refused with `ERR_LIABILITY_INSUFFICIENT` rather than absorbed. The covering liability's available units are the largest of the three figures the row already accounts for — its frozen snapshot, its writable provisional set and the required stated units of the season's claims for that product — less the units already committed against that same product-and-season bucket; the rung bites only when that figure is zero or less, and the units it reports are never silently clamped.

The requirement vector that drives the allocation ladder carries its own arithmetic that must be held to a strict evaluation order. The tier divisor is selected on the base unit count — the sum of `allocated_units` across benefit allocations in the four counted statuses — and only afterwards is the surcharge computed; the surcharge is the base count divided by that divisor, rounded up to a whole number, where the divisor is one hundred when the base is five thousand units or fewer, two hundred when the base is above five thousand but no more than fifteen thousand, and four hundred when the base is above fifteen thousand. Because a request must settle the tier boundary on the base count alone, a request that would select the divisor on a base figure with the surcharge already folded in is rejected with `ERR_ADDON_TIER_BOUNDARY` (HTTP 409). The surcharge units raise the requirement; they are never netted back out. This vector is read only by the plan-commit route, and it guards nothing there until the request itself declares a `base_units` figure: a commit that supplies no such figure is not tier-checked, and a commit's `base_units` is an optional input that must be zero or greater when it is supplied.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Six write operations on this surface are guarded against lost updates by requiring the client to echo the version it last observed. Each such request must carry the named field as a non-optional member of its body; the field is required, not optional, and its absence is a malformed request.

Committing a benefit allocation plan requires `expected_partner_version`, carrying the planner's last observed `version` of the target `GivingPartner`. If the partner's stored version has moved on — because an intervening approval, cancellation or reversal legitimately changed the capacity picture the commit was computed against — the commit is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Running the derive-build carry-forward on a claim requires `expected_liability_version` for the `DonationLiability`; a settlement or freeze that changed the frozen units invalidates the observation and the call is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION`. Setting a season's frozen state requires `expected_season_version` for the `ImpactSeason`; a forecast freeze, a liability carry-forward or a product-season binding that rewrote the season's aggregates invalidates the observation and the call is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION`. Finalizing an audit requires `expected_audit_version` for the `ImpactAudit`; writing an exception count or recording a preliminary review supersedes the auditor's view and the finalization is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION`. Opening a return request requires `expected_line_version` for the `SalesOrderLine`; a prior credit or exchange on the same line changed the units still eligible under the window and the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION`. Reallocating a cancelled allocation requires `expected_allocation_version` for the `DonationAllocation`; an intervening reversal or capacity recompute supersedes the headroom the reallocation was planned against and the call is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION`.

Two allocation lifecycle operations outside that set also read a version they were not handed in the six rules above, and they are stated here so no reader confuses them with OCC. Approving a pending allocation and cancelling an approved allocation each require `expected_allocation_version`, and a reallocation carries it as well. Endpoints that do not appear in this section and in §3's allocation narrative do not accept a version field at all.

### 2.8. Monetary Precision and Unit Rounding

All monetary arithmetic is performed on integer cents. Wherever a rounding step is described in §3, it means round half up to the nearest whole cent. Three rules deserve emphasis because they are not interchangeable with the pooled alternatives.

First, per-unit costs are rounded once and then multiplied: the per-unit obligation cost of a donation variant is rounded to whole cents before it is ever multiplied by a unit count, and the resulting obligation total is never re-rounded after multiplication. Second, currency ledgers are summed from rounded lines, not rounded after summing — each line's donation cost is rounded to whole cents individually and the rounded values are added, because rounding the pooled total once would produce a different cent figure (three apparel tops at a manufacturing cost of 1445 cents each, uplifted at the standard rate, sum to 7371 cents line-by-line but 7370 cents pooled). The line-by-line ordering is the one this system pins. Third, refunds are rounded once per unit and then multiplied by the number of units returned.

Ratios are rounded to four decimal places, half up; partner attribution percentages are rounded to one decimal place, half up, with the rounding residual folded into the last partner so the reported `attributed_percentage`, and the `attributed_percentage_total` summed over the rolled-up pool, come to exactly 100.0 and never 99.9 or 100.1 — the last partner in descending `claim_attributed_units` with ascending `legal_name` as the tie-break carries the residual share; allocation weights are held to six decimal places, half up; and a partner's mean completion time in days is reported to two decimal places, half up. Scorecard totals and need scores are integers derived from a clamped floating-point total by truncating the fractional part toward zero. Unit counts are always integers.

### 2.9. Nested Resource Retrieval

Every path that carries more than one identifier is resolved strictly left to right, and the first identifier that fails to resolve decides the response. A request that adds a variant line to a donation product therefore checks the product identifier before it ever looks at the line payload, while a request that ships units against an allocation checks only the allocation identifier because that is the only identifier in its path. Each resource type raises its own not-found error: `ERR_PARTNER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRODUCT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DONATION_PRODUCT_LINE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SALES_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SALES_ORDER_LINE_NOT_FOUND` (raised when the line segment does not exist or belongs to a different order), `ERR_SEASON_NOT_FOUND` (raised for a missing season identifier or for a requested season code that names no season), `ERR_LIABILITY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ALLOCATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DISTRIBUTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AUDIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VETTING_ROUND_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RETURN_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_CAPACITY_PROFILE_NOT_FOUND`.

Because existence is checked ahead of input shape, a syntactically bad body addressed to a non-existent identifier reports the not-found failure and not `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The same left-to-right order governs the roll-up reads that address a partner, a season, a product or an allocation before returning their aggregates.

### 2.10. Common Resource Properties

Every primary resource in the system — giving partners, capacity profiles, donation products and their variant lines, donation liabilities, allocations, distributions, impact seasons, marketing claims, sales orders, their lines, return requests and impact audits — carries a standard set of system-managed fields. `id` is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix, so a partner id and a claim id are indistinguishable in form. `created_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording first persistence. `updated_at` likewise records the most recent modification and is refreshed by any change to the resource's properties, including changes written by automatic reconciliation rather than by an explicit client request. `version` is an integer that increments on every mutation of the resource; it is the value that the concurrency fields of §2.7 must match, and derived recomputations on a resource count as mutations.

One entity is deliberately excluded from this convention: `GivingPartnerVetting` carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has neither `updated_at` nor `version`, because a vetting round is an immutable record of a single review proceeding — new evidence is recorded by writing forward within the round's declared outcome path, and a fresh round is opened rather than an old one edited. This also means no vetting round ever participates in an optimistic concurrency check.

### 2.11. Season Clocks, Freezes and Immutability

The system does not read wall-clock time. Every temporal decision is taken against a logical clock whose startup default is exactly 2026-09-01T00:00:00Z and which must not fall back to real time before an explicit clock call. Once set, the clock drives season transitions, obligation snapshots and vesting state changes in one deterministic step, and a set that crosses a season boundary reports the transitions it performed.

Season writes are bounded by the season's own calendar. No write may fall after the resolved season's `freezes_at` instant, and no write may mutate a season whose status is `FINALIZED`; a violation of either is rejected with `ERR_SEASON_FROZEN` (HTTP 409). The freeze-bearing endpoints are the plan commit, the claim derive-build, the season close and the allocation reversal. A second clock-specific failure exists: an attempt to move the logical clock into an already materialized season, or backwards across a frozen boundary, is rejected with `ERR_CLOCK_ROLL_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409).

At most one season may hold `FROZEN` status at any instant, and an attempt to place a second one there is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`. The `requested_season_code` and `is_final` inputs on the frozen-state write let an operator nominate which season is being frozen and whether the freeze is the season's final one; a null `requested_season_code` means the season containing the current clock.

### 2.12. Segregation of Duties in Finalization

Audit finalization is a four-eyes operation. The acting `X-User-ID` may not be the same identifier that performed the preliminary review of that audit; if it is, the finalization is refused with `ERR_AUDIT_SOD_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403) rather than the generic stale or state errors. This constraint is structural rather than advisory: it is what stops the role that assesses a season from also rubber-stamping its own assessment, and it is enforced independently of, and prior to, the substantive audit preconditions described in §3.9. Reopening the audit clears the recorded preliminary reviewer, so a reopened audit may be finalized by any auditor, including the one that reopened it.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Onboarding Partners and Running the Vetting Rounds

A giving partner enters the system as a `CANDIDATE` when a `GivingPartnerCoordinator` onboards it. The onboarding payload declares the organization's `legal_name`, its `partner_type` — one of `HOMELESS_SHELTER`, `DOMESTIC_VIOLENCE_SHELTER`, `YOUTH_OUTREACH`, `VETERAN_OUTREACH`, `DISASTER_RELIEF` or `COMMUNITY_HEALTH_CLINIC` — its `operating_region` — `NORTHEAST`, `SOUTHEAST`, `MIDWEST`, `SOUTHWEST` or `WEST` — its `operating_state_code`, its `registry_id`, its `ein`, its `founded_year`, its `unduplicated_recipients_per_facility` and its declared `need_tier` (`CRITICAL`, `HIGH`, `MODERATE` or `LOW`). The `service_radius_miles` may be supplied and defaults to 50 when it is not, and the `founded_year` must fall between 1900 and 2026 inclusive. Onboarding requires both `operating_region` and `operating_state_code` to be supplied. The `registry_verified` flag is computed rather than accepted: it is true when `registry_id` matches the shape two uppercase letters, a hyphen, `CHR`, another hyphen and six digits, and when `ein` is nine digits.

Onboarding is inseparable from the first review. The same request that admits the partner opens its first vetting round with `round_number` 1 and an `outcome` of `REQUESTED`, sets the partner's `vetting_round` counter to 1, and moves the partner's status from `CANDIDATE` to `VETTING`. A newly admitted partner starts with `vetting_round` and `total_rejections` both at 0, and `total_rejections` is the lifetime count that the two-strike rule reads. A partner's `latest_vetting_round_id` is the identifier of its highest-numbered round and is null before that round exists. A successful round write also advances the round's own outcome, and the round is addressed in every subsequent call by the `vetting_id` carried in its path.

Within a round, the coordinator records evidence and advances the outcome. A round begins at `REQUESTED` and may move to `DOCUMENTS_SUBMITTED` once `registry_check_passed` and `tax_check_passed` are both present; from there to `EVALUATING`; and from `EVALUATING` the round can be sent back to `REQUESTED` when the coordinator records a non-empty `outcome_message` asking for more information. That request-for-information is not free: it increments the partner's `total_rejections` and returns the partner to `CANDIDATE`, because a round sent back for information counts as a rejection under the two-strike rule. Alongside the four verification flags — `registry_check_passed`, `tax_check_passed`, `on_site_check_passed` and `capacity_check_passed` — the coordinator records `need_verification_points` (an integer from 0 to 5), the supporting `need_verification_evidence`, a `valid_until` instant and an `outcome_message`.

Approval is gated on two independent conditions and either failure aborts it. The partner's `registry_verified` must be true and all four verification flags must be true on that round; any false flag or an unverified registry is rejected with `ERR_REGISTRY_NOT_VERIFIED` (HTTP 409). Independently, the computed `need_score` must be at least 40; a score below that threshold is rejected with `ERR_NEED_SCORE_BELOW_MINIMUM` (HTTP 409) even when every verification flag is true. Approving the round moves the partner to `APPROVED`, stamps `last_vetted_at` with the current logical clock, recomputes `need_score`, and creates a `PartnerCapacityProfile` for every season whose status is `PLANNED`, copying the partner's `need_tier` into it; seasons that are already `OPEN` deliberately get no automatic profile so that the coordinator declares the ceiling explicitly. The same approval path is available out of the round's `EVALUATING` outcome, with the identical two conditions, and it is likewise available while the round still sits at `DOCUMENTS_SUBMITTED` or already reads `APPROVED`; any other current outcome is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.

The `need_score` composite deserves its own statement because several downstream rules read it. It is assembled from a base value that depends on the partner's declared need band and a regional adjustment, with the band's base drawn from the partner type (a disaster relief organization scores 95, a domestic violence shelter 83, a homeless shelter 70, a veteran outreach organization 61, a youth outreach organization 52, and a community health clinic 20) and the adjustment drawn from the operating region (the midwest adds 6, the northeast 3, the southwest 2, the southeast 0 and the west subtracts 2). A separate access component is added: the partner's average unduplicated recipients per facility divided by one thousand in truncating integer division, capped at 20. Verification points feed this arithmetic in a way that surprises: when `need_verification_points` is 4 or more, five points are subtracted from the base before the access component is added, because a verified round upgrades the applicable band by one step. When the points are 3 or fewer the declared band's base is used unchanged. The one-band upgrade is applied to the base and never to the clamped result. The final figure is clamped between 0 and 100, and there are two worked readings worth keeping: a homeless shelter in the northeast serving 15,000 recipients with 2 verification points scores 88, while a community health clinic in the southeast serving 15,000 recipients with 0 points scores 35 — below the approval minimum, so its approval is refused even with all four verification flags true. `need_score` is recomputed on the partner profile update (which accepts `legal_name`, `need_tier`, `unduplicated_recipients_per_facility` and an optional `service_radius_miles`), on any vetting-round write that changes the verification points, and on approval itself.

Rejection and return are where the two-strike rule bites. Rejecting a round increments the partner's `total_rejections` and moves the partner to `REJECTED`; the round's outcome becomes `REJECTED` as well. If the increment brings `total_rejections` to 2, the partner moves instead to `REJECTED_FINAL`, which is terminal. A rejected partner can be reopened: reopening requires that `total_rejections` is still below 2 and that the current round's outcome is `REJECTED`; when either fails the attempt is refused with `ERR_PARTNER_REJECTION_FINAL` (HTTP 409) — a partner whose two rejections are spent cannot be brought back. A successful reopen creates a fresh round numbered one above the partner's current `vetting_round` with outcome `DOCUMENTS_SUBMITTED`, increments `vetting_round`, and returns the partner to `VETTING`.

Rounds can also expire. If the logical clock passes a round's `valid_until` while the round is still `REQUESTED`, the system expires it: the round's outcome becomes `EXPIRED`, the partner returns to `CANDIDATE`, and — when the season containing the current clock has reached `FROZEN` or `CLOSED` — a capacity profile is created for the next `PLANNED` season. The expiry is applied by the clock roll, and a round that has advanced past `REQUESTED` but has not reached a terminal outcome is still an in-flight review whose lapse expires it too. Any attempt to record a further outcome against a round that is already terminal produces `ERR_VETTING_ROUND_TERMINAL` (HTTP 409). There is a second, narrower reopening path out of a completed round: when a partner performance audit for the season containing the current clock has been finalized and the partner's `partner_performance_grade` is `MONITORED` or `PROBATION`, the round can be moved back to `DOCUMENTS_SUBMITTED`; that path returns the partner to `VETTING` and recomputes `registry_verified`.

Partner standing itself moves along three audited directions, and each cascades. Moving an `APPROVED` partner to `SUSPENDED` requires that the target value is `SUSPENDED` and that no audit of kind `PARTNER_PERFORMANCE` for that partner is in `IN_PROGRESS`; the same write locks every `ACTIVE` capacity profile the partner holds, cancels every `PENDING_ALLOCATION` allocation attributed to it, and restricts every `SUBSTANTIATED` claim naming it to `SEASON_RESTRICTED`. Moving a `SUSPENDED` partner back to `APPROVED` — reachable only before the annual audit freezes — unlocks the locked profiles and returns the season-restricted claims to `SUBSTANTIATED`. Moving an `APPROVED` or a `SUSPENDED` partner to `INACTIVE` requires the target value to be `INACTIVE` and every allocation attributed to the partner to be in a terminal status; it supersedes all of the partner's capacity profiles. The status write accepts the `status` field and is one of the two operations shared between the `GivingPartnerCoordinator` and the `ImpactAuditor`.

### 3.2. Capacity Ceilings and the Benefit Allocation Pool

A capacity profile is declared per partner and per season with `season_code`, `max_allocatable_units`, `effective_from` and `effective_to`. It opens `ACTIVE` with `committed_units` and `reserved_units` both initialized to zero and `capacity_utilization_units` computed immediately. A profile's `effective_from` instant is when its ceiling becomes readable and its `effective_to` instant when it stops being selectable; when a successor profile for the same partner and season carries a later `effective_from`, the earlier profile becomes `SUPERSEDED`, which is terminal. A profile whose owning partner leaves `APPROVED` — that is, whose partner's status is `SUSPENDED` or `INACTIVE` — is moved to `LOCKED`, and it returns to `ACTIVE` only when the partner is restored to `APPROVED`. The duplicate-key rule of §2.4 forbids two profiles sharing a partner and season code; §2.6's ambiguity rule forbids two selectable profiles from resolving at once. A profile the approval path of §3.1 opens automatically is a placeholder rather than a declared ceiling: it carries no `effective_from` or `effective_to`, and a profile with no readable window is never selectable, so it neither contends with an explicitly declared ceiling nor makes one ambiguous — declaring the explicit ceiling for the same partner and season supersedes the placeholder.

Placing donated goods with partners happens two ways. The first is a direct commitment: a `AllocationPlanner` commits a plan to a single named partner, supplying `giving_partner_id`, `claim_id`, `donation_product_id`, `allocated_units`, `priority_tier`, `liquidity_cap`, an optional `contract_id` and the `expected_partner_version` that guards the write. The allocation is created pending and approved within the request, its `rank_score` is computed, the partner's `committed_units` rises by `allocated_units`, and the partner's `version` advances by one. The `priority_tier` vocabulary is `EMERGENCY`, `CRITICAL`, `HIGH` and `MODERATE`, and the tier identity of a commitment is what decides who outranks whom when the pool is finite. Either route is addressed by the plan identifier in its path, and that identifier is what resolves the season the operation is drawn against: when it names an impact season — by the season's own `id` or by its `season_code` — the season so named is the one the operation acts on, and otherwise the season holding the logical clock is used; a direct commitment that resolves no season at all is refused with `ERR_SEASON_NOT_FOUND`.

The second route is a waterfall that spreads a finite pool across every eligible partner at once. It is driven by `claim_id`, `donation_product_id`, `liquidity_cap`, `baseline_units`, `demand_units`, `pool_units` and an optional `contract_id`, and it produces one allocation per winning partner together with the `partner_count`, the `attributed_units_total`, the applied `liquidity_cap`, the `partner_count` and the number of `rounds_applied`. Each allocation it produces is addressed thereafter through its `allocation_id`, and the plan that produced it through its `plan_id`. Every participating capacity profile has its utilization recomputed as the allocations land. The eligible pool is every partner whose status is `APPROVED` at the instant of computation: `SUSPENDED`, `INACTIVE`, `REJECTED` and `REJECTED_FINAL` partners receive no slot at all and take no part in the remainder sweep. The `liquidity_cap` defaults to 10 and must lie between 1 and 50 inclusive.

The waterfall itself has two stages and both matter. The waterfall considers exactly `liquidity_cap` slots, numbered one through that cap, and the ordered eligible pool is cycled across them: slot number *r* is handed to the partner occupying position ((*r* − 1) modulo the pool size) + 1 of that ordered pool, so a pool smaller than the cap sees its leading partners take the later slots again as the assignment wraps. In the first stage each slot is given a weight by its number: a slot whose number is divisible by three carries the pool weight, a slot whose number is even (but not divisible by three) carries the demand weight, and every other slot carries the baseline weight. The weights are `baseline_units` multiplied by one unit of base weight, `demand_units` multiplied by thirty-five hundredths of a unit of index weight, and `pool_units` multiplied by sixty hundredths of a unit of essential weight respectively, held to six decimal places. The weights over all slots are summed. In the second stage the slot weights are aggregated per partner, and each partner's exact share of the cap is the cap times that partner's aggregate divided by the total weight. That exact share is floored to a whole number and the leftover units — the cap minus the sum of the floors — are distributed one apiece in order of descending fractional remainder, with ties broken by `legal_name` ascending. A partner's final allocation is its floor plus one if it stands among the first of those in the winner order. Slot assignment itself, when two partners contend for a slot, is broken by descending `rank_score`, then descending `need_score`, then descending `claim_attributed_units`, then ascending `created_at` — never by identifier.

Ranking is computed by a five-term weighted score and clamped between 0 and 100. The priority term reads the tier directly (an emergency commitment contributes 40 points, a critical one 30, a high one 20 and a moderate one 10). The fit term is the allocation fit percentage divided by ten in integer division, capped at 30. The need term is the partner's `need_score` divided by ten in integer division, capped at 25. The essential term is the count of units the partner contributes from variant lines flagged `is_essential_good`, capped at 10. The recency term contributes 5 points when the partner's last capacity receipt falls strictly inside a five-day window ending at the present — that is, when the elapsed days are greater than zero and no more than five — and zero otherwise. Two properties of the clamped total must be understood. A priority-led rank means a `CRITICAL` partner outranks every `MODERATE` partner regardless of the other terms, because the priority term alone exceeds the whole moderate ladder's ceiling plus its caps. A capacity-led rank means the clamp destroys the raw ordering, so when two candidates tie on the clamped total the tie-break sequence of descending `rank_score`, descending `need_score`, descending `claim_attributed_units` and ascending `created_at` decides, and the same sequence governs the largest-remainder sweep described above.

Once committed, an allocation lives through an approval, a shipment, and possibly a cancellation, a reallocation or a reversal. Approving a pending allocation requires the `expected_allocation_version`, a capacity profile for the season that is `ACTIVE` or `EXHAUSTED`, headroom that accommodates `allocated_units`, and a `rank_score` already computed for the plan's candidate pool; it raises `committed_units`, recomputes the `rank_score` and the `capacity_utilization_units` on both the profile and the partner, and advances the partner's `version`. Cancelling an approved allocation requires the `expected_allocation_version` and that no distribution against it has shipped any units; a cancel after a shipment is refused with `ERR_ALLOCATION_DISTRIBUTION_EXISTS` (HTTP 409). A successful cancel lowers `committed_units` by `allocated_units`, returns a profile sitting at `EXHAUSTED` to `ACTIVE` when headroom reappears, and advances the partner's `version`. Reallocating a cancelled allocation — guarded by `expected_allocation_version` — re-activates it with a refreshed `rank_score` and a fresh `created_at` provided `allocated_units` fits the emptied profile's headroom, recomputes the ranking across the plan's candidate pool, and advances the partner's `version`; an attempt against an allocation whose cancellation has already been superseded by a reversal is refused with `ERR_ALLOCATION_VERSION_SUPERSEDED` (HTTP 409), and an attempt whose reallocation would breach the ceiling reports `ERR_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.

A plan can also be reverted. Reverting the plan's donor lines out of a vesting season — carrying `expected_season_version` and a `reason` — moves the affected variant lines from `VESTED` back to `ACTIVE`, decrements the liability's `obligation_units` by those lines' obligations, and re-selects and canonicalizes the successor season's donor lines under the frozen season. The revert returns the plan identifier, the count of `donor_lines_reverted`, the `season_code` and the resulting `carried_out_units`. A line whose obligation has already vested into a season that is no longer open cannot be reverted; the attempt raises `ERR_OBLIGATION_ALREADY_VESTED` (HTTP 409).

### 3.3. Shipping Donation Goods, Partner Receipt and Distribution Reconciliation

A committed allocation becomes real goods on a truck. A `FulfillmentOperator` ships units against an approved allocation by declaring `shipped_units`, a `shipment_reference` and a `carrier_code`. The shipment must not exceed the allocation's `allocated_units` — an excess raises `ERR_SHIPMENT_EXCEEDS_ALLOCATION` (HTTP 409) — and must be at least one unit. A successful shipment creates a distribution in status `SHIPPED` with `shipped_at` stamped, moves the allocation to `SHIPPED`, and recomputes the season's `total_shipped_units`.

Receipt is confirmed by the partner side. A `GivingPartnerCoordinator` confirms `received_units` against a `receipt_reference`, and the confirmation accumulates rather than replacing: `received_units` is the running sum of confirmed receipts, and the distribution is addressed through its `distribution_id` on every subsequent step of its life. While the cumulative confirmed figure is below `shipped_units` and at least one unit has arrived, the distribution sits at `PARTIALLY_RECEIVED` and the allocation follows it there. Once the cumulative figure reaches `shipped_units`, the distribution becomes `RECEIVED`, `impact_units_confirmed` is set equal to `received_units`, and the allocation moves to `RECEIVED`. A confidence that would push the cumulative confirmed figure above `shipped_units` is refused with `ERR_RECEIPT_EXCEEDS_SHIPMENT` (HTTP 409), and a receipt confirmed against a distribution that has already been completed, written off or voided is refused with `ERR_DISTRIBUTION_NOT_IN_TRANSIT` (HTTP 409). Each confirmation recomputes the profile's `capacity_utilization_units`, and — after the season finalizes — the partner's `impact_units_total`.

Receipts taken inside a season that is still `PLANNED` are provisional by design. A distribution that reaches `RECEIVED` inside a `PLANNED` season is demoted back to `SHIPPED` when the season transitions onward, with its `received_units` and `impact_units_confirmed` reset to zero and its `received_at` instant cleared, so no receipt taken before the season opened survives; the season's `total_received_units` is recomputed at that point. This is why a receipt confirmed before a season opens cannot be relied upon as settled impact.

Short receipts and their aftermath follow a distinct path. A `FulfillmentOperator` raises a discrepancy against a distribution whose `received_units` is below `shipped_units`, supplying an `exception_reason`; the distribution moves to `DISCREPANCY_CLAIMED` and `discrepancy_units` is computed as `shipped_units` minus `received_units`. From there, either the remainder is confirmed and the distribution reaches `COMPLETED`, or the shortfall is written off. A write-off requires a non-empty `exception_reason` and moves the distribution to `WRITTEN_OFF`; the season's `total_shipped_units` is deliberately left unchanged by a write-off, and the allocation stays `RECEIVED`. A write-off attempted against a distribution that is not in `DISCREPANCY_CLAIMED` is refused with `ERR_DISCREPANCY_NOT_RAISED` (HTTP 409).

A fully received distribution is closed by the operator, which requires that `received_units` equals `shipped_units` and that a receipt confirmation timestamp is set; closing it moves the distribution to `COMPLETED`, recomputes the partner's `avg_distribution_completion_days`, and lands the allocation at `RECEIVED`. That mean completion time is the average, over the partner's `RECEIVED` and `COMPLETED` distributions, of the whole days between `shipped_at` and `received_at`, reported to two decimal places; it is null when the partner has none.

An unshipped distribution can be voided. A void requires that `received_units` is zero; it moves the distribution to `VOIDED`, recomputes the season's `total_shipped_units` while excluding voided rows, and returns the allocation to `APPROVED`, which frees the goods to be shipped again.

Reversal is the mechanism by which received goods are made up in a later season, and it is tightly fenced. A `AllocationPlanner` reverses a received allocation by supplying `allocated_units`, `claim_id`, `priority_tier` and a `reason`; the reversal is legal only when the allocation is `RECEIVED` — any other source status raises `ERR_REVERSAL_REQUIRES_RECEIVED` (HTTP 409) — and only when either `impact_units_confirmed` is zero or the season's reconciliation delta is negative, and when `allocated_units` fits the partner's remaining headroom. A reversal that fails those substantive conditions raises `ERR_ALLOCATION_NOT_REVERSIBLE` (HTTP 409), and the allocation settles into `REVERSED` on success. A successful reversal creates a child allocation whose `allocation_kind` is `REVERSAL` carrying the same `allocated_units`, raises the profile's `reserved_units` by that amount, and creates the make-up donation liability for the next impact season with `carried_in_units` equal to those units. The `allocation_kind` distinction matters throughout: an `ALLOCATION` is a benefit that debits capacity, while a `REVERSAL` is a make-up that credits capacity and forecasts the next season.

### 3.4. Designing Donation Goods and Their Variants

A donation product is created by a `ProvisioningPartner` with a `product_name`, a `product_kind` — `ATHLETIC_SOCK`, `APPAREL_TOP`, `UNDERGARMENT` or `SLEEP_ACCESSORY` — a `design_channel`, an `obligation_model`, `approved_donation_ratio_units`, `retail_price_cents`, `unit_cost_cents` and an `is_reusable` flag. The `design_channel` is the central design decision: `DONATION_ONLY` describes a good designed for donation that is never listed at retail, while `DUAL_CHANNEL` describes a good sold at retail and donated one-for-one. The `obligation_model` decides when the obligation attaches: `AT_SALE` attaches it when a sales line is activated, `AT_VESTING` when the donation variant is created. `approved_donation_ratio_units` is the number of donation obligation units behind each sold unit and must be at least one.

Donation suitability is scored, not asserted. A product's `design_weight_score` is assembled from a fabric base determined by `product_kind` (an undergarment contributes 45, an athletic sock 40, an apparel top 30 and a sleep accessory 20), plus 20 points when `is_reusable` is true, plus 10 points when `design_channel` is `DONATION_ONLY` and nothing when it is `DUAL_CHANNEL`; the total is clamped between 0 and 100. The score considers only the product's own declared attributes, so it is stable across regeneration. Activation is gated at a score of 60: a `DRAFT` product whose `design_weight_score` is at least 60 may be activated and every draft variant under it moves to `ACTIVE` with its obligation units computed, while a product below the threshold is refused with `ERR_DESIGN_WEIGHT_BELOW_MINIMUM` (HTTP 409). The gate is exactly at 60 — an apparel top that is reusable and donation-only scores 60 and is activatable, while the same top as a dual-channel product scores 50 and is refused. Activatable examples for orientation: a reusable undergarment in the donation-only channel scores 75, a reusable athletic sock in that channel 70, and a non-reusable dual-channel apparel top 30.

A product moves through `DRAFT`, `ACTIVE`, `RETIRED` and `ARCHIVED`. Active products can be retired by a product update carrying `status` `RETIRED`, which retires every active variant whose `donation_eligible` is false while leaving active variants that do accrue an obligation in place until their season closes. A retired product can be brought back for one more season by a product update carrying `status` `ACTIVE`, provided every active variant has a `design_variant_code` other than `none`; the reinstatement returns all retired variants to `ACTIVE`. Archival happens on its own when the last sales line referencing the product belongs to an order whose season reaches `FINALIZED`, and requires that no line in status `OPEN` or `RETURN_REQUESTED` references the product and that every season referencing it is `FINALIZED`; archival archives every variant under it. A product's `active_season_code` is the code of the season that currently contains the logical clock and holds `FROZEN` status, and is null when no season does. Two rolled-up figures are reported on the product rather than on any one season: `obligation_units_total` sums the obligation units across all of the product's liability rows, and `impact_units_retail_equivalent` sums the retail-equivalent units sold across the variant lines attributed to the product. A product also reports its `design_weight_score`, its `status` and its `obligation_model`, and each variant line reports its `unit_size_label`, its `status`, its `design_variant_code` and whether it is `is_essential_good`.

Variants carry the catalog detail and the obligation identity. A variant line is created under its product with a `gtin`, a `unit_size_label`, an `is_essential_good` flag and a `donation_eligible` flag, and its `design_variant_code` is derived rather than declared: a line that does not accrue an obligation carries the code `none`, a line whose product is `DONATION_ONLY` carries `donation_specific`, and a line whose product is `DUAL_CHANNEL` carries `retail_dual`. Its `obligation_units` is the product's `approved_donation_ratio_units` when `donation_eligible` is true and zero otherwise, whatever the ratio declares. The code decides which channel's obligation attaches when the product settles and therefore which per-unit cost the line reports, and the two channels behave differently downstream: a `donation_specific` line can only ever be allocated and never sold, so it reaches `COMMITTED` and can never reach `VESTED`, while a `retail_dual` line reaches `VESTED` on its first settled sale. The variant line update accepts an optional `unit_size_label`, `is_essential_good` and `donation_eligible`, and recomputes the obligation units and per-unit cost.

The per-unit obligation cost is assembled through four named steps, each rounded once. First the donation uplift is computed: the manufacturing cost multiplied by seventy hundredths, rounded half up to whole cents. Second the retail offset is computed: the retail price multiplied by five hundredths, rounded half up to whole cents. Third the net upcharge is decided by channel — for a `DUAL_CHANNEL` product it is the uplift minus the offset, floored at zero, while for a `DONATION_ONLY` product it is the uplift alone. Fourth, the per-unit obligation cost — reported on the line as `unit_obligation_cost_cents` — is the smaller of the manufacturing cost and that net upcharge. A dual-channel product is thus never charged more than its own manufacturing cost, and a donation-only product takes no retail offset at all: at a manufacturing cost of 1150 cents and a retail price of 1600 cents, the donation-only variant costs 805 cents and the dual-channel variant 725 cents, because the 80-cent retail offset is subtracted first. Rounding happens once at each named step and the smaller-of comparison is applied last, over terms that have already been rounded.

A second, wider per-unit liability figure governs lines whose supply is mixed, and it is where a dual-channel line is charged twice. Its terms are evaluated once each before any clamp. The donation build cost is the unit's declared value multiplied by seventy hundredths, rounded half up to whole cents. The provenance discount is that build cost multiplied by fifty hundredths, rounded half up to whole cents. The per-unit liability is then the sum of the line's per-unit obligation cost and the excess of the build cost over the provenance discount, with that excess floored at zero; that sum is clamped up to a floor of zero cents and down to a ceiling of one hundred thousand cents, with the smaller-of and larger-of comparisons applied after every term has been rounded. When the build cost does not exceed the provenance discount — the degenerate case in which the discount equals or exceeds the whole build — the per-unit liability collapses to the line's per-unit obligation cost instead. A dual-channel line therefore carries the sum of its obligation cost and the provenance gap rather than the obligation cost alone, and the two channels produce different per-unit figures for the same declared value; at a declared value of 1155 cents the build cost is 1964 cents, the provenance discount 982 cents, and the per-unit liability, against a per-unit obligation cost of 1155 cents, is 2137 cents.

Variant status moves through `DRAFT`, `ACTIVE`, `COMMITTED`, `VESTED`, `RETIRED`, `SUNSET` and `ARCHIVED`. A draft variant becomes `ACTIVE` when its owning product is activated, and that activation recomputes its `obligation_units` from the product's approved ratio when it is donation-eligible. From `ACTIVE` there are three forward directions and their preconditions are channel- and model-specific. A variant moves to `COMMITTED` when a plan is committed and the variant's product is `DONATION_ONLY`; the commitment adds the variant's obligation units to the current season's donation liability. A variant moves to `VESTED` when a sales line for it settles, provided the product's `obligation_model` is `AT_VESTING` and its `design_channel` is `DUAL_CHANNEL`; the vesting adds its obligation units to the current season's liability and nothing else. A variant moves to `SUNSET` — and is thereby excluded from the obligations of every later season — when the season containing the current clock reaches `FROZEN`, provided its product is `DONATION_ONLY` and its model is `AT_VESTING`. A `COMMITTED` variant can still reach `VESTED` on the settlement of its first sales line if its product is `DONATION_ONLY` and a liability row for the current season exists, which adds its obligation units to that liability. A variant whose `donation_eligible` is false moves from `ACTIVE` to `RETIRED` on a product update, and a retired variant is archived along with its product when the product is archived.

### 3.5. Retail Sales and the Accrual of Donation Liability

The commercial plane begins when a `CustomerService` operator creates a sales order with an `order_number` and a `channel` of `RETAIL_POS` or `RETAIL_ECOM`. The order opens at `CREATED` with a `placed_at` stamped from the logical clock and an independent `fulfillment_status` of `PLACED`. Its `order_number` is unique across all orders. The `fulfillment_status` dimension is genuinely independent of the order's own status: it moves through `PLACED`, `PICKED`, `SHIPPED`, `DELIVERED` and `CANCELLED`, and it is deliberately not a precondition for the order's donation status, so an order can be donation-satisfied while still merely placed, or delivered while still merely paid. An order reports `ordered_units` as the summed quantity over its lines, and a line's `quantity` must be at least one.

Lines are added to an order with a `donation_product_line_id`, a `quantity`, a `unit_price_cents`, an optional `line_discount_cents` defaulting to zero, and an optional `gtin`. The `quantity` must be at least one, and when a `gtin` is supplied it must agree with the referenced variant line's `gtin` or the request is rejected with `ERR_ENTITY_UNRESOLVED` (HTTP 409), because the two records disagree about the identity of the good. Adding a line accrues the donation obligation immediately and unconditionally: the line's `obligated_units` materializes from the variant line's obligation rules, the season liability's `provisional_liability_units` is recomputed, the season's `claim_frozen_units_total` is recomputed, and the liability's `version` advances. A line added to a variant line whose `donation_eligible` is false accrues nothing and is rejected with `ERR_LINE_NOT_DONATION_ELIGIBLE` (HTTP 409); the line identifies the purchased variant through `donation_product_line_id` and the variant's product through `donation_product_id`. A line is addressed after the fact through its `sales_order_line_id` and the order that owns it through its `sales_order_id`. A line may never be added, settled or paid on an order that has been cancelled, which raises `ERR_SALES_ORDER_CANCELLED` (HTTP 409).

Payment settles the order and starts the clock on returns. Paying an order requires every line to be in status `OPEN`, moves the order to `PAID`, stamps `paid_at`, advances `fulfillment_status` to `PLACED`, materializes each line's `obligated_units` from its variant line's rules, and recomputes `provisional_liability_units` for every line. An order that is already `PAID` or `DONATION_SATISFIED` cannot be paid again, and the attempt raises `ERR_SALES_ORDER_ALREADY_PAID` (HTTP 409). The `paid_at` instant is load-bearing beyond the order: the line's return window is measured from it.

An order reaches `DONATION_SATISFIED` automatically once every one of its lines has reached a terminal donation state — that is, every line is `EXEMPTED`, `RETURNED_CREDITED` or `EXCHANGED_REPLACED`. That transition recomputes the liability's `final_liability_units` and the season's `total_shipped_units`. Cancellation is a separate path with two distinct shapes. An order still in `CREATED` — no payment captured — may be cancelled with an optional `reason`, moving it to `CANCELLED` and its `fulfillment_status` to `CANCELLED`, and no liability row is created for that order at all. An order in `PAID` whose `fulfillment_status` is still `PLACED` may also be cancelled, which returns it to `CREATED` for correction of the verified settlement time and reduces the liability's `provisional_liability_units` by the order's line obligations. The fulfillment dimension is advanced separately by a `FulfillmentOperator` supplying a `fulfillment_status`; the target must be strictly later than the stored one, and a regression is refused with `ERR_FULFILLMENT_REGRESSION` (HTTP 409). Advancing it recomputes `fulfilled_units`. The two fulfillment targets that take an order out of `PLACED` are `PICKED`, `SHIPPED` and `DELIVERED`, and an order already `DONATION_SATISFIED` may still be advanced to `DELIVERED`.

Two ledgers of donation obligation run in parallel, and understanding their membership is the heart of this domain. The provisional ledger counts units of obligation across all lines whose owning order is in `CREATED` or any later non-cancelled status — `CREATED`, `PAID`, `DONATION_SATISFIED`, `PARTIALLY_REFUNDED` or `REFUNDED` — but only for lines whose variant is donation-eligible and whose product's obligation model is `AT_SALE`. Each such line contributes its `quantity` multiplied by its variant's `obligation_units`. Both ledgers are bucketed per product and per season: the liability row names its product through `donation_product_id` and its season through `impact_season_id`, and it is addressed through its `liability_id`. A line that is not donation-eligible contributes nothing, and lines of retired or archived products still contribute while their order remains non-cancelled. The provisional ledger is the one that counts orders that have merely been opened, which is why a refunded order keeps contributing to it: a refund does not undo the promise. When a liability is unfrozen and recomputed, the same line set is re-evaluated at the corrected settlement instant, so identical order statuses yield identical units while an order cancelled in the interim drops out of the delta.

The final ledger is narrower and counts only settled, completed sales. It sums the same line-by-line obligations, but only across lines whose order status is `PAID` or `DONATION_SATISFIED`. Orders still in `CREATED`, cancelled orders, and orders that are `PARTIALLY_REFUNDED` or `REFUNDED` are excluded — a refunded order's donation obligation survives on the partner side, but the order no longer counts as a completed sale for the accrued-liability ledger. Consequently an order that is paid and later partially refunded drops the refunded units from this ledger immediately, while an order that is paid retains every unit, including units whose return request was credited. The two ledgers therefore diverge on identical data, and that divergence is exactly what the season's freeze snapshot records.

A separate currency ledger tracks the money behind the obligation, and it too is summed from rounded lines. Its line set is every sales line whose order status is `PAID`, `DONATION_SATISFIED`, `PARTIALLY_REFUNDED` or `REFUNDED` — note that this set includes the refunded statuses that the unit ledger excludes, and excludes the `CREATED` orders that the provisional unit ledger includes. Each line's donation cost is its quantity multiplied by the product's manufacturing cost multiplied by one plus seventy hundredths, rounded half up to whole cents, and the ledger's `donation_cost_cents` is the sum of those rounded line costs. The ledger also reports `obligation_units` as the summed variant obligation units over the same lines, and a `funding_shortfall_cents` which is the amount by which the obligation cost exceeds the donation cost, floored at zero. Rounding is per line and the rounded values are then summed; pooling the lines and rounding once is explicitly not the behavior, because it produces different cents.

Settling a line's obligation out of the warehouse is the operator's act. Supplying the `shipped_units` for a line settles it, recomputes the season's `final_liability_units`, and recomputes `total_shipped_units`. Settlement is a one-shot act: a line eligible to settle — one in `OPEN`, `RETURN_REQUESTED` or `RETURN_APPROVED` — moves to `SETTLED` on success, so a second settle against the same line trips the state rung with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` rather than counting the final ledger twice. A line settling against an `AT_SALE` product adds its quantity multiplied by its obligation units to the final ledger. A line settling when the liability is already in the carried state adds to the final ledger in exactly the same way and leaves `carried_out_units` untouched until the next freeze; this settlement path accepts lines whose product model is either `AT_SALE` or `AT_VESTING`.

### 3.6. Returns and Exchanges That Never Reverse a Donation

Returns are a commercial correction with an explicit firewall around the donation ledger. Opening a return against a line is done either nested under the order or directly against a line identifier, and either form accepts a `request_reason`, a `requested_units` of at least one, an optional `is_exchange` defaulting to false, an optional `replacement_product_id` and the `expected_line_version` that guards the write. The `request_reason` vocabulary is `SIZE`, `DEFECT`, `CHANGE_OF_MIND` or `GOODWILL`. A successful open creates a return request in status `REQUESTED` with `liability_delta_units` of zero, moves the line to `RETURN_REQUESTED`, and advances the line's `version`. The owning order is dragged along by the same credit: an order sitting at `PAID` whose requested units are fewer than its ordered units moves to `PARTIALLY_REFUNDED`, an order at `DONATION_SATISFIED` does the same whenever at least one of its lines remains uncredited, and an order at `PARTIALLY_REFUNDED` whose every line has reached `RETURNED_CREDITED` with its returned units equal to its ordered units moves to `REFUNDED`. None of those three order movements reduces the final liability ledger, and the movement to `REFUNDED` leaves it unchanged outright. The request's `window_expires_at` is the owning order's `paid_at` plus the line's `return_window_days`, which defaults to 30 days.

Window rules are the first gate, and they have two exceptions. A return opened inside the window is accepted. A return opened after the window expires is normally refused with `ERR_RETURN_WINDOW_CLOSED` (HTTP 422), but is accepted in exactly two situations: when the season containing the order has the window-reset flag enabled, or when the request is a defect claim. Approving a request follows the same shape — inside the window an approval succeeds, and outside the window only a `DEFECT` claim is approvable, because a defect is the organization's problem rather than the customer's. Approving moves the request to `APPROVED` and the line to `RETURN_APPROVED`; rejecting it moves the request to `REJECTED` and returns the line to `OPEN`, and a rejection is available when the reason is `CHANGE_OF_MIND` with the window still open.

Credit is where the two terminal outcomes split. For a refund — `is_exchange` false — crediting moves the request to `CREDITED` with `terminal_outcome` `REFUNDED`, computes the refund, and recomputes the order's `refunded_cents` and `returned_units`. For an exchange — `is_exchange` true — crediting moves the request to `EXCHANGED` with `terminal_outcome` `REPLACED`, and a replacement obligation accrues for the replacement product while the original line's obligation is retained. An exchange requires that `replacement_product_id` names an `ACTIVE` donation product; otherwise it is refused with `ERR_EXCHANGE_REPLACEMENT_INVALID` (HTTP 409). The `replacement_is_donation_good` flag records whether the replacement good itself accrues an obligation.

The refund figure is assembled in three steps. The effective unit ratio is the line's total after discount divided by its total before discount — that is, the unit price multiplied by the quantity, less the line discount, over the unit price multiplied by the quantity. The effective unit refund is the unit price multiplied by that ratio, rounded half up once to a whole cent. The refund amount is that rounded unit refund multiplied by the number of units returned. A discount is therefore spread across all of a line's units rather than carved out of the refunded units alone: a line of two units at 1800 cents apiece with a 500-cent discount refunds 1300 cents for a one-unit return and 2600 cents for a two-unit return, where an undiscounted line at the same price refunds 1800 cents per unit. An exchange refunds nothing — the refund amount is zero — and the same zero figure applies to both variants for the liability delta. Crediting is also where the owning order is finally dragged: crediting a refund moves an order still at `PAID` to `PARTIALLY_REFUNDED` and moves one already at `PARTIALLY_REFUNDED` on to `REFUNDED` once every one of its lines reads `RETURNED_CREDITED` and the cumulative returned units reach the ordered units; an exchange, whose line reaches `EXCHANGED_REPLACED` rather than `RETURNED_CREDITED`, moves the order only as far as `PARTIALLY_REFUNDED`. The computed `refund_amount_cents` is exposed on the return request as its refund figure, and the request is addressed thereafter through its `return_id`.

The firewall itself is absolute. Crediting a return never reduces the final liability ledger and never decreases a line's `obligated_units`; the request's `liability_delta_units` is zero on every path, whether the customer took a refund or a replacement, and the liability row behind the original sale — recorded on the request as the `donation_liability_id` of the sale — retains its units throughout. The only thing a credited return does to the unit ledgers is remove the refunded units from the final ledger by moving the order out of its counted statuses, and even that is a consequence of the order's status change rather than a reversal of the promise. Return volume is nonetheless tracked: the units requested on credited and exchanged requests attributed to a partner are reported as that partner's `entrance_returns_units`, and the partner's return-exposure roll-up reports them as `entrance_returns_units` beside the `returned_units_total` and the `liability_units_retained` figure, keyed by the partner's `partner_id` and the season's `season_code`.

Two return-related transitions loop back. A line that is `RETURNED_CREDITED` can be returned again only when the season containing the original order is still `PLANNED`, in which case a fresh request is opened for at most the line's quantity; outside a planned season the attempt is refused with `ERR_LINE_ALREADY_RETURNED` (HTTP 409). A line that is `EXCHANGED_REPLACED` can be returned again while the replacement order's return window has not yet elapsed, which opens a new request for the replacement unit. Finally, an `OPEN` line can be exempted from the return window entirely by supplying a `request_reason` of `GOODWILL` or `DEFECT`; the exemption moves the line to `EXEMPTED`, closes its return window, and retains its `obligated_units`. A reason other than those two is refused with `ERR_EXEMPTION_REASON_INVALID` (HTTP 409). `EXEMPTED` is the only terminal line state in which the donation obligation is fully discharged by neither sale nor return.

### 3.7. Seasons, Freezes, Carry-Forward and the Logical Clock

An impact season is the reporting container that everything else hangs from. An `ImpactAuditor` opens one with a `season_code`, a `starts_at`, an `ends_at` and a `freezes_at`, and it begins `PLANNED`. Selections may be made against the season's aggregates: `liability_carry_in_units` sums the carried-in units of its liabilities, `claim_frozen_units_total` sums the frozen units of its liabilities, `opening_carry_obligation_cents` sums the accrued obligation cents of its carried liabilities, `attributed_units_total` sums the attributed units of the partners who are `APPROVED` at the instant of computation, `total_shipped_units` and `total_received_units` sum the shipped and received units of its distributions, and `liability_reconciliation_delta` is the frozen claim units minus the shipped units, negative when claims understate what actually shipped.

The season moves along `PLANNED`, `OPEN`, `FROZEN`, `CLOSED` and `FINALIZED`. It opens automatically once the logical clock passes `starts_at`; opening demotes any distributions in the season that had reached `RECEIVED` while the season was planned back to `SHIPPED`, and leaves accumulating liabilities accumulating. It freezes once the clock passes `freezes_at`, provided every claim for the season is in one of `DRAFT`, `SUBSTANTIATED`, `OVERSTATED`, `UNSUBSTANTIATED` or `SEASON_RESTRICTED`; freezing moves every liability row to `FROZEN` with its frozen units taken from its provisional units, recomputes the season's frozen claim total, and re-evaluates the active season code of every active product pointing at the season. Closing a season requires that every one of its liabilities is already `CARRIED` or `CLOSED`; closing moves them to `CLOSED` and the season to `CLOSED`. A closed season can be reopened to `OPEN`, returning its liabilities to `ACCUMULATING`, but only before any audit of the season has been finalized. The season's frozen state can also be set explicitly, and that write takes `is_final`, `expected_season_version` and an optional `requested_season_code`: when `is_final` is true the season becomes `FROZEN` and its liabilities freeze, and when it is false the season returns to `OPEN` with its liabilities returning to accumulation, and the season's `version` advances by one in either case.

The liability row is the unit of obligation per product and season, and its lifecycle is `ACCUMULATING`, `FROZEN`, `CARRIED`, `AUDIT_LOCKED` and `CLOSED`. Its reported figures are the ones the rest of the system reads: `donation_liability_units` is the reporting obligation for the season and equals the final units once frozen; `final_liability_units` counts settled sales; `provisional_liability_units` counts the writable set including non-terminal orders; `obligation_units` holds the units attached at vesting; `accrued_obligation_cents` holds the obligation units multiplied by the per-unit obligation cost, as frozen at the season's freezing; `donation_cost_cents` and `donation_credit_units` come from the per-line ledger recomputed on every settle and payment; `frozen_units` is the snapshot of provisional units taken at the season's `freezes_at` and re-taken at each audit finalization; `required_stated_units` sums the claim obligations for the product and season; `carried_in_units` are make-up units inherited from the season named by `carry_from_season_code`; and `carried_out_units` are the make-up units passed forward when the season closes. The `freeze_recorded_at` instant records when the frozen units were last written.

Freezing is where the two ledgers meet. When the clock passes `freezes_at` and the season has not yet ended, the liability freezes: `frozen_units` becomes the provisional units, `donation_liability_units` becomes the final units, `freeze_recorded_at` is stamped, the accrued obligation cents are frozen at the obligation units multiplied by the per-unit cost, and the season moves to `FROZEN` with its opening carry recomputed. An auditor can unfreeze a frozen liability, which requires that the season is `FROZEN` and that no audit for the season has been finalized; when a finalized audit stands against the season the attempt is refused with `ERR_AUDIT_LOCK_NOT_RESETTABLE` (HTTP 409), and otherwise a season not in `FROZEN` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Unfreezing recomputes the frozen units from the provisional units, returns every one of the season's liabilities to `ACCUMULATING`, and returns the season to `OPEN`; the audit-lock reset flag governs the audit reopen of §3.9 and is not consulted here.

Carry-forward is the make-up mechanism, and it exists because a published claim can promise more than a season actually froze. It requires that `required_stated_units` is strictly greater than `frozen_units` and, whenever the row itself records a `carry_from_season_code` naming the season its carried-in units were inherited from, that the season so named is `CLOSED`; when an applicable clause fails the attempt is refused with `ERR_CARRY_PRECONDITION_UNMET` (HTTP 409). A first carry out of a just-frozen season that records no predecessor is not made to depend on a season that has not yet closed. A successful carry-forward sets `carried_out_units` to the excess of the required stated units over the frozen units, hands the successor liability for the next season that many `carried_in_units`, places that successor in `ACCUMULATING` so the inherited units are folded into its own freeze, and recomputes the season's `liability_carry_in_units`. Once the carried-out units have been written to the successor, the season's own liability can close when its season transitions to `CLOSED`, which recomputes the successor season's carried-in figure.

Audit finalization writes into the liability plane as well. When an audit finalizes against a frozen season, the season's liabilities move to `AUDIT_LOCKED` with their frozen units re-snapshotted from the final units, the season's reconciliation delta is recomputed, and every received allocation for the season has its partner's impact total recomputed. When an audit finalizes against a season that is still `PLANNED`, the season moves straight to `FINALIZED` and its liabilities move to `AUDIT_LOCKED` and then to `CLOSED` where carried-out units were materialized, and the partner performance grades are written from the audit's scorecard. `AUDIT_LOCKED` is not terminal: an audit reopen returns the liability from `AUDIT_LOCKED` to `FROZEN` and lifts the season back to `FROZEN`, provided the audit-lock reset flag is set on the acting auditor's season.

The logical clock and its season rolling are the test-driving controls that make all of this deterministic. Setting the clock takes a `to_iso8601` instant and returns the current instant, the rolling season code and the list of rollover events it produced; it rolls season transitions, obligation freezes and vesting state changes in one step. A parallel season-rolling capability takes the same `to_iso8601` body and returns the instant rolled to, the count of seasons transitioned, the count of ledger rows rewritten and the rollover events, and exists so that a roll may be driven without asserting on the clock itself. Both are permitted to `ImpactAuditor`, `ProvisioningPartner` and `GivingPartnerCoordinator`, and both are exempt from the authentication rung of §2.4. A clock move that would re-enter an already materialized season or step backwards across a frozen boundary is refused with `ERR_CLOCK_ROLL_CONFLICT`. Every temporal check a roll performs — the season it opens or freezes, the receipt demotion that rides the opening, and the round expiry that a lapse triggers — is evaluated at the instant being rolled to, not at the instant the clock previously held.

### 3.8. Marketing Claims and Claim Substantiation

A marketing claim is the public promise itself, and a `MarketingManager` registers it for a product and a partner with a `donation_product_id`, a `partner_id`, `stated_donation_units`, `stated_ratio_text`, a `winback_multiplier` and a `stated_reference_period`. The `winback_multiplier` defaults to 1 and describes a plain one-for-one promise; a value greater than 1 describes a "buy one, we give several" promise. The `stated_reference_period` is one of `CALENDAR_YEAR`, `ROLLING_TWELVE_MONTHS` or `SEASON`, and a claim whose reference period is null or names a window the season does not actually cover cannot be substantiated — the attempt is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_PERIOD_UNSUPPORTED` (HTTP 409). The claim opens at `DRAFT` with its substantiated units computed only when it is first read back.

Substantiation is arithmetic on available units. The required units behind the promise are the stated units multiplied by the winback multiplier. The substantiated units count three sources: shipped units attributed to the claim's partner, capacity-committed units attributed to the claim's partner, and the liability's carried-in units. Shipped units are those of the partner's distributions whose status is not `VOIDED`; capacity-committed units are the partner's allocations in the four counted statuses — `APPROVED`, `SHIPPED`, `PARTIALLY_RECEIVED` or `RECEIVED` — counted only up to a share cap of twelve thousand units; and the carried-in units come from the liability. The figure is reported on the claim as `substantiated_donation_units` and the quotient as its `substantiated_ratio_value`, the latter being the substantiated units divided by the required units, rounded half up to four decimal places. A ratio of at least 1.0000 substantiates the claim; a ratio below 1.0000 overstates it.

Two branches hang off substantiation. A plain one-for-one claim substantiates only when its ratio reaches the floor; when it does not, the claim moves to `UNSUBSTANTIATED` and cannot be published, and its substantiated units stay below its stated units. A claim whose reference-period window contains no shipped or capacity-committed units at all to draw on is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_WINDOW_UNSUBSTANTIATED` (HTTP 409). A win-back claim follows a different arithmetic and can move from `OVERSTATED` back to `UNSUBSTANTIATED` when its multiplier is at least 2 and the win-back arithmetic is fully substantiated by the liability set. Substantiation recomputes the substantiated units, the substantiated ratio and the liability's required stated units. The difference between the shipped-only and shipped-plus-capacity readings is not cosmetic: with 9,000 units shipped and 3,000 committed against a stated 12,000, a shipped-only reading gives a ratio of 0.7500 and an overstatement, while counting the committed units gives 1.0000 and a substantiated claim. Worked readings for orientation: stated 12,500 with a multiplier of 1 and 10,000 shipped plus 2,500 committed gives 1.0000 and substantiates; stated 950 with a multiplier of 2 and 2,200 shipped gives 1.1579; stated 100 with a multiplier of 2 and 120 shipped gives 0.6000 and overstates; stated 12,000 with a multiplier of 1 and 10,000 shipped plus 1,000 committed gives 0.9167 and overstates, and a carry-forward of 1,000 units then brings it to 1.0000.

A claim's own required stated units feed the liability, and this is the coupling that makes claims and allocations part of one graph: the liability's `required_stated_units` are the sum, over the season's claims, of `stated_donation_units` multiplied by `winback_multiplier`, and claims in status `REVOKED` are excluded from that sum.

The claim states run `DRAFT`, `SUBSTANTIATED`, `OVERSTATED`, `UNSUBSTANTIATED`, `SEASON_RESTRICTED`, `REVOKED` and `EXPIRED`. Running the derive-build carry-forward — guarded by `expected_liability_version` — recomputes the liability's required stated units, re-derives the season's reporting claim at the point of the freeze or at the first final read, and moves a substantiated claim to `OVERSTATED` when the ratio falls below the floor after the carry-forward; that move can in turn push the liability to `CARRIED` when its required stated units exceed its frozen units, and it advances the liability's version. Derive-build also has a self-loop: when the season containing the claim is already `FROZEN`, the post-carry-forward ratio is still at least 1.0000, and at least one variant line for the claim's product is `COMMITTED` or `VESTED`, the call re-runs on a substantiated claim and adds the committed units resolved after the freeze to the substantiated total. That recompute is idempotent and does not double-count, so a second run is a no-op.

Suspension and reinstatement of a partner reach into claims. A claim naming a partner that leaves `APPROVED` moves to `SEASON_RESTRICTED`, which confines its substantiation to the review window and forbids it from drawing on either the standing reserve or season carry-over; when the partner returns to `APPROVED` the claim returns to `SUBSTANTIATED` and its substantiated units are recomputed.

Revocation is permanent and has one precondition. A `MarketingManager` may revoke a claim in `SUBSTANTIATED` or `OVERSTATED` — supplying a `reason` — provided the claim has not already been finalized inside an impact audit. A revoked claim can never return to `SUBSTANTIATED`, and any further substantiation or derive-build call against it is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_ALREADY_REVOKED` (HTTP 409). Revoking an overstated claim is not an escape from the ledger: its required stated units still count against the liability until the season freezes. Claims expire on their own when the season they belong to reaches `FINALIZED` through a finalized annual impact audit, which moves them to `EXPIRED`.

### 3.9. The Annual Impact Audit and Partner Performance Grading

An audit is scheduled by an `ImpactAuditor` against an impact season and a claim, with an `audit_kind` of `ANNUAL_IMPACT` or `PARTNER_PERFORMANCE`, and opens at `SCHEDULED`. Fieldwork begins only when the season has at least one shipped distribution; starting moves the audit to `IN_PROGRESS` and computes the verified shipped and received figures together with the sampled transaction-test size. Starting fieldwork or rewriting an exception count against an audit that is already `FINALIZED` is refused with `ERR_AUDIT_ALREADY_FINALIZED` (HTTP 409).

The audit's verified figures are computed over the season's distributions excluding voided rows. `shipped_units_verified` and `received_units_verified` sum the shipped and received units over those rows, and the `unit_clearance_rate` is the received figure divided by the shipped figure, rounded half up to four decimal places and null when nothing shipped. The liability reconciliation delta at the instant of finalization is the verified shipped units minus the season's frozen claim units. Two different windows govern which receipts count. For an annual impact audit the window runs from the season's start to the audit's finalization instant and is closed above, so a receipt confirmed after the season ended but before the audit finalized still counts into the verified received figure. For a partner performance audit the window runs from the season's start to the season's end instant and ignores the finalization instant, so the same receipt is excluded. The two kinds therefore produce different clearance ratios over identical underlying rows. Worked readings: 10,000 shipped and 6,250 received gives a clearance rate of 0.6250 under either window, and 250 received over 300 shipped gives 0.8333, whose four-decimal value is what the grading arithmetic below consumes.

The transaction test is sized before the exception count is accepted, and both bounds are pinned. The sample size is the smaller of the total distribution count and the larger of two floors: one quarter of the count of critical distributions rounded up to a whole number, and the count of critical distributions divided by forty, rounded up to a whole number. The declared `transaction_test_sample_size` recorded by the auditor must reach that floor, and the `transaction_test_exceptions` must lie between zero and the declared sample size inclusive. Either a sample below the floor or an exception count above the sample is refused with `ERR_RECEIPT_SAMPLE_EXCEEDS_PLAN` (HTTP 409).

Finalization itself is the four-eyes step described in §2.12, and it carries a set of substantive preconditions. The acting identifier must not be the one that ran the preliminary review, or the call is refused with `ERR_AUDIT_SOD_VIOLATION`. An audit whose verified shipped units are zero has an undefined clearance rate and cannot be finalized; the attempt raises `ERR_ZERO_SHIPPED_UNITS` (HTTP 409). No claim for the season may be `OVERSTATED` at that instant. Finalizing an annual impact audit against a season that is still `PLANNED` additionally requires that every distribution in the season is `COMPLETED` or `WRITTEN_OFF`, or has zero confirmed impact units, and a violation is refused with `ERR_OUTSTANDING_DISTRIBUTIONS` (HTTP 409). Finalizing against a frozen season instead requires that the verified shipped units are greater than zero. A successful finalization computes the clearance rate, the `scorecard_total` and the grade from the submitted `claimed_donation_units`; writes the partner performance grade for every rated partner; moves the season to `FINALIZED` and advances its version; moves the season's liabilities to `AUDIT_LOCKED` and then to `CLOSED` where carry-out was materialized; recomputes the reconciliation delta; expires the season's claims; stamps `finalized_at`; and advances the audit's own version. An auditor note for a rejected finalization can be recorded through the `finalization_error_message` field.

Reopening an audit requires that the audit-lock reset flag is set on the acting auditor's season; when it is not, both the audit reopen and the liability unfreeze are refused with `ERR_AUDIT_LOCK_NOT_RESETTABLE` (HTTP 409). A reopen moves the audit to `REOPENED`, returns the season to `FROZEN`, returns the season's liabilities to accumulation, re-homes the successor season's carried-in units back to this season, and freezes the partner performance grade at the value this audit wrote. A reopened audit may be finalized again — by the same auditor that reopened it, and subject to the assertion that no claim for the season is overstated — which returns it to `FINALIZED` and the season to `FINALIZED`.

The partner performance grade is a weighted scorecard floored to an integer after clamping. The clearance term is the audit's four-decimal rounded clearance rate multiplied by fifty — rounded first, weighted second. The cycle-time term is the quantity ten minus the partner's mean completion days, multiplied by two and a half; it is omitted entirely when the partner has no received distributions. The receipt-logging term is the proportion of the partner's distributions with a non-null receipt instant over those with received units above zero, capped at one, multiplied by fifteen. The exception penalty is ten points for each sampled transaction-test exception. The total subtracts the penalty from the sum of the three scores, is clamped between 0 and 100, and is truncated toward zero to an integer. The resulting score maps to a grade: 85 or more is `EXEMPLARY`, 60 or more is `SATISFACTORY`, 40 or more is `MONITORED`, and anything lower is `PROBATION`. A dampener applies to exactly one case: when the partner completed no distributions for the audited season, the resolved grade is lowered by one band — never by more, so a probation grade stays at probation because no lower band exists. Omitting the cycle-time term is what makes the grade strictly monotone in received units: a partner with no received distributions keeps the received units required by a strictly monotone grade, so completing a distribution can never lower the audit's score. Worked readings for orientation: a clearance rate of 0.8333 with a three-day mean, perfect receipt logging and no exceptions gives 74 and a satisfactory grade, and the same figures with two exceptions give 54 and a monitored grade; a clearance of 1.0000 with perfect logging and no exceptions gives 65; a clearance of 1.0000 with a five-day mean, perfect logging and one exception gives 67; a clearance of 0.5000 with a five-day mean, perfect logging and four exceptions gives 12 and a probation grade.

The graded partner carries the result forward. A partner's `partner_performance_grade` is null until its first finalized partner performance audit, and thereafter it is read by the vetting path in §3.1, where a monitored or probation grade is what permits a completed partner to be re-vetted. Alongside the grade, the audit writes the audit's own summary — the verified shipped and received figures, the clearance rate, the scorecard total, the grade, the reconciliation delta and the computed sample size — which is the artifact the annual impact review is argued from.
