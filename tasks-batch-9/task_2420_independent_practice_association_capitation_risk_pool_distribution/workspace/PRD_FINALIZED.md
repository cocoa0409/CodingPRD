# IPA Capitation & Risk Pool Distribution API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

An Independent Practice Association sits between a health plan and the physicians who actually see the plan's members, and it survives or fails on the accuracy of two ledgers. The first is the capitation ledger: every month the payer transmits an enrollment file, the IPA reconciles that file against its own roster of covered members and their panel placements, and a remittance is struck that either agrees with the payer's assertion or does not. The second is the risk pool ledger: the capitation the IPA keeps after quality withhold is split between a professional pool and an institutional pool, downstream provider claims are adjudicated against those pools, an incurred-but-not-reported reserve is estimated for the tail of claims nobody has seen yet, reinsurance recovers the part of a catastrophic member above the attachment point, and whatever surplus remains is shared back to physicians in proportion to the member months they carried and the quality scores they earned.

Today that work is done in spreadsheets, and the spreadsheets disagree. Two analysts reconciling the same July enrollment file under two different conventions — one that snapshots the roster on the first of the month, one that prorates by member days — produce answers that differ by thousands of cents, and neither can prove the other wrong. An IBNR reserve estimated from a completion factor and one estimated from a target claims cost per member month diverge by more than a factor of two on identical pool data, which means the settlement waterfall built on top of the reserve is not reproducible either. Physicians who give exit notice take their panels with them before anyone has checked whether continuity-of-care obligations are satisfied. The purpose of this API is to make every one of those decisions a mechanical consequence of recorded data, evaluated in a pinned order, against a logical clock the caller controls.

This document specifies that system. It is mounted under `/ipa`, with a single test-driving clock endpoint outside that prefix. Sections 2 defines the global contract every request obeys; Section 3 walks the business workflows in the order an IPA actually performs them, from standing up a delegation agreement through the final distribution of shared savings; Sections 4 and 5 summarise the HTTP surface and the error vocabulary. Where a number, a threshold, an evaluation order, or a rounding step is specified in Section 2 or 3, it is normative — the reconciliation logic has no discretionary latitude.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the API must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the operational capacity in which that principal acts. Six roles are recognised, and their names are spelled exactly as follows: `IpaAdmin`, `NetworkManager`, `ClaimsExaminer`, `ReinsuranceUnderwriter`, `PhysicianUser`, and `PayerRepresentative`. If either header is absent or empty, or if `X-User-Role` names a string that is not one of those six, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If both headers are well-formed but the named role does not appear in the authorised-role list of the endpoint being called, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

No role is a superset of another. `IpaAdmin` owns agreements, risk pools, IBNR reserving, settlement proposal and scorecard finalisation, but it cannot credential a physician. `NetworkManager` credentials physicians, manages panels, assignments, transfers and physician exit, but it cannot post an enrollment file. `ClaimsExaminer` intakes, approves, denies, pays and voids downstream claims and opens and submits reinsurance recoveries, but it may not adjudicate them. `ReinsuranceUnderwriter` adjudicates submitted recoveries and does nothing else that mutates state. `PhysicianUser` acts on its own physician record — giving exit notice, closing its own panel, accepting or rejecting a panel transfer offered to it, appealing a denied claim. `PayerRepresentative` is the payer-side counterparty for exactly one delegation agreement: it supplies enrollees and enrollment files, disputes remittances, disputes settlement proposals and reports quality scores.

Identity semantics differ by role, and they govern every ownership check in the system. When `X-User-Role` is `IpaAdmin`, `NetworkManager`, `ClaimsExaminer`, or `ReinsuranceUnderwriter`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it — for example as `submitted_by_user_id` on a `StopLossRecovery` when a `ClaimsExaminer` submits one, or as `adjudicated_by_user_id` when a `ReinsuranceUnderwriter` approves or denies one — but it never compares it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `PhysicianUser`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `PhysicianMember`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `PhysicianMember` compare the resource's physician-referencing field to `X-User-ID`, so accepting or rejecting a panel transfer requires `X-User-ID` to equal the assignment's `pending_to_physician_id`, and driving a physician's own credential dimension requires `X-User-ID` to equal that physician record's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `PayerRepresentative`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `DelegationAgreement`; every operation a payer representative performs must resolve to that same agreement. A violation of either ownership rule is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403).

A distinct and stronger rule governs reinsurance adjudication. The `X-User-ID` that moves a `StopLossRecovery` to `SUBMITTED` is retained on the recovery, and the `X-User-ID` that later approves or denies that recovery must be a different value. Attempting to adjudicate a recovery one submitted oneself is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every endpoint that returns a collection accepts two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of items returned; it defaults to 10 when omitted, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` is the number of items to skip before the first returned item; it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100 inclusive, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are ordered by the resource's `created_at` timestamp, ascending. Because `created_at` is stamped from the logical clock of §2.5 rather than from wall-clock time, several resources routinely carry the identical `created_at`; wherever this document orders by `created_at`, ties are resolved by the sequence in which the server created the resources, and that creation sequence is what the system follows even when the clock was moved backwards between two creations. Every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope, where `total_count` is the number of records matching the filter before pagination is applied and `has_more` indicates whether records remain beyond the current window.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Fields and Version Semantics

Every resource in the system carries a server-minted `id`, formatted as a UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp rendered as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits. Eleven of the twelve resource types — `DelegationAgreement`, `PhysicianMember`, `Enrollee`, `MemberAssignment`, `EnrollmentFile`, `CapitationRemittance`, `RiskPool`, `DownstreamClaim`, `StopLossRecovery`, `QualityScorecard` and `PoolDistribution` — additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits re-stamped on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that resource. Both audit stamps are taken from the logical clock of §2.5 — never from the host machine's wall clock — and both are rendered in that normalised three-fractional-digit form whatever literal text the clock was set with, so re-stamping `updated_at` leaves it reading the same instant while the clock stands still even though `version` still advances.

`EnrollmentFileRow` is the single exception. A row carries only `id` and `created_at`; it has neither `updated_at` nor `version`, and neither field appears in its response body. This holds even though the parse step writes a row's `match_status` — that write is not versioned on the row itself, and the containing file's `version` is the only counter that moves.

Version increments are not limited to the resource the caller named. A derived counter changing on a parent is itself a mutation of that parent: when a posted remittance raises a pool's `funded_cents`, that pool's `version` advances and its `updated_at` is refreshed; when a paid claim raises an enrollee's `cumulative_paid_claims_cents`, the enrollee's `version` advances. Section 3 states, for each transition, which resources are touched; every resource named there has its `version` incremented by 1 and its `updated_at` refreshed as part of the same request.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

Every temporal rule in this specification — coverage windows, assignment intervals, continuity-of-care windows, pool incurral close, the settlement window, the instant recorded in `exit_notice_at`, `received_at`, `received_date` and `term_date` — is evaluated against a server-held logical clock, never against real wall-clock time. Before the clock has been set, it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. That fixed startup value is normative: the server must not substitute the host machine's current time.

Instants the server derives by advancing another instant by a whole number of days — a physician's `continuity_of_care_until`, a successor assignment's `continuity_of_care_until` and a pool's `settlement_due_date` — inherit the precision of the instant they were advanced from. When that source instant was supplied without fractional seconds the derived instant is likewise rendered with no fractional digits, so a `pool_year_end` of `2026-12-31T23:59:59Z` advanced by the 180-day professional settlement lag reads `2027-06-29T23:59:59Z`; otherwise the derived instant is rendered with three fractional digits.

Any authenticated role may move the clock by supplying a `current_utc` instant to the clock-setting capability, which returns the instant now in force and mutates no business entity. Every subsequent request evaluates its temporal rules against the new value.

### 2.6. Basis Points, Whole Cents and Rounding Discipline

All monetary quantities in this system are integers denominated in whole cents. There are no fractional cents anywhere in a stored field, a request body, or a response body. All rates, factors and shares are integers denominated in basis points, where 10000 basis points represents exactly 1.00 — so a `risk_score_bps` of 12500 means a risk factor of 1.25, and a `quality_withhold_bps` of 700 means seven percent.

The default rounding method throughout the system is round half up: a value exactly halfway between two whole units rounds away from zero to the larger magnitude. Two deviations from that default are deliberate and normative. First, the surplus ratio that selects a pool's surplus tier is truncated toward zero rather than rounded, so a ratio computing to 1500.6 basis points is treated as 1500 basis points. Second, the largest-remainder allocation that splits withhold and shared savings among physicians takes the floor of each participant's exact share and then distributes the leftover cents one at a time, so the allocation sums exactly to the amount being distributed with no rounding drift.

Where a formula contains more than one rounding opportunity, Section 3 pins how many rounding steps occur and where. Those pins are part of the contract: performing the same arithmetic with the roundings collapsed or reordered produces answers that differ by cents on realistic data and is incorrect.

### 2.7. Path Resolution Order and Agreement Scoping

Requests that address nested resources are resolved strictly left to right. For a path of the form `/ipa/delegation-agreements/{agreementId}/physicians`, the agreement is resolved before anything else; for `/ipa/enrollment-files/{fileId}/rows`, the file is resolved before any row is considered. The first identifier in the path that fails to resolve determines the response, and the error is the one specific to that resource type: `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND` for an unresolvable `agreementId`, `ERR_PHYSICIAN_NOT_FOUND` for `physicianId`, `ERR_ENROLLEE_NOT_FOUND` for `enrolleeId`, `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND` for `assignmentId`, `ERR_ENROLLMENT_FILE_NOT_FOUND` for `fileId`, `ERR_REMITTANCE_NOT_FOUND` for `remittanceId`, `ERR_RISK_POOL_NOT_FOUND` for `poolId`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND` for `claimId`, `ERR_RECOVERY_NOT_FOUND` for `recoveryId`, `ERR_SCORECARD_NOT_FOUND` for `scorecardId`, and `ERR_DISTRIBUTION_NOT_FOUND` for `distributionId`. Each of these carries HTTP 404. Identifiers supplied in a request body are resolved after every path identifier has resolved, and among themselves in the order the endpoint declares its input fields; an unresolvable body identifier raises the same resource-specific 404.

Once existence is settled, referential scope is checked. Every cross-reference in this system must stay inside a single `DelegationAgreement`. An assignment's enrollee and physician must belong to the same agreement; a transfer target must belong to the source physician's agreement; a claim's enrollee, attributed physician and resolved pool must all belong to the claim's agreement; a recovery's enrollee must belong to the agreement that owns the recovery's pool. Any of these crossing an agreement boundary is rejected with `ERR_AGREEMENT_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). Matching an enrollment file's rows against roster data is likewise scoped: an identical `external_member_id` or `physician_npi` living under a different agreement never matches.

### 2.8. Stale-Parent Rejection on Assignment and Scorecard Creation

Two creation operations in this system legitimately overwrite something a previous writer established, and both are therefore guarded by an optimistic-concurrency check against the parent resource the writer believed it had read.

A request to `POST /ipa/member-assignments` must carry `expected_enrollee_version`, a non-optional field holding the client's last observed `version` of the target `Enrollee`. If that value does not equal the enrollee's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ENROLLEE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The guard exists because an earlier create carrying `supersede_prior` set to true may already have re-pointed `assigned_physician_id` on that enrollee — behaviour the product intentionally permits — which means a writer working from an older read holds a stale picture of where the member currently sits on a panel.

A request to `POST /ipa/quality-scorecards` must carry `expected_physician_version`, a non-optional field holding the client's last observed `version` of the target `PhysicianMember`. If that value does not equal the physician's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PHYSICIAN_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Here too the guard is a stale-version check rather than a uniqueness conflict: a newer scorecard for the same physician and the same `measurement_period` legitimately supersedes the prior one, so without the check a writer working from an outdated read would silently supersede a scorecard it never saw.

No other endpoint in this system takes a version field. State-transition endpoints, panel-status changes, row appends, claim intake and recovery creation are all unversioned and must not be given a version input.

### 2.9. Ledger Conservation and Cascading Recomputation

The derived counters in this system are not caches that may drift; they are ledger positions that must be exactly reconstructible from the underlying rows at every instant. `DelegationAgreement.accrued_capitation_cents` is the sum, over the agreement's remittances whose `status` is either `RECONCILED` or `POSTED`, of `expected_capitation_cents` plus `retro_adjustment_cents`. `PhysicianMember.panel_size` is the count of that physician's assignments whose `status` is `ACTIVE`. `DelegationAgreement.total_assigned_members` is the count of `ACTIVE` assignments across every physician of the agreement. `Enrollee.assigned_physician_id` is the `physician_id` of that enrollee's single `ACTIVE` assignment, and is null when the enrollee has none. `RiskPool.paid_claims_cents` is the sum of `paid_cents` over that pool's claims whose `status` is `PAID`.

Because those counters are definitional, every operation that changes an underlying row also recomputes the counters it feeds, in the same request, before the response is returned — the system's consistency model is immediate, not eventual. Several operations cascade two, three, four or five levels deep, and Section 3 enumerates each level explicitly. Compensating actions are held to the same standard in reverse: disputing a reconciled enrollment file must reverse the accrual it created exactly, and voiding a paid claim must remove exactly the amount that claim contributed to the pool and to the enrollee. The conserved quantities are named in Section 3 alongside the transitions that move them.

One category of cascade deserves emphasis because it reaches sideways rather than downward. When an agreement's `capitation_posture` leaves `CURRENT`, every physician of that agreement whose `panel_status` is `OPEN` is closed to new members. Panels are never reopened automatically — not when a suspension is lifted, not when a dispute is resolved, not when a deficit clears. Reopening is always an explicit, separately authorised act on one physician at a time.

### 2.10. Validation Precedence

Validation is strictly ordered. The system evaluates the following checks top to bottom, and the first check whose condition is violated determines the response; no lower-numbered check is skipped and no higher-numbered check is evaluated once a rejection has been determined. Two of these pairings are deliberately reachable within a single request, and the precedence below is what resolves them.

1. **Authentication.** `X-User-Role` is present and names one of the six declared roles, and `X-User-ID` is present and non-empty. Otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Authorisation.** The role in `X-User-Role` appears in the target endpoint's authorised-role list. Otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Request shape.** The body parses; every required input field is present; every field has its declared type; every enum-typed field carries a declared member; every numeric field satisfies its declared range; `coverage_month` is four digits, a hyphen, then a month from `01` through `12`; `measurement_period` is exactly four digits; `limit` is between 1 and 100 inclusive and `offset` is not negative. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Applies to every endpoint.
4. **Path existence.** Every identifier in the path resolves, left to right as described in §2.7. Otherwise the resource-specific 404 from §2.7. Applies to every endpoint that has path parameters.
5. **Body reference existence.** Every foreign reference supplied in the body resolves, in the order the endpoint declares its input fields, raising `ERR_ENROLLEE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PHYSICIAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_RISK_POOL_NOT_FOUND` (404). Applies to `POST /ipa/member-assignments`, `POST /ipa/member-assignments/{id}/state`, `POST /ipa/downstream-claims`, `POST /ipa/stop-loss-recoveries` and `POST /ipa/quality-scorecards`.
6. **Resource ownership.** For a `PhysicianUser`, `X-User-ID` equals the `PhysicianMember` id the operation acts on — its own physician record, its own assignment transfer target, its own claim appeal. For a `PayerRepresentative`, `X-User-ID` equals the `DelegationAgreement` id the operation acts under. Otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). Applies to every endpoint whose authorised-role list includes `PhysicianUser` or `PayerRepresentative`.
7. **Stale parent version.** The supplied `expected_enrollee_version` or `expected_physician_version` equals the parent's current `version`, per §2.8. Otherwise `ERR_STALE_ENROLLEE_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_PHYSICIAN_VERSION` (412). Applies to `POST /ipa/member-assignments` and `POST /ipa/quality-scorecards`.
8. **Governing agreement is active.** The `DelegationAgreement` under which the operation runs has `status` equal to `ACTIVE`. Otherwise `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE` (409). Applies to `POST /ipa/delegation-agreements/{id}/enrollees`, `POST /ipa/delegation-agreements/{id}/enrollment-files`, `POST /ipa/delegation-agreements/{id}/risk-pools`, `POST /ipa/member-assignments` and `POST /ipa/downstream-claims`.
9. **Target physician is accepting.** The target `PhysicianMember` has `credential_status` equal to `ACTIVE`. Otherwise `ERR_PHYSICIAN_NOT_ACCEPTING_ASSIGNMENTS` (409). Applies to `POST /ipa/member-assignments` and `POST /ipa/member-assignments/{id}/state`. This is the first half of a deliberate conflict pair: a physician whose `credential_status` is `NOTICE_GIVEN` and whose `panel_size` already equals `max_panel_size` violates both this check and check 27, and this check wins — the response is `ERR_PHYSICIAN_NOT_ACCEPTING_ASSIGNMENTS` with HTTP 409, never the fiscal capacity error.
10. **Target panel is open.** The target `PhysicianMember` has `panel_status` equal to `OPEN`. Otherwise `ERR_PANEL_CLOSED_TO_NEW` (409). Applies to `POST /ipa/member-assignments`.
11. **Transition legality.** The requested `to_state` is reachable from the resource's current state under that entity's transition list in Section 3, and the transition's own state-level preconditions hold — including that a claim's `appeal_count` is below the appeal limit of 2, and that the logical clock has reached a pool's `pool_year_end` before incurral is closed. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (409). The settlement-window clock check is not evaluated here; it is check 17. Applies to every state-transition endpoint and to `PATCH /ipa/physicians/{id}/panel-status`.
12. **Single-agreement scope.** The cross-agreement rules of §2.7 hold. Otherwise `ERR_AGREEMENT_MISMATCH` (422). Applies to `POST /ipa/member-assignments`, `POST /ipa/member-assignments/{id}/state`, `POST /ipa/downstream-claims` and `POST /ipa/stop-loss-recoveries`.
13. **Specialty suits the age band.** An enrollee whose `age_band` is `PEDIATRIC` may be placed only with a physician whose `specialty` is `PEDIATRICS` or `PRIMARY_CARE`; an enrollee whose `age_band` is `SENIOR` may be placed only with a physician whose `specialty` is `INTERNAL_MEDICINE` or `PRIMARY_CARE`. Otherwise `ERR_SPECIALTY_AGE_BAND_MISMATCH` (422). Applies to `POST /ipa/member-assignments` and `POST /ipa/member-assignments/{id}/state`.
14. **Continuity of care.** An `ACTIVE` assignment may not be terminated while the holding physician's `continuity_of_care_until` is set and the logical clock has not reached it. Otherwise `ERR_CONTINUITY_OF_CARE_ACTIVE` (422). Applies to `POST /ipa/member-assignments/{id}/state`. The supersede path on assignment creation is exempt, because the enrollee retains a panel physician without a gap.
15. **Assignment falls inside coverage.** A new assignment's `effective_date` is at or after the enrollee's `coverage_start_date`, and when `coverage_end_date` is set, at or before it. Otherwise `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_BEFORE_COVERAGE` (422). Applies to `POST /ipa/member-assignments`. This is the second deliberate conflict pair: an `effective_date` earlier than `coverage_start_date` that also opens an interval overlapping the enrollee's existing `ACTIVE` assignment violates both this check and check 18, and this check wins — the response is `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_BEFORE_COVERAGE` with HTTP 422, never `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. The pair is reachable with the target physician in `credential_status` `ACTIVE` and `panel_status` `OPEN`, so no state-category check interferes.
16. **A pool exists for the service date.** A claim's `service_date` falls inside the closed period of some risk pool of the claim's agreement whose `pool_type` matches the claim's `pool_type`. Otherwise `ERR_NO_POOL_FOR_SERVICE_DATE` (422). Applies to `POST /ipa/downstream-claims`.
17. **Settlement window is open.** A pool may reach `SETTLED` only once the logical clock has reached its `settlement_due_date`, which is `pool_year_end` plus 180 days for a `PROFESSIONAL` pool and `pool_year_end` plus 270 days for an `INSTITUTIONAL` pool. Otherwise `ERR_SETTLEMENT_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN` (422). Applies to `POST /ipa/risk-pools/{id}/state`.
18. **Assignment intervals do not overlap.** A new assignment's interval must not overlap any existing non-terminal assignment interval for the same enrollee. Otherwise `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (409). Applies to `POST /ipa/member-assignments`. Interval semantics are half-open, as described in §2.11.
19. **Pool periods do not overlap.** A new pool's period must not overlap an existing pool of the same agreement with the same `pool_type`. Otherwise `ERR_POOL_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (409). Applies to `POST /ipa/delegation-agreements/{id}/risk-pools`. Pool interval semantics are closed, as described in §2.11.
20. **Uniqueness.** `payer_tax_id` is unique across all agreements, raising `ERR_DUPLICATE_TAX_ID`; `npi` is unique among the physicians of one agreement, raising `ERR_DUPLICATE_NPI`; `external_member_id` is unique among the enrollees of one agreement, raising `ERR_DUPLICATE_MEMBER_ID`; and `coverage_month` is unique among the agreement's enrollment files whose `status` is anything other than `DISPUTED`, raising `ERR_COVERAGE_MONTH_DUPLICATE`. All four carry HTTP 409. Applies to `POST /ipa/delegation-agreements`, `POST /ipa/delegation-agreements/{id}/physicians`, `POST /ipa/delegation-agreements/{id}/enrollees` and `POST /ipa/delegation-agreements/{id}/enrollment-files`.
21. **Pool shares sum to par.** `professional_pool_share_bps` and `institutional_pool_share_bps` add to exactly 10000. Otherwise `ERR_POOL_SHARE_SUM_INVALID` (422). Applies to `POST /ipa/delegation-agreements` and `POST /ipa/delegation-agreements/{id}/state`.
22. **File is not empty.** An enrollment file may reach `PARSED` only when its `row_count` is at least 1, and may reach `RECONCILED` only when its `matched_row_count` is at least 1. Otherwise `ERR_ENROLLMENT_FILE_EMPTY` (422). Applies to `POST /ipa/enrollment-files/{id}/state`.
23. **Scorecards are final.** A pool may reach `SETTLEMENT_PROPOSED` only when every physician of the agreement whose `credential_status` is `ACTIVE` or `NOTICE_GIVEN` holds a scorecard whose `status` is `FINAL` and whose `measurement_period` equals the four-digit year of the pool's `pool_year_start`. Otherwise `ERR_SCORECARD_NOT_FINAL` (422). Applies to `POST /ipa/risk-pools/{id}/state`.
24. **Panel is clear before exit.** A physician may reach `EXITED` only when no assignment held by that physician has `status` equal to `ACTIVE`, `TRANSFER_PROPOSED` or `TRANSFER_ACCEPTED`. Otherwise `ERR_PANEL_NOT_TRANSFERRED` (422). Applies to `POST /ipa/physicians/{id}/state`.
25. **Segregation of duties.** The `X-User-ID` approving or denying a recovery differs from that recovery's `submitted_by_user_id`. Otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). Applies to `POST /ipa/stop-loss-recoveries/{id}/state`.
26. **Attachment is pierced.** A recovery may be created only when the enrollee's paid-claim total in the pool strictly exceeds the agreement's `stop_loss_attachment_cents`; exactly at the attachment point the excess is zero and the create is refused. Otherwise `ERR_STOP_LOSS_NOT_ATTACHED` (422). Applies to `POST /ipa/stop-loss-recoveries`.
27. **Panel capacity.** The target physician's `panel_size`, counting only assignments whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, is strictly less than the agreement's `max_panel_size`. Otherwise `ERR_PANEL_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (422). Applies to `POST /ipa/member-assignments` and `POST /ipa/member-assignments/{id}/state`.
28. **Allowed does not exceed billed.** The `allowed_cents` supplied on an approval transition is at most the claim's `billed_cents`. Otherwise `ERR_ALLOWED_EXCEEDS_BILLED` (422). Applies to `POST /ipa/downstream-claims/{id}/state`.
29. **Capitation posture permits reopening.** A panel may move from `CLOSED_TO_NEW` back to `OPEN` only while the agreement's `capitation_posture` is `CURRENT`. Otherwise `ERR_CAPITATION_POSTURE_RESTRICTED` (409). Applies to `PATCH /ipa/physicians/{id}/panel-status`.

### 2.11. Interval Semantics

Two families of interval exist in this system and their endpoint semantics deliberately differ.

Assignment intervals are **half-open**: an assignment occupies a panel slot from its `effective_date` inclusive up to but not including its `term_date`, and an open-ended assignment has a null `term_date`. Consequently an assignment terming exactly at `2026-07-01T00:00:00Z` and a new assignment for the same enrollee starting at that same instant are adjacent rather than overlapping, and both may exist without triggering an overlap rejection.

Pool incurral periods are **closed**: a pool covers from `pool_year_start` inclusive through `pool_year_end` inclusive. A pool ending exactly at `2026-12-31T23:59:59Z` and a new pool of the same `pool_type` starting at that same instant therefore do collide, and the second create is refused. The asymmetry with assignment intervals is intentional; claim-to-pool attribution likewise uses closed-interval containment, so a claim whose `service_date` is exactly `pool_year_end` belongs to that pool.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up a Delegation Agreement

Everything in this system hangs off a **delegation agreement** — the contract by which a payer delegates some slice of its administrative risk to the IPA. An `IpaAdmin` opens one by supplying the payer's legal name in `payer_name` and its federal tax identifier in `payer_tax_id`, which must not already appear on any other agreement. The `delegation_scope` records how much the payer has handed over and takes one of `CLAIMS_ONLY`, `CLAIMS_AND_UM` or `FULL_RISK`. The economics of the deal arrive in the same payload: `base_pmpm_cents` is the base per-member-per-month capitation in whole cents before any risk, age or benefit-tier adjustment and must be at least 1; `professional_pool_share_bps` and `institutional_pool_share_bps` are the basis-point shares of net payable capitation routed to the professional and institutional pools respectively, each between 0 and 10000 inclusive; `quality_withhold_bps` is the basis-point slice of expected capitation held back pending panel quality performance and may not exceed 3000; `stop_loss_attachment_cents` is the per-enrollee, per-pool attachment point above which reinsurance responds and may be zero or more; and `reinsurance_recovery_bps` is the basis-point share of the excess above attachment that the reinsurer will pay, between 0 and 10000 inclusive. Two method selectors are fixed at contract time: `reconciliation_method`, either `ROSTER_SNAPSHOT` or `MEMBER_MONTHS_PRORATED`, chooses how a coverage month's expected capitation is computed, and `ibnr_method`, either `COMPLETION_FACTOR` or `PMPM_LOSS_RATIO`, is the default reserving convention. Each method carries its own parameter: `ibnr_completion_factor_bps` is the completion factor in basis points, between 1 and 10000 inclusive, consumed by the completion-factor variant, and `target_claims_pmpm_cents` is the target claims cost per member month, zero or more, consumed by the loss-ratio variant. `max_panel_size` caps how many members any one physician may hold and must be at least 1. Finally, `continuity_of_care_days` sets the protected window that follows a physician's exit notice; when the field is omitted from the payload it defaults to 90 days.

A newly created agreement begins in `status` `DRAFT` with `capitation_posture` `CURRENT` and every derived counter at zero: `accrued_capitation_cents`, `withheld_balance_cents`, `forfeited_withhold_cents` and `total_assigned_members` all start at 0. The two pool shares are checked for sum at creation, and they are checked again at activation — the agreement moves `DRAFT → ACTIVE` only when `status` is `DRAFT` and `professional_pool_share_bps` and `institutional_pool_share_bps` together come to exactly 10000; a mismatch is refused with `ERR_POOL_SHARE_SUM_INVALID`. Activation sets `status` to `ACTIVE`, sets `capitation_posture` to `CURRENT`, and derives the per-member capitation components for every enrollee already registered under the agreement as described in §3.3.

An active agreement may be suspended and reinstated as often as the parties need. Moving `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` requires only that `status` is currently `ACTIVE`; the transition sets `status` to `SUSPENDED` and, as a second-order effect, closes the network — every physician of that agreement whose `panel_status` is `OPEN` is set to `CLOSED_TO_NEW`, and each such physician's `version` advances. Moving `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` requires only that `status` is `SUSPENDED` and restores `status` to `ACTIVE`. It deliberately does not reopen anything: panels closed by the suspension stay `CLOSED_TO_NEW` until a `NetworkManager` reopens them one at a time.

Termination is a one-way door and it is gated on the risk pools. From either `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, an agreement may move to `TERMINATED` only when no risk pool of that agreement is in `ACCUMULATING`, `CLOSED_FOR_INCURRAL`, `RESERVED` or `SETTLEMENT_PROPOSED` — in other words, every pool must already be `SETTLED`. An attempt with a live pool outstanding is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. `TERMINATED` is terminal; no transition leads out of it.

Sitting alongside `status` is a second, entirely derived indicator: `capitation_posture`, which reads `CURRENT`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `WITHHELD`. It is re-derived on every transition that changes a remittance's status, changes a pool's deficit flag, or changes an enrollment file's status, and it is derived by strict precedence with the first matching condition winning. If any capitation remittance of the agreement has `status` `DISPUTED`, the posture is `WITHHELD`. Otherwise, if any risk pool of the agreement has `deficit_flag` set to true, the posture is `UNDER_REVIEW`. Otherwise the posture is `CURRENT`. The precedence is not a blend and not a sum: an agreement carrying both a disputed remittance and a deficit pool reads `WITHHELD`, and clearing only the deficit leaves it reading `WITHHELD`. The posture matters operationally because a panel may be reopened only while it reads `CURRENT`; an attempt while it reads `UNDER_REVIEW` or `WITHHELD` is refused with `ERR_CAPITATION_POSTURE_RESTRICTED`.

Creating enrollees, enrollment files, risk pools, member assignments or downstream claims all require the governing agreement to be `ACTIVE`; attempting any of them while the agreement sits in `DRAFT`, `SUSPENDED` or `TERMINATED` is refused with `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`. Reading an agreement returns its derived counters and its `capitation_posture` alongside the contract parameters, and agreements may be listed with an optional `status` filter.

### 3.2. The Physician Network: Credentialing, Panels and Exit

A **member physician** is enrolled under an agreement by an `IpaAdmin` or a `NetworkManager`, who supplies the physician's National Provider Identifier in `npi` — unique among the physicians of that agreement — a display name in `full_name`, and a `specialty` drawn from `PRIMARY_CARE`, `PEDIATRICS`, `INTERNAL_MEDICINE` or `OB_GYN`. The `agreement_id` is taken from the creation path and never from the body. A new physician record begins with `credential_status` `APPLICANT`, `panel_status` `OPEN`, `panel_size` 0, `exit_notice_at` and `continuity_of_care_until` both null, `quality_composite_bps` 0, and `latest_scorecard_period` null.

What makes a physician record unusual is that it carries **two independent state dimensions on the same row**. The contracting dimension lives in `credential_status` and runs `APPLICANT → CREDENTIALED → ACTIVE`, with a rescindable detour to `NOTICE_GIVEN` and a terminal `EXITED`. The panel dimension lives in `panel_status` and runs between `OPEN` and `CLOSED_TO_NEW`, with a system-driven `TRANSFER_PENDING` and a terminal `TRANSFERRED`. Neither dimension constrains the other's legal moves except where a precondition below says so explicitly.

On the contracting dimension, a `NetworkManager` advances `APPLICANT → CREDENTIALED` — permitted when `credential_status` is `APPLICANT` and the parent agreement's `status` is `ACTIVE` — and then `CREDENTIALED → ACTIVE`, permitted when `credential_status` is `CREDENTIALED` and the agreement is again `ACTIVE`. Both moves set the new `credential_status` and advance the physician's `version` by 1.

Exit notice is the interesting move, and it may be given either by a `NetworkManager` or by the `PhysicianUser` acting on their own record. It is permitted when `credential_status` is `ACTIVE`, and it does four things at once: `credential_status` becomes `NOTICE_GIVEN`; `exit_notice_at` is stamped with the current logical clock instant; `continuity_of_care_until` is derived as `exit_notice_at` advanced by the agreement's `continuity_of_care_days` — ninety days by default, so a notice given on the first of March protects that panel until the thirtieth of May; and `panel_status` is set to `CLOSED_TO_NEW` if and only if it was `OPEN`, leaving `TRANSFER_PENDING` and `TRANSFERRED` untouched. The move is reversible: a `NetworkManager` may take `NOTICE_GIVEN → ACTIVE`, which restores `credential_status` to `ACTIVE`, clears `exit_notice_at` back to null, clears `continuity_of_care_until` back to null, and leaves `panel_status` exactly as it stood — the panel is not reopened by rescinding the notice.

Reaching `EXITED` requires that the panel has actually been handed over. A `NetworkManager` may take `NOTICE_GIVEN → EXITED` only when `credential_status` is `NOTICE_GIVEN` and zero assignments held by that physician sit in `ACTIVE`, `TRANSFER_PROPOSED` or `TRANSFER_ACCEPTED`; a physician still holding live or in-flight members is refused with `ERR_PANEL_NOT_TRANSFERRED`. When the exit does succeed it drives the panel dimension too: `credential_status` becomes `EXITED` and `panel_status` becomes `TRANSFERRED`, whether the panel dimension stood at `TRANSFER_PENDING` or at `CLOSED_TO_NEW` immediately before. Both `EXITED` and `TRANSFERRED` are terminal.

On the panel dimension, closing a panel — `OPEN → CLOSED_TO_NEW` — may be done by a `NetworkManager` or by the `PhysicianUser` on their own record, and requires only that `panel_status` is `OPEN`. Reopening — `CLOSED_TO_NEW → OPEN` — is reserved to a `NetworkManager` and carries three preconditions: `panel_status` must be `CLOSED_TO_NEW`, `credential_status` must be `ACTIVE`, and the agreement's `capitation_posture` must read `CURRENT`. A physician who has given notice cannot have their panel reopened, and neither can a physician under an agreement whose posture has drifted to `UNDER_REVIEW` or `WITHHELD`. Both directions advance the physician's `version` by 1.

The remaining panel moves are made by the system rather than by a caller. When any assignment held by a physician is proposed for transfer, that physician's `panel_status` moves to `TRANSFER_PENDING` from whichever of `OPEN` or `CLOSED_TO_NEW` it currently occupies. When the last of that physician's assignments leaves `TRANSFER_PROPOSED` and `TRANSFER_ACCEPTED` — whether by rejection back to `ACTIVE` or by completion to `TRANSFERRED` — the system resolves `TRANSFER_PENDING → CLOSED_TO_NEW`, provided no assignment of that physician remains in `TRANSFER_PROPOSED` or `TRANSFER_ACCEPTED` and the physician's `credential_status` is not `EXITED`. Note the asymmetry: the panel resolves to `CLOSED_TO_NEW`, never back to `OPEN`, even if it was `OPEN` when the transfer was first proposed. And when a physician's credential dimension reaches `EXITED`, the panel dimension moves to `TRANSFERRED` from either `TRANSFER_PENDING` or `CLOSED_TO_NEW`. Every one of these system-driven moves advances the physician's `version` by 1.

Two derived quality fields live on the physician record. `quality_composite_bps` holds the `composite_score_bps` of that physician's most recently finalised scorecard, selected by taking the scorecards whose `status` is `FINAL` and ordering them by `measurement_period` descending and then by `created_at` descending; when the physician has no `FINAL` scorecard at all the field reads 0. `latest_scorecard_period` holds the `measurement_period` of the most recently created scorecard for that physician regardless of status, and is null while the physician has none. A physician record read individually exposes both state dimensions, `panel_size`, `quality_composite_bps` and `continuity_of_care_until`; the agreement's physician roster may be listed with optional `credential_status` and `panel_status` filters.

### 3.3. Enrollees and Their Per-Member Capitation Components

A covered member is registered under an agreement by an `IpaAdmin` or by the `PayerRepresentative` for that agreement. The payload carries `external_member_id`, the payer-side member identifier that later enrollment-file rows are matched against and which must be unique among the agreement's enrollees; `age_band`, one of `PEDIATRIC`, `ADULT` or `SENIOR`; `benefit_tier`, one of `HMO`, `POS` or `HMO_PLUS`; `risk_score_bps`, the risk-adjustment factor in basis points between 1 and 50000 inclusive, where 10000 means a factor of exactly 1.00; `coverage_start_date`, the first instant of payer coverage, before which no assignment may start; and optionally `coverage_end_date`, the last instant of coverage, which defaults to null for an open-ended member. The `agreement_id` comes from the creation path. A freshly registered enrollee has `assigned_physician_id` null and `cumulative_paid_claims_cents` 0.

Registration immediately derives the member's three capitation components, and the derivation is also re-run for every enrollee of an agreement at the moment that agreement is activated out of `DRAFT`. Enrollees belonging to an agreement whose `status` is `TERMINATED` are never re-derived. The derivation proceeds as follows. Take the agreement's `base_pmpm_cents`, multiply it by the age-band factor for the member's `age_band` — 6500 basis points, that is a factor of 0.65, for `PEDIATRIC`; 10000 basis points, a factor of exactly 1.00, for `ADULT`; and 14500 basis points, a factor of 1.45, for `SENIOR` — and then multiply that by the member's `risk_score_bps` expressed as a proportion of 10000. Both multiplications are carried left to right on the unrounded product, and the running product is rounded half up to whole cents exactly once, at the end of the chain. To that single rounded figure add the flat benefit-tier add-on for the member's `benefit_tier`: nothing at all for `HMO`, 1200 cents for `POS`, and 2500 cents for `HMO_PLUS`. The tier add-on is a flat cent amount that is never risk-adjusted and never age-adjusted, and it is applied after the rounding, not before. The sum is the member's raw amount.

The raw amount is then clamped, and the clamp is nested rather than parallel. First the raw amount is raised to the floor of 8000 cents if it falls below that floor; the floored value is then reduced to the ceiling of 95000 cents if it exceeds that ceiling. Applying the bounds in this nested order means a member below the floor can never be pushed above the ceiling and a member above the ceiling can never be pulled below the floor. The clamped result is stored as `gross_pmpm_cents`. From it, `withhold_cents` is the gross amount multiplied by the agreement's `quality_withhold_bps` as a proportion of 10000, rounded half up to whole cents, and `net_pmpm_cents` is `gross_pmpm_cents` less `withhold_cents`. Beyond that single rounding on the risk-adjusted product and the single rounding on the withhold, all arithmetic here is integer arithmetic.

Consider an agreement with `base_pmpm_cents` of 30000 and `quality_withhold_bps` of 700. An `ADULT` member on `HMO` coverage with a `risk_score_bps` of 12500 reaches a raw amount of 37500 cents, which sits inside both bounds, so `gross_pmpm_cents` is 37500, `withhold_cents` is 2625, and `net_pmpm_cents` is 34875. A `PEDIATRIC` member on `HMO` with a `risk_score_bps` of 3000 computes a raw amount of 5850 cents, below the floor, so `gross_pmpm_cents` becomes 8000, `withhold_cents` 560, and `net_pmpm_cents` 7440. A `SENIOR` member on `HMO_PLUS` with a `risk_score_bps` of 22000 computes a raw amount of 98200 cents, above the ceiling, so `gross_pmpm_cents` becomes 95000, `withhold_cents` 6650, and `net_pmpm_cents` 88350.

The fourth derived money field on an enrollee, `cumulative_paid_claims_cents`, is the sum of `paid_cents` across that member's downstream claims whose `status` is `PAID`; it is discussed with claim payment in §3.6. Enrollees may be listed with optional `agreement_id`, `assigned_physician_id` and `age_band` filters, and an individual read exposes the gross, withhold and net components together with `cumulative_paid_claims_cents`.

### 3.4. Panel Assignment, Superseding, and the Transfer Handshake

Placing a member on a physician's panel creates a **member assignment**, and an `IpaAdmin` or `NetworkManager` does so by supplying `enrollee_id`, `physician_id`, an `effective_date` that is the inclusive start of the interval, an `assignment_source`, and the `expected_enrollee_version` guard described in §2.8. The `assignment_source` enumeration admits `MANUAL`, `ENROLLMENT_FILE` and `PANEL_TRANSFER`; `PANEL_TRANSFER` is the value the server itself writes on the successor row that a completed panel transfer produces. An optional `supersede_prior` flag changes how an overlap with the member's existing placement is handled.

A new assignment lands in `status` `ACTIVE` with `pending_to_physician_id` null, `term_date` null, `continuity_of_care_until` null and `superseded_by_assignment_id` null. Creation then reconciles the counters it feeds: the target physician's `panel_size` is recomputed, the enrollee's `assigned_physician_id` is repointed at that physician, and the agreement's `total_assigned_members` is recomputed. If the recomputed `panel_size` has reached the agreement's `max_panel_size`, the target physician's `panel_status` moves from `OPEN` to `CLOSED_TO_NEW` as part of the same request. The parent enrollee's `version` advances by 1 because its `assigned_physician_id` moved.

When `supersede_prior` is supplied as true, the member's existing `ACTIVE` assignment is not treated as a conflict; instead the system moves that prior row `ACTIVE → TERMINATED` at the new assignment's `effective_date`, setting its `status` to `TERMINATED`, its `term_date` to that instant, its `superseded_by_assignment_id` to the identifier of the new row, recomputing the prior physician's `panel_size` downward by one, and advancing the prior row's `version` by 1. The supersede path is permitted only when the prior row's `status` is `ACTIVE` and the new assignment's `effective_date` is strictly later than the prior row's `effective_date`. It deliberately bypasses the continuity-of-care gate, because the member keeps a panel physician without any interruption in coverage. Absent `supersede_prior`, an interval that overlaps a non-terminal assignment of the same member is refused with `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_PERIOD_OVERLAP`, subject to the half-open semantics of §2.11 and to the precedence rule of §2.10 that an out-of-coverage `effective_date` is reported as `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_BEFORE_COVERAGE` even when the same request also overlaps.

Terminating an assignment outright is a `NetworkManager` action, permitted when `status` is `ACTIVE` and either the holding physician's `continuity_of_care_until` is null or the logical clock has reached it. The transition sets `status` to `TERMINATED` and stamps `term_date` with the current logical clock instant; it recomputes the physician's `panel_size` downward by one, clears the enrollee's `assigned_physician_id` to null, recomputes the agreement's `total_assigned_members` downward by one, and advances the assignment's `version` by 1. A termination attempted inside a live continuity window is refused with `ERR_CONTINUITY_OF_CARE_ACTIVE`. Both `TERMINATED` and `TRANSFERRED` are terminal states for an assignment.

The **panel transfer** is a three-party handshake spread over three transitions, and it exists so that a departing physician's members move to a successor without the members ever losing a panel. A `NetworkManager` opens it by taking the assignment `ACTIVE → TRANSFER_PROPOSED`, supplying `to_physician_id` as the intended successor and `transfer_effective_date` as the instant the handover takes effect. The proposal is admitted only when `status` is `ACTIVE`, when `to_physician_id` resolves to a physician under the same agreement as the assignment's current holder, when that target physician's `credential_status` is `ACTIVE`, when the target's `panel_size` is strictly less than the agreement's `max_panel_size`, and when `transfer_effective_date` is at or after the current logical clock instant. On success `status` becomes `TRANSFER_PROPOSED`, `pending_to_physician_id` is set to `to_physician_id`, and the *source* physician's `panel_status` moves to `TRANSFER_PENDING`. Because the row has left `ACTIVE`, the source physician's `panel_size` is recomputed one lower and the agreement's `total_assigned_members` is recomputed one lower in the same request. The assignment's `version` advances by 1.

Only the transfer target may answer, and the answer is checked against `X-User-ID`: a `PhysicianUser` whose identifier does not equal the assignment's `pending_to_physician_id` is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. Rejection takes the assignment `TRANSFER_PROPOSED → ACTIVE`, permitted when `status` is `TRANSFER_PROPOSED`. It clears `pending_to_physician_id` back to null and, when no other assignment of the source physician remains in `TRANSFER_PROPOSED` or `TRANSFER_ACCEPTED`, resolves that physician's `panel_status` to `CLOSED_TO_NEW` — pointedly not back to `OPEN`, even if `OPEN` is where it started. Rejection is the compensating action for the proposal and it restores the two count quantities exactly: the source physician's `panel_size` and the agreement's `total_assigned_members` return to precisely the values they held before the proposal was made. Acceptance instead takes `TRANSFER_PROPOSED → TRANSFER_ACCEPTED`, permitted when `status` is `TRANSFER_PROPOSED`, when `X-User-ID` equals `pending_to_physician_id`, and when the target physician's `credential_status` is still `ACTIVE`; the transition changes nothing but `status` and the `version`.

Completion is a `NetworkManager` action and it is the deepest cascade on this entity. Taking `TRANSFER_ACCEPTED → TRANSFERRED` requires `status` to be `TRANSFER_ACCEPTED` and the logical clock to have reached the `transfer_effective_date` recorded on the proposal; the four levels then run in order. First, this assignment's `status` becomes `TRANSFERRED` and its `term_date` is set to `transfer_effective_date`. Second, because this row has termed, a successor assignment is created with the same `enrollee_id`, `physician_id` set to the recorded `pending_to_physician_id`, `effective_date` set to `transfer_effective_date`, `assignment_source` set to `PANEL_TRANSFER`, `status` `ACTIVE`, and `continuity_of_care_until` set to `transfer_effective_date` advanced by the agreement's `continuity_of_care_days`. Third, because the successor row is `ACTIVE`, the source physician's `panel_size` is recomputed one lower, the target physician's `panel_size` is recomputed one higher, and the enrollee's `assigned_physician_id` is repointed at the target physician. Fourth, because the target's `panel_size` has changed and now equals the agreement's `max_panel_size`, that target's `panel_status` moves `OPEN → CLOSED_TO_NEW`. Alongside those four levels, `pending_to_physician_id` is cleared to null, the source physician's `panel_status` resolves to `CLOSED_TO_NEW` when no other assignment of that physician remains in `TRANSFER_PROPOSED` or `TRANSFER_ACCEPTED`, and the agreement's `total_assigned_members` is recomputed back to its pre-proposal value, because the successor row is `ACTIVE` where the predecessor no longer is. The assignment's `version` advances by 1.

The same age-band and specialty rule guards both the initial placement and the transfer proposal: a `PEDIATRIC` member may sit only with a `PEDIATRICS` or `PRIMARY_CARE` physician and a `SENIOR` member only with an `INTERNAL_MEDICINE` or `PRIMARY_CARE` physician, and a violation on either operation is refused with `ERR_SPECIALTY_AGE_BAND_MISMATCH`. So do the capacity and credential gates: a target whose `credential_status` is not `ACTIVE` is refused with `ERR_PHYSICIAN_NOT_ACCEPTING_ASSIGNMENTS` on both operations, and a target already at `max_panel_size` is refused with `ERR_PANEL_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` on both, with the credential error taking precedence when both apply. The requirement that the target's `panel_status` be `OPEN`, refused with `ERR_PANEL_CLOSED_TO_NEW`, applies to initial placement only — a transfer proposal is judged on capacity and credential state, not on whether the successor's panel is nominally open. Assignments may be listed with optional `enrollee_id`, `physician_id`, `pending_to_physician_id` and `status` filters, and an individual read exposes the transfer target and the continuity window.

### 3.5. The Monthly Enrollment File: Receipt, Parsing, Reconciliation, Dispute and Posting

Each month the payer transmits an enrollment file, and that file is the trigger for the entire capitation cycle. An `IpaAdmin` or the agreement's `PayerRepresentative` opens one by supplying `coverage_month` — a four-digit year, a hyphen, and a two-digit month from `01` through `12`, so `2026-07` is well-formed and `2026-13` is not — together with `payer_file_reference`, the payer's own transmission reference retained for audit. The `agreement_id` comes from the creation path, `received_at` is stamped from the logical clock, `status` begins at `RECEIVED`, and `row_count`, `matched_row_count`, `exception_row_count` and `claimed_capitation_cents` all begin at 0. An agreement may hold only one file per `coverage_month` among files whose `status` is anything other than `DISPUTED`; a second live file for the same month is refused with `ERR_COVERAGE_MONTH_DUPLICATE`. The consequence of that carve-out is that a disputed month may be resubmitted as a fresh file without the duplicate check firing.

Rows are appended to a file while it is still in `RECEIVED`. Each row carries `external_member_id` exactly as the payer transmitted it, `physician_npi` as the payer-asserted assigned physician, a `row_action` from `ADD`, `TERM`, `CHANGE`, `RETRO_ADD` or `RETRO_TERM`, the `claimed_capitation_cents` the payer asserts for that row in whole cents which must be zero or more, and an `effective_date` giving the payer-asserted instant for the row action. The `file_id` is taken from the creation path. A new row's `match_status` reads `UNMATCHED` until the file is parsed. Appending a row increments the file's `row_count` and advances the file's `version` by 1; the row itself, uniquely among this system's resources, carries only `id` and `created_at` and has no `updated_at` and no `version`.

Parsing the file — the move `RECEIVED → PARSED`, available to an `IpaAdmin` or the `PayerRepresentative` — requires `status` to be `RECEIVED` and `row_count` to be at least 1; an empty file is refused with `ERR_ENROLLMENT_FILE_EMPTY`. Parsing assigns each row a `match_status`, and the assignment is a four-way cascade evaluated in a fixed order against the file's own agreement. First the system looks for an enrollee of that agreement whose `external_member_id` equals the row's `external_member_id`, and separately for a physician of that agreement whose `npi` equals the row's `physician_npi`. Matching is exact string equality with no trimming and no case folding, and it never reaches outside the file's agreement — an identical member identifier living under a different agreement does not match. If no enrollee was found, the row's `match_status` is `UNMATCHED`. Otherwise, if no physician was found, it is `MATCHED_ENROLLEE_ONLY`. Otherwise, if the physician was found but that physician's `credential_status` is neither `ACTIVE` nor `NOTICE_GIVEN`, it is `EXCEPTION_PHYSICIAN_INACTIVE`. Only when an enrollee was found, a physician was found, and that physician's `credential_status` is `ACTIVE` or `NOTICE_GIVEN` does the row read `MATCHED`. A row's `match_status` is written once by this transition and is immutable thereafter.

Parsing then recomputes the file's three counters. `matched_row_count` is the count of the file's rows whose `match_status` is `MATCHED`. `exception_row_count` is `row_count` less `matched_row_count`. And `claimed_capitation_cents` is the sum of `claimed_capitation_cents` over the file's rows whose `match_status` is `MATCHED` and whose `row_action` is `ADD`, `CHANGE` or `TERM`. Rows with any other `match_status` contribute to neither total; they are exceptions the payer must resolve on resubmission. Retroactive rows are accumulated separately into a net retroactive adjustment: the sum of `claimed_capitation_cents` over `MATCHED` rows whose `row_action` is `RETRO_ADD`, reduced by the sum of `claimed_capitation_cents` over `MATCHED` rows whose `row_action` is `RETRO_TERM`. That net figure may be negative. All of this is pure integer summation in whole cents with no rounding. Parsing advances the file's `version` by 1.

Reconciliation — `PARSED → RECONCILED`, reserved to an `IpaAdmin` — requires `status` to be `PARSED` and `matched_row_count` to be at least 1, again refusing with `ERR_ENROLLMENT_FILE_EMPTY` when nothing matched. It creates a **capitation remittance** whose `status` is `RECONCILED`, whose `file_id` points at the file, whose `coverage_month` is copied from the file, and whose `reconciliation_method_used` is a snapshot of the agreement's `reconciliation_method` taken at this moment — so a later change to the agreement cannot retroactively alter how a struck remittance was computed. A file may accumulate several remittances across dispute and resubmit cycles, and each is individually addressable.

The remittance's central figure is `expected_capitation_cents`, the amount the IPA believes it is owed for the coverage month, computed independently of anything the payer asserted. The computation begins by identifying the roster. Take the first instant of the coverage month and the last instant of the coverage month, and note the calendar day count of that month. The roster is every enrollee of the file's agreement that has at least one member assignment whose half-open interval, from `effective_date` inclusive up to but excluding `term_date`, overlaps the span from the month's first instant to its last instant — and the assignment counts regardless of its `status`, because the interval itself is the record of when the member occupied a panel slot, so a member who termed mid-month is still owed the days served. Enrollees with no assignment at all, enrollees whose assignments term at or before the month's first instant, enrollees whose assignments begin after the month's last instant, and enrollees of other agreements are all excluded from the roster.

For each roster member the system counts that member's covered days: the number of days in the coverage month such that some qualifying assignment interval of that member contains the day *and* the member's own coverage span, from `coverage_start_date` through `coverage_end_date` clamped to the month, also contains the day. The roster is then walked in ascending `created_at` order — never in identifier order — and a per-member amount is produced, which is where the agreement's two reconciliation conventions diverge.

Under `ROSTER_SNAPSHOT`, a member's amount is the member's full `gross_pmpm_cents` when a qualifying assignment covers the first instant of the month itself, and zero otherwise. Mid-month additions therefore contribute nothing at all, while mid-month terminations contribute the entire month; the covered-day count is ignored for the money amount. The member-month contribution under this convention is 10000 basis points for a member on the roster at the snapshot instant and 0 for any other member.

Under `MEMBER_MONTHS_PRORATED`, a member's amount is that member's `gross_pmpm_cents` multiplied by their covered days and divided by the month's calendar day count, rounded half up to whole cents. Both mid-month additions and mid-month terminations are prorated by days actually served. The member-month contribution under this convention is the member's covered days multiplied by 10000 and divided by the month's calendar day count, rounded half up to whole basis points, so it reflects fractional member months.

Under either convention, `expected_capitation_cents` is the sum of the *already rounded* per-member amounts, and `member_month_count_bps` on the remittance is the sum of the already rounded per-member basis-point contributions. **The order is pinned:** each member's amount is rounded to whole cents first and the rounded amounts are then added. Accumulating unrounded prorated amounts and rounding the total once is incorrect and diverges by cents on realistic rosters. To make the difference concrete, take a coverage month of `2026-07` with 31 days under an agreement whose `base_pmpm_cents` is 30000 and `quality_withhold_bps` is 700, holding three members: an `ADULT` on `HMO` with `gross_pmpm_cents` 37500 assigned for all 31 days, a `SENIOR` on `HMO_PLUS` with `gross_pmpm_cents` 89500 whose assignment starts on 16 July for 16 covered days, and a `PEDIATRIC` on `HMO` with `gross_pmpm_cents` 8000 covered from 1 July for 10 days. Prorated, the per-member amounts are 37500, 46194 and 2581, summing to 86275 — where summing unrounded and rounding once would have produced 86274. Snapshotted, the same roster yields 37500 plus 0 plus 8000, or 45500, because the senior member was not on the roster at the first instant of July. The prorated member-month total is 10000 plus 5161 plus 3226, or 18387 basis points.

With `expected_capitation_cents` in hand, the remittance's remaining money fields are computed as a seven-step waterfall, in this order. First, `claimed_capitation_cents` on the remittance is a snapshot of the file's `claimed_capitation_cents`. Second, `retro_adjustment_cents` is the file's net retroactive figure described above. Third, `variance_cents` is `claimed_capitation_cents` plus `retro_adjustment_cents`, less `expected_capitation_cents`; it may be negative, and a negative variance means the payer asserted less than the IPA computed. Fourth, `withhold_cents` is `expected_capitation_cents` multiplied by the agreement's `quality_withhold_bps` as a proportion of 10000, rounded half up to whole cents. Fifth, `net_payable_cents` is `expected_capitation_cents` less `withhold_cents` plus `retro_adjustment_cents`. Sixth, `institutional_allocation_cents` is `net_payable_cents` multiplied by the agreement's `institutional_pool_share_bps` as a proportion of 10000, rounded half up to whole cents. Seventh, `professional_allocation_cents` is `net_payable_cents` less `institutional_allocation_cents` — the professional bucket is the residual and absorbs the rounding remainder, which guarantees the two allocations always re-sum to `net_payable_cents` with zero drift.

Two properties of that waterfall are contractual. The withhold is rounded exactly once, at remittance level, on the expected total; it is emphatically **not** the sum of the per-enrollee `withhold_cents` values computed in §3.3, and it is equally **not** the sum of a withhold taken on each per-member amount separately. Those quantities differ from the remittance-level figure on a realistic roster, and only the remittance-level figure is authoritative. And exactly two rounding steps occur across the whole waterfall — the withhold and the institutional allocation — with the residual absorbing everything else. Working the earlier example through: with `expected_capitation_cents` 86275, a claimed total of 80081, no retroactive adjustment, `quality_withhold_bps` 700, `institutional_pool_share_bps` 4000 and `professional_pool_share_bps` 6000, the variance is negative 6194, the withhold is 6039 — where rounding a withhold on each of the three per-member amounts separately and summing those would have given 6040, and the sum of the three enrollees' own `withhold_cents` fields from §3.3 would have given 9450, both of them divergent and wrong answers — `net_payable_cents` is 80236, `institutional_allocation_cents` is 32094, and `professional_allocation_cents` is 48142.

Reconciliation closes by accruing: the agreement's `accrued_capitation_cents` increases by the new remittance's `expected_capitation_cents` plus its `retro_adjustment_cents`. Both the file's `version` and the agreement's `version` advance by 1.

A payer that disagrees pushes back. The agreement's `PayerRepresentative` takes the file `RECONCILED → DISPUTED`, supplying a `dispute_reason`, permitted whenever `status` is `RECONCILED`. This is the compensating action for reconciliation and the conserved quantity is the agreement's `accrued_capitation_cents`. Four things happen: the file's `status` becomes `DISPUTED`; the file's remittance whose `status` is `RECONCILED` moves to `DISPUTED`, advancing that remittance's `version` by 1; the agreement's `accrued_capitation_cents` decreases by exactly that remittance's `expected_capitation_cents` plus its `retro_adjustment_cents`, precisely reversing the accrual; and the agreement's `capitation_posture` is re-derived, which — because a disputed remittance now exists — makes it read `WITHHELD`. The file's `version` and the agreement's `version` each advance by 1.

Resubmission reopens the cycle. The `PayerRepresentative` takes the file `DISPUTED → RECEIVED`, permitted whenever `status` is `DISPUTED`. The file's `status` returns to `RECEIVED`; `matched_row_count`, `exception_row_count` and `claimed_capitation_cents` are all reset to 0; and every row's `match_status` resets to `UNMATCHED` so that a subsequent parse recomputes them from scratch. The disputed remittance is retained as history and is **not** reopened — it stays `DISPUTED` forever, and a later reconciliation creates an entirely new remittance rather than reviving the old one. The agreement's `capitation_posture` is re-derived, and the file's `version` advances by 1. The full cycle is therefore `RECEIVED → PARSED → RECONCILED → DISPUTED → RECEIVED`, and it may run more than once for the same coverage month.

Posting is the terminal move and the deepest cascade in the capitation half of the system. An `IpaAdmin` takes the file `RECONCILED → POSTED`, permitted when `status` is `RECONCILED` and the agreement's `status` is `ACTIVE`. Five levels then run in order. At the first level the file's `status` becomes `POSTED` and its `RECONCILED` remittance moves to `POSTED`, advancing that remittance's `version` by 1. At the second level, because that remittance became `POSTED`, the money is routed: each risk pool of the agreement whose incurral period contains the first instant of the remittance's `coverage_month` is funded, with a `PROFESSIONAL` pool's `funded_cents` increasing by the remittance's `professional_allocation_cents`, an `INSTITUTIONAL` pool's `funded_cents` increasing by the remittance's `institutional_allocation_cents`, and both pools' `member_months_bps` increasing by the remittance's `member_month_count_bps`; at the same level the agreement's `withheld_balance_cents` increases by the remittance's `withhold_cents`. At the third level, because each funded pool's `funded_cents` changed, that pool's `loss_ratio_bps` is recomputed and its `deficit_flag` re-evaluated. At the fourth level, if any pool's `deficit_flag` has become true, the agreement's `capitation_posture` is re-derived to `UNDER_REVIEW`. At the fifth level, because the posture has left `CURRENT`, every physician of the agreement whose `panel_status` is `OPEN` is set to `CLOSED_TO_NEW`. The file's `version`, the agreement's `version`, and the `version` of every mutated risk pool and every mutated physician all advance by 1. `POSTED` is terminal for a file, and `DISPUTED` and `POSTED` are both terminal for a remittance.

A pool's loss ratio is defined as follows and is recomputed on every movement of either `funded_cents` or `paid_claims_cents`. When `funded_cents` is zero, `loss_ratio_bps` is 0. Otherwise `loss_ratio_bps` is `paid_claims_cents` multiplied by 10000 and divided by `funded_cents`, rounded half up to whole basis points. The `deficit_flag` is then true when `loss_ratio_bps` is **strictly greater** than the deficit threshold of 9500 basis points — that is, when the pool has paid out more than ninety-five percent of what funded it. Equality does not flag: 20000 cents funded against 19000 cents paid gives exactly 9500 basis points and leaves `deficit_flag` false, while 20000 funded against 19001 paid gives 9501 basis points and sets it true.

A remittance is never driven directly by a caller — its two transitions, `RECONCILED → DISPUTED` and `RECONCILED → POSTED`, are consequences of the parent file's dispute and posting respectively, each permitted only when the remittance's `status` is `RECONCILED`, and each advancing the remittance's `version` by 1. Files may be listed under their agreement with optional `status` and `coverage_month` filters; rows may be listed under their file with optional `match_status` and `row_action` filters; the remittances produced by one file across its dispute and resubmit cycles may be listed with an optional `status` filter; and an individual remittance read exposes every computed money field, `member_month_count_bps`, and the `reconciliation_method_used` that produced them.

### 3.6. Delegated Adjudication of Downstream Claims

When the IPA holds delegated claims authority it adjudicates and pays the downstream providers who actually rendered care. A `ClaimsExaminer` or `IpaAdmin` intakes a **downstream claim** by supplying `agreement_id`, `enrollee_id`, `attributed_physician_id` — the panel physician the claim is attributed to for shared-savings purposes — a `pool_type` of `PROFESSIONAL` or `INSTITUTIONAL`, the rendering provider in `downstream_provider_name`, the `billed_cents` amount which must be at least 1, and the `service_date`. Intake resolves `pool_id` by finding the risk pool of that agreement whose `pool_type` matches the claim's `pool_type` and whose closed incurral period contains `service_date`; when no such pool exists the intake is refused with `ERR_NO_POOL_FOR_SERVICE_DATE`. On success `received_date` is stamped from the logical clock, `status` is `RECEIVED`, `allowed_cents` and `paid_cents` are both null, `denial_reason` is null, and `appeal_count` is 0.

Adjudication forks. A `ClaimsExaminer` may take the claim `RECEIVED → ADJUDICATED_APPROVED`, supplying `allowed_cents`; the branch is permitted when `status` is `RECEIVED`, when the agreement's `delegation_scope` is any of `CLAIMS_ONLY`, `CLAIMS_AND_UM` or `FULL_RISK`, and when `allowed_cents` is at most the claim's `billed_cents`, with an excess refused as `ERR_ALLOWED_EXCEEDS_BILLED`. Approval records `allowed_cents`, sets `denial_reason` to null, and advances the claim's `version` by 1; no pool ledger moves on this branch. Alternatively the examiner may take the claim `RECEIVED → ADJUDICATED_DENIED`, supplying a `denial_reason` of `NOT_COVERED`, `DUPLICATE` or `NO_AUTHORIZATION`; the branch is permitted whenever `status` is `RECEIVED`. Denial records the `denial_reason`, sets `allowed_cents` to 0 — note that this is zero, not null, which is what distinguishes a denied claim from a never-adjudicated one — and advances the `version` by 1. No pool ledger movement occurs on this branch either.

A denial may be appealed twice and no more. A `NetworkManager` or the attributed `PhysicianUser` takes the claim `ADJUDICATED_DENIED → RECEIVED`, permitted when `status` is `ADJUDICATED_DENIED` and `appeal_count` is strictly less than the appeal limit of 2. The appeal sets `status` back to `RECEIVED`, increases `appeal_count` by 1, clears `denial_reason` to null, and clears `allowed_cents` back to null so the re-adjudication starts clean. A third appeal — one attempted while `appeal_count` already reads 2 — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. A claim exhausted in this way may be closed out: a `ClaimsExaminer` takes `ADJUDICATED_DENIED → VOIDED`, permitted when `status` is `ADJUDICATED_DENIED` and `appeal_count` is exactly 2, which sets `status` to `VOIDED` with no ledger movement whatsoever because the claim never paid.

Payment is where the pool ledgers move, and it runs four levels deep. A `ClaimsExaminer` takes the claim `ADJUDICATED_APPROVED → PAID`, permitted when `status` is `ADJUDICATED_APPROVED` and the claim's resolved pool has a `status` other than `SETTLED`. First, `status` becomes `PAID` and `paid_cents` is set equal to `allowed_cents`. Second, because `paid_cents` was set, the pool's `paid_claims_cents` increases by that amount and the pool's `loss_ratio_bps` is recomputed per the definition in §3.5. Third, because the pool's paid ledger moved, the enrollee's `cumulative_paid_claims_cents` increases by the same amount, and when that enrollee's paid total within this pool comes to strictly exceed the agreement's `stop_loss_attachment_cents` for the first time, the pool's `stop_loss_eligible_enrollee_count` increases by 1. Fourth, because `loss_ratio_bps` changed, `deficit_flag` is re-evaluated as strictly greater than 9500 basis points, and when it becomes true the agreement's `capitation_posture` is re-derived to `UNDER_REVIEW` — which, per §2.9, closes every `OPEN` panel of that agreement. The claim's `version`, the pool's `version` and the enrollee's `version` each advance by 1.

Voiding a paid claim is the compensating action for payment, and the conserved quantity is the pool's `paid_claims_cents`. A `ClaimsExaminer` takes the claim `PAID → VOIDED`, permitted when `status` is `PAID` and the resolved pool's `status` is either `ACCUMULATING` or `CLOSED_FOR_INCURRAL` — once a pool has reserved, its paid triangle is frozen and recoupment through this path is no longer available. The void removes exactly what the payment added: the pool's `paid_claims_cents` decreases by `paid_cents`, the enrollee's `cumulative_paid_claims_cents` decreases by `paid_cents`, and only then is `paid_cents` itself cleared to null. The pool's `loss_ratio_bps`, `deficit_flag` and `stop_loss_eligible_enrollee_count` are all recomputed, and the agreement's `capitation_posture` is re-derived. The claim's `version`, the pool's `version` and the enrollee's `version` each advance by 1. `VOIDED` is terminal.

Only claims whose `status` is `PAID` contribute to a pool's `paid_claims_cents` or to an enrollee's `cumulative_paid_claims_cents`. Claims sitting in `RECEIVED`, `ADJUDICATED_APPROVED`, `ADJUDICATED_DENIED` or `VOIDED` contribute nothing — an approved-but-unpaid claim is an obligation, not a payment. Claims may be listed with optional `agreement_id`, `enrollee_id`, `attributed_physician_id`, `pool_id` and `status` filters, and an individual read exposes the adjudication and payment fields including `received_date`, `allowed_cents`, `paid_cents`, `denial_reason` and `appeal_count`.

### 3.7. Risk Pool Accounting: Incurral, Reserving and Reinsurance Recovery

A **risk pool** is the accounting container for one incurral period and one side of the professional/institutional split. An `IpaAdmin` opens one under an agreement by supplying `pool_type`, either `PROFESSIONAL` or `INSTITUTIONAL`, together with `pool_year_start` as the inclusive first instant of the incurral period and `pool_year_end` as its inclusive last instant. The `agreement_id` comes from the creation path. A new pool begins in `status` `ACCUMULATING` with every ledger counter at zero — `funded_cents`, `member_months_bps`, `paid_claims_cents`, `ibnr_reserve_cents`, `stop_loss_recovery_cents`, `stop_loss_eligible_enrollee_count`, `loss_ratio_bps`, `raw_surplus_cents`, `admin_fee_cents`, `distributable_surplus_cents` and `shared_savings_pool_cents` all 0, `deficit_flag` false, and both `ibnr_method_used` and `surplus_tier` null. Its `settlement_due_date` is derived at creation as `pool_year_end` advanced by the settlement lag for its `pool_type`: 180 days for a `PROFESSIONAL` pool, reflecting the shorter professional claim tail, and 270 days for an `INSTITUTIONAL` pool. Two pools of the same agreement and the same `pool_type` may not have overlapping periods under the closed-interval semantics of §2.11, and a collision is refused with `ERR_POOL_PERIOD_OVERLAP`.

Closing incurral is the first move: an `IpaAdmin` takes the pool `ACCUMULATING → CLOSED_FOR_INCURRAL`, permitted when `status` is `ACCUMULATING` and the logical clock has reached `pool_year_end`. Attempting to close early is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Closing incurral does not close the claim window: claims with a `service_date` inside the period may still be intaken, adjudicated and paid after this point, because only new incurral accounting is closed, not the reporting of already-incurred service.

Reserving is where the pool takes a position on claims nobody has seen yet. An `IpaAdmin` takes the pool `CLOSED_FOR_INCURRAL → RESERVED`, optionally supplying `ibnr_method_override`; the transition is permitted when `status` is `CLOSED_FOR_INCURRAL`. The method actually applied is written to `ibnr_method_used`: the supplied `ibnr_method_override` when one is present, otherwise the agreement's default `ibnr_method`. The reserve is then computed under that method and stored in `ibnr_reserve_cents`, and the pool's `version` advances by 1. Reserving may be repeated as often as the finance team likes — the pool supports a self-loop from `RESERVED` back to `RESERVED`, permitted when `status` is `RESERVED`, which recomputes `ibnr_method_used` and `ibnr_reserve_cents` in exactly the same way. Re-reserving the same pool data under the other method genuinely changes the answer, which is the point of the loop.

Only claims whose `status` is `PAID` count as reported for reserving purposes. Claims sitting in `RECEIVED`, `ADJUDICATED_APPROVED`, `ADJUDICATED_DENIED` or `VOIDED` are excluded even though a received claim is literally already known, because the reserve models the unreported tail relative to the *paid* triangle rather than relative to the intake queue. Under the `COMPLETION_FACTOR` method, an ultimate figure is computed as the pool's `paid_claims_cents` multiplied by 10000 and divided by the agreement's `ibnr_completion_factor_bps`, rounded half up to whole cents; the reserve is that ultimate figure less `paid_claims_cents`, floored at zero so it is never negative. This method scales the observed paid triangle up to ultimate and is entirely independent of enrolment volume, so a pool with few member months and heavy paid claims reserves a large tail. Under the `PMPM_LOSS_RATIO` method, an expected figure is computed as the agreement's `target_claims_pmpm_cents` multiplied by the pool's `member_months_bps` as a proportion of 10000, rounded half up to whole cents; the reserve is that expected figure less `paid_claims_cents`, again floored at zero. This method anchors on exposure rather than on the paid triangle, so when paid claims already exceed the exposure-based expectation the reserve collapses to zero. Each variant performs a single rounding step and then applies the zero floor.

The divergence is material. A pool with `funded_cents` 20000, `paid_claims_cents` 13520 and `member_months_bps` 10000, under an agreement with `ibnr_completion_factor_bps` 8000 and `target_claims_pmpm_cents` 15000, reserves 3380 cents under `COMPLETION_FACTOR` — an ultimate of 16900 less the 13520 already paid — and 1480 cents under `PMPM_LOSS_RATIO` — an expected 15000 less the same 13520. Same pool, same claims, two reserves differing by more than a factor of two, and therefore two entirely different settlement waterfalls.

Reinsurance sits alongside the pool and responds to individual catastrophic members. A `ClaimsExaminer` opens a **stop-loss recovery** by supplying `pool_id` and `enrollee_id`, nothing more; every money figure on the record is derived at creation. `attachment_cents` is snapshotted from the agreement's `stop_loss_attachment_cents` so a later contract amendment cannot retroactively move an already-filed recovery's attachment point. `eligible_paid_cents` is the sum of `paid_cents` across downstream claims for that same enrollee in that same pool whose `status` is `PAID`, evaluated at the moment of creation; claims for the same member in the *other* pool type's pool never cross-apply, and voided claims drop out of the sum entirely. `excess_cents` is `eligible_paid_cents` less `attachment_cents`, floored at zero. `recovery_cents` is `excess_cents` multiplied by the agreement's `reinsurance_recovery_bps` as a proportion of 10000, rounded half up to whole cents in a single rounding step. The record begins in `status` `DRAFT` with `submitted_by_user_id`, `adjudicated_by_user_id` and `denial_reason` all null.

The attachment point is pierced only when the eligible paid total is **strictly** greater than the attachment amount. Exactly at attachment the excess is zero and the create is refused with `ERR_STOP_LOSS_NOT_ATTACHED`. With an attachment of 5000000 cents and `reinsurance_recovery_bps` of 9000, an eligible paid total of 5000000 is refused outright; a total of 5000001 yields an excess of 1 cent and a recovery of 1 cent, because nine tenths of a cent rounds half up to a whole cent; and a total of 6000000 yields an excess of 1000000 and a recovery of 900000.

The recovery then walks a four-state approval chain with segregation of duties at its centre. The originating `ClaimsExaminer` takes it `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`, permitted when `status` is `DRAFT` and `excess_cents` is greater than zero; the transition records the acting `X-User-ID` in `submitted_by_user_id`. A `ReinsuranceUnderwriter` then takes it `SUBMITTED → APPROVED`, permitted when `status` is `SUBMITTED`, when the acting `X-User-ID` differs from `submitted_by_user_id`, and when the recovery's pool has a `status` of `ACCUMULATING`, `CLOSED_FOR_INCURRAL` or `RESERVED` — a pool already at `SETTLEMENT_PROPOSED` or `SETTLED` will not absorb a new recovery. Approval records the acting identifier in `adjudicated_by_user_id` and increases the pool's `stop_loss_recovery_cents` by the recovery's `recovery_cents`, advancing both the recovery's `version` and the pool's `version` by 1. Alternatively the underwriter takes it `SUBMITTED → DENIED`, permitted when `status` is `SUBMITTED` and the acting `X-User-ID` differs from `submitted_by_user_id`, recording `adjudicated_by_user_id` and a `denial_reason` of `BELOW_ATTACHMENT`, `LATE_FILING` or `POOL_SETTLED`, with no ledger movement at all. Finally an `IpaAdmin` takes an approved recovery `APPROVED → RECOVERED` once the cash actually arrives, permitted when `status` is `APPROVED`; this changes `status` only and explicitly does **not** move `stop_loss_recovery_cents` again, because the amount was already recognised at approval. Both `DENIED` and `RECOVERED` are terminal. Only recoveries whose `status` is `APPROVED` or `RECOVERED` contribute to a pool's `stop_loss_recovery_cents`; `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` and `DENIED` recoveries contribute nothing.

Before committing to a settlement, an `IpaAdmin` or the agreement's `PayerRepresentative` may ask for a settlement preview — a computed, read-only projection that re-runs the settlement waterfall against the pool's current ledger without changing any state, so that the pre-fee tier decision is observable before a proposal is made. The preview accepts an optional `ibnr_method_override` so the effect of the other reserving convention can be inspected, and it returns `gross_claims_cents`, `stop_loss_recovery_cents`, `ibnr_reserve_cents`, `net_claims_cents`, `raw_surplus_cents`, `surplus_ratio_bps`, `surplus_tier`, `admin_fee_bps`, `admin_fee_cents`, `distributable_surplus_cents` and `shared_savings_pool_cents`. Two of those figures, `surplus_ratio_bps` and `admin_fee_bps`, exist only on this preview output: they are not fields of a `RiskPool`, they are never persisted by the settlement proposal of §3.8, and they do not appear in a pool's own representation. Pools may be listed under their agreement with optional `pool_type` and `status` filters, and an individual read exposes the full funding, paid-claims, IBNR, recovery, loss-ratio and settlement picture.

### 3.8. Quality Scorecards, Settlement Proposal and Physician Distribution

Shared savings and withhold release both depend on measured panel performance, which arrives as a **quality scorecard**. An `IpaAdmin` or the agreement's `PayerRepresentative` records one by supplying `physician_id`, a `measurement_period` given as exactly four digits denoting a calendar year, three performance figures each expressed in basis points between 0 and 10000 inclusive — `hedis_composite_bps` for HEDIS composite performance, `access_score_bps` for access to care, and `patient_experience_bps` for patient experience — plus `attributed_member_months`, the member months the payer attributes to that physician for the period, which must be zero or more, and the `expected_physician_version` guard of §2.8. A new scorecard begins in `status` `DRAFT` with `composite_score_bps` 0 and `withhold_release_bps` 0. Creating it has two side effects on its surroundings: any earlier scorecard for the same physician and the same `measurement_period` that is still in `DRAFT` moves to `SUPERSEDED`, advancing that scorecard's `version` by 1; and the physician's `latest_scorecard_period` is updated to the new `measurement_period`, advancing the physician's `version` by 1.

Finalisation is reserved to an `IpaAdmin`, who takes the scorecard `DRAFT → FINAL`, permitted when `status` is `DRAFT` and the subject physician's `credential_status` is either `ACTIVE` or `NOTICE_GIVEN`. Finalisation computes `composite_score_bps` as a weighted blend of the three inputs, with weights that add to exactly 10000 basis points: HEDIS carries 5000 basis points of weight, patient experience 3000, and access 2000. Each input is scaled by its weight as a proportion of 10000 and the three scaled terms are accumulated **unrounded**; the accumulated sum is rounded half up to whole basis points exactly once, at the end, so no per-term rounding drift can creep into the composite. `withhold_release_bps` is then read off the composite in bands, each band using an at-or-above comparison: a composite of at least 8500 basis points releases 10000 basis points, meaning the physician's entire withhold share; at least 7000 releases 7500; at least 5500 releases 5000; and anything below 5500 releases nothing at all. Because each band is inclusive at its lower edge, a composite of exactly 8500 releases the full withhold. A scorecard with `hedis_composite_bps` 9000, `access_score_bps` 8000 and `patient_experience_bps` 7000 accumulates 4500 plus 1600 plus 2100 for a composite of 8200 basis points and a `withhold_release_bps` of 7500. A scorecard reading 7000 on all three inputs accumulates 3500 plus 1400 plus 2100 for a composite of exactly 7000, which sits on the band edge and therefore also releases 7500. A scorecard reading 8500 on all three inputs composites to exactly 8500 and therefore releases 10000.

Finalisation also reaches outward. Any *other* scorecard of the same physician with the same `measurement_period` whose `status` is `FINAL` moves to `SUPERSEDED`, and the physician's `quality_composite_bps` is set to this scorecard's `composite_score_bps`. Both the scorecard's `version` and the physician's `version` advance by 1. `SUPERSEDED` is terminal; only a scorecard whose `status` is `FINAL` ever contributes to a physician's `quality_composite_bps`, and `DRAFT` and `SUPERSEDED` scorecards never do. A physician's scorecards may be listed with optional `status` and `measurement_period` filters, and an individual read exposes the computed `composite_score_bps` and `withhold_release_bps`.

Proposing a settlement is the moment all of this converges. An `IpaAdmin` takes the pool `RESERVED → SETTLEMENT_PROPOSED`, permitted when `status` is `RESERVED` and when every physician of the agreement whose `credential_status` is `ACTIVE` or `NOTICE_GIVEN` holds a scorecard whose `status` is `FINAL` and whose `measurement_period` equals the four-digit year of the pool's `pool_year_start`. A physician in scope whose only scorecard for that year is `DRAFT` or `SUPERSEDED` blocks the proposal outright rather than being quietly skipped, and the refusal is `ERR_SCORECARD_NOT_FINAL`.

The proposal first runs a nine-step settlement waterfall over the pool, in this pinned order. Step one takes the pool's `paid_claims_cents` as the gross claims figure. Step two takes the pool's `stop_loss_recovery_cents` as the recovery figure. Step three computes net claims as gross claims less recoveries, plus the pool's `ibnr_reserve_cents`. Step four computes `raw_surplus_cents` as the pool's `funded_cents` less that net claims figure; it may be negative. Step five derives the surplus ratio: the ratio is 0 basis points when `raw_surplus_cents` is zero or negative or when `funded_cents` is zero, and otherwise it is `raw_surplus_cents` multiplied by 10000 and divided by `funded_cents`, **truncated toward zero rather than rounded**. `surplus_tier` is then assigned from that ratio: `NONE` when `raw_surplus_cents` is zero or negative; `TIER_1` when the ratio is at most 500 basis points; `TIER_2` when the ratio is above 500 and at most 1500; and `TIER_3` when the ratio exceeds 1500. Because the ratio is truncated, a surplus ratio computing to 1500.6 basis points is treated as 1500 and stays in `TIER_2`. Step six looks up the administrative fee rate for the tier: 0 basis points for `NONE`, 300 for `TIER_1`, 600 for `TIER_2` and 1000 for `TIER_3`. Step seven computes `admin_fee_cents` as `raw_surplus_cents` floored at zero, multiplied by that fee rate as a proportion of 10000, rounded half up to whole cents. Step eight computes `distributable_surplus_cents` as `raw_surplus_cents` floored at zero, less `admin_fee_cents`. Step nine computes `shared_savings_pool_cents` as `distributable_surplus_cents` multiplied by the tier's shared-savings share — 0 basis points for `NONE`, 4000 for `TIER_1`, 5000 for `TIER_2` and 6000 for `TIER_3` — as a proportion of 10000, rounded half up to whole cents. Exactly two rounding steps occur, at step seven and step nine, and the ratio at step five is truncated.

**The step order is contractual.** `surplus_tier` is keyed on the *pre-administrative-fee* ratio computed at step five. Recomputing the ratio after the fee has been deducted — that is, re-tiering on `distributable_surplus_cents` against `funded_cents` — is forbidden, and it produces a different tier, a different share rate and a different `shared_savings_pool_cents` on identical data. A pool with `funded_cents` 20000, `paid_claims_cents` 13520, no stop-loss recovery and an `ibnr_reserve_cents` of 3380 has net claims of 16900 and a `raw_surplus_cents` of 3100. The pre-fee ratio truncates to 1550 basis points, placing the pool in `TIER_3`, so the fee rate is 1000 basis points and `admin_fee_cents` is 310. `distributable_surplus_cents` is 2790 and `shared_savings_pool_cents` is 1674. Re-tiering after the fee would have produced a ratio of 1395, a `TIER_2` classification, a 5000 basis-point share and an answer of 1395 — different and wrong.

Immediately after the waterfall, the proposal creates one **pool distribution** per qualifying physician, each in `status` `PROPOSED`, with `pool_id` and `physician_id` set by this cascade. The participant set is the physicians of the pool's agreement whose `credential_status` is `ACTIVE` or `NOTICE_GIVEN` and who hold a `FINAL` scorecard for a `measurement_period` equal to the four-digit year of `pool_year_start`, ordered by `created_at` ascending and then by `npi` ascending — never by identifier. Physicians in `credential_status` `APPLICANT`, `CREDENTIALED` or `EXITED` receive no distribution even when they happen to hold a `FINAL` scorecard for the year.

Each participant's distribution snapshots two figures at proposal time: `composite_score_bps_snapshot` takes the physician's current `quality_composite_bps`, and `withhold_release_bps_snapshot` takes the `withhold_release_bps` of the `FINAL` scorecard used. Each participant's `panel_member_months_bps` is the sum of `member_month_count_bps` contributed by the pool's `POSTED` remittances, attributed to that participant through the enrollees whose qualifying assignment in the relevant coverage month was held by that participant. Two weightings are then derived from those figures: the withhold weighting is `panel_member_months_bps` on its own, and the shared-savings weighting is `panel_member_months_bps` multiplied by `composite_score_bps_snapshot`.

Both money quantities are split by the same largest-remainder procedure, run twice with different weightings. For a given total and set of weights, each participant's exact share is the total multiplied by that participant's weight and divided by the sum of all weights, held as an unrounded rational value. Each participant is first given the floor of their exact share. The leftover — the total less the sum of those floors — is then handed out one cent at a time to the participants with the largest fractional parts of their exact shares, ties broken by the participant ordering above, `created_at` ascending and then `npi` ascending, never by identifier and never randomly. The procedure is exact by construction: the allocated shares always sum to precisely the total being distributed.

Run first against the agreement's `withheld_balance_cents` using the withhold weighting, the procedure produces each participant's `withhold_share_cents`. From that, `withhold_release_cents` is `withhold_share_cents` multiplied by `withhold_release_bps_snapshot` as a proportion of 10000, rounded half up to whole cents — this is the one rounding step in the distribution calculation, and the portion not released is what will later be forfeited. Run second against the pool's `shared_savings_pool_cents` using the shared-savings weighting, the procedure produces each participant's `shared_savings_cents`. `net_distribution_cents` is `withhold_release_cents` plus `shared_savings_cents`. As an illustration of the remainder handling, suppose `shared_savings_pool_cents` is 3348 and there are two participants, the first with `panel_member_months_bps` 10000 and a snapshotted composite of 8200 and the second with `panel_member_months_bps` 10000 and a snapshotted composite of 7000. Their shared-savings weights are 82000000 and 70000000, so their exact shares are 1806 and three nineteenths of a cent, and 1541 and sixteen nineteenths of a cent. The floors are 1806 and 1541, summing to 3347 and leaving one cent over; the larger fractional part, sixteen nineteenths against three nineteenths, belongs to the second participant, so the final allocation is 1806 and 1542, summing to exactly 3348.

The proposal sets the pool's `status` to `SETTLEMENT_PROPOSED` and advances its `version` by 1. A payer that objects may unwind it: the agreement's `PayerRepresentative` takes the pool `SETTLEMENT_PROPOSED → RESERVED`, supplying a `dispute_reason`, permitted whenever `status` is `SETTLEMENT_PROPOSED`. Every distribution of that pool whose `status` is `PROPOSED` moves to `VOIDED`, each advancing its own `version` by 1; `surplus_tier` returns to null and `admin_fee_cents`, `distributable_surplus_cents`, `shared_savings_pool_cents` and `raw_surplus_cents` all return to 0; but `ibnr_reserve_cents` and `ibnr_method_used` are deliberately **retained**, because the reserve was an independent actuarial judgement and the dispute concerns the distribution, not the reserve. A re-proposal recomputes the entire waterfall from whatever the ledger holds at that later moment. The dispute cycle `RESERVED → SETTLEMENT_PROPOSED → RESERVED` may run repeatedly.

Settlement closes the pool. An `IpaAdmin` takes it `SETTLEMENT_PROPOSED → SETTLED`, permitted when `status` is `SETTLEMENT_PROPOSED` and the logical clock has reached the pool's `settlement_due_date`; an attempt before that instant is refused with `ERR_SETTLEMENT_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN`. Every distribution of the pool whose `status` is `PROPOSED` moves to `PAID`, each advancing its own `version` by 1. The agreement's `withheld_balance_cents` then decreases by the sum of those distributions' `withhold_share_cents`, and the agreement's `forfeited_withhold_cents` increases by the sum, over those same distributions, of `withhold_share_cents` less `withhold_release_cents` — the portion of each physician's withhold share that quality performance did not earn back, which is what `forfeited_withhold_cents` accumulates whenever a `withhold_release_bps_snapshot` fell short of 10000. The pool's `status` becomes `SETTLED`, and both the pool's `version` and the agreement's `version` advance by 1. `SETTLED` is terminal for a pool, and both `VOIDED` and `PAID` are terminal for a distribution; neither distribution transition is ever driven directly by a caller, and each is permitted only while the distribution's `status` is `PROPOSED`. Distributions may be listed with optional `pool_id`, `physician_id` and `status` filters, and an individual read exposes `panel_member_months_bps`, `composite_score_bps_snapshot`, `withhold_release_bps_snapshot`, `withhold_share_cents`, `withhold_release_cents`, `shared_savings_cents` and `net_distribution_cents`.
