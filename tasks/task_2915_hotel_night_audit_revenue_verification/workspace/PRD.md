# Hotel Night Audit & Revenue Verification API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Every hotel accounting department lives or dies by the night audit: the nightly sequence in which one operating day is frozen, its room and tax charges are raised, its cash and card takings are reconciled against what the point-of-sale systems actually recorded, its rebates and comp rooms are checked against signed paperwork, and its revenue is published to management. Done by hand, the sequence is brittle. Room charges get posted twice; a remote food-and-beverage terminal transmits a check after the shift it belongs to has already been counted; an allowance is approved by the same auditor who requested it; a one-sided ledger transfer leaves the house account and the city ledger disagreeing by a few cents that nobody finds until month end. The consequences are not merely clerical — a day that closes with a wrong total propagates into the general-ledger tie-out, and the tie-out is what the external auditor reads.

This document specifies the Hotel Night Audit & Revenue Verification API, a back-office service that turns that sequence into an enforced pipeline. The service owns the operating day as a first-class record, drives it through a fixed close sequence, and refuses to advance it while any of its supporting evidence is unresolved. Guest accounts carry two independent lifecycles — where the guest is in their stay, and how much of their account has been settled — because in practice these move on completely different triggers: a guest can depart while still owing money, and a settled account can be driven back into arrears by a late outlet charge. Outlet takings are reconciled window by window against the checks whose transmission instants fall inside them. Allowances are tiered by amount, are never approvable by their own requester, and are corrected by compensating entries rather than by deletion. All monetary movement is recorded in a single append-only journal in which nothing is ever mutated or removed.

Two design commitments shape everything below. First, **evidence before advancement**: the day cannot balance while a settlement batch is unresolved, while a declined card authorization has no follow-up outcome, or while a hand-posted transfer has left the ledgers off zero; the month cannot tie out while any of its days is still open. Second, **segregation of duties over convenience**: the operator who requests a rebate may not grant it, the operator who declares a cash drawer may not accept its variance, the operator who files approval paperwork may not approve it, and the operator who posted a journal line may not reverse it. Where those two commitments make an operation slower, that is the intended outcome.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contract-level rules that hold everywhere in the API: how callers identify themselves, what every response looks like, how lists are paged, how money and time are handled, and which system-wide invariants the service maintains regardless of which endpoint is being called. All endpoints other than the administrative clock capability are mounted under the `/night-audit` prefix.

### 2.1. Operator Identity and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting operator, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which they are acting. Both must be present and non-empty; a request that omits either, or that supplies either as an empty string, is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). The same rejection applies when `X-User-Role` carries a string that is not one of the six roles the service recognizes — an unknown role name fails as an authentication problem, not as an authorization problem.

The six roles are `PropertyAdmin`, `NightAuditor`, `FrontDeskAgent`, `OutletManager`, `RevenueManager`, and `Controller`. `PropertyAdmin` sets up the property, its revenue capture points and its ledger accounts, and holds no operational authority over any business day — it cannot post, approve, or close anything. `NightAuditor` drives the daily close sequence end to end and may raise rebates but approve only those in the lowest tier. `FrontDeskAgent` handles arrivals, departures, tenders and card authorizations, may raise a rebate but never decide one, and may never touch the close sequence. `OutletManager` runs one revenue capture point. `RevenueManager` reviews rebates, rate and comp paperwork, settlement variances and the daily revenue report, sitting above `NightAuditor` and below `Controller` on the approval ladder. `Controller` is financial control: it owns the reopen, the paperwork revocation and the month-end tie-out. When the role in `X-User-Role` is not listed against the endpoint being called, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The approval ladder `NightAuditor` below `RevenueManager` below `Controller` governs one thing only — which role may approve a given rebate — and does **not** make `Controller` a superset of the other roles. `Controller` cannot run the close sequence, cannot check a guest in, and cannot close an outlet check. Conversely, an operator's role authority never overrides segregation of duties: where a rule bars an operator from deciding a record they originated, that bar applies even when their role is otherwise entitled to decide it.

Identity semantics differ by role, and this is what ties `X-User-ID` to resource ownership. When `X-User-Role` is `OutletManager`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Outlet`: the value must resolve to an existing `Outlet` row, and any request that does not is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). Ownership checks for that role compare the `outlet_id` recorded on the addressed check or settlement batch against `X-User-ID`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_OUTLET_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). When `X-User-Role` is `PropertyAdmin`, `NightAuditor`, `FrontDeskAgent`, `RevenueManager`, or `Controller`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it on the rows those operators touch — as `posted_by_user_id` on a journal line, `requested_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` on a rebate, `declared_by_user_id` and `accepted_by_user_id` on a settlement batch, `filed_by_user_id` and `reviewed_by_user_id` on approval paperwork, `resolved_by_user_id` on a card authorization follow-up, `closed_by_user_id` on an outlet check, `generated_by_user_id` on a revenue report, and `tied_out_by_user_id` on an accounting period — but it never compares those values against any entity's `id`.

Every read endpoint in the API is open to all six roles. Write authority is enumerated per endpoint in §4.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints are paged through two query parameters. `limit` sets the maximum number of rows returned; it defaults to 10 when the caller omits it, may not be below 1, and may not exceed 100. `offset` sets how many rows to skip before the page begins and defaults to 0. Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are ordered by the resource's `created_at` timestamp in ascending order, so that a stable page walk reflects the order in which records entered the system.

The `meta` object of every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more`, where `total_count` counts the rows matching the request's filters before paging and `has_more` reports whether rows remain beyond the current page.

### 2.4. Record Identity, Timestamps and Versioning

Every resource the API exposes carries an `id`: a server-minted UUID with no type prefix, which is also the value that appears wherever a path names `{id}`. Alongside it, every resource carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the row was written.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at` — an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, refreshed on every mutation of the row — and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation, including mutations the server performs on its own initiative as a declared side effect of some other operation. This applies to `HotelProperty`, `Outlet`, `AccountingPeriod`, `BusinessDate`, `Folio`, `OutletTicket`, `SettlementBatch`, `LedgerAccount`, `Allowance`, `AuthorizationDocument`, `CardAuthorization`, and `RevenueReport`. Re-derivation of a computed money or statistics field counts as a mutation for this purpose: when a journal line changes a folio's balance, that folio's `updated_at` moves and its `version` increments.

The journal is the exception. A `JournalPosting` row carries only `id` and `created_at`; it has neither `updated_at` nor `version`, and those two fields are absent from its representation entirely. This is deliberate — journal lines are append-only. A line is never edited and never deleted; a correction is a new line that names the line it negates.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock as the Only Time Source

Every ageing, grace-window, expiry, and shift-window rule in this specification reads a single server-side logical clock, never the host's wall clock. Before the clock has ever been set it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time. An administrative capability, available to all six roles and used to drive the system through time in testing, accepts a `current_utc` instant and moves the clock to it; the move itself fires no state transition, but it does change what every subsequent read derives — most visibly, a card authorization's age.

Instants supplied by clients (`scheduled_checkout_at`, `obtained_at`, `opened_at`, `transmitted_at`, `shift_start_at`, `shift_end_at`) and instants stamped by the server (`actual_checkout_at`, `closed_at`, `voided_at`, `submitted_at`, `approved_at`, `reviewed_at`, `distributed_at`, `tied_out_at`) are all ISO 8601 UTC with three fractional digits. Calendar-day fields (`business_date`, `valid_from_date`, `valid_to_date`, `audit_trail_retention_until_date`) are plain `YYYY-MM-DD` strings, and `period_month` is a plain `YYYY-MM` string.

### 2.6. Money, Signs and Rounding

All monetary amounts are JSON numbers, never strings, and are carried to exactly 2 decimal places. Wherever this document specifies a computed money figure, the result is rounded **half up** to 2 decimal places. Rate and percentage configuration fields preserve the precision they were supplied with — an occupancy tax rate of `8.875` is stored and applied as three decimal places — but every amount those rates produce is rounded to 2 decimals. Statistical percentages (`occupancy_pct`, `rate_variance_pct`, `pace_index_pct`) and derived per-room figures (`adr_amount`, `revpar_amount`) are likewise rounded half up to 2 decimal places.

Rounding placement matters and is specified explicitly for each calculation, because rounding once over a sum and rounding each element before summing give different answers. Where this specification says a figure is rounded per element before being summed, that is the required behaviour, and rounding once over the total is incorrect.

The journal is signed. Amounts that increase what a guest owes — room charges, occupancy tax, no-show charges, late-checkout charges, incidental charges, outlet transfers, city-ledger fees — are positive. Amounts that reduce it — payments, rebates, transfers out — are negative. A journal amount may never be zero on a hand-posted line, and it is always carried to 2 decimal places. Empty-set derivations return exactly `0.00` rather than null or an error: a day with no occupied rooms reports an average daily rate of `0.00`, not a division failure.

### 2.7. The Property Configuration Rulebook

Almost every threshold this specification refers to is not a hard-coded constant but a per-property setting, registered once when the property is set up and read afresh on every evaluation. A property carries a trade `name` and a physical sellable inventory `room_count_total`, which must be at least 1. Its published rack rate `standard_room_rate_amount` must be greater than zero and is the denominator against which every guest's contracted rate is measured for variance.

Three tax and surcharge percentages drive charge computation: `occupancy_tax_rate_pct`, applied to room-department journal lines and carried to three decimal places so that a rate such as `8.875` is expressible exactly; `fb_tax_rate_pct`, applied to the taxable base of an outlet check; and `service_charge_pct`, the mandatory outlet service charge applied to a check's gross value. All three must be at least zero. Two further settings govern large-party pricing: `large_ticket_threshold_amount`, greater than zero, is the taxable base at or above which a discount applies, and `large_party_discount_pct`, at least zero, is the percentage of the check's gross value that discount removes.

`late_checkout_grace_minutes` is the number of overdue minutes a departing guest may exceed before any late-checkout charge is raised at all; it must be at least zero and defaults to 30 when the property is registered without it. `rate_variance_tolerance_pct`, at least zero, is the absolute rate variance a guest account may carry before it needs active rate-override paperwork. `settlement_variance_tolerance_amount`, at least zero and carried to 2 decimals, is the absolute settlement variance a shift batch may carry before it is flagged. `card_authorization_valid_days` must be at least 1 and defaults to 7; it is the age in whole days at which an approved card authorization lapses. `city_ledger_fee_pct`, at least zero, is the fee percentage charged on each guest account swept to the city ledger.

Rebate approval authority is tiered by two ascending money thresholds. `allowance_manager_threshold_amount` must be greater than zero, and `allowance_controller_threshold_amount` must be strictly greater than `allowance_manager_threshold_amount`; a registration that violates that ordering is rejected. Finally, `no_show_charge_policy` decides what the night audit raises against a reserved account that never arrived. It is one of `FIRST_NIGHT_PLUS_TAX`, `FULL_STAY_NO_TAX`, or `NONE`, and defaults to `FIRST_NIGHT_PLUS_TAX`.

Every record in the system belongs to exactly one property, and no operation may straddle two. When a single request names several records — a guest account and an operating day, an outlet and a check's operating day, a ledger account and a posting's operating day — the service confirms they all resolve to the same property and rejects the request with `ERR_CROSS_PROPERTY_REFERENCE` (HTTP 422) when they do not. This check applies when a journal line is hand-posted, when an outlet check is opened, when a settlement batch is declared, when a rebate is raised, when approval paperwork is filed, and when a guest account is created.

### 2.8. The Append-Only Journal and Its Charge Classes

All monetary movement — on guest accounts and on non-guest ledger accounts alike — is recorded in one journal. A single line may name a guest account through `folio_id`, a ledger account through `ledger_account_id`, or both left null on one side of a pair; it always names the operating day it is stamped to through `business_date_id`. Because one table serves both sides, a transfer between a guest account and a ledger account is two lines that net to zero. Lines minted as a side effect of another operation carry provenance: `outlet_ticket_id` on the transfer produced by closing a room-charge check, `allowance_id` on the credit produced by approving a rebate, and `reverses_posting_id` on a compensating line that negates an earlier one. On original lines all three of those are null.

Each line carries a `charge_type` drawn from `ROOM`, `OCCUPANCY_TAX`, `TAX_TRUE_UP`, `NO_SHOW`, `LATE_CHECKOUT`, `MISC_CHARGE`, `OUTLET_TRANSFER`, `ALLOWANCE`, `PAYMENT`, `DEPOSIT_APPLIED`, `LEDGER_TRANSFER`, and `CITY_LEDGER_FEE`. Only four of those twelve may be supplied by a client when a line is hand-posted: `MISC_CHARGE`, `PAYMENT`, `LEDGER_TRANSFER`, and `DEPOSIT_APPLIED`. Any other value on a hand-posted line is rejected with `ERR_CHARGE_TYPE_NOT_POSTABLE` (HTTP 422), because every other class is minted only by a declared side effect described in §3. The signed `amount` on each line is carried to 2 decimals and may not be zero.

Three further fields describe a line. `night_index` is the 1-based room-night ordinal, present on `ROOM` and `OCCUPANCY_TAX` lines and null on every other class. `source_channel` records which subsystem produced the line and is stamped by the server, never by the client, from `NIGHT_AUDIT`, `FRONT_DESK`, `OUTLET_POS`, `ALLOWANCE_WORKFLOW`, and `LEDGER_SWEEP`. `reason_code`, drawn from `SERVICE_RECOVERY`, `RATE_ERROR`, `POSTING_ERROR`, `GUEST_DISPUTE`, and `MANAGER_DISCRETION`, is carried by rebate credits and by reversals and is null elsewhere. Every line also records `posted_by_user_id`, the acting operator, and may carry a free-text `memo` for audit.

Correcting the journal never means changing it. Reversal appends a new line whose `amount` is the exact negation of the original, copying the original's `folio_id`, `ledger_account_id`, `business_date_id`, and `charge_type`, and setting `reverses_posting_id` to the original's `id`. A line that is itself a reversal cannot be reversed, and neither can a line another row already names in `reverses_posting_id`; either case is rejected with `ERR_POSTING_ALREADY_REVERSED` (HTTP 409). The reverser may not be the poster: when the acting `X-User-ID` equals the line's `posted_by_user_id`, the request is rejected with `ERR_SELF_REVERSAL_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 403).

The arithmetic consequence of reversal deserves emphasis, because it is asymmetric. Every **net** figure the original line moved returns to exactly its pre-original value: the guest account's `balance_amount`, the ledger account's `current_balance_amount`, the operating day's revenue aggregates, and the day's ledger control total. The **gross** figures do not return. Because the reversing row is an ordinary signed row like any other, a positive original followed by a negative reversal leaves `charges_total_amount` unchanged and raises the magnitude of `credits_total_amount`; the settlement dimension of the account is then re-derived from the new pair rather than restored to what it was.

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline and Report Supersession

A single request routinely mutates several records, and this specification always names the full chain rather than only its first link. When a journal line is appended, the guest account it names re-derives its gross and net money figures and its settlement dimension; the operating day it is stamped to re-derives whichever revenue aggregates that class of line feeds; and any statistics derived from those aggregates move with them. Each of those records is a genuine mutation — `updated_at` is refreshed and `version` increments on each of them independently.

The chain does not stop at the operating day. A revenue report is a frozen snapshot of one day's numbers, so any later change to that day's revenue aggregates invalidates it: every report of that day whose `status` is `DRAFT` or `DISTRIBUTED` is moved to `SUPERSEDED`. Three different producers drive that supersession — generating a newer report for the same day, reopening the day, and any journal line that changes the day's revenue aggregates. A `SUPERSEDED` report is terminal: it can never be distributed, and a day whose only distributed report has been superseded is no longer eligible to close.

Where a cascade is conditional, the condition is stated with the cascade. A late-checkout charge of exactly `0.00` produces no journal line, and because there is no line there is no account re-derivation, no day re-derivation, and no report supersession — the chain simply never starts. A tax true-up whose delta is exactly `0.00` likewise writes nothing.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties

Four distinct originator fields exist for one reason: so that the operator recorded in them can be barred from deciding the record they created. The bar is absolute and is checked before the business merits of the decision are considered.

- On a rebate, the operator recorded in `requested_by_user_id` may not approve it, may not reject it, and may not return it for revision. Any of those three attempts by that operator is rejected with `ERR_SELF_APPROVAL_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 403).
- On a settlement batch, the operator recorded in `declared_by_user_id` may not accept its variance, and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_SELF_APPROVAL_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 403).
- On approval paperwork, the operator recorded in `filed_by_user_id` may not review it, and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_SELF_APPROVAL_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 403).
- On a journal line, the operator recorded in `posted_by_user_id` may not reverse it. This case carries its own code, `ERR_SELF_REVERSAL_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 403), rather than the shared one.

Separately from the originator bar, rebate approval is gated on authority level. Each rebate carries a frozen `required_approval_role`, and the acting role must sit at or above it on the ladder `NightAuditor` below `RevenueManager` below `Controller`. An acting role beneath the requirement is rejected with `ERR_APPROVAL_TIER_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 403). Being entitled by tier does not lift the originator bar, and clearing the originator bar does not satisfy the tier.

### 2.11. Snapshot Currency for Published Revenue

Exactly one write in this API is guarded by an optimistic-concurrency token, and it is the creation of a revenue report. A revenue report snapshots an entire operating day's revenue picture and, on success, supersedes that day's previous report — so a caller who read the day, walked away, and then published must not be allowed to publish figures that have since moved. Any write that lands on the day in the meantime moves the day's `version`: a late outlet transfer, a rebate approval, a settlement verification, a reopen.

Accordingly, a request that creates a revenue report must carry `expected_business_date_version` as a non-optional input field, holding the `version` the client last observed on the operating day it is reporting. The server compares that value against the `BusinessDate.version` it holds at the moment the report row would be written. When the two are equal the snapshot is still current and the report is written. When they differ the caller was working from stale revenue aggregates and the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_BUSINESS_DATE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). On rejection nothing at all is written: no report row is created, the day's previous report keeps whatever `status` it held, and the day's own `version` is untouched. The caller must re-read the operating day and resubmit with the fresh value.

No other endpoint in this API accepts a version token. Every other write mutates a single aggregate whose own state preconditions already reject a caller acting on stale information, so no other request body carries an expected-version field of any kind.

### 2.12. Rejection Precedence

When a request violates several rules at once, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fully determined by the following order. Implementations must evaluate in this sequence so that a caller repairing one problem at a time converges.

1. **Authentication.** Both headers present and non-empty, then `X-User-Role` naming one of the six declared roles — both failures reported as `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. **Authorization.** The acting role appears on the called endpoint's role list; then, on any endpoint that admits `OutletManager`, the caller's `X-User-ID` resolves to an existing `Outlet`. Both are reported as `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
3. **Request shape.** The body of a write request is a JSON object. Unknown field names are ignored rather than rejected, but a body that is not an object, a required field that is absent, a field whose JSON type is wrong, or an enum field carrying a value outside its declared set is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).
4. **Path existence, left to right.** Each identifier in the path is resolved in the order it appears and the first that fails decides the error: `ERR_PROPERTY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BUSINESS_DATE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FOLIO_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TICKET_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BATCH_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ALLOWANCE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DOCUMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_POSTING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LEDGER_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OUTLET_NOT_FOUND`, or `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_FOUND`, each at HTTP 404 for the collection the path addresses.
5. **Body reference existence, in a fixed order.** `property_id` first (`ERR_PROPERTY_NOT_FOUND`), then `business_date_id` (`ERR_BUSINESS_DATE_NOT_FOUND`), then `folio_id` or `room_charge_folio_id` (`ERR_FOLIO_NOT_FOUND`), then `outlet_id` (`ERR_OUTLET_NOT_FOUND`), then `ledger_account_id` (`ERR_LEDGER_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND`), then `allowance_id` (`ERR_ALLOWANCE_NOT_FOUND`).
6. **Archived-day refusal.** Any write naming an operating day whose `status` is `ARCHIVED` is refused with `ERR_BUSINESS_DATE_ARCHIVED` (HTTP 409). This outranks every other state check — an archived day accepts nothing at all.
7. **The charge-seal gate.** A charge-bearing write requires the named day to be `OPEN`, `ROOM_POSTING`, or `REOPENED`, and a day in `CHARGES_SEALED`, `SETTLEMENT_REVIEW`, `BALANCED`, or `CLOSED` refuses it with `ERR_CHARGES_ALREADY_SEALED` (HTTP 409). The gate covers exactly these writes: appending a journal line by hand, applying a tender to a guest account, checking a guest out, opening a check, closing a check, voiding a check, raising an allowance, approving an allowance, reversing an allowance, and capturing a card authorization. Reversing a journal line is the single carve-out: correcting a mis-posted line is precisely what the settlement-review step exists for, so its allowed set widens to `OPEN`, `ROOM_POSTING`, `CHARGES_SEALED`, `SETTLEMENT_REVIEW`, `BALANCED`, and `REOPENED`, and only a `CLOSED` day refuses it. Note which writes the gate does not cover at all: the city-ledger sweep, which writes transfers rather than revenue, and every pipeline step, which carries its own state precondition instead.
8. **Endpoint-specific state preconditions**, each naming its own code.
9. **Business safety and compliance** — segregation of duties, approval tiers, outlet scope, and the paperwork gates.
10. **Business constraints** — uniqueness, temporal validity and overlap, referential coherence, quota limits, and distribution requirements.
11. **Snapshot currency** — the revenue report's expected-version check.
12. **Fiscal checks last** — ledger control, authorization sufficiency, capture and approval amounts, zero-balance and credit-limit rules.

The practical consequence is worth stating plainly: a compliance or duty-of-segregation failure surfaces before an amount check, so an operator who both originated a rebate and typed an approved amount larger than the request sees the segregation failure, not the amount failure.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up a Property, Its Capture Points and Its Calendar

Before any audit can run, a `PropertyAdmin` registers the property itself with the full rulebook described in §2.7. Nothing else is created alongside it: revenue capture points and balancing ledger accounts are registered separately, and accounting periods are never registered at all.

A **revenue capture point** — the system calls it an outlet — is any place takings arise. Registering one names its owning property through `property_id`, gives it a short operator `outlet_code` that must be unique within that property, a human-readable `outlet_name`, and an `outlet_type` of `FRONT_DESK`, `FB_OUTLET`, or `REMOTE_POS`. A second registration reusing an `outlet_code` under the same property is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_OUTLET_CODE` (HTTP 409). The `requires_shift_settlement` flag defaults to true and declares whether this capture point must produce a resolved shift batch before its operating day may reach `BALANCED`. Each outlet also carries a `status` that begins at `ACTIVE`; the only other value is `SUSPENDED`.

Suspension is a `PropertyAdmin` capability and is deliberately non-destructive. Moving an outlet's `status` to `SUSPENDED` leaves it fully addressable for prior-day audit work but stops it accepting anything new: every later attempt to open a check or declare a batch naming it fails with `ERR_OUTLET_SUSPENDED` (HTTP 409). It also drops out of the mandatory-batch requirement, so a suspended capture point no longer blocks settlement verification by having produced no batch. The suspension itself increments the outlet's `version`. The outlet listing narrows by `property_id`, `outlet_type`, `status`, and `requires_shift_settlement`, which is how an operator discovers which capture points belong to a property.

The `id` of an outlet has a second life: it is the value an `OutletManager` presents as `X-User-ID`, as described in §2.1. Registering the outlet is therefore also what provisions the operator identity that runs it.

**Balancing ledger accounts** are the non-guest side of the journal, and either a `PropertyAdmin` or a `Controller` may open one. Each names its `property_id` and a `ledger_type` of `HOUSE_ACCOUNT`, `CITY_LEDGER`, or `ADVANCE_DEPOSIT`, together with an operator `account_code` unique within the property and the `gl_account_code` the month-end tie-out reports against. Its `opening_balance_amount` defaults to `0.00` and is the balance carried in before any journal line exists; its `credit_limit_amount`, at least zero and carried to 2 decimals, caps the balance the account may hold after a posting. The running `current_balance_amount` is always the opening balance plus the signed sum of every journal line naming this account. Ledger accounts are discoverable by `property_id`, `ledger_type`, and `account_code`.

An **operating day** is the aggregate under audit, and either a `PropertyAdmin` or a `NightAuditor` opens one. Creating it names the audited `property_id` and the calendar day itself as `business_date` in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, which must be unique for that property — a repeat is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_BUSINESS_DATE` (HTTP 409). Three optional figures may be supplied at creation: `out_of_order_room_count`, defaulting to 0, at least zero and no greater than the property's `room_count_total`, counting rooms withdrawn from inventory for the day; and the two prior-year pace benchmarks `benchmark_calendar_room_revenue_amount` and `benchmark_weekday_room_revenue_amount`, each defaulting to `0.00`, at least zero, and carried to 2 decimals. The day opens with `status` `OPEN`, `reopen_count` at 0, `rooms_available_count` already computed as the property's `room_count_total` less `out_of_order_room_count`, and every other aggregate at 0 or `0.00`.

Opening a day also attaches it to its calendar-month container. If no **accounting period** yet exists for that property and that month, one is created on demand, its `period_month` derived from the day's `business_date` in `YYYY-MM` form and unique per property; the day's `accounting_period_id` is stamped with it either way. A newly minted period has `status` `OPEN`, `tie_out_attempt_count` at 0, and null in `gl_control_total_amount`, `tied_out_by_user_id`, `tied_out_at`, and `audit_trail_retention_until_date`. Its `subsystem_total_amount` and `tie_out_variance_amount` are derived figures described in §3.9. Periods are reachable only by listing them, filtered by `property_id`, `period_month`, and `status`; the operating-day listing narrows by `property_id`, `accounting_period_id`, `business_date`, and `status`.

### 3.2. Reservations, Arrival Review and Approval Paperwork

A guest account — the **folio** — is created by a `FrontDeskAgent` or a `NightAuditor` well before the guest appears. Creation names the owning `property_id` and the `arrival_business_date_id` of the operating day the reservation is booked to arrive on, together with the reservation's `confirmation_code`, which must be unique within the property, the registered `guest_name`, the assigned `room_number` as a free-form label, a `nights_count` of at least 1, the `contracted_rate_amount` agreed with the guest (at least zero, 2 decimals, and the base of every room, no-show and late-checkout charge), the `rate_plan_code` recorded for audit, a `market_segment` of `TRANSIENT`, `GROUP`, `CORPORATE`, or `COMP`, a `settlement_method` of `CASH`, `CREDIT_CARD`, `DIRECT_BILL`, or `HOUSE_ACCOUNT`, and the contractual `scheduled_checkout_at` instant. Two optional inputs complete it: `is_comp`, defaulting to false, and `credit_limit_amount`, defaulting to `500.00`, at least zero and 2 decimals, which caps the balance the account may hold after any charge.

The account begins in `RESERVED` on its stay dimension and `UNSETTLED` on its settlement dimension, with `charges_total_amount`, `credits_total_amount`, and `balance_amount` all at `0.00`, `settlement_hold` false, and `actual_checkout_at` null. One figure is computed immediately: `rate_variance_pct` is the contracted rate less the property's `standard_room_rate_amount`, divided by that same `standard_room_rate_amount`, multiplied by 100, and rounded half up to 2 decimal places. It is signed — a discount below rack rate reads negative, an upsell positive — and it is re-derived on every read of the account, from that one account and the property's published rack rate alone. No other account and no journal line participates in it. Accounts are discoverable by `property_id`, `arrival_business_date_id`, `stay_status`, `settlement_status`, `settlement_method`, `is_comp`, and `confirmation_code`.

**Approval paperwork** is the evidence layer behind three kinds of exception, and it is filed as a record of its own by a `FrontDeskAgent`, `NightAuditor`, `RevenueManager`, or `Controller`. Filing names the owning `property_id`, the `doc_type`, the `authorized_by_name` of the executive who signed, the archive `document_reference` of the scan, and the closed inclusive coverage interval as `valid_from_date` and `valid_to_date`. `doc_type` is one of `COMP_ROOM`, `RATE_OVERRIDE`, or `ALLOWANCE_APPROVAL`, and the target must agree with it: a `COMP_ROOM` or `RATE_OVERRIDE` document requires `folio_id` and must not supply `allowance_id`, an `ALLOWANCE_APPROVAL` document requires `allowance_id` and must not supply `folio_id`, and a `RATE_OVERRIDE` document additionally requires `authorized_rate_amount` — the rate the paperwork authorizes, at least zero and 2 decimals, which is ignored entirely for the other two types. Any disagreement is rejected with `ERR_DOCUMENT_TARGET_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422).

Two interval rules guard filing. The coverage interval must be well formed: `valid_from_date` at or before `valid_to_date`, otherwise `ERR_SHIFT_WINDOW_INVALID` (HTTP 422). And no other document with the same `folio_id` and the same `doc_type`, whose `status` is `PENDING` or `ACTIVE`, may occupy an overlapping interval. Because both interval endpoints are inclusive, two documents that merely touch — one ending on the very day the next begins — **do** overlap, and the second filing is rejected with `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_INTERVAL_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409).

Filed paperwork begins at `PENDING`, with `filed_by_user_id` stamped from the acting operator and `reviewed_by_user_id` and `reviewed_at` null. A `PENDING` document satisfies no gate whatsoever. Review is a `RevenueManager` or `Controller` action carrying a `decision` of either `APPROVE`, which drives the document `PENDING → ACTIVE`, or `REJECT`, which drives it `PENDING → REJECTED` and is terminal — a rejected document never satisfies any precondition. Either decision stamps `reviewed_by_user_id` and `reviewed_at` and increments the document's `version`. Review demands `PENDING`; a document already reviewed is refused with `ERR_DOCUMENT_NOT_PENDING` (HTTP 409). And the filer may never review their own paperwork, per §2.10. An `ACTIVE` document immediately satisfies the comp, rate-override, or rebate-document gate for every operating day inside its inclusive interval.

Revocation belongs to `Controller` alone and drives an `ACTIVE` document to `REVOKED`, re-stamping `reviewed_by_user_id`; a document that is not `ACTIVE` is refused with `ERR_DOCUMENT_NOT_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409). Revocation is deliberately **not** retroactive: journal lines already posted under the document stay exactly where they are, but every later evaluation treats the document as absent. That is what lets a revocation break tomorrow's audit run without rewriting yesterday's ledger — the next room-and-tax run over a comp account the revoked document used to cover fails with `ERR_COMP_AUTHORIZATION_MISSING`. Paperwork is discoverable by `property_id`, `folio_id`, `allowance_id`, `doc_type`, and `status`.

**Arrival** moves the stay dimension `RESERVED → IN_HOUSE` and is performed by a `FrontDeskAgent` or `NightAuditor`. An account whose `stay_status` is anything other than `RESERVED` is refused with `ERR_FOLIO_NOT_RESERVED` (HTTP 409). The operating day named by `arrival_business_date_id` must still accept charges — that is, be `OPEN`, `ROOM_POSTING`, or `REOPENED` — and an arrival against a day past that point is refused with `ERR_CHARGES_ALREADY_SEALED`. The substantive gate is rate variance: when the absolute value of `rate_variance_pct` exceeds the property's `rate_variance_tolerance_pct`, the account must be covered by an `ACTIVE` `RATE_OVERRIDE` document whose `folio_id` is this account and whose inclusive interval contains the arrival day's `business_date`. Without it the arrival is refused with `ERR_RATE_VARIANCE_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 409). Note the direction of that comparison: a variance exactly equal to the tolerance needs no paperwork; only a variance strictly above it does. Arrival leaves the settlement dimension untouched, increments the account's `version`, and enrols the account in every later room-and-tax run until its `nights_count` is exhausted.

**Cancellation** is the other exit from `RESERVED`, also available to a `FrontDeskAgent` or `NightAuditor`, and it too requires `stay_status` `RESERVED` on pain of `ERR_FOLIO_NOT_RESERVED`. It additionally requires the account's `balance_amount` to be exactly `0.00`; an account carrying any balance is refused with `ERR_FOLIO_BALANCE_NOT_ZERO` (HTTP 409). Cancellation drives `RESERVED → CANCELLED`, increments `version`, and is what keeps the night audit from ever treating the reservation as a no-show — a `CANCELLED` account is skipped by every later room-and-tax run.

### 3.3. The Room-and-Tax Pass: First Step of the Daily Close

The daily close is a fixed five-step sequence driven by a `NightAuditor`, and its first step raises the day's room revenue. The step advances the operating day `OPEN → ROOM_POSTING` and demands `status` `OPEN` to begin with; a day in any other state — including a day already in `ROOM_POSTING` because the step has already run — is refused with `ERR_BUSINESS_DATE_NOT_OPEN` (HTTP 409).

Before a single line is written, the run checks its evidence. Every comp account of this property that is `IN_HOUSE` and still due a room night on this day must be covered by an approval document whose `folio_id` is that account, whose `doc_type` is `COMP_ROOM`, whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, and whose inclusive interval contains the day's `business_date` — meaning `valid_from_date` is at or before the business date and `valid_to_date` is at or after it. One uncovered comp account rejects the **whole run** with `ERR_COMP_AUTHORIZATION_MISSING` (HTTP 409), and no line at all is written, not even for the accounts that were properly covered. The run is all-or-nothing on this gate.

Accounts are then processed in ascending `created_at` order — never by identifier — so that the sequence of `night_index` values a multi-night stay accrues is reproducible. For each account of this property whose `stay_status` is `IN_HOUSE`, the run counts the `ROOM` lines that account already holds. If that count has reached the account's `nights_count`, the account is skipped entirely. Otherwise the room amount is `0.00` when `is_comp` is true and the account's `contracted_rate_amount` when it is false, and two lines are appended: a `ROOM` line for that amount, and an `OCCUPANCY_TAX` line for that amount multiplied by the property's `occupancy_tax_rate_pct` divided by 100, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. Both lines carry a `night_index` equal to the previous `ROOM` count plus 1, and both carry `source_channel` `NIGHT_AUDIT`. A comp account produces a `ROOM` line of exactly `0.00` and an `OCCUPANCY_TAX` line of exactly `0.00`, and both of those rows genuinely exist in the journal. Because a fully posted account is skipped, a repeated run is a no-op in effect for those accounts while still letting a multi-night stay accrue exactly one night per run — but the state gate above still refuses a second call outright.

The same run settles the day's arrivals that never arrived. Every account whose `arrival_business_date_id` is this operating day and whose `stay_status` is still `RESERVED` is moved `RESERVED → NO_SHOW`, and its `version` increments. What is charged alongside that move is decided by the property's `no_show_charge_policy`, and the three branches differ in both amount and tax treatment:

- Under `FIRST_NIGHT_PLUS_TAX`, a `NO_SHOW` line is appended for the account's `contracted_rate_amount`, and an `OCCUPANCY_TAX` line is appended for that same contracted rate multiplied by `occupancy_tax_rate_pct` divided by 100, rounded half up to 2 decimals.
- Under `FULL_STAY_NO_TAX`, a single `NO_SHOW` line is appended for the `contracted_rate_amount` multiplied by the account's `nights_count`, rounded half up to 2 decimals, and **no** occupancy-tax line is appended at all.
- Under `NONE`, no line whatsoever is appended, and the account still moves to `NO_SHOW`.

A `RESERVED` account whose arrival day is some later day is left completely untouched, and a `CANCELLED` account is never a no-show. Accounts already `DEPARTED`, `NO_SHOW`, or `CANCELLED` are skipped by the in-house pass as well.

Each appended line then re-derives its account's `charges_total_amount`, `credits_total_amount`, `balance_amount`, and `settlement_status` by the derivation in §3.7, and those re-derived account figures roll up into the operating day. Four signed revenue aggregates are maintained on the day, each a signed sum over the journal lines stamped to it: `room_night_revenue_amount` sums lines whose `charge_type` is `ROOM` or `ALLOWANCE` and is the numerator of both the average-daily-rate and the revenue-per-available-room statistics — occupancy-tax and tax-true-up lines are deliberately excluded from it; `other_room_revenue_amount` sums lines whose `charge_type` is `NO_SHOW` or `LATE_CHECKOUT`; `room_revenue_amount` is `room_night_revenue_amount` plus `other_room_revenue_amount` and is the pace-comparison numerator; and `tax_revenue_amount` sums lines whose `charge_type` is `OCCUPANCY_TAX` or `TAX_TRUE_UP`. A fifth, `allowance_credit_amount`, sums only the lines whose `charge_type` is `ALLOWANCE` and is therefore zero or negative; it is a reporting figure and is not added again into any total, because rebate credits are already netted inside `room_night_revenue_amount`. The run also refreshes the day's occupancy statistics as described in §3.8, and increments the day's `version`.

Note what occupancy counts and what it does not. A comp room's `0.00` `ROOM` line still marks the room as occupied, because occupancy is counted from `ROOM` lines regardless of amount. A no-show charge marks nothing as occupied, because it is not a `ROOM` line. And a rebate lowers the average daily rate without lowering the occupied count, because it enters the revenue numerator but not the room count.

### 3.4. Outlet Capture: Checks, Service Charges and Large-Party Pricing

Takings arise at capture points all day long, and an `OutletManager` or `NightAuditor` records each one as a check. Opening a check names the `outlet_id` that raised it, the `business_date_id` it belongs to, an operator `ticket_number` that must be unique for that outlet on that operating day, the pre-service-charge pre-tax `gross_amount` (strictly greater than zero, 2 decimals), the `settlement_type`, and the `opened_at` instant the check was started. A duplicate `ticket_number` for the same outlet and day is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_TICKET_NUMBER` (HTTP 409). When the caller is an `OutletManager`, the `outlet_id` must be their own, per §2.1. A check naming a `SUSPENDED` outlet is refused with `ERR_OUTLET_SUSPENDED`, and a check naming an operating day that no longer accepts charges is refused with `ERR_CHARGES_ALREADY_SEALED`.

`settlement_type` is one of `CASH`, `CREDIT_CARD`, or `ROOM_CHARGE`, and it decides where the money ends up. A `ROOM_CHARGE` check transfers to a guest account instead of entering any cash or card settlement batch, so it must carry `room_charge_folio_id`; a `CASH` or `CREDIT_CARD` check must leave that field null. Either violation is rejected with `ERR_TICKET_FOLIO_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422). The optional `transmitted_at` input is the instant the check reached the audit system, and it is the value matched against shift windows — a remote point-of-sale terminal may transmit long after the shift it belongs to has ended, which is exactly why transmission time and opening time are separate fields. When the check is closed without a `transmitted_at` ever having been supplied, it defaults to the closing instant.

A check opens in `status` `OPEN` with `service_charge_amount`, `taxable_base_amount`, `tax_amount`, `large_party_discount_amount`, and `total_amount` all at `0.00` and `closed_at`, `closed_by_user_id`, and `voided_at` null. An `OPEN` check contributes to no settlement batch and to no operating-day aggregate whatsoever. Checks are discoverable by `outlet_id`, `business_date_id`, `room_charge_folio_id`, `status`, and `settlement_type`.

**Closing** a check is what prices it, and it drives `OPEN → CLOSED`. It is available to an `OutletManager` acting on their own outlet or to a `NightAuditor`; a check that is not `OPEN` is refused with `ERR_TICKET_NOT_OPEN` (HTTP 409), an outlet mismatch with `ERR_OUTLET_SCOPE_VIOLATION`, and a day past the charge-seal point with `ERR_CHARGES_ALREADY_SEALED`. The pricing runs in a fixed order, and each intermediate money figure is rounded half up to 2 decimal places on its own before the next is formed:

1. `service_charge_amount` is the check's `gross_amount` multiplied by the property's `service_charge_pct` divided by 100.
2. `taxable_base_amount` is `gross_amount` plus `service_charge_amount`.
3. `tax_amount` is `taxable_base_amount` multiplied by the property's `fb_tax_rate_pct` divided by 100.
4. `large_party_discount_amount` is decided by a threshold test on the base but computed from the gross: when `taxable_base_amount` is at or above the property's `large_ticket_threshold_amount`, it is `gross_amount` multiplied by `large_party_discount_pct` divided by 100; when the base is below that threshold, it is exactly `0.00`.
5. `total_amount` is `taxable_base_amount` plus `tax_amount` minus `large_party_discount_amount`.

The apparent circularity in step 4 is intentional and must not be resolved by iterating: the discount is gated on a base that already contains the service charge, yet the discount never feeds back into `service_charge_amount` or `tax_amount`. Discounting the gross first and then recomputing the service charge and tax on the discounted figure produces a different and incorrect total. Note also that the threshold comparison is inclusive — a base landing exactly on `large_ticket_threshold_amount` earns the discount, while a base one cent below it earns nothing.

Closing stamps `closed_at` from the logical clock and `closed_by_user_id` from the acting operator, defaults `transmitted_at` to `closed_at` when the request supplies neither at open nor at close, and increments the check's `version`. The day's `outlet_revenue_amount` — the sum of `total_amount` over the day's `CLOSED` checks — rises, as do `closed_outlet_ticket_count`, the count of the day's `CLOSED` checks, and the day's `total_revenue_amount`. When `room_charge_folio_id` is set, an `OUTLET_TRANSFER` journal line of the check's `total_amount` is appended with `source_channel` `OUTLET_POS` and `outlet_ticket_id` naming the check, and that guest account's `charges_total_amount`, `balance_amount`, and `settlement_status` are re-derived; if that transfer would carry the account's `balance_amount` above its `credit_limit_amount`, the close is refused with `ERR_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409). From the moment it is closed, the check matches any settlement batch of the same outlet, operating day, and tender type whose half-open window contains its `transmitted_at`.

**Voiding** undoes every figure the close moved without deleting a single row, driving `CLOSED → VOID`. It is available to the same operators, refuses a check that is not `CLOSED` with `ERR_TICKET_NOT_CLOSED` (HTTP 409), and refuses a day past the charge-seal point with `ERR_CHARGES_ALREADY_SEALED`. It stamps `voided_at`, returns `total_amount` to `0.00`, and returns the day's `outlet_revenue_amount`, `closed_outlet_ticket_count`, and `total_revenue_amount` to exactly their pre-close values. When `room_charge_folio_id` is set, a reversing `OUTLET_TRANSFER` line of the negated `total_amount` is appended carrying `reverses_posting_id`, so the guest account's `balance_amount` returns to exactly its pre-close value while — as §2.8 warns — `charges_total_amount` is unchanged and `credits_total_amount` falls by the check's total, because the reversing row is an ordinary signed row. The voided check stops matching any settlement batch, so the next reconciliation of that batch computes a lower system amount. The void increments the check's `version`.

### 3.5. Shift Settlement: Declared Cash, Counted Variances and the Recount Cycle

Against the priced checks stands what the outlet physically counted. A settlement batch is declared by an `OutletManager` for their own outlet or by a `NightAuditor` for any outlet, and it names the `business_date_id` being settled, the `outlet_id` declaring, the `tender_type` (`CASH` or `CREDIT_CARD` only — a `ROOM_CHARGE` check never enters a batch), the inclusive `shift_start_at`, the exclusive `shift_end_at`, and the `declared_amount` the operator counted, which may be zero or more with 2 decimal places. A window whose `shift_start_at` is not strictly before its `shift_end_at` is rejected with `ERR_SHIFT_WINDOW_INVALID` (HTTP 422). An unknown `business_date_id` yields `ERR_BUSINESS_DATE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) and an unknown `outlet_id` yields `ERR_OUTLET_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404); a `SUSPENDED` outlet yields `ERR_OUTLET_SUSPENDED`, and an `OutletManager` declaring for someone else's outlet yields `ERR_OUTLET_SCOPE_VIOLATION`.

Two batches of the same outlet, operating day, and `tender_type` may not both claim the same instant. Overlap is judged on the half-open interval from `shift_start_at` up to but excluding `shift_end_at`, and a batch already sitting in `RECOUNT_REQUESTED` is excluded from the check because its window is being re-established. A collision is rejected with `ERR_SHIFT_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). Because the interval excludes its end, two adjacent shifts where one ends at exactly the instant the next begins do not overlap, and both are accepted — contrast this with the closed, inclusive intervals used for authorization documents in §3.2, where merely touching *is* an overlap. The same overlap test is re-applied when a window is redeclared.

A new batch is born in `status` `DECLARED` with `system_amount` and `variance_amount` still null, `recount_round` at `0`, `declared_by_user_id` stamped from the acting operator, and `accepted_by_user_id` null. `DECLARED` is a transient waiting state: it counts immediately towards the day's `unresolved_settlement_batch_count`, and it is resolved only when the day's settlement verification runs. Batches are discoverable by `business_date_id`, `outlet_id`, `status`, and `tender_type`.

**Reconciliation** is what resolves a `DECLARED` batch, and the system — not any operator — decides the outcome. It is triggered by the day-level settlement verification described in §3.8, and it runs once per batch of that day whose `status` is `DECLARED`. The set of checks it draws on is narrow and exact: a check counts only when its `outlet_id` and `business_date_id` equal the batch's, its `status` is `CLOSED`, its `settlement_type` equals the batch's `tender_type`, and its `transmitted_at` is at or after `shift_start_at` and strictly before `shift_end_at`. `OPEN` and `VOID` checks never count, a `ROOM_CHARGE` check matches no tender type at all, and a remote terminal whose check transmits at exactly `shift_end_at` belongs to the *next* shift, not this one — which is precisely how two adjacent shifts sharing an instant avoid double-counting the same money. `system_amount` is the sum of `total_amount` over that matching set, and `variance_amount` is `declared_amount` minus `system_amount`, rounded half up to 2 decimals. A positive variance means the drawer holds more than the checks account for; a negative one means it holds less.

The verdict is a single tolerance comparison against the property's `settlement_variance_tolerance_amount`, and the comparison is inclusive: when the absolute value of `variance_amount` is at or below the tolerance the batch becomes `MATCHED`, and only when it strictly exceeds the tolerance does the batch become `VARIANCE_FLAGGED`. A variance landing exactly on the tolerance matches; one cent beyond it flags. Either way `system_amount` and `variance_amount` are stamped and the batch's `version` increments. A `MATCHED` batch stops counting towards `unresolved_settlement_batch_count`; a `VARIANCE_FLAGGED` batch keeps counting there and additionally contributes the absolute value of its variance to the day's `settlement_variance_total_amount`.

A flagged batch has three possible futures, and which one it takes is a matter of authority.

**Requesting a recount** is the `NightAuditor`'s move, driving `VARIANCE_FLAGGED → RECOUNT_REQUESTED`: it sends the batch back to the outlet floor to count again. It refuses a batch that is not `VARIANCE_FLAGGED` with `ERR_BATCH_NOT_FLAGGED` (HTTP 409), and it refuses a batch whose `recount_round` has already reached two with `ERR_RECOUNT_LIMIT_REACHED` (HTTP 409). On success `recount_round` increases by one and the `version` increments. While the batch sits in `RECOUNT_REQUESTED` it *leaves* the day's `settlement_variance_total_amount`, because its variance is no longer a settled fact, yet it still counts as unresolved — so a recount can never be used to make a day look balanced.

**Redeclaring** is the outlet's answer, driving `RECOUNT_REQUESTED → DECLARED`. It is available to an `OutletManager` whose own outlet raised the batch or to a `NightAuditor`, and it supplies a replacement `declared_amount` and optionally a revised `shift_start_at` and `shift_end_at`. A batch that is not in `RECOUNT_REQUESTED` is refused with `ERR_BATCH_NOT_AWAITING_RECOUNT` (HTTP 409), and an `OutletManager` reaching outside their own outlet is refused with `ERR_OUTLET_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. On success `declared_amount` is replaced, `variance_amount` is cleared back to null, `declared_by_user_id` is restamped from the operator who redeclared, and the `version` increments. `recount_round` is deliberately left untouched by the redeclaration, which is what makes the cap bite: the counter only ever advances on a recount request, so the second flag is the last one that may be sent back. The redeclared batch is once again `DECLARED` and therefore once again unresolved, awaiting the next verification run — and re-running verification on a day already under settlement review is exactly how the cycle closes.

**Accepting the variance** is management's move, driving `VARIANCE_FLAGGED → ACCEPTED_WITH_VARIANCE`, and it is reserved to a `RevenueManager` or `Controller`. It refuses a batch that is not `VARIANCE_FLAGGED` with `ERR_BATCH_NOT_FLAGGED`, and — under the segregation rule of §2.10 — it refuses an operator whose identity equals the batch's `declared_by_user_id` with `ERR_SELF_APPROVAL_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 403), so the person who counted the drawer can never sign off on their own shortage. On success `accepted_by_user_id` is stamped and the `version` increments. The accounting effect is asymmetric and intentional: the batch stops counting towards `unresolved_settlement_batch_count`, which unblocks the day, while it *continues* contributing the absolute value of its `variance_amount` to the day's `settlement_variance_total_amount`, so the accepted loss stays visible in the day's numbers forever. Both `MATCHED` and `ACCEPTED_WITH_VARIANCE` are final; neither may be recounted, redeclared, or accepted again.

### 3.6. Allowances: Tiered Approval, Bounded Revision and Compensating Reversal

When a guest is owed a rebate, nobody writes a credit line by hand. They raise an **allowance**, and the allowance carries its own approval workflow whose whole purpose is to make the size of the rebate decide who is allowed to grant it. A `NightAuditor`, `FrontDeskAgent`, or `RevenueManager` may raise one, naming the `folio_id` to be credited, the `business_date_id` the credit will be stamped to, and the `requested_amount` sought as a positive magnitude greater than zero with 2 decimal places. Three fields are optional at this stage: the `reason_code`, the free-text `narrative` recorded for audit, and the `supporting_document_reference` pointing at external approval paperwork. An unknown `folio_id` yields `ERR_FOLIO_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) and an unknown `business_date_id` yields `ERR_BUSINESS_DATE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404).

`reason_code` is one of `SERVICE_RECOVERY`, `RATE_ERROR`, `POSTING_ERROR`, `GUEST_DISPUTE`, or `MANAGER_DISCRETION`. A new allowance begins in `status` `DRAFT` with `revision_round` at `0`, `requested_by_user_id` stamped from the raising operator, and `approved_amount`, `approved_by_user_id`, `approved_at`, `submitted_at`, and `reverses_allowance_id` all null.

The one thing creation does compute is `required_approval_role`, the lowest role permitted to approve, and it is derived from `requested_amount` alone against the thresholds of the property that owns the guest account: at or above `allowance_controller_threshold_amount` the tier is `Controller`; otherwise at or above `allowance_manager_threshold_amount` the tier is `RevenueManager`; below both it is `NightAuditor`. Both comparisons are inclusive, so a request of exactly the manager threshold requires a `RevenueManager` while one cent below it does not. This tier is **frozen at creation and never re-derived** — not by a later `approved_amount`, however small, and not by any number of revision rounds. A request for a large sum that a manager later approves down to a trivial figure still demands the `Controller` who was required when it was raised, which is exactly the point: the authority needed is set by what was asked for, not by what was eventually granted. Allowances are discoverable by `folio_id`, `business_date_id`, `reverses_allowance_id`, `status`, `reason_code`, and `required_approval_role`; the `reverses_allowance_id` filter is the only way to find the compensating allowance a reversal mints.

**Submitting** moves an allowance into the approval queue and drives both `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` and `RETURNED_FOR_REVISION → SUBMITTED`. Any of the three raising roles may submit, and the request may replace `reason_code`, `narrative`, and `supporting_document_reference` on the way in — which is how a draft raised without a reason acquires one. An allowance in any other state is refused with `ERR_ALLOWANCE_NOT_SUBMITTABLE` (HTTP 409). Two documentation rules bite at exactly this moment and nowhere else: a `reason_code` that is still null is refused with `ERR_REASON_CODE_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422), and a `reason_code` of `RATE_ERROR` with an empty or absent `supporting_document_reference` is refused with `ERR_SUPPORTING_DOCUMENT_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422). Success stamps `submitted_at` from the logical clock — re-stamping it on every resubmit — and increments the `version`, while `revision_round` and `required_approval_role` are both left exactly where they were.

**Returning for revision** is how a `RevenueManager` or `Controller` sends a submission back to its author without killing it, driving `SUBMITTED → RETURNED_FOR_REVISION`. An allowance that is not `SUBMITTED` is refused with `ERR_ALLOWANCE_NOT_PENDING` (HTTP 409), an operator who is the allowance's own `requested_by_user_id` is refused with `ERR_SELF_APPROVAL_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 403), and an allowance whose `revision_round` has already reached two is refused with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED` (HTTP 409). Success raises `revision_round` by one and increments the `version`, leaving `required_approval_role` untouched so that no amount of back-and-forth can quietly lower the bar for approval. The cycle therefore permits at most two round trips, after which the reviewer must either approve or reject.

**Approving** is where money changes, and it demands the most of the caller. It is open to a `NightAuditor`, `RevenueManager`, or `Controller`, takes the single input `approved_amount`, and drives `SUBMITTED → APPROVED`. The gates, in the precedence order of §2.12, are these: the allowance must be `SUBMITTED`, else `ERR_ALLOWANCE_NOT_PENDING`; the acting role must sit at or above `required_approval_role` on the ladder where `NightAuditor` is lowest, `RevenueManager` sits above it, and `Controller` is highest, else `ERR_APPROVAL_TIER_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 403); the operator must not be the allowance's `requested_by_user_id`, else `ERR_SELF_APPROVAL_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 403); `approved_amount` must be greater than zero and no greater than `requested_amount`, else `ERR_APPROVED_AMOUNT_EXCEEDS_REQUEST` (HTTP 422); when `requested_amount` is at or above `allowance_controller_threshold_amount` there must exist an authorization document naming this allowance in its `allowance_id` with `doc_type` `ALLOWANCE_APPROVAL` and `status` `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_ALLOWANCE_DOCUMENT_MISSING` (HTTP 409); and the operating day of the credited guest account must still accept charges, else `ERR_CHARGES_ALREADY_SEALED` (HTTP 409). Note that the paperwork requirement is keyed to `requested_amount`, on the same frozen basis as the tier.

Approval does not stop at `APPROVED`. That state exists only for an instant, because the system carries the allowance straight on to `POSTED` inside the very same request, and the posting cascade runs four hops deep:

1. `approved_amount`, `approved_by_user_id`, and `approved_at` are stamped, and an `ALLOWANCE` journal line of the negated `approved_amount` is appended to the guest account with `source_channel` `ALLOWANCE_WORKFLOW` and `allowance_id` naming this allowance.
2. That line re-derives the account's `charges_total_amount`, `credits_total_amount`, `balance_amount`, and `settlement_status`.
3. The same line re-derives the operating day's `room_night_revenue_amount`, `allowance_credit_amount`, `room_revenue_amount`, `total_revenue_amount`, `adr_amount`, and `revpar_amount`.
4. Those changed day aggregates set every revenue report of that day whose `status` is `DRAFT` or `DISTRIBUTED` to `SUPERSEDED`.

The allowance's `version` increments, and `POSTED` is where a granted allowance rests. Because the credit is a negative `ALLOWANCE` line rather than a reduction of the original room charge, the day's average daily rate falls while its occupied-room count does not, and the account's `charges_total_amount` is untouched while its `credits_total_amount` deepens.

**Rejecting** is the other branch out of `SUBMITTED`, available to a `RevenueManager` or `Controller`. It refuses a non-`SUBMITTED` allowance with `ERR_ALLOWANCE_NOT_PENDING` and the requester themselves with `ERR_SELF_APPROVAL_FORBIDDEN`, and on success it moves the allowance to the terminal `REJECTED` and increments its `version`. No journal line is ever written by a rejection, and no aggregate anywhere moves.

**Reversing** is how a granted allowance is undone, and it is reserved to a `RevenueManager` or `Controller`. It drives `POSTED → REVERSED`, refusing an allowance that is not `POSTED` with `ERR_ALLOWANCE_NOT_POSTED` (HTTP 409), refusing an allowance that already carries a `reverses_allowance_id` — that is, a compensating allowance minted by an earlier reversal — with `ERR_ALREADY_A_REVERSAL` (HTTP 409), and refusing a sealed operating day with `ERR_CHARGES_ALREADY_SEALED`. A reversal never edits the original. Instead it mints a second allowance, born directly in `status` `POSTED`, whose `reverses_allowance_id` names the original, whose `reason_code` is copied from the original, and whose `requested_amount` and `approved_amount` both equal the original's `approved_amount`, with its own `required_approval_role` derived from that amount in the usual way. Alongside it, an `ALLOWANCE` journal line of the positive `approved_amount` is appended carrying `reverses_posting_id`.

The arithmetic of a reversal is worth stating precisely, because two of the three account figures do not return to where they started. The account's `balance_amount` returns to exactly its pre-approval value. Its `charges_total_amount` *rises* by `approved_amount`, because the compensating line is positive and the running totals are ordinary signed sums. Its `credits_total_amount` keeps the original negative `approved_amount` rather than shedding it. Consequently `settlement_status` is re-derived from the new pair of totals rather than restored to whatever it was before — an account that once read `UNSETTLED` may now read `PARTIALLY_SETTLED`, purely because a non-zero credit total now exists. The day's `room_night_revenue_amount`, `allowance_credit_amount`, `room_revenue_amount`, `total_revenue_amount`, `adr_amount`, and `revpar_amount` do all return to exactly their pre-approval values, every `DRAFT` or `DISTRIBUTED` revenue report of that day is set to `SUPERSEDED` again, and the original allowance's `version` increments. Both `REJECTED` and `REVERSED` are terminal.

### 3.7. Departure, Tenders, Manual Lines and Card Authorizations

Guests leave, money arrives, and mistakes get corrected — three activities that share one mechanism, the signed journal line, and one derived consequence, the guest account's settlement dimension.

**Checking out** is performed by a `FrontDeskAgent` or `NightAuditor` and drives `IN_HOUSE → DEPARTED`. It takes a single input, the `business_date_id` the departure charges belong to. Four gates guard it: the account must be `IN_HOUSE`, else `ERR_FOLIO_NOT_IN_HOUSE` (HTTP 409), which is also what a second departure of an already-departed account receives; `settlement_hold` must be false, else `ERR_DECLINED_AUTH_UNRESOLVED` (HTTP 409); when `settlement_method` is `CREDIT_CARD`, some card authorization of this account must have `status` `APPROVED` with an `authorized_amount` at or above the balance the account will carry *after* the late-checkout charge is applied, else `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_SHORTFALL` (HTTP 409) — note that the sufficiency test looks at the projected balance, not the current one; and the named operating day must still accept charges, else `ERR_CHARGES_ALREADY_SEALED`. Success stamps `actual_checkout_at` from the logical clock and sets `stay_status` to `DEPARTED`, leaving `settlement_status` alone.

The departure charge itself is tiered on lateness. Overdue minutes are the whole minutes between the account's `scheduled_checkout_at` and its freshly stamped `actual_checkout_at`, floored — thirty minutes and fifty-nine seconds is thirty overdue minutes, not thirty-one. When overdue minutes are at or below the property's `late_checkout_grace_minutes` the charge is exactly `0.00` and nothing at all is posted. Otherwise a percentage of the account's `contracted_rate_amount` is taken: one quarter of the rate when overdue minutes are at or below one hundred and eighty, one half when they are above that but at or below three hundred and sixty, and the whole rate when they exceed three hundred and sixty. That tiered figure is rounded half up to 2 decimals **first**, and only then clamped — raised to a floor of twenty-five, then capped at the account's `contracted_rate_amount`. The order of the clamps is not interchangeable and the ceiling deliberately wins: an inexpensive room whose contracted rate is below the twenty-five floor is charged its own rate and never the floor, because a hotel may not bill a guest more for leaving late than the room cost for the whole night. Applying the floor after the ceiling would overcharge exactly those accounts.

When the charge exceeds `0.00`, a chain of four hops follows, each caused by the previous one rather than by the departure itself — an account whose charge is `0.00` produces none of them:

1. A `LATE_CHECKOUT` journal line of that amount is appended with `source_channel` `FRONT_DESK`.
2. That line re-derives the account's `charges_total_amount`, `balance_amount`, and `settlement_status`.
3. The same line re-derives the named day's `other_room_revenue_amount`, `room_revenue_amount`, and `total_revenue_amount`.
4. Those changed day aggregates set every revenue report of that day whose `status` is `DRAFT` or `DISTRIBUTED` to `SUPERSEDED`.

**Applying a tender** is how money reaches an account. A `FrontDeskAgent` or `NightAuditor` supplies the `business_date_id` the tender belongs to, the positive `amount` received, and optionally a `ledger_account_id` to route it against and a `memo`. The service appends a `PAYMENT` line of the negated `amount` with `source_channel` `FRONT_DESK`, re-derives the account's `credits_total_amount`, `balance_amount`, and `settlement_status`, and leaves every revenue aggregate of the day untouched, because a tender is not revenue — it is the collection of revenue already recorded. An account whose `settlement_status` has already reached `TRANSFERRED_TO_CITY_LEDGER` accepts no further tender, posting, or allowance approval and refuses all three with `ERR_FOLIO_ALREADY_TRANSFERRED` (HTTP 409).

The settlement dimension is never set directly by any caller; it is re-derived from the account's own two running totals after every line that touches it. `charges_total_amount` is the sum of the account's positive lines, `credits_total_amount` the sum of its negative lines, and `balance_amount` their sum, rounded half up to 2 decimals. From those three figures the status follows in strict order: a `balance_amount` of exactly `0.00` reads `SETTLED`; otherwise a non-zero `credits_total_amount` reads `PARTIALLY_SETTLED`; otherwise the account reads `UNSETTLED`. Because this is a re-derivation rather than a forward-only workflow, the dimension moves in whichever direction the numbers dictate, and it moves independently of the stay dimension. A single tender clearing the whole balance takes an account straight from `UNSETTLED` to `SETTLED`. A partial tender takes it to `PARTIALLY_SETTLED`, and a later tender finishing the job takes it on to `SETTLED`. A charge appended to an account that had reached `SETTLED` drags it *backwards* — to `UNSETTLED` when the account holds no credits at all, or to `PARTIALLY_SETTLED` when it does — and it does so even when `stay_status` is already `DEPARTED`. These backwards edges are what make the two dimensions genuinely independent: a departed guest may still be unsettled, and a settled account may still be in house. The one exception to re-derivation is the terminal `TRANSFERRED_TO_CITY_LEDGER`, which §3.8 describes and which is never re-derived away.

**Posting a line by hand** is available to a `NightAuditor`, `FrontDeskAgent`, or `Controller`, and it names the `business_date_id`, the `charge_type`, the signed `amount`, and optionally a `folio_id`, a `ledger_account_id`, a `reason_code`, and a `memo`. Only the four client-postable charge classes of §2.8 are accepted here. `source_channel` is stamped `FRONT_DESK` when the caller is a `FrontDeskAgent` and `NIGHT_AUDIT` for every other caller, and `posted_by_user_id` is stamped from the acting operator. A line naming a guest account re-derives that account's `charges_total_amount`, `credits_total_amount`, `balance_amount`, and `settlement_status`; a line naming a ledger account re-derives that account's `current_balance_amount`; and either way the named day's revenue aggregates and its `ledger_control_amount` are re-derived, with any changed revenue aggregate superseding that day's `DRAFT` and `DISTRIBUTED` reports. A one-sided `LEDGER_TRANSFER` or `DEPOSIT_APPLIED` — a line naming only one side of a transfer that ought to have two — is deliberately accepted, and it is exactly what pushes `ledger_control_amount` off zero and blocks the day from balancing until someone reverses it. Journal lines are discoverable by `folio_id`, `ledger_account_id`, `business_date_id`, `outlet_ticket_id`, `allowance_id`, `reverses_posting_id`, `charge_type`, and `source_channel`; that listing is the only way to see lines minted by side effects.

**Reversing a line** is available to a `NightAuditor` or `Controller`, takes an optional `memo`, and creates a new line whose `amount` is the negation of the original's, copying the original's `folio_id`, `ledger_account_id`, `business_date_id`, and `charge_type`, and setting `reverses_posting_id` to the original. The original row is never mutated. Every **net** figure the original moved returns to exactly its pre-original value — the account's `balance_amount`, the ledger account's `current_balance_amount`, the day's revenue aggregates, and `ledger_control_amount` — while the two **gross** figures do not, for the reason given in §2.8: one of `charges_total_amount` or `credits_total_amount` grows by the reversal magnitude and `settlement_status` is re-derived from the new pair rather than restored. Reversing a one-sided `LEDGER_TRANSFER` returns `ledger_control_amount` to exactly `0.00`, which is the ordinary way an out-of-balance day is repaired.

**Card authorizations** are held against a guest account and decay with the clock. A `FrontDeskAgent` or `NightAuditor` records one by naming the `folio_id`, the `obtained_at` instant the issuer approved it, and the `authorized_amount` held. It is born with `status` `APPROVED`, `follow_up_status` `NOT_REQUIRED`, and null in `decline_code`, `captured_amount`, `resolved_by_user_id`, and `follow_up_note`. Its `aging_days` is the whole days between `obtained_at` and the logical clock, floored — computed as the elapsed seconds divided by the eighty-six thousand four hundred seconds in a day and truncated downwards — and it is re-derived on every read of the row and on every write that touches it. Real wall-clock time is never consulted. Whenever `aging_days` reaches or exceeds the property's `card_authorization_valid_days`, an `APPROVED` authorization lapses to `EXPIRED`; the test is inclusive, so the authorization is still good on the last day before the limit and lapses the instant the count reaches it. This lapse is a system act with no endpoint of its own: it can happen on the authorization's very first read, and once lapsed the authorization no longer satisfies the credit-card departure gate, so a departure attempt then fails with `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_SHORTFALL`. Authorizations are discoverable by `folio_id`, `status`, and `follow_up_status`.

**Recording a decline** is the `FrontDeskAgent`'s or `NightAuditor`'s move, taking the issuer's `decline_code` — one of `INSUFFICIENT_FUNDS`, `EXPIRED_CARD`, `DO_NOT_HONOR`, or `SUSPECTED_FRAUD` — and driving `APPROVED → DECLINED`. An authorization in any other state is refused with `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_NOT_APPROVED` (HTTP 409). A decline sets three things in motion beyond the status change: `follow_up_status` is forced from `NOT_REQUIRED` to `PENDING` on the parallel dimension, the owning account's `settlement_hold` becomes true and thereby blocks its departure, and the account's operating day recomputes `declined_authorization_count` upwards, which blocks that day from reaching a balanced state. One declined card therefore stalls both a guest and a night audit until somebody works it.

**Capturing** is reserved to the `NightAuditor` and drives `APPROVED → CAPTURED`. It takes the `captured_amount`, the `business_date_id` the money belongs to, and optionally a `ledger_account_id`. Its gates run in a deliberate order: the aging recompute happens *before* the status gate, so an authorization that lapses during this very request is reported as `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_EXPIRED` (HTTP 409) rather than as a state problem; only then is `status` checked, refusing a declined or already-captured authorization with `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_NOT_APPROVED`; and finally `captured_amount` must be greater than zero and no greater than `authorized_amount`, else `ERR_CAPTURE_EXCEEDS_AUTHORIZATION` (HTTP 422). Success stamps `captured_amount`, appends a `PAYMENT` line of the negated capture with `source_channel` `FRONT_DESK`, and re-derives the account's `credits_total_amount`, `balance_amount`, and `settlement_status`. The day's revenue aggregates do not move, because a capture is a collection and not a sale.

**Following up** advances the second dimension and is available to a `FrontDeskAgent` or `NightAuditor`. It takes a `follow_up_action` of `CONTACT`, `RESOLVE`, or `ESCALATE`, plus an optional `follow_up_note` that replaces whatever note was there before. `CONTACT` drives `PENDING → CONTACTED`, refuses any other state with `ERR_FOLLOW_UP_NOT_PENDING` (HTTP 409), stores the note, and leaves the account held. `RESOLVE` drives `CONTACTED → RESOLVED`, refuses any other state with `ERR_FOLLOW_UP_NOT_CONTACTED` (HTTP 409), and additionally requires that the owning account already carry at least one `PAYMENT` line, else `ERR_FOLLOW_UP_NO_REPLACEMENT_TENDER` (HTTP 409) — an unresolved card may only be declared resolved once replacement money has actually arrived. It stamps `resolved_by_user_id`, recomputes `settlement_hold` to false, and recomputes the day's `declined_authorization_count` downwards, unblocking both the departure and the balancing step. `ESCALATE` also drives `CONTACTED → ESCALATED` and also refuses any other state with `ERR_FOLLOW_UP_NOT_CONTACTED`, and it too stamps `resolved_by_user_id` — but it leaves `settlement_hold` true and `declined_authorization_count` unchanged, handing the outstanding balance to the city-ledger sweep instead. Throughout all of this the authorization's `status` stays `DECLINED` forever: the two dimensions never constrain each other, and a fully resolved follow-up never turns a declined card back into an approved one.

### 3.8. The Rest of the Close: Sealing Tax, Sweeping the City Ledger, Balancing and Publishing

The operating day advances through a fixed pipeline, and every step but one is the `NightAuditor`'s. The room-and-tax pass of §3.3 carried the day from `OPEN` to `ROOM_POSTING`; what follows takes it to `CLOSED`.

**Sealing charges** is the second step and drives `ROOM_POSTING → CHARGES_SEALED`; a day in any other state is refused with `ERR_DATE_NOT_READY_TO_SEAL` (HTTP 409). Its purpose is to true up occupancy tax, because the tax posted during the room-and-tax pass was provisional. The provisional basis taxed each `ROOM` and `NO_SHOW` line on its own gross amount and rounded each result separately, and it could not see the allowances that had not yet been reviewed. The final basis is different in two ways at once: for each guest account it forms **one** base by taking the signed sum of every line of that account stamped to this day whose `charge_type` is `ROOM`, `NO_SHOW`, or `ALLOWANCE` — credits included, which is what makes the base net rather than gross — and it rounds **once**, after multiplying that single base by `occupancy_tax_rate_pct` and dividing by one hundred. No other charge class enters either basis: `OCCUPANCY_TAX`, `TAX_TRUE_UP`, `LATE_CHECKOUT`, `MISC_CHARGE`, `OUTLET_TRANSFER`, `PAYMENT`, `DEPOSIT_APPLIED`, `LEDGER_TRANSFER`, and `CITY_LEDGER_FEE` lines are all invisible to the tax base.

For every account holding at least one taxable line on the day, the true-up is the final figure minus the signed sum of the `OCCUPANCY_TAX` lines already posted to that account for that day, rounded to 2 decimals. When that delta is exactly `0.00` **no line is written at all**; otherwise a single `TAX_TRUE_UP` line of the delta is appended. The delta is signed and comes out negative whenever an allowance landed on the account after its provisional tax was posted, since the net base is then smaller than the gross one. Accounts with no taxable line on the day are skipped entirely. Each appended line re-derives its account's `balance_amount` and `settlement_status`, and the day's `tax_revenue_amount` and `total_revenue_amount` are recomputed from the appended lines. Once the day is `CHARGES_SEALED`, every charge-bearing write naming it fails with `ERR_CHARGES_ALREADY_SEALED`.

**Verifying settlements** is the third step and drives `CHARGES_SEALED → SETTLEMENT_REVIEW`, with the batch-level mechanics given in §3.5. It requires the day to be `CHARGES_SEALED` or already `SETTLEMENT_REVIEW`, else `ERR_DATE_NOT_READY_TO_VERIFY` (HTTP 409). Before it reconciles anything it checks mandatory coverage: for every outlet of the property whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `requires_shift_settlement` is true, at least one settlement batch must exist for this day and that outlet, else the whole run fails with `ERR_SETTLEMENTS_UNRESOLVED` (HTTP 409). It then stamps `system_amount` and `variance_amount` on each `DECLARED` batch, resolves each to `MATCHED` or `VARIANCE_FLAGGED`, and recomputes the day's `settlement_variance_total_amount` and `unresolved_settlement_batch_count`. Running it a second time on a day already in `SETTLEMENT_REVIEW` is legitimate and leaves the day's status exactly where it is: it re-reconciles only the batches that are still `DECLARED` and leaves `MATCHED`, `ACCEPTED_WITH_VARIANCE`, `VARIANCE_FLAGGED`, and `RECOUNT_REQUESTED` batches untouched. That re-run is the only way a redeclared batch is ever judged again, and without it the recount cycle of §3.5 could never terminate.

**Sweeping the city ledger** moves the unpaid balances of departed direct-bill guests onto a receivable account, and it may run at any point while the day is `ROOM_POSTING`, `CHARGES_SEALED`, `SETTLEMENT_REVIEW`, or `REOPENED` — outside that set it is refused with `ERR_DATE_NOT_READY_TO_BALANCE` (HTTP 409). It is deliberately exempt from the charge-seal gate, because it writes transfers rather than revenue. Its input set is narrow: guest accounts of this property whose `stay_status` is `DEPARTED`, whose `settlement_method` is `DIRECT_BILL`, whose `settlement_status` is not already `TRANSFERRED_TO_CITY_LEDGER`, and whose `balance_amount` is strictly greater than `0.00`. Accounts still in house, accounts already settled to zero, card and cash accounts, and accounts swept by an earlier run are all excluded, which makes a second sweep of the same day a genuine no-op that rewrites all three of its totals to zero.

Accounts are swept in ascending `created_at` order, never by identifier, and each one produces three lines and one status change. The fee is the account's `balance_amount` multiplied by `city_ledger_fee_pct` divided by one hundred, rounded half up to 2 decimals **for that account alone**. A `CITY_LEDGER_FEE` line of the positive fee is appended to the account; a `LEDGER_TRANSFER` line of the negated sum of balance and fee is appended to the account; and a matching `LEDGER_TRANSFER` line of the positive sum of balance and fee is appended against the property's `CITY_LEDGER` ledger account. The account's `balance_amount` therefore lands on exactly `0.00` and its `settlement_status` becomes the terminal `TRANSFERRED_TO_CITY_LEDGER`, after which no further tender may ever be applied to it. Three day-level figures are then stamped, replacing whatever a prior sweep left: `city_ledger_swept_folio_count` counts the accounts swept, `city_ledger_transferred_balance_total_amount` sums their pre-fee balances, and `city_ledger_fee_total_amount` sums the already-rounded per-account fees. That last figure is a round-then-sum total and the distinction is observable — rounding one percentage of the summed balances can differ by a cent from summing the individually rounded fees, and only the per-account rounding is correct. The ledger account's `current_balance_amount` rises by the swept total, and because every transfer is written as a balanced equal-and-opposite pair, the day's `ledger_control_amount` is left at exactly `0.00`.

**Balancing** is the fourth step and drives both `SETTLEMENT_REVIEW → BALANCED` and `REOPENED → BALANCED`; any other starting state is refused with `ERR_DATE_NOT_READY_TO_BALANCE`. Its first act, performed *before* any precondition is evaluated, is a lapse pass: every card authorization belonging to this property's guest accounts whose `status` is `APPROVED` and whose `aging_days` has reached `card_authorization_valid_days` becomes `EXPIRED`. Only then are the three assertions checked, and each has its own refusal: `unresolved_settlement_batch_count` must be zero, else `ERR_SETTLEMENTS_UNRESOLVED`; `ledger_control_amount` must be exactly `0.00`, else `ERR_LEDGERS_OUT_OF_BALANCE` (HTTP 409); and `declined_authorization_count` must be zero, else `ERR_DECLINED_AUTH_UNRESOLVED`. The ledger control figure is the rounded sum of every `LEDGER_TRANSFER` and `DEPOSIT_APPLIED` line of the day, counting both the guest-account side and the ledger-account side, which is why a one-sided line posted by hand is the only thing that can push it off zero and why reversing that line is the ordinary repair. Note what balancing does *not* require: a variance may remain in `settlement_variance_total_amount`, provided every batch carrying it has been either matched or formally accepted.

**Publishing the day's numbers** happens through a revenue report, which a `RevenueManager` or `NightAuditor` creates by naming the `business_date_id`, the `recipient_role_list` the report is meant for, the `expected_business_date_version` token described in §2.11, and optionally the `pace_basis`. The named day must be in `SETTLEMENT_REVIEW`, `BALANCED`, or `REOPENED`, else `ERR_DATE_NOT_READY_TO_BALANCE`. A report is a snapshot, not a view: creation copies `rooms_available_count`, `rooms_occupied_count`, `occupancy_pct`, `adr_amount`, `revpar_amount`, `room_revenue_amount`, `tax_revenue_amount`, `outlet_revenue_amount`, and `total_revenue_amount` off the day and freezes them into the report row, sets `status` to `DRAFT`, stamps `generated_by_user_id`, sets every earlier `DRAFT` or `DISTRIBUTED` report of the same day to `SUPERSEDED`, and increments the day's `version`. On a version mismatch nothing at all is written: no snapshot, no supersession, no change to the day's `version`, and the call fails with `ERR_STALE_BUSINESS_DATE_VERSION`.

The day's occupancy statistics — refreshed on every line stamped to the day, on every pipeline transition, and again at report creation — are derived as follows. `rooms_available_count` is the property's `room_count_total` less the day's `out_of_order_room_count`. `rooms_occupied_count` is the count of distinct guest accounts holding a `ROOM` line on the day that no other line reverses. `occupancy_pct` is one hundred times the occupied count divided by the available count; `adr_amount` is `room_night_revenue_amount` divided by the occupied count; `revpar_amount` is `room_night_revenue_amount` divided by the available count; and `total_revenue_amount` is `room_revenue_amount` plus `tax_revenue_amount` plus `outlet_revenue_amount`. Each ratio is rounded half up to 2 decimals, and each has an empty-set case that yields exactly `0.00` rather than an error or a null: zero rooms available yields `0.00` occupancy and `0.00` revenue per available room, and zero rooms occupied yields an average daily rate of `0.00`.

The pace comparison is frozen into the report at creation from the day's two stored prior-year benchmarks. When `pace_basis` is `CALENDAR_ALIGNED` — the default when the caller supplies none — `benchmark_room_revenue_amount` is copied from the day's `benchmark_calendar_room_revenue_amount`, comparing the day against the same calendar day a year earlier. When it is `WEEKDAY_ALIGNED`, the benchmark comes from `benchmark_weekday_room_revenue_amount` instead, comparing the day against the same weekday a year earlier, which is a different calendar date and therefore a different denominator. `pace_index_pct` is one hundred times the day's `room_revenue_amount` divided by whichever benchmark was selected, rounded to 2 decimals, and it is exactly `0.00` when that benchmark is `0.00` rather than a division error. Only room revenue enters the numerator; tax and outlet revenue never do. Identical revenue therefore yields two different pace figures under the two bases, which is why the basis is recorded on the report row. Reports are discoverable by `business_date_id`, `status`, and `pace_basis`.

**Distributing** a report is the `RevenueManager`'s or `Controller`'s act, driving `DRAFT → DISTRIBUTED`. It optionally replaces `recipient_role_list` on the way through. A report that is not `DRAFT` is refused with `ERR_REPORT_NOT_DRAFT` (HTTP 409), and a report whose `recipient_role_list` is an empty array is refused with `ERR_DISTRIBUTION_LIST_EMPTY` (HTTP 422) — the list must name at least one role. Success stamps `distributed_at` from the logical clock and increments the report's `version`.

**Closing the day** is the final step and drives `BALANCED → CLOSED`. A day that is not `BALANCED` is refused with `ERR_DATE_NOT_BALANCED` (HTTP 409), and a day with no `DISTRIBUTED` report of its own is refused with `ERR_REPORT_NOT_DISTRIBUTED` (HTTP 409) — the numbers must have been published to somebody before the day may be shut. Closing recomputes the owning accounting period's `subsystem_total_amount` to include this day's `total_revenue_amount` and increments the day's `version`.

**Reopening** is the `Controller`'s alone, driving `CLOSED → REOPENED`. A day that is not `CLOSED` is refused with `ERR_DATE_NOT_CLOSED` (HTTP 409), and a day whose accounting period is no longer `OPEN` is refused with `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_OPEN` (HTTP 409). A reopen raises `reopen_count` by one, sets every `DRAFT` or `DISTRIBUTED` report of the day to `SUPERSEDED`, and — because no distributed report survives that — recomputes the period's `subsystem_total_amount` so that the day no longer contributes to it. Charge-bearing writes are accepted again until the day is re-balanced, and re-balancing from `REOPENED` runs exactly the same lapse pass and the same three assertions as before. A reopened day must then acquire a fresh distributed report before it can close again, since its old one was superseded.

### 3.9. Month-End Tie-Out, Archival and the Retention Horizon

The last act of the accounting month belongs to the `Controller`, and it is an assertion rather than a computation: what this service says the month earned must equal what the general ledger says, to the cent.

**Tying out** a period is triggered by supplying the single input `gl_control_total_amount`, the control total the `Controller` reads off the general ledger. Two gates guard it. The period must be `OPEN` or `TIE_OUT_FAILED`, else `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_TIEABLE` (HTTP 409) — a period already `TIED_OUT` or mid-attempt is not tieable. And every operating day belonging to the period must have `status` `CLOSED` or `ARCHIVED`, else `ERR_PERIOD_HAS_OPEN_DATES` (HTTP 409). That second gate is a hard refusal, not a filter: a single day left `OPEN`, `ROOM_POSTING`, `CHARGES_SEALED`, `SETTLEMENT_REVIEW`, `BALANCED`, or `REOPENED` blocks the whole tie-out rather than being quietly excluded from the arithmetic.

The attempt stores the supplied `gl_control_total_amount`, recomputes `subsystem_total_amount` as the rounded sum of `total_revenue_amount` over the period's `CLOSED` and `ARCHIVED` days, and computes `tie_out_variance_amount` as `gl_control_total_amount` minus `subsystem_total_amount`, rounded half up to 2 decimals. The period passes through `TIE_OUT_IN_PROGRESS` and resolves inside the very same request, and the tolerance is zero: only an absolute variance of exactly `0.00` passes, so a one-cent difference fails. A passing attempt sets `status` to `TIED_OUT`, stamps `tied_out_at` from the logical clock and `tied_out_by_user_id` from the acting `Controller`, computes `audit_trail_retention_until_date`, and archives every `CLOSED` day of the period — days already `ARCHIVED` are left alone. A failing attempt sets `status` to `TIE_OUT_FAILED`, raises `tie_out_attempt_count` by one, archives nothing, and leaves `audit_trail_retention_until_date` null. Either way the period's `version` increments.

A failed tie-out may be retried, driving `TIE_OUT_FAILED → TIE_OUT_IN_PROGRESS`, and the retry re-reads `gl_control_total_amount` from the new request before re-evaluating the same fork — which is how a mistyped control total is corrected. The retries are bounded: an attempt made once `tie_out_attempt_count` has reached three is refused with `ERR_TIE_OUT_ATTEMPT_LIMIT_REACHED` (HTTP 409). `TIED_OUT` is terminal.

Archival is the strongest gate in the service. An `ARCHIVED` day accepts no write of any kind, and every attempt to write against it — a posting, a check, a batch, an allowance, a report, any pipeline step, even a reopen — is refused with `ERR_BUSINESS_DATE_ARCHIVED`, which as §2.12 explains outranks every other rejection including the state and existence checks that would otherwise fire first. Because a reopen additionally requires the owning period to be `OPEN`, and a tied-out period never returns to `OPEN`, a tied-out month is closed for good.

The retention horizon is computed from the period's `period_month` alone — no day, line, or report participates. It is the last calendar day of that month advanced by seven years, expressed as a calendar date, so a period covering a 31-day month retains until the 31st of the same month seven years later. A 29 February anchor lands on 28 February of the target year, since the target year has no 29th. Periods are never created through an endpoint of their own; they are minted on demand when an operating day is opened, as §3.1 describes, and they are reachable by listing them filtered by `property_id`, `period_month`, and `status`.
