# Airline Catering & Inflight Meal Production API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

An airline catering unit is a factory with a departure board bolted to its wall. Every tray it builds is committed against an aircraft that will push back whether or not the food reached the galley, and every unit of food it produces is simultaneously a food-safety record, a customs-controlled commodity and a line on a contractual invoice. This document specifies the service that governs that factory end to end: the schedule feed that tells the kitchen how many passengers are booked in each cabin, the special meal orders captured against individual seats and the cutoff after which those orders freeze, the production plan that distributes demand across the chill, hot and dry stores under HACCP discipline, the equipment build with numbered security seals and pinned loading manifests, the timed high-lift delivery to the aircraft stand, and finally the post-flight reconciliation of what was actually uplifted, consumed, returned, re-used, donated or destroyed.

The operational pain the service exists to remove is drift between four records that must agree but historically do not: what the airline booked, what the kitchen produced, what the ramp loaded, and what the invoice charges. Accordingly, the service treats unit counts and money as conserved quantities rather than as reports. Voiding a loaded cart item returns the flight's unit balance to exactly the figure it held before the item was added; withdrawing an accepted audit finding returns the airline's accrued penalty to exactly its pre-acceptance value; a waste manifest replaced after a customs rejection recomputes the identical sealed mass from the same immutable disposition lines. Where two parties could plausibly overwrite each other's view of the same aggregate — a manifest draft, a reconciliation, a cart seal — the service demands the writer's last observed version of the parent record and refuses the write outright when that version has moved on.

Two further constraints shape the whole design. First, the service never reads a wall clock: every cutoff, shelf-life deadline, slot window and lateness measurement is evaluated against a logical clock that test harnesses and operators set explicitly, so an entire service day can be replayed deterministically. Second, food safety and commercial convenience are deliberately separated by role. The quality officer who records a temperature probe reading may not release the batch that reading gates; the load master who fits a cart's seal may not issue the manifest that pins it. These separations are not advisory — they are enforced request-by-request, and a violation is a rejected request rather than a logged warning.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contract-level rules that hold across every resource in the service. All collections are mounted as flat peers at the root of the service — there is no shared API prefix — and resources that belong to one another are related by identifier fields carried in request bodies rather than by deep URL nesting.

### 2.1. Actors, Identity Headers and Role-Based Access Control

Every request other than the logical-clock control call must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, naming the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity that user is acting in. A request whose `X-User-ID` is absent or does not resolve to a known user is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401); a header present but blank or wholly whitespace counts as absent, and surrounding whitespace is otherwise trimmed off the identifier before it is used. "Resolving to a known user" bites only when the declared role is `AirlineRep`, whose identifier must name an existing `Airline` row; for every other role the identifier is opaque and any non-blank value authenticates. A request that authenticates successfully but whose role is not among those the target operation permits is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403), and an `X-User-Role` that is absent altogether or that is not one of the seven spellings below is treated the same way — it is never a member of any permitted list, so it too yields `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`.

Seven roles exist, spelled exactly as follows: `PlatformAdmin`, `ScheduleFeedOperator`, `AirlineRep`, `ProductionSupervisor`, `QaOfficer`, `LoadMaster`, and `ComplianceOfficer`. Their remits partition the operational day. `PlatformAdmin` owns reference data only — airline contracts, meal specifications, high-lift trucks and aircraft stands — and is deliberately barred from ingesting flights, planning production, building carts, delivering or reconciling. `ScheduleFeedOperator` owns the inbound schedule feed: flight intake, passenger-count refreshes, retimes, count locking, delay recording and flight cancellation. `AirlineRep` is the customer's own representative and captures or cancels special meal orders, raises findings against its own airline and withdraws findings. `ProductionSupervisor` runs the kitchen: production planning, batch start, completion and release, plus specification substitution on confirmed special meal orders. `QaOfficer` is the only role permitted to record HACCP temperature measurements, and is deliberately not permitted to release the batch those measurements gate. `LoadMaster` owns cart registration, item loading and voiding, build sign-off, sealing, manifest opening and issue, slot reservation, delivery events and recovery runs. `ComplianceOfficer` owns post-flight uplift reconciliation, unused-meal disposition, bonded waste handling and the audit docket. Every role may read every list and detail endpoint, and every role may set the logical clock.

Identity semantics differ by role, and this is what ties `X-User-ID` to resource ownership. When `X-User-Role` is `AirlineRep`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Airline`; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Airline` compare the resource's airline identifier — resolved through the owning `Flight` or `MealSpecification` when the row carries no direct `airline_id` — against `X-User-ID`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_AIRLINE_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. When `X-User-Role` is any of `PlatformAdmin`, `ScheduleFeedOperator`, `ProductionSupervisor`, `QaOfficer`, `LoadMaster` or `ComplianceOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record such an identifier — as `recorded_by_user_id` on a HACCP check taken by a `QaOfficer`, as `released_by_user_id` on a batch released by a `ProductionSupervisor`, and as `sealed_by_user_id` and `issued_by_user_id` on carts and manifests handled by a `LoadMaster` — but it never compares it to any entity's `id`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`, followed by twenty randomly chosen lowercase alphanumeric characters.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. It is the one timestamp in the service read from the host's real clock rather than from the logical clock of §2.4 — it is envelope metadata about the transport, never a recorded fact about an entity, and no stored field is ever derived from it.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and Deterministic List Ordering

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: its default is 10, its minimum accepted value is 1, and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: its default is 0, its minimum accepted value is 0 and it carries no maximum — an `offset` past the end of the collection simply returns an empty `data` array with the true `total_count` and `has_more` false. A `limit` or `offset` outside those bounds, or of the wrong JSON type, is a schema failure and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; because query values arrive as strings, "the right type" means a string of optionally-signed decimal digits and nothing else, so `1.0` or `ten` fails. A boolean-valued filter such as a flight's `production_hold` or a production batch's `is_overflow` accepts only the spellings `true` and `false`, matched without regard to case, and anything else is rejected the same way. Each list endpoint's query string is a closed whitelist of `limit`, `offset` and the filters that endpoint names: an unrecognised parameter is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and that whitelist check runs before `limit` and `offset` are range-checked. Detail reads declare no query parameters and ignore whatever query string they are given.

Unless a specific collection states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending, so paging through a collection is stable. Because `created_at` is a logical-clock reading and the clock need not move between two writes, rows sharing an identical `created_at` are ordered by the sequence in which the service inserted them, oldest insertion first; that tie-break is the ordering's second key everywhere the default order is used. List responses populate `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` in the `meta` object, where `total_count` counts every row matching the supplied filters before paging and `has_more` is true exactly when rows remain beyond the current page. One collection carries a documented secondary ordering that overrides the default: the line array on an uplift record is always ordered by the owning specification's `spec_code` ascending. The HACCP check list is *not* such an exception — it takes the default `created_at`-then-insertion order like every other list, which within any one batch-and-step pair is necessarily ascending `sequence_no` because the series is minted in insertion order, but which interleaves the series of two different steps of the same batch by the order the readings were actually taken rather than by `sequence_no`.

### 2.4. The Deterministic Logical Clock

The service never consults a wall clock. Every temporal rule in this document — special meal cutoffs, batch release deadlines, delivery slot bands, dispatch lead times and lateness measurement — reads a single logical clock instant. Before that clock is ever set, its value is `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. A dedicated control operation accepts a `current_utc` instant and moves the clock to it; time only moves when that operation is called, and it may be moved forward or backward. The call mutates no entity, is permitted for every role, and is the one operation in the service exempt from both the authentication check and the authorisation check — a request carrying neither `X-User-ID` nor `X-User-Role`, or carrying a role spelling that exists nowhere in §2.1, still sets the clock. It answers `200 OK` with a `data` object of the single field `current_utc`, echoing the clock's new reading in the same three-fractional-digit UTC form as every other timestamp the service writes. A `current_utc` value that does not parse as an ISO 8601 UTC instant is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; "as UTC" admits both an explicit `Z` or `+00:00` designator and a bare instant with no designator at all, which is read as UTC, while any non-zero offset such as `+01:00` is refused.

Because the clock is explicit, instants recorded by the service are exactly the clock's value at the moment the recording request was processed, not an approximation of it. Every timestamp the service writes — `requested_at`, `counts_locked_at`, `measured_at_utc`, `released_at`, `voided_at`, `issued_at`, `dispatched_at_utc`, `arrived_at_utc` and `delivered_at_utc` — is the logical clock reading of its own request.

### 2.5. Common Resource Properties and Version Semantics

Every persisted resource exposes an `id`, which is a server-minted UUID carrying no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp rendered as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits. Almost every resource additionally exposes an `updated_at` timestamp in the same format and an integer `version`. A resource is created with `version` equal to 1; every mutation that changes the row increments `version` by exactly 1 and refreshes `updated_at`. This applies to mutations reached indirectly through a cascade just as it applies to mutations requested directly: when a batch rejection voids a cart item and knocks the owning cart back off its built state, both the cart item and the cart have their `version` incremented and their `updated_at` refreshed. The increment is counted once per row per request: when a single request reaches the same row more than once — directly and again down a cascade hop, or down two cascade hops that meet on it, or once per item in a loop that revisits one parent — that row's `version` advances by exactly 1 for the request as a whole rather than once per hop, and the `updated_at` it is left carrying is that request's own logical-clock reading either way.

The single exception is the HACCP check. A HACCP check is an immutable food-safety record: it carries only `id` and `created_at`, and both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from it entirely. There is no operation that updates or deletes a HACCP check.

### 2.6. Left-to-Right Resolution and the Validation Priority Ladder

Path parameters are resolved left to right. For a nested path, the outer identifier is resolved before the inner one, so voiding an item under a cart that does not exist reports the missing cart rather than the missing item. Identifier resolution failures are typed by the entity: a missing `Flight` reports `ERR_FLIGHT_NOT_FOUND`, a missing `ProductionBatch` reports `ERR_BATCH_NOT_FOUND`, a missing `Cart` reports `ERR_CART_NOT_FOUND`, and a missing `Airline`, `MealSpecification`, `SpecialMealOrder`, `HaccpCheck`, `CartItem`, `LoadingManifest`, `Truck`, `Stand`, `DeliveryRun`, `UpliftRecord`, `WasteManifest` or `AuditFinding` reports `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`. All four carry HTTP 404.

Every request is checked against an ordered ladder of validation bands. Bands are evaluated in ascending priority order, and **the first band that reports a violation short-circuits the request — nothing after it runs**. When a single request would fail two different bands, the response therefore always carries the error of the lower-numbered band. The ladder is:

1. **Priority 10 — authentication.** `X-User-ID` is present and names a known user. Violation: `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint except the logical-clock control call.
2. **Priority 20 — authorisation.** The acting role appears in the target operation's permitted role list. Violation: `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403). Applies to every endpoint except the logical-clock control call, which declares no permitted role list and so cannot fail this band.
3. **Priority 30 — request shape.** The body parses; every required field is present; every field matches its declared JSON type; every enum-typed value is a declared member of that enum; every integer respects its declared bounds; every ISO 8601 instant parses as UTC. Unknown fields are rejected rather than ignored. Violation: `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Applies to every endpoint that takes a request body or query parameters.
4. **Priority 40 — path existence.** Every identifier segment in the path resolves to an existing row, checked left to right, with the typed codes listed above. Applies to every endpoint with a path parameter.
5. **Priority 50 — ownership.** An `AirlineRep` may only read or write rows whose airline resolves to its own `X-User-ID`, following the owning `Flight` or `MealSpecification` where the row has no direct `airline_id` of its own. Violation: `ERR_AIRLINE_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (403). Applies to every endpoint an `AirlineRep` may call.
6. **Priority 60 — body reference existence.** Every identifier carried in the request body resolves to an existing row, checked in the field order the endpoint declares. Violation: `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (404). Applies to every endpoint whose body carries an identifier.
7. **Priority 65 — optimistic concurrency.** Where an endpoint declares a parent-version field, its value equals the parent row's current `version`. A `Flight` mismatch reports `ERR_STALE_FLIGHT_VERSION` and a `Cart` mismatch reports `ERR_STALE_CART_VERSION`, both HTTP 412. Applies to exactly three endpoints: `POST /flights/{id}/loading-manifests`, `POST /flights/{id}/uplift-records` and `POST /carts/{id}/seals`.
8. **Priority 70 — state.** The target row is in a state from which the requested transition is defined. A `Flight` in the wrong state reports `ERR_INVALID_FLIGHT_STATE`, a `ProductionBatch` reports `ERR_INVALID_BATCH_STATE`, a `Cart` reports `ERR_INVALID_CART_STATE`, a `DeliveryRun` reports `ERR_INVALID_RUN_STATE`, and any other entity reports `ERR_INVALID_RESOURCE_STATE` — all HTTP 409. Three specialisations share this same band: writing passenger counts or capturing a special meal order once the flight has left `SCHEDULED` reports `ERR_FLIGHT_COUNTS_LOCKED`; loading a cart item from a batch that is not `RELEASED` reports `ERR_BATCH_NOT_RELEASED`; and dispatching a run whose manifest is not `ISSUED` reports `ERR_MANIFEST_NOT_ISSUED`. Applies to every state-advancing endpoint.
9. **Priority 80 — food-safety gates.** These carry HTTP 422 and apply to `POST /production-batches/{id}/start`, `POST /production-batches/{id}/release` and `POST /carts/{id}/items`. On release, four checks run in this pinned order and the first failure short-circuits the rest: first `ERR_HACCP_STEP_MISSING` when a step the specification requires has no recorded check at all; second `ERR_HACCP_HOLD_ACTIVE` when the newest check for a required step failed; third `ERR_HACCP_REWORK_LIMIT` when the batch has consumed its whole rework allowance; and fourth `ERR_RELEASE_DEADLINE_PASSED` when the logical clock has moved strictly past the batch's release deadline — release alone is inclusive at the deadline instant itself, so a release requested at exactly that instant still succeeds. Starting a batch at or after its release deadline also reports `ERR_RELEASE_DEADLINE_PASSED`, and so does loading cart items from such a batch; those two are exclusive at the instant, so for them the deadline instant itself is already too late, and that asymmetry against release is deliberate.
10. **Priority 85 — segregation of duties and bonded-waste gates.** These carry HTTP 422 and apply to `POST /loading-manifests/{id}/issue`, `POST /uplift-records/{id}/dispositions`, `POST /waste-manifests` and `POST /flights/{id}/production-plan`. The `LoadMaster` issuing a manifest must differ from the `sealed_by_user_id` of every cart entering the snapshot, or the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. A `BONDED` airline's dispositions must record zero re-used and zero donated units, or the request is refused with `ERR_BONDED_REUSE_FORBIDDEN`, and its waste manifests must use a permitted disposal route or the request is refused with `ERR_BONDED_WASTE_DISPOSAL_INVALID`. Planning production for a `SUSPENDED` airline, or for a flight carrying a production hold, is refused with `ERR_AIRLINE_SUSPENDED`.
11. **Priority 90 — cross-row coherence.** These carry HTTP 422 and apply to `POST /carts/{id}/items`, `POST /special-meal-orders` and `POST /special-meal-orders/{id}/changes`. A cart item's source batch must sit in the same store zone as the cart's own storage class (`ERR_STORAGE_CLASS_MISMATCH`) and must belong to the same flight as the cart (`ERR_CART_FLIGHT_MISMATCH`). A referenced meal specification must be `ACTIVE` (`ERR_SPEC_RETIRED`) and must belong to the same airline as the flight (`ERR_SPEC_AIRLINE_MISMATCH`).
12. **Priority 100 — capacity, uniqueness and conservation.** These carry HTTP 422 and apply to nine endpoints: `POST /carts`, `POST /carts/{id}/items`, `POST /carts/{id}/seals`, `POST /special-meal-orders`, `POST /production-batches/{id}/complete`, `POST /flights/{id}/production-plan`, `POST /flights/{id}/uplift-records`, `POST /uplift-records/{id}/dispositions` and `POST /waste-manifests`. A cart's active units may not exceed its declared capacity (`ERR_CART_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`); zone allocation may not leave units unplaced after the overflow chain (`ERR_STORE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`); a seal number is unique across carts and waste manifests alike (`ERR_SEAL_NUMBER_DUPLICATE`); a seat may hold only one non-terminal special meal order per flight (`ERR_DUPLICATE_SEAT_ORDER`); produced quantity may not exceed planned quantity (`ERR_PRODUCED_QTY_EXCEEDS_PLAN`); submitted consumed plus returned units must equal the units actually uplifted (`ERR_UPLIFT_UNITS_MISMATCH`); and each disposition line's re-used plus donated plus destroyed units must equal that line's returned units (`ERR_DISPOSITION_UNITS_MISMATCH`).
13. **Priority 110 — clock and window rules.** These carry HTTP 422 and apply to `POST /special-meal-orders`, `POST /special-meal-orders/{id}/changes`, `POST /delivery-runs` and `POST /delivery-runs/{id}/recovery-runs`. Capturing or changing a special meal order at or after the flight's cutoff instant reports `ERR_SPECIAL_MEAL_CUTOFF_PASSED`; an empty or inverted delivery window reports `ERR_SLOT_WINDOW_INVALID`; a window outside the flight's permitted slot band reports `ERR_SLOT_OUTSIDE_FLIGHT_WINDOW`; a truck in maintenance or a closed stand reports `ERR_SLOT_RESOURCE_UNAVAILABLE`; a truck already committed within its buffer reports `ERR_TRUCK_SLOT_CONFLICT`; and a stand pushed past its concurrent-run limit reports `ERR_STAND_CAPACITY_CONFLICT` — and on a slot reservation or a recovery run those five are evaluated in exactly that order.
14. **Priority 120 — the contractual money ceiling.** Checked last, deliberately, so that every safety, referential, capacity and temporal problem is reported before a money problem. Adding cart items whose resulting per-passenger cost would exceed the airline's contractual cap is refused with `ERR_COST_CAP_EXCEEDED` (422). Applies to `POST /carts/{id}/items` alone.

Three collisions are worth stating explicitly, because each is a case where the intuitive answer is the wrong one. A special meal order capture that both targets a flight whose counts are already locked and arrives after the cutoff returns `ERR_FLIGHT_COUNTS_LOCKED` from band 70, never `ERR_SPECIAL_MEAL_CUTOFF_PASSED` from band 110. A cart-item load that both draws from a batch past its release deadline and would overfill the cart returns `ERR_RELEASE_DEADLINE_PASSED` from band 80, never `ERR_CART_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` from band 100. And a cart-item load that both puts a hot-store batch into a chilled cart and breaches the per-passenger cost cap returns `ERR_STORAGE_CLASS_MISMATCH` from band 90, never `ERR_COST_CAP_EXCEEDED` from band 120.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Parent Versions

Three write operations demand the client's last observed `version` of the parent aggregate, and each declares that value as a non-optional field of its request body: a required integer of at least 0, so a missing, non-integer or negative value is a request-shape failure reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than a stale-version failure. Endpoints not named here take no version field at all.

Opening a draft loading manifest, `POST /flights/{id}/loading-manifests`, requires `expected_flight_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Flight`. If that value does not equal the flight's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_FLIGHT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The guard exists because a newly opened manifest legitimately voids any manifest of the same flight still in `DRAFT`; a writer holding a stale view of the flight would silently discard a draft it never saw.

Reconciling the uplift, `POST /flights/{id}/uplift-records`, likewise requires `expected_flight_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Flight`, and a value that does not equal the flight's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_FLIGHT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A new reconciliation supersedes any earlier `DRAFT` reconciliation of the same flight, so without the guard a stale writer would overwrite counts derived from cart state it never observed.

Fitting a security seal, `POST /carts/{id}/seals`, requires `expected_cart_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Cart`, and a value that does not equal the cart's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CART_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A new seal legitimately supersedes the prior one, so a writer working from a stale view would replace a seal number that is already pinned into a manifest snapshot it never observed.

In all three cases the check sits at priority band 65 — after the parent row has been shown to exist and after any airline scope check, but before any state or business rule. A stale version is therefore reported in preference to a wrong-state or capacity problem on the same request.

### 2.8. Money, Mass and Percentage Precision

All monetary amounts in this service are integer counts of cents; there are no fractional-cent fields. All masses are integer counts of grams. Percentages and risk scores are decimal numbers carried to exactly two decimal places, with the sole exception of probe temperatures, which are carried to one decimal place. That precision governs the arithmetic, not the JSON spelling: such a value is emitted as a plain JSON number rather than a string, so a rate of 30.00 appears on the wire as `30.0` and a risk score of 100.00 as `100.0`, and equality against the two-decimal figure is numeric.

Wherever a computation requires rounding, the method is **round half up** — a value exactly midway between two representable results rounds away from zero. Rounding is applied at the points each calculation nominates and nowhere else: intermediate products are never pre-rounded, and a calculation that nominates rounding "once per line and once at the end" must not round in between. Where a calculation converts a duration into whole minutes it rounds **up** to the next whole minute, so a single extra second of lateness counts as a full late minute. Where a calculation distributes a passenger count into a unit count it likewise rounds **up**, and it does so per cabin rather than on the total.

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline, Parallel State Axes and Conserved Quantities

Many operations in this service mutate more than one row. When they do, every affected row is treated as a real mutation: its `version` increments and its `updated_at` refreshes, exactly as if it had been written directly. Cascades are immediate and fully applied before the response is emitted — there is no deferred or eventual settlement anywhere in the service, so a read issued immediately after a cascading write observes every consequence of it.

Cascades in this domain run in a fixed direction: from food, through equipment, through paperwork, to the flight. A condemned batch voids its loaded items; voided items knock their cart off its built state and break its seal; a broken or replaced seal on a pinned cart amends the manifest that pinned it; an amended manifest pulls the flight back from loaded to planned. Each hop is stated in full in the workflow narrative that owns it, and no hop is optional.

The cart carries **two independent state axes on the same row**: a build axis and a seal axis. Signing off the build moves the build axis only and leaves the seal axis exactly as it was; fitting a seal moves the seal axis only and leaves the build axis exactly as it was. Only cascades — never a direct operation — move both at once.

Three quantities are conserved and are load-bearing for the whole design. First, a flight's unit balance is its produced units minus its loaded units; voiding a cart item returns that balance to exactly the value it held before the item was added. Second, an airline's accrued penalty returns to exactly its pre-acceptance value when an accepted finding is later withdrawn. Third, a waste manifest's sealed mass is recomputed from the same immutable disposition lines every time, so a replacement manifest raised after a customs rejection reproduces the identical gram figure.

### 2.10. Airline Scoping and Segregation of Duties

Because `X-User-ID` for an `AirlineRep` is the airline's own `id`, list endpoints called by an `AirlineRep` are filtered to that airline's rows, and detail reads of another airline's rows are refused with `ERR_AIRLINE_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. The scope is resolved transitively: a cart, batch, manifest, delivery run, uplift record or waste manifest belongs to the airline of its flight, and a meal specification belongs to the airline named on it directly. The collections that carry the scope are exactly airlines, meal specifications, flights, special meal orders, production batches, carts, loading manifests, delivery runs, uplift records, waste manifests and audit findings. Two child collections are deliberately left outside it: the HACCP check and the cart item are subordinate audit rows whose list reads are not filtered and whose detail reads never raise `ERR_AIRLINE_SCOPE_VIOLATION`, so a rep reaching one of another airline's rows through its own identifier reads it. Trucks and stands are station reference data with no airline at all and are likewise never scoped.

Two segregation-of-duties rules cut across the role matrix. The first is enforced *within* the `LoadMaster` role: the individual `X-User-ID` recorded in a cart's `sealed_by_user_id` may not be the `X-User-ID` issuing the manifest that pins that cart, and an attempt is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` at priority band 85. The second is enforced *between* roles: HACCP verdicts are recorded only by a `QaOfficer` while batch release is performed only by a `ProductionSupervisor`, so no single role can both certify and release food. That second rule needs no dedicated error code — an attempt by the wrong role is simply an authorisation failure and returns `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` at priority band 20.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

The narrative below follows a service day in the order the catering unit lives it: contracts and specifications first, then the schedule feed, then passenger-driven meal capture, then the kitchen, then the ramp, and finally the money and the audit docket. Parameters, thresholds and derived fields are introduced where the business first needs them rather than gathered into schemas.

### 3.1. Airline Contracts and Meal Specifications

Onboarding a customer is the `PlatformAdmin`'s first act. Registering an airline requires its legal `name`, its two-letter `iata_code` — exactly two uppercase letters, and unique across airlines with case treated as significant, where a code that is not exactly two uppercase letters and a code that repeats one already registered are alike rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — the arrival `waste_regime` under which its unused food will be disposed of, which is either `BONDED` or `DOMESTIC`, and the contractual ceiling `cost_cap_per_pax_cents`, which must be at least 1. Four further contractual parameters may be supplied and otherwise take their defaults. `special_meal_cutoff_minutes` is how long before departure special meal capture closes, defaulting to twelve hours and accepted anywhere from zero minutes up to three days. `late_delivery_grace_minutes` is the free lateness allowance subtracted before penalty banding, defaulting to ten minutes and accepted from zero up to two hours. `unused_recovery_rate_pct` is the percentage of the blended unit rate credited back for units the airline re-uses or donates, defaulting to 30.00 and accepted from 0.00 to 100.00 inclusive at two decimal places. `spec_deviation_tolerance_pct` is the deviation percentage at or above which reconciliation raises a specification finding, defaulting to 5.00 and likewise accepted from 0.00 to 100.00 inclusive at two decimals.

A newly registered airline opens in `ACTIVE` with `open_finding_count` at 0, `penalty_accrued_cents` at 0 and `version` at 1. Its `status` is never written by a client: it is either `ACTIVE`, meaning the unit may plan production for that carrier, or `SUSPENDED`, which the audit docket imposes and lifts on its own. `open_finding_count` counts the airline's findings still sitting in `OPEN`; `penalty_accrued_cents` sums the penalty of every finding the compliance desk has moved to `ACCEPTED`. Both are derived and both are exposed on every read of the airline.

Under each contract sit the meal specifications, also registered by a `PlatformAdmin`. A specification names its owning airline through `airline_id` — an `airline_id` naming no existing row is `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`, resolved before the code uniqueness is examined — carries a `spec_code` that is unique within that airline and whose repetition is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and declares the `cabin` it serves — `FIRST`, `BUSINESS` or `ECONOMY` — and the IATA `meal_code` it represents, drawn from `STANDARD`, `VGML`, `AVML`, `CHML` and `GFML`. `STANDARD` is the cabin's default tray; every other code denotes a special meal. `storage_class` fixes the primary production store the tray is built in and is one of `CHILL`, `HOT` or `DRY`. Three weights and prices follow, each of which must be at least 1: `portion_grams` is the contracted edible portion used for compliance assessment, `gross_weight_grams` is the full tray weight including packaging and is what drives bonded waste mass, and `unit_cost_cents` is the contracted food cost per unit before overhead. `shelf_life_hours`, also at least 1, is the number of hours from the moment the production plan is cut during which units of the specification may still be released. `required_haccp_steps` is a non-empty list, without duplicates, of the step codes that must each carry a passing latest reading before release; each element is one of `COOK_TEMP`, `CHILL_DOWN`, `HOLD_TEMP` or `BLAST_CHILL`. Two optional fields complete the record: `load_factor_pct`, the spoilage uplift buffer used by provisional planning only, which defaults to 0.00 and is accepted from 0.00 to 25.00 inclusive at two decimals; and `status`, which defaults to `ACTIVE` and may be set to `RETIRED` at creation.

Repricing and retiring are the two amendments a specification supports, and both are the `PlatformAdmin`'s. An amendment may set `status`, `unit_cost_cents` or `load_factor_pct`, and it increments the specification's `version`. All three are optional and any subset of them may be sent, including none at all: an amendment carrying an empty body is accepted, changes nothing, and still increments `version` and refreshes `updated_at`. Amending a truck or a stand behaves the same way, and so does a passenger-count refresh. Retirement is forward-looking only: a `RETIRED` specification is skipped by every future requirement evaluation and is refused for new special meal orders, but production batches already planned against it keep their `planned_qty` untouched and remain fully producible, releasable and loadable.

### 3.2. Schedule Feed Intake, Retiming and the Provisional Requirement

Flights arrive from the carrier's schedule feed, and only a `ScheduleFeedOperator` may write them. Ingesting a flight requires the operating carrier's `airline_id`, the carrier's `flight_number` designator, the local `service_date` in year-month-day form, the scheduled departure `std_utc`, the aircraft registration `tail_number` the equipment build will be sized for, and the three-letter catering `station_code` — the station whose trucks and stands are expected to serve it, recorded for reporting only, because slot reservation in §3.7 never compares a flight's `station_code` against the truck's or the stand's and so happily reserves a slot on ramp equipment based elsewhere. `flight_number`, `tail_number` and `station_code` are validated only as non-empty strings, so the three-letter shape of a station code and the carrier-designator shape of a flight number are conventions of the feed rather than constraints the service enforces; and where a station code is matched at all — which is only in the `station_code` list filters of §4, never in any business rule — the comparison is an exact, case-sensitive string equality. The triple of `airline_id`, `flight_number` and `service_date` identifies a flight uniquely, and a repeat of that triple is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — checked after `airline_id` has been shown to name an existing airline, which is otherwise `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`. Per-cabin booked passengers may be supplied as `pax_first`, `pax_business` and `pax_economy`; each is optional, defaults to 0, and may not be negative.

Intake derives a good deal. `total_pax` is the sum of the three cabin counts. `delay_minutes` opens at 0. `revised_std_utc` is `std_utc` advanced by `delay_minutes`, and it is the instant every slot and cutoff rule in this service reads — `std_utc` itself is never consulted by a downstream rule. `special_meal_cutoff_utc` is `revised_std_utc` pulled back by the airline's `special_meal_cutoff_minutes`. `counts_locked_at` stays null. `status` opens at `SCHEDULED`; the full lifecycle is `SCHEDULED`, `COUNTS_LOCKED`, `PLANNED`, `LOADED`, `DEPARTED`, `RECONCILED` and `CANCELLED`, with `RECONCILED` and `CANCELLED` terminal. `production_hold` opens false. The unit counters `units_planned`, `units_produced`, `units_loaded`, `units_scrapped` and `unit_balance` all open at 0, as does `cost_per_pax_cents`.

The one figure intake does compute in full is `units_planned_provisional`, the **provisional meal requirement**. This variant exists so the kitchen has a sizing estimate the moment a flight lands in the feed, long before special meal capture closes, and it is deliberately blunt: it applies each standard tray's spoilage uplift and ignores special meal orders entirely. Working through the cabins in the fixed order `FIRST`, then `BUSINESS`, then `ECONOMY`, the provisional variant first selects, for each cabin, the specification belonging to the flight's airline that serves that cabin, carries meal code `STANDARD` and is `ACTIVE`; where more than one specification qualifies, the one whose `spec_code` sorts first by ASCII comparison ascending is chosen, and where none qualifies the cabin contributes zero. For each cabin the contribution is that cabin's booked passenger count multiplied by one plus the chosen specification's `load_factor_pct` divided by one hundred, with the product rounded **up** to the next whole unit. `units_planned_provisional` is the sum of the three per-cabin contributions, and the rounding up happens per cabin, never once on the total.

Two feed operations keep a `SCHEDULED` flight current, and they are carefully different from each other. A passenger-count refresh may carry any subset of `pax_first`, `pax_business` and `pax_economy`; it recomputes `total_pax`, re-evaluates `units_planned_provisional`, and increments the flight's `version`. It is accepted only while the flight is `SCHEDULED` — once counts are locked, a refresh is refused with `ERR_FLIGHT_COUNTS_LOCKED`, and the three cabin counts become immutable for the rest of the flight's life. That refusal is keyed on the flight simply not being `SCHEDULED`, so every later status alike — `COUNTS_LOCKED`, `PLANNED`, `LOADED`, `DEPARTED`, `RECONCILED` and `CANCELLED` — reports `ERR_FLIGHT_COUNTS_LOCKED` and never `ERR_INVALID_FLIGHT_STATE`. A retime carries a new `std_utc` and is the only operation in the entire service that can move a flight **earlier**. It is permitted solely while the flight is `SCHEDULED`, because the whole downstream plan is keyed on `revised_std_utc`; attempted from any later state it is refused with `ERR_INVALID_FLIGHT_STATE`. A retime sets `std_utc`, recomputes `revised_std_utc` as the new `std_utc` advanced by the existing `delay_minutes`, recomputes `special_meal_cutoff_utc` from that, re-evaluates `units_planned_provisional`, and increments `version`. Crucially, a retime does **not** touch `delay_minutes`, does **not** touch `units_planned`, and does **not** re-evaluate any special meal order's `status`. Because it can pull the cutoff back behind an order that was already captured, a retime is precisely how an order that looked timely at capture becomes late at lock.

### 3.3. Special Meal Orders: Capture, Cutoff, Change and Reopening

Special meal capture belongs to the `AirlineRep`, and because that role's `X-User-ID` is the airline's own identifier, an order can only ever be captured against the rep's own flights. Capture carries the target `flight_id`, the requested `meal_specification_id` — which must be a specification whose `meal_code` is something other than `STANDARD`, a `STANDARD` specification being refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` as soon as both body references have resolved and therefore ahead of every guard listed below — the airline's passenger name record `passenger_ref`, and the assigned `seat_number`. The order is created in `REQUESTED` with `requested_at` set to the logical clock and `cabin` copied from the requested specification. Capture deliberately leaves `units_planned_provisional` alone, because the provisional variant ignores special orders by design.

Capture is guarded at four points beyond the generic ladder. The flight must still be `SCHEDULED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_FLIGHT_COUNTS_LOCKED`. The requested specification must be `ACTIVE`, or the request is refused with `ERR_SPEC_RETIRED`, and it must belong to the same airline as the flight, or the request is refused with `ERR_SPEC_AIRLINE_MISMATCH`. The seat must be free: at most one non-terminal order may exist per seat per flight, where non-terminal means the order's `status` is `REQUESTED`, `CONFIRMED` or `LATE_REJECTED`, and a repeat is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_SEAT_ORDER`. Finally, the logical clock must be strictly before `special_meal_cutoff_utc`; at or after that instant the capture is refused with `ERR_SPECIAL_MEAL_CUTOFF_PASSED`.

An order's `status` moves through `REQUESTED`, `CONFIRMED`, `SUBSTITUTED`, `CANCELLED` and `LATE_REJECTED`, of which `CANCELLED` and `SUBSTITUTED` are terminal. Two last-minute changes are supported, and they are distinguished by an `action` value on the request together with an optional `reason` note; the `reason` is accepted for the operator's convenience but is not persisted on the order and appears in no response. Because one endpoint serves both actions, its permitted role list is the union of the two, and the pairing of action to role is enforced inside it: an `action` the acting role does not own — a `ProductionSupervisor` sending `CANCEL`, or an `AirlineRep` sending `SUBSTITUTE` — is refused with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`, evaluated after the order has been shown to exist and after the airline-scope check but before any state rule. The `CANCEL` action is the `AirlineRep`'s: it applies to an order whose `status` is either `REQUESTED` or `CONFIRMED`, on a flight whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `COUNTS_LOCKED` — a flight in any other status refuses the cancel with `ERR_INVALID_RESOURCE_STATE`, the order being the addressed resource, never with `ERR_INVALID_FLIGHT_STATE` — strictly before the flight's cutoff instant, and only when the acting `X-User-ID` is the flight's own airline. Cancelling drives the order `REQUESTED → CANCELLED` (or `CONFIRMED → CANCELLED`, identically), frees the `seat_number` for reuse by a later order, and increments the order's `version`. At or after the cutoff, a cancel attempt is refused with `ERR_SPECIAL_MEAL_CUTOFF_PASSED`.

The `SUBSTITUTE` action is the `ProductionSupervisor`'s and exists for the case where the kitchen cannot build what was ordered. It applies only to an order in `CONFIRMED`, on a flight whose `status` is `COUNTS_LOCKED` or `PLANNED` — any other flight status refuses the substitution with `ERR_INVALID_RESOURCE_STATE` — and it carries `substitute_meal_specification_id`, which is required for this action alone and whose absence is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, raised after the order has resolved and after the action-to-role pairing has been checked rather than with the rest of the request-shape band. The substitute specification must exist, or the request is `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`, and it is resolved before the order's own state is examined. It must be `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_SPEC_RETIRED` — and must belong to the same airline and serve the same `cabin` as the original, otherwise `ERR_SPEC_AIRLINE_MISMATCH`, the airline comparison being made first and the cabin comparison second under that same code. Substituting drives the order `CONFIRMED → SUBSTITUTED`, records `substitute_meal_specification_id`, and increments `version`. The substituted specification, not the originally requested one, is what the final requirement counts. Applying either action to an order in a state from which it is undefined — cancelling an already-`CANCELLED` order, substituting one still in `REQUESTED` — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_RESOURCE_STATE`.

### 3.4. Locking Counts, the Final Requirement and the Zone Waterfall

Locking the counts is the hinge of the whole day, and it is the `ScheduleFeedOperator`'s. It requires the flight to be in `SCHEDULED` and to have at least one booked passenger across all cabins; a second attempt, or an attempt on a flight already past `SCHEDULED`, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_FLIGHT_STATE`, and so is a flight whose three cabin counts still sum to zero — the empty-flight refusal carries the same `ERR_INVALID_FLIGHT_STATE` code and is evaluated after the status check, so a locked empty flight reports the status problem. The lock drives the flight `SCHEDULED → COUNTS_LOCKED`, sets `counts_locked_at` to the logical clock, and increments `version`. In the same request it resolves every outstanding special meal order against the cutoff: an order in `REQUESTED` whose `requested_at` is at or before `special_meal_cutoff_utc`, and whose referenced specification is still `ACTIVE`, moves `REQUESTED → CONFIRMED`, becoming eligible for the final requirement; an order in `REQUESTED` whose `requested_at` falls after `special_meal_cutoff_utc` moves `REQUESTED → LATE_REJECTED` and is excluded from the final requirement. Each such order has its own `version` incremented. The `ACTIVE` condition on the first branch is a gate, not a reclassification: an order timely against the cutoff whose effective specification has since been retired — or has vanished — is left exactly where it stands, still in `REQUESTED`, with neither its `version` nor its `updated_at` disturbed, and it too is excluded from the final requirement. Orders are examined in `created_at` ascending order, and the cutoff they are measured against is the flight's `special_meal_cutoff_utc` as it stands at the moment of the lock.

That late-rejection edge is reachable only because a retime can move the cutoff backwards between capture and lock — direct capture already refuses anything at or after the then-current cutoff. It is also reversible, which is why `LATE_REJECTED` is not a terminal state; the delay cascade in §3.8 reopens such orders.

Once counts are locked, the `ProductionSupervisor` cuts the production plan. This is permitted only from `COUNTS_LOCKED` — anything else is refused with `ERR_INVALID_FLIGHT_STATE` — and only when the owning airline's `status` is `ACTIVE` and the flight's `production_hold` is false; a suspended airline or a held flight is refused with `ERR_AIRLINE_SUSPENDED`. Planning drives the flight `COUNTS_LOCKED → PLANNED`, evaluates the **final meal requirement**, runs the store-allocation waterfall over it, materialises one production batch per specification-and-zone pair with a non-zero allocation, writes `units_planned` as the sum of those batches' `planned_qty`, and increments the flight's `version`. Uniquely among the service's operations, planning answers with the rows it created rather than with the row it addressed: the response is `201 Created` with `response_type` `"array"` and a `data` array of the newly created production batches in the visit order described below, no flight object anywhere in it, and a `meta` object carrying no pagination fields because the array is a creation result rather than a page. A plan that allocates nothing answers `201` with an empty array. `units_planned` is written exactly once in a flight's life and is never recomputed afterwards, no matter what later happens to orders, batches or the schedule.

The **final requirement** differs from the provisional one on two independent axes at once: it applies no load factor whatsoever, and it substitutes one special tray for one standard tray in the ordering passenger's cabin. It is computed as follows. For each cabin, count the flight's special meal orders whose `status` is `CONFIRMED` and whose *effective* specification serves that cabin, where the effective specification is `substitute_meal_specification_id` when that field is set and `meal_specification_id` otherwise. Then, taking the cabins in the fixed order `FIRST`, then `BUSINESS`, then `ECONOMY`, the standard-tray demand for a cabin is that cabin's locked passenger count minus that cabin's confirmed-order count, floored at zero so a cabin can never contribute negative demand. The specification those standard trays are booked against is chosen by exactly the rule the provisional variant of §3.2 uses — the flight airline's `ACTIVE` specification for that cabin whose `meal_code` is `STANDARD`, and where several qualify the one whose `spec_code` sorts first by ASCII comparison ascending — and a cabin with no qualifying specification contributes no standard trays at all. Alongside the standard trays, one unit is required for each `CONFIRMED` order, at that order's own effective specification; where a cabin's standard specification is also the effective specification of a confirmed order, the two demands merge into the single demand row that specification gets. `units_planned` is therefore the sum across cabins of the standard-tray demand plus the total count of `CONFIRMED` orders on the flight. Orders in `LATE_REJECTED`, `CANCELLED` or `REQUESTED` are excluded entirely, as is the originally requested specification of any order that was substituted away. The result is an integer count of units; no rounding arises because no load factor is applied. A flight with at least one booked passenger whose every matching standard specification has been retired still moves to `PLANNED`, with `units_planned` at 0 and no batches at all.

The **store-allocation waterfall** turns that requirement into physical batches. The requirement is expanded into demand rows of one specification and one quantity each, and those rows are visited in a single total order: first by the specification's `storage_class` in the sequence hot store, then chill store, then dry store; within a storage class by `cabin` in the sequence `FIRST`, then `BUSINESS`, then `ECONOMY`; and within that by `spec_code` ascending by ASCII comparison. Each store begins the plan with its own remaining capacity: the chill store holds 250 units, the hot store 120 units, and the dry store 400 units. For each demand row in visit order, the allocator first takes from the specification's own primary store the smaller of the row's quantity and that store's remaining capacity, allocates it there, and reduces that store's remaining capacity by the amount taken. Whatever quantity is left over is the row's overflow.

When a row overflows, the allocator consults a single fixed fallback hop: the hot store falls back to the chill store, the chill store falls back to the dry store, and the dry store has no fallback at all. If the primary store has no fallback, the plan is refused with `ERR_STORE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. If the specification's `shelf_life_hours` is below the minimum the primary store demands of overflowed food — 24 hours for food whose primary store is the hot store, and 72 hours for food whose primary store is the chill store — the plan is likewise refused with `ERR_STORE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`; the comparison is inclusive at the minimum, so a shelf life of exactly 72 hours may overflow out of the chill store. Otherwise the allocator takes from the fallback store the smaller of the overflow and that store's remaining capacity, allocates it there, and reduces that store's remaining capacity accordingly. If even one unit of the overflow remains unplaced after the single fallback hop, the plan is refused with `ERR_STORE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. The waterfall is strictly first-come-first-served: an early demand row fills a store to its limit and a later row visiting the same store finds it exhausted. Capacity is scoped to the flight being planned; other flights' plans do not consume it.

Each specification-and-zone pair that received at least one unit becomes one `ProductionBatch`, created in visit order. A batch records its `flight_id` and `meal_specification_id`, the `store_zone` it was allocated to, the `planned_qty` allocated there, and `is_overflow`, which is true exactly when the allocated `store_zone` differs from the specification's own `storage_class`. A zone that received zero units for a specification produces no batch row at all. Every batch opens with `production_status` at `PLANNED` — the lifecycle runs through `PLANNED`, `IN_PRODUCTION`, `HACCP_HOLD`, `COMPLETED`, `RELEASED`, `REJECTED` and `DISPOSED`, of which `REJECTED` and `DISPOSED` are terminal — together with `produced_qty` 0, `scrapped_qty` 0, `haccp_rework_rounds` 0, `haccp_risk_score` 0.00, and `released_at` and `released_by_user_id` null.

Planning also pins each batch's `release_deadline_utc`, and this is a nested-minimum selection whose binding candidate depends on the zone. Let the plan instant be the logical clock at the moment the plan was cut. Three candidate instants are evaluated in this order: the plan instant advanced by the specification's `shelf_life_hours`; the flight's `revised_std_utc` pulled back by an hour and a half of slot preparation lead; and the plan instant advanced by the zone cutoff for the batch's allocated `store_zone`, which is 4 hours for the hot store, 72 hours for the chill store, and 720 hours for the dry store. The earliest of the three candidates wins; where candidates tie, the tied instant is the winner and the choice is unambiguous. The chosen instant is expressed to whole seconds in UTC, any sub-second remainder being dropped outright rather than rounded, so the pinned deadline never sits later than the winning candidate. Once pinned at planning time, `release_deadline_utc` is never recomputed — a later delay that moves `revised_std_utc` does not extend it.

### 3.5. The Kitchen Floor: HACCP Measurement, Rework, Completion and Release

Production begins when a `ProductionSupervisor` starts a batch, moving it `PLANNED → IN_PRODUCTION`. A first start requires the batch to be in `PLANNED` and the owning flight to be in `PLANNED` — a flight standing in any other status refuses the start with `ERR_INVALID_BATCH_STATE`, the batch being the addressed resource, and never with `ERR_INVALID_FLIGHT_STATE` — and it requires the logical clock to be strictly before the batch's `release_deadline_utc`; at or after that instant the start is refused with `ERR_RELEASE_DEADLINE_PASSED`. A restart is the same operation applied to a batch sitting in `HACCP_HOLD`, moving it `HACCP_HOLD → IN_PRODUCTION`; a restart requires only that the batch is in `HACCP_HOLD` and that the logical clock is strictly before `release_deadline_utc`, and it additionally increments `haccp_rework_rounds` by one and recomputes `haccp_risk_score`. A restart out of `HACCP_HOLD` is *always* permitted while the deadline holds — the rework allowance is deliberately enforced later, at release, so a twice-reworked batch can still be finished and inspected but can never be certified. Starting a batch from any other state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_BATCH_STATE`. Either form increments the batch's `version`.

Temperature measurement is the `QaOfficer`'s exclusive act, and it is accepted only against a batch whose `production_status` is `IN_PRODUCTION` or `RELEASED`; every other state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_BATCH_STATE`. A measurement names the `step_code` it was taken at — one of `COOK_TEMP`, `CHILL_DOWN`, `HOLD_TEMP` or `BLAST_CHILL` — and the probe reading `measured_temp_c`, a one-decimal Celsius figure accepted from -40.0 to 200.0 inclusive, a reading carrying more than one decimal place being a request-shape failure reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The service creates an immutable check row recording its `production_batch_id`, the reading, a `measured_at_utc` set to the logical clock, the acting `recorded_by_user_id`, a derived `sequence_no`, and a derived `verdict`. The `verdict` is never client-supplied: a cook-temperature reading passes when it is at or above 75.0 degrees; a chill-down reading passes when it is at or below 8.0 degrees; a hold-temperature reading passes when it is at or below 5.0 degrees; and a blast-chill reading passes when it is at or below 3.0 degrees. Every comparison is inclusive at the constant, so a cook reading of exactly 75.0 is `PASS` while 74.9 is `FAIL`. `sequence_no` is assigned as one plus the number of checks that already exist for the same batch-and-step pair, giving each step its own ascending series and making "the latest reading for this step" a deterministic choice.

Every recorded check recomputes the batch's `haccp_risk_score`. That score is the smaller of 100.00 and the quantity formed by weighting each failed check on the batch at 40.00 and each completed rework round at 15.00 and adding the two products together; the fail count includes every `FAIL` check on the batch regardless of `step_code` or `sequence_no`. The result is carried to two decimals, rounded half up, and clamped at 100.00 — so a batch with two failures and two rework rounds, whose unclamped arithmetic reaches 110.00, reports 100.00. The score is a reported food-safety metric only; it never gates release. Because it is derived afresh on every read rather than stored, recording a check is not by itself a mutation of the batch row: a `PASS` check leaves the batch's `version` and `updated_at` exactly as they stood, and only the state-moving `FAIL` branches set out below touch the batch row at all.

What a failed check *does* gate is the batch's state, and here the two branches differ sharply. When the batch is `IN_PRODUCTION` and the resulting failure count for the batch is two or fewer, the batch moves `IN_PRODUCTION → HACCP_HOLD` and its `haccp_risk_score` is recomputed. When the batch is `IN_PRODUCTION` and the resulting failure count exceeds two — the third failure, at which point `haccp_rework_rounds` already stands at two — the batch moves `IN_PRODUCTION → REJECTED`: `scrapped_qty` is set to the full `planned_qty`, `haccp_risk_score` is recomputed, the flight's `units_produced`, `units_scrapped` and `unit_balance` are recomputed, and a `HACCP_BREACH` finding of `CRITICAL` severity is raised with `source` `SYSTEM`, incrementing the airline's `open_finding_count` and possibly suspending it. Nothing else unwinds on this edge, because a batch rejected before release has never been loaded into a cart. A `PASS` verdict never changes `production_status` at all.

The severe branch is a failure recorded against a batch that is already `RELEASED` — a post-release re-inspection of certified food. Here neither the failure count nor `haccp_rework_rounds` is consulted, because certified food that fails re-inspection is condemned on its first failure, and the batch moves `RELEASED → REJECTED` through a four-hop cascade. The first hop sets `scrapped_qty` to the full `planned_qty`, recomputes `haccp_risk_score`, and recomputes the flight's `units_produced`, `units_scrapped` and `unit_balance`. The second hop voids every `ACTIVE` cart item drawn from that batch, which drives each affected cart from `BUILT` back to `BUILDING` on its build axis and from `SEALED` or `RESEALED` to `BROKEN` on its seal axis. The third hop takes each cart whose `seal_number` is pinned in an `ISSUED` manifest's snapshot and drives that manifest `ISSUED → AMENDED`, incrementing its `amendment_count`. The fourth hop drives each amended manifest's flight `LOADED → PLANNED`. A `HACCP_BREACH` finding of `CRITICAL` severity with `source` `SYSTEM` is raised, incrementing the airline's `open_finding_count` and possibly suspending it, and the batch's `version` increments.

Closing production is a separate `ProductionSupervisor` act that carries the quantity actually produced as `produced_qty`, a required integer that may not be negative. It applies only to a batch in `IN_PRODUCTION`, and `produced_qty` may not exceed `planned_qty`, or the request is refused with `ERR_PRODUCED_QTY_EXCEEDS_PLAN`. Completion moves the batch `IN_PRODUCTION → COMPLETED`, stores `produced_qty`, sets `scrapped_qty` to `planned_qty` minus `produced_qty`, and recomputes the flight's `units_produced`, `units_scrapped` and `unit_balance`. A flight's `units_produced` is the sum of `produced_qty` across its batches whose `production_status` is `COMPLETED`, `RELEASED` or `DISPOSED`; its `units_scrapped` is the sum of `scrapped_qty` across *all* its batches regardless of state. Wherever this document says a batch transition recomputes those flight counters — at completion, and at either form of rejection, the pre-release one and the post-release cascade alike — the flight row itself counts as mutated in the sense of §2.5 and has its `version` incremented and its `updated_at` refreshed alongside the batch's, subject to §2.5's once-per-request counting when one cascade moves several batches of the same flight. A transition the document does not describe as recomputing them — a release, or the `RELEASED → DISPOSED` edge of §3.9 — leaves the flight's `version` alone.

Release is the certification act, and it is the one the food-safety gates guard. It applies only to a batch in `COMPLETED`, moving it `COMPLETED → RELEASED`, and it requires that for every step named in the specification's `required_haccp_steps` the check with the highest `sequence_no` for that batch-and-step pair exists and carries verdict `PASS`, that `haccp_rework_rounds` is strictly below two, and that the logical clock is at or before `release_deadline_utc`. The four gate failures are reported in the fixed order set out in §2.6: a required step with no check at all yields `ERR_HACCP_STEP_MISSING`, a required step whose newest check failed yields `ERR_HACCP_HOLD_ACTIVE`, an exhausted rework allowance yields `ERR_HACCP_REWORK_LIMIT`, and a passed deadline yields `ERR_RELEASE_DEADLINE_PASSED`. A successful release sets `released_at` to the logical clock and `released_by_user_id` to the acting operator, and increments the batch's `version`. Release is where the segregation between quality and production bites: the `QaOfficer` who took the readings has no authority to release, and a `ProductionSupervisor` attempting to record readings is refused as an authorisation failure.

One further batch edge exists at the far end of the day. A released batch whose specification receives at least one destroyed unit during disposition moves `RELEASED → DISPOSED`, incrementing its `version`; that edge is described with the disposition workflow in §3.9.

### 3.6. Equipment Build, Sealing and Manifest Issue

Cart registration opens the build phase and belongs to the `LoadMaster`. A cart names the `flight_id` it is built for — a `flight_id` naming no existing row is reported with the flight's own typed code `ERR_FLIGHT_NOT_FOUND` rather than the generic `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` that band 60 otherwise nominates for a body reference, and the flight must still be one the service can build for: `SCHEDULED`, `COUNTS_LOCKED`, `PLANNED` and `LOADED` are all accepted, while `DEPARTED`, `RECONCILED` and `CANCELLED` are refused with `ERR_INVALID_FLIGHT_STATE` — the physical `cart_code` stencilled on it, which the service records as given and does not require to be unique, its `cart_type` — one of `STD_HALF`, `STD_FULL`, `OVEN_RACK` or `DRY_BOX` — the `storage_class` it may carry, and `capacity_units`, the maximum number of active units it holds, accepted from 1 to 400 inclusive. `galley_position`, the target position on the aircraft, is optional. Registration creates the cart alone and does not itself mutate the flight, whose `version` and `updated_at` are left exactly as they stood. A new cart opens with `build_status` `EMPTY`, `seal_status` `UNSEALED`, `seal_count` 0, `loaded_units` 0, and no `seal_number` or `sealed_by_user_id` yet. The build axis runs `EMPTY`, `BUILDING`, `BUILT`, `LOADED` and `RETURNED`, with `RETURNED` terminal; the seal axis runs `UNSEALED`, `SEALED`, `BROKEN` and `RESEALED` and has no terminal state at all. The two axes live on the same row and move independently.

Loading units into a cart names the source `production_batch_id` — carried in the body, and so resolved under band 60 as `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` when it names no existing row, not with the batch's own typed `ERR_BATCH_NOT_FOUND`, which is reserved here for the path identifier of a batch endpoint — and the `qty_units` to transfer, which must be at least 1. The service creates a cart item in `ACTIVE` recording its `cart_id`, the source batch, the quantity, and a `meal_code` copied from the batch's specification, with `voided_at` null. On the first active item the cart moves `EMPTY → BUILDING`; a further item on a cart already in `BUILDING` leaves the build axis where it is. Loading is accepted only while `build_status` is `EMPTY` or `BUILDING`, and any other build state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CART_STATE`; that build-axis test is made before the source batch's own release state is examined, so a load into a `BUILT` cart drawn from a batch that is not yet `RELEASED` reports `ERR_INVALID_CART_STATE` rather than `ERR_BATCH_NOT_RELEASED`. Each successful load recomputes the cart's `loaded_units` — the sum of `qty_units` over that cart's `ACTIVE` items — along with the flight's `units_loaded`, `unit_balance` and `cost_per_pax_cents`. Both of those roll-ups count as real mutations in the sense of §2.5, so an accepted load increments the cart's `version` and the flight's `version` and refreshes both `updated_at` values, even when the build axis stayed where it was; the new cart item is created at `version` 1. A flight's `units_loaded` is the sum of `qty_units` over `ACTIVE` items across all its carts, and its `unit_balance` is `units_produced` minus `units_loaded`.

Loading is the busiest guard point in the service, and the order in which its failures are reported matters. The source batch must be `RELEASED`, or the load is refused with `ERR_BATCH_NOT_RELEASED`; the logical clock must be strictly before its `release_deadline_utc`, so that the deadline instant itself is already too late to load from, or the load is refused with `ERR_RELEASE_DEADLINE_PASSED`; its `store_zone` must equal the cart's own `storage_class`, or the load is refused with `ERR_STORAGE_CLASS_MISMATCH`; its `flight_id` must equal the cart's `flight_id`, or the load is refused with `ERR_CART_FLIGHT_MISMATCH`; the cart's `loaded_units` plus the new `qty_units` may not exceed `capacity_units`, or the load is refused with `ERR_CART_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`; and finally, the flight's resulting `cost_per_pax_cents` may not exceed the airline's `cost_cap_per_pax_cents`, or the load is refused with `ERR_COST_CAP_EXCEEDED`. Those six sit in four different priority bands, so a load that trips several of them reports the earliest band, as §2.6 sets out.

Voiding a loaded item is the compensating act, addressed by the cart together with the item's own `cart_item_id`; an item that exists but hangs off a different cart is reported as `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`, exactly as a `cart_item_id` naming nothing at all is, and a successful void answers `200 OK` carrying the voided cart item rather than the owning cart. The row is never physically deleted: the item moves `ACTIVE → VOID` with `voided_at` set to the logical clock, so the load history survives for audit. Voiding an item that is already `VOID` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_RESOURCE_STATE`. The consequences ripple in a fixed order. The owning cart, if `BUILT`, falls back `BUILT → BUILDING`, and its seal axis moves from `SEALED` or `RESEALED` to `BROKEN` on the same row — the break clears `sealed_by_user_id` but deliberately leaves `seal_number` in place for audit. Any `ISSUED` manifest pinning that cart — pinning being matched on the snapshot entry's `cart_id`, never on whether the entry's `seal_number` still agrees with the number the cart currently carries — moves `ISSUED → AMENDED` with `amendment_count` incremented, and its flight moves `LOADED → PLANNED`. The cart's `loaded_units` and the flight's `units_loaded`, `unit_balance` and `cost_per_pax_cents` are all recomputed, and the flight's `unit_balance` returns to exactly the value it held before the item was added. The item, the owning cart and the flight are each mutated in the sense of §2.5 here, so the cart's `version` and the flight's `version` increment even on a void that found neither cart axis with anywhere to move. `VOID` items are excluded from every unit and cost aggregate in the service.

Signing off a build is a small, deliberately narrow operation: it moves the cart `BUILDING → BUILT` and touches nothing on the seal axis. It requires the cart to be in `BUILDING` with `loaded_units` of at least 1; an `EMPTY` cart, or one in any other build state, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CART_STATE`. The cart's `version` increments.

Sealing is the mirror-image operation: it moves the seal axis and leaves `build_status` exactly as it was. A seal request carries `expected_cart_version` — the optimistic-concurrency guard described in §2.7 — and the `seal_number` being fitted. The cart must be `BUILT`, or the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CART_STATE`, and its seal axis must itself stand in `UNSEALED`, `SEALED` or `BROKEN`: a cart already sitting in `RESEALED` has exhausted the direct sealing edge and refuses a further seal with `ERR_INVALID_CART_STATE` too, so a third numbered seal is reachable only once a break has moved the axis back off `RESEALED`. Every `ACTIVE` item in the cart must then reference a batch whose `production_status` is `RELEASED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_BATCH_NOT_RELEASED`. And the `seal_number` must not be held by any other cart — a cart whose seal is `BROKEN` still holds the number it kept for audit — nor by any waste manifest other than one already `REJECTED`, whose number §3.9 releases for reuse, or the request is refused with `ERR_SEAL_NUMBER_DUPLICATE`. Those tests run in exactly that order, the two cart-state tests and the released-batch test all sitting in band 70 ahead of the uniqueness test in band 100, and neither the flight's own status nor the cart's `loaded_units` is consulted anywhere in the operation. A first seal moves the cart `UNSEALED → SEALED` and sets `seal_count` to 1; a seal fitted over an existing one moves `SEALED → RESEALED`, and a seal fitted after a break moves `BROKEN → RESEALED`. Both of those latter forms replace `seal_number`, replace `sealed_by_user_id` with the acting operator, and increment `seal_count` by one, so a `seal_count` of two or more is the durable evidence that the cart was resealed. Every form increments the cart's `version`. When the cart being resealed is already pinned in an `ISSUED` manifest, the supersede drives that manifest `ISSUED → AMENDED` and its flight `LOADED → PLANNED`. The supersede that does this is the one fitted over a seal still live — the `SEALED → RESEALED` form; a seal fitted after a break, the `BROKEN → RESEALED` form, amends nothing, because whatever broke the seal in the first place has already amended the manifest.

The loading manifest is the flight's paperwork, and opening a draft carries `expected_flight_version` for the reason given in §2.7. Opening a draft is defined from every state in which a flight can still be loaded — `SCHEDULED`, `COUNTS_LOCKED`, `PLANNED` and `LOADED` alike — and is refused only once the flight has reached `DEPARTED`, `RECONCILED` or `CANCELLED`, which report `ERR_INVALID_FLIGHT_STATE`; the version guard is checked ahead of that state test. A new manifest records its `flight_id`, opens in `DRAFT` with an empty `cart_seal_snapshot` array, `total_units` 0, `amendment_count` 0, and `issued_at` and `issued_by_user_id` null, and receives a deterministic `manifest_number` of the form `MFST-` followed by the flight's `flight_number`, a hyphen, and the one-based ordinal of this manifest among the manifests created for that flight. Opening a draft drives any earlier manifest of the same flight still in `DRAFT` to `VOID`, and increments the flight's `version`. The manifest lifecycle is `DRAFT`, `ISSUED`, `AMENDED` and `VOID`, with `VOID` terminal.

Issuing is where the snapshot is pinned. It applies to a manifest in `DRAFT` or in `AMENDED`, moving it `DRAFT → ISSUED` or `AMENDED → ISSUED` — the `AMENDED → ISSUED` edge closes the amendment cycle and re-pins the snapshot against current cart state — and requires the flight to be in `PLANNED`, a flight in any other status being refused with `ERR_INVALID_FLIGHT_STATE`. The manifest's own state is examined before the flight's, so an attempt to issue a manifest that is already `ISSUED` on a flight that has therefore moved to `LOADED` reports the manifest problem, `ERR_INVALID_RESOURCE_STATE`, and not the flight one. Every cart of the flight carrying at least one active unit must be `BUILT` with a seal axis in `SEALED` or `RESEALED`, or the issue is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CART_STATE`. The acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the `sealed_by_user_id` of every cart entering the snapshot, or the issue is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Issuing a `VOID` manifest is refused with `ERR_INVALID_RESOURCE_STATE`.

A successful issue pins `cart_seal_snapshot` as one entry per cart of the flight whose `build_status` is `BUILT`, ordered by the cart's `created_at` ascending, each entry carrying that cart's `cart_id`, its current `seal_number`, its current `cart_version` and its `units`. `total_units` is set to the sum of the `units` values across the pinned entries and is 0 while the manifest is still `DRAFT`. `issued_at` is set to the logical clock and `issued_by_user_id` to the acting operator. A re-issue out of `AMENDED` leaves `amendment_count` at whatever value it had already reached — closing the amendment cycle records the cycle rather than erasing it — and overwrites `cart_seal_snapshot`, `total_units`, `issued_at` and `issued_by_user_id` outright. The manifest moves to `ISSUED`, the flight moves `PLANNED → LOADED`, and both rows have their `version` incremented by one each. From that moment the snapshot is a frozen claim about reality: any later void, reseal or slot movement that contradicts it drives the manifest to `AMENDED`, increments `amendment_count`, and pulls the flight back to `PLANNED` — which is exactly what `AMENDED` means, that the pinned snapshot no longer matches reality.

### 3.7. Ramp Resources and Delivery to the Aircraft

Before a run can be reserved, the ramp resources must exist, and both are `PlatformAdmin` reference data. A high-lift truck records the `station_code` it is based at and its `registration`, which is unique within that station; `buffer_minutes`, the minimum turnaround gap required between two runs on that vehicle, is optional and defaults to twenty minutes, accepted from zero up to two hours; `status` is optional, defaults to `ACTIVE`, and may be `MAINTENANCE`, in which case the truck cannot be reserved. An aircraft stand records its `station_code` and a `stand_code` unique within that station — as with a truck's `registration`, a repeat within the same station is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, while the same code at a different station is accepted; `max_concurrent_runs`, the number of catering runs that may occupy the stand at any single instant, is optional and defaults to 2, accepted from 1 to 6 inclusive; `status` is optional, defaults to `ACTIVE`, and may be `CLOSED`, in which case the stand cannot be reserved. Amending either resource may change its `status` and, for a truck, its `buffer_minutes` or, for a stand, its `max_concurrent_runs`; the amendment increments that row's `version` and deliberately does **not** revalidate delivery run windows that were already reserved against it.

Reserving a slot is the `LoadMaster`'s and names the `loading_manifest_id` being delivered against, the `truck_id` and `stand_id` occupied for the window, and the window bounds `window_start_utc` and `window_end_utc`. The manifest must be `ISSUED`, or the reservation is refused with `ERR_MANIFEST_NOT_ISSUED`. A new run opens in `RESERVED` with `recovery_of_run_id` null, `lateness_minutes` 0 and `penalty_cents` 0, and with `dispatched_at_utc`, `arrived_at_utc` and `delivered_at_utc` all null. The run lifecycle is `RESERVED`, `RESCHEDULED`, `EN_ROUTE`, `AT_AIRCRAFT`, `DELIVERED`, `MISSED`, `RECOVERED` and `CANCELLED`, of which `DELIVERED`, `RECOVERED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal.

Four window rules apply to a reservation, and the same four are re-applied to a recovery run. The window must be non-empty and correctly ordered — `window_start_utc` strictly before `window_end_utc` — or the request is refused with `ERR_SLOT_WINDOW_INVALID`. The window must sit inside the flight's permitted slot band, which opens five hours before `revised_std_utc` and closes forty-five minutes before it: a start earlier than five hours before revised departure, or an end later than forty-five minutes before it, is refused with `ERR_SLOT_OUTSIDE_FLIGHT_WINDOW`. Truck occupancy is half-open and buffered: a run is refused when its `window_start_utc` falls before an existing run's `window_end_utc` advanced by the truck's `buffer_minutes` **and** its `window_end_utc` falls after that existing run's `window_start_utc`, and the refusal is `ERR_TRUCK_SLOT_CONFLICT`. Stand occupancy is a closed interval with an aggregate limit: a run is refused when adding it would make more than `max_concurrent_runs` non-cancelled runs cover any common instant, where a shared endpoint instant counts as common, and the refusal is `ERR_STAND_CAPACITY_CONFLICT`. Both occupancy tests weigh every run standing on that truck or stand whose `status` is anything other than `CANCELLED`, whatever flight it serves — a run already `EN_ROUTE`, `AT_AIRCRAFT`, `DELIVERED`, `MISSED` or `RECOVERED` still occupies its resource, and only a `CANCELLED` run occupies nothing. A truck in `MAINTENANCE` or a stand that is `CLOSED` is refused with `ERR_SLOT_RESOURCE_UNAVAILABLE`. The five checks are evaluated in one pinned order and the first violation short-circuits the rest: window ordering first, then the flight slot band, then truck and stand availability, then truck buffering, and stand concurrency last — so a window that both falls outside the slot band and names a truck in `MAINTENANCE` reports `ERR_SLOT_OUTSIDE_FLIGHT_WINDOW`, while a closed stand that would also breach a truck's buffer reports `ERR_SLOT_RESOURCE_UNAVAILABLE`. Neither reservation consults the `station_code` of the truck or stand at all.

The run is then advanced by events, each carrying an `event_type` together with an optional `note` and, on the failure branch, a `miss_reason`; the `note` is accepted for the operator's convenience and then discarded, stored on no row and echoed in no response. Dispatching moves a run `RESERVED → EN_ROUTE` (and identically `RESCHEDULED → EN_ROUTE`), requires the manifest to still be `ISSUED` — a manifest that fell back to `AMENDED` refuses dispatch with `ERR_MANIFEST_NOT_ISSUED` — and requires the logical clock to be at or after `window_start_utc` pulled back by one hour of dispatch lead; a dispatch attempted earlier than that is refused with `ERR_INVALID_RUN_STATE`, the run being the addressed resource, and there is no dedicated lead-time code. Those three dispatch tests run in that order — the run's own status first, the manifest's status second, the lead-time gate third — so a run already `DELIVERED` under a manifest that has fallen back to `AMENDED` reports `ERR_INVALID_RUN_STATE`, not `ERR_MANIFEST_NOT_ISSUED`. It sets `dispatched_at_utc` to the logical clock. Arrival moves the run `EN_ROUTE → AT_AIRCRAFT` and sets `arrived_at_utc`. Any event applied from a state where it is undefined is refused with `ERR_INVALID_RUN_STATE`. Every event increments the run's `version`.

From `AT_AIRCRAFT` the run forks, and the two outcomes carry deliberately different consequences. **Delivery** moves the run `AT_AIRCRAFT → DELIVERED`, sets `delivered_at_utc` to the logical clock, and evaluates the lateness penalty into `lateness_minutes` and `penalty_cents`. When the resulting `penalty_cents` is at least 1, a `LATE_DELIVERY` finding of `MINOR` severity is raised with `source` `SYSTEM`, incrementing the airline's `open_finding_count`. And when every delivery run belonging to the flight's `ISSUED` manifest has reached `DELIVERED`, `RECOVERED` or `CANCELLED`, with at least one of them in `DELIVERED`, the flight moves `LOADED → DEPARTED`, which in the same cascade drives every cart of the flight still in `BUILT` along the build axis `BUILT → LOADED`, incrementing each cart's `version`. **Missing** the slot moves the run `EN_ROUTE → MISSED` (and identically `AT_AIRCRAFT → MISSED`), requires a `miss_reason` to be supplied and to be something other than `SLOT_LOST` — the permitted reasons are `SLOT_LOST`, `AIRCRAFT_NOT_READY`, `TRAFFIC` and `EQUIPMENT_FAILURE`, and `SLOT_LOST` is reserved for the delay cascade's own use, so an omitted, null or `SLOT_LOST` reason on a `MISS` is a request-shape failure reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and reported before the run identifier in the path is even resolved — sets `penalty_cents` to a flat 750,000 cents, leaves `lateness_minutes` at 0 with no banding applied at all, raises a `LATE_DELIVERY` finding of `MAJOR` severity with `source` `SYSTEM` incrementing the airline's `open_finding_count`, and pointedly does **not** advance the flight, which stays `LOADED` until a recovery run delivers.

The lateness arithmetic on the delivery branch runs as follows. `lateness_minutes` is the elapsed time from `window_end_utc` to `delivered_at_utc` converted to whole minutes and rounded **up**, floored at zero — so a run delivered inside its window records 0, and a run delivered one second past its window records 1. Billable minutes are `lateness_minutes` less the airline's `late_delivery_grace_minutes`, again floored at zero. `penalty_cents` then prices those billable minutes **marginally** across three bands: the first thirty billable minutes at 250 cents each, the next thirty — those from the thirty-first up to and including the sixtieth — at 400 cents each, and every billable minute beyond the sixtieth at 600 cents each. Each band prices only the minutes that fall inside it; the total is not priced at the single rate of the band it happens to land in. To make the distinction concrete: 47 minutes of lateness against a ten-minute grace gives 37 billable minutes, which price as thirty minutes at 250 plus seven minutes at 400, for 10,300 cents — not the 14,800 cents that flat-rating all 37 minutes at 400 would give.

A missed run is recoverable exactly once. Creating a recovery run applies only to a run in `MISSED`, and only when no other run already names that run as the run it recovers; otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_RUN_STATE`. The recovery request carries a fresh `truck_id`, `stand_id`, `window_start_utc` and `window_end_utc`, and the new window is revalidated against all four window rules above, in the same pinned order and with no exemption for the missed run itself: because that run is still `MISSED` rather than `CANCELLED` when the window is tested, it goes on occupying its own truck and stand, so a recovery window that overlaps the missed run's window on the same truck or fills the same stand past its limit is refused exactly as a stranger's run would be. The missed run moves `MISSED → RECOVERED` with its `version` incremented, and a new run is created in `RESERVED` carrying `recovery_of_run_id` set to the recovered run and the same `loading_manifest_id`. This is the join point of the fork: the flight can only reach `DEPARTED` once the recovery run itself reaches `DELIVERED`.

### 3.8. Delay Recovery and Flight Cancellation Cascades

Delays are recorded by the `ScheduleFeedOperator` and carry `delay_minutes`, which must be at least 1, together with an optional `reason` that — like the `reason` on a cancellation and on a special meal order change — is accepted and then discarded rather than stored on any row. A delay is accepted while the flight is in `SCHEDULED`, `COUNTS_LOCKED`, `PLANNED` or `LOADED`, and is refused from `DEPARTED`, `RECONCILED` or `CANCELLED` with `ERR_INVALID_FLIGHT_STATE`. Recording a delay never changes `status` itself; the flight's own state is unaffected while everything hanging off it moves.

The delay first adds the submitted `delay_minutes` to the flight's cumulative `delay_minutes` and recomputes both `revised_std_utc` and `special_meal_cutoff_utc` from it. Because the cutoff has moved later, every special meal order in `LATE_REJECTED` whose `requested_at` now falls at or before the recomputed `special_meal_cutoff_utc` is reopened, moving `LATE_REJECTED → REQUESTED` and incrementing that order's `version`; and when `counts_locked_at` is not null — that is, when the counts were already locked — each reopened order is immediately re-evaluated in the same cascade and continues `REQUESTED → CONFIRMED`, subject to the same `ACTIVE` gate the lock applies, so a reopened order whose effective specification has since been retired stops at `REQUESTED`. Because §2.5 counts a row's version increment once per request, an order that both reopens and re-confirms in this one cascade advances its `version` by exactly 1. That closes the `REQUESTED → LATE_REJECTED → REQUESTED` cycle and is why `LATE_REJECTED` is not terminal. Critically, `units_planned` is **not** recomputed by this cascade, and neither is any batch's `release_deadline_utc`; an order reopened after planning therefore has no tray built for it, and that shortfall surfaces later as specification deviation at reconciliation.

The slot half of the cascade is threshold-gated. Only once the flight's **cumulative** `delay_minutes` has reached an hour and a half does the delay touch delivery runs at all. When it has, every run of the flight in `RESERVED` or `RESCHEDULED` has both `window_start_utc` and `window_end_utc` shifted forward by the cumulative `delay_minutes` — not by this request's increment — and its status set to `RESCHEDULED`. A shifted window that no longer satisfies truck buffering or stand occupancy does not become `RESCHEDULED`: that run is instead `CANCELLED` with `miss_reason` set to `SLOT_LOST`. The runs are visited in `created_at` ascending order and each is shifted and settled before the next is examined, so a later run's buffering and occupancy tests are made against its siblings' already-shifted windows, and against every other run standing on that truck or stand whatever flight it serves; a run that this same cascade has just `CANCELLED`, like any other cancelled run, occupies nothing and is ignored by both tests, and a run never conflicts with its own former position. Every run that moved, whether rescheduled or cancelled, has its `version` incremented. Any moved run whose `loading_manifest_id` points at an `ISSUED` manifest drives that manifest `ISSUED → AMENDED` with `amendment_count` incremented, which in turn drives its flight `LOADED → PLANNED`. The flight's own `version` increments once for the delay.

Cancellation is the other cascade, and it is the blunt one. A `ScheduleFeedOperator` may cancel a flight in `SCHEDULED`, `COUNTS_LOCKED`, `PLANNED` or `LOADED`, optionally recording a `reason`; those four are the whole of the permitted set, so a flight already `DEPARTED`, `RECONCILED` or `CANCELLED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_FLIGHT_STATE` — once the aircraft has pushed back there is nothing left to unwind. The flight moves to `CANCELLED` and every dependent row that is still non-terminal is unwound in one request: every special meal order in `REQUESTED` or `CONFIRMED` becomes `CANCELLED`; every production batch in `PLANNED`, `IN_PRODUCTION`, `HACCP_HOLD`, `COMPLETED` or `RELEASED` becomes `REJECTED` with `scrapped_qty` set to its full `planned_qty`; every cart item in `ACTIVE` becomes `VOID`; every loading manifest in `DRAFT`, `ISSUED` or `AMENDED` becomes `VOID`; and every delivery run in `RESERVED`, `RESCHEDULED`, `EN_ROUTE` or `AT_AIRCRAFT` becomes `CANCELLED` with `miss_reason` set to `SLOT_LOST`. Rows that had already reached a terminal state are left exactly as they were. Those five families are the whole of the cascade: the cart rows themselves are outside it, so a cart keeps its `build_status`, its `seal_status`, its `seal_number`, its `sealed_by_user_id` and its `version` exactly as they stood — even a cart that was `BUILT` and `SEALED` — while its `loaded_units` falls to 0 simply because every item it held has become `VOID`. Neither the `BUILT → BUILDING` fallback nor the seal break nor the manifest amendment of §3.6 is run on this edge; the manifests go straight to `VOID` and the flight to `CANCELLED`. The flight's `version` increments, as does the `version` of every row the cascade touched. The side-effect set is identical from all four permitted starting states.

### 3.9. Uplift Reconciliation, Disposition, Bonded Waste and Cost Reporting

Once a flight has departed, the `ComplianceOfficer` reconciles the uplift. Reconciliation requires the flight to be in `DEPARTED` — anything else is refused with `ERR_INVALID_FLIGHT_STATE` — and carries `expected_flight_version` as described in §2.7, the submitted `lines`, and optionally `observed_seals`. Each entry of `lines` is submitted as a `meal_specification_id` together with that specification's `consumed_units` and `returned_units`; `lines` must carry at least one entry, and every `meal_specification_id` in it is resolved — reporting `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` in submitted order — before the version guard and the flight-state check are reached. `observed_seals` is the list of `seal_number` strings the ramp agent actually read off the returned carts; when it is omitted, or sent explicitly as null, the manifest snapshot is taken as observed in full.

Reconciliation creates an uplift record in `DRAFT` against the flight, drives any earlier uplift record of the same flight still in `DRAFT` to `VOID`, moves the flight `DEPARTED → RECONCILED`, and increments the flight's `version`. The record's `lines` are returned ordered by the owning specification's `spec_code` ascending — a stable reordering, so two entries naming the same specification keep the order they were submitted in — and each entry is augmented with its own `uplifted_units` and `line_gross_cents` alongside the `reuse_units`, `donate_units` and `destroy_units` slots that disposition will later fill, each of those three opening at 0 rather than null. At the record level, `uplifted_units` is the sum of `qty_units` over every `ACTIVE` cart item belonging to a cart of the flight at the moment of reconciliation, `consumed_units` and `returned_units` are the sums of the submitted per-line figures, and `unused_units` is `uplifted_units` minus `consumed_units` — which, once the reconciliation check passes, equals `returned_units`. That check is the conservation rule: submitted `consumed_units` plus submitted `returned_units` must equal `uplifted_units`, and a discrepancy is refused with `ERR_UPLIFT_UNITS_MISMATCH`. `seal_mismatch_count` is the number of entries in the manifest's pinned `cart_seal_snapshot` whose `seal_number` does not appear in `observed_seals`, and is 0 whenever `observed_seals` was omitted. The manifest consulted is the flight's most recently created manifest standing in `ISSUED` or `AMENDED`; when the flight has no such manifest at all, `seal_mismatch_count` is 0 whatever `observed_seals` says. The record's `status` runs `DRAFT`, `DISPOSITIONED` and `CLOSED`, with `CLOSED` terminal; the `VOID` state a superseded draft is driven to sits outside that lifecycle and outside the `status` filter of the list read, which admits `DRAFT`, `DISPOSITIONED` and `CLOSED` alone and rejects `VOID` as an undeclared enum member with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, even though a `VOID` record is still returned by an unfiltered list.

The **gross uplifted cost** is computed at reconciliation and written to `gross_cost_cents`. For each `ACTIVE` cart item of the flight, taken in `created_at` ascending order, the item's overhead-loaded gross is its `qty_units` multiplied by its specification's `unit_cost_cents`, multiplied by one plus a twelve percent overhead rate, with that product rounded half up to whole cents. `gross_cost_cents` is the sum of those per-item figures, and each `lines` entry's `line_gross_cents` is the corresponding sum restricted to the items drawing on that entry's specification. `VOID` items contribute nothing at all. Rounding happens per item and never on an intermediate product.

Reconciliation also derives `cost_per_pax_cents` — a snapshot of the flight's own figure, which is reported as 0 whenever `uplifted_units` is 0, exactly as the flight itself reports 0 while nothing has been loaded — together with `spec_deviation_pct`, `late_penalty_cents`, `handling_fee_cents` and `billing_total_cents`, and it sets `net_cost_cents` equal to `gross_cost_cents`, because no unit has yet been dispositioned. Two audit consequences may follow immediately: when `spec_deviation_pct` is at or above the airline's `spec_deviation_tolerance_pct`, a `SPEC_DEVIATION` finding is raised with `source` `SYSTEM` and severity taken from the deviation bands — and when the airline has tuned its tolerance below 5.00 so that a deviation clears it while still falling under the `MINOR` band's own floor, the severity recorded is `MINOR`; and when `seal_mismatch_count` is at least 1, a `SEAL_MISMATCH` finding of `MAJOR` severity is raised with `source` `SYSTEM`. The specification-deviation finding is raised first and the seal-mismatch finding second, so a reconciliation that raises both increments `open_finding_count` twice in that order. Each finding created increments the airline's `open_finding_count` by one, which may in turn drive the airline `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`.

**Specification deviation** measures how far the trays actually loaded diverged from the trays the plan demanded. For each specification appearing in the flight's final requirement, the required quantity is that specification's unit count in the final requirement, and the delivered quantity is the sum of `qty_units` over `ACTIVE` cart items whose source batch names that specification. The specification's deviation is the absolute difference between delivered and required, divided by required, multiplied by one hundred, and rounded half up to two decimals. `spec_deviation_pct` is the largest such deviation across the flight's specifications, or 0.00 when the flight has no requirement at all. `VOID` items and rejected batches contribute zero delivered units. The severity band is inclusive at its lower bound: a deviation from 5.00 up to and including 9.99 is `MINOR`, from 10.00 up to and including 19.99 is `MAJOR`, and 20.00 or above is `CRITICAL`. A deviation of exactly 5.00 against the default tolerance of 5.00 therefore *does* raise a `MINOR` finding, while 4.44 raises nothing.

**Cost per passenger** is derived on the flight and is also available as a computed read. The read reports the flight's `flight_id`, its `total_pax`, the `gross_cost_cents` for the flight, a `cabin_breakdown` array, the blended `cost_per_pax_cents`, the airline's `cost_cap_per_pax_cents`, a boolean `cap_breached` that is true exactly when the blended `cost_per_pax_cents` is strictly greater than that cap, so a flight landing precisely on its cap is not breaching it, and the `spec_deviation_pct`. The `cabin_breakdown` array carries one entry per cabin in the fixed order `FIRST`, `BUSINESS`, `ECONOMY` — all three always present — and each entry carries exactly four keys: `cabin`, its `cabin_pax` count, that cabin's `cabin_cost_cents`, and its rounded `cabin_cost_per_pax_cents`. A cabin whose passenger count is 0 is reported with `cabin_pax` 0 and `cabin_cost_per_pax_cents` 0 rather than null, keeps whatever `cabin_cost_cents` its items accumulated, and is excluded from the blend. The arithmetic proceeds in a pinned order that is *not* interchangeable with dividing the total by the passenger count. First, each `ACTIVE` cart item's overhead-loaded gross is computed exactly as for `gross_cost_cents`: `qty_units` times the specification's `unit_cost_cents` times one plus the twelve percent overhead, rounded half up. Second, taking the cabins in the fixed order `FIRST`, then `BUSINESS`, then `ECONOMY`, each cabin's cost is the sum of those item figures over the items whose batch specification serves that cabin, accumulated in `created_at` ascending order; a cabin with zero booked passengers is excluded from both the numerator and the denominator. Third — the first rounding — each included cabin's per-passenger cost is that cabin's cost divided by that cabin's booked passenger count, rounded half up to whole cents. Fourth — the second and final rounding — `cost_per_pax_cents` is the sum, across included cabins, of each cabin's rounded per-passenger cost multiplied by that cabin's passenger count, divided by `total_pax`, rounded half up to whole cents. Rounding per cabin *before* blending is load-bearing: on a flight with 7, 23 and 181 passengers and cabin costs of 32,928, 100,464 and 294,616 cents, the per-cabin figures are 4,704, 4,368 and 1,628 and the blended result is 2,029 cents, whereas dividing the 428,008-cent total by 211 passengers would give 2,028.

Disposition is the second `ComplianceOfficer` act on the record, and it applies only while the record is in `DRAFT`; any other state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_RESOURCE_STATE`. The request carries `dispositions`, at least one entry long and submitted as one entry per `lines` entry — matched on `meal_specification_id` — each carrying `reuse_units`, `donate_units` and `destroy_units`, all three required non-negative integers. Every `meal_specification_id` in `dispositions` is resolved under band 60, in submitted order, reporting `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`. The matching itself is then checked, and both of its failures are reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`: a second entry naming a specification an earlier entry already named, and a set of named specifications that is not exactly the set the record's `lines` name — whether an entry is missing or a stranger has been added. Those two checks sit after the specification references have resolved but ahead of the record's own state, so mismatched `dispositions` against a record that is no longer `DRAFT` report `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than `ERR_INVALID_RESOURCE_STATE`. For every line, re-used plus donated plus destroyed units must equal that line's `returned_units`, or the request is refused with `ERR_DISPOSITION_UNITS_MISMATCH`. When the airline's `waste_regime` is `BONDED`, every line must carry `reuse_units` of 0 and `donate_units` of 0, because bonded international waste may be neither re-used nor donated; a non-zero figure in either is refused with `ERR_BONDED_REUSE_FORBIDDEN`. Disposition writes the three figures onto each line, moves the record `DRAFT → DISPOSITIONED`, increments its `version`, and drives every `RELEASED` batch of the flight whose specification received at least one destroyed unit `RELEASED → DISPOSED`. What disposition does **not** do is re-derive the reconciliation figures: `uplifted_units`, `consumed_units`, `returned_units`, `unused_units`, `gross_cost_cents`, each line's `uplifted_units` and `line_gross_cents`, `cost_per_pax_cents`, `spec_deviation_pct` and `seal_mismatch_count` are all left exactly as reconciliation wrote them, however cart state may have moved since.

Disposition is also where the cost calculation switches variant, from gross-uplifted pricing to **net-consumed** pricing, written to `net_cost_cents`. First the blended unit rate is derived once: `gross_cost_cents` divided by `uplifted_units`, rounded half up to whole cents — and rounded exactly once, not per line. Then the credit basis is the sum across lines of `reuse_units` plus `donate_units`; destroyed units earn nothing. The credit is that credit basis multiplied by the blended rate, multiplied by the airline's `unused_recovery_rate_pct` divided by one hundred, rounded half up to whole cents. `net_cost_cents` is `gross_cost_cents` minus that credit. Because a `BONDED` airline's dispositions must be entirely destruction, its credit basis is always zero and its `net_cost_cents` can never diverge from `gross_cost_cents` — while an otherwise identical `DOMESTIC` airline's does.

**Billing** is recomputed at disposition, in a fixed four-step order, and the handling tier is keyed on `net_cost_cents` alone. Step one selects the handling rate from the band containing `net_cost_cents`: nine percent for a net cost from 0 up to and including 199,999 cents, seven percent from 200,000 up to and including 499,999 cents, and five percent for 500,000 cents and above. Step two sets `handling_fee_cents` to `net_cost_cents` multiplied by that rate, rounded half up to whole cents. Step three sets `late_penalty_cents` to the sum of `penalty_cents` over every delivery run belonging to any manifest of this flight, in any run status, accumulated in `created_at` ascending order. Step four sets `billing_total_cents` to `net_cost_cents` plus `handling_fee_cents` plus `late_penalty_cents`. The band is selected before the late penalty is added and is never re-selected afterwards: with a net cost of 489,216 cents and a late penalty of 11,500 cents, the seven percent band applies, giving a handling fee of 34,245 cents and a billing total of 534,961 cents — whereas keying the band on the 500,716-cent combined figure would wrongly select five percent and give 24,461 and 525,177.

Arrival waste handling closes the loop. A `ComplianceOfficer` raises a waste manifest naming the `uplift_record_id` it covers, the `disposal_method` route and the customs `seal_number` fitted to the sealed waste — a required string that may not be empty or blank, a whitespace-only value being a request-shape failure reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The named record must be standing in `DISPOSITIONED`, because the destroyed units the manifest covers are only known once disposition has written them: a record still in `DRAFT`, or already `CLOSED` or `VOID`, refuses the manifest with `ERR_INVALID_RESOURCE_STATE`, and that state test is made before the regime and seal-uniqueness tests below. The manifest's `regime` is copied from the owning airline's `waste_regime` at creation and is never submitted. `disposal_method` is one of `INCINERATION`, `RENDERING` or `LANDFILL`, but the regime constrains the choice: a `BONDED` regime permits `INCINERATION` only, while a `DOMESTIC` regime permits all three; anything outside the permitted set is refused with `ERR_BONDED_WASTE_DISPOSAL_INVALID`. The `seal_number` must be unique across waste manifests and carts alike, or the request is refused with `ERR_SEAL_NUMBER_DUPLICATE`. `sealed_weight_grams` is recomputed, never submitted: it is the sum, across the owning record's lines, of each line's `destroy_units` multiplied by that line's specification `gross_weight_grams`. Re-used and donated units carry no disposal mass. The manifest opens in `DRAFT` with `rejection_reason` null, and its creation drives every cart of the flight still in `LOADED` along the build axis `LOADED → RETURNED`, breaking each such cart's seal axis `SEALED → BROKEN` or `RESEALED → BROKEN`, clearing that cart's `sealed_by_user_id` while leaving its `seal_number` in place for audit, and incrementing each cart's `version`. Carts in any other build state are left untouched. This is the one seal break in the service that does **not** amend the manifest that pinned the cart: the flight has already departed and reconciled, so no loading manifest moves `ISSUED → AMENDED` here, no `amendment_count` changes, and neither the flight nor the uplift record has its `version` touched by the waste manifest's creation.

Three transitions finish the waste manifest, selected by an `action` value. Sealing moves it `DRAFT → SEALED` and requires the owning record's flight to be `RECONCILED`. Disposing moves it `SEALED → DISPOSED` and drives the owning uplift record `DISPOSITIONED → CLOSED`. Rejecting applies from either `DRAFT` or `SEALED`, moves the manifest to `REJECTED`, requires a `rejection_reason` to be supplied — an omitted, null or whitespace-only reason on a `REJECT` is a request-shape failure reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and reported before the manifest identifier in the path is resolved, while a `rejection_reason` sent alongside a `SEAL` or `DISPOSE` action is accepted and ignored — stores it on the manifest, and releases the `seal_number` for reuse by a replacement manifest; `DISPOSED` and `REJECTED` are both terminal. Because `sealed_weight_grams` is recomputed from the same immutable disposition lines, a replacement manifest raised after a rejection reproduces the identical gram figure. Any of the three applied from a state where it is undefined — disposing of a `DRAFT` manifest, for instance — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_RESOURCE_STATE`. Each successful transition increments the manifest's `version`.

### 3.10. The Audit Docket: Findings, Penalties and Airline Suspension

Findings arrive from two directions. Most are raised by the service itself as cascades fire, carrying `source` `SYSTEM` and a null `evidence_ref`; those are the `HACCP_BREACH` findings from condemned food, the `LATE_DELIVERY` findings from banded lateness and missed slots, and the `SPEC_DEVIATION` and `SEAL_MISMATCH` findings from reconciliation. The rest are raised manually, by either an `AirlineRep` — who may only raise findings against its own airline — or a `ComplianceOfficer`. A manual finding names the `airline_id` it is charged to, the `flight_id` it is raised against, the `finding_type`, the `severity`, and optionally an `evidence_ref`, which is stored as null when it is omitted or sent as null. The two body references are resolved in that declared order, the `airline_id` first under band 60's generic `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`, the `flight_id` second but with the flight's own typed code `ERR_FLIGHT_NOT_FOUND` rather than the generic code band 60 otherwise nominates — the same exception §3.6 makes for a cart's `flight_id`. The `AirlineRep`'s own-airline restriction is band 50 and so is applied to the submitted `airline_id` before either reference is resolved, meaning a rep naming another airline's `id` gets `ERR_AIRLINE_SCOPE_VIOLATION` even when that `id` names nothing at all. `source` is recorded as `AIRLINE` when the raiser is an `AirlineRep` and as `SYSTEM` otherwise. `finding_type` is one of `SPEC_DEVIATION`, `HACCP_BREACH`, `SEAL_MISMATCH` or `LATE_DELIVERY`, and `severity` is one of `MINOR`, `MAJOR` or `CRITICAL`. Every finding opens in `OPEN`; the lifecycle runs `OPEN`, `DISPUTED`, `ACCEPTED` and `WITHDRAWN`, with `WITHDRAWN` terminal.

`penalty_cents` is derived at creation from the finding's own `finding_type` and `severity` and can never be submitted, nor is it ever re-derived afterwards. Each type carries a base amount — 120,000 cents for a specification deviation, 500,000 cents for a HACCP breach, 200,000 cents for a seal mismatch, and 80,000 cents for a late delivery — and each severity carries a multiplier: one for `MINOR`, two and a half for `MAJOR`, and five for `CRITICAL`. `penalty_cents` is the base multiplied by the multiplier, rounded half up to whole cents. A `MINOR` specification deviation therefore carries 120,000 cents, a `MAJOR` late delivery 200,000, a `MAJOR` seal mismatch 500,000, and a `CRITICAL` HACCP breach 2,500,000.

Resolution is selected by an `action` value together with an optional `note`, which is accepted for the record-keeper's convenience and then discarded, stored on no row and echoed in no response. Because one endpoint serves all three actions, its permitted role list is the union of the officer's and the rep's, and the pairing of action to role is enforced inside it at band 20: an `action` the acting role does not own — a `ComplianceOfficer` sending `WITHDRAW`, or an `AirlineRep` sending `DISPUTE` or `ACCEPT` — is refused with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`, and unlike the special meal order change of §3.3 that pairing is checked *before* the finding identifier in the path is resolved, so a wrong-role action against a finding that does not exist reports `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` and not a 404. **Disputing** is the `ComplianceOfficer`'s and applies to a finding in `OPEN`, moving it `OPEN → DISPUTED` and decrementing the airline's `open_finding_count` by one, which may drive the airline `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE`. **Accepting** is also the `ComplianceOfficer`'s and applies to a finding in `OPEN` or in `DISPUTED`, moving it `OPEN → ACCEPTED` or `DISPUTED → ACCEPTED`, adding the finding's `penalty_cents` to the airline's `penalty_accrued_cents`, and decrementing `open_finding_count` by one only when the prior status was `OPEN` — an accepted dispute has already been decremented and is not decremented twice — which again may drive the airline `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE`. **Withdrawing** is the `AirlineRep`'s, permitted only when the acting `X-User-ID` is the finding's own airline, and applies from `OPEN`, `DISPUTED` or `ACCEPTED`, moving the finding `OPEN → WITHDRAWN`, `DISPUTED → WITHDRAWN` or `ACCEPTED → WITHDRAWN` respectively. Withdrawal subtracts the finding's `penalty_cents` from `penalty_accrued_cents` only when the prior status was `ACCEPTED`, and decrements `open_finding_count` only when the prior status was `OPEN`. That asymmetry is what makes withdrawal an exact compensating act: an acceptance followed by a withdrawal leaves `penalty_accrued_cents` at precisely its pre-acceptance value. Every resolution increments the finding's `version`, and resolving a finding already `WITHDRAWN` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_RESOURCE_STATE`. Both `open_finding_count` and `penalty_accrued_cents` are derived afresh from the airline's own finding rows on every read — the first counting the findings whose `status` is `OPEN`, the second summing `penalty_cents` over the findings whose `status` is `ACCEPTED` — rather than being stored counters that are edited in place. Wherever this document speaks of a finding incrementing, adding to or decrementing either figure, that is bookkeeping language for the same derivation, and the observable consequence is that raising or resolving a finding does **not** by itself mutate the airline row: the airline's `version` and `updated_at` move only when the suspension threshold below is actually crossed in one direction or the other, even though the two counts it reports change on the very next read.

Suspension is the docket's lever over production. When a finding creation pushes an airline's `open_finding_count` to three or more, the airline moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`, its own `version` increments, and every flight of that airline whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `COUNTS_LOCKED` has `production_hold` set to true with that flight's `version` incremented. A held flight can still take passenger-count refreshes, retimes and special meal orders, but production planning against it is refused with `ERR_AIRLINE_SUSPENDED`. When a resolution drops `open_finding_count` back below three, the airline moves `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE`, its `version` increments, and every flight of that airline carrying a `production_hold` has it cleared to false with that flight's `version` incremented — releasing the kitchen to plan again.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Every endpoint below is served at the root of the service; there is no version or product path prefix, and a trailing slash on any path is tolerated as equivalent to its absence. Creation operations return `201 Created`; every other successful operation, including state transitions, amendments, voids and reads, returns `200 OK`. Collection reads accept the `limit` and `offset` parameters and the filters named in their descriptions, and follow the ordering rules in §2.3. Failures return the status code recorded against the relevant error identifier in §5, and a request that fails authorisation is refused before any business rule is evaluated. In the authorisation column, "Any authenticated role" means all seven roles enumerated in §2.1; where specific roles are named, no other role may call that endpoint, even a `PlatformAdmin`.

## 5. Error Code Catalogue

Every failure in this service is reported through the error envelope defined in §2.2, carrying one of the identifiers below in `error_id`. Where a single request could trip several of these, the identifier reported is the one belonging to the earliest validation band in the ladder of §2.6, and within a band the earliest condition in the order stated for that band. Two transport-level failures reuse identifiers from this table outside any band: a request addressed to a path the service does not serve at all answers `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` with HTTP 404, and a request whose path exists but whose HTTP method that path does not accept answers `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` with HTTP 405 rather than the 400 the table records for it.
