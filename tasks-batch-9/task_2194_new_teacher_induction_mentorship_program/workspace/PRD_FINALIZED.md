# Teacher Induction Mentorship API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Every district that hires new teachers faces the same two-year problem: a novice teacher arrives with a preliminary licence, and the district must both support that teacher and produce a defensible evidence file proving the support happened before the state will advance the licence. Today that evidence lives in mentor notebooks, e-mail threads, spreadsheet stipend claims and PDF observation forms. When the state credentialing office asks why a particular teacher was recommended, nobody can reconstruct which contact hours were counted, who verified them, or whether the mentor who logged them still held a valid mentoring certificate on the day of the visit.

This document specifies the Teacher Induction & Mentorship API, the district system of record for that evidence chain. It governs the full induction program: school sites and their mentor capacity, region-scoped seminar cohorts, mentor training and certification cycles, subject-and-site-aware mentor matching, verified contact-hour logging, classroom observation cycles with documented feedback, seminar attendance credit, induction milestones tied to licence advancement, quarterly mentor stipend disbursements, retention outcome tracking, and the final completion packet submitted to the state.

Two design commitments run through every rule below. The first is **evidence integrity**: an amount, a counter or a licence recommendation may only rest on records that a second person verified, and when a record is withdrawn every figure derived from it must unwind deterministically, all the way to a packet already sitting on a state officer's desk. The second is **separation of duties**: the person who produced a piece of evidence may never be the person who blesses it, which is why the system records an acting user identifier on every consequential write and refuses operations where those identifiers collide.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contract-level rules that apply to every endpoint in the API. Domain workflow lives in §3.

### 2.1. District Identity and Role Authorization

Every request other than the logical-clock test hook must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity that user is acting in. Five roles exist, and their names are spelled exactly as follows: `ProgramAdmin`, `SiteInductionLead`, `Mentor`, `NoviceTeacher`, and `StateCredentialingOfficer`.

`ProgramAdmin` is the district induction office: it owns configuration, certification, fiscal and cohort lifecycle operations, and it is the only role permitted to void verified evidence or move the logical clock. `SiteInductionLead` is the induction lead at a single school. `Mentor` is a veteran teacher carrying a mentoring caseload. `NoviceTeacher` is the new teacher being inducted, and has read-only reach. `StateCredentialingOfficer` is the state credentialing authority: it decides submitted completion packets and reads district data, but never creates or alters district-side evidence.

A missing or empty `X-User-Role` header is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before any path, body or state is inspected. A role string that is not one of the five names above is treated identically — an unrecognised role is indistinguishable from no credential at all, so it also yields `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` rather than a permission error. Once the role is recognised, the system checks whether that role appears in the addressed endpoint's authorized list; if it does not, the request fails with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403). This check runs *before* any existence lookup, so a forbidden role probing an identifier that does not exist still receives 403 and learns nothing about which identifiers are real.

Three of the five roles are backed by a persisted entity, and for those roles `X-User-ID` is not a free-form operator label:

- When `X-User-Role` is `SiteInductionLead`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Site`. Ownership checks compare the addressed resource's site — directly through `site_id` on a mentor or novice, or transitively through the novice reached from a match, log, cycle, attendance row, attainment row or packet — against `X-User-ID`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `Mentor`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Mentor`. That mentor may only touch resources reachable from its own matches: the `mentor_id` of the match behind a contact log or an observation cycle must equal `X-User-ID`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `NoviceTeacher`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `NoviceTeacher`, and read access is confined to rows whose `novice_id` — or whose parent match's `novice_id` — equals `X-User-ID`.

For those three roles, an absent `X-User-ID`, or an `X-User-ID` that resolves to no live row of the backing entity, is a failure of the caller's own identity and is reported as `ERR_IDENTITY_NOT_RESOLVED` (HTTP 403). Identity resolution outranks every resource lookup: the caller must be somebody before the system will tell them whether the target exists.

`ProgramAdmin` and `StateCredentialingOfficer` are both opaque identities. For these two roles `X-User-ID` is an operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it where a workflow demands an actor stamp — for example as `prepared_by_user_id` or `approved_by_user_id` on a disbursement, or as `submitted_by_user_id` and `decided_by_user_id` on a completion packet — but never compares it against any entity's `id`.

Where a caller holds the right role and resolves to a real identity but the addressed row belongs to a different site, mentor or novice, the refusal is `ERR_OWNERSHIP_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). Ownership is deliberately checked *after* existence, so that an owner of nothing cannot enumerate identifiers, and *before* any state rule, so that a foreign caller never learns the target's state.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Listing, Filtering and Pagination

Collection reads are paginated through two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of rows returned; when omitted it defaults to 10, and the accepted range is a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100. `offset` skips rows and defaults to 0, with no upper bound beyond the size of the collection. A `limit` outside 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400). Pagination is validated only on list endpoints, and because list endpoints reach no business rule at all, it is the very last validation in the system-wide precedence order described in §2.7.

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are sorted by `created_at` ascending. List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` in the `meta` object of the envelope.

Because the entity graph is flat — every collection is addressed at the root of the API and children are reached by filtering rather than by nesting — each foreign key declared anywhere in the model is reverse-reachable through a filtered list. Sites may be narrowed by `region_code` and `status`; mentors by `site_id`, `certification_status` and `subject_endorsement`; cohorts by `program_year`, `region_code` and `status`; novice teachers by `site_id`, `cohort_id`, `program_status` and `license_advancement_status`; matches by `mentor_id`, `novice_id` and `match_status`; contact logs by `match_id`, `verification_status` and `contact_date`; observation cycles by `match_id`, `phase` and `feedback_review_status`; milestone definitions by `prerequisite_milestone_id` and `gates_license_recommendation`; attainment rows by `novice_id`, `milestone_id` and `status`; seminar sessions by `cohort_id` and `status`; attendance rows by `session_id`, `novice_id`, `attendance_status` and `credited`; disbursements by `mentor_id`, `status` and `period_code`; and completion packets by `novice_id` and `packet_status`. Milestone definitions are returned in `sequence_index` ascending order rather than by creation time. Every one of these filters is optional, and they combine conjunctively.

Path parameters are always the literal identifier of the addressed row. Since no collection is nested beneath another, there is at most one path identifier per request; when it resolves to nothing, the entity-specific 404 for that collection is returned (see §2.7), and that refusal precedes any inspection of the request body.

### 2.4. Record Identity, Audit Stamps and Program Versioning

Every stored row carries an `id`: a server-minted UUID with no type prefix. Clients never supply it. Every row also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the row was inserted, sourced from the logical clock described in §2.5.

Almost every entity additionally carries `updated_at` — an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp refreshed on every mutation — and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on insert and increments by exactly one on every mutation of that row. Recomputing a derived counter counts as a mutation: when a contact-log verification raises a novice's `verified_contact_minutes`, the novice's `updated_at` moves and the novice's `version` increments, exactly as if a client had edited the record directly. The single exception is the `Milestone` entity, which is immutable configuration: a milestone definition carries only `id` and `created_at`, and both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from it. There is no endpoint that updates or deletes a milestone definition.

When one request mutates several rows, every mutated row's `version` increments — the version bumps listed throughout §3 are cumulative, not alternatives. A single mentor-certification suspension, for example, bumps the mentor, each suspended match, and (through those matches) each affected site.

### 2.5. The Logical Program Clock

Nothing in this system reads wall-clock time. Certification expiry, contact-date windows, seminar weeks, observation buffers and stipend period closure are all evaluated against a server-held logical UTC clock. Before any client sets it, that clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it must never fall back to real time. A `ProgramAdmin` moves it by supplying a `current_utc` instant to the clock-setting test hook, whose response echoes the new `current_utc`.

Advancing the clock has exactly one side effect beyond the new reading, and it is a sweep rather than a broad recomputation. After the clock is set, every `Mentor` whose `certification_status` is `CERTIFIED` or `SUSPENDED` and whose `certification_expires_on` is strictly before the new logical time transitions to `LAPSED`. Mentors are swept in `created_at` ascending order, and each lapse runs that transition's own declared side effects — including, for a mentor leaving `CERTIFIED`, the suspension of every one of that mentor's `ACTIVE` matches. No other stored row is touched by setting the clock, and no other state machine advances as a result of it.

### 2.6. Guarding Against Stale Parent Reads

Three creation operations rewrite or consume capacity that belongs to a parent record, and each therefore demands that the client prove it read that parent recently. In all three cases the version field is a required, non-optional body field, and a mismatch against the parent's current `version` is refused with `ERR_STALE_PARENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

- Proposing a mentor-novice match requires `expected_mentor_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Mentor`. On the automatic-selection path the value is compared against the `version` of the mentor the ranking policy selects. A previous match proposal against the same mentor legitimately consumed caseload headroom and advanced `Mentor.version`, so a client holding an older number is working from a stale view of that mentor's caseload and is told to re-read rather than being allowed to over-commit the mentor.
- Drafting a stipend disbursement requires `expected_mentor_version` against the same `Mentor.version`. A new draft for a mentor and period legitimately supersedes the prior draft for that same period, so the write is destructive with respect to whatever the client last observed; a stale version means the client would be superseding a draft it never saw.
- Creating a completion certification packet requires `expected_novice_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `NoviceTeacher`. Packet scoring reads the novice's readiness and milestone counters at creation time, and those counters advance `NoviceTeacher.version` whenever contact logs, observation cycles, seminar attendance or milestone attainments change; a stale version means the packet would be scored from evidence the client never observed.

No other endpoint accepts a version field, and none should be invented for them. The staleness comparison happens after the parent row is known to exist and to be in an acceptable state, and before any business rule fires, so a caller holding an out-of-date view is told to re-read rather than being handed a business-rule failure that would evaporate on refresh.

### 2.7. Refusal Precedence Across the Rulebook

Many requests violate more than one rule at once. The system evaluates rules in a single, strictly ordered ladder and returns the **first** violated rule; no later rule may mask an earlier one. Implementers should treat this ordering as normative, because it is frequently the only thing distinguishing two plausible error codes for the same request.

1. `X-User-Role` present and non-empty — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. `X-User-Role` names one of the five declared roles — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
3. The role is authorized for the addressed endpoint — otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`.
4. For an entity-backed role, `X-User-ID` is present and resolves to a live row of its backing entity — otherwise `ERR_IDENTITY_NOT_RESOLVED`.
5. The path identifier resolves to an existing row of the addressed collection — otherwise the collection's own 404: `ERR_SITE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MENTOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COHORT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_NOVICE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MATCH_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONTACT_LOG_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OBSERVATION_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MILESTONE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ATTAINMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SEMINAR_SESSION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SEMINAR_ATTENDANCE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DISBURSEMENT_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_CERTIFICATION_NOT_FOUND`. Path existence precedes body validity entirely: an advance posted to an unknown observation cycle with a malformed body returns the 404.
6. Every foreign key supplied in a request body resolves to an existing row of the entity it references, again reported through the referenced collection's 404 code. Body keys are resolved in the order they are declared for that operation, and the first unresolvable one determines the error. This applies to mentor creation, novice enrolment, match proposal, contact-log creation, observation-cycle creation, milestone definition, attainment creation, seminar-session creation, attendance recording, disbursement drafting and packet creation.
7. Required body fields are present, and each field satisfies its declared JSON type, enum membership, string length, numeric range, array cardinality and datetime format — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). This covers a missing required field, an unknown enum member, `minutes` outside 1 through 300, a rubric value outside 1 through 4, a `growth_area_codes` array with zero or more than four entries, a `description` shorter than 20 characters, a datetime that is not ISO 8601, and a `caseload_cap` outside 1 through 4. It applies to every write endpoint.
8. The resolved entity-backed identity owns the addressed resource — otherwise `ERR_OWNERSHIP_VIOLATION`. This applies to every endpoint whose authorized roles include `SiteInductionLead`, `Mentor` or `NoviceTeacher`.
9. The `Site` involved is `OPEN` — otherwise `ERR_SITE_CLOSED` (HTTP 422). Guards mentor registration, novice enrolment and match proposal.
10. The `MentorMatch` is in the `match_status` the operation needs — `ACTIVE` for contact-log creation, contact-log review, observation-cycle creation and a disposition to END; `PROPOSED` for a disposition to ACTIVATE or REJECT; `SUSPENDED` for a disposition back to `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_MATCH_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). It also guards contact-log resubmission. Match-state gating outranks every fiscal rule below it, so verifying against a `SUSPENDED` match reports the match-state error even when the same-day credit ceiling is simultaneously exhausted.
11. The `ContactLog` is in the `verification_status` the operation needs — `SUBMITTED` for review, `DISPUTED` for resubmission, `VERIFIED` for voiding — otherwise `ERR_CONTACT_LOG_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). This is also what blocks a second review of a log already `VERIFIED`, `REJECTED` or `VOIDED`.
12. The observation cycle's `phase` permits the requested advance — `PRE_CONFERENCE` to `OBSERVED`, `OBSERVED` to `POST_CONFERENCE`, `POST_CONFERENCE` to `DOCUMENTED`, or any non-terminal phase to `ABANDONED` — otherwise `ERR_OBSERVATION_PHASE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). It guards the phase dimension on advance, feedback authoring and feedback review alike.
13. The cycle's `feedback_review_status` permits the requested action — `NONE`, `DRAFT` or `RETURNED` for feedback authoring; `SUBMITTED` for a return or an acceptance; `ACCEPTED` for the advance from `POST_CONFERENCE` to `DOCUMENTED` — otherwise `ERR_FEEDBACK_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). An accepted review cannot be re-returned, and a draft cannot be accepted.
14. The attainment row's `status` permits the requested decision — `IN_PROGRESS` for an attestation, `ATTAINED` for a revocation, `REVOKED` for a reopening — otherwise `ERR_ATTAINMENT_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). A `LOCKED` row rejects every decision.
15. The disbursement's `status` permits the requested transition — `DRAFT` to `APPROVED`, `APPROVED` to `PAID`, `PAID` to `CLAWED_BACK` — otherwise `ERR_DISBURSEMENT_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). This blocks paying a draft, approving a paid row, and clawing back something never paid.
16. The packet's `packet_status` permits the requested action — `ASSEMBLING` for submission and withdrawal, `RETURNED` for reopening, `SUBMITTED_TO_STATE` for a state acceptance or return — otherwise `ERR_PACKET_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). An `ACCEPTED` packet is terminal and rejects everything.
17. The mentor's `certification_status` permits the requested certification action — `UNCERTIFIED` or `LAPSED` for a certify action, `CERTIFIED` for recertify and suspend, `SUSPENDED` for reinstate, any non-`RETIRED` status for retire — otherwise `ERR_MENTOR_CERT_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). The same rule refuses training credits posted to a `RETIRED` mentor.
18. The novice's `program_status` permits the operation — `ENROLLED` or `MATCHED` for match proposal; `ACTIVE` for observation-cycle creation and for the explicit creation of a milestone attainment row (the attest, revoke and reopen decisions on an attainment row that already exists do not re-check `program_status`); `ACTIVE` or `COMPLETION_PENDING` for packet creation and for withdrawal — otherwise `ERR_NOVICE_PROGRAM_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). `WITHDRAWN` and `COMPLETED` novices are terminal for every program operation. Packet creation admits `COMPLETION_PENDING` on purpose, so that a novice already mid-packet is refused by rule 41 below and the two refusals stay distinguishable.
19. The seminar session is `SCHEDULED`, which is required both to mark it held or cancelled and to record attendance against it — otherwise `ERR_SESSION_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409).
20. The cohort is in the status the operation needs — `FORMING` or `RUNNING` for novice enrolment and seminar scheduling, `CLOSED` for submitting a packet to the state — otherwise `ERR_COHORT_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 422). The rule is deliberately two-sided: the same field blocks early operations once the cohort is closed and blocks the terminal operation while it is not.
21. The supplied parent version matches the parent's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_PARENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412), per §2.6.
22. Separation of duties holds — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403), per §2.9. This sits above every referential and fiscal rule, so attesting your own evidence against an unattained prerequisite reports the duty conflict, not the prerequisite gap.
23. The mentor is `CERTIFIED` and `certification_expires_on` is strictly after the current logical time — otherwise `ERR_MENTOR_NOT_CERTIFIED` (HTTP 422). Guards match activation, observation-cycle creation and contact-log creation. An expired certificate is treated exactly like a lapsed one even before the sweep has recorded the lapse. Ranked above the temporal rules, so an uncertified mentor booking an overlapping window hears about the certification first.
24. The proposed observation window clears the buffered mentor conflict check — otherwise `ERR_OBSERVATION_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409), per §2.11.
25. The proposed seminar window clears the cohort overlap check — otherwise `ERR_SEMINAR_SESSION_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409), per §2.11.
26. The cohort's weekly seminar minute budget is not exceeded — otherwise `ERR_COHORT_WEEKLY_SEMINAR_CAP_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422), per §2.11.
27. Each interval is well formed — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW` (HTTP 422), per §2.11. Applies to observation-cycle creation, seminar-session creation and disbursement drafting.
28. A stipend period's `period_end` is strictly before the current logical time — otherwise `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_CLOSED` (HTTP 422).
29. The mentor's site equals the novice's site — otherwise `ERR_SITE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422) on match proposal.
30. The novice on an attendance row belongs to the cohort owning the session — otherwise `ERR_COHORT_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422).
31. A named prerequisite milestone is already `ATTAINED` for this novice — otherwise `ERR_PREREQUISITE_NOT_ATTAINED` (HTTP 422) on an attestation or a reopening.
32. A new milestone's prerequisite has a strictly smaller `sequence_index` than the milestone itself — otherwise `ERR_MILESTONE_CYCLE` (HTTP 422).
33. The novice's `license_advancement_status` is `RECOMMENDED` — otherwise `ERR_LICENSE_RECOMMENDATION_MISSING` (HTTP 422) on packet submission.
34. The novice's attained-milestone count and computed base score both clear the submission gate — otherwise `ERR_MILESTONE_EVIDENCE_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 422), per §3.9.
35. A contact log's `contact_date` falls inside the match's active life and is not in the future — otherwise `ERR_CONTACT_DATE_OUT_OF_WINDOW` (HTTP 422).
36. No live match already exists for this exact mentor-and-novice pair — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTIVE_MATCH` (HTTP 409).
37. The novice holds no live match against any other mentor — otherwise `ERR_NOVICE_ALREADY_MATCHED` (HTTP 409).
38. The mentor's caseload has room — otherwise `ERR_CASELOAD_CAP_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422) at activation.
39. The site's match slots have room — otherwise `ERR_SITE_SLOT_CAP_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422) at activation.
40. The uniqueness family: no duplicate observation `cycle_index` on the match (`ERR_DUPLICATE_CYCLE_INDEX`), no duplicate `session_index` in the cohort (`ERR_DUPLICATE_SESSION_INDEX`), no duplicate attendance row for the same session and novice (`ERR_DUPLICATE_ATTENDANCE`), no duplicate `site_code` (`ERR_DUPLICATE_SITE_CODE`), no duplicate milestone `code` or `sequence_index` (`ERR_DUPLICATE_MILESTONE_CODE`), no `APPROVED` or `PAID` disbursement for the same mentor and `period_code` (`ERR_DUPLICATE_DISBURSEMENT_PERIOD`), and no duplicate attainment row for the same novice and milestone (`ERR_DUPLICATE_ATTAINMENT`) — all HTTP 409.
41. The novice holds no packet in `ASSEMBLING`, `SUBMITTED_TO_STATE` or `ACCEPTED` — otherwise `ERR_ACTIVE_PACKET_EXISTS` (HTTP 409).
42. Feedback documentation is complete — otherwise `ERR_FEEDBACK_INCOMPLETE` (HTTP 422), enforced both at feedback submission and again at the advance into `DOCUMENTED`.
43. Same-day credited-minute room remains on the match — otherwise `ERR_DAILY_CREDIT_CAP_REACHED` (HTTP 422), per §3.4.
44. The stipend period contains at least one eligible log — otherwise `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_ACTIVITY` (HTTP 422).
45. A negative adjustment does not exceed the gross — otherwise `ERR_ADJUSTMENT_EXCEEDS_GROSS` (HTTP 422).
46. The net stipend is strictly positive — otherwise `ERR_NON_POSITIVE_NET_STIPEND` (HTTP 422). Because this is evaluated after rule 45, an adjustment exactly equal to the negated gross reports the non-positive net rather than the excess.
47. On the automatic-selection path, at least one mentor satisfies every eligibility predicate — otherwise `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_MENTOR` (HTTP 422). This is reachable only when no mentor was named explicitly; an explicitly named mentor failing the same predicates reports that predicate's own error instead.
48. The novice's live counters meet every requirement of the milestone being attested — otherwise `ERR_MILESTONE_REQUIREMENTS_NOT_MET` (HTTP 422). Evaluated after rule 31, so a novice who has the counters but not the chain hears about the chain.
49. The mentor's current training cycle satisfies the hours and modules required for the requested certification action — otherwise `ERR_TRAINING_REQUIREMENTS_NOT_MET` (HTTP 422).
50. A disputed contact log has been resubmitted fewer than twice — otherwise `ERR_RESUBMIT_LIMIT_REACHED` (HTTP 409).
51. An observation cycle's feedback has been returned fewer than three times — otherwise `ERR_FEEDBACK_RETURN_LIMIT_REACHED` (HTTP 409).
52. An attainment row has been revoked fewer than three times — otherwise `ERR_REVOCATION_LIMIT_REACHED` (HTTP 409).
53. A packet has been reopened fewer than twice — otherwise `ERR_PACKET_RESUBMISSION_LIMIT_REACHED` (HTTP 409).
54. `limit` and `offset` are in range on a list read — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400).

### 2.8. Cascade Discipline and Deterministic Ordering

Several operations in this system reach well beyond the row they address. Cascades are always applied immediately and synchronously within the triggering request — there is no deferred reconciliation — and they are always applied in a fixed order so that capacity checks inside a cascade are reproducible.

The ordering rules are: sets of matches are processed in `created_at` ascending order; sets of observation cycles belonging to one match are processed in `cycle_index` ascending order; sets of milestone attainment rows are processed in the ascending `sequence_index` of their milestone definitions; sets of novices affected by a seminar session are processed in the `created_at` ascending order of their attendance rows; and mentors swept for certification lapse are processed in `created_at` ascending order.

Two cascades are conditional rather than unconditional, and the difference matters. When a mentor's certification returns to `CERTIFIED`, each of that mentor's `SUSPENDED` matches is *offered* reactivation in turn and only takes it if its own preconditions still hold; a match whose reactivation would breach the mentor's caseload cap or the site's slot cap, or whose site has since closed, or whose novice is no longer in an eligible program state, simply stays `SUSPENDED`. Because the offers are processed in order and each successful reactivation consumes a slot, an earlier match can legitimately exhaust the headroom that a later one needed. Likewise, when verified contact minutes are withdrawn, only those attainment rows whose milestone requirements the reduced counters no longer satisfy are revoked; rows still comfortably above their thresholds are left alone.

### 2.9. Separation of Evidence Duties

Four operations in this system are approvals of somebody else's work, and in each of them the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the user recorded as having produced the evidence being judged. A contact log's `verified_by_user_id` may not equal its `submitted_by_user_id`. A milestone attestation's `attested_by_user_id` may not equal the `verified_by_user_id` of any verified contact log on any match of that novice. A disbursement's `approved_by_user_id` may not equal its `prepared_by_user_id`. And a packet's `decided_by_user_id` may not equal its `submitted_by_user_id`. Any collision is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

This is a safety rule, not a role rule: the caller holds a permitted role and owns the resource, and is disqualified purely by having acted earlier in the same evidence chain.

### 2.10. Rounding, Precision and Money Discipline

Money is held in integer cents; there are no fractional cents anywhere in the model. Contact minutes and seminar minutes are held as whole integers. Scores, ratios and training hours are decimal, and every rounding in this system is **round half up** — a value exactly on the halfway mark rounds away from zero.

The precision conventions by quantity are: readiness indices, completion scores, composite rubric scores and training hours are carried to 2 decimal places; a seminar attendance credit ratio is carried to 4 decimal places; credited contact minutes are whole minutes obtained by truncating toward zero (that is, flooring) the weighted product *before* any ceiling is applied; and stipend amounts are whole cents rounded per contact log before the rounded per-log amounts are summed. That last point is a deliberate, load-bearing choice and §3.8 spells it out: summing unrounded per-log products and rounding once at the end is *not* the rule and yields a different total.

Where a threshold comparison is made against a rounded value, the comparison reads the rounded value, not the underlying one. The seminar credit decision compares the rounded 4-decimal ratio against its threshold; the completion bonus tier reads the rounded base score.

### 2.11. Scheduling Window Conventions

Three temporal shapes recur, and they deliberately do not share a convention.

**Well-formedness.** An observation window's `window_end` must be strictly after its `window_start`, and a seminar session's `scheduled_end` must be strictly after its `scheduled_start`. For both of those, the duration must be at least 30 minutes and at most 240 minutes, inclusive at both ends. A stipend period is looser: `period_end` need only be strictly after `period_start`, with no duration bound at all. Violations of any of these produce `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW` (HTTP 422). This check is separated from ordinary field validation precisely because it compares two fields against each other rather than validating one in isolation.

**Observation cycles: closed intervals, padded by a travel buffer.** A proposed observation window conflicts with any other observation cycle *of the same mentor* whose `phase` is `PRE_CONFERENCE`, `OBSERVED` or `POST_CONFERENCE`. The comparison treats both intervals as closed and expands them by a 30-minute buffer on each side: a conflict exists when the proposed start is at or before the other window's end plus 30 minutes and, simultaneously, the other window's start is at or before the proposed end plus 30 minutes. Two windows that merely touch therefore conflict, and so do two windows exactly 30 minutes apart; a gap of 31 minutes is accepted. Cycles already in `DOCUMENTED` or `ABANDONED` never block a new booking. The refusal is `ERR_OBSERVATION_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409).

**Seminar sessions: half-open intervals, no buffer.** A proposed seminar window conflicts with any other session *of the same cohort* whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `HELD`, where a conflict exists when the proposed start is strictly before the other session's end and the other session's start is strictly before the proposed end. This is the opposite convention to the observation rule: back-to-back sessions, where one ends at the exact instant the next begins, are permitted here and forbidden there. The refusal is `ERR_SEMINAR_SESSION_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409).

**Weekly seminar load.** Layered on top of the pairwise seminar check is an aggregate one. For the ISO week containing the proposed session's start — Monday at 00:00:00 UTC up to but not including the following Monday at 00:00:00 UTC — the system sums the `scheduled_minutes` of every `SCHEDULED` or `HELD` session of that cohort whose `scheduled_start` falls in that week, adds the proposed session's own minutes, and refuses the booking if the total exceeds four hours. Every individual session may be legally sized and non-overlapping and the collective weekly load still be refused. A total of exactly 240 minutes is accepted; 241 is not. The refusal is `ERR_COHORT_WEEKLY_SEMINAR_CAP_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). Cancelling a session releases its minutes back into that week's budget.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Program Foundations: Sites, Cohorts and Novice Enrolment

The induction program rests on two configuration entities that a `ProgramAdmin` establishes before any mentoring happens. A **school site** is registered with a display `name` of 1 to 120 characters, a `site_code` of 3 to 10 uppercase alphanumeric characters that must be unique across the district, and a `region_code` naming one of the four district regions `NORTH`, `SOUTH`, `EAST` or `WEST`. A site begins life with `status` `OPEN` and an `active_match_count` of 0. It also carries a `mentor_slot_cap`, the ceiling on how many mentoring relationships may be simultaneously live across all mentors of that site; the cap defaults to 6 when the request omits it and must be at least 1 and no more than 40. A site whose `site_code` duplicates an existing one is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_SITE_CODE`.

Closing a site is a one-way move from `OPEN` to `CLOSED` and requires a `reason`. The closure stamps `closed_at` from the logical clock — the field is null for as long as the site is `OPEN` — and, importantly, leaves the site's existing `ACTIVE` mentoring relationships untouched. What closure prevents is *new* attachment: registering a mentor, enrolling a novice or proposing a match against a `CLOSED` site is refused with `ERR_SITE_CLOSED`.

A **cohort** is the seminar group. It is created with a `name`, a `program_year` between 2020 and 2100 inclusive, and a `region_code` from the same four-region set. Cohorts are region-scoped: the `region_code` declares which part of the district the cohort serves, and enrolment is intended for novices teaching at sites in that region. A cohort also fixes `seminar_sessions_required`, the number of seminar sessions a novice of that cohort must have credited before a completion packet may be submitted to the state; it defaults to 6 and must be between 1 and 24 inclusive. Cohorts open in `FORMING` with `enrolled_novice_count` at 0 and `closed_at` null.

The cohort's own lifecycle has three states and only two moving parts. The step from `FORMING` to `RUNNING` is not requested by anyone: it happens automatically when the first seminar session of that cohort is marked held, and only while the cohort is still `FORMING`. The step to `CLOSED` is requested explicitly by a `ProgramAdmin` supplying a `target_status`, and is available from either `FORMING` or `RUNNING` — a cohort that never ran can still be closed. What it requires is that no seminar session of that cohort remains `SCHEDULED`; every session must first be held or cancelled, or the closure is refused with `ERR_COHORT_STATE_INVALID`. Closing stamps `closed_at` and bumps the cohort's version. A `CLOSED` cohort is terminal, and it is also the gate for the very last step of the whole program: a completion packet cannot be sent to the state until the novice's cohort has closed. Conversely, enrolling a novice into a `CLOSED` cohort or scheduling a seminar for one is refused with the same `ERR_COHORT_STATE_INVALID` code — the field is checked in both directions.

Enrolling a **novice teacher** requires the `site_id` of the school where they teach, the `cohort_id` of their seminar cohort, a `full_name`, a `subject_area` drawn from `MATH`, `SCIENCE`, `ELA`, `SOCIAL_STUDIES`, `SPED`, `WORLD_LANGUAGE` and `CTE`, and an `induction_year` of either 1 or 2 marking which year of the two-year program they are in. Enrolment initialises `license_stage` to `PRELIMINARY`, `program_status` to `ENROLLED`, `license_advancement_status` to `NOT_ELIGIBLE`, and `retention_outcome` to `UNKNOWN`, with `retention_observed_on` and `withdrawn_reason` both null and every progress counter — `verified_contact_minutes`, `documented_observation_cycles`, `credited_seminar_sessions`, `attained_milestone_count`, `readiness_index_live` and `readiness_index_snapshot` — at zero. The cohort's `enrolled_novice_count`, which counts every novice of that cohort whose `program_status` is anything other than `WITHDRAWN`, increments.

Enrolment also seeds the novice's milestone ledger. One attainment row is created for every milestone definition currently in the catalogue, processed in ascending `sequence_index`: a milestone with no prerequisite yields a row in `IN_PROGRESS`, and a milestone that names a prerequisite yields a row in `LOCKED`.

A novice's participation is tracked by `program_status`, one of two entirely independent status dimensions on the record (the other, `license_advancement_status`, is the subject of §3.7). None of the `program_status` moves are requested directly except withdrawal; each of the others rides along with an operation on some other entity. Proposing a match advances `ENROLLED` to `MATCHED`. Rejecting that proposal moves `MATCHED` back to `ENROLLED`, but only once the rejection leaves the novice with no match at all in `PROPOSED`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` — this is what closes the `ENROLLED → MATCHED → ENROLLED` revision cycle. Activating a match advances `MATCHED` to `ACTIVE`. Creating a completion packet advances `ACTIVE` to `COMPLETION_PENDING`, and a state acceptance advances `COMPLETION_PENDING` to `COMPLETED`, which requires the novice's `license_advancement_status` to be `RECOMMENDED` at that moment. A state return or a district withdrawal of the packet drops `COMPLETION_PENDING` back to `ACTIVE`, and reopening the packet raises it again — the `ACTIVE → COMPLETION_PENDING → ACTIVE` cycle. Each of these moves increments the novice's `version`.

Withdrawal is the exception: a `ProgramAdmin` withdraws a novice explicitly, supplying a `reason` that is stored as `withdrawn_reason`, and the move is permitted from `ENROLLED`, `MATCHED`, `ACTIVE` or `COMPLETION_PENDING`. Withdrawal from `ENROLLED` is a quiet affair — there is nothing attached yet — while withdrawal from any of the other three states unwinds the novice's whole live footprint in one request, and identically in each case. Every match of that novice sitting in `PROPOSED` becomes `REJECTED` with an `end_reason` of `NOVICE_WITHDRAWN`; every match in `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` becomes `ENDED` with the same `end_reason`; matches are processed in `created_at` ascending order, and each match that was `ACTIVE` releases its slot by decrementing both the mentor's `active_caseload_count` and the site's `active_match_count`. Every observation cycle on those matches whose `phase` is `PRE_CONFERENCE`, `OBSERVED` or `POST_CONFERENCE` becomes `ABANDONED`. Every completion packet of that novice in `ASSEMBLING`, and likewise one sitting in `RETURNED`, becomes `WITHDRAWN` with a `withdrawn_reason` of `NOVICE_WITHDRAWN`. The novice's `license_advancement_status` returns to `NOT_ELIGIBLE`. `WITHDRAWN` is terminal, as is `COMPLETED`; any subsequent program operation on such a novice is refused with `ERR_NOVICE_PROGRAM_STATE_INVALID`.

One year after induction the district records what actually happened to the teacher. A `ProgramAdmin` supplies a `retention_outcome` of `RETAINED_SAME_SITE`, `RETAINED_DISTRICT` or `LEFT_DISTRICT` (the field starts at `UNKNOWN`) together with the `observed_on` instant, which is stored as `retention_observed_on`. Recording an outcome re-scores any packet of that novice still in `ASSEMBLING`, because the retention result feeds the completion score's bonus tier as described in §3.9.

### 3.2. Mentor Onboarding, Training Hours and Certification Cycles

A **mentor** is a veteran teacher registered against a `site_id` with a `full_name` and a non-empty `subject_endorsements` array whose members are drawn from `MATH`, `SCIENCE`, `ELA`, `SOCIAL_STUDIES`, `SPED`, `WORLD_LANGUAGE` and `CTE`, with no duplicates. The mentor's own capacity is set by `caseload_cap`, the maximum number of simultaneously `ACTIVE` mentoring relationships they may hold; it defaults to 2 when omitted and must be between 1 and 4 inclusive. Registration initialises `certification_status` to `UNCERTIFIED`, `certification_expires_on` and `suspension_reason` to null, and every counter — `active_caseload_count`, `training_hours_current_cycle`, `stipend_accrued_cents`, `stipend_paid_cents`, `stipend_clawed_back_cents` and `verified_minutes_lifetime` — to zero, with `training_modules_current_cycle` an empty array. `active_caseload_count` always reflects the number of that mentor's matches currently in `match_status` `ACTIVE`.

Before a mentor may take on anyone, they must be certified, and certification is earned by accumulating training. A `ProgramAdmin` posts a training credit consisting of a `module_code` from `FOUNDATIONS`, `OBSERVATION_PROTOCOL`, `EQUITY` and `RECERT_REFRESH`, a number of `hours`, and the `completed_on` date the training was finished. Each posted credit is rounded half up to 2 decimal places before it is added to the running `training_hours_current_cycle`, and the module code is added to `training_modules_current_cycle` without duplication, so posting the same module twice adds hours but leaves the module list unchanged. Credits cannot be posted to a `RETIRED` mentor; that attempt is refused with `ERR_MENTOR_CERT_STATE_INVALID`.

The hour ledger is *cycle-scoped*, and this is the subtlety implementers most often miss. A successful certification resets `training_hours_current_cycle` to 0 and `training_modules_current_cycle` to an empty array, so hours banked before a reset never count toward the next cycle. What counts is only what was posted strictly after the most recent reset.

Certification actions are all requested through a single mentor certification operation carrying an `action` and, where the action demands a justification, a `reason`. There are five actions.

**Certify** takes a mentor from `UNCERTIFIED` to `CERTIFIED`. It requires that the mentor currently be `UNCERTIFIED`, that `training_hours_current_cycle` be at least 18.00 hours, and that the current cycle's module list contain all three of `FOUNDATIONS`, `OBSERVATION_PROTOCOL` and `EQUITY` — that is, all three initial modules must be present, though additional modules do no harm. Success sets `certification_expires_on` to the current logical time plus 365 days, resets the hour ledger and module list, clears `suspension_reason` to null, and bumps the mentor's `version`.

**Recertify** takes a `LAPSED` mentor back to `CERTIFIED` on a lighter requirement: at least 6.00 hours in the current cycle and the presence of `RECERT_REFRESH` among the current cycle's modules. Success again pushes `certification_expires_on` to 365 days beyond the current logical time, resets the ledger and the module list, and bumps `version`. Recertification also runs the reactivation cascade described below, and it is what closes the `CERTIFIED → LAPSED → CERTIFIED` cycle. Whichever action is attempted, a shortfall in either hours or modules for the applicable requirement is refused with `ERR_TRAINING_REQUIREMENTS_NOT_MET`.

**Suspend** moves a `CERTIFIED` mentor to `SUSPENDED` and requires a `reason`, which is stored as `suspension_reason`. **Reinstate** moves a `SUSPENDED` mentor back to `CERTIFIED`, and requires that the current logical time still be at or before `certification_expires_on` — a suspension cannot be used to outlive an expiry. Reinstatement clears `suspension_reason` and runs the reactivation cascade.

**Retire** is terminal and is available from `UNCERTIFIED`, `CERTIFIED`, `LAPSED` or `SUSPENDED` with identical preconditions and identical effects in every case. It requires that the mentor hold no match in `PROPOSED` or `ACTIVE`. Retirement sets `certification_expires_on` to null and ends every one of the mentor's `SUSPENDED` matches — each becoming `ENDED` with an `end_reason` of `MENTOR_RETIRED`, processed in `created_at` ascending order — and bumps the mentor's `version`. Any certification action that is not a declared move out of the mentor's current status, including any action at all on a `RETIRED` mentor, is refused with `ERR_MENTOR_CERT_STATE_INVALID`.

Expiry is not an action but a consequence of time passing. When the logical clock is advanced past a mentor's `certification_expires_on`, the sweep described in §2.5 moves that mentor to `LAPSED`. Two shapes exist. A mentor leaving `CERTIFIED` carries a cascade: every match of that mentor in `match_status` `ACTIVE` transitions `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`, processed in `created_at` ascending order, and the mentor's `version` increments. A mentor leaving `SUSPENDED` for `LAPSED` carries no match cascade at all, for the simple reason that every match of a suspended mentor is already suspended or already terminal; only the mentor's `version` increments. A deliberate suspension behaves exactly like a lapse in this respect: it too suspends every `ACTIVE` match of the mentor in `created_at` ascending order.

Suspending a match is not a bookkeeping no-op. It stamps the match's `suspended_at` from the logical clock, decrements both the mentor's `active_caseload_count` and the site's `active_match_count` — a `SUSPENDED` match does not occupy a slot — abandons every observation cycle on that match whose `phase` is `PRE_CONFERENCE`, `OBSERVED` or `POST_CONFERENCE` (processed in ascending `cycle_index`), and bumps the versions of the match, the mentor and the site.

The reverse move is where the conditional cascade lives. When a mentor becomes `CERTIFIED` again through either recertification or reinstatement, each of that mentor's `SUSPENDED` matches is considered in `created_at` ascending order and transitions `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` only if, at the moment it is considered, the mentor's site is `OPEN`, the novice's `program_status` is `MATCHED` or `ACTIVE`, the mentor's `active_caseload_count` is strictly below `caseload_cap`, and the site's `active_match_count` is strictly below `mentor_slot_cap`. A match that reactivates has its `suspended_at` cleared back to null, increments the mentor's `active_caseload_count` and the site's `active_match_count`, and bumps the versions of the match, the mentor and the site — which is precisely why a mentor with a `caseload_cap` of 2 and three suspended matches gets exactly two of them back, the two oldest by creation time. Matches whose preconditions fail simply remain `SUSPENDED`; nothing is reported as an error. This closes the `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` cycle.

### 3.3. Matching Mentors to Novices

A mentoring relationship is a junction record linking one mentor to one novice, and it is where most of the program's graph constraints live. A `ProgramAdmin` or a `SiteInductionLead` proposes one by naming the `novice_id` and supplying `expected_mentor_version` per §2.6. The `mentor_id` is optional: when the request names it, the match records `selection_mode` `EXPLICIT`; when the request omits it, the district's matching policy chooses the mentor and the match records `selection_mode` `AUTO_SELECTED`.

**The matching policy.** Automatic selection first builds a candidate set, admitting only mentors for whom *every* one of these holds: `certification_status` is `CERTIFIED`; the mentor's site is the novice's site; `active_caseload_count` is strictly below `caseload_cap`; that site's `status` is `OPEN`; and no match already exists between that mentor and this novice in `PROPOSED`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`. If the candidate set is empty the proposal is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_MENTOR`. Otherwise the candidates are ranked, and the single best one wins, by four keys applied in order: first, subject affinity, best first — a mentor whose endorsements include the novice's `subject_area` outranks one merely holding an adjacent endorsement, which in turn outranks one holding neither; second, the lighter `active_caseload_count` first; third, the later `certification_expires_on` first, so freshly certified mentors are preferred; and fourth, as the final tie-break, the earlier `created_at` first. The mentor's `id` is never consulted as a tie-break — if two mentors tie on all four keys the earlier-created row wins by definition.

**Subject affinity.** The same affinity notion is recorded on every created match as `subject_match_tier`. When the novice's `subject_area` is one of the mentor's `subject_endorsements`, the tier is `EXACT`. Otherwise, if the novice's subject appears in the adjacency set of any endorsement the mentor holds, the tier is `ADJACENT`. Failing both, the tier is `CROSS`. The adjacency sets are fixed district policy: `MATH` is adjacent to `SCIENCE` and `CTE`; `SCIENCE` to `MATH` and `CTE`; `ELA` to `SOCIAL_STUDIES` and `WORLD_LANGUAGE`; `SOCIAL_STUDIES` to `ELA` and `WORLD_LANGUAGE`; `SPED` to `ELA` and `MATH`; `WORLD_LANGUAGE` to `ELA` and `SOCIAL_STUDIES`; and `CTE` to `MATH` and `SCIENCE`. A mentor holding several endorsements is adjacent to the union of their adjacency sets. Alongside the tier, the match records `site_match`, true exactly when the mentor's site and the novice's site are the same at creation time.

Proposal is guarded by a cluster of graph rules. The mentor's site must equal the novice's site, or the proposal is refused with `ERR_SITE_MISMATCH` — cross-site pairings are refused even when the subject affinity would be `EXACT`, which is why `site_match` is always true on a match that actually exists. No match may already exist for the same mentor-and-novice pair in `PROPOSED`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, or the refusal is `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTIVE_MATCH`; pairs whose earlier match reached `ENDED` or `REJECTED` may be re-proposed, so the uniqueness scope is state-qualified rather than absolute. And the novice must hold no live match against *any other* mentor, or the refusal is `ERR_NOVICE_ALREADY_MATCHED` — a novice carries at most one live mentoring relationship at a time, and the previous one must reach `ENDED` or `REJECTED` first.

A newly created match sits in `match_status` `PROPOSED` with `verified_minutes_total` and `accrued_stipend_cents` at zero, `activated_at`, `suspended_at`, `ended_at` and `end_reason` all null, and — this catches people out — `pinned_stipend_rate_cents_per_hour` still null. The rate is not pinned at proposal; it is pinned only when the match is activated. Creation increments `Mentor.version` and, through the bubble-up described in §3.1, moves the novice from `ENROLLED` to `MATCHED`.

**Disposition.** A `ProgramAdmin` or `SiteInductionLead` disposes of a match by supplying a `decision` of `ACTIVATE`, `REJECT`, `SUSPEND`, `RESUME` or `END`, with a `reason` where the decision requires one. A decision whose target state the match's current `match_status` does not permit is refused with `ERR_MATCH_STATE_INVALID`: activation and rejection require `PROPOSED`, ending requires `PROPOSED`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, and resuming requires `SUSPENDED`.

Activation is the heaviest of the five. It requires the match to be `PROPOSED`, the mentor to be `CERTIFIED` with a `certification_expires_on` strictly after the current logical time (otherwise `ERR_MENTOR_NOT_CERTIFIED`), the mentor's site to be `OPEN`, the mentor's `active_caseload_count` to be strictly below `caseload_cap` (otherwise `ERR_CASELOAD_CAP_EXCEEDED` — a count exactly equal to the cap fails), the site's `active_match_count` to be strictly below `mentor_slot_cap` (otherwise `ERR_SITE_SLOT_CAP_EXCEEDED`), and the novice's `program_status` to be `MATCHED`. Both caps are re-checked here and not merely at proposal, because another activation may have filled the mentor or the site in the meantime. On success the match stamps `activated_at` from the logical clock, pins `pinned_stipend_rate_cents_per_hour` from the tier — 4200 cents per hour for `EXACT`, 3600 for `ADJACENT`, 2800 for `CROSS` — increments the mentor's `active_caseload_count` and the site's `active_match_count` by one each, moves the novice from `MATCHED` to `ACTIVE`, and increments the versions of the match, the mentor, the site and the novice.

Rejection requires a `reason`, stamps `ended_at` from the logical clock and `end_reason` from that reason, and returns the novice from `MATCHED` to `ENROLLED` when no other match of that novice remains in `PROPOSED`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`. The versions of the match and the novice increment. `REJECTED` is terminal.

Ending a match also requires a `reason`, and it carries one precondition that is easy to miss: no contact log on that match may still be sitting in `SUBMITTED` or `DISPUTED`. Every log must be resolved — verified, rejected or voided — before the relationship can be closed, or the request is refused with `ERR_MATCH_STATE_INVALID`. Ending stamps `ended_at` and `end_reason`, and abandons every observation cycle on the match whose `phase` is `PRE_CONFERENCE`, `OBSERVED` or `POST_CONFERENCE`, processed in ascending `cycle_index`. Whether the slot bookkeeping moves depends on where the match came from: ending a match that was `ACTIVE` decrements the mentor's `active_caseload_count` and the site's `active_match_count` and bumps the versions of the match, the mentor and the site, whereas ending a match that was `SUSPENDED` leaves both counters untouched — a suspended match was not occupying a slot — and bumps only the match's own `version`. Ending a match returns the novice from `ACTIVE` to `MATCHED` when another of that novice's matches remains in `PROPOSED`, `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, and to `ENROLLED` when none does. `ENDED` is terminal. Ending is also the state the cascades reach for: retiring a mentor, withdrawing a novice, and the novice's completion all end matches with a system-supplied `end_reason` of `MENTOR_RETIRED`, `NOVICE_WITHDRAWN` and `PROGRAM_COMPLETED` respectively.

### 3.4. Contact Hours: Logging, Verification and the Daily Ceiling

Contact hours are the spine of the evidence file. A `Mentor` or a `SiteInductionLead` records a mentoring contact against a live relationship by supplying the `match_id`, the `contact_date` instant at which the contact occurred, the raw `minutes` claimed (at least 1 and at most 300), a `contact_type` of `ONE_ON_ONE`, `CO_PLANNING`, `MODEL_LESSON` or `DEBRIEF`, and a `description` narrating what was covered, which must be at least 20 and at most 1000 characters. The match must be `ACTIVE` and its mentor must hold a live certification, so a log submitted against a suspended relationship is refused with `ERR_MATCH_STATE_INVALID` and one submitted for an uncertified mentor with `ERR_MENTOR_NOT_CERTIFIED`.

The `contact_date` must fall inside the relationship's active life: on or after the calendar date of the match's `activated_at` and, once the match has ended, on or before the calendar date of its `ended_at`; it must also not be in the future relative to the logical clock. Otherwise the refusal is `ERR_CONTACT_DATE_OUT_OF_WINDOW`. Note that the comparison is made at date granularity against timestamp-granularity boundaries: a log dated the same calendar day as activation is accepted even when its clock time precedes the exact `activated_at` instant.

A new log enters `verification_status` `SUBMITTED` with `credited_minutes` and `stipend_cents` at zero, `resubmit_count` at zero, `rate_tier_at_verification`, `cert_multiplier_at_verification`, `verified_by_user_id`, `verified_at`, `dispute_reason`, `reject_reason` and `void_reason` all null, and `submitted_by_user_id` stamped from the acting `X-User-ID`. Submission also refreshes the mentor's provisional stipend accrual described in §3.8.

**Review.** A `ProgramAdmin` or `SiteInductionLead` reviews a submitted log with a `decision` and, where the decision demands an explanation, a `note`. A `SiteInductionLead` reviewing a log must be the lead of the novice's own site — its `X-User-ID` must equal the novice's `site_id`. The reviewer may never be the person who submitted the log: an acting `X-User-ID` equal to the log's `submitted_by_user_id` is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

Verifying a `SUBMITTED` log requires the parent match to be `ACTIVE` and requires that same-day credit room remain (see below). On success the log's `credited_minutes` is computed, `rate_tier_at_verification` is pinned from the parent match's `subject_match_tier`, `cert_multiplier_at_verification` is pinned to 1.00 when the mentor is `CERTIFIED` at that instant and to 0.50 otherwise, `stipend_cents` is computed from those pinned values, `verified_by_user_id` is stamped from `X-User-ID` and `verified_at` from the logical clock. The credited minutes then flow outward: the match's `verified_minutes_total` and the mentor's `verified_minutes_lifetime` and the novice's `verified_contact_minutes` are all recomputed, as are the match's `accrued_stipend_cents` and the mentor's `stipend_accrued_cents`, and both readiness figures of the novice — `readiness_index_live` and `readiness_index_snapshot` — are re-derived, which may in turn lift the novice's `license_advancement_status` from `NOT_ELIGIBLE` to `ELIGIBLE`. The versions of the log, the match, the mentor and the novice each increment by one.

**How credited minutes are computed.** Three inputs interact. First, the raw `minutes` are weighted by the contact type: a one-on-one session and a co-planning session both count at full value, a model lesson counts at one and one-quarter of its minutes, and a debrief counts at three-quarters. Second, the weighted product is truncated toward zero to a whole number of minutes — a 50-minute debrief weighted to 37.5 becomes 37, not 38. Third, and only then, a per-day ceiling is applied. The system sums the `credited_minutes` of every *other* log on the **same match** whose `contact_date` falls on the **same UTC calendar date** and whose `verification_status` is `VERIFIED`, iterating those logs in `created_at` ascending order; logs in `SUBMITTED`, `DISPUTED`, `REJECTED` or `VOIDED` contribute nothing to that sum. The remaining room for the day is three hours minus that already-credited sum, floored at zero. The log's `credited_minutes` is the smaller of the truncated weighted minutes and that remaining room.

The ceiling has a companion refusal. If the already-credited total for that match and date has reached the full three hours, so that no room at all remains, the verification is refused outright with `ERR_DAILY_CREDIT_CAP_REACHED` rather than producing a zero-minute credit. At exactly 180 already-credited minutes the next verification is refused; at 179 it succeeds and is truncated down to a single credited minute. Because this rule sits far below the match-state rule in the precedence ladder of §2.7, a verification against a `SUSPENDED` match whose day is also exhausted reports the match-state error, not the cap.

**Dispute, resubmission and rejection.** Instead of verifying, a reviewer may dispute a `SUBMITTED` log, supplying a `note` that is stored as `dispute_reason`; only the log's own `version` moves. A disputed log belongs to the mentor again: the `Mentor` whose `X-User-ID` equals the parent match's `mentor_id` may correct and resubmit it, supplying replacement values for `minutes`, `contact_type` and `description`. Resubmission returns the log to `SUBMITTED`, increments `resubmit_count`, clears `dispute_reason` back to null, restamps `submitted_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`, and refreshes the mentor's provisional accrual. The `SUBMITTED → DISPUTED → SUBMITTED` revision cycle is bounded: a log may be resubmitted at most twice, and a third attempt — that is, one made when `resubmit_count` already stands at 2 — is refused with `ERR_RESUBMIT_LIMIT_REACHED`. Alternatively a disputed log may be rejected outright, which requires a `note` stored as `reject_reason` and, for a `SiteInductionLead`, that its `X-User-ID` equal the novice's `site_id`. Rejection leaves `credited_minutes` and `stipend_cents` at zero; `REJECTED` is terminal. Both a dispute and a rejection refresh the mentor's `stipend_accrued_cents` downward.

**Voiding, and the four-hop unwind.** Only a `ProgramAdmin` may void an already-`VERIFIED` log, supplying a `void_reason`. The one thing that can block a void is cash already committed: if any disbursement for that match's mentor sits in `APPROVED` or `PAID` and its period contains the log's `verified_at` — that is, `period_start` is at or before `verified_at`, which is at or before `period_end` — the void is refused, because reversing a paid log would break the stipend ledger. Voiding is the head of the system's longest cascade, and all four hops run inside the one request.

*Hop one* resets the log's own `credited_minutes` and `stipend_cents` to zero while `void_reason` preserves the audit trail, and recomputes the match's `verified_minutes_total` and `accrued_stipend_cents`, the mentor's `verified_minutes_lifetime` and `stipend_accrued_cents`, the novice's `verified_contact_minutes`, and the novice's `readiness_index_live`. A readiness figure that falls back below the eligibility gate drops the novice's `license_advancement_status` from `ELIGIBLE` to `NOT_ELIGIBLE`.

*Hop two* reaches the milestone ledger. Because the novice's verified contact minutes have fallen, every attainment row of that novice in `ATTAINED` whose milestone's evidence requirements the reduced counters no longer satisfy transitions `ATTAINED → REVOKED` with a `revoke_reason` of `EVIDENCE_WITHDRAWN`, processed in ascending `sequence_index` of the milestone definitions. Rows still above their thresholds are untouched.

*Hop three* follows from any such revocation on a milestone that gates the licence recommendation: the novice's `license_advancement_status` falls from `RECOMMENDED` to `NOT_ELIGIBLE` and `license_stage` reverts to `PRELIMINARY`.

*Hop four* reaches the state. Every completion packet of that novice sitting in `SUBMITTED_TO_STATE` transitions to `RETURNED` with a `return_reason` of `EVIDENCE_REVOKED`; on this cascaded path `decided_at` and `decided_by_user_id` are left null, because no state officer decided anything. The versions of the log, the match, the mentor and the novice each increment, alongside those of every attainment row and packet the cascade touched. `VOIDED` is terminal, and voiding is the designated compensating action for a verification.

### 3.5. Observation Cycles and Feedback Documentation

A classroom observation is booked as a **cycle** against a live relationship, and it is the one entity in the model that carries two genuinely independent status dimensions: `phase`, tracking where the observation itself has got to, and `feedback_review_status`, tracking the written record. They move separately, they are guarded by separate rules, and they meet at exactly one point.

A `Mentor` or a `SiteInductionLead` opens a cycle with the `match_id`, a `cycle_index` between 1 and 8 that must be unique within that match (a repeat is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CYCLE_INDEX`), and the `window_start` and `window_end` bounding the classroom visit. The match must be `ACTIVE`, its mentor must hold a live certification, and the novice's `program_status` must be `ACTIVE`. The window must satisfy the well-formedness and buffered-conflict rules of §2.11: at least 30 and at most 240 minutes long, and clear of every other non-terminal observation cycle of the *same mentor* once both windows are padded by half an hour on each side. A new cycle opens with `phase` `PRE_CONFERENCE` and `feedback_review_status` `NONE`, `return_count` at zero, and `narrative`, `growth_area_codes`, `rubric_scores`, `composite_rubric_score`, `observed_at`, `post_conference_at`, `documented_at`, `abandoned_at` and `return_reason` all null.

**The phase dimension.** Phase moves are requested by supplying a `to_phase`, with a `reason` where one is warranted. Either the mentor on the match — the caller whose `X-User-ID` equals the match's `mentor_id` — or the `SiteInductionLead` of the novice's site drives all three forward steps, and each requires the match to still be `ACTIVE`. Advancing `PRE_CONFERENCE → OBSERVED` additionally requires the logical clock to have reached or passed `window_end`: an observation cannot be reported before the window it was booked for has finished. It stamps `observed_at`. Advancing `OBSERVED → POST_CONFERENCE` has no temporal condition and stamps `post_conference_at`.

Advancing `POST_CONFERENCE → DOCUMENTED` is the join point between the two dimensions. It requires that `feedback_review_status` already be `ACCEPTED`; an advance attempted while the feedback dimension sits at `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `RETURNED` is refused with `ERR_FEEDBACK_STATE_INVALID`. It also re-checks documentation completeness — `narrative` at least 120 characters, between 1 and 4 `growth_area_codes`, and all four `rubric_scores` keys present — so that a cycle whose feedback was accepted and then somehow emptied can never be counted as documented evidence; a failure here is `ERR_FEEDBACK_INCOMPLETE`. On success the cycle stamps `documented_at`, the novice's `documented_observation_cycles` and `readiness_index_live` are recomputed, that recomputation may lift `license_advancement_status` from `NOT_ELIGIBLE` to `ELIGIBLE`, and the versions of the cycle and the novice each increment. `DOCUMENTED` is terminal.

Abandonment is available from `PRE_CONFERENCE`, `OBSERVED` or `POST_CONFERENCE` — the three treatments are identical — and is also what the match cascades reach for when a relationship is suspended, ended, or dissolved by a novice's withdrawal. Abandoning stamps `abandoned_at`, and it carries one cross-dimension effect: when the cycle's `feedback_review_status` is `DRAFT` or `SUBMITTED` at that moment, the feedback dimension is driven to `RETURNED` with a `return_reason` of `CYCLE_ABANDONED` and `return_count` increments by one. The cycle's `version` increments. `ABANDONED` is terminal, and an advance requested out of either terminal phase — or any advance that is not one of the declared steps out of the current phase — is refused with `ERR_OBSERVATION_PHASE_INVALID`.

**The feedback dimension.** Only the `Mentor` on the match writes feedback, supplying `narrative`, `growth_area_codes`, `rubric_scores` and a boolean `submit`. The narrative must be between 120 and 4000 characters whenever the feedback is submitted or accepted. The `growth_area_codes` array carries between 1 and 4 members drawn from `PLANNING`, `INSTRUCTION`, `ENVIRONMENT`, `ASSESSMENT` and `EQUITY`. The `rubric_scores` object must carry all four keys — `planning`, `instruction`, `environment` and `assessment` — each an integer from 1 to 4 inclusive. Every write of the feedback package recomputes `composite_rubric_score` as the sum of those four rubric values divided by four, rounded half up to 2 decimal places, yielding a figure between 1.00 and 4.00.

With `submit` false and the dimension at `NONE`, the write parks a draft: the dimension becomes `DRAFT`, and the phase must be either `OBSERVED` or `POST_CONFERENCE`. With `submit` true the dimension becomes `SUBMITTED`, reachable from `NONE` (when no draft was ever parked), from `DRAFT`, or from `RETURNED`; all three of these require the phase to be exactly `POST_CONFERENCE` and enforce the completeness rules above, replacing the stored `narrative`, `growth_area_codes` and `rubric_scores` from the request body and recomputing `composite_rubric_score`. The move from `RETURNED` additionally clears `return_reason` back to null. A feedback write attempted from a dimension state that permits none of these — from `ACCEPTED`, for instance — is refused with `ERR_FEEDBACK_STATE_INVALID`, and completeness failures on a submission are refused with `ERR_FEEDBACK_INCOMPLETE`.

Review of submitted feedback belongs to the `SiteInductionLead` of the novice's site — its `X-User-ID` must equal that novice's `site_id` — or to a `ProgramAdmin`. The reviewer supplies a `decision` and, on a return, a `return_reason`. Returning requires the dimension to be `SUBMITTED` and `return_count` to be strictly below 3; it stores the `return_reason` and increments `return_count`, closing the `SUBMITTED → RETURNED → SUBMITTED` revision loop. A fourth return — attempted when `return_count` already stands at 3 — is refused with `ERR_FEEDBACK_RETURN_LIMIT_REACHED`; by then the feedback must either be accepted or the cycle abandoned. Accepting requires the dimension to be `SUBMITTED` and the phase to be `POST_CONFERENCE`; it clears `return_reason` to null and, crucially, **unlocks but does not perform** the phase advance into `DOCUMENTED`. The two dimensions stay independent: the phase only ever moves on an explicit advance request. `ACCEPTED` is terminal for the feedback dimension.

### 3.6. Cohort Seminars and Attendance Credit

Alongside one-to-one mentoring, novices owe their cohort a series of seminars. A `ProgramAdmin` schedules a **seminar session** with the `cohort_id`, a `session_index` between 1 and 24 that must be unique within the cohort (a repeat is `ERR_DUPLICATE_SESSION_INDEX`), a `topic` of 1 to 160 characters, and the `scheduled_start` and `scheduled_end` bounding it. The system derives `scheduled_minutes` as the whole number of minutes between those two instants, and that derived figure is what the cohort's weekly budget is measured in. Scheduling requires the cohort to be `FORMING` or `RUNNING`, and the window must clear the half-open cohort overlap rule and the four-hour ISO-week budget of §2.11. A new session begins `SCHEDULED` with `credited_attendance_count` at zero and `held_at`, `cancelled_at` and `cancel_reason` null.

A session is resolved by supplying a `status` of either `HELD` or `CANCELLED`, with `cancel_reason` required on the cancellation path. Marking a session `HELD` requires it to be `SCHEDULED`, requires the logical clock to have reached or passed `scheduled_end`, and requires the cohort to be `FORMING` or `RUNNING`. It stamps `held_at`; it drags the cohort from `FORMING` to `RUNNING` if the cohort is still forming; and it then recomputes `credited_seminar_sessions` and `readiness_index_live` for every novice holding an attendance row against that session, processing those novices in the `created_at` ascending order of their attendance rows, which may lift any of them from `NOT_ELIGIBLE` to `ELIGIBLE`. The versions of the session, the cohort and each affected novice increment.

Cancelling requires the session to be `SCHEDULED` and stores `cancelled_at` and `cancel_reason`. Because credited attendance only ever counts against a session that was actually held, cancellation recomputes that session's `credited_attendance_count` to zero and recomputes `credited_seminar_sessions` and `readiness_index_live` for every novice with an attendance row on it. Cancellation also releases the session's `scheduled_minutes` back into its ISO week's budget, so a cancelled slot can be rebooked. The versions of the session and of each affected novice increment. Both `HELD` and `CANCELLED` are terminal; any further attempt to hold, cancel or attend the session is refused with `ERR_SESSION_STATE_INVALID`.

**Attendance.** A `ProgramAdmin` or `SiteInductionLead` records one novice's attendance at one session by supplying the `session_id`, the `novice_id`, an `attendance_status` of `PRESENT`, `LATE`, `ABSENT` or `EXCUSED`, and the raw `minutes_attended` (at least 0 and at most 480). The session must still be `SCHEDULED`; attendance is taken on the roster before the session is closed out. The novice must belong to the cohort that owns the session, or the row is refused with `ERR_COHORT_MISMATCH`. Only one row may exist per session-and-novice pair, and a second is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ATTENDANCE`. The row stamps `recorded_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`.

Every attendance row is scored, whatever its status. A late arrival forfeits a flat 10 minutes: the effective minutes are the raw `minutes_attended` less that penalty for a `LATE` row and the raw minutes themselves otherwise, floored at zero so a novice who arrived 5 minutes into a session and is marked late does not go negative. The `credit_ratio` is then the effective minutes divided by the session's `scheduled_minutes`, rounded half up to 4 decimal places. The row's `credited` flag is true only when both conditions hold: `attendance_status` is `PRESENT` or `LATE`, and the rounded `credit_ratio` is at least three-quarters. A ratio of exactly 0.75 therefore earns credit. `ABSENT` and `EXCUSED` rows still receive a computed `credit_ratio` but are never `credited`.

A credited row raises the session's `credited_attendance_count`, which counts every credited attendance row against that session, and it feeds the novice's `credited_seminar_sessions`, which counts that novice's credited rows whose referenced session has reached `HELD`. Both are recomputed whenever an attendance row is recorded, so a credited row against a session still `SCHEDULED` shows up in the session's count immediately but only lifts the novice's `credited_seminar_sessions` once the session is marked held. Recording attendance also recomputes the novice's readiness, per §3.7.

The `EXCUSED` status deserves its own note, because it is not merely a zero. An excused absence *shrinks the seminar denominator* rather than counting as a miss — the novice is not penalised for a seminar the district agreed they need not attend. §3.7 makes the arithmetic explicit.

### 3.7. Readiness, Milestones and License Advancement

**Milestone definitions are configuration, not activity.** A `ProgramAdmin` defines a milestone with a globally unique `code` (uppercase snake case, 3 to 40 characters), a `title` of 1 to 160 characters, a globally unique `sequence_index` between 1 and 20, the three evidence thresholds `required_contact_minutes` (0 to 100000), `required_observation_cycles` (0 to 12) and `required_credited_seminars` (0 to 24), the `license_stage_unlocked` that attaining it can grant (`PRELIMINARY`, `PROVISIONAL` or `CLEAR`), an optional `gates_license_recommendation` flag defaulting to false, and an optional `prerequisite_milestone_id` pointing at another milestone, null for a root milestone. A clash on either `code` or `sequence_index` is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_MILESTONE_CODE`, and an unknown `prerequisite_milestone_id` is an `ERR_MILESTONE_NOT_FOUND`. Because milestones are immutable configuration they are the one entity with no `updated_at` and no `version`, and there is no endpoint that edits or deletes one.

The prerequisite chain is kept acyclic at insertion by a rule that leans on the ordering index rather than a graph walk: a new milestone's prerequisite must have a `sequence_index` strictly less than the new milestone's own. A prerequisite whose index is greater than or equal to the new one's is refused with `ERR_MILESTONE_CYCLE`. Milestone listings are returned in `sequence_index` ascending order and may be narrowed by `prerequisite_milestone_id` or by `gates_license_recommendation`.

**Attainment rows carry the progress.** Enrolling a novice seeds one `MilestoneAttainment` row per existing milestone, so the ordinary path never needs an explicit creation. The seeding rule is the same rule that governs the `LOCKED → IN_PROGRESS` transition: a row opens `IN_PROGRESS` when its milestone names no prerequisite, or when the prerequisite milestone's own attainment row for that same novice already stands `ATTAINED`, and `LOCKED` otherwise — at enrolment, where no row of that novice can yet be `ATTAINED`, that means every milestone naming a prerequisite is seeded `LOCKED`. Explicit creation exists only for milestones introduced after a cohort is already under way; it takes `novice_id`, `milestone_id` and an optional `evidence_note` (10 to 1000 characters when supplied), initialises `revocation_count` to zero, and refuses a second row for the same novice-and-milestone pair with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ATTAINMENT`. A `LOCKED` row also unlocks to `IN_PROGRESS` on its own, without any request naming it, when the prerequisite milestone's attainment row for that same novice reaches `ATTAINED`.

Attesting a milestone — `IN_PROGRESS → ATTAINED` — is a `SiteInductionLead` decision, and it is the moment evidence is judged sufficient. Four thresholds must all hold: `verified_contact_minutes` at or above `required_contact_minutes`, `documented_observation_cycles` at or above `required_observation_cycles`, `credited_seminar_sessions` at or above `required_credited_seminars`, and the prerequisite (if any) already `ATTAINED`. A shortfall on any of the first three is refused with `ERR_MILESTONE_REQUIREMENTS_NOT_MET`; an unsatisfied prerequisite is `ERR_PREREQUISITE_NOT_ATTAINED`. On top of these sits the segregation-of-duties bar of §2.9: the attesting `X-User-ID` may not appear as `verified_by_user_id` on any `VERIFIED` contact log belonging to any match of that novice. The lead who signed off the hours may not also certify that the hours were enough.

A successful attestation does five things. It **freezes the evidence**, copying `snapshot_contact_minutes`, `snapshot_observation_cycles`, `snapshot_credited_seminars`, `snapshot_held_sessions` and `snapshot_excused_sessions` out of the novice's counters as they stand at that instant. It stamps `attained_on` from the logical clock and `attested_by_user_id` from the caller. It increments `attained_milestone_count` and re-derives `readiness_index_snapshot`. When the milestone's `gates_license_recommendation` is true it lifts `license_advancement_status` from `ELIGIBLE` to `RECOMMENDED` and writes `license_stage` from the milestone's `license_stage_unlocked`. And it unlocks every `LOCKED` attainment row of the same novice whose milestone names this one as prerequisite, processed in `sequence_index` ascending order. The attainment's `version` and the novice's `version` each increment.

Revocation — `ATTAINED → REVOKED`, a `ProgramAdmin` decision, and also hop two of the contact-log void cascade — is the mirror image. It stamps `revoked_on`, records `revoke_reason` and increments `revocation_count`; it decrements `attained_milestone_count` and re-derives `readiness_index_snapshot`; when the milestone gates the recommendation it drops `license_advancement_status` from `RECOMMENDED` back to `NOT_ELIGIBLE`, which reverts `license_stage` to `PRELIMINARY` and carries hop four onto any submitted packet; and it relocks every `IN_PROGRESS` dependent row back to `LOCKED`. A row may be revoked at most three times: a fourth attempt, made when `revocation_count` already stands at 3, is refused with `ERR_REVOCATION_LIMIT_REACHED`. A `REVOKED` row is reopened to `IN_PROGRESS` by a `ProgramAdmin`, which clears `revoke_reason` back to null and requires the prerequisite still to be `ATTAINED`; this closes the `IN_PROGRESS → ATTAINED → REVOKED → IN_PROGRESS` cycle, so a milestone can be earned again after the missing evidence is rebuilt. No attainment state is terminal. A decision that is not a declared step out of the row's present status — any decision at all on a `LOCKED` row, for instance — is refused with `ERR_ATTAINMENT_STATE_INVALID`.

**The readiness index.** Readiness is a weighted score out of 100 that answers one question: how much of the induction evidence a novice has accumulated. It is recomputed whenever evidence moves — on a contact log's verification and on its voiding, on an observation cycle reaching `DOCUMENTED`, on an attendance row being recorded, on a seminar session being held or cancelled, and on a milestone being attained or revoked.

The order of evaluation is fixed, because the seminar denominator has to be settled before the seminar component can be formed. First, `held_sessions` is the count of seminar sessions in the novice's cohort whose `status` is `HELD`; sessions still `SCHEDULED` and sessions `CANCELLED` are excluded. Second, `excused_sessions` is the count of that novice's own attendance rows whose `attendance_status` is `EXCUSED` and whose session has reached `HELD`. Third, the seminar denominator is `held_sessions` less `excused_sessions`, floored at 1 so the arithmetic never divides by zero — this is where an excused absence pays off, by shrinking the denominator rather than counting as a miss. Fourth, the contact component is `verified_contact_minutes` divided by a target of 600 minutes, capped at 1. Fifth, the observation component is `documented_observation_cycles` divided by a target of 2, capped at 1. Sixth, the seminar component is `credited_seminar_sessions` divided by that denominator, capped at 1. Seventh, the index is 100 multiplied by the sum of the contact component weighted by 0.45, the observation component weighted by 0.30 and the seminar component weighted by 0.25, the total rounded half up to 2 decimal places. The three components are *not* rounded before the weighted sum; only the final index is rounded.

The same arithmetic serves two figures that are deliberately allowed to disagree. `readiness_index_live` runs it over the novice's current counters and current held and excused session counts, and it is what drives the `NOT_ELIGIBLE` ↔ `ELIGIBLE` gate at 85.00: any recomputation leaving the live index at or above 85.00 lifts a `NOT_ELIGIBLE` novice to `ELIGIBLE`, and any recomputation leaving it below 85.00 drops an `ELIGIBLE` novice back to `NOT_ELIGIBLE`. `readiness_index_snapshot` runs it over the five frozen figures of a single attainment row — the novice's `ATTAINED` row ranked by its milestone's `sequence_index` descending, and where two `ATTAINED` rows share a `sequence_index`, by the attainment's own `created_at` descending, the first row winning. When the novice holds no `ATTAINED` row the snapshot index is 0.00. Because the snapshot survives a later void of a contact log while the live figure does not, the two variants routinely diverge on identical stored data, and that divergence is the point: the snapshot is what the district attested to, the live figure is what is true now.

Both figures, the three weighted inputs as `contact_component`, `observation_component` and `seminar_component`, the `held_sessions` and `excused_sessions` counts, the resolved `seminar_denominator`, the `snapshot_source_attainment_id` that supplied the frozen evidence and a `meets_readiness_gate` boolean are exposed side by side on a computed readiness read keyed by `novice_id`, so a lead can see exactly which term is holding a novice back.

**License advancement** is the novice's second status dimension, moving independently of `program_status`. It starts `NOT_ELIGIBLE`, oscillates with the readiness gate into and out of `ELIGIBLE`, rises to `RECOMMENDED` when a gating milestone is attained, falls from `RECOMMENDED` back to `NOT_ELIGIBLE` when that milestone is revoked — closing the `ELIGIBLE → RECOMMENDED → NOT_ELIGIBLE → ELIGIBLE` cycle — and reaches the terminal `ADVANCED` only when the state accepts the completion packet, at which point `license_stage` is set to `CLEAR`.

**Retention** is recorded separately, a year after induction. A `ProgramAdmin` writes a `retention_outcome` and the `observed_on` date it was seen. The field starts at `UNKNOWN` and moves to one of `RETAINED_SAME_SITE`, `RETAINED_DISTRICT`, `LEFT_DISTRICT` or `LEFT_PROFESSION`, storing `retention_observed_on` alongside it. Recording it re-scores any of that novice's packets still `ASSEMBLING`, because retention feeds the completion bonus described in §3.9.

### 3.8. Mentor Stipend Preparation and Payment

Mentors are paid quarterly for verified contact time, and the money follows two separate figures that must never be confused. The **provisional accrual** is a running estimate the mentor can watch; the **final disbursement** is what the district actually pays. They are computed by the same tiered-pricing rule over different input sets, and they are expected to differ.

**The shared arithmetic.** Each eligible contact log is priced on its own. The hourly rate comes from the log's tier — 4200 cents for `EXACT`, 3600 cents for `ADJACENT`, 2800 cents for `CROSS` — and is multiplied by the certification multiplier, 1.00 for a certified mentor and 0.50 otherwise. The per-log amount is the log's credited minutes divided by 60, multiplied by that hourly rate, multiplied by that multiplier, and **rounded half up to whole cents at that point**. The gross is then the sum of those already-rounded per-log amounts, taken over the eligible logs in `created_at` ascending order. The rounding order is pinned and it matters: summing the unrounded products and rounding the total once at the end is explicitly not the rule and gives a different number. The sum itself needs no further rounding.

**The provisional accrual** is the mentor's `stipend_accrued_cents`, refreshed on every contact-log verification, void, dispute and resubmission. Its input set is the mentor's contact logs whose `verification_status` is `SUBMITTED` or `VERIFIED`, on matches whose `match_status` is `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`. For a log that is merely `SUBMITTED` nothing has been pinned yet, so the tier is read live from the match's current `subject_match_tier`, the multiplier is read live from the mentor's current `certification_status`, and the credited minutes are replaced by the projected figure — the raw minutes scaled by the contact type's weight and floored, then capped by whatever room is left in that day's ceiling. A mentor whose certification lapses therefore sees the accrual on their pending logs halve at once, without any log being touched.

**The final disbursement** is prepared by a `ProgramAdmin` as a draft naming the `mentor_id`, a `period_code` matching the four-digit-year-then-quarter form (for example the first quarter of a year written as the year followed by a hyphen, the letter Q and the digit 1), the `period_start` and `period_end` bounding the verification window, an `expected_mentor_version` guarding the read of the mentor, and an optional `adjustment_cents` defaulting to zero. The period must be closed: `period_end` must be strictly in the past relative to the logical clock, or the draft is refused with `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_CLOSED`, and `period_end` must be strictly after `period_start`.

The draft's input set is narrower and its prices are frozen. Only contact logs whose `verification_status` is `VERIFIED` count, on any match of that mentor, and only those whose `verified_at` falls inside the **closed** interval from `period_start` to `period_end` — a log verified at exactly either bound is inside. Tier and multiplier are read from the log's own `rate_tier_at_verification` and `cert_multiplier_at_verification`, not from the match or the mentor as they stand now. `SUBMITTED` logs never contribute. The consequence is that the same stored data yields a strictly smaller gross than the accrual whenever any log is still awaiting verification, and a different gross whenever the mentor's certification changed after a log was verified. If no verified log falls in the period at all, the draft is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_ACTIVITY`.

The draft records `gross_cents`, an `eligible_log_count` giving the number of logs that entered that input set, and `net_cents` as `gross_cents` plus `adjustment_cents`. An adjustment may be negative — down-adjusting for an overpaid earlier quarter is routine — but it may not exceed 500000 cents in either direction, and a negative adjustment whose absolute value exceeds the gross is refused with `ERR_ADJUSTMENT_EXCEEDS_GROSS`, since the district does not issue negative stipends. The resulting net must itself be strictly positive, or the draft is refused with `ERR_NON_POSITIVE_NET_STIPEND`; because that check is applied after the excess check, an adjustment exactly equal to the negated gross reports the non-positive net rather than the excess. The draft stamps `prepared_by_user_id` from the caller and increments the mentor's `version`.

**Supersession rather than editing.** A draft is never revised in place. Preparing a second draft for the same mentor and the same `period_code` while an earlier one is still `DRAFT` drives that earlier row from `DRAFT` to the terminal `SUPERSEDED` and writes its `superseded_by_id` with the id of the new draft, so the corrected figure and the discarded one both remain on file. It is only once a row for that mentor and period has reached `APPROVED` or `PAID` that a further draft is refused, with `ERR_DUPLICATE_DISBURSEMENT_PERIOD`.

**Approval, payment and clawback** are all requested by supplying a `status`. Approving requires the row to be `DRAFT`, requires `net_cents` to be strictly greater than zero — there is nothing to approve on a zero or negative net — and requires that the approving `X-User-ID` differ from `prepared_by_user_id`; the same administrator may not both prepare and approve a payment, and an attempt is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Approval stamps `approved_by_user_id`. Paying requires the row to be `APPROVED` and a `payment_reference` in the body; it stamps `paid_at` and `payment_reference` and adds `net_cents` to the mentor's `stipend_paid_cents`. Clawing back requires the row to be `PAID` and a `clawback_reason`; it stamps `clawed_back_at` and `clawback_reason`, subtracts `net_cents` from `stipend_paid_cents` and adds the identical amount to `stipend_clawed_back_cents`, so the mentor's paid-plus-clawed-back total is conserved and never drifts. `CLAWED_BACK` and `SUPERSEDED` are both terminal, and clawback is the designated compensating action for a payment. Any status change that is not one of these declared steps is refused with `ERR_DISBURSEMENT_STATE_INVALID`.

### 3.9. Certifying Completion to the State

The last act of induction is a packet sent to the state credentialing authority. A `ProgramAdmin` or `SiteInductionLead` assembles one for a novice, supplying the `novice_id` and an `expected_novice_version`. The novice's `program_status` must be `ACTIVE`, and assembling moves it to `COMPLETION_PENDING` and increments the novice's `version`. A novice may hold only one live packet: a second is refused with `ERR_ACTIVE_PACKET_EXISTS` while an `ASSEMBLING`, `SUBMITTED_TO_STATE` or `ACCEPTED` packet exists. A `RETURNED` packet is not a blocker, because a returned packet is meant to be reopened rather than replaced. The packet opens `ASSEMBLING` with `resubmission_count` at zero.

**Scoring the packet.** The score is computed when the packet is assembled and again when it is submitted, and it is a threshold rule with a deliberate internal dependency. Step one forms the milestone component as the count of the novice's `ATTAINED` milestone attainment rows divided by a target of 3, capped at 1; `LOCKED`, `IN_PROGRESS` and `REVOKED` rows are excluded, and that count is stored as `evidence_milestone_count`. Step two forms `base_score` as the novice's `readiness_index_live` weighted by 0.60 plus 100 multiplied by the milestone component weighted by 0.40, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. Step three looks up `retention_bonus` from a tier table keyed on the score *and* the retention outcome: a base score at or above 90.00 with a `retention_outcome` of `RETAINED_SAME_SITE` earns 5.00; otherwise a base score at or above 75.00 with a `retention_outcome` of either `RETAINED_SAME_SITE` or `RETAINED_DISTRICT` earns 2.50; every other combination earns 0.00. Step four forms `completion_score` as the lesser of 100.00 and the sum of `base_score` and `retention_bonus`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places, so the bonus can never carry a packet past a perfect hundred.

Two points of order in that computation are load-bearing. The tier comparison in step three reads the **rounded `base_score`** written in step two, never the `completion_score` that step four produces — the bonus depends on the score before the bonus, and resolving it the other way round is circular. And the readiness figure that feeds step two is the **live** index, not the snapshot; a packet is scored against what is true today.

**District-side actions.** A `ProgramAdmin` drives the packet with an `action` and, where warranted, a `reason`. Submitting moves `ASSEMBLING → SUBMITTED_TO_STATE`, and it is the most heavily gated transition in the system. The packet is re-scored first, and then every one of the following must hold: the novice's cohort must be `CLOSED`, or the submission is refused with `ERR_COHORT_STATE_INVALID`; the novice's `license_advancement_status` must be `RECOMMENDED`, or `ERR_LICENSE_RECOMMENDATION_MISSING`; `evidence_milestone_count` must be at least 3 and `base_score` at least 75.00, evaluated together so that a base score of exactly 75.00 with exactly three attainments passes, or `ERR_MILESTONE_EVIDENCE_INSUFFICIENT`; the novice's `credited_seminar_sessions` must have reached the cohort's own `seminar_sessions_required` figure; and every milestone whose `gates_license_recommendation` is true must have an `ATTAINED` attainment row for this novice, not merely the one that granted the recommendation. On success the acting `X-User-ID` is recorded as `submitted_by_user_id` alongside `submitted_at`, because that identity is what the state-side segregation rule will later be checked against. Withdrawing is available from `ASSEMBLING` and from `RETURNED`, requires a `reason`, records it as `withdrawn_reason`, and lands on the terminal `WITHDRAWN`; from `ASSEMBLING` it also returns the novice's `program_status` from `COMPLETION_PENDING` to `ACTIVE`, while from `RETURNED` the novice is already `ACTIVE` and is left untouched. Withdrawal is also what a novice's own withdrawal cascades into. Reopening moves `RETURNED → ASSEMBLING`, clears `return_reason` back to null, returns the novice's `program_status` from `ACTIVE` to `COMPLETION_PENDING` and increments `resubmission_count`, closing the `ASSEMBLING → SUBMITTED_TO_STATE → RETURNED → ASSEMBLING` revision loop. A packet may be reopened twice; a third reopening, attempted when `resubmission_count` already stands at 2, is refused with `ERR_PACKET_RESUBMISSION_LIMIT_REACHED`.

**State-side decisions** belong to the `StateCredentialingOfficer` alone, who supplies a `decision` and either a `state_file_number` or a `return_reason`. Both decisions require the packet to be `SUBMITTED_TO_STATE`, and both stamp `decided_by_user_id` and `decided_at`. The officer's `X-User-ID` may not equal the packet's `submitted_by_user_id`: the district official who submitted a packet may not also be the state official who blesses it, and a collision is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

Accepting requires a `state_file_number` in the body and records it, moves the novice's `program_status` from `COMPLETION_PENDING` to the terminal `COMPLETED`, moves `license_advancement_status` from `RECOMMENDED` to the terminal `ADVANCED`, and sets `license_stage` to `CLEAR`. `ACCEPTED` is terminal for the packet, and no further action on it is permitted. Returning requires a `return_reason` on the officer-driven path and records it, returns the novice's `program_status` from `COMPLETION_PENDING` to `ACTIVE` so the district can rebuild the packet, and leaves the door open for a reopening. A packet action that is not a declared step out of the current `packet_status` — anything at all attempted on an `ACCEPTED` or `WITHDRAWN` packet — is refused with `ERR_PACKET_STATE_INVALID`.

One returned path deserves emphasis because it arrives without any request naming the packet. When a gating milestone of the novice is revoked, hop four of that cascade drives every `SUBMITTED_TO_STATE` packet of that novice to `RETURNED` with a `return_reason` of `EVIDENCE_REVOKED`, and on that cascaded path `decided_at` and `decided_by_user_id` stay null. The distinction is legible in the stored record: a packet returned by a person carries a decider, and a packet returned by the collapse of its own evidence does not.
