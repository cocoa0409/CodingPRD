# MLO Licensing & Compensation Compliance API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A mortgage lender's compliance office carries an unusual burden: the same individual who sells a loan is also a state-licensed professional whose authority to sell it can lapse overnight, and whose pay is regulated so tightly that keying a single dollar of commission to the interest rate is itself a violation. Today that burden is carried in spreadsheets. Licence expiration dates live in one workbook, sponsorship paperwork in another, compensation plan drafts in a shared drive with no record of who approved what, and the reconciliation of paid commission against closed loans happens quarterly by hand — long after an examiner would have asked for it. When a state regulator opens an examination, the office spends weeks reconstructing facts it should have been able to read off a screen.

This document specifies the API for a single system of record that replaces all of it. The system tracks each originator's licensing and registration status across every state it operates in, including continuing-education accumulation and the renewal windows that authority depends on; it models sponsorship as an explicit interval so that the question *"was this originator authorised in this state at this instant?"* has one computable answer; it enforces compensation plan design rules at approval time, refusing to activate any plan that keys pay to a term of the transaction; it monitors dual compensation and steering exposure continuously and escalates supervision when exposure crosses a threshold; it captures pricing exceptions and concessions with documented justification and a segregated approval trail; it reconciles commission to closed loans and recaptures it when a loan pays off early; it inspects advertising copy for the licence-number and NMLS-identifier disclosures each target state demands; and it drives examination responses and unlicensed-activity investigations to a documented conclusion.

Two design commitments run through every requirement below. First, **time is explicit**: nothing in this system reads the wall clock. Every deadline, renewal window, authority interval and clawback bucket is evaluated against a server-held logical clock that an operator sets, so that a compliance scenario can be replayed deterministically. Second, **nothing happens without a trace**: every compliance-relevant mutation appends an audit entry carrying its own monotonic sequence number, and the resulting trail is the artifact handed to an examiner.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contract every endpoint honours: how callers identify themselves, what a response looks like, how lists are paged, how money and time are represented, and in what order the system rejects a bad request. These rules are not negotiable per-endpoint; where a business workflow in §3 adds a constraint, it adds it on top of what follows.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint — reads, writes, and the operator-only test-driving surface alike — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that user is acting in. There is no anonymous surface. If either header is absent or present but empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS` (HTTP 401); both header values are trimmed of surrounding whitespace before any comparison, so a header carrying only whitespace counts as empty and a padded value is compared in its trimmed form. If `X-User-Role` carries a value that is not one of the seven roles enumerated below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE` (HTTP 401). If the role is well-formed but is not among the roles authorised for the endpoint being called, the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The seven roles are `LicensingAnalyst`, `CompensationDesigner`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `Originator`, `LoanOperations`, `ExaminationCoordinator`, and `PlatformAdmin`. Their spelling is exact and case-sensitive.

- A `LicensingAnalyst` staffs the licensing desk: it registers originators, files and activates state licences, records sponsorship intervals, curates the continuing-education course catalogue and files CE credits.
- A `CompensationDesigner` drafts compensation plans and declares their pay variables. It drafts only — a designer can never approve a plan, not even one someone else drafted.
- A `ComplianceOfficer` is the second line of defence: it approves or rejects plans, assigns plans to originators, decides pricing exceptions, reviews and withdraws advertising, runs compensation reconciliation, releases withheld compensation, and owns unlicensed-activity investigations end to end.
- An `Originator` is the licensed mortgage loan originator itself. It takes and locks applications, withdraws its own applications, requests pricing exceptions on its own files, and drafts, submits, revises and publishes its own advertising.
- A `LoanOperations` user staffs the closing and servicing desk: it funds loans and posts payoffs. It cannot take applications and cannot decide pricing exceptions.
- An `ExaminationCoordinator` is the single point of contact for state examiners: it logs inquiries, files responses, issues further-information requests and closes examinations. It has no authority over licences, plans or compensation.
- A `PlatformAdmin` operates the platform: it owns the logical clock and the nightly compliance sweep. It deliberately holds no business write authority — it can neither licence an originator, approve a plan, close a loan nor decide an exception.

Six of the seven roles are **opaque identities**. When `X-User-Role` is `LicensingAnalyst`, `CompensationDesigner`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `LoanOperations`, `ExaminationCoordinator` or `PlatformAdmin`, the accompanying `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a workflow demands an accountability trail — as `drafted_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` on a compensation plan, as `decided_by_user_id` and `second_decided_by_user_id` on a pricing exception, as `reviewed_by_user_id` on an advertisement, as `finding_by_user_id` on an investigation, and as `actor_user_id` on every audit entry — but it never compares that value to any entity's `id`.

The `Originator` role is different: it is **entity-backed**. When `X-User-Role` is `Originator`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Originator` row, and every ownership check compares the target resource's `originator_id` foreign key to `X-User-ID`. Concretely: when an originator takes an application or drafts an advertisement, the `originator_id` in the request body must equal `X-User-ID`; and when an originator acts on an existing loan application, on a pricing exception, or on an advertisement, the originator that owns the path resource must be the caller. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_ORIGINATOR_IDENTITY_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403). On a create the body comparison is made only when the body actually carries `originator_id` as a string; an absent or non-string value leaves nothing to compare and falls through to the input-shape check of §2.9 rule 7, which reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` instead. This check does not apply to the shared read endpoints, which every one of the seven roles may call against any resource.

Audit entries record the role that caused them in `actor_role`. That field carries whichever of the seven role names was presented, plus one additional value, `SYSTEM`, used for entries generated by a cascade or by the nightly sweep rather than by a human caller; for `SYSTEM` entries `actor_user_id` is null. The entry that records the mutation the caller directly requested always carries that caller's own role and `X-User-ID`, even when the mutation goes on to drive a cascade — a revoked licence's `LICENSE_STATUS_CHANGED` entry names the acting `LicensingAnalyst`, not `SYSTEM`.

Beyond role gating, three write operations are additionally constrained by **segregation of duties**, described where each workflow appears in §3: a plan may not be approved by the user that drafted it, a pricing exception may not be decided by the originator user that requested it, and a second approver may be neither of those two. The one place where segregation is deliberately *not* imposed is remediation of an investigation — a single `ComplianceOfficer` may both enter a substantiated finding and later record its remediation.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the uppercase letters and the decimal digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. It is the one value in the API taken from real server time rather than from the logical clock of §2.4, since it describes the transport rather than a compliance fact.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters, `limit` and `offset`. `limit` defaults to `10`, must be an integer, and must fall between a minimum of `1` and a maximum of `100` inclusive. `offset` defaults to `0`, must be an integer, and must be zero or greater; there is no upper bound on `offset`. A `limit` outside the permitted range, or a negative `offset`, or a non-integer value for either, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400). "Integer" here means a decimal string of digits with an optional leading sign, surrounding whitespace tolerated; anything else — a decimal point, an empty value, a non-numeric token — is a non-integer.

Unless a specific endpoint's description in §3 says otherwise, list results are sorted by `created_at` ascending, and because the logical clock makes identical `created_at` values common, rows sharing one are ordered by the sequence in which the server wrote them — never by identifier. The one deliberate exception is the audit trail, which is ordered by its own `sequence_no` ascending so that replay order never depends on a random identifier.

Successful list responses carry four pagination fields in the `meta` object: `limit` and `offset` echoing the effective values used, `total_count` giving the number of rows matching the filters before paging, and `has_more` indicating whether any matching rows remain beyond this page. Single-resource responses omit all four.

### 2.4. The Logical Compliance Clock

Nothing in this system reads wall-clock time for business purposes. The server holds a **logical clock**, and every temporal comparison in this document — renewal windows, licence expiration, authority intervals, sponsorship intervals, examination deadlines, CE completion dates, clawback day buckets — is evaluated against that logical clock instant. At startup the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it continues to read that instant until a `PlatformAdmin` moves it; it never falls back to real time. Moving the clock is done by supplying a required `to_iso8601` instant — any parseable ISO 8601 instant is accepted, including one earlier than the clock's current setting, so a scenario may wind time backwards as freely as forwards — and the response's `data` carries the single field `current_utc` echoing the new setting. Setting the clock mutates no entity: it changes only what subsequent requests observe as "now".

Because the clock is explicit, a licence does not expire the moment its `expiration_date` passes. Expiry is applied by the nightly compliance sweep described in §3.9, which the `PlatformAdmin` triggers; the sweep reports the instant it ran as `swept_at`. Between an `expiration_date` passing and the next sweep, the licence row still reads `ACTIVE`, but the authority computation of §2.8 already treats the instant as outside the licence interval — so an application cannot be taken even though the row has not yet flipped.

### 2.5. Record Identity, Timestamps and Record Versions

Every resource the API returns carries an `id`: a server-minted UUID with no type prefix and no client-supplied component. Clients never propose an `id`.

All persisted resources carry a `created_at` timestamp: an ISO 8601 UTC instant with exactly three fractional-second digits, stamped from the logical clock when the row is written. Every resource in this system except the audit trail additionally carries an `updated_at` timestamp in the same format, refreshed on every mutation of that row, and a `version` integer that starts at `1` when the row is created and increments by exactly one on every mutation of that row. Whenever a workflow in §3 says a row is mutated — a state advanced, a computed field recomputed, a cascade applied — both `updated_at` and `version` move on that row.

The audit trail is the single exception. An `AuditEvent` is append-only and is never modified after it is written, so an audit entry carries only `id` and `created_at`: it has **no** `updated_at` and **no** `version` field at all. Its ordering key is `sequence_no`, a strictly increasing counter that starts at `1` for the first entry the system ever writes and advances by one for each subsequent entry, globally across all entity types and all requests.

Each audit entry names its subject polymorphically rather than through a declared foreign key: `subject_type` carries the entity type — one of `Originator`, `LicenseRegistration`, `SponsorshipTransfer`, `CeCreditRecord`, `CompensationPlan`, `LoanApplication`, `PricingException`, `CompensationRecord`, `Advertisement`, `ExaminationInquiry` or `UnlicensedActivityInvestigation` — and `subject_ref` carries that row's `id` value as an opaque string used only for retrieval and display, never as an input to ordering, ranking or selection. `event_type` classifies the event as one of `LICENSE_STATUS_CHANGED`, `SPONSORSHIP_CHANGED`, `CE_CREDIT_VOIDED`, `PLAN_APPROVED`, `PLAN_REJECTED`, `PROHIBITED_VARIABLE_BLOCKED`, `APPLICATION_TAKEN`, `EXCEPTION_DECIDED`, `COMP_ACCRUED`, `COMP_RECONCILED`, `COMP_CLAWED_BACK`, `AD_DECIDED`, `INVESTIGATION_OPENED`, `INVESTIGATION_FINDING` or `MONITORING_ESCALATED`, and `detail` carries a JSON object of structured key/value pairs summarising what changed — typically the new `status`, and where relevant the id of the row the change superseded or the loan the entry concerns — rather than a prose sentence; it is the empty object `{}` when the operation supplies nothing structured.

### 2.6. Money, Basis Points and Rounding Discipline

All money in this system is an integer count of **cents**. There are no decimal monetary fields and therefore no two-decimal-place formatting rule: a field named with the `_cents` suffix is a whole number of cents. Commission rates are integer **basis points**, where one basis point is one ten-thousandth of the loan amount; converting basis points to cents therefore divides by 10,000.

Wherever a division produces a fractional cent, the result is rounded **half up** to the nearest whole cent — a value of exactly one half cent rounds away from zero. Two rounding disciplines matter and are easy to get wrong:

- **Round per line, then sum.** When commission is computed for several loans in one run, each line's own commission is rounded to whole cents first, and only the already-rounded per-line amounts are added together. Summing exact fractional values across lines and rounding once at the end is forbidden and yields a different total.
- **Round once on an assembled sum.** When a weighted score is assembled from several weighted terms, the terms are added at full precision and the single rounding is applied to the finished sum. Rounding the individual terms first and then adding them is forbidden and yields a different score.

Percentages that scale a money amount (a concession absorption share, a clawback recapture share) are integer percentages, and the resulting cents amount is likewise rounded half up. Day counts derived from two instants are floored to whole days: a duration of eighty-nine days and twenty-three hours is eighty-nine days, not ninety. Continuing-education hours are integers and are never rounded. The steering exposure score is an integer between `0` and `100` inclusive.

### 2.7. Stale-Version Guards on Authority-Displacing Writes

Two write operations in this system legitimately displace something a client may not have seen. Both are therefore guarded by optimistic concurrency control against the **parent** row's `version`.

The first is claiming sponsorship of a licence. That write is a genuine supersede: a new sponsorship displaces whatever `ACTIVE` sponsorship the licence already carries, moving it `ACTIVE → SUPERSEDED`. The request must therefore carry `expected_license_registration_version` — a required, non-optional field — holding the `version` the client last observed on the parent `LicenseRegistration`. If that value does not equal the licence's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_LICENSE_VERSION` (HTTP 412), because a client working from a stale view of the licence would otherwise overwrite an authority interval it never read.

The second is assigning a compensation plan to an originator. That write likewise supersedes whichever plan the originator currently carries, moving that plan `ACTIVE → SUPERSEDED`. The request must therefore carry `expected_originator_version` — again required, not optional — holding the `version` the client last observed on the parent `Originator`. If that value does not equal the originator's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ORIGINATOR_VERSION` (HTTP 412), because a stale writer would otherwise re-point `compensation_plan_id` across an assignment interval it never observed.

These are the only two operations that take a version field. No other endpoint in this system accepts `expected_license_registration_version`, `expected_originator_version`, or any other expected-version input, and supplying one elsewhere is simply not part of that endpoint's contract. Both version checks are evaluated **before** any state precondition on the parent row is examined, so a stale version always surfaces as the 412 rather than being masked by an invalid-state rejection.

### 2.8. Authority Intervals and the Half-Open Convention

The central computed fact in this system is `authorized_state_codes` on an originator: the set of two-letter state codes in which that originator may lawfully take an application right now, held as a list of strings sorted in ascending order.

A state is authorised for an originator at a given instant when **all** of the following hold. There exists a licence row for that originator and that `state_code` whose `status` is either `ACTIVE` or `RENEWAL_PENDING`; that licence's `authority_effective_at` is set rather than null; that `authority_effective_at` is at or before the instant; the instant is strictly before that licence's `expiration_date`; that licence has a sponsorship row whose `effective_at` is at or before the instant and whose `relinquished_at` is either null or strictly after the instant; and the originator's `employment_status` is `EMPLOYED`. Both intervals — the licence interval running from `authority_effective_at` up to `expiration_date`, and the sponsorship interval running from `effective_at` up to `relinquished_at` — are **half-open**: the start instant is inside the interval and the end instant is outside it.

Licences in `PENDING`, `EXPIRED`, `SURRENDERED` or `REVOKED` contribute nothing to authority. Sponsorship rows contribute whenever their half-open interval covers the instant, whether the row is still `ACTIVE` with a null `relinquished_at` or has already moved to `RELINQUISHED` or `SUPERSEDED` with a closed end. Instant comparisons are made at millisecond resolution: any supplied instant carrying finer precision is truncated downwards to whole milliseconds as it is parsed, and it is the truncated value that is stored, compared and echoed back.

A direct consequence of the half-open convention is that **adjacency is not a gap**. A sponsorship relinquished at a given instant, immediately followed by a new sponsorship effective at that same instant, leaves no unauthorised moment; an application taken at exactly that instant is covered by the new sponsorship rather than the old one. This convention governs the authority computation and the sponsorship-overlap rule. It does *not* govern plan assignment intervals, which are closed on both ends — see §3.3, where adjacency deliberately *is* a collision.

Authority is recomputed whenever any of its inputs change: whenever a licence's `status` or `expiration_date` changes, whenever a sponsorship row's `status` changes, and whenever an originator's `employment_status` changes. It is read whenever an application is taken or locked, whenever an advertisement is published, and by the computed authority read, which reports `originator_id`, the instant it evaluated as `as_of`, the resulting `authorized_state_codes`, and the originator's `employment_status`. That read evaluates at the instant supplied as `as_of_iso8601` when the caller supplies one, and otherwise at the current logical clock instant.

### 2.9. The Order of Rejection

Multiple rules can fail on the same request. The system evaluates them in one fixed order and **the first failing rule alone decides the response** — later failures are never reported and never mask an earlier one. Implementations must honour this order, because which error code a caller sees is part of the contract. In ascending order of evaluation:

1. Both authentication headers present and non-empty — otherwise `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS`.
2. `X-User-Role` is one of the seven declared roles — otherwise `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE`.
3. The presented role is authorised for the endpoint — otherwise `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.
4. When the role is `Originator`, `X-User-ID` matches the originator the request acts on — otherwise `ERR_ORIGINATOR_IDENTITY_MISMATCH`. This applies to every endpoint whose authorised roles include `Originator`.
5. Every id in the request path resolves to an existing row, checked left to right — otherwise `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`.
6. Every foreign-key id in the request body — `originator_id`, `ce_course_id`, `compensation_plan_id`, `loan_application_id` — resolves to an existing row, otherwise `ERR_REFERENCED_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`. This applies when filing a licence, filing a CE credit, taking an application, drafting an advertisement, recording an examination inquiry, assigning a compensation plan, and opening an investigation manually.
7. The request body conforms to the endpoint's declared input shape: required fields present, types correct, enum members recognised, numeric ranges honoured, ISO 8601 timestamps parseable, array element formats correct — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The same rule covers the declared non-filter query parameters `as_of_iso8601`, `attribution_variant` and `reporting_year`, whose malformed or unrecognised values are likewise rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; the list filter parameters of §2.10 are not validated this way. Three shape conventions hold everywhere: any endpoint that declares at least one input field requires a JSON object body, so a missing, unparseable or non-object body is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, while an endpoint declaring no input accepts and ignores whatever body arrives, including none; a field supplied as JSON `null` is treated exactly as if it were absent, so an optional field falls back to its default and a required one is reported missing; and a JSON boolean is never accepted where an integer is declared. Fields the endpoint does not declare are ignored rather than rejected.
8. On a list endpoint, `limit` and `offset` are within their permitted ranges — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`.
9. Uniqueness holds: an originator's `nmls_unique_id` is globally unique, a course's `course_code` is globally unique, an inquiry's `exam_reference` is globally unique, and a licence's `state_code` is unique per originator across licence rows whose `status` is `PENDING`, `ACTIVE`, `RENEWAL_PENDING` or `EXPIRED` — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY`. This applies when registering an originator, adding a course, recording an inquiry, and filing a licence.
10. The optimistic-concurrency check of §2.7 — otherwise `ERR_STALE_LICENSE_VERSION` on a sponsorship claim, or `ERR_STALE_ORIGINATOR_VERSION` on a plan assignment.
11. The target row's current state has an outgoing transition matching the requested action — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. This covers every non-terminal invalid-source case across all nine state machines and applies to every action endpoint.
12. The target row is not already in one of its state machine's terminal states — otherwise `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`. This too applies to every action endpoint.
13. A licence exists for the pair of originator and state with `status` in `ACTIVE` or `RENEWAL_PENDING` — otherwise `ERR_LICENSE_NOT_ACTIVE`. Checked when taking and when locking an application.
14. The state is present in the originator's `authorized_state_codes` at the logical clock instant — otherwise `ERR_STATE_NOT_AUTHORIZED`. Checked when taking an application, when locking one, and when publishing an advertisement.
15. The authorising licence has a sponsorship interval covering the logical clock instant — otherwise `ERR_SPONSORSHIP_REQUIRED`. Checked when taking and when locking an application.
16. A plan being approved has no prohibited pay variable — otherwise `ERR_PROHIBITED_COMP_VARIABLE`.
17. Segregation of duties holds — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Checked on a plan approval decision and on a pricing-exception decision.
18. An approve decision on an exception that requires a second approver carries one — otherwise `ERR_SECOND_APPROVAL_REQUIRED`.
19. Heightened justification length is met on a pricing-exception request — otherwise `ERR_JUSTIFICATION_INSUFFICIENT`.
20. Dual compensation is not being reported at close under enhanced supervision — otherwise `ERR_DUAL_COMPENSATION_PROHIBITED`.
21. No investigation on the loan is still unresolved when withheld compensation is released — otherwise `ERR_UNLICENSED_ACTIVITY_OPEN`.
22. An advertisement being approved has no outstanding disclosure findings — otherwise `ERR_AD_DISCLOSURE_INCOMPLETE`.
23. The referenced compensation plan is `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_PLAN_NOT_ACTIVE`. Checked on a plan assignment, at close (against the plan the originator carries), and on a pricing-exception decision.
24. The originator has a plan at all when a loan is closed — otherwise `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_COMP_PLAN`.
25. A plan being approved has at least one pay variable and their weights total exactly one hundred — otherwise `ERR_PLAN_WEIGHTS_NOT_100`.
26. A CE credit's course still has an approved provider — otherwise `ERR_COURSE_PROVIDER_RESCINDED`.
27. A CE credit's course is accepted in a state the originator holds a licence in — otherwise `ERR_COURSE_NOT_APPROVED_IN_STATE`.
28. A renewal request falls inside the renewal window — otherwise `ERR_RENEWAL_WINDOW_CLOSED`.
29. An incoming sponsorship interval does not collide with an existing one on the same licence — otherwise `ERR_SPONSORSHIP_PERIOD_OVERLAP`.
30. An incoming plan-assignment interval does not intersect an existing assignment on the same originator — otherwise `ERR_PLAN_PERIOD_OVERLAP`.
31. Supplied date ranges are coherent — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE`. Checked on a plan assignment, on a CE credit filing, and on a payoff.
32. An examination response is filed on or before its deadline — otherwise `ERR_RESPONSE_DEADLINE_PASSED`.
33. Continuing education is satisfied when a renewal is completed — otherwise `ERR_CE_HOURS_INSUFFICIENT`.
34. A requested concession does not exceed the per-loan cap — otherwise `ERR_CONCESSION_EXCEEDS_CAP`.
35. Reported compensation at close does not exceed the plan's ceiling — otherwise `ERR_COMP_CAP_EXCEEDED`.
36. A finding's restitution amount matches the finding's direction — otherwise `ERR_RESTITUTION_MISMATCH`.
37. A reinstatement is still within the lifetime reinstatement allowance — otherwise `ERR_REINSTATEMENT_LIMIT_REACHED`.
38. A surrender is not blocked by an open pipeline — otherwise `ERR_OPEN_PIPELINE_BLOCKS_SURRENDER`.

The precise trigger condition for each of rules 13 through 38 is stated with the workflow it guards in §3; this list fixes only their relative order.

### 2.10. Nested Resource Retrieval

Several paths address a child through its parent — a licence's sponsorship claim, a plan's pay variables, a loan's pricing-exception request. Path identifiers are resolved strictly **left to right**, and the first identifier that does not resolve produces `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404). A pay-variable declaration against a plan id that does not exist therefore reports the missing plan, and the body is never examined. When a child is addressed through its parent's path, the child's parent reference is taken from the path segment rather than from the body: a `CompensationVariable`'s `compensation_plan_id` and a `PricingException`'s `loan_application_id` are both filled from the path, not from a client-supplied field.

Sub-resource collections are also reachable as peer top-level collections with filters, and every such list endpoint accepts the parent's foreign key as a filter so that a client can walk the graph in either direction. Sponsorship rows filter on `license_registration_id` and `status`; CE credits filter on `originator_id`, `ce_course_id`, `credit_status` and `attributed_year`; plans filter on `status` and `assigned_originator_id`; loan applications filter on `originator_id`, `compensation_plan_id`, `state_code`, `status` and `compensation_status`; pricing exceptions filter on `loan_application_id` and `status`; ledger lines filter on `loan_application_id`, `originator_id`, `compensation_plan_id`, `status` and `variant`; advertisements filter on `originator_id` and `status`; inquiries filter on `originator_id`, `state_code` and `status`; investigations filter on `originator_id`, `loan_application_id`, `status` and `opened_by`; licences filter on `originator_id`, `state_code` and `status`; originators filter on `employment_status`, `monitoring_status` and `compensation_plan_id`; and audit entries filter on `subject_type`, `subject_ref` and `event_type`. Every one of these filters is an exact string equality test against the stored field — the single exception being the numeric `attributed_year` on CE credits, whose supplied value is parsed as a decimal integer and compared against the stored integer, an unparseable value simply matching nothing — and none of them is validated: an unrecognised value — a `status` naming no state, an id resolving to no row — simply matches nothing and yields an empty page with `total_count` `0` rather than an error, and any query parameter not named here or in §2.3 is ignored.

### 2.11. Cascade Discipline

A number of operations in this system mutate more than one entity. Where that happens, the cascade is specified as an ordered chain of hops, each hop caused by the previous hop's mutation rather than by the original call, and **every hop is mandatory**. Two chains run five hops deep — the authority-lapse chain that fires when a licence stops authorising, and the clawback chain that fires when a loan pays off early — and both are described in full in §3.

Three consequences apply to every cascade. Each row a cascade touches has its own `updated_at` refreshed and its own `version` incremented, including rows the caller never named — but a row is refreshed at most once per request, so a row that several hops of the same cascade revisit still moves its `version` by exactly one, and a row the request itself created stays at `version` `1` however many later hops touch it. Audit entries are appended in the order the hops executed, so that the trail reads as the causal chain. And when a cascade must process a set of rows, the processing order is always deterministic and never depends on a random identifier: loans are processed by `funded_at` ascending and then `created_at` ascending — a loan whose `funded_at` is still null, as every `TAKEN` or `LOCKED` loan's is, sorts ahead of any loan that carries one — licences by `expiration_date` ascending and then `created_at` ascending, and target state codes in ascending string order.

Two conserved quantities are maintained exactly, with zero drift, by these cascades. An originator's `lifetime_accrued_cents` is at all times the sum of `accrued_cents` over every one of that originator's ledger lines in any status, `lifetime_clawed_back_cents` is at all times the sum of `clawback_cents` over the same set, and `net_compensation_cents` is at all times exactly `lifetime_accrued_cents` less `lifetime_clawed_back_cents` — it is a derived balance and is never written directly. Similarly, a licence's `ce_hours_applied` is at all times the total of the continuing-education hours currently counting toward its reporting year, so that voiding a credit and re-applying it restores the previous total exactly.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Registering Originators, Filing State Licences and Establishing Sponsored Authority

Everything in this system hangs off an originator record. A `LicensingAnalyst` creates one by supplying the originator's `nmls_unique_id` — the NMLS unique identifier that must be printed on every advertisement and disclosure, given as a string of between six and eight decimal digits and unique across all originators in the system — together with the individual's `legal_name` as filed with NMLS, between one and one hundred and twenty characters, and a `hire_date` giving the instant the institution employed them, accepted as any parseable ISO 8601 instant with no constraint relative to the logical clock. An `employment_status` may optionally be supplied; when it is omitted the record starts as `EMPLOYED`, and the only other permitted values are `SUSPENDED` and `TERMINATED`. A duplicate `nmls_unique_id` is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY`.

A freshly registered originator is deliberately inert. Its `monitoring_status` reads `STANDARD` and its `exposure_score` reads `0`; its three ledger fields `lifetime_accrued_cents`, `lifetime_clawed_back_cents` and `net_compensation_cents` all read `0`; its `authorized_state_codes` is an empty list; its `open_investigation_count` is `0`; and its `compensation_plan_id` is null, because no plan has been assigned yet. `compensation_plan_id` is never set by a client on the originator record — it is written only by a plan assignment and cleared only by assignment expiry during the nightly sweep. Both `monitoring_status` and `exposure_score` are likewise computed, never supplied.

Employment posture can change. A `LicensingAnalyst` updating an originator supplies a new `employment_status`, which is the update's single accepted field and is required — any other field in the body is ignored, and an absent `employment_status` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. No transition gate applies: any of the three values may replace any other, including a move back from `TERMINATED` to `EMPLOYED`. Because employment is an input to the authority computation, `authorized_state_codes` is recomputed immediately: moving an originator to `SUSPENDED` or `TERMINATED` strips every state authority it held, even though its licences remain untouched and still read `ACTIVE`. The update increments the originator's `version` by one.

Filing a state licence is the next step. The analyst supplies the `originator_id` of the individual, the `state_code` — an uppercase two-letter USPS code — the state-issued `license_number` as a string of between four and thirty-two characters, the `issue_date` on which the state issued it, the `expiration_date` at which authority lapses unless renewal completes, and the `renewal_window_opens_at` instant that is the earliest a renewal may be filed. `expiration_date` must fall strictly after `issue_date`, and `renewal_window_opens_at` must fall strictly before `expiration_date`; both are part of this endpoint's declared input shape, so a violation of either is reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) rather than as `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE`. The analyst also supplies the `ce_reporting_year` — the calendar year whose continuing-education credits satisfy the next renewal, between 2020 and 2100 — and may optionally supply `required_ce_hours`, the total continuing-education hours that state demands per reporting period; omitted, it defaults to `8`, and any supplied value must be at least 8 and at most 20. A licence for a state the originator already holds an unretired licence in — that is, where an existing licence row for the same originator and `state_code` sits in `PENDING`, `ACTIVE`, `RENEWAL_PENDING` or `EXPIRED` — is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY`; a `SURRENDERED` or `REVOKED` row does not block a refiling.

A newly filed licence confers no authority whatsoever. It starts in `PENDING` with `authority_effective_at` null, `ce_hours_applied` at `0`, `ce_satisfied` false and `reinstatement_count` at `0`. Authority begins only when the analyst activates it, advancing `PENDING → ACTIVE`. Activation requires that the licence is still `PENDING`, that the owning originator's `employment_status` is `EMPLOYED`, and that the logical clock instant falls strictly before the licence's `expiration_date` — an analyst cannot activate a licence that has already run out. Neither of those two extra conditions has an error code of its own: a non-`EMPLOYED` owner and an already-lapsed `expiration_date` are both reported as `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409), the employment condition first. On activation the system stamps `authority_effective_at` from the logical clock, recomputes the originator's `authorized_state_codes`, and appends an audit entry of type `LICENSE_STATUS_CHANGED` with `subject_type` `LicenseRegistration`.

Reading a licence back returns its computed compliance posture alongside its filed data: `ce_hours_applied` and `ce_satisfied` for the current reporting year, and the two denormalised sponsorship fields `active_sponsoring_institution_code` and `active_sponsorship_effective_at`, which mirror the licence's currently `ACTIVE` sponsorship and read null while the licence is unsponsored. Neither denormalised field is a foreign key — the owning direction always runs from the sponsorship row back to the licence.

An activated licence still confers nothing until an institution claims sponsorship of it. A `LicensingAnalyst` records a sponsorship claim against the licence, supplying the `sponsoring_institution_code` — the institution's NMLS company code, between four and twelve characters — and the `effective_at` instant at which the sponsorship interval inclusively begins. The claim is optimistic-concurrency guarded and must carry `expected_license_registration_version` as described in §2.7. No precondition is placed on the licence's own `status`: once the version guard passes and the geometry below is clear, a claim is recorded against a licence in any status, `PENDING` and the terminal `SURRENDERED` and `REVOKED` included — the licence simply confers no authority in those statuses. A successful claim creates a sponsorship row in `ACTIVE` with a null `relinquished_at`, copies `sponsoring_institution_code` and `effective_at` into the licence's `active_sponsoring_institution_code` and `active_sponsorship_effective_at`, recomputes the originator's `authorized_state_codes`, and increments the licence's `version` by one.

When the licence already carries an `ACTIVE` sponsorship, the incoming claim legitimately supersedes it, provided the incoming `effective_at` is at or after the existing row's `effective_at`. The existing row moves `ACTIVE → SUPERSEDED`, its `relinquished_at` is set to the **incoming** row's `effective_at` rather than to the clock, and its `superseded_by_created_at` records the incoming row's `created_at` — an instant is used rather than an identifier so that no ordering decision ever reads a random id. An audit entry of type `SPONSORSHIP_CHANGED` is appended.

Sponsorship geometry on a single licence is policed with the half-open convention of §2.8. An incoming `effective_at` is rejected with `ERR_SPONSORSHIP_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409) in two situations: when it falls inside the half-open interval of an already-closed sponsorship on the same licence — that is, when some row whose `status` is `RELINQUISHED` or `SUPERSEDED` has an `effective_at` at or before the incoming instant and a `relinquished_at` strictly after it — and when it is back-dated, meaning it falls strictly before the `effective_at` of some existing sponsorship row on that licence. Exact adjacency is legal and is the intended way to hand a licence from one institution to the next: an incoming `effective_at` equal to a previous row's `relinquished_at` is accepted.

A sponsorship can also simply end. When an analyst relinquishes one directly, the row moves `ACTIVE → RELINQUISHED`; this requires only that the row is still `ACTIVE`, and the owning licence's own `status` is deliberately not examined. The system stamps `relinquished_at` from the logical clock, clears the licence's `active_sponsoring_institution_code` and `active_sponsorship_effective_at` to null, increments the licence's `version` by one, recomputes the originator's `authorized_state_codes` — the state drops out, because an unsponsored licence confers no authority at all — and appends a `SPONSORSHIP_CHANGED` audit entry.

Three operator actions retire a licence outright, all reached by supplying an `action` of `SURRENDER`, `REVOKE` or `REINSTATE` together with an optional free-text `note` of at most two thousand characters, which is carried into the audit entry's `detail` when supplied. Reinstatement is covered in §3.2. **Surrender** advances the licence to `SURRENDERED` and is permitted when the licence's `status` is `ACTIVE` or `RENEWAL_PENDING`; it additionally requires that no loan application for that same originator and that same `state_code` is currently in `status` `TAKEN` or `LOCKED`, since surrendering while a file is still in the pipeline would strand it — such an attempt is rejected with `ERR_OPEN_PIPELINE_BLOCKS_SURRENDER` (HTTP 422). On surrender the licence's `ACTIVE` sponsorship is relinquished, `authorized_state_codes` is recomputed, and a `LICENSE_STATUS_CHANGED` audit entry is appended.

**Revocation** advances the licence to `REVOKED`, is permitted when the licence's `status` is `ACTIVE`, `RENEWAL_PENDING` or `EXPIRED`, and — unlike surrender — is never blocked by an open pipeline. Instead it drives the full five-hop **authority-lapse cascade**, and every hop is caused by the mutation the previous hop made:

1. The licence's `ACTIVE` sponsorship moves to `RELINQUISHED` with `relinquished_at` stamped from the logical clock.
2. Because that sponsorship closed, the licence's `active_sponsoring_institution_code` and `active_sponsorship_effective_at` go null and the originator's `authorized_state_codes` is recomputed, losing that `state_code`.
3. Because the state dropped out of `authorized_state_codes`, every loan application belonging to that originator, carrying that `state_code`, and sitting in `status` `TAKEN` or `LOCKED` has its `authority_flag` set to `SUSPENDED`.
4. Because those loans were suspended, one unlicensed-activity investigation is opened for each of them, with `opened_by` set to `CASCADE`, `trigger_reason` forced to `AUTHORITY_LAPSE`, and `status` `OPEN`.
5. Because those investigations were created, the originator's `open_investigation_count` is recomputed — it is derived on every read rather than stored, so this hop lands no write on the originator row and does not move that row's `updated_at` or `version`.

The audit trail records this chain as one `LICENSE_STATUS_CHANGED` entry followed by one `INVESTIGATION_OPENED` entry per created investigation, the investigation entries ordered by their suspended loans' `funded_at` ascending and then `created_at` ascending. `SURRENDERED` and `REVOKED` are both terminal: a licence in either state accepts no further transition, and an attempt is rejected with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`.

The same five-hop cascade fires, hop for hop, when the nightly sweep expires a licence — see §3.9.

### 3.2. Continuing Education, Renewal Windows and Reinstatement

Continuing education is what keeps a licence alive, and the system models it as a catalogue of approved courses plus a ledger of completed credits. A `LicensingAnalyst` adds a course to the catalogue by supplying its `course_code` — the NMLS-approved catalogue code, between four and twenty-four characters and globally unique, with a duplicate rejected as `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY` — the `credit_hours` the course awards for completing it exactly once, an integer of at least 1 and at most 12, and the `category` the hours count toward, one of `FEDERAL_LAW`, `ETHICS`, `NONTRADITIONAL`, `STATE_SPECIFIC` or `ELECTIVE`. Two optional flags shape how the course behaves later. `approved_state_codes` lists the two-letter state codes whose regulator accepts the course; it defaults to an empty list, and an empty list means the course is accepted in **every** state rather than in none. `successive_year_blocked` defaults to true and, when true, means the same `course_code` may not be applied in two consecutive attributed years for one originator — the NMLS successive-years rule. A new course starts with `provider_status` `APPROVED`.

Providers lose their approval sometimes, and the compliance consequence is retroactive. When an analyst rescinds a course, its `provider_status` moves to `RESCINDED`, and every CE credit sourced from that course that was still counting — every credit whose `credit_status` was `APPLIED` — moves to `VOIDED` with `void_reason` set to `PROVIDER_RESCINDED`; no precondition is placed on the course's own `provider_status`, so rescinding a course that already reads `RESCINDED` is accepted and simply re-runs the consequences below over an empty set of `APPLIED` credits. That in turn frees any credit that had been suppressed: every credit sitting in `EXCLUDED_SUCCESSIVE_YEAR` **solely** because of one of the credits just voided returns to `APPLIED`. Finally, `ce_hours_applied` and `ce_satisfied` are recomputed using the make-up attribution variant described below on every affected licence — meaning every licence belonging to any originator one of whose credits was just voided, whatever that licence's own `status`. A rescission appends no audit entry at all, neither for the course nor for the credits it voids. Filing a new credit against a course whose `provider_status` is `RESCINDED` is rejected with `ERR_COURSE_PROVIDER_RESCINDED` (HTTP 422).

Filing a credit is how hours enter the ledger. The analyst supplies the `originator_id` who completed the course, the `ce_course_id` of the course completed, the `completion_date` instant of completion, and the `reporting_period_year` the originator claims the hours against, between 2020 and 2100. An optional `is_makeup` flag, false by default, marks the credit as late make-up education filed for a prior reporting year. Three of the stored fields are server-computed and any client-supplied value for them is ignored: `credit_hours` and `category` are copied from the course at creation, and `attributed_year` is derived. A `completion_date` later than the logical clock instant is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE` (HTTP 422). And when the course carries a non-empty `approved_state_codes`, the originator must hold at least one licence whose `status` is `PENDING`, `ACTIVE` or `RENEWAL_PENDING` and whose `state_code` is a member of that list; otherwise the filing is rejected with `ERR_COURSE_NOT_APPROVED_IN_STATE` (HTTP 422). A successful filing then recomputes `ce_hours_applied` and `ce_satisfied` on every licence belonging to that originator, whatever each licence's own `status`.

**Attribution** decides which reporting year a credit's hours land in, and it is the single most consequential rule in this subsection. A credit filed with `is_makeup` false is attributed to the calendar year, in UTC, of its `completion_date`. A credit filed with `is_makeup` true is attributed instead to its `reporting_period_year`; `reporting_period_year` is ignored entirely for attribution purposes unless `is_makeup` is true. The resulting year is stored on the row as `attributed_year`.

**Successive-year suppression** is evaluated once, at the moment the credit is created. A new credit is written with `credit_status` `EXCLUDED_SUCCESSIVE_YEAR` when its course's `successive_year_blocked` flag is true and there already exists another credit for the same `originator_id`, sourced from the same `ce_course_id`, whose `credit_status` is `APPLIED`, and whose `attributed_year` is exactly one less than the new credit's own `attributed_year`. In every other case the new credit is written with `credit_status` `APPLIED`. The later-attributed credit is always the one suppressed; the earlier row is never retroactively changed by a newer arrival. `credit_status` therefore takes one of three values — `APPLIED` while the hours count, `EXCLUDED_SUCCESSIVE_YEAR` while the successive-years rule suppresses them, and `VOIDED` after an explicit void or a provider rescission — and `void_reason` reads null unless the credit is `VOIDED`, in which case it is either `ANALYST_VOID` or `PROVIDER_RESCINDED`.

The CE evaluation itself aggregates over a **counted set**, and only credits whose `credit_status` is `APPLIED` are ever in it: rows in `EXCLUDED_SUCCESSIVE_YEAR` and rows in `VOIDED` are excluded from every sum. Which `APPLIED` credits belong to the counted set for an originator and a target year depends on the attribution variant in force. Under `MAKEUP_ATTRIBUTION` a credit is counted when its `attributed_year` equals the target year. Under `CALENDAR_YEAR_ATTRIBUTION` a credit is counted when the calendar year of its `completion_date` equals the target year, whatever `is_makeup` says. There is no cross-year carry-over of hours beyond that difference.

Over the counted set the system totals four figures: the total of `credit_hours` across the whole set, and the totals of `credit_hours` restricted to credits whose `category` is `FEDERAL_LAW`, to those whose `category` is `ETHICS`, and to those whose `category` is `NONTRADITIONAL`. The overall total is written to the licence's `ce_hours_applied`. The licence's `ce_satisfied` is set true only when all four of the following minimums are met: the overall total is at least the licence's own `required_ce_hours`, the federal-law total is at least three hours, the ethics total is at least two hours, and the non-traditional total is at least two hours. All four comparisons are inclusive — hitting a minimum exactly satisfies it. Hours are whole integers throughout and are never rounded. Credits in the `STATE_SPECIFIC` and `ELECTIVE` categories contribute to the overall total but to none of the three category minimums. A recompute that leaves both `ce_hours_applied` and `ce_satisfied` exactly as they already stood writes nothing to the licence at all, so that licence's `updated_at` and `version` do not move.

The two variants produce genuinely different answers from identical data, and that is the point. Consider a make-up credit completed on 15 January 2027 and filed for `reporting_period_year` 2026. Under `CALENDAR_YEAR_ATTRIBUTION` it is attributed to 2027 and does **not** enter the 2026 total; under `MAKEUP_ATTRIBUTION` it is attributed to 2026 and **does** enter it. The same credit rows can therefore yield `ce_satisfied` false under one variant and true under the other. `CALENDAR_YEAR_ATTRIBUTION` is used when a renewal request is filed. `MAKEUP_ATTRIBUTION` is used when a renewal is completed, when a credit is filed, when a credit is voided, and when a course provider is rescinded. The computed CE read supports both, with `attribution_variant` as an optional query parameter defaulting to `MAKEUP_ATTRIBUTION`, and an optional `reporting_year` that defaults to the `ce_reporting_year` of whichever of the originator's licences has the greatest `created_at` — the later-written row winning a tie — and falls back to the calendar year of the logical clock instant when the originator holds no licence at all. That read returns `originator_id`, the `reporting_year` it evaluated, the `attribution_variant` it applied, and the four aggregates as `total_hours`, `federal_law_hours`, `ethics_hours` and `nontraditional_hours`, together with the resulting `ce_satisfied` verdict, whose overall-total minimum is the `required_ce_hours` of that same greatest-`created_at` licence whatever `reporting_year` was evaluated, and `8` when the originator holds no licence.

Mis-filed credits are corrected rather than deleted. Voiding a credit sets its `credit_status` to `VOIDED` and its `void_reason` to `ANALYST_VOID`; every credit sitting in `EXCLUDED_SUCCESSIVE_YEAR` that had been suppressed solely by the row now being voided returns to `APPLIED`; and `ce_hours_applied` and `ce_satisfied` are recomputed with `MAKEUP_ATTRIBUTION` on every licence belonging to that originator, restoring the CE-hours conserved quantity exactly. Voiding places no precondition on the credit's current `credit_status`: a credit sitting in `EXCLUDED_SUCCESSIVE_YEAR` may be voided, and a credit already `VOIDED` may be voided again, the repeat simply overwriting `void_reason` with `ANALYST_VOID`. A `CE_CREDIT_VOIDED` audit entry records the correction.

Renewal is a two-step cycle, and the licence stays authorising throughout it. A `LicensingAnalyst` opens the cycle by filing a renewal request, advancing the licence `ACTIVE → RENEWAL_PENDING`. This requires the licence to be `ACTIVE` and the logical clock instant to fall at or after `renewal_window_opens_at` and strictly before `expiration_date`; an attempt outside that window is rejected with `ERR_RENEWAL_WINDOW_CLOSED` (HTTP 422). Filing the request recomputes `ce_hours_applied` and `ce_satisfied` using `CALENDAR_YEAR_ATTRIBUTION`, and it deliberately leaves `authorized_state_codes` unchanged, because `RENEWAL_PENDING` counts as an authorising status exactly as `ACTIVE` does. A `LICENSE_STATUS_CHANGED` entry is appended.

Completing the renewal closes the cycle, advancing `RENEWAL_PENDING → ACTIVE`. Before the gate is evaluated, `ce_hours_applied` and `ce_satisfied` are recomputed using `MAKEUP_ATTRIBUTION`. The completion then requires two things: that `ce_satisfied` is true after that recomputation, and that no CE credit counted toward the licence's `ce_reporting_year` — that is, no credit of that originator whose `attributed_year` equals that year — is sitting in `credit_status` `VOIDED`. Failing either is rejected with `ERR_CE_HOURS_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 422). On success the licence rolls forward: `expiration_date` advances by 365 days, `renewal_window_opens_at` advances by 365 days, `ce_reporting_year` advances by one, and `ce_hours_applied` and `ce_satisfied` are then reset and recomputed against that new reporting year — so a licence that has just renewed reads `ce_hours_applied` of `0` and `ce_satisfied` false unless credits already exist for the new year. `authority_effective_at` is deliberately left unchanged, because authority never lapsed during the renewal cycle. A `LICENSE_STATUS_CHANGED` entry is appended. Because this loop returns to `ACTIVE`, a licence may cycle `ACTIVE → RENEWAL_PENDING → ACTIVE` indefinitely.

**Reinstatement** is the second, quite different route back to `ACTIVE`, taken from `EXPIRED` by an analyst supplying an `action` of `REINSTATE`. It requires that the licence's `status` is `EXPIRED`, that `ce_satisfied` is true for the licence's current `ce_reporting_year`, that `reinstatement_count` is less than two, and that the owning originator's `employment_status` is `EMPLOYED`, evaluated in exactly that order. `ce_satisfied` is read from the licence row as it already stands — reinstatement recomputes nothing first — and a false flag is rejected with `ERR_CE_HOURS_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 422) ahead of the count check. A third reinstatement attempt on the same licence is rejected with `ERR_REINSTATEMENT_LIMIT_REACHED` (HTTP 422), and a non-`EMPLOYED` owner, having no code of its own, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409). On success `reinstatement_count` increments by one, `expiration_date` is set to the logical clock instant plus 365 days, `renewal_window_opens_at` is set to the logical clock instant plus 305 days, and — unlike the renewal loop — `authority_effective_at` is **re-stamped** from the logical clock. That re-stamping is the whole point: the pre-lapse authority interval is closed and does not retroactively authorise any application taken while the licence was `EXPIRED`. `authorized_state_codes` is recomputed and a `LICENSE_STATUS_CHANGED` entry is appended.

### 3.3. Designing, Approving and Assigning Compensation Plans

A compensation plan is drafted by a `CompensationDesigner` and can only be activated by someone else. Drafting requires a `plan_name` — the human-readable label quoted back in examination responses, between one and eighty characters — and the three figures that define the commission arithmetic: `base_basis_points`, the base originator commission expressed in basis points of the funded loan amount, an integer of at least 0 and at most 275; `min_fee_cents`, the per-loan commission floor in cents, zero or greater; and `max_fee_cents`, the per-loan commission ceiling in cents, which is likewise zero or greater and must be greater than or equal to `min_fee_cents` — a ceiling below the floor is part of this endpoint's declared input shape and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400), the comparison being made only once every single-field check has passed. Three optional inputs complete the design. `salary_cents_per_period` is the fixed, non-loan-dependent salary component paid per reporting period, defaulting to `0` and never negative; it is recorded for examination purposes and does not enter the per-loan commission arithmetic at all. `volume_tier_enabled`, false by default, decides whether the volume-tier basis-point adder described in §3.5 contributes anything — when it is false the adder is fixed at zero. `concession_absorption_enabled`, also false by default, decides whether an approved pricing concession reduces the originator's commission — when it is false a concession never touches commission and the exception's `comp_impact_cents` stays at `0` permanently.

A new plan starts in `DRAFT` with `prohibited_variable_count` at `0`, `approved_by_user_id` null, and its assignment fields `assigned_originator_id`, `assignment_effective_from` and `assignment_effective_to` all null. The system stamps `drafted_by_user_id` with the drafting designer's `X-User-ID`; that value is the anchor for the segregation-of-duties check at approval time.

Pay variables are declared against a draft plan one at a time, each carrying a `variable_type`, a `weight_pct` between 0 and 100, and a `justification_text` of between twenty and one thousand characters giving the designer's written basis for including it. Declaration requires the plan to be still `DRAFT`: a plan already `ACTIVE` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409) and one in the terminal `REJECTED` or `SUPERSEDED` with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` (HTTP 409). Fourteen variable types exist, and the system — not the designer — decides each one's `permissibility`. Six are `PERMISSIBLE` because they describe the originator's work rather than the loan's terms: `LOAN_VOLUME_UNITS`, `LOAN_VOLUME_DOLLARS`, `PERCENTAGE_OF_LOAN_AMOUNT`, `HOURS_WORKED`, `FILE_QUALITY_SCORE` and `EXISTING_BORROWER_FLAG`. Eight are `PROHIBITED` because they are terms of the transaction: `INTEREST_RATE`, `APR`, `LOAN_TERM_MONTHS`, `LTV_RATIO`, `CREDIT_SCORE`, `PRODUCT_TYPE`, `PREPAYMENT_PENALTY_FLAG` and `LOAN_PROFITABILITY`. A prohibited variable is nonetheless **accepted** into the draft rather than refused — the compliance office wants the attempt on the record — but declaring one raises the plan's recomputed `prohibited_variable_count` and appends an audit entry of type `PROHIBITED_VARIABLE_BLOCKED`. Every variable declaration increments the plan's `version` by one, and the plan's declared variables are readable as a sub-resource list of the plan, paged with the usual `limit` and `offset`. Each variable's `compensation_plan_id` is taken from the path segment naming its plan.

Approval is a `ComplianceOfficer` decision and forks two ways, driven by a `decision` of either `APPROVE` or `REJECT` accompanied by an optional `note` of at most two thousand characters. **Approval** advances the plan `DRAFT → ACTIVE` and is the only route to an assignable plan. It requires that the plan is still `DRAFT`; that `prohibited_variable_count` is exactly zero, so that any plan carrying even one term-of-the-transaction variable is unapprovable and is rejected with `ERR_PROHIBITED_COMP_VARIABLE` (HTTP 422); that the approving `X-User-ID` differs from `drafted_by_user_id`, with a self-approval rejected as `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 422); and that the plan has at least one declared variable whose `weight_pct` values sum to exactly one hundred, with any other total — including a plan with no variables at all — rejected as `ERR_PLAN_WEIGHTS_NOT_100` (HTTP 422). Where more than one of these fails at once, the order fixed in §2.9 decides: the prohibited-variable rejection is reported ahead of the segregation-of-duties rejection, which is itself reported ahead of the weight-sum rejection. On success `approved_by_user_id` is stamped with the approver's `X-User-ID`, the plan's `version` increments, and a `PLAN_APPROVED` audit entry is appended.

**Rejection** advances the plan `DRAFT → REJECTED` and is deliberately far less demanding: it requires only that the plan is `DRAFT` and that the rejecting `X-User-ID` differs from `drafted_by_user_id`. It checks neither `prohibited_variable_count` nor the weight sum, because a non-compliant plan must always be rejectable — refusing to record a rejection because the plan is bad would be perverse. On rejection `approved_by_user_id` is likewise stamped with the deciding officer's `X-User-ID`, every declared variable is left exactly as it stands so that the rejected design remains inspectable at examination, and a `PLAN_REJECTED` audit entry is appended. `REJECTED` is terminal, as is `SUPERSEDED`; a further decision on a plan in either state is rejected with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`.

Assignment binds an `ACTIVE` plan to one originator over a **closed** interval. A `ComplianceOfficer` supplies the `compensation_plan_id` of the plan, the `effective_from` and `effective_to` instants bounding the assignment inclusively at both ends, and the `expected_originator_version` stale-version guard of §2.7. The referenced plan's `status` must be `ACTIVE`; assigning a plan in `DRAFT`, `REJECTED` or `SUPERSEDED` is rejected with `ERR_PLAN_NOT_ACTIVE` (HTTP 422). `effective_to` must be strictly after `effective_from`, and an inverted or degenerate range is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE` (HTTP 422).

Assignment intervals on one originator may not intersect. Because both bounds are inclusive, the collision test is inclusive on both sides: the request is rejected with `ERR_PLAN_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409) when there exists a plan already assigned to this originator whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `SUPERSEDED` and whose `assignment_effective_to` is at or after the incoming `effective_from` while its `assignment_effective_from` is at or before the incoming `effective_to`. Note the deliberate contrast with sponsorship geometry in §3.1: here **adjacency is a collision** — an incoming `effective_from` exactly equal to an existing assignment's `assignment_effective_to` is rejected, because at most one assignment may cover any single instant.

A successful assignment writes `assigned_originator_id`, `assignment_effective_from` and `assignment_effective_to` onto the plan, sets the originator's `compensation_plan_id` to the assigned plan, and increments the originator's `version` by one. A plan is assignable to at most one originator. When the originator already carried an `ACTIVE` assigned plan, that plan moves `ACTIVE → SUPERSEDED` and an audit entry of type `PLAN_APPROVED` records the supersession in its `detail`; ledger lines already pinned to the superseded plan keep their own `compensation_plan_id` unchanged, because historic commission lines are never re-pointed at a newer plan. The response to an assignment is the updated originator record, so the caller can read the new `compensation_plan_id` and `version` directly; because an assignment mints no new row of its own — it writes onto the existing plan and originator — it is one of the state-advancing actions that return HTTP 200 under §4 rather than 201.

### 3.4. The Application Pipeline: Taking, Locking and Withdrawing

A loan application carries **two independent state dimensions on the same row**, and confusing them is the most common way to misread this specification. The `status` field tracks origination and moves through `TAKEN`, `LOCKED`, `CLOSED` and `PAID_OFF`, with `WITHDRAWN` as its only terminal state. The `compensation_status` field tracks the commission ledger and moves through `NOT_APPLICABLE`, `PENDING`, `ACCRUED`, `RECONCILED` and `CLAWED_BACK`. The two advance independently: a loan can be `PAID_OFF` while its compensation is still `PENDING`, and its compensation can be `RECONCILED` while its `status` is still `CLOSED`.

An `Originator` takes an application by supplying its own `originator_id`, the `state_code` of the subject property as an uppercase two-letter code, an opaque `borrower_reference` file reference of between one and forty characters used in examination responses, the `product_type` — one of `FIXED_30`, `FIXED_15`, `ARM_5_1`, `FHA_30` or `VA_30` — the `requested_amount_cents` on the application, an integer of at least 1,000,000 cents and at most 500,000,000 cents, and the `par_rate_bps`, the no-points rate available for the file at application time, in basis points, of at least 100 and at most 2,000. Note that `product_type` is a term of the transaction and therefore never enters any compensation arithmetic; it is captured for examination purposes only.

Intake is where authority is enforced, and three distinct rejections guard it in the order fixed in §2.9. First, the originator must hold a licence for that exact `state_code` whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `RENEWAL_PENDING`; when the only licence row for the state sits in `PENDING`, `EXPIRED`, `SURRENDERED` or `REVOKED`, and equally when no licence row exists for the state at all, the request is rejected with `ERR_LICENSE_NOT_ACTIVE` (HTTP 422). Second, the `state_code` must be present in the originator's `authorized_state_codes` at the logical clock instant, computed exactly as §2.8 specifies; this rejection, `ERR_STATE_NOT_AUTHORIZED` (HTTP 422), is what fires when the licence sits in an authorising status but the instant falls outside its half-open interval from `authority_effective_at` up to `expiration_date`, or when the originator's `employment_status` is anything other than `EMPLOYED`. Third, the authorising licence must have a sponsorship interval whose half-open span from `effective_at` up to `relinquished_at` covers the logical clock instant; a licence with no covering sponsorship is rejected with `ERR_SPONSORSHIP_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422).

A successfully taken application stamps `application_taken_at` from the logical clock — this is the instant the authority window check was evaluated against, and it is recorded so that a later examination can reconstruct the decision — and initialises `status` to `TAKEN`, `compensation_status` to `NOT_APPLICABLE` and `authority_flag` to `VERIFIED`. Its `note_rate_bps` and `locked_amount_cents` read null while the loan is `TAKEN`; its `funded_amount_cents`, `funded_at`, `payoff_date` and `closed_unit_index` read null until the loan closes or pays off; its `compensation_plan_id` reads null until close; and its `lender_paid_comp_cents`, `borrower_paid_comp_cents`, `file_quality_score` and `hours_worked_pct` all read `0` until they are reported at close. An `APPLICATION_TAKEN` audit entry is appended.

The `authority_flag` field is the loan's own record of whether the authority that justified it still holds. It reads `VERIFIED` while the authority window check passed at `application_taken_at` and has not since been invalidated, `SUSPENDED` after an authority-lapse cascade has reached the loan, and `UNAUTHORIZED` once an unlicensed-activity investigation on that loan has been substantiated.

Locking the rate advances the loan `TAKEN → LOCKED`. The originator supplies the `note_rate_bps` actually locked, in basis points, and the `locked_amount_cents` — the loan amount at rate lock, and the amount the provisional commission variant multiplies. Both are required integers of zero or greater and neither carries the tighter intake bounds placed on `par_rate_bps` and `requested_amount_cents`, so a `note_rate_bps` above two thousand and a `locked_amount_cents` below the intake minimum are both accepted. Locking requires the loan to be `TAKEN`, **re-verifies** authority from scratch at the current logical clock instant — all three of the licence-active, state-authorised and sponsorship-covered checks above are evaluated again, with the same three error codes — and requires its `authority_flag` to read `VERIFIED`. This matters because time has passed since intake — a licence may have expired, or a sponsorship may have been relinquished, between taking the file and locking it. The `authority_flag` precondition is not one of the numbered rules of §2.9 and is evaluated **after** the re-verification, so when authority has genuinely lapsed the lock reports the authority error of rules 13 through 15 rather than the flag failure — a lock attempted after the owning licence was revoked answers `ERR_LICENSE_NOT_ACTIVE`, even though the lapse cascade has by then already set the flag to `SUSPENDED`. The flag precondition therefore decides the response only where authority is otherwise intact at the current instant and the flag alone is not `VERIFIED`, and it is then rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409). The lock leaves `compensation_status` untouched, since the two dimensions are independent, and increments the loan's `version` by one.

Withdrawal is available from either of the two live origination states: an originator may withdraw a loan whose `status` is `TAKEN` or `LOCKED`, advancing it to `WITHDRAWN`. Withdrawal cascades to pricing: every pricing exception on that loan whose `status` is `SUBMITTED` moves to `WITHDRAWN` as well, and the loan's `compensation_status` is set to `NOT_APPLICABLE`. `WITHDRAWN` is the origination dimension's terminal state, so any further action on the loan is rejected with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`.

### 3.5. Closing a Loan, Provisional Accrual and Withheld Compensation

Closing is the `LoanOperations` desk's work and is where commission first enters the ledger. The desk supplies the `funded_amount_cents` actually funded, the `funded_at` funding instant — which becomes day zero of the clawback window — the `lender_paid_comp_cents` and `borrower_paid_comp_cents` originator compensation reported at close, the `file_quality_score` for the file as an integer from 0 to 100, and the `hours_worked_pct` giving the percentage of a full period the originator worked on this file, also 0 to 100; `funded_amount_cents`, `lender_paid_comp_cents` and `borrower_paid_comp_cents` are each a required integer of zero or greater carrying no ceiling of their own. The loan must be `LOCKED`. The originator must carry a plan at all — a null `compensation_plan_id` on the originator is rejected with `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_COMP_PLAN` (HTTP 422) — that plan's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, rejected otherwise with `ERR_PLAN_NOT_ACTIVE`, and the plan's closed assignment interval from `assignment_effective_from` through `assignment_effective_to`, inclusive at both ends, must contain the supplied `funded_at` — a `funded_at` outside that interval, and equally a plan carrying no assignment interval at all, is rejected with `ERR_PLAN_NOT_ACTIVE` as well.

Two fiscal gates apply at close. Reporting **both** a positive `lender_paid_comp_cents` and a positive `borrower_paid_comp_cents` on the same file is dual compensation. It is permitted-but-monitored while the originator's `monitoring_status` is `STANDARD`, and hard-blocked while that status is `ELEVATED`, where the close is rejected with `ERR_DUAL_COMPENSATION_PROHIBITED` (HTTP 422). Separately, the sum of `lender_paid_comp_cents` and `borrower_paid_comp_cents` must not exceed the assigned plan's `max_fee_cents`; exceeding it is rejected with `ERR_COMP_CAP_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). Per §2.9, the dual-compensation rejection is reported ahead of the cap rejection when both apply.

On success the loan's origination dimension advances `LOCKED → CLOSED`. The loan pins its `compensation_plan_id` from the originator's current `compensation_plan_id`, freezing the plan that governs this file even if the originator is reassigned later. It also receives its `closed_unit_index`: the one-based chronological ordinal of this loan among the originator's closed loans, computed as one plus the count of that originator's loans already sitting in `status` `CLOSED` or `PAID_OFF`, with the ordering taken by `funded_at` ascending and then `created_at` ascending. This ordinal is null until the loan closes and is the unit count the provisional commission tier reads.

The compensation dimension then **forks on the loan's `authority_flag`**, and the two branches are mutually exclusive:

- **Branch A — authority intact.** When `authority_flag` reads `VERIFIED`, `compensation_status` is set to `ACCRUED` and exactly one ledger line is created for the loan with `variant` `PROVISIONAL`, computed as described below. A `COMP_ACCRUED` audit entry is appended.
- **Branch B — authority in doubt.** When `authority_flag` reads anything other than `VERIFIED` — that is, `SUSPENDED` or `UNAUTHORIZED` — `compensation_status` is set to `PENDING` and **no** ledger line is created at all. Commission is withheld rather than accrued, and no `COMP_ACCRUED` entry is appended.

Either way the close recomputes the originator's `lifetime_accrued_cents` and `net_compensation_cents`, recomputes the originator's `exposure_score` and `monitoring_status` as §3.7 describes, and increments both the originator's `version` and the loan's `version` by one.

The **volume-tier basis-point adder** is the first input to commission and is computed fresh for every ledger line written. When the governing plan's `volume_tier_enabled` is false the adder is zero and nothing further is evaluated. When it is true, the adder is assembled in a strictly pinned order from a unit count supplied by the calling variant and two permissible variables reported on the loan:

1. **The unit tier.** A unit count of four or fewer yields a tier value of zero basis points. A count of five through nine — that is, more than four but no more than nine — yields fifteen basis points. Ten or more yields thirty basis points.
2. **The quality penalty.** A `file_quality_score` of ninety or more incurs no penalty. A score of at least seventy but below ninety incurs a penalty of ten basis points. A score below seventy incurs a penalty of twenty-five basis points.
3. **The clamp, applied in this order.** Subtract the quality penalty from the tier value; take the smaller of that result and thirty basis points; then take the larger of that result and zero. The intermediate order matters and must not be rearranged.
4. **The hours cap.** When the loan's `hours_worked_pct` is strictly below fifty, the adder is additionally capped at fifteen basis points — the smaller of the value from step 3 and fifteen. When `hours_worked_pct` is fifty or more, step 3's value stands.

The resulting adder is stored on the ledger line as `tier_bps_adder`. Nothing else feeds it: `INTEREST_RATE`, `APR`, `LTV_RATIO`, `CREDIT_SCORE`, `PRODUCT_TYPE`, `LOAN_TERM_MONTHS`, `PREPAYMENT_PENALTY_FLAG` and `LOAN_PROFITABILITY` are terms of the transaction and never enter this computation — their appearance as a declared pay variable is a design-time violation caught at plan approval, not a runtime input.

**Per-line commission** is then computed as follows. The effective rate in basis points is the plan's `base_basis_points` plus the `tier_bps_adder`. That effective rate is applied to a loan amount supplied by the variant by multiplying the amount in cents by the effective basis points and dividing by ten thousand; the result is kept at exact precision through the multiplication and division and only then rounded half up to a whole number of cents, giving `base_comp_cents`. `base_comp_cents` is then clamped into the plan's fee band: take the larger of it and the plan's `min_fee_cents`, then take the smaller of that and the plan's `max_fee_cents`. Next, `comp_impact_cents` on the line is zero unless the governing plan's `concession_absorption_enabled` is true, in which case it takes the `comp_impact_cents` of the approved pricing exception on that loan — where more than one exception on the loan has reached `APPROVED`, the earliest by `created_at` is the one read, the earlier-written row winning a tie — and remains zero when the loan has no approved exception. The line's `accrued_cents` is `base_comp_cents` less `comp_impact_cents`, and that difference is not floored at zero: a line whose absorbed impact exceeds its clamped commission carries a negative `accrued_cents`. A line's `paid_cents` reads `0` while its `status` is `ACCRUED` and equals its `accrued_cents` once the line reaches `RECONCILED`; `clawback_cents` reads `0` unless the line has been clawed back; and `days_held` and `recapture_pct` both read null until a payoff is recorded against the loan.

The **provisional variant** — the one written at close — supplies those two inputs chronologically: the amount is the loan's `locked_amount_cents`, the amount at rate lock, and the unit count is the loan's `closed_unit_index` less one, that is, the originator's closed-unit count strictly *before* this loan. The tier a provisional line earns is therefore the tier the originator had already earned when the file closed. Every provisional line is created with `variant` `PROVISIONAL` and `status` `ACCRUED`, and it copies the loan's `loan_application_id`, the originator's `originator_id` and the pinned `compensation_plan_id` onto itself; none of those three is client-supplied.

Compensation withheld at close is released later by a `ComplianceOfficer`, advancing the loan's compensation dimension `PENDING → ACCRUED`. Release requires that `compensation_status` is `PENDING`, that the loan's `status` is `CLOSED` or `PAID_OFF`, and — the cross-aggregate guard — that **every** unlicensed-activity investigation naming that loan has reached `UNSUBSTANTIATED` or `REMEDIATED`. The loan's own `status` is examined first: a `WITHDRAWN` loan is rejected with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` (HTTP 409), while a loan still `TAKEN` or `LOCKED`, and equally a closed loan whose `compensation_status` is anything other than `PENDING`, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409). While any such investigation is still `OPEN` or `SUBSTANTIATED`, the release is rejected with `ERR_UNLICENSED_ACTIVITY_OPEN` (HTTP 422). On release the loan's `authority_flag` is set to `VERIFIED`, one ledger line with `variant` `PROVISIONAL` is created using exactly the provisional inputs described above, the originator's `lifetime_accrued_cents` and `net_compensation_cents` are recomputed along with `exposure_score` and `monitoring_status`, a `COMP_ACCRUED` audit entry is appended, and the loan's `version` increments by one.

### 3.6. Pricing Exceptions, Documented Justification and Concession Absorption

When a borrower needs a better price than the rate sheet gives, the originator files a pricing exception against the locked file. The request carries the `requested_rate_bps` the originator asks to deliver to the borrower, in basis points, at least 100 and at most 2,000; the `concession_cents` lender credit requested, at least 1 cent and at most 2,000,000 cents; a `reason_code` drawn from `COMPETITIVE_MATCH`, `SERVICE_ISSUE`, `PRICING_ERROR`, `UNDERWRITING_DELAY` and `RELATIONSHIP_PRICING`; and the `justification_text` retained for examination, of between forty and two thousand characters. The exception's `loan_application_id` comes from the path segment naming the loan, not from the body. The named loan must still be in `status` `LOCKED`: a loan in `TAKEN`, `CLOSED` or `PAID_OFF` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409) and a `WITHDRAWN` one with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` (HTTP 409), both ahead of the justification-length and concession-cap gates below.

Documentation expectations are not uniform across reasons. A `PRICING_ERROR` exception — the office's own mistake — demands a heavier written record: its `justification_text` must run to at least one hundred and twenty characters, and a shorter-but-schema-valid justification is rejected with `ERR_JUSTIFICATION_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 422). The generic forty-character floor that applies to every reason code is enforced earlier, as a request-shape violation reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

The concession itself is capped at two percent of the file. Precisely, `concession_cents` must not exceed the loan's `locked_amount_cents` multiplied by two, divided by one hundred, and then floored to a whole cent; anything above that ceiling is rejected with `ERR_CONCESSION_EXCEEDS_CAP` (HTTP 422). Per the order in §2.9, the justification-length rejection is reported ahead of the concession-cap rejection when both apply.

A filed exception starts in `SUBMITTED` with `comp_impact_cents` at `0`, `requested_by_user_id` stamped from the filing originator's `X-User-ID`, and `decided_by_user_id`, `second_decided_by_user_id`, `decided_at` and `decision_note` all null. One further field is stamped at filing: `requires_second_approval` is set true when the owning originator's `monitoring_status` reads `ELEVATED` at that instant, and false otherwise. That flag can also flip to true later, without the exception being touched, when the originator escalates to `ELEVATED` while the exception is still `SUBMITTED` — see the payoff cascade in §3.7.

Deciding an exception is a `ComplianceOfficer` action carrying a `decision` of either `APPROVE` or `DENY`, a required `decision_note` recording the reviewer's reasoning, of between one and two thousand characters, and optionally a `second_approver_user_id` of at most sixty-four characters. The two branches are deliberately asymmetric.

**Approval** advances the exception `SUBMITTED → APPROVED` and is the only branch that touches compensation. It requires that the exception is still `SUBMITTED`; that the loan it names is still in `status` `LOCKED`, any other loan status — the terminal `WITHDRAWN` included, since the terminal-state gate is on the exception rather than on the loan — being rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409); that the deciding `X-User-ID` differs from `requested_by_user_id`; that, when `requires_second_approval` is true, the request carries a non-empty `second_approver_user_id` — a value that is absent, empty or only whitespace all count alike as missing — whose value differs from **both** the deciding `X-User-ID` and `requested_by_user_id`; and that the owning originator's `compensation_plan_id` is non-null with that plan's `status` equal to `ACTIVE`. A missing second approver on an exception that requires one is rejected with `ERR_SECOND_APPROVAL_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422); a second approver who is the decider or the requester, and equally a decider who is the requester, is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 422) — and per §2.9 the segregation rejection is reported ahead of the missing-second-approver rejection. A plan that is absent or not `ACTIVE` is rejected with `ERR_PLAN_NOT_ACTIVE`. On approval the system writes `decided_by_user_id` from the deciding `X-User-ID`, stamps `decided_at` from the logical clock, stores the supplied `decision_note`, sets `second_decided_by_user_id` to the supplied `second_approver_user_id` when `requires_second_approval` is true and to null otherwise, computes `comp_impact_cents` as described below, recomputes the originator's `exposure_score` and `monitoring_status`, appends an `EXCEPTION_DECIDED` audit entry, and increments the exception's `version` by one.

**Denial** advances the exception `SUBMITTED → DENIED` and requires only that the exception is `SUBMITTED` and that the deciding `X-User-ID` differs from `requested_by_user_id`. It deliberately does **not** require the loan to still be `LOCKED`, does not require a second approver even when `requires_second_approval` is true, and does not require an active plan — a denial must always be recordable, whatever has happened to the file since. Denial writes `decided_by_user_id`, `decided_at` and `decision_note` exactly as approval does, leaves `comp_impact_cents` at `0`, and appends an `EXCEPTION_DECIDED` audit entry.

The requesting originator may also retract its own request, advancing the exception `SUBMITTED → WITHDRAWN`; this requires the exception to be `SUBMITTED` and the acting `X-User-ID` to equal `requested_by_user_id`, and it leaves `comp_impact_cents` at `0`. All three of `APPROVED`, `DENIED` and `WITHDRAWN` are terminal, so a second decision on a decided exception is rejected with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`.

**Concession absorption** decides how much of an approved concession the originator personally absorbs, and its evaluation order is pinned because the arithmetic looks circular: the absorption bracket is keyed on the commission, and the commission is then reduced by the absorption. The bracket is decided from the **pre-absorption** commission only, and feeding the reduced figure back into the bracket test is forbidden and produces a different number. The five steps run in this order:

1. Compute the pre-absorption commission from the locked amount: multiply the loan's `locked_amount_cents` by the plan's `base_basis_points`, divide by ten thousand, round half up to whole cents, then clamp by taking the larger of that and the plan's `min_fee_cents` and then the smaller of the result and the plan's `max_fee_cents`. The volume-tier adder is deliberately **not** applied here, because `file_quality_score` and `hours_worked_pct` are only reported at close and both still read `0` at decision time.
2. Decide the bracket from that step-1 figure alone: it is the high bracket when the figure is at least three hundred thousand cents — that is, three thousand dollars of commission — and the low bracket otherwise.
3. Take the absorption share from the bracket: fifty percent in the high bracket, twenty-five percent in the low bracket.
4. Compute the originator's share of the concession: multiply the exception's `concession_cents` by that percentage, divide by one hundred, and round half up to whole cents. This is the exception's `comp_impact_cents`.
5. Report the net commission as the step-1 figure less the step-4 figure. This net figure is informational and is never fed back into step 2.

Rounding happens exactly twice — once at step 1 and once at step 4 — and nowhere else. When the governing plan's `concession_absorption_enabled` is false the whole absorption is skipped: `comp_impact_cents` is `0` and the reported net commission is simply the step-1 figure. The plan used is the one referenced by the originator's `compensation_plan_id` at decision time, which is why an `ACTIVE` plan is a precondition of approval; the loan must be `LOCKED` so that `locked_amount_cents` is non-null.

### 3.7. The Commission Ledger: Reconciliation, Early Payoff, Clawback and Steering Exposure

Provisional commission is an estimate; reconciliation is the truth. A `ComplianceOfficer` runs reconciliation for one originator at a time, and the run takes no request body at all. The eligible set is every loan application belonging to that originator whose `status` is `CLOSED` or `PAID_OFF` **and** whose `compensation_status` is `ACCRUED` at the instant the run executes. Loans whose `compensation_status` is `NOT_APPLICABLE`, `PENDING`, `RECONCILED` or `CLAWED_BACK` are skipped entirely. Eligible lines are processed in a deterministic order: by the owning loan's `funded_at` ascending, then by the owning loan's `created_at` ascending, never by identifier.

For each eligible loan the run rewrites that loan's existing provisional ledger line in place rather than creating a second one. The line's `variant` flips `PROVISIONAL → FINAL`; its `tier_bps_adder`, `base_comp_cents` and `accrued_cents` are recomputed using the final variant's inputs; its `status` advances `ACCRUED → RECONCILED`; its `paid_cents` is set equal to its freshly computed `accrued_cents`, which is what makes the money recapturable later; and its `reconciled_at` is stamped from the logical clock. The owning loan's `compensation_status` advances `ACCRUED → RECONCILED` in step. The line the run rewrites for a given loan is that loan's ledger line whose own `status` is `ACCRUED`, its owning loan's `status` already being `CLOSED` or `PAID_OFF` by virtue of the eligible set; an otherwise-eligible loan that carries no such line, or whose line names a plan that no longer resolves, is skipped silently and contributes to neither `reconciled_count` nor the reported totals. A `COMP_RECONCILED` audit entry is appended, and the originator's `lifetime_accrued_cents`, `net_compensation_cents`, `exposure_score` and `monitoring_status` are all recomputed, incrementing the originator's `version` by one.

The **final variant** differs from the provisional variant in both of its inputs, and that difference is the whole reason reconciliation exists. The amount it multiplies is the loan's `funded_amount_cents` — the amount actually funded — rather than `locked_amount_cents`. And the unit count it feeds into the volume tier is the count of the originator's loan applications whose `status` is `CLOSED` or `PAID_OFF` at the instant the reconciliation run executes, rather than the loan's own `closed_unit_index` less one. The final unit count is therefore **forward-looking**: loans that closed *after* this one raise this loan's tier. Identical loan data consequently yields a different `accrued_cents` under the final variant whenever `locked_amount_cents` differs from `funded_amount_cents`, or whenever the period's unit count has crossed a tier boundary since the file closed. Every other step of the per-line arithmetic — the effective basis points, the exact-then-round-half-up division by ten thousand, the clamp between `min_fee_cents` and `max_fee_cents`, and the subtraction of `comp_impact_cents` — is identical to the provisional case in §3.5.

Rounding order in the roll-up is pinned and is a frequent source of off-by-one totals. Each line's `accrued_cents` is rounded to whole cents on its own, and the run's reported total is the sum of those **already-rounded** per-line amounts. Summing the exact fractional values across lines and rounding once at the end is forbidden and yields a different total. The run reports `originator_id`, a `reconciled_count` giving how many lines it rewrote, a `total_final_cents` equal to the sum of the rewritten lines' new `accrued_cents`, a `total_delta_cents` equal to that total less the sum of those same lines' pre-run `accrued_cents`, and the `compensation_record_ids` of the lines it touched. When the eligible set is empty the run still succeeds, returning `reconciled_count` of `0`, `total_final_cents` and `total_delta_cents` of `0` and an empty `compensation_record_ids`, rather than an error — and the originator's `version` still moves by one, because the closing recompute of `exposure_score` and `monitoring_status` runs unconditionally.

**Early payoff** is where commission comes back. The `LoanOperations` desk posts a payoff by supplying the `payoff_date`, and the loan advances `CLOSED → PAID_OFF`. The loan must be `CLOSED`, and the supplied `payoff_date` must be at or after the loan's `funded_at`; an earlier payoff date is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE` (HTTP 422). The recapture arithmetic then runs. The number of whole days the loan was held is the elapsed time from `funded_at` to `payoff_date`, expressed in seconds and divided by eighty-six thousand four hundred, floored to a whole day — so a payoff at eighty-nine days and twenty-three hours counts as eighty-nine days, not ninety. That day count becomes `days_held`. A `days_held` of ninety days or fewer sets `recapture_pct` to one hundred percent; a `days_held` above ninety but no more than one hundred and eighty days sets it to fifty percent; anything longer sets `recapture_pct` to zero. The amount recaptured, `clawback_cents`, is the line's `paid_cents` multiplied by `recapture_pct`, divided by one hundred, rounded half up to whole cents.

Recapture reaches exactly one ledger line: the row for that loan whose `variant` is `FINAL` and whose `status` is `RECONCILED`. A line still sitting in `status` `ACCRUED` has never been reconciled, so its `paid_cents` reads `0`, nothing is recaptured from it at all, and the loan's `compensation_status` simply stays `ACCRUED` through the payoff — that line is left entirely alone, its `days_held` and `recapture_pct` still null and its `version` unmoved, and no exposure recompute runs. When the reconciled line does exist, `days_held` and `recapture_pct` are written onto it and its `version` moves by one before the recapture share is examined, so a payoff beyond one hundred and eighty days still records both figures on the line and then stops there: the line keeps `status` `RECONCILED`, its `clawback_cents` stays `0`, the loan's `compensation_status` stays `RECONCILED`, none of the five hops below runs, and no audit entry is appended.

When a reconciled line does exist and `recapture_pct` comes out greater than zero, posting the payoff drives a **five-hop cascade**, each hop caused by the previous hop's mutation rather than by the payoff call itself:

1. Because the loan's `status` became `PAID_OFF`, its `compensation_status` advances `RECONCILED → CLAWED_BACK`.
2. Because the loan's compensation dimension moved, the loan's `FINAL` ledger line advances `RECONCILED → CLAWED_BACK`, writing `days_held`, `recapture_pct` and `clawback_cents` onto the line. `CLAWED_BACK` is the ledger line's terminal state.
3. Because the line now carries a `clawback_cents`, the originator's `lifetime_clawed_back_cents` and `net_compensation_cents` are recomputed from it, so that `net_compensation_cents` remains exactly `lifetime_accrued_cents` less `lifetime_clawed_back_cents` with zero drift. When every one of an originator's lines is recaptured at one hundred percent, `net_compensation_cents` returns to exactly the value it held before those loans closed.
4. Because the ledger moved, the originator's `exposure_score` is recomputed, and `monitoring_status` flips to `ELEVATED` as soon as that score reaches sixty.
5. Because supervision escalated, **every** pricing exception whose `status` is `SUBMITTED` and whose loan belongs to this same originator has its `requires_second_approval` set to true — retroactively raising the approval bar on work already in flight. Those exceptions are visited in `created_at` order, and one already carrying true is left exactly as it stands, so its `updated_at` and `version` do not move. This hop fires only when hop 4 actually changed `monitoring_status`.

The audit trail records a `COMP_CLAWED_BACK` entry against the recaptured ledger line, its `detail` carrying the `loan_application_id`, `days_held`, `recapture_pct` and `clawback_cents`, followed — when hop 4 changed `monitoring_status`, and after hop 5 has raised its flags — by a `MONITORING_ESCALATED` entry against the originator whose `detail` carries the new `exposure_score` and `monitoring_status`. Both are cascade hops rather than the payoff the caller directly requested, so both carry `actor_role` `SYSTEM` and a null `actor_user_id`, and a payoff appends no caller-attributed entry of its own. The loan's `version` increments by one, as does the `version` of every other row the cascade touched.

**Steering and dual-compensation exposure** is a single integer score between `0` and `100` on the originator, recomputed after any event that could move it: at close, at compensation release, at reconciliation, on an approved pricing-exception decision, and as hop 4 of the payoff cascade. It reads only the originator's loans whose `status` is `CLOSED` or `PAID_OFF`; loans in `TAKEN`, `LOCKED` or `WITHDRAWN` never enter any term, so a `SUBMITTED` exception on a merely locked loan does not move the score at all. The ledger terms read `lifetime_accrued_cents` and `lifetime_clawed_back_cents` as they stand *after* the mutation that triggered the recompute.

When the originator has no closed or paid-off loans, the score is zero and `monitoring_status` is `STANDARD`; the computation stops there, and no division is attempted. Otherwise, with the closed set's size as the denominator, four terms are assembled:

- The **dual-compensation ratio** is one hundred times the number of closed loans reporting both a positive `lender_paid_comp_cents` and a positive `borrower_paid_comp_cents`, divided by the number of closed loans, kept at exact precision.
- The **average rate spread** is, for each closed loan, the greater of zero and that loan's `note_rate_bps` less its `par_rate_bps`; those per-loan spreads are totalled and divided by the number of closed loans, and the result is then capped at one hundred basis points by taking the smaller of the two.
- The **exception rate** is one hundred times the number of closed loans that have at least one pricing exception in `status` `APPROVED`, divided by the number of closed loans, kept at exact precision.
- The **clawback ratio** is zero when the originator's `lifetime_accrued_cents` is zero; otherwise it is one hundred times `lifetime_clawed_back_cents` divided by `lifetime_accrued_cents`, and it is then capped at one hundred by taking the smaller of the two.

Those four terms are weighted and added: the dual-compensation ratio at thirty-five hundredths, the capped average spread at twenty-five hundredths, the exception rate at fifteen hundredths, and the capped clawback ratio at twenty-five hundredths. **One** rounding half up is applied to the fully assembled weighted sum; rounding the four terms individually and then adding them is forbidden and yields a different score. The rounded figure is then clamped by taking the larger of it and zero and the smaller of that and one hundred, and stored as `exposure_score`. Finally, `monitoring_status` is set to `ELEVATED` when `exposure_score` is sixty or more, and to `STANDARD` otherwise. The consequences of `ELEVATED` are felt in two places already described: dual compensation becomes a hard block at close, and new pricing exceptions are stamped as requiring a second approver.

### 3.8. Advertising Disclosure Review and Publication

Advertising is where a licensing failure becomes public, so every piece of copy passes through a disclosure inspection before it can run. An `Originator` drafts an advertisement by supplying its own `originator_id`, the `medium` it will be distributed through — one of `WEB`, `PRINT`, `SOCIAL`, `DIRECT_MAIL` or `RADIO` — the full `content_text` of the copy, between ten and four thousand characters, and the `target_state_codes` it will be distributed in, a required list of between one and ten uppercase two-letter codes. Two optional arrays declare what the copy actually prints: `disclosed_nmls_ids` lists the NMLS unique identifiers appearing in the copy as strings, and `disclosed_license_numbers` lists the state licence numbers appearing in the copy as strings; both default to empty lists. Each must be an array of strings when supplied, but neither its entry count nor its entry format is constrained, so an NMLS identifier belonging to nobody and a licence number matching no licence row are both accepted verbatim; `target_state_codes` is likewise stored exactly as supplied, neither sorted nor de-duplicated. A new advertisement starts in `DRAFT` with an empty `disclosure_findings`, a null `reviewed_by_user_id` and a null `published_at`.

Submitting the draft advances it `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` and requires the submitting `X-User-ID` to be the advertisement's own `originator_id`. Submission runs the **disclosure inspection** and stores its verdict in `disclosure_findings`, a list of finding codes that is empty when the copy is compliant; submission itself appends no audit entry, `AD_DECIDED` being written only by a review or a withdrawal. The inspection produces codes as follows, reading the advertisement's own fields plus the originator's licences that are in `status` `ACTIVE` or `RENEWAL_PENDING` at the logical clock instant:

- When the originator's `nmls_unique_id` does not appear among the entries of `disclosed_nmls_ids`, the finding `MISSING_NMLS_ID` is produced.
- Each entry of `target_state_codes` is then examined, iterated in ascending string order. For a given state the inspection looks for the originator's licence in that state whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `RENEWAL_PENDING`; where more than one such licence row exists, the one with the greatest `created_at` is taken — with the later-written row winning a tie — never the greatest identifier. If no such licence exists, the finding is `NO_LICENSE_` immediately followed by that two-letter state code. If the licence exists but its `license_number` does not appear among the entries of `disclosed_license_numbers`, the finding is `MISSING_LICENSE_NUMBER_` immediately followed by that state code. A state that has a licence and whose licence number is disclosed produces no finding.
- When the `medium` is `RADIO` and the `content_text` runs longer than three hundred characters, the finding `RADIO_SCRIPT_TOO_LONG` is produced.
- When the `medium` is `WEB` or `SOCIAL`, and the `content_text` contains the trigger phrase `no closing cost` matched without regard to letter case, and the `content_text` does **not** contain `APR` matched with regard to letter case, the finding `TRIGGER_TERM_WITHOUT_APR` is produced.

The assembled finding list is de-duplicated and then sorted in ascending string order before being stored, so `disclosure_findings` is always deterministic.

Review is a `ComplianceOfficer` decision carrying a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `REJECT` plus an optional `note` of at most two thousand characters, and the inspection is re-run as part of it, its freshly computed verdict written onto the advertisement before either branch is taken — so a refused approval still leaves the recomputed `disclosure_findings` on the row, while that row's `status`, `reviewed_by_user_id`, `updated_at` and `version` all stay exactly as they stood. **Approval** advances the advertisement `SUBMITTED → APPROVED` and requires that `disclosure_findings` is empty after that recomputation; a non-empty list is rejected with `ERR_AD_DISCLOSURE_INCOMPLETE` (HTTP 422). **Rejection** advances it `SUBMITTED → REJECTED` and carries no such requirement — non-compliant copy must always be rejectable. Both branches stamp `reviewed_by_user_id` with the reviewing officer's `X-User-ID` and append an `AD_DECIDED` audit entry whose `detail` carries the `decision`, the resulting `status` and the recomputed `disclosure_findings`, plus the `note` when one was supplied.

Rejected copy is revised rather than re-drafted. The owning originator supplies a replacement `content_text` — required, and bounded exactly as at drafting — and optionally replacement `disclosed_nmls_ids` and `disclosed_license_numbers`, an omitted array leaving the previously declared list untouched rather than clearing it, while `medium` and `target_state_codes` are not revisable at all and any value supplied for them is ignored, advancing the advertisement `REJECTED → DRAFT`; the revision requires the acting `X-User-ID` to be the advertisement's `originator_id`, clears `disclosure_findings` back to an empty list — the inspection will run again at the next submission — and resets `reviewed_by_user_id` to null. The `REJECTED → DRAFT → SUBMITTED` loop is unbounded: an advertisement may go round it as many times as it takes.

Publication advances an approved advertisement `APPROVED → PUBLISHED` and adds a live authority check: **every** state code in `target_state_codes` must be present in the originator's `authorized_state_codes` at the logical clock instant, and a target state the originator is no longer authorised in is rejected with `ERR_STATE_NOT_AUTHORIZED` (HTTP 422). Publishing copy whose recomputed `disclosure_findings` is non-empty is likewise rejected with `ERR_AD_DISCLOSURE_INCOMPLETE` — the authority check runs first, per rules 14 and 22 of §2.9, so a publication failing both reports `ERR_STATE_NOT_AUTHORIZED` and leaves `disclosure_findings` exactly as it stood, the inspection never having been reached; where the copy does reach the inspection, its recomputed findings are written onto the row even when publication is then refused. Publication declares no input field at all, so whatever body arrives is ignored. On success `published_at` is stamped from the logical clock, `reviewed_by_user_id` keeps the reviewing officer it already carried, and no audit entry is appended.

Compliance can pull an advertisement at any point after it leaves draft: a `ComplianceOfficer` may withdraw one whose `status` is `SUBMITTED`, `APPROVED` or `PUBLISHED`, optionally supplying a `reason` of at most two thousand characters — the whole body is optional here, so a withdrawal may carry none at all — advancing it to `WITHDRAWN` and appending an `AD_DECIDED` audit entry whose `detail` carries `status` `WITHDRAWN` plus that `reason` when one was supplied. A withdrawal neither re-runs the disclosure inspection nor stamps `reviewed_by_user_id`, and an advertisement still in `DRAFT` — or sitting in `REJECTED` — is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409). `WITHDRAWN` is terminal.

### 3.9. Examinations, Unlicensed Activity and the Nightly Compliance Epoch

When a state regulator opens an examination, an `ExaminationCoordinator` logs it. The inquiry carries the `state_code` of the examining regulator, the regulator's `exam_reference` number — between four and thirty-two characters and globally unique across all inquiries, with a duplicate rejected as `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY` — and the `response_due_at` deadline, which must fall strictly after the logical clock instant at the moment the inquiry is recorded; that comparison belongs to this endpoint's declared input shape, so a deadline at or before the clock is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) rather than `ERR_INVALID_DATE_RANGE`, and it is evaluated ahead of the `exam_reference` uniqueness check. An `originator_id` may optionally be supplied to name the examined individual; omitted — or supplied as anything other than a string, which is treated exactly as if it were absent — it stays null and the inquiry is institution-level. A new inquiry starts in `OPEN` with `opened_at` stamped from the logical clock, `response_round_count` at `0`, `last_response_at` null and `escalation_reason` null.

Responses are filed with a `narrative`, a required string of at most eight thousand characters carrying no minimum length, and the same action serves both the first response and every later one: an inquiry whose `status` is `OPEN` or `INFO_REQUESTED` advances to `RESPONSE_SUBMITTED`, provided the logical clock instant is at or before `response_due_at`. Filing after the deadline is rejected with `ERR_RESPONSE_DEADLINE_PASSED` (HTTP 422). Every entry into `RESPONSE_SUBMITTED` increments `response_round_count` by one — including the cyclic re-entries described next — and stamps `last_response_at` from the logical clock.

A coordinator responds to a regulator's follow-up by recording a further-information request with a `request_note`, likewise a required string of at most eight thousand characters, and this action forks on the round count. While `response_round_count` is fewer than three, the inquiry advances `RESPONSE_SUBMITTED → INFO_REQUESTED` and its `response_due_at` is reset to the logical clock instant plus fourteen days, opening a new response round. Once `response_round_count` has reached three, the same action instead advances the inquiry `RESPONSE_SUBMITTED → ESCALATED` and sets `escalation_reason` to `ROUND_LIMIT_REACHED`. The `RESPONSE_SUBMITTED → INFO_REQUESTED → RESPONSE_SUBMITTED` cycle is otherwise unbounded. `escalation_reason` reads null on an inquiry that has not escalated and otherwise carries either `ROUND_LIMIT_REACHED` or `DEADLINE_MISSED`.

Closing an inquiry is permitted from either `RESPONSE_SUBMITTED` or `ESCALATED` and advances it to `CLOSED`, which is terminal — no further transition of any kind is permitted afterwards, and an attempt is rejected with `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`.

**Unlicensed-activity investigations** arrive by two routes. The authority-lapse cascade of §3.1 and the nightly sweep open them automatically, with `opened_by` set to `CASCADE` and `trigger_reason` forced to `AUTHORITY_LAPSE`. A `ComplianceOfficer` can also open one by hand, supplying the `loan_application_id` of the application allegedly taken without authority and a `trigger_reason` drawn from `AUTHORITY_LAPSE`, `STATE_NOT_AUTHORIZED`, `SPONSORSHIP_GAP` and `MANUAL_REFERRAL`, with `MANUAL_REFERRAL` being the code for a plain referral. No precondition is placed on the named loan's own `status`: an investigation may be opened by hand against a loan in any status, the terminal `WITHDRAWN` included. A manually opened investigation records `opened_by` as `MANUAL`, copies its `originator_id` from the named loan rather than accepting one from the caller, stamps `detected_at` from the logical clock, starts in `status` `OPEN`, carries `restitution_cents` `0` with `finding_by_user_id` and `remediated_at` null, and triggers a recompute of the originator's `open_investigation_count` — which at all times equals the number of that originator's investigations whose `status` is `OPEN` or `SUBSTANTIATED`, and which is derived on every read rather than stored, so the opening writes nothing to the originator row and leaves its `updated_at` and `version` untouched. A manual opening appends no audit entry either: `INVESTIGATION_OPENED` is written only by the authority-lapse cascade and the nightly sweep. Cascade-created investigations are discoverable only through the investigation list, filtered by `opened_by` or by the loan they name.

Entering a finding closes the question and rewrites the loan's authority record. The officer supplies a `finding` of either `SUBSTANTIATED` or `UNSUBSTANTIATED` together with a `restitution_cents` amount — a required integer of zero or greater carrying no ceiling of its own — and the two must agree: a `SUBSTANTIATED` finding requires `restitution_cents` of at least 1, an `UNSUBSTANTIATED` finding requires exactly `0`, and any mismatch is rejected with `ERR_RESTITUTION_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). A substantiated finding advances the investigation `OPEN → SUBSTANTIATED`, records the ordered borrower restitution in `restitution_cents`, and sets the referenced loan's `authority_flag` to `UNAUTHORIZED`. An unsubstantiated finding advances it `OPEN → UNSUBSTANTIATED`, leaves `restitution_cents` at `0`, and sets the referenced loan's `authority_flag` back to `VERIFIED`. Both branches stamp `finding_by_user_id` with the deciding officer's `X-User-ID`, increment the referenced loan's `version` by one, recompute the originator's `open_investigation_count` — again a derived read, so no write lands on the originator row and its `version` does not move — and append an `INVESTIGATION_FINDING` audit entry whose `detail` carries the `finding`, the `restitution_cents` and the `loan_application_id`. `UNSUBSTANTIATED` is terminal.

Remediation closes out a substantiated case. The officer supplies a `remediation_note`, a required string of at most eight thousand characters, and the investigation advances `SUBSTANTIATED → REMEDIATED` provided its `restitution_cents` is at least 1; `remediated_at` is stamped from the logical clock, the referenced loan's `authority_flag` returns to `VERIFIED` and its `version` moves by one, and `open_investigation_count` is recomputed as the derived read it is. Remediation appends no audit entry. `REMEDIATED` is terminal. This step is deliberately **not** segregated: the same `ComplianceOfficer` may both enter the substantiated finding and later record its remediation. Once every investigation on a loan has reached `UNSUBSTANTIATED` or `REMEDIATED`, withheld compensation on that loan becomes releasable as §3.5 describes.

The **nightly compliance sweep** advances the compliance epoch and is the only thing in the system that expires anything. A `PlatformAdmin` triggers it with no request body, and it performs three sweeps in turn against the current logical clock instant. First, every licence whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `RENEWAL_PENDING` and whose `expiration_date` is at or before the logical clock instant moves to `EXPIRED` and runs the full five-hop authority-lapse cascade of §3.1 — relinquish the `ACTIVE` sponsorship with `relinquished_at` stamped from the clock, clear the licence's denormalised sponsorship fields and recompute `authorized_state_codes`, suspend the originator's `TAKEN` and `LOCKED` loans in that state by setting `authority_flag` to `SUSPENDED`, open one `CASCADE` investigation with `trigger_reason` `AUTHORITY_LAPSE` per suspended loan, and recompute `open_investigation_count` — with the licences processed in order of `expiration_date` ascending and then `created_at` ascending, and with each licence's audit entries appended as one `LICENSE_STATUS_CHANGED` — its `detail` carrying `status` `EXPIRED` and `reason` `SWEEP_EXPIRY` — followed by one `INVESTIGATION_OPENED` per investigation it opened, each of those carrying `trigger_reason` `AUTHORITY_LAPSE`, `opened_by` `CASCADE` and the `loan_application_id` in its `detail`; because the sweep is not a human caller, every one of these entries carries `actor_role` `SYSTEM` and a null `actor_user_id`. This is the transition that reaches `EXPIRED` from `RENEWAL_PENDING` as well as from `ACTIVE`: a renewal that was requested but never completed before the deadline expires exactly like an untouched licence. Second, every compensation plan whose `assignment_effective_to` falls strictly before the logical clock instant has its assignment treated as lapsed, clearing `compensation_plan_id` on the originator it was assigned to — plans are examined in `created_at` order, the clearing happens only where that originator's `compensation_plan_id` still points at this very plan, so a plan carrying no `assigned_originator_id`, one whose assignee has since been re-pointed at a newer plan, and one whose assignee no longer resolves are all skipped, and the plan row itself is left completely untouched: its `status`, its three assignment fields and its `version` all stand exactly as they were. Third, every examination inquiry whose `status` is `OPEN` or `INFO_REQUESTED` and whose `response_due_at` falls strictly before the logical clock instant moves to `ESCALATED` with `escalation_reason` set to `DEADLINE_MISSED`, inquiries likewise being examined in `created_at` order. Neither the second nor the third sweep appends an audit entry of any kind.

The sweep reports what it did so that a caller can verify the epoch advance without polling every collection: `swept_at` gives the instant it ran, and `expired_license_ids`, `relinquished_sponsorship_ids`, `suspended_loan_ids`, `opened_investigation_ids`, `escalated_inquiry_ids` and `unassigned_originator_ids` list the identifiers touched by each of the three sweeps. Each list runs in the order the sweep processed the rows it names, and `unassigned_originator_ids` carries any one originator at most once however many of its lapsed assignments qualified. A sweep that finds nothing to do still succeeds, returning empty lists — and because each of the three selections re-reads the statuses the previous run left behind, a re-run at the same logical instant is idempotent: the second run has nothing left to expire, unassign or escalate and answers with all six lists empty.
