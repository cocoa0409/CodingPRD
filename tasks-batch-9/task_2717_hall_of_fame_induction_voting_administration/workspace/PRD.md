# Hall of Fame Induction & Voting Administration API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A sports hall of fame lives or dies by the credibility of its election. Every year the institution must decide who among thousands of retired players, coaches and contributors deserves enshrinement, and it must reach that decision through a process that survives public scrutiny: published eligibility criteria with waiting periods and career thresholds, a nomination intake screened by a committee that cannot rubber-stamp its own submissions, a voting body whose credentials are actively maintained, a printed ballot with a hard cap on how many names an elector may select, and a tabulation certified by an auditor who did not count the votes. Today that entire apparatus is run on spreadsheets, email threads and institutional memory. The consequence is predictable — a ballot is printed from a slate that a late screening decision has already changed, an elector whose credential lapsed two years ago votes anyway, a candidate's fourth appearance is scored even though the rules allow three, and the reconciliation happens after the results have been announced.

This document specifies the backend service that replaces that apparatus. The service owns the full election calendar for a sport's hall of fame — cycle setup, nomination intake, screening committee review, ballot construction, confidential voting, draft tabulation, independent audit certification, election certification with carryover and ballot expiration, and finally ceremony planning with inductee travel funding, artifact collection and exhibit development. It also owns the special pathways that every hall of fame maintains alongside its modern ballot: the veterans committee route for candidates whose modern eligibility has lapsed, the posthumous route with its own waiting period and a two-person travelling delegation, and the strictly rationed special election.

Two design commitments run through every requirement below. The first is **segregation of duties**: the elector who filed a nomination may not screen it, and the operator who produced a draft tally may not be the auditor who certifies it. The second is **determinism** — every threshold, tie-break, rounding step and evaluation order in this document is fixed, because an election that cannot be recomputed identically from the stored record is an election that cannot be defended. All temporal reasoning is likewise deterministic: the service reads a logical clock, never the host's wall clock.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

Every collection in this service is mounted at the top level — there are no nested ownership paths. `/induction-cycles`, `/candidacies`, `/ballots`, `/vote-submissions`, `/travel-grants`, `/exhibit-installations` and their siblings are all addressed directly, and relationships between them are carried by identifier fields inside request bodies rather than by URL nesting.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint, reads included, must carry two headers: `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role`. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the five role names declared below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). This is the very first check the service performs — it precedes payload parsing, resource lookup and every business rule. Once the caller is identified, the service confirms that the acting role appears in the endpoint's authorized-role list in §4; a caller whose role is not listed is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The five roles are `ElectionAdministrator`, `ScreeningCommitteeMember`, `Elector`, `IndependentAuditor` and `CuratorialStaff`.

An `ElectionAdministrator` is hall-of-fame staff running the cycle: configuration, credentialing, ballot construction, tabulation, election certification, ceremony planning and travel funding. When `X-User-Role` is `ElectionAdministrator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — the draft tally stores the acting administrator's `X-User-ID` in the ballot's `tallied_by_user_id` field — but it never compares it to any entity's `id`.

An `IndependentAuditor` is an external auditor with sole authority to certify or reject a draft tally, and the only role permitted to read individual ballot selections. When `X-User-Role` is `IndependentAuditor`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server compares it to the stored `tallied_by_user_id` string to enforce segregation of duties, but never resolves it to an entity row.

`CuratorialStaff` are the museum curators who take in artifacts, plan display cases and request inductee travel. When `X-User-Role` is `CuratorialStaff`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity and is not compared to any entity's `id`.

`Elector` and `ScreeningCommitteeMember` are the two entity-backed roles. When `X-User-Role` is `Elector`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Elector` row; ownership checks on any resource that references an elector compare the resource's elector-identifier field to `X-User-ID` — a nomination's `nominated_by_elector_id`, a vote submission's `elector_id`. When `X-User-Role` is `ScreeningCommitteeMember`, `X-User-ID` is likewise the `id` of the acting `Elector` row, and that row must additionally carry `is_screening_member` set to true. For both of these roles, a request whose `X-User-ID` does not resolve to an existing elector row is rejected with `ERR_ELECTOR_IDENTITY_UNKNOWN` (HTTP 403); this applies to nomination intake, screening decisions, supplements, vote casting and vote withdrawal.

A `ScreeningCommitteeMember` is a credentialed elector serving on the screening committee. The role builds the ballot by advancing, rejecting, deferring or querying nominations, and it also votes exactly like any other elector — vote casting and vote withdrawal are shared between `Elector` and `ScreeningCommitteeMember`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters, `limit` and `offset`. `limit` defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` or `offset` outside these bounds is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

List results are always ordered by `created_at` ascending, with `id` ascending as the deterministic tie-break for rows sharing a `created_at` value. This ordering is applied before `offset` and `limit` are applied, so paging through a stable collection visits every row exactly once.

The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit` (the effective page size), `offset` (the effective starting position), `total_count` (the number of rows matching the filters, before paging) and `has_more` (true when rows remain beyond the current page).

### 2.4. The Logical Election Clock

Nothing in this service reads the host machine's wall clock. All temporal reasoning — credential expiry, ballot opening and closing windows, the ceremony date gate, escort occupation intervals and exhibit display windows — is evaluated against a single server-side **logical clock**. Before any caller moves it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to wall-clock time under any circumstance.

Any of the five roles may move the logical clock through the test-driving clock endpoint, which takes a single field `current_utc` carrying the instant the clock should read from that moment on, and returns HTTP 200 with an empty `data` object. Moving the clock has one side effect: it changes the instant that every temporal validation and the live `credentialed_roster_count` derivation compare against. It does not retroactively re-evaluate stored decisions — a ballot that was closed before its `closes_at` stays closed, and a grant already approved stays approved.

### 2.5. Record Identity, Timestamps and Versioning

Every resource carries a server-minted `id`: a UUID string with no type prefix of any kind. Clients never supply an `id`; a client-supplied `id` in a creation payload is ignored.

Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with exactly three fractional-second digits, recording the logical-clock instant at which the row was written. Most resources additionally carry `updated_at` (same format, refreshed on every mutation of the row) and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row.

Two configuration resources are deliberately leaner: `EligibilityRule` and `VoterBloc` carry only `id` and `created_at`. Both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from those two resources entirely — they are declared once and never versioned, which is also why neither of them participates in any concurrency check.

`version` is the currency of the concurrency rules in §2.8, so it matters exactly which writes advance it. A cycle's `version` advances on each of its own lifecycle transitions and on every derived-counter change a child write causes; a ballot's `version` advances when the slate is certified for voting, on each recorded or withdrawn submission, on each struck slot, on the draft tally and on the audit decision. Two convenience mirrors exist so that a client does not have to re-read a parent after a write: a vote submission reports `ballot_version_after`, the ballot's `version` immediately after that submission was recorded, and a travel grant reports `cycle_version_after`, the funding cycle's `version` immediately after that grant's most recent mutation.

### 2.6. Resource Lookup and Filter Semantics

Path identifiers are resolved before any state or business rule is evaluated, and each resource family reports its own not-found code: a missing induction cycle yields `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND`, a missing candidate `ERR_CANDIDATE_NOT_FOUND`, a missing candidacy `ERR_CANDIDACY_NOT_FOUND`, a missing elector `ERR_ELECTOR_NOT_FOUND`, a missing ballot, ballot slot or vote submission `ERR_BALLOT_NOT_FOUND`, a missing induction or travel grant `ERR_INDUCTION_NOT_FOUND`, a missing exhibit case or exhibit installation `ERR_EXHIBIT_NOT_FOUND`, and a missing voter bloc `ERR_BLOC_NOT_FOUND`. All eight are HTTP 404. The same codes apply to identifiers carried in a request body rather than in the path — `cycle_id`, `candidate_id`, `carryover_from_candidacy_id`, `nominated_by_elector_id`, `bloc_id`, `ballot_id`, `induction_id`, `exhibit_case_id` — and the existence checks are evaluated in that order, cycle first and bloc last, so that a payload naming two non-existent parents reports the earlier family's code.

Read endpoints accept filters that narrow the collection without changing its ordering. Cycles may be filtered by `sport_code` and `status`. Eligibility rules may be filtered by `category` and `pathway`. Voter blocs may be filtered by `bloc_code`. Candidates may be filtered by `category`, `hof_status` and `is_deceased`. Candidacies may be filtered by any of their identifier fields — `cycle_id`, `candidate_id`, `nominated_by_elector_id`, `screened_by_elector_id`, `carryover_from_candidacy_id` — as well as by `status` and `pathway`. Electors may be filtered by `bloc_id`, `status` and `is_screening_member`. Ballots may be filtered by `cycle_id`, `round_number`, `voting_status` and `tally_status`. Ballot slots may be filtered by `ballot_id`, `candidacy_id` and `status`. Vote submissions may be filtered by `ballot_id`, `elector_id` and `status`. Inductions may be filtered by `cycle_id`, `candidacy_id`, `candidate_id`, `status`, `travel_status`, `artifact_status` and `exhibit_status`. Travel grants may be filtered by `cycle_id`, `induction_id`, `status` and `variant`. Exhibit cases may be filtered by `case_code`, and exhibit installations by `induction_id`, `exhibit_case_id` and `status`.

### 2.7. Segregation of Duties and Ballot Secrecy

Two duties in this system may never be performed by the same identity. A screening decision may not be recorded by the elector who filed the nomination: when the acting `X-User-ID` equals the candidacy's `nominated_by_elector_id`, the decision is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). An audit decision may not be taken by the operator who produced the draft tally: when the acting `X-User-ID` equals the ballot's `tallied_by_user_id`, the audit decision is refused with the same `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Those are the only two operations this rule guards, and it carries the same error code in both.

Ballot secrecy is enforced at the response layer rather than the storage layer. A vote submission's `selected_slot_ids` array is stored in full, but it is echoed back as a non-null array only when the acting role is `IndependentAuditor`. For every other role — `ElectionAdministrator` and `Elector` included, and on both the single-submission read and the submission list — the `selected_slot_ids` field is returned as `null`. The elector's own submission is no exception: even the caster sees `null`. What every role may see is the derived aggregate — `selection_count`, the ballot-level `ballots_cast` and `total_selections_counted`, and the per-slot `votes_received` and `vote_pct` — because those disclose the result without disclosing any individual's choices.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Three write endpoints are guarded by an optimistic concurrency check against a parent record's `version`. In each case the version field is a required, non-optional member of the request body; omitting it is a malformed request that yields `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400), and supplying a value that does not equal the parent's current `version` yields the rule's declared rejection code. No other endpoint in this service accepts a version field at all.

A `POST /ballots` request must carry `expected_cycle_version` matching the target `InductionCycle`'s current `version`. The slate a ballot prints is derived from the cycle's advanced candidacies, so if another writer has advanced the cycle's `version` since the administrator last read it — a screening `ADVANCE` that landed after the read, or another round's ballot that already superseded this one — the construction is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CYCLE_VERSION` (HTTP 412) rather than printing a slate that no longer matches the record.

A `POST /travel-grants` request must likewise carry `expected_cycle_version` matching the funding cycle's current `version`. The award is frozen at creation from the pre-commitment budget ratio, so a stale cycle view means the grant would be priced against a budget position that another approved grant has already superseded; that request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CYCLE_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

A `POST /vote-submissions` request must carry `expected_ballot_version` matching the target `Ballot`'s current `version`. Each recorded submission, each withdrawal and each struck slot advances the ballot's `version`, so a value that no longer matches means the elector's client is voting a slate that is not the slate now printed on the ballot; the submission is rejected with `ERR_STALE_BALLOT_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and the elector must re-read the ballot before submitting. The `ballot_version_after` value returned with each accepted submission exists precisely so that the next elector can supply a fresh `expected_ballot_version` without an extra read.

### 2.9. Cascades and Compensating Reversal

Several operations in this service mutate more than one record. Where they do, the full cascade is part of the contract and is described with the workflow that triggers it in §3. Three invariants govern all of them.

First, **derived counters are re-derived, never patched from a stale value.** `nomination_count`, `advanced_candidacy_count`, `special_election_used`, `credentialed_roster_count`, `travel_committed_cents`, `induction_count` and `ceremony_ready_count` on a cycle, `seats_filled` on a voter bloc, `slot_count`, `ballots_cast` and `total_selections_counted` on a ballot, `artifact_count` on an induction and `artifacts_installed` on an exhibit case are all definitionally counts or sums over their contributing rows, and each is recomputed from those rows whenever a contributor changes.

Second, **reversals restore the prior value exactly.** Cancelling an approved travel grant decreases the cycle's `travel_committed_cents` by exactly the grant's `award_cents`, returning the budget position to what it was before the approval. Withdrawing an exhibit installation decreases the case's `artifacts_installed` by exactly that installation's `artifact_count`. Neither reversal recomputes an award, a tier or a capacity from scratch, and neither leaves a residue.

Third, **every cascade that changes a row also refreshes that row's `updated_at` and increments its `version` by 1**, including when the row was touched only as the downstream target of another entity's transition. A cascade that changes nothing changes no version.

### 2.10. Numeric Precision

Three numeric regimes coexist in this service and must not be confused.

**Percentages** — `election_threshold_pct`, `carryover_min_pct`, `runoff_floor_pct`, `min_participation_pct` and the derived `vote_pct` and `latest_round_pct` — are fractions of one, not values out of a hundred: a threshold of seventy-five percent is stored and compared as 0.75. Every derived percentage is rounded to 2 decimal places using round-half-up.

**Vote counts and thresholds** are whole numbers. Where a threshold is derived by multiplying a denominator by a percentage, the product is rounded *up* to the next whole vote (ceiling) before it is compared, and the comparison against a slot's `votes_received` is inclusive — a slot exactly at the threshold has met it.

**Money** is always integer cents: `travel_budget_cents`, `travel_committed_cents`, `requested_airfare_cents` and `award_cents` are whole-cent integers, never decimal currency. Where a monetary intermediate is fractional, it is rounded to whole cents using round-half-up at the exact point in the calculation §3.9 specifies, and never later.

**Years** are integers throughout, and every year comparison in the eligibility machinery — waiting periods, the veterans floor, career thresholds — is inclusive at the boundary.

### 2.11. Order of Validation

Where several checks could reject the same request, the order in which they are evaluated is fixed, because the caller's remediation depends on which code comes back. The service evaluates in ascending priority band and stops at the first failure.

1. **Authentication** — headers present and `X-User-Role` recognised: `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. **Authorization** — the acting role is listed for the endpoint: `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
3. **Payload shape** — on every POST and PATCH endpoint: every required field present, every enum value a declared member, integers actually integers, timestamps parseable ISO 8601: `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
4. **Entity-backed identity** — for the `Elector` and `ScreeningCommitteeMember` roles, `X-User-ID` resolves to an existing elector: `ERR_ELECTOR_IDENTITY_UNKNOWN`.
5. **Existence** — in this order: cycle, candidate, candidacy, elector, ballot family, induction family, exhibit family, eligibility rule, voter bloc.
6. **State preconditions** — the addressed record is in the from-state the operation requires: `ERR_INVALID_CYCLE_STATE`, then `ERR_INVALID_CANDIDACY_STATE`, then `ERR_INVALID_ELECTOR_STATE`, then `ERR_INVALID_BALLOT_STATE`, then `ERR_BALLOT_EXPIRED`, then `ERR_INVALID_TALLY_STATE`, then `ERR_ELECTION_NOT_FINAL`, then `ERR_CEREMONY_NOT_PLANNED`, then `ERR_INVALID_GRANT_STATE`, then `ERR_INVALID_INDUCTION_STATE`.
7. **Optimistic concurrency** — `ERR_STALE_CYCLE_VERSION`, then `ERR_STALE_BALLOT_VERSION`. Both are evaluated *after* every state precondition above, so a request that is both stale and in the wrong state reports the state error.
8. **Duty and credential compliance** — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, then `ERR_NOMINATOR_IDENTITY_MISMATCH`, then `ERR_NOT_SCREENING_MEMBER`, then `ERR_ELECTOR_NOT_CREDENTIALED`, then `ERR_CREDENTIAL_EXPIRED`.
9. **Eligibility and quota** — `ERR_CANDIDATE_INELIGIBLE`, then `ERR_POSTHUMOUS_PATHWAY_REQUIRES_DECEASED`, then `ERR_WAITING_PERIOD_NOT_MET`, then `ERR_CAREER_THRESHOLD_NOT_MET`, then `ERR_APPEARANCE_LIMIT_REACHED`, then `ERR_JUSTIFICATION_REQUIRED`, then `ERR_SPECIAL_ELECTION_QUOTA_EXHAUSTED`, then `ERR_DUPLICATE_CANDIDACY`.
10. **Remaining business constraints, in this order** — `ERR_INFO_REQUEST_LIMIT`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_CONFIGURATION`, `ERR_BLOC_SEATS_EXHAUSTED`, `ERR_NO_SLATE_AVAILABLE`, `ERR_INVALID_TIME_WINDOW`, `ERR_VOTING_WINDOW_CLOSED`, `ERR_SLOT_NOT_ON_BALLOT`, `ERR_SLOT_STRUCK`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_SUBMISSION`, `ERR_MAX_VOTES_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_NO_BALLOTS_CAST`, `ERR_BLOC_QUORUM_NOT_MET`, `ERR_INDUCTION_CYCLE_MISMATCH`, `ERR_ARTIFACTS_NOT_RECEIVED`, `ERR_INVALID_ESCORT_INTERVAL`, `ERR_CASE_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, `ERR_CASE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_ESCORT_POOL_EXHAUSTED`, `ERR_TRAVEL_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_CEREMONY_DATE_NOT_REACHED`, `ERR_INDUCTIONS_NOT_READY`.

Two consequences of this ladder are worth calling out because they are easy to get backwards. All existence checks precede all state checks, so a nomination whose cycle exists but is in the wrong phase and whose named `carryover_from_candidacy_id` does not exist reports the candidacy's 404 rather than the cycle's 409. And because the concurrency band sits below the state band, an administrator who both holds a stale `expected_cycle_version` and addresses a cycle in the wrong phase learns about the phase first.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up an Election: Cycles, Criteria, Blocs and Cases

An election begins with an `InductionCycle`, which an `ElectionAdministrator` opens by supplying the `cycle_year` the cycle elects for — a whole year no earlier than 1900 and no later than 2200 — together with the `sport_code` identifying the hall of fame's sport, for example `BASEBALL`. Two optional figures may be supplied at this point and both may be revised later during ceremony planning: `travel_budget_cents`, which defaults to 0, and `escort_pool_size`, the number of ceremony escorts available concurrently for posthumous delegations, which also defaults to 0. A third optional figure, `max_inductees`, is the hard cap on how many candidacies this cycle may elect; it defaults to 5 and must be at least 1. The pair of `sport_code` and `cycle_year` is unique across all cycles — a second attempt to open the 2027 baseball cycle is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CONFIGURATION`.

A newly opened cycle is in `SETUP`, with `current_round` at 0, `austerity_tier` at `FULL`, `ceremony_date` and `ceremony_venue` unset, and all seven of its derived counters at zero: `nomination_count`, `advanced_candidacy_count`, `special_election_used`, `credentialed_roster_count`, `travel_committed_cents`, `induction_count` and `ceremony_ready_count`. Its lifecycle then runs `SETUP` → `NOMINATIONS_OPEN` → `SCREENING` → `BALLOTING` → `CERTIFIED` → `CEREMONY_PLANNED` → `CLOSED`, and `CLOSED` is terminal. Any write that addresses a cycle whose `status` is not the from-state that write requires is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CYCLE_STATE` (HTTP 409) — `SETUP` is required to open nominations, `NOMINATIONS_OPEN` to close them and to file a nomination, `SCREENING` to record a screening decision and to construct the first ballot, `BALLOTING` to certify the election, `CERTIFIED` to plan the ceremony, and `CEREMONY_PLANNED` to hold it.

Before nominations may open, the institution must publish its criteria. An `EligibilityRule` governs exactly one pairing of a candidate `category` — `PLAYER`, `COACH` or `CONTRIBUTOR` — with an induction `pathway` — `MODERN`, `VETERANS`, `POSTHUMOUS` or `SPECIAL`. Each rule declares `waiting_period_years` (zero or more years that must elapse after the baseline year before a candidacy may be filed), `min_career_seasons` (zero or more completed qualifying seasons) and `min_career_metric` (a non-negative career threshold metric, games for coaches and a statistical total for players), plus four ceiling and floor figures: `max_ballot_appearances`, at least 1, is how many ballots a candidate may appear on under this pathway; `election_threshold_pct` is the fraction of the vote denominator a slot must reach to be elected, greater than 0 and at most 1; `carryover_min_pct` is the minimum vote share a non-elected slot needs to stay alive for a future cycle, between 0 and 1 inclusive; and `runoff_floor_pct` is the minimum vote share a non-elected slot needs to be carried into the next runoff round, also between 0 and 1 inclusive. The pairing of `category` and `pathway` is unique across the table, and a duplicate is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CONFIGURATION`. One rule row has a special standing that surfaces later in tabulation: the row for `PLAYER` paired with `MODERN` is the **house threshold row**, and its `election_threshold_pct` is the fraction every ballot's election thresholds are derived from, whatever categories actually appear on that ballot.

Opening nominations moves the cycle from `SETUP` to `NOMINATIONS_OPEN`, and it will not proceed while the eligibility-rule table is empty: a cycle whose criteria have not been published yet is refused with `ERR_ELIGIBILITY_RULE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404). The transition recomputes `credentialed_roster_count` from the live elector roster and increments the cycle's `version` by 1.

The voting body's structure is declared as `VoterBloc` rows — the writers' bloc, the veterans' bloc, and so on. Each carries a `bloc_code` that is unique across the table, a human-readable `name`, a `seat_cap` of at least 1 limiting how many seated electors the bloc may hold, and a `min_participation_pct` between 0 and 1 inclusive: the minimum fraction of the bloc's seated electors that must have a counted submission before a tally may be audit-certified. A bloc starts with `seats_filled` at 0, and that counter tracks every elector of the bloc whose `status` is `CREDENTIALED` or `SUSPENDED`. Duplicate `bloc_code` values are refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CONFIGURATION`.

Museum infrastructure is declared in parallel and may be created either by `CuratorialStaff` or by an `ElectionAdministrator`. An `ExhibitCase` carries a `case_code` unique across the table, a `capacity_artifacts` of at least 1 bounding how many artifacts the case can hold across its installed windows, and a `changeover_buffer_days` of zero or more whole days of dark time required between the display windows of two *different* inductions in that case. Its `artifacts_installed` occupancy starts at 0. A duplicate `case_code` is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CONFIGURATION`.

Candidates are registered independently of any cycle, since the same person may be nominated in several successive years. Registration takes the candidate's `full_name`, their `category`, the `retirement_year` in which their qualifying career ended (again between 1900 and 2200), the `career_seasons` they completed and their `career_metric_value`. Two optional fields record mortality: `is_deceased`, which defaults to false, and `death_year`, which defaults to null and is required whenever `is_deceased` is true. A new candidate starts with `hof_status` at `NOT_INDUCTED`, `ballot_appearances_used` at 0, `veterans_eligible_from_year` unset and `permanently_ineligible_reason` unset.

Records get corrected, and people die between cycles. An administrator may therefore revise a registered candidate's `career_seasons`, `career_metric_value`, `is_deceased` and `death_year` after the fact; such a revision increments the candidate's `version` by 1 and deliberately leaves `hof_status`, `ballot_appearances_used` and `veterans_eligible_from_year` exactly as they were, because those three are outcomes of the election machinery rather than biographical facts.

Barring a candidate permanently is the one destructive act in this domain, and it cascades hard. The administrator supplies a `permanently_ineligible_reason`, which sets the candidate's `hof_status` to `PERMANENTLY_INELIGIBLE`. Every candidacy of that candidate whose `status` is `SUBMITTED`, `SCREENING`, `INFO_REQUESTED`, `ADVANCED` or `ON_BALLOT` is driven to `STRUCK`. Every ballot slot printed for those candidacies — which is to say every slot whose ballot is still in `DRAFT` or `OPEN` — is set to `STRUCK` as well, which decrements that ballot's `slot_count` and increments the ballot's `version` by 1. Because a struck slot no longer counts, every selection of that slot in an already-counted submission stops counting: the slot's `votes_received` freezes at 0 and the ballot's `total_selections_counted` is reduced by exactly the selections the struck slots had accumulated. The `version` bump on the ballot is the intended serialization point — any elector holding an `expected_ballot_version` read before the strike will be turned away and forced to re-read the slate.

### 3.2. The Voter Body: Credentialing and Continuing Eligibility

Adding an elector to the voting body takes their `full_name`, the `bloc_id` of the bloc whose seat they will occupy, and a `credential_expires_on` instant at which their credential lapses. The optional `is_screening_member` flag, which defaults to false, is what allows that person to act under the `ScreeningCommitteeMember` role later; a bloc that does not exist yields `ERR_BLOC_NOT_FOUND`. A new elector begins in `PROVISIONAL` standing with `consecutive_missed_cycles` at 0, `last_participated_cycle_year` unset and `suspension_reason` unset. `PROVISIONAL` electors may neither nominate nor vote.

Credentialing an elector advances them from `PROVISIONAL` to `CREDENTIALED` and is where the bloc's seat arithmetic bites. The bloc must have room — its `seats_filled` must be strictly below its `seat_cap`, and a full bloc is refused with `ERR_BLOC_SEATS_EXHAUSTED` (HTTP 409). The credential must also be live: the elector's `credential_expires_on` must be strictly later than the logical clock's current instant. An optional `credential_expires_on` may be supplied with the credentialing request, in which case it overwrites the stored value before the liveness comparison is made. On success the bloc's `seats_filled` increases by 1, and `credentialed_roster_count` is recomputed for every cycle whose `status` is `NOMINATIONS_OPEN`, `SCREENING` or `BALLOTING` — that counter is the number of elector rows whose `status` is `CREDENTIALED` and whose `credential_expires_on` is at or after the logical clock's current instant.

Suspension and reinstatement close a loop that a working voting body runs constantly: `CREDENTIALED` → `SUSPENDED` → `CREDENTIALED`. Suspending a credentialed elector requires a `suspension_reason`, which is stored on the row. It deliberately does **not** free the bloc seat — a suspended elector still occupies one, so `seats_filled` is unchanged — but it does recompute `credentialed_roster_count` on every cycle in `NOMINATIONS_OPEN`, `SCREENING` or `BALLOTING`, because a suspended elector is no longer part of the credentialed roster. Reinstating a suspended elector returns them to `CREDENTIALED`, clears `suspension_reason`, accepts an optional replacement `credential_expires_on`, requires that the resulting `credential_expires_on` be strictly later than the logical clock's current instant, and again recomputes `credentialed_roster_count` on those same three cycle phases. An operation addressing an elector who is not in the required from-state — `PROVISIONAL` for credentialing, `CREDENTIALED` for suspension, `SUSPENDED` for reinstatement — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ELECTOR_STATE` (HTTP 409).

The fourth elector state, `LAPSED`, is terminal and is never reached by an administrator's action. It is reached only through the participation maintenance sweep described in §3.8, and when it is reached the bloc's `seats_filled` decreases by 1, freeing the seat for a new credential. This asymmetry is intentional: discipline (suspension) holds the seat, while disengagement (lapsing) surrenders it.

Two credential checks guard every operation that depends on an elector's standing — nomination intake and vote casting. First, the elector the operation depends on must be `CREDENTIALED`; a `PROVISIONAL`, `SUSPENDED` or `LAPSED` elector may neither nominate nor vote, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_ELECTOR_NOT_CREDENTIALED` (HTTP 403). Second, that elector's `credential_expires_on` must be at or after the logical clock's current instant; a lapsed-by-time credential is refused with `ERR_CREDENTIAL_EXPIRED` (HTTP 403). The credential-status check is evaluated before the expiry check, so an elector who is both suspended and time-expired reports the former.

### 3.3. Nomination Intake and the Career Eligibility Assessment

While a cycle is in `NOMINATIONS_OPEN`, a credentialed elector may file a nomination — a `Candidacy` — and an `ElectionAdministrator` may file one on a credentialed elector's behalf. The payload names the `cycle_id` the nomination competes in, the `candidate_id` of the person being nominated, the `nominated_by_elector_id` of the credentialed elector filing it, and the `pathway` being claimed, which is one of `MODERN`, `VETERANS`, `POSTHUMOUS` or `SPECIAL`. Two optional fields complete it: `justification`, a written argument that is mandatory for one pathway and ignored for the others, and `carryover_from_candidacy_id`, which points at the prior-cycle candidacy this nomination continues. Filing against a cycle that is not in `NOMINATIONS_OPEN` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CYCLE_STATE`.

Who may file for whom is tightly bound. When the acting role is `Elector`, the supplied `nominated_by_elector_id` must equal the acting `X-User-ID`; an elector filing under someone else's name is refused with `ERR_NOMINATOR_IDENTITY_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403). The nominating elector — whoever submitted the request — must be `CREDENTIALED` with a live credential, per the two credential checks of §3.2. And the candidate must still be in play: a candidate whose `hof_status` is `INDUCTED` or `PERMANENTLY_INELIGIBLE` is refused with `ERR_CANDIDATE_INELIGIBLE` (HTTP 422).

A fresh candidacy is written in `SUBMITTED` status with `appearance_number` at 0 (it is off-ballot), `info_request_count` at 0, `latest_round_votes` and `latest_round_pct` at 0, and `screened_by_elector_id` and `screening_notes` unset. Its creation increments the cycle's `nomination_count` by 1 and recomputes the cycle's `special_election_used`, which counts the cycle's candidacies whose `pathway` is `SPECIAL` and whose `status` is anything other than `REJECTED`. The full candidacy lifecycle spans `SUBMITTED`, `SCREENING`, `INFO_REQUESTED`, `ADVANCED`, `REJECTED`, `DEFERRED`, `ON_BALLOT`, `ELECTED`, `CARRYOVER_ELIGIBLE`, `EXPIRED`, `ELIMINATED` and `STRUCK`; of those, `REJECTED`, `DEFERRED`, `ELECTED`, `CARRYOVER_ELIGIBLE`, `EXPIRED` and `STRUCK` are terminal.

Before any of that is written, the nomination must clear the **career eligibility assessment**, which is the heart of nomination intake. The assessment first locates the single eligibility rule whose `category` equals the candidate's `category` and whose `pathway` equals the candidacy's claimed `pathway`. If no such rule has been published, the nomination is refused with `ERR_ELIGIBILITY_RULE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) — an institution that never wrote down what a posthumous contributor must achieve cannot process one.

The assessment then establishes a **baseline year** and an **effective waiting period**, and the two depend on the pathway. For a `POSTHUMOUS` nomination the candidate must actually be deceased: `is_deceased` must be true and `death_year` must not be null, otherwise the nomination is refused with `ERR_POSTHUMOUS_PATHWAY_REQUIRES_DECEASED` (HTTP 422). For such a nomination the baseline year is the later of the candidate's `retirement_year` and their `death_year`, and the effective waiting period is a single year rather than the rule's own figure. For a `SPECIAL` nomination the baseline year is the `retirement_year` and the effective waiting period is zero years — the special election waives the wait, and only the wait. For a `MODERN` or `VETERANS` nomination the baseline year is the `retirement_year` and the effective waiting period is the rule's `waiting_period_years`. The candidacy's `eligible_from_year` is then the baseline year plus the effective waiting period, and it is stored on the row.

Waiting is checked inclusively: the cycle's `cycle_year` must be greater than or equal to the computed `eligible_from_year`, and a cycle running earlier than that is refused with `ERR_WAITING_PERIOD_NOT_MET` (HTTP 422). A `VETERANS` nomination must additionally clear a long-gap floor on top of that comparison. That floor is the candidate's stored `veterans_eligible_from_year` when it is not null; when it is null, the floor is the candidate's `retirement_year` plus twenty-five years, which is the quarter-century gap the veterans committee route exists to serve. The cycle's `cycle_year` must be greater than or equal to that floor, and a nomination that falls short reports the same `ERR_WAITING_PERIOD_NOT_MET`.

Career thresholds are never waived by any pathway, not even the special election. The candidate's `career_seasons` must be greater than or equal to the rule's `min_career_seasons`, and their `career_metric_value` must be greater than or equal to the rule's `min_career_metric`; both comparisons are inclusive at the boundary, and either shortfall is refused with `ERR_CAREER_THRESHOLD_NOT_MET` (HTTP 422). Next comes the appearance budget: the candidate's `ballot_appearances_used` must be strictly less than the rule's `max_ballot_appearances`, and a candidate who has already used every appearance the pathway allows is refused with `ERR_APPEARANCE_LIMIT_REACHED` (HTTP 422).

The special election carries two further gates that apply to no other pathway. A `SPECIAL` nomination must supply a non-empty `justification`, and one that omits it or sends an empty string is refused with `ERR_JUSTIFICATION_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422). And the special election is rationed to exactly one per cycle: the cycle's `special_election_used` must be strictly less than one at the moment of filing, so a second live special nomination in the same cycle is refused with `ERR_SPECIAL_ELECTION_QUOTA_EXHAUSTED` (HTTP 409). Because the counter excludes `REJECTED` candidacies, a special nomination that the screening committee rejects releases the cycle's single slot for another attempt.

The last intake gate is uniqueness of the person within the cycle: no other candidacy may exist for the same combination of `cycle_id` and `candidate_id` with a `status` other than `REJECTED`, and a second live nomination of the same person in the same cycle is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CANDIDACY` (HTTP 409). These eight nomination checks are evaluated in a fixed order and the first failure is reported: candidate standing (`ERR_CANDIDATE_INELIGIBLE`) first, then the posthumous deceased requirement, then the waiting period including the veterans floor, then the two career thresholds, then the appearance budget, then the special-election justification, then the special-election quota, and finally the duplicate-person check. A nomination that is both a duplicate and short on career seasons therefore reports the career threshold, not the duplicate.

### 3.4. Screening Committee Review and the Information-Request Loop

Closing nominations moves the cycle from `NOMINATIONS_OPEN` to `SCREENING` and sweeps the intake queue into the committee's hands: every candidacy of that cycle still in `SUBMITTED` is transitioned to `SCREENING`, processed in ascending `created_at` order, and each swept candidacy has its own `version` incremented by 1. The cycle's `version` is incremented by 1 as well. A candidacy filed but never swept cannot exist — after this transition the cycle holds no `SUBMITTED` rows.

A screening decision is recorded by a `ScreeningCommitteeMember` and carries a `decision` field whose value is `ADVANCE`, `REJECT`, `DEFER` or `REQUEST_INFO`, plus an optional `screening_notes` free-text note attached by that decision. Three guards precede the decision itself. The acting elector must genuinely sit on the committee: their elector row must have `is_screening_member` true *and* `status` `CREDENTIALED`, and a caller failing either condition is refused with `ERR_NOT_SCREENING_MEMBER` (HTTP 403). The acting `X-User-ID` must not equal the candidacy's `nominated_by_elector_id` — nobody screens their own nomination — and a self-screening attempt is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). And the candidacy must be in `SCREENING`, the from-state all four decisions require; anything else is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CANDIDACY_STATE` (HTTP 409). Whichever decision is recorded, the candidacy's `screened_by_elector_id` is set to the acting `X-User-ID`, so the record always shows who last touched it.

`ADVANCE` moves the candidacy from `SCREENING` to `ADVANCED`, which is the state a ballot slate is built from. It carries two additional preconditions beyond the three above: the owning cycle must itself still be in `SCREENING`, and the candidate's `hof_status` must still be `NOT_INDUCTED` — a candidate elected by another cycle in the meantime is refused with `ERR_CANDIDATE_INELIGIBLE`, exactly as at intake. Advancing increments the cycle's `advanced_candidacy_count` by 1, and that counter change increments the cycle's `version` by 1, which is precisely how a late advance invalidates an administrator's in-flight ballot construction.

`REJECT` moves the candidacy from `SCREENING` to the terminal `REJECTED` state and recomputes the cycle's `special_election_used` when the rejected candidacy's `pathway` was `SPECIAL`, releasing the cycle's single special slot. `DEFER` moves it from `SCREENING` to `DEFERRED`, which is likewise terminal for this cycle; a later cycle may file a fresh nomination whose `carryover_from_candidacy_id` points back at the deferred row, which is how a deferral becomes a second look rather than a dead end.

`REQUEST_INFO` is the one decision that does not end the review. It moves the candidacy from `SCREENING` to `INFO_REQUESTED` and increments its `info_request_count` by 1. The loop it opens — `SCREENING` → `INFO_REQUESTED` → `SCREENING` — is bounded: the committee may request information at most twice on the same candidacy, so a `REQUEST_INFO` decision on a candidacy whose `info_request_count` has already reached two is refused with `ERR_INFO_REQUEST_LIMIT` (HTTP 409).

The loop is closed by the nominating elector, and only by them. Supplying a supplement takes a single `supplement_note`, requires the candidacy to be in `INFO_REQUESTED` (otherwise `ERR_INVALID_CANDIDACY_STATE`), and requires the acting `X-User-ID` to equal the candidacy's `nominated_by_elector_id` — a different elector answering someone else's information request is refused with `ERR_NOMINATOR_IDENTITY_MISMATCH`. The supplement note is appended to the candidacy's `screening_notes` and the candidacy returns to `SCREENING`, where the committee may advance, reject, defer or — if it has a request left in the budget — query it again.

### 3.5. Printing the Ballot: Slates, Rounds and the Selection Cap

Ballot construction is the act that turns a screened field into a printed slate, and it is reserved to the `ElectionAdministrator`. The payload names the `cycle_id`, the `opens_at` instant at which voting opens, the `closes_at` instant at which voting closes, and the mandatory `expected_cycle_version` described in §2.8. The window must be well-formed — `opens_at` strictly before `closes_at` — and an inverted or zero-length window is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TIME_WINDOW` (HTTP 422).

Construction serves three different situations, and the slate is drawn from a different source in each. For the **first ballot of a cycle** the cycle must be in `SCREENING` with `advanced_candidacy_count` of at least 1, and the slate is every candidacy of the cycle whose `status` is `ADVANCED`; the construction drives the cycle `SCREENING` → `BALLOTING` and sets its `current_round` to 1. For a **runoff round** the cycle is already in `BALLOTING`, and the slate is exactly those slots that the previous round's audit certification marked `CARRIED_TO_RUNOFF`. For a **replacement of an expired ballot** the cycle is also in `BALLOTING`, and the slate is every candidacy of the cycle still in `ON_BALLOT`. In the two `BALLOTING` cases the newest ballot of the cycle must either be `AUDIT_CERTIFIED` with an `outcome` of `RUNOFF_REQUIRED`, or have a `voting_status` of `EXPIRED`; and in every case no ballot of the cycle may still be in `DRAFT` or `OPEN` — an attempt to print a second live slate is refused with `ERR_INVALID_BALLOT_STATE` (HTTP 409). If the applicable source yields nothing at all — no `ADVANCED` candidacy for a first round, no slot carried to the runoff for a runoff round, no `ON_BALLOT` candidacy when replacing an expired ballot — the construction is refused with `ERR_NO_SLATE_AVAILABLE` (HTTP 409). The cycle stays in `BALLOTING` across every round; it is a self-loop, not a chain of phases.

The new ballot's `round_number` records the round it serves. For a cycle's first ballot that is round 1, reached from a `current_round` of 0. For a runoff ballot it is the round the cycle is now contesting — the audit certification that declared the runoff already advanced `current_round`, so the runoff ballot's `round_number` is that advanced value and `current_round` is not advanced a second time. For a replacement of an expired ballot it is the `round_number` the expired ballot carried, and `current_round` is left exactly as it was, because a void round is re-run rather than skipped. Each construction increments the cycle's `version` by 1.

One `BallotSlot` is created per source candidacy. Each slot records its `ballot_id` and its `candidacy_id`, and then freezes three values so that a later edit cannot rewrite a printed ballot: `candidate_name_snapshot` copies the candidate's `full_name` at construction, `pathway_snapshot` copies the candidacy's `pathway`, and `candidacy_version_pinned` copies the candidacy's `version`. A slot is born `LISTED` with `votes_received` and `vote_pct` at 0; its other possible states are `ELECTED`, `NOT_ELECTED`, `CARRIED_TO_RUNOFF` and `STRUCK`. A slot whose candidacy later diverges from the pinned version is struck rather than silently re-read. The ballot's `slot_count` is the number of its slots whose `status` is anything other than `STRUCK`. On a first round each `ADVANCED` candidacy is simultaneously driven `ADVANCED` → `ON_BALLOT`, and that transition sets the candidacy's `appearance_number` to the candidate's `ballot_appearances_used` plus one — the appearance this printing represents.

Once the slots exist, the ballot's `max_votes_per_elector` is computed and frozen, and the order of the three steps is fixed. Start with the count of `LISTED` slots on this ballot, halve it and discard any fraction; struck slots are excluded from that count. Clamp the result into the range from a floor of 1 to a ceiling of 5 selections — a nine-slot ballot yields four, a three-slot ballot yields one, and a twelve-slot ballot is capped at five. Then, and only if this ballot's own `round_number` is greater than 1, halve the clamped figure again and round *up* to the next whole selection, with the same floor of 1, because a runoff is a narrowed contest. Finally, if at least one of this ballot's `LISTED` slots has a `pathway_snapshot` of `VETERANS`, add one selection for the veterans committee, capped once more at 5. The three steps happen in that order — clamp, then runoff halving, then veterans bonus — and reordering them produces different caps on the same slate. Note that the round used for the halving is this ballot's own `round_number`, not the cycle's `current_round`.

A constructed ballot is in `DRAFT` on the voting dimension and `NOT_TALLIED` on the tabulation dimension, with `ballots_cast`, `total_selections_counted`, `credentialed_roster_at_close`, `threshold_votes_provisional`, `threshold_votes_final` and `elected_slot_count` all at 0, and `outcome`, `tallied_by_user_id` and `audit_note` unset. The two dimensions are genuinely independent: `voting_status` runs `DRAFT` → `OPEN` → `CLOSED` (or `OPEN` → `EXPIRED`, which is terminal), while `tally_status` runs `NOT_TALLIED` → `PENDING_AUDIT` → `AUDIT_CERTIFIED`, with `PENDING_AUDIT` → `AUDIT_REJECTED` → `PENDING_AUDIT` as its rework loop, and the tabulation dimension advances while the voting dimension sits still at `CLOSED`.

Certifying the printed slate opens it for voting, moving `voting_status` from `DRAFT` to `OPEN` and incrementing the ballot's `version` by 1. It requires at least one slot on the ballot, and it requires the logical clock to be inside the declared window: at or after `opens_at` and strictly before `closes_at`. A certification attempt against a ballot that is not in `DRAFT`, or one made outside that window, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_BALLOT_STATE`.

Closing the ballot is where the ballot expiration rule lives, and the same administrator action produces two different outcomes depending on the clock. If the logical clock is still strictly before `closes_at`, `voting_status` moves `OPEN` → `CLOSED` and the ballot is ready to be tabulated. If the logical clock has reached or passed `closes_at`, `voting_status` moves `OPEN` → `EXPIRED` instead: the round is void, the ballot is permanently untabulatable, no vote cast on it is ever counted, and the cycle must construct a replacement ballot for the same `round_number`. Both outcomes snapshot `credentialed_roster_at_close` — the count of elector rows whose `status` is `CREDENTIALED` and whose `credential_expires_on` is at or after the closing instant — and both increment the ballot's `version` by 1. That snapshot is frozen on the ballot row and is never recomputed at read time, because it is the denominator of the final election threshold.

### 3.6. Casting and Withdrawing a Confidential Ballot

Voting is open to `Elector` and `ScreeningCommitteeMember` alike. A submission names the `ballot_id` being voted, carries the mandatory `expected_ballot_version` of §2.8, and expresses the elector's choices through two optional fields: `selected_slot_ids`, an array of ballot-slot identifiers, and `is_abstention`, a boolean defaulting to false that declares a deliberately empty ballot. The service takes the elector's identity from the acting `X-User-ID` and stores it as `elector_id`; a client cannot vote on another elector's behalf. The submission is written with `status` `COUNTED`, `selection_count` set to the length of `selected_slot_ids` (zero for an abstention), `cast_at` set to the logical clock's current instant, and `invalidated_reason` unset.

The ballot must be `OPEN`, otherwise the submission is refused with `ERR_INVALID_BALLOT_STATE`, and the logical clock must be strictly before the ballot's `closes_at`; a submission at or after that instant is refused with `ERR_VOTING_WINDOW_CLOSED` (HTTP 409). The elector must be `CREDENTIALED` with a `credential_expires_on` at or after the logical clock, per §3.2 — the two credential checks that guard nomination intake guard vote casting identically, with `ERR_ELECTOR_NOT_CREDENTIALED` and `ERR_CREDENTIAL_EXPIRED`.

Four further checks then run in a fixed order. First, every identifier in `selected_slot_ids` must belong to the ballot named by `ballot_id`; a stray slot from another ballot is refused with `ERR_SLOT_NOT_ON_BALLOT` (HTTP 422). Second, no selected slot may have a `status` of `STRUCK`; voting for a barred candidate is refused with `ERR_SLOT_STRUCK` (HTTP 409). Third, the elector may hold only one live submission per ballot: if another submission already exists for the same `ballot_id` and `elector_id` with a `status` of either `COUNTED` or `INVALIDATED`, the new one is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_SUBMISSION` (HTTP 409) — an invalidated submission still spends the seat, so an elector whose ballot the auditor threw out does not get a second one. Fourth, the selection count must respect the cap and the abstention flag together: the number of entries in `selected_slot_ids` must not exceed the ballot's `max_votes_per_elector`, and the list must be empty exactly when `is_abstention` is true. An over-long list, an empty list without the abstention flag, or an abstention that nonetheless names slots are all refused with `ERR_MAX_VOTES_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422).

An accepted submission moves three counters and refreshes one derived figure. The ballot's `ballots_cast` increases by 1 and its `total_selections_counted` increases by that submission's `selection_count`, so an abstention raises turnout without adding a single slot vote. The ballot's `version` increments by 1, and the new value is echoed back on the submission as `ballot_version_after` so that the next elector can supply a fresh `expected_ballot_version` without re-reading the ballot. And the elector's participation record is refreshed immediately: `last_participated_cycle_year` is set to the `cycle_year` of the ballot's cycle and `consecutive_missed_cycles` is reset to 0.

An elector may take a submission back while the window is still open. Withdrawal requires the acting `X-User-ID` to equal the submission's `elector_id` — withdrawing another elector's vote is refused with `ERR_NOMINATOR_IDENTITY_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403) — and requires the owning ballot to be `OPEN` with the logical clock strictly before `closes_at`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_BALLOT_STATE` or `ERR_VOTING_WINDOW_CLOSED` respectively. The submission moves `COUNTED` → `WITHDRAWN`, the ballot's `ballots_cast` decreases by 1 and its `total_selections_counted` decreases by exactly that submission's `selection_count`, and the ballot's `version` increments by 1 with the new value reported in `ballot_version_after`. Because the duplicate-submission check ignores `WITHDRAWN` rows, withdrawal frees the elector to cast a replacement on the same ballot — which is the whole point of the state.

As §2.7 sets out, an elector's `selected_slot_ids` is visible only to the `IndependentAuditor`; every other role, including the casting elector and the administrator, reads that field as `null` on both the single-submission read and the list. `selection_count` remains visible to all, which lets the institution publish turnout without publishing choices.

### 3.7. Tabulation, Independent Audit and Round Resolution

Tabulation happens in two stages performed by two different people, and the separation is the point. The `ElectionAdministrator` produces a **draft tally**; the `IndependentAuditor` then either certifies it or throws it back. Neither can do the other's job.

The draft tally requires the ballot's `voting_status` to be `CLOSED` — a ballot still `DRAFT` or `OPEN` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_BALLOT_STATE` — and its `tally_status` to be either `NOT_TALLIED` (a first tally) or `AUDIT_REJECTED` (a re-tally), with anything else refused as `ERR_INVALID_TALLY_STATE` (HTTP 409). A ballot whose `voting_status` is `EXPIRED` is never tabulated and never audited under any circumstances; both operations on such a ballot are refused with `ERR_BALLOT_EXPIRED` (HTTP 409). And there must be something to count: a ballot whose `ballots_cast` is 0 is refused with `ERR_NO_BALLOTS_CAST` (HTTP 409).

Counting itself considers only submissions whose `status` is `COUNTED`; `WITHDRAWN` and `INVALIDATED` submissions are excluded from every figure. Those submissions are processed in ascending `cast_at` order, with ascending `created_at` as the tie-break — a fixed order, although the result is order-independent by construction. The ballot's `ballots_cast` is the number of such submissions and its `total_selections_counted` is the sum of their `selection_count` values. Then, for each slot of the ballot whose `status` is `LISTED`, `votes_received` becomes the number of counted submissions whose `selected_slot_ids` contains that slot's identifier, and `vote_pct` becomes `votes_received` divided by `ballots_cast`, rounded half-up to two decimal places — or exactly 0 when `ballots_cast` is 0. Slots whose `status` is `STRUCK` keep `votes_received` at 0 and `vote_pct` at 0 and take no part in the count. An abstention raises `ballots_cast`, and therefore raises every slot's denominator, while contributing no votes to any slot — abstaining is a way of voting against the whole field.

The draft tally also computes the **provisional election threshold**, which is turnout-based. Take the `election_threshold_pct` of the house threshold row (the eligibility rule for `PLAYER` paired with `MODERN`, per §3.1), multiply it by the ballot's `ballots_cast`, and round the product up to the next whole vote; the result is stored as `threshold_votes_provisional`. Because the denominator is turnout, electors who did not vote lower the bar. Finally the draft tally records `tallied_by_user_id` as the acting administrator's `X-User-ID`, moves `tally_status` from `NOT_TALLIED` (or `AUDIT_REJECTED`) to `PENDING_AUDIT`, and increments the ballot's `version` by 1.

The audit decision is exclusive to the `IndependentAuditor` and carries a `decision` of either `REJECT` or `CERTIFY`, an optional `invalidate_submission_ids` array, and an optional `audit_note`. The ballot's `tally_status` must be `PENDING_AUDIT`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_TALLY_STATE`. And the auditor's `X-User-ID` must differ from the ballot's stored `tallied_by_user_id`: an operator auditing their own count is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

A `REJECT` decision moves `tally_status` from `PENDING_AUDIT` to `AUDIT_REJECTED` and cascades three levels deep. Every submission whose identifier appears in `invalidate_submission_ids` moves `COUNTED` → `INVALIDATED` and stores the auditor's `audit_note` as its `invalidated_reason`. Each invalidation decrements the ballot's `ballots_cast` by 1 and reduces its `total_selections_counted` by that submission's `selection_count`. Each of those reductions in turn reduces `votes_received` on every slot the invalidated submission had selected and re-derives that slot's `vote_pct` against the new `ballots_cast`. The rejection then resets `threshold_votes_provisional` to 0 and clears `tallied_by_user_id`, which is what allows a re-tally — possibly by a different administrator — to claim the count afresh. A re-tally over the surviving counted submissions closes the loop `PENDING_AUDIT` → `AUDIT_REJECTED` → `PENDING_AUDIT`, recomputing every slot figure and the provisional threshold from scratch and writing the new `tallied_by_user_id`.

A `CERTIFY` decision first tests bloc participation. For every voter bloc whose `seats_filled` is at least 1, the number of counted submissions on this ballot cast by electors of that bloc, divided by that bloc's `seats_filled`, must be greater than or equal to the bloc's `min_participation_pct`. A single under-participating seated bloc blocks the whole certification with `ERR_BLOC_QUORUM_NOT_MET` (HTTP 409); blocs with no seated electors are ignored.

Certification then computes the **final election threshold**, which is roster-based and replaces nothing — it sits alongside the provisional figure. Take the same house `election_threshold_pct`, multiply it by the ballot's frozen `credentialed_roster_at_close`, round the product up to the next whole vote, and store it as `threshold_votes_final`. Because the denominator is the full credentialed roster rather than turnout, non-voters now count against every candidate: the final threshold is always at least as large as the provisional one, and a slot can sit exactly at the provisional threshold and still fall short of the final one. Both thresholds are compared against a slot's `votes_received` inclusively, and both denominators are frozen values on the ballot row that are never recomputed at read time.

With `threshold_votes_final` in hand, the round is resolved by a seat waterfall over the ballot's `LISTED` slots — struck slots never qualify for anything. The qualifying slots are those whose `votes_received` is greater than or equal to `threshold_votes_final`. They are ranked by `votes_received` descending, with the older candidacy first (ascending candidacy `created_at`) as the tie-break; the row identifier is never used as a tie-break. The number of seats available is the cycle's `max_inductees` minus the number of candidacies of this cycle already in `ELECTED` status from earlier rounds. The elected set is the leading run of the ranked list, taking as many as there are seats or as many as qualified, whichever is fewer. Separately, the **carry set** is every `LISTED` slot that was not elected and whose `vote_pct` is greater than or equal to the `runoff_floor_pct` of the eligibility rule matching that slot's candidacy — its candidate's `category` paired with the candidacy's `pathway`.

The `outcome` is resolved *before* any slot status is written, so a carried status can never exist without a runoff round to carry it into. If at least one slot was elected, the `outcome` is `ELECTION_COMPLETE`. Otherwise, if this ballot's `round_number` is less than 3 — three rounds is the hard ceiling on the runoff loop — and the carry set holds at least two slots, the `outcome` is `RUNOFF_REQUIRED`. In every other case the `outcome` is `NO_ELECTION`; note in particular that a round in which exactly one slot cleared the runoff floor and nobody was elected resolves `NO_ELECTION`, because a runoff needs at least two contenders to be a contest.

Slot statuses follow from the resolved outcome. Every slot in the elected set becomes `ELECTED`. Every slot in the carry set becomes `CARRIED_TO_RUNOFF`, but only when the `outcome` is `RUNOFF_REQUIRED`. Every other `LISTED` slot becomes `NOT_ELECTED`. The ballot's `elected_slot_count` is the size of the elected set. Certification then copies each scored candidacy's figures back onto the candidacy itself: `latest_round_votes` takes its slot's `votes_received` and `latest_round_pct` takes its slot's `vote_pct`, rounded to two decimal places, so that the carryover machinery in §3.8 can read a candidacy's last result without re-reading the ballot.

Two candidacy-level cascades ride along. A slot set to `CARRIED_TO_RUNOFF` leaves its candidacy in `ON_BALLOT`, alive for the next round. A slot set to `NOT_ELECTED` under an `outcome` of `RUNOFF_REQUIRED` — which is exactly the case where its `vote_pct` fell below the applicable `runoff_floor_pct` — drives its candidacy `ON_BALLOT` → `ELIMINATED`, and the slot is marked `NOT_ELECTED` so the runoff ballot does not reprint it. Finally, when the `outcome` is `RUNOFF_REQUIRED`, the certification increments the cycle's `current_round` by 1 and the cycle's `version` by 1; when the outcome is `ELECTION_COMPLETE` or `NO_ELECTION` it leaves both alone, because the cycle is finished balloting. Either way the ballot's own `version` increments by 1, and the auditor's `audit_note` is recorded on the ballot.

### 3.8. Certifying the Election: Inductions, Carryover, Expiration and Roster Maintenance

Election certification is a single administrator action that closes out the balloting phase and pays out every consequence at once. It requires the cycle to be in `BALLOTING`, and it requires the newest ballot of the cycle to have a `tally_status` of `AUDIT_CERTIFIED` with an `outcome` of either `ELECTION_COMPLETE` or `NO_ELECTION`. A cycle whose newest ballot has not been certified, or whose certified outcome was `RUNOFF_REQUIRED`, is refused with `ERR_ELECTION_NOT_FINAL` (HTTP 409) — the runoff must be run first. On success the cycle moves `BALLOTING` → `CERTIFIED` and its `version` increments by 1.

For each slot on that final ballot whose `status` is `ELECTED`, the owning candidacy is driven `ON_BALLOT` → `ELECTED`. That transition sets the candidate's `hof_status` to `INDUCTED`, increments the candidate's `ballot_appearances_used` by 1, and creates exactly one `Induction`. Each new induction records the `cycle_id` that elected it, the `candidacy_id` that won and the `candidate_id` of the person, and it freezes a `delegation_type` derived from the candidate's `is_deceased` flag as read at certification: `LIVING` when the candidate was not deceased, `POSTHUMOUS_DELEGATION` when they were. That frozen value is what later selects the travel award variant, and it does not change if the candidate's mortality is recorded afterwards. Each induction creation increments the cycle's `induction_count` by 1 and recomputes the cycle's `ceremony_ready_count`.

Every candidacy of the cycle that is still `ON_BALLOT` or `ELIMINATED` — that is, every scored candidacy that was not elected — is then resolved by the carryover-and-expiration rule, processed in ascending candidacy `created_at` order. Elected candidacies are excluded, having already had their increment. The rule increments the candidate's `ballot_appearances_used` by 1 first, because the appearance was in fact used, and only then compares. If the incremented `ballot_appearances_used` is greater than or equal to the `max_ballot_appearances` of the eligibility rule for the candidate's `category` paired with the candidacy's `pathway`, the candidacy becomes `EXPIRED`. Otherwise, if the candidacy's `latest_round_pct` is greater than or equal to that rule's `carryover_min_pct` — an inclusive comparison — it becomes `CARRYOVER_ELIGIBLE`, alive for a future cycle. In any other case it becomes `EXPIRED`. Because the increment precedes the exhaustion test, a candidate whose third appearance was just scored under a `max_ballot_appearances` of 3 expires even on a commanding vote share; the appearance budget is a hard stop, not a tiebreaker.

Every candidacy that lands in `EXPIRED` writes a veterans-committee floor onto its candidate. The candidate's `veterans_eligible_from_year` becomes the later of two years: the candidate's `retirement_year` plus twenty-five years, and this cycle's `cycle_year` plus three years. The first term is the quarter-century career gap the veterans route requires; the second is a three-year cooling-off period after a failed modern candidacy, so a candidate who exhausts the modern ballot cannot reappear immediately through the veterans door. Whichever is later governs, and §3.3's veterans check reads exactly this stored value.

Certification closes with **roster maintenance**, which is how a voting body stays a voting body. Every elector whose `status` is `CREDENTIALED` at certification time is examined, in ascending elector `created_at` order. `SUSPENDED` electors are skipped entirely and keep their counters untouched — suspension is not disengagement. An elector counts as having participated when at least one submission exists with `status` `COUNTED`, with their `elector_id`, on a ballot of this cycle whose `tally_status` is `AUDIT_CERTIFIED`; votes cast on an `EXPIRED` or `AUDIT_REJECTED` ballot are not participation, because those rounds produced no result. A participating elector has `consecutive_missed_cycles` reset to 0 and `last_participated_cycle_year` set to this cycle's `cycle_year`. A non-participating elector has `consecutive_missed_cycles` incremented by 1, and if the incremented counter is greater than or equal to 2 the elector's `status` flips to the terminal `LAPSED` — the comparison is inclusive and is evaluated after the increment, so two consecutive missed certified cycles is the trigger, not three. A lapse decrements that elector's bloc `seats_filled` by 1, freeing the seat for a new credential, and the freed seat is immediately available to the credentialing check of §3.2.

### 3.9. From Election to Enshrinement: Ceremony, Travel, Artifacts and Exhibits

A certified election produces inductions, and an induction is not a single state but three parallel dimensions plus an overall standing. The overall `status` runs `ANNOUNCED` → `ENSHRINED`, and `ENSHRINED` is terminal. Underneath it, `travel_status` runs across `PENDING`, `REQUESTED`, `APPROVED` and `WAIVED`; `artifact_status` across `AWAITED`, `RECEIVED` and `EXEMPT`; and `exhibit_status` across `NOT_PLANNED` and `INSTALLED`. A fresh induction sits at `ANNOUNCED` / `PENDING` / `AWAITED` / `NOT_PLANNED` with `artifact_count` at 0 and `ceremony_ready` false. The three dimensions advance independently of one another, driven by three different workflows, and any mutation that addresses a dimension already past its from-state — or any mutation at all on an induction whose `status` is `ENSHRINED` — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_INDUCTION_STATE` (HTTP 409).

Ceremony planning is the administrator's gate between election and logistics. It requires the cycle to be in `CERTIFIED` with an `induction_count` of at least 1, and it takes a `ceremony_date` and a `ceremony_venue`, optionally revising `travel_budget_cents` and `escort_pool_size` at the same time. The `ceremony_date` must be strictly later than the logical clock's current instant; a ceremony scheduled in the past or exactly now is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TIME_WINDOW` (HTTP 422). The transition stores all four values, moves the cycle `CERTIFIED` → `CEREMONY_PLANNED`, recomputes `austerity_tier` from the ratio of `travel_committed_cents` to `travel_budget_cents`, and increments the cycle's `version` by 1. Until the cycle reaches `CEREMONY_PLANNED`, no travel grant may be created, approved, denied or cancelled — all four operations are refused with `ERR_CEREMONY_NOT_PLANNED` (HTTP 409).

**Requesting travel.** Either an `ElectionAdministrator` or `CuratorialStaff` may request funding for one induction. The payload names the `induction_id`, the number of hotel `nights` funded per traveler (at least 1), the `requested_airfare_cents` per traveler (zero or more), and the mandatory `expected_cycle_version` of §2.8. Two optional timestamps, `escort_from` and `escort_to`, declare the ceremony-escort occupation interval, and an optional `cycle_id` may be supplied purely as a cross-check: when present it must equal the induction's own `cycle_id`, and a mismatch is refused with `ERR_INDUCTION_CYCLE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). The funding cycle whose phase, budget and `version` are actually read is always the induction's own cycle, whether or not the client names it. The grant requires the induction's `travel_status` to be `PENDING`, and it drives that dimension `PENDING` → `REQUESTED`, which recomputes `ceremony_ready` (still false, since a merely requested grant funds nothing). The grant's `cycle_id` is copied from the induction, its `variant` and `traveler_count` are derived from the induction's frozen `delegation_type`, its `austerity_tier_applied` is frozen from the cycle's pre-commitment tier, its `status` is `REQUESTED`, and its `cancellation_reason` is unset. The cycle's `version` increments by 1 and the new value is reported on the grant as `cycle_version_after`.

The escort interval is mandatory for one variant only. A grant whose `variant` is `POSTHUMOUS_DELEGATION` must supply both `escort_from` and `escort_to`, with `escort_from` no later than `escort_to`; a missing endpoint or an inverted interval is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ESCORT_INTERVAL` (HTTP 422). For a `LIVING_INDUCTEE` grant the interval is optional and may be omitted entirely.

**The award.** `award_cents` is computed once, at creation, and frozen; approval never recomputes it. The computation runs in three pinned steps. Step one reads the **austerity tier** from the cycle's budget position *before this grant exists*: divide `travel_committed_cents` by `travel_budget_cents` to get the commitment ratio. A ratio below one half yields the `FULL` tier; a ratio from one half up to but not including four fifths yields `REDUCED`; a ratio of four fifths or more yields `MINIMAL`. Both tier boundaries are inclusive at their lower edge — a cycle exactly half-committed is `REDUCED`, and a cycle exactly four-fifths committed is `MINIMAL`. The tier selects a spending factor: one for `FULL`, eight tenths for `REDUCED`, six tenths for `MINIMAL`. Only grants already in `APPROVED` status contribute to `travel_committed_cents`; grants sitting in `REQUESTED`, `DENIED` or `CANCELLED` contribute nothing, so the tier reflects committed money rather than pending intent. That tier is written onto the grant as `austerity_tier_applied` and never revisited.

Step two reads the variant. A `POSTHUMOUS_DELEGATION` grant funds two travellers — the delegation that accepts on the inductee's behalf — at a per-diem factor of three quarters. A `LIVING_INDUCTEE` grant funds one traveller at a per-diem factor of one. Step three computes the cost **per traveller and rounds it there**, then multiplies. The nightly per-diem allowance is 18,333 cents — one hundred eighty-three dollars and thirty-three cents a night — and one traveller's per-diem is that allowance multiplied by `nights`, multiplied by the tier's spending factor, multiplied by the variant's per-diem factor, then rounded half-up to whole cents. One traveller's airfare is the lesser of `requested_airfare_cents` and the airfare cap of 95,000 cents, so a nine-hundred-fifty-dollar cap silently absorbs any larger request. Those two figures are added to give one traveller's total, and `award_cents` is that per-traveller total multiplied by `traveler_count`.

The rounding order in step three is not a detail — it is the contract. Rounding each traveller's per-diem to whole cents *first* and multiplying afterwards differs by a cent from summing raw per-diems and rounding once at the end. A three-night posthumous grant at the `REDUCED` tier illustrates it: each traveller's raw per-diem is 32,999.4 cents, which rounds to 32,999 cents per traveller and therefore 65,998 cents for the pair, whereas summing first and rounding once would give 65,999. The per-traveller rounding is correct.

**Approving, denying, cancelling.** All three decisions belong to the `ElectionAdministrator`, all three require the funding cycle to be in `CEREMONY_PLANNED`, and all three refresh the grant's `cycle_version_after`. A grant not in the required from-state — `REQUESTED` for approval and denial, `APPROVED` for cancellation — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_GRANT_STATE` (HTTP 409).

Approval is the moment money moves. The frozen `award_cents` must fit the remaining budget: it must be less than or equal to the cycle's `travel_budget_cents` minus its `travel_committed_cents`, and a grant that does not fit is refused with `ERR_TRAVEL_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409). That same fit test is also applied at creation time, so an award that could never be funded is rejected before it is ever recorded. Approval then increases the cycle's `travel_committed_cents` by `award_cents`, which recomputes the cycle's `austerity_tier` from the new ratio and increments the cycle's `version` by 1; drives the induction's `travel_status` `REQUESTED` → `APPROVED`, which recomputes that induction's `ceremony_ready` and, when readiness flips to true, increments the cycle's `ceremony_ready_count` by 1; and occupies the escort interval for posthumous delegations. The grant's own `status` becomes `APPROVED`.

Escort capacity is checked at approval, before any money moves, and it treats the escort interval as **closed at both ends** — an interval that ends at exactly the instant another begins counts as one instant of overlap. The check considers only grants of the same cycle whose `status` is `APPROVED` and whose `escort_from` is not null, and only those whose interval intersects the new one under closed-interval arithmetic: the existing `escort_from` is at or before the new `escort_to`, and the new `escort_from` is at or before the existing `escort_to`. It then probes concurrency at a finite set of instants — the `escort_from` and `escort_to` of every such occupied interval, plus the new interval's own two endpoints, all clipped to the new interval. At each probe instant it counts the occupied intervals that contain that instant, and if that count plus one for the new grant would be greater than the cycle's `escort_pool_size`, the approval is refused with `ERR_ESCORT_POOL_EXHAUSTED` (HTTP 409). Capacity is aggregate, not exclusive: overlap is an error only once the concurrent count would exceed the pool, so a pool of three tolerates three simultaneous delegations. Grants in `REQUESTED`, `DENIED` or `CANCELLED` status free their interval immediately and never obstruct anyone.

Denial takes a `cancellation_reason`, moves the grant `REQUESTED` → `DENIED`, and deliberately leaves the cycle's `travel_committed_cents` and `austerity_tier` untouched, because nothing was ever committed. Its interesting effect is on the induction: `travel_status` moves `REQUESTED` → `WAIVED`, meaning the inductee travels unfunded and stops blocking ceremony readiness. `WAIVED` is as good as `APPROVED` for readiness purposes.

Cancellation of an approved grant takes a `cancellation_reason` and is a true compensating reversal. The grant moves `APPROVED` → `CANCELLED`. The cycle's `travel_committed_cents` decreases by exactly this grant's `award_cents`, restoring the pre-approval budget position precisely, and that decrease recomputes the cycle's `austerity_tier` and increments the cycle's `version` by 1. The induction's `travel_status` moves `APPROVED` → `PENDING`, which recomputes `ceremony_ready` and, when readiness flips from true to false, decrements the cycle's `ceremony_ready_count` by 1. The escort occupation interval is freed for other grants. Note that returning the induction to `PENDING` rather than `REQUESTED` is what allows a fresh grant to be requested for that inductee at the current tier.

**Artifacts.** `CuratorialStaff` record what an inductee donates. The intake accepts either an `artifact_count` of at least 1 or an `is_exempt` flag set to true, and never both meaningfully: a request that supplies neither an `artifact_count` of at least 1 nor `is_exempt` set to true is malformed and refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). The induction's `artifact_status` must be `AWAITED`. When artifacts are donated, `artifact_status` moves `AWAITED` → `RECEIVED` and the induction's `artifact_count` is set to the number supplied. When the induction is exempt, `artifact_status` moves `AWAITED` → `EXEMPT` and `artifact_count` stays at 0. Either way, ceremony readiness is recomputed for that induction and the cycle's `ceremony_ready_count` is re-derived.

**Exhibits.** An installation couples an induction to a display case for a display window, and it is `CuratorialStaff` work. The payload names the `induction_id`, the `exhibit_case_id`, the window's inclusive start `display_starts_on`, its exclusive end `display_ends_on`, and the `artifact_count` occupying case capacity for this window (at least 1). The window must be well formed — `display_starts_on` strictly before `display_ends_on` — or it is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TIME_WINDOW` (HTTP 422). The induction's artifacts must actually be in hand: `artifact_status` must be `RECEIVED`, and an installation requested while it is `AWAITED` or `EXEMPT` is refused with `ERR_ARTIFACTS_NOT_RECEIVED` (HTTP 409). An artifact-exempt induction never gets a case window at all, which is the deliberate reason its exhibit dimension can remain `NOT_PLANNED` and still let a ceremony proceed.

Windows are **half-open**: a window runs from its start inclusive to its end exclusive, so one window may begin at exactly the instant another ends. Against every other installation in the same case whose `status` is `INSTALLED`, the new window is tested one of two ways. If the other installation belongs to the **same induction**, plain half-open intersection applies: the conflict is real when the new start is strictly before the other's end and the other's start is strictly before the new end, so back-to-back windows for one inductee are legal. If the other installation belongs to a **different induction**, the case's `changeover_buffer_days` of dark time is required on both sides: the conflict is real when the new start is strictly before the other's end extended forward by that many whole days, and the other's start is strictly before the new end extended forward by the same amount. Either kind of conflict is refused with `ERR_CASE_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409). The gap comparison is inclusive, so a window starting exactly `changeover_buffer_days` after the previous induction's window ended is accepted.

Capacity is checked only after the window survives. The case's current `artifacts_installed` plus the new `artifact_count` must not exceed the case's `capacity_artifacts`, and an over-subscription is refused with `ERR_CASE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409). On success the installation is written with `status` `INSTALLED`, the case's `artifacts_installed` increases by the new `artifact_count`, and the induction's `exhibit_status` moves `NOT_PLANNED` → `INSTALLED` if this is the first installation for that induction, leaving it at `INSTALLED` for later ones — the exhibit dimension is a first-installation flag, not a one-shot lock, and an induction already holding an installed window may take further windows. That transition requires the induction's `artifact_status` to be `RECEIVED`, recomputes `ceremony_ready`, and increments the cycle's `ceremony_ready_count` by 1 when readiness flips to true.

Withdrawing an installation moves it `INSTALLED` → `WITHDRAWN`, requires the owning induction's `status` to still be `ANNOUNCED`, and is the exhibit counterpart of grant cancellation: the case's `artifacts_installed` decreases by exactly that installation's `artifact_count`, restoring the pre-installation occupancy precisely, and both the window and the capacity are released so a later installation may occupy them. If no other installation of that induction is still `INSTALLED`, the induction's `exhibit_status` moves `INSTALLED` → `NOT_PLANNED`, which recomputes `ceremony_ready` and decrements the cycle's `ceremony_ready_count` when readiness flips to false. If a sibling installation is still `INSTALLED`, the dimension stays at `INSTALLED` and readiness is unaffected.

**Readiness.** An induction's `ceremony_ready` flag is re-derived from scratch on every mutation of any of its three dimensions, and it is true exactly when the travel dimension is settled *and* the display obligation is discharged. Concretely: `travel_status` must be either `APPROVED` or `WAIVED`; and either `artifact_status` is `RECEIVED` with `exhibit_status` at `INSTALLED`, or `artifact_status` is `EXEMPT` — an exempt induction has nothing to display and is ready without any installation. The cycle's `ceremony_ready_count` is likewise re-derived as the number of inductions of that cycle whose `ceremony_ready` is true, never incrementally patched from a stale value.

**Holding the ceremony.** The final act requires the cycle to be in `CEREMONY_PLANNED`, requires the logical clock to have reached or passed the stored `ceremony_date` — an early ceremony is refused with `ERR_CEREMONY_DATE_NOT_REACHED` (HTTP 409) — and requires the cycle's `ceremony_ready_count` to equal its `induction_count` exactly, so that no inductee is left behind; a shortfall is refused with `ERR_INDUCTIONS_NOT_READY` (HTTP 409). On success the cycle moves `CEREMONY_PLANNED` → `CLOSED` and every induction of the cycle is driven `ANNOUNCED` → `ENSHRINED`, which freezes all three of its dimensions: no further travel, artifact or exhibit mutation is accepted on an enshrined induction. The cycle's `version` increments by 1, and with `CLOSED` the cycle's life is over.
