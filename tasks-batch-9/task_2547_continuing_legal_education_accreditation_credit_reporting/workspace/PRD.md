# CLE Accreditation & Credit Reporting API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A state continuing legal education board sits between two populations that never talk to each other directly. On one side are course sponsors — bar associations, law schools, government agencies, commercial training companies and law firms — who want their programming to count toward the state's mandatory education requirement. On the other side are licensed attorneys who must accumulate a fixed quantity of instructional minutes in defined categories inside a fixed compliance period, prove they attended, and file on time or pay for the privilege of filing late. The board's staff must reconcile these two ledgers continuously: a course that is revoked eighteen months after it was presented invalidates credit that attorneys have already banked, and an attorney whose banked credit evaporates may fall out of compliance in a period that was previously certified as satisfied.

This document specifies the API that runs that reconciliation. It covers two distinct accreditation pathways — blanket provider accreditation, under which a sponsor in good standing self-certifies its own catalogue, and individual course approval, under which a single course is reviewed, possibly denied, and possibly appealed to an independent panel. It covers the credit side: category classification across general, ethics, technology and elimination-of-bias instruction; per-cycle caps that discourage attorneys from satisfying the entire requirement through self-study; attendance verification by roster, attendance code, sponsor certificate or personal affirmation; carryover of surplus credit into the following period; and reciprocity and comity treatment of out-of-state instruction. It covers enforcement: delinquency notices, tiered late fees, administrative suspension and reinstatement, provider audits with risk-weighted sampling, complaint handling, and the sanctions that flow from a substantiated complaint.

Three design commitments shape everything below. First, **every deadline in this domain is measured against a logical clock the board controls**, never against host wall-clock time, so that a compliance period, an appeal window and a roster filing window can all be exercised deterministically. Second, **money is conserved**: an attorney's outstanding balance is always exactly the sum of the assessments still standing against them, and every settlement path moves that balance by exactly the assessed amount. Third, **credit figures are always reproducible from stored data**: the same credit-application algorithm runs on every mutation and on every read of a cycle summary, in one of two variants, and its output is persisted rather than cached from an earlier run.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

Everything in this section is a contract. Where §3 narrates how the business behaves, §2 fixes the shape of the wire, the ordering of rejections, and the arithmetic conventions that the whole domain shares. All business paths are mounted under `/cle`; the single test-driving clock endpoint is mounted at the server root.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request — read or write — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming that principal's role. If either header is absent or empty, or if `X-User-Role` does not name one of the five declared roles, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before anything else is examined. When the headers are well formed but the named role is not among the roles the addressed endpoint permits, the rejection is `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403). The same code covers action-level reservations on shared endpoints: fee settlement is open to three roles, but only a `BoardAdmin` may request the `WAIVE` action there, so a `WAIVE` from an `Attorney` or a `ProviderAdmin` is an `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.

The five roles are `BoardAdmin`, `AccreditationReviewer`, `AppealsPanelist`, `ProviderAdmin` and `Attorney`.

- **`BoardAdmin`** is state CLE board staff. It owns provider accreditation decisions, attorney registration and standing changes, compliance-period creation, reciprocity determinations, audits, complaint dispositions, credit adjudication, fee waivers, the reporting sweep and the logical clock.
- **`AccreditationReviewer`** is the board reviewer who adjudicates individual course-approval applications, and nothing else on the write side.
- **`AppealsPanelist`** is an independent panelist who decides appeals of course-approval denials. A panelist cannot file, review or supplement an application — only decide one that is under appeal, and only if that panelist did not review it.
- **`ProviderAdmin`** administers exactly one provider. It registers courses under its own sponsorship, files applications and session rosters, and settles its own provider-side fees.
- **`Attorney`** is a licensed attorney subject to the requirement. An attorney claims credit, files affirmations, certifies its own compliance periods, files reciprocity and comity claims and complaints, and may seek individual approval of a course that has no sponsoring provider.

Identity binding differs by role, and the difference is load-bearing. When `X-User-Role` is `ProviderAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Provider`; ownership checks on any resource that references a provider compare the resource's provider reference to `X-User-ID` — a course's or an audit's `provider_id`, and for a session roster the `provider_id` of the roster's course. When `X-User-Role` is `Attorney`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Attorney`; ownership checks compare `X-User-ID` to the `attorney_id` of the compliance cycle, credit record, reciprocity claim or fee assessment being touched, and to the `attorney_id` of the cycle behind an affirmation or a certification. When `X-User-Role` is `BoardAdmin`, `AccreditationReviewer` or `AppealsPanelist`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record such an identifier — as `reviewer_user_id` when a reviewer acts on an application, as `appeal_panelist_user_id` when a panelist decides one, as `complainant_user_id` when a board member files a complaint, and as `applicant_user_id`/`complainant_user_id` generally — but it never compares an opaque identifier to any row's `id`. An entity-backed role addressing a resource it does not own is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403); the same code covers a supplement or an appeal attempted by anyone other than the application's own `applicant_user_id`.

Two seats in this system are deliberately incompatible with each other, and the incompatibility is enforced on `X-User-ID` rather than on role. The operator who issued a review action on an application (recorded as `reviewer_user_id`) may not decide that application's appeal, and the operator who filed a complaint (recorded as `complainant_user_id`) may not substantiate or dismiss it. Either violation is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). No `BoardAdmin` may decide an appeal at all — that endpoint admits only `AppealsPanelist` — so appeals are structurally separated from the staff who grant and revoke accreditation.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Collection endpoints accept two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A supplied value outside those bounds is a malformed request rather than a silently clamped one, and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Unless a specific collection declares otherwise, a list is ordered by `created_at` ascending. Four collections declare otherwise because their natural reading order is not their insertion order: the course list is returned newest first, ordered by `created_at` descending; the compliance-cycle list is ordered by `period_start` ascending and then `created_at` ascending; the credit-record list is ordered by `completion_date` ascending and then `created_at` ascending, which is exactly the order the credit-application algorithm consumes records in; and the provider-audit list is ordered by `audit_period_start` ascending and then `created_at` ascending. Every nested sub-collection — a course's credit allocations, a course's attendance reports, a course's applications, an attorney's reciprocity claims, a cycle's affirmations — is ordered by `created_at` ascending.

For every list response, the `meta` object carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`. `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied, and `has_more` is true exactly when rows remain beyond the returned page.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock

Nothing in this domain reads the host clock. A single logical clock supplies "now" to every deadline, expiry, window and lateness comparison: compliance-period membership, filing deadlines, the delinquency-to-suspension interval, the late-fee grace window, appeal windows, roster due dates and hard-close dates, course expiry, accreditation-term expiry, and the staleness term of the provider risk score. Before the clock is ever set it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. That fixed startup default is the system's earliest instant and must never fall back to real wall-clock time. A `BoardAdmin` may move the clock to an arbitrary instant by supplying `to_iso8601`; the move mutates no business row, and it never retroactively re-evaluates deadlines by itself — the reporting sweep is what observes the clock and acts on what it finds.

All timestamps on the wire are ISO 8601 UTC with three fractional digits, for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`. Day arithmetic — the roster due date, the appeal window, the suspension interval, the grace window, the filing-deadline offsets — is UTC calendar-day arithmetic against the logical clock. Month arithmetic — accreditation terms, on-demand approval validity, the requirement-proration month count and the risk-score staleness term — is whole-calendar-month arithmetic.

### 2.5. Record Identity, Timestamps and Versioning

Every persisted resource carries a server-minted `id` that is a UUID string with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp recorded at insertion. Most resources also carry an `updated_at` timestamp, refreshed on every mutation of that row, and an integer `version` that starts at 1 on creation and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row — including mutations the row did not ask for, such as a counter bump driven by a cascade from another entity.

Three resources are exceptions and carry only `id` and `created_at`: a credit allocation, an affirmation and a reporting sweep. All three are immutable once written — an allocation is fixed by the course that created it, an affirmation is a sworn statement that is never edited, and a sweep is a historical run record — so for these three `updated_at` and `version` are absent from the payload entirely, not merely null.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Three write endpoints are guarded by optimistic concurrency control on a parent row's `version`. In each case the guarded write is legitimately repeatable — a renewal supersedes the prior term, a re-filed claim supersedes the earlier filing, a redraw supersedes the prior sample — so the guard is not there to break ties between simultaneous callers. It is there to ensure that a supersede-style write is based on the parent's standing as the client actually observed it.

- `POST /cle/providers/{id}/accreditation-actions` requires `expected_provider_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Provider`. If it does not equal that provider's current `version`, the action is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This is what stops a board clerk from extending a term on a provider whose standing, term or self-certification flag has already moved — for instance because an audit sanction pushed it to `PROBATIONARY`.
- `POST /cle/reciprocity-claims` requires `expected_attorney_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the named `Attorney`. A mismatch with that attorney's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412), because the attorney row may meanwhile have absorbed an approved claim, a fee assessment or an administrative suspension, all of which change whether and how a new claim converts.
- `POST /cle/provider-audits/{id}/audit-actions` requires `expected_audit_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `ProviderAudit`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412), which prevents a redraw or a findings filing computed against a sample the audit no longer holds.

In all three cases the version field is a required input, not an optional one; omitting it is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. No other endpoint takes a version field, and no other endpoint may reject a request for staleness.

### 2.7. Minutes, Credit Hours and Rounding Discipline

Instructional time is stored and transported in whole minutes, never in fractional hours. One credit hour is 60 minutes. The board's rounding increment is 15 minutes: every course-level category allocation must be a positive whole multiple of 15 minutes, and every per-category proration of an attendance claim is rounded **down** to a multiple of 15 minutes.

Three rounding conventions are pinned and are not interchangeable:

1. **Per-category proration rounds down, then sums.** When an attendance claim is prorated across categories, each category's minutes are floored to a 15-minute multiple *before* the four category figures are added together. Summing the exact prorated values first and rounding the sum once is a different rule and produces a different total; it is explicitly not the rule here.
2. **A cycle's total requirement rounds up to a whole credit hour.** A prorated requirement is computed as an exact rational number of credit hours, rounded up to the next whole hour, and then expressed in minutes.
3. **Deficiency hours round up.** When a shortfall in minutes is converted to hours for late-fee purposes, any fraction of an hour counts as a whole hour.

Money is stored in whole cents as integers; no monetary figure is ever fractional and no monetary figure is ever rounded, because every fee in this domain is either a table constant, a sum of table constants, or a minimum of the two against a cap. The only decimal quantity in the system is the provider risk score, which lies between 0.00 and 1.00 inclusive and is rounded half up to exactly two decimal places, applied once to the finished weighted sum and never to the individual weighted terms.

### 2.8. Order of Rejection

When a single request violates several rules, the response must name the violation that comes first in the following order. This order is normative: it is what makes an error response predictable, and it is why, for example, a request from an unauthorized role against a non-existent resource returns a permission error rather than a not-found error.

1. **Authentication.** `X-User-Role` present and naming one of the five declared roles, and `X-User-ID` present and non-empty — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Authorization.** The acting role appears in the endpoint's permitted roles, and where a shared endpoint reserves an action for one role the acting role owns that action — otherwise `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Path resource existence**, validated left to right along the path template: a provider identifier that resolves to nothing yields `ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_FOUND`, a course `ERR_COURSE_NOT_FOUND`, a course application `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_FOUND`, an attorney `ERR_ATTORNEY_NOT_FOUND`, a compliance cycle `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND`, a credit record `ERR_CREDIT_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`, a reciprocity claim `ERR_RECIPROCITY_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, an audit `ERR_AUDIT_NOT_FOUND`, a complaint `ERR_COMPLAINT_NOT_FOUND`, a fee assessment `ERR_FEE_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FOUND` — all HTTP 404. Applies to every endpoint whose path carries an identifier segment.
4. **Body foreign-key existence**, validated in the order the fields appear in the endpoint's input: `provider_id`, `course_id`, `attorney_id`, `cycle_id`, `attendance_report_id`, `reciprocity_claim_id`, `affirmation_id`, `previous_cycle_id`, `originating_complaint_id`. Each unresolved reference yields its own not-found code from the list above (HTTP 404). This step applies to course creation, credit-record creation, roster filing, reciprocity-claim filing, audit opening, complaint filing, compliance-cycle creation and reciprocity determination.
5. **Resource ownership** for the entity-backed roles, as described in §2.1 — otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). This step applies to course creation, application filing, roster filing, appellant actions, credit-record creation, affirmation filing, certification, reciprocity-claim filing, fee settlement, reinstatement and the cycle summary read.
6. **Request shape.** Every required input present and well formed: enums take a declared value, minute counts are non-negative integers, `origin_credit_hours` is a positive number with at most one decimal place, `period_start` is strictly earlier than `period_end`, `session_date` is a date, `action` names an action the endpoint declares, and `credit_allocations` is a non-empty array whose `category` values are all distinct — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422). Applies to every write endpoint.
7. **Optimistic concurrency.** Where the endpoint declares a version input, its value equals the current `version` of the parent row — otherwise `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412), on the three endpoints listed in §2.6.
8. **State preconditions.** The addressed resource is in a state from which the requested trigger is declared, and every cross-entity state precondition of that transition holds — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (409). Applies to every action endpoint: accreditation actions, review actions, appellant actions, appeal decisions, audit actions, complaint disposition, reciprocity determination, credit adjudication, certification, fee settlement and reinstatement.
9. **Administrative suspension of a cycle.** A cycle whose `administrative_status` is `ADMIN_SUSPENDED` accepts no credit-bearing write at all — no credit-record creation, no affirmation, no certification — and such attempts are rejected with `ERR_CYCLE_ADMIN_SUSPENDED` (409).
10. **Appeal window.** An appeal action arrives no later than `appeal_window_ends_at`, which is the application's `denied_at` instant plus 30 days measured on the logical clock — otherwise `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_EXPIRED` (409), on the appellant-actions endpoint.
11. **The cleared-audit gate.** A provider may leave `PROBATIONARY` for `ACCREDITED` only when at least one of that provider's audits has reached `CLEARED` — otherwise `ERR_NO_CLEARED_AUDIT` (409).
12. **Segregation of duties.** The identifier deciding an appeal differs from the application's `reviewer_user_id`, and the identifier substantiating or dismissing a complaint differs from that complaint's `complainant_user_id` — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). Applies to appeal decisions and complaint dispositions.
13. **Regulatory standing** of the entities a credit-bearing write depends on: the course is `APPROVED` (`ERR_COURSE_NOT_APPROVED`), the sponsoring provider is `ACCREDITED` or `PROBATIONARY` (`ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_IN_GOOD_STANDING`), and the attorney's `status` is `ACTIVE` or `EXEMPT` rather than `ADMIN_SUSPENDED` or `INACTIVE` (`ERR_ATTORNEY_NOT_IN_GOOD_STANDING`) — all HTTP 409. Applies to credit-record creation, roster filing, application filing and reciprocity-claim filing.
14. **Referential coherence between two rows that both exist**: the cycle named by a request belongs to the same attorney as the request's `attorney_id`, and an affirmation's target credit record belongs to the addressed cycle (`ERR_CYCLE_ATTORNEY_MISMATCH`); a roster's course belongs to the acting provider (`ERR_COURSE_PROVIDER_MISMATCH`); an escalating audit names the same provider its originating complaint resolves to (`ERR_COURSE_PROVIDER_MISMATCH`) — HTTP 409. Applies to credit-record creation, audit opening, reciprocity determination and affirmation filing.
15. **Temporal coherence.** The credit's `completion_date` falls inside some cycle's half-open period (`ERR_OUTSIDE_COMPLIANCE_PERIOD`); the credit's `completion_date` does not exceed the course's `expires_at` (`ERR_COURSE_APPROVAL_EXPIRED`); a new cycle's half-open window does not overlap another cycle of the same attorney (`ERR_CYCLE_PERIOD_OVERLAP`); a new audit's closed window neither overlaps nor abuts another audit window of the same provider (`ERR_AUDIT_PERIOD_OVERLAP`) and the provider holds no other non-terminal audit (`ERR_AUDIT_ENGAGEMENT_ACTIVE`); a new live-in-person course does not push its provider's concurrent session count above the capacity limit (`ERR_SESSION_WINDOW_CONFLICT`); a roster arrives inside its hard filing window (`ERR_ROSTER_WINDOW_CLOSED`) — HTTP 409. Applies to credit-record creation, course creation, roster filing, compliance-cycle creation and audit opening.
16. **Uniqueness, cardinality and sum constraints**, all HTTP 409: `ERR_DUPLICATE_CREDIT_RECORD`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_APPLICATION`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_BAR_NUMBER`, `ERR_ALLOCATION_SUM_MISMATCH`, `ERR_ALLOCATION_NOT_MULTIPLE_OF_15`, `ERR_BELOW_MINIMUM_CREDIT`, `ERR_EMPTY_ROSTER`, `ERR_NO_SAMPLE_POPULATION`, `ERR_INFO_REQUEST_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_REMAND_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_FINDINGS_EXCEED_SAMPLE`, `ERR_COMPLAINT_ALREADY_ESCALATED`, `ERR_VERIFICATION_METHOD_NOT_PERMITTED`, `ERR_ATTENDANCE_CODE_INVALID`, `ERR_AFFIRMATION_REQUIRED`, `ERR_COMITY_NOT_AVAILABLE` and `ERR_JURISDICTION_NOT_RECIPROCAL`. Each condition is stated in §3 next to the workflow it guards. This step applies to course creation, attorney registration, credit-record creation, roster filing, application filing, review actions, audit actions, certification and reciprocity determination.
17. **Fiscal gates, evaluated last of all.** Reinstatement requires a zero outstanding balance (`ERR_OUTSTANDING_FEE_BALANCE`, HTTP 402), and a settlement must target an assessment still standing (`ERR_FEE_ALREADY_SETTLED`, HTTP 409). Certification is deliberately **not** fee-gated: a late filing is still a filing, and it must be able to cure the very delinquency that produced the outstanding assessment.

### 2.9. Reference Resolution and Reverse Reachability

The entity graph here is flat rather than deeply nested: peers are linked by explicit reference fields, and only four collections are addressed as sub-resources of their owner — a course's credit allocations, a course's applications, a course's attendance reports, and a cycle's affirmations. Where a path does carry more than one identifier segment, segments are resolved strictly left to right, and the leftmost unresolved segment decides the error code. A nested collection also enforces containment: a sub-collection returns only rows belonging to the addressed parent, never a global list filtered client-side.

Because references point "downward" in the payload, each referenced-from side is discoverable through a filtered list. The course list accepts `provider_id`, `status`, `delivery_format` and `approval_pathway` filters and is the reverse-reachability channel for a course's sponsoring provider. The compliance-cycle list accepts `attorney_id`, `previous_cycle_id`, `comity_claim_id`, `credit_status` and `administrative_status`, which is how a cycle's owning attorney, its predecessor and its closing comity claim are found. The credit-record list accepts `attorney_id`, `cycle_id`, `course_id`, `attendance_report_id`, `reciprocity_claim_id`, `affirmation_id` and `status`, covering all six of a record's references. The audit list accepts `provider_id`, `originating_complaint_id`, `status` and `trigger_type`. The fee-assessment list accepts `attorney_id`, `cycle_id`, `provider_id`, `assessed_by_sweep_id`, `fee_type` and `status`, and doubles as the audit trail behind the conserved balance described in §2.11. Every one of these lists also accepts `limit` and `offset`. Filters combine conjunctively: supplying two filters returns only rows matching both.

One reference is deliberately polymorphic. A complaint names its target with a `subject_type` of either `PROVIDER` or `COURSE` and a `subject_id` that must resolve in the table `subject_type` selects — a provider identifier when the type is `PROVIDER`, a course identifier when it is `COURSE`. Because the reference can point at either table it is not declared as a single-parent link; existence is validated against whichever table the type resolves to, and a failure to resolve is reported as `ERR_COMPLAINT_NOT_FOUND`.

### 2.10. Cascade Discipline and the Cycle's Two Status Dimensions

Consistency in this system is immediate, not eventual: when one operation moves several rows, all of those moves land inside the same request, and the response reflects the finished state. Every row a cascade touches has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented, including rows whose only change was a counter. Where the specification below says a cascade "re-runs" a computation, it means the computation is re-executed and its outputs re-persisted, overwriting whatever the previous run stored — never merged with it.

A compliance cycle is unusual in carrying **two independent status dimensions**. `credit_status` tracks the credit picture and takes `OPEN`, `SATISFIED`, `DEFICIENT` or `CLOSED_COMITY`, starting at `OPEN`. `administrative_status` tracks the filing picture and takes `GOOD_STANDING`, `DELINQUENT`, `ADMIN_SUSPENDED` or `REINSTATED`, starting at `GOOD_STANDING`. The two advance independently, and every combination is legitimate: a cycle can be `SATISFIED` and yet `DELINQUENT` (the credits were earned but the filing was late), or `OPEN` and `GOOD_STANDING`, or `DEFICIENT` and `ADMIN_SUSPENDED`. No transition writes both dimensions unless it is explicitly stated to do so, and a cycle has no terminal state in either dimension — a satisfied cycle can be dragged back open years later by a voided credit.

### 2.11. The Conserved Fee Ledger

Every charge the board raises is a `FeeAssessment` row, and there are exactly four kinds, recorded in `fee_type`: `LATE_COMPLIANCE` for a missed filing deadline, `REINSTATEMENT` for an administrative suspension, `AUDIT_SANCTION` for a sanctioned audit, and `PROVIDER_ANNUAL` for an accreditation grant or renewal. An assessment carries `amount_cents`, an `assessed_at` instant, a `status` of `ASSESSED`, `PAID`, `WAIVED` or `VOIDED` starting at `ASSESSED`, a `settled_at` instant recorded when it leaves `ASSESSED`, and a `waiver_reason` recorded only when it is waived. Attorney-side assessments carry `attorney_id` and, where a specific compliance period produced them, `cycle_id`; provider-side assessments carry `provider_id` instead and leave the attorney fields null. An assessment created by a batch run also records `assessed_by_sweep_id`, the identifier of the run that created it; assessments created by any other action leave that field null.

The invariant that binds the ledger is stated on the attorney: `outstanding_fee_cents` always equals the sum of `amount_cents` over that attorney's assessments whose `status` is still `ASSESSED`. Every path out of `ASSESSED` therefore decrements the balance by exactly that assessment's `amount_cents` — payment, waiver and the automatic void described in §3.9 alike — and every new attorney-side assessment increments it by exactly `amount_cents`. Provider-side assessments never touch any attorney's balance. A settlement that addresses an assessment already `PAID`, `WAIVED` or `VOIDED` is rejected with `ERR_FEE_ALREADY_SETTLED`.

### 2.12. Denormalized Counters

Several counters are maintained incrementally by the cascades that cause them to change, rather than computed by scanning at read time. They are part of the contract and must be correct after every mutation.

On a provider: `courses_total_count` is the number of that provider's courses in any status; `courses_self_certified_count` is the number of them whose approval pathway is self-certification; `attendance_reports_total_count` is the number of that provider's accepted rosters, counting both on-time and late acceptances; `attendance_reports_late_count` is the number accepted late; `substantiated_complaint_count` is the number of substantiated complaints whose subject resolves either to the provider itself or to one of its courses; `audit_findings_count` is the lifetime sum of findings across that provider's audits that have reached findings-issued, cleared or sanctioned; `courses_sampled_lifetime` is the lifetime sum of sample sizes across that provider's audits that have reached sampled, findings-issued, cleared or sanctioned; and `last_audit_closed_at` is the closing instant of the most recently closed audit for that provider — the latest by closing instant, breaking ties by the later `created_at` — and null when the provider has never had an audit closed.

On a course: `credited_minutes_total` is the sum of its allocation minutes, which must equal `total_minutes`, and `verified_credit_record_count` is the number of credit records for that course currently in the verified state — incremented when a record becomes verified and decremented when a verified record is voided.

On an attorney: `open_deficiency_count` is the number of that attorney's cycles whose `credit_status` is `DEFICIENT`, and `pending_recertification_count` is the number of times a previously satisfied cycle of that attorney has been dragged back to `OPEN` by a downstream credit void.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Provider Accreditation Lifecycle

A sponsor enters the register when board staff file an accreditation application on its behalf, supplying the sponsor's legal `name` (between 1 and 200 characters, required) and its `provider_type`, which is one of `BAR_ASSOCIATION`, `LAW_SCHOOL`, `GOVERNMENT`, `COMMERCIAL` or `LAW_FIRM`. An `annual_fee_cents` figure may be supplied and must be zero or greater; when it is omitted the board's standard annual accreditation fee of 120000 cents — twelve hundred dollars — is used. A new sponsor begins in `APPLIED` with `self_certification_enabled` false, a `risk_score` of 0.0, a `risk_score_variant` of `REVIEWED_WEIGHTING`, `accreditation_term_start` and `accreditation_term_end` both null, `probation_reason` null, and every counter at zero.

Six board actions drive the sponsor forward and backward: `GRANT_FULL`, `GRANT_PROBATIONARY`, `DENY`, `RENEW`, `SUSPEND` and `REINSTATE`. Each one is submitted with the sponsor's currently observed version in `expected_provider_version` (see §2.6), and a free-text note may accompany it as `probation_reason`. Every one of them increments the sponsor's `version` by one and refreshes its `risk_score` and `risk_score_variant`.

**Presumptive full accreditation.** `GRANT_FULL` on an `APPLIED` sponsor moves it `APPLIED → ACCREDITED`, but only where the sponsor is presumptively trustworthy: its `provider_type` must be `BAR_ASSOCIATION`, `LAW_SCHOOL` or `GOVERNMENT`. A `COMMERCIAL` or `LAW_FIRM` sponsor cannot be granted full accreditation from `APPLIED` at all, and the attempt is an `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. The grant stamps `accreditation_term_start` with the current logical instant and `accreditation_term_end` with that instant plus 24 months, flips `self_certification_enabled` to true, raises a `PROVIDER_ANNUAL` assessment against the sponsor for exactly its `annual_fee_cents`, and re-scores it under the self-certified weighting, writing `risk_score_variant` as `SELF_CERTIFIED_WEIGHTING`.

**Probationary accreditation.** `GRANT_PROBATIONARY` on an `APPLIED` sponsor moves it `APPLIED → PROBATIONARY` regardless of `provider_type`. It stamps the same two-year term into `accreditation_term_start` and `accreditation_term_end`, leaves `self_certification_enabled` false, raises the same `PROVIDER_ANNUAL` assessment for `annual_fee_cents`, and re-scores the sponsor under `REVIEWED_WEIGHTING`. A probationary sponsor is in good standing for the purpose of credit-bearing writes but cannot self-certify: every course it registers must be individually approved.

**Denial.** `DENY` on an `APPLIED` sponsor moves it `APPLIED → DENIED`, recording the supplied note in `probation_reason`. `DENIED` is the sponsor's only terminal state; no action leads out of it.

**Earning full accreditation from probation.** `GRANT_FULL` on a `PROBATIONARY` sponsor moves it `PROBATIONARY → ACCREDITED`, and it is gated on a cross-entity fact: at least one audit of that same sponsor must have reached `CLEARED`. Without such an audit the request is `ERR_NO_CLEARED_AUDIT`. This promotion flips `self_certification_enabled` to true and re-scores the sponsor under `SELF_CERTIFIED_WEIGHTING`; it does not start a new term and does not raise a new annual fee, because the term granted at probation is still running.

**Renewal.** `RENEW` is legitimately repeatable and takes two distinct forms. On an `ACCREDITED` sponsor it is a self-transition `ACCREDITED → ACCREDITED`, permitted only when the sponsor has no `PROVIDER_ANNUAL` assessment still in `ASSESSED` — the prior term's fee must be settled first, and an unsettled one makes the renewal an `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. It re-stamps `accreditation_term_start` to the current instant and `accreditation_term_end` to that instant plus 24 months, so the new term wholly supersedes the prior one, and it raises a fresh `PROVIDER_ANNUAL` assessment for `annual_fee_cents`. On a `LAPSED` sponsor, `RENEW` moves it `LAPSED → PROBATIONARY` — a lapsed sponsor re-enters on probation and never straight into full accreditation, which is what makes the sponsor lifecycle cyclic: `ACCREDITED → LAPSED → PROBATIONARY → ACCREDITED`. This form is not gated on the prior fee. It stamps the same fresh 24-month term, raises the same fresh `PROVIDER_ANNUAL` assessment, and additionally runs a compensation cascade: every course of that sponsor sitting in `SUSPENDED_PENDING_REVIEW` whose `expires_at` is still in the future returns to `APPROVED`.

**Suspension.** `SUSPEND` may be applied to a sponsor in either `ACCREDITED` or `PROBATIONARY`, moving it to `SUSPENDED`, and the same two transitions also fire automatically when an audit closes with a suspension sanction. Both branches set `self_certification_enabled` to false and both run the sponsor-suspension cascade: every course of that sponsor that is currently `APPROVED`, was approved through the self-certification pathway, and has not yet reached its `expires_at` moves `APPROVED → SUSPENDED_PENDING_REVIEW`. The only difference between the branches is scoring: leaving `ACCREDITED` also switches `risk_score_variant` to `REVIEWED_WEIGHTING` and re-scores under it, whereas a sponsor leaving `PROBATIONARY` was already being scored that way.

**Reinstatement.** `REINSTATE` moves a `SUSPENDED` sponsor to `PROBATIONARY` — never directly back to `ACCREDITED` — and requires that the sponsor have no `AUDIT_SANCTION` assessment still in `ASSESSED`; an unpaid sanction fee makes it an `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Reinstatement runs the compensation cascade: every course of that sponsor in `SUSPENDED_PENDING_REVIEW` whose `expires_at` is still in the future returns to `APPROVED`. Note carefully that no credit records are compensated by either the reinstatement or the renewal cascade — records rejected while the sponsor was out of standing stay rejected and must be re-filed.

**Probation by enforcement.** Two events push an `ACCREDITED` sponsor to `PROBATIONARY` without any board action on the sponsor itself: an audit of that sponsor closing with a probation sanction, and a complaint against that sponsor being substantiated. Either way `self_certification_enabled` drops to false, `risk_score_variant` becomes `REVIEWED_WEIGHTING` with the score recomputed under it, and `probation_reason` records the audit or complaint that caused the reversion. Probation alone does **not** suspend the sponsor's courses — only `SUSPENDED` and `LAPSED` cascade into courses. And a substantiated complaint whose subject is a *course* raises the sponsoring provider's `substantiated_complaint_count` and re-scores it, but never moves its `accreditation_status`.

**Lapse.** A sponsor's term does not expire by itself; the board's batch run notices it. When a run observes an `ACCREDITED` sponsor whose `accreditation_term_end` is at or before the run's evaluation instant, the sponsor moves `ACCREDITED → LAPSED`, `self_certification_enabled` drops to false, the sponsor-suspension course cascade runs exactly as it does for `SUSPEND` (self-certified `APPROVED` courses whose `expires_at` is still in the future move to `SUSPENDED_PENDING_REVIEW`), and the run's `providers_lapsed` tally rises by one. When the same run observes a `PROBATIONARY` sponsor past its `accreditation_term_end`, the sponsor moves `PROBATIONARY → LAPSED` and the run's `providers_lapsed` tally likewise rises by one; there is no course cascade on this branch, because a probationary sponsor never had self-certified courses to suspend.

Throughout all of this, `self_certification_enabled` is not an independent switch: it is true exactly when `accreditation_status` is `ACCREDITED`, and false in every other state. `risk_score_variant` follows the same fact from the other side, reading `SELF_CERTIFIED_WEIGHTING` exactly when the sponsor is `ACCREDITED` and `REVIEWED_WEIGHTING` in every other state including `APPLIED` and `DENIED`.

### 3.2. Registering a Course: Formats, Category Allocations and Session Capacity

Course registration is open to three roles, with different meanings. A `ProviderAdmin` registers a course under its own sponsorship and must supply a `provider_id` equal to its own `X-User-ID`; supplying anyone else's is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. An `Attorney` registering a course must omit `provider_id` entirely, which is how an attorney seeks individual approval of instruction that has no sponsoring provider in this state. A `BoardAdmin` may register a course either way. Registration carries the course `title` (1 to 200 characters), its `delivery_format`, its `total_minutes`, the `presented_at` instant, the `attendance_verification_method` the sponsor commits to, and a `credit_allocations` array.

`delivery_format` is one of `LIVE_IN_PERSON`, `LIVE_WEBCAST`, `ON_DEMAND` or `SELF_STUDY`, describing the course as offered. `presented_at` is the live session start instant for the two live formats, and the first-availability instant for the two non-live ones. `total_minutes` counts the instructional minutes of the whole course; it must be at least 30 and must be a whole multiple of 15 minutes.

**Category allocation.** A course is not a single undifferentiated block of time — it is a set of slices, each carrying one credit category. Every element of `credit_allocations` names a `category` — `GENERAL`, `ETHICS`, `TECHNOLOGY` or `BIAS_ELIMINATION` — and the `minutes` allocated to it, which must be greater than zero and a whole multiple of 15 minutes. The array must be non-empty and no category may appear twice; an empty array or a repeated category is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Each element becomes an immutable allocation row bound to the new course by its `course_id`, and those rows are never editable afterwards. The allocated minutes must sum exactly to `total_minutes`; a mismatch in either direction is `ERR_ALLOCATION_SUM_MISMATCH`, and an element whose `minutes` is not a positive multiple of 15 is `ERR_ALLOCATION_NOT_MULTIPLE_OF_15`. The accepted sum is stored on the course as `credited_minutes_total`, which is therefore always equal to `total_minutes`.

**Which pathway the course takes.** `approval_pathway` is derived at creation and never revisited. It is `PROVIDER_ACCREDITED` when `provider_id` is supplied *and* that sponsor's `self_certification_enabled` is true at the moment of creation; in every other case — no sponsor at all, or a sponsor that is not fully accredited — it is `INDIVIDUAL_APPROVAL`. A course on the self-certification pathway is presumptively approved: it is created and immediately transitions `PENDING_APPROVAL → APPROVED` in the same request, its sponsor's `courses_self_certified_count` and `courses_total_count` each rise by one, and the sponsor's `risk_score` is recomputed because the denominator behind its findings rate has changed. No approval application is created for such a course, and none is permitted. Every other course is created in `PENDING_APPROVAL` and stays there until an application is decided; if it names a sponsor, only that sponsor's `courses_total_count` rises. Where a sponsor is named, its `accreditation_status` must be `ACCREDITED` or `PROBATIONARY` — a sponsor that is `APPLIED`, `SUSPENDED`, `LAPSED` or `DENIED` cannot have courses registered against it, and the attempt is `ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_IN_GOOD_STANDING`.

**How long approval lasts.** `expires_at` is computed at creation from the delivery format. For `LIVE_IN_PERSON` and `LIVE_WEBCAST` it is `presented_at` plus the course's own `total_minutes` — a live session's approval covers the session and nothing more. For `ON_DEMAND` and `SELF_STUDY` it is `presented_at` plus 36 months, three years of shelf life for recorded material. A batch run moves any `APPROVED` course whose `expires_at` has been reached or passed to `EXPIRED` and counts it in that run's `courses_expired` tally. `EXPIRED` and `REVOKED` are the course's two terminal states.

**Verification commitment.** `attendance_verification_method` is one of `ROSTER`, `ATTENDANCE_CODE`, `CERTIFICATE` or `AFFIRMATION_ONLY`, and it constrains how attendees may later prove they were there. Where and only where the sponsor commits to `ATTENDANCE_CODE`, the system mints a six-character `attendance_code` at creation; for the other three methods `attendance_code` is null.

**Concurrent live capacity.** A provider cannot be in two places at once, and the board treats a live course's window from `presented_at` through `expires_at` as occupying that provider as a resource. Registering a new `LIVE_IN_PERSON` course must not leave the sponsor with more than two courses whose session windows all overlap some common instant; a third overlapping live session is rejected with `ERR_SESSION_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.

**The rest of the course lifecycle.** A course's `status` is one of `PENDING_APPROVAL`, `APPROVED`, `DENIED`, `SUSPENDED_PENDING_REVIEW`, `REVOKED` or `EXPIRED`, starting at `PENDING_APPROVAL`. Sponsor suspension or lapse moves a self-certified `APPROVED` course whose `expires_at` is still in the future to `SUSPENDED_PENDING_REVIEW`, and that move itself rejects every credit record for that course still awaiting verification, stamping each with a `void_reason` of `SPONSOR_NOT_IN_GOOD_STANDING` and re-running the owning cycle's credit application. Sponsor reinstatement or renewal returns such a course to `APPROVED`, again only while `expires_at` is still in the future, and — this asymmetry is deliberate — performs no credit-record compensation whatsoever. A substantiated complaint against the course itself drives it to `REVOKED` from `APPROVED`, `SUSPENDED_PENDING_REVIEW` or `EXPIRED`, copying the complaint's disposition text into the course's `denial_reason`; a denial through the application pathway likewise copies the deciding application's denial text into `denial_reason`, and that field is null on a course that has never been denied or revoked.

### 3.3. Individual Course Approval: Information Rounds, Denial and Appeal

A course on the individual-approval pathway needs an application. Either a `ProviderAdmin` (for a course sponsored by its own provider — anyone else's is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`) or an `Attorney` may file one, and filing carries no payload at all: the target course is the one addressed, and the filer's identity is captured from the headers. The course must still be in `PENDING_APPROVAL`; filing against a course in any other status is `ERR_COURSE_NOT_APPROVED`. Only one live application may exist per course, so if that course already has an application sitting in `SUBMITTED`, `INFO_REQUESTED` or `APPEALED`, the filing is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_APPLICATION` — note that an application already in a terminal disposition does not block a re-filing.

The new application starts in `SUBMITTED` with `info_request_round`, `supplement_count` and `remand_count` all at zero and `reviewer_user_id`, `denial_reason`, `appeal_grounds`, `appeal_panelist_user_id`, `appeal_filed_at`, `denied_at` and `decided_at` all null. Two fields freeze the filer: `applicant_role` records which role filed and takes the value `PROVIDER_ADMIN` or `ATTORNEY`, and `applicant_user_id` records the filer's `X-User-ID`. That identifier is thereafter the *only* identity permitted to supplement or appeal this application, whatever role it holds.

**The reviewer's three moves.** An `AccreditationReviewer` acts on an application by naming an `action` of `REQUEST_INFO`, `APPROVE` or `DENY`, optionally with a `denial_reason`. Whichever move is made, the acting `X-User-ID` is stamped into `reviewer_user_id`, which is precisely what disqualifies that operator from later deciding the appeal.

Requesting information moves the application `SUBMITTED → INFO_REQUESTED` and raises `info_request_round` by one. The budget is two rounds: a request that would push `info_request_round` above 2 is rejected with `ERR_INFO_REQUEST_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. Nothing happens automatically when an information request goes unanswered — there is no timer, and an application can sit in `INFO_REQUESTED` indefinitely without being auto-denied.

Approval moves the application to `APPROVED` from either `SUBMITTED` or `INFO_REQUESTED`, stamps `decided_at` with the current logical instant, and cascades the course `PENDING_APPROVAL → APPROVED`. It requires the course to still be in `PENDING_APPROVAL`. A course with no sponsoring provider keeps its `approval_pathway` of `INDIVIDUAL_APPROVAL` after approval — approval by the board does not convert it into a self-certified course.

Denial moves the application to `DENIED` from either `SUBMITTED` or `INFO_REQUESTED`. A `denial_reason` must be supplied; without one the request is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The reason is recorded on the application, `denied_at` is stamped with the current logical instant, and the course cascades `PENDING_APPROVAL → DENIED` carrying the identical reason text into its own `denial_reason`. Stamping `denied_at` is what opens the appeal window.

**The applicant's two moves.** The application's own `applicant_user_id` — acting as either a `ProviderAdmin` or an `Attorney` — may name an `action` of `SUPPLEMENT` or `APPEAL`, optionally with `appeal_grounds`. Supplementing answers an information request and moves the application `INFO_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED`, raising `supplement_count` by one; together with the reviewer's information request this forms the review system's first loop, `SUBMITTED → INFO_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED`, bounded by the two-round budget. Appealing contests a denial and moves the application `DENIED → APPEALED`. It requires `appeal_grounds` text, and it must arrive strictly before 30 days have elapsed since `denied_at` as measured on the logical clock — at or after that instant the appeal is `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. An accepted appeal records the grounds and stamps `appeal_filed_at`.

**The panel's three dispositions.** An `AppealsPanelist` decides an `APPEALED` application by naming a `disposition` of `GRANT`, `DENY` or `REMAND`, optionally with a `decision_reason`. Every disposition requires the application to be in `APPEALED` and requires the acting `X-User-ID` to differ from `reviewer_user_id`; a panelist who previously reviewed the application is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. All three dispositions record the acting identifier in `appeal_panelist_user_id`.

`GRANT` moves the application `APPEALED → APPEAL_GRANTED`, stamps `decided_at`, clears the application's `denial_reason` to null, and cascades the course `DENIED → APPROVED` while clearing the course's own `denial_reason` to null. `DENY` moves the application `APPEALED → APPEAL_DENIED`, stamps `decided_at`, records the supplied reason in `denial_reason`, and leaves the course sitting in `DENIED`; this closes the matter for that application, and a fresh application becomes possible only once the course itself is re-created. `REMAND` moves the application `APPEALED → SUBMITTED` and sends the whole matter back for re-review: it raises `remand_count` by one, clears `reviewer_user_id`, `denial_reason` and `denied_at` back to null, resets `info_request_round` to 0 so the application re-enters review with a fresh two-round information budget, and cascades the course `DENIED → PENDING_APPROVAL` with the course's `denial_reason` cleared. This is the second loop, `DENIED → APPEALED → SUBMITTED`, and the board allows it at most once: a remand that would push `remand_count` above 1 is rejected with `ERR_REMAND_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`.

An application's `status` is therefore one of `SUBMITTED`, `INFO_REQUESTED`, `APPROVED`, `DENIED`, `APPEALED`, `APPEAL_GRANTED` or `APPEAL_DENIED`, and `APPROVED`, `APPEAL_GRANTED` and `APPEAL_DENIED` are terminal. Applying any action from a status the action is not declared for — supplementing a `SUBMITTED` application, appealing an `APPROVED` one, deciding an appeal on one that is not `APPEALED` — is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.

### 3.4. The Attorney Register and the Derivation of a Compliance Period

Board staff register an attorney with a `bar_number` (1 to 20 characters, required, and unique across the whole register — a collision is `ERR_DUPLICATE_BAR_NUMBER`), a `full_name` (1 to 200 characters), an `admission_date`, a `compliance_group`, and a `principal_office_jurisdiction` given as exactly two uppercase letters. The two-letter code `ST` denotes this state itself, and an attorney whose principal office is `ST` is in-state for the purposes of §3.7. `compliance_group` is `GROUP_1`, `GROUP_2` or `GROUP_3`, and it selects nothing except the filing-deadline offset described below. A new attorney is `ACTIVE` with `outstanding_fee_cents`, `open_deficiency_count` and `pending_recertification_count` all zero.

An attorney's `status` is one of `ACTIVE`, `EXEMPT`, `ADMIN_SUSPENDED` or `INACTIVE`. Board staff may move an attorney between `ACTIVE`, `EXEMPT` and `INACTIVE` by supplying the target `status` and an optional `reason`; this is the only actor-driven writer of that field. `ADMIN_SUSPENDED` is deliberately unreachable this way — it is written only by the board's batch run and cleared only by reinstatement — so a standing change that names `ADMIN_SUSPENDED` as its target, or that addresses an attorney already sitting in `ADMIN_SUSPENDED`, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. A standing change leaves every one of that attorney's compliance cycles exactly as it found them: neither `credit_status` nor `administrative_status` moves, and the attorney's own `version` rises by one.

**Opening a compliance period.** A `BoardAdmin` opens a period for an attorney by supplying `period_start` and `period_end`, optionally the identifier of the immediately preceding period as `previous_cycle_id`, and optionally a `carryover_election`. The period is the half-open interval running from `period_start` inclusive to `period_end` exclusive, and `period_start` must be strictly earlier than `period_end`. Two periods of the same attorney may not intersect: an overlap is `ERR_CYCLE_PERIOD_OVERLAP`, while exact adjacency — a new period whose `period_start` equals an existing period's `period_end` — is explicitly allowed, because the half-open convention means the two share no instant. `carryover_election` is either `CARRY_FORWARD` or `WAIVE_CARRYOVER` and defaults to `CARRY_FORWARD`.

Where `previous_cycle_id` is supplied it must name an existing cycle, and the new cycle's `carryover_in_general_minutes` and `carryover_in_ethics_minutes` are set to the predecessor's `carryover_out_general_minutes` and `carryover_out_ethics_minutes` respectively. Where it is omitted, both carried-in figures are zero. This is a conserved transfer rather than a snapshot: whenever a predecessor's carried-out figures change, its successor's carried-in figures are rewritten to match in the same request, so a successor's carried-in general minutes always equal its predecessor's carried-out general minutes, and likewise for ethics.

**Deriving the requirement.** The four requirement figures, the proration flag and the filing deadline are all computed once, at the instant the cycle is created, from the single attorney record named on it. No credit record participates, and none of these figures is ever re-derived afterwards — not when the attorney's standing changes, not at certification, not by a batch run.

If the attorney's `status` is `EXEMPT` at that instant, the derivation short-circuits: `required_total_minutes`, `required_ethics_minutes`, `required_technology_minutes` and `required_bias_minutes` are all zero and `prorated` is false. An exempt attorney has nothing to earn.

Otherwise, `prorated` is true exactly when the attorney's `admission_date` falls inside the cycle's half-open window — that is, when it is at or after `period_start` and strictly before `period_end`. A newly admitted attorney does not owe a full period's education for a period they spent only part of.

When `prorated` is false, the number of months the attorney is on the hook for is the full 24-month cycle. When `prorated` is true, the count is measured from an anchor rather than from the admission date itself: the anchor is UTC midnight on the first day of the calendar month *after* the month containing `admission_date`, and the month count is the number of whole calendar months from that anchor to `period_end`, clamped so that it is never below 0 and never above 24.

The total requirement is then derived from the board's standard 1800 minutes — 30 credit hours over a two-year cycle. Multiply 1800 by the month count, divide by 24, and divide by 60 to obtain an exact rational number of credit hours; round that number **up** to the next whole credit hour, and multiply by 60 to express it back in minutes. That figure is `required_total_minutes`. A full 24-month cycle therefore requires exactly 1800 minutes, and a shortened one requires a whole number of hours' worth of minutes and never a fraction of an hour.

The three specialty minimums are not prorated proportionally; they either apply in full or drop to a reduced floor. When the cycle is not prorated at all, or when the month count is 12 or more, they apply in full: `required_ethics_minutes` is 180 minutes (3 hours), `required_technology_minutes` is 60 minutes (1 hour) and `required_bias_minutes` is 120 minutes (2 hours). When the cycle is prorated to fewer than 12 months, the reduced floors apply: `required_ethics_minutes` is 90 minutes, `required_technology_minutes` remains 60 minutes, and `required_bias_minutes` is 60 minutes.

Finally, `filing_deadline` is `period_end` plus an offset drawn from the attorney's `compliance_group`: 31 days for `GROUP_1`, 61 days for `GROUP_2` and 91 days for `GROUP_3`. That deadline, not `period_end`, is what a delinquency is measured against.

### 3.5. Claiming and Verifying Attendance Credit

An attorney claims credit for one attended course at a time. The claim names `attorney_id` (which must equal the acting `X-User-ID`), the `course_id` attended, the `verification_method` being relied on, the `attended_minutes` actually sat through (greater than zero), and the `completion_date` — the instant attendance finished. Two optional fields accompany particular methods: `certificate_reference`, the sponsor's certificate identifier, is required when the method is `CERTIFICATE`, and `attendance_code` carries the code the attendee was given at the session.

**Which period the credit lands in.** The owning period is never supplied by the client. The server resolves `cycle_id` to the one cycle of that attorney whose half-open window from `period_start` inclusive to `period_end` exclusive contains `completion_date`. If no cycle of that attorney covers the completion instant, the claim is rejected with `ERR_OUTSIDE_COMPLIANCE_PERIOD`; if the resolved cycle turns out to belong to a different attorney than the claim's `attorney_id`, that is `ERR_CYCLE_ATTORNEY_MISMATCH`. The claim also requires the course to be `APPROVED` (otherwise `ERR_COURSE_NOT_APPROVED`), the `completion_date` to be no later than the course's `expires_at` (otherwise `ERR_COURSE_APPROVAL_EXPIRED`), and the attorney's `status` to be `ACTIVE` or `EXEMPT` (otherwise `ERR_ATTORNEY_NOT_IN_GOOD_STANDING`). If the resolved cycle's `administrative_status` is `ADMIN_SUSPENDED`, no credit may be claimed into it at all and the rejection is `ERR_CYCLE_ADMIN_SUSPENDED`. Finally, an attorney may hold at most one live claim per course: a second claim for the same attorney and course while an earlier one is still `PENDING_VERIFICATION` or `VERIFIED` is `ERR_DUPLICATE_CREDIT_RECORD`.

**Verification methods must match the sponsor's commitment.** A record's `verification_method` is one of `ROSTER`, `ATTENDANCE_CODE`, `CERTIFICATE` or `AFFIRMATION`, and it must be the declared partner of the course's `attendance_verification_method` under a fixed pairing: a course committed to `ROSTER` accepts only `ROSTER`, one committed to `ATTENDANCE_CODE` accepts only `ATTENDANCE_CODE`, one committed to `CERTIFICATE` accepts only `CERTIFICATE`, and one committed to `AFFIRMATION_ONLY` accepts only `AFFIRMATION`. Any other combination is `ERR_VERIFICATION_METHOD_NOT_PERMITTED`. That same code also rejects an attorney who names `ROSTER` on a self-filed claim: roster records may only be minted by the sponsor's roster cascade, never claimed directly.

The method decides whether the record is born verified. With `ATTENDANCE_CODE`, the supplied `attendance_code` must equal the course's minted code — an absent or mismatched code is `ERR_ATTENDANCE_CODE_INVALID` — and a matching code transitions the new record `PENDING_VERIFICATION → VERIFIED` in the same request, raising the course's `verified_credit_record_count` by one and re-running the owning cycle's credit application. With `CERTIFICATE` or `AFFIRMATION`, the record is created in `PENDING_VERIFICATION` and waits: a certificate claim waits for board adjudication, an affirmation claim waits for the attorney's own sworn affirmation. A record's `status` is therefore one of `PENDING_VERIFICATION`, `VERIFIED`, `REJECTED` or `VOIDED`, of which the last two are terminal, and `void_reason` carries the text recorded whenever a record is rejected or voided.

**Prorating the claim across categories.** Credited minutes are derived from the course's allocations, prorated by how much of the course the attorney actually attended, and rounded per category. First, the attended figure is clamped: it is the smaller of the claim's `attended_minutes` and the course's `total_minutes`, so a claim for more time than the course contains earns no bonus. Then, working through the four categories in the pinned order `ETHICS`, `TECHNOLOGY`, `BIAS_ELIMINATION`, `GENERAL`, each category's raw entitlement is that category's allocated minutes multiplied by the clamped attended figure and divided by the course's `total_minutes` — kept as an exact rational value, never rounded at this step, and taken as zero when the course has no allocation in that category. Each raw entitlement is then rounded **down** to the nearest whole multiple of 15 minutes, and the four results are written to `credited_ethics_minutes`, `credited_technology_minutes`, `credited_bias_minutes` and `credited_general_minutes`. Only after all four have been individually rounded down are they added together to give `credited_minutes`. This order is pinned: rounding each category first and summing afterwards is the rule, and summing the exact prorated values and rounding once at the end is not — the two produce different totals, and only the first is correct here. Once the sum is known, a floor applies: a claim whose `credited_minutes` comes to less than 30 minutes is not worth recording, and the request is rejected with `ERR_BELOW_MINIMUM_CREDIT`.

**What format the credit really is.** `effective_format` classifies the credit for cap purposes and is derived rather than supplied, and it does not always agree with the course's advertised `delivery_format`. A `LIVE_IN_PERSON` course always yields `LIVE`. A `LIVE_WEBCAST` course yields `LIVE` only when attendance was verified by `ATTENDANCE_CODE` or by `ROSTER` — the two methods that prove someone was actually watching in real time — and otherwise yields `ON_DEMAND`. An `ON_DEMAND` course always yields `ON_DEMAND`, and a `SELF_STUDY` course always yields `SELF_STUDY`. Alongside it, `source` records where the credit came from: `RECIPROCITY` when the record was created by an approved out-of-state claim on the reciprocity basis, `COMITY` when created by one on the comity basis, and `IN_STATE` in every other case.

**Sponsor-filed session rosters.** A `ProviderAdmin` may file a roster for one session of one of its own courses, supplying `course_id`, the `session_date` the session began, and an `entries` array whose elements each carry a `bar_number` and the `attended_minutes` that attendee sat through. The roster's course must belong to the acting provider; someone else's course is `ERR_COURSE_PROVIDER_MISMATCH`. The array must contain at least one element — an empty `entries` array is `ERR_EMPTY_ROSTER`. On acceptance the roster stores `entry_count` (the number of rows supplied), `submitted_at` (the logical instant of acceptance) and `session_end`, which is `session_date` plus the course's `total_minutes` and marks the closed interval the session occupies on the course as a resource.

Lateness is measured against the logical clock in two stages. The roster's `due_at` is `session_date` plus 30 days, and its hard close is `session_date` plus 90 days. A roster arriving after the hard close is refused outright with `ERR_ROSTER_WINDOW_CLOSED` and no roster row is written at all. A roster arriving at or before `due_at` has `days_late` of 0 and a `status` of `ACCEPTED`. A roster arriving after `due_at` but at or before the hard close has `days_late` set to the number of days from `due_at` to now, rounded **up** so that any part of a day counts as a whole day, and a `status` of `ACCEPTED_LATE`; a late acceptance also raises the sponsor's `attendance_reports_late_count` by one, which feeds directly into its risk score. `ACCEPTED` and `ACCEPTED_LATE` are the only two roster statuses that are ever persisted — a refused submission returns an error instead of a rejected row. Every accepted roster, late or not, raises the sponsor's `attendance_reports_total_count` by one, and the sponsor's `risk_score` is recomputed afterwards.

Each accepted entry cascades a credit record that is born `VERIFIED`, with `verification_method` set to `ROSTER` and `attendance_report_id` pointing at the roster, which raises the course's `verified_credit_record_count` and triggers a credit-application re-run for that record's owning cycle. Individual entries may fail without failing the request: an entry is skipped when its `bar_number` matches no attorney on the register, when that attorney has no compliance cycle whose half-open window covers the roster's `session_date`, or when that attorney already holds a `PENDING_VERIFICATION` or `VERIFIED` record for that course. `accepted_entry_count` counts the entries that produced a record and `rejected_entry_count` counts the rest, and the two always sum to `entry_count`.

**Affirmations.** An attorney files an affirmation against one of its own cycles, supplying an `affirmation_type`, a `statement` of between 1 and 500 characters recording the affirmation verbatim, and — conditionally — a `target_credit_record_id`. The affirmation is bound to the cycle it is filed against by `cycle_id`, and `affirmed_by_user_id` records the acting `X-User-ID`, which must equal that cycle's `attorney_id`. Affirmations are immutable once filed. Three types exist. `ATTENDANCE_AFFIRMATION` verifies exactly one credit record and therefore requires `target_credit_record_id`; the two other types forbid it, and supplying it anyway — or omitting it on an attendance affirmation — is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. `SELF_STUDY_AFFIRMATION` attests to unverified self-study and targets nothing. `FINAL_COMPLIANCE_AFFIRMATION` is the sworn statement that the period is complete, and it is the prerequisite for certification.

An attendance affirmation cascades: the targeted record must be in `PENDING_VERIFICATION` with a `verification_method` of `AFFIRMATION`, and it must belong to the same cycle the affirmation is filed against — a target in another cycle is `ERR_CYCLE_ATTORNEY_MISMATCH`, and a target that resolves to nothing is `ERR_CREDIT_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`. On acceptance the record moves `PENDING_VERIFICATION → VERIFIED`, its `affirmation_id` is set to the new affirmation (this reverse link on the record, not the forward link on the affirmation, is the queryable side of the association), the course's `verified_credit_record_count` rises by one, and the cycle's credit application is re-run. No affirmation of any type may be filed against a cycle whose `administrative_status` is `ADMIN_SUSPENDED`; the rejection is `ERR_CYCLE_ADMIN_SUSPENDED`.

**Board adjudication.** A `BoardAdmin` adjudicates a single credit record by naming an `action` of `VERIFY`, `REJECT` or `VOID`, optionally with a `void_reason`. `VERIFY` moves a `PENDING_VERIFICATION` record to `VERIFIED`, and it is permitted while the course is `APPROVED` **or** `EXPIRED` — an expired course may still be adjudicated for attendance that was completed before expiry — raising the course's `verified_credit_record_count` by one and re-running the owning cycle's credit application. `REJECT` moves a `PENDING_VERIFICATION` record to `REJECTED`, records the reason in `void_reason`, and re-runs the cycle's credit application; because a rejected record leaves the provisional input set, the cycle's earned figures can *fall* as a result. `VOID` applies only to a record already `VERIFIED`, moving it to `VOIDED`, recording the reason, zeroing that record's `countable_minutes`, and lowering the course's `verified_credit_record_count` by one before the cycle's credit application is re-run. Adjudicating a record already in `REJECTED` or `VOIDED` is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Voiding is the most consequential single action in the system, and §3.8 describes the chain it can set off.

### 3.6. Applying Credit to a Period: Caps, Chronology and the Two Variants

Claiming credit and *counting* credit are different things. A single algorithm — the credit application — decides how much of an attorney's claimed time actually counts toward a period, and it runs on every event that could change the answer: the creation, verification, rejection or voiding of a credit record; an approved out-of-state determination; certification; the cure evaluation inside a reinstatement; and every read of a cycle summary. Each run rewrites the cycle's `earned_countable_minutes`, `earned_ethics_minutes`, `earned_technology_minutes`, `earned_bias_minutes` and `earned_live_minutes`, and rewrites `countable_minutes` on every credit record of that cycle.

**Two input sets, one algorithm.** The algorithm runs in one of two variants that differ only in which records they look at. The **provisional** variant includes the cycle's records in `PENDING_VERIFICATION` and `VERIFIED`. The **final** variant includes only those in `VERIFIED`. Records in `REJECTED` or `VOIDED` participate in neither variant, ever. While a cycle's `credit_status` is `OPEN`, the provisional result is what gets persisted into the cycle's `earned_*` fields and into each record's `countable_minutes`. The final variant is what runs at certification, inside a reinstatement's cure evaluation, inside the batch run's late-fee derivation, and on every re-run triggered against a cycle whose `credit_status` is no longer `OPEN` — at which point the persisted `earned_*` figures hold the final result instead.

The two variants are not merely stricter and looser versions of each other, and this is the subtlest part of the domain. Because the caps below are consumed in chronological order, an early pending record can eat the self-study or on-demand allowance that a later verified record would otherwise have taken. Dropping pending records therefore frees cap room for later verified ones, and a specialty total can move from non-zero to zero or from zero to non-zero between the two variants over identical stored data.

**Chronological accumulation.** The records in the variant's input set are processed in ascending order of `completion_date`, breaking ties by ascending `created_at`. The order is never by identifier and never insertion order alone. Four running allowances start each run at zero: self-study minutes used, on-demand minutes used, non-live minutes used, and reciprocity minutes used. Four running category totals and two running overall totals — countable minutes and live minutes — likewise start at zero.

For each record in turn, the algorithm begins with the record's full `credited_minutes` as the room available and then narrows it by whichever caps apply. A record whose `effective_format` is `SELF_STUDY` has its room reduced to the smallest of three quantities: its own credited minutes, the 360-minute self-study allowance (6 credit hours per cycle) less self-study minutes already used, and the 900-minute non-live allowance (15 credit hours per cycle) less non-live minutes already used. A record whose `effective_format` is `ON_DEMAND` has its room reduced to the smallest of its own credited minutes, the 720-minute on-demand allowance (12 credit hours per cycle) less on-demand minutes already used, and the same 900-minute non-live allowance less non-live minutes already used. A record whose `source` is `RECIPROCITY` has its room further reduced to the smaller of the room reached so far and the 900-minute reciprocity allowance (15 credit hours per cycle) less reciprocity minutes already used — and note that this narrowing composes with the format narrowing rather than replacing it. Live minutes are never capped. Whatever room survives is floored at zero, and the result is that record's `countable_minutes`.

**The truncation waterfall.** When a cap bites, the lost minutes have to be taken out of some category, and the board takes them from the least valuable category first so that specialty minutes survive truncation. The shortfall for a record is its `credited_minutes` less its surviving countable minutes. That shortfall is absorbed in the pinned bucket order `GENERAL`, then `BIAS_ELIMINATION`, then `TECHNOLOGY`, then `ETHICS`: for each bucket in that order, the amount taken is the smaller of the shortfall still outstanding and that record's credited minutes in that bucket; the bucket contributes its credited minutes *less* the amount taken to the cycle's running total for that category; and the outstanding shortfall drops by the amount taken. A record with no cap pressure therefore contributes each of its four credited category figures in full.

After the record is accounted for, the run adds its countable minutes to the overall countable total; adds them to the self-study and non-live allowances used when its `effective_format` is `SELF_STUDY`; adds them to the on-demand and non-live allowances used when its `effective_format` is `ON_DEMAND`; adds them to the running live total when its `effective_format` is `LIVE`; and adds them to the reciprocity allowance used when its `source` is `RECIPROCITY`.

When every record in the input set has been processed, the overall countable total becomes `earned_countable_minutes`, the three specialty running totals become `earned_ethics_minutes`, `earned_technology_minutes` and `earned_bias_minutes`, and the running live total becomes `earned_live_minutes`. Every credit record of the cycle that was *not* in this variant's input set has its `countable_minutes` rewritten to zero, so the persisted per-record figures always reconcile against the cycle's earned totals for whichever variant last ran.

**Deficiency, with carryover on top.** Carried-in minutes are never folded into `earned_countable_minutes`; they are applied only when the shortfall is measured. The effective total for the period is `earned_countable_minutes` plus `carryover_in_general_minutes` plus `carryover_in_ethics_minutes` — carried-in ethics minutes count toward the general requirement as well as their own. The effective ethics figure is `earned_ethics_minutes` plus `carryover_in_ethics_minutes`. The four shortfalls are then: the total shortfall, being `required_total_minutes` less the effective total, floored at zero; the ethics shortfall, being `required_ethics_minutes` less the effective ethics figure, floored at zero; the technology shortfall, being `required_technology_minutes` less `earned_technology_minutes`, floored at zero; and the bias shortfall, being `required_bias_minutes` less `earned_bias_minutes`, floored at zero. Carried-in minutes never reduce the technology or bias shortfalls — only live and current-period instruction can satisfy those.

**Seeing both answers at once.** Because the provisional and final pictures can diverge sharply, the cycle summary read runs the algorithm *both* ways over the same stored data and returns the two results side by side without persisting anything. It echoes `cycle_id`, `credit_status`, `administrative_status` and the four requirement figures `required_total_minutes`, `required_ethics_minutes`, `required_technology_minutes` and `required_bias_minutes`. The provisional run supplies `provisional_total_minutes`, `provisional_ethics_minutes`, `provisional_technology_minutes`, `provisional_bias_minutes`, `provisional_live_minutes`, `provisional_deficiency_total_minutes` and `provisional_deficiency_ethics_minutes`. The final run supplies `projected_final_total_minutes`, `projected_final_ethics_minutes`, `projected_final_technology_minutes`, `projected_final_bias_minutes`, `projected_final_live_minutes`, `projected_final_deficiency_total_minutes`, `projected_final_deficiency_ethics_minutes`, `projected_final_deficiency_technology_minutes` and `projected_final_deficiency_bias_minutes`. Four bucket fields report the **provisional** run's cap consumption: `self_study_used_minutes`, `on_demand_used_minutes`, `nonlive_used_minutes` and `reciprocity_used_minutes`. The carryover ledger appears as `carryover_in_general_minutes` and `carryover_in_ethics_minutes` alongside `projected_carryover_out_general_minutes` and `projected_carryover_out_ethics_minutes`, which are the carryover determination of §3.8 applied to the final run, and `projected_late_fee_cents`, which is the late-fee derivation of §3.9 applied to the final run. Two counts close the payload: `records_counted` is the size of the provisional input set, and `records_excluded` is the number of the cycle's records in `REJECTED` or `VOIDED`.

### 3.7. Out-of-State Instruction: Reciprocity and Comity

Attorneys move, and instruction accredited elsewhere sometimes deserves recognition here. The board offers two quite different accommodations, and an attorney chooses between them with the `basis` field when filing a claim: `RECIPROCITY` converts a specific out-of-state course into home-jurisdiction minutes, while `COMITY` extinguishes the home general-hour requirement altogether for an attorney whose practice is genuinely centred in a comity jurisdiction.

A claim is filed by the attorney themselves and names `attorney_id` (which must equal the acting `X-User-ID`), the `basis`, the two-letter `origin_jurisdiction` that accredited the instruction, the `origin_course_title` (1 to 200 characters), the `origin_credit_hours` as awarded there, the `origin_category` label used there, and the `completion_date`. `origin_credit_hours` must be greater than zero and may carry at most one decimal place; more precision than that is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. `origin_category` is one of `GENERAL`, `PROFESSIONALISM`, `ETHICS`, `TECHNOLOGY` or `DIVERSITY_INCLUSION` — the origin jurisdiction's taxonomy, not the board's. Filing also requires `expected_attorney_version` as described in §2.6, and requires the attorney's `status` to be `ACTIVE` or `EXEMPT`, failing which the filing is `ERR_ATTORNEY_NOT_IN_GOOD_STANDING`. A new claim is created in `SUBMITTED` with `converted_minutes` at zero, `converted_category` at `GENERAL` and `determination_reason` null, and the act of filing raises the attorney's own `version` by one.

Board staff then determine the claim by naming an `action` of `APPROVE` or `DENY`, optionally with a `determination_reason`. `DENY` requires that reason and moves the claim `SUBMITTED → DENIED`, recording it. Both outcomes require the claim to still be in `SUBMITTED`. Approval requires the attorney's `status` to be `ACTIVE` or `EXEMPT`, and requires that the attorney have a compliance cycle whose half-open window contains the claim's `completion_date` and whose `credit_status` is `OPEN`; where no such open cycle exists the approval is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, and where the resolved cycle belongs to a different attorney it is `ERR_CYCLE_ATTORNEY_MISMATCH`.

**The reciprocity pathway.** Approval on the `RECIPROCITY` basis first checks that the `origin_jurisdiction` is one the board has a reciprocal arrangement with — `NY`, `CA`, `TX`, `IL` or `FL`. Anything else is `ERR_JURISDICTION_NOT_RECIPROCAL`. Conversion then accounts for the fact that an "hour" is not the same length everywhere: `NY` and `FL` award credit in 50-minute hours, while `CA`, `TX` and `IL` use 60-minute hours. The origin minutes are `origin_credit_hours` multiplied by the origin jurisdiction's hour length, and `converted_minutes` is that product rounded **down** to a whole multiple of 15 minutes. Two New York credit hours therefore convert as follows: two hours at 50 minutes each is 100 minutes, and 100 minutes floored to a 15-minute multiple is 90 minutes. `converted_category` is the home category the origin label maps onto: `GENERAL` stays `GENERAL`, both `PROFESSIONALISM` and `ETHICS` map to `ETHICS`, `TECHNOLOGY` stays `TECHNOLOGY`, and `DIVERSITY_INCLUSION` maps to `BIAS_ELIMINATION`.

An approved reciprocity claim then cascades a credit record into the covering cycle. That record is created already `VERIFIED`, naming the claiming attorney and the covering cycle and carrying no `course_id`, with `source` set to `RECIPROCITY`, `reciprocity_claim_id` pointing back at the claim, `verification_method` recorded as `CERTIFICATE`, and `effective_format` of `LIVE`. Its credited minutes for the mapped category equal `converted_minutes`, and its `credited_minutes` equals `converted_minutes` as well, the other three category figures being zero. The cycle's credit application is then re-run, at which point the 900-minute reciprocity allowance described in §3.6 applies to the new record just as it does to any other reciprocity-sourced credit.

**The comity pathway.** Approval on the `COMITY` basis grants no minutes at all — `converted_minutes` stays 0 and `converted_category` stays at its `GENERAL` default, unused — and creates no credit record. Instead it must pass a three-part jurisdiction gate, every part of which reports `ERR_COMITY_NOT_AVAILABLE` on failure: the claim's `origin_jurisdiction` must be one of the two comity jurisdictions `NY` or `IL`; the attorney's `principal_office_jurisdiction` must equal that same jurisdiction; and that jurisdiction must not be the home code `ST`. In other words, comity is available only to an attorney whose principal office genuinely sits in a recognised comity jurisdiction outside this state.

Where the gate passes, the covering cycle records the claim in its `comity_claim_id` and then takes one of two divergent outcomes, decided by the cycle's specialty totals computed over `VERIFIED` records only. If `earned_ethics_minutes` has reached `required_ethics_minutes`, `earned_technology_minutes` has reached `required_technology_minutes`, and `earned_bias_minutes` has reached `required_bias_minutes`, the cycle moves `credit_status` from `OPEN` to `CLOSED_COMITY`: `certified_at` is stamped with the current logical instant, `earned_countable_minutes` is set to `required_total_minutes` because the general requirement is deemed met by comity, the three specialty earned figures keep their computed values, and both `carryover_out_general_minutes` and `carryover_out_ethics_minutes` are set to zero because a comity closure never carries anything forward. If even one of the three specialty totals is below its requirement, the cycle instead moves `credit_status` from `OPEN` to `DEFICIENT`, still recording `comity_claim_id` even though nothing was closed, and the attorney's `open_deficiency_count` rises by one. Comity waives the general hour requirement only; the ethics, technology and elimination-of-bias minimums survive it intact. Either outcome raises the cycle's `version` by one.

A third route into `DENIED` exists that no one requests: when a batch run drives an attorney into administrative suspension, every one of that attorney's claims still sitting in `SUBMITTED` is denied automatically, with `determination_reason` set to `ATTORNEY_ADMIN_SUSPENDED`.

### 3.8. Certifying a Period, Carrying Surplus Forward, and Recertification

Certification is the attorney's own act: a sworn declaration that the period is complete, filed by the attorney against one of its own cycles. It is the moment the provisional picture becomes a finding.

**What certification requires.** The addressed cycle's `credit_status` must still be `OPEN`. Its `administrative_status` must not be `ADMIN_SUSPENDED`, and an attempt against a suspended cycle is `ERR_CYCLE_ADMIN_SUSPENDED`. A `FINAL_COMPLIANCE_AFFIRMATION` must already exist against this cycle; certifying without one is `ERR_AFFIRMATION_REQUIRED`. The attorney's own `status` must be `ACTIVE` or `EXEMPT`, failing which the request is `ERR_ATTORNEY_NOT_IN_GOOD_STANDING`. What certification pointedly does **not** require is a zero fee balance — as §2.8 sets out, a late filing is still a filing, and the whole point is that it must be able to cure the delinquency that generated the balance in the first place.

The only body field is an optional `carryover_election`. When supplied it overwrites whatever the cycle was carrying, so an attorney may change its mind about carryover right up to the moment of filing; when omitted the stored election stands.

**What certification does.** The credit application runs in its final variant over `VERIFIED` records only. The resulting `earned_countable_minutes`, `earned_ethics_minutes`, `earned_technology_minutes`, `earned_bias_minutes` and `earned_live_minutes` are frozen into the cycle, every credit record in the cycle has its `countable_minutes` rewritten to its final-variant value, and `certified_at` is stamped with the current logical instant. The four deficiency figures of that final run then decide the outcome, and the fork is exhaustive: if the total deficiency is zero **and** all three specialty deficiencies are zero, `credit_status` moves `OPEN → SATISFIED`; if the total deficiency is above zero **or** any one specialty deficiency is above zero, `credit_status` moves `OPEN → DEFICIENT` and the attorney's `open_deficiency_count` rises by one. Either way the cycle's `version` rises by one. A deficient certification writes both `carryover_out_general_minutes` and `carryover_out_ethics_minutes` as zero: a period that fell short never carries anything forward, however large its raw total.

**The carryover determination.** For a satisfied certification the surplus is computed as follows, and it runs at the end of every persisted final run — at certification and on every final re-run a void provokes.

First, two circumstances suppress carryover outright, setting both `carryover_out_general_minutes` and `carryover_out_ethics_minutes` to zero: an election of `WAIVE_CARRYOVER`, or any of the four deficiency figures being above zero.

Otherwise the general surplus is computed as `earned_countable_minutes` plus `carryover_in_general_minutes` plus `carryover_in_ethics_minutes`, less `required_total_minutes`. `carryover_out_general_minutes` is then the smallest of three quantities: the general carryover ceiling of 600 minutes, that surplus floored at zero, and the cycle's `earned_live_minutes`. That third term is the substantive constraint — **only live minutes travel forward**. An attorney who satisfies a period largely on demand may show a large arithmetic surplus and still carry almost nothing, because the surplus is clamped down to however many of its countable minutes came from live instruction.

The ethics surplus is `earned_ethics_minutes` less `required_ethics_minutes`, and `carryover_out_ethics_minutes` is the smaller of the ethics carryover ceiling of 120 minutes and that surplus floored at zero. Note that this term is computed from `earned_ethics_minutes` alone: minutes that *arrived* as `carryover_in_ethics_minutes` help satisfy the current period's requirements, but they are never re-carried out of it. Carried-in ethics minutes cannot bounce down the chain of cycles indefinitely.

**Carryover is a conserved transfer.** A successor cycle's `carryover_in_general_minutes` and `carryover_in_ethics_minutes` always equal its predecessor's `carryover_out_general_minutes` and `carryover_out_ethics_minutes` — not merely at the moment the successor was created, but at all times. So certification cascades: any cycle whose `previous_cycle_id` names the just-certified cycle has its two `carryover_in_*` fields rewritten to match the two `carryover_out_*` figures just computed, and then has its own earned and deficiency figures recomputed under the provisional variant, since that successor is presumably still open. The invariant holds in the opposite direction too, which is what makes recertification necessary.

**Recertification: when a finding comes undone.** A certified period is not immune to later events. When a `VERIFIED` credit record inside a `SATISFIED` cycle is voided — by board adjudication, or by the revocation of the course it was earned at — the final variant is re-run, and if that re-run now leaves any deficiency, the cycle moves `credit_status` from `SATISFIED` back to `OPEN`. Its `certified_at` reverts to null, both `carryover_out_*` figures are reset to zero, and the attorney's `pending_recertification_count` rises by one — that counter exists precisely to record how many times one of this attorney's settled periods has been reopened by a downstream void. The reversion then cascades in exactly the conserved way described above: any cycle whose `previous_cycle_id` names the reopened cycle has its `carryover_in_general_minutes` and `carryover_in_ethics_minutes` rewritten to zero and its own figures re-derived. A void two links down the chain therefore reaches back into a period the attorney thought was closed, and forward into the one that inherited its surplus. The cycle's `version` rises by one.

**Curing a delinquency by filing.** The second thing certification can do is repair the cycle's other status dimension. Where the addressed cycle's `administrative_status` is `DELINQUENT` and the final run yields zero total and zero specialty deficiency, the same request also moves `administrative_status` from `DELINQUENT` back to `GOOD_STANDING`, sets `delinquent_since` back to null, and moves `credit_status` to `SATISFIED` — the one place in this specification where a single request writes both status dimensions of a cycle. Whether the late fee survives depends on how quickly the attorney cured: if the current logical instant is no later than seven days after the recorded `delinquent_since`, every `FeeAssessment` against this cycle with `fee_type` of `LATE_COMPLIANCE` still in `ASSESSED` moves `ASSESSED → VOIDED`, and each such void lowers the attorney's `outstanding_fee_cents` by that assessment's `amount_cents`. Once that seven-day grace window has passed the assessment simply stays `ASSESSED` and remains payable, even though the delinquency itself is cured. The comparison is made against the `delinquent_since` value the cycle carried on arrival, before the request nulls it.

### 3.9. Oversight and Enforcement: Complaints, Audits, Risk Scoring, the Batch Run and Fees

The last part of the domain is the board's own supervisory machinery. It has three entry points — a complaint filed by a human, an audit engagement opened by the board, and a batch run executed against the logical clock — and they interlock through a single numeric summary of how much trouble a sponsor is worth watching for.

**Complaints.** Either an attorney or a board member may file a complaint against a `Provider` or against an individual `Course`. The filing names a `subject_type` of `PROVIDER` or `COURSE`, a polymorphic `subject_id` validated against whichever table `subject_type` selects, a free-text `allegation` of 1 to 2000 characters, and an `allegation_category` of `MISREPRESENTED_CREDIT`, `NO_SHOW_FACULTY`, `ATTENDANCE_FRAUD` or `FEE_DISPUTE`. A `subject_id` that resolves to nothing in the selected table is `ERR_COMPLAINT_NOT_FOUND`. The complaint is created in `RECEIVED` with `escalated` false, and the filer is recorded in two fields — `complainant_role`, which takes the value `ATTORNEY` or `BOARD_ADMIN` (note that these are the recorded enumeration labels, not the header spellings of the roles), and `complainant_user_id`, which holds the acting `X-User-ID`.

Recording the filer has a purpose: it disqualifies them. Disposition is board work, driven by an `action` of `OPEN_REVIEW`, `SUBSTANTIATE` or `DISMISS`, and on every one of those actions the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the stored `complainant_user_id`. A board member who files a complaint and then tries to dispose of it is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. `OPEN_REVIEW` moves `RECEIVED → UNDER_REVIEW` and does nothing else. `DISMISS` is available from either `RECEIVED` or `UNDER_REVIEW`, requires a `disposition_reason`, and has no side effects beyond recording it. `SUBSTANTIATE` is available only from `UNDER_REVIEW` — substantiating a complaint nobody has opened for review is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` — and likewise requires a `disposition_reason`. Both `SUBSTANTIATED` and `DISMISSED` are terminal.

`SUBSTANTIATE` is the head of the longest cascade in this specification, and it branches on the complaint's subject. Against a provider, the provider's `substantiated_complaint_count` rises by one, its `risk_score` is recomputed, and the provider itself moves `ACCREDITED → PROBATIONARY`. Against a course, the course moves to `REVOKED` from `APPROVED`, `SUSPENDED_PENDING_REVIEW` or `EXPIRED`; that revocation copies the `disposition_reason` into the course's `denial_reason` and voids every `VERIFIED` credit record earned at that course, stamping each with a `void_reason` of `COURSE_REVOKED`; each void re-runs the owning cycle's credit application; and that re-run can revert a `SATISFIED` cycle to `OPEN` as §3.8 describes, which in turn rewrites the successor cycle's `carryover_in_general_minutes` and `carryover_in_ethics_minutes` to zero and re-derives its figures. When the revoked course has a sponsoring provider, that provider also takes `substantiated_complaint_count` plus one and a recomputed `risk_score`. One board decision can therefore reach four entities deep and reopen a compliance period certified long ago.

**Opening an audit.** The board opens an audit engagement over a **closed** window `[audit_period_start, audit_period_end]`, naming the `provider_id`, a `trigger_type` of `RISK_BASED`, `COMPLAINT_ESCALATION` or `RANDOM`, and optionally an `originating_complaint_id`. Where that complaint reference is supplied, `trigger_type` must be `COMPLAINT_ESCALATION`, the named complaint must not already have `escalated` true — otherwise `ERR_COMPLAINT_ALREADY_ESCALATED` — and the complaint must resolve to the same provider the audit names, a mismatch being `ERR_COURSE_PROVIDER_MISMATCH`. Accepting the audit sets that complaint's `escalated` to true, so a single complaint can seed at most one audit.

Two structural constraints apply. A provider may hold only one live engagement at a time: opening a second while an existing audit of that provider is in `OPENED`, `SAMPLED` or `FINDINGS_ISSUED` is `ERR_AUDIT_ENGAGEMENT_ACTIVE`. And audit windows of the same provider may neither overlap nor abut — because the audit window is closed at both ends, a new window whose start equals an existing window's end shares that instant and collides, giving `ERR_AUDIT_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. This is deliberately the opposite of the compliance-cycle rule of §3.4, where the half-open period makes exact adjacency legal.

A new audit is created in `OPENED` with `sample_size` zero, `sampled_course_ids` empty, `findings_count` zero and `sanction_type` of `NONE`. It also captures `risk_score_at_open`: the provider's risk score computed at that moment and **frozen** onto the audit row, never refreshed afterwards even as the provider's live `risk_score` moves. That frozen number, and nothing else, decides how large a sample the engagement draws.

**How the risk score is computed.** The score is a weighted blend of four rates, each derived purely from the provider's own denormalized counters plus the logical clock — no course, complaint or audit rows are scanned at score time.

The delinquency rate is `attendance_reports_late_count` divided by `attendance_reports_total_count`, where the denominator is treated as one whenever the stored count is zero, so a provider that has filed nothing has a delinquency rate of zero rather than an undefined one. The complaint rate is `substantiated_complaint_count` divided by three, capped at 1.0 — three substantiated complaints saturate the term. The finding rate is `audit_findings_count` divided by a denominator that depends on the variant below, again capped at 1.0. The staleness rate measures how long it has been since anyone looked: the anchor is `last_audit_closed_at` when that field is set, and otherwise `accreditation_term_start`; if the anchor is still null the staleness rate is 1.0 outright, and otherwise it is the number of months between the anchor and the current logical instant divided by 24, capped at 1.0.

`risk_score` is then the delinquency rate weighted at 0.35, plus the complaint rate weighted at 0.30, plus the finding rate weighted at 0.25, plus the staleness rate weighted at 0.10, and the sum is rounded half-up to two decimal places. Each of the four rates is clamped into the range 0.0 to 1.0 **before** it is weighted, which is what guarantees `risk_score` always lies between 0.00 and 1.00. The rounding is applied once, to the weighted sum, and never to the individual terms.

**The two weightings.** The finding denominator differs by the provider's standing, and the difference is recorded in `risk_score_variant` alongside the score itself. Under `SELF_CERTIFIED_WEIGHTING`, used when the provider's `accreditation_status` is exactly `ACCREDITED`, the denominator is `courses_self_certified_count` (treated as one when zero): an accredited sponsor certifies its own catalogue, so its findings are judged only against the courses it waved through itself. Under `REVIEWED_WEIGHTING`, used for every other `accreditation_status` — `APPLIED`, `PROBATIONARY`, `SUSPENDED`, `LAPSED` or `DENIED` — the denominator is `courses_total_count` (again treated as one when zero), because every course such a sponsor offers passed board review. Since the self-certified subset can only be smaller than or equal to the whole catalogue, identical counters yield a rate that is at least as large, and a score that is strictly higher whenever the two counts differ, under the self-certified weighting. Losing accreditation can therefore *lower* a sponsor's computed risk score, because responsibility for review shifts back to the board.

The score is refreshed at each of these moments: when an audit is opened (into `risk_score_at_open`), on every provider accreditation action, on a `SUBSTANTIATE` disposition, on the `CLOSE_CLEARED` and `CLOSE_SANCTIONED` audit actions, and on every batch run. On an audit action that cascades a change to the provider's own `accreditation_status`, the recomputation happens **after** that cascade lands, so the variant chosen reflects the provider's post-cascade standing rather than its standing on arrival.

**Drawing the sample.** Every audit action carries an `action` and an `expected_audit_version` as §2.6 requires, plus `findings_count` or `sanction_type` where the action needs them. `DRAW_SAMPLE` moves the audit `OPENED → SAMPLED`, and is also legitimately repeatable as a `SAMPLED → SAMPLED` redraw, which is exactly why the version guard exists.

The eligible pool is the audited provider's courses whose `status` is `APPROVED`, `EXPIRED` or `SUSPENDED_PENDING_REVIEW` and whose `presented_at` falls inside the audit's closed window, both endpoints included. A course in `REVOKED`, `PENDING_APPROVAL` or `DENIED` is never sampled. The pool is ordered by `presented_at` ascending and then `created_at` ascending — never by identifier, and never randomly, so the same engagement over the same data always draws the same courses. The target size comes from the frozen score in three tiers: ten courses when `risk_score_at_open` is 0.60 or above, five when it is 0.30 or above but below 0.60, and two otherwise. `sample_size` is then the smaller of the target and the pool size — a pool smaller than the target simply yields a short sample and is not an error — and `sampled_course_ids` is the identifiers of the first `sample_size` entries of the sorted pool, in that order. An empty pool *is* an error: the request is rejected with `ERR_NO_SAMPLE_POPULATION` and nothing at all is written, so `sampled_course_ids` stays empty, `sample_size` stays zero and `status` stays `OPENED`. A redraw recomputes the whole list from scratch rather than appending to it, and the provider's `courses_sampled_lifetime` is adjusted by the difference between the new and prior `sample_size` — on a first draw that difference is simply the new sample size.

**Findings and closure.** `ISSUE_FINDINGS` moves `SAMPLED → FINDINGS_ISSUED` and records a `findings_count` that must be at least zero and no greater than the audit's `sample_size`; more findings than courses examined is `ERR_FINDINGS_EXCEED_SAMPLE`. The count is added to the provider's `audit_findings_count` and the risk score is recomputed. From `FINDINGS_ISSUED` the engagement closes one of two ways. `CLOSE_CLEARED` requires `findings_count` of at most one, stamps `closed_at` with the current instant, sets `sanction_type` to `NONE`, writes that instant into the provider's `last_audit_closed_at` — which resets the staleness term of the score — and unlocks the sponsor's `PROBATIONARY → ACCREDITED` gate described in §3.1. `CLOSE_SANCTIONED` requires `findings_count` of at least one and a supplied `sanction_type` of `PROBATION` or `SUSPENSION`; it stamps `closed_at`, records the sanction, writes `last_audit_closed_at`, and assesses a `FeeAssessment` of `fee_type` `AUDIT_SANCTION` against the provider for 250000 cents. `PROBATION` then moves the provider `ACCREDITED → PROBATIONARY`; `SUSPENSION` moves it to `SUSPENDED` from either `ACCREDITED` or `PROBATIONARY`, which suspends that sponsor's self-certified courses, which in turn rejects those courses' credit records still awaiting verification — the same chain §3.1 describes. A single audit with exactly one finding may therefore be closed either way, and the board's choice of action alone decides whether a sponsor is cleared or suspended.

**The batch run.** Nothing in this system ages by itself. Deadlines pass, approvals expire and accreditation terms run out only when the board executes its reporting run, which a board administrator triggers with no body at all and which returns a `ReportingSweep` record of what it did. Each run stamps `as_of` with the current logical instant and takes a `sweep_number` one higher than the previous run's, the first run being number 1. It is the sole producer of delinquency notices, administrative suspensions, late fees, course expiry and provider lapse — no interactive request creates any of those five things.

Compliance cycles are examined in `filing_deadline` ascending and then `created_at` ascending order, which makes the run reproducible and makes the ordering of the assessments it creates deterministic. `cycles_examined` counts the cycles the run considered.

For each cycle in that order, two enforcement steps may fire. The first: a cycle whose `administrative_status` is `GOOD_STANDING`, whose `credit_status` is `OPEN` or `DEFICIENT`, and whose `filing_deadline` is at or before the run's `as_of`, moves `administrative_status` from `GOOD_STANDING` to `DELINQUENT`. Its `delinquent_since` is set to the run's `as_of` — not to the deadline it missed — and a `FeeAssessment` is created with `fee_type` `LATE_COMPLIANCE`, the cycle's `attorney_id` and `cycle_id`, an `assessed_by_sweep_id` naming this run, `status` of `ASSESSED` and an `amount_cents` derived as described below. That assessment raises the attorney's `outstanding_fee_cents` by its `amount_cents`, and the run's `delinquencies_issued` and `late_fees_assessed_cents` tallies rise by one and by that amount respectively. Note what this step does not check: a `SATISFIED` cycle is skipped because a cycle that certified on time cannot be delinquent, but a `DEFICIENT` cycle is not skipped — certifying short of the requirement does not protect an attorney from the deadline.

**How the late fee is computed.** The amount follows seven steps whose order is pinned; performing them in any other sequence produces a different number.

Step one suppresses unreliable carryover. If the cycle has a `previous_cycle_id` and that predecessor's `credit_status` is still `OPEN`, then the effective carried-in general minutes are zero, because a predecessor that has not certified has not established any surplus to hand forward. Otherwise the effective figure is the cycle's stored `carryover_in_general_minutes`. Step two runs the credit application in its final variant — `VERIFIED` records only — substituting that effective figure for `carryover_in_general_minutes`. Records still in `PENDING_VERIFICATION` never reduce a late fee. Step three totals the shortfall across all four requirements: the total deficiency plus the ethics, technology and elimination-of-bias deficiencies, added together as one number of minutes. Step four converts that to whole hours, rounding **up** — one minute short is one deficient hour. Step five picks a base amount from the rounded hour count: zero hours gives 15000 cents, one to five hours gives 25000, six to fifteen gives 40000, and sixteen or more gives 60000. Step six adds a surcharge of 2500 cents for each deficient hour, counting at most twenty hours. Step seven caps the total: `amount_cents` is the smaller of 100000 cents and the base plus surcharge.

The ceiling of step four must be taken before step five selects its tier, the surcharge of step six must be computed on the hour count already capped at twenty, and the overall cap of step seven must be applied to the sum rather than to either component. Observe that a perfectly compliant but late filer still pays: zero deficient hours yields the 15000-cent base plus a zero surcharge, because the fee is for filing late, not for being short. The same derivation is exposed, without persisting anything, as the `projected_late_fee_cents` field of the cycle summary read described in §3.6.

**Suspension.** The second enforcement step: a cycle whose `administrative_status` is `DELINQUENT`, whose `credit_status` is `OPEN` or `DEFICIENT`, and whose `delinquent_since` is at least 60 days before the run's `as_of`, moves `administrative_status` from `DELINQUENT` to `ADMIN_SUSPENDED`. This cascades: the attorney's own `status` moves `ACTIVE → ADMIN_SUSPENDED`, which is the only way that value is ever written; every reciprocity or comity claim of that attorney still in `SUBMITTED` is denied with `determination_reason` of `ATTORNEY_ADMIN_SUSPENDED`, as §3.7 notes; and a second `FeeAssessment` is created with `fee_type` `REINSTATEMENT` for 50000 cents, naming the same `attorney_id`, `cycle_id` and `assessed_by_sweep_id`, which raises `outstanding_fee_cents` again. The run's `suspensions_issued` rises by one and its `late_fees_assessed_cents` rises by the reinstatement amount as well — that tally is the run's total money assessed, not its late-fee money alone. From this point the cycle accepts no credit-bearing write of any kind, per §2.8.

**Expiry and lapse.** The same run also ages the sponsor side. Every course in `APPROVED` whose `expires_at` is at or before `as_of` moves to `EXPIRED`, and `courses_expired` counts them. Every provider whose `accreditation_status` is `ACCREDITED` or `PROBATIONARY` and whose `accreditation_term_end` is at or before `as_of` moves to `LAPSED` — with the course-suspension cascade on the `ACCREDITED` branch only, exactly as §3.1 sets out — and `providers_lapsed` counts them. Finally, `risk_score` and `risk_score_variant` are recomputed for every provider the run touched — which matters because lapsing changes a provider's `accreditation_status` and therefore changes which weighting its score is computed under.

**Settling an assessment.** An assessment is settled by naming an `action` of `PAY` or `WAIVE`. `PAY` is available to the attorney the assessment names — its `X-User-ID` must equal the assessment's `attorney_id` — and to the board. `WAIVE` is reserved to the board alone and requires a `waiver_reason`; an attorney or a provider administrator requesting a waiver is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` even though the endpoint itself admits their role. Both actions require the assessment to still be in `ASSESSED`; settling one already `PAID`, `WAIVED` or `VOIDED` is `ERR_FEE_ALREADY_SETTLED`. Either action stamps `settled_at` with the current instant, moves `status` to `PAID` or `WAIVED` accordingly, and — when the assessment names an `attorney_id` — lowers that attorney's `outstanding_fee_cents` by exactly this assessment's `amount_cents`. A provider-side assessment, whose `attorney_id` is null and whose `provider_id` is set, moves no attorney balance. `VOIDED` is unreachable through this endpoint: as §3.8 explains, the only producer of that state is the automatic void of a `LATE_COMPLIANCE` assessment when a certification cures the delinquency inside the seven-day grace window.

**Reinstatement.** Lifting an administrative suspension is board work. The request names the `cycle_id` of the suspended cycle, a `cure_basis` of `FEES_AND_CREDITS_CURED` or `BOARD_ORDER`, and an optional `reason`. Both bases require the attorney's `status` to be `ADMIN_SUSPENDED` and require the attorney's `outstanding_fee_cents` to be exactly zero — every assessment paid, waived or voided — and a non-zero balance is rejected with `ERR_OUTSTANDING_FEE_BALANCE`, which is evaluated after every other check in the order of §2.8. `FEES_AND_CREDITS_CURED` additionally requires that a final-variant run over `VERIFIED` records only shows no total deficiency and no specialty deficiency; `BOARD_ORDER` requires only the zero balance, and is how the board readmits an attorney whose credits are still short.

On success the named cycle moves `administrative_status` from `ADMIN_SUSPENDED` to `REINSTATED` and its `delinquent_since` returns to null, and the attorney's `status` moves `ADMIN_SUSPENDED → ACTIVE`. Under `FEES_AND_CREDITS_CURED` the cycle's `credit_status` also moves to `SATISFIED`, since the cure evaluation just proved the requirement met; under `BOARD_ORDER` the `credit_status` is left exactly as it was, so a reinstated attorney may perfectly well hold a cycle that is `REINSTATED` and `DEFICIENT` at the same time. Both the attorney's `version` and the cycle's `version` rise by one. Note that `REINSTATED` is a terminal administrative state in the sense that no transition leads out of it: a reinstated cycle cannot become delinquent again, because the batch run's first step only examines cycles in `GOOD_STANDING`.
