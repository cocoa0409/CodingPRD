# Esports Org Operations API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A professional esports organization lives or dies on two things it cannot see clearly today: what it has promised its players, and what those promises cost. Contract negotiations run through agents over days of counters and revisions; league offices impose roster locks, minimum salaries and player-association floors; guardians of minor players hold veto power over both signings and practice schedules; sponsors expect a fixed number of content deliverables placed across a roster that is itself changing through transfers, releases and buyouts. When prize money finally arrives, it must be split between the association, the organization and the players, with withholding rates that depend on where each player is a tax resident and a trust holdback for anyone under age. Today all of this is tracked in spreadsheets that disagree with each other, and the disagreements are only discovered when a league auditor asks for the numbers.

This document specifies the **Esports Org Operations API**, a single system of record for player scouting, contract negotiation, roster administration, facility and practice operations, sponsorship obligations, wellness monitoring and prize distribution. The service is deliberately opinionated about arithmetic: every money amount is an integer number of cents, every proportional share is expressed in basis points, and every derived figure has exactly one defined evaluation order. It is equally opinionated about authority: the organization that requests a roster change is not the party that approves it, and the finance officer who computes a prize split is not the person who signs it off.

Two properties are treated as non-negotiable throughout. First, **conservation**: a salary commitment released by executing a transfer must be restorable byte-for-byte if that transfer is later reversed, and the four components of a prize pot must always add up to the gross prize. Second, **protection of minors**: age is derived, never asserted, and a minor's guardian consent, schooling reservation and weekly workload ceiling gate every operation that would put that player to work. Everything else in this document — states, cascades, error codes, precedence rules — exists to make those two properties observable through the API.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint, including read-only ones, must carry two headers: `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role`. Header names are matched case-insensitively, as HTTP requires. If either header is absent or its value is an empty string, the request is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before anything else about the request is examined. If `X-User-Role` carries a value that is not one of the ten recognised role names, or carries a recognised role that is not authorised for the endpoint being addressed, the request is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). The authoritative role-to-endpoint mapping is the table in §4.

The ten roles are `LeagueAdmin`, `GeneralManager`, `PlayerAgent`, `Scout`, `CoachingStaff`, `PerformanceSupport`, `SponsorshipManager`, `FinanceOfficer`, `OpsManager` and `GuardianRepresentative`. Their business remits are, respectively: the league office (calendar, roster locks, contract suspensions, roster-move decisions and reversals, prize sign-off and the logical clock); an organization's front office (sending and revising offers, filing roster moves and executing approved ones); a player's representative (countering, accepting and declining offers, and submitting sponsorship deliverables); talent scouting (registering prospects and maintaining their profiles); coaching (booking, completing and cancelling practice, review and tryout blocks); performance and wellness staff (filing wellness reports and reading obligation summaries); the commercial team (drafting and activating sponsorship deals, placing deliverables and reviewing submissions); finance (recording tournament prizes, running the waterfall and voiding bad distributions); facility operations (opening team houses and tuning their buffers and seat capacities); and the guardian of a minor player (recording consent and pinning a stricter weekly workload cap).

Three of those roles are **entity-backed on an Organization**. When `X-User-Role` is `GeneralManager`, `SponsorshipManager` or `OpsManager`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Organization`; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Organization` compare that resource's organization foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. Two roles are **entity-backed on a Player**. When `X-User-Role` is `PlayerAgent` or `GuardianRepresentative`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Player`; ownership checks compare `X-User-ID` to the player the addressed resource resolves to — directly for a player resource, and through the `ContractOffer`, `PlayerContract` or `DeliverableAssignment` that names that player for the others. An entity-backed caller that addresses a resource belonging to a different `Organization` or a different `Player` is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403).

The remaining five roles are **opaque**. When `X-User-Role` is `LeagueAdmin`, `Scout`, `CoachingStaff`, `PerformanceSupport` or `FinanceOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity and is never compared to an entity's `id`. The server does, however, record it where the workflow demands an audit trail: a scout's identifier lands in `scouted_by_user_id` on the player they register, a coach's identifier in `coach_user_id` on the block they book, a finance officer's identifier in `calculated_by_user_id` on the distribution they compute, and a league administrator's identifier in `approved_by_user_id` on the distribution or roster move they decide. Because those recorded identifiers are the anchors of duty segregation, two operations additionally compare identifiers and refuse with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403): a prize distribution may not be approved by the same `X-User-ID` that calculated it, and a roster move may not be decided by the same `X-User-ID` recorded in its `requested_by_user_id`. The acceptance of an offer records the accepting caller in `signed_by_user_id` on the contract it creates.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters, `limit` and `offset`. `limit` defaults to 10, has a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100; `offset` defaults to 0 and has a minimum of 0. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) rather than being silently clamped.

Unless a subsection states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit` and `offset` as they were applied, `total_count` as the number of records matching the filters before the page window is applied, and `has_more` as true exactly when records remain beyond the current window. Filter query parameters, where an endpoint offers them, combine conjunctively: a request that supplies both an organization filter and a status filter returns only records matching both.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties, Timestamps and Versioning

Every resource this API creates carries a server-minted `id`: a UUID string with no type prefix and no embedded meaning. Clients never supply an `id`; they supply foreign keys that reference ids they have already been given. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recorded at insertion.

All twelve resource kinds in this system — leagues, organizations, players, contract offers, player contracts, team houses, practice blocks, sponsorship deals, deliverable assignments, prize distributions, prize allocations and roster moves — additionally carry `updated_at` and `version`. `updated_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, initially equal to `created_at`. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that resource, whether the mutation was requested directly by a client or produced as a side effect of an operation on a different resource. This matters in both directions: when suspending a contract cancels a scheduled practice block, that block's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` is incremented even though nobody addressed the block; and when accepting an offer moves a player to a new lifecycle state, the player's `version` advances as part of the same request.

Computed fields are never client-writable. Where a field is described below as derived, an attempt to supply it in a request body is simply ignored — the server's own evaluation is authoritative. Where a field is described as frozen at a particular moment, later changes to the source of that value do not propagate: adjusting a team house's turnaround buffer does not re-validate blocks already scheduled there, and adjusting a league's minimum salary does not disturb contracts already signed.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock and Instant Conventions

The service does not read wall-clock time. It maintains a **logical clock**, referred to throughout this document as the current instant, and before any client sets it that clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. An administrative test-driving capability lets a league administrator move the clock to a supplied `current_utc` instant, and the response echoes the `current_utc` now in force. Moving the clock mutates no entity eagerly: offers do not become expired at the moment the clock passes their deadline, wellness holds do not lift by themselves, and no counter is recomputed until the next request touches the affected resource. Every subsequent evaluation of a league window, an age, a weekly cap, a burnout score or a transfer settlement uses the new instant.

All instants exchanged with this API are ISO 8601 UTC values with three fractional digits, and comparisons between them are exact. Two boundary conventions apply throughout and are stated once here. Where a limit is a *deadline*, the boundary instant is already past: the roster lock is active once the current instant has reached `roster_lock_at`, an offer is expired once the current instant has reached `expires_at`, a practice block may be completed once the current instant has reached its `ends_at`, and a wellness hold has lifted once the current instant has reached `wellness_hold_until`. Where a limit is a *window*, the window is half-open — inclusive of its opening instant and exclusive of its closing instant: the transfer window spans from `transfer_window_open_at` up to but not including `transfer_window_close_at`, a practice block occupies its facility from `starts_at` up to but not including `ends_at`, a sponsorship deliverable's deadline must fall from the deal's `starts_at` up to but not including its `ends_at`, and a contract's `effective_at` must fall from the league's `season_start_at` up to but not including its `season_end_at`.

### 2.6. Order of Evaluation for Rejections

A single request can violate several rules at once, and which error the client sees is part of the contract rather than an implementation detail. Checks are grouped into thirteen precedence bands, evaluated in ascending order; **the first violated band determines the response, and no later band runs**. Within the description of each workflow in §3 the individual checks are described in business terms; the bands below fix their relative order.

1. **Authentication** — both headers present and non-empty, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. **Authorization** — `X-User-Role` recognised and permitted on the endpoint, else `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
3. **Path existence** — every `{id}` segment in the URL is resolved from left to right, and the leftmost segment that fails to resolve determines the error. An unresolvable league yields `ERR_LEAGUE_NOT_FOUND`, an organization `ERR_ORGANIZATION_NOT_FOUND`, a player `ERR_PLAYER_NOT_FOUND`, a contract offer `ERR_OFFER_NOT_FOUND`, a player contract `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`, a team house `ERR_TEAM_HOUSE_NOT_FOUND`, a practice block `ERR_PRACTICE_BLOCK_NOT_FOUND`, a sponsorship deal `ERR_DEAL_NOT_FOUND`, a deliverable assignment `ERR_DELIVERABLE_NOT_FOUND`, a prize distribution `ERR_DISTRIBUTION_NOT_FOUND` and a roster move `ERR_ROSTER_MOVE_NOT_FOUND`. All of these carry HTTP 404.
4. **Resource ownership** — for the five entity-backed roles, the check described in §2.1, yielding `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. It applies to every endpoint whose target resource resolves to an `Organization` or to a `Player`.
5. **Request shape** — required body fields present; every enum-typed value inside its declared value set; every integer inside its declared range; every basis-point field between 0 and 10000 inclusive; `ends_at` strictly after `starts_at` on a practice block and on a sponsorship deal; `participant_player_ids` non-empty and free of duplicate entries; a manual block cancellation supplying `cancel_reason` equal to `COACH_CANCELLED`; a `decision` field equal to either `APPROVE` or `REJECT`; and pagination inside the bounds of §2.3. Any of these yields `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).
6. **Foreign keys in the body or query** — a `league_id` that does not resolve yields `ERR_LEAGUE_NOT_FOUND`; an `organization_id` or `transfer_to_organization_id` that does not resolve yields `ERR_ORGANIZATION_NOT_FOUND`; a `player_id`, or any single entry of `participant_player_ids`, that does not resolve yields `ERR_PLAYER_NOT_FOUND`; a `contract_id` that does not resolve yields `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`; a `team_house_id` that does not resolve yields `ERR_TEAM_HOUSE_NOT_FOUND`; and a `supersedes_offer_id` that either does not resolve or names an offer belonging to a different player or a different organization yields `ERR_OFFER_NOT_FOUND`. Band 6 applies to offer creation, practice-block creation, sponsorship-deal creation, prize-distribution creation, organization registration, roster-move filing and deliverable placement — wherever those keys appear.
7. **Optimistic concurrency** — the two parent-version checks of §2.8, yielding `ERR_DEAL_VERSION_STALE` or `ERR_ORGANIZATION_VERSION_STALE` (HTTP 412).
8. **State preconditions** — the lifecycle guards of the entity being operated on, described per workflow in §3. This band contains `ERR_OFFER_EXPIRED`, `ERR_OFFER_NOT_OPEN`, `ERR_NEGOTIATION_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`, `ERR_PLAYER_ALREADY_SIGNED`, `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`, `ERR_BLOCK_NOT_SCHEDULED`, `ERR_DEAL_NOT_ACTIVE`, `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_SUBMITTED`, `ERR_DISTRIBUTION_NOT_DRAFT`, `ERR_DISTRIBUTION_NOT_CALCULATED`, `ERR_MOVE_NOT_PENDING`, `ERR_MOVE_NOT_APPROVED`, `ERR_MOVE_NOT_EXECUTED` and `ERR_MOVE_NOT_REVERSIBLE`. Inside this band, lazy offer expiry is evaluated before the offer's own status guard, so an offer whose deadline has passed reports `ERR_OFFER_EXPIRED` even when its status would also have failed the action's status requirement.
9. **Minor protection, education and wellness safety** — `ERR_GUARDIAN_CONSENT_REQUIRED`, `ERR_MINOR_CATEGORY_FORBIDDEN`, `ERR_EDUCATION_HOURS_RESERVED`, `ERR_WEEKLY_HOUR_CAP_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_WELLNESS_HOLD_ACTIVE` and `ERR_OBLIGATION_BREACH_BLOCKS_ASSIGNMENT`, in that internal order, with the education-versus-workload tie-break spelled out in §3.5.
10. **League calendar and temporal windows** — `ERR_ROSTER_LOCK_ACTIVE`, `ERR_TRANSFER_WINDOW_CLOSED`, `ERR_FREE_AGENCY_COOLDOWN`, `ERR_EFFECTIVE_AT_OUTSIDE_SEASON`, `ERR_DELIVERABLE_DUE_OUTSIDE_DEAL` and `ERR_BLOCK_NOT_FINISHED`.
11. **Facility, quota and roster-size constraints** — `ERR_FACILITY_SLOT_CONFLICT`, `ERR_PLAYER_DOUBLE_BOOKED`, `ERR_FACILITY_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_DELIVERABLE_QUOTA_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_TRYOUT_REQUIRED`, `ERR_ROSTER_SIZE_EXCEEDED` and `ERR_ROSTER_SIZE_BELOW_MINIMUM`.
12. **Referential exclusivity and duty segregation** — `ERR_EXCLUSIVITY_CONFLICT` and `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
13. **Fiscal and player-association terms** — `ERR_BELOW_MINIMUM_SALARY`, `ERR_PRIZE_SPLIT_BELOW_ASSOCIATION_MIN`, `ERR_STREAMING_HOURS_EXCEED_ASSOCIATION_MAX`, `ERR_SALARY_CAP_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_NEGATIVE_SETTLEMENT` and `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_CONTRACTS`.

Two consequences of this ladder deserve emphasis. A stale parent version (band 7) is reported before any lifecycle guard (band 8), so a client working from an out-of-date read learns that fact rather than learning about a state it may no longer be seeing. And the fiscal checks sit last, so a booking or a signing that is simultaneously unsafe for a minor and unaffordable for the organization reports the safety failure, not the money failure.

### 2.7. Money, Basis Points and Rounding Discipline

Every monetary quantity in this system is an integer number of **cents**; there are no decimal money values anywhere in the API. Every proportional quantity — salary shares, prize splits, withholding rates, buyout multipliers, agent commissions, association levies — is expressed in **basis points**, where 10000 basis points is the whole, so 250 basis points is two and a half percent and 3500 basis points is thirty-five percent. Unless a specific field's constraint says otherwise, a basis-point input must lie between 0 and 10000 inclusive; the sole exception is a contract's buyout multiplier, which may run from 0 up to 30000 basis points because a buyout may legitimately cost three times the remaining salary.

Wherever a proportion is applied to an amount, the product is divided by 10000 and the result is rounded **half up** to a whole cent — that is, a fractional part of exactly one half rounds away from zero. Rounding is applied once per named step in the order the step list for that calculation prescribes, never re-applied to an already-rounded figure and never deferred past the step in which it is written. Two calculations deliberately use truncation instead: a sponsorship deal's per-deliverable fee share and a prize allocation's per-row share are both computed by discarding the fractional remainder (rounding **down** to a whole cent), with the discarded remainder handled explicitly — the prize remainder is handed to a single designated row, and the sponsorship remainder is paid out with the final approval — so that no cent is ever created or destroyed.

Durations follow the same discipline. A "month" for contract-term arithmetic is exactly 30 days, a season for proration purposes is exactly 365 days, a year for commission and monthly-salary arithmetic is exactly 12 months, an hour is 3600 seconds and a day is 86400 seconds. Where a duration must be reduced to whole days or whole months, the specific calculation states whether the value is truncated downwards or raised to the next whole unit.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency on Parent Versions

Two write operations in this system are **additive** — repeating them with different arguments is legitimate product behaviour, so a client cannot discover from the result alone that it was reasoning about a stale parent. Those two are protected by an explicit optimistic-concurrency check on the parent's `version`.

A `POST /sponsorship-deals/{id}/deliverables` request must carry `expected_deal_version` as a mandatory body field, holding the `version` of that `SponsorshipDeal` as the client last read it. If the value does not equal the deal's current `version` — because another writer has placed a deliverable, activated the deal or otherwise advanced it since the client's read — the placement is rejected with `ERR_DEAL_VERSION_STALE` and HTTP 412, and no assignment is created. This is the only defence against a client that believes it is filling the deal's second of three slots when the second slot is already gone.

A `POST /roster-moves` request must carry `expected_organization_version` as a mandatory body field, holding the `version` of the filing `Organization` as the client last read it. If the value does not equal the organization's current `version`, the filing is rejected with `ERR_ORGANIZATION_VERSION_STALE` and HTTP 412, and no move is created. Filing a second pending move for a different player is otherwise perfectly legal, so the version check is what tells a general manager that the `committed_salary_cents` and `active_roster_count` figures the filing was based on have moved on.

No other endpoint takes a version field. Neither check consults its own resource's `version`; both consult the named parent — the deal in the first case, the organization in the second.

### 2.9. Cascades, Conserved Quantities and Deliberate Asymmetries

Several operations in this system mutate more than one resource, and the secondary mutations are as much part of the contract as the primary one. §3 spells each cascade out where it belongs, but three global rules apply to all of them. Every entity touched by a cascade has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented. Cascades run to completion inside the request that triggered them, so the response body and any immediately following read both reflect the finished state — consistency is immediate, never eventual. And a cascade that would violate a rule of its own does not silently skip: where a compensating action cannot restore a quantity, its precondition refuses the whole operation rather than performing part of it.

Two quantities are **conserved** and must hold exactly after every transition. First, an organization's `annual_salary_budget_cents` always equals its `committed_salary_cents` plus its `uncommitted_salary_cents`. Because commitments are always moved by a contract's frozen `provisional_cap_charge_cents` and never by a recomputed figure, executing a release or transfer and then reversing it returns `committed_salary_cents` to exactly its pre-execution value. Second, a prize distribution's `gross_prize_cents` always equals the sum of its `association_levy_cents`, its `org_share_cents`, its `allocated_cents` and its `unallocated_cents`; voiding the distribution restores that identity to its pre-calculation shape.

Not everything is reversible, and the asymmetries are intentional. Reversing an executed roster move restores the contract, the player and the salary commitment, but the practice blocks that were cancelled by the cascade stay cancelled and the deliverable assignments that were voided stay voided — only the salary quantity is conserved. A contract's obligation dimension escalates but never de-escalates: a burnout score that falls back below its threshold does not return a warned or breached contract to compliance. And a sponsorship deal's fulfilment does bubble both ways: it is reached when its approved deliverable count reaches its required count and it reverts when a void drops that count below the requirement.

### 2.10. Nested Paths and Reverse-Reachability Reads

Most collections in this API are addressed at the top level; nesting is used for two purposes only. The first is **nested creation**, where the parent is identified by the path and the child inherits the key: opening a team house names its organization in the URL and the created facility's `organization_id` is copied from that path segment rather than from the body, and placing a deliverable names its sponsorship deal in the URL so the created assignment's `sponsorship_deal_id` comes from the path. The second is **reverse reachability**, where a nested read lists the children that point back at a parent — an organization's roster, team houses and sponsorship deals; a league's organizations; a deal's deliverables; a distribution's allocations; and the prize rows paid against one contract.

Nested paths are validated strictly left to right, as band 3 of §2.6 requires. A request naming an organization that does not exist and a team house that does not exist reports the organization failure, because the organization segment stands to the left. This holds even when the right-hand segment's failure would carry a different status code.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The League Charter and the Organizations Competing Under It

Everything in this system happens inside a **league**, and the league record is where the sport's rulebook is expressed as numbers. A league administrator registers one with a `name` of between 1 and 120 characters and six calendar instants: `season_start_at` and `season_end_at`, where the closing instant must be strictly later than the opening one; `roster_lock_at`, the instant from which roster-changing operations freeze; and `transfer_window_open_at` with `transfer_window_close_at`, where the closing instant must again be strictly later than the opening one. The remaining charter terms are optional at registration and fall back to league-standard defaults: a `minimum_salary_cents` floor of 3,600,000 cents (thirty-six thousand dollars a year) which may be any value of zero or more; a `min_roster_size` of 1 and a `max_roster_size` of 7, both of which must be zero or more and where the maximum must be greater than or equal to the minimum; an `offer_response_window_hours` of 72 (three days), which must be at least 1; and a `max_negotiation_rounds` of 3, which must also be at least 1.

Player-association terms sit on the same record. The association takes a levy of `player_association_levy_bp`, defaulting to 250 basis points, off every gross prize before anyone else is paid; it imposes a prize-split floor of `association_min_prize_split_bp`, defaulting to 4000 basis points; and it caps contracted streaming at `association_max_streaming_hours_per_week`, defaulting to 20 hours. The levy and the split floor must each lie between 0 and 10000; the streaming ceiling must be zero or more. Statutory workload protections are also charter-level: `minor_max_weekly_minutes` defaults to 1200 minutes (twenty hours a week) and `adult_max_weekly_minutes` to 2700 minutes (forty-five hours), both of which must be zero or more; `education_reserved_minutes` defaults to 600 minutes (ten hours a week) held back for schooling; and `minor_age_threshold_years` defaults to 18 and must lie between 1 and 25. Finally, `free_agency_cooldown_days` defaults to 14 days and must be zero or more, and `tryout_required_before_offer` defaults to false.

Reading a league returns one derived flag alongside the stored terms. `roster_lock_active` is true exactly when the current instant has reached `roster_lock_at`, and false while the current instant is still earlier than that. It is never stored as a client-settable value; it is evaluated fresh on every read and at the start of every operation that a league window governs. Alongside it the same window arithmetic decides whether the transfer window is open — true when the current instant is at or after `transfer_window_open_at` and strictly before `transfer_window_close_at` — whether a given offer has expired, which is true once the current instant has reached that offer's `expires_at`, and whether a free-agency cooldown is biting, which requires all three of: the player carries a non-null `free_agency_window_ends_at`, the current instant is strictly earlier than it, and the organization in question is the one recorded in the player's `last_release_organization_id`. Which league governs a given operation is found by walking the graph: an organization-scoped write uses the organization's own `league_id`, an offer uses the league of the organization named on the offer, and a contract uses the league of the organization named on the contract.

A league administrator may later adjust the calendar and the terms, supplying any subset of `roster_lock_at`, `transfer_window_open_at`, `transfer_window_close_at`, `minimum_salary_cents`, `min_roster_size`, `max_roster_size`, `offer_response_window_hours`, `max_negotiation_rounds`, `minor_max_weekly_minutes`, `adult_max_weekly_minutes`, `education_reserved_minutes`, `association_min_prize_split_bp`, `association_max_streaming_hours_per_week` and `tryout_required_before_offer`. The derived lock flag is recomputed on the next evaluation and the league's `version` advances. Leagues may be listed page by page, and the organizations competing in one league are reachable as a nested read.

Organizations are also registered by the league office, each naming its `league_id`, a `name` of 1 to 120 characters, a `tax_jurisdiction_code` of 2 to 8 uppercase characters, an `annual_salary_budget_cents` of zero or more, and optionally an `org_prize_share_bp` which defaults to 3500 basis points and must lie between 0 and 10000. A newly registered organization starts with `committed_salary_cents` at 0 and `uncommitted_salary_cents` equal to its whole `annual_salary_budget_cents`. Four further counters begin at 0: `active_roster_count`, `prize_payable_cents`, `wellness_flag_count`, and the committed figure already mentioned. Organizations may be listed with an optional `league_id` filter, and the league office may later adjust an organization's `annual_salary_budget_cents` or `org_prize_share_bp`, after which the uncommitted figure is recomputed and the organization's `version` advances.

Those counters are not free-form. `committed_salary_cents` is the sum of `provisional_cap_charge_cents` across every one of the organization's player contracts whose `contract_status` is `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`; because only frozen integers are summed, no rounding takes place. `uncommitted_salary_cents` is `annual_salary_budget_cents` less `committed_salary_cents`. `active_roster_count` is the count of the organization's contracts whose `contract_status` is exactly `ACTIVE`, so a suspended contract still consumes budget while no longer counting as a roster seat. `prize_payable_cents` is the sum of `org_share_cents` over that organization's prize distributions whose `distribution_status` is `CALCULATED` or `APPROVED`. `wellness_flag_count` counts, monotonically and without ever decreasing, the number of times any of the organization's contracts has escalated its obligation dimension into `BREACH`. All four are recomputed whenever a contract enters or leaves the `ACTIVE`-or-`SUSPENDED` set — at offer acceptance, at suspension, at roster-move execution and at roster-move reversal.

A dedicated computed read gives the league office, the general manager and finance a single view of an organization's cap position: it returns `annual_salary_budget_cents`, `committed_salary_cents`, `uncommitted_salary_cents`, `active_roster_count`, `prize_payable_cents` and `wellness_flag_count` as stored, plus the two cap-charge variants side by side. `provisional_committed_cents` is the sum of `provisional_cap_charge_cents` over the organization's `ACTIVE` and `SUSPENDED` contracts — the same quantity the committed figure tracks — while `final_committed_cents` is the sum of `final_cap_charge_cents` over that same set of contracts. For an organization whose signings all took effect at the very start of the season the two are equal; for one that signed mid-season the final figure is the larger, and the gap is exactly the budget headroom that league approval of a signing will refuse to grant.

### 3.2. Scouting, Age Derivation and the Protection of Minors

New talent enters the system through a scout, who registers a prospect with a `full_name` of 1 to 120 characters, a `birth_date` written as `YYYY-MM-DD`, and a `region_code` of 2 to 8 uppercase characters naming the competitive region the player was found in. Four further attributes are optional at registration: `association_member`, which defaults to false and declares whether player-association terms bind this player's contracts; `guardian_name` and `guardian_email`, which default to null and may be recorded at scouting time or later; `guardian_weekly_cap_minutes`, which defaults to null and, when present, must be zero or more; and `education_enrollment_status`, which defaults to `NOT_ENROLLED` and may otherwise be `ENROLLED_PART_TIME` or `ENROLLED_FULL_TIME`. The registering scout's identifier is stamped into `scouted_by_user_id`. A newly registered player's `player_status` is `PROSPECT`, and `current_organization_id`, `free_agency_window_ends_at` and `last_release_organization_id` all start null.

Age is derived, not declared. `age_years` is the number of whole years between the player's `birth_date` and the current instant, counting an anniversary falling exactly on the current date as reached — so a player whose eighteenth birthday is today has `age_years` of 18. `is_minor` is true exactly when `age_years` is strictly less than the governing league's `minor_age_threshold_years`, which is 18 by default; the league used for that comparison is the league of the organization named in `current_organization_id` when the player has one, and otherwise the league of the organization named in the request being evaluated. Both figures, and the consent status derived alongside them, are re-evaluated on every read or write that touches a player, an offer or a contract, which means a prospect scouted at 17 silently becomes an adult when the logical clock is moved past their birthday.

`guardian_consent_status` follows directly from that derivation: it is `NOT_REQUIRED` for a player who is not a minor, `REQUIRED` for a minor with no consent on file, and `GRANTED` once a guardian has recorded consent. Only a guardian representative may record it, supplying `guardian_name` and `guardian_email` as mandatory values and optionally `guardian_weekly_cap_minutes` to pin a stricter workload ceiling than the league's; the operation sets `guardian_consent_status` to `GRANTED` and advances the player's `version`. Consent is exactly what unlocks three otherwise-forbidden things for a minor: accepting a contract offer on that player's behalf, listing that player as a participant on a practice block, and placing a sponsorship deliverable on that player's contract. In all three, a minor whose `guardian_consent_status` is not `GRANTED` is refused with `ERR_GUARDIAN_CONSENT_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422).

Both scouts and guardian representatives may amend a player profile, supplying any subset of `full_name`, `region_code`, `education_enrollment_status` and `guardian_weekly_cap_minutes`. Amendments are forward-looking only: later weekly-cap arithmetic uses the new enrolment status and the new guardian ceiling, while blocks already on the calendar are not re-validated. Every amendment advances the player's `version`. Players may be listed with any combination of the filters `player_status`, `region_code`, `current_organization_id` and `last_release_organization_id`, and an organization's roster read returns the players whose `current_organization_id` or `last_release_organization_id` names that organization, optionally narrowed by `player_status`.

A player's lifecycle is a cycle rather than a funnel with an end. The five states are `PROSPECT`, `TRYOUT_INVITED`, `TRYOUT_COMPLETED`, `SIGNED` and `FREE_AGENT`, and none of them is terminal. A player in `PROSPECT` or `FREE_AGENT` who is listed as a participant on a newly booked block whose `block_type` is `TRYOUT` moves to `TRYOUT_INVITED`, provided the workload arithmetic of §3.5 admits them. When that tryout block is later marked as held, every participant sitting in `TRYOUT_INVITED` advances to `TRYOUT_COMPLETED`. Acceptance of an offer moves a player from `TRYOUT_COMPLETED`, from `PROSPECT` (which is only reachable when the league's `tryout_required_before_offer` is false, since otherwise the offer's acceptance would have been refused) or from `FREE_AGENT` to `SIGNED`. Execution of a release or an outgoing transfer moves a `SIGNED` player to `FREE_AGENT`; reversing that execution moves them back from `FREE_AGENT` to `SIGNED`. A released player therefore re-enters the scouting funnel and can be invited to a tryout again, which is what closes the `SIGNED` → `FREE_AGENT` → `TRYOUT_INVITED` → `SIGNED` loop.

### 3.3. Negotiating Terms: Offers, Counters, Revisions and Expiry

A general manager opens a negotiation by sending an offer, naming the `organization_id` making the offer and the `player_id` receiving it, and putting seven commercial terms on the table: an annual `base_salary_cents` of zero or more; a `term_months` between 1 and 60, where a month is always 30 days for date arithmetic; a `prize_split_player_pct_bp` between 0 and 10000 expressing the player's weight in future prize splits; a `streaming_hours_per_week` between 0 and 80; a `tax_residency_code` of 2 to 8 uppercase characters, which will later decide the player's withholding rate; an optional `signing_bonus_cents` of zero or more defaulting to 0; and an optional `buyout_multiplier_bp` between 0 and 30000 defaulting to 5000 basis points. Optionally, the general manager may name a `supersedes_offer_id` — an earlier offer to the same player from the same organization, which is thereby retired. A freshly created offer has `offer_status` of `SENT`, `round_number` of 1, `sent_at` stamped from the logical clock, and `expires_at` set to `sent_at` plus the league's `offer_response_window_hours` hours. All four `counter_base_salary_cents`, `counter_prize_split_player_pct_bp`, `counter_streaming_hours_per_week` and `counter_note` start null, as does `decline_note`.

An offer's six states are `SENT`, `COUNTERED`, `ACCEPTED`, `DECLINED`, `EXPIRED` and `SUPERSEDED`, of which the last four are terminal — an offer in any of them accepts no further action, and an attempt to act on one is refused with `ERR_OFFER_NOT_OPEN` (HTTP 409). Concretely: countering and accepting require `SENT`, revising requires `COUNTERED`, and declining accepts either `SENT` or `COUNTERED`. Expiry, however, is **lazy** and takes precedence. At the start of any operation that reads or mutates an offer — including a single-offer read and every offer inside a list page — an offer sitting in `SENT` or `COUNTERED` whose `expires_at` the current instant has reached is first moved to `EXPIRED`. A read then simply returns the expired offer; a mutation is refused with `ERR_OFFER_EXPIRED` (HTTP 409), and that refusal is reported even where the offer's status would independently have failed the action's status requirement.

Countering belongs to the player's agent, who may act only on offers naming their own player. The counter carries `counter_base_salary_cents`, `counter_prize_split_player_pct_bp` and `counter_streaming_hours_per_week` as mandatory values plus an optional `counter_note`, and it moves the offer `SENT` → `COUNTERED` while recording all four fields. Countering does not itself change `round_number`, but it is only permitted while `round_number` is strictly less than the league's `max_negotiation_rounds`; once the round counter has reached that ceiling the counter is refused with `ERR_NEGOTIATION_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED` (HTTP 409). With the default ceiling of three rounds, an agent therefore gets three bites at the terms.

Answering a counter belongs back to the general manager, who supplies fresh `base_salary_cents`, `prize_split_player_pct_bp` and `streaming_hours_per_week` values. That revision moves the offer `COUNTERED` → `SENT`, increases `round_number` by exactly 1, overwrites the three headline terms with the revised ones, clears all four counter fields back to null, re-stamps `sent_at` to the current instant and recomputes `expires_at` from that new instant plus the league's response window — so each round of haggling restarts the clock rather than eating into a single fixed deadline. Revising is subject to the same round ceiling as countering, and a revision that would carry `round_number` past `max_negotiation_rounds` is likewise refused with `ERR_NEGOTIATION_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`.

Player-association terms are enforced against whichever numbers are being put on the table, and the three checks do not all apply at the same moments. The salary floor is universal: on the initial offer, on a counter, on a revision and at acceptance, the base salary in question — the countered value when an agent is countering, the revised value when a manager is revising, otherwise the offer's own `base_salary_cents` — must be greater than or equal to the league's `minimum_salary_cents`, failing which the request is refused with `ERR_BELOW_MINIMUM_SALARY` (HTTP 422). The two association-membership terms bind only players whose `association_member` is true, and they are checked on the initial offer, on a revision and at acceptance — but deliberately **not** on an agent's counter, since an agent countering below their own association's floor is the association's problem rather than the league's. For such a player, `prize_split_player_pct_bp` must be greater than or equal to the league's `association_min_prize_split_bp` or the request is refused with `ERR_PRIZE_SPLIT_BELOW_ASSOCIATION_MIN` (HTTP 422), and `streaming_hours_per_week` must be less than or equal to the league's `association_max_streaming_hours_per_week` or the request is refused with `ERR_STREAMING_HOURS_EXCEED_ASSOCIATION_MAX` (HTTP 422). For a player who is not an association member neither of the two applies at all.

An agent who wants nothing to do with the terms declines instead, optionally recording a `decline_note`; the offer moves from `SENT` or from `COUNTERED` to `DECLINED` and nothing else in the system changes. Retirement of an offer can also happen without the agent's involvement, in two ways: naming it as the `supersedes_offer_id` of a new offer moves it from `SENT` or `COUNTERED` to `SUPERSEDED`, and so does the acceptance of any sibling offer for the same player. Offers may be listed with any combination of the filters `organization_id`, `player_id`, `supersedes_offer_id` and `offer_status`, which together make the whole negotiation chain — including its superseded links — reconstructible.

### 3.4. Acceptance: Turning an Offer into a Contract

Acceptance is the single densest operation in this system, and it is the agent's to perform. The payload carries at most one field, `effective_at`, the instant the resulting employment begins; when the payload omits it, the contract's `effective_at` defaults to the current instant. Beyond the offer's own openness and the agent's ownership of the player, acceptance runs seven substantive gates. The player must have no other `PlayerContract` whose `contract_status` is `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_PLAYER_ALREADY_SIGNED` (HTTP 409). A minor's guardian consent must be `GRANTED`, or `ERR_GUARDIAN_CONSENT_REQUIRED` follows. The league's roster lock must not be active — once the current instant has reached `roster_lock_at`, no offer may be accepted at all and the refusal is `ERR_ROSTER_LOCK_ACTIVE` (HTTP 422). Where the player was released by this very organization and is still inside the cooldown, the refusal is `ERR_FREE_AGENCY_COOLDOWN` (HTTP 422); an offer from any *other* organization is unaffected. The supplied or defaulted `effective_at` must fall at or after the league's `season_start_at` and strictly before its `season_end_at`, or `ERR_EFFECTIVE_AT_OUTSIDE_SEASON` (HTTP 422) is returned. When the league's `tryout_required_before_offer` is true the player's `player_status` must be `TRYOUT_COMPLETED` at acceptance time, or `ERR_TRYOUT_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422) is returned; when it is false a `PROSPECT` or `FREE_AGENT` player is equally acceptable. The organization's `active_roster_count` must be strictly less than the league's `max_roster_size`, or `ERR_ROSTER_SIZE_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422) is returned. Finally, the money must be there, as described two paragraphs below.

The salary charge a signing places against a budget is computed in one of **two variants**, and the difference between them is the mechanism by which the league office retains a veto over mid-season spending. Both start from the contract's own frozen terms. The *prorated remaining term* variant first counts `remaining_season_days` as the number of whole days from the contract's `effective_at` to the league's `season_end_at`, discarding any fractional day; it then multiplies `base_salary_cents` by that day count, divides by the 365-day season, rounds the result half up to a whole cent, and finally adds `signing_bonus_cents` in full — the bonus is never prorated. The *full-season-equivalent* variant simply adds `base_salary_cents` and `signing_bonus_cents` together, charging the whole annual salary no matter when in the season the ink dried. For any contract whose `effective_at` is later than the season's start the prorated figure is the smaller of the two, which is precisely why a signing that comfortably clears the acceptance gate can still be refused later by league approval. Terminated contracts are excluded from both variants.

Acceptance uses the prorated variant, and uses it twice: as a gate and as a bookkeeping entry. The prorated charge for the contract about to be created must be less than or equal to the organization's current `uncommitted_salary_cents`, and if it is not, acceptance is refused with `ERR_SALARY_CAP_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). If it fits, the figure is frozen onto the new contract as `provisional_cap_charge_cents` and never recomputed thereafter; it is the only quantity by which `committed_salary_cents` ever moves, which is what makes later execution and reversal exactly reversible. The companion `final_cap_charge_cents` is not frozen at all — it is recomputed from the full-season variant every time it is read.

When every gate clears, a **four-link cascade** runs inside the request. First, the offer's `offer_status` becomes `ACCEPTED` and a `PlayerContract` is created with `contract_status` of `ACTIVE` and `obligation_status` of `COMPLIANT`, its `offer_id`, `player_id` and `organization_id` taken from the offer, its `base_salary_cents`, `term_months`, `signing_bonus_cents`, `prize_split_player_pct_bp`, `streaming_hours_per_week`, `buyout_multiplier_bp` and `tax_residency_code` copied from the offer and frozen against later league or offer changes, its `ends_at` set to `effective_at` plus `term_months` times 30 days, its `provisional_cap_charge_cents` frozen as described, its `season_practice_minutes`, `last_self_reported_load`, `reported_streaming_minutes_7d` and `burnout_score` all at 0, and its `wellness_hold_until`, `suspension_reason` at null. The accepting caller is recorded in `signed_by_user_id`. Second, because that contract now exists, the player's `player_status` becomes `SIGNED`, their `current_organization_id` becomes the offering organization, and both `free_agency_window_ends_at` and `last_release_organization_id` are cleared to null. Third, because the player is now signed under this contract, the organization's `committed_salary_cents` increases by the contract's `provisional_cap_charge_cents`, its `uncommitted_salary_cents` is recomputed as budget less commitment, and its `active_roster_count` increases by 1. Fourth, because the player is now signed, every *other* offer to the same `player_id` sitting in `SENT` or `COUNTERED` is moved to `SUPERSEDED`. The response to a successful acceptance is the created contract, with HTTP 201.

Contracts themselves are read-only to clients apart from the three operations described in §3.6 and the roster-move machinery of §3.9. A single contract read returns both status dimensions and every frozen money field; the contract list accepts the filters `organization_id`, `player_id`, `offer_id`, `contract_status` and `obligation_status`, the last two of which make it easy to sweep for suspended employment or breached obligations across a whole organization.

### 3.5. Team Houses, Practice Scheduling and Weekly Workload Limits

Facilities belong to an organization and are opened by its operations manager as a nested creation, so the new facility's `organization_id` comes from the path rather than the body. A team house is described by a `name` of 1 to 120 characters, a `timezone` carrying an IANA timezone label which is metadata only — every instant in this API remains UTC — an optional `turnaround_buffer_minutes` between 0 and 720 defaulting to 60, and an optional `concurrent_seat_capacity` of at least 1 defaulting to 10. The buffer is the mandatory changeover time padded around every booking in that room; the seat capacity is the largest number of participants a single booking there may hold. An operations manager may later adjust the facility's `name`, `turnaround_buffer_minutes` or `concurrent_seat_capacity`; later bookings are judged against the new values while bookings already on the calendar are never re-validated, and the facility's `version` advances. An organization's facilities are reachable as a nested read, and a single facility read returns its buffer and capacity.

Booking is the coaching staff's job. A block names the `organization_id` running it and the `team_house_id` it occupies, declares a `block_type` of `SCRIM`, `REVIEW`, `INDIVIDUAL` or `TRYOUT`, spans the half-open interval from `starts_at` inclusive to `ends_at` exclusive with the end strictly after the start, and lists its attendees in `participant_player_ids` — an array of player ids that must be non-empty, must contain no duplicates, and every entry of which must resolve to a real player. An optional `coach_note` may be supplied at booking and again at completion. On insertion the server derives `duration_minutes` as the number of whole minutes between `starts_at` and `ends_at`, stamps the booking coach's identifier into `coach_user_id`, sets `block_status` to `SCHEDULED` and leaves `cancel_reason` null. Blocks may be listed with any combination of `organization_id`, `team_house_id`, `block_type`, `block_status`, and the interval filters `starts_from` and `starts_to`, which bound the block's `starts_at`.

Before a booking is admitted, each attendee is examined against their employment. The rule is deliberately permissive at the edges: a participant who owns no `PlayerContract` at all — a prospect or a free agent invited in to try out — is admitted without further contract checks, and that is exactly what makes tryout blocks for unsigned players possible. A participant who owns at least one contract in `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED` or `TERMINATED` must own an `ACTIVE` one; if their only employment is suspended or terminated, the booking is refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409). A minor attendee whose `guardian_consent_status` is not `GRANTED` causes `ERR_GUARDIAN_CONSENT_REQUIRED`. An attendee with an `ACTIVE` contract whose `wellness_hold_until` the current instant has not yet reached causes `ERR_WELLNESS_HOLD_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409) — the hold lifts on the boundary instant itself.

The **weekly workload cap** is where minor protection, education and streaming obligations meet, and it is evaluated per attendee. A week runs from the most recent Monday at midnight UTC at or before the instant being examined, and spans the following seven days as a half-open window that includes its Monday and excludes the next. A block is attributed in its entirety to the week that contains its `starts_at`, even if it runs past midnight on Sunday. An attendee's **scheduled minutes** for that week are the sum of `duration_minutes` over every block, in any team house of any organization, whose `block_status` is `SCHEDULED` or `HELD` and whose `starts_at` falls inside that week window and which lists that attendee — cancelled blocks are excluded, and the candidate block being booked is included in the sum. A player's cap is global, not per organization. Their **streaming minutes** are added on top: for a minor who owns a contract in `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, the contract's `streaming_hours_per_week` multiplied by 60; for a player who is not a minor, or for one who owns no such contract, zero. The sum of the two is the attendee's committed minutes.

The cap itself is computed as follows. For a player who is not a minor it is simply the league's `adult_max_weekly_minutes` — 2,700 minutes, or forty-five hours, by default. For a minor it is the smaller of the league's `minor_max_weekly_minutes` and the player's `guardian_weekly_cap_minutes`, treating a null guardian cap as if it equalled the league ceiling, and then, only when the minor's `education_enrollment_status` is exactly `ENROLLED_FULL_TIME`, reduced by the league's `education_reserved_minutes`. A part-time enrolment reserves nothing. The comparison is strictly greater-than, so committed minutes exactly equal to the cap are admitted and only one minute more is a violation. A violation is normally refused with `ERR_WEEKLY_HOUR_CAP_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). The education reservation gets its own, narrower error: when the booking fails the cap *with* the reservation applied but the identical request would have passed had `education_reserved_minutes` been zero, the reservation was decisive and the refusal is `ERR_EDUCATION_HOURS_RESERVED` (HTTP 422) instead; when the request would have failed even with no reservation at all, the plain `ERR_WEEKLY_HOUR_CAP_EXCEEDED` is reported.

Two overlap checks then guard rooms and people, and they use deliberately different rules. **Room conflict** pads the candidate — and only the candidate — by the facility's `turnaround_buffer_minutes` at each end, then compares that padded interval against the raw, unpadded intervals of every `SCHEDULED` or `HELD` block in the same team house. An overlap exists when the padded interval starts strictly before an existing block ends and that existing block starts strictly before the padded interval ends; where it does, the booking is refused with `ERR_FACILITY_SLOT_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409). Padding only the candidate is intentional — padding both sides would double-count the changeover — so a block that starts exactly `turnaround_buffer_minutes` after another block in that room ends is allowed, while one minute earlier conflicts. Cancelled blocks free their slot entirely. **Participant conflict** applies no padding whatsoever, because the changeover belongs to the room and not to the person: an attendee may not already appear on a `SCHEDULED` or `HELD` block, in any house of any organization, whose raw interval overlaps the candidate's raw interval on the same strictly-before comparison, and if one does the booking is refused with `ERR_PLAYER_DOUBLE_BOOKED` (HTTP 409). Back-to-back blocks where one starts exactly as the other ends are therefore fine for a player even when the same pair would violate the room's buffer. Finally, the number of entries in `participant_player_ids` must not exceed the facility's `concurrent_seat_capacity`; a count exactly equal to capacity is allowed, and anything larger is refused with `ERR_FACILITY_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422).

Booking a `TRYOUT` block does double duty as a scouting action: every attendee whose `player_status` is `PROSPECT` or `FREE_AGENT` moves to `TRYOUT_INVITED` as the block is created. Completing a block is a separate coaching action, permitted only while `block_status` is `SCHEDULED` — otherwise `ERR_BLOCK_NOT_SCHEDULED` (HTTP 409) — and only once the current instant has reached the block's `ends_at`, the boundary instant being sufficient; an early attempt is refused with `ERR_BLOCK_NOT_FINISHED` (HTTP 409). Completion moves `block_status` `SCHEDULED` → `HELD`, records any `coach_note`, and then runs a four-link cascade: for every attendee holding an `ACTIVE` contract, that contract's `season_practice_minutes` increases by the block's `duration_minutes`; because the season total and the trailing seven-day window have both changed, that contract's `burnout_score` is recomputed by the rolling method of §3.6; because the score may have crossed a threshold, the contract's `obligation_status` escalates to `WARNED` at 70 or to `BREACH` at 85; and because the obligation dimension left `COMPLIANT`, every block still `SCHEDULED` whose `starts_at` is strictly later than the current instant and which lists that player is cancelled with `cancel_reason` of `WELLNESS_HOLD`. On a `TRYOUT` block, completion additionally advances every attendee sitting in `TRYOUT_INVITED` to `TRYOUT_COMPLETED`.

Cancellation is the coach's escape hatch and is allowed at any time while the block is `SCHEDULED`, including before it has started — the completion timing rule does not restrict it. It moves `block_status` `SCHEDULED` → `CANCELLED`, and both `HELD` and `CANCELLED` are terminal for a block — an attempt to cancel a block that is already `HELD` or `CANCELLED` is refused with `ERR_BLOCK_NOT_SCHEDULED`, exactly as an attempt to complete one is. A manual cancellation must supply `cancel_reason` equal to `COACH_CANCELLED`, and any other value is a malformed request; an optional `coach_note` may accompany it. The other two reasons, `WELLNESS_HOLD` and `CONTRACT_SUSPENDED`, are written only by the cascades described in this section and in §3.6 and are never accepted from a client. Once a block is cancelled its minutes leave every attendee's weekly arithmetic and its interval stops occupying the room, so cancelling and rebooking is a legitimate way to resolve a conflict.

### 3.6. Wellness Monitoring, Burnout Escalation and Contract Suspension

A player contract carries **two independent status dimensions** on the same record. `contract_status` is the employment dimension and runs across `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED` and `TERMINATED`. `obligation_status` is the wellness dimension and runs across `COMPLIANT`, `WARNED` and `BREACH`. Neither drives the other: suspending a contract leaves its obligation dimension exactly where it was, and a burnout escalation leaves employment untouched. The wellness dimension is escalation-only — `COMPLIANT` may move to `WARNED` or straight to `BREACH`, and `WARNED` may move to `BREACH`, but nothing ever moves back down, so `BREACH` is terminal for that dimension. Employment's `TERMINATED` is left only by the compensating reversal of §3.9.

Performance-support staff drive the wellness dimension by filing a report against an `ACTIVE` contract — a `SUSPENDED` or `TERMINATED` contract takes no new reports and yields `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`. The report carries `last_self_reported_load`, a mandatory integer from 0 to 10, and optionally `reported_streaming_minutes_7d`, an integer of zero or more describing the streaming minutes the player actually delivered over the trailing seven days. Both are stored on the contract and both feed the score.

The **burnout score** is a weighted 0-to-100 index with two variants that share one scoring function. The scoring function takes a practice-minutes figure and combines three normalised components: the practice component contributes half the weight and is the practice-minutes figure divided by the player's weekly cap minutes as computed in §3.5, capped at 1 so that overshooting the cap cannot push the component above its ceiling; the self-reported load component contributes three tenths of the weight and is `last_self_reported_load` divided by 10; and the streaming component contributes one fifth of the weight and is `reported_streaming_minutes_7d` divided by the contract's committed streaming minutes, which are `streaming_hours_per_week` multiplied by 60, again capped at 1, and taken as zero outright whenever the committed streaming minutes are zero. The three weighted components are summed at full precision, multiplied by 100, and only then rounded half up exactly once to a whole integer.

The two variants differ solely in the practice-minutes figure fed to that function. The **rolling seven-day** variant, which is the one persisted as `burnout_score`, sums `duration_minutes` over the player's blocks whose `block_status` is `HELD` and whose `starts_at` falls in the window that begins strictly after seven days before the current instant and ends at the current instant inclusive; blocks that are merely `SCHEDULED`, and blocks that were cancelled, never feed the score. Because that window is anchored to the clock, the score decays purely by time passing. The **season-cumulative** variant instead divides the contract's `season_practice_minutes` by the number of elapsed weeks and rounds that quotient half up before scoring it; elapsed weeks are the whole days from the contract's `effective_at` to the current instant, divided by seven and raised to the next whole week, with a floor of one week so that a contract signed today is never divided by zero. Old workload therefore keeps weighing on the cumulative figure long after the rolling window has emptied, and for any clock more than seven days past the last completed block the two variants disagree.

Escalation compares the rounded rolling score against two thresholds using greater-than-or-equal. A contract sitting in `COMPLIANT` whose refreshed score is at least 70 but strictly below 85 moves to `WARNED`. A contract in `COMPLIANT` or in `WARNED` whose refreshed score is at least 85 moves to `BREACH`. The consequences of the two escalations are **not** symmetric, and the difference is exactly two side effects. Both escalations cancel every block that is still `SCHEDULED` and whose `starts_at` is strictly later than the current instant and which lists that player, writing `cancel_reason` of `WELLNESS_HOLD` on each. Only entry into `BREACH` additionally stamps `wellness_hold_until` at the current instant plus seven days and increases the organization's `wellness_flag_count` by 1; a `WARNED` escalation stamps neither. Since the hold blocks new bookings for that player until the current instant reaches it, a breach effectively removes the player from the practice calendar for a week. Both a wellness report and a practice-block completion can trigger any of these escalations, because both refresh the rolling score.

Suspension belongs to the league office and applies only to a contract whose `contract_status` is `ACTIVE`; anything else yields `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`. The league administrator supplies a mandatory `suspension_reason`, which is recorded, and the contract moves `ACTIVE` → `SUSPENDED`. Three consequences follow. The obligation dimension is left untouched, as the independence rule requires. The organization's `active_roster_count` decreases by 1, but its `committed_salary_cents` is **not** released — a suspended contract still consumes cap space, which is what stops suspension from being used as a budget trick. And every block that is still `SCHEDULED` and whose `starts_at` is strictly later than the current instant and which lists the suspended contract's player is cancelled with `cancel_reason` of `CONTRACT_SUSPENDED`.

A computed obligation summary gathers the whole picture for one contract without persisting anything. It returns the contract's current `obligation_status`, its rolling `burnout_score` and the freshly-derived `season_burnout_score`, the `scheduled_minutes_this_week` and `streaming_minutes_this_week` that the weekly arithmetic charges for that player together with the resulting `cap_minutes`, the frozen `provisional_cap_charge_cents` next to the recomputed `final_cap_charge_cents`, and the current `wellness_hold_until`. Note that the streaming figure reported here is the one the cap arithmetic actually charged, which is zero for a player who is not a minor.

### 3.7. Sponsorship Deals and the Deliverables Placed Across a Roster

The commercial team drafts a sponsorship deal for its own organization, naming the `organization_id` that signed the sponsor, the `sponsor_name` as a 1-to-120-character brand name, an `exclusivity_category` drawn from `ENERGY_DRINK`, `PERIPHERALS`, `APPAREL`, `BETTING` and `CRYPTO`, a `total_fee_cents` of zero or more, a `deliverable_count_required` of at least 1 stating how many pieces of content the organization must place across its roster, and a coverage window from `starts_at` to `ends_at` with the end strictly after the start. A drafted deal has `deal_status` of `DRAFT`, `assigned_count` and `approved_count` both 0, and one derived flag: `minor_permitted` is false when the `exclusivity_category` is `ENERGY_DRINK`, `BETTING` or `CRYPTO`, and true for `PERIPHERALS` and `APPAREL`. The flag is advisory reporting; the hard prohibition below is narrower than the flag. Deals may be listed with any combination of `organization_id`, `deal_status` and `exclusivity_category`, and an organization's deals are also reachable as a nested read filterable by `deal_status`.

`DRAFT` deals are inert: they carry no obligations and they never conflict with anything. Activation, performed by the sponsorship manager of the owning organization, moves `deal_status` `DRAFT` → `ACTIVE` and advances the deal's `version`; a deal that is not `DRAFT` yields `ERR_DEAL_NOT_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409). Because activation is the moment a deal starts to matter, the **exclusivity check** is re-run at that point as well as at every placement. A conflict exists when some *other* sponsorship deal of the same organization is in `ACTIVE` or `FULFILLED`, carries exactly the same `exclusivity_category` compared case-sensitively, has a coverage window that overlaps this deal's window on the half-open comparison — that other deal starts strictly before this deal ends and this deal starts strictly before that other deal ends — and already carries a non-`VOIDED` deliverable on the very same player contract that is at issue. Where it does, the request is refused with `ERR_EXCLUSIVITY_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409). Two energy-drink sponsors may coexist in an organization's portfolio; what they may not do is both hold live content obligations on one player.

Placing a deliverable is a nested creation against an `ACTIVE` deal, and it is the one operation in this area that carries a concurrency token. The payload names the `contract_id` carrying the obligation, a `deliverable_type` of `STREAM_SEGMENT`, `SOCIAL_POST`, `APPEARANCE` or `VOD_FEATURE`, a `due_at` deadline, and the mandatory `expected_deal_version` described in §2.8. The target contract's `contract_status` must be `ACTIVE`, or the placement is refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`; its `obligation_status` must not be `BREACH`, since a breached contract accepts no new commercial work, and a breached one is refused with `ERR_OBLIGATION_BREACH_BLOCKS_ASSIGNMENT` (HTTP 422). A minor player's guardian consent must be `GRANTED`, failing which `ERR_GUARDIAN_CONSENT_REQUIRED` applies. Independently of consent, a deal whose `exclusivity_category` is `BETTING` or `CRYPTO` may never carry a deliverable for a minor player's contract at all, and such a placement is refused with `ERR_MINOR_CATEGORY_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 422) — no guardian can waive that one. The `due_at` must fall at or after the deal's `starts_at` and strictly before its `ends_at`, or `ERR_DELIVERABLE_DUE_OUTSIDE_DEAL` (HTTP 422) is returned. The deal's `assigned_count` must be strictly less than its `deliverable_count_required`, otherwise the quota is full and `ERR_DELIVERABLE_QUOTA_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422) is returned. And the exclusivity check described above runs for the target player.

A successful placement creates an assignment whose `sponsorship_deal_id` comes from the path, whose `assignment_status` is `ASSIGNED`, whose `rejection_count` is 0 and whose `submission_note` and `review_note` are null. Its `fee_share_cents` is frozen at creation as the deal's `total_fee_cents` divided by its `deliverable_count_required` with the fractional remainder discarded. The deal's `assigned_count` increases and the deal's own `version` advances — which, by design, invalidates any other client's `expected_deal_version` and forces it to re-read before placing its own deliverable. The deal's `assigned_count` counts assignments in `ASSIGNED`, `SUBMITTED`, `APPROVED` or `REJECTED` and excludes `VOIDED` ones, so voiding an assignment frees a quota slot for a replacement.

Content review is a cycle rather than a straight line. The player's agent submits produced content, optionally attaching a `submission_note`; submission is permitted from `ASSIGNED` and from `REJECTED`, requires the underlying contract to still be `ACTIVE`, and moves the assignment to `SUBMITTED`. An attempt to submit something already `SUBMITTED`, `APPROVED` or `VOIDED` is refused with `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_SUBMITTED` (HTTP 409), as is any review of an assignment that is not `SUBMITTED`. The sponsorship manager of the owning organization then reviews it, supplying a mandatory `decision` of either `APPROVE` or `REJECT` and an optional `review_note`. A rejection moves the assignment `SUBMITTED` → `REJECTED` and increases its `rejection_count` by 1; the agent may then resubmit, and the rejection tally is preserved across as many loops as the pair needs. An approval moves it `SUBMITTED` → `APPROVED` and increases the deal's `approved_count` by 1.

Fulfilment bubbles up from those child counts rather than being set directly. Once `approved_count` equals `deliverable_count_required`, the deal moves `ACTIVE` → `FULFILLED` in the same request as the final approval. The deal's earned fee follows the same arithmetic: it is the per-deliverable share multiplied by `approved_count` while the deal is still short of its quota, and on the final approval the residue discarded by the earlier truncation — the `total_fee_cents` less the per-deliverable share multiplied by `deliverable_count_required` — is paid out alongside, so a fulfilled deal has earned exactly its `total_fee_cents` and not a cent less. Fulfilment also bubbles back down: when a contract termination voids an approved deliverable and drops `approved_count` below `deliverable_count_required`, a `FULFILLED` deal reverts to `ACTIVE`. Voiding is only ever a cascade, never a client action: an assignment in `ASSIGNED`, `SUBMITTED`, `APPROVED` or `REJECTED` moves to the terminal `VOIDED` when its contract terminates, decreasing the deal's `assigned_count`, and additionally decreasing `approved_count` when the voided row had been `APPROVED`. Assignments may be listed with any combination of `sponsorship_deal_id`, `contract_id` and `assignment_status`, and one deal's deliverables are reachable as a nested read filterable by `assignment_status`.

### 3.8. Prize Money: the Waterfall, its Sign-off and its Undoing

Finance records a tournament result as a distribution, naming the `organization_id` that won it, the `league_id` whose association levy applies, a `tournament_name` of 1 to 120 characters, a `placement` of at least 1 where 1 is first place, a `gross_prize_cents` of zero or more, and the `tournament_end_at` instant the event finished. A new distribution has `distribution_status` of `DRAFT`, `unallocated_cents` equal to the whole `gross_prize_cents`, and `association_levy_cents`, `org_share_cents`, `allocated_cents`, `withholding_total_cents` and `escrow_total_cents` all at 0, with `calculated_by_user_id`, `approved_by_user_id` and `void_reason` all null. Distributions may be listed with any combination of `organization_id`, `league_id` and `distribution_status`.

Running the waterfall is finance's exclusive action and is permitted only from `DRAFT`; anything else yields `ERR_DISTRIBUTION_NOT_DRAFT` (HTTP 409). **The six steps below are normative and each consumes the output of the one above it.** Step one takes the association's cut: `association_levy_cents` is `gross_prize_cents` multiplied by the league's `player_association_levy_bp`, divided by 10000 and rounded half up; what remains after subtracting it is the distributable amount. Step two takes the house's cut: `org_share_cents` is that distributable amount multiplied by the organization's `org_prize_share_bp`, divided by 10000 and rounded half up; what remains after subtracting it is the player pool. Step three fixes the eligible population: exactly those player contracts of the distributing organization whose `contract_status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `effective_at` is at or before the distribution's `tournament_end_at`. Suspended contracts, terminated contracts and contracts signed after the event are all excluded, which changes the weight total and therefore every surviving row's share. Each eligible contract's weight is its `prize_split_player_pct_bp`, recorded on the row as `weight_bp`, and the weight total is the sum of those weights across the eligible set. If no contract is eligible there is nothing to divide and the calculation is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_CONTRACTS` (HTTP 422).

Step four divides the pool without losing a cent. Each row's floor share is the player pool multiplied by that row's weight, divided by the weight total, with the fraction discarded. The remainder is the player pool less the sum of all those floor shares, and the entire remainder is handed to exactly one row: the eligible contract with the largest floor share, with ties broken by the earlier `created_at` of the contract. Every other row keeps its floor share unchanged, and the chosen row's `gross_share_cents` is its floor share plus the whole remainder. Step five computes each row's deductions from that row's **own final** `gross_share_cents`, never from the pre-remainder floor value. A row's `withholding_rate_bp` is 1500 when the contract's `tax_residency_code` equals the paying organization's `tax_jurisdiction_code` and 3000 when it does not; `withholding_cents` is `gross_share_cents` multiplied by that rate, divided by 10000 and rounded half up. `escrow_holdback_cents` is `gross_share_cents` multiplied by 1000 basis points — one tenth — divided by 10000 and rounded half up when the paid player is a minor at calculation time, and 0 otherwise; it is the trust holdback the guardian regime requires. `net_cents` is `gross_share_cents` less `withholding_cents` less `escrow_holdback_cents`. Step six freezes the totals: `allocated_cents` is the sum of `gross_share_cents` across the rows and therefore equals the player pool exactly, `withholding_total_cents` and `escrow_total_cents` are the sums of the corresponding per-row deductions, and `unallocated_cents` is `gross_prize_cents` less `association_levy_cents` less `org_share_cents` less `allocated_cents`.

The calculation moves `distribution_status` `DRAFT` → `CALCULATED`, stamps `calculated_by_user_id` with the calling finance officer's identifier, writes one allocation row per eligible contract with `allocation_status` of `CALCULATED` and a `prize_distribution_id` and `contract_id` naming its parents, and increases the organization's `prize_payable_cents` by `org_share_cents`. That ledger identity — `gross_prize_cents` equalling the sum of `association_levy_cents`, `org_share_cents`, `allocated_cents` and `unallocated_cents` — is recomputed and must hold exactly after the calculation, after the approval and after a void.

Sign-off belongs to the league office and enforces segregation of duties. A distribution must be in `CALCULATED`, or `ERR_DISTRIBUTION_NOT_CALCULATED` (HTTP 409) is returned, and the approving `X-User-ID` must differ from the recorded `calculated_by_user_id`, or `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403) is returned. Approval moves the distribution `CALCULATED` → `APPROVED`, moves every child allocation `CALCULATED` → `APPROVED`, and stamps `approved_by_user_id`. It does not change `prize_payable_cents`: that counter already includes every distribution in `CALCULATED` or `APPROVED`, so the amount was added when the waterfall ran and simply stays counted.

Voiding is finance's compensating action and is permitted from `CALCULATED` and from `APPROVED`, with any other status — including `DRAFT` and an already-voided distribution — refused with `ERR_DISTRIBUTION_NOT_CALCULATED`. A mandatory `void_reason` is recorded, the distribution moves to the terminal `VOIDED`, every child allocation moves to `VOIDED`, and the ledger is wound back to its pre-calculation shape: `allocated_cents`, `withholding_total_cents`, `escrow_total_cents`, `org_share_cents` and `association_levy_cents` all return to 0 while `unallocated_cents` returns to the full `gross_prize_cents`. The `org_share_cents` the calculation had added to the organization's `prize_payable_cents` is withdrawn again, exactly. A distribution's own allocation rows are reachable as a nested read filterable by `allocation_status`, and the rows paid against one contract are reachable from that contract.

### 3.9. Roster Changes: Filing, League Decision, Execution and Reversal

Every change to who plays for whom is filed as a **roster move**, and no single party can carry one through from end to end. A general manager files it, the league office decides it, the general manager executes the decision, and only the league office can undo an execution. A filing names the `organization_id` requesting the change, the `player_id` being moved, a `move_type` of `SIGN`, `RELEASE` or `TRANSFER_OUT`, and the mandatory `expected_organization_version` described in §2.8. It must also name a `contract_id` for every `move_type`: for `RELEASE` and `TRANSFER_OUT` that is the contract being ended, and for `SIGN` it is the already-accepted contract the league is being asked to sign off on — the contract whose full-season charge the approval decision re-tests. A `transfer_to_organization_id` is required when `move_type` is `TRANSFER_OUT` and must be absent for the other two types. Optionally the filing carries a `gross_transfer_fee_cents` of zero or more, defaulting to 0 and meaningful only for a transfer, and an `effective_at`, which defaults to the current instant. The move is created with `move_status` of `PENDING`, its `requested_by_user_id` stamped from the filing manager, its `approved_by_user_id`, `decision_note` and `reversal_reason` null, and its five money fields — `buyout_credit_cents`, `commission_tier_bp`, `agent_commission_cents`, `net_to_org_cents` and `released_cap_cents` — all at 0.

The league calendar bites at filing time. A `SIGN` filing is refused with `ERR_ROSTER_LOCK_ACTIVE` once the current instant has reached the league's `roster_lock_at`, and a `TRANSFER_OUT` filing is refused with `ERR_TRANSFER_WINDOW_CLOSED` (HTTP 422) unless the current instant falls at or after `transfer_window_open_at` and strictly before `transfer_window_close_at`. Moves may be listed with any combination of `organization_id`, `player_id`, `contract_id`, `transfer_to_organization_id` and `move_status`, and a single move read exposes its settlement figures.

The league office's decision forks the move, and the two branches are deliberately unequal. Both require `move_status` to be `PENDING`, or `ERR_MOVE_NOT_PENDING` (HTTP 409) is returned; both require the deciding `X-User-ID` to differ from the recorded `requested_by_user_id`, or `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` is returned; both require the league's roster lock to be inactive, or `ERR_ROSTER_LOCK_ACTIVE` is returned; both take a mandatory `decision` of `APPROVE` or `REJECT` and an optional `decision_note`; and both stamp `approved_by_user_id` with the deciding administrator and record the note. A `REJECT` decision then does nothing further at all: the move moves to the terminal `REJECTED`, and no contract, player, budget figure or deliverable is touched. An `APPROVE` decision moves it to `APPROVED`, which unlocks execution, and for a `SIGN` move it first re-tests the budget with the **stricter full-season variant**: take the organization's `committed_salary_cents`, replace the named contract's contribution of `provisional_cap_charge_cents` with that contract's `final_cap_charge_cents`, and require the result to be no greater than the organization's `annual_salary_budget_cents`. A mid-season signing whose prorated charge fit comfortably at acceptance can fail here, and the refusal is `ERR_SALARY_CAP_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). Approval of a `RELEASE` or `TRANSFER_OUT` runs no such test.

Execution belongs back to the filing organization's general manager, who may act only on their own organization's moves and only while `move_status` is `APPROVED`; anything else yields `ERR_MOVE_NOT_APPROVED` (HTTP 409). For a `TRANSFER_OUT` the transfer window must still be open at this moment too, and a closed window again yields `ERR_TRANSFER_WINDOW_CLOSED`. For both `RELEASE` and `TRANSFER_OUT` the departure must leave a legal squad: the organization's `active_roster_count` less one must remain greater than or equal to the league's `min_roster_size`, and a departure that would take the roster below that floor is refused with `ERR_ROSTER_SIZE_BELOW_MINIMUM` (HTTP 422).

For a `TRANSFER_OUT`, execution then runs the **transfer settlement**, whose seven steps are normative and each of which may only read the steps above it. Step one measures what is left of the contract: the whole days from the current instant to the contract's frozen `ends_at`, discarding any fraction and treating a contract already past its end as zero days, and then those days divided by 30 and raised to the next whole month. Step two derives the monthly rate from the base salary alone and never from any cap charge: `base_salary_cents` divided by 12 and rounded half up. Step three computes the `buyout_credit_cents` owed to the departing player as that monthly rate multiplied by the remaining whole months, multiplied by the contract's `buyout_multiplier_bp`, divided by 10000 and rounded half up. Step four computes the gross settlement the releasing organization retains as the move's `gross_transfer_fee_cents` less that buyout credit. Step five selects the agent's `commission_tier_bp` **from that gross settlement, before any commission is deducted**: 500 basis points when the gross settlement is strictly below 5,000,000 cents, and 300 basis points at or above that breakpoint — the larger the deal, the thinner the agent's percentage. `agent_commission_cents` is the gross settlement multiplied by the selected tier, divided by 10000 and rounded half up. Step six sets `net_to_org_cents` to the gross settlement less the agent's commission, and if that figure is negative the whole execution is refused with `ERR_NEGATIVE_SETTLEMENT` (HTTP 422) and nothing is written. Step seven takes the cap relief from the contract's frozen charge: `released_cap_cents` is set to the contract's `provisional_cap_charge_cents`, which is what makes a later reversal exact. Selecting the commission tier from the net figure, or from the raw transfer fee, changes the result for any settlement near the breakpoint; deducting the commission before the buyout credit changes it for every input. A `RELEASE` runs step seven only, so its `buyout_credit_cents`, `commission_tier_bp`, `agent_commission_cents` and `net_to_org_cents` all stay 0 while its `released_cap_cents` is populated.

Execution of a `RELEASE` or a `TRANSFER_OUT` moves `move_status` `APPROVED` → `EXECUTED` and fires a **four-link cascade**. First, the named contract's `contract_status` becomes `TERMINATED`, whether it was `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` beforehand. Second, because the contract terminated, the player's `player_status` becomes `FREE_AGENT`, their `current_organization_id` is cleared to null, their `free_agency_window_ends_at` is stamped at the current instant plus the league's `free_agency_cooldown_days` days, and their `last_release_organization_id` is set to the releasing organization — the pair that will later block a re-signing by that same organization while allowing any other organization to sign the player immediately. Third, because the contract left the `ACTIVE`-or-`SUSPENDED` set, the organization's `committed_salary_cents` decreases by `released_cap_cents` and its `active_roster_count` decreases by 1; where the contract was already `SUSPENDED` the roster count was decremented at suspension and is not decremented a second time. Fourth, because the contract terminated, every one of its deliverable assignments in `ASSIGNED`, `SUBMITTED`, `APPROVED` or `REJECTED` moves to `VOIDED`, dropping each affected deal's `assigned_count` and, for previously approved rows, its `approved_count`, which reverts a `FULFILLED` deal to `ACTIVE`. Every future `SCHEDULED` block listing the departing player is cancelled as part of the same termination.

A `SIGN` execution is deliberately empty of all that. None of the four cascade links fires, no money field is written, and the contract stays `ACTIVE`: the move is only the league's record of a signing that the offer acceptance already performed, so `move_status` becoming `EXECUTED` is its sole effect and `buyout_credit_cents`, `commission_tier_bp`, `agent_commission_cents`, `net_to_org_cents` and `released_cap_cents` all remain 0.

Reversal is the league office's compensating action and requires `move_status` to be `EXECUTED`, with anything else refused as `ERR_MOVE_NOT_EXECUTED` (HTTP 409). An executed `SIGN` has nothing to unwind and is refused with `ERR_MOVE_NOT_REVERSIBLE` (HTTP 422); only a `RELEASE` or a `TRANSFER_OUT` may be reversed. Because the reversal must re-commit the released salary, the organization's `uncommitted_salary_cents` must be greater than or equal to the move's `released_cap_cents` — if the organization has spent the freed budget elsewhere in the meantime the reversal is refused with `ERR_SALARY_CAP_EXCEEDED` rather than being performed partially. A permitted reversal records a mandatory `reversal_reason`, moves `move_status` `EXECUTED` → `REVERSED`, returns the contract `TERMINATED` → `ACTIVE`, returns the player `FREE_AGENT` → `SIGNED` with `current_organization_id` restored to the reversing move's organization and both `free_agency_window_ends_at` and `last_release_organization_id` cleared to null, increases `committed_salary_cents` by exactly `released_cap_cents` and `active_roster_count` by 1, and zeroes the settlement money fields. Because the commitment moves by the same frozen integer in both directions, `committed_salary_cents` returns to precisely its pre-execution value and the organization's budget identity holds. The blocks cancelled by the execution stay cancelled and the deliverables voided by it stay voided; only the salary quantity is conserved.
