# School Psych Evaluation Timeline API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

District psychological services live and die by a calendar. When an intervention team refers a student for a psychoeducational evaluation, a statutory clock starts the moment the parent signature arrives, and everything the district does afterwards — who administers the testing, which instrument they pull off the shelf, whether an interpreter can be booked, when the eligibility meeting can lawfully be convened — has to fit inside that window or the district owes the family compensatory service. Today that arithmetic is done in spreadsheets, one per building, with the result that timeline breaches are discovered during a state audit rather than while there is still time to prevent them.

This document specifies the School Psych Evaluation Timeline API, the district's system of record for referral intake, consent documentation, statutory timeline tracking, psychologist caseload balancing, assessment-instrument and scoring-licence inventory, interpreter-supported session scheduling, eligibility-meeting notice, and the breach-remedy pipeline that follows when the clock is nonetheless missed. Every business collection is mounted under the `/psych-services` prefix. The design centre of the product is a single derived quantity — the number of statutory days a given evaluation has left — because that quantity is what every other subsystem perturbs and what every other subsystem is judged against. Approving a permissible exclusion moves the due date; moving the due date changes the evaluation's compliance posture; the compliance posture re-weights the assigned psychologist's caseload pressure; caseload pressure decides which psychologist the server picks for the next referral; and that pick determines which protocol booklets and scoring seats get consumed.

Two governance principles run through the whole specification and should be read as product requirements rather than implementation detail. First, **segregation of duties**: the psychologist who drafts an evaluation report may never be the peer who finalizes it, and the compliance officer who proposes a compensatory remedy may never be the person who approves it. Second, **determinism under a logical clock**: the server never reads wall-clock time. Every day count, licence-expiry test, notice-lead test and meeting-due test is evaluated against an explicitly settable logical UTC clock, so that an auditor replaying a sequence of requests gets byte-identical results.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contract-level rules that hold for every endpoint in the service: authentication, the response envelope, pagination, the logical clock, common resource properties, derived-field reconciliation, concurrency protection, rounding discipline, and the fixed order in which validations are evaluated.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request — reads included — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which they act. The service recognizes exactly five roles: `IntakeCoordinator`, `SchoolPsychologist`, `PsychServicesSupervisor`, `ComplianceOfficer`, and `DistrictAdmin`. If either header is absent or blank — empty, or whitespace only — or if `X-User-Role` is not one of those five exact strings, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If both headers are well-formed but the declared role is not listed among the authorized roles for the endpoint being called (see the table in §4), the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The five roles divide the work as follows. An `IntakeCoordinator` staffs the district intake desk: they file referrals, record consent-to-evaluate events, book interpreter support, propose eligibility meetings and issue statutory parent notices. A `SchoolPsychologist` is the assigned evaluator: they administer testing, record scores, draft reports, peer-review a colleague's draft, and claim timeline exclusions on their own cases. A `PsychServicesSupervisor` routes caseloads, decides exclusion claims, books protocol orders and records that eligibility meetings were held. A `ComplianceOfficer` runs the district compliance sweep and proposes compensatory remedies and corrective action, but may never approve them. A `DistrictAdmin` owns reference data — buildings, psychologists, instruments and licence seats — approves protocol orders, and is the only role permitted to approve a compensatory remedy or to place a psychologist on leave.

Identity resolution differs by role, and the difference is load-bearing. When `X-User-Role` is `SchoolPsychologist`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Psychologist` record; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Psychologist` compare the resource's psychologist reference to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a `SchoolPsychologist` may start testing, record scores, or submit a report draft only on an evaluation whose `psychologist_id` equals their own `X-User-ID`, and may claim a timeline exclusion only against such an evaluation; a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_PSYCH_NOT_OWNER` (HTTP 403). Peer review is the deliberate exception: it does **not** require ownership, because the whole point is that somebody else reviews the draft.

When `X-User-Role` is `IntakeCoordinator`, `PsychServicesSupervisor`, `ComplianceOfficer`, or `DistrictAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it for audit — as `decided_by_user_id` on a `TimelineExclusion` decided by a supervisor, as `decided_by_user_id` on a `ProtocolOrder` decided by a district administrator, as `proposed_by_user_id` on a `TimelineBreach` whose remedy a compliance officer proposed, and as `approved_by_user_id` on a `TimelineBreach` whose remedy a district administrator approved — but it is never compared against any entity's `id`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, read from the logical clock of §2.4 rather than from the host's wall clock, so two requests taken at the same logical instant carry the same envelope timestamp.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints are paginated through two query parameters. `limit` sets the maximum number of records returned; when omitted it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` sets how many records are skipped before the page begins and defaults to 0. Either parameter supplied as an empty string reads as omitted and falls back to its default; either parameter that is not an integer, a `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422).

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, every list endpoint returns its records sorted by `created_at` in ascending order, and because the logical clock only moves when a client moves it, many records legitimately share one `created_at` value: such ties are broken by the order in which the records were created, never by their `id`.

List responses populate four fields inside the envelope's `meta` object: `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more`. `total_count` reports the number of records matching the filters before pagination is applied, and `has_more` reports whether any matching record remains beyond the current page.

### 2.4. The Logical District Clock

Nothing in this service reads the host machine's wall-clock time. The server maintains a **logical UTC clock**, and every piece of date or time arithmetic in this document — the statutory day counts, scoring-licence expiry, parent-notice lead, whether a meeting may yet be recorded as held, whether a consent timestamp is back-dated, whether an interpreter session has finished — is evaluated against that clock. Before any client has moved it, the clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; it must never fall back to real time.

A test-driving capability lets any of the five roles set the clock to a supplied `current_utc` instant, and the response is the single-field object `{"current_utc": …}` echoing the supplied string back verbatim rather than a re-normalized rendering of it; a `current_utc` that is missing, not a string, or not a parseable ISO 8601 instant is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Setting the clock mutates no entity: no `updated_at` is refreshed and no `version` is incremented anywhere. Its only effect is that subsequent computed reads, and subsequent compliance sweeps, evaluate against the new instant. Because several derived quantities on an evaluation are clock-relative, a plain read of an evaluation re-derives them at the clock's current instant rather than returning a stale snapshot.

Where this document speaks of a **calendar date**, it means the UTC calendar date (`YYYY-MM-DD`) obtained from the relevant instant, and the *current clock date* means the UTC calendar date of the logical clock. Where it speaks of the number of calendar days between two dates, it means the whole-day difference obtained by subtracting the earlier date from the later one, counting the earlier date as day zero — so two consecutive dates are one day apart.

### 2.5. Common Resource Properties

Eleven resource types are persisted by this service: `School`, `Psychologist`, `Referral`, `Evaluation`, `TimelineExclusion`, `AssessmentInstrument`, `ProtocolOrder`, `ScoringLicenseSeat`, `InterpreterSession`, `EligibilityMeeting`, and `TimelineBreach`. Every one of them carries the same four system-managed properties, and no resource type in this service omits any of them:

- **`id`** (string): a server-minted unique identifier formatted as a UUID. It carries no type prefix — the bare UUID is the identifier.
- **`created_at`** (string): an ISO 8601 timestamp in UTC with three fractional digits (for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`) recording when the record was first created.
- **`updated_at`** (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp, same format, refreshed on every mutation of the record. Every instant this service stores or echoes is normalized to this same UTC-with-three-fractional-digits form, including the ones derived from client-supplied instants — `consent_received_at` from a consent event's `effective_at`, an interpreter session's `session_start_at` and `session_end_at`, a meeting's `scheduled_start_at` and `scheduled_end_at` — so a supplied instant carrying a non-UTC offset is converted to UTC before it is stored.
- **`version`** (integer): a monotonically increasing integer that increments by one on every mutation of the record. A freshly created record opens at version 1.

Refreshing a derived field counts as a mutation. When a cascade re-derives an evaluation's remaining days, or recomputes a psychologist's caseload figures, or frees a licence seat, the affected record's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increments — including when the client never addressed that record directly. Two qualifications are pinned. First, a re-derivation of a psychologist's *stored* caseload figures counts as a mutation only when it actually moves one of them: `updated_at` and `version` advance only if `open_case_points`, `caseload_status`, `pressure_index_assigned_basis` or `pressure_index_clock_weighted` lands on a different value than it held, and a re-derivation that reproduces all four identically leaves the psychologist record untouched. Second, a derivation performed purely to answer a read is never a mutation at all: a plain read of an evaluation re-derives its five clock fields, and a read of an instrument re-derives `license_seats_free` and `license_status`, without refreshing `updated_at` or incrementing `version` on either record.

Path identifiers are always the `id` of the addressed record. The service uses `school_id`, `psychologist_id`, `referral_id`, `evaluation_id`, `instrument_id`, `seat_id`, `order_id`, `exclusion_id`, `meeting_id` and `breach_id` as path variables, and on the sub-resource list channels the owning record's identifier appears in the path simply as `id`.

### 2.6. The Statutory Evaluation Clock

The statutory clock is the hub of the product, and its evaluation order is **pinned**. The dependency between the credit window and the due date looks circular — the credit window is bounded by the due date, and the due date is extended by the credit — so the following seven-step resolution is normative and must be followed exactly. Every input is a calendar date, so there are no fractional days anywhere in this calculation; all seven outputs are whole integers or calendar dates.

An evaluation's clock is (re-)resolved at four moments: when the evaluation is created by a consent event, whenever a timeline exclusion claim on it is approved, on every district compliance sweep, and on every read of the evaluation, because the elapsed and remaining counts are clock-relative.

1. **Base due date.** `base_due_date` is `clock_start_date` advanced by 60 calendar days. Sixty days is the statutory evaluation window, and this first step deliberately ignores every exclusion.
2. **Per-exclusion credit.** Each approved exclusion is credited independently by the rule described in §3.7. Its intersection window is the closed window running from `clock_start_date` to the step-1 `base_due_date` — never the already-extended `statutory_due_date`. This is the pin that makes the resolution non-circular.
3. **Aggregate credit.** `credited_exclusion_days` is the smaller of two quantities: the sum of `days_credited` over this evaluation's exclusions in `APPROVED` status, accumulated in ascending `created_at` order; and the whole-number part of 60 multiplied by 30 percent — that is, 18 days, discarding any fraction. The aggregate ceiling is therefore a share of the **base** 60-day window, not of the extended one. Exclusions in `CLAIMED` or `REJECTED` status contribute nothing to this sum.
4. **Statutory due date.** `statutory_due_date` is `base_due_date` advanced by `credited_exclusion_days` calendar days.
5. **Elapsed statutory days.** `elapsed_statutory_days` is the number of calendar days from `clock_start_date` to the current clock date, less `credited_exclusion_days`; if that difference is negative it is reported as zero.
6. **Days remaining.** `days_remaining` is 60 less `elapsed_statutory_days`. It may be negative, and a negative value is the definition of a blown deadline.
7. **Compliance posture.** The evaluation's `compliance_status` is derived from `days_remaining`: negative means `BREACHED`; zero through ten inclusive means `AT_RISK`; greater than ten means `ON_TRACK`. Ten days is the at-risk window, and both of its boundaries matter — exactly ten remaining days is `AT_RISK`, eleven is `ON_TRACK`, zero remaining days is still `AT_RISK` rather than a breach.

Step 7 is written to the record only by system actors: the compliance sweep, the exclusion-approval cascade, the meeting-held cascade, the remedy approval, and consent withdrawal. No client sets `compliance_status` directly. Once an evaluation reaches one of the three closed postures — `COMPLIANT_CLOSED`, `BREACH_CLOSED_REMEDIED` or `WAIVED` — compliance sweeps skip it entirely and its stored day counts freeze at the values they held at closure. All five derived clock fields freeze together, `base_due_date` and `statutory_due_date` alongside `credited_exclusion_days`, `elapsed_statutory_days` and `days_remaining`, and the snapshot is taken immediately *before* the closed posture is written, so it captures the counts as of the closing request's clock instant. Every later read of that evaluation replays the frozen snapshot verbatim however far the clock subsequently runs.

### 2.7. Cascade Discipline and Derived-Field Reconciliation

Several operations in this service mutate more than one record, and in the deepest cases the mutation propagates four levels. The rule is that a cascade is part of the request that triggered it: it is applied immediately, in the same request, before the response is written, and each record it touches has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented. There is no deferred reconciliation and no eventual consistency anywhere in this product.

Derived quantities are always recomputed by a full sweep of their source rows, never incrementally adjusted. A psychologist's `open_case_points` is re-derived by re-summing every qualifying evaluation, not by adding or subtracting a delta, precisely so that a four-level cascade cannot leave the aggregate drifting away from the underlying rows. The same holds for an instrument's free licence seats and for an evaluation's clock fields.

An evaluation is unusual in that it carries **two independent state dimensions**. `assessment_status` tracks the assessment workflow through `PENDING_ASSIGNMENT`, `ASSIGNED`, `IN_TESTING`, `SCORED`, `REPORT_DRAFTED`, `REPORT_FINAL` and `CANCELLED`. `compliance_status` tracks the timeline posture through `ON_TRACK`, `AT_RISK`, `BREACHED`, `COMPLIANT_CLOSED`, `BREACH_CLOSED_REMEDIED` and `WAIVED`. The two dimensions evolve independently: a `REPORT_FINAL` evaluation may perfectly well still be `BREACHED`, and closing the compliance dimension does not move the assessment dimension.

### 2.8. Reading the Record: Left-to-Right Path Validation and Filters

Whenever a path carries more than one identifier, the identifiers are resolved strictly left to right and the first one that fails to resolve determines the error. A seat addressed under an instrument resolves the instrument first, so a request naming a non-existent instrument returns `ERR_INSTRUMENT_NOT_FOUND` before the seat is ever looked up; a seat that exists but belongs to a different instrument than the one in the path returns `ERR_SEAT_NOT_FOUND`. The same discipline applies to a breach addressed before a remedy action, an evaluation addressed before its sub-collections, and a referral addressed before its consent events. Each missing identifier has its own code: `ERR_SCHOOL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PSYCHOLOGIST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REFERRAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EVALUATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INSTRUMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SEAT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EXCLUSION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MEETING_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_BREACH_NOT_FOUND`, each returned with HTTP 404. These codes belong to the addressed *resources*; a request aimed at a path this service does not define at all, or at a method the path does not support, falls outside the catalogue of §5 and answers with `ERR_NOT_FOUND` (404) or `ERR_METHOD_NOT_ALLOWED` (405) respectively, in the same error envelope.

Foreign keys supplied in a request body are held to the same standard and resolve after the path identifiers. A referral's `school_id` and `prior_evaluation_id`, every element of a psychologist's `assigned_school_ids`, a protocol order's `instrument_id`, a licence seat's `psychologist_id`, the `evaluation_id` on an exclusion claim, an interpreter session or an eligibility meeting, the `school_id` on an eligibility meeting, the `psychologist_id` supplied to an assignment, and the `instrument_id` supplied at testing-start must all resolve to live records, otherwise the corresponding 404 code is returned.

Because the entity graph is flat — no business record's identifier is nested inside another record's path — the service provides reverse-reachability read channels so that every foreign key can be navigated backwards. The seats of one psychologist and the seats of one instrument are each listable and accept a `status` filter; an instrument's protocol orders are listable with a `status` filter; an evaluation's exclusion claims, interpreter sessions and breach records are each listable with a `status` filter. Referrals are listable filtered by `school_id`, `status`, `referral_type`, `prior_evaluation_id` and `active_evaluation_id`. Evaluations are listable filtered by `referral_id`, `psychologist_id`, `prior_evaluation_id`, `testing_instrument_id`, `report_drafted_by_psychologist_id`, `assessment_status` and `compliance_status`. Eligibility meetings are listable filtered by `evaluation_id`, `school_id` and `status`. All of these list channels accept `limit` and `offset` as described in §2.3. Every one of these filters is matched by exact string equality against the stored field: a filter naming a value that no record carries — including a value outside the field's declared enum — simply returns an empty page rather than an error, and a filter supplied as an empty string is ignored altogether rather than matching records whose field is null.

### 2.9. Stale-Evaluation Protection

Two write operations in this service are guarded by optimistic concurrency control against the `Evaluation` record, and both use the same input field and the same rejection code. In each case the request body must carry `expected_evaluation_version`, holding the `version` value the client last observed on the target evaluation. The field is **not** optional on those two operations. If the supplied value does not equal the evaluation's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_EVALUATION_VERSION` (HTTP 412), and nothing is written.

The first guarded operation is evaluator assignment. Reassignment legitimately supersedes a prior assignment, so without the guard a client holding a stale view would silently re-route a case that another supervisor had already re-routed, or would route on a caseload snapshot that the exclusion and compliance cascades had already invalidated. The second guarded operation is eligibility-meeting creation. A newly created meeting legitimately supersedes any earlier non-terminal meeting for the same evaluation, so without the guard a stale client would silently discard a meeting whose parent notice had already gone out.

No other endpoint in this service accepts a version field, and no other endpoint performs a version check. In particular, the notice, reschedule-request, hold, exclusion-decision, testing, scoring, drafting and review operations take no `expected_evaluation_version`.

### 2.10. Rounding Discipline: Cents, Points, Minutes and Indices

Four distinct numeric conventions coexist in this service, and mixing them up is the most common way to get the arithmetic wrong.

**Whole cents, rounded independently.** All money is integer cents. Protocol-order pricing rounds three separate components half up to whole cents *independently*, and only then sums them; applying the percentages to a running total and rounding once at the end produces a different — and wrong — total. The pinned order is given in §3.4.

**Whole points.** Caseload weights are integers throughout: an evaluation's `caseload_points` and a psychologist's `open_case_points` and `weekly_capacity_points` never carry a fraction.

**Two decimal places, round half up.** Both caseload pressure indices and the assignment selector's rank score are numbers rounded half up to exactly two decimal places. A freshly registered psychologist reports both indices as `0.00`. They are carried in the response as JSON numbers rather than strings, so a two-decimal value whose last digits are zero renders with them dropped — `0.00` serializes as `0.0` and `1.50` as `1.5`.

**Minutes rounded to the nearest five.** Compensatory service awards are whole minutes rounded to the nearest multiple of five, with halves going up. Concretely: divide the raw minute figure by five, round that quotient half up to a whole number, and multiply back by five.

Calendar-day quantities are exact integers and are never rounded, because every input to them is a calendar date.

### 2.11. The Order of Validation

Validations are evaluated in one fixed ascending order and **the first failure wins** — later checks never run, and never mask an earlier one. Any endpoint applies only the checks in this ladder that are scoped to it, but it always applies them in this relative order:

1. Authentication headers present and the role recognized — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401), on every endpoint.
2. The acting role is authorized for the endpoint — `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403), on every endpoint.
3. Request-body shape — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422), on every write endpoint. This covers: every required field present; every enum value inside its declared set; integers non-negative where constrained; `session_end_at` strictly after `session_start_at` and `scheduled_end_at` strictly after `scheduled_start_at`; `end_date` on or after `start_date` on an exclusion claim; `requested_domains` a non-empty, duplicate-free subset of the four assessment domains; and `prior_evaluation_id` supplied exactly when `referral_type` is `REEVALUATION` and omitted when it is `INITIAL`. It also covers the finer shape rules: a required string field must be non-empty once trimmed of whitespace; a `building_code` must match the two-to-eight-character uppercase-alphanumeric form of §3.1 and every element of `languages_certified` and of `supported_language_editions` the two-letter lowercase ISO 639-1 form; a declared boolean must be a JSON boolean and a declared integer a JSON integer, with a boolean never accepted in an integer's place; a declared calendar date must be exactly `YYYY-MM-DD`; and a request body that is absent, empty or unparseable is treated as an empty JSON object, so it fails on whichever required field is checked first. A field present but explicitly null counts as absent — it falls back to the default where one exists and fails the required-field check where none does.
4. Every identifier in the path resolves, left to right — the 404 family listed in §2.8.
5. Every foreign key in the body resolves — the same 404 family.
6. Ownership for the entity-backed role — `ERR_PSYCH_NOT_OWNER` (403), on testing-start, score recording, report drafting and exclusion claiming.
7. `expected_evaluation_version` matches the evaluation's current `version` — `ERR_STALE_EVALUATION_VERSION` (412), on evaluator assignment and eligibility-meeting creation.
8. The target record is in a state that permits the requested transition — the 409 state family: `ERR_EVALUATION_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_REFERRAL_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_EXCLUSION_ALREADY_DECIDED`, `ERR_ORDER_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_MEETING_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_BREACH_STATE_INVALID`, `ERR_SEAT_STATE_INVALID` and `ERR_PSYCHOLOGIST_STATE_INVALID`.
9. The cross-entity consent gate — `ERR_CONSENT_NOT_ON_FILE` (409), on assignment, testing-start, score recording, report drafting, peer review and exclusion claiming.
10. The cross-entity report gate — `ERR_REPORT_NOT_FINAL` (409), on recording a meeting as held.
11. Segregation of duties on peer review — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
12. Scoring-software licensing — `ERR_SCORING_LICENSE_INVALID` (422), on testing-start.
13. Parent-notice lead — `ERR_NOTICE_LEAD_INSUFFICIENT` (422), on issuing a notice.
14. Building coverage — `ERR_PSYCH_BUILDING_MISMATCH` (422), on assignment with an explicit `psychologist_id`.
15. Bilingual coverage — `ERR_LANGUAGE_CERT_MISSING` (422), on assignment with an explicit `psychologist_id`.
16. Credential scope — `ERR_INTERN_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (422), on assignment with an explicit `psychologist_id`.
17. Interpreter support present — `ERR_INTERPRETER_SESSION_MISSING` (409), on testing-start.
18. Psychologist calendar conflict — `ERR_PSYCH_CALENDAR_CONFLICT` (409), on booking an interpreter session.
19. Hosting-building meeting capacity — `ERR_SCHOOL_MEETING_CAPACITY_FULL` (409), on eligibility-meeting creation **and** on a reschedule request.
20. Consent timestamp not in the future — `ERR_CONSENT_TIMESTAMP_FUTURE` (422), on a consent event.
21. Exclusion credit is at least one day — `ERR_EXCLUSION_ZERO_DAYS` (422), on an exclusion decision when the decision is to approve.
22. The meeting is due — `ERR_MEETING_NOT_DUE` (409), on recording a meeting as held.
23. The reschedule ceiling — `ERR_RESCHEDULE_LIMIT_REACHED` (409), on a reschedule request.
24. Consent-to-waive applicability — `ERR_WAIVER_NOT_APPLICABLE` (422), on a consent event.
25. Building-code uniqueness — `ERR_DUPLICATE_BUILDING_CODE` (409), on registering a building.
26. Licence-seat availability — `ERR_LICENSE_SEATS_EXHAUSTED` (409), on assigning a scoring seat.
27. The caseload ceiling — `ERR_PSYCH_CASELOAD_OVER_CAP` (409), on assignment with an explicit `psychologist_id`.
28. The selector found at least one candidate — `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_PSYCHOLOGIST` (409), on assignment with `psychologist_id` omitted.
29. Protocol inventory — `ERR_PROTOCOL_STOCK_INSUFFICIENT` (409), on testing-start.
30. Receipt reconciliation — `ERR_RECEIPT_QUANTITY_EXCEEDS_ORDER` (422), on recording a receipt.
31. The compensatory award ceiling — `ERR_REMEDY_MINUTES_EXCEED_CAP` (422), on proposing a remedy.

Two consequences of this ordering are worth calling out, because they are easy to get backwards. On testing-start, the licensing check at position 12 runs before the interpreter check at position 17, which in turn runs before the inventory check at position 29 — so a psychologist with no valid scoring seat, no interpreter session and no protocol stock sees `ERR_SCORING_LICENSE_INVALID`, not the other two. On a reschedule request, the hosting building's capacity check at position 19 runs before the reschedule ceiling at position 23 — so a third reschedule into a full slot reports `ERR_SCHOOL_MEETING_CAPACITY_FULL`, not `ERR_RESCHEDULE_LIMIT_REACHED`.

### 2.12. Conservation Invariants

Two identities must hold after every single request, including every cascade, and they are the cheapest way to detect that a cascade has been implemented incompletely.

**Licence seats are conserved.** For every instrument, the sum of the seats currently in `ASSIGNED` status plus `license_seats_free` equals `license_seat_total`. Seats are never deleted — the population of `ASSIGNED` plus `RELEASED` seats only grows — and only `ASSIGNED` seats occupy capacity. Assigning a seat is the forward action and reduces the free count by one; releasing a seat is the compensating action and raises it by one.

**Caseload points are conserved.** The sum of `open_case_points` across all psychologists equals the sum of `caseload_points` across every evaluation that has a non-null `psychologist_id` and sits in one of the four open assessment statuses (`ASSIGNED`, `IN_TESTING`, `SCORED`, `REPORT_DRAFTED`). Assigning an evaluation to a psychologist is the forward action. Reassigning it is the compensating action: the outgoing psychologist loses exactly that evaluation's `caseload_points` and the incoming psychologist gains exactly the same number, so the district total is unchanged. Placing a psychologist on leave, finalizing a report, and withdrawing consent each remove the points from both sides of the identity at once, so the identity still balances.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Buildings, Evaluators, and the District Caseload Picture

Before any referral can be filed, district administration must stand up the reference data. A building is registered with a display `name` and a `building_code`, which is an uppercase alphanumeric string of two to eight characters and must be unique across all buildings — a second building registered with a code already in use, compared case-sensitively and exactly, is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_BUILDING_CODE`. Each building declares the `grade_band` it serves, one of `ELEMENTARY`, `MIDDLE` or `HIGH`. Two numeric attributes follow: `concurrent_meeting_capacity`, the maximum number of eligibility meetings whose intervals may overlap at that building, which defaults to one and must be at least one; and `enrollment_count`, the enrolled student headcount, which defaults to zero, may not be negative, and is carried for reporting only — nothing in this specification branches on it.

A psychologist is registered with a `full_name` and an `employee_number`, both required non-empty strings. The employee number is informational and is deliberately **not** enforced unique, since districts reuse numbers across payroll systems. The `credential_type` is one of `SSP`, `PHD` or `INTERN`, and the `INTERN` value restricts which evaluations may be routed to that person (see §3.3). Language coverage is declared as `languages_certified`, a non-empty array of two-letter lowercase ISO 639-1 codes that defaults to a single-element array containing `en`. Building coverage is declared as `assigned_school_ids`, a non-empty array of building identifiers — a genuine many-to-many membership set rather than a single reference — and every element must resolve to an existing building at write time, otherwise the registration fails with `ERR_SCHOOL_NOT_FOUND` for the first unresolvable building element; the acting `X-User-ID` on a registration is an opaque administrator identifier and is never resolved against any record, so `ERR_PSYCHOLOGIST_NOT_FOUND` does not arise here. Finally, `weekly_capacity_points` sets the caseload ceiling used both by the caseload posture and by the assignment selector; it defaults to 20 and must be at least one.

Registration initialises the derived side of the record deterministically: `status` becomes `ACTIVE`, `open_case_points` becomes 0, `caseload_status` becomes `BALANCED`, and both `pressure_index_assigned_basis` and `pressure_index_clock_weighted` become `0.00`. A psychologist's `status` is one of exactly two values, `ACTIVE` or `ON_LEAVE`, and only the leave operation moves it.

**How caseload is measured.** `open_case_points` is the sum of `caseload_points` over every evaluation whose `psychologist_id` is this psychologist and whose `assessment_status` is one of `ASSIGNED`, `IN_TESTING`, `SCORED` or `REPORT_DRAFTED`. Evaluations that have reached `REPORT_FINAL` or `CANCELLED` are excluded regardless of their compliance posture, and no time window applies — the whole evaluation table is swept, and the sum is recomputed from scratch rather than adjusted incrementally. Because a sum is order-insensitive, no tie-break arises here; comparisons between two psychologists happen only in the selector described in §3.3. From that sum, `caseload_status` follows: `BALANCED` when `open_case_points` is strictly less than `weekly_capacity_points`, `AT_CAP` when the two are exactly equal, and `OVER_CAP` when the points exceed the capacity. Recomputation is triggered by every transition that changes an evaluation's `psychologist_id` or moves its `assessment_status` into or out of those four open statuses — that is, by assignment, by an approving peer review, by a psychologist going on leave, and by the consent-withdrawal cascade.

**Two views of pressure.** Alongside the raw point total the district tracks two pressure indices, both stored on the psychologist, both rounded half up to two decimal places, and both sweeping exactly the same set of open evaluations. Each open evaluation carries an urgency weight determined by its compliance posture: an evaluation that is `BREACHED` weighs double, at a weight of 2.0; one that is `AT_RISK` weighs one and a half, at 1.5; anything else weighs 1.0. `pressure_index_assigned_basis` is `open_case_points` divided by `weekly_capacity_points`, rounded half up to two decimals — the headcount-planning view, in which timeline posture is ignored entirely and every open evaluation weighs its raw points. `pressure_index_clock_weighted` is the sum, across the same open evaluations, of each evaluation's `caseload_points` multiplied by its urgency weight, that total divided by `weekly_capacity_points`, and the quotient rounded half up to two decimals. The two figures are identical while every open case is `ON_TRACK` and diverge as soon as one goes `AT_RISK` or `BREACHED`, which is the point: a psychologist carrying a single breached case can register as more loaded than a colleague carrying more raw points. The clock-weighted figure is the one the assignment selector ranks on. Both indices are recomputed on the same triggers as `open_case_points`, and additionally on every compliance-posture change on any evaluation assigned to that psychologist.

**Staffing disruption.** A `DistrictAdmin` may place an `ACTIVE` psychologist on leave, moving `status` from `ACTIVE` to `ON_LEAVE`; attempting it on someone already `ON_LEAVE` is rejected with `ERR_PSYCHOLOGIST_STATE_INVALID`. The operation is the deepest cascade in the product and runs four levels. First, the psychologist leaves the selector's candidate pool for good. Second, every evaluation assigned to them whose `assessment_status` is `ASSIGNED`, `IN_TESTING`, `SCORED` or `REPORT_DRAFTED` is driven back to `PENDING_ASSIGNMENT`, with `psychologist_id` cleared and `reassignment_count` incremented by one; the evaluation's `version` increments as part of that write. Third, every interpreter session of each of those evaluations that is still `SCHEDULED` is cancelled with `cancellation_reason` set to `PSYCHOLOGIST_UNAVAILABLE`. Fourth, the caseload figures are re-derived for this psychologist, driving `open_case_points` to 0, `caseload_status` to `BALANCED` and both pressure indices to `0.00`. Two details are deliberate and must not be "helpfully" changed: `protocol_units_consumed` on an evaluation that was already in testing is **not** refunded to instrument stock, and `report_drafted_by_psychologist_id` is retained on an evaluation that had reached `REPORT_DRAFTED`, so that when the case is re-assigned and the draft comes back the original author still cannot approve it. Scoring licence seats held by the departing psychologist are likewise **not** released — licences follow the employee, not the caseload.

### 3.2. Referral Intake and the Consent Event Stream

An `IntakeCoordinator` files a referral on behalf of a building's intervention team. The payload names the `school_id` of the building raising it and the `student_local_id`, an opaque district-local student identifier. The `referral_type` is either `INITIAL`, for a first evaluation, or `REEVALUATION`, for a re-examination of an existing eligibility; when and only when it is `REEVALUATION` the payload must also carry `prior_evaluation_id`, pointing at the most recent completed evaluation being re-examined. Supplying that reference on an `INITIAL` referral, or omitting it on a `REEVALUATION`, is a shape violation and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. This self-reference is what makes the reevaluation cycle traceable: an evaluation created from a reevaluation referral carries the same reference forward.

Family and linguistic circumstances are captured at intake. `primary_language` is the ISO 639-1 code of the family's primary language and defaults to `en`; unlike a psychologist's `languages_certified`, it is validated only as a non-empty string and not against the two-letter lowercase form. `interpreter_required` is a boolean, defaulting to false, meaning the family needs an interpreter present for evaluation sessions. `bilingual_evaluation_required` is a separate boolean, also defaulting to false, meaning something stronger — that the evaluation must be administered by a psychologist personally certified in the family's primary language. The two flags are independent and are enforced by different rules at different moments. The intervention team's assessment request arrives as `requested_domains`, a non-empty, duplicate-free subset of the four assessment domains `COGNITIVE`, `ACADEMIC`, `SOCIAL_EMOTIONAL` and `ADAPTIVE`, and their prior work arrives as free-text `intervention_team_summary`, which defaults to the empty string.

A filed referral opens in `INTAKE_RECEIVED` status with `consent_received_at`, `active_evaluation_id`, `last_consent_event_type`, `parent_signature_reference` and `waiver_reason` all null. Crucially, **filing a referral does not start the statutory clock.** Nothing is due yet, because nothing has been consented to.

The clock starts with a consent event. Consent events are polymorphic: one operation, recorded by an `IntakeCoordinator` against a referral, accepts an `event_type` of `CONSENT_RECEIVED`, `CONSENT_WAIVED` or `CONSENT_WITHDRAWN`, together with an `effective_at` instant, an optional `parent_signature_reference` (the signature-packet document reference) and an optional `waiver_reason`. Every event records `last_consent_event_type`, whatever else it does, and every event — creating, waiving or withdrawing — responds with the updated **referral** object rather than with the evaluation it may have created; that evaluation is reached through the referral's `active_evaluation_id`. No event may be back-dated into the future: an `effective_at` later than the current logical clock instant is rejected with `ERR_CONSENT_TIMESTAMP_FUTURE`. A consent event that the referral's current status does not permit — a second `CONSENT_RECEIVED`, or any event at all once the referral has reached `CONSENT_WITHDRAWN` or `COMPLETED`, both of which are terminal — is rejected with `ERR_REFERRAL_STATE_INVALID`.

Recording `CONSENT_RECEIVED` against a referral in `INTAKE_RECEIVED` moves it `INTAKE_RECEIVED → CONSENTED`, sets `consent_received_at` to the event's `effective_at`, and stores the supplied `parent_signature_reference`, leaving it null when the field was omitted; a `waiver_reason` supplied alongside a `CONSENT_RECEIVED` event is ignored and the referral's `waiver_reason` stays null. It then creates exactly one evaluation: `referral_id` pointing back at the referral, `assessment_status` `PENDING_ASSIGNMENT`, `compliance_status` `ON_TRACK`, `clock_start_date` set to the UTC calendar date of `effective_at` — day zero of the statutory clock — and `evaluation_domains`, `interpreter_required` and `referral_type` all copied from the referral, along with `prior_evaluation_id` copied from the referral's own. The new evaluation opens with `psychologist_id`, `testing_instrument_id`, `report_drafted_by_psychologist_id`, `score_summary` and `report_narrative` all null, with `reassignment_count` and `protocol_units_consumed` at zero. Its statutory clock is resolved by the seven pinned steps of §2.6, producing `base_due_date`, `credited_exclusion_days`, `statutory_due_date`, `elapsed_statutory_days` and `days_remaining`, and its workload weight is computed as described below. Finally the referral's `active_evaluation_id` is set to the new evaluation's identifier; it is never reassigned afterwards.

`CONSENT_WAIVED` exists for the reevaluation cycle: parents may waive the full reevaluation consent process, and the statutory clock still runs from the waiver signature. Recording it moves the referral `INTAKE_RECEIVED → CONSENT_WAIVED`, sets `consent_received_at` to the event's `effective_at`, stores the supplied `waiver_reason` — and, symmetrically, ignores any `parent_signature_reference` supplied alongside it, leaving that field null — and creates the evaluation in exactly the same shape and with exactly the same clock treatment as `CONSENT_RECEIVED` does — same `PENDING_ASSIGNMENT` assessment posture, same `ON_TRACK` compliance posture, same `clock_start_date` derived from `effective_at`, same copied domains and flags, same `active_evaluation_id` linkage. Because a waiver is documentation of a reevaluation decision, it is accepted only when the referral's `referral_type` is `REEVALUATION` **and** the supplied `waiver_reason` is a non-empty string; a waiver on an `INITIAL` referral, or one with a missing or empty reason, is rejected with `ERR_WAIVER_NOT_APPLICABLE`.

**Workload weight.** An evaluation's `caseload_points` is fixed at creation and is immutable thereafter, since the domains and referral type it depends on are themselves copied at creation. It starts from a base of three points. One additional point is added for each requested assessment domain beyond the first — a single-domain evaluation adds nothing, a four-domain evaluation adds three. Two further points are added when the evaluation requires an interpreter, and none when it does not. Then the referral type contributes: two points for an `INITIAL` evaluation, one point for a `REEVALUATION`. The result is a whole number of points and is the figure that flows into every caseload aggregate in §3.1.

**Withdrawal.** Parents may withdraw consent at any point while the referral sits in `CONSENTED` or `CONSENT_WAIVED`, and from either of those two states the event moves the referral to the terminal `CONSENT_WITHDRAWN` status with an identical four-part cascade. First, the referral's active evaluation moves to `assessment_status` `CANCELLED`, unless it has already reached `REPORT_FINAL` or `CANCELLED`, in which case its assessment dimension is left alone. Second, and caused by that cancellation, the evaluation's `compliance_status` becomes `WAIVED`, which stops its clock — compliance sweeps skip `WAIVED` evaluations entirely from then on. Because the two dimensions are independent, this second step is taken whenever the compliance dimension is not already one of the three closed postures, including on an evaluation whose assessment dimension had already reached `REPORT_FINAL` or `CANCELLED` and was therefore left alone in the first step; conversely an evaluation already `COMPLIANT_CLOSED` or `BREACH_CLOSED_REMEDIED` is not re-closed as `WAIVED`. The two steps together are a single write on the evaluation, so its `version` advances by one, not two. The evaluation's `psychologist_id` is retained for audit, but because the case has left the open-caseload window the assigned psychologist's `open_case_points`, `caseload_status` and both pressure indices are re-derived. Third, and also caused by the cancellation, every interpreter session of that evaluation still in `SCHEDULED` status is cancelled with `cancellation_reason` set to `CONSENT_WITHDRAWN`, and every eligibility meeting of that evaluation whose status is `PROPOSED`, `NOTICED` or `RESCHEDULE_REQUESTED` is cancelled, releasing the hosting building's capacity slot in each case. Fourth, any timeline breach of that evaluation still in `DETECTED` or `REMEDY_PROPOSED` status is vacated and its `remedy_minutes_awarded` is driven to zero.

The one other way a referral leaves the workflow is completion: when the eligibility meeting for the referral's active evaluation is recorded as held, the referral moves from `CONSENTED` or `CONSENT_WAIVED` to the terminal `COMPLETED` status, after which no further consent event is accepted.

### 3.3. Routing the Evaluator: Explicit Assignment and the Server-Side Selector

A newly created evaluation sits in `PENDING_ASSIGNMENT` with no evaluator. A `PsychServicesSupervisor` routes it, and the routing operation has two modes distinguished purely by whether the payload names a target. Both modes require `expected_evaluation_version` in the body, carrying the `version` the supervisor last read on the evaluation, and both reject a mismatch with `ERR_STALE_EVALUATION_VERSION` as described in §2.9. Both modes also require that the evaluation's `assessment_status` is currently `PENDING_ASSIGNMENT` or `ASSIGNED` — any other assessment posture is rejected with `ERR_EVALUATION_STATE_INVALID` — and that the parent referral's `status` is `CONSENTED` or `CONSENT_WAIVED`, since routing an evaluation whose consent has been withdrawn is rejected with `ERR_CONSENT_NOT_ON_FILE`.

In **explicit mode** the payload carries `psychologist_id`, and five eligibility rules are applied to that named target in the priority order of §2.11. The target must be `ACTIVE`; naming someone whose `status` is `ON_LEAVE` is rejected with `ERR_PSYCHOLOGIST_STATE_INVALID`. That `ACTIVE` test is the first of the two position-8 state checks, so naming an `ON_LEAVE` psychologist who also happens to be the one already holding the case reports `ERR_PSYCHOLOGIST_STATE_INVALID` rather than `ERR_EVALUATION_STATE_INVALID`. The building whose intervention team raised the referral must appear in the target's `assigned_school_ids`; otherwise the request fails with `ERR_PSYCH_BUILDING_MISMATCH`. When the referral has `bilingual_evaluation_required` set to true, the referral's `primary_language` must appear in the target's `languages_certified`; otherwise the request fails with `ERR_LANGUAGE_CERT_MISSING`. A target whose `credential_type` is `INTERN` may only take evaluations whose `referral_type` is `REEVALUATION`; handing an intern an `INITIAL` case fails with `ERR_INTERN_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. Finally the caseload ceiling: the target's current `open_case_points` plus this evaluation's `caseload_points` must be no greater than the target's `weekly_capacity_points`. Landing exactly on the ceiling is permitted and simply drives `caseload_status` to `AT_CAP`; exceeding it by even a single point is rejected with `ERR_PSYCH_CASELOAD_OVER_CAP`.

In **selector mode** `psychologist_id` is omitted and the server picks the evaluator. Selection runs in two stages. The first stage filters a candidate pool from every psychologist on file, keeping only those who satisfy all of the following: `status` is `ACTIVE`; the referral's building appears in the candidate's `assigned_school_ids`; either the referral does not require a bilingual evaluation, or the referral's `primary_language` appears in the candidate's `languages_certified`; either the candidate's `credential_type` is `SSP` or `PHD`, or the evaluation's `referral_type` is `REEVALUATION`; and the candidate's `open_case_points` plus this evaluation's `caseload_points` is no greater than their `weekly_capacity_points`. Note that in selector mode these clauses act as filters rather than as errors — an ineligible psychologist is silently skipped, not reported. If the filter leaves nobody at all, the request is rejected with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_PSYCHOLOGIST`, the evaluation stays in `PENDING_ASSIGNMENT`, and its `version` is **not** incremented.

The second stage ranks the surviving candidates, and **lower scores win**. Each candidate's score starts from their `pressure_index_clock_weighted`. To that is added a breach penalty of twenty-five hundredths of a point for each of their open evaluations in `BREACHED` posture beyond the first — count their breached open evaluations, subtract one, treat any negative result as zero, and multiply by 0.25, so one breached case adds nothing and three breached cases add half a point. From that is subtracted a continuity bonus of one tenth of a point for each of their open evaluations whose referral was raised at the same building as this referral, counting at most two such evaluations — so the bonus is capped at two tenths of a point however many same-building cases the candidate already carries. The resulting figure is rounded half up to two decimal places, and throughout this calculation "open evaluations of a candidate" means evaluations whose `psychologist_id` is that candidate and whose `assessment_status` is `ASSIGNED`, `IN_TESTING`, `SCORED` or `REPORT_DRAFTED`. Ties are broken in this exact order: lowest score first; then lowest `open_case_points`; then earliest `created_at`; and finally, when even the `created_at` values coincide — which they routinely do under a logical clock — the order in which the psychologists were registered. A psychologist's `id` is never a decision input, so an implementation must not fall back to identifier ordering.

Whichever mode resolved the target, the successful routing sets `psychologist_id` on the evaluation, increments the evaluation's `version` by one, and re-derives the newly assigned psychologist's `open_case_points`, `caseload_status`, `pressure_index_assigned_basis` and `pressure_index_clock_weighted`. When the evaluation was already `ASSIGNED`, routing performs a reassignment — `ASSIGNED → ASSIGNED` — which legitimately supersedes the prior assignment. In that case the resolved target must differ from the psychologist currently on the case; routing an evaluation to the person already holding it is rejected with `ERR_EVALUATION_STATE_INVALID`. A genuine reassignment additionally increments `reassignment_count` by one and re-derives the caseload figures for **both** psychologists, the outgoing and the incoming. The outgoing loses exactly this evaluation's `caseload_points` and the incoming gains exactly the same number, which is how the district-wide conservation identity of §2.12 survives a re-route. All the same eligibility rules that govern a first assignment apply unchanged to the incoming psychologist on a reassignment.

### 3.4. The Instrument Cupboard: Protocols, Pricing and Scoring Licences

Testing cannot begin without two consumables in place: physical protocol booklets, and a seat on the publisher's scoring software. Both hang off an assessment instrument, which a `DistrictAdmin` registers with a display `name` and a publisher `instrument_code` (informational only). Inventory is tracked as `protocol_stock_count`, the consumable booklets on hand, which defaults to zero and may not be negative, and `protocol_units_per_administration`, the number of booklets burned per assessment domain administered, which defaults to one and must be at least one. Pricing is anchored on `protocol_unit_cost_cents`, the list price per booklet in whole cents before any tier or contract adjustment, which must be at least one cent. Licensing is declared as `license_seat_total`, the number of concurrent scoring-software seats the district owns for this instrument, defaulting to zero and never negative, together with `license_expires_on`, the last calendar date on which the scoring licence is valid, inclusive. Translated editions are declared as `supported_language_editions`, an array of ISO 639-1 codes defaulting to a single-element array containing `en`.

Two instrument attributes are derived rather than supplied. `license_seats_free` is `license_seat_total` less the number of that instrument's seats currently in `ASSIGNED` status; at registration, when no seats exist, it therefore equals `license_seat_total`. `license_status` is `ACTIVE` when the current clock date is on or before `license_expires_on` and `EXPIRED` otherwise — so a licence expiring today is still `ACTIVE` today and becomes `EXPIRED` tomorrow. Because it is clock-derived, moving the logical clock past an expiry date flips the value with no write to the instrument.

Seats are handed out one at a time. A `DistrictAdmin` or a `PsychServicesSupervisor` assigns a seat of a given instrument to a psychologist by naming the `psychologist_id`; the seat records the owning `instrument_id` from the path, opens in `ASSIGNED` status with `assigned_at` set to the current clock instant and `released_at` null, and reduces the instrument's free-seat count by one. Two conditions block a seat assignment and share a single code: `license_seats_free` already standing at zero, and the named psychologist already holding an `ASSIGNED` seat for that same instrument. Either case is rejected with `ERR_LICENSE_SEATS_EXHAUSTED`. Releasing a seat moves it `ASSIGNED → RELEASED`, stamps `released_at` with the current clock instant, and raises the instrument's free-seat count by one, conserving `license_seat_total` as described in §2.12. Both directions are cascades onto the instrument: assigning a seat and releasing one each refresh the owning instrument's `updated_at` and increment its `version` by one, even though `license_seats_free` is derived at read time. Releasing a seat that is already `RELEASED` is rejected with `ERR_SEAT_STATE_INVALID`, and `RELEASED` is terminal — a released seat is never revived, a fresh seat is assigned instead.

**Ordering booklets.** A `PsychServicesSupervisor` or a `DistrictAdmin` orders protocol booklets by naming the `instrument_id`, the `quantity_requested` (at least one booklet) and an optional `rush_flag`, which defaults to false and requests rush handling. Pricing is computed at creation, and the **rounding order is pinned**: three components are each rounded half up to whole cents *independently*, and only then combined. Applying the percentages to a running total and rounding once at the end yields a different total and is wrong.

The sequence is as follows. First, a volume tier multiplier is chosen from the ordered quantity alone: orders of fewer than 25 booklets get a multiplier of one, orders of 25 through 99 booklets get a multiplier of ninety-five hundredths, and orders of 100 booklets or more get a multiplier of nine tenths. Second, `unit_price_cents` is the instrument's `protocol_unit_cost_cents` multiplied by that tier multiplier, rounded half up to a whole cent — this is the first of the three independent roundings. Third, `base_cents` is `unit_price_cents` multiplied by `quantity_requested`, an exact integer product with no rounding. Fourth, `contract_discount_cents` is `base_cents` multiplied by seven percent, rounded half up to a whole cent — the second independent rounding, reflecting the district's master contract. Fifth, `rush_surcharge_cents` is `base_cents` multiplied by eight and one-half percent, rounded half up to a whole cent, when `rush_flag` is true, and exactly zero when it is false — the third independent rounding. Sixth, `handling_fee_cents` is a flat 1,250 cents when `quantity_requested` is fewer than 25 booklets — the same 25-booklet threshold that governs the first tier — and zero at 25 booklets or more. Finally, `total_cost_cents` is `base_cents`, less `contract_discount_cents`, plus `rush_surcharge_cents`, plus `handling_fee_cents`.

Small orders skip human review. `auto_approved` is true when `total_cost_cents` is at or below 40,000 cents — four hundred dollars, with the boundary inclusive, so an order landing exactly on the threshold auto-approves — and in that case the order is created directly in `APPROVED` status with no `decided_by_user_id` recorded, because no human decided it. Otherwise `auto_approved` is false and the order opens in `SUBMITTED` status awaiting a decision. Either way a newly created order carries `received_quantity` at zero and `decided_by_user_id` null. Creating an order never touches `protocol_stock_count`; booklets move only on receipt.

A `DistrictAdmin` decides a `SUBMITTED` order by supplying a `decision` of either `APPROVE` or `CANCEL`, which moves it `SUBMITTED → APPROVED` or `SUBMITTED → CANCELLED` and records `decided_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID`. Cancellation leaves instrument stock untouched, and `CANCELLED` is terminal. A decision submitted against an order that is not `SUBMITTED` is rejected with `ERR_ORDER_STATE_INVALID`, as is a receipt recorded against an order that is not `APPROVED`. Receipt is recorded by a `PsychServicesSupervisor` or a `DistrictAdmin` supplying `received_quantity`, which must be an integer at least one and no greater than `quantity_requested`; anything outside that range is rejected with `ERR_RECEIPT_QUANTITY_EXCEEDS_ORDER`, and that includes zero and negative figures — the range is enforced by the receipt rule at position 30 of the ladder, not as a shape violation at position 3. A valid receipt moves the order `APPROVED → RECEIVED`, stores `received_quantity`, and credits the instrument's `protocol_stock_count` by exactly that number. Partial receipts are normal and do not reopen the order — `RECEIVED` is terminal.

### 3.5. Interpreter Support and the Psychologist's Calendar

When a family needs an interpreter present, an `IntakeCoordinator` or a `PsychServicesSupervisor` books an interpreter-supported session against the evaluation. The booking names the `evaluation_id`, the `language_code` being interpreted, the vendor-supplied `interpreter_name` (informational), and the interval as `session_start_at`, treated as inclusive, and `session_end_at`, treated as exclusive and required to be strictly after the start. The session opens in `SCHEDULED` status with `cancellation_reason` null, and `duration_minutes` is derived as the number of whole minutes between the two instants, any remaining seconds discarded. Beyond its own shape, a booking is gated on nothing but the `evaluation_id` resolving and the calendar check below: it is accepted whatever the evaluation's assessment posture and compliance posture, whether or not the evaluation's `interpreter_required` flag is set, whether or not an evaluator has been assigned yet, and whether or not the parent referral still holds consent. Nor is `language_code` checked against the referral's `primary_language` or against any format — it need only be a non-empty string, as must `interpreter_name`.

The session occupies the calendar of the psychologist assigned to that evaluation, and the building it occupies is the building of that evaluation's referral. Neither of those is supplied in the payload — both are resolved through the evaluation. Before a booking is accepted, the server checks the whole occupied calendar of that psychologist under **half-open interval semantics with a travel buffer**. Consider every other interpreter session whose status is `SCHEDULED` or `COMPLETED` and whose evaluation is assigned to the same psychologist. For each such existing session, the buffer is zero minutes when that session's building is the same building as the new session's, and fifteen minutes when the buildings differ, reflecting travel time between sites. The new booking conflicts when the interval running from fifteen minutes (or zero) before its `session_start_at` up to, but not including, fifteen minutes (or zero) after its `session_end_at` overlaps the existing session's own half-open interval from its `session_start_at` up to but not including its `session_end_at`. A conflict is rejected with `ERR_PSYCH_CALENDAR_CONFLICT`. Because the semantics are half-open, two sessions in the same building that merely touch — where the earlier session's end instant equals the later session's start instant — do **not** conflict; the same two intervals in different buildings do conflict, because the fifteen-minute travel buffer pushes them into overlap. If the evaluation has no psychologist assigned yet, there is no occupied calendar to compare against and the check finds nothing to conflict with.

A session leaves `SCHEDULED` in one of two ways. The district compliance sweep completes it — `SCHEDULED → COMPLETED` — once the current logical clock instant has reached or passed its `session_end_at`; that transition changes no field other than the status, `updated_at` and `version`, and, importantly, a `COMPLETED` session **still occupies** its interval on the psychologist's calendar for the purpose of the conflict check above. Alternatively a cascade cancels it — `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED` — either because the assigned psychologist went on leave, in which case `cancellation_reason` is `PSYCHOLOGIST_UNAVAILABLE`, or because consent was withdrawn, in which case `cancellation_reason` is `CONSENT_WITHDRAWN`. A `CANCELLED` session stops occupying the calendar, so a later booking over the same window succeeds where it would previously have been refused. Both `COMPLETED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal.

### 3.6. Testing, Scoring, and the Peer-Reviewed Report

Once an evaluation is `ASSIGNED`, its owning psychologist begins testing. Starting testing moves the evaluation `ASSIGNED → IN_TESTING` and names the `instrument_id` of the instrument being administered; the server records that identifier as the evaluation's `testing_instrument_id`. Only the psychologist named as the evaluation's `psychologist_id` may take this step, and the parent referral must still hold consent. Starting testing from any assessment posture other than `ASSIGNED` — from `PENDING_ASSIGNMENT`, or a second time from `IN_TESTING` — is rejected with `ERR_EVALUATION_STATE_INVALID`.

Three consumable gates then apply, in the order fixed by §2.11. The licensing gate comes first: the named instrument's `license_status` must be `ACTIVE`, and a scoring licence seat in `ASSIGNED` status must exist pairing that instrument with the acting psychologist's own `X-User-ID`. Either failing — an expired licence, or no seat held — is rejected with `ERR_SCORING_LICENSE_INVALID`. The interpreter gate comes second: when the evaluation's `interpreter_required` flag is true, at least one interpreter session of that evaluation must already exist in `SCHEDULED` or `COMPLETED` status, otherwise the request is rejected with `ERR_INTERPRETER_SESSION_MISSING`. Note that a cancelled session does not satisfy this gate, and that a completed one does. The inventory gate comes third: the instrument's `protocol_stock_count` must be at least its `protocol_units_per_administration` multiplied by the number of entries in the evaluation's `evaluation_domains`. Exact equality passes and drives the shelf to zero; anything less is rejected with `ERR_PROTOCOL_STOCK_INSUFFICIENT`. On success the same product is written to the evaluation's `protocol_units_consumed` and debited from the instrument's `protocol_stock_count`.

Recording scores moves the evaluation `IN_TESTING → SCORED` and stores the free-text `score_summary` supplied by the owning psychologist. Submitting the draft moves it `SCORED → REPORT_DRAFTED`, stores the free-text `report_narrative`, and — this is the part that matters for governance — stamps `report_drafted_by_psychologist_id` with the drafting psychologist's `X-User-ID`. Both steps are restricted to the evaluation's own `psychologist_id` and both require consent still on file; performing either from the wrong assessment posture is rejected with `ERR_EVALUATION_STATE_INVALID`.

Peer review is the only path to a final report, and the drafter can never approve their own work. A `SchoolPsychologist` other than the drafter, or a `PsychServicesSupervisor`, reviews a `REPORT_DRAFTED` evaluation by supplying a `decision` of either `APPROVE` or `RETURN`. If the reviewing `X-User-ID` equals the evaluation's `report_drafted_by_psychologist_id`, the request is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — no exception, no override, no supervisor bypass. When the reviewer acts as a `SchoolPsychologist`, their `X-User-ID` must resolve to an existing psychologist record, otherwise `ERR_PSYCHOLOGIST_NOT_FOUND` applies, and that record's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_PSYCHOLOGIST_STATE_INVALID` applies; a `PsychServicesSupervisor` reviews with an opaque identifier and is therefore subject only to the segregation comparison. Those two reviewer checks straddle the posture check: the reviewer's identifier is resolved first, but the `ACTIVE` test is taken *after* the evaluation's posture has been examined, so a review aimed at an evaluation that is not `REPORT_DRAFTED` reports `ERR_EVALUATION_STATE_INVALID` even when the reviewing psychologist is also `ON_LEAVE`. Review is also gated on consent, so a review attempted after consent was withdrawn is rejected with `ERR_CONSENT_NOT_ON_FILE`, and review from any posture other than `REPORT_DRAFTED` is rejected with `ERR_EVALUATION_STATE_INVALID`.

An `APPROVE` decision moves the evaluation `REPORT_DRAFTED → REPORT_FINAL`, which is terminal on the assessment dimension. That transition cascades: because `REPORT_FINAL` is outside the four open statuses, the assigned psychologist's `open_case_points` is re-derived and drops by exactly this evaluation's `caseload_points`, and from that new total the psychologist's `caseload_status`, `pressure_index_assigned_basis` and `pressure_index_clock_weighted` are all re-derived in turn. Note carefully that finalizing the report does **not** touch the evaluation's `compliance_status`: an evaluation may reach `REPORT_FINAL` while still `BREACHED`, and closing the compliance dimension is the job of the meeting hold or the remedy approval.

A `RETURN` decision sends the draft back — `REPORT_DRAFTED → SCORED` — and clears both `report_narrative` and `report_drafted_by_psychologist_id`, because the draft must be re-authored from scratch. The revision loop from `SCORED` to `REPORT_DRAFTED` and back to `SCORED` may repeat without limit; nothing in the product caps the number of returns. A returned draft leaves the caseload aggregates untouched, since `SCORED` and `REPORT_DRAFTED` are both open statuses carrying the same points.

### 3.7. Permissible Exclusions and the Moving Due Date

Not every day between consent and completion counts against the district. A `SchoolPsychologist` claims an exclusion window against one of their own evaluations by naming the `evaluation_id`, the `exclusion_type` establishing the statutory basis, the inclusive window bounds `start_date` and `end_date`, and an optional narrative `claim_note` that defaults to the empty string. The end date must be on or after the start date. The claiming psychologist must be the evaluation's `psychologist_id`, or the claim is rejected with `ERR_PSYCH_NOT_OWNER`, and the parent referral must hold consent, or it is rejected with `ERR_CONSENT_NOT_ON_FILE`. Those two are the *only* gates on a claim: neither the evaluation's assessment posture nor its compliance posture blocks a filing, and the claimed window is not required to overlap the statutory window or to respect the per-type ceiling at claim time — a window that lies outside the statutory window, or one longer than its type allows, is filed happily and is dealt with only at the approval described below. A fresh claim opens in `CLAIMED` status, records `days_claimed` as the inclusive calendar-day count of its window — a window whose start and end dates are the same day counts as one day — and leaves `days_credited` at zero, `decided_by_user_id` null and `decided_at` null. A claim on its own moves nothing: the clock is untouched while a claim sits in `CLAIMED`.

Four statutory bases exist and each carries its own per-claim day ceiling. `BREAK_CLOSURE`, covering scheduled district closures, is capped at fourteen days — two full weeks. `PARENT_UNAVAILABLE` is capped at ten days. `STUDENT_ABSENT_STREAK` is capped at one week, that is seven days. `TRANSFER_IN_TRANSIT`, covering a student in the middle of an inter-district transfer, is capped at five days.

A `PsychServicesSupervisor` decides a claim by supplying a `decision` of either `APPROVE` or `REJECT`. A decision against a claim that has already been decided — one already `APPROVED` or `REJECTED`, both of which are terminal — is rejected with `ERR_EXCLUSION_ALREADY_DECIDED`. An approval additionally requires that the evaluation's `compliance_status` is not one of the three closed postures `COMPLIANT_CLOSED`, `BREACH_CLOSED_REMEDIED` or `WAIVED`; crediting days to a closed clock is rejected with `ERR_EVALUATION_STATE_INVALID`.

**How many days a claim actually earns.** Approval computes `days_credited` by intersecting the claimed window with the evaluation's base statutory window and then applying the per-type ceiling. The base window is the closed window from the evaluation's `clock_start_date` through its `base_due_date` — the step-1 date of §2.6, before any exclusion credit, never the already-extended `statutory_due_date`. The overlap starts on the later of the claim's `start_date` and the evaluation's `clock_start_date`, and ends on the earlier of the claim's `end_date` and the evaluation's `base_due_date`. If that overlap end falls before the overlap start, the overlap is zero days; otherwise the overlap is the inclusive count of calendar days between them. `days_credited` is then the smaller of that overlap and the ceiling for the claim's `exclusion_type`. A claim that earns zero days at approval time — because its window lies entirely outside the statutory window — is rejected with `ERR_EXCLUSION_ZERO_DAYS`, and the claim stays `CLAIMED` so it can be corrected and re-decided. Overlapping claims are deliberately **not** merged: each approved claim earns its own credit independently, and the only ceiling on the total is the aggregate cap of step 3 in §2.6.

An approval cascades four levels, and every level must land in the same request; the response body is the updated exclusion claim, not the evaluation whose clock it moved. First, the claim records `days_credited`, `decided_by_user_id` from the deciding supervisor's `X-User-ID`, and `decided_at` from the current clock instant, and moves `CLAIMED → APPROVED`. Second, and caused by the first, the evaluation's `credited_exclusion_days`, `statutory_due_date`, `elapsed_statutory_days` and `days_remaining` are recomputed by the pinned seven-step resolution. Third, and caused by the second, the evaluation's `compliance_status` is re-derived from the new `days_remaining`: an evaluation may move `BREACHED → AT_RISK` when the credit pulls it back to between zero and ten days remaining inclusive, or `BREACHED → ON_TRACK` when the credit pulls it back past ten days remaining, and equally `AT_RISK → ON_TRACK` when more than ten days remain after the credit. This third level only ever *improves* the posture. Rank the three open postures `ON_TRACK` before `AT_RISK` before `BREACHED`; the freshly derived posture is written only when it ranks strictly better than the one already stored, and is otherwise discarded. So an approval never demotes an evaluation — if the logical clock has run on since the stored posture was last written, so that the re-derivation would land on `AT_RISK` or `BREACHED` where the record still reads `ON_TRACK`, the approval leaves the stored posture where it is and the demotion waits for the next compliance sweep. The evaluation record is nonetheless written on every approval, since its clock fields moved, so its `version` increments by one whether or not the posture changed. Fourth, and caused by the evaluation leaving `BREACHED`, any timeline breach of that evaluation whose status is `DETECTED` or `REMEDY_PROPOSED` is vacated and its `remedy_minutes_awarded` is driven to zero — a breach that has been cured by a legitimate exclusion no longer counts against the district. On each of these levels the assigned psychologist's `pressure_index_assigned_basis` and `pressure_index_clock_weighted` are re-derived, because the urgency weight attached to this evaluation has changed. The `BREACHED` posture is genuinely cyclic: an evaluation may leave it on one approval and re-enter it on a later sweep as the clock keeps running.

A rejection is far simpler. It moves the claim `CLAIMED → REJECTED`, records `decided_by_user_id` and `decided_at`, leaves `days_credited` at zero, and touches the evaluation's clock not at all.

### 3.8. Eligibility Meetings, Parent Notice, and Closure

An eligibility meeting is where the district makes its determination, and it is the only operation besides evaluator assignment that is version-guarded. An `IntakeCoordinator` or a `PsychServicesSupervisor` proposes a meeting by naming the `evaluation_id` whose determination it makes, the `school_id` of the hosting building, the interval `scheduled_start_at` and `scheduled_end_at` — the end instant strictly after the start — and the `expected_evaluation_version` required by §2.9. The meeting opens in `PROPOSED` status with `reschedule_count` at zero and `parent_notice_sent_at`, `notice_lead_days` and `held_at` all null. Creating a meeting increments the parent evaluation's `version` by one, which is exactly why the guard is needed: a client holding an older version is refused with `ERR_STALE_EVALUATION_VERSION`. That version guard aside, a proposal is gated only on its own shape, on both identifiers resolving and on the capacity check below — no assessment posture, no compliance posture and no consent state on the evaluation blocks it, and the hosting `school_id` need not be the building that raised the referral. The response body is the new meeting; the evaluation whose `version` the creation advanced is not returned.

A newly created meeting **supersedes** any earlier meeting of the same evaluation whose status is `PROPOSED`, `NOTICED` or `RESCHEDULE_REQUESTED`, moving each of them to the terminal `SUPERSEDED` status; a superseded interval stops occupying its hosting building's capacity. Because supersession is part of the same creation, the meetings being superseded have already released their slots when the capacity check below is taken, so a replacement meeting never conflicts with the meeting it replaces.

**Building capacity.** Every building declares a `concurrent_meeting_capacity`, and meeting intervals at a building are compared under **closed interval semantics** — both endpoints included, so two meetings that merely touch at an instant **do** conflict, in deliberate contrast to the half-open interpreter calendar of §3.5. The server counts the meetings at the same `school_id`, other than the one being written, whose status is `PROPOSED`, `NOTICED`, `RESCHEDULE_REQUESTED` or `HELD` and whose closed interval intersects the requested closed interval. If that count is greater than or equal to the building's `concurrent_meeting_capacity`, the request is rejected with `ERR_SCHOOL_MEETING_CAPACITY_FULL`. Superseded and cancelled meetings are outside the counted set and therefore free their slots. The same check runs on a reschedule request against the newly requested interval.

**Parent notice.** Families are entitled to advance written notice, and an `IntakeCoordinator` issues it. A notice may be issued from `PROPOSED` or re-issued from `RESCHEDULE_REQUESTED`, in both cases moving the meeting to `NOTICED`; from any other status the request is rejected with `ERR_MEETING_STATE_INVALID`. The statutory lead requirement is ten calendar days: the number of calendar days from the current clock date to the calendar date of `scheduled_start_at` must be at least ten. Exactly ten days passes; nine days fails with `ERR_NOTICE_LEAD_INSUFFICIENT`. A successful notice stamps `parent_notice_sent_at` with the current clock instant and stores that calendar-day difference as `notice_lead_days`. Re-issuing a notice after a reschedule overwrites both fields for the new date.

**Rescheduling.** A `NOTICED` meeting may be moved by an `IntakeCoordinator`, who supplies the replacement `scheduled_start_at` and `scheduled_end_at`; the meeting goes `NOTICED → RESCHEDULE_REQUESTED`. Rescheduling is bounded: a meeting may accept at most two reschedules, so the request is accepted only while `reschedule_count` is strictly below two and is rejected with `ERR_RESCHEDULE_LIMIT_REACHED` once it has reached two. The new interval must clear the hosting building's closed-interval capacity check, and per §2.11 that capacity check is evaluated *before* the reschedule ceiling. A successful reschedule overwrites the interval, increments `reschedule_count` by one, and clears both `parent_notice_sent_at` and `notice_lead_days`, because a fresh notice for the new date is mandatory — the cycle `NOTICED → RESCHEDULE_REQUESTED → NOTICED` is legitimate and bounded only by that ceiling of two.

**Holding the meeting.** A `PsychServicesSupervisor` records that a `NOTICED` meeting was held; from any other status the attempt is rejected with `ERR_MEETING_STATE_INVALID`, and `HELD` is terminal. Two cross-entity gates apply in the order of §2.11. The evaluation's `assessment_status` must be `REPORT_FINAL`, since a determination cannot be made without a finalized report; otherwise the attempt is rejected with `ERR_REPORT_NOT_FINAL`. And the meeting must actually be due: the current clock instant must be at or after `scheduled_start_at`, otherwise the attempt is rejected with `ERR_MEETING_NOT_DUE`. On success `held_at` is stamped with the current clock instant, and the closure cascade runs. If the evaluation's `days_remaining` is zero or greater at that moment, its `compliance_status` moves to the terminal `COMPLIANT_CLOSED` — from either `ON_TRACK` or `AT_RISK`. If `days_remaining` is negative the evaluation stays `BREACHED` and closure of the compliance dimension waits for the remedy path of §3.9; and if the compliance dimension is already one of the three closed postures it is left exactly as it stands. Either way the parent referral moves to the terminal `COMPLETED` status, since the determination has been made and no further consent event is meaningful — provided it is still `CONSENTED` or `CONSENT_WAIVED`; a referral that has already reached `CONSENT_WITHDRAWN` or `COMPLETED` is left untouched rather than rewritten. The response body is the held meeting.

### 3.9. Compliance Sweeps, Breach Detection, and Compensatory Remedies

The district's timeline posture is refreshed by a sweep that a `ComplianceOfficer` or a `DistrictAdmin` runs at the current logical clock. The sweep takes no input. It re-resolves the pinned seven-step clock for every evaluation whose `compliance_status` is `ON_TRACK`, `AT_RISK` or `BREACHED`, skipping the three closed postures entirely. Evaluations whose `assessment_status` is `REPORT_FINAL` or `CANCELLED` are not moved into `AT_RISK` or `BREACHED` by the sweep, because their work is over; they are nonetheless re-resolved and counted in `evaluated_count`, and the recovery move `AT_RISK → ON_TRACK` is still written for them — only the two demotions are suppressed, and a suppressed demotion leaves the stored posture exactly as it was and opens no breach. Posture then follows the thresholds of §2.6: an `ON_TRACK` evaluation with between zero and ten days remaining inclusive moves `ON_TRACK → AT_RISK`; an evaluation with negative days remaining moves `ON_TRACK → BREACHED` or `AT_RISK → BREACHED`; and an `AT_RISK` evaluation whose remaining days have risen back above ten moves `AT_RISK → ON_TRACK`. Because a posture change alters the urgency weight of an open evaluation, the sweep re-derives `pressure_index_assigned_basis` and `pressure_index_clock_weighted` for every `ACTIVE` psychologist. The sweep also completes interpreter sessions, moving every `SCHEDULED` session whose `session_end_at` has been reached or passed to `COMPLETED`.

Each newly breached evaluation opens exactly one breach record. The record names the `evaluation_id`, stamps `detected_at` with the clock instant of the detecting sweep, and freezes `breach_days` as the number of days the evaluation is past its statutory due date — that is, the absolute value of its negative `days_remaining`. `breach_days` is frozen at detection and is never recomputed by later clock movement, however far the clock subsequently runs. Its `severity` is classified from that same frozen count: `MINOR` when `breach_days` is five or fewer, `MODERATE` when it is between six and fifteen inclusive, and `SEVERE` when it is greater than fifteen. The sweep never opens a second breach for an evaluation that already carries one in `DETECTED`, `REMEDY_PROPOSED` or `REMEDY_APPROVED` status; only a vacated breach leaves the evaluation eligible for a fresh detection. A breach opens in `DETECTED` status with `remedy_basis`, `corrective_action_note`, `proposed_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` all null, `additional_minutes` at zero, and `remedy_minutes_awarded` at zero.

The sweep responds with a summary object rather than a stored entity. It reports `run_at`, the clock instant the sweep executed at; `evaluated_count`, how many evaluations it re-resolved; `at_risk_evaluation_ids` and `breached_evaluation_ids`, the identifiers it moved into each of those postures on this run; `breach_ids`, the identifiers of the breach records it opened; and `sessions_completed_count`, how many interpreter sessions it completed. All three identifier arrays list their entries in the order the sweep walked the evaluations — `created_at` ascending, creation order breaking ties — and each is an empty array when the run moved nothing. The summary is not a persisted resource, so it carries no `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` or `version`, and the envelope's `response_type` is `object`.

**Two ways to price a remedy.** Both compensatory-minute figures are computed and frozen at detection, from `breach_days` and the severity multiplier, which is one for `MINOR`, one and a quarter for `MODERATE`, and one and a half for `SEVERE`. Both are rounded to the nearest five minutes as described in §2.10 — divide by five, round the quotient half up to a whole number, multiply back by five. `minutes_breach_day_basis` charges the district one thirty-minute service block per breached calendar day: multiply `breach_days` by thirty minutes, multiply by the severity multiplier, then round to the nearest five. `minutes_missed_service_basis` charges whole missed service weeks instead: divide `breach_days` by seven and round that quotient **up** to a whole number of weeks, multiply by one hundred and fifty minutes — a week's service allocation — multiply by the severity multiplier, then round to the nearest five. The two bases are never reconciled and routinely disagree; the proposal picks one and the resulting award is final.

A `ComplianceOfficer` proposes the remedy on a `DETECTED` breach, supplying the `remedy_basis` — either `BREACH_DAY_BASIS` or `MISSED_SERVICE_BASIS` — an optional `additional_minutes` of discretionary time that defaults to zero, and a mandatory `corrective_action_note` narrating the corrective action the district will take. The breach moves `DETECTED → REMEDY_PROPOSED`, records those three inputs plus `proposed_by_user_id` from the officer's `X-User-ID`, and sets `remedy_minutes_awarded` to the frozen minutes for the chosen basis plus `additional_minutes`. That sum may not exceed 1,800 minutes — thirty hours of compensatory service — and a proposal that would exceed the ceiling is rejected outright with `ERR_REMEDY_MINUTES_EXCEED_CAP`; the award is **never** silently clamped down to the ceiling. Proposing against a breach that is not `DETECTED` is rejected with `ERR_BREACH_STATE_INVALID`.

Approval is a different role's job, and that separation is the point. A `DistrictAdmin` — never the proposing compliance officer's role — approves a `REMEDY_PROPOSED` breach, moving it to the terminal `REMEDY_APPROVED` status and recording `approved_by_user_id`. The approval cascades onto the evaluation, moving its `compliance_status` from `BREACHED` to the terminal `BREACH_CLOSED_REMEDIED`, and the assigned psychologist's pressure indices are re-derived because that evaluation no longer carries breach urgency. That cascade is conditional on the evaluation actually reading `BREACHED` at that moment: if an exclusion approval or some other movement has already pulled it back to `AT_RISK` or `ON_TRACK`, the breach still moves to `REMEDY_APPROVED` and records `approved_by_user_id`, but the evaluation's compliance dimension is left untouched. Approving a breach that is not `REMEDY_PROPOSED` — including one already approved, and including one that has been vacated — is rejected with `ERR_BREACH_STATE_INVALID`.

The alternative fork out of `DETECTED` is vacation, and it is driven only by system cascades: an exclusion approval that pulls `days_remaining` back to zero or above so the evaluation leaves `BREACHED`, or a consent withdrawal. From either `DETECTED` or `REMEDY_PROPOSED` the breach moves to the terminal `VACATED` status and its `remedy_minutes_awarded` is driven to zero, discarding any proposed award. A vacated breach no longer counts against the district, and no remedy action may be taken on it afterwards. `remedy_minutes_awarded` therefore reads zero in exactly two situations — while the breach is still `DETECTED`, and once it has been `VACATED` — and carries the awarded figure only in `REMEDY_PROPOSED` and `REMEDY_APPROVED`.
