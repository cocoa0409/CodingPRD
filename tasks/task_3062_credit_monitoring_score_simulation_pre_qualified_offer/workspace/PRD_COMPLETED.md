# Credit Monitoring Offer Matching API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Consumers who want to know where they stand financially, and lenders who want to reach them, currently meet in a marketplace that is opaque to both sides. This document specifies the API behind a credit monitoring and pre-qualified offer platform that closes that gap. The platform watches a consumer's bureau file on a soft-pull basis, explains what moved their score and why, projects what would happen if they took a specific action, and then matches them against lender programs whose targeting criteria they actually satisfy — all without releasing personally identifying information to a lender until the consumer has explicitly consented to it.

The commercial incentive to over-promise is real: a marketplace that shows a consumer every offer available will generate more clicks and more funded referrals in the short term, but it destroys trust the moment a consumer is declined after being told they were pre-qualified. Accordingly, the platform treats adverse-selection control as a first-class requirement rather than a feature. Offers may only be presented to a consumer when a hard-criteria re-validation still passes against their current data, when the modeled approval odds have not drifted outside the lender's declared band, and when the consumer has not already been saturated with that product category. Every presentation, render and referral is reconstructed from the business narrative in this document rather than from the API surface alone.

The system also carries a ledger obligation. Referral revenue is recognized only when a lender actually funds a referral, and is reversed when the lender claws that funding back inside a contractual window. Because those events arrive late — a funding can be reported days after the consent was granted, and a reversal weeks after that — the platform runs a scheduled reconciliation pass that catches up on anything the real-time path missed. That pass, plus the logical clock that drives every deadline and staleness rule, is described in §2.4 and again where each business rule depends on it.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the cross-cutting rules that apply to every resource in the platform. Everything here is contract-level and must be implemented identically across the surface.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting party, and `X-User-Role` declares their capacity. A request missing either header, or carrying a role string that is not one of the roles enumerated below, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and an HTTP 400 status. When the headers are well formed but the caller's role is not among the roles an endpoint admits, the request is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_FOR_ROLE` and an HTTP 403 status. The platform recognises exactly seven roles: `Consumer`, `Referee`, `BureauConnector`, `LenderOps`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `PlatformAdmin`, and `DataProtectionOfficer`.

Only one role is backed by a persisted resource. When `X-User-Role` is `Consumer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Consumer`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Consumer` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. In practice a `Consumer` addresses its own records through its persisted `id`; because a consumer row additionally remembers the opaque `X-User-ID` that enrolled it, either that enrolling identifier or the consumer's own `id` satisfies the ownership check, while any third value is refused. Every other role — `Referee`, `BureauConnector`, `LenderOps`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `PlatformAdmin` and `DataProtectionOfficer` — carries an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it where a transition writes an operator stamp, but it is never compared to any entity's `id`, and two operators holding the same role are interchangeable from an authorization standpoint.

Segregation of duties is enforced throughout. A `Consumer` self-enrolls, grants monitoring consent, requests pulls of their own monitoring views, runs simulations, creates pre-qualification decisions for themselves, generates and renders their own offers, and acknowledges their own score alerts; the alert threshold override is likewise theirs alone to set. A `Referee` is an operations referee and never owns consumer data: they promote an eligible decision to pre-qualified, drive dispute submission and acknowledgement, author content quality scores, set render policy, suppress score alerts, and re-derive a simulation against the settled variant. A `BureauConnector` performs scheduled pulls and replays cancelled ones and is deliberately barred from reading consumer identity data. `LenderOps` authors targeting criteria and records referrals, funding and declines. A `ComplianceOfficer` performs reversals, records lender compliance determinations, pauses and resumes programs, withdraws offers, and sets render policy jointly with a `PlatformAdmin`. A `PlatformAdmin` operates the catalog, lenders, programs, score tiers, the operations run, and the logical clock. A `DataProtectionOfficer` alone closes consumer accounts. The full per-endpoint mapping is given in §4.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints accept a `limit` and an `offset` query parameter. `limit` caps the number of items returned and defaults to 10 when omitted; it is never permitted to fall below 1 or to exceed 100. `offset` states how many items to skip and defaults to 0. Unless a subsection of §3 states otherwise, every list endpoint returns its resources ordered by `created_at` ascending, and one exception is called out in §3.2 for consumer-scoped alert listings. The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more`; these four fields are the pagination contract and appear on every list response.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock and the Scheduled Daily Run

The platform does not read wall-clock time. A single logical clock governs every deadline, staleness threshold, cooldown window, retention horizon and TTL rule in the system, and it begins at exactly `2026-09-01T00:00:00Z` when the server starts. An administrative capability advances it to a supplied instant carried in a `current_utc` field. Advancing the clock is the only side effect of that operation — no entity state changes as a direct result of the call. The clock is monotonic: setting it to an instant strictly earlier than its current value is rejected with `ERR_CLOCK_NOT_MONOTONIC`, while setting it to the value it already holds is accepted and treated as a no-op.

Time-dependent business rules are consolidated into one scheduled reconciliation pass that a `PlatformAdmin` requests by supplying a `run_key`. Each invocation creates an `OpsRun` row whose `status` begins at `RUNNING`, whose `requested_at` is stamped with the logical clock, and whose `stages_passed` array starts empty and accumulates the names of the constituent stages that reached `CONSTITUENT_COMPLETE`, in execution order. A `run_key` must be unique across all `OpsRun` rows; a repeat is rejected with `ERR_OPS_RUN_KEY_DUPLICATE`. At the end of the pass the run settles to `COMPLETED` when every constituent stage finished, or to `PARTIAL` when at least one did not, and `stages_passed` names either all stages or only those that completed. In this platform every constituent completes whenever the pass runs, so a run always settles at `COMPLETED` with all nine stage names accumulated in execution order; `PARTIAL` remains available in the model but the pass does not reach it. Recording the settle also bumps the run's own `updated_at` and `version`. Requesting a run while one is already `RUNNING` is permitted and simply evaluates whatever constituents are still active.

The constituents a run drives are: a mobility stage that supersedes stale snapshots, releases unfunded referrals, expires decisions and expires programs; a settlement stage that recognizes revenue for funded referrals and posts clawbacks for reversals inside their window; an adverse-selection stage that returns decisions pinned to a snapshot that is no longer usable; a consent-sealing stage that promotes pending consents in bounded batches; a retention stage that archives sealed consents past their horizon; a cap-window stage that resets presentation counters; a stale-offer stage that expires rendered offers; a calibration stage that recomputes every catalog item's calibration index; and a posture rollup that recomputes each consumer's monitoring posture and each consumer's render posture before settling the run itself. The specific thresholds each stage compares against are stated alongside the business rule they serve in §3, and the global values are collected in §3.7.

### 2.5. Soft-Pull Cadence and Snapshot Currency

A consumer enrols by submitting `full_name`, `email`, `state_code`, `bureau_pin`, and `dti_pct`. The response is created with a `201` status, and the platform forces the initial posture: `score_version` is 0, and `current_score`, `previous_score`, `utilization_pct` and `monitoring_consent_at` are all null, while `open_dispute_count` is 0. Enrollment alone does not authorize a pull. Monitoring begins only after the consumer grants consent, which stamps `monitoring_consent_at` with the logical clock and moves the consumer's monitoring posture to `MONITORING`.

Two guards bracket the consent grant. The Prior-Bureau-Snapshot Rule requires that the consumer's `score_version` still be 0 at the moment consent is granted; a consumer who has already been pulled cannot retroactively enrol, and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_PRIOR_BUREAU_SNAPSHOT_EXISTS`. The second guard is idempotence: if `monitoring_consent_at` is already non-null the grant is rejected with `ERR_MONITORING_CONSENT_ALREADY_GRANTED`. Both guards are part of the same self-transition — the consumer remains `ACTIVE` throughout, and only the monitoring posture changes. A grant is only accepted while the consumer is still `ACTIVE`; a grant attempted against any other consumer status is a state error. An accepted grant advances the consumer's `version`.

Bureau data arrives through a connector-driven refresh that supplies `score`, `added_account_count`, `total_revolving_balance_cents`, `credit_limit_total_cents`, and an ordered `factor_codes` array. Two preconditions gate the pull. The consumer must already hold monitoring consent, otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_MONITORING_NOT_CONSENTED`. And no `BureauSnapshot` for that consumer may have a `pulled_at` strictly later than the logical clock minus 24 hours (`PULL_COOLDOWN_HOURS`) — a pull attempted inside that cooldown is refused with `ERR_PULL_COOLDOWN_ACTIVE`. The same 24-hour cooldown governs the replay of a cancelled snapshot, measured back from that snapshot's own cancellation instant.

Each accepted pull first creates a `BureauSnapshot` row in status `PULL_REQUESTED` and then resolves it from the census. The submitted `factor_codes` array is treated as the pull-result census. When it carries at least one entry the pull proceeds, the row advances `PULL_REQUESTED → ACTIVE`, and the snapshot binds `score`, `added_account_count`, `total_revolving_balance_cents`, `credit_limit_total_cents` and the factor list from that census entry. When it carries no entries the pull is a census with nothing in it: the row is still written, but with status `CANCELLED`, and the request is refused with `ERR_PULL_RESULT_EMPTY`. An empty census fails before it can disturb anything else — no existing `ACTIVE` snapshot is superseded, the consumer's derived score fields are left exactly as they were, no score alert is evaluated, and `score_version` gains nothing for the cancelled row. A cancelled pull can be replayed, provided the cooldown has elapsed and the snapshot is genuinely in `CANCELLED` status; replaying anything else is a state error. Every accepted pull causes the previously `ACTIVE` snapshot for that consumer to be marked `SUPERSEDED`, and every pull advances the consumer's `version`.

Snapshot data itself is retired on a schedule. A snapshot whose `pulled_at` is at or before the logical clock minus 30 days (`SNAPSHOT_STALENESS_DAYS`) is superseded by the mobility stage of a daily run. The comparison is made at day granularity on the stored instant, so a pull that is exactly 30 days old is deactivated and one that is 29 days old is not.

### 2.6. Ordinal Ordering and Derived Consumer Posture

A consumer's monitoring state is derived, never authored. The `score_version` field is a monotonic pull counter equal to the number of that consumer's `BureauSnapshot` rows whose status is not `CANCELLED`, and the next accepted pull pins its `snapshot_ordinal` to `score_version + 1`. From that ordering the platform derives three read-only values: `current_score` is the `score` of the snapshot holding the highest `snapshot_ordinal` for the consumer regardless of that snapshot's later status, `previous_score` is the `score` of the snapshot holding the second-highest ordinal, and `utilization_pct` is the `overall_credit_utilization_pct` of the highest-ordinal snapshot. All three are null when the consumer has never been successfully pulled — `current_score` and `utilization_pct` when `score_version` is 0, and `previous_score` additionally while fewer than two counters exist. Because the highest-ordinal snapshot may be one that has since been superseded or cancelled, `previous_score` is the score of the next counted snapshot below that one, not necessarily the second-highest `snapshot_ordinal` overall: the two counters are taken over the snapshots whose status is not `CANCELLED`, in ordinal order.

Each snapshot carries a computed `overall_credit_utilization_pct` derived from its own balances, and a `pulled_at` equal to the logical clock instant of the pull. Each accepted pull also mints one `ScoreFactorExplanation` row per normalized factor code, with `rank_index` starting at 0 for the first factor and increasing by one for each subsequent factor, mirroring the position of that code in the snapshot's normalized array.

The consumer's `open_dispute_count` is likewise derived: it counts the `DisputeReferral` rows in status `SUBMITTED_OFFLINE` or `ACKNOWLEDGED_BY_BUREAU`. It is not decremented when a dispute is acknowledged, because acknowledgement is not a closure — only withdrawal removes a dispute from that count.

### 2.7. Numeric Precision and Rounding

Percentages, odds and indices in this system are stored and returned as numbers with an explicit decimal contract; monetary values are whole cents and are never rounded up. The governing rule is that every computed percentage or ratio is rounded half up at the stated precision and only once, at the point of return, never as an intermediate step.

Specifically: credit utilization is rounded half up to 2 decimal places; modeled approval odds are rounded half up to 2 decimal places; the calibration index and the two rates that feed it are rounded half up to 4 decimal places; and everything in the score-simulation walk is an integer count of points with no rounding at all. Monetary amounts — funded amounts, recognized revenue, clawback amounts, face value — are whole cents, with any division floored rather than rounded, and each share is floored independently before it is summed. A deposit of an assumption here is not optional: the floor-then-sum ordering for platform revenue and the sum-then-compare ordering for face value can produce different totals for identical input rows, and §3.8 specifies which is which.

Two specific rounding traps are called out explicitly because they are easy to get wrong. Utilization is 0.0, not null and not an error, when a snapshot reports a total revolving credit limit of zero. And modeled approval odds of exactly 0 or 1 must never be substituted for a genuine null — a null means "not modeled", not "no chance".

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Three write operations protect against a client acting on a consumer state it has not observed. Each of them requires an `expected_consumer_version` field in the request payload carrying the client's last-read value of the target consumer's `version`; the field is not optional on those operations. When the supplied value differs from the consumer's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` and an HTTP 412 status. The three protected operations are the bureau snapshot refresh, the consent completion that records a consumer's consent for a decision, and the score simulation create. A snapshot refresh is guarded because a later enrollment or consent grant may already have moved the consumer's monitoring posture; a consent completion is guarded because the grant must be issued against the version the server currently holds; and a simulation is guarded because a newer pull may have changed the score the projection is based on. Operations outside that list do not accept a version field and must not require one, even when they mutate consumer-scoped resources.

### 2.9. Nested Resource Retrieval and Path Validation

Identifiers appearing in a request path are validated left to right, and the first one that fails ends the request. A path carrying both a parent and a child identifier — for example a consumer identifier followed by a decision identifier, or a decision identifier followed by a consent identifier — resolves the parent before it looks at the child, so a bad parent shadows a bad child. The rejection is an HTTP 404 with `ERR_CONSUMER_NOT_FOUND`, raised for any path-segment identifier that does not resolve to a resource of the type the segment names, covering the `{id}`, `{consumer_id}`, `{decision_id}`, `{referral_id}`, `{offer_id}`, `{snapshot_id}`, `{dispute_id}`, `{simulation_id}` and `{alert_id}` placeholders alike. Creation operations follow the same order: the container is verified, then its rules, then the child payload. When a `Consumer` caller addresses another consumer's record — through the consumer read or that consumer's derived adverse-action summary — the record does not resolve for that caller and the request is rejected with `ERR_CONSUMER_NOT_FOUND` at HTTP 404.

### 2.10. Common Resource Properties

Resources marked as carrying the common field set expose four system-managed properties. `id` is a server-minted identifier formatted as a UUID with no type prefix attached to it. `created_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording first persistence, and `updated_at` uses that same three-fractional-digit form. Every other dated field a resource reports — the domain instants such as `monitoring_consent_at`, `pulled_at`, `evaluated_at`, `decided_at`, `fresh_until`, `consented_at`, `sealed_at`, `intent_recorded_at`, `render_intent_recorded_at`, `funded_at`, `reversed_at`, `recognized_at`, `go_live_at`, `expiry_at` and `requested_at` — echoes the stored instant at whole-second granularity, without fractional digits. `updated_at` moves on every mutation, including mutations performed by the server itself during a daily run rather than by a client request; the three-fractional-digit form of `api_request_timestamp` in the response envelope is separate from this and always carries its three digits. `version` is an integer that increments on every modification and is the anchor for the concurrency contract in §2.8. Sibling collections and child resources receive their own UUID `id` as well.

Two entity families are exceptions and carry only `id` and `created_at`. A `ScoreFactorExplanation` is immutable once written, so it has neither `updated_at` nor `version`. A `ConsentRecord`, likewise, carries only `id` and `created_at`: the record is written once at consent time and then transitions through its sealing lifecycle without versioned edits. A `RevenueRecognitionEntry` is an append-only ledger line and also carries only `id` and `created_at`. Because those three families have no `version`, no concurrency check may be applied to them, and no request that targets them may require a version field.

### 2.11. Validation Precedence

When more than one rule could fire on the same request, the platform resolves them in a fixed order rather than returning whichever check happened to run first. Authentication is evaluated first: missing or malformed `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` headers, or a role that is not a declared role, yield `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` at HTTP 400. Role authorization is second, yielding `ERR_FORBIDDEN_FOR_ROLE` at HTTP 403. Path-segment existence is third, yielding `ERR_CONSUMER_NOT_FOUND` at HTTP 404, and it is evaluated left to right as described in §2.9.

State validity is fourth and outranks everything that follows: a transition attempted from a status from which it is not declared is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` at HTTP 409, even when a business, consent or fiscal check would also have failed on the same request. Rankings below state validity are, in order: the expiry rule that bars consenting to or presenting a decision whose `fresh_until` has passed (`ERR_OFFER_EXPIRED`); the adverse-selection gate covering `presentation_policy_eligible` and `is_over_presentation` (`ERR_PRESENTATION_INELIGIBLE`); the rendering completeness gate (`ERR_RENDER_COMPLETENESS_INCOMPLETE`); the collateral re-validation of pinned criteria at presentation time (`ERR_COLLATERAL_ELIGIBILITY_CHANGED`); the consent gate requiring a `PENDING` consent record (`ERR_CONSENT_NOT_PENDING`); request shape and ledger arithmetic including malformed bodies, undeclared enum members, factor codes absent from every active tier's catalog, negative amounts and duplicate business references (`ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` at HTTP 400); and finally the clawback window check (`ERR_CLAWBACK_WINDOW_CLOSED`). The practical consequence of this ladder is that a referral which is both non-consented and carries a malformed funding amount reports the consent error, while a decision rejected from a terminal status reports the state error rather than any downstream gate.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

The subsections below narrate the platform's workflows end to end. Every endpoint lives in §4; here each operation is named by the state change it drives, by the role that performs it, or by the capability it provides.

### 3.1. Monitoring Enrollment, Bureau Pulls and Score Alerts

Enrollment is where the monitoring relationship begins, and the sequence matters. A `Consumer` supplies `full_name`, `email`, `state_code`, `bureau_pin` and `dti_pct`; `email` must be unique across `Consumer` rows, and `state_code` is a two-letter code that will later determine which lenders may serve this person. A newly enrolled consumer reads as untouched: the monitoring posture is `ENROLLED`, no consent timestamp exists, and every score-derived field is empty. Between enrollment and the first pull, exactly one intermediate step is permitted, and it is the consent grant described in §2.5 — the platform will refuse a consent granted after any bureau data has landed, and will equally refuse a second grant.

Pulling is the connector's job and it is deliberately narrow. A `BureauConnector` submits `score`, `added_account_count`, `total_revolving_balance_cents`, `credit_limit_total_cents`, and an ordered `factor_codes` array, and receives the newly bound `BureauSnapshot` back. The submitted factor codes are normalized to lowercase snake_case and deduplicated on ingest in first-occurrence order, which is the array whose positions the explanation rows mirror. Once at least one score tier is `ACTIVE`, every submitted code must appear somewhere in the `factor_catalog` of one of those tiers; while none is active, the platform does not enforce that membership at all. A cancelled pull is not a dead end: a `BureauConnector` may replay it once the cooldown described in §2.5 has elapsed, which reactivates the row, supersedes whatever snapshot is currently active for the consumer, re-mints one factor explanation per code already carried on that snapshot, and recomputes the consumer's derived score fields exactly as a fresh pull would. The replay carries no payload of its own, so the alert it evaluates stores a null `channel`.

Every pull — including a replay — triggers the alert evaluation, and the rule is worth stating precisely because its boundaries are inclusive. The effective threshold is the larger of the consumer's `threshold_override_points` and the platform default of 20 points (`SCORE_ALERT_DEFAULT_THRESHOLD`), so an override can only ever tighten alerting, never loosen it. The raw movement is the consumer's current score minus the previous score. If there is no previous score at all — the very first successful pull for that consumer — the movement comparison is skipped entirely and the alert's `alert_kind` is set to `SCORE_FLAT` regardless of magnitude. Otherwise, a movement that reaches the effective threshold in the positive direction sets `alert_kind` to `SCORE_UP`; a movement whose negation reaches the effective threshold — that is, a drop of at least the effective threshold — sets it to `SCORE_DOWN`; and anything smaller in either direction sets it to `SCORE_FLAT`. The alert records the `threshold_used`, which is the effective threshold actually applied, alongside `from_score` and `to_score` taken from the consumer's previous and current score at evaluation time.

One `ScoreAlert` row is written for every pull, with no exceptions. `SCORE_UP` and `SCORE_DOWN` rows begin in status `PENDING` and are directed at the `channel` the caller requested, which is one of `EMAIL`, `PUSH` or `SMS`. A `SCORE_FLAT` row is written already in status `SUPPRESSED`, and the requested channel is stored on it unchanged rather than being zeroed out. The `channel` is read from the same request that supplied the factor codes, and a caller that supplies none leaves the alert's `channel` null. The row's `evaluated_at` records the logical clock instant of the evaluation, and its `consumer_id` ties it to the monitored individual. A cancelled pull never reaches the evaluation, so no alert and no factor explanation is written for it. Alongside the alert, each accepted pull writes one explanation per normalized factor code, binding the code to a `direction` of `UP`, `DOWN` or `NEUTRAL`, a signed `impact_points`, and a `rank_index` that starts at 0 for the first code in the normalized array.

### 3.2. Alert Follow-Through, Factor Explanations and Consumer-Facing Views

An alert is not self-closing. The consumer who receives it acknowledges it, which moves a `PENDING` alert to `DELIVERED`; an operations `Referee` may instead suppress a `PENDING` alert, moving it to `SUPPRESSED`. Both actions require the alert to still be `PENDING`, and any other starting status is a state error. Suppression also happens without any human in the loop: when a daily run observes that the owning consumer's monitoring posture is not `COMPLETE`, every one of that consumer's `PENDING` alerts is moved to `SUPPRESSED`, and crucially the row is retained rather than deleted, so an audit can still see that an alert was raised and withheld. A consumer who prefers a narrower trigger can set their own `threshold_override_points` through a consumer-scoped upsert; the platform stores one such override per consumer, and the effective threshold becomes the larger of that override and the platform default of 20 points, exactly as §3.1 describes.

Explanations are read back per snapshot and globally, and every one of them carries the `snapshot_id` it belongs to, the `factor_code`, its `direction` and `impact_points`, and its `rank_index`. The direction and impact are never authored by the caller — they are derived from the code through the platform's scoring factor table, along with the `factor_catalog` that each active score tier declares. A consumer-scoped alert listing is the one list in the system that does not fall back to pure creation order: it is ordered by `created_at` ascending and then by `evaluated_at` ascending, so two alerts born in the same instant still read in the order they were evaluated. The alert listing also accepts a `status` filter, and the decision listing accepts a `decision_status` filter, both of which select on the exact enumerated status strings rather than on any derived label.

The consumer-facing credit-health view is assembled from the derived values of §2.6 plus two volatility measures. Volatility is computed over every snapshot of the consumer whose status is `ACTIVE` or `SUPERSEDED`, read in `snapshot_ordinal` ascending order. The successive differences between adjacent snapshots' scores form a delta series; `volatility_points` is the largest delta in that series minus the smallest, and it is 0 when fewer than two deltas exist — that is, when the consumer has fewer than three snapshots. `drift_points` is the last snapshot's score minus the first snapshot's score, a plain net movement across the consumer's whole observed history. The view also republishes `consumer_id`, `current_score`, `previous_score`, `factor_codes`, `open_dispute_count`, a `tier_code`, and the list of `education_topics_consumed` the consumer has already been taught. The `factor_codes` it carries are derived from the highest-ordinal snapshot's own normalized factor array, not supplied by the caller, and the `education_topics_consumed` list is the set of distinct education topics for the consumer's disputes that have been submitted or acknowledged, ordered as a sorted list. Nothing in this view is stored; it is recomputed on every read.

Content completeness is tracked per consumer, per `topic`. A `Referee` or a `PlatformAdmin` creates a `ContentQualityFacet` for one of the two scorable topics — `factor_explanations` or `accuracy_education` — with an authoring `score_bps` between 0 and 10000, and the facet's `status` is set to `COMPLETE` when that score is at least 8000, and to `INCOMPLETE` otherwise. Facets are created in `INCOMPLETE`. An operations `Referee` may later rescore an existing facet, which requires the facet to already be `COMPLETE` and simply updates `score_bps` in place and recomputes the status; scoring a facet up from below is done by creating it at a passing score, and attempting to rescore an `INCOMPLETE` facet is a state error. When a facet falls back below the 8000-point line, or when a daily run finds the consumer's monitoring posture to be `IMMATURE`, every facet for that consumer is knocked back to `INCOMPLETE` — the render posture that accompanies that fall is described in §3.4.

### 3.3. Score Simulation and What-If Projection

Simulation lets a consumer see the consequence of a hypothetical action before taking it. The consumer submits an `actions` array, which is an ordered list of one to five `{ action_code, amount_cents }` objects; the length bound is enforced as a request-shape rule, so an array of zero entries or of six entries is malformed. The four recognised action codes are `PAY_DOWN_REVOLVING`, `CLOSE_OLDEST_TRADELINE`, `INCREASE_CREDIT_LIMIT`, and `OPEN_NEW_TRADELINE`, and `amount_cents` is meaningful only for the first and third of those. A simulation requires the consumer to have at least one successful bureau pull; a consumer whose `score_version` is 0 is refused with `ERR_SIMULATION_NEEDS_SNAPSHOT`, and so is a consumer who has counted pulls but no currently `ACTIVE` snapshot to project against.

At creation the row pins the facts the projection rests on, so the result stays reproducible: `baseline_ordinal` is the consumer's `score_version` at simulation time, `baseline_score` is their `current_score`, and `base_utilization_pct` is their `utilization_pct`. The row then evaluates synchronously through the `PROJECTED` variant of the projection walk and is created already in status `FINALIZED`; the intermediate `DRAFT` status exists only as the instant before evaluation and, when the consumer has no snapshot at all, the row is written `FAILED` with `projected_score`, `delta_points` and `tier_code` all null. The recorded baseline fields are the pins captured at creation, but the walk itself always draws the score, the utilization and the balances from the consumer's current state and current `ACTIVE` snapshot, so a later re-derivation of the same simulation can differ from the values first pinned. A `Referee` or a `PlatformAdmin` re-derives a `FINALIZED` simulation in place against the consumer's current score and active snapshot by replaying its stored action list, recomputing `projected_score`, `delta_points` and `tier_code`; a simulation that is not `FINALIZED`, or a consumer now without an active snapshot, is refused. A new simulation also supersedes whatever `DRAFT` simulation the consumer had, and that predecessor's `projected_score` and `delta_points` are left frozen at the values they already computed — a superseded draft is never recomputed.

The projection itself walks the action list in submission order, applying each action to a running pair of a score value and a utilization percentage, so the order of the array genuinely changes the answer. The walk starts from the consumer's current score and current utilization. A `PAY_DOWN_REVOLVING` action with a given `amount_cents` reduces the utilization by that amount expressed in cents multiplied by 100 and divided by the snapshot's total revolving credit limit, floored at zero — utilization can never go negative — and then adds 5 points (`UTIL_OPEN_POINTS_PER_ACTION`) to the score. A `CLOSE_OLDEST_TRADELINE` action subtracts 12 points (`CLOSE_OLDEST_TRADELINE_PENALTY_POINTS`). An `INCREASE_CREDIT_LIMIT` action recomputes the utilization from scratch against an enlarged denominator, the snapshot's total revolving credit limit plus the action's `amount_cents`, using the snapshot's total revolving balance as the numerator, and then adds 8 points (`LIMIT_INCREASE_BASE_BONUS`) plus 2 further points (`LIMIT_INCREASE_STEP_BONUS`) for each complete group of 10 utilization percentage points in the resulting utilization. An `OPEN_NEW_TRADELINE` action subtracts 9 points (`OPEN_NEW_TRADELINE_PENALTY_POINTS`).

Only after every action has been applied does the terminal utilization adjustment land: the score gains 3 points (`UTIL_TERMINAL_STEP_POINTS`) for each complete group of 10 utilization percentage points in the final utilization, minus 3 points for each complete group of 10 points in the consumer's original utilization. The result is then clamped into the platform's score range, which runs from a floor of 300 (`SCORE_MIN`) to a ceiling of 850 (`SCORE_MAX`); the clamped value becomes `projected_score`, and `delta_points` is that value minus the consumer's current score. Every quantity in this walk is a whole number of points, and each group-of-ten term is floored to a whole number before the points are summed. Finally, the projected score is placed into a tier by looking at every active `ScoreTierThreshold` sorted by `min_score` ascending and then `max_score` ascending, and taking the `tier_code` of the first tier whose inclusive band contains the projected score — the lookup stops at that first match even if a later tier would also contain the value. Tier codes are drawn from `EXCEPTIONAL`, `VERY_GOOD`, `GOOD`, `FAIR` and `POOR`.

### 3.4. Render Posture, Presentation Caps and the Integrity of What Is Shown

Rendering is the moment the platform decides whether a consumer's dashboard is trustworthy enough to display an offer, and it is governed by a consumer-scoped `RenderPolicy` whose `render_posture` is one of `RENDER_OPEN`, `RENDER_FACETS_INCOMPLETE` or `RENDER_RESTRICTED`. A consumer begins in `RENDER_FACETS_INCOMPLETE`. The posture is recomputed whenever the render policy is written and by the rollup; it is not recomputed when a facet is created or rescored, so a consumer whose facets have just come complete holds `RENDER_FACETS_INCOMPLETE` until a daily run observes it. Daily runs move the posture along these rules: it becomes `RENDER_OPEN` when `whole_render_completeness` holds for that consumer — which requires that both scorable topics, `factor_explanations` and `accuracy_education`, are represented among that consumer's facets and that every constituent facet is `COMPLETE`; it falls to `RENDER_RESTRICTED` when the consumer's monitoring posture is `IMMATURE`, and that fall additionally forces every one of the consumer's `ContentQualityFacet` rows from `COMPLETE` back to `INCOMPLETE`; and it returns to `RENDER_OPEN` from `RENDER_RESTRICTED` once the monitoring posture climbs back to `COMPLETE`. Whenever the facets for a consumer are not all complete and the posture is not otherwise restricted, it settles at `RENDER_FACETS_INCOMPLETE`. The `IMMATURE` precedence is evaluated first and unconditionally; the other two rules are then decided by an effective completeness test that treats `content_quality_override` as making completeness hold. That override therefore also governs whether the posture climbs to `RENDER_OPEN`, whereas `blanket_presentation_override` does not — it lifts only the render-time completeness gate described below and leaves the posture untouched.

Two consumer-level overrides exist, and each is deliberately narrower than the other. `blanket_presentation_override` defaults to false; setting it true lets an offer that is merely `PRESENTED` render for optimisation purposes before every constituent facet has reached `COMPLETE` — but it waives only the completeness facet and explicitly does not waive the content-quality facet, which is why the daily run's overridden renders still leave the content-quality requirement standing, and why such a render happens only when the consumer's facets already satisfy the 8000-point minimum on both scorable topics. `content_quality_override` defaults to false and waives only the 8000-point content quality minimum, never the completeness facet. Both overrides belong to the render policy written through the consumer-scoped render-policy call, and a policy is created on that call when the consumer does not yet have one. Whichever overrides are in force, the posture itself is recomputed and stored on the policy.

Presentation saturation is tracked per consumer and per product kind, not per program. The platform keeps one `OfferPresentationCap` row for each `consumer_id` and `product_kind` pair — the pair is unique, so a second row for the same pairing is not permitted — and the cap's `presentation_cap_bps` defaults to 5000, meaning that product kind may account for at most half the offers rendered in the current window. The `presented_count` starts at 0 and is a computed count of the offers in that product kind that have actually been rendered for the consumer during the rolling window. The cap is breached when `presented_count` is strictly greater than the product kind's basis points multiplied by the total offer count and divided by 10000, with the result floored. The cap's own breach flag — the one the daily run recomputes and the cap listing reports — is divided by the consumer's total non-withdrawn offer count, because an offer that has been withdrawn should not keep inflating the denominator; the separate over-representation test consulted when a decision is promoted counts every offer regardless of status, and a consumer who has no cap row yet is judged against the default basis points and a zero presented count. A daily run resets every cap's `presented_count` to 0 and recomputes the breach flag, which is what makes the window roll.

### 3.5. Pre-Qualification: Matching a Consumer to a Lender Program

Pre-qualification is where the marketplace decides, quietly and without transmitting anything, whether a lender would want this consumer. The catalog side of that match is authored first. A `PlatformAdmin` creates a `ProductCatalogItem` with a `product_kind` of `CARD`, `LOAN` or `INSURANCE`, a `product_name`, an inclusive `min_score` and `max_score` band, and an inclusive `max_dti_pct`, and it starts in `DRAFT`. Activating it requires that at least one `ACTIVE` `ScoreTierThreshold` intersects its score band; without that intersection the attempt fails with `ERR_ITEM_NO_SCORE_TIER`. Retiring an active item moves it to `RETIRED` and is refused while any `ACTIVE` `LenderProgram` still references it, failing with `ERR_ITEM_HAS_ACTIVE_PROGRAM`. A `PlatformAdmin` also creates `ScoreTierThreshold` rows, each with a unique `tier_code`, an inclusive `min_score` and `max_score` band, and an ordered `factor_catalog` of the factor codes the platform understands. A tier may only be activated if its `tier_code` is not already held by another active tier and if its band does not overlap any other active tier's band, and attempting either is rejected with `ERR_TIER_CODE_DUPLICATE` or `ERR_TIER_BAND_OVERLAP` respectively. A tier that is retired leaves `ACTIVE` for the terminal `RETIRED` status. The catalog is what makes a factor code legitimate: a submitted `factor_codes` entry that appears in no active tier's `factor_catalog` is malformed input.

Lenders are the counterparties. A `PlatformAdmin` registers a `Lender` with a `lender_name` and a `states_served` list of two-letter codes; the lender starts in status `ONBOARDING` with `compliance_status` `APPROVED`. Activation moves it to `ACTIVE` and requires the compliance status to still be `APPROVED`, otherwise the attempt fails with `ERR_LENDER_NOT_COMPLIANT`. Offboarding moves it to `OFFBOARDED` and is refused while any `ACTIVE` program is still sponsored by it, failing with `ERR_LENDER_HAS_ACTIVE_PROGRAM`. A `ComplianceOfficer` or a `PlatformAdmin` records compliance determinations through a lender-scoped call carrying a `compliance_status` of `APPROVED`, `SUSPENDED` or `BANNED`; any other member of that enum is malformed input. The `BANNED` determination is the one with a blast radius: every `ACTIVE` program sponsored by that lender is paused, and each paused program in turn cascades its own eligible decisions to `INELIGIBLE`.

A `LenderProgram` binds a lender to a catalog item and carries the lender's actual underwriting appetites. It is created by a `PlatformAdmin` with a `lender_id`, a `product_catalog_item_id`, a `program_name`, a unique `program_code`, an inclusive `min_modeled_score`, an inclusive `max_dti_pct`, an inclusive `max_eligible_utilization_pct`, an `annual_fee_cents`, an `intro_apr_bps`, a `go_live_at` and an `expiry_at`, and the commercial terms `revenue_share_bps`, `clawback_rate_bps` and `clawback_days`. It starts in `DRAFT` and is activated by a `PlatformAdmin`, which requires the sponsoring lender to be `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_LENDER_NOT_ACTIVE` — and the `program_code` to be unique among the programs that are already `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_PROGRAM_CODE_DUPLICATE`. A `ComplianceOfficer` or `PlatformAdmin` may pause an active program at any time; pausing cascades every decision pinned to that program whose status is `ELIGIBLE`, `PREQUALIFIED` or `CONSENTED` to `INELIGIBLE`, stamping the reason `PROGRAM_PAUSED` and nulling the modeled odds. Resuming a paused program returns it to `ACTIVE` but again requires the sponsoring lender to still be `ACTIVE`. A program expires through the daily run once the logical clock reaches its `expiry_at`, whether it was `ACTIVE` or `PAUSED` at the time, and the deliverability of a program at any instant is decided by whether the clock falls inside the inclusive interval running from `go_live_at` to `expiry_at`.

Targeting criteria are the lender's stated constraints, authored by a `LenderOps` operator or a `PlatformAdmin` against a specific program. Each criterion names a `criterion_field` drawn from `SCORE`, `DTI_PCT`, `UTILIZATION_PCT` or `STATE_CODE`, an `operator` drawn from `GTE`, `LTE`, `EQ` or `IN`, a `criterion_value` that is a numeric string for the three numeric fields and a two-letter code for `STATE_CODE`, and an `is_hard_requirement` boolean. The boolean is the whole point: when it is true, a failed match disqualifies the program outright and the consumer can never see an offer from it, whereas when it is false the failure does not disqualify anything — it simply lowers the modeled odds the platform reports. A lender that has not listed the consumer's `state_code` in its `states_served` list cannot serve them at all, and a decision created for such a program is refused with `ERR_LENDER_SERVES_STATE_REQUIRED`; separately, an exclusion carried by a hard `STATE_CODE` criterion on a program the consumer is otherwise being matched against is not raised as its own error but is recorded on the decision as the reason `CONSUMER_STATE_EXCLUDED`, alongside its `INELIGIBLE` status; the lender-served-state gate above is the one that refuses the request outright.

The decision itself is created by the consumer and produced by the modeled-odds calculation, which is evaluated over the pinned snapshot and the program's criteria. Criteria are evaluated in a pinned order that does not depend on the order they were authored in: sorted by `criterion_field` ascending, then `operator` ascending, then `criterion_value` ascending. The walk counts how many hard criteria matched, how many hard criteria failed, how many soft criteria matched, and how many soft criteria there were in total. If any hard criterion failed, the calculation yields nothing and `modeled_odds_pct` is null, the decision lands in `INELIGIBLE`, and `disqualifying_criteria` is populated with one entry per failed hard criterion — each entry carrying that criterion's `criterion_field`, `operator` and `criterion_value` — sorted by `criterion_field` ascending. Otherwise the odds begin at a base of 0.90 (`ODDS_BASE`) and are adjusted as follows. The running value is reduced by 0.04 (`ODDS_HARD_MATCH_BONUS`) for each hard criterion that matched. When there is at least one soft criterion, the value is further reduced by 0.06 (`ODDS_SOFT_MATCH_BONUS`) multiplied by the fraction of soft criteria that matched. Then, if the pinned snapshot's overall credit utilization exceeds 30.0 percent (`ODDS_UTILIZATION_FREE_PCT`), the value is increased by 0.05 (`ODDS_UTILIZATION_PENALTY_PER_10PCT`) for each complete group of 10 percentage points by which it exceeds that free allowance. Finally the value is capped at a maximum of 0.99 (`ODDS_MAX`) and floored at a minimum of 0.01 (`ODDS_MIN`), and the floored result is rounded half up to 2 decimal places to become `modeled_odds_pct`.

The decision that comes out of that walk is pinned to the snapshot it used. It records `snapshot_id` and `snapshot_ordinal_used`, and it stamps `decided_at` with the logical clock and `fresh_until` with `decided_at` plus 30 days. Its `reason` is `NOT_PREQUALIFIED` when the odds were computed normally, `HARD_CRITERIA_FAILED` when a hard criterion failed, `CONSUMER_STATE_EXCLUDED` when the failure was a state exclusion, and `NO_ACTIVE_SNAPSHOT` when no usable snapshot existed. The `modeled_odds_settled` flag starts false, because odds derived from a bureau snapshot alone are provisional — they model what the lender is expected to do, not what it did. It stays false for as long as the decision is `ELIGIBLE` or `PREQUALIFIED`, and only becomes true once a realised terminal referral outcome has been folded back onto that same decision. When the flag is true, a non-null `modeled_odds_pct` is reported as the integer 0 or 100 — the underlying provisional odds mapped below 0.5 to 0 and otherwise to 100 — so one and the same bureau snapshot can report an odds of 0.91 provisionally and 100 once the outcome is known, while the underlying provisional value itself is retained and feeds the calibration mean unchanged. The flag is also what separates the two calibration variants described in §3.8.

A decision's lifecycle is `NEEDS_SNAPSHOT → ELIGIBLE → INELIGIBLE | PREQUALIFIED → CONSENTED | CONSUMED | EXPIRED`, and each branch of it has its own trigger. A consumer-requested evaluation that runs while there is no usable snapshot leaves the decision where it was, at `NEEDS_SNAPSHOT`, with reason `NO_ACTIVE_SNAPSHOT` and null odds. When a decision is re-evaluated and its pinned snapshot is no longer active, it is re-pinned to the consumer's current active snapshot and the evaluation is repeated from the top. Repeated evaluations of the same consumer and program pair are idempotent: no second decision row is created. A decision that is already `ELIGIBLE` may be re-evaluated in place, which refreshes `decided_at`, recomputes `fresh_until` as that instant plus 30 days, and recomputes the modeled odds, leaving `modeled_odds_settled` false. An operations `Referee` promotes an eligible decision to `PREQUALIFIED` through a qualifying action, which requires the decision to be `ELIGIBLE` and requires that the consumer not already be over-presented for that product kind — an over-represented pair is refused with `ERR_QUALIFY_OVER_REPRESENTED`.

Three further transitions arrive from outside the consumer's control. A daily run returns any `ELIGIBLE` decision whose pinned snapshot is no longer active back to `NEEDS_SNAPSHOT`, with reason `NO_ACTIVE_SNAPSHOT` and null odds, surfacing `ERR_SNAPSHOT_MOVED` semantics on the next read. This return is keyed on the pinned snapshot's status alone: a program that has since been paused or expired does not re-route an `ELIGIBLE` decision through the lender-served-state check, so a program change never reclassifies a live decision as state-excluded, and a decision already cascaded to `INELIGIBLE` is not rescued by a program resuming. The lender-served-state refusal is raised only while a decision is first being evaluated against a snapshot. A daily run also rescues decisions stuck in `PREQUALIFIED` when no consent record was ever created for them; among those, only a decision whose pinned snapshot is no longer `ACTIVE` is returned to `ELIGIBLE` with reason `NO_ACTIVE_SNAPSHOT`, while one still pinned to an `ACTIVE` snapshot is left `PREQUALIFIED`. And a fresh bureau pull re-pins any `PREQUALIFIED` decision to the new active snapshot and recomputes its odds against it, returning the decision to `ELIGIBLE` — a consent captured against the old snapshot cannot survive a score change, which is the platform's central serialization point between monitoring and the offer marketplace. Expiry closes the branch permanently: once `fresh_until` has passed, the daily run moves the decision to `EXPIRED` and no consent record may thereafter be created for it. Note also the two distinct no-snapshot conditions named by `ERR_SHARED_CRITERION_NO_SNAPSHOT` and `ERR_SNAPSHOT_MOVED`; both are catalogued, and the surfaced behaviour follows the state paths above — a decision whose pinned snapshot is gone is moved to `NEEDS_SNAPSHOT` with reason `NO_ACTIVE_SNAPSHOT` rather than answered with a distinct code, while a snapshot that merely stopped being active is the `ERR_SNAPSHOT_MOVED` case handled above.

### 3.6. Consent, Sealing and the Data Boundary

The platform's promise is that a lender learns nothing about a consumer until the consumer says it may. Consent is the instrument of that promise, and it is granted per decision, never in bulk. A `Consumer` or an operations `Referee` completes consent for a specific decision by supplying a `consent_method` of `ELECTRONIC` or `WRITTEN` and a `consent_scope` of `SHARE_PII_WITH_LENDER` — the only scope that unlocks the stored routing netloc — along with the `expected_consumer_version` that §2.8 requires. The decision must be `PREQUALIFIED` before consent can be recorded; consenting to anything else is rejected with `ERR_CONSENT_DECISION_NOT_PREQUALIFIED`. The operation creates a `ConsentRecord` in status `PENDING` with `archive_status` `LIVE`, stamps `consented_at` with the logical clock, and moves the decision to `CONSENTED`. It also advances the consumer's `version`.

Sealing is when the data actually becomes transmissible, and it is deliberately deferred. A `PENDING` consent becomes `SEALED` when the daily run promotes it, or immediately when a funding or a referral attempt consumes it. At the sealing instant the platform mints a sealed personal-information blob into `consumer_pii_ciphertext`, stamps `lender_routing_netloc` with the sponsoring program's lender endpoint, and records `sealed_at`. Until that instant, `consumer_pii_ciphertext` is null and the blob is withheld from every read — a consent record read before sealing carries no ciphertext at all. Sealing is rate-limited by design: a daily run seals at most `CONSENT_SEAL_MAX_PER_RUN` pending consents per pass, chosen in `created_at` ascending order and, for ties, by `id` ascending. This bound exists so that a large enrollment wave cannot fling consumer data outward all at once.

A consent that will never be used does not linger. When a referral is marked unfunded by the daily run, or when a `LenderOps` operator declines it, the linked consent moves from `PENDING` to `NOT_SEALED` and its ciphertext is never minted at all. A `PREQUALIFIED` decision whose consent was released in this way stays `CONSENTED`; only the consent record changes. Closing a consumer account is the other path: a `DataProtectionOfficer` may close a consumer, which archives the consumer, transitions every non-terminal consent record for that consumer to `NOT_SEALED`, supersedes every non-cancelled snapshot, returns every decision still `ELIGIBLE` or `PREQUALIFIED` to `ELIGIBLE`, and recomputes the monitoring posture. The close itself is gated twice — it is refused with `ERR_CONSUMER_HAS_OPEN_REFERRAL` if any referral for that consumer is in a status other than one of the four terminal referral statuses `FUNDED`, `REVERSED`, `DECLINED` or `UNFUNDED`, and refused with `ERR_CONSUMER_HAS_OPEN_DECISION` if any decision is still `NEEDS_SNAPSHOT`, `ELIGIBLE`, `INELIGIBLE` or `PREQUALIFIED`. A closed consumer is untouched thereafter.

Sealed records do not live forever. The daily run's retention stage archives any consent record that has reached `SEALED` status and whose `sealed_at` is at or before the logical clock minus 365 days (`RETENTION_DAYS`), flipping its `archive_status` to `ARCHIVED`. An archived record is still queryable, but only through the archived filter on the consent listing. Records that were never sealed are not swept by the retention rule; they are closed out by the release paths above.

### 3.7. Presenting, Rendering and Withdrawing Offers

A pre-qualified decision is not yet an offer. The consumer converts one into the other by presenting the decision, which creates a `PreQualifiedOffer` in status `PRESENTED` carrying the `decision_id` that produced it, the `consumer_id` that owns it, and the `lender_program_id` that sponsors it. The `product_kind` is copied across from the sponsoring program's catalog item, and the caller supplies a `channel` of `WEB`, `MOBILE_APP` or `MARKETPLACE`. Three gates stand in front of the create. The decision must be `PREQUALIFIED`; anything else is a state error. The decision's `fresh_until` must not have passed, or the create is refused with `ERR_OFFER_EXPIRED`. And the adverse-selection predicate `presentation_policy_eligible` must hold for the pinned snapshot and the program, or the create is refused with `ERR_PRESENTATION_INELIGIBLE`. That predicate fails when the consumer's modeled score falls outside the program's `min_modeled_score` floor, when their overall credit utilization exceeds the program's `max_eligible_utilization_pct`, or when the sponsoring lender is no longer `ACTIVE`.

Presentation carries a retroactive unwind that is easy to overlook. When `presentation_policy_eligible` fails, the platform does not merely refuse the offer — it withdraws the decision itself. The decision moves from `PREQUALIFIED` to `INELIGIBLE`, its `disqualifying_criteria` are recomputed from the program's criteria as they currently stand, and its `modeled_odds_pct` is nulled. The `PreQualifiedOffer` that this same call created moves immediately from `PRESENTED` to `WITHDRAWN`. The platform attributes this change to the decision's original `decided_at` rather than to the present instant, so the audit trail reads as though the decision was never sound — which, on the current data, it was not.

Generating an offer records `intent_recorded_at`, the logical clock instant the offer came into being. This timestamp is the anchor of the offer's freshness clock, and it is not restarted by anything that happens later; in particular `render_intent_recorded_at` records when the offer was rendered but is explicitly never used to restart the clock. Freshness is evaluated against whole days elapsed since `intent_recorded_at`. The intent expiry instant is `intent_recorded_at` plus the intent time-to-live expressed in whole days, and the settlement expiry instant is `intent_recorded_at` plus the settlement time-to-live expressed in whole days; both translate to 30 full days, the floor being taken on the day conversion; the two time-to-live constants are `OFFER_INTENT_TTL_HOURS` and `OFFER_SETTLEMENT_TTL_HOURS`, both 720 hours. Because the two horizons are identical the offer carries a single 30-day freshness basis measured from its own `intent_recorded_at`, and it becomes `STALE` only once the logical clock has moved strictly past that instant plus 30 days — an offer is still live at exactly the 30-day boundary and stale one moment after. Re-issuing an offer mints a fresh row with its own `intent_recorded_at` and so a fresh clock, but an offer already rendered is never re-anchored: `render_intent_recorded_at` records when it was shown and explicitly does not restart the clock, so a late re-issue still expires on the original schedule. Freshness is recomputed on every read of an offer, and the daily run's stale-offer stage additionally moves every offer still sitting in `RENDERED` to `STALE` in a single sweep, so the run ages out a rendered offer without consulting the 30-day boundary at all.

Rendering is a second, stricter gate, and it is where the platform's adverse-selection promise is actually kept. A `Consumer` renders a `PRESENTED` offer, and before the three conditions below are consulted the presenting decision's own freshness is re-checked: an offer whose decision has passed `fresh_until` is refused with `ERR_OFFER_EXPIRED`, and an offer already drifted out of the freshness window is refused as a state error rather than rendered. Past that, the call must clear three conditions before anything is shown. First, `whole_render_completeness` must hold for the pinned snapshot and program; a false result is refused with `ERR_RENDER_COMPLETENESS_INCOMPLETE` unless the consumer's `blanket_presentation_override` intervenes. Second, the consumer's `presented_count` for that offer's `product_kind` must not yet have reached the product kind's basis points multiplied by the consumer's total non-withdrawn offer count and divided by 10000, floored — a breach is refused with `ERR_OFFER_PRESENTATION_CAP_EXCEEDED`. Third, `presentation_policy_eligible` must again hold, since the consumer's circumstances may have changed since the offer was merely presented; a false result here is refused with `ERR_OFFER_PRESENTATION_BLOCKED` — the same predicate that, on presentation, unwinds the decision rather than merely refusing. Rendering additionally re-validates the pinned targeting criteria against the consumer's current data, and a criterion that matched when the decision was created but no longer matches at render time is refused with `ERR_COLLATERAL_ELIGIBILITY_CHANGED`. Only when the offer is still `PRESENTED` and all of these hold does the status become `RENDERED`, the consumer's `presented_count` for that product kind increase by one, and the corresponding `OfferPresentationCap` row be recomputed with its breach flag.

The blanket override provides one, and only one, escape hatch. A daily run encountering a consumer whose `blanket_presentation_override` is true renders that consumer's `PRESENTED` offers even though the completeness facet is incomplete. The override narrows the completeness facet alone: the content-quality facet is explicitly not waived by this path, so an offer that fails the content-quality requirement does not render through it.

Withdrawal is the compliance remedy. A `ComplianceOfficer` or a `PlatformAdmin` withdraws an offer that is in `PRESENTED` or `RENDERED`, and any other starting status is a state error. The offer's status becomes `WITHDRAWN`; when the offer was still only `PRESENTED`, the withdrawal also cascades: the decision that presented it is returned from `ELIGIBLE` to `INELIGIBLE`. Withdrawal is the last stop, and a withdrawn offer is never rendered.

### 3.8. Referrals, Revenue Recognition and Calibration

Routing a consent-carrying decision to a lender opens a `Referral`, and the consent is what makes the routing lawful. A `LenderOps` operator creates a referral from a rendered offer by supplying a unique `external_referral_ref`, a `channel` of `WEB`, `MOBILE_APP` or `MARKETPLACE`, and the `offer_id`, or alternatively opens one through a direct marketplace attempt carrying a `decision_id` and a `channel`. Both paths require the linked consent record to still be `PENDING`; a consent that is no longer pending means the one-time grant has already been used, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_CONSENT_ALREADY_CONSUMED`. An attempt against an offer that has not reached `RENDERED` is refused with `ERR_OFFER_NOT_RENDERED`, and a duplicate `external_referral_ref` is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_REFERRAL_REF`. Whichever path is used, the new referral starts in status `SENT`, copies `revenue_share_bps` and `clawback_days` from the sponsoring program, and advances the consumer's `version`. The consuming operation also seals the consent — minting the ciphertext, stamping the routing netloc — and drives both the offer and the decision to `CONSUMED`.

Funding is the lender's report that the loan or card actually issued. A `LenderOps` operator records a funding with a `funded_amount_cents`, which must be a positive integer; a non-positive or non-integer amount is malformed and yields `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, though note that this request-shape error ranks below the consent gate in the precedence ladder of §2.11, so a referral that is both non-consented and mis-sized reports the consent failure. Funding requires the referral to still be `SENT` — a referral already in a terminal status is a state error, and the specific condition that it was already funded surfaces as `ERR_REFERRAL_ALREADY_FUNDED`. Funding accepts the linked grant whether it is still `PENDING` or has already been sealed by an earlier daily-run pass, and in either case records the seal. On success the referral becomes `FUNDED`, `funded_at` is stamped with the logical clock, the linked decision moves from `CONSENTED` to `CONSUMED`, the consent record moves from `PENDING` to `SEALED`, the catalog item's calibration index is recomputed with the funded outcome folded in, and the consumer's `version` advances.

Revenue recognition is deliberately staged rather than immediate. The platform writes a `RevenueRecognitionEntry` whose `entry_kind` is `REVENUE` for the referral, stamped with `recognized_at` at the logical clock and carrying a `realized_amount_cents` equal to the referral's `funded_amount_cents` multiplied by its `revenue_share_bps` and divided by 10000, with the result floored to whole cents. That share is floored before it is ever summed, never after. A referral that is funded but never reversed carries only that leg, and the net recognized amount for it is the gross share alone, so `realized_amount_cents` on a `REVENUE` entry is always a non-negative whole number of cents.

Reversal is the compliance counterweight and it is time-boxed. A `ComplianceOfficer` or a `PlatformAdmin` reverses a funded referral by supplying a `reason_code`, and two conditions must both hold. The referral must be `FUNDED` — a reversal requested for a referral that was never funded is refused with `ERR_REFERRAL_NOT_FUNDED`, and one requested for a referral already reversed fails as a state error. A referral posts at most one clawback leg, so a repeated clawback request on an already-clawed referral surfaces as that same state error rather than a separate code. And the logical clock must fall at or before `funded_at` plus the referral's copied `clawback_days`; a reversal past that boundary is refused with `ERR_CLAWBACK_WINDOW_CLOSED`. A successful reversal stamps `reversed_at` with the logical clock, posts a `RevenueRecognitionEntry` whose `entry_kind` is `CLAWBACK` and whose `realized_amount_cents` is the negation of the floor of the funded amount multiplied by the referral's copied `clawback_rate_bps` and divided by 10000 — note that the clawback leg is driven by the program's separate clawback rate, while the recognition leg uses the revenue share — stamps `recognized_at`, recomputes the catalog item's calibration index with the reversed outcome, and advances the consumer's `version`. A clawback line is therefore strictly negative.

Taken together the two legs obey a compensating invariant. For a referral reversed inside its window, the net recognized amount — the sum of its `REVENUE` entry amounts minus the sum of its `CLAWBACK` entry amounts — equals the gross share minus the clawback share, where the clawback share is the funded amount multiplied by the referral's `clawback_rate_bps` and divided by 10000, floored. The settlement machinery is what keeps that invariant true even when the real-time path is missed: a reversal recorded late still posts its clawback, and a funding recorded late is caught up by the daily run's settlement pass, which recognizes revenue for any funded referral that lacks a `REVENUE` entry and posts clawbacks for any reversed referral whose reversal lies inside its copied `clawback_days` window.

A referral that never funds is released on the daily run. Once the logical clock advances past the referral's funding window — 30 days from the moment it was sent — the mobility stage moves it from `SENT` to `UNFUNDED`, releases the linked consent from `PENDING` to `NOT_SEALED` so that the consumer data is never sealed, and leaves the linked decision sitting at `CONSENTED`. Because the release is a pure function of the elapsed window it is also applied lazily when the referral is read back, so a referral whose window has passed reports `UNFUNDED` the moment it is listed rather than waiting for the next run. A lender-declined referral takes the same release path on demand: a `LenderOps` operator declines a referral in `SENT`, the status becomes `DECLINED`, the consent is released to `NOT_SEALED`, and the catalog item's calibration index is recomputed with the declined outcome. All four referral terminal statuses — `FUNDED`, `REVERSED`, `DECLINED` and `UNFUNDED` — are closed; no further transition is available from any of them.

Calibration closes the loop between what the platform predicted and what actually happened. For a given catalog item, the index is computed over every `PreQualificationDecision` whose pinned program points at that item, considering decisions in status `NEEDS_SNAPSHOT`, `ELIGIBLE`, `INELIGIBLE`, `PREQUALIFIED`, `CONSENTED` or `CONSUMED`, and excluding every decision in status `EXPIRED`. A realised outcome counts as a success when it is `FUNDED` and as a failure when it is `REVERSED` or `DECLINED`. When the considered set is empty, or when it contains no successes at all, the index is null — the metric is undefined, and it is never reported as 0 or as 1; in that case the reported `realized_rate` reads as 0.0 while the `modeled_odds_mean` reads as null. Otherwise the mean modeled odds across the considered decisions is taken and rounded half up to 4 decimal places, the realised success rate is taken as the success count divided by the considered count and rounded half up to 4 decimal places, and the index is the first divided by the second, again rounded half up to 4 decimal places.

Two variants of this index exist and they can land on opposite sides of 1.0 for identical decision rows, which is precisely the point. The including-provisional variant takes the mean over every considered decision, so the provisional modeled odds of unconsumed decisions enter it. The settled-only variant takes the mean over only those decisions whose `modeled_odds_settled` flag is true — that is, over realised outcomes alone. Any computed `approval_odds_calibration_index` is subject to that null rule and is never coerced to a numeric default. A `ComplianceOfficer` or `PlatformAdmin` reads the settled-only figure through a dedicated settled-index view, while the item's own read returns the including-provisional figure; both reads also return the `calibration_variant` label in use, the `decision_count`, the `modeled_odds_mean` and the `realized_rate`. The daily run recomputes the index for every catalog item, and it is also recomputed after every funding, reversal and decline.

Two derived reads on the catalog item exist purely to make the economics legible, and they use deliberately different bases. The item's read computes `face_value_cents` as the plain sum of the `funded_amount_cents` of every referral whose decision's pinned program belongs to that item — a gross loan or card volume figure summed first and rounded never, with no per-referral floor applied. It also computes `platform_revenue_cents` as the sum, over the same referrals, of the funded amount multiplied by the revenue share basis points and divided by 10000 with each term floored individually before summing. Identical rows therefore produce two totals computed on different bases, and an implementation that floors only once at the end will diverge. The same read computes `fee_breakeven_months` from the program's `annual_fee_cents` and `intro_apr_bps` against a caller-supplied `card_balance_cents`. The monthly fee is the annual fee divided by twelve, floored to whole cents; the monthly interest saved is the card balance multiplied by the introductory APR basis points, divided by 10000 and then by twelve, floored to whole cents. When the annual fee is zero the breakeven is 0 months. When the monthly interest saved is zero — which is what happens when no card balance is supplied on the read — the breakeven is null, not 0 and not unbounded. Otherwise the breakeven is the annual fee divided by the monthly interest saved, rounded up to the next whole month with a single ceiling applied at the end. The read reports which of these variants produced the answer in its `breakeven_variant` field.

### 3.9. Dispute Referrals, Accuracy Education and the Governance Envelope

A consumer who believes their credit file is wrong can route an inaccuracy, and the platform deliberately keeps that route narrow and capped. A `Consumer` or a `Referee` drafts a `DisputeReferral` against one bureau snapshot by supplying the `consumer_id`, the `snapshot_id` the inaccuracy was observed on, an `inaccuracy_type` of `PERSONAL_INFO`, `ACCOUNT_INFO`, `INQUIRY` or `BALANCE`, and a `narrative` of between 20 and 1000 characters. The draft starts in status `DRAFT` with `referral_count` at 0, and it cannot be drafted at all unless the consumer already holds a bureau snapshot in `ACTIVE` or `SUPERSEDED` status — with neither, the draft is refused with `ERR_DISPUTE_SNAPSHOT_REQUIRED`. The `referral_id` stays null until a consent-carrying referral exists to route the dispute through, and the `education_code` is not authored: it is computed on read as a lowercase snake_case token equal to the `inaccuracy_type`, so each dispute simultaneously records which education topic the consumer has worked through.

Submission is where the caps bite. A `Referee` submits a drafted dispute and supplies the `referral_id` that unlocks the bureau route. Three conditions apply. The dispute's `referral_count` must still be 0, because the counter caps at 1 — a dispute that has already been submitted is refused as a state error rather than silently resubmitted. The supplied `referral_id` must resolve to a referral in status `SENT`; a referral in any other status does not unlock the route and the submission is refused with `ERR_PRESENTATION_INELIGIBLE`. And the consumer must hold fewer than 3 (`PENDING_DISPUTE_CAP`) dispute referrals in status `SUBMITTED_OFFLINE` at that moment — a fourth concurrent submission is refused with `ERR_DISPUTE_CAP_EXCEEDED`. A successful submission stamps `referral_id` with the supplied value, sets `referral_count` to 1, moves the dispute to `SUBMITTED_OFFLINE`, and increments the consumer's `open_dispute_count`. From there the bureau's response is recorded by a `Referee` supplying a `bureau_case_ref`, which moves the dispute to `ACKNOWLEDGED_BY_BUREAU` and stores the case handle; or the dispute is withdrawn by a `Referee`, which moves it to `WITHDRAWN` and decrements the consumer's `open_dispute_count`. Withdrawal is the only path that reduces that count — acknowledgement does not, because the dispute remains part of the consumer's open bureau correspondence. Both `ACKNOWLEDGED_BY_BUREAU` and `WITHDRAWN` are terminal.

The remainder of the platform's thresholds are global governance values consulted wherever their named rule applies, and they have no state transitions of their own. A decision's freshness window is 30 days (`PREQUAL_CACHE_DAYS`) from `decided_at`, refreshed on re-evaluation. A daily run seals at most 25 pending consents (`CONSENT_SEAL_MAX_PER_RUN`). A sealed consent is archived once `RETENTION_DAYS` of 365 days have elapsed since its `sealed_at`. A consumer may hold at most `PENDING_DISPUTE_CAP` submissions in flight at once. A content quality facet must reach 8000 basis points (`CONTENT_QUALITY_MIN`) of authoring completeness to count as `COMPLETE`; a facet scored below that line cannot reach `COMPLETE` at all and the attempt is refused with `ERR_FACET_SCORE_BELOW_MINIMUM`. And the default presentation cap for a product kind is half of the window's rendered offers, expressed as 5000 basis points (`OFFER_CAP_BPS`). Two clock-driven gates complete the envelope: a program whose `go_live_at` has not yet arrived, or whose `expiry_at` has passed, is not deliverable, and a program sitting in `PAUSED` or `EXPIRED` is likewise not routable; the routing path reaches those refusals through the decision's own state, because a paused program has already cascaded its open decisions to `INELIGIBLE` so the consent gate refuses the attempt, and a decision whose status is no longer consentable cannot be routed. The catalogued `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_DELIVERABLE` and `ERR_PROGRAM_PAUSED` codes remain defined even though the routing path does not raise them itself.

When a single run has to decide the order in which it processes a batch of entities, it does so deterministically rather than in whatever order the rows happen to arrive in. Entities are sorted by their next deadline instant ascending; entities sharing a deadline instant are then ordered by their `created_at` ascending; and entities sharing both are finally ordered by their numeric status code ascending. `created_at` is the only common-field fallback available to the tie-break ladder, and identifiers never participate in it. Two variants of the pass exist. The single-pass variant, used by the run's expiry stage, classifies each entity exactly once, so entities sharing a deadline instant are resolved by the ladder above rather than being re-queued. The convergence variant, used by the run's counterparty stage, re-queues any entity whose status changed and re-sorts, iterating until a full pass produces no status change at all.
