# Carrier Interconnection & Access Billing API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A competitive local exchange carrier does not simply sell dial tone; it lives inside a web of negotiated interconnection agreements, tariffed rate elements, trunk facilities it shares with the incumbent, and monthly settlements measured in minutes of use. The commercial exposure is concentrated in places that are invisible to a normal billing system: a reciprocal compensation rate agreed in the fourth round of a negotiation and never re-read, a trunk group whose blocking percentage crossed the engineering threshold two study periods ago, a quarter of interstate usage factors the counterparty reported and we never audited, an access invoice sitting unpaid past its due date with half its balance formally disputed. Each of these is a revenue event, and today each is tracked in a different spreadsheet by a different department.

This document specifies the backend service that consolidates all of it. The system carries the interconnection agreement from its opening ask through the offer-and-counter negotiation cycle to execution and commercial activation; it provisions trunk groups against a hard facility ceiling at each point of interconnection and raises augment orders automatically when measured blocking breaches the engineering threshold; it ingests call detail records and decides, per call, whether the traffic is local, intrastate or interstate, falling back to negotiated traffic factors when the signaling cannot answer the question; it rates the resulting minute buckets at the tariffed rates on file for the period; it issues access invoices, takes payments, intakes billing disputes on a reason-coded resolution clock, reconciles our usage records against the counterparty's, and holds an uncollectible and disputed revenue reserve that moves on every one of those events and is journaled to an append-only ledger.

Two properties shape the whole design. First, **every deadline is measured against a server-controlled logical clock**, not wall time — regulatory windows, dispute SLAs, installation windows and invoice due dates are all evaluated against it, which makes the system's temporal behaviour fully deterministic and testable. Second, **money and minutes are conserved**: a reversal restores exactly what a completion added, a withdrawn dispute releases exactly the reserve it provisioned, and the three jurisdiction minute buckets always re-sum to the batch total. Where the specification below fixes a rounding step or a boundary inclusivity, that choice exists to keep those invariants exact, and implementations must follow it literally. The entire API is mounted under the `/interconnection` prefix, with the sole exception of the clock-driving endpoint described in §2.5.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Actors, Identity and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the service must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that principal is acting in. If either header is absent or empty, the request is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before anything else is examined. If both headers are present but the named role is not among the roles authorised for the endpoint being called, the request is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

Eight roles exist, spelled exactly as follows.

- **`PlatformAdmin`** operates the interconnection platform itself. It registers interconnecting carriers, drives the logical clock, and runs the dispute deadline sweep. It also holds read access to every carrier, agreement, trunk group, traffic study, augment order, tariff filing, factor filing, usage batch, call detail record, access invoice, billing dispute, reconciliation case and reserve ledger entry in the system.
- **`InterconnectionManager`** is the CLEC-side negotiator. It opens agreements, exchanges offers with the counterparty, and owns the agreement lifecycle operations — activation, termination and withdrawal.
- **`NetworkPlanner`** is the engineer who requests trunk groups, provisions and activates them, disconnects them at teardown, records traffic studies, and completes or reverses augment installation work.
- **`AccessBillingAnalyst`** is the biller. It opens usage batches, appends call detail records, runs jurisdiction determination and rating, creates and issues access invoices, reviews reported traffic factors, applies payments and opens reconciliation cases.
- **`DisputeResolutionOfficer`** adjudicates billing disputes: it takes them under review and sustains or denies them inside the SLA.
- **`RegulatoryAffairsSpecialist`** owns the regulatory rate book. It files tariffed rate elements, shepherds them through approval, and withdraws filings.
- **`RevenueAssuranceController`** owns traffic factor audits, record-exchange reconciliation, uncollectible write-offs, and the uncollectible and disputed revenue reserves.
- **`CarrierRepresentative`** is the counterparty carrier's own representative. It counters offers and accepts them, commits to augment due dates or refuses them, records traffic studies, files quarterly traffic factors, appends call detail records, pays invoices, and files, escalates and withdraws billing disputes.

Seven of the eight roles carry an **opaque** identity. When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `InterconnectionManager`, `NetworkPlanner`, `AccessBillingAnalyst`, `DisputeResolutionOfficer`, `RegulatoryAffairsSpecialist` or `RevenueAssuranceController`, the `X-User-ID` value is an operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server never compares it to any entity's `id`. It is, however, recorded where a transition writes it: issuing an access invoice stores the acting `X-User-ID` in that invoice's `issued_by_user_id`, and resolving a billing dispute stores the acting `X-User-ID` in that dispute's `resolved_by_user_id`.

The eighth role is different. When `X-User-Role` is `CarrierRepresentative`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Carrier`; ownership checks on any resource that references a carrier compare the resource's carrier reference to `X-User-ID`. That comparison reaches through the graph: an agreement's carrier, a trunk group's agreement's carrier, a usage batch's carrier, an invoice's carrier, a dispute's invoice's carrier and a reconciliation case's batch's carrier are all in scope. A `CarrierRepresentative` acting on a resource that belongs to a different carrier is refused with `ERR_CARRIER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). This check applies to every endpoint that lists `CarrierRepresentative` among its authorised roles, reads as well as writes.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every collection endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls the page size: it defaults to 10, may be no smaller than 1 and no larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may be no smaller than 0. A `limit` or `offset` outside those bounds is a schema violation and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422).

List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside `meta`. `total_count` is the number of rows matching the request's filters before the page window is applied, and `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond the current window. Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, every collection is ordered by `created_at` ascending; where two rows share a `created_at` instant, insertion order breaks the tie. This ordering is not cosmetic — several rules below depend on it, including "the most recent traffic study for a trunk group", "the factor filing with the latest creation instant", and the order in which the sweep and the audit cascade process their candidate rows.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Row Versioning

Every persisted resource carries an `id` that is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix and no client-supplied component, and a `created_at` timestamp in ISO 8601 UTC form with exactly three fractional digits, stamped from the logical clock at creation.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, refreshed to the logical clock on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 each time the row is mutated. Carriers, interconnection agreements, trunk groups, trunk augment orders, tariff filings, traffic factor filings, usage batches, access invoices, billing disputes and reconciliation cases all carry both.

Four record types are deliberately immutable-shaped ledger rows and carry **only** `id` and `created_at` — both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from them entirely: negotiation offers, traffic measurements, call detail records and reserve ledger entries. Where the narrative below says an offer "moves to `SUPERSEDED`" or a measurement's derived figures are read back, no version field is involved on those rows.

Version increments matter beyond bookkeeping, because two write operations gate on them (see §2.6) and because a cascade that touches a parent row bumps that parent's `version` too. Where a transition's effects reach a second or third entity, each touched row's `version` increments by 1 and its `updated_at` refreshes, even when the field that changed was a derived aggregate the client never sent.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

The service does not read wall-clock time for business decisions. It maintains a **logical clock** that every deadline, window, expiry and effective-date rule consults. Before any request is made, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; that is a mandatory startup default and the server never falls back to real time.

A `PlatformAdmin` may advance the clock by supplying a `target_utc` instant to the clock-setting capability, which responds with the new `current_utc` value and mutates no entity — subsequent requests simply observe the new instant. The clock is monotonic: a `target_utc` earlier than the current logical clock is refused with `ERR_CLOCK_MUST_ADVANCE` (HTTP 422). Supplying the current instant again is permitted, since the requirement is that the clock must be at or after where it already stands.

Because every temporal comparison in this specification resolves against this clock, boundary inclusivity is stated explicitly wherever it is decidable, and implementations must honour it exactly.

### 2.6. Stale-View Protection on Agreement and Carrier Writes

Two write operations can silently destroy work that a client never saw, and both are therefore guarded by optimistic concurrency control.

The first is **appending a negotiation offer to an interconnection agreement**. Because a new offer supersedes the standing open offer, a writer working from an out-of-date view of the agreement would discard terms it never read. The offer-append request must therefore include `expected_agreement_version` as a required, non-optional field carrying the `version` of the `InterconnectionAgreement` the client last observed. If that value does not equal the agreement's current `version`, the append is rejected with `ERR_STALE_AGREEMENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412), forcing the writer to re-read the agreement and its standing offer before trying again.

The second is **creating a quarterly traffic factor filing for a carrier**. A new filing for a `(carrier_id, quarter)` pair supersedes the prior filing for that pair and changes which factors future jurisdiction determination will pin, so the create request must include `expected_carrier_version` as a required, non-optional field carrying the `version` of the `Carrier` the client last observed. A mismatch with the carrier's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CARRIER_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

No other endpoint in the system takes a version field. Provisioning a trunk group, accepting an augment order, approving a tariff filing, rating a batch, issuing an invoice, resolving a dispute and adjusting a reconciliation case are all unguarded in this sense, and a client must not send a version expectation with them.

### 2.7. Nested Path Resolution and the Order in Which Requests Are Refused

Path parameters are resolved strictly left to right, and the first unresolvable segment decides the error. Each addressable resource has exactly one path identifier, and the failure code follows from which identifier failed to resolve: a `carrier_id` segment yields `ERR_CARRIER_NOT_FOUND`; an `agreement_id` segment yields `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`; an `offer_id` segment yields `ERR_OFFER_NOT_FOUND`; a `trunk_group_id` segment yields `ERR_TRUNK_GROUP_NOT_FOUND`; a `measurement_id` segment yields `ERR_MEASUREMENT_NOT_FOUND`; a `cdr_id` segment yields `ERR_CDR_NOT_FOUND`; an `order_id` segment, which addresses a trunk augment order, yields `ERR_AUGMENT_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`; a `filing_id` segment, which addresses a tariff filing, yields `ERR_TARIFF_FILING_NOT_FOUND`; a `factor_filing_id` segment yields `ERR_FACTOR_FILING_NOT_FOUND`; a `batch_id` segment, which addresses a usage batch, yields `ERR_USAGE_BATCH_NOT_FOUND`; an `invoice_id` segment yields `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_FOUND`; a `dispute_id` segment yields `ERR_DISPUTE_NOT_FOUND`; a `case_id` segment, which addresses a reconciliation case, yields `ERR_RECONCILIATION_CASE_NOT_FOUND`; and an `entry_id` segment addresses a single reserve ledger entry. All thirteen not-found codes carry HTTP 404.

The full evaluation order for any request is fixed, and it is what a client sees when several things are wrong at once. Checks are applied in this sequence, and the first failure is returned:

1. **Authentication** — both headers present and non-empty, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. **Authorization** — the role is listed on the endpoint, else `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
3. **Path existence** — every path segment resolves, left to right, with the not-found codes above.
4. **Request schema** — every required body field is present; types, enum membership, decimal scales, the string patterns for OCN, CLLI, LATA, NPA-NXX and calendar quarter, all declared numeric ranges, and the pagination bounds are satisfied. Any violation yields `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422).
5. **Body-reference existence** — identifiers carried inside the payload are resolved in payload field order. A `carrier_id` in the body that does not resolve yields `ERR_CARRIER_NOT_FOUND` (this applies when opening an agreement, filing traffic factors and opening a usage batch); an `agreement_id` in the body yields `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND` (when requesting a trunk group); a `usage_batch_id` in the body yields `ERR_USAGE_BATCH_NOT_FOUND` (when creating an access invoice and when opening a reconciliation case); an `invoice_id` in the body yields `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_FOUND` (when filing a billing dispute); and a `supersede_filing_id` in the body, when present, yields `ERR_TARIFF_FILING_NOT_FOUND` (when approving a tariff filing).
6. **Carrier scope** — the `CarrierRepresentative` ownership comparison of §2.1, else `ERR_CARRIER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`.
7. **Segregation of duties** — see §2.9, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
8. **Optimistic concurrency** — the two version expectations of §2.6, yielding `ERR_STALE_AGREEMENT_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_CARRIER_VERSION`.
9. **State preconditions** — the target row is in a status from which the requested transition exists, yielding the entity-specific conflict codes catalogued in §3.
10. **Regulatory and compliance caps**, then **uniqueness, overlap, capacity and round limits**, then **temporal windows and deadlines**, then **cross-entity readiness**, then **fiscal amount checks**, in that order.

Within that tenth band the ordering is likewise fixed. Regulatory caps come first: the access charge reform parity cap, the reciprocal compensation rate cap, and the traffic factor sum ceiling are evaluated before the transit-permission and unrateable-record refusals. Uniqueness, window-overlap, capacity and round-limit constraints come next, followed by the two payload-shape constraints on installed trunks and agreed minutes and the clock monotonicity check. Temporal window checks follow — first the four that carry HTTP 422 (tariff window ordering, measurement period ordering, usage period ordering and closure, the dispute filing window, and the committed due date), then the three that carry HTTP 409 (agreement effective date reached, dispute SLA not yet expired, invoice due date reached). Cross-entity readiness checks come after those: the parent agreement being effective, an effective tariff rate existing for every jurisdiction carrying minutes, an effective reciprocal compensation rate covering the agreement's effective date, and the batch's invoice being issued. Fiscal amount checks are always last: the empty-invoice refusal, the overpayment refusal, the dispute-amount ceiling and the credit-amount decision match.

The practical consequence is worth stating plainly. A regulatory cap breach surfaces as its own code rather than being masked by a later capacity or fiscal check, and a state conflict always beats a business-rule breach on the same request — a dispute in the wrong status is reported as an invalid dispute state, not as a credit-amount problem.

### 2.8. Precision, Rounding and Unit Conventions

All monetary amounts are held at **2 decimal places** and rounded **half up**, with two deliberate exceptions. Tariffed and negotiated per-minute rates are held at **6 decimal places** and are never re-rounded once pinned; and per-record call charges are computed at **6 decimal places**, also rounded half up, because a single call's charge at a sub-cent rate would otherwise vanish.

Minutes are held at **4 decimal places**, rounded half up. Percentages — traffic factors, blocking percentages and reconciliation variances — are held at **2 decimal places**, rounded half up. The traffic balance ratio used by reciprocal compensation settlement is held at **4 decimal places**, rounded half up.

Trunk quantities are integers, and every division that produces a trunk count **rounds up to the next whole number**, never to the nearest. That is a capacity-engineering convention: a facility that needs a fraction of a trunk needs the whole trunk.

Two boundary conventions recur and are worth fixing here. A **half-open window** written as a start instant and an end instant includes the start and excludes the end; tariff filing effective windows work this way, which is why a new filing whose effective instant equals a prior filing's expiry instant does not overlap it. A **closed interval** includes both edges; usage batch periods, augment installation windows and the agreed-minutes range all work this way.

### 2.9. Carrier Scope and Segregation of Duties

Two invoice operations are protected by segregation of duties. The user whose `X-User-ID` was stamped into an invoice's `issued_by_user_id` at issuance may neither resolve a billing dispute filed against that invoice nor write that invoice's balance off, even while holding the `DisputeResolutionOfficer` or `RevenueAssuranceController` role that otherwise authorises the operation. In both cases the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the invoice's `issued_by_user_id`; when it does not, the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Those are the only two operations the rule guards.

### 2.10. Cascade Discipline, Parallel Status Dimensions and Compensating Actions

Several entities in this system carry **more than one independent status dimension on a single row**, and the dimensions advance separately. A trunk group has a `service_status` describing where it sits in provisioning and a `augment_status` describing its capacity posture; a trunk group can be `IN_SERVICE` on the first dimension and `AUGMENT_BLOCKED` on the second at the same time, and neither dimension's transition implies anything about the other unless this document says so. An access invoice carries three such dimensions: `lifecycle_status` for the document, `payment_status` for settlement, and `dispute_status` for the dispute position. An invoice can be `ISSUED`, `PARTIALLY_PAID` and `DISPUTED` simultaneously.

Where dimensions do interact, the interaction is a **derivation**, stated once and applied consistently: once an invoice is `ISSUED`, its `lifecycle_status` becomes `CLOSED` as soon as `payment_status` has reached either `PAID` or `WRITTEN_OFF` **and** `dispute_status` is either `NONE` or `RESOLVED`. That auto-transition is evaluated at the end of any operation that moves either of the other two dimensions.

Cascades in this system are **transitive**: an operation mutates one row, that mutation causes a second row to be recomputed, and that recomputation causes a third. The specification below traces those chains hop by hop, and each hop is caused by the previous hop's mutation rather than by the original request. Cascades are also **conditional on the child's own state** — a parent-driven teardown touches only non-terminal children and leaves already-terminal rows alone.

Finally, several operations exist purely as **compensating actions** whose job is to restore a conserved quantity to its exact pre-operation value. Reversing a completed augment order returns the agreement's in-service trunk total to precisely what it was before the completion. Withdrawing a billing dispute releases precisely the reserve provisions that dispute booked, with no residual drift. Where such an exactness guarantee exists, it is a hard requirement, not an approximation: recomputing from scratch rather than adjusting incrementally is the intended implementation strategy wherever the two could diverge.

### 2.11. Reserve Provisioning and the Append-Only Reserve Ledger

The carrier-level reserve position is the system's financial summary, and it is recomputed **from scratch** on every event that changes a carrier's receivable or dispute position: issuing an invoice, applying a payment, writing a balance off, submitting, escalating, withdrawing or resolving a dispute, a deemed-allowed sweep outcome, a reconciliation adjustment, and a factor-audit re-rate. It is never adjusted incrementally.

The recomputation proceeds in a fixed, non-circular order. **First**, the two exposure bases are rebuilt. `open_receivable_usd` is the sum of `open_balance_usd` over the carrier's access invoices whose `lifecycle_status` is `ISSUED` and whose `payment_status` is either `UNPAID` or `PARTIALLY_PAID`; each such invoice's `open_balance_usd` is its `billed_amount_usd` less its `credited_amount_usd` less its `paid_amount_usd`. `disputed_receivable_usd` is the sum of `disputed_amount_usd` over the carrier's access invoices whose `lifecycle_status` is `ISSUED`, regardless of payment position. Draft invoices carry no exposure and closed invoices have left the receivable set, so neither contributes to either base.

**Second**, the tier is selected. `net_receivable_usd` is `open_receivable_usd` minus `disputed_receivable_usd`. If that net figure is zero or negative the applicable reserve percentage is zero. If it is positive but below twenty-five dollars the percentage is 2.00. If it is at least twenty-five dollars but below two hundred and fifty dollars the percentage is 5.00. At two hundred and fifty dollars or above the percentage is 8.50. The tier floors are inclusive at their lower edge: a net receivable of exactly 25.00 sits in the second tier, while 24.99 sits in the first, and exactly 250.00 sits in the third.

**Third**, the two reserve balances are computed. `uncollectible_reserve_balance_usd` is the greater of `net_receivable_usd` and zero, multiplied by the selected percentage and divided by one hundred, rounded half up to two decimal places. The disputed reserve is computed on **two different bases from identical data**, and both figures matter:

- The **regulatory basis** is what the system persists. Because the regulator requires disputed access revenue to be held in full until the dispute closes, the persisted `disputed_reserve_balance_usd` on the carrier equals the whole `disputed_receivable_usd`, and every reserve ledger entry in the `DISPUTED_REVENUE` bucket moves that regulatory figure.
- The **GAAP basis** reserves only the probable loss: `disputed_receivable_usd` multiplied by 60.00 percent (the dispute loss probability), rounded half up to two decimal places. This figure is not persisted on the carrier; it is reported as `gaap_disputed_reserve_usd` by the carrier reserve-summary read, alongside `regulatory_disputed_reserve_usd`, the difference between them as `disputed_reserve_basis_delta_usd`, and the supporting `open_receivable_usd`, `disputed_receivable_usd`, `net_receivable_usd`, `tier_pct` and `uncollectible_reserve_balance_usd` values.

**Fourth**, and only after both balances are known, the ledger is written. For each of the two reserve buckets whose balance actually moved, a reserve ledger entry is appended carrying `amount_usd` equal to the new balance minus the old balance — positive when the reserve grows, negative when it releases — and `balance_after_usd` equal to the new balance. The entry names its `carrier_id`, its `reserve_bucket` (either `DISPUTED_REVENUE` or `UNCOLLECTIBLE`), its `entry_type` (one of `DISPUTE_PROVISION`, `DISPUTE_RELEASE`, `UNCOLLECTIBLE_ADJUSTMENT`, `WRITE_OFF` or `RECONCILIATION_ADJUSTMENT`), and a stable `source_event` tag drawn from `BILLING_DISPUTE_SUBMITTED`, `BILLING_DISPUTE_ESCALATED`, `BILLING_DISPUTE_WITHDRAWN`, `BILLING_DISPUTE_RESOLVED`, `DISPUTE_DEADLINE_SWEEP`, `INVOICE_CREATED`, `INVOICE_PAYMENT_APPLIED`, `INVOICE_WRITTEN_OFF`, `RECONCILIATION_ADJUSTED` and `FACTOR_AUDIT_RERATE`. An entry's `invoice_id` names the access invoice that caused the movement, and is null for a carrier-level reserve re-measurement that no single invoice can be attributed to.

The reserve balances are outputs only. They never feed back into `open_receivable_usd` or `net_receivable_usd`, so a single pass reaches the fixed point and re-running the calculation on unchanged inputs produces no ledger entries at all.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Carrier Onboarding and the Agreement Negotiation Cycle

A `PlatformAdmin` brings a counterparty onto the platform by registering it with a `carrier_name` of at most 120 characters, an `ocn` — the four-character, uppercase-alphanumeric Operating Company Number that identifies the carrier in industry databases — and a `carrier_type` drawn from `ILEC`, `CLEC`, `IXC` and `CMRS`, which is the regulatory classification that governs which rate elements may be billed against it. The `ocn` is globally unique; registering a second carrier with an OCN already on file is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_OCN`. A freshly registered carrier's six financial figures — `uncollectible_reserve_balance_usd`, `disputed_reserve_balance_usd`, `open_receivable_usd`, `disputed_receivable_usd`, `net_receivable_usd` — all initialise to 0.00, and are thereafter maintained entirely by the reserve recomputation of §2.11.

An `InterconnectionManager` opens a negotiation by creating an interconnection agreement against a `carrier_id`, giving it an `agreement_label` (the docket label under which the negotiation is tracked, non-empty and unique among that carrier's agreements — a repeat is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_AGREEMENT_LABEL`), an opening ask consisting of `proposed_reciprocal_rate_per_mou_usd` at six decimal places and greater than zero and `proposed_term_months` between 12 and 60 inclusive, an optional `transit_traffic_allowed` flag defaulting to false, a requested commercial `effective_date`, and a `poi_facility_capacity_trunks` ceiling that must be at least 24 and an exact multiple of 24. The new agreement lands in `DRAFT` with `negotiation_round` at 0 and `capacity_posture` at `NORMAL`. Its `reciprocal_rate_per_mou_usd`, `term_months`, `poi_financial_responsibility`, `executed_at` and `terminated_at` are all null at this point, and `total_in_service_trunks` and `poi_augment_pending_count` are both 0.

The negotiation itself runs through **offers**, and the offer sequence is where the cycle lives. Each offer carries a `reciprocal_rate_per_mou_usd` at six decimals and greater than zero, a `term_months` between 12 and 60 inclusive, a `poi_financial_responsibility` drawn from `ORIGINATOR_PAYS`, `SHARED` and `TERMINATOR_PAYS`, a `transit_traffic_allowed` flag, and an `offer_type` that is either `PROPOSAL` or `COUNTER`. The server derives the offer's `offering_party` from the acting role — `CLEC_SIDE` when an `InterconnectionManager` submits it, `COUNTERPARTY_SIDE` when a `CarrierRepresentative` does — and stamps `round_number` as the agreement's `negotiation_round` after the append, one-based. Each offer is born in status `OPEN` with `responded_at` null; only one offer per agreement is `OPEN` at a time, and that one is the **standing offer**.

Appending the first offer to a `DRAFT` agreement moves it `DRAFT → PROPOSED`. That first offer must have `offer_type` `PROPOSAL` and must come from the CLEC side; a first offer typed `COUNTER` is refused with `ERR_OFFER_PARTY_SEQUENCE`. From there the negotiation alternates. A counter from the counterparty against a standing `CLEC_SIDE` offer moves the agreement `PROPOSED → COUNTERED`; a counter from the CLEC against a standing `COUNTERPARTY_SIDE` offer moves it `COUNTERED → PROPOSED`. Those two edges close the `PROPOSED → COUNTERED → PROPOSED` cycle that constitutes multi-round negotiation. A party may not counter its own standing offer — when the submitting side's derived `offering_party` equals the standing offer's `offering_party`, the append is refused with `ERR_OFFER_PARTY_SEQUENCE`. An append against an agreement whose `status` is anything other than `DRAFT`, `PROPOSED` or `COUNTERED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_AGREEMENT_STATE`.

Every append does four things. The standing `OPEN` offer, if one exists, moves `OPEN → SUPERSEDED` with its `responded_at` stamped from the logical clock; the new offer row is appended in `OPEN`; the agreement's `negotiation_round` increments by 1; and the agreement's `version` increments by 1. The agreement's `proposed_reciprocal_rate_per_mou_usd` and `proposed_term_months` are **never** overwritten by an offer — they remain the opening ask so that the pinned terms and the original position can be compared after execution.

Two ceilings bound the negotiation. Rounds are capped at **six**: the round number the append would create, which is the agreement's current `negotiation_round` plus one, must be no greater than six, and an append that would exceed it is refused with `ERR_NEGOTIATION_ROUND_LIMIT`. And the reciprocal compensation rate offered in any round may not exceed **one tenth of a dollar per minute of use** — a rate above 0.100000 is refused with `ERR_RECIPROCAL_RATE_CAP_EXCEEDED`. That cap is enforced as a business-safety rule at its own priority band, not as a range check on the field, so an offer at exactly 0.100000 is accepted while one at 0.100001 is not. Recall from §2.6 that every append also requires `expected_agreement_version` to match the agreement's current `version`, on pain of `ERR_STALE_AGREEMENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

### 3.2. Execution, Activation and Termination of Interconnection

Acceptance is what ends the negotiation, and it may only be performed by the party that did **not** author the standing offer. When the standing `OPEN` offer has `offering_party` `CLEC_SIDE` and the agreement is in `PROPOSED`, a `CarrierRepresentative` accepts it and the agreement moves `PROPOSED → EXECUTED`. When the standing `OPEN` offer has `offering_party` `COUNTERPARTY_SIDE` and the agreement is in `COUNTERED`, an `InterconnectionManager` accepts it and the agreement moves `COUNTERED → EXECUTED`. Both directions are specified identically: the accepted offer must belong to the agreement in question, must be in status `OPEN` — an attempt to accept an offer in `SUPERSEDED`, `ACCEPTED` or `WITHDRAWN` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_OFFER_STATE` — and the acting party must not be the offeror. An acceptance attempted while the agreement sits in `DRAFT`, `EXECUTED`, `EFFECTIVE`, `TERMINATED` or `WITHDRAWN` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_AGREEMENT_STATE`.

On acceptance the offer moves `OPEN → ACCEPTED` with `responded_at` stamped from the logical clock, and the agreement **pins** four commercial terms verbatim from that offer: `reciprocal_rate_per_mou_usd` (copied at its full six decimal places with no re-rounding), `term_months`, `poi_financial_responsibility` and `transit_traffic_allowed`. The agreement's `executed_at` is stamped from the logical clock and its `version` increments by 1.

Execution is a paper event; **activation** is the commercial one. An `InterconnectionManager` activates an `EXECUTED` agreement, moving it `EXECUTED → EFFECTIVE`. The agreement must be in `EXECUTED`; activation attempted from `DRAFT`, `PROPOSED`, `COUNTERED`, `EFFECTIVE`, `TERMINATED` or `WITHDRAWN` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_AGREEMENT_STATE`. Beyond that, two gates stand in the way. The logical clock must have reached the agreement's `effective_date`; if it has not, activation is refused with `ERR_EFFECTIVE_DATE_NOT_REACHED`. And a reciprocal compensation rate must be on file: there must exist a tariff filing whose `rate_element` is `LOCAL_RECIPROCAL_COMP`, whose `status` is `EFFECTIVE`, and whose half-open effective window covers the agreement's `effective_date` — that is, whose window starts at or before that instant and ends strictly after it. Absent such a filing, activation is refused with `ERR_NO_EFFECTIVE_RECIPROCAL_RATE`. Activation recomputes `capacity_posture`, which resolves to `NORMAL` when no child augment order is open, and increments the agreement's `version` by 1.

A negotiation that goes nowhere is **withdrawn**. An `InterconnectionManager` may withdraw an agreement sitting in either `PROPOSED` or `COUNTERED`, supplying a `withdrawal_reason`; the agreement moves to `WITHDRAWN`, the standing `OPEN` offer moves `OPEN → WITHDRAWN` with its `responded_at` stamped, and the agreement's `version` increments by 1. Withdrawal from `DRAFT`, `EXECUTED`, `EFFECTIVE` or the two terminal states is refused with `ERR_INVALID_AGREEMENT_STATE`.

**Termination** is the heaviest cascade in the network half of the system. An `InterconnectionManager` terminates an `EFFECTIVE` agreement with a `termination_reason`, and the operation is blocked while billing is still in flight: if any usage batch belonging to the agreement's carrier is in `SUBMITTED` or `JURISDICTIONALIZED`, termination is refused with `ERR_INVALID_AGREEMENT_STATE`, because those batches' minutes have not yet been settled. When it does proceed, the agreement moves `EFFECTIVE → TERMINATED`, which is terminal, its `terminated_at` is stamped from the logical clock and the teardown propagates downward in `created_at` ascending order over the agreement's trunk groups. Every trunk group whose `service_status` is `REQUESTED`, `PROVISIONED` or `IN_SERVICE` moves to `DISCONNECT_PENDING`, and its `version` increments by 1; trunk groups already in `DISCONNECTED` are left untouched. Each trunk group that moves then cancels its own open augment work: every child augment order in `PENDING` or `ACCEPTED` moves to `REJECTED` with its `rejection_reason` set to the literal `AGREEMENT_TERMINATED`, and its `version` increments by 1, while orders already in `REJECTED`, `COMPLETED` or `REVERSED` are left alone. Each cancelled order decrements the agreement's `poi_augment_pending_count` by 1, and once every decrement has landed the agreement's `capacity_posture` is recomputed. Because no trunk group remains `IN_SERVICE`, `total_in_service_trunks` recomputes to 0. Finally the agreement's own `version` increments by 1.

### 3.3. Trunk Group Provisioning at a Point of Interconnection

A `NetworkPlanner` requests a **trunk group** under an agreement, naming the `agreement_id`, a `trunk_group_serial` that is non-empty and unique among that agreement's trunk groups (a repeat is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_TRUNK_GROUP_SERIAL`), the `poi_clli_code` of the point of interconnection it lands on — exactly eleven uppercase characters — the `lata_code` of the LATA in which that point sits, given as exactly three digits, a `poi_type` drawn from `MEET_POINT`, `COLLOCATION` and `LEASED_FACILITY`, a `direction` drawn from `ONE_WAY_OUTBOUND`, `ONE_WAY_INBOUND` and `TWO_WAY`, a `traffic_type` drawn from `LOCAL_INTERCONNECTION`, `ACCESS_TANDEM` and `TRANSIT`, plus the forecast the counterparty has committed to: `forecast_busy_hour_ccs` at two decimals and greater than zero, covering `forecast_horizon_quarters` quarters, between 1 and 8 inclusive. Transit traffic is a negotiated privilege: a trunk group whose `traffic_type` is `TRANSIT` may only be created under an agreement whose pinned `transit_traffic_allowed` is true, and otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_TRANSIT_TRAFFIC_NOT_PERMITTED`.

A newly requested trunk group starts with `service_status` `REQUESTED` and `augment_status` `NONE`, with `provisioned_trunks`, `required_trunks` and `open_augment_order_count` all 0, `latest_blocking_pct` at 0.00, and `in_service_at` null.

Provisioning moves the trunk group `REQUESTED → PROVISIONED`. The `NetworkPlanner` supplies `provisioned_trunks`, which must be a **positive multiple of 24** — the number of trunks in a DS1 — and the request is measured against the shared facility at the point of interconnection. Sum `provisioned_trunks` over the sibling trunk groups that share the same agreement and the same `poi_clli_code` and whose `service_status` is either `PROVISIONED` or `IN_SERVICE`, add the requested quantity, and the total must be no greater than the agreement's `poi_facility_capacity_trunks`. Exceeding that ceiling is refused with `ERR_POI_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`; a total exactly equal to the ceiling is permitted. On success the trunk group's `provisioned_trunks` is set to the supplied value and its `version` increments by 1. Provisioning a trunk group that is not in `REQUESTED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TRUNK_GROUP_STATE`.

Activation moves the trunk group `PROVISIONED → IN_SERVICE`; a trunk group in `REQUESTED`, `IN_SERVICE`, `DISCONNECT_PENDING` or `DISCONNECTED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TRUNK_GROUP_STATE`. Activation also carries a cross-entity guard: the parent agreement's `status` must be `EFFECTIVE`, since a trunk group cannot carry traffic under an agreement that has not gone live. When the parent is in any other status the request is refused with `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_EFFECTIVE`. On success the trunk group's `in_service_at` is stamped from the logical clock, the agreement's `total_in_service_trunks` increases by that trunk group's `provisioned_trunks`, and both the trunk group's and the agreement's `version` increment by 1.

Teardown is the mirror image. When a trunk group has been driven to `DISCONNECT_PENDING` by its agreement's termination, a `NetworkPlanner` completes the disconnection, moving it `DISCONNECT_PENDING → DISCONNECTED`, which is terminal. That completion is blocked while any augment work remains open: the trunk group's `open_augment_order_count` must be 0, and otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TRUNK_GROUP_STATE`. On success `provisioned_trunks` is set to 0 and `version` increments by 1. Note the asymmetry in the in-service accounting: the `IN_SERVICE → DISCONNECT_PENDING` edge is where the agreement's `total_in_service_trunks` decreases by the trunk group's `provisioned_trunks`, because that is the moment the trunks stop carrying traffic; the `REQUESTED → DISCONNECT_PENDING` and `PROVISIONED → DISCONNECT_PENDING` edges move no in-service total, because those trunk groups were never counted in it. All three of those edges are driven by the parent agreement's termination rather than by a direct request, and each increments the trunk group's `version` by 1.

### 3.4. Traffic Studies, Blocking Breaches and Capacity Augments

Capacity management is driven by **traffic studies**. Either a `NetworkPlanner` or the counterparty's `CarrierRepresentative` records a study against a trunk group, and a study may only be recorded while that trunk group's `service_status` is `IN_SERVICE`; a study against a trunk group in `REQUESTED`, `PROVISIONED`, `DISCONNECT_PENDING` or `DISCONNECTED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TRUNK_GROUP_STATE`. The `augment_status` dimension places no restriction on recording a study. The study supplies `measurement_period_start` and `measurement_period_end` bounding the study period, `offered_calls` and `blocked_calls` (each zero or greater, with the blocked count no greater than the offered count), and the measured `busy_hour_ccs` at two decimals and zero or greater. The period end must fall strictly after the period start; equal or inverted instants are refused with `ERR_INVALID_MEASUREMENT_PERIOD`.

Recording a study runs the sizing-and-blocking calculation and writes the results in three places: onto the study row itself, onto the trunk group, and — when the outcome demands capacity — onto a new augment order. The calculation proceeds as follows.

**Blocking.** If the study offered no calls at all, `blocking_pct` is 0.00. Otherwise `blocking_pct` is the number of blocked calls multiplied by one hundred and divided by the number of offered calls, rounded half up to two decimal places. The engineering threshold is **three percent**, and the comparison is inclusive: a `blocking_pct` of exactly 3.00 is a breach, while 2.99 is not.

**Sizing.** The per-trunk carrying capacity is chosen from the trunk group's `direction`: a trunk group whose `direction` is `TWO_WAY` carries **31** hundred-call-seconds of busy-hour load per trunk, while one whose `direction` is `ONE_WAY_OUTBOUND` or `ONE_WAY_INBOUND` carries **27** per trunk. The sizing load is the greater of two figures: the measured `busy_hour_ccs`, and the trunk group's committed `forecast_busy_hour_ccs` grown by fifteen percent — that is, multiplied by 1.15 and rounded half up to two decimal places. Dividing the sizing load by the per-trunk carrying capacity and rounding **up** to the next whole number gives `required_trunks`. The shortfall is `required_trunks` minus the trunk group's current `provisioned_trunks`.

**The augment decision** then branches three ways, and the branch is what moves the trunk group's `augment_status`.

- If `blocking_pct` is **below** three percent there is no action: `requested_additional_trunks` is 0, no augment order is created, and the trunk group's `augment_status` does not move. The study's own `augment_triggered` flag is false.
- If blocking breached the threshold but the shortfall is zero or negative — the sized demand is already met by the trunks in the ground — the breach is a grooming problem rather than a capacity problem. `requested_additional_trunks` is 0, no augment order is created, the agreement's `poi_augment_pending_count` is unchanged, and the trunk group's `augment_status` moves to `GROOMING_REVIEW`.
- If blocking breached the threshold and the shortfall is positive, the ordered quantity is the shortfall rounded **up** to a whole number of DS1s and then multiplied back out — that is, the shortfall divided by 24, rounded up to the next whole number, multiplied by 24. That quantity is then tested against the shared facility. Sum `provisioned_trunks` over the sibling trunk groups sharing the same agreement and `poi_clli_code` whose `service_status` is `PROVISIONED` or `IN_SERVICE`, add `requested_additional_trunks` over those same trunk groups' augment orders whose `status` is `PENDING` or `ACCEPTED`, and add the newly sized quantity. If that total exceeds the agreement's `poi_facility_capacity_trunks`, the augment cannot be placed: `requested_additional_trunks` falls back to 0, no augment order is created, the trunk group's `augment_status` moves to `AUGMENT_BLOCKED`, and the agreement's `capacity_posture` is recomputed to `BLOCKED`. Otherwise the augment proceeds and `augment_status` moves to `AUGMENT_ORDERED`.

The augment-ordered branch is a **three-hop transitive cascade**, and each hop is caused by the previous hop's mutation rather than by the request. In the first hop the new study row sets the trunk group's `latest_blocking_pct` and `required_trunks` from the calculation, snapshots the same computed figures onto itself as `blocking_pct` and `required_trunks_at_measurement` so that later studies never rewrite history, and moves `augment_status` to `AUGMENT_ORDERED` with `augment_triggered` true. Because `augment_status` became `AUGMENT_ORDERED`, the second hop creates an augment order in status `PENDING`, carrying `trunk_group_id` pointing at the measured trunk group, `triggering_measurement_id` pointing at the new study, `requested_additional_trunks` and `required_trunks_target` from the same calculation, `committed_due_date`, `install_window_start`, `install_window_end`, `installed_trunks`, `rejection_reason` and `reversed_at` all null, and increments the trunk group's `open_augment_order_count` by 1. Because a `PENDING` order now exists, the third hop increments the agreement's `poi_augment_pending_count` by 1 and recomputes `capacity_posture` to `CONSTRAINED`. The trunk group's `version` increments by 1 across the whole operation, and the agreement's `version` increments by 1 whenever the posture changed. In the grooming and blocked branches the trunk group's `latest_blocking_pct` and `required_trunks` are still updated and its `version` still increments; only the order creation and the pending-count movement are absent.

The agreement's `capacity_posture` is a derived summary with a fixed precedence. It is `BLOCKED` whenever any of the agreement's trunk groups has `augment_status` `AUGMENT_BLOCKED`; failing that, it is `CONSTRAINED` whenever `poi_augment_pending_count` is greater than zero; otherwise it is `NORMAL`. `BLOCKED` wins over `CONSTRAINED`.

A `PENDING` augment order is the counterparty's to answer, and only the `CarrierRepresentative` whose `X-User-ID` equals the agreement's carrier may answer it. **Accepting** it moves the order `PENDING → ACCEPTED` and requires a `committed_due_date` strictly after the logical clock — a due date at or before the current instant is refused with `ERR_INVALID_COMMITTED_DUE_DATE`. Acceptance fixes the installation window: `install_window_start` is the committed due date less **five days**, and `install_window_end` is the committed due date itself. That raw window is a **closed interval** — installation work may land on either edge.

Because there is one installation crew per point of interconnection, accepted windows may not collide. For collision testing each window is padded by **twenty-four hours** on both edges: the buffered window runs from `install_window_start` less twenty-four hours to `install_window_end` plus twenty-four hours. The candidate order's buffered window is compared against the buffered window of every other augment order whose trunk group shares the same agreement and `poi_clli_code` and whose `status` is `ACCEPTED`. Two buffered windows collide when the peer's buffered start is at or before the candidate's buffered end **and** the candidate's buffered start is at or before the peer's buffered end; both comparisons are inclusive, so buffered windows that merely touch at a single instant do collide. A collision is refused with `ERR_AUGMENT_WINDOW_COLLISION`. Orders in `PENDING`, `REJECTED`, `COMPLETED` or `REVERSED` hold no crew slot and are ignored by the test. On success the order's `version` increments by 1.

**Rejecting** the order instead moves it `PENDING → REJECTED` with a supplied `rejection_reason`, and that refusal propagates: the trunk group's `augment_status` moves `AUGMENT_ORDERED → AUGMENT_BLOCKED` and its `open_augment_order_count` decrements by 1; the agreement's `poi_augment_pending_count` decrements by 1 and its `capacity_posture` is recomputed to `BLOCKED`; and the order's, the trunk group's and the agreement's `version` each increment by 1. Accepting or rejecting an order that is not in `PENDING` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_AUGMENT_ORDER_STATE`.

**Completion** is the `NetworkPlanner`'s to record against an `ACCEPTED` order, supplying `installed_trunks`. That quantity must be a positive multiple of 24 and must not exceed the order's `requested_additional_trunks`; a violation of either condition is refused with `ERR_INSTALLED_TRUNKS_INVALID`. The facility ceiling is re-tested at this moment as well: the sum of `provisioned_trunks` over the trunk groups sharing the agreement and `poi_clli_code` whose `service_status` is `PROVISIONED` or `IN_SERVICE`, plus `installed_trunks`, must be no greater than the agreement's `poi_facility_capacity_trunks`. Completing an order that is not in `ACCEPTED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_AUGMENT_ORDER_STATE`. On success the order moves `ACCEPTED → COMPLETED`; the trunk group's `provisioned_trunks` increases by `installed_trunks`, its `augment_status` moves `AUGMENT_ORDERED → NONE` and its `open_augment_order_count` decrements by 1; the agreement's `total_in_service_trunks` increases by `installed_trunks`, its `poi_augment_pending_count` decrements by 1 and its `capacity_posture` is recomputed; and all three rows' `version` values increment by 1.

**Reversal** exists for the case where the acceptance test fails after the fact, and it is a strict compensating action. A `NetworkPlanner` reverses a `COMPLETED` order with a `reversal_reason`, and the order's trunk group must still be in `PROVISIONED` or `IN_SERVICE`; the order moves `COMPLETED → REVERSED`, which is terminal, with `reversed_at` stamped from the logical clock. The trunk group's `provisioned_trunks` decreases by exactly the `installed_trunks` the completion added, and its `augment_status` moves `NONE → GROOMING_REVIEW` so that the re-opened capacity gap is not silently lost. The agreement's `total_in_service_trunks` decreases by the same `installed_trunks`, returning it to precisely its pre-completion value. The trunk group's `open_augment_order_count` is **unchanged**, because a reversed order is not an open order. All three rows' `version` values increment by 1. Reversing an order that is not in `COMPLETED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_AUGMENT_ORDER_STATE`.

### 3.5. The Tariff Rate Book and Rate Change Supersession

Every dollar this system bills traces back to a **tariff filing** made by a `RegulatoryAffairsSpecialist`. A filing names a `filing_docket` — the regulatory docket number, non-empty and globally unique, with a repeat refused as `ERR_DUPLICATE_FILING_DOCKET` — a `rate_element` drawn from `INTERSTATE_SWITCHED_ACCESS`, `INTRASTATE_SWITCHED_ACCESS` and `LOCAL_RECIPROCAL_COMP`, the `rate_per_mou_usd` it sets at six decimals and greater than zero, the carrier's own `benchmark_interstate_rate_per_mou_usd` at six decimals and greater than zero, and the `effective_date` and `expiry_date` bounding the rate's window. That window is **half-open**: it includes the effective instant and excludes the expiry instant. The expiry instant must fall strictly after the effective instant, and otherwise the filing is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TARIFF_WINDOW`.

Access charge reform imposes a parity cap. A filing whose `rate_element` is `INTRASTATE_SWITCHED_ACCESS` may not price above the carrier's own interstate benchmark: its `rate_per_mou_usd` must be no greater than its `benchmark_interstate_rate_per_mou_usd`, with equality being compliant. A breach is refused with `ERR_ACCESS_REFORM_RATE_CAP_EXCEEDED`. The cap does not constrain interstate or local reciprocal compensation filings.

Two filings for the same rate element may not both claim the same instant. A new filing's half-open window must not overlap the window of any existing filing for the same `rate_element` whose `status` is `FILED` or `EFFECTIVE`; an overlap is refused with `ERR_TARIFF_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. Because the windows are half-open, **adjacency is not overlap**: a new filing whose `effective_date` equals an existing filing's `expiry_date` is accepted without complaint. A new filing lands in `status` `FILED`, with `approved_at`, `superseded_by_filing_id` and `superseded_at` all null.

Approval moves the filing `FILED → EFFECTIVE`. Two conditions apply. The logical clock must have reached the filing's `effective_date`. And no other filing for the same `rate_element` in `status` `EFFECTIVE` may have a half-open window intersecting this one's — **unless** the approval request names exactly that filing in an optional `supersede_filing_id`, which is how a mid-window rate change is executed. Approval stamps `approved_at` from the logical clock and increments the filing's `version` by 1. When `supersede_filing_id` is supplied, the named filing is truncated in place: its `expiry_date` is moved back to the approving filing's `effective_date`, its `status` moves `EFFECTIVE → SUPERSEDED`, its `superseded_by_filing_id` is set to the approving filing's `id`, its `superseded_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and its `version` also increments by 1. The superseded filing's own preconditions are that it was `EFFECTIVE` and that the approving filing's effective instant falls strictly inside its half-open window. The net effect is that at most one `EFFECTIVE` filing per rate element covers any given instant, which is what lets rating look up a unique rate.

A filing that was never approved may be **withdrawn** by a `RegulatoryAffairsSpecialist` with a `withdrawal_reason`, moving it `FILED → WITHDRAWN`, which is terminal, and incrementing its `version` by 1. Both approval and withdrawal require the filing to be in `FILED`; any other status — `EFFECTIVE`, `SUPERSEDED` or `WITHDRAWN` — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TARIFF_FILING_STATE`.

### 3.6. Traffic Factor Reporting, Review and Audit

When signaling cannot establish a call's jurisdiction, the parties fall back on negotiated **traffic factors**: the percentage of a carrier's usage that is interstate and the percentage that is local. Either a `CarrierRepresentative` acting for the carrier or an `AccessBillingAnalyst` files them quarterly, naming the `carrier_id`, the `quarter` in the form of a four-digit year beginning `20` followed by `Q` and a digit from 1 to 4, `reported_piu_pct` (Percent Interstate Usage) and `reported_plu_pct` (Percent Local Usage), each at two decimals between 0.00 and 100.00 inclusive. The two reported percentages together may not exceed 100.00; a pairwise sum above that is refused with `ERR_FACTOR_SUM_EXCEEDS_TOTAL`, and that ceiling is enforced as a business-safety rule in its own priority band rather than as a range check on either field individually.

A new filing lands in `status` `REPORTED`, with `audited_piu_pct`, `audited_plu_pct`, `dispute_reason`, `audit_completed_at` and `superseded_at` all null. Its `effective_piu_pct` and `effective_plu_pct` — the pair that jurisdiction determination actually consumes — mirror the reported values. The general rule for those two derived fields is simple: they resolve to the audited pair when and only when the filing's `status` is `AUDITED`, and to the reported pair in every other status.

Filing a new quarter's factors **supersedes** the prior filing for that same carrier and quarter. Whichever prior filing is in `REPORTED`, `ACCEPTED`, `DISPUTED` or `AUDITED` moves to `SUPERSEDED`, which is terminal, with its `superseded_at` stamped from the logical clock and its `version` incremented by 1. There is one refusal built into this: a filing for a carrier-and-quarter pair that currently has a filing in `DISPUTED` is rejected with `ERR_FACTOR_FILING_DISPUTE_OPEN`, because the standing challenge must be audited or accepted before new factors may displace it. Creating a filing also increments the carrier's `version` by 1, and — as covered in §2.6 — requires `expected_carrier_version` to match the carrier's current `version`.

An `AccessBillingAnalyst` **reviews** a filing by supplying a `decision`. A decision of `ACCEPT` moves the filing to `ACCEPTED` and its `effective_piu_pct` and `effective_plu_pct` resolve to the reported values; it is permitted from `REPORTED` and from `AUDITED`. A decision of `DISPUTE` moves the filing to `DISPUTED` and requires a non-empty `dispute_reason`, which is stored on the filing; it is permitted from `REPORTED`, from `ACCEPTED` and from `AUDITED`. Disputing a filing that had already been audited is the interesting case: the `audited_piu_pct` and `audited_plu_pct` values are retained on the row, but `effective_piu_pct` and `effective_plu_pct` revert to the reported values for as long as the filing sits in `DISPUTED`. A review requested from a status where the chosen decision has no transition is refused with `ERR_INVALID_FACTOR_FILING_STATE`.

Only a `RevenueAssuranceController` may **audit**, and only from `DISPUTED`; an audit attempted from `REPORTED`, `ACCEPTED`, `AUDITED` or `SUPERSEDED`, and a review attempted from `DISPUTED` or `SUPERSEDED`, are both refused with `ERR_INVALID_FACTOR_FILING_STATE`. The audit supplies `audited_piu_pct` and `audited_plu_pct`, each at two decimals between 0.00 and 100.00 inclusive, and their sum may not exceed 100.00 — a breach is refused with the same `ERR_FACTOR_SUM_EXCEEDS_TOTAL`. The filing moves `DISPUTED → AUDITED`, the audited pair is stored, `effective_piu_pct` and `effective_plu_pct` now resolve to the audited values, and `audit_completed_at` is stamped from the logical clock. Because `AUDITED` can be disputed again, the `AUDITED → DISPUTED → AUDITED` cycle lets a second audit round replace the first audit's substituted factors.

Auditing is the deepest cascade in the billing half of the system — **four hops** — and it exists because substituting factors changes money that has already been billed. In the first hop, every usage batch whose pinned `traffic_factor_filing_id` is this filing and whose `status` is `RATED` or `INVOICED` is re-jurisdictionalised and re-rated with the audited factors, processed in `created_at` ascending order; each such batch has its three minute buckets, its three jurisdiction subtotals and its `rated_amount_usd` rewritten, its `rating_variant` set to `FINAL`, its `rerate_count` incremented by 1, its `rated_at` refreshed from the logical clock, and its `version` incremented by 1; the batch's `status` does not move, so a re-rated batch stays `RATED` if it was `RATED` and stays `INVOICED` if it was `INVOICED`. Because a re-rated batch's `rated_amount_usd` changed, the second hop refreshes that batch's access invoice — if one exists and only while its `lifecycle_status` is `ISSUED` — rewriting `billed_amount_usd`, the three jurisdiction subtotals and `rating_variant`, recomputing `open_balance_usd`, incrementing `adjustment_count` by 1 and incrementing the invoice's `version` by 1. Because an invoice's `open_balance_usd` changed, the third hop re-runs the reserve recomputation of §2.11 for the carrier, rewriting `open_receivable_usd`, `uncollectible_reserve_balance_usd` and `disputed_reserve_balance_usd` and incrementing the carrier's `version` by 1. Because the uncollectible balance changed, the fourth hop appends a reserve ledger entry in bucket `UNCOLLECTIBLE` with `entry_type` `UNCOLLECTIBLE_ADJUSTMENT` and `source_event` `FACTOR_AUDIT_RERATE`, carrying the signed delta. The audited filing's own `version` increments by 1.

Re-rating is a **self-loop on the batch's status dimension**: a batch that was `RATED` stays `RATED` and a batch that was `INVOICED` stays `INVOICED` while its amounts move underneath it.

### 3.7. Usage Collection, Jurisdiction Determination and Access Charge Rating

An `AccessBillingAnalyst` opens a **usage batch** for a closed exchange period, naming the `carrier_id`, the `period_start` and `period_end` bounding it, a `direction` that is either `TERMINATING_TO_US` (traffic the counterparty handed us to terminate) or `ORIGINATING_FROM_US` (traffic we handed the counterparty), and the counterparty's `exchange_reference` for the batch, which must be non-empty. The batch period is a **closed interval** including both edges. The period end must fall strictly after the period start **and** strictly before the logical clock — a period that has not yet closed cannot be exchanged — and a violation of either condition is refused with `ERR_INVALID_USAGE_PERIOD`. Periods for the same carrier and the same direction may neither overlap **nor touch**: a new `period_start` equal to an existing batch's `period_end` is rejected, as is any true overlap, with `ERR_BATCH_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. A new batch lands in `status` `SUBMITTED` with `record_count` at 0, `total_billable_minutes`, `signaling_determined_minutes`, `indeterminate_minutes`, `interstate_minutes`, `intrastate_minutes` and `local_minutes` all at 0, `interstate_amount_usd`, `intrastate_amount_usd`, `local_amount_usd` and `rated_amount_usd` all at 0.00, `rerate_count` at 0, and `traffic_factor_filing_id`, `factor_source`, `rating_variant`, `jurisdictionalized_at` and `rated_at` all null.

**Call detail records** are appended one at a time, by either an `AccessBillingAnalyst` or the carrier's own `CarrierRepresentative`, and only while the batch is `SUBMITTED`; appending to a batch in any later state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_BATCH_STATE`. Each record carries the `originating_npanxx` and `terminating_npanxx` of the calling and called parties as exactly six digits each, the `duration_seconds` of conversation time (at least 1), a `signaling_cpn_present` flag recording whether Calling Party Number was populated in the SS7 signaling, an optional `jip_state_code` giving the two-uppercase-letter state code of the Jurisdiction Information Parameter when one was signaled, an optional `originating_state_code` derived from the originating NPA-NXX, a `terminating_state_code` derived from the terminating NPA-NXX, and a `same_local_calling_area` flag recording whether the two rate centres share a local calling area under the exchange boundary table. A record without a `terminating_state_code` is **unrateable** and is refused at ingest with `ERR_CDR_UNRATEABLE`. On acceptance the record's `billable_minutes` is computed immediately as its `duration_seconds` divided by 60 and rounded **up** to a whole number, its `jurisdiction`, `jurisdiction_source` and `rated_amount_usd` are left null, and the batch's `record_count` and `total_billable_minutes` increase accordingly with the batch's `version` incrementing by 1.

**Jurisdiction determination** is run by an `AccessBillingAnalyst` and moves the batch `SUBMITTED → JURISDICTIONALIZED`. The batch must be in `SUBMITTED` and must hold at least one record; an empty batch cannot be jurisdictionalised. The calculation runs in three stages.

The **per-record signaling pass** walks the batch's records in `created_at` ascending order. A record whose `signaling_cpn_present` is false, or whose `originating_state_code` is null, cannot be placed from its own signaling: its `jurisdiction` becomes `INDETERMINATE` and its `jurisdiction_source` becomes `FACTOR_ALLOCATED`. Otherwise the record's effective origin state is its `jip_state_code` when one is present and its `originating_state_code` when the JIP is null. If that effective origin state differs from the record's `terminating_state_code`, the call is `INTERSTATE`. If they are the same and `same_local_calling_area` is true, the call is `LOCAL`. If they are the same and `same_local_calling_area` is false, the call is `INTRASTATE`. In all three of those cases `jurisdiction_source` becomes `SIGNALING`. The batch's `signaling_determined_minutes` is the sum of `billable_minutes` over records whose source is `SIGNALING`, and its `indeterminate_minutes` is the sum of `billable_minutes` over records whose jurisdiction is `INDETERMINATE`.

**Factor selection** then decides how the indeterminate pool is split, and it pins the answer onto the batch. If `indeterminate_minutes` is zero there is nothing to allocate: `factor_source` is set to `SIGNALING_ONLY`, `traffic_factor_filing_id` is set to null, and both working percentages are 0.00. Otherwise the calendar quarter of the batch's `period_start` is formed in the four-digit-year-plus-quarter shape, and the candidate filings are that carrier's traffic factor filings for that quarter whose `status` is anything other than `SUPERSEDED`. If there are no candidates, the tariff defaults apply: `factor_source` is set to `DEFAULT_TARIFF`, `traffic_factor_filing_id` stays null, the working interstate percentage is **50.00** and the working local percentage is **20.00**. If there are candidates, the one with the latest `created_at` wins; the batch's `traffic_factor_filing_id` is set to that filing's `id`, `factor_source` is set to `AUDITED` when that filing's `status` is `AUDITED` and to `CARRIER_REPORTED` in every other case, and the working percentages are that filing's `effective_piu_pct` and `effective_plu_pct`.

**Allocation** carves interstate and local out of the indeterminate pool and leaves intrastate as the residual. The allocated interstate minutes are `indeterminate_minutes` multiplied by the working interstate percentage and divided by one hundred, rounded half up to four decimal places; the allocated local minutes are `indeterminate_minutes` multiplied by the working local percentage and divided by one hundred, rounded half up to four decimal places; and the allocated intrastate minutes are `indeterminate_minutes` less the allocated interstate minutes less the allocated local minutes, with no rounding of their own — which is precisely why no rounding remainder is ever lost. The batch's `interstate_minutes` is the sum of `billable_minutes` over `SIGNALING` records whose jurisdiction is `INTERSTATE` plus the allocated interstate minutes; `intrastate_minutes` is the signaling-derived intrastate sum plus the allocated intrastate residual; and `local_minutes` is the signaling-derived local sum plus the allocated local minutes. The conservation invariant holds by construction: the three buckets always re-sum to `total_billable_minutes`. The batch's `jurisdictionalized_at` is stamped from the logical clock and its `version` increments by 1.

**Rating** is likewise run by an `AccessBillingAnalyst` and moves the batch `JURISDICTIONALIZED → RATED`. Rates come from the tariff rate book: for each jurisdiction, the rate is the `rate_per_mou_usd` of the tariff filing whose `status` is `EFFECTIVE`, whose `rate_element` is the one mapped to that jurisdiction — `INTERSTATE_SWITCHED_ACCESS` for interstate traffic, `INTRASTATE_SWITCHED_ACCESS` for intrastate traffic and `LOCAL_RECIPROCAL_COMP` for local traffic — and whose half-open window covers the batch's `period_start`, meaning the window's effective instant is at or before the period start and its expiry instant is strictly after it. Because the supersession rule of §3.5 has already truncated any earlier overlapping filing, that covering filing is unique. Every jurisdiction whose minute bucket is greater than zero must have such a filing; if any does not, rating is refused with `ERR_NO_EFFECTIVE_TARIFF_RATE`.

Rating then runs in three steps. **Per-record charges** are written for the signaling-determined records only: each such record's `rated_amount_usd` is its `billable_minutes` multiplied by the rate for its own `jurisdiction`, rounded half up to six decimal places. Records whose `jurisdiction_source` is `FACTOR_ALLOCATED` keep `rated_amount_usd` null, because their charge is only defined at the batch bucket level. **Per-jurisdiction subtotals** are then formed for interstate, intrastate and local, and the two legs of each subtotal are rounded **separately before being added**: the signaling leg is the sum of `rated_amount_usd` over the signaling-determined records of that jurisdiction, rounded half up to two decimal places; the factor leg is that jurisdiction's allocated minutes multiplied by that jurisdiction's rate, rounded half up to two decimal places; and the subtotal is the sum of those two already-rounded figures. Those three subtotals become the batch's `interstate_amount_usd`, `intrastate_amount_usd` and `local_amount_usd`. **The batch total**, `rated_amount_usd`, is the exact sum of the three two-decimal subtotals and is never re-rounded. The batch's `rated_at` is stamped from the logical clock and its `version` increments by 1.

Rating produces one of **two variants from otherwise identical call data**, and which one it produces is recorded in `rating_variant`. When the pinned factor filing's `status` is `AUDITED`, the indeterminate minutes were allocated with audited percentages and the batch is `FINAL`. When the pinned filing sits in `REPORTED`, `ACCEPTED` or `DISPUTED`, or when no filing existed and the tariff defaults were used, the batch is `PRELIMINARY` — the interstate bucket is understated whenever a later audit raises the interstate percentage, and the batch remains eligible for the re-rate cascade of §3.6 that will move it to `FINAL` and post the delta to the invoice.

Jurisdiction determination is refused unless the batch is `SUBMITTED`, and rating is refused unless it is `JURISDICTIONALIZED`; in both cases the refusal is `ERR_INVALID_BATCH_STATE`.

**Reciprocal compensation settlement** is a computed read over an agreement, available to an `InterconnectionManager`, an `AccessBillingAnalyst`, a `RevenueAssuranceController` or the carrier's own `CarrierRepresentative`. It aggregates that agreement's carrier's usage batches whose `status` is `RATED` or `INVOICED`; batches still in `SUBMITTED` or `JURISDICTIONALIZED` are excluded because their minutes are not yet settled. `inbound_minutes` is the sum of `total_billable_minutes` over the qualifying batches whose `direction` is `TERMINATING_TO_US`, and `outbound_minutes` is the same sum over those whose `direction` is `ORIGINATING_FROM_US`. If `outbound_minutes` is zero, `traffic_balance_ratio` is null, `settlement_mode` is `UNDETERMINED` and `net_settlement_usd` is 0.00. Otherwise `traffic_balance_ratio` is `inbound_minutes` divided by `outbound_minutes`, rounded half up to four decimal places. When that ratio falls within the bill-and-keep band — at least 0.9500 and at most 1.0500, both edges inclusive — the traffic is deemed balanced: `settlement_mode` is `BILL_AND_KEEP` and `net_settlement_usd` is 0.00. Outside that band, `settlement_mode` is `METERED` and `net_settlement_usd` is `inbound_minutes` less `outbound_minutes`, multiplied by the agreement's pinned `reciprocal_rate_per_mou_usd`, rounded half up to two decimal places. The sign convention is that a positive figure means the counterparty owes us and a negative figure means we owe them. The read also echoes back the `reciprocal_rate_per_mou_usd` it applied.

### 3.8. Access Invoicing, Payment and Write-Off

An `AccessBillingAnalyst` creates an **access invoice** from a `RATED` usage batch by naming the `usage_batch_id` and the `due_date` printed on the invoice. The batch must be in `RATED` — any other pipeline state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_BATCH_STATE` — and at most one invoice may reference a given batch, with a second attempt refused as `ERR_BATCH_ALREADY_INVOICED`. Creation snapshots the batch's figures onto the invoice: `billed_amount_usd` from the batch's `rated_amount_usd`, the three jurisdiction subtotals into the invoice's own `interstate_amount_usd`, `intrastate_amount_usd` and `local_amount_usd`, `rating_variant` mirroring the batch's, and `carrier_id` copied from the batch. It also resolves `rate_authority_filing_id`: the `EFFECTIVE` tariff filing that governed the **largest** jurisdiction subtotal at rating, ranked by subtotal amount descending, with ties broken by `rate_element` ascending alphabetically. The invoice lands with `lifecycle_status` `DRAFT`, `payment_status` `UNPAID` and `dispute_status` `NONE`; `credited_amount_usd`, `paid_amount_usd`, `disputed_amount_usd` all 0.00; `open_balance_usd` equal to `billed_amount_usd` less `credited_amount_usd` less `paid_amount_usd`; `adjustment_count` at 0; and `issued_at`, `dispute_deadline_at`, `issued_by_user_id` and `written_off_at` all null. The source batch moves `RATED → INVOICED` and its `version` increments by 1.

**Issuance** moves the invoice `DRAFT → ISSUED` and is what puts it on the books. The invoice must be in `DRAFT` — an issue attempt against an invoice already `ISSUED` or `CLOSED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_INVOICE_STATE` — and its `billed_amount_usd` must be greater than 0.00 — a zero-amount invoice has nothing to bill and is refused with `ERR_EMPTY_INVOICE`. Issuance stamps `issued_at` from the logical clock, sets `dispute_deadline_at` to that instant plus **90 days** (the dispute filing window), and records the acting `X-User-ID` in `issued_by_user_id`, which is the anchor for the segregation-of-duties rule of §2.9. Because the invoice now enters the receivable set, the carrier's receivable and reserve figures are recomputed per §2.11 and a reserve ledger entry with `source_event` `INVOICE_CREATED` is appended; the invoice's and the carrier's `version` each increment by 1.

**Payments** are applied by either the carrier's `CarrierRepresentative` or an `AccessBillingAnalyst`, supplying an `amount_usd` and a `remittance_reference`. The invoice's `lifecycle_status` must be `ISSUED` and its `payment_status` must be `UNPAID` or `PARTIALLY_PAID`; an invoice already `PAID` or `WRITTEN_OFF` accepts no further payment and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_INVOICE_STATE`. The amount may not exceed the invoice's current `open_balance_usd`; overpayment is refused rather than credited, with `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_BALANCE`. Where the payment lands determines the resulting dimension move: an amount strictly less than the open balance moves `payment_status` `UNPAID → PARTIALLY_PAID`, while an amount exactly equal to the open balance moves it `UNPAID → PAID` when no earlier payment had been applied and `PARTIALLY_PAID → PAID` when one had.

Applying a payment is a three-hop cascade. In the first hop the invoice's `paid_amount_usd` increases by the amount and `open_balance_usd` is recomputed. Because the open balance changed, the second hop re-runs the reserve recomputation of §2.11, rewriting the carrier's `open_receivable_usd` and `uncollectible_reserve_balance_usd` — and when the payment drives the balance to 0.00 the invoice leaves the receivable input set entirely, so both reserve balances are rebuilt without it. Because the uncollectible balance changed, the third hop appends a reserve ledger entry in bucket `UNCOLLECTIBLE` with `entry_type` `UNCOLLECTIBLE_ADJUSTMENT` and `source_event` `INVOICE_PAYMENT_APPLIED`, carrying the signed delta. When the payment brings the invoice to `PAID`, the derivation of §2.10 fires and `lifecycle_status` auto-moves `ISSUED → CLOSED` provided `dispute_status` is `NONE` or `RESOLVED`. The invoice's and the carrier's `version` each increment by 1.

**Write-off** is the `RevenueAssuranceController`'s tool for a balance that will not be collected, and it carries five preconditions. The invoice's `lifecycle_status` must be `ISSUED`, and a write-off attempted against a `DRAFT` or `CLOSED` invoice is refused with `ERR_INVALID_INVOICE_STATE`; its `payment_status` must be `UNPAID` or `PARTIALLY_PAID`; its `dispute_status` must not be `DISPUTED`, since a live dispute must be resolved before the balance can be abandoned; the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the invoice's `issued_by_user_id`, on pain of `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; and the logical clock must have reached the invoice's `due_date`, with a premature attempt refused as `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_YET_DUE`. An invoice whose `payment_status` is already `WRITTEN_OFF` accepts no second write-off and is refused with `ERR_INVOICE_ALREADY_WRITTEN_OFF`. On success the caller's `write_off_reason` is recorded, `payment_status` moves `UNPAID → WRITTEN_OFF` or `PARTIALLY_PAID → WRITTEN_OFF`, `written_off_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and the remaining `open_balance_usd` is removed from the receivable set. Two reserve ledger entries follow, in order: first a `WRITE_OFF` entry in bucket `UNCOLLECTIBLE` with `source_event` `INVOICE_WRITTEN_OFF` for the written-off balance, then an `UNCOLLECTIBLE_ADJUSTMENT` entry in the same bucket for the recomputed reserve delta. As with payment, `lifecycle_status` auto-moves to `CLOSED` when `dispute_status` is `NONE` or `RESOLVED`, and the invoice's and the carrier's `version` each increment by 1.

### 3.9. Billing Disputes, Escalation and the Deadline Sweep

Only the counterparty's `CarrierRepresentative` may **file a billing dispute**, naming the `invoice_id`, the `disputed_amount_usd` it refuses to pay pending resolution (at two decimals and greater than 0.00), a `reason_code` drawn from `RATE_APPLICATION`, `JURISDICTION_FACTOR` and `USAGE_DISCREPANCY`, and a `supporting_detail` narrative that is non-empty and at most 500 characters. Four conditions gate the filing. The invoice's `lifecycle_status` must be `ISSUED`, and a dispute against a `DRAFT` or `CLOSED` invoice is refused with `ERR_INVALID_INVOICE_STATE`. Its `payment_status` must not be `WRITTEN_OFF`, with a violation refused as `ERR_INVOICE_ALREADY_WRITTEN_OFF`. The logical clock must be at or before the invoice's `dispute_deadline_at` — a dispute filed at **exactly** the deadline instant is accepted, while one instant later is refused with `ERR_DISPUTE_WINDOW_CLOSED`. And the amount must fit inside the undisputed part of the balance: `disputed_amount_usd` must be no greater than the invoice's `open_balance_usd` less its current `disputed_amount_usd`, with an excess refused as `ERR_DISPUTE_AMOUNT_EXCEEDS_BALANCE`.

The new dispute lands in `status` `SUBMITTED` with `escalation_round` at 0, `credited_amount_usd` at 0.00, and `resolution_note`, `resolved_at` and `resolved_by_user_id` all null. Its clock is set from the reason code: `sla_days` is **30** calendar days for `RATE_APPLICATION`, **60** for `JURISDICTION_FACTOR` and **45** for `USAGE_DISCREPANCY`. `filed_at` is stamped from the logical clock and `resolution_due_at` is `filed_at` plus `sla_days` days.

Filing runs a four-hop cascade. The first hop writes the dispute's `filed_at`, `sla_days` and `resolution_due_at` as just described. The second hop increases the invoice's `disputed_amount_usd` by the new dispute's `disputed_amount_usd` and moves the invoice's `dispute_status` to `DISPUTED` — from `NONE` for a first dispute, or from `RESOLVED` for a re-dispute filed inside the window, which requires the clock to be at or before `dispute_deadline_at` and leaves `payment_status` and any partial payments already applied entirely untouched. Because the invoice's disputed amount changed, the third hop re-runs the reserve recomputation of §2.11: the carrier's `disputed_receivable_usd` rises, its `net_receivable_usd` falls by the same amount, its `disputed_reserve_balance_usd` is rebuilt on the regulatory basis, and its `uncollectible_reserve_balance_usd` is **re-tiered from the lowered net receivable** — which is why a dispute can shrink the uncollectible reserve even as it grows the disputed one. The fourth hop appends a reserve ledger entry in bucket `DISPUTED_REVENUE` with `entry_type` `DISPUTE_PROVISION` and `source_event` `BILLING_DISPUTE_SUBMITTED` carrying the signed delta, followed by a second entry in bucket `UNCOLLECTIBLE` with `entry_type` `UNCOLLECTIBLE_ADJUSTMENT` and the same `source_event` whenever the re-tier actually moved the uncollectible balance. The invoice's and the carrier's `version` each increment by 1.

A `DisputeResolutionOfficer` **acknowledges** the dispute to take it under review, moving it `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW` or, after an escalation, `ESCALATED → UNDER_REVIEW`. The logical clock must be **strictly before** the dispute's `resolution_due_at`; once the due instant has been reached the dispute can only be closed by the sweep, and an acknowledgement attempt is refused with `ERR_DISPUTE_SLA_EXPIRED`. Acknowledgement does not move `resolution_due_at`; it increments the dispute's `version` by 1.

**Resolution** is also the `DisputeResolutionOfficer`'s, may only be performed from `UNDER_REVIEW`, requires the logical clock to be **strictly before** `resolution_due_at` — at or after that instant the dispute can only be deemed allowed by the sweep, and a resolution attempt is refused with `ERR_DISPUTE_SLA_EXPIRED` — and requires the acting `X-User-ID` to differ from the invoice's `issued_by_user_id`. The request supplies a `decision`, a `credited_amount_usd` and a `resolution_note`. A `decision` of `SUSTAINED` requires `credited_amount_usd` to be greater than 0.00 and no greater than the dispute's `disputed_amount_usd`; a `decision` of `DENIED` requires `credited_amount_usd` to be exactly 0.00. Any other pairing is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CREDIT_AMOUNT`, and that decision-dependent ceiling is enforced as a fiscal rule rather than as a range check on the field.

The two outcomes are specified symmetrically, and they differ in exactly one respect — whether money moves. A **sustained** dispute moves `UNDER_REVIEW → RESOLVED_SUSTAINED`, which is terminal. Its `resolved_at` and `resolved_by_user_id` are stamped; the invoice's `credited_amount_usd` increases by the granted credit and its `open_balance_usd` falls by the same amount; the invoice's `disputed_amount_usd` is reduced by the dispute's `disputed_amount_usd` and its `dispute_status` moves `DISPUTED → RESOLVED` once no open dispute remains against it; the reserve recomputation re-runs and both a `DISPUTE_RELEASE` reserve ledger entry and an `UNCOLLECTIBLE_ADJUSTMENT` reserve ledger entry are appended, each with `source_event` `BILLING_DISPUTE_RESOLVED`; and if the credit drives `open_balance_usd` to 0.00 the invoice's `payment_status` auto-moves to `PAID`. A **denied** dispute moves `UNDER_REVIEW → RESOLVED_DENIED`. Its `resolved_at` and `resolved_by_user_id` are likewise stamped and no credit is posted; the invoice's `disputed_amount_usd` is reduced by the dispute's `disputed_amount_usd` and its `dispute_status` moves `DISPUTED → RESOLVED`; and a `DISPUTE_RELEASE` reserve ledger entry is appended for the released provision. In both outcomes the dispute's, the invoice's and the carrier's `version` values each increment by 1.

`RESOLVED_DENIED` is not terminal — it is the entry point to the **escalation cycle**. The `CarrierRepresentative` escalates a denied dispute with an `escalation_note`, moving it `RESOLVED_DENIED → ESCALATED`. Escalations are capped at **two**: the round the escalation would create, which is the dispute's current `escalation_round` plus one, must be no greater than two, and an attempt beyond that is refused with `ERR_ESCALATION_LIMIT_REACHED`. Escalation increments `escalation_round` by 1 and extends `resolution_due_at` by **fifteen days from its previous due instant**, not from the current clock. It also returns the dispute to the open set: the invoice's `disputed_amount_usd` increases again by the dispute's `disputed_amount_usd`, the invoice's `dispute_status` moves `RESOLVED → DISPUTED`, and a `DISPUTE_PROVISION` reserve ledger entry with `source_event` `BILLING_DISPUTE_ESCALATED` is appended. The dispute's, the invoice's and the carrier's `version` values each increment by 1. From `ESCALATED` the officer acknowledges again into `UNDER_REVIEW`, closing the `RESOLVED_DENIED → ESCALATED → UNDER_REVIEW → RESOLVED_DENIED` loop that the escalation cap terminates. Across that loop the same provision is booked, released and re-booked.

**Withdrawal** is the counterparty's escape hatch, available from any of the three open statuses — `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `ESCALATED` — with a `withdrawal_reason`, and it moves the dispute to `WITHDRAWN`, which is terminal. All three source states behave identically: `resolved_at` and `resolved_by_user_id` are stamped, `credited_amount_usd` stays 0.00, the invoice's `disputed_amount_usd` is reduced by the dispute's `disputed_amount_usd` and its `dispute_status` moves `DISPUTED → RESOLVED`, and a `DISPUTE_RELEASE` reserve ledger entry with `source_event` `BILLING_DISPUTE_WITHDRAWN` is appended that **exactly offsets every `DISPUTE_PROVISION` entry booked for this dispute** — including the escalation provision when withdrawal happens from `ESCALATED` — plus an `UNCOLLECTIBLE_ADJUSTMENT` entry restoring the pre-dispute uncollectible balance. After withdrawal the carrier's `disputed_reserve_balance_usd` returns to its pre-dispute value with no drift. The dispute's, the invoice's and the carrier's `version` values each increment by 1.

Acknowledgement, resolution, escalation and withdrawal each require the dispute to be in a status from which their transition exists, and every other status is refused with `ERR_INVALID_DISPUTE_STATE`: acknowledgement only from `SUBMITTED` or `ESCALATED`, resolution only from `UNDER_REVIEW`, escalation only from `RESOLVED_DENIED`, and withdrawal only from `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `ESCALATED`.

The **dispute deadline sweep** is the operations checkpoint that enforces the SLA. A `PlatformAdmin` runs it with no payload, and it walks every dispute whose `status` is `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `ESCALATED` and whose `resolution_due_at` the logical clock has **reached or passed** — the comparison is inclusive, so a sweep at exactly the due instant deems the dispute allowed, which is precisely the boundary the resolve operation excludes. Candidates are processed in `filed_at` ascending order, with `created_at` ascending breaking ties. Each swept dispute moves to `RESOLVED_DEEMED_ALLOWED`, which is terminal, from whichever open status it held; its `credited_amount_usd` is set to its full `disputed_amount_usd`, its `resolved_at` is stamped, and its `resolved_by_user_id` is set to the literal string `SYSTEM_SWEEP`. Each affected invoice has its `credited_amount_usd` increased by the full disputed amount, its `open_balance_usd` reduced, its `disputed_amount_usd` recomputed and its `dispute_status` moved `DISPUTED → RESOLVED`, and its `lifecycle_status` may auto-move to `CLOSED` under the derivation of §2.10. The reserve recomputation runs and `DISPUTE_RELEASE` plus `UNCOLLECTIBLE_ADJUSTMENT` reserve ledger entries with `source_event` `DISPUTE_DEADLINE_SWEEP` are appended. Each swept dispute's, its invoice's and its carrier's `version` values each increment by 1. Already resolved, deemed-allowed and withdrawn disputes are never re-swept. The sweep reports `swept_count`, the number of disputes it moved, and `total_credited_usd`, the total credit it granted.

Missing the SLA therefore deems the dispute allowed in the counterparty's favour, which is exactly why the due instant is an inclusive boundary for the sweep and an exclusive one for the officer.

### 3.10. Mutual Record Exchange Reconciliation

Carriers exchange usage records with each other, and the two sides rarely agree exactly. A `RevenueAssuranceController` or an `AccessBillingAnalyst` opens a **reconciliation case** against a batch, naming the `usage_batch_id`, the `counterparty_reported_minutes` (at four decimals, zero or greater) and `counterparty_reported_amount_usd` (at two decimals, zero or greater) the other side computed for the same period and direction, and the `exchange_reference` of the counterparty file being reconciled, which must be non-empty. At most one case may exist per batch-and-exchange-reference pair; a repeat is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RECONCILIATION_CASE`. Creation snapshots our side of the comparison: `our_minutes` from the batch's `total_billable_minutes` and `our_amount_usd` from the batch's `rated_amount_usd`. The case lands in `status` `OPEN` with `minute_variance_pct`, `amount_variance_usd`, `agreed_minutes`, `adjustment_amount_usd` and `adjusted_at` all unset.

**Evaluation** is the `RevenueAssuranceController`'s, runs only from `OPEN`, and computes both variance figures. If the counterparty reported zero minutes, `minute_variance_pct` is 100.00 when our own minutes are greater than zero and 0.00 when they are not. Otherwise `minute_variance_pct` is our minutes less the counterparty's minutes, multiplied by one hundred and divided by the counterparty's minutes, rounded half up to two decimal places. `amount_variance_usd` is `our_amount_usd` less `counterparty_reported_amount_usd`, rounded half up to two decimal places. Both figures are written to the case regardless of the outcome.

Evaluation then branches on a **two percent** tolerance, and the comparison is strictly greater-than. If the absolute value of `minute_variance_pct` exceeds 2.00, the case moves `OPEN → VARIANCE_IDENTIFIED` and a settlement conversation is required. If the absolute value is 2.00 or less, the case moves `OPEN → ACCEPTED`, which is terminal, and no adjustment is posted. A variance of exactly 2.00 therefore resolves to `ACCEPTED`, not `VARIANCE_IDENTIFIED`. Either way the case's `version` increments by 1.

From `VARIANCE_IDENTIFIED` the controller has two exits. **Adjusting** the case settles on `agreed_minutes`, which must lie inside the closed interval spanned by the smaller and the larger of `our_minutes` and `counterparty_reported_minutes`, both edges included; a value outside that interval is refused with `ERR_AGREED_MINUTES_OUT_OF_RANGE`. The batch's access invoice must exist with `lifecycle_status` `ISSUED`, or the adjustment is refused with `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_ISSUED`, and that invoice's `payment_status` must not be `WRITTEN_OFF`. The credit is computed at the invoice's blended effective rate: `blended_rate_per_mou_usd` is `our_amount_usd` divided by `our_minutes`, rounded half up to six decimal places, and `adjustment_amount_usd` is `our_minutes` less `agreed_minutes`, multiplied by that blended rate, rounded half up to two decimal places. Because agreed minutes may never exceed our own by more than the interval allows, and the interval is bounded by the counterparty's figure, a negative adjustment cannot arise from a valid request. The case moves `VARIANCE_IDENTIFIED → ADJUSTED`, which is terminal, with `adjusted_at` stamped from the logical clock; the invoice's `credited_amount_usd` increases by `adjustment_amount_usd` and its `open_balance_usd` falls by the same amount, with `payment_status` possibly advancing as a result; the reserve recomputation of §2.11 re-runs for the carrier and an `UNCOLLECTIBLE_ADJUSTMENT` reserve ledger entry with `source_event` `RECONCILIATION_ADJUSTED` is appended; and the case's, the invoice's and the carrier's `version` values each increment by 1.

**Accepting** a variance instead documents it without posting money. The controller supplies an `acceptance_note`, the case moves `VARIANCE_IDENTIFIED → ACCEPTED`, `adjustment_amount_usd` stays unset, no invoice or carrier row is touched, and the case's `version` increments by 1.

Evaluation requires `OPEN`; adjustment and acceptance both require `VARIANCE_IDENTIFIED`. Any other status is refused with `ERR_INVALID_RECONCILIATION_STATE`.

The **reserve ledger** itself is read-only and append-only. Filtering it by `carrier_id` gives a carrier's full reserve history in `created_at` ascending order, and narrowing further by `invoice_id` isolates the movements attributable to a single invoice; `reserve_bucket` and `entry_type` filters separate the disputed-revenue story from the uncollectible one. Because entries are never rewritten, the last entry in a bucket's history always carries that bucket's current `balance_after_usd`, and the entries between any two points sum exactly to the difference between their balances.
