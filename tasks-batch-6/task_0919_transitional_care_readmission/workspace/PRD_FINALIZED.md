# Transitional Care & Readmission Program API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Hospital discharge is the single most dangerous handoff in American healthcare. A patient who leaves an inpatient bed on Friday afternoon with eleven medications, a follow-up appointment nobody scheduled, and no ride to the clinic has a materially higher chance of being back in a hospital bed within a month than a clinically identical patient whose transition was actively managed. The Transitional Care & Readmission Program exists to manage that thirty-day gap deliberately: to screen every discharge referral for programme eligibility, to score the patient's readmission risk, to reach the patient by telephone within a contractual service-level window, to reconcile the discharge medication list against what the patient is actually taking, to get a qualifying face-to-face visit on the calendar inside the window that the risk tier demands, to clear the transport, interpreter, prior-authorisation and pharmacy obstacles that quietly cause missed appointments, and to escalate clinical warning signs to a named human being on a clock.

This document specifies the REST API that runs that programme. The system is mounted under `/tcm`, with a single administrative clock endpoint at `/_clock/set_current_utc`. It is best understood as a compliance and audit system wearing a workflow-orchestration coat: every deadline it enforces, every escalation it raises and every dollar it bills has to be reconstructable after the fact from immutable, versioned records. Because the programme's revenue depends on Transitional Care Management billing codes — and because those codes may only be claimed when the interactive contact, the medication reconciliation and the face-to-face visit all happened inside their respective windows — the API treats timeliness facts as first-class persisted state rather than as something recomputed at billing time.

Two design consequences run through everything that follows. First, the whole system is driven by a **logical clock** rather than by wall-clock time, so that a thirty-day window can be exercised deterministically. Second, the `CareEpisode` is the aggregate root: risk assessments, outreach attempts, the face-to-face visit, the medication reconciliation, barriers, red flags, readmission events, the claim and any care-team handoff all hang beneath it, and the episode carries an independent record-posture axis that can freeze the entire subtree against further mutation once the episode's financial and clinical story is finished.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Programme Actors and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to this API — including every read — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that user is acting under. If either header is absent or present but empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` and HTTP 401, before any other check runs. If both headers are present but the supplied role is not one of the roles authorised for the endpoint being called, the request is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` and HTTP 403. The role names are case-sensitive and are spelled exactly: `ProgramAdmin`, `IntakeCoordinator`, `CareNavigator`, `Clinician`, `BillingSpecialist`.

`ProgramAdmin` is programme operations. It owns configuration (facilities, payers, navigators, the holiday calendar), the logical clock, the operational sweep, service-period closure, administrative episode closure, readmission recording and retraction, claim adjudication and claim voiding, and it is the only role that may close a barrier as waived. `IntakeCoordinator` staffs the discharge-referral intake desk: it records referrals and makes the accept-or-close decision. `CareNavigator` is the transitional-care navigator who owns outreach, appointment scheduling, barrier management, re-assessment and handoff for the episodes assigned to them. `Clinician` covers treating clinicians and pharmacists, and owns the face-to-face encounter record, its attestation and amendment, and the entire medication-reconciliation surface. `BillingSpecialist` is revenue-cycle staff: it prepares, submits and appeals the TCM claim, and it owns barriers of the insurance-authorisation type. All five roles may call every read endpoint.

The meaning of `X-User-ID` differs by role, and this distinction is load-bearing. When `X-User-Role` is `CareNavigator`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Navigator` row; ownership checks on any resource that hangs off a `CareEpisode` compare that episode's `navigator_id` to `X-User-ID`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_NOT_EPISODE_OWNER` and HTTP 403. When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramAdmin`, `IntakeCoordinator`, `Clinician` or `BillingSpecialist`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a workflow demands an audit trail — as `performed_by_user_id` on a reconciliation, `attested_by_user_id` on a visit, `raised_by_user_id` and `acknowledged_by_user_id` on a red flag, `recorded_by_user_id` on a readmission event, and `prepared_by_user_id` and `submitted_by_user_id` on a claim — but it never compares those opaque identifiers to any entity's `id`.

Two operations enforce **segregation of duties** by comparing two recorded identifiers rather than checking a role. A claim may not be submitted by the same `X-User-ID` that prepared it, and a red flag may not be resolved by the same `X-User-ID` that raised it. Either violation yields `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` with HTTP 403.

Finally, three authorisation decisions in this system are made against data rather than against the endpoint's static role list, and all three surface as `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`: closing a barrier as resolved is permitted only to the role recorded in that barrier's `owner_role`; closing a barrier as waived is permitted only to `ProgramAdmin`; and acknowledging a red flag is permitted only to the routing role that corresponds to the flag's **current** `tier`, which may have changed since the flag was raised.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10 when omitted, may be no lower than 1 and no higher than 100. `offset` defaults to 0 when omitted and may be no lower than 0. A value outside those bounds, or one that is not an integer, is a request-validation failure and is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION` and HTTP 400.

List results are always ordered by `created_at` ascending — oldest first — regardless of which filters are applied. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit` and `offset` echoing the effective values used, `total_count` giving the number of rows matching the filters before pagination was applied, and `has_more` set to true when rows remain beyond the current page. Every list endpoint also exposes domain filters, which are documented alongside the workflows they serve in section 3; filters combine conjunctively, and an unrecognised query parameter is ignored.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock, the Calendar and the Holiday Set

Nothing in this API consults wall-clock time. The server maintains a single logical clock, referred to throughout this document as the current instant, and every deadline, service-level window, cooldown, breach test and thirty-day boundary is evaluated against it. Before any caller has moved it, the clock reads exactly `2026-05-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time. A `ProgramAdmin` may move it forwards or backwards by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant; the response reports the resulting `current_utc`. Moving the clock mutates no entity — it neither escalates a barrier nor expires a handoff on its own. Those consequences are produced only by the operational sweep described in section 3.6, or by whatever request happens to be evaluated next.

Two kinds of temporal value appear in payloads and must be formatted precisely. Calendar dates — `discharge_date`, `date_of_birth`, `visit_due_date`, `service_period_end_date`, `readmission_window_end_date`, `readmission_date`, `service_period_start_date` and the members of the holiday set — are plain `YYYY-MM-DD` strings with no time component. Instants — `discharge_datetime`, `scheduled_start`, `scheduled_end`, `referred_at`, `attempted_at`, `disposition_at`, `assessed_at`, `opened_at`, `sla_due_at`, `closed_at`, `raised_at`, `ack_due_at`, `acknowledged_at`, `completed_at`, `attested_at`, `contact_deadline_at`, `successful_contact_at`, `visit_completed_at`, `proposed_at`, `expires_at`, `responded_at` and `paid_at` — are ISO 8601 UTC timestamps with exactly three fractional-second digits and a trailing `Z`. Wherever this document speaks of a calendar date's **end of day**, it means that date with the time suffix `T23:59:59.999Z` appended; that is the instant a date-valued deadline actually expires.

Business-day arithmetic recognises exactly five weekdays — Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday and Friday — as business days. Saturday and Sunday never count. On top of that, the programme maintains a single global holiday set: a `ProgramAdmin` may replace it wholesale by supplying `holiday_dates`, an array of `YYYY-MM-DD` strings, and the response echoes the stored `holiday_dates`. Replacing the holiday set is not inert: it immediately recomputes `contact_deadline_at` for every care episode whose payer measures the contact deadline in business days and whose record posture is still open, and then recomputes those episodes' `contact_timely` flag against the new deadline. Episodes belonging to a payer that measures the deadline in calendar hours are untouched by a holiday change, as are episodes whose records have been frozen.

### 2.5. Common Resource Properties and Nested Retrieval

Every persisted resource in this system — `Patient`, `Facility`, `Payer`, `Navigator`, `DischargeReferral`, `CareEpisode`, `RiskAssessment`, `ContactAttempt`, `TcmVisit`, `MedReconciliation`, `MedDiscrepancy`, `Barrier`, `RedFlag`, `ReadmissionEvent`, `TcmClaim` and `HandoffRequest` — carries the same four common properties. `id` is a server-minted UUID string with no type prefix and no client control over its value. `created_at` and `updated_at` are ISO 8601 UTC instants with three fractional digits, taken from the logical clock; `updated_at` is refreshed on every mutation that changes the row. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. There are no exceptions: no resource in this API omits `updated_at` or `version`.

URLs in this API are hierarchical, and path parameters are validated strictly left to right. The `{episode_id}` segment is special-cased: whenever a path contains it, failure to resolve it to an existing `CareEpisode` is reported as `ERR_EPISODE_NOT_FOUND` with HTTP 404, and that check runs before any other identifier is examined. Every other identifier — whether it arrives as a path segment or as a foreign key in the request body — is reported as `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` with HTTP 404, and those identifiers are examined left to right through the path and then in body-field declaration order: `patient_id`, `facility_id`, `payer_id`, `navigator_id`, `referral_id`, `risk_assessment_id`, `contact_attempt_id`, `visit_id`, `med_reconciliation_id`, `discrepancy_id`, `barrier_id`, `red_flag_id`, `readmission_id`, `claim_id`, `handoff_id`, `to_navigator_id`.

Resolving to *some* row is not the same as resolving to the *right* row. Two operations additionally check that a body-supplied reference actually belongs to the parent named in the path, and both report `ERR_ENTITY_LINK_MISMATCH` with HTTP 409: preparing a claim under an episode while naming a `visit_id` whose visit belongs to a different episode, and proposing a handoff whose `to_navigator_id` is the episode's own current `navigator_id`.

Several relationships are stored as a pointer on one side and are discovered from the other side through list filters rather than through a nested collection URL. A referral records the episode it minted in `created_episode_id`, and episodes can be listed filtered by `referral_id`. An episode records `current_risk_assessment_id` and `claim_id`, and risk assessments can be listed filtered by `episode_id`, by `supersedes_id` and by `is_current`, while claims can be listed filtered by `episode_id`, `patient_id`, `visit_id`, `payer_id` and `status`. A barrier records `escalated_red_flag_id`, and red flags can be listed filtered by `source_barrier_id`. A red flag records `spawned_readmission_id`, and readmission events can be listed filtered by `source_red_flag_id`. A superseded handoff records `superseded_by_id`, and handoffs can be listed filtered by `episode_id`, `from_navigator_id`, `to_navigator_id` and `status`.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency on Contested Aggregates

Three write operations in this system can silently destroy work that the caller never saw, and all three are therefore guarded by an optimistic-concurrency check on the parent record's `version`. In each case the version field is a **required** part of the request body — omitting it is a plain request-validation failure — and in each case a value that does not equal the parent's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` and HTTP 412.

Proposing a handoff on a care episode requires `expected_episode_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of that `CareEpisode`. A new proposal legitimately supersedes any handoff still awaiting a response on the same episode, so a caller working from a stale view of the episode would cancel a proposal it never read; if the episode's `version` has advanced since the client last read it, the proposal is refused rather than applied.

Appending a discrepancy line item to a medication reconciliation requires `expected_med_reconciliation_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of that `MedReconciliation`. Every disposition and every reopen shifts the reconciliation's counters, so a caller with a stale view would be appending a line item against a conservation state it has not seen; a mismatch is refused.

Amending attested visit documentation requires `expected_visit_version` carrying the client's last observed `version` of that `TcmVisit`. An amendment replaces the previously attested element set outright, so an amendment written from a stale view would discard an attestation the writer never read; a mismatch is refused.

No other endpoint in this API accepts a version field, and no other endpoint performs a version comparison.

### 2.7. Record Posture: the Global Write Gate

A `CareEpisode` carries two independent status axes. The first, `status`, is the clinical and financial lifecycle: `INTAKE`, `ACTIVE`, `SERVICE_PERIOD_CLOSED`, `BILLED`, `CLOSED_READMITTED`, `ADMIN_CLOSED`. The second, `record_posture`, is a pure write gate with two values, `OPEN` and `FROZEN`, and it advances on its own schedule. An episode can sit in `BILLED` or `CLOSED_READMITTED` with `record_posture` still `OPEN` — that happens whenever a claim is still moving.

The freeze evaluation is re-run after every mutation that changes an episode's `status` or the `status` of any claim belonging to that episode. It flips `record_posture` from `OPEN` to `FROZEN` when two things are simultaneously true: the episode's `status` is one of `BILLED`, `CLOSED_READMITTED` or `ADMIN_CLOSED`, **and** the episode has no claim sitting in `PREPARED`, `SUBMITTED`, `DENIED` or `APPEALED`. An episode with no claim at all satisfies the second condition trivially.

Freezing is not merely a flag; it runs a three-branch cascade over the episode's children. Every handoff request still in `PROPOSED` moves to `SUPERSEDED` with `superseded_by_id` left null, because no newer proposal caused it. Every red flag in `RAISED` or `ACKNOWLEDGED` moves to `CLOSED_ADMINISTRATIVE`, which drives the episode's `open_red_flag_count` down to 0 and its `escalation_state` back to `NONE`. Every barrier in `OPEN` or `ESCALATED` moves to `CLOSED_ADMINISTRATIVE` with `closed_at` stamped at the current instant.

Once an episode is frozen, every subsequent mutation aimed at that episode or at any `RiskAssessment`, `ContactAttempt`, `TcmVisit`, `MedReconciliation`, `MedDiscrepancy`, `Barrier`, `RedFlag`, `HandoffRequest` or `TcmClaim` beneath it is rejected with `ERR_EPISODE_RECORDS_FROZEN` and HTTP 409. Exactly three mutating capabilities are exempt, because the programme must be able to keep tracking readmissions after the money is settled: recording a readmission event against the episode, retracting a recorded readmission event, and setting the logical clock. Every read endpoint is likewise unaffected.

Thawing is possible in exactly one direction. Retracting the unplanned-inpatient readmission that closed an episode restores the episode's `status` from `pre_readmission_status`, which takes it back out of the frozen status set, and the freeze evaluation then returns `record_posture` to `OPEN`. Children that the cascade already moved to `SUPERSEDED` or `CLOSED_ADMINISTRATIVE` are **not** restored — those states are terminal. And because `BILLED` and `ADMIN_CLOSED` are terminal on the status axis, a freeze reached through either of them is permanent.

### 2.8. Points, Money and Rounding Discipline

Two families of numbers in this system are rounded, and both round **half up** — a value exactly at the midpoint rounds away from zero.

Risk points are whole integers. The rounding is applied **per component, before the components are summed**: each of the five risk components is rounded to a whole number on its own, and only then are the five whole numbers added together. Summing the raw products first and rounding the total once is a different calculation that produces a different score for the same inputs, and it is not what this system does.

Money is held exclusively in whole cents as integers; there are no fractional-cent values anywhere in a claim, and no floating-point currency is ever returned. Rounding half up to whole cents is applied at each of the three points in the claim waterfall where a multiplication occurs — the allowed amount, the coinsurance amount and the withhold amount — and never retroactively to a figure that has already been rounded.

### 2.9. Counter Conservation

Three counter families in this system are conservation quantities: they may move between buckets, but the totals never drift. Each invariant must hold after every single mutation, not merely at the end of a workflow.

First, on a medication reconciliation, `discrepancy_count` always equals `resolved_count` plus `unresolved_count` plus `deferred_count`. A disposition moves one unit out of the unresolved bucket into either the resolved or the deferred bucket; a reopen moves one unit back. Discrepancies are never deleted, so the total only grows when a genuinely new line item is appended.

Second, on a care episode, `readmission_event_count` always equals `counted_readmission_count` plus `excluded_readmission_count` plus `retracted_readmission_count`. Retracting an event moves one unit out of whichever of the first two buckets held it and into the retracted bucket, leaving the grand total untouched.

Third, across the whole programme, the sum of `active_episode_count` over all navigators always equals the number of care episodes whose `status` is `INTAKE`, `ACTIVE` or `SERVICE_PERIOD_CLOSED`. An accepted handoff decrements one navigator and increments another inside the same request, so the global total never drifts; episodes in `BILLED`, `CLOSED_READMITTED` or `ADMIN_CLOSED` contribute to nobody's count.

### 2.10. Request Validation Order

When a single request would fail more than one rule, the rule with the **lowest rung number** below decides the response. This ordering is normative: an implementation that reports a later rung's error where an earlier rung also failed is wrong, even if both conditions genuinely hold. The conditions themselves are described in full in the workflow narratives of section 3; this list fixes only their relative precedence and the error each one raises.

1. Both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` are present and non-empty — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. The supplied role appears in the endpoint's authorised role list — otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. Every required body field is present, carries the declared JSON type, and satisfies its numeric, format or ordering constraint — otherwise `ERR_VALIDATION` (400). Unknown body fields are ignored. This rung also covers the conditional requiredness on referral creation (`length_of_stay_days` is required when `stay_type` is `INPATIENT`; `length_of_stay_hours` is required when `stay_type` is `OBSERVATION`) and the requirement that `scheduled_end` be strictly after `scheduled_start`. Applies to every endpoint that accepts a request body.
4. Every supplied enum-typed body field carries a declared member value — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_ENUM` (400). The complete set of body fields that route to this error is: `discharge_disposition`, `stay_type` and `admission_acuity` on referral creation; `outcome` on referral closure, on visit encounter closure and on barrier closure; `facility_type` and `network_status` on facility registration; `contact_deadline_basis` on payer registration; `cap_policy` on navigator registration; `modality` on logging a contact attempt; `outcome` on recording a contact-attempt disposition; the members of `documented_elements` on visit completion and on visit amendment; `discrepancy_type` and `severity` on appending a discrepancy; `disposition` on dispositioning a discrepancy; `barrier_type` on raising a barrier; `category` and `initial_tier` on raising a red flag; `resolution_outcome` on resolving a red flag; `classification` on recording a readmission; `handoff_type` on proposing a handoff; `response` on responding to a handoff; and `outcome` and `denial_reason_code` on claim adjudication. No other field routes to this error.
5. An `{episode_id}` path segment resolves to an existing care episode — otherwise `ERR_EPISODE_NOT_FOUND` (404).
6. Every other path identifier and every body-supplied foreign key resolves to an existing row, checked left to right — otherwise `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (404).
7. A body-supplied reference belongs to the parent named in the path — otherwise `ERR_ENTITY_LINK_MISMATCH` (409). The two loci are claim preparation (`visit_id` from another episode) and handoff proposal (`to_navigator_id` equal to the episode's current navigator).
8. When the acting role is `CareNavigator`, `X-User-ID` equals the `navigator_id` of the care episode that owns the target resource — otherwise `ERR_NOT_EPISODE_OWNER` (403). This rung guards exactly ten mutating operations: creating a risk re-assessment, logging a contact attempt, recording a contact-attempt disposition, booking a visit, confirming a visit, closing a visit encounter, rescheduling a visit, raising a barrier, raising a red flag, and proposing a handoff.
9. **The global write gate.** The care episode that owns the target resource has `record_posture` equal to `OPEN` — otherwise `ERR_EPISODE_RECORDS_FROZEN` (409). Exempt: recording a readmission, retracting a readmission, setting the logical clock, and every read.
10. The target resource is in a state from which the requested transition is declared — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409). One explicit carve-out: on claim preparation the episode-status requirement is **not** evaluated here; it is evaluated at rung 26 and reported as `ERR_SERVICE_PERIOD_OPEN`.
11. The supplied `expected_episode_version`, `expected_med_reconciliation_version` or `expected_visit_version` equals the parent's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412).
12. Segregation of duties holds — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). Applies to claim submission and red-flag resolution.
13. **Intake gauntlet rung 1.** The referral's `discharge_disposition` is one of `HOME`, `HOME_HEALTH`, `SNF_SHORT_TERM` or `ASSISTED_LIVING` — otherwise `ERR_DISPOSITION_INELIGIBLE` (422). Applies to referral acceptance.
14. **Intake gauntlet rung 2.** The calendar date of the referral's `referred_at` is on or before its `discharge_date` plus 2 days — otherwise `ERR_INTAKE_WINDOW_EXPIRED` (422).
15. **Intake gauntlet rung 3.** The patient's `open_episode_count` is 0 — otherwise `ERR_EPISODE_ALREADY_OPEN` (409).
16. **Intake gauntlet rung 4.** The referral's payer has `tcm_covered` true — otherwise `ERR_PAYER_NOT_COVERED` (422).
17. **Intake gauntlet rung 5.** The referral's facility has `network_status` equal to `IN_NETWORK` — otherwise `ERR_FACILITY_OUT_OF_NETWORK` (422).
18. The episode's `contact_attempt_count` is strictly less than 4 — otherwise `ERR_CONTACT_ATTEMPT_LIMIT` (422). Applies to logging a contact attempt.
19. Temporal-overlap rules hold — otherwise `ERR_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT` (409). On booking and on rescheduling a visit, the requested interval must not collide with another scheduled visit of the same navigator under the twenty-minute trailing buffer; on logging a contact attempt, at least 240 minutes must have elapsed since the previous attempt.
20. The supplied `modality` matches the ladder position for this attempt number — otherwise `ERR_MODALITY_OUT_OF_SEQUENCE` (422).
21. The episode has no blocking-class barrier in `OPEN` or `ESCALATED` — otherwise `ERR_BLOCKING_BARRIER_OPEN` (422). Applies to appointment confirmation.
22. The episode's medication reconciliation exists and is `COMPLETED`, and on attestation was completed no later than the visit's `completed_at` — otherwise `ERR_MEDREC_NOT_COMPLETE` (422). Applies to visit attestation and to claim submission.
23. The episode's medication reconciliation has `blocking_unresolved_count` equal to 0 — otherwise `ERR_BLOCKING_DISCREPANCY_UNRESOLVED` (422). Applies to visit attestation and to reconciliation completion.
24. A discrepancy may be deferred only when its `severity` is `LOW` or `MODERATE` — otherwise `ERR_DEFERRAL_NOT_PERMITTED` (422).
25. The visit's `documented_elements` contains every member of the moderate required-element set — otherwise `ERR_REQUIRED_ELEMENTS_INCOMPLETE` (422). Applies to visit attestation.
26. The service-period gate: closing the service period requires the current instant to be strictly after the end of `service_period_end_date`, and preparing a claim requires the episode's `status` to be `SERVICE_PERIOD_CLOSED` — otherwise `ERR_SERVICE_PERIOD_OPEN` (422).
27. A readmission's `day_index` is between 1 and 30 inclusive — otherwise `ERR_READMISSION_OUT_OF_WINDOW` (422).
28. The episode has a completed visit whose documentation is attested or amended — otherwise `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_BILLABLE` (422). Applies to claim preparation.
29. The visit's `completed_at` is on or before the end of `discharge_date` plus 14 days — otherwise `ERR_VISIT_WINDOW_MISSED` (422). Applies to claim preparation.
30. The episode's `contact_timely` is true — otherwise `ERR_CONTACT_NOT_TIMELY` (422). Applies to claim submission.
31. No other non-voided claim exists for the same patient over an overlapping service period — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_TCM_CLAIM` (409). Applies to claim preparation.
32. The claim's `appeal_count` is strictly less than 2 — otherwise `ERR_APPEAL_LIMIT_REACHED` (422). Applies to filing an appeal.
33. The current instant is on or before the handoff's `expires_at` — otherwise `ERR_HANDOFF_EXPIRED` (409). Applies to responding to a handoff.
34. For an internal-navigator handoff, the receiving navigator's `workload_score` plus the incoming episode's weight is at most their `active_episode_cap` — otherwise `ERR_NAVIGATOR_CAP_EXCEEDED` (409). Applies to accepting a handoff.

Two of these rungs deliberately mutate state before returning their rejection, and the persisted change survives the error: rung 32 flips the claim from `DENIED` to `DENIED_FINAL` with `net_payable_cents` zeroed before returning `ERR_APPEAL_LIMIT_REACHED`, and rung 33 flips the handoff to `EXPIRED` before returning `ERR_HANDOFF_EXPIRED`. Because both flips persist — and because a handoff can equally be moved out of `PROPOSED` unattended, by phase 3 of the operational sweep, by a newer proposal or by the freeze cascade — a request that arrives once the row has **already** left the state its transition is declared from never reaches rung 32 or rung 33 at all: it fails the rung-10 state gate and is reported as `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Responding to a handoff is declared only from `PROPOSED`, so a response to a handoff already in `EXPIRED`, `SUPERSEDED`, `ACCEPTED` or `DECLINED` is `ERR_INVALID_STATE` rather than `ERR_HANDOFF_EXPIRED`; filing an appeal is declared only from `DENIED`, so a further attempt on a claim already in `DENIED_FINAL` is `ERR_INVALID_STATE` rather than `ERR_APPEAL_LIMIT_REACHED`. The earlier rungs keep their precedence as usual — on an episode whose records are frozen, for instance, both of those operations report `ERR_EPISODE_RECORDS_FROZEN` at rung 9 before the state gate is ever consulted.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Programme Reference Data and the Discharge-Referral Gauntlet

Before any referral can be recorded, the programme's reference data must exist. Registering a patient captures an `external_mrn` — the medical record number issued by the discharging health system, which must be non-empty and unique across all patients — together with `full_name` and a `date_of_birth` in `YYYY-MM-DD` form. An optional `primary_language` holds the ISO-639-1 code used for outreach and defaults to `EN` when omitted. A freshly registered patient starts with all three of its rolling counters at 0: `open_episode_count`, which counts that patient's care episodes in `INTAKE`, `ACTIVE` or `SERVICE_PERIOD_CLOSED`; `lifetime_episode_count`, which counts every care episode the patient has ever had in any status; and `lifetime_readmission_count`, which counts the recorded readmission events across all of the patient's episodes that count toward the readmission rate.

A discharging facility is registered with a `name`, a `facility_type` that is one of `ACUTE_HOSPITAL`, `CRITICAL_ACCESS`, `REHAB` or `LTACH_FACILITY`, and a `network_status` that is either `IN_NETWORK` or `OUT_OF_NETWORK`. Only an in-network facility can feed the programme. A payer is registered with a `name` and a `tcm_covered` boolean stating whether that plan reimburses the transitional-care management codes at all; a `tcm_rate_multiplier`, which must be greater than 0 and at most 2.0 and defaults to 1.0; a `coinsurance_rate` between 0 and 0.5 inclusive, defaulting to 0.2; and a `contact_deadline_basis` of either `BUSINESS_DAYS` or `CALENDAR_HOURS`, defaulting to `BUSINESS_DAYS`. That last field is the fork that decides how the follow-up contact deadline is measured, and it is read from the payer that the episode pinned at intake — later edits to the payer do not retroactively re-pin an episode.

Registering a transitional-care navigator captures a `full_name`, an `active_episode_cap` which is an integer between 1 and 40 inclusive and defaults to 5, and a `cap_policy` of either `COUNT` or `WEIGHTED`, defaulting to `COUNT`. A new navigator starts with `active_episode_count` and `workload_score` at 0 and `availability_status` at `AVAILABLE`; `availability_status` reads `AVAILABLE` while `workload_score` is strictly less than `active_episode_cap` and `AT_CAP` otherwise, and it is recomputed every time the navigator's caseload moves.

An `IntakeCoordinator` records a discharge referral by supplying the `patient_id`, `facility_id` and `payer_id` it concerns, the `discharge_date` that will serve as day 0 of every downstream window, the exact `discharge_datetime` instant of discharge, the `discharge_disposition`, the `stay_type`, the `admission_acuity`, and the four utilisation and social inputs: `comorbidity_count` (an integer from 0 to 30), `prior_ed_visits_6mo` (0 to 30), `prior_admissions_6mo` (0 to 30) and `social_risk_flags_count` (0 to 12). `discharge_disposition` must be one of `HOME`, `HOME_HEALTH`, `SNF_SHORT_TERM`, `ASSISTED_LIVING`, `HOSPICE`, `LTACH`, `EXPIRED` or `AMA`. `stay_type` is either `INPATIENT` or `OBSERVATION`, and it decides which length-of-stay field the referral must carry: an inpatient referral requires `length_of_stay_days`, a whole number of days between 0 and 90 inclusive, and ignores `length_of_stay_hours`; an observation referral requires `length_of_stay_hours`, a whole number of hours between 0 and 240 inclusive, and ignores `length_of_stay_days`. `admission_acuity` is `ELECTIVE`, `URGENT` or `EMERGENT`. The referral is stamped with a `referred_at` reading of the logical clock and enters `RECEIVED`; `closure_reason_code` and `created_episode_id` are both null at this point. Referrals may be listed filtered by `patient_id`, `facility_id`, `payer_id` and `status`.

Crucially, recording a referral performs **no** eligibility screening whatsoever. A referral for a patient discharged to hospice, from an out-of-network facility, under a plan that does not cover the codes, recorded three weeks late, is accepted into `RECEIVED` without complaint. All of the screening happens when an `IntakeCoordinator` accepts the referral, and it happens as an **ordered gauntlet** whose sequence is observable: the first failing rung decides the response, so a referral that fails several rungs always reports the earliest one.

The gauntlet runs as follows. First, the disposition must be programme-eligible — that is, one of `HOME`, `HOME_HEALTH`, `SNF_SHORT_TERM` or `ASSISTED_LIVING`. The four ineligible dispositions — `HOSPICE`, `LTACH`, `EXPIRED` and `AMA` — are rejected with `ERR_DISPOSITION_INELIGIBLE`. Second, the referral must be timely: the calendar date on which it was recorded must fall on or before the discharge date plus two days, so a discharge on 2026-05-01 admits referrals dated 2026-05-01, 2026-05-02 or 2026-05-03, and anything later is rejected with `ERR_INTAKE_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. Third, the patient must have no open episode — an `open_episode_count` of exactly 0 — since a patient already in `INTAKE`, `ACTIVE` or `SERVICE_PERIOD_CLOSED` cannot be enrolled twice; a duplicate enrolment is rejected with `ERR_EPISODE_ALREADY_OPEN`. Fourth, the payer must have `tcm_covered` true, or the acceptance is rejected with `ERR_PAYER_NOT_COVERED`. Fifth and last, the facility's `network_status` must read `IN_NETWORK`, or the acceptance is rejected with `ERR_FACILITY_OUT_OF_NETWORK`. Acceptance is legal only from `RECEIVED`; attempting it on a referral that has already been accepted, denied or withdrawn is an `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

Acceptance also carries one body field: the `navigator_id` of the navigator who will be accountable for the episode. That navigator must exist — an unknown identifier is an `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` — and, notably, the receiving navigator's workload cap is **not** consulted at intake. Cap enforcement exists only on the handoff path described in section 3.7.

When every rung passes, acceptance moves the referral `RECEIVED → ACCEPTED` and mints, in one atomic step, a great deal of state. A new `CareEpisode` is created in `status` `INTAKE` with `record_posture` `OPEN`, copying `patient_id`, `payer_id`, `facility_id`, `discharge_date` and `discharge_datetime` from the referral and taking the supplied `navigator_id`; the referral's `created_episode_id` is set to the new episode's `id`, and the episode's `referral_id` points back. The episode's first `RiskAssessment` is created with `is_current` true and `supersedes_id` null, and the risk score, the risk tier, the tier-driven programme parameters, the follow-up contact deadline and the visit due date are all derived as described in sections 3.2 and 3.3. The episode's `service_period_end_date` is set to `discharge_date` plus 29 days and its `readmission_window_end_date` to `discharge_date` plus 30 days. The patient's `open_episode_count` and `lifetime_episode_count` each increase by 1. The navigator's `active_episode_count` increases by 1, and their `workload_score` and `availability_status` are recomputed.

A referral that will not become an episode is closed instead, again only from `RECEIVED`. Closure takes an `outcome` of either `DENIED` or `WITHDRAWN` and a `closure_reason_code` of `NOT_ELIGIBLE`, `DUPLICATE`, `PATIENT_DECLINED` or `OTHER`. The two outcomes are deliberately symmetrical: both move the referral to a terminal state, both store the supplied `closure_reason_code`, neither mints a `CareEpisode`, and neither touches any patient or navigator counter. They differ only in the `status` recorded: `RECEIVED → DENIED` when the programme declined the referral, and `RECEIVED → WITHDRAWN` when the referring party pulled it.

Care episodes may be read individually, exposing every derived field, both status axes and all four readmission counters, or listed with filters on `patient_id`, `navigator_id`, `referral_id`, `payer_id`, `facility_id` and `status`.

### 3.2. Readmission Risk Scoring and the Tier Fork

Every care episode is scored the moment it is created, and may be re-scored afterwards. A `RiskAssessment` records the inputs it used, the five component scores it produced, the total and the tier. Four of its inputs are always copied from the episode's originating referral and can never be re-supplied: `stay_type`, `length_of_stay_days`, `length_of_stay_hours` and `admission_acuity`. The other four — `comorbidity_count`, `prior_ed_visits_6mo`, `prior_admissions_6mo` and `social_risk_flags_count` — come from the referral on the very first assessment and from the request body on every subsequent re-assessment. Each assessment stamps `assessed_at` from the logical clock and records its `episode_id`.

The score is built from five components, each computed and **rounded to a whole number on its own** before anything is added together.

The first component is length of stay, and it is a genuine fork on `stay_type`. For an inpatient stay, `los_variant` is recorded as `INPATIENT_DAYS` and `los_points` is one and a half points for every whole day of stay — the number of days multiplied by 1.5, rounded half up to a whole number, and then capped at 12 points. For an observation stay, `los_variant` is recorded as `OBSERVATION_HOURS` and `los_points` is one point for every twelve hours — the number of hours divided by 12, rounded half up to a whole number, and then capped at 6 points. The observation cap is deliberately half the inpatient cap, so no observation stay, however long, can out-score a long inpatient stay on this component. Whichever variant runs, the other length field is ignored entirely.

The second component is acuity, and it is an exact lookup with no arithmetic and no rounding: an `ELECTIVE` admission contributes 0 points, an `URGENT` admission contributes 6 points, and an `EMERGENT` admission contributes 10 points, stored in `acuity_points`.

The third component is comorbidity burden. `comorbidity_points` is two and a half points per active chronic condition on the discharge problem list — the `comorbidity_count` multiplied by 2.5, rounded half up to a whole number — capped at 20 points.

The fourth component is prior utilisation. `utilization_points` is three points for each emergency-department visit in the previous six months plus five points for each inpatient admission in the previous six months, capped at 24 points. Both multipliers are whole numbers, so no rounding is needed here; only the cap can bind.

The fifth component is the social modifier. `social_modifier_points` is two and a half points for each documented social-risk flag — the `social_risk_flags_count` multiplied by 2.5, rounded half up to a whole number — capped at 10 points.

Only once all five components are whole numbers are they added, and the sum is then capped at 60 to yield `risk_score`. The order matters and is not negotiable: rounding each component first and summing afterwards is the specified calculation, and summing the raw products before a single rounding is a different, incorrect calculation. Consider a patient with a nine-day inpatient stay, an urgent admission, seven comorbidities, two prior ED visits, one prior admission and three social flags: the components round to 14 capped at 12, 6, 18, 11 and 8 respectively, giving a total of 55.

The tier is banded directly off `risk_score`, with every floor inclusive and every ceiling exclusive. A score of 45 or more is `SEVERE`; a score from 32 through 44 is `HIGH`; a score from 18 through 31 is `MODERATE`; anything below 18 is `LOW`. The boundaries are worth stating exactly: 17 is `LOW`, 18 is `MODERATE`, 31 is `MODERATE`, 32 is `HIGH`, 44 is `HIGH` and 45 is `SEVERE`. The resulting `risk_tier` is written onto the assessment and mirrored onto the episode, together with `risk_score` and `current_risk_assessment_id`.

The tier immediately determines two programme parameters. `contact_sla_business_days` is 1 for a `SEVERE` episode and 2 for `LOW`, `MODERATE` and `HIGH` alike — the severe tier is the only one that compresses the follow-up window. `required_visit_complexity` is `HIGH_COMPLEXITY` for `HIGH` and `SEVERE` episodes and `MODERATE_COMPLEXITY` for `LOW` and `MODERATE` episodes. From the required complexity follows the face-to-face deadline: a high-complexity episode's `visit_due_date` is the discharge date plus 7 calendar days, and a moderate-complexity episode's is the discharge date plus 14 calendar days — exactly twice the window from the same discharge date. These are calendar days, not business days, and the discharge date itself is day 0, so a high-complexity episode discharged on 2026-05-01 is due by the end of 2026-05-08. The due date expires at the end of that day, meaning `T23:59:59.999Z`.

A `CareNavigator` who learns new clinical facts may re-score their own episode by supplying fresh `comorbidity_count`, `prior_ed_visits_6mo`, `prior_admissions_6mo` and `social_risk_flags_count` values. Because a re-assessment can change the tier, and the tier drives two deadlines, this operation cascades four hops deep and the implementer must run all of them.

The first hop creates a new `RiskAssessment` with `is_current` true and `supersedes_id` pointing at the assessment it replaces, and flips that predecessor's `is_current` to false; exactly one assessment per episode is ever current, and the superseded rows remain readable. The second hop rewrites the episode's `risk_score`, `risk_tier`, `current_risk_assessment_id`, `contact_sla_business_days`, `required_visit_complexity`, `contact_deadline_at` and `visit_due_date` from the new assessment. The third hop pushes the new figures down onto every non-terminal visit of the episode, refreshing each visit's `required_complexity`, `visit_due_date` and `deadline_status`; a visit still in `SCHEDULED` whose `scheduled_start` now falls past the newly tightened due instant flips its `deadline_status` from `SCHEDULED_IN_WINDOW` to `SCHEDULED_LATE`. The fourth hop reacts to that: **each** visit that flipped to `SCHEDULED_LATE` spawns a red flag with `category` `VISIT_WINDOW_AT_RISK` and tier `TIER_2`, increasing the episode's `open_red_flag_count` and setting its `escalation_state` to `ACTIVE`. Finally, `contact_timely` is recomputed against the new deadline. Risk assessments may be read individually — showing all five component scores — or listed filtered by `episode_id`, by `supersedes_id` and by `is_current`.

### 3.3. Interactive Follow-Up Contact

The programme's first obligation after discharge is to reach the patient — actually reach them, not leave a message. The episode tracks this on its own axis, `contact_status`, which begins at `NOT_STARTED`, moves to `IN_PROGRESS` as soon as the first outreach attempt is logged, and ends at either `SUCCESSFUL` or `REFUSED`.

The deadline for that contact, `contact_deadline_at`, is computed at intake and re-computed on every re-assessment, and it is one of the two places in this API where the payer's own configuration changes the arithmetic. Let N stand for the episode's `contact_sla_business_days`.

When the episode's payer measures the deadline in **business days** — the default basis — the system walks forward from `discharge_date` one calendar day at a time. A day counts toward N only when its weekday is Monday through Friday **and** its date is not in the programme's holiday set. The discharge date itself is day 0 and never counts, even when it falls on a business day. When N business days have been consumed, the deadline is the end of the day reached, that is that date with `T23:59:59.999Z` appended. A patient discharged on Friday 2026-05-01 with N of 2, and no holidays declared, has a deadline of `2026-05-05T23:59:59.999Z`: Saturday and Sunday are skipped, Monday 2026-05-04 is the first business day and Tuesday 2026-05-05 the second.

When the episode's payer measures the deadline in **calendar hours**, no weekend or holiday is skipped at all. The SLA is read as N multiplied by 24 wall-clock hours, measured from the exact `discharge_datetime` instant, and the deadline lands wherever that arithmetic puts it — typically mid-day. The same Friday discharge at `2026-05-01T14:00:00Z` with N of 2 yields a deadline of `2026-05-03T14:00:00.000Z`, two full days earlier than the business-days variant produced from identical facts.

Outreach itself is a bounded, ordered ladder. At most **four** attempts may ever be logged against an episode; a fifth is rejected with `ERR_CONTACT_ATTEMPT_LIMIT`. Each attempt's `attempt_number` is assigned by the server as the episode's current `contact_attempt_count` plus 1, and `contact_attempt_count` counts every attempt row ever created for the episode regardless of how it turned out. The modality is prescribed by position, not chosen freely: the first attempt must be `PHONE`, the second must be `PHONE` again, the third must be `SECURE_MESSAGE` and the fourth must be `HOME_VISIT`. A `modality` that does not match the position is rejected with `ERR_MODALITY_OUT_OF_SEQUENCE`. Attempts also observe a cooldown: at least 240 minutes — four hours — must have elapsed between the previous attempt's `attempted_at` and the current instant. The boundary is inclusive, so an attempt logged exactly 240 minutes later is allowed while one logged at 239 minutes is rejected with `ERR_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT`. The previous attempt for this purpose is the one with the greatest `attempt_number`, which is unique within an episode, so no tie-break is needed. When the checks are ordered, the four-attempt cap is tested first, the cooldown second and the modality ladder third.

Logging an attempt is the navigator's own work and is guarded by episode ownership. The attempt row is stamped with `attempted_at` from the logical clock, enters `attempt_status` `LOGGED`, carries an optional free-text `notes` string that defaults to the empty string, and leaves `disposition_at` null. The very first attempt on an episode also drives the episode from `INTAKE` to `ACTIVE` and its `contact_status` from `NOT_STARTED` to `IN_PROGRESS`. Attempts may be listed filtered by `episode_id` and by `attempt_status`, and read individually. A new attempt may not be logged while an earlier attempt on the same episode is still sitting in `LOGGED` without a disposition, nor once `contact_status` has reached `SUCCESSFUL` or `REFUSED`; all three situations are rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

Recording the outcome closes the attempt, and the four outcomes diverge sharply in what they do to the episode. `CONNECTED` moves the attempt `LOGGED → CONNECTED`, sets the episode's `contact_status` to `SUCCESSFUL` and its `successful_contact_at` to the current instant, and recomputes `contact_timely` as true when `successful_contact_at` is on or before `contact_deadline_at` — the comparison is inclusive at the deadline instant. It also unblocks completion of the face-to-face visit, which cannot be recorded until interactive contact has succeeded. `NO_ANSWER` moves the attempt `LOGGED → NO_ANSWER` and leaves the episode at `IN_PROGRESS`, as does `VOICEMAIL`, which moves it `LOGGED → VOICEMAIL`; in both cases the next attempt must use the next modality on the ladder, and neither outcome touches `contact_timely`. `REFUSED` is the sharp one: it moves the attempt `LOGGED → REFUSED`, sets the episode's `contact_status` to `REFUSED` so that no further attempt may ever be created for that episode, and raises a system red flag with `category` `CONTACT_REFUSED` at `TIER_3` in `RAISED` status, whose `raised_by_user_id` is recorded as the literal string `SYSTEM` and whose acknowledgement deadline is 480 minutes after the current instant. That flag increments the episode's `open_red_flag_count` and sets `escalation_state` to `ACTIVE`. Every disposition stamps the attempt's `disposition_at`, and a disposition may only be recorded once, from `LOGGED`.

Once the service period has been closed, `contact_timely` is frozen at whatever value it then held, and no later contact activity or holiday-set change alters it.

### 3.4. The Face-to-Face Visit: Two Independent Axes

The transitional-care visit is the billable clinical event, and the record of it advances along two axes that do not wait for each other. `encounter_status` tracks what happened in the room — `SCHEDULED`, `COMPLETED`, `NO_SHOW`, `CANCELLED` — while `documentation_status` tracks the paperwork — `DRAFT`, `ATTESTED`, `AMENDED`. A third, smaller axis, `confirmation_status`, records only whether the appointment has been confirmed with the patient and holds either `UNCONFIRMED` or `CONFIRMED`.

Booking a slot is navigator work and requires the `navigator_id` whose calendar the visit will occupy together with `scheduled_start` and `scheduled_end`. The interval is half-open — it includes its start instant and excludes its end instant — and `scheduled_end` must be strictly after `scheduled_start`, or the request fails validation. A newly booked visit copies `required_complexity` and `visit_due_date` from its episode, opens at `encounter_status` `SCHEDULED`, `documentation_status` `DRAFT` and `confirmation_status` `UNCONFIRMED`, and receives an initial `deadline_status`.

Double-booking is prevented by a calendar check that spans **all** episodes, not merely the one being scheduled: the candidate set is every visit belonging to the same `navigator_id` whose `encounter_status` is `SCHEDULED`, excluding the visit currently being rescheduled. Completed, no-show and cancelled visits release their interval and never occupy the calendar again. Each candidate is treated as occupying its own interval **plus a trailing turnaround buffer of 20 minutes appended to its end**; there is deliberately no leading buffer before a candidate's start. A conflict exists when the proposed start falls before the candidate's end plus twenty minutes and the candidate's start falls before the proposed end. The asymmetry is visible at the boundaries: a new visit starting exactly twenty minutes after an existing visit ends does **not** conflict, one starting nineteen minutes after it ends **does** conflict, and a new visit ending exactly when an existing visit starts does **not** conflict. Any collision is rejected with `ERR_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT`.

Timeliness is carried in `deadline_status`, which is recomputed whenever a visit is booked, rescheduled, completed or closed, and whenever a re-assessment moves the episode's `visit_due_date`. The deadline instant is the end of `visit_due_date`. While the encounter is still `SCHEDULED`, the status reads `SCHEDULED_IN_WINDOW` when `scheduled_start` is on or before that instant and `SCHEDULED_LATE` when it is after it. Once the encounter is `COMPLETED`, the status reads `MET` when `completed_at` is on or before the deadline instant and `MISSED` when it is after. For an encounter closed as `NO_SHOW` or `CANCELLED`, the status becomes `MISSED` when the current instant is strictly past the deadline, and otherwise keeps whatever value it already held.

Confirming the appointment with the patient sets `confirmation_status` to `CONFIRMED`, and it is the one visit operation that a barrier can veto. While any barrier of a blocking class on the episode sits in `OPEN` or `ESCALATED`, confirmation is refused with `ERR_BLOCKING_BARRIER_OPEN`; the appointment cannot be promised to a patient who has no way to get there, no interpreter-independent path and no authorised medication.

A `Clinician` records the completed encounter, supplying `documented_elements`, the array of service elements actually delivered. Completion is legal only from `SCHEDULED`, only when `confirmation_status` reads `CONFIRMED`, and only when the episode's `contact_status` has already reached `SUCCESSFUL` — a face-to-face visit cannot be booked into existence as a substitute for the interactive contact, and attempting completion without it is an `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Completion moves the encounter `SCHEDULED → COMPLETED`, stamps the visit's `completed_at` and the episode's `visit_completed_at` with the current instant, stores the supplied elements, sets `deadline_status` to `MET` or `MISSED` against the due instant, and recomputes the episode's `visit_timely` — true exactly when `visit_completed_at` is non-null and on or before the end of `visit_due_date`, inclusive at that instant. Like `contact_timely`, `visit_timely` is frozen once the service period closes.

An encounter that does not happen is closed instead, with an `outcome` of either `NO_SHOW` or `CANCELLED`, and here the two branches genuinely differ. `SCHEDULED → NO_SHOW` releases the navigator's calendar interval so that a replacement slot may reuse it, and marks `deadline_status` `MISSED` if the current instant is strictly past the due instant — but it is not the end of the road. A no-show visit may be rescheduled: supplying a fresh `scheduled_start` and `scheduled_end` moves the encounter `NO_SHOW → SCHEDULED`, re-runs the full double-booking check against the new interval, resets `confirmation_status` to `UNCONFIRMED` so the patient must be re-confirmed, and recomputes `deadline_status` as `SCHEDULED_IN_WINDOW` or `SCHEDULED_LATE`. That pair of edges is a genuine cycle: a visit may bounce between `NO_SHOW` and `SCHEDULED` any number of times while the episode's records stay open. `CANCELLED`, reachable from either `SCHEDULED` or `NO_SHOW`, releases the interval permanently and is terminal on the encounter axis — a cancelled visit can never be rescheduled.

Documentation runs on its own clock. Attestation is a `Clinician` action and is gated on four things at once: the encounter must already be `COMPLETED`; the episode's medication reconciliation must exist and be `COMPLETED`, and must have been completed no later than the visit's `completed_at` instant, or the request is refused with `ERR_MEDREC_NOT_COMPLETE`; that reconciliation must have no blocking unresolved discrepancy, or the request is refused with `ERR_BLOCKING_DISCREPANCY_UNRESOLVED`; and the visit's `documented_elements` must contain every member of the moderate required-element set — `MED_REC_REVIEWED`, `DISCHARGE_SUMMARY_REVIEWED` and `PATIENT_EDUCATION` — or the request is refused with `ERR_REQUIRED_ELEMENTS_INCOMPLETE`. A successful attestation moves the documentation `DRAFT → ATTESTED`, records `attested_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID` and `attested_at` from the logical clock, and derives `achieved_complexity`: `HIGH_COMPLEXITY` when the documented elements cover the full high-complexity set — `MED_REC_REVIEWED`, `DISCHARGE_SUMMARY_REVIEWED`, `PATIENT_EDUCATION`, `HIGH_COMPLEXITY_MDM` and `COMMUNITY_SERVICE_REFERRAL` — and `MODERATE_COMPLEXITY` otherwise. Before the first attestation `achieved_complexity` is null.

Attested documentation may be revised. An amendment replaces `documented_elements` outright, moves the documentation to `AMENDED`, increments `amendment_count` by 1, re-derives `achieved_complexity` from the new element set and increments the visit's own `version`; it is legal from either `ATTESTED` or `AMENDED`, and it is the operation that carries `expected_visit_version`. Re-attesting an amended record moves it `AMENDED → ATTESTED`, re-stamps `attested_by_user_id` and `attested_at` and re-derives `achieved_complexity`, subject to the same reconciliation and required-element gates as the first attestation. `ATTESTED → AMENDED → ATTESTED` is a revision cycle that may run any number of times, entirely independently of the encounter axis, which is already terminal at `COMPLETED`. Visits may be read individually, showing both axes, or listed filtered by `episode_id`, `navigator_id`, `encounter_status` and `documentation_status`.

### 3.5. Medication Reconciliation and Discrepancy Governance

Every episode gets at most one medication reconciliation, opened by a `Clinician`. Opening it takes no body fields; the reconciliation is created in `status` `OPEN` with `discrepancy_count`, `resolved_count`, `unresolved_count` and `deferred_count` all at 0, `blocking_unresolved_count` at 0, `reopen_count` at 0, `completed_at` null, and `performed_by_user_id` recording the acting `X-User-ID`. A second attempt to open a reconciliation for the same episode is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Reconciliations may be listed filtered by `episode_id` and `status`.

Each discrepancy found is appended as a line item carrying the `medication_name` involved, a `discrepancy_type` of `OMISSION`, `DUPLICATION`, `DOSE_MISMATCH`, `INTERACTION` or `ALLERGY_CONFLICT`, and a clinical `severity` of `LOW`, `MODERATE`, `HIGH` or `CRITICAL`. Appending is the operation that carries `expected_med_reconciliation_version`. A new line item is created in `status` `UNRESOLVED` with a null `resolution_note` and a null `disposition_at`, its `med_reconciliation_id` pointing at the parent. The parent's `discrepancy_count` and `unresolved_count` each rise by 1, its `blocking_unresolved_count` rises by 1 as well when the severity is `HIGH` or `CRITICAL`, and its `version` increments. The **first** line item also drives the reconciliation `OPEN → IN_REVIEW`. Line items may be read individually or listed filtered by `med_reconciliation_id`, `severity` and `status`.

An unresolved line item is dispositioned with a `disposition` of either `RESOLVED` or `DEFERRED`, optionally accompanied by a `resolution_note`, and the disposition stamps `disposition_at`. `UNRESOLVED → RESOLVED` moves one unit out of the parent's `unresolved_count` and into its `resolved_count`, then recomputes `blocking_unresolved_count`. `UNRESOLVED → DEFERRED` moves one unit out of `unresolved_count` and into `deferred_count` — but deferral is available only to line items whose `severity` is `LOW` or `MODERATE`. A `HIGH` or `CRITICAL` discrepancy is clinically non-deferrable and must actually be resolved; attempting to defer one is rejected with `ERR_DEFERRAL_NOT_PERMITTED`. Either disposition requires the parent reconciliation to be in `OPEN` or `IN_REVIEW`.

A dispositioned line item can be pulled back. Reopening moves it `RESOLVED → UNRESOLVED` or `DEFERRED → UNRESOLVED`, taking one unit back out of `resolved_count` or `deferred_count` respectively and returning it to `unresolved_count`, and then recomputing `blocking_unresolved_count`. Reopening is permitted while the parent reconciliation reads `OPEN`, `IN_REVIEW` **or** `COMPLETED`, and it never changes the parent's own status. That produces a deliberately awkward and important state: a reconciliation can read `COMPLETED` while a freshly reopened `HIGH` or `CRITICAL` line item sits in `UNRESOLVED`, and in that state visit attestation is blocked again by `ERR_BLOCKING_DISCREPANCY_UNRESOLVED` even though the reconciliation's own status still says it is done. Throughout all of this, the parent's `discrepancy_count` always equals `resolved_count` plus `unresolved_count` plus `deferred_count` — a disposition and a reopen each move exactly one unit between buckets and never create or destroy one.

Completing the reconciliation has two legal entry points and they are not the same check. From `OPEN` it is allowed only when `discrepancy_count` is exactly 0 — the empty-set boundary, where a reconciliation that found nothing completes directly without ever passing through `IN_REVIEW`. From `IN_REVIEW` it is allowed only when `blocking_unresolved_count` is 0, meaning no `HIGH` or `CRITICAL` line item remains unresolved; deferred low and moderate items do not block completion. A blocking item present at this moment is reported as `ERR_BLOCKING_DISCREPANCY_UNRESOLVED`. Completion stamps `completed_at` with the current instant, sets the episode's `medrec_complete` to true, and unblocks visit attestation.

A completed reconciliation may be reopened by a `Clinician`, moving it `COMPLETED → IN_REVIEW`, incrementing `reopen_count` by 1, clearing `completed_at` and setting the episode's `medrec_complete` back to false — which re-blocks attestation until the reconciliation is completed again. `COMPLETED → IN_REVIEW → COMPLETED` is a revision cycle that may run repeatedly. Like the other two compliance flags, `medrec_complete` is frozen at its then-current value once the service period is closed.

### 3.6. Barriers, Red Flags and the Operational Sweep

A **barrier** is a concrete obstruction between the patient and their care plan, and its type alone fixes three things: how long the programme has to clear it, who is accountable for clearing it, and whether it blocks the appointment. A `CareNavigator` raises one on their own episode by supplying a `barrier_type` and an optional free-text `description` that defaults to the empty string. The barrier is stamped with `opened_at` from the logical clock and opens in `status` `OPEN` with a null `closed_at` and a null `escalated_red_flag_id`.

The five types resolve as follows. `TRANSPORTATION` carries an `sla_hours` of 48, is owned by `CareNavigator`, and is blocking. `INSURANCE_AUTH` carries 72 hours, is owned by `BillingSpecialist`, and is blocking. `LANGUAGE` carries 24 hours, is owned by `CareNavigator`, and is **not** blocking. `CAREGIVER_AVAILABILITY` carries 96 hours, is owned by `CareNavigator`, and is **not** blocking. `PHARMACY_ACCESS` carries 36 hours, is owned by `Clinician`, and is blocking. The derived `blocking_class` is therefore true for exactly the transportation, insurance-authorisation and pharmacy-access types, and the derived `owner_role` and `sla_hours` follow the table above. `sla_due_at` is `opened_at` plus `sla_hours` hours. Breach is tested strictly: a barrier is breached only when the current instant is strictly after `sla_due_at`, so a barrier sitting exactly at its due instant is not yet breached. Barriers may be read individually or listed filtered by `episode_id`, `barrier_type` and `status`.

Closing a barrier takes an `outcome` of either `RESOLVED` or `WAIVED` and is legal from `OPEN` or from `ESCALATED`. Resolving is restricted to the barrier's own `owner_role` — an insurance-authorisation barrier is resolved by a `BillingSpecialist` and a pharmacy-access barrier by a `Clinician`, and anyone else is turned away with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`. Waiving is restricted to `ProgramAdmin` on the same terms. Either outcome stamps `closed_at` with the current instant and stops the barrier from blocking appointment confirmation; once no blocking barrier on the episode remains `OPEN` or `ESCALATED`, confirmation becomes available again. Closing a barrier does **not** clean up after it: a red flag that an earlier escalation already spawned is left exactly where it is and keeps running its own acknowledgement clock. A barrier may also end in `CLOSED_ADMINISTRATIVE`, but only as a consequence of the episode's records being frozen, which likewise stamps `closed_at`.

A **red flag** is a clinical or operational escalation with a named owner and a countdown. It may be raised directly by a `CareNavigator` or a `Clinician` with a `category` of `CLINICAL_SYMPTOM`, `MEDICATION_ISSUE`, `BARRIER_SLA_BREACH`, `CONTACT_REFUSED` or `VISIT_WINDOW_AT_RISK`, and an `initial_tier` of `TIER_1`, `TIER_2` or `TIER_3`. The flag opens in `RAISED` with `tier` equal to `initial_tier`, `promotion_count` at 0, `raised_by_user_id` set to the acting `X-User-ID`, `raised_at` set to the current instant, and `source_barrier_id` null. Raising a flag increments the episode's `open_red_flag_count` and sets its `escalation_state` to `ACTIVE`; the episode's `escalation_state` reads `ACTIVE` for as long as `open_red_flag_count` is above 0 and `NONE` once it returns to 0.

The tier is not decoration — it sets both the clock and the owner. `TIER_1` must be acknowledged within 30 minutes and is routed to a `Clinician`. `TIER_2` must be acknowledged within 120 minutes and is routed to a `CareNavigator`. `TIER_3` must be acknowledged within 480 minutes and is also routed to a `CareNavigator`. `ack_due_at` is set to `raised_at` plus the current tier's allowance. Acknowledging moves the flag `RAISED → ACKNOWLEDGED`, records `acknowledged_by_user_id` and `acknowledged_at`, and permanently stops the promotion clock — an acknowledged flag can never be promoted or breached. Only the routing role for the flag's **current** tier may acknowledge it, and because the tier can change under promotion, the acceptable role can change with it; the wrong role is refused with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`. Red flags may be listed filtered by `episode_id`, `source_barrier_id`, `tier` and `status`.

Resolving a flag moves it `ACKNOWLEDGED → RESOLVED` and requires a `resolution_outcome` of `RESOLVED_AT_HOME`, `RESOLVED_URGENT_CARE` or `SENT_TO_ED`. The resolver may not be the same `X-User-ID` that raised the flag. Resolution decrements the episode's `open_red_flag_count`, returning `escalation_state` to `NONE` when the counter reaches 0. A `SENT_TO_ED` outcome has a further consequence: when the current calendar date falls between the day after `discharge_date` and `readmission_window_end_date` inclusive, the system automatically creates a readmission event classified `ED_TREAT_AND_RELEASE`, dated the current calendar date, whose `source_red_flag_id` points back at this flag and whose `recorded_by_user_id` is the literal string `SYSTEM`; the flag's `spawned_readmission_id` records the new event. When the current date lies outside that window, no event is created at all and the flag's `ed_event_suppressed` is set to true instead. The other two outcomes never create anything.

Barriers breach and flags escalate only when a `ProgramAdmin` runs the **operational sweep** on an episode. Nothing in this system escalates spontaneously; the sweep is the checkpoint, and it runs three phases in a fixed order, reporting `barriers_escalated`, `red_flags_created`, `red_flags_promoted`, `red_flags_breached` and `handoffs_expired`.

Phase 1 handles barriers. Every barrier of a **blocking** class that is still `OPEN` and whose `sla_due_at` is strictly in the past moves `OPEN → ESCALATED` and spawns a red flag with `category` `BARRIER_SLA_BREACH` at `TIER_2` in `RAISED`, whose `source_barrier_id` names the breached barrier, whose `raised_by_user_id` is `SYSTEM`, and whose `ack_due_at` is 120 minutes after the current instant; the barrier's `escalated_red_flag_id` records that flag. Escalation also reaches sideways into the calendar: every visit of the episode still `SCHEDULED` and `CONFIRMED` has its `confirmation_status` reverted to `UNCONFIRMED`, because a confirmed appointment behind a breached blocking barrier is not a promise the programme can keep. Non-blocking barrier types never escalate; a language or caregiver-availability barrier simply sits `OPEN` past its due instant until somebody closes it.

Phase 2 handles red flags, and it processes **only** flags in `RAISED` whose `ack_due_at` is strictly in the past — acknowledged flags are immune. Such a flag either promotes or breaches. It promotes when its current `tier` is not already `TIER_1` **and** its `promotion_count` is below 2: the tier steps up one notch along `TIER_3 → TIER_2 → TIER_1`, `promotion_count` increases by 1, `ack_due_at` is reset to the current instant plus the **new** tier's allowance (120 minutes when the new tier is `TIER_2`, 30 minutes when it is `TIER_1`), and the role entitled to acknowledge it changes with the tier. The flag stays in `RAISED` throughout; promotion is a self-loop, not a state change. It breaches instead — moving `RAISED → BREACHED` and decrementing the episode's `open_red_flag_count`, with `escalation_state` returning to `NONE` at 0 — when its `tier` is already `TIER_1` **or** its `promotion_count` has already reached 2. The loop is therefore bounded twice over: a flag raised at `TIER_3` promotes at most twice before breaching, and a flag raised at `TIER_2` promotes at most once. A red flag created by phase 1 of a sweep is **never** processed by phase 2 of that same sweep; it gets its full acknowledgement window and can only be promoted by a later sweep.

Phase 3 handles handoffs and is described in section 3.7.

### 3.7. Care-Team Handoff and Navigator Workload

Accountability for an episode can move, but only through an explicit two-sided handshake. The episode's current `CareNavigator` proposes the transfer by naming a `to_navigator_id` and a `handoff_type` of `INTERNAL_NAVIGATOR`, `COMMUNITY_PROGRAM` or `PCP_PROGRAM`, and by supplying `expected_episode_version`. The proposal records `from_navigator_id` as the episode's navigator at the moment of proposal, opens in `PROPOSED`, stamps `proposed_at` from the logical clock, and sets `expires_at` to 24 hours after `proposed_at`. Proposing also increments the episode's own `version` by 1 — which is precisely why the operation is version-guarded, since a second proposal made from a stale view would silently cancel a proposal its author never saw. Naming the episode's own current navigator as the recipient is a link error and is refused with `ERR_ENTITY_LINK_MISMATCH`. A fresh proposal supersedes any handoff on the same episode still sitting in `PROPOSED`, moving it `PROPOSED → SUPERSEDED` with `superseded_by_id` set to the new proposal's `id`. Handoffs may be read individually or listed filtered by `episode_id`, `from_navigator_id`, `to_navigator_id` and `status`.

Only the proposed recipient may answer. A response from any `X-User-ID` other than the recorded `to_navigator_id` is refused with `ERR_NOT_EPISODE_OWNER` and HTTP 403 — note that the responder is by construction *not* the episode's current navigator, so this is the one navigator-facing operation where ownership is measured against the handoff rather than against the episode. The answer is a `response` of either `ACCEPT` or `DECLINE`, optionally accompanied by a `decline_reason`, and it must arrive on or before `expires_at`. A response that arrives after expiry does not simply fail: the handoff row is first flipped `PROPOSED → EXPIRED`, that change is persisted, and only then is the caller told `ERR_HANDOFF_EXPIRED`. The same expiry also happens unattended in phase 3 of the operational sweep, where every proposal past its `expires_at` is moved to `EXPIRED`; either way, the episode's `navigator_id` is left exactly where it was.

`DECLINE` moves the handoff `PROPOSED → DECLINED`, stores the supplied `decline_reason` and stamps `responded_at`. The episode's `navigator_id` does not move and no workload counter changes anywhere.

`ACCEPT` moves the handoff `PROPOSED → ACCEPTED`, stamps `responded_at`, reassigns the episode's `navigator_id` to the accepting navigator, decrements the sending navigator's `active_episode_count` by 1 and increments the receiving navigator's by 1, and recomputes `workload_score` and `availability_status` for both. Because the decrement and the increment happen in the same request, the programme-wide total of `active_episode_count` across all navigators continues to equal the number of episodes in `INTAKE`, `ACTIVE` or `SERVICE_PERIOD_CLOSED`.

Acceptance is where the workload cap bites, and how heavy an episode weighs depends on the **receiving navigator's own** `cap_policy` rather than on any programme-wide setting. A navigator's `active_episode_count` is always the plain number of episodes assigned to them whose `status` is `INTAKE`, `ACTIVE` or `SERVICE_PERIOD_CLOSED`; episodes in `BILLED`, `CLOSED_READMITTED` or `ADMIN_CLOSED` never contribute. Under the `COUNT` policy, `workload_score` is simply that count, every open episode weighs exactly 1 whatever its risk tier, and an incoming episode weighs 1 — a navigator with a cap of 2 can hold two `SEVERE` episodes without difficulty. Under the `WEIGHTED` policy, each open episode weighs according to its `risk_tier`: 1 for `LOW`, 1 for `MODERATE`, 2 for `HIGH` and 3 for `SEVERE`; `workload_score` is the sum of those weights across the navigator's open episodes, and the incoming episode weighs whatever its own tier is worth. The same two `SEVERE` episodes score 6 under this policy, and a navigator with a cap of 2 could not hold even one of them alongside anything else. `availability_status` reads `AVAILABLE` while `workload_score` is strictly below `active_episode_cap` and `AT_CAP` otherwise, and a navigator can be read at any time to see both live figures.

The acceptance gate itself is inclusive: the receiving navigator's current `workload_score` plus the incoming episode's weight must be **at most** their `active_episode_cap`, so landing exactly on the cap is permitted and exceeding it is refused with `ERR_NAVIGATOR_CAP_EXCEEDED`. This gate applies only when `handoff_type` is `INTERNAL_NAVIGATOR`. A `COMMUNITY_PROGRAM` or `PCP_PROGRAM` handoff bypasses the cap entirely, because the destination is outside the navigator roster.

A proposal can also die without anyone answering it. When an episode's records freeze, every handoff still in `PROPOSED` is moved to `SUPERSEDED` with `superseded_by_id` left null — the null is what distinguishes a freeze-driven supersession from one caused by a newer proposal.

### 3.8. Closing the Service Period and Billing the TCM Code

The transitional-care service period is 30 calendar days long counted inclusively from the discharge date. Day 0 is `discharge_date` itself, which is also `service_period_start_date`, so `service_period_end_date` is `discharge_date` plus 29 days. The readmission tracking window is one day longer: `readmission_window_end_date` is `discharge_date` plus 30 days. That one-day offset is deliberate, and it means the readmission window is still open on the very first day the service period becomes closable.

Closing the service period is a `ProgramAdmin` action available only from `ACTIVE`, and only once the current instant is strictly after the end of `service_period_end_date`. The earliest legal moment is therefore `discharge_date` plus 30 days at `00:00:00.000Z`; an attempt before that is refused with `ERR_SERVICE_PERIOD_OPEN`, and an attempt on an episode not in `ACTIVE` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Closure moves the episode `ACTIVE → SERVICE_PERIOD_CLOSED` and freezes the three compliance flags — `contact_timely`, `visit_timely` and `medrec_complete` — at exactly their then-current values, after which no later mutation changes them. Closure is also what makes billing legal.

A `BillingSpecialist` prepares the episode's single claim by naming the `visit_id` of the attested face-to-face visit being billed. That visit must belong to this episode, or the request fails with `ERR_ENTITY_LINK_MISMATCH`. The episode's `status` must read `SERVICE_PERIOD_CLOSED`; if it does not, the failure is reported as `ERR_SERVICE_PERIOD_OPEN` rather than as a generic invalid-state error — this is the one place where an episode-status requirement is deliberately taken out of the ordinary state check and evaluated later in the ladder. The episode must have a visit whose `encounter_status` is `COMPLETED` and whose `documentation_status` is `ATTESTED` or `AMENDED`, since without attested documentation there is no billable service; the absence of one is `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_BILLABLE`. The visit's `completed_at` must be on or before the end of `discharge_date` plus 14 days, because no TCM code covers a later encounter; a later visit is `ERR_VISIT_WINDOW_MISSED`. And exactly one billable TCM claim may exist per patient per service period: if another claim for the same `patient_id` covers an overlapping service period and is not `VOIDED`, preparation is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_TCM_CLAIM`.

Code selection is a two-way fork driven by the interaction of the tier the episode required, the complexity the clinician actually documented, and the day the encounter happened. Two deadlines matter: the high-complexity deadline at the end of `discharge_date` plus 7 days, and the moderate deadline at the end of `discharge_date` plus 14 days. When the episode's `required_visit_complexity` is `HIGH_COMPLEXITY`, the visit's `achieved_complexity` is also `HIGH_COMPLEXITY`, and `completed_at` is on or before the high-complexity deadline, the `payable_code` is `99496`. Otherwise, provided `completed_at` is on or before the moderate deadline, the `payable_code` is `99495`. Otherwise there is nothing billable and the preparation is refused. There is no upgrade above the tier the episode required: an episode whose required complexity was only `MODERATE_COMPLEXITY` still bills `99495` even if the visit happened on day 3 with the entire high-complexity element set documented. The `payable_variant` records which side of that fork the claim landed on — `DOWNGRADED` when the episode required high complexity but the claim fell back to `99495`, and `FULL` in every other case. `base_rate_cents` follows the code: 27680 cents for `99496` and 20450 cents for `99495`.

The money is then computed as a strictly ordered waterfall, and the order cannot be collapsed or rearranged because a later step's rate is keyed on an earlier step's rounded result. First, `allowed_cents` is `base_rate_cents` multiplied by the payer's `tcm_rate_multiplier`, rounded half up to whole cents. Second, `coinsurance_cents` is that **already-rounded** `allowed_cents` multiplied by the payer's `coinsurance_rate`, rounded half up to whole cents — never the raw product from the first step. Third, `net_before_withhold_cents` is `allowed_cents` minus `coinsurance_cents`. Fourth, the quality withhold rate is selected from that third figure: `withhold_pct` is 0.03 when `net_before_withhold_cents` is at least 20000 cents, and 0.01 when it is below 20000 cents. Fifth, `withhold_cents` is `net_before_withhold_cents` multiplied by `withhold_pct`, rounded half up to whole cents. Sixth and last, `net_payable_cents` is `net_before_withhold_cents` minus `withhold_cents`.

The two worked cases below use a payer with a multiplier of 1.0 and a coinsurance rate of 0.20. A `99496` claim yields an allowed amount of 27680 cents, coinsurance of 5536 cents, a net before withhold of 22144 cents, a withhold rate of 0.03, a withhold of 664 cents and a net payable of 21480 cents. A `99495` claim yields 20450, 4090, 16360, a withhold rate of 0.01, 164 and a net payable of 16196 cents. Notice that the withhold **tier itself** flips between the two codes — the same payer and the same rounding rules produce a different withhold percentage purely because the code fork moved the net across the 20000-cent line.

A prepared claim records `service_period_start_date` and `service_period_end_date` copied from the episode, `patient_id` and `payer_id` copied from the episode, the `visit_id` it bills, `prepared_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`, `status` `PREPARED`, `appeal_count` 0, `readmission_flagged` false, `denial_reason_code` null, `submitted_by_user_id` null and `paid_at` null. The episode's `claim_id` is set to point at it. Claims may be listed filtered by `episode_id`, `patient_id`, `visit_id`, `payer_id` and `status`.

Submission moves the claim `PREPARED → SUBMITTED` and records `submitted_by_user_id`. It is legal only while the episode reads `SERVICE_PERIOD_CLOSED` with `billing_eligible` true, and only when the episode's compliance record supports it: `medrec_complete` must be true, or the submission is refused with `ERR_MEDREC_NOT_COMPLETE`, and `contact_timely` must be true, or it is refused with `ERR_CONTACT_NOT_TIMELY` — an episode where the programme never reached the patient inside the window cannot be billed no matter how good the visit was. And the submitter must not be the preparer.

Adjudication is a `ProgramAdmin` action recording the payer's answer as an `outcome` of `PAID` or `DENIED`, with an optional `denial_reason_code` of `MISSING_DOCUMENTATION`, `NOT_COVERED`, `DUPLICATE_SERVICE` or `TIMELY_FILING`. It is legal from `SUBMITTED` and from `APPEALED` alike, and both entry states behave identically. `PAID` stamps `paid_at` with the current instant and drives the episode `SERVICE_PERIOD_CLOSED → BILLED`, which then triggers the freeze evaluation — and that evaluation now succeeds, because no non-terminal claim remains. `DENIED` stores the supplied `denial_reason_code` and leaves `appeal_count` untouched; denial never advances the appeal counter.

The appeal loop is bounded at exactly two. Filing an appeal on a `DENIED` claim whose `appeal_count` is below 2 moves it `DENIED → APPEALED` and increments `appeal_count` by 1; the money fields are deliberately **not** recomputed, so an appeal can never double-count the withhold. An appealed claim can be adjudicated again in either direction — `APPEALED → PAID` behaves exactly like `SUBMITTED → PAID`, and `APPEALED → DENIED` stores a fresh `denial_reason_code` and leaves the claim eligible for another appeal while the bound allows. When the bound is already exhausted — `appeal_count` already equal to 2 — a third attempt does something unusual: the claim is first flipped `DENIED → DENIED_FINAL` with `net_payable_cents` set to 0 and the freeze evaluation triggered, that change is persisted, and only then is the caller told `ERR_APPEAL_LIMIT_REACHED`. Note that the freeze evaluation at that moment does nothing, because the episode is still in `SERVICE_PERIOD_CLOSED`, which is not a frozen-eligible status.

A `ProgramAdmin` may also void a claim outright from any of `PREPARED`, `SUBMITTED`, `DENIED` or `APPEALED`. Voiding sets `status` to `VOIDED`, zeroes `net_payable_cents` and triggers the freeze evaluation — which will freeze the episode if its `status` has already reached one of the frozen-eligible values. `PAID`, `DENIED_FINAL` and `VOIDED` are the three terminal claim statuses; `PREPARED`, `SUBMITTED`, `DENIED` and `APPEALED` are the four non-terminal ones, and it is the presence of a claim in one of those four that holds an otherwise-eligible episode out of the frozen posture.

### 3.9. Thirty-Day Readmission Tracking, Retraction and Episode Closure

The programme is ultimately judged on whether the patient came back, and readmission tracking therefore continues even after the money has settled. Recording a readmission is a `ProgramAdmin` action requiring a `readmission_date` in `YYYY-MM-DD` form and a `classification`, and it is one of the two mutating capabilities exempt from the record-freeze gate. An episode whose records are frozen can still receive readmission events; that is the whole reason the exemption exists.

The event's `day_index` is the whole number of calendar days from the episode's `discharge_date` to the `readmission_date`. The window is open at the left and closed at the right: a `day_index` of 1 through 30 inclusive is inside the window, while a `day_index` of 0 — a same-day return — is outside it, a `day_index` of 31 is outside it, and any negative value is outside it. Anything outside is refused with `ERR_READMISSION_OUT_OF_WINDOW`. An accepted event opens in `status` `RECORDED`, records `recorded_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`, leaves `retraction_reason` and `source_red_flag_id` null, and sets `counts_toward_rate` true only when the `classification` is `UNPLANNED_INPATIENT`. Events may be read individually or listed filtered by `episode_id`, `source_red_flag_id`, `classification` and `status`.

The four classifications differ radically in what they do beyond the counters. `UNPLANNED_INPATIENT` is the one that counts toward the readmission rate and the only one with teeth: it sets the episode's `billing_eligible` to false; if the episode's `status` is `ACTIVE` or `SERVICE_PERIOD_CLOSED` it stores that value in `pre_readmission_status` and drives the episode to `CLOSED_READMITTED`, which also decrements the navigator's `active_episode_count` and the patient's `open_episode_count` by 1 each and recomputes the navigator's `workload_score` and `availability_status`; if the episode has a claim sitting in `PREPARED`, `SUBMITTED`, `DENIED` or `APPEALED`, that claim is voided with `net_payable_cents` zeroed; and if the claim has already reached `PAID`, the claim is **not** voided — instead the episode's `recoupment_required` is set to true, because money that has already moved must be clawed back rather than cancelled. The episode's `counted_readmission_count` rises by 1, and so does the patient's `lifetime_readmission_count`.

`PLANNED_INPATIENT` and `OBSERVATION_ONLY` are excluded from the rate and behave as a matched pair: the episode's `status` is untouched, `billing_eligible` stays true, the episode's `excluded_readmission_count` rises by 1, and if a claim exists its `readmission_flagged` is set to true so that the payer-facing record notes the return. `ED_TREAT_AND_RELEASE` is also excluded from the rate and is completely inert: no episode status change, no claim change, no flag — only the event row itself and the episode's `excluded_readmission_count`. In all four cases `readmission_event_count` rises by 1, and the identity that `readmission_event_count` equals `counted_readmission_count` plus `excluded_readmission_count` plus `retracted_readmission_count` holds immediately.

An unplanned-inpatient readmission is consequently the deepest cascade in the system, and every hop must run. The event row is created and the counters move; the episode is driven to `CLOSED_READMITTED` with `billing_eligible` false; the closure voids any non-terminal claim; the claim reaching a terminal status makes the episode eligible for the freeze evaluation, which flips `record_posture` from `OPEN` to `FROZEN`; and the freeze in turn supersedes every proposed handoff, closes every open or acknowledged red flag administratively and closes every open or escalated barrier administratively.

Mistakes happen, and a recorded event may be retracted by a `ProgramAdmin` with a `retraction_reason`. Retraction is legal only from `RECORDED`, is the second capability exempt from the record-freeze gate, and compensates precisely what the recording moved. One unit leaves `counted_readmission_count` when the event counted toward the rate, or `excluded_readmission_count` when it did not, and lands in `retracted_readmission_count`; `readmission_event_count` does not change. The patient's `lifetime_readmission_count` falls by 1 when the retracted event counted toward the rate. For an `UNPLANNED_INPATIENT` event, the episode's `status` is restored from `pre_readmission_status`, `pre_readmission_status` is cleared back to null, `billing_eligible` returns to true, and the navigator's `active_episode_count` and the patient's `open_episode_count` each rise by 1 again — restoring `ACTIVE` if that was the stored value, or `SERVICE_PERIOD_CLOSED` if that was. For a `PLANNED_INPATIENT` or `OBSERVATION_ONLY` event, the claim's `readmission_flagged` returns to false only when no other recorded event of those two classes remains on the episode. Two things are deliberately **not** undone: a claim voided by the cascade is never reinstated — a replacement claim must be prepared from scratch — and children the freeze cascade already moved to `SUPERSEDED` or `CLOSED_ADMINISTRATIVE` stay there. Restoring the episode's status out of the frozen-eligible set does, however, return `record_posture` to `OPEN`, and this retraction path is the only way an episode ever thaws.

Finally, a `ProgramAdmin` may close an episode administratively at any point from `INTAKE`, `ACTIVE` or `SERVICE_PERIOD_CLOSED`, optionally recording a free-text `reason`. Administrative closure drives the episode to `ADMIN_CLOSED`, voids any claim sitting in `PREPARED`, `SUBMITTED`, `DENIED` or `APPEALED` with `net_payable_cents` zeroed, decrements the navigator's `active_episode_count` and the patient's `open_episode_count` by 1 each with the navigator's workload figures recomputed, and triggers the freeze evaluation together with its three-branch cascade. Because `ADMIN_CLOSED` is terminal on the status axis, an episode closed this way can never thaw.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Endpoints that bring a brand-new resource into existence respond with HTTP 201; every other successful call responds with HTTP 200. The table below is a directory, not a specification: request payloads, response side effects and the ordering of the checks each endpoint performs are all defined by the narratives in section 3 and the validation ladder in section 2.10. Every endpoint requires the `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` headers described in section 2.1, and every list endpoint honours the `limit` and `offset` parameters described in section 2.3.
