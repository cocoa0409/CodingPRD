# Premium Seating & Suite Licensing API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A stadium's premium sales office lives and dies by a small number of very large relationships. Suites and club inventory are not sold ticket-by-ticket; they are licensed under multi-year agreements with escalating annual fees, food-and-beverage minimums, transfer and sublease controls, renewal windows, and a waitlist of prospective buyers whose priority must be computed defensibly. Around that core sit the operational concerns of event day — catering orders with hard cutoffs, guest-list credentials bounded by suite capacity — and the financial machinery that keeps the book clean: invoicing, payment allocation, late fees, credit standing, and the end-of-season settlement run.

This document specifies the Premium Seating & Suite Licensing API, the system of record for that entire business. It covers venue, suite, season, and event inventory; the license agreement lifecycle from drafting through execution, suspension, transfer, renewal, and expiry; single-event suite rentals with tiered pricing and cancellation fees; catering and event-day access credentials; amenity and renovation assessments levied across licensed suites; and the seasonal closing run that reconciles food-and-beverage minimums, suspends delinquent accounts, and sweeps expired offers. All resources are served under the `/api` mount. The specification is written for the implementing engineer: every rule stated here is normative, and financial rules are stated with exact rounding behavior so that independent implementations agree to the cent.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section pins the contracts that every workflow in §3 relies on.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two HTTP headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, identifying their operational capacity. A request missing either header, carrying a malformed value, or naming a role outside the five declared roles is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_OR_INVALID_USER_CONTEXT_HEADER` (HTTP 400). The five roles are spelled exactly as follows: `StadiumAdmin`, `PremiumSalesRep`, `FinanceController`, `EventServicesManager`, and `AccountHolder`.

Each endpoint declares the roles permitted to call it; a caller whose role is valid but not listed for that endpoint is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_FOR_ROLE` (HTTP 403). The same rejection applies when a workflow reserves a particular transition of an otherwise permitted endpoint to a subset of that endpoint's roles — for example a `PremiumSalesRep` attempting a payment status change, or an `AccountHolder` attempting a catering confirmation. The stadium operations superuser `StadiumAdmin` performs venue, suite, season, event, and assessment configuration, controls the logical clock, and may perform nearly every staff operation except where a workflow reserves a step to another role. `PremiumSalesRep` is the premium sales office account executive — selling and managing license agreements, renewals, the waitlist, transfers, and single-event rentals — with no finance-writing powers. `FinanceController` owns the finance office: invoicing, payment lifecycle, credit standing, account suspension and reinstatement, season settlement, assessment levy, and agreement cure. `EventServicesManager` runs the event services desk: catering confirmation and fulfilment, and credential issue, activation, and revocation. `AccountHolder` is the license-holding or renting customer acting in self-service on its own agreements, catering, credentials, rentals, transfers, renewals, waitlist entries, and payments.

Role identity is resolved as follows. When `X-User-Role` is `AccountHolder`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting Account; ownership checks on any resource that references an Account compare the resource's owning-account field — `agreement.account_id`, `rental.account_id`, `payment.account_id`, `transfer.from_account_id`, `waitlist.account_id`, or the account that owns a catering order's or credential's agreement or rental — to `X-User-ID`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_ACCOUNT_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). Staff roles are exempt from this scope check. When `X-User-Role` is `StadiumAdmin`, `PremiumSalesRep`, `FinanceController`, or `EventServicesManager`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server never compares it to any entity's `id`. On list endpoints, an `AccountHolder` caller sees only resources belonging to its own account, and on single-resource access an `AccountHolder` may only address its own account and the resources its account owns.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits; it is stamped from the logical clock of §2.4, so it tracks operator-set time rather than the machine's wall clock.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

Requests to paths the API does not define, or with a method the path does not support, receive the standard error envelope with `ERR_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404).

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

List endpoints accept two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of items returned; it defaults to 10, with a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100 — a `limit` outside that range is clamped to the nearest bound rather than rejected, and a non-integer `limit` or `offset` falls back to its default. `offset` specifies how many items to skip and defaults to 0; a negative `offset` is clamped to 0. The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more`, where `has_more` is true exactly when `offset` plus the number of items returned is less than `total_count`.

Unless otherwise specified, list endpoints return resources sorted by `created_at` in ascending order. Rows created within the same logical-clock instant — for example, a batch of cascade-created invoices — keep their creation sequence within that instant. Resource ids never influence ordering or any other observable decision.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock

The server maintains a logical clock; it never falls back to wall-clock time. At startup, before any operator intervention, the clock reads exactly 2026-08-01T00:00:00Z. A `StadiumAdmin` may advance or set the clock through an administrative capability that accepts a `to_iso8601` instant (ISO 8601 UTC; a timezone-naive value is read as UTC) and reports the resulting `current_utc`. Every clock-derived computation in this document — catering cutoffs, late-fee accrual, rental hold expiry, credential activation windows, renewal windows, season settlement eligibility, waitlist aging — evaluates against this logical clock, not against the machine's wall clock.

### 2.5. Common Resource Properties

Every resource in the system — Venue, Suite, Season, Event, Account, LicenseAgreement, Invoice, Payment, CateringOrder, AccessCredential, SingleEventRental, TransferRequest, RenewalOffer, WaitlistEntry, and Assessment — carries a standard set of system-managed fields:

- `id` (string): a unique, server-minted identifier formatted as a UUID, with no type prefix.
- `created_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the resource was created.
- `updated_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, refreshed on any modification of the resource.
- `version` (integer): a system-managed integer that begins at 1 when the resource is created and increments by one on every mutation of the resource, including mutations driven by cascades and recomputed derived fields.

### 2.6. Monetary Precision and Rounding

All monetary amounts in the API are integer cents. Every calculation that produces a monetary value rounds half-up to a whole cent, and the rounding step is pinned at the point each calculation specifies: per order line before summing for catering, per season step for license fee schedules, once at the end of the quoted formula for rental pricing. Percentages and rates used in calculations are exact decimal constants; an escalator expressed in basis points divides by ten thousand, so an `escalator_bp` of 300 means exactly 3.00 percent. Derived balances and scores recomputed at read time follow the same precision rules, and no monetary field is ever represented as a string in a response body.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Three write operations are guarded by optimistic concurrency control, and each names its parent entity's version explicitly in the request body:

- Creating a renewal offer (`POST /api/renewal-offers`) requires `expected_agreement_version`, carrying the client last observed `version` of the target LicenseAgreement. Because a successful create supersedes the agreement's outstanding offer and increments the agreement's `version` by one, a create carrying a pre-supersede version is working from a stale view of the agreement and is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412).
- Amending a catering order (`PATCH /api/catering-orders/{id}`) requires `expected_catering_order_version`, the client last observed `version` of the CateringOrder. Each successful amendment recomputes totals and bumps the order's `version`, so an amendment carrying an older version would silently overwrite another writer's line changes; it is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412).
- Updating an account profile (`PATCH /api/accounts/{id}`) requires `expected_account_version`, the client last observed `version` of the Account. Status changes and cascade-driven recomputes bump the account's `version`, so a profile edit carrying an older version is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

Endpoints not listed here do not take a version field. Within the gate order of §2.8, the version comparison is evaluated only after the request body's fields have passed validation (gate 6); the catering amendment additionally applies the from-state check (gate 7) before comparing versions, and renewal-offer creation compares versions before evaluating the renewal-window gate (gate 11).

### 2.8. The Validation Gate Order

Incoming requests are validated against a fixed sequence of gates; an earlier failure masks all later checks. The order is:

1. **Header presence and validity.** `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` must be present and the role must be one of the five declared roles, else `ERR_MISSING_OR_INVALID_USER_CONTEXT_HEADER`. This gate applies to every endpoint.
2. **Role authorization.** The caller's role must be listed in the endpoint's roles, else `ERR_FORBIDDEN_FOR_ROLE`. This gate applies to every endpoint.
3. **Path identifier existence.** Identifiers embedded in the URL are resolved left to right; the first identifier that does not resolve to an existing resource yields `ERR_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404), and no later identifier is examined.
4. **AccountHolder scope.** For endpoints that admit `AccountHolder`, the caller's `X-User-ID` must equal the owning account of the target resource, else `ERR_ACCOUNT_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. Staff roles are exempt. On creation endpoints the owning account arrives in the request body rather than the path, so this check is evaluated as soon as the body has yielded that account: for catering orders and credentials that is after the agreement/rental context (including its referential consistency) has been resolved, and for payments, rentals, waitlist entries, and transfer requests it is immediately after the owning account — or, for transfers, the agreement and `from_account_id` — resolves; earlier failures there mask the scope violation.
5. **Body-supplied references.** Foreign keys supplied in the request body — such as `suite_id`, `account_id`, `event_id`, `season_id`, `agreement_id`, `rental_id`, or the invoice ids inside payment `allocations` — must resolve to existing resources, else `ERR_NOT_FOUND`.
6. **Body well-formedness and referential consistency.** Required fields must be present, enum values must be in their declared domains, integers must be positive where required, a payment's `amount_cents` must equal the sum of its allocation amounts, catering orders and credentials must carry exactly one of `agreement_id` or `rental_id` (for catering orders and credentials this exactly-one check fires before the supplied `agreement_id` or `rental_id` is resolved), a transfer's `to_account_id` must differ from its `from_account_id`, and cross-references must agree — the referenced agreement's or rental's suite must match the body's `suite_id`, and for a rental its event must match the body's `event_id`. Agreement-context catering orders and credentials additionally require a license-covered event: the event's `event_type` must be `REGULAR` or `RIVALRY` and the event's season must be among the agreement's covered seasons. Violations yield `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400), as do a duplicate `tax_id`, a duplicate `suite_code` within a venue, or a duplicate season `label` within a venue.
7. **From-state validity.** The target resource must be in a valid from-state for the requested transition, else `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409); terminal states are locked down. On the `set_status`-style endpoints a `target_status` naming no status of the resource's enum is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, while a recognized status that is not a legal target from the current state — including states never set by hand, such as a suite's `LICENSED` — is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.
8. **Guard states**, evaluated in this fixed sub-order: (i) the account must not be `SUSPENDED` for new commitments — agreement execution, placing a rental hold, placing a catering order, issuing a credential, or joining the waitlist — else `ERR_ACCOUNT_SUSPENDED`; (ii) the event must be `SCHEDULED` for catering placement or confirmation and for credential issue or activation, else `ERR_EVENT_NOT_SCHEDULED`; (iii) the linked agreement must be `ACTIVE` for agreement-context catering placement or confirmation, agreement-context credential issue, and transfer approval, else `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`; (iv) the linked rental must be `CONFIRMED` for rental-context catering placement and credential issue — `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE` is not used for this check; a non-`CONFIRMED` rental context fails with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.
9. **Season lock.** Writes scoped to a `SETTLED` season are rejected with `ERR_SEASON_LOCKED` — executing an agreement whose start season has settled, or voiding an `ISSUED` invoice tagged with a settled season.
10. **Catering cutoff.** The clock must be at or before the event's start minus the effective cutoff for catering placement, confirmation, or free cancellation, else `ERR_CATERING_CUTOFF_PASSED`; a call landing exactly at the cutoff passes.
11. **Renewal window.** The clock must lie within the last covered season's renewal window, both ends inclusive, else `ERR_RENEWAL_WINDOW_CLOSED`.
12. **Rental hold timing.** Confirming a held rental requires the clock at or before its `hold_expires_at`, else `ERR_HOLD_EXPIRED`; expiring a held rental requires the clock strictly after `hold_expires_at`, else `ERR_HOLD_NOT_EXPIRED`.
13. **Credential activation window.** Activation requires the clock within the event's admission window, else `ERR_CREDENTIAL_WINDOW`.
14. **Suite availability for rentals.** The suite must be free for the requested event — no `HELD` or `CONFIRMED` rental on the same suite and event; for `REGULAR` and `RIVALRY` events additionally no agreement in `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED`, or `EXPIRING` covering that season for that suite; and the suite must not be `OUT_OF_SERVICE` — else `ERR_SUITE_NOT_AVAILABLE`.
15. **Agreement suite exclusivity.** Executing an agreement must not overlap covered seasons with another agreement on the same suite in `PENDING_EXECUTION`, `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED`, or `EXPIRING`, else `ERR_SUITE_ALREADY_LICENSED`.
16. **Event overlap.** A new event's occupancy interval must not intersect another `SCHEDULED` event on the same venue, else `ERR_EVENT_OVERLAP`; two events where one ends exactly when the other starts are permitted.
17. **Credential capacity.** Issuing a credential must not push the count of credentials in `ISSUED` or `ACTIVATED` for the same event and suite beyond the suite's seat capacity, else `ERR_CREDENTIAL_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`; issuing the credential that exactly reaches capacity succeeds, and the next one fails.
18. **Guest uniqueness.** A `guest_email` may appear on at most one credential that is not `REVOKED` or `EXPIRED` for the same event and suite, else `ERR_DUPLICATE_GUEST`.
19. **Waitlist uniqueness.** An account may hold only one non-terminal waitlist entry — one in `WAITING` or `OFFERED` — for the same tier and target season, else `ERR_WAITLIST_DUPLICATE`.
20. **Transfer exclusivity and target eligibility.** No other transfer for the same agreement may be in `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `INFO_REQUESTED`, or `APPROVED`, else `ERR_TRANSFER_ALREADY_PENDING`; the target account must be `ACTIVE` and, for a sublease, must not be a `BROKER` account, else `ERR_TRANSFER_TARGET_INELIGIBLE`.
21. **Suite lifecycle guards.** Taking a suite out of service is blocked while it is `LICENSED` (`ERR_SUITE_LICENSED`); waitlist promotion and conversion require the named suite's tier to match the entry's `desired_tier` (`ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`) and the suite to be `AVAILABLE` (`ERR_SUITE_NOT_AVAILABLE`), checked in that order on promotion; a suite with another entry already `OFFERED` against it rejects a further promotion with `ERR_SUITE_OFFER_PENDING`; and promoting any entry other than the top-ranked eligible one fails with `ERR_NOT_NEXT_IN_LINE`.
22. **Fiscal referential guards.** Voiding an invoice is blocked while payments in `INITIATED` or `CLEARED` allocate to it, else `ERR_INVOICE_HAS_PAYMENTS`; cancelling a confirmed rental is blocked unless its rental invoice is still `ISSUED`, else `ERR_RENTAL_SETTLEMENT_REQUIRED`.
23. **Credit gates.** An account on `HOLD` credit standing may not execute an agreement or confirm a rental (`ERR_ACCOUNT_CREDIT_HOLD`); agreements and accounts with open or past-due invoices block agreement termination, agreement cure, transfer approval, and transfer completion (`ERR_UNSETTLED_BALANCE`).
24. **Allocation caps.** A payment allocation may not exceed its target invoice's `amount_due_cents` (`ERR_ALLOCATION_EXCEEDS_DUE`, HTTP 422), and every allocated invoice must belong to the payment's account (`ERR_ALLOCATION_ACCOUNT_MISMATCH`, HTTP 422).

### 2.9. Cascades, Recomputation, and Hierarchical Consistency

The system maintains alignment between parents and children automatically. When an event is cancelled, its catering orders, credentials, and rentals are resolved without further client action; when an account is suspended, its agreements follow; when a payment clears or is refunded, the affected invoices, the account's balances, and potentially the account's status and its agreements' statuses all move together. Each such reconciliation independently mutates the affected resource, refreshing its `updated_at` and incrementing its `version`. Cascades are specified link by link in §3 alongside the workflow that triggers them; no cascade requires a separate client call, and no intermediate state is observable between a trigger and its cascaded effects.

Several derived fields are recomputed at read time rather than stored durably: an Account's `credit_standing`, `balance_due_cents`, and `lifetime_spend_cents`; an Invoice's `late_fee_cents` (and therefore its `amount_due_cents`); an agreement's `current_season_index` and `f_b_accrued_cents_current_season`; and a waitlist entry's `priority_score`. A read of these resources always reflects the logical clock and the current ledger, never a cached snapshot.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Building the Venue: Suites, Seasons, and Events

Everything in the premium sales office hangs off a **Venue**. A `StadiumAdmin` creates one with a display `name` and a street `address`, plus an optional `default_catering_cutoff_hours` that must be an integer of zero or greater and defaults to 72 hours — the period before an event's start after which catering orders and cancellations become locked or penalized. Individual events may override this default.

Within a venue, the saleable unit is the **Suite**, created with the owning `venue_id`, a human-readable `suite_code` such as "S-201" that must be unique within its venue, a `tier` of `FOUNDERS`, `PLATINUM`, `GOLD`, or `SILVER`, a `capacity_seats` that must be a positive integer and later caps how many active credentials a single event may have for the suite, a `base_annual_fee_cents` used as the default annual license fee in quotes and waitlist conversions, and an `f_b_minimum_cents` holding the default annual food-and-beverage minimum; both amounts must be zero or greater. A new suite begins life `AVAILABLE`. From there, a `StadiumAdmin` may toggle a suite between `AVAILABLE` and `OUT_OF_SERVICE` by naming a `target_status`; the third status, `LICENSED`, is never set by hand — naming it as a `target_status` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. A suite becomes `LICENSED` only as a side effect of a license agreement being executed, at which point the suite's `current_agreement_id` points at that agreement, and it returns to `AVAILABLE` only when that agreement ends by termination, expiry, or transfer, at which point `current_agreement_id` clears back to null. Because a licensed suite has a contractual occupant, taking it out of service while `LICENSED` is forbidden and is rejected with `ERR_SUITE_LICENSED` — the agreement must end first. Suites can be browsed in a list filtered by `venue_id`, `status`, or `tier`.

A **Season** structures time for a venue. Creation takes the owning `venue_id`, a `label` such as "2026-27" that must be unique within the venue (a duplicate is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`), a `starts_on` and an `ends_on` that must fall strictly after the start, and the pair `renewal_window_opens_on` and `renewal_window_closes_on` bracketing the period in which renewal offers for the following season may be created; the window's close is inclusive, so an offer created exactly at `renewal_window_closes_on` is still inside it. A season is born `DRAFT` and becomes `PUBLISHED` through publication, which re-verifies that `ends_on` is after `starts_on` and otherwise fails with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Only `PUBLISHED` seasons can host agreements, and settlement — the `PUBLISHED` to `SETTLED` closing run — is described in §3.5. Seasons may be listed filtered by `venue_id` or `status`.

An **Event** lives inside a season and is created with the hosting `season_id`, a `name`, an `event_type` of `REGULAR`, `RIVALRY`, `PLAYOFF`, or `CONCERT`, a `starts_at` instant, an optional `duration_hours` that must be a positive integer and defaults to 4, a `rental_base_price_cents` of zero or greater used as the base for single-event rental quotes, and an optional `catering_cutoff_hours` that must be an integer of zero or greater and overrides the venue default for that event only, with a null value meaning "inherit the venue default." The event's start must fall within its season's window from `starts_on` through `ends_on` inclusive. The event type carries commercial meaning: `REGULAR` and `RIVALRY` events are covered by season-long license agreements, while `PLAYOFF` and `CONCERT` events are not — a distinction that drives both catering eligibility and rental availability. An event occupies the half-open interval from its `starts_at` up to but not including `starts_at` plus `duration_hours`; creating an event whose interval intersects another `SCHEDULED` event at the same venue is rejected with `ERR_EVENT_OVERLAP`, while two events that merely touch — one ending exactly when the other begins — are allowed. Events can be listed filtered by `season_id`, `event_type`, or `status`.

Events begin `SCHEDULED` and end in one of two terminal states. A `StadiumAdmin` may cancel an event only while the logical clock is strictly before its `starts_at`; once the event has started, cancellation is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Cancellation is a wide cascade: every catering order of the event still in `DRAFT`, `PLACED`, or `CONFIRMED` moves to `CANCELLED` with `cancel_fee_cents` of zero and its accrual removed, pulling the owning agreement's `f_b_accrued_cents_current_season` back down; every access credential of the event in `ISSUED` or `ACTIVATED` moves to `EXPIRED`; and every single-event rental in `QUOTED`, `HELD`, or `CONFIRMED` moves to `CANCELLED` with `cancel_fee_cents` of zero, with its rental invoice voided if still `ISSUED` (a rental invoice that has already moved past `ISSUED` is left as it stands). Marking an event `COMPLETED` requires the clock to have reached the end of the occupancy interval — `starts_at` plus `duration_hours` — and earlier attempts are rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`; completion expires the event's still-`ISSUED` credentials (activated ones are sticky, as §3.7 explains) and settles its `CONFIRMED` rentals into `SETTLED`.

### 3.2. Accounts and Credit Standing

The customer of record is the **Account**, created by a `PremiumSalesRep` or `StadiumAdmin` with a `legal_name`, an `account_type` of `CORPORATE`, `INDIVIDUAL`, or `BROKER`, a `tax_id` that must be unique across all accounts, and a `billing_email`. A duplicate `tax_id` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. New accounts begin as `PROSPECT`, a status enterable only at creation — any attempt to move an `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` account back to `PROSPECT` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.

Activation moves `PROSPECT` to `ACTIVE` and is available to `FinanceController`, `PremiumSalesRep`, and `StadiumAdmin`. Suspension and reinstatement are finance functions: a `PremiumSalesRep` may drive an account's status only from `PROSPECT` to `ACTIVE` and cannot suspend or reinstate — a rep attempting any other account status transition is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_FOR_ROLE`. Moving `ACTIVE` to `SUSPENDED` happens two ways — manually by a `FinanceController` or `StadiumAdmin`, or automatically during the season settle run when the account's recomputed `credit_standing` lands on `HOLD` — and both ways cascade identically down the hierarchy: every license agreement of the account sitting in `ACTIVE` or `EXPIRING` is pulled into `SUSPENDED`, recording a `suspension_reason` of `ADMINISTRATIVE` when a manual suspension triggered the cascade and `ACCOUNT_DEFAULT` when the settle sweep did.

Reinstatement from `SUSPENDED` to `ACTIVE` likewise has a manual and an automatic path. A manual reinstate by finance or admin requires the account's `credit_standing` to be `GOOD` — meaning no invoice even a single day past due — and is otherwise rejected with `ERR_ACCOUNT_CREDIT_HOLD`. The automatic path fires inside the payment-clearing cascade of §3.4: when a clearing payment drives `balance_due_cents` to zero and no invoice of the account is past due, a `SUSPENDED` account returns to `ACTIVE` on its own. Either way, reinstatement cascades to agreements, but only partially: agreements suspended with reason `ACCOUNT_DEFAULT` auto-cure back to `ACTIVE`, while `ADMINISTRATIVE` suspensions deliberately do not and require the explicit agreement cure described in §3.3.

Three computed fields summarize an account's financial posture and are refreshed on every read. `credit_standing` is `HOLD` when any invoice of the account in `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_PAID` is 30 or more whole days past due, `WATCH` when any such invoice is between 1 and 29 whole days past due, and otherwise `GOOD`; `DRAFT`, `PAID`, and `VOID` invoices never figure into the standing. `balance_due_cents` is the sum of `amount_due_cents` across the account's invoices currently in `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_PAID`. `lifetime_spend_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` across the account's payments that have ever reached `CLEARED`; refunds do not decrement it, so the figure measures gross historical spend. Profile maintenance — a new `legal_name`, `billing_email`, or `tax_id` — flows through the OCC-guarded account update of §2.7 and increments the account's `version` by one. The account list can be filtered by `status` or `account_type`, and an `AccountHolder` reading accounts sees only its own `id`.

### 3.3. The License Agreement Lifecycle

The **LicenseAgreement** is the commercial heart of the system. A `PremiumSalesRep` or `StadiumAdmin` drafts one by naming the `suite_id` being licensed, the `account_id` of the holder, and the `start_season_id` of the first covered season, then setting the commercial terms: `term_seasons` between 1 and 10 inclusive, `base_annual_fee_cents` as the season-one fee before escalation (zero or greater), an `escalator_bp` between 0 and 2000 basis points inclusive, and the annual `f_b_minimum_cents` per covered season. The covered seasons are anchored by the start season itself — whatever its status — followed by the suite's venue's non-`DRAFT` seasons (`PUBLISHED` or `SETTLED`) whose `starts_on` falls strictly after the start season's `starts_on`, ordered by `starts_on` ascending, taking the start season plus enough later seasons to reach `term_seasons` entries in total. Agreements can be listed filtered by `suite_id`, `account_id`, `start_season_id`, or `status`, with an `AccountHolder` seeing only its own.

A fresh agreement is `DRAFT`. Submission moves it to `PENDING_EXECUTION`, ready for signature. Execution is the holder's own act: only an `AccountHolder` whose `X-User-ID` matches the agreement's `account_id` may execute, and anyone else is rejected with `ERR_ACCOUNT_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. Execution then runs a battery of checks — the account must be `ACTIVE` (a `SUSPENDED` account fails with `ERR_ACCOUNT_SUSPENDED`, while a `PROSPECT` account, which was never eligible to sign, fails with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`); the account's `credit_standing` must not be `HOLD` (else `ERR_ACCOUNT_CREDIT_HOLD`); the suite must be `AVAILABLE`, with `OUT_OF_SERVICE` rejected as `ERR_SUITE_NOT_AVAILABLE` and `LICENSED` as `ERR_SUITE_ALREADY_LICENSED`; no other agreement on the same suite in `PENDING_EXECUTION`, `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED`, or `EXPIRING` may have covered seasons overlapping the new one's (else `ERR_SUITE_ALREADY_LICENSED`); and the start season must be `PUBLISHED` and not `SETTLED` (else `ERR_SEASON_LOCKED`).

A successful execution stamps `executed_on` — null until the agreement reaches `ACTIVE` — with the logical clock, computes the agreement's `fee_schedule_cents`, issues the first invoice, and flips the suite to `LICENSED` with `current_agreement_id` pointing at the new agreement. The fee schedule compounds on itself: the season-one fee equals `base_annual_fee_cents`, and each subsequent season's fee is the previous season's already-rounded fee multiplied by one plus the escalator — `escalator_bp` divided by ten thousand — with the product rounded half-up to a whole cent at every season step. The compounding on rounded values is canonical; computing every season's fee directly from the unrounded base would diverge from season three onward for most fee-and-escalator pairs and is not what the server stores. The first invoice is a `LICENSE_FEE` invoice, created already `ISSUED` with `due_on` set thirty days after the clock, covering the season-one fee — prorated when execution happens after the season has begun.

Proration is forward-looking at execution time. The covered events of a season are its events of type `REGULAR` or `RIVALRY` that are not `CANCELLED` — and at execute time merely `SCHEDULED` events still count. The proration ratio is the number of covered events whose `starts_at` falls at or after `executed_on`, divided by the total number of covered events in the start season; when the season has no covered events at all, the ratio is undefined and the full season-one fee and full food-and-beverage minimum apply. The season-one invoice charges `fee_schedule_cents` entry one multiplied by that ratio, rounded half-up, and the same ratio scales the season's food-and-beverage minimum: the prorated minimum is `f_b_minimum_cents` multiplied by the ratio, rounded half-up. When the season eventually settles, the ratio is recomputed with any events cancelled after execution excluded from both the numerator and the denominator, so a late cancellation shifts the shortfall comparison to the settled ratio.

Two computed fields track the agreement's position in time. `current_season_index` is the number of covered seasons whose `starts_on` the logical clock has reached, pinned below at 1 and above at `term_seasons`; it therefore reads 1 before the first covered season starts — and in any gap between covered seasons it stays on the most recently started one — and reads `term_seasons` after the last covered season ends. `f_b_accrued_cents_current_season` is the net food-and-beverage accrual toward the current covered season's minimum, maintained per §3.6.

An agreement leaves `ACTIVE` along several roads, none of them a manual suspension — administrative suspension of a single agreement is deliberately not exposed. When its account is suspended, the cascade of §3.2 pulls an `ACTIVE` or `EXPIRING` agreement into `SUSPENDED`, increments its `suspension_count` — which begins at zero — and records the reason. Cure returns `SUSPENDED` to `ACTIVE`: either automatically when the account is reinstated and the reason was `ACCOUNT_DEFAULT`, or explicitly through the cure operation reserved to `FinanceController` and `StadiumAdmin`. Cure requires the account to be `ACTIVE` (else `ERR_ACCOUNT_SUSPENDED`) and requires that no invoice of the agreement in `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_PAID` has a `due_on` before the clock (else `ERR_UNSETTLED_BALANCE`); on success the `suspension_reason` clears to null.

Termination by a `PremiumSalesRep` or `StadiumAdmin` behaves differently by stage. From `DRAFT` or `PENDING_EXECUTION`, the agreement simply lands in `WITHDRAWN` with no fee. From `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` it lands in `TERMINATED`, but only when no invoice of the agreement remains in `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_PAID` (else `ERR_UNSETTLED_BALANCE`); termination then creates a `TERMINATION_FEE` invoice, issued immediately with `due_on` thirty days out, clears any `suspension_reason` to null, and releases the suite to `AVAILABLE` with `current_agreement_id` cleared. The early termination fee is the greater of two figures: twice the fee scheduled for the agreement's `current_season_index`, or half of the sum of all scheduled fees for seasons beyond the current one, that half rounded half-up. When the current season is the last covered season, nothing remains beyond it and the fee is simply twice the current season's fee.

Transfer and expiry complete the map. When a sale transfer completes per §3.9, the agreement becomes `TRANSFERRED` and the system spawns its successor directly in `ACTIVE`: the successor belongs to the transferee account on the same suite, covers the remaining covered seasons — those strictly beyond the agreement's `current_season_index` — with its `start_season_id` set to the first of them, carries the remaining tail of the original `fee_schedule_cents` as its own schedule, takes the first tail entry as its `base_annual_fee_cents`, an `escalator_bp` of zero, and a `term_seasons` equal to the number of remaining seasons — or, when no covered seasons remain beyond the current one, a `term_seasons` of one, a zero `base_annual_fee_cents`, an empty `fee_schedule_cents`, and the original agreement's `start_season_id` — keeps the original `f_b_minimum_cents`, and is stamped with an `executed_on` of the clock; the suite stays `LICENSED` throughout, its `current_agreement_id` swinging to the successor. Season settlement moves `ACTIVE` to `EXPIRING` when the next covered season is the agreement's last, and when that last season settles the agreement becomes `RENEWED` if its `renewal_agreement_id` names a successor created by an accepted renewal offer or `EXPIRED` when no renewal exists; in either case the suite is released to `AVAILABLE` with `current_agreement_id` cleared, and a successor renewal agreement re-licenses the suite only through its own later execution. `RENEWED`, `EXPIRED`, `TERMINATED`, `TRANSFERRED`, and `WITHDRAWN` are terminal. While a sublease is in effect the agreement also records the sublessee in `active_sublease_account_id`, as §3.9 describes.

### 3.4. Invoicing, Payments, and the Collection Cascade

Every charge the system raises becomes an **Invoice**. An invoice always names the billed `account_id`; it may additionally carry an `agreement_id`, a `rental_id`, or an `assessment_id` identifying what spawned it — each null when not applicable — and a `season_id` giving season context, which is set automatically on `LICENSE_FEE` and `F_B_SHORTFALL` invoices, null on the other automatically created types, and may be supplied on a `MANUAL` invoice. The `invoice_type` enumerates `LICENSE_FEE`, `F_B_SHORTFALL`, `TRANSFER_FEE`, `TERMINATION_FEE`, `RENTAL`, `ASSESSMENT`, and `MANUAL`; automatically created invoices set their own type, while an invoice created directly by a `FinanceController` or `StadiumAdmin` is always `MANUAL`. An invoice's `lines` are a list of line items, each with a `description`, a `quantity` that must be a positive integer, and a `unit_price_cents` of zero or greater; its `subtotal_cents` is the sum of quantity times unit price across the lines, pure integer arithmetic with no rounding. Every automatically created invoice carries exactly one line of quantity 1 whose unit price is the invoiced amount, with the description naming the charge. The manual creation call requires `account_id`, `lines`, and a `due_on`, with optional `agreement_id` and `season_id`; automatically created invoices set their own due dates — thirty days after issue for `LICENSE_FEE`, `F_B_SHORTFALL`, and `TERMINATION_FEE`, fourteen days after issue for `TRANSFER_FEE` and for a cancellation-fee `RENTAL` invoice, seven days before the event's start for a standard `RENTAL` invoice, and sixty days after levy for an `ASSESSMENT` invoice. Invoices can be listed filtered by `account_id`, `agreement_id`, `rental_id`, `assessment_id`, `season_id`, `invoice_type`, or `status`, with an `AccountHolder` seeing only its own.

Lateness is priced continuously. For an invoice in `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_PAID`, days overdue is the number of whole days between `due_on` and the logical clock, clamped at zero so an invoice never accrues before its due date. Late fees charge in thirty-day periods and only once at least thirty days have elapsed: at day 29 the fee is zero, and at exactly day 30 one full period has accrued. Each period contributes one and a half percent of the invoice's `subtotal_cents`, with the per-period product computed over the whole period count and rounded half-up. The resulting `late_fee_cents` is zero while no full period has elapsed; otherwise the raw accrual is first capped at fifteen percent of the subtotal (rounded half-up), and then a 2,500-cent floor is applied — the floor prevails, so on a small invoice the fee can exceed the fifteen-percent cap. The late fee is recomputed on every read of the invoice and again during the season settle sweep, so a reported balance is never stale. What remains collectible is `amount_due_cents` — the subtotal plus the late fee minus `amount_paid_cents`, floored at zero — where `amount_paid_cents` sums cleared allocations to this invoice, net of refunds.

A manually created invoice begins `DRAFT`, with `issued_at` null. Issuing it — a finance or admin action — requires at least one line (an empty `lines` fails with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`), stamps `issued_at` with the clock, moves it to `ISSUED`, and lifts the account's `balance_due_cents`. Automatically created invoices skip `DRAFT` and enter `ISSUED` at creation, with `issued_at` stamped at creation. From `ISSUED`, cleared money moves the invoice: an allocation smaller than the amount due moves it to `PARTIALLY_PAID`, an allocation exactly covering the amount due moves it to `PAID`, and a later allocation covering the remainder moves `PARTIALLY_PAID` to `PAID`; each step recomputes the account balance downward. Voiding is available from `DRAFT` or `ISSUED` to finance and admin; voiding an `ISSUED` invoice is blocked while a payment in `INITIATED` or `CLEARED` allocates to it (else `ERR_INVOICE_HAS_PAYMENTS`) and, when the invoice carries a `season_id`, while that season is `SETTLED` (else `ERR_SEASON_LOCKED`) — a `DRAFT` invoice, which no payment can reference, may be voided without those two guards; voiding recomputes the account balance downward. `PAID` and `VOID` are terminal for an invoice, with one compensation exception: when a cleared payment is refunded, invoices it touched re-open — a fully refunded `PAID` invoice returns to `ISSUED`, a `PARTIALLY_PAID` invoice whose entire paid portion is refunded returns to `ISSUED`, and a `PAID` invoice refunded only in part drops to `PARTIALLY_PAID` — in each case `amount_paid_cents` falls by the refunded allocation and the account balance recomputes upward.

A **Payment** is created by an `AccountHolder` paying its own account or by `FinanceController` or `StadiumAdmin` staff, naming the `account_id`, a total `amount_cents` that must be a positive integer and must exactly equal the sum of its allocations, a `method` of `ACH`, `CARD`, or `WIRE`, and an `allocations` list whose entries each pair an `invoice_id` with an `amount_cents`. Every allocated invoice must belong to the payment's account (else `ERR_ALLOCATION_ACCOUNT_MISMATCH`) and must be in `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_PAID`, and no allocation may exceed its invoice's `amount_due_cents` (else `ERR_ALLOCATION_EXCEEDS_DUE`); a total that does not match the sum of allocations is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Payments begin `INITIATED` and can be listed filtered by `account_id` or `status`.

The payment lifecycle is short but consequential. Marking a payment `CLEARED` — a finance or admin action — triggers a four-link cascade. Link one: each allocated invoice's `amount_paid_cents` grows by its allocation and the invoice moves to `PARTIALLY_PAID` or `PAID` as coverage dictates. Link two: the account's `balance_due_cents` recomputes downward and `lifetime_spend_cents` grows by the payment's `amount_cents`. Link three: if the balance has reached zero, no invoice of the account is past due, and the account was `SUSPENDED`, the account auto-reinstates to `ACTIVE`. Link four: a reinstated `ACTIVE` account's agreements that were suspended with reason `ACCOUNT_DEFAULT` auto-cure back to `ACTIVE`. Marking `INITIATED` to `FAILED` simply records the failure with no ledger effects. Marking a `CLEARED` payment `REFUNDED` runs the compensation in reverse: each allocated invoice's `amount_paid_cents` falls by its allocation and re-opens as described above, the account's `balance_due_cents` recomputes upward by exactly the payment's `amount_cents` — so a clear followed by a refund nets the balance to zero — and `lifetime_spend_cents` is deliberately not decremented. `FAILED` and `REFUNDED` are terminal.

### 3.5. Season Settlement: The Closing Run

Settlement is the season's closing act, invoked by a `FinanceController` or `StadiumAdmin` against a `PUBLISHED` season. Two preconditions gate it: the logical clock must lie strictly after the season's `ends_on`, and every event of the season must have reached `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED`; either failure is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Settlement is not re-entrant — `SETTLED` is terminal, and a second settle attempt hits the same error.

Once admitted, the settle runs as a five-part ops cascade. Link one evaluates the food-and-beverage minimum for every agreement covering the settled season whose status is `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED`, or `EXPIRING`. The accrual is net of the service fee: each of the agreement's catering orders on the season's events contributes its `subtotal_cents` while `CONFIRMED` or `FULFILLED`, its `cancel_fee_cents` while `CANCELLED_LATE`, and nothing while `PLACED` or `CANCELLED`. The minimum it is compared against is prorated for mid-season executions: when the agreement's `executed_on` falls at or before the season's `starts_on`, the full `f_b_minimum_cents` applies; otherwise the minimum is `f_b_minimum_cents` multiplied by the settled proration ratio of §3.3 — covered events at or after execution over all covered events, with `CANCELLED` events excluded from both counts — rounded half-up. Link two: when the accrued total falls short of that minimum, the difference — the shortfall, never negative — becomes an `F_B_SHORTFALL` invoice, created already `ISSUED` with `due_on` thirty days after the clock, and the account's `balance_due_cents` recomputes upward.

Link three propagates credit consequences: each account that received a shortfall invoice in this run and whose recomputed standing has fallen to `HOLD` is suspended — an account already `SUSPENDED` is left untouched — and the cascade of §3.2 then pulls its `ACTIVE` or `EXPIRING` agreements into `SUSPENDED` with reason `ACCOUNT_DEFAULT`. Link four performs term bookkeeping, running only after the credit cascade and deliberately skipping any agreement the cascade just suspended: an agreement whose last covered season is the one being settled becomes `RENEWED` when its `renewal_agreement_id` is set and `EXPIRED` otherwise — with its suite released to `AVAILABLE` in either case — while an agreement whose next covered season is its last becomes `EXPIRING`. Link five sweeps stragglers: renewal offers of the season's agreements sitting in `OFFERED`, `REVISED`, or `COUNTERED` with a `response_due_on` before the clock become `EXPIRED`, and waitlist entries still `WAITING` whose `target_season_id` is this season become `EXPIRED`.

### 3.6. Event-Day Service I: Catering Orders and the F&B Minimum

A **CateringOrder** is placed for a specific event and suite by an `AccountHolder` ordering against its own agreement or rental, or by `EventServicesManager` or `StadiumAdmin` staff. The order carries the `event_id` and `suite_id`, a `package_tier` of `STANDARD`, `PREMIUM`, or `LUXE` that is purely informational, a `guest_count` that must be positive and no greater than the suite's `capacity_seats`, and a `lines` list whose entries each hold an `item_code`, a `description`, a positive integer `quantity`, a non-negative `unit_price_cents`, and an optional `discount_pct` between 0 and 1 inclusive. Exactly one of `agreement_id` or `rental_id` must be supplied — both or neither is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and the references must agree: the agreement's suite must equal the body's `suite_id`, the rental's suite and event must equal the body's `suite_id` and `event_id`, and an agreement-context order is only valid for a license-covered event, one of type `REGULAR` or `RIVALRY` whose season is among the agreement's covered seasons.

Placement is gated, in the gate order of §2.8: the ordering account must not be `SUSPENDED` (`ERR_ACCOUNT_SUSPENDED`); the event must still be `SCHEDULED` (`ERR_EVENT_NOT_SCHEDULED`); an agreement-context order requires the agreement `ACTIVE` (`ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`), while a rental-context order requires the rental `CONFIRMED` and anything else fails with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`; and the clock must be at or before the event's `starts_at` minus the effective cutoff — the event's own `catering_cutoff_hours` when set, otherwise the venue's `default_catering_cutoff_hours` — with anything strictly later rejected as `ERR_CATERING_CUTOFF_PASSED` and a call exactly at the cutoff admitted. An `AccountHolder` placing or amending an order against another account's agreement or rental is rejected with `ERR_ACCOUNT_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. Orders are created directly in `PLACED`, and the list view filters by `event_id`, `suite_id`, `agreement_id`, `rental_id`, or `status`.

The order's money is computed line by line and never re-rounded at the end. Each line's amount is its `quantity` times its `unit_price_cents` times one minus its `discount_pct`, rounded half-up to a whole cent per line; `subtotal_cents` is the sum of those already-rounded line amounts. The `service_fee_cents` is eighteen percent of the subtotal, rounded half-up, and `total_cents` is the subtotal plus the service fee. Two further fields track the order's relationship to the food-and-beverage minimum: `accrual_cents` counts toward the minimum and equals the `subtotal_cents` while the order is `CONFIRMED` or `FULFILLED`, the `cancel_fee_cents` while it is `CANCELLED_LATE`, and zero otherwise; `cancel_fee_cents` itself is zero unless the order lands in `CANCELLED_LATE`, in which case it is half of the order's `total_cents` at cancellation time, rounded half-up.

While an order is `PLACED`, its `lines`, `guest_count`, or `package_tier` may be amended through the OCC-guarded amendment of §2.7; each successful amendment recomputes the totals and increments the order's `version` by one. Confirmation — moving `PLACED` to `CONFIRMED`, reserved to `EventServicesManager` and `StadiumAdmin` — repeats the placement gates: the event must still be `SCHEDULED`, the clock must still be at or before the cutoff, and the agreement or rental context must still be valid. Confirming an agreement-context order feeds the minimum: the agreement's `f_b_accrued_cents_current_season` grows by the order's net accrual — its subtotal — provided the event falls within the agreement's current covered season. Fulfilment, moving `CONFIRMED` to `FULFILLED` under the same two roles, requires the clock to have reached the event's `starts_at` and is otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.

Cancellation routes by timing. A `PLACED` order may be cancelled outright — `PLACED` to `CANCELLED` — by the owning holder or event-services staff, with no fee and at any time, even after the event has started. A `CONFIRMED` order cancelled while the clock is still at or before the cutoff lands in `CANCELLED` and its accrual is removed from the agreement, so a confirm followed by a free cancel nets the accrual to zero. The same cancellation request arriving after the cutoff but before the event starts lands instead in `CANCELLED_LATE`: `cancel_fee_cents` becomes half the total, rounded half-up; the order's `accrual_cents` switches from its subtotal to its cancellation fee; and the agreement's `f_b_accrued_cents_current_season` adjusts by the difference between the fee and the former subtotal accrual. Once the clock reaches the event's start, cancelling a `CONFIRMED` order is no longer possible and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. `FULFILLED`, `CANCELLED`, and `CANCELLED_LATE` are terminal.

### 3.7. Event-Day Service II: Guest Credentials

The **AccessCredential** is the event-day guest list entry for a suite. Issuing one — open to the owning `AccountHolder`, the `EventServicesManager`, or `StadiumAdmin` — takes the `event_id` and `suite_id`, the guest's `guest_name` and `guest_email`, a `credential_type` of `HOLDER`, `GUEST`, or `VIP`, and exactly one of `agreement_id` or `rental_id`, subject to the same referential-consistency rules as catering: the agreement or rental must match the body's suite and event, and an agreement-context credential requires a license-covered `REGULAR` or `RIVALRY` event in one of the agreement's covered seasons. Guards mirror catering as well: the account must not be `SUSPENDED` (`ERR_ACCOUNT_SUSPENDED`), the event must be `SCHEDULED` (`ERR_EVENT_NOT_SCHEDULED`), an agreement context requires the agreement `ACTIVE` (`ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`), and a rental context requires the rental `CONFIRMED`, failing otherwise with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.

Two capacity rules bound the guest list. First, the count of credentials in `ISSUED` or `ACTIVATED` for the same event and suite must remain within the suite's `capacity_seats`: issuing the credential that exactly reaches capacity succeeds, and the next attempt fails with `ERR_CREDENTIAL_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Second, a `guest_email` may appear on only one credential per event and suite among those not `REVOKED` or `EXPIRED`; a repeat is `ERR_DUPLICATE_GUEST`. A newly issued credential carries a server-minted unique `barcode` and starts in `ISSUED`. The guest list itself is read through the credential list endpoint, which filters by `event_id`, `suite_id`, `agreement_id`, `rental_id`, `guest_email`, or `status`.

Activation is the turnstile moment, reserved to `EventServicesManager` and `StadiumAdmin`: moving `ISSUED` to `ACTIVATED` requires the event still `SCHEDULED` (else `ERR_EVENT_NOT_SCHEDULED`) and the clock inside the admission window, which runs from four hours before the event's `starts_at` through `starts_at` plus `duration_hours`, both ends inclusive; outside that window the attempt is `ERR_CREDENTIAL_WINDOW`. Revocation — `ISSUED` to `REVOKED` — is open to event-services staff and to the `AccountHolder` who owns the credential's agreement or rental account, with other holders rejected by `ERR_ACCOUNT_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. Expiry is systemic: an event cancellation expires its credentials whether `ISSUED` or `ACTIVATED`, while an event completion expires only those still `ISSUED`. `ACTIVATED` is deliberately sticky through completion — the guest attended — so only a cancelled event takes an activated credential to `EXPIRED`. `REVOKED` and `EXPIRED` are terminal.

### 3.8. Single-Event Suite Rentals

Not every suite night belongs to a license holder; the **SingleEventRental** sells one event at a time. A quote is created by a `PremiumSalesRep`, the renting `AccountHolder`, or a `StadiumAdmin`, naming the `event_id`, `suite_id`, renting `account_id`, and an expected `guest_count` that must be positive. The price is computed once at creation and is immutable afterwards. The event's `rental_base_price_cents` is multiplied by the suite's tier multiplier — 1.60 for `FOUNDERS`, 1.30 for `PLATINUM`, 1.00 for `GOLD`, and 0.80 for `SILVER` — then a per-guest overage of 1,500 cents is added for every guest beyond the suite's `capacity_seats`, with no overage when the party fits within capacity. If the event is a `PLAYOFF`, that intermediate sum is then multiplied by 1.25; any other event type multiplies by 1.00. The `quoted_price_cents` is that final product, rounded half-up in a single rounding at the end, and the `deposit_cents` is computed separately as thirty percent of the quoted price, rounded half-up. Rentals begin `QUOTED` and can be listed filtered by `event_id`, `suite_id`, `account_id`, or `status`, with holders seeing only their own.

Placing a hold moves `QUOTED` to `HELD` and is open to the rep, the renting holder, or admin. The suite must be free for that event: no other rental on the same suite and event may sit in `HELD` or `CONFIRMED`; for `REGULAR` and `RIVALRY` events the suite must additionally be free of any agreement in `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED`, or `EXPIRING` covering the event's season; and the suite must not be `OUT_OF_SERVICE` — any of these is `ERR_SUITE_NOT_AVAILABLE`. The renting account must not be `SUSPENDED` (else `ERR_ACCOUNT_SUSPENDED`). The same availability set-membership check also guards the initial quote creation. A successful hold stamps `hold_expires_at` — null until the rental first enters `HELD` — at exactly forty-eight hours after the clock.

Confirmation, moving `HELD` to `CONFIRMED`, is a staff action for the rep or admin. The hold must still be live — the clock at or before `hold_expires_at`, else `ERR_HOLD_EXPIRED` — the account's `credit_standing` must not be `HOLD` (else `ERR_ACCOUNT_CREDIT_HOLD`), and availability is re-checked in case the suite filled behind the hold (else `ERR_SUITE_NOT_AVAILABLE`). Confirmation bills the rental: a `RENTAL` invoice is created already `ISSUED` for the full `quoted_price_cents`, with `due_on` seven days before the event's `starts_at`.

Cancelling a rental is cheapest early and free in the bookend states. From `QUOTED` or `HELD`, cancellation by the rep, the renting holder, or admin lands in `CANCELLED` with a `cancel_fee_cents` of zero; a holder cancelling someone else's rental is rejected with `ERR_ACCOUNT_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. Cancelling from `CONFIRMED` requires the rental's invoice to still be `ISSUED` — a `PARTIALLY_PAID` or `PAID` invoice demands settlement first and yields `ERR_RENTAL_SETTLEMENT_REQUIRED` — and requires the clock strictly before the event's `starts_at`, else `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. The fee is tiered by the number of whole days between the cancellation clock and the event's start: fourteen days or more out costs ten percent of the quoted price; seven to thirteen days costs thirty percent; three to six days costs fifty percent; and under three days costs the entire quoted price. Cancelling from `QUOTED` or `HELD`, or cancelling because the event itself was `CANCELLED`, always costs zero. On a fee-bearing cancellation the original rental invoice is voided and, when the fee is greater than zero, a new `RENTAL` invoice is issued containing a single cancellation-fee line, due fourteen days after the clock.

Holds do not linger forever: a `StadiumAdmin` may move `HELD` to `EXPIRED` once the clock is strictly past `hold_expires_at` (earlier attempts fail with `ERR_HOLD_NOT_EXPIRED`), and may move `QUOTED` to `EXPIRED` without condition. When the underlying event completes, a `CONFIRMED` rental settles automatically into `SETTLED`. `SETTLED`, `CANCELLED`, and `EXPIRED` are terminal.

### 3.9. Transfers and Subleases

A holder that wants out — or wants to sublet — files a **TransferRequest**. Filing is the holder's own act: the `AccountHolder` caller must own `from_account_id`, which must equal the agreement's current `account_id`, and the request names the `agreement_id`, the `to_account_id` of the prospective transferee — which must differ from `from_account_id`, a self-transfer being `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — a `transfer_type` of `SALE_TRANSFER` or `SUBLEASE`, and a declared `asking_price_cents` of zero or greater, which drives the sublease fee. Two standing rules apply: a `SUBLEASE` may never target a `BROKER` account, and only one transfer per agreement may be in flight — a second request while one sits in `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `INFO_REQUESTED`, or `APPROVED` is rejected with `ERR_TRANSFER_ALREADY_PENDING`. A fresh request is `SUBMITTED`, and the list filters by `agreement_id`, `from_account_id`, `to_account_id`, or `status`.

Review begins when a rep or admin moves `SUBMITTED` to `UNDER_REVIEW`, at which moment the `transfer_fee_cents` is computed once and frozen on the request. The fee divides by type. For a `SALE_TRANSFER`, the elapsed fraction of the agreement is its `current_season_index` divided by its `term_seasons`, and the percentage applied to the agreement's remaining value — the sum of all `fee_schedule_cents` entries beyond the current season index — is ten percent when the fraction is below 0.25, six percent when it is at least 0.25 and below 0.75, and three percent when it is at least 0.75; a fraction of exactly 0.25 therefore prices at six percent and exactly 0.75 at three percent. The fee is the greater of a 50,000-cent flat minimum and the percentage of remaining value rounded half-up. For a `SUBLEASE`, the fee is the greater of the same 50,000-cent flat minimum and five percent of `asking_price_cents` rounded half-up.

The review loop itself is unbounded: staff may move `UNDER_REVIEW` to `INFO_REQUESTED` to ask the holder for documentation, and the holder — and only the holder owning `from_account_id`, else `ERR_ACCOUNT_SCOPE_VIOLATION` — returns it to `UNDER_REVIEW` with the requested information, as many times as needed. The holder may also walk away, moving `SUBMITTED` or `INFO_REQUESTED` to `WITHDRAWN`, and staff may decline the file at review, moving `UNDER_REVIEW` to `REJECTED`.

Approval — `UNDER_REVIEW` to `APPROVED`, by rep or admin — re-verifies the world: the agreement must be `ACTIVE` (else `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`), the target account must be `ACTIVE` and, for a sublease, not a `BROKER` (else `ERR_TRANSFER_TARGET_INELIGIBLE`), and the transferring account must have no invoice in `ISSUED` or `PARTIALLY_PAID` that is past due (else `ERR_UNSETTLED_BALANCE`). Approval bills the seller: a `TRANSFER_FEE` invoice is created already `ISSUED` to the `from_account_id`, due fourteen days after the clock.

Completion, moving `APPROVED` to `COMPLETED`, is gated on that fee being settled — the transfer-fee invoice must be `PAID`, else `ERR_UNSETTLED_BALANCE`. What completion does depends on the type. A `SALE_TRANSFER` drives the agreement to `TRANSFERRED`, spawning the successor `ACTIVE` agreement described in §3.3 — transferee account, same suite, remaining covered seasons, the tail of the original fee schedule, unchanged food-and-beverage minimum, execution stamped at the clock — while the suite remains `LICENSED` and points at the successor. A `SUBLEASE` leaves the agreement `ACTIVE` and simply records the sublessee by setting the agreement's `active_sublease_account_id` to the `to_account_id`. `COMPLETED`, `REJECTED`, and `WITHDRAWN` are terminal.

### 3.10. Renewal Negotiation and the Waitlist

As an agreement's final season approaches, the sales office works the **RenewalOffer**. A rep or admin creates an offer against an agreement by naming the `agreement_id`, the `season_id` that begins the proposed renewal term, the commercial terms — `proposed_fee_cents` of zero or greater as the renewal term's season-one fee, `proposed_escalator_bp` between 0 and 2000 inclusive, and `proposed_term_seasons` between 1 and 10 inclusive — and a `response_due_on` deadline by which the holder must answer; the deadline is inclusive, and once it passes the offer becomes eligible for expiry in the settle sweep. The `season_id` is constrained: it must be the venue's `PUBLISHED` season with the smallest `starts_on` strictly after the `starts_on` of the agreement's last covered season. The clock must also fall within the renewal window of the agreement's last covered season — from `renewal_window_opens_on` through `renewal_window_closes_on`, both ends inclusive — or creation fails with `ERR_RENEWAL_WINDOW_CLOSED`. Creation is OCC-guarded per §2.7: the request must carry `expected_agreement_version` matching the agreement's current `version`, because creating an offer legitimately supersedes the agreement's outstanding offer — any existing offer of the same agreement in `OFFERED` or `REVISED` moves to `SUPERSEDED` — and increments the agreement's `version` by one; a create carrying a pre-supersede version is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION`. Offers can be listed filtered by `agreement_id`, `season_id`, or `status`.

A new offer is `OFFERED` with a `round_index` of 1, and from there negotiation is a free cycle until a terminal answer. The holder counters — moving `OFFERED` or `REVISED` to `COUNTERED` with a `counter_fee_cents` naming the fee it would accept — provided the caller owns the agreement's account, else `ERR_ACCOUNT_SCOPE_VIOLATION`; each counter increments `round_index` by one. The office revises — moving `COUNTERED` to `REVISED` with a `revised_fee_cents`, which replaces the offer's `proposed_fee_cents` — a step reserved to the rep and admin, likewise incrementing `round_index` by one. The cycle of offer, counter, revision, counter, and so on may repeat without bound.

Acceptance ends the loop and is the holder's call from either `OFFERED` or `REVISED`: the caller must own the agreement's account, and the clock must be at or before `response_due_on`, after which acceptance is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Acceptance immediately creates the renewal LicenseAgreement in `DRAFT` — same suite and account, `start_season_id` taken from the offer's `season_id`, term, escalator, and season-one fee taken from the offer, and the food-and-beverage minimum taken from the suite's current `f_b_minimum_cents` — and records it on the expiring agreement's `renewal_agreement_id`, which is what §3.5's term bookkeeping consults at settle. Declining, likewise the holder's call from `OFFERED` or `REVISED`, simply ends the offer in `DECLINED`. Two systemic endings remain: a newer offer for the same agreement supersedes one in `OFFERED` or `REVISED`, and the settle sweep expires offers in `OFFERED`, `REVISED`, or `COUNTERED` whose `response_due_on` has passed. `ACCEPTED`, `DECLINED`, `EXPIRED`, and `SUPERSEDED` are terminal.

Demand that cannot yet be placed waits on the **WaitlistEntry**. Joining — open to the holder for its own account, or to rep and admin staff — takes the `account_id`, a `desired_tier` of `FOUNDERS`, `PLATINUM`, `GOLD`, or `SILVER`, and an optional `target_season_id`, where null means any future season. An account that is `SUSPENDED` may not join (else `ERR_ACCOUNT_SUSPENDED`), and an account may hold only one non-terminal entry for the same tier and target season: a second entry while one is `WAITING` or `OFFERED` for the same combination is `ERR_WAITLIST_DUPLICATE`. Entries begin `WAITING` and can be listed filtered by `account_id`, `desired_tier`, `target_season_id`, or `status`.

Fairness on the list is computed, not negotiated. An entry's `priority_score` is recomputed on every read from three components: waiting time contributes half a point for every whole thirty-day period since the entry's `created_at`, capped at ten periods; spending history contributes three-tenths of a point for every whole 100,000 cents — one thousand dollars — of the account's `lifetime_spend_cents`, with the counted units capped at fifty (so spending contributes at most 15.0 points); and a `CORPORATE` account adds a flat 2.0-point bonus while other account types add nothing. The score is rounded to four decimal places.

Promotion moves the top of the list to `OFFERED` and names a `suite_id` — a staff step reserved to `PremiumSalesRep` and `StadiumAdmin`, with an `AccountHolder` attempt rejected as `ERR_FORBIDDEN_FOR_ROLE`. The named suite's `tier` must equal the entry's `desired_tier` (else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`), the suite must be `AVAILABLE` (else `ERR_SUITE_NOT_AVAILABLE`), no other entry may already be `OFFERED` against that same suite (else `ERR_SUITE_OFFER_PENDING`), and the promoted entry must be the top-ranked eligible `WAITING` entry, else `ERR_NOT_NEXT_IN_LINE`. Eligibility for a candidate suite means the entry is `WAITING`, its `desired_tier` matches the suite's tier, and its `target_season_id` is either null or names a season whose `starts_on` lies after the clock. Ranking is by `priority_score` descending, with ties broken first by the entry's earlier `created_at`, then by the entry's creation sequence within the same logical-clock instant, and then by the account's earlier `created_at`. An empty eligible pool is not an error in itself — promotion is simply impossible until the pool refills, and any promote call naming a non-top entry fails with `ERR_NOT_NEXT_IN_LINE`. A successful promotion records the suite in the entry's `offered_suite_id`.

Conversion turns the offer into paper: moving `OFFERED` to `CONVERTED` — a rep or admin step — requires the offered suite still `AVAILABLE` (else `ERR_SUITE_NOT_AVAILABLE`) and creates a LicenseAgreement in `DRAFT` for the offered suite and the entry's account, with `start_season_id` taken from the entry's `target_season_id` when set and otherwise from the venue's earliest `PUBLISHED` season whose `starts_on` lies after the clock (when the entry names no target season and no such future `PUBLISHED` season exists, the conversion fails with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`), the base fee and food-and-beverage minimum taken from the suite's defaults, an escalator of zero, and a term of one season. Declining the offer — open to the entry's holder or a rep — releases the suite by clearing `offered_suite_id` to null and ends the entry in `DECLINED`; a holder acting on another account's entry is rejected with `ERR_ACCOUNT_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. Withdrawal from `WAITING` by the holder or a rep ends the entry in `WITHDRAWN`, and the settle sweep expires `WAITING` entries whose `target_season_id` is the season being settled. `CONVERTED`, `DECLINED`, `EXPIRED`, and `WITHDRAWN` are terminal.

### 3.11. Amenity and Renovation Assessments

Capital projects — a club renovation, a new amenity floor — are recovered through the **Assessment**, created by a `StadiumAdmin` with the `venue_id` being improved, a `name` such as "2027 Club Renovation", a `total_cost_cents` that must be positive, and an `allocation_method` of `PER_SUITE_EQUAL` or `BY_SEAT`. The review flow is administrative throughout: a `StadiumAdmin` moves the assessment from `DRAFT` to `PROPOSED` and from `PROPOSED` to `APPROVED`, and may abandon it from either `DRAFT` or `PROPOSED` into `CANCELLED`. Assessments can be listed filtered by `venue_id` or `status`.

Levying — the `APPROVED` to `LEVIED` step, reserved to `FinanceController` and `StadiumAdmin` — requires at least one suite of the venue currently `LICENSED`, else `ERR_NO_LICENSED_SUITES`. The levy stamps `levied_at` — null until the assessment reaches `LEVIED` — with the clock and allocates the total cost across exactly those `LICENSED` suites, considered in a stable order by their `created_at` ascending. Under `PER_SUITE_EQUAL`, every share is the whole-cent floor of the total divided by the number of eligible suites, and the leftover cents — the total minus the sum of those floored shares — are distributed one cent each to the earliest-created suites until exhausted. Under `BY_SEAT`, each suite's raw share is the total cost multiplied by its `capacity_seats` divided by the sum of `capacity_seats` across all eligible suites; the share is the whole-cent floor of that raw figure, and the leftover cents are distributed one cent each in descending order of fractional part — the raw share minus its floor — with ties broken by earlier suite `created_at`. Either way, one `ASSESSMENT` invoice is created per eligible suite, billed to the account of that suite's current agreement, created already `ISSUED` with `due_on` sixty days after the clock, and each billed account's `balance_due_cents` recomputes upward.

The assessment settles itself: once every `ASSESSMENT` invoice it spawned has reached `PAID` or `VOID`, the assessment moves automatically from `LEVIED` to `SETTLED`. `SETTLED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal.
