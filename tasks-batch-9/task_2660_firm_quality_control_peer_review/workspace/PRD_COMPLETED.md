# Firm Quality Control & Peer Review API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A public accounting firm lives or dies by the credibility of its system of quality management. Regulators, the peer review programme it enrols in, and its own partners all need the same question answered continuously: does this firm identify its quality risks, respond to them, inspect its own engagements honestly, find the root cause when something goes wrong, remediate it, and submit to an independent external review of the whole apparatus? Today that evidence is scattered across spreadsheets, partner memories and PDF letters. Nothing connects a material inspection failure on a released audit back to the monitoring conclusion it invalidates, and nothing stops the partner who owns a remediation plan from being the person who signs off that it worked.

This service is the firm's quality management system of record. It carries the firm's quality risks and the responses designed against them, the engagements the firm performs and the two independent lifecycles every engagement runs through, the engagement quality reviews that must precede report release on higher-risk work, the internal inspection programme with its deterministic engagement selector, the classification and root-cause analysis of every finding, the remediation plans that answer those findings, and the external peer review cycle from scheduling through reviewer selection, scope determination, fieldwork, report rating and corrective action. Every consequence is immediate and computed by the server: a firm's `risk_posture` is a derived value, not a judgement call; an inspection cycle's engagement list is produced by a pinned selector rather than by the caller; a remediation plan's acceptability is a scored number with a fixed threshold.

Two design commitments run through everything that follows. The first is **segregation of duties**: an engagement partner may never quality-review their own engagement, a quality control director may never raise an inspection finding on an engagement they personally led, and the partner who owns a remediation plan may never be the partner who returns, accepts or verifies it. The second is **conservation**: every denormalised counter the firm exposes — open deficiencies, open material failures, critical risks, committed peer review hours — must read exactly the same value after a compensating operation as it did before the operation it compensates. Callers are expected to treat those counters as trustworthy inputs to their own decisions, so the server never leaves them drifting.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request carries two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting individual, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which they act. Both headers are trimmed of surrounding whitespace before they are read, so the trimmed `X-User-ID` is what every ownership comparison uses. When either header is missing, empty, or made up entirely of whitespace the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401); the single exception is firm registration (`POST /firms`), which is not covered by the header-presence check but is still restricted to `SystemAdmin`, so an unheadered registration attempt is refused by the role check with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` instead. `X-User-Role` must be one of exactly seven values, spelled as follows:

- `SystemAdmin` — the platform operator. Registers firms, drives the logical clock, and may exercise every other endpoint in the system for setup and recovery purposes, subject to exactly the same business rules that bind the role which normally performs the operation.
- `FirmManagingPartner` — the partner with ultimate responsibility for the firm's system of quality management. Maintains the partner roster, accepts clients, records quality risks, opens inspection cycles, and answers to the peer review committee.
- `EngagementPartner` — the partner with final responsibility for an individual engagement. Drives that engagement's performance and documentation lifecycles and owns remediation of findings raised against it, but may never review or inspect their own work.
- `QualityControlDirector` — the partner who designs and monitors the system of quality management: quality risks and responses, the internal inspection programme, finding classification, and remediation oversight.
- `EngagementQualityReviewer` — the partner assigned to perform an engagement quality review, acting only on reviews assigned to them.
- `PeerReviewer` — the external reviewer drawn from the committee's roster who performs the firm's peer review: scope determination, engagement selection, dispositions and the rating draft.
- `PeerReviewCommittee` — the programme administrator of the peer review body. Maintains the reviewer roster, schedules reviews, assigns reviewers, issues reports and adjudicates corrective-action evidence, and never performs the fieldwork it adjudicates.

When `X-User-Role` is not listed among the roles authorised for the requested endpoint (see the table in §4), the request fails with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

Five of the seven roles are **entity-backed**, which means `X-User-ID` is expected to resolve to a persisted row, and ownership checks compare that value against the resource's own foreign keys. When `X-User-Role` is `FirmManagingPartner`, `EngagementPartner`, `QualityControlDirector` or `EngagementQualityReviewer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Partner`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Partner` compare the resource's partner foreign-key field to `X-User-ID` — `Engagement.engagement_partner_id` for an engagement partner, `EngagementQualityReview.reviewer_partner_id` for an engagement quality reviewer, and `RemediationPlan.owner_partner_id` for a plan submitter or completer. When `X-User-Role` is `PeerReviewer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `PeerReviewer` row, and ownership on a peer review compares `PeerReview.assigned_reviewer_id` to `X-User-ID`. If an entity-backed role presents an `X-User-ID` that does not resolve to a row of its backing entity — a `Partner` row for the four firm-side roles, a `PeerReviewer` row for `PeerReviewer` — the request fails with `ERR_ROLE_IDENTITY_UNKNOWN` (HTTP 403). If it resolves but does not match the resource's owner field, the request fails with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403); a resource whose owner field is still null is owned by nobody, so an entity-backed actor addressing it fails exactly the same way.

The remaining two roles are opaque. When `X-User-Role` is `SystemAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it for audit purposes but never compares it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `PeerReviewCommittee`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque programme-administrator identifier, never compared to any entity's `id` — the committee's authority is programme-wide rather than resource-scoped.

Firm-scoped isolation applies to the four `Partner`-backed roles. Such an actor may only read or write resources belonging to the firm named by their own `Partner.firm_id`, resolved through the target resource's own foreign-key chain (a finding, for example, resolves through its inspection selection to its inspection cycle to that cycle's firm). A cross-firm attempt — including one whose foreign-key chain resolves to no firm at all — is refused with `ERR_CROSS_FIRM_ACCESS_DENIED` (HTTP 403). The comparison is made on every write and on every single-resource read, with one exception: the reviewer roster is not a firm-scoped aggregate at all, so `GET /peer-reviewers/{reviewer_id}` carries no isolation check and is open to every role §4 authorises for it. **List endpoints are never firm-scoped.** A `Partner`-backed actor listing any collection sees the matching rows of every firm, and narrows the page itself with the `firm_id` filter where one is offered; only the single-resource reads and the writes refuse a foreign firm.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`, followed by exactly 20 characters each drawn at random from the twenty-six uppercase letters `A` through `Z` and the ten digits `0` through `9`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: its default is 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: its default is 0 and it may never be negative. A `limit` below 1, a `limit` above 100, or a negative `offset` is rejected with `ERR_PAGINATION_OUT_OF_RANGE` (HTTP 422). A parameter that is omitted altogether, or supplied as an empty string, takes its default. Either parameter must otherwise spell a whole decimal integer in canonical form once surrounding whitespace has been stripped, and a value that does not — `abc`, `1.5`, `true`, `+5` and `05` among them — is rejected with `ERR_PAGINATION_OUT_OF_RANGE` as well, never with `ERR_VALIDATION_FAILED`.

List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending unless a subsection below states otherwise for a specific collection. Because the logical clock can be held still, rows sharing an identical `created_at` are ordered among themselves by the order in which the server created them, and that same creation order resolves a tie in whatever alternative sort key a subsection names for a specific collection; the resource `id` is never a sort or tie-break key. The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` (the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied) and `has_more` (true when rows remain beyond the current page).

The query filters each list endpoint offers are named in that endpoint's row of §4, and they behave uniformly. A filter is **matched, never validated**. One that is omitted, or supplied as an empty string, narrows nothing; several supplied together are conjunctive, so a row must satisfy every one of them. A string-typed filter — every `*_id` filter and every enum-valued filter among them — is compared verbatim against the stored field, with no trimming, case folding or vocabulary check; a boolean-typed filter reads `true`, `false`, `1` and `0`, matched case-insensitively once surrounding whitespace is stripped; and the one integer-typed filter, `inspection_year`, is read as a decimal integer once surrounding whitespace is stripped. A filter value that cannot be read as its declared type at all, or that spells an enum member no vocabulary contains, or that names an `id` no row carries, is nonetheless never an error: it simply matches no row, so the response is HTTP 200 carrying an empty `data` array with `total_count` 0 — never `ERR_VALIDATION_FAILED` and never `ERR_INVALID_ENUM_VALUE`. `ERR_PAGINATION_OUT_OF_RANGE` remains the only way a list read fails on its query string.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Versioning and the Logical Clock

The service holds all of its state in memory and seeds none of it, so a freshly started server owns no rows at all and every entity a caller reads must first have been created through a declared endpoint. Every resource carries an `id` that is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp in ISO 8601 UTC form with three fractional digits. A resource object returned in `data` always carries its full declared field set, with `null` for any value not yet established. Every entity in this system also carries `updated_at` (same format as `created_at`, refreshed on every mutation) and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row — including mutations the row suffers as a side effect of an operation aimed at a different resource, with §2.9 pinning when a merely recomputed row counts as mutated at all. `Firm`, `Partner`, `Engagement`, `QualityRisk`, `RiskResponse`, `EngagementQualityReview`, `InternalInspection`, `InspectionSelection`, `InspectionFinding`, `RootCauseAnalysis`, `RemediationPlan`, `PeerReviewer`, `PeerReview`, `PeerReviewSelection` and `CorrectiveActionPlan` all expose the full set of `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` and `version`.

All date and datetime rules in this specification read a **logical clock** rather than wall-clock time. Before any caller sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; it never falls back to real wall-clock time. A `SystemAdmin` may move it by supplying a `current_utc` instant to the clock-setting endpoint, which mutates no entity and increments no `version`. That instant is read as ISO 8601 and normalised to UTC, so a trailing `Z`, an explicit numeric offset (whose value is converted to UTC), a bare value carrying no designator at all (read as already being UTC) and a date-only `YYYY-MM-DD` value (read as midnight) are all accepted, while a value that cannot be parsed at all is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION_FAILED`. The endpoint answers HTTP 200 with a `data` object whose single field `current_utc` echoes the new instant in the standard three-fractional-digit form. The logical clock is what the 60-day documentation window, the 730-day cooling-off gate, the corrective-action due-date check, the lateness penalty in remediation scoring, and every `*_at` stamp in the system are measured against.

### 2.5. Request Evaluation Order

When a request would fail several checks at once, the order in which the server evaluates them decides which `error_id` the caller sees. That order is fixed and global:

1. **Header presence and role vocabulary** — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. **Endpoint role authorisation** — `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.
3. **Actor identity resolution** for entity-backed roles — `ERR_ROLE_IDENTITY_UNKNOWN`.
4. **Path resolution, left to right.** A `{firm_id}` segment is resolved first and yields `ERR_FIRM_NOT_FOUND`; then `{engagement_id}`, yielding `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_NOT_FOUND`; then any remaining path identifier, each with its own code — `{eqr_id}` gives `ERR_QUALITY_REVIEW_NOT_FOUND`, `{risk_id}` gives `ERR_QUALITY_RISK_NOT_FOUND`, `{response_id}` gives `ERR_RISK_RESPONSE_NOT_FOUND`, `{inspection_id}` gives `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_FOUND`, a `{selection_id}` addressed as an inspection selection gives `ERR_INSPECTION_SELECTION_NOT_FOUND`, `{finding_id}` gives `ERR_FINDING_NOT_FOUND`, `{rca_id}` gives `ERR_RCA_NOT_FOUND`, `{plan_id}` gives `ERR_REMEDIATION_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, `{reviewer_id}` gives `ERR_PEER_REVIEWER_NOT_FOUND`, `{peer_review_id}` gives `ERR_PEER_REVIEW_NOT_FOUND`, a `{selection_id}` addressed beneath a peer review gives `ERR_PEER_REVIEW_SELECTION_NOT_FOUND` (also raised when the selection exists but is not attached to the peer review named in the path), `{cap_id}` gives `ERR_CORRECTIVE_ACTION_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, and `{partner_id}` gives `ERR_PARTNER_NOT_FOUND`. All of these are HTTP 404.
5. **Body foreign-key resolution.** A `Partner` reference in a body that does not resolve gives `ERR_PARTNER_NOT_FOUND` — this covers `engagement_partner_id` on engagement acceptance, `reviewer_partner_id` on quality-review assignment, and `owner_partner_id` on remediation-plan creation. A `root_cause_analysis_id` that does not resolve gives `ERR_RCA_NOT_FOUND`. An `engagement_id` supplied in a peer-review selection body, or a `reviewer_id` supplied in a reviewer-assignment body, that does not resolve gives `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_PEER_REVIEWER_NOT_FOUND` respectively.
6. **Request shape.** Every required input field must be present and correctly typed, and numeric inputs must honour their declared ranges — `likelihood` from 1 through 5, `impact` from 1 through 5, `action_count` from 1 through 20, `action_specificity_score` from 0.00 through 10.00, `hours_committed` from 1 through 200, `target_engagement_count` at least 1, and `cpe_hours_last_3y` at least 0. Every declared range is inclusive at both ends. Violations give `ERR_VALIDATION_FAILED` (HTTP 422). "Correctly typed" is lenient about how a number is spelled and strict about everything else: an integer-typed field also accepts a decimal string such as `"1200"` and a JSON number whose fractional part is zero such as `900.0`, and a decimal-typed field also accepts a JSON integer or a numeric string, but a JSON boolean is never accepted where a number is wanted, and a boolean-typed field accepts only a literal `true` or `false` and never the strings `"true"` or `"false"`. A request body that is absent, empty or unparseable as JSON is treated as an empty object, so such a request fails on its first missing required field rather than with a parse-specific code; a body that parses to something other than a JSON object — an array or a bare scalar — gives `ERR_VALIDATION_FAILED` directly.
7. **Enum membership.** Every enum-typed input — including `to_state`, `basis`, `severity_index_basis`, `outcome`, `disposition`, `classification` and `pervasiveness` — must be a member of its declared set, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_ENUM_VALUE` (HTTP 422). A `to_state` naming a state that does not exist in the target machine at all is an enum violation, not a transition conflict. An enum-typed **array** input — `designations`, `industry_specializations` and `qualified_categories` — must itself be a JSON array, otherwise `ERR_VALIDATION_FAILED`, and every element must be a member of the declared set, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_ENUM_VALUE`; the accepted array is then stored de-duplicated, each surviving value keeping the position of its first occurrence. A free-text string array — `independence_conflict_client_names`, `independence_conflict_firm_names` and `prior_reviewer_ids` — is stored exactly as supplied, duplicates and order included.
8. **Pagination range** on list endpoints — `ERR_PAGINATION_OUT_OF_RANGE`.
9. **Resource ownership** — `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`.
10. **Firm isolation** — `ERR_CROSS_FIRM_ACCESS_DENIED`.
11. **Optimistic concurrency** — `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (see §2.6).
12. **State preconditions on the target resource**, then **cross-entity state dependencies**, then **referential consistency across aggregates**, then **safety, objectivity, independence and segregation-of-duties checks**, then **capacity, uniqueness and coverage constraints**, and last of all **fiscal checks** (`ERR_SCOPE_HOURS_EXCEEDED` and `ERR_ACCEPTANCE_SCORE_BELOW_THRESHOLD`). The subsections of §3 state the internal ordering wherever two checks in these bands can fire on the same request.

Three worked collisions pin the ordering where it is least obvious. When an engagement quality reviewer is assigned to an engagement that has already reached `REPORT_RELEASED` **and** the candidate reviewer's separation from the client's engagement team falls short of the cooling-off requirement, the response is `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_NOT_IN_EQR_WINDOW`, because the target-state precondition outranks the independence-and-objectivity band. When a peer review selection names an engagement belonging to a different firm **and** commits more hours than the review has left, the response is `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_FIRM_MISMATCH`, because referential consistency outranks the fiscal band. When a named peer reviewer both has a colliding on-site window and is already at their concurrent-review ceiling, the response is `ERR_REVIEWER_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, because the temporal-window check is evaluated ahead of the capacity check.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency on Parent-Anchored Creates

Exactly three write operations create a child row whose meaning depends on a parent row the client must have observed first. Each of them requires a non-optional version field in its request body, and each rejects a mismatch with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

- Assigning an engagement quality reviewer (`POST /engagements/{engagement_id}/quality-reviews`) requires `expected_engagement_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Engagement`. If the engagement's current `version` differs, the assignment is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This is the serialization point that catches a client whose view of the engagement predates a withdrawal of an earlier quality review (which legitimately frees the engagement for a fresh reviewer and advances its `version`) or predates the material-failure posture cascade that set `eqr_required` on it.
- Creating a remediation plan (`POST /inspection-findings/{finding_id}/remediation-plans`) requires `expected_finding_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `InspectionFinding`. A mismatch with the finding's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Creating a plan is itself a legitimate operation on a reopened finding — it supersedes the finding's prior accepted or completed plan — so the version check is what prevents a writer holding a pre-reopen view of the finding from attaching a plan to it.
- Committing hours to a peer review selection (`POST /peer-reviews/{peer_review_id}/selections`) requires `expected_peer_review_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the parent `PeerReview`. A mismatch with the review's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Every selection commit and every selection withdrawal re-derives the review's remaining hours and advances its `version`, so a stale writer would otherwise be committing hours against an hours budget that no longer exists.

No other endpoint accepts a version field, and no other endpoint may reject a request for concurrency reasons.

### 2.7. Numeric Precision and Rounding Order

Monetary-style precision does not arise in this domain, but scoring precision does, and it is exact. Unless a rule says otherwise, every computed non-integer value is rounded to two decimal places using round-half-up, and integer-valued scores are stored as integers with no rounding at all. Three rules additionally pin **where** rounding happens relative to summation, because the alternative orders produce different cents:

- A quality risk's residual score rounds once, at the end of the chosen variant — never partway through the fold.
- An inspection cycle's severity index under the engagement-weighted variant rounds each selection's weighted points to two decimals **first**, then sums those already-rounded values, divides, and rounds once more. Summing raw points across selections and applying the multiplier once at the end is not an acceptable substitute.
- A peer review's scope hours round the firm-size component and the must-select component to two decimals **separately**, then add them, then take the ceiling of the sum to a whole hour, which leaves an already-whole sum unchanged. Adding the raw components first and rounding once at the end is not an acceptable substitute.

Remediation acceptance scoring rounds exactly twice: once when unpenalised completeness is derived, and once when the lateness penalty has been applied. Root-cause depth scores are stored with two decimals even when the arithmetic yields a whole number, so a depth of eight reads as 8.00.

Every two-decimal quantity this specification writes as `8.00`, `0.00`, `100.00` or `16.50` is nevertheless carried on the wire as an ordinary JSON number rather than as a string or a fixed-width decimal, so the two-decimal form describes the rounding that produced the value and not its printed width: trailing zeros are not preserved, and those four values serialize as `8.0`, `0.0`, `100.0` and `16.5` respectively, while a value with non-zero cents such as `66.67` serializes as `66.67`. Equality against a stated figure is therefore numeric equality, and a score that has not been derived yet stays `null` rather than reading `0.0`. Rounding also applies only to values the server computes: the one decimal a caller supplies, `action_specificity_score`, is stored exactly as it was supplied and is never quantized on the way in, so a third decimal place is neither refused nor rounded away and the value enters the acceptance formula at the full precision received.

### 2.8. Segregation of Duties and Objectivity

Four separations are enforced by comparing `X-User-ID` against a partner foreign key on the target resource, and each has its own refusal code. A candidate engagement quality reviewer who is the engagement's own `engagement_partner_id` is refused with `ERR_EQR_SELF_REVIEW` (HTTP 403). A quality control director raising an inspection finding on an engagement they personally led is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). The same code refuses the owner of a remediation plan who attempts to return or accept that plan, and refuses a partner who attempts to verify a finding whose completed remediation plan they own. Conversely, only the plan's owner may submit it or report it complete; another partner attempting those legs is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`.

Objectivity is affirmative rather than assumed: an engagement quality reviewer assignment must carry an explicit attestation, and independence is checked against declared conflict lists on both the internal and the external side.

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline and Counter Conservation

Several operations in this system mutate more than one entity. When they do, every affected row's `version` is incremented and its `updated_at` refreshed, and the whole chain lands within the single request — there is no deferred or eventual settlement. What decides a borderline case is direct versus merely re-derived. The resource an operation is aimed at, and any related row a cascade link in §3 explicitly says gains a `version`, advance by exactly 1 in every case — even when the write leaves every one of their values at what they already held. A row an operation only recomputes as a background consequence — a firm whose counters, `last_peer_review_rating` and `risk_posture` are re-derived, a partner whose maintained counters and `eqr_eligible` are refreshed, a parent quality risk whose `response_count` is re-derived — advances only when that recomputation actually changes one of its stored values, and is left entirely untouched, `version` and `updated_at` alike, when it changes nothing. A firm's counter block (`open_deficiency_count`, `open_material_failure_count`, `critical_risk_count` and `last_peer_review_rating`) and a firm's `risk_posture` are re-derived as **separate** writes, each advancing the firm's `version` on its own whenever it changes a stored value of its own, so a single request can legitimately advance one firm by more than 1 — by 2 where a posture re-run moves both a counter and the posture, and by as many as 3 where the same request also rewrites `prior_reviewer_ids` or re-derives the counters twice over the course of a cascade. Cascades are described link by link in §3, and each link is triggered by the link before it, not by the original request independently; a link whose cause did not fire does not fire either.

Conservation applies to five denormalised counters. `Firm.open_deficiency_count` and `Firm.open_material_failure_count` must return to their exact pre-verification values when a verified finding is reopened. `PeerReview.unallocated_hours` and `PeerReviewer.committed_hours_balance` must return to their exact pre-selection values when a committed selection is withdrawn, so that a review's `total_scope_hours` always equals its `unallocated_hours` plus the sum of `hours_committed` over its committed selections. `Firm.critical_risk_count` is recomputed from the underlying quality risks every time one of them enters or leaves its evaluated state rather than being adjusted incrementally.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Firm, Its People and Its Quality Posture

A firm enters the system when a platform operator registers it with a `legal_name` of 1 to 200 characters that no other firm already uses, a `state_code` of exactly two uppercase letters naming the licensing state of its main office, an `aa_hours_last_year` count of accounting-and-auditing hours billed in the prior calendar year (zero or more), and a `peer_review_year_end` date in `YYYY-MM-DD` form that its peer reviews are scheduled against. The `enrolled_program` may be supplied as either `AICPA_PRC` or `STATE_BOARD` and defaults to `AICPA_PRC` when omitted. A duplicate `legal_name` is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION_FAILED`; the uniqueness comparison is an exact string match, so two names differing only in letter case or in surrounding whitespace are distinct names. Registration starts every derived value at its floor: `risk_posture` is `STABLE`, `open_deficiency_count`, `open_material_failure_count` and `critical_risk_count` are each 0, `last_peer_review_rating` is `NONE`, `prior_reviewer_ids` is an empty array and `unassigned_review_flag` is false.

The committee maintains the programme half of that record afterwards and may revise `aa_hours_last_year`, `enrolled_program`, `peer_review_year_end` and `prior_reviewer_ids` — the last of these being the rotation history the committee carries forward from reviews performed outside this system. A revision to `aa_hours_last_year` changes the base tier of any peer review whose scope is determined afterwards but never restates a scope already set, and `prior_reviewer_ids` feeds the rotation-exclusion clause described in §3.9.

**The firm's `risk_posture` is the single most consequential derived value in the system**, and it is computed by a three-clause ladder evaluated strictly from the top; the first clause that holds decides the outcome. The posture is `CRITICAL` when the firm's `last_peer_review_rating` is `FAIL`, or its `open_material_failure_count` is at least 1, or its `critical_risk_count` is at least 3. Otherwise the posture is `ELEVATED` when `last_peer_review_rating` is `PASS_WITH_DEFICIENCY`, or `critical_risk_count` is at least 1, or `open_deficiency_count` is at least 5. Otherwise it is `STABLE`. The counters feeding this ladder are always recomputed from the underlying finding and quality-risk rows immediately before the ladder runs within the same request, so the posture never reads a stale counter. `open_deficiency_count` counts the firm's findings whose `classification` is `DEFICIENCY`, `SIGNIFICANT_DEFICIENCY` or `MATERIAL_FAILURE` and whose state is neither `VERIFIED` nor `WITHDRAWN`; `open_material_failure_count` counts the subset of those whose `classification` is `MATERIAL_FAILURE`; and `critical_risk_count` counts the firm's quality risks that are in `EVALUATED` with a `severity_band` of `CRITICAL`. The posture is recomputed on quality-risk evaluation, on the material-failure classification cascade, on both legs of the finding verification cycle, and on all three terminal legs of the peer review lifecycle.

Two consequences hang off the posture. A `CRITICAL` posture freezes client acceptance for the three must-select engagement types, and either `ELEVATED` or `CRITICAL` forces an engagement quality review onto every engagement the firm accepts from that moment on. The firm's `last_peer_review_rating` mirrors the rating of its most recent peer review that reached either `ACCEPTED` or `FOLLOW_UP_REQUIRED`, ordered by `report_issued_at` descending and, where two reviews share that instant, by `created_at` descending. That derivation is the whole rule: it runs as part of the same counter recomputation that feeds the posture ladder, so wherever §3.9 describes a terminal leg of the peer review lifecycle as writing this field with the closing review's own `rating`, the field is in fact re-derived by the rule above rather than copied from that review. The two readings agree unless the logical clock was moved backwards between two issuances, letting a review with the earlier `report_issued_at` be the later one to close out — in which case the firm keeps the rating of the review whose `report_issued_at` is later, not the rating of the review that closed out last. `prior_reviewer_ids` holds the reviewers of the firm's last three such reviews, newest first; it is consulted only as a membership test for rotation exclusion and never as an ordering, ranking or tie-break key. `unassigned_review_flag` is raised when a reviewer-assignment attempt finds an empty eligible pool and cleared as soon as a reviewer is successfully assigned.

Partners are added to a firm one at a time by the managing partner or the platform operator, and the created row takes its `firm_id` from the firm the partner is added to. A partner needs a `full_name` of 1 to 120 characters and a `cpa_license_state` of exactly two uppercase letters. Four further inputs are optional and each defaults to an empty collection or a floor value: `designations` is an array whose elements are drawn from `MANAGING_PARTNER`, `ENGAGEMENT_PARTNER`, `QC_DIRECTOR` and `EQ_REVIEWER`; `industry_specializations` is an array whose elements are drawn from `EBP`, `GOVERNMENT`, `BROKER_DEALER`, `NONPROFIT` and `MANUFACTURING`; `cpe_hours_last_3y` records continuing-education hours completed over the trailing three years and defaults to 0, never negative; and `prior_engagement_team_clients` is the partner's cooling-off history, each element being an object pairing a non-empty `client_name` with the `exit_date` in `YYYY-MM-DD` form on which the partner left that client's engagement team; only those two keys are retained, so any further key a supplied element carries is discarded. `independence_conflict_client_names` lists client names the partner has declared a conflict against, each element being a string of at most 200 characters, and `status` is either `ACTIVE` (the default) or `INACTIVE`. All of these fields, plus `status`, remain maintainable afterwards; a maintenance write recomputes the partner's eligibility but never retroactively invalidates an engagement quality review that already exists. Such a write is one single mutation of the partner row: the caller-supplied fields and the values the server recomputes beside them share one `version` increment, so the row's `version` advances by exactly 1 per maintenance write — and it advances even when the write leaves every value at what it already held.

`eqr_eligible` is recomputed on every partner write and is true only when all three of the following hold: the partner's `status` is `ACTIVE`, their `cpe_hours_last_3y` is at least 120, and their `designations` array contains `EQ_REVIEWER`. Three further partner values are maintained by the system rather than the caller. `open_eqr_count` counts the engagement quality reviews assigned to this partner whose state is `ASSIGNED`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `CONCERNS_RAISED`. `open_engagement_count` counts the engagements on which this partner is the engagement partner and whose `workflow_state` is `PLANNING`, `FIELDWORK`, `EQR_PENDING` or `EQR_CLEARED`. `last_inspected_year` records the highest inspection year in which one of this partner's engagements was selected for internal inspection, and is null while the partner has never been selected.

### 3.2. Accepting a Client and Scoring the Engagement

Accepting an engagement is the firm's first quality decision, and the system treats it as one. The acceptance payload names the `firm_id` taking the work, the `client_name` (1 to 200 characters), the `engagement_type` — one of `AUDIT`, `REVIEW`, `ATTESTATION`, `ERISA_AUDIT`, `SINGLE_AUDIT` or `BROKER_DEALER` — the client's `period_end` in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, and the `engagement_partner_id` of the partner taking final responsibility. Three optional flags refine the risk picture: `is_public_interest` marks a public-interest entity and defaults to false, `is_first_year_client` marks the firm's first engagement for this client and defaults to false, and `prior_finding_count_3y` records how many inspection or peer review findings have been raised against this client engagement in the trailing three years, defaulting to 0 and never negative. Both body references are resolved ahead of the payload's shape check, `firm_id` first and `engagement_partner_id` second, so a `firm_id` naming no firm gives `ERR_FIRM_NOT_FOUND` and an `engagement_partner_id` naming no partner gives `ERR_PARTNER_NOT_FOUND` even when some other required field of the payload is missing; a value of the wrong JSON type matches no row and therefore takes the same not-found code. A firm may not hold two engagements sharing the same `client_name` and `period_end`; the second attempt is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ENGAGEMENT`. The named engagement partner must belong to the accepting firm, otherwise `ERR_PARTNER_FIRM_MISMATCH`. When two or more of acceptance's three business checks would fire on the same request the order is fixed: `ERR_PARTNER_FIRM_MISMATCH` first, then the acceptance freeze of the next paragraph, then `ERR_DUPLICATE_ENGAGEMENT`.

Before anything is persisted, acceptance is refused outright with `ERR_ACCEPTANCE_FROZEN` when the firm's `risk_posture` is `CRITICAL` and the `engagement_type` is one of the three must-select types `ERISA_AUDIT`, `SINGLE_AUDIT` or `BROKER_DEALER`. A firm in `CRITICAL` posture may still take on ordinary audits, reviews and attestations; what it may not do is take on new employee-benefit-plan, single-audit or broker-dealer work while its monitoring conclusions are compromised.

The engagement's `risk_score` is then computed as an integer from 0 through 100 by a weighted sum whose parts are all integers, so no rounding arises. The base begins with a type weight: 20 points for an `AUDIT`, 8 for a `REVIEW`, 6 for an `ATTESTATION`, 30 for an `ERISA_AUDIT`, 32 for a `SINGLE_AUDIT` and 28 for a `BROKER_DEALER`. To that base add 15 points when `is_first_year_client` is true, 12 points when `is_public_interest` is true, and 4 points for each prior finding recorded in `prior_finding_count_3y` — with the prior-finding contribution capped at 20 points, so a client with five or more prior findings contributes the same 20 as a client with exactly five. A **load penalty** is then added: the engagement partner is allowed eight concurrent engagements without penalty, and every engagement beyond that eighth adds 3 points. The partner's `open_engagement_count` used here is the value as it stands **before** the new engagement is inserted, and the penalty is never negative — a partner carrying eight or fewer open engagements contributes zero. A **specialization credit** of 10 points is then subtracted, but only for the three specialised types and only when the partner actually holds the matching specialization: an `ERISA_AUDIT` earns the credit when the partner's `industry_specializations` contains `EBP`, a `SINGLE_AUDIT` when it contains `GOVERNMENT`, and a `BROKER_DEALER` when it contains `BROKER_DEALER`. An `AUDIT`, `REVIEW` or `ATTESTATION` has no matching specialization and therefore never earns the credit. The final `risk_score` is the signed sum of base plus load penalty minus specialization credit, and only then is it clamped into the 0-through-100 range — the clamp is applied last, after the subtraction, never to the intermediate terms.

Immediately after the score, the server decides `eqr_required` on the same engagement. It is true when any one of four conditions holds: the `risk_score` is at least 70 (a score of exactly 70 requires a review, a score of 69 does not, when no other condition applies); or the `engagement_type` is `ERISA_AUDIT`, `SINGLE_AUDIT` or `BROKER_DEALER`; or `is_public_interest` is true; or the firm's `risk_posture` is `ELEVATED` or `CRITICAL`. Once true, `eqr_required` is never set back to false — the only later write to it promotes false to true.

A newly accepted engagement begins with `workflow_state` `PLANNING` and `file_state` `OPEN`, `eqr_concurred_at` and `report_release_date` both null, and `reopened_count` at 0. Its partner's `open_engagement_count` increases by one. Engagement lists can be narrowed by `firm_id`, `engagement_partner_id`, `workflow_state`, `file_state`, `engagement_type` or `eqr_required`, which is how a caller reconstructs the reverse relationships this specification never exposes as declared keys.

### 3.3. The Engagement's Twin Lifecycles

Every engagement runs two state dimensions that advance independently of one another. The **performance dimension**, held in `workflow_state`, moves `PLANNING → FIELDWORK → EQR_PENDING → EQR_CLEARED → REPORT_RELEASED` and carries one revert cycle. The **documentation dimension**, held in `file_state`, moves `OPEN → ASSEMBLED → LOCKED` and carries one reopen cycle. Both dimensions are driven by supplying a `to_state`, and the two dimensions share only two coupling points: a locked file blocks writes to both dimensions, and a material inspection failure can reopen a locked file. Requesting a `to_state` that is a real state of the dimension but not reachable from the row's current state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409).

The engagement partner named on the engagement is the actor for every leg of both dimensions, and each leg verifies that `X-User-ID` equals the engagement's `engagement_partner_id`, refusing anyone else with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. Advancing `PLANNING → FIELDWORK` and `FIELDWORK → EQR_PENDING` requires only that the engagement sit in the state being left and that its `file_state` is not `LOCKED`; each increments the engagement's `version` by 1 and refreshes `updated_at`. Attempting either leg on a locked file yields `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_FILE_LOCKED` (HTTP 409) — the same code that guards documentation transitions and quality-reviewer assignment, making three operations in total that a locked file blocks.

Clearing quality review, the `EQR_PENDING → EQR_CLEARED` leg, is where `eqr_required` bites. When the engagement's `eqr_required` is true, the firm must be able to point at an engagement quality review of this engagement whose state is `CONCURRED`; if it cannot, the leg is refused with `ERR_EQR_NOT_CONCURRED` (HTTP 409). When `eqr_required` is false no quality review is required at all and the leg proceeds unconditionally. On success the engagement's `eqr_concurred_at` is set to the `concluded_at` instant of the concurring review — the earliest-created such review should more than one review of this engagement have reached `CONCURRED`, or left null when `eqr_required` was false — and the `version` increments.

There is a **revision cycle** on the performance dimension: an engagement sitting in `EQR_PENDING` is driven back to `FIELDWORK` by the system whenever the reviewer of its engagement quality review raises unresolved concerns. That revert clears `eqr_concurred_at` back to null and increments the engagement's `version`; the engagement may then be resubmitted to `EQR_PENDING` and reviewed again by the same reviewer.

Releasing the report, `EQR_CLEARED → REPORT_RELEASED`, stamps `report_release_date` with the date portion of the logical clock, recomputes the engagement partner's `open_engagement_count` (which decreases by one, since released engagements leave the open set), and increments the engagement's `version`. `REPORT_RELEASED` is the terminal state of the performance dimension.

Documentation assembly follows the report rather than leading it. `OPEN → ASSEMBLED` requires that the engagement's `workflow_state` already be `EQR_CLEARED` or `REPORT_RELEASED`; an engagement still short of that is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TRANSITION`, never with `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_NOT_RELEASED`. `ASSEMBLED → LOCKED` requires `workflow_state` to be exactly `REPORT_RELEASED` — again `ERR_INVALID_TRANSITION` when it is not, and that precondition is evaluated before the window below — and requires the logical clock's date to fall no more than 60 days after `report_release_date`; a request on the sixty-first day or later is refused with `ERR_DOCUMENTATION_WINDOW_CLOSED` (HTTP 409). Once `LOCKED`, both dimensions are frozen: any further performance or documentation transition on that engagement is refused with `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_FILE_LOCKED`.

The one thing that can unfreeze a locked file is a material failure found during internal inspection. That reopen — `LOCKED → OPEN` — is never requested by a caller; it is driven by the system when a finding on this engagement is classified `MATERIAL_FAILURE` with `affects_report_conclusion` true, and it increments the engagement's `reopened_count` by 1 alongside its `version`. Because the file reopened, a further link of that cascade fires against the firm's quality risks, as §3.7 sets out.

### 3.4. Engagement Quality Review: Assignment Gates and the Concurrence Negotiation

An engagement quality review is created by assigning a reviewer, an operation reserved to the managing partner or the quality control director. The payload names the `reviewer_partner_id` of the candidate reviewer, an `objectivity_attested` boolean, and the `expected_engagement_version` described in §2.6. A locked documentation file is the first business precondition examined, ahead of the window check itself: when the engagement's `file_state` is `LOCKED` the assignment is refused with `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_FILE_LOCKED`, even though such an engagement necessarily sits outside the review window as well. The engagement must then be inside its review window — `workflow_state` in `FIELDWORK` or `EQR_PENDING` — otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_NOT_IN_EQR_WINDOW` (HTTP 409), and this window check outranks every eligibility check that follows it. The engagement's `eqr_required` flag is not itself a gate on assignment: a reviewer may be assigned to an engagement whose `eqr_required` is false, and such a review is simply not consulted when the engagement later clears.

The candidate is then tested by a chain of eligibility clauses evaluated in one exact order, where the first clause to fail decides the error and no later clause is consulted:

1. The candidate's `status` must be `ACTIVE` — an `INACTIVE` partner is never eligible regardless of anything else, and is refused with `ERR_PARTNER_INACTIVE` (HTTP 409).
2. The candidate's `firm_id` must equal the engagement's `firm_id`, otherwise `ERR_PARTNER_FIRM_MISMATCH` (HTTP 409).
3. The candidate must not be the engagement's own `engagement_partner_id`, otherwise `ERR_EQR_SELF_REVIEW` (HTTP 403).
4. The candidate's `designations` must contain `EQ_REVIEWER` **and** their `cpe_hours_last_3y` must be at least 120; failing either half yields `ERR_CPE_REQUIREMENT_NOT_MET` (HTTP 409).
5. The engagement's `client_name` must not appear in the candidate's `independence_conflict_client_names`, otherwise `ERR_REVIEWER_INDEPENDENCE_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409).
6. `objectivity_attested` must be present and true, otherwise `ERR_OBJECTIVITY_NOT_ATTESTED` (HTTP 422).
7. Cooling-off is then measured. The server looks through the candidate's `prior_engagement_team_clients` for an entry whose `client_name` matches the engagement's `client_name`; when several entries name the same client it takes the one with the latest `exit_date`, breaking a tie between identical exit dates by taking the earlier array position. If such an entry exists, `cooling_off_days_at_assignment` is written as the exact number of calendar days from that `exit_date` to the engagement's `period_end`, and that separation must be at least 730 days — two full years counted in days, where exactly 730 days passes and 729 fails, refusing with `ERR_COOLING_OFF_NOT_MET` (HTTP 409). If no such entry exists the candidate never served on this client's team, and `cooling_off_days_at_assignment` is written as null.

Only once every clause has passed is the reviewer's **aggregate capacity** examined. Each open review a partner holds occupies a half-open time interval that starts at that review's `assigned_at` instant and ends at its `concluded_at` instant, with a null `concluded_at` meaning the interval is still open and extends indefinitely. The prospective assignment occupies an interval starting at the current logical-clock instant and likewise extending indefinitely. The server counts how many of the partner's reviews in state `ASSIGNED`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `CONCERNS_RAISED` have intervals that overlap the prospective one, where two intervals overlap when each one starts strictly before the other ends. There is no buffer of any kind, and touching endpoints do not overlap: an interval that ends exactly at the instant the new one begins is not counted. If three or more existing intervals overlap, the assignment is refused with `ERR_EQR_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409) — a partner may hold at most three concurrent open engagement quality reviews. Reviews that reached `CONCURRED` or `WITHDRAWN` have a non-null `concluded_at` and therefore close their interval.

Finally, an engagement may only have one live review at a time: if any engagement quality review of this engagement is already in `ASSIGNED`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `CONCERNS_RAISED`, the assignment is refused with `ERR_EQR_ALREADY_ASSIGNED` (HTTP 409).

A successful assignment creates the review in state `ASSIGNED`, stamps `assigned_at` from the logical clock, records the `objectivity_attested` value and the computed `cooling_off_days_at_assignment`, leaves `concluded_at`, `last_concern_summary` null and `concern_round_count` at 0, increases the reviewer's `open_eqr_count` by one, and increments the engagement's `version` by 1.

The reviewer then drives their own review by supplying a `to_state`, and whenever the actor holds the `EngagementQualityReviewer` role every leg verifies that `X-User-ID` equals the review's `reviewer_partner_id`, refusing anyone else with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`; a `FirmManagingPartner` acting on a review of their own firm is not subject to that comparison and may drive any leg. Beginning work advances `ASSIGNED → IN_PROGRESS`. From `IN_PROGRESS`, the reviewer may raise unresolved concerns, advancing `IN_PROGRESS → CONCERNS_RAISED` with a mandatory `concern_summary` of 1 to 500 characters; that leg increments `concern_round_count` by 1, copies the text into `last_concern_summary`, and cascades onto the engagement, driving it `EQR_PENDING → FIELDWORK` and clearing its `eqr_concurred_at`. The leg is not itself gated on the engagement's `workflow_state`, so a reviewer whose engagement is still sitting in `FIELDWORK` may raise concerns; the cascade link then does not fire and the engagement is left entirely untouched, `version` included. Once the engagement partner has resubmitted the engagement — so that its `workflow_state` reads `EQR_PENDING` again — the reviewer may return `CONCERNS_RAISED → IN_PROGRESS`; attempting that return while the engagement has not been resubmitted is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TRANSITION`. Rounds repeat as often as the negotiation requires, and `concern_round_count` records how many there were.

Concurrence, `IN_PROGRESS → CONCURRED`, is only reachable from `IN_PROGRESS`: a review sitting in `CONCERNS_RAISED` has unresolved concerns and its concurrence is refused with `ERR_UNRESOLVED_CONCERNS` (HTTP 409), which is the one check consulted ahead of the reachability table, while a review still in `ASSIGNED` or already in a terminal state is refused with plain `ERR_INVALID_TRANSITION`. Concurrence stamps `concluded_at` with the logical-clock instant — closing the reviewer's capacity interval — and recomputes the reviewer's `open_eqr_count`, which decreases by one. `CONCURRED` is terminal.

Withdrawal is the firm's escape hatch — driven by the assigned reviewer themselves or by the managing partner, the two firm-side roles this endpoint authorises — and reaches the other terminal state, `WITHDRAWN`, from either `ASSIGNED` or `CONCERNS_RAISED`. Both withdrawal legs behave identically: `concluded_at` is stamped from the logical clock, the reviewer's `open_eqr_count` is recomputed downward by one, and — this is the part callers most often miss — **the linked engagement's `version` is incremented by 1**. Withdrawal exists to free the engagement for a fresh reviewer, but any client that read the engagement before the withdrawal now holds a stale version and will be refused by the concurrency check of §2.6 when it tries to assign one.

### 3.5. Designing and Evaluating the System of Quality Management

The firm's system of quality management is expressed as a set of quality risks, each sitting inside one of seven SQM components: `GOVERNANCE_LEADERSHIP`, `ETHICAL_REQUIREMENTS`, `ACCEPTANCE_CONTINUANCE`, `ENGAGEMENT_PERFORMANCE`, `RESOURCES`, `INFORMATION_COMMUNICATION` or `MONITORING_REMEDIATION`. Recording a risk requires the `component`, a firm-assigned `quality_objective_code` of 1 to 40 characters, a `risk_statement` narrative of 1 to 500 characters, and the assessed `likelihood` and `impact`, each an integer from 1 through 5. A firm may not hold two risks sharing the same `component` and `quality_objective_code`; the duplicate is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_QUALITY_OBJECTIVE` (HTTP 409).

A quality risk's lifecycle runs `DRAFT → ASSESSED → RESPONDED → EVALUATED` and carries one reopen cycle back from `EVALUATED` to `RESPONDED`. `DRAFT` is nominal only: within the same create request the server computes `inherent_risk_score` as the `likelihood` multiplied by the `impact`, yielding a value from 1 through 25, and advances the risk to `ASSESSED` before the response is written, so a freshly created risk is never observable in `DRAFT`. `residual_risk_score` starts null, `evaluation_basis` starts `NONE`, `severity_band` starts `NONE`, and both `response_count` and `reopen_count` start at 0. That nominal advance is folded into creation rather than counted as a mutation of its own, so the created row's `version` reads 1.

Responses are designed against a risk one at a time by the quality control director, each naming its `response_type` — `PREVENTIVE` when it stops the risk from materialising, `DETECTIVE` when it catches it afterwards — and a `description` of 1 to 500 characters. A new response is created with `status` `DESIGNED`, `design_effectiveness` `NOT_ASSESSED`, `operating_effectiveness` `NOT_TESTED` and `tested_on` null. Creating a response recomputes the parent risk's `response_count`, which counts the risk's responses whose `status` is `DESIGNED` or `TESTED`, and the **first** response advances the risk `ASSESSED → RESPONDED` — the advance fires exactly when the risk is sitting in `ASSESSED` as the response is created. Designing a response is not otherwise gated on the risk's state: one may be added to a risk already in `RESPONDED` or in `EVALUATED`, and in the latter case the risk keeps its `EVALUATED` state, its existing `residual_risk_score` and `severity_band`, and the firm's posture is not recomputed. A response whose `status` has become `SUPERSEDED` is excluded from `response_count` and from every residual-risk computation.

Recording an effectiveness test on a designed response writes both conclusions at once — `design_effectiveness` and `operating_effectiveness`, each one of `EFFECTIVE`, `PARTIALLY_EFFECTIVE` or `INEFFECTIVE` — stamps `tested_on` with the logical-clock instant in the same three-fractional-digit ISO 8601 form as every other timestamp in the system, and moves the response's `status` from `DESIGNED` to `TESTED`; a response whose `status` is not `DESIGNED` — one that has already been tested, for instance — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409). The `tested_on` stamp matters beyond the audit trail: it is the ascending sort key of the chronological fold used by one of the two residual-risk variants.

Evaluating residual risk advances the risk `RESPONDED → EVALUATED`, and the requirement that the risk hold at least one counted response is carried entirely by that state precondition: a risk in any other state — `ASSESSED` with nothing yet designed against it, or already `EVALUATED` — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409), decided before either basis-specific check below. The request carries a single `basis` field selecting one of two genuinely different computations. Under **`DESIGN_BASIS`** the firm concludes on design alone and responses need not have been tested: each response whose `status` is `DESIGNED` or `TESTED` contributes a mitigation weight of 0.35 when its `design_effectiveness` is `EFFECTIVE`, 0.20 when `PARTIALLY_EFFECTIVE`, and nothing when `INEFFECTIVE` or still `NOT_ASSESSED`; those weights are added together, the total is capped at 0.80, and the residual score is the inherent score multiplied by one minus that capped mitigation factor, rounded half-up to two decimals once at the end. Under **`OPERATING_BASIS`** the firm concludes on tested operating effectiveness, and only responses whose `status` is `TESTED` participate: they are ordered by `tested_on` ascending, breaking ties on `created_at` ascending, and a running value that begins at the inherent score is multiplied, response by response in that order, by one minus that response's operating weight — 0.30 for `EFFECTIVE`, 0.10 for `PARTIALLY_EFFECTIVE`, nothing for `INEFFECTIVE` or `NOT_TESTED`. This multiplicative fold has no cap, and the result is rounded half-up to two decimals once at the end. Requesting `OPERATING_BASIS` on a risk with no response in `TESTED` status is refused with `ERR_NO_TESTED_RESPONSE` (HTTP 409).

The two variants deliberately disagree. A risk with an inherent score of 20 and two `EFFECTIVE` responses yields 6.00 under `DESIGN_BASIS` (mitigation 0.35 plus 0.35, applied once) but 9.80 under `OPERATING_BASIS` (two successive multiplications by 0.70). Whichever variant ran is recorded in `evaluation_basis`, and the resulting `residual_risk_score` is banded into `severity_band` by a top-down ladder: `CRITICAL` at 15.00 or above, otherwise `HIGH` at 8.00 or above, otherwise `MODERATE` at 4.00 or above, otherwise `LOW`. The ladder reads the stored two-decimal figure and not the unrounded intermediate, so a fold that lands on 7.995 bands as `HIGH` on the strength of the 8.00 it was rounded to; each boundary is inclusive at its lower end. Evaluation then recomputes the firm's `critical_risk_count` and re-runs the posture ladder of §3.1, and increments the risk's `version`.

The reopen cycle is never caller-driven. When a material inspection failure reopens a released engagement's documentation file, the firm's `EVALUATED` risk sitting in the same SQM component as that finding is driven `EVALUATED → RESPONDED`, its `residual_risk_score` reset to null, its `severity_band` back to `NONE` and its `evaluation_basis` back to `NONE`, with `reopen_count` incremented by 1. The reasoning is that a material failure on a released engagement invalidates the firm's monitoring conclusion for that component, so the conclusion must be re-reached rather than merely annotated. Because the risk left `EVALUATED`, the firm's `critical_risk_count` and `risk_posture` are recomputed in the same breath.

### 3.6. The Internal Inspection Cycle and Its Deterministic Selector

Monitoring is organised into annual cycles. Opening a cycle requires an `inspection_year` between 2000 and 2100 inclusive and a `target_engagement_count` of at least 1, being the number of engagements the cycle intends to inspect. A firm may hold only one cycle per year: a second attempt at the same `inspection_year` is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_INSPECTION_YEAR` (HTTP 409), so no two cycles of one firm ever share a year; the selector's own precondition is simply that its cycle still sits in `PLANNING`, and running it against a cycle that has already left that state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409). A new cycle opens in `PLANNING` with `engagements_selected_count`, `must_select_eligible_count` and `must_select_selected_count` all 0, `must_select_coverage_pct` at 100.00, `severity_index_basis` `NONE`, `deficiency_severity_index` null and `concluded_at` null. The cycle's states are `PLANNING`, `SELECTION_COMPLETE`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `FINDINGS_CLASSIFIED` and the terminal `CONCLUDED`. Of these, `SELECTION_COMPLETE` is reached only by running the selector and is never a requestable `to_state` on the cycle's transitions endpoint, which refuses it with `ERR_INVALID_TRANSITION`.

**The engagements to be inspected are chosen by the server, not by the caller.** The quality control director triggers the selector, which takes no input beyond the cycle itself and produces the cycle's selections in one deterministic pass, driving the cycle `PLANNING → SELECTION_COMPLETE`. Eligibility comes first: an engagement is window-eligible when it belongs to the cycle's firm, its `workflow_state` is exactly `REPORT_RELEASED`, and its `period_end` falls within the calendar year of `inspection_year` — from the first of January through the thirty-first of December, both endpoints inclusive. An engagement in any other `workflow_state` is excluded even when its `period_end` lands squarely in the window.

The eligible set is then drained by three blocks in a fixed order. **Block A, must-select by type**, takes every eligible engagement whose `engagement_type` is `ERISA_AUDIT`, `SINGLE_AUDIT` or `BROKER_DEALER`, ordered by `period_end` ascending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break. Each such selection records a `selection_reason` of `MUST_SELECT_ERISA_AUDIT`, `MUST_SELECT_SINGLE_AUDIT` or `MUST_SELECT_BROKER_DEALER` to match its type, and `is_must_select` true. Block A takes precedence over `target_engagement_count` — a cycle whose must-select population exceeds its target inspects them all anyway. **Block B, partner coverage**, then looks at the partners of the eligible engagements that Block A did not take, and keeps those whose `last_inspected_year` is null or is at least three years behind `inspection_year` (that is, no later than the inspection year minus three). For each such partner the selector picks that partner's highest-`risk_score` engagement among the ones Block A left behind, breaking ties by `period_end` ascending and then `created_at` ascending; the resulting picks are ordered among themselves by `risk_score` descending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break, and each records a `selection_reason` of `MUST_SELECT_PARTNER_COVERAGE` with `is_must_select` true. **Block C, risk-based fill**, orders whatever remains by `risk_score` descending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break and takes as many rows as are needed to reach `target_engagement_count` once Blocks A and B are counted — and takes none at all when those two blocks have already met or exceeded the target. Block C selections record `selection_reason` `RISK_BASED` and `is_must_select` false. `selected_rank` is then assigned as 1, 2, 3 and so on across the concatenation of Block A, then Block B, then Block C, in exactly that order. Because every ordering ends in `created_at` ascending, no tie is ever unresolved, and the resource `id` is never consulted as a ranking or tie-break key.

Running the selector also sets `engagements_selected_count` to the number of selections created, computes the coverage figures described below, and sets `last_inspected_year` to the cycle's `inspection_year` for every engagement partner of a selected engagement — a partner whose `last_inspected_year` already reads that year is left entirely untouched, `version` and `updated_at` alike, and no other partner field is refreshed by the selector. The selector answers with the updated cycle itself in `data`, never with the selections it created, and it advances the cycle's `version` by exactly 1 even though the one pass writes the state, the selected count and all three coverage figures. Each selection begins with `finding_count` at 0 and `weighted_points` at 0.00. When the eligible set is empty the selector creates no selections at all: `engagements_selected_count` stays 0, `must_select_eligible_count` is 0, and `must_select_coverage_pct` reads exactly 100.00. Selections may be listed filtered by `inspection_id`, by `engagement_id` or by `is_must_select`, and when a non-empty `inspection_id` is supplied the list is ordered by `selected_rank` ascending, with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break, rather than by creation time. This is the only collection in the system whose default order a filter changes, and it is the presence of the parameter that switches the order rather than the parameter matching anything: an `inspection_id` naming no cycle still selects the rank ordering over an empty page, while an `inspection_id` supplied as an empty string narrows nothing and leaves the ordering at `created_at` ascending.

Coverage is measured as a percentage and is the gate on concluding a cycle. `must_select_eligible_count` counts the window-eligible engagements whose `engagement_type` is one of the three must-select types. `must_select_selected_count` counts this cycle's selections whose `is_must_select` is true **excluding** those whose `selection_reason` is `MUST_SELECT_PARTNER_COVERAGE` — partner-coverage picks are deliberately kept out of the numerator, since they evidence partner rotation rather than must-select category coverage. When `must_select_eligible_count` is 0, `must_select_coverage_pct` is exactly 100.00. Otherwise it is 100 multiplied by `must_select_selected_count`, divided by `must_select_eligible_count`, rounded half-up to two decimals — three eligible with two selected reads 66.67. The three stored figures are written once, when the selector runs, and are never refreshed afterwards; the coverage gate on each conclusion leg below re-derives the percentage from the firm's engagement population as it stands at the moment of that request rather than reading the stored `must_select_coverage_pct`, so the stored figure and the gate can disagree once further engagements of the firm reach `REPORT_RELEASED` inside the inspection year.

Advancing `SELECTION_COMPLETE → IN_PROGRESS` opens the cycle for findings and requires `engagements_selected_count` to be at least 1; a cycle whose selector produced nothing is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_ENGAGEMENTS` (HTTP 409). Such an empty cycle has its own exit instead: from `SELECTION_COMPLETE` it may go straight to `CONCLUDED` provided `engagements_selected_count` is 0 and `must_select_coverage_pct` is 100.00 (which holds by definition when nothing was eligible), and that leg sets `deficiency_severity_index` to 0.00 with `severity_index_basis` `ENGAGEMENT_WEIGHTED` and stamps `concluded_at` from the logical clock. On that leg the selected-count condition is judged first — a cycle that did select engagements is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TRANSITION` — and the coverage condition only afterwards, its failure giving `ERR_MUST_SELECT_COVERAGE_INCOMPLETE`; a `severity_index_basis` supplied in the body of this leg is ignored, since the leg pins the basis itself. A firm with no window-eligible engagements therefore closes its monitoring cycle without inspecting anything.

Advancing `IN_PROGRESS → FINDINGS_CLASSIFIED` requires a `severity_index_basis` of either `ENGAGEMENT_WEIGHTED` or `FIRM_PERVASIVE` in the request and requires that no finding on this cycle's selections is still `UNCLASSIFIED`; otherwise the leg is refused with `ERR_UNCLASSIFIED_FINDINGS_PRESENT` (HTTP 409). It records the chosen basis and computes the severity index described in §3.7. Under `ENGAGEMENT_WEIGHTED` this leg also writes the per-selection figure it derived into each of the cycle's selections' `weighted_points`, advancing only those selections whose stored value actually changes; under `FIRM_PERVASIVE` no selection is touched at all, because that variant derives no per-selection quantity. The final leg, `FINDINGS_CLASSIFIED → CONCLUDED`, imposes two conditions: `must_select_coverage_pct` must read exactly 100.00, otherwise `ERR_MUST_SELECT_COVERAGE_INCOMPLETE` (HTTP 409); and every finding of the cycle classified `SIGNIFICANT_DEFICIENCY` or `MATERIAL_FAILURE` must have reached `REMEDIATION_ASSIGNED`, `REMEDIATED` or `VERIFIED`, otherwise `ERR_OPEN_SIGNIFICANT_FINDINGS` (HTTP 409). Concluding stamps `concluded_at` from the logical clock, and — a cross-aggregate dependency that surprises firms the first time — it is what unblocks the firm's peer review from entering fieldwork.

### 3.7. Findings, Classification and the Firm-Wide Material-Failure Cascade

A finding is raised against an inspected engagement — that is, against one of the cycle's selections, identified in the path by its `selection_id` — by the quality control director. It carries a `title` of 1 to 200 characters describing the matter observed, the `sqm_component` it maps to (drawn from the same seven components as the firm's quality risks), and an optional `prior_cycle_recurrence` flag, defaulting to false, marking a matter that was already raised in an earlier cycle. Three conditions gate the raising: the owning cycle must be in `IN_PROGRESS`, otherwise `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_IN_PROGRESS` (HTTP 409); the engagement behind the selection must have reached `workflow_state` `REPORT_RELEASED`, otherwise `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_NOT_RELEASED` (HTTP 409); and the acting director must not be that engagement's own engagement partner, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). A new finding is created in state `IDENTIFIED` with `classification` `UNCLASSIFIED`, `pervasiveness` `UNDETERMINED`, `affects_report_conclusion` false, `class_points` 0, `verified_at` null and `reopen_count` 0. Creating it does not touch its selection at all: `finding_count` is a derived count of the selection's non-withdrawn findings and is refreshed only when a finding of that selection is classified, so a selection whose findings are every one of them still `UNCLASSIFIED` goes on reading `finding_count` 0 and `weighted_points` 0.00. Findings can be listed filtered by `selection_id`, `state`, `classification` or `sqm_component`.

Classification is a single write carrying three values: the `classification` itself (which must be one of `OBSERVATION`, `DEFICIENCY`, `SIGNIFICANT_DEFICIENCY` or `MATERIAL_FAILURE` — `UNCLASSIFIED` is the state a finding starts in and never a value a caller may write, so supplying it gives `ERR_INVALID_ENUM_VALUE`), the `pervasiveness` (which must be either `ISOLATED` or `SYSTEMIC` — `UNDETERMINED` is not a classifiable value), and `affects_report_conclusion`. All three are required on every classification request whatever the `classification` names, and none of them has a default: a request that omits any of the three is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION_FAILED`. The finding must still be in `IDENTIFIED` and its cycle must still be `IN_PROGRESS`. A `classification` of `MATERIAL_FAILURE` must be accompanied by `affects_report_conclusion` true; submitting it with false is refused with `ERR_MATERIAL_FAILURE_REQUIRES_REPORT_IMPACT` (HTTP 422). Those three conditions are judged in a fixed order: the finding's own state first (`ERR_INVALID_TRANSITION`), then the cycle's state (`ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_IN_PROGRESS`), and the material-failure report-impact rule last of all — so a `MATERIAL_FAILURE` submitted with `affects_report_conclusion` false against a finding that has already been classified surfaces `ERR_INVALID_TRANSITION` rather than that 422.

Classifying a finding `OBSERVATION` closes it rather than opening a remediation chain: `class_points` is set to 0 and the finding goes straight `IDENTIFIED → WITHDRAWN`, which is its terminal state; the `pervasiveness` and `affects_report_conclusion` the caller submitted are nevertheless recorded on the withdrawn row. Its selection's `finding_count` is recomputed and drops the withdrawn row, since withdrawn findings are excluded from that count, and the firm's `open_deficiency_count` is left unchanged. No root cause analysis is ever performed on an observation.

Classifying a finding `DEFICIENCY`, `SIGNIFICANT_DEFICIENCY` or `MATERIAL_FAILURE` advances it `IDENTIFIED → RCA_PENDING` and fires a cascade whose links each depend on the link before it:

- **Link 1** writes `classification`, `pervasiveness`, `affects_report_conclusion` and `class_points` — 3 points for a `DEFICIENCY`, 8 for a `SIGNIFICANT_DEFICIENCY`, 20 for a `MATERIAL_FAILURE`, and 0 for `OBSERVATION` or `UNCLASSIFIED` — then recomputes the selection's `weighted_points` and `finding_count`, increments the firm's `open_deficiency_count` by 1 and, when the classification is `MATERIAL_FAILURE`, increments the firm's `open_material_failure_count` by 1 as well.
- **Link 2** fires only because link 1 recorded `MATERIAL_FAILURE`: the selection's engagement has its `file_state` driven `LOCKED → OPEN` and its `reopened_count` incremented by 1. An engagement whose file was not locked has nothing to reopen.
- **Link 3** fires because link 2 reopened a file: the firm's quality risk whose `component` equals the finding's `sqm_component` and whose state is `EVALUATED` is driven `EVALUATED → RESPONDED`, clearing its `residual_risk_score` back to null, its `severity_band` back to `NONE` and its `evaluation_basis` back to `NONE`, and incrementing its `reopen_count`. Should the firm hold more than one `EVALUATED` risk in that component, only the earliest-created of them is driven back and the rest are untouched; a firm holding no `EVALUATED` risk in that component has nothing to drive back, so this link does not fire.
- **Link 4** fires because link 3 took a risk out of `EVALUATED`: the firm's `critical_risk_count` is recomputed and the posture ladder is re-run. With `open_material_failure_count` now at least 1, the posture becomes `CRITICAL`.
- **Link 5** fires because link 4 produced a `CRITICAL` posture: every engagement of the firm whose `workflow_state` is `PLANNING` or `FIELDWORK` and whose `eqr_required` is still false has `eqr_required` promoted to true, and each such engagement's `version` is incremented by 1. Engagements already past fieldwork, and engagements already requiring a review, are untouched.

A `DEFICIENCY` or `SIGNIFICANT_DEFICIENCY` classification therefore stops after link 1 (plus the counter recomputation that link 1 itself performs), while `MATERIAL_FAILURE` propagates all the way out to client acceptance being frozen and to any client holding a pre-cascade view of an engagement being unable to assign a quality reviewer to it. Because link 4 is the only place in this cascade where the posture ladder is re-run, a `MATERIAL_FAILURE` whose chain stops early — the selection's engagement was not `LOCKED`, or the firm holds no `EVALUATED` risk in the finding's `sqm_component` — leaves `risk_posture` reading exactly what it read before, even though link 1 has already raised `open_material_failure_count` to at least 1, and link 5's promotion of `eqr_required` does not happen either.

The cycle's `deficiency_severity_index` is computed when the cycle reaches `FINDINGS_CLASSIFIED`, under whichever `severity_index_basis` the caller requested, over all findings of the cycle's selections whose state is not `WITHDRAWN` — so auto-withdrawn observations never contribute, and findings belonging to other cycles of the same firm are excluded. Under **`ENGAGEMENT_WEIGHTED`**, which measures how bad the average inspected engagement was, each selection's own points are summed from the classification points of its findings, multiplied by 1.5 when that selection's `is_must_select` is true and by 1 otherwise, and **rounded to two decimals per selection first**; those already-rounded `weighted_points` are then summed across the cycle's selections, divided by `engagements_selected_count`, and rounded half-up to two decimals once. Under **`FIRM_PERVASIVE`**, which measures how concentrated the failures are in a single SQM component, the classification points are instead grouped by `sqm_component`, the largest single component total is taken (zero when the cycle has no contributing findings), 5 points are added for each finding whose `pervasiveness` is `SYSTEMIC`, and that sum is divided by the number of distinct components represented among the findings — using 1 as the divisor when no component is represented — then rounded half-up to two decimals. The pervasive variant ignores which engagement a finding came from and ignores `is_must_select` entirely. The two variants diverge by construction: a cycle with one must-select selection carrying a single `SYSTEMIC` `MATERIAL_FAILURE` and one ordinary selection carrying a single `DEFICIENCY` in a different component reads 16.50 under `ENGAGEMENT_WEIGHTED` and 12.50 under `FIRM_PERVASIVE`.

### 3.8. Root Cause, Remediation, Verification and the Reopen Loop

A finding sitting in `RCA_PENDING` needs a documented root cause before anything can be planned against it. The analysis names its `method` — `FIVE_WHYS`, `FISHBONE` or `PARETO` — a `root_cause_category` drawn from `SUPERVISION_REVIEW`, `TECHNICAL_COMPETENCE`, `RESOURCE_ALLOCATION`, `TONE_AT_THE_TOP`, `METHODOLOGY_TOOLING` or `CLIENT_ACCEPTANCE`, a `contributing_factor_count` from 1 through 10, and a `narrative` of 1 to 1000 characters. It is created in state `DRAFTED` with `depth_score` 0.00, `recurrence_flag` false and `validated_at` null, and the finding stays in `RCA_PENDING` until the analysis is validated. A finding in any other state, `REOPENED` included, is refused an analysis with `ERR_INVALID_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409), and while the finding waits in `RCA_PENDING` more than one analysis may be drafted against it. Analyses may be listed filtered by `finding_id`, `method`, `root_cause_category` or `state`.

Validation is a separate adjudication carrying a single `outcome`. A `REJECTED` outcome moves the analysis `DRAFTED → REJECTED` and leaves the finding where it is; a rejected analysis never establishes recurrence for anything. A `VALIDATED` outcome moves it `DRAFTED → VALIDATED`, stamps `validated_at` from the logical clock, computes the two derived values below, and drives the finding `RCA_PENDING → RCA_COMPLETE`. Both outcomes require the analysis to be in `DRAFTED`, and the analysis being validated must belong to the finding it would advance: a mismatch between the analysis's `finding_id` and the finding in question is refused with `ERR_RCA_FINDING_MISMATCH` (HTTP 409).

`recurrence_flag` is set true when some other validated analysis of the **same firm** shares this analysis's `root_cause_category` and belongs to a cycle in either of the two inspection years immediately preceding this finding's own cycle — the firm and the year being resolved through the analysis's finding, that finding's selection, and that selection's cycle. Analyses still in `DRAFTED`, and analyses that were `REJECTED`, never establish recurrence. `depth_score` is then twice the `contributing_factor_count`, plus a method bonus of 2 for `FIVE_WHYS`, 3 for `FISHBONE` or 1 for `PARETO`, plus a further 2 points when `recurrence_flag` came out true, with the total capped at 10 and stored with two decimals — so a depth of eight reads 8.00.

A remediation plan may then be created against the finding, citing the `root_cause_analysis_id` of the validated analysis, the `owner_partner_id` of the partner accountable for execution, a `target_completion_date` in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, an `action_count` from 1 through 20, an `action_specificity_score` from 0.00 through 10.00 with two decimals as rated by the assessor, and the `expected_finding_version` of §2.6. Citing an analysis whose state is not `VALIDATED` is refused with `ERR_RCA_NOT_VALIDATED` (HTTP 409), citing one that belongs to a different finding with `ERR_RCA_FINDING_MISMATCH`, and naming an owner from another firm with `ERR_PARTNER_FIRM_MISMATCH`. The cited analysis's own state is judged **before** the finding's state on this create, so a create that both cites a still-`DRAFTED` analysis and addresses a finding that has not yet left `RCA_PENDING` surfaces `ERR_RCA_NOT_VALIDATED`; a finding that is neither in `RCA_COMPLETE` nor in `REOPENED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TRANSITION` once the cited analysis has been found `VALIDATED`. The two referential checks come after the finding's state and in the order owner-firm then analysis-ownership, so a create that both names an owner from another firm and cites an analysis belonging to a different finding surfaces `ERR_PARTNER_FIRM_MISMATCH`. The plan is created in state `DRAFTED` with `completed_action_count` 0, `raw_completeness` and `acceptance_score` null, `completeness_tier` `NONE`, `return_count` 0 and `accepted_at` null, and it drives the finding `RCA_COMPLETE → REMEDIATION_ASSIGNED` while incrementing the finding's `version`.

The plan's own lifecycle is `DRAFTED → SUBMITTED`, a `SUBMITTED ↔ RETURNED` rework cycle, `SUBMITTED → ACCEPTED`, `ACCEPTED → COMPLETED`, and the terminal `CLOSED`. Submission — from `DRAFTED` or back from `RETURNED` — is the owner's act: `X-User-ID` must equal the plan's `owner_partner_id`, otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`, and it may carry a `completed_action_count` that must be at least 0 and no greater than `action_count`. That field is optional: omitting it leaves the count at whatever the plan already holds and re-scores against that value. Its upper bound is a comparison against the plan's own `action_count` and so cannot be judged until the plan has been resolved — it is checked after the leg has been found legal, with a value above `action_count` giving `ERR_VALIDATION_FAILED`, which means a submission that is both out of range and an illegal leg answers `ERR_INVALID_TRANSITION`. Every submission re-scores the plan in four pinned steps. **Step one** derives `raw_completeness`, rounded half-up to two decimals, as 0.40 of the cited analysis's `depth_score`, plus 0.35 of the plan's `action_specificity_score`, plus 0.25 of an action-progress term equal to ten multiplied by `completed_action_count` and divided by `action_count`. **Step two** sets `completeness_tier` from `raw_completeness` alone: `CRITICAL` below 5.00, `STANDARD` from 5.00 up to but not including 8.00, and `STRONG` at 8.00 or above. **Step three** measures lateness as the number of days from `target_completion_date` to the logical clock's date at the moment of submission, treated as zero when the clock has not yet passed the target, and picks the daily penalty rate from the tier: 0.15 per late day for a `CRITICAL` tier, 0.10 for `STANDARD`, 0.05 for `STRONG`. **Step four** subtracts the penalty rate multiplied by the late-day count from `raw_completeness`, floors the result at 0.00, and rounds half-up to two decimals into `acceptance_score`. The evaluation order is pinned: the tier is derived from `raw_completeness` and never re-derived from `acceptance_score`, and exactly one penalty pass runs per submission — the circular reading in which a penalty lowers the score, the lower score implies a harsher tier, and the harsher tier implies a bigger penalty is explicitly rejected. Because each round re-derives the tier from `raw_completeness` only, a plan that gains completed actions between rounds can legitimately change tier.

Returning a plan for rework, `SUBMITTED → RETURNED`, is the quality control director's act and is barred to the plan's own owner with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; it increments `return_count` by 1 and retains the existing `acceptance_score` and `completeness_tier`, which will be recomputed on the next submission. Acceptance, `SUBMITTED → ACCEPTED`, is likewise barred to the owner with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` and requires `acceptance_score` to be at least 7.00 — exactly 7.00 is accepted, 6.99 is refused with `ERR_ACCEPTANCE_SCORE_BELOW_THRESHOLD` (HTTP 409). Acceptance stamps `accepted_at` from the logical clock and cascades onto the finding, driving it `REMEDIATION_ASSIGNED → REMEDIATED`; note that a `REMEDIATED` finding is still an open finding, so the firm's `open_deficiency_count` does not move. Reporting the plan complete, `ACCEPTED → COMPLETED`, is back in the owner's hands and requires `completed_action_count` to equal `action_count` exactly; anything less is refused with `ERR_REMEDIATION_ACTIONS_INCOMPLETE` (HTTP 409). That request may itself supply a `completed_action_count` — again refused with `ERR_VALIDATION_FAILED` when it exceeds `action_count` — which is stored on the plan before the equality gate is judged, so an owner may record the last actions and report completion in a single call; no re-scoring runs on this leg, and `raw_completeness`, `completeness_tier` and `acceptance_score` keep the values the last submission left. Plans may be listed filtered by `finding_id`, `root_cause_analysis_id`, `owner_partner_id`, `state` or `completeness_tier`.

Verification closes the loop on a finding and belongs to someone other than the plan's owner. It requires the finding to be in `REMEDIATED`; a finding already in `VERIFIED` is refused with `ERR_FINDING_ALREADY_VERIFIED` (HTTP 409). The plan it consults is the most recently created plan of the finding whose state is `ACCEPTED` or `COMPLETED`; plans in any other state, `CLOSED` among them, are passed over entirely, and a finding with no such plan at all is refused with `ERR_REMEDIATION_ACTIONS_INCOMPLETE`. Of the two conditions on that plan the owner comparison is made **first**: `X-User-ID` must differ from the plan's `owner_partner_id`, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, and only then must the plan have reached `COMPLETED` — a plan still merely `ACCEPTED` yields `ERR_REMEDIATION_ACTIONS_INCOMPLETE` for every caller except that plan's own owner, who sees `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` instead. `REMEDIATED → VERIFIED` stamps `verified_at` from the logical clock, decrements the firm's `open_deficiency_count` by 1 and, for a `MATERIAL_FAILURE`, decrements `open_material_failure_count` by 1 as well, then re-runs the posture ladder.

The loop can be run again. `VERIFIED → REOPENED` clears `verified_at` back to null, increments the finding's `reopen_count` by 1, and **compensates the firm's counters exactly**: `open_deficiency_count` is incremented by 1 and, for a `MATERIAL_FAILURE`, `open_material_failure_count` is incremented by 1, restoring both to the values they held immediately before verification, after which the posture is recomputed. A reopened finding returns to `REMEDIATION_ASSIGNED` not through its own transition endpoint but by creating a superseding remediation plan against it, which requires a validated analysis exactly as the first plan did; that create drives every existing plan of the finding whose state is `ACCEPTED` or `COMPLETED` to `CLOSED` — incrementing each closed plan's `version` — and creates the new plan in `DRAFTED`. A `CLOSED` plan no longer satisfies the verification precondition, so the finding must earn its verification afresh. Because a fresh analysis can only be drafted against a finding sitting in `RCA_PENDING`, the superseding plan necessarily cites an analysis of that same finding that was already validated before the reopen.

### 3.9. The External Peer Review Cycle

**The roster.** The committee maintains a roster of external reviewers, each identified by a `reviewer_firm_name` of 1 to 200 characters unique across the roster, a `license_state` of exactly two uppercase letters, and an `accepts_out_of_state` flag defaulting to false. A reviewer's `qualified_categories` array holds the must-select categories they may cover, its elements drawn from `ERISA_AUDIT`, `SINGLE_AUDIT`, `BROKER_DEALER`, `GOVERNMENTAL` and `PUBLIC_INTEREST`; `independence_conflict_firm_names` lists the firm legal names they are not independent of; `max_concurrent_reviews` caps how many reviews they may hold in a pre-report state and defaults to 2, accepting values from 1 through 10; `experience_hours` records lifetime peer review hours, defaulting to 0 and never negative; and `status` is either `ACTIVE` (the default) or `SUSPENDED`. Two counters are the system's own: `active_review_count`, the number of the reviewer's peer reviews in `PLANNING`, `SCOPE_SET`, `FIELDWORK` or `RATING_DRAFTED`, and `committed_hours_balance`, the conserved sum of `hours_committed` over every committed selection across all of the reviewer's reviews. Both start at 0. The committee may later revise `accepts_out_of_state`, `qualified_categories`, `independence_conflict_firm_names`, `max_concurrent_reviews`, `experience_hours` and `status`; such a revision never retroactively unassigns a review already in flight. A revision reads only those six fields: any other member of the body, `reviewer_firm_name` and `license_state` included, is ignored rather than refused, and the reviewer's `version` advances by exactly 1 even when the body changes nothing at all. A create whose `reviewer_firm_name` duplicates one already on the roster is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION_FAILED` (HTTP 422). Reviewers may be listed filtered by `status`, `license_state` or `accepts_out_of_state`.

**Scheduling.** The committee schedules a review against a firm with a `review_type` of `SYSTEM_REVIEW` or `ENGAGEMENT_REVIEW`, a `year_end` in `YYYY-MM-DD` form naming the period the review covers, and an on-site window given as `scheduled_window_start` and `scheduled_window_end`, both `YYYY-MM-DD`. The window is half-open: the start day is inside it and the end day is not. An end that is on or before the start is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION_FAILED`. The review is created in `SCHEDULED` with `assigned_reviewer_id` null, `rating` `NONE`, `is_follow_up` false, `prior_peer_review_id` null, `must_select_categories` empty, `total_scope_hours` and `unallocated_hours` both null, `scope_limitation_count` 0 and `report_issued_at` null. Follow-up reviews are never scheduled through this route; they are synthesized by the corrective-action rejection cascade described at the end of this subsection, and are the only reviews whose `prior_peer_review_id` is populated and whose `is_follow_up` reads true. Reviews may be listed filtered by `firm_id`, by `prior_peer_review_id`, by `assigned_reviewer_id`, by `state`, by `rating` or by `is_follow_up`. Its lifecycle states are `SCHEDULED`, `PLANNING`, `SCOPE_SET`, `FIELDWORK`, `RATING_DRAFTED`, `REPORT_ISSUED`, `CORRECTIVE_ACTION_REQUIRED`, `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED`, and the two terminal states `ACCEPTED` and `FOLLOW_UP_REQUIRED`. Of these only `FIELDWORK`, `RATING_DRAFTED`, `REPORT_ISSUED` and `ACCEPTED` are ever requestable as a `to_state` on the review's transitions endpoint: `PLANNING` and `SCOPE_SET` belong to the reviewer-assignment and scope endpoints, and `CORRECTIVE_ACTION_REQUIRED`, `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED` and `FOLLOW_UP_REQUIRED` are only ever entered by cascade, so naming any of those five — or `SCHEDULED`, which is only ever the creation state — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TRANSITION`.

**Choosing a reviewer.** Assignment advances the review `SCHEDULED → PLANNING` and is the committee's act; a review that has already left `SCHEDULED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409). Before either path below is taken, `active_review_count` and `committed_hours_balance` are re-derived for every reviewer on the roster, so a counter that had drifted is corrected within this same request. That sweep is a background recomputation in the sense of §2.9: a reviewer whose two counters both come back reading what they already held is left entirely untouched, `version` and `updated_at` alike, while one whose counter actually moves advances by 1. The correction is not undone when the assignment then fails, so a reviewer corrected during a request that ends in `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_REVIEWER` or in one of the seven named-candidate refusals keeps the corrected counters. The same re-derivation, applied to a single reviewer, is what every later commit, withdrawal and close-out of a review performs, and it follows the same version rule. The request may supply a `reviewer_id`, in which case that named candidate is validated clause by clause; when it is omitted, the server runs a deterministic selector over the whole roster and silently filters out any candidate failing any clause. The categories the reviewer must be able to cover — call them the firm's must-select categories — are derived exactly as they are at scope determination, described below.

A candidate is eligible only when every one of the following holds: their `status` is `ACTIVE`; their `license_state` equals the firm's `state_code` or their `accepts_out_of_state` is true; every one of the firm's must-select categories appears in their `qualified_categories` (a firm with no must-select categories imposes no constraint here); the firm's `legal_name` does not appear in their `independence_conflict_firm_names`; their `id` is not among the firm's `prior_reviewer_ids`; their `active_review_count` is strictly below their `max_concurrent_reviews`; and they hold no other peer review in `SCHEDULED`, `PLANNING`, `SCOPE_SET`, `FIELDWORK` or `RATING_DRAFTED` whose on-site window collides with this one. **Window collision is pairwise and buffered**: an existing review's window is treated as extended by 5 travel days beyond its `scheduled_window_end`, and the two windows collide when this review's `scheduled_window_start` falls strictly before that extended end **and** the existing review's `scheduled_window_start` falls strictly before this review's `scheduled_window_end`. A new window starting exactly on an existing window's end date therefore collides, while one starting exactly 5 days after that end date does not. This is deliberately unlike the internal engagement-quality-review capacity rule of §3.4, which counts overlapping intervals in aggregate with no buffer at all.

Among the eligible candidates the selector ranks by three keys in order: first, the largest count of categories shared between the candidate's `qualified_categories` and the firm's must-select categories, largest first; then the smallest `active_review_count`; then the largest `experience_hours`. Any remaining tie is broken by `created_at` ascending — never by `id`. The winner's `id` is written to `assigned_reviewer_id`, their `active_review_count` increases by one, and the firm's `unassigned_review_flag` is cleared to false — a firm whose flag already read false is left entirely untouched by the assignment, `version` and `updated_at` alike. If no candidate survives the filter, the review stays in `SCHEDULED`, the firm's `unassigned_review_flag` is set true, and the request is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_REVIEWER` (HTTP 409).

When a `reviewer_id` **is** named, the same clauses are applied individually and in ascending order of the global evaluation ladder of §2.5, so each rejection surfaces its own code rather than collapsing into an empty pool: independence first (`ERR_REVIEWER_INDEPENDENCE_CONFLICT`, raised when the firm's `legal_name` sits on the reviewer's `independence_conflict_firm_names`), then rotation (`ERR_REVIEWER_ROTATION_REQUIRED`, raised when the named reviewer is one of the firm's `prior_reviewer_ids` and must rotate off), then the buffered window collision (`ERR_REVIEWER_WINDOW_CONFLICT`), then roster status (`ERR_REVIEWER_SUSPENDED`, raised when `status` is not `ACTIVE`), then licensing reach (`ERR_REVIEWER_STATE_NOT_LICENSED`), then capacity (`ERR_REVIEWER_AT_CAPACITY`, raised when `active_review_count` has reached `max_concurrent_reviews`), and finally category qualification (`ERR_MUST_SELECT_CATEGORY_NOT_QUALIFIED`). All seven are HTTP 409. None of the seven touches the firm's `unassigned_review_flag`: that flag is raised only when the unnamed selector's own pool comes up empty.

**Determining scope.** Scope determination advances `PLANNING → SCOPE_SET`, takes no request body, and may be performed by the assigned reviewer or by the committee; when a `PeerReviewer` performs it, `X-User-ID` must equal `assigned_reviewer_id`, otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. A review not in `PLANNING` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409), that ownership comparison being made ahead of the state check. It first computes the firm's `must_select_categories` from the firm's engagements whose `workflow_state` is `REPORT_RELEASED` and whose `period_end` is on or before the review's `year_end`. Each category is emitted at most once, and the array is emitted in this fixed declaration order regardless of the order the engagements were found in: `ERISA_AUDIT` when at least one such engagement has that `engagement_type`; `SINGLE_AUDIT` likewise; `BROKER_DEALER` likewise; `GOVERNMENTAL` when at least one such engagement has `engagement_type` `AUDIT` and its engagement partner's `industry_specializations` contains `GOVERNMENT`; and `PUBLIC_INTEREST` when at least one such engagement has `is_public_interest` true. The assigned reviewer's `qualified_categories` are then re-checked against that array, and a reviewer who no longer covers every category is refused with `ERR_MUST_SELECT_CATEGORY_NOT_QUALIFIED`.

Scope hours are then tiered on firm size and uplifted by posture. The base tier is 16 hours when the firm's `aa_hours_last_year` is 500 or fewer, 24 hours when it is 2000 or fewer, 40 hours when it is 10000 or fewer, and 64 hours above that — the upper bound of each tier is inclusive, so 2000 annual hours draws the 24-hour tier while 2001 draws the 40-hour tier. Each must-select category adds hours of its own: 8 for `ERISA_AUDIT`, 10 for `SINGLE_AUDIT`, 8 for `BROKER_DEALER`, 6 for `GOVERNMENTAL` and 12 for `PUBLIC_INTEREST`. An uplift of one quarter is applied when the firm's `risk_posture` is `CRITICAL` and of one tenth when it is `ELEVATED`, with no uplift for `STABLE`. **The rounding order is pinned**: the base component is the base tier multiplied by one plus the uplift, rounded half-up to two decimals; the category component is the sum of the per-category hours multiplied by one plus the same uplift, rounded half-up to two decimals separately; the two rounded components are then added and the sum raised to the next whole hour by taking its ceiling, giving `total_scope_hours` as a whole number — a sum that is already whole is left exactly as it is rather than nudged up, so 40.00 gives 40 and 40.01 gives 41. Adding the raw base and category hours first and applying the uplift once at the end is not an acceptable substitute. `unallocated_hours` is initialised equal to `total_scope_hours`, which opens the hours-conservation invariant.

**Entering fieldwork.** `SCOPE_SET → FIELDWORK` is the assigned reviewer's act and carries two cross-aggregate conditions. The firm's internal inspection cycle with the highest `inspection_year` must exist and must have reached `CONCLUDED`; a firm with no cycle at all, or whose latest cycle is unfinished, blocks fieldwork with `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_CONCLUDED` (HTTP 409). And every category in the review's `must_select_categories` must already be the `must_select_category` of at least one committed selection of this review, otherwise `ERR_MUST_SELECT_CATEGORY_NOT_COVERED` (HTTP 409) — which is why selections are committed while the review still sits in `SCOPE_SET`.

**Selecting engagements and committing hours.** The reviewer selects an engagement into scope by naming its `engagement_id`, the `hours_committed` to it (an integer from 1 through 200), an optional `is_scope_limitation` flag defaulting to false, and the `expected_peer_review_version` of §2.6. The review must be in `SCOPE_SET` or `FIELDWORK`; a review that has already been rated or moved beyond fieldwork refuses both commitment and withdrawal with `ERR_PEER_REVIEW_ALREADY_RATED` (HTTP 409); a review that has not yet reached `SCOPE_SET` — one still in `SCHEDULED` or `PLANNING` — draws that same code, which is the single answer for every state outside the two. The engagement's `firm_id` must equal the review's `firm_id`, otherwise `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_FIRM_MISMATCH` (HTTP 409); its `period_end` must fall on or before the review's `year_end`, otherwise `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_OUTSIDE_REVIEW_SCOPE` (HTTP 409); no other committed selection of this review may already name the same engagement, otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_SELECTION` (HTTP 409); and `hours_committed` may not exceed the review's remaining `unallocated_hours`, otherwise `ERR_SCOPE_HOURS_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409) — the fiscal check that runs last of all.

A committed selection is created with `status` `COMMITTED`, `disposition` `NONE` and `non_conforming` false, and its `must_select_category` is derived by the server from the engagement rather than supplied by the caller, taking one of `ERISA_AUDIT`, `SINGLE_AUDIT`, `BROKER_DEALER`, `GOVERNMENTAL` or `PUBLIC_INTEREST`, or null when the engagement satisfies no must-select category. Committing debits the review's `unallocated_hours` by `hours_committed` and credits the reviewer's `committed_hours_balance` by the same amount, so that at every instant the review's `total_scope_hours` equals its `unallocated_hours` plus the sum of `hours_committed` across its committed selections. When `is_scope_limitation` is true the review's `scope_limitation_count` rises by one. Selections may be listed filtered by `peer_review_id`, `engagement_id`, `status` or `must_select_category`.

Withdrawing a committed selection moves its `status` `COMMITTED → WITHDRAWN` — the row is retained, never deleted — and compensates exactly: the review's `unallocated_hours` is credited back by that selection's `hours_committed` and the reviewer's `committed_hours_balance` debited by the same amount, restoring both to the values they held before the selection existed, and `scope_limitation_count` is recomputed downward when the withdrawn selection was a scope limitation. Only the assigned reviewer may withdraw, and a selection that is not currently `COMMITTED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TRANSITION`. When both would fail — a selection that is no longer `COMMITTED` beneath a review that is no longer in `SCOPE_SET` or `FIELDWORK` — the selection's own status is judged first, so the answer is `ERR_INVALID_TRANSITION`.

**Dispositions.** During fieldwork the reviewer records a conclusion on each selected engagement, writing both a `disposition` — one of `NO_MATTER`, `FFC`, `DEFICIENCY` or `SIGNIFICANT_DEFICIENCY` — and a `non_conforming` boolean marking an engagement judged not performed in conformity with professional standards in all material respects. Dispositions may only be recorded on a committed selection while the review is in `FIELDWORK`: a review still in `SCOPE_SET` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409), and one already rated or beyond with `ERR_PEER_REVIEW_ALREADY_RATED`. A selection that is not currently `COMMITTED` draws `ERR_INVALID_TRANSITION` too, and that judgement is made ahead of the review-state one, so a withdrawn selection beneath an already-rated review answers `ERR_INVALID_TRANSITION`.

**Drafting the rating.** `FIELDWORK → RATING_DRAFTED` requires every committed selection to carry a `disposition` other than `NONE`, otherwise `ERR_SELECTION_DISPOSITION_MISSING` (HTTP 409), and a review whose `rating` is already set is refused a second pass with `ERR_PEER_REVIEW_ALREADY_RATED`. The rating is then derived from the committed selections only — withdrawn selections are excluded from every count, so withdrawing the sole non-conforming selection genuinely moves a would-be `PASS_WITH_DEFICIENCY` back to `PASS`. The server counts, across committed selections: those flagged `is_scope_limitation` (recorded as `scope_limitation_count`), those whose `disposition` is `SIGNIFICANT_DEFICIENCY`, those whose `disposition` is `DEFICIENCY`, those whose `disposition` is `FFC`, those whose `non_conforming` is true, and those whose `non_conforming` is true **and** whose `must_select_category` is not null. The `rating` ladder is then evaluated strictly top to bottom, first match winning: `FAIL` when at least one selection carries a `SIGNIFICANT_DEFICIENCY` disposition, or at least one non-conforming selection sits in a must-select category, or the scope-limitation count is 2 or more; otherwise `PASS_WITH_DEFICIENCY` when at least one selection carries a `DEFICIENCY` disposition, or at least one selection is non-conforming, or the scope-limitation count is exactly 1; otherwise `PASS`. A count of `FFC` dispositions never moves the rating on its own — a review whose only matters are findings for further consideration rates `PASS`, and that count exists purely for the report narrative. A review that committed no selections at all satisfies the disposition requirement vacuously and rates `PASS`.

**Issuing the report and the fork.** `RATING_DRAFTED → REPORT_ISSUED` is the reviewer's act, requires the `rating` to be something other than `NONE`, and stamps `report_issued_at` from the logical clock. Issuance is the fork point. On a `PASS`, no corrective action plan is created at all and the committee may accept the report directly, `REPORT_ISSUED → ACCEPTED`; attempting that direct acceptance on a review whose `rating` is not `PASS` is refused with `ERR_CORRECTIVE_ACTION_OUTSTANDING` (HTTP 409). Because issuance forks a non-`PASS` review onward within the same request, the attempt that sentence describes is made against a review sitting in `CORRECTIVE_ACTION_REQUIRED`: `ACCEPTED` is a requestable `to_state` from `CORRECTIVE_ACTION_REQUIRED` just as it is from `REPORT_ISSUED` and `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED`, and an acceptance request is refused with `ERR_CORRECTIVE_ACTION_OUTSTANDING` — not with `ERR_INVALID_TRANSITION` — while any corrective action plan of that review has yet to reach `ACCEPTED` status. On a rating of `PASS_WITH_DEFICIENCY` or `FAIL` the system instead forks the review to `CORRECTIVE_ACTION_REQUIRED` within the same issuance request and synthesizes exactly one corrective action plan in `status` `REQUIRED`, with `evidence_summary` and `evidence_submitted_at` null and `rejection_count` 0. Its `action_code` — drawn from the declared set `FFC_LETTER`, `MONITORING_VISIT`, `CPE_COURSEWORK`, `PREISSUANCE_REVIEW` and `ACCELERATED_REVIEW` — is `MONITORING_VISIT` with a `due_date` 90 days after the report issue date when the rating is `PASS_WITH_DEFICIENCY`, and `PREISSUANCE_REVIEW` with a `due_date` 45 days after the report issue date when the rating is `FAIL`. Every leg of the review's transitions endpoint applies one ownership rule: a `PeerReviewer` must be that review's `assigned_reviewer_id`, otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`, while the committee is never subject to the comparison — so both acceptance legs are open to the assigned reviewer as well as to the committee, and the comparison is made before the leg's own legality is judged. Corrective action plans may be listed filtered by `peer_review_id`, `status` or `action_code`.

Direct acceptance from `REPORT_ISSUED` sets the firm's `last_peer_review_rating` to `PASS`, prepends the reviewer to the firm's `prior_reviewer_ids` and truncates that array to three entries, decrements the reviewer's `active_review_count` by one, and re-runs the posture ladder.

**Corrective action and the follow-up cascade.** The firm answers a required corrective action by submitting an `evidence_summary` narrative, which stamps `evidence_submitted_at` from the logical clock and moves the plan's `status` `REQUIRED → EVIDENCE_SUBMITTED` (and likewise `REJECTED → EVIDENCE_SUBMITTED` on a resubmission). The logical clock's date must be on or before the plan's `due_date`; a late submission is refused with `ERR_CORRECTIVE_ACTION_PAST_DUE` (HTTP 409). Once every plan of the review carries evidence, the review is driven `CORRECTIVE_ACTION_REQUIRED → REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED`. A review that is no longer in `CORRECTIVE_ACTION_REQUIRED` — including one the rejection cascade below has already driven to the terminal `FOLLOW_UP_REQUIRED` — is left exactly where it is by a resubmission, which still records the evidence on the plan.

The committee then adjudicates with an `outcome`; a plan that does not currently read `EVIDENCE_SUBMITTED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409). An `ACCEPTED` outcome moves the plan to its terminal `ACCEPTED` status and leaves the review in `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED` until every plan is accepted, after which the committee transitions the review `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED → ACCEPTED`. That leg writes the firm's `last_peer_review_rating` with the review's own `rating`, prepends the reviewer to `prior_reviewer_ids` truncated to three entries, decrements the reviewer's `active_review_count` by one, and re-runs the posture ladder — which, because the rating was not a pass, lands on `ELEVATED` for `PASS_WITH_DEFICIENCY` and `CRITICAL` for `FAIL`. This is where the deficiency branch of the fork re-joins the pass branch, and the only difference between the two branches is that posture outcome.

A `REJECTED` outcome fires a five-link cascade. **Link 1** sets the plan's `status` to the terminal `REJECTED` and increments its `rejection_count` by 1. **Link 2** fires because the plan was rejected: the review is driven `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED → FOLLOW_UP_REQUIRED`. **Link 3** fires because of link 2: a new peer review is created for the same firm in state `SCHEDULED`, with `prior_peer_review_id` set to the rejected review's `id`, `is_follow_up` true, `review_type` `SYSTEM_REVIEW`, `year_end` copied from the original review, `scheduled_window_start` set 180 days after the original review's `scheduled_window_end` and `scheduled_window_end` set 14 days after that new start; the original reviewer's `active_review_count` is decremented by 1. **Link 4** fires because link 3 closed out the original review: the firm's `last_peer_review_rating` is written with that review's `rating`, the reviewer is prepended to `prior_reviewer_ids`, and the posture ladder is re-run. **Link 5** fires when link 4 produced a `CRITICAL` posture: client acceptance is frozen at the firm, so any subsequent attempt to accept an `ERISA_AUDIT`, `SINGLE_AUDIT` or `BROKER_DEALER` engagement there is refused with `ERR_ACCEPTANCE_FROZEN`. `FOLLOW_UP_REQUIRED` is terminal for the original review; the firm's remediation continues in the follow-up review the cascade created.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Every endpoint below is exposed at the root of the service, with no shared path prefix, and a trailing slash on any of these paths addresses the same endpoint as the path without one. Successful resource creation returns HTTP 201 with the created resource in `data`. Successful modifications — patches, transitions, classifications, validations, dispositions, evaluations, scope determinations, reviewer assignments, selector runs, evidence submissions and clock changes — return HTTP 200 with the affected resource in `data`. The single withdrawal endpoint for a peer review selection returns HTTP 204; the standard envelope with an empty `data` object is built for it, but a 204 reply carries no body on the wire, so its status code is the only observable part of the response. Every list endpoint honours the pagination and ordering rules of §2.3 and accepts the filters named in its description. `SystemAdmin` is authorized on every endpoint for setup and recovery purposes.

## 5. Error Code Catalogue

The evaluation order of §2.5 determines which identifier a request that violates several rules at once receives.
