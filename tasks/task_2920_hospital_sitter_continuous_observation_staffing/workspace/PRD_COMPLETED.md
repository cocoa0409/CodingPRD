# Hospital Sitter & Continuous Observation Staffing API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Continuous observation — the practice of placing a patient safety attendant, colloquially a "sitter", at the bedside of a patient judged to be at risk of falling, of leaving the unit unaccompanied, or of harming themselves — is simultaneously one of the most clinically valuable and one of the most operationally expensive interventions a hospital runs. A single one-to-one watch consumes a full labor shift; a unit that quietly accumulates a dozen of them can burn through a quarter's observation budget in a fortnight. Yet the decision to stop watching a patient is a clinical decision, not a financial one, and the two must be kept structurally apart.

This document specifies the Hospital Sitter & Continuous Observation Staffing API, mounted under `/observation`. The system governs the whole arc of an observation episode: the ordering provider who documents which risk criteria a patient meets, the derived risk score that sets how often that judgement must be revisited, the staffing coordinator who sources an observer from the internal float pool, an agency, or the trained-volunteer pool, the observer who keeps the watch and hands it off at shift change, the remote observer who watches several patients at once from a video console under a span-of-control ceiling, the safety officer who reviews what happened when a patient nonetheless tried to leave or struck out, and the cost ledger that has to reconcile all of it to the cent.

Three design commitments shape almost every rule that follows. First, **money is a ledger, not a counter**: every labor charge is an immutable ledger row, compensating actions reverse rows rather than editing them, and the unit's running total is always a full recomputation over the posted rows so that a scheduled-then-voided watch leaves the books exactly where it found them. Second, **safety decisions require two people**: terminating an order, closing an incident review, and countersigning a handoff each require a second distinct acting user, enforced by the server rather than by policy. Third, **time is explicit**: the server runs on a logical clock that tests and operators advance deliberately, and every observation window is a half-open interval so that back-to-back shifts and back-to-back feeds never spuriously collide.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contracts that hold for every endpoint in the API, regardless of which part of the observation workflow the endpoint belongs to.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which they act. Both headers must be present and non-empty; a header supplied as an empty string, or as nothing but whitespace, counts as absent, and either condition is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. The role name is matched exactly as supplied — case-sensitively and with no trimming — so a value carrying surrounding whitespace is not one of the seven. Seven roles are recognized, and their names must be spelled exactly as given here: `AttendingProvider`, `NurseManager`, `StaffingCoordinator`, `Sitter`, `RemoteObserver`, `SafetyOfficer`, and `SystemAdmin`. A value of `X-User-Role` that is not one of those seven, or that is one of the seven but is not authorized for the endpoint being called, is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.

The seven roles divide the write surface by professional function. An `AttendingProvider` writes observation orders, performs risk reassessments, opens the discontinuation review and renders the discontinuation decision. A `NurseManager` registers nursing units, admits and discharges patients, and voids scheduled watches. A `StaffingCoordinator` registers observation staff, records completed competency training, schedules watches, opens monitoring consoles, and closes the unit cost period. A `Sitter` keeps a one-to-one watch, takes break relief, drafts and countersigns handoff documentation, and reports safety events. A `RemoteObserver` admits and releases video feeds on a console, takes a console offline, and reports events seen on camera. A `SafetyOfficer` records the escalation response, opens the incident review, and closes an event with a disposition. A `SystemAdmin` drives the logical clock and may exercise every other write in the system **except** reporting a safety event, which is reserved to `Sitter` and `RemoteObserver` because the report must be attributable to a real observer. Every read endpoint in the API is readable by all seven roles.

Two of the seven roles are backed by a persisted entity, and the distinction matters because it is what ties `X-User-ID` to resource ownership:

- When `X-User-Role` is `Sitter`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Staff` row. Ownership checks on any resource that references a `Staff` compare the resource's own staff reference to `X-User-ID`: for the watch operations (starting a watch, starting and ending break relief, completing a watch) the comparison is against the assignment's `staff_id`; for drafting a handoff it is against the outgoing assignment's `staff_id`; for countersigning a handoff it is against the incoming assignment's `staff_id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `RemoteObserver`, `X-User-ID` is likewise the `id` of the acting `Staff` row, and ownership checks compare it to the `observer_staff_id` of the `MonitoringStation` being operated (directly for console and feed-admission operations, and through the feed's `station_id` when a feed is released).
- When `X-User-Role` is `AttendingProvider`, `NurseManager`, `StaffingCoordinator`, `SafetyOfficer`, or `SystemAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — for example as `ordering_provider_user_id` and `discontinuation_review_opened_by_user_id` on an observation order, as `performed_by_user_id` on a reassessment record, and as `review_opened_by_user_id` and `closed_by_user_id` on a safety event — but it never compares any of those values to an entity's `id`.

A mismatch between an entity-backed caller's `X-User-ID` and the owning `Staff` reference is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. An `X-User-ID` presented by a `Sitter` or `RemoteObserver` that names no `Staff` row at all is rejected with `ERR_STAFF_NOT_FOUND`. That resolution of the caller's own `Staff` row happens at the ownership rung rather than ahead of the request, so when a single request both presents an unknown entity-backed `X-User-ID` and names a resource that does not exist, the resource's own 404 is reported first and `ERR_STAFF_NOT_FOUND` is never reached. Because `SystemAdmin` is opaque, no ownership comparison is ever applied to it; it remains fully subject to every state, temporal, capacity, referential and fiscal rule in this document.

Segregation of duties is enforced at three places, and in all three the rejection is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The `AttendingProvider` who renders a discontinuation decision must present an `X-User-ID` different from the order's `ordering_provider_user_id`, so two distinct provider identities are always required to terminate an order through review. The `SafetyOfficer` who closes a safety event must present an `X-User-ID` different from that event's `review_opened_by_user_id`; on the escalation-response branch that field is null and the check therefore passes vacuously. The `Sitter` who countersigns a handoff must be a different `Staff` from the outgoing assignment's `staff_id`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` followed by exactly 20 characters drawn from the uppercase letters `A` through `Z` and the digits `0` through `9`, so the whole value is always 24 characters long.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. It is the one timestamp in the API taken from the host's real clock rather than from the logical clock of §2.4, so it does not sit at `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z` on a fresh server and does not move when the logical clock is set.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field. A resource object always carries every field this document declares for it: a field that currently holds no value is rendered as an explicit JSON `null` rather than omitted from the object.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx and 5xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong. Its wording is not part of the contract; only `error_id` and the HTTP status are.
- Failures raised by the HTTP layer before any endpoint of §4 is reached use the same envelope but carry error ids outside the catalogue of §5: a request whose path matches no route returns `ERR_NOT_FOUND` with 404, a request whose method the matched path does not support returns `ERR_METHOD_NOT_ALLOWED` with 405, a request the HTTP layer itself judges malformed returns `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` with 400, and an unexpected server fault returns `ERR_INTERNAL` with 500. A trailing slash is not significant, so `/observation/units` and `/observation/units/` reach the same endpoint.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every endpoint that returns a collection is paginated through two query parameters. The `limit` parameter caps how many items come back: it defaults to 10 when omitted, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. A `limit` outside that range is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than silently clamped, and so is a `limit` or `offset` that is not a base-ten integer literal. The `offset` parameter says how many items to skip and defaults to 0; a negative `offset` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, but there is no upper bound on it, so an `offset` at or past the end of the collection simply yields an empty `data` array while `total_count` still reports the full unpaged count. Either parameter supplied as an empty string is treated as though it had been omitted and falls back to its default. Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are sorted by `created_at` in ascending order; because the logical clock stands still between clock calls, many rows share a `created_at`, and ties are broken by ascending order of creation, so a row written earlier within the same logical instant sorts first.

List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope, alongside `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`. The `limit` and `offset` reported back are the effective values the server used after defaulting, `total_count` is the number of rows that survived the endpoint's filters before paging was applied, and `has_more` is true exactly when `offset` plus the number of items in this page is strictly less than `total_count`.

Beyond paging, list endpoints accept optional filters drawn from the fields of the resource being listed, and a filter is applied before paging. Units, patients, orders, cost entries, feeds, stations, safety events and staff can all be narrowed by `status`. Staff listings additionally accept `home_unit_id`, `source_pool` and `competency_level`. Order listings accept `patient_id` and `required_modality`. Assignment listings accept `watch_status`. Cost-entry listings accept `entry_kind`. Handoff listings accept `documentation_state`. Safety-event listings accept `event_kind`. A supplied filter value that is not a declared member of the corresponding enumeration is a request-shape failure and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Because the query string is read at rung 3 of §2.6 while path resolution only happens at rung 4, that failure — and an out-of-range `limit` or a negative `offset` with it — is reported before the enclosing resource is looked up, so a nested listing carrying a bad `limit`, `offset` or filter value against a parent identifier that names no row returns `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than that parent's not-found error. A filter supplied as an empty string is treated as though it had been omitted and narrows nothing, the enumerated filters are matched exactly and case-sensitively, the non-enumerated filters (`home_unit_id`, `patient_id`) are matched by exact string equality and a value naming no existing row simply yields an empty page rather than a 404, and query parameters the endpoint does not declare are ignored rather than rejected.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties and the Logical Clock

Every persisted resource carries a server-minted `id`, formatted as a lowercase random (version 4) UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp rendered as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with exactly three fractional digits (for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`). Every instant this API renders is rendered in that one form — always three fractional digits, always a literal `Z` suffix and never a numeric UTC offset — and any part of a stored instant finer than a millisecond is truncated rather than rounded on the way out. Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC instant that is equal to `created_at` on a freshly created row and is refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that resource — including mutations the server performs on its own initiative as part of a cascade, and including mutations that only touch a derived field such as a recomputed ledger head or feed count.

Derived fields themselves are not stored anywhere: `observed_cost_to_date_cents` and `open_order_count` on a unit, `last_shift_end_at` and `total_watch_minutes` on an observer, `reassessment_count` on an order, `scheduled_minutes`, `watch_segment_count` and `watched_minutes` on an assignment, and `active_feed_count`, `active_acuity_weight` and `effective_span_limit` on a console are all recomputed from the store every time the owning resource is read, whether it is fetched singly or returned in a list. A read therefore always reflects the current state of the rows a derived field is computed over. Storage is process-local and held in memory, and it is completely empty at startup — no unit, patient, observer, order or any other row is seeded — so every collection endpoint returns an empty page with a `total_count` of 0 until a client writes something.

The one exception is the reassessment record. A `Reassessment` is an immutable, append-only clinical artifact: it carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has no `updated_at` and no `version` field at all, and no endpoint mutates one after it is written. Every other entity in this specification — units, patients, staff, observation orders, sitter assignments, monitoring stations, video feeds, handoff records, safety events and cost entries — carries the full set of `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` and `version`.

Time in this system comes from a **logical clock**, never from wall-clock time. At startup the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and the server must not fall back to real time before the first clock call arrives. A `SystemAdmin` moves the clock by supplying a `current_utc` instant; the new value must be greater than or equal to the current logical clock, and an attempt to rewind the clock is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Every field in this document described as a "logical-clock instant" — `discharged_at`, `last_reassessed_at`, `next_reassessment_due_at`, `discontinued_at`, `lapsed_at`, `current_segment_started_at`, `break_started_at`, `voided_at`, `offlined_at`, `released_at`, `reassessed_at`, `countersigned_at`, `occurred_at`, `posted_at`, `reversed_at` — is stamped from that clock and not from the host's calendar.

### 2.5. Path Resolution and Reference Validation Order

Requests whose URL contains a resource identifier are resolved strictly left to right. The first path segment whose identifier names no existing row terminates the request with the 404 belonging to that segment's entity type: `ERR_UNIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PATIENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STAFF_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FEED_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_HANDOFF_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SAFETY_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REASSESSMENT_NOT_FOUND`, or `ERR_COST_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`. A request that asks for the assignments of a non-existent order therefore fails with `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND` before the server ever considers the assignment collection.

References that arrive in a request body are resolved after all path segments, and among themselves they are resolved in the order the endpoint declares its input fields. Creating a patient resolves `unit_id`; registering staff resolves `home_unit_id`; writing an order resolves `patient_id`; scheduling a watch resolves `order_id` and then `staff_id`; opening a console resolves `observer_staff_id`; admitting a feed resolves `station_id` and then `order_id`; drafting a handoff resolves `outgoing_assignment_id` and then `incoming_assignment_id`; reporting a safety event resolves `order_id` and then, when supplied, `assignment_id`. Each unresolvable reference raises the 404 for its own entity type.

### 2.6. The Enforcement Ladder

Validations are not independent. They form a single ordered ladder, and the server evaluates them in ascending order and returns the **first** failure it reaches, so a request that violates several rules always surfaces the earliest one. The ladder, in order, is:

1. Header presence — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. Role recognition and role authorization for the target endpoint — `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.
3. Request-body shape: every required input field present, every enumerated value a declared member, every integer inside its declared range, every array non-empty and duplicate-free where declared so, every ISO 8601 instant parseable — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Rung 3 is strict and never coerces: a value of the wrong JSON type is rejected rather than converted, so a number sent where a string is declared, a quoted `"5"` sent where an integer is declared, a `true` sent where an integer is declared, and any non-array sent where an array is declared all fail here. A field that is present but holds JSON `null` is treated exactly as an omitted field — a `null` in a required field is reported as that field being missing, and a `null` in an optional field takes that field's documented default. String lengths are counted on the raw value with no trimming, so a string made only of spaces satisfies a "1 to N characters" bound and satisfies the "present and non-empty" requirement on `float_authorization_code` and on `review_findings`; only the literal empty string `""` fails those. Non-emptiness and duplicate-freedom bind only the arrays this document declares them for — `risk_categories` and `risk_categories_after` — while an array not so declared, namely `interventions_used` and `response_actions`, accepts both an empty array and repeated members; in either case an array is stored and echoed back in the exact order its members were submitted. Keys the endpoint does not declare are ignored rather than rejected, in the body as in the query string, so an unrecognized field never fails a request. A request with no body at all, or with an empty body, is read as an empty JSON object and therefore reports its first required field as missing; a body that is not syntactically valid JSON, or that parses to anything other than a JSON object, fails here too, and the request's `Content-Type` header is never consulted. Finally, an acceptable ISO 8601 instant is a four-digit year, two-digit month and two-digit day, then `T` (a lowercase `t` or a single space is equally accepted), then at least `HH:MM`, with the seconds and a fractional part of one to nine digits (introduced by either `.` or `,`) each optional, and with a trailing zone designator of `Z`, `z`, `+HH:MM`, `-HH:MM`, `+HHMM` or `-HHMM` that may be omitted altogether — an instant carrying no designator is read as UTC, and one carrying an offset is converted to UTC before anything else looks at it. A date-only value with no time part, a value that is not a JSON string, and a calendar-impossible value such as `2026-02-30` are all rejected; fractional digits beyond the sixth are discarded on the way in, and only the first three ever survive into a rendered timestamp. When more than one field of the same request fails this rung, the failure reported is the one belonging to the field the endpoint declares first, following the same declared field order §2.5 uses for resolving body references.
4. Existence of path-segment resources, left to right — the corresponding 404.
5. Existence of body foreign-key references, in the endpoint's declared field order — the corresponding 404.
6. Ownership for the entity-backed roles — `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`.
7. Optimistic concurrency on the observation order — `ERR_STALE_ORDER_VERSION`.
8. State legality: the target resource is in a state from which the requested transition is declared, which also covers terminal lockdown and a replayed second attempt at a transition already taken — `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_ASSIGNMENT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_STATION_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_FEED_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_EVENT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_HANDOFF_STATE`, or `ERR_PATIENT_DISCHARGED`.
9. The start-watch cross-entity guard on observer duty posture — `ERR_STAFF_NOT_AVAILABLE`.
10. De-escalation certification currency — `ERR_DEESCALATION_CERT_EXPIRED`.
11. Competency floor — `ERR_COMPETENCY_INSUFFICIENT`.
12. Observer not suspended — `ERR_STAFF_SUSPENDED`.
13. Volunteer-pool eligibility — `ERR_VOLUNTEER_NOT_ELIGIBLE`.
14. The video-monitoring modality gate — `ERR_VIDEO_MODALITY_NOT_PERMITTED`.
15. The trailing-24-hour discontinuation safety window — `ERR_RECENT_SAFETY_EVENT_BLOCKS_DISCONTINUATION`.
16. Segregation of duties — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
17. Shift overlap for the same observer — `ERR_STAFF_SHIFT_OVERLAP`.
18. Rest buffer between an observer's non-overlapping shifts — `ERR_STAFF_REST_BUFFER_VIOLATION`.
19. Shift-window sanity — `ERR_INVALID_SHIFT_WINDOW`.
20. The start-watch time window — `ERR_OUTSIDE_SHIFT_WINDOW`.
21. Break eligibility and minimum break length — `ERR_BREAK_NOT_YET_ELIGIBLE` and `ERR_BREAK_TOO_SHORT`.
22. Span of control on feed admission — `ERR_SPAN_OF_CONTROL_EXCEEDED`.
23. A console with live feeds may not be taken offline — `ERR_STATION_HAS_ACTIVE_FEEDS`.
24. At most one live feed per order — `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTIVE_FEED`.
25. Uniqueness of the unit code and the patient medical record number — `ERR_DUPLICATE_IDENTIFIER`.
26. Cross-unit float authorization — `ERR_FLOAT_AUTHORIZATION_MISSING`.
27. Handoff linkage between the two assignments — `ERR_HANDOFF_ORDER_MISMATCH`.
28. The unit observation budget ceiling — `ERR_UNIT_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`.
29. The restricted-unit block on agency labor — `ERR_UNIT_RESTRICTED_AGENCY_BLOCKED`.

Three further rules are documented with the workflows they guard rather than on this ladder, because each is specific to a single operation: a handoff-complete watch closure without signed documentation is rejected with `ERR_HANDOFF_NOT_SIGNED`, closing an incident-review event without findings is rejected with `ERR_REVIEW_FINDINGS_REQUIRED`, and opening a discontinuation review on a never-reassessed order is rejected with `ERR_REASSESSMENT_REQUIRED_BEFORE_REVIEW`.

The practical consequence of the ordering is worth stating outright. Because ownership sits at rung 6 and the concurrency check at rung 7, a `Sitter` acting on someone else's watch always sees `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` and never a state error. Because state legality sits at rung 8 and the safety and competency rules at rungs 10 through 14, scheduling a watch against an order that has already been discontinued reports `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATE` even when the nominated observer's certification has also lapsed. And because the fiscal rules sit last, at rungs 28 and 29, a request that would both breach the budget and name an untrained observer reports the competency failure, not the budget failure.
### 2.7. Numeric Precision: Cents, Risk Scores, Weights and Whole Minutes

Four different kinds of number circulate in this system, and each has its own precision contract.

**Money is always an integer count of cents.** `observation_cost_budget_cents`, `observed_cost_to_date_cents`, `hourly_rate_cents`, `provisional_cost_cents`, `final_cost_cents` and a ledger row's `amount_cents` are all whole-cent integers, never decimals and never strings. Wherever a labor computation produces a fractional cent, the result is rounded half up to a whole cent — that is, a value whose fractional part is exactly one half rounds away from zero to the next whole cent. How many times that rounding is applied is itself part of the specification and differs between the provisional and final labor variants; see §3.4 and §3.5.

**Risk scores and acuity weights carry exactly one decimal place**, also rounded half up, and like money they are rendered as JSON numbers and never as strings — a score whose one decimal place is zero is still a JSON number, rendered as `4.0` rather than as `4`. This applies to `elopement_risk_score` on an order, to `score_before` and `score_after` on a reassessment record, to `acuity_weight` on a video feed, and to `active_acuity_weight` on a monitoring console. Where a score is capped, the cap is applied to the unrounded intermediate value and the rounding happens afterwards.

**Interval and span figures are integers.** `reassessment_interval_hours` is a whole number of hours and takes only the values 4, 8, 12 or 24. `declared_max_span`, `active_feed_count`, `effective_span_limit`, `watch_segment_count`, `segment_index`, `open_order_count` and `reassessment_count` are whole counts. The coverage surcharge percentage `coverage_surcharge_pct` is the only rate returned as a decimal fraction rather than a count.

**Durations are counted in whole minutes.** `scheduled_minutes`, `segment_minutes`, `watched_minutes` and `total_watch_minutes` are all whole-minute integers, obtained by counting the complete minutes that elapsed between the two instants and discarding any incomplete trailing minute. Every duration comparison in this document — break eligibility, minimum break length, the rest buffer, the maximum shift length — is made on those whole-minute figures.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency on the Observation Order

Exactly one write in this API carries an optimistic-concurrency guard. Scheduling a sitter watch — `POST /observation/sitter-assignments` — requires a non-optional `expected_order_version` field carrying the `version` of the target `ObservationOrder` as the client last observed it. Rung 3 constrains that field only to being an integer and declares no range for it, so a zero or a negative value clears the shape rung and then fails the comparison at rung 7 like any other wrong number. If `expected_order_version` does not equal the order's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ORDER_VERSION` and HTTP 412, and no assignment row, no cost row and no unit mutation results.

The reason this particular write is guarded is not that concurrent creates would collide. An order legitimately accepts many watches over its life — relief shifts, handoff successors, a night observer following a day observer — so a second create that observed the same order state would itself be a perfectly valid business action. The guard exists because an order's `required_modality`, `elopement_risk_score` and reassessment posture can all have moved since the coordinator last read it, and committing labor against a stale reading of those fields is exactly the mistake worth preventing. The check forces the coordinator to re-read the order before spending money on it.

One consequence follows directly and is intentional: because a successful schedule increments the order's `version` by 1, replaying the identical request with the same `expected_order_version` fails the second time with `ERR_STALE_ORDER_VERSION`. Any other write in this system that advances the order's version — a reassessment, an escalation, a review being opened or decided — likewise invalidates a previously read version. No endpoint other than the watch-scheduling endpoint accepts a version field of any kind, and none of them may be sent one.

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline and Version Bookkeeping

Many operations in this system mutate more than one row. When they do, three disciplines hold without exception.

First, **every mutated row is versioned**. Each entity touched by an operation — whether the operation targeted it directly or reached it through a cascade — has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by exactly 1. A single request can therefore advance the versions of an order, several assignments, several cost rows, a unit, a station and a staff member all at once, each by one. "By exactly 1" is per row per request, not per write to that row: however many times one request touches the same row, directly and through however many cascade hops, that row's `version` rises by 1 and its `updated_at` is set once to the current logical clock. A row the same request also created is exempt from the increment entirely — it is returned at `version` 1 with `updated_at` equal to `created_at`, even when later steps of that same request mutate it, so a newly created row is never observed at `version` 2.

Second, **cascade order is deterministic**. Wherever a cascade fans out over a set of sibling rows, those rows are processed in ascending `created_at` order: the orders of a discharged patient, the video feeds of a terminating order, the scheduled assignments being voided. Where one cascade hop can be contradicted by a later step in the same operation, the specification pins which one wins; the clearest case is the incident review, where the observer suspension is applied only after the console-idling hops so that a `SUSPENDED` duty posture is never overwritten by an `AVAILABLE` written earlier in the same request.

Third, **the server transitions rows on its own initiative** whenever a declared criterion becomes true, with no separate client request against the affected row. A patient discharge terminates that patient's orders; a terminating order voids and completes its watches, releases its feeds, and moves its ledger; a released feed can idle a console and free its observer; a posted cost row can restrict a unit. These are not optional reconciliations that a client may trigger later — they are part of the originating request.

### 2.10. The Unit Observation Ledger as a Conserved Quantity

Labor cost is tracked as an append-only ledger of `CostEntry` rows. A row is written once, in status `POSTED`, and its `amount_cents` is never edited afterwards. When an action needs to be undone, the row is not adjusted and not deleted: its `status` moves to `REVERSED` and `reversed_at` is stamped, while `amount_cents` stays exactly as posted. Every row also freezes the `source_pool` of the observer it billed at the moment of posting, so a later change to that observer's pool cannot retroactively re-price historical labor.

A unit's `observed_cost_to_date_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` across every `CostEntry` whose `unit_id` is that unit and whose `status` is `POSTED`. It is recomputed in full — over the whole posted set, never as an incremental delta applied to the previous value — on every single post and every single reversal, wherever those occur: scheduling a watch, starting a break, completing a watch, voiding a watch, and every cascade that posts or reverses on the system's own initiative. Full recomputation is what makes the following invariant exact: for any forward action paired with its compensating action, the ledger head returns to its pre-action value to the cent. Scheduling a watch and then voiding it must leave `observed_cost_to_date_cents` at precisely the number it held before the schedule, with zero drift, no matter how many other posts and reversals happened in between.

A parallel invariant holds on the console side. A monitoring station's `active_feed_count` is a full recount of the feeds on that station whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, never an incremented counter, and admitting a feed and then releasing that same feed must return both `active_feed_count` and `effective_span_limit` to their exact pre-admission values.

### 2.11. Half-Open Windows

Every scheduling window in this system is half-open: it includes its start instant and excludes its end instant. A sitter watch occupies the window from `shift_start_at` up to but not including `shift_end_at`; a video feed occupies the window from `monitor_start_at` up to but not including `monitor_end_at`. The consequence is uniform and deliberate: two windows overlap only when each one starts strictly before the other ends, so a shift that ends at exactly the instant the next shift begins does **not** overlap it, and a feed that ends at exactly the instant a candidate feed begins does **not** count against that candidate's span. Back-to-back coverage is therefore always schedulable, which is precisely what continuous observation requires.
## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up a Unit, Admitting a Patient, Sourcing the Pool

A `NurseManager` establishes a nursing unit before anything else can happen on it, supplying a short `unit_code` of 3 to 12 characters that must be unique across all units, a human-readable `name` of 1 to 120 characters, and an `observation_cost_budget_cents` ceiling that must be zero or greater. Whether continuous video monitoring is even a permitted modality on that unit is declared at registration through the boolean `video_monitoring_enabled`, which defaults to `false` when the field is omitted. A freshly registered unit opens in posture `OPEN` with `restriction_reason` null, an `observed_cost_to_date_cents` ledger head of 0, and an `open_order_count` of 0. A `unit_code` already taken by another unit is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_IDENTIFIER`.

A unit's posture is either `OPEN` or `RESTRICTED`, and `restriction_reason` explains a restriction: it is null while the unit is `OPEN`, and otherwise carries either `BUDGET_THRESHOLD_REACHED` or `OBSERVATION_ORDER_LAPSED`. The `open_order_count` field is a derived figure — the number of observation orders written for patients who occupy this unit whose own status is `ACTIVE`, `REASSESSMENT_DUE`, `ESCALATED` or `DISCONTINUATION_REVIEW` — and it is recomputed whenever a new order is written for one of the unit's patients, which is itself a mutation of the unit and therefore advances the unit's `version` by 1. Admitting a patient to the unit, by contrast, does not touch the unit at all — `open_order_count` counts orders, not occupants — and neither registering an observer whose `home_unit_id` is this unit nor opening a monitoring console for one of its observers advances any `version` other than that of the row being created.

Patients are admitted to a unit by the same `NurseManager`, who supplies the `unit_id` of the occupied unit, an `mrn` medical record number of 4 to 24 characters that must be unique across all patients, a `full_name` of 1 to 120 characters, and `age_years` as a whole number from 0 through 120 inclusive — a figure that later feeds the risk-score age adjustment. An admitted patient's `status` is `ADMITTED` and `discharged_at` is null. An `mrn` already in use is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_IDENTIFIER`.

Observers are registered by a `StaffingCoordinator`, who declares the `home_unit_id` the observer is based on, a `full_name` of 1 to 120 characters, the `source_pool` the observer is drawn from (`INTERNAL_FLOAT`, `AGENCY` or `VOLUNTEER`), an `hourly_rate_cents` base labor rate from 0 through 100000 inclusive, the `competency_level` earned through observation training (`NOT_TRAINED`, `BASIC` or `ADVANCED`), and `deescalation_cert_expires_on`, the ISO 8601 UTC instant at which the observer's de-escalation certification lapses. The certification counts as expired the moment the logical clock reaches that instant or passes it — expiry is inclusive, so an instant exactly equal to the clock is already expired. Registration leaves the observer's duty posture `status` at `AVAILABLE`, `suspension_reason` null, `last_shift_end_at` null and `total_watch_minutes` at 0.

An observer's duty posture is `AVAILABLE`, `ON_ASSIGNMENT` or `SUSPENDED`, and the only value `suspension_reason` ever takes besides null is `INCIDENT_REVIEW_OPENED`. Two derived figures ride along on the observer record: `last_shift_end_at` is the latest `shift_end_at` among all of that observer's assignments whose `watch_status` is anything other than `VOIDED`, and is null when the observer holds no non-voided assignment at all; `total_watch_minutes` is the sum of closed watch-segment minutes across all of that observer's assignments whose `watch_status` is not `VOIDED`.

Training is recorded as a certification event. A `StaffingCoordinator` submits a new `competency_level` together with a renewed `deescalation_cert_expires_on`, and both fields are written onto the observer. This operation carries a second, decisive effect: it is the **only** path out of `SUSPENDED`. When the observer is `SUSPENDED` and the submitted `deescalation_cert_expires_on` is strictly later than the current logical clock, the observer returns to `AVAILABLE` and `suspension_reason` is cleared to null. An observer already in `AVAILABLE` or `ON_ASSIGNMENT` keeps that posture untouched, and so does a `SUSPENDED` observer whose submitted expiry is not strictly later than the clock — the two certification fields are written in that case all the same, but the suspension stands. Only the two certification fields move. Either way the observer's `version` advances by 1. Competency training is thus the gate on reinstatement: a suspended observer cannot be put back on watch by any other means.

### 3.2. Writing the Observation Order

An `AttendingProvider` opens an observation episode by writing an order against a patient. The payload names the `patient_id`, pins the writing provider's own identity in `ordering_provider_user_id`, declares the `risk_categories` the patient meets — a non-empty, duplicate-free subset of `FALL`, `ELOPEMENT` and `SELF_HARM` — selects the `required_modality` in force, either `ONE_TO_ONE_SITTER` or `VIDEO_MONITORING`, and documents a `clinical_rationale` of 1 to 500 characters. The patient must still be `ADMITTED`; writing an order against a discharged patient is rejected with `ERR_PATIENT_DISCHARGED`. The `ordering_provider_user_id` is stored exactly as supplied and is never compared to the caller's own `X-User-ID`, so nothing stops it naming a provider identity other than the writer's; it is that stored value, not the writer's, that the segregation-of-duties check of §3.9 later compares the decider against. Neither the unit's posture nor its budget is consulted when an order is written — a `RESTRICTED` unit accepts new observation orders freely — and nothing limits an admitted patient to one order at a time, so a patient may carry several non-terminal orders concurrently.

An order's lifecycle `status` runs over six values: `ACTIVE`, `REASSESSMENT_DUE`, `ESCALATED`, `DISCONTINUATION_REVIEW`, and the two terminal values `DISCONTINUED` and `LAPSED`. Every order begins `ACTIVE`. The remaining system-managed fields start out empty: `discontinuation_recommendation`, `discontinuation_review_opened_by_user_id`, `discontinued_at` and `lapsed_at` are all null, `reassessment_count` is 0, and `coverage_gap_flag` is `false`.

Two derived clinical figures are produced at creation. The first is `elopement_risk_score`, persisted to one decimal place. It is built by summing a weight for each risk category the order carries — a `FALL` criterion contributes 1.5 points, an `ELOPEMENT` criterion contributes 2.5 points, and a `SELF_HARM` criterion contributes 3.0 points — then adding an age adjustment of 1.0 point when the patient's `age_years` is 75 or greater (a patient of exactly 75 receives the adjustment; a patient of 74 does not) and 0.0 points otherwise, then adding an event component described below. The resulting total is first capped at a ceiling of 10.0 — the cap is applied to the unrounded total, before any rounding — and the capped value is then rounded half up to one decimal place and stored.

At order creation the event component is fixed at exactly 0.0 points. This is the **admission-baseline** reading of the score, and the field `risk_score_variant` records it as `ADMISSION_BASELINE`. No safety-event row is consulted at all in this variant — not even events the same patient already accumulated under some earlier, now-terminal order. The baseline is deliberately a snapshot of stated criteria plus age, nothing more.

The second derived figure is `reassessment_interval_hours`, which converts the score into a mandated reassessment cadence over four tiers, each boundary inclusive: a score of 8.0 or higher demands reassessment every 4 hours; a score below 8.0 but 6.0 or higher demands every 8 hours; a score below 6.0 but 3.0 or higher demands every 12 hours; and anything below 3.0 falls to the default of every 24 hours. Because the comparisons are inclusive, a score of exactly 6.0 selects the 8-hour tier rather than the 12-hour one. Order creation stamps `last_reassessed_at` with the current logical clock and sets `next_reassessment_due_at` to that instant advanced by `reassessment_interval_hours` hours.

When the provider asks for `VIDEO_MONITORING` at creation time, the modality gate is enforced immediately. Video monitoring is permitted only when all three of the following hold: the unit the patient occupies has `video_monitoring_enabled` set to `true`; `SELF_HARM` is **not** among the order's `risk_categories`; and the order's persisted `elopement_risk_score` is strictly below 6.0. The score boundary is strict in a way the reassessment tiers are not — a score of 5.9 is permitted for video, and a score of exactly 6.0 is not. A request failing any of the three conditions is rejected with `ERR_VIDEO_MODALITY_NOT_PERMITTED`. Note that the gate reads the order's *persisted* score and consults no safety-event row of its own, which means the four recomputation points listed next fully determine the gate's answer at any moment.

Finally, it is worth stating plainly where `elopement_risk_score` and `risk_score_variant` can change, because nothing else touches them: at order creation, at every reassessment, when an incident review is opened on one of the order's safety events, and when one of the order's safety events is closed. No other operation in this system recomputes the score. Order creation also recomputes `open_order_count` on the patient's unit and advances that unit's `version` by 1.
### 3.3. The Reassessment Cycle and the Clock Sweep

Reassessment is the clinical heartbeat of an observation episode, and it is performed by an `AttendingProvider` who submits the `risk_categories_after` — the non-empty, duplicate-free subset of `FALL`, `ELOPEMENT` and `SELF_HARM` judged to be in force from this moment on. A reassessment is accepted from an order in `ACTIVE`, in which case the order stays `ACTIVE` (an early reassessment performed before the interval has elapsed is explicitly permitted), and from an order in `REASSESSMENT_DUE`, in which case the order returns `REASSESSMENT_DUE → ACTIVE`. In both cases the patient must still be `ADMITTED`, otherwise the request is rejected with `ERR_PATIENT_DISCHARGED`. Both of those are rung-8 conditions and the order's own state is examined first, which in practice puts `ERR_PATIENT_DISCHARGED` out of reach here: the discharge cascade of §3.9 has already driven every one of that patient's non-terminal orders to `DISCONTINUED`, so a reassessment aimed at a discharged patient's order reports `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATE`. The two transitions carry an identical set of effects; nothing about the outcome depends on which of the two states the order was in.

Each reassessment writes an immutable record. That record carries the `order_id` it belongs to, the acting provider's identity in `performed_by_user_id`, the `risk_categories_after` as submitted, the order's `elopement_risk_score` immediately before the reassessment in `score_before`, the score immediately after it in `score_after`, the resulting cadence in `interval_hours_after`, and the logical-clock instant of the reassessment in `reassessed_at`. On the order itself, `risk_categories` is replaced by the submitted `risk_categories_after`, the score is recomputed under the rolling event-weighted reading described next with `risk_score_variant` set to `ROLLING_EVENT_WEIGHTED`, `reassessment_interval_hours` is recomputed from the new score, `last_reassessed_at` is stamped with the current logical clock, `next_reassessment_due_at` is set to that same instant advanced by the freshly computed interval, `reassessment_count` is increased by 1, and the order's `version` advances by 1. An order's reassessment history is readable in `reassessed_at` order.

The **rolling event-weighted** reading of the score keeps the same category weights and the same age adjustment as the baseline, but replaces the fixed zero event component with a live count of what has actually happened under this order. It contributes 1.0 point for each safety event on the order whose `event_kind` is `ELOPEMENT_ATTEMPT` or `AGGRESSION` and whose `occurred_at` falls at or after the instant 24 hours before the current logical clock — the boundary is inclusive, so an attempt exactly 24 hours old still counts — plus 2.0 points for each safety event on the order whose `event_kind` is `FALL_WITH_INJURY` or `SELF_HARM_ATTEMPT`, counted over all time with no window at all. Events are counted regardless of their own workflow `status`: an event that has already reached `CLOSED` still contributes its points. The two components are added to the category sum and the age adjustment, the total is capped at 10.0 before rounding, and the capped figure is rounded half up to one decimal place. Because the event component can only add points, identical order data always produces a score at least as high under this reading as under the admission baseline — but not necessarily strictly higher merely because a safety event exists. It is strictly higher only when the order carries a harm event, or an `ELOPEMENT_ATTEMPT` or `AGGRESSION` still inside the trailing 24-hour window: an escalating event that has aged out of that window contributes nothing and leaves the two readings identical, and so does a category-and-age total that already reaches the 10.0 ceiling on its own, since the cap swallows the event points. Every one of the three occasions on which this reading is used — a reassessment, the opening of an incident review, and the closing of a safety event — also sets `risk_score_variant` to `ROLLING_EVENT_WEIGHTED`, and `reassessment_interval_hours` is recomputed from the new score immediately afterwards on every one of them.

Falling behind on reassessment has consequences, and they are applied by a sweep that runs as a side effect of **every** advance of the logical clock. A `SystemAdmin` supplies a `current_utc` instant; the clock moves to it and the sweep then walks the orders that are candidates for it. The sweep does not depend on the clock having actually moved: because a `current_utc` equal to the current logical clock is an accepted call rather than a rewind, such a call still runs the sweep in full. Only orders in `ACTIVE` or `REASSESSMENT_DUE` are candidates — an order sitting in `ESCALATED` or `DISCONTINUATION_REVIEW` is immune to the sweep entirely, which is what makes an open discontinuation review safe from lapsing while it is being decided.

The sweep considers candidates in ascending `next_reassessment_due_at`, breaking ties by ascending `created_at`, and applies **at most one** transition to any given order per clock call. Two rules compete, and the precedence is pinned: the lapse rule is considered first, and only then the due rule. An order in `REASSESSMENT_DUE` transitions `REASSESSMENT_DUE → LAPSED` when the new clock value is greater than or equal to its `next_reassessment_due_at` advanced by the 6-hour reassessment grace period — inclusive, so the instant exactly 6 hours past due already lapses. An order in `ACTIVE` transitions `ACTIVE → REASSESSMENT_DUE` when the new clock value is greater than or equal to its `next_reassessment_due_at`, equality being sufficient to fire it. Because at most one transition applies per order per call, an `ACTIVE` order that is already many days overdue still needs two separate clock advances to reach `LAPSED`: one to become `REASSESSMENT_DUE` and a second to lapse. The clock response reports the instant now in force as `current_utc`, together with `orders_marked_reassessment_due` and `orders_lapsed` as counts of the orders the sweep transitioned in each direction. Observation orders are the only rows the sweep itself examines, and nothing in this system expires merely because its own window ran out: no sitter assignment is completed because its `shift_end_at` has passed, and no video feed is released because its `monitor_end_at` has passed. A watch left running stays `ON_WATCH` and a feed left open stays `ACTIVE` indefinitely until an explicit request, or one of the order-terminating cascades, moves it.

Becoming `REASSESSMENT_DUE` is a minimal transition: nothing on the order changes except its `status`, its `updated_at` and its `version`, which advances by 1. In particular `next_reassessment_due_at` is left exactly where it was, which is what allows the grace period to be measured from the original due instant. Lapsing, by contrast, is expensive. It stamps `lapsed_at` with the new clock value and then cascades. First, every video feed on the lapsed order whose `status` is `ACTIVE` is released with `release_reason` set to `ORDER_TERMINATED`, processed in ascending `created_at`. Second, because those feeds left `ACTIVE`, each affected console recomputes its `active_feed_count` and `active_acuity_weight`, and any console whose `active_feed_count` reaches 0 transitions `ACTIVE_MONITORING → IDLE`. Third, because a console became `IDLE`, its observer transitions `ON_ASSIGNMENT → AVAILABLE`. Alongside those hops, the unit occupied by the lapsed order's patient is driven to `RESTRICTED` with `restriction_reason` set to `OBSERVATION_ORDER_LAPSED`; a unit already `RESTRICTED` is left as it is — its existing `restriction_reason` is not overwritten, whichever of the two values it holds, and its `version` does not advance — and this step is then a no-op. What lapsing pointedly does **not** touch is labor: every sitter assignment on the lapsed order is left exactly where it stands, no watch is voided or completed, no open segment is closed, not one ledger row is posted or reversed, and the unit's budget is not re-evaluated. That is precisely what distinguishes a lapse from the discontinuation cascade of §3.9. Every entity touched by any of those hops has its `version` advanced by 1, the order included.

### 3.4. Scheduling a One-to-One Watch

Scheduling is where clinical need meets labor supply, and it is the most heavily gated write in the system. A `StaffingCoordinator` names the `order_id` the watch will cover, the `staff_id` of the observer who will keep it, a planned `shift_start_at` and `shift_end_at`, and the `expected_order_version` demanded by §2.8. The optional `float_authorization_code` is discussed among the safety and referential gates below. The new assignment lands with `watch_status` at `SCHEDULED` and `documentation_status` at `PENDING`, with `scheduled_minutes` derived as the whole minutes between `shift_start_at` and `shift_end_at`, and with `final_cost_cents`, `current_segment_started_at`, `break_started_at`, `completion_reason` and `voided_at` all null and `watch_segment_count` and `watched_minutes` both 0.

The order must be in a state that still accepts labor; scheduling against an order that has reached `DISCONTINUED` or `LAPSED` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATE`. Past that, five observer-fitness gates apply in the ladder order of §2.6. The nominated observer's `deescalation_cert_expires_on` must be strictly later than the current logical clock, or the request fails with `ERR_DEESCALATION_CERT_EXPIRED`. The observer must clear the competency floor: an order whose `risk_categories` include `SELF_HARM` requires a `competency_level` of `ADVANCED`, any other order accepts `BASIC` or `ADVANCED`, and `NOT_TRAINED` is never eligible for any watch — a failure here is `ERR_COMPETENCY_INSUFFICIENT`. The observer must not be `SUSPENDED`, or the request fails with `ERR_STAFF_SUSPENDED`. And trained volunteers are deliberately kept away from the two highest-stakes criteria: an observer whose `source_pool` is `VOLUNTEER` may not be assigned to an order whose `risk_categories` include `SELF_HARM` or `ELOPEMENT`, and an attempt is rejected with `ERR_VOLUNTEER_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. It is worth noting what is deliberately *not* checked here: the observer's duty posture is irrelevant at scheduling time. A watch may be scheduled for an observer who is currently `ON_ASSIGNMENT` elsewhere, because the shift window may well be hours away. Duty posture is checked only when the watch actually starts. Nor is the order's `required_modality` consulted: a one-to-one watch may be scheduled perfectly well against an order whose modality reads `VIDEO_MONITORING`. And an order may carry several watches whose windows overlap, provided each is kept by a different observer — the two conflict rules below bind one observer against their own other shifts, never one order against its own other watches.

Three temporal gates follow, all evaluated against the observer's other commitments. Two shifts of the same observer conflict when their half-open windows overlap — that is, when each window starts strictly before the other ends — and a conflict is rejected with `ERR_STAFF_SHIFT_OVERLAP`. Assignments whose `watch_status` is `VOIDED` are excluded from this comparison entirely, so voiding a shift genuinely frees the interval it held. Where the candidate does not overlap a peer shift, the gap between them is measured in whole minutes — from the peer's `shift_end_at` to the candidate's `shift_start_at` when the candidate comes after, and from the candidate's `shift_end_at` to the peer's `shift_start_at` when the candidate comes before — and a gap shorter than the mandatory rest buffer of 480 whole minutes, that is eight hours, is rejected with `ERR_STAFF_REST_BUFFER_VIOLATION`. The rest-buffer comparison is inclusive in the observer's favour: a gap of exactly 480 minutes passes. Both checks look across all of that observer's non-voided assignments, on every order and every unit, not merely those on the order being staffed. Separately, the window itself must be sane: `shift_end_at` must be strictly later than `shift_start_at`, and the window may span at most 720 whole minutes — twelve hours — with any violation rejected as `ERR_INVALID_SHIFT_WINDOW`. That sanity check sits at rung 19, *below* the two conflict rules rather than above them, so a request whose `shift_end_at` is not strictly later than its `shift_start_at` is nonetheless compared against the observer's existing shifts first and may report `ERR_STAFF_SHIFT_OVERLAP` or `ERR_STAFF_REST_BUFFER_VIOLATION` instead.

Cross-unit float is authorized explicitly. When the observer's `home_unit_id` differs from the `unit_id` of the unit the order's patient occupies, the payload's `float_authorization_code` must be present and non-empty; omitting it, or supplying an empty string, is rejected with `ERR_FLOAT_AUTHORIZATION_MISSING`. When the observer is based on the patient's own unit the code is not required and may be omitted. Whatever code does arrive is stored verbatim on the assignment as `float_authorization_code` and echoed back on every read of it, including on a same-unit assignment where it was never required; when the field is omitted the assignment carries `float_authorization_code` as null.

The last two gates are fiscal, and they are the last for a reason: the coordinator should learn about a clinical or scheduling problem before learning about a money problem. First, the candidate watch is costed provisionally, and the unit's current `observed_cost_to_date_cents` plus that `provisional_cost_cents` must not exceed the unit's `observation_cost_budget_cents`. The comparison is generous at the boundary — a total that lands exactly on the budget is accepted, and only a total strictly greater than the budget is rejected, with `ERR_UNIT_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` and HTTP 402. Second, a unit whose posture is `RESTRICTED` may not take on new agency labor at all: an observer whose `source_pool` is `AGENCY` is refused with `ERR_UNIT_RESTRICTED_AGENCY_BLOCKED`, also HTTP 402, while `INTERNAL_FLOAT` and `VOLUNTEER` observers remain schedulable on a restricted unit.

The **provisional** costing of a watch is deliberately crude, because at scheduling time not a single watch minute has been worked. It bills the entire scheduled window at the observer's base rate scaled by their pool: the observer's `hourly_rate_cents` is multiplied by `scheduled_minutes` divided by 60, and that product is multiplied by the pool multiplier — 1.00 for `INTERNAL_FLOAT`, 1.45 for `AGENCY`, and 0.00 for `VOLUNTEER`, whose labor is donated and therefore free. The result is rounded half up to whole cents **once**, over the whole window, and stored as `provisional_cost_cents`. No night differential is applied in this variant and no break time is deducted from it. The consequence is that for one and the same observer working exactly the minutes they were scheduled for, the provisional figure and the final figure computed later will generally differ.

That provisional figure is immediately posted to the ledger as a `CostEntry` whose `entry_kind` is `PROVISIONAL`. The row copies the `unit_id` from the unit occupied by the patient of the assignment's order, records the `assignment_id` it bills, carries the provisional figure as `amount_cents`, opens in `status` `POSTED` with `posted_at` stamped from the logical clock and `reversed_at` null, freezes the observer's `source_pool` as of this moment, sets `night_differential_applied` to `false`, and leaves both `segment_index` and `segment_minutes` null because a provisional row bills no particular segment. The unit's `observed_cost_to_date_cents` is then recomputed over the posted set, and if the recomputed head has reached or passed `observation_cost_budget_cents` the unit transitions `OPEN → RESTRICTED` with `restriction_reason` set to `BUDGET_THRESHOLD_REACHED` — the threshold is inclusive, so a head exactly equal to the budget restricts the unit. The observer's `last_shift_end_at` is recomputed as well, but the observer's `status` is explicitly not changed by scheduling. The order's `version`, the unit's `version` and the observer's `version` each advance by 1.
### 3.5. Keeping the Watch: Segments, Break Relief and Completion

A sitter assignment carries **two independent state dimensions on the same row**, and understanding that they are independent is essential. The `watch_status` dimension tracks physical presence and runs `SCHEDULED`, `ON_WATCH`, `ON_BREAK`, `COMPLETED` and `VOIDED`, with `COMPLETED` and `VOIDED` terminal. The `documentation_status` dimension tracks the handoff paperwork and runs `PENDING`, `HANDOFF_DRAFTED`, `HANDOFF_SIGNED` and `WAIVED`, with `HANDOFF_SIGNED` and `WAIVED` terminal. Neither dimension constrains the other except at exactly one coupling point, described at the end of this subsection. Drafting or signing paperwork never moves the watch dimension, and starting or ending a break never moves the documentation dimension.

One further thing none of the watch operations after `start-watch` consults is the owning order. Break relief, return from relief, completion and voiding all read the assignment's own `watch_status` and never the order's `status`, so a watch still standing on an order that has reached `LAPSED` — a lapse touches no labor whatsoever, per §3.3 — can still take relief, return from it, be completed by its sitter, or be voided by a `NurseManager`. Nor do any of them re-check the observer's duty posture, so an observer suspended mid-watch by the incident review of §3.8 keeps a watch that is already `ON_WATCH` and may carry it through relief all the way to completion.

The assigned sitter begins the watch personally, advancing `SCHEDULED → ON_WATCH`. Four conditions guard it. The order must be in `ACTIVE`, `REASSESSMENT_DUE` or `ESCALATED` — an escalated order still needs watching, so escalation does not block a watch from starting. Those three are an exhaustive list, and every other status is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATE`: not only the two terminal values but `DISCONTINUATION_REVIEW` as well, so an order whose discontinuation is under deliberation admits no new watch even though it is not yet terminal. Two rung-8 conditions apply at this endpoint and the order's is examined before the assignment's, so replaying `start-watch` on a watch that is already `ON_WATCH` under an order that has since gone terminal reports `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATE` and not `ERR_INVALID_ASSIGNMENT_STATE`. The observer's duty posture must be `AVAILABLE`; an observer who is already `ON_ASSIGNMENT` on another watch or staffing a console, or who is `SUSPENDED`, is refused with `ERR_STAFF_NOT_AVAILABLE`. This is the cross-entity guard that scheduling deliberately skipped. The clock must be inside the permitted start window: at or after `shift_start_at` less the 15-minute early-start grace, and strictly before `shift_end_at`. Arriving 20 minutes early, or arriving at or after the shift's own end instant, is rejected with `ERR_OUTSIDE_SHIFT_WINDOW`. And the caller must be the assignment's own observer. Starting the watch stamps `current_segment_started_at` with the logical clock, opening the first billable segment, and moves the observer `AVAILABLE → ON_ASSIGNMENT`; both rows advance their `version` by 1.

Break relief closes a segment rather than pausing one. Advancing `ON_WATCH → ON_BREAK` requires that at least 120 whole minutes — two full hours — have elapsed on the currently open segment since `current_segment_started_at`; an earlier attempt is rejected with `ERR_BREAK_NOT_YET_ELIGIBLE`. The open segment is closed at the current logical clock and one `CostEntry` of `entry_kind` `FINAL_SEGMENT` is posted for it, carrying `segment_index` set to the assignment's `watch_segment_count` plus 1 (so segment ordinals are 1-based and strictly increasing) and `segment_minutes` set to the whole minutes of the segment just closed. `break_started_at` is stamped and `current_segment_started_at` is cleared to null. Four derived figures are then recomputed: the assignment's `watch_segment_count`, which is the number of that assignment's `FINAL_SEGMENT` ledger rows still in `status` `POSTED`; the assignment's `watched_minutes`, which is the sum of `segment_minutes` across those same rows; the observer's `total_watch_minutes`; and the unit's `observed_cost_to_date_cents`. The assignment's `PROVISIONAL` row is deliberately left in `status` `POSTED` at break relief — it is reversed only when the watch is completed or voided — so from the first break until the watch closes the ledger head carries the provisional row and the closed segments at the same time. The unit then transitions `OPEN → RESTRICTED` with `restriction_reason` `BUDGET_THRESHOLD_REACHED` if the recomputed head has reached or passed the budget. The assignment, the observer and the unit each advance their `version` by 1.

Returning from relief advances `ON_BREAK → ON_WATCH` and requires that at least 15 whole minutes of break have elapsed since `break_started_at`; an earlier return is rejected with `ERR_BREAK_TOO_SHORT`. It stamps a fresh `current_segment_started_at`, clears `break_started_at` to null, and advances the assignment's `version` by 1. No ledger row whatsoever is written for a break — **break minutes are never billed**, which is exactly why the watch is modelled as a series of closed segments rather than as one continuous span. Nothing beyond the assignment row is touched either: the observer's row and the unit's row are both left alone and neither advances its `version`. The asymmetry with break relief, which touches both, is deliberate and follows from the ledger — relief posts a row and therefore moves the unit's head, whereas returning from relief posts and reverses nothing.

Ending the watch advances `ON_WATCH → COMPLETED` and requires a `completion_reason` from the caller, either `HANDOFF_COMPLETE` or `SHIFT_END_NO_SUCCESSOR`. (The third value the field can hold, `ORDER_DISCONTINUED`, is written only by the system when a terminating order sweeps the watch up; a caller may not supply it, and an attempt to do so is a rung-3 enumeration failure rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.) Here is the single coupling point between the two dimensions: when the reason is `HANDOFF_COMPLETE` the assignment's `documentation_status` must already be `HANDOFF_SIGNED`, and if it is not the request is rejected with `ERR_HANDOFF_NOT_SIGNED`; when the reason is `SHIFT_END_NO_SUCCESSOR` no documentation state is required at all. Completing the watch closes the open segment at the current clock and posts its `FINAL_SEGMENT` row — a row is posted whenever a segment is open at all, even when zero whole minutes have elapsed on it, in which case the row carries `segment_minutes` 0 and `amount_cents` 0 and still consumes the next `segment_index`; the same holds for a segment closed by break relief or by a cascade — then sets the assignment's `PROVISIONAL` row to `status` `REVERSED` with `reversed_at` stamped — the row's `amount_cents` is never edited. `final_cost_cents` is then set to the sum of `amount_cents` across this assignment's `FINAL_SEGMENT` rows that are still in `status` `POSTED`; reversed rows never contribute. The submitted reason is stored in `completion_reason`, the unit's ledger head is recomputed with the same inclusive budget re-evaluation as before, and the observer moves `ON_ASSIGNMENT → AVAILABLE` — an observer sitting in `SUSPENDED` is left `SUSPENDED`, since certification training remains the only way out of it, though the observer's row is touched and its `version` advances either way. When the reason is `SHIFT_END_NO_SUCCESSOR` the documentation dimension moves `PENDING → WAIVED` and, provided the owning order is still non-terminal, the order's `coverage_gap_flag` is set to `true` — the flag is how an uncovered gap in continuous observation becomes visible after the fact, and once `true` it is never cleared again, by a reassessment, by a review decision, or by any cascade in this system. The assignment, the observer, the unit, and (when the flag was set) the order each advance their `version` by 1. Completion is only reachable from `ON_WATCH`; attempting it while the assignment sits in `ON_BREAK` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_ASSIGNMENT_STATE`.

A `NurseManager` may void a watch, but only one that has not yet begun: `SCHEDULED → VOIDED` is the sole void transition, and a watch that has already started can never be voided — an attempt from `ON_WATCH`, `ON_BREAK` or either terminal state is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_ASSIGNMENT_STATE`, as is a second attempt at any transition already taken. Voiding stamps `voided_at`, moves `documentation_status` `PENDING → WAIVED`, and sets the assignment's `PROVISIONAL` ledger row to `REVERSED` with `reversed_at` stamped and `amount_cents` untouched. The unit's `observed_cost_to_date_cents` is recomputed over the posted set, and by the conserved-quantity guarantee of §2.10 it returns to precisely the value it held before the watch was ever scheduled. One thing a reversal explicitly does **not** do is change the unit's posture: a `RESTRICTED` unit stays `RESTRICTED` even when the reversal drops the ledger head well back below the budget. The only operation that lifts a restriction is the cost close described in §3.9. The assignment and the unit each advance their `version` by 1; the observer's row is deliberately not touched at all and does not advance, even though voiding frees the interval that shift held and therefore changes the observer's derived `last_shift_end_at`. The order is not touched either.

The **final** costing of realised labor is where precision matters most, and it differs from the provisional costing in three ways: it bills only minutes actually watched, it applies a night differential, and it rounds per segment rather than once. Each closed watch segment is costed on its own, taking the segments in ascending start instant. For a segment, the whole minutes it lasted are divided by 60 and multiplied by the observer's `hourly_rate_cents`, then by the pool multiplier (1.00 for `INTERNAL_FLOAT`, 1.45 for `AGENCY`, 0.00 for `VOLUNTEER`), then by the night differential of 1.15 when that segment qualifies as a night segment and by 1.00 when it does not. That per-segment product is rounded half up to whole cents, and the rounded figure becomes that segment's `amount_cents`. A segment qualifies as a night segment purely by the UTC hour-of-day of the instant it *started*: the segment is a night segment when that hour is 23 or later, or earlier than 7 — in other words, segments beginning anywhere from 23:00 through 06:59 UTC. Nothing about the segment's end matters; a segment that begins at 06:59 UTC and runs until noon is a night segment in its entirety, and a segment that begins at 07:00 and runs past midnight is not one at all. The `night_differential_applied` flag on each ledger row records whether that row was multiplied by the differential. The assignment's `final_cost_cents` is then the **sum of the already-rounded per-segment amounts**. Rounding each segment first and summing afterwards is the rule; summing the unrounded per-segment products and rounding once at the end is a different and explicitly wrong reading, and the two can differ by one or more cents.

### 3.6. Shift Handoff Documentation

Continuous observation only stays continuous if the outgoing observer tells the incoming observer what they saw, and the documentation dimension of the assignment record is how the system enforces that. The outgoing sitter drafts the handoff, naming the `outgoing_assignment_id` — their own watch — and the `incoming_assignment_id` of the watch that will take over, and documenting a `behavior_summary` of 1 to 500 characters describing the patient's behaviour during the outgoing watch together with `interventions_used`, an array whose members are drawn from `VERBAL_REDIRECTION`, `DISTRACTION`, `FAMILY_PRESENCE`, `BED_ALARM` and `NONE`.

Four conditions guard the draft. The outgoing assignment's `documentation_status` must be `PENDING`; a second draft against an assignment already in `HANDOFF_DRAFTED` or `HANDOFF_SIGNED` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_HANDOFF_STATE`. The outgoing assignment's `watch_status` must be `ON_WATCH` or `ON_BREAK` — one cannot hand off a watch that has not started or has already ended. The incoming assignment's `watch_status` must be `SCHEDULED`. And the two must be watches on the same episode: the incoming assignment's `order_id` must equal the outgoing assignment's `order_id`, and the two assignment identifiers must differ, with either failure rejected as `ERR_HANDOFF_ORDER_MISMATCH`. The caller must be the outgoing assignment's own observer. A successful draft creates a handoff record whose `documentation_state` is `DRAFTED`, with `countersigned_by_staff_id` and `countersigned_at` both null, and moves the outgoing assignment `PENDING → HANDOFF_DRAFTED` while leaving its `watch_status` completely untouched; the assignment's `version` advances by 1. The four gates are evaluated in exactly the order set out above, the documentation-state gate ahead of both watch-state gates, so an outgoing watch that has already been drafted against reports `ERR_INVALID_HANDOFF_STATE` even when its `watch_status` has meanwhile left `ON_WATCH` and `ON_BREAK`. The incoming assignment is read but never written: neither its `documentation_status` nor its `version` moves, and nothing prevents two different outgoing watches on the same order from each naming the same incoming watch.

Countersignature closes the loop and is performed by the **incoming** sitter. The handoff record's `documentation_state` must still be `DRAFTED`; countersigning one already `SIGNED` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_HANDOFF_STATE`. The caller's `X-User-ID` must equal the incoming assignment's `staff_id`, and — this is the documentation leg of segregation of duties — the incoming assignment's observer must be a different `Staff` from the outgoing assignment's observer, so a sitter can never sign off on their own report; a violation is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Countersigning advances the record `DRAFTED → SIGNED`, records the countersigner in `countersigned_by_staff_id` and the moment in `countersigned_at`, and moves the outgoing assignment `HANDOFF_DRAFTED → HANDOFF_SIGNED` while again leaving its `watch_status` untouched. Both the handoff record and the outgoing assignment advance their `version` by 1 — the outgoing assignment is touched whether or not its `documentation_status` was still in `HANDOFF_DRAFTED` for the countersignature to move, while the incoming assignment is again left entirely alone. `countersigned_by_staff_id` holds the caller's `X-User-ID` exactly as presented, which for the `SystemAdmin` who may also call this endpoint is an opaque operator identifier rather than a `Staff` id.

`WAIVED` is the documentation dimension's other terminal value, and it is reached only from `PENDING`, only when a watch ends with no successor to hand off to. Three routes lead there and they are equivalent in effect: completing a watch with `completion_reason` `SHIFT_END_NO_SUCCESSOR`, voiding a scheduled watch, and the system cascade that sweeps a watch up when its order reaches `DISCONTINUED`. In every case the waiver applies only if `documentation_status` was still `PENDING` at the moment the watch ended; a dimension already sitting in `HANDOFF_DRAFTED` or `HANDOFF_SIGNED` is left exactly as it is, and the assignment's `version` advances by 1 for the waiver. This is the only place where the watch dimension reaches into the documentation dimension; the coupling in the opposite direction is the `HANDOFF_SIGNED` prerequisite for a `HANDOFF_COMPLETE` closure.
### 3.7. The Video-Monitoring Alternative: Consoles, Feeds and Span of Control

Not every observation order needs a body in the room. Centralized video monitoring lets one trained remote observer watch several patients at once from a console, and the whole point of the rules in this subsection is to stop that economy from being pushed past the point where the observer can actually see what is happening.

A `StaffingCoordinator` opens a console for a remote observer, naming the `observer_staff_id` who will staff it, a `station_label` of 1 to 60 characters, and a `declared_max_span` from 1 through 12 inclusive — the nominal ceiling on how many patients that console is rated to carry. A new console opens in `status` `IDLE` with `active_feed_count` at 0, `active_acuity_weight` at 0.0, `effective_span_limit` equal to `declared_max_span`, and `offlined_at` null. A console's posture is `IDLE`, `ACTIVE_MONITORING` or the terminal `OFFLINE`. No fitness test whatever is applied to the named observer at this point: any `Staff` row may be named, whatever its `source_pool`, its `competency_level`, the currency of its certification or its duty posture, and that observer's own row is not touched by the console's creation. The certification, competency and suspension gates bite only when a feed is admitted, and the same observer may staff more than one console.

The nominal ceiling is not the operative one. **The operative ceiling shrinks as the patients on the console get sicker.** Each feed freezes an `acuity_weight` at the moment it is admitted, computed from the risk categories of the order it serves: a `FALL` criterion contributes 1.0, an `ELOPEMENT` criterion contributes 2.0, and a `SELF_HARM` criterion contributes 3.0, with the sum rounded half up to one decimal place. A console's `active_acuity_weight` is the sum of `acuity_weight` across the feeds on it whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, again to one decimal place; released feeds are excluded, which is precisely what lets the console's capacity recover after a release. From that weight a penalty is derived over inclusive thresholds: a weight of 6.0 or higher costs 2 slots, a weight below 6.0 but 4.0 or higher costs 1 slot, and anything below 4.0 costs nothing. The console's `effective_span_limit` is then its `declared_max_span` reduced by that penalty, but never allowed below a floor of 1 — a console rated for 2 patients carrying a heavy weight still retains capacity for one.

A `RemoteObserver` admits an order onto their own console by naming the `station_id`, the `order_id` the feed will satisfy, and the feed's planned window as `monitor_start_at` and `monitor_end_at`, where the end must be strictly later than the start. That window comparison belongs to rung 3 rather than to the shift-window rung, so a violation is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and is reported before the console and the order are so much as looked up — unlike the sitter shift window of §3.4, which has an error code of its own and sits at rung 19. The caller must be the console's own observer. The console must not be `OFFLINE`; an `OFFLINE` console accepts nothing further and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATION_STATE`. The observer staffing the console is subject to the same three fitness gates as a bedside sitter — an unexpired `deescalation_cert_expires_on` or `ERR_DEESCALATION_CERT_EXPIRED`, the competency floor (`ADVANCED` when the order's `risk_categories` include `SELF_HARM`, otherwise `BASIC` or `ADVANCED`, never `NOT_TRAINED`) or `ERR_COMPETENCY_INSUFFICIENT`, and a duty posture that is not `SUSPENDED` or `ERR_STAFF_SUSPENDED`. The video modality gate of §3.2 is re-evaluated here against live data — unit `video_monitoring_enabled` true, `SELF_HARM` absent from the order's categories, and the order's `elopement_risk_score` strictly below 6.0 — with `ERR_VIDEO_MODALITY_NOT_PERMITTED` on failure. Those three conditions are the whole of the gate. The order's own `required_modality` is not among them, so a feed may legitimately be admitted for an order whose modality reads `ONE_TO_ONE_SITTER`; and the order's lifecycle `status` is not among them either. Feed admission is gated on the console, on the observer and on that modality calculation alone, and on the order's status never — a feed may be admitted against an order in `ACTIVE`, `REASSESSMENT_DUE`, `ESCALATED`, `DISCONTINUATION_REVIEW`, or even against one that has already reached the terminal `DISCONTINUED` or `LAPSED`. An order may carry at most one feed in `status` `ACTIVE` at a time, counted across every console in the system, and a second concurrent feed on the same order is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTIVE_FEED`.

Span of control is in fact evaluated **before** that one-live-feed-per-order rule and not after it, whatever the order of presentation here suggests: rung 22 sits above rung 24, so a candidate that would both overrun its console's span and duplicate a live feed on its order reports `ERR_SPAN_OF_CONTROL_EXCEEDED`. The internal evaluation order of the span rule itself is pinned and deliberately asymmetric. The capacity limit is read from the console's `effective_span_limit` as it stands **before** the candidate is admitted, so the candidate's own acuity never lowers the very limit it has to satisfy. Occupancy, on the other hand, **includes** the candidate: the server counts the console's feeds in `status` `ACTIVE` whose window overlaps the candidate's window — each starting strictly before the other ends, per the half-open convention of §2.11 — and adds 1 for the candidate itself. The feed is admitted when that total is less than or equal to the pre-candidate limit; the comparison is inclusive, so filling the console exactly to its limit is permitted. Exceeding it is rejected with `ERR_SPAN_OF_CONTROL_EXCEEDED`. Note carefully that this is an **aggregate capacity rule over instants, not a pairwise rule over rows**: a console may legitimately hold more `ACTIVE` feeds than its `effective_span_limit`, provided no single instant is covered by more feeds than the limit allows. A feed that ends at exactly the instant the candidate begins does not overlap it and does not count.

An admitted feed opens in `status` `ACTIVE` with `release_reason` and `released_at` null. The console then recomputes `active_feed_count`, `active_acuity_weight` and `effective_span_limit`. On the first admitted feed the console transitions `IDLE → ACTIVE_MONITORING` and its observer moves `AVAILABLE → ON_ASSIGNMENT`; an observer who is already `ON_ASSIGNMENT` simply stays there. The console advances its `version` by 1 on every admission, the second and third feed included and not only the first, and the observer advances theirs by 1 only if their posture actually moved. The order the feed serves is not mutated by the admission and its `version` does not advance.

The observer releases a feed from the console when the patient no longer needs it, advancing `ACTIVE → RELEASED` with `release_reason` set to `OBSERVER_RELEASE` and `released_at` stamped from the logical clock. Releasing a feed that is already `RELEASED` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_FEED_STATE`. The console recomputes `active_feed_count`, `active_acuity_weight` and `effective_span_limit`; when `active_feed_count` reaches 0 the console transitions `ACTIVE_MONITORING → IDLE`, with `active_acuity_weight` back at 0.0 and `effective_span_limit` back at the full `declared_max_span`, and its observer moves `ON_ASSIGNMENT → AVAILABLE`. An observer sitting in `SUSPENDED` is left `SUSPENDED` — idling a console never reinstates a suspended observer. The feed and the console each advance their `version` by 1.

Feeds are also released by the system, without any observer asking, and the `release_reason` distinguishes why. Two triggers force the modality back to a bedside sitter and stamp `MODALITY_FORCED_TO_SITTER`: an incident review being opened on one of the order's safety events, and a safety event on the order being closed with a `resolution` of `MODALITY_ESCALATED`. Two triggers terminate the order itself and stamp `ORDER_TERMINATED`: the order reaching `DISCONTINUED`, and the order reaching `LAPSED`. In every one of the four cases the owning console recomputes its counters and may transition `ACTIVE_MONITORING → IDLE` exactly as it would after an observer release, and every entity touched advances its `version` by 1.

Taking a console offline is permanent and is performed by its own observer. The transition `IDLE → OFFLINE` is the only route, stamping `offlined_at`. A console in `ACTIVE_MONITORING` — equivalently, one whose `active_feed_count` is greater than 0 — cannot be taken offline, and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_STATION_HAS_ACTIVE_FEEDS`; the observer must release the live feeds first. Because `OFFLINE` is terminal a console can never be brought back, and a second offline attempt is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATION_STATE`. The console advances its `version` by 1.

### 3.8. Safety Events, Escalation Response and Incident Review

When something goes wrong under observation, the observer who saw it reports it — and only an observer can, which is why reporting is the one write reserved to `Sitter` and `RemoteObserver` with no administrative substitute. The report names the `order_id` the event occurred under, an `event_kind` from `ELOPEMENT_ATTEMPT`, `AGGRESSION`, `FALL_WITH_INJURY` and `SELF_HARM_ATTEMPT`, a `narrative` of 1 to 1000 characters, and optionally the `assignment_id` of the sitter watch that was in force. That last field is left absent when the event was seen from a video console rather than at the bedside; when it *is* supplied, the caller must be that assignment's own observer, or the report is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. Beyond that ownership test the cited watch is unconstrained: it need not be a watch on the very `order_id` being reported against, and it may sit in any `watch_status`, `COMPLETED` and `VOIDED` included. Reporting is not gated on the order's lifecycle `status` in any way either — an event may be filed against an order in any of the six statuses, the terminal `DISCONTINUED` and `LAPSED` among them — which leaves the escalation rule below, rather than any admission gate, as the only place a report reads the order's status at all. The event is recorded with `status` `REPORTED`, `occurred_at` stamped from the logical clock, and `reported_by_staff_id` taken from the caller's `X-User-ID`; `response_actions`, `review_opened_by_user_id`, `review_findings`, `closed_by_user_id` and `resolution` all begin null. The `occurred_at` instant is the one both the trailing-24-hour discontinuation window and the rolling risk variant read.

The `event_kind` decides immediately whether the order escalates. An `ELOPEMENT_ATTEMPT` or an `AGGRESSION` report drives the order `ACTIVE → ESCALATED`, or `REASSESSMENT_DUE → ESCALATED` when the order was already overdue; either way the order's `version` advances by 1. Those two are the only source statuses the escalation fires from. From any other status the escalation is simply not applied and the order is left entirely alone, its `version` included, so an `ELOPEMENT_ATTEMPT` filed against an order already in `ESCALATED`, or against one sitting in `DISCONTINUATION_REVIEW`, `DISCONTINUED` or `LAPSED`, records the event and touches nothing else. Escalation notably does **not** change `required_modality` — only the incident-review branch forces the modality — and when the order was in `REASSESSMENT_DUE` the pending reassessment obligation is preserved untouched, with `next_reassessment_due_at` left exactly where it stood, so satisfying the escalation never quietly forgives an overdue reassessment. A `FALL_WITH_INJURY` or a `SELF_HARM_ATTEMPT` report leaves the order's `status` alone entirely; that event waits for the incident-review branch to act.

From `REPORTED` the event forks to **exactly one** of two review branches, and which branch is available is determined by `event_kind`, not by the officer's preference. A `SafetyOfficer` records the immediate escalation response on an `ELOPEMENT_ATTEMPT` or `AGGRESSION` event, advancing `REPORTED → ESCALATED_RESPONSE` and storing the submitted `response_actions` array — a field that is required on this branch, so omitting it is an `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, even though the array it carries may be empty. This branch is deliberately inert: `review_opened_by_user_id` stays null — which is what makes the segregation-of-duties check at close time vacuous on this branch — and no field of the order, of any feed, of any console or of any observer is mutated. Only the event's own `version` advances by 1. Attempting this branch on a `FALL_WITH_INJURY` or `SELF_HARM_ATTEMPT` event, or from any status other than `REPORTED`, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_EVENT_STATE`.

A `SafetyOfficer` opens an incident review on a `FALL_WITH_INJURY` or `SELF_HARM_ATTEMPT` event instead, advancing `REPORTED → INCIDENT_REVIEW` and recording their own identity in `review_opened_by_user_id`. This branch is anything but inert; it is the most far-reaching cascade in the system. First, the order's `required_modality` is forced to `ONE_TO_ONE_SITTER` — a no-op when it is already that. Second, because the order's modality has left `VIDEO_MONITORING`, every feed on that order whose `status` is `ACTIVE` is released with `release_reason` `MODALITY_FORCED_TO_SITTER`, processed in ascending `created_at`. Third, because those feeds left `ACTIVE`, each affected console recomputes `active_feed_count`, `active_acuity_weight` and `effective_span_limit`, and any console whose `active_feed_count` reaches 0 transitions `ACTIVE_MONITORING → IDLE`. Fourth, because a console became `IDLE`, its observer transitions `ON_ASSIGNMENT → AVAILABLE`. Alongside the cascade the order's `elopement_risk_score` is recomputed under the rolling event-weighted reading, and `reassessment_interval_hours` is recomputed from the new score with `next_reassessment_due_at` reset to `last_reassessed_at` advanced by that interval — note that `last_reassessed_at` itself is not moved, because opening a review is not a reassessment. Finally, and strictly **after** all four cascade hops, if the event named an `assignment_id` then the observer who was keeping that watch transitions to `SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` `INCIDENT_REVIEW_OPENED`. The ordering is load-bearing: applying the suspension last guarantees that `SUSPENDED` wins over any `AVAILABLE` that the fourth hop may have written for the same person. Every entity mutated anywhere in this operation advances its `version` by 1. None of that cascade is conditioned on the order's own `status`: the modality forcing, the feed release and the score and interval recomputation all run exactly as described even on an order that has already reached the terminal `DISCONTINUED` or `LAPSED`. The suspension likewise applies from whatever posture the watching observer held — `AVAILABLE`, `ON_ASSIGNMENT`, or an existing `SUSPENDED` whose `suspension_reason` is simply overwritten with `INCIDENT_REVIEW_OPENED` — and the assignment row the event named is itself never mutated, so a watch that was `ON_WATCH` stays `ON_WATCH` under an observer who is now suspended. Attempting this branch on an `ELOPEMENT_ATTEMPT` or `AGGRESSION` event, or from any status other than `REPORTED`, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_EVENT_STATE`.

The two branches rejoin at closure, performed by a `SafetyOfficer` from either `ESCALATED_RESPONSE` or `INCIDENT_REVIEW`, in both cases reaching the terminal `CLOSED`. Closing from `REPORTED`, or closing an already-`CLOSED` event, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_EVENT_STATE`. The closer submits a `resolution` from `CONTINUE_OBSERVATION`, `MODALITY_ESCALATED` and `STAFF_RETRAINING_REQUIRED`, and may submit `review_findings`. On the incident-review branch `review_findings` is mandatory and must be non-empty, and its absence is rejected with `ERR_REVIEW_FINDINGS_REQUIRED`; on the escalation-response branch it is not required and is not recorded. The closer's `X-User-ID` must differ from `review_opened_by_user_id`, so an incident review always takes two distinct officers; on the escalation-response branch that field is null and the comparison passes with nothing to compare. The two checks are ranked against each other, and the segregation check comes first: it sits at rung 16 while the findings requirement is one of the three off-ladder rules of §2.6 evaluated below it, so an officer who both closes their own incident review and omits the findings sees `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` and not `ERR_REVIEW_FINDINGS_REQUIRED`. Closure stores the `resolution`, records the closer in `closed_by_user_id`, and stores `review_findings` on the incident-review branch only.

Closure then reaches back into the order in three ways. If the owning order is `ESCALATED` and no other safety event on that order remains in any status other than `CLOSED`, the order transitions `ESCALATED → ACTIVE` — the last open event is what un-escalates the order, so an order with two open events stays escalated until the second one closes. The order's `elopement_risk_score` is recomputed under the rolling event-weighted reading, and `reassessment_interval_hours` and `next_reassessment_due_at` follow from it, with `next_reassessment_due_at` set to `last_reassessed_at` advanced by the new interval; a close never moves `last_reassessed_at`, so closing an event cannot postpone an overdue reassessment. And when the `resolution` is `MODALITY_ESCALATED`, the order's `required_modality` is forced to `ONE_TO_ONE_SITTER` and every feed on the order still in `status` `ACTIVE` is released with `release_reason` `MODALITY_FORCED_TO_SITTER`. Every entity mutated advances its `version` by 1.

All three of those reach-backs are unconditional on the order's own `status`, and this is the one place a caller-driven write in this system deliberately reaches into a terminal row. An order that has already come to rest in `DISCONTINUED` or in `LAPSED` still has its `elopement_risk_score`, its `risk_score_variant`, its `reassessment_interval_hours` and its `next_reassessment_due_at` recomputed by the close, and a `resolution` of `MODALITY_ESCALATED` still forces that terminal order's `required_modality` to `ONE_TO_ONE_SITTER` and still releases any feed that somehow remains `ACTIVE` on it. Only the un-escalation is naturally confined, because a terminal order is by definition not in `ESCALATED`. Contrast the `coverage_gap_flag` of §3.5, which is the one order field a caller-driven write does guard on the order being non-terminal. One consequence is worth stating: because the recomputation always rewrites `risk_score_variant`, the owning order counts as mutated by every single close, so closing any event advances that order's `version` by 1 even when the recomputed score, interval and due instant all land on the values they already held.
### 3.9. Discontinuation Review, Discharge, and Closing the Unit's Books

Stopping continuous observation is a two-person clinical decision wrapped around a safety window. An `AttendingProvider` first opens a discontinuation review on an order in `ACTIVE`, submitting a `recommendation` of either `DISCONTINUE` or `CONTINUE`. The order must already have been reassessed at least once — its `reassessment_count` must be 1 or greater — because a discontinuation judgement on an order that has never been revisited is a judgement on stale information; an order with a `reassessment_count` of 0 is rejected with `ERR_REASSESSMENT_REQUIRED_BEFORE_REVIEW`. The state gate is reached first, so a never-reassessed order that is not in `ACTIVE` reports `ERR_INVALID_ORDER_STATE` rather than the reassessment error. Opening the review is not otherwise gated: no segregation of duties applies to it, so the ordering provider may open the review on their own order, and neither the patient's status nor the trailing safety window is consulted here. Opening the review advances `ACTIVE → DISCONTINUATION_REVIEW`, stores the submitted recommendation in `discontinuation_recommendation`, records the opening provider in `discontinuation_review_opened_by_user_id`, and advances the order's `version` by 1. While the order sits in `DISCONTINUATION_REVIEW` it is immune to the clock reassessment sweep, so a review can be deliberated across clock advances without the order lapsing underneath it.

A second `AttendingProvider` then renders the decision, submitting a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `REJECT` and, optionally, a free-text `rationale` — a field validated as a string when it is present, under no length bound, and then discarded, since no entity in this specification carries it and no response echoes it back. The order must be in `DISCONTINUATION_REVIEW`, and in both branches the deciding `X-User-ID` must differ from the order's `ordering_provider_user_id` — the provider who wrote the order may never be the one who signs off on ending it, and an attempt is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

An `APPROVE` decision faces one further gate: the trailing safety window. The order must have **no** safety event whose `occurred_at` falls at or after the instant 24 hours before the current logical clock, whatever that event's own workflow `status` may be — an event already `CLOSED` blocks the approval exactly as an open one does, just as it still contributes its points to the rolling score of §3.3. The boundary is inclusive, so an event exactly 24 hours old still blocks the approval, and the rejection is `ERR_RECENT_SAFETY_EVENT_BLOCKS_DISCONTINUATION`. Clearing that gate advances the order `DISCONTINUATION_REVIEW → DISCONTINUED` and stamps `discontinued_at` from the logical clock, then cascades over everything the order was consuming. Every assignment on the order whose `watch_status` is `SCHEDULED` transitions to `VOIDED` with `voided_at` stamped from the logical clock, processed in ascending `created_at`, each reversing its `PROVISIONAL` ledger row, posting no final row at all and leaving `final_cost_cents` null — a system void is in every respect the void of §3.5 minus the caller. Every assignment whose `watch_status` is `ON_WATCH` or `ON_BREAK` transitions to `COMPLETED` with `completion_reason` set to `ORDER_DISCONTINUED`; an assignment that was `ON_WATCH` closes its open segment at the current clock and posts that segment's `FINAL_SEGMENT` row, whereas an assignment that was `ON_BREAK` has no open segment and therefore posts no final row, and in both cases `current_segment_started_at` and `break_started_at` are both cleared to null, the `PROVISIONAL` row is reversed, `final_cost_cents` is recomputed from the surviving posted final rows, `documentation_status` moves `PENDING → WAIVED` if it was still pending, and the observer moves `ON_ASSIGNMENT → AVAILABLE` — an observer sitting in `SUSPENDED` is left `SUSPENDED`, since certification training remains the only way out of it. The order's `coverage_gap_flag` is **not** set by this cascade; that flag belongs to a caller-driven `SHIFT_END_NO_SUCCESSOR` closure alone. Every feed on the order still in `status` `ACTIVE` is released with `release_reason` `ORDER_TERMINATED`, cascading into console counters and observer availability as described in §3.7. The hops run in exactly the order just given, and the two assignment groups are two separate passes rather than one merged walk: the order's own row first, then every `SCHEDULED` void in ascending `created_at`, then every `ON_WATCH` or `ON_BREAK` completion in ascending `created_at`, then the feeds, then the unit. Last of all, and **only** when the cascade actually moved at least one ledger row — that is, only when at least one watch was voided or completed — the unit's `observed_cost_to_date_cents` is recomputed over the posted set and the unit re-evaluates its budget, transitioning `OPEN → RESTRICTED` with `restriction_reason` `BUDGET_THRESHOLD_REACHED` when the recomputed head has reached or passed `observation_cost_budget_cents`. An order that carried no assignment in either group therefore leaves the unit entirely alone: no version advance and no budget re-evaluation, even though the order and possibly its feeds and consoles did move. Every entity mutated anywhere in the cascade advances its `version` by 1.

A `REJECT` decision returns the order `DISCONTINUATION_REVIEW → ACTIVE` and is not subject to the safety window at all. It clears both `discontinuation_recommendation` and `discontinuation_review_opened_by_user_id` back to null, recomputes `reassessment_interval_hours`, and restarts the reassessment clock outright: `last_reassessed_at` is stamped with the current logical clock and `next_reassessment_due_at` is set to that instant advanced by `reassessment_interval_hours`. The rejected review therefore buys the order a full fresh interval before it can become due again. The order's `version` advances by 1.

Discharge is the blunter route to the same terminus, and it belongs to the `NurseManager`. Discharging a patient advances that patient `ADMITTED → DISCHARGED` and stamps `discharged_at` from the logical clock; discharging a patient who is already `DISCHARGED` is rejected with `ERR_PATIENT_DISCHARGED`. From there a four-hop cascade runs. In the first hop, because the patient has left `ADMITTED`, every non-terminal observation order belonging to that patient — that is, every order in `ACTIVE`, `REASSESSMENT_DUE`, `ESCALATED` or `DISCONTINUATION_REVIEW` — transitions to `DISCONTINUED` with `discontinued_at` stamped, processed in ascending `created_at`. In the second hop, because each of those orders became `DISCONTINUED`, its assignments in `watch_status` `ON_WATCH` or `ON_BREAK` transition to `COMPLETED` with `completion_reason` `ORDER_DISCONTINUED` and `documentation_status` `PENDING → WAIVED`, while its assignments in `SCHEDULED` transition to `VOIDED`. In the third hop, because those assignments reached a terminal watch state, each completing assignment closes its open segment at the current clock, posts a `FINAL_SEGMENT` row under the final costing rules of §3.5, reverses its `PROVISIONAL` row and recomputes `final_cost_cents`, while each voided assignment merely reverses its `PROVISIONAL` row with no final row posted — and the unit's `observed_cost_to_date_cents` is recomputed over the posted set. In the fourth hop, because the unit's ledger head has moved, the unit re-evaluates its budget and transitions `OPEN → RESTRICTED` with `restriction_reason` `BUDGET_THRESHOLD_REACHED` when the head has reached or passed `observation_cost_budget_cents`. Independently of those hops, every feed on each discontinued order still in `status` `ACTIVE` is released with `release_reason` `ORDER_TERMINATED`, idling consoles and freeing their observers where the counters reach zero; each order's cascade runs exactly in the sequence pinned for the approve branch above, with that order's feeds released after its watches are settled and its unit hop, conditional on the ledger having moved, last of all. Every entity mutated by any hop advances its `version` by 1, the patient included.

Closing the unit's books is the `StaffingCoordinator`'s job, and it does two things at once: it prices a period, and it is the sole mechanism that lifts a restriction. The coordinator supplies `close_through_utc`, the exclusive upper bound of the period being closed, and optionally `budget_increase_cents` to raise the unit's ceiling. That field must be zero or greater whenever it is supplied; a negative value is a rung-3 failure rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, so a cost close can never lower a budget. Omitted, it leaves `observation_cost_budget_cents` exactly as it stood. The close itself is gated on nothing beyond role and the unit's own existence — no state, no ownership and no concurrency check applies to it.

The pricing is a waterfall, and its evaluation order is pinned to break an apparent circularity. Step one computes `subtotal_cents` as the exact integer sum of `amount_cents` over the unit's ledger rows whose `status` is `POSTED` and whose `posted_at` falls strictly before `close_through_utc`; the bound is strict, so a row posted at exactly `close_through_utc` is excluded, and rows in `status` `REVERSED` are excluded entirely — which is why a voided watch leaves no trace whatsoever in the charge. Step two selects `coverage_surcharge_pct` from a tier keyed on `subtotal_cents` **alone**, over inclusive thresholds: a subtotal of 150000 cents or more takes 0.12, a subtotal below that but 60000 cents or more takes 0.08, a subtotal below that but 20000 cents or more takes 0.04, and anything below 20000 cents takes 0.00. Step three computes `coverage_surcharge_cents` as the subtotal multiplied by that percentage, rounded half up to whole cents — this is the only rounding in the entire calculation. Step four computes `charge_total_cents` as `subtotal_cents` plus `coverage_surcharge_cents`. Reading step two as a tier keyed on the *total* rather than on the subtotal is the natural but wrong reading: it is circular, and it produces a different number whenever the surcharge would push the subtotal across a tier boundary. The response returns `subtotal_cents`, `coverage_surcharge_pct`, `coverage_surcharge_cents` and `charge_total_cents`, together with the unit's resulting posture as `unit_status` and its resulting `version` as `unit_version`. None of these four charge figures is persisted on the unit.

The restriction lift runs after the pricing and in a specific order. When `budget_increase_cents` is supplied it is added to the unit's `observation_cost_budget_cents` **before** the lift condition is evaluated, which is what allows a single cost-close call both to raise the ceiling and to reopen the unit in one step. A unit in `RESTRICTED` then transitions `RESTRICTED → OPEN` with `restriction_reason` cleared to null when two conditions hold together: the unit's `observed_cost_to_date_cents` is strictly less than the (possibly just-increased) `observation_cost_budget_cents`, and no observation order on this unit sits in `LAPSED`. A unit restricted because an order lapsed therefore cannot be reopened by money alone — the lapsed order has to be dealt with clinically first. A cost-close call against a unit that is already `OPEN` still performs the charge calculation and still applies the budget increase, but makes no status transition. No ledger row is created, mutated or reversed by a cost close under any circumstances, and the unit's `version` advances by 1.
