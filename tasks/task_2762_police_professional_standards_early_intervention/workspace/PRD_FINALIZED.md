# Police Professional Standards API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Public confidence in a police department is decided by how the department handles the cases it would rather not publish. A complaint arrives from a member of the public, from a supervisor filing on behalf of the department, or through an anonymous tip line; it names conduct, it names a unit, and sometimes it names an officer. From that moment the department is bound simultaneously by a statute of limitations, by a collective bargaining agreement that grants the accused officer notice and representation, by a civil-service appeal right, by a records-retention schedule, and by a disclosure obligation to prosecutors that can outlive the file itself. Today those obligations live in separate spreadsheets maintained by separate bureaus, and the department discovers a conflict between them only after a deadline has passed.

This document specifies the Police Professional Standards API: a single system of record for complaint intake and classification, investigation assignment and tolling, compelled-interview scheduling under officer rights, adjudication under the preponderance standard, application of the progressive discipline matrix, appeal through arbitration or civil service with reinstatement and back pay, and the department's Early Intervention System (EIS) — the non-disciplinary risk-monitoring layer that watches use-of-force, pursuit, complaint and sick-leave patterns and answers a rising risk profile with a supervised intervention plan rather than with charges.

Two design constraints run through everything that follows. First, **every temporal rule is evaluated against a logical clock, never against wall-clock time**; deadlines, statutory windows, notice periods, retention expiry and interest accrual are all deterministic and reproducible because the clock is an explicit, settable input. Second, **the same computation is deliberately performed twice at two different moments and is allowed to disagree with itself**: the discipline matrix is evaluated once as a *presumptive* proposal at adjudication and again as a *final* award at imposition, and the EIS composite score is computed both on an absolute rolling-twelve-month basis and on a peer-relative basis. Where the two results diverge, the divergence is the product, not a bug — it is what lets a commander see how much of a proposed penalty rests on priors that were never actually imposed, and how much of an officer's risk profile is explained by the cohort they work in.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

Section 2 defines the contract every endpoint honours: who may call, what a response looks like, how lists page, how numbers round, and the fixed order in which rejection reasons are evaluated. Section 3 then describes the domain workflows themselves.

### 2.1. Actors, Identity and Role-Based Access Control

Authentication is header-based. Every request — without exception, including the reference-data reads and the clock-setting capability — must carry both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role`. If either header is absent, or present but empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before anything else about the request is examined. If `X-User-Role` carries a value that is not one of the eight declared roles, or carries a declared role that the target endpoint does not authorize, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The eight roles, spelled exactly as they must appear in `X-User-Role`, are `Complainant`, `Supervisor`, `Investigator`, `IACommander`, `AppealsOfficer`, `OfficerRepresentative`, `RecordsCustodian` and `SystemAdmin`.

Three of those roles are backed by a persisted departmental record, and for them `X-User-ID` is not an arbitrary token:

- When `X-User-Role` is `Supervisor`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Officer`; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Officer` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a supervisor may activate, close or record a check-in against an intervention plan only when that plan's `supervisor_officer_id` equals `X-User-ID`; otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_NOT_PLAN_SUPERVISOR` (HTTP 403).
- When `X-User-Role` is `Investigator`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Officer`; the investigation-scoped operations compare `assigned_investigator_officer_id` to `X-User-ID`, and a mismatch is `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_INVESTIGATOR` (HTTP 403).
- When `X-User-Role` is `IACommander`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Officer`; segregation of duties is enforced by comparing `X-User-ID` against the investigation's `assigned_investigator_officer_id` and against the allegation's `subject_officer_id`, and a match on either is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). The same comparison also excludes the acting commander from being the officer selected as investigator.

The remaining five roles carry an opaque identifier:

- When `X-User-Role` is `Complainant`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it but does not compare it to any entity's `id`; a complainant's authority over a complaint file is proven instead by the `intake_reference_code` issued at intake, and a supplied code that does not match the stored one is `ERR_INTAKE_CODE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403).
- When `X-User-Role` is `AppealsOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity — the arbitrator or civil-service examiner is never an officer of the department — and it is not compared to any entity's `id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `OfficerRepresentative`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the collective-bargaining representative acts for a subject officer but is not that officer, and the value is not compared to any entity's `id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `RecordsCustodian`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity and is not compared to any entity's `id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `SystemAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity and is not compared to any entity's `id`.

Role responsibilities divide as follows. `SystemAdmin` seeds the standing reference data — units, hearing rooms and officer records — moves an officer along the employment dimension, may record activity events, may run the early-intervention sweep, may read the public outcome report, and holds the logical clock. `Complainant` files and withdraws complaints and reads its own complaint. `Supervisor` files complaints on behalf of the department, attaches allegations, records officer activity events, creates and closes intervention plans, exclusively activates plans and completes monitoring check-ins, and reads the officer, complaint, allegation, investigation, session, alert and plan resources. `Investigator` exclusively schedules interview sessions, records that they were held and submits findings, shares the tolling and postponement capabilities, and reads the complaint, allegation, investigation and session resources. `IACommander` exclusively screens and classifies intake, opens investigations, assigns investigators, closes investigations, dispositions allegations, imposes discipline and dismisses alerts; shares intake, allegation creation, tolling, the early-intervention sweep, plan creation and plan closure; and reads every resource other than the hearing rooms. `AppealsOfficer` exclusively hears and decides appeals and finalizes reinstatements, and reads the complaint, allegation, investigation, discipline, appeal and reinstatement resources. `OfficerRepresentative` exclusively files appeals, shares interview postponement, and reads the officer, session, discipline, appeal, reinstatement and disclosure resources. `RecordsCustodian` exclusively purges retention-expired complaint files and logs credibility-record releases, and reads the complaint, allegation, investigation, session, discipline, appeal and disclosure resources alongside the public outcome report. The authoritative per-endpoint role list is the table in Section 4.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is a schema failure and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Unless a specific list endpoint states otherwise, results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Two list endpoints override that default because their domain ordering is more useful: an officer's activity events are returned in `occurred_at` ascending order, and a plan's monitoring check-ins are returned in `sequence_index` ascending order.

List responses populate all four pagination fields in `meta`: `limit` and `offset` echo the effective values actually applied, `total_count` reports the number of records matching the filters before the page window is applied, and `has_more` is true when records remain beyond the current window. Single-resource reads and mutations omit those four fields.

### 2.4. Record Identity, Timestamps and the Logical Clock

Every persisted resource carries a server-minted `id` in UUID form. The identifier never carries a type prefix, and clients never supply it — an `id` present in a request body is ignored.

Most resources also carry the full audit triplet. `created_at` and `updated_at` are ISO 8601 UTC timestamps rendered with three fractional digits; `created_at` is stamped when the row is first written and never changes, `updated_at` is refreshed on every accepted mutation of that row, and `version` is an integer that begins at 1 and increments by exactly 1 on every accepted mutation. A cascade counts as a mutation: when a write to one resource changes a second resource, the second resource's `updated_at` and `version` both advance too.

Three record types are append-only history and therefore carry only `id` and `created_at` — for a `Unit`, a `HearingRoom` and an `OfficerActivityEvent` both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from the resource entirely. Units and hearing rooms are immutable once created, and activity events are immutable facts about the past; none of them is ever the target of an update.

The logical clock is the single source of "now". Before any client sets it, the logical clock reads exactly the startup default `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real wall-clock time. A `SystemAdmin` moves it by supplying `current_utc`; the response reports the new `current_utc` alongside the `previous_utc` it replaced, and no entity is mutated by the move. Every subsequent request evaluates every deadline, window, notice period, expiry and interest computation against the new value. Fields documented as "the logical clock instant" — `received_at`, `disposed_at`, `held_at`, `imposed_at`, `filed_at`, `heard_at`, `decided_at`, `activated_at`, `completed_at`, `closed_at`, `finalized_at`, `effective_date` on a reinstatement, `purged_at` and `last_released_at` — are all stamped from it.

### 2.5. Precision, Rounding and Day Arithmetic

Four kinds of number appear in this system, and each has one rounding discipline.

**Scores and percentages** are decimals carried to exactly 2 decimal places and rounded half up: `support_pct` on an allegation, `composite_score` on an alert, `eis_score` on an officer, `compliance_score` on a plan and `notice_hours` on an interview session are all 2-decimal values. Wherever a formula accumulates intermediate terms — the EIS weighted total, the peer-group mean — the accumulation is exact decimal arithmetic with no intermediate rounding, and the single rounding is applied to the final figure only. One percentage breaks the pattern deliberately: `sustain_rate_pct` in the public outcome report is rounded half up to exactly 1 decimal place.

**Money** is always an integer count of cents; there are no fractional currency values anywhere in the system. `annual_salary_cents`, `gross_back_pay_cents`, `interim_earnings_cents`, `net_back_pay_cents`, `interest_cents` and `total_award_cents` are all whole cents, and every intermediate money figure is rounded half up to a whole cent at the point the formula names, never later.

**Day counts** are whole integers obtained by truncation toward zero — that is, by discarding any partial day. `tolled_days`, `days_to_file`, and the day counts used for interest accrual and for EIS event ageing are all computed by dividing an elapsed duration by a whole day and discarding the remainder. Similarly, `notice_hours` derives from an elapsed duration divided by a whole hour with the remainder discarded, and `duration_minutes` from an elapsed duration divided by a whole minute with the remainder discarded. Adding a day count to a timestamp preserves the original time of day.

**Suspension days** are whole integers throughout the discipline matrix. The one rounding step inside the matrix — the mitigation credit — is rounded half up to zero decimal places, so a credit of 6.5 days becomes 7. Percentages inside the matrix and inside the interest calculation are exact decimals, never binary floating-point approximations.

Boundary inclusivity is specified individually for every comparison in Section 3 and is never approximate. As a general orientation: notice sufficiency, retention expiry, appeal timeliness, indicator breach and plan success are all evaluated with non-strict comparisons (the exact boundary value passes), while the preponderance standard, small-cell suppression and room capacity are evaluated with strict comparisons.

### 2.6. Version Pinning on Supersession Writes

Two write operations in this system legitimately supersede a decision another user may already have made, and both therefore require the caller to prove which version of the parent record it was looking at. This is optimistic concurrency control on the parent, and it applies only to these two operations — no other endpoint accepts or requires a version field of any kind.

Assigning an investigator requires `expected_investigation_version` as a non-optional field of the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Investigation`. If that value does not equal the investigation's current `version`, the write is rejected with `ERR_STALE_INVESTIGATION_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and nothing is mutated — no assignee is written, no caseload counter moves, and the investigation's own `version` does not advance. The pin matters because reassignment is a legitimate operation in its own right: an assignment write carrying a stale version would otherwise silently supersede an assignment the client never observed, and would double-count the caseload ledger by crediting a new assignee without debiting the one the client did not know about.

Creating an intervention plan against an alert requires `expected_alert_version` as a non-optional field of the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `EarlyInterventionAlert`. If that value does not equal the alert's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ALERT_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and no plan row is written. Here too the pin exists because the operation supersedes: re-planning an alert in `ESCALATED` legitimately replaces the plan that failed, so a stale-version write would attach a second `DRAFT` plan to an alert state the client has not observed.

Both accepted writes advance the parent's `version` by exactly 1, so a client that wishes to write twice in a row must re-read the parent — or read the returned resource — between the two writes.

### 2.7. Nested Paths and the Order in Which Rejections Are Decided

Several resources are addressed through a parent path. Path parameters are resolved strictly left to right, and the leftmost unresolvable segment decides the error. A request naming a complaint that does not exist fails with `ERR_COMPLAINT_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) whatever else is wrong with the body; the same principle yields `ERR_OFFICER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INVESTIGATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ALLEGATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SESSION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DISCIPLINE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPEAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REINSTATEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ALERT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CHECKIN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DISCLOSURE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_UNIT_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_ROOM_NOT_FOUND` for their respective resources, each with HTTP 404.

Because most operations in this system carry many guards at once, the order in which those guards fire is part of the contract. Validation proceeds strictly in ascending priority tier; **the first violated rule determines the response, and no later rule is evaluated.** Two requests that violate several rules at once must therefore produce the same, predictable error. The thirteen tiers are:

1. **Header presence** — both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` present and non-empty, on every endpoint. Failure: `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. **Role authorization** — `X-User-Role` names a declared role and that role is authorized on the target endpoint, on every endpoint. Failure: `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403).
3. **Path-parameter existence** — every `{id}` in the URL resolves. A complaints path segment must resolve to a `Complaint` on the single-complaint read, allegation creation, classification, investigation opening, withdrawal and purge; an officers path segment must resolve to an `Officer` on the single-officer read, the activity-event list, the EIS profile read, activity-event creation and the employment-status write; an investigations path segment must resolve to an `Investigation` on the single-investigation read, assignment, tolling, interview-session creation, findings and close; an allegations path segment must resolve to an `Allegation` on the single-allegation read and the disposition write; an interview-sessions path segment must resolve to an `InterviewSession` on the single-session read, hold and postpone; a disciplines path segment must resolve to a `Discipline` on the single-discipline read, impose and appeal filing; an appeals path segment must resolve to an `Appeal` on the single-appeal read, the reinstatement list, hear and decide; a reinstatements path segment must resolve to a `Reinstatement` on finalize; an eis-alerts path segment must resolve to an `EarlyInterventionAlert` on the single-alert read, dismiss and plan creation; an intervention-plans path segment must resolve to an `InterventionPlan` on the single-plan read, the check-in list, activate and close; a check-ins path segment must resolve to a `MonitoringCheckIn` on complete; and a credibility-disclosures path segment must resolve to a `CredibilityDisclosure` on the single-disclosure read and release.
4. **Schema** — on every endpoint, the body parses, every field the endpoint requires is present, and every supplied value matches its declared type, its enum domain and its numeric range. The declared ranges are: `certification_level` from 0 through 3, `priority` from 1 through 3, `aggravator_count` and `mitigator_count` each from 0 through 5, `required_checkin_count` from 2 through 6, `duration_days` exactly one of 30, 60 or 90, `sworn_headcount` at least 1, `concurrent_capacity` at least 1, `max_case_load` at least 0, `annual_salary_cents` at least 0, `evidence_weight_for` and `evidence_weight_against` each at least 0 with no upper bound, `limit` from 1 through 100 and `offset` at least 0. Failure: `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400).
5. **Body-reference existence** — every foreign key inside the body resolves. `reporting_supervisor_officer_id` on intake, `subject_officer_id` on allegation creation and on interview-session creation, `investigator_officer_id` on assignment, and `supervisor_officer_id` on plan creation must each resolve to an `Officer`, failing with `ERR_OFFICER_NOT_FOUND` (404); `subject_unit_id` on intake and `unit_id` on officer creation must resolve to a `Unit`, failing with `ERR_UNIT_NOT_FOUND` (404); and `hearing_room_id` on interview-session creation and on postponement must resolve to a `HearingRoom`, failing with `ERR_ROOM_NOT_FOUND` (404).
6. **Resource-level ownership** — the acting user is the right individual, not merely the right role. `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_INVESTIGATOR` (403) guards findings submission, interview-session creation and interview hold. `ERR_NOT_PLAN_SUPERVISOR` (403) guards plan activation, plan closure and check-in completion whenever the acting role is `Supervisor`. `ERR_INTAKE_CODE_MISMATCH` (403) guards withdrawal: when the complaint's `intake_reference_code` is non-null the supplied `intake_reference_code` must equal it, and because an anonymous complaint stores no code at all, an anonymous withdrawal passes this tier and falls through to the tier-12 anonymous-withdrawal guard.
7. **Version pinning** — `expected_investigation_version` must equal the investigation's `version` on assignment, failing with `ERR_STALE_INVESTIGATION_VERSION` (412); `expected_alert_version` must equal the alert's `version` on plan creation, failing with `ERR_STALE_ALERT_VERSION` (412).
8. **State legality** — the target resource is in a state from which the requested operation is a declared transition. This tier produces `ERR_COMPLAINT_STATE_INVALID` (409), `ERR_COMPLAINT_NOT_PURGEABLE` (409), `ERR_INVESTIGATION_STATE_INVALID` (409), `ERR_INVESTIGATION_NOT_INTERVIEWABLE` (409), `ERR_OFFICER_SEPARATED` (409), `ERR_SESSION_STATE_INVALID` (409), `ERR_ALLEGATION_ALREADY_DISPOSED` (409), `ERR_DISCIPLINE_STATE_INVALID` (409), `ERR_APPEAL_STATE_INVALID` (409), `ERR_REINSTATEMENT_ALREADY_FINALIZED` (409), `ERR_ALERT_STATE_INVALID` (409), `ERR_PLAN_STATE_INVALID` (409), `ERR_CHECKIN_NOT_PENDING` (409) and `ERR_DISCLOSURE_NOT_ACTIVE` (409). Section 3 gives the legal from-states for each; the scopes are: complaint-state checks on allegation creation, classification, investigation opening and withdrawal; the purgeability check on purge; investigation-state checks on assignment, tolling, findings and close; the interviewability check on interview-session creation; the separation check on interview-session creation, interview hold, imposition and the employment-status write; the session-state check on hold and postpone; the disposition check on the disposition write; the discipline-state check on impose and appeal filing; the appeal-state check on hear and decide; the reinstatement check on finalize; the alert-state check on dismiss and plan creation; the plan-state check on activate and close; the check-in check on complete; and the disclosure-status check on release.
9. **Business safety** — the integrity guards that protect the fairness of the process: `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403) on the disposition write and on imposition; `ERR_INVESTIGATOR_CONFLICT` (422) on assignment; `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_NOTICE` (422) on interview-session creation, postponement and hold; `ERR_REPRESENTATION_NOT_PRESENT` (409) on hold; and `ERR_CREDIBILITY_HOLD` (409) together with `ERR_APPEAL_PENDING` (409) on purge.
10. **Referential business rules** — `ERR_NO_ALLEGATIONS` (422) and `ERR_INVESTIGATION_ALREADY_EXISTS` (409) on investigation opening; `ERR_FINDINGS_INCOMPLETE` (422) and `ERR_NO_INTERVIEW_HELD` (422) on findings; `ERR_PENDING_DISPOSITIONS` (409) on investigation close; `ERR_NO_INTAKE_UNIT` (422) on intake and on classification; `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_INVESTIGATOR` (422) on assignment; `ERR_ALERT_ALREADY_OPEN` (409) on the early-intervention sweep; `ERR_PENDING_CHECKINS` (409) on plan closure; and `ERR_PLAN_EXISTS` (409) on alert dismissal.
11. **Temporal rules** — `ERR_STATUTE_EXPIRED` (422) on intake; `ERR_EVENT_IN_FUTURE` (422) on activity-event creation; `ERR_INVALID_SESSION_WINDOW` (422), `ERR_OFFICER_REST_BUFFER_CONFLICT` (409) and `ERR_ROOM_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409) on interview-session creation and on postponement; `ERR_INTERVIEW_NOT_STARTED` (409) on hold; `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_CLOSED` (422) on appeal filing; `ERR_RETENTION_NOT_EXPIRED` (409) on purge; and `ERR_INVALID_REPORT_PERIOD` (422) on the outcome report.
12. **Business constraints** — `ERR_PREPONDERANCE_NOT_MET` (422) and `ERR_DISPOSITION_NOT_SUPPORTED` (422) on the disposition write; `ERR_MAX_POSTPONEMENTS_REACHED` (409) on postponement; `ERR_MAX_RECLASSIFICATIONS_REACHED` (409) on classification; `ERR_COMPLAINANT_IDENTITY_REQUIRED` (422) on intake; `ERR_ANONYMOUS_NOT_WITHDRAWABLE` (422) and `ERR_SUSTAINED_NOT_WITHDRAWABLE` (409) on withdrawal; `ERR_BADGE_NUMBER_DUPLICATE` (409) on officer creation; `ERR_CASE_LOAD_EXCEEDED` (422) and `ERR_CERTIFICATION_INSUFFICIENT` (422) on assignment; and `ERR_MODIFIED_DAYS_INVALID` (422) on the appeal decision.
13. **Fiscal constraints** — `ERR_INVALID_INTERIM_EARNINGS` (422) and `ERR_SALARY_UNKNOWN` (422) on reinstatement finalization.

Three pairings inside this ladder deserve calling out because they are the ones most often mis-ordered. On purge, the credibility hold at tier 9 is evaluated **before** the retention-expiry check at tier 11: a file that is both still inside its retention window and encumbered by an active disclosure reports `ERR_CREDIBILITY_HOLD`, not `ERR_RETENTION_NOT_EXPIRED`. On the disposition write, segregation of duties at tier 9 is evaluated **before** the preponderance test at tier 12: a commander who investigated the case and also lacks a preponderance receives `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. And on interview-session creation, the interviewability state check at tier 8 is evaluated **before** the officer rest-buffer conflict at tier 11: a session requested against an investigation that is not open reports `ERR_INVESTIGATION_NOT_INTERVIEWABLE` even when the requested times would also have collided with an existing session.

### 2.8. Cascade Integrity and Ledger Conservation

Many operations in this system are one client write and several record mutations. Cascades are always executed inside the originating request — the consistency model is immediate, and there is no deferred or eventual settlement anywhere. When a cascade mutates a record, that record's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` advances, exactly as if it had been written directly.

Five counters on the `Officer` record are ledgers, meaning their value is not merely informational but is maintained by paired increments and decrements that must balance:

- `open_case_load` counts the investigations currently assigned to the officer whose `status` is `ASSIGNED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `TOLLED` or `PENDING_REVIEW`. It rises by 1 when an investigation is assigned to the officer and falls by 1 when that investigation leaves the open set — on close, on void, or on the officer being displaced by a reassignment. Because a reassignment debits the outgoing assignee in the same request in which it credits the incoming one, the sum of `open_case_load` across all officers is conserved by reassignment.
- `imposed_suspension_days_total` is the sum of `effective_days` across the officer's disciplines whose `status` is `IMPOSED`, `UPHELD` or `MODIFIED`. Imposition adds, an upheld appeal leaves it untouched, a modified appeal adjusts it by the difference between the reduced and the original award, and an overturned appeal subtracts the original award in full — restoring exactly the pre-imposition value.
- `open_alert_count` counts the officer's early-intervention alerts whose `status` is `OPEN`, `PLAN_REQUIRED`, `PLAN_ACTIVE` or `ESCALATED`. Alert creation increments it; dismissal and a successful plan closure decrement it; an escalation does not, because an escalated alert is still open business.
- `intervention_escalation_count` counts the officer's intervention plans that closed in `ESCALATED`, and only ever rises.
- `credibility_disclosure_active` is true exactly when at least one `CredibilityDisclosure` for the officer is in `ACTIVE`. It is recomputed — not merely toggled — whenever a disclosure is created, expunged or expired, so that expunging one of two active disclosures leaves the flag true.

Two counters on the `Complaint` record work the same way: `allegation_count` is the number of allegations attached to the complaint, and `sustained_allegation_count` is the number of those allegations whose `disposition` is `SUSTAINED` — which means it falls by 1 when an appeal overturns a sustained finding, because an allegation in `OVERTURNED_ON_APPEAL` is no longer sustained.

### 2.9. Controlled Vocabularies

Enumerated values are exact, case-sensitive strings. A value outside its domain is a schema failure and yields `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

A `Unit` has a `unit_role` of `INTERNAL_AFFAIRS`, `PATROL_DIVISION` or `SPECIAL_OPERATIONS`. An `Officer` has a `rank` of `OFFICER`, `SERGEANT`, `LIEUTENANT` or `CAPTAIN`; an `assignment_type` of `PATROL`, `INVESTIGATIVE` or `ADMIN`; a `demographic_code` of `DG_A`, `DG_B`, `DG_C` or `DG_D`; and an `employment_status` of `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED`, `RELIEVED_OF_DUTY` or `SEPARATED`, alongside an `eis_alert_level` of `NONE`, `WATCH`, `ALERT` or `CRITICAL`.

A `Complaint` has an `intake_channel` of `PUBLIC`, `SUPERVISOR` or `ANONYMOUS`; a `complainant_demographic_code` of `DG_A`, `DG_B`, `DG_C`, `DG_D` or `UNKNOWN`, defaulting to `UNKNOWN` when the caller omits it; a `classification` of `CLASS_I`, `CLASS_II` or `CLASS_III`; and a `status` of `INTAKE_RECEIVED`, `SCREENING`, `CLASSIFIED`, `UNDER_INVESTIGATION`, `ADJUDICATION`, `CLOSED`, `REJECTED_INTAKE`, `WITHDRAWN` or `PURGED`, of which `REJECTED_INTAKE`, `WITHDRAWN` and `PURGED` are terminal.

An `Allegation` has an `allegation_type` — and every member of a complaint's `reported_allegation_types` array is drawn from the same domain — of `EXCESSIVE_FORCE`, `UNLAWFUL_SEARCH`, `UNTRUTHFULNESS`, `NEGLECT_OF_DUTY`, `PURSUIT_VIOLATION` or `DISCOURTESY`; an `offense_class` of `A`, `B`, `C` or `D`, where `A` is the most severe matrix row and `D` the least; and a `disposition` of `PENDING`, `SUSTAINED`, `NOT_SUSTAINED`, `UNFOUNDED`, `EXONERATED`, `POLICY_FAILURE`, `WITHDRAWN` or `OVERTURNED_ON_APPEAL`. `PENDING` is the initial value; `NOT_SUSTAINED`, `UNFOUNDED`, `EXONERATED`, `POLICY_FAILURE`, `WITHDRAWN` and `OVERTURNED_ON_APPEAL` are terminal.

An `Investigation` has a `status` of `UNASSIGNED`, `ASSIGNED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `TOLLED`, `PENDING_REVIEW`, `CLOSED` or `VOIDED`, of which `CLOSED` and `VOIDED` are terminal. An `InterviewSession` has a `status` of `SCHEDULED`, `HELD`, `POSTPONED` or `CANCELLED`, of which `HELD` and `CANCELLED` are terminal, and a `representation_status` of `WAIVED`, `REP_REQUESTED` or `REP_PRESENT`, defaulting to `REP_REQUESTED`.

A `Discipline` has a `status` of `PROPOSED`, `IMPOSED`, `APPEALED`, `UPHELD`, `MODIFIED` or `OVERTURNED`, of which the last three are terminal. An `Appeal` has a `forum` of `ARBITRATION` or `CIVIL_SERVICE`, a `status` of `FILED`, `HEARD` or `DECIDED` — `DECIDED` being terminal — and an `outcome` of `PENDING`, `UPHELD`, `MODIFIED` or `OVERTURNED`, defaulting to `PENDING`. A `Reinstatement` has a `status` of `PENDING` or `FINALIZED`.

An `OfficerActivityEvent` has an `event_type` of `USE_OF_FORCE`, `PURSUIT`, `SICK_LEAVE_DAY`, `COMPLAINT_INTAKE` or `COMPLAINT_SUSTAINED`, and a `severity_code` of `LEVEL_1`, `LEVEL_2`, `LEVEL_3` or `NA`, defaulting to `NA`.

An `EarlyInterventionAlert` has a `trigger_source` of `EVENT_INGEST`, `EIS_RUN`, `PLAN_ESCALATION` or `DISPOSITION_CASCADE`; a `score_variant` of `ROLLING_12M` or `PEER_RELATIVE`; an `alert_level` of `WATCH`, `ALERT` or `CRITICAL` (an alert row is never created at the `NONE` band); and a `status` of `OPEN`, `PLAN_REQUIRED`, `PLAN_ACTIVE`, `CLOSED_SUCCESS`, `ESCALATED` or `DISMISSED`, of which `CLOSED_SUCCESS` and `DISMISSED` are terminal.

An `InterventionPlan` has a `status` of `DRAFT`, `ACTIVE`, `CLOSED_SUCCESS` or `ESCALATED` — the last two terminal — and an `interventions` array whose every member is one of `RETRAINING`, `COUNSELING`, `PEER_REVIEW`, `REASSIGNMENT` or `EAP_REFERRAL`. A `MonitoringCheckIn` has a `status` of `PENDING`, `COMPLETED` or `MISSED` and an `outcome` of `PENDING`, `SATISFACTORY` or `CONCERN`, both defaulting to `PENDING`.

A `CredibilityDisclosure` has a `basis` of `UNTRUTHFULNESS_SUSTAINED`, `BIAS_SUSTAINED` or `EVIDENCE_MISHANDLING_SUSTAINED`, and a `status` of `ACTIVE`, `EXPUNGED` or `EXPIRED`, of which the last two are terminal.

Finally, four request-only vocabularies. `screening_outcome` on a classification decision is `ACCEPT` or `REJECT`. `action` on a tolling write is `SUSPEND` or `RESUME`. `variant` on the EIS profile read is `ROLLING_12M` or `PEER_RELATIVE` and defaults to `ROLLING_12M`. `breakdown` on the outcome report is `DEMOGRAPHIC` or `CHANNEL`, and when omitted the report is produced as a single ungrouped total.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Reference Data and the Officer Record

Before any complaint can be taken, a `SystemAdmin` seeds the department's structure. Registering a command unit supplies its display `name`, its `unit_role`, its bureau `command_code` — which is unique across units — its `peer_group_code`, and its `sworn_headcount`, which must be at least 1. A sixth field, `intake_enabled`, defaults to true when omitted and is the switch that takes a unit out of the routing pool: a unit whose `intake_enabled` is false is never an eligible routing target, even for a complaint that names it. The `peer_group_code` has no effect on routing at all; it exists solely to define the peer-comparison cohort used by the peer-relative risk score, which pools every unit sharing the same code. Units are immutable once registered, and they are listed with optional `unit_role` and `peer_group_code` filters.

Hearing rooms are seeded the same way, with a `facility_code`, a `room_name`, a `concurrent_capacity` of at least 1 and a `recording_enabled` flag that defaults to true and records whether the room supports a recorded interview. `concurrent_capacity` is the load-bearing field: it is the maximum number of interview sessions whose occupied windows may overlap inside that room at one time, and Section 3.5 describes exactly how the overlap is counted. Hearing rooms are also immutable, and are listed with an optional `facility_code` filter.

Creating a sworn officer record requires the officer's `unit_id`, `badge_number`, `full_name`, `rank`, `assignment_type`, `annual_salary_cents`, `demographic_code` and `hire_date`, the last of which is an ISO 8601 UTC date of hire. Three further fields are optional and carry defaults: `is_investigator` defaults to false, `certification_level` defaults to 0, and `max_case_load` defaults to 5. Together those three decide whether the officer can ever carry an internal investigation — `is_investigator` gates eligibility at all, `certification_level` must meet the level the case classification demands, and `max_case_load` is the ceiling on how many open investigations the officer may hold at any one time. `annual_salary_cents` is the annual base salary in cents and is the sole basis for any back-pay award, which is why it may not be negative. `badge_number` must be unique across every officer record; a duplicate is refused with `ERR_BADGE_NUMBER_DUPLICATE`. `demographic_code` records the officer's demographic cohort and is used only in aggregate reporting.

A newly created officer starts with every derived field at its zero point: `employment_status` is `ACTIVE`, `eis_score` is 0.00, `eis_alert_level` is `NONE`, `open_case_load` is 0, `open_alert_count` is 0, `imposed_suspension_days_total` is 0, `intervention_escalation_count` is 0, `credibility_disclosure_active` is false, and `suspension_end_date` is null. Officers are listed with optional `unit_id`, `is_investigator`, `employment_status`, `eis_alert_level` and `credibility_disclosure_active` filters — the exact combination an internal-affairs commander needs when screening for an available, certified, unencumbered investigator — and reading a single officer returns the whole stored risk ledger alongside the identity fields.

An `Officer` evolves along **two independent state dimensions**, and confusing them is the most common modelling mistake in this domain. `employment_status` is the personnel dimension; `eis_alert_level` is the risk dimension. Neither constrains the other: a `SEPARATED` officer keeps accumulating EIS history for reporting purposes, and an officer at `CRITICAL` risk remains `ACTIVE` unless discipline or an administrative decision moves the employment dimension.

A `SystemAdmin` moves the employment dimension by supplying the target `employment_status` and an optional `reason`. The legal moves are as follows. `ACTIVE` becomes `SUSPENDED` only as a consequence of imposed discipline, never by direct administrative write. `SUSPENDED` returns to `ACTIVE` either through an administrative write or through the reversal cascade described in Section 3.7; on either path `suspension_end_date` is cleared. An officer who is `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` may be moved to `RELIEVED_OF_DUTY`, and that officer remains fully interviewable and remains an eligible subject of discipline — relief from duty is a precaution, not an exit. Any officer who is not already `SEPARATED` may be moved to `SEPARATED`, at which point the officer stops being an eligible investigator candidate and stops being interviewable, while every open investigation already assigned to that officer is left exactly as it was and the caseload ledger is not adjusted. `SEPARATED` is terminal: every further employment-status write on that officer is refused with `ERR_OFFICER_SEPARATED`, including a write that merely repeats `SEPARATED`.

### 3.2. Complaint Intake: Classification, Priority and Routing

Intake is the busiest single operation in the system, because it takes a narrative from a stranger and turns it into a routed, prioritised, deadline-bearing case file in one request. A `Complainant`, a `Supervisor` or an `IACommander` submits the `intake_channel`, the free-text `narrative`, the `incident_datetime` at which the alleged conduct occurred, the `alleged_injury` flag, a non-empty `reported_allegation_types` array and the `subject_unit_id` the complaint concerns. Four further fields are conditional: `complainant_name` and `complainant_contact`, the optional `complainant_demographic_code`, and `reporting_supervisor_officer_id`.

The conditional fields follow the channel. When `intake_channel` is `PUBLIC` or `SUPERVISOR`, both `complainant_name` and `complainant_contact` must be present and non-empty; when it is `ANONYMOUS`, both must be absent. Either violation is `ERR_COMPLAINANT_IDENTITY_REQUIRED`. `reporting_supervisor_officer_id` is required when `intake_channel` is `SUPERVISOR` and forbidden on any other channel — a departure from that pairing is a schema failure — and when supplied it must resolve to an existing officer. `complainant_demographic_code` is optional and stores `UNKNOWN` when omitted, which matters later because `UNKNOWN` forms its own reporting group rather than being excluded.

Filing is time-barred. An incident may be complained of for one year: the supplied `incident_datetime` must be at or after the logical clock less 365 whole days, and an older incident is refused with `ERR_STATUTE_EXPIRED`. The same 365-day figure is stored forward as `statute_expires_at`, which is `incident_datetime` plus 365 whole days — the deadline for filing about this incident, entirely separate from the investigation's own due date. `received_at` is stamped from the logical clock at intake and becomes the anchor for the statutory investigation window.

Classification then runs in five steps, and the order is not negotiable.

**Step one — the severity band.** Three checks are evaluated top to bottom and the first match wins. The complaint is `CLASS_I` when `alleged_injury` is true, or when at least one member of `reported_allegation_types` is `EXCESSIVE_FORCE`, `UNLAWFUL_SEARCH` or `UNTRUTHFULNESS`. Otherwise it is `CLASS_II` when at least one reported type is `NEGLECT_OF_DUTY` or `PURSUIT_VIOLATION`. Otherwise — which in practice means only `DISCOURTESY` was reported — it is `CLASS_III`. Because injury is tested first, a complaint reporting only `NEGLECT_OF_DUTY` but alleging injury classifies `CLASS_I`, not `CLASS_II`: injury outranks the moderate band. The order of members inside `reported_allegation_types` is irrelevant.

**Step two — the override.** On a later classification decision the commander may supply a `classification_override`; when present and different from the value step one would produce or the value already stored, the override replaces the step-one result. The override never bypasses step one — step one still runs, so that a stored `classification` is always defined even when no override is sent.

**Step three — the priority.** `priority` is derived mechanically from `classification`: 1 for `CLASS_I`, 2 for `CLASS_II` and 3 for `CLASS_III`, where 1 is the most urgent.

**Step four — the screening gate.** `screening_required` is true when, and only when, `classification` is `CLASS_III` **and** `intake_channel` is `ANONYMOUS`. Every other combination yields false. Anonymous minor complaints are held for corroboration before any investigation exists, which is why they land in `SCREENING` rather than `CLASSIFIED`.

**Step five — the routing target.** `assigned_unit_id` is chosen by three rules evaluated top to bottom, first match wins. When `classification` is `CLASS_I`, or when `screening_required` is true, the target is the unit whose `unit_role` is `INTERNAL_AFFAIRS` with the earliest `created_at` — the subject unit never investigates a Class I complaint about itself. Otherwise, when the unit identified by `subject_unit_id` has `intake_enabled` true, the target is that same `subject_unit_id`. Otherwise the target is again the `INTERNAL_AFFAIRS` unit with the earliest `created_at`. Where several internal-affairs units exist, `sworn_headcount` is the department's ranking key for describing their relative capacity, but the tie-break that actually selects the routing target is the earliest `created_at`. If the department has registered no unit at all whose `unit_role` is `INTERNAL_AFFAIRS`, there is nowhere to route and the request is refused with `ERR_NO_INTAKE_UNIT`.

Intake also issues the withdrawal credential. `intake_sequence` is a monotonic counter: each accepted intake takes the previous maximum plus 1, and the very first accepted intake takes 1. `intake_reference_code` is that sequence rendered as the four literal characters `CMP-` followed by the sequence zero-padded to six digits — sequence 123 yields `CMP-000123`. Anonymous intake is the exception: the sequence is still assigned, but `intake_reference_code` stays null, and an anonymous complainant consequently holds no credential with which to withdraw.

A complaint's lifecycle formally begins at `INTAKE_RECEIVED`, but no client ever observes that value: inside the same intake request the complaint moves to `SCREENING` when `screening_required` is true, and to `CLASSIFIED` otherwise. On the screening path the routing target is forced to the internal-affairs unit regardless of `subject_unit_id`, and `intake_reference_code` remains null because the channel is anonymous. On the direct path the classification, priority and routing target are all written and the reference code is issued. On both paths `allegation_count`, `sustained_allegation_count` and `reclassification_count` start at 0, and `closed_at`, `retention_until` and `purged_at` are null. Complaints are listed with optional `assigned_unit_id`, `subject_unit_id`, `reporting_supervisor_officer_id`, `status`, `classification` and `intake_channel` filters.

Naming the accused officer is a separate act. An `IACommander` or a `Supervisor` attaches an allegation to a complaint that is in `SCREENING`, `CLASSIFIED` or `UNDER_INVESTIGATION`, supplying the `subject_officer_id`, the `allegation_type`, an allegation-specific `narrative`, and the `offense_class` — the discipline-matrix row the classifying commander assigns, stored verbatim and later copied onto the discipline record. Attempting to attach an allegation to a complaint in any other state is `ERR_COMPLAINT_STATE_INVALID`. Each accepted allegation raises the complaint's `allegation_count` by 1 and starts life with `disposition` `PENDING`, `evidence_weight_for` and `evidence_weight_against` both 0, `support_pct` 0.00, `redacted` false, and `policy_compliant`, `disposed_at` and `prior_sustained_count_at_disposition` all unset. Allegations are listed with optional `complaint_id`, `subject_officer_id`, `disposition` and `allegation_type` filters, and a single allegation read returns its evidence weights and disposition.

Attaching an allegation also has a consequence the caller does not ask for: the system appends a `system_generated` activity event of type `COMPLAINT_INTAKE` for the named subject officer, timestamped with `occurred_at` equal to the owning complaint's `received_at` rather than the current clock, and then recomputes that officer's `eis_score` and `eis_alert_level`. Being complained about is itself a risk indicator, and the early-intervention layer must see it the moment the allegation is filed rather than waiting for an outcome.

### 3.3. Screening, Reclassification and Withdrawal

An `IACommander` disposes of a screening decision by supplying a `screening_outcome` of `ACCEPT` or `REJECT`, together with an optional `classification_override` and an optional `rejection_reason`. The same capability serves three distinct purposes, distinguished by the complaint's current status and by the fields supplied.

Accepting a complaint that sits in `SCREENING` moves it `SCREENING → CLASSIFIED`. The full classification and routing computation is re-run — with the `classification_override` applied when one was supplied — and `screening_required` is set back to false, because the screening question has now been answered.

Rejecting a complaint that sits in `SCREENING` moves it `SCREENING → REJECTED_INTAKE`, and requires a `rejection_reason`. Three things happen together: `closed_at` is stamped from the logical clock, every allegation on the complaint is set to `disposition` `WITHDRAWN`, and `retention_until` is set to `closed_at` plus the withdrawal retention class of 730 whole days — two years for a file that never became a case.

Accepting a complaint that is already `CLASSIFIED` or `UNDER_INVESTIGATION`, with a `classification_override` supplied, is a **reclassification**: a self-loop that leaves the complaint's status exactly where it was — `CLASSIFIED` stays `CLASSIFIED`, and `UNDER_INVESTIGATION` stays `UNDER_INVESTIGATION`. A reclassification is accepted only when `reclassification_count` is strictly less than 2 and the supplied `classification_override` differs from the currently stored `classification`; either failure is `ERR_MAX_RECLASSIFICATIONS_REACHED`. On acceptance `reclassification_count` rises by 1, and `priority` and `assigned_unit_id` are recomputed from the new classification by the same five-step rule — so a complaint reclassified upward to `CLASS_I` is pulled away from its subject unit and re-routed to internal affairs.

Reclassification is the system's most far-reaching cascade, because the statutory window and the certification bar are both derived from the classification. When the complaint already has an investigation, that investigation's `statutory_days` and `required_certification_level` are recomputed from the new classification while the accumulated `tolled_days` is preserved untouched, and `due_at` is then recomputed from the complaint's `received_at`. And when the recomputed `required_certification_level` now exceeds the current assignee's `certification_level`, the investigation is forced back to `UNASSIGNED`: `assigned_investigator_officer_id` is cleared, and the displaced assignee's `open_case_load` is decremented by 1. A Class III complaint upgraded to Class I therefore does not merely acquire a longer deadline; it loses its investigator, because a level-1-certified investigator may not carry a Class I case.

Withdrawal is the complainant's own exit. A `Complainant` or a `Supervisor` supplies the `intake_reference_code` issued at intake plus an optional `withdrawal_reason`, and the complaint may be withdrawn from `CLASSIFIED`, from `UNDER_INVESTIGATION` or from `ADJUDICATION` — any other status is `ERR_COMPLAINT_STATE_INVALID`. Three guards then apply in the order the ladder in Section 2.7 sets out. First the credential: when the complaint stores a non-null `intake_reference_code`, the supplied value must equal it, and a mismatch is `ERR_INTAKE_CODE_MISMATCH`. Second the channel: `intake_channel` must be `PUBLIC` or `SUPERVISOR`, and because an anonymous complaint stores no code at all it passes the credential check and is stopped here instead, with `ERR_ANONYMOUS_NOT_WITHDRAWABLE`. Third the substance: `sustained_allegation_count` must be 0, and a complaint that already carries a sustained finding is refused with `ERR_SUSTAINED_NOT_WITHDRAWABLE`. That last guard does real work precisely because withdrawal remains available after findings have been filed — a complainant may change their mind during adjudication, but once any allegation has been sustained the file belongs to the discipline process and is no longer the complainant's to close.

An accepted withdrawal always stamps `closed_at` from the logical clock, sets `retention_until` to `closed_at` plus 730 whole days, and sets every allegation still in `PENDING` to `disposition` `WITHDRAWN`. What else it does depends on how far the case had travelled. Withdrawing from `CLASSIFIED`, before any investigation was opened, does nothing further. Withdrawing from `UNDER_INVESTIGATION` also drives the investigation to `VOIDED` and stamps its `closed_at`; because the investigation became `VOIDED`, every interview session on it in `SCHEDULED` or `POSTPONED` moves to `CANCELLED` and releases the room and officer time it was holding; and because the investigation left the open set, the assignee's `open_case_load` is decremented by 1. Withdrawing from `ADJUDICATION` drives the investigation `PENDING_REVIEW → VOIDED` and stamps its `closed_at`, and again decrements the assignee's `open_case_load` by 1 — but `findings_submitted_at` is deliberately left in place as the historical record of work that really was done, and there are no scheduled sessions left to cancel at that stage.

### 3.4. The Investigation: Opening, Assignment and the Statutory Clock

Opening the investigation is an `IACommander` act and moves the complaint `CLASSIFIED → UNDER_INVESTIGATION`. Only a complaint in `CLASSIFIED` may be opened; any other status is `ERR_COMPLAINT_STATE_INVALID`. Two substantive preconditions follow: the complaint must carry at least one allegation, because an investigation with nothing to investigate is refused with `ERR_NO_ALLEGATIONS`, and no investigation may already exist for that complaint, because a second one would fork the case file and is refused with `ERR_INVESTIGATION_ALREADY_EXISTS`. The commander may attach an `opening_note`. The new investigation records the `complaint_id` of the file it belongs to and is created in `UNASSIGNED` with `assigned_investigator_officer_id` null, `tolled_days` 0, `assignment_count` 0, `interview_held_count` 0, and `findings_submitted_at`, `suspended_at` and `closed_at` all null.

Two derived quantities are written at that moment, both selected purely by the complaint's `classification`. `statutory_days` — the investigation window the state grants — is 180 days for `CLASS_I`, 121 days for `CLASS_II` and 90 days for `CLASS_III`. `required_certification_level` — the internal-affairs certification an assignee must hold — is 3 for `CLASS_I`, 2 for `CLASS_II` and 1 for `CLASS_III`. From those, `due_at` is the complaint's `received_at` advanced by `statutory_days` plus `tolled_days` whole days; at opening `tolled_days` is 0, so a Class I complaint received at the start of September 2026 is due at the end of February 2027. `overdue` is a derived flag, true when the logical clock is strictly after `due_at` and the investigation is neither `CLOSED` nor `VOIDED`; it is re-evaluated on every read, so a case becomes overdue with the passage of the clock and not by any write. Investigations are listed with optional `complaint_id`, `assigned_investigator_officer_id`, `status` and `overdue` filters.

**Assignment.** An `IACommander` assigns the case, and every assignment write must carry `expected_investigation_version` matching the investigation's current `version`, as Section 2.6 requires. The write is legal from `UNASSIGNED`, producing `UNASSIGNED → ASSIGNED`, and from `ASSIGNED`, producing the `ASSIGNED → ASSIGNED` reassignment self-loop; from any other status it is `ERR_INVESTIGATION_STATE_INVALID`. The commander may attach an `assignment_note`. The remaining field, `investigator_officer_id`, is optional, and whether it is supplied changes the shape of the operation entirely.

When `investigator_officer_id` is omitted, the system runs a deterministic selector. An officer is a candidate only when all eight of the following hold: `is_investigator` is true; `employment_status` is `ACTIVE`; `certification_level` is at least the investigation's `required_certification_level`; `open_case_load` is strictly less than `max_case_load`, which is the same statement as saying that adding one more case would not exceed the ceiling; the officer is not the `subject_officer_id` of any allegation on the complaint; the officer's `unit_id` differs from the `unit_id` of every subject officer on the complaint; the officer's `id` differs from the acting commander's `X-User-ID`; and the officer's `id` differs from the current `assigned_investigator_officer_id`, which is what makes the reassignment self-loop a genuine change of assignee rather than a no-op. The eligible pool is then sorted on four keys applied in this exact order: `open_case_load` ascending, then `certification_level` descending, then `badge_number` ascending in lexicographic byte order, then `created_at` ascending. The first row after that sort is selected. Given three eligible officers with caseloads of 2, 1 and 1 and certification levels of 3, 2 and 3, the third officer wins: the two caseloads of 1 tie on the first key, and the higher certification level of 3 breaks the tie on the second. Because `badge_number` is unique across officers the fourth key is a belt-and-braces fallback that is never reached in practice. If the eligible pool is empty — for instance because every officer flagged `is_investigator` already sits at their `max_case_load` — the request is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_INVESTIGATOR`, the investigation stays exactly where it was, and no caseload counter moves.

When `investigator_officer_id` **is** supplied, the selector is skipped but all eight conditions are still enforced individually, and each failure now reports its own specific reason rather than the generic empty-pool error. A nominated officer who is a subject of the complaint, who shares a `unit_id` with any subject officer, or who is the acting commander is refused with `ERR_INVESTIGATOR_CONFLICT`. A nominated officer whose `open_case_load` already equals `max_case_load` is refused with `ERR_CASE_LOAD_EXCEEDED`. A nominated officer whose `is_investigator` is false, whose `employment_status` is not `ACTIVE`, or whose `certification_level` falls below the investigation's `required_certification_level` is refused with `ERR_CERTIFICATION_INSUFFICIENT`.

An accepted assignment writes `assigned_investigator_officer_id`, raises `assignment_count` by 1 — the counter includes reassignments, so it is a count of accepted assignment writes rather than of distinct people — credits the new assignee's `open_case_load` by 1, and advances the investigation's `version` by 1. On the reassignment self-loop the outgoing assignee's `open_case_load` is debited by 1 in the same request, so the department-wide caseload total is conserved.

**Tolling the statutory clock.** Either the assigned `Investigator` or an `IACommander` may stop and restart the statutory clock by supplying an `action` of `SUSPEND` or `RESUME`, with an optional `tolling_reason` that must be present when suspending. Suspension is legal only from `IN_PROGRESS` and produces `IN_PROGRESS → TOLLED`, stamping `suspended_at` from the logical clock. Resumption is legal from `TOLLED` and from `PENDING_REVIEW`; from anything else, and likewise for a suspension attempted from a status other than `IN_PROGRESS`, the answer is `ERR_INVESTIGATION_STATE_INVALID`.

Resuming from `TOLLED` produces `TOLLED → IN_PROGRESS` and accrues the tolled time: the elapsed duration from `suspended_at` to the logical clock is converted to whole days with any partial day discarded, that figure is added to `tolled_days`, `suspended_at` is cleared, and `due_at` is recomputed. The recomputation always runs from the complaint's `received_at` plus `statutory_days` plus the new `tolled_days` — never from the previous `due_at` — so a case that is tolled and resumed many times cannot drift. A Class I case suspended on 10 September 2026 and resumed at six in the morning on 17 September has an elapsed span of seven and a quarter days, which floors to 7; `tolled_days` becomes 7 and the due date moves out by exactly a week. The `IN_PROGRESS → TOLLED → IN_PROGRESS` cycle may repeat without limit, and each pass adds to the same accumulator.

Resuming from `PENDING_REVIEW` is a different act with the same verb: it is an `IACommander` returning a filed case for more work, producing `PENDING_REVIEW → IN_PROGRESS`. It is permitted only while no allegation of the complaint has yet been given a disposition other than `PENDING` — once adjudication has begun the file cannot be pulled back. The return clears `findings_submitted_at` and moves the owning complaint back from `ADJUDICATION` to `UNDER_INVESTIGATION`, so findings must be resubmitted in full before the complaint can be adjudicated again. No tolled time accrues on this path, because the clock was never suspended.

The `ASSIGNED → IN_PROGRESS` move, by contrast, is not a tolling act at all: an investigation leaves `ASSIGNED` for `IN_PROGRESS` when the first interview session against it is accepted, or when a still-`ASSIGNED` investigation has a session recorded as held. No ledger changes on that move, because `open_case_load` already counts `ASSIGNED` and `IN_PROGRESS` alike.

### 3.5. Interview Procedure and Officer Rights

A compelled interview is the point at which the collective bargaining agreement and state law bear directly on the mechanics, and the system encodes three separate protections: a written-notice period, a representation requirement, and a rest buffer between sessions.

Only the officer named in `assigned_investigator_officer_id` may schedule a session; anyone else, even another certified investigator, is refused with `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_INVESTIGATOR`. The investigation must be `ASSIGNED` or `IN_PROGRESS`, and a session requested against an investigation in any other status — `UNASSIGNED`, `TOLLED`, `PENDING_REVIEW`, `CLOSED` or `VOIDED` — is refused with `ERR_INVESTIGATION_NOT_INTERVIEWABLE`. The scheduling payload names the `subject_officer_id` to be interviewed, who must be a subject of an allegation on the owning complaint; the `hearing_room_id` the session will occupy; the `scheduled_start` and `scheduled_end` of the session; the `notice_served_at` instant at which written notice was served on the officer; and the `representation_status` observed at booking, which defaults to `REP_REQUESTED`. A subject officer whose `employment_status` is `SEPARATED` can no longer be compelled to attend and the request is refused with `ERR_OFFICER_SEPARATED`; an officer who is `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED` or `RELIEVED_OF_DUTY` remains interviewable.

The requested window must be coherent and in the future: `scheduled_end` must be strictly after `scheduled_start`, and `scheduled_start` must be strictly after the logical clock. Either failure is `ERR_INVALID_SESSION_WINDOW`. From the accepted window the system records `duration_minutes`, the elapsed span from `scheduled_start` to `scheduled_end` in whole minutes with any partial minute discarded.

**Notice.** `notice_hours` is the elapsed span from `notice_served_at` to `scheduled_start` expressed in whole hours with any partial hour discarded, and it is also exposed as a 2-decimal figure on the session. The officer bill-of-rights test is non-strict: `notice_hours` must be at least 48, which is two full days of written notice. Exactly 48 hours passes. Notice served one minute later than that — say at one minute past nine on the eighth of a month for a session starting at nine on the tenth — yields an elapsed span of forty-seven hours and fifty-nine minutes, which floors to 47 and fails with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_NOTICE`.

**The officer rest buffer.** No officer may be interviewed twice back to back, and the department enforces a one-hour cushion — sixty minutes — on each side of every session. The candidate window is widened by that buffer at both ends and treated as a **closed** interval: the widened window runs from sixty minutes before the proposed start to sixty minutes after the proposed end, endpoints included. Every other session of the same subject officer whose status is `SCHEDULED` or `POSTPONED` contributes its own closed interval running from its `scheduled_start` to its `scheduled_end`; sessions in `HELD` or `CANCELLED` contribute nothing at all. Two closed intervals conflict when the first begins at or before the second ends and the second begins at or before the first ends. Because both ends are inclusive, a gap of exactly sixty minutes between the end of one session and the start of the next **is** a conflict — the widened window's left endpoint lands precisely on the earlier session's end instant, and touching counts. A gap of sixty-one minutes is accepted. A conflict is refused with `ERR_OFFICER_REST_BUFFER_CONFLICT`.

**Room capacity.** The room test is deliberately built the other way round. For the requested `hearing_room_id`, the system counts every session in `SCHEDULED` or `POSTPONED` whose window, treated as **half-open** — start included, end excluded — intersects the candidate window treated the same way. Two half-open intervals intersect when the first begins strictly before the second ends and the second begins strictly before the first ends, which means two bookings that merely share a single boundary instant do not intersect at all: a session ending at eleven and another starting at eleven yield a count of zero. The booking is accepted only when that count is **strictly less than** the room's `concurrent_capacity`. A room with capacity 2 already holding one overlapping session accepts the new booking, because 1 is less than 2; the same room holding two overlapping sessions refuses a third with `ERR_ROOM_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, because 2 is not less than 2. The asymmetry between the two rules is intentional: the room rule is a half-open aggregate count and the officer rule is a closed-interval buffered pair test, so the same pair of times can pass one and fail the other.

An accepted session is created in `SCHEDULED` with `postpone_count` 0 and `held_at` and `interview_summary` both null, records the `investigation_id` of the investigation it belongs to, occupies the officer's buffered window and one unit of the room's concurrent capacity for its scheduled window, and — when it is the first accepted session on a still-`ASSIGNED` investigation — moves that investigation `ASSIGNED → IN_PROGRESS`. Sessions are listed with optional `investigation_id`, `subject_officer_id`, `hearing_room_id` and `status` filters.

**Holding the interview.** The assigned investigator records that the interview actually took place, supplying the `interview_summary` and optionally an updated `representation_status` as observed at the door. Five preconditions apply. The session must still be `SCHEDULED`, otherwise `ERR_SESSION_STATE_INVALID`. `notice_hours` must still be at least 48, otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_NOTICE`. `representation_status` must be `WAIVED` or `REP_PRESENT`; an interview may not be held while representation is still `REP_REQUESTED`, and attempting it is `ERR_REPRESENTATION_NOT_PRESENT`. The subject officer must not be `SEPARATED`, otherwise `ERR_OFFICER_SEPARATED`. And the logical clock must be at or after the session's `scheduled_start` — an interview cannot be recorded as held before it was due to begin, and trying is `ERR_INTERVIEW_NOT_STARTED`. On acceptance the session moves `SCHEDULED → HELD`, `held_at` is stamped from the logical clock, the `interview_summary` is stored, the owning investigation's `interview_held_count` rises by 1, the room and officer occupation is released, and a still-`ASSIGNED` investigation moves to `IN_PROGRESS`.

**Postponement.** Either the assigned `Investigator` or the `OfficerRepresentative` may postpone, supplying `new_scheduled_start`, `new_scheduled_end`, `new_notice_served_at` and a `postpone_reason`, plus optionally a `hearing_room_id` to move the session to a different room — omitting it keeps the session in the room it already holds. A single request performs the whole cycle `SCHEDULED → POSTPONED → SCHEDULED`: the session leaves `SCHEDULED`, releases the room and officer time the old times were holding, and returns to `SCHEDULED` with the new times booked. The session must be in `SCHEDULED` to begin with, otherwise `ERR_SESSION_STATE_INVALID`, and `postpone_count` must be strictly less than 2 — the department allows two postponements and no more, so a third attempt is `ERR_MAX_POSTPONEMENTS_REACHED`.

Re-booking is not a lighter test than booking. The new window must satisfy the same coherence rule — `new_scheduled_end` strictly after `new_scheduled_start`, and `new_scheduled_start` strictly after the logical clock — on pain of `ERR_INVALID_SESSION_WINDOW`. Both overlap calculations are re-run against the new times, so the postponed session can be refused with `ERR_OFFICER_REST_BUFFER_CONFLICT` or `ERR_ROOM_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` exactly as an original booking can. And `notice_hours` is recomputed from `new_notice_served_at` to `new_scheduled_start` and re-tested against the same non-strict 48-hour minimum: **the original notice does not carry over to the new date**, so a postponement to a date less than forty-eight hours after the newly served notice fails with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_NOTICE`. An accepted postponement raises `postpone_count` by 1 and rewrites `scheduled_start`, `scheduled_end`, `notice_served_at`, `notice_hours` and `duration_minutes`.

A session in `SCHEDULED` or `POSTPONED` may also end at `CANCELLED` without anyone cancelling it directly: when the owning investigation is driven to `VOIDED` by a complaint withdrawal, every such session moves to `CANCELLED` and releases its room and officer occupation. `HELD` and `CANCELLED` are both terminal.

### 3.6. Findings and Adjudication under the Preponderance Standard

Findings close out the investigative phase. The assigned `Investigator` — and only that officer, on pain of `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_INVESTIGATOR` — submits an `allegation_findings` array together with an optional narrative `summary`. The investigation must be `IN_PROGRESS`, otherwise `ERR_INVESTIGATION_STATE_INVALID`, and the owning complaint must correspondingly be `UNDER_INVESTIGATION`. Findings may not be filed before the subject officer has actually been heard: `interview_held_count` must be at least 1, and a premature submission is refused with `ERR_NO_INTERVIEW_HELD`.

The `allegation_findings` array must cover the complaint's allegations exactly — one entry for every allegation, with no duplicates and no entries for allegations belonging to some other complaint. Anything else is `ERR_FINDINGS_INCOMPLETE`. Each entry identifies its `allegation_id` and carries the three findings values: `evidence_weight_for`, the weight of credible evidence supporting the allegation, a number that may not be negative but carries no upper bound — the scale is nominally 0.00 to 100.00, yet larger magnitudes are accepted, as the worked example later in this section relies on, while a negative weight is refused as a tier-4 schema failure; `evidence_weight_against`, the weight of credible evidence rebutting it, under the same rule; and `policy_compliant`, the investigator's flag that the conduct did occur but complied with departmental policy.

From those two weights the system derives the preponderance result on each allegation. The total credible weight is the sum of `evidence_weight_for` and `evidence_weight_against`. When that total is 0 — no credible evidence either way — `support_pct` is 0.00 and `preponderance_met` is false. Otherwise `support_pct` is one hundred times `evidence_weight_for` divided by the total, rounded half up to two decimal places. `preponderance_met` is then true only when `support_pct` is **strictly greater than** 50.00. An exact tie at 50.00 does not meet the standard: the burden of proof rests on the department, so equipoise is a failure to carry it. Note carefully that the comparison is made on the **rounded** `support_pct` and not on the raw ratio — weights of 5001 for and 5000 against give a raw ratio of 50.005 percent, which exceeds the threshold, yet rounds to `support_pct` 50.00 and therefore leaves `preponderance_met` false. Weights of 51 and 49 give `support_pct` 51.00 and `preponderance_met` true.

An accepted submission stamps `findings_submitted_at`, writes `evidence_weight_for`, `evidence_weight_against` and `policy_compliant` onto every allegation and recomputes each one's `support_pct` and `preponderance_met`, moves the investigation `IN_PROGRESS → PENDING_REVIEW`, and moves the owning complaint `UNDER_INVESTIGATION → ADJUDICATION`.

**Adjudication.** An `IACommander` decides one allegation at a time, supplying the `disposition`, an optional `rationale`, and optionally the `aggravator_count` and `mitigator_count` that Section 3.7 consumes. The owning complaint must be in `ADJUDICATION`, the allegation's `disposition` must still be `PENDING` — a second decision on the same allegation is `ERR_ALLEGATION_ALREADY_DISPOSED` — and segregation of duties is absolute: the acting commander's `X-User-ID` may be neither the investigation's `assigned_investigator_officer_id` nor the allegation's `subject_officer_id`, because the officer who built the case may not decide it and no officer may decide their own case. Either match is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, and that check fires ahead of the evidentiary tests, so a commander who both investigated the case and lacks a preponderance is told about the conflict rather than about the evidence.

Every accepted disposition stamps `disposed_at` from the logical clock and records `prior_sustained_count_at_disposition`, the number of prior sustained allegations against the same subject officer inside the presumptive look-back window. Beyond that, the five commander-driven branches differ sharply, and the difference in their side effects is the single most consequential asymmetry in the system:

- **`PENDING → SUSTAINED`** requires `preponderance_met` to be true; a sustained finding without a preponderance is refused with `ERR_PREPONDERANCE_NOT_MET`. Acceptance raises the complaint's `sustained_allegation_count` by 1 and fires five cascades. First, a `Discipline` row is created in `PROPOSED`, with `offense_class` copied verbatim from the allegation and with `presumptive_prior_tier` and `presumptive_days` computed under the presumptive variant of the matrix. Second, because a sustained finding was recorded, a `system_generated` activity event of type `COMPLAINT_SUSTAINED` is appended for the subject officer, with `occurred_at` equal to the allegation's `disposed_at`. Third, because that event has entered the rolling window, the officer's `eis_score` and `eis_alert_level` are recomputed. Fourth, when that recomputation puts `eis_alert_level` above `NONE`, an `EarlyInterventionAlert` is created with `trigger_source` `DISPOSITION_CASCADE` and the officer's `open_alert_count` rises by 1. Fifth, when the allegation's `allegation_type` is `UNTRUTHFULNESS`, a `CredibilityDisclosure` is created in `ACTIVE` with `permanent_retention` true and the officer's `credibility_disclosure_active` is set to true.
- **`PENDING → NOT_SUSTAINED`** carries no evidentiary precondition beyond the complaint state and the segregation check. Acceptance stamps `disposed_at` and nothing else: no discipline row, no activity event, no early-intervention alert and no credibility disclosure are created. Where the sustained branch fires five cascades, this branch fires none.
- **`PENDING → UNFOUNDED`** requires `evidence_weight_for` to be exactly 0 — the allegation is unfounded precisely when nothing at all supports it — and a request for `UNFOUNDED` with any non-zero supporting weight is refused with `ERR_DISPOSITION_NOT_SUPPORTED`. Acceptance stamps `disposed_at` only, with no cascades.
- **`PENDING → EXONERATED`** requires `policy_compliant` to have been recorded true by the findings; without it the request is refused with `ERR_DISPOSITION_NOT_SUPPORTED`. Acceptance stamps `disposed_at` only, with no cascades.
- **`PENDING → POLICY_FAILURE`** likewise requires `policy_compliant` recorded true, and is refused with `ERR_DISPOSITION_NOT_SUPPORTED` otherwise. Acceptance stamps `disposed_at` only. No discipline is created on this branch either, because the finding attaches to the policy rather than to the officer — the conduct complied with a policy that should not have permitted it.

Two further dispositions exist but are never requested directly. `WITHDRAWN` is written by the system when the owning complaint moves to `WITHDRAWN` or `REJECTED_INTAKE`, and the allegation's `disposed_at` in that case is set to the complaint's `closed_at` rather than to the clock. `OVERTURNED_ON_APPEAL` is written by the system when an appeal reverses the discipline arising from the allegation, as Section 3.7 describes.

**Closing the file.** Once every allegation carries a disposition other than `PENDING`, an `IACommander` closes the investigation with an optional `closing_note`. The investigation must be in `PENDING_REVIEW`, and if any allegation is still `PENDING` the close is refused with `ERR_PENDING_DISPOSITIONS`. Acceptance moves the investigation `PENDING_REVIEW → CLOSED` and stamps its `closed_at`, decrements the assignee's `open_case_load` by 1, and drives the complaint `ADJUDICATION → CLOSED` — stamping the complaint's own `closed_at` from the logical clock, recomputing `sustained_allegation_count`, and setting `retention_until` by the retention rule in Section 3.9.

### 3.7. Discipline, Appeal and Reinstatement

A sustained finding produces a proposal, not a penalty. The `Discipline` row created by the cascade records the `allegation_id` it arose from and the `officer_id` it will be charged to, copies the `offense_class` from the allegation, and starts in `PROPOSED` with `effective_days` 0, `imposed_at` and `appeal_deadline_at` null, and `final_prior_tier` and `final_days` null. Discipline records are listed with optional `allegation_id`, `officer_id` and `status` filters, and a single read exposes both the presumptive and the final matrix results side by side. `aggravator_count` and `mitigator_count` — the aggravating and mitigating factors found at adjudication, each between 0 and 5 — are supplied by the commander, and only the first two mitigators earn credit.

**The discipline matrix.** The department's progressive-discipline matrix runs in seven steps, and steps three through five in particular cannot be reordered or folded together.

*Step one — the prior tier.* Count the subject officer's allegations whose `disposition` is `SUSTAINED`, excluding the allegation under consideration, whose `disposed_at` falls at or after the reference instant less 1095 whole days — three years of look-back — and at or before the reference instant. An allegation in `OVERTURNED_ON_APPEAL` is not sustained and therefore never counts. The tier index is 0 when that count is 0, 1 when it is exactly 1, and 2 when it is 2 or more. The reference instant is what distinguishes the two variants, and is defined below.

*Step two — the base.* The tier index selects one element from the row of the matrix named by `offense_class`. Class `A` — the most severe row — offers 30, 60 and 120 days for tiers 0, 1 and 2. Class `B` offers 10, 20 and 45. Class `C` offers 3, 7 and 15. Class `D` — the least severe — offers 1, 2 and 5.

*Step three — the aggravators.* Each aggravating factor adds five days. The pre-credit figure is the base plus five days multiplied by `aggravator_count`.

*Step four — the mitigation rate.* The mitigation percentage is selected from the **pre-credit** figure, not from the base. Four bands are walked from the top and the first match wins: 10 percent when the pre-credit figure is at most 10 days, 20 percent when it is at most 30 days, 25 percent when it is at most 60 days, and 30 percent for anything larger. This is the step that looks circular and is not: the aggravators must be applied before the mitigation rate can be known, which is exactly why the two adjustments cannot be reordered or collapsed into a single multiplier.

*Step five — the mitigation credit.* At most two mitigators earn credit, so the credited count is `mitigator_count` when it is 0, 1 or 2 and is 2 when it is 3, 4 or 5. The credit in days is the pre-credit figure multiplied by the credited count and by the selected percentage, rounded half up to a whole number of days.

*Step six — the net.* The net figure is the pre-credit figure less the credit, and never falls below 0.

*Step seven — the floor and the cap, in that order.* When `offense_class` is `A`, the net is raised to 30 days if it fell below that class-A floor. Only then is the global cap applied: the result is capped at 180 days.

Worked through: offense class `B` at tier index 1 with two aggravators and one mitigator gives a base of 20, a pre-credit figure of 30, a rate of 20 percent because 30 is at most 30, a credit of six days, and 24 days. The same class `B` at tier index 0 with the same factors gives a base of 10, a pre-credit figure of 20, the same 20 percent rate, a credit of four days, and 16 days — which is precisely how one officer ends up with a presumptive award of 24 days and a final award of 16 when a single prior drops out between the two evaluations. Offense class `A` at tier index 0 with no aggravators and two mitigators gives a base and pre-credit figure of 30, a 20 percent rate, a credit of twelve days and a net of 18, which the class-A floor then raises back to 30. Offense class `A` at tier index 2 with five aggravators and no mitigators gives a base of 120, a pre-credit figure of 145, a 30 percent rate that credits nothing because there are no mitigators, and 145 days, comfortably under the 180-day cap.

**The two variants.** The `PRESUMPTIVE` variant is computed inside the sustained disposition and written to `presumptive_prior_tier` and `presumptive_days`. Its reference instant is the allegation's `disposed_at`, its `aggravator_count` and `mitigator_count` are taken from the disposition payload, and its prior tier counts every qualifying sustained allegation regardless of whether the discipline arising from it was ever imposed — at proposal time only the findings are known. The `FINAL` variant is computed inside imposition and written to `final_prior_tier` and `final_days`. Its reference instant is the impose-time logical clock, its `aggravator_count` and `mitigator_count` are re-read from the impose payload and may legitimately differ from the disposition payload, and its prior tier counts only those qualifying sustained allegations whose own `Discipline` reached `IMPOSED`, `UPHELD` or `MODIFIED` with `effective_days` of at least 1. Priors that were proposed but never imposed drop out, so the final tier can be lower than the presumptive tier for the same officer — and the final award correspondingly lighter.

**Imposition.** An `IACommander` imposes the proposed discipline, supplying the `effective_date` — the first calendar day of the suspension — and optionally revised `aggravator_count` and `mitigator_count`. The discipline must be `PROPOSED`, otherwise `ERR_DISCIPLINE_STATE_INVALID`; the owning allegation must still be `SUSTAINED`; the owning complaint must be in `ADJUDICATION` or `CLOSED`, so discipline may be imposed after the file has closed; the officer's `employment_status` must not be `SEPARATED`, otherwise `ERR_OFFICER_SEPARATED`; and the same segregation-of-duties check applies as at disposition, yielding `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` when the acting commander investigated the case or is its subject. On acceptance the discipline moves `PROPOSED → IMPOSED`: `final_prior_tier` and `final_days` are computed under the final variant, `effective_days` is set to `final_days`, `imposed_at` is stamped from the logical clock, `appeal_deadline_at` is set to `imposed_at` plus 15 whole days, `final_days` is added to the officer's `imposed_suspension_days_total`, and — when `final_days` is at least 1 — an `ACTIVE` officer moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` with `suspension_end_date` set to `effective_date` plus `final_days` days.

**Filing an appeal.** The `OfficerRepresentative` files, supplying the `forum` — `ARBITRATION` or `CIVIL_SERVICE`, elected by the representative — and the stated `grounds`. The discipline must be `IMPOSED`, otherwise `ERR_DISCIPLINE_STATE_INVALID`, and the logical clock must be at or before `appeal_deadline_at`; the boundary is inclusive, so a filing at exactly the deadline instant is timely, and a filing one instant later is refused with `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_CLOSED`. Filing stamps the appeal's `filed_at` from the logical clock and computes `days_to_file` as the elapsed span from the discipline's `imposed_at` to `filed_at` in whole days with any partial day discarded — an appeal filed at exactly a fifteen-day-old deadline has `days_to_file` 15. The appeal is created in `FILED` with `outcome` `PENDING` and `modified_days` null, recording the `discipline_id` it challenges, and the discipline moves `IMPOSED → APPEALED`. The suspension-day ledger is deliberately untouched while the appeal is pending: `effective_days` stays at `final_days` and `imposed_suspension_days_total` does not move. Appeals are listed with optional `discipline_id`, `forum`, `status` and `outcome` filters.

**Hearing and deciding.** An `AppealsOfficer` records that the arbitration or civil-service hearing took place, optionally supplying `hearing_notes`; the appeal must be `FILED`, otherwise `ERR_APPEAL_STATE_INVALID`, and the move `FILED → HEARD` stamps `heard_at` from the logical clock. The decision then moves `HEARD → DECIDED` — again `ERR_APPEAL_STATE_INVALID` from any other status — stamping `decided_at`, writing the `outcome`, and driving the corresponding branch on the discipline. The three branches are:

- **`UPHELD`.** The discipline moves `APPEALED → UPHELD`. `effective_days` stays at `final_days`, and the officer's `imposed_suspension_days_total` is therefore unchanged. Nothing else moves: no reinstatement is created, the allegation stays `SUSTAINED`, and any credibility disclosure stays `ACTIVE`.
- **`MODIFIED`.** `modified_days` must be present, at least 0, and strictly less than the discipline's `final_days`; a negative value, an absent value, or a value that is not a genuine reduction is refused with `ERR_MODIFIED_DAYS_INVALID`. The discipline moves `APPEALED → MODIFIED`, `effective_days` is set to `modified_days`, and the officer's `imposed_suspension_days_total` is adjusted by `modified_days` less `final_days` — a negative adjustment, since the award was reduced. A `Reinstatement` is created in `PENDING` with `reversed_days` equal to `final_days` less `modified_days`.
- **`OVERTURNED`.** The discipline moves `APPEALED → OVERTURNED` and `effective_days` is set to 0, and four cascades follow. First, `final_days` is subtracted from the officer's `imposed_suspension_days_total`, restoring exactly the pre-imposition value. Second, when that subtraction brings `imposed_suspension_days_total` to exactly 0, the officer returns `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` and `suspension_end_date` is cleared; when other imposed discipline still stands the officer remains `SUSPENDED`. Third, because the discipline was overturned, the source allegation moves `SUSTAINED → OVERTURNED_ON_APPEAL`, the owning complaint's `sustained_allegation_count` falls by 1, and any `CredibilityDisclosure` whose `source_allegation_id` is that allegation moves `ACTIVE → EXPUNGED`, after which the officer's `credibility_disclosure_active` is recomputed over the disclosures that remain `ACTIVE`. Fourth, a `Reinstatement` is created in `PENDING` with `reversed_days` equal to the full `final_days`.

**Reinstatement and back pay.** A reinstatement records the `appeal_id` of the decision that produced it, stamps `effective_date` from the logical clock at creation, and starts in `PENDING` with `interim_earnings_cents` 0 and `gross_back_pay_cents`, `net_back_pay_cents`, `interest_cents`, `total_award_cents` and `finalized_at` all null. An appeal's reinstatements are listed — the array is always of length 0 or 1 — with an optional `status` filter.

An `AppealsOfficer` finalizes the award by supplying `interim_earnings_cents`, the outside earnings the officer made during the reversed period. The reinstatement must still be `PENDING`, otherwise `ERR_REINSTATEMENT_ALREADY_FINALIZED`; `interim_earnings_cents` may not be negative, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_INTERIM_EARNINGS`; and the officer's `annual_salary_cents` must be strictly greater than 0, because a back-pay award cannot be computed from an unknown salary, otherwise `ERR_SALARY_UNKNOWN`.

The award is built in five steps. First, `reversed_days` is divided into consecutive whole pay periods of ten days each, with a final short period holding the remainder: the number of full periods is `reversed_days` divided by ten discarding the remainder, and the remainder days are what is left over. Second, the amount owed for a period of a given number of days is the officer's `annual_salary_cents` multiplied by that number of days, divided by 260 working days in the year, rounded half up to a whole cent. **Each period is rounded on its own and the rounded period amounts are then summed**; `gross_back_pay_cents` is the number of full periods multiplied by the rounded ten-day amount, plus the rounded remainder-day amount when there are remainder days at all. Rounding per period before summing is mandatory — summing unrounded period amounts and rounding once at the end produces a different total and is wrong. Third, `net_back_pay_cents` is `gross_back_pay_cents` less `interim_earnings_cents`, floored at 0. Fourth, the elapsed span from the discipline's `imposed_at` to the finalize-time logical clock is taken in whole days with any partial day discarded, and `interest_cents` is `net_back_pay_cents` multiplied by five percent per annum, multiplied by that day count, divided by 365 days in the year, rounded half up to a whole cent — interest accrues on the net figure after the interim-earnings offset, never on the gross. Fifth, `total_award_cents` is `net_back_pay_cents` plus `interest_cents`. Acceptance moves the reinstatement `PENDING → FINALIZED` and stamps `finalized_at` from the logical clock.

To make the per-period rounding concrete: an officer on an annual salary of 9,100,013 cents with 24 reversed days has two full ten-day periods and a four-day remainder. Each ten-day period rounds to 350,001 cents and the four-day period to 140,000 cents, giving a gross of 840,002 cents — a single rounding at the end would have produced 840,001. An interim-earnings offset of 125,000 cents leaves a net of 715,002 cents; a 73-day span from imposition to finalization yields interest of 7,150 cents; and the total award is 722,152 cents. Had the interim earnings been 900,000 cents instead, the net would have floored at 0, the interest would have been 0, and the total award 0.

### 3.8. The Early Intervention System

The early intervention system is non-disciplinary. Its purpose is to notice a pattern of risk-relevant activity before that pattern becomes a sustained complaint, and to answer the pattern with retraining or counselling rather than with a penalty.

**Activity events.** A `Supervisor` or a `SystemAdmin` records a risk-relevant event against an officer, supplying the `event_type`, an optional `severity_code` that defaults to `NA`, the `occurred_at` instant and an optional `source_reference` naming the source report. Events are immutable once written: they carry only an `id` and a `created_at`, and there is no update path. The `occurred_at` instant must be at or before the logical clock — the system does not accept events that have not happened yet — and a future instant is refused with `ERR_EVENT_IN_FUTURE`. On acceptance the system derives `base_weight` from the event type's base weight in the scoring table below, and derives `weekday_index` from `occurred_at`: an integer from 0 to 6 where 0 is Monday, computed in UTC. `system_generated` is false for events recorded through this endpoint and true for the events the system appends of its own accord — both the `COMPLAINT_INTAKE` event of the allegation-attachment cascade in Section 3.2 and the `COMPLAINT_SUSTAINED` event of the sustained-disposition cascade. An officer's events are listed in `occurred_at` ascending order with an optional `event_type` filter.

Recording an event also rescores the officer under the rolling variant, and when the resulting band is above `NONE` and the officer has no alert already open, an `EarlyInterventionAlert` is created with `trigger_source` `EVENT_INGEST` and the officer's `open_alert_count` rises by 1.

**The composite score.** The scoring window is the 365 days ending at the logical clock, and it is **half-open on the left**: an event is in the window when its `occurred_at` is strictly after the clock less 365 whole days and at or before the clock. An event exactly 365 days old is therefore excluded and contributes nothing.

Each in-window event contributes the product of three factors. The first is the base weight of its `event_type`: 3 for `USE_OF_FORCE`, 2 for `PURSUIT`, 1 for `SICK_LEAVE_DAY`, 1 for `COMPLAINT_INTAKE` and 4 for `COMPLAINT_SUSTAINED` — a sustained complaint weighs more than any single incident. The second is the multiplier of its `severity_code`: 1.0 for `LEVEL_1`, 1.5 for `LEVEL_2`, 2.0 for `LEVEL_3`, and 1.0 for `NA`, so an unclassified event is scored as though it were level one. The third is a recency decay: the event's age in whole days is the elapsed span from `occurred_at` to the clock with any partial day discarded, and the decay factor is 1.0 when that age is at most 180 days and 0.5 when it is more. The boundary matters — an event aged exactly 180 days still contributes in full, and one aged 181 days contributes half. The weighted total is the exact decimal sum of every contribution, accumulated in `occurred_at` ascending order with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break and with no intermediate rounding anywhere in the sum.

Four indicators are then evaluated over the same window, each with a non-strict at-least comparison: `FORCE_COUNT` is breached when there are at least 3 `USE_OF_FORCE` events; `PURSUIT_COUNT` when there are at least 2 `PURSUIT` events; `COMPLAINT_COUNT` when the `COMPLAINT_INTAKE` and `COMPLAINT_SUSTAINED` events together number at least 3; and `SICK_LEAVE_DAYS` when there are at least 12 `SICK_LEAVE_DAY` events. `breached_indicators` lists the breached names in the fixed order `FORCE_COUNT`, `PURSUIT_COUNT`, `COMPLAINT_COUNT`, `SICK_LEAVE_DAYS` regardless of the order in which the events arrived, and the breached count is the length of that list.

The `ROLLING_12M` variant is the default and the only one written to the officer record. Its score is the weighted total multiplied by the officer's assignment multiplier — 1.0 for `PATROL`, 0.8 for `INVESTIGATIVE` and 0.6 for `ADMIN`, discounting officers whose duties expose them to fewer incidents — multiplied by a scale factor of 2.5, plus a flat additive bonus of 10 for each breached indicator. The result is clamped into the range 0 to 100 and rounded half up to two decimal places. A patrol officer with a weighted total of 17.5 and two breached indicators scores 63.75, which lands in the `ALERT` band.

The `PEER_RELATIVE` variant is returned only on request from the computed profile read, and is stored on an alert when the alert's `score_variant` is `PEER_RELATIVE`. The peer pool is every **other** officer whose `employment_status` is `ACTIVE` and whose unit's `peer_group_code` equals the subject officer's unit's `peer_group_code`; `peer_pool_size` reports its size. `peer_mean` is the exact decimal mean of those officers' rolling weighted totals, computed **without** the assignment multiplier and **without** the indicator bonus. The score is the subject's weighted total divided by `peer_mean`, multiplied by a peer scale of 40, capped at 100 and rounded half up to two decimal places. Because neither the assignment multiplier nor the indicator bonus appears here, the two variants can disagree on both the score and the band for identical event data: the same officer scoring 63.75 and `ALERT` on the rolling variant scores 46.67 and `WATCH` against a peer pool whose two members have weighted totals of 30 and 0, since `peer_mean` is 15 and the ratio is just under 1.167. When the peer pool is empty, or when `peer_mean` is exactly 0, the peer-relative score falls back to the officer's rolling score and `peer_pool_size` is reported as 0.

The band is read off the final score: `CRITICAL` at 75 or above, otherwise `ALERT` at 50 or above, otherwise `WATCH` at 30 or above, otherwise `NONE`. No alert row is ever created at `NONE`.

The computed profile read returns the `officer_id`, the `variant` applied, the `window_start` and `window_end` bounding the scoring window, the `event_count` of in-window events, the `weighted_total`, the `composite_score`, the `alert_level`, the `breached_indicators`, and the `peer_pool_size` and `peer_mean` belonging to the peer comparison. A read never overwrites the stored `eis_score`.

**The sweep.** An `IACommander` or a `SystemAdmin` runs the early-intervention sweep, optionally naming a single `officer_id` to rescore instead of the whole roster. When an `officer_id` is supplied, that officer must have no alert in `ESCALATED` — an escalated alert must be answered with a replacement plan before a targeted rescore is accepted — and a request that names such an officer is refused with `ERR_ALERT_ALREADY_OPEN`. The run reports six counters: the `run_sequence` of this run, the `evaluated_officer_count`, the number of `alerts_created`, the number of `alerts_promoted`, the number of `checkins_missed`, and the number of `disclosures_expired`.

The sweep performs five kinds of work. It rescores every `ACTIVE` officer under the rolling variant, or the single named officer. For each officer whose band is not `NONE` and who has no alert already open, it creates an alert with `trigger_source` `EIS_RUN` and raises `open_alert_count` by 1. For each officer who does already have an open alert, it refreshes that alert's `composite_score`, `score_variant`, `alert_level` and `breached_indicators` to the newly computed values and stamps the alert's `run_sequence`. It promotes an alert `OPEN → PLAN_REQUIRED` when the recomputed band reaches `ALERT` or `CRITICAL`, setting `review_due_at` to the promotion instant plus 30 whole days — the same 30-day rule that sets `review_due_at` at creation. And it sweeps time-driven records: every `PENDING` check-in whose `due_at` is strictly in the past, on a plan that is still `ACTIVE`, moves `PENDING → MISSED`, and every `ACTIVE` credibility disclosure with `permanent_retention` false whose `retention_until` the clock has reached moves `ACTIVE → EXPIRED`, after which the affected officer's `credibility_disclosure_active` is recomputed.

**Alerts.** An alert records the `officer_id` it concerns, the `trigger_source` that produced it, the `composite_score` and `score_variant` behind it, the `alert_level`, the `breached_indicators`, the `review_due_at` by which it must be answered, the `run_sequence` of the sweep that last touched it, and its `plan_count`. Alerts are listed with optional `officer_id`, `status`, `alert_level` and `score_variant` filters. An alert created at `ALERT` or `CRITICAL` is placed in `PLAN_REQUIRED` immediately, since a score at or above the alert threshold demands a plan; an alert created at `WATCH` sits in `OPEN`.

An `IACommander` may dismiss an alert as not indicating risk, supplying a `dismissal_reason`. The alert must be `OPEN` or `PLAN_REQUIRED`, otherwise `ERR_ALERT_STATE_INVALID`, and it must have no intervention plan attached — once a plan exists the alert must be worked through rather than dismissed — so a dismissal against an alert whose `plan_count` is not 0 is refused with `ERR_PLAN_EXISTS`. Dismissal writes the `dismissal_reason`, moves the alert to `DISMISSED` and lowers the officer's `open_alert_count` by 1. `DISMISSED` and `CLOSED_SUCCESS` are the two terminal alert states.

**Intervention plans.** A `Supervisor` or an `IACommander` attaches a plan to an alert, pinning `expected_alert_version` as Section 2.6 requires and supplying the `supervisor_officer_id` who will do the monitoring, the `required_checkin_count` between 2 and 6, the `duration_days` of 30, 60 or 90, and the `interventions` to be applied, drawn from `RETRAINING`, `COUNSELING`, `PEER_REVIEW`, `REASSIGNMENT` and `EAP_REFERRAL`. The alert must be in `PLAN_REQUIRED` or `ESCALATED`, otherwise `ERR_ALERT_STATE_INVALID`. The plan is created in `DRAFT` with `completed_checkin_count`, `on_time_checkin_count` and `concern_count` all 0 and with `compliance_score` null — the score stays null until the first check-in is completed, rather than starting at 0.00 — and the alert's `plan_count` rises by 1 and its `version` rises by 1. When the alert was `ESCALATED`, it moves `ESCALATED → PLAN_REQUIRED`, which is the cycle in this system: `PLAN_REQUIRED → PLAN_ACTIVE → ESCALATED → PLAN_REQUIRED`, one revolution for every plan that fails. Plans are listed with optional `alert_id`, `officer_id`, `supervisor_officer_id` and `status` filters.

Activation is reserved to the monitoring supervisor: when the acting role is `Supervisor`, the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the plan's `supervisor_officer_id`, and any other supervisor is refused with `ERR_NOT_PLAN_SUPERVISOR`. The plan must be `DRAFT`, otherwise `ERR_PLAN_STATE_INVALID`, and the owning alert must be `PLAN_REQUIRED`. Activation accepts an optional `activation_note`, moves the plan `DRAFT → ACTIVE`, stamps `activated_at` from the logical clock, sets `end_date` to `activated_at` plus `duration_days` whole days, materialises the check-in schedule, and moves the owning alert `PLAN_REQUIRED → PLAN_ACTIVE`. The alert keeps counting toward `open_alert_count` throughout.

**The check-in schedule.** The interval between check-ins is `duration_days` divided by one more than `required_checkin_count`, discarding any remainder. One check-in row is then created for each `sequence_index` from 1 up to and including `required_checkin_count`, with `due_at` equal to `activated_at` plus the interval multiplied by that index, in whole days, carrying the time of day of `activated_at`. Dividing by one more than the check-in count means the last check-in falls strictly before `end_date`, leaving the final interval as a review gap: a 30-day plan with 2 check-ins has an interval of 10 days, check-ins due 10 and 20 days after activation, and an `end_date` 30 days after activation; a 60-day plan with 3 check-ins has an interval of 15 days and check-ins due 15, 30 and 45 days out. Every row records the `plan_id` it belongs to and is created with `status` `PENDING`, `outcome` `PENDING`, and `on_time` and `completed_at` null. A plan's check-ins are listed in `sequence_index` ascending order with optional `status` and `outcome` filters.

**Completing a check-in.** The monitoring supervisor records the `outcome` — `SATISFACTORY` or `CONCERN` — with optional `notes`. The check-in must be `PENDING` and its owning plan must be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_CHECKIN_NOT_PENDING`, and the acting supervisor must be the plan's monitoring supervisor, otherwise `ERR_NOT_PLAN_SUPERVISOR`. Acceptance moves the check-in `PENDING → COMPLETED`, stamps `completed_at` from the logical clock, and sets `on_time` true when `completed_at` is at or before `due_at` — the boundary is inclusive, so a completion at exactly the due instant is on time. The plan's `completed_checkin_count` rises by 1, its `on_time_checkin_count` rises by 1 only when `on_time` is true, and its `concern_count` rises by 1 when the `outcome` is `CONCERN`. The plan's `compliance_score` is recomputed in that same request by the formula below, so a partially completed plan already reports a score — one on-time completion out of two required check-ins reads 50.00 mid-plan, not null. Sweeping a check-in to `MISSED`, by contrast, does not itself rewrite `compliance_score`; the missed check-in shows up the next time the score is written. `COMPLETED` and `MISSED` are both terminal for a check-in, and only the sweep writes `MISSED`.

**The compliance score.** `compliance_score` is one hundred multiplied by `on_time_checkin_count` divided by `required_checkin_count`, less 15 for each concern recorded, floored at 0 and rounded half up to two decimal places. Only on-time completions feed the positive term: a check-in completed after its `due_at` and a check-in swept to `MISSED` both contribute nothing, which is why a missed check-in shows up as a lower score rather than as a separate penalty. The concern penalty is applied after the ratio, not inside it. Two check-ins of which one was missed and none raised a concern gives 50.00; two on-time completions of which one raised a concern gives 85.00; three on-time completions with two concerns gives 70.00.

**Closing a plan.** A `Supervisor` — again necessarily the monitoring supervisor — or an `IACommander` closes the plan with an optional `closing_note`. The plan must be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_PLAN_STATE_INVALID`, and no check-in of the plan may still be `PENDING`, otherwise `ERR_PENDING_CHECKINS`. The close writes `compliance_score` and `closed_at`, and then chooses between two outcomes on two conditions:

- The plan moves `ACTIVE → CLOSED_SUCCESS` when `compliance_score` is at least 80 — the comparison is non-strict, so a score of exactly 80.00 succeeds — **and** no early-intervention alert other than the plan's own was created for the officer with `created_at` inside the closed interval running from `activated_at` to the logical clock. The owning alert moves `PLAN_ACTIVE → CLOSED_SUCCESS` and the officer's `open_alert_count` falls by 1.
- The plan moves `ACTIVE → ESCALATED` when `compliance_score` is below 80, **or** when another alert was created for the officer inside that window. A new alert during the monitoring window means the plan did not work even if every check-in was kept, so a perfect compliance score does not rescue it. The owning alert moves `PLAN_ACTIVE → ESCALATED` and keeps counting toward `open_alert_count`; because the alert escalated, the officer's `intervention_escalation_count` rises by 1 and the officer's `eis_alert_level` is raised one band, capped at `CRITICAL`.

Both plan outcomes are terminal for the plan. Only the escalated alert can be worked again, by attaching a replacement plan.

### 3.9. Records Retention, Disclosure and Public Reporting

**The retention clock.** Every closed complaint carries a `retention_until` instant, and how long the file must be kept depends on how it came out. The governing disposition is the strongest disposition present across the complaint's allegations, chosen by this precedence with the first match winning: `SUSTAINED`, then `POLICY_FAILURE`, then `NOT_SUSTAINED`, then `EXONERATED`, then `UNFOUNDED`, then `WITHDRAWN`. An allegation in `OVERTURNED_ON_APPEAL` counts as `NOT_SUSTAINED` for this purpose — the finding is gone but the file must outlive the appeal record. For a withdrawn or intake-rejected complaint the governing disposition is `WITHDRAWN`.

The retention period follows from that disposition: 2555 days for `SUSTAINED`, 1825 days for `POLICY_FAILURE`, 1825 days for `NOT_SUSTAINED`, 1095 days for `EXONERATED`, 1095 days for `UNFOUNDED` and 730 days for `WITHDRAWN`. `retention_until` is the complaint's `closed_at` plus that many whole days. A file closed with one sustained and one unfounded allegation is governed by the sustained finding and keeps the full 2555 days; a file whose only allegation was not sustained keeps 1825.

Credibility disclosures run on their own clock: a disclosure's `retention_until` is its `created_at` plus 2555 whole days, and `permanent_retention` is true when the `basis` is `UNTRUTHFULNESS_SUSTAINED`, in which case the row is never swept to `EXPIRED` no matter what `retention_until` says. Untruthfulness is disclosable for the length of the officer's career.

**Purging a complaint.** A `RecordsCustodian` redacts a retention-expired file, supplying an optional `purge_reason`. Four guards apply, and their order is deliberate. The complaint must be `CLOSED` or `WITHDRAWN` with `purged_at` still null, otherwise `ERR_COMPLAINT_NOT_PURGEABLE`. No `ACTIVE` credibility disclosure may reference any allegation of the complaint, and no allegation of the complaint may be a sustained untruthfulness finding, otherwise `ERR_CREDIBILITY_HOLD` — the constitutional obligation to disclose officer credibility outranks the retention schedule, so this check is decided before the retention clock is even consulted. No appeal in `FILED` or `HEARD` may exist for any discipline arising from the complaint's allegations, otherwise `ERR_APPEAL_PENDING`. And the logical clock must have reached `retention_until`, a non-strict comparison so that a purge at exactly the retention instant is allowed, otherwise `ERR_RETENTION_NOT_EXPIRED`. Because the credibility hold is decided first, a file that is both under a credibility hold and not yet retention-expired reports the hold.

A purge erases identity and narrative but preserves statistics. The complaint's `narrative`, `complainant_name` and `complainant_contact` are nulled and `complainant_demographic_code` is set to `UNKNOWN`; every allegation's `narrative` is nulled and each allegation's `redacted` flag is set to true; `purged_at` is stamped and the complaint's `status` becomes `PURGED`. Credibility disclosure rows are left entirely untouched. The outcome counters — `allegation_count` and `sustained_allegation_count` — survive the purge, which is what allows purged files to keep appearing in the aggregate report below.

**Credibility disclosures.** A disclosure records the `officer_id` it concerns, the `source_allegation_id` that produced it, the `basis` on which it was created — `UNTRUTHFULNESS_SUSTAINED`, `BIAS_SUSTAINED` or `EVIDENCE_MISHANDLING_SUSTAINED` — its `retention_until` and `permanent_retention`, and a disclosure ledger of `release_count` and `last_released_at`. Disclosures are created only by the sustained-disposition cascade and are listed with optional `officer_id`, `source_allegation_id`, `status` and `basis` filters. There are exactly two ways out of `ACTIVE`, both system-driven and both terminal: `ACTIVE → EXPUNGED` when the source allegation is overturned on appeal, and `ACTIVE → EXPIRED` when the sweep finds a non-permanent disclosure whose `retention_until` the clock has reached. Either transition recomputes the officer's `credibility_disclosure_active` over the disclosures that remain `ACTIVE`, so the flag is a true summary of the officer's current disclosure obligations rather than a latch.

A `RecordsCustodian` records each release of the disclosure record to a `requesting_party` such as a prosecutor or defense counsel. The disclosure must be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_DISCLOSURE_NOT_ACTIVE` — an expunged or expired record may not be released. Acceptance raises `release_count` by 1 and stamps `last_released_at` from the logical clock.

**The public outcome report.** A `RecordsCustodian`, an `IACommander` or a `SystemAdmin` reads the aggregate complaint-outcome report over a period bounded by `period_start` and `period_end`, optionally requesting a `breakdown`. `period_start` must be at or before `period_end`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REPORT_PERIOD`. The report is derived entirely on read and mutates nothing.

The population is every complaint whose `status` is `CLOSED` or `WITHDRAWN` and whose `closed_at` falls at or after `period_start` and at or before `period_end`. Purged complaints **are** included in the totals: their identity fields are gone but their outcome counters survive, and excluding them would silently understate the department's history. Grouping follows the `breakdown`: `DEMOGRAPHIC` groups by `complainant_demographic_code`, with `UNKNOWN` forming a group of its own; `CHANNEL` groups by `intake_channel`; and when `breakdown` is omitted the whole population forms a single group whose `group_key` is the literal `ALL`.

Within each group, `total_complaints` is the number of complaints in the group, `sustained_complaints` is the number whose `sustained_allegation_count` is at least 1, and `sustain_rate_pct` is one hundred multiplied by `sustained_complaints` divided by `total_complaints`, rounded half up to one decimal place — one decimal place here, in contrast to the two used everywhere else — and reported as 0.0 when `total_complaints` is 0.

Small-cell suppression protects individuals from re-identification in published statistics. `suppression_min_cell` reports the threshold, which is 3. Any group whose `total_complaints` is **strictly less than** 3 is suppressed: its `total_complaints`, `sustained_complaints` and `sustain_rate_pct` are all reported as null and its `suppressed` flag is true, and its counts are rolled into a single trailing group whose `group_key` is the literal `SUPPRESSED`. That rolled-up group always discloses its own counts in full, so nothing is lost from the total. A group of exactly 3 is disclosed in full, since the comparison is strict. The `groups` array is emitted in ascending `group_key` byte order with the `SUPPRESSED` group last regardless of where its key would otherwise sort. The report's top-level `period_start`, `period_end`, `breakdown`, `total_complaints`, `sustained_complaints` and `sustain_rate_pct` describe the whole population and are never suppressed.
