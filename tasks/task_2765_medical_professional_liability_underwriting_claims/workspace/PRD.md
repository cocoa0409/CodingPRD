# Medical Professional Liability API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A medical malpractice carrier lives or dies by the quality of two judgements it must make years apart: what a physician's or facility's exposure is worth at the moment coverage is written, and what an asserted claim will cost once it has run through investigation, litigation and either a verdict or a negotiated settlement. Those two judgements are made by different departments, on different data, under different regulatory obligations — and today they are reconciled by hand. Underwriters rate a renewal without seeing the open claim inventory that the claims department has already reserved. Claims adjusters authorise settlements without a machine-checkable record of whether the insured's consent-to-settle provision gives the physician an absolute veto. Compliance staff discover a National Practitioner Data Bank reporting deadline only after it has passed. Actuaries rebuild the same experience extract every quarter to justify a rate filing.

This document specifies the API for a single system of record that closes those gaps. It carries the whole professional-liability book: rating specialty classes and the manual base rates published against them, physician and facility insureds with their credentialing verdicts and risk classifications, claims-made and occurrence policies with their step-rated terms and extended reporting period endorsements, claim intake with automatic coverage-trigger resolution, case and formula reserving, defence counsel assignment from a qualified panel, settlement negotiation under consent-to-settle and hammer provisions, statutory data bank reporting, and the actuarial review that revises the rate manual. Every derived figure — a premium, a reserve, an incurred loss, a rate indication — is computed by the server from data the server already holds, and every figure a client can read is the figure the server will act on.

Two properties of the domain drive most of the design that follows. First, the same underlying facts are legitimately measured in more than one way: a claim carries a case-incurred figure that the claims department reports and an actuarial-incurred figure that feeds rate filings, and a policy term is rated either from an underwriting snapshot or from a prospective renewal view of the insured's claim history. The system maintains both readings side by side rather than picking one. Second, authority is deliberately split. The person who prepares a settlement offer may not be the person who authorises it, the actuary who prepares a rate filing may not be the officer who approves it, and the physician — not the carrier — holds the consent right over a settlement made in their name. The API enforces those separations rather than documenting them.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

Every route in this specification is mounted under the `/mpl` prefix. The rules in this section apply to every endpoint unless a later section states a narrower rule for a specific operation.

### 2.1. Actors, Headers and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that principal is acting in. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` does not spell one of the eight declared roles exactly, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If both headers are well formed but the named role is not authorised for the addressed operation, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). The eight roles are `UnderwritingOfficer`, `ClaimsAdjuster`, `ClaimsCommitteeApprover`, `PeerReviewChair`, `ActuarialAnalyst`, `ChiefActuary`, `ComplianceOfficer` and `Insured`.

Seven of the eight roles are carrier-side operator roles, and for all seven `X-User-ID` is an **opaque operator identifier** that is not tied to any persisted entity. When `X-User-Role` is `UnderwritingOfficer`, `ClaimsAdjuster`, `ClaimsCommitteeApprover`, `PeerReviewChair`, `ActuarialAnalyst`, `ChiefActuary` or `ComplianceOfficer`, the server may record that identifier on the records the actor touches — as `prepared_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` on a settlement negotiation, as `prepared_by_user_id` and `decided_by_user_id` on a rate filing, as `set_by_user_id` on a reserve posting, and as `chair_user_id` on a peer review case — but it never compares that identifier to any entity's `id`.

The eighth role is different. When `X-User-Role` is `Insured`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting insured record; ownership checks on any resource that references an insured compare the resource's insured foreign key to `X-User-ID`. An `Insured` actor may read only its own insured record, its own policies, the terms of those policies, tail endorsements on those policies, claims asserted against it and settlement negotiations on those claims; an attempt to read any record belonging to a different insured is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). The same ownership check governs the one write the role may perform, the consent-to-settle response: only the physician or facility named on the claim may consent to or veto a settlement offered in its name.

Two roles carry a further restriction that survives authorisation. A `ClaimsCommitteeApprover` may not decide settlement authority on a negotiation whose `prepared_by_user_id` equals its own `X-User-ID`, and a `ChiefActuary` may not approve or reject a rate filing whose `prepared_by_user_id` equals its own `X-User-ID`. Either violation is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Withdrawing a rate filing is deliberately exempt from that rule: an actuary is expected to be able to withdraw work it prepared itself.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, may be no lower than 1 and no higher than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may be no lower than 0. A `limit` outside the inclusive range 1 to 100, a non-integer `limit` or `offset`, or a negative `offset` is rejected with `ERR_PAGINATION_INVALID` (HTTP 400).

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Every list response carries the four pagination fields in its `meta` block: `limit` and `offset` echoing the effective values used, `total_count` giving the number of records matching the filters before the page window is applied, and `has_more` indicating whether records remain beyond the current page. Filter parameters offered by individual list endpoints are all optional and combine conjunctively — a request that supplies several filters returns only the records matching all of them.

Each collection offers its own filter set, and every one of them is optional. Specialty classes filter on `specialty_code` and `base_class_code`. Insureds filter on `specialty_class_id`, `entity_type`, `status`, `credentialing_status` and `risk_class_code`. Risk-management enrollments filter on `insured_id`, `program_code` and `status`. Peer review cases filter on `insured_id`, `status` and `trigger_source`. Policies filter on `insured_id`, `status` and `coverage_form`. Policy terms filter on `policy_id`, `predecessor_term_id`, `status` and `rating_variant`. Tail endorsements filter on `policy_id`, `triggering_term_id`, `tail_type` and `status`. Claims filter on `insured_id`, `policy_term_id`, `tail_endorsement_id`, `claim_status`, `coverage_status`, `venue_code` and `allegation_category`. The reserve entries of one claim filter on `method`. Panel defence firms filter on `panel_status`, `venue_code` — matching firms admitted in that venue — and `allegation_category`, matching firms carrying that specialty. Counsel assignments filter on `claim_id`, `counsel_firm_id` and `assignment_status`. Settlement negotiations filter on `claim_id`, `status` and `consent_clause_applied`. Data bank reporting obligations filter on `claim_id`, `settlement_negotiation_id`, `insured_id`, `report_status` and `reportable`. Rate filings filter on `specialty_class_id`, `status`, `adequacy_flag` and `indication_method`.

### 2.4. Record Identity, Audit Fields and Append-Only Postings

Every resource carries a server-minted `id` formatted as a UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp recorded as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits. Almost every resource additionally carries `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp refreshed whenever any stored property of the record changes, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of the record. Recomputation of a derived field counts as a mutation: when a claim's reserve figures are re-derived, or a policy's premium roll-ups are recomputed, that record's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` incremented.

Reserve postings are the single exception. A reserve entry is an append-only accounting document: it is never updated after creation, and it therefore carries only `id` and `created_at`. Both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from a reserve entry. Its `created_at` is load-bearing rather than decorative — it is the secondary ordering key when two postings on the same claim declare the same effective instant.

Client-supplied dates that name a calendar day — enrollment, completion and expiry dates, term effective and expiration dates, cancellation dates, incident, report, suit-filed, resolution, closing, payment, notice and experience-period dates — are supplied and returned as `YYYY-MM-DD`. Instants that need a time of day, such as a reserve posting's `effective_at` or the logical timestamps written to `issued_at`, `assigned_at`, `closed_at` and `consent_requested_at`, are ISO 8601 UTC.

### 2.5. Numeric Precision and Pinned Evaluation Order

Every monetary amount is a JSON number carrying at most two decimal places, and every rounding operation in this specification rounds half up. Percentages expressed as rates in the rating engine — the credit and surcharge percentages — are carried to four decimal places; percentages expressed in whole-percent units in the actuarial engine, such as a proposed or indicated rate change, are carried to two decimal places. Loss ratios, adequacy ratios, credibility weights, trend indices and win rates are carried to four decimal places.

Where a calculation proceeds in stages, the order of rounding is part of the contract and not an implementation detail. Two ladders in particular are pinned. The term rating ladder rounds to two decimal places at each of its four multiplicative stages rather than carrying full precision to the end. The fee ladder keys the flat administrative fee on the net premium — not on the term total — and keys the guaranty fund assessment on the net premium plus that administrative fee, again not on the term total. In the reserve adequacy review, each claim's required reserve is rounded to two decimal places before the claims are summed, never after. Each of these is spelled out in the workflow that owns it; where a later section describes an evaluation order, that order governs.

### 2.6. The Logical Clock and Scheduled Checkpoints

The server keeps a logical UTC clock rather than reading wall-clock time. Before any client sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time. An operator in any of the seven carrier-side roles may advance it by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant; the response reports the resulting `current_utc` together with the identifier lists `promoted_term_ids`, `expired_term_ids`, `expired_enrollment_ids`, `lapsed_insured_ids` and `closed_claim_ids`, each naming the records the checkpoint sweep touched.

Setting the clock runs six steps in a pinned order. First the clock itself is set to the supplied instant. Second, every policy term in `ISSUED` status whose effective date is on or before the new date is promoted to `IN_FORCE`, processed in order of effective date ascending and then creation timestamp ascending. Third, every term in `IN_FORCE` status whose expiration date is on or before the new date becomes `EXPIRED`, in the same ordering, after which each affected policy's compound status and current-term pointer are re-derived. Because promotion runs before expiry inside the same call, a term whose whole period lies in the skipped interval is promoted and then expired by a single clock advance. Fourth, every completed risk-management enrollment whose expiry date is on or before the new date becomes `EXPIRED`, after which the insured's stacked credit is recomputed and every drafted term on that insured's policies is re-rated. Fifth, every insured whose credentialing verdict is `CREDENTIALED` or `PROVISIONAL` and whose credentialing expiry date falls strictly before the new date has that verdict changed to `LAPSED`, and if the insured's underwriting status was `ACTIVE` it becomes `SUSPENDED`. Sixth, every claim in `RESOLVED` handling status whose resolution date plus 30 days is on or before the new date becomes `CLOSED`, with its closing date set to the new date.

### 2.7. Nested Resource Retrieval and Left-to-Right Path Resolution

Several routes address a child resource through its parent — a policy's terms, a policy's tail endorsements, an insured's enrollments, a claim's reserve entries, a claim's counsel assignments, a claim's settlement negotiations. For any such path the server resolves identifiers strictly left to right and fails on the first one that does not resolve, returning the 404 that belongs to that segment rather than a generic one. A reserve posting addressed under a claim identifier that does not exist therefore fails with `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND` and never reaches the payload checks; a term creation addressed under a policy identifier that does not exist fails with `ERR_POLICY_NOT_FOUND` before the supplied predecessor term is looked up. Identifiers supplied inside a payload are resolved after the path has resolved completely: a `predecessor_term_id` or `triggering_term_id` that names no term, or that names a term belonging to a different policy than the one addressed, yields `ERR_POLICY_TERM_NOT_FOUND`.

### 2.8. Validation Precedence

When several checks could reject the same request, they are evaluated in a fixed order, and the response carries the error of the first check that fails. Reading a rule further down this list into a request that already fails an earlier one is a defect, because a client cannot then see the condition that actually blocks it.

1. **Authentication** — headers present and role recognised (`ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`).
2. **Authorisation** — the acting role appears in the endpoint's role list (`ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`).
3. **Resource ownership** — for an actor in the `Insured` role, `X-User-ID` matches the insured referenced by the addressed claim, policy, term, tail endorsement, negotiation or insured record (`ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`). This guards the consent-to-settle response and the six single-record reads the role may perform.
4. **Segregation of duties** — the acting `X-User-ID` differs from the recorded preparer on a settlement authority decision and on a rate-filing approval or rejection (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`).
5. **Payload shape** — on every write endpoint: required fields present, enum values drawn from the declared members, money fields non-negative and carrying at most two decimal places, and every declared numeric range respected (`ERR_VALIDATION`, HTTP 400).
6. **Pagination arguments** — on every list endpoint (`ERR_PAGINATION_INVALID`).
7. **Specialty uniqueness** — on publishing a specialty class, its `specialty_code` is not already used by another class (`ERR_VALIDATION`).
8. **Retroactive date coherence** — on quoting a policy (`ERR_RETRO_DATE_INVALID`).
9. **Reserve method inputs** — on posting a reserve entry, the supplied amounts match the declared method (`ERR_RESERVE_METHOD_INPUT_MISMATCH`).
10. **Existence, first group** — the addressed or referenced specialty class (`ERR_SPECIALTY_CLASS_NOT_FOUND`), insured (`ERR_INSURED_NOT_FOUND`), enrollment (`ERR_ENROLLMENT_NOT_FOUND`), peer review case (`ERR_PEER_REVIEW_CASE_NOT_FOUND`), policy (`ERR_POLICY_NOT_FOUND`), policy term (`ERR_POLICY_TERM_NOT_FOUND`), tail endorsement (`ERR_TAIL_ENDORSEMENT_NOT_FOUND`), claim (`ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`), defence firm (`ERR_COUNSEL_FIRM_NOT_FOUND`) and counsel assignment (`ERR_COUNSEL_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`), resolved in that order.
11. **Optimistic concurrency** — the parent-version field required by §2.9 matches the parent's current `version`, failing with `ERR_STALE_CLAIM_VERSION`, `ERR_STALE_INSURED_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_POLICY_VERSION` according to which parent is guarded.
12. **Existence, second group** — the addressed settlement negotiation (`ERR_NEGOTIATION_NOT_FOUND`), data bank report (`ERR_NPDB_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`) and rate filing (`ERR_RATE_FILING_NOT_FOUND`), resolved in that order. These three sit deliberately after the concurrency check.
13. **State preconditions** — the insured is not suspended when a policy is quoted (`ERR_INSURED_STATE_INVALID`), then the negotiation (`ERR_NEGOTIATION_STATE_INVALID`), claim handling axis (`ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID`), coverage axis already decided (`ERR_COVERAGE_ALREADY_DECIDED`), term (`ERR_TERM_STATE_INVALID`), tail (`ERR_TAIL_STATE_INVALID`), enrollment (`ERR_ENROLLMENT_STATE_INVALID`), peer review case (`ERR_PEER_REVIEW_STATE_INVALID`), counsel assignment (`ERR_ASSIGNMENT_STATE_INVALID`), data bank report (`ERR_NPDB_STATE_INVALID`) and rate filing (`ERR_FILING_STATE_INVALID`) checks, in that order.
14. **Business safety** — credentialing validity at issuance (`ERR_CREDENTIALING_NOT_VALID_FOR_TERM`), duty to defend (`ERR_COVERAGE_DENIED_NO_DEFENSE`), committee majority (`ERR_VALIDATION`), hammer availability (`ERR_HAMMER_NOT_AVAILABLE`), consent prerequisites (`ERR_CONSENT_REQUIRED`), reserve deficiency blocking approval (`ERR_RESERVE_DEFICIENCY_BLOCKS_FILING`) and data bank submission dating (`ERR_NPDB_SUBMISSION_DATE_INVALID`), in that order.
15. **Temporal coherence** — report date against incident date (`ERR_REPORT_DATE_BEFORE_INCIDENT`), suit-filed and resolution dates against report date (`ERR_VALIDATION`), term expiration after effective (`ERR_VALIDATION`), experience period coherence (`ERR_EXPERIENCE_PERIOD_INVALID`), step-chain adjacency (`ERR_TERM_PERIOD_GAP`), term period overlap (`ERR_TERM_PERIOD_OVERLAP`), cancellation dating (`ERR_CANCEL_DATE_INVALID`), tail effective dating (`ERR_TAIL_EFFECTIVE_DATE_INVALID`) and tail window overlap (`ERR_TAIL_WINDOW_OVERLAP`), in that order.
16. **Domain constraints** — tail applicability (`ERR_TAIL_NOT_APPLICABLE`), death/disability/retirement eligibility (`ERR_DDR_NOT_ELIGIBLE`), one issued tail per triggering term (`ERR_TAIL_ALREADY_ISSUED`), duplicate program enrollment (`ERR_DUPLICATE_PROGRAM_ENROLLMENT`), duplicate review track (`ERR_REVIEW_TRACK_ALREADY_RECORDED`), counsel already assigned (`ERR_COUNSEL_ALREADY_ASSIGNED`), authority grant bounds (`ERR_AUTHORITY_GRANT_EXCEEDS_REQUEST`), assignment-to-claim mismatch (`ERR_ASSIGNMENT_CLAIM_MISMATCH`) and empty counsel pool (`ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_COUNSEL`), in that order.
17. **Fiscal limits** — authority sufficiency (`ERR_AUTHORITY_INSUFFICIENT`), then the aggregate limit (`ERR_AGGREGATE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`), then the per-claim limit (`ERR_PER_CLAIM_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`), in that order.

Note that `ERR_VALIDATION` serves several checks at different points in this order: the generic payload check, specialty-code uniqueness, the committee-majority rule, the suit-filed and resolution date rules, the term-period rule that requires an expiration date strictly after the effective date, and the refusal of a settled disposition submitted through the disposition endpoint.

### 2.9. Optimistic Concurrency on Parent Records

Four write operations create a child record that legitimately supersedes something already attached to its parent, and each of the four is therefore guarded by an optimistic-concurrency check against that parent's `version`.

Posting a reserve entry against a claim requires `expected_claim_version` in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of that claim. If it does not equal the claim's current `version`, the posting is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CLAIM_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A newly posted entry becomes the reserve of record, so a writer holding a stale view of the claim would otherwise silently overwrite a reserve movement it never saw.

Opening a settlement negotiation on a claim likewise requires `expected_claim_version` matching the claim's current `version`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CLAIM_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A new negotiation moves any prior open negotiation on the same claim to `ABANDONED`, so a writer working from a stale claim view would abandon an offer it never read.

Enrolling an insured in a risk-management program requires `expected_insured_version` matching the insured's current `version`; a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_INSURED_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The new enrollment supersedes the insured's participation profile and moves the stacked-credit total, so a stale writer would compute the credit from a superseded profile.

Adding a term to a policy requires `expected_policy_version` matching the policy's current `version`; a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_POLICY_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A new term supersedes the policy's quoted-premium picture and extends the step progression, so a stale writer would build a step chain on a superseded base.

These four fields are mandatory on those four operations — they are not optional hints. No other endpoint in this specification accepts a version field, and none applies a concurrency check.

### 2.10. Derived Roll-Ups and the Re-Rating Cascade

A great deal of this system's state is derived, and the derivation is always immediate: by the time a write returns, every roll-up affected by it has been recomputed. Three cascades recur often enough to state once.

The **premium ledger cascade** keeps a policy's two money roll-ups honest. `quoted_premium_total` is the sum of `term_total` over that policy's terms in `DRAFT` status, rounded to two decimal places. `written_premium_net_total` is the sum, over that policy's terms in `ISSUED`, `IN_FORCE`, `EXPIRED`, `CANCELLED_FLAT` or `CANCELLED_PRO_RATA` status, of each term's `premium_net` less its `return_premium`, rounded to two decimal places. Both are recomputed whenever a term is created, re-rated, issued or cancelled.

The **compound policy status cascade** re-derives a policy's `status` and `current_term_id` from its terms after every term transition. A policy is `QUOTED` until at least one of its terms reaches `ISSUED` or `IN_FORCE`, at which point it becomes `ACTIVE`. Once no term remains in `DRAFT`, `ISSUED` or `IN_FORCE`, the policy becomes `EXPIRED` if at least one term is `EXPIRED`, and `CANCELLED` if at least one term is `CANCELLED_FLAT` or `CANCELLED_PRO_RATA`. `current_term_id` names the term with the latest effective date among terms in `ISSUED` or `IN_FORCE` status, breaking ties by creation timestamp ascending, and is null when no term qualifies.

The **re-rating cascade** propagates rating inputs into unbound quotes. Whenever an insured's credit or surcharge position moves, or the manual base rate of its specialty class is rewritten, every policy term still in `DRAFT` status on that insured's policies is re-rated from scratch and each affected policy's `quoted_premium_total` is recomputed. Terms that are already `ISSUED`, `IN_FORCE` or terminal keep the premium they were bound at; they are never re-rated. Two events re-rate a narrower set: the intake of a new claim and the payment of a settlement re-rate only those `DRAFT` terms whose rating variant is `RENEWAL_PROSPECTIVE`, leaving drafted first terms of a step chain untouched.

### 2.11. Interval and Window Conventions

Three kinds of date interval appear in this domain and they do not share the same endpoint semantics, so each is stated once here and relied on thereafter.

A **policy term period** is half-open: it runs from its effective date inclusive to its expiration date exclusive. A date equal to the expiration date therefore falls outside the term. This is what makes a step chain possible without a gap — the successor term's effective date equals the predecessor's expiration date exactly, and the two periods are adjacent without intersecting. It also means two terms that share a single boundary date do not overlap.

An **extended reporting window** on a tail endorsement is closed at both ends: it runs from its effective date inclusive to its `reporting_window_end` inclusive. Because both endpoints are inclusive, two reporting windows that touch at a single shared date do overlap.

An **experience period** on a rate filing is inclusive at both ends: a claim enters the experience set when its report date is on or after `experience_period_start` and on or before `experience_period_end`.

Day counts are calendar days. Where a rule adds a number of days to a date — 730 days from a program completion, 30 days from a payment, 30 days from a claim resolution — the result is that many calendar days later.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Rate Manual: Specialty Classes and Actuarial Governance of Base Rates

Everything the carrier charges begins with the rate manual. An `ActuarialAnalyst` or a `ChiefActuary` publishes a **specialty class** by supplying a `specialty_code` — one of `NEUROSURGERY`, `OBSTETRICS_GYNECOLOGY`, `ORTHOPEDIC_SURGERY`, `EMERGENCY_MEDICINE` or `FAMILY_MEDICINE` — together with the ISO-style hazard band `base_class_code`, drawn from `CLASS_1` through `CLASS_8`, the manual `base_rate_per_million` charged for a one-million-dollar per-claim limit, the specialty's `hazard_index`, and the industry `benchmark_severity` against which its paid severity is later measured. The `specialty_code` must be unique across specialty classes; a duplicate is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION`. The base rate must be greater than zero and carry at most two decimal places, the hazard index must lie between 1.0 and 10.0 inclusive, and the benchmark severity must be greater than zero with at most two decimal places. Each new class starts with `rate_revision_index` at 0 and `last_approved_rate_change_pct` null, since no filing has yet touched it.

A **rate filing** is how that manual rate changes. An `ActuarialAnalyst` or `ChiefActuary` drafts one against a specialty class by supplying `specialty_class_id`, the `indication_method` — either `LOSS_RATIO_METHOD` or `PURE_PREMIUM_METHOD` — the `proposed_rate_change_pct` (a percentage between −50.00 and 100.00 inclusive, carried to two decimal places), the `experience_period_start` and `experience_period_end` bounding the data reviewed, and the `effective_date` on which the revised rate would take effect. The filing receives a `filing_number` of the form `RF-` followed by a six-digit zero-padded creation sequence, assigned in creation-timestamp ascending order beginning at `RF-000001`; `prepared_by_user_id` records the drafting actor; and the filing opens in `DRAFT`. The experience period must be coherent — the end strictly after the start, and the filing's effective date on or after the experience period end — or the draft is refused with `ERR_EXPERIENCE_PERIOD_INVALID`.

Drafting the filing immediately runs the full actuarial block, and submitting it for decision runs that block again against whatever the data looks like at that moment, so a filing that sat in `DRAFT` while claims developed is decided on refreshed figures. The block is computed as follows.

The **experience claim scope** holds every claim whose insured belongs to the filing's specialty class, whose coverage position is `COVERED` or `RESERVED_RIGHTS`, and whose report date falls within the inclusive experience period. Claims still `UNDER_REVIEW` and claims `DENIED` are excluded no matter what reserves they carry. `claim_count_in_scope` is the number of claims in that scope. `required_reserve_total` is built by taking, for each claim in scope, the product of its `demand_amount`, its `severity_band_factor` and the venue severity factor for its own `venue_code`, multiplying that product by 1.28 (one plus the twenty-eight percent expense loading), rounding that per-claim figure half up to two decimal places, and only then summing the rounded per-claim figures. `carried_reserve_total` is the sum, over the same scope, of each claim's `indemnity_reserve` plus its `expense_reserve`, each claim's sum rounded to two decimal places before being added. When `required_reserve_total` is zero, `reserve_adequacy_ratio` is null and `adequacy_flag` is `NOT_EVALUABLE`; otherwise the ratio is the carried total divided by the required total, rounded half up to four decimal places. A ratio strictly below 0.9000 flags the filing `DEFICIENT`; a ratio from 0.9000 up to and including 1.1000 flags it `ADEQUATE`; a ratio above 1.1000 flags it `REDUNDANT`. Both flag boundaries are inclusive on the `ADEQUATE` side.

The **premium scope** is separate. `in_scope_premium_total` is the sum of `premium_net` over the policy terms whose policy's insured belongs to the filing's specialty class, whose status is `IN_FORCE`, `EXPIRED` or `CANCELLED_PRO_RATA`, whose effective date is on or before the experience period end and whose expiration date is strictly after the experience period start. Terms in `DRAFT`, `ISSUED` or `CANCELLED_FLAT` status contribute no premium at all. `actuarial_incurred_total` is the sum of `actuarial_incurred` over the same claim scope the adequacy review used. When the in-scope premium is zero, `experience_loss_ratio` and `indicated_change_pct` are both null; otherwise `experience_loss_ratio` is the actuarial incurred total divided by the in-scope premium total, rounded half up to four decimal places.

Under `LOSS_RATIO_METHOD`, `indicated_change_pct` is the experience loss ratio divided by the permissible loss ratio of 0.6500, less one, multiplied by 100, rounded half up to two decimal places. Under `PURE_PREMIUM_METHOD` the same target loss ratio is used, but per claim: a pure premium is the actuarial incurred total divided by `claim_count_in_scope`, rounded to two decimal places; a target premium is the in-scope premium total divided by `claim_count_in_scope`, also rounded to two decimal places; and `indicated_change_pct` is the pure premium divided by the product of the target premium and 0.6500, less one, multiplied by 100, rounded half up to two decimal places.

Credibility then tempers the indication. `credibility_weight` is 1.00 when `claim_count_in_scope` is at least 30, 0.60 when it is at least 10 but below 30, and 0.30 otherwise. `credibility_weighted_indication_pct` blends the already-rounded indication with a trend complement of 4.50 percent: it is the credibility weight multiplied by `indicated_change_pct`, plus one minus that weight multiplied by 4.50, rounded half up to two decimal places. When `indicated_change_pct` is null the weighted indication is null as well, though `credibility_weight` is still assigned from the claim count.

Submission moves the filing from `DRAFT` to `SUBMITTED` and takes no other action beyond the block refresh. The decision is reserved to a `ChiefActuary` and carries a `decision` of `APPROVE`, `REJECT` or `WITHDRAW` together with a `decision_note`. An approval requires the filing to be `SUBMITTED`, requires the deciding `X-User-ID` to differ from `prepared_by_user_id`, and requires the filing's `adequacy_flag` not to be `DEFICIENT` — a deficiency must be cured and the filing resubmitted, and an attempt to approve a deficient filing is refused with `ERR_RESERVE_DEFICIENCY_BLOCKS_FILING`. A rejection requires only `SUBMITTED` status and the same segregation-of-duties separation; it records `decided_by_user_id` and the note and leaves `base_rate_per_million` untouched. A withdrawal is permitted from either `DRAFT` or `SUBMITTED`, records only the note, and is exempt from the preparer separation. Any decision attempted from a status the filing is not in is refused with `ERR_FILING_STATE_INVALID`, and a submission attempted on a filing that is not `DRAFT` is refused the same way.

Approval is the one decision with consequences outside the filing. `DRAFT` → `SUBMITTED` → `APPROVED` sets `approved_rate_change_pct` to the filing's `proposed_rate_change_pct`, records `decided_by_user_id` and the note, and then rewrites the class's manual rate: the new `base_rate_per_million` is the old base rate multiplied by one plus the approved change expressed as a fraction of 100, rounded half up to two decimal places. The class's `rate_revision_index` increments by 1 and its `last_approved_rate_change_pct` records the change just approved. Finally every policy term still in `DRAFT` on the policies of insureds belonging to that class is re-rated against the new base rate, and each affected policy's `quoted_premium_total` is recomputed.

### 3.2. Onboarding an Insured: Registration, Credentialing and Risk Classification

An `UnderwritingOfficer` registers an **insured** by supplying its `entity_type` — `PHYSICIAN` or `FACILITY` — its `legal_name`, the `specialty_class_id` that sets its rating specialty, its primary litigation `venue_code` from `COOK_IL`, `MIAMI_DADE_FL`, `KINGS_NY`, `TRAVIS_TX` or `MARICOPA_AZ`, and `high_severity_procedure_pct`, the share of the annual procedure mix falling in the high-severity band, which must lie between 0 and 100 inclusive. Two further inputs are optional: `years_in_practice`, a non-negative integer defaulting to 0 that later gates death, disability and retirement tail eligibility, and `board_certified`, a boolean defaulting to false. A registration naming a specialty class that does not exist is refused with `ERR_SPECIALTY_CLASS_NOT_FOUND`. The new record opens with `status` `PROSPECT` on the underwriting axis and `credentialing_status` `PENDING` on the credentialing axis, with `credentialing_expires_on` null while the verdict is `PENDING`.

Registration also scores the insured. **Risk classification** produces a score from three weighted components. The specialty component is simply the `hazard_index` of the insured's specialty class. The procedure component is `high_severity_procedure_pct` divided by 10, rounded half up to four decimal places. The claim-history component starts from a raw figure and is then capped: the raw figure weights each closed paid claim at 2.0 and, in the renewal reading only, each open claim at 1.0, and the component is the lesser of 10.0 and that raw figure. `risk_class_score` is 0.40 times the specialty component, plus 0.35 times the procedure component, plus 0.25 times the capped history component, rounded half up to two decimal places. The score maps to `risk_class_code` on bands whose lower bound is inclusive and upper bound exclusive: below 3.00 is `R1`, from 3.00 up to but not including 5.00 is `R2`, from 5.00 up to but not including 7.50 is `R3`, and 7.50 or above is `R4`. `risk_class_factor` is the multiplicative rating factor for that band — 0.85 for `R1`, 1.00 for `R2`, 1.30 for `R3` and 1.75 for `R4`. The classification reads only the insured's specialty class and the insured's own two maintained counters; it never rescans claim records.

Those two counters are the reason the classification has two readings. `closed_paid_claim_count` is the number of claims against this insured whose handling status is `RESOLVED` or `CLOSED` and whose `paid_indemnity` is greater than zero. `open_claim_count` is the number whose handling status is `INTAKE`, `INVESTIGATION` or `LITIGATION` and whose coverage position is anything other than `DENIED`. Under the **`UNDERWRITING_SNAPSHOT`** reading, open claims are excluded from the history component entirely and only closed paid claims count. Under the **`RENEWAL_PROSPECTIVE`** reading, open claims are added to the history component at weight 1.0 alongside closed paid claims at weight 2.0, so the same underlying facts can land a renewal quote in a higher band than the insured's own record shows. The `risk_class_score`, `risk_class_code` and `risk_class_factor` stored on the insured are always the `UNDERWRITING_SNAPSHOT` reading. Both counters are recomputed, and the insured's classification re-derived from them, whenever a claim is intaken, whenever a coverage decision denies coverage, whenever a claim is disposed of, and whenever a settlement is paid.

**Credentialing** advances on its own axis. An `UnderwritingOfficer` records a verdict by supplying `credentialing_status` — `PENDING`, `PROVISIONAL`, `CREDENTIALED`, `LAPSED` or `DENIED` — with an optional `credentialing_expires_on` naming the date the verdict expires. Recording `LAPSED` or `DENIED` on an insured whose underwriting status is `ACTIVE` drives that status to `SUSPENDED`. Recording `CREDENTIALED` or `PROVISIONAL` on an insured whose status is `SUSPENDED` returns it to `ACTIVE`, provided the submitted expiry date is on or after the current logical date; this is the cycle that lets a suspended insured be reinstated, and no credentialing verdict is permanent — even a `DENIED` insured may later be credentialed. A verdict addressed to an insured that does not exist is refused with `ERR_INSURED_NOT_FOUND`.

The two axes meet in exactly three places. An insured moves from `PROSPECT` to `ACTIVE` when the first term on one of its policies is issued. An insured in `ACTIVE` status drops to `SUSPENDED` when the credentialing clock sweep lapses a `CREDENTIALED` or `PROVISIONAL` verdict whose expiry date has fallen behind the logical date. And an insured in `ACTIVE` status drops to `SUSPENDED` when a peer review committee returns a major deviation — a conditional cascade that leaves a `PROSPECT` insured untouched. A suspended insured may not have a new policy quoted for it; that attempt is refused with `ERR_INSURED_STATE_INVALID`.

### 3.3. Risk-Management Credits and Peer Review Surcharges

Participation in the carrier's loss-prevention programmes earns premium credit. An `UnderwritingOfficer` enrols an insured by supplying a `program_code` — `CME_RISK_101`, `SIM_LAB`, `CHART_AUDIT`, `TEAMSTEPPS` or `EHR_SAFETY` — an `enrolled_on` date, and the `expected_insured_version` described in §2.9. The insured may not already hold an enrollment in the same programme that is still `ENROLLED`; a second concurrent enrollment in the same programme is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PROGRAM_ENROLLMENT`. A fresh enrollment opens in `ENROLLED` status with `completed_on`, `attestation_reference` and `expires_on` all null and `credit_pct_snapshot` at 0, because nothing has yet been earned, and it increments the insured's `version` by 1.

Completion is recorded against an enrollment still in `ENROLLED` status — a completion recorded on an enrollment that is `COMPLETED` or `EXPIRED` is refused with `ERR_ENROLLMENT_STATE_INVALID`, and one addressed to an enrollment that does not exist with `ERR_ENROLLMENT_NOT_FOUND`. The officer supplies `completed_on`, which must be on or after `enrolled_on` (an earlier date is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`), and an `attestation_reference` pointing at the attestation document. `ENROLLED` → `COMPLETED` freezes `credit_pct_snapshot` at the credit percentage published for that programme, sets `expires_on` to 730 calendar days after `completed_on`, recomputes the insured's stacked credit, re-rates every `DRAFT` term on the insured's policies and recomputes each affected policy's `quoted_premium_total`. When the clock sweep later reaches an enrollment's `expires_on`, `COMPLETED` → `EXPIRED` fires, the stacked credit is recomputed without it, and the drafted terms are re-rated again. `EXPIRED` is terminal.

The **stacked credit** an insured carries as of a given date is assembled from its completed enrollments. Eligible enrollments are those in `COMPLETED` status whose `completed_on` is on or before the date in question and no earlier than 730 calendar days before it — a two-year lookback. Within that set, at most one credit counts per `program_code`: where an insured has completed the same programme more than once, the row with the latest `completed_on` wins, and an exact tie on `completed_on` is broken by the earlier creation timestamp. The surviving rows' credit percentages are then added — three percent for `CME_RISK_101`, five percent for `SIM_LAB`, four percent for `CHART_AUDIT`, two percent for `TEAMSTEPPS` and two and a half percent for `EHR_SAFETY` — the sum is rounded half up to four decimal places, and the result is capped at ten percent however many programmes the insured has completed. Enrollments in `ENROLLED` or `EXPIRED` status contribute nothing.

Peer review pulls in the opposite direction. A `PeerReviewChair` or a `ClaimsAdjuster` refers an insured to **peer review** by supplying `insured_id`, a `trigger_source` of `CLAIM_INTAKE`, `CREDENTIALING_REVIEW`, `SENTINEL_EVENT` or `COMPLAINT`, and a free-text `referral_note`. The case opens in `REFERRED` with both review tracks unrecorded, so `chart_review_outcome`, `external_review_outcome`, `committee_vote_result`, `votes_for`, `votes_against` and `closed_on` are all null and `surcharge_pct_assigned` is 0.

A referred case is worked by two independent review tracks that may land in either order. A `PeerReviewChair` records one by supplying a `review_track` of `CHART` or `EXTERNAL` and an `outcome` of `NO_CONCERN`, `CONCERN` or `INDETERMINATE`; a `CHART` submission writes `chart_review_outcome` and an `EXTERNAL` submission writes `external_review_outcome`. The first track recorded moves the case `REFERRED` → `IN_REVIEW`. The second moves it `IN_REVIEW` → `REVIEWS_COMPLETE`, the join at which both tracks have landed. Recording a track that has already been recorded on the case is refused with `ERR_REVIEW_TRACK_ALREADY_RECORDED`, and recording a track on a case that is neither `REFERRED` nor `IN_REVIEW` is refused with `ERR_PEER_REVIEW_STATE_INVALID`. A case addressed by an identifier that resolves to nothing yields `ERR_PEER_REVIEW_CASE_NOT_FOUND`.

Only from `REVIEWS_COMPLETE` may the committee vote, and the vote is what closes the case. The chair supplies a `vote_result` of `NO_DEVIATION`, `DEVIATION_MINOR` or `DEVIATION_MAJOR` together with `votes_for` and `votes_against`. The vote must carry a majority — `votes_for` strictly greater than `votes_against` — or it is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`; a vote submitted from any status other than `REVIEWS_COMPLETE` is refused with `ERR_PEER_REVIEW_STATE_INVALID`. `REVIEWS_COMPLETE` → `CLOSED` records the verdict, the two vote counts and the chair's `chair_user_id`, freezes `surcharge_pct_assigned` at the surcharge published for that verdict, and sets `closed_on` to the current logical date. It then recomputes the insured's `surcharge_pct`, re-rates every `DRAFT` term on the insured's policies, recomputes each affected policy's `quoted_premium_total`, and — when the verdict is `DEVIATION_MAJOR` and the insured's underwriting status is `ACTIVE` — suspends the insured. `CLOSED` is terminal.

An insured's **surcharge position** as of a given date is drawn from its closed peer review cases whose `closed_on` is on or before that date and no earlier than 1,095 calendar days before it — a thirty-six-month window. Cases still `REFERRED`, `IN_REVIEW` or `REVIEWS_COMPLETE` contribute nothing whatever their tracks say. Surcharges do not stack: where several closed cases fall inside the window, the position is the greatest of their surcharge percentages, which are zero percent for `NO_DEVIATION`, ten percent for `DEVIATION_MINOR` and twenty-five percent for `DEVIATION_MAJOR`. With no qualifying case the position is zero. The figure is carried to four decimal places.

### 3.4. Quoting a Policy and Rating the Claims-Made Step Chain

An `UnderwritingOfficer` quotes a **policy** for an insured by supplying `insured_id`, the `coverage_form` — `CLAIMS_MADE` or `OCCURRENCE` — the `limit_per_claim`, which must be exactly one of 1000000.00, 2000000.00, 3000000.00 or 5000000.00, the annual `limit_aggregate`, which must be greater than zero with at most two decimal places and is not required to be at least the per-claim limit, and the `consent_to_settle_clause` governing the physician's veto rights, drawn from `ABSOLUTE`, `HAMMER` and `NONE`. Two optional inputs shape the step chain: `retroactive_date`, the prior-acts retroactive date, and `prior_acts_step_credit`, an integer between 0 and 4 inclusive defaulting to 0 that counts step years credited from the expiring carrier. A claims-made policy must carry a retroactive date and an occurrence policy must omit it or send it null; either violation is refused with `ERR_RETRO_DATE_INVALID`. Quoting for an insured that does not exist yields `ERR_INSURED_NOT_FOUND`, and quoting for a `SUSPENDED` insured yields `ERR_INSURED_STATE_INVALID`. The new policy receives a `policy_number` of the form `MPL-` followed by a six-digit zero-padded creation sequence assigned in creation-timestamp ascending order beginning at `MPL-000001`, opens in `QUOTED` status with `current_term_id` null, and starts with `quoted_premium_total` and `written_premium_net_total` both at 0.00.

A policy earns premium only through its **terms**. Adding one requires `effective_date`, `expiration_date`, the `expected_policy_version` of §2.9, and optionally `predecessor_term_id` naming the immediately preceding term in the step chain. The expiration date must be strictly after the effective date, or the request is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`. When a predecessor is supplied, the new term's effective date must equal the predecessor's expiration date exactly — the step chain admits neither a gap nor a back-dated overlap — and any other value is refused with `ERR_TERM_PERIOD_GAP`. A predecessor that is `CANCELLED_FLAT` may not be built on, and a term named as a predecessor in any other status is acceptable; the flat-cancelled case is refused with `ERR_TERM_STATE_INVALID`. Independently, the new term's half-open period must not intersect the period of any existing term on the same policy whose status is not `CANCELLED_FLAT`; sharing a single boundary date is legal adjacency, while a genuine intersection is refused with `ERR_TERM_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. Creating the term increments the policy's `version` by 1.

`term_index` is 1 when no predecessor is supplied and one more than the predecessor's index otherwise. `rating_variant` follows from the same fact: a first term with no predecessor is rated `UNDERWRITING_SNAPSHOT`, and a term with a predecessor is rated `RENEWAL_PROSPECTIVE`. The term also snapshots `rate_revision_index_used` from the specialty class's `rate_revision_index` at the moment it was last rated, so a reader can tell which edition of the rate manual produced the figures. A newly created term opens in `DRAFT` status with `return_premium` at 0, `aggregate_limit_remaining` at 0, and `cancel_effective_date` and `issued_at` null.

**Rating a term** is a fixed sequence, and the sequence is the specification. First the step position: on a claims-made policy, `claims_made_step_year` is the lesser of 5 and the sum of the term's `term_index` and the policy's `prior_acts_step_credit`, and `step_factor` is the factor published for that step year — 0.35 for step 1, 0.60 for step 2, 0.80 for step 3, 0.92 for step 4 and 1.00 for step 5. On an occurrence policy the step machinery does not apply: `claims_made_step_year` is 5 and `step_factor` is always 1.00. Next the three multiplicative factors. `increased_limits_factor` is keyed on the policy's per-claim limit: 1.00 at one million, 1.45 at two million, 1.75 at three million and 2.10 at five million. `venue_severity_factor` is keyed on the insured's `venue_code`: 1.45 for `COOK_IL`, 1.60 for `MIAMI_DADE_FL`, 1.35 for `KINGS_NY`, 1.05 for `TRAVIS_TX` and 1.00 for `MARICOPA_AZ`. `rated_risk_class_code` and `rated_risk_class_factor` are the risk band and factor produced by running the classification of §3.2 under *this term's* rating variant, and both are frozen on the term.

The manual premium is then built on a four-stage ladder, and each stage is rounded half up to two decimal places before the next stage multiplies it — intermediate values are deliberately not carried at full precision. Stage one multiplies the specialty class's `base_rate_per_million` by `increased_limits_factor`. Stage two multiplies that result by `rated_risk_class_factor`. Stage three multiplies that result by `venue_severity_factor`. Stage four multiplies that result by `step_factor`, and the outcome is `premium_manual`.

Credit and surcharge are applied together on top of the manual premium. `credit_pct_applied` is the insured's stacked credit as of this term's `effective_date`, already capped at ten percent, and `surcharge_pct_applied` is the insured's surcharge position as of the same date. `premium_net` is `premium_manual` multiplied by the quantity one minus `credit_pct_applied` plus `surcharge_pct_applied`, rounded half up to two decimal places.

Fees follow, in a pinned order that matters because each is keyed on a different base. `administrative_fee` is a flat charge selected from three tiers on `premium_net` alone — 250.00 when `premium_net` is below 25000.00, 500.00 when it is at least 25000.00 but below 100000.00, and 900.00 when it is 100000.00 or more. `guaranty_fund_assessment` is one and a quarter percent of the sum of `premium_net` and `administrative_fee`, rounded half up to two decimal places. `term_total` is `premium_net` plus `administrative_fee` plus `guaranty_fund_assessment`, rounded half up to two decimal places. Neither the fee tier nor the assessment is keyed on `term_total`.

Only terms in `DRAFT` status are ever re-rated. Once a term is `ISSUED`, `IN_FORCE` or terminal, its whole rating block — from `step_factor` through `term_total` — is frozen at the figures it was bound with, whatever later happens to the insured's credits, surcharges, claim counters or the specialty class's manual rate.

### 3.5. Binding Coverage, Cancelling a Term and Extending the Reporting Period

Issuing a drafted term is what binds coverage, and it takes no payload at all: the term is addressed by identity, and everything the operation needs is already on the record. Only a term in `DRAFT` may be issued — any other status is refused with `ERR_TERM_STATE_INVALID`, and an unknown term with `ERR_POLICY_TERM_NOT_FOUND`. Issuance also tests the insured's credentials against the term being bound: the insured's `credentialing_status` must be `CREDENTIALED` or `PROVISIONAL`, and its `credentialing_expires_on` must be on or after the term's `effective_date`. A term whose coverage period would begin after the insured's credentials expire cannot be bound, and either failure is refused with `ERR_CREDENTIALING_NOT_VALID_FOR_TERM`.

Where issuance lands depends on the logical clock. When the term's `effective_date` is on or before the current logical date, `DRAFT` → `IN_FORCE` fires and coverage is live immediately. When the effective date is still in the future, `DRAFT` → `ISSUED` fires and the term waits for the clock sweep to promote it. Both branches behave identically in every other respect: `issued_at` records the current logical timestamp, `aggregate_limit_remaining` is set to the policy's `limit_aggregate`, the policy's `written_premium_net_total` and `quoted_premium_total` are recomputed, `current_term_id` is re-derived, the policy's `status` becomes `ACTIVE`, and an insured still in `PROSPECT` is advanced to `ACTIVE`. A term promoted later by the clock sweep, `ISSUED` → `IN_FORCE`, changes only its own status and the policy's `current_term_id`. When the sweep expires a live term, `IN_FORCE` → `EXPIRED`, the policy's `current_term_id` is re-derived and the policy's `status` bubbles up to `EXPIRED` once every term on it has reached a terminal status and at least one of them is `EXPIRED`.

Cancellation is the branch point in a term's life, and the two branches differ in more than their arithmetic. An `UnderwritingOfficer` supplies a `cancel_type` of `FLAT` or `PRO_RATA` together with a `cancel_effective_date`. The term must be `ISSUED` or `IN_FORCE`, or the request is refused with `ERR_TERM_STATE_INVALID`. A flat cancellation requires `cancel_effective_date` to equal the term's `effective_date` exactly; a pro-rata cancellation requires it to lie strictly after the effective date and strictly before the expiration date. Either dating violation is refused with `ERR_CANCEL_DATE_INVALID`.

A **flat cancellation** treats the coverage relationship as never having existed. `IN_FORCE` → `CANCELLED_FLAT` records the cancellation date, returns the whole net premium by setting `return_premium` to `premium_net`, and surrenders the limit by setting `aggregate_limit_remaining` to 0.00. It then rescinds what the term supported: every claim whose responding term is this term and whose coverage position is not already `DENIED` has its `coverage_status` set to `DENIED` with `denial_reason` `TERM_VOIDED`; every settlement negotiation on those claims whose status is neither `PAID` nor `ABANDONED` is moved to `ABANDONED`; and every tail endorsement on the same policy that is `ISSUED` is moved `ISSUED` → `VOID` with `void_reason` recorded as `POLICY_RESCINDED_BY_FLAT_CANCELLATION`, because rescinding the relationship from inception leaves no reporting obligation for a tail to extend. The policy's `written_premium_net_total` is recomputed, its `current_term_id` re-derived, and its `status` set to `CANCELLED` once no term on it remains in `DRAFT`, `ISSUED` or `IN_FORCE`.

A **pro-rata cancellation** ends coverage prospectively and disturbs nothing already reported. `IN_FORCE` → `CANCELLED_PRO_RATA` records the cancellation date and returns only the unearned portion of the premium: the term's whole span in calendar days is measured from `effective_date` to `expiration_date`, the unearned span is measured from `cancel_effective_date` to `expiration_date`, and `return_premium` is `premium_net` multiplied by the unearned span divided by the whole span, with that single ratio applied at full precision and the result rounded half up to two decimal places once. `aggregate_limit_remaining` is left exactly as it was, claims already reported under the term keep the coverage position they had, no tail endorsement is voided, the policy's `written_premium_net_total` is recomputed, its `current_term_id` re-derived, and its `status` set to `CANCELLED`. Both cancellation branches are terminal for the term.

A claims-made policy leaves an exposure behind when its last term ends: incidents that occurred during the term but are reported afterwards. An **extended reporting period endorsement** — a tail — closes it. An `UnderwritingOfficer` quotes one against a policy by supplying the `triggering_term_id` of the term whose reporting obligation is being extended, a `tail_type` of `ERP_5YR`, `ERP_UNLIMITED` or `DEATH_DISABILITY_RETIREMENT`, a `trigger_reason` of `RETIREMENT`, `DISABILITY`, `DEATH`, `CARRIER_CHANGE` or `PRACTICE_CLOSURE`, and the `effective_date` on which the extended window opens. Tails exist only on claims-made business — quoting one on an occurrence policy is refused with `ERR_TAIL_NOT_APPLICABLE`, since an occurrence form already answers for incidents reported after expiry. The triggering term must be `EXPIRED` or `CANCELLED_PRO_RATA` (any other status is refused with `ERR_TERM_STATE_INVALID`) and must belong to the addressed policy, and the tail's `effective_date` must be on or after that term's `expiration_date`, or its `cancel_effective_date` where the term was cancelled pro rata; an earlier date is refused with `ERR_TAIL_EFFECTIVE_DATE_INVALID`.

Pricing a tail deliberately ignores the discount the insured actually paid. `mature_premium_basis` is the triggering term's `premium_manual` divided by that term's `step_factor`, rounded half up to two decimal places — dividing the step factor out reconstructs the mature, step-five premium the term would have carried at full maturity. `tail_factor` is 1.55 for `ERP_5YR`, 2.00 for `ERP_UNLIMITED` and 0.00 for `DEATH_DISABILITY_RETIREMENT`, and `tail_premium` is `mature_premium_basis` multiplied by `tail_factor`, rounded half up to two decimal places. `reporting_window_end` closes the window: for `ERP_5YR` it is five years after the `effective_date` less one day, so that the closed window spans exactly five years; for `ERP_UNLIMITED` and `DEATH_DISABILITY_RETIREMENT` it is `9999-12-31`, an effectively unlimited window. The free death, disability and retirement form is gated rather than open: it requires the insured's `years_in_practice` to be at least 5 **and** the triggering term's `claims_made_step_year` to be at least 5, both bounds inclusive, and a request failing either is refused with `ERR_DDR_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. Finally, the new tail's closed window must not intersect the closed window of any `ISSUED` tail on the same policy; because both endpoints are inclusive, two windows sharing even a single date do intersect and the quote is refused with `ERR_TAIL_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. A quoted tail opens in `QUOTED` status with `issued_at` and `void_reason` null.

Issuing a tail takes no payload and requires `QUOTED` status; any other status is refused with `ERR_TAIL_STATE_INVALID`, and an unknown endorsement with `ERR_TAIL_ENDORSEMENT_NOT_FOUND`. `QUOTED` → `ISSUED` records `issued_at` at the current logical timestamp and opens the extended reporting window as a coverage trigger available at claim intake. At most one tail per triggering term may be live: if another endorsement on the same `triggering_term_id` is already `ISSUED`, issuance is refused with `ERR_TAIL_ALREADY_ISSUED`. Both `ISSUED` and `VOID` are terminal for a tail.

### 3.6. Claim Intake, Coverage Trigger Resolution and the Coverage Position

A `ClaimsAdjuster` intakes a **claim** by supplying the `insured_id` against whom it is asserted, the `incident_date` of the alleged act, error or omission, the `report_date` on which it was first reported to the carrier, the `venue_code` in which it is or would be litigated, an `allegation_category` of `DIAGNOSIS_FAILURE`, `SURGICAL_ERROR`, `MEDICATION_ERROR`, `OBSTETRIC_INJURY` or `INFORMED_CONSENT`, and the plaintiff's `demand_amount`, which must be non-negative and carry at most two decimal places. Intake against an insured that does not exist yields `ERR_INSURED_NOT_FOUND`, and a claim whose `report_date` falls earlier than its `incident_date` is refused with `ERR_REPORT_DATE_BEFORE_INCIDENT`. The claim opens with `claim_status` `INTAKE` on the handling axis and, unless coverage is denied outright at intake, `coverage_status` `UNDER_REVIEW` on the coverage axis. All of its money fields — `indemnity_reserve`, `expense_reserve`, `paid_indemnity`, `paid_expense`, `case_incurred`, `actuarial_incurred` and `insured_share_receivable` — start at 0, `reserve_method_current` is null, `counsel_reassignment_required` is false, `excluded_counsel_firm_ids` is empty, and `disposition`, `verdict_amount`, `suit_filed_on`, `resolved_on`, `closed_on` and `reserved_rights_reason` are all null.

Intake's central act is **resolving which coverage responds**, and the answer is written to the claim as `policy_term_id`, `tail_endorsement_id`, `coverage_status` and, where coverage fails, `denial_reason`. Neither pointer is client-supplied. The candidate set holds the policy terms belonging to policies of the claim's insured whose status is `IN_FORCE`, `EXPIRED` or `CANCELLED_PRO_RATA`; terms in `DRAFT`, `ISSUED` or `CANCELLED_FLAT` status never respond to anything.

On a **claims-made** policy the report date triggers. A candidate term responds when its `effective_date` is on or before the `report_date` and the `report_date` falls strictly before its `expiration_date`. If at least one term responds but the claim's `incident_date` falls strictly before the policy's `retroactive_date`, prior acts are excluded: `coverage_status` is set to `DENIED` with `denial_reason` `PRIOR_ACTS_EXCLUDED` and `policy_term_id` is left null. If at least one term responds and the incident is not excluded by the retroactive date, `policy_term_id` names the responding term with the latest `effective_date`, ties broken by the earlier creation timestamp, and `coverage_status` is `UNDER_REVIEW`. If no term responds, the extended reporting windows are consulted: a tail endorsement responds when it is `ISSUED`, when the `report_date` falls inside its closed window from `effective_date` through `reporting_window_end` inclusive, and when the period of its triggering term contains the claim's `incident_date`. Where one or more tails respond, `tail_endorsement_id` names the tail with the latest `effective_date` (ties again broken by the earlier creation timestamp), `policy_term_id` is set to that tail's `triggering_term_id`, and `coverage_status` is `UNDER_REVIEW`. Where no tail responds either, `coverage_status` is `DENIED` with `denial_reason` `NO_RESPONDING_COVERAGE`. Tails in `QUOTED` or `VOID` status never respond.

On an **occurrence** policy the incident date triggers and the report date is irrelevant. A candidate term responds when its `effective_date` is on or before the `incident_date` and the `incident_date` falls strictly before its `expiration_date`. Where one or more respond, `policy_term_id` names the one with the latest `effective_date` and `coverage_status` is `UNDER_REVIEW`; where none respond, `coverage_status` is `DENIED` with `denial_reason` `NO_RESPONDING_COVERAGE`. The retroactive-date test and the tail search play no part on an occurrence form.

Independently of how coverage resolved, intake assigns the claim's `severity_band` from the demand. A demand below 250000.00 is `BAND_1`; from 250000.00 up to but not including 750000.00 is `BAND_2`; from 750000.00 up to but not including 2000000.00 is `BAND_3`; 2000000.00 or above is `BAND_4`. `severity_band_factor` follows the band: 0.20, 0.32, 0.45 and 0.60 respectively. Both are written even on a denied claim. Intake finally recomputes the insured's `open_claim_count` and re-derives its risk classification, then re-rates every `DRAFT` term whose rating variant is `RENEWAL_PROSPECTIVE` on that insured's policies — the mechanism by which a fresh claim immediately makes an unbound renewal quote more expensive.

The adjuster's **coverage position** is then recorded on the coverage axis, and this is the only operation that may move it. The submitted `coverage_status` is `COVERED`, `RESERVED_RIGHTS` or `DENIED`, with an optional `reserved_rights_reason` narrative that is recorded when the position taken is `RESERVED_RIGHTS`. A denial recorded here sets `denial_reason` to `ADJUSTER_DENIAL`, distinguishing an adjuster's coverage call from the two denials the trigger resolution can produce and from the `TERM_VOIDED` denial a flat cancellation produces. The claim's handling status must be `INTAKE` — any other handling status is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID` — and its current coverage position must be `UNDER_REVIEW` or `RESERVED_RIGHTS`; a claim already `COVERED` or `DENIED` cannot have coverage re-decided and the attempt is refused with `ERR_COVERAGE_ALREADY_DECIDED`. All three outcomes advance the handling axis `INTAKE` → `INVESTIGATION`, including a denial: coverage may be refused while the file still has to be worked. Where the position taken is `DENIED`, the insured's `open_claim_count` and risk classification are recomputed, since a denied claim no longer counts as open exposure.

Litigation is recorded separately and touches only the handling axis. The adjuster supplies `suit_filed_on`, which must be on or after the claim's `report_date` or the request is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`; the claim must be in `INVESTIGATION`, or `ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID` applies. `INVESTIGATION` → `LITIGATION` records the filing date and leaves `coverage_status` exactly as it was — a claim may litigate under a reservation of rights, and the two axes never move each other here.

### 3.7. Reserving the Claim, Assigning Defence Counsel and Paying Expense

Reserves are posted, never edited. A `ClaimsAdjuster` posts a **reserve entry** against a claim in `INTAKE`, `INVESTIGATION` or `LITIGATION` handling status — any other status is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID` — supplying a `method` of `CASE_BASIS` or `FORMULA_BASIS`, the `effective_at` instant from which the posting is the reserve of record, a `rationale` narrative, the `expected_claim_version` of §2.9, and, depending on the method, `indemnity_amount` and `expense_amount`. The two methods have mutually exclusive inputs: `CASE_BASIS` requires both amounts and `FORMULA_BASIS` requires neither, and any other combination — a case-basis posting missing one amount, or a formula-basis posting carrying either — is refused with `ERR_RESERVE_METHOD_INPUT_MISMATCH`. The posting records `set_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID` and increments the claim's `version` by 1.

Each posting stores its own projection in `computed_indemnity_amount` and `computed_expense_amount`. Under `CASE_BASIS` those are simply the adjuster's `indemnity_amount` and `expense_amount`, each rounded half up to two decimal places and taken verbatim otherwise: neither the per-claim limit cap nor the expense floor is applied to an adjuster's own numbers. Under `FORMULA_BASIS` the system projects them. The raw indemnity is the claim's `demand_amount` multiplied by its `severity_band_factor` multiplied by the venue severity factor for the claim's own `venue_code`, rounded half up to two decimal places; `computed_indemnity_amount` is the lesser of that rounded figure and the policy's `limit_per_claim`, with the cap applied after the rounding. `computed_expense_amount` is the greater of two figures: twenty-eight percent of `computed_indemnity_amount` rounded half up to two decimal places, and an expense reserve floor of 15000.00 — again with the floor applied after the rounding.

Which posting governs the claim is decided by effective dating, not by arrival order. The **reserve of record** is the entry with the greatest `effective_at`; where two entries on the same claim share an `effective_at` exactly, the one with the greater `created_at` wins. An entry created later but effective earlier therefore does not displace the standing reserve. The claim's `indemnity_reserve`, `expense_reserve` and `reserve_method_current` are re-derived from that governing entry after every posting — 0.00, 0.00 and null respectively when the claim has no postings at all — and every posting on the claim is visible in the claim's append-only posting list, filterable by `method`.

Two incurred-loss figures are then recomputed from the claim's four money fields, and both are always maintained side by side. `case_incurred` is the figure the claims department reports and treats payments as having consumed the reserve: it is `paid_indemnity` plus `paid_expense`, plus the excess of `indemnity_reserve` over `paid_indemnity` where that excess is positive and zero otherwise, plus the excess of `expense_reserve` over `paid_expense` on the same clamped basis, rounded half up to two decimal places. `actuarial_incurred` is the figure that feeds rate filings and reserve adequacy, and it stacks payments on top of the full carried reserve: it is `paid_indemnity` plus `paid_expense` plus `indemnity_reserve` plus `expense_reserve`, rounded half up to two decimal places. Because of the two clamps, the actuarial figure is always greater than or equal to the case figure; the gap between them is exactly the lesser of `paid_indemnity` and `indemnity_reserve` plus the lesser of `paid_expense` and `expense_reserve`. Neither figure is rebuilt from posting history — both are derived from the claim row itself, and both are refreshed on every posting, every expense payment, every indemnity payment and every disposition.

Defence work is placed with **panel firms**. A `ClaimsCommitteeApprover` admits a firm by supplying its `firm_name`, the non-empty `admitted_venue_codes` array of venues where it may practise, the non-empty `allegation_specialties` array of allegation categories it is qualified to defend, its negotiated `blended_hourly_rate` (greater than zero, at most two decimal places), its `quality_score` (an integer from 0 to 100 inclusive), and optionally `max_concurrent_assignments`, a capacity ceiling of at least 1 defaulting to 5, and `conflict_insured_ids`, the insureds it is conflicted out of representing, defaulting to empty. A new firm opens with `panel_status` `ACTIVE`, `open_assignment_count` 0, `billed_expense_total` 0.00 and `panel_status_reason` null.

Assigning counsel to a claim is a selection, not a nomination: the adjuster supplies no payload, and the firm is chosen by the panel selector. A firm is eligible when its `panel_status` is `ACTIVE`, when the claim's `venue_code` appears in its `admitted_venue_codes`, when the claim's `allegation_category` appears in its `allegation_specialties`, when the claim's `insured_id` does not appear in its `conflict_insured_ids`, when its `open_assignment_count` is strictly below its `max_concurrent_assignments`, and when its identifier does not appear in the claim's `excluded_counsel_firm_ids`. Firms in `PROBATION` or `REMOVED` standing are excluded however well they otherwise qualify, and a firm at capacity is excluded rather than queued. `candidate_pool_size` records how many firms passed that filter. The survivors are ranked by `quality_score` descending, then by `blended_hourly_rate` ascending, then by creation timestamp ascending — never by identifier — and the first-ranked firm is selected, so `selection_rank_snapshot` on the resulting assignment is 1. An empty pool is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_COUNSEL`.

The claim must be in `INVESTIGATION` or `LITIGATION` for counsel to be assigned, or `ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID` applies. It must also carry a duty to defend: a claim whose `coverage_status` is `DENIED` gets no counsel, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_COVERAGE_DENIED_NO_DEFENSE`, while positions of `UNDER_REVIEW`, `COVERED` and `RESERVED_RIGHTS` all support an assignment. And a claim may hold only one live assignment: if it already has an assignment in `ASSIGNED` status, the request is refused with `ERR_COUNSEL_ALREADY_ASSIGNED`. A successful assignment records `claim_id` and the selected `counsel_firm_id`, opens in `ASSIGNED` status with `assigned_at` at the current logical timestamp, `billed_expense_amount` at 0.00 and `decline_reason` and `closed_at` null, increments the firm's `open_assignment_count` by 1, and sets the claim's `counsel_reassignment_required` back to false.

A firm may refuse the matter. The adjuster records a decline against an assignment in `ASSIGNED` status — any other status is refused with `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_STATE_INVALID` — supplying a `decline_reason` of `CONFLICT_OF_INTEREST`, `CAPACITY`, `RATE_DISPUTE` or `EXPERTISE_MISMATCH`. `ASSIGNED` → `DECLINED` records the reason and `closed_at`, decrements the firm's `open_assignment_count` by 1 so its capacity counter returns to its pre-assignment value, appends the firm's identifier to the claim's `excluded_counsel_firm_ids` so the selector will not choose it again for this claim, and sets the claim's `counsel_reassignment_required` to true.

Panel standing is administered by the same `ClaimsCommitteeApprover` role, which supplies a `panel_status` of `ACTIVE`, `PROBATION` or `REMOVED` together with a `reason` recorded as `panel_status_reason`. Moving a firm to `PROBATION` leaves its existing assignments untouched but stops it being eligible for future selection. Moving a firm to `REMOVED` also unwinds its live work: every assignment it holds in `ASSIGNED` status moves `ASSIGNED` → `REPLACED` with `closed_at` recorded, the firm's `open_assignment_count` is decremented once for each, the firm's identifier is appended to each affected claim's `excluded_counsel_firm_ids`, and each affected claim's `counsel_reassignment_required` is set to true — the signal that those claims need a fresh selector run. A standing change addressed to a firm that does not exist yields `ERR_COUNSEL_FIRM_NOT_FOUND`.

Defence expense is paid against a specific assignment. The adjuster supplies an `amount`, an `invoice_reference` and the `counsel_assignment_id` being billed under, on a claim in `INVESTIGATION` or `LITIGATION`. The named assignment must exist (`ERR_COUNSEL_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`), must belong to the addressed claim rather than another one (`ERR_ASSIGNMENT_CLAIM_MISMATCH`), and must still be in `ASSIGNED` status (`ERR_ASSIGNMENT_STATE_INVALID`). The payment increases the claim's `paid_expense`, the assignment's `billed_expense_amount` and the firm's `billed_expense_total` each by the paid amount, and recomputes both incurred figures — which is the point at which `case_incurred` and `actuarial_incurred` begin to diverge, since paid expense erodes the carried expense reserve in the first figure and sits on top of it in the second.

### 3.8. Settlement Negotiation, Consent to Settle and the Hammer Clause

A **settlement negotiation** is opened on a claim by either a `ClaimsAdjuster` or a `ClaimsCommitteeApprover`, who supplies the `proposed_amount` to be offered to the plaintiff, the `authority_requested` from the claims committee, and the `expected_claim_version` required by §2.9. The claim must be in `INVESTIGATION` or `LITIGATION`. It must also have coverage that can be charged: a claim whose `coverage_status` is `DENIED`, or which resolved to neither a responding `policy_term_id` nor a responding `tail_endorsement_id`, cannot be negotiated and the attempt is refused with `ERR_COVERAGE_DENIED_NO_DEFENSE` — the same error the assignment of counsel raises, since both defence and settlement presuppose a responding limit. The amount being negotiated must not exceed the responding policy's `limit_per_claim` less the claim's `paid_indemnity`, or the request is refused with `ERR_PER_CLAIM_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`; this ceiling is tested both here, when the offer is first framed, and again when it is disbursed.

A successful create increments the claim's `version` by 1 and opens the negotiation in `AUTHORITY_REQUESTED` status with `round_index` 1, `prepared_by_user_id` set to the acting `X-User-ID`, `authority_granted`, `approved_by_user_id`, `physician_response`, `veto_reason`, `consent_requested_at`, `paid_amount`, `payment_date` and `notice_sent_on` all null, `hammer_invoked` false, and `hammer_exposure_estimate`, `insured_share_amount` and `carrier_share_amount` at 0. Crucially, `consent_clause_applied` is frozen at creation from the responding policy's `consent_to_settle_clause`, so the negotiation carries its own copy of the physician's consent right for its whole life and is unaffected by anything later done to the policy record. Opening a negotiation also supersedes its predecessors: every other negotiation on the same claim whose status is not already `PAID` or `ABANDONED` is set to `ABANDONED`, whatever stage of the consent loop it had reached. That create-with-supersede is precisely why the write is guarded by optimistic concurrency on the claim — two adjusters framing offers concurrently would otherwise silently abandon each other's work.

The committee then records an **authority decision**. A `ClaimsCommitteeApprover` supplies a `decision` of `GRANT` or `DENY`, an `approver_note`, and, on a grant, a `granted_amount`. The negotiation must be in `AUTHORITY_REQUESTED`; from any other status the request is refused with `ERR_NEGOTIATION_STATE_INVALID`. The acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the negotiation's `prepared_by_user_id`: whoever framed the offer may not authorise it, and self-approval is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. On a grant, `granted_amount` must be present, strictly greater than zero and no greater than `authority_requested`; on a denial it must be absent. Every departure from that shape — a grant above the request, a grant of zero or a negative figure, a grant with no amount, or a denial carrying one — is refused with `ERR_AUTHORITY_GRANT_EXCEEDS_REQUEST`. Note what is deliberately *not* checked here: a grant below the `proposed_amount` is perfectly legal, because the committee is entitled to authorise less than the adjuster wants to offer. The mismatch surfaces only when the adjuster tries to disburse. `AUTHORITY_REQUESTED` → `AUTHORITY_GRANTED` records `authority_granted` and `approved_by_user_id`; `AUTHORITY_REQUESTED` → `AUTHORITY_DENIED` records `approved_by_user_id` and leaves `authority_granted` null.

With authority in hand, whether the insured has a say depends on the frozen clause. Under a `consent_clause_applied` of `NONE` the carrier may settle over the physician's head and no consent is tendered. Under `ABSOLUTE` or `HAMMER` the adjuster tenders the offer: `AUTHORITY_GRANTED` → `CONSENT_REQUESTED` takes no payload and stamps `consent_requested_at` with the current logical timestamp. Tendering consent on a negotiation whose clause is `NONE` is refused with `ERR_CONSENT_REQUIRED`, since there is no consent-to-settle provision to exercise.

The **consent response** is the one operation reserved to the `Insured` role, and the acting `X-User-ID` must be the claim's own `insured_id` or the request is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. The physician supplies a `response` of `CONSENTED` or `VETOED` and, on a veto, a `veto_reason`. `CONSENT_REQUESTED` → `CONSENT_GRANTED` records `physician_response` as `CONSENTED`; `CONSENT_REQUESTED` → `VETOED` records `physician_response` as `VETOED` together with the reason. Either way the negotiation must be in `CONSENT_REQUESTED`.

A refused offer is not the end. The negotiation is **cyclic**: a `ClaimsAdjuster` revises it from either `VETOED` or `AUTHORITY_DENIED` back to `AUTHORITY_REQUESTED`, supplying a fresh `proposed_amount` and `authority_requested`. The claim itself must still be in `INVESTIGATION` or `LITIGATION`. A revision increments `round_index` by 1, overwrites both amounts with the submitted values, resets `authority_granted`, `approved_by_user_id`, `physician_response` and `veto_reason` to null, and sets `prepared_by_user_id` to the revising `X-User-ID` — which means the segregation-of-duties test on the next authority decision is measured against the reviser, not the original preparer, and the approver who legitimately decided round one may be locked out of round two if it was that approver who revised. `consent_clause_applied`, `round_index` and the hammer fields are the only things carried forward; the same row may traverse the loop as many times as needed until it terminates at `PAID` or `ABANDONED`.

Where the clause is `HAMMER`, the carrier has a third option over a veto: **invoke the hammer**. A `ClaimsCommitteeApprover` supplies `notice_sent_on`, the date the hammer notice went to the insured. The negotiation must be in `VETOED` — a veto is the only thing a hammer overrides — and its `consent_clause_applied` must be `HAMMER`; invoking it under `ABSOLUTE`, which gives the physician an unqualified veto, or under `NONE`, where there is no veto to override, is refused with `ERR_HAMMER_NOT_AVAILABLE`. `VETOED` → `HAMMER_INVOKED` sets `hammer_invoked` to true, records `notice_sent_on`, and computes the insured's share of the consequences of refusing to settle.

That computation projects the verdict exposure the refusal creates and splits the excess. The projected exposure is the negotiation's `proposed_amount` multiplied by a hammer exposure multiplier of 1.60, capped at the responding policy's `limit_per_claim`, with the smaller of the two figures rounded half up to two decimal places and stored as `hammer_exposure_estimate`. The excess over the offer the physician rejected is the projected exposure less the `proposed_amount`, floored at zero. `insured_share_amount` is half of that excess — fifty percent — rounded half up to two decimal places, and `carrier_share_amount` is `hammer_exposure_estimate` less `insured_share_amount`, rounded the same way. The order of the three operations is pinned and materially changes the answer: the per-claim cap is applied before the excess is taken, and the excess is taken before the share split. A consequence worth stating plainly is that a very large offer produces a *smaller* insured share than the uncapped arithmetic would, because once 1.60 times the offer exceeds the per-claim limit the cap holds the projected exposure down while the offer keeps rising, squeezing the excess towards zero. Invocation also increases the claim's `insured_share_receivable` by `insured_share_amount`, the running total of what the carrier may charge back to insureds under invoked hammer clauses.

**Payment** disburses the settlement. A `ClaimsAdjuster` supplies the `payment_date`. Three statuses are payable — `AUTHORITY_GRANTED`, `CONSENT_GRANTED` and `HAMMER_INVOKED` — and any other status is refused with `ERR_NEGOTIATION_STATE_INVALID`. Payment directly from `AUTHORITY_GRANTED` is permitted only when `consent_clause_applied` is `NONE`; under `ABSOLUTE` or `HAMMER` the consent must first have been obtained or the hammer invoked, and skipping that step is refused with `ERR_CONSENT_REQUIRED` — the same error identifier as the misplaced consent request, reached from the opposite direction. The `proposed_amount` must not exceed `authority_granted`, which is where a partial grant finally bites: the offer must be reduced by a revision until it fits the authority, and disbursing over it is refused with `ERR_AUTHORITY_INSUFFICIENT`. The amount being disbursed must not exceed the responding term's `aggregate_limit_remaining` (`ERR_AGGREGATE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`) nor the policy's `limit_per_claim` less the claim's `paid_indemnity` (`ERR_PER_CLAIM_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`).

What is actually disbursed depends on the hammer. From `AUTHORITY_GRANTED` or `CONSENT_GRANTED`, `paid_amount` is the `proposed_amount`. From `HAMMER_INVOKED`, `paid_amount` is `carrier_share_amount` only: the carrier pays its share of the projected exposure and the balance stays on the claim's `insured_share_receivable` as a charge-back to the physician. In all three cases — `AUTHORITY_GRANTED` → `PAID`, `CONSENT_GRANTED` → `PAID` and `HAMMER_INVOKED` → `PAID` — the status becomes `PAID`, `payment_date` is recorded, and the full claim resolution cascade of §3.9 runs against the disbursed amount.

### 3.9. Resolving the Claim, Data Bank Reporting and Severity Trend Analysis

A claim that is tried, dismissed or withdrawn is resolved through the **disposition** operation. A `ClaimsAdjuster` supplies a `disposition` of `DEFENSE_VERDICT`, `PLAINTIFF_VERDICT`, `DISMISSED` or `WITHDRAWN`, a `resolved_on` date, and, on a plaintiff's verdict, the gross `verdict_amount` rendered. `SETTLED` is a declared member of the disposition enumeration but is rejected here with `ERR_VALIDATION`: a settlement is recorded only by disbursing a negotiation, never by asserting the disposition directly. The claim must be in `INVESTIGATION` or `LITIGATION`, and `resolved_on` must be on or after the claim's `report_date` or the request is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`.

The **resolution cascade** is identical whether resolution arrives through a disposition or through a settlement payment, and it is the single largest write in the system. The claim moves `INVESTIGATION` → `RESOLVED` or `LITIGATION` → `RESOLVED`, and `resolved_on` is recorded. Indemnity moves: on a `PLAINTIFF_VERDICT` the claim's `paid_indemnity` increases by the lesser of `verdict_amount` and the policy's `limit_per_claim`, and the responding term's `aggregate_limit_remaining` is debited by that same capped figure, so a verdict above the per-claim limit erodes the aggregate only to the extent the carrier actually owes it; on a settlement the increase and the debit are both the negotiation's `paid_amount`. On `DEFENSE_VERDICT`, `DISMISSED` and `WITHDRAWN` no indemnity is paid and no limit is debited. Both `indemnity_reserve` and `expense_reserve` are then set to 0.00 — a resolved claim carries no reserve, whatever its last reserve entry said, though the postings themselves remain in the append-only history — and `case_incurred` and `actuarial_incurred` are recomputed from the new figures, which after resolution converge on the sum of the payments. Every counsel assignment on the claim whose `assignment_status` is `ASSIGNED` moves `ASSIGNED` → `CLOSED` with `closed_at` recorded, and each affected firm's `open_assignment_count` is decremented by 1, releasing panel capacity. Finally the insured's `open_claim_count` and `closed_paid_claim_count` are recomputed and its `risk_class_code`, `risk_class_score` and `risk_class_factor` re-derived from the new counters. A settlement payment goes one step further than a disposition: it also re-rates every `DRAFT` term whose `rating_variant` is `RENEWAL_PROSPECTIVE` on the insured's policies, so the paid claim immediately reprices the pending renewal.

Resolution is not closure. A resolved claim sits open for a thirty-day run-off and is closed only by the clock: when the logical date reaches or passes `resolved_on` plus 30 days, `RESOLVED` → `CLOSED` fires as the sixth scheduled checkpoint, setting `closed_on` to the new current date. The identifiers of the claims closed in that sweep are returned in `closed_claim_ids`.

Every disbursement of indemnity on behalf of an insured creates exactly one **data bank reporting obligation**, and the obligation is created by the system rather than by any client operation. A plaintiff's verdict creates one with its `settlement_negotiation_id` null; a settlement payment creates one naming the negotiation that produced it. `claim_id` and `insured_id` are copied from the claim, `payment_amount` is the amount actually disbursed and `payment_date` the date it was disbursed. Dispositions of `DEFENSE_VERDICT`, `DISMISSED` and `WITHDRAWN` disburse nothing and create no obligation at all — not a voided one, but none.

Whether the obligation is genuinely reportable is decided by three conjoined tests: the `payment_amount` must be strictly greater than 0.00, the insured's `entity_type` must be `PHYSICIAN`, and the claim's `disposition` must be `SETTLED` or `PLAINTIFF_VERDICT`. When all three hold, `reportable` is true, `non_reportable_reason` is null and the obligation opens in `PENDING` status awaiting transmission. When they do not, the obligation is created already in `VOID` status with `reportable` false and a `non_reportable_reason` explaining why: `NON_PRACTITIONER_PAYMENT` whenever the insured is not a physician — the entity-type test is examined first and its answer governs — and `ZERO_DOLLAR_RESOLUTION` otherwise, which is the reason recorded for a physician payment that failed one of the other two tests. `void_reason` is populated at creation on those system-voided obligations exactly as it is on an explicit void. Regardless of reportability, `due_date` is set to `payment_date` plus 30 calendar days, the statutory submission deadline.

A `ComplianceOfficer` **transmits** a pending obligation, supplying the `submitted_on` date of transmission and a `submitter_note`. The obligation must be in `PENDING`, or the request is refused with `ERR_NPDB_STATE_INVALID`, and `submitted_on` must be on or after the obligation's `payment_date` or it is refused with `ERR_NPDB_SUBMISSION_DATE_INVALID`. `PENDING` → `SUBMITTED` records both inputs, then derives the punctuality fields from the deadline: `submitted_late` is true when `submitted_on` falls strictly after `due_date` and false otherwise, and `days_late` is the whole number of calendar days by which `submitted_on` exceeds `due_date`, floored at zero — a report transmitted on the due date itself is on time with zero days late. Transmission also increases the insured's `npdb_reported_payment_total` by the obligation's `payment_amount`, so that running total reflects only obligations actually reported, never pending or voided ones. The same officer may instead **void** a pending obligation, supplying a `void_reason`; `PENDING` → `VOID` records it. Both operations are refused on an obligation that is already `SUBMITTED` or `VOID`, and an obligation that does not exist yields `ERR_NPDB_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`. Obligations are listed filterable by `claim_id`, `settlement_negotiation_id`, `insured_id`, `report_status` and `reportable`.

The **severity trend analysis** is a computed read over resolved claim experience, addressed to a required `specialty_class_id` with optional `venue_code`, `from_report_date` and `to_report_date` narrowing. Its defining subtlety is that it reports over two different populations, and confusing them produces wrong answers on almost every input.

The *severity scope* holds the claims of insureds in the named specialty class whose `claim_status` is `RESOLVED` or `CLOSED` and whose `disposition` is `SETTLED` or `PLAINTIFF_VERDICT` — the claims that actually paid indemnity — further restricted, when the optional filters are supplied, to a matching `venue_code`, a `report_date` on or after `from_report_date` and a `report_date` on or before `to_report_date`. `closed_severity_claim_count` is the number of claims in that scope and `paid_indemnity_total` the sum of their `paid_indemnity`, rounded half up to two decimal places. When the scope is empty, `paid_severity_avg` and `trend_index` are both null rather than zero. Otherwise `paid_severity_avg` is `paid_indemnity_total` divided by `closed_severity_claim_count` rounded half up to two decimal places, and `trend_index` is that average divided by the specialty class's `benchmark_severity` rounded half up to four decimal places — a figure above 1 meaning the book is running worse than the industry benchmark it is measured against. `benchmark_severity` is echoed in the response so the denominator is visible. `top_allegation_category` is the `allegation_category` occurring most often in the severity scope, ties broken by the earliest creation timestamp among the tied claims, and null when the scope is empty.

The *verdict scope* applies the same specialty, venue and report-date filters and the same requirement of `RESOLVED` or `CLOSED` status, but selects on `disposition` in `DEFENSE_VERDICT` or `PLAINTIFF_VERDICT` — the claims that were tried, rather than the claims that paid. `defense_verdict_count` and `plaintiff_verdict_count` count each of those dispositions within it, and `defense_win_rate` is `defense_verdict_count` divided by the sum of the two counts, rounded half up to four decimal places, or null when no claim was tried at all. The two scopes overlap only on `PLAINTIFF_VERDICT`: a specialty whose only resolutions were settlements reports a severity average and trend index alongside a null `defense_win_rate` and two zero verdict counts, while a specialty whose only resolution was a defence verdict reports a defence win rate of 1.0000 alongside a null `paid_severity_avg` and a `paid_indemnity_total` of 0.00. The `specialty_class_id` and `venue_code` used are echoed back with the figures. A `specialty_class_id` naming no class yields `ERR_SPECIALTY_CLASS_NOT_FOUND`.
