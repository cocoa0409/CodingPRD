# Landfill Gas & RNG Operations API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A landfill gas-to-renewable-natural-gas project is three businesses stapled together. In the field, technicians tune a wellfield of extraction wells so that the gas coming out of the waste mass stays rich in methane and free of intruding air; in the plant yard, an upgrading skid strips carbon dioxide and oxygen until the product gas clears the interconnecting pipeline's injection specification; and on the commercial desk, that same molecule is nominated against a gas-day schedule, sold under an offtake contract, converted into federal D3 RINs or Californian LCFS credits, reported to two different regulators, and finally revenue-shared with the landfill owner who hosts the whole operation. Each of those businesses keeps its own books today, and the books disagree. A well that alarms at three in the morning silently starves the skid, the skid flares instead of injecting, the flare never lands in the environmental attribute calculation, and the host landfill is invoiced for a royalty on gas that was burned rather than sold.

This document specifies the API that makes those three businesses share one ledger. Every cubic foot of collected gas is tracked from the wellhead to one of exactly three destinations — the pipeline, the flare, or the atmosphere — and the system refuses to let the totals drift apart. Environmental attributes are generated only from energy that actually reached the pipeline, are frozen against the availability of the plant that produced them, and can be walked back when a regulator returns a filing. Royalty settlement reads the same injection ledger the compliance filing reads, so the host and the operator argue about business terms rather than about numbers.

The system is deliberately conservative about ordering and about who may act. Wells recover through evidence, not assertion. A compliance officer who prepared a filing may never be the one who reviews it. A commercial nomination written against a stale view of its contract is rejected rather than merged. What follows describes the global contracts every endpoint honours, the business rules that govern each lifecycle, and the complete endpoint and error surfaces.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to this API must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the caller, and `X-User-Role`, naming the authority the caller is acting under. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` holds a value that is not one of the seven declared roles, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` and HTTP 401. There is no anonymous surface and no unauthenticated health probe.

The seven roles are `ProjectAdmin`, `WellfieldTechnician`, `PlantOperator`, `CommercialScheduler`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `HostRepresentative`, and `Offtaker`. A `ProjectAdmin` is the project operator with the broadest surface: it registers sites, plants and wells, settles royalties, decommissions equipment and drives the logical clock. A `WellfieldTechnician` tunes wells, records gas quality readings, and executes collection-system maintenance and condensate hauling. A `PlantOperator` runs the upgrading skid's two state dimensions, logs downtime and flare backup, and produces injection batches. A `CommercialScheduler` staffs the commercial desk: offtake contracts, pipeline nominations, attaching batches to nominations, and the pipeline meter true-up. A `ComplianceOfficer` generates and stewards environmental attributes and prepares, reviews and accepts regulatory filings under segregation of duties. A `HostRepresentative` speaks for the landfill owner. An `Offtaker` is the counterparty buying the gas. When the acting role is not listed among the roles permitted for the endpoint being called, the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` and HTTP 403.

Five of the seven roles carry an opaque identity. When `X-User-Role` is `ProjectAdmin`, `WellfieldTechnician`, `PlantOperator`, `CommercialScheduler`, or `ComplianceOfficer`, the `X-User-ID` value is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — for example as `recorded_by_user_id` on a tuning reading submitted by a `WellfieldTechnician`, or as `prepared_by_user_id` and `reviewed_by_user_id` on a compliance report handled by a `ComplianceOfficer` — but it never compares such an identifier against any entity's `id` for ownership purposes.

The remaining two roles are entity-backed and their identity is load-bearing. When `X-User-Role` is `HostRepresentative`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `LandfillSite`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `LandfillSite` compare the resource's site reference to `X-User-ID`, so a host representative may read and dispute only royalty statements whose `site_id` equals its own identifier. When `X-User-Role` is `Offtaker`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `OfftakeContract`; ownership checks compare a nomination's `contract_id` — or, when the contract itself is being read, the contract's own `id` — to `X-User-ID`. An entity-backed role that reaches for a resource it does not own is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` and HTTP 403. That ownership check guards exactly six operations: reading a single royalty statement, listing royalty statements, driving a royalty statement's status, reading a single offtake contract, reading a single pipeline nomination, and driving a pipeline nomination's status.

Two further authority rules are narrower than a whole endpoint. Compliance filings are governed by segregation of duties, enforced against `prepared_by_user_id` and `reviewed_by_user_id` and reported as `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` with HTTP 403; the details live with the filing workflow in §3.9. And on the plant status surface, only a `PlantOperator` may move the spec dimension — a `ProjectAdmin` sharing that endpoint is limited to the process-mode dimension.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.


### 2.3. Pagination, Filtering and Sort Order

Every collection endpoint accepts `limit` and `offset` as query-string parameters. `limit` defaults to 10 when omitted, may be no smaller than 1 and no larger than 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` or `offset` outside those bounds is a schema violation and is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION` and HTTP 400. Successful list responses set `response_type` to `"array"` and populate `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` in `meta`, where `total_count` counts every row matching the request's filters before the window is applied and `has_more` is true whenever rows remain beyond the returned window.

Unless a listing states otherwise, rows are returned sorted by `created_at` ascending. There is exactly one exception in this API: the tuning readings of a single well are returned newest first, ordered by `taken_at` descending.

Collections also accept domain filters, and every filter is optional. Landfill sites filter on `state_code` and on `compliance_posture`. Gas wells filter on `site_id` and `operating_state`. The readings of one well filter on `quality_class`. Work orders filter on `well_id` and `status`. Processing plants filter on `site_id` and on either state dimension, `process_mode` or `spec_status`. Downtime records filter on `plant_id`, `status` and `cause`. Offtake contracts filter on `site_id` and `status`. Pipeline nominations filter on `contract_id`, `plant_id` and `status`. Injection batches filter on `plant_id`, `nomination_id` and `status`. Attribute batches filter on `injection_batch_id`, `program`, `status` and `generation_period`. Compliance reports filter on `site_id`, `report_type`, `period` and `status`. Royalty statements filter on `site_id`, `period` and `status`. Condensate hauls of one work order take no domain filter beyond the pagination window.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Versioning and the Logical Clock

Every persisted resource carries an `id` that the server mints as a UUID. Identifiers carry no type prefix and are never supplied by the client. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recorded when the row was written.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, refreshed to the current logical instant on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly one on every mutation of the row. Two resources are append-only event records and deliberately do not carry them: a `WellTuningReading` and a `CondensateHaul` each expose only `id` and `created_at` — neither `updated_at` nor `version` is present on those two, because neither is ever modified after it is written.

Time in this system is a logical clock, not wall-clock time. Every deadline, every window test, every month boundary and every server-stamped instant reads the same `current_utc` value. Before the clock is ever set, `current_utc` reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and the server never falls back to real wall-clock time. A `ProjectAdmin` may advance or rewind the clock by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant; doing so mutates no entity, and subsequent requests simply observe the new `current_utc`.

Status-change requests share a common shape across the API. The requested target state is carried as `to_status` for a work order, an offtake contract, a pipeline nomination, an injection batch, an attribute batch, a compliance filing and a royalty statement, and as `to_state` for a gas well. The processing plant is the sole exception, because it carries two independent state dimensions and therefore names them directly as `process_mode` and `spec_status` rather than through a single target-state field.

### 2.5. Rejection Ordering

When a single request violates more than one rule, exactly one error is returned, and which one is not a matter of implementation convenience. Checks run in a fixed ascending priority order and the **first** failing check decides the response. The ladder is:

1. **Authentication (priority 10).** Both headers present and the role recognised, or `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Role permission (priority 20).** The acting role appears in the endpoint's permitted list, or `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`. Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Path existence (priority 30).** Every resource identifier in the URL path resolves, validated left to right, or the not-found error naming that resource type: `ERR_SITE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WELL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WORK_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PLANT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DOWNTIME_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_NOMINATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INJECTION_BATCH_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ATTRIBUTE_BATCH_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REPORT_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_ROYALTY_STATEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, each with HTTP 404. Applies to every endpoint that has a path parameter. Left-to-right means the outer segment is resolved before the inner one: a request naming a work order that does not exist reports the missing work order, and only once that work order is found is anything nested beneath it considered.
4. **Body schema (priority 40).** Required fields present, types correct, enum members recognised, numeric ranges respected, and string patterns matched — including a `generation_period` shaped `YYYY-MM` and a compliance report `period` shaped `YYYY-Qn`. Otherwise `ERR_VALIDATION` with HTTP 400. Applies to every write endpoint.
5. **Pagination bounds (priority 45).** `limit` between 1 and 100 inclusive and `offset` at least 0, or `ERR_VALIDATION`. Applies to every list endpoint.
6. **Body foreign keys (priority 50).** Every foreign key supplied in a request body resolves to an existing row, or the matching not-found error from the set `ERR_SITE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WELL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PLANT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_NOMINATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INJECTION_BATCH_NOT_FOUND`. This guards well creation, plant creation, offtake contract creation, nomination creation, injection batch creation, attribute batch creation, compliance report creation, royalty statement creation, work order creation, downtime event creation, and the `nomination_id` supplied when an injection batch is attached to a nomination.
7. **Gas composition coherence (priority 55).** On a tuning reading, the methane, oxygen and nitrogen percentages must sum to no more than 100, or `ERR_GAS_COMPOSITION_INVALID` with HTTP 422.
8. **Batch interval shape (priority 56).** On injection batch creation, the span between `interval_start` and `interval_end` must be a whole number of hours and the number of entries in `hourly_volumes_mscf` must equal that hour count, or `ERR_BATCH_INTERVAL_MISMATCH` with HTTP 422.
9. **Interval direction (priority 57).** An interval's end instant must be strictly after its start instant, or `ERR_INTERVAL_INVALID` with HTTP 422. This covers a work order's `window_end` against `window_start`, a nomination's `gas_day_end` against `gas_day_start`, a contract's `term_end` against `term_start`, and the `end_at` supplied when a downtime record is closed against that record's `start_at`.
10. **Resource ownership (priority 60).** For the two entity-backed roles, the acting identity owns the resource, or `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`, on the six operations enumerated in §2.1.
11. **Segregation of duties (priority 65).** On a compliance report status change, the acting officer holds the right seat, or `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
12. **Optimistic concurrency (priority 70).** `expected_contract_version` matches the contract's current `version` on nomination creation, and `expected_site_version` matches the site's current `version` on royalty statement creation, or the corresponding stale-version error described in §2.7.
13. **State legality (priority 80).** The target resource sits in a state from which the requested transition is declared, or `ERR_INVALID_TRANSITION` with HTTP 409. Several state failures have their own specific code at this same rung: `ERR_WELL_DECOMMISSIONED` when an operation targets a well whose `operating_state` is `DECOMMISSIONED` — which also blocks planning a work order against that well; `ERR_WELL_IN_LOCKOUT` when a reading is recorded, or a second work order is started, against a well in `MAINTENANCE_LOCKOUT`; `ERR_DOWNTIME_ALREADY_CLOSED` when a downtime record that is already `CLOSED` is closed again; `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE` when a nomination is written or confirmed against a contract whose `status` is not `ACTIVE`; and `ERR_BATCH_NOT_INJECTED` when attribute generation is requested against an injection batch that has not reached `INJECTED`. This rung applies to every write that drives a state transition, and additionally to recording a tuning reading, planning a work order, creating a nomination, and creating an attribute batch. Two carve-outs are deliberately **not** reported here but one rung later, by their own specific errors: an injection batch already in `OFF_SPEC` pushed toward `NOMINATED` or `INJECTED` reports `ERR_BATCH_OFF_SPEC`, and a batch whose plant is not `UPGRADING` reports `ERR_PLANT_NOT_UPGRADING`. Concretely, a `DRAFT` batch under a `STARTUP` plant returns `ERR_PLANT_NOT_UPGRADING`, never `ERR_INVALID_TRANSITION`, because the batch's own state check passes.
14. **Reading chronology (priority 85).** A tuning reading's `taken_at` is strictly after the well's `last_reading_at`, or `ERR_READING_OUT_OF_ORDER` with HTTP 422.
15. **Process-safety and permit gates (priority 90).** These guard plant status changes, well status changes, injection batch status changes, condensate haul recording, and compliance report status changes. The plant is `UPGRADING` where the operation requires it, or `ERR_PLANT_NOT_UPGRADING` (409). The plant's `spec_status` is `ON_SPEC` where the operation requires it, or `ERR_PLANT_OFF_SPEC` (409). The batch itself is not `OFF_SPEC`, or `ERR_BATCH_OFF_SPEC` (409). A well being returned to service has the required run of consecutive good readings, or `ERR_WELL_NOT_RECOVERED` (422). Leaving quarantine leaves no unresolved off-spec batch, or `ERR_OFF_SPEC_BATCH_UNRESOLVED` (409). An exceedance filing is accepted only with a corrective action note, or `ERR_PERMIT_EXCEEDANCE_UNRESOLVED` (422). Hazardous condensate carries a hazardous-waste manifest, or `ERR_CONDENSATE_MANIFEST_REQUIRED` (422). And moving a plant to `UPGRADING` requires the site's collected flow to have reached the plant's minimum feed, or `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_FEED_FLOW` (422).
16. **Cross-entity prerequisites (priority 100).** Separating an attribute batch requires an accepted compliance filing of the matching type covering the generation period, or `ERR_REPORT_NOT_ACCEPTED` (409); a settled-metered basis requires the source injection batch to carry a metered volume, or `ERR_METERED_VOLUME_REQUIRED` (422). These guard attribute batch creation and attribute batch status changes.
17. **Window and occupancy rules (priority 110).** A buffered lockout collision on a well reports `ERR_WELL_LOCKOUT_CONFLICT` (409); starting a work order outside its window reports `ERR_WORK_ORDER_WINDOW_NOT_ACTIVE` (422); a nomination window escaping the contract term reports `ERR_CONTRACT_TERM_WINDOW` (422); and a royalty period that has not fully elapsed reports `ERR_ROYALTY_PERIOD_NOT_CLOSED` (422). These guard work order creation, work order status changes, nomination creation, and royalty statement creation.
18. **Uniqueness (priority 120).** A well code duplicated among the non-decommissioned wells of a site reports `ERR_WELL_CODE_DUPLICATE`; a second non-reversed attribute batch for the same injection batch and program reports `ERR_ATTRIBUTE_DUPLICATE_PROGRAM`; a second royalty statement for the same site and period in a live status reports `ERR_ROYALTY_PERIOD_DUPLICATE`; and a second compliance filing for the same site, type and period in a status other than returned reports `ERR_REPORT_PERIOD_DUPLICATE`. All four carry HTTP 409, and they guard well creation, attribute batch creation, royalty statement creation and compliance report creation respectively.
19. **Capacity and quota (priority 130).** A nomination exceeding its contract's maximum daily quantity reports `ERR_CONTRACT_MDQ_EXCEEDED` (422); a nomination exceeding the pooled receipt-point hourly rate reports `ERR_RECEIPT_POINT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409); and attaching a batch that would push a nomination past its nominated volume reports `ERR_NOMINATION_VOLUME_EXCEEDED` (422). These guard nomination creation and injection batch status changes.

The practical consequence worth internalising is that a request can be wrong in several ways at once and the caller will only ever be told the earliest reason. A nomination that is simultaneously stale, oversized and written against a suspended contract reports the stale version, because concurrency sits at rung twelve and the contract state at rung thirteen.

### 2.6. Measurement Precision and Rounding Discipline

Landfill gas accounting is an argument about rounding, so this API pins it. Unless a rule states otherwise, every computed number is rounded **half up** — a value exactly at the midpoint rounds away from zero — and the number of decimal places is fixed per quantity rather than per caller.

Money is always carried to two decimal places: every revenue, deduction, floor and royalty figure. Energy in MMBtu is carried to two decimal places, and so are durations expressed in minutes, percentages such as availability and collection efficiency, and the composite air-intrusion score of a tuning reading. Hourly nomination rates are carried to four decimal places, and so is the eligibility factor captured on an attribute batch. Carbon-intensity figures are carried to three decimal places, LCFS credit quantities to four. RIN counts are whole numbers produced by truncating toward zero — never by rounding — and the RIN ledger counters are exact integers throughout.

Where a formula rounds at more than one step, the step order is part of the specification and not an optimisation to be collapsed. The two places where this bites hardest are the energy content of an injection batch, where each hour is rounded before the hours are summed, and the commodity revenue inside a royalty settlement, where each batch's revenue is rounded before the batches are summed. Both are spelled out where they belong, in §3.7 and §3.9 respectively. Comparisons against a threshold use the rounded value, not the raw one: a tuning reading's air-intrusion score is rounded to two decimal places first and the classification ladder then compares that rounded figure.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Contract and Site Writes

Two writes in this system compute their result from a parent aggregate that other writers are also moving, and both therefore require the client to prove it was looking at the current parent when it decided to write.

A `POST /pipeline-nominations` request must carry `expected_contract_version`, a non-optional field holding the `version` of the target `OfftakeContract` that the client last observed. If that value does not equal the contract's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CONTRACT_VERSION` and HTTP 412. Writing many nominations against one contract is entirely legitimate and the product deliberately allows it; what the check enforces is that the client's view of the contract's committed volume was still current when it sized this nomination. A successful create increments the contract's `version` by one, so the next writer must re-read before it can write.

A `POST /royalty-statements` request must likewise carry `expected_site_version`, a non-optional field holding the `version` of the target `LandfillSite` that the client last observed. A mismatch against the site's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SITE_VERSION` and HTTP 412. A restated statement legitimately supersedes a disputed one for the same period, so the write is always valid in product terms; the version check is what guarantees the statement was computed from the energy and attribute ledger the client actually saw. A successful create increments the site's `version` by one.

No other endpoint takes a version field. Every write outside those two is accepted on its own terms without an echoed parent version, and clients must not send one.

### 2.8. Conserved Quantities: the Energy and RIN Ledgers

A `LandfillSite` maintains two ledgers that the system treats as conserved quantities, meaning the identity holds at every observable instant rather than after a nightly reconciliation.

The first is energy. `energy_produced_mmbtu` is the running total of all landfill-gas energy the site has accounted for, and it must always equal the sum of `energy_injected_mmbtu`, `energy_flared_mmbtu` and `energy_vented_mmbtu`. Every forward action credits `energy_produced_mmbtu` and exactly one destination bucket — a batch reaching the pipeline credits injected, a batch sent to the flare credits flared, a closed downtime record credits flared and vented by the amounts booked on it — and every compensating action debits precisely the same pair it credited. A dedicated computed read exposes this as `balance_ok`, true when `energy_produced_mmbtu` rounded to two decimal places equals the sum of the other three rounded to two decimal places.

The second is D3 RINs. `rin_generated_total` is monotonic: it counts every D3 RIN ever generated at the site and is never reduced. The three destination counters — `rin_available_count` for attributes currently held, `rin_retired_count` for attributes retired, and `rin_reversed_count` for attributes reversed — must always sum to exactly `rin_generated_total`. Retirement and reversal move an attribute batch's `rin_count` out of the available bucket into the matching destination; neither touches the total. The same computed read exposes this as `rin_ledger_ok`, an exact integer comparison. That read returns `site_id`, the four energy figures, `balance_ok`, the four RIN counters, and `rin_ledger_ok`, and it is available to a `ProjectAdmin`, a `ComplianceOfficer`, and the site's own `HostRepresentative`.

A third running total, `royalty_payable_usd`, tracks the sum of `royalty_amount_usd` over the site's statements that are currently issued. It rises when a statement is issued and falls when that statement is disputed or paid, so a paid statement and a disputed statement both leave the payable balance at zero contribution.

### 2.9. Cascade Propagation and Derived-Field Recomputation

Several operations in this API mutate more than one entity, and the secondary effects are as much part of the contract as the primary one. Three cascades run deep enough to name here; each is specified in full where its trigger lives.

The **wellfield-shortfall cascade** starts at a tuning reading that alarms or throttles a well. The well's credited flow drops, which changes the site's collected flow, which can drop a plant below its minimum feed, which forces that plant from upgrading to flaring, which diverts every in-flight injection batch of that plant to the flare, which curtails whatever nominations those batches were attached to. That is five hops from one field reading, and every hop is mandatory.

The **report-return cascade** starts when a reviewer returns a compliance filing. Returning bumps the filing's revision count, puts the period's generated attribute batches on QA hold, and — because at least one attribute batch entered that hold — disputes the site's issued royalty statements for months inside the filing's period, moving the site's payable balance down by each disputed statement's amount.

The **void cascade** starts when an injected batch is voided. The site's energy ledger is unwound in the same pair of buckets it was credited in, the nomination and contract delivery totals are unwound, and every attribute batch generated from that injection batch is reversed.

Derived fields are recomputed eagerly, never lazily. A site's `collected_flow_scfm` and `collection_efficiency_pct` are recalculated on every change to any of its wells' flow or operating state; a plant's `availability_factor_pct`, `downtime_minutes_mtd` and `flare_minutes_mtd` are recalculated whenever a downtime record of that plant is closed; a site's `compliance_posture` is recalculated on every compliance filing status change. Any entity mutated by a cascade — not merely the entity the request named — advances its own `updated_at` and increments its own `version`.

### 2.10. Interval Geometry

This domain reasons about time windows in two deliberately different geometries, and confusing them produces wrong answers at the boundary.

Scheduling windows are **half-open**. A nomination's window runs from `gas_day_start` inclusive to `gas_day_end` exclusive; a contract term runs from `term_start` inclusive to `term_end` exclusive; a downtime record covers `start_at` inclusive to `end_at` exclusive; a royalty settlement month runs from the first instant of the month inclusive to the first instant of the following month exclusive. Two half-open windows that merely touch — one ending at the exact instant the next begins — do not intersect, and therefore do not compete for shared capacity.

Maintenance lockout windows are the opposite: **closed on both ends and inflated by a purge buffer**. A work order's window runs from `window_start` through `window_end` inclusive, and for collision testing each end is pushed outward by the `lockout_buffer_minutes` value carried on the work order, which is 120 minutes — two full hours — at each end. Two such buffered windows collide if they share even a single instant, so touching endpoints do collide here. There is no aggregate capacity on a well: occupancy is exclusive.

One further geometry rule governs downtime accounting. When the closed downtime records of a plant are merged for month-to-date reporting, intervals that overlap **or** merely touch are joined into one contiguous block and their union counted once. That is the merge rule, not the intersection rule, and it applies only after each record has been clipped to the month-to-date window.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Registering a Project: the Host Landfill and its Wellfield

Everything in this system hangs off a host landfill. A `ProjectAdmin` registers one by supplying its `name` — non-empty, at most 120 characters — and the two-letter uppercase `state_code` of the state it sits in. The state code is not decorative: Californian low-carbon-fuel-standard attributes are only generated for a site whose `state_code` is `CA`. The registration also captures the commercial and permit terms that will govern the project for its life: `host_royalty_pct`, the fraction of revenue owed to the landfill owner, which must be greater than zero and no greater than 0.50; `royalty_variant`, the site's default settlement method, either `GROSS_REVENUE_SHARE` or `NETBACK_SHARE`; `royalty_floor_usd_per_mmbtu`, a contractual minimum payment per billable MMBtu that defaults to 0.25 and may not be negative; and `permit_nmoc_limit_lb_per_hr`, the Title V permit ceiling on non-methane organic compound emissions in pounds per hour, which must be greater than zero. A `design_collection_efficiency_pct` may also be supplied, defaulting to 85.0; it appears in the permit narrative and is a reference value only, never participating in any calculation. Note that `attribute_share_pct` on an offtake contract is reference-only in exactly the same way.

A freshly registered site starts with every ledger counter at zero — `energy_produced_mmbtu`, `energy_injected_mmbtu`, `energy_flared_mmbtu`, `energy_vented_mmbtu`, `rin_generated_total`, `rin_available_count`, `rin_retired_count`, `rin_reversed_count` and `royalty_payable_usd` are all 0 — and with `compliance_posture` at `COMPLIANT`. That posture is a derived summary of the site's regulatory standing, recomputed on every filing status change. It reads `VIOLATION` when any compliance filing of the site whose `status` is `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `RETURNED` or `ACCEPTED` carries an `exceedance_flag` of true. Failing that, it reads `WATCH` when any filing of the site is in `RETURNED`. Otherwise it reads `COMPLIANT`.

Extraction wells — each a `GasWell` — are added to a site one at a time, by a `ProjectAdmin` or a `WellfieldTechnician`, with a payload carrying the owning `site_id`, an operator label `well_code` of at most 20 characters, and a `target_flow_scfm` design extraction rate that must be greater than zero. A well code must be unique among the wells of that site that have not been decommissioned; reusing a code that a live well already holds is rejected with `ERR_WELL_CODE_DUPLICATE`, while reusing the code of a decommissioned well is permitted. Every new well starts in `operating_state` `ONLINE` with `current_flow_scfm` at 0, `last_reading_flow_scfm` and `wellhead_vacuum_inhg` at 0, `consecutive_good_readings` at 0, `last_quality_class` at `NONE`, and `last_reading_at` null — it produces nothing on paper until its first field reading arrives. Adding a well nevertheless changes the site immediately, because the new well joins the denominator of the site's collection efficiency.

Two derived figures on the site describe the wellfield at a glance. `collected_flow_scfm` is the sum of `current_flow_scfm` over the site's wells whose `operating_state` is either `ONLINE` or `THROTTLED`; wells in `ISOLATED`, `MAINTENANCE_LOCKOUT` or `DECOMMISSIONED` contribute nothing. `collection_efficiency_pct` is that collected flow, multiplied by 100, divided by the sum of `target_flow_scfm` over the site's wells whose `operating_state` is anything other than `DECOMMISSIONED`, rounded half up to two decimal places; when that denominator is zero the efficiency is reported as 0.00 rather than as an error. Both are recomputed on every well flow change and every well state change.

Retiring a well is a `ProjectAdmin` action and moves it to `DECOMMISSIONED` from `ONLINE`, `THROTTLED` or `ISOLATED`. The well's `current_flow_scfm` is zeroed and the site's collected flow and efficiency are recomputed — and because a decommissioned well also leaves the target-flow denominator, retiring an underperforming well raises the site's reported efficiency. `DECOMMISSIONED` is terminal: no further transition is accepted from it, and any operation aimed at such a well, including planning maintenance on it, is refused with `ERR_WELL_DECOMMISSIONED`. Work orders already planned against a well at the moment it is decommissioned are deliberately left in place; they can never start, and they keep occupying their buffered lockout window until they are cancelled.

### 3.2. Tuning Readings, Gas Quality, and How a Well Responds

Wellfield tuning is the daily work of the project, and it is the only place in this API where the server, rather than an operator, decides a state change. A `WellfieldTechnician` — and only a `WellfieldTechnician` — records a `WellTuningReading` against a well by supplying the instant it was taken as `taken_at`, the gas composition as `methane_pct` (between 0 and 100 inclusive), `oxygen_pct` (between 0 and 25 inclusive) and `nitrogen_pct` (between 0 and 100 inclusive), the wellhead `temperature_f` in degrees Fahrenheit (between 0 and 250 inclusive), the measured `flow_scfm` (at least 0), and the applied `vacuum_inhg` in inches of mercury (at least 0). The three composition percentages together may not exceed 100, since gas that is more than entirely itself is a bad instrument rather than a bad well; a sum above 100 is rejected with `ERR_GAS_COMPOSITION_INVALID`. Readings must arrive in chronological order: `taken_at` must be strictly after the well's current `last_reading_at`, and a reading that is not is rejected with `ERR_READING_OUT_OF_ORDER`. A well that is currently locked out for maintenance accepts no readings at all and reports `ERR_WELL_IN_LOCKOUT`. Each stored reading records the submitting technician's identifier as `recorded_by_user_id`, and readings are append-only — once written, a reading is never edited.

Before any state change is contemplated, the server scores the reading. The composite `air_intrusion_index` is built by taking the oxygen percentage multiplied by four, adding the nitrogen percentage multiplied by 0.35, subtracting a methane credit equal to the methane percentage multiplied by 0.05, and adding a temperature penalty equal to 0.25 for every degree Fahrenheit by which the wellhead temperature exceeds 130 — with that excess floored at zero, so a reading at or below 130 degrees contributes no temperature penalty at all. The result is rounded half up to two decimal places, and it is that rounded figure that every subsequent comparison uses. Only the four composition and temperature values of the reading being posted feed this score; no earlier reading participates.

The reading's `quality_class` then comes from a three-rung ladder evaluated top down, where the **first** matching rung wins. The reading is `ALARM` when the oxygen percentage is at least 5.00, or the temperature is at least 145 degrees Fahrenheit, or the air-intrusion index is at least 24.00. Failing that, it is `WATCH` when the oxygen percentage is at least 2.00, or the methane percentage is below 45.00, or the air-intrusion index is at least 6.00. Otherwise it is `GOOD`. The oxygen alarm threshold is an inclusive floor — exactly 5.00 alarms, 4.99 does not — and the alarm index threshold sits at 24.00 precisely so that an oxygen reading just below 5.00 cannot trip the index rung by itself, keeping the oxygen boundary independently observable. Each reading also stores `resulting_well_state`, the well's `operating_state` immediately after the reading was applied, which is always one of `ONLINE`, `THROTTLED` or `ISOLATED`.

Every reading, whatever its class, refreshes the well's `last_quality_class`, sets `last_reading_at` to the reading's `taken_at`, sets `last_reading_flow_scfm` to the reading's `flow_scfm`, and sets `wellhead_vacuum_inhg` to the reading's `vacuum_inhg`. What differs is the credited flow and the recovery counter, and both depend on where the well already stood. An `ALARM` reading isolates the well: from `ONLINE` or from `THROTTLED` the well moves to `ISOLATED`, its `current_flow_scfm` is set to 0 and its `consecutive_good_readings` is reset to 0. A `WATCH` reading throttles it: an `ONLINE` well takes `ONLINE → THROTTLED` and a `THROTTLED` well stays `THROTTLED` on a self-loop, and in both cases the credited flow becomes the smaller of the reading's own flow and 60 percent of the well's `target_flow_scfm`, rounded half up to two decimal places, while `consecutive_good_readings` resets to 0. A `GOOD` reading never promotes a well on its own. An `ONLINE` well stays `ONLINE`, takes the reading's flow at face value as its `current_flow_scfm`, and increments `consecutive_good_readings` by one. A `THROTTLED` well stays `THROTTLED` and increments `consecutive_good_readings` by one, but its credited flow is still capped at the smaller of the reading's flow and 60 percent of target — a good reading never lifts a throttled well above its throttled cap. An `ISOLATED` well stays `ISOLATED` on any class of reading, and its `current_flow_scfm` stays 0 no matter how good the gas looks; a `GOOD` reading there increments `consecutive_good_readings` by one and a `WATCH` or `ALARM` reading resets it to 0. That isolated self-loop is exactly how a well accumulates the recovery evidence that returning it to service demands.

Because credited flow moved, the site's `collected_flow_scfm` and `collection_efficiency_pct` are recomputed on every reading. If the recomputed collected flow has fallen below the `min_feed_scfm` of any plant of that site that is currently upgrading, that plant is forced out of upgrading and into flaring, with all the consequences described in §3.4 — five hops in total, from one technician's handheld reading to a curtailed pipeline nomination.

Returning a well to service is always an explicit act by a `WellfieldTechnician`, never an automatic promotion, and it is gated on evidence. Moving `ISOLATED → THROTTLED` requires the well's `last_quality_class` to be `GOOD` and its `consecutive_good_readings` to be at least 2; the credited flow becomes the smaller of `last_reading_flow_scfm` and 60 percent of `target_flow_scfm`, rounded half up to two decimal places. Moving `THROTTLED → ONLINE` requires `last_quality_class` to be `GOOD` and `consecutive_good_readings` to be at least 3, and the credited flow becomes `last_reading_flow_scfm` in full. Attempting either without the required run of consecutive good readings is rejected with `ERR_WELL_NOT_RECOVERED`. A `reason` may accompany a manual well status change and is recorded for the field log. Both promotions recompute the site's collected flow and efficiency, and both then run the recovery check in the other direction: if the recomputed collected flow has reached or exceeded the `min_feed_scfm` of a plant of that site that is currently flaring and whose `spec_status` is `ON_SPEC`, that plant is returned to upgrading. Together with the alarm path, this closes the `ONLINE → ISOLATED → THROTTLED → ONLINE` recovery cycle that a well walks over the course of a bad week. Requesting any state pair not named here — say, jumping straight from `ISOLATED` to `ONLINE` — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TRANSITION`.

### 3.3. Collection-System Maintenance, Lockout Windows and Condensate

Collection-system maintenance takes a well out of service, so it is planned before it is executed. A `ProjectAdmin` or a `WellfieldTechnician` plans a `WorkOrder` by naming the `well_id`, the `work_type` — one of `WELL_RETUNE`, `LATERAL_REPAIR`, `SUMP_PUMP_SERVICE` or `HEADER_DEWATERING` — and the planned maintenance window as `window_start` and `window_end`, where the end must be strictly after the start or the request is refused with `ERR_INTERVAL_INVALID`. The job is created in `PLANNED` with `actual_start_at` and `actual_end_at` both null and `condensate_gallons_removed` at 0. A job may not be planned against a well that has been decommissioned; that attempt reports `ERR_WELL_DECOMMISSIONED`.

Planning also tests occupancy, and the geometry here is the strict one described in §2.10. Each job — the one being planned and every incumbent — is treated as occupying a closed interval that runs from its `window_start` less the purge buffer through its `window_end` plus the same buffer, where the buffer is the job's `lockout_buffer_minutes`, fixed by the system at 120 minutes. Incumbents are the jobs on the same well whose `status` is `PLANNED` or `IN_PROGRESS`; jobs that are `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED` release their window entirely. If the new job's buffered closed interval shares even a single instant with an incumbent's buffered closed interval, the request is refused with `ERR_WELL_LOCKOUT_CONFLICT`. Two jobs whose buffered windows merely touch at one endpoint therefore do conflict — the opposite of how nomination windows behave — because the buffer exists so the well can be purged and allowed to settle, and a purge that begins the instant the previous one ends is not a purge.

Starting a job moves it `PLANNED → IN_PROGRESS`, and the logical clock must be inside the planned window, from `window_start` through `window_end` inclusive; starting early or late is refused with `ERR_WORK_ORDER_WINDOW_NOT_ACTIVE`. The well must be in `ONLINE`, `THROTTLED` or `ISOLATED`; a decommissioned well refuses with `ERR_WELL_DECOMMISSIONED`, and a well already locked out by another job refuses with `ERR_WELL_IN_LOCKOUT`. On success, `actual_start_at` is stamped from `current_utc`, the well moves from whichever of those three states it occupied into `MAINTENANCE_LOCKOUT`, the well's `lockout_work_order_id` is set to the job's identifier, its `current_flow_scfm` drops to 0, and the site's collected flow and efficiency are recomputed — which, as always, may starve an upgrading plant and fire the shortfall cascade.

Completing a job moves it `IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED`, stamps `actual_end_at` from `current_utc`, and recomputes `condensate_gallons_removed` as the sum of `gallons` over every condensate haul recorded against the job. The well is released from lockout, and the release is deliberately cautious: it drops back to `ISOLATED`, never straight to `ONLINE`. Its `lockout_work_order_id` is cleared to null, its `consecutive_good_readings` is reset to 0, and its `current_flow_scfm` stays 0 until fresh good readings and an explicit return to service bring it back, exactly as described in §3.2. A job may also be abandoned before it starts, moving `PLANNED → CANCELLED`, which releases the buffered lockout window so a later job may occupy it. A job that is already `IN_PROGRESS` cannot be cancelled — it must be completed — and any other status pair is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TRANSITION`.

Condensate is the liquid that collects in the collection headers and sumps, and hauling it away is regulated waste handling. A `WellfieldTechnician`, and only a `WellfieldTechnician`, records a `CondensateHaul` against a job that is currently `IN_PROGRESS`; a haul against a job in any other status is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TRANSITION`. The haul carries the `work_order_id` of the job it belongs to, the `gallons` removed, which must be greater than zero, the `disposal_manifest_no` issued by the receiving facility, non-empty and at most 40 characters, and the `hauled_at` instant the load left the site. The optional `is_hazardous` flag defaults to false; when it is true the manifest number must begin with the prefix `HW-`, and a hazardous haul carrying a manifest that does not is refused with `ERR_CONDENSATE_MANIFEST_REQUIRED`. Recording a haul immediately refreshes the job's `condensate_gallons_removed`. Hauls, like readings, are append-only records.

### 3.4. The Upgrading Plant and its Two Independent State Dimensions

A `ProjectAdmin` registers an upgrading skid — a `ProcessingPlant` — against a site by supplying the owning `site_id`, an operator label `plant_code` of at most 20 characters, the `receipt_point_code` of the pipeline interconnect it injects into (at most 20 characters), the `receipt_point_mdq_dth_per_day` that interconnect will accept in dekatherms per day, which must be greater than zero, and the `min_feed_scfm` — the minimum site collected flow required to sustain upgrading — which must also be greater than zero. The pipeline injection specification may be supplied or left at its defaults: `spec_min_methane_pct` defaults to 96.0, `spec_max_oxygen_pct` to 0.2, and `spec_max_co2_pct` to 2.0. Nomination capacity is pooled per plant row, so two plants sharing a receipt point code are still metered separately.

What makes a plant unusual in this system is that it carries **two independent state dimensions on the same row**, and neither one forces the other except where a transition explicitly says so. `process_mode` describes what the plant is doing with the feed gas and starts at `STARTUP`; `spec_status` describes whether the product gas currently meets pipeline specification and starts at `ON_SPEC`. Both are advanced through the same operator action, which accepts a `process_mode` value, a `spec_status` value, or both, and requires at least one of them. Only a `PlantOperator` may move the spec dimension; a `ProjectAdmin` may move the process dimension, and only a `ProjectAdmin` may decommission the plant.

On the process dimension, a plant in `STARTUP` may go to `UPGRADING` or to `FLARING`. Going to `UPGRADING` demands that the plant's `spec_status` be `ON_SPEC` — otherwise `ERR_PLANT_OFF_SPEC` — and that the site's `collected_flow_scfm` have reached or exceeded the plant's `min_feed_scfm`, otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_FEED_FLOW`. Going to `FLARING` from `STARTUP` has no further precondition. From `UPGRADING` the plant may go to `FLARING` or to `DOWN`; from `FLARING` it may return to `UPGRADING` — again requiring `spec_status` of `ON_SPEC` and sufficient collected flow — or drop to `DOWN`. From `DOWN` it may restart into `STARTUP`, or a `ProjectAdmin` may retire it to `DECOMMISSIONED`, which is terminal: a decommissioned plant accepts no further mode or spec transition at all. Any pair not named here is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TRANSITION`.

The `UPGRADING → FLARING` transition is the pivot of the whole system and it behaves differently depending on who caused it. It fires either because a `PlantOperator` asked for it, or automatically because the site's `collected_flow_scfm` dropped below the plant's `min_feed_scfm` after a well throttled, isolated or locked out. When it fires **automatically**, three additional things happen that a manual switch does not do: the plant's `spec_status` is forced to `OFF_SPEC`, and a downtime record is opened for this plant with `cause` set to `WELLFIELD_SHORTFALL`, `disposition` set to `FLARED`, `start_at` set to `current_utc`, `status` `OPEN`, and `auto_generated` set to true. A manual switch to flaring leaves `spec_status` untouched and opens no downtime record. In **both** cases, every injection batch of this plant whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `PENDING_ANALYSIS`, `ON_SPEC` or `NOMINATED` is diverted to the flare; each such divert credits the site's `energy_produced_mmbtu` and `energy_flared_mmbtu` by that batch's `gross_mmbtu`, and any nomination such a batch was attached to is curtailed from `CONFIRMED` or `SCHEDULED` to `CURTAILED` with its `curtail_reason` recorded as `UPSTREAM_DIVERT`.

`UPGRADING → DOWN` diverts exactly the same set of in-flight batches with exactly the same ledger and nomination consequences, but opens no downtime record and does not touch `spec_status`. `FLARING → DOWN` diverts nothing and leaves any open auto-generated downtime record open. `FLARING → UPGRADING` is the recovery leg: every downtime record of this plant that is `OPEN` and `auto_generated` is closed with its `end_at` set to `current_utc` and both `flared_mmbtu` and `vented_mmbtu` booked as 0, and the plant's `availability_factor_pct`, `downtime_minutes_mtd` and `flare_minutes_mtd` are then recomputed. That same recovery leg is what a well returning to service or an operator raising collected flow can trigger from the wellfield side.

The spec dimension moves on a shorter loop. It falls from `ON_SPEC` to `OFF_SPEC` automatically, either when an injection batch of this plant fails its spec analysis or when the automatic wellfield-shortfall transition fires. While the plant sits in `OFF_SPEC` or `QUARANTINE`, no further batch of that plant may be attached to a nomination, and any attempt is refused with `ERR_PLANT_OFF_SPEC`. Recovery is deliberately two-step: a `PlantOperator` first moves `OFF_SPEC → QUARANTINE`, and only then `QUARANTINE → ON_SPEC`. Going directly from `OFF_SPEC` back to `ON_SPEC` is not a declared transition and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TRANSITION` — the quarantine step is mandatory. Leaving quarantine additionally requires that no injection batch of this plant still sits in `OFF_SPEC`; if one does, the request is refused with `ERR_OFF_SPEC_BATCH_UNRESOLVED`, forcing the operator to divert or void the offending batch before the plant is declared clean.

### 3.5. Downtime, Flare Backup and Month-to-Date Availability

Every hour the skid is not upgrading is an hour the project owes an explanation for, so downtime is booked as its own record. A `ProjectAdmin` or `PlantOperator` opens a `DowntimeEvent` against a `plant_id` with the outage `start_at`, a `cause` drawn from `PLANNED_MAINTENANCE`, `COMPRESSOR_TRIP`, `OFF_SPEC_TRIP`, `WELLFIELD_SHORTFALL` or `PIPELINE_CURTAILMENT`, and a `disposition` describing where the raw gas went, drawn from `FLARED`, `VENTED` or `BYPASS`. The record opens with `status` `OPEN`, `end_at` null, `duration_minutes` 0, `flared_mmbtu` and `vented_mmbtu` both 0, and `auto_generated` false — that last flag is true only on records the wellfield-shortfall cascade opened by itself.

Closing a record supplies the outage `end_at`, which must be strictly after `start_at` or the close is refused with `ERR_INTERVAL_INVALID`, together with the optional `flared_mmbtu` and `vented_mmbtu` energy figures, each defaulting to 0. Closing a record whose `status` is already `CLOSED` is refused with `ERR_DOWNTIME_ALREADY_CLOSED`. On success `duration_minutes` is set to the length from `start_at` to `end_at` expressed in minutes and rounded half up to two decimal places, and the site ledger moves: `energy_produced_mmbtu` rises by the sum of the flared and vented figures, `energy_flared_mmbtu` rises by the flared figure, and `energy_vented_mmbtu` rises by the vented figure. This is the one place vented energy enters the ledger at all, and it is why the energy identity of §2.8 needs a third destination bucket.

Closing a record then refreshes the plant's month-to-date availability picture. The month-to-date window runs from the first instant of the calendar month containing `current_utc`, inclusive, to `current_utc`, exclusive. Only records of this plant whose `status` is `CLOSED` are candidates; open records contribute nothing at all. Each candidate's own interval, from `start_at` inclusive to `end_at` exclusive, is intersected with that window, and the resulting clipped intervals are sorted by start instant ascending, with the `created_at` of the source record ascending as tie-break. They are then merged: two intervals join into one when they overlap **or** when they merely touch, meaning one ends at the exact instant the next begins. `downtime_minutes_mtd` is the total length of that merged union in minutes, rounded half up to two decimal places, so a plant that logged two overlapping outages is charged for the union once rather than for both. `flare_minutes_mtd` is computed the same way over the merged union restricted to candidates whose `disposition` is `FLARED`.

`availability_factor_pct` follows from that. Let the elapsed minutes be the length from the first instant of the month to `current_utc`. If that elapsed figure is zero or negative — which happens only at the exact first instant of a month — availability is reported as 100.00. Otherwise availability is 100 multiplied by the elapsed minutes less `downtime_minutes_mtd`, divided by the elapsed minutes, rounded half up to two decimal places. A plant with no closed downtime records in the month-to-date window therefore reports 100.00. This one number matters far beyond the plant page, because it is captured as the eligibility factor on every environmental attribute batch generated while it stands.

### 3.6. Offtake Contracts, Gas-Day Nominations and Receipt-Point Capacity

The commercial side of the project begins with an `OfftakeContract`. A `ProjectAdmin` or `CommercialScheduler` writes one against a `site_id` with the offtaker's legal `counterparty_name` (non-empty, at most 120 characters), the `contract_mdq_dth_per_day` maximum daily quantity the counterparty may nominate in dekatherms per gas day, the `price_usd_per_dth` commodity price paid for delivered gas, and the contract term as `term_start` inclusive and `term_end` exclusive, where the end must be strictly after the start or the write is refused with `ERR_INTERVAL_INVALID`. Both the maximum daily quantity and the price must be greater than zero. An `attribute_share_pct` may be supplied, defaulting to 0; like the site's design collection efficiency it is a reference value recorded for the commercial file and never enters any calculation. A new contract opens in `status` `PENDING` with `delivered_dth_mtd` at 0, and a `ProjectAdmin` or `CommercialScheduler` then moves it to `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED` or `TERMINATED` as the commercial relationship dictates. Every such status change increments the contract's `version` by one, which matters a great deal for the concurrency rule below.

A `PipelineNomination` is a promise to deliver a specific quantity of gas across a specific gas-day window at a specific receipt point. Writing one requires the `contract_id` it is sold under, the `plant_id` whose receipt point it will occupy, the window as `gas_day_start` inclusive and `gas_day_end` exclusive, the `nominated_dth` quantity, which must be greater than zero, and `expected_contract_version` carrying the contract `version` the client last read. The checks that follow run in the ladder order of §2.5, and the sequence is worth walking because a nomination can fail in six distinct ways. The contract and the plant must exist. The window must run forward, or `ERR_INTERVAL_INVALID`. The echoed contract version must be current, or `ERR_STALE_CONTRACT_VERSION`. The contract must be in `ACTIVE`; a nomination against a contract still `PENDING`, or one that has been `SUSPENDED` or `TERMINATED`, is refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`. The nominated window must lie entirely inside the contract term, from `term_start` inclusive to `term_end` exclusive, or the write is refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_TERM_WINDOW`. The nominated quantity must not exceed the contract's `contract_mdq_dth_per_day`, or `ERR_CONTRACT_MDQ_EXCEEDED`. And only after that daily-quantity check does the receipt point itself get tested.

Receipt-point capacity is pooled and measured as an hourly rate rather than as a daily total, because the interconnect meters flow, not volume. The nomination's own `hourly_rate_dth` is its `nominated_dth` divided by the length of its window in hours, rounded half up to four decimal places. The nominations competing for the same capacity are those of the same plant whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `CONFIRMED` or `SCHEDULED` and whose half-open window intersects the requested half-open window; nominations that are `DELIVERED`, `CURTAILED` or `CANCELLED` have released their capacity and are excluded entirely. Their `hourly_rate_dth` values are summed into a committed hourly rate. The cap is the plant's `receipt_point_mdq_dth_per_day` divided by 24. The new nomination is accepted when the committed hourly rate plus the new nomination's hourly rate, rounded half up to four decimal places, is less than or equal to the cap rounded half up to four decimal places — landing exactly on the cap is accepted. Exceeding it is refused with `ERR_RECEIPT_POINT_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Because the windows are half-open, a nomination that ends at the exact instant another begins does not intersect it and does not consume shared capacity, which is precisely the geometry the pipeline's own gas-day accounting uses. A successful write also increments the parent contract's `version` by one.

A nomination is created in `DRAFT` with `committed_dth` and `delivered_dth` at 0 and `curtail_reason` null, and it moves through a lifecycle only part of which is operator-driven. Confirming it takes `DRAFT → CONFIRMED` and requires the parent contract to be in `ACTIVE`, failing which the confirmation is refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`; a `ProjectAdmin`, a `CommercialScheduler`, or the `Offtaker` that owns the contract may confirm, and an offtaker acting on a nomination whose `contract_id` is not its own identifier is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. `CONFIRMED → SCHEDULED` is never requested directly: it happens automatically the first time an injection batch is attached to the nomination. `SCHEDULED → DELIVERED` is likewise automatic, firing when an injected batch pushes the nomination's `delivered_dth` to or past its `nominated_dth`; once delivered, no further batch may be attached. Curtailment takes a nomination from either `CONFIRMED` or `SCHEDULED` to `CURTAILED`, either because a `CommercialScheduler` asked for it with a `curtail_reason`, or because the upstream divert cascade curtailed it; the reason is recorded and the window stops occupying receipt-point capacity for later overlap checks. Cancellation takes `DRAFT → CANCELLED` and likewise releases the window. Curtailment and cancellation are `CommercialScheduler` prerogatives on the operator path. Every other status pair is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TRANSITION`.

### 3.7. Product Gas: Batches, Spec Analysis, Injection and Meter True-Up

Product gas is booked in hour-by-hour blocks, each one an `InjectionBatch`. A `ProjectAdmin` or `PlantOperator` records a batch against a `plant_id` with the production interval as `interval_start` inclusive and `interval_end` exclusive, the per-hour volumes as `hourly_volumes_mscf` — an array of between 1 and 24 entries in chronological order, each at least 0, expressed in thousand standard cubic feet — and the product gas composition as `methane_pct` (0 to 100 inclusive), `oxygen_pct` (0 to 25 inclusive) and `co2_pct` (0 to 100 inclusive). The interval must span a whole number of hours and the array must hold exactly that many entries; a mismatch in either direction is refused with `ERR_BATCH_INTERVAL_MISMATCH`.

Energy content is computed once, at creation, and frozen on the row thereafter. First the methane fraction is taken as `methane_pct` divided by 100. Then, walking the submitted hourly volumes in index order, each hour's energy is that hour's volume multiplied by the methane fraction multiplied by the heating value of 1.0122 MMBtu per thousand standard cubic feet of methane, and **that per-hour product is rounded half up to two decimal places before anything else happens**. Only then are the already-rounded hourly figures summed, and the sum is rounded half up to two decimal places again to give `gross_mmbtu`. The order is load-bearing and is not an implementation detail: summing the unrounded hourly products and rounding once at the end produces a different number. Three hours of 115.0 thousand standard cubic feet at 97.4 percent methane give 340.14 under the pinned order and 340.13 under sum-then-round, and the pinned order is the specification. A new batch opens in `status` `DRAFT` with `metered_mmbtu` and `nomination_id` null, `spec_failure_reason` `NONE`, and `void_reason` null.

Submitting a batch for analysis takes `DRAFT → PENDING_ANALYSIS` and requires the plant's `process_mode` to be `UPGRADING`; a batch under a plant that is starting up, flaring, down or decommissioned is refused with `ERR_PLANT_NOT_UPGRADING`, and that specific refusal takes precedence over the generic invalid-transition code because the batch's own state check has already passed. The analysis then runs inside the same request, and the batch never rests in `PENDING_ANALYSIS`: the server compares the batch against the plant's specification and lands it on `ON_SPEC` or `OFF_SPEC` before responding. The batch is on spec when its `methane_pct` is at least the plant's `spec_min_methane_pct`, its `oxygen_pct` is no greater than `spec_max_oxygen_pct`, and its `co2_pct` is no greater than `spec_max_co2_pct`; in that case `spec_failure_reason` is set to `NONE` and nothing else changes. If any one of the three comparisons fails, the batch lands on `OFF_SPEC`, `spec_failure_reason` is set to the **first** rule violated in the fixed order `LOW_METHANE`, then `HIGH_OXYGEN`, then `HIGH_CO2`, and — this is the asymmetry between the two branches — the plant itself is pushed from `ON_SPEC` to `OFF_SPEC` on its spec dimension if it was on spec. The on-spec branch never touches the plant; the off-spec branch always does.

Attaching a batch to a nomination takes `ON_SPEC → NOMINATED` and is a `CommercialScheduler` action carrying the `nomination_id` to attach to. The nomination must exist, must be in `CONFIRMED` or `SCHEDULED`, and must belong to the same plant that produced the batch; a nomination in any other status, or one written against a different plant, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TRANSITION`. The plant's `process_mode` must be `UPGRADING`, or `ERR_PLANT_NOT_UPGRADING`, and its `spec_status` must be `ON_SPEC`, or `ERR_PLANT_OFF_SPEC`. A batch that is itself in `OFF_SPEC` and is pushed toward `NOMINATED` reports `ERR_BATCH_OFF_SPEC` rather than a generic transition error. Finally, the nomination's existing `committed_dth` plus this batch's `gross_mmbtu` must not exceed the nomination's `nominated_dth`, or the attachment is refused with `ERR_NOMINATION_VOLUME_EXCEEDED`. On success, the batch's `nomination_id` is set, the nomination's `committed_dth` rises by the batch's `gross_mmbtu`, and a nomination sitting in `CONFIRMED` advances to `SCHEDULED`.

Injecting takes `NOMINATED → INJECTED` and is a `PlantOperator` action; the plant must still be `UPGRADING`, and a batch in `OFF_SPEC` pushed toward `INJECTED` again reports `ERR_BATCH_OFF_SPEC`. Injection is where the energy ledger finally moves: the site's `energy_produced_mmbtu` and `energy_injected_mmbtu` each rise by the batch's `gross_mmbtu`, the nomination's `delivered_dth` rises by the same figure, and the offtake contract's `delivered_dth_mtd` rises by it too. If that push takes the nomination's `delivered_dth` to or beyond its `nominated_dth`, the nomination advances from `SCHEDULED` to `DELIVERED`.

Not every batch reaches the pipeline. Diverting to the flare is available from `DRAFT`, `PENDING_ANALYSIS`, `ON_SPEC`, `OFF_SPEC` or `NOMINATED`, whether because a `PlantOperator` asked for it or because the plant's move out of upgrading cascaded onto it, and it always lands on `DIVERTED_TO_FLARE`. The site's `energy_produced_mmbtu` and `energy_flared_mmbtu` each rise by the batch's `gross_mmbtu`, and when the batch had been attached to a nomination, that nomination's `committed_dth` falls by the same figure and the nomination is curtailed from `CONFIRMED` or `SCHEDULED` to `CURTAILED`. Voiding is the compensating action and always carries a `void_reason`. Voiding a batch that had reached `INJECTED` debits `energy_produced_mmbtu` and `energy_injected_mmbtu` by its `gross_mmbtu`, debits the nomination's `delivered_dth` and `committed_dth` by the same figure, debits the contract's `delivered_dth_mtd`, and reverses every attribute batch generated from it whose `status` is `PENDING`, `GENERATED`, `QA_HOLD` or `SEPARATED`, stamping each with a `reversal_reason` of `UPSTREAM_BATCH_DIVERTED`. Voiding a batch that had been diverted debits `energy_produced_mmbtu` and `energy_flared_mmbtu` by its `gross_mmbtu` — exactly the pair the divert credited, which is why the energy identity holds either side of the operation. Voiding a batch that never entered any bucket, meaning one still in `DRAFT`, `ON_SPEC` or `OFF_SPEC`, moves no ledger at all. `INJECTED`, `DIVERTED_TO_FLARE` and `VOIDED` are terminal.

One further commercial step exists between injection and settlement. The pipeline eventually confirms what its own meter saw, and a `ProjectAdmin` or `CommercialScheduler` records that as `metered_mmbtu` against a batch that has reached `INJECTED`; a true-up against a batch in any other status is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TRANSITION`. The true-up is deliberately inert: it does **not** alter `gross_mmbtu`, does not move the site energy ledger, and does not restate any attribute batch already generated. Its only effect is to make `metered_mmbtu` non-null, and that is the sole route by which a settled-metered environmental attribute batch can ever be generated.

### 3.8. Environmental Attributes: Generation, Stewardship and Reversal

Environmental attributes are the second revenue stream of an RNG project and the one regulators scrutinise hardest, so the system generates them only from energy that demonstrably reached the pipeline. A `ProjectAdmin` or `ComplianceOfficer` generates an `AttributeBatch` against an `injection_batch_id`, naming the `program` — either `RIN_D3` for federal cellulosic RINs or `LCFS` for Californian credits — and the `generation_period`, a calendar month formatted as `YYYY-MM`, and optionally a `basis` which defaults to `PROVISIONAL_NOMINATED`. The source injection batch must have reached `INJECTED`; anything earlier is refused with `ERR_BATCH_NOT_INJECTED`. When the `basis` is `SETTLED_METERED`, the source batch's `metered_mmbtu` must already be non-null, and a request that asks for the settled basis before the pipeline meter true-up has landed is refused with `ERR_METERED_VOLUME_REQUIRED`. At most one non-reversed attribute batch may exist for any given injection batch and program pairing; a second is refused with `ERR_ATTRIBUTE_DUPLICATE_PROGRAM`. Note the ordering here — the metered-volume prerequisite is checked before the duplicate check, so a request that is both premature and duplicated reports the missing metered volume.

The row is created in `status` `PENDING` and auto-advances to `GENERATED` inside the same request, with every computed figure populated on the way through. Two figures are common to both programs. `energy_basis_mmbtu` is the MMBtu quantity the attributes are actually computed from: it is the source batch's `gross_mmbtu` when the `basis` is `PROVISIONAL_NOMINATED`, and the source batch's `metered_mmbtu` when the `basis` is `SETTLED_METERED`. Because the pipeline's meter and the plant's own calculation disagree in practice, the same injection batch yields materially different attribute quantities under the two bases, which is exactly why the basis is recorded on the row. `eligibility_factor` is the producing plant's `availability_factor_pct` divided by 100 and rounded half up to four decimal places, captured at the instant of generation and never restated afterwards — a plant that later suffers an outage does not retroactively devalue attributes already generated.

For the `RIN_D3` program, `rin_count` is the energy basis multiplied by 11.727 RINs per MMBtu and then multiplied by the eligibility factor, with the result **truncated toward zero** to a whole number — partial RINs do not exist and are never rounded up. `lcfs_credits` is 0 and `ci_project_gco2e_per_mj` is null for this program. Generating D3 RINs raises the site's `rin_generated_total` and its `rin_available_count`, each by the batch's `rin_count`.

For the `LCFS` program the calculation is a four-step carbon-intensity waterfall, and its evaluation order is pinned because the dependency looks circular. Step one derives a flare penalty from the site's **current** energy ledger: the denominator is the site's `energy_injected_mmbtu` plus its `energy_flared_mmbtu`; the flare fraction is 0.0000 when that denominator is zero and otherwise the site's `energy_flared_mmbtu` divided by that denominator, rounded half up to four decimal places; the flare penalty is that fraction multiplied by 60.000, rounded half up to three decimal places; and the pre-credit carbon intensity is the baseline landfill-gas RNG figure of 44.000 grams of carbon dioxide equivalent per megajoule plus that penalty, rounded half up to three decimal places. Step two selects the avoided-methane credit **from the pre-credit intensity, not from the final one**: a pre-credit intensity below 20.000 earns a credit of 22.500, one below 40.000 earns 15.000, and anything at or above 40.000 earns 7.500. Step three applies that credit exactly once — `ci_project_gco2e_per_mj` is the pre-credit intensity less the selected credit, rounded half up to three decimal places — with no re-selection and no fixed-point iteration. To make the pinning unambiguous: a site with a clean ledger and no flaring has a pre-credit intensity of 44.000, which lands in the highest tier and earns a credit of 7.500, giving a project intensity of 36.500. Re-selecting the tier from 36.500 would earn 15.000 and yield 29.000 — a materially larger credit — and that is **not** what this system computes. Step four converts energy and produces the credits: the energy in megajoules is the energy basis multiplied by 1055.06 and rounded half up to two decimal places, and `lcfs_credits` is the benchmark carbon intensity of 91.980 less the project intensity, multiplied by the compressed-natural-gas energy economy ratio of 0.90, multiplied by the energy in megajoules, divided by one million, and rounded half up to four decimal places. For this program `rin_count` is 0. LCFS generation moves no RIN counter on the site.

Once generated, attributes are stewarded by a `ComplianceOfficer`, who alone may drive their status. A generated batch may be put on `QA_HOLD` pending review and released from `QA_HOLD` back to `GENERATED`, closing a review loop that moves no ledger at all — attributes on QA hold are still counted in the site's `rin_available_count`. A generated batch may be `SEPARATED`, but only once the paperwork supports it: there must exist an accepted compliance filing for the site behind this attribute batch whose `period` covers the batch's `generation_period` and whose `report_type` is `QUARTERLY_RIN_QAP` when the program is `RIN_D3` or `LCFS_QUARTERLY` when the program is `LCFS`. Without such a filing the separation is refused with `ERR_REPORT_NOT_ACCEPTED`. Separation itself moves no ledger; separated attributes remain in `rin_available_count`. A separated batch may then be `RETIRED`, which for the `RIN_D3` program moves its `rin_count` out of the site's `rin_available_count` and into `rin_retired_count`.

Reversal is the compensating leg and is reachable from `PENDING`, `GENERATED`, `QA_HOLD` or `SEPARATED`, either because a `ComplianceOfficer` asked for it with a `reason` or because an upstream void or divert cascaded onto it. The supplied reason is stored as `reversal_reason`, drawn from `UPSTREAM_BATCH_DIVERTED`, `BASIS_RESTATEMENT`, `QA_FAILURE` or `REPORT_REJECTED`. For the `RIN_D3` program, reversal moves the batch's `rin_count` out of `rin_available_count` and into `rin_reversed_count`. What reversal never does is reduce `rin_generated_total` — that counter is monotonic by design, which is what keeps the identity of §2.8 true across generate, retire and reverse in any order. `RETIRED` and `REVERSED` are terminal; any other status pair is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TRANSITION`.

### 3.9. Regulatory Filings, Compliance Posture and Host Royalty Settlement

A `ComplianceOfficer` opens a regulatory filing — a `ComplianceReport` — against a `site_id`, naming the `report_type` — `NSPS_ANNUAL`, `QUARTERLY_RIN_QAP`, `LCFS_QUARTERLY` or `TITLE_V_SEMIANNUAL` — the `period` formatted `YYYY-Qn` where Q1 covers months 01 through 03, Q2 covers 04 through 06, Q3 covers 07 through 09 and Q4 covers 10 through 12, and the `nmoc_lb_per_hr_reported` non-methane organic compound emission rate for that period, which may not be negative. The filing's `exceedance_flag` is computed at creation and frozen there: it is true when the reported rate is strictly greater than the site's `permit_nmoc_limit_lb_per_hr`, and a rate that exactly equals the permit limit is not an exceedance. The creating officer's identifier is recorded as `prepared_by_user_id`. Only one filing may exist for a given site, report type and period in any status other than `RETURNED`; a second is refused with `ERR_REPORT_PERIOD_DUPLICATE`, which is what allows a returned filing to be superseded while a live one cannot be. A filing also exposes `attached_attribute_batch_count`, the number of attribute batches whose `generation_period` falls inside the filing's period, whose producing plant belongs to the filing's site, and whose `status` is anything other than `REVERSED`. New filings open in `DRAFT` with `revision_count` at 0 and `reviewed_by_user_id`, `return_reason` and `corrective_action_note` all null.

Filings move through `DRAFT → SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW` and then either to `RETURNED` and back to `DRAFT`, or to `ACCEPTED`, and every hop is governed by segregation of duties enforced against `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The officer who submits a filing must be its `prepared_by_user_id`. The officer who takes it `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW` must **not** be its `prepared_by_user_id`, and that officer's identifier is stamped as `reviewed_by_user_id`. The officer who then returns or accepts it must be that same `reviewed_by_user_id` — a third officer wandering in cannot dispose of a review they did not take. And the officer who takes a returned filing back to `DRAFT` for revision must again be the `prepared_by_user_id`. Accepting a filing whose `exceedance_flag` is true requires a `corrective_action_note`, without which the acceptance is refused with `ERR_PERMIT_EXCEEDANCE_UNRESOLVED`; the note is recorded on the filing when supplied. Acceptance is terminal, and it is what unlocks separation for the site's attribute batches of that period. Every one of these transitions recomputes the site's `compliance_posture`.

Returning a filing is the most consequential action in the compliance workflow because it reaches two entities the reviewer never named. Taking `UNDER_REVIEW → RETURNED` requires a `return_reason`, which is recorded on the filing, and increments the filing's `revision_count` by one. It then puts every attribute batch whose `status` is `GENERATED`, whose `generation_period` falls inside the filing's period, and whose producing plant belongs to the filing's site, onto `QA_HOLD`. And because at least one attribute batch has entered QA hold, every royalty statement of that site whose `status` is `ISSUED` and whose settlement month falls inside the filing's period is moved to `DISPUTED` with its `dispute_reason` recorded as `ATTRIBUTE_QA_HOLD`, and the site's `royalty_payable_usd` is reduced by each such statement's `royalty_amount_usd`. That is a three-hop cascade from a single reviewer decision, and the money movement at the third hop is the part implementers most often miss.

Royalty settlement closes the loop. A `ProjectAdmin` raises a `RoyaltyStatement` settling one calendar month for one site, supplying the `site_id`, the `period` formatted `YYYY-MM`, and `expected_site_version`; optionally `allowed_deductions_usd`, which defaults to 0 and may not be negative, and `variant_override`, which when supplied overrides the site's default settlement method for this statement only. The settlement window runs from the first instant of the named month inclusive to the first instant of the following month exclusive, and that month must have fully elapsed against `current_utc` — settling a month still in progress is refused with `ERR_ROYALTY_PERIOD_NOT_CLOSED`. Only one statement may exist for a given site and period in `DRAFT`, `ISSUED` or `PAID`; a second is refused with `ERR_ROYALTY_PERIOD_DUPLICATE`. The `variant_applied` recorded on the statement is the `variant_override` when one was supplied and the site's `royalty_variant` otherwise, and like every money field on the row it is frozen at creation.

The settlement itself is a waterfall over the month's injected gas. The batches that count are those whose `status` is `INJECTED`, whose `interval_start` falls inside the settlement window, and whose producing plant belongs to the statement's site, taken in order of `interval_start` ascending with `created_at` ascending as tie-break; batches that were diverted, voided or merely nominated are excluded outright. `billable_mmbtu` is the sum of those batches' `gross_mmbtu`, rounded half up to two decimal places. `gas_revenue_usd` is built per batch and only then summed: each batch's `gross_mmbtu` is multiplied by the `price_usd_per_dth` of the offtake contract behind the nomination the batch was attached to, **that per-batch product is rounded half up to two decimal places**, the rounded per-batch figures are summed, and the sum is rounded half up to two decimal places again. `attribute_revenue_usd` sums, over every attribute batch whose source injection batch is in that set and whose `status` is `GENERATED`, `QA_HOLD`, `SEPARATED` or `RETIRED`, that batch's `rin_count` multiplied by the RIN price of 2.85 US dollars plus its `lcfs_credits` multiplied by the LCFS credit price of 62.00 US dollars, with the total rounded half up to two decimal places; attribute batches that are `REVERSED` or still `PENDING` contribute nothing. `gross_revenue_usd` is the gas revenue plus the attribute revenue, rounded half up to two decimal places.

The host's share then depends on the variant. Under `GROSS_REVENUE_SHARE`, `calculated_amount_usd` is `gross_revenue_usd` multiplied by the site's `host_royalty_pct`, rounded half up to two decimal places, and `allowed_deductions_usd` is ignored entirely. Under `NETBACK_SHARE`, the deductions are subtracted from the gross revenue first, with the difference floored at zero so a month of heavy deductions can never produce a negative base, and that floored difference is multiplied by `host_royalty_pct` and rounded half up to two decimal places — which yields a strictly smaller calculated amount than the gross variant whenever the deductions are greater than zero. Separately, `floor_amount_usd` is `billable_mmbtu` multiplied by the site's `royalty_floor_usd_per_mmbtu`, rounded half up to two decimal places, and the final `royalty_amount_usd` is whichever of the calculated amount and the floor amount is larger. A month with no qualifying batches is perfectly legal: billable energy, every revenue figure, the floor and the royalty all settle at 0.00 and the statement is still created in `DRAFT`.

Statements then move on a short settlement loop. A `ProjectAdmin` issues one, taking `DRAFT → ISSUED` and raising the site's `royalty_payable_usd` by the statement's `royalty_amount_usd`. The site's `HostRepresentative` — and only the representative whose `X-User-ID` equals the statement's `site_id` — may dispute an issued statement with a `dispute_reason`, taking it `ISSUED → DISPUTED`, which lowers the site's `royalty_payable_usd` by the same amount and releases the site-and-period uniqueness slot so a restated statement may be written for that month. The compliance-report return cascade drives the same transition without a human disputant. A `ProjectAdmin` may restate a dispute back to `ISSUED`, but only when no other statement of that site and period sits in `DRAFT`, `ISSUED` or `PAID`; that re-issue raises `royalty_payable_usd` again and clears `dispute_reason` back to null. Finally a `ProjectAdmin` pays an issued statement, taking `ISSUED → PAID`, which lowers `royalty_payable_usd` by the statement's amount because the obligation has been discharged rather than cancelled. `PAID` is terminal, and any other status pair is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TRANSITION`.
