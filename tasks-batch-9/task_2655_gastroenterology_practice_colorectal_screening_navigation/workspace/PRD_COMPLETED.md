# Colorectal Screening Navigation API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A gastroenterology practice that runs a colorectal cancer screening program is really running four businesses at once, and today those four businesses do not talk to each other. The clinical business decides who is eligible to be screened and how often they should come back. The navigation business chases patients through a bowel-preparation regimen that determines whether the procedure will even be diagnostic. The suite business allocates a scarce, expensive resource — an endoscopy room with an anesthesia clinician attached to it — into slots that must not collide. And the revenue business prices the same colonoscopy twice: once optimistically at booking under a screening-preventive interpretation, and once again after the pathologist has spoken, under a diagnostic interpretation that can cost the patient hundreds of dollars more. When these four views drift apart, the practice books ineligible patients into MAC rooms with no anesthesia, sends surveillance recall letters computed from pathology that has since been amended, and bills patients for preventive care.

This document specifies the Colorectal Screening Navigation API, a single backend that holds all four views in one consistent state. It is deliberately opinionated about two things. First, **the endoscopy suite is contended along two geometrically different axes** — a room block enforces buffered single occupancy in which turnover minutes are part of the occupied interval, while an anesthesia clinician enforces an aggregate ceiling on how many of their coverage intervals may contain any single instant. Second, **the same underlying facts legitimately produce two different numbers**, and both must be retained: a claim carries a provisional price and a final price, and an endoscopist's adenoma detection rate differs depending on whether completed exams are attributed by the date of the procedure or by the date pathology was finalized. Nothing in this system is allowed to quietly replace one of those numbers with the other.

The intended readers are the engineers who will implement the service. Where this document states a threshold, an ordering, or an inclusivity boundary, that statement is normative: two rules that are individually plausible but composed in the wrong order produce a different surveillance interval or a different patient bill, and that is exactly the class of defect this specification exists to prevent.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Users and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role they are acting under. If either header is absent — a header that is present but empty counts as absent — or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the eight role names below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If the headers are well formed but the named role is not authorized for the endpoint being called, the request is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403). The eight roles are `PracticeAdmin`, `ReferralCoordinator`, `PatientNavigator`, `Endoscopist`, `Pathologist`, `BillingSpecialist`, `QualityOfficer`, and `Patient`. Section 4 lists the authorized roles for each endpoint.

Six of the eight roles are operational rather than personal. When `X-User-Role` is `PracticeAdmin`, `ReferralCoordinator`, `PatientNavigator`, `Pathologist`, `BillingSpecialist`, or `QualityOfficer`, the accompanying `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it — for example, a `QualityOfficer` who attests a measurement window has their identifier stored in `attesting_user_id` on that window — but it never compares such an identifier to any entity's `id`, and no ownership check applies to those six roles.

The remaining two roles are entity-backed. When `X-User-Role` is `Endoscopist`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Clinician`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Clinician` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`, which in practice means a booking's `performing_endoscopist_id`. When `X-User-Role` is `Patient`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Patient`; ownership checks compare `Patient.id` itself, or a `ScreeningEpisode`'s `patient_id`, to `X-User-ID`. A violation of either ownership rule is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). Concretely, ownership is enforced on six operations: reading a single patient record, reading a single screening episode, submitting a bowel-preparation attestation, and starting, completing, or aborting a procedure. A `Patient` may read only their own patient row and only episodes whose `patient_id` equals their `X-User-ID`; an `Endoscopist` may start, complete, or abort only bookings whose `performing_endoscopist_id` equals their `X-User-ID`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`, followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the upper-case letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size; it defaults to 10, may be no smaller than 1, and may be no larger than 100. `offset` controls how many rows are skipped before the page begins; it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100 inclusive, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). Each parameter must be written as a plain decimal integer literal, optionally wrapped in surrounding whitespace: a value carrying a sign, a leading zero, a decimal point, or any non-digit text is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, while a parameter supplied as an empty string is treated as though it had not been supplied at all and takes its default.

Collections are returned in ascending `created_at` order — the oldest row first — unless a subsection of this document states otherwise for a specific resource; because the logical clock only moves when a client moves it, rows frequently share a `created_at`, and such ties are broken by insertion order, the earlier-created row first. The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit` and `offset` as they were resolved for the request, `total_count` as the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied, and `has_more` as a boolean that is true when rows remain beyond the current page. Filter query parameters, where an endpoint offers them, combine conjunctively: a row must match every supplied filter to appear in the result. A filter supplied as an empty string is treated as absent; a filter whose value is drawn from an enumeration is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` when the supplied value is not a member of that enumeration; a filter that names an identifier is compared literally against the stored field and is never resolved as a foreign key, so an identifier that matches nothing simply yields an empty page rather than a not-found error; and any query parameter an endpoint does not declare is ignored.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties

Every resource this API persists carries a server-minted `id` that is a UUID string with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp recorded as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits. Every one of the thirteen resource types in this system — payer plans, patients, clinicians, room blocks, anesthesia coverage rows, referrals, screening episodes, non-invasive tests, procedure bookings, pathology reports, surveillance recommendations, billing claims, and quality measurement windows — additionally carries an `updated_at` timestamp in the same format and an integer `version`. There are no resources in this system that omit `updated_at` or `version`. That three-fractional-digit rendering is how the server writes the instants it mints or derives itself — `created_at`, `updated_at`, the envelope's `api_request_timestamp`, a pathology report's `finalized_at`, a non-invasive test's `resulted_at`, an outreach entry's `occurred_at`, and a booking's derived `slot_end_utc` and `occupancy_end_utc`. An ISO 8601 UTC instant that a client supplies in a request body — a block's `window_start_utc` and `window_end_utc`, a coverage row's `coverage_start_utc` and `coverage_end_utc`, and a booking's `slot_start_utc` — is persisted and echoed back exactly as the client wrote it rather than re-rendered into that form; it is of course still interpreted as the instant it denotes wherever a rule in this document compares or offsets it. When the logical clock has been set to an instant carrying finer than millisecond precision, a server-minted rendering keeps the first three fractional digits and discards the rest rather than rounding them.

The `version` field starts at 1 when the resource is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that resource, whether the mutation was requested directly or applied as a side effect of an operation on a different resource. `updated_at` is refreshed to the current instant on the same occasions. Clients must treat `version` as the observation token for optimistic concurrency, described in Section 2.7.

Timestamps are never taken from wall-clock time. The server maintains a **logical clock** that reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` before any request moves it, and that clock is the sole source of "now" for every `created_at` and `updated_at` stamp, every due-date computation, every recency decay in the preparation-adherence score, the instant stamped into a pathology report's `finalized_at`, the instant recorded on an outreach touchpoint, the instant recorded in a non-invasive test's `resulted_at`, and the date compared against a measurement window's end. A `PracticeAdmin` may move the logical clock forward or backward by submitting a `to_iso8601` instant to the clock-setting capability; that capability returns no resource payload.

### 2.5. Validation Precedence

When a single request would fail more than one rule, the rejection that reaches the client is the one from the highest-precedence rule. The precedence order below is normative and global; it is not a suggestion about how to structure code, and a test of this API will observe it. Implementations must evaluate, in this order:

1. Header authentication — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. Role authorization against the endpoint's role list — `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`.
3. Request-shape validation — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. This covers missing required fields, unknown enum members, integers outside their declared bounds, calendar dates that are not in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, instants that are not ISO 8601 UTC, a `limit` outside 1 through 100, and a negative `offset`. Four conventions make this step precise. A field supplied as JSON `null` is treated as though it were absent, so a required field written as `null` is a missing field while an optional field written as `null` silently takes its documented default. Types are checked strictly and nothing is coerced: a field declared as a string rejects a number, a field declared as an integer rejects both a boolean and a number carrying a fractional part, a field declared as a boolean accepts only `true` or `false`, and a field declared as a fraction accepts an integer or a decimal number but not a boolean. A string field supplied as the empty string is rejected as too short, except where a subsection of this document gives an empty value its own meaning. And a request body that is present but is not parseable JSON, or that parses to something other than a JSON object, is rejected here as well; an absent or empty body is read as the empty object, so its required fields report as missing. Within this step, a calendar date must be exactly ten characters in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, and an instant must contain the `T` separator and either end in `Z`, carry an explicitly zero UTC offset, or omit the offset entirely — in which case it is read as UTC — while any non-zero offset is rejected.
4. Path-parameter resolution, evaluated left to right along the URL — the not-found error naming the segment that failed to resolve.
5. Foreign-key resolution for identifiers supplied in the request body, evaluated in the order the fields appear in the endpoint's input — the not-found error naming the unresolvable reference.
6. Resource ownership for entity-backed roles — `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`.
7. Referral state admissibility — `ERR_INVALID_REFERRAL_STATE`.
8. Screening-episode state admissibility — `ERR_INVALID_EPISODE_STATE`.
9. Room-block state admissibility — `ERR_INVALID_SESSION_STATE`.
10. Room-block booking posture — `ERR_SESSION_NOT_ACCEPTING_BOOKINGS`.
11. Booking state admissibility — `ERR_INVALID_BOOKING_STATE`.
12. Pathology-report state admissibility — `ERR_INVALID_PATHOLOGY_STATE`.
13. Claim state admissibility — `ERR_INVALID_CLAIM_STATE`.
14. Measurement-window state admissibility — `ERR_INVALID_QUALITY_PERIOD_STATE`.
15. Non-invasive test result admissibility — `ERR_TEST_ALREADY_RESULTED`.
16. Stale room-block version — `ERR_STALE_SESSION_VERSION`.
17. Stale episode version — `ERR_STALE_EPISODE_VERSION`.
18. Open-access risk screen — `ERR_OPEN_ACCESS_RISK_SCREEN_FAILED`.
19. Performing clinician credential — `ERR_CLINICIAN_CREDENTIAL_SUSPENDED`.
20. Pre-procedure risk clearance — `ERR_PREPROCEDURE_RISK_UNCLEARED`.
21. Preparation confirmation at check-in — `ERR_PREP_NOT_CONFIRMED`.
22. Anesthesia coverage requirement at procedure start — `ERR_ANESTHESIA_COVERAGE_REQUIRED`.
23. Clinician type suitability — `ERR_CLINICIAN_TYPE_MISMATCH`.
24. Referral acceptance status — `ERR_REFERRAL_NOT_ACCEPTED`.
25. Episode eligibility — `ERR_EPISODE_NOT_ELIGIBLE`.
26. Anchoring pathology report finalization — `ERR_PATHOLOGY_NOT_FINALIZED`.
27. Slot containment inside the block window — `ERR_SLOT_OUTSIDE_SESSION_WINDOW`.
28. Room occupancy disjointness — `ERR_SLOT_OVERLAP`.
29. Anesthesia supervision ceiling — `ERR_ANESTHESIA_CONCURRENCY_EXCEEDED`.
30. Coverage window sufficiency — `ERR_COVERAGE_WINDOW_INSUFFICIENT`.
31. Measurement window has ended — `ERR_QUALITY_PERIOD_NOT_ENDED`.
32. Measurement window non-overlap — `ERR_QUALITY_PERIOD_OVERLAP`.
33. Unique business identifiers — `ERR_DUPLICATE_IDENTIFIER`.
34. Duplicate open episode for the same indication — `ERR_DUPLICATE_OPEN_EPISODE`.
35. Specimen presence at finalize — `ERR_NO_SPECIMENS`.
36. Override interval range and reason — `ERR_OVERRIDE_OUT_OF_RANGE`.
37. Segregation of duties on an attested override — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
38. Room-block headcount ceiling — `ERR_SESSION_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.

Two consequences of this ordering deserve emphasis because they are counter-intuitive. A booking request that is simultaneously outside its block's window and overlapping another booking reports `ERR_SLOT_OUTSIDE_SESSION_WINDOW`, because containment is checked before disjointness. And a booking request against a block whose headcount is already full, but which also carries a stale block version, reports `ERR_STALE_SESSION_VERSION`, because the concurrency check precedes every business rule on that endpoint.

### 2.6. Nested Path Resolution

Several resources are addressed through a parent segment — coverage rows and bookings hang off a room block, non-invasive tests and surveillance recommendations hang off a screening episode, and pathology reports are received against a booking. Path parameters are always resolved left to right: the parent identifier is validated first, and if it does not resolve, the parent's not-found error is returned without any attempt to resolve the child. The path parameters this API defines are `plan_id`, `patient_id`, `clinician_id`, `session_id`, `coverage_id`, `referral_id`, `episode_id`, `test_id`, `booking_id`, `report_id`, `recommendation_id`, `claim_id`, and `period_id`, and each maps to exactly one not-found error: `ERR_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PATIENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLINICIAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SESSION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COVERAGE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REFERRAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EPISODE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PATHOLOGY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RECOMMENDATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, and `ERR_QUALITY_PERIOD_NOT_FOUND`.

The same catalogue serves body-supplied references. A `preceding_episode_id` that does not resolve reports `ERR_EPISODE_NOT_FOUND`; an `insurance_plan_id` that does not resolve reports `ERR_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`; a `lead_endoscopist_id`, `anesthesia_clinician_id`, `performing_endoscopist_id`, or `endoscopist_id` that does not resolve reports `ERR_CLINICIAN_NOT_FOUND`; a `pathology_report_id` that does not resolve reports `ERR_PATHOLOGY_NOT_FOUND`. Body-supplied references are resolved in the order the endpoint's input declares them, so the first unresolvable reference is the one reported. Body-level foreign keys are checked on eight write operations: patient registration, room-block creation, anesthesia-coverage assignment, referral intake, episode creation, booking creation, surveillance-recommendation creation, and measurement-window creation.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Contended Parents

Two write operations in this system mutate a parent resource whose state the client must have observed first, and both require the client to pin that observation explicitly.

A `POST /endoscopy-sessions/{session_id}/procedure-bookings` request must carry `expected_session_version` as a required, non-optional field, holding the client's last observed `version` of the addressed `EndoscopySession`. A booking consumes room minutes and slot geometry that another writer may have just consumed, so if the block's current `version` differs from the supplied `expected_session_version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SESSION_VERSION` (HTTP 412) — the request priced its slot against a capacity picture that no longer holds.

A `POST /screening-episodes/{episode_id}/surveillance-recommendations` request must likewise carry `expected_episode_version` as a required, non-optional field, holding the client's last observed `version` of the addressed `ScreeningEpisode`. An attested override recommendation legitimately supersedes the guideline recommendation that the pathology-finalization cascade produced, so this operation cannot be rejected on episode state alone; it is rejected only when the writer's view of the episode is stale, and that rejection carries `ERR_STALE_EPISODE_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

No other endpoint in this API accepts a version field. Endpoints not named in this subsection neither require nor honour `expected_session_version` or `expected_episode_version`.

### 2.8. Numeric Precision and Rounding

All monetary amounts in this system are integer cents. There are no fractional-cent values in any stored field or any response payload. Where a fraction multiplies a cent amount — a coverage percentage or a coinsurance percentage — the product is rounded to zero decimal places using round-half-up, and **the rounding is applied once per line item and the rounded results are then summed**. Rounding a summed total instead of summing rounded lines produces a different answer at percentages such as 0.175 and is incorrect.

Two non-monetary quantities also carry fixed precision. The bowel-preparation adherence score is a number with two decimal places, rounded half-up, and the two-decimal rounding is applied once per outreach touchpoint and once more to the final clamped total — never once to a raw sum. An adenoma detection rate is a number with four decimal places, rounded half-up, and it is `null` rather than zero when its denominator is zero. Surveillance intervals and every minute quantity are whole integers; calendar outputs are `YYYY-MM-DD` strings.

### 2.9. Two-Dimensional Episode State and Cascade Integrity

A `ScreeningEpisode` is unusual: it carries two independent status dimensions and its observable state is the pair of them. `clinical_status` tracks the clinical pathway and takes the values `OPEN`, `PROCEDURE_SCHEDULED`, `PATHOLOGY_PENDING`, `CLOSED_SURVEILLANCE_SET`, `CLOSED_DECLINED`, and `CLOSED_SUPERSEDED`, beginning at `OPEN`, with `CLOSED_DECLINED` and `CLOSED_SUPERSEDED` terminal. `navigation_status` tracks bowel-preparation navigation and takes the values `NOT_STARTED`, `OUTREACH_ACTIVE`, `PREP_AT_RISK`, `PREP_CONFIRMED`, and `NAVIGATION_COMPLETE`, beginning at `NOT_STARTED`, with `NAVIGATION_COMPLETE` terminal. The navigation dimension advances purely on preparation-adherence thresholds and never reads `clinical_status`; the clinical dimension advances on scheduling and pathology events. Neither dimension is derivable from the other.

Several operations here mutate more than one resource, and every such cascade must complete atomically before the response is emitted. When an operation changes a child resource in a way that changes a parent's derived fields, the parent's derived fields are **fully recomputed from the current set of children, never incrementally adjusted**, and the parent's `version` is incremented and its `updated_at` refreshed. The cascades that cross resource boundaries are: booking creation (which advances the episode's clinical dimension, recomputes the block's minute ledger, prices a claim, and increments the block's `version`); booking cancellation (which reverts the episode, returns minutes, voids the claim, and increments the block's `version`); booking abort (which reverts the episode, demotes navigation, and reprices the claim without returning minutes); booking completion (which either advances the episode to `PATHOLOGY_PENDING` or runs a full closure cascade); pathology finalization (a four-hop cascade detailed in Section 3.8 that increments the episode's `version`); block closure (which releases every active coverage row); claim finalization and dispute (which move the patient's two running ledgers); and the positive non-invasive result cascade (which creates an entire follow-up episode).

### 2.10. Interval Geometry in the Suite

Both contended axes in the endoscopy suite use **half-open intervals** — the start instant is included, the end instant is excluded — but they mean different things by "occupied".

A room block's occupancy is *buffered*: a booking occupies its room from `slot_start_utc` until `occupancy_end_utc`, and `occupancy_end_utc` is the slot's end plus the block's `turnover_minutes`. Because the interval is half-open, a booking that starts at exactly another booking's `occupancy_end_utc` is accepted; one that starts a single minute earlier is not. Turnover minutes are part of the occupied interval and part of the minute ledger, which is why a block with a fifteen-minute turnover cannot host four back-to-back sixty-minute exams in a four-hour window.

An anesthesia clinician's load is *aggregate*: coverage runs from `coverage_start_utc` to `coverage_end_utc`, no buffer is appended, and the constraint is a ceiling on how many of that clinician's simultaneously active coverage intervals may contain any single instant. A coverage interval beginning exactly when another ends therefore adds no load. The two axes differ only in that the room axis appends a turnover buffer before testing adjacency; both treat the end instant as excluded.

### 2.11. Segregation of Duties

The practice separates the person who performs a procedure from the person who overrides the guideline surveillance interval derived from it. A surveillance recommendation whose interval is the computed guideline interval carries no such restriction and may be recorded by anyone authorized for the operation. A recommendation that carries an attested override, however, must be attested by someone other than the clinician who performed the anchoring procedure: when `X-User-ID` equals that booking's `performing_endoscopist_id`, the override is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Quality attestation is separated in the same spirit — the `QualityOfficer` who attests a measurement window is recorded in `attesting_user_id`, distinct from the `Endoscopist` being measured.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up the Practice: Plans, Clinicians and Patients

Before any screening work can happen, a `PracticeAdmin` populates the practice's reference data. A payer plan is registered with a `plan_code` of between 1 and 32 characters that must be unique across all payer plans — uniqueness of this and of every other business identifier in this system is an exact, case-sensitive comparison of the whole stored string against every existing row — and a `payer_name` for display. Everything else about a plan is a cost-sharing term with a default, so a minimal registration is legal: `preventive_coverage_pct` is the fraction of an allowed line amount the plan pays when a claim is classified as screening-preventive and defaults to 1.00 with a permitted range of 0.00 through 1.00; `diagnostic_coinsurance_pct` is the patient's coinsurance fraction applied to the post-deductible remainder of each line under a diagnostic classification and defaults to 0.20 across the same range; `deductible_cents` is the annual deductible, defaulting to 0 and never negative, and it is consumed only by diagnostic claims; and `out_of_pocket_max_cents` is the annual out-of-pocket ceiling that truncates what a patient can be asked to pay, defaulting to 400000 cents — four thousand dollars — and never negative.

Three further plan terms are switches that decide, later, whether a colonoscopy is preventive care or diagnostic care. `polypectomy_carveout` governs whether removing a polyp during a screening exam converts the claim, and takes one of `PREVENTIVE_RETAINED` or `DIAGNOSTIC_CONVERTED`, defaulting to `DIAGNOSTIC_CONVERTED`. `modifier_33_honored` is a boolean defaulting to true; when true, a colonoscopy that follows a positive non-invasive screening test retains the screening-preventive classification at provisional pricing. `anesthesia_bundled` is a boolean defaulting to false; when it is false, the anesthesia line is charged the diagnostic coinsurance fraction even under a screening-preventive classification, which is the single most common source of a surprise bill in a preventive colonoscopy. A registration whose `plan_code` duplicates an existing plan is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_IDENTIFIER`.

Clinicians come in exactly two flavours, and the distinction is load-bearing throughout the suite. A clinician is registered with an `npi` of 1 to 20 characters, unique across all clinicians, a `full_name`, and a `clinician_type` of either `ENDOSCOPIST` or `ANESTHESIA`. Only an `ENDOSCOPIST` row may lead a room block, perform a booking, or be the subject of a quality measurement window; only an `ANESTHESIA` row may hold anesthesia coverage. Using one where the other is required is rejected with `ERR_CLINICIAN_TYPE_MISMATCH`, and that check applies to four write operations: room-block creation validating `lead_endoscopist_id`, coverage assignment validating `anesthesia_clinician_id`, booking creation validating `performing_endoscopist_id`, and measurement-window creation validating `endoscopist_id`. Three optional attributes complete the record: `max_overlapping_rooms`, the supervision ceiling, is an integer from 1 to 4 defaulting to 1 and expresses the largest number of that clinician's active coverage intervals that may contain any single instant; `adr_benchmark_pct` is the adenoma detection rate benchmark this endoscopist is measured against, a number from 0.00 to 1.00 defaulting to 0.25 — one adenoma-positive exam in every four; and `credential_status` is either `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, defaulting to `ACTIVE`, where `SUSPENDED` blocks the clinician from starting a procedure. A duplicate `npi` is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_IDENTIFIER`.

Patient registration is where risk stratification happens, and it happens exactly once. The submitted record carries an `mrn` of 1 to 32 characters that must be unique across all patients — a duplicate is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_IDENTIFIER` — a `birth_date` in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, and an `insurance_plan_id` naming the plan whose cost-sharing terms will price this patient's claims. Five optional clinical flags follow: `family_history_crc` records a first-degree relative with colorectal cancer, `ibd_diagnosis` records inflammatory bowel disease involving the colon, `prior_crc_history` records a personal history of colorectal cancer, and `anticoagulant_therapy` records that the patient is on anticoagulants — each a boolean defaulting to false — while `asa_class` is the ASA physical status class used later by pre-procedure risk screening, an integer from 1 to 5 defaulting to 2.

From those flags the server derives `risk_tier`, which is one of `AVERAGE`, `INCREASED`, or `HIGH`, using a strictly ordered three-branch rule that reads only the patient row being created and no other data. If either `ibd_diagnosis` or `prior_crc_history` is true, the tier is `HIGH`. Otherwise, if `family_history_crc` is true, the tier is `INCREASED`. Otherwise the tier is `AVERAGE`. Because the first branch dominates, a patient with inflammatory bowel disease *and* a family history is `HIGH`, not `INCREASED`.

Registration also seeds four bookkeeping fields that no client may set directly. `deductible_applied_cents` and `oop_applied_cents` are the patient's two running ledgers — the deductible consumed and the patient responsibility committed by claims that have reached a finalized state — and both start at 0. `open_episode_count` starts at 0 and is the count of that patient's screening episodes whose `clinical_status` is `OPEN`, `PROCEDURE_SCHEDULED`, or `PATHOLOGY_PENDING`. `navigation_priority` starts at `ROUTINE` and becomes `HIGH` whenever `open_episode_count` reaches two or more; it returns to `ROUTINE` whenever the count falls below two. Both are recomputed from scratch whenever an episode is opened or closed, and whenever the positive non-invasive cascade creates an episode; the same recompute also runs at the end of a booking completion, abort or cancellation and at the end of a pathology finalization, and it bumps the patient's `version` and refreshes `updated_at` only when it actually changes `open_episode_count` or `navigation_priority` — a recompute that lands on the two values already stored leaves the patient row, its `version` and its `updated_at` untouched. The patient roster may be listed with optional `insurance_plan_id`, `risk_tier`, and `navigation_priority` filters alongside `limit` and `offset`.

### 3.2. Open-Access Referral Triage

Outside providers send patients to the practice, and the front office decides whether each one may go straight into an open-access colonoscopy slot or must first be seen in clinic. A `ReferralCoordinator` records an inbound referral against an existing `patient_id`, capturing the `referring_provider_npi` of the outside provider, a `reason` drawn from `AVERAGE_RISK_SCREENING`, `FAMILY_HISTORY`, `POSITIVE_FIT`, `SURVEILLANCE`, or `SYMPTOMATIC_BLEEDING`, an `asa_class_reported` integer from 1 to 5 as stated on the referral, and an optional `anticoagulant_reported` boolean defaulting to false. A new referral begins with `status` `SUBMITTED`, `info_request_rounds` at 0, `risk_screen_result` at `PENDING`, and `triage_decision_note` null. Referrals may be listed with optional `patient_id`, `status`, and `reason` filters.

Triage is a single action-driven operation that carries an `action` naming one of `BEGIN_TRIAGE`, `REQUEST_INFO`, `RESPOND_INFO`, `ACCEPT_OPEN_ACCESS`, `ACCEPT_OFFICE_VISIT`, or `DECLINE`, plus an optional `note`, and — for information responses — optional `updated_asa_class` and `updated_anticoagulant` revisions. The referral's `status` moves through `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_TRIAGE`, and `INFO_REQUESTED` before settling into one of the three terminal states `ACCEPTED_OPEN_ACCESS`, `ACCEPTED_OFFICE_VISIT`, or `DECLINED`. Requesting an action that is not declared from the referral's current state is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REFERRAL_STATE`. All four optional inputs are shape-validated on every action, but each is honoured by only some of them: a supplied `note` is copied into `triage_decision_note` by `REQUEST_INFO` below the round ceiling and by the three terminating actions, while `BEGIN_TRIAGE` and `RESPOND_INFO` ignore it; `updated_asa_class` and `updated_anticoagulant` are honoured only by `RESPOND_INFO` and ignored by every other action. A `triage_decision_note` already recorded is never cleared by a later action that carries no `note`. Every accepted triage action increments the referral's `version` by 1 and refreshes its `updated_at`.

`BEGIN_TRIAGE` moves `SUBMITTED → UNDER_TRIAGE` and recomputes the pre-procedure risk screen. That screen writes `risk_screen_result` as one of `PENDING`, `CLEARED_OPEN_ACCESS`, or `OFFICE_VISIT_REQUIRED`, and it reads only three values on the referral itself, as most recently revised: the result is `CLEARED_OPEN_ACCESS` when `asa_class_reported` is at most 3 **and** `anticoagulant_reported` is false **and** `reason` is anything other than `SYMPTOMATIC_BLEEDING`; in every other case the result is `OFFICE_VISIT_REQUIRED`. An ASA class of 4 or 5, a patient on anticoagulants, or a bleeding complaint each independently forces a clinic visit.

`REQUEST_INFO` is the interesting one, because the same action has two possible destinations and the branch is selected by a counter rather than by a different action name. Applied from `UNDER_TRIAGE` while `info_request_rounds` is strictly below two, it moves `UNDER_TRIAGE → INFO_REQUESTED`, increases `info_request_rounds` by 1, and copies the supplied `note` into `triage_decision_note` when one is present. Applied from `UNDER_TRIAGE` when `info_request_rounds` has already reached two, it moves `UNDER_TRIAGE → DECLINED` instead, sets `triage_decision_note` to the literal string `AUTO_DECLINED_INFO_EXHAUSTED` while ignoring any supplied `note`, and leaves `info_request_rounds` unchanged at two. The practice will chase an outside provider for missing information at most twice before closing the loop administratively.

`RESPOND_INFO` returns the referral from `INFO_REQUESTED → UNDER_TRIAGE`. When `updated_asa_class` is supplied it overwrites `asa_class_reported`; when `updated_anticoagulant` is supplied it overwrites `anticoagulant_reported`; then the risk screen is recomputed from the revised values. This is the mechanism by which a referral that initially failed the screen can pass it — an outside office confirms the patient is off anticoagulants, and the same referral clears on the next pass.

Three actions terminate triage. `ACCEPT_OPEN_ACCESS` moves `UNDER_TRIAGE → ACCEPTED_OPEN_ACCESS`, but only if the recomputed `risk_screen_result` is `CLEARED_OPEN_ACCESS`; if it is `OFFICE_VISIT_REQUIRED`, the acceptance is rejected with `ERR_OPEN_ACCESS_RISK_SCREEN_FAILED` (HTTP 422). `ACCEPT_OFFICE_VISIT` moves `UNDER_TRIAGE → ACCEPTED_OFFICE_VISIT` and is always permitted from `UNDER_TRIAGE` — it is the deliberate fallback when the open-access screen fails. `DECLINE` moves `UNDER_TRIAGE → DECLINED`. All three copy the supplied `note` into `triage_decision_note` when one is present.

### 3.3. Opening a Screening Episode

A screening episode is the unit of work that carries one patient through one screening cycle. It is opened by a `ReferralCoordinator` or a `PatientNavigator` against a `patient_id` with an `indication` drawn from `SCREENING_AVERAGE_RISK`, `SCREENING_HIGH_RISK`, `SURVEILLANCE`, or `DIAGNOSTIC_FOLLOWUP`. Two optional links may be supplied: a `referral_id` pointing at the triaged referral that produced this episode, and a `preceding_episode_id` pointing at the episode this one supersedes or follows. When a `referral_id` is supplied, that referral's `status` must be either `ACCEPTED_OPEN_ACCESS` or `ACCEPTED_OFFICE_VISIT`; anything else is rejected with `ERR_REFERRAL_NOT_ACCEPTED`. Neither link has to belong to the patient named in the request: only the referral's `status` is examined and a `preceding_episode_id` need only resolve to some episode, so a link that points at another patient's row is accepted. A patient may not hold two live episodes with the same `indication` at once: if another episode for the same patient with the same `indication` has a `clinical_status` of `OPEN`, `PROCEDURE_SCHEDULED`, or `PATHOLOGY_PENDING`, the creation is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_OPEN_EPISODE`.

From those inputs the server derives `origin`, which classifies how the episode came to exist and is one of `DIRECT`, `REFERRAL`, `NONINVASIVE_POSITIVE`, or `SURVEILLANCE_RECALL`. It is `REFERRAL` whenever `referral_id` is set. It is `NONINVASIVE_POSITIVE` when the episode was created by the positive non-invasive follow-up cascade described in Section 3.4. It is `SURVEILLANCE_RECALL` when `preceding_episode_id` is set and `referral_id` is not. It is `DIRECT` when both links are absent. The episode's `index_date` is the calendar date of the logical clock at creation, in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, and it anchors both the age calculation and the due date.

Eligibility is evaluated once, at creation, written to `eligibility_status`, and never revised afterwards even if the patient record changes. The evaluation first computes whole-year age at `index_date`: take the index year minus the birth year, then subtract one more year if the index month and day, compared as a pair, fall before the birth month and day. It then walks these branches in order, the first match winning. If the `indication` is either `SCREENING_AVERAGE_RISK` or `SCREENING_HIGH_RISK` and the patient's `prior_crc_history` is true, the status is `INELIGIBLE_PRIOR_CRC` — a patient with a personal cancer history is not a screening candidate, they are a surveillance candidate. Otherwise, for `SCREENING_AVERAGE_RISK`, the status is `INELIGIBLE_AGE` when age is below 45 or above 75, and `ELIGIBLE` otherwise; both endpoints are inclusive, so exactly 45 and exactly 75 are eligible. Otherwise, for `SCREENING_HIGH_RISK`, the status is `INELIGIBLE_AGE` when age is below 40; if the age passes but the patient's `risk_tier` is neither `INCREASED` nor `HIGH`, the status is `INELIGIBLE_RISK_MISMATCH`; otherwise `ELIGIBLE`. Otherwise — that is, for `SURVEILLANCE` or `DIAGNOSTIC_FOLLOWUP` — the status is `ELIGIBLE` when age is at least 18 and `INELIGIBLE_AGE` when it is below 18. Creation succeeds regardless of the outcome; ineligibility only bites later, when someone tries to book a room.

Creation also freezes `risk_tier_snapshot` as a copy of the patient's `risk_tier` at that instant. This snapshot, not the live patient field, is what caps the surveillance interval months later, so a patient whose record is updated to `HIGH` after their episode opened still has that episode's interval capped by the tier the episode captured. Four more fields start out at their initial values: `assigned_interval_months` is null until a surveillance recommendation exists, `closure_reason` is null while the episode is not closed, `outreach_log` is an empty array, and `prep_adherence_score` is 0.00 with `prep_attestation` at `NONE`.

The `due_date` is computed at creation in `YYYY-MM-DD` form by adding a whole number of calendar days to `index_date`. A `DIAGNOSTIC_FOLLOWUP` episode is due in 90 days; every other indication is due in 180 days. There is one re-entry into this rule, and it fires only inside the positive non-invasive cascade: after that cascade has recomputed the patient's `navigation_priority`, if the priority now stands at `HIGH`, the due date is recomputed as `index_date` plus 30 days instead. Outside the cascade this second branch never runs, because `navigation_priority` is read before any episode exists.

Finally, episode creation recomputes the owning patient's `open_episode_count` and `navigation_priority` from the full set of that patient's episodes. Episodes may be listed with optional `patient_id`, `referral_id`, `preceding_episode_id`, `clinical_status`, `navigation_status`, and `indication` filters. A `PatientNavigator` or `ReferralCoordinator` may close an `OPEN` episode by supplying a `reason` of `PATIENT_DECLINED`, `LOST_TO_FOLLOW_UP`, or `SUPERSEDED_BY_OTHER_PATHWAY`; the reason is stored in `closure_reason`, the first two both drive `OPEN → CLOSED_DECLINED` and the third drives `OPEN → CLOSED_SUPERSEDED`, and in every case the patient's `open_episode_count` and `navigation_priority` are recomputed afterwards. Closing an episode whose `clinical_status` is not `OPEN` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_EPISODE_STATE`.

### 3.4. Non-Invasive Screening and the Positive-Result Cascade

Not every screening cycle starts with a colonoscopy. A `ReferralCoordinator` or `PatientNavigator` may order a stool-based test against an open episode by supplying a `test_type` of either `FIT` or `MT_SDNA` and a `collected_date` in `YYYY-MM-DD` form recording when the specimen was collected. The test is created with `result` at `PENDING` and `resulted_at` null; its `episode_id` is the ordering episode, and `follow_up_episode_id` is null. Ordering a test against an episode whose `clinical_status` is already closed is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_EPISODE_STATE`. Tests may be listed with optional `episode_id`, `follow_up_episode_id`, `result`, and `test_type` filters.

Only a `Pathologist` may post a laboratory outcome, and only once: the submitted `result` must be one of `NEGATIVE`, `POSITIVE`, or `INSUFFICIENT_SPECIMEN`, and a test whose `result` is no longer `PENDING` rejects a second posting with `ERR_TEST_ALREADY_RESULTED`. Posting a result stamps `resulted_at` from the logical clock in every case.

A `POSITIVE` stool test is the trigger for a four-hop cascade, because a positive non-invasive screen is not a diagnosis — it is an indication for a diagnostic colonoscopy, and the practice is on the hook for getting the patient into a room quickly. Hop one creates a new `ScreeningEpisode` for the same patient with `indication` `DIAGNOSTIC_FOLLOWUP`, `origin` `NONINVASIVE_POSITIVE`, and `preceding_episode_id` set to the episode that ordered this test; the cascade is not subject to the duplicate-open-episode rule of Section 3.3, so it creates that episode even when the patient already holds a live `DIAGNOSTIC_FOLLOWUP` episode, and it derives `index_date`, `eligibility_status` and `risk_tier_snapshot` exactly as a client-driven creation would while leaving `referral_id` null. Hop two records the new episode's identifier on the test as `follow_up_episode_id`, closing the loop between laboratory result and clinical action. Hop three recomputes the patient's `open_episode_count` and, from it, `navigation_priority` — and because the patient now holds both the original episode and the follow-up, that count is frequently at two, which flips the priority to `HIGH`. Hop four then recomputes the new episode's `due_date`; since the priority was just recomputed, the 30-day high-priority window applies rather than the 90-day diagnostic-followup window, and the new episode's `navigation_status` is moved to `OUTREACH_ACTIVE` so the navigator picks it up immediately. The hops run in that order, and hop four's due date is read from the priority hop three produced — this is the only place in the system where the due-date rule's second branch can fire. Hops two through four belong to the follow-up episode's own creation rather than to later mutations of it, so that episode is first observable at `version` 1 with the cascade's `due_date` and `navigation_status` already in place, while the test row is mutated once by the result posting and comes back at `version` 2.

### 3.5. Bowel-Preparation Navigation and the Adherence Score

Colonoscopy quality is decided the night before, in the patient's kitchen. The navigation dimension of an episode exists to keep score of how much contact the practice has actually achieved with the patient and how recently, and to gate check-in on that score. Every outreach touchpoint is recorded by a `PatientNavigator` with a `channel` — one of `PHONE`, `SMS`, `PORTAL`, or `MAIL` — and an optional `acknowledged` boolean recording whether the patient responded, which defaults to false when it is omitted or written as `null`. Each accepted touchpoint appends one object to the episode's `outreach_log` holding `channel`, `occurred_at` taken from the logical clock, and `acknowledged`; the log is append-only and ordered by insertion. Outreach against an episode whose `clinical_status` is `CLOSED_SURVEILLANCE_SET`, `CLOSED_DECLINED`, or `CLOSED_SUPERSEDED` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_EPISODE_STATE`.

`prep_adherence_score` is then recomputed **from the whole log, from scratch, every single time** — it is never incrementally adjusted, and no status filter applies because the log has no statuses. Start a running raw total at zero and walk the log in insertion order. Each entry contributes a channel weight of 3.0 for `PHONE`, 2.0 for `SMS`, 1.5 for `PORTAL`, and 1.0 for `MAIL`; that weight is multiplied by an acknowledgement multiplier of 1.0 when the entry was acknowledged and 0.4 when it was not; and that product is multiplied by a recency decay. The decay is computed by taking the elapsed time from the entry's `occurred_at` to the logical clock's current instant, converting it to whole days by discarding any partial day, subtracting 0.05 from 1.00 for each of those whole days, then raising the result to a floor of 0.25 if it fell below that and lowering it to a ceiling of 1.00 if it somehow exceeded it. The three factors multiplied together are **rounded half-up to two decimal places once, per touchpoint**, and only then added to the running raw total; summing first and rounding at the end is a different number and is wrong.

An attestation bonus is then added to the raw total: 2.50 when the episode's `prep_attestation` is `COMPLETED`, minus 1.00 when it is `NOT_COMPLETED`, and zero when it is `NONE`. The sum is clamped into the range 0.00 through 10.00 inclusive — floored at 0.00, ceilinged at 10.00 — and the clamped value is rounded half-up to two decimal places to become the stored `prep_adherence_score`. Only this episode's own outreach participates; another episode's log for the same patient is invisible here.

The navigation dimension moves on two thresholds. Recording outreach compares the freshly recomputed score against 3.00: at 3.00 or above the episode advances `NOT_STARTED → OUTREACH_ACTIVE` (or holds/returns to `OUTREACH_ACTIVE` from `PREP_AT_RISK`), and below 3.00 it moves to `PREP_AT_RISK` — from `NOT_STARTED → PREP_AT_RISK` on a first weak touchpoint, or `OUTREACH_ACTIVE → PREP_AT_RISK` when decay has eroded a previously adequate score. Because a recovering score sends `PREP_AT_RISK → OUTREACH_ACTIVE` again, this pair of states genuinely cycles, and the cycle is bounded only by the score threshold and never by a round counter. Both branches move only an episode currently sitting in `NOT_STARTED`, `OUTREACH_ACTIVE` or `PREP_AT_RISK`: outreach recorded against an episode already in `PREP_CONFIRMED` is accepted and rescores `prep_adherence_score`, but leaves `navigation_status` at `PREP_CONFIRMED` whichever side of 3.00 the new score falls on.

Attestation is the patient's own act, and only the `Patient` role may perform it, on their own episode: `X-User-ID` must equal the episode's `patient_id`, else the request is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. The submitted `regimen_completed` boolean sets `prep_attestation` to `COMPLETED` when true and `NOT_COMPLETED` when false, after which the adherence score is recomputed with the corresponding bonus applied. The resulting score is then compared against the higher threshold of 5.00, and unlike outreach this comparison rewrites `navigation_status` from whatever state it held: at 5.00 or above the episode advances `OUTREACH_ACTIVE → PREP_CONFIRMED` or `PREP_AT_RISK → PREP_CONFIRMED` and holds at `PREP_CONFIRMED` when it was already there; below 5.00 the episode lands in `PREP_AT_RISK` from any of `NOT_STARTED`, `OUTREACH_ACTIVE` or `PREP_CONFIRMED`, including the `OUTREACH_ACTIVE → PREP_AT_RISK` demotion, and the patient must be worked again. As with outreach, attestation is refused with `ERR_INVALID_EPISODE_STATE` on an episode whose clinical dimension has already closed. `PREP_CONFIRMED` is the only navigation state from which a patient may be checked in.

The navigation dimension reaches its terminal `PREP_CONFIRMED → NAVIGATION_COMPLETE` when the procedure is completed by its endoscopist, after which no further outreach or attestation is accepted on that episode — either is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_EPISODE_STATE`. It can also be knocked backwards: an endoscopist who aborts the exam for inadequate preparation demotes `PREP_CONFIRMED → PREP_AT_RISK` and resets `prep_attestation` to `NONE`, so that the navigation cycle can legitimately resume even though the patient had attested completion. What the endoscope sees overrides what the patient said.

### 3.6. Building the Suite Day: Room Blocks and Anesthesia Coverage

A `PracticeAdmin` lays out the endoscopy suite as a series of room blocks. A block is created with a `room_code` labelling the suite room — a non-empty string with no upper length bound and no uniqueness requirement, so two blocks may legitimately name the same room — a `session_date` giving the calendar date of the block in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, a `window_start_utc` that is the inclusive start instant of the bookable window and a `window_end_utc` that is its exclusive end, and a `lead_endoscopist_id` naming the `Clinician` who leads it — who must be of type `ENDOSCOPIST`. The two window instants are shape-checked but never compared with each other, so a zero-length or an inverted window is accepted and simply yields a `capacity_minutes` of zero or a negative one. Three optional block parameters follow, and each of them changes the arithmetic of the day: `anesthesia_mode` is either `MODERATE_SEDATION` or `MAC` and defaults to `MODERATE_SEDATION`; `turnover_minutes` is the number of minutes appended to every booking's slot to form its room occupancy interval, an integer from 0 to 60 defaulting to 15; and `max_procedures` is the ceiling on how many non-cancelled bookings the block may hold, an integer from 1 to 20 defaulting to 8. A new block is created in `status` `DRAFT` with `suspension_reason` null, and `capacity_minutes` is computed from the window as the whole number of minutes between `window_start_utc` and `window_end_utc`, discarding any partial minute; `booked_minutes` starts at 0 and `remaining_minutes` already equals `capacity_minutes` while the block still sits in `DRAFT`. Blocks may be listed with optional `lead_endoscopist_id`, `session_date`, and `status` filters, the `session_date` filter being compared literally against the stored string rather than re-validated as a calendar date.

The block's posture is driven by an action-carrying operation that accepts an `action` of `PUBLISH`, `SUSPEND`, `RESUME`, or `CLOSE` plus an optional `reason`, and moves `status` among `DRAFT`, `PUBLISHED`, `COVERAGE_CONFIRMED`, `SUSPENDED`, and `CLOSED`, of which `CLOSED` is terminal. Any action not declared from the block's current status is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_SESSION_STATE`. `PUBLISH` moves `DRAFT → PUBLISHED` and sets `remaining_minutes` equal to `capacity_minutes`, opening the block for booking. `SUSPEND` moves either `PUBLISHED → SUSPENDED` or `COVERAGE_CONFIRMED → SUSPENDED`, requires a `reason` — omitting it is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — records it in `suspension_reason`, and stops the block accepting new bookings. A `reason` supplied to any of the other three actions is shape-validated and then ignored, and `suspension_reason` is cleared only by `RESUME`, so a block closed out of `SUSPENDED` keeps the reason it was suspended with.

`RESUME` is deliberately not a memory operation. From `SUSPENDED` it clears `suspension_reason` and returns the block to whichever posture its *current* coverage implies, not the posture it held before suspension: `SUSPENDED → COVERAGE_CONFIRMED` when the block still has an `ACTIVE` coverage row that fully spans its window, and `SUSPENDED → PUBLISHED` when it does not. A block whose only covering row was released while it sat suspended therefore comes back as `PUBLISHED`.

`CLOSE` may be applied from `PUBLISHED`, `COVERAGE_CONFIRMED`, or `SUSPENDED`, but only when no booking on the block is still in `BOOKED`, `CHECKED_IN`, or `IN_PROGRESS`; an attempt to close over live work is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_SESSION_STATE`. Closing the block also releases every one of its `ACTIVE` anesthesia coverage rows to `RELEASED`, which immediately frees those intervals from the covering clinician's supervision load. Every one of these four posture actions increments the block's `version` by 1.

Anesthesia coverage is assigned by a `PracticeAdmin` supplying an `anesthesia_clinician_id` — a `Clinician` of type `ANESTHESIA` — with `coverage_start_utc` as the inclusive start and `coverage_end_utc` as the exclusive end of the coverage interval. The block must be sitting in `PUBLISHED` for coverage to be assigned at all: `DRAFT`, `COVERAGE_CONFIRMED`, `SUSPENDED` and `CLOSED` are each rejected with `ERR_INVALID_SESSION_STATE`, which is also why a block never holds more than one `ACTIVE` coverage row at a time. Two independent geometry rules then govern the request, and the precedence of Section 2.5 settles which rejection a request that fails both receives: the supervision ceiling is evaluated before the coverage-span rule. Taking the span rule first, the coverage must fully span the block: `covers_full_window` is true exactly when `coverage_start_utc` is at or before the block's `window_start_utc` **and** `coverage_end_utc` is at or after the block's `window_end_utc`; when it is false the assignment is rejected with `ERR_COVERAGE_WINDOW_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 422). Partial coverage of an endoscopy block is not a thing this practice does.

Second, the assignment is tested against the supervision ceiling, computed across **every** block in the system rather than just this one, since an anesthesia clinician's attention is a single resource. Collect that clinician's coverage rows whose `status` is `ACTIVE`; rows in `RELEASED` are excluded and stop counting the instant they are released. Then take a set of event instants consisting of the candidate's `coverage_start_utc` together with the `coverage_start_utc` of every collected row that falls at or after the candidate's start and strictly before the candidate's end. At each event instant compute the load as one — the candidate itself — plus the number of collected rows whose own interval contains that instant, meaning the row's start is at or before the instant and the row's end is strictly after it. The peak load is the largest of those per-instant loads, and if that peak exceeds the clinician's `max_overlapping_rooms`, the assignment is rejected with `ERR_ANESTHESIA_CONCURRENCY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409). When the assignment is accepted, `overlap_index` is stored on the new row as a one-based count: one plus the number of that clinician's `ACTIVE` rows whose interval contains the candidate's own start instant, so the first coverage at that instant records 1.

A coverage row is created in `status` `ACTIVE`, and its `session_id` records the block it staffs. Accepting a fully covering row against a block in `PUBLISHED` advances that block `PUBLISHED → COVERAGE_CONFIRMED`, and increments the block's `version` by 1. The inverse operation releases a coverage row: it sets that row's `status` to `RELEASED` so it stops counting toward its clinician's overlap ceiling, returns the released row as the response payload, moves the block `COVERAGE_CONFIRMED → PUBLISHED` when no `ACTIVE` fully covering row remains on it, and increments the block's `version` by 1. Releasing a row that is not `ACTIVE` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_SESSION_STATE`, and so is releasing a row whose block is neither `COVERAGE_CONFIRMED` nor `SUSPENDED`; the row's own status is examined before the block's. A release against a `SUSPENDED` block leaves that block in `SUSPENDED`, and the block's `version` increments either way — whether or not the `COVERAGE_CONFIRMED → PUBLISHED` move actually occurred. Coverage rows may be listed with optional `session_id`, `anesthesia_clinician_id`, and `status` filters.

### 3.7. Booking, Performing and Abandoning a Colonoscopy

Booking a slot is the busiest write in this system. A `ReferralCoordinator` or `PracticeAdmin` books against a published block by supplying the `episode_id` this colonoscopy satisfies, the `performing_endoscopist_id` who will do it, a `slot_start_utc` giving the inclusive start of the procedure slot, a `duration_minutes` between 15 and 120, and `expected_session_version` pinning the block version the client observed — a required integer that the shape step accepts at any value, so a zero or negative figure is not a malformed request but merely a version that cannot match, and is reported as `ERR_STALE_SESSION_VERSION`. The block itself must be in `PUBLISHED` or `COVERAGE_CONFIRMED`; a `DRAFT`, `SUSPENDED`, or `CLOSED` block accepts no bookings and rejects the request with `ERR_SESSION_NOT_ACCEPTING_BOOKINGS`. The episode's `clinical_status` must be `OPEN`, else `ERR_INVALID_EPISODE_STATE`, and its `eligibility_status` must be `ELIGIBLE`, else `ERR_EPISODE_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (HTTP 422) — this is where an ineligible episode finally stops being bookable.

Pre-procedure risk clearance is enforced here too. A patient whose `asa_class` is 4 or 5 may not be slotted straight into the suite; such a booking is permitted only when the episode carries a `referral_id` whose referral reached `ACCEPTED_OFFICE_VISIT`, evidencing that the patient was seen in clinic first. Otherwise the booking is rejected with `ERR_PREPROCEDURE_RISK_UNCLEARED` (HTTP 422). A patient with `asa_class` of 3 or below clears this rule unconditionally.

The slot geometry is then checked in a fixed order. First, `slot_end_utc` is derived as `slot_start_utc` advanced by `duration_minutes`, and `occupancy_end_utc` as `slot_end_utc` advanced by the block's `turnover_minutes`; the whole occupancy interval, turnover included, must sit inside the block window — `slot_start_utc` at or after `window_start_utc` and `occupancy_end_utc` at or before `window_end_utc` — else the request is rejected with `ERR_SLOT_OUTSIDE_SESSION_WINDOW` (HTTP 422). Only those two window instants are consulted; the block's `session_date` is a label and is never compared against `slot_start_utc`. Second, the candidate occupancy interval must be disjoint from the occupancy interval of every booking already on the block whose `status` is `BOOKED`, `CHECKED_IN`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `COMPLETED`, or `ABORTED_INADEQUATE_PREP`; `CANCELLED` rows are skipped entirely. Two intervals conflict when the candidate starts strictly before the other's `occupancy_end_utc` **and** the other starts strictly before the candidate's `occupancy_end_utc`, so touching intervals do not conflict and a slot beginning exactly at another's `occupancy_end_utc` is accepted. A conflict is rejected with `ERR_SLOT_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). Third, the headcount ceiling: when the number of bookings on the block whose `status` is anything other than `CANCELLED` already equals `max_procedures`, the request is rejected with `ERR_SESSION_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409).

Once accepted, the booking is created in `status` `BOOKED` with `released_minutes` at 0 and `prep_quality_bbps`, `polyps_removed_count`, `largest_polyp_mm`, `specimen_count`, `final_classification`, and `cancellation_reason` all still empty. The block's minute ledger is recomputed — never adjusted incrementally — as follows: `capacity_minutes` is the whole minutes between the window instants; `booked_minutes` is the sum, over every booking on the block whose `status` is `BOOKED`, `CHECKED_IN`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `COMPLETED`, or `ABORTED_INADEQUATE_PREP`, of that booking's `duration_minutes` plus the block's `turnover_minutes`; and `remaining_minutes` is `capacity_minutes` less `booked_minutes`. The invariant that booked and remaining minutes sum to capacity holds at every observable instant. The episode moves `OPEN → PROCEDURE_SCHEDULED`, the provisional pricing pass described in Section 3.9 runs and writes `planned_classification` on the booking while creating a `BillingClaim` in `PROVISIONAL_PRICED`, and the block's `version` increments by 1. Bookings may be listed with optional `episode_id`, `session_id`, `performing_endoscopist_id`, and `status` filters.

A booking then walks a short branching lifecycle whose states are `BOOKED`, `CHECKED_IN`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `COMPLETED`, `ABORTED_INADEQUATE_PREP`, and `CANCELLED`, the last three terminal. Any action requested from a state that does not declare it is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_BOOKING_STATE`. A `PatientNavigator` checks the patient in, moving `BOOKED → CHECKED_IN`, and this is the gate the navigation dimension guards: the episode's `navigation_status` must be `PREP_CONFIRMED`, else the check-in is rejected with `ERR_PREP_NOT_CONFIRMED` (HTTP 422). Check-in makes no change to the minute ledger, because the room minutes were committed at booking time, and it reads neither the block's posture — a `SUSPENDED` block still admits check-in of a booking it already holds — nor the episode's clinical dimension.

Starting the procedure moves `CHECKED_IN → IN_PROGRESS` and is reserved to the `Endoscopist` whose `X-User-ID` equals the booking's `performing_endoscopist_id`; another endoscopist is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. That clinician's `credential_status` must be `ACTIVE` — a `SUSPENDED` credential is rejected with `ERR_CLINICIAN_CREDENTIAL_SUSPENDED` (HTTP 422) — and when the block's `anesthesia_mode` is `MAC`, the block's `status` must be `COVERAGE_CONFIRMED` at the moment of the start, else the request is rejected with `ERR_ANESTHESIA_COVERAGE_REQUIRED` (HTTP 409). A `MODERATE_SEDATION` block carries no coverage requirement. The start stamps the booking's `updated_at` from the logical clock.

Completion moves `IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED` and is likewise restricted to the performing endoscopist by `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. The completing endoscopist records four findings: `prep_quality_bbps`, the Boston Bowel Preparation Scale total, an integer from 0 to 9; `polyps_removed_count`, the number of polyps removed, never negative; `largest_polyp_mm`, the largest polyp diameter measured endoscopically, never negative; and `specimen_count`, the number of specimen containers sent to pathology, never negative. In every case completion sets the episode's `navigation_status` to `NAVIGATION_COMPLETE`. The clinical dimension then branches on `specimen_count`: with at least one specimen the episode moves `PROCEDURE_SCHEDULED → PATHOLOGY_PENDING` and waits for the laboratory; with zero specimens there is nothing to wait for, and the **no-specimen closure cascade** runs instead — a `SurveillanceRecommendation` is created with `pathology_report_id` null, `basis` `GUIDELINE`, and `status` `ACTIVE`, computed from an all-zero, all-false finalized-pathology aggregate; its `assigned_interval_months` and `next_due_date` are copied onto the episode's `assigned_interval_months` and `due_date`; the episode moves `PROCEDURE_SCHEDULED → CLOSED_SURVEILLANCE_SET`; and the claim is repriced under the final pricing pass, moving `PROVISIONAL_PRICED → REPRICED` and writing the booking's `final_classification`.

Aborting is what happens when the colon is not clean enough to examine. An `Endoscopist` — again the performing one, else `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` — may abort from either `CHECKED_IN` or `IN_PROGRESS`, supplying the observed `prep_quality_bbps` and a `cancellation_reason`; the booking moves to `ABORTED_INADEQUATE_PREP`. The episode's clinical dimension reverts `PROCEDURE_SCHEDULED → OPEN` so the patient can be rebooked, and when the recorded `prep_quality_bbps` is 5 or below the navigation dimension is demoted to `PREP_AT_RISK` and `prep_attestation` is reset to `NONE`. Crucially, **an abort releases no room minutes**: `booked_minutes` is left as it stands, because the room really was occupied. The claim is repriced to a single technical line, described in Section 3.9.

Cancellation is the one operation that is an exact compensation of the booking. A `ReferralCoordinator` or `PracticeAdmin` cancels a booking that is still in `BOOKED`, supplying a `cancellation_reason`; the booking moves `BOOKED → CANCELLED`, `released_minutes` is set to that booking's `duration_minutes` plus the block's `turnover_minutes` — exactly the quantity the booking had consumed, and zero for every booking in any other status — and the block's ledger is recomputed so that booked and remaining minutes again sum to capacity. The episode reverts `PROCEDURE_SCHEDULED → OPEN`, the claim is voided as described in Section 3.9, and the block's `version` increments by 1.

### 3.8. Pathology Routing and Surveillance Interval Assignment

Specimens sent from a completed colonoscopy come back as a pathology report, received by a `Pathologist` against that booking. The report carries an `accession_number` of 1 to 32 characters that must be unique across all pathology reports — a duplicate is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_IDENTIFIER` — a `specimens` array, and an optional `high_grade_dysplasia` boolean, defaulting to false, by which the pathologist asserts high-grade dysplasia anywhere in the report. Each element of `specimens` is an object with a non-empty `label` string, a `histology` drawn from `TUBULAR_ADENOMA`, `TUBULOVILLOUS_ADENOMA`, `SESSILE_SERRATED_LESION`, `HYPERPLASTIC`, `ADENOCARCINOMA`, or `NORMAL_MUCOSA`, and a `size_mm` integer that is never negative; each element is normalised to exactly those three keys, so any further key a client writes into a specimen object is discarded rather than stored or echoed back. A report may only be received against a booking that has reached `COMPLETED`; against a booking in any other status the request is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_BOOKING_STATE`. The `specimens` array is itself required — omitting it, or writing it as `null`, is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — but it may be empty, and it may be empty in any of the three report statuses rather than only in `RECEIVED`: an amendment that supplies an empty array replaces the list with it and leaves the report sitting in `AMENDED` carrying no specimens at all. What an empty list forecloses is not a status but a transition, since finalizing an empty report is refused. Reports may be listed with optional `booking_id`, `routed_to_endoscopist_id`, and `status` filters.

Five findings fields are derived from `specimens` whenever the list is written, and re-derived whenever it is replaced. `adenoma_count` is the number of specimens whose `histology` is `TUBULAR_ADENOMA` or `TUBULOVILLOUS_ADENOMA`. `sessile_serrated_count` is the number whose `histology` is `SESSILE_SERRATED_LESION`. `largest_adenoma_mm` is the greatest `size_mm` among the adenoma specimens — those two adenoma histologies only — and is 0 when there are none. `villous_component` is true when any specimen's `histology` is `TUBULOVILLOUS_ADENOMA`. `adenocarcinoma_present` is true when any specimen's `histology` is `ADENOCARCINOMA`. A new report is created in `status` `RECEIVED` with `amendment_count` at 0, `finalized_at` null, `routed_to_endoscopist_id` null, and `amendment_reason` null.

A report's routing lifecycle cycles among `RECEIVED`, `FINALIZED`, and `AMENDED` and has no terminal state. Finalization is admitted from `RECEIVED` or from `AMENDED`; amendment is admitted only from `FINALIZED`; anything else is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PATHOLOGY_STATE`. Finalizing a report whose `specimens` list is empty is rejected with `ERR_NO_SPECIMENS` (HTTP 422) — the practice will not close a surveillance interval on a report with nothing in it. Neither transition reads anything outside the report itself: the report's own status is tested first and the emptiness of its `specimens` second, and no state of the booking or of the episode can refuse either one.

Finalizing a report runs a **four-hop cascade** — against an episode in `PATHOLOGY_PENDING` in the ordinary case, but against whatever clinical state the episode happens to hold — and every hop must land. Hop one: the report moves `RECEIVED → FINALIZED` (or `AMENDED → FINALIZED`), `finalized_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and `routed_to_endoscopist_id` is set to the booking's `performing_endoscopist_id` — this is the routing act, delivering the result to the physician who did the exam. Hop two: because the report is now finalized, a `SurveillanceRecommendation` is created with `basis` `GUIDELINE` from the finalized-pathology aggregate, and any recommendation on that episode that was previously `ACTIVE` becomes `SUPERSEDED`, since only one recommendation per episode may be `ACTIVE` at a time. Hop three: because that recommendation now exists, the episode's `assigned_interval_months` and `due_date` are set from the recommendation's `assigned_interval_months` and `next_due_date`, and the episode moves `PATHOLOGY_PENDING → CLOSED_SURVEILLANCE_SET`, that closed status being written unconditionally from whichever clinical state the episode held. Hop four: because the episode has closed, the booking's `BillingClaim` is repriced under the final pricing pass, moving `PROVISIONAL_PRICED → REPRICED`, incrementing the claim's `reprice_count`, and writing the booking's `final_classification`. Finalization also increments the episode's `version` by 1.

Amending a finalized report is how the laboratory corrects itself. A `Pathologist` supplies a replacement `specimens` array, an optional `high_grade_dysplasia`, and a mandatory `amendment_reason` — omitting the reason is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, though unlike an override reason it is not tested for consisting only of whitespace. The report moves `FINALIZED → AMENDED`, `specimens` and `high_grade_dysplasia` are replaced — the latter by its own default of false whenever the amendment omits it, so an amendment silent about high-grade dysplasia clears a flag the earlier report carried — all five derived findings fields are recomputed, `amendment_count` increases by 1, and — critically — **while the report sits in `AMENDED` it is excluded from every finalized-only aggregate**, both the surveillance aggregate and the quality numerator, until it is finalized again. The amendment itself mutates nothing but the report row: the episode's `clinical_status`, `assigned_interval_months` and `due_date`, its previously `ACTIVE` surveillance recommendation, and every money field on the booking's claim all stay exactly as the earlier finalization left them, and none of those rows has its `version` bumped, until the amended report is finalized again. Re-finalizing an amended report against an episode that already sits in `CLOSED_SURVEILLANCE_SET` re-runs the whole cascade against an already-closed state as a self-loop: the episode stays in `CLOSED_SURVEILLANCE_SET`, `finalized_at` is re-stamped from the current logical clock, the previously `ACTIVE` recommendation is superseded and a new `ACTIVE` one is created from the amended aggregate, the episode's `assigned_interval_months` and `due_date` are overwritten, and the claim is repriced again under the final pass with `reprice_count` incremented once more. Each pass moves `finalized_at` forward, which can move a booking into or out of a pathology-finalization-date measurement window.

The surveillance interval itself is produced by a three-stage rule whose stage order is normative — applying the risk cap before the findings tier, or the preparation cap before the risk cap, yields a different number of months. The aggregate it reads is assembled over the specimens of **every** pathology report belonging to the episode's booking whose `status` is `FINALIZED`; reports in `RECEIVED` or `AMENDED` are excluded outright. Across those included reports, `adenoma_count` and `sessile_serrated_count` are summed, `villous_component` is true when any included report has it true, `largest_adenoma_mm` is the maximum across them and 0 when there are none, and `high_grade_dysplasia` and `adenocarcinoma_present` are each true when any included report has them true. When no finalized report exists at all, the aggregate is entirely zeros and falses.

Stage one selects a base interval in months from that aggregate, with the first matching branch winning and the branch order normative. If aggregate high-grade dysplasia is true or aggregate adenocarcinoma is present, the base is 6 months. Otherwise, if both the aggregate adenoma count and the aggregate sessile serrated count are zero — a clean exam — the base is 120 months, ten years. Otherwise, if the aggregate adenoma count is 10 or more, the base is 12 months. Otherwise, if the aggregate adenoma count is 5 or more, or the aggregate largest adenoma measures 10 millimetres or more, or a villous component is present, the base is 36 months. Otherwise, if the aggregate sessile serrated count is at least 1, the base is 60 months. Otherwise the base is 84 months, seven years.

Stage two applies the patient risk cap, read from the episode's frozen `risk_tier_snapshot` rather than the patient's live tier: the cap is 120 months for `AVERAGE`, 60 months for `INCREASED`, and 24 months for `HIGH`, and the stage-two result is the smaller of the stage-one base and that cap. Stage three applies the preparation-quality cap: when the anchoring booking's `prep_quality_bbps` is 5 or below, the stage-two result is further reduced to at most 12 months; otherwise it passes through untouched — and an anchoring booking that has no recorded `prep_quality_bbps` at all reads as an absent value rather than as zero, so the stage-three cap does not fire on it. Finally a global floor of 6 months is applied — the result is the larger of the stage-three value and 6 — and that becomes `computed_interval_months`.

An `Endoscopist` or `PracticeAdmin` may also record a recommendation directly against an episode, and this is the path by which a guideline interval is overridden. The request may name a `pathology_report_id` as the anchoring report, may carry an `override_interval_months` with a mandatory accompanying `override_reason`, and must carry `expected_episode_version`. The episode's `clinical_status` must be `CLOSED_SURVEILLANCE_SET`, else `ERR_INVALID_EPISODE_STATE`. When a `pathology_report_id` is cited, that report's `status` must be `FINALIZED` and its `booking_id` must belong to the addressed episode's booking; a report still in `RECEIVED` or `AMENDED`, or one belonging to a different booking, is rejected with `ERR_PATHOLOGY_NOT_FINALIZED` (HTTP 409). The anchoring booking whose `prep_quality_bbps`, `slot_start_utc` and `performing_endoscopist_id` the rest of this subsection reads is that cited report's booking; when no `pathology_report_id` is cited the anchor is selected from the episode's own bookings — the `COMPLETED` ones if the episode has any, otherwise those that are not `CANCELLED`, otherwise all of them — taking the most recently created row of that pool, ties broken by insertion order. An episode that holds no booking at all has no anchor, and the request is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_EPISODE_STATE`. `pathology_report_id` is null on a recommendation whose exam produced no specimens.

An override must be reasonable and attested. The supplied `override_interval_months` must be at least 6 and at most 120, must differ from `computed_interval_months` by no more than 48 months in either direction, and must be accompanied by an `override_reason` that is neither empty nor composed entirely of whitespace; a violation of any of those three conditions is rejected with `ERR_OVERRIDE_OUT_OF_RANGE` (HTTP 422). The segregation rule of Section 2.11 then applies: an override attested by the clinician who performed the anchoring procedure is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, while a recommendation with no override carries no such restriction. When an override survives both checks, `assigned_interval_months` takes the override value and `basis` is recorded as `OVERRIDE_ATTESTED`, with `attesting_endoscopist_id` set to the acting `X-User-ID`; with no override, `assigned_interval_months` equals `computed_interval_months`, `basis` is `GUIDELINE`, and `attesting_endoscopist_id` stays null. Every recommendation persists `computed_interval_months` alongside `assigned_interval_months`, and persists `override_interval_months` and `override_reason` only as they were honoured: both are null on a `GUIDELINE` recommendation, so an `override_reason` supplied without an accompanying `override_interval_months` is shape-checked, then discarded rather than stored, and it is not subjected to the non-empty test.

`next_due_date` is then derived by taking the calendar date of the anchoring booking's `slot_start_utc` and advancing it by `assigned_interval_months` whole calendar months, clamping the day of month to the last day of the target month when the anchor's day does not exist there — an anchor on 31 January advanced by one month lands on 28 or 29 February. Recording a recommendation supersedes the episode's previously `ACTIVE` row to `SUPERSEDED`, copies `assigned_interval_months` and `next_due_date` onto the episode's `assigned_interval_months` and `due_date`, and increments the episode's `version` by 1. Recommendations may be listed with optional `episode_id`, `pathology_report_id`, `attesting_endoscopist_id`, and `status` filters.

### 3.9. Two Numbers for the Same Facts: Claim Pricing and Detection Rates

The financial and quality surfaces of this practice share one property: each computes the same quantity two ways, and both answers are legitimate and must be retained. A claim is priced once provisionally and once finally, and the two prices legitimately differ. An endoscopist's detection rate is computed either by procedure date or by pathology finalization date, and the two rates legitimately differ. Neither pair may be collapsed.

**Pricing, step by step.** A `BillingClaim` is created when a booking is created, records the `booking_id` it prices and the `insurance_plan_id` copied from the patient at booking time as the plan snapshot used for pricing, and begins in `status` `PROVISIONAL_PRICED` with `variant` `PROVISIONAL`, `reprice_count` at 0 and `dispute_reason` null. Pricing itself is a six-step waterfall and the six steps are evaluated in the stated order.

Step one builds `line_items` as an ordered array of objects each holding a `code` and an `allowed_cents`, in exactly the order facility, professional, anesthesia. A polyp count feeds this step, and it is where the two variants first diverge: under the provisional pass the polyp count is forced to zero, while under the final pass it is the booking's recorded `polyps_removed_count`. The `FACILITY` line is allowed 78000 cents plus 950 cents for each scheduled minute in `duration_minutes`. The `PROFESSIONAL` line is allowed 41500 cents plus 13750 cents for each polyp in the polyp count. An `ANESTHESIA` line is appended only when the block's `anesthesia_mode` is `MAC`, allowed 22000 cents plus 410 cents for each scheduled minute. One override replaces that whole schedule: when the booking's `status` is `ABORTED_INADEQUATE_PREP`, `line_items` becomes a single line with `code` `ABORTED_TECHNICAL` allowed a flat 30000 cents.

Step two sets `base_allowed_cents` to the sum of `allowed_cents` across the lines built in step one. Step three selects `complexity_tier` **from `base_allowed_cents` alone**: `TIER_3` when the base is 250000 cents or more, `TIER_2` when it is 150000 cents or more but below that, and `TIER_1` otherwise. The tier carries an addon of 0 cents for `TIER_1`, 8500 cents for `TIER_2`, and 19000 cents for `TIER_3`; the addon is added to the `allowed_cents` of the first line in the array, and `allowed_amount_cents` becomes `base_allowed_cents` plus the addon. The tier is **never re-selected from `allowed_amount_cents`**: a base of 247250 cents stays `TIER_2` even though its adjusted total of 255750 cents sits above the tier-three floor.

Step four decides `classification`, which is either `SCREENING_PREVENTIVE` or `DIAGNOSTIC`. A provisional determination is made first: it is `SCREENING_PREVENTIVE` when the episode's `indication` is `SCREENING_AVERAGE_RISK`, `SCREENING_HIGH_RISK`, or `SURVEILLANCE`, or when the `indication` is `DIAGNOSTIC_FOLLOWUP` **and** the episode's `origin` is `NONINVASIVE_POSITIVE` **and** the plan's `modifier_33_honored` is true; in every other case it is `DIAGNOSTIC`. Under the provisional pass, and also whenever the booking was aborted, that provisional determination stands as the final answer. Under the final pass on a non-aborted booking, the polypectomy carveout gets a chance to flip it: if the booking's `polyps_removed_count` is 1 or more and the plan's `polypectomy_carveout` is `DIAGNOSTIC_CONVERTED`, the classification becomes `DIAGNOSTIC`. A plan set to `PREVENTIVE_RETAINED` leaves a screening exam preventive even when a polyp came out.

Step five walks `line_items` in the same fixed order and accumulates the patient's share, reading each line's `allowed_cents` as step three left it — so the first line's figure already carries the complexity addon. Before the walk, the deductible remaining is the plan's `deductible_cents` less the patient's `deductible_applied_cents`, floored at zero. For each line, under a `SCREENING_PREVENTIVE` classification no deductible is applied to that line at all, and the line's coinsurance is the line's `allowed_cents` multiplied by one minus the plan's `preventive_coverage_pct`, rounded half-up to whole cents — except that an `ANESTHESIA` line on a plan whose `anesthesia_bundled` is false is instead charged the line's `allowed_cents` multiplied by the plan's `diagnostic_coinsurance_pct`, rounded the same way. Under a `DIAGNOSTIC` classification, the line first absorbs deductible equal to the smaller of the line's `allowed_cents` and the deductible remaining, the remaining figure is reduced by that amount before the next line is considered, and the line's coinsurance is the line's `allowed_cents` less the deductible it absorbed, multiplied by the plan's `diagnostic_coinsurance_pct`, rounded half-up to whole cents. Line by line, the absorbed deductible accumulates into `deductible_applied_cents` on the claim — which is therefore always zero under a `SCREENING_PREVENTIVE` classification — the rounded coinsurance accumulates into `coinsurance_cents`, and the patient running total accumulates both. Per-line rounding then summation is normative; summing then rounding diverges by a cent at coinsurance fractions such as 0.175.

Step six truncates. `patient_responsibility_cents` is the smaller of the patient running total and the plan's `out_of_pocket_max_cents` less the patient's `oop_applied_cents`, that headroom itself floored at zero, so a patient who has already exhausted their annual out-of-pocket maximum owes nothing further. `plan_paid_cents` is `allowed_amount_cents` less `patient_responsibility_cents`. Pricing reads the patient's `deductible_applied_cents` and `oop_applied_cents` ledgers as they stand at pricing time, and only claims that have reached `FINALIZED` have contributed to those ledgers.

The provisional pass runs at booking creation, when `polyps_removed_count` is not yet known, and writes its classification to the booking's `planned_classification`. The final pass runs inside the pathology finalization cascade, inside the no-specimen completion cascade, on an abort, and on any later reprice, and writes its classification to the booking's `final_classification`. Because the final pass counts polyps, its `PROFESSIONAL` line is larger, which can push `base_allowed_cents` into a higher complexity tier, and its carveout can flip a `SCREENING_PREVENTIVE` claim to `DIAGNOSTIC`, which switches step five from preventive coverage to the deductible waterfall.

**The claim lifecycle.** A claim's `status` cycles among `PROVISIONAL_PRICED`, `REPRICED`, `FINALIZED`, `DISPUTED`, and the terminal `VOIDED`. Any action requested from a status that does not admit it is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_CLAIM_STATE`. The system itself drives `PROVISIONAL_PRICED → REPRICED` when the final pass is applied by the pathology finalization cascade, the no-specimen completion cascade, or a booking abort: `line_items`, `base_allowed_cents`, `complexity_tier`, `allowed_amount_cents`, `deductible_applied_cents`, `coinsurance_cents`, `patient_responsibility_cents`, and `plan_paid_cents` are all replaced, `variant` becomes `FINAL`, `reprice_count` increases by 1, and the booking's `final_classification` is set from the claim's `classification`. Every application of the final pass writes that `final_classification`, but it stamps the booking's own `version` only when it was driven by a pathology finalization or by a `BillingSpecialist`'s reprice; inside a booking abort or a no-specimen completion the booking is already being stamped by the operation that triggered the reprice, so it is stamped exactly once. A `REPRICED → REPRICED` self-loop occurs when the final pass is re-applied after a pathology amendment is finalized, recomputing every money field and incrementing `reprice_count` again.

A `BillingSpecialist` then finalizes a repriced claim, moving `REPRICED → FINALIZED`, which is the moment the claim touches the patient's ledgers: the claim's `deductible_applied_cents` is added to the patient's `deductible_applied_cents`, and the claim's `patient_responsibility_cents` is added to the patient's `oop_applied_cents`. Disputing a finalized claim moves `FINALIZED → DISPUTED`, requires a `dispute_reason` — omitting it is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and is the exact compensation of the finalize: the claim's `deductible_applied_cents` is subtracted from the patient's `deductible_applied_cents` and the claim's `patient_responsibility_cents` is subtracted from the patient's `oop_applied_cents`, while **every money field on the claim itself is left unchanged** so that the reversal is exact and the two patient ledgers return to precisely the values they held before the finalize. Each of those two operations bumps the patient's `version` and refreshes the patient's `updated_at` unconditionally, even when the amounts it moves are zero. Repricing a disputed claim moves `DISPUTED → REPRICED` and re-runs the final pass against current booking and pathology state, incrementing `reprice_count`. Cancelling the underlying booking voids the claim from either `PROVISIONAL_PRICED → VOIDED` or `REPRICED → VOIDED`, zeroing `base_allowed_cents`, `allowed_amount_cents`, `deductible_applied_cents`, `coinsurance_cents`, `patient_responsibility_cents`, and `plan_paid_cents` and emptying `line_items`, while `classification`, `complexity_tier`, `variant` and `reprice_count` are left exactly as they stood. Claims may be listed with optional `booking_id`, `insurance_plan_id`, `status`, and `classification` filters.

**Detection rates.** A `QualityOfficer` opens a measurement window for one endoscopist under one attribution basis, supplying an `endoscopist_id` — a `Clinician` of type `ENDOSCOPIST` — a `period_start_date` as the inclusive first calendar date and a `period_end_date` as the inclusive last, both in `YYYY-MM-DD` form and never compared with each other, so an inverted window is created without complaint and simply captures nothing, and an optional `attribution_basis` of either `PROCEDURE_DATE` or `PATHOLOGY_FINALIZATION_DATE`, defaulting to `PROCEDURE_DATE`. The requested window may not intersect an existing window that has both the same `endoscopist_id` **and** the same `attribution_basis` — every existing window counts here whatever its own `status`, so a `LOCKED` or an `ATTESTED` window still blocks a fresh one that overlaps it — and such a collision is rejected with `ERR_QUALITY_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). Windows that differ in `attribution_basis` may overlap freely — indeed, measuring the same calendar month both ways is the point. A new window begins in `status` `OPEN` with `screening_colonoscopy_count` and `adenoma_positive_count` at 0, `remediation_required` false, and `adr_pct`, `benchmark_pct`, `benchmark_result`, `remediation_plan`, and `attesting_user_id` all empty. Windows may be listed with optional `endoscopist_id`, `status`, and `benchmark_result` filters.

Locking a window moves `OPEN → LOCKED` and is the computation event. It is refused with `ERR_QUALITY_PERIOD_NOT_ENDED` (HTTP 422) unless the calendar date of the logical clock is on or after the window's `period_end_date` — a window cannot be measured before it has finished. The denominator is built by considering every booking whose `status` is `COMPLETED`, whose `performing_endoscopist_id` is the window's endoscopist, and whose episode's `indication` is `SCREENING_AVERAGE_RISK` or `SCREENING_HIGH_RISK`. Bookings in `BOOKED`, `CHECKED_IN`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `ABORTED_INADEQUATE_PREP`, or `CANCELLED` never enter either the numerator or the denominator, and surveillance or diagnostic-followup indications are outside the measure entirely. For each candidate, the reports consulted are only those pathology reports on that booking whose `status` is `FINALIZED`; a report sitting in `AMENDED` is invisible both to the numerator and to finalization-date attribution.

The attribution date is then chosen by the window's basis, and this is the second two-algorithm surface. Under `PROCEDURE_DATE` the attribution date is the calendar date of the booking's `slot_start_utc`, so a completed exam whose pathology is finalized after `period_end_date` still counts in the denominator, and counts in the numerator only if a finalized report with an adenoma exists at lock time. Under `PATHOLOGY_FINALIZATION_DATE` a booking with no finalized report is dropped from the denominator altogether, and for the rest the attribution date is the earliest `finalized_at` among that booking's finalized reports — the aggregate is order-free and needs no tie-break. A candidate joins the denominator when its attribution date falls on or after `period_start_date` and on or before `period_end_date`; **both endpoints are inclusive**. The same completed exams therefore yield a different denominator, a different numerator, and a different rate under the two bases.

`screening_colonoscopy_count` is the size of that denominator set, and `adenoma_positive_count` is the number of those bookings for which at least one pathology report on the booking is `FINALIZED` and has an `adenoma_count` of 1 or more. When the denominator is zero, `adr_pct` is `null` rather than 0.0000; otherwise it is the numerator divided by the denominator, rounded half-up to four decimal places. `benchmark_pct` is snapshotted at lock time from the measured clinician's `adr_benchmark_pct`. `benchmark_result` is then `INSUFFICIENT_VOLUME` when `screening_colonoscopy_count` is below the minimum measurable volume of one exam — that is, when it is zero — otherwise `MET` when `adr_pct` is at or above `benchmark_pct`, and otherwise `MISSED`. `remediation_required` is set true only when `benchmark_result` is `MISSED`, and false in both other outcomes. Attesting a locked window moves `LOCKED → ATTESTED`, requires a `remediation_plan` — omitting it is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — stores that plan text, and records the acting `X-User-ID` in `attesting_user_id`.

## 5. Error Code Catalogue
