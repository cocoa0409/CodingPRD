# Mobile Dentistry API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Mobile dental programmes carry an operatory into places that were never built for one. A two-person team loads a portable unit, a sterilizer and a tray of instruments into a van, sets up in a school cafeteria on Tuesday and in the day room of a skilled-nursing facility on Wednesday, and works through a roster of children and residents who would otherwise never see a dentist. Everything that a fixed clinic takes for granted — a signed consent on file, a sterilizer whose biological indicator has already been read, a licensed clinician standing in the room, an eligibility check that came back clean — has to be carried along with the equipment and proven at the chairside. This document specifies the back-office system that carries it.

The service described here is a single HTTP API mounted under `/mobile-dentistry`, with one auxiliary test-driving route outside that prefix. It exists to make four interlocking obligations enforceable rather than aspirational. First, **nobody is treated without a consent that is currently in force and broad enough for the procedure being performed** — and because school programmes and nursing facilities run under legally different consent regimes, with different signatories, different expiry arithmetic and different evidentiary requirements, the system carries both regimes on one record and knows which one applies. Second, **no procedure survives a sterilization failure**: a biological spore indicator that comes back FAILED reaches backwards through every instrument load run on that unit since the last clean result and retroactively marks the work performed with those instruments, unwinding the billing, the treatment plans and the referrals that were built on top of it. Third, **one clinical encounter can be priced under any of three completely different money regimes** — a Medicaid all-inclusive encounter rate, a commercial per-line fee schedule, or an uninsured sliding fee keyed on the federal poverty level — and all three have to close the same conservation identity to the cent. Fourth, **a programme period, once reported to the state, freezes**: clinical and financial writes against it stop, deliberately and reversibly, while roster administration, safety operations and case management keep running.

The reader should treat this document as the complete specification. Every field name, threshold, ordering rule and error identifier the implementation needs is somewhere in these pages; nothing is left to convention or to a companion schema. Where two rules could plausibly interact in more than one order, the order is stated explicitly, because in a compliance system the order in which two refusals are evaluated is itself a requirement.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Personnel, Roles and Identity

Every request to this API carries two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that principal is acting in. Both must be present, and `X-User-Role` must name one of the seven declared roles, or the request is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. If the role is well-formed but is not one of the roles authorised for the endpoint being called, the refusal is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.

The seven roles are `ProgramAdmin`, `SiteCoordinator`, `FieldDentist`, `FieldHygienist`, `DentalAssistant`, `BillingSpecialist` and `Guardian`. The role model is deliberately one of segregation of duties rather than a privilege ladder: `ProgramAdmin` is **not** a superset of the clinical roles. A `ProgramAdmin` runs the programme — periods, sites, holds, portable units, staff records, calibration, reporting and the logical clock — and cannot screen, examine, perform a procedure, price, claim or adjudicate. A `BillingSpecialist` owns coverage verification and the entire billing axis and cannot touch the clinical axis at all. Conversely no clinical role may verify a coverage or price an encounter.

Identity binds differently per role, and this binding is what ownership checks compare against:

- When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it for audit purposes but never compares it to any row's `id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `SiteCoordinator`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the `Site` that coordinator runs — the school nurse's school, or the nursing facility's activities office. Ownership checks on any resource that references a site compare that resource's `site_id` to `X-User-ID`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `FieldDentist`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `StaffMember` record. The same holds for `FieldHygienist` and for `DentalAssistant`: in all three cases `X-User-ID` is a `StaffMember` `id`, and any rule that speaks of "the acting staff member" resolves it through that id.
- When `X-User-Role` is `BillingSpecialist`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity and is never compared to a row's `id`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `Guardian`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque identifier for a parent, guardian or nursing-facility responsible party. It is not a `Patient` id and not a `StaffMember` id; it is matched only against a report's `recipient_user_id`. A `Guardian`'s entire authorised surface is the single-report read, and every other endpoint refuses a `Guardian` with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.

Segregation of duties is also enforced *within* a role. The staff member who ran a sterilizer cycle may not be the staff member who reads that cycle's biological indicator, even though both actions are open to a `DentalAssistant`; that conflict surfaces as `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, read from the **logical clock** of §2.5 rather than from host time, so every response issued while the clock is parked on one instant carries that same instant.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination, Ordering and Filter Semantics

Every list endpoint accepts `limit` and `offset` as query-string parameters. `limit` defaults to 10 when omitted, has a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100; `offset` defaults to 0 and has a minimum of 0. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is a range violation and is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`. Every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside `meta`, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters *before* the page window is applied, and `has_more` is true exactly when rows remain beyond the current window.

**Default ordering is `created_at` ascending on every list endpoint, with no exceptions and no caller-selectable sort.** Because two rows minted inside the same millisecond can carry identical `created_at` values, ties are broken by insertion order — the order in which the rows were actually written to the store, which is the order of the requests that created them. Ties are never broken by `id`; identifiers are random UUIDs and would produce an ordering unrelated to the sequence of events. The same insertion-order tie-break governs every internal ordering the business rules depend on, including the "most recent sterilization load" selection and the roster line ordering inside a generated report.

Filters on list endpoints are combined with logical AND: a request supplying both a `site_id` and a `status` returns only rows matching both. **A filter value that is syntactically acceptable but matches nothing returns an empty page — `data` is an empty array, `total_count` is 0, `has_more` is false — and never an error.** This includes a value outside an enumerated field's declared set: filtering the site list by a `site_type` of `CLINIC`, or the encounter list by a `clinical_status` of `PENDING`, produces an empty page rather than `ERR_INVALID_ENUM`, because the enum-membership rung governs write payloads and not query strings. It also includes an unknown foreign-key value: filtering procedures by a `treatment_plan_id` that resolves to no row returns an empty page rather than `ERR_NOT_FOUND`. Only `limit` and `offset` are range-checked on a list request.

Each list endpoint recognises a fixed set of filter keys, compares each one as an exact string equality against the stored value, treats a filter supplied with an empty value as absent, and **silently ignores every other query parameter** rather than refusing it. The sets are: sites — `site_type`, `status`; site holds — `site_id`, `hold_status`; patients — `site_id`, `patient_type`; consents — `patient_id`, `site_id`, `program_period_id`, `superseded_by_consent_id`, `status`; sterilization loads — `portable_unit_id`, `site_visit_id`, `loaded_by_staff_id`, `result_recorded_by_staff_id`, `spore_result`; site visits — `site_id`, `program_period_id`, `portable_unit_id`, `lead_staff_id`, `onsite_dentist_staff_id`, `remote_dentist_staff_id`, `status`; encounters — `site_visit_id`, `patient_id`, `consent_id`, `coverage_id`, `clinical_status`, `billing_status`; procedures — `encounter_id`, `treatment_plan_id`, `sterilization_load_id`, `performing_staff_id`, `procedure_code`, `status`; treatment plans — `encounter_id`, `patient_id`, `authoring_staff_id`, `plan_status` and `follow_up_status`, the last of which is matched against the freshly derived projection rather than against any stored value; referrals — `treatment_plan_id`, `patient_id`, `status`; coverages — `patient_id`, `payer_type`, `verification_status`; reports — `program_period_id`, `site_id`, `patient_id`, `report_type`, `status`.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamp Rendering and the Version Ledger

Every persisted resource in this system carries four common properties. `id` is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix and no embedded meaning; clients never supply it. `created_at` is the instant the row was written. `updated_at` is the instant the row last changed. Both are stamped from the **logical clock** of §2.5 rather than from host time, so every row a single request writes carries one identical instant, and so do all the rows written while an administrator has left the clock parked where it was. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 on creation and counts the mutations the row has absorbed. All fifteen entity types — `ProgramPeriod`, `Site`, `SiteHold`, `Patient`, `Consent`, `PortableUnit`, `SterilizationLoad`, `StaffMember`, `SiteVisit`, `Coverage`, `Encounter`, `Procedure`, `TreatmentPlan`, `Referral` and `Report` — carry all four; none of them is a bare-record type that omits `updated_at` or `version`.

**Timestamp rendering is uniform and is not negotiable.** Every instant-valued field in every response body is rendered as an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with exactly three fractional-second digits and a trailing `Z` — the shape `2026-05-01T09:30:00.000Z`. This applies to `created_at`, `updated_at`, `api_request_timestamp`, and to every domain instant: `frozen_at`, `starts_at`, `ends_at`, `released_at`, `capacity_determined_at`, `signed_at`, `expires_at`, `reaffirmation_due_at`, `revoked_at`, `expired_at`, `quarantined_at`, `last_passed_load_at`, `cycle_completed_at`, `incubation_ends_at`, `result_recorded_at`, `scheduled_start_at`, `scheduled_end_at`, `started_at`, `completed_at`, `cancelled_at`, `verified_at`, `teledentistry_reviewed_at`, `performed_at`, `last_contact_at`, `due_at`, `last_sent_at`, `next_eligible_send_at`, `abandoned_at`, `case_management_opened_at`, `generated_at` and `submitted_at`. **Caller-supplied instants are re-rendered canonically rather than echoed verbatim**: a client that submits `scheduled_start_at` as `2026-05-04T14:00:00Z` reads it back as `2026-05-04T14:00:00.000Z`, and one that submits `2026-05-04T14:00:00.123456Z` reads it back truncated to `2026-05-04T14:00:00.123Z`. Sub-millisecond precision is **truncated and never rounded**; a submitted value carrying a non-UTC offset is converted to UTC before it is stored, so `2026-05-04T16:00:00+02:00` reads back as `2026-05-04T14:00:00.000Z`; and a value carrying no zone designator at all is read as if it were UTC. Comparisons between instants are performed at millisecond resolution on the underlying values, not on their string renderings.

Date-valued fields are a separate family and are always rendered as a bare `YYYY-MM-DD` calendar date with no time component and no zone suffix: `starts_on`, `ends_on`, `date_of_birth`, `last_calibrated_on`, `calibration_due_on`, `verified_through_date`, `determination_date`, `activated_on`, `follow_up_due_date`, `last_varnish_date` and `credential_expires_on`, plus the `calibrated_on` value supplied on a calibration request.

`version` and `updated_at` move together and only together. **A row's `version` increases by exactly 1 for each request that changes any of its persisted fields — never by 2 in a single request, no matter how many cascade links reach the same row — and `updated_at` is refreshed at exactly the same moments.** A request that only reads a row, or that only refreshes a value the system derives fresh on every read, changes neither. Two families of field are explicitly outside the version ledger:

- **Read-derived projections.** `PortableUnit.calibration_due_on` and `PortableUnit.calibration_status`, and `TreatmentPlan.follow_up_status`, are recomputed against the logical clock every time the row is read. `Site.active_hold_count` and `Encounter.tainted_procedure_count` belong to the same family but are recomputed by counting rows rather than by consulting the clock — which is why voiding the last tainted procedure on an encounter drops `tainted_procedure_count` to zero and re-opens pricing without any field on the encounter itself having been rewritten. Recomputation on read never bumps `version` and never refreshes `updated_at`.
- **Cross-aggregate ledgers.** `SiteVisit.booked_chair_minutes`, `Patient.varnish_applications_in_period` and `Patient.last_varnish_date` are sums and counts over rows belonging to *other* aggregates. When a procedure is planned, performed, voided, tainted or made ineligible, these ledgers are re-established immediately, but the owning `SiteVisit` and `Patient` rows do **not** take a `version` increment and do **not** get a fresh `updated_at` from that recomputation alone. They take an increment only when a rule elsewhere in this document explicitly says so.

Section 2.11 gives the complete per-operation version ledger, naming exactly which rows increment on every mutating request in the system.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock and Time Arithmetic

Nothing in this system reads the host machine's wall clock. Every expiry, incubation window, service-level deadline, follow-up cadence and calendar comparison reads a **logical clock** whose value is exactly `2026-05-01T00:00:00Z` from server start until a `ProgramAdmin` moves it, and which never falls back to real time afterwards. The clock is moved by an administrator-only test-driving capability that accepts a single field, `to_iso8601`, and answers with `{ current_utc: string }` rendered in the same canonical three-fractional-digit form as every other instant. Moving the clock writes no rows.

"The logical clock date" means the calendar date of the logical clock instant in UTC. Where a rule compares a date to a date, the comparison is a pure calendar comparison with no time-of-day component; where it compares an instant to an instant, the comparison is at millisecond resolution. "Whole calendar days elapsed" between two dates is the exact count of day boundaries between them: from `2026-02-01` to `2026-05-02` is 90 whole days.

Calendar-day addition is exact and month-length aware. Adding 365 days to `2026-05-01T09:00:00.000Z` yields `2027-05-01T09:00:00.000Z`, preserving the time of day. **Business-day addition**, used only for school-site referral deadlines, works differently: starting from the base instant, the system steps forward one calendar day at a time and counts only those days that fall on Monday through Friday, until it has counted the required number; the time of day is preserved throughout and no holiday calendar of any kind is applied.

### 2.6. The Thirty-Eight-Rung Validation Ladder

Every request is evaluated against an ordered ladder of thirty-eight checks. **The ladder is strict: rungs are evaluated from 1 upward, the first rung whose check fails decides the response, and no later rung runs.** A rung that does not apply to the endpoint being called is skipped without effect. This ordering is a requirement in its own right — when two conditions are simultaneously wrong on the same request, the error that surfaces is always the one belonging to the lower-numbered rung, and an implementation that reports the other one is incorrect even though both conditions genuinely hold.

**Rung 1 — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).** Both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` are present, and `X-User-Role` names one of the seven declared roles. Applies to every endpoint including reads and the clock control.

**Rung 2 — `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403).** The acting role appears in the authorised-role list for the endpoint being called, as tabulated in §4. Applies to every endpoint.

**Rung 3 — `ERR_NOT_FOUND` (404).** Every identifier resolves to an existing row. Resolution is strictly left-to-right: path segments first, in the order they appear in the URL, and then the foreign keys carried in the request body, in the order the endpoint's input fields are listed in §4's narrative. A request that names both a non-existent path resource and a non-existent body foreign key reports the path failure. A body foreign key that is absent, null or not a JSON string is **not** a rung-3 failure at all — there is no identifier to resolve, so it falls through to rung 6, which reports the missing or mistyped field. Applies to every endpoint.

**Rung 4 — `ERR_NOT_FOUND` (404) — the Guardian confidentiality wall.** When the acting role is `Guardian` on the single-report read (`GET /mobile-dentistry/reports/{id}`), the report must satisfy both conditions: its `report_type` is `PARENT`, **and** its `recipient_user_id` equals the caller's `X-User-ID`. Any other report — a `FACILITY_ROSTER` report, a `PROGRAM_PERIOD` report, or another family's `PARENT` report — is answered with `ERR_NOT_FOUND` and **not** with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`, so that the very existence of other families' reports is never revealed to a guardian. This rung covers exactly one endpoint, `GET /mobile-dentistry/reports/{id}`, and covers it only for the `Guardian` role; it does not apply to the report list, because a `Guardian` is not an authorised role there and is stopped at rung 2.

**Rung 5 — `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403) — site scoping for coordinators.** When the acting role is `SiteCoordinator` on either report endpoint, the coordinator may see only reports belonging to their own site. On the single-report read, the report's `site_id` must equal the coordinator's `X-User-ID`; a report for another site, and a `PROGRAM_PERIOD` report whose `site_id` is null, are both refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`. This is a deliberately different outcome from the guardian wall one rung above: a coordinator learns that the report exists and is not theirs, while a guardian learns nothing. On the report list, the same scoping is applied as a restriction of the result set rather than as an error — after any caller-supplied filters, the returned rows are narrowed to those whose `site_id` equals `X-User-ID`, so a coordinator who asks for the whole list simply receives their own site's reports, and a coordinator who filters explicitly for another site receives an empty page. The narrowing happens before the page window is cut, so `total_count` counts the coordinator's own reports and not the programme's. This rung covers exactly two endpoints, `GET /mobile-dentistry/reports/{id}` and `GET /mobile-dentistry/reports`, and only for `SiteCoordinator`.

**Rung 6 — `ERR_VALIDATION` (400).** Every field the endpoint requires is present, and every field present carries the declared JSON type. This rung also owns all range and format checking: `household_size` at least 1; `chair_count` at least 1; `tooth_number` between 1 and 32 inclusive when supplied; `oral_hygiene_score` between 0 and 4 inclusive; `manual_patient_responsibility_cents` between 0 and the total charge the pricing pass is *about to* compute — the sum of the encounter's currently-`PERFORMED` `charge_cents` plus the supply surcharge that subtotal selects — inclusive, and **not** the `total_charge_cents` value stored on the row, which is still zero on an encounter that has never been priced; `annual_household_income_cents` at least 0 when supplied; `limit` between 1 and 100 inclusive; `offset` at least 0; `state_code` and `license_state` exactly two uppercase letters; `name` on a programme period between 1 and 120 characters; `name` on a site between 1 and 160 characters. It also owns format checking of every ISO 8601 instant and every `YYYY-MM-DD` date, and it owns start-before-end ordering: a programme period's `starts_on` must be strictly before its `ends_on`, a site hold's `starts_at` strictly before its `ends_at`, and a site visit's `scheduled_start_at` strictly before its `scheduled_end_at`. The two integer screening counts, `cavitated_lesion_count` and `medical_complexity_count`, carry no range of their own — any JSON integer, a negative one included, is accepted and simply fails the "at least 1" and "at least 3" tests of the triage rules. Applies to every write endpoint and every list endpoint.

**Rung 7 — `ERR_INVALID_ENUM` (400) — the closed enum-routing list.** Exactly the following request fields route an off-enum value to `ERR_INVALID_ENUM`: a site's `site_type` and `roster_disclosure_level`; a site hold's `hold_type`; a patient's `patient_type` and `capacity_determination`; a consent's `regime`, `scope_tier` and `responsible_party_authority`; the `new_scope_tier` supplied on a tier amendment; a staff member's `staff_role`, `supervision_level` and `credential_status`; a coverage's `payer_type`; a procedure's `procedure_code` and `surface`; the `exam_modality` supplied on an exam; the `outcome` supplied on an adjudication; the `outcome` supplied on a referral response; the `to_status` supplied on a programme-period status change; a report's `report_type`; and the `result` supplied on a spore-indicator reading. Their closed value sets are, respectively: `SCHOOL` or `SNF`; `SUMMARY_ONLY` or `CLINICAL_DETAIL`; `WEATHER_CLOSURE`, `OUTBREAK_LOCKDOWN` or `ADMINISTRATIVE`; `STUDENT` or `RESIDENT`; `NOT_ASSESSED`, `HAS_CAPACITY` or `LACKS_CAPACITY`; `SCHOOL_GUARDIAN` or `SNF_RESPONSIBLE_PARTY`; `SCREENING_ONLY`, `SCREENING_FLUORIDE`, `SCREENING_FLUORIDE_SEALANT` or `FULL_WITH_RADIOGRAPHS` (both for `scope_tier` and for `new_scope_tier`); `DURABLE_POA`, `HEALTHCARE_PROXY`, `COURT_GUARDIAN` or `SELF`; `DENTIST`, `HYGIENIST`, `PUBLIC_HEALTH_HYGIENIST` or `DENTAL_ASSISTANT`; `DIRECT`, `INDIRECT`, `GENERAL` or `SELF`; `ACTIVE`, `LAPSED` or `INACTIVE`; `MEDICAID_MCO`, `COMMERCIAL_FFS` or `UNINSURED`; `D0190`, `D0150`, `D1206`, `D1351` or `D0272`; `O`, `B`, `L`, `M` or `D`; `ONSITE` or `TELEDENTISTRY`; `PAID` or `DENIED`; `ACCEPTED`, `DECLINED`, `NO_RESPONSE` or `COMPLETED`; `REPORTING`, `OPEN` or `CLOSED`; `PARENT`, `FACILITY_ROSTER` or `PROGRAM_PERIOD`; and `PASSED` or `FAILED`. **No other field routes to this error.** Everything else that is malformed — a wrong type, a missing required field, an out-of-range integer, a bad date — is `ERR_VALIDATION` at rung 6, and every list-endpoint filter, enumerated or not, yields an empty page rather than an error. This rung applies to write endpoints only.

**Rung 8 — `ERR_CONSENT_REGIME_FIELDS` (400).** Consent creation must supply the signer fields its regime demands and must not supply the other regime's. A `SCHOOL_GUARDIAN` consent requires both `guardian_name` and `guardian_relationship`, and must not carry `responsible_party_name`. An `SNF_RESPONSIBLE_PARTY` consent requires both `responsible_party_name` and `responsible_party_authority`, additionally requires `poa_document_ref` whenever `responsible_party_authority` is anything other than `SELF`, and must not carry `guardian_name`. The regime must also agree with the site: a `SCHOOL` site accepts only `SCHOOL_GUARDIAN` consents, and an `SNF` site accepts only `SNF_RESPONSIBLE_PARTY` consents. This rung covers exactly one endpoint, `POST /mobile-dentistry/consents`.

**Rung 9 — `ERR_PERIOD_FROZEN` (409) — the global write gate.** Detailed in §2.7. A gated write is refused whenever the entity it targets resolves to a `ProgramPeriod` whose `status` is `SUBMITTED` or `CLOSED`.

**Rung 10 — `ERR_SITE_ON_HOLD` (409) — the site hold gate.** Detailed in §2.7. A write that schedules, reschedules, starts or registers against a site visit is refused when the visit's site is `SUSPENDED`, or when the visit's own scheduled window intersects any `ACTIVE` hold on that site.

**Rung 11 — `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409) — the state gate.** The target row must currently be in a state from which the requested transition is declared. This rung also owns the parent-state guards that make a child operation legal: screening, examining and performing all require the owning site visit to be `IN_PROGRESS`; activating a treatment plan requires its encounter's `clinical_status` to be `EXAMINED`, `TREATED` or `CLOSED`; submitting a report requires the covered programme period to be `REPORTING`. It applies to every action endpoint — that is, every write addressed to an `/{id}/` sub-path — and to every create whose parent must be in a given state. Because it sits above the referral cap rung and the consent expiry rung, a state that is already terminal always pre-empts them: a send against an already-`ABANDONED` referral reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, not `ERR_REFERRAL_CAP_REACHED`, and a revoke or verify against an already-`EXPIRED` consent reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, not `ERR_CONSENT_EXPIRED`. This rung also owns every declared transition precondition that carries no error identifier of its own — the reschedule attempt cap and the reschedule window in §3.4, and the "no procedure still `PLANNED`" guard on completing a visit or closing an encounter — all of which report `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Five further preconditions belong here too, each of them a state that has no error identifier of its own: a cited consent still in `PENDING_VERIFICATION` on **screening and examining only** — planning a procedure and performing one deliberately never test for it, so an unverified consent blocks the chairside assessment while leaving the treatment rungs to the tier, revocation and expiry checks; a portable unit already `IN_SERVICE` on a visit start, checked immediately after the visit's own status and refused because no `IN_SERVICE → IN_SERVICE` leg is declared (a `QUARANTINED` unit is answered four rungs lower instead, with `ERR_UNIT_QUARANTINED`); a sterilization load whose `spore_result` is no longer `PENDING` on an indicator reading; a coverage already `VERIFIED` on a re-verification, the verify leg being declared only out of `UNVERIFIED` and `EXPIRED`; and a report already `SUBMITTED` on a regeneration. Conversely, four writes declare **no** state precondition at all and so never reach this rung on their target's own status: registering an encounter, planning a procedure, voiding a procedure and creating a treatment plan.

**Rung 12 — `ERR_PROCEDURE_TAINTED` (409).** The encounter carries no procedure currently in status `TAINTED` — that is, its `tainted_procedure_count` is zero. A tainted encounter must have its tainted procedures voided before it can be priced or claimed. This rung covers exactly two endpoints, `POST /mobile-dentistry/encounters/{id}/price` and `POST /mobile-dentistry/encounters/{id}/claim`.

**Rung 13 — `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412) — optimistic concurrency.** The supplied expected-version field equals the current `version` of the parent row named by the matching concurrency rule. This rung covers exactly three endpoints: `POST /mobile-dentistry/consents/{id}/amend-tier`, `POST /mobile-dentistry/encounters` and `POST /mobile-dentistry/procedures`. Full detail is in §2.9.

**Rung 14 — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (422).** The staff member reading a biological indicator, named by `result_recorded_by_staff_id` in the payload, must not be the staff member who ran that load, recorded as `loaded_by_staff_id` on the `SterilizationLoad`. This rung covers exactly one endpoint, `POST /mobile-dentistry/sterilization-loads/{id}/spore-result`.

**Rung 15 — `ERR_UNIT_QUARANTINED` (409), with a lazy incubation-timeout sweep in front of it.** This is a mutate-then-reject rung; §2.8 gives its full first-touch and second-touch behaviour. In summary: the sweep runs first, and if the unit's most recent sterilization load is still `PENDING` while the logical clock has reached that load's `incubation_ends_at`, the load is written to `FAILED`, the full spore-taint cascade runs, and the unit is quarantined — after which the request is refused. If the unit is quarantined at all, whether it was already quarantined or has just become so, the refusal is `ERR_UNIT_QUARANTINED`. This rung covers exactly two endpoints, `POST /mobile-dentistry/site-visits` and `POST /mobile-dentistry/site-visits/{id}/start`.

**Rung 16 — `ERR_UNIT_CALIBRATION_EXPIRED` (409).** The portable unit assigned to the visit has a `calibration_status` of `CURRENT` or `DUE`. A unit whose calibration has lapsed to `EXPIRED`, including a unit that has never been calibrated at all and therefore has a null `last_calibrated_on`, is refused. This rung covers exactly two endpoints, `POST /mobile-dentistry/site-visits` and `POST /mobile-dentistry/site-visits/{id}/start`.

**Rung 17 — `ERR_SPORE_RESULT_PENDING` (409), two disjoint clauses on one rung.** Clause (a), on `POST /mobile-dentistry/site-visits/{id}/start`: no sterilization load on the assigned portable unit may be `PENDING` while the logical clock is still before that load's `incubation_ends_at` — instruments whose indicator is still incubating may not be taken into service. Clause (b), on `POST /mobile-dentistry/sterilization-loads/{id}/spore-result`: *this* load's own `incubation_ends_at` must already have been reached by the logical clock, because a biological indicator read early is not a reading at all. Sibling loads on the same unit are irrelevant to clause (b); if they were not, a unit could never clear a backlog of loads. These two endpoints are the rung's entire scope.

**Rung 18 — `ERR_CREDENTIAL_LAPSED` (409).** Every staff member the request relies on has `credential_status` equal to `ACTIVE`. On visit scheduling and visit start that means the visit's `lead_staff_id` and, where assigned, its `onsite_dentist_staff_id` and `remote_dentist_staff_id`; on screening and examining it additionally means the clinician named by `performing_staff_id`; on performing a procedure it means the performing clinician. A `LAPSED` or `INACTIVE` credential on any of them refuses the whole request. This rung covers exactly five endpoints: `POST /mobile-dentistry/site-visits`, `POST /mobile-dentistry/site-visits/{id}/start`, `POST /mobile-dentistry/encounters/{id}/screen`, `POST /mobile-dentistry/encounters/{id}/exam` and `POST /mobile-dentistry/procedures/{id}/perform`.

**Rung 19 — `ERR_LICENSE_STATE_MISMATCH` (422).** Every staff member the request relies on — the same set enumerated for rung 18 — has a `license_state` equal to the `state_code` of the site the visit is at. A clinician licensed in one state cannot work a van parked in another. This rung covers the same five endpoints as rung 18.

**Rung 20 — `ERR_SUPERVISION_NOT_PERMITTED` (422).** The supervision matrix, specified in §3.5, permits this combination of procedure code, acting staff role, acting supervision level and the visit's dentist assignments. Screening is evaluated against code `D0190`, examining against `D0150`, and performing against the procedure's own `procedure_code`. This rung covers exactly three endpoints: `POST /mobile-dentistry/encounters/{id}/screen`, `POST /mobile-dentistry/encounters/{id}/exam` and `POST /mobile-dentistry/procedures/{id}/perform`.

**Rung 21 — `ERR_TELEDENTISTRY_REVIEW_WINDOW` (422).** When an exam declares an `exam_modality` of `TELEDENTISTRY`, `teledentistry_reviewed_at` must be present and must fall inside the closed interval that begins at the visit's `scheduled_start_at` and ends 72 hours later — both endpoints inclusive. An absent value, a value before the window opens, or a value after it closes all refuse the exam. This rung covers exactly one endpoint, `POST /mobile-dentistry/encounters/{id}/exam`.

**Rung 22 — `ERR_CONSENT_MISSING` (422).** The consent cited on an encounter registration must belong to the same patient as the encounter, and to the same site as the visit — its `patient_id` must equal the encounter's `patient_id`, and its `site_id` must equal the visit's `site_id`. This rung covers exactly one endpoint, `POST /mobile-dentistry/encounters`.

**Rung 23 — `ERR_CONSENT_REVOKED` (409).** The cited consent's `status` is neither `REVOKED` nor `SUPERSEDED`. This rung covers exactly five endpoints: `POST /mobile-dentistry/encounters`, `POST /mobile-dentistry/encounters/{id}/screen`, `POST /mobile-dentistry/encounters/{id}/exam`, `POST /mobile-dentistry/procedures` and `POST /mobile-dentistry/procedures/{id}/perform`.

**Rung 24 — `ERR_CONSENT_EXPIRED` (409), with a lazy expiry sweep in front of it.** This is a mutate-then-reject rung; §2.8 gives its full first-touch and second-touch behaviour. It covers the same five endpoints as rung 23. Note that revoking or verifying a consent targets the consent row itself rather than citing it, so on those two endpoints the state gate at rung 11 pre-empts this rung and surfaces `ERR_INVALID_STATE` instead.

**Rung 25 — `ERR_CAPACITY_DETERMINATION_REQUIRED` (422).** Verifying a consent whose `regime` is `SNF_RESPONSIBLE_PARTY` requires the patient's `capacity_determination` to be `HAS_CAPACITY` or `LACKS_CAPACITY`; `NOT_ASSESSED` is refused. A `SCHOOL_GUARDIAN` consent is never subject to this check. This rung covers exactly one endpoint, `POST /mobile-dentistry/consents/{id}/verify`.

**Rung 26 — `ERR_CONSENT_TIER_INSUFFICIENT` (422).** The rank of the procedure's `required_tier` must be less than or equal to the rank of the `scope_tier` on the consent the encounter currently cites. The tier ranking, from lowest authority to highest, is `SCREENING_ONLY`, then `SCREENING_FLUORIDE`, then `SCREENING_FLUORIDE_SEALANT`, then `FULL_WITH_RADIOGRAPHS`. Screening is evaluated against `D0190`'s required tier and examining against `D0150`'s, under exactly the same rule. This rung covers exactly four endpoints: `POST /mobile-dentistry/procedures`, `POST /mobile-dentistry/procedures/{id}/perform`, `POST /mobile-dentistry/encounters/{id}/screen` and `POST /mobile-dentistry/encounters/{id}/exam`.

**Rung 27 — `ERR_SURFACE_INELIGIBLE` (422).** A `D1351` sealant must name both a `tooth_number` and a `surface`; the tooth must be one of the eight permanent molars the programme seals, and the surface must be one of the three the programme seals; and no `PLANNED`-or-`PERFORMED` `D1351` may already occupy that same tooth-and-surface pair for the same patient. The eligible sets and the exact matching rule are given in §3.5. This rung covers exactly two endpoints, `POST /mobile-dentistry/procedures` and `POST /mobile-dentistry/procedures/{id}/perform`.

**Rung 28 — `ERR_VARNISH_INTERVAL` (422).** For a `D1206` fluoride varnish, the number of whole calendar days elapsed since the patient's `last_varnish_date` must be at least 90. Exactly 90 days elapsed passes; 89 fails. A patient with a null `last_varnish_date` passes automatically. This rung covers exactly one endpoint, `POST /mobile-dentistry/procedures/{id}/perform`.

**Rung 29 — `ERR_VARNISH_CAP_EXCEEDED` (422).** For a `D1206`, the patient's `varnish_applications_in_period` must be strictly less than 2 before the application is recorded. This rung covers exactly one endpoint, `POST /mobile-dentistry/procedures/{id}/perform`.

**Rung 30 — `ERR_UNIT_DOUBLE_BOOKED` (409).** The portable unit's schedule, widened by a transport buffer on both sides, must not collide with another live visit. The exact interval geometry is in §3.4. This rung covers exactly two endpoints, `POST /mobile-dentistry/site-visits` and `POST /mobile-dentistry/site-visits/{id}/reschedule`.

**Rung 31 — `ERR_STAFF_DOUBLE_BOOKED` (409).** No staff member assigned to the visit — through `lead_staff_id`, `onsite_dentist_staff_id` or `remote_dentist_staff_id` — may already be assigned to another live visit whose closed interval touches or overlaps this one. The exact geometry, which differs deliberately from the unit rule, is in §3.4. This rung covers the same two endpoints as rung 30.

**Rung 32 — `ERR_CHAIR_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (422).** The visit's `booked_chair_minutes` plus the chair minutes the new procedure would book must be less than or equal to the visit's `capacity_chair_minutes`. Equality passes. This rung covers exactly three endpoints: `POST /mobile-dentistry/procedures`, `POST /mobile-dentistry/encounters/{id}/screen` and `POST /mobile-dentistry/encounters/{id}/exam` — the last two because they auto-create a procedure that books chair time.

**Rung 33 — `ERR_REFERRAL_NOT_DUE` (422).** A send from status `NO_RESPONSE` requires the logical clock to have reached the referral's `next_eligible_send_at`; the instant itself qualifies. A send from status `DECLINED` has no waiting period at all and passes this rung unconditionally. This rung covers exactly one endpoint, `POST /mobile-dentistry/referrals/{id}/send`. Because it sits above the cap rung, an over-cap attempt made too early reports `ERR_REFERRAL_NOT_DUE` and does **not** abandon the referral.

**Rung 34 — `ERR_REFERRAL_CAP_REACHED` (409), mutate-then-reject.** Detailed in §2.8. Covers exactly one endpoint, `POST /mobile-dentistry/referrals/{id}/send`.

**Rung 35 — `ERR_COVERAGE_UNVERIFIED` (422), with a lazy coverage-expiry sweep in front of it.** Detailed in §2.8. The encounter must cite a coverage whose `verification_status` is `VERIFIED`; an encounter with a null `coverage_id` fails here too. Covers exactly one endpoint, `POST /mobile-dentistry/encounters/{id}/price`.

**Rung 36 — `ERR_SLIDING_FEE_MISSING` (422).** Verifying a coverage whose `payer_type` is `UNINSURED` requires the patient's `annual_household_income_cents` to be non-null, because the federal-poverty percentage and the sliding-fee band cannot otherwise be determined. This rung covers exactly one endpoint, `POST /mobile-dentistry/coverages/{id}/verify`.

**Rung 37 — `ERR_MEDICAID_BALANCE_BILL` (422).** A `manual_patient_responsibility_cents` override may not be supplied on an encounter whose coverage `payer_type` is `MEDICAID_MCO`; balance-billing a Medicaid member is prohibited outright rather than merely computed to zero. This rung covers exactly one endpoint, `POST /mobile-dentistry/encounters/{id}/price`.

**Rung 38 — `ERR_REPORT_STALE` (409).** A `PROGRAM_PERIOD` report whose `status` is `STALE` cannot be submitted; it must be regenerated first. This rung covers exactly one endpoint, `POST /mobile-dentistry/reports/{id}/submit`.

### 2.7. The Four Gates and Their Deliberate Exclusions

Four rungs act as *gates* rather than as field checks: they refuse a whole class of request because of the state of something other than the row being written. Because a gate that covered everything would make the rungs below it unreachable, each gate's scope is enumerated exactly — and for the programme freeze, so are the exclusions and the reasons for them.

**The programme-period freeze (rung 9).** When a `ProgramPeriod` reaches `SUBMITTED` or `CLOSED`, clinical and financial writes against everything that rolls up into that period stop. An entity resolves to its period by following the chain: a `SiteVisit` through its own `program_period_id`; an `Encounter` through its site visit's `program_period_id`; a `Procedure` through its encounter's site visit; a `TreatmentPlan` through its encounter's site visit; a `SterilizationLoad` through its `site_visit_id`, but only when that field is non-null — a load recorded without a visit reference resolves to no period and is therefore never gated.

The gated set is exactly these nineteen writes: `POST /mobile-dentistry/site-visits`, `POST /mobile-dentistry/site-visits/{id}/reschedule`, `POST /mobile-dentistry/site-visits/{id}/start`, `POST /mobile-dentistry/site-visits/{id}/complete`, `POST /mobile-dentistry/site-visits/{id}/cancel`, `POST /mobile-dentistry/encounters`, `POST /mobile-dentistry/encounters/{id}/screen`, `POST /mobile-dentistry/encounters/{id}/exam`, `POST /mobile-dentistry/encounters/{id}/close`, `POST /mobile-dentistry/encounters/{id}/price`, `POST /mobile-dentistry/encounters/{id}/claim`, `POST /mobile-dentistry/encounters/{id}/adjudicate`, `POST /mobile-dentistry/procedures`, `POST /mobile-dentistry/procedures/{id}/perform`, `POST /mobile-dentistry/procedures/{id}/void`, `POST /mobile-dentistry/treatment-plans`, `POST /mobile-dentistry/treatment-plans/{id}/activate`, `POST /mobile-dentistry/sterilization-loads` and `POST /mobile-dentistry/sterilization-loads/{id}/spore-result`.

Everything else is outside the gate, and the exclusions are as deliberate as the inclusions:

- **Every read endpoint.** Reads are never gated. A frozen period must stay fully inspectable, and the reporting workflow itself depends on reading it.
- **The clock control.** Driving the logical clock is neither a clinical nor a financial write, and gating it would make every time-dependent rung untestable once a period had frozen.
- **`POST /mobile-dentistry/program-periods` and `POST /mobile-dentistry/program-periods/{id}/status`.** These are the gate's own controls. Gating them would strand the period in `SUBMITTED` forever, make `CLOSED` unreachable, and make the freeze irreversible.
- **`POST /mobile-dentistry/reports`, `POST /mobile-dentistry/reports/{id}/generate` and `POST /mobile-dentistry/reports/{id}/submit`.** Reporting is what causes the freeze; it must keep working afterwards so that parent and facility-roster reports over frozen data remain obtainable.
- **`POST /mobile-dentistry/patients`, `POST /mobile-dentistry/consents`, `POST /mobile-dentistry/consents/{id}/verify`, `POST /mobile-dentistry/consents/{id}/revoke` and `POST /mobile-dentistry/consents/{id}/amend-tier`.** Roster and consent records span periods. A student's consent for next year must be recordable while last year is frozen, and a mid-programme revocation is a safety action that no reporting deadline may block.
- **`POST /mobile-dentistry/coverages` and `POST /mobile-dentistry/coverages/{id}/verify`.** Eligibility administration is period-independent; a coverage verified today is used by encounters in whichever period is currently open.
- **`POST /mobile-dentistry/referrals`, `POST /mobile-dentistry/referrals/{id}/send`, `POST /mobile-dentistry/referrals/{id}/record-response` and `POST /mobile-dentistry/treatment-plans/{id}/follow-up-contact`.** Referral and follow-up outreach is post-period case management rather than a clinical or financial write on an encounter. Keeping it outside the gate is precisely what keeps the referral waiting-period and attempt-cap rungs, and the follow-up cadence, reachable after a freeze.
- **`POST /mobile-dentistry/sites`, `POST /mobile-dentistry/site-holds` and `POST /mobile-dentistry/site-holds/{id}/release`.** Site safety operations — weather closures and outbreak lockdowns — must never be blocked by a reporting deadline.
- **`POST /mobile-dentistry/portable-units`, `POST /mobile-dentistry/portable-units/{id}/calibrate`, `POST /mobile-dentistry/staff-members` and `PATCH /mobile-dentistry/staff-members/{id}`.** Asset and credential administration is period-independent; calibrating a unit or recording a lapsed licence must stay possible while any period is frozen.

The freeze is reversible. A period that the state agency returns can be moved from `SUBMITTED` back to `REPORTING`, which clears `frozen_at` to null and disarms the gate; moving it on to `CLOSED` instead arms the gate permanently.

**The site-hold gate (rung 10).** An `ACTIVE` `SiteHold` suspends its site and blocks scheduling work against it. The gate covers exactly four endpoints: `POST /mobile-dentistry/site-visits`, `POST /mobile-dentistry/site-visits/{id}/reschedule`, `POST /mobile-dentistry/site-visits/{id}/start` and `POST /mobile-dentistry/encounters`. It deliberately does **not** cover completing or cancelling a visit — a team caught by a lockdown mid-visit must still be able to close the visit out — and it does not cover screening, examining, performing, pricing, claiming or adjudicating, which is what keeps every clinical and financial rung below it reachable even while a hold is open.

**The unit-quarantine gate (rung 15).** A `QUARANTINED` `PortableUnit` cannot be assigned to a new visit or taken into service. The gate covers exactly two endpoints, `POST /mobile-dentistry/site-visits` and `POST /mobile-dentistry/site-visits/{id}/start`. It does not cover recording a new sterilization load or reading a spore indicator on that unit — a quarantined unit must be able to run and pass a fresh load, which is the only way it ever returns to `AVAILABLE`.

**The Guardian confidentiality wall (rung 4).** Covered above; it is a gate over exactly one read, `GET /mobile-dentistry/reports/{id}`, and only for the `Guardian` role.

### 2.8. Lazy Sweeps and Mutate-then-Reject Semantics

Four rungs perform a bookkeeping write *before* refusing the request that triggered them. This is not a rollback situation and it is not an anomaly: the state of the world genuinely changed at the moment somebody looked, and the system records that change durably before reporting that the request cannot proceed. In all four cases the committed write survives the refusal, is visible to the next reader, and increments the swept row's `version`. In all four cases a **second** attempt behaves differently from the first, because the row is by then already in its terminal state and there is nothing left to sweep.

**Sweep 1 — the incubation-timeout sweep (rung 15).** Before the quarantine gate answers, the system inspects the portable unit's **most recent** sterilization load, selected by `cycle_completed_at` descending with insertion order as the tie-break. If that load's `spore_result` is still `PENDING` and the logical clock has reached its `incubation_ends_at`, the load is written to `FAILED` with `auto_failed` set to true, `result_recorded_at` stamped with the logical clock instant and `result_recorded_by_staff_id` left null — there is no operator to attribute it to. The full depth-five taint cascade described in §3.3 then runs exactly as though an operator had recorded the failure, and the unit moves to `QUARANTINED` with `quarantined_at` stamped. **Only then** is the triggering request refused with `ERR_UNIT_QUARANTINED`. First touch therefore commits a `SterilizationLoad` version bump, a `PortableUnit` version bump, and every version bump the cascade produces. On a **second** attempt against the same unit, the most recent load is no longer `PENDING`, so the sweep finds nothing, no row is written, `PortableUnit.version` and `SterilizationLoad.version` do not change — and the identical `ERR_UNIT_QUARANTINED` is returned. The two cases are distinguishable only by the versions and by the load's `spore_result`, never by the error identifier.

**Sweep 2 — the consent expiry sweep (rung 24).** When an endpoint cites a consent whose `status` is `PENDING_VERIFICATION` or `ACTIVE` and whose `expires_at` has been reached by the logical clock, the consent row is first written to `EXPIRED`, `expired_at` is stamped with the logical clock instant, and `Consent.version` is incremented by 1 — and the triggering request is **then** refused with `ERR_CONSENT_EXPIRED`. When the cited consent is **already** in status `EXPIRED`, the same `ERR_CONSENT_EXPIRED` is returned with no write whatsoever: `expired_at` keeps whatever value it already had and `Consent.version` does not change. Note the boundary: `expires_at` "has been reached" means the logical clock instant is at or after it, so an instant exactly equal to `expires_at` expires the consent.

**Sweep 3 — the referral attempt-cap sweep (rung 34).** When a send is requested against a referral in status `NO_RESPONSE` or `DECLINED` whose `attempt_count` has already reached 3, the referral is first written to `ABANDONED` with `abandoned_at` and `case_management_opened_at` both stamped with the logical clock instant, `abandon_reason` set to `ATTEMPT_CAP_REACHED`, and `attempt_count` left frozen at 3 rather than incremented; `Referral.version` increments by 1. The request is **then** refused with `ERR_REFERRAL_CAP_REACHED`. On a **second** send against that same referral, the row is now `ABANDONED`, which is not a state from which a send is declared, so the state gate at rung 11 pre-empts this rung entirely: the answer is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, nothing is written, and `Referral.version` does not change. Note also that rung 33 sits above this one, so a fourth attempt made from `NO_RESPONSE` before `next_eligible_send_at` returns `ERR_REFERRAL_NOT_DUE` and leaves the referral un-abandoned and its version untouched.

**Sweep 4 — the coverage expiry sweep (rung 35).** When an encounter is priced and the cited coverage's `verification_status` is `VERIFIED` but its `verified_through_date` is strictly earlier than the logical clock date, the coverage is first written to `EXPIRED` and `Coverage.version` is incremented by 1; the pricing request is **then** refused with `ERR_COVERAGE_UNVERIFIED`. If the coverage is already `UNVERIFIED` or already `EXPIRED` — or if the encounter has no `coverage_id` at all — the same `ERR_COVERAGE_UNVERIFIED` is returned with no write and no version change.

### 2.9. Optimistic Concurrency on Parent Rows

Three write endpoints take a decision that depends on a *parent* row the client has just read, and each requires the client to declare which version of that parent it looked at. The declared field is a required input, not an optional one; omitting it is a rung-6 `ERR_VALIDATION`, and supplying a value that does not equal the parent's current `version` is a rung-13 refusal with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` and HTTP status 412. No other endpoint in this system takes a version field of any kind.

- **Amending a consent's scope tier** must carry `expected_patient_version`, matching the current `version` of the `Patient` the consent belongs to. Amending mints a replacement consent that legitimately supersedes the prior one; a caller whose view of the patient is stale — because an earlier amendment has already superseded the consent it is holding, for instance — is stopped on the version check rather than being allowed to write a second superseding consent over the first. A mismatch yields `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412).
- **Registering an encounter** must carry `expected_site_visit_version`, matching the current `version` of the `SiteVisit`. Registration is decided against the visit's remaining chair capacity, which the caller reads from the visit first; if another registration or a reschedule has moved the capacity picture since that read, the create is refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412).
- **Planning a procedure** must carry `expected_encounter_version`, matching the current `version` of the `Encounter`. Planning is decided against the encounter's consent tier and the visit's remaining chair minutes, both read from the encounter; if the consent has been amended, a sibling procedure has been planned, or the encounter has been repriced since that read, the create is refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412).

### 2.10. Money, Precision and the Four Conservation Identities

All money in this system is carried as whole integer cents. Fee-schedule amounts, allowed amounts, patient shares, plan payments and write-offs are integers throughout, and no field ever holds a fractional cent. Where a rule multiplies a money amount by a rate, the product is rounded **half up to the nearest whole cent** — a value ending in exactly half a cent rounds away from zero. Where a rule sums several rounded products, the **rounding happens per line first and the sum is taken afterwards**; summing first and rounding once is a different number and is not what this system computes. The only non-integer quantity anywhere in the model is `fpl_percent`, which is carried to exactly one decimal place, likewise rounded half up.

Four quantities in this system are conserved. Each names a value that a forward action and its compensating action must leave in exact agreement with its defining formula, with zero permitted drift.

1. **The money split closes.** For every encounter whose `billing_status` is `PRICED`, `CLAIMED`, `PAID` or `DENIED`, its `total_charge_cents` equals the sum of `plan_paid_cents`, `patient_responsibility_cents` and `write_off_cents`. The identity holds under all three pricing regimes, holds when a manual patient-share override has been applied, and must be re-established exactly after every void-driven or taint-driven recompute. Pricing is the forward action; voiding a procedure is the compensating action, since it reverts the billing axis to `UNBILLED` and zeroes the four money fields, after which a re-price restores the identity.
2. **The chair-minute ledger balances.** For every site visit, `booked_chair_minutes` equals the sum of `chair_minutes` over every procedure belonging to that visit's encounters whose `status` is `PLANNED` or `PERFORMED`. This holds at all times. Planning a procedure and the auto-created screening and exam procedures debit the ledger; voiding a procedure, the consent-downgrade cascade that makes procedures `INELIGIBLE`, and the spore cascade that makes them `TAINTED` each credit back exactly the same `chair_minutes`. Because `TAINTED`, `VOIDED` and `INELIGIBLE` all leave the `PLANNED`-or-`PERFORMED` set, no special-casing is needed for any of them.
3. **The varnish counter is exact.** For every patient, `varnish_applications_in_period` equals the count of that patient's procedures with `procedure_code` `D1206` and `status` `PERFORMED` whose encounter's site visit carries the programme period in question. This holds at all times. Performing a `D1206` is the forward action; voiding it, or the spore cascade moving it to `TAINTED`, is the compensating action — each decrements the counter by exactly one and recomputes `last_varnish_date` from whatever `D1206` rows remain `PERFORMED`.
4. **Consent tier coverage is total.** For every encounter, the count of that encounter's procedures whose `status` is `PLANNED` or `PERFORMED` and whose `required_tier` outranks the `scope_tier` of the consent the encounter currently cites is zero — always, evaluated against whichever consent the encounter references at that moment. Amending a consent downward is the forward action that would otherwise break it; the same request's cascade, which moves every over-tier `PLANNED` procedure to `INELIGIBLE`, is the compensating action that returns the count to zero.

### 2.11. Cascade Bookkeeping: Which Rows Bump, and How Often

The rule from §2.4 — one increment per changed row per request, never two — needs an explicit reading for every operation that touches more than one row. The following ledger is exhaustive. Rows created by a request are minted at `version` 1 and are not separately "incremented" by the same request that made them, however many fields the request sets on them afterwards.

- **Moving the logical clock** writes no rows at all.
- **Creating a programme period, site, patient, portable unit, staff member, site visit, coverage, treatment plan, referral, sterilization load or report** mints exactly one row at `version` 1 and touches nothing else.
- **Changing a programme period's status** increments `ProgramPeriod.version` by 1 and touches nothing else. The status endpoint accepts only `REPORTING`, `OPEN` and `CLOSED` as a `to_status`; entry into `SUBMITTED` is reachable only through report submission.
- **Opening a site hold** mints the `SiteHold` at `version` 1 (with its `blocked_visit_count` already populated), increments `Site.version` by exactly 1 whether the site was `ACTIVE` or already `SUSPENDED`, increments `version` by 1 on each intersecting `SCHEDULED` site visit that moved to `HOLD_BLOCKED`, and increments `version` by 1 on each `REGISTERED`-or-`SCREENED` encounter on those visits that moved to `DEFERRED`.
- **Releasing a site hold** increments `SiteHold.version` by 1, increments `Site.version` by exactly 1 (its `active_hold_count` decreases by one on every release; only a release that takes the count to zero also returns `status` to `ACTIVE`), and increments `version` by 1 on each `HOLD_BLOCKED` visit that returned to `SCHEDULED`. Encounters already `DEFERRED` are deliberately not restored and are not touched.
- **Verifying a consent** increments `Consent.version` by 1 and touches nothing else.
- **Revoking a consent** increments `Consent.version` by 1 and increments `version` by 1 on each `PLANNED` procedure that moved to `INELIGIBLE`. The affected site visits' `booked_chair_minutes` are re-established without a `SiteVisit` version bump.
- **Amending a consent's tier** mints the replacement `Consent` at `version` 1, increments the superseded `Consent.version` by 1, and increments `Patient.version` by exactly 1. On a downgrade it additionally increments `version` by 1 on each over-tier `PLANNED` procedure that became `INELIGIBLE`, by 1 on each encounter that moved to `DEFERRED`, and by 1 on each `ACTIVE` treatment plan that moved to `CANCELLED`. Chair-minute ledgers are re-established without a `SiteVisit` version bump.
- **Calibrating a portable unit** increments `PortableUnit.version` by 1.
- **Recording a `PASSED` spore result** increments `SterilizationLoad.version` by 1 and `PortableUnit.version` by exactly 1 (the unit's `last_passed_load_at` always changes, and a `QUARANTINED` unit additionally returns to `AVAILABLE` with `quarantined_at` cleared).
- **Recording a `FAILED` spore result** increments `SterilizationLoad.version` by 1 on the failing load, `PortableUnit.version` by exactly 1, `version` by 1 on each procedure that moved from `PERFORMED` to `TAINTED`, `version` by exactly 1 on each encounter touched by the recompute (once, whether or not its billing axis reverted), `version` by 1 on each `ACTIVE` treatment plan that moved to `SUPERSEDED`, and `version` by 1 on each `OPEN`-or-`SENT` referral that moved to `ABANDONED`. Other `PENDING` loads swept into the tainted set are **not** themselves rewritten — only the failing load's own `spore_result` changes — and patient varnish ledgers and visit chair ledgers are re-established without version bumps on those rows.
- **Updating a staff member** increments `StaffMember.version` by 1 on every accepted `PATCH`, including one that omits every updatable field or supplies values identical to the ones already stored: the increment tracks the request, not the diff. Only the four fields named in §3.4 are applied; any other field present in the payload is ignored rather than refused, and an explicit null for `credential_expires_on` clears it.
- **Rescheduling a visit** increments `SiteVisit.version` by 1.
- **Starting a visit** increments `SiteVisit.version` by 1 and `PortableUnit.version` by 1. If the incubation-timeout sweep fires first, its writes commit as described in §2.8 and the start itself is refused, leaving `SiteVisit.version` unchanged.
- **Completing a visit** increments `SiteVisit.version` by 1, `PortableUnit.version` by 1 **only when the unit actually returns from `IN_SERVICE` to `AVAILABLE`** — a unit that a spore failure quarantined while the visit was under way stays `QUARANTINED` and takes no increment at all — and `version` by 1 on each encounter that moved to `CLOSED` or to `NO_SHOW`.
- **Cancelling a visit** increments `SiteVisit.version` by 1 and `version` by 1 on each `REGISTERED` encounter that moved to `NO_SHOW`.
- **Registering an encounter** mints the `Encounter` at `version` 1 and increments `SiteVisit.version` by exactly 1.
- **Screening** increments `Encounter.version` by 1 and mints the auto-created `D0190` `Procedure` at `version` 1. The visit's chair ledger moves without a `SiteVisit` version bump.
- **Examining** increments `Encounter.version` by 1 and mints the auto-created `D0150` `Procedure` at `version` 1, again without a `SiteVisit` version bump.
- **Closing, pricing, claiming and adjudicating an encounter** each increment `Encounter.version` by exactly 1. A pricing request stopped by the coverage sweep increments `Coverage.version` by 1 instead and leaves the encounter untouched.
- **Planning a procedure** mints the `Procedure` at `version` 1 and increments `Encounter.version` by exactly 1. The visit's chair ledger moves without a `SiteVisit` version bump.
- **Performing a procedure** increments `Procedure.version` by 1, and increments `Encounter.version` by exactly 1 **only when** the encounter's `clinical_status` actually moved from `EXAMINED` to `TREATED`; performing a second procedure on an already-`TREATED` encounter leaves the encounter's `version` alone. The patient's varnish ledger moves without a `Patient` version bump.
- **Voiding a procedure** increments `Procedure.version` by 1 and `Encounter.version` by exactly 1 — once, covering both the charge recompute and any reversion of the billing axis from `PRICED` to `UNBILLED`. Patient and visit ledgers move without version bumps.
- **Activating a plan** and **recording a follow-up contact** each increment `TreatmentPlan.version` by 1.
- **Sending a referral** increments `Referral.version` by 1, both on a successful send and on the cap-abandon path described in §2.8.
- **Recording a referral response** increments `Referral.version` by 1, and additionally increments `TreatmentPlan.version` by 1 when a `COMPLETED` outcome moves an `ACTIVE` plan to `COMPLETED`.
- **Verifying a coverage** increments `Coverage.version` by 1.
- **Generating a report** increments `Report.version` by 1. **Submitting a report** increments `Report.version` by 1, increments `ProgramPeriod.version` by exactly 1, increments `version` by 1 on each `SCHEDULED` site visit in the period that was cancelled, and increments `version` by 1 on each `REGISTERED` encounter on those visits that moved to `NO_SHOW`.

One further cascade rides on top of all of the above. **Whenever any of the nineteen period-gated writes listed in §2.7 succeeds, every report that is currently `GENERATED`, whose `report_type` is `PROGRAM_PERIOD`, and whose `program_period_id` is the period the written entity resolves to, moves to `STALE` with `stale_reason` set to `PERIOD_DATA_CHANGED` and its `version` incremented by 1.** Reports still in `DRAFT`, already in `STALE`, or already `SUBMITTED` are not touched, and neither are `PARENT` or `FACILITY_ROSTER` reports. A request that is refused at any rung never marks anything stale. The period consulted is always the one **the written entity itself** resolves to, even when the write's cascade has moved rows belonging to other periods — a spore failure that taints procedures across two programme years stales only the reports of the failing load's own period, and a load recorded with a null `site_visit_id` resolves to no period and stales nothing. Writes outside the nineteen stale nothing whatever they move: a consent revocation, a tier amendment and a report submission each rewrite procedures, encounters, plans or visits inside a period and still leave a `GENERATED` programme report `GENERATED`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Programme Year, the Sites and the Safety Holds

Everything the programme does happens inside a **programme period** — in practice a school year, labelled by a `name` such as `SY2025-26`, running from a `starts_on` calendar date to an `ends_on` calendar date. A `ProgramAdmin` opens the period, which begins life in status `OPEN`, with `frozen_at` and `state_submission_ref` both null. The period's `starts_on` must be strictly earlier than its `ends_on`, and both are plain calendar dates.

The period's status axis is a cycle rather than a line, and only four movements are declared. `OPEN → REPORTING` closes the year to new planning and opens it to reconciliation. `REPORTING → OPEN` is a revert leg that reopens the period for late data entry before anything has been sent to the state. `REPORTING → SUBMITTED` is not driven by an administrator at all — it happens only as a consequence of a programme report being submitted, and it stamps `frozen_at` with the logical clock instant and arms the write gate described in §2.7. `SUBMITTED → REPORTING` is the amendment leg, used when the state agency returns or rejects a submission; it clears `frozen_at` back to null and disarms the gate, and it is what makes the freeze reversible instead of fatal. It leaves `state_submission_ref` exactly as the submission wrote it — that reference is the durable record that a submission happened, is never cleared by any leg, and is simply overwritten by a later resubmission. `SUBMITTED → CLOSED` retires the year and keeps the gate armed permanently. `CLOSED` is terminal: nothing moves out of it. A `to_status` naming any state other than `REPORTING`, `OPEN` or `CLOSED` is refused as an enum violation, and an administrator asking for a movement that is not one of the declared legs — reopening a `CLOSED` period, for instance — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

A **site** is either a school or a skilled-nursing facility, and the distinction runs through the whole system. Registration supplies a `name`, a `site_type` of either `SCHOOL` or `SNF`, and a two-uppercase-letter `state_code` against which every clinician's licence will later be matched. Two optional fields shape the site's reporting relationship: `roster_disclosure_level`, defaulting to `SUMMARY_ONLY` and settable to `CLINICAL_DETAIL`, which decides how much per-child or per-resident detail a facility roster report may expose, and `facility_contact_name`, the named person facility reports are nominally addressed to — recorded for reference only, since no report payload carries it and no rule anywhere reads it. A new site opens in `status` `ACTIVE` with `active_hold_count` at zero; that count is a live projection over the site's `ACTIVE` holds, derived afresh on every read rather than stored.

Weather closures, outbreak lockdowns and administrative shutdowns are recorded as **site holds**. A hold names its `site_id`, a `hold_type` of `WEATHER_CLOSURE`, `OUTBREAK_LOCKDOWN` or `ADMINISTRATIVE`, an inclusive window opening at `starts_at` and closing exclusively at `ends_at` (which must be strictly after `starts_at`), and an optional free-text `reason`. It is created in `hold_status` `ACTIVE` with `released_at` null. Creating a hold sets off a three-link cascade. Link one takes the site from `ACTIVE` to `SUSPENDED` and raises its `active_hold_count` by one — and when a second hold is opened on a site that is already `SUSPENDED`, the site stays `SUSPENDED` while `active_hold_count` rises again, so the self-loop is a real transition and not a no-op. Link two, caused by link one, examines every site visit at that site whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` and whose scheduled window, treated as half-open from `scheduled_start_at` inclusive to `scheduled_end_at` exclusive, intersects the hold's own half-open window; every such visit moves to `HOLD_BLOCKED`, and the number of visits moved is recorded on the hold as `blocked_visit_count`. Link three, caused by link two, reaches into those blocked visits and moves every encounter whose `clinical_status` is `REGISTERED` or `SCREENED` to `DEFERRED`, stamping `deferred_reason` with the literal `SITE_HOLD`. Encounters that had already progressed to `EXAMINED`, `TREATED`, `CLOSED`, `DEFERRED` or `NO_SHOW` are left where they are.

Releasing a hold is deliberately **asymmetric** — it undoes less than the hold did. The hold moves to `hold_status` `RELEASED` with `released_at` stamped, and the site's `active_hold_count` drops by one. Only when that count reaches zero does the site return from `SUSPENDED` to `ACTIVE`. Every visit sitting in `HOLD_BLOCKED` for which no remaining `ACTIVE` hold on the site still intersects its window returns to `SCHEDULED`; a visit that another open hold still covers stays blocked. **Encounters that link three moved to `DEFERRED` are not restored.** A deferred encounter stays deferred, because the child or resident was genuinely not seen that day and the clinical record should say so; the release leg exists to free the calendar, not to rewrite history.

### 3.2. The Roster and the Two Consent Regimes

A **patient** is a student on a school roster or a resident on a facility roster. Adding one supplies the `site_id` they belong to, a `patient_type` of `STUDENT` or `RESIDENT`, an `external_ref` carrying the school's student number or the facility's resident number, `first_name`, `last_name` and `date_of_birth`. The `patient_type` must agree with the site: a `SCHOOL` site takes `STUDENT` patients and an `SNF` site takes `RESIDENT` patients, and a mismatch is a payload violation reported as `ERR_VALIDATION`. So is a duplicate `external_ref` within the same site — the pair of site and external reference is unique — while the same external reference at a different site is perfectly acceptable. Two optional labels are carried for readability and are meaningful for only one population each: `grade_level` for students and `room_number` for residents.

Three further optional fields on a patient exist for downstream rules rather than for the roster itself. `household_size`, defaulting to 1 and never less than 1, and `annual_household_income_cents`, defaulting to null and never negative when supplied, together drive the sliding-fee determination described in §3.6. `capacity_determination`, defaulting to `NOT_ASSESSED` and settable to `HAS_CAPACITY` or `LACKS_CAPACITY`, records whether a nursing-facility resident can make their own treatment decisions; whenever it is set to anything other than `NOT_ASSESSED`, `capacity_determined_at` is stamped with the logical clock instant. Each patient also carries two computed counters that the clinical workflow maintains: `varnish_applications_in_period` starts at zero, and `last_varnish_date` starts null.

**Consent is the pivot of the whole compliance story, and it carries two legally distinct regimes on one record.** A consent names the `patient_id` it authorises, the `site_id` (which must equal the patient's own site), the `program_period_id` it is scoped to, a `regime`, a `scope_tier` and the `signed_at` instant at which the form was actually signed. A `SCHOOL_GUARDIAN` consent additionally requires `guardian_name` and `guardian_relationship` and must not carry `responsible_party_name`. An `SNF_RESPONSIBLE_PARTY` consent additionally requires `responsible_party_name` and a `responsible_party_authority` of `DURABLE_POA`, `HEALTHCARE_PROXY`, `COURT_GUARDIAN` or `SELF`, must carry a `poa_document_ref` pointing at the filed power-of-attorney or proxy document whenever the authority is anything other than `SELF`, and must not carry `guardian_name`. The regime must also match the site type. Every one of those completeness failures is `ERR_CONSENT_REGIME_FIELDS`; a `site_id` that does not match the patient's site is `ERR_VALIDATION`. A new consent lands in `status` `PENDING_VERIFICATION`.

The **expiry arithmetic differs completely between the two regimes**, and this is computed at consent creation and recomputed at every verification. For a `SCHOOL_GUARDIAN` consent, `expires_at` is the last moment of the programme period: the period's `ends_on` date at 23:59:59 UTC, rendered as `expires_at` with three fractional digits and therefore exactly `<ends_on>T23:59:59.000Z` — and `reaffirmation_due_at` is null, because a school consent is not reaffirmed, it simply ends with the school year. This expiry is entirely independent of `signed_at`: a form signed on the final day of the period expires that same night. For an `SNF_RESPONSIBLE_PARTY` consent, `expires_at` is `signed_at` advanced by exactly 365 calendar days, preserving the time of day, and `reaffirmation_due_at` is set to the identical instant. This expiry is entirely independent of the programme period; the nursing-facility consent is a standing authorisation with an annual reaffirmation clock.

Verification is what puts a consent to work. A `SiteCoordinator` or `ProgramAdmin` moves a consent from `PENDING_VERIFICATION` to `ACTIVE`, which recomputes `expires_at` and `reaffirmation_due_at` under the regime rule above. Verification refuses a `PENDING_VERIFICATION` consent whose `expires_at` has already been reached by the logical clock — that is a declared precondition with no error identifier of its own, so it surfaces as `ERR_INVALID_STATE` — and refuses an `SNF_RESPONSIBLE_PARTY` consent whose patient is still `NOT_ASSESSED` with `ERR_CAPACITY_DETERMINATION_REQUIRED`. The expiry precondition is attached to that first leg alone: the reaffirmation self-loop below carries no expiry test whatever, so an `ACTIVE` nursing-facility consent whose `expires_at` has long since passed is reaffirmed successfully rather than refused, and the reaffirmation is precisely how such a consent is brought back inside its window. The same verification action is also the **annual reaffirmation self-loop**: a consent already in `ACTIVE` whose `regime` is `SNF_RESPONSIBLE_PARTY` can be verified again, which resets `signed_at` to the logical clock instant, slides `expires_at` forward another 365 days from that new signing instant, mirrors the result into `reaffirmation_due_at`, and leaves the consent in `ACTIVE`. The capacity requirement applies to the self-loop exactly as it does to the first verification. A `SCHOOL_GUARDIAN` consent sitting in `ACTIVE` has **no** re-verify leg at all: the `ACTIVE → ACTIVE` transition is declared only for the nursing-facility regime, so a school consent presented for reaffirmation is refused by the state gate with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

An `ACTIVE` consent has three ways out, and they are terminal. It can lapse into `EXPIRED` through the lazy sweep of §2.8, stamping `expired_at`. It can be **revoked** by a coordinator or administrator, which stamps `revoked_at` with the logical clock instant, records the supplied `revoke_reason` — an optional field, stored as null when it is omitted — and, this being the part that matters clinically, reaches into every encounter that cites this consent and moves every procedure still in `PLANNED` to `INELIGIBLE`, stamping each one's `void_reason` with the same literal the tier cascade uses, `CONSENT_TIER_DOWNGRADE`, and releasing each one's `chair_minutes` back to its site visit's `booked_chair_minutes`. Revocation runs that one link and no more: no encounter is deferred, no treatment plan is cancelled, and the revoked consent's own `invalidated_procedure_count` stays at zero — that counter is written only by an amendment. Work already `PERFORMED` is untouched; revocation stops future treatment, it does not erase past treatment. And it can be **superseded** by a tier amendment.

The `scope_tier` is an ordered permission ladder with four rungs, and a procedure is permitted only when the rank of its own `required_tier` is less than or equal to the rank of the consent's tier. From narrowest to broadest the tiers rank as `SCREENING_ONLY` at rank 1, `SCREENING_FLUORIDE` at rank 2, `SCREENING_FLUORIDE_SEALANT` at rank 3 and `FULL_WITH_RADIOGRAPHS` at rank 4. **Amending the tier does not edit the consent in place — it mints a replacement.** The amendment supplies a `new_scope_tier`, the `expected_patient_version` demanded by §2.9, and an optional `amend_reason`. A brand-new `Consent` row is created in `ACTIVE` at the new tier, copying the original's `patient_id`, `site_id`, `program_period_id`, `regime`, whichever signer fields that regime owns, and `signed_at`; its `expires_at` and `reaffirmation_due_at` are computed from those copied values by the same regime rule. The old row moves to `SUPERSEDED` and its `superseded_by_consent_id` is set to the replacement's `id`, which is also the value a caller can filter the consent list by in order to find the row a given consent replaced. The `Patient` row's `version` is incremented, which is what makes a second concurrent amendment fail its own version check. The replacement consent is what the caller receives back, with HTTP 201.

**On a downgrade — and only on a downgrade — the amendment sets off a three-link retro-invalidation cascade.** Link one is the minting of the replacement described above. Link two, caused by link one, walks every encounter that cites the superseded consent and moves every procedure still in `PLANNED` whose `required_tier` outranks the new tier into `INELIGIBLE`, stamping each one's `void_reason` with the literal `CONSENT_TIER_DOWNGRADE`; the number of procedures moved is recorded on the **old** consent row as `invalidated_procedure_count`. Procedures at or below the new tier are left `PLANNED`, and procedures already `PERFORMED` are never retro-invalidated by a tier change. Link three, caused by link two, credits each released `chair_minutes` back to the owning site visit's `booked_chair_minutes`; then, for any encounter that link two has stripped down to no remaining procedures beyond the automatically-created screening and exam rows and whose `clinical_status` is `EXAMINED` or `TREATED`, the encounter moves to `DEFERRED` with `deferred_reason` set to `CONSENT_TIER_DOWNGRADE`, and any treatment plan on that encounter still in `plan_status` `ACTIVE` moves to `CANCELLED` with `superseded_reason` set to `CONSENT_TIER_DOWNGRADE` and `follow_up_due_date` cleared to null.

Three legs of that cascade are conditional, and it matters which ones do not fire. **An amendment that raises the tier, or leaves it unchanged, runs link one alone**: no procedure is invalidated, `invalidated_procedure_count` stays at zero on the superseded row, no chair minutes move, no encounter is deferred and no plan is cancelled. **An encounter that still holds at least one procedure in `PLANNED` or `PERFORMED` beyond its screening and exam rows is not deferred**, even if some of its procedures were invalidated — the visit still produced treatment. And **an encounter whose `clinical_status` is `REGISTERED`, `SCREENED`, `CLOSED`, `DEFERRED` or `NO_SHOW` is not moved by link three at all**, because the deferral leg is declared only out of `EXAMINED` and out of `TREATED`; a plan on such an encounter is likewise left alone. A plan already in `DRAFT`, `COMPLETED`, `SUPERSEDED` or `CANCELLED` is never touched by this cascade either.

### 3.3. Portable Equipment, Sterilization and the Spore Cascade

A **portable unit** is the operatory itself — chair, compressor, suction and instrument trays — identified by a `unit_code` that must be unique across the whole programme; a duplicate code is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`. A newly registered unit sits in `status` `AVAILABLE` with `last_calibrated_on`, `last_passed_load_at` and `quarantined_at` all null. Its assignability axis is a cycle with three states: it becomes `IN_SERVICE` when a visit starts and returns to `AVAILABLE` when that visit completes; it can drop to `QUARANTINED` from either `AVAILABLE` or `IN_SERVICE` when a biological indicator fails; and it climbs back out of `QUARANTINED` to `AVAILABLE` only by a subsequent load on that same unit passing its indicator.

**Calibration** has a 180-day life. Recording a calibration supplies the `calibrated_on` date, which becomes the unit's `last_calibrated_on`; `calibration_due_on` is then that date advanced by 180 calendar days. From those two values, `calibration_status` is derived fresh on every read and on every rule that consults it: a unit that has never been calibrated reads a null `calibration_due_on` and is `EXPIRED`; a unit whose `calibration_due_on` is strictly earlier than the logical clock date is `EXPIRED`; a unit whose due date is still ahead but is no more than 30 days away — that is, the logical clock date has reached the due date minus 30 days — is `DUE`; anything earlier than that is `CURRENT`. `DUE` is a warning, not a block: both `CURRENT` and `DUE` units may be scheduled and taken into service, and only `EXPIRED` is refused, with `ERR_UNIT_CALIBRATION_EXPIRED`.

Instruments are processed in **sterilization loads**. Recording a load names the `portable_unit_id`, the `loaded_by_staff_id` of the staff member who ran the cycle, a `load_number` label, and the `cycle_completed_at` instant at which the sterilizer finished. An optional `site_visit_id` ties the load to the visit it was run for; when supplied it is also the path by which the load resolves to a programme period for the freeze gate, and when omitted the load resolves to no period and the gate cannot reach it. Every load starts with `spore_result` `PENDING`, `result_recorded_at` and `result_recorded_by_staff_id` null, `auto_failed` false and `tainted_procedure_count` zero. Its `incubation_ends_at` is computed at creation as `cycle_completed_at` advanced by exactly 24 hours — the biological indicator has to sit in the incubator overnight before it means anything.

Reading the indicator supplies a `result` of `PASSED` or `FAILED` together with the `result_recorded_by_staff_id` of the person reading it. Two guards apply before the reading is accepted. The reader may not be the loader: a `result_recorded_by_staff_id` equal to the load's `loaded_by_staff_id` is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, which is the one place in the clinical workflow where the same physical person genuinely cannot fill both chairs. And the incubation must have finished: a reading taken while the logical clock is still before *this* load's `incubation_ends_at` is refused with `ERR_SPORE_RESULT_PENDING`. Sibling loads on the same unit have no bearing on that second guard, which is what lets a unit work through a backlog of loads one at a time.

The two outcomes have **completely disjoint consequences**, which is what makes this fork the sharpest branch in the model. A `PASSED` reading stamps `result_recorded_at` with the logical clock instant and `result_recorded_by_staff_id` with the reader, sets the unit's `last_passed_load_at` to **this load's `cycle_completed_at`** — not to the reading instant — and, if the unit was sitting in `QUARANTINED`, returns it to `AVAILABLE` with `quarantined_at` cleared to null. Nothing else in the system moves.

A `FAILED` reading launches a **five-link cascade** whose reach is the whole point of the sterilization discipline. Link one writes `spore_result` `FAILED` and stamps `result_recorded_at`; when the failure came from the automatic sweep of §2.8 rather than from an operator, `auto_failed` is set true and `result_recorded_by_staff_id` stays null.

Link two, caused by link one, first works out **which loads are tainted**. Only loads on the failing unit are ever considered; loads on any other unit are out of scope no matter what their timing. A lower cutoff instant is established: if the unit has a non-null `last_passed_load_at`, the cutoff is the later of that instant and the failing load's `cycle_completed_at` minus 14 days; if the unit has never passed a load, the cutoff is simply the failing load's `cycle_completed_at` minus 14 days. The tainted set is then every load on that unit whose `cycle_completed_at` is **strictly after** the cutoff and **at or before** the failing load's own `cycle_completed_at`, and whose `spore_result` is anything other than `PASSED` — which admits the failing load itself and any still-`PENDING` siblings inside the window, while excluding every load that has already been cleared. Loads that tie on `cycle_completed_at` are processed in insertion order, and that order is observable because the resulting count is stamped onto the failing load. Having fixed the load set, link two moves **every procedure whose `sterilization_load_id` names one of those loads and whose `status` is exactly `PERFORMED`** to `TAINTED`, stamping `taint_reason` with the literal `SPORE_TEST_FAILED`, and records the number moved on the failing load as `tainted_procedure_count`. Procedures in `PLANNED`, `VOIDED`, `INELIGIBLE` or already-`TAINTED` are excluded from both the move and the count. Each tainted procedure releases its `chair_minutes` from its visit's `booked_chair_minutes`, and each tainted `D1206` decrements its patient's `varnish_applications_in_period` by one and forces `last_varnish_date` to be recomputed from whatever `D1206` rows for that patient are still `PERFORMED` — specifically to the **latest** `performed_at` calendar date among the survivors inside the same programme period, or to null when none survive.

Link three, caused by link two, touches every encounter that gained at least one newly `TAINTED` procedure. Its `tainted_procedure_count`, `subtotal_cents`, `supply_surcharge_cents` and `total_charge_cents` are all recomputed from the procedures that survive as `PERFORMED`. If — and only if — that encounter's `billing_status` was `PRICED`, it reverts to `UNBILLED` and its `allowed_amount_cents`, `patient_responsibility_cents`, `plan_paid_cents` and `write_off_cents` are all cleared to zero with `pricing_regime` cleared to null. An encounter that was still `UNBILLED` has nothing to revert; an encounter already `CLAIMED`, `PAID` or `DENIED` is **not** reverted by this link, because the claim has left the building and the correction belongs to the payer's process rather than to this one.

Link four, caused by link three, moves every treatment plan on such an encounter whose `plan_status` is `ACTIVE` to `SUPERSEDED`, setting `superseded_reason` to `SPORE_TEST_FAILED` and clearing `follow_up_due_date` to null. Plans in `DRAFT`, `COMPLETED`, `SUPERSEDED` or `CANCELLED` are left exactly as they are — a plan that was never activated has no cadence to stop, and a completed plan has already achieved its purpose.

Link five, caused by link four, moves every referral on a plan that link four just superseded whose `status` is `OPEN` or `SENT` to `ABANDONED`, stamping `abandoned_at` and `case_management_opened_at` with the logical clock instant and setting `abandon_reason` to `PLAN_SUPERSEDED`. A referral that was `SENT` keeps its `attempt_count` frozen at whatever it had reached. Referrals in `ACCEPTED`, `DECLINED`, `NO_RESPONSE`, `COMPLETED` or already-`ABANDONED` are not moved: a dental home that has already answered is not un-answered by a sterilizer failure, and the escalation this link opens is meant for cases still waiting in the outbound queue.

Finally, the unit itself moves to `QUARANTINED` with `quarantined_at` stamped, from whichever of `AVAILABLE` or `IN_SERVICE` it was in — and from `QUARANTINED` as well, because that write is unconditional rather than a guarded transition: when a further load on an already-quarantined unit fails, `quarantined_at` is simply re-stamped at the new failure instant and the unit takes another version increment.

### 3.4. Staffing, Scheduling and the Chair-Minute Ledger

A **staff member** is registered with a `full_name`, a `staff_role` of `DENTIST`, `HYGIENIST`, `PUBLIC_HEALTH_HYGIENIST` or `DENTAL_ASSISTANT`, a `license_number`, a two-letter `license_state`, and a `supervision_level` of `DIRECT`, `INDIRECT`, `GENERAL` or `SELF` describing the authorisation they personally hold. `credential_status` defaults to `ACTIVE` and can also be `LAPSED` or `INACTIVE`; `credential_expires_on` records the date the credential lapses and is informational. A `ProgramAdmin` can later revise any of `credential_status`, `supervision_level`, `license_state` and `credential_expires_on` on an existing record — that is the mechanism by which a licence is moved between states or a credential is marked lapsed mid-programme, and it stays available even while a period is frozen.

A **site visit** is a van, a crew and a window. Scheduling one names the `site_id`, the `program_period_id` the visit rolls up into, the `portable_unit_id` being taken, the `lead_staff_id` leading it, and a window from `scheduled_start_at` to a strictly later `scheduled_end_at`. Two optional assignments carry the supervision story: `onsite_dentist_staff_id` for a dentist physically present, and `remote_dentist_staff_id` for a teledentistry reviewer. `chair_count` defaults to 1 and is never less than 1. A new visit lands in `status` `SCHEDULED` with `reschedule_count` zero, `booked_chair_minutes` zero, and `started_at`, `completed_at` and `cancelled_at` all null.

The visit's **capacity** is computed at scheduling time and again on every reschedule. Take the scheduled duration in whole minutes — the difference between `scheduled_end_at` and `scheduled_start_at`, with any sub-minute remainder discarded before anything else happens — multiply it by `chair_count`, multiply that by a utilisation cap of 0.85, and take the floor to a whole number of minutes; the result is `capacity_chair_minutes`. Two chairs over a four-hour window therefore yield 2 times 240 times 0.85, floored, which is 408 chair minutes of usable capacity out of 480 nominal. The cap exists because a portable operatory never runs at nominal throughput.

Against that capacity sits `booked_chair_minutes`, a ledger recomputed in full — never incrementally patched — as the sum of `chair_minutes` over every procedure belonging to that visit's encounters whose `status` is `PLANNED` or `PERFORMED`. Procedures in `TAINTED`, `VOIDED` or `INELIGIBLE` contribute nothing; a tainted procedure genuinely consumed chair time on the day, but the booking ledger is a forward-looking capacity instrument and not a historical one. Booking a procedure whose `chair_minutes` would push `booked_chair_minutes` past `capacity_chair_minutes` is refused with `ERR_CHAIR_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`; landing exactly on the capacity is allowed.

Two resources are protected against double-booking, and **the two rules use deliberately different interval geometry**. Both consider only visits in `SCHEDULED`, `HOLD_BLOCKED` or `IN_PROGRESS`, excluding the visit being written; `COMPLETED` and `CANCELLED` visits never block anything.

- **The portable unit** rule widens both intervals by a 90-minute transport buffer and treats them as half-open. A collision exists when the new visit's `scheduled_start_at` is strictly earlier than the other visit's `scheduled_end_at` plus 90 minutes, **and** the other visit's `scheduled_start_at` is strictly earlier than the new visit's `scheduled_end_at` plus 90 minutes. Both comparisons are strict, so a new visit starting exactly 90 minutes after another ends on the same unit does **not** collide — the van has exactly enough time to drive. Anything less does collide, and is refused with `ERR_UNIT_DOUBLE_BOOKED`.
- **The staff** rule applies no buffer at all but closes both intervals. A collision exists when the new visit's `scheduled_start_at` is less than or equal to the other visit's `scheduled_end_at`, **and** the other visit's `scheduled_start_at` is less than or equal to the new visit's `scheduled_end_at`. Because the comparisons are non-strict, a new visit beginning at the exact instant another one ends **does** collide — a clinician cannot be in two places at the same moment even for an instant. The rule considers the union of `lead_staff_id`, `onsite_dentist_staff_id` and `remote_dentist_staff_id` on both visits, ignoring nulls, and fires when any staff member appears on both. The refusal is `ERR_STAFF_DOUBLE_BOOKED`.

A `SCHEDULED` visit can be **rescheduled**, which is a self-loop rather than a new visit. The request supplies a fresh `scheduled_start_at` and `scheduled_end_at`; the old window is replaced, `capacity_chair_minutes` is recomputed against the new duration, and `reschedule_count` rises by one. Two constraints bound it: `reschedule_count` must be strictly below 2 before the move, so a visit may be rescheduled at most twice, and the new `scheduled_start_at` must lie within 30 days of the visit's current `scheduled_start_at` — an **absolute** distance, so a move 30 days earlier is bounded exactly as a move 30 days later is, and exactly 30 days in either direction passes while 30 days and one millisecond does not. Both refusals are `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Both overlap rules are re-run against the new window exactly as they were at scheduling. Nothing else is re-checked: the credential, licence, calibration and quarantine gates are all outside this endpoint's scope, and the freshly recomputed `capacity_chair_minutes` is written even when a shorter new window leaves it below the chair minutes the visit has already booked — a reschedule can put a visit over its own capacity, and no rung objects.

**Starting a visit** is where the largest cluster of safety gates bites. The visit must be `SCHEDULED`; its site must be `ACTIVE`; the assigned unit must be `AVAILABLE`, with a `calibration_status` of `CURRENT` or `DUE`, and with no load still `PENDING` inside its incubation window; and the lead clinician must hold an `ACTIVE` credential whose `license_state` matches the site's `state_code`. Those refusals are not interchangeable, and their order is the ladder's: the visit's own status is tested first and a visit that is not `SCHEDULED` is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; the unit's assignability is tested immediately after on the same rung, so a unit already `IN_SERVICE` is likewise `ERR_INVALID_STATE` rather than a quarantine or calibration refusal; only then do the gates below answer, a `QUARANTINED` unit with `ERR_UNIT_QUARANTINED` and a lapsed calibration with `ERR_UNIT_CALIBRATION_EXPIRED`. When all of that holds, `started_at` is stamped with the logical clock instant, the visit moves `SCHEDULED → IN_PROGRESS`, and the portable unit moves `AVAILABLE → IN_SERVICE`.

**Completing a visit** requires it to be `IN_PROGRESS` and requires that no procedure anywhere on the visit is still in `PLANNED` — every planned item must have been performed, voided or made ineligible first. Completion stamps `completed_at`, returns the unit from `IN_SERVICE` to `AVAILABLE` — and out of `IN_SERVICE` only, so a unit that a spore failure quarantined while the visit was under way is left `QUARANTINED`, untouched and un-incremented — moves every encounter on the visit whose `clinical_status` is `EXAMINED` or `TREATED` to `CLOSED`, and moves every encounter still in `REGISTERED` to `NO_SHOW`. Encounters already `DEFERRED`, `CLOSED` or `NO_SHOW` are untouched.

**Cancelling a visit** is permitted from `SCHEDULED` and from `HOLD_BLOCKED`, and takes an optional `cancel_reason` recorded for audit but not surfaced on the visit resource. Cancellation stamps `cancelled_at`. When it is cancelled from `SCHEDULED`, every encounter still in `REGISTERED` moves to `NO_SHOW`; the `HOLD_BLOCKED → CANCELLED` leg carries no such cascade, because a blocked visit's registered encounters were already moved to `DEFERRED` when the hold was opened. Both `COMPLETED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal.

### 3.5. The Chairside Encounter: Screening, Exam, Procedures

An **encounter** is one patient at one visit, and it runs on **two independent status axes at once**. `clinical_status` tracks what happened in the chair; `billing_status` tracks what happened to the money. Neither constrains the other: an encounter can be `CLOSED` and still `UNBILLED`, or `TREATED` and already `PAID`. Registration names the `site_visit_id`, the `patient_id`, the `consent_id` authorising the work, and the `expected_site_visit_version` demanded by §2.9; an optional `coverage_id` names the coverage the encounter will eventually be priced against, and may be left null and supplied later only by re-registering, since the encounter itself is not editable. A new encounter lands with `clinical_status` `REGISTERED` and `billing_status` `UNBILLED`, `triage_band` null, every screening finding null, every money field zero and `pricing_regime` null. The cited consent must belong to the same patient as the encounter and to the same site as the visit, or the registration is refused with `ERR_CONSENT_MISSING`. Registration carries **no visit-state precondition** — the visit's `status` is never consulted, so a patient may be registered against a visit that is still `SCHEDULED`, already `IN_PROGRESS`, or even `HOLD_BLOCKED`, `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED`; the only things standing between the caller and a new row are the freeze gate, the site-hold gate, the version check and the three consent rungs.

**Screening** moves the encounter `REGISTERED → SCREENED` and requires the visit to be `IN_PROGRESS` and the cited consent to be `ACTIVE` and not yet past its `expires_at`. The request names the `performing_staff_id` of the clinician doing the screen and carries the seven findings. Four of them are always recorded: `cavitated_lesion_count`, the number of cavitated carious lesions observed; `pain_reported`, whether the patient reported dental pain; `abscess_or_swelling`, whether an abscess or facial swelling is present; and `untreated_decay_present`, whether any untreated decay is present at all. Three more are optional because only the nursing-facility variant scores them: `denture_problem` for an ill-fitting or missing denture, `oral_hygiene_score` on a scale where 0 is good and 4 is very poor, and `medical_complexity_count`, the number of complicating medical conditions.

**The triage band is computed from those findings by two completely different rule sets, chosen by the site's `site_type`, and in each set the first matching branch wins.**

At a `SCHOOL` site: if `abscess_or_swelling` is true, the band is `URGENT`. Otherwise, if `pain_reported` is true **and** `cavitated_lesion_count` is at least 1, the band is `URGENT`. Otherwise, if `cavitated_lesion_count` is at least 1 **or** `untreated_decay_present` is true, the band is `EARLY_CARE_NEEDED`. Otherwise the band is `NO_OBVIOUS_PROBLEM`. Pain on its own never reaches `URGENT` in a school — a child who reports discomfort but shows no cavitated lesion is triaged as early care at most — and `denture_problem`, `oral_hygiene_score` and `medical_complexity_count` are ignored entirely, whatever values are supplied.

At an `SNF` site: if `abscess_or_swelling` is true **or** `pain_reported` is true, the band is `URGENT` — pain alone is enough, because a resident who can report pain at all is reporting something that has usually gone unaddressed. Otherwise, if `cavitated_lesion_count` is at least 1 **and** `medical_complexity_count` is at least 3, the band is `URGENT`; medical complexity escalates even a single lesion. Otherwise, if `cavitated_lesion_count` is at least 1, **or** `denture_problem` is true, **or** `oral_hygiene_score` is at least 3, the band is `EARLY_CARE_NEEDED` — either of the last two can raise a mouth with no lesions at all out of the lowest band. Otherwise the band is `NO_OBVIOUS_PROBLEM`.

Screening also **auto-creates a procedure**. A `Procedure` row is written for code `D0190` directly in `status` `PERFORMED`, with `performed_at` stamped at the logical clock instant, `performing_staff_id` set to the screening clinician, and `sterilization_load_id` set to the **most recent sterilization load on the visit's portable unit** — chosen by `cycle_completed_at` descending, with the most recently inserted row winning a tie, and **regardless of that load's `spore_result`**, so a `PENDING` or even a `FAILED` load is still what gets recorded. When the unit has no load at all, `sterilization_load_id` is null. This is deliberate: the instruments actually on the tray came from the latest cycle, and it is precisely that linkage which lets a later `FAILED` indicator reach back and taint the work. The new procedure's `chair_minutes` are added to the visit's `booked_chair_minutes`, which is why the chair-capacity rung applies to screening as well as to explicit procedure planning.

**Examining** moves the encounter `SCREENED → EXAMINED`, again requiring an `IN_PROGRESS` visit and an `ACTIVE` consent. The request names a `performing_staff_id` and an `exam_modality` of `ONSITE` or `TELEDENTISTRY`. A teledentistry exam must also carry `teledentistry_reviewed_at`, the instant the remote dentist actually reviewed the case, and that instant must fall inside the closed window that opens at the visit's `scheduled_start_at` and closes 72 hours later; an absent or out-of-window value is refused with `ERR_TELEDENTISTRY_REVIEW_WINDOW`. An `ONSITE` exam never consults the field at all: a `teledentistry_reviewed_at` supplied alongside `ONSITE` is neither window-checked nor written, and the encounter's own `teledentistry_reviewed_at` stays null. Examining auto-creates a `D0150` procedure on exactly the same terms as screening's `D0190` — `PERFORMED`, stamped at the clock, attributed to the examining clinician, carrying the same latest-load reference, and booking its own chair minutes.

An encounter reaches `CLOSED` from `EXAMINED` or from `TREATED`, either by an explicit close or as part of a visit completing, and in both cases only once no procedure on the encounter is still `PLANNED`. `CLOSED`, `DEFERRED` and `NO_SHOW` are the three terminal clinical states.

**Procedures** are the billable units of work. Five codes are recognised, and each one fixes three attributes from the code alone at the moment the row is created — nothing else participates in that lookup, and none of the three is ever recomputed afterwards:

| Code | Required consent tier | Chair minutes | Charge |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `D0190` screening | `SCREENING_ONLY` | 10 | 3475 cents |
| `D0150` exam | `SCREENING_ONLY` | 15 | 5825 cents |
| `D1206` fluoride varnish | `SCREENING_FLUORIDE` | 8 | 4525 cents |
| `D1351` sealant | `SCREENING_FLUORIDE_SEALANT` | 12 | 6350 cents |
| `D0272` bitewing radiographs | `FULL_WITH_RADIOGRAPHS` | 10 | 4150 cents |

Those values populate `required_tier`, `chair_minutes` and `charge_cents`. Planning a procedure names the `encounter_id`, the `procedure_code`, the `expected_encounter_version` demanded by §2.9, and — for a sealant — a `tooth_number` and a `surface`. An optional `treatment_plan_id` links the procedure back to the plan it realises. A planned procedure lands in `status` `PLANNED` with `performed_at`, `performing_staff_id`, `sterilization_load_id`, `taint_reason` and `void_reason` all null, and immediately books its `chair_minutes` against the visit ledger. Planning consults neither the encounter's `clinical_status` nor the visit's `status`: unlike screening, examining and performing it declares no parent-state precondition, so a procedure can legitimately be planned onto a `CLOSED` encounter belonging to a `COMPLETED` visit. The optional `treatment_plan_id` is checked for existence and nothing further — no rule requires the named plan to belong to this encounter, to this patient, or to be in any particular `plan_status`. A `tooth_number` or `surface` supplied for a code other than `D1351` is range- and enum-checked like any other field and then stored on the row unchanged, where no rule ever reads it.

**The supervision matrix** decides who may put their hands on a patient, and it is genuinely three-dimensional: the procedure code, the acting clinician's own `staff_role` and `supervision_level`, and which dentists the visit has assigned. The matrix names the supervision level each code *requires* of each role:

| Code | `DENTIST` | `HYGIENIST` | `PUBLIC_HEALTH_HYGIENIST` | `DENTAL_ASSISTANT` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `D0190` | `SELF` | `GENERAL` | `GENERAL` | not permitted |
| `D0150` | `SELF` | not permitted | `GENERAL` | not permitted |
| `D1206` | `SELF` | `INDIRECT` | `GENERAL` | `DIRECT` |
| `D1351` | `SELF` | `INDIRECT` | `GENERAL` | not permitted |
| `D0272` | `SELF` | `DIRECT` | `DIRECT` | not permitted |

Evaluation proceeds in three steps. First, if the matrix marks the combination not permitted, the answer is no. Second, the clinician's own authorisation is ranked against the requirement, where `DIRECT` ranks 1, `INDIRECT` ranks 2, `GENERAL` ranks 3 and `SELF` ranks 4; if the clinician's `supervision_level` ranks strictly below the required level, the answer is no. Third — and this is the step that makes the rule non-linear — a **presence** requirement is applied, keyed on the level the *matrix required*, never on the level the clinician personally holds. When the required level is `DIRECT` or `INDIRECT`, the visit's `onsite_dentist_staff_id` must be non-null and must name a staff member whose `staff_role` is `DENTIST` and whose `credential_status` is `ACTIVE`. When the required level is `GENERAL`, either the visit's `onsite_dentist_staff_id` or its `remote_dentist_staff_id` may satisfy it, on the same terms — an active dentist, present onsite or reviewing remotely. When the required level is `SELF`, no presence is required at all. The consequence is that a clinician can clear the rank test and still fail on presence, and that a more highly authorised clinician can be permitted for a code where a less authorised one is not. A failure at any of the three steps is `ERR_SUPERVISION_NOT_PERMITTED`. Screening is always evaluated as `D0190`, examining always as `D0150`, and performing always as the procedure's own code.

**Sealants** are constrained to specific teeth and specific surfaces, and to one live sealant per site. A `D1351` must carry both a `tooth_number` and a `surface`; the tooth must be one of the eight permanent first and second molars the programme seals — 2, 3, 14, 15, 18, 19, 30 and 31 in universal numbering — and the surface must be `O`, `B` or `L`. The surface field itself also admits `M` and `D` as legal enum values, but a sealant naming either of them is ineligible rather than malformed. Beyond the sets, the system looks across **all** of that patient's `D1351` procedures, in every encounter and every programme period, and refuses the new one if any other `D1351` for the same patient already names the identical `tooth_number` and `surface` pair while sitting in `PLANNED` or `PERFORMED`. Sealants that were voided, made ineligible or tainted do not block; the empty blocking set is the eligible case. Every one of these failures is `ERR_SURFACE_INELIGIBLE`.

**Fluoride varnish** is rate-limited in two independent ways, and both are checked when the varnish is actually performed rather than when it is planned. The interval rule requires at least 90 whole calendar days to have elapsed between the patient's `last_varnish_date` and the logical clock date; exactly 90 days passes and 89 does not, and a patient with a null `last_varnish_date` passes unconditionally. The per-period cap requires the patient's `varnish_applications_in_period` to be strictly less than 2 before the application is recorded, so two applications per programme period is the ceiling. Both counters look only at `PERFORMED` `D1206` rows for that patient whose encounter's site visit carries the same `program_period_id` as the visit being worked; rows that are `PLANNED`, `VOIDED`, `TAINTED` or `INELIGIBLE` count for neither the date nor the tally. The refusals are `ERR_VARNISH_INTERVAL` and `ERR_VARNISH_CAP_EXCEEDED`, in that order of precedence.

**Performing** a procedure requires it to be `PLANNED` and its visit to be `IN_PROGRESS`. The request names the `performing_staff_id` doing the work and the `sterilization_load_id` of the instrument load actually used, both of which are written onto the procedure along with `performed_at` at the logical clock instant, and the procedure moves `PLANNED → PERFORMED`. Both identifiers are checked only for existence: the named load is never required to sit on the visit's assigned portable unit, nor to be `PENDING`, nor to have passed its indicator. Performing also leaves the owning encounter's money fields entirely alone — `subtotal_cents`, `supply_surcharge_cents` and `total_charge_cents` stay at the zero they were created with on an encounter that has never been priced, because those three are written only by the pricing pass of §3.6 and by the void-driven and taint-driven recomputes. A `D1206` additionally raises the patient's `varnish_applications_in_period` by one and sets `last_varnish_date` to the logical clock date. If the owning encounter's `clinical_status` is `EXAMINED`, it advances to `TREATED`; an encounter already `TREATED` stays where it is.

**Voiding** is the universal compensating action, and it is available from three different states: `PLANNED`, `PERFORMED` and `TAINTED`. It is restricted to a `FieldDentist` and, like planning, it consults neither the encounter's `clinical_status` nor the visit's `status`. The `void_reason` is optional: it is recorded on the procedure exactly as supplied, or written as null when it is omitted. The procedure moves to `VOIDED`, and any `taint_reason` a previous spore failure stamped on it is deliberately left standing rather than cleared, so a voided-after-taint row carries both reasons at once. Because both the visit's chair ledger and the patient's varnish counters are re-established by full recomputation over the surviving rows rather than by incremental adjustment, voiding is idempotent with respect to those ledgers no matter which state the procedure came from: voiding a `PLANNED` procedure releases its chair minutes, voiding a `PERFORMED` one releases its chair minutes and, for a `D1206`, drops the varnish count by one and recomputes `last_varnish_date` from the remaining `PERFORMED` varnishes, and voiding a `TAINTED` one changes neither ledger because the taint cascade already removed it from both. In every case the owning encounter's `subtotal_cents`, `supply_surcharge_cents` and `total_charge_cents` are recomputed from the procedures that remain `PERFORMED`, and if the encounter's `billing_status` was `PRICED` it reverts to `UNBILLED` with its four money fields zeroed and `pricing_regime` cleared to null. `INELIGIBLE` and `VOIDED` are terminal states for a procedure; `TAINTED` is not, since a tainted procedure can still be voided, which is exactly what the tainted-encounter rung forces a billing team to do before pricing can proceed.

### 3.6. Eligibility, Sliding Fees and the Three Pricing Regimes

A **coverage** records how one patient's care is paid for. It names the `patient_id`, a `payer_type` of `MEDICAID_MCO`, `COMMERCIAL_FFS` or `UNINSURED`, an optional `payer_name` giving the plan or managed-care organisation (conventionally null for an uninsured patient, though nothing enforces its absence — a `payer_name` supplied on an `UNINSURED` coverage is accepted and stored), and an optional `member_id` carrying the subscriber identifier — required whenever `payer_type` is anything other than `UNINSURED`, and its absence in that case is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`. Coverage is created in `verification_status` `UNVERIFIED` with `verified_at`, `verified_through_date`, `fpl_percent`, `sliding_fee_band` and `determination_date` all null.

Verification is the `BillingSpecialist`'s job and takes a single field, `verified_through_date`, the last calendar date the verification is good for. That date must not be earlier than the logical clock date; a verification asserted through a date that has already passed is out of range and refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`. Verification stamps `verified_at` with the logical clock instant, records the supplied `verified_through_date` and moves the coverage to `VERIFIED`. The axis then cycles: a `VERIFIED` coverage whose `verified_through_date` has fallen behind the clock is swept to `EXPIRED` by the lazy sweep of §2.8, and an `EXPIRED` coverage can be verified again on exactly the same terms, returning it to `VERIFIED` with a fresh `verified_through_date` and, for an uninsured patient, a freshly recomputed determination. The verify leg is declared only out of `UNVERIFIED` and out of `EXPIRED`: a coverage that is currently `VERIFIED` cannot be re-verified to push its date out, and the attempt is refused by the state gate with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

**The sliding-fee determination runs only for `UNINSURED` coverage, and only at verification.** The federal poverty threshold for the household is 15,600 dollars — 1,560,000 cents — for a household of one, plus 5,500 dollars, or 550,000 cents, for each additional person; that is, the base amount plus the per-person increment multiplied by one less than the patient's `household_size`. The patient's `annual_household_income_cents` is divided by that threshold, multiplied by 100, and rounded half up to exactly one decimal place to give `fpl_percent`. The band follows, and **every boundary is inclusive of its upper limit**: an `fpl_percent` at or below 100.0 is band `A`; above 100.0 and at or below 138.0 is band `B`; above 138.0 and at or below 175.0 is band `C`; above 175.0 and at or below 200.0 is band `D`; anything above 200.0 is band `E`. A percentage of exactly 100.0 is therefore band `A`, exactly 138.0 is band `B`, exactly 175.0 is band `C` and exactly 200.0 is band `D`. `determination_date` is set to the logical clock date. The household inputs are frozen at the instant of verification: editing the patient's income or household size afterwards does not retroactively re-band a coverage that is already verified — only a re-verification does that. A verification attempted for an uninsured patient with a null `annual_household_income_cents` is refused with `ERR_SLIDING_FEE_MISSING`.

**Pricing an encounter runs six calculations in one pinned pass**, and the order is part of the specification because two of the steps depend on the pre-surcharge figure rather than the post-surcharge one. Pricing requires the encounter's `clinical_status` to be `EXAMINED`, `TREATED` or `CLOSED`, its `billing_status` to be `UNBILLED`, its `tainted_procedure_count` to be zero, and its cited coverage to be `VERIFIED`.

**Step one — the subtotal.** `subtotal_cents` is the sum of `charge_cents` over every procedure of this encounter whose `status` is exactly `PERFORMED`. Procedures in `PLANNED`, `TAINTED`, `VOIDED` and `INELIGIBLE` contribute nothing. No rounding occurs.

**Step two — the supply surcharge.** A flat per-encounter surcharge is selected by a tier keyed **on `subtotal_cents`**, the pre-surcharge figure, and computed exactly once; it is never re-evaluated against `total_charge_cents` even though `total_charge_cents` contains it. A subtotal of exactly zero yields a `supply_surcharge_cents` of zero. A subtotal above zero and at or below 5,000 cents yields 500 cents. A subtotal above 5,000 and at or below 15,000 cents yields 1,200 cents. Anything above 15,000 cents yields 2,000 cents. Both tier bounds are inclusive of their upper limit. `total_charge_cents` is then `subtotal_cents` plus `supply_surcharge_cents`.

**Step three — the regime fork.** The coverage's `payer_type` selects the pricing regime, which is pinned onto the encounter as `pricing_regime` and remains the record of which branch produced the money: `MEDICAID_MCO` selects `MEDICAID_ENCOUNTER_RATE`, `COMMERCIAL_FFS` selects `COMMERCIAL_FEE_SCHEDULE`, and `UNINSURED` selects `UNINSURED_SLIDING_FEE`.

**Step four — the allowed amount, under the Medicaid and commercial branches.** Under `MEDICAID_ENCOUNTER_RATE`, an encounter with no `PERFORMED` procedures at all has an `allowed_amount_cents` of zero; otherwise the allowed amount is the lesser of a single all-inclusive encounter rate of 18,500 cents and the encounter's own `total_charge_cents`. The procedure mix is invisible to Medicaid: an encounter of a screening and a varnish totalling 9,200 cents is allowed all 9,200, while an encounter whose total charge exceeds 18,500 cents is allowed exactly 18,500 and no more. Under `COMMERCIAL_FEE_SCHEDULE`, the allowed amount is built line by line: for each `PERFORMED` procedure, take the lesser of its own `charge_cents` and the contracted schedule amount for its code — 2,600 cents for `D0190`, 4,300 for `D0150`, 3,375 for `D1206`, 4,725 for `D1351` and 3,100 for `D0272` — and sum those per-line minima. **The supply surcharge is never allowable under the commercial schedule**, even though it sits inside `total_charge_cents` and is therefore inside the Medicaid cap. The same 9,200-cent encounter of a screening and a varnish is allowed 5,975 under the commercial branch rather than 9,200.

**Step five — the patient's share.** An encounter with no `PERFORMED` procedures has a `patient_responsibility_cents` of zero under every regime. Under `MEDICAID_ENCOUNTER_RATE` the patient's share is zero, flatly, because balance-billing a Medicaid member is prohibited. Under `COMMERCIAL_FEE_SCHEDULE` the share is 20 percent of the allowed amount, rounded half up to a whole cent — a single multiplication against the allowed amount, in which the patient's household size and income never participate and no floor ever applies. Under `UNINSURED_SLIDING_FEE` the arithmetic is completely different: the discount fraction is taken from the coverage's `sliding_fee_band` — band `A` discounts 100 percent, band `B` 75 percent, band `C` 55 percent, band `D` 35 percent and band `E` nothing at all — and is applied **per line, with rounding before summation**. Take the encounter's `PERFORMED` procedures in `created_at` ascending order, multiply each one's `charge_cents` by one minus the band's discount fraction, round each product half up to a whole cent, and only then sum the rounded per-line amounts. Summing first and rounding once produces a different number and is not what this system computes. For every band except `E`, the result is then raised to a nominal minimum fee of 2,500 cents if it falls below it — the greater of the discounted total and 2,500 — while band `E`, which discounts nothing, takes the discounted total as it stands with no floor. The floor genuinely *raises* the share for the poorest households: a band `A` patient's discounted total is zero and their responsibility is 2,500 cents.

**Step six — the uninsured allowed amount, and the split.** Under `UNINSURED_SLIDING_FEE` the encounter is self-pay, so the allowed amount **is** the patient's share: `allowed_amount_cents` is set equal to `patient_responsibility_cents` after step five has run. Finally, `plan_paid_cents` is `allowed_amount_cents` minus `patient_responsibility_cents`, and `write_off_cents` is `total_charge_cents` minus `allowed_amount_cents`. The conservation identity of §2.10 closes exactly under all three regimes, and closes again after every void-driven or taint-driven recompute.

**A manual override** is available on the pricing request through `manual_patient_responsibility_cents`, which must lie between zero and the encounter's `total_charge_cents` inclusive. Supplying it on an encounter whose coverage is `MEDICAID_MCO` is refused outright with `ERR_MEDICAID_BALANCE_BILL` — the prohibition is on the attempt, not merely on the resulting number. For the other two regimes the supplied value **replaces** the computed share, and the allowed amount follows the regime: under `UNINSURED_SLIDING_FEE` the `allowed_amount_cents` is set equal to the override, so `plan_paid_cents` becomes zero and the whole remainder falls into `write_off_cents`; under `COMMERCIAL_FEE_SCHEDULE` the `allowed_amount_cents` is left exactly as step four computed it and `plan_paid_cents` absorbs the entire difference. Because the override's ceiling is `total_charge_cents`, which contains the never-allowable supply surcharge, an override may legitimately exceed the commercial allowed amount; `plan_paid_cents` is then `allowed_amount_cents` minus the override **unclamped**, and is permitted to come out negative. In both cases the conservation identity must still close to the cent, and it closes precisely because nothing is clamped.

Once priced, the encounter's billing axis is a cycle with a revert. `PRICED → CLAIMED` submits the claim; under `MEDICAID_ENCOUNTER_RATE` this is only legal when `patient_responsibility_cents` is zero, which the pricing rules guarantee it always is. `CLAIMED → PAID` and `CLAIMED → DENIED` record the payer's decision, driven by an `outcome` of `PAID` or `DENIED`, with a `denial_reason` recorded on the denial — an optional field, written as null when it is omitted, and touched by the `DENIED` leg alone. `DENIED → CLAIMED` resubmits a denied claim without repricing it and clears `denial_reason` back to null; the clearing is in truth unconditional on both claim legs, though on `PRICED → CLAIMED` there is never anything there to clear. The Medicaid zero-share check likewise guards the `PRICED → CLAIMED` leg only: a resubmission out of `DENIED` does not re-run it. And `PRICED → UNBILLED` is the revert leg, reached only by a procedure void or by the spore-taint cascade, never by an operator asking for it directly. `PAID` is the terminal billing state.

### 3.7. Treatment Plans, Referrals and the Follow-Up Cadence

A **treatment plan** carries the findings of one encounter out of the van and into the rest of the child's or resident's life. It is authored against an `encounter_id`, names the `patient_id` (which must equal the encounter's own patient, or the create is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`) and the `authoring_staff_id` of the clinician who wrote it, and may carry `planned_procedure_codes`, an array of the procedure codes the plan recommends. That array defaults to an empty list and is **informational only** — nothing in the system instantiates procedures from it. At creation the plan copies the encounter's `triage_band` into its own `urgency_band`, which is what drives both the referral deadline and the follow-up cadence from then on. Nothing requires the encounter to have been screened first — the create declares no clinical-state precondition — so an encounter still carrying a null `triage_band` yields a plan whose `urgency_band` is null, and every band-keyed rule downstream, the follow-up cadence and the school and facility referral deadlines alike, falls through to its `NO_OBVIOUS_PROBLEM` branch for such a plan. A new plan is `DRAFT` with `activated_on`, `follow_up_due_date`, `last_contact_at` and `superseded_reason` null, `contact_attempt_count` zero and `follow_up_status` `NOT_DUE`.

**Activation** moves the plan `DRAFT → ACTIVE` and is restricted to a `FieldDentist`. It requires the plan's encounter to have reached `EXAMINED`, `TREATED` or `CLOSED`. Activation stamps `activated_on` with the logical clock date and computes the first `follow_up_due_date`. The cadence is keyed on the band: 7 calendar days for an `URGENT` plan, 30 for `EARLY_CARE_NEEDED`, and 180 for `NO_OBVIOUS_PROBLEM`. The anchor is the plan's `last_contact_at` date when a contact has been recorded, and its `activated_on` otherwise, so at activation the anchor is always the activation date itself.

`follow_up_status` is not stored so much as re-derived: it is recomputed on every read of a plan and on both transitions that touch `follow_up_due_date`. A plan whose `follow_up_due_date` is null is `NOT_DUE`. Otherwise, if a contact has been recorded — `last_contact_at` is non-null — and the logical clock date is still strictly before the due date, the status is `CONTACTED`. Otherwise, if the clock date is strictly before the due date, it is `NOT_DUE`. Otherwise, if the clock date is at or before the due date plus a 14-day grace window, it is `DUE` — a window inclusive of both its endpoints, so the due date itself and the fourteenth day after it both read `DUE`. Beyond that the status is `OVERDUE`.

**Recording a follow-up contact** is a self-loop on `ACTIVE` and is the mechanism by which the cadence restarts. A `SiteCoordinator`, `FieldDentist` or `FieldHygienist` records it with an optional `contact_note`, which is kept for audit and is not surfaced on the plan resource. The contact stamps `last_contact_at` with the logical clock instant, raises `contact_attempt_count` by one, recomputes `follow_up_due_date` from that contact date using the same band cadence, and sets `follow_up_status` to `CONTACTED`. Because the anchor moves with each contact, a plan that is being actively worked never falls into `OVERDUE`.

A plan leaves `ACTIVE` in one of three ways, all terminal. It reaches `COMPLETED` when a referral on it is recorded as completed, which also clears `follow_up_due_date` to null and leaves `follow_up_status` at `CONTACTED`. It reaches `SUPERSEDED` when the spore cascade of §3.3 invalidates the work the plan was written from, with `superseded_reason` `SPORE_TEST_FAILED`. And it reaches `CANCELLED` when a consent tier downgrade strands its encounter, with `superseded_reason` `CONSENT_TIER_DOWNGRADE`. Both of the latter two clear `follow_up_due_date` to null.

A **referral** hands the case to an outside dental home. It names the `treatment_plan_id`, the `patient_id` (which must equal the plan's patient) and the `dental_home_name` of the receiving practice, and opens in `status` `OPEN` with `attempt_count` zero and `due_at`, `last_sent_at`, `next_eligible_send_at`, `abandoned_at`, `abandon_reason` and `case_management_opened_at` all null. Sending it the first time requires the plan to be `ACTIVE` and moves the referral `OPEN → SENT`.

**Every send raises `attempt_count` by one, stamps `last_sent_at` with the logical clock instant, and recomputes `next_eligible_send_at` as that instant plus 21 days.** The service-level deadline `due_at` is computed on the **first** send only and is never moved by a re-send; the clock on the dental home runs from the moment the case first left the clinic, not from the last time somebody chased it.

**The deadline itself is computed two entirely different ways, chosen by the site's `site_type`.** For a `SCHOOL` site the deadline is measured in **business days** advanced from the first-send instant — stepping forward one calendar day at a time, counting only Monday through Friday, preserving the time of day and applying no holiday calendar: 3 business days for an `URGENT` plan, 15 for `EARLY_CARE_NEEDED` and 45 for `NO_OBVIOUS_PROBLEM`. An urgent school referral first sent on a Monday at 14:00 UTC is therefore due on Thursday at 14:00 UTC, and the same referral first sent on a Friday lands on the following Wednesday. For an `SNF` site the deadline runs on the plain calendar clock and the urgent band is measured in **hours** rather than days: 48 hours for `URGENT`, 10 calendar days for `EARLY_CARE_NEEDED` and 45 calendar days for `NO_OBVIOUS_PROBLEM`, with weekends counted throughout. The identical Monday 14:00 UTC urgent send at a nursing facility is therefore due Wednesday at 14:00 UTC — a full calendar day earlier than the school variant of the same urgency.

The referral loop is a **capped cycle**. From `SENT`, recording a response with an `outcome` of `ACCEPTED`, `DECLINED` or `NO_RESPONSE` moves the referral to that state. The `NO_RESPONSE` entry deliberately carries **no timing precondition at all**: the dental home simply did not answer, and the clinic may record that at any moment. The waiting period lives on the re-send leg instead, which is what makes it reachable — if entering `NO_RESPONSE` itself required the waiting period to have elapsed, no referral could ever be in `NO_RESPONSE` and simultaneously too early to re-send. From `NO_RESPONSE`, a re-send requires `attempt_count` to be strictly below 3 **and** the logical clock to have reached `next_eligible_send_at`; too early is `ERR_REFERRAL_NOT_DUE`. From `DECLINED`, a re-send requires only that `attempt_count` be strictly below 3 — a practice that has actively declined imposes no waiting period. A replacement `dental_home_name` may in fact be supplied on **any** send, not only this one: whenever the field is present on a send that succeeds, it overwrites the stored name, whether the referral is leaving `OPEN`, `NO_RESPONSE` or `DECLINED`, and when it is absent the stored name stands. Both re-sends move the referral back to `SENT`, raise `attempt_count`, restamp `last_sent_at` and `next_eligible_send_at`, and leave `due_at` untouched.

From `ACCEPTED`, recording an `outcome` of `COMPLETED` moves the referral to `COMPLETED` and moves its plan from `ACTIVE` to `COMPLETED`. `COMPLETED` is reachable only from `ACCEPTED`; a `COMPLETED` outcome recorded against a referral in any other status is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The other terminal state is `ABANDONED`, reached either by the attempt-cap sweep of §2.8 — from `NO_RESPONSE` or from `DECLINED`, with `abandon_reason` `ATTEMPT_CAP_REACHED` — or by the spore cascade of §3.3 from `OPEN` or `SENT`, with `abandon_reason` `PLAN_SUPERSEDED`. Both abandonment paths stamp `abandoned_at` and open a case-management escalation by stamping `case_management_opened_at` at the same instant, because a child whose referral has run out of road needs a person, not another letter.

### 3.8. Reporting, Disclosure and the Programme Freeze

Three audiences read the programme's output, and each gets a different shape of truth. A **report** names the `program_period_id` it covers and a `report_type` of `PARENT`, `FACILITY_ROSTER` or `PROGRAM_PERIOD`. A `FACILITY_ROSTER` report requires a `site_id`; a `PARENT` report requires both a `site_id` and a `patient_id`, together with the `recipient_user_id` naming the opaque guardian identity it is addressed to; a `PROGRAM_PERIOD` report *requires* none of the three, and any of them supplied on one is accepted and stored on the row rather than refused — the requirement is a floor, not a prohibition. Reports are created in `status` `DRAFT` with `payload`, `generated_at`, `submitted_at` and `stale_reason` all null.

Generating a report builds its `payload` and stamps `generated_at`. The status axis is a self-loop with a cycle: `DRAFT → GENERATED` on the first generation, `GENERATED → GENERATED` on any regeneration, `GENERATED → STALE` whenever period data moves underneath it, and `STALE → GENERATED` when it is rebuilt — the last of which also clears `stale_reason` back to null. Only a `PROGRAM_PERIOD` report ever goes `STALE`, and it does so exactly as described at the end of §2.11.

**A `PROGRAM_PERIOD` payload** aggregates the whole period. Its scope is every encounter whose site visit carries that `program_period_id`. It reports `patients_screened_count`, the number of **distinct** patients among those encounters whose `clinical_status` is `SCREENED`, `EXAMINED`, `TREATED` or `CLOSED`; `urgent_count`, `early_care_count` and `no_problem_count`, the numbers of those same in-period encounters whose `triage_band` is `URGENT`, `EARLY_CARE_NEEDED` and `NO_OBVIOUS_PROBLEM` respectively; `varnish_applications_count` and `sealant_teeth_count`, the numbers of in-period procedure rows with codes `D1206` and `D1351` respectively whose `status` is `PERFORMED`; `referrals_sent_count`, the number of referrals on plans of in-period encounters whose `attempt_count` is at least 1, and `referrals_abandoned_count`, the number of those same referrals whose `status` is `ABANDONED`; and four money roll-ups — `total_charge_cents`, `total_plan_paid_cents`, `total_patient_responsibility_cents` and `total_write_off_cents` — each summing the corresponding encounter field over in-period encounters whose `billing_status` is `PRICED`, `CLAIMED`, `PAID` or `DENIED`, and therefore excluding everything still `UNBILLED`.

**A `FACILITY_ROSTER` payload** is where the disclosure wall is enforced in data rather than in access control. It carries the `site_id`, the site's `roster_disclosure_level`, a `patient_line_count`, a `lines` array, and the same `urgent_count`, `early_care_count` and `no_problem_count` computed over that site's in-period encounters. The `lines` array holds one entry per patient of that site who has at least one in-period encounter, **ordered by the patient's `created_at` ascending**. When the site's `roster_disclosure_level` is `CLINICAL_DETAIL`, each line carries `patient_id`, `external_ref`, `triage_band` — the band of the last of that patient's in-period encounters, taken in `created_at` ascending order, that carries one at all, so a later encounter with a null band never erases an earlier band and a patient with no banded encounter reads null — `procedure_codes_performed`, the codes of that patient's in-period `PERFORMED` procedures listed one entry per procedure row in `created_at` ascending order with repeated codes repeated rather than collapsed, and `follow_up_due_date`, read off the last treatment plan created against any of those encounters and left null both when there is no such plan and when that plan's own due date is null. When the level is `SUMMARY_ONLY`, each line carries only `patient_id`, `external_ref` and a `seen` flag set to true; the three clinical keys are **omitted from the object entirely rather than present with null values**, because a facility that is not entitled to clinical detail should not receive keys implying that detail exists.

**A `PARENT` payload** covers exactly one patient and never carries a `lines` array. It carries `patient_id`, `external_ref`, `triage_band`, `procedure_codes_performed` and `follow_up_due_date`, each derived from that patient's in-period encounters by exactly the rules the roster lines use, together with `patient_responsibility_cents` — the sum of that field over **every** one of the patient's in-period encounters whatever its `billing_status`, so an encounter still `UNBILLED` contributes its stored zero rather than being excluded the way the programme roll-ups exclude it. A family therefore sees what was found, what was done, when the next contact is due and what they owe.

Submission is the act that freezes the year, and it is restricted to a `ProgramAdmin`. It is legal only for a `PROGRAM_PERIOD` report that is currently `GENERATED` — a `STALE` report is refused with `ERR_REPORT_STALE` and must be regenerated first — and only while the covered period is in `REPORTING`. The report-type guard and the period-state guard both sit on the state gate at rung 11 and therefore pre-empt rung 38: a `STALE` report whose period is not in `REPORTING`, or a `STALE` report of the wrong type, answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE` and not `ERR_REPORT_STALE`, and only a `STALE` `PROGRAM_PERIOD` report over a period genuinely in `REPORTING` ever reaches the staleness refusal. The request supplies `state_submission_ref`, the state agency's submission reference, which is written onto the `ProgramPeriod` as well as being the reason the submission exists. Submission stamps the report's `submitted_at` and moves it to `SUBMITTED`, which is terminal. It then runs two further links: the covered period moves `REPORTING → SUBMITTED` with `frozen_at` stamped at the logical clock instant, arming the global write gate over everything that resolves to that period; and every site visit in that period still in `status` `SCHEDULED` moves to `CANCELLED` with `cancelled_at` stamped, taking every encounter on those visits that is still `REGISTERED` to `NO_SHOW` with it. Visits already `IN_PROGRESS`, `HOLD_BLOCKED`, `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED` are not touched by that link — a visit that is under way when the year is submitted is allowed to finish, and a blocked visit is already off the calendar.
