# Grassroots Advocacy Campaign Management API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Advocacy organisations lose legislative fights for administrative reasons far more often than for political ones. A campaign team builds a supporter list from petition sheets, house parties and digital acquisition; it drafts copy, tests it, and sends it; it matches constituents to their own legislators and thanks them afterwards; it holds a coalition of partner organisations to a single message; and it does all of this against a legislative calendar it does not control. When any one of those threads is tracked in a spreadsheet, the failure is predictable — a supporter is texted after revoking consent, a retired message keeps running in market, a partner who quietly withdrew is still counted toward a funding split, or an in-district meeting is booked with an untrained spokesperson two minutes after another meeting across town.

This document specifies the Grassroots Advocacy Campaign Management API, the system of record for a campaign team's entire operating surface. It is mounted under `/advocacy`, with a single additional test-driving endpoint under `/_clock`. Thirteen peer collections live directly under that namespace: campaigns, legislative-calendar milestones, target legislators, supporters, consent events, segments, message variants, coalition partners, partner endorsements, activations (send waves), constituent action records, spokesperson profiles and in-district meetings. Relationships between them are carried as identifiers in request bodies rather than as nested URLs; the only nested read paths in the system exist so that a caller can walk backwards from a campaign to its milestones and coalition partners, or from a supporter to that supporter's consent log and spokesperson profiles.

Two design commitments run through every rule that follows. The first is that **compliance dominates convenience**: consent and lawful basis are checked before scheduling, capacity or budget concerns, and the operator who submits work may never be the operator who approves it. The second is that **derived state is never accepted as input**: pressure scores, engagement scores, membership snapshots, funding splits, quorum requirements and the campaign's own send posture are all computed by the server from the rows it holds, recomputed as a consequence of every mutation that touches their inputs, and returned to callers as read-only fields. An implementer who treats any of those as a writable column will pass simple tests and fail every cascade.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to this API — including reads and including the logical-clock endpoint — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that principal is acting in. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the six role names below spelled exactly, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If both headers are well-formed but the named role is not authorised for the endpoint being called, the rejection is `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403). The authoritative per-endpoint role list is the table in §4.

The six roles are `CampaignDirector`, `FieldOrganizer`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `MessageStrategist`, `PartnerDelegate` and `Supporter`. They own disjoint resource families:

- A `CampaignDirector` owns campaign definition, the timeline, coalition composition and the posture override, and is the accountable operator for launch, close and outcome analysis. Campaign creation, launch, close, posture overrides, milestone creation, milestone rescheduling and coalition-partner creation are exclusively theirs.
- A `FieldOrganizer` runs the field program: list building, action logging, thank-you loops, story collection, spokesperson training and in-district meetings. Acknowledging an action, thanking an action, advancing a training ladder, recording a meeting response and completing a meeting are exclusively theirs. A `FieldOrganizer` may not create, clear or block an activation, may not create or review a message variant, and may not perform any campaign lifecycle call.
- A `ComplianceOfficer` is the independent compliance authority over consent records, message review, wave clearance and action disputes — the segregation-of-duties counterweight to the operators who submit work. Wave clearance and wave blocking are exclusively theirs. A `ComplianceOfficer` may not create, launch, close or override a campaign, may not create, start or complete an activation, and may not create, submit or lock a message variant.
- A `MessageStrategist` owns message testing and segmentation. Drafting a variant, submitting a variant for review and resolving a message test are exclusively theirs. A `MessageStrategist` may never record a review decision on any variant (review belongs to compliance, not to the author), may not clear or block a wave, and may not write consent events or action records.
- A `PartnerDelegate` is a coalition partner organisation acting for itself: it endorses message copy, revokes its own endorsements, and may withdraw its own membership. It has no exclusive operations. It may not read a campaign's outcome report, and it may not write supporters, consent events, segments, activations, action records, spokesperson profiles or meetings.
- A `Supporter` is a constituent acting for themselves: they record their own consent changes and offer their own story. A `Supporter` may not list campaigns, legislators or activations, may not read the outcome report, and may not write any campaign, milestone, legislator, segment, variant, partner, activation, action-record or meeting resource.

Four of the six roles are operator roles whose identity is opaque, and two are entity-backed. When `X-User-Role` is `CampaignDirector`, `FieldOrganizer`, `ComplianceOfficer` or `MessageStrategist`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it where a transition says so — as `submitted_by_user_id` on a message variant at submit time and as `reviewed_by_user_id` at review time — and compares it to other recorded operator identifiers for segregation of duties, but never compares it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `PartnerDelegate`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `CoalitionPartner`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `CoalitionPartner` compare the resource's partner reference to `X-User-ID`, so a delegate may only endorse or withdraw where the `partner_id` equals `X-User-ID`, and may only revoke endorsements owned by that partner. When `X-User-Role` is `Supporter`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Supporter`; a supporter may only append consent events and open a story profile whose `supporter_id` equals `X-User-ID`, and may only read their own consent log and their own spokesperson profiles. Any entity-backed role acting outside its own rows is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403).

Segregation of duties is enforced on `X-User-ID` **within** a role as well as across roles. Three distinct pairings are policed, and all three carry `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403): the operator who submitted a message variant may not record that variant's review decision, even when that operator legitimately holds `ComplianceOfficer`; the operator who created an activation may not clear it; and the operator who filed an action record may not resolve that record's dispute.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and Default Ordering

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: its default is 10, its minimum accepted value is 1, and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: its default is 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. A value outside those bounds is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422).

List responses populate four pagination fields inside `meta`: `limit` and `offset` echo the effective values applied to the query, `total_count` reports the number of rows matching the filters before the page window is applied, and `has_more` is true exactly when rows remain beyond the current window. Single-resource reads omit all four.

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. One endpoint states otherwise in a strengthening rather than a contradicting way: a supporter's consent log is ordered by `created_at` ascending and then by `sequence_no` ascending, because two consent events can share a `created_at` when the logical clock has not advanced between them.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Version Counters

Every resource carries a server-minted `id`, formatted as a UUID with no type prefix. Clients never supply an `id`; a client-supplied `id` is an unknown field and therefore `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, stamped from the logical clock at insertion time and never mutated thereafter.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp refreshed on every mutation of that row, and `version`, an integer that begins at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row — including mutations the caller did not ask for directly, such as a counter recomputed as a cascade hop of some other entity's transition. Campaigns, milestones, legislators, supporters, segments, message variants, coalition partners, partner endorsements, activations, action records, spokesperson profiles and district meetings all carry `updated_at` and `version`.

`ConsentEvent` is the one exception, and it is deliberate: consent events are immutable append-only records, so a consent event carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has no `updated_at` and no `version` field at all, and neither field appears in its response payload.

### 2.5. The Logical Legislative Clock

Time in this domain is a server-held logical clock, not wall-clock time. Its startup default is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; before any clock call has been made the server reads that default, and it never falls back to real wall-clock time. An administrative test-driving endpoint sets the clock to an explicit ISO 8601 UTC instant supplied as `current_utc`, and answers with both the new `current_utc` and the `previous_utc` it replaced. All six roles may drive the clock.

Every window comparison, decay bucket and milestone position in this specification is evaluated against that clock. Setting the clock has two observable consequences beyond the returned payload: the `status` of every milestone is recomputed against its window, and the decay bucket that the rolling engagement variant of supporter scoring will use on its next evaluation changes.

### 2.6. Path Resolution and Reverse-Reachability Reads

Path parameters are resolved strictly left to right, and the first identifier that fails to resolve determines the error. The identifiers that appear in paths are `campaign_id`, `milestone_id`, `legislator_id`, `supporter_id`, `consent_event_id`, `segment_id`, `variant_id`, `partner_id`, `endorsement_id`, `activation_id`, `action_record_id`, `spokesperson_id` and `meeting_id`. Twelve entity families each have their own HTTP 404 code: `ERR_CAMPAIGN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MILESTONE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LEGISLATOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SUPPORTER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SEGMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VARIANT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PARTNER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ENDORSEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ACTIVATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ACTION_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SPOKESPERSON_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_MEETING_NOT_FOUND`.

Foreign keys supplied in a request body are resolved after all path parameters, in the field order the endpoint declares its input list, and each carries the same entity-specific 404 code. `ERR_VARIANT_NOT_FOUND` covers `variant_id` in a path as well as `message_variant_id`, `derived_from_variant_id` and `winning_message_variant_id` in a body; `ERR_SPOKESPERSON_NOT_FOUND` covers `spokesperson_id` in a path as well as `lead_spokesperson_id` in a body.

Because relationships are expressed as body identifiers rather than URL nesting, four nested read paths exist purely to make those relationships reverse-reachable: a campaign's milestones, a campaign's coalition partners, a supporter's consent events and a supporter's spokesperson profiles. Everything else is reachable through a filtered flat list.

### 2.7. Version Pinning on Audience and Copy Writes

Two write endpoints are guarded by optimistic concurrency control against a *parent* resource's `version`. In both cases the version field is a required, non-optional input; omitting it is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

A `POST /advocacy/activations` request must carry `expected_segment_version`, holding the `version` of the target `Segment` as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the segment's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SEGMENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The product deliberately permits many waves against one segment, so a second create is legitimate on its own terms; what the guard catches is that the audience snapshot the client sized its wave against — `member_supporter_ids`, `member_count` and `active_activation_count` — is no longer the audience the server holds. A supporter may have been added, a consent may have been revoked, or another wave may have taken reservations that shrink the frequency headroom.

A `POST /advocacy/partner-endorsements` request must carry `expected_variant_version`, holding the `version` of the target `MessageVariant` as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the variant's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VARIANT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Every coalition partner may legitimately endorse the same variant, so a later endorsement is a normal product operation; the guard exists because the copy under endorsement may have changed — a review decision, a lock, a reopen or another partner's endorsement all bump the variant's `version` — and message discipline requires a partner to endorse the exact revision it reviewed. That is why an accepted endorsement pins `endorsed_variant_version`.

No other endpoint in this API takes a version field. In particular, refreshing a segment and reopening a locked variant both increment the parent's `version`, which is precisely what invalidates an `expected_segment_version` or `expected_variant_version` a caller is already holding.
### 2.8. The Rejection Ladder

This is an advocacy-compliance domain, and the order in which a request's problems are reported is itself part of the contract. When a single request violates several rules at once, the response always carries the `error_id` of the **lowest-numbered** rung below. Twenty-four rungs are evaluated in this exact sequence:

1. **Authentication.** Both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` are present and `X-User-Role` is one of the six declared roles — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Role permission.** The acting role appears in the target endpoint's authorised list — otherwise `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`. Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Path existence.** Every identifier in the URL path resolves, checked left to right in path order — otherwise the matching not-found code from §2.6. Applies to every endpoint that has a path parameter.
4. **Body foreign-key existence.** Every foreign key supplied in the request body resolves, checked in the field order the endpoint declares — otherwise the matching not-found code. Applies to every write endpoint that carries a body foreign key.
5. **Request shape.** The body is well formed: no unknown fields, every required field present, every enum value inside its declared set, integers non-negative where declared non-negative, cent amounts integral, and timestamps parseable as ISO 8601 UTC — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Applies to every write endpoint. The *order* of two parseable window bounds is deliberately **not** checked here: a well-typed but inverted window passes this rung and is caught at rung 15, so it surfaces as `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`, never as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
6. **Resource ownership.** For the two entity-backed roles, the acting `X-User-ID` owns the addressed resource — otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. This rung guards exactly four operations: appending a consent event, creating a partner endorsement, revoking a partner endorsement, and opening a spokesperson profile.
7. **Version pinning.** The supplied `expected_segment_version` or `expected_variant_version` equals the parent's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_SEGMENT_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_VARIANT_VERSION` respectively. This rung guards exactly two operations: activation creation and endorsement creation.
8. **Campaign lifecycle.** The owning campaign's `status` is `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_CAMPAIGN_NOT_ACTIVE`. This rung guards every write beneath a campaign, with a carefully drawn set of exclusions: campaign creation, launch, close and posture overrides are exempt because they *are* the lifecycle; and legislator creation and milestone creation are additionally permitted while the campaign is still `DRAFT`, because the target list and the legislative calendar are built before launch — which is also what makes launch reachable at all, since launch requires at least one legislator row. Both of those planning writes are still refused once the campaign is `CLOSED`.
9. **Frozen posture.** The owning campaign's derived `posture` is not `FROZEN` — otherwise `ERR_CAMPAIGN_FROZEN`. This rung guards eight operations: activation creation, wave clearance, send start, action-record creation, the thank-you touch, district-meeting creation, endorsement creation and variant locking.
10. **Restricted posture.** When the owning campaign's `posture` is `RESTRICTED`, only `EMAIL` waves remain open: creating, clearing or starting an `SMS` or `PHONE` activation is refused with `ERR_CAMPAIGN_RESTRICTED` while `EMAIL` waves proceed normally. This rung guards exactly three operations: activation creation, wave clearance and send start.
11. **Declared transition.** The requested transition is declared for the entity's current state, including terminal-state lockdown and cycle limits — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Applies to every transition endpoint.
12. **Segregation of duties.** The three `X-User-ID` pairings of §2.1 hold — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. This rung guards exactly three operations: recording a variant review decision, clearing a wave, and resolving an action-record dispute.
13. **Contactability.** The target supporter is contactable on the addressed channel. This rung has a strict internal order of its own: first the suppression flag, so a supporter whose `suppressed` value is true is rejected with `ERR_SUPPORTER_SUPPRESSED`; only then the resolved consent state of the addressed channel, which must be `GRANTED` or the rejection is `ERR_CONSENT_MISSING`. The addressed channel is the action record's `channel` when logging a constituent action, `thank_channel` on the thank-you touch, and `EMAIL` on a district-meeting request; the channel-less spokesperson intake instead requires at least one of `EMAIL`, `SMS` or `PHONE` to be `GRANTED`. This rung guards exactly three operations: action-record creation, district-meeting creation and spokesperson intake.
14. **Lawful basis.** A consent event whose `action` is `GRANT` supplies a `lawful_basis` drawn from `EXPRESS_WRITTEN`, `OPT_IN_FORM`, `EVENT_CARD` or `VERBAL_RECORDED`, and a `REVOKE` event supplies no `lawful_basis` at all — otherwise `ERR_LAWFUL_BASIS_INVALID`. This rung guards exactly two operations: appending a consent event and creating a supporter with initial consent.
15. **Window sanity.** `window_start` is strictly before `window_end` and both bounds are aligned to a whole minute — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`. This rung guards exactly four operations: milestone creation, milestone rescheduling, activation creation and district-meeting creation.
16. **Window containment.** The window falls entirely inside the owning campaign's span from `start_date` to `end_date` inclusive — otherwise `ERR_WINDOW_OUTSIDE_CAMPAIGN`. This rung guards the same four operations as rung 15.
17. **Blackout.** The send window does not intersect any milestone of kind `RECESS` belonging to the same campaign — otherwise `ERR_BLACKOUT_WINDOW`. This rung guards activation creation only.
18. **Meeting collision.** The requested meeting does not collide with another `REQUESTED`, `CONFIRMED` or `HELD` meeting for the same legislator once the travel buffer is applied to both sides of the existing interval — otherwise `ERR_MEETING_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. This rung guards district-meeting creation only.
19. **Channel saturation.** No more than two activations on the same campaign-and-channel pairing hold overlapping closed send windows while in delivery state `SCHEDULED` or `SENDING` — a third such wave is refused with `ERR_CHANNEL_WINDOW_SATURATED`. This rung guards activation creation only.
20. **Uniqueness.** At most one `ACTIVE` endorsement exists per partner-and-variant pairing, and at most one spokesperson profile exists per supporter-and-campaign pairing — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_ENDORSEMENT` or `ERR_DUPLICATE_SPOKESPERSON` respectively. This rung guards endorsement creation and spokesperson intake.
21. **Revision ceiling.** A variant has not exhausted its revision allowance — otherwise `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED`. This rung guards variant submission only.
22. **Collaborator readiness.** The cross-entity collaborator named in the request is ready: an activation's variant must be `LOCKED` or the rejection is `ERR_VARIANT_NOT_LOCKED`; a district meeting's lead spokesperson must be `CERTIFIED` or the rejection is `ERR_SPOKESPERSON_NOT_CERTIFIED`; a variant lock requires at least the variant's `required_endorsement_quorum` in `ACTIVE` endorsements or the rejection is `ERR_ENDORSEMENT_QUORUM_NOT_MET`; and both endorsement creation and endorsement revocation require the named coalition partner to still be `ACTIVE` or the rejection is `ERR_PARTNER_WITHDRAWN`. This rung guards five operations: activation creation, district-meeting creation, variant locking, endorsement creation and endorsement revocation.
23. **Scope agreement.** Co-referenced rows agree: all foreign keys in one request resolve to the same campaign or the rejection is `ERR_CAMPAIGN_MISMATCH`; the activation `channel` appears in the variant's `channel_scope` or the rejection is `ERR_CHANNEL_NOT_IN_VARIANT_SCOPE`; and a meeting's lead spokesperson's supporter lives in the addressed legislator's district or the rejection is `ERR_DISTRICT_MISMATCH`. This rung guards five operations: activation creation, endorsement creation, district-meeting creation, action-record creation and segment creation.
24. **Capacity, reach and budget.** Last of all, capacity floors, again with a strict internal order: an activation targeting a segment whose `member_count` is 0 is refused with `ERR_SEGMENT_EMPTY`; a `projected_reach` that evaluates to 0 because every member already sits at or above the wave's `effective_touch_cap` is refused with `ERR_FREQUENCY_CAP_EXHAUSTED`; a `projected_reach` greater than 0 but strictly below `min_viable_reach` is refused with `ERR_REACH_BELOW_MINIMUM`; and a `delivered_count` reported at completion that exceeds `projected_reach` is refused with `ERR_DELIVERY_TALLY_MISMATCH`. This rung guards three operations: activation creation, activation completion and message-test resolution.

### 2.9. Numeric Precision: Scores, Cents and Basis Points

Three numeric families coexist in this API, each with its own rounding contract.

**Scores** — a legislator's `contact_pressure_score`, a supporter's `engagement_score` and `retention_score`, and a meeting's `pressure_credit` — are decimal numbers carried to exactly 2 decimal places, rounded half up. Rounding is applied **once**, at the end of the calculation, after every cap and bonus has been summed; intermediate products are never rounded. A meeting's `pressure_credit` and a milestone's `pressure_multiplier` are taken verbatim from their constant tables rather than computed, the multiplier to one decimal place.

**Cents** — `funded_total_cents`, `committed_cents`, `committed_share_total_cents`, `send_cost_cents`, `send_cost_total_cents` and `cost_per_action_cents` — are integers throughout. There is no fractional cent anywhere in this system. Where an integer division is needed, the specification names the direction explicitly: the coalition funding split floors each partner's line and then distributes the leftover cents one at a time, and `cost_per_action_cents` floors rather than rounds.

**Basis points** — `response_rate_bps` and the outcome report's `thank_loop_closure_bps` — are integers on a scale of 10,000, computed by multiplying the numerator by 10,000, dividing by the denominator and rounding the result half up to a whole number. Both are defined as 0 rather than as an error when their denominator is 0.

### 2.10. Derived Fields and Cascade Discipline

A large fraction of the fields in this system are computed. No computed field is ever accepted as request input; supplying one is an unknown field and therefore `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Recomputation is immediate and transactional: by the time a request returns, every derived field reachable from the rows it touched has been brought up to date, and the response reflects the post-cascade state.

Cascades in this domain are multi-hop, and the hops matter because each one bumps a different row's `version` and `updated_at`. A partner withdrawal, for example, does not merely mark one partner `WITHDRAWN` — it revokes that partner's endorsements, which lowers affected variants' `endorsement_count`, which can drop a `LOCKED` variant below its `required_endorsement_quorum` and push it to `CHANGES_REQUESTED`, which blocks that variant's scheduled waves, which raises the campaign's `blocked_activation_count`, which re-derives the campaign's `posture`. Every one of those five hops is observable, and §3 states each cascade hop by hop for the transition that triggers it.

The campaign `posture` is always the **last** hop of any request that changes it. Its inputs are recomputed first, and the posture derivation then runs exactly once over the settled counts; it is never re-entered within the same request.

### 2.11. Campaign Posture: the System-Wide Send Gate

Beyond its lifecycle `status` of `DRAFT`, `ACTIVE` or `CLOSED`, every campaign carries a second, independent dimension called `posture`, taking one of `OPEN`, `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN`. Posture is never set directly and never accepted as input; it is a pure derivation, re-evaluated as the final hop of every mutation that changes an activation's `approval_state`, a legislator's `priority_tier` or `stance`, the campaign's `status`, or the campaign's `posture_override`.

The derivation reads two counters, both recomputed immediately before it runs. `blocked_activation_count` is the number of the campaign's activations whose `approval_state` is `BLOCKED`. `opposed_priority_legislator_count` is the number of the campaign's legislators whose `priority_tier` is `PRIMARY` **and** whose `stance` is either `LEAN_OPPOSE` or `OPPOSE`. Six branches are then evaluated in this exact order, and the first one that matches wins:

1. If the campaign's `status` is `CLOSED`, the posture is `FROZEN`.
2. Otherwise, if `posture_override` is `FORCE_FROZEN`, the posture is `FROZEN`.
3. Otherwise, if `posture_override` is `FORCE_OPEN`, the posture is `OPEN`.
4. Otherwise, if `blocked_activation_count` is at least 2, the posture is `FROZEN`.
5. Otherwise, if `blocked_activation_count` is at least 1 or `opposed_priority_legislator_count` is at least 1, the posture is `RESTRICTED`.
6. Otherwise the posture is `OPEN`.

The same request also refreshes two campaign totals as part of this pass: `total_actions_logged`, the number of the campaign's action records whose `status` is `MATCHED`, `ACKNOWLEDGED` or `THANKED`; and `distinct_acting_supporter_count`, the number of distinct supporters appearing on those same rows.

Because posture is a pure derivation, it un-gates itself without any explicit call. Aborting the second blocked wave drops the campaign from `FROZEN` back to `RESTRICTED`; moving the only opposed `PRIMARY` legislator back to `UNDECIDED` returns the campaign to `OPEN`. `FORCE_OPEN` is the one branch that can mask real blockers — it forces `posture` to `OPEN` irrespective of `blocked_activation_count` and `opposed_priority_legislator_count`, and it does **not** clear `blocked_activation_count`, nor does it un-block the blocked waves, which stay `BLOCKED`. And even `FORCE_OPEN` cannot un-freeze a `CLOSED` campaign, because the closed branch is evaluated first.

What each posture gates is stated on the ladder in §2.8: `FROZEN` refuses activation creation, wave clearance, send start, action-record creation, thank-you touches, district-meeting creation, endorsement creation and variant locking with `ERR_CAMPAIGN_FROZEN`; `RESTRICTED` refuses only the `SMS` and `PHONE` cases of activation creation, clearance and send start with `ERR_CAMPAIGN_RESTRICTED`, leaving `EMAIL` waves running. `RESTRICTED` has one further effect that is not a refusal: it halves the frequency surge grant available to milestone-anchored waves, from 2 additional touches down to 1.

### 2.12. Consent, Lawful Basis and Frequency Governance

Consent is the regulatory core of this system and sits above every scheduling, capacity or budget concern on the rejection ladder. A supporter's consent is **not** a stored flag that operators toggle; it is the replay of an append-only, immutable log of consent events, resolved independently per channel.

For each of the three channels `EMAIL`, `SMS` and `PHONE`, the server gathers every consent event of that supporter whose `channel` is either that channel or the wildcard `ALL`. If that set is empty, the resolved state is `NONE`. Otherwise the latest event wins, where "latest" means highest `created_at`, and if two candidates share a `created_at`, highest `sequence_no` — a per-supporter monotonic insertion counter starting at 1, which exists precisely because the logical clock may not advance between two calls. The winning event's `action` decides the outcome: `GRANT` resolves to `GRANTED`, `REVOKE` resolves to `REVOKED`. The three results are written to `email_consent_state`, `sms_consent_state` and `phone_consent_state`.

One consequence of per-channel replay deserves stating plainly: a channel-scoped `GRANT` recorded after an `ALL`-scoped `REVOKE` re-grants that one channel, because the per-channel event is the later event *for that channel*. The `ALL`-scoped revoke still governs the other two.

A supporter's `suppressed` flag is true exactly when all three of `email_consent_state`, `sms_consent_state` and `phone_consent_state` are `REVOKED`. That distinction drives which of two error codes a caller sees: a supporter with all three channels revoked yields `ERR_SUPPORTER_SUPPRESSED`, while a supporter whose addressed channel is `NONE` or `REVOKED` but who still has at least one other channel `GRANTED` yields `ERR_CONSENT_MISSING`. A spokesperson intake attempted for a supporter with no `GRANTED` channel at all is likewise `ERR_CONSENT_MISSING`.

Frequency governance is a two-column ledger on each supporter. `touches_reserved` counts outstanding reservations — the number of activations in delivery state `SCHEDULED` or `SENDING` that list this supporter in their `target_supporter_ids`. `touches_consumed` counts delivered touches and thank-you touches; it is incremented once per delivered activation touch and once per thank-you touch, and it is **never** decremented. The base allowance is 3 touches per supporter, and a supporter is eligible for a further touch only while the sum of `touches_consumed` and `touches_reserved` is **strictly below** the applicable cap — never merely equal to it. Reservations are compensating: every path that takes a wave out of `SCHEDULED` or `SENDING` releases exactly the reservations that wave took, which is what keeps the sum of all supporters' `touches_reserved` equal to the sum of `projected_reach` over the campaign's `SCHEDULED` and `SENDING` waves.

Coalition funding obeys a matching conservation rule: the sum of `committed_cents` over a campaign's `ACTIVE` partners, published as the campaign's `committed_share_total_cents`, always equals that campaign's `funded_total_cents`. Both invariants are reported as booleans on the outcome report.
## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Founding a Campaign and Laying Out the Legislative Calendar

A `CampaignDirector` opens the file by creating a campaign. The payload names the campaign — `name`, non-empty and at most 160 characters — and states what it is trying to achieve: `goal_statement`, a single sentence of at most 400 characters describing what the decision makers must do, and `bill_reference`, at most 40 characters, naming the legislative vehicle being targeted (for instance "HB-1420"). `target_chamber` declares whose members are the target decision makers and takes one of `HOUSE`, `SENATE` or `BOTH`. The timeline is `start_date` and `end_date`, both ISO 8601 UTC instants, with `end_date` strictly after `start_date`; the span is inclusive at both ends and every milestone, send window and meeting window in the campaign must sit inside it. Two success metrics are committed up front — `success_metric_target_actions`, the number of constituent actions the campaign promises to deliver, and `success_metric_target_supporters`, the number of distinct acting supporters it promises to mobilise — and both must be zero or greater. Finally `funded_total_cents` records the total coalition-funded budget in whole cents, again zero or greater.

A freshly created campaign lands in `status` `DRAFT`, `posture` `OPEN` and `posture_override` `NONE`. Every computed counter starts at zero: `blocked_activation_count`, `opposed_priority_legislator_count`, `total_actions_logged`, `distinct_acting_supporter_count` and `committed_share_total_cents`. Campaigns can be listed with an optional `status` filter on the lifecycle dimension and an optional `posture` filter on the derived gating dimension.

Before a campaign can be launched it needs at least one target decision maker. Registering a legislator takes `campaign_id`, `full_name` (at most 120 characters), `district_code` (at most 16 characters, for example "HD-14"), and `chamber`, which is either `HOUSE` or `SENATE`. An opening whip-count `stance` may be supplied from `SUPPORT`, `LEAN_SUPPORT`, `UNDECIDED`, `LEAN_OPPOSE` and `OPPOSE`; when omitted the stance is `UNDECIDED`. At creation the server snapshots that value into `initial_stance`, which is never mutated afterwards and exists so outcome analysis can measure how far the whip count moved. A new legislator starts with `contact_pressure_score` of 0.00, `priority_tier` `MONITOR`, `constituent_action_count` of 0 and `meeting_held_count` of 0. Legislators can be listed filtered by `campaign_id`, `district_code`, `chamber`, `stance` or derived `priority_tier`.

Launching a `DRAFT` campaign advances `status` from `DRAFT` to `ACTIVE`. It has two preconditions: the campaign must currently be in `DRAFT`, and at least one legislator row must exist whose `campaign_id` names this campaign — a campaign with no target legislator is refused with `ERR_NO_TARGET_LEGISLATOR`. On success the campaign's `posture` is recomputed and the campaign's `version` increments by 1. Legislator creation and milestone creation are the two writes deliberately permitted while a campaign is still `DRAFT`, precisely so this precondition is reachable.

Whip-count work continues after launch. Recording a stance change supplies the new `stance` together with an `evidence_note` justifying it. The change re-runs the district pressure index for that legislator, re-derives `priority_tier` from the resulting score, and then re-derives the campaign posture — which is why a single stance change from `UNDECIDED` to `OPPOSE` on a `PRIMARY`-tier legislator can move a campaign from `OPEN` to `RESTRICTED` with no activation involved at all.

Posture overrides are the `CampaignDirector`'s escape hatch. The payload carries `posture_override`, one of `NONE`, `FORCE_OPEN` or `FORCE_FROZEN`, together with an `override_reason`. Latching `FORCE_OPEN` drives the posture from `RESTRICTED` to `OPEN` or from `FROZEN` to `OPEN`, on the single precondition that the campaign's `status` is not `CLOSED` — a closed campaign stays `FROZEN`. Latching `FORCE_FROZEN` drives the posture from `OPEN` to `FROZEN` with no preconditions at all. In every case the override is stored, the posture is re-derived through the branch ladder of §2.11, and the campaign's `version` increments by 1. Latching `FORCE_OPEN` does not clear `blocked_activation_count` and does not un-block any wave; the blocked waves remain `BLOCKED` and reappear the moment the override is set back to `NONE`.

The legislative calendar is modelled as milestones, each added with `campaign_id`, a `kind` drawn from `COMMITTEE_HEARING`, `FLOOR_VOTE`, `RECESS` and `SIGNING`, a human `label` of at most 120 characters (for example "House floor vote"), and the window bounds `window_start` and `window_end`. Milestone windows are **half-open**: the window includes `window_start` and excludes `window_end`. Two fields are derived at creation and never supplied by the caller. `is_blackout` is true exactly when `kind` is `RECESS`, and blackout windows forbid overlapping activation send windows. `pressure_multiplier` is taken verbatim from the calendar-weight table: a `COMMITTEE_HEARING` window is worth 1.5, a `FLOOR_VOTE` window 2.0, a `RECESS` window 0.5, and a `SIGNING` window 1.0. `reschedule_count` starts at 0.

A milestone's `status` is a position report on the logical clock, recomputed on every read and after every clock movement. It is `SCHEDULED` while the clock has not reached the window; it becomes `IN_WINDOW` when the clock is at or after `window_start` **and** strictly before `window_end`; and it becomes `PASSED` once the clock is at or after `window_end`. While a window is `IN_WINDOW`, action records created inside it are stamped with that milestone's `pressure_multiplier`. Milestones can be listed for the whole system filtered by `campaign_id`, `kind`, `is_blackout` and `status`, or read back per campaign as a reverse-reachability sub-resource filtered by `kind`.

Rescheduling a milestone is the one edge that runs *backwards* along the clock: a `PASSED` milestone returns to `SCHEDULED`. The request supplies new `window_start` and `window_end` values and is subject to three preconditions — the new `window_end` must be strictly after the new `window_start`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`; the new window must lie inside the campaign's span from `start_date` to `end_date`, otherwise `ERR_WINDOW_OUTSIDE_CAMPAIGN`; and the campaign's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_CAMPAIGN_NOT_ACTIVE`. On success the bounds move, `reschedule_count` increments by 1, and `status` is re-derived from the logical clock against the new half-open window. Then a three-hop cascade runs. Because the window moved, every activation of the same campaign still in delivery state `SCHEDULED` is re-validated against the campaign's blackout milestones; because a re-validated wave now intersects a `RECESS` window, that wave's `approval_state` becomes `BLOCKED`; and because a wave became `BLOCKED`, the campaign's `blocked_activation_count` is recomputed and the posture derivation re-runs. The milestone's own `version` increments by 1. A `RECESS` milestone moved on top of an already-scheduled send window is the classic blackout collision, and the product's answer is to block the wave rather than to let it send silently. Note also that already-stamped action multipliers are **not** retroactively changed by a reschedule.

### 3.2. Building the Supporter List and Replaying the Consent Log

Supporters enter the list through petitions, events, digital acquisition or referral. Adding one takes `campaign_id`, `full_name` (at most 120 characters), an `email` that is non-empty and must contain an "@" character, a `district_code` (at most 16 characters) that will drive constituent-to-legislator matching, and an `acquisition_source` drawn from `PETITION`, `EVENT`, `DIGITAL_AD` and `REFERRAL`. A `phone` in E.164 form is optional in general but **required** when the same request captures initial consent for `SMS` or `PHONE`; when omitted the stored value is null.

Consent may be captured in the same breath as acquisition, which is what a petition sheet or an event card actually looks like in the field. `initial_consent_channels` is an optional list of channels, and `lawful_basis` and `consent_source_reference` document the artifact behind them. For each entry of `initial_consent_channels`, in the order listed, the server appends one consent event whose `action` is `GRANT`, each taking the next `sequence_no` for that supporter. The supporter's three consent states are then resolved by replay, `consent_event_count` is updated to the number of consent-event rows the supporter now has, and the supporter's `engagement_score` is initialised from the acquisition-source bonus alone: 1.0 for a `PETITION` supporter, 2.0 for an `EVENT` supporter, 0.5 for a `DIGITAL_AD` supporter and 1.5 for a `REFERRAL` supporter, carried to two decimal places.

Every later consent change is a new immutable row in the log rather than an edit. Appending one takes `supporter_id`, a `channel` drawn from `EMAIL`, `SMS`, `PHONE` and the wildcard `ALL`, an `action` of either `GRANT` or `REVOKE`, and a `source_reference` of at most 120 characters carrying the provenance handle of the consent artifact — a petition sheet identifier, a web form identifier. A `GRANT` must carry a `lawful_basis`; a `REVOKE` must not, and the stored value for a revoke is null. The server assigns the supporter's next monotonic `sequence_no` and stamps `effective_at` from the logical clock, which is the same instant as the row's `created_at`.

Appending a consent event has consequences well beyond the supporter row. The supporter's `email_consent_state`, `sms_consent_state`, `phone_consent_state` and `suppressed` flag are re-resolved by replay; then every segment of that supporter's campaign is re-evaluated against its membership predicate, bumping each affected `Segment` `version`; and then every activation of those segments that is still in delivery state `SCHEDULED` has its `projected_reach` and its touch reservations recomputed. A single revocation can therefore shrink an audience, shrink a scheduled wave, drive that wave's `projected_reach` below its `min_viable_reach`, block the wave, and move the campaign posture — all inside one request. Consent events can be listed across supporters for compliance audit filtered by `supporter_id`, `channel` and `action`, read individually as the atom of a consent audit, or walked as one supporter's append-only log filtered by `channel`.

Supporters carry the two frequency-governance counters described in §2.12 — `touches_reserved` and `touches_consumed` — plus `action_count`, the number of that supporter's action records in status `MATCHED`, `ACKNOWLEDGED` or `THANKED`, and `thanked_count`, the number in status `THANKED`. They can be listed filtered by `campaign_id`, `district_code`, `acquisition_source` and `suppressed`.

The rolling **engagement** score is what segmentation filters on, and it is computed as follows. Start from the acquisition-source bonus for the supporter's `acquisition_source` (`PETITION` 1.0, `EVENT` 2.0, `DIGITAL_AD` 0.5, `REFERRAL` 1.5). Then, for each of that supporter's action records whose `status` is `MATCHED`, `ACKNOWLEDGED` or `THANKED`, add the channel pressure weight of that record's `channel` — 1.0 for `EMAIL`, 1.5 for `SMS`, 3.0 for `PHONE` — multiplied by an age decay factor. The age is the whole number of days between the record's `created_at` and the current logical clock, computed by dividing the elapsed seconds by 86,400 and discarding any fraction. An action aged at most 7 days keeps a factor of 1.00; one aged more than 7 days but at most 30 days takes 0.75; anything older takes 0.50. Records in `DISPUTED`, `VOIDED` or `UNMATCHED` contribute nothing at all. Add a story bonus determined by the supporter's highest spokesperson training level — 0.0 with no profile, 0.0 while `UNTRAINED`, 1.0 once `TRAINED`, 3.0 once `CERTIFIED`. Take the lesser of the running total and the engagement ceiling of 50.0, and round that result half up to two decimal places, writing it to `engagement_score`. Thank-you loops and meeting leadership contribute nothing to this variant, by design: the rolling score is a statement about recent contactability, not about lifetime loyalty. Because the decay factor is selected by an integer day count, an action aged exactly 7 days still keeps 1.00 while one aged 8 days drops to 0.75, and moving the logical clock alone is enough to change the score on its next evaluation.
### 3.3. Coalition Composition, Funding Shares and Endorsement Quorum

Coalitions are how small organisations pool money and credibility, and this API treats coalition membership as a funding fact rather than a courtesy. A `CampaignDirector` adds a partner with `campaign_id`, `org_name` (at most 160 characters), a funding `tier` drawn from `ANCHOR`, `CORE` and `ALLY`, and a `branding_asset_ref` of at most 120 characters — the shared-branding asset handle the partner supplies for co-branded materials. The tier fixes the partner's `share_weight` and nothing else does: an `ANCHOR` carries weight 5, a `CORE` carries 3, an `ALLY` carries 1. A new partner's `status` is `ACTIVE` and its `active_endorsement_count` is 0. Partners can be listed filtered by `campaign_id`, `tier` and `status`, or read back per campaign as a reverse-reachability sub-resource filtered by `status`.

Adding or removing a partner re-splits the whole budget, and the **order of operations in that split is pinned** because two plausible orders give different answers. The split proceeds as follows. Collect the campaign's partners whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, ordered by `created_at` ascending, and total their `share_weight` values. If that total is zero — no active partners at all — every partner's `committed_cents` is set to 0 and the campaign's `committed_share_total_cents` is 0. Otherwise, compute each active partner's line as the campaign's `funded_total_cents` multiplied by that partner's `share_weight`, divided by the weight total, with any fraction **discarded** (floored, never rounded). Sum those floored lines and subtract the sum from `funded_total_cents`; the difference is the leftover, which is at least zero and strictly smaller than the number of active partners. Distribute that leftover one cent at a time, walking a queue of the active partners ordered by `share_weight` descending and then by `created_at` ascending, cycling back to the head of the queue if the leftover exceeds the queue length, until nothing is left. The campaign's `committed_share_total_cents` is then the sum of all active partners' `committed_cents`, and any partner whose `status` is `WITHDRAWN` has `committed_cents` forced to 0. The alternative of rounding each line half up and then summing is explicitly **not** the rule and must not be implemented: it can produce a committed total of 100,001 or 99,999 against a `funded_total_cents` of 100,000, breaking the conservation invariant. Worked through: with `funded_total_cents` of 100,000 split across one `ANCHOR`, one `CORE` and one `ALLY`, the floored lines are 55,555 plus 33,333 plus 11,111, totalling 99,999; the single leftover cent goes to the `ANCHOR` because it holds the highest `share_weight`, giving 55,556, 33,333 and 11,111. After the `ANCHOR` withdraws, the remaining two split 75,000 and 25,000 with no leftover. The sum is 100,000 in both cases.

Coalition size also fixes how much partner sign-off a piece of copy needs. A message variant's `required_endorsement_quorum` is the greater of 1 and the count of the campaign's `ACTIVE` partners multiplied by one half and rounded **up** to a whole number. Withdrawn partners are excluded from that count. So a campaign with three active partners requires 2 endorsements, and a campaign with four active partners also requires 2. The quorum is recomputed whenever a partner is added, whenever a partner withdraws, and on every read of a message variant, so it never goes stale.

Withdrawal is the coalition's most consequential single call, and it carries a `withdraw_reason`. The partner must currently be `ACTIVE`; its `status` becomes `WITHDRAWN`, its `committed_cents` is zeroed, and the funding split re-runs over the remaining active partners so that `committed_share_total_cents` still equals `funded_total_cents`. Then five cascade hops fire in order. First, every partner endorsement of this partner whose `status` is `ACTIVE` becomes `REVOKED` with `revoked_reason` recorded as "PARTNER_WITHDRAWN". Second, because those endorsements were revoked, each affected message variant has its `endorsement_count` decremented and its `required_endorsement_quorum` recomputed against the now-smaller coalition. Third, because `endorsement_count` fell below `required_endorsement_quorum` on a variant that was `LOCKED`, that variant moves from `LOCKED` to `CHANGES_REQUESTED`. Fourth, because the variant left `LOCKED`, every activation carrying it that is still in delivery state `SCHEDULED` has its `approval_state` set to `BLOCKED`. Fifth, because waves became `BLOCKED`, the campaign's `blocked_activation_count` is recomputed and the posture derivation re-runs. The partner's own `version` increments by 1. Note that the quorum recomputation in hop two cuts both ways: shrinking the coalition can *lower* a variant's quorum enough that it stays above water even after losing an endorsement.

### 3.4. Message Testing: Drafting, Review, Endorsement, Lock and Retirement

A `MessageStrategist` drafts copy as a message variant with `campaign_id`, a `test_cell` label of at most 60 characters (variants sharing a `test_cell` compete in one test — for example "ECONOMIC_FRAME"), a `headline` of at most 160 characters, a `body` of at most 2,000 characters, and a non-empty `channel_scope` array naming the channels this copy is cleared for, drawn from `EMAIL`, `SMS` and `PHONE`. A revision may point back at the copy it grew out of through `derived_from_variant_id`, which is null for an original draft and makes revision lineage walkable; variants can be listed filtered by `campaign_id`, `derived_from_variant_id`, `status`, `test_cell` and `test_winner`. A new draft lands in `status` `DRAFT` with `revision_round` of 1 and its `required_endorsement_quorum` computed from the current coalition.

Review is a cycle, not a straight line. Submitting a variant, optionally with a `submission_note`, moves it from `DRAFT` to `IN_REVIEW`, or from `CHANGES_REQUESTED` back to `IN_REVIEW` when the strategist has made the requested edits. Both edges require the owning campaign's `status` to be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_CAMPAIGN_NOT_ACTIVE`. The resubmission edge additionally requires that `revision_round` has not yet reached the ceiling of 3 revision rounds; a fourth attempt is refused with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED`, and that ceiling is what terminates the cycle. Either way the server records `submitted_by_user_id` as the `X-User-ID` of the submitting request and increments the variant's `version` by 1.

A review decision is recorded by a compliance authority with `decision`, which is either `APPROVE` or `REQUEST_CHANGES`, together with a `review_note`. The variant must be `IN_REVIEW`, and the reviewing `X-User-ID` must differ from the stored `submitted_by_user_id`, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. `APPROVE` moves the variant from `IN_REVIEW` to `APPROVED`. `REQUEST_CHANGES` moves it from `IN_REVIEW` to `CHANGES_REQUESTED` and increments `revision_round` by 1 — which is what walks the variant towards the ceiling. In both cases `reviewed_by_user_id` is set to the reviewing `X-User-ID` and the variant's `version` increments by 1.

Endorsement is how coalition partners buy into specific copy. An endorsement names `partner_id` and `message_variant_id`, and requires two explicit affirmations: `discipline_pledge`, the partner's pledge to hold message discipline, and `talking_points_ack`, the partner's acknowledgement of the shared talking points. Both must be exactly true; a false value is a malformed request. The request also carries `expected_variant_version` as described in §2.7, and the accepted endorsement stores `endorsed_variant_version` — the variant's `version` at endorsement time — so a later reader can tell whether the endorsed copy is still the current copy. A new endorsement's `status` is `ACTIVE` and its `revoked_reason` is null. Creating one increments the variant's `endorsement_count`, increments the partner's `active_endorsement_count`, and re-evaluates whether the variant now satisfies its quorum. Endorsements can be listed filtered by `partner_id`, `message_variant_id` and `status`. A partner whose `status` is `WITHDRAWN` may neither be endorsed for nor have its endorsements revoked: both are refused with `ERR_PARTNER_WITHDRAWN`.

Locking is the point at which message discipline becomes enforceable. An `APPROVED` variant moves to `LOCKED` on the single business precondition that its `endorsement_count` is greater than or equal to its `required_endorsement_quorum`; falling short is `ERR_ENDORSEMENT_QUORUM_NOT_MET`. Once `LOCKED`, the copy may not be edited, and — critically — only a `LOCKED` variant may be named by an activation. The variant's `version` increments by 1.

Reopening drags a `LOCKED` variant back to `CHANGES_REQUESTED` when the copy must change, and carries a `reopen_reason`. The operator-triggered form has three preconditions: the variant is currently `LOCKED`; `revision_round` has not reached the ceiling of 3, otherwise `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED`; and no activation carrying this variant is in delivery state `SENDING`, otherwise `ERR_ACTIVATION_IN_FLIGHT`. Scheduled waves, by contrast, do **not** block the reopen — they are cascaded instead. On success `revision_round` increments by 1 and two cascade hops fire: because the variant left `LOCKED`, every activation carrying it whose delivery state is `SCHEDULED` and whose `approval_state` is `PENDING` or `CLEARED` has its `approval_state` set to `BLOCKED`; and because those waves became `BLOCKED`, the campaign's `blocked_activation_count` is recomputed and the posture derivation re-runs. The variant's `version` increments by 1, which invalidates any `expected_variant_version` a partner delegate is already holding. The same `LOCKED` to `CHANGES_REQUESTED` edge also fires without any operator request whenever `endorsement_count` drops below `required_endorsement_quorum`; in that system-triggered form the revision ceiling and the in-flight check do not gate the move, because the coalition, not the strategist, forced it.

Revoking an endorsement carries a `revoked_reason` and requires the endorsement to be `ACTIVE`. In the operator-triggered form the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the endorsement's `partner_id`, otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. The endorsement's `status` becomes `REVOKED`, the `revoked_reason` is stored, and the partner's `active_endorsement_count` is decremented. Then four hops: the variant's `endorsement_count` is decremented, bumping the variant's `version`; because `endorsement_count` fell below `required_endorsement_quorum` while the variant was `LOCKED`, the variant moves from `LOCKED` to `CHANGES_REQUESTED`; because the variant left `LOCKED`, every activation carrying it in delivery state `SCHEDULED` has its `approval_state` set to `BLOCKED`; and because waves became `BLOCKED`, the campaign's `blocked_activation_count` is recomputed and the posture derivation re-runs. The endorsement's own `version` increments by 1. The identical `ACTIVE` to `REVOKED` edge is also driven by the system as the first cascade hop of a partner withdrawal, where the ownership check does not apply.

Resolving a message test is how a campaign converges on one piece of copy. A `MessageStrategist` names the `test_cell` being resolved and the `winning_message_variant_id`. The named winner must be `LOCKED` and must carry a `realized_reach_total` of at least the minimum test reach of 3; falling short of that reach floor is `ERR_REACH_BELOW_MINIMUM`. If no variant in the named `test_cell` is `LOCKED`, or if the named winner does not belong to that `test_cell`, the resolution is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_VARIANT`. On success the test metrics are recomputed for every variant in the cell, `test_winner` becomes true on the winner and false on every other variant of the cell, and every losing variant whose `status` was `APPROVED` or `LOCKED` is `RETIRED`. Both retirement edges — from `APPROVED` and from `LOCKED` — carry the same two cascade hops: because the variant retired, every activation carrying it in delivery state `SCHEDULED` has its `approval_state` set to `BLOCKED`; and because those waves became `BLOCKED`, the campaign's `blocked_activation_count` is recomputed and the posture derivation re-runs. Each retired variant's `version` increments by 1. `RETIRED` is terminal: a retired loser can never be resubmitted, relocked or endorsed again. Message discipline retires the losing copy even when it was locked and already running in market — that is the whole point of resolving a test — while its `ACTIVE` endorsements are deliberately left intact for the audit trail.

The per-variant test metrics themselves are aggregates over that variant's completed waves. Collect the variant's activations whose delivery state is `SENT` or `PARTIAL`, excluding anything still `SCHEDULED` or `SENDING` and anything `ABORTED`. `realized_reach_total` is the sum of those waves' `realized_reach`. `send_cost_total_cents` is the sum of their `send_cost_cents`. `attributed_action_count` is the number of action records whose `activation_id` names one of those waves and whose `status` is `MATCHED`, `ACKNOWLEDGED` or `THANKED` — `DISPUTED`, `VOIDED` and `UNMATCHED` rows are excluded. `response_rate_bps` is 0 when `realized_reach_total` is 0; otherwise it is `attributed_action_count` multiplied by 10,000, divided by `realized_reach_total`, and rounded half up to a whole number of basis points. `cost_per_action_cents` is the sentinel value 999999 when `attributed_action_count` is 0; otherwise it is `send_cost_total_cents` divided by `attributed_action_count` with any fraction discarded — floored, never rounded. The sentinel replaces the undefined division rather than a null, so a completed wave that delivered nothing at all contributes reach 0 and cost 0, leaves `response_rate_bps` at 0, leaves `cost_per_action_cents` at the sentinel, and attempts no division. These metrics are recomputed at wave completion and again at test resolution.
### 3.5. Segmentation and Scheduling a Send Wave

Segments are stored rules with a materialised answer. Defining one takes `campaign_id`, a `name` of at most 120 characters, and a `rule_required_channel` drawn from `EMAIL`, `SMS` and `PHONE` — the channel whose consent state a supporter must hold as `GRANTED` to be a member. Three further rules are optional and each has a permissive default. `rule_district_codes` is a district allow-list defaulting to the empty array, and an empty array means every district qualifies. `rule_min_engagement_score` is an **inclusive** floor on the supporter's rolling engagement score, defaulting to 0.00, so a supporter qualifies when their `engagement_score` is greater than or equal to it. `rule_sources` is an acquisition-source allow-list drawn from `PETITION`, `EVENT`, `DIGITAL_AD` and `REFERRAL`, also defaulting to the empty array with the same "empty means all" reading. Segments can be listed filtered by `campaign_id` and `rule_required_channel`.

Membership is computed, not curated. A supporter of the same campaign is a member exactly when all five conditions hold: their resolved consent state for the segment's `rule_required_channel` is `GRANTED`; their `suppressed` flag is false; either `rule_district_codes` is empty or their `district_code` appears in it; their `engagement_score` is at least `rule_min_engagement_score`; and either `rule_sources` is empty or their `acquisition_source` appears in it. A supporter whose consent state on the required channel is `NONE` or `REVOKED` is excluded. The comparison always uses the rolling engagement variant and never the campaign-final retention variant. The qualifying supporters' identifiers are written to `member_supporter_ids` ordered by the supporters' `created_at` ascending, and `member_count` is simply the length of that list. A segment also publishes `active_activation_count`, the number of activations referencing it whose delivery state is `SCHEDULED` or `SENDING`, and `last_refreshed_at`, which is null until the segment is first refreshed and thereafter holds the logical-clock instant of the most recent refresh.

Membership is recomputed on segment creation, on an explicit refresh, and as a cascade hop of any mutation that changes a supporter's consent states, `engagement_score`, `suppressed` flag or spokesperson training level. Refreshing a segment re-materialises `member_supporter_ids` and `member_count` against current consent and engagement data, stamps `last_refreshed_at` from the logical clock, and increments the segment's `version`. Every change to `member_count` or to `active_activation_count` likewise bumps the segment's `version`, and that version is exactly what the audience-pinning guard of §2.7 observes.

Scheduling a wave is the densest write in the system, because it reads seven things at once. The payload names `campaign_id`, the audience through `segment_id`, the copy through `message_variant_id`, the delivery `channel` (one of `EMAIL`, `SMS`, `PHONE`), the send window through `window_start` and `window_end`, and the audience version through `expected_segment_version`. Two further inputs are optional: `milestone_id` binds the wave to a legislative-calendar anchor and is the only way to unlock the frequency surge grant, and `min_viable_reach` sets the minimum projected reach the wave must retain to stay approvable, defaulting to 1 and never accepted below 1. Unlike milestone windows, an activation's send window is a **closed** interval: both `window_start` and `window_end` are included, and channel-saturation arithmetic treats it that way.

Before reach can be sized, the wave's touch ceiling is pinned, and its evaluation order is fixed because the dependency would otherwise look circular — the surge grant reads the campaign's posture while this very request may change that posture through the blocking and reach cascade. The five steps run exactly once, in this order, and step two is never re-evaluated afterwards. **Step one**: take the campaign's `posture` as it stands persisted *before* this request. **Step two**: determine the surge grant. It is 2 when a `milestone_id` was supplied, that milestone's `kind` is `COMMITTEE_HEARING` or `FLOOR_VOTE`, the milestone's `window_start` is at or before the wave's `window_start`, the wave's `window_end` is at or before the milestone's `window_end`, and the pre-request posture was `OPEN`. It is 1 when those same milestone conditions hold but the pre-request posture was `RESTRICTED`. It is 0 in every other case — no `milestone_id`, the wrong milestone `kind`, a milestone window that does not fully contain the send window, or any other posture. **Step three**: `effective_touch_cap` is the base allowance of 3 touches per supporter plus that surge grant, and it is persisted on the wave as an audit snapshot. **Step four**: reach is computed against that cap and `projected_reach` is written. **Step five**: only now is the campaign posture recomputed; the surge grant is not recalculated against the new posture.

Projected reach is a forward-looking statement about who the wave *may* touch. Resolve the segment's `member_supporter_ids` snapshot to supporter rows in `created_at` ascending order, and keep each supporter for whom all three conditions hold: their resolved consent state for the wave's `channel` is `GRANTED`; their `suppressed` flag is false; and the sum of their `touches_consumed` and `touches_reserved` is **strictly less than** the wave's `effective_touch_cap` — a supporter sitting exactly at the cap is excluded. The surviving identifiers become `target_supporter_ids` in that same order, `projected_reach` is the length of that list, and every one of those supporters has `touches_reserved` incremented by 1, taking the reservation that later paths must compensate. The ordering matters beyond aesthetics: it fixes which supporters the channel vendor's delivery tally will later be applied to.

Creating a wave also increments the segment's `active_activation_count` (bumping the segment's `version`) and lands the wave on both of its independent state dimensions: `approval_state` `PENDING` and `delivery_state` `SCHEDULED`. Those two dimensions advance on separate triggers and neither is derivable from the other. Beyond the capacity floors of rung 24, three earlier rungs bite hard here: a send window intersecting any `RECESS` milestone of the campaign is `ERR_BLACKOUT_WINDOW`; a third wave holding an overlapping closed send window on the same campaign-and-channel pairing while two others are `SCHEDULED` or `SENDING` is `ERR_CHANNEL_WINDOW_SATURATED`; and a `channel` absent from the named variant's `channel_scope` is `ERR_CHANNEL_NOT_IN_VARIANT_SCOPE`. A variant that is not `LOCKED` is `ERR_VARIANT_NOT_LOCKED`. Activations can be listed filtered by `campaign_id`, `segment_id`, `message_variant_id`, `milestone_id`, `channel`, `approval_state` and `delivery_state`.

The approval dimension is compliance's lane. A `ComplianceOfficer` clears a wave with a `clearance_note`, moving `approval_state` from `PENDING` to `CLEARED`. Seven preconditions apply: the wave is currently `PENDING`; its `delivery_state` is `SCHEDULED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`; the campaign's posture is not `FROZEN`, otherwise `ERR_CAMPAIGN_FROZEN`; the campaign's posture is not `RESTRICTED` when the wave's `channel` is `SMS` or `PHONE`, otherwise `ERR_CAMPAIGN_RESTRICTED`; the named variant's `status` is `LOCKED`, otherwise `ERR_VARIANT_NOT_LOCKED`; the wave's window does not intersect any `RECESS` milestone of the campaign, otherwise `ERR_BLACKOUT_WINDOW`; and the wave's `projected_reach` is greater than or equal to its `min_viable_reach`, otherwise `ERR_REACH_BELOW_MINIMUM`. Clearance increments the wave's `version` by 1.

The same clearance call is also the re-clearance path after remediation, moving `approval_state` from `BLOCKED` back to `CLEARED`. That edge requires the wave to be currently `BLOCKED`, its `delivery_state` to be `SCHEDULED`, and every precondition of the `PENDING` to `CLEARED` edge to hold again. It carries one extra side effect: the campaign's `blocked_activation_count` is decremented by 1 and the posture derivation re-runs, so a campaign sitting at `RESTRICTED` can fall back to `OPEN` and one sitting at `FROZEN` can fall back to `RESTRICTED` purely by remediating a wave. `CLEARED` to `BLOCKED` to `CLEARED` is reachable within one wave's life.

A `ComplianceOfficer` may also block a wave outright with a `block_reason`, setting `approval_state` to `BLOCKED`, incrementing the campaign's `blocked_activation_count` and re-running the posture derivation — two blocked waves on one campaign are what freeze it. The system drives the same `BLOCKED` outcome without an operator request, from either `PENDING` or `CLEARED`, as the cascade tail of consent revocation, segment shrinkage, milestone rescheduling, variant reopen, variant retirement or partner withdrawal. Both system-driven edges share identical preconditions — the wave's `delivery_state` is `SCHEDULED`, and either the recomputed `projected_reach` has fallen below `min_viable_reach`, or the variant has left `LOCKED`, or the window now intersects a `RECESS` milestone window — and identical side effects: `approval_state` becomes `BLOCKED`, `blocked_activation_count` increments by 1, the posture derivation re-runs, and the wave's `version` increments by 1. A wave that had already been cleared loses its clearance retroactively; there is no grandfathering.

### 3.6. Delivery Execution: Start, Complete, Abort

Starting a send moves `delivery_state` from `SCHEDULED` to `SENDING` and has five preconditions: the wave is currently `SCHEDULED`; its `approval_state` is `CLEARED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`; the campaign's posture is not `FROZEN`, otherwise `ERR_CAMPAIGN_FROZEN`; the campaign's posture is not `RESTRICTED` when the wave's `channel` is `SMS` or `PHONE`, otherwise `ERR_CAMPAIGN_RESTRICTED`; and the logical clock sits inside the wave's closed send window, at or after `window_start` and at or before `window_end`, otherwise `ERR_OUTSIDE_SEND_WINDOW`. The decisive side effect is that `target_supporter_ids` is **frozen** from this point: cascades recompute the target list only while a wave is `SCHEDULED`, so a consent revocation arriving after the send starts no longer changes who was targeted. The wave's `version` increments by 1.

Completion is where the channel vendor's numbers land. The request supplies `delivered_count`, the number of touches the vendor reported as delivered, and `bounced_count`, the number it reported as bounced. Both edges out of `SENDING` share one arithmetic precondition: `delivered_count` plus `bounced_count` must equal the length of `target_supporter_ids`, otherwise `ERR_DELIVERY_TALLY_MISMATCH`. The branch is then decided by the tally itself. When `bounced_count` is 0 **and** `delivered_count` equals the length of `target_supporter_ids`, `delivery_state` becomes `SENT`. When `bounced_count` is greater than 0 **or** `delivered_count` is below the length of `target_supporter_ids`, `delivery_state` becomes `PARTIAL`. A completion reporting `delivered_count` of 0 with `bounced_count` equal to the full target length is the zero-delivery boundary: the wave lands in `PARTIAL`, its `realized_reach` is 0 and its `send_cost_cents` is 0, and the operation still succeeds.

Both completion edges write exactly the same ledger, cost, reach and metric side effects. Realized reach is the **chronological** variant of the reach calculation and is a much narrower question than projected reach: it counts only touches that both landed and are still lawful at completion. Take the first `delivered_count` entries of `target_supporter_ids` in the stored `created_at` ascending order — that ordered prefix is the delivered set — and count how many of those supporters still have `GRANTED` consent on the wave's `channel` and a `suppressed` flag of false at the moment of completion. That count is `realized_reach`. The difference between `delivered_count` and `realized_reach` is written to `suppressed_by_consent_count`. `send_cost_cents` is the channel's unit cost multiplied by `delivered_count`, where the unit cost is 2 cents for `EMAIL`, 15 cents for `SMS` and 60 cents for `PHONE` — note that the vendor bills for what it delivered, not for what was lawful, so cost uses `delivered_count` while reach does not. Identical wave data therefore yields a `projected_reach` of 4 and a `realized_reach` of 3 when one targeted supporter revokes the channel between send start and completion.

The touch ledger settles in two halves. For each of the first `delivered_count` supporters of `target_supporter_ids` in `created_at` ascending order, `touches_consumed` is incremented by 1 and `touches_reserved` is decremented by 1 — the reservation converts into consumption. For every remaining targeted supporter, `touches_reserved` is decremented by 1 with no consumption at all, because no touch reached them. The segment's `active_activation_count` is decremented by 1, bumping the segment's `version`. Finally the carrying variant's `realized_reach_total`, `send_cost_total_cents`, `response_rate_bps` and `cost_per_action_cents` are recomputed, and the wave's `version` increments by 1.

Aborting a wave carries an `abort_reason` and is legal from either `SCHEDULED` or `SENDING`. From `SCHEDULED` the compensation is simple: `delivery_state` becomes `ABORTED`, the `abort_reason` is stored, every supporter listed in `target_supporter_ids` has `touches_reserved` decremented by 1 — the exact compensating action of the reservations taken at creation — and the segment's `active_activation_count` is decremented by 1, bumping the segment's `version`. If the wave's `approval_state` was `BLOCKED`, the campaign's `blocked_activation_count` is decremented by 1 and the posture derivation re-runs. The wave's `version` increments by 1.

Aborting from `SENDING` is the deepest compensating cascade in the system, because actions may already have been logged against the wave. `delivery_state` becomes `ABORTED` and the `abort_reason` is stored, then five hops fire in order. First, every supporter in `target_supporter_ids` has `touches_reserved` decremented by 1, restoring the touch ledger to its pre-creation value. Second, because the wave became `ABORTED`, every action record whose `activation_id` names this wave and whose `status` is `RECORDED`, `MATCHED` or `ACKNOWLEDGED` becomes `VOIDED`; records already in `THANKED` are deliberately left untouched, because the thank-you was a real touch that really happened. Third, because those action records became `VOIDED`, each referenced legislator has `constituent_action_count` and `contact_pressure_score` recomputed. Fourth, because a recomputed `contact_pressure_score` fell below the primary-tier threshold of 60.00, that legislator's `priority_tier` is downgraded. Fifth, because a legislator left `PRIMARY`, the campaign's `opposed_priority_legislator_count` is recomputed and the posture derivation re-runs. The segment's `active_activation_count` is decremented by 1 (bumping the segment's `version`), `engagement_score` and `retention_score` are recomputed for every supporter whose action records were voided, and the wave's `version` increments by 1. This is the observable proof of the touch-reservation conservation invariant: after an abort, the ledger reads exactly as it did before the wave existed.
### 3.7. Constituent Action, District Matching and the Thank-You Loop

Logging a constituent action is a single request that does three things atomically: it stamps the calendar pressure of the moment, it matches the constituent to their own legislator, and it re-scores everything downstream. The payload is small — `supporter_id`, the `channel` the constituent used to contact the office (`EMAIL`, `SMS` or `PHONE`), and an optional `activation_id` naming the wave that prompted the action, which is null for an organic action. Records are created in `status` `RECORDED`, but `RECORDED` is resolved to either `MATCHED` or `UNMATCHED` **inside the creating request**, so no caller ever observes a persisted `RECORDED` row. Action records can be listed filtered by `supporter_id`, `activation_id`, `legislator_id`, `channel` and `status`.

The multiplier is stamped first, before matching runs. Gather the milestones of the supporter's campaign whose half-open window contains the current logical clock — at or after `window_start` and strictly before `window_end`. If that set is empty, the record's `pressure_multiplier` is 1.0. Otherwise the strongest window wins: sort the containing milestones by `pressure_multiplier` descending and then by `created_at` ascending, and take the first one's `pressure_multiplier` — 1.5 inside a `COMMITTEE_HEARING`, 2.0 inside a `FLOOR_VOTE`, 0.5 inside a `RECESS`, 1.0 inside a `SIGNING`. The snapshot is written once and never recomputed, so a later reschedule does not retroactively rescore existing actions.

Matching runs immediately after. The eligible pool is every legislator whose `campaign_id` equals the supporter's `campaign_id` and whose `district_code` equals the supporter's `district_code`; no stance filter and no tier filter applies. If that pool is empty, the record's `status` becomes `UNMATCHED` and its `legislator_id` stays null — this is the selector's empty-pool fallback, the request still succeeds with a 201, and `UNMATCHED` is terminal. An unmatched action changes nothing else at all: no pressure, engagement or campaign counter moves, because the row exists purely for audit. If the pool is non-empty, candidates are ranked on three keys in order. The first key is a chamber preference: a candidate ranks ahead when its `chamber` equals the campaign's `target_chamber`, and when the campaign's `target_chamber` is `BOTH` the preferred chamber is `HOUSE`, so a `HOUSE` member ranks ahead of a `SENATE` member. The second key is the candidate's current `contact_pressure_score`, **ascending** — the selector deliberately spreads pressure by preferring the least-contacted office. The third key is `created_at` ascending, and it is the final tie-break; ties are never broken on `id`. The winner's identifier is written to `legislator_id` and the record's `status` becomes `MATCHED`.

A match then propagates. The matched legislator's `constituent_action_count` is incremented by 1 and their `contact_pressure_score` and `priority_tier` are recomputed. The supporter's `action_count` is incremented by 1 and their `engagement_score` and `retention_score` are recomputed. The campaign's `total_actions_logged`, `distinct_acting_supporter_count` and `opposed_priority_legislator_count` are recomputed and the posture derivation re-runs.

The district pressure index is the whip-count arithmetic at the heart of the campaign, and it takes five inputs with two nested caps and a branch on stance. Start by collecting the action records pointing at this legislator whose `status` is `MATCHED`, `ACKNOWLEDGED` or `THANKED`; `UNMATCHED`, `DISPUTED` and `VOIDED` rows are excluded entirely. For each counted record, multiply the channel pressure weight of its `channel` — 1.0 for `EMAIL`, 1.5 for `SMS`, 3.0 for `PHONE` — by that record's stamped `pressure_multiplier`, and sum those products without rounding any of them. Multiply that sum by the action pressure factor of 2.0, and take the lesser of the result and the action cap of 60.0; that is the action component. Separately, multiply the meeting pressure credit of 12.0 by the legislator's `meeting_held_count` and take the lesser of that and the meeting cap of 24.0; that is the meeting component — so a legislator's third and later held meetings add nothing. Add a stance bonus determined by the legislator's current `stance`: 0.0 for `SUPPORT`, 10.0 for `LEAN_SUPPORT`, 10.0 for `UNDECIDED`, 4.0 for `LEAN_OPPOSE` and 0.0 for `OPPOSE` — the bonus is highest for the persuadable middle, which is exactly where a campaign should spend pressure. Sum the action component, the meeting component and the stance bonus, take the lesser of that total and the overall ceiling of 100.0, and round the result half up to two decimal places, writing it to `contact_pressure_score`. Rounding happens exactly once, after both caps and the bonus; the per-action products are never rounded. Finally `priority_tier` is re-derived from the rounded score: `PRIMARY` when the score is at least 60.00, `SECONDARY` when it is at least 30.00 but below 60.00, and `MONITOR` otherwise. And `constituent_action_count` is set to the number of counted records. Worked through: a legislator whose `stance` is `UNDECIDED` with four `PHONE` actions each stamped with a `FLOOR_VOTE` multiplier of 2.0 and one `EMAIL` action stamped 1.0 has a weighted sum of 25.0, an action component of 50.0 (below the 60.0 cap), no meeting component, and a stance bonus of 10.0 — a score of exactly 60.00, which lands on `PRIMARY`. Remove the `EMAIL` action and the score is 58.00, which is `SECONDARY`.

The thank-you loop is a two-step ladder on the action record. A `FieldOrganizer` first records the legislator office's acknowledgement, optionally with an `acknowledgement_note`, moving the record from `MATCHED` to `ACKNOWLEDGED` and stamping `acknowledged_at` from the logical clock; the record must be `MATCHED` and the campaign's posture must not be `FROZEN`, otherwise `ERR_CAMPAIGN_FROZEN`. The record's `version` increments by 1.

Thanking then closes the loop, moving the record from `ACKNOWLEDGED` to `THANKED`, and it is the most heavily guarded touch in the system because it reaches out to a real person. The payload names `thank_channel`, the channel used for the thank-you touch, drawn from `EMAIL`, `SMS` and `PHONE`. Five preconditions apply in order: the record's `status` is `ACKNOWLEDGED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`; the campaign's posture is not `FROZEN`, otherwise `ERR_CAMPAIGN_FROZEN`; the supporter's `suppressed` flag is false, otherwise `ERR_SUPPORTER_SUPPRESSED`; the supporter's resolved consent state for `thank_channel` is `GRANTED`, otherwise `ERR_CONSENT_MISSING`; and the sum of the supporter's `touches_consumed` and `touches_reserved` is strictly below the base allowance of 3 touches, otherwise `ERR_FREQUENCY_CAP_EXHAUSTED`. Note that the thank-you touch is measured against the *base* allowance, never against any wave's surged `effective_touch_cap`. On success `thank_channel` and `thanked_at` are stored, the supporter's `touches_consumed` is incremented by 1 — a thank-you is a governed touch, consumed without a prior reservation — the supporter's `thanked_count` is incremented, and the supporter's `retention_score` is recomputed while `engagement_score` is left unchanged, because a thank-you is not a constituent action. The record's `version` increments by 1. `THANKED` is terminal.

Disputes are how a campaign keeps its own numbers honest — a duplicate submission, a mis-districted contact, a supporter who says they never called. A `dispute_reason` is supplied and the record moves to `DISPUTED` from either `MATCHED` or `ACKNOWLEDGED`. Both edges carry identical consequences: the row leaves the counted input set, so the legislator's `constituent_action_count` and `contact_pressure_score` are recomputed and `priority_tier` re-derived; the supporter's `action_count`, `engagement_score` and `retention_score` are recomputed; the campaign counters are recomputed and the posture derivation re-runs; and the record's `version` increments by 1. On the `ACKNOWLEDGED` to `DISPUTED` edge, `acknowledged_at` is preserved.

Resolving a dispute supplies an `outcome`, either `REINSTATED` or `VOIDED`, together with a `resolution_note`, and the resolving `X-User-ID` may not be the `X-User-ID` that filed the record. `REINSTATED` returns the record from `DISPUTED` to `MATCHED`, closing the cycle: the original `pressure_multiplier` and `legislator_id` are intact, `acknowledged_at` (if any) is preserved even though the record returns to `MATCHED` rather than to `ACKNOWLEDGED`, the row re-enters the pressure input set so the legislator's counters and `contact_pressure_score` are recomputed to exactly their pre-dispute values and `priority_tier` is re-derived, and the supporter's `action_count`, `engagement_score` and `retention_score` are recomputed. The `MATCHED` to `DISPUTED` to `MATCHED` round trip must leave `contact_pressure_score` bit-identical — that is the pressure-conservation invariant. `VOIDED` instead makes the record terminally `VOIDED`, with the legislator, supporter and campaign counters recomputed with the record permanently excluded. Either way the record's `version` increments by 1.

The system also drives records to `VOIDED` without any dispute, from either `MATCHED` or `ACKNOWLEDGED`, as the second cascade hop of an activation abort or a campaign close. Both of those system edges have the same precondition — the parent activation has just become `ABORTED` — and the same effects: `status` becomes `VOIDED` and the legislator, supporter and campaign counters are recomputed.

### 3.8. Story Collection, Spokesperson Training and In-District Meetings

Personal stories are what turn a whip count into a movement, and they are also the most consent-sensitive asset a campaign holds. Opening a spokesperson profile takes `supporter_id` and a `story_summary` of at most 1,000 characters — the collected personal story used in media work and legislator meetings — plus an optional `publication_consent` stating how far the story may travel, drawn from `WITHHELD`, `INTERNAL` and `PUBLIC` and defaulting to `WITHHELD`. Intake is contactability-gated: the supporter must not be `suppressed`, and because the intake addresses no particular channel, at least one of `EMAIL`, `SMS` or `PHONE` must be `GRANTED` or the rejection is `ERR_CONSENT_MISSING`. Only one profile may exist per supporter-and-campaign pairing; a second is `ERR_DUPLICATE_SPOKESPERSON`. A new profile starts `UNTRAINED`, with `training_sessions_completed` at 0 and `media_ready` false. Profiles can be listed filtered by `supporter_id`, `training_state`, `publication_consent` and `media_ready`, or walked as a supporter's own sub-resource returning zero or one row.

Training is a two-rung ladder advanced by naming a `to_level` together with a `session_reference` documenting the session. Advancing to `TRAINED` requires the profile to be currently `UNTRAINED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`; on success `training_state` becomes `TRAINED`, `training_sessions_completed` increments by 1, the supporter's `engagement_score` and `retention_score` are recomputed because the story bonus rises from 0.0 to 1.0, every segment whose membership predicate the supporter now satisfies or stops satisfying is recomputed (bumping each affected segment's `version`), and the profile's `version` increments by 1. Advancing to `CERTIFIED` requires the profile to be currently `TRAINED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, and additionally requires `publication_consent` to be exactly `PUBLIC`, otherwise `ERR_STORY_CONSENT_REQUIRED` — a campaign may not certify a spokesperson whose story it is not allowed to publish. On success `training_state` becomes `CERTIFIED`, `training_sessions_completed` increments by 1, `media_ready` becomes true, the supporter's `engagement_score` and `retention_score` are recomputed because the story bonus rises from 1.0 to 3.0, affected segments are recomputed (bumping their `version`), and the profile's `version` increments by 1. `media_ready` is true exactly when `training_state` is `CERTIFIED` **and** `publication_consent` is `PUBLIC`.

In-district meetings put a certified constituent spokesperson in front of the office. Requesting one takes `campaign_id`, `legislator_id`, `lead_spokesperson_id`, a `location_district_code` of at most 16 characters naming the district the meeting is held in, and the window bounds `window_start` and `window_end`. Meeting windows are half-open — `window_start` included, `window_end` excluded — and overlap arithmetic is performed per legislator with a travel buffer of 30 minutes applied to **both sides** of each existing interval, so a request colliding with an existing `REQUESTED`, `CONFIRMED` or `HELD` meeting for the same legislator once that buffer is applied is refused with `ERR_MEETING_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Two agreement rules also bite: `location_district_code` must equal the legislator's `district_code`, and the lead spokesperson's supporter must live in that same district, with either mismatch reported as `ERR_DISTRICT_MISMATCH`. The lead spokesperson must already be `CERTIFIED`, otherwise `ERR_SPOKESPERSON_NOT_CERTIFIED`, and the request is contactability-gated on the supporter's `EMAIL` channel. A new meeting lands in `status` `REQUESTED` with `pressure_credit` of 0.00 and `decline_reason` null. Meetings can be listed filtered by `campaign_id`, `legislator_id`, `lead_spokesperson_id`, `location_district_code` and `status`.

The office's answer is recorded as a `decision` of either `CONFIRM` or `DECLINE`, with an optional `decline_reason`. Confirming moves the meeting from `REQUESTED` to `CONFIRMED` and has three preconditions: the meeting is currently `REQUESTED`; the lead spokesperson's `training_state` is `CERTIFIED`, otherwise `ERR_SPOKESPERSON_NOT_CERTIFIED`; and the campaign's `status` is `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_CAMPAIGN_NOT_ACTIVE`. The certification check is deliberately re-run here rather than trusted from request time — a profile that was merely `TRAINED` when the request was accepted cannot be confirmed. Declining moves the meeting from `REQUESTED` to `DECLINED`, stores the `decline_reason`, and releases the legislator's calendar interval so a later meeting may occupy the window, because `DECLINED` rows leave the overlap input set. Either edge increments the meeting's `version` by 1.

Closing a confirmed meeting supplies `attendee_count`, the number of constituents who actually attended, and it branches on zero. Both edges require the meeting to be currently `CONFIRMED` and require the logical clock to be at or after the meeting's `window_end`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` — a meeting cannot be reported before it has ended. An `attendee_count` of 1 or more moves the meeting to `HELD`: the count is stored, `pressure_credit` is set to the meeting pressure credit of 12.00, the legislator's `meeting_held_count` is incremented by 1 and their `contact_pressure_score` and `priority_tier` are recomputed, the campaign's `opposed_priority_legislator_count` is recomputed and the posture derivation re-runs, and the lead spokesperson's supporter has their `retention_score` recomputed because the meeting-lead bonus applies to the campaign-final variant only. An `attendee_count` of exactly 0 instead moves the meeting to `NO_SHOW`: `attendee_count` is stored as 0, `pressure_credit` stays 0.00, and **no** legislator or supporter counter changes at all. Either edge increments the meeting's `version` by 1. `DECLINED`, `HELD` and `NO_SHOW` are all terminal.
### 3.9. Closing the Campaign, Retention Scoring and Outcome Analysis

Closing is the campaign-wrap trigger: it flushes in-flight work, freezes the posture and finalises retention scoring for outcome analysis. A `CampaignDirector` closes an `ACTIVE` campaign under two preconditions — the campaign's `status` is `ACTIVE`, and no activation of the campaign is in delivery state `SENDING`, otherwise `ERR_ACTIVATION_IN_FLIGHT`. A wave that has genuinely started sending must be completed or aborted first; the product will not let a close orphan a send in progress.

On success `status` becomes `CLOSED` and a wide cascade fires. Every activation of the campaign still in delivery state `SCHEDULED` becomes `ABORTED` with its `abort_reason` recorded as "CAMPAIGN_CLOSED". Because those activations left `SCHEDULED`, each supporter listed in their `target_supporter_ids` has `touches_reserved` decremented by 1 for each aborted activation they appeared in, and `active_activation_count` is decremented on each of their segments, bumping those segments' `version`. Because those activations became `ABORTED`, every action record whose `activation_id` names one of them and whose `status` is `RECORDED`, `MATCHED` or `ACKNOWLEDGED` becomes `VOIDED`, while records already in `THANKED` are preserved. Because those action records became `VOIDED`, `contact_pressure_score` and `priority_tier` are recomputed for each affected legislator. Separately, every district meeting of the campaign still in `status` `REQUESTED` becomes `DECLINED` with its `decline_reason` recorded as "CAMPAIGN_CLOSED". The campaign-final `retention_score` is then written for every supporter of the campaign. Finally the campaign's `posture` is set to `FROZEN` — a `CLOSED` campaign is `FROZEN` even when `posture_override` is `FORCE_OPEN` — and the campaign's `version` increments by 1. `CLOSED` is terminal.

The campaign-final **retention** score answers a different question from the rolling engagement score, and it deliberately gives a different answer over identical rows. It is computed once, at close, and written to `retention_score`. Start from the same acquisition-source bonus for the supporter's `acquisition_source` (`PETITION` 1.0, `EVENT` 2.0, `DIGITAL_AD` 0.5, `REFERRAL` 1.5). Then, for each of that supporter's action records whose `status` is `MATCHED`, `ACKNOWLEDGED` or `THANKED`, add the channel pressure weight of that record's `channel` — 1.0 for `EMAIL`, 1.5 for `SMS`, 3.0 for `PHONE` — with **no** age decay at all: every action counts at full weight regardless of how old it is. Add 2.0 for each of that supporter's action records in `status` `THANKED`, and add 5.0 for each district meeting in `status` `HELD` whose `lead_spokesperson_id` resolves to a spokesperson profile of this supporter. Add the same story bonus keyed to the supporter's highest spokesperson training level — 0.0 with no profile, 0.0 while `UNTRAINED`, 1.0 once `TRAINED`, 3.0 once `CERTIFIED`. Take the lesser of the running total and the retention ceiling of 80.0, and round the result half up to two decimal places. Worked through against the rolling variant: an `EVENT`-sourced supporter with one `PHONE` action and one `EMAIL` action, both aged 46 days, with no story training, no thank-yous and no meetings, has an `engagement_score` of 2.0 plus 3.0 times 0.50 plus 1.0 times 0.50, which is 4.00, while their `retention_score` is 2.0 plus 3.0 plus 1.0, which is 6.00. Same rows, different answer, because the decay applies to one variant and not to the other.

The outcome report is a computed read available to a `CampaignDirector` or a `ComplianceOfficer`, and it is also recomputed as part of the close. It reports thirteen values. `supporter_total` is the number of supporters of the campaign. `consented_reachable_total` is the number of those supporters whose `suppressed` flag is false. `action_total` is the number of the campaign's action records whose `status` is `MATCHED`, `ACKNOWLEDGED` or `THANKED`; `UNMATCHED`, `DISPUTED` and `VOIDED` rows are excluded. `thanked_total` is the number whose `status` is `THANKED`. `thank_loop_closure_bps` is 0 when `action_total` is 0, and otherwise `thanked_total` multiplied by 10,000, divided by `action_total`, rounded half up to a whole number of basis points. `primary_flip_count` is the number of the campaign's legislators whose `priority_tier` is `PRIMARY` **and** whose `stance` is `SUPPORT` or `LEAN_SUPPORT` — the mirror image of the opposed count that drives the posture, and the reason `initial_stance` is retained for comparison. `retained_supporter_count` is the number of supporters whose `retention_score` is at least 10.00. `committed_share_total_cents` and `funded_total_cents` are reported side by side, and `coalition_funding_balanced` is the exact equality of those two integers. `touch_reservation_total` is the sum of `touches_reserved` across the campaign's supporters, `open_wave_projected_reach_total` is the sum of `projected_reach` across the campaign's activations whose delivery state is `SCHEDULED` or `SENDING`, and `touch_ledger_balanced` is the exact equality of those two integers. Both reconciliation flags are exact integer equality checks, never tolerances.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Unless a row's description says otherwise, a write returns the created or updated resource in `data`, and a list returns an array of resources together with the four pagination fields in `meta`.
