# Inventory Accuracy & Item-Level Tagging Program API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Retail inventory integrity teams live with a permanent gap between what the book says a store holds and what is physically on the shelf. That gap is not a single number; it is the accumulated residue of tags that were never encoded correctly at source, cycle counts taken with the wrong reader technology, variances nobody investigated, on-hand corrections posted without authority, negative and phantom quantities that no one ever swept, and vendor shortages that were absorbed rather than charged back. This API is the system of record for closing that gap. It governs item-level tag encoding and receipt verification, category-driven cycle-count scheduling with handheld and overhead reader coverage, count-to-system variance investigation with root-cause categorization, on-hand adjustment authority and approval thresholds, automatic detection and correction of negative and phantom inventory, shrink measurement separated into theft, damage, process error and vendor shortage, and the annual physical inventory performed by third-party counters and reconciled to book value.

Two design commitments run through every rule that follows. The first is that inventory accuracy has consequences: a position whose accuracy has decayed, whose tags have failed verification, or whose variance is still under investigation is progressively withdrawn from omnichannel promising, and a store that accumulates enough such positions loses its store-fulfillment eligibility and has its count calendar accelerated. The second is segregation of duties. The analyst who proposes a root cause may not approve it; the operator who requests an on-hand adjustment may not approve, reject or reverse it. Both rules are enforced on the acting user identity, not merely on the acting role, because a single person holding two roles is exactly the audit finding the program exists to prevent.

The API is mounted under `/inventory-integrity`, plus a single administrative logical-clock surface at `/_clock/set_current_utc`. Every entity in the domain is independently addressable by its own identifier; the small number of nested creation paths exist to supply context for the resource being created, never to imply that a child can only be read through its parent. Money is carried in integer cents throughout, rates and scores in integer basis points, and quantities in whole units. Where the document pins an evaluation order, a rounding direction, or a comparison boundary, that pin is part of the contract: two implementations that differ on it will produce different, and therefore wrong, numbers.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which they act. If either header is missing, empty, or if `X-User-Role` names anything outside the seven roles defined below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). When the headers are well-formed but the declared role is not among the roles authorized for the requested operation in §4, the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The seven roles are `SystemAdmin`, `InventoryAnalyst`, `InventorySupervisor`, `ShrinkController`, `StoreOps`, `VendorRep` and `ThirdPartyCounter`.

- `SystemAdmin` owns reference data. It registers merchandise categories, stores, vendors, SKUs and third-party counting firms, opens stock positions, may open and encode tag batches, may schedule and abandon physical inventories, drives the logical clock, and may perform every read in the system. It is deliberately excluded from every approval, attribution and chargeback decision.
- `InventoryAnalyst` is the day-to-day integrity analyst. It opens stock positions, runs integrity scans, requests on-hand adjustments, builds cycle-count schedules and tasks, starts counts, records counted lines, submits counts, assigns investigators and proposes root causes. It approves nothing.
- `InventorySupervisor` is the store-level approver. It approves, rejects, posts and reverses adjustments it did not itself request, approves and returns cause proposals it did not itself make, closes investigations, returns counts to the floor, reconciles and voids count tasks, pauses and resumes count schedules, receives, verifies, reworks and scraps tag batches, opens shrink periods, and orders physical-inventory recounts.
- `ShrinkController` is the financial owner of shrink. It alone provisionally closes, reopens and finally closes shrink periods, issues vendor chargebacks and chargeback credits, reconciles and certifies physical inventories, and approves adjustments whose value impact exceeds the supervisor's single-approval cap. It also shares the supervisor's adjustment and investigation powers and may drive physical-inventory counting steps.
- `StoreOps` is the store receiving and counting crew.
- `VendorRep` is the supplier-side source-tagging contact.
- `ThirdPartyCounter` is the independent physical-inventory counting crew.

**Identity semantics.** Three roles are entity-backed and four are opaque, and the difference determines how `X-User-ID` is interpreted.

When `X-User-Role` is `StoreOps`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Store`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Store` compare the resource's store-bearing field to `X-User-ID` — specifically a tag batch's `destination_store_id` and a count task's `store_id`. When `X-User-Role` is `VendorRep`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Vendor`; ownership checks compare a tag batch's `vendor_id`, or the vendor identifier named in the request path, to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `ThirdPartyCounter`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `CounterFirm`; ownership checks compare a physical inventory's `counter_firm_id` to `X-User-ID`. A write attempted by one of these three roles against a resource scoped to a different entity is rejected with `ERR_IDENTITY_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403).

When `X-User-Role` is `SystemAdmin`, `InventoryAnalyst`, `InventorySupervisor` or `ShrinkController`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a workflow step demands attribution — as `requested_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` on an adjustment, and as `proposed_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` on a variance investigation — but it never compares such a value to any entity's `id`. Note that the investigator named on an investigation, `assigned_investigator_id`, is likewise an opaque operator identifier supplied in the request body rather than a foreign key.

**Segregation of duties.** Wherever a second operator reviews a first operator's work, the two `X-User-ID` values must differ. Approving a cause proposal and returning a cause proposal both compare the acting `X-User-ID` against the investigation's `proposed_by_user_id`; approving, rejecting and reversing an adjustment all compare the acting `X-User-ID` against the adjustment's `requested_by_user_id`. A match in any of these five operations is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403), regardless of how senior the acting role is.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every endpoint that returns a collection accepts two query parameters. `limit` sets the maximum number of rows returned; when omitted it defaults to 10, and the accepted range is a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100. `offset` sets the number of rows skipped before the page begins; when omitted it defaults to 0, and the accepted range is any integer of 0 or greater. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100 inclusive, or an `offset` below 0, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 422).

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are sorted by `created_at` in ascending order.

List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope, where `total_count` reflects the size of the filtered result set before pagination is applied and `has_more` states whether rows remain beyond the current page.

Where §4 declares additional query parameters on a list endpoint, those parameters filter the result set by exact match and combine conjunctively. Filters that name an enumerated value — `fulfillment_status` on the store list, `promising_state` on the stock-position list, `approval_state` and `posting_state` on the adjustment list, `approved_root_cause` on the investigation list, `superseded` and `pass_round` on the physical-inventory-line list, and `status` wherever it appears — accept only the values enumerated for the corresponding field; anything else is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_ENUM_VALUE` (HTTP 422).

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties, Timestamps and the Logical Clock

Every resource is minted with an `id`, which is a server-generated UUID carrying no type prefix. Every resource also carries a `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording the instant the row was minted.

All resources except one additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits refreshed on every mutation of the row, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation — including mutations the server performs on its own initiative, such as recomputing a derived accuracy figure or a store posture. The single exception is `CounterFirm`, which is immutable after registration: a counting firm carries only `id` and `created_at`, has no `updated_at` and no `version`, and has no update endpoint at all.

Time in this domain is never read from the wall clock. A logical clock supplies every "now" that any date or datetime rule consults — the instant stamped into `last_counted_at`, `posted_at`, `reversed_at` and `certified_at`, the date used when a phantom-inventory staleness test measures elapsed days, and the date written into an accelerated `next_due_date`. Before the clock has ever been set it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time. An administrative capability sets the clock to a supplied `to_iso8601` instant and reports it back as `current_utc`; setting the clock mutates no entity and re-runs no calculation. A `to_iso8601` value that is not a valid UTC instant is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422).

### 2.5. Stale-Read Protection on Three Write Paths

Three write paths in this domain would silently destroy work if executed against data the caller had not actually read, and each therefore demands that the caller present the version it last observed of the governing parent resource. Endpoints not named here take no version field at all.

Requesting an on-hand adjustment against a stock position requires `expected_stock_position_version` in the request body as a non-optional field, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `StockPosition`. If that value does not equal the position's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_STOCK_POSITION_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The product deliberately allows unlimited adjustments per position and re-derives on-hand from the posted set every time, so an adjustment authored against an on-hand reading that has since moved would double-correct the same discrepancy; the check forces a re-read before a second correction is authored.

Submitting a counted line for a physical inventory requires `expected_physical_inventory_version` in the request body as a non-optional field, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `PhysicalInventory`. A mismatch with the inventory's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PHYSICAL_INVENTORY_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Because a later line for the same item legitimately supersedes an earlier one, a counter re-submitting an item on a stale reading would overwrite an aggregate that has already moved.

Proposing a root cause on a variance investigation requires `expected_investigation_version` in the request body as a non-optional field, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `VarianceInvestigation`. A mismatch with the investigation's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_INVESTIGATION_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Since a proposal legitimately supersedes an earlier proposal, a proposal authored on a stale reading would overwrite a colleague's root cause and evidence note without the author ever having read it.

### 2.6. Units, Money and Rounding Discipline

Monetary amounts are integer cents; rates, scores, ratios and index figures are integer basis points, where 10000 basis points is 100 percent; physical quantities are whole units. Accuracy percentages are the only fractional values in the domain: `unit_accuracy_pct`, `value_accuracy_pct`, `coverage_pct` and `min_accuracy_pct` all carry exactly 2 decimal places.

Three rounding disciplines apply and they are not interchangeable.

- **Round half up** governs every valuation and rate: a per-line variance value, a chargeback gross amount, a goodwill credit amount, a shrink rate in basis points, and the two accuracy percentages (to 2 decimal places). A value exactly at the halfway point rounds away from zero.
- **Floor** — truncation toward the lower integer — governs every ratio and index computed from a division: a tag read rate in basis points, a physical-inventory net variance in basis points, a store accuracy index in basis points, blocked and restricted ratios in basis points, the adjustment authority threshold, and each waterfall bucket cap.
- **Ceiling** governs exactly one figure: the required verification sample size for a tag batch, which is always rounded up so a fractional sample never rounds away to a smaller obligation.

Where a calculation both aggregates and rounds, the order in which the two happen is pinned by the rule that defines it, and the alternative order is forbidden because it yields a different answer.

### 2.7. Derivation Order and Cascading Recomputation

Many of the derived figures in this domain sit in a chain, and the chain is always evaluated in the same direction: a line-level fact refreshes its stock position, the position's promising gate is then re-derived, and only after every position touched by the request has settled is the owning store's posture re-derived. A store posture is therefore never computed against half-updated positions.

Concretely, the chain is: adjustments and counted lines write facts onto a `StockPosition`; the position's `on_hand_units`, `book_value_cents`, `unit_accuracy_pct`, `value_accuracy_pct`, `phantom_units` and `tag_integrity_hold` settle; the position's `promising_state` is re-derived from those settled values; the store's `total_position_count`, `blocked_position_count`, `restricted_position_count`, `accuracy_index_bps` and `fulfillment_status` are re-derived from the whole population of that store's positions; and, only when the store lands in `SUSPENDED`, the store's active cycle-count schedules are accelerated. Each entity the chain touches has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented, exactly as a client-initiated mutation would.

Server-initiated transitions are real transitions. A store's `fulfillment_status` is never supplied in any request body and has no endpoint of its own; it moves only as a consequence of position-level change, and each such move increments the store's `version`. The same is true of an investigation being waived because the count line that opened it was discarded.

One cascade is deliberately one-way. When a store falls to `SUSPENDED`, each of its `ACTIVE` cycle-count schedules has its cadence halved and its next due date pulled forward to today. If the store subsequently recovers to `PROBATION` or `ELIGIBLE`, those cadence and due-date changes are **not** rolled back — the accelerated calendar stands.

### 2.8. Path Resolution and Resource Discovery

Path identifiers are resolved strictly left to right, and the first identifier that resolves to nothing terminates the request with the 404 belonging to that identifier's type: `ERR_CATEGORY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STORE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VENDOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COUNTER_FIRM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STOCK_POSITION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TAG_BATCH_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COUNT_SCHEDULE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COUNT_TASK_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COUNT_LINE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INVESTIGATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ADJUSTMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SHRINK_PERIOD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PHYSICAL_INVENTORY_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_PHYSICAL_INVENTORY_LINE_NOT_FOUND`. Foreign keys supplied in a request body are resolved after path identifiers, in the order the endpoint's input list declares them, and an unresolvable body foreign key raises the same 404 for the type it names — a body `store_id` or `destination_store_id` that matches no store raises `ERR_STORE_NOT_FOUND`, a body `primary_vendor_id` or `vendor_id` that matches no vendor raises `ERR_VENDOR_NOT_FOUND`, a body `category_id` raises `ERR_CATEGORY_NOT_FOUND`, a body `item_id` raises `ERR_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, a body `counter_firm_id` raises `ERR_COUNTER_FIRM_NOT_FOUND`, a body `count_schedule_id` raises `ERR_COUNT_SCHEDULE_NOT_FOUND`, a body `shrink_period_id` raises `ERR_SHRINK_PERIOD_NOT_FOUND`, and a body `investigation_id` raises `ERR_INVESTIGATION_NOT_FOUND`.

Although counted lines, variance investigations and physical-inventory lines are created in the context of a parent, each is independently addressable by its own identifier and each has its own list endpoint. The investigation list is the only discovery channel for investigations opened automatically by a count submission, and the count-line list is the only discovery channel for lines recorded inside a task; there is no nested collection read under the parent. A count line whose parent task was later returned to the floor may be gone while an investigation still points at its identifier — that dangling reference is expected and the investigation is still returned by its own reads.

### 2.9. Order of Rejection

When a single request violates several rules, exactly one error is reported, and which one is fixed rather than incidental. The order reflects an inventory-integrity organisation's priorities: tell the caller who they are before telling them what exists, tell them to re-read before telling them about business state, and report an audit finding ahead of an accounting one.

Checks are evaluated in this order:

1. Authentication of the two headers (`ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`).
2. Role authorization for the endpoint (`ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`).
3. Identity scope for the three entity-backed roles (`ERR_IDENTITY_SCOPE_VIOLATION`).
4. Existence of every path identifier, left to right (the 404 family).
5. Existence of every body foreign key, in the endpoint's declared input order (the 404 family).
6. Request shape: required fields present, declared types honoured, numeric and length ranges satisfied (`ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`), then enum membership (`ERR_INVALID_ENUM_VALUE`), then pagination bounds on list reads (`ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`), then natural-key uniqueness on reference-data creation (`ERR_DUPLICATE_CATEGORY_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_STORE_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_VENDOR_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_COUNTER_FIRM_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_SKU`).
7. Optimistic-concurrency version match on the three write paths of §2.5 (`ERR_STALE_STOCK_POSITION_VERSION`, `ERR_STALE_PHYSICAL_INVENTORY_VERSION`, `ERR_STALE_INVESTIGATION_VERSION`).
8. State preconditions on the target entity (`ERR_TAG_BATCH_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_COUNT_TASK_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_INVESTIGATION_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_ADJUSTMENT_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_SHRINK_PERIOD_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_PHYSICAL_INVENTORY_INVALID_STATE`), then the backing schedule's activity (`ERR_COUNT_SCHEDULE_NOT_ACTIVE`).
9. The compliance tier: segregation of duties (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`), then the tag-integrity hold on posting (`ERR_TAG_INTEGRITY_HOLD`), then the still-open shrink period on certification (`ERR_SHRINK_PERIOD_STILL_OPEN`).
10. Referential coupling: `ERR_INVESTIGATION_NOT_CLOSED`, then `ERR_ITEM_CATEGORY_MISMATCH`, then `ERR_ITEM_NOT_STOCKED_AT_STORE`, then `ERR_NO_COVERING_SHRINK_PERIOD`, then `ERR_OPEN_INVESTIGATIONS`.
11. Geometric, cardinality and hardware constraints: `ERR_READER_MODE_UNSUPPORTED`, then `ERR_CATEGORY_READER_MODE_MISMATCH`, then `ERR_COUNT_WINDOW_OVERLAP`, then `ERR_OVERHEAD_READER_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, then `ERR_PHYSICAL_INVENTORY_BLACKOUT_CONFLICT`, then `ERR_SHRINK_PERIOD_OVERLAP`, then `ERR_DUPLICATE_STOCK_POSITION`, then `ERR_DUPLICATE_COUNT_LINE`, then `ERR_ZERO_DELTA_UNITS`, then `ERR_REWORK_LIMIT_REACHED`, then `ERR_TAG_SCHEME_MISMATCH`, then `ERR_SAMPLE_BELOW_REQUIRED`, then `ERR_INVALID_VERIFICATION_COUNTS`, then `ERR_NO_COUNT_LINES`, then `ERR_COUNT_COVERAGE_BELOW_MIN`, then `ERR_MAX_LINES_PER_PASS_EXCEEDED`.
12. Temporal constraints: `ERR_COUNT_WINDOW_NOT_DUE`, then `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`, then `ERR_INVALID_PERIOD_RANGE`.
13. The fiscal tier, last of all: `ERR_ADJUSTMENT_ABOVE_SUPERVISOR_CAP`, then `ERR_ADJUSTMENT_APPROVAL_REQUIRED`, then `ERR_RECOUNT_REQUIRED`, then `ERR_NO_VENDOR_SHORTAGE`, then `ERR_CHARGEBACK_ALREADY_ISSUED`, then `ERR_CREDIT_EXCEEDS_BALANCE`.

Two consequences are worth stating outright. A request that fails both an audit rule and a money rule always reports the audit rule — for example, an adjustment approval by the very operator who requested it reports `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` even when the value impact also exceeds the supervisor cap. And a stale version is reported before any business-state complaint, so a caller holding an outdated reading is told to re-read rather than being told about a state it may no longer be looking at.

### 2.10. Conserved Quantities

Three invariants hold at all times and every rule in §3 is written to preserve them.

**Unit conservation on a position.** A position's `on_hand_units` is always exactly its immutable `baseline_on_hand_units` plus the sum of `delta_units` over every adjustment on that position whose posting dimension currently reads `POSTED`. On-hand is never incremented in place; it is re-derived from that set. Consequently reversing a posted adjustment removes it from the set and returns on-hand to precisely the value it would have had if the adjustment had never posted, with zero drift.

**Shrink-bucket conservation on a period.** A period's `total_attributable_variance_cents` is always the sum of `theft_cents`, `damage_cents`, `process_error_cents`, `vendor_shortage_cents` and `unresolved_cents`. Attributing an investigation's value to a named bucket moves the amount out of `unresolved_cents` and into that bucket, so the conserved total is unchanged by the attribution itself.

**Vendor money conservation.** A vendor's `chargeback_balance_cents` is always `lifetime_chargeback_gross_cents` minus `credited_cents`. Gross is only ever added to and is never rewritten; credits are only ever added to. No operation may drive the balance below zero.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Merchandise Frame: Categories, Stores, Vendors, SKUs and Counting Firms

Before anything can be counted, a `SystemAdmin` establishes the reference frame. A **merchandise category** is registered with a `code` of 1 to 32 characters drawn from uppercase alphanumerics and the hyphen and unique across all categories, together with a human-readable `name` of 1 to 120 characters. A second category submitted with a `code` already on file is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CATEGORY_CODE`. The category also carries the policy that governs every count of every SKU inside it: `count_cadence_days`, the default number of days between cycle counts, which must be at least 7 and at most 365; `variance_tolerance_units`, the absolute unit discrepancy a counted line may show and still be treated as within tolerance, which must be 0 or greater; `variance_tolerance_value_cents`, the landed-cost variance value in cents at or above which a line is out of tolerance regardless of unit count, which must be greater than 0; `landed_cost_uplift_bps`, the basis-point freight-and-duty uplift applied to unit cost when a count variance is valued, which defaults to 0 and must be between 0 and 5000 inclusive; `min_accuracy_pct`, the minimum value-basis accuracy a position in this category must retain to stay promisable, expressed to 2 decimal places between 0.00 and 100.00 inclusive; `reader_mode_required`, which is one of `HANDHELD`, `OVERHEAD` or `EITHER` and defaults to `EITHER`; and `auto_correct_negative`, a boolean defaulting to true that decides whether a small negative on-hand discovered by an integrity scan is corrected automatically or routed to a supervisor.

A **store** is registered with a unique `code` of 1 to 16 characters and a `name` of 1 to 120 characters — a duplicate `code` raises `ERR_DUPLICATE_STORE_CODE` — plus two hardware facts. `reader_capability` declares the reader technology installed and is one of `HANDHELD_ONLY`, `OVERHEAD_ONLY` or `BOTH`. `overhead_reader_capacity` declares how many non-terminal overhead count tasks may hold overlapping windows at that store simultaneously; it defaults to 2 and must be between 0 and 8 inclusive. A freshly registered store has no positions yet, so `total_position_count`, `blocked_position_count` and `restricted_position_count` all start at 0, `accuracy_index_bps` starts at 10000 and `fulfillment_status` starts at `ELIGIBLE` — the vacuous-accuracy reading described in §3.10.

A **vendor** carries a unique `code` of 1 to 16 characters and a legal `name` of 1 to 160 characters, with a duplicate `code` raising `ERR_DUPLICATE_VENDOR_CODE`. Every vendor begins with a perfect tag-quality record: `tag_compliance_score_bps` starts at 10000 and, because that score sits in the top band, `required_verification_sample_pct` starts at 5. The four ledger figures — `shortage_event_count`, `lifetime_chargeback_gross_cents`, `credited_cents` and `chargeback_balance_cents` — all start at 0. None of these six figures is ever supplied by a client; they move only as a consequence of tag verifications, closed shortage findings, chargebacks and credits.

A **third-party counting firm** carries a unique `code` of 1 to 16 characters — duplicates raise `ERR_DUPLICATE_COUNTER_FIRM_CODE` — a `name` of 1 to 160 characters, a `certification_level` of either `TIER_1` or `TIER_2`, and `max_lines_per_pass`, the maximum number of counted lines the firm may submit within a single pass of one physical inventory, which must be at least 1 and at most 100000. A counting firm is immutable once registered: as noted in §2.4 it carries no `updated_at` and no `version`.

A **SKU** is registered against a category and a primary vendor, the payload naming the `category_id` it belongs to and the `primary_vendor_id` to which a vendor-shortage finding on this SKU is charged back. It carries a `sku` code of 1 to 32 characters, unique across all SKUs and raising `ERR_DUPLICATE_SKU` on collision, a `description` of 1 to 240 characters, a `unit_cost_cents` greater than 0 — the single cost basis behind every valuation in this domain — and a ticketed `retail_price_cents` greater than 0. Tagging policy lives on the SKU too: `tag_encoding_scheme` is one of `SGTIN_96`, `SGTIN_198` or `NONE`; `epc_prefix_length` is the required length of the EPC company prefix on tags encoded for this SKU, defaulting to 7 and constrained to between 6 and 12 inclusive; and `tag_required`, defaulting to true, states that receipts of this SKU must arrive as a verified tag batch.

### 3.2. Stock Positions: The Opening Balance and the Promising Gate

A **stock position** is the intersection of one store and one SKU, and it is where the book quantity actually lives. Either a `SystemAdmin` or an `InventoryAnalyst` opens one by naming the `store_id` and the `item_id`, optionally declaring `baseline_on_hand_units` — the opening quantity, which must be 0 or greater and defaults to 0 — and optionally `reserved_units`, the quantity already committed to open omnichannel orders, which must be 0 or greater, defaults to 0, and is never mutated again by any operation in this domain. Only one position may exist per store-and-SKU pair; a second attempt is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_STOCK_POSITION`.

At creation the position's derived fields are seeded deliberately. `on_hand_units` equals `baseline_on_hand_units` and `book_value_cents` equals `on_hand_units` multiplied by the SKU's `unit_cost_cents`. `phantom_units` and `negative_correction_count` are 0, `tag_integrity_hold` is false, `last_counted_at` is null, and `last_count_system_units`, `last_count_book_value_cents`, `last_variance_units` and `last_variance_value_cents` are all 0. Both accuracy figures, `unit_accuracy_pct` and `value_accuracy_pct`, read exactly 100.00, because a position that has never been counted is treated as perfectly accurate. The promising gate is then evaluated and `promising_state` set accordingly, the owning store's `total_position_count` is incremented, and the store's posture is re-derived.

`baseline_on_hand_units` is immutable after creation. From that moment on, `on_hand_units` is re-derived rather than edited: it is always the baseline plus the sum of `delta_units` across every adjustment on this position whose posting dimension reads `POSTED`, and it is allowed to go negative. `book_value_cents` follows on-hand and is likewise allowed to be negative. Reads of a position always show the live derived values.

**The promising gate.** A position's `promising_state` is one of `PROMISABLE`, `RESTRICTED` or `BLOCKED`, and it is re-derived by evaluating three clauses from top to bottom, with the first matching clause winning.

The first clause is a hard integrity failure and yields `BLOCKED`: it matches when the position's `tag_integrity_hold` is true, or its `on_hand_units` is less than 0, or its `phantom_units` is greater than 0. Any one of the three is sufficient, and a tag-integrity hold dominates every other consideration.

The second clause yields `RESTRICTED` and matches when either of two conditions holds. The first is that the position's `value_accuracy_pct` is strictly less than the `min_accuracy_pct` of its SKU's category — a position whose value accuracy exactly equals the category floor remains promisable, because the comparison is strict. The second is that at least one variance investigation whose originating counted line points at this position is in `OPEN`, `IN_REVIEW`, `CAUSE_PROPOSED` or `CAUSE_APPROVED` status. Investigations in `CLOSED` or `WAIVED` status never restrict a position.

When neither clause matches, `promising_state` is `PROMISABLE`.

The gate is re-derived by any request that changes the position's `on_hand_units`, `phantom_units`, `tag_integrity_hold` or `value_accuracy_pct`, and by any request that changes the status of an investigation pointing at the position. Note that the value-basis variant, not the unit-basis variant, is the one the accuracy floor consults.

**Reading accuracy.** A dedicated computed read exposes both accuracy variants of a position side by side together with the reason its gate holds, returning `unit_accuracy_pct`, `value_accuracy_pct`, `last_count_system_units`, `last_count_book_value_cents`, `last_variance_units`, `last_variance_value_cents`, `promising_state`, a human-readable `promising_reason` naming the clause that decided the state, and the `min_accuracy_pct` the position is measured against.

**The two accuracy variants.** Both variants are recomputed whenever a count submission writes fresh frozen figures onto the position, and both are re-run — against those same frozen figures — whenever an adjustment posts or is reversed. Adjustments never rewrite the frozen inputs, so the two accuracy figures are stable across postings and move only at the next count.

The unit-basis variant works from a denominator that is the greater of `last_count_system_units` and 1. It takes the absolute value of `last_variance_units`, divides it by that denominator, subtracts the quotient from 1, multiplies by 100, rounds the result half up to 2 decimal places, and clamps it into the range 0.00 through 100.00 inclusive. That figure is `unit_accuracy_pct`, and it is the input the store accuracy index consumes.

The value-basis variant works from a denominator that is the greater of `last_count_book_value_cents` and 1. It takes `last_variance_value_cents` as recorded — that figure is already a non-negative landed-cost amount — divides it by that denominator, subtracts the quotient from 1, multiplies by 100, rounds half up to 2 decimal places, and clamps to the range 0.00 through 100.00 inclusive. That figure is `value_accuracy_pct`, and it is the input the category accuracy floor consumes.

The two variants legitimately disagree, and the direction of disagreement is fixed: the unit variant is blind to the landed-cost uplift that the value variant's numerator carries while its denominator remains pure book cost, so whenever the category's `landed_cost_uplift_bps` is greater than 0 an identical unit discrepancy scores strictly lower on the value variant than on the unit variant. While `last_counted_at` is still null, both fields read exactly 100.00 regardless of anything else.

### 3.3. Source Tagging: Encoding, Receipt and Read-Quality Verification

Item-level tags are applied at source by the vendor and their quality is proved at the receiving store. A **tag batch** covers one SKU moving to one store, and a `VendorRep` — or a `SystemAdmin` acting on the vendor's behalf — opens one by naming the `vendor_id` that will encode and apply the tags, the `item_id` being tagged, the `destination_store_id` that will receive and verify the batch, and `quantity_encoded`, the number of units tagged at source, which must be greater than 0. A batch is born in `DRAFT` with `rework_round` at 0, with `sampled_units`, `readable_units`, `duplicate_epc_count`, `unencoded_units` and `read_rate_bps` all 0, and with `verification_outcome` null. Nothing is frozen yet.

Encoding the batch advances it from `DRAFT` to `ENCODED` and is performed by the vendor named on the batch — a `VendorRep` whose `X-User-ID` is not that vendor is refused under §2.1's identity scope. The payload declares the `encoding_scheme` actually used, which must be either `SGTIN_96` or `SGTIN_198`, and the `epc_prefix` printed on the tags, which must consist of digits only. Two things must line up with the SKU: the submitted `encoding_scheme` must equal the SKU's `tag_encoding_scheme`, and the number of digits in `epc_prefix` must equal the SKU's `epc_prefix_length`. A failure of either check is rejected with `ERR_TAG_SCHEME_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). Attempting to encode a batch that is not in `DRAFT` — or, on the rework path described below, not in `VERIFICATION_FAILED` — is rejected with `ERR_TAG_BATCH_INVALID_STATE`, as is any transition attempted out of the two terminal states `VERIFIED_PASS` and `SCRAPPED`.

Encoding is also the moment the receiving store's obligation is frozen. `required_sample_units` is set to the batch's `quantity_encoded` multiplied by the vendor's `required_verification_sample_pct` as that percentage stands at this instant, divided by 100, and rounded **up** to a whole number. The vendor's current band is used, not the band in force when the batch was drafted, so a vendor whose compliance score has fallen in the meantime hands the store a larger sample obligation.

Receipt is a separate step from verification. `StoreOps` at the destination store — or an `InventorySupervisor` — acknowledges physical arrival, moving the batch from `ENCODED` to `RECEIVED`. A `StoreOps` actor whose `X-User-ID` is not the batch's `destination_store_id` is refused under identity scope.

Verification is where the batch, the vendor's reputation and the store's promising posture all move at once. The receiving store submits four counts from the read-quality sample: `sampled_units`, the units actually read; `readable_units`, those within the sample whose EPC decoded successfully; `duplicate_epc_count`, the duplicate EPCs observed; and `unencoded_units`, the units in the sample carrying no tag at all. Three gates apply in order. The batch must be in `RECEIVED`. `sampled_units` must be at least the `required_sample_units` frozen at encoding, otherwise the submission is rejected with `ERR_SAMPLE_BELOW_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422). And the counts must be internally coherent: `readable_units` may not exceed `sampled_units`, `unencoded_units` may not exceed `sampled_units`, and the sum of `readable_units` and `unencoded_units` may not exceed `sampled_units`; a violation of any of the three is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_VERIFICATION_COUNTS` (HTTP 422).

The outcome is then decided. `read_rate_bps` is 10000 multiplied by `readable_units`, divided by `sampled_units`, with the quotient floored to a whole basis-point figure. The batch passes when and only when all three of the following hold: `read_rate_bps` is at least 9800, `duplicate_epc_count` is exactly 0, and `unencoded_units` is exactly 0. Otherwise it fails. Consider a batch of 1000 units at the 5 percent band: 50 units must be sampled, and 49 readable out of 50 yields a rate of exactly 9800 which passes at the boundary, while 48 readable out of 50 yields 9600 which fails.

On a pass, the four submitted counts and the computed `read_rate_bps` are stored, `verification_outcome` becomes `PASS`, and the batch moves from `RECEIVED` to `VERIFIED_PASS`, which is terminal. The vendor's `tag_compliance_score_bps` rises by 200 basis points but never past 10000, and `required_verification_sample_pct` is re-derived from the new score. Any stock position for the batch's destination store and SKU has its `tag_integrity_hold` cleared to false — this is the **only** way a hold is ever cleared — after which that position's `promising_state` is re-derived and then the store's `fulfillment_status` follows.

On a failure the cascade runs four hops. First, the sample is stored, `read_rate_bps` is computed, `verification_outcome` becomes `FAIL`, and the batch moves from `RECEIVED` to `VERIFICATION_FAILED`; at the same time the vendor's `tag_compliance_score_bps` falls by 1500 basis points but never below 0, and `required_verification_sample_pct` is re-derived from the reduced score. Second, caused by the first hop, any stock position for that destination store and SKU has `tag_integrity_hold` set to true. Third, caused by the second, that position's `promising_state` is re-derived and necessarily becomes `BLOCKED`, since a tag-integrity hold dominates the gate. Fourth, caused by the third, the store's `blocked_position_count`, `accuracy_index_bps` and `fulfillment_status` are re-derived.

The sample band is a three-step ladder read off the vendor's score: `required_verification_sample_pct` is 5 when `tag_compliance_score_bps` is 9000 or more, 10 when the score is 7500 or more but below 9000, and 20 when the score is below 7500. A vendor starting at a perfect 10000 who fails one verification drops to 8500 and therefore into the 10 percent band, so the next thousand-unit batch that vendor encodes will demand 100 sampled units rather than 50.

A failed batch has exactly two futures. A `VendorRep` or `InventorySupervisor` may send it back for re-encoding, which returns it from `VERIFICATION_FAILED` to `ENCODED`, increments `rework_round` by 1, resets `sampled_units`, `readable_units`, `duplicate_epc_count`, `unencoded_units` and `read_rate_bps` to 0 and `verification_outcome` to null, and re-freezes `required_sample_units` from the vendor's current — that is, already penalised — band. Rework is permitted only while `rework_round` is strictly below 2; a third attempt is rejected with `ERR_REWORK_LIMIT_REACHED`. This edge closes the loop from `ENCODED` through `RECEIVED` and `VERIFICATION_FAILED` back to `ENCODED`. Alternatively an `InventorySupervisor` or `ShrinkController` may scrap the batch, moving it from `VERIFICATION_FAILED` to the terminal `SCRAPPED`; a scrapped batch may carry an explanatory `note`. Scrapping does **not** clear the position's `tag_integrity_hold` — the hold survives until a batch for the same store and SKU actually passes verification, which is precisely what makes a failed receipt expensive.

### 3.4. Cycle-Count Scheduling, Reader Coverage and Window Geometry

A **cycle-count schedule** pairs one store with one merchandise category and states how often that category is counted there. An `InventoryAnalyst` or `InventorySupervisor` creates one by naming the `store_id` and the `category_id`, choosing a `reader_mode` of either `HANDHELD` or `OVERHEAD`, and supplying a `first_due_date`, the calendar date on or after which the first count window may start. `cadence_days` may be supplied explicitly, in which case it must be at least 7 and at most 365; when omitted it is seeded from the category's `count_cadence_days`. The schedule is born `ACTIVE` with `completed_cycle_count` at 0 and `next_due_date` equal to `first_due_date`, expressed as a calendar date in year-month-day form.

Two hardware compatibility rules bracket the choice of reader technology, and they consult different entities. The schedule's `reader_mode` must be permitted by the category's `reader_mode_required`: a category set to `EITHER` permits both, while a category set to `HANDHELD` or `OVERHEAD` permits only that same mode. A violation is rejected with `ERR_CATEGORY_READER_MODE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). Separately, any reader mode must be supported by the store's `reader_capability`: `HANDHELD_ONLY` accepts only `HANDHELD`, `OVERHEAD_ONLY` accepts only `OVERHEAD`, and `BOTH` accepts either; a violation is rejected with `ERR_READER_MODE_UNSUPPORTED` (HTTP 422).

A schedule's `status` is one of `ACTIVE`, `PAUSED` or `RETIRED`. An `InventorySupervisor` or `ShrinkController` may pause an `ACTIVE` schedule, optionally with a `note`, so that no new count task can be opened against it, and may resume a `PAUSED` schedule back to `ACTIVE`. Attempting to pause a schedule that is not `ACTIVE`, or resume one that is not `PAUSED`, is rejected with `ERR_COUNT_SCHEDULE_NOT_ACTIVE`.

A **count task** is one execution of a schedule. An `InventoryAnalyst` or `InventorySupervisor` opens one by naming the `count_schedule_id` that authorises it, the `reader_mode` the task will actually be executed with, and the half-open window it occupies: `window_start` is the inclusive start instant and `window_end` the exclusive end. The task copies `store_id` and `category_id` from its schedule rather than accepting them, and is born `SCHEDULED` with `recount_round`, `counted_line_count`, `variance_line_count` and `total_variance_value_cents` all 0, `coverage_pct` at 0.00, and `in_scope_position_count` left at 0 until the task is started.

Creating a task runs a specific sequence of checks. The backing schedule must be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_COUNT_SCHEDULE_NOT_ACTIVE`. The task's `reader_mode` must be supported by the store's `reader_capability`, otherwise `ERR_READER_MODE_UNSUPPORTED`. Then the two geometric checks run, and their order matters. **Check A** enforces exclusivity over the merchandise area: no other count task on the same store and the same category, in `SCHEDULED`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `COUNTED` status, may hold a window overlapping the requested one; tasks that have reached `RECONCILED` or `VOIDED` release their claim. Windows are half-open, so two tasks overlap when the requested `window_start` is strictly earlier than the existing `window_end` **and** the existing `window_start` is strictly earlier than the requested `window_end`. Adjacency is therefore legal: a task starting at the exact instant another ends does not collide. A collision is rejected with `ERR_COUNT_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. **Check B** enforces aggregate capacity over the overhead reader pool and runs only when the requested `reader_mode` is `OVERHEAD`: the number of count tasks at the same store with `reader_mode` of `OVERHEAD`, in `SCHEDULED`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `COUNTED` status, whose windows overlap the requested one under the same half-open test, must be strictly below the store's `overhead_reader_capacity`; when that count has already reached the capacity the request is rejected with `ERR_OVERHEAD_READER_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Because Check A precedes Check B, a same-category overlap always reports the window-overlap error even when the overhead pool is also exhausted. Note that a physical-inventory blackout, described in §3.9, has no bearing on cycle counts.

Two temporal rules complete task creation. The calendar date of `window_start` must be on or after the schedule's `next_due_date`; an earlier window is rejected with `ERR_COUNT_WINDOW_NOT_DUE` (HTTP 422). And `window_end` must be strictly after `window_start`; otherwise `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW` (HTTP 422).

Starting a count moves the task from `SCHEDULED` to `IN_PROGRESS` and is performed by `StoreOps` at the task's store, by an `InventoryAnalyst` or by an `InventorySupervisor`; the backing schedule must still be `ACTIVE` at this moment too, and a schedule paused between task creation and task start makes the start fail with `ERR_COUNT_SCHEDULE_NOT_ACTIVE`. The start is also the only moment the coverage denominator is captured: `in_scope_position_count` is snapshotted as the number of stock positions at the task's store whose SKU belongs to the task's category. That snapshot is **not** refreshed if positions appear later inside the window.

Counted lines are then recorded one at a time against the in-progress task, by `StoreOps` at that store or by an `InventoryAnalyst`. Each line names the `item_id` counted, the `counted_units` physically observed, which must be 0 or greater, and the `read_mode_used`, which is either `HANDHELD` or `OVERHEAD` and records the technology actually applied to that line. The created line records its parent task as `count_task_id`, and that same identifier is the filter by which the count-line list narrows to one task's lines. The task must be `IN_PROGRESS`; a line offered against a task in any other status is rejected with `ERR_COUNT_TASK_INVALID_STATE`. The SKU's `category_id` must equal the task's `category_id`, otherwise `ERR_ITEM_CATEGORY_MISMATCH`. A stock position must exist for the task's store and the named SKU, otherwise `ERR_ITEM_NOT_STOCKED_AT_STORE`. Only one line per SKU may be attached to a task; a second is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_COUNT_LINE`. And the `read_mode_used` must itself be supported by the store's `reader_capability`, otherwise `ERR_READER_MODE_UNSUPPORTED`.

Recording a line resolves and freezes its context. The line's `stock_position_id` is resolved from the task's store together with the named SKU, and `system_units_snapshot` captures that position's `on_hand_units` at this instant and is frozen thereafter. `variance_units` is then computed as `counted_units` minus `system_units_snapshot` — a signed figure, negative when the shelf holds fewer units than the book claims. The task's `counted_line_count` is incremented and its `coverage_pct` recomputed.

**Valuing a line's variance.** The landed-cost uplift factor for the line is 10000 plus the category's `landed_cost_uplift_bps`, divided by 10000. The raw line value is the absolute value of `variance_units`, multiplied by the SKU's `unit_cost_cents`, multiplied by that uplift factor. `variance_value_cents` is that raw value rounded half up to a whole number of cents. The rounding happens here, once, per line.

`within_tolerance` is then true when **both** of two conditions hold: the absolute value of `variance_units` is at most the category's `variance_tolerance_units`, and `variance_value_cents` is strictly less than the category's `variance_tolerance_value_cents`. If either condition fails the line is out of tolerance. Note the asymmetry of the two boundaries: a unit discrepancy exactly equal to the unit tolerance is still within tolerance, whereas a value exactly equal to the value tolerance is already out of tolerance.

**Weighted coverage.** Coverage is not a line count, because a line read with the wrong reader technology earns only partial or no credit. Each line's weight is 1.00 when its `read_mode_used` equals the task's `reader_mode`; 0.50 when the line was read `HANDHELD` while the task's mode is `OVERHEAD`; and 0.00 when the line was read `OVERHEAD` while the task's mode is `HANDHELD`. The effective line total is the exact decimal sum of those weights over every line currently attached to the task, accumulated in ascending line creation order. When `in_scope_position_count` is 0, `coverage_pct` is exactly 0.00 by rule. Otherwise `coverage_pct` is 100 multiplied by the effective line total, divided by `in_scope_position_count`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places and clamped into 0.00 through 100.00 inclusive. For instance, a task whose in-scope population is 4 and whose mode is `OVERHEAD` with three overhead lines and one handheld line has an effective total of 3.50 and therefore a coverage of 87.50, whereas four overhead lines give 4.00 and a coverage of 100.00.

### 3.5. Submitting a Count: Valuation, Accuracy Refresh and Investigation Opening

Submission is the busiest moment in the cycle-count workflow. `StoreOps` at the task's store, an `InventoryAnalyst` or an `InventorySupervisor` submits an `IN_PROGRESS` task, advancing it to `COUNTED`. Four preconditions guard the transition, and they are evaluated in this order. The task must be `IN_PROGRESS`, otherwise `ERR_COUNT_TASK_INVALID_STATE`. At least one counted line must be attached — a `counted_line_count` of 0 is rejected with `ERR_NO_COUNT_LINES`. The task's weighted `coverage_pct` must be at least 90.00, otherwise `ERR_COUNT_COVERAGE_BELOW_MIN`; note that this rejection also catches the case where lines do exist but every one of them earned a weight of 0.00 because it was read with an overhead reader on a handheld task. Finally, if at least one attached line has `within_tolerance` false, a shrink period must be available to receive the resulting variance: there must exist a shrink period for the task's store whose inclusive date range from `period_start` through `period_end` contains the calendar date of the task's `window_end` and whose status is either `OPEN` or `PROVISIONAL_CLOSED`. When no such period exists the submission is rejected with `ERR_NO_COVERING_SHRINK_PERIOD`. A task on which every line is within tolerance needs no covering period at all.

On success the task's rollups are written. `coverage_pct` is recomputed as described in §3.4. `variance_line_count` is set to the number of attached lines whose `within_tolerance` is false. `total_variance_value_cents` is the sum of the per-line `variance_value_cents` figures — that is, the sum of integers that were each already rounded when their line was created. **The evaluation order here is pinned and the alternative is forbidden:** each line is rounded first and the already-rounded integers are then added. Summing the unrounded decimal line values and rounding the total once yields a different number and is not permitted. To make the divergence concrete: a line of 5 units at 1234 cents under a 750-basis-point uplift values at 6632.750 and rounds to 6633, while a line of 3 units at 2500 cents under the same uplift values at 8062.500 and rounds to 8063; rounding per line and then summing gives 14696, whereas summing first and rounding once would give 14695. Only 14696 is correct. The summation walks the attached lines in ascending creation order; because integer addition is order-independent the order is stated for auditability rather than for arithmetic.

Every counted position is then refreshed, walking the attached lines in ascending line-creation order. For each line, the position it referenced receives `last_counted_at` set to the current logical-clock instant, `last_variance_units` set to the line's `variance_units`, `last_variance_value_cents` set to the line's `variance_value_cents`, `last_count_system_units` set to the line's `system_units_snapshot`, and `last_count_book_value_cents` set to that same snapshot multiplied by the SKU's `unit_cost_cents`. With those four frozen figures in place, the position's `unit_accuracy_pct` and `value_accuracy_pct` are recomputed by the two variants of §3.2.

For every attached line whose `within_tolerance` is false, a **variance investigation** is opened in `OPEN` status. The investigation copies `variance_units` and `variance_value_cents` from the line — both immutable thereafter — records the originating line as its `count_line_id`, stamps the `store_id` from the line's task, and stamps `shrink_period_id` with the covering shrink period identified by the precondition above. Each opened investigation immediately enlarges the covering period's `unresolved_cents` by its `variance_value_cents` under the provisional variant of §3.8, and that period's `total_attributable_variance_cents`, `provisional_total_shrink_cents` and `shrink_rate_bps` are re-derived. Because an open investigation exists against them, the affected positions are pulled down to `RESTRICTED` unless a harder failure already holds them at `BLOCKED`.

Finally every touched position has its `promising_state` re-derived, then the store's `fulfillment_status` follows, and the task's status becomes `COUNTED`.

**Returning a count to the floor.** An `InventorySupervisor` or `ShrinkController` may reject the submitted numbers and send the task back, moving it from `COUNTED` to `IN_PROGRESS` with an optional `note`. This is permitted only while `recount_round` is strictly below 1, so exactly one return is available per task; a second attempt is rejected with `ERR_REWORK_LIMIT_REACHED`. The return increments `recount_round` by 1 and then discards the count wholesale: every counted line attached to the task is deleted, and `counted_line_count`, `coverage_pct`, `variance_line_count` and `total_variance_value_cents` reset to 0, 0.00, 0 and 0 respectively. The cascade onto investigations is deliberately conditional. Every investigation whose originating line was just deleted and whose status is still `OPEN` moves to `WAIVED`, and its `variance_value_cents` is removed from the covering period's `unresolved_cents`, with that period's `total_attributable_variance_cents` and both shrink totals re-derived. Investigations that have already moved past `OPEN` — those in `IN_REVIEW`, `CAUSE_PROPOSED`, `CAUSE_APPROVED` or `CLOSED` — are **not** touched: their attribution work is preserved even though the line they cite no longer exists, which leaves a dangling reference that reads of the investigation still return, exactly as §2.8 anticipates. The affected positions have their promising gate re-evaluated and the store's posture follows. This edge closes the loop from `IN_PROGRESS` through `COUNTED` back to `IN_PROGRESS`.

**Reconciling a count.** An `InventorySupervisor` or `ShrinkController` closes out a submitted count, moving it from `COUNTED` to the terminal `RECONCILED`. Every investigation this task opened must already be in `CLOSED` or `WAIVED`; if even one remains in `OPEN`, `IN_REVIEW`, `CAUSE_PROPOSED` or `CAUSE_APPROVED`, the reconcile is rejected with `ERR_OPEN_INVESTIGATIONS`. On success the backing schedule advances: `completed_cycle_count` is incremented by 1 and `next_due_date` is set to the calendar date of the task's `window_end` plus the schedule's `cadence_days` days. Both the task and the schedule have their `version` incremented.

**Voiding a count.** An `InventorySupervisor` or `ShrinkController` may abandon a task that will never be completed, optionally with a `note`, from either `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS`, landing it in the terminal `VOIDED`. Every counted line on the task is deleted and `counted_line_count` resets to 0. Unlike a reconcile, a void neither advances the schedule's `next_due_date` nor increments its `completed_cycle_count` — an abandoned count buys the store no time.

### 3.6. Variance Investigation: Root Cause, Segregated Approval and Attribution

An investigation exists to answer one question: why did the shelf disagree with the book, and which shrink bucket should absorb the value? Investigations are never created by a client; they appear only as a consequence of a count submission, and they are discovered through the investigation list read. An investigation's `status` runs through `OPEN`, `IN_REVIEW`, `CAUSE_PROPOSED`, `CAUSE_APPROVED` and terminates in either `CLOSED` or `WAIVED`. Any transition attempted from a state the requested step does not declare — including any transition out of `CLOSED` or `WAIVED` — is rejected with `ERR_INVESTIGATION_INVALID_STATE`.

**Assignment.** An `InventoryAnalyst` or `InventorySupervisor` assigns an investigator to an `OPEN` investigation, supplying `assigned_investigator_id` — the opaque operator identifier of the analyst who will do the work — and moving the investigation to `IN_REVIEW`.

**Proposing a cause.** An `InventoryAnalyst` or `InventorySupervisor` proposes a root cause, supplying `proposed_root_cause`, which is one of `THEFT`, `DAMAGE`, `PROCESS_ERROR` or `VENDOR_SHORTAGE`, an `evidence_note` of 1 to 2000 characters supporting the proposal, and `expected_investigation_version` as required by §2.5. A proposal is accepted from `IN_REVIEW`, moving the investigation to `CAUSE_PROPOSED`, and is also accepted from `CAUSE_PROPOSED` itself, in which case the investigation stays in `CAUSE_PROPOSED` and the earlier proposal is superseded. Both branches behave identically in every other respect: `proposed_root_cause` and `evidence_note` are overwritten by the new submission, `proposed_by_user_id` is set to the acting `X-User-ID`, and `proposal_round` is incremented by 1. Because a later proposal legitimately displaces an earlier one, a sequential second proposal would succeed on its own merits — which is exactly why the version check exists: without it, an analyst working from a stale reading would erase a colleague's root cause and evidence without ever having seen them.

**Returning a proposal.** An `InventorySupervisor` or `ShrinkController` may send a proposal back for more evidence, optionally with a `note`, moving the investigation from `CAUSE_PROPOSED` to `IN_REVIEW`. The returner may not be the proposer: a match between the acting `X-User-ID` and `proposed_by_user_id` is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. On success `proposed_root_cause` is reset to null and `proposed_by_user_id` is reset to null, but `proposal_round` is **not** decremented — the counter records how many proposals have been made, not how many currently stand. This edge closes the revision loop from `IN_REVIEW` through `CAUSE_PROPOSED` back to `IN_REVIEW`.

**Approving a cause.** An `InventorySupervisor` or `ShrinkController` approves the standing proposal, moving the investigation from `CAUSE_PROPOSED` to `CAUSE_APPROVED`. Two guards apply beyond the state check. The approver may not be the proposer, again reported as `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. And the covering shrink period — the period named by the investigation's `shrink_period_id` — must be in `OPEN` status; if it has been provisionally or finally closed, the approval is rejected with `ERR_SHRINK_PERIOD_INVALID_STATE`. On success `approved_root_cause` is frozen from the standing `proposed_root_cause` and `approved_by_user_id` is set to the acting `X-User-ID`. Crucially the money does not move yet: the amount stays in the period's `unresolved_cents` under the provisional variant, and attribution to a named bucket happens only at close. The position also remains `RESTRICTED`, because a `CAUSE_APPROVED` investigation is still non-terminal.

**Closing an investigation.** An `InventorySupervisor` or `ShrinkController` closes a `CAUSE_APPROVED` investigation, and this is the head of a four-hop cascade. The covering period must still be `OPEN`, otherwise `ERR_SHRINK_PERIOD_INVALID_STATE`. Hop one sets the investigation's status to `CLOSED`. Hop two, caused by hop one, moves the investigation's `variance_value_cents` out of the period's `unresolved_cents` and into the bucket named by `approved_root_cause` — `theft_cents` for `THEFT`, `damage_cents` for `DAMAGE`, `process_error_cents` for `PROCESS_ERROR`, `vendor_shortage_cents` for `VENDOR_SHORTAGE` — leaving `total_attributable_variance_cents` unchanged by construction, and re-deriving `provisional_total_shrink_cents`, `final_total_shrink_cents` and `shrink_rate_bps`. Hop three, caused by hop two and occurring **only** when `approved_root_cause` is `VENDOR_SHORTAGE`, reaches the vendor: the `primary_vendor_id` of the SKU on the investigation's originating count line has its `shortage_event_count` incremented by 1 and its `tag_compliance_score_bps` reduced by 1500 basis points, floored at 0. Hop four, caused by hop three, re-derives that vendor's `required_verification_sample_pct` from the new score band and re-freezes `required_sample_units` on every one of that vendor's tag batches still in `DRAFT` or `ENCODED`, so a shortage finding tightens the vendor's future receipt sampling as well as its score. Alongside the cascade the investigated position's `promising_state` is re-derived — the position is no longer held back by a non-terminal investigation — and the store's `fulfillment_status` follows. Both the investigation's and the period's `version` are incremented.

**Waiving an investigation.** The only route into `WAIVED` is the system event described in §3.5: a return-to-count on the parent task deletes the originating counted line, and any investigation still in `OPEN` is waived. A waived investigation's `variance_value_cents` leaves the covering period's `unresolved_cents` and the period's totals are re-derived. A waived amount contributes to no bucket under either variant.

### 3.7. On-Hand Adjustments: Authority, Approval, Posting and Reversal

An adjustment is the only instrument that moves `on_hand_units`. It carries two independent state dimensions on the same row, `approval_state` over `NOT_REQUIRED`, `PENDING`, `APPROVED` and `REJECTED`, and `posting_state` over `UNPOSTED`, `POSTED` and `REVERSED`. The two advance separately and never drive each other: approving an adjustment does not post it, posting one does not change its approval, an adjustment created as `NOT_REQUIRED` may be posted with no approval step ever occurring, and an adjustment that is approved but never posted stays `UNPOSTED` forever and contributes nothing to any position. `REJECTED` is terminal on the approval dimension and `REVERSED` is terminal on the posting dimension. Any step requested from a state that does not permit it is rejected with `ERR_ADJUSTMENT_INVALID_STATE`.

**Requesting an adjustment.** An `InventoryAnalyst`, `InventorySupervisor` or `ShrinkController` requests an adjustment against a position, supplying `delta_units`, `reason_code`, `expected_stock_position_version` and optionally `investigation_id`. `delta_units` is a signed whole-unit movement and must not be zero — a zero is rejected with `ERR_ZERO_DELTA_UNITS`. `reason_code` is one of `COUNT_VARIANCE`, `NEGATIVE_CORRECTION`, `PHANTOM_CLEAR` or `PHYSICAL_RECONCILIATION`; the latter three arise in practice only from the integrity scan and the physical-inventory reconciliation described below. `requested_by_user_id` is set from the acting `X-User-ID`, `posting_state` is set to `UNPOSTED`, and the referenced position's `version` is incremented — the adjustment request itself is a mutation of the position's audit surface even though it moves no units yet. The version check exists precisely because the authority threshold is frozen at creation against the position's then-current data; see §2.5.

**The authority threshold.** `value_impact_cents` and `authority_threshold_cents` are both computed once at creation and frozen on the row. The ladder is evaluated in the following order and the exemptions come first.

1. **Exemptions.** If the adjustment was minted by the integrity scan on its auto-correct branch — that is, a `NEGATIVE_CORRECTION` or `PHANTOM_CLEAR` that the scan chose to post immediately — then `authority_threshold_cents` is 0, `approval_state` is `NOT_REQUIRED`, and the rest of the ladder is skipped. Likewise, if `reason_code` is `PHYSICAL_RECONCILIATION`, `authority_threshold_cents` is 0, `approval_state` is `NOT_REQUIRED`, and the ladder is skipped. Note that a threshold of 0 does not mean "always requires approval"; the exemption sets `approval_state` directly and the comparison below is never reached.
2. **Value impact.** `value_impact_cents` is the absolute value of `delta_units` multiplied by the `unit_cost_cents` of the SKU behind the position. A `delta_units` of −8 against a 1234-cent item gives 9872.
3. **Root-cause strictness.** A root cause tightens the threshold, but only once the investigation that established it is finished. The governing cause is the `approved_root_cause` of the investigation named by `investigation_id`, and only when `investigation_id` is not null **and** that investigation's `status` is `CLOSED`. An investigation sitting in `CAUSE_APPROVED` but not yet closed is treated as if there were no linked cause at all. The strictness factor is 5000 basis points when the cause is `THEFT`, 7500 basis points when it is `VENDOR_SHORTAGE`, and 10000 basis points in every other case — including `DAMAGE`, `PROCESS_ERROR`, a null `investigation_id`, and a linked investigation that has not reached `CLOSED`.
4. **Repeat-offender penalty.** Count the sibling adjustments on the same `stock_position_id` whose `posting_state` is `POSTED`; rows in `REVERSED` do not count, and neither do rows still `UNPOSTED`. The penalty is 1000 basis points for each such prior posting, capped at 5000 basis points, so the fifth and every later prior posting adds nothing.
5. **Base amount.** Take the smaller of the position's category `variance_tolerance_value_cents` and 100000 cents, then take the larger of that result and 5000 cents. A category tolerance of 20000 gives a base of 20000; a tolerance of 150000 is first pulled down to 100000; a tolerance of 2000 is first kept at 2000 and then raised to 5000.
6. **Effective threshold.** Multiply the base by the strictness factor divided by 10000, then multiply that by the quantity 10000 minus the penalty, divided by 10000. The two basis-point multiplications are applied left to right in exact decimal arithmetic and the result is floored to a whole cent **once**, at the end. Then take the larger of that floored figure and 1000 cents. `authority_threshold_cents` is that number.
7. **Decision.** Approval is required when `value_impact_cents` is greater than **or equal to** `authority_threshold_cents`; the boundary requires approval. When approval is required `approval_state` is `PENDING`, otherwise `NOT_REQUIRED`.

To make the ladder concrete: a category tolerance of 20000, a linked and `CLOSED` investigation whose `approved_root_cause` is `THEFT`, and one prior `POSTED` sibling on the position give a candidate of 20000, a base of 20000, a strictness of 5000 basis points and a penalty of 1000 basis points, so the threshold is the floor of 20000 × 0.5 × 0.9, which is 9000. Against that threshold a `delta_units` of −8 on a 1234-cent item has a value impact of 9872, which is at or above 9000 and therefore lands `PENDING`; a `delta_units` of −5 on the same item has a value impact of 6170, which is below 9000 and therefore lands `NOT_REQUIRED`.

**Approving.** An `InventorySupervisor` or `ShrinkController` approves a `PENDING` adjustment, moving `approval_state` to `APPROVED` while `posting_state` stays `UNPOSTED`. The approver may not be the requester: a match between the acting `X-User-ID` and `requested_by_user_id` is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. A second, financial guard applies on top: when `value_impact_cents` is strictly greater than 500000 cents, only a `ShrinkController` may approve, and an `InventorySupervisor` attempting it is rejected with `ERR_ADJUSTMENT_ABOVE_SUPERVISOR_CAP`. An impact of exactly 500000 is within the supervisor's cap. On success `approved_by_user_id` is set to the acting `X-User-ID`.

**Rejecting.** An `InventorySupervisor` or `ShrinkController` rejects a `PENDING` adjustment, optionally with a `note`, moving `approval_state` to the terminal `REJECTED` and setting `approved_by_user_id` to the acting `X-User-ID`. The same segregation-of-duties guard applies. A rejected adjustment's `posting_state` stays `UNPOSTED` and can never leave it — the posting step's approval gate can no longer be satisfied.

**Posting: the five-hop cascade.** An `InventorySupervisor` or `ShrinkController` posts an adjustment, and four preconditions apply. `posting_state` must be `UNPOSTED`, otherwise `ERR_ADJUSTMENT_INVALID_STATE`. `approval_state` must be either `NOT_REQUIRED` or `APPROVED`, otherwise `ERR_ADJUSTMENT_APPROVAL_REQUIRED` — this catches both the still-`PENDING` and the `REJECTED` row. When `investigation_id` is not null, that investigation's `status` must be `CLOSED`, otherwise `ERR_INVESTIGATION_NOT_CLOSED`; an adjustment justified by an investigation may not move units until the attribution work behind it is finished. And the position's `tag_integrity_hold` must be false, otherwise `ERR_TAG_INTEGRITY_HOLD` — a position whose tag reads are untrustworthy is not a position whose ledger may be edited.

On success the cascade runs in a fixed order, each hop caused by the one before it. Hop one sets `posting_state` to `POSTED` and stamps `posted_at` with the current logical-clock instant. Hop two re-derives the position: `on_hand_units` becomes `baseline_on_hand_units` plus the sum of `delta_units` over every adjustment on that position whose `posting_state` is `POSTED`, and `book_value_cents`, `unit_accuracy_pct` and `value_accuracy_pct` follow. When `reason_code` is `PHANTOM_CLEAR`, `phantom_units` is also set to 0 as the correction lands. Hop three re-derives the position's `promising_state` — a negative `on_hand_units` or a `value_accuracy_pct` that has fallen below the category's `min_accuracy_pct` flips the position downward. Hop four re-derives the store's `blocked_position_count`, `restricted_position_count`, `accuracy_index_bps` and `fulfillment_status`. Hop five fires only when hop four leaves the store's `fulfillment_status` at `SUSPENDED`: every count schedule at that store whose `status` is `ACTIVE` has its `cadence_days` replaced by the larger of 7 and the floor of half its current `cadence_days`, and its `next_due_date` set to the calendar date of the current logical-clock instant, making every such schedule immediately due. A cadence of 30 becomes 15; a cadence of 13 becomes 7 by way of the floor of 6.5 being raised to the minimum; a cadence of 7 stays 7. Both the adjustment's and the position's `version` are incremented.

**Reversing.** An `InventorySupervisor` or `ShrinkController` reverses a `POSTED` adjustment, optionally with a `note`. The reverser may not be the requester, again reported as `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. On success `posting_state` becomes the terminal `REVERSED`, `reversed_at` is stamped with the current logical-clock instant, and `approval_state` is left exactly as it was — a reversal is not a disapproval. Because the row has left the `POSTED` set, `on_hand_units` re-derives to `baseline_on_hand_units` plus the sum of `delta_units` over the remaining `POSTED` adjustments, which restores the pre-posting figure exactly, with no drift, as §2.10 requires. When `reason_code` is `PHANTOM_CLEAR` the position's `phantom_units` is restored to the value the posting had cleared. `book_value_cents`, `unit_accuracy_pct`, `value_accuracy_pct` and `promising_state` are re-derived, and the store's posture follows. One thing is deliberately **not** undone: if the earlier posting's hop five accelerated the store's count schedules, a recovery of `fulfillment_status` does not restore the old `cadence_days` or the old `next_due_date`. Schedule acceleration is one-way, as §2.7 states.

**The integrity scan.** An `InventoryAnalyst` or `InventorySupervisor` may run a detection pass over one position. The scan reads exactly that position, the category of its SKU and the logical clock; sibling positions are never consulted. It takes no request body. Two rules run in a fixed order, and rule two reads the on-hand value that rule one leaves behind.

Rule one addresses a negative on-hand. It fires when `on_hand_units` is strictly below 0. When it fires, the scan mints a `NEGATIVE_CORRECTION` adjustment whose `delta_units` is the arithmetic negation of the current `on_hand_units` — so a position at −4 receives a `delta_units` of +4 — with `investigation_id` null and `requested_by_user_id` set to the literal string `system`. Which branch that adjustment takes depends on two conditions together: the absolute value of `on_hand_units` must be at most 5 units **and** the category's `auto_correct_negative` must be true. When both hold, the adjustment is minted with `approval_state` `NOT_REQUIRED` and `posting_state` `POSTED`, so it takes effect immediately, `on_hand_units` rises to exactly 0, and the position's `negative_correction_count` is incremented by 1. When either fails — the shortfall is 6 units or more, or the category forbids auto-correction — the adjustment is minted with `approval_state` `PENDING` and `posting_state` `UNPOSTED` so that a supervisor must approve it, `on_hand_units` is left untouched and therefore still negative, and the position's `tag_integrity_hold` is set to true. That hold is significant: it will block the very posting the scan just queued until a `VERIFIED_PASS` tag batch clears it.

Rule two addresses phantom inventory, and it is evaluated against the on-hand value as it stands after rule one. It fires only when all five of the following hold: `on_hand_units` is strictly greater than 0; `reserved_units` is exactly 0; `last_counted_at` is not null; the number of calendar days from the UTC date of `last_counted_at` to the UTC date of the current logical-clock instant is at least 45; and `on_hand_units` is at most 3. When it fires, `phantom_units` is set to the current `on_hand_units`, a `PHANTOM_CLEAR` adjustment is minted with a `delta_units` equal to the negation of that figure, `investigation_id` null, `requested_by_user_id` the literal `system`, `approval_state` `NOT_REQUIRED` and `posting_state` `POSTED`, and once that correction posts `phantom_units` returns to 0 and `on_hand_units` falls to exactly 0.

After both rules have run — and only once, not once per rule — the position's `promising_state` is re-derived and then the owning store's `fulfillment_status` follows, and the position's `version` is incremented by 1. Three pinned outcomes illustrate the interaction. A position at −4 in a category with `auto_correct_negative` true is auto-corrected to 0 with `negative_correction_count` becoming 1, and rule two does not fire because 0 is not strictly greater than 0. A position at −6 in the same category yields a `PENDING`, `UNPOSTED` correction of +6, the hold is set, `on_hand_units` stays at −6 and `promising_state` becomes `BLOCKED`. A position at 2 units with `reserved_units` 0 and `last_counted_at` on 2026-06-01 read against a clock of 2026-08-01 spans 61 days, which is at least 45, and 2 is at most 3, so a `PHANTOM_CLEAR` of −2 auto-posts and `on_hand_units` becomes 0.

The scan is idempotent in effect but not in record-keeping: running it a second time on a position already corrected to 0 detects nothing and mints nothing. Its response reports `corrections_applied` as the number of adjustments minted by this invocation, `negative_corrected_units` as the units restored by rule one and `phantom_zeroed_units` as the units removed by rule two, `auto_posted_adjustment_ids` and `pending_adjustment_ids` as the identifiers of the minted rows split by which branch they took, and then the resulting position state as `resulting_on_hand_units`, `resulting_phantom_units`, `resulting_promising_state` and `tag_integrity_hold`.

### 3.8. Shrink Periods: Attribution, Closure and Vendor Chargebacks

A shrink period is the accounting envelope that collects variance value for one store over a stretch of calendar days. A `ShrinkController` or `InventorySupervisor` opens one by supplying `store_id`, `period_start`, `period_end` and `sales_at_cost_cents`. The two dates are calendar dates in `YYYY-MM-DD` form and the range is inclusive at both ends; `period_end` must be on or after `period_start`, and an inverted range is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PERIOD_RANGE`. `sales_at_cost_cents` must be strictly greater than 0 because it is the denominator of the shrink rate. Periods for one store may not overlap: if any existing period for the same store has an inclusive date range that intersects the requested one, the creation is rejected with `ERR_SHRINK_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. Sharing a single endpoint date counts as an intersection. A new period starts in `OPEN` status with `reopen_count` at 0, an empty `chargeback_issued_vendor_ids`, and `theft_cents`, `damage_cents`, `process_error_cents`, `vendor_shortage_cents`, `unresolved_cents`, `total_attributable_variance_cents`, `provisional_total_shrink_cents`, `final_total_shrink_cents` and `shrink_rate_bps` all at 0.

**Attribution and its two variants.** A period's buckets are derived, never written directly. The candidate set is every variance investigation whose `shrink_period_id` names this period, processed in ascending `created_at`. Membership is decided by that foreign key, written once when the investigation was opened — never by comparing the investigation's date against `period_start` and `period_end` — so reopening a period cannot silently gain or lose rows. Integer addition makes the traversal order immaterial to the arithmetic; it is pinned for auditability.

Under the **provisional** variant, an investigation in `CLOSED` adds its `variance_value_cents` to the bucket named by its `approved_root_cause`: `theft_cents` for `THEFT`, `damage_cents` for `DAMAGE`, `process_error_cents` for `PROCESS_ERROR`, `vendor_shortage_cents` for `VENDOR_SHORTAGE`. An investigation in `OPEN`, `IN_REVIEW`, `CAUSE_PROPOSED` or `CAUSE_APPROVED` adds its `variance_value_cents` to `unresolved_cents` instead. An investigation in `WAIVED` contributes nothing. Any waterfall allocation already written into the buckets by a physical-inventory reconciliation (§3.9) sits alongside these amounts. `provisional_total_shrink_cents` is the sum of the four named buckets plus `unresolved_cents`.

Under the **final** variant the loop is identical except that the non-terminal branch is deleted outright: `OPEN`, `IN_REVIEW`, `CAUSE_PROPOSED` and `CAUSE_APPROVED` investigations are excluded entirely rather than parked, and `WAIVED` is still excluded. `final_total_shrink_cents` is therefore the sum of the four named buckets alone. Whenever any investigation is non-terminal the final total is strictly smaller than the provisional total on the same data.

`total_attributable_variance_cents` is always the sum of the four named buckets plus `unresolved_cents`, which is the conserved quantity of §2.10. `shrink_rate_bps` is 10000 multiplied by the active total, divided by `sales_at_cost_cents`, rounded half up to a whole basis point; the active total is `final_total_shrink_cents` when the period's `status` is `FINAL_CLOSED` and `provisional_total_shrink_cents` in every other status. The divergence is pinned by example: with one `CLOSED` `THEFT` investigation of 6633 cents, one `IN_REVIEW` investigation of 32250 cents and `sales_at_cost_cents` of 5000000, the provisional view is a theft bucket of 6633, `unresolved_cents` of 32250, a total of 38883 and a rate of 78 basis points, while the final view is a theft bucket of 6633, `unresolved_cents` of 0, a total of 6633 and a rate of 13 basis points.

The attribution computed read exposes both variants side by side so the divergence is observable without closing anything. It returns `theft_cents`, `damage_cents`, `process_error_cents`, `vendor_shortage_cents`, `unresolved_cents`, `total_attributable_variance_cents`, `provisional_total_shrink_cents`, `final_total_shrink_cents`, `provisional_shrink_rate_bps` and `final_shrink_rate_bps` — the two rates being 10000 times the respective total over `sales_at_cost_cents`, each rounded half up — plus `active_variant`, which names the variant the period's stored `shrink_rate_bps` currently reflects.

**Closure.** A period runs `OPEN` to `PROVISIONAL_CLOSED` to `FINAL_CLOSED`, with a reopen loop back from `PROVISIONAL_CLOSED` to `OPEN`. Every one of these steps belongs to the `ShrinkController` alone, and any of them attempted from the wrong status is rejected with `ERR_SHRINK_PERIOD_INVALID_STATE`. A provisional close freezes the buckets at their provisional values — `unresolved_cents` keeps carrying every non-terminal investigation's amount — re-derives both totals and `shrink_rate_bps` from the provisional variant, and sets `status` to `PROVISIONAL_CLOSED`. A reopen, which accepts an optional `note`, increments `reopen_count` by 1 and returns `status` to `OPEN`, which is what makes investigation approve-cause and close possible again so a mis-attributed bucket can be re-attributed; this closes the cycle from `OPEN` through `PROVISIONAL_CLOSED` and back. A final close requires that every investigation whose `shrink_period_id` names this period be in `CLOSED` or `WAIVED`; if even one is still non-terminal, it is rejected with `ERR_OPEN_INVESTIGATIONS`. On success `unresolved_cents` is forced to 0 — which changes nothing arithmetically, since no non-terminal investigations remain and the two variants have converged — `shrink_rate_bps` switches to the final-variant total, and `status` becomes the terminal `FINAL_CLOSED`.

**Vendor chargebacks.** A `ShrinkController` issues a chargeback against a vendor out of one period, supplying `shrink_period_id` in the body with the vendor identified by the path. The computation runs in five steps and the evaluation order matters.

Step one aggregates the gross shortage attributable to this vendor: sum `variance_value_cents` over every investigation whose `shrink_period_id` is the named period, whose `status` is exactly `CLOSED`, whose `approved_root_cause` is exactly `VENDOR_SHORTAGE`, and whose originating count line references a SKU whose `primary_vendor_id` is the path vendor. Investigations in `CAUSE_APPROVED` are excluded — only a closed attribution supports a charge. Membership is by the `shrink_period_id` foreign key and includes every such row regardless of the period's own status, so a provisionally closed or finally closed period can still be charged against. If the sum is 0, the request is rejected with `ERR_NO_VENDOR_SHORTAGE`. A vendor may be charged out of a given period only once: if the vendor already appears in that period's `chargeback_issued_vendor_ids`, the request is rejected with `ERR_CHARGEBACK_ALREADY_ISSUED`.

Step two selects the tier **on the gross shortage**: `TIER_1` when the shortage is at most 50000 cents, `TIER_2` when it is at most 200000 cents, and `TIER_3` above that. The chargeback rate is 8000 basis points for `TIER_1`, 9000 for `TIER_2` and 10000 for `TIER_3`, and `chargeback_gross_cents` is the shortage multiplied by that rate divided by 10000, rounded half up.

Step three applies the goodwill credit, and its rate is keyed on **the same gross tier selected in step two**, not on the net amount: 1500 basis points for `TIER_1`, 1000 for `TIER_2` and 500 for `TIER_3`. `vendor_credit_cents` is `chargeback_gross_cents` multiplied by that credit rate divided by 10000, rounded half up. Step four derives `net_chargeback_cents` as `chargeback_gross_cents` minus `vendor_credit_cents`, and that net feeds nothing back into the tiering.

**Re-tiering off the net is forbidden**, and the divergence is pinned. A gross shortage of 205000 cents falls in `TIER_3`, so the rate is 10000 basis points and `chargeback_gross_cents` is 205000. Keyed correctly on the gross tier, the credit rate is 500 basis points, `vendor_credit_cents` is 10250 and `net_chargeback_cents` is 194750. Keyed incorrectly on the net — 194750 would fall in `TIER_2` and draw 1000 basis points — the credit would be 20500 and the net 184500. Only 10250 and 194750 are correct. Rounding half up is applied independently in step two and step three and never to the net.

Step five writes the vendor ledger: `lifetime_chargeback_gross_cents` grows by `chargeback_gross_cents`, `credited_cents` grows by `vendor_credit_cents`, `chargeback_balance_cents` is re-derived as `lifetime_chargeback_gross_cents` minus `credited_cents`, and the vendor is appended to the period's `chargeback_issued_vendor_ids`. Both the vendor's and the period's `version` are incremented. The response reports `vendor_id`, `shrink_period_id`, `attributable_shortage_cents`, `tier`, `chargeback_gross_cents`, `vendor_credit_cents`, `net_chargeback_cents` and then the post-write ledger as `lifetime_chargeback_gross_cents`, `credited_cents` and `chargeback_balance_cents`.

**Additional credits.** A `ShrinkController` may grant a further rebuttal or goodwill credit against a vendor's outstanding balance, supplying `credit_cents` and an optional `note`. The credit may not exceed the vendor's current `chargeback_balance_cents`; an excess is rejected with `ERR_CREDIT_EXCEEDS_BALANCE`, and a credit exactly equal to the balance is allowed and drives it to 0. On success `credited_cents` grows by `credit_cents` and `chargeback_balance_cents` is re-derived. `lifetime_chargeback_gross_cents` is never rewritten by a credit, which is what keeps the money invariant of §2.10 intact. The response reports `vendor_id`, `credit_cents`, `lifetime_chargeback_gross_cents`, `credited_cents` and `chargeback_balance_cents`.

### 3.9. The Physical Inventory: Blackout Geometry, Passes and the Reconciliation Waterfall

A physical inventory is the wall-to-wall count a third-party firm performs against a whole store. Its lifecycle runs `PLANNED`, `COUNTING`, `FIRST_PASS_COMPLETE`, then either `RECONCILING` and `CERTIFIED`, or out to `ABANDONED` from `PLANNED`. `CERTIFIED` and `ABANDONED` are terminal, and there is one loop, from `FIRST_PASS_COMPLETE` back to `COUNTING`. Any step requested from a status that does not permit it is rejected with `ERR_PHYSICAL_INVENTORY_INVALID_STATE`.

**Scheduling and the blackout interval.** A `ShrinkController` or `SystemAdmin` schedules one by supplying `store_id`, `counter_firm_id`, `scheduled_start_date`, `scheduled_end_date` and optionally `recount_threshold_bps`. The two dates are calendar dates and `scheduled_end_date` must be on or after `scheduled_start_date`; an inverted range is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PERIOD_RANGE`. `recount_threshold_bps` defaults to 150 when omitted and must be at least 1 and at most 10000. A new inventory starts in `PLANNED` with `recount_round` and `line_count` at 0, `book_value_cents`, `counted_value_cents`, `net_variance_cents` and `net_variance_bps` at 0, and `certified_at` null.

The scheduling window is guarded by a blackout rule whose geometry is deliberately different from the cycle-count window rule of §3.4. Take every other physical inventory at the same store whose `status` is `PLANNED`, `COUNTING`, `FIRST_PASS_COMPLETE`, `RECONCILING` or `CERTIFIED` — an `ABANDONED` inventory releases its interval and is skipped, and no time filter is applied, so inventories arbitrarily far in the past are still consulted. Each such inventory projects a blackout interval running from its `scheduled_start_date` minus 3 calendar days through its `scheduled_end_date` plus 3 calendar days, and both ends of that padded interval are **inclusive**. The requested interval, also inclusive at both ends, collides when the requested `scheduled_start_date` is on or before the existing blackout's upper end **and** the requested `scheduled_end_date` is on or after the existing blackout's lower end. A collision is rejected with `ERR_PHYSICAL_INVENTORY_BLACKOUT_CONFLICT`. Because the interval is closed on both sides, merely touching endpoints do collide — the opposite of the half-open cycle-count rule. Pinned: an existing inventory scheduled 2026-09-10 through 2026-09-12 blacks out 2026-09-07 through 2026-09-15, so a request for 2026-09-15 through 2026-09-16 collides because 2026-09-15 is on or before 2026-09-15, while a request for 2026-09-16 through 2026-09-17 is accepted because 2026-09-16 is after 2026-09-15.

**Counting.** The assigned counting firm — a `ThirdPartyCounter` whose `X-User-ID` matches the inventory's `counter_firm_id`, or a `ShrinkController` — starts the count, moving it from `PLANNED` to `COUNTING`. The same actors then submit lines, each supplying `item_id`, `counted_units` and `expected_physical_inventory_version`; `counted_units` must be at least 0, since counting nothing on the shelf is a legitimate observation. A stock position must exist for the pair of the inventory's store and the named SKU, otherwise `ERR_ITEM_NOT_STOCKED_AT_STORE`. On creation the line records its parent as `physical_inventory_id` — the same identifier by which the physical-inventory-line list narrows to one count — and freezes `book_units_snapshot` from that position's current `on_hand_units` and `book_value_snapshot_cents` as that snapshot multiplied by the SKU's `unit_cost_cents`, stamps `pass_round` with the inventory's current `recount_round`, and computes `variance_units` as `counted_units` minus `book_units_snapshot` and `variance_value_cents` as `variance_units` multiplied by `unit_cost_cents`. That valuation is at **pure book cost with no landed-cost uplift** — unlike the cycle-count valuation of §3.4, which applies the category's uplift factor. The result is signed, so a shortfall is negative.

A second line for the same SKU in the same pass supersedes the first: any earlier line for the same inventory, SKU and `pass_round` has its `superseded` flag set to true. More generally a line is superseded when another line exists for the same inventory and SKU with either a strictly greater `pass_round`, or the same `pass_round` and a strictly later `created_at`. The number of non-superseded lines in the current pass, counting the one being created, may not exceed the counting firm's `max_lines_per_pass`, otherwise `ERR_MAX_LINES_PER_PASS_EXCEEDED`. Because a superseding line replaces rather than adds, re-counting a SKU does not consume additional capacity. The inventory's `line_count` is then recomputed over the non-superseded lines of the current pass, and its `version` is incremented — which is what makes the version check meaningful: a supersede built on a stale reading would silently discard a colleague's more recent count of the same SKU.

**Completing a pass.** The counting firm or a `ShrinkController` closes the pass, moving the inventory from `COUNTING` to `FIRST_PASS_COMPLETE`. At least one line must exist for the current pass, otherwise `ERR_NO_COUNT_LINES`. The aggregates are then rolled up over the lines of the current `recount_round` whose `superseded` is false — earlier rounds are retained for audit and excluded from every aggregate. `book_value_cents` is the sum of `book_value_snapshot_cents` across those lines. `counted_value_cents` is the sum, across those lines, of `counted_units` multiplied by the SKU's `unit_cost_cents`. `net_variance_cents` is `counted_value_cents` minus `book_value_cents` and is signed. `net_variance_bps` is 10000 multiplied by the absolute value of `net_variance_cents`, divided by `book_value_cents`, floored to a whole basis point; when `book_value_cents` is 0 it is 0 rather than undefined. Pinned: a pass with a 100-unit book line at 1234 cents counted at 90 units and a 40-unit book line at 1500 cents counted at 30 units has a book value of 183400, a counted value of 156060, a net variance of −27340 and a `net_variance_bps` of the floor of 10000 × 27340 ÷ 183400, which is 1490.

**Ordering a recount.** A `ShrinkController` or `InventorySupervisor` may send the count back for a second pass, optionally with a `note`, moving it from `FIRST_PASS_COMPLETE` back to `COUNTING`. This is permitted only while `recount_round` is strictly below 1, so exactly one recount is available; a second attempt is rejected with `ERR_REWORK_LIMIT_REACHED`. The recount increments `recount_round` by 1 and resets `line_count` to 0 for the new pass. Prior lines are **not** deleted — they stay on file under their own `pass_round` and simply stop contributing to the aggregates. This closes the cycle from `COUNTING` through `FIRST_PASS_COMPLETE` and back.

**Reconciling: the waterfall.** A `ShrinkController` reconciles a `FIRST_PASS_COMPLETE` inventory, moving it to `RECONCILING`. Two guards apply. First, the variance must be either small enough or already re-counted: the reconcile proceeds when `net_variance_bps` is strictly below `recount_threshold_bps`, or when `recount_round` is at least 1. A first pass whose `net_variance_bps` is greater than **or equal to** `recount_threshold_bps` is rejected with `ERR_RECOUNT_REQUIRED` — the boundary demands a recount — and the way past that rejection is to order the recount, after which the reconcile is permitted regardless of the variance. Second, a shrink period for the inventory's store whose inclusive range from `period_start` through `period_end` contains `scheduled_end_date` must exist with `status` exactly `OPEN`; otherwise `ERR_NO_COVERING_SHRINK_PERIOD`. Note the difference from a count submit, which also accepts `PROVISIONAL_CLOSED`; a physical reconciliation writes into the buckets and therefore requires a fully open period.

On success, first the book is moved. For every non-superseded line of the current pass whose `variance_units` is not zero, walking those lines in ascending line-creation order, an adjustment is minted against the stock position for that store and SKU with `delta_units` equal to the line's `variance_units`, `reason_code` `PHYSICAL_RECONCILIATION`, `investigation_id` null, `approval_state` `NOT_REQUIRED` and `posting_state` `POSTED` — posted immediately and exempt from the authority ladder as §3.7 states. Each posting re-derives that position's `on_hand_units`, `book_value_cents`, `unit_accuracy_pct`, `value_accuracy_pct` and `promising_state`, and the store's `fulfillment_status` follows once every touched position has settled.

Then the shrink buckets are filled. The **explained** portion is read from the covering period's final variant, that is from `final_total_shrink_cents`, which counts only `CLOSED` investigations; non-terminal investigations therefore do not reduce the unexplained pool. The **unexplained** portion is the larger of 0 and the absolute value of `net_variance_cents` minus that explained figure. The unexplained amount is then poured into three buckets in a **fixed order that never depends on the data** — `process_error_cents` first, then `damage_cents`, then `theft_cents` — each with a cap computed as a share of the absolute value of `net_variance_cents`: the floor of that absolute value times 3000 divided by 10000 for `process_error_cents`, times 2000 divided by 10000 for `damage_cents`, and times 4000 divided by 10000 for `theft_cents`. Each bucket takes the smaller of what remains and its own cap, and what it takes is subtracted from the remainder before the next bucket is considered. Whatever remains after the third bucket falls through into `unresolved_cents` with no cap at all. `total_attributable_variance_cents` and both period totals are then re-derived.

Pinned against the example above: with a net variance of −27340 and a covering period that already holds one `CLOSED` `THEFT` investigation of 6633 cents, the explained figure is 6633 and the unexplained is 20707. The `process_error_cents` cap is the floor of 27340 × 0.30, which is 8202, so it takes 8202 and 12505 remains. The `damage_cents` cap is the floor of 27340 × 0.20, which is 5468, so it takes 5468 and 7037 remains. The `theft_cents` cap is the floor of 27340 × 0.40, which is 10936, which exceeds the remaining 7037, so it takes 7037 and nothing remains. `unresolved_cents` grows by 0. The period's buckets then read `theft_cents` 6633 plus 7037, that is 13670, `damage_cents` 5468, `process_error_cents` 8202, `vendor_shortage_cents` 0 and `unresolved_cents` 0, summing to exactly 27340 — the absolute value of the net variance, with zero drift, because every cap is floored and the remainder is exact.

**Certifying and abandoning.** A `ShrinkController` certifies a `RECONCILING` inventory, moving it to the terminal `CERTIFIED` and stamping `certified_at` with the current logical-clock instant. The shrink period covering `scheduled_end_date` must **not** be in `OPEN` status, otherwise `ERR_SHRINK_PERIOD_STILL_OPEN`: certification asserts that the shrink figures are settled, so it may only follow a provisional or final close. Separately, a `ShrinkController` or `SystemAdmin` may abandon a `PLANNED` inventory, optionally with a `note`, moving it to the terminal `ABANDONED`; that releases the store's blackout interval so a new inventory may be scheduled over the same dates.

### 3.10. Store Fulfillment Posture: The Omnichannel Consequence

A store's `fulfillment_status` is never set by a request body. It is re-derived at the end of every request that changes any position's `promising_state` or `unit_accuracy_pct`, or that adds a position to the store, and every transition of the store's posture is a system event with no endpoint of its own.

The derivation begins with `total_position_count`, the count of every stock position at the store with no status filter. **The empty-store case is decided first:** when `total_position_count` is 0, `accuracy_index_bps` is 10000, `blocked_position_count` and `restricted_position_count` are 0, `fulfillment_status` is `ELIGIBLE`, and nothing further is evaluated. A store with nothing on its shelves is vacuously accurate rather than a division by zero.

Otherwise `accuracy_index_bps` is the sum of `unit_accuracy_pct` across every position at the store, multiplied by 100, divided by `total_position_count`, and floored to a whole basis point. `blocked_position_count` is the number of positions whose `promising_state` is `BLOCKED` and `restricted_position_count` the number whose `promising_state` is `RESTRICTED`. From those, `blocked_ratio_bps` is 10000 multiplied by `blocked_position_count`, divided by `total_position_count`, floored; `restricted_ratio_bps` is the same computation over `restricted_position_count`. The posture is then decided by the first matching clause of a three-step ladder. `fulfillment_status` is `SUSPENDED` when `blocked_ratio_bps` is at least 2000 **or** `accuracy_index_bps` is strictly below 9000. Otherwise it is `PROBATION` when `blocked_position_count` is at least 1 **or** `restricted_ratio_bps` is at least 3000 **or** `accuracy_index_bps` is strictly below 9500. Otherwise it is `ELIGIBLE`. All inputs are read after every position-level recomputation of the current request has completed, and ordering is immaterial because only sums and counts are involved.

Three outcomes are pinned. Two positions at 95.00 and 100.00, both `PROMISABLE`, give an `accuracy_index_bps` of the floor of 195.00 × 100 ÷ 2, which is 9750, and the store is `ELIGIBLE`. The same two positions with one `BLOCKED` give a `blocked_ratio_bps` of the floor of 10000 × 1 ÷ 2, which is 5000 and is at least 2000, so the store is `SUSPENDED`. Six positions with one `BLOCKED` give a `blocked_ratio_bps` of the floor of 10000 × 1 ÷ 6, which is 1666 and is below 2000, so the first clause does not fire; but `blocked_position_count` is at least 1, so the store is on `PROBATION`. A single blocked position therefore always demotes a store at least to `PROBATION`, however large the store is.

The posture carries real consequences. On `PROBATION` the store may still fulfil, but every `RESTRICTED` position is excluded from omnichannel promising. On `SUSPENDED` the store is removed from omnichannel promising entirely, and — as §2.7 and §3.7 describe — every count schedule at that store whose `status` is `ACTIVE` has its `cadence_days` replaced by the larger of 7 and the floor of half its current value, with `next_due_date` set to the calendar date of the current logical-clock instant. Both entries into `SUSPENDED`, whether from `ELIGIBLE` or from `PROBATION`, carry that same acceleration. Recovery moves — `SUSPENDED` to `PROBATION`, `SUSPENDED` to `ELIGIBLE`, and `PROBATION` to `ELIGIBLE` — are governed by the same ladder read in reverse: a return to `PROBATION` requires `blocked_ratio_bps` below 2000 and `accuracy_index_bps` at least 9000 while at least one probation clause still holds, and a return to `ELIGIBLE` requires `blocked_position_count` exactly 0, `restricted_ratio_bps` strictly below 3000 and `accuracy_index_bps` at least 9500. **No recovery ever restores an accelerated `cadence_days` or the `next_due_date` that came with it.** Every posture change increments the store's `version` by 1.

The fulfillment-eligibility computed read exposes every input of the derivation so the verdict can be audited without inference. It returns `store_id`, `total_position_count`, `blocked_position_count`, `restricted_position_count`, `blocked_ratio_bps`, `restricted_ratio_bps`, `accuracy_index_bps`, `fulfillment_status`, and `demotion_reason`, which names the clause that decided the posture — the blocked ratio, the accuracy index, the presence of a blocked position, or the restricted ratio — and is empty for a store that is `ELIGIBLE`. For a store with no positions the read returns a `total_position_count` of 0, an `accuracy_index_bps` of 10000 and a `fulfillment_status` of `ELIGIBLE`.
