# Store Cash Office & Deposit Reconciliation API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A retail chain's cash office is the one place in the business where physical money, point-of-sale data and bank statements are forced to agree. Today that agreement is reached on paper: a clerk issues a starting bank out of the safe, a cashier drops cash during the shift, someone counts the till at close, and days later a treasury analyst discovers that the bank credited less than the store handed over. By then the cashier who was short has worked four more shifts, the drop bag has been re-counted twice, and nobody can say whether the money went missing at the register, in the safe, or in transit. Chains lose real money to this ambiguity, and — more damagingly — they lose the ability to distinguish honest error from theft.

This document specifies the Store Cash Office & Deposit Reconciliation API, a back-office service that makes every movement of cash an auditable ledger event and every disagreement a tracked research case. The service owns eleven cooperating record types: stores and their cash-office policy parameters, cashiers with their variance history, safes (both conventional drop safes and note-validating smart safes), the daily sales journal, register till sessions, the append-only safe transaction ledger, armored and smart-safe deposits, bank credit records, card settlement advices, variance research cases, and change orders with their denomination plans. Every endpoint is mounted under the `/cash-ops` prefix, with the sole exception of the test-driving clock endpoint described in §2.4.

Two design commitments shape everything that follows. The first is **segregation of duties**: the person who counts a till may not reconcile it, the person who arms a safe-open request may not execute the open, the person who prepares a deposit bag may not seal it, and only a loss-prevention investigator may close an escalated theft case. The second is **derived truth**: balances, variances, rolling windows and the journal's daily balanced/out-of-balance verdict are never typed in by an operator. They are recomputed by the server from the ledger rows and child records that support them, so that an auditor reading any record can trace the number back to the events that produced it.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

The rules in this section apply to every endpoint unless a later section explicitly narrows them. They are contract-level: response shape, header semantics, precision, and the order in which the server rejects a bad request are fixed and not negotiable per endpoint.

### 2.1. Operators, Roles and Header Identity

Every request — including reads and including the clock endpoint — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting operator, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role the operator is acting in. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the six role names below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If the headers are well-formed but the named role does not appear on the endpoint's authorized-role list, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The six roles are `Cashier`, `CashOfficeClerk`, `StoreManager`, `ArmoredCarrier`, `TreasuryAnalyst`, and `LossPreventionInvestigator`.

A `Cashier` operates a register till: takes the starting bank, makes mid-shift and closing drops, closes the register at shift end, and inserts sealed bags into a smart safe. Recording a till drop is exclusive to this role. A `Cashier` never sees the expected-cash figure and never counts their own till. A `CashOfficeClerk` runs the back-office cash room and is the only role that may open the day's journal, post POS tender totals, blind-count a till, order a recount, reconcile a till session, prepare an armored deposit, book a carrier pickup window, create a change order, or receive a change order into the safe; the clerk may not approve, void, or investigate anything. A `StoreManager` is the accountable store officer and the only role that may enroll a cashier, void a deposit, or approve or reject a change order; the manager also supplies the second pair of hands for dual control and escalates suspected theft. An `ArmoredCarrier` is external crew: it sees the deposits booked for pickup and signs for them, which is exclusive to the role. A `TreasuryAnalyst` is central treasury and is alone able to register a store, commission a safe, post a bank credit record, post a card settlement, or reconcile a deposit against the bank; the analyst never touches physical cash and never counts a till. A `LossPreventionInvestigator` investigates suspected theft and is the only role that may restore a frozen safe's posture or close a variance case with a disposition.

Identity semantics differ by role, and this is what ties `X-User-ID` to resource ownership. When `X-User-Role` is `Cashier`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Cashier` record; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Cashier` compare the resource's cashier reference to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a `Cashier` acting on a till session whose `cashier_id` differs from `X-User-ID` is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`, and the same comparison governs reading a cashier record or acknowledging coaching. For the other five roles — `CashOfficeClerk`, `StoreManager`, `ArmoredCarrier`, `TreasuryAnalyst`, and `LossPreventionInvestigator` — `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a rule demands provenance (for example as `open_requested_by_user_id` and `unlocked_at`'s companion on a safe, as `counted_by_user_id` on a till session, as `prepared_by_user_id` and `sealed_by_user_id` on a deposit, as `actor_user_id` on a safe ledger row, as `escalated_by_user_id` on a variance case, and as `approved_by_user_id` on a change order) but never compares it to any record's `id`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every collection endpoint accepts two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of records returned; it defaults to 10, may be no smaller than 1, and may be no larger than 100. `offset` skips a number of records from the start of the ordered result set; it defaults to 0 and may be no smaller than 0. A value outside these bounds is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Results are always ordered by `created_at` ascending, so that a paged sweep over a collection reads the chain's history in the order it happened. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` — the number of records matching the filters before pagination is applied — and `has_more`, which is true exactly when records matching the filters remain beyond the current page. Filter query parameters, where an endpoint offers them, are combined conjunctively and are applied before pagination.

### 2.4. Record Identity, Versioning and the Logical Clock

Every record carries an `id` that is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix; clients never supply it. Records also carry `created_at`, the ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits at which the record was first written. Most record types additionally carry `updated_at`, refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that begins at 1 on creation and increases by exactly 1 on every server-accepted mutation of that record. Safe ledger rows are the exception: a safe transaction is immutable once written, so it carries only `id` and `created_at` — it has no `updated_at` and no `version` field at all. Its own timing information lives in `occurred_at`, the logical-clock reading at which the row was written, alongside `actor_user_id` and a `memo` line.

Every instant-valued field the API returns is rendered in that same form — the ISO 8601 UTC instant with exactly three fractional digits and a trailing `Z` — and this holds however the value arose: server-stamped instants such as `created_at`, `updated_at` and `occurred_at`, server-derived instants such as `coaching_due_at`, `open_requested_at`, `unlocked_at` and an attribution's `recorded_at`, and instants the client supplied and the server echoes back, such as a session's `shift_start_at` and `shift_end_at`, a deposit's `pickup_window_start` and `pickup_window_end`, and the clock endpoint's `current_utc`. A client instant accepted in some other ISO 8601 spelling — no fractional digits, more or fewer than three of them, or a non-zero UTC offset — is normalized to UTC and comes back in the three-fractional-digit form rather than as the client wrote it, with any precision finer than a millisecond truncated away; a field whose instant is not set is returned as null. Calendar-date fields such as `business_date`, `value_date` and `target_week_start` are not instants: they are plain `YYYY-MM-DD` dates and are carried as supplied.

All time-sensitive logic in this service — the safe time-delay device, the coaching grace deadline, the rolling variance window, the pickup-window comparisons, and the `recorded_at` stamp on research attributions — reads a single server-held **logical clock** rather than wall-clock time. On startup the clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and the server never falls back to wall-clock time. Any of the six roles may advance the clock through `POST /_clock/set_current_utc`, supplying `current_utc`; the endpoint answers 200 with the new reading. The clock may only move forward: a `current_utc` earlier than the clock's present value is rejected with `ERR_CLOCK_MONOTONIC` (HTTP 422). Setting the clock to its present value is permitted.

### 2.5. Store Policy Parameters and Their Defaults

A store is more than a name: it is the policy envelope inside which its cash office operates. Registering a store requires `store_code` (chain-unique, between 1 and 16 characters, for example `S-1001`), `name`, and `region_code`. Every remaining parameter is optional on creation and takes a chain default when omitted.

`safe_time_delay_minutes` is the time-delay device setting — the number of minutes that must elapse between an open request and the open — and defaults to 5, with an accepted range from 0 through 120 inclusive. `safe_open_window_minutes` is how long after the delay elapses the request stays executable; it defaults to 10 and ranges from 1 through 240 inclusive. `dual_control_required` defaults to true and, when true, forces safe opens and deposit seals to be performed by a second, different `X-User-ID`. `pickup_buffer_minutes` defaults to 30 and ranges from 0 through 240 inclusive; it is applied to both sides of an already-scheduled armored pickup window before overlap testing. `max_daily_pickups` defaults to 2 and ranges from 1 through 6 inclusive. `single_event_variance_threshold_cents` defaults to 2500 — twenty-five dollars — and is the absolute per-session over/short at or above which the cashier is flagged for coaching; it must be zero or greater. `rolling_variance_threshold_cents` defaults to 10000 and is the rolling absolute over/short at or above which the cashier is restricted and investigated. `rolling_window_days` defaults to 30 and ranges from 1 through 365 inclusive. `theft_escalation_threshold_cents` defaults to 50000 — five hundred dollars — and is the case total at or above which a variance case is marked as suspected theft. `provisional_credit_cap_cents` defaults to 500000 and is the per-deposit ceiling on provisional credit, applied identically to the smart-safe and the carrier variants. `seasonal_profile` defaults to `BASELINE` and may otherwise be `BACK_TO_SCHOOL` or `HOLIDAY`; it is the default seasonal demand profile used by change-order forecasting.

### 2.6. The Safe Ledger and Balance Recomputation

A safe's `balance_cents` is never adjusted incrementally. It is the sum of `effective_amount_cents` over every safe ledger row whose `safe_id` names that safe, recomputed in full every time a row is written and every time a drop is verified. No status filter is applied, because the ledger is append-only and has no void rows: a voided deposit is represented by a compensating return row, never by deleting the removal row that preceded it.

Each row carries an unsigned `declared_amount_cents`, the amount stated by the actor who created it, and an optional `verified_amount_cents`, the amount the cash office re-counted during deposit preparation. The row's basis is `verified_amount_cents` when that field is populated and `declared_amount_cents` otherwise. The row's `effective_amount_cents` is that basis carried with the sign its `txn_type` implies. Nine transaction kinds exist, and their signs are fixed: `OPENING_BALANCE`, `MID_SHIFT_DROP`, `CLOSING_DROP`, `SMART_DEPOSIT`, `DEPOSIT_RETURN` and `CHANGE_ORDER_RECEIPT` add to the safe; `STARTING_BANK_ISSUE`, `DEPOSIT_REMOVAL` and `VARIANCE_ADJUSTMENT` subtract from it.

Rows are polymorphic in their linkage. `till_session_id` is populated only on `STARTING_BANK_ISSUE`, `MID_SHIFT_DROP` and `CLOSING_DROP` rows; `deposit_id` only on `SMART_DEPOSIT`, `DEPOSIT_REMOVAL` and `DEPOSIT_RETURN` rows; `change_order_id` only on `CHANGE_ORDER_RECEIPT` rows. The remaining slots stay null. `actor_user_id` records the `X-User-ID` of the request that produced the row, or the literal string `system` for rows produced by a cascade rather than by a direct request.

Alongside the balance, two further safe aggregates are maintained. `provisional_credit_balance_cents` is the sum of the amounts of provisional bank credit records belonging to this safe's deposits that have not yet reached the reconciled state — the float the bank has advanced and may still reverse. `suspect_note_cents` is the sum of the suspect-note values across this safe's smart-safe deposits that are not yet reconciled.

### 2.7. Dual Control and Segregation of Duties

Three separations of duty are enforced, and all three surface as `ERR_DUAL_CONTROL_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). While a store's `dual_control_required` is true, the `X-User-ID` opening a safe must differ from the `open_requested_by_user_id` recorded on the pending request; the `X-User-ID` sealing a deposit must differ from the deposit's `prepared_by_user_id`; and the `X-User-ID` reconciling a till session must differ from that session's `counted_by_user_id`. One person may never both request and open a safe, both prepare and seal a bag, or both count and reconcile a till. When a store sets `dual_control_required` to false, all three comparisons are skipped and a single operator may complete the pair.

### 2.8. The Time-Delay Device

Conventional drop safes in this chain are fitted with time-delay devices, and the API models the device rather than hiding it. A safe's `open_state` cycles `LOCKED` → `OPEN_REQUESTED` → `UNLOCKED` → `LOCKED` and returns to the start after each cash-office visit, so the cycle repeats without bound over the life of the safe. Arming a request stamps `open_requested_at` with the logical clock and `open_requested_by_user_id` with the requester. The open is permitted only once the clock has reached `open_requested_at` plus the store's `safe_time_delay_minutes` — the boundary instant itself is permitted — and only while the clock is strictly before `open_requested_at` plus `safe_time_delay_minutes` plus `safe_open_window_minutes`. An open before the lower bound is refused with `ERR_TIME_DELAY_NOT_ELAPSED`; an open at or after the upper bound is refused with `ERR_SAFE_OPEN_REQUEST_EXPIRED`. Both carry HTTP 409.

The expiry case is the single place in this specification where a rejected request still advances state. When the window has lapsed, the server clears `open_requested_at` and `open_requested_by_user_id`, moves `open_state` from `OPEN_REQUESTED` back to `LOCKED`, increments the safe's `version` by 1, and *then* returns `ERR_SAFE_OPEN_REQUEST_EXPIRED`. A lapsed request must not be left pending, so the next arming starts cleanly from `LOCKED`.

Note-validating smart safes are not opened at all. Cash enters through the validator and never comes out, so no arming, delay or opening applies to a safe whose `safe_type` is `SMART`; its `open_state` stays `LOCKED` throughout its life.

### 2.9. Investigation Freeze

A safe carries a second, independent dimension called `posture`, which is either `OPEN` or `FROZEN`. Posture and `open_state` never constrain each other: a safe can be `UNLOCKED` and `FROZEN` at the same time, and freezing does not relock anything. While a store has any variance case in the escalated state, every safe at that store is `FROZEN`, and while a safe is `FROZEN` no cash may enter or leave it. Five operations are refused with `ERR_SAFE_FROZEN` (HTTP 409) on a frozen safe: assigning a till session (which issues a starting bank), recording a till drop, inserting a smart-safe deposit, preparing an armored deposit, and receiving a change order into the safe. Reads are unaffected, and so is posture restoration itself. The freeze check runs before the dual-control check and before the time-delay clock is consulted, so a frozen safe cannot be probed for its unlock timing.

### 2.10. Money, Denominations and Rounding

All monetary quantities in this service are integer cents. Balances, variances, fees and credits are exact integers, and no field carries fractional cents. Rounding therefore appears in exactly three places, and in each the method is round half up: the percentage component of a card settlement fee is rounded half up to whole cents before the per-transaction component is added; a change order's seasonally adjusted forecast is rounded half up to whole cents; and nothing else is rounded. Two further non-rounding reductions are used and must not be confused with rounding: the armored-carrier fee counts thousand-dollar increments by rounding the count *up* to the next whole increment, and a change order's per-denomination unit count is rounded *up* to the next whole strap or roll. Capping a provisional credit takes the smaller of two exact integers and never rounds.

Denomination lines appear in two places — a till session's blind count and a deposit bag — and share one shape: each line is an object with `denomination_cents` and `quantity`. A line's `denomination_cents` must be one of 2000, 1000, 500, 100, 25, 10, 5 or 1, and its `quantity` must be zero or greater; any other denomination is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The value of a set of lines is the sum, over every line, of `denomination_cents` multiplied by `quantity`. An empty list is worth zero.

Cents fields are non-negative integers throughout, with exactly two deliberate exceptions: a bank credit record whose `credit_type` is `ADJUSTMENT` may carry a negative `amount_cents`, and a variance attribution's `amount_cents` may be negative so that a researcher can reverse an earlier over-attribution. Provisional and final bank credit rows must be strictly greater than zero. Every other cents field that receives a negative value is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

### 2.11. Stale-Version Rejection

Two write operations are guarded by optimistic concurrency control, because in both the operation is a legitimate repeat action that no state precondition would block, and the only defence against a client working from an out-of-date view is the version check.

Preparing an armored deposit through `POST /cash-ops/deposits` requires `expected_safe_version` as a non-optional field of the request body, carrying the `version` of the target `Safe` that the clerk last observed. A single safe opening legitimately supports several bags, so a second preparation is not blocked by any state rule — the safe is still `UNLOCKED` and the action is valid product behaviour. If the safe's current `version` has advanced past `expected_safe_version` since the client read it, the preparation is rejected with `ERR_SAFE_VERSION_STALE` (HTTP 412). That check is what stops a clerk whose view of `balance_cents` and of which drops have already been verified is out of date from removing the same cash twice.

Appending a research attribution through `POST /cash-ops/variance-cases/{id}/attributions` requires `expected_variance_case_version` as a non-optional field of the request body, carrying the `version` of the target `VarianceCase` the researcher last observed. Attributions legitimately accumulate, so a later entry observing the same status would itself succeed; if the case's current `version` no longer equals `expected_variance_case_version`, the append is rejected with `ERR_CASE_VERSION_STALE` (HTTP 412). This is what prevents a researcher whose view of `unexplained_cents` predates another entry from over-attributing the case.

No other endpoint accepts a version field, and none may be added: every write not named above proceeds without a client-supplied version.

### 2.12. Validation Precedence and Path Resolution

When a request fails more than one rule, the response must name the highest-precedence failure. The order is fixed across the whole service, and several of the orderings are deliberate security choices rather than conveniences.

First, header authentication is checked — missing headers or an unrecognised role yield `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. Second, role authorization: the acting role must appear on the endpoint's list, and additionally, when `X-User-Role` is `Cashier` the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the target till session's `cashier_id`; failure is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`. Third, request-body shape: required fields present, enum members valid, integer cents fields integral and non-negative subject to the two exceptions in §2.10, denomination lines drawn from the permitted set with non-negative quantities, `shift_end_at` strictly after `shift_start_at`, `pickup_window_end` strictly after `pickup_window_start`, `days_covered` between 1 and 14 inclusive, and `growth_pct` between -0.50 and 2.00 inclusive. Closing a till session requires `pos_cash_sales_cents` and `pos_card_sales_cents` unless `forced_close` is true, and reconciling a session whose `pos_cash_sales_cents` is null also fails at this step. All of these yield `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Fourth, path resolution: every templated `{id}` segment must resolve, validated left to right, yielding the not-found error for the record type that failed — `ERR_STORE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CASHIER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SAFE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SALES_JOURNAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TILL_SESSION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SAFE_TRANSACTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DEPOSIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BANK_CREDIT_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CARD_SETTLEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VARIANCE_CASE_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_CHANGE_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, all HTTP 404. Fifth, foreign keys carried in the request body must resolve — `store_id`, `safe_id`, `cashier_id`, `sales_journal_id`, `till_session_id`, `deposit_id`, `change_order_id` — again with the matching not-found error. Store scoping is part of resolution rather than a separate rule: when a till session is assigned, an armored deposit prepared, or a smart-safe deposit inserted, a `Cashier` or `Safe` whose `store_id` differs from the sales journal's `store_id` is treated as non-existent for that request and reported as `ERR_CASHIER_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_SAFE_NOT_FOUND`, so no caller can enumerate another store's records.

Sixth, the target aggregate must be in a state from which the requested transition is declared. This covers a till session's `status`, a deposit's `status`, a variance case's `status`, a change order's `status`, a sales journal's `status`, and a safe's `open_state` — a deposit preparation, deposit removal or manual drop all require the safe to be `UNLOCKED`. The failures are `ERR_TILL_SESSION_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_DEPOSIT_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_VARIANCE_CASE_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_CHANGE_ORDER_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_TENDERS_ALREADY_POSTED`, `ERR_SAFE_NOT_UNLOCKED`, `ERR_SAFE_OPEN_REQUEST_ACTIVE` and `ERR_SMART_SAFE_REQUIRED`, all HTTP 409.

Seventh, the safe-freeze check of §2.9 (`ERR_SAFE_FROZEN`). Eighth, the barred-cashier check on till assignment (`ERR_CASHIER_BARRED`, HTTP 403). Ninth, dual control (`ERR_DUAL_CONTROL_VIOLATION`, HTTP 403) on safe opening and deposit sealing — deliberately ahead of the time-delay check, so that a lone operator is turned away on segregation-of-duties grounds even when the delay has not yet elapsed and the response never reveals whether the delay was satisfied. Tenth, the coaching and restriction gating on till assignment (`ERR_CASHIER_RESTRICTED` or `ERR_COACHING_NOT_ACKNOWLEDGED`, HTTP 409). Eleventh, the time-delay device bounds (`ERR_TIME_DELAY_NOT_ELAPSED` or `ERR_SAFE_OPEN_REQUEST_EXPIRED`, HTTP 409) on safe opening.

Twelfth, the temporal-overlap and uniqueness family, all HTTP 409, applying to till session assignment, pickup scheduling, sales journal creation, card settlement posting, bank reconciliation and the clock endpoint: `ERR_CASHIER_SHIFT_OVERLAP`, `ERR_PICKUP_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, `ERR_REGISTER_OCCUPIED`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_BUSINESS_DATE`, `ERR_SETTLEMENT_ALREADY_POSTED`, `ERR_BANK_CREDIT_ALREADY_MATCHED` and `ERR_CLOCK_MONOTONIC` — the last of which carries HTTP 422. Thirteenth, cross-record referential coherence, HTTP 422, applying to bank reconciliation, attributions and escalation: `ERR_CREDIT_TYPE_INVALID`, `ERR_ATTRIBUTION_EXCEEDS_VARIANCE` and `ERR_ESCALATION_THRESHOLD_NOT_MET`. Fourteenth, fiscal capacity, HTTP 422, applying to till session assignment, deposit preparation and pickup scheduling: `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_SAFE_BALANCE` and `ERR_PICKUP_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. This step sits deliberately *after* the state check, so preparing a deposit that exceeds the safe balance while the safe is still `LOCKED` returns `ERR_SAFE_NOT_UNLOCKED`, not `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_SAFE_BALANCE`: the physical-control failure outranks the arithmetic one.

Last, the two optimistic-concurrency checks of §2.11 (`ERR_SAFE_VERSION_STALE` and `ERR_CASE_VERSION_STALE`, HTTP 412) run after every other rule has been satisfied.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Opening the Business Day and the Daily Tie-Out

Each store's cash office begins a business day by opening a **sales journal**, supplying the `store_id` of the store and a `business_date` as an ISO calendar date in `YYYY-MM-DD` form. Exactly one journal may exist per store and business date; a second attempt at the same pair is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_BUSINESS_DATE`. A freshly opened journal has `status` `OPEN`, both of its posted tender figures null, and all of its derived counters at zero. Journals may be listed with `store_id`, `business_date` and `status` filters.

Once the point-of-sale system has closed the day, the clerk posts the store-level tender totals onto the journal: `journal_cash_sales_cents` and `journal_card_sales_cents`, both required. Posting moves the journal from `OPEN` to `TENDERS_POSTED`, recomputes the register tie-out described below, and re-evaluates the journal's compound verdict. Tenders are posted exactly once — a second attempt is refused with `ERR_TENDERS_ALREADY_POSTED`, which is also the answer whenever the journal's `status` is anything other than `OPEN`.

The journal is a **compound** record: its `status` is not driven by an operator action but derived from the state of its children. Seven derived figures feed the derivation. `session_count` is the number of till sessions attached to the journal. `reconciled_session_count` is how many of those sessions have reached `RECONCILED`. `settlement_count` is the number of card settlement rows attached to the journal. `register_cash_tieout_cents` is the sum of `pos_cash_sales_cents` across the journal's `RECONCILED` sessions minus `journal_cash_sales_cents`, and is therefore zero exactly when the registers tie out to the posted cash figure. `settlement_variance_total_cents` is the sum of the absolute values of the journal's card settlement variances. `open_case_count` is the number of variance cases on the journal whose `status` is `OPEN`, `RESEARCHING` or `ESCALATED`. `net_unexplained_cents` is the sum of `unexplained_cents` over that same set of cases.

Two clauses then decide the verdict. The journal is **structurally complete** when its `status` is `TENDERS_POSTED` or `OUT_OF_BALANCE`, its `session_count` is at least 1, its `reconciled_session_count` equals its `session_count`, its `settlement_count` is at least 1, and at least one deposit attached to the journal has reached `SEALED`, `SCHEDULED`, `IN_TRANSIT` or `RECONCILED`. The journal is **balanced** when `register_cash_tieout_cents` is zero, `settlement_variance_total_cents` is zero, and `open_case_count` is zero. When the journal is structurally complete and balanced, its `status` becomes `BALANCED`, which is terminal. When it is structurally complete but not balanced, its `status` becomes `OUT_OF_BALANCE`. An out-of-balance day is not lost: once the last variance case reaches an explained or closed state, or the last settlement variance is corrected, the same derivation moves the journal from `OUT_OF_BALANCE` to `BALANCED`. This derivation is re-evaluated after every till reconciliation, every card settlement posting, every deposit seal and bank reconciliation, and every variance case status change.

### 3.2. Enrolling Cashiers and Issuing a Till

A `StoreManager` enrolls a cashier by supplying the `store_id` of the employing store, an `employee_code` that must be unique within that store, and a `display_name`; the enrolment may optionally set `till_eligibility` directly. A new cashier record starts with `till_eligibility` `ELIGIBLE` when the enrolment leaves that field out, `coaching_state` `NONE`, `rolling_absolute_variance_cents` zero, `rolling_net_variance_cents` zero, `reconciled_session_count` zero and `open_case_count` zero, and its `coaching_due_at` is null while `coaching_state` remains `NONE`. A cashier's `open_case_count` is the number of variance cases naming that cashier whose `status` is `OPEN`, `RESEARCHING` or `ESCALATED`. Cashiers may be listed with `store_id`, `till_eligibility` and `coaching_state` filters.

Two dimensions govern a cashier, and they advance on different triggers and never constrain each other. `till_eligibility` is the assignment dimension and takes the values `ELIGIBLE`, `RESTRICTED` and `BARRED`: `ELIGIBLE` becomes `RESTRICTED` on a rolling-threshold breach, and either becomes `BARRED` when a variance case naming that cashier is escalated to loss prevention. `coaching_state` is the coaching dimension and takes the values `NONE`, `COACHING_DUE` and `COACHING_ACKNOWLEDGED`: a single-event variance breach moves it to `COACHING_DUE`, and the cashier's own acknowledgement moves it to `COACHING_ACKNOWLEDGED`. A later breach moves an already-acknowledged cashier back to `COACHING_DUE` and resets the deadline. Crucially, movements on one dimension leave the other untouched — restricting a cashier does not create a coaching obligation, and acknowledging coaching does not lift a restriction.

Acknowledging coaching is a small, explicit operation: the cashier (acting on their own record) or a `StoreManager` on the cashier's behalf records that the coaching conversation took place, optionally attaching an `acknowledgement_note`. The acknowledgement moves `coaching_state` from `COACHING_DUE` to `COACHING_ACKNOWLEDGED` and increments the cashier's `version`; it leaves `till_eligibility` exactly as it was. When `coaching_state` is not `COACHING_DUE`, there is nothing to acknowledge and the request is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A `RESTRICTED` cashier becomes assignable again *only* through this acknowledgement — there is no override field on the assignment request that can substitute for it.

Assigning a till issues a starting bank out of the safe in the same breath, and the request therefore carries `sales_journal_id`, `cashier_id`, `safe_id`, `register_number`, `shift_start_at`, `shift_end_at` and `starting_bank_cents`. The `register_number` is the physical register the till is mounted on for the shift and must be between 1 and 99 inclusive. `starting_bank_cents` must be zero or greater. The `safe_id` names both the safe the starting bank comes out of and the safe every drop of this session will land in; it is fixed at assignment and never changed thereafter. The journal must be `OPEN` or `TENDERS_POSTED`, and both the cashier and the safe must belong to the journal's store (§2.12 explains why a cross-store reference reads back as a not-found error). The safe's `posture` must be `OPEN`, and its `balance_cents` must be at least `starting_bank_cents` — a starting bank that would drive the safe negative is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_SAFE_BALANCE`.

Eligibility gating on assignment reads both cashier dimensions together. A cashier whose `till_eligibility` is `BARRED` may never be assigned a till: the answer is `ERR_CASHIER_BARRED`. A cashier whose `till_eligibility` is `RESTRICTED` may be assigned only while `coaching_state` is `COACHING_ACKNOWLEDGED`; otherwise the answer is `ERR_CASHIER_RESTRICTED`. A cashier who is still `ELIGIBLE` but whose `coaching_state` is `COACHING_DUE` may be assigned only while the logical clock is at or before `coaching_due_at` — the boundary instant itself is permitted — and once that deadline has passed the assignment is refused with `ERR_COACHING_NOT_ACKNOWLEDGED`. `coaching_due_at` is the instant of the single-event breach plus a grace period of three days, that is 72 hours.

Two occupancy rules protect the schedule. A cashier's shift window is treated as half-open, running from `shift_start_at` inclusive to `shift_end_at` exclusive, and the new window must not overlap the window of any other non-terminal session of the same cashier; because the interval is half-open, back-to-back shifts that touch at a single instant are allowed. An overlap is refused with `ERR_CASHIER_SHIFT_OVERLAP`. Separately, no two sessions on the same journal may hold the same `register_number` with overlapping windows — an `ACTIVE` or `PENDING_COUNT` session already holding that register yields `ERR_REGISTER_OCCUPIED`.

A successful assignment produces a session with `status` `ACTIVE`, an empty `counted_lines` list, and `counted_cash_cents`, `expected_cash_cents`, `variance_cents` and `variance_basis` all null, with `recount_count` at zero and `forced_close` false. It writes one safe ledger row of kind `STARTING_BANK_ISSUE` against the supplied `safe_id`, linked to the new session, with `declared_amount_cents` equal to `starting_bank_cents`; the safe's `balance_cents` is recomputed (falling by the starting bank) and the safe's `version` increments by 1. The journal's `session_count` increments by 1. The cashier's `coaching_state` and `till_eligibility` are deliberately left untouched: taking a till is not an acknowledgement of coaching.

### 3.3. Commissioning Safes and Working the Time-Delay Cycle

A `TreasuryAnalyst` commissions a safe with the `store_id` of the store it physically sits in, a `safe_code` unique within that store (for example `SAFE-A`), a `safe_type` of either `MANUAL` for a conventional drop safe or `SMART` for a note-validating smart safe, and an `opening_balance_cents`. Commissioning writes the safe's first ledger row, of kind `OPENING_BALANCE` with `declared_amount_cents` equal to `opening_balance_cents`, and recomputes `balance_cents` from it. The new safe starts with `open_state` `LOCKED` and `posture` `OPEN`, and with `open_requested_at`, `open_requested_by_user_id` and `unlocked_at` all null. Safes may be listed with `store_id` and `safe_type` filters, and the safe ledger may be listed with `safe_id`, `till_session_id`, `deposit_id`, `change_order_id` and `txn_type` filters.

Arming the time-delay device on a conventional safe is the first step of any cash-office visit. A `CashOfficeClerk` or `StoreManager` files an open request, optionally recording a `reason`; the safe moves from `LOCKED` to `OPEN_REQUESTED`, `open_requested_at` takes the current logical clock, `open_requested_by_user_id` takes the requester's `X-User-ID`, and the safe's `version` increments by 1. Arming a safe that is already `OPEN_REQUESTED` or already `UNLOCKED` is refused with `ERR_SAFE_OPEN_REQUEST_ACTIVE`, and arming a frozen safe is refused with `ERR_SAFE_FROZEN`.

The open itself takes no body. It requires `open_state` to be `OPEN_REQUESTED` — any other value answers `ERR_SAFE_NOT_UNLOCKED` — and is then subject, in order, to the dual-control comparison of §2.7 and the timing bounds of §2.8. On success the safe moves from `OPEN_REQUESTED` to `UNLOCKED`, `unlocked_at` takes the current clock, `open_requested_at` and `open_requested_by_user_id` remain populated for audit, and `version` increments by 1. The cycle closes when a prepared bag is sealed, which returns the safe from `UNLOCKED` to `LOCKED` and clears all three timing fields; preparing a bag does *not* relock the safe, which is precisely why several bags may be prepared inside one opening.

Lifting an investigation freeze is a loss-prevention action rather than a cash-office one. Once no variance case at the store remains escalated, a `LossPreventionInvestigator` restores posture — optionally recording an `investigation_note` — and every safe at that store moves from `FROZEN` back to `OPEN`, each with its `version` incremented by 1. Restoration touches `posture` only; `open_state` is untouched.

### 3.4. Drops, Register Close, and the Blind Count

During the shift, cash accumulates in the till and is periodically moved into the safe. Recording a drop is exclusive to the `Cashier`, who supplies the `txn_type` — either `MID_SHIFT_DROP` or `CLOSING_DROP` — the `declared_amount_cents` being dropped, and optionally a `memo`; when no memo is supplied the server authors a one-line provenance note of its own. A drop requires the session to be `ACTIVE`, requires the acting `X-User-ID` to be the session's own cashier, requires the safe's `posture` to be `OPEN`, and — for a conventional safe — requires the safe's `open_state` to be `UNLOCKED`, failing which the answer is `ERR_SAFE_NOT_UNLOCKED`. Cash bound for a smart safe does not go through this route at all; it is inserted through the validator, as described in §3.6.

A drop writes one safe ledger row of the requested kind with `verified_amount_cents` left null and `till_session_id` set to the session, adds the amount into the session's `dropped_declared_cents`, recomputes the safe's balance from declared amounts, and increments the session's `version` by 1. The session's `status` does not change: both mid-shift and closing drops leave it `ACTIVE`, and a session may take any number of drops. The response body is the new ledger row itself.

Closing the register moves the session from `ACTIVE` to `PENDING_COUNT` and freezes the point-of-sale figures: `pos_cash_sales_cents`, `pos_card_sales_cents` and `pos_paid_out_cents`, the last being refunds and vendor payouts taken out of the till. The first two are required unless `forced_close` is true. The close writes **no** safe ledger row — cash leaves the till only through a drop — and the response deliberately omits `expected_cash_cents`, returning only the session's `id`, `status`, `register_number`, `shift_end_at`, `dropped_declared_cents` and `version`, so that the count which follows is genuinely blind. Either the session's own `Cashier` or a `StoreManager` may close a session, but `forced_close` is reserved to `StoreManager`: a forced close leaves all three point-of-sale figures null, ignores any figures supplied, and sets `forced_close` to true. Because a forced-closed session has no cash-sales figure, it can never be reconciled — every later reconciliation attempt on a session whose `pos_cash_sales_cents` is null answers `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

The cash office then counts the till. A `CashOfficeClerk` submits `counted_lines`, the denomination breakdown of the cash actually found in the drawer, and the session moves from `PENDING_COUNT` to `COUNTED`. Each blind count replaces `counted_lines` wholesale rather than appending to it, and `counted_cash_cents` is recomputed as the value of those lines, an empty list yielding zero. The count records `counted_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID` and increments the session's `version`. The count is blind in a strict sense: it does **not** compute `expected_cash_cents` or `variance_cents`, the response carries only the session's `id`, `status`, `counted_cash_cents`, `recount_count` and `version`, and any read of the session while it is `PENDING_COUNT` or `COUNTED` returns `expected_cash_cents` and `variance_cents` as null so the expected figure cannot be back-solved.

When a count looks wrong, the clerk orders a recount with a `recount_reason`, sending the session from `COUNTED` back to `PENDING_COUNT`. The recount increments `recount_count` by 1, clears `counted_lines` to an empty list, sets `counted_cash_cents` back to null, clears `counted_by_user_id`, and increments `version`. It deliberately leaves `dropped_declared_cents`, `dropped_verified_cents`, the cashier's rolling variance figures and the safe's `balance_cents` exactly as they were — a discarded count moved no money. The `COUNTED` → `PENDING_COUNT` → `COUNTED` cycle may repeat without bound, and the next blind count fully supersedes the discarded one; only the most recent count ever feeds the reconciliation. Sessions may be listed with `sales_journal_id`, `cashier_id`, `safe_id` and `status` filters.

### 3.5. Reconciling the Till: Over/Short, Coaching and the Rolling Window

Reconciliation is where the blind count meets the expected figure, and it is the single most consequential operation in the service. A session may be reconciled only from `COUNTED`; any other `status` answers `ERR_TILL_SESSION_INVALID_STATE`. Under dual control the reconciling `X-User-ID` must differ from the session's `counted_by_user_id`, otherwise the answer is `ERR_DUAL_CONTROL_VIOLATION`. A forced-closed session cannot be reconciled at all, because `pos_cash_sales_cents` is null on such a session and the request fails validation with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

The reconciliation first chooses which drop total to trust, and this is the only branch in the arithmetic. The session tracks its drops twice: `dropped_declared_cents` is the sum of `declared_amount_cents` over the session's mid-shift and closing drop ledger rows, while `dropped_verified_cents` is the sum of `verified_amount_cents` over that same set of rows and stays null for as long as any one of those rows is still unverified. If `dropped_verified_cents` is non-null at the moment reconciliation runs, `variance_basis` is recorded as `VERIFIED_DROPS` and the drop total used is `dropped_verified_cents`; otherwise `variance_basis` is recorded as `DECLARED_DROPS` and the drop total used is `dropped_declared_cents`. The declared branch trusts the cashier's own statement of what went into the bag; the verified branch trusts the cash office's re-count of that bag during deposit preparation. The two branches are not cosmetic: relative to the declared branch, the verified branch raises `expected_cash_cents` by the amount by which the declared drops exceeded the verified drops, and moves the reported variance by that same amount — which can push the absolute variance across the store's single-event threshold on an otherwise identical reconciliation. Put the other way, a drop bag that was over-declared reports a *smaller* short under the declared branch than under the verified branch.

The expected till contents are then the session's `starting_bank_cents`, plus its `pos_cash_sales_cents`, minus its `pos_paid_out_cents`, minus the chosen drop total; the result is stored as `expected_cash_cents`. The over/short is `counted_cash_cents` — taken from the most recent blind count only, since a recount discards the previous lines entirely — minus `expected_cash_cents`, and is stored as `variance_cents`. A positive `variance_cents` means the drawer was **over**; a negative one means it was **short**. Everything here is exact integer cents and nothing is rounded. The session's `status` becomes `RECONCILED`, which is terminal, and `expected_cash_cents` and `variance_cents` stop being masked on subsequent reads.

Writing `variance_cents` sets off a four-link cascade, and each link exists only because the link before it changed something. **Link two** fires because `variance_cents` was written: the cashier's rolling window is recomputed from scratch. The window runs from the current logical clock minus the store's `rolling_window_days` days up to the current clock, and both boundaries are inclusive — a session whose `updated_at` falls exactly on the earlier boundary instant is still inside the window. The window set is the cashier's sessions whose `status` is `RECONCILED` and whose `updated_at` lies inside it; sessions still `ACTIVE`, `PENDING_COUNT` or `COUNTED` are excluded even when a discarded count exists for them. From that set, `reconciled_session_count` becomes the number of sessions, `rolling_absolute_variance_cents` becomes the sum of the absolute values of their variances, and `rolling_net_variance_cents` becomes the signed sum of the same variances. In the same link, if the absolute value of the session's own `variance_cents` is at or above the store's `single_event_variance_threshold_cents` — twenty-five dollars by default — the cashier's `coaching_state` is set to `COACHING_DUE`, `coaching_due_at` is set to the current clock plus 72 hours, and a variance case is opened with `case_type` `SESSION_VARIANCE` naming this cashier and this sales journal.

**Link three** fires because link two recomputed `rolling_absolute_variance_cents`: if that recomputed figure is at or above the store's `rolling_variance_threshold_cents` — one hundred dollars by default — the cashier's `till_eligibility` is set to `RESTRICTED` and a second variance case is opened with `case_type` `ROLLING_PATTERN`, again naming this cashier and this sales journal. The restriction never touches `coaching_state`, and a restricted cashier becomes assignable again only through the coaching acknowledgement of §3.2. Note that a single reconciliation can breach both thresholds and therefore open two cases. **Link four** fires because links two and three may have created variance cases: the journal's `open_case_count` and `net_unexplained_cents` are recomputed and the journal's compound derivation of §3.1 is re-evaluated. Independently of the cascade, the journal's `reconciled_session_count` increments and its `register_cash_tieout_cents` is recomputed. A successful reconciliation increments the `version` of the till session, of the cashier and of the sales journal by 1 each.

### 3.6. From Cash Office to Carrier: Preparing, Sealing and Banking the Cash

Preparing an armored deposit is the cash office's counter-count of the day's drops, and it can only happen with the safe physically open. A `CashOfficeClerk` supplies the `safe_id` of the open safe, the `sales_journal_id` of the business day the deposit settles against, the `business_date` the deposit is banked under, the bag's `counted_lines`, and `expected_safe_version` as described in §2.11; the clerk may also supply `verify_till_session_ids` and `suspect_note_cents`. The safe's `open_state` must be `UNLOCKED` (`ERR_SAFE_NOT_UNLOCKED`) and its `posture` must be `OPEN` (`ERR_SAFE_FROZEN`). Only conventional safes hold bags to be prepared; because a smart safe is never unlocked, an attempt to prepare a deposit out of one fails the unlock check. The journal must belong to the safe's store, and the safe's `balance_cents` must be at least the value of the submitted `counted_lines`, failing which the answer is `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_SAFE_BALANCE`.

A successful preparation creates a deposit with `status` `PREPARING`, `deposit_type` `MANUAL_ARMORED`, `credit_basis` `CARRIER_PROVISIONAL`, `prepared_by_user_id` set to the acting `X-User-ID`, and `declared_total_cents` set to the value of `counted_lines`. Those lines are immutable once the deposit exists. `suspect_note_cents` is always recorded as 0 on a manual armored deposit — only a note validator can reject notes as suspect. `provisional_credit_cents` starts at 0, `credited_total_cents` at 0, `credit_variance_cents` null, and `pickup_window_start`, `pickup_window_end`, `seal_number`, `sealed_by_user_id` and `carrier_reference` all null. The preparation writes one safe ledger row of kind `DEPOSIT_REMOVAL` linked to the deposit with `declared_amount_cents` equal to `declared_total_cents`, recomputes the safe's `balance_cents`, and increments the safe's `version` by 1. It deliberately does **not** relock the safe.

Preparation is also the moment at which drops become verified. For every till session named in `verify_till_session_ids`, the cash office's re-count is written onto that session's drop ledger rows: mid-shift drop rows are verified at the amounts they were declared at, and the session's closing drop row takes whatever `verified_amount_cents` makes the session's verified drop rows total that session's share of the re-count. A session's share of the re-count is the deposit's `declared_total_cents` apportioned across the listed sessions in proportion to each session's `dropped_declared_cents`, rounded half up to whole cents; when exactly one session is listed — the ordinary case — its share is simply the deposit's `declared_total_cents`. Once every drop row of a session carries a `verified_amount_cents`, that session's `dropped_verified_cents` becomes non-null, which is precisely what flips a later reconciliation of that session onto the verified branch of §3.5. Because the safe ledger takes the verified amount as its basis whenever one exists, the safe's `balance_cents` is recomputed to reflect both the removal and any newly verified drop amounts.

The armored-carrier fee is priced at preparation time and its evaluation order is pinned. The fee is twelve dollars — `1200` cents — plus thirty-five cents for every thousand-dollar increment of the bag, where the number of increments is `declared_total_cents` divided by 100000 and rounded **up** to the next whole increment. That result is stored as `carrier_fee_cents`, and `expected_credit_cents` is then `declared_total_cents` minus `carrier_fee_cents`. The increment count is keyed on `declared_total_cents` — the amount the store hands over — and never on `expected_credit_cents`, which already has the fee subtracted and would make the calculation self-referential. The difference is real whenever the fee straddles a thousand-dollar boundary: a bag of `declared_total_cents` 200500 is priced at a `carrier_fee_cents` of 1305 and an `expected_credit_cents` of 199195, whereas keying the increment count on the net figure would have given 1270 and 199230.

Sealing the bag is the dual-control counterpart of preparing it. A `CashOfficeClerk` or `StoreManager` supplies a tamper-evident `seal_number`; the deposit must be `PREPARING` (any other `status` answers `ERR_DEPOSIT_INVALID_STATE`) and, while the store requires dual control, the sealing `X-User-ID` must differ from `prepared_by_user_id`. Sealing records `seal_number` and `sealed_by_user_id`, moves the deposit from `PREPARING` to `SEALED`, and ends the cash-office opening: the parent safe's `open_state` returns from `UNLOCKED` to `LOCKED` with `open_requested_at`, `open_requested_by_user_id` and `unlocked_at` all cleared, and the safe's `version` increments by 1 alongside the deposit's. Because a sealed deposit satisfies the journal's deposit-present condition, sealing also re-evaluates the journal's compound derivation.

A deposit that has not yet left the store can be undone. A `StoreManager` voids it with a `void_reason`; the deposit must be `PREPARING` or `SEALED` and must be of type `MANUAL_ARMORED`, and any other combination answers `ERR_DEPOSIT_INVALID_STATE`. Voiding writes a compensating safe ledger row of kind `DEPOSIT_RETURN` linked to the deposit with `declared_amount_cents` equal to `declared_total_cents`, so that the recomputed `balance_cents` returns to its pre-preparation value — the removal row is never deleted, because the ledger is append-only. The deposit's `status` becomes `VOIDED`, which is terminal, while `declared_total_cents`, `carrier_fee_cents` and `expected_credit_cents` are left untouched for audit, and the drop rows' `verified_amount_cents` values are also left untouched, because verification is a counting fact rather than a cash movement. Where a voided deposit had already earned smart-safe provisional credit, that `provisional_credit_cents` is subtracted from the safe's `provisional_credit_balance_cents` and its `suspect_note_cents` from the safe's `suspect_note_cents`. Both the deposit's and the safe's `version` increment by 1.

Booking the carrier is the clerk's last step. Scheduling supplies `pickup_window_start`, `pickup_window_end` and the carrier's `carrier_reference`, requires the deposit to be `SEALED`, and requires `pickup_window_end` to be strictly after `pickup_window_start`. Two aggregate rules then apply. First, the requested window, expanded by the store's `pickup_buffer_minutes` on **both** sides, must not intersect the identically expanded window of any other deposit of the same store that is `SCHEDULED` or `IN_TRANSIT`; these intervals are treated as closed, so two windows that merely touch after expansion **do** conflict, and the answer is `ERR_PICKUP_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Second, the number of the store's deposits sharing this deposit's `business_date` and sitting in `SCHEDULED` or `IN_TRANSIT` must be strictly below the store's `max_daily_pickups`; at or above it the answer is `ERR_PICKUP_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. A successful booking stores the two window fields and moves the deposit from `SEALED` to `SCHEDULED`.

When the crew arrives, an `ArmoredCarrier` records the handover with `picked_up_at` and `carrier_signature`, moving the deposit from `SCHEDULED` to `IN_TRANSIT`. Signing for the bag starts the provisional-credit clock: the carrier variant of the provisional-credit rule sets `provisional_credit_cents` to the smaller of `declared_total_cents` and the store's `provisional_credit_cap_cents`. The carrier signs for the sealed bag exactly as declared — coin and one-dollar notes included — and the store's own suspect-note tally is irrelevant to the carrier. The pickup writes a bank credit record with `credit_type` `PROVISIONAL`, `amount_cents` equal to `provisional_credit_cents`, and both the deposit and store references populated, and adds that amount into the safe's `provisional_credit_balance_cents`.

Smart safes short-circuit this entire chain. A `Cashier` or `CashOfficeClerk` inserts a bag through the note validator, supplying the `sales_journal_id`, the `business_date`, the bag's `counted_lines`, and optionally `suspect_note_cents` and the `till_session_id` the cash came from. The target safe's `safe_type` must be `SMART`, failing which the answer is `ERR_SMART_SAFE_REQUIRED`; its `posture` must be `OPEN`; the journal must belong to the safe's store; and `suspect_note_cents` may not exceed the value of `counted_lines`, otherwise the insertion is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. No safe opening is required or possible, because cash goes in through the validator and never comes out. The insertion creates a deposit that is already `SEALED`, with `deposit_type` `SMART_SAFE`, `credit_basis` `SMART_PROVISIONAL`, and a `seal_number` the server composes as the literal prefix `SMART-` followed by the safe's `safe_code`. It writes a safe ledger row of kind `SMART_DEPOSIT` linked to the deposit with `declared_amount_cents` equal to `declared_total_cents`, recomputes the safe's `balance_cents`, prices `carrier_fee_cents` and `expected_credit_cents` by the same pinned fee rule as an armored bag, and increments the safe's `version` by 1.

Credit is granted at insertion, and the smart variant of the provisional-credit rule is materially stingier than the carrier variant. Only denominations the validator can read earn credit: the validated value is the sum, over every line whose `denomination_cents` is at least 500, of `denomination_cents` multiplied by `quantity`. From that the deposit's `suspect_note_cents` is subtracted — the notes the validator rejected — and the result is capped, so `provisional_credit_cents` is the smaller of that net validated figure and the store's `provisional_credit_cap_cents`. The two variants can diverge sharply on identical physical cash: a bag of one hundred twenty-dollar notes, four hundred one-dollar notes and eighty quarters, with forty dollars of notes rejected as suspect, earns 196000 cents on insertion into a smart safe but 242000 cents when the same bag is signed for by a carrier — a 46000-cent divergence. The insertion writes a `PROVISIONAL` bank credit record for the credited amount and recomputes both the safe's `provisional_credit_balance_cents` and its `suspect_note_cents`. Deposits may be listed with `safe_id`, `sales_journal_id`, `status` and `business_date` filters.

### 3.7. Tying Out to the Bank and to the Card Networks

The bank's side of the story arrives as **bank credit records**, posted by a `TreasuryAnalyst` with the `store_id` whose account was credited, a `credit_type`, a signed `amount_cents`, a `value_date` in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, the statement line's `bank_reference`, and optionally the `deposit_id` the analyst believes it belongs to. `PROVISIONAL` rows are system-written at the credit events of §3.6; `FINAL` and `ADJUSTMENT` rows are posted from the bank statement. `PROVISIONAL` and `FINAL` amounts must be strictly greater than zero, while an `ADJUSTMENT` may be negative. A record's `match_state` is `UNMATCHED` at creation **even when `deposit_id` is supplied at creation** — supplying the link is a hint, not a match, and the record only becomes `MATCHED` when a bank reconciliation consumes it. Records may be listed with `store_id`, `deposit_id`, `credit_type` and `match_state` filters.

Bank reconciliation is the `TreasuryAnalyst`'s act of consuming those records against a deposit, supplying `bank_credit_record_ids`. The deposit's `status` must be `IN_TRANSIT`, `SEALED` or `DISCREPANT` — `SEALED` is reachable only for smart-safe deposits, which are never picked up — and at least one record with `credit_type` `FINAL` or `ADJUSTMENT` must exist for the deposit. A record whose `credit_type` is `PROVISIONAL`, or one belonging to another store, is refused with `ERR_CREDIT_TYPE_INVALID`; a record whose `match_state` is already `MATCHED` is refused with `ERR_BANK_CREDIT_ALREADY_MATCHED`; an unknown identifier is refused with `ERR_BANK_CREDIT_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`. Each supplied record then has its `match_state` set to `MATCHED` and its `deposit_id` set to this deposit.

The arithmetic continues the pinned sequence begun at preparation. `credited_total_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` over the deposit's records whose `credit_type` is `FINAL` or `ADJUSTMENT`; `PROVISIONAL` rows are deliberately excluded, because provisional credit is a float the bank later reverses and counting it would double-count the deposit. Negative `ADJUSTMENT` rows are included with their sign. `credit_variance_cents` is then `credited_total_cents` minus `expected_credit_cents`.

The outcome forks, and the two branches differ in more than their end state. When `credit_variance_cents` computes to zero, the deposit moves from `IN_TRANSIT` to `RECONCILED`: `credited_total_cents` and a zero `credit_variance_cents` are stored, the deposit leaves the provisional pool so the safe's `provisional_credit_balance_cents` is recomputed, the journal's compound derivation is re-evaluated, and **no** variance case is opened. When `credit_variance_cents` computes to a non-zero value, the deposit moves from `IN_TRANSIT` to `DISCREPANT` instead: `credited_total_cents` and the non-zero `credit_variance_cents` are stored, a variance case is opened with `case_type` `DEPOSIT_CREDIT` naming this deposit and this sales journal with a null cashier reference and a `total_variance_cents` equal to the absolute value of `credit_variance_cents`, the journal's `open_case_count` and `net_unexplained_cents` are recomputed and its compound derivation re-evaluated, and the provisional credit stays outstanding.

A discrepant deposit is not stuck. Once further `ADJUSTMENT` rows have been posted and a fresh reconciliation recomputes `credit_variance_cents` to zero, the deposit moves from `DISCREPANT` to `RECONCILED`, releasing the provisional credit and re-evaluating the journal. That recovery has one extra guard: the deposit's variance case must already have reached `EXPLAINED`, `CLOSED_SUBSTANTIATED` or `CLOSED_UNSUBSTANTIATED`, and while the case is still open the recovery is refused with `ERR_VARIANCE_CASE_INVALID_STATE`. Each reconciliation increments the `version` of the deposit, of the safe and of the sales journal by 1.

Card tenders are tied out separately, one settlement advice per network per day. A `TreasuryAnalyst` posts the advice with the `sales_journal_id`, the `card_network` — either `VISA_MC` or `AMEX` — the `gross_card_sales_cents` the processor reports for the batch, the `txn_count` in the batch, the `monthly_volume_cents` of month-to-date processed volume, the `funded_amount_cents` the processor actually paid into the bank account, and optionally the `chargeback_cents` withheld from the batch, which defaults to 0. Only one settlement may exist per sales journal and network pair; a second answers `ERR_SETTLEMENT_ALREADY_POSTED`.

Three inputs interact to price the batch. The month-to-date volume selects a discount tier: the rate is 0.0250 while `monthly_volume_cents` is below 5000000, 0.0220 from 5000000 up to but not including 20000000, and 0.0195 at or above 20000000. Tier boundaries are inclusive at the lower edge, so a `monthly_volume_cents` of exactly 5000000 selects 0.0220 and one of exactly 20000000 selects 0.0195. The network then contributes an adder of 0.0035 for `AMEX` and nothing at all for `VISA_MC`. `expected_fee_cents` is the product of `gross_card_sales_cents` and the sum of the tier rate and the network adder, rounded half up to whole cents, **plus** twelve cents for each transaction in `txn_count`. The rounding happens on the percentage component alone and before the per-transaction component is added; rounding the combined figure instead changes the cents on many batches. `expected_funded_cents` is `gross_card_sales_cents` minus `expected_fee_cents` minus `chargeback_cents`, and `settlement_variance_cents` is `funded_amount_cents` minus `expected_funded_cents`. The `settlement_status` is `MATCHED` when `settlement_variance_cents` is exactly zero and `VARIANCE` otherwise. The tier is always selected from the `monthly_volume_cents` supplied on the row and never recomputed from stored settlements. Posting a settlement increments the journal's `settlement_count`, folds the absolute value of the settlement's variance into the journal's `settlement_variance_total_cents`, increments the journal's `version` by 1, and re-derives the journal's balanced clause. Settlements may be listed with `sales_journal_id`, `card_network` and `settlement_status` filters.

### 3.8. Variance Research, Escalation and Loss-Prevention Closure

Variance cases are never created by an operator. They are opened by the server at three moments: the two threshold breaches inside the till reconciliation cascade of §3.5, and the discrepant fork of the bank reconciliation of §3.7. Whichever moment opens the case, its magnitude is fixed once and never recomputed. For a `SESSION_VARIANCE` case the `total_variance_cents` is the absolute value of the triggering session's `variance_cents`; for a `ROLLING_PATTERN` case it is the cashier's freshly recomputed `rolling_absolute_variance_cents`; for a `DEPOSIT_CREDIT` case it is the absolute value of the deposit's `credit_variance_cents`. The figure is an unsigned magnitude frozen at creation, so later attributions and later sessions cannot move the denominator of the research. In the same instant, `suspected_theft` is set to true when `total_variance_cents` is at or above the store's `theft_escalation_threshold_cents` and to false otherwise — again evaluated once, at creation.

A new case has `status` `OPEN`, an empty `attributions` list, `attributed_register_cents`, `attributed_safe_cents` and `attributed_bank_cents` all zero, `unexplained_cents` equal to `total_variance_cents`, and `escalated_by_user_id`, `disposition` and `closing_note` all null. Its `sales_journal_id` always names the business day it is researched under; its `cashier_id` names the responsible cashier or is null for a case not attributable to one cashier; its `deposit_id` is null unless the case originated from a bank-credit discrepancy. Opening a case increments the sales journal's `open_case_count`, adds `total_variance_cents` into the journal's `net_unexplained_cents`, and increments the cashier's `open_case_count` whenever a cashier is named. Cases may be listed with `sales_journal_id`, `cashier_id`, `deposit_id`, `case_type` and `status` filters.

Research proceeds one **attribution** at a time. A `CashOfficeClerk` or `StoreManager` records where a slice of the variance was found by supplying a `bucket` — one of `REGISTER`, `SAFE` or `BANK` — an `amount_cents`, a free-text `note`, and the `expected_variance_case_version` of §2.11. The case must be `OPEN` or `RESEARCHING`; a case in any other status answers `ERR_VARIANCE_CASE_INVALID_STATE`. The entry may not push the research outside the case: the sum of `attributed_register_cents`, `attributed_safe_cents`, `attributed_bank_cents` and the new `amount_cents` must be at least zero and at most `total_variance_cents`, and an entry that would violate either bound is refused with `ERR_ATTRIBUTION_EXCEEDS_VARIANCE`. An `amount_cents` may itself be negative, which is how a researcher reverses an earlier over-attribution.

An accepted entry appends an object carrying its `bucket`, `amount_cents`, `note` and a `recorded_at` stamp taken from the logical clock onto the case's `attributions` list, recomputes the three bucket totals from that list, and recomputes `unexplained_cents` as `total_variance_cents` less the sum of the three bucket totals. The conservation identity therefore holds after every single entry: `total_variance_cents` always equals `attributed_register_cents` plus `attributed_safe_cents` plus `attributed_bank_cents` plus `unexplained_cents`. The journal's `net_unexplained_cents` is recomputed to match, and the case's `version` increments by 1. The very first attribution also moves the case from `OPEN` to `RESEARCHING`; every subsequent one, including a correcting entry, leaves it in `RESEARCHING`.

Reaching zero unexplained closes an ordinary case automatically. When an attribution brings `unexplained_cents` to exactly zero and the case's `suspected_theft` is false, the case moves from `RESEARCHING` to `EXPLAINED`, which is terminal; the sales journal's `open_case_count` and the cashier's `open_case_count` are each decremented, the journal's `net_unexplained_cents` is recomputed, and the journal's compound derivation is re-evaluated. A case whose `suspected_theft` is true behaves differently on the identical input: it reaches `unexplained_cents` of zero and *stays* `RESEARCHING`, because only loss prevention may end a suspected-theft case.

Escalation is the store manager's judgement call, and a `LossPreventionInvestigator` may also file it. The escalation carries an `escalation_reason` and is available from `OPEN` or `RESEARCHING`; any other status answers `ERR_VARIANCE_CASE_INVALID_STATE`. It is gated on evidence: either the case's `total_variance_cents` is at or above the store's `theft_escalation_threshold_cents`, or the case's cashier has at least two other variance cases created within the preceding 90 days — the rolling-pattern route. Neither condition holding means `ERR_ESCALATION_THRESHOLD_NOT_MET`.

Escalation then sets off a three-link cascade plus one fan-out, and every consequence matters. The first link sets the case's `status` to `ESCALATED`, records `escalated_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`, and forces `suspected_theft` to true even if the magnitude alone would not have warranted it. The second link fires *because* the case became escalated: when the case names a cashier, that cashier's `till_eligibility` becomes `BARRED`, with `coaching_state` left untouched. The third link fires *because* that cashier became barred: every till session of that cashier whose `status` is still `ACTIVE` is force-closed, moving from `ACTIVE` to `PENDING_COUNT` with `forced_close` set to true, leaving all three point-of-sale figures null, and writing **no** safe ledger row — no cash moves during a forced close, and those sessions can consequently never be reconciled. Separately from that chain, and as a direct fan-out of the first link, every safe belonging to the case's store has its `posture` set to `FROZEN`, which blocks all cash movement at that store as described in §2.9. The `version` of the case, of the barred cashier and of each frozen safe increments by 1.

Closing an escalated case belongs to the `LossPreventionInvestigator` alone, who records a `disposition` of either `SUBSTANTIATED` or `UNSUBSTANTIATED` and may attach a `closing_note`; a `closing_note`, when supplied, must be a non-empty string, and an empty one is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The case must be `ESCALATED`; closing a case in any other status answers `ERR_VARIANCE_CASE_INVALID_STATE`. The two dispositions diverge deliberately. A `SUBSTANTIATED` finding moves the case to `CLOSED_SUBSTANTIATED`, writes the confirmed loss off the ledger with one safe transaction of kind `VARIANCE_ADJUSTMENT` against the store's earliest-created safe — the one with the lowest `created_at` — carrying `declared_amount_cents` equal to the case's `unexplained_cents`, recomputes that safe's `balance_cents`, and leaves the cashier's `till_eligibility` at `BARRED`. An `UNSUBSTANTIATED` finding moves the case to `CLOSED_UNSUBSTANTIATED`, writes **no** safe transaction at all — an unsubstantiated case moves no cash — and reinstates the cashier: provided no other case naming that cashier is still `ESCALATED`, `till_eligibility` is restored to `RESTRICTED` when the cashier's `rolling_absolute_variance_cents` is at or above the store's `rolling_variance_threshold_cents`, and to `ELIGIBLE` otherwise, with `coaching_state` untouched in either outcome.

Both closures share the same housekeeping. The sales journal's `open_case_count` and the cashier's `open_case_count` are decremented, the journal's `net_unexplained_cents` is recomputed, every safe of the store has its `posture` restored to `OPEN` once no escalated case remains for that store, the journal's compound derivation is re-evaluated — which is what lets an `OUT_OF_BALANCE` day finally reach `BALANCED` — and the case's `version` increments by 1.

### 3.9. Forecasting Change Orders and Planning Denominations

The last workflow looks forward rather than back. A `CashOfficeClerk` files a change order to bring coin and small notes in from the bank, supplying the `store_id`, the `target_week_start` — the first business date the order covers, in `YYYY-MM-DD` form — the `days_covered` of demand the order must cover, and the store's `baseline_daily_cash_demand_cents` before seasonal and growth adjustment. Two optional inputs shape the forecast: `growth_pct`, a year-over-year adjustment expressed as a decimal fraction such as 0.05, which defaults to 0 and must lie between -0.50 and 2.00 inclusive; and `seasonal_profile_override`, which overrides the store's standing profile for this order alone. `days_covered` must lie between 1 and 14 inclusive, and `baseline_daily_cash_demand_cents` must be zero or greater. Change orders may be listed with `store_id`, `status` and `target_week_start` filters.

The profile actually used is recorded on the order as `applied_seasonal_profile`: it is `seasonal_profile_override` when that field is supplied, and otherwise the store's `seasonal_profile` as read at creation time. Each profile carries a demand multiplier — `BASELINE` multiplies by 1.00, `BACK_TO_SCHOOL` by 1.35, and `HOLIDAY` by 1.80, reflecting how much harder a store's drawers work in late August and in December. The order's `forecast_demand_cents` is `baseline_daily_cash_demand_cents` multiplied by `days_covered`, multiplied by that seasonal multiplier, multiplied by one plus `growth_pct`, with the result rounded half up to whole cents.

That forecast is then broken into a physical denomination plan, because banks ship cash in whole straps and whole rolls. Five denomination rows are always planned, always in descending order of denomination, and each always produces one entry in `planned_lines` — even when its raw unit count rounds up from a fraction. Twenty-dollar notes take a 0.50 share of the forecast and come in straps of 25 notes; ten-dollar notes take 0.20 and also come in straps of 25; five-dollar notes take 0.15 and come in straps of 50; one-dollar notes take 0.10 and come in straps of 50; and quarters take the remaining 0.05 and come in rolls of 40 coins.

For each row the raw unit count is `forecast_demand_cents` multiplied by that row's share and divided by the row's `denomination_cents`. The row's `strap_count` is that raw count divided by the row's units-per-strap and rounded **up** to the next whole strap or roll. The row's `units` is `strap_count` multiplied by the units-per-strap, and its `planned_cents` is `units` multiplied by `denomination_cents`. Each entry in `planned_lines` therefore carries `denomination_cents`, `strap_count`, `units` and `planned_cents`. Only after every line has been rounded up individually is `total_planned_cents` formed as the sum of the lines' `planned_cents`. This order is pinned and is not interchangeable with summing the exact per-line targets and rounding the total once: for a `forecast_demand_cents` of 1008000, rounding each line up first yields a `total_planned_cents` of 1106000, whereas summing first and rounding once yields 1007200.

A new order sits at `status` `DRAFT` with `approved_by_user_id` and `received_amount_cents` null. A `StoreManager` decides it. Approval, which may carry an `approval_note`, moves the order from `DRAFT` to `APPROVED` and records the deciding `X-User-ID` as `approved_by_user_id`. Rejection, which requires a `rejection_reason`, moves the order from `DRAFT` to `REJECTED` and likewise records the deciding manager; a rejected order is terminal and can never be received. Deciding an order that is not `DRAFT` answers `ERR_CHANGE_ORDER_INVALID_STATE`.

Receiving the delivery closes the loop back into the safe ledger. A `CashOfficeClerk` records the `received_amount_cents` actually delivered by the bank; the order must be `APPROVED`, failing which the answer is again `ERR_CHANGE_ORDER_INVALID_STATE`. The cash is booked into the store's earliest-created safe — the one with the lowest `created_at` — whose `posture` must be `OPEN`, otherwise the receipt is refused with `ERR_SAFE_FROZEN`. Receipt writes one safe ledger row of kind `CHANGE_ORDER_RECEIPT` linked to the order and to that safe, with `declared_amount_cents` equal to `received_amount_cents`, recomputes the safe's `balance_cents`, and increments both the order's and the safe's `version` by 1. The order's `status` becomes `RECEIVED`, which is terminal.
