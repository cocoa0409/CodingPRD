# Fair Trade Certification & Producer Premium Administration API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Fair Trade certification is only as credible as the paper trail behind it. A bag of certified arabica that reaches a supermarket shelf has passed through farms, cooperatives, processors, exporters, importers and manufacturers, and at every one of those handovers somebody must be able to answer three questions: was the seller certified on the day of the contract, was the certified volume actually available at that node, and did the community development premium the standard promises actually reach the producers' fund. Today those three questions are answered by disconnected spreadsheets, PDF audit reports and email threads between certification staff and licensees. The consequence is predictable — premium money that cannot be reconciled against the trades that generated it, mark usage claims printed on packaging whose upstream chain never supported them, and corrective action deadlines that quietly expire.

This document specifies the Fair Trade Certification & Producer Premium Administration API, a single deterministic service that owns the whole certification and premium record. It registers certificate holders at each node of the supply chain, maps those nodes into rooted upstream chains, prices every certified trade against the fair trade minimum floor and the community development premium, keeps a conserved append-only ledger for each producer's premium fund, runs premium spending through producer-committee ballots, schedules audits with auditor-travel and holder-exclusivity constraints, grades nonconformities and tracks their corrective action deadlines to escalation, prices and collects label licence fees, approves on-pack mark usage claims, and reconciles the volume both sides of a trade relationship declare.

Two engineering constraints shape everything that follows. First, the service is **deterministic**: it never reads the wall clock. All timestamps, deadlines, window comparisons and fiscal-year attributions are evaluated against a logical clock that an operator sets explicitly, so a compliance sweep run twice from the same state produces the same result twice. Second, the service is **conservative about money and volume**: the premium fund and each node's certified-volume position are governed by stated invariants that must hold after every single mutation, not merely at the end of a batch. Every endpoint in this specification is mounted beneath the `/fairtrade` prefix.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section fixes the contract-level behaviour that applies uniformly across the API: authentication, response shape, pagination, the logical clock, common resource properties, numeric precision, cascade discipline, referential resolution order, optimistic concurrency, validation precedence, and the two conservation principles that the domain is built on.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request other than the logical-clock endpoint must carry two mandatory HTTP headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which that principal is acting. If either header is absent or empty, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the six roles enumerated below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If both headers are well-formed but the acting role is not listed among the authorized roles for the endpoint being called, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The six recognized roles are `CertificationOfficer`, `Auditor`, `CommitteeChair`, `CommitteeMember`, `ProducerRepresentative` and `BrandLicensee`.

- A `CertificationOfficer` is Fair Trade USA certification staff. Officers register certificate holders, drive the certification lifecycle, price licences, settle and void trades, reconcile volume reports, schedule and close audits, decide mark usage requests, disburse approved premium uses, and run the compliance sweep.
- An `Auditor` is a third-party auditor bound to a specific audit. Auditors start their assigned audits on site, raise graded nonconformities, review corrective action plans they did not themselves raise, and record verification outcomes on trades.
- A `CommitteeChair` is the elected chair of a producer premium committee. Chairs draft premium use proposals, open and close voting rounds, revise returned proposals and cancel them.
- A `CommitteeMember` is an elected member of a producer premium committee and casts exactly one ballot per proposal voting round.
- A `ProducerRepresentative` acts on behalf of one producer-side certificate holder: it declares harvest volumes at its own nodes, records certified trades out of its own nodes, files volume reports and submits corrective action plans.
- A `BrandLicensee` acts on behalf of one brand-side certificate holder: it pays label licence fees, files mark usage requests, registers its own downstream nodes and files buyer-basis volume reports.

**How `X-User-ID` is interpreted per role.** When `X-User-Role` is `ProducerRepresentative`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `CertificateHolder`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `CertificateHolder` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. The same rule applies when `X-User-Role` is `BrandLicensee`: `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `CertificateHolder`. Concretely, the compared field is `holder_id` for a supply chain node, a volume report or a premium committee; `brand_holder_id` for a mark usage request; the `holder_id` of the seller node's owning holder when a trade transaction is created; and the `holder_id` of the finding's audit when a corrective action plan is submitted. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_HOLDER_OWNERSHIP_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403).

When `X-User-Role` is `CertificationOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it (for example as `decided_by_user_id` on a mark usage request the officer decides) but never compares it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `Auditor`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it (as `raised_by_user_id` on a finding the auditor raises) and compares it only against opaque identifier fields — the `auditor_user_id` stored on an audit when that audit is started, and the `raised_by_user_id` of a finding whose corrective action plan is being reviewed. When `X-User-Role` is `CommitteeChair`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; it is compared against the `chair_user_id` recorded on the target committee, never against any entity's `id`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_HOLDER_OWNERSHIP_MISMATCH`. When `X-User-Role` is `CommitteeMember`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; it is stored as `voter_user_id` on the ballot the member casts and is used only for ballot-uniqueness and segregation-of-duties comparisons.

**Segregation of duties.** Two comparisons in this system exist purely to keep one principal from both proposing and blessing the same thing. The `X-User-ID` reviewing a corrective action plan must differ from the `raised_by_user_id` of the finding under review, and the `X-User-ID` of the chair closing a voting round must not appear as a `voter_user_id` among the ballots of the round being closed. Either violation is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). More broadly, that error covers any attempt by an acting `X-User-ID` to review a corrective action plan it raised, to verify a trade it recorded, to decide a mark usage request it filed, or to vote on a proposal whose committee it chairs.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every endpoint that returns a collection is paginated through two query parameters. The `limit` parameter caps the number of items returned; it defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1 and may not be larger than 100. The `offset` parameter is the number of items to skip and defaults to 0; it may not be negative. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422).

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, list results are ordered by the resource's creation timestamp in ascending order. The `meta` object of every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`, where `total_count` counts the rows matching the filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` indicates whether any matching row remains beyond the returned page.

All filter parameters described in this document are optional and combine conjunctively: a request supplying two filters returns only rows matching both.

### 2.4. The Deterministic Logical Clock

Nothing in this service reads the host's wall clock. Instead a single logical instant, which this document refers to as *the clock*, is the sole source of "now" for every timestamp the server stamps, every deadline it computes, and every window or period comparison it evaluates. Until an operator sets it explicitly, the clock reads `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`.

A `CertificationOfficer` moves the clock by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant; the response reports the new value as `current_utc`. Setting the clock performs no business mutation of its own — no state machine advances, no counter is recomputed, no version is bumped — it only changes what later requests will read as the present moment. This endpoint is the one exception to the header rules of §2.1 in that it is exempt from the `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` check for a missing header pair; when headers are supplied, the role restriction still applies.

Where a rule compares a date-only value (`window_start`, `window_end`, `period_start`, `period_end`, `contract_date`, `use_period_start`, `use_period_end`, all formatted `YYYY-MM-DD`) against the clock, the clock's calendar date in UTC is used. Where a rule compares a full instant (`deadline_at`, `transition_deadline_at`), the comparison is made against the clock instant itself.

### 2.5. Common Resource Properties

Certificate holders, supply chain nodes, premium committees, premium funds, trade transactions, volume reports, premium use proposals, audits, nonconformities and mark usage requests are all first-class resources and each carries the same four system-managed properties:

- `id` — a server-minted UUID, with no type prefix of any kind.
- `created_at` — an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with exactly three fractional digits (for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`), taken from the clock at the moment the row was created.
- `updated_at` — an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format, refreshed from the clock on every mutation of the row.
- `version` — an integer that starts at 1 and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of the row, including mutations the server applies to it as a cascade from some other resource and including recomputation of a derived counter.

Two record types in this system are append-only events rather than mutable resources, and they are deliberately narrower: a premium fund ledger entry and a committee ballot carry only `id` and `created_at`. Neither has an `updated_at` field and neither has a `version` field, because neither is ever modified after it is written.

A consequence worth stating plainly: recomputing a derived field is a mutation. When a finding is closed and the owning holder's `compliance_score` changes, that holder's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increments, exactly as if a client had written to it directly.

### 2.6. Precision, Rounding and Units

Three numeric domains coexist in this service and each has its own fixed precision.

**Money** is always an integer number of cents. There are no fractional cents anywhere in a stored field or a response body. Wherever a monetary figure is derived from a multiplication or a percentage, the result is rounded **half up** to a whole cent. When the quantity being rounded may be negative, rounding half up is applied to the absolute value and the sign is reapplied afterwards, so that a magnitude of exactly one half cent always rounds away from zero.

**Volume** is always kilograms carried to exactly 3 decimal places, rounded half up. This applies to `volume_kg`, `certified_volume_kg`, `premium_volume_kg`, `declared_volume_kg`, `counterparty_volume_kg`, `requested_volume_kg`, `licensed_volume_kg`, `annual_certified_volume_kg`, `certified_input_kg`, `reserved_output_kg`, `claimed_output_kg` and `available_credit_kg`.

**Percentages** are carried to exactly 2 decimal places, rounded half up, and this applies to `quorum_pct`, `turnout_pct`, `approval_pct` and the signed `variance_pct`. As with money, a percentage that may be negative is rounded on its absolute value with the sign reapplied.

Two places in this specification pin the *order* in which rounding is applied, because rounding once at the end of a sum yields a different figure from rounding each term first. Those two places are the mass-balance variant of the community development premium and the tiered label licence fee; both are spelled out where they are specified, and both must be implemented in the stated order.

One integer scale is exempt from the money rule: `compliance_score` is a plain integer constrained to the closed range 0 through 100. `chain_depth`, `member_count`, `current_round`, `round_number`, `cap_round`, `nonconformity_count`, `open_nonconformity_count`, `open_proposal_count`, `pending_proposal_count`, `open_critical_count`, `open_major_count`, `open_minor_count`, `escalation_grace_days`, `license_year` and `fiscal_year` are likewise plain integers.

### 2.7. Cascade Discipline Across the Certification Hierarchy

Certification is hierarchical, and so are its consequences. A certificate holder owns supply chain nodes; nodes originate trade transactions; a settled transaction moves money into a premium fund; a fund balance governs whether a premium committee may vote; a restricted committee cancels its own draft proposals. When the server mutates a resource at the top of that hierarchy it must carry the consequences all the way down in the same request, atomically, before it responds.

Three discipline rules govern every cascade in this document. First, **ordering is deterministic**: whenever a cascade touches a set of sibling rows, those rows are processed in ascending `created_at` order unless the specific rule names a different ordering. Second, **every mutated row is a real mutation**: each row the cascade touches has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by 1, including rows several hops away from the resource the client actually addressed. Third, **cascades are all-or-nothing**: if any part of an operation fails a validation, no part of it is persisted — a rejected reconciliation, for example, leaves the report exactly as it was, with no field written.

The deepest cascades in this system run four hops. Suspending a holder moves the holder, then its nodes, then those nodes' pending transactions, and separately its label licence and its pending mark usage requests. Approving a premium use proposal moves the proposal, then appends a ledger entry and moves the fund, then possibly moves the committee, then possibly cancels that committee's sibling draft proposals. Implementers should not treat the far end of a cascade as optional bookkeeping: the counters and versions it writes are part of the observable contract.

### 2.8. Resolution Order and Referential Integrity

Path identifiers are resolved strictly left to right. When a path contains more than one identifier, the server rejects the request on the *first* identifier that does not name an existing row, with the 404 error belonging to that identifier's type, before it examines anything further to the right. The 404 codes are `ERR_HOLDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_NODE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRANSACTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COMMITTEE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROPOSAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AUDIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_NONCONFORMITY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VOLUME_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LEDGER_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_MARK_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`. `ERR_HOLDER_NOT_FOUND` covers any unresolvable certificate holder reference, whether it arrived as a path identifier, as `holder_id`, as `counterparty_holder_id` or as `brand_holder_id`; `ERR_NODE_NOT_FOUND` likewise covers a path identifier, `seller_node_id`, `buyer_node_id` or `upstream_node_id`.

Foreign keys supplied in a request body are resolved after path identifiers, in the declaration order of that endpoint's input list. So a trade transaction create with an unknown seller and an unknown buyer reports the seller's failure, because `seller_node_id` is declared before `buyer_node_id`.

### 2.9. Optimistic Concurrency on Parent Snapshots

Two creation endpoints in this service consume a shared resource whose balance the client cannot see atomically, and both are therefore guarded by an optimistic concurrency check against the parent's `version`. Neither check is a timing rule: each is a stale-snapshot rule that a single client can trigger with two strictly sequential requests.

A `POST /fairtrade/premium-use-proposals` request must carry `expected_fund_version`, a non-optional integer field holding the `version` of the target committee's `PremiumFund` as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the fund's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_FUND_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Proposal creation is gated on the fund's `balance_cents` and itself bumps the fund's `pending_proposal_count` and `version`, so a client that read the fund, created one proposal, and then submitted a second create still carrying the pre-create version is deterministically rejected — it would otherwise be asking for money that its own earlier proposal, or somebody else's approved proposal, has already laid claim to.

A `POST /fairtrade/trade-transactions` request must carry `expected_seller_node_version`, a non-optional integer field holding the `version` of the seller `SupplyChainNode` as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the seller node's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_NODE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Transaction creation reserves certified credit out of the seller node's `available_credit_kg` and bumps that node's `version`, so a create carrying a stale node version is asking to reserve credit that a previously created transaction may already hold. Again, sequential requests suffice: read the node's version, create one transaction, then submit a second create still carrying the pre-create version.

No other endpoint in this specification accepts a version field. Verification, settlement, voiding, vote opening and closing, disbursement, cancellation, audit actions and licence operations all take no `expected_*_version` input, and supplying one is simply ignored.

### 2.10. Validation Precedence

When several rules could reject the same request, exactly one error must come back, and which one is fixed. Validations are evaluated in the strict order below; the first rule that fails determines the response, and no later rule is consulted. Readers should treat this list as a total ordering, not as an unordered set of independent checks.

1. Header presence and role legality — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`, on every endpoint.
2. Endpoint role authorization — `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`, on every endpoint.
3. Request-body and query shape — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, on every write endpoint and every list endpoint. This covers presence of every required input field, correct JSON type per the field's declared type, enum membership, the numeric ranges stated throughout this document (`volume_kg` strictly greater than 0, `declared_unit_price_cents_per_kg` at least 0, `requested_amount_cents` strictly greater than 0, `member_count` at least 3, `conversion_ratio` at least 0.001 and at most 1.000, `approved_use_categories` holding at least one element), date strings parsing as `YYYY-MM-DD`, `limit` between 1 and 100 inclusive, and `offset` at least 0.
4. Existence of every path-templated resource, left to right — the 404 family of §2.8.
5. Existence of every foreign key supplied in the body, in the endpoint's input declaration order — `ERR_HOLDER_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_NODE_NOT_FOUND`. This applies to node registration, committee constitution, transaction recording, volume report filing, proposal drafting, mark usage filing and audit scheduling.
6. Ownership for the two entity-backed roles — `ERR_HOLDER_OWNERSHIP_MISMATCH`, on node registration, harvest declaration, transaction recording, volume report filing, mark usage filing, corrective action plan submission and licence fee payment.
7. Chair and auditor binding — `ERR_HOLDER_OWNERSHIP_MISMATCH`, on proposal drafting, vote opening, vote closing, proposal revision, proposal cancellation and audit start.
8. Corrective-action-review segregation of duties — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, on corrective action plan review.
9. Vote-closing segregation of duties — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, on vote closing.
10. Optimistic concurrency on the parent snapshot — `ERR_STALE_FUND_VERSION` on proposal creation, `ERR_STALE_NODE_VERSION` on transaction creation.
11. The target resource's own state permits the requested transition — `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409), on every action endpoint.
12. Node activity for trade operations — `ERR_NODE_NOT_ACTIVE`, on transaction recording, verification and settlement.
13. Owning-holder certification for premium voting and settlement — `ERR_HOLDER_NOT_CERTIFIED`, on vote opening and settlement.
14. Committee activity for voting — `ERR_COMMITTEE_NOT_ACTIVE`, on vote opening.
15. Suspension block on premium spending — `ERR_PREMIUM_USE_BLOCKED_BY_SUSPENSION`, on proposal creation and disbursement.
16. Escalated-finding block on new trade — `ERR_OPEN_CRITICAL_NONCONFORMITY`, on transaction recording.
17. Licence activity for mark usage — `ERR_LABEL_LICENSE_INACTIVE`, on mark usage filing and mark usage decision.
18. Traceability-mode compatibility — `ERR_TRACEABILITY_MODE_MISMATCH`, on mark usage decision and node registration.
19. Certified-link existence between the trading nodes — `ERR_SUPPLY_CHAIN_LINK_MISSING`, on transaction recording.
20. Chain depth ceiling — `ERR_SUPPLY_CHAIN_DEPTH_EXCEEDED`, on node registration.
21. Ballot uniqueness — `ERR_DUPLICATE_VOTE`, on ballot casting.
22. Committee uniqueness and holder eligibility — `ERR_COMMITTEE_ALREADY_EXISTS`, on committee constitution.
23. Audit window sanity — `ERR_INVALID_AUDIT_WINDOW`, on audit scheduling.
24. Auditor window exclusion with travel buffer — `ERR_AUDITOR_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, on audit scheduling.
25. Holder window exclusion — `ERR_HOLDER_AUDIT_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, on audit scheduling.
26. Corrective action deadline — `ERR_CAP_DEADLINE_PASSED`, on corrective action plan submission.
27. Volume report period exclusion — `ERR_REPORT_PERIOD_OVERLAP`, on volume report filing.
28. Fund window capacity — `ERR_FUND_PERIOD_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, on vote closing.
29. Use category approval — `ERR_USE_CATEGORY_NOT_APPROVED`, on proposal drafting and proposal revision.
30. Mass-balance credit gate — `ERR_MASS_BALANCE_CREDIT_EXCEEDED`, on transaction recording.
31. Fund sufficiency — `ERR_FUND_BALANCE_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 402), on proposal drafting, vote closing, disbursement and transaction voiding.
32. Licence fee settlement — `ERR_LICENSE_FEE_UNPAID` (HTTP 402), on mark usage decision.
33. Reconciliation hard cap — `ERR_VOLUME_VARIANCE_EXCEEDED`, on volume report reconciliation.
34. Audit closure completeness — `ERR_AUDIT_HAS_OPEN_NONCONFORMITIES`, on audit closing.

Note the practical consequences of this ordering. On audit scheduling, an invalid window is reported before either overlap conflict, and the auditor conflict is reported before the holder conflict, so a request that violates both reports `ERR_AUDITOR_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. On transaction recording, the escalated-finding block is evaluated before the certified-link check, which is evaluated before the mass-balance credit gate, which is evaluated before nothing else — a request that both lacks a certified link and exceeds credit reports `ERR_SUPPLY_CHAIN_LINK_MISSING`. On vote closing, segregation of duties precedes the tally, the fund window capacity check precedes the fund sufficiency check, and both precede any state write. On proposal drafting, the suspension block precedes the use-category check, which precedes the fund sufficiency check.

### 2.11. Premium Fund Conservation

Every producer organization's community development premium is held in a `PremiumFund`, and every movement of that money is recorded as an immutable `PremiumLedgerEntry`. The ledger is append-only: entries are never updated and never deleted, which is why they carry no `updated_at` and no `version`.

Each entry records the fund it belongs to in `fund_id`, a positive magnitude in `amount_cents`, an `entry_type` drawn from `ACCRUAL`, `REVERSAL`, `COMMITMENT`, `RELEASE` and `DISBURSEMENT`, a human-readable provenance string in `memo` stamped by the transition that wrote it, and the fund's `balance_cents` immediately after the entry was applied in `balance_after_cents`. Provenance is also structural: an entry carries `trade_transaction_id` — populated for the `ACCRUAL` and `REVERSAL` types and null otherwise — or `proposal_id` — populated for the `COMMITMENT`, `RELEASE` and `DISBURSEMENT` types and null otherwise. Exactly one of the two is ever set on a given entry.

The derived field `signed_amount_cents` gives each entry its sign: it equals `amount_cents` for an `ACCRUAL`, it equals the negation of `amount_cents` for a `REVERSAL`, and it is 0 for a `COMMITMENT`, a `RELEASE` or a `DISBURSEMENT`, because those three are internal moves between the fund's own compartments and bring no new money in or out.

Three invariants hold after **every** fund mutation, not merely at the end of a working day:

- The fund's `total_accrued_cents` equals the sum of `signed_amount_cents` across every ledger entry of that fund.
- The fund's `total_accrued_cents` equals the sum of its `balance_cents`, its `committed_cents` and its `disbursed_cents`. In business terms, `balance_cents` is premium accrued and not yet committed to an approved proposal, `committed_cents` is premium promised to approved proposals not yet paid out, and `disbursed_cents` is premium actually paid out.
- None of `balance_cents`, `committed_cents` or `disbursed_cents` is ever negative.

No rounding occurs anywhere in this conservation arithmetic; all of it is exact integer cents. Where a single request appends more than one entry, the entries are appended in one deterministic order and are read back ordered by `created_at` ascending. A fund also carries the `holder_id` of the producer organization that owns it, a `fiscal_year` equal to the clock's calendar year at the moment the fund was minted, and a `pending_proposal_count` tracking how many of the committee's proposals are still in play.

### 2.12. Certified Volume and the Mass-Balance Credit Invariant

The second conserved quantity in this system is physical certified volume, tracked per supply chain node. Each node stores three running totals and exposes one derived position:

`certified_input_kg` is the cumulative certified volume booked *into* the node — harvest declarations plus certified volume arriving from settled inbound transactions after that node's processing yield has been applied. `reserved_output_kg` is certified volume that recorded-but-not-yet-final transactions out of this node are holding. `claimed_output_kg` is certified volume permanently claimed out of the node by settled transactions. The derived field `available_credit_kg` is the node's `certified_input_kg` minus its `claimed_output_kg` minus its `reserved_output_kg`, rounded half up to 3 decimal places, and it is recomputed after every mutation of any of those three stored totals.

The writers of those totals, in the order the transitions apply them, are: a harvest declaration adds the declared certified volume to `certified_input_kg`; recording a trade adds the traded `volume_kg` to the seller node's `reserved_output_kg`; moving a trade's verification to `DISPUTED` subtracts that `volume_kg` from the seller node's `reserved_output_kg`; re-verifying a disputed trade back to `VERIFIED` adds it again; moving a trade's verification to `REJECTED` from a state that was still holding a reservation subtracts it; settling a trade subtracts `volume_kg` from the seller node's `reserved_output_kg` and adds the same `volume_kg` to its `claimed_output_kg`, while adding the traded `volume_kg` multiplied by the *buyer* node's `conversion_ratio`, rounded half up to 3 decimals, to the buyer node's `certified_input_kg`; and voiding a settled trade subtracts `volume_kg` from the seller's `claimed_output_kg` and subtracts the same conversion-adjusted figure from the buyer's `certified_input_kg`, exactly unwinding what settlement applied.

**The invariant.** For every node whose `traceability_mode` is `MASS_BALANCE`, `available_credit_kg` is greater than or equal to 0 at all times. That is why mass-balance sellers are credit-gated when a trade is recorded. A node whose `traceability_mode` is `PHYSICAL` is *not* credit-gated at recording time — physical traceability is proven by the chain of custody, not by a running balance — and its `available_credit_kg` therefore may legitimately go negative. The field is still stored and reported for physical nodes, computed by the same formula at the same precision.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Registering Holders and Mapping the Certified Chain

Everything in this system hangs off a **certificate holder** — one legal organization holding one certification scope. A `CertificationOfficer` registers a holder by supplying its registered `legal_name` (non-empty, at most 200 characters), its `holder_type`, the ISO-3166 alpha-2 `country_code` of registration (exactly two uppercase letters), and the `product_code` naming the certified product scope. `holder_type` is one of `PRODUCER_ORG`, `PROCESSOR`, `EXPORTER`, `IMPORTER` or `BRAND`, describing where in the certified chain the organization sits; `product_code` is one of `COFFEE_ARABICA`, `COCOA` or `BANANA`. A newly registered holder opens with `certificate_status` set to `PENDING`, a perfect `compliance_score` of 100, `label_license_status` set to `NONE`, `certified_at` null, `suspension_reason` set to `NONE`, `transition_deadline_at` null, `license_year` null, `licensed_volume_kg` null, `license_tier` null, `license_fee_cents` 0, `license_fee_paid_cents` 0, `license_fee_settled` false, `annual_certified_volume_kg` 0, `next_audit_due_at` null, and `open_critical_count`, `open_major_count` and `open_minor_count` all 0.

Registration has one side effect that is easy to miss. When and only when the new holder's `holder_type` is `PRODUCER_ORG`, the server also mints that holder's community development premium fund in the same request: a fund carrying the holder's `holder_id`, a `fiscal_year` equal to the clock's calendar year, and `total_accrued_cents`, `balance_cents`, `committed_cents`, `disbursed_cents` and `pending_proposal_count` all 0. Holders of any other type get no fund. Holders can be read individually — the read exposes every computed compliance and licence field — and listed with optional filters on `holder_type`, `certificate_status`, `country_code` and `product_code`.

**Nodes.** The certified chain itself is a graph of **supply chain nodes**, each one a physical or commercial point of custody operated by a holder. A `CertificationOfficer`, a `ProducerRepresentative` or a `BrandLicensee` registers a node by naming the operating `holder_id`, the node's `node_role` — one of `FARM`, `COOPERATIVE`, `PROCESSOR`, `EXPORTER`, `IMPORTER`, `MANUFACTURER` or `BRAND_NODE` — and the `traceability_mode` under which certified volume will be accounted at that node, either `PHYSICAL` or `MASS_BALANCE`. Two inputs are optional: `upstream_node_id`, naming the immediate upstream node that supplies this one, which is left null for chain-origin nodes (typically those whose `node_role` is `FARM` or `COOPERATIVE`); and `conversion_ratio`, the processing yield applied to inbound certified volume when it is booked as certified input here, which defaults to 1.000 and must lie between 0.001 and 1.000 inclusive at 3 decimal places. Nodes may be listed with optional filters on `holder_id`, `upstream_node_id`, `status`, `traceability_mode` and `node_role`.

A node's own `status` at birth is a branch on its owner's certification, not a fixed default. If the owning holder is still `PENDING`, the node is created `REGISTERED` and waits. If the owning holder is already `CERTIFIED` or `TRANSITIONING`, the node is created directly `ACTIVE` and may trade immediately. In both branches the node's `chain_depth` is derived the same way — 0 when `upstream_node_id` is null, otherwise the upstream node's `chain_depth` plus 1 — and in both branches `certified_input_kg`, `reserved_output_kg`, `claimed_output_kg` and `available_credit_kg` all start at 0. The full state vocabulary for a node is `REGISTERED`, `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED` and `RETIRED`, with `RETIRED` terminal; a node has no action endpoint of its own, so every subsequent movement is either this creation-time branch or a cascade from its holder.

Two structural guards apply when the link is drawn. A certified chain may not run deeper than six hops from its origin: if attaching the requested `upstream_node_id` would give the new node a `chain_depth` greater than 6, the registration is refused with `ERR_SUPPLY_CHAIN_DEPTH_EXCEEDED`. The same ceiling bounds any upward traversal of the chain — walking from a node up through successive `upstream_node_id` links may visit at most seven nodes in total before the traversal itself is reported as `ERR_SUPPLY_CHAIN_DEPTH_EXCEEDED`. (A repeated node identifier is unreachable through this API: `upstream_node_id` is supplied only at creation time and must always name a row that already exists, so the graph is acyclic by construction and the length ceiling is the only traversal bound needed.) The second guard protects the integrity of physical traceability: a node declaring `PHYSICAL` may not be registered directly downstream of a node whose `traceability_mode` is `MASS_BALANCE`, because physical custody cannot be reconstructed through a mass-balance accounting break. That attempt is refused with `ERR_TRACEABILITY_MODE_MISMATCH`.

**Chain traversal as a read.** A holder's traceability position is exposed as a computed summary. Starting from that holder's nodes whose `node_role` is `BRAND_NODE` and whose `status` is either `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, taken in ascending `created_at` order, the server walks each one upward through `upstream_node_id` until it reaches a node with no upstream, and unions the visited nodes across all those walks. Nodes that are `RETIRED` or still `REGISTERED` are excluded from the *starting* set but are still traversed when they turn up upstream of an eligible brand node. The summary reports the holder's `holder_id`, a `chain_mode`, a `chain_node_count` giving the size of that unioned set, a `brand_node_count`, a `physical_node_count`, a `mass_balance_node_count` and a `total_available_credit_kg` summing the holder's nodes' credit positions. `chain_mode` reads `ALL_PHYSICAL` when every node in the unioned set has `traceability_mode` equal to `PHYSICAL`, and `CONTAINS_MASS_BALANCE` when at least one does not. A holder with no eligible brand node at all is a distinct third case: `chain_mode` reads `NO_CHAIN`, which is emphatically not the same as `ALL_PHYSICAL`.

**Harvest declarations.** A `ProducerRepresentative` books certified volume into one of its own nodes by declaring a harvest: the request carries `certified_volume_kg`, the volume arriving, and `harvest_season`, the season label it belongs to. The node's `certified_input_kg` rises by the declared volume, its `available_credit_kg` is recomputed, and the owning holder's `annual_certified_volume_kg` is increased by the same declared volume; the node's `version` increments. A declaration is only accepted against a node whose `status` is `ACTIVE` — a `REGISTERED`, `SUSPENDED` or `RETIRED` node refuses it with `ERR_NODE_NOT_ACTIVE` — and only from the representative whose `X-User-ID` is the node's owning holder.

### 3.2. Granting, Suspending and Withdrawing Certification

A holder's `certificate_status` moves through `PENDING`, `CERTIFIED`, `SUSPENDED` and `TRANSITIONING` to the terminal `DECERTIFIED`, and the path is neither linear nor one-way. Only a `CertificationOfficer` drives it.

**Granting certification.** A `PENDING` holder is granted certification, advancing `PENDING → CERTIFIED`, provided it has at least one supply chain node whose `status` is `REGISTERED`; an application with no registered node at all cannot be certified. On success `certified_at` is stamped from the clock, every one of that holder's nodes sitting at `REGISTERED` cascades `REGISTERED → ACTIVE` in ascending `created_at` order, `compliance_score` is recomputed (with no findings on record it is 100), and `version` increments by 1 on the holder and on each cascaded node.

**Suspension.** A `CERTIFIED` holder can be suspended, moving `CERTIFIED → SUSPENDED`. The officer supplies a `reason`, which is recorded as `suspension_reason` and is one of `NONCONFORMITY_ESCALATION`, `VOLUME_VARIANCE` or `MANUAL` (the fourth member of that enumeration, `NONE`, is what the field reads when the holder is not under suspension). Suspension is the deepest cascade in the certification machine and it runs three hops from the holder. First, every node of that holder sitting at `ACTIVE` moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`, in ascending `created_at` order. Second, each node that just became suspended releases the reservations it was holding: every trade transaction whose seller is that node and which is still `UNVERIFIED` on the verification dimension and `PENDING` on the settlement dimension moves its verification `UNVERIFIED → DISPUTED`, and the node's `reserved_output_kg` is reduced by that transaction's `volume_kg`, processed in ascending transaction `created_at` order — so the node's `available_credit_kg` rises back. Third, if the holder's `label_license_status` was `ACTIVE` it becomes `SUSPENDED`, and every mark usage request of that holder still sitting at `SUBMITTED` moves `SUBMITTED → WITHDRAWN` with `decision_reason` set to the string `HOLDER_SUSPENDED` and `decided_at` stamped from the clock, again in ascending `created_at` order. Every holder row, node row, transaction row and mark usage request row the cascade touched has its `version` incremented by 1.

The same `CERTIFIED → SUSPENDED` transition, with the same three-hop cascade, is also driven by the server itself in two places: by nonconformity escalation during the compliance sweep, where `suspension_reason` is set to `NONCONFORMITY_ESCALATION` rather than taken from a request body, and by a flagged volume reconciliation, where it is set to `VOLUME_VARIANCE`.

**Reinstatement.** A `SUSPENDED` holder can be reinstated, closing the cycle `SUSPENDED → CERTIFIED`, but only when its compliance record is clean enough: its `open_critical_count` must be 0, and no nonconformity reachable through any of its audits may be sitting at `ESCALATED`. On success `suspension_reason` resets to `NONE`, every node of that holder at `SUSPENDED` cascades `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` in ascending `created_at` order, and — provided `license_year` is not null and `label_license_status` is `SUSPENDED` — the licence returns to `ACTIVE`. `version` increments on the holder and on each cascaded node. Two deliberate asymmetries: reservations that suspension released are **not** re-reserved by reinstatement, and mark usage requests that suspension withdrew are **not** resurrected. A licensee that wants those claims back must file them again.

**The sell-down transition.** Instead of reinstating or decertifying, an officer may grant a suspended holder a sell-down window, moving `SUSPENDED → TRANSITIONING`. This stamps `transition_deadline_at` at 90 days after the clock — a full calendar quarter in which remaining certified stock may still be moved — and, like reinstatement, cascades every `SUSPENDED` node back to `ACTIVE` in ascending `created_at` order so that stock can actually be sold, incrementing `version` on the holder and each cascaded node. Note what this branch does *not* do: it changes no licence field and touches no mark usage request.

**Decertification.** The sibling branch out of `SUSPENDED` is immediate decertification, `SUSPENDED → DECERTIFIED`, and its side-effect set diverges sharply from the sell-down branch — where sell-down reactivates nodes, decertification retires them. Every node of the holder whose `status` is not already `RETIRED` moves to `RETIRED` in ascending `created_at` order — a node sitting at `ACTIVE` makes the move `ACTIVE → RETIRED` and a node sitting at `SUSPENDED` makes the move `SUSPENDED → RETIRED`, and both branches carry the same pipeline consequence described next. Each node that just retired then rejects its open pipeline: every trade transaction whose seller is that node and whose settlement dimension is still `PENDING` has its verification moved to `REJECTED`, and the node's `reserved_output_kg` is reduced by that transaction's `volume_kg`, in ascending transaction `created_at` order. Every audit of that holder sitting at `SCHEDULED` moves `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED`, in ascending `created_at` order. Every premium committee of that holder whose `status` is not already `DISSOLVED` moves to `DISSOLVED`. And if `label_license_status` was either `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, it becomes the terminal `TERMINATED`. Every mutated row's `version` increments by 1. The officer supplies a `reason` with the request.

Decertification is also reachable from two other states. A `PENDING` application may be denied outright, moving `PENDING → DECERTIFIED`; in that narrower cascade every node of the holder — all of which are necessarily still `REGISTERED`, so the move is `REGISTERED → RETIRED` — moves to `RETIRED` in ascending `created_at` order and each mutated row's `version` increments, but there is no transaction pipeline, no audit, no committee and no licence to unwind. Separately, a `TRANSITIONING` holder whose `transition_deadline_at` has arrived at or passed the clock is moved `TRANSITIONING → DECERTIFIED` by the server during the compliance sweep, running exactly the same cascade as the `SUSPENDED → DECERTIFIED` branch described above.

Any attempt to drive a holder along a transition its current `certificate_status` does not offer — certifying an already-certified holder, reinstating a `PENDING` one, granting a sell-down window to a `CERTIFIED` one — is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.

### 3.3. Recording a Certified Trade: the Minimum Floor and the Premium

A **trade transaction** is the edge of the certified chain: one movement of certified volume from a seller node to a buyer node, priced at the moment it is recorded. Only a `ProducerRepresentative` records one, and only out of a node belonging to the holder it acts for. The request names the `seller_node_id` releasing the volume and the `buyer_node_id` receiving it, the `product_code` traded, the `quality_grade` — either `STANDARD` or `SPECIALTY` — the certified `volume_kg` (strictly greater than 0, three decimals), the `declared_unit_price_cents_per_kg` the two parties freely contracted before any fair trade floor is applied (an integer of at least 0), and the `contract_date` in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, which is what later attributes the trade to a reporting period and a licence year. Two further inputs are described elsewhere: the optional `is_organic` flag, which defaults to false and declares that the lot carries organic certification on top of fair trade, and the mandatory `expected_seller_node_version` of §2.9. Transactions may be listed with optional filters on `seller_node_id`, `buyer_node_id`, `verification_status`, `settlement_status` and `product_code`.

Before anything is priced, four business gates apply in the order §2.10 fixes. Both the seller and the buyer node must have `status` equal to `ACTIVE`, or the request is refused with `ERR_NODE_NOT_ACTIVE`. No nonconformity reachable through the seller node's owning holder may be sitting at `ESCALATED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_OPEN_CRITICAL_NONCONFORMITY` — a producer under escalated sanction cannot keep booking certified trade. The buyer node must name the seller node as its immediate `upstream_node_id`, establishing that a certified link between the two actually exists in the mapped chain; when it does not, the request is refused with `ERR_SUPPLY_CHAIN_LINK_MISSING`. And finally the mass-balance credit gate of §2.12 applies: when the *seller* node's `traceability_mode` is `MASS_BALANCE`, the requested `volume_kg` may not be strictly greater than that node's `available_credit_kg`, and a request that asks for more is refused with `ERR_MASS_BALANCE_CREDIT_EXCEEDED`. Because the comparison is strictly greater than, a `volume_kg` exactly equal to the node's `available_credit_kg` is accepted and drains it to precisely 0. A `PHYSICAL` seller node is not credit-gated here at all.

**Step one: the minimum price floor.** Fair Trade's core promise is a price floor, and the floor is assembled from three additive components, two of them conditional. The base component is the minimum free-on-board price for the traded product: 320 cents per kilogram for `COFFEE_ARABICA`, 260 cents per kilogram for `COCOA`, and 45 cents per kilogram for `BANANA`. To that is added an organic differential of 80 cents per kilogram, but only when `is_organic` is true; when it is false, nothing is added on that account. To that is added a quality differential determined by `quality_grade`: nothing at all for `STANDARD`, and 40 cents per kilogram for `SPECIALTY`. The sum of those three components is stored on the transaction as `applied_floor_cents_per_kg`.

The floor is a floor and never a ceiling: it may never pull a freely negotiated higher price downward. So `settled_unit_price_cents_per_kg` is whichever is larger of the `declared_unit_price_cents_per_kg` the parties agreed and the `applied_floor_cents_per_kg` just computed. From that pair the server derives what the floor actually cost the buyer: `floor_topup_cents` is the settled unit price minus the declared unit price, multiplied by `volume_kg`, and rounded half up to a whole cent — which is 0 whenever the negotiated price already met or beat the floor. The contract's total at the settled price, `gross_value_cents`, is the settled unit price multiplied by `volume_kg`, rounded half up to a whole cent. All four of these outputs are frozen at the moment of recording and are never recomputed afterwards, no matter what happens to the constant tables or to the transaction's own status.

**Step two: the community development premium.** Immediately after the floor is priced, and from the same request body plus one field read off the seller node, the server prices the community development premium that the producer's fund will receive. It begins with a base premium rate per kilogram for the traded product: 80 cents per kilogram for `COFFEE_ARABICA`, 60 cents per kilogram for `COCOA`, and 15 cents per kilogram for `BANANA`. Organic lots earn a proportional uplift rather than an additive tier: when `is_organic` is true that base rate is multiplied by 1.25, a twenty-five percent uplift; when it is false the base rate is used unchanged.

Which of two accounting variants prices the premium is a *structural* decision, not a client choice. It is read off the seller node's `traceability_mode` at recording time and stored on the transaction as `premium_basis`: a seller node in `PHYSICAL` mode yields `premium_basis` of `PHYSICAL_TRACEABILITY`, and a seller node in `MASS_BALANCE` mode yields `premium_basis` of `MASS_BALANCE`. In both variants `premium_volume_kg` — the volume the premium was assessed on — is the full traded `volume_kg`.

Under `PHYSICAL_TRACEABILITY`, the whole shipment is premium-bearing and the arithmetic is a single product rounded exactly once at the end: `premium_cents` is the uplift-adjusted base rate multiplied by `volume_kg`, rounded half up to a whole cent.

Under `MASS_BALANCE`, certified volume is attributed lot by lot rather than shipment by shipment, and **the rounding order is pinned**. The shipment is first decomposed into whole one-tonne lots: the number of full lots is the traded `volume_kg` divided by 1000 kilograms and rounded *down* to a whole number, and the remainder in kilograms is the traded `volume_kg` minus the full-lot count multiplied by 1000. A single full lot's premium is then computed once — the uplift-adjusted base rate, multiplied by 1000 kilograms, multiplied by a mass-balance attribution factor of 0.9725 (that is, 97.25 percent, the discount that mass-balance accounting carries against physical traceability) — and that product is rounded half up to a whole cent *before* it is used. The remainder's premium is computed separately as the uplift-adjusted base rate multiplied by the remainder kilograms multiplied by the same 0.9725 factor, and is likewise rounded half up to a whole cent on its own. Finally `premium_cents` is the already-rounded per-lot figure multiplied by the full-lot count, plus the already-rounded remainder figure. Summing the exact unrounded products and rounding once at the end is a different number and is wrong. For identical inputs this variant always yields a strictly smaller premium than `PHYSICAL_TRACEABILITY`.

`premium_cents`, `premium_volume_kg` and `premium_basis` are frozen at recording time. They are not recomputed when the transaction is verified, not when it is settled, and not when it is voided — which is precisely what makes a later reversal exactly cancel the original accrual.

**What recording writes.** A new transaction opens with `verification_status` of `UNVERIFIED` and `settlement_status` of `PENDING`, with `verified_at`, `settled_at` and `voided_at` all null. The seller node's `reserved_output_kg` rises by the traded `volume_kg`, its `available_credit_kg` is recomputed, and the seller node's `version` increments by 1 — that increment is what makes the next create carrying the old `expected_seller_node_version` fail.

### 3.4. Verification and Settlement: Two Independent Dimensions

A trade transaction carries two state dimensions on one row, and they advance independently of one another. `verification_status` records what the certification body believes about the trade and moves through `UNVERIFIED`, `VERIFIED` and `DISPUTED` to the terminal `REJECTED`. `settlement_status` records what happened to the money and volume and moves through `PENDING` and `SETTLED` to the terminal `REVERSED`. Advancing one dimension deliberately leaves the other untouched.

**Verification.** An `Auditor` records a verification outcome by supplying an `outcome`. An `UNVERIFIED` trade verified with an outcome of `VERIFIED` advances `UNVERIFIED → VERIFIED`, which requires that the seller node's `status` be `ACTIVE`, and stamps `verified_at` from the clock. An `UNVERIFIED` trade whose outcome is `DISPUTED` advances `UNVERIFIED → DISPUTED`; that branch imposes no node-activity requirement of its own, and it is also the transition the server drives as a cascade when a seller node moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`. When a trade enters `DISPUTED` the seller node's `reserved_output_kg` falls by the trade's `volume_kg` and its `available_credit_kg` is recomputed — the credit goes back on the shelf.

A disputed trade can be rehabilitated, closing the cycle `UNVERIFIED → DISPUTED → VERIFIED`. Re-verification requires three things: the trade's `verification_status` is `DISPUTED`, the seller node's `status` is `ACTIVE`, and the seller node's `available_credit_kg` is at least the trade's `volume_kg` — the reservation released on dispute must still be re-reservable, and if somebody else's trade has consumed that credit in the meantime the re-verification cannot proceed. On success `verified_at` is stamped from the clock, the seller node's `reserved_output_kg` rises again by `volume_kg` and the node's `version` increments, and the transaction's `version` increments. Alternatively a disputed trade may be finally rejected, `DISPUTED → REJECTED`, which requires that its `settlement_status` still be `PENDING`; that branch changes no node quantity, because the reservation was already released when the trade was disputed.

Rejection is also reached by cascade. When a seller node moves from `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` to `RETIRED` under a decertification, each of its trades still `PENDING` on the settlement dimension is moved to `REJECTED`. A trade that was `UNVERIFIED` at that moment moves `UNVERIFIED → REJECTED` and its `volume_kg` is subtracted from the node's `reserved_output_kg`, because it was still holding a reservation; a trade that was already `DISPUTED` moves `DISPUTED → REJECTED` and no quantity changes, because its reservation was released earlier. Both increment `version`.

**Settlement.** A `CertificationOfficer` settles a trade, advancing `PENDING → SETTLED` on the settlement dimension. Four preconditions must hold together: `settlement_status` is `PENDING`, `verification_status` is `VERIFIED`, the seller node's `status` is `ACTIVE` (otherwise `ERR_NODE_NOT_ACTIVE`), and the seller node's owning holder has `certificate_status` of either `CERTIFIED` or `TRANSITIONING` (otherwise `ERR_HOLDER_NOT_CERTIFIED`) — a holder in its sell-down window may still settle, which is the whole point of granting one.

Settlement is a three-hop cascade. It stamps `settled_at` from the clock. On the seller node, `reserved_output_kg` falls by `volume_kg` while `claimed_output_kg` rises by the same `volume_kg`, so `available_credit_kg` is unchanged, and the node's `version` increments. On the buyer node, `certified_input_kg` rises by `volume_kg` multiplied by that buyer node's own `conversion_ratio`, rounded half up to 3 decimals, and the buyer node's `version` increments — this is where processing yield is applied. A ledger entry with `entry_type` of `ACCRUAL` is then appended to the seller holder's premium fund, carrying `amount_cents` equal to the transaction's frozen `premium_cents`, `trade_transaction_id` pointing at this transaction, and `fund_id` naming that fund. That append drives the fund: `total_accrued_cents` and `balance_cents` each rise by `premium_cents`, the entry's `balance_after_cents` is stamped with the fund's `balance_cents` immediately after the application, and the fund's `version` increments. The changed fund balance in turn drives the committee — if the seller holder has a premium committee sitting at `FORMING` and the fund's `balance_cents` has reached at least 1,000,000 cents (ten thousand currency units, the threshold at which a committee is considered to have real money to govern), that committee advances `FORMING → ACTIVE`; and if the committee was sitting at `RESTRICTED` and `balance_cents` has returned to at least 200,000 cents, it advances `RESTRICTED → ACTIVE`. Finally the seller holder's `annual_certified_volume_kg` is recomputed as the sum of `volume_kg` over its settled outbound trades whose `contract_date` falls inside its current `license_year`, and the holder's `version` increments.

**Voiding.** Settlement's compensating action is a void, `SETTLED → REVERSED`, also restricted to a `CertificationOfficer`, who supplies a `reason`. It requires that the seller holder's fund still hold at least `premium_cents` in `balance_cents`; a fund that has already committed the money away cannot absorb the reversal, and the void is refused with `ERR_FUND_BALANCE_INSUFFICIENT`. On success `voided_at` is stamped from the clock; the seller node's `claimed_output_kg` falls by `volume_kg`, returning `available_credit_kg` to its pre-recording value, and the node's `version` increments; the buyer node's `certified_input_kg` falls by exactly the same conversion-adjusted figure that settlement added, and its `version` increments. A ledger entry with `entry_type` of `REVERSAL` and `amount_cents` equal to `premium_cents` is appended, lowering the fund's `total_accrued_cents` and `balance_cents` by that amount and incrementing the fund's `version`. If the committee was `ACTIVE` and the fund's `balance_cents` has now dropped below 200,000 cents, the committee moves `ACTIVE → RESTRICTED`. The seller holder's `annual_certified_volume_kg` is recomputed and its `version` increments. `verification_status` is deliberately left untouched by a void: the trade remains `VERIFIED`, because the audit finding about it has not changed.

### 3.5. Premium Fund Governance: Committees, Proposals and Ballots

The community development premium belongs to the producers, and the standard requires that they decide collectively how to spend it. That decision is made by a **premium committee** whose ballots this API records, and whose spending authority is bounded by both a category whitelist and the fund's own conserved balances.

**Constituting the committee.** A `CertificationOfficer` constitutes a committee by naming the governing `holder_id`, the `chair_user_id` of the elected chair (an opaque operator identifier, compared against the acting chair's `X-User-ID` and never against any entity's `id`), the `member_count` of elected members — at least 3, and the denominator of every later turnout calculation — and the `approved_use_categories` the committee is authorised to spend on. That last input is an array holding at least one element drawn from `EDUCATION`, `HEALTHCARE`, `INFRASTRUCTURE`, `PRODUCTIVITY_TOOLS`, `WORKING_CAPITAL` and `CASH_DISTRIBUTION`. One input is optional: `quorum_pct`, the minimum turnout percentage a voting round must reach to be tallied as decisive, which defaults to 60.00 when omitted and must be strictly greater than 0.00 and at most 100.00 at two decimal places. A committee may be constituted only for a holder whose `holder_type` is `PRODUCER_ORG`, and a holder may hold only one committee whose `status` is not `DISSOLVED` at a time; either violation is refused with `ERR_COMMITTEE_ALREADY_EXISTS`. New committees open at `status` `FORMING` with `open_proposal_count` 0. Committees may be listed per holder with an optional `status` filter, and a holder's funds may be listed with an optional `fiscal_year` filter. Ledger entries may be listed with optional filters on `fund_id`, `trade_transaction_id`, `proposal_id` and `entry_type`, and read individually.

**The committee's own lifecycle.** A committee's `status` cycles between `FORMING`, `ACTIVE` and `RESTRICTED`, with `DISSOLVED` terminal. It is never moved by a direct client action — every movement is a consequence of what the fund's `balance_cents` does. It leaves `FORMING` for `ACTIVE` when a settlement pushes the fund's `balance_cents` to at least 1,000,000 cents. It falls `ACTIVE → RESTRICTED` when a mutation leaves the fund's `balance_cents` strictly below 200,000 cents — either the commitment written on an approved proposal, or the reversal written on a void. Falling into `RESTRICTED` has its own consequence: every proposal of that committee still sitting at `DRAFT` is moved `DRAFT → CANCELLED` in ascending `created_at` order, and the committee's `version` plus each cancelled proposal's `version` increments by 1. It returns `RESTRICTED → ACTIVE`, closing the cycle, when the fund's `balance_cents` comes back to at least 200,000 cents — through a settlement, or through the release written when an approved proposal is cancelled, or through a disbursement that leaves the balance at or above that line. And when its holder is decertified it moves to the terminal `DISSOLVED` from wherever it stood — `FORMING → DISSOLVED`, `ACTIVE → DISSOLVED` or `RESTRICTED → DISSOLVED` — each of those three branches doing nothing beyond the state change itself and a `version` increment of 1.

**Drafting a proposal.** A `CommitteeChair` drafts a premium use proposal against its own committee, naming the `committee_id`, a `title` (non-empty, at most 200 characters), the `use_category` requested, the `requested_amount_cents` (strictly greater than 0), and the half-open planned spend window `use_period_start` inclusive through `use_period_end` exclusive, both `YYYY-MM-DD`. The mandatory `expected_fund_version` of §2.9 travels with it. Three business gates apply in the order §2.10 fixes: the committee's holder may not be `SUSPENDED`, `TRANSITIONING` or `DECERTIFIED`, or the draft is refused with `ERR_PREMIUM_USE_BLOCKED_BY_SUSPENSION`; the requested `use_category` must be a member of the committee's `approved_use_categories`, or the draft is refused with `ERR_USE_CATEGORY_NOT_APPROVED`; and the fund's `balance_cents` must already be at least the `requested_amount_cents`, or the draft is refused with `ERR_FUND_BALANCE_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 402). A committee may draft while `FORMING` or `RESTRICTED`; only voting is gated on being `ACTIVE`.

A new proposal opens at `status` `DRAFT` with `current_round` 0 — it has never been opened for a vote — with `turnout_pct` and `approval_pct` both 0.00, and with `approved_at`, `disbursed_at` and `cancelled_at` all null. Creation raises the committee's `open_proposal_count` by 1 and the fund's `pending_proposal_count` by 1, and increments the fund's `version` by 1. Proposals may be listed per committee with optional `status` and `use_category` filters, and read individually with their tally fields. The proposal's `status` vocabulary is `DRAFT`, `UNDER_VOTE`, `REVISION_REQUESTED`, `APPROVED`, `REJECTED`, `DISBURSED` and `CANCELLED`, with the last four terminal except that `APPROVED` is a way-station.

**Opening a round.** The chair opens a voting round on a draft proposal, advancing `DRAFT → UNDER_VOTE` and incrementing `current_round` by 1. Four preconditions hold: the proposal is `DRAFT`, the acting `X-User-ID` equals the committee's `chair_user_id`, the committee's `status` is `ACTIVE` — a `FORMING` or `RESTRICTED` committee may draft and revise but may not vote, and the refusal is `ERR_COMMITTEE_NOT_ACTIVE` — and the committee's holder has `certificate_status` of either `CERTIFIED` or `TRANSITIONING`, the refusal otherwise being `ERR_HOLDER_NOT_CERTIFIED`. The same `ERR_COMMITTEE_NOT_ACTIVE` code is what comes back whenever a proposal is drafted, opened, balloted or closed while its committee's `status` is not `ACTIVE`.

**Casting ballots.** A `CommitteeMember` casts one ballot per round, supplying a `ballot` of `APPROVE`, `REJECT` or `ABSTAIN` and an optional free-text `rationale` that defaults to null. The server stamps `voter_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`, `round_number` from the proposal's `current_round` at the instant the ballot is accepted, and `cast_at` from the clock, then recomputes the proposal's `turnout_pct` and `approval_pct` and increments the proposal's `version` by 1. No member may vote twice in the same round: a second ballot with the same `proposal_id`, `voter_user_id` and `round_number` is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_VOTE`. Ballots are never modified once cast; when a round is reopened, the old ballots stay on the record but stop counting, because they carry the previous `round_number`. Ballots may be listed for a proposal across all rounds, with optional `round_number` and `ballot` filters.

**Tallying and the three-way fork.** The chair closes the open round, and the outcome is computed, not chosen. Only ballots whose `proposal_id` is this proposal and whose `round_number` equals the proposal's `current_round` are counted; ballots from earlier rounds are excluded, no time window applies, and because the tally is a pure count it is order-insensitive and needs no tie-break. Let the ballot count be the number of counted ballots and the approve count be the number of those whose `ballot` is `APPROVE`. Then `turnout_pct` is the ballot count divided by the committee's elected `member_count`, multiplied by 100, rounded half up to two decimal places — the denominator is the elected membership, *not* the number of ballots received. And `approval_pct` is the approve count divided by the ballot count, multiplied by 100, rounded half up to two decimal places — the denominator here *is* the ballot count, and it includes `ABSTAIN` ballots, so abstentions dilute approval. When no ballot at all was counted, `turnout_pct` is 0.00 and `approval_pct` is 0.00 by definition.

The outcome forks three ways, and both comparisons are inclusive at their boundary. If `turnout_pct` is strictly less than the committee's `quorum_pct`, the round was not decisive and the outcome is a revision request. Otherwise, if `approval_pct` is at least 66.67 — two thirds of the ballots cast — the outcome is approval. Otherwise the outcome is rejection. Before segregating any of that, one authorization rule bites: the chair closing the round must not appear as a `voter_user_id` among the ballots of the round being closed, or the close is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

*The approval branch* (`UNDER_VOTE → APPROVED`) additionally requires that the fund's `balance_cents` be at least the `requested_amount_cents`, failing which the close is refused with `ERR_FUND_BALANCE_INSUFFICIENT`. It also carries a window-capacity ceiling: taking the closing proposal's half-open window from `use_period_start` inclusive to `use_period_end` exclusive together with every already-`APPROVED` proposal of the same committee whose own window overlaps it — two windows overlap when each one's start is strictly before the other's end, so windows that merely touch end-to-start do not overlap — the sum of `requested_amount_cents` across that overlapping set including the closing proposal must not exceed 80 percent of the fund's `total_accrued_cents`. A committee may not encumber more than four fifths of everything it has ever accrued inside a single overlapping planning window; breaching the ceiling is refused with `ERR_FUND_PERIOD_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, and it is checked before the balance sufficiency check. On success `turnout_pct` and `approval_pct` are stamped and `approved_at` is set from the clock; a ledger entry with `entry_type` `COMMITMENT` and `amount_cents` equal to `requested_amount_cents` and `proposal_id` pointing at this proposal is appended; the fund's `balance_cents` falls and its `committed_cents` rises by `requested_amount_cents` while `total_accrued_cents` is unchanged, the fund's `pending_proposal_count` falls by 1 and the committee's `open_proposal_count` falls by 1, and the fund's `version` increments. If that leaves `balance_cents` strictly below 200,000 cents the committee falls `ACTIVE → RESTRICTED`, and that restriction in turn cancels every sibling `DRAFT` proposal of the committee, `DRAFT → CANCELLED` with `cancelled_at` stamped from the clock, in ascending `created_at` order. `version` increments by 1 on every row this four-hop cascade mutated.

*The rejection branch* (`UNDER_VOTE → REJECTED`) applies when turnout met quorum but `approval_pct` fell short of 66.67. Its side-effect set is deliberately much thinner than its approving sibling's: `turnout_pct` and `approval_pct` are stamped, the fund's `pending_proposal_count` falls by 1 and the committee's `open_proposal_count` falls by 1, the fund's `version` increments, and the proposal's `version` increments. No ledger entry is written and `balance_cents` is untouched — nothing was ever committed, so nothing needs releasing.

*The revision branch* (`UNDER_VOTE → REVISION_REQUESTED`) applies when `turnout_pct` fell short of `quorum_pct`. `turnout_pct` and `approval_pct` are stamped, `current_round` increments by 1 so that a later round starts with a clean ballot set, and the proposal's `version` increments. That increment stands on its own and is not consumed by the later reopening: because **Opening a round** above increments `current_round` on every `DRAFT → UNDER_VOTE` without exception, a proposal that is sent back, revised and then reopened arrives at a `current_round` two higher than the round just closed — revision itself leaves `current_round` alone, so the number in between is skipped and no ballot ever carries it, ballots being castable only while the proposal is `UNDER_VOTE`. No ledger entry is written, and `pending_proposal_count` is *not* decremented here — the proposal is still in play, unlike in either of the other two branches.

**Revision.** The chair returns a revision-requested proposal to the drafting table, closing the loop `UNDER_VOTE → REVISION_REQUESTED → DRAFT → UNDER_VOTE`. Revision requires the proposal be `REVISION_REQUESTED` and the acting `X-User-ID` be the committee's `chair_user_id`, and it replaces `title`, `use_category` and `requested_amount_cents` with the values supplied in the request. The amended `use_category` is itself re-checked against `approved_use_categories`, with the same `ERR_USE_CATEGORY_NOT_APPROVED` refusal. Both `turnout_pct` and `approval_pct` are cleared to null, and the proposal's `version` increments by 1.

**Disbursement.** A `CertificationOfficer`, and only an officer, actually pays money out, advancing `APPROVED → DISBURSED`. Three preconditions hold: the proposal is `APPROVED`, the committee's holder is not `SUSPENDED` (otherwise `ERR_PREMIUM_USE_BLOCKED_BY_SUSPENSION`), and the fund's `committed_cents` is at least `requested_amount_cents` (otherwise `ERR_FUND_BALANCE_INSUFFICIENT`). On success `disbursed_at` is stamped from the clock, a ledger entry with `entry_type` `DISBURSEMENT` and `amount_cents` equal to `requested_amount_cents` is appended, and `requested_amount_cents` moves out of the fund's `committed_cents` into its `disbursed_cents` — `total_accrued_cents` and `balance_cents` are both unchanged, because a disbursement is an internal move. The fund's `version` increments, and the committee returns `RESTRICTED → ACTIVE` when the fund's `balance_cents` stands at or above 200,000 cents. The proposal's `version` increments by 1.

**Cancellation.** The chair may cancel a proposal, optionally supplying a `reason`, and the effect depends entirely on where the proposal stood. Cancelling an `APPROVED` proposal (`APPROVED → CANCELLED`) is the compensating action for its commitment: `cancelled_at` is stamped from the clock, a ledger entry with `entry_type` `RELEASE` and `amount_cents` equal to `requested_amount_cents` is appended, and `requested_amount_cents` moves from the fund's `committed_cents` back to its `balance_cents` with `total_accrued_cents` unchanged, restoring `balance_cents` to exactly what it was before approval and leaving the identity of §2.11 intact; the fund's `version` increments; and if the committee was `RESTRICTED` and `balance_cents` now stands at or above 200,000 cents, the committee returns `RESTRICTED → ACTIVE`. Cancelling a proposal that had not yet been approved — one sitting at `DRAFT`, which is also the transition the server drives when a committee falls into `RESTRICTED`, or one sitting at `REVISION_REQUESTED` — stamps `cancelled_at`, writes **no** ledger entry, and lowers both the committee's `open_proposal_count` and the fund's `pending_proposal_count` by 1 while incrementing the fund's `version`. In every branch the proposal's own `version` increments by 1.

### 3.6. Audits, Graded Findings and Corrective Action

Certification is maintained by audit, and this service owns both the calendar and the findings.

**Scheduling.** A `CertificationOfficer` schedules an audit by naming the `holder_id` under audit, the `auditor_user_id` of the assigned third-party auditor (an opaque operator identifier, and the scheduling resource that the exclusion rules below contend for), the `audit_type` — one of `INITIAL`, `SURVEILLANCE`, `FOLLOW_UP` or `UNANNOUNCED` — and the on-site window as `window_start` inclusive through `window_end` exclusive, both `YYYY-MM-DD`. Three temporal rules apply, in this order.

First, the window must be sane: `window_end` must fall strictly after `window_start`, and `window_start` must not fall earlier than the clock's current date. A backdated or inverted window is refused with `ERR_INVALID_AUDIT_WINDOW`.

Second, an auditor cannot be in two places at once, and needs travel time between engagements. The requested window is compared against every existing audit already carrying the same `auditor_user_id` whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `REPORT_PENDING`. The comparison widens the existing window by a three-day travel buffer on **both** sides and treats both ends as **inclusive**: the request conflicts when the new `window_start` is at or before the existing `window_end` plus three days, and simultaneously the new `window_end` is at or after the existing `window_start` minus three days. A conflict is refused with `ERR_AUDITOR_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Because the buffer is applied on both sides and both comparisons are inclusive, an auditor booked through a Friday cannot be booked again until the fourth day after that.

Third, a holder may host only one open audit at a time, and here the comparison is the ordinary half-open one with **no** buffer at all. The request conflicts when the new `window_start` falls strictly before the existing `window_end` and the existing `window_start` falls strictly before the new `window_end`, over that holder's audits whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `REPORT_PENDING`. Back-to-back windows that merely touch — where the existing `window_end` equals the new `window_start` — are therefore accepted. A genuine overlap is refused with `ERR_HOLDER_AUDIT_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.

A scheduled audit opens at `status` `SCHEDULED` with `nonconformity_count` 0, `open_nonconformity_count` 0, and `started_at` and `closed_at` null; scheduling also recomputes the holder's `next_audit_due_at`. Audits may be listed per holder with optional `status` and `audit_type` filters, and read individually with their counters.

**Running the audit.** The assigned auditor — and only the principal whose `X-User-ID` equals the audit's `auditor_user_id` — opens the audit on site, advancing `SCHEDULED → IN_PROGRESS` and stamping `started_at` from the clock. Two further preconditions apply: the clock's current date must have reached `window_start`, and the holder's `certificate_status` must be one of `CERTIFIED`, `SUSPENDED` or `TRANSITIONING` — a suspended holder is exactly the sort of holder one audits. A scheduled audit whose holder is later decertified is moved `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED` by that cascade and can never be started.

**Raising a finding.** The auditor raises a nonconformity by supplying the `clause_code` of the standard clause the finding is written against (non-empty, at most 40 characters), the severity `grade` — one of `CRITICAL`, `MAJOR`, `MINOR` or `OBSERVATION` — and a `description` narrative (non-empty, at most 1000 characters). Findings may be raised while the audit's `status` is either `IN_PROGRESS` or `REPORT_PENDING`, and the audit itself advances `IN_PROGRESS → REPORT_PENDING` on the **first** finding only; subsequent findings leave the audit where it is. The finding opens at `status` `OPEN` with `cap_round` 0, `cap_narrative`, `cap_submitted_at`, `cap_reviewed_at` and `closed_at` all null, and with `raised_by_user_id` taken from the acting `X-User-ID` and `audit_id` from the audit in whose context it was raised. The audit's `nonconformity_count` and `open_nonconformity_count` each rise by 1, and both the audit's `version` and the holder's `version` increment by 1. Findings may be listed per audit with optional `grade` and `status` filters, and read individually with their derived deadline and grace window.

**Scoring the holder and pricing the deadline.** Raising a finding runs a five-step evaluation whose **order is pinned**, because the holder's score feeds the new finding's deadline while the new finding itself feeds the holder's score. Reading the steps out of order gives a different answer.

*Step one* scores the holder as it stood **before** the new finding is inserted. A finding counts as open when its `status` is any of `OPEN`, `CAP_SUBMITTED`, `CAP_REJECTED`, `OVERDUE` or `ESCALATED`; findings that are `CLOSED` do not count. Over all findings reachable through audits of this holder that pass that open filter, the server counts how many are graded `CRITICAL`, how many `MAJOR` and how many `MINOR`. The pre-insert score is 100, reduced by 40 points for each open critical finding, 15 points for each open major finding and 5 points for each open minor finding, with the result floored at 0 so it never goes negative. Findings graded `OBSERVATION` carry a weight of zero and never move the score at all.

*Step two* derives the corrective action allowance from that pre-insert score. A score of at least 90 earns a deadline multiplier of 1.5 and an escalation grace of 14 days. A score of at least 70 but below 90 earns a multiplier of 1.0 and a grace of 7 days. Anything below 70 earns a multiplier of 0.5 and no grace at all. A well-behaved producer gets half again as long to fix a problem, and two weeks of slack before sanction; a producer already in trouble gets half the time and no slack.

*Step three* freezes those figures onto the new finding. Its `compliance_score_at_raise` records the pre-insert score, its `escalation_grace_days` records the grace days just derived, and its `deadline_at` is the clock instant plus a whole number of days computed as the grade's base allowance multiplied by the multiplier and then truncated downward to a whole number of days. The base allowances are one week — 7 days — for a `CRITICAL` finding, 30 days for a `MAJOR`, 90 days for a `MINOR` and 180 days for an `OBSERVATION`. So a critical finding against a holder scoring 95 is due in 10 days (7 multiplied by 1.5 is 10.5, truncated to 10), while the same finding against a holder scoring 60 is due in 3 days (7 multiplied by 0.5 is 3.5, truncated to 3).

*Step four* — and only now — inserts the finding and recomputes the holder's aggregates **including** it. The holder's `open_critical_count`, `open_major_count` and `open_minor_count` are recounted under the same open filter, and the holder's `compliance_score` is recomputed from those post-insert counts with the same weights and the same floor at 0: 100 less 40 per open critical, 15 per open major and 5 per open minor.

*Step five* re-derives the audit cadence from the **post-insert** score. A `compliance_score` of at least 90 earns a 24-month interval, at least 70 but below 90 earns 12 months, and below 70 earns 6 months. That interval is added, calendar-wise, to an anchor date: the latest `window_end` among this holder's audits whose `status` is `CLOSED`, with a tie on `window_end` broken by taking the earlier `created_at`. The result is stored as the holder's `next_audit_due_at`. When the holder has no `CLOSED` audit at all, the anchor does not exist and `next_audit_due_at` is null. Adding months calendar-wise means the day of month is preserved where possible and clamped to the last day of the target month where it is not.

Steps four and five run again — without steps one through three, which belong to the act of raising — whenever a finding is closed by an accepted corrective action plan, and whenever an audit is closed. Step four alone runs again inside the compliance sweep every time a finding changes status.

**Corrective action plans.** A `ProducerRepresentative` acting as the audited holder submits a corrective action plan by supplying a `narrative`. A plan may be submitted against a finding sitting at `OPEN`, advancing `OPEN → CAP_SUBMITTED`, or against one sitting at `CAP_REJECTED`, advancing `CAP_REJECTED → CAP_SUBMITTED` and closing the resubmission loop `CAP_SUBMITTED → CAP_REJECTED → CAP_SUBMITTED`. Both branches require that the acting `X-User-ID` be the `holder_id` of the finding's audit, and both require that the clock not yet have passed the finding's `deadline_at` — the comparison is inclusive, so a submission made at exactly the deadline instant is accepted. A submission after the deadline, or against a finding whose `status` is already `OVERDUE` or `ESCALATED`, is refused with `ERR_CAP_DEADLINE_PASSED`. On success `cap_round` increments by 1, `cap_narrative` is replaced with the supplied narrative, `cap_submitted_at` is stamped from the clock, the parent audit's `open_nonconformity_count` is recomputed, and the finding's `version` increments by 1.

**Reviewing the plan.** Either an `Auditor` or a `CertificationOfficer` reviews a submitted plan, supplying a `decision` of `ACCEPT` or `REJECT` and an optional `review_note`. Whoever reviews, their `X-User-ID` must differ from the finding's `raised_by_user_id` — the auditor who wrote the finding may never bless the fix — and a violation is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. A decision of `REJECT` advances `CAP_SUBMITTED → CAP_REJECTED`, stamps `cap_reviewed_at` from the clock and increments the finding's `version`; the producer may resubmit while the deadline still allows it. A decision of `ACCEPT` advances `CAP_SUBMITTED → CLOSED`, a terminal state: `closed_at` and `cap_reviewed_at` are both stamped from the clock, the parent audit's `nonconformity_count` and `open_nonconformity_count` are recomputed and its `version` increments, the holder's `compliance_score`, its three open-grade counters and its `next_audit_due_at` are recomputed and the holder's `version` increments, and the finding's `version` increments by 1.

**Closing the audit.** A `CertificationOfficer` closes an audit. From `IN_PROGRESS` — an audit that raised no finding at all — the transition `IN_PROGRESS → CLOSED` carries no completeness precondition. From `REPORT_PENDING` the transition `REPORT_PENDING → CLOSED` requires that the audit's `open_nonconformity_count` be 0, meaning every finding it raised has reached `CLOSED`; any finding still at `OPEN`, `CAP_SUBMITTED`, `CAP_REJECTED`, `OVERDUE` or `ESCALATED` blocks closure with `ERR_AUDIT_HAS_OPEN_NONCONFORMITIES`. In both branches `closed_at` is stamped from the clock, the holder's `compliance_score` and `next_audit_due_at` are recomputed by steps four and five above — closing the audit is what finally supplies an anchor for the cadence — the holder's `version` increments, and the audit's `version` increments by 1.

### 3.7. The Compliance Sweep

Deadlines do not enforce themselves in a deterministic system. Because this service never reads a wall clock, the passage of time only becomes visible when a `CertificationOfficer` runs the **compliance sweep**, a single idempotent operation that walks the whole compliance backlog against the current clock and applies every consequence that has become due. It takes no request body and reports what it did: the `swept_at` instant it ran at, an `overdue_count`, an `escalated_count`, a `suspended_holder_count`, a `suspended_node_count`, a `disputed_transaction_count` and a `decertified_holder_count`.

The sweep executes six steps in a fixed order, and the ordering matters because each step feeds the next.

*Step one — overdue.* Every finding whose `status` is `OPEN`, `CAP_SUBMITTED` or `CAP_REJECTED` and whose `deadline_at` falls strictly before the clock moves to `OVERDUE`. Those three source states each have their own transition — `OPEN → OVERDUE`, `CAP_SUBMITTED → OVERDUE` and `CAP_REJECTED → OVERDUE` — and all three behave identically: the only side effect is that the finding's `version` increments by 1. Findings are processed in ascending `deadline_at` order, with ties broken by ascending `created_at`.

*Step two — escalation.* Every finding sitting at `OVERDUE` whose `deadline_at` plus its pinned `escalation_grace_days` has been reached by the clock — that is, where the clock is at or after that grace-adjusted instant — moves `OVERDUE → ESCALATED`, a terminal state. This applies both to findings that step one has just moved to `OVERDUE` in this same sweep and to findings that were already `OVERDUE` from an earlier one. A finding raised against a low-scoring holder, which carries zero grace days, therefore becomes overdue and escalates within a single sweep; a finding raised against a holder scoring 90 or above carries fourteen grace days and needs a sweep run at least two weeks past its deadline to escalate.

*Step three — sanction.* Escalation is the second hop of the cascade, caused by the escalation rather than by the sweep request itself. When an escalated finding's `grade` is either `CRITICAL` or `MAJOR`, the owning holder of that finding's audit moves `CERTIFIED → SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` set to `NONCONFORMITY_ESCALATION`. A `MINOR` or `OBSERVATION` finding escalates without suspending anybody. In either case the holder's `open_critical_count`, `open_major_count`, `open_minor_count` and `compliance_score` are recomputed, and each mutated row's `version` increments by 1.

*Steps four and five — the suspension cascade.* A holder suspended in step three runs exactly the cascade of §3.2: every one of its nodes at `ACTIVE` moves to `SUSPENDED` in ascending `created_at` order, and every trade transaction out of a just-suspended node that is still `UNVERIFIED` on the verification dimension and `PENDING` on the settlement dimension moves its verification `UNVERIFIED → DISPUTED` with its reservation released from the seller node's `reserved_output_kg`.

*Step six — expired sell-down windows.* Finally, every holder sitting at `TRANSITIONING` whose `transition_deadline_at` has been reached by the clock — at or before it — moves `TRANSITIONING → DECERTIFIED`, running the complete decertification cascade of §3.2: nodes retired, pending transactions rejected with their reservations released, scheduled audits cancelled, committees dissolved and the label licence terminated.

Every entity the sweep mutates, at any depth, has its `version` incremented by 1 and its `updated_at` refreshed.

### 3.8. Label Licensing and On-Pack Mark Usage

The FAIRTRADE mark is a licensed asset, and printing it on a package requires two separate things: an active licence for which the fee has been paid, and an upstream chain that actually supports the claim being made.

**Issuing the licence.** A `CertificationOfficer` issues or renews a holder's mark licence for a calendar year by supplying the `license_year` the licence covers and the `licensed_volume_kg` declared for that year. The declared volume is the sole input to the fee — the holder's `annual_certified_volume_kg`, however large, plays no part in pricing the licence. Issuing sets `label_license_status` to `ACTIVE`, stores the `license_year` and `licensed_volume_kg`, resets `license_fee_paid_cents` to 0, and prices the licence in full; a re-issue for a new licence year recomputes everything from scratch. The `label_license_status` vocabulary is `NONE`, `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED` and `TERMINATED`, the last being terminal and reached only by decertification.

**Pricing the licence.** The fee is a marginal-rate tiered schedule on the declared volume, plus a flat base fee, plus an administrative surcharge, and **the rounding order is pinned**. First the declared volume is split across three tiers. The first-tier volume is the declared volume or 100,000 kilograms, whichever is smaller. The second-tier volume is the declared volume capped at 500,000 kilograms, less 100,000 kilograms, floored at zero — so it is the portion of the volume between 100,000 and 500,000 kilograms. The third-tier volume is the declared volume less 500,000 kilograms, floored at zero.

Each tier is then priced at its own marginal rate and **rounded to a whole cent before the tiers are added together**: the first-tier volume at 1.20 cents per kilogram, the second-tier volume at 0.80 cents per kilogram, and the third-tier volume at 0.45 cents per kilogram, each product rounded half up to a whole cent on its own. The three rounded figures are summed into the volume-based fee. Adding a flat base fee of 250,000 cents — the 2,500-unit annual licence charge every licensee pays regardless of volume — gives the subtotal. The administrative surcharge is 3.5 percent of that subtotal, rounded half up to a whole cent. `license_fee_cents` is the subtotal plus the surcharge.

Alongside the fee, `license_tier` labels the licence, and both breakpoints are inclusive: a declared volume at or below 100,000 kilograms is `TIER_1`, a volume above that but at or below 500,000 kilograms is `TIER_2`, and anything above 500,000 kilograms is `TIER_3`.

**Paying the fee.** A `BrandLicensee` records a payment against its own holder's current licence year by supplying an `amount_cents`. The payment raises `license_fee_paid_cents` by that amount and re-derives `license_fee_settled`, which is true when and only when `license_fee_paid_cents` has reached at least `license_fee_cents` **and** `license_fee_cents` is strictly greater than 0 — a holder with no priced licence is never treated as settled. The holder's `version` increments by 1.

**Requesting mark usage.** A `BrandLicensee` files a mark usage request against its own brand holder, naming the `brand_holder_id`, the retail `sku_code` the mark would appear on (non-empty, at most 40 characters), the `artwork_ref` identifying the submitted artwork proof (non-empty, at most 80 characters), the `claim_type` requested, and the `requested_volume_kg` the SKU is expected to carry annually (strictly greater than 0, three decimals). The `claim_type` is either `FT_CERTIFIED_100`, the strongest on-pack claim, or `FT_INGREDIENT_MASS_BALANCE`. Filing requires the brand holder's `label_license_status` to be `ACTIVE`; otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_LABEL_LICENSE_INACTIVE`. The request opens at `status` `SUBMITTED` with `decided_by_user_id`, `decision_reason` and `decided_at` all null. Neither fee settlement nor claim/chain compatibility is enforced at filing time — both are deferred to the decision. Requests may be listed per brand holder with optional `status` and `claim_type` filters, and read individually.

**Deciding.** A `CertificationOfficer` decides the request, supplying a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `REJECT` and an optional `reason`. The two branches are deliberately asymmetric. `SUBMITTED → APPROVED` requires three things beyond the state itself: the brand holder's `label_license_status` is `ACTIVE`, refused otherwise with `ERR_LABEL_LICENSE_INACTIVE`; the brand holder's `license_fee_settled` is true, refused otherwise with `ERR_LICENSE_FEE_UNPAID` (HTTP 402); and, when and only when the `claim_type` is `FT_CERTIFIED_100`, the chain traversal of §3.1 over that brand holder must return a `chain_mode` of `ALL_PHYSICAL`. A `chain_mode` of `CONTAINS_MASS_BALANCE` refuses the approval with `ERR_TRACEABILITY_MODE_MISMATCH`, and so does a `chain_mode` of `NO_CHAIN` — a brand with no mapped brand node cannot make a hundred-percent-certified claim by default. A `claim_type` of `FT_INGREDIENT_MASS_BALANCE` imposes no traceability requirement. `SUBMITTED → REJECTED`, by contrast, skips every one of those licence and traceability preconditions: a request may always be turned down. Both branches stamp `decided_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`, `decided_at` from the clock and `decision_reason` from the supplied reason, and increment the request's `version` by 1.

There is a third way out of `SUBMITTED`, and it is not a client action: when the brand holder is suspended, every request still sitting at `SUBMITTED` moves `SUBMITTED → WITHDRAWN` with `decision_reason` set to `HOLDER_SUSPENDED` and `decided_at` stamped from the clock. `APPROVED`, `REJECTED` and `WITHDRAWN` are all terminal; deciding an already-decided request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.

### 3.9. Volume Reporting and Counterparty Reconciliation

The last line of defence against certified-volume laundering is that both sides of a trading relationship must declare the same numbers. A **volume report** is one holder's periodic declaration of how much certified volume moved between itself and one named counterparty, and reconciliation checks that declaration against what this service's own transaction record says.

**Filing.** A `ProducerRepresentative` or a `BrandLicensee` files a report for its own holder, naming its own `holder_id`, the `counterparty_holder_id` whose books the report will be reconciled against, the reporting period as `period_start` inclusive through `period_end` exclusive in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, and the `declared_volume_kg` it claims moved in that period (at least 0, three decimals). Periods may not overlap for the same filer-and-counterparty pair: over that pair's existing reports whose `reconciliation_status` is any of `SUBMITTED`, `MATCHED` or `VARIANCE_FLAGGED`, the request is refused with `ERR_REPORT_PERIOD_OVERLAP` when the new `period_start` falls strictly before an existing `period_end` and that existing `period_start` falls strictly before the new `period_end`; and `period_end` must in any case fall strictly after `period_start`. A filed report opens at `reconciliation_status` `SUBMITTED`, with `counterparty_volume_kg` 0, `variance_pct` 0.00, `basis` null and `reconciled_at` null. Reports may be listed per holder — with an optional `role_in_report` filter selecting whether the holder appears as the filer or as the counterparty, and an optional `reconciliation_status` filter — and read individually.

**Reconciling.** A `CertificationOfficer` reconciles the report, pinning the accounting `basis` in the request as either `SELLER_BASIS` or `BUYER_BASIS`. The candidate set is the same for both bases: trade transactions whose seller node belongs to the report's `holder_id`, whose buyer node belongs to the report's `counterparty_holder_id`, and whose `contract_date` falls inside the half-open period — at or after `period_start` and strictly before `period_end`. The aggregation is a pure sum, so it is order-insensitive and needs no tie-break.

What differs is which candidates count. Under `SELLER_BASIS` the seller's books recognise volume only once it is both verified and paid, so only candidates whose `verification_status` is `VERIFIED` **and** whose `settlement_status` is `SETTLED` are counted; disputed or unpaid shipments shrink the counterparty total and push the variance positive. Under `BUYER_BASIS` the buyer's books recognise volume on receipt, disputes and unpaid invoices included, and exclude only what the certification body has finally rejected or reversed: candidates whose `verification_status` is either `VERIFIED` or `DISPUTED` **and** whose `settlement_status` is either `PENDING` or `SETTLED` are counted. The same underlying rows therefore produce a larger counterparty total under the buyer basis, a different and typically negative variance, and can flip the outcome between a match, a flag and outright refusal.

`counterparty_volume_kg` is the sum of `volume_kg` over the counted rows, rounded half up to 3 decimals. `variance_pct` is the report's `declared_volume_kg` minus that `counterparty_volume_kg`, divided by `counterparty_volume_kg`, multiplied by 100, and rounded half up to 2 decimal places as a signed figure — with rounding applied to the absolute value and the sign reapplied. When `counterparty_volume_kg` is 0 the division is undefined and `variance_pct` is 0.00 by definition.

The outcome is a three-way decision on the magnitude of that variance, ignoring its sign. When the absolute variance is strictly greater than 10.00 percent the discrepancy is too large to reconcile at all: the whole request is refused with `ERR_VOLUME_VARIANCE_EXCEEDED`, **no** field is written and the report stays exactly as it was at `SUBMITTED`. When the absolute variance is at most 2.00 percent the books agree within tolerance and `reconciliation_status` becomes `MATCHED`. Otherwise — an absolute variance above 2.00 but not above 10.00 percent — `reconciliation_status` becomes `VARIANCE_FLAGGED`. In the two branches that write, `basis`, `counterparty_volume_kg`, `variance_pct` and `reconciliation_status` are all stored, `reconciled_at` is stamped from the clock, and the report's `version` increments by 1.

A flag is not merely a note. When reconciliation lands on `VARIANCE_FLAGGED` and the filing holder's `certificate_status` is `CERTIFIED`, that holder is moved `CERTIFIED → SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` set to `VOLUME_VARIANCE`, and the full suspension cascade of §3.2 runs behind it — nodes to `SUSPENDED`, their unverified pending trades to `DISPUTED` with reservations released, the licence to `SUSPENDED` and pending mark usage requests to `WITHDRAWN`.
