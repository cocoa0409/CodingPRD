# App Store Submission Compliance API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A mobile application publisher does not simply "upload a build". Between the moment an engineering team declares a binary ready and the moment a single end user receives it, the build must be mapped against a living corpus of platform guidelines, self-reviewed by the publisher's own compliance function, described in localized store metadata, rated for age suitability from a structured questionnaire, disclosed in a privacy label that must match what the binary actually does at runtime, signed with key material whose custody and validity the platform trusts, reviewed by a platform operator, and finally released to a progressively widening slice of the audience with a rollback lever always within reach. Every one of those steps is a place where a release can — and should — stop.

This document specifies the API that governs that pipeline for both sides of the relationship: the publisher organisation that wants a build shipped, and the platform organisation that decides whether it may be. The design is deliberately adversarial in the compliance sense. Publisher duties are split three ways so that no single operator can author a build, attest to its compliance and push it live; platform duties are split so that the operator who rejected a build can never be the operator who hears the appeal against that rejection. Derived state — a compliance posture, a weighted compliance index, two different standing scores, two different privacy-disclosure accuracy scores — is recomputed by the server from stored rows rather than supplied by clients, so that no caller can talk its way past a gate by asserting a number.

Two structural decisions shape everything that follows. First, all resources live in flat root-level collections and are linked by explicit identifier fields in request bodies rather than by URL nesting; the single exception is a nested read that exists purely so a cascade-created child is reachable from its parent. Second, the system has no wall clock: time is a logical value that a test-driving endpoint sets explicitly, and every expiry, dwell period, rolling window and deadline in this document is evaluated against that logical instant. Before that endpoint is ever called, the logical clock reads `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the individual operator making the call, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that operator is acting in. There are exactly seven roles, spelled exactly as follows: `PublisherAdmin`, `ReleaseManager`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `PlatformReviewer`, `PlatformAppealBoard`, `PlatformPolicyAdmin` and `Auditor`. The first three are publisher-side roles; the remaining four are platform-side roles.

The three publisher-side roles are **account-backed**: when `X-User-Role` is `PublisherAdmin`, `ReleaseManager` or `ComplianceOfficer`, the request must additionally carry a third header, `X-Publisher-Account-Id`, whose value is the `id` of the `PublisherAccount` the operator is acting for. That header is the tenancy subject: every entity reached through a path identifier or through an entity-reference field in the body must resolve transitively to that account, and `X-User-ID` for these roles is the operator's own identifier, recorded on artifacts the operator produces (for example as the attester of a self-review or the filer of an appeal) and compared against other recorded operator identifiers by the segregation-of-duties rules. The four platform-side roles — `PlatformReviewer`, `PlatformAppealBoard`, `PlatformPolicyAdmin` and `Auditor` — carry an **opaque** identity: their `X-User-ID` is an operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity, they do not send `X-Publisher-Account-Id`, and the server never compares their `X-User-ID` to any entity's `id`. The server does record it — as the claiming reviewer of a submission, the recorder of a rejection reason, or the decider of an appeal or an expedite request — and uses those recorded values for segregation-of-duties comparisons, but it is never resolved to a row.

Header enforcement is shape-only and happens before anything else. If `X-User-Role` is absent or is not one of the seven names above, if `X-User-ID` is absent or empty, or if an account-backed role omits `X-Publisher-Account-Id` or sends it empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_OR_INVALID_USER_CONTEXT_HEADER` and HTTP 400 — never a 403, even when the role that would have been sent is not permitted on the endpoint in question. Once the headers are well-formed, the role is checked against the endpoint's authorised role list in §4; a role that does not appear there is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` and HTTP 403. That permission check deliberately precedes resource lookup: a request from an unauthorised role that also names a non-existent identifier returns 403 `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`, never 404, because resource existence must not leak to callers who have no business asking.

Tenancy is checked next, and only for the three account-backed roles. Ownership resolves through the obvious chains: an `AppRecord` and a `SigningCredential` carry `publisher_account_id` directly; a `Submission`, a `FeatureMapping`, a `RolloutPlan`, a `CommerceItem` and an `ImpactAssessment` resolve through their `AppRecord`; a `MetadataLocale`, a `BehaviorFinding`, a `RejectionReason` and an `AppealRequest` resolve through their `Submission` and then that submission's `AppRecord`; an `ExpediteRequest` carries `publisher_account_id` of its own. An identifier that resolves to a row belonging to a different account is rejected with `ERR_CROSS_ACCOUNT_ACCESS_DENIED` and HTTP 403 — and note the ordering: tenancy precedes existence, so a resolvable identifier owned by somebody else is a 403, while an identifier that resolves to nothing at all is a 404. The four platform roles skip the tenancy rule entirely and address every account. List endpoints behave differently rather than erroring: for the three account-backed roles a list result is silently narrowed to the account named in `X-Publisher-Account-Id`. Four endpoints are outside tenancy scope altogether because they own no publisher-side data: setting the logical clock, drafting a guideline, publishing a guideline, and creating or closing a reviewer capacity window.

The duty split is a hard part of the authorisation model, not a convention. A `PublisherAdmin` owns the account, its signing material and its commerce catalogue, and may neither create a build submission, nor self-review a guideline mapping, nor drive a release. A `ReleaseManager` owns build creation, store metadata and the staged rollout, and may neither self-review mappings, nor answer rejection reasons, nor file appeals. A `ComplianceOfficer` owns guideline mapping, pre-submission self-review, the privacy declaration, rejection responses, appeals and impact remediation, and may neither create builds, nor queue them for review, nor start a rollout. On the platform side, a `PlatformReviewer` claims and decides builds and reviews commerce items; a `PlatformAppealBoard` operator hears appeals and rules on expedited-review quota; a `PlatformPolicyAdmin` owns the guideline corpus, the scanner findings, reviewer capacity, account enforcement and the platform-side emergency controls; and an `Auditor` holds read-only oversight across every account with no mutating permission whatsoever, including the remediation endpoints. Every one of the seven roles, without exception, may set the logical clock.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, may not be smaller than 1 and may not be larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` outside the inclusive range 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is a schema violation and is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION` and HTTP 422.

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit` and `offset` echoing the effective values used, `total_count` giving the number of rows matching the filter before pagination was applied, and `has_more` indicating whether any row remains beyond the current page. All other query parameters documented for a list endpoint are exact-match filters and may be combined freely with each other and with pagination.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Revision Counters

Every one of the fifteen resource types in this system carries the same four server-managed properties. `id` is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix; clients never supply it. `created_at` is the ISO 8601 UTC instant of creation, rendered with three fractional digits, and never changes. `updated_at` is the ISO 8601 UTC instant of the most recent mutation, rendered the same way, and is refreshed on every mutation. `version` is an integer that begins at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row — including mutations a caller did not directly request, such as a row touched by a cascade or by the logical clock's sweep. No entity in this system omits any of the four.

Because `version` is what optimistic concurrency is built on (§2.6), it is worth stating plainly what counts as a mutation. A cascade that moves a child's state bumps that child's `version`. A cascade that recomputes a parent's derived field bumps the parent's `version`. Where a transition explicitly states that it increments the version of more than one entity, each named entity's counter advances by exactly 1 — the counters are per-row, never shared. Read-only endpoints, including the two aggregate reads that recompute derived values on the fly without persisting them, never advance any counter.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

There is no system clock. Every temporal comparison in this specification — credential validity, membership expiry, pre-review freshness, appeal windows, phase dwell, rollout schedules, capacity-window occupancy, remediation deadlines and rolling 365-day windows — is evaluated against a single stored logical instant. Until the clock is explicitly set, it reads `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never advances on its own.

Setting the clock is available to all seven roles and takes a single `current_utc` instant. The clock is monotonic: a `current_utc` earlier than the currently stored value is rejected with `ERR_CLOCK_NOT_MONOTONIC` and HTTP 409. When the value is accepted, the server runs a sweep in this pinned order, and the order matters because each step feeds the next: (1) signing credentials move from `ACTIVE` to `EXPIRING_SOON` and onward to `EXPIRED`; (2) publisher accounts whose paid-through instant has passed move from `ACTIVE` to `EXPIRED`; (3) impact assessments that are past their remediation deadline move from `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED` to `OVERDUE`; (4) the compliance posture of every app record is recomputed; (5) rollout plans in `PHASE_ACTIVE` belonging to apps that have just become frozen are paused; (6) the standing-score and expedite-credit rolling windows are re-evaluated. Every entity the sweep touches has its `version` incremented by 1.

The response to a clock advance reports what the sweep did: the echoed `current_utc`, plus `credentials_warned_count` (credentials that entered `EXPIRING_SOON`), `credentials_expired_count`, `accounts_expired_count`, `assessments_overdue_count`, `postures_frozen_count` and `rollouts_paused_count`.

### 2.6. Revision Guarding on Parent-Owned Writes

Four write operations create or extend something whose safety depends on the caller having an up-to-date view of a *parent* row. Each of them therefore requires a non-optional expected-version field in its request body, carrying the `version` value the client last observed on that parent.

Creating a submission requires `expected_app_record_version` to equal the current `version` of the `AppRecord` named by `app_record_id`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_APP_RECORD_VERSION` and HTTP 412. The guard exists because a new submission legitimately supersedes the app's other `DRAFT` submissions by moving them to `DEVELOPER_WITHDRAWN`; a stale expected value means the client's picture of the app's draft set, its posture or its live build is out of date, and the write would destroy work the client never saw.

Creating a rollout plan likewise requires `expected_app_record_version` to equal the target app record's current `version`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_APP_RECORD_VERSION` and HTTP 412. A newly created plan legitimately cancels the app's `SCHEDULED` plans and re-targets the audience share fields, so planning a release from an outdated view of `distribution_state`, `released_share_pct` or the rollback cooldown baseline is refused outright.

Registering a signing credential requires `expected_publisher_account_version` to equal the current `version` of the `PublisherAccount` named by `publisher_account_id`; a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PUBLISHER_ACCOUNT_VERSION` and HTTP 412. Activating a new distribution certificate later supersedes the account's existing active certificate for the same platform, so key material may only be enrolled against the exact account revision the client observed — otherwise the client cannot know which certificate it is about to displace.

Renewing program membership also requires `expected_publisher_account_version` to match the account's current `version`, with the same `ERR_STALE_PUBLISHER_ACCOUNT_VERSION` and HTTP 412 on mismatch. Renewal extends membership forward from the account's current value and is a legitimately repeatable operation, so a renewal computed against a stale account revision would extend the wrong baseline.

No other endpoint in this system accepts an expected-version field, and supplying one where it is not defined is an unknown field and therefore a schema violation. The revision guard is also positioned deliberately in the rejection ladder of §2.7: it is evaluated *before* the target entity's own state is examined, so when the supplied expected version is stale **and** the target is in a state that forbids the operation, the response is the 412 revision error, never the 409 state error.

### 2.7. The Rejection Ladder: Which Failure Wins

When a request violates several rules at once, exactly one of them determines the response. The order is fixed and no later rule may ever mask an earlier one. In ascending order of precedence the ladder is: (1) header shape; (2) role permission; (3) tenancy; (4) existence; (5) request schema; (6) parent revision guard; (7) the target entity's own state; (8) the app's compliance posture; (9) the account's program status; (10) segregation of duties; then the five business bands in this order — (11) safety, (12) referential integrity, (13) business constraints, (14) temporal windows, (15) fiscal quotas. Within a single rung, path identifiers are resolved left to right, and then body fields are examined in the order in which they are declared for that endpoint.

The first five rungs are mechanical. Header shape yields `ERR_MISSING_OR_INVALID_USER_CONTEXT_HEADER` (400); role permission yields `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403); tenancy yields `ERR_CROSS_ACCOUNT_ACCESS_DENIED` (403). Existence yields a 404 whose code names the resource type that failed to resolve, drawn from a family of fifteen: `ERR_PUBLISHER_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SIGNING_CREDENTIAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APP_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_GUIDELINE_RULE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FEATURE_MAPPING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SUBMISSION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_METADATA_LOCALE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BEHAVIOR_FINDING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REJECTION_REASON_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPEAL_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EXPEDITE_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REVIEW_CAPACITY_WINDOW_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ROLLOUT_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COMMERCE_ITEM_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_IMPACT_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FOUND`. Because path identifiers precede body fields and body fields are examined in declaration order, a rollout-plan creation that names both an unknown app record and an unknown submission reports `ERR_APP_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`, since `app_record_id` is declared first. The `ERR_REJECTION_REASON_NOT_FOUND` code also covers any unresolvable element inside `cited_rejection_reason_ids` or `overturned_rejection_reason_ids`, and `ERR_EXPEDITE_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND` covers an unresolvable `expedite_request_id` supplied at queue entry.

Schema validation sits on the fifth rung and yields `ERR_VALIDATION` with HTTP 422. It covers missing required fields, wrong types, values outside a declared enumeration, string lengths outside their declared bounds, array cardinalities, numeric ranges, malformed ISO 8601 UTC instants, unknown fields, out-of-bounds pagination, and a handful of body-shape rules stated with their workflows in §3 — a non-zero price on an external payment link, an empty rejection-reason array on a reject decision, an overturned-reason list that is not a strict subset of the cited list, a provisioning profile enrolled without a linked certificate, or a validity end that is not after the validity start. Two shape rules deserve naming here because they are structured objects: the age-rating questionnaire must carry exactly the six intensity keys with values from 0 through 3 inclusive plus the five declared booleans, and every value inside a screenshot count map must be an integer of at least 0. Note the rung order: a malformed body sent to a non-existent submission returns 404, not 422.

Four precedence pairs are load-bearing and are called out explicitly because implementations habitually get them backwards. An unauthorised role naming a non-existent identifier is 403 `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`, not 404. A stale parent revision on an entity that is also in a forbidding state is 412, not 409. An app whose posture is frozen and whose submission also carries an unreconciled critical behaviour finding reports `ERR_APP_COMPLIANCE_FROZEN`, not `ERR_CRITICAL_FINDING_UNRESOLVED`. And an appeal that is both filed outside its window and filed by an account below the standing floor reports `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_EXPIRED`, never `ERR_STANDING_TOO_LOW`.

The seventh rung — the target entity's own state — deserves its own note. Each state machine has its own 409 code, and the response always uses the code of the entity named in the path: `ERR_INVALID_SUBMISSION_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_LISTING_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_DISTRIBUTION_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_CREDENTIAL_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_ROLLOUT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_GUIDELINE_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_MAPPING_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_IMPACT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_APPEAL_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_EXPEDITE_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_COMMERCE_ITEM_STATE` and `ERR_INVALID_FINDING_STATE`. A transition that is not declared for the entity's current state is refused even when a similar-looking transition exists elsewhere in the same machine — advancing a paused rollout is `ERR_INVALID_ROLLOUT_STATE` despite the fact that an active rollout may advance to itself. For the app record, which carries two independent state dimensions on one row, the code names the dimension the endpoint actually drives.

### 2.8. Compliance Posture: The Gate Over Every Child Write

Each `AppRecord` carries a derived `compliance_posture` with three possible values — `OPEN`, `RESTRICTED` and `FROZEN` — together with an ordered `posture_reason_codes` array explaining it. Both are recomputed by the server after every mutation of a feature mapping, an impact assessment, a behaviour finding or a signing credential, after any change to the owning account's `program_status`, after every submission decision, and after every clock advance. Neither is ever client-supplied. A newly created app record starts at `RESTRICTED`, because it has neither an active distribution certificate nor any mapping coverage yet.

The computation walks two tiers in a fixed order and the first tier with any match wins; `posture_reason_codes` then lists **every** matching code of that winning tier, in the order given below. The frozen tier has three codes: `ACCOUNT_NOT_IN_GOOD_STANDING` when the owning account's `program_status` is `SUSPENDED` or `TERMINATED`; `IMPACT_OVERDUE` when any impact assessment of this app is in status `OVERDUE`; and `CRITICAL_FINDING_OPEN` when any behaviour finding belonging to any submission of this app has severity `CRITICAL` and status `OPEN` or `DISPUTED`. If no frozen code matched, the restricted tier is evaluated, with four codes in this order: `MEMBERSHIP_EXPIRED` when the account's `program_status` is `EXPIRED`; `NO_ACTIVE_DISTRIBUTION_CERT` when the account owns no signing credential of type `DISTRIBUTION_CERT` for this app's platform whose status is `ACTIVE` or `EXPIRING_SOON`; `COMPLIANCE_INDEX_BELOW_FLOOR` when the app's `feature_compliance_index` is strictly below 90.00; and `OPEN_IMPACT_ASSESSMENT` when any impact assessment of this app is in status `OPEN`, `IN_ASSESSMENT` or `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED`. If neither tier matched, the posture is `OPEN` and `posture_reason_codes` is the empty array. The index comparison uses the two-decimal rounded index and is strict, so an index of exactly 90.00 does **not** restrict the app.

A frozen posture blocks sixteen operations: creating a submission, running a submission's pre-review check, queueing a submission for review, attaching a metadata locale, recording an age rating, recording a privacy declaration, driving the listing dimension, recording a feature-mapping self-review outcome, creating a rollout plan, starting a rollout, advancing a rollout phase, pausing or resuming a rollout, creating a commerce item, submitting a commerce item for commerce review, and creating an expedite request. In all of those the code is `ERR_APP_COMPLIANCE_FROZEN` with HTTP 409. A restricted posture is narrower: it blocks only the release-advancing subset — queueing a submission, creating a rollout plan, starting a rollout, advancing a rollout phase, resuming a paused rollout, submitting a commerce item for commerce review, and creating an expedite request — with the code `ERR_APP_COMPLIANCE_RESTRICTED` and HTTP 409. Resuming is release-advancing and therefore restricted-blocked; pausing is not.

Eleven operations are deliberately **exempt** from the posture rung altogether, because otherwise a frozen app could never be unfrozen: working an impact assessment, triaging a behaviour finding, returning a submission to draft, withdrawing a submission, rolling a release back, waiving a feature mapping, registering a signing credential, activating one, revoking one, reporting one compromised, and renewing program membership. These are the remediation levers, and they stay available at every posture.

### 2.9. Program Standing of the Publisher Account

Independently of the app-level posture, the owning `PublisherAccount` must be in good standing for any create or release-advancing operation. The account's `program_status` is one of `ACTIVE`, `EXPIRED`, `SUSPENDED` or `TERMINATED`. When it is `EXPIRED`, the affected operations are rejected with `ERR_PROGRAM_MEMBERSHIP_EXPIRED` and HTTP 403; when it is `SUSPENDED` or `TERMINATED`, they are rejected with `ERR_ACCOUNT_SUSPENDED` and HTTP 403. Fourteen operations carry this gate: creating an app record, driving the listing dimension, activating a signing credential, creating a submission, running a pre-review check, queueing a submission, creating a feature mapping, creating a rollout plan, starting a rollout, advancing a rollout phase, creating an expedite request, filing an appeal, creating a commerce item, and submitting a commerce item for commerce review.

An account that is expired, suspended or terminated may still read everything it could read before, may still triage and remediate, and may still renew its membership — that is how it recovers. This rung sits *after* the app-posture rung, so a frozen app owned by a suspended account reports the app-level cause first.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties

Six operations judge an artifact somebody else produced, and in each of them the acting `X-User-ID` must be distinct from — or, in one case, identical to — an operator identifier already recorded on the artifact. A violation is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES_VIOLATION` with HTTP 409, and it is evaluated after the posture and program-status rungs but before any business rule.

Concretely: recording a review decision on a submission requires that the acting `X-User-ID` **equals** that submission's `reviewed_by_user_id` — only the reviewer who claimed the build may decide it — and simultaneously that it **differs** from that submission's `attested_by_user_id`, so the publisher-side operator who attested the pre-review can never also decide the build. Taking an appeal up for board review, and deciding it, both require that the acting `X-User-ID` differs from the `reviewed_by_user_id` of the submission under appeal: the reviewer who rejected a build may neither review nor judge the appeal against that rejection. Deciding an expedite request requires the same distinction from the cited submission's `reviewed_by_user_id`. Waiving a feature mapping requires that the acting `X-User-ID` differs from that mapping's `attested_by_user_id`, so whoever attested a self-review may not waive that same mapping. Reviewing a commerce item requires that the acting `X-User-ID` differs from the `attested_by_user_id` of any commerce-category feature mapping of the same app record.

### 2.11. Precision, Rounding Order and Conserved Quantities

Money is handled exclusively in integer cents; no monetary value in this system is ever a decimal. Percentages, indices and scores are decimals with a fixed scale: the weighted compliance index, both standing scores, an impact severity, an overturn ratio, a priority score, an applied commission rate and both audience share percentages all carry 2 decimal places. The two privacy-disclosure accuracy scores are the exception: they carry 4 decimal places and live in the closed interval from 0.0000 to 1.0000. Every rounding operation in this document, without exception, rounds half up.

Where a formula involves both summation and rounding, the **order** of the two is pinned and is part of the contract, because the two orders produce different numbers. The weighted compliance index and both disclosure accuracy scores accumulate their terms unrounded and round exactly once at the very end. Commission settlement does the opposite: it rounds each line's commission to whole cents first and only then sums the rounded lines. Rounding each per-mapping contribution before summing an index is explicitly not the rule; summing unrounded per-line commissions and rounding the total is explicitly not the rule either.

Three quantities in this system are conserved, and the invariant must hold at every instant an observer could read them. A rollout plan's `released_share_pct` and `held_back_share_pct` always sum to exactly 100.00, on the plan and on the mirrored app-record fields alike, including while the plan is paused. A capacity window's `slots_reserved`, `slots_consumed` and `slots_available` always sum to exactly its `capacity_slots`. And an account's `expedite_credits_remaining` plus the credits it has used inside the rolling window always sum to exactly 2. Each of those three has a compensating action — a rollback undoing an advance, a withdrawal undoing a queue reservation, a refund undoing a granted credit — and after a compensating action the conserved quantity must return to precisely the integer or decimal it held before, with no drift, even when the logical clock moved in between.

### 2.12. Resource Addressing and the One Nested Read

All fifteen collections are addressed at the root: publisher accounts, signing credentials, app records, guideline rules, feature mappings, submissions, metadata locales, behaviour findings, rejection reasons, appeal requests, expedite requests, review capacity windows, rollout plans, commerce items and impact assessments. Parentage is expressed by identifier fields inside request bodies, never by URL nesting, which is why the revision guards of §2.6 are attached to body fields rather than to path segments.

One read is nested, because a metadata locale is reachable in no other way once it has been created as part of a build's store page: a build's locales may be listed under that build. Where a path carries more than one segment, its identifiers are validated left to right, so an unresolvable parent is reported before anything about the child is considered. Three further reads are aggregate rather than row-shaped — a publisher's standing summary, an app's compliance summary, and an app's commerce settlement for a named period — and all three recompute their derived values from current rows at read time without persisting anything and without advancing any `version`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Enrolling a Publisher, Keeping Membership Current, and Enforcement

A `PlatformPolicyAdmin` enrols a publisher into the developer program by supplying the registered `legal_name` (between 1 and 120 characters, and unique across all accounts), an operational `contact_email` (which must contain an `@` character), the `membership_paid_through_at` instant through which membership has been paid, and optionally the `commerce_program_tier`. The tier is either `SMALL_BUSINESS` or `STANDARD` and defaults to `STANDARD`; it is what selects the base commission rate later. The new account's `program_status` is set to `ACTIVE` when `membership_paid_through_at` is later than the current logical instant, and to `EXPIRED` otherwise. A brand-new account starts with a perfect reputation: both `standing_score_rolling_365` and `standing_score_lifetime` are 100.00, and `expedite_credits_remaining` is 2. Its `enforcement_reason_code` is null, its `rejection_strike_count` is 0, and its `settlement_finalized_periods` array is empty.

Membership decays with the clock rather than with activity. When the logical clock passes an account's `membership_paid_through_at`, the sweep moves `program_status` from `ACTIVE` to `EXPIRED`, which immediately shows up as the restricted-tier reason `MEMBERSHIP_EXPIRED` on every app the account owns. The only way back is renewal, which a `PublisherAdmin` performs by supplying `expected_publisher_account_version` together with a `paid_through_at` instant. That instant must be later than the current logical instant, or the request is a schema violation. On success `membership_paid_through_at` becomes the supplied `paid_through_at`, `program_status` returns from `EXPIRED` to `ACTIVE`, the compliance posture of every app record of the account is recomputed, and the account's `version` advances by one. Renewal is one of the eleven posture-exempt operations, so a frozen app never traps its own account in expiry.

Enforcement is the platform's own lever over an account and is available only to a `PlatformPolicyAdmin`, who supplies an `action` and a `reason_code`. An `action` of `SUSPEND` drives `program_status` to `SUSPENDED`; `TERMINATE` drives it to `TERMINATED`; and `REINSTATE` returns it to `ACTIVE`, or to `EXPIRED` when the account's `membership_paid_through_at` is already behind the logical clock. The supplied `reason_code` is stored as `enforcement_reason_code`, and is cleared to null on a reinstatement — that field is null whenever `program_status` is `ACTIVE` or `EXPIRED`. Every enforcement action recomputes the posture of every app the account owns, and a terminated account leaves all of its apps frozen through the reason code `ACCOUNT_NOT_IN_GOOD_STANDING`.

The account's reputation is readable side by side through a standing summary, which evaluates both standing variants against the same stored rows and returns `standing_score_rolling_365`, `standing_score_lifetime`, `expedite_credits_remaining`, `credits_used_in_window`, `rejection_strike_count`, `penalty_event_count` (how many rejection reasons are in penalty scope), `decayed_penalty_total`, `undecayed_penalty_total` and `credit_total`. Both variants are described in §3.8, and `rejection_strike_count` counts the account's rejection reasons of severity `CRITICAL` whose recording instant falls inside the rolling 365-day window and whose appeal outcome is not `OVERTURNED`.

### 3.2. Signing Material: Custody, Validity and the Compromise Cascade

Nothing reaches review unsigned. A `PublisherAdmin` registers signing material against the account by supplying `expected_publisher_account_version`, the owning `publisher_account_id`, a `credential_type`, the `platform` the material is for, a `key_custody` declaration, the validity bounds `not_before` and `not_after`, and optionally a `linked_cert_id` and an `entitlements` array. A `credential_type` is either `DISTRIBUTION_CERT` — the account's root signing identity for one platform — or `PROVISIONING_PROFILE`, the derived artifact a specific binary is actually signed with. `platform` is `IOS` or `ANDROID`. `key_custody` is `HSM`, `CLOUD_MANAGED` or `LOCAL_KEYCHAIN`. `entitlements` is a subset of `IN_APP_PURCHASE`, `EXTERNAL_PAYMENT_LINK`, `PUSH_NOTIFICATIONS` and `BACKGROUND_LOCATION`, and defaults to the empty array. The validity interval is half-open: `not_before` is inclusive, `not_after` is exclusive, and `not_after` must be strictly later than `not_before` or the registration is a schema violation.

Registration shape is policed strictly. A provisioning profile requires a non-null `linked_cert_id` pointing at a distribution certificate of the same account and the same platform; a missing `linked_cert_id` on a profile is a schema violation, while a profile whose platform differs from that of its linked certificate is `ERR_CREDENTIAL_PLATFORM_MISMATCH`. A distribution certificate requires the opposite: `linked_cert_id` must be null, and its `key_custody` may not be `LOCAL_KEYCHAIN` — a root signing key held in a local keychain is never acceptable and is refused with `ERR_KEY_CUSTODY_NOT_PERMITTED`. Newly registered material lands in status `PENDING_ACTIVATION` with `revocation_reason` and `compromised_at` null and `blocked_submission_count` at 0, and the registration advances the owning account's `version` by one. Registered material is discoverable by filtering on `publisher_account_id`, `linked_cert_id`, `credential_type`, `platform`, `status` or `key_custody`.

Activation is what makes material usable, and it is where the account's key hygiene is enforced. A `PublisherAdmin` activating a credential moves it `PENDING_ACTIVATION → ACTIVE` only when the logical clock sits inside the validity interval — at or after `not_before` and strictly before `not_after`, otherwise `ERR_CREDENTIAL_NOT_YET_VALID` — and only when the owning account's `program_status` is `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_PROGRAM_MEMBERSHIP_EXPIRED`. A distribution certificate whose custody is `LOCAL_KEYCHAIN` is refused here too with `ERR_KEY_CUSTODY_NOT_PERMITTED`, and a provisioning profile whose linked certificate is not itself `ACTIVE` is refused with `ERR_LINKED_CERT_NOT_ACTIVE`. Activation then supersedes: any other credential of the same account sharing the same `credential_type` and the same `platform` and sitting in status `ACTIVE` or `EXPIRING_SOON` is moved to `REVOKED` with `revocation_reason` set to `SUPERSEDED`. Because an active distribution certificate is one of the posture inputs, activation also recomputes the compliance posture of every app record the account owns.

Expiry is driven purely by the clock. When the clock lands within the final 168 hours before `not_after` — that is, one week out — an `ACTIVE` credential moves to `EXPIRING_SOON`. That is still a *usable* signing status: a build may be queued for review on an expiring credential, and the only thing expiry costs the publisher is the deadline check described in §3.7. When the clock reaches or passes `not_after`, the credential moves to `EXPIRED`, whether it arrives there from `EXPIRING_SOON` or jumps straight from `ACTIVE` because the clock skipped over the warning window entirely; the interval is half-open, so landing exactly on `not_after` expires the credential. Reaching `EXPIRED` recomputes the posture of every app record of the account, which is how a lapsed distribution certificate silently restricts an entire portfolio through `NO_ACTIVE_DISTRIBUTION_CERT`.

Reporting a compromise is the emergency lever, available to a `PublisherAdmin` and to a `PlatformPolicyAdmin`, who supplies a `reason_code`. It applies to material in status `ACTIVE` or `EXPIRING_SOON` — anything else is `ERR_INVALID_CREDENTIAL_STATE` — and it is exempt both from the posture gate and from the in-use check, because a leaked key must always be stoppable. The credential moves to `COMPROMISED` with `compromised_at` set to the current logical instant, and then a four-hop cascade runs. First, every credential whose `linked_cert_id` points at the compromised one and whose own status is `PENDING_ACTIVATION`, `ACTIVE` or `EXPIRING_SOON` moves to `REVOKED` with `revocation_reason` set to `COMPROMISE_CASCADE`. Second — caused by the first — every submission signed with one of those newly revoked profiles that is sitting in `WAITING_FOR_REVIEW` or `IN_REVIEW` moves to `BLOCKED_INVALID_SIGNING`, has its `review_window_id` cleared (which decrements that window's `slots_reserved` by one and increments its `slots_available` by one), and increments the revoked credential's `blocked_submission_count`. Third — caused by the second — the compliance posture of each affected app record is recomputed, and with no active distribution certificate left it becomes `RESTRICTED`. Fourth — caused by the third — every rollout plan of those apps sitting in `PHASE_ACTIVE` moves to `PAUSED` with `pause_reason_code` set to `POSTURE_DEGRADED`, which in turn drives each app's `distribution_state` from `PHASED_ROLLOUT` to `PAUSED`.

Revocation is the orderly counterpart, available to a `PublisherAdmin`, who may supply a `revocation_reason` and otherwise gets `MANUAL`. A compromised credential may always be revoked. An `ACTIVE` credential may be revoked only when no submission signed with it is currently in `WAITING_FOR_REVIEW` or `IN_REVIEW`; otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_CREDENTIAL_IN_USE`, precisely so that an orderly retirement cannot strand a build mid-review. Revoking an active credential recomputes the posture of every app record of the account. Both `EXPIRED` and `REVOKED` are terminal statuses; the recognised values of `revocation_reason` are `SUPERSEDED`, `MANUAL` and `COMPROMISE_CASCADE`.

### 3.3. The App Listing and Its Two Independent State Dimensions

A `PublisherAdmin` creates the shell of a store listing by supplying the owning `publisher_account_id`, a reverse-DNS `bundle_id` (unique per account and platform), the `platform`, a `display_name` of 1 to 30 characters, and a `primary_category` from `GAMES`, `FINANCE`, `HEALTH`, `SOCIAL`, `PRODUCTIVITY` and `KIDS`. What makes an app record unusual is that it tracks two entirely independent state dimensions on one row. `listing_state` describes the store page and moves through `DRAFT`, `METADATA_COMPLETE`, `LISTED` and `DELISTED`. `distribution_state` describes whether any binary is actually reaching users and moves through `NOT_DISTRIBUTED`, `PHASED_ROLLOUT`, `FULLY_RELEASED`, `PAUSED` and `ROLLED_BACK`. A new record starts at `DRAFT` and `NOT_DISTRIBUTED`, with `compliance_posture` computed immediately — `RESTRICTED` until both an active distribution certificate and full mapping coverage exist — and with `feature_compliance_index` at 0.00, `live_submission_id` null, `released_share_pct` at 0.00, `held_back_share_pct` at 100.00 and `rollback_count` at 0.

The listing dimension is driven by exactly one operation, available to a `PublisherAdmin` and, in one narrow case, to a `PlatformPolicyAdmin`. It takes a `listing_action` of `COMPLETE_METADATA`, `LIST`, `DELIST` or `RETURN_TO_DRAFT`. `COMPLETE_METADATA` moves `DRAFT → METADATA_COMPLETE`, and requires that at least one submission of this app owns a primary metadata locale that is itself complete; otherwise the operation is refused with `ERR_METADATA_INCOMPLETE`, or with `ERR_SCREENSHOT_SET_INCOMPLETE` when the shortfall is specifically in the screenshot counts. `LIST` moves `METADATA_COMPLETE → LISTED`, or `DELISTED → LISTED`, and requires two things: the posture must not be `FROZEN`, and `distribution_state` must not be `NOT_DISTRIBUTED` — a store page may only go live once a build is actually being distributed, and attempting it before then is `ERR_APP_NOT_DISTRIBUTED`. `DELIST` moves `LISTED → DELISTED` with no preconditions at all, and is the only listing action a `PlatformPolicyAdmin` may take. `RETURN_TO_DRAFT` moves `DELISTED → DRAFT`, and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ROLLOUT_STATE` while any rollout plan of the app is `SCHEDULED`, `PHASE_ACTIVE` or `PAUSED`. Every one of these advances the app record's `version` by one, and none of them ever touches `distribution_state`, `live_submission_id` or the two share fields — delisting a store page leaves an in-flight rollout running untouched. An action that is not declared for the current `listing_state` is `ERR_INVALID_LISTING_STATE`.

The distribution dimension is never driven directly by a client. It moves only as a consequence of rollout-plan transitions, described in §3.9, and its edges are declared: `NOT_DISTRIBUTED → PHASED_ROLLOUT` when a phased plan starts; `PHASED_ROLLOUT → FULLY_RELEASED` when a plan reaches its final phase and completes, which requires `released_share_pct` to have reached 100.00 and sets `held_back_share_pct` to 0.00; `PHASED_ROLLOUT → PAUSED` on a pause or a posture-degradation cascade, freezing both share fields at their current values; `PAUSED → PHASED_ROLLOUT` on a resume, which requires the posture to be `OPEN` and is otherwise `ERR_APP_COMPLIANCE_RESTRICTED`; `PHASED_ROLLOUT → ROLLED_BACK` on a halting rollback; `PAUSED → NOT_DISTRIBUTED` on a non-halting rollback; `FULLY_RELEASED → PHASED_ROLLOUT` when a later plan for a different, newer approved build starts and `live_submission_id` re-points to it; and `ROLLED_BACK → PHASED_ROLLOUT` when a replacement plan starts after the cooldown buffer has elapsed, which requires the logical clock to be at or after the previous plan's termination instant plus 72 hours and is otherwise `ERR_ROLLOUT_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. A cascade that would drive this dimension along an undeclared edge is `ERR_INVALID_DISTRIBUTION_STATE`. Every one of these edges also advances the app record's `version` by one. The field `live_submission_id` always names the build currently being distributed — the submission cited by the plan whose state is `PHASE_ACTIVE`, `PAUSED` or `COMPLETED` — and is null whenever `distribution_state` is `NOT_DISTRIBUTED` or `ROLLED_BACK`.

App records can be listed and filtered by `publisher_account_id`, `live_submission_id`, `platform`, `primary_category`, `listing_state`, `distribution_state` and `compliance_posture`. An app's compliance position is also readable as a single explanation, which recomputes the index and the posture from current rows and returns `compliance_posture`, the ordered `posture_reason_codes`, the `feature_compliance_index`, and the blocking child counts `mapping_total`, `mapping_passed`, `mapping_stale`, `mapping_failed`, `open_impact_count`, `overdue_impact_count`, `critical_finding_open_count` and the boolean `active_distribution_cert_present`.

### 3.4. The Guideline Corpus and How Publishing a Rule Reshapes Every Portfolio

The platform's rulebook is versioned data, not code. A `PlatformPolicyAdmin` drafts a guideline rule by supplying a `rule_code`, a `title`, a `category` from `SAFETY`, `PRIVACY`, `COMMERCE`, `LEGAL` and `DESIGN`, a `version_label`, an `effective_from` instant, optionally a `supersedes_rule_id` naming the rule this one replaces, and optionally `requires_feature_mapping`, which defaults to true and marks whether publishers must map a feature against this rule at all. The pair of `rule_code` and `version_label` must be unique across the corpus; because no dedicated code exists for it, a duplicate is rejected on the schema rung as `ERR_VALIDATION`, as are all other uniqueness breaches without a dedicated code — the unique `legal_name` of an account, the unique `bundle_id` per account and platform, and the unique `reference_name` per app record. Two fields are derived from the category and are never client-supplied. `severity_weight` is 5 for `SAFETY`, 4 for `PRIVACY`, 3 for `COMMERCE`, 3 for `LEGAL` and 1 for `DESIGN`. `remediation_grace_days` is 14 for `SAFETY`, 30 for `PRIVACY`, 45 for `COMMERCE`, 30 for `LEGAL` and 60 for `DESIGN` — the more dangerous the category, the less time a publisher gets. A drafted rule has `status` `DRAFT`, `published_at` null and `impacted_app_count` at 0. Rules are filterable by `supersedes_rule_id`, `category`, `status`, `version_label` and `requires_feature_mapping`.

Publishing is the single most consequential operation in the system. A `PlatformPolicyAdmin` moves a rule `DRAFT → EFFECTIVE`, which requires that `effective_from` is at or before the current logical instant — a rule may not be published into the future, and attempting it is `ERR_RULE_NOT_YET_EFFECTIVE` — and, when `supersedes_rule_id` is set, that the superseded rule is itself currently `EFFECTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_GUIDELINE_RULE_NOT_EFFECTIVE`. On success `published_at` is set to the current instant, the superseded rule moves `EFFECTIVE → SUPERSEDED`, and a four-hop cascade fans out across every publisher on the platform. First, every feature mapping that pointed at the superseded rule is re-pointed at the new rule, and each such mapping whose `self_review_status` was `PASSED` or `WAIVED_BY_PLATFORM` is moved to `STALE` with `staled_by_rule_id` set to the new rule's identifier — prior attestations do not survive a rulebook change. Second — caused by the first — exactly one impact assessment is opened for each distinct app record that owns at least one re-pointed mapping, and `impacted_app_count` is set to that number of apps. Third — caused by the second — each affected app's `feature_compliance_index` is recomputed with the now-stale mappings contributing at half weight, and its posture is recomputed, which yields `RESTRICTED` through the `OPEN_IMPACT_ASSESSMENT` reason and, where the index has fallen below the floor, `COMPLIANCE_INDEX_BELOW_FLOOR` as well. Fourth — caused by the third — every rollout plan of those apps that is `PHASE_ACTIVE` moves to `PAUSED` with `pause_reason_code` set to `POSTURE_DEGRADED`, driving `distribution_state` to `PAUSED` with both share fields frozen. A single publish can therefore stop distribution for hundreds of apps that did nothing wrong. A rule already `EFFECTIVE` or `SUPERSEDED` cannot be published again; that is `ERR_INVALID_RULE_STATE`.

Against that corpus each app declares its own surface. A `ReleaseManager` or a `ComplianceOfficer` creates a feature mapping by supplying the `app_record_id`, the `guideline_rule_id` it answers to, a `feature_code` naming the feature, and optionally a `risk_weight` (a value from 0.00 to 10.00, defaulting to 1.00) and an `evidence_ref`. The triple of `app_record_id`, `guideline_rule_id` and `feature_code` must be unique, and a repeat is rejected as `ERR_VALIDATION`. A mapping may only cite a rule that is currently `EFFECTIVE` — citing a draft or superseded rule is `ERR_GUIDELINE_RULE_NOT_EFFECTIVE` — and the app it names must not be frozen. A new mapping starts with `self_review_status` `NOT_STARTED` and with `attested_by_user_id`, `attested_at`, `staled_by_rule_id` and `waiver_reason` all null. Mappings are filterable by `app_record_id`, `guideline_rule_id`, `staled_by_rule_id`, `attested_by_user_id`, `self_review_status` and `feature_code`.

Self-review is how a publisher attests. A `ComplianceOfficer` records an `outcome` of `START`, `PASS` or `FAIL`, optionally with an `evidence_ref`. `START` moves `NOT_STARTED → IN_PROGRESS`. `PASS` moves `IN_PROGRESS → PASSED`, or `FAILED → PASSED`, or `STALE → PASSED` for a re-attestation after a rulebook change, and in every case requires a non-empty `evidence_ref` either already on the mapping or supplied with the request; without one the attestation is refused with `ERR_EVIDENCE_REQUIRED`. `FAIL` moves `IN_PROGRESS → FAILED` or `PASSED → FAILED`. Passing or failing stamps `attested_by_user_id` with the acting operator and `attested_at` with the current logical instant, clears `staled_by_rule_id`, and recomputes the app's index and posture. An outcome that is not declared for the current status is `ERR_INVALID_MAPPING_STATE`.

Waiving is the platform's escape hatch and is deliberately narrow. A `PlatformPolicyAdmin` supplies a `waiver_reason` and moves a mapping `FAILED → WAIVED_BY_PLATFORM` or `STALE → WAIVED_BY_PLATFORM`; no other starting status is permitted. Three conditions apply. The mapping's rule may not be in category `SAFETY` — safety findings are never waivable, and attempting it is `ERR_WAIVER_NOT_PERMITTED`. The `waiver_reason` must be at least 20 characters, so that the audit trail carries an actual justification; a shorter one is `ERR_EVIDENCE_REQUIRED`. And the acting operator must differ from the `attested_by_user_id` already recorded on the mapping, per §2.10. A waiver clears `staled_by_rule_id`, stamps `attested_at`, and recomputes the app's index and posture; a waived mapping counts as fully compliant for index purposes.

That index — `feature_compliance_index` — is the number the posture gate leans on, and its definition is exact. Consider every feature mapping of the app whose guideline rule is currently `EFFECTIVE`; mappings pointing at draft or superseded rules are excluded entirely. Each contributing mapping has a weight equal to its `risk_weight` and a compliance factor determined solely by its `self_review_status`: 1.0 for `PASSED`, 1.0 for `WAIVED_BY_PLATFORM`, 0.5 for `STALE`, 0.25 for `IN_PROGRESS`, 0.0 for `NOT_STARTED` and 0.0 for `FAILED`. Multiply each weight by its factor, sum those products, sum the weights separately, divide the first unrounded sum by the second unrounded sum, multiply by 100, and round the single result half up to two decimal places. Do not round the individual products or either subtotal. When the contributing set is empty — an app with no mappings, or an app whose every mapping points at a superseded rule — the index is 0.00, not 100.00: absence of evidence is not evidence of compliance, and this is the boundary that keeps a brand-new app record `RESTRICTED` rather than `OPEN`.

### 3.5. Impact Assessments: The Only Rows No Client Ever Creates

An impact assessment is the work item a rule publication leaves behind on an app. There is no operation that creates one; every row is minted by the publication cascade of §3.4, exactly one per affected app record, and clients may only read them and work them. Each row records the `app_record_id` it burdens and the `guideline_rule_id` that caused it, plus `opened_at` set to the instant of the cascade. `affected_feature_codes` holds the `feature_code` values of precisely those mappings the cascade moved to `STALE`, ordered by the mappings' own `created_at` ascending, so the list is stable and reproducible. `assessed_by_user_id`, `assessed_at`, `note` and `remediated_at` all start null. Assessments are filterable by `app_record_id`, `guideline_rule_id` and `status`.

Two numbers are computed and they encode the platform's judgement of how much this rule change actually matters for this app. `exposure_factor` is 1.5 when the app's `distribution_state` is `PHASED_ROLLOUT`, `FULLY_RELEASED` or `PAUSED`, and 1.0 otherwise — a rule change against an app that is genuinely in users' hands, including one that is merely paused, weighs half again as much as the same change against an app that has never shipped. `impact_severity` is the rule's `severity_weight` multiplied by the count of entries in `affected_feature_codes` multiplied by `exposure_factor`, with the single product rounded half up to two decimal places. `remediation_deadline_at` is the rule's `effective_from` advanced by its `remediation_grace_days` times 24 hours; note that the clock starts at the rule's effective instant, not at the moment of publication or of assessment, so a rule published well after it became effective arrives with its grace period already partly spent, and a rule published long enough after that instant arrives already past its deadline.

A `ComplianceOfficer` works an assessment with an `action` of `START`, `REQUIRE_REMEDIATION`, `ACCEPT_NO_CHANGE` or `MARK_REMEDIATED`, optionally attaching a `note` that is stored as the narrative of the latest action. `START` moves `OPEN → IN_ASSESSMENT`, stamps `assessed_by_user_id` with the acting operator and `assessed_at` with the current instant, and recomputes `impact_severity` against the app's *current* `exposure_factor` — an app that has begun distributing since the row was opened becomes more severe at this moment.

From `IN_ASSESSMENT` the officer chooses between two outcomes that diverge sharply. `REQUIRE_REMEDIATION` moves the row to `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED`: the app's posture stays `RESTRICTED`, the stale mappings stay stale, and the deadline clock begins to matter. `ACCEPT_NO_CHANGE` instead terminates the row at `ACCEPTED_NO_CHANGE`, and it is gated twice: the rule's `category` must not be `SAFETY`, and `impact_severity` must be at or below 6.00 — the ceiling is inclusive, so a severity of exactly 6.00 may be accepted while 6.01 may not, and a breach is refused with `ERR_IMPACT_TOO_SEVERE`. Acceptance is not merely a bookkeeping close: every feature mapping named in `affected_feature_codes` that is still `STALE` is restored to `PASSED`, the app's `feature_compliance_index` is recomputed on that basis, and with no other objection outstanding the app's posture returns to `OPEN`. This is the only path by which a stale mapping recovers without a fresh attestation, and it is why the severity ceiling and the safety exclusion exist.

If the logical clock advances strictly past `remediation_deadline_at` while the row is `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED`, the row moves to `OVERDUE`. The boundary is strict: landing exactly on the deadline leaves the row `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED`, and only the next tick past it trips the transition. Going overdue is the harshest automatic consequence in the system — the app's posture becomes `FROZEN`, which blocks every publisher write on the app and all of its children, and every rollout plan of the app that is `PHASE_ACTIVE` is paused with `pause_reason_code` set to `POSTURE_DEGRADED`.

`MARK_REMEDIATED` moves the row to `REMEDIATED` from either `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED` or `OVERDUE`, and requires that every feature mapping named in `affected_feature_codes` now has `self_review_status` of `PASSED` or `WAIVED_BY_PLATFORM`; if any remains unattested the request is refused with `ERR_REMEDIATION_INCOMPLETE`. It sets `remediated_at` to the current instant and recomputes the app's posture, which leaves `FROZEN` only once no `OVERDUE` row remains against the app. Remediating an overdue assessment is deliberately exempt from the posture gate and is therefore the *only* publisher action permitted while an app is frozen — without that exemption a frozen app could never dig itself out. `REMEDIATED` and `ACCEPTED_NO_CHANGE` are terminal. An action not declared for the current status is refused with `ERR_INVALID_IMPACT_STATE`.

### 3.6. Composing a Submission: Build, Metadata Locales and Age Rating

A submission is one candidate binary of one app together with everything the platform needs to judge it. A `ReleaseManager` creates one by supplying `expected_app_record_version` as a revision guard, the `app_record_id`, the `signing_credential_id` the build is signed with, a `build_number`, a `version_string`, and optionally a `release_mode` of `IMMEDIATE`, `PHASED` or `MANUAL`, which defaults to `PHASED`. The `build_number` must be strictly greater than the `build_number` of every existing submission of the same app whose state is not `DEVELOPER_WITHDRAWN`; anything else, equal or lower, is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_BUILD_NUMBER`. Withdrawn builds are excluded from that comparison precisely so a publisher who withdraws a mistaken build can reuse its number. The cited credential must be usable, and the check runs in a fixed field order: its `credential_type` must be `PROVISIONING_PROFILE`, its `status` must be `ACTIVE` or `EXPIRING_SOON`, its `platform` must equal the app's `platform`, its `not_before` must be at or before the current instant, and the credential named by its `linked_cert_id` must itself be `ACTIVE` or `EXPIRING_SOON`. A distribution certificate supplied where a profile is required, or a profile that is `PENDING_ACTIVATION`, `EXPIRED`, `REVOKED` or `COMPROMISED`, is `ERR_SIGNING_CREDENTIAL_INVALID`; a platform disagreement is `ERR_CREDENTIAL_PLATFORM_MISMATCH`; a `not_before` still in the future is `ERR_CREDENTIAL_NOT_YET_VALID`, with equality passing; and an unusable linked certificate is `ERR_LINKED_CERT_NOT_ACTIVE`. `EXPIRING_SOON` is deliberately still usable here — imminent expiry is policed later, by the deadline rule of §3.8. The app must not be frozen, and its posture must not be `RESTRICTED` for this operation either. A successful creation advances the app record's `version` by one.

A new submission lands in state `DRAFT` with `submission_channel` `STANDARD`, `pre_review_passed` false, `pre_review_blocking_reasons` empty, `declared_privacy_items` empty, `tracking_consent_prompt_present` false, `resubmission_count` and `metadata_locale_count` at 0, and with `review_window_id`, `age_rating_answers`, `attested_age_rating`, `derived_age_rating`, `age_rating_driving_dimension`, `disclosure_accuracy_score_provisional`, `disclosure_accuracy_score_final`, `pre_review_checked_at`, `attested_by_user_id`, `queued_at`, `provisional_deadline_at`, `priority_score`, `priority_band`, `effective_review_deadline_at`, `reviewed_by_user_id`, `decided_at` and `rejected_at` all null. Its `state` moves through a twelve-value enum: `DRAFT`, `PRE_REVIEW_PASSED`, `WAITING_FOR_REVIEW`, `IN_REVIEW`, `APPROVED`, `REJECTED`, `METADATA_REJECTED`, `APPEAL_PENDING`, `REJECTED_FINAL`, `BLOCKED_INVALID_SIGNING`, `DEVELOPER_WITHDRAWN` and `SUPERSEDED`. Submissions are filterable by `app_record_id`, `signing_credential_id`, `review_window_id`, `state`, `priority_band` and `submission_channel`.

The store page itself is carried by metadata locales, each owned by one submission. A `ReleaseManager` creates one by supplying the `submission_id`, a `locale` tag, a `title` of 1 to 30 characters, a `screenshot_counts` map from device class to a count, and optionally `is_primary`, a `subtitle` of up to 30 characters, `keywords` of up to 100 characters and `promotional_text` of up to 170 characters. Every value in `screenshot_counts` must be an integer of zero or greater. Exactly one locale of a submission may be primary; both a second locale carrying `is_primary` true and a repeat of the same `locale` on the same submission are refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PRIMARY_LOCALE`. Attaching a locale is a content mutation, so it increments the submission's `metadata_locale_count` by one and, if the submission was `PRE_REVIEW_PASSED`, drives it back to `DRAFT` — changing the store page invalidates the clearance that was granted against the old one. It also requires that the app is not frozen. Locales are created and read but never edited or deleted. Two fields are derived. `required_device_classes` follows the app's platform: an `IOS` submission requires both `PHONE_65` and `TABLET_129`, while an `ANDROID` submission requires only `PHONE_65`. `is_complete` is true exactly when `title` is non-empty and, for every one of the required device classes, `screenshot_counts` carries an entry of at least 2 — the minimum screenshot count per class — and false otherwise, including when a required class is absent from the map altogether. Locales are filterable by `submission_id`, `locale` and `is_primary`, and a submission's locales are also readable as a nested collection under the submission itself.

### 3.7. Age Rating, Privacy Disclosure and the Self-Review Report

A `ComplianceOfficer` or a `ReleaseManager` sets a submission's content questionnaire by supplying `age_rating_answers` and an `attested_age_rating`. The answers object carries six intensity dimensions — violence, sexual content, profanity, simulated gambling, drugs and alcohol, and horror — each an integer from 0 to 3, plus five booleans: unrestricted web access, user-generated content, whether UGC moderation is enabled, real-money gambling, and contests. `attested_age_rating` must be one of the six declared rating bands 0, 4, 9, 12, 17 and 18.

From those answers alone — no other entity participates — the platform derives `derived_age_rating` and `age_rating_driving_dimension`. First map each intensity level to a band: level 0 maps to age 0, level 1 to 9, level 2 to 12 and level 3 to 17. The content base is the highest such band across the six dimensions, and the driving dimension is the *first* dimension attaining that base in the fixed priority order violence, sexual content, drugs and alcohol, simulated gambling, horror, profanity — so when violence and horror are both at level 3, violence is named. Next compute a content-independent floor as an if/else chain in which only the first matching clause applies: real-money gambling forces a floor of 18; failing that, unrestricted web access, or user-generated content without moderation enabled, forces 17; failing that, contests force 12; otherwise the floor is 0. Take the greater of the content base and the floor. Then, if the simulated gambling intensity is at level 2 or higher, raise the result to at least 17 — level 2 alone is enough, which is why simulated gambling behaves unlike every other dimension, whose level 2 would only have reached 12. Finally snap the result *up* to the lowest declared band that is greater than or equal to it, which is what turns an intermediate value such as 13 into 17. The `age_rating_driving_dimension` becomes the literal `FLOOR_RULE` when the floor strictly exceeded the content base; on a tie the content dimension keeps the label. Setting the questionnaire recomputes both derived fields, and, being a content mutation, drives a `PRE_REVIEW_PASSED` submission back to `DRAFT` and voids the attestation.

Separately a `ComplianceOfficer` sets the privacy label by supplying `declared_privacy_items` — an array of tuples each naming a `data_type`, a purpose, whether the collection is linked to a user identity, and whether it is used for cross-app tracking — together with `tracking_consent_prompt_present`, the publisher's assertion that the binary actually shows a consent prompt. This too is a content mutation that voids a passed self-review.

Against those declarations the platform's scanner files evidence. A `PlatformPolicyAdmin` records a behavior finding by supplying the `submission_id`, the `data_type` observed from `PRECISE_LOCATION`, `CONTACTS`, `DEVICE_ID`, `EMAIL`, `PURCHASE_HISTORY`, `HEALTH` and `USAGE_DATA`, an `observed_purpose` from `ANALYTICS`, `ADVERTISING`, `APP_FUNCTIONALITY`, `PERSONALIZATION` and `THIRD_PARTY_SHARING`, the booleans `observed_linked_to_user` and `observed_tracking`, the `endpoint_domain` the data went to, a `severity` of `CRITICAL`, `MAJOR` or `MINOR`, a `scanner_confidence` from 0.0000 to 1.0000, and the `detected_at` instant of the observation — which may legitimately be later than the submission's `pre_review_checked_at`, because scanners report late. A finding lands `OPEN` with `triaged_by_user_id`, `triage_justification` and `triaged_at` null, and a `CRITICAL` finding immediately recomputes the app's posture, which freezes the app. Findings are filterable by `submission_id`, `data_type`, `status`, `severity` and `observed_tracking`.

Triage is shared between the two sides with a strict split. A `ComplianceOfficer` may take `action` `DISPUTE`, moving a finding `OPEN → DISPUTED`; a `PlatformPolicyAdmin` may take `RECONCILE`, moving `OPEN → RECONCILED` or `DISPUTED → RECONCILED`, or `DISMISS`, moving `OPEN → DISMISSED` or `DISPUTED → DISMISSED`. Either party may attach a `justification`, which is stored with `triaged_by_user_id` and `triaged_at`. `RECONCILE` requires that the submission's `declared_privacy_items` now contains a tuple matching the finding on all four attributes — `data_type` equal to `data_type`, purpose equal to `observed_purpose`, linked-to-user equal to `observed_linked_to_user` and tracking equal to `observed_tracking`. A partial match does not count: a declared tuple with the right data type but the wrong purpose, or with linked-to-user false where the scanner saw true, leaves the finding unreconciled and the request is refused with `ERR_DISCLOSURE_STILL_MISSING`. `DISMISS` is refused for a `CRITICAL` finding with `ERR_WAIVER_NOT_PERMITTED` — the platform may not simply wave away its own most serious evidence. `RECONCILED` and `DISMISSED` are terminal, and an action not declared for the current status is `ERR_INVALID_FINDING_STATE`. Triage recomputes both disclosure scores and, for a `CRITICAL` finding, the app's posture; like the other remediation paths it is exempt from the posture gate.

The disclosure accuracy score compares what was declared against what was observed, and it exists in two variants that deliberately disagree. In both, a finding is *matched* when some declared tuple agrees with it on all four attributes. Each unmatched finding in scope contributes an integer penalty of 40 if `CRITICAL`, 20 if `MAJOR` and 5 if `MINOR`. An over-disclosure term counts each declared tuple whose `data_type` appears in no finding in scope and charges 2 per such data type. Start from 100, subtract the total undisclosed penalty, subtract the over-disclosure term where the variant applies it, floor the result at 0, then divide by 100 and round half up to four decimal places. The pinning matters: the penalties are summed as integers first and the single division by 100 happens last, so no intermediate rounding occurs. The pass threshold is 0.9500 inclusive — exactly 0.9500 passes, 0.9499 fails.

The two variants differ in scope. The provisional variant, `disclosure_accuracy_score_provisional`, counts only findings whose `status` is `OPEN`, whose `detected_at` is at or before the submission's `pre_review_checked_at`, and whose `scanner_confidence` is at or above 0.8000; it does **not** apply the over-disclosure term, because declaring more than was observed costs a publisher nothing at self-review time. The final variant, `disclosure_accuracy_score_final`, counts findings whose `status` is `OPEN` *or* `DISPUTED`, with no time bound and no confidence floor, and it *does* apply the over-disclosure term. For identical stored data the two therefore return different numbers whenever a finding arrived after the pre-review instant, was disputed, scored below the confidence floor, or the publisher over-declared a data type. Disputing a finding removes it from the provisional scope but leaves it in the final scope, so a publisher who disputes everything watches the provisional score rise while the final score does not move at all. The provisional score is rewritten by the self-review check and by any finding or declaration mutation; the final score is rewritten when a reviewer claims the build and again at the decision gate.

The self-review check is the publisher's dress rehearsal, run by a `ComplianceOfficer`, and it is a report rather than a gate: it always returns 200 with the submission, never a business error, even when the build is hopeless. It first sets `pre_review_checked_at` to the current instant and only then scores, so a finding detected at exactly this instant is inside the provisional window. It then evaluates the same content rules that the queue gate enforces and, instead of raising them, records each as a code in `pre_review_blocking_reasons`, ordered by ascending rule priority with ties broken by the fixed sequence `METADATA_INCOMPLETE`, `SCREENSHOT_SET_EMPTY`, `AGE_RATING_UNDERSTATED`, `TRACKING_CONSENT_PROMPT_MISSING`, `PRIVACY_DISCLOSURE_INACCURATE`, `CRITICAL_FINDING_UNRESOLVED`, `GUIDELINE_MAPPING_INCOMPLETE`, `SIGNING_CREDENTIAL_INVALID`. When at least one code is recorded the submission stays in `DRAFT` with `pre_review_passed` false; when the array comes back empty the submission moves `DRAFT → PRE_REVIEW_PASSED`, `pre_review_passed` becomes true, and `attested_by_user_id` is stamped with the acting officer, who thereby becomes the subject of the segregation-of-duties rule at the decision gate. The check requires the app not to be frozen and the account to be `ACTIVE`.

### 3.8. Reviewer Capacity, Queue Entry and the Priority Forward Pass

Review effort is a finite resource modelled explicitly. A `PlatformPolicyAdmin` opens a capacity window by supplying the `platform` it serves, a `reviewer_pool_code` naming the pool of reviewers, a half-open interval of `starts_at` inclusive and `ends_at` exclusive with `ends_at` strictly after `starts_at`, and a `capacity_slots` count of at least 1. A window opens with `status` `OPEN`, `slots_reserved` and `slots_consumed` at 0 and `slots_available` equal to `capacity_slots`. Creation is subject to a pool-level ceiling: summed across every `OPEN` window of the same `platform` and `reviewer_pool_code` whose interval intersects the new one, total `capacity_slots` may not exceed 10, and a breach is refused with `ERR_REVIEW_POOL_OVERSUBSCRIBED`. Intersection is half-open, so adjacency never counts — a window ending exactly when another begins is always legal — and the ceiling is inclusive: a total of exactly 10 passes while 11 fails. Windows are filterable by `platform`, `reviewer_pool_code` and `status`. A `PlatformPolicyAdmin` may close a window, moving it `OPEN → CLOSED`; already-reserved submissions keep their slots and may still be claimed and decided, but a closed window drops out of the selector, which is one way a queue call can find no capacity at all.

The three slot counters are derived, never stored independently. `slots_reserved` is the count of submissions assigned to the window whose `state` is `WAITING_FOR_REVIEW` or `IN_REVIEW`. `slots_consumed` is the count whose `state` is `APPROVED`, `REJECTED`, `METADATA_REJECTED`, `APPEAL_PENDING`, `REJECTED_FINAL` or `SUPERSEDED`. `slots_available` is `capacity_slots` minus both. The invariant is absolute: the three always sum to `capacity_slots`. Queue entry reserves — reserved up one, available down one — and its compensating actions, withdrawal and return-to-draft, must restore `slots_available` to the exact integer it held before the reservation.

Queue entry is the busiest gate in the system. A `ReleaseManager` queues a build, optionally citing an `expedite_request_id`, and the submission moves `PRE_REVIEW_PASSED → WAITING_FOR_REVIEW`. Every content rule that the self-review merely reported is enforced here as a hard error, re-evaluated against the *current* clock and the current rows, not against whatever the self-review saw. In addition: `pre_review_passed` must be true, otherwise `ERR_PRE_REVIEW_NOT_PASSED`; the attestation must be no more than 72 hours old, measured from `pre_review_checked_at`, with exactly 72 hours still passing and anything older refused as `ERR_PRE_REVIEW_STALE`; the app's posture must be `OPEN`, so `FROZEN` yields `ERR_APP_COMPLIANCE_FROZEN` and `RESTRICTED` yields `ERR_APP_COMPLIANCE_RESTRICTED`; the signing credential must still be usable; guideline coverage must be complete, meaning that every `EFFECTIVE` rule with `requires_feature_mapping` true has at least one mapping from this app and that every mapping of the app pointing at an `EFFECTIVE` rule is `PASSED` or `WAIVED_BY_PLATFORM`, and any shortfall — including an app with no mappings at all, for which there is no vacuous pass — is `ERR_GUIDELINE_MAPPING_INCOMPLETE`; any declared privacy tuple marked as used for cross-app tracking obliges `tracking_consent_prompt_present` to be true, and declared tracking without the prompt is a hard block regardless of the accuracy score, refused with `ERR_TRACKING_CONSENT_PROMPT_MISSING`; and a capacity window must be selectable, otherwise `ERR_NO_REVIEW_CAPACITY`. When an `expedite_request_id` is supplied it must name a request whose `submission_id` is this submission and whose `state` is `GRANTED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_EXPEDITE_STATE`; on success that request moves to `CONSUMED` with `consumed_at` stamped and the submission's `submission_channel` becomes `EXPEDITED`.

The window a queued build lands in is chosen by an explicit selector rather than by whim. Eligible windows are those whose `status` is `OPEN`, whose `platform` equals the app's `platform`, whose half-open interval contains the current instant — `starts_at` at or before it and `ends_at` strictly after it — and whose `slots_available` is at least 1. Among the eligible, rank by `slots_available` descending, then by `starts_at` ascending, and break any remaining tie by `created_at` ascending; never by `id`. If no window is eligible the queue call is refused with `ERR_NO_REVIEW_CAPACITY` and nothing at all changes.

Priority is then computed as a strict single forward pass, and the order is pinned because the inputs are almost circular. Step one happens only at queue entry and never again: `queued_at` is set to the current instant and `provisional_deadline_at` is frozen at `queued_at` plus 48 hours. Step two reads that frozen deadline and nothing else — hours remaining is the deadline minus the current instant expressed in hours and may be negative, and deadline pressure is 48 minus hours remaining, divided by 48, clamped to the range 0.0 to 1.0. At queue entry hours remaining equals 48 exactly, so deadline pressure is 0.0 by construction. Step three combines three components each on a 0-to-100 scale: the standing component is the account's `standing_score_lifetime`; the urgency component is 100 times deadline pressure; the risk component is 100 times one minus `disclosure_accuracy_score_provisional`. Then `priority_score` is 0.50 times standing plus 0.30 times urgency *minus* 0.20 times risk, rounded half up to two decimals and clamped to 0.00 through 100.00. Step four assigns `priority_band`: `EXPEDITED` whenever `submission_channel` is `EXPEDITED`, regardless of score; otherwise `HIGH` at 70.00 or above, `NORMAL` at 40.00 or above, and `LOW` below that, both floors inclusive. Step five maps the band to an SLA: 12 hours for `EXPEDITED`, 24 for `HIGH`, 48 for `NORMAL` and 96 for `LOW`. Step six sets `effective_review_deadline_at` to `queued_at` plus those SLA hours.

The circularity is broken by decree: step two may never read `effective_review_deadline_at`, whose value depends on the band, which depends on the score, which depends on step two. There is exactly one forward pass over the six steps, no fixed-point iteration and no second pass. When the logical clock advances, steps two through six are re-evaluated for every submission still `WAITING_FOR_REVIEW` — pressure rises, priority moves and the band and effective deadline can change — but step one is never redone, so `provisional_deadline_at` remains the value frozen at queue entry forever. This is also why a low-standing publisher's build climbs the queue over time rather than starving: urgency accumulates.

One temporal rule joins at queue entry and is the only reason an `EXPIRING_SOON` credential can block: the credential's `not_after` must be strictly later than the `effective_review_deadline_at` this very queue entry just computed. A credential expiring exactly at the review deadline is refused with `ERR_CREDENTIAL_EXPIRES_BEFORE_DEADLINE`, because the review may legitimately conclude at that instant.

Expedited review is rationed. A `ReleaseManager` or a `ComplianceOfficer` files an expedite request against a `submission_id` with a `justification_category` of `CRITICAL_BUG`, `SECURITY_FIX` or `TIME_SENSITIVE_EVENT` and a `justification` of 20 to 1000 characters. The request lands `REQUESTED` with `requested_at` stamped and `publisher_account_id` copied from the submission's app record — the account, not the submission, is the quota subject. Filing itself checks that the account's `standing_score_rolling_365` is at or above 60.00, refusing below it with `ERR_STANDING_TOO_LOW`, but consumes no credit. A `PlatformAppealBoard` operator then decides with an `outcome` of `GRANT` or `DENY` plus a `decision_rationale`. `GRANT` moves `REQUESTED → GRANTED`, stamps `granted_at`, records `decided_by_user_id`, and decreases `expedite_credits_remaining` by exactly one; it is refused with `ERR_EXPEDITE_QUOTA_EXHAUSTED` when no credit remains and with `ERR_STANDING_TOO_LOW` when standing has since fallen below the floor, and the deciding operator must differ from the submission's `reviewed_by_user_id`. `DENY` moves the request from `REQUESTED` to `DENIED`, which is terminal and consumes nothing. A granted request is `CONSUMED` at queue entry, and a `REQUESTED`, `GRANTED` or `CONSUMED` request becomes `REFUNDED` with `refunded_at` stamped when the cited submission is withdrawn, restoring the credit to its pre-grant value. Expedite requests are filterable by `submission_id`, `publisher_account_id`, `state` and `justification_category`.

The credit ledger itself is derived, never stored as a running total. Credits used is the count of the account's expedite requests whose `state` is `GRANTED` or `CONSUMED` and whose `granted_at` falls at or after 365 days before the current instant, with the lower bound inclusive; `DENIED` and `REFUNDED` rows are out of scope, and a `REQUESTED` row has a null `granted_at` and therefore never counts. `expedite_credits_remaining` is 2 minus that count, so remaining plus used always equals 2 at every observable instant. A grant followed by its refund must restore `expedite_credits_remaining` to the exact pre-grant integer, with no drift tolerated even when the clock moved between the two actions, and the third grant inside a rolling window is refused at zero credits.

### 3.9. Review, Decision, Rejection Response and Appeal

A `PlatformReviewer` claims a queued build, moving it `WAITING_FOR_REVIEW → IN_REVIEW`. Two things must hold: the assigned capacity window must still be `OPEN` with the current instant inside its half-open interval, otherwise `ERR_REVIEW_WINDOW_CLOSED`; and the signing credential must still be `ACTIVE` or `EXPIRING_SOON`, otherwise `ERR_SIGNING_CREDENTIAL_INVALID`. Claiming stamps `reviewed_by_user_id` with the claiming operator — the identity every later segregation-of-duties comparison is made against — and recomputes `disclosure_accuracy_score_final`, at which point late-arriving and disputed findings enter scope for the first time and the number the reviewer sees may differ sharply from the one the publisher saw. Slot accounting does not move: the build remains in the reserved set.

The decision takes an `outcome` of `APPROVE`, `REJECT` or `METADATA_REJECT` and, for the two rejecting outcomes, a `rejection_reasons` array. Only the claiming reviewer may decide — the acting operator must equal `reviewed_by_user_id` — and that same operator must differ from `attested_by_user_id`, the publisher-side officer whose self-review cleared the build; either breach is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES_VIOLATION`. Every branch moves the assigned window's accounting from reserved to consumed, decrementing `slots_reserved` by one and incrementing `slots_consumed` by one while `slots_available` stays exactly where it was, and every branch recomputes both of the account's standing scores.

`APPROVE` moves `IN_REVIEW → APPROVED` and re-checks the content gates against the authoritative final figures: `disclosure_accuracy_score_final` must be at or above 0.9500, otherwise `ERR_PRIVACY_DISCLOSURE_INACCURATE`; no finding of severity `CRITICAL` may be `OPEN` or `DISPUTED`, otherwise `ERR_CRITICAL_FINDING_UNRESOLVED` — note that disputing a critical finding does not clear this gate, only reconciling or dismissing it does; `attested_age_rating` must be at or above `derived_age_rating`, otherwise `ERR_AGE_RATING_UNDERSTATED`; and no commerce item of the app may still be sitting in `SUBMITTED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_COMMERCE_ITEM_STATE`. Approval stamps `decided_at`, and any other submission of the same app already `APPROVED` is moved to `SUPERSEDED`, which cancels that build's `SCHEDULED` rollout plans and is refused if that build has a plan in `PHASE_ACTIVE`.

`REJECT` moves `IN_REVIEW → REJECTED` and requires a non-empty `rejection_reasons` array in which every cited `guideline_rule_id` names a rule whose `status` is `EFFECTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_GUIDELINE_RULE_NOT_EFFECTIVE`. One rejection reason row is created per entry, each carrying its `submission_id`, `guideline_rule_id`, `reason_code`, `severity` of `CRITICAL`, `MAJOR` or `MINOR`, and reviewer `detail`, with `recorded_at` set to the current instant, `recorded_by_user_id` set to the deciding reviewer, `developer_response` null and both `disposition` and `appeal_disposition` at `PENDING`. Both `rejected_at` and `decided_at` are stamped. Then a four-hop cascade runs: every feature mapping of the app whose `guideline_rule_id` is cited by a `CRITICAL` or `MAJOR` reason moves `PASSED → FAILED`; the app's `feature_compliance_index` is recomputed from those changed statuses and, when it falls below the posture floor, the posture becomes `RESTRICTED`; every rollout plan of the app in `PHASE_ACTIVE` moves to `PAUSED` with `pause_reason_code` set to `POSTURE_DEGRADED`; and the app's `distribution_state` goes `PHASED_ROLLOUT → PAUSED` with `released_share_pct` and `held_back_share_pct` held at their pre-pause values. The account's `rejection_strike_count` is also recomputed.

`METADATA_REJECT` moves `IN_REVIEW → METADATA_REJECTED` and is the deliberately gentle sibling. It requires a non-empty `rejection_reasons` array in which *every* entry carries `severity` `MINOR`; a `CRITICAL` or `MAJOR` entry here is `ERR_VALIDATION`. It creates the rejection reason rows exactly as the reject branch does and stamps the same two timestamps, and then diverges completely: no feature mapping is touched, the `feature_compliance_index` and `compliance_posture` are unchanged, no rollout plan is paused, the app keeps distributing, and `rejection_strike_count` does not move because `MINOR` reasons never count as strikes. Only the standing scores are recomputed, absorbing the `MINOR` penalty weight. A metadata rejection is also not appealable, as described below.

The publisher answers each reason individually. A `ComplianceOfficer` supplies a `disposition` of `ACCEPTED` or `CONTESTED` — never back to `PENDING` — together with a `developer_response` narrative, and the operation is refused with `ERR_INVALID_SUBMISSION_STATE` unless the submission is `REJECTED` or `METADATA_REJECTED`. Only a `CONTESTED` reason may later be cited in an appeal. Reasons are filterable by `submission_id`, `guideline_rule_id`, `severity`, `disposition`, `appeal_disposition` and `reason_code`. Once every reason of the submission has a `disposition` other than `PENDING`, a `ReleaseManager` may return the build to draft — refused with `ERR_REJECTION_RESPONSE_REQUIRED` while any reason is still unanswered, and refused with `ERR_INVALID_APPEAL_STATE` while an appeal of the submission is `SUBMITTED` or `BOARD_REVIEW`. Returning to draft increments `resubmission_count`, voids the attestation by clearing `pre_review_passed`, `pre_review_checked_at` and `attested_by_user_id`, releases the capacity slot by clearing `review_window_id` — which decrements the old window's `slots_consumed` and increments its `slots_available`, restoring the pre-queue integer — and may optionally re-point `signing_credential_id`, which must then name a usable credential of the same account. The same operation is the only way out of `BLOCKED_INVALID_SIGNING`, and there `signing_credential_id` is mandatory and must name an `ACTIVE` or `EXPIRING_SOON` `PROVISIONING_PROFILE` on the app's platform. Return-to-draft is exempt from the posture gate, as is withdrawal: a `ReleaseManager` may withdraw a build from `DRAFT`, `PRE_REVIEW_PASSED` or `WAITING_FOR_REVIEW`, optionally citing a `reason_code`, but never from `IN_REVIEW` — once a reviewer has the build the publisher can no longer pull it, and trying is `ERR_INVALID_SUBMISSION_STATE`. Withdrawal is terminal, releases any reserved slot, and refunds any expedite request of the build that is `REQUESTED`, `GRANTED` or `CONSUMED`.

An appeal contests a rejection. A `ComplianceOfficer` files one against a `submission_id` with an `appeal_type` of `GUIDELINE_INTERPRETATION` or `FACTUAL_ERROR`, an `argument` of 20 to 2000 characters, and a non-empty `cited_rejection_reason_ids` array. Three gates apply. The filing must happen within 14 days of `rejected_at`, inclusive — an appeal filed at exactly `rejected_at` plus 14 days is accepted, later is `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. Every cited reason must belong to this submission and have `disposition` `CONTESTED`; a reason still `PENDING`, already `ACCEPTED`, or whose `appeal_disposition` is already decided, is `ERR_REJECTION_NOT_CONTESTED`. And no other appeal of the submission may be `SUBMITTED` or `BOARD_REVIEW` — one live appeal at a time, otherwise `ERR_APPEAL_ALREADY_OPEN`. Filing creates the appeal in state `SUBMITTED` with `filed_at` and `filed_by_user_id` stamped, and moves the submission `REJECTED → APPEAL_PENDING`. A `METADATA_REJECTED` build is deliberately not appealable: filing from that state is `ERR_INVALID_SUBMISSION_STATE`. Appeals are filterable by `submission_id`, `state` and `appeal_type`.

A `PlatformAppealBoard` operator takes the appeal into `BOARD_REVIEW`, which requires the submission to be `APPEAL_PENDING` and stamps `board_started_at`, and then decides with an `outcome` of `OVERTURNED`, `PARTIALLY_OVERTURNED` or `UPHELD`, a `rationale`, and for the partial branch an `overturned_rejection_reason_ids` array. Both the board-review step and the decision require the acting operator to differ from the submission's `reviewed_by_user_id`: the reviewer who rejected the build may neither hear nor judge the appeal against it. `overturn_ratio` is the count of overturned reasons divided by the count of cited reasons, rounded to two decimals, and is null before any decision. The three outcomes diverge completely. `OVERTURNED` sets `overturned_rejection_reason_ids` to the full cited list and `overturn_ratio` to 1.00, marks every cited reason `OVERTURNED` so that it leaves the standing penalty scope, returns every feature mapping that the original rejection cascade drove to `FAILED` back to `IN_PROGRESS` — to `IN_PROGRESS`, not to `PASSED`, so the publisher must re-attest — recomputes the index and posture, and drives the submission `APPEAL_PENDING → APPROVED`. `PARTIALLY_OVERTURNED` requires `overturned_rejection_reason_ids` to be a non-empty *strict* subset of the cited list, otherwise `ERR_VALIDATION`; those reasons become `OVERTURNED` and the remainder `UPHELD`, only the mappings tied to overturned reasons return to `IN_PROGRESS`, and the submission goes back to `REJECTED` so the publisher may respond and return it to draft — critically, `rejected_at` is **not** refreshed, so the original appeal window keeps running and does not reopen. `UPHELD` marks every cited reason `UPHELD` with `overturn_ratio` 0.00, leaves all penalties in scope, and drives the submission to `REJECTED_FINAL`, which is terminal: that build can never be revived and the publisher must create a new submission with a higher `build_number`. A `ComplianceOfficer` may instead withdraw an appeal that is still `SUBMITTED`, optionally citing a `reason_code`; the appeal becomes `WITHDRAWN`, which is terminal, and the submission returns to `REJECTED`, again without refreshing `rejected_at`.

Both standing scores are recomputed on every submission decision, every appeal decision, every rollout completion, every rollback and every clock advance, and they share one definition with two scopes. The penalty set is every rejection reason recorded against this account's submissions whose `appeal_disposition` is not `OVERTURNED` and whose `recorded_at` falls inside the variant's window. Each carries weight 12 if `CRITICAL`, 6 if `MAJOR` and 2 if `MINOR`, multiplied by a decay factor. Credit is added: 3 points per appeal of the account in an overturning terminal state — `OVERTURNED` or `PARTIALLY_OVERTURNED` — decided inside the window, plus 2 points per rollout plan of the account's apps that reached `COMPLETED` inside the window on an app whose `rollback_count` is 0. The score is 100 minus the total penalty plus the total credit, rounded half up to two decimals once at the very end and clamped to 0.00 through 100.00; the sums themselves are computed unrounded. `standing_score_rolling_365` counts only rows dated at or after 365 days before the current instant, with the lower bound inclusive, and decays each penalty by 1.0 when it is at most 90 days old, 0.5 when older than 90 but at most 180 days, and 0.25 when older than 180 days, where age is the whole number of complete days since `recorded_at`. `standing_score_lifetime` has no window at all — every row of the account's history counts — and applies no decay whatsoever. For identical stored data the two therefore disagree as soon as any rejection is more than 90 days old. The distinction is load-bearing: the expedite eligibility floor reads the rolling score *only*, while the review priority calculation reads the lifetime score *only*, so an account can be simultaneously too poorly-regarded to expedite and comfortably prioritised, or the reverse.

### 3.10. Staged Release: Phases, Dwell, Exclusivity and Rollback

An approved build reaches users only through a rollout plan. A `ReleaseManager` creates one by supplying `expected_app_record_version` as a revision guard, the `app_record_id`, the `submission_id` of the approved build, a `mode` of `IMMEDIATE` or `PHASED`, and a `scheduled_start_at` instant. The plan's `mode` must equal the submission's `release_mode`, and a submission whose `release_mode` is `MANUAL` can never own a plan at all — that build is released outside the platform's staged machinery — so any mismatch, in either direction, is `ERR_ROLLOUT_MODE_MISMATCH`. A plan is created in state `SCHEDULED` with `phase_index` 0, `released_share_pct` 0.00 and `held_back_share_pct` 100.00, and it advances the app record's `version` by one. Its `projected_end_at` is derived: for a phased plan it is `scheduled_start_at` plus seven phases times 24 hours of minimum dwell; for an immediate plan it is `scheduled_start_at` itself. Plans are filterable by `app_record_id`, `submission_id`, `state` and `mode`.

Only one plan may occupy an app's release calendar at a time, and the interval arithmetic here uses the **opposite** convention from reviewer capacity. A plan's interval runs from `scheduled_start_at` to `projected_end_at` and is **closed at both ends**, so touching endpoints *do* conflict: a new plan starting exactly when an existing plan is projected to end is rejected, whereas back-to-back capacity windows are always legal. Against every other plan of the same app in state `SCHEDULED`, `PHASE_ACTIVE` or `PAUSED`, a conflict exists when the new start is at or before that plan's projected end and that plan's start is at or before the new projected end. Against a plan in `ROLLED_BACK` a one-sided cooldown applies instead: the new `scheduled_start_at` must be at or after that plan's `terminated_at` plus 72 hours, and the comparison is strict, so scheduling exactly at the buffer edge is legal. Plans in `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED` never conflict with anything. Any conflict is `ERR_ROLLOUT_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.

Starting a plan is where the two modes diverge most visibly. Starting requires the current instant to be at or after `scheduled_start_at`, with equality succeeding and anything earlier refused as `ERR_ROLLOUT_NOT_DUE`; the cited submission must still be `APPROVED`; the app's posture must be `OPEN`; and no other plan of the app may be `PHASE_ACTIVE` or `PAUSED`, otherwise `ERR_ROLLOUT_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. A phased plan then moves `SCHEDULED → PHASE_ACTIVE` with `phase_index` 0, `released_share_pct` 1.00, `held_back_share_pct` 99.00 and `phase_entered_at` stamped, and the app's `distribution_state` becomes `PHASED_ROLLOUT`. An immediate plan instead goes straight from `SCHEDULED` to `COMPLETED` in a single step, with `phase_index` at the final index, `released_share_pct` 100.00, `held_back_share_pct` 0.00 and `completed_at` stamped, and the app's `distribution_state` becomes `FULLY_RELEASED` — an immediate release never exposes a partial audience and never passes through `PHASED_ROLLOUT` at all. Either way `live_submission_id` is set to the plan's `submission_id` and the app record's `version` advances.

The phase ladder is fixed at seven audience shares: 1.00, 2.00, 5.00, 10.00, 20.00, 50.00 and 100.00 percent, at indices 0 through 6. A `ReleaseManager` advances by supplying `to_phase_index`, which must equal the current `phase_index` plus exactly one and must not exceed 6 — phases may neither be skipped nor repeated, and a violation is `ERR_PHASE_SEQUENCE_INVALID`. The dwell rule requires the current instant to be at or after `phase_entered_at` plus 24 hours, inclusively: advancing exactly 24 hours after entering the phase is legal, 23 hours and 59 minutes is not, and the refusal is `ERR_PHASE_DWELL_NOT_MET`. The posture must still be `OPEN`, otherwise `ERR_APP_COMPLIANCE_RESTRICTED`. A successful advance sets `phase_index`, re-stamps `phase_entered_at`, sets `released_share_pct` to the share at the new index and `held_back_share_pct` to 100.00 minus it, and mirrors both onto the app record. Advancing *to* index 6 is the loop's exit edge rather than another iteration: the plan moves `PHASE_ACTIVE → COMPLETED` with `released_share_pct` 100.00, `held_back_share_pct` 0.00 and `completed_at` stamped, and the app goes `PHASED_ROLLOUT → FULLY_RELEASED`.

A plan can be held. A `ReleaseManager` or a `PlatformPolicyAdmin` supplies an `action` of `PAUSE` or `RESUME` with an optional `pause_reason_code`. `PAUSE` moves `PHASE_ACTIVE → PAUSED`, records the reason — the code `POSTURE_DEGRADED` is what the compliance cascades write here — freezes both share fields exactly as they stand, and drives the app to `PAUSED`. `RESUME` moves `PAUSED → PHASE_ACTIVE`, requires the posture to be `OPEN` (otherwise `ERR_APP_COMPLIANCE_RESTRICTED`), clears `pause_reason_code`, and re-stamps `phase_entered_at` to the resume instant — which means the **full 24-hour dwell restarts from the resume**, so a pause does not bank time toward the next advance. Attempting to advance a paused plan is `ERR_INVALID_ROLLOUT_STATE` even though a `PHASE_ACTIVE` self-loop is otherwise legal, because the undeclared transition is refused on its own rung.

Rollback is the emergency lever, available to a `ReleaseManager` and a `PlatformPolicyAdmin`, exempt from the posture gate, and reachable from `PHASE_ACTIVE`, `PAUSED` or even `COMPLETED`. It takes a `rollback_reason_code` and a `halt_distribution` boolean. In every case the plan moves to `ROLLED_BACK`, `released_share_pct` returns to 0.00 with `held_back_share_pct` back to exactly 100.00 and no residue, `terminated_at` is stamped, the app's `rollback_count` increments, `live_submission_id` is cleared, and both standing scores are recomputed — a rollback costs the account the clean-rollout credit it would otherwise have earned. The boolean then decides two consequential things. With `halt_distribution` true the app's `distribution_state` becomes `ROLLED_BACK`, every `APPROVED` commerce item of the app whose `item_type` is `EXTERNAL_PAYMENT_LINK` is torn down to `REMOVED`, and the next plan for the app must respect the 72-hour cooldown. With `halt_distribution` false the app's `distribution_state` becomes `NOT_DISTRIBUTED` instead, no commerce item is touched, and no cooldown applies at all, so a replacement plan may be scheduled immediately. `ROLLED_BACK` and `CANCELLED` are terminal; a `SCHEDULED` plan is cancelled automatically, with `terminated_at` stamped, when its submission is superseded, and because no share field ever moved there is no audience accounting to undo.

### 3.11. The Commerce Catalogue and Commission Settlement

A `PublisherAdmin` catalogues each purchasable item by supplying the `app_record_id`, an `item_type` of `CONSUMABLE`, `NON_CONSUMABLE`, `SUBSCRIPTION` or `EXTERNAL_PAYMENT_LINK`, a `reference_name` unique within the app, a `price_tier_cents` of zero or more, an `entitlement_class` of `DIGITAL_CONTENT`, `PHYSICAL_GOOD` or `SERVICE`, and optionally an `external_link_url` and a `subscriber_retention_months` count that defaults to 0. The external-payment shape is policed on both creation and submission: an `EXTERNAL_PAYMENT_LINK` item requires a non-null `external_link_url`, a `price_tier_cents` of exactly 0, and an `entitlement_class` that is not `DIGITAL_CONTENT`, while every other `item_type` requires `external_link_url` to be null. Pure shape breaches — a non-zero price on an external link, a stray URL on an in-app purchase — are `ERR_VALIDATION` on the schema rung; the policy breaches are `ERR_EXTERNAL_LINK_NOT_PERMITTED`, namely selling digital content through an external link, and submitting an external link when the account's `ACTIVE` distribution certificate does not carry the `EXTERNAL_PAYMENT_LINK` entitlement. An item is created in `DRAFT` with `rejection_note` null, `reported_gross_cents` 0, `reported_period_label` null, `commission_rate_applied` null and `commission_cents` 0, and is filterable by `app_record_id`, `item_type`, `status`, `entitlement_class` and `reported_period_label`.

A `PublisherAdmin` submits an item for review, moving it `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` or `REJECTED → SUBMITTED` and clearing any `rejection_note`; submission is blocked by both the `FROZEN` and the `RESTRICTED` posture and re-checks the external-link entitlement. A `PlatformReviewer` then reviews with an `outcome` of `APPROVE` or `REJECT` and an optional `rejection_note`. `APPROVE` moves `SUBMITTED → APPROVED` and recomputes `commission_rate_applied` and `commission_cents` from whatever gross is already stored; `REJECT` moves `SUBMITTED → REJECTED` and requires a non-empty `rejection_note`, otherwise `ERR_VALIDATION`. The reviewing operator must differ from the `attested_by_user_id` recorded on any `COMMERCE`-category feature mapping of the same app, per §2.10. This queue is coupled to the build queue in one direction: a build may not be approved while any commerce item of its app is still `SUBMITTED`.

Revenue is reported once per item per period. A `PublisherAdmin` supplies `reported_gross_cents` and a `period_label`, and the operation requires the item to be `APPROVED` and the app to be distributing. A second report for the same item, or a report against a period already recorded in the account's `settlement_finalized_periods`, is refused with `ERR_REVENUE_ALREADY_REPORTED` — gross proceeds are stated exactly once, and commission is recomputed from that stored figure on every settlement read thereafter. A successful report appends the `period_label` to `settlement_finalized_periods` if it is not already present and recomputes the line's rate and commission.

Settlement itself is a read: a `PublisherAdmin`, a `PlatformPolicyAdmin` or an `Auditor` requests the settlement of one app for a `period_label`, and the platform computes without persisting anything new. The line items are the app's commerce items whose `status` is `APPROVED` and whose `reported_period_label` equals the requested period, ordered by `created_at` ascending — which pins the order of the per-line output, though the total does not depend on it. `REMOVED`, `REJECTED`, `DRAFT` and `SUBMITTED` items are excluded entirely, which is precisely how a halting rollback removes external-payment revenue from the next settlement. The base rate is 0.15 when the account's `commerce_program_tier` is `SMALL_BUSINESS` and 0.30 otherwise. Each line then adjusts that rate: a `SUBSCRIPTION` item whose `subscriber_retention_months` is 12 or more takes the lower of the base rate and the retained-subscriber rate of 0.15 — 12 months qualifies, 11 does not — and an `EXTERNAL_PAYMENT_LINK` item has 0.03 subtracted from its rate, rounded to two decimals. The resulting rate is stored as `commission_rate_applied`, and `commission_cents` is `reported_gross_cents` multiplied by that rate, rounded half up to a whole cent. The rounding order is pinned as round-per-line-then-sum: each line is rounded first, and the settlement total is the sum of the already-rounded line commissions. Summing the unrounded products and rounding once at the end is explicitly *not* the rule, and the two differ by whole cents whenever two or more lines each carry a half-cent remainder. The read also returns the gross total across the lines and the publisher net, which is the gross total minus the settlement commission. An empty line set yields a settlement commission of 0 and a publisher net of 0.
