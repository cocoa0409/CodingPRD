# Newborn Hearing Screening API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Every year a state early hearing detection and intervention (EHDI) program must account for every baby born inside its borders: was the newborn screened for hearing loss before it left the birth facility, and if the screen produced a **refer** result, did the family actually reach the outpatient rescreen, the diagnostic audiologic evaluation, and — where permanent hearing loss was confirmed — an early intervention program? The clinical protocol is well understood; the operational failure is not. Families disappear between the birth admission and the first outpatient appointment, out-of-hospital and home births never enter the pipeline at all, interpreter needs go unmet, and facilities file screening counts that do not reconcile with what the state can observe. This API is the system of record that closes those gaps.

The service tracks each newborn along two independent dimensions at once. The **screening dimension** follows the infant from an unscreened birth admission through pass, refer, diagnostic work-up, and a confirmed or excluded diagnosis, and it tolerates the unhappy path where a family is lost to follow-up and later re-engaged by outreach. The **intervention dimension** follows the separate question of whether a diagnosed infant was referred to, accepted by, and enrolled in an early intervention program. Around those two dimensions sit the supporting structures a real program needs: a clinic-slot and appointment ledger that protects families from being asked to attend two visits in the same day, a family-navigation work queue funded by a per-facility support budget, a delayed-onset risk registry that keeps surveillance alive for months after a passing screen, and a reporting-compliance regime in which facilities attest their own counts and the state assesses penalty points when those attestations diverge from observable reality.

Two design constraints shape the whole specification. First, all timeliness in this domain is measured in **age**, and age is not a single number: an infant born at 32 weeks of gestation, or one who spent a week in the NICU, is benchmarked on corrected age, while the figures the program publishes for federal comparability are always chronological. The system therefore computes and stores both answers side by side rather than choosing one. Second, the program is audited, so nothing may be silently overwritten: penalty assessments, attestations, and performance scores are recomputed from primary rows on a deterministic operations run, corrections supersede rather than mutate, and the two write paths that could clobber unseen follow-up work are guarded by optimistic concurrency. All resources are mounted as flat top-level collections directly under the service root; relationships between them are carried as identifier fields in request bodies rather than as nesting in URL paths.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contract-level rules that hold for every endpoint in the service: authentication, the response envelope, pagination, record properties, the logical clock, the age and timeliness machinery, numeric precision, cascade integrity, concurrency, and the order in which a request is validated.

### 2.1. Program Actors and Role-Based Access Control

Authentication is header-based. Every request must carry `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which that user acts. A request that omits either header, or whose `X-User-Role` is not one of the seven role names defined below, is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). A syntactically valid request whose role is not listed among the authorized roles for the addressed endpoint in §4 is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

The seven roles are `ProgramAdmin`, `ProgramAuditor`, `FacilityAdmin`, `FacilityScreener`, `Audiologist`, `Navigator`, and `EICoordinator`. A `ProgramAdmin` is a state EHDI program operator: it sets up the registry, opens and closes reporting periods, overrides early intervention placement, and administers penalties. A `ProgramAuditor` is a state compliance auditor: it reviews facility filings and reads every dashboard, but it never files a report, never closes a period, and never waives a penalty. A `FacilityAdmin` administers a birth facility or audiology clinic — registering providers and infants, publishing clinic slots, and filing period reports. A `FacilityScreener` is a birth-facility screening technician who records physiologic screens and registers newborns. An `Audiologist` performs, attests, and finalizes diagnostic evaluations and also runs delayed-onset surveillance screens. A `Navigator` is a family navigator who books and closes appointments, works navigation tasks, records family declines, and opens follow-up episodes. An `EICoordinator` is an early intervention program coordinator who accepts, rejects, and enrolls referrals on behalf of the program that employs them.

Roles differ in what `X-User-ID` means. When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramAdmin` or `ProgramAuditor`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it (for example as `accepted_by_user_id` on a facility report accepted by an auditor, and it compares two such recorded identifiers to each other when enforcing segregation of duties) but it never compares it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `FacilityAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Facility`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Facility` compare the resource's facility identifier field to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `FacilityScreener`, `Audiologist`, `Navigator`, or `EICoordinator`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Provider`, and ownership checks on any resource that references a `Provider` compare the resource's provider identifier field to `X-User-ID`.

Entity-backed identity is enforced beyond mere resolution. For a `FacilityAdmin`, `X-User-ID` must equal the `id` of the `Facility` the request acts for. For a `FacilityScreener`, `X-User-ID` must resolve to a `Provider` whose `provider_type` is `SCREENER` and whose `facility_id` equals the facility of the encounter or infant being acted on. For an `Audiologist`, it must resolve to a `Provider` whose `provider_type` is `AUDIOLOGIST`; for a `Navigator`, a `Provider` whose `provider_type` is `NAVIGATOR`; for an `EICoordinator`, a `Provider` whose `provider_type` is `EI_COORDINATOR` and whose `facility_id` equals the addressed referral's `ei_program_facility_id`. Any failure of these identity conditions is rejected with `ERR_IDENTITY_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403) — distinct from `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`, which concerns the role name alone.

Two operations are subject to hard segregation of duties, described in detail in §3.6 and §3.9: the audiologist who attests a diagnostic evaluation must not be the provider recorded as having performed it, and the operator who waives a facility report's penalty must not be the operator who accepted that report. Both violations surface as `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, its minimum accepted value is 1, and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls how many rows are skipped before the page begins: it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400).

Unless a specific list states otherwise, rows are returned in ascending `created_at` order — oldest first. List responses populate `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more` in the `meta` object, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the request's filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` indicates whether any matching row remains beyond the returned page. Single-resource responses omit those four fields.

### 2.4. Registry Record Properties

Every persisted resource carries a server-minted `id`, which is a UUID string with no type prefix or other decoration, and a `created_at` timestamp recording when the row was first written. Timestamps everywhere in this API — stored fields, request bodies, and response bodies alike — are ISO 8601 instants in UTC with three fractional-second digits.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, refreshed on every mutation of the row, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation. `Facility`, `Provider`, `Infant`, `FollowUpEpisode`, `ClinicSlot`, `RescreenAppointment`, `DiagnosticEvaluation`, `EarlyInterventionReferral`, `RiskFactorEntry`, `NavigationTask`, `ReportingPeriod`, and `FacilityReport` all behave this way. `ScreeningEncounter` is the single exception: a screening encounter is an append-only clinical event with no update path at all, so it carries only `id` and `created_at` — it has neither an `updated_at` field nor a `version` field, and no operation ever modifies a screening encounter once it has been recorded. Corrections to screening history are expressed by recording a further encounter that names the earlier one in `prior_encounter_id`, never by editing the original.

Because relationships are flat, resources are addressed by single-segment paths and identifiers travel in request bodies. Where a path does contain an identifier, it is resolved before any body-level work happens (see §2.11).

### 2.5. The Logical Program Clock

The service does not read the host's wall clock. Every age computation, benchmark comparison, appointment window check, submission deadline, and surveillance due-date test is evaluated against a **logical clock** held by the server. At startup the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; until it is explicitly moved it stays at that instant and never falls back to real time.

Any authenticated role may move the clock by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant to the clock-setting capability described in §4. Setting the clock mutates no entity and triggers no cascade; it only changes the instant against which subsequent requests evaluate their temporal rules. The response reports the new instant as `current_utc`. Because the clock is the single source of "now", tests and operators can replay a whole birth-to-enrollment journey deterministically.

Several temporal rules follow directly from the clock. An infant's `birth_datetime` may not be later than the logical clock, and a violation is rejected with `ERR_BIRTH_DATETIME_IN_FUTURE` (HTTP 422). Event instants must fall inside the closed range that begins at the infant's `birth_datetime` and ends at the logical clock — this covers a screening encounter's `performed_at`, a diagnostic evaluation's `evaluation_datetime`, a risk entry's `identified_at`, the `discharge_datetime` recorded at discharge, and the `ifsp_date` recorded at enrollment — and a violation of any of them is rejected with `ERR_TIMESTAMP_OUT_OF_RANGE` (HTTP 422).

### 2.6. Corrected Versus Chronological Age: the Dual Basis

All milestone timeliness in this program is a function of the infant's age, and the program deliberately maintains two answers to the question "how old is this baby?"

The **chronological age in days** is the number of whole 86400-second days that have elapsed from the infant's `birth_datetime` to a reference instant, with any partial day discarded — that is, the elapsed seconds divided by 86400 and rounded down to a whole number. It is exposed as `chronological_age_days`. The **prematurity offset in days** is the number of weeks by which the birth fell short of term gestation, multiplied by seven, and floored at zero: term gestation is 40 completed weeks, so an infant of 32 completed weeks carries an offset of 56 days, while an infant of 40 or more completed weeks carries an offset of 0. It is exposed as `prematurity_offset_days`. The **corrected age in days** is the chronological age minus the prematurity offset, floored at zero, and is exposed as `corrected_age_days`.

Which of the two the program treats as clinical truth depends on the infant. The `benchmark_age_basis` is `CORRECTED` when the infant's `gestational_age_weeks` is below 35 or the infant's `nicu_stay_days` is 5 or more, and `CHRONOLOGICAL` otherwise. The `effective_age_days` is the corrected age when the basis is `CORRECTED` and the chronological age when the basis is `CHRONOLOGICAL`. The reference instant is the logical clock for a live read, and the milestone's own instant for a stored status — a screening encounter's `performed_at`, an evaluation's `finalized_at`, or a referral's `ifsp_date`, as applicable.

Two variants of this basis are used, and they are not interchangeable. The **applied basis** uses `effective_age_days` and is what every field the program treats as clinical truth is measured against: a follow-up episode's `timeliness_status`, a diagnostic evaluation's `timeliness_status`, a referral's `ei_timeliness_status`, the loss-to-follow-up sweep performed at period close, and the age-pressure term of the loss-to-follow-up risk score. The **chronological basis** always uses `chronological_age_days` and ignores the prematurity offset entirely; it is what the program publishes for federal comparability, namely a diagnostic evaluation's `timeliness_status_chronological_basis` and the chronological-basis figure on the per-infant timeliness panel. The two genuinely disagree: a 32-week infant whose evaluation is finalized at 130 chronological days is `ON_TIME` under the applied basis, because its corrected age is 74 days, and `LATE` under the chronological basis — two stored answers on one row, by design.

The per-infant timeliness panel described in §4 returns `infant_id`, `chronological_age_days`, `corrected_age_days`, `effective_age_days`, `prematurity_offset_days`, `benchmark_age_basis`, `screening_timeliness_status`, `diagnostic_timeliness_status`, `diagnostic_timeliness_status_chronological_basis`, `ei_timeliness_status`, `ltf_risk_score`, and `priority_band` — the screening figure being the timeliness of the infant's screening-track episode, the two diagnostic figures being the applied-basis and chronological-basis statuses of its diagnostic evaluation, and the early intervention figure being the enrollment timeliness of its referral.

### 2.7. Milestone Benchmarks and Timeliness Classification

Three benchmarks govern the program's 1-3-6 expectations, each expressed in days of age. A screening milestone — that is, an episode whose `episode_type` is `INITIAL_SCREEN` or `RESCREEN` — is benchmarked at 30 days. A diagnostic milestone is benchmarked at 90 days. Early intervention enrollment is benchmarked at 180 days. A single grace band of 45 days sits above every benchmark and distinguishes reporting tiers only; it never re-opens a record.

Given a benchmark and an age evaluated at the milestone instant under the requested basis, the status is assigned as follows. If the milestone has been completed, it is `ON_TIME` when the age is less than or equal to the benchmark, and `LATE` in every other case — both when the age exceeds the benchmark but is no greater than the benchmark plus 45 days, and when the age exceeds the benchmark plus 45 days. A completed milestone, however late, is never `OVERDUE`. If the milestone has not yet been completed, the status is `PENDING` when the age is less than or equal to the benchmark and `OVERDUE` once the age exceeds the benchmark. The benchmark boundary is inclusive: a diagnostic finalized at exactly 90 days of applied age is `ON_TIME`, and one finalized at 91 days is `LATE`.

Statuses are written when a milestone completes — episode closure, diagnostic finalization, early intervention enrollment — and open episodes are re-evaluated by the period-close checkpoint described in §3.9.

### 2.8. Precision and Rounding

The program publishes exactly one class of fractional number: performance scores and the component rates behind them. Each component rate is rounded to 4 decimal places using round-half-up. The three rounded rates are then each multiplied by their weight, and the three products are summed at full precision — the products themselves are never rounded. The sum is multiplied by 100 and rounded once, to 2 decimal places, using round-half-up. Weighting, summing, and then rounding at the very end is the pinned order; rounding each weighted product to two decimals before summing is explicitly not the rule.

Everything else in the program is integer arithmetic. Ages are whole days obtained by flooring on whole seconds. Navigation support units are whole units. Loss-to-follow-up risk points are integers end to end, and the clamp that keeps the score inside its range is applied once to the summed raw total, never term by term. Penalty points are integers, and the single rounding operation in the penalty assessment — round-half-up — is applied to the surcharge-multiplied base points and to nothing else.

### 2.9. Cascade Integrity and Conservation Invariants

Many operations in this program touch more than one row. A single screening encounter can resolve an episode, cancel that episode's booked appointment, release the slot capacity that appointment held, cancel the interpreter task linked to it, credit navigation units back to the funding facility, and rewrite the infant's risk score and priority band. All such propagation is immediate and synchronous: by the time a request returns, every row the cascade names has reached its new state, and a subsequent read observes the finished result. There is no deferred or eventual settlement anywhere in this API.

Whenever a cascade mutates a row that carries `version`, that row's `version` increments and its `updated_at` is refreshed, exactly as if the row had been written directly. This is what makes the two optimistic-concurrency guards in §2.10 meaningful: a client that read an infant before a cascade touched it holds a stale version number afterwards.

Two conservation invariants hold at every observable instant. **Slot capacity conservation**: a clinic slot's `booked_count` always equals the number of its appointments whose `status` is `BOOKED` or `ATTENDED`, its `remaining_capacity` always equals `total_capacity` minus `booked_count` and is never negative, and its `status` is `OPEN` whenever `remaining_capacity` is at least 1 and `FULL` when it is 0. An appointment that becomes `ATTENDED` continues to occupy capacity; one that becomes `NO_SHOW` or `CANCELLED` releases it. **Navigation budget conservation**: a facility's `navigation_budget_units_remaining` plus the sum of `units_reserved` across its navigation tasks whose `status` is `UNASSIGNED`, `OPEN`, or `COMPLETED` always equals its `navigation_budget_units_total`. A task that is cancelled releases its units; a task that is completed does not, because completed work has been consumed.

Determinism matters as much as correctness here. Wherever the specification sweeps or ranks a set of rows, the ordering key is `created_at` — ascending unless stated otherwise — and row identifiers are never used as an ordering or tie-breaking key.

### 2.10. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Two write paths can legitimately supersede work that a client may not have observed, and both are guarded by optimistic concurrency against the parent row's `version`.

Opening a follow-up episode requires a non-optional `expected_infant_version` field in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Infant`. If that value does not equal the infant's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_INFANT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The guard exists because episode creation legitimately supersedes the infant's currently open episode, so a create built on a version the server has already moved past would silently discard follow-up work the writer never saw.

Filing a facility report requires a non-optional `expected_facility_version` field in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Facility`. If that value does not equal the facility's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_FACILITY_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A corrected filing legitimately supersedes the prior report for the same period, so a submission built on a stale view of the facility — its posture, its penalty balance, or an already-accepted report having changed in the meantime — must be refused rather than layered on top.

No other endpoint in this API accepts a version field of any kind. Requests to endpoints outside these two must not be expected to carry one, and supplying one elsewhere has no effect.

### 2.11. Request Validation Order

When a single request violates several rules at once, the response is decidable: checks are evaluated strictly in the order below, and the **first** failing check determines the error and status code. Later checks never mask earlier ones.

1. Header authentication — `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` present and the role recognized, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. Role authorization — the acting role appears among the endpoint's authorized roles in §4, else `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`.
3. Request schema on every write endpoint — every required body field present; every enum value inside its declared set; every integer inside its declared range; every datetime a well-formed ISO 8601 UTC instant; `visit_starts_at` and `visit_ends_at` present when and only when `task_type` is `HOME_VISIT`; `ifsp_date` present on an enrollment; `points_delta` non-zero. A violation yields `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).
4. Pagination bounds on every list endpoint, else `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`.
5. Path identifier resolution, validated left to right across the path — facility, then infant, then encounter, then episode, then slot, then appointment, then evaluation, then referral, then risk entry, then task, then period, then report. The first unresolvable segment determines the code: `ERR_FACILITY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INFANT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ENCOUNTER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EPISODE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SLOT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPOINTMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EVALUATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REFERRAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TASK_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_FOUND`, or `ERR_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`, each HTTP 404.
6. Body foreign-key resolution — `birth_facility_id`, `facility_id`, `provider_id`, `performed_by_provider_id`, `infant_id`, `episode_id`, `slot_id`, `appointment_id`, `prior_encounter_id`, `supersedes_episode_id`, `supersedes_report_id`, `reporting_period_id`, `assigned_provider_id`, and `ei_program_facility_id` must all resolve, yielding the same HTTP 404 codes as above (`ERR_FACILITY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INFANT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EPISODE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SLOT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPOINTMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ENCOUNTER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`). A missing `linked_navigation_task_id` or navigation-task identifier yields `ERR_TASK_NOT_FOUND`.
7. Entity-backed identity, as specified in §2.1, else `ERR_IDENTITY_MISMATCH`.
8. `expected_infant_version` agreement on episode creation, else `ERR_STALE_INFANT_VERSION`.
9. `expected_facility_version` agreement on report filing, else `ERR_STALE_FACILITY_VERSION`.
10. through 15. State preconditions — already-discharged infants (`ERR_INFANT_ALREADY_DISCHARGED`), episode schedulability (`ERR_EPISODE_NOT_SCHEDULABLE`), appointment openness (`ERR_APPOINTMENT_NOT_OPEN`), the action-specific state of evaluations, referrals, tasks, reports and periods (`ERR_EVALUATION_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_REFERRAL_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_TASK_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_REPORT_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_PERIOD_ALREADY_CLOSED`), facility suspension (`ERR_FACILITY_SUSPENDED`), and chronological period closing (`ERR_EARLIER_PERIOD_OPEN`) — in that relative order.
16. through 19. Safety and compliance — guardian consent (`ERR_GUARDIAN_CONSENT_REFUSED`), segregation of duties (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`), threshold/outcome agreement (`ERR_OUTCOME_THRESHOLD_MISMATCH`), and the open-risk-factor requirement for a surveillance screen (`ERR_NO_OPEN_RISK_FACTOR`) — in that relative order.
20. through 25. Referential fitness — provider credential standing (`ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_CREDENTIALED`), provider type fitness (`ERR_PROVIDER_TYPE_MISMATCH`), slot type fitness (`ERR_SLOT_TYPE_MISMATCH`), cross-reference linkage integrity (`ERR_LINKAGE_MISMATCH`), the diagnostic-track requirement on the infant (`ERR_INFANT_NOT_REFERRED`), and facility type fitness (`ERR_FACILITY_TYPE_MISMATCH`) — in that relative order.
26. through 31. Temporal rules — `ERR_BIRTH_DATETIME_IN_FUTURE`, `ERR_TIMESTAMP_OUT_OF_RANGE`, `ERR_SLOT_IN_PAST`, `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_ENDED`, `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`, and `ERR_PERIOD_OVERLAP` — in that relative order.
32. through 38. Structural constraints — `ERR_SLOT_OVERLAP`, `ERR_FAMILY_BURDEN_BUFFER`, `ERR_NAVIGATOR_TRAVEL_BUFFER`, `ERR_ATTEMPT_LIMIT_REACHED`, `ERR_ACTIVE_EPISODE_EXISTS`, the duplicate guards (`ERR_DUPLICATE_BIRTH_SCREEN` and `ERR_DUPLICATE_REPORT`), and `ERR_NEGATIVE_PENALTY_BALANCE` — in that relative order.
39. through 42. Capacity and budget — `ERR_SLOT_FULL`, then `ERR_NAVIGATION_BUDGET_EXHAUSTED`, then `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_NAVIGATOR`, then `ERR_NAVIGATOR_AT_CAPACITY`.

The practical consequence is worth stating plainly. A booking request that names a slot of the wrong type **and** violates the 24-hour family-burden buffer **and** targets a full slot returns `ERR_SLOT_TYPE_MISMATCH`, because slot-type fitness sits in the referential band and both of the others sit below it. A booking that violates the family-burden buffer and also exceeds the attempt ceiling returns `ERR_FAMILY_BURDEN_BUFFER`. An unauthenticated request to a non-existent facility returns `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`, never a 404.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

The nine subsections below follow a newborn's journey through the program — from the network of facilities and providers that must exist before any baby is registered, through screening, follow-up scheduling, diagnosis and early intervention linkage, and finally to the compliance machinery by which the state assesses the facilities that did or did not do the work. Cross-cutting concerns are woven into the stage where they bite rather than gathered into a single block, so a rule that governs both booking and navigation appears where the operator meets it.

### 3.1. Standing Up the Program Network: Facilities, Providers and Clinic Capacity

Nothing can be recorded until the state program registers the facilities that participate. A facility is created by a `ProgramAdmin` with a legal `name` of 1 to 120 characters, a `facility_type` drawn from `BIRTH_HOSPITAL`, `BIRTHING_CENTER`, `MIDWIFE_PRACTICE`, `AUDIOLOGY_CLINIC`, and `EI_PROGRAM`, and a `region_code` drawn from `R1`, `R2`, `R3`, and `R4`. The region code is not decorative: it is the matching key used by both the navigator selector and the early intervention program selector described later. Registration also accepts an optional `navigation_budget_units_total`, the number of family-navigation support units the facility may have reserved at any one time; it defaults to 40 and must lie between 0 and 999 inclusive. A newly registered facility starts with `navigation_budget_units_remaining` equal to `navigation_budget_units_total`, a `penalty_points_balance` of 0, a `manual_adjustment_points_total` of 0, a `reporting_compliance_status` of `COMPLIANT`, and null in `performance_score`, `encounter_basis_performance_score`, and `last_closed_period_label` — the last three stay null until the first reporting period that assesses the facility closes.

Facility type constrains where a facility may appear. An `EI_PROGRAM` facility files no period reports and registers no births, and it is the only kind of facility that may be named as an `ei_program_facility_id`; clinic slots belong to `AUDIOLOGY_CLINIC` facilities. Naming a facility whose `facility_type` does not fit its role in the request — an early intervention program named as a birth facility on a newborn registration, a non-`EI_PROGRAM` facility named as the destination of a referral placement, a facility that is not an audiology clinic publishing slots, or an `EI_PROGRAM` facility filing a period report — is rejected with `ERR_FACILITY_TYPE_MISMATCH`.

Providers are the people. Registering one requires the employing `facility_id`, a `provider_type` of `SCREENER`, `AUDIOLOGIST`, `NAVIGATOR`, or `EI_COORDINATOR`, a `full_name` of 1 to 120 characters, and the `region_code` the provider covers, which is matched against the region of an infant's birth facility when navigation work is allocated. Three optional attributes carry defaults: `languages`, a non-empty array drawn from `EN`, `ES`, `ZH`, `VI`, `AR`, `ASL`, and `OTHER`, defaulting to a single-element array containing `EN`; `credential_status`, one of `ACTIVE`, `LAPSED`, and `SUSPENDED`, defaulting to `ACTIVE`; and `max_open_tasks`, the navigator's workload ceiling, defaulting to 5 and constrained to the range 1 to 50 inclusive. The server also maintains `open_task_count`, initialised to 0, which always equals the number of navigation tasks assigned to that provider whose status is `OPEN`.

Credential standing is enforced wherever a provider is put to work. Any provider that performs a screening encounter, that is named as the performer of a diagnostic evaluation, that attests an evaluation, that staffs a clinic slot, or that is assigned a navigation task — whether the assignment happens at task creation or on a later assignment — must have `credential_status` of `ACTIVE`; a `LAPSED` or `SUSPENDED` provider is rejected with `ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_CREDENTIALED`. Type fitness is enforced across an overlapping but distinct set of five operations: a screening encounter may be performed by a `SCREENER` or an `AUDIOLOGIST`; a diagnostic evaluation must be performed and attested by an `AUDIOLOGIST`, and an `AUDIOLOGIST` is also the only kind of provider who may staff a clinic slot; a navigation task may only be held by a `NAVIGATOR`, whether named at creation or at assignment. A mismatch yields `ERR_PROVIDER_TYPE_MISMATCH`.

Publishing an audiology clinic slot pins a `facility_id`, the `provider_id` of the audiologist staffing it, a half-open window whose `starts_at` is inclusive and whose `ends_at` is exclusive, and a `slot_type` of either `RESCREEN` or `DIAGNOSTIC` declaring which episode types may book into it. An optional `total_capacity` — the number of appointments the slot can hold concurrently — defaults to 1 and must lie between 1 and 20 inclusive. A new slot begins with `booked_count` at 0, `remaining_capacity` equal to `total_capacity`, and `status` `OPEN`. An `ends_at` that is not strictly after `starts_at` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`. Slots are exclusive per staffing provider under half-open semantics: a new slot for a provider is rejected with `ERR_SLOT_OVERLAP` when that provider already staffs a slot whose window intersects the new one — that is, when the new window's start falls strictly before the existing window's end and the existing window's start falls strictly before the new window's end. Windows that merely touch, where the new slot's `starts_at` equals the existing slot's `ends_at`, are permitted and are the normal way a clinic publishes back-to-back capacity.

### 3.2. Registering a Newborn and the Out-of-Hospital Capture Pathways

Registration puts a baby on the EHDI registry. The mandatory attributes are the `birth_facility_id` of the facility of record — for a home birth, the midwife practice — the `birth_datetime` as an ISO 8601 UTC instant, the `birth_setting`, the completed `gestational_age_weeks` in the range 22 to 44 inclusive, the `guardian_name` of 1 to 120 characters, and a `guardian_contact_phone` of 7 to 20 characters that family-navigation outreach will dial. Four further attributes are optional: `nicu_stay_days`, the NICU length of stay in days, defaulting to 0 and constrained to 0 through 365; `preferred_language`, drawn from `EN`, `ES`, `ZH`, `VI`, `AR`, `ASL`, and `OTHER` and defaulting to `EN`, which governs every piece of program communication with the family; `interpreter_required`, a boolean defaulting to false; and `guardian_consent_status`, one of `PENDING`, `GRANTED`, and `REFUSED`, defaulting to `GRANTED`. A `birth_datetime` later than the logical clock is refused with `ERR_BIRTH_DATETIME_IN_FUTURE`.

The `birth_setting` is the capture pathway, and it does real work. Its permitted values are `INPATIENT`, `BIRTHING_CENTER`, `OUT_OF_HOSPITAL`, and `HOME_BIRTH`. A newly registered infant always starts with `screening_status` `NOT_SCREENED` and `intervention_status` `NOT_APPLICABLE`, and its `ltf_risk_score` and `priority_band` are computed immediately per §3.8. When the setting is `OUT_OF_HOSPITAL` or `HOME_BIRTH`, there will be no discharge screen to catch the baby, so the registry opens the follow-up case at once: a `FollowUpEpisode` of `episode_type` `INITIAL_SCREEN` is created in `PENDING_SCHEDULING` with `benchmark_days` of 30 and a `due_date` 30 days after the infant's `birth_datetime`. This is the out-of-hospital capture pathway, and it is why a home-birth baby appears on a navigator's worklist within seconds of registration.

Registration also seeds the surveillance registry in one case. When `nicu_stay_days` is 5 or more, the server mints a `RiskFactorEntry` with `risk_factor_code` `NICU_5_PLUS_DAYS`, `source` `REGISTRY_AUTO`, `identified_at` equal to the infant's `birth_datetime`, and `status` `ACTIVE`, and computes that entry's `surveillance_due_date` by the rule in §3.8. The same threshold of 5 NICU days independently forces the corrected-age basis described in §2.6, so a long NICU stay both extends surveillance and relaxes the milestone clock.

Discharge from the birth facility is recorded as its own event, supplying the `discharge_datetime` to stamp on the infant. The stamp is what freezes the before-discharge screening denominator that facility performance is later measured against, so it may be recorded only once: a second attempt on an infant whose `discharge_datetime` is already set is rejected with `ERR_INFANT_ALREADY_DISCHARGED`. If the infant's `screening_status` was still `NOT_SCREENED` at the moment of discharge, `discharged_unscreened` is set to true, and — provided the infant has no episode in `PENDING_SCHEDULING`, `SCHEDULED`, or `ESCALATED_DIAGNOSTIC` — an `INITIAL_SCREEN` episode is created in `PENDING_SCHEDULING` so the baby is chased in the community rather than lost. Discharge then recomputes `ltf_risk_score` and `priority_band` and increments the infant's `version` by 1.

Family communication preferences remain editable after registration. A `ProgramAdmin`, a `FacilityAdmin`, or a `Navigator` may revise any subset of `preferred_language`, `interpreter_required`, `guardian_contact_phone`, and `guardian_consent_status` on the infant. Every such revision recomputes `ltf_risk_score` and `priority_band` — importantly, the language-gap term re-evaluates the navigator pool of the birth facility's region against the new language and interpreter flag, so flipping `interpreter_required` on for a family whose language nobody in the region speaks visibly raises the score — and increments the infant's `version` by 1. Setting `guardian_consent_status` to `REFUSED` here is the mechanism by which a family blocks diagnostic result submission, as §3.6 explains.

Infants are discoverable as a worklist filtered by `birth_facility_id`, by either state dimension — `screening_status` or `intervention_status` — by `priority_band`, and by `birth_setting`, which is how a program officer pulls up every home birth in `PENDING_SCHEDULING` at once.

### 3.3. The Physiologic Screen and the Pass / Refer Fork

A screening encounter is the program's atomic clinical event. Recording one — the province of a `FacilityScreener` or an `Audiologist` — requires the `infant_id` being screened, the `facility_id` where the screen happened, the `performed_by_provider_id` who performed it, the `encounter_type`, the physiologic `method`, the `performed_at` instant, and the per-ear findings `left_ear_result` and `right_ear_result`. `encounter_type` is one of `BIRTH_ADMISSION`, `OUTPATIENT_RESCREEN`, `HOME_VISIT`, and `SURVEILLANCE`; `method` is either `OAE` or `AABR`; each ear result is `PASS`, `REFER`, or `NOT_TESTED`. Three optional linkage identifiers may accompany the encounter: `episode_id`, naming the follow-up episode this screen answers, which is null for a birth-admission screen; `appointment_id`, naming the booked appointment this screen fulfils, which is null for a walk-in or inpatient screen; and `prior_encounter_id`, naming the earlier screen this one repeats, which forms the self-referential attempt chain and is null for a first screen. At most one `BIRTH_ADMISSION` encounter may ever exist for a given infant — a second is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_BIRTH_SCREEN`.

The encounter's verdict is derived, never supplied. When both `left_ear_result` and `right_ear_result` are `PASS`, `overall_result` is `PASS`. When both ears are `NOT_TESTED`, `overall_result` is `INCOMPLETE`. In every other combination `overall_result` is `REFER` — which means a single untested ear cannot certify a pass, because `NOT_TESTED` in exactly one ear is treated as a refer for that ear. `INCOMPLETE` is handled identically to `REFER` by every state transition in this specification; it exists as a separate value only so facility dashboards can distinguish protocol failures from genuine refers. Two further fields are computed on the encounter row: `before_discharge` is true when the `encounter_type` is `BIRTH_ADMISSION` and either the infant has no `discharge_datetime` yet or the `performed_at` instant is at or before that `discharge_datetime`, and it is what the cohort-basis screening rate counts; `age_days_at_encounter` is the number of whole days from the infant's `birth_datetime` to `performed_at`, computed as the elapsed seconds divided by 86400 with the remainder discarded.

A suspended facility cannot screen. Recording any encounter at a facility whose `reporting_compliance_status` is `SUSPENDED` is rejected with `ERR_FACILITY_SUSPENDED`, which is the sharp end of the compliance regime in §3.9. Linkage must also hold together: the encounter's `episode_id`, when supplied, must name an episode of the same infant, and its `appointment_id`, when supplied, must name an appointment on that same episode; either disagreement is `ERR_LINKAGE_MISMATCH`. A linked episode must be in `SCHEDULED` or `ESCALATED_DIAGNOSTIC` for the encounter to attach to it, failing which the request is refused with `ERR_EPISODE_NOT_SCHEDULABLE`, and a referenced appointment must still be `BOOKED`, failing which it is refused with `ERR_APPOINTMENT_NOT_OPEN`.

Consider the ordinary happy path. A screener records a birth-admission screen with both ears passing while the infant is still `NOT_SCREENED`. The infant advances `NOT_SCREENED → PASSED`, its `ltf_risk_score` and `priority_band` are recomputed, and — if the baby was one of the out-of-hospital captures and therefore already carried an `INITIAL_SCREEN` episode in `PENDING_SCHEDULING` or `SCHEDULED` — that episode closes as `RESOLVED_PASS` with `closure_reason` `RESCREEN_PASSED`. Because that episode closed, any appointment on it still in `BOOKED` becomes `CANCELLED` and the slot it occupied decrements its `booked_count`, freeing the capacity for another family. A home-visit screen with a passing result drives exactly the same transition and the same cascade.

Now the fork. When a birth-admission or home-visit screen produces `REFER` or `INCOMPLETE`, a four-link cascade runs. First the infant advances `NOT_SCREENED → REFERRED`. Second, because the screening dimension became `REFERRED`, and only if no episode of that infant currently sits in `PENDING_SCHEDULING`, `SCHEDULED`, or `ESCALATED_DIAGNOSTIC`, the system opens a `FollowUpEpisode` with `episode_type` `RESCREEN`, `status` `PENDING_SCHEDULING`, `benchmark_days` of 30, a `due_date` 30 days after the infant's `birth_datetime`, and `owner_scope` `FACILITY`. Third, because that episode now exists and contributes an age-pressure term, `ltf_risk_score` is recomputed and `priority_band` is rewritten. Fourth — and only when the recomputed band is `CRITICAL` — the system creates an `OUTREACH_CALL` navigation task against the new episode and assigns it through the navigator selector of §3.5. The infant's `version` increments by 1. A referred screen at a birthing centre or a home birth therefore very often lands on a navigator's queue in the same request that recorded the refer, because the setting weight alone pushes those babies toward the critical band.

Outpatient rescreens close the loop. An `OUTPATIENT_RESCREEN` encounter whose `episode_id` names an episode of that infant in `SCHEDULED` and whose derived result is `PASS` advances the infant `REFERRED → PASSED`; the named episode closes as `RESOLVED_PASS` with `closure_reason` `RESCREEN_PASSED` and its `timeliness_status` written per §2.7 against the 30-day screening benchmark; the encounter's `appointment_id` appointment moves `BOOKED → ATTENDED`, and the slot's `booked_count` is deliberately left unchanged, because an attended appointment still occupies its capacity. Risk and band are recomputed. If instead the rescreen produces `REFER` or `INCOMPLETE` on an episode whose `episode_type` is `INITIAL_SCREEN` or `RESCREEN`, the appointment still becomes `ATTENDED`, but the episode advances `SCHEDULED → ESCALATED_DIAGNOSTIC` — its `episode_type` is **not** mutated and its `benchmark_days` stays 30 for the purposes of its own timeliness — and the infant advances `REFERRED → DIAGNOSTIC_IN_PROGRESS`. Risk and band are recomputed. This escalated episode is the one a diagnostic evaluation attaches to; a separate `DIAGNOSTIC` episode is created only when the diagnostic pathway is entered directly from a birth-admission refer.

Encounters whose `encounter_type` is `SURVEILLANCE` serve the delayed-onset registry and are governed by §3.8. The encounter log is queryable by `infant_id`, `facility_id`, `performed_by_provider_id`, `episode_id`, `appointment_id`, `prior_encounter_id`, `encounter_type`, and `overall_result`, the last two making it straightforward to audit every refer at a facility or to walk an infant's whole repeat chain.

### 3.4. Follow-Up Episodes: Scheduling, Attempts, Loss and Re-Engagement

A follow-up episode is the unit of accountability for chasing a milestone. It names its `infant_id` and an `episode_type` of `INITIAL_SCREEN`, `RESCREEN`, or `DIAGNOSTIC`, and it may name a `supersedes_episode_id` — the infant's currently open episode that this one replaces. Its `status` moves through `PENDING_SCHEDULING`, `SCHEDULED`, `ESCALATED_DIAGNOSTIC`, and `LOST_TO_FOLLOW_UP` and settles in one of five terminal values: `RESOLVED_PASS`, `RESOLVED_DIAGNOSED`, `RESOLVED_NO_LOSS`, `CLOSED_DECLINED`, or `SUPERSEDED`. Its `owner_scope` is `FACILITY` at birth and becomes `STATE_PROGRAM` when the responsible facility's compliance posture degrades; its `escalation_flag` is false until a period close escalates it. Its `benchmark_days` is 30 when `episode_type` is `INITIAL_SCREEN` or `RESCREEN` and 90 when it is `DIAGNOSTIC`, and its `due_date` is the infant's `birth_datetime` advanced by that many days, counted as exact 86400-second multiples rather than by calendar arithmetic. Its `attempt_count` always equals the number of appointments on it whose status is `BOOKED`, `ATTENDED`, or `NO_SHOW`. Its `timeliness_status` is written per §2.7. When it leaves the active set, `closure_reason` records why — one of `RESCREEN_PASSED`, `DIAGNOSIS_CONFIRMED`, `DIAGNOSIS_NO_LOSS`, `FAMILY_DECLINED`, `MOVED_OUT_OF_STATE`, `SUPERSEDED`, or `LOST_TO_FOLLOW_UP` — and `closed_at` records the logical-clock instant of closure; both are null while the episode is active.

Opening an episode is a `ProgramAdmin`, `FacilityAdmin`, or `Navigator` action, and it carries the `infant_id`, the `episode_type`, the `expected_infant_version` guard of §2.10, and optionally the `supersedes_episode_id`. An infant may not carry two episodes in `PENDING_SCHEDULING`, `SCHEDULED`, or `ESCALATED_DIAGNOSTIC` at once: a second create that does not name the open one in `supersedes_episode_id` is rejected with `ERR_ACTIVE_EPISODE_EXISTS`, and a `supersedes_episode_id` that belongs to a different infant is rejected with `ERR_LINKAGE_MISMATCH`. Where supersession does apply, the named episode must itself be in `PENDING_SCHEDULING` or `SCHEDULED`; it advances to `SUPERSEDED` with `closure_reason` `SUPERSEDED` and `closed_at` set to the logical clock, and any appointment on it still in `BOOKED` becomes `CANCELLED`, releasing the slot capacity it held. Creating a `DIAGNOSTIC` episode for an infant whose `screening_status` is `REFERRED` also advances that infant `REFERRED → DIAGNOSTIC_IN_PROGRESS`. Every episode creation recomputes `ltf_risk_score` and `priority_band` and increments the infant's `version` by 1.

Booking an episode into a clinic slot is how `PENDING_SCHEDULING` becomes `SCHEDULED`. The request names the `episode_id` and the `slot_id` and may set `interpreter_needed`. Six conditions guard it, and §2.11 fixes their relative order: the episode must be in `PENDING_SCHEDULING`, else `ERR_EPISODE_NOT_SCHEDULABLE`; the slot's `slot_type` must be `RESCREEN` for an `INITIAL_SCREEN` or `RESCREEN` episode and `DIAGNOSTIC` for a `DIAGNOSTIC` episode, else `ERR_SLOT_TYPE_MISMATCH`; the slot's `starts_at` must be strictly after the logical clock, else `ERR_SLOT_IN_PAST`; no other appointment of the same infant whose status is `BOOKED` or `ATTENDED` may sit on a slot whose `starts_at` is strictly less than 24 hours away from the requested slot's `starts_at` in either direction — the absolute difference must be at least 86400 seconds, and exactly 24 hours apart is allowed — else `ERR_FAMILY_BURDEN_BUFFER`; the episode's `attempt_count` must be below 3, else `ERR_ATTEMPT_LIMIT_REACHED`; and the slot must have `remaining_capacity` of at least 1, that is `status` `OPEN`, else `ERR_SLOT_FULL`. The buffer exists to protect families from being summoned twice in a day, which is itself a driver of no-shows.

A successful booking creates a `RescreenAppointment` in `BOOKED`, copies `infant_id` from the episode so that family-burden checks stay single-hop, increments the slot's `booked_count` by 1 and re-derives its `remaining_capacity` and `status`, re-derives the episode's `attempt_count`, advances the episode `PENDING_SCHEDULING → SCHEDULED`, and recomputes the infant's `ltf_risk_score` and `priority_band`. When `interpreter_needed` is true, the same request additionally mints an `INTERPRETER_SESSION` navigation task against this episode, debits that task's `units_reserved` from the birth facility's `navigation_budget_units_remaining` per §3.5, and writes the task's identifier into the appointment's `linked_navigation_task_id`; when `interpreter_needed` is false or omitted, `linked_navigation_task_id` stays null.

Appointments do not survive contact with reality unchanged, so closing one is a first-class operation taking a `disposition` of either `NO_SHOW` or `CANCELLED` and an optional `reason` that is stored as the appointment's `disposition_reason`. The appointment must still be `BOOKED`, else `ERR_APPOINTMENT_NOT_OPEN`, and a `NO_SHOW` may only be recorded once the slot's `starts_at` is at or before the logical clock — one cannot fail to attend a visit that has not begun. Either disposition decrements the slot's `booked_count` by 1 and re-derives its `remaining_capacity` and `status`, so a no-show releases capacity just as a cancellation does, and either disposition cancels the appointment's `linked_navigation_task_id` interpreter task when that task is still in `UNASSIGNED` or `OPEN`, crediting its `units_reserved` back to the funding facility. A `NO_SHOW` alone re-derives the infant's `missed_appointment_count` upward by one and recomputes `ltf_risk_score` and `priority_band` with the 6-point-per-no-show term. In both cases the episode returns `SCHEDULED → PENDING_SCHEDULING`, and the family may be re-booked — up to three counted attempts, since `attempt_count` continues to count `NO_SHOW` attempts but not `CANCELLED` ones.

An episode may also be closed administratively by a `ProgramAdmin` or `Navigator` when the family will not continue, supplying a `closure_reason` of `FAMILY_DECLINED` or `MOVED_OUT_OF_STATE`. The episode must be in `PENDING_SCHEDULING` or `SCHEDULED`, else `ERR_EPISODE_NOT_SCHEDULABLE`. It advances to `CLOSED_DECLINED` with the supplied reason and `closed_at` set to the logical clock, any appointment on it still in `BOOKED` becomes `CANCELLED` and releases its slot capacity, that appointment's linked interpreter task is cancelled with its units credited back to the funding facility, and the infant's `ltf_risk_score` and `priority_band` are recomputed.

The failure mode the whole program exists to prevent is loss to follow-up. During the period-close checkpoint of §3.9, an episode still in `PENDING_SCHEDULING` whose applied-basis effective age exceeds its `benchmark_days` plus 45 days and whose `attempt_count` has reached 3 is moved `PENDING_SCHEDULING → LOST_TO_FOLLOW_UP` with `closure_reason` `LOST_TO_FOLLOW_UP`, `closed_at` at the logical clock, and `timeliness_status` `OVERDUE`; the infant's `screening_status` follows it to `LOST_TO_FOLLOW_UP`, its risk and band are recomputed, and the period's `episodes_lost_count` increments by 1.

Loss is not permanent. When a navigator completes an outreach task with an outcome of `FAMILY_REENGAGED`, a three-link cascade runs: that infant's episode in `LOST_TO_FOLLOW_UP` returns to `PENDING_SCHEDULING` with `closure_reason` and `closed_at` both cleared to null while `escalation_flag` and `owner_scope` are preserved; because the episode left `LOST_TO_FOLLOW_UP`, the infant advances `LOST_TO_FOLLOW_UP → REFERRED` and its `reengagement_count` increments by 1; and because the infant changed, its `ltf_risk_score` and `priority_band` are recomputed, now with the navigation-relief term applying because a live task exists. An infant may be lost and re-engaged repeatedly — this is the deliberate cycle in the screening dimension.

Episodes are discoverable by `infant_id`, by `supersedes_episode_id`, by `status`, by `episode_type`, by `owner_scope`, and by `timeliness_status`, which is how the state program pulls every `OVERDUE` episode it has taken ownership of. Appointments are discoverable by `episode_id`, `slot_id`, `infant_id`, `linked_navigation_task_id`, and `status`; slots by `facility_id`, `provider_id`, `slot_type`, and `status`.

### 3.5. Family Navigation, Interpreter Support and the Support Budget

Navigation is the program's human glue: somebody phones the family, arranges an interpreter, drives to the house, or walks the guardian through an appointment. Each such piece of work is a navigation task naming the `infant_id` it serves, an optional `episode_id` for the follow-up case it supports — null for early intervention linkage work, which serves the infant rather than an episode — and a `task_type` of `OUTREACH_CALL`, `INTERPRETER_SESSION`, `HOME_VISIT`, `EI_LINKAGE`, or `APPOINTMENT_NAVIGATION`. A home visit, and only a home visit, must carry the window fields `visit_starts_at` and `visit_ends_at`, with the end strictly after the start; supplying either field on a task of any other type, or omitting them on a home visit, is a schema violation reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and a window whose end is at or before its start is `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`. On creation the server copies `funding_facility_id` from the infant's `birth_facility_id` — that facility's budget pays for the work regardless of where the work happens — and the task begins with `status` `UNASSIGNED`, `assigned_provider_id` null, `outcome` null, `contacted_language` null, and `closure_note` null.

Support units are the currency. The cost of a task is a base of 2 units, plus a further 3 units when the infant's `interpreter_required` flag is set, plus a further 4 units when the `task_type` is `HOME_VISIT`. An interpreter-flagged home visit therefore costs 9 units and a plain outreach call costs 2. If the funding facility's `navigation_budget_units_remaining` is at least the required amount, the task's `units_reserved` is set to that amount, the facility's remaining budget is debited by it, and `budget_shortfall_flag` stays false. If the budget is short, the outcome depends on who asked. An operator-initiated task — one created directly, or a booking that asked for interpreter support — is rejected outright with `ERR_NAVIGATION_BUDGET_EXHAUSTED`, because the program will not promise support it cannot fund. A task minted by a cascade, whether the `EI_LINKAGE` task of a confirmed diagnosis, the `OUTREACH_CALL` task of a newly critical infant, or the interpreter task attached to a booking cascade, is instead created with `units_reserved` of 0 and `budget_shortfall_flag` set to true: the task still exists, still appears on a queue, and still relieves the infant's risk score, but its funding is visibly unmet. §2.9 states the conservation invariant these two paths must respect.

Assignment is selector-driven. The request may name an `assigned_provider_id` explicitly, may set `auto_assign` to true to let the server choose, or may do neither and leave the task queued in `UNASSIGNED` for later. When the server chooses, it builds a candidate pool of every provider whose `provider_type` is `NAVIGATOR`, whose `credential_status` is `ACTIVE`, whose `region_code` equals the region code of the infant's birth facility, whose `open_task_count` is strictly below their `max_open_tasks`, and who can serve the family linguistically — meaning either the infant's `interpreter_required` is false, or the infant's `preferred_language` appears in the provider's `languages` array. Any provider excluded by a preceding attempt on the same task is dropped from the pool. The pool is then ordered by `open_task_count` ascending, with ties broken by `max_open_tasks` descending and any remaining ties by `created_at` ascending; provider identifiers are never consulted as a tie-break. The first row of that ordering is chosen. When the pool is empty, an operator-initiated assignment is rejected with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_NAVIGATOR`, whereas a cascade-minted task is simply left in `UNASSIGNED` with `assigned_provider_id` null, sitting in a deferred queue until somebody assigns it. When the request names a navigator explicitly, that navigator must still satisfy the type, credential, and workload conditions: one already holding `max_open_tasks` tasks in `OPEN` is rejected with `ERR_NAVIGATOR_AT_CAPACITY`.

Home visits carry a travel buffer. A `HOME_VISIT` task is rejected when the chosen navigator already holds a `HOME_VISIT` task in `UNASSIGNED` or `OPEN` whose closed window comes within 45 minutes of the requested window on either side; put positively, the assignment is permitted only when the new visit's `visit_starts_at` is at or after the other visit's `visit_ends_at` plus 45 minutes, or the other visit's `visit_starts_at` is at or after the new visit's `visit_ends_at` plus 45 minutes. A violation is `ERR_NAVIGATOR_TRAVEL_BUFFER`, and the rule is enforced both when the task is created with a navigator and when a queued task is later assigned to one.

Whichever path assigns it, a task advances `UNASSIGNED → OPEN`, its `assigned_provider_id` is written, the holding provider's `open_task_count` is re-derived upward by one, and the infant's `ltf_risk_score` and `priority_band` are recomputed — a live task earns the navigation-relief term. Assigning a task that is not in `UNASSIGNED` is rejected with `ERR_TASK_INVALID_STATE`.

Closing a task supplies a `disposition` of `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED`, and, for a completion, an `outcome` drawn from `CONTACTED`, `FAMILY_REENGAGED`, `NO_CONTACT`, `INTERPRETER_PROVIDED`, and `LINKED`; a `contacted_language` — one of `EN`, `ES`, `ZH`, `VI`, `AR`, `ASL`, or `OTHER`, recording what language was actually spoken with the family — is required whenever the outcome is `CONTACTED`, `FAMILY_REENGAGED`, or `INTERPRETER_PROVIDED`. A `COMPLETED` task keeps its `units_reserved`: completed work has been consumed, not released, so the funding facility's remaining budget does not recover. A `CANCELLED` task stores the supplied `reason` as its `closure_note` and credits its `units_reserved` back to the funding facility's `navigation_budget_units_remaining`. Either disposition on a task that was `OPEN` re-derives the holding provider's `open_task_count` downward by one, and either recomputes the infant's `ltf_risk_score` and `priority_band` — note that the relief term survives completion, because a `COMPLETED` task still counts as navigation having reached this family, and disappears only once no task outside `CANCELLED` remains. Closing a task that is already `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED` is rejected with `ERR_TASK_INVALID_STATE`. Completing with `FAMILY_REENGAGED` triggers the re-engagement cascade of §3.4.

Tasks are discoverable by `infant_id`, `episode_id`, `funding_facility_id`, `assigned_provider_id`, `status`, and `task_type`; because so many tasks are born inside cascades, that list is the primary channel through which an operator finds work the system created on its own, and the `budget_shortfall_flag` on each row tells the program office which of that work is unfunded.

### 3.6. Diagnostic Audiologic Evaluation: Submission, Attestation and Finalization

A diagnostic evaluation is opened by a `ProgramAdmin`, `FacilityAdmin`, or `Audiologist` against the infant's diagnostic-track episode, naming the `infant_id`, the `episode_id` of that episode, the `facility_id` of the audiology clinic performing the work, the `performed_by_provider_id` of the audiologist performing it, the `evaluation_datetime` of the session, and the diagnostic `method`, which is one of `DIAGNOSTIC_ABR`, `ASSR`, and `BEHAVIORAL`. The evaluation's `episode_id` must name an episode of the same infant, else `ERR_LINKAGE_MISMATCH`. A newly opened evaluation is in `DRAFT` with `left_ear_threshold_db`, `right_ear_threshold_db`, `outcome`, `attested_by_provider_id`, `attestation_note`, `finalized_at`, and `age_days_at_finalize` all null, and with both `timeliness_status` and `timeliness_status_chronological_basis` at `PENDING`.

Results are supplied in a second step, reserved to the `Audiologist` role, which submits `left_ear_threshold_db` and `right_ear_threshold_db` — integers in dB nHL constrained to the range 0 through 120 inclusive — together with the clinical `outcome`, one of `NO_LOSS`, `UNILATERAL_LOSS`, and `BILATERAL_LOSS`. Four conditions guard the submission. The evaluation must be in `DRAFT`, else `ERR_EVALUATION_INVALID_STATE`. The infant's `guardian_consent_status` must not be `REFUSED`, else `ERR_GUARDIAN_CONSENT_REFUSED` — a family that has refused consent cannot have diagnostic results recorded against the baby. The infant's `screening_status` must be `REFERRED` or `DIAGNOSTIC_IN_PROGRESS`, else `ERR_INFANT_NOT_REFERRED`, since diagnostic work only makes sense on the diagnostic track. And the outcome must agree with the thresholds under the 25 dB rule: `NO_LOSS` requires both ear thresholds to be at most 25 dB, `UNILATERAL_LOSS` requires exactly one of the two to exceed 25 dB, and `BILATERAL_LOSS` requires both to exceed 25 dB. A contradiction is rejected with `ERR_OUTCOME_THRESHOLD_MISMATCH`. The performing provider must also still hold `credential_status` `ACTIVE`. On success the thresholds and outcome are stored and the evaluation advances `DRAFT → PENDING_ATTESTATION`.

Attestation is second-reader review, and it is where segregation of duties bites. The attesting request supplies a `decision` of either `ACCEPT` or `RETURN` and an optional `note`. The evaluation must be in `PENDING_ATTESTATION`, else `ERR_EVALUATION_INVALID_STATE`, and the acting `X-User-ID` must resolve to a provider whose `provider_type` is `AUDIOLOGIST` with `credential_status` `ACTIVE` and must not equal the evaluation's `performed_by_provider_id` — an audiologist may not attest their own work, and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. On `ACCEPT`, `attested_by_provider_id` is set to the acting user, the supplied note is stored as `attestation_note`, and the evaluation advances `PENDING_ATTESTATION → ATTESTED`. On `RETURN`, the evaluation goes back `PENDING_ATTESTATION → DRAFT` with `left_ear_threshold_db`, `right_ear_threshold_db`, and `outcome` all cleared to null and the note stored in `attestation_note`, so the performing audiologist must re-enter corrected results; this revision loop may run as many times as review requires, and the same non-identity condition applies on every pass.

Finalization is the moment the program commits to a diagnosis, and it is the sharpest branch in the specification. Only an `Audiologist` may finalize, the evaluation must be in `ATTESTED`, else `ERR_EVALUATION_INVALID_STATE`, and the performing facility's `reporting_compliance_status` must not be `SUSPENDED`, else `ERR_FACILITY_SUSPENDED`. Both branches write the same timeliness block: `finalized_at` is set to the logical clock, `age_days_at_finalize` records the infant's chronological age in days at that instant, and both `timeliness_status` (under the applied age basis) and `timeliness_status_chronological_basis` (under the chronological variant) are evaluated against the 90-day diagnostic benchmark per §2.6 and §2.7.

Where the branches diverge is everything else. When the `outcome` is `UNILATERAL_LOSS` or `BILATERAL_LOSS`, the evaluation advances `ATTESTED → FINALIZED_LOSS` and a six-link cascade runs. Because the evaluation reached `FINALIZED_LOSS`, its episode advances to `RESOLVED_DIAGNOSED` with `closure_reason` `DIAGNOSIS_CONFIRMED`, `closed_at` at the logical clock, and its own `timeliness_status` written — and any appointment on that episode still in `BOOKED` becomes `CANCELLED`, releasing slot capacity. Because that episode resolved, the infant's `screening_status` advances `DIAGNOSTIC_IN_PROGRESS → DIAGNOSED_LOSS` and, on the independent intervention dimension, `intervention_status` advances `NOT_APPLICABLE → REFERRAL_DUE`. Because `intervention_status` became `REFERRAL_DUE`, an `EarlyInterventionReferral` is minted in `PENDING` with its `ei_program_facility_id` chosen by the program selector of §3.7, and `intervention_status` immediately advances `REFERRAL_DUE → REFERRED`. Because that referral row now exists, an `EI_LINKAGE` navigation task is created and assigned through the navigator selector of §3.5, with its `units_reserved` debited from the infant's birth facility. And because that task now exists, the infant's `ltf_risk_score` is recomputed with the navigation-relief term and its `priority_band` rewritten. All six links complete before the request returns.

When the `outcome` is `NO_LOSS`, the evaluation advances `ATTESTED → FINALIZED_NO_LOSS` and the cascade is deliberately much shorter. The linked episode advances to `RESOLVED_NO_LOSS` with `closure_reason` `DIAGNOSIS_NO_LOSS` and `closed_at` at the logical clock, its `timeliness_status` written; the infant's `screening_status` advances to `DIAGNOSED_NO_LOSS`, which is the terminal state of the screening dimension for a cleared baby; and `ltf_risk_score` and `priority_band` are recomputed. `intervention_status` is left untouched at `NOT_APPLICABLE`: **no** early intervention referral and **no** `EI_LINKAGE` task is created on this branch. The two branches share one trigger and one precondition set, and diverge completely in their effects — an implementation that mints a referral on the `NO_LOSS` path has misread the fork.

Evaluations are discoverable by `infant_id`, `episode_id`, `facility_id`, `performed_by_provider_id`, `attested_by_provider_id`, and `status`, the fifth of those being how a program office audits who attested what.

### 3.7. Early Intervention Referral, Program Placement and Enrollment

A referral row is the intervention dimension's paperwork. It carries the `infant_id`, the `diagnostic_evaluation_id` of the `FINALIZED_LOSS` evaluation that generated it, and the `ei_program_facility_id` of the early intervention program it was routed to. Its `status` starts at `PENDING` and settles at `ENROLLED`, `DECLINED_BY_FAMILY`, or `REJECTED_BY_PROGRAM`, passing through `ACCEPTED` on the happy path. `referred_at` records the logical-clock instant at which the finalize cascade created the row; `accepted_at` records program acceptance and is null before it; `ifsp_date` records the Individualized Family Service Plan date supplied at enrollment; `enrollment_age_days` and `ei_timeliness_status` are written at enrollment and are null and `PENDING` respectively before it; and `resolution_reason` holds the reason text recorded with a rejection or a family decline, and is null otherwise.

Placement is a waterfall. The selector first builds a regional tier: every facility whose `facility_type` is `EI_PROGRAM`, whose `reporting_compliance_status` is `COMPLIANT` or `WATCH`, and whose `region_code` equals the region code of the infant's birth facility, minus any facility excluded by a previous attempt on this diagnosis. If that regional tier is empty — and only then — it falls back to a statewide tier of every `EI_PROGRAM` facility that is `COMPLIANT` or `WATCH` regardless of region, again minus the exclusions. The regional tier is always exhausted before the statewide tier is considered; the two are never merged. Whichever tier supplies the pool, candidates are ordered by the number of referrals currently sitting at that facility in `PENDING` or `ACCEPTED`, ascending, so the least-loaded program wins, with ties broken by `created_at` ascending and never by identifier. The first row is chosen. Programs in `PROBATION` or `SUSPENDED` are not eligible at all. When both tiers come up empty, the referral is created with `ei_program_facility_id` null; the infant still advances to `intervention_status` `REFERRED`, and the unplaced referral waits for a `ProgramAdmin` to place it by hand.

That manual placement supplies an `ei_program_facility_id` and is available only while the referral's `ei_program_facility_id` is still null — addressing a referral that already has a program is rejected with `ERR_REFERRAL_INVALID_STATE`. The named facility must have `facility_type` `EI_PROGRAM`, else `ERR_FACILITY_TYPE_MISMATCH`. Placement writes the identifier and leaves `status` at `PENDING`; it is a routing fix, not a decision.

Decisions come in three flavours, all expressed through a single `decision` field accompanied by an optional `reason`. `ACCEPT` is an `EICoordinator` action: the referral must be `PENDING`, its `ei_program_facility_id` must not be null, and the acting user must be a provider whose `provider_type` is `EI_COORDINATOR` employed by exactly that program facility. Acceptance advances `PENDING → ACCEPTED` and stamps `accepted_at` with the logical clock. `REJECT` is also an `EICoordinator` action on a `PENDING` referral by a coordinator of the named program; it advances `PENDING → REJECTED_BY_PROGRAM` with the supplied reason stored in `resolution_reason`, and then runs the intervention dimension's cycle inside the same request: the infant's `intervention_status` returns `REFERRED → REFERRAL_DUE`, a replacement referral is minted in `PENDING` for the same `diagnostic_evaluation_id` with the selector re-run and the rejecting facility excluded from the candidate pool — the replacement is created with `ei_program_facility_id` null when the post-exclusion pool is empty — and then the infant's `intervention_status` advances `REFERRAL_DUE → REFERRED` again, completing the loop. `FAMILY_DECLINED` is a `Navigator` action available while the referral is `PENDING` or `ACCEPTED`: it advances the referral to `DECLINED_BY_FAMILY` with the reason stored in `resolution_reason`, moves the infant's `intervention_status` to `DECLINED`, and cancels any `EI_LINKAGE` navigation task for that infant still in `UNASSIGNED` or `OPEN`, crediting its `units_reserved` back to the funding facility's `navigation_budget_units_remaining`. A decision that does not match the referral's current status — accepting or rejecting anything other than a `PENDING` referral, or family-declining one that is already terminal — is rejected with `ERR_REFERRAL_INVALID_STATE`.

Enrollment is reserved to the `EICoordinator` and is the terminal step of the intervention dimension. It supplies the `ifsp_date`. The referral must be in `ACCEPTED`, else `ERR_REFERRAL_INVALID_STATE`; the referral's `diagnostic_evaluation_id` row must be in `FINALIZED_LOSS`, because an infant cannot be enrolled on an unconfirmed diagnosis; and the `ifsp_date` must fall at or after the referral's `referred_at` and at or before the logical clock, else `ERR_TIMESTAMP_OUT_OF_RANGE`. On success the referral advances `ACCEPTED → ENROLLED`; `enrollment_age_days` is written as the infant's applied-basis age in days at enrollment; `ei_timeliness_status` is written against the 180-day early intervention benchmark under the applied basis per §2.7; the infant's `intervention_status` advances `REFERRED → ENROLLED`; every `EI_LINKAGE` navigation task for that infant still in `OPEN` advances to `COMPLETED` with `outcome` `LINKED`, keeping its `units_reserved` because completed linkage work has been consumed rather than released; and the infant's `ltf_risk_score` and `priority_band` are recomputed.

Referrals are discoverable by `infant_id`, `diagnostic_evaluation_id`, `ei_program_facility_id`, and `status`. Since every referral in the system is born inside the finalize cascade rather than from an operator request, that list is also the discovery channel by which a coordinator learns a new baby has been routed to their program.

### 3.8. The Delayed-Onset Risk Registry and Loss-to-Follow-Up Risk Scoring

Passing a newborn screen does not settle the question forever. A cohort of infants carries indicators for delayed-onset hearing loss, and the program keeps them under surveillance for months. A registry entry names the `infant_id`, a `risk_factor_code` drawn from `CMV`, `FAMILY_HISTORY`, `CRANIOFACIAL_ANOMALY`, `SYNDROME`, `BACTERIAL_MENINGITIS`, `ECMO`, `HYPERBILIRUBINEMIA_EXCHANGE`, `OTOTOXIC_MEDICATION`, and `NICU_5_PLUS_DAYS`, and the `identified_at` instant at which the factor was documented, which must fall at or after the infant's `birth_datetime` and at or before the logical clock. An optional `source` records who documented it — `BIRTH_FACILITY`, `AUDIOLOGY`, or `REGISTRY_AUTO` — and defaults to `BIRTH_FACILITY`; `REGISTRY_AUTO` marks the rows the NICU auto-registration described in §3.2 mints on the program's own initiative.

The surveillance clock is set at creation. For the three highest-risk codes — `CMV`, `BACTERIAL_MENINGITIS`, and `ECMO` — the entry's `surveillance_due_date` is the infant's `birth_datetime` advanced by exactly 180 days; every other code is given exactly 270 days. Days here are exact 86400-second multiples, never calendar months. A new entry begins in `ACTIVE` with `surveillance_result` and `surveillance_recorded_at` both null, re-derives the infant's `risk_factor_count`, and recomputes the infant's `ltf_risk_score` and `priority_band`.

Entries age forward under the period-close checkpoint of §3.9, which sweeps the registry in ascending `created_at` order. An entry in `ACTIVE` advances to `SURVEILLANCE_DUE` once the logical clock has reached the 30-day window that precedes its `surveillance_due_date` — that is, once the clock is at or after the due date minus 30 days and still at or before the due date. An entry in `SURVEILLANCE_DUE` advances to `SURVEILLANCE_OVERDUE` once the clock passes the due date. An entry still in `ACTIVE` when the clock has already passed the due date — the case where no close run observed it during its window — goes straight to `SURVEILLANCE_OVERDUE`. None of these three transitions changes the infant's `risk_factor_count`, because `ACTIVE`, `SURVEILLANCE_DUE`, and `SURVEILLANCE_OVERDUE` all count as open, so no risk recomputation is required by the sweep itself.

Surveillance is discharged by an `Audiologist` recording an encounter whose `encounter_type` is `SURVEILLANCE`. Such an encounter may only be recorded for an infant who has at least one registry entry in `ACTIVE`, `SURVEILLANCE_DUE`, or `SURVEILLANCE_OVERDUE`; otherwise it is refused with `ERR_NO_OPEN_RISK_FACTOR`. When the derived `overall_result` is `PASS`, **every** open entry of that infant advances to `CLEARED` with `surveillance_result` `PASS` and `surveillance_recorded_at` at the logical clock, and because `risk_factor_count` has dropped, the infant's `ltf_risk_score` and `priority_band` are recomputed. When the derived result is `REFER` or `INCOMPLETE`, every open entry of that infant advances to `LOSS_IDENTIFIED` with `surveillance_result` `REFER`; because a delayed-onset refer has been recorded, the infant's `screening_status` returns to `REFERRED` — from `PASSED`, from `DIAGNOSED_NO_LOSS`, or even from `LOST_TO_FOLLOW_UP` — a fresh `FollowUpEpisode` of `episode_type` `DIAGNOSTIC` is created in `PENDING_SCHEDULING`, and risk and band are recomputed. `CLEARED` and `LOSS_IDENTIFIED` are the two terminal states of a registry entry. An infant's registry is readable on its own, filtered by entry `status`.

**Loss-to-follow-up risk scoring.** The `ltf_risk_score` is the program's triage signal, an integer between 0 and 100 recomputed by every transition whose narrative says risk is recomputed — registration, discharge, contact-preference revision, every screening encounter, every episode creation, closure, escalation, loss, and re-engagement, every appointment booking, no-show, and cancellation, every navigation task creation, assignment, and closure, every registry entry creation and clearance, and every period close. Seven terms are summed, all in integer arithmetic.

The **setting term** reflects how hard the baby will be to reach: an `INPATIENT` birth contributes 0 points, a `BIRTHING_CENTER` birth 8, an `OUT_OF_HOSPITAL` birth 12, and a `HOME_BIRTH` 15. The **language-gap term** contributes 10 points when the infant's `interpreter_required` is true and there is no provider at all whose `provider_type` is `NAVIGATOR`, whose `credential_status` is `ACTIVE`, whose `region_code` matches the region code of the infant's birth facility, and whose `languages` array contains the infant's `preferred_language`; when an interpreter is not required, or when at least one such navigator exists, the term is 0. The **missed-appointment term** is 6 points for each no-show, capped at 24 points, so a fourth no-show adds nothing — formally, the smaller of 24 and 6 times the infant's `missed_appointment_count`. The **risk-factor term** is 4 points for each open registry entry, capped at 16 points — the smaller of 16 and 4 times the count of that infant's entries in `ACTIVE`, `SURVEILLANCE_DUE`, or `SURVEILLANCE_OVERDUE`.

The next two terms depend on the infant's single active episode, defined as the episode of that infant whose `status` is `PENDING_SCHEDULING`, `SCHEDULED`, or `ESCALATED_DIAGNOSTIC` with the most recent `created_at`; identifiers are never consulted to pick it. If the infant has no such episode, both terms are 0. Otherwise, let the benchmark be that episode's `benchmark_days` and the age be the infant's applied-basis `effective_age_days` evaluated at the logical clock. The **age-pressure term** is 0 while the age is at or below the benchmark minus 10 days, becomes 12 points once the age is above the benchmark minus 10 days but still at or below the benchmark, and becomes 20 points once the age exceeds the benchmark. The **escalation term** adds 6 points when that active episode's `escalation_flag` is true and 0 when it is false. Finally, the **navigation-relief term** subtracts 8 points whenever the infant has at least one navigation task whose `status` is `UNASSIGNED`, `OPEN`, or `COMPLETED`; cancelled tasks do not count, and neither do cleared or loss-identified registry entries count toward the risk-factor term.

The seven terms are added together, and the resulting raw total is then clamped once — never term by term — to lie between 0 and 100 inclusive: any negative total becomes 0 and any total above 100 becomes 100. The `priority_band` is read off the final score: `CRITICAL` when the score is 45 or more, `HIGH` when it is at least 30 but below 45, `ELEVATED` when it is at least 15 but below 30, and `LOW` otherwise. A home-birth baby with an interpreter need nobody in the region can meet, one no-show, and an episode already past its benchmark sits at 15 plus 10 plus 6 plus 20, minus 8 if a navigator is on the case — comfortably `CRITICAL` either way, which is exactly the population the program wants surfaced first.

### 3.9. Provider Reporting Compliance, Penalties and the Period-Close Checkpoint

The state cannot audit what it cannot compare, so facilities attest their own screening counts for a window of time and the program reconciles those attestations against the encounters it can observe. A reporting period is opened by a `ProgramAdmin` with a `period_start`, a `period_end`, and a `submission_due_datetime`. The window is half-open: `period_start` is inclusive and `period_end` is exclusive, `period_end` must be strictly after `period_start`, and `submission_due_datetime` must be at or after `period_end` — a violation of either ordering is `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`. A new window must not intersect the half-open window of any existing period, else `ERR_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. A fresh period starts in `OPEN` with `closed_at` null and all three run counters — `facilities_assessed_count`, `episodes_escalated_count`, and `episodes_lost_count` — at 0.

Filing a report is a `FacilityAdmin` action and the only place `expected_facility_version` appears. The filing names the `facility_id`, the `reporting_period_id`, the facility's own `attested_screened_count` and `attested_refer_count` — integers in the range 0 through 99999 inclusive — and optionally a `supersedes_report_id` naming the incumbent filing this corrected one replaces. At most one non-`SUPERSEDED` report may exist per facility and period: a second filing that does not name the incumbent in `supersedes_report_id` is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_REPORT`, and a `supersedes_report_id` belonging to a different facility or a different period is rejected with `ERR_LINKAGE_MISMATCH`. The superseded report must itself be in `SUBMITTED` or `RETURNED_FOR_CORRECTION`; it advances to `SUPERSEDED` and thereby drops out of both the penalty assessment and the facility's `penalty_points_balance` entirely. Each filing increments the facility's `version` by 1, which is precisely why the next filing needs a fresh `expected_facility_version`.

Against the attested figures the server computes its own. `actual_screened_count` is the number of distinct infants having a `BIRTH_ADMISSION` encounter at that facility inside the period window, and `actual_refer_count` is the number of that facility's encounters inside the window whose `overall_result` is `REFER`. The `variance_case_count` is the absolute difference between attested and actual screened counts added to the absolute difference between attested and actual refer counts. `submitted_at` is stamped with the logical clock, and `submitted_late` is true when `submitted_at` is later than the period's `submission_due_datetime`. A filed report begins in `SUBMITTED` with `auto_generated` false, `accepted_by_user_id` null, `review_note` null, `assessed_penalty_points` 0, and `waiver_reason` null.

Auditor review is the `ProgramAuditor`'s exclusive province and supplies a `decision` of `ACCEPT` or `RETURN` with an optional `note` stored as `review_note`. An auditor may only act while the report's period is still `OPEN`; addressing a report whose period has closed is rejected with `ERR_PERIOD_ALREADY_CLOSED`. `ACCEPT` applies to a report in `SUBMITTED` or `RETURNED_FOR_CORRECTION` and advances it to `ACCEPTED`, recording the acting `X-User-ID` in `accepted_by_user_id` and recomputing `actual_screened_count`, `actual_refer_count`, and `variance_case_count` from the period window — acceptance freezes the counts. `RETURN` applies only to a report in `SUBMITTED` and advances it to `RETURNED_FOR_CORRECTION`, storing the note and assessing **no** penalty: returning a filing keeps it open for a superseding correction rather than punishing it. Reviewing a report in any other status is rejected with `ERR_REPORT_INVALID_STATE`.

Penalties are assessed only at period close, once per assessed facility, and the arithmetic is order-sensitive. The report of record for a facility and period is the one whose `status` is not `SUPERSEDED` with the latest `created_at`. If that report has `auto_generated` true — meaning the facility filed nothing and the close run minted a non-filing report on its behalf — the variance is taken to be the report's `actual_refer_count`, because with no attested counts at all every refer case is an unreported case, and the base points are twice that variance plus a flat 5 points for the missing report. Otherwise the variance is the report's `variance_case_count`, and the base points are twice that variance plus 3 further points when `submitted_late` is true and nothing when it is not. The carryover is the facility's `penalty_points_balance` as observed **before** this assessment writes anything. The surcharge tier is keyed on the pre-surcharge total, that is the base points added to the carryover: a total of 4 or less attracts no surcharge, a total from 5 through 9 attracts a 25 percent surcharge, and a total of 10 or more attracts a 50 percent surcharge. The surcharge then multiplies the **base points only** — never the carryover — and that single product is rounded half-up to a whole number to give the report's `assessed_penalty_points`. The facility's new `penalty_points_balance` is the carryover plus the assessed points. Reading the tier off the post-surcharge total, or applying the surcharge to the carryover as well, are both explicitly not the rule.

Posture follows the balance immediately, wherever the balance moves: `reporting_compliance_status` is `COMPLIANT` while `penalty_points_balance` is 4 or less, `WATCH` from 5 through 9, `PROBATION` from 10 through 17, and `SUSPENDED` at 18 or more. A facility's balance is always the sum of `assessed_penalty_points` across its reports in `ACCEPTED` plus its `manual_adjustment_points_total`. `SUSPENDED` is not cosmetic: it blocks every new screening encounter at that facility and blocks diagnostic finalization there, as §3.3 and §3.6 describe, and it also removes an early intervention program from the placement pool of §3.7.

Two operations move the balance outside a close run. A `ProgramAdmin` may apply a manual adjustment supplying a non-zero `points_delta` — positive to sanction, negative to remit — and a `reason`; the delta accumulates into `manual_adjustment_points_total`, the `penalty_points_balance` is re-derived, `reporting_compliance_status` is re-derived from the new balance, and the facility's `version` increments by 1. An adjustment that would drive the balance below 0 is rejected with `ERR_NEGATIVE_PENALTY_BALANCE`. Separately, a `ProgramAdmin` may waive an assessed penalty by supplying a `waiver_reason`. The report must be in `ACCEPTED` with `assessed_penalty_points` strictly greater than 0, else `ERR_REPORT_INVALID_STATE`, and its period must already be `CLOSED`. Crucially, the waiving `X-User-ID` must differ from the report's `accepted_by_user_id`: the operator who accepted a filing may not later forgive its penalty, and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. A waiver advances the report `ACCEPTED → PENALTY_WAIVED`, drops the facility's `penalty_points_balance` by the waived `assessed_penalty_points`, re-derives `reporting_compliance_status` from the new balance, and increments the facility's `version` by 1.

**The facility performance score.** Each close run also publishes a 0-to-100 performance figure per facility, built from three component rates. The **screened-before-discharge rate** is the program's headline measure and exists in two attribution variants. On the *cohort basis*, the denominator is the number of infants whose `birth_facility_id` is this facility and whose `birth_datetime` falls inside the half-open period window, and the numerator is how many of those infants have at least one `BIRTH_ADMISSION` encounter at this facility with `before_discharge` true. On the *encounter basis*, the denominator is the number of distinct infants appearing across this facility's `BIRTH_ADMISSION` encounters whose `performed_at` falls inside the window, and the numerator is the number of distinct infants among those same encounters where `before_discharge` is true. The difference is attribution: the cohort basis charges a period with every baby born in it, so a baby born on the last day of the window and screened afterwards still counts against the denominator, whereas the encounter basis charges a period with the screens performed in it, so a baby never screened — or screened after the window — leaves the denominator entirely instead of counting against it. For one small cohort of two infants where the second is discharged unscreened, the cohort-basis rate is 0.5000 while the encounter-basis rate is 1.0000 from identical rows.

The second component, the **follow-up resolution rate**, has as its denominator the number of follow-up episodes belonging to this facility's infants whose `created_at` falls inside the window, and as its numerator how many of those episodes stand in `RESOLVED_PASS`, `RESOLVED_DIAGNOSED`, or `RESOLVED_NO_LOSS` at the moment of close. The third, the **report timeliness rate**, is 1.0 when the facility's report of record for the period has `submitted_late` false and `auto_generated` false, and 0.0 in every other case — a facility that filed nothing scores zero here even though the close run gave it a report.

Each of the three rates is rounded to 4 decimal places, round-half-up. The screened-before-discharge rate is weighted at 0.50, the follow-up resolution rate at 0.30, and the report timeliness rate at 0.20. The three rounded rates are multiplied by their weights and the three products summed at full precision without intermediate rounding; the sum is multiplied by 100 and rounded once to 2 decimal places, round-half-up. Substituting the cohort-basis screening rate yields `performance_score`, the official state metric; substituting the encounter-basis rate into the same weighted sum yields `encounter_basis_performance_score`, retained purely for reconciliation and never used to drive posture. Continuing the two-infant illustration above, with a follow-up resolution rate of 0.0000 and a report timeliness rate of 1.0, the two published figures are 45.00 and 70.00 — same rows, two answers, which is the whole point of carrying both.

Empty denominators are not re-normalised. If the cohort denominator is 0, the cohort rate is null and `performance_score` is null. If the encounter denominator is 0, `encounter_basis_performance_score` is null. If the follow-up episode denominator is 0, that rate is null and **both** scores are null. A null score never moves `reporting_compliance_status`; only the penalty balance does. The facility dashboard exposes `facility_id`, `performance_score`, `encounter_basis_performance_score`, `cohort_screened_rate`, `encounter_screened_rate`, `followup_resolution_rate`, `report_timeliness_rate`, `penalty_points_balance`, `reporting_compliance_status`, and `last_closed_period_label` — the last being the `period_start`-to-`period_end` label of the most recently closed period, written by the close run.

**The close run itself.** Closing a period is a `ProgramAdmin` operation and the program's single ops-run checkpoint. Three preconditions guard it: the period must be `OPEN`, else `ERR_PERIOD_ALREADY_CLOSED`; the logical clock must have reached the period's `period_end`, else `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_ENDED`; and no earlier period — one whose `period_end` is at or before this period's `period_start` — may still be `OPEN`, since periods must close in chronological order, else `ERR_EARLIER_PERIOD_OPEN`.

The run then executes in a fixed sequence. First, every facility whose `facility_type` is `BIRTH_HOSPITAL`, `BIRTHING_CENTER`, `MIDWIFE_PRACTICE`, or `AUDIOLOGY_CLINIC` is assessed in ascending `Facility.created_at` order — early intervention programs are not assessed and are not counted. For each such facility: when no non-`SUPERSEDED` report exists for that facility and period, the run mints one with `auto_generated` true, `status` `ACCEPTED`, `accepted_by_user_id` null, `attested_screened_count` and `attested_refer_count` null, `submitted_at` at the logical clock, and `variance_case_count` equal to its `actual_refer_count`; any report still sitting in `SUBMITTED` or `RETURNED_FOR_CORRECTION` is auto-accepted to `ACCEPTED`, again with `accepted_by_user_id` left null because no human accepted it; `assessed_penalty_points` is written by the assessment above; `penalty_points_balance` and `reporting_compliance_status` are updated; `performance_score` and `encounter_basis_performance_score` are written; and `facilities_assessed_count` increments by 1.

Second, the escalation cascade runs. For every facility whose posture became `PROBATION` or `SUSPENDED` in this run, each follow-up episode belonging to that facility's infants whose `status` is `PENDING_SCHEDULING` or `SCHEDULED` has its `escalation_flag` set to true and its `owner_scope` moved to `STATE_PROGRAM` — the state takes over outreach the facility has demonstrably failed at — and `episodes_escalated_count` increments once per escalated episode. Because those episodes escalated, each affected infant's `ltf_risk_score` is recomputed with the 6-point escalation term and its `priority_band` rewritten. And because a band may have become `CRITICAL` in that recomputation, any such infant holding no navigation task in `UNASSIGNED` or `OPEN` receives a freshly minted `OUTREACH_CALL` task, auto-assigned through the navigator selector — subject to the deferred-queue and shortfall behaviour of §3.5.

Third, the episode timeliness sweep runs in ascending `FollowUpEpisode.created_at` order: an episode in `PENDING_SCHEDULING` whose applied-basis effective age has passed its `benchmark_days` has its `timeliness_status` written as `OVERDUE`, and one whose age has passed `benchmark_days` plus 45 days with `attempt_count` of 3 or more is moved to `LOST_TO_FOLLOW_UP` with the cascade described in §3.4, incrementing `episodes_lost_count`. Fourth, the surveillance registry sweep runs in ascending `RiskFactorEntry.created_at` order per §3.8. Finally the period itself advances `OPEN → CLOSED` with `closed_at` at the logical clock, and each assessed facility's `last_closed_period_label` is written.

Ordering is pinned by `created_at` ascending at every sweep so that a close run is fully deterministic and reproducible; row identifiers are never used as an ordering key anywhere in the run. Reports are discoverable by `facility_id`, `reporting_period_id`, `supersedes_report_id`, `status`, and `auto_generated`, the last being how the program office finds every facility that filed nothing at all.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

All endpoints are mounted at the top level of the service — there is no version prefix and no nesting beyond the sub-resource routes listed below. Every path segment written as `{id}` is the UUID identifier of the resource named by the preceding collection segment. Resource creation returns HTTP 201; every other successful operation, including all state transitions, sub-resource actions, list reads and single reads, returns HTTP 200. Unless a section above says otherwise, an operation returns the created or updated primary resource in `data`, and list operations return an array in `data` with `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more` in `meta`. Every request must carry the `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` headers described in §2.1; the roles column below lists the roles permitted to call each endpoint, and any other role receives `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`.
