# Home Electrification Rebates API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

State home energy offices are being asked to move households off combustion appliances and onto electricity, and to do it with a fixed appropriation, a published rebate schedule, and an auditable paper trail for every dollar. The operational reality is messier than the policy: a household's rebate ceiling depends on a verified income tier, the measures a dwelling can actually receive depend on the amperage of its electrical service, a panel upgrade only earns money if a qualifying appliance is going in behind it, and the appropriation itself has to be encumbered months before a single invoice arrives. Meanwhile utility programs and the federal tax code are paying for the same equipment, and the state may not let the combined public benefit exceed what the project actually cost.

This document specifies the Home Electrification Rebates API — the system of record a state administrator uses to run that program end to end. It covers program and catalog configuration, household intake and income tier verification, contractor enrollment and quality standing, the pre-installation assessment that produces the eligible-measure list, project creation with rate pinning and measure-level incentive allocation, budget reservation with expiry and extension, installation reporting, spot and post-installation inspection, benefit stacking limits, disbursement, and clawback when an inspection fails after payment.

Two properties dominate the design. The first is **fiscal conservation**: the appropriation and each household's lifetime entitlement are conserved quantities, and every reserve must be matched by a compensating release, commitment, or recovery that returns the books to a provable balance. The second is **determinism**: every ordering, every rounding step, and every cap-proration residual is pinned by rule rather than left to implementation choice, because two administrators reading the same project must compute the same cents. Because the service is mounted at the root path, every collection in the system is a top-level collection joined by explicit identifier fields; child discovery happens through filtered list endpoints and sub-resource list endpoints rather than through deeply nested URLs.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the API must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which that user is acting. A request with a missing or empty `X-User-ID` is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401) before any other consideration, including the shape of the body. A request whose `X-User-Role` is absent, unrecognised, or not among the roles authorised for the addressed endpoint is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403).

The system recognises exactly five roles: `Applicant`, `Contractor`, `FieldInspector`, `ProgramReviewer`, and `ProgramAdmin`. An `Applicant` is the homeowner: they own the household record, file income documentation, open and submit projects, declare stacked benefits, and request reservation extensions. A `Contractor` is the enrolled installer: they perform pre-installation assessments, may open and submit projects on a household's behalf, report installation, and open remediation cycles. A `FieldInspector` records spot and post-installation inspection results and nothing else — inspectors never schedule, reserve, or pay. A `ProgramReviewer` is state program staff who adjudicate income verifications, assessments, contractor enrollment, and project reviews, schedule inspections, and encumber budget. A `ProgramAdmin` configures the program and its catalog, decides contractor suspension, runs the expiry sweep, disburses payment, and drives the logical clock; an administrator may not review projects or record inspection results.

Three of the five roles are **entity-backed**, meaning `X-User-ID` is the identifier of a persisted record and ownership is checked against it:

- When `X-User-Role` is `Applicant`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Household`; ownership checks on any resource that references a household compare the resource's household identifier — the addressed household's own `id`, or a project's `household_id` — to `X-User-ID`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `Contractor`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Contractor`; ownership checks compare the addressed contractor's `id`, or a project's `contractor_id`, or an assessment's `contractor_id`, to `X-User-ID`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `FieldInspector`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Inspector`; ownership checks compare the addressed inspector's `id`, or an inspection's `inspector_id`, to `X-User-ID`.

A request from an entity-backed role that addresses a resource belonging to a different household, contractor, or inspector is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403).

The remaining two roles carry **opaque** identity. When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramReviewer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque staff identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it where a transition writes it — as `verified_by_user_id` on an income verification, `enrollment_decided_by_user_id` on a contractor, `approved_by_user_id` on an assessment, `reviewed_by_user_id` on a rebate project, `scheduled_by_user_id` on an inspection, and `recorded_by_user_id` on a clawback action — but never compares it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier, recorded where a transition writes it (for example `recorded_by_user_id` on a clawback action produced by an administrator's request) and never matched against an entity identifier. Note that `recorded_by_user_id` on an `Inspection` is the exception in the other direction: it stores the acting `FieldInspector`'s inspector identifier, and the segregation-of-duties comparison described in §2.9 reads it.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints accept two optional query parameters. `limit` caps the number of records returned; when omitted it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` is the number of records to skip; when omitted it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` outside the inclusive range 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (400).

Unless a specific endpoint documents a different order, list endpoints return records sorted by `created_at` ascending. Two endpoints override this: the project measure-line list is ordered by `pinned_sort_order` ascending and then `created_at` ascending, and the eligible-measure list of an assessment is ordered by `evaluation_order` ascending.

Every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Fields and Immutable Records

Most resources in the system carry a uniform set of server-managed properties:

- `id` (string): a server-minted UUID with no type prefix.
- `created_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, stamped when the record is first written.
- `updated_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, refreshed on every mutation of the record.
- `version` (integer): a counter that increments by one on every mutation of the record, including mutations caused by a cascade from another resource rather than by a request addressed to this one.

`Program`, `MeasureCatalogItem`, `Household`, `IncomeVerification`, `Contractor`, `Assessment`, `RebateProject`, `ProjectMeasure`, `Reservation`, `StackingDeclaration`, and `Inspection` all carry the full set. Three record types are append-only audit rows and carry only `id` and `created_at` — they have **no** `updated_at` and **no** `version` field at all: `Inspector`, `EligibleMeasure`, and `ClawbackAction`. An inspector record has no update endpoint; an eligible-measure row is written once when its assessment is created and never revised; a clawback action is a permanent ledger entry.

When a computed field changes — a budget bucket on a program, a cap balance on a household, an incentive amount on a project or a measure line — that is a mutation of the owning record: `updated_at` is refreshed and `version` increments by one. A single request that cascades across several records increments the `version` of each record it touches.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

Every time-dependent rule in this system reads a server-controlled **logical clock** rather than wall-clock time: reservation expiry, the extension window, contractor license and insurance currency, inspector window conflicts, install-window overlap, and the trailing horizon of the severity-weighted quality score. Before any clock manipulation the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to wall-clock time.

A `ProgramAdmin` may move the clock to an arbitrary UTC instant by calling `POST /_clock/set_current_utc` with a `current_utc` value; the response reports the new `current_utc` alongside the `previous_utc` it replaced. Setting the clock changes no entity — it only changes what every subsequent read of "now" returns.

### 2.6. Money, Amperage and Rounding Discipline

All money in this system is expressed as an integer number of cents; there are no fractional cents anywhere, and every field whose name ends in `_cents` is an integer. Electrical service capacity is an integer number of amps. The rounding rules are specific and are not interchangeable:

- **Percent-of-income arithmetic** rounds half up to one decimal place, and it rounds **before** any threshold comparison is made. The tier test therefore reads the rounded percentage, never the unrounded quotient.
- **Every incentive computation floors to whole cents.** Each intermediate product — a cost-share ceiling, a conditional reduction, a prorated share of a cap — is floored to a whole number of cents before it participates in any comparison or sum. The system never sums first and rounds afterwards.
- **Cap proration assigns the residual to the last line.** When a cap forces a set of lines to be scaled down, every member except the last in the pinned order is floored, and the last member receives the cap minus the sum of the floored members. The prorated set therefore totals exactly the binding cap, with no cents lost to flooring and none created.
- **Quality scores are integers** produced by rounding the final quotient half up. Individual terms in the numerator are never rounded on their own.
- **Stacking arithmetic floors every percentage product** and clamps each intermediate at zero where the rule says so.

### 2.7. Appropriation and Entitlement Conservation

A `Program` holds four money fields, and only the first three of them are conserved buckets. `budget_total_cents` is the appropriation. `budget_available_cents` is the unencumbered remainder, `budget_reserved_cents` the amount held against live reservations, and `budget_committed_cents` the amount actually disbursed. After every mutation that touches any of these, the invariant holds that `budget_total_cents` equals the sum of `budget_available_cents`, `budget_reserved_cents`, and `budget_committed_cents`. Further, `budget_reserved_cents` equals the sum of `reserved_amount_cents` over that program's reservations in status `RESERVED`, and `budget_committed_cents` equals the same sum over its reservations in status `COMMITTED`. A reserve followed by its compensating action — expiry, failed-reinspection release, or post-payment clawback — must return `budget_available_cents` to exactly its pre-reservation value, with zero drift.

The fourth field, `budget_clawed_back_cents`, is deliberately **not** one of the conserved buckets: it is a cumulative counter that equals the sum of `recovered_cents` over the clawback actions recorded against that program's projects. Money recovered in a clawback flows back into `budget_available_cents`; the counter merely records that it happened.

A household's lifetime entitlement is a separate conserved quantity with the same shape. After every mutation that touches it, `rebate_cap_cents` equals `lifetime_cap_remaining_cents` plus `encumbered_cents`, and `encumbered_cents` equals the sum of `reserved_amount_cents` over reservations whose project belongs to that household and whose status is `RESERVED` or `COMMITTED`. Reservations in `EXPIRED`, `RELEASED`, or `CLAWED_BACK` are excluded from the encumbrance. When a payment reconciles a reservation downward, the difference must be credited back to `lifetime_cap_remaining_cents` rather than left sitting in `encumbered_cents`.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency on Programs and Households

Two write operations in this system would otherwise succeed on their own terms while silently invalidating a reading the client made earlier, so both require the client to declare the parent version it last observed.

Creating a reservation for a project — the request addressed to `POST /rebate-projects/{id}/reservations` — requires a non-optional `expected_program_version` field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `Program`. If it does not equal the program's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PROGRAM_VERSION` (412). Reserving funds for a second approved project is perfectly legitimate, so no state precondition can catch this case; the stale version is precisely what tells the reviewer that their view of `budget_available_cents` predates somebody else's reservation.

Creating an assessment — the request addressed to `POST /assessments` — requires a non-optional `expected_household_version` field carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `Household`. A mismatch against the household's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_HOUSEHOLD_VERSION` (412). Because a new assessment legitimately supersedes the household's prior live assessment, a second create would otherwise succeed; the stale version is what tells the contractor that their view of the household's income tier, remaining cap, and prior assessment is out of date.

No other endpoint takes a version field, and no other endpoint performs a version check. Both checks compare against the **parent** record's `version` — the program's and the household's respectively — not the addressed resource's own.

### 2.9. Segregation of Duties

Four operations enforce that the user recording a decision is not the user who produced the artefact being decided. In all four the rejection is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403):

- Verifying an income verification: the acting `X-User-ID` may not equal the `submitted_by_user_id` recorded when the packet was filed.
- Creating a reservation for a project: the acting `X-User-ID` may not equal the project's `reviewed_by_user_id`. The reviewer who returned the review decision may not also encumber budget against it.
- Disbursing payment for a project: the acting `X-User-ID` may not equal the project's `reviewed_by_user_id` either. The person who pays may not be the person who reviewed.
- Recording a post-installation inspection result: no earlier `SPOT` inspection of the same project may carry a `recorded_by_user_id` equal to the acting `X-User-ID`. The inspector who spot-audited a project may not be the inspector who signs off its verification gate.

### 2.10. The Validation Ladder

Every request is evaluated against an ordered ladder of checks. Evaluation stops at the **first** violated rung; no later rung runs, and the response carries that rung's error. The order is normative — a caller with two simultaneous problems always learns about the lower-numbered one.

1. **Identity present.** `X-User-ID` is present and non-empty → otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). This precedes everything, including malformed bodies. Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Role permitted.** `X-User-Role` is present and listed among the endpoint's authorised roles → otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403). This beats body validation: a `Contractor` posting a malformed payment body receives 403, never 400. Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Request shape.** The body or query parses; required fields are present; types and enumerated values are valid; numeric ranges are respected; a date or datetime range is ordered so that the end is not earlier than the start → otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). This runs before any database lookup, so a malformed body aimed at a non-existent parent yields 400, not 404. Applies to every endpoint.
4. **Pagination arguments.** When supplied, `limit` lies within 1 through 100 inclusive and `offset` is at least 0 → otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (400). Applies to every list endpoint, and is checked separately from the rest of the request so the caller can tell the two apart.
5. **Path entities exist,** resolved strictly left to right along the path. The error names the leftmost missing segment, drawn from `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_HOUSEHOLD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONTRACTOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INSPECTOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INCOME_VERIFICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RESERVATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_FOUND`, and `ERR_CLAWBACK_ACTION_NOT_FOUND`, each 404. Applies to every endpoint with path parameters. The first path parameter of every path is literally `{id}` and addresses the collection named by the first path segment; the retirement endpoint additionally takes a second path parameter named `item_id`, and a catalog item identifier that matches no item of the addressed program yields `ERR_MEASURE_CATALOG_ITEM_NOT_FOUND` (404).
6. **Body-referenced entities exist and belong to the same program.** This covers `program_id`, `household_id`, `contractor_id`, `inspector_id`, `assessment_id`, `income_verification_id`, `project_id`, and every `measure_code` that must resolve to a catalog item of that program. Body references are resolved after path references, in the declaration order of the request body. A `measure_code` that exists only in another program's catalog is treated as absent and yields `ERR_MEASURE_CODE_NOT_IN_CATALOG` (404). This rung applies to `POST /assessments`, `POST /rebate-projects`, `POST /inspections`, `POST /households`, `POST /contractors`, `POST /inspectors`, and `POST /programs/{id}/measure-catalog-items`.
7. **Ownership for entity-backed roles** as described in §2.1 → otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). This rung deliberately sits *after* existence, so probing for a foreign identifier returns 404 when the identifier does not exist and 403 when it does. It applies to exactly nine operations: `POST /households/{id}/income-verifications`, `POST /rebate-projects`, `POST /rebate-projects/{id}/submit`, `POST /rebate-projects/{id}/installation`, `POST /rebate-projects/{id}/stacking-declarations`, `POST /rebate-projects/{id}/remediation`, `POST /inspections/{id}/result`, `GET /households/{id}`, and `GET /rebate-projects/{id}`.
8. **State gate.** The source entity is in a state from which the requested transition is declared, and the enclosing program is `OPEN` where the transition requires it. This is the umbrella rung, and an entity in a terminal state always fails here. Its errors are all 409: `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_OPEN`, `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_APPROVED`, `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_APPROVED_FOR_RESERVATION`, `ERR_RESERVATION_NOT_ACTIVE`, `ERR_RESERVATION_EXPIRED`, `ERR_INSPECTION_ALREADY_RECORDED`, `ERR_OPEN_INSPECTION_PENDING`, and `ERR_INCOME_TIER_ALREADY_VERIFIED`. It applies to every transition endpoint. When the blocking condition is specifically a sibling inspection still in `SCHEDULED` — as when payment is requested, or a re-inspection is scheduled out of `REMEDIATION` — the error is `ERR_OPEN_INSPECTION_PENDING` rather than the generic `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.
9. **Concurrency token.** The declared parent version matches, per §2.8 → otherwise `ERR_STALE_PROGRAM_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_HOUSEHOLD_VERSION` (412). Applies to `POST /rebate-projects/{id}/reservations` and `POST /assessments`. It sits after the state gate deliberately: a caller holding a stale token against an already-terminal entity learns about the state problem first.
10. **Segregation of duties** per §2.9 → otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). Applies to `POST /income-verifications/{id}/verify`, `POST /rebate-projects/{id}/reservations`, `POST /rebate-projects/{id}/payment`, and `POST /inspections/{id}/result`.
11. **Installer fitness.** The contractor attached to the operation is `ENROLLED_ACTIVE`, and both `license_expires_on` and `insurance_expires_on` fall on or after the logical clock's date. Enrollment status is checked *before* credential currency, so a suspended contractor with lapsed paperwork reports `ERR_CONTRACTOR_NOT_ACTIVE` rather than `ERR_CONTRACTOR_CREDENTIAL_EXPIRED`; both are 409. Applies to `POST /assessments`, `POST /rebate-projects`, `POST /rebate-projects/{id}/submit`, `POST /rebate-projects/{id}/installation`, and `POST /rebate-projects/{id}/remediation`.
12. **Electrical safety.** Every non-panel-upgrade measure line's `required_panel_capacity_amps` is no greater than the project's effective service capacity — the assessment's `effective_panel_amps` before installation, or `installed_panel_amps` once installation is reported → otherwise `ERR_PANEL_CAPACITY_INSUFFICIENT` (422). Applies to `POST /rebate-projects`, `POST /rebate-projects/{id}/submit`, and `POST /rebate-projects/{id}/installation`. Physical safety outranks program bookkeeping: an appliance the dwelling cannot power is rejected before any referential or fiscal reasoning happens.
13. **Measure-set coherence.** A panel or service upgrade line requires at least one qualifying appliance anchor line on the same project, and every line whose catalog item declares a `prerequisite_measure_code` requires that measure to be present and qualified on the same project. The anchor rule is evaluated first and reports `ERR_PANEL_UPGRADE_ANCHOR_MISSING`; the prerequisite rule follows and reports `ERR_PREREQUISITE_MEASURE_MISSING`; both are 422. Applies to `POST /rebate-projects` and `POST /rebate-projects/{id}/submit`.
14. **Non-empty measure set.** The request carries at least one measure line, and the linked assessment has a `qualified_measure_count` of at least 1 → otherwise `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_MEASURES` (422). An empty measures array is *structurally* valid and passes rung 3; it is rejected here as a business empty set, so the zero-line edge is observable as `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_MEASURES` rather than `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. This rung sits before the per-line checks so that a zero-qualified assessment reports the set-level failure rather than a per-line one. Applies to `POST /rebate-projects`, `POST /rebate-projects/{id}/submit`, and `POST /assessments/{id}/approve`.
15. **Per-line eligibility and uniqueness.** Every requested `measure_code` appears on the linked assessment as an eligible-measure row whose `qualified` flag is true, and no `measure_code` repeats within one request. Ineligibility is reported before duplication, so a repeated ineligible code reports `ERR_MEASURE_NOT_ELIGIBLE` rather than `ERR_DUPLICATE_MEASURE_LINE`; both are 422. Applies to `POST /rebate-projects` and `POST /rebate-projects/{id}/submit`.
16. **Inspector window availability** → otherwise `ERR_INSPECTOR_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409). Applies to `POST /inspections`.
17. **Contractor crew capacity** → otherwise `ERR_CONTRACTOR_CREW_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409). Applies to `POST /rebate-projects/{id}/installation`. This is a different resource and a different interval semantics from rung 16.
18. **Extension limits,** applied in a fixed inner order: window membership first (`ERR_EXTENSION_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN`), then the per-reservation count limit (`ERR_EXTENSION_LIMIT_REACHED`), then the length limit (`ERR_EXTENSION_DAYS_EXCEEDED`); all three are 422. Applies to `POST /reservations/{id}/extensions`.
19. **Income tier verified.** The household holds a `VERIFIED` income verification, so an `income_tier` and a `rebate_cap_cents` exist → otherwise `ERR_INCOME_TIER_NOT_VERIFIED` (422). Applies to `POST /rebate-projects` and `POST /rebate-projects/{id}/reservations`. An unverified household may still hold assessments.
20. **Household cap not exhausted.** At reservation time `lifetime_cap_remaining_cents` is at least 1 → otherwise `ERR_HOUSEHOLD_CAP_EXHAUSTED` (422). Applies to `POST /rebate-projects/{id}/reservations`. A fully encumbered household cannot open a second reservation even though allocation would clamp the amount to zero anyway.
21. **Stacking headroom.** The declared utility rebate and the computed federal credit leave a state incentive above zero; a declaration that would drive the allowed state incentive to 0 is rejected rather than recorded, with `ERR_STACKING_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (422). Applies to `POST /rebate-projects/{id}/stacking-declarations`. It runs after all referential checks because the stacking arithmetic needs a settled measure set.
22. **Appropriation available.** `budget_available_cents` is at least the amount to be reserved → otherwise `ERR_PROGRAM_BUDGET_EXHAUSTED` (422). Applies to `POST /rebate-projects/{id}/reservations`. This is the last rung in the entire ladder: appropriation scarcity is reported only when every other aspect of the request is sound.

Two worked consequences of the ordering are worth spelling out. A reservation requested for a project still in `SUBMITTED`, inside a program whose `budget_available_cents` is 0, reports `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_APPROVED_FOR_RESERVATION` (409) — the state gate at rung 8 wins over the budget rung by fourteen levels. A project opened with a suspended contractor **and** a panel upgrade with no qualifying appliance anchor reports `ERR_CONTRACTOR_NOT_ACTIVE` (409): installer fitness at rung 11 outranks measure-set coherence at rung 13.

### 2.11. Cascade Semantics

Several transitions in this system reach beyond the record the request addressed. Cascades are immediate and synchronous — by the time a response is returned, every downstream record has already been mutated, and each of those mutations refreshes that record's `updated_at` and increments its `version`. Cascades are described in full where the workflow that triggers them is described, but three families recur and are worth naming here.

The **contractor-suspension cascade** moves that contractor's reserved projects to a hold state and back again on reinstatement. The **expiry cascade** unwinds a reservation into the program's budget and the household's cap, expires the project, voids its measure lines, and cancels its scheduled inspections. The **inspection-failure cascade** rejects the named measure lines, then rejects any panel-upgrade line whose anchor was just rejected, then recomputes the project total over what survives — and, at the remediation limit, releases the reservation and writes a clawback action. Each hop in a cascade is caused by the hop before it, not directly by the request, and the ordering matters because later hops read state that earlier hops wrote.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up a Program and Its Measure Catalog

A `ProgramAdmin` opens the season by creating a program, which is born in `DRAFT`. The mandatory inputs are a published `name`, a `state_code` of exactly two uppercase letters identifying the state that owns the program, and a `budget_total_cents` appropriation of zero or more. Everything else is an overridable parameter with a documented default, because the whole rebate schedule is program-configurable: the funding year `program_year` defaults to 2026; `reservation_ttl_days` defaults to 120; `extension_window_days` to 30; `max_extension_days` to 60; `max_extensions_per_reservation` to 1; the lifetime caps `low_tier_cap_cents`, `moderate_tier_cap_cents`, and `standard_tier_cap_cents` to 1400000, 800000, and 300000 cents respectively; the cost-share fractions `low_tier_cost_share_pct`, `moderate_tier_cost_share_pct`, and `standard_tier_cost_share_pct` to 1.00, 0.50, and 0.30; the tier boundaries `ami_low_threshold_pct` and `ami_moderate_threshold_pct` to 80.0 and 150.0 percent; `group_cap_cents` to 800000; `panel_upgrade_cap_cents` to 400000; `federal_credit_pct` to 0.30 and `federal_credit_cap_cents` to 200000; `max_combined_benefit_pct` to 1.00; `max_remediations_per_project` to 1; `quality_suspension_threshold` to 70; and `recertification_threshold` to 75. At creation the program's `budget_available_cents` is set equal to `budget_total_cents`, and `budget_reserved_cents`, `budget_committed_cents`, and `budget_clawed_back_cents` all start at 0.

The program lifecycle is driven by an administrator supplying a `new_status`, optionally with a `status_note`. `DRAFT → OPEN` requires that the program still be `DRAFT` and that `budget_total_cents` be strictly greater than zero; on that transition `budget_available_cents` is set to `budget_total_cents` and the program's version increments. `OPEN → SUSPENDED` requires the program to be `OPEN`; suspension does **not** pause any clock, so existing reservations keep their `expires_at` exactly as it stood, and while suspended, reservation creation and payment are refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_OPEN` while the expiry sweep continues to run normally. `SUSPENDED → OPEN` returns a suspended program to service, which makes the program machine cyclic. `CLOSED` is terminal and may be reached from either `OPEN` or `SUSPENDED`, but only when no reservation of that program is still in status `RESERVED`. Every one of these five transitions increments the program's version by one. A requested status that is not declared for the program's current state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.

Catalog items are the priced measures a household can receive, and each belongs to exactly one program through its `program_id`. An administrator adds one by supplying a `measure_code` — uppercase alphanumeric with hyphens, and unique within that program's catalog — plus a human-readable `measure_name`, a `category` drawn from `HVAC`, `WATER_HEATING`, `APPLIANCE`, `ENVELOPE`, and `ELECTRICAL_INFRASTRUCTURE`, a per-unit `unit_incentive_cents` of zero or more, a per-line ceiling `measure_cap_cents` of zero or more, a `combined_cap_group` drawn from `HVAC_GROUP`, `WATER_GROUP`, `APPLIANCE_GROUP`, `ELECTRICAL_GROUP`, and `NONE`, and a non-negative `sort_order` that pins deterministic ordering for cap proration and eligibility evaluation. Reusing a `measure_code` already present in that program's catalog is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_MEASURE_CODE` (409).

The optional catalog fields carry the electrification logic. `max_quantity` defaults to 1 and must be at least 1; it is the point beyond which a proposed quantity is clamped rather than rejected. `required_panel_capacity_amps` defaults to 0 and states the minimum service capacity the dwelling must have for the measure to qualify. `upgraded_capacity_amps` also defaults to 0 and states the capacity the dwelling *has* after the item is installed — a value that is only meaningful when `is_panel_upgrade` is true. `is_panel_upgrade` defaults to false and, when true, marks the item as a panel or service upgrade, subjecting it to the program's panel-upgrade ceiling and to anchor gating. `requires_qualifying_appliance` defaults to false and, when true, means the item only qualifies if the same assessment proposes a qualifying appliance anchor. `qualifies_as_appliance_anchor` defaults to false and marks an item that is able to *be* that anchor. `prerequisite_measure_code` defaults to null and, when set, names another catalog item's `measure_code` in the same program that must also be proposed and qualified. `standard_tier_eligible` defaults to false and gates whether a household in the highest income tier may receive the measure at all. `active` defaults to true.

Retirement is how a program withdraws a measure without rewriting history. An administrator retires an item — optionally recording a `retirement_note` — which sets `active` to false and increments that item's version. Retirement deliberately touches no existing project measure line: already-pinned rates survive untouched, while new assessments now disqualify the measure with the reason `RETIRED_CATALOG_ITEM`. Reviewers and administrators can browse a program's catalog filtered by `active`, `category`, and `combined_cap_group`, and can browse the program's `Household`, `Contractor`, and `Inspector` populations — the households list accepts `income_tier` and `county_code` filters, the contractors list accepts a `status` filter, and the programs list itself accepts `status` and `state_code` filters.

### 3.2. Household Intake and Income Tier Verification

A household enters the system as an intake record naming the `program_id` it applies under, an `applicant_name` of record, a contact `email`, the dwelling's `service_address_line1`, the `county_code` used to select the median-income benchmark, the `utility_territory_code` used later when a utility rebate is stacked, an occupant `household_size` of at least 1, and `existing_panel_amps` — the electrical service capacity reported at intake, before anyone has been on site — of zero or more. The identifier returned by that registration is the value the `Applicant` role sends as `X-User-ID` from then on. At intake the household's `ami_percent` and `income_tier` are both null, and `rebate_cap_cents`, `lifetime_cap_remaining_cents`, and `encumbered_cents` are all 0; nothing is assigned until a verification is decided.

Income documentation is filed as an income verification packet against the household. The packet carries `annual_household_income_cents` (zero or more), a `county_median_income_cents` benchmark for a four-person household (strictly greater than zero), a `household_size_attested` occupant count of at least 1, and an optional `document_reference` naming the document bundle for the audit trail. A filed packet is recorded in `SUBMITTED`, its `submitted_by_user_id` stamped with the acting `X-User-ID`, and its `computed_ami_percent` and `assigned_income_tier` left null with `assigned_cap_cents` at 0. A household may hold at most one `VERIFIED` verification ever; filing or verifying a packet for a household that already holds one is refused with `ERR_INCOME_TIER_ALREADY_VERIFIED`. The household's verification history can be listed and filtered by `status`.

A `ProgramReviewer` records the outcome by supplying a `decision` together with optional `reviewer_notes`. A decision of `REJECT` moves the packet `SUBMITTED → REJECTED` and leaves the household entirely untouched. A decision of `VERIFY` moves it `SUBMITTED → VERIFIED` and runs the tier assignment, which is computed from **exactly** the packet being verified — the household's own `household_size` field is never used, only `household_size_attested`:

1. Take the attested occupant count, and if it exceeds 7 treat it as 7. Look up the size adjustment factor for that count: 0.70 for one occupant, 0.80 for two, 0.90 for three, 1.00 for four, 1.08 for five, 1.16 for six, and 1.24 for seven or more.
2. Multiply `county_median_income_cents` by that factor to obtain the size-adjusted median income.
3. Divide `annual_household_income_cents` by the size-adjusted median and multiply by 100 to obtain the raw percent of area median income.
4. Round that raw percentage half up to one decimal place. This rounded value is what gets stored and what the tier test reads — the comparison is never made against the unrounded quotient.
5. If the rounded percentage is less than or equal to the program's `ami_low_threshold_pct`, the tier is `LOW`. Otherwise, if it is less than or equal to `ami_moderate_threshold_pct`, the tier is `MODERATE`. Otherwise the tier is `STANDARD`.
6. The lifetime cap is the program's cap for that tier: `low_tier_cap_cents` for `LOW`, `moderate_tier_cap_cents` for `MODERATE`, `standard_tier_cap_cents` for `STANDARD`. The cost-share fraction is selected the same way from `low_tier_cost_share_pct`, `moderate_tier_cost_share_pct`, and `standard_tier_cost_share_pct`, and that fraction governs every later incentive computation for this household.

The decision writes `computed_ami_percent`, `assigned_income_tier`, and `assigned_cap_cents` onto the verification, along with `verified_by_user_id` and a `verified_at` stamped from the logical clock. It also writes the household's `ami_percent`, `income_tier`, and `rebate_cap_cents`, and sets `lifetime_cap_remaining_cents` to the assigned cap minus whatever the household already has in `encumbered_cents`. The household's version increments by one. Both cap-conservation identities of §2.7 must hold immediately afterwards.

### 3.3. Contractor Enrollment, Credentials and Quality Standing

Installers apply for enrollment by naming their `program_id`, `business_name`, `license_number`, a `license_expires_on` date in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, an `insurance_expires_on` date in the same form, and optionally a `crew_capacity` of at least 1, defaulting to 2 — the number of install windows the contractor may have open on overlapping calendar dates. An application is created in `PENDING_ENROLLMENT` with `quality_score_rolling` at 100, `completed_inspection_count` at 0, and `suspension_reason` null. The identifier returned is the value the `Contractor` role sends as `X-User-ID`.

A `ProgramReviewer` decides the application with a `decision` and optional `decision_notes`. Approving it moves the contractor `PENDING_ENROLLMENT → ENROLLED_ACTIVE`, but only if `license_expires_on` and `insurance_expires_on` are both strictly later than the logical clock's date; the transition stamps `enrollment_decided_by_user_id`, sets `quality_score_rolling` to 100, and increments the contractor's version. Rejecting it moves the contractor `PENDING_ENROLLMENT → ENROLLMENT_REJECTED`, which is terminal, stamping `enrollment_decided_by_user_id` and incrementing the version.

Suspension is a `ProgramAdmin` action taking an `action` of either suspend or reinstate, with an optional `suspension_reason`. Suspending an `ENROLLED_ACTIVE` contractor moves it `ENROLLED_ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` and sets `suspension_reason` to `ADMIN_ACTION` when the administrator initiated it, or to `QUALITY_SCORE` when the system initiated it as a quality cascade. That transition cascades: every other rebate project of this contractor sitting in `RESERVED` moves `RESERVED → CONTRACTOR_HOLD`, and each of those projects has its own version incremented. The cascade is caused by the suspension itself, never directly by whatever request produced the suspension. Reinstating a suspended contractor moves it `SUSPENDED → ENROLLED_ACTIVE` — permitted only when `license_expires_on` is still later than the logical clock's date — and cascades in the opposite direction: every project of this contractor in `CONTRACTOR_HOLD` whose reservation is still in status `RESERVED` moves `CONTRACTOR_HOLD → RESERVED`. Because a contractor can go from active to suspended and back again, this machine is cyclic. Each suspension or reinstatement increments the contractor's version by one.

Quality standing is measured over that contractor's inspection history, and it is measured two different ways over the same history. The candidate set is every inspection whose project belongs to this contractor and whose status is `PASSED`, `CONDITIONAL_PASS`, or `FAILED`, regardless of what state the project itself is in; inspections still `SCHEDULED` and inspections `CANCELLED` are excluded entirely. Candidates are ordered by `result_recorded_at` descending, and where those tie, by `created_at` descending. Each candidate contributes a point value determined by its status: 100 points for `PASSED`, 70 for `CONDITIONAL_PASS`, and 0 for `FAILED`.

The **rolling-window** measure takes at most the ten most recent candidates in that order, weights each equally, and is the sum of their point values divided by the number of candidates counted, rounded half up to a whole number. It ignores `deficiency_count` and ignores any date horizon. This is the measure persisted on `quality_score_rolling` every time an inspection result is recorded, alongside a refreshed `completed_inspection_count` counting the candidate set. When it lands strictly below the program's `quality_suspension_threshold`, the contractor is automatically taken `ENROLLED_ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` set to `QUALITY_SCORE`, dragging the contractor-hold cascade behind it.

The **severity-weighted** measure is never persisted; it is computed on demand. It takes every candidate whose `result_recorded_at` falls at or after 365 days before the logical clock's instant — with no cap on how many that is — and weights each candidate by one plus its `deficiency_count`. The score is the sum of each weight multiplied by that candidate's point value, divided by the sum of the weights, rounded half up to a whole number; only that final quotient is rounded, never the individual terms. Because a single failure carrying many deficiencies can dominate the weighted sum, the same inspection history routinely yields a different integer under the two measures. This variant has no side effect of its own; it decides only whether the contractor remains recertification-eligible, which is true when the weighted score is greater than or equal to the program's `recertification_threshold`. An empty window yields a score of 100 under either measure.

A dedicated computed read exposes both measures side by side for one contractor and persists neither: it returns the `contractor_id`, the `rolling_window_score` and `severity_weighted_score`, the `rolling_window_inspection_count` and `weighted_inspection_count` that each measure actually counted, the `suspension_threshold` and `recertification_threshold` in force, and the boolean `recertification_eligible`.

Field inspectors are registered by an administrator with a `program_id`, an `inspector_name`, a `certification_id`, and optionally a `certification_expires_on` date in `YYYY-MM-DD` form. The record is immutable — it has no version field and no update path — and the identifier it returns is what the `FieldInspector` role sends as `X-User-ID`.

### 3.4. The Pre-Installation Assessment and the Eligible-Measure Determination

An enrolled contractor visits the dwelling and records an assessment naming the `household_id`, its own `contractor_id`, the `assessed_panel_amps` measured on site (zero or more), the `expected_household_version` demanded by §2.8, a `measures` array of proposed lines, and optionally free-text `assessment_notes`. Each entry of `measures` is a `measure_code` together with a `proposed_quantity` of at least 1 and an `estimated_cost_cents` of zero or more. The assessment is created in `PENDING_REVIEW`, and creating it moves the household's prior live assessment — whether it stood in `PENDING_REVIEW` or `APPROVED` — to `SUPERSEDED`, incrementing the superseded row's version. A prior `APPROVED` assessment may only be superseded when no rebate project referencing it is in `RESERVED`, `CONTRACTOR_HOLD`, `INSTALLED`, `INSPECTION_FAILED`, `REMEDIATION`, `VERIFIED`, or `PAID`. Creating the assessment also increments the household's version by one. Both `REJECTED` and `SUPERSEDED` are terminal for an assessment.

Every proposed line becomes an immutable eligible-measure row carrying its `assessment_id`, its `measure_code`, the `proposed_quantity`, and the `estimated_cost_cents` as filed. Qualification runs across the whole proposed set in a **pinned evaluation order**: catalog `sort_order` ascending, and where those tie, the eligible-measure row's own `created_at` ascending. Each row's 1-based rank in that order is stored as its `evaluation_order`. The determination then proceeds in five stages, and the stage order is normative — it is executed exactly once and never re-run to a fixed point.

**Stage A — catalog and tier, panel capacity deliberately ignored.** If the named code resolves to no catalog item of the program, or the item's `active` flag is false, the line is disqualified with `RETIRED_CATALOG_ITEM`. Otherwise, if the household's `income_tier` is `STANDARD` and the item's `standard_tier_eligible` is false, the line is disqualified with `TIER_INELIGIBLE`. Any line surviving both tests is *tentatively* qualified.

**Stage B — the panel-upgrade anchor.** For each line whose catalog item has `requires_qualifying_appliance` true, the system looks for a tentatively qualified line whose item has `qualifies_as_appliance_anchor` true. If no such line exists, the requiring line is disqualified with `ANCHOR_MISSING`. Note that "tentatively qualified" here means it survived Stage A only — the anchor has not yet faced any capacity test.

**Stage C — capacity propagation.** The assessment's effective service capacity starts at `assessed_panel_amps`. If any line whose item has `is_panel_upgrade` true survived Stage B, the effective capacity becomes the greater of the assessed capacity and the largest `upgraded_capacity_amps` among those surviving panel-upgrade lines. The result is stored as the assessment's `effective_panel_amps`.

**Stage D — the capacity gate, from which panel-upgrade lines are exempt.** For each surviving line whose item has `is_panel_upgrade` false, if the item's `required_panel_capacity_amps` is strictly greater than the effective capacity, the line is disqualified with `PANEL_CAPACITY_INSUFFICIENT`.

**Stage E — prerequisites by business key.** For each line still surviving whose item declares a non-null `prerequisite_measure_code`, if that code is absent from this assessment, or if its line did not survive Stage D, the dependent line is disqualified with `PREREQUISITE_MISSING`.

A row's `qualified` flag is true precisely when its `disqualification_reason` is null, and `disqualification_reason` is null precisely when the row survived all five stages. The reason enumeration is exactly `RETIRED_CATALOG_ITEM`, `TIER_INELIGIBLE`, `PANEL_CAPACITY_INSUFFICIENT`, `ANCHOR_MISSING`, and `PREREQUISITE_MISSING`.

The mutual entanglement between the anchor and the capacity gate is the point of the stage order, and it is worth stating plainly. A panel upgrade needs an anchor appliance to earn money, and that anchor appliance may itself need the very capacity the panel upgrade provides. The order resolves the deadlock in one pass: an anchor that is tentatively qualified in Stage A on catalog and tier grounds alone is enough to qualify the panel upgrade in Stage B, and the capacity raised in Stage C then rescues that same anchor in Stage D. Only lines proposed on *this* assessment participate; measures on prior, now superseded assessments are excluded from every stage.

The assessment also records `qualified_measure_count`, the number of child rows whose `qualified` flag is true, and `estimated_total_incentive_cents`, the sum of `estimated_incentive_cents` over the qualified rows after the group and household cap allocation of §3.5 has been applied to the estimate. Each qualified row's `estimated_incentive_cents` is that line's estimated incentive after the measure and cost-share ceilings and after cap allocation; a row that is not qualified carries 0.

Review is a `ProgramReviewer` action taking a `decision` and optional `review_notes`. Approving moves the assessment `PENDING_REVIEW → APPROVED`, permitted only when `qualified_measure_count` is at least 1 and the assessment's contractor is `ENROLLED_ACTIVE`; an assessment with no qualified lines is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_MEASURES`. Rejecting moves it `PENDING_REVIEW → REJECTED`. Either decision stamps `approved_by_user_id` and increments the assessment's version by one. Only an `APPROVED` assessment can back a rebate project; anything else is refused with `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_APPROVED`. Assessments can be listed filtered by `household_id`, `contractor_id`, and `status`, and an assessment's eligible-measure rows can be listed filtered by `qualified`.

### 3.5. Opening a Rebate Project, Pinning Rates and the Provisional Allocation

A project is the money-bearing record. It is opened by an `Applicant`, a `Contractor`, or a `ProgramAdmin` naming the `household_id`, the `assessment_id` of the approved assessment the measures come from, the `contractor_id` of the installer of record copied from that assessment, the `income_verification_id` of the verified verification that set the tier and cap, an `estimated_project_cost_cents` strictly greater than zero, and a `measures` array. Each entry of `measures` is a `measure_code`, a `planned_quantity` of at least 1, and an `estimated_cost_cents` of zero or more. The project is created in `DRAFT` with `submission_count` at 0, `remediation_count` at 0, `conditional_pass_flag` false, `disbursed_amount_cents` at 0, `provisional_total_incentive_cents` at 0, `final_total_incentive_cents` and `stacking_adjusted_state_incentive_cents` null, `actual_project_cost_cents` null, and `install_start_date`, `install_end_date`, and `installed_panel_amps` all null.

Each entry becomes a project measure line carrying its `project_id`, its `measure_code`, and its `planned_quantity` and `estimated_cost_cents` as filed, starting with `install_status` at `PLANNED` and `verification_status` at `PENDING`, `actual_cost_cents` and `verified_quantity` null, `rejection_reason` null, `provisional_incentive_cents` at 0, and `final_incentive_cents` null. Crucially, creation **pins** six values from the live catalog item onto the line: `pinned_unit_incentive_cents`, `pinned_measure_cap_cents`, `pinned_max_quantity`, `pinned_sort_order`, `pinned_combined_cap_group`, and `pinned_is_panel_upgrade`. Every later incentive computation reads the pinned copies and never the live catalog item, so retiring or re-pricing a measure afterwards changes nothing about a project already open. A project's lines can be listed filtered by `install_status` and `verification_status`.

**The per-line incentive.** For one line at a time, the system computes a raw amount as the pinned unit incentive multiplied by the smaller of the line's quantity and its `pinned_max_quantity` — quantity above the pinned maximum is clamped, not rejected. It computes a cost-share ceiling as the line's cost multiplied by the household's tier cost-share fraction, floored to whole cents. The line amount is then the smallest of three values: the raw amount, the `pinned_measure_cap_cents`, and the cost-share ceiling. If `pinned_is_panel_upgrade` is true, the line amount is further reduced to the smaller of itself and the program's `panel_upgrade_cap_cents`. If the project's `conditional_pass_flag` is true *and* this is the final computation, the line amount is then multiplied by one minus the ten-percent conditional reduction — that is, by 0.90 — and floored to whole cents. Finally, a line whose `verification_status` is `REJECTED` has a line amount of 0. Which quantity and which cost feed the computation depends on the variant: the provisional computation uses `planned_quantity` and `estimated_cost_cents`, while the final computation uses `verified_quantity` and `actual_cost_cents`.

**The allocation waterfall.** Line amounts are then squeezed through two caps in a fixed order — group first, household second, never the reverse. Only lines whose `install_status` is not `VOID` and whose `verification_status` is not `REJECTED` participate; voided and rejected lines contribute 0 and are excluded from the proration ordering entirely.

*Stage 1* computes each participating line's amount as described above.

*Stage 2* applies the combined-measure cap group by group, taking the groups in the fixed order `HVAC_GROUP`, then `WATER_GROUP`, then `APPLIANCE_GROUP`, then `ELECTRICAL_GROUP`. Lines whose `pinned_combined_cap_group` is `NONE` are never prorated. Within a group, members are ordered by `pinned_sort_order` ascending and then `created_at` ascending, and their amounts are summed. If that sum is **strictly greater** than the program's `group_cap_cents` — a sum exactly equal to the cap does not prorate — then every member except the last in that order is reduced to its amount multiplied by the group cap divided by the sum, floored to whole cents, and the last member receives the group cap minus the sum of those floored members.

*Stage 3* applies the household lifetime cap, and only after Stage 2 has settled. The available ceiling is the household's `lifetime_cap_remaining_cents` plus whatever this project has already encumbered for itself — that is, plus this project's own reservation's `reserved_amount_cents` when a reservation in status `RESERVED` exists, and plus 0 when none does. Adding the project's own encumbrance back is what stops a project from squeezing its own final total with money it already holds. The members here are **all** participating lines of the project, again ordered by `pinned_sort_order` ascending then `created_at` ascending, and their post-Stage-2 amounts are summed. If that sum exceeds the ceiling, every member except the last is reduced to its amount multiplied by the ceiling divided by the sum, floored, and the last member receives the ceiling minus the sum of the floored members.

The project total is the sum of the resulting line amounts, which therefore equals the binding cap exactly whenever a cap bound. Applying the two stages in reverse order, or flooring every line including the last, produces a different per-line split and a total that falls short of the cap — both are wrong.

The provisional variant of this waterfall runs at project creation, again on resubmission from `INCOMPLETE`, and its result is the amount the reservation encumbers; it uses planned quantities and estimated costs, its Stage 3 ceiling excludes any reservation of this project because none exists yet, and it writes `provisional_incentive_cents` on each line and `provisional_total_incentive_cents` on the project. The final variant runs at post-installation verification and on the anchor-rejection cascade hop; it uses verified quantities and actual costs, its Stage 3 ceiling adds this project's own `reserved_amount_cents` back, it applies the conditional reduction in Stage 1 when a conditional pass is in force, and it writes `final_incentive_cents` on each line and `final_total_incentive_cents` on the project.

Because both cap stages are invisible in the stored totals alone, a computed read replays the whole waterfall over the project's current lines and exposes each stage without changing any state. It accepts an optional `variant` selecting which computation to replay, defaulting to the provisional one, and returns the `project_id`, the `variant` it ran, a `lines` array, and the aggregates `group_capped_total_cents`, `household_cap_limit_cents`, `total_incentive_cents`, the booleans `group_cap_applied` and `household_cap_applied`, and `residual_cents_assigned_to_measure_code` naming the line that absorbed the residual. Each entry of `lines` reports a `measure_code`, its `raw_incentive_cents`, its `cost_share_cap_cents`, its `capped_line_cents` after the per-line ceilings, and its `allocated_cents` after both cap stages, in `pinned_sort_order` ascending then `created_at` ascending order.

Submission is available to an `Applicant` or a `Contractor` and optionally replaces the measure set: it accepts an optional `measures` array and an optional replacement `estimated_project_cost_cents`. Submitting a `DRAFT` project moves it `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`, permitted only when at least one project measure line exists and when the acting applicant's identifier equals the project's `household_id` or the acting contractor's identifier equals its `contractor_id`; the transition increments `submission_count` by one. Resubmitting a project that was returned incomplete moves it `INCOMPLETE → SUBMITTED` under the same ownership rule, increments `submission_count`, clears the stored `incomplete_reason`, and re-runs the provisional allocation over every line against the household's *current* `lifetime_cap_remaining_cents`. When `measures` is supplied, the line set is replaced and the pinned rates are re-pinned from the catalog as it stands at that moment. This revision loop is what makes the project machine cyclic. Both submission paths re-run the full eligibility, safety, and referential chain of §2.10, so a measure that has since become ineligible surfaces as `ERR_MEASURE_NOT_ELIGIBLE`, a repeated code as `ERR_DUPLICATE_MEASURE_LINE`, and an emptied set as `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_MEASURES`.

### 3.6. Program Review, Budget Reservation, Expiry and Extension

A `ProgramReviewer` records the review decision on a submitted project by supplying a `decision`, with an `incomplete_reason` where the decision returns the project for revision and optional `review_notes` in every case. Approving moves the project `SUBMITTED → APPROVED`, permitted only when the project's contractor is `ENROLLED_ACTIVE` and the household's `income_tier` is not null. Returning it moves the project `SUBMITTED → INCOMPLETE` and requires an `incomplete_reason`, which is stored on the project and returned to the applicant. Rejecting moves the project `SUBMITTED → REJECTED`, which is terminal and sets every one of the project's measure lines to `install_status` `VOID`. All three decisions stamp `reviewed_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID` and increment the project's version by one — and that stamped identifier is what the segregation-of-duties checks on reservation and on payment later read.

Reserving funds is the moment the appropriation is actually committed, and it is a `ProgramReviewer` action taking the `expected_program_version` demanded by §2.8 plus optional `reservation_notes`. It requires the project to be `APPROVED`, the program to be `OPEN`, the contractor to be `ENROLLED_ACTIVE`, the acting `X-User-ID` to differ from the project's `reviewed_by_user_id`, the household's `lifetime_cap_remaining_cents` to be at least the project's `provisional_total_incentive_cents`, and the program's `budget_available_cents` to be at least that same amount.

When those conditions hold, the operation writes a reservation in status `RESERVED` naming the `project_id` holding the funds and the `program_id` whose appropriation is encumbered. Its `reserved_amount_cents` and its immutable `initial_reserved_amount_cents` are both set to the project's `provisional_total_incentive_cents`; `reserved_at` is the logical clock's instant; `expires_at` is that instant plus the program's `reservation_ttl_days` days; `extension_count` and `extension_days_granted` start at 0; `committed_at` and `release_reason` are null. The initial amount is never mutated afterwards — it exists precisely so the budget invariant can be proven across later reconciliation. The same operation debits the program's `budget_available_cents` and credits its `budget_reserved_cents` by the reserved amount, incrementing the program's version; debits the household's `lifetime_cap_remaining_cents` and credits its `encumbered_cents` by the same amount, incrementing the household's version; and moves the project `APPROVED → RESERVED`, incrementing the project's version. A reservation's own lifecycle terminates in `COMMITTED`, `EXPIRED`, `RELEASED`, or `CLAWED_BACK`. Reservations can be listed filtered by `project_id`, `program_id`, and `status`.

Reservations expire, and expiry is driven deliberately rather than by a background timer. A `ProgramAdmin` runs the expiry sweep for a program, which collects that program's reservations in status `RESERVED` whose `expires_at` is less than or equal to the logical clock — the comparison is inclusive, so a reservation expiring exactly now does expire — and processes them in `expires_at` ascending order and then `created_at` ascending order. Ordering never falls back to identifiers. Reservations in `COMMITTED`, `RELEASED`, `EXPIRED`, or `CLAWED_BACK` are skipped, and a `SUSPENDED` or `CLOSED` program is still swept. Each due reservation moves `RESERVED → EXPIRED` with `release_reason` set to `EXPIRY`, and that transition drags a chain of cascade hops behind it: the program's `budget_reserved_cents` is debited and its `budget_available_cents` credited by the reservation's `reserved_amount_cents` with the program's version incremented; the household's `encumbered_cents` is debited and its `lifetime_cap_remaining_cents` credited by the same amount with the household's version incremented; the project moves to `EXPIRED` from either `RESERVED` or `CONTRACTOR_HOLD`; caused by that project transition, every measure line of the project has `install_status` set to `VOID`; and, also caused by it, every inspection of the project still in `SCHEDULED` is moved to `CANCELLED`. Each of those records has its own version incremented. The sweep reports a `program_id`, a `swept_reservation_count`, a `released_cents` total summing the released reserved amounts, an `expired_project_ids` array in processing order, and the resulting `program_budget_available_cents`. It increments the program's version once per released reservation.

An extension buys a project more time, and may be requested by an `Applicant`, a `Contractor`, or a `ProgramAdmin` supplying `additional_days` and an optional `extension_reason`. The extension window opens at the reservation's `expires_at` minus the program's `extension_window_days` days, and the lower bound is inclusive: a request made exactly `extension_window_days` before expiry is accepted, while a request made earlier than that is refused with `ERR_EXTENSION_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN`. The upper bound is exclusive: the logical clock must be strictly before `expires_at`, so a request made at or after expiry is refused with `ERR_RESERVATION_EXPIRED` by the state gate of §2.10, which is evaluated before any of the extension limits below. The reservation's `extension_count` must be strictly below the program's `max_extensions_per_reservation`, or the request is refused with `ERR_EXTENSION_LIMIT_REACHED`. And `additional_days` must be at least 1 and no more than the program's `max_extension_days`, or the request is refused with `ERR_EXTENSION_DAYS_EXCEEDED`. The three checks run in exactly that inner order. A granted extension pushes `expires_at` out by `additional_days` days, increments `extension_count` by one, adds `additional_days` to `extension_days_granted`, and increments the reservation's version. The grant creates no separate extension resource — it modifies the reservation itself, so it returns `200 OK` carrying the updated reservation, and the optional `extension_reason` is validated as a string but not persisted on it. Only the reservation named in the path is considered, and its project must be in `RESERVED` or `CONTRACTOR_HOLD`; a reservation that is not in status `RESERVED` is refused with `ERR_RESERVATION_NOT_ACTIVE`, and one already past its `expires_at` before the sweep has run is refused with `ERR_RESERVATION_EXPIRED`.

### 3.7. Installation Reporting and Crew Capacity

The installer of record reports completed work on a reserved project, supplying an `install_start_date` and `install_end_date` in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, the `installed_panel_amps` attested after the work, an `actual_project_cost_cents` invoiced total, and a `measure_results` array. Each entry of `measure_results` names a `measure_code` together with the `verified_quantity` and `actual_cost_cents` for that line. A code that is not one of the project's own lines is refused with `ERR_MEASURE_NOT_ON_PROJECT`.

The report moves the project `RESERVED → INSTALLED` and is permitted only when the project is `RESERVED`, the acting contractor's identifier equals the project's `contractor_id`, the project's reservation is still in status `RESERVED` with the logical clock strictly before its `expires_at`, the contractor is `ENROLLED_ACTIVE` with both `license_expires_on` and `insurance_expires_on` on or after the logical clock's date, `install_end_date` is not earlier than `install_start_date`, the crew-capacity rule below is satisfied, and `installed_panel_amps` is at least the largest `required_panel_capacity_amps` across the reported installed measures. Falling short on that last condition is `ERR_PANEL_CAPACITY_INSUFFICIENT`. On success the project records `actual_project_cost_cents`, `install_start_date`, `install_end_date`, and `installed_panel_amps`; each reported line moves `install_status` from `PLANNED` to `INSTALLED` and stores its `actual_cost_cents`, with each line's version incremented; and the project's version increments. A line's `install_status` evolves independently of its `verification_status`, so a line can sit at `INSTALLED` while still `PENDING` verification — the two dimensions live on the same row but never gate each other. On the physical dimension a line goes `PLANNED` → `INSTALLED` when installation is reported, and reaches the terminal `VOID` from either `PLANNED` or `INSTALLED` — from `PLANNED` when the project is rejected at review or expires, and from `INSTALLED` when the project reaches `FAILED_FINAL`. On the verification dimension a line goes `PENDING` → `VERIFIED` on a passing or conditionally passing post-installation inspection, `PENDING` → `REJECTED` when an inspection names it or its anchor, `REJECTED` → `PENDING` when a remediation cycle reopens it, and `VERIFIED` → `CLAWED_BACK` — terminal — when a post-payment spot failure recovers the money.

Crew capacity is an **aggregate** constraint over closed calendar-date intervals. The requested window is the closed interval from `install_start_date` through `install_end_date` inclusive. The occupied windows are those of *other* rebate projects with the same `contractor_id` whose status is `INSTALLED`, `INSPECTION_FAILED`, `REMEDIATION`, `VERIFIED`, or `PAID` and whose `install_start_date` and `install_end_date` are both non-null. Projects in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `APPROVED`, or `RESERVED` have no install window yet, and windows belonging to projects in `EXPIRED`, `FAILED_FINAL`, `CLAWED_BACK`, or `REJECTED` are released. Two windows overlap when the requested start is on or before the existing end **and** the existing start is on or before the requested end — closed on both ends, so sharing a single calendar date is an overlap. Comparison is on whole dates, not timestamps. If the number of overlapping occupied windows is greater than or equal to the contractor's `crew_capacity`, the report is refused with `ERR_CONTRACTOR_CREW_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`; overlaps are therefore permitted right up to one fewer than the crew capacity.

### 3.8. Inspection Scheduling and the Three Result Branches

Inspections come in two flavours, and the `inspection_type` decides which project states they touch: a `SPOT` inspection is a quality-standards audit, while a `POST_INSTALL` inspection is the verification gate that stands between an installed project and its money. A `ProgramReviewer` or `ProgramAdmin` schedules one by naming the `project_id`, the `inspector_id`, the `inspection_type`, and the on-site window as a `scheduled_start` that is inclusive and a `scheduled_end` that is exclusive. The inspection is created in `SCHEDULED` with `scheduled_by_user_id` stamped, `result_recorded_at` and `recorded_by_user_id` null, `deficiency_count` at 0, `deficiency_severity_max` at 0, `rejected_measure_codes` an empty array, and `notes` null. Inspections can be listed filtered by `project_id`, `inspector_id`, `inspection_type`, and `status`.

Inspector availability is a **single-capacity** constraint over half-open time intervals with a travel buffer, and it is a genuinely different rule from crew capacity. The occupied windows are every inspection of the **same** inspector — across every program and every project, since the candidate project is irrelevant — whose status is `SCHEDULED`, `PASSED`, `CONDITIONAL_PASS`, or `FAILED`; a `CANCELLED` inspection frees its slot. The travel buffer is 45 minutes. A requested window conflicts with an existing one when the requested start falls before the existing `scheduled_end` plus the buffer **and** the existing `scheduled_start` falls before the requested end plus the buffer. Capacity is exactly one, so a single conflicting window is enough to refuse the request with `ERR_INSPECTOR_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Timestamps are compared at minute resolution and the interval is half-open on the right, so a window beginning exactly 45 minutes after another ends is accepted.

Scheduling a `POST_INSTALL` inspection for a project sitting in `REMEDIATION` also returns that project `REMEDIATION → INSTALLED` for re-inspection, creating the re-inspection in `SCHEDULED` and incrementing the project's version. That is permitted only when no inspection of the project is currently in `SCHEDULED`; otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_OPEN_INSPECTION_PENDING`. The chain `INSTALLED → INSPECTION_FAILED → REMEDIATION → INSTALLED` is the re-inspection cycle, and it is the second reason the project machine is cyclic.

A scheduled inspection may be cancelled by a `ProgramReviewer` or `ProgramAdmin` with an optional `cancellation_reason`, moving it `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED` and incrementing its version; the cancelled window stops participating in the conflict rule. The same `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED` transition also fires as a system cascade whenever the inspection's project moves to `EXPIRED`, `FAILED_FINAL`, or `CLAWED_BACK`.

Recording the outcome is exclusively a `FieldInspector` action, supplying a `result` together with optional `deficiency_count`, `deficiency_severity_max` (an integer from 0 for none through 5 for life safety), `rejected_measure_codes`, and `notes`. Every branch requires the inspection to still be in `SCHEDULED` — a second attempt is refused with `ERR_INSPECTION_ALREADY_RECORDED` — and requires the acting inspector's identifier to equal the inspection's `inspector_id`. Every branch stamps `result_recorded_at` from the logical clock and `recorded_by_user_id` with the acting identifier, recomputes the contractor's `quality_score_rolling` and `completed_inspection_count`, and increments the inspection's version. For a `POST_INSTALL` result there are two further preconditions: no earlier `SPOT` inspection of the same project may carry a `recorded_by_user_id` equal to the acting identifier, and every measure line of the project must be at `install_status` `INSTALLED` carrying the `verified_quantity` and `actual_cost_cents` that the installation report supplied. Beyond that shared spine, the three result branches diverge sharply.

**The pass branch** moves the inspection `SCHEDULED → PASSED`. On a `POST_INSTALL` inspection of an installed project it moves the project `INSTALLED → VERIFIED`: every measure line moves `verification_status` from `PENDING` to `VERIFIED` and stores its `verified_quantity` from the result payload, the final allocation runs over the project and writes each line's `final_incentive_cents` and the project's `final_total_incentive_cents`, and the project's active stacking declaration, if one exists, is recomputed along with the project's `stacking_adjusted_state_incentive_cents`. The project's version increments. A passing `SPOT` inspection of a paid project changes no money at all.

**The conditional-pass branch** moves the inspection `SCHEDULED → CONDITIONAL_PASS` and is permitted only when `deficiency_severity_max` is no greater than 2. It drives the same project transition `INSTALLED → VERIFIED` with the same line-level verification, the same final allocation, and the same stacking recomputation — but it additionally sets the project's `conditional_pass_flag` to true, which means every final line amount is reduced by the ten-percent conditional reduction before the cap stages run. The reduction therefore lands *before* group and household proration, not after.

**The fail branch** moves the inspection `SCHEDULED → FAILED`, and what happens next depends on which kind of inspection failed and on how many remediation cycles the project has already consumed.

A `POST_INSTALL` failure on a project whose `remediation_count` is still strictly below the program's `max_remediations_per_project` moves the project `INSTALLED → INSPECTION_FAILED` and unrolls a three-hop cascade. First, every measure line named in `rejected_measure_codes` moves `verification_status` from `PENDING` to `REJECTED` with `rejection_reason` set to `INSPECTION_DEFICIENCY` and its `final_incentive_cents` set to 0. Second — and caused by that first hop, not by the request — every measure line whose `pinned_is_panel_upgrade` is true and whose qualifying appliance anchor line has now become `REJECTED` also moves `verification_status` from `PENDING` to `REJECTED`, this time with `rejection_reason` set to `ANCHOR_REJECTED`. Third — and caused by the second hop — the project's `final_total_incentive_cents` is recomputed by the final allocation over only the lines still eligible for verification, so the project total drops by whatever the rejected lines had been allocated. The contractor's rolling quality score is recomputed, which may itself take the contractor `ENROLLED_ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` and thereby move that contractor's *other* reserved projects `RESERVED → CONTRACTOR_HOLD`. Each touched record's version increments.

The installer may then open a remediation cycle on that failed project, supplying optional `remediation_notes` and an optional `measure_results` array. That moves the project `INSPECTION_FAILED → REMEDIATION`, permitted only when the acting contractor's identifier matches the project's `contractor_id`, `remediation_count` is still below the program's limit, and the project's reservation is still in status `RESERVED` with the logical clock strictly before its `expires_at`. The cycle increments `remediation_count` by one and resets every measure line whose `verification_status` is `REJECTED` back to `PENDING`, clearing each line's `rejection_reason` and incrementing each line's version. That `REJECTED → PENDING` step is what closes the verification-dimension cycle on a measure line.

A `POST_INSTALL` failure on a project whose `remediation_count` has already reached the program's `max_remediations_per_project` is the end of the road: the project moves `INSTALLED → FAILED_FINAL`, which is terminal, and the money unwinds. The project's reservation moves `RESERVED → RELEASED` with `release_reason` set to `FAILED_REINSPECTION`. Caused by that release, the program's `budget_reserved_cents` is debited and its `budget_available_cents` credited by the reservation's `reserved_amount_cents` with the program's version incremented, and the household's `lifetime_cap_remaining_cents` is credited and its `encumbered_cents` debited by the same amount with the household's version incremented. Also caused by that release, a clawback action is appended with `reason` `FAILED_REINSPECTION`, `recovered_cents` of 0 — nothing was ever paid, so nothing is recovered from the household — and `released_reservation_cents` equal to the released amount. Every measure line of the project has `install_status` set to `VOID` and `verification_status` set to `REJECTED`. The contractor's rolling quality score is recomputed, and the project's version increments.

A `SPOT` failure recorded against a project already in `PAID` is the post-payment clawback, described in §3.9.

### 3.9. Benefit Stacking, Disbursement and Post-Payment Recovery

Households often receive money for the same equipment from a utility program and from the federal tax code, and the state will not let the combined public benefit run away from what the project actually cost. An `Applicant`, `Contractor`, or `ProgramAdmin` therefore files a stacking declaration against the project naming the `utility_program_name` paying the additional rebate, the `utility_rebate_cents` the household will receive (zero or more), and optionally the `federal_credit_claimed_cents` the household intends to claim (zero or more). That last figure is recorded strictly for the audit trail: it never enters any computation. A new declaration moves any prior `ACTIVE` declaration of the same project to `SUPERSEDED`, so at most one declaration per project is `ACTIVE` at a time, and that single active row is the authoritative one. Declarations can be listed filtered by `status`.

The stacking limit is evaluated in a pinned order, exactly once, with no iteration:

1. The cost basis is the project's `actual_project_cost_cents` when that has been recorded, and its `estimated_project_cost_cents` otherwise.
2. The utility contribution is the declaration's `utility_rebate_cents`.
3. The state incentive going in is the project's `final_total_incentive_cents` when that is not null, and its `provisional_total_incentive_cents` otherwise. This value is stored as the declaration's `computed_state_incentive_before_cents`.
4. A first federal credit figure is computed as the program's `federal_credit_pct` multiplied by the cost basis less the state incentive less the utility contribution, floored to whole cents, clamped up to 0 if that came out negative, and then clamped down to the program's `federal_credit_cap_cents`.
5. The total benefit is the state incentive plus the utility contribution plus that federal credit figure. The limit is the cost basis multiplied by the program's `max_combined_benefit_pct`, floored to whole cents, and is stored as the declaration's `combined_benefit_limit_cents`.
6. If the total benefit exceeds the limit, the allowed state incentive becomes the state incentive less the excess, clamped up to 0; otherwise the allowed state incentive is the state incentive unchanged. The result is stored as `allowed_state_incentive_cents`.
7. The federal credit is then recomputed **exactly once** from the reduced state incentive: the program's `federal_credit_pct` multiplied by the cost basis less the allowed state incentive less the utility contribution, floored, clamped up to 0, and clamped down to `federal_credit_cap_cents`. The result is stored as `allowed_federal_credit_cents`. Because reducing the state incentive raises the net cost, this recomputed credit can legitimately come out larger than the first figure, and the system deliberately does **not** loop back to step 6 to squeeze it again.
8. Whatever overage survives is recorded rather than iterated away: `residual_overage_cents` is the allowed state incentive plus the utility contribution plus the recomputed federal credit, less the limit, clamped up to 0.

The allowed state incentive is also written onto the project as `stacking_adjusted_state_incentive_cents`, and the project's version increments. A declaration whose arithmetic would drive the allowed state incentive to 0 is refused with `ERR_STACKING_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` rather than recorded. The same evaluation is re-run whenever an inspection result changes `final_total_incentive_cents` while an `ACTIVE` declaration exists, so the declaration and the project stay consistent as the final total moves.

Disbursement is the last money-moving step, available to a `ProgramReviewer` or `ProgramAdmin` with an optional `payment_reference`. It requires the project to be `VERIFIED`, the program to be `OPEN`, the project's reservation to be in status `RESERVED`, no inspection of the project to be in `SCHEDULED` — otherwise `ERR_OPEN_INSPECTION_PENDING` — and the acting `X-User-ID` to differ from the project's `reviewed_by_user_id`.

The amount paid is reconciled against what was reserved. The basis is the project's `stacking_adjusted_state_incentive_cents` when the project has an `ACTIVE` stacking declaration, and its `final_total_incentive_cents` otherwise. The disbursed amount is the smaller of that basis and the reservation's `initial_reserved_amount_cents`, and the release difference is the initial reserved amount less the disbursed amount — never negative, because the disbursement is capped by the initial reservation. The operation writes `disbursed_amount_cents` on the project and reconciles the reservation's `reserved_amount_cents` down to the same figure, moving the reservation `RESERVED → COMMITTED` and stamping `committed_at` from the logical clock. On the program side, `budget_reserved_cents` is debited by the reservation's `initial_reserved_amount_cents`, `budget_committed_cents` is credited by the disbursed amount, and `budget_available_cents` is credited by the release difference, with the program's version incremented. On the household side, `encumbered_cents` is reconciled to the disbursed amount and `lifetime_cap_remaining_cents` is credited by the release difference, with the household's version incremented. The project moves `VERIFIED → PAID`, which is terminal, and its version increments. Both conservation identities of §2.7 must hold when the dust settles.

Payment is not the end of accountability. When a `FieldInspector` records a failing `SPOT` result against a project already in `PAID` — the spot inspection must belong to that project and still be in `SCHEDULED` — the project moves `PAID → CLAWED_BACK`, which is terminal, and a four-hop recovery cascade fires. The project's reservation moves `COMMITTED → CLAWED_BACK` with `release_reason` set to `CLAWBACK`. Caused by that reservation clawback, the program's `budget_committed_cents` is debited by the reservation's `reserved_amount_cents`, its `budget_available_cents` credited by the same amount, and its `budget_clawed_back_cents` incremented by the same amount, with the program's version incremented — note that the clawed-back counter grows while the three conserved buckets rebalance among themselves. Also caused by the reservation clawback, the household's `encumbered_cents` is debited and its `lifetime_cap_remaining_cents` credited by the recovered amount, with the household's version incremented. And also caused by it, a clawback action is appended with `reason` `POST_PAYMENT_INSPECTION_FAILURE` and `recovered_cents` equal to `disbursed_before_cents`, the project's `disbursed_amount_cents` as it stood immediately before the action. Every measure line of the project has `verification_status` set to `CLAWED_BACK`, the terminal value of that dimension, and the project's `disbursed_amount_cents` is set to 0. The contractor's rolling quality score is recomputed and the project's version increments.

A clawback action is a permanent audit row. Besides its `reason`, it names the `project_id` the recovery applies to, the `reservation_id` whose funds were released or recovered, and the `triggered_by_inspection_id` of the failed inspection that caused it. Alongside `disbursed_before_cents` and `recovered_cents` it records `released_reservation_cents` — the amount this action returned to the program's `budget_available_cents` — and, for reconciliation, `program_budget_available_after_cents` and `household_cap_remaining_after_cents` capturing those two balances immediately after the action, plus `recorded_by_user_id` for the actor whose request produced the cascade. The distinction between the two reasons is exactly the distinction between releasing and recovering: `FAILED_REINSPECTION` releases a still-reserved amount and recovers nothing from the household, while `POST_PAYMENT_INSPECTION_FAILURE` recovers an amount that had already been disbursed. Clawback actions can be listed filtered by `project_id`, `reservation_id`, `triggered_by_inspection_id`, and `reason`.
