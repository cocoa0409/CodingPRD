# Brand Inspection Board API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A state brand inspection board sits at the intersection of property law and animal agriculture. It records the brands that identify livestock ownership, it renews and retires those records on a statutory calendar, and it puts commissioned inspectors in the field whenever cattle, horses or sheep change hands, cross a district line, or arrive at a packing plant. Every one of those touchpoints produces a paper trail today — a filing receipt, an inspection certificate, a bill of sale, an impound ticket, a fee invoice — and each of those papers is presently reconciled by hand across offices that do not share a ledger. The consequence is familiar to every board: brands recorded twice in the same district because two clerks worked from two drawers, estray animals held past their statutory release because nobody tracked the twentieth day, and inspection fees that quietly age into uncollectable arrears.

This document specifies the Brand Inspection Board API, a single system of record that replaces those drawers. It governs four intertwined lifecycles: the **recording lifecycle**, in which an owner's filing is examined, corrected, and either recorded as a brand of record or refused because an earlier claimant outranks it; the **inspection lifecycle**, in which an owner requests inspection, the board assigns a commissioned inspector to a concrete window, and the inspector enters an ownership finding on every lot of head presented; the **disposition lifecycle**, in which an unidentified animal becomes an estray case with a statutory hold, an auction, and a proceeds distribution, or a mismatched brand becomes a theft investigation and a law-enforcement referral; and the **fiscal lifecycle**, in which inspection work is assessed, ages into delinquency, is remitted against, waived, or unwound.

The design constraint that shapes everything below is that these lifecycles are not independent. A theft investigation opened in the field encumbers a brand record, and that encumbrance freezes the renewal filing an owner lodged against the same record a week earlier. A conflict claim sustained by a recording officer revokes a brand of record, which invalidates the ownership finding on every lot read against it, which in turn tears down inspection windows an administrator had already assigned. Because the board's obligations are statutory rather than commercial, the system must be conservative: it refuses work it cannot justify, it records who did what, and it never lets one office's convenience silently overwrite another office's finding. The API is mounted under `/brand-inspection`.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the rules that hold across every endpoint in the API, regardless of which lifecycle the caller is exercising. Where §3 describes what the board does, this section describes how every request is admitted, evaluated, stamped and answered.

### 2.1. Board Users and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers that together establish the caller's operating context: `X-User-ID`, which identifies the acting individual or operator account, and `X-User-Role`, which names the capacity the caller is acting in. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` carries a value that is not one of the six roles the board recognises, the request is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401 Unauthorized) before any other consideration.

The six roles are `LivestockOwner`, `FieldInspector`, `BrandRecorder`, `FeeAdministrator`, `TheftInvestigator` and `BoardAdmin`. A `LivestockOwner` is a producer of record: they file brand applications and renewals, request inspections, tender ownership instruments, claim estrayed head and remit fees. A `FieldInspector` is a commissioned officer who works assigned orders, records the head presented, and enters ownership findings. A `BrandRecorder` is the board's recording officer, who examines filings, returns them for correction, records or rejects them, and adjudicates conflict claims. A `FeeAdministrator` is the board's fee officer, who waives and voids assessments, reconciles remittances, disburses estray proceeds and runs the board's delinquency sweep. A `TheftInvestigator` handles brand-mismatch referrals to law enforcement. A `BoardAdmin` is the operations administrator, maintaining owners, districts and commissions, assigning inspectors to orders, deciding estray claims, driving the statutory auction, and operating the board's logical clock. Each endpoint in §4 names the roles admitted to it; a caller whose role is not on that list is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403 Forbidden).

Two of the six roles are backed by a persisted entity, and for those two `X-User-ID` is not merely an audit token but the key that ownership is checked against. When `X-User-Role` is `LivestockOwner`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Owner` row; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Owner` compare that resource's owner-side foreign key to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `FieldInspector`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Inspector` row; ownership checks on an inspection order compare the order's `inspector_id` to `X-User-ID`. The remaining four roles carry an opaque identity. When `X-User-Role` is `BrandRecorder`, `FeeAdministrator`, `TheftInvestigator` or `BoardAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it where a workflow calls for attribution — as `examined_by_user_id` and `recorded_by_user_id` on a brand application, and as `closed_by_user_id` on a theft investigation — but it never compares such an identifier to any row's `id`.

Ownership enforcement carries its own rejection code, `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403 Forbidden), and it guards exactly thirteen write operations. Nine of them belong to a `LivestockOwner` acting on their own affairs: filing a new brand application, filing a renewal application against an existing record, resubmitting a corrected filing, requesting an inspection, cancelling one's own inspection order, filing a conflict claim (where `claimant_owner_id` must equal `X-User-ID`), recording a bill of sale (where `X-User-ID` must equal either the instrument's `buyer_owner_id` or its `seller_owner_id`), tendering a fee remittance, and topping up a short-paid remittance. The remaining four belong to a `FieldInspector` acting on an order they personally hold: opening the order in the field, recording a lot of head under it, entering an ownership finding on one of its lots, and closing out the field work. In each of the nine owner cases the comparison is between `X-User-ID` and the owner-side identifier on the target row; in each of the four inspector cases it is between `X-User-ID` and the target order's `inspector_id`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Endpoints that return a collection accept two query-string parameters. `limit` caps the number of rows returned; when it is omitted the server applies a default of 10, and a supplied value must be at least 1 and at most 100. `offset` is the number of rows to skip before the returned page begins; when it is omitted the server applies a default of 0.

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, every list is ordered by the row's creation timestamp in ascending order, oldest first. Every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope, where `total_count` reports the number of rows matching the filter across all pages and `has_more` reports whether any rows remain beyond the returned page.

### 2.4. Common Record Properties and the Version Token

Every record the board persists is addressable by an `id` that the server mints as a UUID. The identifier carries no type prefix and no embedded meaning; it is opaque to clients and is never supplied by them.

Most records also carry three system-managed properties. `created_at` is an ISO 8601 timestamp in UTC with three fractional digits, recording the logical-clock instant at which the row was first written. `updated_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format, refreshed on every mutation of the row — including mutations the server performs on its own initiative as part of a cascade, and including rewrites of purely derived values such as an owner's arrears aggregates or a district's live occupancy. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by one on every mutation of that row, again including server-initiated cascades. The `version` field is the token clients use for stale-record protection, as described in §2.7.

One record type is deliberately leaner. A bill of sale is an ownership instrument transcribed from paper and is immutable once written: it carries only `id` and `created_at`. A bill of sale has no `updated_at` field and no `version` field, and no operation in this API modifies one after creation.

### 2.5. The Board's Logical Clock

Nothing in this system reads the host's wall clock. All temporal reasoning — statutory hold expiry, registration expiry, fee due dates, prior-use backdating, inspection windows and the after-hours determination — is evaluated against a server-held logical clock. Before any client sets it, that clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time thereafter.

A `BoardAdmin` moves the clock by supplying a target instant as `to_iso8601`; the response reports the resulting instant as `current_utc`. Moving the clock is a pure assignment: no row is recomputed, no state advances, and no aggregate is refreshed as a side effect. The board's delinquency sweep is the only operation that acts on the newly current instant, and it must be invoked explicitly by a `FeeAdministrator` after the clock has been moved.

### 2.6. Money, Head Counts and Rounding Discipline

Every monetary quantity in this API is an integer number of cents and is named with a `_cents` suffix. There are no fractional cents, no floating-point money fields, and no currency strings. Head counts are whole animals, and every interval the system reasons about — an inspector's assigned window, an estray's occupancy of a district impound facility — is half-open, covering its start instant and excluding its end instant.

Rounding is applied sparingly and always half-up, and the point at which it is applied is part of the specification rather than an implementation detail. Two calculations round: the inspection fee assessment rounds the administrative surcharge once per lot before the lots are summed (§3.9), and the estray proceeds distribution rounds the uncapped handling fee and the handling-fee cap independently before comparing them (§3.8). The renewal filing fee rounds the late-filing penalty once before adding it to the base (§3.3). Every other arithmetic step in this system is exact integer arithmetic, and no result is rounded a second time.

### 2.7. Stale-Record Protection

Two write operations in this API can succeed on their own merits while nevertheless being wrong, because the caller's picture of a shared parent record has moved on since they read it. Both are therefore guarded by an optimistic-concurrency token: the request must carry the client's last-observed `version` of the parent record, and the server compares it to the parent's current `version` before proceeding. These version fields are required inputs, not optional ones; a request that omits them is malformed.

Filing a renewal application against a brand record — the nested renewal route `POST /brand-inspection/brand-registrations/{id}/renewal-applications` — requires `expected_registration_version`, carrying the client's last-observed `version` of the `BrandRegistration` named in the path. If the record's `version` has advanced since the client read it, the filing is refused with `ERR_STALE_REGISTRATION_VERSION` (HTTP 412 Precondition Failed). This guard matters precisely because a newly filed renewal legitimately supersedes any earlier pending renewal on the same record, so the second filing would otherwise be accepted on its own merits; the version mismatch is the only signal that the filer's view of the record — its status, its encumbrance count, its expiry date — is out of date.

Assigning an inspection order to an inspector — `POST /brand-inspection/inspection-orders/{id}/schedule` — requires `expected_inspector_version`, carrying the client's last-observed `version` of the `Inspector` being assigned. If the inspector's `version` has advanced since the client read that inspector's calendar, the assignment is refused with `ERR_STALE_INSPECTOR_VERSION` (HTTP 412 Precondition Failed). Another assignment, a commission status change, or a cascade that released one of the inspector's windows all advance that `version`, and each of them changes the calendar the caller was reasoning about — a non-overlapping window computed against a stale calendar would otherwise be accepted.

No other endpoint in this API takes a version field. Callers do not supply `version` values anywhere else, and the server performs no concurrency check on operations outside these two.

### 2.8. The Order in Which a Request Is Evaluated

Because most board operations are guarded by several independent checks at once, the order in which those checks fire is itself part of the contract. Callers rely on the first failure they see to tell them what to fix, and a check that fires out of turn masks the one that should have been reported. The server therefore evaluates every request through a fixed ladder, stopping at the first rung that fails and returning that rung's error.

The ladder runs as follows. **First**, the authentication rung: both context headers are present and `X-User-Role` names a declared role, or `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. **Second**, the authorization rung: the acting role appears in the called endpoint's role list, or `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`. **Third**, the request-shape rung, which applies to every write operation: every required body field is present, every enum-typed value is a declared member of its enumeration, `mailing_state` and `state_code` are each exactly two uppercase letters, `head_count` is at least 1, `amount_cents` is greater than 0, `additional_cents` is greater than 0, `impound_capacity_head` is at least 1, `max_daily_assignments` is at least 1, `travel_surcharge_cents` is at least 0, `documented_prior_use_years` is at least 0, `requested_window_end_at` is strictly later than `requested_window_start_at`, `scheduled_end_at` is strictly later than `scheduled_start_at`, and the assigned window sits inside the requested one, meaning `scheduled_start_at` is at or after `requested_window_start_at` and `scheduled_end_at` is at or before `requested_window_end_at`. Any of these failing yields `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400 Bad Request).

**Fourth**, path resolution. Every identifier embedded in the URL must resolve to a live row, and identifiers are resolved strictly left to right so that the outermost missing resource is the one reported. Depending on which identifier failed, the response carries `ERR_OWNER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TERRITORY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INSPECTOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REGISTRATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LOT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BILL_OF_SALE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ESTRAY_CASE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INVESTIGATION_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_REMITTANCE_NOT_FOUND`, each at HTTP 404 Not Found. **Fifth**, body reference resolution: every foreign key supplied in the request payload must likewise resolve, which covers `owner_id`, `territory_id`, `inspector_id`, `prior_registration_id`, `respondent_registration_id`, `contested_application_id`, `brand_registration_id`, `bill_of_sale_id`, `seller_owner_id`, `buyer_owner_id` and each element of `covered_order_ids`. These failures reuse the same not-found codes at HTTP 404.

**Sixth**, the ownership rung described in §2.1, yielding `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. **Seventh**, the segregation-of-duties rung, which applies only to recording a brand filing and yields `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. **Eighth**, the state rung: the target row must currently sit in a state from which the requested transition is declared, evaluated independently for each state dimension a row carries. This rung yields `ERR_APPLICATION_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_REGISTRATION_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_CLAIM_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_ORDER_NOT_SCHEDULABLE`, `ERR_ORDER_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_FEE_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_ESTRAY_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_INVESTIGATION_INVALID_STATE` or `ERR_REMITTANCE_INVALID_STATE`, each at HTTP 409 Conflict. **Ninth**, the stale-record rung of §2.7, yielding `ERR_STALE_REGISTRATION_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_INSPECTOR_VERSION` at HTTP 412.

Only once all of the above have passed does the server reach the board's substantive rules, and those too are ordered. Brand exclusivity by recording priority (`ERR_BRAND_CONFLICT_EXISTS`) is evaluated first, then the theft-hold gate on fee operations (`ERR_ORDER_HOLD_ACTIVE`), then the district intake posture for an estray impound (`ERR_FACILITY_INTAKE_RESTRICTED`), then inspector availability (`ERR_INSPECTOR_UNAVAILABLE`), then the buffered window collision (`ERR_INSPECTOR_WINDOW_CONFLICT`), then the statutory-hold gate on scheduling an auction (`ERR_HOLD_PERIOD_NOT_ELAPSED`), then the claim-window gate on filing an estray claim (`ERR_CLAIM_WINDOW_CLOSED`), then the inspector's daily assignment ceiling (`ERR_INSPECTOR_DAILY_LIMIT`), then projected facility capacity (`ERR_FACILITY_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`), then the uniqueness constraints (`ERR_DUPLICATE_DOCUMENT_NUMBER`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_BADGE_NUMBER`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_TERRITORY_CODE`), then the renewability of the prior record at recording time (`ERR_PRIOR_REGISTRATION_NOT_RENEWABLE`), then the tendered instrument's agreement with the lot (`ERR_BILL_OF_SALE_MISMATCH`), then the inspector's home-state match (`ERR_TERRITORY_MISMATCH`), then the owner's fiscal standing (`ERR_OWNER_STANDING_SUSPENDED`), then the late-renewal cutoff (`ERR_REGISTRATION_UNRENEWABLE`), and last of all the remittance overpayment test (`ERR_REMITTANCE_AMOUNT_INVALID`).

A worked consequence of this ordering: when a `BoardAdmin` attempts to assign an inspector to an order whose owner is in arrears, using a stale inspector version, against a window that also collides with existing work, the caller sees `ERR_STALE_INSPECTOR_VERSION` — the stale-record rung sits above every substantive scheduling rule. Once the version is refreshed, the same request surfaces `ERR_INSPECTOR_WINDOW_CONFLICT` before it ever surfaces `ERR_OWNER_STANDING_SUSPENDED`, because the window collision sits above the fiscal gate.

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline and Deterministic Processing Order

Many of the board's operations are not confined to the row named in the request. Sustaining a conflict claim reaches through a brand record to the lots read against it and onward to the inspection orders that parent those lots; entering a mismatch finding in the field reaches through a new investigation to a brand record and onward to every pending filing lodged against it. These multi-hop effects are not best-effort conveniences; they are part of the operation's definition and complete within the same request.

Three properties hold for every such cascade. First, each row a cascade touches is mutated in its own right: its `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increases by one, exactly as if it had been the direct target of a request. Second, when a cascade fans out over a set of rows, that set is processed in a deterministic order so that derived values and rank assignments are reproducible; unless a workflow in §3 names a different key, the fan-out order is by `created_at` ascending. Third, derived aggregates are recomputed from their source rows rather than adjusted incrementally, so that a compensating action returns an aggregate to exactly the value it held before the action it compensates. `head_count_presented` on an inspection order, `impound_current_head` and `intake_status` on a district, `active_assignment_count` on an inspector, `encumbrance_count` on a brand record, and both arrears figures plus `standing` on an owner are all recomputed rather than nudged.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties and Operator Attribution

The board separates the officer who examines a filing from the officer who records it. When a `BrandRecorder` takes a filing up for examination, the acting operator's `X-User-ID` is stamped onto the filing as `examined_by_user_id`; when a filing is recorded, the acting operator's `X-User-ID` is stamped as `recorded_by_user_id`. The two must differ. A `BrandRecorder` who attempts to record a filing they themselves examined is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403 Forbidden), and the enforcement compares the recording caller's `X-User-ID` against the value currently stamped in `examined_by_user_id`.

Attribution extends to the theft caseload as well, though without a separation requirement. A theft investigation records `opened_by_user_id` — the `X-User-ID` of the `FieldInspector` whose mismatch finding brought it into existence — and `closed_by_user_id`, the `X-User-ID` of the `TheftInvestigator` who closed it. Both are recorded for audit only; neither is compared to any row's `id`, and the same investigator may close a case they previously referred or reopened.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Board's Standing Registry: Owners, Districts and Commissions

Before any filing or inspection can happen, three kinds of reference record must exist, and all three are maintained by a `BoardAdmin`.

Enrolling a livestock owner of record captures three particulars: the `legal_name` under which the producer holds property, a `mailing_state` given as the two-letter USPS code of the producer's mailing address, and a `contact_email` at which the board serves delinquency notices. A newly enrolled owner begins in good fiscal standing — `standing` is `GOOD`, both `posted_arrears_cents` and `projected_arrears_cents` are 0, and `suspended_since` is null, since the owner has never been suspended. The board also offers a computed statement read on any owner, which returns `owner_id`, the current `standing`, both arrears figures side by side, an `open_order_count` of the owner's inspection orders whose fee is still outstanding, and a `delinquent_order_count` of those that have already aged past their due date.

An inspection district is created with a board-assigned `territory_code`, the two-letter `state_code` the district belongs to, a flat `travel_surcharge_cents` that is added to every fee assessed in that district, and an `impound_capacity_head` giving the maximum head the district's impound facility may hold at any single instant. The `territory_code` must be unique across every district on file; reusing one is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_TERRITORY_CODE`. Both `travel_surcharge_cents` and `impound_capacity_head` are bounded — a surcharge may be zero but never negative, and a facility must have room for at least one head. A new district opens empty: `impound_current_head` is 0 and `intake_status` is `OPEN`. Thereafter `impound_current_head` is the summed `head_count` of every estray case at that district whose status is `IMPOUNDED`, `CLAIM_FILED`, `AUCTION_SCHEDULED` or `SOLD`, and the district's intake posture follows directly from that occupancy: the district reads `RESTRICTED` once the live head count reaches ninety percent of `impound_capacity_head` — precisely, when the live head count multiplied by one hundred is greater than or equal to the capacity multiplied by ninety — and reads `OPEN` at any lower occupancy. A district that is exactly at the ninety percent line is already `RESTRICTED`.

Commissioning a field inspector records a board-issued `badge_number`, the `home_territory_id` of the district the inspector is commissioned in, and a `certification_expires_on` instant after which the commission is no longer valid for scheduling. The caller may also set `max_daily_assignments`, the ceiling on how many orders the inspector may hold on a single UTC calendar date; when the field is omitted the board applies its default of 3, and a supplied value must be at least 1. Badge numbers are unique across the whole commission roll, and a duplicate is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_BADGE_NUMBER`. A new inspector is commissioned with `status` `ACTIVE` and `version` 1. A `BoardAdmin` may later move an inspector's `status` between `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED` and `RETIRED` by supplying the new `status`; every such change increments the inspector's `version` by one, which by design invalidates any scheduling token a client was holding. The roster read exposes `active_assignment_count`, the number of orders currently held by that inspector whose scheduling dimension reads `SCHEDULED` or `IN_FIELD`, and the roster listing may be narrowed by `home_territory_id` or by commission `status`.

### 3.2. Filing a Brand and Recording It

A brand is not property until the board records it, and the road from filing to record runs through examination, optional correction, and a priority contest against everything already on file.

A `LivestockOwner` opens the road by filing an application. The filing names the applicant as `owner_id`, declares an `application_type` of `NEW`, `TRANSFER` or `RENEWAL`, and describes the mark itself: a `brand_symbol` giving the textual rendering of the design (`7X` or `BAR-L`, for instance), a `position_code` naming the body location the brand is applied to — one of `LEFT_RIB`, `RIGHT_RIB`, `LEFT_HIP`, `RIGHT_HIP`, `LEFT_SHOULDER` or `RIGHT_SHOULDER` — a `species_class` of `CATTLE`, `HORSE` or `SHEEP`, and the two-letter `state_code` the mark is being recorded in. Brand exclusivity is scoped per state, so the same symbol may legitimately be recorded in two states at once. A filing that renews or transfers an existing record names it as `prior_registration_id`; a `NEW` filing leaves that field null. The applicant may also claim unrecorded prior use of the mark, declaring `documented_prior_use_years` (a count of whole years, never negative, defaulting to 0) and setting `prior_use_affidavit_on_file` to indicate whether a notarised prior-use affidavit accompanies the filing; when no affidavit is on file that flag reads false, which is also its default.

Filing stamps three system values. `original_filed_at` records the logical-clock instant at which the application row was first created and is never changed again, not by correction and not by resubmission. `assessed_filing_fee_cents` records the filing fee owed, which for a `NEW` or `TRANSFER` filing is a flat 9,000 cents. And `effective_priority_at` records the instant from which the board will reckon this filing's place in line. That priority instant is the whole point of the prior-use affidavit: the board first determines the credited years, which is the smaller of the applicant's `documented_prior_use_years` and the statutory cap of 10 years when `prior_use_affidavit_on_file` is true, and is zero years whenever the affidavit is absent — an applicant who claims forty years of prior use without an affidavit is credited nothing at all. The credited years are then converted to days at 365 days per year, and `effective_priority_at` is `original_filed_at` moved backward by that many days, preserving the original time of day. A new filing enters at status `SUBMITTED` with `correction_rounds` at 0, `examined_by_user_id`, `recorded_by_user_id` and `rejection_reason` all null. Filings may be listed and narrowed by `owner_id`, by `prior_registration_id`, by `status` or by `application_type`.

Examination is a `BrandRecorder`'s work. Taking a `SUBMITTED` filing up moves it to `UNDER_EXAMINATION` and stamps `examined_by_user_id` with the examining operator's identifier, incrementing the filing's `version`. From `UNDER_EXAMINATION` the recorder has three exits. Returning the filing for correction advances `correction_rounds` by one and sends the filing to `RETURNED_FOR_CORRECTION` — but only while `correction_rounds` is still below the board's ceiling of two rounds. The first return (from 0) and the second return (from 1) both hand the filing back to the applicant; a third return, attempted when `correction_rounds` already stands at 2, does not hand it back at all. It advances `correction_rounds` to 3 and terminates the filing at `REJECTED` with `rejection_reason` set to the fixed string `MAX_CORRECTION_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`. The breakpoint is exact: a filing at `correction_rounds` 1 is returned, a filing at `correction_rounds` 2 is rejected.

An applicant whose filing sits in `RETURNED_FOR_CORRECTION` may resubmit it, moving it back to `SUBMITTED` — the cycle `UNDER_EXAMINATION → RETURNED_FOR_CORRECTION → SUBMITTED` may run twice before the ceiling terminates it. Resubmission accepts corrected values for `brand_symbol`, `position_code`, `species_class`, `documented_prior_use_years` and `prior_use_affidavit_on_file`, each overwriting the filed value only when supplied; fields the applicant omits keep whatever they held. Because the correction may change either prior-use input, `effective_priority_at` is recomputed from the same rule described above. Crucially, `original_filed_at` is not touched, so an applicant who corrects a clerical error does not surrender the queue position they filed for.

Recording is the second `BrandRecorder`'s work, and it is the only path out of `UNDER_EXAMINATION` that produces property. It is barred to the operator who examined the filing (§2.10). It is also barred when the filing is a `RENEWAL` whose prior record is no longer renewable: at recording time the record named by `prior_registration_id` must stand at `ACTIVE` or `GRACE`, and a prior record that has drifted to `LAPSED`, `SUPERSEDED`, `REVOKED` or `ENCUMBERED` yields `ERR_PRIOR_REGISTRATION_NOT_RENEWABLE` (HTTP 422). And it is barred by the exclusivity contest described in §3.3. When recording succeeds, the filing moves to `RECORDED`, `recorded_by_user_id` is stamped, and a brand of record comes into existence at status `ACTIVE`. The new record copies `brand_symbol`, `position_code`, `species_class` and `state_code` from the filing, points back at it through `source_application_id`, names the holder through its own `owner_id`, inherits the filing's `effective_priority_at` unchanged, stamps `recorded_at` from the logical clock, and sets `expires_on` to `recorded_at` advanced by 1,825 days. Its `encumbrance_count` starts at 0 and its `superseded_by_registration_id` is null. Recording a `RENEWAL` additionally retires the prior record, as §3.3 describes.

Rejection is the recorder's remaining exit and is available from two states. A filing that is `UNDER_EXAMINATION` and a filing held in `CONFLICT_HOLD` may both be rejected outright, in each case writing the recorder's supplied `rejection_reason` onto the filing and moving it to `REJECTED`. Attempting any of these recorder actions against a filing in a state that does not declare the transition is refused with `ERR_APPLICATION_INVALID_STATE`.

### 3.3. Recording Priority, Renewal and the Life of a Brand of Record

Two producers may honestly believe the same mark is theirs, and the board resolves that by queue position rather than by first-come service. All brands of record that share the same `state_code`, the same `brand_symbol`, the same `position_code` and the same `species_class` form one **conflict group**. Membership in the group is restricted to records that stand at `ACTIVE` or `GRACE`. A record at `ENCUMBERED`, `LAPSED`, `SUPERSEDED` or `REVOKED` has left the group: it occupies no rank, and it blocks nothing.

Inside a group, records are ordered by three keys applied in sequence — first by `effective_priority_at` ascending, so the earliest priority instant leads; then, for records sharing a priority instant, by the `correction_rounds` of the filing that produced each record, ascending, so a filing that needed no correction leads one that did; and finally by `created_at` ascending. A record's `priority_rank` is its one-based position in that ordering, so the leading record carries `priority_rank` 1.

That same ordering decides whether a filing may be recorded at all. Recording is refused when the filing's conflict group already holds a record, owned by an owner other than the applicant, whose sort key sorts strictly ahead of the filing's — the refusal is `ERR_BRAND_CONFLICT_EXISTS` (HTTP 409 Conflict). The rule is a genuine contest and not a first-writer bar: a filing whose sort key sorts ahead of every incumbent **is** recorded, enters at `priority_rank` 1, and demotes each incumbent by one rank. This is exactly why the prior-use affidavit matters — a producer who files second but proves ten years of documented prior use is backdated 3,650 days and takes the front of the queue from an incumbent who filed last month. Whenever a record is created, `priority_rank` is re-derived for every `ACTIVE` or `GRACE` member of the group, walking them in the group's own sort order: `effective_priority_at` ascending, then `correction_rounds` ascending, then `created_at` ascending.

A brand of record is not permanent. Its `expires_on` sits 1,825 days after `recorded_at`, and the board's delinquency sweep is what moves it across the calendar. A record standing at `ACTIVE` whose `expires_on` has been reached — that is, whose `expires_on` is at or before the logical clock — is moved to `GRACE`. A record standing at `GRACE` is moved to `LAPSED` once the clock has passed 90 days beyond its `expires_on`, meaning the clock is strictly later than `expires_on` advanced by 90 days; a clock sitting exactly on that ninetieth day still leaves the record in `GRACE`. The sweep processes records by `expires_on` ascending and then by `created_at` ascending. Reaching `LAPSED` is fatal to anything pending against the record: every renewal filing whose `prior_registration_id` names the newly lapsed record and whose status is `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_EXAMINATION`, `RETURNED_FOR_CORRECTION` or `CONFLICT_HOLD` is moved to `REJECTED` with `rejection_reason` set to `PRIOR_REGISTRATION_LAPSED`.

Renewal is therefore a race against that calendar, and the board charges for lateness. A renewal is lodged against a specific brand of record rather than filed free-standing, precisely so the filer can be held to the version of the record they were looking at (§2.7): the request names the applicant as `owner_id`, carries `expected_registration_version`, and may optionally supply corrected values for `brand_symbol`, `position_code`, `species_class`, `documented_prior_use_years` and `prior_use_affidavit_on_file`. Values the filer omits are inherited from the record being renewed, and `state_code` is always inherited. The resulting filing is created with `application_type` `RENEWAL` and `prior_registration_id` pointing at the record. Lodging the renewal increments that record's `version` by one. A renewal may only be lodged against a record that is still `ACTIVE`, `GRACE` or `ENCUMBERED`; against a record already `SUPERSEDED`, `LAPSED` or `REVOKED` the attempt is refused with `ERR_REGISTRATION_INVALID_STATE`.

The renewal filing fee starts from a base of 12,000 cents and is loaded with a late-filing penalty determined by how far past expiry the board's clock stands. The board counts whole elapsed days from the record's `expires_on` to the current logical clock, discarding any partial day. A renewal filed on or before the expiry instant — zero or fewer elapsed days — carries no penalty and costs the base amount. A renewal filed from one through thirty days past expiry carries a penalty of one quarter of the base; the thirtieth day is still inside the grace window and the thirty-first is not. A renewal filed from thirty-one through ninety days past expiry carries a penalty of three quarters of the base; the ninetieth day is still recoverable and the ninety-first is not. A renewal attempted more than ninety days past expiry is refused outright with `ERR_REGISTRATION_UNRENEWABLE` (HTTP 422). The penalty is computed against the base, rounded half up to whole cents, and added to the base to give `assessed_filing_fee_cents`.

Only the most recent renewal on a record survives. When a renewal is lodged, every earlier filing against the same `prior_registration_id` that still stands at `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_EXAMINATION`, `RETURNED_FOR_CORRECTION` or `CONFLICT_HOLD` is moved to `REJECTED` with `rejection_reason` set to `SUPERSEDED_BY_LATER_RENEWAL`, processed in `created_at` ascending order. This is the legitimate supersession that the stale-version guard exists to make deliberate rather than accidental. When a renewal is finally recorded, the prior record itself retires: a record standing at `ACTIVE` or `GRACE` moves to `SUPERSEDED`, and its `superseded_by_registration_id` is set to the identifier of the record just created, forming a chain that a caller can walk backwards through the register. Brands of record may be listed and narrowed by `owner_id`, by `source_application_id`, by `superseded_by_registration_id`, and by the four conflict-group keys `state_code`, `brand_symbol`, `position_code` and `species_class`, as well as by `status`.

### 3.4. Contesting a Recorded Brand

A producer who believes a recorded mark should not stand contests it rather than filing over it. A `LivestockOwner` opens a contest by naming themselves as `claimant_owner_id`, naming the record they are contesting as `respondent_registration_id`, and stating the statutory `grounds`, which is one of `PRIOR_USE`, `DECEPTIVE_SIMILARITY` or `RECORDING_ERROR`. Where the claimant has a filing of their own that the respondent record is blocking, they may name it as `contested_application_id`; a claimant with no pending filing leaves it null. A new contest enters at `FILED` with `decision_note` null. Contests may be listed and narrowed by `claimant_owner_id`, by `respondent_registration_id`, by `contested_application_id` or by `status`.

Adjudication is a `BrandRecorder`'s work and proceeds in two steps, `FILED → UNDER_ADJUDICATION → SUSTAINED` or `FILED → UNDER_ADJUDICATION → DISMISSED`. Taking the contest up moves it to `UNDER_ADJUDICATION` and increments its `version`; a contest taken up when it is not `FILED`, or decided when it is not `UNDER_ADJUDICATION`, is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_INVALID_STATE`. Deciding the contest requires a `decision` of either `SUSTAIN` or `DISMISS`, and the recorder may attach a written `decision_note` explaining the rationale; the note is written onto the contest in both outcomes.

Dismissal is the quiet outcome: the contest moves to `DISMISSED`, its `decision_note` is written, and the respondent record is left entirely untouched — no status change, no rank re-derivation, nothing.

Sustaining is the loud one, and it unwinds three levels of the board's work in a single request. Sustaining is permitted only against a respondent record standing at `ACTIVE`, `GRACE` or `ENCUMBERED`; against a record already `LAPSED`, `SUPERSEDED` or `REVOKED` the decision is refused with `ERR_REGISTRATION_INVALID_STATE`. When it proceeds, the first effect is that the respondent record moves to `REVOKED`. The second effect follows from the first: every lot of head that was read against that record and still stands at lot status `PRESENTED` has its ownership finding moved to `UNVERIFIABLE`, processed in `created_at` ascending order — the inspector's reading is no longer evidence of anything, because the mark it referenced is no longer a mark of record. The third effect follows from the second: every inspection order that parents one of those lots and whose scheduling dimension reads `SCHEDULED` or `IN_FIELD` is pulled back to `REQUESTED`, its `inspector_id` cleared, its `scheduled_start_at` and `scheduled_end_at` cleared, and the released inspector's `version` incremented by one, again processed in `created_at` ascending order. Field work already under way on a revoked mark is aborted and the order returns to the request queue to be re-assigned. All three levels complete within the sustaining request.

### 3.5. Getting an Inspector to the Livestock

An inspection order carries two state dimensions on the same row, and they evolve independently of one another. `schedule_status` tracks the physical work — where the order sits between request and field completion — and runs `REQUESTED`, `SCHEDULED`, `IN_FIELD`, `COMPLETED` and `CANCELLED`. `fee_status` tracks the money and is the subject of §3.9. Neither dimension constrains the other's declared transitions except where a workflow explicitly says so, and a state check evaluates only the dimension the requested operation acts on.

A `LivestockOwner` requests inspection by naming themselves as `owner_id`, naming the district the work will happen in as `territory_id`, declaring the statutory `inspection_purpose` — `SALE`, `MOVEMENT` or `SLAUGHTER` — and offering an availability window as `requested_window_start_at` and `requested_window_end_at`, where the end must be strictly later than the start. The new order enters at `schedule_status` `REQUESTED` and `fee_status` `UNASSESSED`, with `inspector_id`, `remittance_id`, `scheduled_start_at`, `scheduled_end_at` and `fee_due_on` all null, `assessed_fee_cents` and `head_count_presented` both 0, and `hold_flag` false. Orders may be listed and narrowed by `owner_id`, by `territory_id`, by `inspector_id`, by the settling `remittance_id`, or by either state dimension independently through `schedule_status` and `fee_status`.

Assignment is a `BoardAdmin`'s work and is the most heavily guarded operation in the API, because it commits a scarce public officer to a fixed block of time. The assignment names the `inspector_id` to be committed, the concrete `scheduled_start_at` and `scheduled_end_at` of the assigned window, and the `expected_inspector_version` token of §2.7. The order must be sitting at `REQUESTED`; an order already scheduled, in the field, completed or cancelled yields `ERR_ORDER_NOT_SCHEDULABLE`. The assigned window must fall inside the window the owner offered, and it must be a real interval — both conditions are enforced at the request-shape rung of §2.8.

Four substantive tests then decide whether the officer may be committed. **Availability:** the named inspector's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, and their `certification_expires_on` must be strictly later than `scheduled_end_at`; a suspended or retired commission, or one that expires at or before the proposed window ends, yields `ERR_INSPECTOR_UNAVAILABLE`. **Window occupancy:** the proposed window is tested against every other order already held by that inspector whose `schedule_status` is `SCHEDULED` or `IN_FIELD` — orders at `REQUESTED`, `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED` occupy nothing, and the order being scheduled never tests against itself. Each occupied window is padded by a travel buffer that depends on geography: when the occupied order sits in the same district as the one being scheduled the buffer is zero, and when it sits in a different district the buffer is 45 minutes on each comparison. The pair collides when the proposed start falls strictly before the occupied window's end extended by the buffer **and** the occupied window's start falls strictly before the proposed end extended by the buffer. Both comparisons are strict, which is what makes windows half-open: two back-to-back windows inside one district that touch exactly at an endpoint do not collide, while two windows that touch exactly at an endpoint across a district line do collide, because the 45-minute buffer pushes them into each other. A collision yields `ERR_INSPECTOR_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. **Daily ceiling:** among those same occupied orders, the board counts how many have a `scheduled_start_at` falling on the same UTC calendar date as the proposed start; if that count is already at or above the inspector's `max_daily_assignments`, the assignment yields `ERR_INSPECTOR_DAILY_LIMIT`. **Jurisdiction:** the inspector's home district and the order's district must carry the same `state_code`. Working a neighbouring district inside the same state is entirely permitted — it is exactly the case the cross-district travel buffer exists for — but crossing a state line is refused with `ERR_TERRITORY_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422).

One further gate is fiscal rather than operational: the order's owner must be in good standing. An owner whose `standing` reads `SUSPENDED` cannot have new work scheduled, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_OWNER_STANDING_SUSPENDED` (HTTP 402 Payment Required). §3.9 describes how an owner reaches that state and how they leave it.

When every gate clears, the order advances `REQUESTED → SCHEDULED`, its `inspector_id` and both scheduled instants are stamped from the payload, the assigned inspector's `version` increments by one, and that inspector's `active_assignment_count` is recomputed.

The assigned inspector opens the work by moving the order `SCHEDULED → IN_FIELD`; only the inspector named in `inspector_id` may do so, and their commission must still read `ACTIVE` at that moment. An order may also be withdrawn before or after assignment but never after the inspector has arrived: withdrawal is available from `REQUESTED` and from `SCHEDULED`, and moves the order to `CANCELLED`. Either the owner of the order or a `BoardAdmin` may withdraw it; when the caller acts as a `LivestockOwner` the ownership rung of §2.8 applies, whereas a `BoardAdmin` acts on any order. Withdrawal voids every lot recorded under the order, moving each to lot status `VOIDED`, and where the order had already been assigned it also releases the officer — `inspector_id`, `scheduled_start_at` and `scheduled_end_at` are cleared and the freed inspector's `version` increments by one. Attempting to withdraw an order that is already `IN_FIELD`, `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED` is refused with `ERR_ORDER_INVALID_STATE`.

### 3.6. Ownership Documentation and the Field Finding

Paperwork is tendered before the inspector arrives. A `LivestockOwner` records an ownership instrument by naming the `seller_owner_id` and `buyer_owner_id` of the two parties, the `document_number` printed on the instrument, the `sale_date` it bears, the `head_count` it declares, and the `species_class` it covers; the seller's transcription of the mark may be captured as `brand_symbol_declared`, which is left null when the instrument bears no brand. The caller must be one of the two named parties. `document_number` is unique across every instrument on file, and a reused number is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_DOCUMENT_NUMBER`. An instrument is immutable once written — nothing in this API amends one — and it may be located afterwards by `seller_owner_id` or by `buyer_owner_id`.

In the field, the assigned inspector records each **lot** of head presented under the order, which is only permitted while the order reads `IN_FIELD`. A lot declares its `species_class` and its `head_count`, which must be at least one animal. The inspector may name the recorded mark they read on the animals as `brand_registration_id`, leaving it null when the head carry no legible mark of record; they may name the instrument tendered for the lot as `bill_of_sale_id`, leaving it null when none was tendered; and they may transcribe any secondary earmark or tag notation as `earmark_code`. Each lot belongs to the order it was recorded under through `inspection_order_id`. A new lot enters with `lot_status` `PRESENTED`, `match_status` `UNCHECKED` and `disposition` `NONE`, and recording it refreshes the parent order's `head_count_presented`, which is the summed `head_count` of that order's lots whose `lot_status` is `PRESENTED`, `VERIFIED` or `HELD`. Lots may be listed and narrowed by `inspection_order_id`, by `brand_registration_id`, by `bill_of_sale_id`, by `match_status` or by `lot_status`.

The inspector then enters an ownership finding on each lot, supplying `finding` as one of `MATCH_CONFIRMED`, `BRAND_MISMATCH` or `NO_LEGIBLE_BRAND`, and optionally attaching a `bill_of_sale_id` at the moment of the finding. Every finding shares four preconditions: the parent order must read `IN_FIELD`, the caller must be the order's assigned inspector, and the lot itself must still be at `match_status` `UNCHECKED` with `lot_status` `PRESENTED`. A finding entered against a lot outside those conditions, or against an order not in the field, is refused with `ERR_ORDER_INVALID_STATE`.

**`MATCH_CONFIRMED`** is the clean outcome and requires that the lot actually names a recorded mark: `brand_registration_id` must be non-null and the record it names must stand at `ACTIVE` or `GRACE`. When an instrument is tendered with the finding, it must agree with what the inspector sees on three points at once — the instrument's `head_count` must equal the lot's `head_count`, its `species_class` must equal the lot's `species_class`, and its `buyer_owner_id` must equal the `owner_id` of the parent order, since the party presenting the head for inspection must be the party the paper says bought them. Any of the three disagreeing is refused with `ERR_BILL_OF_SALE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). On success the lot moves `UNCHECKED → MATCHED`, its `lot_status` becomes `VERIFIED`, its `disposition` becomes `CLEARED`, and the tendered `bill_of_sale_id` is written onto the lot when one was supplied.

**`BRAND_MISMATCH`** is the theft outcome and likewise requires a non-null `brand_registration_id` — the inspector read a mark of record, and the papers do not support it. The finding moves the lot `UNCHECKED → MISMATCHED` and sets off a four-level cascade described in §3.7.

**`NO_LEGIBLE_BRAND`** is the estray outcome and requires the opposite: `brand_registration_id` must be null, because there was no mark of record to read. Two district gates apply before the board will take the animals in, and they are described alongside the impound facility in §3.8. The finding moves the lot `UNCHECKED → UNMATCHED` and sets off a three-level cascade, also in §3.8.

Field work closes when the inspector completes the order, moving it `IN_FIELD → COMPLETED`. Completion is refused while any lot of the order that is not `VOIDED` still carries `match_status` `UNCHECKED`; the board will not close a certificate with an unexamined lot on it, and the attempt yields `ERR_ORDER_INVALID_STATE`. Completing recomputes `head_count_presented` and, in the same request, advances the order's fee dimension for the first time — see §3.9.

### 3.7. When the Brand and the Papers Disagree: Theft Investigations

A mismatch finding is the board's most consequential field entry, because it accuses somebody. Recording one sets off four levels of effect inside the same request, and each level is caused by the one above it.

At the first level the lot itself is impounded on paper: its `lot_status` becomes `HELD` and its `disposition` becomes `THEFT_REFERRAL`. At the second level a theft investigation comes into existence, opened at status `OPENED` with `outcome` `PENDING`, `reopen_count` 0, `referral_agency` and `closed_by_user_id` null, `animal_lot_id` naming the lot, `brand_registration_id` naming the mark the inspector read, `opened_at` stamped from the logical clock and `opened_by_user_id` stamped with the reporting inspector's `X-User-ID`; at the same moment the parent inspection order's `hold_flag` is raised to true. At the third level the mark itself is encumbered: a record standing at `ACTIVE` or `GRACE` moves to `ENCUMBERED` and its `encumbrance_count` rises by one, that count being the number of investigations against the record whose status is `OPENED` or `REFERRED`. At the fourth level the encumbrance freezes paperwork already in motion: every brand filing whose `prior_registration_id` names the encumbered record and whose status is `SUBMITTED` or `UNDER_EXAMINATION` is moved to `CONFLICT_HOLD`, processed in `created_at` ascending order. An owner who lodged a renewal last week and has done nothing wrong will find that renewal frozen because an inspector two districts away read their mark on somebody else's cattle — that freeze is intended, and it lifts only when the investigation closes.

A `TheftInvestigator` works the resulting caseload, which may be listed and narrowed by `animal_lot_id`, by `brand_registration_id` or by `status`. Referring the case moves it `OPENED → REFERRED` and records the `referral_agency` the file was handed to. Closing the case is available from either `OPENED` or `REFERRED`, moves it to `CLOSED`, records the investigator's `X-User-ID` as `closed_by_user_id`, and requires an `outcome` of either `SUBSTANTIATED` or `UNFOUNDED`. Closing always decrements the encumbered record's `encumbrance_count` by one, and it always clears the parent order's `hold_flag` back to false once no `OPENED` or `REFERRED` investigation remains against any lot of that order — an order carrying two open investigations stays held until the second one closes.

Beyond that, the two outcomes diverge sharply. An `UNFOUNDED` close exonerates the mark: once the decrement brings `encumbrance_count` to zero, the record moves `ENCUMBERED → ACTIVE` and every filing that was frozen against it is released from `CONFLICT_HOLD` to `UNDER_EXAMINATION`, processed in `created_at` ascending order. Note that released filings all land in `UNDER_EXAMINATION` regardless of which state they were frozen from — a filing that was `SUBMITTED` when the freeze hit comes back examinable rather than back to `SUBMITTED`. A `SUBSTANTIATED` close condemns it instead: the record moves `ENCUMBERED → REVOKED`, and every lot read against that record that still stands at `lot_status` `PRESENTED` has its `match_status` moved to `UNVERIFIABLE`, processed in `created_at` ascending order. This path stops at the lots; unlike a sustained conflict contest (§3.4), a substantiated theft close does not pull scheduled orders back to `REQUESTED`.

A closed case may be reopened, but only once. Reopening moves it `CLOSED → OPENED`, is permitted only while `reopen_count` is below the board's ceiling of one reopen, and requires that the encumbered record still stand at `ACTIVE`, `GRACE` or `ENCUMBERED` — a record already `REVOKED`, `LAPSED` or `SUPERSEDED` cannot be re-encumbered, and the attempt yields `ERR_REGISTRATION_INVALID_STATE`. A successful reopen advances `reopen_count` by one, returns `outcome` to `PENDING`, raises `encumbrance_count` by one and moves an `ACTIVE` or `GRACE` record back to `ENCUMBERED`, and re-raises the parent order's `hold_flag` to true. After that single reopen, `CLOSED` is effectively terminal: a second reopen attempt is refused with `ERR_INVESTIGATION_INVALID_STATE`, which is also the refusal for referring a case that is not `OPENED` or closing one that is already `CLOSED`.

### 3.8. Estrays: Impound, Statutory Hold, Auction and Proceeds

When an inspector reads no mark of record on a lot, the animals become the board's problem. Before the board will take them, the district facility must be willing and able to hold them, and two distinct gates test that.

The first gate is posture. The district's `intake_status` must read `OPEN` at the moment of the finding; a district that has already crossed the ninety-percent occupancy line described in §3.1 reads `RESTRICTED` and refuses further intake with `ERR_FACILITY_INTAKE_RESTRICTED`. The second gate is a forward-looking capacity projection, and unlike the inspector's window test it is an aggregate test rather than a pairwise one. The board projects the proposed occupancy as the half-open interval beginning at the logical clock and ending 20 days later. Every live estray case at the same district — those standing at `IMPOUNDED`, `CLAIM_FILED`, `AUCTION_SCHEDULED` or `SOLD` — is projected as its own half-open interval, beginning at that case's `impounded_at` and ending either at its `sale_at` when the case has reached `SOLD` or at its `statutory_release_on` otherwise. Cases at `RELEASED_TO_CLAIMANT`, `PROCEEDS_DISBURSED` or `FORFEITED_TO_BOARD` have vacated the facility and contribute nothing. The board then cuts the proposed interval at every interval endpoint among those projections and its own, and for each resulting half-open segment it sums the new lot's `head_count` together with the `head_count` of every live case whose interval covers that segment. If any segment's total exceeds the district's `impound_capacity_head`, the impound is refused with `ERR_FACILITY_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Because the intervals are half-open, a case whose occupancy ends exactly at the instant the new intake begins contributes nothing to the load.

Clearing both gates produces a three-level cascade. The lot moves to `lot_status` `HELD` with `disposition` `ESTRAY_HOLD`. An estray case is created at status `IMPOUNDED`, naming the lot as `animal_lot_id` and the district as `territory_id`, copying the lot's `head_count`, stamping `impounded_at` from the logical clock, and setting `statutory_release_on` to `impounded_at` advanced by 20 days. Its `claimant_owner_id`, `estimated_gross_cents`, `gross_proceeds_cents` and `sale_at` all start null, all four money buckets — `care_cost_cents`, `handling_fee_cents`, `claimant_residual_cents` and `board_forfeiture_cents` — start at 0, `distribution_variant` starts at `NONE`, and `claim_denied_count` starts at 0. Finally the district's `impound_current_head` rises by the lot's `head_count` and its `intake_status` is recomputed against the ninety-percent breakpoint, which may itself flip the district to `RESTRICTED` for the next inspector. Cases may be listed and narrowed by `animal_lot_id`, by `territory_id`, by `claimant_owner_id` or by `status`.

The statutory hold exists so an owner can come forward. A `LivestockOwner` files an ownership claim while the hold is still running — the logical clock must be strictly before `statutory_release_on`, and a claim filed at or after that instant is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_WINDOW_CLOSED`. Filing moves the case `IMPOUNDED → CLAIM_FILED` and records the claimant's `X-User-ID` as `claimant_owner_id`. A `BoardAdmin` then rules on it with a `decision` of `APPROVED` or `DENIED`. Approval releases the animals: the case moves `CLAIM_FILED → RELEASED_TO_CLAIMANT`, the district's `impound_current_head` falls by the case's `head_count` with `intake_status` recomputed, and the impounded lot is rehabilitated — its `lot_status` becomes `VERIFIED`, its `match_status` moves `UNMATCHED → MATCHED`, and its `disposition` becomes `CLEARED`. Denial sends the case back: it moves `CLAIM_FILED → IMPOUNDED`, `claim_denied_count` rises by one, and `claimant_owner_id` is cleared to null so that the same owner or a different one may file again while the window remains open.

Once the hold has run, the board sells. Scheduling the auction is available from `IMPOUNDED` and from `CLAIM_FILED` alike, and requires that the logical clock be at or after `statutory_release_on`; scheduling earlier is refused with `ERR_HOLD_PERIOD_NOT_ELAPSED`. A pending claim does not stay the statutory auction — the case moves to `AUCTION_SCHEDULED` and carries `claimant_owner_id` forward untouched, so a claimant who was never ruled on still stands to be paid. Scheduling records the auctioneer's `estimated_gross_cents` and writes a provisional distribution. Recording the hammer price moves the case `AUCTION_SCHEDULED → SOLD`, records `gross_proceeds_cents`, stamps `sale_at` from the logical clock, and rewrites the distribution from actuals.

The distribution itself is a waterfall of three buckets, and the order of evaluation is fixed because the handling fee's cap depends on what the care bucket leaves behind. **First**, the care cost: the board charges 275 cents per head per day of care, so `care_cost_cents` is 275 multiplied by the case's `head_count` multiplied by the number of care days the variant supplies. **Second**, the net available amount is the gross amount less that care cost. **Third**, an uncapped handling fee is computed as fifteen percent of the gross amount, rounded half up to whole cents. **Fourth**, a cap on that fee is computed as twenty percent of the net available amount — treating a negative net as zero — likewise rounded half up to whole cents. **Fifth**, `handling_fee_cents` is zero whenever the net available amount is zero or less, and is otherwise the smaller of the uncapped fee and the cap. **Sixth**, `claimant_residual_cents` is the net available amount less the handling fee, floored at zero so it is never negative. A seventh step applies only when a sold case terminates with nobody to pay, and is described below. After every write the conserved-quantity invariant holds: the gross amount equals the care cost plus the handling fee plus whichever of the claimant residual or the board forfeiture is in play.

The two variants of that waterfall differ only in what they feed it. The **PROVISIONAL** variant runs when the auction is scheduled: the gross amount is the case's `estimated_gross_cents`, and the care-day count is the statutory floor of 20 days regardless of where the logical clock actually stands. It sets `distribution_variant` to `PROVISIONAL`. The **FINAL** variant runs when the sale is recorded: the gross amount is the case's `gross_proceeds_cents`, and the care-day count is the number of calendar dates from the date of `impounded_at` through the date of `sale_at` inclusive — that is, the difference between the two calendar dates plus one, counting both the intake day and the sale day. It sets `distribution_variant` to `FINAL` and overwrites every bucket the provisional pass wrote. A case sold on the twenty-fifth day thus carries a materially larger care bucket than the provisional figures suggested, and the claimant residual shrinks accordingly.

Closing out a sold case is a `FeeAdministrator`'s work and takes one of two exits depending on whether anybody claimed the animals. When `claimant_owner_id` is set, the case moves `SOLD → PROCEEDS_DISBURSED`, `board_forfeiture_cents` is set to 0, and the claimant is paid the residual already computed. When `claimant_owner_id` is null — nobody ever claimed, or the only claim was denied — the case moves `SOLD → FORFEITED_TO_BOARD`, `claimant_residual_cents` is set to 0, and `board_forfeiture_cents` takes the gross proceeds less the care cost less the handling fee. Both exits vacate the facility identically: the district's `impound_current_head` falls by the case's `head_count`, its `intake_status` is recomputed, and the impounded lot moves to `lot_status` `VERIFIED` with `disposition` `CLEARED`. Attempting any of these estray transitions from a state that does not declare it — disbursing a case that was never sold, ruling on a claim that was never filed — is refused with `ERR_ESTRAY_INVALID_STATE`.

### 3.9. The Fee Dimension: Assessment, Delinquency, Remittance and Standing

The fee dimension of an inspection order begins at `UNASSESSED` and leaves that state exactly once, at the moment the inspector completes the field work. In that same request the order's `fee_status` advances `UNASSESSED → ASSESSED`, `assessed_fee_cents` is stamped with the computed fee, `fee_due_on` is set to the logical clock advanced by 30 days, and the owner's `projected_arrears_cents` is recomputed.

The fee is built from marginal per-head bands that are consumed cumulatively across the order's lots rather than restarting at each lot. The board charges 110 cents for each of the first 50 head presented under the order, 85 cents for each head from the fifty-first through the two-hundredth, and 60 cents for every head beyond the two-hundredth. Only lots whose `lot_status` is `PRESENTED`, `VERIFIED` or `HELD` are priced; a lot that was voided is excluded entirely, contributing neither head nor money. Qualifying lots are priced in `created_at` ascending order, and a running head count carries forward from one lot to the next, so a second lot of 20 head on an order that already presented 40 is priced at 110 cents for its first ten head and 85 cents for its remaining ten.

Each lot's own base charge is then loaded with an administrative surcharge of 12.5 percent of that base, and the point at which the board rounds is part of the rule rather than an accident of implementation: the surcharge is rounded half up to whole cents **per lot**, and only the already-rounded per-lot amounts are summed. Summing every lot's base first and rounding the surcharge once at the end produces a different answer by whole cents whenever a lot's base lands on a half cent, and that is not the answer the board wants. The order's subtotal is the sum, over qualifying lots, of each lot's base charge plus its own rounded surcharge.

Two flat additions complete the assessment. The district's `travel_surcharge_cents` is always added, which is why an order that completes with no qualifying lots at all still carries a fee: its subtotal is zero but the travel surcharge stands. And an after-hours premium of 4,500 cents is added when the order's `scheduled_start_at` falls outside normal board hours in UTC — that is, when its UTC hour is strictly before 7 or is 18 or later. An order starting at 06:59 UTC and one starting at 18:00 UTC both attract the premium; one starting at 07:00 UTC does not. `assessed_fee_cents` is the subtotal plus the travel surcharge plus the after-hours premium where it applies.

Time then does its work through the board's delinquency sweep, which a `FeeAdministrator` runs after moving the logical clock. The sweep advances the board's epoch in three ordered stages. It first moves every order whose `fee_status` is `ASSESSED` and whose `fee_due_on` has been reached — meaning `fee_due_on` is at or before the logical clock — to `DELINQUENT`, processing them by `fee_due_on` ascending and then `created_at` ascending. It then works the register as described in §3.3, moving expired records to `GRACE` and long-expired ones to `LAPSED` and rejecting their pending renewals. It finally recomputes `posted_arrears_cents`, `projected_arrears_cents`, `standing` and `suspended_since` for every owner the sweep touched, processing owners by `created_at` ascending. The sweep reports what it did: `delinquent_order_count`, `graced_registration_count`, `lapsed_registration_count`, `suspended_owner_count`, and the `swept_at` instant it ran at.

An owner's arrears are kept in two flavours, and the difference between them is what an owner's statement exists to show. `posted_arrears_cents` is the summed `assessed_fee_cents` of that owner's orders whose `fee_status` is exactly `DELINQUENT` — money that has already passed its due date under a sweep. `projected_arrears_cents` is the summed `assessed_fee_cents` of that owner's orders whose `fee_status` is `ASSESSED` or `DELINQUENT` — everything outstanding, whether or not it has come due. A fee assessed this morning contributes its full amount to the projected figure and nothing at all to the posted one, so for an owner with a single freshly assessed order the two figures differ by exactly that order's `assessed_fee_cents`. Both aggregates count the owner's orders irrespective of scheduling dimension; a cancelled order can never carry a non-zero `assessed_fee_cents`, because the fee dimension only leaves `UNASSESSED` on completion.

Only the posted figure gates work. An owner's `standing` reads `SUSPENDED` whenever `posted_arrears_cents` is at or above 25,000 cents — two hundred and fifty dollars — and reads `GOOD` at any lower amount; the breakpoint is inclusive, so an owner sitting at exactly 25,000 cents of posted arrears is suspended. On the recompute that flips an owner from `GOOD` to `SUSPENDED`, `suspended_since` is stamped with the logical clock; on the recompute that flips them back to `GOOD`, `suspended_since` returns to null. Both aggregates and the standing gate are recomputed on every fee-dimension transition — assessment at completion, the slide into delinquency, settlement by remittance, waiver, and the unwinding of an assessment — as well as during the sweep. A suspended owner is refused new scheduling (§3.5) and recovers automatically the moment their posted arrears fall back below the threshold.

A `FeeAdministrator` has two ways to take a fee off the books without money changing hands, and both are barred while the order is under a theft hold. Waiving moves the fee dimension from `ASSESSED` or `DELINQUENT` to `WAIVED`. Unwinding an assessment is the compensating action for the assessment stamp itself: it moves the fee dimension from `ASSESSED` or `DELINQUENT` back to `UNASSESSED`, sets `assessed_fee_cents` to 0 and `fee_due_on` to null, and — because the owner aggregates are recomputed from their source rows rather than nudged (§2.9) — returns the owner's arrears and `standing` to exactly the values they held before the assessment. Either operation attempted against an order whose `hold_flag` is true is refused with `ERR_ORDER_HOLD_ACTIVE`; either attempted against a fee dimension that is `UNASSESSED`, `REMITTED` or `WAIVED` is refused with `ERR_FEE_INVALID_STATE`.

Money arrives as a remittance. A `LivestockOwner` tenders one by naming themselves as `owner_id`, the tendered `amount_cents` (which must be greater than zero), and `covered_order_ids`, the list of their inspection orders the payment is offered against. A remittance may not name an order under a theft hold; tendering against one is refused with `ERR_ORDER_HOLD_ACTIVE`. The gate sits at tender time because the fee dimension only ever reaches `REMITTED` for an order whose `hold_flag` is false. A new remittance enters at status `SUBMITTED` with `applied_cents` and `shortfall_cents` both 0, and remittances may be listed and narrowed by `owner_id` or by `status`.

Reconciliation is the `FeeAdministrator`'s pass over that tender, and it is a strict waterfall with no skip-ahead. The board first selects, from `covered_order_ids`, only those orders whose `fee_status` is still `ASSESSED` or `DELINQUENT` — orders already settled or waived by an earlier pass are skipped entirely and contribute nothing — and re-sorts that selection by `fee_due_on` ascending and then `created_at` ascending, ignoring the order in which the client listed them. The covered total is the summed `assessed_fee_cents` of that selection. Before applying anything, the board tests for overpayment: if the remittance's `amount_cents` exceeds its `applied_cents` plus that covered total, the pass is refused with `ERR_REMITTANCE_AMOUNT_INVALID` (HTTP 422); equality is accepted, and only a strictly greater tender is an overpayment.

The board then walks the sorted selection with the funds not yet consumed, which is `amount_cents` less `applied_cents`. For each order in turn, if the available funds are greater than or equal to that order's `assessed_fee_cents`, the order's `fee_status` moves to `REMITTED`, its `remittance_id` is stamped with the settling remittance, the available funds fall by that amount and the remittance's `applied_cents` rises by the same amount. The first order that cannot be fully covered stops the walk outright — the board never settles a cheaper order sitting behind an expensive one out of turn, and it never writes a partial settlement of a single order. After the walk, the still-open amount is the summed `assessed_fee_cents` of the selected orders that remain at `ASSESSED` or `DELINQUENT`, and `shortfall_cents` is that still-open amount less whatever funds remain available, floored at zero. A remittance whose `shortfall_cents` lands at zero moves `SUBMITTED → RECONCILED`; one with any shortfall moves `SUBMITTED → SHORT_PAID`. Either way the affected owner's arrears aggregates and `standing` are recomputed.

A short-paid remittance is not dead. Its owner may top it up by supplying `additional_cents`, which must be greater than zero and is added to the remittance's `amount_cents`, returning it `SHORT_PAID → SUBMITTED` for another reconciliation pass — the cycle `SUBMITTED → SHORT_PAID → SUBMITTED → RECONCILED` is the normal path for an owner who underestimated their bill. Because `applied_cents` accumulates across passes, the second pass picks up exactly where the first one stopped. Reconciling a remittance that is not `SUBMITTED`, or topping up one that is not `SHORT_PAID`, is refused with `ERR_REMITTANCE_INVALID_STATE`.
