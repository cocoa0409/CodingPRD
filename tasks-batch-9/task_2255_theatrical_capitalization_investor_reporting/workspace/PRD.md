# Theatrical Capitalization & Investor Reporting API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A Broadway general management office lives at the intersection of two very different disciplines. On one side sits securities practice: a show is capitalized by syndicating a limited partnership or a limited liability company, which means offering documents, accreditation determinations, subscription agreements, escrow, and a capital account for every investor who signs. On the other side sits weekly show accounting: eight performances, a box-office gross, a variable-cost deduction for credit-card fees and group commissions, running expenses, a royalty burden owed to authors and directors and stars, and — if the week is good — a contribution toward paying the investors back. The two disciplines meet in a single number, recoupment, and a general manager who cannot state that number accurately on a Monday morning has no business asking investors for anything.

This system replaces the spreadsheet-and-email workflow that most general management offices still run on. It holds the capitalization budget (production, rehearsal, advertising, reserve, and bonds-and-deposits components), the syndication file (offering instruments, investors, subscriptions, capital accounts), the weekly operating budget with its running costs and break-even arithmetic, the royalty structure in both of its contractual shapes, the weekly wrap with grosses and attendance and capacity, the recoupment ledger, subsidiary-rights licensing income, investor distributions, and the closing decision with its notice period and reserve release. Every one of those artifacts is a first-class resource, and every mutation to any of them re-derives the production's rolled-up ledger immediately — there is no overnight batch, and no report is ever assembled from figures that have gone stale.

Two design commitments drive most of the rules that follow. First, **reversibility**: almost every money-moving action in a theatrical office has a compensating action — a subscription can be rescinded, a certified wrap can be voided, a closing notice can be withdrawn — and each compensating action must unwind the ledger exactly as far as the original action moved it, no further. Second, **separation of the person who reports from the person who certifies**: a general manager submits the weekly wrap and a comptroller certifies it; a lead producer posts the closing notice and a comptroller settles it. These are not conveniences. They are the controls that make the resulting investor statements defensible.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to this API must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting user, and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity in which they are acting. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` does not spell one of the five declared roles exactly, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If both headers are well formed but the declared role is not authorized for the endpoint being called, the request is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403). This pair of checks runs before anything else: they are the first and second validations applied to every request, ahead of resource lookup, body validation, and all business rules.

The five roles are `GeneralManager`, `Comptroller`, `SyndicationCounsel`, `LeadProducer`, and `Investor`.

A `GeneralManager` is general management office staff. They register houses and open production files, build the capitalization budget, draft the weekly operating budget, register royalty participants, open and submit and amend the weekly wraps, and record subsidiary-rights licensing income. A `Comptroller` is the production comptroller: they release escrowed capital, activate operating budgets, certify and dispute and void wraps, allocate licensing income to the ledger, declare and pay investor distributions, and settle the closing notice. `SyndicationCounsel` runs the offering — opening the offering axis, drafting and making effective and withdrawing the offering instrument, registering investors and recording their accreditation, and processing subscriptions through return-for-cure, acceptance and rescission. A `LeadProducer` approves the capitalization budget, advances the production's operating calendar, converts the royalty structure to a pool, terminates and reinstates participations, and makes the closing decision. An `Investor` is a limited partner or LLC member who subscribes for units and reads only their own syndication records.

Four of the five roles are operator roles whose identity is opaque. When `X-User-Role` is `GeneralManager`, `Comptroller`, `SyndicationCounsel`, or `LeadProducer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a workflow demands an audit trail — as `submitted_by_user_id` and `certified_by_user_id` on a weekly wrap, and as `issued_by_user_id` and `settled_by_user_id` on a closing notice — and it compares two such recorded identifiers against each other to enforce separation of duties, but it never compares an operator's `X-User-ID` to any row's `id`.

The `Investor` role is different: it is entity-backed. When `X-User-Role` is `Investor`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Investor` row, and ownership checks compare the resource's investor reference to `X-User-ID`. An `Investor` may create a subscription only for themselves (the `investor_id` in the request body must equal `X-User-ID`), may resubmit only a subscription whose `investor_id` equals `X-User-ID`, may read only a subscription whose `investor_id` equals `X-User-ID`, may read only a capital account whose `investor_id` equals `X-User-ID`, and may read only the investor row whose `id` equals `X-User-ID`. Any of those five operations attempted against another party's record is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). This ownership check is the sixth validation in the global order — it runs after path and body validation and after referential resolution of body foreign keys, but before every state and business rule.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints accept two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of rows returned; it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1, and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` is the number of rows to skip and defaults to 0; its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` outside the range of 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, every list endpoint returns rows sorted by `created_at` in ascending order.

List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope. `total_count` reflects the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied, and `has_more` states whether any matching row remains beyond the returned page.

### 2.4. The Logical Production Calendar

Theatrical deadlines are calendar deadlines, so the system does not read the host machine's wall clock for any date comparison. Instead it keeps a **logical UTC clock**. On startup that clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it continues to read that instant — never falling back to real wall-clock time — until an operator sets it. Any of the four operator roles may set the clock by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant; the response reports the resulting `current_utc`. Setting the clock mutates no entity.

Every date comparison in the system reads the logical clock's UTC calendar date: the offering document's effective window, the weekly wrap's performance week, and the closing notice's notice period. All date-valued fields — `start_date`, `end_date`, `effective_date`, `expiration_date`, `week_ending_date`, `received_date`, `notice_date`, `final_performance_date` — are ISO 8601 calendar dates, and a malformed date is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). Where two dates form a range, the later bound may equal the earlier bound but may not precede it: `end_date` must be on or after `start_date`, and `expiration_date` must be on or after `effective_date`. Both of those ordering violations are also `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

### 2.5. Money, Points and Percentage Precision

Every monetary amount in this system is an integer number of cents, and every field name that ends in `_cents` carries such an integer. There are no fractional cents anywhere — not on a subscription, not on a royalty allocation, not on a distribution breakdown entry. Wherever a monetary amount is derived by multiplying by a percentage or dividing by a count, the result is rounded to a whole number of cents using **round half up**: a value exactly halfway between two whole cents rounds away from zero to the larger magnitude.

Percentage fields carry a fixed number of decimals. Contractual and configuration percentages — `pre_recoupment_pool_pct`, `post_recoupment_pool_pct`, `subsidiary_rights_investor_share_pct`, `variable_cost_pct`, `fixed_gross_pct` — carry at most four decimals. Derived percentages follow the precision of the calculation that produces them: `pool_share_pct`, `paid_capacity_pct`, `recoupment_pct`, and `break_even_paid_capacity_pct` are all rounded half up to four decimals, while an investor's `ownership_pct` is rounded half up to six decimals. Supplying a percentage with more decimals than its field allows, or outside its declared range, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

The ranges themselves matter. A royalty-pool percentage on either side of recoupment must be at least 0.0000 and strictly less than 1.0000 — the pool may not be defined as the whole of operating profit. The same strictly-below-one bound applies to the operating budget's `variable_cost_pct` and to a participant's `fixed_gross_pct`. The subsidiary-rights investor share is the one percentage that may reach 1.0000: it must be at least 0.0000 and at most 1.0000, because it is legitimate for a deal to assign licensing income entirely to the investors. Royalty pool points and `total_pool_points` are non-negative integers, not percentages; `pool_points` may be zero.

Where a calculation distributes a whole amount across several rows — the royalty pool across participants, ownership across capital accounts, a distribution across capital accounts — the rounding order is pinned and is part of the contract: each row's own share is rounded first, and only then is the signed difference between the whole amount and the sum of the rounded shares pushed onto a single designated row. Summing exact fractions and rounding once at the end is not the specified order and will produce different per-row figures.

### 2.6. Records, Revisions and the Version Counter

Every resource in the system is addressed by an `id`, which is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the row was first written.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at` — an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp, in the same format, refreshed on every mutation — and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increments on every mutation. Houses, production files, budget lines, operating budgets, offering documents, investors, subscriptions, capital accounts, royalty participants, weekly wraps, royalty allocations, licensing-income rows, distributions, and closing notices all carry the full set: `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at`, and `version`.

Theatre engagements are the single exception. A `TheatreEngagement` is a booking record that is written once and never modified, so it carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has no `updated_at` field and no `version` field, and neither field appears in its response payload.

The version counter obeys one rule that applies to every entity carrying it, and getting this rule wrong is the most common way to break the concurrency contract: **a successful request increments the `version` of each row it writes by exactly 1, no matter how many cascade hops of that request touch the row.** Accepting a subscription both re-rolls the production's money fields and may advance the production's offering axis, yet the production's `version` moves by 1 for that request, not by 2. A rejected request increments nothing and refreshes no `updated_at`. This holds for derived-field refreshes as well: when a cascade recomputes a production's rolled-up ledger fields, that is a mutation of the production row, so its `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` moves by 1 for the request as a whole.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on the Production File

Two write operations draw on a rolled-up figure of the production file that any number of other operations can legitimately move, and both are therefore guarded by optimistic concurrency control on the production.

Creating a budget line requires `expected_production_version` in the request body. This is not an optional field: it must be supplied, and it must carry the `version` of the production as the client last observed it. A budget line draws on the same capitalization headroom as every other line, so a line created after the client's read legitimately shrinks the headroom the client believed it had. If `expected_production_version` does not equal the production's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PRODUCTION_VERSION` (HTTP 412), forcing the client to re-read the rolled-up `budget_total_cents` before allocating again.

Declaring an investor distribution likewise requires `expected_production_version` carrying the client's last observed production `version`. Distributable cash is a rolled-up field that a wrap certification, a subsidiary-rights allocation, a reserve release, or a prior declaration all move; a declaration written against a stale view of `distributable_cash_cents` is refused with `ERR_STALE_PRODUCTION_VERSION` (HTTP 412) rather than silently over-distributing.

No other endpoint in this API takes a version field. Committing a budget line, accepting a subscription, certifying a wrap, allocating licensing income, paying a distribution, settling a closing notice — none of these carry `expected_production_version` or any other expected-version input, and supplying one has no effect. The stale-version check itself is the tenth validation in the global order: it runs after role, path, body, referential, ownership, duty-segregation and state checks, and before the securities, temporal, occupancy and fiscal rules.

### 2.8. Cascade Discipline: One Write, Many Re-derived Fields

This is a ledger system, and the defining property of its writes is that they cascade. No client ever tells the system what a production's `recouped_amount_cents` is; the client certifies a wrap, and the system re-derives the ledger. Each cascade is applied within the same request, immediately, before the response is built — there is no eventual consistency and no deferred reconciliation.

The cascades run in a fixed order, and the order matters because later hops read what earlier hops wrote. Accepting a subscription first flips the subscription, then creates or updates the investor's capital account, then re-derives ownership for **every** capital account of the production, then re-rolls the production's capital and escrow and investor-count fields and may advance the offering axis, and finally re-rolls the offering document's accepted totals. Certifying a wrap first settles the wrap's own money fields, then creates the royalty allocations, then re-rolls each participant's cumulative royalty, then re-derives the recoupment ledger, and finally — if the ledger crossed the capitalization target — flips the production to `RECOUPED` and re-bases every capital account of the production. Posting a closing notice first writes the notice, then commits the reserve budget lines, then re-rolls the production's committed budget total.

Every compensating action reverses its cascade to exactly the same depth. Rescinding an accepted subscription unwinds the capital account, re-derives ownership for every account, re-rolls the production, and may revert the offering axis. Voiding a certified wrap reverses the royalty allocations, re-rolls the participants downward, re-derives the recoupment ledger, and may revert the production to `UNRECOUPED` and re-base every capital account back. Withdrawing a closing notice reverts the reserve budget lines and re-rolls the committed total. A compensating action never leaves a residue: the pair of actions conserves the figure the first action moved.

### 2.9. Twin State Axes on a Production File

A production file is unusual in carrying two independent state fields that evolve on the same row and never merge. `offering_status` is the syndication axis and runs `DRAFT`, `OFFERING`, `FULLY_SUBSCRIBED`, `CAPITALIZED`, beginning at `DRAFT` and ending at `CAPITALIZED`. `operating_status` is the production-calendar axis and runs `PRE_PRODUCTION`, `REHEARSAL`, `PREVIEWS`, `RUNNING`, `CLOSING_NOTICE`, `CLOSED`, beginning at `PRE_PRODUCTION` and ending at `CLOSED`. A transition on one axis is never a transition on the other, and each axis reports its own rejection code: an inadmissible move on the syndication axis is `ERR_INVALID_OFFERING_STATE` (HTTP 409), and an inadmissible move on the calendar axis is `ERR_INVALID_OPERATING_STATE` (HTTP 409).

The two axes are independent but not unrelated: exactly one cross-axis guard couples them, and it runs in one direction only. The calendar axis cannot leave `PRE_PRODUCTION` until the syndication axis has reached `CAPITALIZED` — you do not start paying a company out of money you have not released from escrow. Nothing on the syndication axis is gated on the calendar axis.

Two of the axes' paths are cycles rather than one-way ladders. On the syndication axis, `OFFERING → FULLY_SUBSCRIBED → OFFERING` is a legitimate round trip driven entirely by subscription arithmetic. On the calendar axis, `RUNNING → CLOSING_NOTICE → RUNNING` is a legitimate round trip driven by posting and then withdrawing a closing notice.

Beyond the production file, the system carries a state field on the offering document (`DRAFT`, `EFFECTIVE`, `EXPIRED`, `WITHDRAWN`, with `EXPIRED` and `WITHDRAWN` terminal), the investor's accreditation determination (`UNVERIFIED`, `ACCREDITED`, `NON_ACCREDITED`, freely re-determinable in either direction), the subscription (`SUBMITTED`, `RETURNED_FOR_CURE`, `ACCEPTED`, `REJECTED`, `RESCINDED`, with `REJECTED` and `RESCINDED` terminal), the operating budget (`DRAFT`, `ACTIVE`, `SUPERSEDED`, with `SUPERSEDED` terminal), the budget line's commitment (`PLANNED`, `COMMITTED`, `SPENT`, with `SPENT` terminal), the royalty participation (`ACTIVE`, `TERMINATED`, cyclable in both directions), the weekly wrap (`DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `CERTIFIED`, `DISPUTED`, `AMENDED`, `VOID`, with `VOID` terminal), the royalty allocation (`ALLOCATED`, `REVERSED`, with `REVERSED` terminal), licensing income (`RECORDED`, `ALLOCATED`, with `ALLOCATED` terminal), the distribution (`DECLARED`, `PAID`, with `PAID` terminal), and the closing notice (`ISSUED`, `WITHDRAWN`, `SETTLED`, with both `WITHDRAWN` and `SETTLED` terminal). Each machine reports its own state-rejection code, catalogued in §5.

### 2.10. Flat Addressing and the Order of Validation

Every entity in this system is a peer collection addressed directly at the URL root. There are no nested resource paths: a budget line is not addressed underneath its production, and a subscription is not addressed underneath its offering document. Parentage is expressed exclusively through foreign-key fields in request bodies and through list filters — `production_id`, `theatre_id`, `offering_document_id`, `investor_id`, `operating_budget_id`, `wrap_id`, `royalty_participant_id`.

Because paths are flat, path validation reduces to a single identifier per request, but the ordering discipline still holds and generalizes to the body. Path identifiers are resolved left to right, and the first identifier that does not resolve determines the rejection: `ERR_THEATRE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRODUCTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_THEATRE_ENGAGEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BUDGET_LINE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OPERATING_BUDGET_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OFFERING_DOCUMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INVESTOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SUBSCRIPTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INVESTOR_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ROYALTY_PARTICIPANT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WEEKLY_WRAP_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ROYALTY_ALLOCATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SUBSIDIARY_RIGHTS_INCOME_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INVESTOR_DISTRIBUTION_NOT_FOUND`, or `ERR_CLOSING_NOTICE_NOT_FOUND` — each HTTP 404.

The global validation order is fixed, and it is fixed precisely so that a request failing several rules at once reports a predictable one. In order:

1. **Authentication.** Both headers present and `X-User-Role` one of the five declared roles, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. **Authorization.** The declared role appears in the called endpoint's authorized list, else `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403).
3. **Path existence.** Every path identifier resolves, left to right, else the matching 404 above.
4. **Request shape.** Required body fields present; enum fields hold a declared member; integer fields are integers inside their declared constraints; percentage fields carry no more than their declared decimals; date fields are well-formed ISO 8601 calendar dates; `end_date` is on or after `start_date` and `expiration_date` is on or after `effective_date`; `limit` is between 1 and 100 inclusive and `offset` is at least 0. Any violation is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400).
5. **Body referential integrity.** Every foreign key supplied in the body — `production_id`, `theatre_id`, `offering_document_id`, `investor_id` — resolves to an existing row, else the matching 404 (`ERR_PRODUCTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_THEATRE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OFFERING_DOCUMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INVESTOR_NOT_FOUND`).
6. **Resource ownership.** The `Investor` role owns the record it is acting on, else `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403).
7. **Segregation of duties.** The certifying operator differs from the wrap's submitter and the settling operator differs from the notice's issuer, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
8. **Own-state admissibility.** The target row is in a state from which the requested transition exists, else that machine's state error (409).
9. **Cross-entity state gates.** The related rows are in the states the operation requires, else one of `ERR_OFFERING_NOT_EFFECTIVE`, `ERR_PRODUCTION_NOT_PERFORMING`, `ERR_PRODUCTION_NOT_OFFERING`, `ERR_PRODUCTION_NOT_CAPITALIZED`, `ERR_UNCERTIFIED_WRAPS_EXIST`, `ERR_OPERATING_BUDGET_CHANGED`, or `ERR_ACTIVE_OPERATING_BUDGET_MISSING` (all 409).
10. **Optimistic concurrency.** `expected_production_version` equals the production's current `version`, else `ERR_STALE_PRODUCTION_VERSION` (412).
11. **Securities compliance.** Accreditation, offering-instrument agreement, and the investor headcount ceiling, else `ERR_INVESTOR_NOT_ACCREDITED`, `ERR_OFFERING_TYPE_MISMATCH`, or `ERR_MAX_INVESTORS_EXCEEDED` (all 422).
12. **Temporal rules.** Offering window, wrap week, and notice period, else `ERR_OFFERING_EXPIRED`, `ERR_WRAP_WEEK_IN_FUTURE`, `ERR_NOTICE_DATE_IN_PAST`, or `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_CLOSING_NOTICE` (all 422).
13. **Occupancy and uniqueness.** House and performance-week collisions, single-notice and single-conversion constraints, else `ERR_THEATRE_ENGAGEMENT_OVERLAP`, `ERR_WRAP_WEEK_OVERLAP`, `ERR_CLOSING_NOTICE_ALREADY_ISSUED`, or `ERR_ROYALTY_BASIS_ALREADY_POOL` (all 409).
14. **Fiscal ceilings.** The money limits, evaluated last and among themselves in the fixed order set out in §2.11.

Two orderings inside this sequence are deliberate and worth calling out, because a naive implementation gets them backwards. First, the cross-entity gate at step 9 treats a `DRAFT` or `WITHDRAWN` offering document as `ERR_OFFERING_NOT_EFFECTIVE` (409), but it deliberately does **not** catch an `EXPIRED` document: the lapsed-window case is deferred to the temporal rules at step 12 so that it reports the more specific `ERR_OFFERING_EXPIRED` (422). Second, the investor-headcount ceiling is a securities rule at step 11 and therefore fires *before* the oversubscription amount ceiling at step 14 — a subscription that would both add a disallowed investor and breach the offering amount reports `ERR_MAX_INVESTORS_EXCEEDED`, never `ERR_OVERSUBSCRIPTION`.

### 2.11. Fiscal Ceilings and Their Evaluation Order

The fiscal rules are the last gate a write passes, and they are evaluated among themselves in exactly this order:

1. Accepting a subscription may not push the offering document's `accepted_amount_cents` above its `offering_amount_cents`; a breach is `ERR_OVERSUBSCRIPTION` (HTTP 422).
2. A new budget line may not push the production's `budget_total_cents` above its `capitalization_target_cents`; a breach is `ERR_BUDGET_EXCEEDS_CAPITALIZATION` (HTTP 422).
3. A distribution requires that the production have at least one capital account whose `units_total` is greater than zero. This is a structural check on the existence of a payee set, and it is evaluated **before** any cash ceiling: a production with no funded capital accounts reports `ERR_NO_INVESTOR_ACCOUNTS` (HTTP 422) and never the amount error.
4. A distribution whose `distribution_type` is `NET_PROFIT` requires the production's `recoupment_status` to be `RECOUPED`; otherwise the declaration is rejected with `ERR_NOT_YET_RECOUPED` (HTTP 422).
5. A declared distribution's `total_amount_cents` may not exceed the production's `distributable_cash_cents`; a breach is `ERR_DISTRIBUTION_EXCEEDS_DISTRIBUTABLE` (HTTP 422).

### 2.12. Segregation of Duties and Investor Confidentiality

Two operations in this system may not be performed by the operator who set them up. A weekly wrap must be certified by an operator whose `X-User-ID` differs from the `submitted_by_user_id` recorded when the wrap was submitted. A closing notice must be settled by an operator whose `X-User-ID` differs from the `issued_by_user_id` recorded when the notice was posted. Either violation is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403), and that check runs ahead of every state and business rule so that a self-certification attempt reports the control failure rather than some downstream condition.

Confidentiality runs the other way. Investors see the production-level picture — the production file with its rolled-up ledger, the recoupment report, offering documents, licensing income, distributions and closing notices are all readable by the `Investor` role — but the party-level detail of other investors is not. Investors cannot list subscriptions, cannot list capital accounts, and cannot read another party's subscription, capital account or investor row. Nor can an investor read a house record, a budget line, an operating budget, a royalty participant, a weekly wrap, a royalty allocation, a theatre engagement, or a break-even analysis; those are management artifacts. Attempting an operation for which the role is not authorized at all yields `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`; attempting an operation the role may perform, but on somebody else's record, yields `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Houses, Engagements and Opening a Production File

Before anything can be capitalized there must be a house to play it in. A `GeneralManager` registers a theatre with a `name` of between 1 and 120 characters as it appears on the license agreement, a `seat_count` of at least 1 sellable seat per performance, and a `top_ticket_price_cents` of at least 1 cent. Those last two numbers are the arithmetic backbone of every weekly report the house will ever produce, because capacity gross is computed as though every seat in the house sold at the top scaled price. Two further attributes are optional at registration. `changeover_buffer_days` is the number of dark days the house requires between two engagements; it must be at least 0 and defaults to 3 when omitted. `weekly_fixed_rent_cents` records the house's weekly rent for reference; it must be at least 0, defaults to 0, and — deliberately — feeds no calculation anywhere in this system.

Occupations of a house are booked as engagements. A `GeneralManager` books one by supplying the `production_id` of the show taking the house, the `theatre_id` of the house being occupied, an `engagement_type` of `REHEARSAL`, `TECH`, or `PERFORMANCE_RUN`, and the inclusive first and last occupied calendar days as `start_date` and `end_date`. Bookings are immutable once written: an engagement record has an `id` and a `created_at` and nothing else in the way of revision metadata.

Collisions are checked against the house, not the production, and the check honours the changeover buffer on both sides. Take the existing engagement's inclusive span from its `start_date` to its `end_date` and widen it by the house's `changeover_buffer_days` at each end; if the new engagement's inclusive span touches that widened span at any point, the booking is rejected with `ERR_THEATRE_ENGAGEMENT_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). Equivalently: two engagements of the same house are acceptable only when the count of dark calendar days strictly between the earlier one's last occupied day and the later one's first occupied day is at least the house's `changeover_buffer_days`. With the default buffer of three days, an engagement ending on the 10th and another beginning on the 14th collide — there are only three dark days, the 11th, 12th and 13th, which satisfies the buffer exactly, so that pair is acceptable; a second engagement beginning on the 13th leaves only two dark days and is refused.

Opening the production file is also a `GeneralManager` action. It takes a `title` of 1 to 200 characters, an `offering_entity_type` of `LP` or `LLC` naming the legal form of the producing entity, a `capitalization_target_cents` of at least 1 cent — this figure is the denominator of every recoupment percentage the show will ever report — a `reserve_target_cents` of at least 0 cents naming the intended size of the closing reserve, and the `theatre_id` of the performance house whose seat count and top price will define capacity gross for every weekly wrap. Four deal terms are optional and carry defaults: `pre_recoupment_pool_pct` defaults to 0.3500, `post_recoupment_pool_pct` defaults to 0.4000, `subsidiary_rights_investor_share_pct` defaults to 0.4000, and `closing_notice_min_days` defaults to 14 whole days.

A freshly opened production file starts in a known state on every axis. Its `offering_status` is `DRAFT`, its `operating_status` is `PRE_PRODUCTION`, its `recoupment_status` is `UNRECOUPED`, its `budget_status` is `DRAFT`, and its `royalty_basis` is `FIXED_GROSS`. Every rolled-up money and point field — `budget_total_cents`, `budget_committed_cents`, `capital_raised_cents`, `released_capital_cents`, `escrow_balance_cents`, `recouped_amount_cents`, `deferred_royalty_cents`, `distributed_total_cents`, `distributable_cash_cents`, `total_pool_points`, and `active_investor_count` — initialises to 0, as does `recoupment_pct`, and its `version` begins at 1. Productions can be listed and filtered by `theatre_id`, by either state axis using `offering_status` or `operating_status`, and by `recoupment_status`.

### 3.2. Building and Approving the Capitalization Budget

The capitalization budget is a flat list of lines, each one belonging to a production by its `production_id` and each classified by a `component`. There are five components: `PRODUCTION` for physical production costs, `REHEARSAL`, `ADVERTISING`, `RESERVE` for the closing reserve, and `BONDS_AND_DEPOSITS`. A line also carries a `description` of 1 to 200 characters and an `amount_cents` of at least 1 cent. New lines are created by a `GeneralManager` in `PLANNED` commitment status, and — as set out in §2.7 — the create is guarded by `expected_production_version`, so a client that has not re-read the production since another line landed is turned away with `ERR_STALE_PRODUCTION_VERSION`.

Adding a line re-rolls two figures on the production. `budget_total_cents` becomes the sum of `amount_cents` over every budget line of the production whose `commitment_status` is `PLANNED`, `COMMITTED`, or `SPENT` — that is, over all of them, because lines are never soft-deleted. `budget_committed_cents` becomes the narrower sum of `amount_cents` over the lines whose `commitment_status` is `COMMITTED` or `SPENT`. Both roll-ups are recomputed whenever a line is added, whenever a line's commitment moves, and whenever the budget is approved.

The fiscal gate on the capitalization budget applies at line creation only: after the new line is taken into account, the production's `budget_total_cents` may not exceed its `capitalization_target_cents`. A line that would breach the target is rejected with `ERR_BUDGET_EXCEEDS_CAPITALIZATION` (HTTP 422). Note the asymmetry — committing an existing line moves money between the two roll-ups but adds nothing to the total, so commitment carries no ceiling check of its own.

Approval is the `LeadProducer`'s act. Approving flips `budget_status` from `DRAFT` to `APPROVED` and re-rolls both budget figures. Until approval has happened, a line cannot be committed: a `GeneralManager` may move a line from `PLANNED` to `COMMITTED` only while the production's `budget_status` reads `APPROVED`, and only from `PLANNED` — a line already `COMMITTED` or `SPENT` reports `ERR_INVALID_BUDGET_LINE_STATE` (HTTP 409). A successful commitment re-rolls `budget_committed_cents` and moves both the line's and the production's `version` forward by one. Budget lines can be listed and filtered by `production_id`, by `component`, and by `commitment_status`.

`RESERVE` lines have a second, automatic life that belongs to the closing story and is told in §3.9: they are driven `PLANNED → COMMITTED` when a closing notice is posted, reverted `COMMITTED → PLANNED` if that notice is withdrawn, and driven `COMMITTED → SPENT` when the notice is settled. Those movements are performed by the system rather than requested by an operator, they apply only to lines whose `component` is `RESERVE`, and each of them re-rolls `budget_committed_cents` and advances the affected line's `version`.

### 3.3. Syndicating the Offering: Instruments, Investors and Accreditation

Syndication begins when `SyndicationCounsel` moves the production's syndication axis from `DRAFT` to `OFFERING`. That is the only transition available out of `DRAFT` on that axis, and requesting it from any other offering state reports `ERR_INVALID_OFFERING_STATE`. Until the axis reads `OFFERING`, no offering instrument may be made effective.

The instrument itself is drafted by `SyndicationCounsel` against a `production_id`. Its `doc_type` is either `LP_AGREEMENT` or `LLC_OPERATING_AGREEMENT`, and it must agree with the producing entity's legal form: an `LP` production requires `LP_AGREEMENT` and an `LLC` production requires `LLC_OPERATING_AGREEMENT`. A mismatch in either direction is rejected with `ERR_OFFERING_TYPE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). The instrument declares an `offering_amount_cents` of at least 1 cent as the maximum aggregate subscription amount it may accept, a `unit_price_cents` of at least 1 cent per investment unit, a `max_investors` count of at least 1 for the number of distinct investors it may carry with accepted subscriptions, and the inclusive window `effective_date` through `expiration_date` during which it may be effective. Instruments are drafted in `DRAFT` status.

Making an instrument effective is again `SyndicationCounsel`'s act, and it carries three preconditions. The document must be in `DRAFT` — any other status reports `ERR_INVALID_OFFERING_DOCUMENT_STATE` (HTTP 409). The production's syndication axis must read `OFFERING`, else `ERR_PRODUCTION_NOT_OFFERING` (HTTP 409). And the current logical UTC date must fall inside the inclusive window from `effective_date` through `expiration_date`; a date before the window opens or after it closes yields `ERR_OFFERING_EXPIRED` (HTTP 422). Withdrawal is available from either `DRAFT` or `EFFECTIVE`, takes a `withdrawal_reason` which is recorded on the document, and lands the document in the terminal `WITHDRAWN` state; the field reads null on any document that has not been withdrawn.

Expiry, by contrast, is nobody's act — it happens lazily. Whenever a request reads or writes an offering document, the system first checks whether that document is `EFFECTIVE` and whether the current logical UTC date has passed its `expiration_date`; if so, the document transitions `EFFECTIVE → EXPIRED` and its `version` advances before the request's own validations run. This sweep applies on reading a single document, on listing documents, on creating a subscription, on accepting a subscription, and on resubmitting a subscription. Because the sweep runs first, a subscription attempt against a document whose window lapsed yesterday sees a document already in `EXPIRED` — and, per the ordering note in §2.10, is rejected with `ERR_OFFERING_EXPIRED` (HTTP 422) rather than with the more generic not-effective code. `EXPIRED` is terminal; a lapsed document cannot be revived.

Investors are registered by `SyndicationCounsel` with a `legal_name` of 1 to 200 characters exactly as it appears on the subscription agreement, a two-character `jurisdiction` code naming the US state or ISO country of domicile, and a `contact_email` for investor relations. Every new investor starts `UNVERIFIED`. Counsel then records the securities determination by supplying an `accreditation_status` of `ACCREDITED` or `NON_ACCREDITED`; that determination is freely re-recordable in either direction and from any current value, so `UNVERIFIED → ACCREDITED`, `UNVERIFIED → NON_ACCREDITED`, `ACCREDITED → NON_ACCREDITED`, and `NON_ACCREDITED → ACCREDITED` are all admissible and each advances the investor's `version`. Only an investor reading `ACCREDITED` may subscribe, and only one reading `ACCREDITED` may have a subscription accepted; a subscription created or accepted for anyone else is rejected with `ERR_INVESTOR_NOT_ACCREDITED` (HTTP 422). Offering documents can be listed and filtered by `production_id` and by `status`.

### 3.4. Subscription Processing and the Capital Accounts

Subscribing is the one write an `Investor` performs. The investor supplies the `offering_document_id` of the instrument being subscribed under, their own `investor_id` — which must equal the `X-User-ID` on the request — and a `units` count of at least 1. The system computes `amount_cents` as `units` multiplied by the instrument's `unit_price_cents` and files the subscription as `SUBMITTED` with `cure_round_count` at 0. Creating a subscription re-rolls the instrument's `submitted_units_total`, which is the sum of `units` across that instrument's subscriptions currently in `SUBMITTED`, and advances the instrument's `version`. A subscription may only be created while its instrument reads `EFFECTIVE`: a `DRAFT` or `WITHDRAWN` instrument yields `ERR_OFFERING_NOT_EFFECTIVE` (HTTP 409), while an instrument outside its date window yields `ERR_OFFERING_EXPIRED` (HTTP 422). Subscriptions can be listed by management roles and filtered by `offering_document_id`, `investor_id`, and `status`.

Counsel reviews each submission and has three moves. The first is to send it back to the investor for cure, recording a `defect_note` — the note reads null on any subscription that has never been returned. This move is where the subscription's single branch lives: returning a subscription always increments its `cure_round_count` by one and always records the note, but the state it lands in depends entirely on the resulting count. Two cure rounds are the limit. If the incremented count is at most 2, the subscription becomes `RETURNED_FOR_CURE` and the investor gets another chance; if the increment would carry the count past 2 — that is, on the third return — the subscription is instead driven straight to the terminal `REJECTED` state. Either outcome re-rolls the instrument's `submitted_units_total` and advances the instrument's `version`. Returning a subscription that is not in `SUBMITTED` reports `ERR_INVALID_SUBSCRIPTION_STATE` (HTTP 409).

The investor closes the cure loop by resubmitting, which returns the subscription from `RETURNED_FOR_CURE` to `SUBMITTED`. Resubmission accepts a `units` count, which may differ from the original, and `amount_cents` is recomputed as the new `units` multiplied by the instrument's `unit_price_cents`; the instrument's `submitted_units_total` is re-rolled and its `version` advances. Resubmission requires that the acting `X-User-ID` equal the subscription's `investor_id` and that the instrument still read `EFFECTIVE`. Because a return can happen at most twice before the subscription is rejected, the `SUBMITTED → RETURNED_FOR_CURE → SUBMITTED` cycle can turn at most twice.

Acceptance is the heaviest write in the syndication half of the system. Counsel may accept only a subscription in `SUBMITTED`, only while its instrument reads `EFFECTIVE`, only while the subscribing investor reads `ACCREDITED`, and only within the instrument's date window. Two ceilings then apply, in this order. First the headcount: counting this investor as new if and only if they hold no other accepted subscription under the same instrument, the instrument's distinct accepted-investor count after the acceptance may not exceed its `max_investors`, else `ERR_MAX_INVESTORS_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). Then the amount: the instrument's `accepted_amount_cents` plus this subscription's `amount_cents` may not exceed its `offering_amount_cents`, else `ERR_OVERSUBSCRIPTION` (HTTP 422).

An accepted subscription starts a four-hop cascade. The subscription becomes `ACCEPTED`. A capital account for the pair of `production_id` and `investor_id` is created if none exists, and that account's `units_total` — the sum of `units` across the investor's accepted subscriptions under any instrument of the production — and `contributed_cents` — the matching sum of `amount_cents` — are recomputed. Because that account's units changed, `ownership_pct` is then recomputed for **every** capital account of the production, not merely the one that moved. And because ownership and contributions changed, the production's `capital_raised_cents` (the sum of `amount_cents` over accepted subscriptions across all of the production's instruments), `escrow_balance_cents`, and `active_investor_count` (the number of the production's capital accounts whose `units_total` is greater than zero) are re-rolled; if `capital_raised_cents` has reached or passed `capitalization_target_cents`, the syndication axis advances `OFFERING → FULLY_SUBSCRIBED`. The instrument's `accepted_units_total`, `accepted_amount_cents`, `accepted_investor_count` and `submitted_units_total` are all re-rolled and its `version` advances, as does the production's.

Ownership is allocated by a pinned procedure. Order the production's capital accounts by `units_total` descending, breaking ties by `created_at` ascending. Sum `units_total` across all of them; if that total is zero, every account's `ownership_pct` is 0.000000 and the procedure stops. Otherwise each account's `ownership_pct` is its own `units_total` divided by the production-wide total, rounded half up to six decimals. The rounded shares are then reconciled: subtract their sum from 1.000000 and add the signed difference — which may be negative — to the first account in the order, that is, to the account with the largest `units_total`, or the earliest-created among those tied for largest. The result is that the accounts' shares sum to exactly 1.000000 whenever any units exist at all. Finally, each account's `recoupment_credited_cents` is refreshed as the production's `recouped_amount_cents` multiplied by that account's `ownership_pct`, rounded half up to whole cents — a snapshot taken at the moment of the most recent recoupment recompute.

Rescission is acceptance's compensating action. Counsel may rescind a subscription that is `ACCEPTED`, and only while the production's syndication axis reads `OFFERING` or `FULLY_SUBSCRIBED` — once capital has been released, rescission is closed off with `ERR_INVALID_OFFERING_STATE`. Rescission records a `rescission_reason`, which reads null on any subscription that has not been rescinded, and lands the subscription in the terminal `RESCINDED` state. It then unwinds the cascade to exactly the same depth: the investor's capital account is recomputed downward, `ownership_pct` is recomputed for every capital account of the production, and the production's `capital_raised_cents`, `escrow_balance_cents` and `active_investor_count` are re-rolled — and if `capital_raised_cents` has fallen below `capitalization_target_cents`, the syndication axis reverts `FULLY_SUBSCRIBED → OFFERING`. The instrument's accepted roll-ups are refreshed and both the instrument's and the production's `version` advance. Accept-then-rescind conserves the production's `capital_raised_cents` exactly.

Escrow is the last step of syndication. While money is being raised it sits in escrow: `released_capital_cents` reads 0 and `escrow_balance_cents` is `capital_raised_cents` less `released_capital_cents`, so it equals everything raised. A `Comptroller` releases it once the axis reads `FULLY_SUBSCRIBED`, which sets `released_capital_cents` equal to `capital_raised_cents`, drives `escrow_balance_cents` to 0, and advances the syndication axis `FULLY_SUBSCRIBED → CAPITALIZED`, the terminal state on that axis. Requesting the release from any other offering state reports `ERR_INVALID_OFFERING_STATE`. Capital accounts can be listed by management roles and filtered by `production_id` and `investor_id`; an individual account is readable by its own investor.

### 3.5. The Operating Calendar and the Weekly Operating Budget

The production calendar is advanced by the `LeadProducer`, one step at a time, by naming the desired `to_status`. Three advances are available: `PRE_PRODUCTION → REHEARSAL`, `REHEARSAL → PREVIEWS`, and `PREVIEWS → RUNNING`. Requesting a `to_status` that is not reachable from the current `operating_status` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_OPERATING_STATE` (HTTP 409). Two of the three advances carry an extra precondition. Leaving `PRE_PRODUCTION` requires that the syndication axis have reached `CAPITALIZED` — the cross-axis guard of §2.9. Leaving `REHEARSAL` for `PREVIEWS` requires that exactly one operating budget of the production be in `ACTIVE` status; if none is, the request is rejected with `ERR_ACTIVE_OPERATING_BUDGET_MISSING` (HTTP 409).

The weekly operating budget is drafted by a `GeneralManager` against a `production_id`. It declares a `weekly_fixed_running_cost_cents` of at least 1 cent as the budgeted weekly running cost, a `variable_cost_pct` capturing credit-card fees plus group commissions as a fraction of gross receipts, and a `performances_per_week` count between 1 and 16 inclusive, defaulting to 8 when omitted. Budgets are drafted in `DRAFT` status. A `Comptroller` activates one; activation is admissible only from `DRAFT`, and requesting it for a budget that is already `ACTIVE` or `SUPERSEDED` reports `ERR_INVALID_OPERATING_BUDGET_STATE` (HTTP 409).

Activation is deliberately not exclusive — a production may activate a second budget, and doing so supersedes the first. The previously `ACTIVE` budget of the same production, if there is one, transitions `ACTIVE → SUPERSEDED` and its `version` advances. That `version` bump is not bookkeeping: it is the mechanism by which superseding a budget invalidates every weekly wrap that pinned it, as §3.7 explains. At any moment a production therefore has at most one `ACTIVE` budget, which is the one every new wrap pins. Operating budgets can be listed and filtered by `production_id` and by `status`.

The budgeted running cost and the variable-cost percentage play different roles. `variable_cost_pct` is applied to actual reported grosses every week to convert gross receipts into adjusted gross. `weekly_fixed_running_cost_cents` is *not* applied to actual weeks at all — actual running costs are reported per wrap — and instead feeds the break-even projection of §3.8. `performances_per_week` likewise feeds only the break-even projection; the count of performances actually played is reported on the wrap.

### 3.6. Royalty Participations and the Conversion to a Pool

Royalty participants are the creative and producing parties owed a share of the show's receipts. A `GeneralManager` registers one against a `production_id` with a `participant_name` of 1 to 200 characters and a `role` drawn from `AUTHOR`, `COMPOSER`, `DIRECTOR`, `CHOREOGRAPHER`, `DESIGNER`, `LEAD_PRODUCER`, and `STAR`. Every participant carries the terms for *both* possible royalty structures at once, because a show can convert from one to the other mid-run and the contracts are drafted for both eventualities. `fixed_gross_pct` is the participant's contractual royalty expressed as a fraction of adjusted gross and matters only while the show is on the fixed-gross structure. `pool_points` is the participant's point count in the royalty pool, at least 0, and matters only once the show has converted. `min_guarantee_cents` is the participant's weekly minimum guarantee, at least 0 — and this field has a narrow, easily-overstated role: it participates **only** in the aggregate pool floor and in the deferral accrual. Per-participant guarantees are never topped up. A participant whose points-proportional share falls short of their own guarantee does not receive the difference.

Every participant starts `ACTIVE`. A `LeadProducer` may terminate an `ACTIVE` participation and may reinstate a `TERMINATED` one; requesting terminate on a participation that is not `ACTIVE`, or reinstate on one that is not `TERMINATED`, reports `ERR_INVALID_PARTICIPANT_STATE` (HTTP 409). Terminating sets that participant's `pool_share_pct` to 0.0000, re-rolls the production's `total_pool_points`, recomputes every surviving `ACTIVE` participant's `pool_share_pct`, and advances the production's `version`. Reinstating re-rolls `total_pool_points`, recomputes every `ACTIVE` participant's `pool_share_pct`, and advances the production's `version`. The pair conserves `total_pool_points` exactly: terminate then reinstate returns the production to the point total it had. Registering a participant recomputes the same two things. Participants can be listed and filtered by `production_id`, by `status`, and by `role`.

Pool shares are derived, never supplied, and the derivation is precise. The production's `total_pool_points` is the sum of `pool_points` over every `ACTIVE` participation of the production — including any `ACTIVE` participant whose `pool_points` is 0, who contributes nothing to the sum but is still counted as active. The participants who actually receive a share are the narrower set: those that are `ACTIVE` and whose `pool_points` is at least 1, ordered by `pool_points` descending and, among equal point counts, by `created_at` ascending. If `total_pool_points` is 0, or if that ordered set is empty, every participant's `pool_share_pct` is 0.0000 and the derivation stops. Otherwise each member of the ordered set gets its own `pool_points` divided by `total_pool_points`, rounded half up to four decimals; the sum of those rounded shares is then subtracted from 1.0000 and the signed difference is added to the first member of the order — the highest-points participant, earliest-created among ties. Participants that are `TERMINATED`, and `ACTIVE` participants holding 0 points, are assigned 0.0000. The shares over the receiving set therefore total exactly 1.0000, or exactly 0.0000 when the set is empty. A participant's `cumulative_royalty_cents` is a separate figure: the sum of `allocated_cents` across that participant's royalty allocations whose `status` is `ALLOCATED`.

Conversion from fixed royalties to a pool is the `LeadProducer`'s prerogative and a one-way door. Converting sets the production's `royalty_basis` from `FIXED_GROSS` to `POOL`, recomputes `total_pool_points` and every participant's `pool_share_pct`, and advances the production's `version`. Because it is one-way, a second conversion request against a production whose `royalty_basis` already reads `POOL` is rejected with `ERR_ROYALTY_BASIS_ALREADY_POOL` (HTTP 409). Every production starts on `FIXED_GROSS`, and the choice determines which of two genuinely different settlement arithmetics governs each week — the subject of §3.7.

### 3.7. The Weekly Wrap: Reporting, Certification and Settlement

Each performance week is reported as a wrap. A `GeneralManager` opens one against a `production_id`, supplying the `week_ending_date` that closes the performance week, the count of `performances` actually played (between 1 and 16 inclusive — sixteen is the ceiling on performances in any week, on the wrap and on the operating budget alike), the `gross_receipts_cents` taken at the box office (at least 0), the `paid_attendance` admitted (at least 0), and the `running_expenses_cents` actually incurred, excluding all royalties (at least 0). A wrap opens in `DRAFT`.

Opening a wrap has three preconditions beyond shape. The production's `operating_status` must be one of `PREVIEWS`, `RUNNING`, or `CLOSING_NOTICE`; a production still in `PRE_PRODUCTION` or `REHEARSAL`, or already `CLOSED`, is rejected with `ERR_PRODUCTION_NOT_PERFORMING` (HTTP 409). Exactly one operating budget of the production must be `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_ACTIVE_OPERATING_BUDGET_MISSING` (HTTP 409) — because opening a wrap **pins** that budget, capturing its identifier as `operating_budget_id` and its current `version` as `operating_budget_version_pinned`. And the `week_ending_date` may not be later than the current logical UTC date; a week that has not finished yet cannot be wrapped, and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_WRAP_WEEK_IN_FUTURE` (HTTP 422).

Weeks may not overlap. A wrap occupies the half-open interval running from seven days before its `week_ending_date` up to but not including the `week_ending_date` itself. If a new wrap's interval intersects the interval of any other wrap of the same production that is not `VOID`, the create is rejected with `ERR_WRAP_WEEK_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). Equivalently, two non-`VOID` wraps of the same production collide whenever their `week_ending_date` values are fewer than seven calendar days apart; voided wraps are excluded from the check, so a week can be re-reported after its original wrap has been voided.

Opening a wrap immediately derives the front half of the settlement provisionally, through the royalty base but no further. The first step is capacity and attendance. `capacity_gross_cents` is the pinned house's `seat_count` multiplied by its `top_ticket_price_cents` multiplied by the wrap's `performances` — what the week would have grossed with every seat sold at the top price at every performance. `paid_capacity_pct` is the `paid_attendance` divided by the product of the house's `seat_count` and the wrap's `performances`, rounded half up to four decimals. The second step converts gross to adjusted gross: `variable_cost_cents` is the `gross_receipts_cents` multiplied by the pinned operating budget's `variable_cost_pct`, rounded half up to whole cents, and `adjusted_gross_cents` is `gross_receipts_cents` less `variable_cost_cents`. The third step is the royalty base: `royalty_base_cents` is `adjusted_gross_cents` less `running_expenses_cents`, floored at 0 — a losing week has a royalty base of zero, never a negative one. Theatre and operating-budget figures are read from the pinned rows and are never re-selected later.

Submission is the `GeneralManager`'s hand-off. A wrap may be submitted from `DRAFT` or from `AMENDED`, and only while the production's `operating_status` is `PREVIEWS`, `RUNNING`, or `CLOSING_NOTICE` — otherwise `ERR_PRODUCTION_NOT_PERFORMING`. Submission records the caller's `X-User-ID` as the wrap's `submitted_by_user_id` and advances the wrap's `version`. Submitting from any other state reports `ERR_INVALID_WRAP_STATE` (HTTP 409).

A `Comptroller` reviews a `SUBMITTED` wrap and either disputes it or certifies it. Disputing records a `dispute_note` — null on any wrap never disputed — moves the wrap to `DISPUTED`, and advances its `version`. A `DISPUTED` wrap goes back to the `GeneralManager` for amendment, which is where the `SUBMITTED → DISPUTED → AMENDED → SUBMITTED` cycle turns. Amendment accepts any subset of `performances`, `gross_receipts_cents`, `paid_attendance`, and `running_expenses_cents`, overwrites the supplied fields, and re-derives `capacity_gross_cents`, `paid_capacity_pct`, `variable_cost_cents`, `adjusted_gross_cents` and `royalty_base_cents` provisionally. Amendment also **re-pins**: `operating_budget_id` and `operating_budget_version_pinned` are recaptured from the production's current `ACTIVE` operating budget, which requires that exactly one such budget exist and otherwise reports `ERR_ACTIVE_OPERATING_BUDGET_MISSING`. Amendment clears `submitted_by_user_id`, so the next submission records a fresh submitter, and it advances the wrap's `version`. Amending a wrap that is not `DISPUTED` reports `ERR_INVALID_WRAP_STATE`, and amending while the production is not performing reports `ERR_PRODUCTION_NOT_PERFORMING`. The performance-week temporal rule is re-applied on amendment as well as on creation: a wrap whose `week_ending_date` is later than the current logical UTC date at the moment of amendment cannot be amended, and the attempt reports `ERR_WRAP_WEEK_IN_FUTURE` (HTTP 422). The `week_ending_date` itself is not among the amendable fields, so this rule bites when the logical clock has been moved backwards since the wrap was opened.

Certification is the pivotal write in the show-accounting half of the system, and it has three preconditions. The wrap must be `SUBMITTED`. The certifying operator's `X-User-ID` must differ from the wrap's `submitted_by_user_id`, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). And the pinned budget must be unchanged: the current `version` of the operating budget identified by `operating_budget_id` must still equal the wrap's `operating_budget_version_pinned`. If a newer budget has superseded it in the meantime — bumping the old row's `version` — the certification is refused with `ERR_OPERATING_BUDGET_CHANGED` (HTTP 409), and the wrap must be disputed and amended so that it re-pins to the budget now in force.

On certification the wrap's `royalty_basis_applied` is frozen to whatever the production's `royalty_basis` reads at that instant, and that frozen value selects between two settlement variants that produce genuinely different numbers from identical inputs. Both variants read the same participant set: the production's participations that are `ACTIVE` and hold at least 1 `pool_point`, ordered by `pool_points` descending then `created_at` ascending. Terminated participations, and active ones holding zero points, are excluded from every part of the settlement — from the aggregate guarantee floor, from the fixed-gross sum, and from the split. The aggregate floor, `pool_floor_cents`, is the sum of `min_guarantee_cents` across that set.

Under the **fixed-gross variant** — the structure every production starts in — `royalty_pool_cents` is the sum, over each participant in the ordered set, of that participant's `fixed_gross_pct` applied to the wrap's `adjusted_gross_cents` and rounded half up to whole cents *per participant* before summing. The rounding happens participant by participant, in the pinned order, and only then are the amounts added together. The aggregate guarantee floor and the pool percentages play no part whatsoever in this variant. The consequence is commercially significant: because the burden is a percentage of adjusted gross, it is entirely insensitive to running expenses and is owed in full even in a week that lost money. `deferred_royalty_added_cents` is 0 under this variant.

Under the **pool variant** the burden is a percentage of weekly operating profit rather than of gross, and the percentage itself switches on recoupment. The applicable percentage is the production's `post_recoupment_pool_pct` when its `recoupment_status` reads `RECOUPED`, and its `pre_recoupment_pool_pct` otherwise. If the wrap's `royalty_base_cents` is 0 or less — a week with no operating profit at all — then `royalty_pool_cents` is 0 and `deferred_royalty_added_cents` is set to the full `pool_floor_cents`: the guarantees are not paid, they accrue. Otherwise `royalty_pool_cents` is computed in three pinned steps: take `royalty_base_cents` multiplied by the applicable pool percentage and round half up to whole cents; take the greater of that figure and `pool_floor_cents`, which is the floor the guarantees impose in aggregate; then take the lesser of that result and `royalty_base_cents`, which caps the burden so that a week can never owe more royalty than it earned in operating profit. `deferred_royalty_added_cents` is 0 in this branch.

Whichever variant applied, `weekly_operating_profit_cents` is the wrap's `royalty_base_cents` less its `royalty_pool_cents`.

Certification then cascades five hops deep. The wrap's settled figures are written and `certified_by_user_id` is recorded. Second, the royalty pool is split into one allocation row per eligible participant. Third, each participant's `cumulative_royalty_cents` is re-rolled. Fourth, because the week's operating profit has entered the ledger, the recoupment figures are re-derived — and if the week's `royalty_base_cents` was 0, this wrap's `deferred_royalty_added_cents` is added to the production's `deferred_royalty_cents`. Fifth, if that recompute has carried `recouped_amount_cents` to or past `capitalization_target_cents`, the production's `recoupment_status` becomes `RECOUPED`, and every capital account of the production flips its `basis` from `RECOUPMENT_RETURN` to `NET_PROFIT` and refreshes its `recoupment_credited_cents`. The production's `version` and the wrap's `version` each advance by one.

The split itself is precisely ordered. Sum `pool_points` across the ordered eligible set to get the point total. If the wrap's `royalty_pool_cents` is 0, or the point total is 0, or the eligible set is empty, **no allocation rows are created at all** and the split stops. Under the fixed-gross variant no reconciliation is needed, because each participant's own percentage already *is* their amount and their sum is by construction the wrap's `royalty_pool_cents`: each allocation's `allocated_cents` is that participant's `fixed_gross_pct` applied to the wrap's `adjusted_gross_cents`, rounded half up to whole cents, with `basis` recorded as `FIXED_GROSS` and `points_at_allocation` recorded as 0. Under the pool variant, reconciliation is required and its order is pinned: first compute each participant's raw share as the wrap's `royalty_pool_cents` multiplied by that participant's `pool_points` divided by the point total, rounding **each participant's share to whole cents first**; then subtract the sum of those rounded shares from the wrap's `royalty_pool_cents` to get a residue, which may be negative; then add that residue to the first participant in the order — highest `pool_points`, earliest `created_at` among ties. Each allocation records `basis` as `POOL` and `points_at_allocation` as the participant's `pool_points` observed at certification. Summing exact fractions and rounding once at the end is *not* the specified order and will disagree by cents. Every allocation row also carries the `wrap_id` of the certified wrap that produced it and the `royalty_participant_id` of the participant paid, and is written with `status` `ALLOCATED`. Allocations are created only by cascade — there is no endpoint that creates one — so listing them, filtered by `wrap_id`, by `royalty_participant_id`, or by `status`, is the only channel through which a client discovers them.

Voiding is certification's compensating action, available to a `Comptroller` on a `CERTIFIED` wrap while the production's `operating_status` is anything other than `CLOSED`. It records a `void_reason` — null on any wrap never voided — and unwinds all five hops. Every allocation of that wrap whose `status` is `ALLOCATED` becomes `REVERSED` and its `version` advances; each affected participant's `cumulative_royalty_cents` is re-rolled downward, since reversed allocations no longer count; the recoupment figures are re-derived without this wrap, because a `VOID` wrap is outside the certified input set, and this wrap's `deferred_royalty_added_cents` is subtracted from the production's `deferred_royalty_cents`; and if the recompute drops `recouped_amount_cents` back below `capitalization_target_cents`, the production reverts to `UNRECOUPED` and every capital account flips its `basis` back to `RECOUPMENT_RETURN`. The production's and the wrap's `version` each advance. Certify-then-void conserves the production's `recouped_amount_cents` exactly. `VOID` is terminal — a voided wrap cannot be revived, though its week becomes free for a fresh wrap. Wraps can be listed and filtered by `production_id`, by `operating_budget_id`, and by `status`.

### 3.8. The Recoupment Ledger, Licensing Income and Break-Even Reporting

Recoupment is the number investors care about, and it is derived — never supplied — on two bases that report different figures from the same stored data.

The **cash basis** is authoritative: it is what is persisted on the production and the only basis that may move `recoupment_status`. It sums three streams. First, the `weekly_operating_profit_cents` of every wrap of the production whose `status` is `CERTIFIED` — wraps in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `DISPUTED`, `AMENDED` or `VOID` contribute nothing. Second, the `investor_share_cents` of every licensing-income row of the production whose `status` is `ALLOCATED`. Third, the `reserve_released_cents` of every closing notice of the production whose `status` is `SETTLED`. Their total is the production's `recouped_amount_cents`. Its `recoupment_pct` is `recouped_amount_cents` divided by `capitalization_target_cents`, rounded half up to four decimals. Its `recoupment_status` is `RECOUPED` when `recouped_amount_cents` is greater than or equal to `capitalization_target_cents`, and `UNRECOUPED` otherwise — the comparison is inclusive of equality, so hitting the target to the cent is recoupment. A rounding subtlety follows and matters for reporting: `recoupment_pct` can round up to 1.0000 while `recoupment_status` still reads `UNRECOUPED`, because the percentage is rounded and the status test is not. `distributed_total_cents` is the sum of `total_amount_cents` over the production's distributions whose `status` is `DECLARED` or `PAID`, and `distributable_cash_cents` is `recouped_amount_cents` less `distributed_total_cents`, floored at 0. The cash basis ignores the production's `deferred_royalty_cents` entirely and ignores every licensing-income row still in `RECORDED`.

The **accrual basis** exists for reporting only and never touches `recoupment_status`. It starts from the cash-basis `recouped_amount_cents`, subtracts the production's `deferred_royalty_cents` — the accrued but unpaid minimum guarantees banked by loss weeks under the pool structure — and adds a receivable: the sum, over every licensing-income row of the production whose `status` is still `RECORDED`, of that row's `gross_income_cents` multiplied by the production's `subsidiary_rights_investor_share_pct` and rounded half up to whole cents per row. The result is the accrual-basis recouped amount, and dividing it by `capitalization_target_cents` and rounding half up to four decimals gives the accrual-basis percentage.

Both bases are published side by side for investor reporting. The recoupment report names its `production_id` and the `capitalization_target_cents` against which everything is measured, then reports `cash_basis_recouped_cents` and `cash_basis_recoupment_pct` alongside `accrual_basis_recouped_cents` and `accrual_basis_recoupment_pct`, together with the `deferred_royalty_cents` subtracted and the `subsidiary_rights_receivable_cents` added to get from one to the other, plus the current `recoupment_status` and `distributable_cash_cents`. It is a pure read: it recomputes both bases and persists nothing. Investors may read it; so may the general management office, the comptroller, and the lead producer.

Subsidiary-rights licensing income is recorded by a `GeneralManager` against a `production_id`, with a `source` drawn from `CAST_ALBUM`, `FILM_LICENSE`, `STOCK_AND_AMATEUR`, `TOURING_LICENSE`, and `MERCHANDISE`, a `gross_income_cents` of at least 1 cent, and the `received_date` on which the money arrived. Income is filed in `RECORDED` status with `investor_share_cents` at 0, and in that state it feeds only the accrual basis. A `Comptroller` allocates it, which is what moves it into the cash ledger. Allocation is admissible only from `RECORDED` — a second attempt reports `ERR_INVALID_INCOME_STATE` (HTTP 409) — and only while the production's `offering_status` reads `CAPITALIZED`, else `ERR_PRODUCTION_NOT_CAPITALIZED` (HTTP 409). Allocation computes `investor_share_cents` as the row's `gross_income_cents` multiplied by the production's `subsidiary_rights_investor_share_pct`, read from the production at that moment, and rounded half up to whole cents. It then cascades: the recoupment figures are re-derived, and if the recompute crosses the target, `recoupment_status` becomes `RECOUPED` and every capital account of the production flips its `basis` to `NET_PROFIT`. The production's `version` advances. `ALLOCATED` is terminal. Licensing income can be listed and filtered by `production_id`, by `status`, and by `source`.

Break-even is a forward-looking read available to the general management office, the comptroller and the lead producer, and it answers the question every producer asks on a Monday: what does this show have to gross to stop losing money, and how long until the investors are whole? It requires exactly one `ACTIVE` operating budget for the production and reports `ERR_ACTIVE_OPERATING_BUDGET_MISSING` (HTTP 409) when there is none. The analysis names its `production_id` and the `operating_budget_id` it read.

The arithmetic starts from the guarantee floor. `royalty_min_guarantee_floor_cents` is the sum of `min_guarantee_cents` over the production's participations that are `ACTIVE` and hold at least 1 `pool_point`; an empty set gives a floor of 0. Break-even is defined as the smallest weekly gross whose weekly operating profit is *strictly* positive, and under the pool structure profit is positive exactly when the royalty base clears the guarantee floor — so `target_adjusted_gross_cents` is the operating budget's `weekly_fixed_running_cost_cents`, plus `royalty_min_guarantee_floor_cents`, plus one further cent.

Turning that adjusted-gross target into a gross is circular, because adjusted gross is defined by subtracting a percentage of the very gross being sought, and the percentage is applied with rounding. The evaluation order is therefore pinned. Seed the search by dividing `target_adjusted_gross_cents` by one minus the operating budget's `variable_cost_pct` and rounding the quotient up to the next whole cent. Then walk down: while the candidate gross is greater than zero and the candidate one cent lower would still clear the target — that is, while that lower candidate less its own variable cost, rounded half up to whole cents, is greater than or equal to `target_adjusted_gross_cents` — decrease the candidate by one cent. Then walk up: while the candidate less its own variable cost, rounded half up to whole cents, is less than `target_adjusted_gross_cents`, increase the candidate by one cent. Because the quantity "gross less its rounded variable cost" never decreases as gross increases, both walks are monotone and both terminate; neither needs more than one hundred steps from the seed. The surviving candidate is `break_even_gross_cents`.

Two figures follow. `capacity_gross_projection_cents` is the house's `seat_count` multiplied by its `top_ticket_price_cents` multiplied by the operating budget's `performances_per_week` — the theoretical weekly ceiling on a full schedule, which is why the projection uses the *budgeted* performance count rather than any actual week's. `break_even_paid_capacity_pct` is `break_even_gross_cents` divided by `capacity_gross_projection_cents`, rounded half up to four decimals: the percentage of the house the show must sell, at top price, to break even.

The run-rate projection closes the report. Take the production's wrap with `status` `CERTIFIED` and the greatest `week_ending_date`, breaking ties by `created_at` descending — the most recently certified week. If there is no such wrap, or if its `weekly_operating_profit_cents` is 0 or negative, then `weeks_to_recoup_at_last_certified_run_rate` is null: a show that is not making money has no run rate to project. Otherwise take the production's `capitalization_target_cents` less its `recouped_amount_cents`, floored at 0, divide by that wrap's `weekly_operating_profit_cents`, and round the quotient up to the next whole week.

### 3.9. Returning Cash: Distributions, and Closing the Production

Once the ledger holds cash the investors have not yet received, a `Comptroller` declares a distribution against a `production_id`, naming a `distribution_type` of `RECOUPMENT_RETURN`, `NET_PROFIT`, `SUBSIDIARY_RIGHTS`, or `RESERVE_RELEASE`, a `total_amount_cents` of at least 1 cent, and — as §2.7 requires — the `expected_production_version` the client last observed. The fiscal gates of §2.11 apply in their stated order: the production must have at least one capital account with units before any cash test runs, a `NET_PROFIT` distribution requires `recoupment_status` to read `RECOUPED`, and the amount may not exceed `distributable_cash_cents`.

Declaration freezes the split, and freezing is the whole point. Order the production's capital accounts whose `units_total` is greater than zero by `units_total` descending, breaking ties by `created_at` ascending; accounts reduced to zero units by rescission are excluded, and if no account survives the filter the declaration is refused with `ERR_NO_INVESTOR_ACCOUNTS`. Compute each surviving account's share as the distribution's `total_amount_cents` multiplied by that account's `ownership_pct`, rounded half up to whole cents — each share rounded first. Subtract the sum of those rounded shares from `total_amount_cents` and add the signed residue to the first account in the order. The result is written as the distribution's `allocation_breakdown`: one entry per account, each naming the `investor_account_id`, the `investor_id`, and the `amount_cents` that account is owed, and the entry amounts sum to exactly `total_amount_cents`. Each account's `declared_distribution_cents` — its cumulative share across the production's distributions in `DECLARED` or `PAID` — increases by its frozen share. The production's `distributed_total_cents` and `distributable_cash_cents` are re-rolled and its `version` advances. The distribution is filed as `DECLARED`.

Payment replays the frozen breakdown and never recomputes it: this is deliberate, because ownership can legitimately move between declaration and payment and the investors are owed what was declared, not what the arithmetic would say today. A `Comptroller` pays a `DECLARED` distribution, which walks each entry of `allocation_breakdown` and increases the referenced capital account's `distributions_paid_cents` by that entry's `amount_cents`, moves the distribution to the terminal `PAID`, and advances its `version`. Paying a distribution that is not `DECLARED` reports `ERR_INVALID_DISTRIBUTION_STATE` (HTTP 409). Distributions can be listed and filtered by `production_id`, by `status`, and by `distribution_type`; investors may read them.

Closing a show is the `LeadProducer`'s decision, and it is posted as a notice against a `production_id`. The notice carries a `notice_date` on which it is posted, a `final_performance_date`, and a `reason` of `SCHEDULED_END`, `ECONOMIC`, or `VENUE_LOSS`. Three rules govern the posting. The production may not already hold a notice in `ISSUED` status, else `ERR_CLOSING_NOTICE_ALREADY_ISSUED` (HTTP 409). The `notice_date` may not be earlier than the current logical UTC date — you cannot back-date a closing notice — else `ERR_NOTICE_DATE_IN_PAST` (HTTP 422). And the notice period must be long enough: the system computes `notice_days` as the whole days from `notice_date` to `final_performance_date`, and that count must be at least the production's `closing_notice_min_days`, else `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_CLOSING_NOTICE` (HTTP 422). The notice is filed as `ISSUED` with the caller's `X-User-ID` recorded as `issued_by_user_id`.

Posting the notice cascades three hops. The production's `operating_status` moves to `CLOSING_NOTICE`, which is admissible from either `PREVIEWS` or `RUNNING` and from nowhere else. Every budget line of the production whose `component` is `RESERVE` and whose `commitment_status` is `PLANNED` becomes `COMMITTED` — the reserve is being earmarked for the costs of shutting down. And because those lines moved, the production's `budget_committed_cents` is re-rolled. The production's `version` advances.

Withdrawal reverses all three hops. A `LeadProducer` may withdraw a notice that is `ISSUED` while the production's `operating_status` reads `CLOSING_NOTICE`; the notice records a `withdrawal_reason`, becomes the terminal `WITHDRAWN`, and its `version` advances. The production returns to `RUNNING`, every `RESERVE` line that is `COMMITTED` reverts to `PLANNED`, and `budget_committed_cents` is re-rolled. This is the `RUNNING → CLOSING_NOTICE → RUNNING` cycle of §2.9, and it conserves `budget_committed_cents`.

Settlement is the last write in the production's life and belongs to a `Comptroller`. It carries four preconditions: the notice must be `ISSUED`, else `ERR_INVALID_CLOSING_NOTICE_STATE` (HTTP 409); the production's `operating_status` must read `CLOSING_NOTICE`; the settling operator's `X-User-ID` must differ from the notice's `issued_by_user_id`, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403); and no wrap of the production may still be in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `DISPUTED`, or `AMENDED`, else `ERR_UNCERTIFIED_WRAPS_EXIST` (HTTP 409) — the books must be closed before the show is. The comptroller supplies the actual `closing_costs_cents`, at least 0; that field reads null on any notice not yet settled, and the operator who settles is recorded as `settled_by_user_id`.

The reserve is then applied in two buckets. `reserve_balance_at_settlement_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` over the production's budget lines whose `component` is `RESERVE` and whose `commitment_status` is `COMMITTED` at the settlement instant — these are exactly the lines the notice's posting moved from `PLANNED`, so `PLANNED` and `SPENT` lines are outside the scope. The first bucket pays the closing costs from that balance. The second releases whatever survives: `reserve_released_cents` is the reserve balance less `closing_costs_cents`, floored at 0. If the costs ran over the reserve, the excess is recorded as `closing_cost_shortfall_cents`, which is `closing_costs_cents` less the reserve balance, floored at 0. Exactly one of the two figures can be positive for any given settlement; no rounding is involved, since both are differences of whole cents.

Settlement then cascades four hops. The notice becomes the terminal `SETTLED`. Every `RESERVE` line that was `COMMITTED` becomes the terminal `SPENT`, and the reserve release is booked. Because `reserve_released_cents` has entered the cash ledger, the production's `recouped_amount_cents`, `recoupment_pct` and `distributable_cash_cents` are re-derived. And if that recompute carries the ledger to or past `capitalization_target_cents`, the production's `recoupment_status` becomes `RECOUPED` and every capital account of the production flips its `basis` to `NET_PROFIT` and refreshes its `recoupment_credited_cents` — a show can, and often does, recoup on its closing reserve release. The production's `operating_status` moves to the terminal `CLOSED`, and its `version` advances. Once the production is `CLOSED`, no wrap of it may be voided. Closing notices can be listed and filtered by `production_id` and by `status`.
