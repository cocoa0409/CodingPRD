# State Veterans Home Admission & Per Diem API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A state veterans home sits at the junction of three accounting systems that rarely agree with one another. The state decides who is eligible to live there and in what order applicants are offered beds. The clinical staff decides what level of care each resident actually needs and whether a bed in a licensed unit exists to deliver it. The federal per diem program pays a fixed daily rate for each recognized resident day — but only if the home's federal recognition is intact, only if the unit passed its survey, and only if the census the home reports can be reconstructed day by day. Today those three views are maintained in separate spreadsheets, reconciled monthly by hand, and routinely disagree: a bed is offered to an applicant whose unit was decertified that morning, a resident's cost share is billed against income that was never verified, and a month's per diem claim double-counts days that a prior claim already consumed.

This document specifies the API that replaces those spreadsheets with a single authoritative record. The system is mounted under `/svh` and governs the full arc of a veteran's stay: application intake and eligibility adjudication, waitlist priority and bed offers, clinical assessment and the admission decision, care-plan development and level-of-care transfers, hospitalization bed-holds and readmission, resident cost-share determination against income and federal per diem offsets, trust-fund and personal-needs-allowance bookkeeping, discharge, and the assembly, submission and federal adjudication of monthly per diem claims. Overlaid on all of it is the survey-and-certification regime, which can freeze a unit and suspend a home's federal recognition in a single cascade.

Two design commitments shape everything that follows. First, **every derived number is reconstructible**: bed counts, priority scores, cost shares and claim amounts are all defined as pinned, single-pass computations over persisted rows, never as running totals that could drift. Second, **contested writes are serialized explicitly**: where two operators could reasonably act on the same bed, the same resident's finances, or the same month's census, the API demands that the client name the version of the record it observed, and rejects the write if that version has moved. Engineers implementing this specification should treat both commitments as non-negotiable acceptance criteria.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the rules that hold across every endpoint. Where a rule in this section conflicts with a convenience reading of §3, this section governs.

### 2.1. Actors and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which that user acts. If either header is absent or present but empty, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the seven role names the system recognizes — the comparison being exact and case-sensitive — the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If the headers are well-formed but the declared role is not among the roles authorized for the target operation in the endpoint table of §4, the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The seven roles are `StateProgramAdmin`, `AdmissionsCoordinator`, `ClinicalAssessor`, `BusinessOfficeManager`, `FederalPerDiemAuditor`, `SurveyAgency` and `ResidentRepresentative`.

A `StateProgramAdmin` is the state veterans affairs program administrator: this role alone registers veterans homes, creates care units, changes a home's federal recognition posture, sets the logical clock, and runs the nightly census and operations checkpoint. An `AdmissionsCoordinator` is home admissions staff: this role alone files applications, runs eligibility determinations, creates waitlist entries, records admission decisions, transfers residents between units, and discharges residents. A `ClinicalAssessor` is the licensed clinician: this role alone creates and submits clinical assessments and authors and activates care plans. Starting a hospitalization bed-hold and recording a readmission are shared between the `AdmissionsCoordinator` and the `ClinicalAssessor`. A `BusinessOfficeManager` is home business-office staff: this role alone records and verifies income records, creates cost-share determinations, posts and reverses trust-fund transactions, and opens, corrects and submits per diem claims. A `FederalPerDiemAuditor` adjudicates submitted claims and does nothing else in the write direction — it can neither create, correct nor submit a claim. A `SurveyAgency` records survey results and does nothing else in the write direction. A `ResidentRepresentative` is the resident or the resident's legal representative and is read-only throughout.

Identity semantics differ between these roles and must be implemented exactly. When `X-User-Role` is any of `StateProgramAdmin`, `AdmissionsCoordinator`, `ClinicalAssessor`, `BusinessOfficeManager`, `FederalPerDiemAuditor` or `SurveyAgency`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it where a workflow demands an audit trail — as `decided_by_user_id` on an `Applicant`, `assessor_user_id` on a `ClinicalAssessment`, `authored_by_user_id` on a `CarePlan`, `verified_by_user_id` on an `IncomeRecord`, `determined_by_user_id` on a `CostShareDetermination`, and `recorded_by_user_id` on a `TrustFundTransaction` — but never compares it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `ResidentRepresentative`, by contrast, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting resident; every request made in that capacity must address that same resident, either through the resident identifier in the path or through the `resident_id` of the addressed child row. A `ResidentRepresentative` reaching for another resident's record, income, cost-share determinations, care plans or trust-fund activity is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). One collection read open to that role carries no resident identifier in its path at all — the trust-fund transaction list — and there the acting `ResidentRepresentative` must scope the read itself, supplying a `resident_id` query filter equal to their own id; a read left unscoped, or scoped to another resident, is rejected with the same `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` once the request-shape rung has passed.

Two segregation-of-duties separations are layered on top of role checks and are described in §2.10.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`, followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the uppercase Latin letters and the decimal digits — a total length of twenty-four characters.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, read from the logical clock of §2.5 rather than from wall-clock time, so every response minted between two clock settings reports the identical instant.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination, Filtering and Collection Ordering

Every collection read is paginated by offset. The `limit` query parameter caps the number of rows returned; when omitted it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. The `offset` query parameter is the number of rows to skip and defaults to 0; negative values are not accepted. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is a request-shape violation and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422); so is a `limit` or `offset` that is supplied but is not a plain base-ten integer literal — an empty value, a decimal fraction, a non-numeric string or a zero-padded form such as `01` are all `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, since the accepted form is the canonical decimal rendering of the integer, optionally surrounded by whitespace.

Every array response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope. `total_count` is the number of rows matching the request's filters across the whole collection, not the number of rows on the returned page, and `has_more` is true exactly when rows matching the filters remain beyond the returned page. Single-object responses carry none of these four fields.

Unless an endpoint declares its own ordering in §4, a collection is returned sorted by `created_at` ascending. Because the logical clock of §2.5 stands still until a client moves it, several rows routinely share one `created_at`, so every ordering in this system carries one final, implicit tie-break: rows whose declared sort keys are all equal come back in the order the server persisted them, following the direction of the last declared sort key — oldest insertion first where that key is ascending, newest insertion first where it is descending. The record `id` is never a sort key anywhere in this system. Where a sort key is an enumerated value, "ascending" means ascending by the enum member's own spelling, so `care_level` ascending runs `ADULT_DAY_HEALTH`, `DOMICILIARY`, `NURSING` rather than in clinical-intensity order. Several collections in this system do declare their own ordering, because operators read them in domain order rather than in insertion order: veterans homes come back newest first by `created_at` descending and then by `name` ascending; a home's care units by `care_level` ascending then `unit_name` ascending then `created_at` ascending; a home's applicants by `application_received_date` ascending then `created_at` ascending; a unit's waitlist entries by `queue_position` ascending then `created_at` ascending; an applicant's waitlist entries by `listed_at` ascending then `created_at` ascending; an applicant's clinical assessments by `assessment_date` descending then `created_at` descending; residents by `admission_date` ascending then `created_at` ascending; income records by `effective_month` ascending then `income_type` ascending; cost-share determinations by `effective_month` ascending then `created_at` ascending; care plans by `plan_date` ascending then `revision_number` ascending; census days by `service_date` ascending then `created_at` ascending; per diem claims by `claim_month` ascending then `correction_generation` ascending then `created_at` ascending; and trust fund transactions by `effective_date` ascending then `created_at` ascending.

Collection reads also accept equality filters, all optional and all combinable with `limit` and `offset`. A filter is applied by exact string equality against the stored field, and a filter supplied with an empty value is treated as though it had not been supplied at all. Identifier- and enum-valued filters are deliberately *not* validated against their declared sets: a value outside the set is a legal request that simply matches no row and answers 200 with an empty `data` array and a `total_count` of 0, never `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The two boolean filters, `claimed` on census days and `reversed` on trust fund transactions, accept `true`, `false`, `1` and `0` in any letter case and reject anything else with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; the date-window bounds `service_date_from` and `service_date_to` must parse as `YYYY-MM-DD` calendar dates and the month filters as `YYYY-MM`, else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Query parameters the endpoint does not declare are ignored. Veterans homes filter on `state_code` and `recognition_status`. A home's care units filter on `care_level` and `certification_status`. A home's applicants filter on `status` and `requested_care_level`. Waitlist entry lists — both the per-unit and the per-applicant view — filter on `status`. An applicant's clinical assessments filter on `status`. The resident collection filters on `applicant_id`, `care_unit_id`, `occupancy_status` and `billing_status`. A resident's income records filter on `effective_month` and `verification_status`; a resident's cost-share determinations filter on `effective_month` and `variant`. The care plan collection filters on `resident_id`, `supersedes_care_plan_id` and `status`. The census day collection filters on `resident_id`, `care_unit_id`, `per_diem_claim_id`, `census_class` and `claimed`, and additionally accepts the inclusive date-window bounds `service_date_from` and `service_date_to`. The per diem claim collection filters on `veterans_home_id`, `care_unit_id`, `corrects_claim_id`, `claim_month`, `status` and `basis`. The trust fund transaction collection filters on `resident_id`, `reverses_transaction_id`, `txn_type` and `reversed`.

### 2.4. Common Record Properties and Version Semantics

Every persisted record carries a server-minted `id`, formatted as a UUID with no type prefix — a randomly generated version-4 UUID in the canonical lowercase hyphenated thirty-six-character form, drawn from the same generator for every entity type so that nothing about a record's kind can be read off its `id` — and a `created_at` timestamp: an ISO 8601 UTC instant with exactly three fractional digits, taken from the logical clock described in §2.5. Every other instant-valued field the server stamps from that clock is serialised in the same form — an ISO 8601 UTC instant with exactly three fractional digits and a trailing `Z` — including a waitlist entry's `listed_at` and `offer_expires_at`, a care plan's `activated_at`, a claim's `submitted_at` and `adjudicated_at`, and the `current_utc` reported by the logical-clock and checkpoint endpoints. Fields that hold a calendar date or a month keep their `YYYY-MM-DD` or `YYYY-MM` form instead. Every field this document names on a record appears in that record's serialised form on every response that carries it, whether the field was written by a client, derived by the server or never set at all: an unset field is rendered as JSON `null` and is never omitted, so a client may read a record's full field set without probing for key presence. Conversely, nothing this document does not name for a record is serialised; the server keeps no additional public field.

Most records additionally carry `updated_at` — an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, refreshed on every mutation of the record — and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the record is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation. This applies to `VeteransHome`, `CareUnit`, `Applicant`, `WaitlistEntry`, `ClinicalAssessment`, `Resident`, `IncomeRecord`, `CarePlan`, `CensusDay`, `PerDiemClaim` and `TrustFundTransaction`. A recomputation of a derived field counts as a mutation for this purpose: when a resident's admission drives a unit's `occupied_bed_count` down and its `available_bed_count` and `operational_posture` with it, that unit's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increments even though no client wrote to the unit directly. Increments are collapsed per request: however many of a request's hops touch one record — a unit whose bed counts, posture and queue all move inside a single admission, or a resident touched by several steps of one checkpoint run — that record's `version` advances by exactly 1 for the whole request and its `updated_at` is stamped once, so `version` counts *requests that changed the row*, not internal hops. The single deliberate exception is the redetermination call of §3.2, which is specified as two successive determinations of the same applicant and advances that applicant's `version` by 2; where §3 states a per-request increment count explicitly, that count governs.

`CostShareDetermination` is the single exception. A determination is immutable once computed — a revision is expressed by creating a superseding row rather than by editing the old one — so a `CostShareDetermination` carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has no `updated_at` field and no `version` field at all.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

Nothing in this system reads wall-clock time. Every timestamp the server mints, every offer-window expiry, every bed-hold day count, every care-plan review due date and every claim-month closure check is evaluated against a server-held logical clock. Before any client sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time. A `StateProgramAdmin` moves it by supplying `to_iso8601`, an explicit instant; the response's `data` object carries `current_utc` as its only field, formatted like every other instant the server mints. The clock accepts any of the ordinary ISO 8601 renderings of an instant: the date and time may be joined by `T` or by a single space, the seconds component may be omitted, one to six fractional digits are accepted, and the zone designator may be a `Z`, a numeric `±HH:MM` or `±HHMM` offset which is normalised to UTC before it is stored, or omitted altogether in which case the value is read as UTC already. A `to_iso8601` that does not parse as such an instant — including a syntactically well-formed but non-existent date such as `2026-02-30` — is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422). The clock may be set backwards as freely as forwards; no rule forbids moving it to an earlier instant than the one it already holds.

Dates supplied by clients are calendar dates in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, and months are in `YYYY-MM` form; a value that does not match its form is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422). Where a date is compared with the logical clock, the comparison uses the clock's UTC calendar date. A `survey_date`, `plan_date`, `effective_date` or `through_date` that lies in the future relative to the current logical clock is rejected with `ERR_DATE_OUT_OF_RANGE` (HTTP 409), as is any supplied date that violates its ordering rule against the resident's `admission_date`, `bed_hold_started_on`, or another prior date named in §3.

### 2.6. Version Pinning on Contested Writes

Three writes in this system can be issued by two operators holding different views of the same record, and none of them can be made safe by a state precondition alone. Each therefore demands that the client name the version it last observed, as a required — never optional — field in the request body.

Recording an admission decision requires `expected_care_unit_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `CareUnit`. If that value does not equal the unit's current `version`, the decision is rejected with `ERR_CARE_UNIT_VERSION_STALE` (HTTP 412). This is the guard against a coordinator deciding against a stale view of `available_bed_count` and `operational_posture` — another writer may already have consumed the last bed, or listed another applicant on that unit's queue, either of which advances the unit's `version`.

Creating a cost-share determination requires `expected_resident_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Resident`. A mismatch with the resident's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_RESIDENT_VERSION_STALE` (HTTP 412). Because a new determination legitimately supersedes the prior governing one for the same `effective_month`, no state check can catch this case: without the version pin, a determination computed from a stale reading would silently overwrite a determination the client never saw.

Opening a correcting claim requires `expected_claim_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the claim being corrected. A mismatch with that claim's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_CLAIM_VERSION_STALE` (HTTP 412). A correcting claim takes over its target's census attribution, so a second correction built on a stale view of the target would double-attribute the same census days.

No other endpoint in this system accepts a version field, and no other endpoint performs a version check. Where §3 describes a write not named above, the client sends no version and the server compares none.

### 2.7. Path Resolution and Foreign-Key Resolution Order

Identifiers embedded in a path are resolved strictly left to right, and the first one that resolves to nothing determines the error. The identifier segments the system resolves are those addressing a veterans home, a care unit, an applicant, a clinical assessment, a resident, an income record, a care plan, a census day, a per diem claim and a trust fund transaction; the corresponding rejections are `ERR_HOME_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CARE_UNIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPLICANT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RESIDENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INCOME_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CARE_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CENSUS_DAY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_TRANSACTION_NOT_FOUND`, each HTTP 404.

Foreign keys supplied in a request body are resolved after every path identifier has resolved, and they are resolved in a pinned field order: `veterans_home_id`, then `care_unit_id`, then `applicant_id`, then `resident_id`, then `target_care_unit_id`, then `clinical_assessment_id`, then `supersedes_care_plan_id`. Whichever of those fields the endpoint actually accepts, the first unresolvable one determines the 404 that is returned. Body-level foreign-key resolution applies to filing an applicant, creating a care unit under a home, creating a waitlist entry, recording an admission decision, authoring a care plan for a resident, transferring a resident, opening a per diem claim and confining a nightly census run to one home through its optional `veterans_home_id`.

Nesting in the URL hierarchy exists in this system only as a reverse-reachability read channel and as a creation-in-parent-context write channel. No entity is addressed by a multi-level path; every child record carries its parents as explicit foreign-key fields in its own body, so a resident's care plans, income records and trust-fund postings are all addressable in their own right once created.

### 2.8. Cascade Discipline

Several operations in this system mutate more than one record, and the propagation is defined as an ordered chain of hops rather than as an unspecified "refresh". Each hop is a real mutation: the record it touches has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented, and the hop fires because a preceding hop changed an input it depends on. Per §2.4 those increments collapse — when two hops of one chain land on the same record, that record still advances by only 1 for the request — so the hop-by-hop narration below fixes *which* records move, not how many times each one's counter ticks.

The recurring chain runs from bed occupancy outward. Any change to a resident's `occupancy_status` or `care_unit_id` recomputes the affected unit's `occupied_bed_count` and `held_bed_count`, which recomputes `available_bed_count`, which recomputes `operational_posture`; a posture change can in turn revoke outstanding bed offers on that unit. A second chain runs from certification outward: a survey result that decertifies a unit forces that unit's posture to `FROZEN`, raises the parent home's `decertified_unit_count`, moves the home's `recognition_status` from `RECOGNIZED` to `SUSPENDED`, and voids the home's `DRAFT` per diem claims. §3 states each chain hop by hop; implementers must apply the whole chain within the request that triggered it, because this system's consistency model is immediate — there is no deferred reconciliation pass.

### 2.9. Beds, Rates, Cents and Rounding

Bed counts are integers and are never clamped. For a care unit, `occupied_bed_count` is the number of residents assigned to that unit whose `occupancy_status` is `ADMITTED`; `held_bed_count` is the number assigned to that unit whose `occupancy_status` is `HOSPITALIZED` and whose `bed_hold_days_used` is still strictly below the thirty-day bed-hold ceiling; and `available_bed_count` is the unit's `licensed_bed_count` less its `out_of_service_bed_count`, less `occupied_bed_count`, less `held_bed_count`. Residents whose `occupancy_status` is `DISCHARGED` are counted in neither occupancy tally. A hospitalized resident whose `bed_hold_days_used` has reached the thirty-day ceiling drops out of `held_bed_count`, which releases the bed to `available_bed_count` even though that resident's record has not yet been discharged. The four quantities `occupied_bed_count`, `held_bed_count`, `available_bed_count` and `out_of_service_bed_count` must always sum to exactly `licensed_bed_count`; an `available_bed_count` that has gone negative is a defect, not a clamped floor.

Every monetary amount in this system is an integer number of cents, and the API carries cents — never dollars, never a decimal fraction. Rates that are not whole percentages are expressed in basis points, where ten thousand basis points is one hundred percent; `countable_rate_bps` on an income record and `per_diem_rate_cents` on a care unit are both stored fields of this kind. Wherever a rate is applied to an amount, the product is divided by ten thousand and the quotient is rounded half up to a whole cent. Rounding happens only at the points §3 names explicitly, and the *order* of rounding is part of the specification: rounding a per-day figure once and then multiplying by a day count is not interchangeable with multiplying first and rounding the monthly total, and the two produce observably different cents. No monetary quantity in the trust-fund ledger is ever rounded, because every posting is already an integer cent amount.

The federal daily per diem rate is fixed by care level: 11,857 cents for `NURSING`, 5,502 cents for `DOMICILIARY` and 4,230 cents for `ADULT_DAY_HEALTH`. A care unit's `per_diem_rate_cents` is the rate for its own `care_level`, set at unit creation and never supplied by a client.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties

Two pairs of steps in this system must be performed by different operators, and the check compares `X-User-ID` against a user identifier the server recorded earlier. First, the `AdmissionsCoordinator` who records an admission decision must not be the `ClinicalAssessor` who authored the cited assessment: if the acting `X-User-ID` equals that assessment's `assessor_user_id`, the decision is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Second, the operator who reverses a trust-fund posting must not be the operator who recorded it: if the acting `X-User-ID` equals the target posting's `recorded_by_user_id`, the reversal is rejected with the same `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Both checks are evaluated after state preconditions and before the compliance gates described in §2.11.

Role separation reinforces the same intent structurally. A `ClinicalAssessor` cannot record an admission decision at all, and a `FederalPerDiemAuditor` can neither open, correct nor submit the claims it adjudicates.

### 2.11. The Validation Ladder

When several rules could reject the same request, the order in which they are evaluated is part of the contract, because the error the client receives must be deterministic. Every endpoint evaluates its applicable rules in the following sequence, and the first rule that fails is the one reported.

1. **Authentication.** Both headers present and `X-User-Role` one of the seven declared roles, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. **Authorization by role.** The acting role appears in the target endpoint's authorized-role list, else `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.
3. **Request shape.** Every required field present, every enum value inside its declared set, every numeric field inside its declared range, dates in `YYYY-MM-DD` form and months in `YYYY-MM` form, `licensed_bed_count` at least 1, `out_of_service_bed_count` no greater than `licensed_bed_count`, `amount_cents` strictly greater than zero, `adl_dependency_count` between 0 and 6 inclusive, `service_connected_disability_pct` between 0 and 100 inclusive, and pagination `limit` between 1 and 100 inclusive with `offset` at least zero. Any violation is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Three details of this rung are pinned. Types are checked strictly: an integer field rejects a boolean and rejects a numeric string, a string field rejects a number, and a boolean field rejects `0`, `1` and `"true"`. A field supplied explicitly as `null` is treated exactly as an absent field — required if the endpoint requires it, and otherwise falling back to the endpoint's declared default. And **unknown keys are ignored rather than rejected**: a body carrying fields this document does not name for the operation is accepted, the surplus keys having no effect. A wholly absent or zero-length body is read as the empty object, which then fails only on whatever required fields the endpoint declares — so a write with no required fields, such as a reversal, needs no body at all; a body that is present but is either unparseable JSON or parses to something other than a JSON object is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422). The request's `Content-Type` is never itself a ground for rejection: a JSON object is parsed from the body whatever it claims to be.
4. **Path existence,** resolved left to right as described in §2.7.
5. **Body foreign-key existence,** resolved in the pinned field order of §2.7.
6. **Owner scoping** for a `ResidentRepresentative`, else `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`.
7. **Version pinning** for the three writes of §2.6, else the matching stale-version rejection.
8. **State preconditions** on the record being moved — the applicant, assessment, resident, care plan, income record, claim, trust-fund posting or home recognition posture, each with its own rejection code as §3 describes.
9. **Segregation of duties,** else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
10. **Recognition and certification gates** — `ERR_HOME_NOT_RECOGNIZED` and `ERR_UNIT_NOT_CERTIFIED`.
11. **Operational posture gates** — `ERR_UNIT_POSTURE_BLOCKED`.
12. **Care-level agreement** — `ERR_CARE_LEVEL_MISMATCH`.
13. **Referential prerequisites** — `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_CARE_PLAN` and `ERR_NO_WAITLIST_ENTRY_FOR_UNIT`, then the transfer-target rule `ERR_INVALID_TRANSFER_TARGET`, then the income-verification rule `ERR_INCOME_NOT_VERIFIED`, then the uniqueness rules `ERR_DUPLICATE_WAITLIST_ENTRY`, `ERR_CLAIM_PERIOD_OVERLAP` and `ERR_CLAIM_MONTH_MISMATCH`, and then the single-attribution rule `ERR_CENSUS_DAY_ALREADY_CLAIMED`.
14. **Temporal rules** — `ERR_CLAIM_MONTH_NOT_CLOSED`, `ERR_BED_HOLD_OVERLAP` and `ERR_DATE_OUT_OF_RANGE`.
15. **Capacity rules** — `ERR_NO_AVAILABLE_BED` and `ERR_BED_HOLD_EXHAUSTED`, then the claim-census rule `ERR_CLAIM_CENSUS_EMPTY`.
16. **Fiscal rules** — `ERR_TRUST_FUND_INSUFFICIENT` and `ERR_PNA_RESERVE_BREACH`, evaluated last.

Two consequences of this ordering deserve emphasis, because they are easy to get backwards. A stale version is reported *before* any state or capacity check, so a coordinator holding a stale `expected_care_unit_version` learns that their view is out of date rather than that the bed is gone. And the fiscal guards sit at the very bottom, so a trust-fund posting on a discharged resident reports the resident-state violation rather than an insufficient balance.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Homes, Care Units and Bed Capacity

A `StateProgramAdmin` brings a facility into the system by registering it as a **veterans home**. Registration declares the home's legal `name` (between 1 and 200 characters, not blank), the `state_code` of the operating state (exactly two uppercase letters), and the `recognition_effective_date` — the calendar date on which the home's current federal standing took effect. Two further fields are optional. `recognition_status` declares the federal standing itself and may be `RECOGNIZED`, `PROVISIONAL` or `SUSPENDED`; when omitted it defaults to `RECOGNIZED`, and `PROVISIONAL` is supplied only for a home still awaiting federal recognition. `personal_needs_allowance_monthly_cents` sets the monthly personal needs allowance the home credits each resident and reserves from cost-share debits; it must not be negative and defaults to 9,000 cents, that is ninety dollars a month. A newly registered home starts with `active_resident_count` at 0, `decertified_unit_count` at 0, `recognition_suspended_reason` null — unless the home was registered directly as `SUSPENDED`, in which case the derived reason reads `UNIT_DECERTIFIED` from the moment of creation — `last_survey_date` null and `version` at 1.

Three of those fields are derived and never written by a client. `active_resident_count` is the number of residents in any of this home's units whose `occupancy_status` is either `ADMITTED` or `HOSPITALIZED`. `decertified_unit_count` is the number of this home's care units whose `certification_status` is `DECERTIFIED`. `recognition_suspended_reason` explains a suspension and takes only two values: it is `UNIT_DECERTIFIED` while the home is suspended and null whenever `recognition_status` is anything other than `SUSPENDED`. The home's own `last_survey_date` mirrors the `survey_date` of the survey most recently *recorded* against *any* of its units — each survey result overwrites it outright, even when the survey being recorded carries an earlier `survey_date` than the value already stored — and stays null until the first survey lands.

Federal standing is also movable by hand. A `StateProgramAdmin` supplies an `action`, an `effective_date` and an optional `note`. The `GRANT` action moves a home from `PROVISIONAL` to `RECOGNIZED` and is permitted only while `decertified_unit_count` is 0; the `SUSPEND` action moves a home from `RECOGNIZED` to `SUSPENDED`; the `RESTORE` action moves a home from `SUSPENDED` back to `RECOGNIZED` and, like `GRANT`, requires `decertified_unit_count` to be 0. In every case `recognition_effective_date` is set to the supplied `effective_date`. A `SUSPEND` sets `recognition_suspended_reason` to `UNIT_DECERTIFIED`; a `GRANT` or a `RESTORE` sets it back to null. Suspension is not merely a label: every per diem claim of that home whose status is `DRAFT` is voided in the same request, and each claim so voided releases the census days it had attributed exactly as hop 4 of §3.8 describes. Restoring recognition afterwards does **not** resurrect those voided claims — a fresh claim must be opened for the month. An action that does not match the home's current standing — a `GRANT` on a home that is not `PROVISIONAL`, a `SUSPEND` on a home that is not `RECOGNIZED`, a `RESTORE` on a home that is not `SUSPENDED` — is rejected with `ERR_HOME_RECOGNITION_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409), and so is a `RESTORE` attempted while at least one of the home's units is still decertified.

Within a home, the `StateProgramAdmin` creates **care units**. A unit declares its `unit_name` (between 1 and 120 characters, not blank), its `care_level` — one of `NURSING`, `DOMICILIARY` or `ADULT_DAY_HEALTH` — and its `licensed_bed_count`, which must be at least 1. `out_of_service_bed_count`, the number of beds physically unavailable for renovation or infection control, is optional, defaults to 0, must not be negative and must not exceed `licensed_bed_count`. The unit records the home it belongs to in `veterans_home_id`. On creation the unit's `certification_status` is `CERTIFIED`, its `occupied_bed_count`, `held_bed_count` and `waitlist_active_count` are all 0, its `last_survey_compliance_score` is 0, its `last_survey_date` is null, its `per_diem_rate_cents` is set from the federal rate schedule for its own `care_level`, and its `available_bed_count` and `operational_posture` are computed from the bed arithmetic of §2.9. Creating a unit is a mutation of its parent home, so the home's `version` increments.

`waitlist_active_count` on a unit is the number of waitlist entries on that unit whose status is either `ACTIVE` or `OFFERED`; entries in any other status do not count.

**Operational posture** is the single gate through which every operation touching a unit's beds must pass, and it is derived, never set. If the unit's `certification_status` is `DECERTIFIED`, `operational_posture` is `FROZEN`. Otherwise, if `certification_status` is `CONDITIONAL`, the posture is `RESTRICTED` — a conditional unit is always restricted regardless of how many beds are free. Otherwise, if `available_bed_count` is zero or less, the posture is `RESTRICTED`; note the boundary, which keys on "zero or fewer", so a unit with exactly zero free beds is already restricted. Only a certified unit with at least one free bed is `OPEN`.

The three postures gate different things. `FROZEN` blocks admission decisions, transfers into the unit, readmissions and new waitlist entries — though the last of those is tested against the unit's `certification_status` directly and is therefore refused with `ERR_UNIT_NOT_CERTIFIED` rather than as a posture rejection. `RESTRICTED` blocks admission decisions and blocks new bed offers, and it *permits* the readmission of a resident who is already holding a bed in that unit. Its effect on a transfer into the unit is split across two rungs and is worth stating exactly: the posture rung on a transfer refuses only a `FROZEN` target, so a certified unit left `RESTRICTED` merely because its beds are full clears that rung and is stopped one rung lower by the bed check, while a `CONDITIONAL` unit — always `RESTRICTED` — never reaches the posture rung at all, because the transfer's own certification gate demands a `certification_status` of exactly `CERTIFIED` and refuses anything else with `ERR_UNIT_NOT_CERTIFIED`. `OPEN` imposes no restriction. Where a posture blocks an operation, the rejection is `ERR_UNIT_POSTURE_BLOCKED` (HTTP 409): specifically, an admission decision requires the target unit to be `OPEN`, and a transfer in requires only that the target unit not be `FROZEN`.

### 3.2. Application Intake, Eligibility and Redetermination

An `AdmissionsCoordinator` files an **applicant** against a specific home, naming that home with `veterans_home_id`. The filing payload carries the applicant's `full_name` (between 1 and 200 characters, not blank) and `date_of_birth` in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, the service evidence — `service_days` (the total days of qualifying active military service, which must not be negative), `discharge_character` (one of `HONORABLE`, `GENERAL`, `OTHER_THAN_HONORABLE`, `BAD_CONDUCT` or `DISHONORABLE`), the boolean `wartime_service` recording whether any qualifying service fell inside a recognized wartime period, `service_connected_disability_pct` (the VA disability rating, between 0 and 100 inclusive) and the boolean `nursing_care_for_service_connected` recording whether nursing care is required specifically for that service-connected disability — the residency evidence `state_residency_years` (completed years of residency in the operating state, not negative), the `requested_care_level` the applicant is applying for (`NURSING`, `DOMICILIARY` or `ADULT_DAY_HEALTH`), and the `application_received_date`. That last date matters twice over: it starts the tenure clock that accrues waitlist points, and it is the tie-break when two applicants score identically. The named home's own `recognition_status` is not consulted at filing: an application may be filed against a `PROVISIONAL` or `SUSPENDED` home exactly as against a `RECOGNIZED` one.

A freshly filed applicant has `status` `SUBMITTED` — the full lifecycle is `SUBMITTED`, `ELIGIBLE`, `INELIGIBLE`, `WAITLISTED`, `ADMITTED` and `DENIED`, with `ADMITTED` and `DENIED` terminal. Its `declined_offer_count` and `redetermination_count` are both 0, its `eligibility_reason_code` is null, its `priority_class` is null, its `priority_score` is 0, and both `decided_by_user_id` and `decision_note` are null. Filing an applicant is a mutation of the parent home, so the home's `version` increments.

Eligibility is adjudicated by the coordinator in a determination step that takes **no decision field at all** — the server derives the outcome from three criteria applied to the stored evidence. The applicant is `ELIGIBLE` only when all three hold: the `discharge_character` is either `HONORABLE` or `GENERAL`; the applicant has at least 90 days of qualifying service, or else `nursing_care_for_service_connected` is true, which waives the day requirement entirely; and the applicant has completed at least one full year of state residency. When all three hold, `status` becomes `ELIGIBLE`, `eligibility_reason_code` is set to `ELIGIBLE`, and both `priority_class` and `priority_score` are recomputed as described in §3.3.

When at least one criterion fails, `status` becomes `INELIGIBLE`, `priority_class` is set back to null and `priority_score` back to 0, and `eligibility_reason_code` records the **first** failing criterion in a pinned order that does not depend on how many criteria failed: discharge character first, reported as `DISCHARGE_CHARACTER_DISQUALIFYING`; then service days, reported as `SERVICE_DAYS_INSUFFICIENT`; then residency, reported as `RESIDENCY_INSUFFICIENT`. An applicant with a dishonorable discharge, 10 days of service and no state residency therefore reports `DISCHARGE_CHARACTER_DISQUALIFYING` and nothing else. Both outcomes increment the applicant's `version` by 1.

An `INELIGIBLE` determination is not the end of the road. The determination request accepts three optional amended-evidence fields — `amended_service_days`, `amended_state_residency_years` and `amended_discharge_character` — plus an optional `review_note` that is stored on the applicant as `decision_note` — and that field is rewritten on every determination, so a determination omitting `review_note` writes `decision_note` back to null rather than leaving the previous note standing. Supplying at least one amended field against an `INELIGIBLE` applicant reopens the case: each supplied amended value overwrites the corresponding stored field (`service_days`, `state_residency_years`, `discharge_character` respectively), `redetermination_count` increases by 1, `status` returns to `SUBMITTED` and `eligibility_reason_code` is cleared to null, and the applicant's `version` increments. The very same request then immediately re-runs the three criteria and lands the applicant back on either `ELIGIBLE` or `INELIGIBLE`, which increments `version` a second time — so a single successful redetermination call advances the applicant's `version` by two. Consider an applicant refiled with corrected discharge paperwork: they pass through `INELIGIBLE` to `SUBMITTED` to `ELIGIBLE` inside one call, and their `redetermination_count` records that the loop ran.

The reopening loop is bounded. An `INELIGIBLE` applicant whose `redetermination_count` has already reached two may not be reopened again, and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_REDETERMINATION_LIMIT` (HTTP 409). A determination request against an `INELIGIBLE` applicant that carries none of the three amended fields has no evidence to re-adjudicate and is rejected with `ERR_APPLICANT_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409), as is any determination request against an applicant whose `status` is neither `SUBMITTED` nor `INELIGIBLE`. When both faults hold at once — an `INELIGIBLE` applicant already at the limit whose request carries no amended field — the missing-evidence `ERR_APPLICANT_STATE_INVALID` is the one reported, because the amended-evidence test is evaluated before the limit test. The same `ERR_APPLICANT_STATE_INVALID` guards the two later steps: waitlist placement requires the applicant to be `ELIGIBLE` or already `WAITLISTED`, and an admission decision requires the applicant to be `WAITLISTED`.

### 3.3. Waitlist Priority, Queue Order and Bed Offers

Every eligibility determination that lands on `ELIGIBLE` classifies the applicant into one of four priority classes, and the classification is evaluated top down with the **first** matching branch winning — a class-one applicant is never re-tested against the class-two predicate. An applicant whose VA `service_connected_disability_pct` is at least 70, or whose `nursing_care_for_service_connected` flag is true, is `CLASS_1`. Failing that, an applicant with `wartime_service` true who has completed at least five years of state residency is `CLASS_2`. Failing that, an applicant with at least two years of state residency is `CLASS_3`. Everyone else is `CLASS_4`. All three thresholds are inclusive lower bounds. Only the applicant's own service and residency fields participate; no other record is consulted. The result is stored as `priority_class`, and it is null before the first determination.

The applicant's `priority_score` is a weighted total rebuilt from scratch on every recomputation — it is never incremented in place. Five terms feed it. The **class base** contributes 1,000 points for `CLASS_1`, 700 for `CLASS_2`, 400 for `CLASS_3` and 100 for `CLASS_4`. The **disability term** contributes three points for each percentage point of `service_connected_disability_pct`, with the percentage first capped at 100. The **residency term** contributes five points for each completed year of `state_residency_years`, with the year count first capped at twenty years — so residency stops earning after two decades, at 100 points. The **acuity term** is drawn from at most one clinical assessment: the applicant's assessment with the greatest `created_at` among those whose status is `SUBMITTED` or `ACCEPTED`. Assessments still in `DRAFT`, and assessments that ended `REJECTED`, contribute nothing, and when no qualifying assessment exists the acuity term is exactly zero. When one does exist, it contributes twenty points for each activity of daily living in its `adl_dependency_count` (capped at six dependencies, hence at 120 points), a further forty points when its `cognitive_impairment` flag is true, and a *deduction* of thirty points when its `behavioral_risk_flag` is true — behavioural risk narrows the set of units that can safely take the applicant, so it lowers rather than raises the queue position. That per-assessment subtotal is also mirrored onto the assessment itself as `acuity_points`, and it is *not* floored at zero: an assessment with no ADL dependencies, no cognitive impairment and a behavioural risk flag carries `acuity_points` of −30 and contributes −30 to the score. The **tenure term** contributes one point per calendar day elapsed from the `application_received_date` to the current logical-clock date, with negative elapsed day counts treated as zero and the day count capped at 365 days, so tenure is worth at most one year's wait. Finally the **decline penalty** subtracts twenty-five points for each offer counted in `declined_offer_count`.

Those five terms are summed with the penalty subtracted, and then a floor is applied: `priority_score` is the greater of the class base and that raw total. The floor is what makes the penalty saturate — once an applicant has declined enough offers to push the raw total below their class base, further declines stop changing the score, though they continue to accumulate in `declined_offer_count` for the expiry rule below. Every term is an integer product, so no rounding occurs anywhere in the score.

An `AdmissionsCoordinator` places an applicant on a unit's queue by creating a **waitlist entry**, supplying `applicant_id` and `care_unit_id`. Three gates apply beyond the applicant-status check of §3.2. The applicant's `requested_care_level` must equal the unit's `care_level`, or the placement is rejected with `ERR_CARE_LEVEL_MISMATCH` (HTTP 409). The unit must not be `DECERTIFIED`, or the placement is rejected with `ERR_UNIT_NOT_CERTIFIED` (HTTP 409). And the applicant must not already hold an entry on that same unit whose status is `ACTIVE` or `OFFERED`, or the placement is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_WAITLIST_ENTRY` (HTTP 409) — note that an entry which has reached `ADMITTED`, `EXPIRED` or `CANCELLED` does not block a fresh placement.

A new entry starts with status `ACTIVE` — the queue lifecycle runs across `ACTIVE`, `OFFERED`, `ADMITTED`, `EXPIRED` and `CANCELLED`, of which the last three are terminal. Its `listed_at` is stamped from the logical clock, its `offer_expires_at` is null, and `priority_class_snapshot` and `rank_score_snapshot` are copied from the applicant's current `priority_class` and `priority_score`; both snapshots are re-read from the applicant at every subsequent queue recomputation rather than frozen forever, and re-read only while the entry is still in the candidate set — an entry that has reached `ADMITTED`, `EXPIRED` or `CANCELLED` keeps whichever snapshot values it last carried. Placement sets the applicant's `status` to `WAITLISTED`, increments the unit's `waitlist_active_count` by 1, increments both the unit's `version` and the applicant's `version`, and recomputes `queue_position` for every `ACTIVE` or `OFFERED` entry on that unit.

An applicant is not restricted to one queue. A `WAITLISTED` applicant may be placed on a *second* unit of the same requested care level, which creates a further entry with status `ACTIVE`, leaves the applicant's `status` at `WAITLISTED`, and increments `waitlist_active_count` and `version` on the newly joined unit only — the unit the applicant was already queued on is untouched, while the applicant's own `version` still increments even though their `status` did not move. Holding several live entries at once is what makes the cancellation cascade at admission time meaningful.

Queue order on a unit is fully pinned. The candidate set is every entry on that unit whose status is `ACTIVE` or `OFFERED`; those candidates are ranked by the owning applicant's `priority_score` descending, then by the applicant's `application_received_date` ascending, then by the entry's own `created_at` ascending, and `queue_position` is the one-based index in that ranking. The record `id` is never consulted as a tie-break; where two entries share all three keys — which the frozen logical clock makes ordinary, since entries created between two clock settings carry the identical `created_at` — the earlier-persisted entry ranks first, per the implicit insertion-order tie-break of §2.3. Entries in `ADMITTED`, `EXPIRED` or `CANCELLED` are removed from the candidate set and have `queue_position` forced to 0. The queue is recomputed whenever an entry is created, whenever any entry on the unit changes status, whenever an applicant's `priority_score` is recomputed, and during the nightly checkpoint. A recomputation rewrites `queue_position`, `priority_class_snapshot` and `rank_score_snapshot` in place *without* incrementing the rewritten entries' own `version`: within any request, only an entry whose own `status` moved is a versioned mutation, alongside the unit itself.

Bed offers are issued only by the nightly checkpoint, never by the operation that freed the bed. An `ACTIVE` entry moves to `OFFERED` when four conditions all hold: its unit's `operational_posture` is `OPEN`, that unit has at least one free bed in `available_bed_count`, the entry itself sits at `queue_position` 1 on that unit, and no other entry on that unit is already `OFFERED`. The transition stamps `offer_expires_at` at the current logical clock plus a seven-day offer window and increments the entry's `version`. When the candidate set for a unit is empty no offer is made, the checkpoint's `offered_waitlist_entry_count` simply stays at zero, and this is not an error.

An offer that is never taken up lapses. When the logical clock has moved strictly past an entry's `offer_expires_at`, that `OFFERED` entry reverts to `ACTIVE` with `offer_expires_at` cleared to null, the owning applicant's `declined_offer_count` increases by 1, the applicant's `priority_score` is recomputed — picking up the additional twenty-five-point penalty, still floored at the class base — every `ACTIVE` or `OFFERED` entry on that unit has its `queue_position` recomputed, and the entry's `version` increments. The applicant keeps their place in line, but at a penalised score. Note carefully that this decline cycle is `ACTIVE → OFFERED → ACTIVE` and is the *only* path that raises `declined_offer_count`: when an offer is instead revoked because the unit's posture tightened, the applicant's `declined_offer_count` is left exactly as it was, because a posture revocation is not a decline.

Persistent declining ends the applicant's place in every queue. Within the same checkpoint pass, after offers have been declined, any `ACTIVE` entry whose applicant has reached three declined offers moves to `EXPIRED` with `queue_position` set to 0, its unit's `waitlist_active_count` decremented by 1 and that unit's `version` incremented.

### 3.4. Clinical Assessment and the Admission Decision

A `ClinicalAssessor` records a **clinical assessment** against an applicant, and the applicant's own identifier is carried on the assessment as `applicant_id`. The assessment declares the `assessed_care_level` the clinician concludes the applicant needs (`NURSING`, `DOMICILIARY` or `ADULT_DAY_HEALTH`), the `adl_dependency_count` of activities of daily living the applicant depends on assistance for (an integer from 0 through 6 inclusive), the booleans `cognitive_impairment` and `behavioral_risk_flag`, the documented `skilled_nursing_hours_per_day` (at least 0, no more than 24, and carrying at most two decimal places), and the `assessment_date` on which the assessment was performed. A new assessment is `DRAFT` — the lifecycle runs `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `ACCEPTED` and `REJECTED`, with the last two terminal — its `assessor_user_id` is taken from the acting `X-User-ID`, its `acuity_points` are computed from the ADL, cognitive and behavioural weights of §3.3, and its `rejection_reason` is null. Recording an assessment increments the parent applicant's `version`, and it imposes no precondition on that applicant's `status`: a `DRAFT` may be recorded against a `SUBMITTED`, `INELIGIBLE`, `ADMITTED` or `DENIED` applicant just as against an `ELIGIBLE` or `WAITLISTED` one, because the status gate falls at submission rather than at creation.

Submitting the assessment is what makes it citable by a decision, and any `ClinicalAssessor` may submit a draft — the acting `X-User-ID` is not compared with the assessment's `assessor_user_id` here. Submission requires the assessment to be `DRAFT`, and an assessment in any other status is rejected with `ERR_ASSESSMENT_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409); it requires the owning applicant to be `ELIGIBLE` or `WAITLISTED`, and an applicant outside those two statuses is rejected with `ERR_APPLICANT_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409); and when the `assessed_care_level` is `NURSING`, it requires `skilled_nursing_hours_per_day` of at least 2.00, since a nursing placement that documents less than two skilled hours a day contradicts its own care level and is rejected with `ERR_CARE_LEVEL_MISMATCH` (HTTP 409). On success the assessment becomes `SUBMITTED`, its `acuity_points` are recomputed, and the applicant's `priority_score` is recomputed — the acuity term becomes non-zero for the first time — which re-orders every queue the applicant sits in, refreshes `rank_score_snapshot` on each of those entries, increments the applicant's `version`, increments the `version` of every care unit on which the applicant holds an `ACTIVE` or `OFFERED` entry — whether or not the recomputation actually moved a position on it — and increments the assessment's own `version`.

The **admission decision** is the fork on which the whole intake pipeline turns. An `AdmissionsCoordinator` supplies a `decision` of either `APPROVED` or `DENIED`, the `care_unit_id` of the unit the decision is made against, the `clinical_assessment_id` of the assessment being cited, the `expected_care_unit_version` demanded by §2.6, the `admission_date` of physical admission — required on both branches, even though a `DENIED` decision never uses it — and an optional `decision_note`. Both branches record `decision_note` on the applicant, writing null when the field is omitted, and set the applicant's `decided_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`, and both branches respond with HTTP 201.

Before either branch runs, the decision passes a long gate sequence in the ladder order of §2.11: `expected_care_unit_version` must equal the target unit's current `version`, else `ERR_CARE_UNIT_VERSION_STALE`; the applicant must be `WAITLISTED`; the cited assessment must belong to this applicant and be `SUBMITTED`, else `ERR_ASSESSMENT_STATE_INVALID`; the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from that assessment's `assessor_user_id`, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; the target unit's `certification_status` must be exactly `CERTIFIED`, else `ERR_UNIT_NOT_CERTIFIED`; the target unit's `operational_posture` must be `OPEN`, else `ERR_UNIT_POSTURE_BLOCKED`; the cited assessment's `assessed_care_level` must equal the target unit's `care_level`, else `ERR_CARE_LEVEL_MISMATCH`; the applicant must hold an entry on that very unit whose status is `ACTIVE` or `OFFERED`, else `ERR_NO_WAITLIST_ENTRY_FOR_UNIT` (HTTP 409); and the unit must have at least one free bed in `available_bed_count`, else `ERR_NO_AVAILABLE_BED` (HTTP 409).

On `APPROVED`, the applicant's `status` becomes `ADMITTED`, the cited assessment's `status` becomes `ACCEPTED`, and a **resident** record is created. The new resident carries `applicant_id` naming the admitted application and `care_unit_id` naming the unit, `admission_date` copied from the payload, `occupancy_status` `ADMITTED`, `billing_status` `PROVISIONAL_COST_SHARE`, `current_care_level` stamped from the admitting unit's `care_level`, `bed_hold_days_used` 0, `bed_hold_active` false, `bed_hold_started_on` null, `last_hospitalization_end_date` null, `hospitalization_count` 0, `readmission_count` 0, `monthly_cost_share_cents` 0, `trust_fund_balance_cents` 0, `last_census_date` null, and both `discharge_date` and `discharge_reason` null. No care plan is created here: care planning is an explicit follow-up step, and the resident deliberately begins life with no plan at all.

The approval's cascade then runs in four ordered hops, all of them within the same request. First, the applicant's entry on the admitting unit moves to `ADMITTED` with `queue_position` 0 and that unit's `waitlist_active_count` decremented by 1, while every *other* entry of this applicant on other units whose status was `ACTIVE` or `OFFERED` moves to `CANCELLED` with `offer_expires_at` cleared, `queue_position` 0, each of those units' `waitlist_active_count` decremented by 1 and each of those units' `version` incremented. Second, the admitting unit's `occupied_bed_count` increases by 1, which drops its `available_bed_count` by 1, and the unit's `version` increments; the parent home's `active_resident_count` increases by 1 and the home's `version` increments. Third, if that drop brought `available_bed_count` to zero, the unit's `operational_posture` is recomputed from `OPEN` to `RESTRICTED`. Fourth — and this is the hop most easily missed — because the posture became `RESTRICTED`, every entry on that unit still sitting at `OFFERED` reverts to `ACTIVE` with `offer_expires_at` set to null, and the owning applicants' `declined_offer_count` values are left unchanged. Finally the remaining `ACTIVE` and `OFFERED` entries on the unit have `queue_position` recomputed. The response returns the created resident.

On `DENIED`, the divergent branch runs: the applicant's `status` becomes `DENIED`, the cited assessment's `status` becomes `REJECTED` with `rejection_reason` set to `ADMISSION_DENIED`, and *every* entry of this applicant whose status was `ACTIVE` or `OFFERED` — on the decided unit and on all others alike — moves to `CANCELLED` with `offer_expires_at` cleared and `queue_position` 0, each affected unit's `waitlist_active_count` decremented by 1 and each affected unit's `version` incremented. No resident is created, no bed is consumed, and no bed count on any unit changes. The response's `data` is null on this branch.

### 3.5. Care Planning and Level-of-Care Transfers

A resident's care is governed by a **care plan** authored by a `ClinicalAssessor`. The plan names the resident it governs through `resident_id`, prescribes a `care_level` (`NURSING`, `DOMICILIARY` or `ADULT_DAY_HEALTH`) and an `adl_support_level` of `LOW`, `MODERATE` or `HIGH`, and records the `plan_date` on which it was authored. A revision additionally supplies `supersedes_care_plan_id`, naming the plan this one will replace when it is activated; the field is omitted for a resident's first plan. Authoring a plan for a resident whose `occupancy_status` is `DISCHARGED` is rejected with `ERR_RESIDENT_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409), a `plan_date` in the future relative to the logical clock is rejected with `ERR_DATE_OUT_OF_RANGE` (HTTP 409), and a `supersedes_care_plan_id` that names a plan whose status is not `ACTIVE` or `REVIEW_DUE` is rejected with `ERR_CARE_PLAN_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). At authoring time that status is the only thing tested about the cited plan: it is not yet required to belong to this same resident, a check deferred to activation.

A new plan is `DRAFT` — the plan lifecycle runs `DRAFT`, `ACTIVE`, `REVIEW_DUE`, `SUPERSEDED` and `VOID`, the last two terminal — with `authored_by_user_id` taken from the acting `X-User-ID`, `activated_at` null, `review_due_date` null, and `revision_number` set to one more than the superseded plan's `revision_number`, or to 1 when `supersedes_care_plan_id` is absent. Authoring a plan increments the parent resident's `version`.

Activation is what makes a plan governing, and it serves two distinct cases. Activating a `DRAFT` requires the plan to be `DRAFT`, the resident not to be `DISCHARGED`, and one of two mutually exclusive conditions on succession: either `supersedes_care_plan_id` is present and names a plan belonging to this same resident whose status is `ACTIVE` or `REVIEW_DUE`, or `supersedes_care_plan_id` is absent and the resident holds no plan at all whose status is `ACTIVE` or `REVIEW_DUE`. In other words a resident may never carry two governing plans, and a first plan may not be activated over an existing one. On success the plan's `status` becomes `ACTIVE`, `activated_at` is stamped from the logical clock, `review_due_date` is stamped as the plan's own `plan_date` plus a ninety-day review cycle, `revision_number` is set to one more than the superseded plan's `revision_number` (or 1 when there is no predecessor), the superseded plan — when one was named — moves to `SUPERSEDED`, the `version` of both plans increments, and the resident's `current_care_level` is re-stamped from this plan's `care_level`, incrementing the resident's `version`.

The second case is the review cycle. The nightly checkpoint flags any `ACTIVE` plan whose `review_due_date` falls strictly earlier than the checkpoint's `through_date` as `REVIEW_DUE`, incrementing that plan's `version`. Activating that same plan again returns it to `ACTIVE` and re-stamps `review_due_date` — but this time as the **current logical-clock date** plus the ninety-day cycle, not as `plan_date` plus ninety days, because the clinician has just re-reviewed a plan that may be months old. `activated_at` is re-stamped from the logical clock on this path too, so it always names the most recent activation rather than the first one, and the resident's `current_care_level` is likewise re-stamped from the plan; the succession conditions above are *not* re-tested, since a re-activation names no new predecessor and the plan's own `supersedes_care_plan_id` has already been consumed. `revision_number` does not change on a re-activation, because no new plan was authored: the cycle `ACTIVE → REVIEW_DUE → ACTIVE` runs entirely on one row. Attempting to activate a plan whose status is neither `DRAFT` nor `REVIEW_DUE` is rejected with `ERR_CARE_PLAN_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409), and activating any plan for a `DISCHARGED` resident is rejected with `ERR_RESIDENT_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409).

A plan can also leave the governing set without a clinician touching it. Any plan that is `ACTIVE` or `REVIEW_DUE` moves to `SUPERSEDED` when a later plan citing it through `supersedes_care_plan_id` is activated, and also when the resident is discharged. A plan still sitting in `DRAFT` when the resident is discharged moves instead to `VOID`. Each of those system-driven moves increments the affected plan's `version`.

**Level-of-care changes** are executed as a transfer between units of the same home, and the plan is the clinical authority for the move. An `AdmissionsCoordinator` supplies the `target_care_unit_id`, the `effective_date` of the move, and an optional `transfer_note`. The resident's `occupancy_status` must be `ADMITTED` — a hospitalized resident cannot be transferred — else `ERR_RESIDENT_STATE_INVALID`. The target unit's `certification_status` must be exactly `CERTIFIED`, else `ERR_UNIT_NOT_CERTIFIED`; its `operational_posture` must not be `FROZEN`, else `ERR_UNIT_POSTURE_BLOCKED`; note that unlike at admission the posture rung does not demand `OPEN`, so a certified unit left `RESTRICTED` by full beds passes it and is stopped only by the bed check below, while a `CONDITIONAL` target has already been refused as uncertified. When the resident holds an `ACTIVE` plan, that plan's `care_level` must equal the target unit's `care_level`, else `ERR_CARE_LEVEL_MISMATCH`; the separate case of a resident holding *no* `ACTIVE` plan at all is checked one rung later and is rejected with `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_CARE_PLAN` (HTTP 409). Only a plan whose status is exactly `ACTIVE` satisfies this requirement — one the checkpoint has flagged `REVIEW_DUE` does not, so a resident whose sole governing plan is `REVIEW_DUE` is refused with `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_CARE_PLAN` until the clinician re-activates it. The `target_care_unit_id` must differ from the resident's current `care_unit_id` and must belong to the same `veterans_home_id`, else `ERR_INVALID_TRANSFER_TARGET` (HTTP 409). Finally the target unit must have at least one free bed in `available_bed_count`, else `ERR_NO_AVAILABLE_BED`.

A transfer leaves the resident's `occupancy_status` at `ADMITTED` — the label does not move — while the bed accounting migrates between two units. The resident's `care_unit_id` becomes the target and `current_care_level` is re-stamped from the target unit's `care_level`. The source unit's `occupied_bed_count` falls by 1 and the target unit's rises by 1; `available_bed_count` and `operational_posture` are recomputed on **both** units; `queue_position` is recomputed for the `ACTIVE` and `OFFERED` entries on both units; both units' `version` values increment, as does the resident's. The cross-unit total of occupied plus held beds is conserved exactly. A transfer revokes no bed offer: even where the target unit's `available_bed_count` falling to zero tightens its posture to `RESTRICTED`, an entry already sitting at `OFFERED` on either unit keeps that status and keeps its `offer_expires_at` — unlike the admission cascade, this move carries no offer-revocation hop. The parent home is not touched either, since `active_resident_count` cannot move within one home. Census days already generated are left alone: each keeps its original `care_unit_id`, its original `payable_rate_cents` and its existing claim attribution, and only rows generated by a *later* checkpoint carry the target unit. Because of that, an operator who wants a clean unit-month split should run the checkpoint immediately before the transfer as well as after it.

### 3.6. Hospitalization Bed-Holds, Readmission and Discharge

Occupancy and billing advance on two independent dimensions of the same resident row. The occupancy dimension moves `ADMITTED` to `HOSPITALIZED` and back, and eventually to the terminal `DISCHARGED`; the billing dimension moves `PROVISIONAL_COST_SHARE` to `FINAL_COST_SHARE` and eventually to `CLOSED`. Neither dimension constrains the other except where this document says so explicitly: a hospitalized resident's finances continue to be determinable, and a change of `billing_status` never moves a bed.

When an `ADMITTED` resident is hospitalized, either an `AdmissionsCoordinator` or a `ClinicalAssessor` opens a **bed hold** by supplying the `hospitalization_start_date` and an optional `facility_note`. The start date must not fall before the resident's `admission_date`, else `ERR_DATE_OUT_OF_RANGE`. Bed-hold intervals must not overlap: when the resident has a prior hospitalization on record, `hospitalization_start_date` must fall strictly after `last_hospitalization_end_date`, else `ERR_BED_HOLD_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). Those two date rules share one rung of the ladder and their order between themselves is pinned: the `admission_date` rule is evaluated first, so a start date that both precedes `admission_date` and collides with a prior closed hold reports `ERR_DATE_OUT_OF_RANGE` rather than `ERR_BED_HOLD_OVERLAP`. No rule forbids a `hospitalization_start_date` in the future relative to the logical clock. Intervals are half-open — a hold runs from its start date up to but not including the readmission date — so a new hold starting the day after a prior readmission is legal zero-gap adjacency, while a new hold starting *on* the prior readmission date overlaps and is refused. And the resident's `bed_hold_days_used` must still be strictly below the thirty-day bed-hold ceiling, else `ERR_BED_HOLD_EXHAUSTED` (HTTP 409).

Opening the hold sets `occupancy_status` to `HOSPITALIZED`, stamps `bed_hold_started_on` with the supplied `hospitalization_start_date`, increases `hospitalization_count` by 1, and sets `bed_hold_active` to true — `bed_hold_active` being true exactly when the resident is `HOSPITALIZED` and `bed_hold_days_used` is still below the thirty-day ceiling. The unit-side cascade is a compensating pair: `occupied_bed_count` falls by 1 while `held_bed_count` rises by 1, so `available_bed_count` does not move at all, and the unit's `version` increments alongside the resident's. `billing_status` is untouched.

Readmission closes the interval. Either of the same two roles supplies the `readmission_date`, which must fall strictly after `bed_hold_started_on`, else `ERR_DATE_OUT_OF_RANGE`. The closed interval's length in whole days, added to `bed_hold_days_used`, must not exceed thirty, else `ERR_BED_HOLD_EXHAUSTED`. The unit must not be `DECERTIFIED`, else `ERR_UNIT_NOT_CERTIFIED`; a `RESTRICTED` unit will still take back a resident who is already holding one of its beds. On success `occupancy_status` returns to `ADMITTED`, `last_hospitalization_end_date` is set to the `readmission_date`, `bed_hold_days_used` increases by the number of days between `bed_hold_started_on` and the `readmission_date`, `readmission_count` increases by 1, `bed_hold_active` becomes false, and the compensating pair reverses — `held_bed_count` falls by 1 and `occupied_bed_count` rises by 1, again leaving `available_bed_count` unchanged — with both the unit's and the resident's `version` incrementing.

One subtlety is load-bearing for census reporting: `bed_hold_started_on` is **not** cleared at readmission, and it is not cleared at discharge either. It always names the start of the *most recent* hold, so the closed interval running from `bed_hold_started_on` up to `last_hospitalization_end_date` stays replayable by a later checkpoint that has not yet materialised those days. Implementations must therefore never test the nullness of `bed_hold_started_on` to decide whether a hold is currently open; the open-hold test is `occupancy_status` being `HOSPITALIZED`, or equivalently `bed_hold_active` being true. `bed_hold_started_on` is null only before the resident's very first hospitalization, and `last_hospitalization_end_date` is null only before the resident's first readmission.

**Discharge** is recorded by an `AdmissionsCoordinator` with a `discharge_date` and a `discharge_reason` drawn from `DEATH`, `TRANSFER_OUT`, `VOLUNTARY`, `ADMINISTRATIVE` and `BED_HOLD_EXPIRED`; the field is null on a resident who has not been discharged. A resident may be discharged from `ADMITTED` or from `HOSPITALIZED`, and the date rule differs between the two: from `ADMITTED` the `discharge_date` must not fall before `admission_date`, while from `HOSPITALIZED` it must not fall before `bed_hold_started_on`. Either violation is `ERR_DATE_OUT_OF_RANGE`. A discharge from `ADMITTED` may not cite `BED_HOLD_EXPIRED` — that reason is reserved for a resident whose hold ran out while hospitalized — and such a request is rejected with `ERR_RESIDENT_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409), as is any discharge attempted on a resident who is already `DISCHARGED`.

Discharge sets `occupancy_status` to `DISCHARGED`, stores `discharge_date` and `discharge_reason`, sets `billing_status` to `CLOSED`, and sets `bed_hold_active` to false while leaving `bed_hold_started_on` in place. Every one of the resident's plans still in `DRAFT` moves to `VOID`, and every plan that was `ACTIVE` or `REVIEW_DUE` moves to `SUPERSEDED`. The unit-side cascade then runs three hops: whichever count the resident occupied falls by 1 — `occupied_bed_count` when the resident was `ADMITTED`, `held_bed_count` when the resident was `HOSPITALIZED` — and the unit's `version` increments; because that count fell, `available_bed_count` rises by 1; and because `available_bed_count` is now at least 1, the unit's `operational_posture` is recomputed toward `OPEN`, which it reaches when the unit's `certification_status` is `CERTIFIED`. The parent home's `active_resident_count` falls by 1 and the home's `version` increments, and the resident's own `version` increments. Crucially, discharge itself makes **no** bed offer: the freed bed is offered only by the offer step of the next nightly checkpoint, which is precisely why the posture recomputation to `OPEN` matters — an offer requires an `OPEN` unit.

Once a resident is `DISCHARGED`, the record is closed to further activity: *recording* income records, cost-share determinations, care plans and trust-fund postings is refused with `ERR_RESIDENT_STATE_INVALID`, as are hospitalization, readmission, transfer and care-plan activation. Two steps that act on a row already recorded stay deliberately open, because closing them would leave the ledger and the income file unfinishable: verifying an income record and reversing a trust-fund posting consult the resident's `occupancy_status` not at all.

### 3.7. Income, Cost Share and the Resident Trust Fund

Resident finances are the responsibility of the `BusinessOfficeManager`, and they rest on three record types: declared income, an immutable monthly cost-share determination, and a trust-fund ledger.

An **income record** declares one income stream for one month. It names the resident through `resident_id`, declares the `income_type` — `SSA`, `VA_PENSION`, `VA_COMPENSATION`, `MILITARY_RETIREMENT` or `OTHER` — the gross `monthly_amount_cents` from that source (which must not be negative), and the `effective_month` in `YYYY-MM` form to which the amount applies. A cost-share determination only ever reads income records whose `effective_month` equals the determination's own month, so a resident's January income has no bearing on a February determination.

The income type fixes how much of the gross amount counts. `countable_rate_bps` is set from the countable-rate schedule, in basis points of the gross: 10,000 basis points — the whole amount — for `SSA`, `MILITARY_RETIREMENT` and `OTHER`; 8,000 basis points, that is eighty percent, for `VA_COMPENSATION`; and 0 basis points for `VA_PENSION`, which therefore never contributes to cost share at all. From it, `countable_amount_cents` is computed as the gross `monthly_amount_cents` multiplied by `countable_rate_bps`, divided by ten thousand, and rounded half up to a whole cent. A new record's `verification_status` is `UNVERIFIED` and its `verified_by_user_id` is null; recording it increments the parent resident's `version`.

Verification is a separate step and its `decision` is either `VERIFIED` or `REJECTED`. Only a record whose `verification_status` is still `UNVERIFIED` may be decided; a second attempt on an already-decided record is rejected with `ERR_INCOME_RECORD_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). The decision is written to `verification_status`, `verified_by_user_id` is taken from the acting `X-User-ID`, the record's own `version` increments and so does the parent resident's. The distinction matters downstream: a provisional cost share counts records that are `UNVERIFIED` **or** `VERIFIED`, while a final cost share counts `VERIFIED` records only, and a `REJECTED` record is never counted by either. Verification imposes no precondition on the resident: a record belonging to a `DISCHARGED` resident may still be verified or rejected, since the closure rule of §3.6 bites on recording a new record rather than on deciding one already recorded.

A **cost-share determination** is computed for one resident and one month, in one of two variants, and is immutable once written. The request declares the `variant` (`PROVISIONAL` or `FINAL`), the `effective_month` the determination governs, the `expected_resident_version` demanded by §2.6, and optionally `declared_medical_expense_cents` — the medical expenses the resident claims for the month, defaulting to 0 and never negative. The acting operator is recorded as `determined_by_user_id`. The determination is computed as a strict ten-step waterfall, and every step is pinned:

1. **Gross countable income.** For each of the resident's income records whose `effective_month` equals the determination's `effective_month` and whose `verification_status` lies in the variant's included set, take that record's countable contribution — its `monthly_amount_cents` multiplied by its `countable_rate_bps`, divided by ten thousand, **rounded half up per record** — and add the rounded contributions together. The total is `gross_countable_income_cents`. The rounding order here is part of the specification: each record is rounded before the sum, never the other way round.
2. **Personal needs allowance.** `pna_deduction_cents` is the `personal_needs_allowance_monthly_cents` of the home that operates the resident's unit.
3. **Net before the medical deduction** is `gross_countable_income_cents` less `pna_deduction_cents`, floored at zero.
4. **Tier selection.** `income_tier` is chosen from the net figure of step 3 — that is, from net income **before** any medical deduction. It is `TIER_1` when that figure is 100,000 cents or less, `TIER_2` when it is above 100,000 cents and at most 250,000 cents, and `TIER_3` when it is above 250,000 cents. This single pin is what resolves what would otherwise be circular: the medical cap depends on the tier, and the tier would otherwise depend on the post-deduction net. The tier is chosen once and is never iterated to a fixed point.
5. **Allowed medical deduction.** For the `PROVISIONAL` variant, `allowed_medical_deduction_cents` is 0 — a provisional determination deducts no medical expense whatsoever. For the `FINAL` variant it is the lesser of `declared_medical_expense_cents` and the cap for the tier chosen in step 4: 5,000 cents for `TIER_1`, 15,000 cents for `TIER_2` and 30,000 cents for `TIER_3`.
6. **Net after the medical deduction** is the step-3 figure less `allowed_medical_deduction_cents`, floored at zero.
7. **Marginal bands.** The step-6 figure is split into three marginal bands: the first band is that figure capped at 100,000 cents; the second band is the amount by which it exceeds 100,000 cents, floored at zero and then capped at 150,000 cents; the third band is the amount by which it exceeds 250,000 cents, floored at zero. `tiered_share_cents` is the sum of three separately rounded products: the first band at 4,000 basis points (forty percent), the second at 5,500 basis points (fifty-five percent) and the third at 7,000 basis points (seventy percent), where each band's product is divided by ten thousand and **rounded half up before the three are added**.
8. **Resident day count and the federal offset.** `resident_day_count` is derived differently by variant. For `PROVISIONAL` it is a pure calendar count: the number of days from the later of the resident's `admission_date` and the first day of `effective_month`, through the last day of `effective_month`, both bounds inclusive, and floored at zero — a determination requested for a month wholly earlier than the admission counts no days at all and so prorates to a cost share of zero. For `FINAL` it is the number of the resident's census days classified `RESIDENT_DAY` whose `service_date` falls inside `effective_month`. `federal_per_diem_offset_cents` is 0 for `PROVISIONAL`. For `FINAL` it is the lesser of two separately rounded quantities: the federal daily per diem rate for the resident's `current_care_level`, multiplied by `resident_day_count`, multiplied by 500 basis points (five percent), divided by ten thousand and rounded half up; and `tiered_share_cents` multiplied by 3,000 basis points (thirty percent), divided by ten thousand and rounded half up. The second quantity is the cap that keeps the offset from swallowing the whole cost share.
9. **Proration** applies to the `PROVISIONAL` variant only, and it is folded into step 10.
10. **The monthly cost share.** For `PROVISIONAL`, `monthly_cost_share_cents` is `tiered_share_cents` less `federal_per_diem_offset_cents` (which is zero), multiplied by `resident_day_count`, divided by the number of calendar days in `effective_month`, rounded half up, and finally floored at zero. For `FINAL`, it is simply `tiered_share_cents` less `federal_per_diem_offset_cents`, floored at zero, with no proration at all.

`delta_from_prior_cents` records the change this determination introduces: this determination's `monthly_cost_share_cents` less the `monthly_cost_share_cents` of the prior governing determination for the same resident and the same `effective_month`, or 0 when this is the first determination for that resident-month. The upshot of the variant split is that identical income records and an identical home allowance yield different `monthly_cost_share_cents`, because the two variants disagree on four things at once: which verification statuses count, whether medical expense is deducted, where the day count comes from, and whether the result is prorated.

Which determination *governs* a month is also pinned, and a resident may accumulate several for the same month. Among the resident's determinations for a target month, if none exists the governing determination is nothing at all and the resident's `monthly_cost_share_cents` is 0. If at least one `FINAL` determination exists, the governing one is the `FINAL` row with the greatest `created_at`. Otherwise it is the `PROVISIONAL` row with the greatest `created_at`. `FINAL` always outranks `PROVISIONAL` regardless of which was created later, and the record `id` is never consulted. For the value mirrored onto the resident as `monthly_cost_share_cents`, the target month is the calendar month of the current logical clock; if that month carries no determination at all, the governing row of the most recent *earlier* month is used instead, and only when the resident has no determination for any month is the mirrored value 0. An empty candidate set is a zero cost share, never an error.

The billing dimension advances off the back of these determinations. A resident whose `billing_status` is `PROVISIONAL_COST_SHARE` moves to `FINAL_COST_SHARE` the first time a `FINAL` determination is created, provided `expected_resident_version` matches, the resident is not `DISCHARGED`, and at least one of the resident's income records for that `effective_month` is `VERIFIED`; a `FINAL` determination requested for a month with no verified income is rejected with `ERR_INCOME_NOT_VERIFIED` (HTTP 409). A resident already in `FINAL_COST_SHARE` may receive further `FINAL` determinations for the same or a later `effective_month`, each of which stays in `FINAL_COST_SHARE`, supersedes the prior governing row for that month and records its `delta_from_prior_cents`. Note that this dimension moves independently of occupancy: a `HOSPITALIZED` resident can be moved to `FINAL_COST_SHARE` exactly as an `ADMITTED` one can. A `PROVISIONAL` determination leaves `billing_status` where it is. Every determination refreshes the resident's mirrored `monthly_cost_share_cents` and increments the resident's `version`.

The **trust fund** is a resident-scoped ledger with no soft delete and no rounding anywhere. A posting declares its `txn_type`, its `amount_cents` — an absolute magnitude that must be strictly greater than zero — its `effective_date`, and an optional `memo`. Four types may be posted directly: `DEPOSIT` and `PNA_CREDIT` are positive, `WITHDRAWAL` and `COST_SHARE_DEBIT` are negative. A fifth type, `REVERSAL`, may never be supplied by a client and exists only as the product of the reversal operation described below. `signed_amount_cents` is therefore the `amount_cents` itself for a `DEPOSIT` or `PNA_CREDIT`, the negation of `amount_cents` for a `WITHDRAWAL` or `COST_SHARE_DEBIT`, and, for a `REVERSAL`, the negation of the reversed row's own `signed_amount_cents`. The acting operator is recorded as `recorded_by_user_id`.

The ledger is replayed, never accumulated: rows are ordered by `effective_date` ascending and then by `created_at` ascending — the record `id` is never a sort key — and each row's `balance_after_cents` is the sum of `signed_amount_cents` over every row at or before it in that order. The resident's `trust_fund_balance_cents` is the `balance_after_cents` of the last row in that order, or 0 when the resident has no postings. As a standing invariant, `trust_fund_balance_cents` must always equal the sum of `signed_amount_cents` across all of the resident's rows, including rows that have been reversed — a reversed row stays in the ledger and keeps its own `signed_amount_cents`.

Two fiscal guards are evaluated against the balance the new row *would* produce, and they are the last checks any posting faces. A `WITHDRAWAL` that would drive the balance below zero is rejected with `ERR_TRUST_FUND_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 409). A `COST_SHARE_DEBIT` that would drive the balance below the home's `personal_needs_allowance_monthly_cents` reserve is rejected with `ERR_PNA_RESERVE_BREACH` (HTTP 409) — the personal needs allowance is money the resident keeps, and cost share may not reach it. `DEPOSIT`, `PNA_CREDIT` and `REVERSAL` are never rejected on balance grounds. An `effective_date` in the future relative to the logical clock is rejected with `ERR_DATE_OUT_OF_RANGE`, and any posting against a `DISCHARGED` resident is rejected with `ERR_RESIDENT_STATE_INVALID`.

A posting is undone by a compensating **reversal** rather than by deletion. Reversal takes only an optional `memo` and creates a new row whose `txn_type` is `REVERSAL`, whose `reverses_transaction_id` names the original, whose `amount_cents` equals the magnitude of the original's `signed_amount_cents`, whose `signed_amount_cents` is that original's `signed_amount_cents` negated, and whose `effective_date` is copied from the original. The original's `reversed` flag becomes true — `reversed` being a derived flag, true exactly when some other row names this row through `reverses_transaction_id`. The new row's `balance_after_cents` is computed by the same replay, the resident's `trust_fund_balance_cents` is refreshed to the resulting balance, and the resident's `version` increments; posting a row and then reversing it returns the resident's balance to exactly its pre-posting value. A row may be reversed only once and a `REVERSAL` may not itself be reversed: an attempt on a row whose `reversed` flag is already true, or on a row whose `txn_type` is `REVERSAL`, is rejected with `ERR_TRANSACTION_ALREADY_REVERSED` (HTTP 409). Reversal imposes no precondition on the resident and no date rule of its own: a posting belonging to a `DISCHARGED` resident may still be reversed, and because the compensating row copies the original's `effective_date` rather than accepting a new one, no future-date check can arise. And by §2.10, the reversing operator must not be the operator recorded in the original's `recorded_by_user_id`.

### 3.8. Survey, Certification and Federal Recognition

Certification is the state's judgement on a single care unit, and only the `SurveyAgency` role may record it. A survey outcome carries the `survey_date` on which the survey concluded and a `deficiencies` array, which is itself a required field: an absent or null `deficiencies`, a value that is not an array, or an element that is not an object or that omits either of its two fields, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Each element of that array is an object with two fields: a `scope` of `ISOLATED`, `PATTERN` or `WIDESPREAD`, and a `severity` of `MINIMAL`, `MINIMAL_HARM`, `ACTUAL_HARM` or `IMMEDIATE_JEOPARDY`. An empty `deficiencies` array is entirely legal and represents a clean survey.

The unit's `last_survey_compliance_score` is the sum, over every deficiency in the payload, of the scope weight multiplied by the severity weight. The scope weights are 1 for `ISOLATED`, 2 for `PATTERN` and 4 for `WIDESPREAD`. The severity weights are 1 for `MINIMAL`, 3 for `MINIMAL_HARM`, 8 for `ACTUAL_HARM` and 20 for `IMMEDIATE_JEOPARDY`. The score is a whole number and an empty array scores exactly 0. Only the deficiencies supplied in this request contribute: prior surveys never accumulate into the score, so each survey wholly replaces the unit's compliance picture.

The outcome is then read off the score in a fixed order of tests. If **any** deficiency in the payload has a `severity` of `IMMEDIATE_JEOPARDY`, the outcome is `DECERTIFIED`, and this override defeats the numeric bands entirely. Otherwise, a score of 25 or more decertifies; a score of at least 1 but below 25 yields `CONDITIONAL`; and a score of exactly 0 yields `CERTIFIED` — unless the unit's current `certification_status` is `DECERTIFIED`, in which case a clean survey yields `CONDITIONAL`. The band edges are inclusive on the lower side, so a score of 24 is `CONDITIONAL` and a score of 25 decertifies. The severity override is load-bearing rather than decorative: a single `ISOLATED` × `IMMEDIATE_JEOPARDY` deficiency scores only 20, which sits comfortably inside the `CONDITIONAL` band numerically, and is decertified purely because immediate jeopardy was found.

Every survey, whatever its outcome, writes `last_survey_compliance_score`, sets the unit's `last_survey_date` to the supplied `survey_date`, sets the operating home's `last_survey_date` to the same value and increments that home's `version`, recomputes `operational_posture`, and increments the unit's `version`. The transitions themselves then differ:

- **`CERTIFIED` → `CONDITIONAL`** on a score of at least 1 but below 25 with no immediate jeopardy. `certification_status` becomes `CONDITIONAL` and `operational_posture` becomes `RESTRICTED`. A `CONDITIONAL` unit is always restricted, regardless of how many beds are free — the certification branch of the posture rule outranks the bed-availability branch.
- **`CONDITIONAL` → `CERTIFIED`** on a score of exactly 0. `operational_posture` is recomputed from bed availability alone, so a unit with free beds returns to `OPEN` and a full one stays `RESTRICTED`. A single unit can therefore cycle `CERTIFIED` → `CONDITIONAL` → `CERTIFIED` across successive surveys.
- **`CERTIFIED` → `DECERTIFIED`** and **`CONDITIONAL` → `DECERTIFIED`**, both on a score of 25 or more *or* on any immediate-jeopardy deficiency, and both producing the identical four-hop cascade described below.
- **`DECERTIFIED` → `CONDITIONAL`** on a score below 25 with no immediate jeopardy, producing the three-hop reinstatement cascade described below. Note carefully that reinstatement never jumps straight back to `CERTIFIED`: a decertified unit must pass through `CONDITIONAL` first, which is precisely why a clean score of 0 lands on `CONDITIONAL` when the unit was decertified.

The **decertification cascade** runs four hops in order, and each hop is caused by the one before it:

1. The unit's `certification_status` becomes `DECERTIFIED`, `last_survey_compliance_score` and `last_survey_date` are written, and the unit's `version` increments.
2. Because `certification_status` became `DECERTIFIED`, `operational_posture` is recomputed to `FROZEN`. Every `ACTIVE` or `OFFERED` waitlist entry on the unit is set to `CANCELLED` with `offer_expires_at` cleared to null and `queue_position` forced to 0, and the unit's `waitlist_active_count` becomes 0 — nobody queues for a bed in a decertified unit.
3. Because the unit now counts toward the home's `decertified_unit_count`, which rises to at least 1, the home's `recognition_status` moves from `RECOGNIZED` to `SUSPENDED` with `recognition_suspended_reason` set to `UNIT_DECERTIFIED`, and the home's `version` increments. This hop fires only when the home stood at `RECOGNIZED`: a home already `SUSPENDED`, or one still `PROVISIONAL`, is left exactly as it was and hop 4 does not run either.
4. Because the home's `recognition_status` became `SUSPENDED`, every per diem claim of that home whose `status` is `DRAFT` — which necessarily includes the decertified unit's own drafts — is set to `VOID` and each such claim's `version` increments. Each voided claim releases the census days it had attributed: their `per_diem_claim_id` returns to null and their `claimed` flag returns to false, which puts those days back in the pool for a future claim. Claims already `SUBMITTED`, `PAID` or `REJECTED` are untouched; only drafts are voided.

The **reinstatement cascade** runs three hops:

1. The unit's `certification_status` becomes `CONDITIONAL`, `last_survey_compliance_score` and `last_survey_date` are written, and the unit's `version` increments.
2. Because `certification_status` left `DECERTIFIED`, `operational_posture` is recomputed from `FROZEN` to `RESTRICTED`.
3. Because the home's `decertified_unit_count` falls — and only when it reaches 0, meaning no other unit of the home is still decertified — the home's `recognition_status` moves from `SUSPENDED` back to `RECOGNIZED`, `recognition_suspended_reason` returns to null, and the home's `version` increments. If a second unit of the home remains `DECERTIFIED`, the count does not reach 0, the home stays `SUSPENDED` and its `version` does not increment on this hop.

Voided claims are not un-voided by reinstatement. Recognition returning to `RECOGNIZED` restores the home's ability to submit *new* claims; the drafts destroyed by hop 4 stay `VOID` forever, and the work must be re-drafted. Because `VOID` is terminal, the released census days are the only thing that survives.

### 3.9. Census Materialisation, Per Diem Claims and Federal Adjudication

Everything downstream of occupancy — federal per diem, final cost share, trust-fund postings, waitlist tenure — is driven off a materialised daily census. A census day is one row per resident per calendar date, and rows are produced only by the nightly operations checkpoint, which the `StateProgramAdmin` runs with a `through_date` and, optionally, a `veterans_home_id` to confine the run to a single home. Its `through_date` may not lie in the future relative to the logical clock.

The checkpoint executes five steps in this pinned order, and its response body is a plain object of counters rather than a resource: `generated_census_day_count`, `pna_credited_resident_count`, `cost_share_debited_resident_count`, `skipped_cost_share_debit_count`, `offered_waitlist_entry_count`, `expired_offer_count`, `review_due_care_plan_count` and `current_utc`.

**Step 1 — materialise the census.** For every resident whose stay overlaps the run window, one census day row is created per calendar date, and `last_census_date` is then advanced to the end of the window. The window opens at `window_start`, the later of the resident's `admission_date` and — when `last_census_date` is not null — the day after `last_census_date`; when `last_census_date` is null the window simply opens at `admission_date`. It closes at `window_end`, which is `through_date` for a resident with no `discharge_date` and the earlier of `through_date` and `discharge_date` for a discharged one. Both bounds are inclusive and both are whole UTC calendar days. A resident is in scope when their `occupancy_status` is anything other than `DISCHARGED`, and a discharged resident remains in scope only while their `discharge_date` is at or after `window_start`, which is how the final days of a stay get materialised after the discharge is recorded. At most one census day may exist per resident and `service_date`, so a second run with the same or an earlier `through_date` creates nothing at all — `window_start` has moved past `window_end` and the loop does not execute.

Each date is classified from the resident's occupancy as it stood at 23:59:59 UTC of that date, reconstructed from persisted resident fields only. The reconstruction hinges on the most recent bed-hold interval, taken as half-open from `hold_start` up to but excluding `hold_end`. `hold_start` is `bed_hold_started_on`, which is never cleared; a null value means the resident has never held a bed and every date is a resident day. `hold_end` is `last_hospitalization_end_date` when both `bed_hold_started_on` and `last_hospitalization_end_date` are present and the latter is strictly after the former — that is, when a readmission closed the interval. Failing that, it is the day after `discharge_date` when the resident's `occupancy_status` is `DISCHARGED`, so a resident discharged straight out of hospital counts the `discharge_date` itself as a hold day. Otherwise the interval is still open at this run and `hold_end` is the day after `window_end`. A date is a hold day when `hold_start` is present and the date falls at or after `hold_start` and strictly before `hold_end`. The consequences are worth stating plainly: the `hospitalization_start_date` is itself a `BED_HOLD_DAY`, and the `readmission_date` is itself a `RESIDENT_DAY`. Adjacency therefore never double-counts and never leaves a gap, and every date from `admission_date` through `discharge_date` receives exactly one row.

Whether a hold day is payable depends on how much of the thirty-day allowance has already been consumed at the end of that date. Prior closed days are `bed_hold_days_used` less the length of the current interval when `hold_end` is `last_hospitalization_end_date` — because a readmission has already folded that interval's length into `bed_hold_days_used` — and are simply `bed_hold_days_used` otherwise, since an open interval, or one closed by discharge, was never folded in. The days used at the end of a given date are those prior closed days plus the number of days from `hold_start` to that date. The hospitalization start date therefore counts zero, and the thirty-first consecutive day of one unbroken hold is the first `ABSENT_DAY`. A hold day is classified `BED_HOLD_DAY` while the days used at the end of it are below thirty, and `ABSENT_DAY` from that point on. Any date that is not a hold day is a `RESIDENT_DAY`. The checkpoint never mutates `bed_hold_days_used`; that field advances only at readmission.

Two properties of step 1 are pinned rather than incidental. First, only the **most recent** hold interval is reconstructible: if a resident starts and closes more than one hospitalization between two checkpoint runs, the earlier intervals are no longer present on the resident row, and their dates are classified `RESIDENT_DAY`. This is a pinned consequence of the nightly-checkpoint design, not an implementation liberty. Second, the `care_unit_id` written onto every generated row is the resident's `care_unit_id` **at the moment of the run**, not the unit they occupied historically. A transfer that happens between two runs therefore attributes the still-uncensused earlier dates to the *new* unit, which is exactly why the checkpoint is expected to run before each transfer.

Each generated row also freezes its own pricing at creation. `care_level` is the care level of the attributed unit; `per_diem_eligible` is true exactly when `census_class` is `RESIDENT_DAY` or `BED_HOLD_DAY`, since an `ABSENT_DAY` is never payable; and `payable_rate_cents` is the full federal rate for that `care_level` on a `RESIDENT_DAY`, that rate at 5,000 basis points rounded half up on a `BED_HOLD_DAY`, and 0 on an `ABSENT_DAY`. A row's `per_diem_claim_id` starts null and its `claimed` flag is derived: true exactly when `per_diem_claim_id` is set and the referenced claim's `status` is `SUBMITTED` or `PAID`.

**Step 2 — credit the personal needs allowance.** One `PNA_CREDIT` trust-fund posting is made per in-scope resident for the operating home's `personal_needs_allowance_monthly_cents`, with `effective_date` set to `through_date` and `memo` set to `personal needs allowance`. Steps 2 and 3 read a narrower scope than step 1: only residents of the run's scope whose `occupancy_status` is not `DISCHARGED` are posted against, so a resident discharged inside the window has their final census days materialised by step 1 and is then credited and debited nothing. A home whose `personal_needs_allowance_monthly_cents` is 0 posts nothing at all and leaves its residents out of `pna_credited_resident_count`, since every trust-fund posting's `amount_cents` must be strictly positive. This is pinned to once per resident per calendar month: a resident who already holds a `PNA_CREDIT` row whose `effective_date` falls in the calendar month of `through_date` is skipped silently and is **not** counted in `pna_credited_resident_count`, so re-running the checkpoint on a later date in the same month never double-credits.

**Step 3 — debit the monthly cost share.** One `COST_SHARE_DEBIT` posting is made per resident of that same non-discharged scope whose governing `monthly_cost_share_cents` (per the selector in §3.7, which keys on the calendar month of the logical clock and not on the month of `through_date`) is strictly greater than zero, with `effective_date` set to `through_date` and `memo` set to `monthly cost share`. When the posting would breach the personal-needs-allowance reserve it is skipped and counted in `skipped_cost_share_debit_count` — the checkpoint declines the debit rather than failing the whole run. A resident whose governing cost share is exactly zero is neither debited nor counted as skipped, so such a resident appears in neither counter. The once-per-resident-per-calendar-month pin applies here too: a resident already holding a `COST_SHARE_DEBIT` row whose `effective_date` falls in the calendar month of `through_date` is skipped silently and counted in **neither** `cost_share_debited_resident_count` nor `skipped_cost_share_debit_count`. Every resident's `trust_fund_balance_cents` is then refreshed by replaying the ledger.

**Step 4 — advance the waitlist.** Priority scores are recomputed for every applicant holding an `ACTIVE` or `OFFERED` entry on a touched unit, so the tenure term advances with the clock; offers are issued on each `OPEN` unit holding at least one free bed to the single `ACTIVE` entry sitting at `queue_position` 1 — at most one offer per unit per run however many beds are free, and none at all while any entry on that unit is already `OFFERED` — with `offer_expires_at` set seven days ahead of the clock; elapsed offers are then declined and exhausted applicants expired, all as described in §3.3, `expired_offer_count` counting the offers that lapsed and not the entries the three-decline ceiling drove to `EXPIRED`, which land in no counter at all; and `waitlist_active_count` and `queue_position` are recomputed on every touched unit.

**Step 5 — flag care-plan reviews.** Every `ACTIVE` care plan whose `review_due_date` is strictly earlier than `through_date` is set to `REVIEW_DUE`, and the count lands in `review_due_care_plan_count`. A plan whose `review_due_date` equals `through_date` is not yet due.

Across all five steps, `Resident.version`, `CareUnit.version` and `Applicant.version` increment for every row the run touches.

With the census materialised, the month can be billed. A per diem claim is the home's monthly bill to the federal programme for one care unit. The `BusinessOfficeManager` opens one by naming the `veterans_home_id`, the `care_unit_id` and the `claim_month` in `YYYY-MM` form, and optionally a `basis` — which defaults to `AUDITED_BASIS`, with `CENSUS_BASIS` available for the census-reported variant. A new claim starts `DRAFT` with `correction_generation` 0 and `corrects_claim_id`, `submitted_at`, `adjudicated_at`, `paid_amount_cents`, `rejection_code` and `adjudication_note` all null, and `recognition_verified` false. Creation immediately attributes census days to the claim and prices them; both the operating home's `version` and the unit's `version` increment. The two identifiers are each resolved for existence but their pairing is never cross-checked: a claim may name a `care_unit_id` that belongs to some other home, and the system raises nothing, pricing the claim from the named unit while the recognition gate at submission reads the named home.

**Attribution** is the heart of claim assembly. A census day belongs to a claim's attributable set when its `care_unit_id` matches the claim's, its `service_date` falls inside the claim's `claim_month`, and its `per_diem_claim_id` is either null, equal to this claim, or points at a claim that is not live — one whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `REJECTED` or `VOID`. Only rows attributed to a `SUBMITTED` or `PAID` claim are excluded — a day may be *billed* once and once only, but a draft holds no lock on the days it has provisionally gathered, so another claim entitled to the month simply takes them over and the draft is silently left with rows it no longer holds until it is re-priced. From that set, `resident_day_count` is the number of rows classified `RESIDENT_DAY`, `bed_hold_day_count` the number classified `BED_HOLD_DAY`, and `census_reported_resident_count` the number of distinct `resident_id` values appearing in it. `ABSENT_DAY` rows are never attributable and are never counted anywhere.

**Pricing** starts from the federal daily rate for the unit's `care_level` — 11,857 cents for `NURSING`, 5,502 cents for `DOMICILIARY`, 4,230 cents for `ADULT_DAY_HEALTH` — and then diverges by basis:

- Under `AUDITED_BASIS`, the bed-hold day rate is the full rate at 5,000 basis points (half), divided by ten thousand and **rounded half up once per day**. Payable bed-hold days are `bed_hold_day_count` capped at ten per claim month. `billable_day_count` is `resident_day_count` plus those payable bed-hold days — so bed-hold days beyond the cap are excluded from the billable count entirely — and `claim_amount_cents` is the full rate times `resident_day_count`, plus the per-day bed-hold rate times the payable bed-hold days.
- Under `CENSUS_BASIS`, there is no cap and no half rate: `billable_day_count` is `resident_day_count` plus the whole `bed_hold_day_count`, and `claim_amount_cents` is the full rate times `billable_day_count`. Both figures are therefore strictly greater than the audited figures whenever `bed_hold_day_count` is above zero.

The per-day rounding of the bed-hold half rate is pinned and observable. At the `NURSING` rate of 11,857 cents over three bed-hold days, the correct order rounds 5,928.5 up to 5,929 and multiplies to 17,787 cents; rounding the monthly total instead would give 17,786 cents. Rounding the total is wrong.

**Month occupancy and single attribution** are enforced by two adjacent checks. At most one claim per care unit and `claim_month` may occupy the month at a time: opening a plain claim is rejected with `ERR_CLAIM_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409) when another claim for the same `care_unit_id` and `claim_month` already has `status` `DRAFT` or `SUBMITTED`. A `PAID` month is not caught by that rule; it is caught one rung later by `ERR_CENSUS_DAY_ALREADY_CLAIMED` (HTTP 409), because its census days are already consumed. A `REJECTED` or `VOID` month is free, and its days are back in the pool. A correcting claim is exempt from the occupancy rule entirely, because it supersedes its own target's attribution. The single-attribution rule itself — that no census day of the unit inside the month may be locked by a *live* claim, meaning one whose `status` is `SUBMITTED` or `PAID` and which is neither this claim nor the claim named in its `corrects_claim_id`, while days unattributed or held by a `DRAFT`, `REJECTED` or `VOID` claim pass freely — is checked on claim creation, on correction and again on submission, with the one exception that a correcting claim may take over the rows held by the claim named in its own `corrects_claim_id`. The practical consequence: the only legal way to revise a paid month is the correction operation.

**Submission** moves a claim from `DRAFT` to `SUBMITTED` and is gated on five independent conditions, evaluated in the ladder order below. The claim's `status` must be `DRAFT`, or `ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID` (HTTP 409). The home's `recognition_status` must be `RECOGNIZED`, or `ERR_HOME_NOT_RECOGNIZED` (HTTP 409). The unit must not be `DECERTIFIED`, or `ERR_UNIT_NOT_CERTIFIED` (HTTP 409) — note that a `CONDITIONAL` unit may still submit; only decertification blocks. The single-attribution rule is then re-applied to the live census, so a day of this unit-month now locked by another `SUBMITTED` or `PAID` claim is `ERR_CENSUS_DAY_ALREADY_CLAIMED` (HTTP 409). The `claim_month` must be strictly earlier than the month of the current logical clock, because the census month must be closed, or `ERR_CLAIM_MONTH_NOT_CLOSED` (HTTP 409) — a claim for the current month is never submittable however complete it looks. Finally the claim must not be empty: `census_reported_resident_count` must be at least 1 and `billable_day_count` at least 1, or `ERR_CLAIM_CENSUS_EMPTY` (HTTP 409). Submission re-prices the claim from the live census before anything is written, because days may have been generated, released or taken over since the draft was assembled; then it sets `status` to `SUBMITTED`, stamps `submitted_at` from the logical clock, sets `recognition_verified` to true, sets `per_diem_claim_id` on every attributed census day so that each row's `claimed` flag becomes true, and increments both the claim's `version` and the unit's `version`.

**Adjudication** is the `FederalPerDiemAuditor`'s decision on a `SUBMITTED` claim, and no other role may make it — the business office prepares and submits, the auditor pays or rejects. The request carries an `outcome`, an optional `rejection_code` and an optional `adjudication_note`. On an outcome of `PAID`, `status` becomes `PAID`, `adjudicated_at` is stamped from the logical clock, `paid_amount_cents` is set equal to `claim_amount_cents`, `rejection_code` stays null, and the claim's census days are permanently consumed. On an outcome of `REJECTED`, a `rejection_code` of `CENSUS_MISMATCH`, `RECOGNITION_LAPSE` or `DUPLICATE_PERIOD` is required, and its absence is a request-shape violation reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422) on rung 3 of the ladder — before the claim's own path identifier has been resolved, so an adjudication naming no such claim and carrying no code answers 422 rather than 404; `status` becomes `REJECTED`, `adjudicated_at` is stamped, `paid_amount_cents` is set to 0, the code is stored, and every attributed census day is released with `per_diem_claim_id` set back to null and `claimed` back to false, which returns those days to the pool. Either way `adjudication_note` is stored exactly as supplied and is written back to null when the field is omitted — which on a correcting claim discards the `correction_note` that its creation had parked in that same field — and both the claim's `version` and the unit's `version` increment while the home is left untouched. `PAID`, `REJECTED` and `VOID` are all terminal: an adjudicated claim never changes status again, and a claim already adjudicated cannot be adjudicated twice.

**Correction** is how a settled month is revised. A correcting claim may be opened only against a claim whose `status` is `PAID` or `REJECTED` — a `DRAFT` or `SUBMITTED` target yields `ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID`, and a `VOID` one likewise — and the request must carry `expected_claim_version` matching the target's current `version`, or `ERR_CLAIM_VERSION_STALE` (HTTP 412). It may also carry `basis`, which defaults to the target's own basis, a `correction_note`, and `claim_month`. That last field is an optional confirmation echo and nothing more: the new claim always copies its `claim_month` and `care_unit_id` from the target, but when the caller does supply `claim_month` it must equal the target's, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_CLAIM_MONTH_MISMATCH` (HTTP 409) before anything is written. The correction creates a fresh `DRAFT` claim whose `corrects_claim_id` names the target and whose `correction_generation` is the target's plus one, so a chain of corrections is self-describing. Every census day of the unit inside the month whose `per_diem_claim_id` is null, points at the target, or points at any other claim that is not live is re-attributed to the new claim with `claimed` reset to false; the new claim's day counts, `billable_day_count`, `claim_amount_cents` and `census_reported_resident_count` are computed for the declared basis; and `adjudication_note` on the new claim is set to `correction_note`. The target's `version` increments, as do the home's and the unit's. The correcting claim then proceeds through submission and adjudication exactly like any other draft.

## 5. Error Code Catalogue

Every failed request returns the error envelope of §2.2 with one of the identifiers below. When a request violates several rules at once, the ladder of §2.11 decides which identifier is reported: the lowest-numbered rung wins, and evaluation stops there.

The status column below is the status each identifier carries when a rule of this document raises it. Two transport-level failures reuse `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` at a status of their own rather than at 422: a request to a path this API does not define answers `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` at HTTP 404, and a defined path addressed with a method it does not declare answers `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` at HTTP 405. Both still carry the standard error envelope. A trailing slash is not a distinct path — `/svh/residents/` and `/svh/residents` address the same collection.
