# FQHC Sliding Fee & Section 330 Compliance API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A Federally Qualified Health Center lives inside two accounting systems at once. One is clinical and financial: patients walk in, income and family size are documented, a sliding fee discount is applied, a claim goes out at a prospective payment system encounter rate, and a state wraparound payment closes the residual. The other is regulatory: the grantee's scope of project lists exactly which sites and which services the federal award covers, a patient-majority governing board must approve the sliding fee schedule, the schedule must be reviewed every year against the current federal poverty guidelines, an annual uniform data system report must reconcile the year's patients into income bands, and an operational site visit can put a condition on the award that freezes every pending change-in-scope request until a corrective action plan is accepted. Today these two systems are reconciled by hand, in spreadsheets, weeks after the fact — which is why a retroactively verified income document silently leaves a dozen already-billed encounters overstating patient responsibility, and why a board that has quietly dropped below a patient majority can keep approving scope expansions.

This document specifies the API for a compliance platform that holds both accountings in one consistent store. The platform is deliberately opinionated about ordering and about conservation. Every deadline, expiry window, effective period and recency test is evaluated against a server-controlled **logical clock** rather than wall-clock time, so a compliance epoch can be replayed deterministically. Every derived counter on a grantee — in-scope sites, in-scope services, seated board seats, open award conditions, outstanding wraparound receivable — is a conserved quantity that must equal a recount of its children after every request, including after the compensating requests that undo earlier ones. Every patient's outstanding responsibility is a conserved ledger that must return to its exact pre-encounter value when an encounter is voided, with zero drift, even when a waiver and a retroactive recomputation intervened.

The reader should treat two themes as pervasive rather than local. The first is **segregation of duties**: the staff member who submits an income verification may not adjudicate it, the officer who prepares a change-in-scope request may not be the board chair who decides it, the officer who prepares a corrective action plan may not be the project officer who accepts it, and the representative who requests an inability-to-pay waiver may not decide it. The second is **pinning**: an encounter permanently records which fee schedule, which income verification and which discount tier produced its charge, so that later supersessions and adoptions never silently rewrite history — history is rewritten only by the explicitly specified retroactive cascades described in section 3. All collections in this API are mounted at the top level of the URL space; there is no shared API prefix, and ownership is expressed through identifier fields carried in request payloads rather than through deep URL nesting.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting principal and `X-User-Role` declares the role that principal is acting in. If either header is absent or present but empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS` (HTTP 401). If `X-User-Role` carries a value that is not one of the seven roles enumerated below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE` (HTTP 401). If the role is recognised but is not among the roles authorised for the endpoint being called, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The seven roles, spelled exactly as they must appear in `X-User-Role`, are:

- **`PlatformAdmin`** — operator of the compliance platform. Registers grantees, publishes federal poverty guidelines, establishes the Notice-of-Award baseline scope listing, drives the logical clock, and runs the compliance sweep.
- **`ComplianceOfficer`** — grantee-side Section 330 compliance staff. Prepares change-in-scope requests, drafts fee schedules and their tier ladders, seats and resigns board members, records annual reviews, decides inability-to-pay waivers, and prepares corrective action plans and uniform data system reports.
- **`BoardChair`** — chair of the patient-majority governing board. Takes change-in-scope requests under review, decides them, and adopts fee schedules.
- **`EligibilityVerifier`** — adjudicates income and family size documentation.
- **`PatientServicesRep`** — front-desk patient services. Registers patients, submits and resubmits income verifications, and requests inability-to-pay waivers.
- **`BillingSpecialist`** — owns the encounter and both of its state dimensions. Records visits, bills the prospective payment system claim, posts payer payments, claims state wraparound payments, and voids encounters.
- **`ProjectOfficer`** — awarding-agency project officer. Issues operational site visit findings, suspends and reinstates sites, and adjudicates corrective action plans and uniform data system reports.

Six of the seven roles carry an **opaque** identity. When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `EligibilityVerifier`, `PatientServicesRep`, `BillingSpecialist` or `ProjectOfficer`, the `X-User-ID` value is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it wherever a transition in section 3 names an audit identity — `prepared_by_user_id` on a change-in-scope request or a corrective action plan, `submitted_by_user_id` and `verified_by_user_id` on an income verification, `requested_by_user_id` and `decided_by_user_id` on a waiver, `issued_by_user_id` on a site visit finding, `submitted_by_user_id` and `adjudicated_by_user_id` on a uniform data system report, and `adjudicated_by_user_id` on a corrective action plan — but it never compares that value to any entity's `id`. These recorded identities exist for audit and for the segregation-of-duties comparisons described below; they are not foreign keys and are never dereferenced.

The `BoardChair` role is different: its identity is **entity-backed by a `BoardMember` row**. When `X-User-Role` is `BoardChair`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `BoardMember`. Any board-business operation on a grantee therefore compares the acting `X-User-ID` against the `BoardMember` rows of that grantee: the acting chair must be a `BoardMember` whose `health_center_id` is the grantee in question and whose `status` is `SEATED`. A chair that holds no seated seat on that grantee's board is rejected with `ERR_NOT_SEATED_BOARD_MEMBER` (HTTP 403). This check guards three operations: taking a change-in-scope request under review, deciding a change-in-scope request, and adopting a fee schedule. Consistently with this, the `reviewed_by_user_id` recorded on a change-in-scope request and the `adopted_by_user_id` recorded on a fee schedule hold the `id` of a `BoardMember`, although the server still treats them as audit strings rather than as dereferenceable references.

Segregation of duties is enforced by comparing the acting `X-User-ID` against the identity already recorded on the row being adjudicated. Deciding a change-in-scope request compares against its `prepared_by_user_id`; adjudicating an income verification compares against its `submitted_by_user_id`; deciding an inability-to-pay waiver compares against its `requested_by_user_id`; adjudicating a corrective action plan compares against its `prepared_by_user_id`. If the acting identifier is equal to the recorded one, the request is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 409).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- Every instant-valued field the API returns inside `data` is rendered in that same form — an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with exactly three fractional digits and a trailing `Z`, for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`. This holds for `created_at` and `updated_at`, for every instant the server stamps from the logical clock (`in_scope_since`, `adopted_at`, `last_reviewed_at`, `expires_at`, `cap_due_at`, `escalated_at`, `computed_at`, the echoed `current_utc`), and for every date or instant the client supplied that the server echoes back (`effective_start`, `effective_end`, `board_vote_date`, `term_start`, `term_end` — including a `term_end` truncated to a `resignation_date` — `resignation_date`, `effective_date`, `encounter_date`, `target_completion_date`). A supplied value carrying another UTC offset, or carrying no time component at all, is normalised to UTC before it is rendered this way; a field whose value is null is returned as null.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints accept two query-string parameters. `limit` caps the number of items returned; when it is omitted it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` is the number of items to skip; when it is omitted it defaults to 0, and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400).

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are ordered by the resource's creation timestamp, `created_at`, in ascending order. One collection overrides this: the tier ladder of a fee schedule is returned in ascending `sort_order` rather than by creation timestamp, because the ladder's meaning is its order. The quality measures of a uniform data system report follow the default ascending `created_at`, which for that collection is also the order in which the composite calculation walks them.

List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope. `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filter before pagination is applied, and `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond the returned page.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties and the Immutable Guideline Table

Nearly every resource in this system is a mutable, versioned record. `HealthCenter`, `BoardMember`, `ServiceSite`, `ServiceDefinition`, `ScopeChangeRequest`, `FeeSchedule`, `DiscountTier`, `Patient`, `IncomeVerification`, `Encounter`, `InabilityToPayWaiver`, `UDSReport`, `QualityMeasureResult`, `SiteVisitFinding` and `CorrectiveActionPlan` all carry the following system-managed properties:

- `id` (string): a server-minted UUID. It carries no type prefix and no embedded meaning; clients never supply it.
- `created_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`, recording when the row was first persisted. Because `created_at` carries three fractional digits and no two rows of one entity are created by the same request, `created_at` is a total ordering within an entity — which is why every ordering rule in this document that says "in ascending `created_at` order" is unambiguous.
- `updated_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp recording the most recent mutation of the row.
- `version` (integer): a counter that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of the row. Recomputation of a derived field counts as a mutation: when a grantee's counters, postures or receivable are recalculated, its `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increments, even though no client supplied a value.

`PovertyGuideline` is the single exception. A published guideline year is immutable — there is no endpoint that modifies one — so a `PovertyGuideline` row carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has no `updated_at` field and no `version` field, and neither field appears in its response payload.

### 2.5. The Logical Compliance Clock

Nothing in this system reads wall-clock time for business purposes. The server maintains a **logical clock** whose value at process startup is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; until the clock is explicitly set, every rule reads that default, and the server must never silently substitute real time. A `PlatformAdmin` moves the clock by calling `POST /_clock/set_current_utc` with a `current_utc` instant in the body; the response echoes `{ current_utc }` and no entity is mutated by the call itself.

Every temporal rule in this document evaluates against the logical clock: a corrective-action-plan deadline (`cap_due_at`), an income verification's expiry (`expires_at`), a fee schedule's effective period, the elapsed-days input to a schedule's annual-review posture, the two-year recency test that qualifies a patient board seat, and the future-dated check on an encounter's date of service. Instants that the server stamps — `in_scope_since` on a site, `adopted_at` and `last_reviewed_at` on a schedule, `computed_at` on a report, `escalated_at` on a finding — are all the logical clock's value at the moment of the request, not wall-clock time.

### 2.6. Interval Conventions: Half-Open Periods, Closed Windows, and Band Semantics

Five different interval conventions coexist in this domain, and confusing any two of them changes the answer. They are pinned as follows.

A fee schedule's effective period is **half-open**: it runs from `effective_start` inclusive to `effective_end` exclusive. A date equal to `effective_end` is outside the period. Two schedules of one grantee are therefore permitted to be adjacent — the earlier one's `effective_end` may equal the later one's `effective_start` — without being treated as overlapping.

An income verification's window is **closed**: it runs from `effective_date` inclusive to `expires_at` inclusive, where `expires_at` is exactly 365 days after `effective_date`. An encounter dated exactly on `expires_at` is still covered.

A board member's term is **closed**: it runs from `term_start` inclusive to `term_end` inclusive. Because both endpoints are inclusive, adjacency is itself an overlap: if an existing seated member's `term_end` equals a new member's `term_start` for the same `seat_number` of the same grantee, the two terms collide and the new seating is rejected.

A discount tier's band is **half-open**: it covers percentages from `fpg_min_pct` inclusive up to `fpg_max_pct` exclusive, and a `fpg_max_pct` of null means the band is unbounded above. A percentage exactly equal to a band's `fpg_max_pct` therefore belongs to the *next* band, never to the band it bounds.

The four uniform data system reporting bands are **closed above** and deliberately disagree with the tier bands: the first band is every percentage at or below 100.00, the second is above 100.00 up to and including 150.00, the third is above 150.00 up to and including 200.00, and the fourth is everything above 200.00. The consequence is worth stating plainly: a percentage of exactly 100.00 counts in the *first* reporting band, while for tier assignment the same 100.00 falls into the *second* tier of a ladder whose lowest band ends at 100.

### 2.7. Money, Percentages, and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts in this system are integers denominated in cents. Field names ending in `_cents` are integers, never decimals, and no monetary value is ever represented as a string. Where a monetary computation produces a fractional cent, the result is rounded half up to a whole cent, and each calculation in section 3 states exactly how many roundings it performs and at which step — the number and placement of roundings is part of the contract, because clamping an already-rounded integer and then re-rounding it produces a different answer.

Percentages are decimal numbers with a pinned scale. A verification's `fpg_percent` and `uds_fpg_percent`, and a grantee's `patient_seat_pct`, are each rounded half up to **2 decimal places**. A quality measure's `rate_pct` and a report's `composite_quality_rate_pct` are each rounded half up to **1 decimal place**. Every comparison that consumes one of these values compares the already-rounded value, not an unrounded intermediate: the patient-majority test compares the rounded `patient_seat_pct`, tier band selection compares the rounded `fpg_percent`, the reporting bands compare the rounded `uds_fpg_percent`, and the benchmark test compares the rounded `rate_pct`.

Two values are not percentages in the hundreds scale and must not be treated as such. A tier's `discount_pct` is a *fraction* strictly greater than 0 and strictly less than 1, stored exactly as supplied. A measure's `benchmark_pct` is a percentage between 0 and 100 inclusive, also stored as supplied.

Elapsed time, where it feeds a threshold, is measured in **whole days, truncated downward** — a partial day never rounds up.

### 2.8. Conserved Quantities: Grantee Counters and the Patient Responsibility Ledger

Six values in this system are conserved quantities. They are stored for reading convenience, but their stored value must equal a fresh recount at the end of every request, with a drift tolerance of exactly zero. Any request that changes a contributing row must recompute them in the same request, and a request that *undoes* an earlier change must restore them to the value they held before it.

On a grantee, `in_scope_site_count` equals the number of that grantee's `ServiceSite` rows whose `status` is `IN_SCOPE`; `in_scope_service_count` equals the number of that grantee's `ServiceDefinition` rows whose `status` is `IN_SCOPE`; `open_condition_count` equals the number of that grantee's `SiteVisitFinding` rows whose `status` is `ESCALATED`; `seated_board_count` equals the number of that grantee's `BoardMember` rows whose `status` is `SEATED`; and `wraparound_receivable_cents` equals the sum of `wraparound_due_cents` over the grantee's encounters whose `wraparound_status` is `PENDING`, `CLAIMED` or `REBILL_REQUIRED`. An encounter whose wraparound has reached `RECEIVED`, or which is `NOT_APPLICABLE`, contributes nothing to the receivable.

On a patient, `patient_balance_cents` equals the sum, over that patient's encounters whose `pps_claim_status` is anything other than `VOID`, of the encounter's `patient_charge_cents` less its `waived_amount_cents`. A voided encounter drops out of the sum entirely rather than being counted as zero. The four movements that touch this ledger are: recording an encounter, which adds that encounter's `patient_charge_cents`; a retroactive recomputation, which applies the encounter's `charge_adjustment_cents`; a granted inability-to-pay waiver, which subtracts the waived amount; and voiding an encounter, which subtracts that encounter's `patient_charge_cents` less its `waived_amount_cents`. The invariant that matters most is the round trip: an encounter followed by its void must leave `patient_balance_cents` at exactly its pre-encounter value, even when a waiver was granted and a retroactive verification recomputed the charge in between.

The forward-and-compensating pairs on the grantee counters work the same way. An approved request that adds a site raises `in_scope_site_count` by one; an approved request that removes a site lowers it by one, back to the original count. A finding that escalates raises `open_condition_count` by one and freezes scope; an accepted corrective action plan lowers it and unfreezes scope, restoring the held requests and the parked sites.

### 2.9. Award Posture, Scope Freeze, and Cascade Propagation

A grantee's regulatory posture is derived, never set by a client. `operational_status` is `ACTIVE` while `open_condition_count` is zero and `CONDITIONS_IMPOSED` while it is greater than zero. `scope_posture` is `CHANGE_FROZEN` whenever `operational_status` is `CONDITIONS_IMPOSED`, and `OPEN` otherwise. `governance_posture` is either `GOVERNANCE_COMPLIANT` or `GOVERNANCE_DEFICIENT` according to the patient-majority calculation described in section 3.2, and a newly registered grantee starts at `GOVERNANCE_DEFICIENT` because an empty board can never hold a majority.

Because these postures are derived, the server transitions dependent rows on its own initiative. When a posture flips, the change propagates through several hops in the *same* request: a freeze parks pending change-in-scope requests, and parking those requests in turn parks their proposed target sites; unfreezing restores both, in reverse. No client request addresses the parked request or the parked site directly, and each affected row's `updated_at` and `version` advance as part of the propagating request. Every hop of every cascade in this system is enumerated explicitly in section 3 — an implementation that performs the first hop and stops is incorrect, and the secondary hops are the ones that carry the conserved quantities of section 2.8 back into balance.

### 2.10. Path Resolution and Reference Integrity

Identifiers embedded in a URL path are resolved **left to right**, and the first one that does not resolve decides the response with HTTP 404. A grantee identifier resolves before the child collection under it, so a request for a grantee's fee schedules under a grantee that does not exist yields `ERR_HEALTH_CENTER_NOT_FOUND` and never a fee-schedule error. The full set of existence codes is `ERR_HEALTH_CENTER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_POVERTY_GUIDELINE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PATIENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SERVICE_SITE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SERVICE_DEFINITION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SCOPE_CHANGE_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FEE_SCHEDULE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DISCOUNT_TIER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INCOME_VERIFICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ENCOUNTER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WAIVER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BOARD_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_UDS_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_QUALITY_MEASURE_RESULT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SITE_VISIT_FINDING_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_CORRECTIVE_ACTION_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, each returned with HTTP 404.

Identifiers supplied in a request *body* are resolved after the path and in the order the owning entity declares its fields, again yielding the not-found code of the referenced entity. Body-supplied references are checked this way on every creation that carries one: a grantee's service site, service definition, change-in-scope request, board member, fee schedule and uniform data system report; a schedule's discount tier; a patient's income verification; an encounter; an encounter's waiver; a report's quality measure result; a grantee's site visit finding; and a finding's corrective action plan.

Existence is not enough — a reference must also belong to the right grantee. A change-in-scope request's `target_site_id` or `target_service_definition_id` must name a row whose `health_center_id` equals the request's own `health_center_id`; a site visit finding's `service_site_id` must belong to the finding's grantee; an encounter's `service_site_id` and `service_definition_id` must both belong to one and the same grantee; and a patient board seat's `patient_id` must name a `Patient` who has at least one non-`VOID` encounter at a site of that grantee. A violation of any of these is `ERR_CROSS_GRANTEE_REFERENCE` (HTTP 409), and it is checked on change-in-scope-request drafting, site-visit-finding issuance, board-member seating and encounter creation.

Where a child is created through its parent's collection — a site under a grantee, a tier under a schedule, a verification under a patient, a waiver under an encounter, a measure under a report, a plan under a finding — the owning identifier may be omitted from the body and is then taken from the path. When it is supplied in the body it must agree with the path.

### 2.11. Optimistic Concurrency Control on Patient and Grantee Versions

Exactly two write operations in this API take a version-pinning field, and for those two the field is required, not optional.

Submitting an income verification under a patient — `POST /patients/{id}/income-verifications` — requires `expected_patient_version` in the body, carrying the `version` the client last observed on that `Patient`. If `expected_patient_version` does not equal the patient's current `version`, the submission is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PATIENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The reason this write needs the guard is that it is semantically valid on its own: a new verification legitimately supersedes the patient's prior verification once it is adjudicated. The stale version is the only signal available that the submitter's view of the patient's verification history has been overtaken.

Adopting a fee schedule — `POST /fee-schedules/{id}/adopt` — requires `expected_health_center_version` in the body, carrying the `version` the client last observed on that schedule's `HealthCenter`. If it does not equal the grantee's current `version`, the adoption is rejected with `ERR_STALE_HEALTH_CENTER_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Adoption of a schedule that shares an `effective_start` with an already-adopted schedule legitimately supersedes that schedule, so the adopting board is required to prove it observed the grantee state — its currently adopted schedule, its counters and its governance posture — that the vote was based on.

No other endpoint accepts a version field. Endpoints not named in this subsection perform no version check, and supplying a version-like field to them is a request-shape violation.

### 2.12. Validation Precedence

When more than one rule would reject a request, the rule with the **lowest priority number wins** and its error is the one returned. The ladder below is the total ordering; it is evaluated top to bottom and stops at the first violation.

1. Both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` are present and non-empty, on every endpoint — otherwise `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS` (401).
2. `X-User-Role` names one of the seven declared roles, on every endpoint — otherwise `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE` (401).
3. The acting role is authorised for the endpoint, on every endpoint — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403).
4. On every write endpoint, the body contains every required input field, every enum-typed value is a declared member of that enum, every integer and number satisfies its declared constraint, and no system-managed or computed field is supplied by the client — otherwise `ERR_VALIDATION` (400).
5. On every list endpoint, `limit` is at least 1 and at most 100 and `offset` is at least 0 — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (400).
6. On every endpoint carrying a path identifier, each path identifier resolves, left to right — otherwise the matching not-found code of the unresolved entity, drawn from the list in section 2.10 (404).
7. On every creation that carries a body reference, each referenced row resolves, in declared field order — otherwise the matching not-found code of the unresolved entity, drawn from the same list (404).
8. Cross-grantee references belong to the operation's grantee, on change-in-scope-request drafting, site-visit-finding issuance, board-member seating and encounter creation — otherwise `ERR_CROSS_GRANTEE_REFERENCE` (409).
9. The polymorphic target pairing agrees with `request_type`, on change-in-scope-request drafting and on change-in-scope-request submission — otherwise `ERR_SCOPE_TARGET_MISMATCH` (422).
10. The version guard matches, on income-verification submission and on fee-schedule adoption — otherwise `ERR_STALE_PATIENT_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_HEALTH_CENTER_VERSION` (412).
11. The addressed row occupies a state from which the requested transition departs, on every action endpoint that drives a state machine — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (409).
12. The loop cap for the cycle being re-entered has not been reached, on change-in-scope-request submission, income-verification resubmission, corrective-action-plan submission and uniform-data-system-report computation — otherwise `ERR_LOOP_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (409).
13. Segregation of duties holds, on change-in-scope-request decision, income-verification adjudication, waiver decision and corrective-action-plan adjudication — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (409).
14. The acting board chair holds a `SEATED` seat on the grantee's board, on change-in-scope-request begin-review, change-in-scope-request decision and fee-schedule adoption — otherwise `ERR_NOT_SEATED_BOARD_MEMBER` (403).
15. The grantee's `scope_posture` is `OPEN`, on change-in-scope-request submission, begin-review and decision — otherwise `ERR_SCOPE_CHANGE_FROZEN` (409).
16. The verification's `documentation_type` is not `REFUSED`, when an income verification is adjudicated with the verify decision — otherwise `ERR_DOCUMENTATION_REFUSED` (422).
17. The encounter is not protected by a granted inability-to-pay waiver against a non-payment void, on encounter voiding — otherwise `ERR_INABILITY_TO_PAY_PROTECTED` (409).
18. The encounter is waiver-eligible, on waiver request — otherwise `ERR_WAIVER_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (422).
19. The grantee's pre-transition `governance_posture` is `GOVERNANCE_COMPLIANT`, when a change-in-scope request is decided with the approve decision — otherwise `ERR_GOVERNANCE_DEFICIENT` (409).
20. The grantee's effective adopted schedule is not out of date, when a change-in-scope request is decided with the approve decision — otherwise `ERR_FEE_SCHEDULE_OUT_OF_DATE` (409).
21. A fee schedule's `effective_start` is strictly earlier than its `effective_end`, on fee-schedule drafting — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PERIOD` (422).
22. A board member's `term_start` is at or before its `term_end` on seating, and a `resignation_date` is at or after `term_start` on resignation — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_TERM` (422).
23. `encounter_date` is not after the logical clock, on encounter creation — otherwise `ERR_ENCOUNTER_DATE_IN_FUTURE` (422).
24. Fee-schedule period exclusivity holds, on fee-schedule adoption — otherwise `ERR_FEE_SCHEDULE_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (409).
25. Board seat exclusivity holds, on board-member seating — otherwise `ERR_BOARD_SEAT_OVERLAP` (409).
26. A corrective action plan's `target_completion_date` is not after its finding's `cap_due_at`, on corrective-action-plan drafting — otherwise `ERR_CAP_TARGET_AFTER_DUE` (422).
27. An explicitly supplied `income_verification_id` is verified and covers the date of service, on encounter creation — otherwise `ERR_VERIFICATION_DOES_NOT_COVER_ENCOUNTER` (422).
28. Global uniqueness holds for a grantee's `grant_number`, a patient's `mrn` and a guideline's `guideline_year`, on grantee registration, patient registration and guideline publication — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY` (409).
29. Uniqueness within the parent holds for a service definition's `service_code` within its grantee, a tier's `tier_label` and `sort_order` within its schedule, a report's `reporting_year` within its grantee and a measure's `measure_code` within its report, on service-definition creation, discount-tier creation, report creation and quality-measure attachment — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_CHILD_KEY` (409).
30. The conditional seat fields agree with `seat_type`, on board-member seating — otherwise `ERR_SEAT_FIELD_MISMATCH` (422).
31. The conditional tier fields agree with `discount_kind`, on discount-tier creation — otherwise `ERR_TIER_DISCOUNT_MISMATCH` (422).
32. The tier ladder is well formed, on fee-schedule adoption — otherwise `ERR_TIER_BANDS_INVALID` (422).
33. The encounter's site is `IN_SCOPE`, on encounter creation and on encounter billing — otherwise `ERR_SITE_NOT_IN_SCOPE` (409).
34. The encounter's site is not administrative, on encounter creation — otherwise `ERR_ADMINISTRATIVE_SITE_NOT_BILLABLE` (422).
35. The encounter's service is `IN_SCOPE`, on encounter creation — otherwise `ERR_SERVICE_NOT_IN_SCOPE` (409).
36. An adopted fee schedule covers the reference date, on encounter creation and on income-verification adjudication — otherwise `ERR_NO_EFFECTIVE_FEE_SCHEDULE` (409).
37. Every federal poverty guideline year the calculation needs has been published, on income-verification adjudication and on fee-schedule adoption — otherwise `ERR_POVERTY_GUIDELINE_MISSING` (422).
38. Baseline-scope admission is still open, on service-site creation, service-definition creation and baseline locking — otherwise `ERR_SCOPE_BASELINE_LOCKED` (409).
39. Seating this member would not push `seated_board_count` past its ceiling, on board-member seating — otherwise `ERR_BOARD_SEAT_LIMIT` (409).
40. The report has been computed and carries at least one included measure, on report submission — otherwise `ERR_UDS_REPORT_NOT_COMPUTED` (409).
41. `nominal_fee_cents` does not exceed the grantee's `nominal_fee_cap_cents`, on fee-schedule adoption — otherwise `ERR_NOMINAL_FEE_ABOVE_CAP` (422).
42. `mco_paid_cents` does not exceed the encounter's `pps_encounter_rate_cents`, on payment recording — otherwise `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_PPS_RATE` (422).

Two consequences of this ordering deserve emphasis. Because the scope-freeze check at rung 15 sits above the governance check at rung 19 and the schedule-staleness check at rung 20, a frozen grantee whose board is also deficient reports `ERR_SCOPE_CHANGE_FROZEN`, not `ERR_GOVERNANCE_DEFICIENT`. And because the state check at rung 11 sits above every safety and fiscal rule, an action aimed at a row in the wrong state always reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, even when a later rule would also have rejected it.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up a Grantee and the Notice-of-Award Baseline Scope

A `PlatformAdmin` brings a Section 330 grantee onto the platform by registering a **health center** with its `legal_name`, its HRSA `grant_number`, its prospective payment system base encounter rate `pps_base_rate_cents`, and its per-encounter Medicare ceiling `medicare_upper_limit_cents`. Both rate fields are amounts in cents and both must be greater than zero. A fourth field, `nominal_fee_cap_cents`, is optional: it is the regulatory ceiling that a fee schedule's nominal charge may not exceed, it must be greater than zero, and when the registration omits it the grantee is created with a cap of 2,500 cents — that is, twenty-five dollars. The `grant_number` is unique across all grantees; a second registration reusing one is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY`.

A freshly registered grantee is deliberately in the weakest possible posture. `scope_baseline_locked` is false, meaning the Notice-of-Award baseline listing is still open. `open_condition_count`, `in_scope_site_count`, `in_scope_service_count`, `seated_board_count` and `wraparound_receivable_cents` are all zero and `patient_seat_pct` is 0. `operational_status` is `ACTIVE` and `scope_posture` is `OPEN`, because no condition sits on the award yet. `governance_posture`, however, is `GOVERNANCE_DEFICIENT`: an empty board cannot hold a patient majority, so scope expansion is blocked until seats are filled. Grantees can later be listed and filtered by `grant_number`, `operational_status` and `governance_posture`.

The federal poverty guideline table is published separately and globally, not per grantee. Each published row names a `guideline_year` — unique across the table, and accepted only in the range 2000 through 2100 inclusive — together with `base_amount_cents`, the annual guideline amount in cents for a household of one, and `increment_cents`, the additional annual amount in cents for each household member beyond the first. Both amounts must be greater than zero, and a duplicate `guideline_year` is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY`. A published guideline year is permanently immutable; there is no way to correct one. Publishing a *later* guideline year has a system-wide side effect that surprises operators the first time they see it: every adopted fee schedule whose `fpg_year` now trails the newest published year immediately reads as out of date, for the reason given in section 3.3. Guidelines can be listed and filtered by `guideline_year`.

Sites come next. A **service site** is created under its grantee with a `site_name`, a `site_type`, an `address_line` and `weekly_service_hours`, the scheduled service hours per week, which must be zero or greater. The `site_type` is one of `SERVICE_DELIVERY`, `INTERMITTENT` or `ADMINISTRATIVE`; the last of these is an important distinction, because an `ADMINISTRATIVE` site may never carry an encounter and an `INTERMITTENT` site attracts a reduced encounter rate. The site's owning grantee may be given explicitly as `health_center_id` in the body or left to be taken from the path. By default a new site is `PROPOSED` — listed as a proposal, not yet part of the scope of project. Setting the optional `in_initial_scope` flag to true on creation admits the site straight to `IN_SCOPE` as part of the Notice-of-Award baseline; the server then stamps `in_scope_since` with the logical clock, recomputes the grantee's `in_scope_site_count` and increments the grantee's `version` by one. Baseline admission is only available while `scope_baseline_locked` is false; once the baseline is locked, `in_initial_scope` set to true is rejected with `ERR_SCOPE_BASELINE_LOCKED`, and sites may only reach scope through an approved change-in-scope request. Sites may be listed for a grantee and filtered by `status` and `site_type`.

Services are listed the same way. A **service definition** carries a `service_code`, unique within its grantee, a `service_category` of either `REQUIRED_PRIMARY` or `ADDITIONAL`, and a `delivery_method` naming the Form 5A column it is listed under: `COLUMN_I_DIRECT`, `COLUMN_II_CONTRACT` or `COLUMN_III_REFERRAL`. Like a site, it accepts an optional `health_center_id` and an optional `in_initial_scope`. Without the flag a service definition sits at `PENDING`; with it, and only while the baseline is unlocked, it goes directly to `IN_SCOPE`, which recomputes the grantee's `in_scope_service_count` and increments the grantee's `version` by one. A repeated `service_code` within one grantee is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CHILD_KEY`. Service definitions may be listed for a grantee and filtered by `status` and `service_category`.

When the baseline listing is complete, the `PlatformAdmin` locks it. Locking sets `scope_baseline_locked` to true and increments the grantee's `version` by one. It is a once-only action: a second attempt to lock an already-locked baseline is rejected with `ERR_SCOPE_BASELINE_LOCKED`.

Beyond the baseline path, a site moves through a small lifecycle of its own. `PROPOSED → IN_SCOPE` is the baseline admission just described, and it is also the effect of an approved add-site change request. `PROPOSED → ON_HOLD` happens on the server's initiative when the change-in-scope request targeting the site is parked by a scope freeze; the site's `hold_reason` is then set to `SCOPE_FROZEN`, which is the only non-null value that field ever takes. `ON_HOLD → PROPOSED` is the reverse, clearing `hold_reason`, and fires when the request targeting the site leaves the parked state — the two edges together form a cycle a site can traverse more than once. A `ProjectOfficer` may take an in-scope site out of service after an operational visit, moving it `IN_SCOPE → SUSPENDED`; this recomputes the grantee's `in_scope_site_count`, recomputes `governance_posture` (because, as section 3.2 explains, a patient board seat is only qualified by an encounter at an in-scope site) and increments the grantee's `version` by one. The same officer may reverse it, moving the site `SUSPENDED → IN_SCOPE`, which re-stamps `in_scope_since` with the logical clock and again recomputes `in_scope_site_count` and `governance_posture` with a `version` increment. Finally, `IN_SCOPE → REMOVED` is terminal and is reached only through the approved removal cascade of section 3.8. A service definition's lifecycle is simpler: `PENDING → IN_SCOPE` on baseline admission or an approved add-service request, and `IN_SCOPE → OUT_OF_SCOPE` on an approved remove-service request, each recomputing `in_scope_service_count` and incrementing the grantee's `version` by one. `OUT_OF_SCOPE` is terminal.

### 3.2. Patient-Majority Governance and the Board Roster

Section 330 requires that the majority of a health center's governing board be patients of that health center, and this platform treats the board roster as a set of numbered, capacity-one temporal resources. A `ComplianceOfficer` seats a **board member** on a grantee's board by supplying a `seat_number` of at least 1, a `seat_type`, a `term_start` and a `term_end`, optionally naming the owning grantee as `health_center_id` in the body. A seated member's `status` begins at `SEATED` and its `resignation_date` begins null.

The `seat_type` drives three conditional fields, and getting the combination wrong is rejected with `ERR_SEAT_FIELD_MISMATCH`. A `PATIENT` seat must name the occupant's `patient_id` and must leave `expertise_category` null. A `NON_PATIENT` seat must carry an `expertise_category` — one of `FINANCE`, `LEGAL`, `COMMUNITY` or `HEALTH_PROFESSION` — and must leave `patient_id` null. The boolean `derives_over_10pct_income_from_healthcare`, which records that a member draws more than ten percent of annual income from the health care industry, defaults to false and may only be set true on a `NON_PATIENT` seat. A `PATIENT` seat's `patient_id` must additionally name a patient with at least one non-`VOID` encounter at a site of that same grantee; a patient who has never been seen there cannot hold a patient seat, and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_CROSS_GRANTEE_REFERENCE`.

Terms are validated in two ways. `term_start` must be at or before `term_end`, or the seating is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_TERM`. And a `seat_number` of one grantee may be held by at most one seated member at any instant. Because terms are closed at both ends, touching terms collide: if an existing seated member of that seat has a `term_end` exactly equal to the new member's `term_start`, the new seating is rejected with `ERR_BOARD_SEAT_OVERLAP`. There is also a hard roster ceiling — a seating that would push `seated_board_count` above twenty-five seats is rejected with `ERR_BOARD_SEAT_LIMIT`. Board seats may be listed and filtered by `health_center_id`, `patient_id`, `status`, `seat_type` and `seat_number`.

A `ComplianceOfficer` also retires a seat. Resigning a member supplies a `resignation_date`, which must be at or after that member's `term_start`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_TERM`. The transition is `SEATED → RESIGNED`; it truncates `term_end` down to the `resignation_date`, thereby freeing the `seat_number` for a new term, and it recomputes the grantee's `seated_board_count`, `patient_seat_pct` and `governance_posture` with a `version` increment. `RESIGNED` is terminal.

The patient-majority verdict itself is computed in six steps, and it is worth reading them as a whole because a great deal in this system gates on the result.

First, the *seated set* is every board member of the grantee whose `status` is `SEATED`; `seated_board_count` is the size of that set. Resigned members leave both the numerator and the denominator entirely — they are not counted as non-patient seats.

Second, a seated seat is a **qualified patient seat**, recorded on the member as `patient_seat_qualified` being true, when all of the following hold: its `seat_type` is `PATIENT`; its `patient_id` is not null; and there exists an encounter for that patient whose `pps_claim_status` is anything other than `VOID`, whose `encounter_date` is at or after the instant twenty-four months (two years) before the logical clock, and whose service site has `status` equal to `IN_SCOPE` at the moment of evaluation. The recency boundary is inclusive: an encounter dated exactly twenty-four months before the clock still qualifies the seat. A voided encounter never qualifies a seat, and an encounter at a suspended or removed site stops qualifying one — which is why suspending a site recomputes governance.

Third, three tallies are taken over the seated set: `qualified_patient_seat_count`, the number of seats qualified by step two; `non_patient_seat_count`, the number of seats whose `seat_type` is `NON_PATIENT`; and `healthcare_industry_seat_count`, the number of non-patient seats whose `derives_over_10pct_income_from_healthcare` is true.

Fourth, `patient_seat_pct` is 0.00 when the seated set is empty, and otherwise the qualified patient seat count divided by the seated board count, multiplied by 100, rounded half up to two decimal places.

Fifth, `governance_posture` is `GOVERNANCE_COMPLIANT` when *all four* of these hold, and `GOVERNANCE_DEFICIENT` otherwise: the seated board count is at least 3; the seated board count is at most 25; `patient_seat_pct` is at least 51.00 — a rounded value of exactly 51.00 satisfies the majority, it need not exceed it; and `healthcare_industry_seat_count` is at most half of `non_patient_seat_count` with any fraction discarded, so a board with three non-patient seats may carry at most one healthcare-industry seat. The empty-board case therefore lands on `GOVERNANCE_DEFICIENT` through a `patient_seat_pct` of 0.00 rather than through a division by zero, and an empty board is never treated as vacuously holding a majority.

Sixth, a snapshot rule breaks what would otherwise be a circular dependency. When this posture *gates* a transition — and it gates exactly one, the approval of a change-in-scope request — it is evaluated on the site statuses as they stand *before* that request's own site mutation. The posture is then recomputed on the post-transition statuses as the last side effect of the same request. Without this rule, "a site becomes in scope only if governance is compliant" and "governance depends on encounters at in-scope sites" would chase each other.

The posture is recomputed whenever an input to it can have moved: on seating a board member, on resigning one, on recording an encounter (a new encounter can newly qualify a patient seat), on any change to a service site's status, and during the compliance sweep. A read-only governance summary is also available for a grantee, exposing every intermediate the majority test uses — `health_center_id`, `seated_board_count`, `qualified_patient_seat_count`, `non_patient_seat_count`, `healthcare_industry_seat_count`, `patient_seat_pct`, `governance_posture` and the grantee's current `version` — so that an operator can see *why* a board reads deficient rather than merely that it does.

### 3.3. The Sliding Fee Schedule, Its Tier Ladder, and the Annual Review

A **fee schedule** is the grantee's sliding fee discount schedule for a period of time. A `ComplianceOfficer` drafts one under a grantee with an `fpg_year` — the `guideline_year` of the published poverty guideline table whose amounts the tiers were built from, matched by year value rather than by identifier — an `effective_start`, an `effective_end`, a `nominal_fee_cents` amount of zero or more, and optionally the `board_vote_date` on which the patient-majority board voted to adopt it. The owning grantee may be named as `health_center_id` in the body or taken from the path. The period is half-open, and `effective_start` must be strictly earlier than `effective_end`; otherwise the draft is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PERIOD`. A new draft has `status` `DRAFT`, `review_status` `CURRENT`, `last_reviewed_at` null, `adopted_at` null, `adopted_by_user_id` null, `reviewed_against_guideline_year` null and `superseded_by_fee_schedule_id` null. Schedules may be listed and filtered by `health_center_id`, by `superseded_by_fee_schedule_id`, by `status`, by `review_status` and by `fpg_year`.

Onto a draft the same officer hangs a ladder of **discount tiers**, each one a band of federal-poverty-guideline percentage. A tier carries a `tier_label` unique within its schedule, an `fpg_min_pct` of zero or more as the inclusive lower bound of the band, an `fpg_max_pct` as the exclusive upper bound (null meaning unbounded above), a `discount_kind`, and a `sort_order` of at least 1 that is likewise unique within the schedule. The owning schedule may be named as `fee_schedule_id` in the body or taken from the path. The `discount_kind` decides how the band converts a gross charge into a patient charge and is one of `NOMINAL`, meaning the patient owes the flat nominal fee, `PERCENT_DISCOUNT`, meaning a fraction of the gross charge is discounted, or `NONE`, meaning no discount applies. `discount_pct` is required when and only when `discount_kind` is `PERCENT_DISCOUNT`, in which case it must be strictly greater than 0 and strictly less than 1; for `NOMINAL` and `NONE` it must be null. A mismatch here is rejected with `ERR_TIER_DISCOUNT_MISMATCH`, and a repeated `tier_label` or `sort_order` within one schedule is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CHILD_KEY`. Notably, the *shape of the ladder as a whole* is not checked while tiers are being added one at a time; a half-built ladder with gaps is a legitimate intermediate state. The whole ladder is validated once, at adoption.

Adoption is a board act. A `BoardChair` who holds a seated seat on that grantee's board adopts a draft schedule, supplying `expected_health_center_version` as described in section 2.11. Adoption performs, in addition to the ladder check, three substantive checks. The schedule's `nominal_fee_cents` must not exceed the grantee's `nominal_fee_cap_cents`, or adoption is rejected with `ERR_NOMINAL_FEE_ABOVE_CAP`. No other adopted schedule of the same grantee may overlap this one's half-open period — with one carefully chosen exception: if an existing adopted schedule has an `effective_start` exactly equal to this one's, it is *superseded* rather than treated as a conflict, which is how a board replaces a schedule it has already voted on. Any other overlap is rejected with `ERR_FEE_SCHEDULE_PERIOD_OVERLAP`, while adjacency — an existing schedule whose `effective_end` equals the new schedule's `effective_start` — is always permitted. And the poverty guideline row for the schedule's `fpg_year` must have been published, or adoption is rejected with `ERR_POVERTY_GUIDELINE_MISSING`.

The ladder check at adoption, which reports `ERR_TIER_BANDS_INVALID`, requires all of the following of the schedule's tiers: at least two bands exist; sorting the bands by ascending `sort_order` also sorts them by ascending `fpg_min_pct`; the lowest band's `fpg_min_pct` is 0; each subsequent band's `fpg_min_pct` equals the previous band's `fpg_max_pct`, so that on the half-open convention the ladder has neither a gap nor an overlap; and exactly one band — the one with the highest `sort_order` — has a null `fpg_max_pct`.

A successful adoption moves the schedule `DRAFT → ADOPTED` and stamps `adopted_at` and `last_reviewed_at` with the logical clock, records the adopting chair's identifier in `adopted_by_user_id`, and sets `reviewed_against_guideline_year` to the schedule's own `fpg_year` — adoption *is* the first annual review. Any adopted schedule of the same grantee sharing this schedule's `effective_start` moves `ADOPTED → SUPERSEDED` and has its `superseded_by_fee_schedule_id` pointed at the newly adopted schedule. The new schedule's `review_status` is then recomputed, and the grantee's `version` increments by one. Supersession never rewrites history: encounters already recorded against the superseded schedule keep the `fee_schedule_id` they pinned. `SUPERSEDED` is terminal.

The 365-day review clock is the second half of the story. A `ComplianceOfficer` records an **annual review** of a schedule by supplying `reviewed_against_guideline_year`, the guideline year the tiers were just compared against. The review stamps `last_reviewed_at` with the logical clock, stores the supplied year, and recomputes `review_status`.

`review_status` is derived in a pinned precedence and takes one of three values. First, let the *latest published guideline year* be the greatest `guideline_year` present in the poverty guideline table, and let *elapsed days* be the whole number of days from the schedule's `last_reviewed_at` to the logical clock, truncated downward. If the schedule's `fpg_year` is less than the latest published guideline year, `review_status` is `OUT_OF_DATE`. Otherwise, if elapsed days is greater than 365, `review_status` is `REVIEW_DUE`. Otherwise `review_status` is `CURRENT`. The precedence direction matters: guideline staleness dominates elapsed time, so a schedule reviewed yesterday against last year's guideline table reads `OUT_OF_DATE`, not `CURRENT`. The day boundary is strict — 365 elapsed days is still `CURRENT`, and 366 is `REVIEW_DUE`. This posture is recomputed on adoption, on annual review, during the compliance sweep, and on every read of a schedule, so publishing a new guideline year is visible in a schedule's `review_status` without any write against the schedule itself.

Finally, an adopted schedule whose period has run out is retired by the compliance sweep: `ADOPTED → EXPIRED` once `effective_end` is at or before the logical clock, with `review_status` recomputed at the same time. `EXPIRED` is terminal.

### 3.4. Patient Intake, Income Documentation, and Discount Tier Assignment

A `PatientServicesRep` registers a **patient** with an `mrn` — the medical record number, unique across all patients and non-empty — a `full_name`, and a `household_size` of at least 1 recording the declared family size of record. A duplicate `mrn` is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY`. A new patient starts with `sliding_fee_status` `UNVERIFIED` and `patient_balance_cents` at 0. Patients may be listed and filtered by `mrn` and `sliding_fee_status`.

The same representative submits an **income verification** under a patient, carrying `declared_annual_income_cents` (zero or more), the `household_size` documented for *this* verification (at least 1), a `documentation_type` and an `effective_date`, plus the mandatory `expected_patient_version` of section 2.11 and optionally `patient_id` when it is not being taken from the path. The documented `household_size` is intentionally allowed to differ from the patient's own `household_size` — a family of four last year may be a family of five this year — and every percentage computation below uses the verification's own value, never the patient's. `documentation_type` is one of `TAX_RETURN`, `PAY_STUBS`, `W2`, `SELF_DECLARATION` or `REFUSED`. On submission the row's `status` is `SUBMITTED`, its `resubmit_round` is 0, its `submitted_by_user_id` is the acting operator's identifier, its `expires_at` is set to exactly 365 days after `effective_date` — making the verification's closed window run from `effective_date` through `expires_at` inclusive — and the patient's `version` increments by one. Verifications may be listed and filtered by `patient_id`, by `assigned_discount_tier_id`, by `supersedes_income_verification_id` and by `status`.

Adjudication belongs to an `EligibilityVerifier`, and it is a two-way fork driven by a `decision` field carrying either `VERIFY` or `MARK_INSUFFICIENT`. Either way the acting verifier must not be the operator recorded in `submitted_by_user_id`, on pain of `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

Choosing `MARK_INSUFFICIENT` moves the row `SUBMITTED → INSUFFICIENT_DOCUMENTATION`. It stores the supplied `insufficiency_reason` and records the adjudicating operator in `verified_by_user_id`, and it deliberately leaves every computed field null — `fpg_percent`, `uds_fpg_percent` and `assigned_discount_tier_id` are untouched. No charge recomputation whatsoever fires on this branch.

Choosing `VERIFY` moves the row `SUBMITTED → VERIFIED` and does a great deal. It is refused outright when the verification's `documentation_type` is `REFUSED`: a refusal to document income can never be adjudicated as verified — it may only be marked insufficient — and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_DOCUMENTATION_REFUSED`.

Verification first computes the two federal-poverty-guideline percentages. For a given guideline year and household size, the *guideline amount* is that year's `base_amount_cents` plus its `increment_cents` multiplied by one less than the household size. The percentage for a year is then the verification's `declared_annual_income_cents` divided by that guideline amount, multiplied by 100, rounded half up to two decimal places. Two variants of exactly this computation run over exactly the same verification row, and they differ only in which guideline year they read — which is why they routinely disagree.

The **schedule-year** variant is the one that drives money. Its year is the `fpg_year` of the adopted fee schedule that covers the verification's `effective_date` for that patient's grantee — that is, the year the adopted sliding fee schedule's bands were actually built from, *not* the calendar year of the visit. Its result is written to `fpg_percent` and the year it used is recorded in `guideline_year_used`. If no adopted schedule covers `effective_date` on the half-open convention, the adjudication is rejected with `ERR_NO_EFFECTIVE_FEE_SCHEDULE`.

The **intake-year** variant is the one that drives reporting. Its year is simply the UTC calendar year of the verification's `effective_date`. Its result is written to `uds_fpg_percent` and the year it used is recorded in `uds_guideline_year_used`. Because an adopted schedule commonly lags the calendar year, the same verification often yields one percentage for tier assignment and a different percentage for the annual report — and, as section 2.6 notes, the two are compared against band conventions that also differ.

If the poverty guideline row for *either* required year has not been published, the verification cannot be verified and the request is rejected with `ERR_POVERTY_GUIDELINE_MISSING`.

With `fpg_percent` in hand, the server assigns the discount tier. It takes the adopted fee schedule covering `effective_date` and finds the single tier of *that* schedule whose `fpg_min_pct` is less than or equal to `fpg_percent` and whose `fpg_max_pct` is either null or strictly greater than `fpg_percent`. Tiers belonging to draft or superseded schedules are never consulted. The chosen tier's identifier goes to `assigned_discount_tier_id` and its label is denormalised into `assigned_tier_label` for reporting. The half-open band convention has a consequence that must not be smoothed over: a `fpg_percent` of exactly 100.00 lands in the band that *starts* at 100 — the partial-discount band of a typical ladder — and never in the full-discount band that ends there.

Verification then fires a five-hop cascade, and every hop is required.

The first hop supersedes history. Any *other* income verification of the same patient whose `status` is `VERIFIED` and whose closed window overlaps this one's moves `VERIFIED → SUPERSEDED`, and this row's `supersedes_income_verification_id` is set to that row's identifier. The superseded row's owning patient has `sliding_fee_status` recomputed.

The second hop rewrites already-billed charges. Every encounter of that patient whose `pps_claim_status` is `BILLED` or `PAID` and whose `encounter_date` falls inside the newly verified closed window is recomputed under the **final** variant of the patient-charge calculation of section 3.5. For each such encounter the server rewrites `patient_charge_cents`, `charge_basis`, `pinned_discount_tier_id` and `income_verification_id`, records the difference between the newly computed charge and the previously stored one in `charge_adjustment_cents`, and moves the claim to `PENDING_ADJUSTMENT` — from `BILLED → PENDING_ADJUSTMENT` or from `PAID → PENDING_ADJUSTMENT` as the case may be.

The third hop restores the ledger. Because those encounters' `patient_charge_cents` changed, the patient's `patient_balance_cents` is recomputed from the conservation rule of section 2.8 and the patient's `version` increments by one.

The fourth hop revisits the wraparound residual. Because those encounters changed, `wraparound_due_cents` is recomputed for each of them whose `payer_type` is `MEDICAID_MCO`; any such encounter whose `wraparound_status` is `CLAIMED` and whose recomputed residual differs from the amount already claimed moves `CLAIMED → REBILL_REQUIRED`.

The fifth hop rolls the change up to the grantee. Because residuals moved, `wraparound_receivable_cents` is recomputed and the grantee's `version` increments by one.

The patient's own `sliding_fee_status` is recomputed as the last act. It reads `VERIFIED` when the patient has an income verification whose `status` is `VERIFIED` and whose closed window contains the logical clock; `EXPIRED` when the patient's most recent verified verification has since been swept to `EXPIRED`; and `UNVERIFIED` otherwise.

A verification marked insufficient can be repaired. The submitting representative resubmits it, always supplying a fresh `documentation_type` and optionally revised `declared_annual_income_cents` and `household_size`, which overwrite the stored values. The transition is `INSUFFICIENT_DOCUMENTATION → SUBMITTED`; it increments `resubmit_round` by one, clears both `insufficiency_reason` and `verified_by_user_id`, and re-records `submitted_by_user_id` as the acting operator. The loop is capped: resubmission is only permitted while `resubmit_round` is below 3, so a verification may traverse the insufficiency cycle three times and no more. A fourth attempt is rejected with `ERR_LOOP_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`.

Two terminal fates close the verification lifecycle. `VERIFIED → SUPERSEDED` is the server-initiated hop already described. `VERIFIED → EXPIRED` is applied by the compliance sweep once `expires_at` is at or before the logical clock, and it sets the owning patient's `sliding_fee_status` to `EXPIRED`. Expiry pointedly does *not* retro-adjust encounters that were already billed under the verification — the discount was correct when it was applied, and a lapsed document does not claw it back.

### 3.5. Recording an Encounter: Selectors, the Provisional Charge, and the PPS Rate

A `BillingSpecialist` records an **encounter** — a visit — by naming the `patient_id` of the patient seen, the `service_site_id` of the site of service, the `service_definition_id` of the service rendered, the `encounter_date`, the `payer_type` of record, and the undiscounted `gross_charge_cents`, which must be greater than zero. The `payer_type` is one of `SELF_PAY`, `MEDICAID_MCO`, `MEDICAID_FFS`, `MEDICARE` or `PRIVATE`. A seventh field, `income_verification_id`, is optional and is discussed below.

Before anything is computed, the encounter must clear a set of scope and calendar gates. The `encounter_date` must not be after the logical clock — there are no future dates of service — or the request is rejected with `ERR_ENCOUNTER_DATE_IN_FUTURE`. The named site must have `status` equal to `IN_SCOPE`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_SITE_NOT_IN_SCOPE`. A site whose `site_type` is `ADMINISTRATIVE` may never carry an encounter at all, and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_ADMINISTRATIVE_SITE_NOT_BILLABLE`. The named service definition must likewise have `status` equal to `IN_SCOPE`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_SERVICE_NOT_IN_SCOPE`. And the site and the service must belong to one and the same grantee, or the request is rejected with `ERR_CROSS_GRANTEE_REFERENCE`.

Two **selectors** then run, and both are server-side policies rather than client choices.

The *fee-schedule selector* picks the schedule the encounter will be priced against. Its candidate set is every fee schedule whose `health_center_id` equals the site's grantee, whose `status` is `ADOPTED`, and whose half-open period contains the `encounter_date` — that is, whose `effective_start` is at or before the `encounter_date` and whose `effective_end` is strictly after it. Candidates are ranked by `effective_start` descending, and a tie on `effective_start` is broken by `created_at` descending. If the candidate set is empty, the encounter cannot be priced and the request is rejected with `ERR_NO_EFFECTIVE_FEE_SCHEDULE`. The winner's identifier is pinned onto the encounter as `fee_schedule_id`, permanently.

The *income-verification selector* picks the documentation the discount will be based on, and it runs only when the client omits `income_verification_id`. Its candidate set is every income verification whose `patient_id` equals the encounter's patient, whose `status` is `VERIFIED`, and whose closed window contains the `encounter_date` — that is, whose `effective_date` is at or before the `encounter_date` and whose `expires_at` is at or after it. Candidates are ranked by `effective_date` descending, with ties broken by `created_at` descending. An empty candidate set is *not* an error here; it routes the charge computation into its unverified branch. When the client does supply `income_verification_id` explicitly, the named verification must have `status` equal to `VERIFIED` and its closed window must contain the `encounter_date`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_VERIFICATION_DOES_NOT_COVER_ENCOUNTER`.

The **patient charge** is then computed in eight steps, and the placement of its single rounding is part of the contract.

Step one handles third-party payers. If `payer_type` is `MEDICARE`, `MEDICAID_FFS` or `PRIVATE`, then `sliding_fee_applied` is false, `charge_basis` is `THIRD_PARTY`, `pinned_discount_tier_id` is null, and `patient_charge_cents` is 0. No sliding fee logic runs at all for these payers.

Step two handles the absence of documentation. If the income-verification selector produced no row (and none was supplied), then `sliding_fee_applied` is false, `charge_basis` is `FULL_CHARGE_UNVERIFIED`, both `pinned_discount_tier_id` and `income_verification_id` are null, and `patient_charge_cents` is the full `gross_charge_cents` when `payer_type` is `SELF_PAY` and 0 for any other remaining payer.

Step three selects the tier. Using the chosen verification's `fpg_percent`, the server picks the tier of the *pinned* fee schedule under the half-open band rule of section 3.4.

Step four converts the gross charge into a raw amount according to the tier's `discount_kind`. When the kind is `NOMINAL`, the raw amount is the pinned schedule's `nominal_fee_cents`. When the kind is `PERCENT_DISCOUNT`, the raw amount is `gross_charge_cents` multiplied by the quantity one minus the tier's `discount_pct`, rounded half up to a whole cent. When the kind is `NONE`, the raw amount is `gross_charge_cents` unchanged. This is the *only* rounding in the whole calculation.

Step five caps the raw amount at the gross charge: the patient may never owe more than the undiscounted charge.

Step six applies the nominal floor. If the tier's `discount_kind` is `PERCENT_DISCOUNT` and the raw amount has fallen below the pinned schedule's `nominal_fee_cents`, the raw amount is raised to the `nominal_fee_cents`, but still capped at `gross_charge_cents` — so a gross charge smaller than the nominal fee is never inflated to it.

Step seven applies the Medicaid managed-care clamp. If `payer_type` is `MEDICAID_MCO`, the raw amount is reduced to the pinned schedule's `nominal_fee_cents` when it exceeds it: a Medicaid managed-care patient pays at most the nominal fee.

Step eight records the outcome. `sliding_fee_applied` is true, `charge_basis` is `SLIDING_FEE_TIER`, `pinned_discount_tier_id` is the chosen tier's identifier, `income_verification_id` is the chosen verification's identifier, and `patient_charge_cents` is the raw amount as it stands after steps five, six and seven.

The clamps in steps five, six and seven operate on the already-rounded whole-cent integer from step four. Re-rounding after a clamp is forbidden, because it would produce a different answer for the same inputs.

This calculation has two variants, and both are used. The **provisional** variant runs at encounter creation, against whatever verifications exist at that moment — which is exactly why a self-pay patient with no covering verification is charged the full gross amount with `charge_basis` `FULL_CHARGE_UNVERIFIED`. The **final** variant runs in the retroactive cascade of section 3.4, after a covering verification exists: the same encounter with the same `gross_charge_cents` then yields a different `patient_charge_cents`, the difference is written to `charge_adjustment_cents` as the final amount minus the provisional one, and the claim moves to `PENDING_ADJUSTMENT`.

Separately from patient responsibility, the encounter's allowable **prospective payment system rate** is computed. Start from a multiplier of 1.00. If the encounter's service definition has `service_category` equal to `ADDITIONAL`, multiply the multiplier by 0.85 — an additional service is reimbursed at eighty-five percent of the base rate. If the encounter's service site has `site_type` equal to `INTERMITTENT`, multiply the multiplier by 0.90 — an intermittent site is reimbursed at ninety percent. Both reductions compound when both conditions hold. Then `pps_encounter_rate_cents` is the grantee's `pps_base_rate_cents` multiplied by the final multiplier, rounded half up to a whole cent — a single rounding, taken after all factors have multiplied, never once per factor. Finally, if `payer_type` is `MEDICARE`, the rate is reduced to the grantee's `medicare_upper_limit_cents` when it exceeds that ceiling. The Medicare clamp is applied *after* the rounding, to the rounded integer.

The order in which all of this evaluates is pinned, because the dependency graph looks circular at first reading — a waiver decision is only legal once the patient charge equals the nominal fee, the patient charge is only known once the tier is assigned, the tier is only known once the percentage is computed, and the wraparound residual is only known once the waiver outcome is known. The fixed order is: first the schedule-year percentage; second the tier assignment; third the patient charge through its eight steps including the Medicaid managed-care clamp; fourth the waiver eligibility test and the grant-or-deny decision; fifth the counted patient portion; sixth the wraparound residual. Every later recomputation re-enters at the fifth step with the first four frozen: a granted waiver never re-runs the tier assignment, and a retroactive verification never re-opens a decided waiver.

Creation of the encounter has several side effects beyond the pins and the two amounts. `pps_claim_status` starts at `UNBILLED` and `wraparound_status` starts at `NOT_APPLICABLE`; `charge_adjustment_cents`, `waived_amount_cents` and `mco_paid_cents` all start at 0; `claim_reference` and `void_reason` start null. The patient's `patient_balance_cents` increases by the encounter's `patient_charge_cents` and the patient's `version` increments by one. The grantee's `governance_posture` is recomputed — a brand-new encounter can newly qualify a patient board seat — and the grantee's `version` increments by one. Encounters may be listed and filtered by every pinned reference and both state dimensions: `patient_id`, `service_site_id`, `service_definition_id`, `fee_schedule_id`, `income_verification_id`, `pinned_discount_tier_id`, `pps_claim_status`, `wraparound_status` and `payer_type`.

### 3.6. Nominal-Fee Protection: The Inability-to-Pay Waiver

Section 330's inability-to-pay policy protects one specific amount: the nominal fee charged to the deepest-discount tier. It does not protect a partial-discount charge, and it does not protect a third-party or full-charge balance. Accordingly, a `PatientServicesRep` may request an **inability-to-pay waiver** against an encounter only when that encounter's `charge_basis` is `SLIDING_FEE_TIER` *and* its `patient_charge_cents` is exactly equal to the `nominal_fee_cents` of the fee schedule it pinned. Any other encounter is rejected with `ERR_WAIVER_NOT_ELIGIBLE`.

The request names a `waiver_reason` — one of `HOMELESS`, `NO_INCOME` or `HARDSHIP_ATTESTATION` — and may name `encounter_id` explicitly rather than taking it from the path. A new waiver has `status` `REQUESTED`, records the acting operator in `requested_by_user_id`, and starts with `waived_amount_cents` at 0, `decided_by_user_id` null and `decision_note` null. Requesting a waiver mutates nothing on the encounter and nothing on the patient's ledger. An encounter's waivers may be listed and filtered by `status`.

Deciding the waiver belongs to a `ComplianceOfficer` who is not the requester, and is driven by a `decision` field carrying `GRANT` or `DENY`, optionally with a `decision_note` that is stored either way. The officer recorded in `requested_by_user_id` may not decide the row, on pain of `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, and the officer's identifier is recorded in `decided_by_user_id`. A decision is only accepted while the underlying encounter's `pps_claim_status` is neither `RECONCILED` nor `VOID`; once the claim has been reconciled or voided the waiver can no longer move, and the attempt reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.

`REQUESTED → GRANTED` fires a three-hop cascade. The waiver's own `waived_amount_cents` is set to the encounter's `patient_charge_cents` and the same amount is copied onto the encounter's `waived_amount_cents`. In the first hop, because the encounter's `waived_amount_cents` changed, the patient's `patient_balance_cents` is reduced by that amount and the patient's `version` increments by one. In the second hop, because the counted patient portion has dropped to zero, the encounter's `wraparound_due_cents` is recomputed. In the third hop, because the residual changed while the encounter's `wraparound_status` was `CLAIMED`, that status moves `CLAIMED → REBILL_REQUIRED`, the grantee's `wraparound_receivable_cents` is recomputed, and the grantee's `version` increments by one. Notice what the grant does *not* touch: `pps_claim_status` is untouched, which is the clearest illustration that the encounter's two state dimensions move independently.

`REQUESTED → DENIED` is by comparison inert. The waiver's `waived_amount_cents` stays at 0, and no ledger movement and no wraparound recomputation occur. Both `GRANTED` and `DENIED` are terminal.

One granted waiver leaves a permanent protection on its encounter. A granted waiver means the patient was found unable to pay, so the encounter may never afterwards be voided for non-payment: an attempt to void it with `void_reason` equal to `NONPAYMENT` is rejected with `ERR_INABILITY_TO_PAY_PROTECTED`. Voids for other reasons remain available. Only a waiver whose `status` is `GRANTED` has this effect and only such a waiver zeroes the counted patient portion — a waiver still `REQUESTED`, or one `DENIED`, changes nothing.

### 3.7. Billing the Claim, Settling the Wraparound, and Compensating a Void

An encounter carries **two independent state dimensions** on the same row. `pps_claim_status` tracks the claim against the payer and moves through `UNBILLED`, `BILLED`, `PAID`, `PENDING_ADJUSTMENT`, `RECONCILED` and `VOID`, the last two being terminal. `wraparound_status` tracks the state wraparound payment and moves through `NOT_APPLICABLE`, `PENDING`, `CLAIMED`, `REBILL_REQUIRED` and `RECEIVED`. The two dimensions evolve independently: billing moves the claim without touching the wraparound, a granted waiver moves the wraparound without touching the claim, and only one operation — a confirmed wraparound receipt — deliberately couples them.

A `BillingSpecialist` bills the claim by supplying a `claim_reference`, the payer's claim identifier. The transition is `UNBILLED → BILLED`, and it requires that the encounter's site still have `status` equal to `IN_SCOPE`; billing an encounter at a site that has since left scope is rejected with `ERR_SITE_NOT_IN_SCOPE`. The same operation is also the rebilling edge: `PENDING_ADJUSTMENT → BILLED`, which overwrites the stored `claim_reference` with the new one. In both cases `wraparound_status` is left exactly as it was.

Posting the payer's payment supplies `mco_paid_cents`, the managed-care organisation payment, which must be zero or greater and must not exceed the encounter's `pps_encounter_rate_cents`; a payment above the allowable rate is rejected with `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_PPS_RATE`. The transition is `BILLED → PAID`. Posting the payment stores `mco_paid_cents`, recomputes `wraparound_due_cents`, and for an encounter whose `payer_type` is `MEDICAID_MCO` with a positive residual moves `wraparound_status` to `PENDING` (from `NOT_APPLICABLE` or from `PENDING` itself). For any *non*-managed-care payer, the same request additionally moves the claim `PAID → RECONCILED`, since no wraparound is owed and there is nothing left to settle. Either way the grantee's `wraparound_receivable_cents` is recomputed and its `version` increments by one.

The **wraparound residual** is computed as follows. If the encounter's `payer_type` is anything other than `MEDICAID_MCO`, then `wraparound_status` is `NOT_APPLICABLE` and `wraparound_due_cents` is 0. Otherwise, let the *counted patient portion* be 0 when a waiver with `status` equal to `GRANTED` exists on the encounter, and the encounter's `patient_charge_cents` when none does. Then `wraparound_due_cents` is the greater of 0 and the quantity `pps_encounter_rate_cents` minus `mco_paid_cents` minus the counted patient portion. The floor at zero matters: an over-paying managed-care organisation must never create a negative receivable.

Claiming the wraparound is the same operation as recording its receipt, distinguished by an optional `receipt_confirmed` flag. Without the flag, or with it false, the transition is `PENDING → CLAIMED` or `REBILL_REQUIRED → CLAIMED` — the second of these being the re-claim edge that closes the cycle after a residual changed underneath an outstanding claim. With `receipt_confirmed` set to true, the transition is `CLAIMED → RECEIVED`. A confirmed receipt is the one place the two dimensions couple: when the wraparound reaches `RECEIVED` while `pps_claim_status` is `PAID`, the claim also moves `PAID → RECONCILED` and the encounter's `patient_charge_cents` is thereafter frozen against any further recomputation — a reconciled encounter is never revisited by a retroactive verification. The claim may reach `RECONCILED` from `PAID` only while `wraparound_status` is `NOT_APPLICABLE` or `RECEIVED`. Each of these operations recomputes the grantee's `wraparound_receivable_cents` and increments the grantee's `version` by one.

Two of the claim dimension's edges are driven by the server rather than by a specialist. `BILLED → PENDING_ADJUSTMENT` and `PAID → PENDING_ADJUSTMENT` both fire when a retroactively verified income verification covers the encounter's `encounter_date`, with the full five-hop cascade described in section 3.4.

Voiding is the compensating action, and it removes the encounter from every conserved quantity. The specialist supplies a `void_reason`, one of `DUPLICATE`, `CODING_ERROR`, `NONPAYMENT` or `SITE_OUT_OF_SCOPE`. A void is accepted from four states — the edges are `UNBILLED → VOID`, `BILLED → VOID`, `PAID → VOID` and `PENDING_ADJUSTMENT → VOID` — and is refused for `void_reason` equal to `NONPAYMENT` when a granted waiver protects the encounter, per section 3.6. The `UNBILLED → VOID` edge is also driven by the server, as the third hop of the approved `REMOVE_SITE` cascade in section 3.8; the other three are operator-driven only. All four carry the same compensating side effects: the transition sets `pps_claim_status` to `VOID` and sets `charge_adjustment_cents` to the negative of the quantity `patient_charge_cents` minus `waived_amount_cents`. It reduces the patient's `patient_balance_cents` by that same quantity — with zero drift, so that an encounter and its void cancel exactly — and increments the patient's `version` by one. It sets `wraparound_status` to `NOT_APPLICABLE`, removing the encounter from the receivable base, then recomputes the grantee's `wraparound_receivable_cents` and its `governance_posture` (a voided encounter no longer qualifies a patient board seat) and increments the grantee's `version` by one. `VOID` is terminal.

### 3.8. Change-in-Scope Governance, Site Visit Findings, and the Scope Freeze

Once a grantee's baseline is locked, the scope of project changes only through a **change-in-scope request**. A `ComplianceOfficer` drafts one under a grantee with a `request_type`, a narrative `justification` addressed to the awarding agency, and exactly one polymorphic target; the owning grantee may be named as `health_center_id` or taken from the path. The `request_type` is one of `ADD_SITE`, `REMOVE_SITE`, `ADD_SERVICE` or `REMOVE_SERVICE`, and the pairing rule is strict: an `ADD_SITE` or `REMOVE_SITE` request must carry `target_site_id` and must leave `target_service_definition_id` null, while an `ADD_SERVICE` or `REMOVE_SERVICE` request must carry `target_service_definition_id` and must leave `target_site_id` null. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_SCOPE_TARGET_MISMATCH`, and the pairing is re-checked at submission as well as at drafting, because a request may sit in draft across other changes. A new request has `status` `DRAFT`, `revision_round` 0, `held_from_status` null, `reviewed_by_user_id` null, `decision_note` null, and records the drafting officer in `prepared_by_user_id`. Requests may be listed and filtered by `health_center_id`, `target_site_id`, `target_service_definition_id`, `status` and `request_type`.

Submission moves the request `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`. It is permitted only while the grantee's `scope_posture` is `OPEN`; a frozen grantee reports `ERR_SCOPE_CHANGE_FROZEN`. Submission touches the grantee — it increments the grantee's `version` by one without changing any grantee field — which is how a client holding a stale grantee version learns that scope work has moved. The same operation is also the loop edge `REVISION_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED`, which increments `revision_round` by one, clears `reviewed_by_user_id`, and likewise requires an open scope posture. The loop is capped: resubmission is permitted only while `revision_round` is below 3, and a fourth pass is rejected with `ERR_LOOP_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`.

A `BoardChair` holding a seated seat on that grantee's board then takes the request under review, moving it `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW` and recording the chair's identifier in `reviewed_by_user_id`. This too requires an open scope posture.

The decision is a three-way fork driven by a `decision` field carrying `APPROVE`, `REQUEST_REVISION` or `DENY`, with an optional `decision_note`. All three branches require that the request be `UNDER_REVIEW`, that the acting chair not be the officer recorded in `prepared_by_user_id`, and that the chair hold a seated seat.

The `APPROVE` branch carries three further preconditions. The grantee's `scope_posture` must be `OPEN`, or the decision reports `ERR_SCOPE_CHANGE_FROZEN`. The grantee's `governance_posture` must be `GOVERNANCE_COMPLIANT` when evaluated on the pre-transition snapshot described in section 3.2, or the decision reports `ERR_GOVERNANCE_DEFICIENT`. And the grantee's adopted fee schedule covering the logical clock must have a `review_status` other than `OUT_OF_DATE` — scope may not expand while the sliding fee schedule lags the current federal poverty guidelines — or the decision reports `ERR_FEE_SCHEDULE_OUT_OF_DATE`. A decision refused by any one of these three leaves the request exactly as it stood: still `UNDER_REVIEW`, with no `decision_note` stored and no hop of the cascade below fired.

Approval moves the request `UNDER_REVIEW → APPROVED`, which is terminal, stores the `decision_note`, and fires a five-hop cascade. The first hop mutates the target according to `request_type`: an `ADD_SITE` request moves its target site `PROPOSED → IN_SCOPE` and stamps `in_scope_since` with the logical clock; a `REMOVE_SITE` request moves its target site `IN_SCOPE → REMOVED`; an `ADD_SERVICE` request moves its target service definition `PENDING → IN_SCOPE`; a `REMOVE_SERVICE` request moves its target service definition `IN_SCOPE → OUT_OF_SCOPE`. The second hop follows from the first: because a target's status changed, the grantee's `in_scope_site_count` or `in_scope_service_count` is recomputed and the grantee's `version` increments by one. The third hop applies to `REMOVE_SITE` only: because a site left scope, every encounter at that site whose `pps_claim_status` is `UNBILLED` is voided — `pps_claim_status` becomes `VOID`, `void_reason` becomes `SITE_OUT_OF_SCOPE`, and `charge_adjustment_cents` becomes the negative of that encounter's `patient_charge_cents`. The fourth hop follows from the third: because those encounters were voided, each affected patient's `patient_balance_cents` is reduced by the voided encounter's `patient_charge_cents` less its `waived_amount_cents`, restoring the ledger invariant of section 2.8. The fifth hop also follows from the third: because those encounters were voided, they leave the receivable base and the grantee's `wraparound_receivable_cents` is recomputed. As the last side effect of the whole transition, the grantee's `governance_posture` is recomputed on the *post*-transition site statuses.

The `REQUEST_REVISION` branch moves the request `UNDER_REVIEW → REVISION_REQUESTED`, stores the `decision_note`, and mutates nothing else — no target status changes and no grantee counter changes. It is the loop edge that closes the cycle `REVISION_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW → REVISION_REQUESTED`.

The `DENY` branch moves the request `UNDER_REVIEW → DENIED`, which is terminal, and stores the `decision_note`. A denied add-site request leaves its target site sitting at `PROPOSED`; no counter changes and no cascade fires.

Operational oversight comes from the other direction. A `ProjectOfficer` issues a **site visit finding** against a grantee, naming the `requirement_code` of the Section 330 program requirement cited — for example a sliding-fee requirement code or a board-composition requirement code — a `severity`, a narrative `finding_summary`, and `cap_due_days`, the number of days allowed to obtain an accepted corrective action plan, which must be at least 1. The finding may optionally be scoped to one site through `service_site_id`, which when supplied must belong to the finding's grantee; leaving it null makes the finding grantee-wide. `severity` is either `CONDITION`, which places a condition on the award and makes the finding escalatable, or `PROGRESS_REPORT_ONLY`, which does not. A new finding has `status` `OPEN`, `escalated_at` null, records the issuing officer in `issued_by_user_id`, and has `cap_due_at` computed as its `created_at` plus `cap_due_days` days, evaluated on the logical clock. Findings may be listed and filtered by `health_center_id`, `service_site_id`, `status` and `severity`.

A `ComplianceOfficer` answers a finding with a **corrective action plan** carrying a `narrative`, a `milestone_count` of at least 1 recording how many remediation milestones are committed to, and a `target_completion_date`; the owning finding may be named as `site_visit_finding_id` or taken from the path. The committed date must not be after the parent finding's `cap_due_at`, or the draft is rejected with `ERR_CAP_TARGET_AFTER_DUE`. A new plan has `status` `DRAFT`, `submit_round` 0, `rejection_reason` null, `adjudicated_by_user_id` null, and records the preparing officer in `prepared_by_user_id`. Plans may be listed for a finding and filtered by `status`.

Submitting the plan moves it `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`, and is permitted only while the parent finding's `status` is `OPEN` or `ESCALATED`. The submission drives the parent finding to `CAP_SUBMITTED` — from `OPEN → CAP_SUBMITTED` or from `ESCALATED → CAP_SUBMITTED`. The second of those is important and easy to miss: a finding that has already escalated remains counted in the grantee's `open_condition_count` while its plan is under consideration, so submitting a plan does *not* by itself unfreeze scope; only an accepted plan does. The same operation is also the loop edge `REJECTED → SUBMITTED`, which increments `submit_round` by one, clears `rejection_reason` and `adjudicated_by_user_id`, and again drives the parent finding to `CAP_SUBMITTED`. This loop is capped tighter than the others: resubmission is permitted only while `submit_round` is below 2, and a third pass is rejected with `ERR_LOOP_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`.

Adjudication belongs to a `ProjectOfficer` who is not the plan's preparer, driven by a `decision` field carrying `ACCEPT` or `REJECT`, with an optional `rejection_reason`.

`ACCEPT` moves the plan `SUBMITTED → ACCEPTED`, which is terminal, records the adjudicating officer in `adjudicated_by_user_id`, and moves the parent finding `CAP_SUBMITTED → RESOLVED`, also terminal. That resolution fires a four-hop **compensating** cascade. The first hop recomputes the grantee's `open_condition_count` as the number of that grantee's findings whose `status` is `ESCALATED`, which matters when the resolved finding had itself been escalated. The second hop follows: because `open_condition_count` has reached zero, the grantee's `operational_status` returns `CONDITIONS_IMPOSED → ACTIVE`, its `scope_posture` returns `CHANGE_FROZEN → OPEN`, and its `version` increments by one. The third hop follows from the second: because scope reopened, every parked change-in-scope request of that grantee leaves `HELD` and returns to whatever status its `held_from_status` records — `HELD → SUBMITTED` when it records `SUBMITTED`, `HELD → UNDER_REVIEW` when it records `UNDER_REVIEW` — and `held_from_status` is cleared. The fourth hop follows from the third: because those requests left the parked state, every target site sitting at `ON_HOLD` returns `ON_HOLD → PROPOSED` and its `hold_reason` is cleared.

`REJECT` moves the plan `SUBMITTED → REJECTED`, stores the `rejection_reason`, and returns the parent finding to `OPEN` when that finding had *never* been escalated — the edge `CAP_SUBMITTED → OPEN` — while a finding that has been escalated at any point stays `ESCALATED` instead. A rejected plan may then loop back to submission, subject to its cap of two rounds.

The escalation edge itself is driven by the compliance sweep, not by any operator, and it is the deepest cascade in the system. A finding escalates — `OPEN → ESCALATED` or `CAP_SUBMITTED → ESCALATED` — when its `severity` is `CONDITION`, its `cap_due_at` is at or before the logical clock, and no corrective action plan of that finding has `status` equal to `ACCEPTED`. The first hop stamps `escalated_at` with the logical clock and recomputes the grantee's `open_condition_count`. The second hop follows: because that count has become greater than zero, the grantee's `operational_status` moves `ACTIVE → CONDITIONS_IMPOSED`. The third hop follows: because conditions are now imposed, the grantee's `scope_posture` moves `OPEN → CHANGE_FROZEN` and the grantee's `version` increments by one. The fourth hop follows: because scope froze, every change-in-scope request of that grantee whose `status` is `SUBMITTED` or `UNDER_REVIEW` is parked — `SUBMITTED → HELD` or `UNDER_REVIEW → HELD` — with the departed status recorded in `held_from_status` so that the compensating cascade can restore it exactly. The fifth hop follows from the fourth: because those requests were parked, every `ADD_SITE` request's target site sitting at `PROPOSED` moves `PROPOSED → ON_HOLD` with `hold_reason` set to `SCOPE_FROZEN`. Requests may only leave `HELD` while the grantee's `open_condition_count` is zero.

### 3.9. Uniform Data System Reporting and the Compliance Epoch

A `ComplianceOfficer` opens the annual **uniform data system report** for a grantee by naming a `reporting_year`, unique within that grantee, optionally with `health_center_id` in the body rather than taken from the path; a repeat is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CHILD_KEY`. A new report has `status` `DRAFT`, `computed_at` null, `return_round` 0, `submitted_by_user_id` null, `adjudicated_by_user_id` null, `return_reason` null, `composite_quality_rate_pct` null, and `total_patient_count`, `patients_at_or_below_100_count`, `patients_101_to_150_count`, `patients_151_to_200_count`, `patients_over_200_count`, `unverified_patient_count` and `included_measure_count` all at 0. Reports may be listed for a grantee and filtered by `status` and `reporting_year`.

Onto a report the officer attaches **quality measure results**, one per clinical measure. Each carries a `measure_code` unique within its report, a `numerator` and a `denominator` that must each be zero or greater, and a `benchmark_pct` between 0 and 100 inclusive giving the national benchmark the rate is compared against; the owning report may be named as `uds_report_id` or taken from the path. A freshly attached measure has `rate_pct` null, `inclusion_status` `INCLUDED` and `meets_benchmark` null — rates are only ever derived by the compute operation, never supplied. Measures may be listed for a report and filtered by `inclusion_status`.

Computing the report runs two aggregations. It also serves as the loop edge `RETURNED → DRAFT`, which increments `return_round` by one; that loop is capped at three, and a fourth pass is rejected with `ERR_LOOP_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. In every case computing stamps `computed_at` with the logical clock.

The **income distribution** aggregation first determines the reported patient set: the distinct `patient_id` values over encounters whose service site belongs to the report's grantee, whose `pps_claim_status` is anything other than `VOID`, and whose `encounter_date` falls in the report's `reporting_year`. `total_patient_count` is the size of that set. Then, for each reported patient, the server chooses exactly one *counted* verification: among that patient's income verifications whose `status` is `VERIFIED`, `SUPERSEDED` or `EXPIRED`, the one with the latest `effective_date` that is at or before the 31st of December of the `reporting_year`, with ties broken by `created_at` descending. Verifications still `SUBMITTED` or `INSUFFICIENT_DOCUMENTATION` are never counted, but superseded and expired ones are — the report is a record of what was documented during the year, not of what is currently in force. If a patient has no counted verification, `unverified_patient_count` increases by one. Otherwise the patient's counted `uds_fpg_percent` — the intake-year percentage, never `fpg_percent` — decides the band: at or below 100.00 increments `patients_at_or_below_100_count`; above 100.00 and at or below 150.00 increments `patients_101_to_150_count`; above 150.00 and at or below 200.00 increments `patients_151_to_200_count`; above 200.00 increments `patients_over_200_count`.

The **composite quality rate** aggregation walks the report's measures in ascending `created_at` order. For a measure whose `denominator` is 0, `rate_pct` is null, `inclusion_status` is `EXCLUDED_ZERO_DENOMINATOR` and `meets_benchmark` is null — a measure with no eligible population is excluded rather than scored as zero. For every other measure, `rate_pct` is the `numerator` divided by the `denominator`, multiplied by 100, rounded half up to one decimal place; `inclusion_status` is `INCLUDED`; and `meets_benchmark` is true when the rounded `rate_pct` is greater than or equal to `benchmark_pct`, so a rate exactly equal to the benchmark meets it. Then `included_measure_count` is the number of measures whose `inclusion_status` is `INCLUDED`. When that count is 0, `composite_quality_rate_pct` is null. Otherwise `composite_quality_rate_pct` is the sum of the included measures' `rate_pct` values divided by `included_measure_count`, rounded half up to one decimal place. The order of operations is pinned: each measure is rounded first and the already-rounded rates are then averaged. Pooling the numerators and denominators of all measures and computing a single rate is explicitly *not* this system's composite, and produces a different number from the same data.

Submitting the report moves it `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` and records the submitting officer in `submitted_by_user_id`. It is permitted only when `computed_at` is not null and `included_measure_count` is at least 1; an uncomputed report, or one whose every measure was excluded for a zero denominator, is rejected with `ERR_UDS_REPORT_NOT_COMPUTED`.

A `ProjectOfficer` adjudicates the submitted report through a `decision` field carrying `ACCEPT` or `RETURN`, with an optional `return_reason`. `ACCEPT` moves the report `SUBMITTED → ACCEPTED`, which is terminal, and records the officer in `adjudicated_by_user_id`. `RETURN` moves it `SUBMITTED → RETURNED` and stores the `return_reason`, from where recomputation reopens it as a draft.

The **compliance sweep** is an administrative capability, available to a `PlatformAdmin`, that advances the compliance epoch in a pinned four-step order; within each step rows are processed in ascending `created_at` order and never by identifier. Step one expires documentation: every income verification whose `status` is `VERIFIED` and whose `expires_at` is at or before the logical clock moves `VERIFIED → EXPIRED`, and the owning patient's `sliding_fee_status` becomes `EXPIRED`. Step two escalates findings: every site visit finding whose `severity` is `CONDITION`, whose `status` is `OPEN` or `CAP_SUBMITTED`, whose `cap_due_at` is at or before the logical clock, and which has no accepted corrective action plan, escalates and fires the five-hop escalation cascade of section 3.8. Step three retires schedules: every adopted fee schedule has its `review_status` recomputed, and those whose `effective_end` is at or before the logical clock move `ADOPTED → EXPIRED`. Step four recomputes grantees: every grantee touched by the first three steps, plus every grantee with board or site changes, has `governance_posture`, `patient_seat_pct`, `seated_board_count`, `in_scope_site_count`, `in_scope_service_count`, `open_condition_count`, `wraparound_receivable_cents`, `operational_status` and `scope_posture` recomputed, with a `version` increment for each grantee touched.

The step order is itself load-bearing: because escalations in step two freeze scope *before* step four recomputes posture, one sweep can both escalate a finding and report the resulting frozen posture in the same pass. The sweep is global across all grantees and idempotent at a fixed clock value — running it twice without moving the clock changes nothing the second time. It reports four counts: `expired_verification_count`, `escalated_finding_count`, `expired_fee_schedule_count` and `recomputed_health_center_count`.
