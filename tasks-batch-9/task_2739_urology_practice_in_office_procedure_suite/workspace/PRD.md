# Urology Procedure Suite API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A mid-size urology group runs its cystoscopy, urodynamics, prostate biopsy and office-ultrasound work out of a small suite of procedure rooms rather than a hospital operating theatre. The economics of that model are unforgiving: a room that sits idle during an unbudgeted turnover is lost revenue, a flexible cystoscope that goes onto a patient without a documented high-level disinfection is a reportable safety event, and a pathology result that nobody manages to communicate is both a clinical failure and a malpractice exposure. Today the practice coordinates all of this with a scheduling spreadsheet, a paper reprocessing binder, a shoebox of consignment packing slips and a shared inbox for surveillance recalls. Nothing reconciles, and no one can answer the question "is this case actually ready to start?" without walking the corridor.

This document specifies the API that replaces those artefacts with a single authoritative record. The service owns the room calendar and the arithmetic of occupancy — how long a case will take given the patient's anticoagulation and renal function, how long the room needs afterwards given the procedure's cleanup class, and whether the resulting block collides with another booking or with the operating urologist's own day. It owns the pre-procedure gate: which preparation instructions the patient acknowledged, whether prophylaxis was actually given inside its window, whether the assigned scope has completed its reprocessing cycle, and whether the single-use device the case consumes has been held against a live consignment lot. It owns the chain of custody from the specimen container leaving the room to the graded result reaching the patient, and it owns the surveillance recall book that stone, bladder-cancer and prostate patients are carried on for years.

Two design commitments run through every section that follows. First, **readiness is computed, never asserted**: the system derives an ordered list of unmet gates for each case, and the front desk sees exactly the reason the next attempted check-in would fail. Second, **quantities and versions are conserved**: consigned device units, authorised therapy visits and the surveillance recall book are all ledgers whose totals must reconcile after every mutation, and every write against a shared parent record carries the version the client last read so that no caller silently overwrites a decision it never saw.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contracts that hold for every endpoint in the service: authentication, envelope shape, pagination, identity and versioning, the logical clock, precision, the validation ladder, and the conserved-quantity invariants. All resources are mounted under the `/urology` prefix.

### 2.1. Practice Identity and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which they are acting. If either header is absent or blank, the request is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. The service recognises exactly seven roles: `PracticeAdmin`, `Scheduler`, `Urologist`, `ProcedureNurse`, `SterileProcessingTech`, `PathologyLiaison`, and `PelvicHealthCoordinator`. Every endpoint declares which of those roles may invoke it (see §4); a syntactically valid caller whose role is not on an endpoint's list is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.

Six of the seven roles are **entity-backed**: the acting user is a real `Provider` row on the practice roster, and the role claimed in the header must agree with that row's `role_type`. When `X-User-Role` is `Scheduler`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Provider`, and that Provider's `role_type` must be `SCHEDULER`. When `X-User-Role` is `Urologist`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Provider`, whose `role_type` must be `UROLOGIST`. When `X-User-Role` is `ProcedureNurse`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Provider`, whose `role_type` must be `RN`. When `X-User-Role` is `SterileProcessingTech`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Provider`, whose `role_type` must be `TECH`. When `X-User-Role` is `PathologyLiaison`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Provider`, whose `role_type` must be `PATHOLOGY_LIAISON`. When `X-User-Role` is `PelvicHealthCoordinator`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Provider`, whose `role_type` must be `PELVIC_COORDINATOR`. In all six cases the resolved Provider must additionally have `credential_status` equal to `ACTIVE`; a Provider whose credential standing has been moved to `SUSPENDED` is refused on every request, including read-only ones, with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`. Ownership checks on any resource that names a Provider compare that resource's provider-referencing field to `X-User-ID` directly — the identity in the header is the identity written into the record.

The seventh role is different. When `X-User-Role` is `PracticeAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity: the service may record or log it, but it never resolves it to a `Provider` row and never compares it to any entity's `id`. This is what allows a `PracticeAdmin` to bootstrap the very first Provider onto an empty roster, and to drive the logical clock, without already existing as a Provider themselves.

Role checks are layered, and the layering is observable. The identity resolution described above runs **before** the per-endpoint role allowlist, so a caller who presents an `X-User-ID` that does not resolve to a Provider of the claimed role is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` even on an endpoint their claimed role is permitted to use. One authorisation rule is decision-sensitive rather than endpoint-sensitive: on a case's clearance review, the `WAIVE` decision is reserved to a `Urologist`, and a `ProcedureNurse` submitting `WAIVE` is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` even though the same nurse may submit `CLEAR` and `BLOCK` on the same case. Separately, three creation payloads name the acting provider explicitly and the named provider must be the caller; a mismatch is `ERR_ACTOR_IDENTITY_MISMATCH`. Reprocessing attestation goes further still and is treated as a segregation-of-duties control, reported as `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — see §3.5.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection read is paginated by two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` outside the inclusive range 1 through 100, an `offset` below 0, or a non-integer value for either is a malformed request and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Collection reads are always returned in ascending `created_at` order. Every list response reports `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside `meta`, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filter before pagination is applied and `has_more` indicates whether any matching row remains beyond the current page.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and the Version Counter

Every resource in this service — providers, patients, rooms, procedure types, cases, antibiotic orders, scope assets, reprocessing logs, specimens, surveillance recalls, pelvic health referrals and consignment lots alike — carries the same four common properties. `id` is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix and no embedded meaning: it is identity only and never participates in a business decision, a ranking, a tie-break or an ordering rule anywhere in this specification. `created_at` and `updated_at` are ISO 8601 UTC timestamps rendered with exactly three fractional-second digits. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row.

The phrase "every mutation" is deliberate and it includes mutations the client did not ask for. When completing a case advances the assigned scope's sterile-processing state, the scope's own `version` increments and its `updated_at` refreshes, even though the request named the case. When a cascade recomputes a patient's derived counters, the patient's `version` increments. This is what makes the stale-version guards of §2.6 useful: a client that has read a room, a scope, a lot or a patient can tell that something happened to it since, purely from the counter. Cascaded side effects are described in full in §3; the rule here is that any row whose stored fields change as a result of a request has its `version` incremented once for that request and its `updated_at` refreshed.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

Nothing in this system reads the host machine's wall clock. Every window, deadline, expiry, grace period, lapse computation, escalation threshold and readiness gate is evaluated against a **logical UTC clock** that the service maintains as mutable state. Before any request has moved it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it must never fall back to real time.

A `PracticeAdmin` may set the clock by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant; the response reports the resulting `current_utc`. Setting the clock mutates no entity and implies no sweep — recall lapse, notification escalation, accreditation expiry and the check-in early-arrival window are all re-evaluated lazily, at the moment of the next request that reads them. Where a rule speaks of "the logical clock date" it means the calendar date of that instant in UTC, with no time component; where it speaks of "the logical clock instant" it means the full timestamp.

### 2.6. Stale-Version Guards on Shared Parent Records

Four write operations in this service reach across into a record that other writers also mutate, and each of those four is guarded by the parent record's `version`. In every case the request must carry the version the client last observed, the server compares it to the parent's current `version`, and a mismatch is refused with HTTP 412 and the guard's own error identifier. These four fields are required inputs, not optional ones — a request that omits them is a malformed request under `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. No other endpoint in this specification accepts a version field.

- Committing a drafted case to the room calendar must carry `expected_room_version`, matching the current `version` of the `ProcedureRoom` the case names. Scheduling, rescheduling, cancelling and no-showing a case each rewrite that room's occupancy ledger and therefore advance its version, as does any administrative edit of the room. If the value does not match, the commit is refused with `ERR_STALE_ROOM_VERSION` and HTTP 412: a client holding an older room version cannot have evaluated the overlap rules against the ledger the server now holds.
- Assigning a scope to a case must carry `expected_scope_asset_version`, matching the current `version` of the `ScopeAsset` being assigned. A reprocessing stage closing, a leak-test debt being cleared, or another case releasing the scope each advance that version, which means the assigning client's view of `leak_test_required` and `hld_pass_at` is out of date. A mismatch is refused with `ERR_STALE_SCOPE_VERSION` and HTTP 412.
- Reserving single-use device units for a case must carry `expected_lot_version`, matching the current `version` of the `ConsignmentLot` the units come from. Another reservation, a consumption, a wastage or a supplier return will have re-derived `quantity_available` since the client read it. A mismatch is refused with `ERR_STALE_LOT_VERSION` and HTTP 412.
- Opening a surveillance recall must carry `expected_patient_version`, matching the current `version` of the `Patient` the recall is for. This guard exists because recall creation legitimately supersedes any open recall of the same patient and protocol, so a second create would otherwise succeed on its own terms rather than being caught by a state precondition. A mismatch is refused with `ERR_STALE_PATIENT_VERSION` and HTTP 412 — which is exactly the situation where a recall the client never saw has already superseded the row the client intended to supersede.

### 2.7. Precision, Rounding and Calendar Arithmetic

All monetary amounts in this system are **integer cents**. There are no fractional currency fields and no floating-point money anywhere in a request or a response. Wherever a monetary value must be derived from a percentage factor, the result is rounded **half up** to a whole cent. Where a total is assembled from several contributions, the order of rounding is pinned and is part of the contract: each contributing line is rounded to whole cents first, and the rounded line values are then summed. Summing raw products and rounding once at the end is explicitly not the rule and produces different totals; §3.5 works the divergence through with concrete numbers.

Durations are whole minutes and whole hours; there are no fractional-minute procedure lengths and no fractional-hour escalation windows. Doses are whole milligrams, and where a dose must be snapped to a dispensing increment the division by the increment is rounded half up **before** multiplying back up, never after. Calendar arithmetic that advances a date by a number of months clamps the day of month to the last valid day of the target month and never rolls over into the following month: advancing 31 January by one month yields 28 February in a common year, not 3 March. Dates with no time component are rendered as `YYYY-MM-DD`; all instants are ISO 8601 UTC with three fractional digits.

### 2.8. The Validation Ladder

A single request can violate several rules at once. When it does, the response reports exactly one error, and which one is fully determined: the service evaluates its checks in a **total priority order** and returns the error belonging to the lowest-numbered rule that fails. This ladder is normative and the numbers below are the ordering keys — they are not exposed in any response, but the relative order they impose is observable on every multiply-invalid request.

1. **Priority 1 — headers.** `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` are both present and non-blank. Otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`, HTTP 401. Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Priority 2 — identity resolution.** For each of the six entity-backed roles, `X-User-ID` resolves to an existing `Provider` whose `role_type` matches the binding declared in §2.1 for the claimed `X-User-Role`, and whose `credential_status` is `ACTIVE`; a `SUSPENDED` provider is refused even for reads. Otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`, HTTP 403. Applies to every endpoint except provider registration and setting the logical clock, both of which a `PracticeAdmin` performs with an opaque identifier.
3. **Priority 3 — role allowlist.** `X-User-Role` appears in the endpoint's declared role list in §4. Otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`, HTTP 403. Applies to every endpoint.
4. **Priority 4 — waiver authority.** On a case's clearance review, a `decision` of `WAIVE` requires the `Urologist` role; a `ProcedureNurse` may submit `CLEAR` and `BLOCK` but never `WAIVE`. Otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`, HTTP 403. Applies to clearance review only.
5. **Priority 6 — request shape.** The body parses; every required input field named for that operation in §3 is present; every enum-valued field carries a declared member; every integer field is a non-negative integer; every date and datetime field is ISO 8601; and `limit` and `offset` are integers satisfying the bounds of §2.3. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, HTTP 400. Applies to every endpoint.
6. **Priority 8 — path resolution.** Path parameters resolve to their declared entity, evaluated strictly left to right. The error is the not-found code of the first path segment that fails to resolve, chosen from `ERR_PATIENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ROOM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROCEDURE_TYPE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CASE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SCOPE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REPROCESSING_LOG_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SPECIMEN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RECALL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REFERRAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LOT_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_ANTIBIOTIC_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, all HTTP 404. Applies to every endpoint carrying a path parameter.
7. **Priority 10 — body reference resolution.** Foreign keys carried in the body resolve, evaluated in the order the fields are listed for that operation. On case drafting that order is `patient_id`, then `procedure_type_id`, then `room_id`, then `primary_provider_id`. The error is the matching not-found code for the first field that cannot be resolved, HTTP 404. Applies to every operation carrying a reference in its body.
8. **Priority 14 — acting-provider identity.** The provider named in the body is the caller: `ordered_by_provider_id` on antibiotic ordering, `performed_by_provider_id` on opening a reprocessing log, and `collected_by_provider_id` on specimen collection must each equal `X-User-ID`. Otherwise `ERR_ACTOR_IDENTITY_MISMATCH`, HTTP 403. Applies to exactly those three operations.
9. **Priority 16 — attestation segregation of duties.** On completing a reprocessing log, `attested_by_provider_id` differs from that log's `performed_by_provider_id` and equals `X-User-ID`. Otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, HTTP 403. Applies to reprocessing log completion only.
10. **Priority 18 — natural-key uniqueness.** `Patient.mrn`, `ProcedureType.code`, `ProcedureRoom.name` and `ScopeAsset.serial_number` are unique globally, and `Specimen.container_label` is unique within a single `case_id`. The errors are `ERR_DUPLICATE_MRN`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_PROCEDURE_TYPE_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_ROOM_NAME`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_SCOPE_SERIAL` and `ERR_DUPLICATE_CONTAINER_LABEL` respectively, all HTTP 409. Applies to patient registration, procedure type definition, room commissioning, scope registration and specimen collection.
11. **Priority 22 — declared transition.** The target row is in a state from which the requested action is declared in §3. A case in `COMPLETED` cannot be rescheduled, a specimen in `REJECTED` cannot be resulted, a `CLOSED` reprocessing log cannot be completed a second time, a `SUPERSEDED` recall cannot be closed, and a `WITHDRAWN` referral cannot log visits. The error is the transition code of the entity concerned — `ERR_INVALID_CASE_TRANSITION`, `ERR_INVALID_SPECIMEN_TRANSITION`, `ERR_INVALID_NOTIFICATION_TRANSITION`, `ERR_INVALID_REPROCESSING_TRANSITION`, `ERR_INVALID_RECALL_TRANSITION` or `ERR_INVALID_REFERRAL_TRANSITION` — all HTTP 409. Applies to every action endpoint.
12. **Priority 26 — cross-entity state guard.** Creating a pelvic health referral requires `source_case_id` to name a case whose `schedule_status` is `COMPLETED`; creating a surveillance recall that carries `source_specimen_id` requires that specimen's `chain_status` to be `RESULTED`. Otherwise `ERR_SOURCE_CASE_NOT_COMPLETED`, HTTP 422. Applies to referral creation and recall creation.
13. **Priority 30 — optimistic concurrency.** The request's expected-version field equals the current `version` of the parent named in §2.6. The error is `ERR_STALE_ROOM_VERSION`, `ERR_STALE_SCOPE_VERSION`, `ERR_STALE_LOT_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_PATIENT_VERSION`, selected by which parent the operation guards as set out in §2.6, all HTTP 412. Applies to exactly four operations: case scheduling, scope assignment, device reservation and recall creation.
14. **Priority 34 — preparation completeness.** On a clearance `decision` of `CLEAR`, every code in the case's procedure type's `required_prep_codes` appears in the case's `acknowledged_prep_codes`. When `required_prep_codes` is the empty array this passes vacuously and `CLEAR` is granted with no acknowledgements at all. Otherwise `ERR_PREP_INCOMPLETE`, HTTP 422. Applies to clearance review only.
15. **Priority 36 — instruction relevance.** The acknowledged `instruction_code` is a member of the case's procedure type's `required_prep_codes`. Otherwise `ERR_PREP_CODE_NOT_REQUIRED`, HTTP 422. Applies to prep acknowledgement only.
16. **Priority 40 — clearance granted.** At check-in the case's `clearance_status` is `CLEARED` or `PREP_WAIVED`; `PENDING_PREP`, `PREP_ACKNOWLEDGED` and `BLOCKED` all fail. Otherwise `ERR_CASE_NOT_CLEARED`, HTTP 409. Applies to check-in only.
17. **Priority 42 — room accreditation.** When the case's room is of type `IMAGING_BAY` or `URODYNAMICS_LAB`, that room's `accreditation_expires_on` is non-null and falls on or after the logical clock date. Otherwise `ERR_ROOM_ACCREDITATION_LAPSED`, HTTP 422. Applies to two operations: check-in and case scheduling.
18. **Priority 43 — provider accreditation.** When the case's room is of type `IMAGING_BAY` or `URODYNAMICS_LAB`, the primary provider's `ultrasound_accreditation_expires_on` is non-null and falls on or after the logical clock date. Otherwise `ERR_PROVIDER_ACCREDITATION_LAPSED`, HTTP 422. Applies to two operations: check-in and case scheduling.
19. **Priority 45 — explicit agent safety.** An explicitly requested antibiotic agent is not contraindicated: its drug class differs from the patient's `antibiotic_allergy_class`, and the patient's `egfr` is at least that agent's minimum. Otherwise `ERR_ANTIBIOTIC_CONTRAINDICATED`, HTTP 422. Applies to antibiotic ordering only.
20. **Priority 46 — automatic agent availability.** With `requested_agent` set to `AUTO` the eligible agent pool is non-empty. This also fails whenever the case's procedure type does not require antibiotics, because such a type has no protocol ranking and therefore no eligible pool. Otherwise `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_ANTIBIOTIC`, HTTP 422. Applies to antibiotic ordering only.
21. **Priority 47 — prophylaxis window.** `administered_at` falls inside the closed interval that begins at the order's `prophylaxis_window_start_at` and ends at the case's `scheduled_start_at`; both endpoints are accepted. Otherwise `ERR_PROPHYLAXIS_WINDOW_MISSED`, HTTP 422. Applies to antibiotic administration only.
22. **Priority 48 — prophylaxis actually given.** When the case's procedure type has `requires_antibiotic` true, at least one antibiotic order on the case has `status` `ADMINISTERED`. Otherwise `ERR_ANTIBIOTIC_NOT_ADMINISTERED`, HTTP 422. Applies to check-in only.
23. **Priority 50 — leak-test debt.** The scope being assigned does not have `leak_test_required` true. Otherwise `ERR_SCOPE_LEAK_TEST_DUE`, HTTP 422. Applies to scope assignment only.
24. **Priority 51 — scope availability.** The scope being assigned has `status` `AVAILABLE`; `SOILED`, `REPROCESSING`, `IN_USE` and `QUARANTINED` are all refused. Otherwise `ERR_SCOPE_NOT_AVAILABLE`, HTTP 409. Applies to scope assignment only.
25. **Priority 52 — scope readiness at check-in.** When the case's procedure type has `requires_scope` true, the case has a `scope_asset_id` whose scope has `status` `IN_USE`, a non-null `hld_pass_at`, and a null `reprocess_due_at`. Otherwise `ERR_SCOPE_NOT_READY`, HTTP 422. Applies to check-in only.
26. **Priority 53 — high-level disinfection certification.** A `HIGH_LEVEL_DISINFECT` stage may only be performed by a Provider whose `hld_certified` is true, and only attested by another Provider whose `hld_certified` is true. Otherwise `ERR_HLD_CERTIFICATION_REQUIRED`, HTTP 422. Applies to two operations: opening a reprocessing log and completing one.
27. **Priority 54 — room open.** The target room's `room_status` is `OPEN`. Otherwise `ERR_ROOM_CLOSED`, HTTP 422. Applies to two operations: case scheduling and case rescheduling.
28. **Priority 55 — room type match.** The target room's `room_type` equals the case's procedure type's `allowed_room_type`. Otherwise `ERR_ROOM_TYPE_MISMATCH`, HTTP 422. Applies to two operations: case scheduling and case rescheduling.
29. **Priority 56 — reprocessing stage order.** A newly opened stage log is the successor of the scope's last closed passing stage, in the fixed sequence described in §3.5. Otherwise `ERR_REPROCESSING_STAGE_OUT_OF_ORDER`, HTTP 422. Applies to opening a reprocessing log only.
30. **Priority 57 — one open log per scope.** The scope has no reprocessing log whose `log_status` is `OPEN`. Otherwise `ERR_REPROCESSING_LOG_ALREADY_OPEN`, HTTP 409. Applies to opening a reprocessing log only.
31. **Priority 58 — room occupancy conflict.** The case's half-open room occupancy interval does not intersect that of any other occupying case in the same room, as defined in §3.3. Otherwise `ERR_ROOM_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, HTTP 409. Applies to two operations: case scheduling and case rescheduling.
32. **Priority 59 — provider double booking.** The case's closed buffered provider interval does not intersect that of any other occupying case with the same `primary_provider_id`, as defined in §3.3. Otherwise `ERR_PROVIDER_DOUBLE_BOOKED`, HTTP 409. Applies to two operations: case scheduling and case rescheduling.
33. **Priority 60 — early arrival.** The logical clock is at or after the case's `scheduled_start_at` less the early check-in allowance of 30 minutes. Otherwise `ERR_CHECKIN_TOO_EARLY`, HTTP 422. Applies to check-in only.
34. **Priority 61 — no-show grace.** The logical clock is strictly after the case's `scheduled_start_at` plus the 15-minute grace allowance. Otherwise `ERR_NO_SHOW_GRACE_NOT_ELAPSED`, HTTP 422. Applies to no-show marking only.
35. **Priority 62 — specimen transit.** Accession happens no later than 24 hours after the specimen's `collected_at`, and `received_at` is not earlier than `collected_at`. Otherwise `ERR_SPECIMEN_TRANSIT_EXPIRED`, HTTP 422. Applies to specimen accession only.
36. **Priority 63 — specimen expected.** A specimen may only be collected on a case whose procedure type has `requires_specimen` true and whose `schedule_status` is `IN_ROOM` or `COMPLETED`. Otherwise `ERR_SPECIMEN_NOT_EXPECTED`, HTTP 422. Applies to specimen collection only.
37. **Priority 64 — lot not expired.** The consignment lot's `lot_expires_on` falls on or after the logical clock date. Otherwise `ERR_LOT_EXPIRED`, HTTP 422. Applies to device reservation only.
38. **Priority 66 — device catalogue match.** The lot's `sku` equals the case's procedure type's `single_use_device_sku`. Otherwise `ERR_DEVICE_SKU_MISMATCH`, HTTP 422. Applies to device reservation only.
39. **Priority 67 — no double reservation.** The case's `device_state` is `NONE` or `RELEASED`; a case already holding `RESERVED`, `CONSUMED` or `WASTED` units cannot reserve again. Otherwise `ERR_DEVICE_ALREADY_RESERVED`, HTTP 409. Applies to device reservation only.
40. **Priority 68 — device reserved at check-in.** When the case's procedure type has `consignment_required` true, the case's `device_state` is `RESERVED`. Otherwise `ERR_DEVICE_NOT_RESERVED`, HTTP 422. Applies to check-in only.
41. **Priority 70 — sufficient consignment stock.** The requested `quantity` is at most the lot's `quantity_available`. Otherwise `ERR_CONSIGNMENT_INSUFFICIENT`, HTTP 422. Applies to device reservation only.
42. **Priority 72 — returnable stock.** The returned `quantity` is at most the lot's `quantity_available`; reserved and consumed units cannot be returned. Otherwise `ERR_CONSIGNMENT_RETURN_EXCEEDS_AVAILABLE`, HTTP 422. Applies to lot return only.
43. **Priority 74 — therapy start window.** On a referral `decision` of `ACCEPT`, the logical clock date falls on or before the source case's completion date plus 30 days. Otherwise `ERR_THERAPY_START_WINDOW_MISSED`, HTTP 422. Applies to referral decision only.
44. **Priority 76 — visit ledger balance.** With `action` `LOG`, `visits_remaining` is at least 1; with `action` `VOID_LAST`, `visits_used` is at least 1. Otherwise `ERR_VISIT_ALLOCATION_EXHAUSTED`, HTTP 422. Applies to the visit ledger operation only.

Three pairs in this ladder are reachable by a single well-formed request, and the ordering decides what the caller sees. A check-in of a case that is neither cleared nor waived *and* has no administered prophylaxis reports `ERR_CASE_NOT_CLEARED` (priority 40), not `ERR_ANTIBIOTIC_NOT_ADMINISTERED` (priority 48). A device reservation against an expired lot that also asks for more units than remain reports `ERR_LOT_EXPIRED` (priority 64), not `ERR_CONSIGNMENT_INSUFFICIENT` (priority 70). And a scheduling attempt into a closed room whose calendar also collides reports `ERR_ROOM_CLOSED` (priority 54), not `ERR_ROOM_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (priority 58).

### 2.9. Reference Resolution and Reverse Reachability

The resource topology is flat: every collection sits directly beneath the `/urology` prefix and no resource is nested inside another's path. Parent-child relationships are carried by reference fields on the child row, and every such reference is reverse-reachable through a filter on the parent's collection read. Where an operation's path carries more than one identifier, the identifiers are resolved strictly left to right and the first failure determines the error, so a request naming a non-existent case and a non-existent nested resource always reports the case as missing.

### 2.10. Conserved Ledgers

Two ledgers in this system are conserved quantities, and their invariants must hold before and after every request that touches them.

For a consignment lot, `quantity_received` is the conserved total: at every observable moment it equals the sum of `quantity_available`, `quantity_reserved`, `quantity_consumed`, `quantity_returned` and `quantity_wasted`. Units move between those five buckets; they are never created or destroyed. For a pelvic health referral, `visits_authorized` is the conserved total: at every observable moment it equals the sum of `visits_remaining`, `visits_used` and `visits_forfeited`.

### 2.11. Filtered Collection Reads

Every entity has both a single-resource read and a collection read, and every collection read is open to all seven roles. The collection reads accept the following optional filters in addition to `limit` and `offset`, and a filter that is supplied restricts the result set to exact matches on that field.

Providers may be filtered by `role_type`, `credential_status` and `hld_certified`. Patients may be filtered by `primary_urologist_id` and `mrn`. Rooms may be filtered by `room_type` and `room_status`. Procedure types may be filtered by `code`, `allowed_room_type` and `requires_scope`. Scope assets may be filtered by `status`, `leak_test_required` and `serial_number`. Consignment lots may be filtered by `sku`, `device_class` and `billing_status`. Procedure cases may be filtered by `patient_id`, `procedure_type_id`, `room_id`, `primary_provider_id`, `scope_asset_id`, `device_lot_id`, `schedule_status` and `clearance_status`. Antibiotic orders may be filtered by `case_id`, `ordered_by_provider_id`, `status` and `agent`. Reprocessing logs may be filtered by `scope_asset_id`, `case_id`, `performed_by_provider_id`, `attested_by_provider_id`, `stage`, `log_status` and `outcome` — this is the practice's reprocessing audit trail. Specimens may be filtered by `case_id`, `collected_by_provider_id`, `chain_status`, `notification_status` and `specimen_type`. Surveillance recalls may be filtered by `patient_id`, `predecessor_recall_id`, `source_specimen_id`, `satisfying_case_id`, `protocol`, `status` and `due_basis`. Pelvic health referrals may be filtered by `patient_id`, `source_case_id`, `program` and `status`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up the Practice: Roster, Patients, Rooms, Catalogue and Scope Registry

Nothing in this system can happen until a `PracticeAdmin` has populated the reference data, and because the admin identity is opaque, roster registration is the one write that does not require an existing Provider. Registering a clinician or staff member records their `full_name` and their `npi`, the ten-character national provider identifier that must be unique across the roster, together with the `role_type` that governs which header role they may subsequently authenticate as — one of `UROLOGIST`, `RN`, `TECH`, `SCHEDULER`, `PATHOLOGY_LIAISON` or `PELVIC_COORDINATOR`. Two further attributes may be supplied at registration and are otherwise defaulted: `hld_certified`, which starts out `false` and marks the technicians permitted to touch high-level disinfection, and `ultrasound_accreditation_expires_on`, an `YYYY-MM-DD` date that defaults to null, null meaning the clinician has never held ultrasound accreditation at all. `credential_status` is never supplied at registration; it always begins as `ACTIVE`, its only other value being `SUSPENDED`.

Administrative edits to the roster are narrow by design. Only `credential_status`, `hld_certified` and `ultrasound_accreditation_expires_on` can be changed after registration, each optional in the update payload, and any such edit increments the Provider's `version`. Moving someone to `SUSPENDED` is the practice's off-switch: from that moment the identity-resolution rung of §2.8 refuses their very next request, read or write, with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.

Patients are registered by a `PracticeAdmin` or a `Scheduler` and are bound to a treating clinician at the moment of registration. The payload carries `mrn` — the medical record number, unique across all patients, a duplicate drawing `ERR_DUPLICATE_MRN` — plus `full_name`, `date_of_birth` as an `YYYY-MM-DD` date, `primary_urologist_id` naming the Provider who will carry the patient, and `egfr`, the estimated glomerular filtration rate, which may not be negative. The `primary_urologist_id` must name a Provider whose `role_type` is `UROLOGIST`. Two optional clinical flags complete the record: `anticoagulant_flag`, defaulting to `false`, and `antibiotic_allergy_class`, defaulting to `NONE` and otherwise one of `FLUOROQUINOLONE`, `CEPHALOSPORIN`, `SULFA` or `AMINOGLYCOSIDE`. Each patient also exposes two derived counters that the service maintains and the client never writes: `outstanding_recall_count`, the number of that patient's surveillance recalls whose `status` is either `SCHEDULED_DUE` or `OVERDUE`, and `open_notification_count`, the number of specimens belonging to that patient's cases whose `notification_status` is `PENDING`, `ATTEMPTED` or `ESCALATED`. Both initialise to 0.

Renal function drifts, anticoagulation is started and stopped, and allergies are discovered late; a `PracticeAdmin` or the treating `Urologist` may therefore revise `egfr`, `anticoagulant_flag` and `antibiotic_allergy_class` on an existing patient. Two consequences of that edit matter. It increments the patient's `version`, which is precisely what makes a previously captured `expected_patient_version` stale for the recall guard of §2.6. And it does **not** retroactively recompute the occupancy window of any case already on the calendar: a case scheduled while the patient's renal function was still above the adjustment threshold keeps the duration it was given.

Procedure rooms are commissioned with a `name` that must be unique across the suite — a collision is `ERR_DUPLICATE_ROOM_NAME` — and a `room_type` of `CYSTO_SUITE`, `URODYNAMICS_LAB` or `IMAGING_BAY`. Three optional attributes may be supplied: `turnover_minutes`, the room's baseline cleaning allowance, which may not be negative and defaults to 10 minutes; `accreditation_body`, the accrediting organisation for in-office imaging and ultrasound work in that room, defaulting to null; and `accreditation_expires_on`, an `YYYY-MM-DD` date defaulting to null, where null means the room has never been accredited. `room_status` is not an input: a newly commissioned room is `OPEN`, its only other value being `CLOSED`. Each room also exposes `occupied_block_count`, a derived count of the cases naming that room whose `schedule_status` is `SCHEDULED`, `IN_ROOM` or `COMPLETED`; it initialises to 0. Administrative edits may change `room_status`, `turnover_minutes`, `accreditation_body` and `accreditation_expires_on`, each optional; any such edit increments the room's `version` — which is what invalidates a client's held `expected_room_version` — and, like the patient edit, does not retune the turnover already baked into cases sitting on that room's calendar.

The procedure catalogue is the practice's rulebook, and it is the single richest piece of reference data in the system. A procedure type carries a `code` that must be unique and must be one of `CYSTOSCOPY`, `URODYNAMICS`, `PROSTATE_BIOPSY` or `OFFICE_ULTRASOUND`, a duplicate raising `ERR_DUPLICATE_PROCEDURE_TYPE_CODE`. It carries `base_minutes`, the baseline in-room duration before any patient-specific adjustment, which must be at least 1. It carries a `cleanup_class` of `LOW` or `HIGH`, where `HIGH` doubles the room's baseline turnover allowance. It carries an `allowed_room_type` of `CYSTO_SUITE`, `URODYNAMICS_LAB` or `IMAGING_BAY`, and that is the only room type into which cases of this procedure may ever be booked. Four boolean obligations then declare what a case of this type needs before a patient may be brought in: `requires_scope`, `requires_specimen`, `requires_antibiotic` and `consignment_required`. All four are optional in the payload and all four default to `false`. Where `consignment_required` is true, `single_use_device_sku` names the only catalogue number acceptable for that type's device reservation; it defaults to null. Finally, `required_prep_codes` is an array of preparation instruction codes drawn from `NPO_MIDNIGHT`, `BOWEL_PREP`, `HOLD_ANTICOAGULANT`, `BLADDER_FILL` and `ARRIVE_WITH_ESCORT`; it is optional and defaults to the empty array, and an empty array is a meaningful configuration — it means the preparation-completeness rung at priority 34 passes vacuously for every case of that type, so clearance may be granted with no acknowledgements at all.

Flexible cystoscopes enter the sterile-processing registry with a `serial_number` unique across the inventory (a collision being `ERR_DUPLICATE_SCOPE_SERIAL`), a `scope_model` designation, and a `channel_count` of at least 1 recording how many working channels a technician must clean by hand. Everything else about a new scope is fixed by the service: `status` starts at `AVAILABLE`, `cycles_since_leak_test` at 0, `leak_test_required` at `false`, and both `hld_pass_at` and `reprocess_due_at` at null. That last default has a consequence worth stating plainly here, because it surprises people: a freshly registered scope cannot be used on a patient. Its `hld_pass_at` is null, so the scope-readiness gate at priority 52 fails at check-in. It must first be either commissioned or put through a full reprocessing cycle, both of which are described in §3.5.

### 3.2. Drafting a Case and the Occupancy Window Arithmetic

A case begins life as a draft. A `Scheduler` or a `Urologist` supplies the four references that define it — `patient_id`, `procedure_type_id`, `room_id` and `primary_provider_id` — together with `scheduled_start_at`, the intended start of the in-room window as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits, and optionally an `indication_note` carrying free-text clinical justification, which defaults to null. The `primary_provider_id` names the operating urologist, who will be subject to the buffered overlap rule of §3.3. Those four references are resolved in exactly the order listed, so a payload naming both a non-existent patient and a non-existent room reports `ERR_PATIENT_NOT_FOUND`.

The new case is created with `schedule_status` `DRAFT`, `clearance_status` `PENDING_PREP`, `device_state` `NONE` and `acknowledged_prep_codes` as the empty array. Crucially, **a draft occupies nothing**: it holds neither the room nor the urologist, so no overlap check runs at drafting time and two drafts may sit on top of each other indefinitely. Only cases whose `schedule_status` is `SCHEDULED`, `IN_ROOM` or `COMPLETED` occupy a room or a provider; `DRAFT`, `CANCELLED` and `NO_SHOW` cases occupy neither.

Even though a draft occupies nothing, its window is computed immediately, because the front desk needs to see how long the case will run before committing it. The arithmetic — call it the **occupancy window** — reads exactly three rows resolved from the case's own references at the moment of the mutation: one procedure type, one room and one patient. It runs at three moments and three moments only: when the case is drafted, when it is committed to the calendar, and when it is moved. It is never re-run because a room's `turnover_minutes` or a patient's `egfr` changed afterwards.

The in-room duration starts from the procedure type's `base_minutes` and takes at most two additions. A quarter of an hour is added when the patient's `anticoagulant_flag` is true **and** the procedure type's `code` is `PROSTATE_BIOPSY` — anticoagulated prostate biopsies bleed and take longer to achieve haemostasis; the same patient having a cystoscopy gets no such addition. Ten further minutes are added when the patient's `egfr` is strictly below 45 **and** the procedure type's `requires_antibiotic` is true, covering the extra time renal-dosed prophylaxis takes to run in. The sum of `base_minutes` and those two additions is stored as the case's `procedure_minutes`.

The room's post-procedure allowance is computed differently, and the order of operations is part of the contract. First the room's own `turnover_minutes` is multiplied by a cleanup multiplier of 2 when the procedure type's `cleanup_class` is `HIGH` and by 1 when it is `LOW`. That product is then floored at a quarter of an hour: whichever of the product and 15 minutes is larger becomes the working figure. Only then, if the procedure type's `requires_scope` is true, a further twenty minutes is added for scope handling. The result is stored as the case's own `turnover_minutes`, which is a distinct field from the room's baseline of the same name. The floor applies to the multiplied product, not to the final figure — a `LOW`-cleanup scope procedure in a room with a baseline of 8 minutes therefore ends up with 15 plus 20, that is 35 minutes of turnover, not 28.

Two derived instants close the window out. `scheduled_end_at` is `scheduled_start_at` advanced by `procedure_minutes` minutes, and `block_end_at` is `scheduled_end_at` advanced by `turnover_minutes` minutes. `block_end_at` is the exclusive upper bound of the room's occupancy interval. All four of `procedure_minutes`, `turnover_minutes`, `scheduled_end_at` and `block_end_at` are server-computed and are rejected as unrecognised input if a client tries to supply them; all durations are whole minutes.

### 3.3. The Room Calendar: Committing, Moving, Cancelling and Abandoning a Case

Committing a case to the calendar is the `Scheduler`'s exclusive act, and it advances `schedule_status` from `DRAFT` to `SCHEDULED`. The payload carries nothing but `expected_room_version`. Before the commit stands, five conditions must hold. The room's `room_status` must be `OPEN`, or the request is refused with `ERR_ROOM_CLOSED`. The room's `room_type` must equal the procedure type's `allowed_room_type`, or the request is refused with `ERR_ROOM_TYPE_MISMATCH`. The supplied `expected_room_version` must equal the room's current `version`, or the request is refused with `ERR_STALE_ROOM_VERSION`. And the recomputed window must clear both the room test and the provider test described below. Two accreditation conditions also bite at commit time and not only at check-in: for a case whose room is an `IMAGING_BAY` or a `URODYNAMICS_LAB`, the room's `accreditation_expires_on` must be non-null and on or after the logical clock date (`ERR_ROOM_ACCREDITATION_LAPSED`), and the primary provider's `ultrasound_accreditation_expires_on` must likewise be non-null and on or after the logical clock date (`ERR_PROVIDER_ACCREDITATION_LAPSED`).

**The room and the urologist are booked with different geometries, and the difference is deliberate.** A room's occupancy is the *half-open* interval that begins at `scheduled_start_at` and ends at `block_end_at`, the endpoint excluded. Two cases in the same room conflict when their half-open intervals intersect; a new case whose `scheduled_start_at` falls exactly on an existing case's `block_end_at` therefore fits perfectly and is accepted, while a start one minute earlier than that is refused with `ERR_ROOM_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. The urologist's occupancy is the *closed* interval that begins ten minutes before `scheduled_start_at` and ends ten minutes after `scheduled_end_at`. Note both differences: the provider interval ignores turnover entirely, because the urologist walks out while the room is being cleaned, and it treats mere touching as a collision, so two of the same provider's buffered intervals sharing a single boundary instant are refused with `ERR_PROVIDER_DOUBLE_BOOKED`. In both tests the case being scheduled is compared only against *other* cases — never itself — and only against cases whose `schedule_status` is `SCHEDULED`, `IN_ROOM` or `COMPLETED`.

A successful commit recomputes `procedure_minutes`, `turnover_minutes`, `scheduled_end_at` and `block_end_at` from scratch, increments the room's `version` by 1 because the room's occupancy ledger has changed, recomputes that room's `occupied_block_count`, and recomputes the case's readiness gates.

Cases move. Rescheduling is also the `Scheduler`'s exclusive act and it leaves `schedule_status` at `SCHEDULED` — a self-loop rather than a transition. The payload carries a new `scheduled_start_at` and, optionally, a `room_id`; when `room_id` is absent the case stays in its current room. The target room — the one named in the body when present, otherwise the case's current room — must have `room_status` `OPEN` and a `room_type` matching the procedure type's `allowed_room_type`, and the recomputed half-open interval must not intersect any other occupying case in that target room, nor may the recomputed closed buffered provider interval intersect any other occupying case of the same `primary_provider_id`. Note that rescheduling carries no expected-version field: it is not one of the four guarded writes of §2.6. The whole window is recomputed against the target room's *current* `turnover_minutes`, which is how a room whose baseline allowance was retuned after the original booking finally gets its new figure applied. The room's `version` is incremented by 1 for the room being vacated and by 1 for the room being taken — a single increment when those are the same room — and `occupied_block_count` is recomputed for every room whose version changed. The case's readiness gates are recomputed as well.

Cancellation is available to a `Scheduler` or a `Urologist` from either `DRAFT` or `SCHEDULED`, and it is the system's compensating action. The payload carries a `cancel_reason_code`, which is recorded on the case. Attempting to cancel a case that is already `IN_ROOM`, `COMPLETED`, `CANCELLED` or `NO_SHOW` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CASE_TRANSITION`. Cancellation then unwinds everything the case was holding. If the case holds a scope and that scope's `status` is `IN_USE`, the scope returns to `AVAILABLE`, the scope's `version` increments by 1, and the case's `scope_asset_id` is set back to null; because no patient cycle occurred, `cycles_since_leak_test` is emphatically **not** incremented. If the case's `device_state` is `RESERVED`, it becomes `RELEASED`, the lot's `quantity_reserved` is reduced by the case's `device_quantity` and its `quantity_available` increased by the same amount, the lot's `version` increments by 1, and the lot is re-billed under the provisional basis described in §3.6. Every antibiotic order on the case whose `status` is `ORDERED` transitions to `CANCELLED`, while orders already `ADMINISTERED` are left exactly as they are — the practice needs the administration record even for a case that never happened. The case's `blocking_reason_codes` is recomputed to the empty array. Finally, when the cancelled case had been `SCHEDULED` rather than merely `DRAFT`, the room's `version` is incremented by 1 and its `occupied_block_count` recomputed, because an occupancy block has just been released; a cancelled draft touches no room.

Marking a case as a no-show is the `Scheduler`'s alone, is available only from `SCHEDULED`, and takes no payload at all. It may not be exercised early: the logical clock must be *strictly after* `scheduled_start_at` plus the fifteen-minute grace allowance, and an attempt at or before that instant is refused with `ERR_NO_SHOW_GRACE_NOT_ELAPSED`. The case's `cancel_reason_code` is then set to the constant string `NO_SHOW_GRACE_ELAPSED` — the client does not supply it. Scope release behaves exactly as it does for a cancellation, with the scope returning to `AVAILABLE`, the scope's `version` incremented and `scope_asset_id` nulled and no leak-test cycle counted. Antibiotic orders in `ORDERED` are likewise cancelled, the room's `version` is incremented by 1 and its `occupied_block_count` recomputed, and the case's `blocking_reason_codes` collapses to the empty array.

**The one place cancellation and no-show diverge is the device ledger, and it is a business decision, not an oversight.** A cancellation happens before the patient arrives, the sterile package was never opened, and the units go back to `quantity_available` as `RELEASED`. A no-show happens after the room was set up, the package *was* opened, and the units are unrecoverable: `device_state` becomes `WASTED`, the lot's `quantity_reserved` is reduced by `device_quantity` and its `quantity_wasted` increased by the same amount, the lot's `version` increments by 1, and the lot is re-billed provisionally. Both branches leave `quantity_received` untouched, as §2.10 requires — the units simply sit in a different bucket, and the practice is invoiced for the wasted ones.

### 3.4. Preparing the Patient: Instruction Acknowledgements, Clearance Decisions and Prophylaxis

Every case carries two independent state dimensions, and understanding that independence is essential to implementing it. `schedule_status` tracks where the case sits on the calendar; `clearance_status` tracks whether the patient is prepared. They advance on different operations and neither gates the other's transitions — with one exception, check-in, where the two finally meet. A case can be `SCHEDULED` and `PENDING_PREP`, or `DRAFT` and `CLEARED`, or `SCHEDULED` and `BLOCKED`, and all three are ordinary working states. `clearance_status` begins at `PENDING_PREP` and takes its values from `PENDING_PREP`, `PREP_ACKNOWLEDGED`, `PREP_WAIVED`, `CLEARED` and `BLOCKED`; unlike the schedule dimension it has no terminal state at all, so a case can move around inside it indefinitely.

Preparation acknowledgements are recorded one instruction at a time by a `Scheduler` or a `ProcedureNurse`, each call naming a single `instruction_code` and the `channel` through which the patient confirmed it. The case's `schedule_status` must be `DRAFT` or `SCHEDULED` for an acknowledgement to be accepted, and the acknowledgement is declared only from `clearance_status` `PENDING_PREP` (which advances to `PREP_ACKNOWLEDGED`) and from `PREP_ACKNOWLEDGED` itself (which stays put — a self-loop). Acknowledging against a case whose clearance has already reached `CLEARED`, `PREP_WAIVED` or `BLOCKED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CASE_TRANSITION`. The supplied `instruction_code` must be one of the codes in the procedure type's `required_prep_codes`; an instruction that type never asked for is refused with `ERR_PREP_CODE_NOT_REQUIRED`. On success the code is appended to the case's `acknowledged_prep_codes` array — which holds distinct codes in the order they were acknowledged — unless it is already present, in which case the array is left untouched. **A repeat acknowledgement of the same code is idempotent and is explicitly not an error.** Every acknowledgement recomputes the case's readiness gates.

Clearance decisions are submitted by a `ProcedureNurse` or a `Urologist` and are shaped by a single `decision` field taking one of three values, with `block_reason_code` an optional companion. Here too the case's `schedule_status` must be `DRAFT` or `SCHEDULED`, and each decision is declared from a specific set of clearance states.

- `CLEAR` is declared from `PENDING_PREP`, from `PREP_ACKNOWLEDGED` and from `BLOCKED`, and lands on `CLEARED`. It requires that every code in the procedure type's `required_prep_codes` is present in the case's `acknowledged_prep_codes`; a missing code is refused with `ERR_PREP_INCOMPLETE`. When `required_prep_codes` is the empty array the requirement is satisfied vacuously, which is the only way a case reaches `CLEARED` straight out of `PENDING_PREP` with no acknowledgements recorded. A successful clear sets `clearance_block_reason` back to null and recomputes the readiness gates.
- `BLOCK` is declared from `PENDING_PREP`, from `PREP_ACKNOWLEDGED` and from `CLEARED`, and lands on `BLOCKED`. It requires `block_reason_code` to be present in the payload; without it the request is malformed and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The supplied value is stored in the case's `clearance_block_reason`, and the readiness gates are recomputed. Note that `BLOCK` is *not* declared from `BLOCKED`: re-blocking an already blocked case is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CASE_TRANSITION`.
- `WAIVE` is declared from `PENDING_PREP` and from `BLOCKED`, and lands on `PREP_WAIVED`. It is reserved to the `Urologist` role — a `ProcedureNurse` submitting it is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` — and it is the clinical override that lets an urgent case proceed without the paperwork. It clears `clearance_block_reason` to null and recomputes the readiness gates. `WAIVE` is not declared from `PREP_ACKNOWLEDGED`, from `CLEARED` or from `PREP_WAIVED`, and an attempt from any of those is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CASE_TRANSITION`.

The clearance dimension is genuinely cyclic: a case blocked because the patient ate breakfast can be re-cleared once the case is moved to the afternoon, and blocked again if something else surfaces, all without the case ever leaving `SCHEDULED`.

Antibiotic prophylaxis is ordered by a `Urologist` and only by a `Urologist`. The payload names the `case_id` the order belongs to, the `ordered_by_provider_id` — which must be the acting user's own identifier, a mismatch being `ERR_ACTOR_IDENTITY_MISMATCH` — and a `requested_agent` of `AUTO`, `CIPROFLOXACIN`, `CEFAZOLIN`, `TMP_SMX` or `GENTAMICIN`. `AUTO` delegates the choice to the practice's protocol; any other value pins the agent explicitly.

Agent selection runs against a fixed formulary. `CIPROFLOXACIN` belongs to drug class `FLUOROQUINOLONE`, needs an estimated glomerular filtration rate of at least 30, has a base dose of 500 milligrams and is given by the `ORAL` route. `CEFAZOLIN` belongs to `CEPHALOSPORIN`, needs at least 15, has a base dose of 1000 milligrams and is given `IV`. `TMP_SMX` belongs to `SULFA`, needs at least 30, has a base dose of 160 milligrams and is `ORAL`. `GENTAMICIN` belongs to `AMINOGLYCOSIDE`, needs at least 60, has a base dose of 240 milligrams and is `IV`. An agent is **eligible** for a given order when all three of the following hold: its drug class is different from the patient's `antibiotic_allergy_class`; the patient's `egfr` is greater than or equal to the agent's minimum; and the agent appears in the protocol ranking that applies to the case's procedure type. Two protocol rankings exist, and they are consulted only for procedure types whose `requires_antibiotic` is true. For a `CYSTOSCOPY` the ranking is `CIPROFLOXACIN` first, `TMP_SMX` second, `CEFAZOLIN` third, `GENTAMICIN` fourth. For a `PROSTATE_BIOPSY` the ranking places `CEFAZOLIN` and `CIPROFLOXACIN` jointly first, `GENTAMICIN` third and `TMP_SMX` fourth. A procedure type whose `requires_antibiotic` is false has no ranking at all, so its eligible pool is empty by construction.

When `requested_agent` names a specific drug, that drug must itself be in the eligible pool; if it is not — because its class matches the patient's allergy, because the patient's renal function is below the agent's floor, or because the case's procedure type has no ranking that includes it — the order is refused with `ERR_ANTIBIOTIC_CONTRAINDICATED`. When `requested_agent` is `AUTO` and the eligible pool is empty, the order is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_ANTIBIOTIC`; this is also what happens to every `AUTO` order placed against a case whose procedure type does not require antibiotics. Otherwise `AUTO` selects the eligible agent with the lowest protocol rank, and ties on rank are broken by agent name in ascending alphabetical order. The joint first place in the prostate-biopsy ranking exists precisely to exercise that tie-break: for a patient with no relevant allergy the resolved `agent` is `CEFAZOLIN`, because `CEFAZOLIN` sorts before `CIPROFLOXACIN`; for a patient whose `antibiotic_allergy_class` is `CEPHALOSPORIN`, cefazolin drops out of the pool and `CIPROFLOXACIN` is chosen instead.

Dosing is renal-adjusted and then snapped to the pharmacy's 25-milligram dispensing increment, in that order. The selected agent's base dose is taken at full strength when the patient's `egfr` is at least 45, and at exactly half strength when it is below 45. That raw figure is divided by 25, the quotient is rounded half up to a whole number, and the result is multiplied back by 25 to give the stored `dose_mg`. Rounding the raw dose first and snapping afterwards is not the rule. The `route` is copied from the formulary entry for the selected agent and is never client-supplied. The order also records `prophylaxis_window_start_at`, fixed at one hour before the case's `scheduled_start_at`, and is created with `status` `ORDERED` and `administered_at` null. Placing an order recomputes the case's readiness gates.

Administration is recorded by a `ProcedureNurse` or a `Urologist`, who supply the `administered_at` instant the dose was actually given. That instant must fall inside the closed interval running from the order's `prophylaxis_window_start_at` through the case's `scheduled_start_at`, both endpoints inclusive; a dose given even a minute before the window opens or after the scheduled start is refused with `ERR_PROPHYLAXIS_WINDOW_MISSED`. On success the order's `status` moves from `ORDERED` to `ADMINISTERED`, `administered_at` is stored, and the antibiotic gate drops out of the case's `blocking_reason_codes`. An order whose `status` is not `ORDERED` — because it has already been administered, or because the case was cancelled and the order swept to `CANCELLED` — cannot be administered and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CASE_TRANSITION`.

### 3.5. Sterile Processing: Scopes, the Reprocessing Cycle and Second-Person Attestation

A scope's `status` describes where it sits in a genuine cycle, not a one-way lifecycle: `AVAILABLE` when it is clean and on the shelf, `IN_USE` when a case is holding it, `SOILED` immediately after a patient cycle, `REPROCESSING` while its stage logs are being worked through, and `QUARANTINED` when something has gone wrong. `QUARANTINED` is the one terminal state: no reprocessing log may be opened against a quarantined scope, it can never be assigned to a case again, and there is no path back.

A scope that has never been used presents a chicken-and-egg problem, since its `hld_pass_at` is null and the readiness gate demands a non-null value. The answer is **commissioning**: a `SterileProcessingTech` records the manufacturer's factory high-level-disinfection attestation for a never-used scope by supplying the `attesting_provider_id` who signs for it. Commissioning sets `hld_pass_at` to the logical clock instant, leaves `status` at `AVAILABLE` and `reprocess_due_at` at null, and increments the scope's `version` by 1. It is a one-shot: a scope whose `status` is anything other than `AVAILABLE`, or whose `hld_pass_at` is already non-null, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REPROCESSING_TRANSITION`.

Assigning a scope to a case is a `ProcedureNurse`'s exclusive act, and the payload names the `scope_asset_id` together with `expected_scope_asset_version`. The case must be in `DRAFT` or `SCHEDULED`, and its procedure type's `requires_scope` must be true — assigning a scope to a case that does not need one is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CASE_TRANSITION`. Two checks then guard the scope itself, and their order matters because a single scope can fail both. The leak-test debt is checked first: a scope whose `leak_test_required` is true is refused with `ERR_SCOPE_LEAK_TEST_DUE`. Availability is checked second: a scope whose `status` is anything other than `AVAILABLE` — `IN_USE`, `SOILED`, `REPROCESSING` or `QUARANTINED` — is refused with `ERR_SCOPE_NOT_AVAILABLE`. A soiled scope that also owes a leak test therefore reports the leak-test debt, not the availability failure. On success the case's `scope_asset_id` is set, the scope moves from `AVAILABLE` to `IN_USE`, the scope's `version` increments by 1, and the case's readiness gates are recomputed.

The scope comes out of the room dirty. When the case that holds it is completed, the scope moves from `IN_USE` to `SOILED`, `cycles_since_leak_test` is increased by 1, `reprocess_due_at` is set to twelve hours after the logical clock instant of the completion — the deadline by which reprocessing must finish — and the scope's `version` increments. Immediately after that increment, and as a consequence of it, the leak-test debt is re-evaluated: once `cycles_since_leak_test` has reached 10 or more, `leak_test_required` is set to `true`, and from then on the scope cannot be assigned to another case until the debt is discharged. Contrast this with the cancellation and no-show paths of §3.3, where the scope returns straight to `AVAILABLE` and `cycles_since_leak_test` is left alone, because no patient cycle actually took place.

Reprocessing itself is recorded stage by stage by `SterileProcessingTech` staff, and only by them. Opening a stage log names the `scope_asset_id`, the `stage`, and the `performed_by_provider_id` — who must be the acting user, must have `role_type` `TECH`, and must have `credential_status` `ACTIVE` — plus optionally the `case_id` of the case that soiled the scope, which is null for a maintenance cycle. The five stages run in the fixed sequence `PRECLEAN`, `LEAK_TEST`, `MANUAL_CLEAN`, `HIGH_LEVEL_DISINFECT`, `DRY_STORE`, and a newly opened log's `stage` must be the immediate successor of the scope's last closed passing stage. Two standing exceptions bend that rule where the business needs it: `LEAK_TEST` may always be opened whenever `leak_test_required` is true, and `PRECLEAN` may always be opened on a `SOILED` scope. Anything else is refused with `ERR_REPROCESSING_STAGE_OUT_OF_ORDER`. Only one log may be open per scope at a time — a second concurrent open is refused with `ERR_REPROCESSING_LOG_ALREADY_OPEN` — and a `HIGH_LEVEL_DISINFECT` log may only be opened where the performing technician's `hld_certified` is true, otherwise `ERR_HLD_CERTIFICATION_REQUIRED`. The new log is created with `log_status` `OPEN`, `started_at` at the logical clock instant, `outcome` null and `attested_by_provider_id` null. **Opening a log changes nothing about the scope.** The scope moves only when the log closes.

Closing a log is where the practice's segregation-of-duties control lives. The closing payload carries the `outcome`, either `PASS` or `FAIL`, and the `attested_by_provider_id` of the second technician who signs the stage off. The log's `log_status` must still be `OPEN`; completing an already-closed log is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REPROCESSING_TRANSITION`. The attester must be the acting user, must hold `role_type` `TECH` with `credential_status` `ACTIVE`, and — the point of the control — must be a *different* Provider from the log's `performed_by_provider_id`. One person may not both perform and attest a reprocessing stage; an attempt is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. For a `HIGH_LEVEL_DISINFECT` stage the certification requirement binds both sides: the performer's `hld_certified` and the attester's `hld_certified` must both be true, or the close is refused with `ERR_HLD_CERTIFICATION_REQUIRED`. A successful close sets `log_status` to `CLOSED`, records `completed_at` at the logical clock instant, and stores the `outcome`.

What the close does to the scope depends entirely on the stage-and-outcome pair, and each pair has its own consequence.

- A passing `PRECLEAN` on a `SOILED` scope moves it to `REPROCESSING` and increments its `version`.
- A passing `LEAK_TEST` zeroes `cycles_since_leak_test` and sets `leak_test_required` back to `false`, incrementing the scope's `version`. This is the *only* path from a leak-test debt back to a clean slate. On an `AVAILABLE` scope this is a self-loop that leaves the status alone; on a `SOILED` or `REPROCESSING` scope the status is likewise unchanged and only the two counters move.
- A passing `MANUAL_CLEAN` closes its own log but drives no scope-side change at all: the scope's `status`, counters, timestamps and `version` are all left exactly as they were.
- A passing `HIGH_LEVEL_DISINFECT` sets the scope's `hld_pass_at` to the log's `completed_at` and increments the scope's `version`, while the `status` stays at `REPROCESSING`. This is the attestation the check-in gate is looking for.
- A passing `DRY_STORE` returns the scope from `REPROCESSING` to `AVAILABLE`, clears `reprocess_due_at` to null and increments the scope's `version` — but only if a closed, passing `HIGH_LEVEL_DISINFECT` log exists for that scope whose `completed_at` is at or after the moment the scope most recently became `SOILED`. A dry-store cannot launder a cycle whose disinfection step was skipped or belongs to an earlier cycle.
- An `outcome` of `FAIL` at any stage is the quarantine cascade, and it has three linked hops. The log closes with its failing outcome; because it closed failing, the scope's `status` becomes `QUARANTINED` (the declared paths are out of `SOILED` and out of `REPROCESSING`, and an `AVAILABLE` scope that fails a leak test is quarantined on the same terms); and because the scope reached `QUARANTINED`, `leak_test_required` is set to `true` and `reprocess_due_at` is cleared to null. The scope's `version` increments by 1 for the whole cascade.

### 3.6. Consignment Inventory: Holding, Spending and Billing Single-Use Devices

Single-use devices arrive on consignment: the supplier's stock sits in the practice's cupboard and is invoiced only as it is used. A `PracticeAdmin` receives a lot by recording the `supplier_name`, the `sku` — the catalogue number that will be matched against a procedure type's `single_use_device_sku` — a `device_class` of `SINGLE_USE_SCOPE_SHEATH`, `BIOPSY_NEEDLE`, `URODYNAMICS_CATHETER_KIT` or `ULTRASOUND_PROBE_COVER`, the `quantity_received` (at least 1), the `unit_price_cents` list price per unit (which may be 0 but never negative), and the `lot_expires_on` date after which the lot may no longer be drawn against. On receipt `quantity_available` is set equal to `quantity_received` and `quantity_reserved`, `quantity_consumed`, `quantity_returned` and `quantity_wasted` all start at 0; `billed_amount_cents` starts at 0, `billing_basis` at `PROVISIONAL` and `billing_status` at `CONSIGNED`.

Holding units for a case is done by a `ProcedureNurse` or a `PracticeAdmin`, who name the `consignment_lot_id`, the `quantity` of units wanted, and `expected_lot_version`. Four business conditions guard the hold, and because more than one can fail at once their relative order is what the caller observes. The lot's `lot_expires_on` must fall on or after the logical clock date, or the request is refused with `ERR_LOT_EXPIRED`. The lot's `sku` must equal the case's procedure type's `single_use_device_sku`, or `ERR_DEVICE_SKU_MISMATCH`. The case's `device_state` must be `NONE` or `RELEASED`, since a case already holding `RESERVED`, `CONSUMED` or `WASTED` units may not reserve again, or `ERR_DEVICE_ALREADY_RESERVED`. And the requested `quantity` must be no greater than the lot's `quantity_available`, or `ERR_CONSIGNMENT_INSUFFICIENT`. An expired lot that is also short of stock therefore reports the expiry, not the shortage.

A successful hold writes `device_lot_id` and `device_quantity` onto the case and sets its `device_state` to `RESERVED`; the lot's `quantity_available` falls by the reserved quantity and its `quantity_reserved` rises by the same amount; the lot is re-billed provisionally, its `billing_status` re-derived and its `version` incremented by 1; and the case's readiness gates are recomputed. The case retains `device_lot_id` for the rest of its life, including after the units have been consumed, because the practice needs the billing trace even years later.

Units leave the reserved bucket by exactly three routes, described from the case's side in §3.3 and §3.7 and summarised here from the lot's. Completing the case moves them to `CONSUMED`: `device_state` becomes `CONSUMED`, the lot's `quantity_reserved` falls and its `quantity_consumed` rises by `device_quantity`, and the lot is re-billed on the **final** basis. Cancelling the case moves them to `RELEASED`: `quantity_reserved` falls and `quantity_available` rises, and the lot is re-billed **provisionally**. Marking the case a no-show moves them to `WASTED`: `quantity_reserved` falls and `quantity_wasted` rises, and the lot is again re-billed provisionally. All three increment the lot's `version` by 1 and re-derive its `billing_status`.

Unspent stock can also go back to the supplier. A `PracticeAdmin` returns units by naming a `quantity`, which may be no greater than the lot's `quantity_available` — reserved and consumed units cannot be returned, and an over-large request is refused with `ERR_CONSIGNMENT_RETURN_EXCEEDS_AVAILABLE`. The return reduces `quantity_available` and increases `quantity_returned` by that amount, re-bills the lot on the **final** basis with `billing_basis` set to `FINAL`, re-derives `billing_status`, and increments the lot's `version` by 1. A lot whose entire `quantity_received` has been returned with nothing consumed and nothing wasted ends up with `billed_amount_cents` of 0 and `billing_basis` `FINAL`.

**The chargeable value of a lot is assembled line by line, and the arithmetic is variant-dependent.** A *line* is one procedure case holding units of that lot, and the lines are enumerated in ascending `created_at` order — the order does not change the sum but is pinned so that the sequence of per-line roundings is reproducible. Each line's value is the lot's `unit_price_cents` multiplied by the applicable factor, multiplied by that case's `device_quantity`, and then **rounded half up to a whole number of cents**. `billed_amount_cents` is the sum of those already-rounded line values. Rounding once at the end, over the summed raw products, is not the rule and produces a different figure: with a `unit_price_cents` of 1999, a markup factor of 1.185 and three reserved lines of one unit each, the pinned per-line-then-sum order gives 2369 cents a line and 7107 cents in total, whereas summing first would give 7106 cents. The one-cent gap is the observable signature of the pinned order, and the pinned order is the one this service implements.

Two variants of that calculation exist, and `billing_basis` records which of them last ran.

- The `PROVISIONAL` variant runs whenever units are merely held or handed back while still unspent — that is, on a device reservation, on a case cancellation and on a case no-show. Its factor is 1 plus the 18.5% consignment markup, so 1.185, and it sums only the lines whose `device_state` is `RESERVED`. The volume rebate never applies on this basis.
- The `FINAL` variant runs once units have actually been spent — that is, on a case completion and on a supplier return. Its factor is 1 plus the 18.5% markup, less a 5-percentage-point volume rebate whenever the lot's `quantity_consumed` has reached at least 3; with the rebate active the factor is therefore 1.135 and without it 1.185. It sums only the lines whose `device_state` is `CONSUMED` or `WASTED`. Identical unit counts consequently produce different totals under the two bases: those same three one-unit lines at a `unit_price_cents` of 1999, once consumed with the rebate active, give 2269 cents a line and 6807 cents in total against the provisional 7107.

Lines whose `device_state` is `RELEASED` are selected by neither variant, and neither variant ever reads a lot's history — each run recomputes `billed_amount_cents` from the lines standing at that moment.

`billing_status` is a **compound status derived entirely from the lot's own unit counters**, never supplied by a client, and it is re-derived after every mutation of those counters using the following ordered tests. If `quantity_returned` equals `quantity_received` while both `quantity_consumed` and `quantity_wasted` are 0, the lot is `RETURNED`. Otherwise, if `quantity_available` and `quantity_reserved` are both 0 and the sum of `quantity_consumed` and `quantity_wasted` is at least 1, the lot is `INVOICED`. Otherwise, if the sum of `quantity_consumed` and `quantity_wasted` is at least 1, the lot is `PARTIALLY_INVOICED`. Otherwise it remains `CONSIGNED`. `INVOICED` and `RETURNED` are the terminal positions; a lot walks from `CONSIGNED` to `PARTIALLY_INVOICED` as its first units are spent while stock remains, and on to `INVOICED` when nothing is left either available or reserved. A lot may also go straight from `CONSIGNED` to `INVOICED` when a single operation spends the last of it, and straight from `CONSIGNED` to `RETURNED` when the whole lot goes back untouched. Whatever happens, the conservation rule of §2.10 holds: `quantity_received` always equals `quantity_available` plus `quantity_reserved` plus `quantity_consumed` plus `quantity_returned` plus `quantity_wasted`.

### 3.7. Readiness, Check-In and Completion

The single most-used read in the whole system is the front desk asking "can this case start?", and the service answers it with a computed, ordered list rather than a boolean. Every case carries `blocking_reason_codes`, the **ordered subsequence of gates the case currently fails**. Seven gates exist, they are evaluated in the fixed order below, and the array is deduplicated and emitted strictly in that order — never in the order the failures were discovered, and never sorted any other way.

1. `CLEARANCE_NOT_GRANTED` — the case's `clearance_status` is not `CLEARED` and not `PREP_WAIVED`.
2. `ROOM_ACCREDITATION_LAPSED` — the case's room is an `IMAGING_BAY` or a `URODYNAMICS_LAB`, and that room's `accreditation_expires_on` is either null or strictly before the logical clock date.
3. `PROVIDER_ACCREDITATION_LAPSED` — the case's room is an `IMAGING_BAY` or a `URODYNAMICS_LAB`, and the primary provider's `ultrasound_accreditation_expires_on` is either null or strictly before the logical clock date.
4. `ANTIBIOTIC_NOT_ADMINISTERED` — the procedure type's `requires_antibiotic` is true and no antibiotic order on this case has `status` `ADMINISTERED`.
5. `SCOPE_NOT_READY` — the procedure type's `requires_scope` is true and the assigned scope is not ready, meaning any one of: `scope_asset_id` is null, the scope's `status` is not `IN_USE`, the scope's `hld_pass_at` is null, or the scope's `reprocess_due_at` is non-null.
6. `CHECKIN_TOO_EARLY` — the logical clock instant is strictly earlier than `scheduled_start_at` less the thirty-minute early-arrival allowance.
7. `DEVICE_NOT_RESERVED` — the procedure type's `consignment_required` is true and the case's `device_state` is not `RESERVED`.

That ordering is not arbitrary: it is exactly the validation-priority order of §2.8, which is why the first element of `blocking_reason_codes` is always the very gate a check-in attempt would report. Each gate code maps to its check-in error by prefixing `ERR_`, with the single exception of `CLEARANCE_NOT_GRANTED`, whose check-in error is `ERR_CASE_NOT_CLEARED`.

The gates are recomputed on every mutation of the case — drafting, committing to the calendar, moving, acknowledging an instruction, deciding clearance, assigning a scope, reserving devices, checking in, completing, cancelling and marking a no-show all recompute them — and also whenever an antibiotic order on the case is placed or administered. They describe a *pending* check-in only, so a case whose `schedule_status` is `IN_ROOM`, `COMPLETED`, `CANCELLED` or `NO_SHOW` carries the empty array regardless of anything else about it. Computing the gates reads one case together with its procedure type, its room, its primary provider, its assigned scope when there is one, and every antibiotic order sharing its `case_id`.

A dedicated computed read exposes the whole picture for one case and is open to all seven roles. It reports the `case_id`, both dimensions as `schedule_status` and `clearance_status`, the ordered `blocking_reason_codes`, the convenience field `first_blocking_reason_code` — the first element of that array, or null when the array is empty — and `check_in_allowed`, a boolean that is true exactly when the case's `schedule_status` is `SCHEDULED` and the gate array is empty.

Bringing the patient into the room is a `ProcedureNurse`'s exclusive act and takes no payload; it advances `schedule_status` from `SCHEDULED` to `IN_ROOM`. Every one of the seven gates is enforced here, in the priority order above, so a case failing several reports only the first. On success the case's `checked_in_at` records the logical clock instant and `blocking_reason_codes` collapses to the empty array. Checking in a case that is not `SCHEDULED` — including a second check-in of a case already `IN_ROOM` — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CASE_TRANSITION`.

Closing out the procedure is available to the attending `ProcedureNurse` or the `Urologist`, optionally carrying an `outcome_note`, and it advances `schedule_status` from `IN_ROOM` to `COMPLETED`. Only an `IN_ROOM` case may be completed. There is one further precondition and it is easy to miss: **every specimen belonging to the case must already have left the room**, meaning no specimen with this `case_id` may still have `chain_status` `COLLECTED`. A case with an unhandled container is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CASE_TRANSITION`. On success `completed_at` records the logical clock instant, and the room's `occupied_block_count` is recomputed — a completed case still occupies its historical block, so the count does not fall.

Completion is the widest fan-out in the system, and it has four independent consequences beyond the case's own state.

The first is the **recall satisfaction cascade**. Because the case reached `COMPLETED`, the service looks for surveillance recalls belonging to that case's patient whose `status` is `SCHEDULED_DUE` or `OVERDUE` and whose protocol counts this procedure as a surveillance event — `STONE` recalls are satisfied by an `OFFICE_ULTRASOUND`, `BLADDER_CANCER` recalls by a `CYSTOSCOPY`, and `PROSTATE` recalls by a `PROSTATE_BIOPSY`. Among those candidates exactly **one** is chosen: the earliest `due_on`, with ties broken by the earlier `created_at`. That single recall transitions to `SATISFIED`, its `satisfying_case_id` is set to the completing case, and its `lapse_days` is reset to 0. Sibling recalls are left alone, and when the candidate set is empty no recall is touched at all. Then, because that recall reached `SATISFIED`, the patient's `outstanding_recall_count` is recomputed and the patient's `version` increments by 1 — the third hop of the cascade, and the reason a completion can invalidate a client's held `expected_patient_version`.

The second is scope soiling, described in full in §3.5: the assigned scope moves from `IN_USE` to `SOILED`, its `cycles_since_leak_test` rises by 1, its `reprocess_due_at` is set twelve hours out, its `version` increments, and the leak-test debt flips on once the counter reaches 10.

The third is device consumption, described in full in §3.6: reserved units become `CONSUMED`, the lot's counters shift, the lot is re-billed on the final basis, its `billing_status` is re-derived and its `version` increments.

The fourth is a deliberate non-consequence, worth stating because implementers guess otherwise: completing a `URODYNAMICS` case does **not** create a pelvic health referral. Referrals in this system are always entered explicitly by a clinician; nothing auto-creates them.

### 3.8. Pathology: Chain of Custody, Results and the Notification Loop

A specimen carries two independent state dimensions, just as a case does. `chain_status` follows the container — `COLLECTED`, then `ACCESSIONED` or `REJECTED`, then `RESULTED` — and `RESULTED` and `REJECTED` are its terminal positions. `notification_status` follows the conversation with the patient and advances on its own schedule: `NOT_REQUIRED`, `PENDING`, `ATTEMPTED`, `ACKNOWLEDGED`, `ESCALATED` and `FAILED`, with `ACKNOWLEDGED` and `FAILED` terminal.

Containers are labelled and collected in the room by the attending `Urologist` or `ProcedureNurse`. The payload names the `case_id`, the `collected_by_provider_id` — which must be the acting user's own identifier, a mismatch being `ERR_ACTOR_IDENTITY_MISMATCH` — the `container_label` written on the pot, the `specimen_type` of `PROSTATE_CORE`, `BLADDER_WASHING` or `BIOPSY_FRAGMENT`, and the `site_code` identifying the anatomic site sampled. The `container_label` must be unique among the specimens sharing that same `case_id`, though the same label may legitimately reappear on a different case; a within-case collision is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CONTAINER_LABEL`. Collection is only permitted where the case's procedure type has `requires_specimen` true and the case's `schedule_status` is `IN_ROOM` or `COMPLETED`; anything else is refused with `ERR_SPECIMEN_NOT_EXPECTED`. The new specimen records `collected_at` at the logical clock instant, `chain_status` `COLLECTED`, `notification_status` `NOT_REQUIRED` and `notification_attempt_count` 0, with `accession_number`, `received_at`, `condition_on_receipt`, `pathology_result`, `result_at` and `escalation_due_at` all null.

Receiving the container in the laboratory is the `PathologyLiaison`'s exclusive act and is the one place in this system where a single operation forks into two disjoint outcomes. The payload carries the laboratory's `accession_number`, the `received_at` instant the container arrived, and `condition_on_receipt`, either `INTACT` or `COMPROMISED`. Both branches share the same preconditions: the specimen's `chain_status` must still be `COLLECTED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_SPECIMEN_TRANSITION`; and transit must be sound, meaning `received_at` is not earlier than `collected_at` and no more than 24 hours after it, or the request is refused with `ERR_SPECIMEN_TRANSIT_EXPIRED`. Both branches record `accession_number`, `received_at` and `condition_on_receipt`. From there they diverge completely.

An `INTACT` container simply advances `chain_status` from `COLLECTED` to `ACCESSIONED` and does nothing else. The notification dimension stays at `NOT_REQUIRED`, and no recall is created.

A `COMPROMISED` container advances `chain_status` from `COLLECTED` to `REJECTED` and sets three further things in motion. First, the notification dimension opens: `notification_status` moves from `NOT_REQUIRED` to `PENDING`, `pathology_result` stays null because no grade was ever assigned, `escalation_due_at` is computed using the `INSUFFICIENT` grade window described below, and the patient's `open_notification_count` is recomputed. Second, if the specimen's source case was a `PROSTATE_BIOPSY`, a fresh surveillance recall is opened for that patient with `protocol` `PROSTATE`, `risk_tier` `INTERMEDIATE`, `anchor_date` set to the logical clock date, `due_basis` `PROTOCOL_ANCHORED`, and `source_specimen_id` pointing at this specimen — a ruined prostate core still has to be re-taken, so the surveillance obligation survives the container. Third, any pre-existing `PROSTATE` recall of that patient whose `status` is `SCHEDULED_DUE` or `OVERDUE` becomes `SUPERSEDED`, with the newest such row recorded as the new recall's `predecessor_recall_id`, after which the patient's `outstanding_recall_count` is recomputed and the patient's `version` increments by 1.

Filing the pathology result is likewise the `PathologyLiaison`'s exclusive act, takes a single `pathology_result` of `BENIGN`, `ATYPICAL`, `MALIGNANT` or `INSUFFICIENT`, and requires the specimen's `chain_status` to be `ACCESSIONED`; filing against any other chain state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_SPECIMEN_TRANSITION`. Filing runs a four-hop cascade, and each hop is caused by the one before it.

The first hop records the result on the specimen: `pathology_result` is stored, `result_at` is set to the logical clock instant, `chain_status` advances from `ACCESSIONED` to `RESULTED`, `notification_status` moves from `NOT_REQUIRED` to `PENDING` with `notification_attempt_count` still at 0, and `escalation_due_at` is computed. Because that hop filed a graded result, the second hop opens a surveillance recall — but only for a graded result, and `INSUFFICIENT` is not one. A result of `BENIGN`, `ATYPICAL` or `MALIGNANT` creates a recall for the case's patient whose `protocol` is determined by the specimen class (a `PROSTATE_CORE` maps to `PROSTATE`, a `BLADDER_WASHING` to `BLADDER_CANCER`, and a `BIOPSY_FRAGMENT` to `PROSTATE`) and whose `risk_tier` is determined by the grade (`MALIGNANT` maps to `HIGH`, `ATYPICAL` to `INTERMEDIATE`, and `BENIGN` to `LOW`), with `anchor_date` set to the logical clock date, `due_basis` `PROTOCOL_ANCHORED`, and `source_specimen_id` naming this specimen. An `INSUFFICIENT` result creates no recall, so the cascade stops after the first hop. Because the second hop inserted a new recall, the third hop supersedes the old ones: every *other* surveillance recall of that patient sharing the new recall's `protocol` whose `status` is `SCHEDULED_DUE` or `OVERDUE` is processed in ascending `created_at` order and transitions to `SUPERSEDED`, and the newest of those rows is recorded as the new recall's `predecessor_recall_id`. Because the third hop changed recall statuses, the fourth hop recomputes the patient's `outstanding_recall_count` and increments the patient's `version` by 1.

**The escalation clock decides how long the practice has to reach the patient, and its evaluation order is pinned.** The grade drives the base window: a `MALIGNANT` result must be communicated within 24 hours, an `ATYPICAL` result within 72 hours, a `BENIGN` result within 168 hours — a full week — and where no grade exists at all, which is the compromised-container case, the `INSUFFICIENT` window of 72 hours applies. The anchor instant is the specimen's `result_at` when a result has been filed; for a compromised container, where `pathology_result` and `result_at` both stay null, the anchor is the logical clock instant of the accession itself. From there, four steps run in this exact order. Step one adds the base window hours to the anchor, producing a provisional due instant. Step two tests **that provisional instant, and only that instant**: if it falls on a Saturday or a Sunday in UTC, a weekend extension of 48 hours applies, otherwise the extension is zero. Step three computes a backoff of 12 hours multiplied by the attempt count less one, floored at zero, where the attempt count is the value *after* the current attempt has been counted — so a first attempt contributes no backoff at all, a second contributes 12 hours and a third 24. Step four adds the weekend extension and the backoff to the provisional instant, and the sum is `escalation_due_at`. The weekend test is evaluated exactly once, against the provisional instant, and is **never** re-evaluated after the extension or the backoff have shifted the result — an escalation deadline pushed onto a Saturday by its own backoff does not earn a second extension.

Reaching the patient is recorded as one attempt at a time by a `PathologyLiaison` or a `ProcedureNurse`, naming the `outcome` — `REACHED` or `UNREACHABLE` — and the `channel` used. Attempts are accepted only while the notification dimension is `PENDING`, `ATTEMPTED` or `ESCALATED`; an attempt against a specimen whose `notification_status` is `NOT_REQUIRED`, `ACKNOWLEDGED` or `FAILED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_NOTIFICATION_TRANSITION`. Either outcome increments `notification_attempt_count` by 1.

An outcome of `REACHED` ends the loop: `notification_status` becomes `ACKNOWLEDGED` from any of `PENDING`, `ATTEMPTED` or `ESCALATED`, and the patient's `open_notification_count` is recomputed because the specimen has left the open set. An outcome of `UNREACHABLE` re-arms it, and which state it lands in depends on the incremented counter against a ceiling of three attempts. While the incremented `notification_attempt_count` is still strictly below 3, the specimen lands on `ATTEMPTED` — from `PENDING`, from `ATTEMPTED` itself as a self-loop, or from `ESCALATED` — and `escalation_due_at` is recomputed with the new attempt count, which pushes the deadline out by a further 12 hours each time. When the incremented count reaches exactly 3, the loop is abandoned: `notification_status` becomes `FAILED`, `escalation_due_at` is still recomputed with the final count, and the patient's `open_notification_count` is recomputed because a failed notification also leaves the open set.

### 3.9. Surveillance Recalls, the Recall Sweep and Pelvic Health Referrals

A surveillance recall is the practice's promise to look at a patient again. Only a `Urologist` may open one, and because opening one rewrites counters on the patient chart it is guarded by `expected_patient_version`. The payload names the `patient_id`, the `protocol` — `STONE`, `BLADDER_CANCER` or `PROSTATE` — the `risk_tier` of `LOW`, `INTERMEDIATE` or `HIGH`, the `anchor_date` as an ISO date in `YYYY-MM-DD` form from which the interval is measured, and optionally a `source_specimen_id` naming the pathology result that motivated the recall; a manually entered recall leaves `source_specimen_id` null. When a `source_specimen_id` is supplied, that specimen's `chain_status` must be `RESULTED`, and a specimen still in transit or already rejected is refused with `ERR_SOURCE_CASE_NOT_COMPLETED`.

**The recall interval is a lookup on the pair of `protocol` and `risk_tier`, and it lands in `interval_months`.** A `STONE` recall is due in 24 months at `LOW` risk, 12 months at `INTERMEDIATE` and 6 months at `HIGH`. `BLADDER_CANCER` runs on a tighter cadence: 12 months at `LOW`, 6 at `INTERMEDIATE`, 3 at `HIGH`. `PROSTATE` uses the same figures as `BLADDER_CANCER` — 12, 6 and 3 months respectively. There is no default and no fallback interval; every one of the nine combinations is defined above.

`due_on` is that interval added to a date, and the arithmetic is calendar-month arithmetic with a clamp rather than a rollover: advancing a date by *n* months lands in the target year and month, and the day of month is the smaller of the original day and the last day of the target month. A recall anchored on 31 March with a six-month interval is due on 30 September, not 1 October. February absorbs 29th, 30th and 31st anchors the same way, honouring leap years.

`due_basis` records which of the two variants produced `due_on`. **At creation the variant is always `PROTOCOL_ANCHORED`**: the interval is measured from the recall's own `anchor_date`, and the patient's procedure history is not consulted at all. The new row is stored with `status` `SCHEDULED_DUE`, `lapse_days` 0, `due_basis` `PROTOCOL_ANCHORED`, and `predecessor_recall_id` null unless there is something to supersede — and if there is, the same supersession rule that governs cascade-created recalls applies here: every other recall of the same `patient_id` and `protocol` whose `status` is `SCHEDULED_DUE` or `OVERDUE` becomes `SUPERSEDED`, the newest of them is recorded as this row's `predecessor_recall_id`, and the patient's `outstanding_recall_count` is recomputed with the patient's `version` incremented by 1. `satisfying_case_id` and `closed_reason` start null.

A recall leaves the open set in one of three ways. It can be **satisfied** by a completed surveillance case, which is CASCADE A hop 2 described earlier: the protocol's qualifying procedure codes are `OFFICE_ULTRASOUND` for `STONE`, `CYSTOSCOPY` for `BLADDER_CANCER` and `PROSTATE_BIOPSY` for `PROSTATE`, exactly one recall is satisfied per completion, chosen by earliest `due_on` and then earliest `created_at`, and it records `satisfying_case_id` and resets `lapse_days` to 0 whether it was `SCHEDULED_DUE` or `OVERDUE`. It can be **superseded** by a newer recall of the same protocol. Or a `Urologist` can **close** it administratively — a deceased patient, a transfer of care, a retired protocol — supplying a `closed_reason` that is stored on the row, which again recomputes the patient's `outstanding_recall_count` and increments the patient's `version` by 1. Closing is permitted only from `SCHEDULED_DUE` or `OVERDUE`; closing an already-satisfied, already-superseded or already-closed recall is refused with `ERR_INVALID_RECALL_TRANSITION`. `SATISFIED`, `SUPERSEDED` and `CLOSED` are all terminal.

**The recall sweep is a system checkpoint over the whole recall book and the notification queue, and it runs three steps in a pinned order.** A `PracticeAdmin` or a `Urologist` may trigger it; it takes no payload.

Step 1 recomputes `due_on` for every recall whose `status` is `SCHEDULED_DUE` or `OVERDUE`, this time under the `EVENT_ANCHORED` variant. That variant asks a different question from the creation-time variant: instead of measuring from the recall's own `anchor_date`, it measures from the patient's most recent actual surveillance event. It gathers every procedure case of the same patient whose `schedule_status` is `COMPLETED` and whose procedure type code is one of the protocol's qualifying codes — cases still in `DRAFT`, `SCHEDULED` or `IN_ROOM`, and cases that ended in `CANCELLED` or `NO_SHOW`, are all excluded. If that set is empty the variant falls back to the anchor: `due_on` is the interval added to `anchor_date` and `due_basis` is written as `PROTOCOL_ANCHORED`. If the set is non-empty, the anchoring case is the one with the latest `scheduled_start_at`, and where two share the same start instant the one created later wins; `due_on` becomes the interval added to the date part of that case's `scheduled_start_at`, and `due_basis` is written as `EVENT_ANCHORED`. The two variants genuinely disagree on the same row — a `BLADDER_CANCER` recall at `INTERMEDIATE` risk anchored on 2026-03-31 is due 2026-09-30 while it is protocol-anchored, but once a qualifying `CYSTOSCOPY` completes the due date moves to six months after that cystoscopy instead.

Step 2 then reconciles `status` against the freshly recomputed dates. A recall sitting at `SCHEDULED_DUE` whose `due_on` is now strictly before the logical clock's date moves to `OVERDUE`, and `lapse_days` is set to the whole number of days from `due_on` to the clock date, floored at zero. A recall due exactly on the clock date is not late and stays `SCHEDULED_DUE`. In the other direction, a recall sitting at `OVERDUE` whose step-1 recompute produced a `due_on` on or after the clock date is pulled back to `SCHEDULED_DUE` with `lapse_days` reset to 0. Because step 1 always runs before step 2 inside a single sweep, a recall whose event-anchored due date has just moved into the future never appears as `OVERDUE` at all in that sweep — it is recomputed first and never lapses.

Step 3 works the notification queue rather than the recall book. Every specimen whose `notification_status` is `PENDING` or `ATTEMPTED` and whose `escalation_due_at` is strictly before the logical clock instant moves to `ESCALATED`. Escalation is a supervisory flag, not an attempt, so `notification_attempt_count` is left exactly as it was, and the specimen remains open to further attempts. Across all three steps the sweep recomputes `outstanding_recall_count` and `open_notification_count` for every patient it touched.

The sweep reports what it did. Its response carries `swept_at` holding the logical clock instant of the sweep, `recomputed_recall_count` for the number of recalls step 1 recomputed, `overdue_recall_count` for the number step 2 moved into `OVERDUE`, `restored_recall_count` for the number step 2 pulled back to `SCHEDULED_DUE`, and `escalated_specimen_count` for the number of notifications step 3 escalated. A sweep that finds nothing to do is not an error; it returns all four counters at zero.

**Pelvic health referrals are the practice's other longitudinal thread, and they carry a conserved visit ledger.** A `Urologist` or a `PelvicHealthCoordinator` opens one naming the `patient_id`, the `source_case_id` of the case that generated it, the `program` — `PELVIC_FLOOR_PT`, `BEHAVIORAL_TRAINING` or `BIOFEEDBACK` — and the `requested_visits` the referring clinician asked for, which must be at least 1. A referral may also be direct, with `source_case_id` null; but when a source case *is* named it must have reached `schedule_status` `COMPLETED`, and naming a case that has not is refused with `ERR_SOURCE_CASE_NOT_COMPLETED`. The new referral starts at `status` `REQUESTED` with `visits_authorized`, `visits_used`, `visits_remaining` and `visits_forfeited` all 0, and with `waitlist_position`, `therapy_start_by`, `first_visit_date` and `decline_reason` all null.

Every subsequent decision belongs to the `PelvicHealthCoordinator` alone, and the `decision` field forks four ways. A decision that is not declared from the referral's current `status` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REFERRAL_TRANSITION`.

`ACCEPT` is the only branch that opens the ledger. It is accepted from `REQUESTED` and from `WAITLISTED`, requires `authorized_visits` in the body at 1 or above, and where the referral names a source case that case must be `COMPLETED`. It is also the only branch with a deadline: the logical clock's date must be on or before the source case's `completed_at` date plus 30 days, and a referral accepted more than 30 days after its source procedure finished is refused with `ERR_THERAPY_START_WINDOW_MISSED`. On acceptance `visits_authorized` becomes the smallest of three numbers — the `requested_visits` recorded on the referral, the `authorized_visits` the payer approved, and the programme's own ceiling, which is 8 visits for `PELVIC_FLOOR_PT`, 4 for `BEHAVIORAL_TRAINING` and 6 for `BIOFEEDBACK`. `visits_remaining` is set equal to `visits_authorized`, `visits_used` and `visits_forfeited` are set to 0, `waitlist_position` is cleared back to null, and `therapy_start_by` is set to the source case's `completed_at` date plus 30 days — or, for a direct referral with no source case, to the logical clock date plus 30 days. That date is the deadline by which the first logged visit must fall.

`DECLINE` is terminal and ledgerless. It is accepted from `REQUESTED` and from `WAITLISTED`, requires a `decline_reason` in the body which is stored on the row, and leaves every visit counter at zero. `WAITLIST` is accepted only from `REQUESTED` and grants a queue position instead of a ledger: `waitlist_position` becomes 1 plus the number of referrals in the same `program` already sitting at `status` `WAITLISTED`, counted immediately before this mutation is applied. The waitlist is a detour, not a dead end — a waitlisted referral can later be accepted, rejoining the accepted branch with exactly the acceptance side effects above.

`WITHDRAW` is the compensating action for the ledger and is accepted from `ACCEPTED`, `WAITLISTED` and `COMPLETED`. Whatever is left in `visits_remaining` is added to `visits_forfeited` and `visits_remaining` is then set to 0, so units leave the available bucket without ever leaving the referral; for a waitlisted or completed referral the amount moved happens to be zero. A withdrawal from `WAITLISTED` additionally clears `waitlist_position` to null. `DECLINED` and `WITHDRAWN` are the referral's terminal states.

Visits are recorded one at a time on an accepted referral, with an `action` of `LOG` or `VOID_LAST` and, for a log, the `visit_date` on which the therapy session took place. `LOG` moves one unit across the ledger: `visits_used` increases by 1, `visits_remaining` decreases by 1, and `first_visit_date` holds the earliest logged visit date still standing. When that log drives `visits_remaining` to 0 the referral auto-advances from `ACCEPTED` to `COMPLETED` in the same operation, with no further ledger movement beyond the unit just logged. `VOID_LAST` reverses the most recent log: `visits_used` decreases by 1, `visits_remaining` increases by 1, and `first_visit_date` is recomputed as the earliest remaining logged visit date, or null when the ledger has been emptied. Voiding the very visit that completed a referral returns it from `COMPLETED` to `ACCEPTED` with the unit restored, so a coordinator who logs a session in error can undo it without reopening the authorisation.

Both actions are guarded by the ledger itself: `LOG` requires `visits_remaining` to be at least 1, `VOID_LAST` requires `visits_used` to be at least 1, and a violation of either is refused with `ERR_VISIT_ALLOCATION_EXHAUSTED`. Note where that check sits relative to the transition guard: `VOID_LAST` is a declared action on every accepted referral, so voiding against an empty ledger is *not* an undeclared transition — it passes the transition guard and is caught by the allocation check, which is why it answers `ERR_VISIT_ALLOCATION_EXHAUSTED` rather than `ERR_INVALID_REFERRAL_TRANSITION`. Logging a visit on a referral that is `REQUESTED`, `DECLINED`, `WAITLISTED` or `WITHDRAWN`, by contrast, is undeclared and answers `ERR_INVALID_REFERRAL_TRANSITION`. After every one of these calls the ledger identity holds exactly: `visits_authorized` equals `visits_remaining` plus `visits_used` plus `visits_forfeited`.
