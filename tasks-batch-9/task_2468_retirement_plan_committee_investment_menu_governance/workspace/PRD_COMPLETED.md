# Retirement Plan Committee Governance API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

When a retirement plan fiduciary committee is sued, the defence is not the committee's judgement — it is the committee's paper trail. A prudent process that was never documented is indistinguishable, in litigation, from no process at all. This service exists to make that paper trail a structural property of the system rather than an administrative afterthought: every menu change traces back to a scored quarterly review, every review traces back to an adopted investment policy statement, every decision traces back to a motion that carried at a quorate meeting whose minutes were approved, and every committee member who participated in that vote can be shown to have completed fiduciary training and signed a documented acceptance of responsibility.

The system serves six constituencies with deliberately non-overlapping powers. A plan sponsor's administrator builds and maintains the governance structure and holds the compensating powers that reverse decisions after the fact. The committee chair runs meetings and executes the committee's approvals. Ordinary committee members sign their own acceptances, complete their own training, and cast their own votes. The investment consultant produces every recommendation and approves none of them. The recordkeeper executes what the committee has authorised — participant notices, asset mappings, ledger postings — and authorises nothing itself. An independent auditor reads everything and writes nothing. This segregation is not advisory: it is enforced per endpoint, and a role acting outside its lane is rejected before any business rule is evaluated.

Two mechanical concerns shape the design. First, the plan's investment menu is a **conserved resource**: a fixed slot capacity is partitioned at all times into occupied, reserved and available positions, and every seating, approval, execution and retirement moves exactly one position between partitions. Second, the plan carries a **derived posture** — a plan-wide gate computed from how much of the menu is under surveillance — that can freeze child operations without any operator touching a switch. Because that posture moves as a side effect of scoring a quarter, and because scoring a quarter can be reversed, the specification pins snapshot fields on the affected rows so that a reopen restores the prior world exactly rather than approximately. Implementers should treat those snapshot fields as first-class contract, not as internal bookkeeping.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request other than the logical-clock control endpoint must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the actor, and `X-User-Role`, naming the actor's role. If either header is absent or empty, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the six role names below, the request is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_USER_CONTEXT` (HTTP 400). If both headers are well formed but the named role is not authorised for the endpoint being called, the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The six roles, spelled exactly as they must appear in `X-User-Role`, are `PlanSponsorAdmin`, `CommitteeChair`, `CommitteeMember`, `InvestmentConsultant`, `Recordkeeper` and `PlanAuditor`.

Two of those roles are backed by persisted entities. When `X-User-Role` is `CommitteeChair`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting committee member row; ownership and presence checks compare committee-member references — the attendance roster, a vote's `member_id`, an acceptance target — against `X-User-ID`, and the resolved member's seat must additionally have a `seat_class` of `CHAIR`. When `X-User-Role` is `CommitteeMember`, `X-User-ID` is likewise the `id` of the acting committee member row, and the same ownership comparisons apply without the chair-seat requirement. For both of these roles, if `X-User-ID` does not resolve to a committee member whose seat belongs to the plan named or implied by the request — or if a `CommitteeChair` caller's seat is not a `CHAIR` seat — the request is rejected with `ERR_ACTOR_NOT_COMMITTEE_MEMBER` (HTTP 403). This check is evaluated on meeting creation, convene, adjourn and minutes approval, on motion creation, vote casting and motion closing, on review finalization, on replacement approval, revision request and approval rescission, and on levelization approval.

The remaining four roles carry opaque identity. When `X-User-Role` is `PlanSponsorAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it for audit purposes but never compares it to any entity's `id`. The same holds when `X-User-Role` is `InvestmentConsultant`, when it is `Recordkeeper`, and when it is `PlanAuditor`: each is an opaque operator identifier, never resolved against a stored row.

Where a role acts on its own behalf, self-identity is enforced separately and more strictly. A committee member may only sign their own acceptance of fiduciary responsibility and may only complete their own training record; a member may only cast their own vote, and the chair may not vote on an absentee's behalf. Any of these attempted on another member's behalf — the vote payload's `member_id` differing from `X-User-ID`, or an acceptance or completion targeting a different member — is rejected with `ERR_ACTOR_NOT_SELF` (HTTP 403).

The role split is a segregation-of-duties control and is intentionally lopsided. `PlanSponsorAdmin` cannot vote, cannot close a motion, cannot approve minutes, and cannot approve either a fund replacement or a levelization run — but it alone may reopen a finalized review, unwind an executed mapping, reverse a posted levelization run, terminate a provider engagement and award a request for proposal. `CommitteeChair` may approve but may not post a levelization run, may not issue a participant notice, may not execute a mapping, and may not reverse or unwind anything. `InvestmentConsultant` produces every recommendation — policy drafts, slots, funds, scorecards, proposals, levelization drafts, provider benchmarks — and approves none of them, casts no votes and touches no ledger. `Recordkeeper` issues notices, executes mappings and posts approved runs, and never approves, votes, reopens, reverses or unwinds. `PlanAuditor` holds no write capability on any business entity; it may read every entity and may move the logical clock, and that read-only posture is what makes the audit trail credible.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.
- The same envelope covers failures raised by the transport rather than by a business rule. A request to a path no endpoint claims answers HTTP 404 with `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`; a request to a real path with a method that path does not define answers HTTP 405 with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; any other framework-level 4xx answers `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` at its own status; and an unexpected server-side failure answers HTTP 500, again with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A trailing slash is never significant, so `/governance/plans` and `/governance/plans/` address the same endpoint.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, may be no smaller than 1 and no larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may be no smaller than 0. A value outside those bounds is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending unless a narrative rule for a specific collection states otherwise. The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit` and `offset` as echoed back, `total_count` as the number of rows matching the filters before the page window is applied, and `has_more` as a boolean that is true exactly when rows remain beyond the current window.

Two collections have a deterministic secondary ordering key that exists precisely so cascades and listings are reproducible. Committee seats carry `seat_index`, unique per plan and at least 1, which is the charter ordering used for seat-scoped iteration. Menu slots carry `slot_index`, unique per plan and at least 1, which is the menu ordering used as the iteration order for every slot-level cascade described later in this document. Both indices are unique across *every* row on the plan including retired ones, so a retired seat's or slot's index is never reissued; those two collections also order their own list reads by `created_at` ascending and then by that index.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Stamps and the Two Clocks

Every persisted resource is addressed by `id`, a server-minted UUID with no type prefix and no client-supplied component. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recorded when the row was first written. All fourteen entity types in this system — plans, committee seats, committee members, fiduciary training records, investment policy statements, asset-class slots, funds, quarterly reviews, fund scorecards, committee meetings, motions, fund replacement proposals, fee levelization runs and service provider engagements — additionally carry `updated_at`, refreshed to the current instant on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. There is no entity in this system that omits `updated_at` or `version`.

Two distinct notions of time coexist, and confusing them is the single most common implementation error in this domain.

The **logical clock** is a server-held UTC instant. Before any call to the clock-control endpoint it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and the server never falls back to real wall-clock time. The clock may be moved forward or backward by supplying a `current_utc` instant. Every temporal comparison in the system reads this clock: participant-notice lead times, blackout window containment, unwind windows, committee term intervals, and the timestamps written into `acceptance_signed_at`, `minutes_approved_at`, `finalized_at`, `closed_at`, `notice_delivered_at`, `posted_at`, `reversed_at` and each vote's `cast_at`. Moving the clock recomputes no entity field; in particular, fiduciary training currency is quarter-indexed and is therefore unaffected by a clock move.

The **fiscal epoch** is the plan's `fiscal_quarter_index`, an integer that defaults to 0 at plan registration and advances only through the plan's explicit quarter-advance operation. Everything with a `_quarter_index` suffix is measured against it: `charter_adopted_quarter_index`, `assigned_quarter_index`, `completed_quarter_index`, `adopted_quarter_index`, `superseded_quarter_index`, `at_risk_since_quarter_index`, `watch_started_quarter_index`, `last_scored_quarter_index`, `finalized_quarter_index`, `last_benchmarked_quarter_index` and `last_rfp_quarter_index`. Windows expressed in quarters — training validity, reopen eligibility, levelization reversal, re-benchmarking cadence, request-for-proposal cadence — are all evaluated as integer arithmetic on quarter indices and never by comparing dates.

### 2.5. A Flat Address Space with Referential Discipline

The governance graph is deep but its address space is deliberately flat: every entity is reachable through its own top-level collection, and no entity's `id` ever appears as a path prefix of another entity's collection. Relationships are carried in request bodies and exposed through filtered list reads. A plan's seats are found by filtering seats on `plan_id`; a review's scorecards by filtering scorecards on `review_id`; a proposal's slot by the `slot_id` it names.

Identifier resolution is strictly ordered, and the order is part of the contract. Path identifiers are resolved left to right first; any that fails to resolve to a live row yields `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) before any body field is examined. Body references are then resolved in this declared field order: `plan_id`, `seat_id`, `member_id`, `ips_id`, `slot_id`, `fund_id`, `review_id`, `meeting_id`, `motion_id`, `subject_id`, `outgoing_fund_id`, `incoming_fund_id`, `approving_motion_id`, `successor_engagement_id`, `supersedes_scorecard_id`, then each element of `pool_fund_ids`, then each element of `attendee_member_ids`. The first unresolvable reference in that order determines the error.

Once every reference resolves, all of them must belong to the same plan. A write carrying two or more entity references whose owning plans differ — a slot from one plan and a fund from another, a review and a meeting from different plans, a levelization pool containing a foreign fund, or a member whose seat sits on a different plan — is rejected with `ERR_CROSS_PLAN_REFERENCE` (HTTP 422). Where the polymorphic subject of a motion is involved, a subject that does not resolve within its declared `subject_type`, or an approving motion whose subject does not match the row being approved, is rejected with `ERR_MOTION_SUBJECT_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422).

A motion's `subject_type` fixes how its `subject_id` is interpreted: `IPS_ADOPTION` resolves against an investment policy statement, `FUND_REPLACEMENT` against a fund replacement proposal, `TARGET_DATE_SELECTION` against a fund, `FEE_LEVELIZATION` against a fee levelization run, `RFP_AWARD` against a service provider engagement, and `WATCH_LIST_ACTION` against a fund.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency on the Plan and on the Review

Two writes in this system read a parent aggregate, derive a decision from it, and then withdraw or supersede sibling rows. Both are protected by optimistic concurrency control against that parent's `version`, and both use a non-optional request field.

Creating a fund replacement proposal requires `expected_plan_version` in the request body, carrying the `version` of the plan the client last read. If `expected_plan_version` does not equal the plan's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PLAN_VERSION` and HTTP 412; no proposal row is written and no prior draft is withdrawn. The reason the guard is mandatory here is that the create reads the plan's capacity partition (`menu_slot_capacity`, `occupied_menu_slots`, `reserved_menu_slots`, `available_menu_slots`), reads `menu_posture` and `draft_proposal_count`, and withdraws any prior draft proposal on the same `slot_id`. On success the endpoint increments the plan's `version` by 1, so a second caller still holding the earlier snapshot is rejected on its next attempt and must re-read the plan — which may by then be frozen or capacity-exhausted — before retrying.

Submitting a fund scorecard requires `expected_review_version` in the request body, carrying the `version` of the quarterly review the client last read. If `expected_review_version` does not equal the review's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_REVIEW_VERSION` and HTTP 412, with no scorecard written and no supersession applied. That guard exists because the write supersedes the existing submitted scorecard for the same review and fund, recomputes `scorecard_count` and `submitted_scorecard_count`, and on the first scorecard after opening or reopening drives the review's automatic move into its scoring state. On success the endpoint increments the review's `version` by 1, so a consultant working from a snapshot taken before a colleague's submission must re-read the review. Note the interaction with the ordering rules in §2.11: if the review has meanwhile advanced to its minutes-pending or finalized state, the state check fires first and the caller sees `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_SCORING` (HTTP 409) rather than the 412.

No other endpoint in this system takes a version field. Every other write is last-writer-wins on its own row.

### 2.7. Decimal Discipline: Basis Points, Cents, Percentages and Ratios

Money is always an integer count of cents; there are no fractional cents anywhere, and no rounding occurs when cents move between rows. Costs, expense ratios and revenue-sharing rates are always integer basis points on the input side, though derived cost figures may carry two decimal places.

Scores and percentages carry two decimal places. This applies to the five scorecard component subscores (`performance_component`, `peer_component`, `expense_component`, `style_component`, `manager_component`), to `composite_score`, to `latest_composite_score`, to the policy thresholds `watch_trigger_score_max`, `remediation_trigger_score_max` and `cure_margin_points`, to `glidepath_fit_score`, to `equity_at_65_pct`, and to derived cost figures in basis points.

Ratios and fractions carry four decimal places: `style_drift_r2` and `style_drift_r2_floor`, `at_risk_slot_ratio`, `voting_threshold_pct`, `carried_threshold_pct` and `support_pct`.

Every rounding operation in this specification rounds **half up**, at the decimal scale stated for the quantity being produced. Where a calculation prescribes an order of rounding steps — and several do — that order is part of the contract and may not be optimised away. The composite score in particular rounds each of its five components to two decimal places *before* weighting them and then rounds the weighted sum to two decimal places exactly once; a single-rounding shortcut produces different answers at the boundaries and is wrong. Intermediate arithmetic is carried in decimal with four decimal places of intermediate precision, never in binary floating point, and the weighted terms are summed in the fixed order performance, peer, expense, style, manager so that no accumulation-order dependence exists.

Where an integer division is prescribed, the truncation direction is stated explicitly as flooring, and flooring happens at exactly the point stated — for revenue-credit accrual, per fund before any pooling, never once over a summed pool.

### 2.8. Motions, Minutes and the Fiduciary-Defense Documentation Gate

Five decisions in this system may only take effect on the strength of committee action that has been documented. Adopting an investment policy statement, approving a fund replacement proposal, approving a fee levelization run, awarding a request for proposal, and finalizing a quarterly review all sit behind the same two-part gate.

The first part is the motion itself. The decision must name an `approving_motion_id` referencing a motion whose `status` is `CARRIED`, whose `subject_type` matches the kind of row being approved, and whose `subject_id` equals that row's `id`. A motion that is not carried, or whose subject does not match, is rejected with `ERR_MOTION_NOT_CARRIED` (HTTP 409). A carried motion is spent once consumed: rescinding a replacement approval clears the proposal's `approving_motion_id` back to null, and recomputing a levelization run clears its `approving_motion_id` back to null, in both cases requiring a fresh motion before the decision can be retried.

The second part is the minutes. The meeting behind that carried motion must itself have reached `MINUTES_APPROVED`; until it does, the decision the motion authorises is rejected with `ERR_MINUTES_NOT_APPROVED` (HTTP 409). Approving minutes records `minutes_doc_ref` — the fiduciary-defense artefact, which must be at least 6 characters — stamps `minutes_approved_at`, and freezes the meeting's `carried_motion_count`. This is the single unlock point for all five downstream decisions, and it exists so that no fiduciary decision can be traced back to a vote that was never written up.

Motion states are `OPEN`, `CARRIED`, `FAILED`, `TABLED` and `WITHDRAWN`, of which the last four are terminal. A tabled motion can never be reopened; if the committee still wants the outcome, a new motion must be moved.

The paper trail extends to narrative as well as to references. Several lifecycle actions accept a free-text `reason` recording why the actor took the step. It is required on reopening a finalized review, on rescinding a replacement approval, on unwinding an executed mapping, on reversing a posted levelization run, and on terminating a provider engagement — every one of them a compensating or destructive act. It is optional on changing a seat's status and on retiring a menu slot. The revision-request action takes an analogous `revision_note` carrying the chair's instruction back to the consultant. These narrative fields are stored with the row they justify and are never interpreted by any rule.

### 2.9. Menu Posture: the Plan-Wide Derived Gate

Every plan carries `menu_posture`, a computed enumeration over `OPEN`, `RESTRICTED` and `FROZEN`. It is never set directly by any operator. A newly registered plan begins `FROZEN`, because it has neither an adopted charter nor an adopted investment policy statement.

Posture is derived from `at_risk_slot_ratio`, which measures how much of the live menu is under surveillance. Count the plan's asset-class slots whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, `AT_RISK` or `PENDING_REPLACEMENT`; that count is the denominator. Count those same slots whose `status` is `AT_RISK` or `PENDING_REPLACEMENT`; that count is the numerator. When the denominator is zero, `at_risk_slot_ratio` is exactly 0.0000; otherwise it is the numerator divided by the denominator, rounded half up to four decimal places. Slots in `EMPTY` or `RETIRED` are counted in neither figure.

The posture ladder is then evaluated top down, stopping at the first arm that matches:

1. The posture is `FROZEN` when the plan's `charter_status` is anything other than `ADOPTED`, or the plan has no investment policy statement in `ADOPTED` status, or the number of `ACTIVE` committee members occupying `FILLED` voting seats on the plan is below `quorum_min_voting_members`, or `at_risk_slot_ratio` is at least 0.5000.
2. Otherwise the posture is `RESTRICTED` when `at_risk_slot_ratio` is at least 0.2500 and below 0.5000, or at least one fund on the plan has `watch_status` of `TERMINATION_PENDING`, or the plan's `fee_disclosure_status` is `RESTATEMENT_REQUIRED`.
3. Otherwise the posture is `OPEN` — which requires, jointly, an `ADOPTED` charter, exactly one `ADOPTED` investment policy statement, at least `quorum_min_voting_members` active members in filled voting seats, `at_risk_slot_ratio` strictly below 0.2500, no fund in `TERMINATION_PENDING`, and `fee_disclosure_status` of `COMPLIANT`.

The boundaries are inclusive on the lower side. A ratio of exactly 0.2500 is `RESTRICTED` while 0.2499 is `OPEN`; a ratio of exactly 0.5000 is `FROZEN` while 0.4999 is `RESTRICTED`. With four counted slots, one at risk is exactly 0.2500 and therefore `RESTRICTED`; with five counted slots, one at risk is 0.2000 and therefore `OPEN`. A plan whose charter is not adopted is `FROZEN` regardless of the ratio.

Posture is recomputed as a side effect of charter adoption, policy-statement adoption, seating a fund, retiring a slot, finalizing a review, reopening a review, executing a replacement, activating a member, ending a member's term, changing a seat's status, advancing the fiscal quarter, and the explicit posture-recompute capability — and equally of appointing a member, registering a fund, refreshing a fund's monitoring data, changing a fund's share class, selecting a target-date suite, snapshotting or posting or reversing a levelization pool, and every replacement-proposal write from creation through unwind, because each of those moves a figure the posture is derived from. Every one of those recomputations, not merely the explicit capability, also releases the plan's `BLOCKED` replacement proposals back to their `pre_block_status` whenever the recomputed posture comes out `OPEN`, and each recomputation re-derives `at_risk_slot_ratio`, the capacity partition, `draft_proposal_count`, `total_plan_assets_cents`, the credit ledger and `documented_acceptance_count` from the underlying rows before the ladder is evaluated. A `FROZEN` posture rejects every menu-changing write with `ERR_MENU_FROZEN` (HTTP 409): seating a fund, creating, submitting, revising, approving or executing a replacement proposal, selecting a target-date suite, and changing a fund's share class. A `RESTRICTED` posture is narrower — it blocks only those writes that touch a slot whose `status` is `AT_RISK` or `PENDING_REPLACEMENT`, and the rejection carries the same `ERR_MENU_FROZEN`.

### 2.10. Conserved Quantities and Exact Compensation

Three quantities in this system are conserved, and every write that touches them must leave the conservation equation true.

**The capacity partition.** At all times, `occupied_menu_slots` plus `reserved_menu_slots` plus `available_menu_slots` equals `menu_slot_capacity`, and `available_menu_slots` is never negative. Occupied counts asset-class slots whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, `AT_RISK` or `PENDING_REPLACEMENT`. Reserved counts fund replacement proposals whose `status` is `APPROVED` or `NOTICE_ISSUED` — each in-flight replacement holds a second menu position, because for a while both the outgoing and the incoming fund are committed. Seating a fund moves one position from available to occupied. Approving a proposal moves one from available to reserved. Executing or rescinding an approval returns the reserved position to available. Retiring a slot returns an occupied position to available. A write that would push `available_menu_slots` below zero is rejected with `ERR_MENU_CAPACITY_EXHAUSTED` (HTTP 409) before any counter moves; in particular, an available count of zero blocks both fund seating and proposal approval, and a rescission must free a position first. Rescinding an approval restores the partition to exactly its pre-approval values. None of the four figures is maintained by incremental arithmetic: `occupied_menu_slots` and `reserved_menu_slots` are recounted from the qualifying rows and `available_menu_slots` is re-derived as `menu_slot_capacity` minus the two of them at every posture recomputation, so the movements described above are consequences of that recount. `draft_proposal_count` is derived the same way, as the number of proposals on the plan still in `DRAFT`. Creating an asset-class slot performs no capacity test at all, so a plan may carry more slots than `menu_slot_capacity`; it is the seating and approval checks that keep occupied-plus-reserved inside the capacity.

**The plan-wide asset total.** `total_plan_assets_cents` is the sum of `plan_assets_cents` across funds whose `menu_status` is `ACTIVE` or `PENDING_REMOVAL`, re-derived from those rows at every posture recomputation rather than adjusted in place. Mapping participant assets from an outgoing fund to a receiving fund is an exact integer move with no rounding: the sum across all fund rows is identical before an execution and after the corresponding unwind, to the cent.

**The revenue-credit ledger.** At all times, `revenue_credit_balance_cents` equals `revenue_credit_accrued_cents` minus `revenue_credit_allocated_cents`. Only `revenue_credit_allocated_cents` is a running total; the other two are derived, and at every posture recomputation `revenue_credit_accrued_cents` is re-derived as the sum, across every fund on the plan whatever its `menu_status` or `credit_accrual_status`, of that fund's `accrued_revenue_credit_cents` plus its `levelized_credit_cents`, with the balance re-derived as that sum minus allocated. Accrued credit is monotonic and never decreases, precisely because the only writes to a fund's pair of credit figures either raise their sum, when fresh accrual is brought up to date, or move cents from one to the other. Allocated credit is the cumulative amount consumed by posted levelization runs, net of reversed ones. The equality is re-asserted after every levelization posting and every reversal. Posting adds the run's `allocated_total_cents` to allocated, and moves each pool fund's line amount out of that fund's `accrued_revenue_credit_cents` and into its `levelized_credit_cents`. Reversing subtracts the same `allocated_total_cents` from allocated and restores each pool fund's two figures line by line from the stored allocation lines. A posting that would drive `revenue_credit_balance_cents` below zero is rejected with `ERR_CREDIT_POOL_INSUFFICIENT` before any field is written; a posting that lands the balance at exactly zero is legal. A run can be reversed at most once, because the reversed state is terminal.

Compensating actions in this system restore state **exactly**, from stored snapshots, rather than recomputing an approximation. Four snapshot fields exist solely for that purpose: an asset-class slot's `status_before_last_review`, a fund's `watch_status_before_last_review` and `menu_status_before_last_review`, and a replacement proposal's `pre_block_status`. Each is written immediately before the cascade that would otherwise destroy the information, and each is read by the compensating action to restore the field it shadows. A fund's `last_scored_quarter_index` is the guard that keeps a reopen from reverting funds that a *later* review has since rescored.

### 2.11. Cascade Ordering and Deterministic Iteration

Several operations in this system fan out across many rows. Where they do, the iteration order is specified, because a cascade that visits rows in a different order can produce a different final state and because the audit trail must be reproducible.

The rules are uniform. Slot-level cascades iterate asset-class slots by `slot_index` ascending. Where a cascade visits both seated and unseated funds, seated funds are visited first in their slots' `slot_index` order, and unseated funds afterwards in `created_at` ascending order. Proposal-level cascades iterate by `created_at` ascending, and where they are nested inside a slot iteration, by `slot_index` ascending then `created_at` ascending. The blocked-proposal sweeps are of the nested kind in both directions: both the blocking sweep and the release sweep iterate by the `slot_index` of each proposal's slot ascending and then by proposal `created_at` ascending, whichever write triggers them. Motion sweeps — tabling, withdrawing — iterate motions by `created_at` ascending. Policy-statement retirement sweeps iterate by `created_at` ascending. No cascade ever falls back to the server-minted `id` as an ordering key.

Where an allocation waterfall distributes indivisible cents, the ordering keys are stated with the calculation and are total: fund `plan_assets_cents` descending, then fund `created_at` ascending, then `ticker` ascending as the final tie-break. Again, `id` is never consulted.

### 2.12. Validation Order and Error Precedence

When a single request violates more than one rule, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fully determined by the following priority ladder. Lower numbers are evaluated first. This ordering is a contract: an implementation that returns a correct-but-later error for a request that also violates an earlier rule is wrong.

1. **Priority 10 — user context.** Both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` present, non-empty, and the role one of the six named roles. Otherwise `ERR_MISSING_USER_CONTEXT` (400). Applies to every endpoint except the logical-clock control endpoint.
2. **Priority 20 — role authorisation.** The acting role appears in the endpoint's authorised list. Otherwise `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403). Applies to every endpoint except the logical-clock control endpoint.
3. **Priority 25 — committee identity.** For the entity-backed roles `CommitteeChair` and `CommitteeMember`, `X-User-ID` resolves to a committee member whose seat belongs to the plan named or implied by the request, and a `CommitteeChair` caller's seat has `seat_class` of `CHAIR`. Otherwise `ERR_ACTOR_NOT_COMMITTEE_MEMBER` (403). Applies to meeting creation, convene, adjourn and minutes-approve; motion creation, vote casting and motion close; review finalize; replacement approve, request-revision and rescind-approval; and levelization approve. It does not apply to target-date selection, which authorises the chair by role alone.
4. **Priority 27 — self-identity on voting.** A vote's `member_id` equals `X-User-ID`. Otherwise `ERR_ACTOR_NOT_SELF` (403). Applies to vote casting.
5. **Priority 30 — existence.** Every path identifier resolves, left to right; then every body reference resolves, in the declared field order given in §2.5. Otherwise `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (404). Applies to every endpoint.
6. **Priority 35 — single-plan referential integrity.** Every referenced row belongs to the same plan. Otherwise `ERR_CROSS_PLAN_REFERENCE` (422). Applies to every write carrying two or more entity references.
7. **Priority 38 — request shape and field domains.** Enum values in range; money and basis-point integers non-negative; percentages within 0.00 to 100.00; decimal scale respected (two places for scores and percentages, four places for `style_drift_r2` and ratios); closed date intervals with start no later than end; half-open blackout windows with start strictly before end; the five policy weights each present, recognised and inside 0.00 to 1.00 — a set that is individually well formed but does not sum to exactly 1.00 is still a 400 at this priority, but under the distinct identifier `ERR_IPS_WEIGHTS_INVALID`, raised both when the statement is drafted and again when it is adopted; a proposal `rationale` of at least 40 characters on submission; a `minutes_doc_ref` of at least 6 characters on minutes approval. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Applies to every write.
8. **Priority 42 — state.** The target row is in a state from which the requested transition is declared. Where the situation matches a specific state error, that specific error is raised in preference to the generic one: `ERR_CHARTER_NOT_ADOPTED`, `ERR_MEETING_NOT_IN_SESSION`, `ERR_MEETING_IN_SESSION`, `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_SCORING`, `ERR_FUND_NOT_CANDIDATE`, `ERR_SLOT_NOT_REPLACEABLE` and `ERR_NOTICE_ALREADY_ISSUED`. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (409). Applies to every state-changing endpoint.
9. **Priority 44 — menu posture.** Not `FROZEN` for any menu-changing write; under `RESTRICTED`, no write touching an `AT_RISK` or `PENDING_REPLACEMENT` slot. Otherwise `ERR_MENU_FROZEN` (409). Applies to fund seating, replacement create, submit, revise, approve and execute, target-date selection, and share-class change.
10. **Priority 46 — optimistic concurrency.** `expected_plan_version` equals the plan's `version` on replacement creation, yielding `ERR_STALE_PLAN_VERSION` (412) on mismatch; `expected_review_version` equals the review's `version` on scorecard submission, yielding `ERR_STALE_REVIEW_VERSION` (412) on mismatch. Both sit *below* the state checks at priority 42, which is why a review that has advanced past scoring rejects a stale scorecard with `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_SCORING` (409) rather than a 412.
11. **Priority 50 — acting-member fitness.** Every acting or voting committee member has `fiduciary_training_status` of `CURRENT` and a non-null `acceptance_signed_at`. Otherwise `ERR_TRAINING_NOT_CURRENT` (409). Applies to meeting convene, minutes approval, vote casting, and review finalize. On vote casting the voter's own `status` must additionally still be `ACTIVE`, and a voter who is not answers with this same `ERR_TRAINING_NOT_CURRENT` rather than a state error.
12. **Priority 52 — conflict-of-interest recusal.** A member whose `affiliated_provider_role` is non-null must vote `RECUSE` on any motion whose `subject_type` is `RFP_AWARD` and whose subject engagement carries that same `provider_role`, and on any `FEE_LEVELIZATION` motion when the member's `independence_flag` is false. A `FOR`, `AGAINST` or `ABSTAIN` vote from such a member is rejected with `ERR_RECUSAL_REQUIRED` (409), and the request-for-proposal award re-checks the closed tally for the same violation. Applies to vote casting and to request-for-proposal award.
13. **Priority 55 — policy selection criteria.** The candidate fund satisfies every selection criterion of the plan's adopted policy statement — or, on a revision, of the proposal's `pinned_ips_id`. Otherwise `ERR_FUND_FAILS_IPS_CRITERIA` (422). Applies to fund seating, replacement submission, replacement revision, and target-date selection.
14. **Priority 56 — asset class and designation compatibility.** The fund's `asset_class_code` equals the slot's `asset_class_code`, and a slot whose `designation` is `QDIA` accepts only a fund whose `is_target_date_suite` is true. Otherwise `ERR_ASSET_CLASS_MISMATCH` (422). Applies to fund seating, replacement creation and replacement submission.
15. **Priority 58 — quorum.** The meeting's `quorum_met` is true. Otherwise `ERR_QUORUM_NOT_MET` (409). Applies to motion close, minutes approval, and review presentation.
16. **Priority 60 — carried motion.** As described in §2.8. Otherwise `ERR_MOTION_NOT_CARRIED` (409). Applies to policy-statement adoption, replacement approval, levelization approval, request-for-proposal award, and target-date selection.
17. **Priority 61 — minutes gate.** As described in §2.8. Otherwise `ERR_MINUTES_NOT_APPROVED` (409). Applies to policy-statement adoption, replacement approval, levelization approval, request-for-proposal award, and review finalization.
18. **Priority 62 — an adopted policy statement exists.** The plan has exactly one investment policy statement in `ADOPTED` status whenever a policy-dependent write is attempted. Otherwise `ERR_NO_ADOPTED_IPS` (409). Applies to slot creation, fund seating, review opening, review finalization, target-date selection, and replacement creation, submission and approval. On fund seating this check is taken *ahead* of the policy-selection criteria at priority 55, the asset-class compatibility at priority 56 and the pinned-version check at priority 63, which on that endpoint alone run after it in that order.
19. **Priority 63 — pinned policy version still current.** The pinned policy identifier on the target row — a review's `ips_id`, a slot's `ips_id`, a proposal's `pinned_ips_id` — still equals the plan's currently adopted policy identifier. Otherwise `ERR_IPS_VERSION_STALE` (409). Applies to review finalization, replacement approval, and fund seating.
20. **Priority 65 — uniqueness.** Seat code unique per plan; fund `ticker` unique per plan; asset class and designation unique per plan; quarter index unique per plan among reviews; one submitted scorecard per review and fund; one non-expired training record per member and module; `version_label` unique per plan among policy statements; at most one `QDIA`-designated slot per plan. Otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE` (409). Applies to every create endpoint.
21. **Priority 66 — seat term non-overlap.** Described in §3.2. Otherwise `ERR_SEAT_TERM_OVERLAP` (409). Applies to member appointment.
22. **Priority 67 — engagement term non-overlap.** An engagement's closed term interval must not overlap another engagement on the same plan and `provider_role` whose `status` is `PENDING`, `ACTIVE` or `UNDER_RFP`. Otherwise `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_TERM_OVERLAP` (409). Applies to engagement creation and engagement benchmarking.
23. **Priority 70 — notice period elapsed.** Described in §3.7. Otherwise `ERR_NOTICE_PERIOD_NOT_ELAPSED` (409). Applies to notice issuance and mapping execution.
24. **Priority 72 — blackout window integrity.** Described in §3.7. Otherwise `ERR_BLACKOUT_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409). Applies to notice issuance and mapping execution.
25. **Priority 74 — unwind window open.** The clock is strictly before `blackout_end_at`. Otherwise `ERR_UNWIND_WINDOW_CLOSED` (409). Applies to mapping unwind.
26. **Priority 75 — quarter windows.** A review may be reopened only while the plan's `fiscal_quarter_index` minus the review's `quarter_index` is at most 1; a levelization run may be approved only in its own quarter and reversed only within one quarter of its own quarter; a re-benchmark requires the plan's `fiscal_quarter_index` to be strictly greater than the engagement's `last_benchmarked_quarter_index`. Otherwise `ERR_QUARTER_CLOSED` (409). Applies to review reopen, levelization approve, levelization recompute, and levelization reverse.
27. **Priority 76 — repetition limits.** A review may be reopened at most twice, so a `reopen_count` already at 2 yields `ERR_REOPEN_LIMIT_REACHED` (409); a reopen is also forbidden once any decision authorised at the review's meeting has reached `APPROVED`, `NOTICE_ISSUED` or `EXECUTED`, which yields `ERR_REVIEW_DECISIONS_EXECUTED` (409); a proposal may cycle through revision at most three times, so a `revision_round` already at 3 yields `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED` (409). Applies to review reopen and to replacement revision requests.
28. **Priority 78 — scorecard coverage.** Every asset-class slot whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, `AT_RISK` or `PENDING_REPLACEMENT` has a `SUBMITTED` scorecard for its `current_fund_id` in this review, and at least one scorecard exists at all. Otherwise `ERR_SCORECARD_COVERAGE_INCOMPLETE` (422). Applies to review presentation.
29. **Priority 79 — rolling window availability.** A scorecard scored under the `ROLLING_SINCE_WATCH` variant requires the fund to have a non-null `watch_started_quarter_index` and both rolling fields populated. Otherwise `ERR_ROLLING_WINDOW_UNAVAILABLE` (422). Applies to scorecard submission.
30. **Priority 80 — menu capacity.** `available_menu_slots` is at least 1 before a seating or an approval reserves a position, and the capacity partition holds after the write. Otherwise `ERR_MENU_CAPACITY_EXHAUSTED` (409). Applies to fund seating and replacement approval.
31. **Priority 82 — credit pool solvency.** The run's `credit_pool_cents` is no greater than the plan's `revenue_credit_balance_cents`, checked at approval and re-checked at posting, and every pool fund has `credit_accrual_status` of `ACCRUING`. Otherwise `ERR_CREDIT_POOL_INSUFFICIENT` (409), or `ERR_POOL_FUND_INELIGIBLE` (422) for the pool-membership arm. Applies to levelization creation, approval and posting — the pool-membership arm is checked at creation too, before the pool is ever snapshotted, while the solvency arm is not.
32. **Priority 83 — allocation balance.** `allocated_total_cents` plus `remainder_cents` equals the eligible pool, and every allocation line amount is non-negative. Otherwise `ERR_ALLOCATION_NOT_BALANCED` (422). Applies to levelization posting.
33. **Priority 84 — fee-disclosure integrity.** The plan's `fee_disclosure_status` is not `RESTATEMENT_REQUIRED`. Otherwise `ERR_FEE_DISCLOSURE_RESTATEMENT_REQUIRED` (409). Applies to notice issuance and levelization posting.
34. **Priority 85 — share-class eligibility.** Described in §3.8. Otherwise `ERR_SHARE_CLASS_INELIGIBLE` (422). Applies to share-class change.
35. **Priority 86 — training module rules.** `FIDUCIARY_DUTIES` may never be waived, yielding `ERR_MODULE_NOT_WAIVABLE` (422); a completed record requires `score_pct` of at least 80.00, otherwise `ERR_TRAINING_SCORE_INSUFFICIENT` (422); a waived record requires a `waiver_basis` of at least 12 characters. Applies to training-record creation and training-record completion. On creation these waiver rules run *ahead* of the uniqueness check at priority 65, so a waiver naming `FIDUCIARY_DUTIES` answers `ERR_MODULE_NOT_WAIVABLE` even when an open record for that module already exists; on completion the record's own `ASSIGNED` state is checked ahead of both the self-identity comparison and the score floor.
36. **Priority 87 — fiduciary acceptance present.** A member cannot be activated without both `acceptance_signed_at` and `acceptance_doc_ref`, regardless of training completion. Otherwise `ERR_FIDUCIARY_ACCEPTANCE_MISSING` (409). Applies to member activation.
37. **Priority 88 — structural minimums.** A plan must retain at least one `QDIA`-designated slot and at least one `PENDING` or `ACTIVE` `RECORDKEEPER` engagement; a `QDIA` slot may not be retired, yielding `ERR_QDIA_SLOT_REQUIRED` (409), and a sole recordkeeper may not be terminated, yielding `ERR_RECORDKEEPER_REQUIRED` (409). Applies to slot retirement and engagement termination.
38. **Priority 89 — open-work guards.** A slot with a non-terminal proposal on it may not be retired; an engagement may not be terminated while a `NOTICE_ISSUED` mapping is outstanding on the plan; a seat may not be retired while it holds a non-terminal member. Otherwise `ERR_SLOT_HAS_OPEN_PROPOSAL` (409). Applies to slot retirement, seat status change, and engagement termination.
39. **Priority 90 — divisor sanity.** The plan's `participant_account_count` is greater than zero before a per-capita levelization, yielding `ERR_NO_PARTICIPANT_ACCOUNTS` (422); the plan's `total_plan_assets_cents` is greater than zero before a provider benchmark, yielding `ERR_NO_PLAN_ASSETS` (422); and a motion tally has a non-empty, non-recused voting base, yielding `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_VOTERS` (422). Applies to levelization creation, approval and recomputation, engagement benchmarking, and motion close.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

This section describes the domain in the order a plan actually lives it: the plan and its charter, the committee that governs it, the policy statement and the menu skeleton, the meetings at which decisions are taken, the quarterly surveillance cycle, the watch-list ladder, fund replacement and participant mapping, the target-date and fee-levelization work, and finally provider oversight and the quarter advance. Every rule that a caller can observe is stated here; where a rule also constrains the shape of a request, the priority ladder in §2.12 decides which error wins.

### 3.1. Registering a Plan and Adopting the Committee Charter

A plan is the root of everything. Registering one accepts the sponsor's legal name in the request field `plan_sponsor_name`, which is stored on the plan as `sponsor_name`; a `plan_name` for display; a `plan_type` of `401K`, `403B` or `457B`; an `erisa_covered` boolean defaulting to true; a `participant_account_count` of at least 1; and a `menu_slot_capacity` between 3 and 40 inclusive. Four charter parameters may be seeded at registration and each has a default: `quorum_min_voting_members` defaults to 3 and must lie between 2 and 9; `voting_threshold_pct` defaults to 0.6000 and must lie between 0.5000 and 1.0000; `max_voting_seats` defaults to 7 and must lie between 2 and 11; `notice_lead_days_required` defaults to 30 and need only be non-negative, with no upper bound. A `blackout_buffer_days` value, default 3 and likewise merely non-negative, records the mandatory cooling buffer appended to a blackout window before the next window may open on the same plan. `plan_sponsor_name` and `plan_name` each run from 1 to 120 characters. None of these charter parameters is editable afterwards except through charter adoption, and `menu_slot_capacity`, `participant_account_count`, `erisa_covered` and `blackout_buffer_days` are fixed for the life of the plan.

Registration sets `fiscal_quarter_index` to 0, `charter_status` to `DRAFT`, `menu_posture` to `FROZEN`, `fee_disclosure_status` to `COMPLIANT`, `at_risk_slot_ratio` to 0.0000, `charter_adopted_quarter_index` to null, and zeroes the derived counters `occupied_menu_slots`, `reserved_menu_slots`, `draft_proposal_count`, `total_plan_assets_cents`, `revenue_credit_accrued_cents`, `revenue_credit_allocated_cents`, `revenue_credit_balance_cents` and `documented_acceptance_count`. `available_menu_slots` starts equal to `menu_slot_capacity`.

Adopting the charter is what turns a registered plan into a governed one. It is a plan-sponsor act. Three preconditions apply. The plan must have at least two committee seats with `voting` true and `status` other than `RETIRED`, and it must have no more such seats than the `max_voting_seats` value submitted in this very request; either failure is `ERR_CHARTER_SEATS_INSUFFICIENT`, which is also the error raised when adoption would leave the plan with fewer filled voting seats than `quorum_min_voting_members`. The submitted `quorum_min_voting_members` must be no greater than the submitted `max_voting_seats`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. All three of `quorum_min_voting_members`, `voting_threshold_pct` and `max_voting_seats` are optional on this request and each defaults to the value the plan already carries, so an adoption naming none of them re-adopts the charter on its existing terms; whatever value each resolves to is the value the seat tests above are measured against. On success `charter_status` becomes `ADOPTED`, `charter_adopted_quarter_index` is set to the current `fiscal_quarter_index`, those three charter parameters are overwritten with the resolved values, and `charter_doc_ref` may be supplied to record the charter document — omitting it leaves whatever reference the plan already held rather than clearing it. Menu posture is then recomputed — leaving `FROZEN` only becomes possible once a charter exists *and* an adopted policy statement exists. The plan's `version` increments by 1.

The same act re-adopts an amended charter. An already-`ADOPTED` plan passes through `SUPERSEDED` and is immediately re-set to `ADOPTED` inside the same request: `SUPERSEDED` is a within-request pass-through state visible only in the audit trail, and the response always shows `ADOPTED`. Re-adoption requires that no committee meeting on the plan is `IN_SESSION`, otherwise `ERR_MEETING_IN_SESSION`, and that test is taken before the three seat tests, so a plan sitting in session answers `ERR_MEETING_IN_SESSION` even when its seat roster would have failed as well. Because the charter basis has changed underneath them, every motion on any of the plan's meetings whose `status` is `OPEN` is moved to `TABLED`, iterated by motion `created_at` ascending. `charter_adopted_quarter_index` is refreshed and `version` increments by 1.

Until the charter is adopted, convening a meeting and adopting a policy statement both fail with `ERR_CHARTER_NOT_ADOPTED`.

### 3.2. Seats, Appointments, Fiduciary Training and Documented Acceptance

A committee seat is a charter position, not a person. Creating one takes `plan_id`, a `seat_code` of 1 to 60 characters unique among that plan's non-`RETIRED` seats, a `seat_class` of `CHAIR`, `SPONSOR_FIDUCIARY`, `FINANCE`, `HR`, `PARTICIPANT_REP` or `INDEPENDENT`, a `voting` boolean, and a `seat_index` of at least 1 that is unique across every seat on the plan including `RETIRED` ones — unlike `seat_code`, whose uniqueness test ignores retired seats — and serves as the deterministic charter ordering key. A new seat starts `VACANT` with `consecutive_vacant_quarters` at 0.

Appointing a member fills the seat. The request supplies `seat_id`, the appointee's legal name in the field `member_name`, 1 to 120 characters — stored on the member as `person_name` — a `person_email` of 1 to 200 characters unique per seat among appointments in `APPOINTED`, `TRAINING_ASSIGNED`, `ACCEPTANCE_PENDING` or `ACTIVE`, a closed term interval `term_start_date` to `term_end_date` where the end is no earlier than the start, an optional `acceptance_doc_ref`, and an `affiliated_provider_role` of `NONE`, `RECORDKEEPER`, `ADVISOR`, `AUDITOR` or `CUSTODIAN` defaulting to `NONE`. The derived `independence_flag` is true exactly when `affiliated_provider_role` is `NONE`. Three preconditions guard the appointment: the seat must be `VACANT`, otherwise `ERR_SEAT_NOT_VACANT`; the new closed term must not intersect any existing appointment on this seat in a non-terminal status, and adjacency is forbidden — a gap of a single day counts as abutment, so the successor's `term_start_date` must be at least two days later than the predecessor's `term_end_date`, otherwise `ERR_SEAT_TERM_OVERLAP`, and the duplicate-email test is taken after the vacancy check but ahead of this one; and if the seat is a voting seat, the count of that plan's `FILLED` voting seats plus one must not exceed `max_voting_seats`, otherwise `ERR_VOTING_SEATS_EXCEEDED`. On success the seat becomes `FILLED` with `consecutive_vacant_quarters` reset to 0, a member row is created in `APPOINTED` with `fiduciary_training_status` of `PENDING` and `votes_cast_count` at 0, and the plan's menu posture is recomputed.

From `APPOINTED` a member reaches `ACTIVE` only by clearing two independent branches that may run in either order. The training branch begins when the first training record for that member is created: the record is written with `status` of `ASSIGNED` and `assigned_quarter_index` equal to the plan's `fiscal_quarter_index`, the member moves from `APPOINTED` to `TRAINING_ASSIGNED`, and `fiduciary_training_status` is recomputed — it stays `PENDING` until every required module has a current record. This branch deliberately does *not* touch `documented_acceptance_count`. If the member is not in `APPOINTED` the record is still created but no member transition occurs. A creation request may also close the record on the spot: an `action` of `WAIVE` or `WAIVED`, or a bare `waiver_basis` with no `action` field at all, makes the record born `WAIVED` with `completed_quarter_index` already stamped at the plan's `fiscal_quarter_index`, subject to the same 12-character `waiver_basis` floor and the same `ERR_MODULE_NOT_WAIVABLE` refusal on `FIDUCIARY_DUTIES` that a later waiver faces — and those two waiver tests run *before* the per-module duplicate test, so a request that both waives a module illegitimately and duplicates an open record answers with the waiver error. A `score_pct` supplied at creation is validated and stored on the still-`ASSIGNED` record without completing it, and is discarded when the record is born waived. Either way the member still moves out of `APPOINTED` and `fiduciary_training_status` is still recomputed. The acceptance branch begins when the member signs their own acceptance of fiduciary responsibility: `X-User-ID` must equal the member's `id`, otherwise `ERR_ACTOR_NOT_SELF`, `acceptance_signed_at` must still be null, otherwise `ERR_ACCEPTANCE_ALREADY_SIGNED`, and the member's own `status` must be `APPOINTED` or `TRAINING_ASSIGNED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` — those three tests are taken in exactly that order. Signing stamps `acceptance_signed_at` with the logical clock, stores `acceptance_doc_ref` when one is supplied and otherwise leaves whatever reference the appointment already recorded rather than clearing it, and — this branch's distinguishing side effect — increments the plan's `documented_acceptance_count` by 1. That count is nonetheless a derived aggregate: every menu-posture recomputation re-derives it from scratch as the number of members on the plan that carry an `acceptance_signed_at` and whose `status` is `ACCEPTANCE_PENDING` or `ACTIVE`, so the increments and decrements described here only ever agree with the recount, and a member who leaves those two states for any reason — including the quarter-advance term-expiry sweep — drops out of the count at the next recomputation without anyone adjusting it. A member in `APPOINTED` moves to `ACCEPTANCE_PENDING`; a member already in `TRAINING_ASSIGNED` also moves to `ACCEPTANCE_PENDING`, retaining whatever `fiduciary_training_status` it had.

A training record is closed out by a completion act carrying an `action` of `COMPLETED` or `WAIVED`, defaulting to `COMPLETED` when the field is absent. The record must still be `ASSIGNED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, and that state test is taken after the request-shape validation of `waiver_basis` but ahead of the actor test, the score floor and the `ERR_MODULE_NOT_WAIVABLE` refusal — so a record that is already `COMPLETED`, `WAIVED` or `EXPIRED` answers with the state error whoever calls it and whatever they send. A completion is the member's own act: `X-User-ID` must equal the record's `member_id`, otherwise `ERR_ACTOR_NOT_SELF`, and `score_pct` must be present and at least 80, otherwise `ERR_TRAINING_SCORE_INSUFFICIENT`. `score_pct` is a percentage carrying up to two decimal places from 0.00 to 100.00 wherever it is supplied, so 80.00 clears the bar and 79.99 does not, while a third decimal place or a figure outside that range is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A waiver is the sponsor's act: it requires a `waiver_basis` — the priority-86 floor is 12 characters, and the record transition itself states a minimum of 8, so an implementation that enforces 12 satisfies both — and it may never be applied to the `FIDUCIARY_DUTIES` module, which yields `ERR_MODULE_NOT_WAIVABLE`. Either outcome sets `completed_quarter_index` to the plan's `fiscal_quarter_index`; a waiver leaves `score_pct` null. Each record carries `validity_quarters`, default 8, constrained to 1 through 20, and a `module_code` of `FIDUCIARY_DUTIES`, `IPS_MECHANICS`, `FEE_BENCHMARKING` or `PROHIBITED_TRANSACTIONS`, unique per member among records that are `ASSIGNED`, `COMPLETED` or `WAIVED`.

Training currency is derived, never set. A record counts as current when its `status` is `COMPLETED` or `WAIVED` and the plan's `fiscal_quarter_index` minus the record's `completed_quarter_index` is strictly less than that record's `validity_quarters`. The required module set is exactly `FIDUCIARY_DUTIES` and `IPS_MECHANICS`; the other two modules are informational. `fiduciary_training_status` is `CURRENT` when every required module has a current record; `EXPIRED` when every required module has a record but at least one is stale, meaning the quarter gap has reached or passed that record's `validity_quarters`; and `PENDING` otherwise, that is, whenever at least one required module has no completed or waived record at all. A required module counts as *having a record* as soon as one of its records carries a `completed_quarter_index`, and that includes a record the quarter-advance sweep has since restamped `EXPIRED` — so a member whose validity has run out reads `EXPIRED` rather than falling back to `PENDING`. A record that is still `ASSIGNED`, and equally one that a removal for cause moved from `ASSIGNED` to `EXPIRED` without it ever being completed or waived, carries no `completed_quarter_index` and therefore does not count. Currency itself is stricter than that: only a record whose own `status` is still `COMPLETED` or `WAIVED` can be current, so a restamped record can never be. With the default validity of 8 quarters, a gap of exactly 8 is `EXPIRED` while 7 is still `CURRENT`. Because `FIDUCIARY_DUTIES` can never be waived, that module's currency always rests on a completion scoring at least 80.

Activation is the join point, and it is the sponsor's act. The member must first be in `ACCEPTANCE_PENDING` or `TRAINING_ASSIGNED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Four further preconditions then apply: `acceptance_signed_at` must be non-null *and* an `acceptance_doc_ref` must be on record — recorded either at appointment or at signing — otherwise `ERR_FIDUCIARY_ACCEPTANCE_MISSING`; `fiduciary_training_status` must be `CURRENT`, otherwise `ERR_TRAINING_NOT_CURRENT`; the seat's `status` must be `FILLED`, otherwise `ERR_SEAT_NOT_ACTIVE`; and the logical clock's date must fall inside the inclusive interval from `term_start_date` to `term_end_date`, otherwise `ERR_TERM_NOT_IN_EFFECT`. On success the member becomes `ACTIVE`, at which point it counts toward quorum and may cast votes, and the plan's menu posture is recomputed — a plan can leave `FROZEN` here once active voting members reach `quorum_min_voting_members`. Activation is declared from `TRAINING_ASSIGNED` as well, but a member in that state has by definition not signed acceptance, so that path always returns `ERR_FIDUCIARY_ACCEPTANCE_MISSING`. The training branch alone can never reach `ACTIVE`.

Ending a term is also the sponsor's act and carries an `end_term_reason` of `RESIGNATION`, `REMOVAL_FOR_CAUSE` or `TERM_EXPIRY`. From `ACTIVE`, a resignation moves the member to `RESIGNED` and a removal for cause moves it to `REMOVED`; both require that no `OPEN` motion on any meeting of this plan already records a vote by this member, otherwise `ERR_OPEN_MOTION_VOTE_LOCK` — a lock consulted only while the member is `ACTIVE`, so a departure from any earlier state never looks at open motions. Either outcome sets the seat back to `VACANT` when the seat was `FILLED` and leaves it untouched otherwise, so a departure from a `SUSPENDED` seat leaves that seat suspended, freezes `votes_cast_count` while retaining the historical votes for fiduciary defense, decrements `documented_acceptance_count` by 1 — never below zero — when `acceptance_signed_at` had been set *and* the status being left behind was `ACCEPTANCE_PENDING` or `ACTIVE`, and recomputes menu posture — which may cascade a plan from `OPEN` straight to `FROZEN` when active voting members fall below `quorum_min_voting_members`. A removal for cause additionally moves every one of that member's `ASSIGNED` training records to `EXPIRED` and then refreshes `fiduciary_training_status`, and it does so from whatever state the member was in rather than only from `ACTIVE`. From `APPOINTED`, a removal for cause moves the member to `REMOVED` and vacates the seat with no vote-lock test and no acceptance decrement, while a resignation from `APPOINTED` is refused outright with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` — an appointment that has not yet reached acceptance may only end by removal for cause or by term expiry. From `ACCEPTANCE_PENDING`, a resignation moves the member to `RESIGNED`, vacates the seat, and decrements `documented_acceptance_count` by 1. `TERM_EXPIRY` is accepted from every non-terminal state and moves the member to `TERM_EXPIRED`; a member already in a terminal state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` before any reason-specific rule is consulted. Whichever reason is given it is recorded on the member as `end_term_reason`, and the closing posture recomputation re-derives `documented_acceptance_count` in any case.

Seat status is changed by a single sponsor act taking an `action` of `SUSPEND`, `REINSTATE` or `RETIRE`, optionally accompanied by a free-text `reason` of at least one character that is recorded on the seat as `status_change_reason` on whichever of the three paths succeeds. Suspension is legal from `VACANT` or `FILLED` — anything else is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` — and, from `FILLED`, additionally requires that no meeting on the plan is `IN_SESSION`, otherwise `ERR_MEETING_IN_SESSION`; a suspended seat counts toward neither `eligible_voting_seat_count` nor quorum, and suspending a filled seat recomputes menu posture, while suspending a vacant one has no further effect. Reinstatement returns a suspended seat to `FILLED` when it still holds a non-terminal appointment and to `VACANT` when it does not, recomputing posture on both of those outcomes. Retirement is legal from `VACANT` or `SUSPENDED` only — otherwise `ERR_SEAT_NOT_VACANT` — requires that the plan would still retain at least two non-retired voting seats after this seat is removed from that count, otherwise `ERR_CHARTER_SEATS_INSUFFICIENT`, and is refused with `ERR_SLOT_HAS_OPEN_PROPOSAL` while the seat still holds a non-terminal member — that holder test coming last, after the state check and after the two-voting-seat floor. Retirement recomputes menu posture and is terminal.

### 3.3. The Investment Policy Statement, the Menu Skeleton and Fund Eligibility

The investment policy statement is the rulebook every later decision is measured against, and it is versioned. Drafting one takes `plan_id`, a `version_label` of 1 to 60 characters unique per plan such as IPS-2026-A, an optional `effective_date` — a calendar date stored verbatim, left null when omitted and never itself enforced against the logical clock — and a `scoring_variant` of either `TRAILING_PERIOD` or `ROLLING_SINCE_WATCH` which selects the composite-score variant for every review pinned to this version. It also takes the thresholds that drive surveillance: `watch_trigger_score_max`, default 40.00, within 0.00 to 100.00; `remediation_trigger_score_max`, default 25.00, which must be strictly less than `watch_trigger_score_max`; `cure_margin_points`, default 10.00, the number of points above the watch trigger a watched fund must score before it may be cleared, within 0.00 to 100.00; `expense_peer_multiple`, default 1.25, carried to four decimal places within 0.0000 to 10.0000; `min_track_record_quarters`, default 12, any non-negative integer; `style_drift_r2_floor`, default 0.8000, within 0.0000 to 1.0000; and `watch_grace_quarters`, default 2, within 1 and 8, the number of consecutive breach quarters in remediation before termination is recommended. Two structured objects complete it. `weights` is required and carries the five composite weights under the keys `performance`, `peer`, `expense`, `style` and `manager`, each between 0.00 and 1.00 at two decimal places, and the five must sum to exactly 1.00; a `weights` object that is absent, is not an object, omits one of the five keys, sets one of them null or carries any sixth key is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, whereas five individually valid weights that miss the 1.00 total are `ERR_IPS_WEIGHTS_INVALID` already at draft time. `benchmark_map` maps each `asset_class_code` to a benchmark code; it is optional, defaults to an empty object, must be an object when it is supplied, and is carried for the record only — no scorecard input is ever validated against it. Those request-shape tests are taken in the order the fields are listed here, with the `remediation_trigger_score_max` versus `watch_trigger_score_max` comparison after all of them and the duplicate-`version_label` check last of all. A new statement starts `DRAFT` with `adopted_quarter_index` and `superseded_quarter_index` null.

Adopting a statement is the chair's act and passes through the documentation gate. The plan's `charter_status` must be `ADOPTED`, otherwise `ERR_CHARTER_NOT_ADOPTED`. The statement itself must still be `DRAFT`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` — a test taken immediately after the charter gate and before anything at all is asked of the motion, so re-adopting an already `ADOPTED`, `SUPERSEDED` or `RETIRED` statement answers with the state error rather than with a motion error. The referenced motion must exist with `subject_type` of `IPS_ADOPTION` and `subject_id` equal to this statement's `id`, otherwise `ERR_MOTION_SUBJECT_MISMATCH`; it must be `CARRIED`, otherwise `ERR_MOTION_NOT_CARRIED`; and its meeting must be `MINUTES_APPROVED`, otherwise `ERR_MINUTES_NOT_APPROVED`. The five `weights` entries must sum to exactly 1.00, otherwise `ERR_IPS_WEIGHTS_INVALID`, and `remediation_trigger_score_max` must be strictly below `watch_trigger_score_max`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. On success the statement becomes `ADOPTED`, `adopted_quarter_index` is stamped with the plan's `fiscal_quarter_index`, and `approving_motion_id` is stored. Any other statement on the plan that was `ADOPTED` becomes `SUPERSEDED` with `superseded_quarter_index` stamped, and every other `DRAFT` statement on the plan is `RETIRED`, iterated by `created_at` ascending. Menu posture is recomputed, which is where a plan first becomes capable of leaving `FROZEN`.

Version pinning is what makes supersession safe. A review already pinned to a superseded statement keeps reading its criteria for that review's whole life; new reviews pin the newly adopted version. A replacement proposal whose `pinned_ips_id` points at the superseded version must be revised before it can be approved, and an approval attempted against a stale pin fails with `ERR_IPS_VERSION_STALE`. `RETIRED` is terminal.

Asset-class slots are the menu skeleton. Creating one takes `plan_id`, an optional `ips_id` naming the statement that defined it — omitted, it is filled in from the plan's currently adopted statement; supplied, it is stored exactly as given, so a slot may be born already pinned to a superseded version — an `asset_class_code` from `US_LARGE_CAP`, `US_SMALL_CAP`, `INTL_DEVELOPED`, `EMERGING_MKTS`, `CORE_BOND`, `TIPS`, `STABLE_VALUE`, `TARGET_DATE`, `REAL_ASSETS` or `SELF_DIRECTED` and unique per plan among non-`RETIRED` slots, a `designation` of `CORE`, `SPECIALTY` or `QDIA` with at most one `QDIA` slot per plan among non-retired slots, and a `slot_index` of at least 1, unique across every slot on the plan including `RETIRED` ones, that is the deterministic menu ordering key and the cascade iteration order for slot-level side effects. Slot creation requires the plan to have exactly one adopted statement, otherwise `ERR_NO_ADOPTED_IPS`, and that requirement is tested before the duplicate scans, so a plan without one answers `ERR_NO_ADOPTED_IPS` even when the request also repeats an asset class, a second `QDIA` designation or a `slot_index`. A new slot is `EMPTY` with `current_fund_id`, `at_risk_since_quarter_index` and `status_before_last_review` all null.

Funds live in the plan's candidate universe before they are ever seated. Creating one takes `plan_id`, a `ticker` of 1 to 20 characters unique per plan among funds whose `menu_status` is not `REMOVED`, a `fund_name` of 1 to 160 characters, an `asset_class_code` drawn from the same enumeration as slots, a `share_class_code` of `R1`, `R3`, `R6`, `INSTITUTIONAL`, `ADMIN` or `INVESTOR`, a `gross_expense_ratio_bps` within 0 to 300, a `net_expense_ratio_bps` within 0 to 300 that must not exceed `gross_expense_ratio_bps`, a `revenue_share_bps` within 0 to 100 covering 12b-1 plus sub-transfer-agency revenue sharing paid to the plan, a `peer_median_expense_bps` within 1 to 300, a non-negative `track_record_quarters`, a non-negative `manager_tenure_quarters`, a `manager_change_flag` defaulting to false, a `style_drift_r2` within 0.0000 to 1.0000 at four decimal places, and an optional non-negative `plan_assets_cents` defaulting to 0. A fund that represents a whole target-date suite sets `is_target_date_suite` true — the flag itself is optional and defaults to false — in which case `glidepath_type` of `TO_RETIREMENT` or `THROUGH_RETIREMENT`, `construction` of `OFF_THE_SHELF` or `CUSTOM`, `equity_at_65_pct` within 0.00 to 100.00, and `landing_point_age` within 60 to 95 are all required, each missing one being reported in that order. A fund that is not a suite may still supply any of those four, which are then validated over exactly the same domains and stored, and may equally omit them all. The net-versus-gross relation is checked immediately after the field domains and therefore before the duplicate-`ticker` scan. A new fund is `CANDIDATE` on the menu dimension and `CLEAR` on the watch dimension, with `credit_accrual_status` of `NOT_ACCRUING`, `qdia_flag` false, `consecutive_breach_quarters` at 0, `accrued_revenue_credit_cents` and `levelized_credit_cents` at 0, and `watch_started_quarter_index`, `latest_composite_score`, `last_scored_quarter_index`, `watch_status_before_last_review` and `menu_status_before_last_review` all null. `glidepath_fit_score` is not among those nulls: it is derived from the glidepath inputs by the rule of §3.8 as part of the creation itself, so a target-date suite is registered already carrying its score while a fund that is not a suite — and therefore has no reference shape to be measured against — carries null. The same derivation runs again on every later write that can move one of its inputs, which means a monitoring update touching `net_expense_ratio_bps`, `equity_at_65_pct` or `landing_point_age`, and a share-class change.

**Policy eligibility.** Before a fund may be seated, submitted in a replacement, revised into one, or selected as a target-date suite, it must satisfy every selection criterion of the governing statement. All four of the following must hold. First, the fund's `track_record_quarters` must be greater than or equal to `min_track_record_quarters`; equality passes and one quarter less fails. Second, the fund's `net_expense_ratio_bps` must be less than or equal to the peer expense cap, where that cap is the fund's `peer_median_expense_bps` multiplied by `expense_peer_multiple` and then floored to whole basis points — the flooring happens before the comparison, never after, so a peer median of 40 with a multiple of 1.25 gives a cap of exactly 50 basis points and a fund at exactly 50 passes. Third, the fund's `style_drift_r2` must be greater than or equal to `style_drift_r2_floor`, inclusive. Fourth, either `manager_change_flag` is false or `manager_tenure_quarters` is at least 4. A failure yields `ERR_FUND_FAILS_IPS_CRITERIA` and names the first failing criterion in that exact order.

**Seating a fund.** Seating is the investment consultant's act and moves a slot from `EMPTY` to `ACTIVE`. The request names the `fund_id` and may name a `motion_id`, which is resolved and cross-plan checked like any other reference but is never otherwise consulted — the QDIA test below scans the plan's motions for itself. The preconditions are then taken in exactly this order. The fund's `plan_id` must match the slot's, otherwise `ERR_CROSS_PLAN_REFERENCE`, that check coming first because it belongs to reference resolution. The slot must be `EMPTY`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. The fund's `menu_status` must be `CANDIDATE`, otherwise `ERR_FUND_NOT_CANDIDATE`. Menu posture must not be `FROZEN`, otherwise `ERR_MENU_FROZEN`. The plan must have an adopted statement, otherwise `ERR_NO_ADOPTED_IPS`. The fund must satisfy that statement's policy eligibility, otherwise `ERR_FUND_FAILS_IPS_CRITERIA`. The fund's `asset_class_code` must equal the slot's, otherwise `ERR_ASSET_CLASS_MISMATCH`. The slot's `ips_id` must still equal the plan's currently adopted statement, otherwise `ERR_IPS_VERSION_STALE`. The plan's `available_menu_slots` must be at least 1, otherwise `ERR_MENU_CAPACITY_EXHAUSTED`. And if the slot's `designation` is `QDIA`, the fund's `is_target_date_suite` must be true *and* a carried target-date-selection motion must name this fund, otherwise `ERR_QDIA_REQUIRES_TARGET_DATE_SUITE`. On success the slot records `current_fund_id` and becomes `ACTIVE`; the fund moves from `CANDIDATE` to `ACTIVE` on the menu dimension with `credit_accrual_status` set to `ACCRUING` and `qdia_flag` set to whether the slot is the `QDIA` slot; `occupied_menu_slots` rises by 1 and `available_menu_slots` falls by 1; `total_plan_assets_cents` and menu posture are recomputed. The response body is the updated slot, not the fund.

**Retiring a slot.** Retirement is the sponsor's act and is legal from `EMPTY`, `ACTIVE` or `AT_RISK`, anything else being `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. The slot's `designation` must not be `QDIA`, otherwise `ERR_QDIA_SLOT_REQUIRED`, because a plan must always retain a qualified default — and the state check precedes that designation check, so retiring an already retired QDIA slot answers with the state error. The act may carry an optional free-text `reason` of at least one character, which is recorded on the slot as `retire_reason`. From `ACTIVE` or `AT_RISK` it additionally requires that no replacement proposal on this slot is in `DRAFT`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `REVISION_REQUESTED`, `BLOCKED`, `APPROVED` or `NOTICE_ISSUED`, otherwise `ERR_SLOT_HAS_OPEN_PROPOSAL`. Retiring an occupied slot moves its seated fund from `ACTIVE` to `PENDING_REMOVAL` on the menu dimension while `current_fund_id` is retained for audit; `occupied_menu_slots` falls by 1 and `available_menu_slots` rises by 1. Retiring an empty slot moves no counters beyond the ratio. In every case `at_risk_slot_ratio` and menu posture are recomputed, and `RETIRED` is terminal.

**Changing a share class.** A fund's `share_class_code` may be changed only downward in cost. The target class must have a strictly lower `net_expense_ratio_bps` and a `revenue_share_bps` less than or equal to the current class, and the fund must not be inside a replacement proposal that is `APPROVED` or `NOTICE_ISSUED`; a violation is `ERR_SHARE_CLASS_INELIGIBLE`. The write is also a menu-changing write, so a `FROZEN` posture rejects it with `ERR_MENU_FROZEN`; the gate is evaluated against whichever non-`RETIRED` slot currently seats this fund, so a `RESTRICTED` posture rejects it too when that slot is `AT_RISK` or `PENDING_REPLACEMENT`, while a fund seated in no slot at all is only ever stopped by `FROZEN`. That posture test runs ahead of the three eligibility tests below. The request names the target `share_class_code` together with its `net_expense_ratio_bps` and `revenue_share_bps`, and may name a `gross_expense_ratio_bps` which otherwise defaults to the fund's current gross; all four values are written onto the fund on success, over the same domains as at registration. The three eligibility tests are taken in the order stated — strictly lower net, then revenue sharing no higher, then the in-flight-proposal guard — and only once all three have passed is the requirement that net not exceed gross applied. Because that relation is a creation-time domain constraint rather than one of the request-shape rules of priority 38, a target class that is more expensive than the incumbent answers `ERR_SHARE_CLASS_INELIGIBLE` even when its net would also have exceeded the gross.

**Refreshing monitoring data.** A fund's monitoring inputs are refreshed in place as fresh data arrives from the recordkeeper: `net_expense_ratio_bps`, `gross_expense_ratio_bps`, `revenue_share_bps`, `peer_median_expense_bps`, `style_drift_r2`, `manager_tenure_quarters`, `manager_change_flag` and `plan_assets_cents` may all be updated on an existing fund row, and so may `track_record_quarters`, `equity_at_65_pct` and `landing_point_age`, each subject to the same domain constraints as at creation; a request naming none of those eleven fields is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and the net-versus-gross relation is re-tested against the merged row — the supplied values where given and the fund's existing ones otherwise — so a patch that raises net above the stored gross, or lowers gross below the stored net, is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Updating `plan_assets_cents` recomputes the plan's `total_plan_assets_cents`, and in fact every accepted patch recomputes the derived figures and the menu posture. This is a data-maintenance write, not a governance decision; it does not move either of the fund's status dimensions.

### 3.4. Meetings, Quorum, Motions and the Vote Roll

A committee meeting is scheduled with `plan_id`, a `meeting_date` unique per plan among meetings whose `status` is not `VOID`, and a `quarter_index` of at least 0 naming the fiscal quarter it belongs to. `meeting_date` must parse as a calendar date and is stored and compared as the exact string supplied. It starts `SCHEDULED` with `quorum_met` false, `voting_attendee_count` and `eligible_voting_seat_count` at 0, `carried_motion_count` at 0, and `charter_version_pinned`, `minutes_doc_ref` and `minutes_approved_at` null.

**Convening.** The chair convenes the meeting, supplying `attendee_member_ids` — the roster of members recorded present. Every identifier in `attendee_member_ids` must resolve to a member whose seat belongs to this plan, otherwise `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` for one that resolves to nothing and `ERR_CROSS_PLAN_REFERENCE` for one that resolves to another plan's member, and no identifier may appear twice, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; both of those are settled first, as part of reading the request. The plan's `charter_status` must then be `ADOPTED`, otherwise `ERR_CHARTER_NOT_ADOPTED`. The meeting itself must still be `SCHEDULED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. No other meeting on the plan may be `IN_SESSION`, otherwise `ERR_MEETING_IN_SESSION`. The acting chair's `X-User-ID` must itself appear in `attendee_member_ids`, otherwise `ERR_CHAIR_NOT_PRESENT`. Convening finally requires the chair's own `fiduciary_training_status` to be `CURRENT` with a signed acceptance, otherwise `ERR_TRAINING_NOT_CURRENT`. Those checks are taken in exactly that order.

Convening is the moment quorum is decided, and the verdict is frozen. `eligible_voting_seat_count` is the number of seats on the plan whose `status` is `FILLED`, whose `voting` is true, and whose occupying member is `ACTIVE` with `fiduciary_training_status` of `CURRENT`. `voting_attendee_count` is the number of identifiers in `attendee_member_ids` whose member is `ACTIVE`, has `fiduciary_training_status` of `CURRENT`, and sits in a voting seat. `quorum_met` is true only when `voting_attendee_count` is greater than or equal to the plan's `quorum_min_voting_members` *and* twice `voting_attendee_count` is strictly greater than `eligible_voting_seat_count`. The majority test is strict: 3 attendees against 6 eligible seats is not a majority, while 4 of 6 is. A member whose training has lapsed to `EXPIRED` counts toward neither figure, which shrinks the eligible denominator and can make the majority test easier to satisfy — the absolute `quorum_min_voting_members` floor is what stops that from degenerating. Both figures and `quorum_met` are computed at convene and are never recomputed, so a member resigning mid-session does not disturb them. Convening also sets `charter_version_pinned` to the plan's `version`, against which approvals arising from this meeting re-validate. A false `quorum_met` does not prevent convening; it prevents presenting a review, carrying a motion and approving minutes.

**Adjourning.** The chair adjourns, and must still appear in `attendee_member_ids`, otherwise `ERR_CHAIR_NOT_PRESENT`. Every motion on this meeting still `OPEN` is moved to `WITHDRAWN`, iterated by `created_at` ascending, with `closed_at` stamped. Where such a withdrawn motion was a fund-replacement motion, its subject proposal keeps its current status — there is no cascade onto the proposal.

**Approving minutes.** The chair approves the minutes of a meeting that has reached `ADJOURNED`, any other state being `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. `minutes_doc_ref` is required and must be at least 6 characters, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — a test taken ahead of the state check, which in turn precedes the chair-present test. The acting chair must appear in `attendee_member_ids` and that member's `status` must still be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_CHAIR_NOT_PRESENT`. `quorum_met` must be true, otherwise `ERR_QUORUM_NOT_MET`. The chair's training must be current with a signed acceptance, otherwise `ERR_TRAINING_NOT_CURRENT`. On success `minutes_approved_at` is stamped from the logical clock, `carried_motion_count` is recounted from the meeting's `CARRIED` motions and thereby frozen, and the meeting reaches `MINUTES_APPROVED`, which is terminal and which unlocks every decision gated on `ERR_MINUTES_NOT_APPROVED`.

A meeting that is still `SCHEDULED` when the plan's fiscal quarter moves past its `quarter_index` is voided by the quarter advance; any quarterly review pointing at it has its `meeting_id` reset to null.

**Moving a motion.** A motion is created against a meeting with `meeting_id`, a `subject_type` of `IPS_ADOPTION`, `FUND_REPLACEMENT`, `TARGET_DATE_SELECTION`, `FEE_LEVELIZATION`, `RFP_AWARD` or `WATCH_LIST_ACTION`, the polymorphic `subject_id` resolved according to §2.5, and an optional `tally_basis` of `VOTES_CAST` or `ELIGIBLE_VOTERS` defaulting to `VOTES_CAST`. A subject that does not resolve within its declared `subject_type` is `ERR_MOTION_SUBJECT_MISMATCH`, and one that resolves to a row belonging to another plan is `ERR_CROSS_PLAN_REFERENCE`. The meeting must be `IN_SESSION`, otherwise `ERR_MEETING_NOT_IN_SESSION`, and that state test comes after the subject has been resolved and cross-plan checked, so a motion moved against an adjourned meeting with an unresolvable subject answers with the subject error. Two values are fixed at the moment the motion is moved and are immutable thereafter: `carried_threshold_pct`, which the request may state outright as a four-decimal figure from 0.0000 to 100.0000 and which is otherwise copied from the plan's `voting_threshold_pct`, and `eligible_voter_count` from the meeting's `voting_attendee_count`. For a motion whose `subject_type` is `RFP_AWARD`, the derived `subject_provider_role` records the `provider_role` of the subject engagement — that value is what drives the recusal rule — and it is `NONE` for every other subject type. A new motion is `OPEN` with `votes` empty, all four counters at 0, and `support_pct` and `closed_at` null.

**Casting a vote.** A vote is appended to the motion's `votes` log. Each entry carries `member_id`, a `vote` of `FOR`, `AGAINST`, `ABSTAIN` or `RECUSE`, a `cast_at` stamped from the logical clock, and an optional `recusal_basis`. At most one entry may exist per `member_id`, and a later vote from the same member replaces that member's earlier entry rather than adding a second one. The voter must be a committee member on this plan, otherwise `ERR_ACTOR_NOT_COMMITTEE_MEMBER`, and the entry's `member_id` must equal `X-User-ID` — no member may cast another member's vote and the chair may not vote for an absentee — otherwise `ERR_ACTOR_NOT_SELF`; that self-identity test is taken before the motion is even looked up, so a vote purporting to be another member's on a motion that does not exist answers `ERR_ACTOR_NOT_SELF` rather than `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`. The motion must still be `OPEN`, otherwise `ERR_OPEN_MOTION_VOTE_LOCK`. The voter's own `status` must still be `ACTIVE`, and their `fiduciary_training_status` must be `CURRENT` with a signed acceptance; all three of those failures answer `ERR_TRAINING_NOT_CURRENT`. Recusal is then enforced: a member whose `affiliated_provider_role` is not `NONE` must vote `RECUSE` on any motion whose `subject_type` is `RFP_AWARD` and whose subject engagement carries that same `provider_role`, and a member whose `independence_flag` is false must vote `RECUSE` on any `FEE_LEVELIZATION` motion; a `FOR`, `AGAINST` or `ABSTAIN` from such a member is `ERR_RECUSAL_REQUIRED`. Each recorded vote updates `for_count`, `against_count`, `abstain_count` or `recuse_count` — all four recounted from the vote log rather than incremented — and a `FOR`, `AGAINST` or `ABSTAIN` also increments the member's `votes_cast_count`; a recusal does not. That personal counter rises only when the member had cast nothing before or had previously recused, so replacing a `FOR` with an `AGAINST` leaves it untouched while replacing a `RECUSE` with any of the three raises it by 1, and replacing a cast vote with a `RECUSE` never lowers it. `recusal_basis` is optional even on a `RECUSE` vote — at least one character when it is given — and is stored on the entry as supplied.

**Closing a motion.** The chair closes the motion, and the tally decides the outcome. The meeting must be `IN_SESSION`, otherwise `ERR_MEETING_NOT_IN_SESSION`; the motion must still be `OPEN`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`; and `quorum_met` must be true, otherwise `ERR_QUORUM_NOT_MET` — in that order, so an already-closed motion whose meeting has adjourned answers with the meeting error. The denominator depends on `tally_basis`. Under `VOTES_CAST` it is `for_count` plus `against_count` plus `abstain_count`, with recusals excluded entirely. Under `ELIGIBLE_VOTERS` it is `eligible_voter_count` minus `recuse_count`, so recusals shrink the base. A denominator of zero or less is `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_VOTERS`; a motion on which every voter recused fails that way under both variants. Otherwise `support_pct` is `for_count` multiplied by 100 and divided by the denominator, rounded half up to four decimal places. The comparison then puts both sides onto the percentage scale, because the plan's `voting_threshold_pct` is a fraction of 0.5000 to 1.0000 while `support_pct` is a percentage: a `carried_threshold_pct` at or below 1 is multiplied by 100 before the comparison, so the plan default of 0.6000 acts as 60 percent, while a value above 1 is already a percentage and is compared exactly as it stands. The motion is `CARRIED` when `support_pct` is greater than or equal to the threshold read that way and `quorum_met` is true, and `FAILED` otherwise. The rounding happens before the comparison, so a raw 66.66665 becomes 66.6667 and carries a 66.6667 threshold, and a `support_pct` exactly equal to the threshold carries. The difference between the two bases is material: two `FOR` votes out of three cast is 66.6667 percent and carries a 66.6667 threshold under `VOTES_CAST`, while the same two `FOR` against five eligible non-recused seats is 40.0000 percent and fails under `ELIGIBLE_VOTERS`. An abstention is a cast vote and dilutes support under `VOTES_CAST`; a recusal is a removal from the body and never enters the denominator under either basis.

Closing freezes `support_pct` and the four counters and stamps `closed_at`. A carried motion increments the meeting's `carried_motion_count` by 1. A failed fund-replacement motion cascades two levels: the subject proposal moves from `UNDER_REVIEW` to `REJECTED`, and that rejection releases nothing further because no menu position had yet been reserved. A motion may also leave `OPEN` without a vote — to `TABLED` when the charter is re-adopted underneath it or when the fee-disclosure cascade tables the plan's open fee-levelization motions, and to `WITHDRAWN` when the meeting adjourns with it still open. Both stamp `closed_at`, and all four exits are terminal: a tabled motion can never be reopened, and the committee must move a fresh one.

### 3.5. The Quarterly Review: Scorecards, Scoring, Presentation and Finalization

**Opening a review.** A quarterly review is created with `plan_id`, a `quarter_index` of at least 0 unique per plan among reviews whose `status` is not `VOID`, and optionally the `meeting_id` of the meeting at which it will be presented, which is resolved and cross-plan checked when supplied and recorded as null otherwise. The plan must have exactly one adopted policy statement, otherwise `ERR_NO_ADOPTED_IPS`, and that requirement is tested before the duplicate-quarter test. Opening pins `ips_id` to that adopted statement — every criterion for this review's whole life is read from that row, even if a newer statement is adopted mid-review — and copies `scoring_variant` from it, immutably. The review starts `DRAFT` with `scorecard_count`, `submitted_scorecard_count`, `watch_additions_count` and `reopen_count` at 0, and `finalized_at` and `finalized_quarter_index` null. `scorecard_count` counts every scorecard row attached to the review whose `status` is not `VOID`, superseded rows included, while `submitted_scorecard_count` counts only the rows still `SUBMITTED`; both are recounted from the rows themselves on each submission and again when the review is presented, never incremented.

**Submitting a scorecard.** A scorecard is the consultant's quarterly measurement of one fund. It takes `review_id`, `fund_id`, the concurrency token `expected_review_version`, and the raw inputs: `trailing_return_3y_bps` and `trailing_return_5y_bps` for the fund's annualised trailing returns in basis points, `benchmark_return_3y_bps` and `benchmark_return_5y_bps` for the mapped benchmark's, both pairs stored on the scorecard as `trailing_3y_return_bps`, `trailing_5y_return_bps`, `benchmark_3y_return_bps` and `benchmark_5y_return_bps` — each of the four may equally be supplied under its stored spelling, and only when neither spelling carries a value is it `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; optionally `rolling_since_watch_return_bps` and `rolling_since_watch_benchmark_bps`; a `peer_rank_pct` from 1 to 100 where 1 is best and 100 is worst; a `style_drift_r2` at four decimal places; the quarter's observed `net_expense_ratio_bps`, which need only be non-negative here rather than obeying the 0-to-300 band the fund row imposes; a `peer_median_expense_bps` of at least 1; `manager_tenure_quarters` at quarter end; and a `manager_change_flag` defaulting to false. The review must be `DRAFT`, `SCORING` or `REOPENED`, otherwise `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_SCORING`, and that state test precedes the `expected_review_version` comparison, so a stale token presented against a finalized review answers `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_SCORING` rather than `ERR_STALE_REVIEW_VERSION`. If the review's `scoring_variant` is `ROLLING_SINCE_WATCH` and the fund's `watch_status` is not `CLEAR`, the fund must have a non-null `watch_started_quarter_index` and both rolling fields must be populated, otherwise `ERR_ROLLING_WINDOW_UNAVAILABLE`; and if that same variant meets a fund whose `watch_status` is still `CLEAR` the submission is refused with the same `ERR_ROLLING_WINDOW_UNAVAILABLE`, since a clear fund has no window to roll from — so under this variant only a non-clear fund can be scored at all. The two rolling fields are otherwise optional and are stored as null when omitted. A second scorecard for the same review and fund is legal and supersedes the first: the older row moves from `SUBMITTED` to `SUPERSEDED`, the new row's `supersedes_scorecard_id` points at it, and `submitted_scorecard_count` is unchanged because exactly one submitted row exists per review-and-fund pair at all times. The first scorecard on a `DRAFT` review moves that review to `SCORING`; the first after a reopen moves it from `REOPENED` back to `SCORING`, closing the cycle. Every accepted submission increments the review's `version` by exactly 1, and an auto-transition performed by that same submission is carried inside that single increment rather than adding a second one — so the first scorecard on a freshly created `DRAFT` review leaves the review at `version` 2, and the next `expected_review_version` a client must present is 2.

**The composite score.** Every scorecard derives six numbers. The first step is the only one that differs between the two variants. Under `TRAILING_PERIOD` the excess return in basis points is 0.6 times the 3-year fund-minus-benchmark difference plus 0.4 times the 5-year fund-minus-benchmark difference, rounded half up to a whole number of basis points. Under `ROLLING_SINCE_WATCH` the excess is simply `rolling_since_watch_return_bps` minus `rolling_since_watch_benchmark_bps`, with both trailing pairs ignored entirely; this variant requires the fund to have a `watch_started_quarter_index`, and a fund still `CLEAR` scored under it is a 422 rather than a silent fallback to the trailing blend.

The five component scores follow, each on a 0.00 to 100.00 scale and each identical under both variants. `performance_component` is 50.00 plus the excess basis points divided by 10, clamped into 0.00 through 100.00. `peer_component` is 100.00 minus `peer_rank_pct`. `expense_component` is 100.00 minus twice the amount by which `net_expense_ratio_bps` exceeds `peer_median_expense_bps` — where a fund at or below the median contributes nothing to that subtraction — clamped into 0.00 through 100.00. `style_component` is exactly 100.00 when `style_drift_r2` is greater than or equal to the statement's `style_drift_r2_floor`; otherwise it is 100.00 minus 500 times the shortfall below the floor, with a lower bound of 0.00. `manager_component`, when `manager_change_flag` is true, is the smaller of 40.00 and twice `manager_tenure_quarters`; when the flag is false it is the smaller of 100.00 and 40.00 plus three times `manager_tenure_quarters`.

The rounding order is pinned. Each of the five components is rounded half up to two decimal places *before* it is weighted. The weighted sum is then formed by multiplying each rounded component by its `weights` entry — `performance`, `peer`, `expense`, `style`, `manager`, summed in exactly that order — and the result is rounded half up to two decimal places exactly once. That single value is `composite_score`. The boundaries are exact: an excess of zero basis points gives a `performance_component` of exactly 50.00, neither 49.99 nor 50.01; a `net_expense_ratio_bps` equal to `peer_median_expense_bps` gives an `expense_component` of exactly 100.00 and every basis point above the median costs exactly 2.00 points; a `style_drift_r2` exactly at the floor passes at 100.00 while one ten-thousandth below the floor gives 99.95; and a `peer_rank_pct` of 100 gives a `peer_component` of exactly 0.00.

**Presenting the review.** Presentation is the consultant's act and locks the review's contents. The review must be `SCORING`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. The request may carry a `meeting_id` of its own, which overrides whatever the review holds and is written onto the review on success; failing that the review's own `meeting_id` is used, and it must be non-null, otherwise `ERR_REVIEW_MEETING_MISSING`. The referenced meeting must be `IN_SESSION`, otherwise `ERR_MEETING_NOT_IN_SESSION`, and its `quorum_met` must be true, otherwise `ERR_QUORUM_NOT_MET`. Every asset-class slot on the plan whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, `AT_RISK` or `PENDING_REPLACEMENT` must have a `SUBMITTED` scorecard for its `current_fund_id` in this review, otherwise `ERR_SCORECARD_COVERAGE_INCOMPLETE` — the slots being examined in `slot_index` order, so the failure names the lowest-indexed uncovered slot — and `submitted_scorecard_count`, recounted first, must be at least 1, otherwise `ERR_NO_SCORECARDS`. On success the review moves from `SCORING` to `PENDING_MINUTES` and further scorecards are refused at priority 42.

**Finalizing the review.** Finalization is the chair's act and it is the largest cascade in the system, running as five ordered hops. The preconditions, taken in this order, are that the review is `PENDING_MINUTES`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`; that it still points at a meeting that resolves, otherwise `ERR_REVIEW_MEETING_MISSING`; that the referenced meeting has reached `MINUTES_APPROVED`, otherwise `ERR_MINUTES_NOT_APPROVED`; that the plan has an adopted statement at all, otherwise `ERR_NO_ADOPTED_IPS`; that the review's pinned `ips_id` is still that statement, otherwise `ERR_IPS_VERSION_STALE`; and that the acting chair's training is current with a signed acceptance, otherwise `ERR_TRAINING_NOT_CURRENT`.

Hop 0 sets the review's `status` to `FINALIZED`, stamps `finalized_at` from the logical clock and `finalized_quarter_index` from the plan's `fiscal_quarter_index`, and for each submitted scorecard writes that scorecard's `composite_score` onto its fund's `latest_composite_score` and the review's `quarter_index` onto that fund's `last_scored_quarter_index`.

Hop 1, caused by hop 0's score write, transitions each scored fund's `watch_status` according to that fund's `watch_recommendation`, iterated over seated funds by their slots' `slot_index` ascending and then over unseated funds by `created_at` ascending. `watch_additions_count` counts the funds that left `CLEAR` in this hop. Before each change the fund's `watch_status_before_last_review` and, where the menu dimension is touched, `menu_status_before_last_review`, are snapshotted. Both snapshots describe this finalization and nothing earlier. For every fund the hop visits, each of the two fields is written with the value its dimension held on entry when the hop actually moved that dimension, and is cleared to null when the hop left that dimension where it stood — so a fund this review scored but did not move on the watch dimension carries no watch snapshot, and one whose `menu_status` this review never touched carries no menu snapshot, whatever an earlier review had left in either field. A fund the review did not score is not visited at all and keeps its existing snapshots untouched.

Hop 2, caused by hop 1, moves each asset-class slot whose seated fund left `CLEAR` from `ACTIVE` to `AT_RISK`, snapshotting `status_before_last_review` as `ACTIVE` and setting `at_risk_since_quarter_index` to the review's `quarter_index`. Slots whose fund was cured move from `AT_RISK` back to `ACTIVE` with `at_risk_since_quarter_index` cleared to null. A slot that was already `AT_RISK` and whose fund escalated further stays `AT_RISK` with `at_risk_since_quarter_index` unchanged. A slot's `status_before_last_review` follows the same rule as the fund snapshots: it is written only by a hop that actually moved that slot, and any slot this hop visited — that is, one seating a fund this review scored — but did not move has the field cleared to null.

Hop 3, caused by hop 2, recomputes `at_risk_slot_ratio` and drives `menu_posture` down the ladder — from `OPEN` to `RESTRICTED` at a ratio of 0.2500 or more, and to `FROZEN` at 0.5000 or more.

Hop 4, caused by hop 3, moves every replacement proposal on the plan that is `DRAFT`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `REVISION_REQUESTED` into `BLOCKED`, snapshotting `pre_block_status` with the exact prior status. Under a `FROZEN` posture every such proposal is blocked; under `RESTRICTED` only those whose incoming fund's `asset_class_code` matches the `asset_class_code` of a slot that is now `AT_RISK` or `PENDING_REPLACEMENT` are blocked. The sweep is iterated by slot `slot_index` ascending and then by proposal `created_at` ascending.

### 3.6. The Watch-List Ladder, Cure, and Reopening a Finalized Review

**Determining the recommendation.** Every scorecard carries a derived `watch_recommendation` of `NONE`, `WATCH`, `REMEDIATION` or `TERMINATE`, and it is that value which drives the finalize cascade. The determination begins with a breach test. A fund is in breach when any one of three things holds: its `composite_score` is less than or equal to the statement's `watch_trigger_score_max`; or its `style_drift_r2` is strictly below `style_drift_r2_floor`; or `manager_change_flag` is true and `composite_score` is less than or equal to `watch_trigger_score_max` plus `cure_margin_points`. That third arm is what puts a nominally acceptable fund on watch when its manager has just changed.

The ladder is then evaluated in this exact order, stopping at the first arm that matches. First, a breaching fund whose `composite_score` is less than or equal to `remediation_trigger_score_max` and whose `consecutive_breach_quarters` plus one has reached `watch_grace_quarters` is recommended `TERMINATE`. Second, a breaching fund whose `composite_score` is less than or equal to `remediation_trigger_score_max` is recommended `REMEDIATION`. Third, a breaching fund already in `WATCH` or `REMEDIATION` whose `consecutive_breach_quarters` plus one has reached `watch_grace_quarters` is recommended `REMEDIATION`. Fourth, any other breaching fund is recommended `WATCH`. Fifth, a non-breaching fund currently in `WATCH`, `REMEDIATION` or `TERMINATION_PENDING` whose `composite_score` is strictly greater than `watch_trigger_score_max` plus `cure_margin_points` is recommended `NONE`, which is the cure. Sixth and otherwise the recommendation is `NONE` with no change. Throughout, the phrase "`consecutive_breach_quarters` plus one" means the count including the quarter being finalized.

The asymmetry between entry and exit is deliberate. With the default thresholds a fund enters `WATCH` at a `composite_score` of 40.00 or below but only leaves it above 50.00, so the band from 40.01 through 50.00 keeps an already-watched fund on watch while never putting a clear fund on watch. A score of exactly 40.00 is a breach because the comparison is inclusive, while 40.01 is not; exactly 25.00 is remediation-grade while 25.01 is only watch-grade; and exactly 50.00 does not cure a watched fund while 50.01 does.

**Applying the recommendation.** During finalization the watch dimension moves as follows. A `CLEAR` fund whose scorecard recommends `WATCH` moves to `WATCH`: `watch_status_before_last_review` is snapshotted as `CLEAR`, `watch_started_quarter_index` and `last_scored_quarter_index` are set to the review's `quarter_index`, `consecutive_breach_quarters` rises by 1, and the slot holding that fund moves from `ACTIVE` to `AT_RISK`. A `CLEAR` fund whose scorecard recommends `REMEDIATION` or `TERMINATE` takes that same step in a single hop but lands straight on `REMEDIATION`, with the same `CLEAR` snapshot, the same `watch_started_quarter_index`, the same single increment of `consecutive_breach_quarters`, the same contribution to `watch_additions_count` and the same move of its slot to `AT_RISK`: the ladder is never walked one rung per quarter when the measurement already justifies the higher rung. A fund in `WATCH` moves to `REMEDIATION` whenever its scorecard recommends `REMEDIATION` or `TERMINATE`, whatever the breach count, and on a repeated `WATCH` recommendation only once `consecutive_breach_quarters` plus one has reached 2, staying in `WATCH` otherwise: where it moves, `watch_status_before_last_review` is snapshotted as `WATCH`, and where it stays no snapshot is written at all; `consecutive_breach_quarters` rises by 1 either way, and its slot stays `AT_RISK` with `at_risk_since_quarter_index` untouched. A fund in `REMEDIATION` whose scorecard recommends `TERMINATE` moves to `TERMINATION_PENDING`: `watch_status_before_last_review` is snapshotted as `REMEDIATION`, `consecutive_breach_quarters` rises by 1, and — uniquely in the whole system — the watch dimension reaches across and moves the menu dimension, taking `menu_status` from `ACTIVE` to `PENDING_REMOVAL`. Because a fund in `TERMINATION_PENDING` now exists, the recomputed posture is `RESTRICTED` at minimum. A fund already sitting in `TERMINATION_PENDING` is past the ladder's reach: a further `WATCH`, `REMEDIATION` or `TERMINATE` recommendation moves neither dimension, leaves `consecutive_breach_quarters` exactly as it stands and writes no snapshot of either kind, so nothing but a cure can take such a fund anywhere. A fund in `WATCH`, in `REMEDIATION` or in `TERMINATION_PENDING` whose scorecard recommends `NONE` and whose `composite_score` clears the cure margin — strictly above `watch_trigger_score_max` plus `cure_margin_points`, read from the adopted statement — moves to `CLEAR`: `watch_status_before_last_review` is snapshotted with the prior value, `watch_started_quarter_index` is cleared to null, `consecutive_breach_quarters` is reset to 0, and its slot moves from `AT_RISK` back to `ACTIVE`. Curing acts on the watch dimension only: a fund the ladder had already carried to `PENDING_REMOVAL` keeps that `menu_status`, and unwinding it is the reopen's business rather than the cure's. A watched fund whose recommendation is `NONE` because it merely stopped breaching, scoring above the watch trigger but not clear of the cure margin, is left exactly where it stands: both dimensions hold, `consecutive_breach_quarters` is neither raised nor reset, and its slot does not move. A fund that was already `CLEAR` and is recommended `NONE` likewise moves nothing and carries no snapshot, but has `consecutive_breach_quarters` reset to 0, which clears any residue an earlier reopen may have left on the counter.

Note that the two dimensions on a fund are otherwise independent. A fund may be `ACTIVE` with `REMEDIATION`, `CANDIDATE` with `CLEAR`, `PENDING_REMOVAL` with `WATCH`, and so on; no transition on one dimension constrains the other except where a precondition explicitly says so.

**Reopening a finalized review.** Reopening is the sponsor's compensating power and it inverts the finalize cascade exactly rather than approximately. The act carries a mandatory free-text `reason` of at least one character, which is validated for shape and then used only as the audit justification for the call — no field on the review records it. Beyond the requirement that the review be `FINALIZED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, three preconditions apply in the order given. The plan's `fiscal_quarter_index` minus the review's `quarter_index` must be at most 1, so a review may only be reopened in its own quarter or the one immediately after, otherwise `ERR_QUARTER_CLOSED`. `reopen_count` must be strictly below 2, otherwise `ERR_REOPEN_LIMIT_REACHED`. And no replacement proposal whose approving motion belongs to this review's `meeting_id` may have reached `APPROVED`, `NOTICE_ISSUED` or `EXECUTED`, otherwise `ERR_REVIEW_DECISIONS_EXECUTED` — once a decision authorised at that meeting has been acted upon, the quarter's scoring can no longer be unwound.

On success the review moves from `FINALIZED` to `REOPENED`, `reopen_count` rises by 1, and `finalized_at` is cleared to null. Every fund whose `last_scored_quarter_index` equals this review's `quarter_index` — and only those funds, so that a later review's rescoring is never clobbered, visited in `created_at` ascending order — has its `watch_status` restored to `watch_status_before_last_review` and its `menu_status` restored to `menu_status_before_last_review`, both read straight from the snapshot, with `consecutive_breach_quarters` decremented by 1 subject to a floor of 0. A null snapshot is not a value to restore but the record that finalization left that dimension alone, so the dimension is left alone here too; and a fund that reads `CLEAR` once the watch dimension has been restored has `watch_started_quarter_index` cleared to null. Each asset-class slot whose `status_before_last_review` is non-null, and whose seated fund is one of the funds this review scored, has its `status` restored to that snapshot, the slots being visited in `slot_index` ascending order, with `at_risk_since_quarter_index` cleared to null whenever the restored status is anything other than `AT_RISK`; a slot already sitting at its snapshot value is left untouched and its `version` does not move. Each affected fund's `latest_composite_score` is reverted to the `composite_score` recorded for that fund by the most recent *earlier* finalized review, or to null when no earlier review scored it. Then `at_risk_slot_ratio` and `menu_posture` are recomputed, and when the posture relaxes back to `OPEN` every proposal sitting in `BLOCKED` is restored to its `pre_block_status` exactly, iterated by the `slot_index` of each proposal's slot ascending and then by proposal `created_at` ascending, with `pre_block_status` cleared to null and `revision_round` untouched. A reopened review returns to `SCORING` on the next scorecard submission, which is what makes the review cycle closed rather than one-way.

The blocked-and-restored detour is symmetric in both directions. A posture that tightens to `RESTRICTED` blocks only the proposals whose incoming fund's asset class matches an at-risk or pending-replacement slot; a posture that tightens to `FROZEN` blocks every non-terminal proposal in `DRAFT`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `REVISION_REQUESTED`. A posture that relaxes to `OPEN` restores each blocked proposal to the exact status its `pre_block_status` records — `DRAFT` restores to `DRAFT`, `UNDER_REVIEW` to `UNDER_REVIEW`, `REVISION_REQUESTED` to `REVISION_REQUESTED` — never to a canonical default. The same restoration runs when the posture is recomputed explicitly rather than as a side effect.

### 3.7. Fund Replacement: Drafting, Approval, Participant Notice, Mapping and Unwind

**Creating a proposal.** A replacement proposal is created with `plan_id`, the `slot_id` whose occupant is being replaced, the `outgoing_fund_id` currently seated in that slot, the proposed `incoming_fund_id`, a `rationale` of 10 to 500 characters, a `mapping_strategy` of `DIRECT_MAP`, `MAP_TO_QDIA` or `REENROLLMENT`, and the concurrency token `expected_plan_version`. Creation is a create-with-supersede write: it reads the plan's capacity triple, its `menu_posture` and its `draft_proposal_count`, and it withdraws any prior `DRAFT` proposal on the same `slot_id`, setting that older row's `superseded_by_proposal_id` to the winning proposal's identifier and decrementing `draft_proposal_count`. Because it both reads and writes plan-level aggregates, `expected_plan_version` must equal the plan's current `version`; a mismatch is `ERR_STALE_PLAN_VERSION` at HTTP 412 and neither the proposal row nor the withdrawal is written. On success the plan's `version` increments by 1, `pinned_plan_version` records the accepted value, `pinned_ips_id` records the plan's currently adopted policy statement, and the proposal starts `DRAFT` with `revision_round` at 0, `mapped_assets_cents` at 0, and `approving_motion_id`, `pre_block_status`, `notice_delivered_at`, `blackout_start_at`, `blackout_end_at` and `superseded_by_proposal_id` all null. Creation also checks asset-class compatibility, so an incoming fund whose `asset_class_code` differs from the slot's, or a non-target-date fund offered to a `QDIA` slot, is `ERR_ASSET_CLASS_MISMATCH`. Creation is a menu-changing write besides, so it is refused with `ERR_MENU_FROZEN` when the plan's posture is `FROZEN` and equally when the posture is `RESTRICTED` and the target slot is itself under surveillance; that posture test is taken before the concurrency comparison, so a frozen plan answers `ERR_MENU_FROZEN` even when the token presented is also stale, and the plan must further hold an adopted policy statement, otherwise `ERR_NO_ADOPTED_IPS`, which is tested after the two asset-class tests. `expected_plan_version` is an integer of at least 0. `notice_lead_days_required` is computed at creation too, from the strategy and the slot the request names, rather than waiting for submission, and the supersede sweep visits the slot's prior proposals in `created_at` ascending order.

**The notice lead time.** Each proposal carries a derived `notice_lead_days_required`, computed at creation, computed again at submission and recomputed on revision. It starts at the plan's `notice_lead_days_required`, default 30. It gains a further 30 days when `mapping_strategy` is `REENROLLMENT`. It gains a further 30 days when the slot's `designation` is `QDIA` and the plan's `erisa_covered` is true. It gains a further 15 days when `mapping_strategy` is `MAP_TO_QDIA` and the slot's `designation` is not `QDIA`. The additions stack, so a re-enrolment into an ERISA-covered qualified-default slot is 30 plus 30 plus 30, that is 90 days. `MAP_TO_QDIA` and `REENROLLMENT` are mutually exclusive strategies, so the 15-day and the re-enrolment 30-day additions never both apply. The earliest legal execution instant is `notice_delivered_at` plus that many days, compared inclusively against the logical clock: exactly at that instant is legal, one second earlier is `ERR_NOTICE_PERIOD_NOT_ELAPSED`.

**Submitting for review.** Submission is the consultant's act and moves the proposal from `DRAFT` to `UNDER_REVIEW`; any other starting status is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, and that state test comes first, ahead of the posture test. Menu posture must not be `FROZEN`, otherwise `ERR_MENU_FROZEN`. The plan must have an adopted policy statement, otherwise `ERR_NO_ADOPTED_IPS`. The `rationale` must be at least 40 characters at this point — a stricter floor than the 10-character minimum the field itself allows — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The incoming fund's `menu_status` must be `CANDIDATE` and its `asset_class_code` must equal the slot's, otherwise `ERR_ASSET_CLASS_MISMATCH`. The incoming fund must satisfy policy eligibility, otherwise `ERR_FUND_FAILS_IPS_CRITERIA`. And `outgoing_fund_id` must still be the slot's `current_fund_id`, otherwise `ERR_OUTGOING_FUND_MISMATCH`. On success `pinned_ips_id` is set to the plan's currently adopted statement, `pinned_plan_version` to the plan's current `version`, `notice_lead_days_required` is computed, and `draft_proposal_count` falls by 1. "The plan's current `version`" is read after this write's own plan-level recomputation has settled, so it is the value a client re-reading the plan straight afterwards would see — one higher than the value the plan carried when the submission arrived. This is unlike creation, where `pinned_plan_version` records the `expected_plan_version` the caller presented, that is the value from *before* the creation's own increment.

**The revision loop.** The chair may send a proposal back with a revision request carrying a mandatory `revision_note` of at least one character, which is recorded on the proposal as `revision_note`; the request moves the proposal from `UNDER_REVIEW` to `REVISION_REQUESTED` and increments `revision_round` by 1, any other starting status being `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` and a `revision_round` already at 3 being `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED`, the state test preceding the limit test. The consultant then revises, moving it back to `UNDER_REVIEW` from `REVISION_REQUESTED` and from nowhere else. All three revisable fields are optional — a new `incoming_fund_id`, a new `mapping_strategy` and a new `rationale` of 10 to 500 characters — and each is left exactly as it stands when omitted, so a revision with an empty body is legal and simply re-runs the checks against the existing terms. Revision requires that posture is not `FROZEN`, otherwise `ERR_MENU_FROZEN`, that the proposal's `pinned_ips_id` still resolves to a statement, otherwise `ERR_NO_ADOPTED_IPS`, that the incoming fund's `asset_class_code` still equals the slot's, otherwise `ERR_ASSET_CLASS_MISMATCH`, and that the incoming fund still satisfies the selection criteria — measured against the proposal's own `pinned_ips_id` rather than against whatever statement is adopted now — otherwise `ERR_FUND_FAILS_IPS_CRITERIA`. Revision recomputes `notice_lead_days_required` and refreshes `pinned_plan_version` to the plan's current `version`, read the same way submission reads it: after this write's plan-level recomputation has settled. The loop may run at most three laps.

**Approval.** Approval is the chair's act and it reserves a menu position. The preconditions are taken in this exact order: the proposal must be `UNDER_REVIEW`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`; posture must not be `FROZEN`, otherwise `ERR_MENU_FROZEN`; `approving_motion_id` must reference a motion that is `CARRIED` with `subject_type` of `FUND_REPLACEMENT` and `subject_id` equal to this proposal's `id`, otherwise `ERR_MOTION_NOT_CARRIED`; that motion's meeting must be `MINUTES_APPROVED`, otherwise `ERR_MINUTES_NOT_APPROVED`; the plan must have an adopted statement, otherwise `ERR_NO_ADOPTED_IPS`; `pinned_ips_id` must still be that statement, otherwise `ERR_IPS_VERSION_STALE`; the target slot must be `ACTIVE` or `AT_RISK`, otherwise `ERR_SLOT_NOT_REPLACEABLE`; no other proposal on the same `slot_id` may be `APPROVED` or `NOTICE_ISSUED`, otherwise `ERR_SLOT_HAS_OPEN_PROPOSAL`; and `available_menu_slots` must be at least 1, otherwise `ERR_MENU_CAPACITY_EXHAUSTED`. On success the proposal becomes `APPROVED`, the slot moves from `ACTIVE` or `AT_RISK` to `PENDING_REPLACEMENT`, `reserved_menu_slots` rises by 1 and `available_menu_slots` falls by 1 while `occupied_menu_slots` is unchanged, and every other proposal on the same slot in `DRAFT`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `REVISION_REQUESTED` or `BLOCKED` is moved to `WITHDRAWN` with `superseded_by_proposal_id` set to this proposal's identifier, iterated by `created_at` ascending.

**Rescinding an approval.** The chair may rescind while no notice has gone out, supplying a mandatory `reason` of at least one character that is recorded on the proposal as `rescind_reason`; once `notice_delivered_at` is set the rescission is refused with `ERR_NOTICE_ALREADY_ISSUED`, and that test is taken before the requirement that the proposal be `APPROVED`, so a proposal already executed answers `ERR_NOTICE_ALREADY_ISSUED` rather than `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Rescission returns the proposal to `UNDER_REVIEW` and restores the capacity triple to exactly its pre-approval values: `reserved_menu_slots` falls by 1 and `available_menu_slots` rises by 1. The slot's return state is deterministic — it goes back to `ACTIVE` when its seated fund's `watch_status` is `CLEAR`, and to `AT_RISK` when it is anything else — and `current_fund_id` is untouched. `approving_motion_id` is cleared to null, because the carried motion has been spent and cannot be reused; a fresh motion is required to approve again.

**Issuing the participant notice.** Notice issuance is the recordkeeper's act and moves the proposal from `APPROVED` to `NOTICE_ISSUED`, any other starting status being `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` and that test coming first. Both `blackout_start_at` and `blackout_end_at` are required timestamps and are stored exactly as the request spelled them. The plan's `fee_disclosure_status` must not be `RESTATEMENT_REQUIRED`, otherwise `ERR_FEE_DISCLOSURE_RESTATEMENT_REQUIRED` — a plan whose fee disclosure is under restatement may not tell participants anything about a menu change. `blackout_start_at` must be strictly before `blackout_end_at`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The half-open window running from `blackout_start_at` inclusive to `blackout_end_at` exclusive must not overlap the window of any other proposal on this plan that is `NOTICE_ISSUED` or `EXECUTED`, otherwise `ERR_BLACKOUT_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. The plan's `blackout_buffer_days` enters that test asymmetrically: only the *other* proposal's end is pushed out by the buffer, this proposal's own end is compared as it stands, so the two conflict exactly when this `blackout_start_at` falls strictly before that buffered end and the other's start falls strictly before this `blackout_end_at`. The other proposals are examined in `created_at` ascending order and any that has not yet recorded both ends of a window is skipped. And `blackout_start_at` must be no earlier than `notice_delivered_at` plus `notice_lead_days_required` days, otherwise `ERR_NOTICE_PERIOD_NOT_ELAPSED`. On success `notice_delivered_at` is stamped from the logical clock and the blackout window is reserved for the plan.

**Executing the mapping.** Execution is the recordkeeper's act and moves the proposal from `NOTICE_ISSUED` to `EXECUTED`. The checks run in this order: the proposal must be `NOTICE_ISSUED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`; posture must not be `FROZEN`, otherwise `ERR_MENU_FROZEN`; the clock must have reached `notice_delivered_at` plus `notice_lead_days_required` days, otherwise `ERR_NOTICE_PERIOD_NOT_ELAPSED`; it must be at or after `blackout_start_at` and strictly before `blackout_end_at`, otherwise `ERR_BLACKOUT_WINDOW_CONFLICT`; and the slot must be `PENDING_REPLACEMENT`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.

Execution captures `mapped_assets_cents` as the outgoing fund's `plan_assets_cents` at that instant — captured once, never recomputed later — and moves that amount according to `mapping_strategy`. Under `DIRECT_MAP` and `MAP_TO_QDIA` the incoming fund's `plan_assets_cents` rises by exactly that amount. Under `REENROLLMENT` the amount goes instead to the plan's qualified-default fund, while the incoming fund still becomes the seated fund; a re-enrolment on a plan with no `QDIA`-designated slot, or with such a slot but no fund seated in it, fails with `ERR_QDIA_REQUIRES_TARGET_DATE_SUITE` before anything moves; and where that qualified-default fund happens to be the outgoing fund itself the transfer is a no-op that leaves its assets where they are rather than zeroing them. The outgoing fund's `plan_assets_cents` is then set to 0. The move is an exact integer transfer with no rounding, and a `mapped_assets_cents` of 0 — an empty fund — is legal. The slot's `current_fund_id` becomes the `incoming_fund_id` and the slot returns to `ACTIVE` with `at_risk_since_quarter_index` cleared to null, the incoming fund becomes `ACTIVE` on the menu dimension with `credit_accrual_status` set to `ACCRUING` and `qdia_flag` set to whether this slot is the `QDIA` slot, and `reserved_menu_slots` falls by 1 while `available_menu_slots` rises by 1.

Execution also drives the deepest cascade in the system, five hops long. Hop 0 is the outgoing fund's removal: its `menu_status` moves to `REMOVED`, whether it was sitting in `PENDING_REMOVAL` after a termination recommendation or still `ACTIVE` because it had never been marked for removal. Hop 1, caused by hop 0, sets that fund's `credit_accrual_status` from `ACCRUING` to `CLOSED_OUT` and its `qdia_flag` to false. Hop 2, caused by hop 1, moves every fee levelization run on the plan that is `APPROVED` and whose `pool_fund_ids` includes the removed fund into `RECALC_REQUIRED`, leaving the ledger untouched because nothing was posted. Hop 3 flips the plan's `fee_disclosure_status` from `COMPLIANT` to `RESTATEMENT_REQUIRED`, because the credit-pool basis that the fee disclosure rested on has gone stale. It fires on every execution, whether or not hop 2 found a run to flag: the removal itself is what makes the disclosure stale. A plan already sitting in `RESTATEMENT_REQUIRED` simply stays there. Hop 4, caused by hop 3, moves every motion on the plan that is `OPEN` with `subject_type` of `FEE_LEVELIZATION` to `TABLED`, iterated by `created_at` ascending, and runs only where hop 3 actually flipped the status — on a plan whose disclosure was already `RESTATEMENT_REQUIRED` the open fee-levelization motions are left `OPEN`.

**Unwinding.** Unwinding is the sponsor's compensating power, carries a mandatory `reason` of at least one character that is recorded on the proposal as `unwind_reason`, and is legal only from `EXECUTED` — anything else being `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, checked before the window — and only while the logical clock is strictly before `blackout_end_at`; at or after that instant it is `ERR_UNWIND_WINDOW_CLOSED`. It moves the proposal to `UNWOUND` and reverses the asset move exactly: the fund that actually received the assets at execution — the incoming fund under `DIRECT_MAP` and `MAP_TO_QDIA`, the qualified-default fund under `REENROLLMENT`, recorded at execution so that a later change of qualified default cannot misdirect the reversal — has its `plan_assets_cents` fall by `mapped_assets_cents` and the outgoing fund's `plan_assets_cents` is restored to `mapped_assets_cents`, so the sum of `plan_assets_cents` across every fund row is identical to the pre-execution total, to the cent. The outgoing fund returns from `REMOVED` to `ACTIVE` with `credit_accrual_status` restored from `CLOSED_OUT` to `ACCRUING`, and the incoming fund returns from `ACTIVE` to `CANDIDATE` with its `credit_accrual_status` set to `NOT_ACCRUING` and its `qdia_flag` cleared — a step skipped entirely in the degenerate case where the incoming and outgoing funds are one and the same row. The slot's `current_fund_id` is set back to `outgoing_fund_id` and the slot is `ACTIVE`, and the restored outgoing fund's `qdia_flag` is re-derived from that slot's `designation` rather than assumed false. The plan's `fee_disclosure_status` returns from `RESTATEMENT_REQUIRED` to `COMPLIANT` only when no other proposal on the plan that is still `EXECUTED` has a `blackout_end_at` in the future; levelization runs sitting in `RECALC_REQUIRED` are deliberately *not* restored, because the fee arithmetic has to be re-derived by hand. `UNWOUND` is terminal, as are `EXECUTED`, `WITHDRAWN` and `REJECTED`.

A proposal may also leave the pipeline without ever reaching approval: it is `REJECTED` when the motion that would have authorised it closes `FAILED`, and `WITHDRAWN` when a superseding proposal is created on the same slot or when the approve-time supersede sweep passes over it.

### 3.8. Target-Date Suites, Glidepath Fit and Revenue-Sharing Levelization

**Glidepath fit.** A target-date suite is scored on how closely its glidepath matches a reference shape and on what it costs. The reference depends on `glidepath_type`: a `TO_RETIREMENT` suite is measured against a target equity allocation at 65 of 35.0 percent and a target landing age of 65, while a `THROUGH_RETIREMENT` suite is measured against 50.0 percent and a landing age of 80. The cost allowance depends on `construction`: 45 basis points for `CUSTOM`, 30 for `OFF_THE_SHELF`. The equity gap is the absolute difference between `equity_at_65_pct` and the reference equity; the landing gap is the absolute difference between `landing_point_age` and the reference age; and the cost penalty is half of the amount by which `net_expense_ratio_bps` exceeds the allowance, or zero when it does not exceed it. `glidepath_fit_score` is then 100.00 minus 1.5 times the equity gap, minus 2.0 times the landing gap, minus the cost penalty, rounded half up to two decimal places, with a floor of 0.00 applied *after* the rounding so that a raw value of negative 0.004 becomes 0.00 rather than negative zero. The absolute values are taken before multiplication, and there is a single rounding step.

The four combinations of `glidepath_type` and `construction` produce four different reference-and-allowance pairs from the same three fund inputs, and the divergence is large. A 45-basis-point `CUSTOM` `THROUGH_RETIREMENT` suite with 50.0 percent equity at 65 and a landing age of 80 scores a perfect 100.00; that identical suite priced as `OFF_THE_SHELF` loses 7.50 points of cost penalty; and that same 50.0 percent equity read against the `TO_RETIREMENT` reference loses a further 22.50 points of equity gap. A `net_expense_ratio_bps` exactly equal to the allowance yields a cost penalty of exactly 0.00.

**Selecting the qualified-default suite.** Target-date selection is the chair's act, authorised by a carried motion of `subject_type` `TARGET_DATE_SELECTION` naming the winning fund, and it is a menu-changing write, so a `FROZEN` posture rejects it with `ERR_MENU_FROZEN`. The preconditions run in a fixed order. The named fund's `menu_status` must be `CANDIDATE`, otherwise `ERR_FUND_NOT_CANDIDATE`, and that is the very first check, ahead of the slot and posture gates; the plan must then carry a non-`RETIRED` slot whose `designation` is `QDIA`, otherwise `ERR_QDIA_SLOT_REQUIRED`; the posture gate is evaluated only then, and against that qualified-default slot, so a `RESTRICTED` posture also answers `ERR_MENU_FROZEN` when the slot is itself `AT_RISK` or `PENDING_REPLACEMENT`; and the remaining checks follow in the order motion subject, motion `CARRIED`, approved minutes, adopted statement, the named fund's own policy eligibility, its suite flag, a non-empty pool, the ranking, and finally capacity. The selector is deterministic and the committee does not get to pick freely. A candidate qualifies only when all five of the following hold: `is_target_date_suite` is true; `menu_status` is `CANDIDATE`; `glidepath_type` equals the `glidepath_type` the request supplies, which when the request omits it falls back to the named fund's own `glidepath_type` and is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` when neither is present — the motion itself does not carry a glidepath type; the fund satisfies policy eligibility against the plan's adopted statement; and `track_record_quarters` is at least 12. The qualifying pool is then ordered by `glidepath_fit_score` descending, then by `net_expense_ratio_bps` ascending, then by `track_record_quarters` descending, then by `ticker` ascending — a total order in which the server-minted `id` is never consulted, so the outcome never depends on creation order. The first row wins. Two suites with identical fit and expense are separated by track record and then by ticker. Naming a fund that is not the top-ranked candidate is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; the fund must also satisfy the selection criteria against an adopted statement that must exist at all, otherwise `ERR_NO_ADOPTED_IPS`, and whose criteria it must clear, otherwise `ERR_FUND_FAILS_IPS_CRITERIA`; it must itself carry `is_target_date_suite` true, otherwise `ERR_QDIA_REQUIRES_TARGET_DATE_SUITE`; and the motion must be carried with approved minutes. When no candidate qualifies at all, the request fails with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_TARGET_DATE_SUITE`, nothing is mutated, and the qualified-default slot keeps whatever fund it already had. Selection compares candidates only within the `glidepath_type` the committee actually moved. A successful selection moves the chosen fund from `CANDIDATE` to `ACTIVE` on the menu dimension with `credit_accrual_status` set to `ACCRUING` and `qdia_flag` true, and seats it in the qualified-default slot: `current_fund_id` is overwritten and a slot standing at `EMPTY` moves to `ACTIVE`. Menu capacity is consulted only in that seating case — when the qualified-default slot is `EMPTY` and `available_menu_slots` is below 1, the request fails `ERR_MENU_CAPACITY_EXHAUSTED`, while a slot that already holds a suite consumes no further capacity and skips the check entirely. Any fund the slot previously held, other than the winner itself, has `qdia_flag` cleared and, if it was `ACTIVE`, is demoted to `PENDING_REMOVAL` rather than `REMOVED`, so its assets stay in the plan-wide total until a replacement proposal or review retires it. The plan's derived aggregates and posture are recomputed afterwards, and the response body is the updated fund, returned with HTTP 200.

**Levelizing revenue-sharing credit.** A levelization run is created with `plan_id`, a `quarter_index` unique per plan among runs whose `status` is not `VOID`, and a `method` of `PER_CAPITA` or `PRO_RATA`. Creation derives `pool_fund_ids` — the funds whose revenue sharing forms the pool, which is also the staleness basis for the fee-disclosure cascade — and snapshots `credit_pool_cents`. The request may name `pool_fund_ids` itself, in which case a repeated identifier is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and an identifier that resolves to no fund is `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`; a `pool_fund_ids` present but empty is an explicit naming of an empty pool rather than a request for the default, and yields a run with no lines and a `credit_pool_cents` of 0. When the field is absent altogether, the pool is every fund on the plan whose `credit_accrual_status` is `ACCRUING`, ordered by `plan_assets_cents` descending and then `created_at` ascending. Snapshotting the pool means two things, in this order. First each pool fund's generated credit is brought up to date: where a fund's `accrued_revenue_credit_cents` plus `levelized_credit_cents` falls short of its pass-1 accrual, the shortfall is added to `accrued_revenue_credit_cents`, which raises the plan's `revenue_credit_accrued_cents` and `revenue_credit_balance_cents` with it, so that a run created against funds that have never been levelized finds the credit it needs already on the ledger; the plan's `version` increments on every creation whether or not any fund was actually topped up, and every fund that was topped up has its own `version` incremented too. Then `credit_pool_cents` is stored as the smaller of a `credit_pool_cents` named on the request and the sum of those per-fund accruals, defaulting to that sum when the request names no figure — the cap is silent rather than an error, and it is what keeps `allocated_total_cents` plus `remainder_cents` equal to `credit_pool_cents` when the run is later posted. A `PER_CAPITA` run requires the plan's `participant_account_count` to be greater than zero, otherwise `ERR_NO_PARTICIPANT_ACCOUNTS`. The run starts `DRAFT` with `allocation_lines` empty, `allocated_total_cents` and `remainder_cents` at 0, and `per_account_cents`, `approving_motion_id`, `posted_at` and `reversed_at` null.

The allocation itself runs in three passes, and it runs at approval and at recomputation, never at creation: the run described above sits in `DRAFT` with nothing allocated until a carried motion approves it, and pass 1's per-fund accrual figures inform the creation-time pool snapshot without any allocation line being written. Pass 1 is the accrual floor and is identical under both methods: for each pool fund, iterated by `plan_assets_cents` descending and then `created_at` ascending, that fund's accrual is its `plan_assets_cents` multiplied by its `revenue_share_bps`, divided by 10,000, and floored to whole cents. The eligible pool is the smaller of the run's `credit_pool_cents` and the sum of those per-fund accruals; a `credit_pool_cents` larger than the sum of accruals is silently capped and is not an error. The flooring is applied per fund in this pass, before any pooling — never summed first and floored once.

Pass 2 splits the eligible pool according to `method`. Under `PER_CAPITA`, `per_account_cents` is the eligible pool divided by the plan's `participant_account_count` and floored; `allocated_total_cents` is `per_account_cents` multiplied by that account count; `remainder_cents` is the eligible pool minus `allocated_total_cents`, retained in the plan's credit balance and never distributed. One allocation line is then written per pool fund, each entry carrying `fund_id`, `basis_cents` and `allocated_cents`, where `basis_cents` is that fund's own pass-1 accrual and the allocated amount is `allocated_total_cents` multiplied by that accrual, divided by the sum of all pool-fund accruals, and floored; a sum of accruals of zero makes every line amount 0. Any shortfall arising from those per-line floors is added to `remainder_cents`, and `allocated_total_cents` is then restated as the sum of the line amounts rather than left at `per_account_cents` times the account count — so under `PER_CAPITA` the stored `allocated_total_cents` is the post-floor line total, and it is that restated figure the balance invariant and the posting ledger both use. Under `PRO_RATA`, `per_account_cents` is null because it is not meaningful. Each fund's raw share is the eligible pool multiplied by that fund's `plan_assets_cents` and divided by the plan's `total_plan_assets_cents`, and the line amount is that raw share floored; each line's `basis_cents` under this method is the fund's `plan_assets_cents`, not its accrual. The leftover — the eligible pool minus the sum of the floored line amounts — is then distributed one cent at a time in a waterfall, iterating the pool funds ordered by `plan_assets_cents` descending, then `created_at` ascending, then `ticker` ascending, wrapping around as many times as needed until the leftover reaches zero. Under `PRO_RATA`, `allocated_total_cents` equals the eligible pool exactly and `remainder_cents` is always exactly 0.

Pass 3 asserts the invariant: `allocated_total_cents` plus `remainder_cents` must equal the eligible pool, otherwise `ERR_ALLOCATION_NOT_BALANCED`. The two methods are genuinely different answers to the same question. `PER_CAPITA` levels the credit per participant account and therefore parks a deliberate undistributed remainder — up to one cent less than the account count — in the plan balance, while `PRO_RATA` levels by fund assets and distributes every last cent through a largest-assets-first waterfall. The same pool can therefore produce two different `allocated_total_cents` values, and only `PRO_RATA` can hand a fund a cent it did not itself accrue. Every sort key is total and stable, and none of them falls back to the server-minted `id`.

**Approving, posting, recomputing and reversing.** Approval is the chair's act. Only a `DRAFT` run may be approved, any other status being `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, and that test comes first, ahead of the motion gate. `approving_motion_id` is required and must reference a carried motion with `subject_type` of `FEE_LEVELIZATION` and `subject_id` equal to the run's `id`, otherwise `ERR_MOTION_NOT_CARRIED`; that motion's meeting must be `MINUTES_APPROVED`, otherwise `ERR_MINUTES_NOT_APPROVED`; the run's `quarter_index` must equal the plan's current `fiscal_quarter_index`, otherwise `ERR_QUARTER_CLOSED`; `credit_pool_cents` must not exceed the plan's `revenue_credit_balance_cents`, otherwise `ERR_CREDIT_POOL_INSUFFICIENT`; and every identifier in `pool_fund_ids` must resolve to a fund on this plan whose `credit_accrual_status` is `ACCRUING`, otherwise `ERR_POOL_FUND_INELIGIBLE`; and a `PER_CAPITA` run again requires a `participant_account_count` above zero, otherwise `ERR_NO_PARTICIPANT_ACCOUNTS`, that being the last gate before the passes run. Approval then runs the three passes and freezes `allocation_lines`, `per_account_cents`, `allocated_total_cents` and `remainder_cents`; because pass 3 executes here, approval is itself capable of answering `ERR_ALLOCATION_NOT_BALANCED`, and `approving_motion_id` is written only after the passes have balanced. Approval touches no fund and no ledger figure — the credit is only moved at posting.

Posting is the recordkeeper's act and is the only step that touches the ledger. Only an `APPROVED` run may be posted, any other status being `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` and that test coming first. The remaining gates then run in the order the priority ladder implies rather than the order they are usually recited: `credit_pool_cents` must not exceed the plan's `revenue_credit_balance_cents`, otherwise `ERR_CREDIT_POOL_INSUFFICIENT`; then `allocated_total_cents` plus `remainder_cents` must equal `credit_pool_cents`, otherwise `ERR_ALLOCATION_NOT_BALANCED`; then the plan's `fee_disclosure_status` must not be `RESTATEMENT_REQUIRED`, otherwise `ERR_FEE_DISCLOSURE_RESTATEMENT_REQUIRED`; and finally `allocated_total_cents` on its own must not exceed the balance either, so that posting can never drive the balance below zero, otherwise `ERR_CREDIT_POOL_INSUFFICIENT` again. Posting deliberately does not re-check the run's quarter, so an `APPROVED` run may still be posted after the plan's clock has moved on, and it does not re-run the passes. Posting adds `allocated_total_cents` to the plan's `revenue_credit_allocated_cents`, restates `revenue_credit_balance_cents` as accrued minus allocated, and for each pool fund adds that fund's line amount to its `levelized_credit_cents` while subtracting the same amount from its `accrued_revenue_credit_cents`. No per-fund floor is applied to that subtraction: under `PRO_RATA` a fund handed a cent it did not itself accrue can be left with a negative `accrued_revenue_credit_cents`, and that is not an error, because posting only moves cents between a fund's two credit figures and so leaves the plan-level identity intact. `posted_at` is stamped from the logical clock.

An approved run whose basis has gone stale — because a pool fund reached `REMOVED`, or because the plan's fiscal quarter advanced past the run's `quarter_index` — is moved to `RECALC_REQUIRED` with the ledger untouched, since nothing had been posted. Recomputation is the consultant's act and returns the run to `DRAFT`; only a `RECALC_REQUIRED` run may be recomputed, any other status being `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` and that test coming first, and it then requires the run's `quarter_index` to equal the plan's current `fiscal_quarter_index`, otherwise `ERR_QUARTER_CLOSED`. Recomputation prunes `pool_fund_ids` down to those funds whose `credit_accrual_status` is still `ACCRUING`, dropping alongside them any identifier that no longer resolves at all, which is why the pool-membership check is not re-run here — pruning has already made it vacuous — while a `PER_CAPITA` run still requires a `participant_account_count` above zero, otherwise `ERR_NO_PARTICIPANT_ACCOUNTS`. It clears `approving_motion_id` to null so that a fresh motion is required, re-snapshots `credit_pool_cents` over the pruned pool under exactly the rule creation used, and recomputes `allocation_lines`, `per_account_cents`, `allocated_total_cents` and `remainder_cents`. The request may carry a replacement `method`, which when omitted leaves the run's method in force, and a `credit_pool_cents` to feed that re-snapshot, which when omitted defaults the same way creation does — to the full accrual sum of the pruned pool — rather than preserving whatever the run had snapshotted before. The cycle from `DRAFT` to `APPROVED` to `RECALC_REQUIRED` and back to `DRAFT` may repeat.

Reversal is the sponsor's compensating power over a run in `POSTED`, any other status being `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` and that test coming ahead of the quarter test, and legal while the plan's `fiscal_quarter_index` minus the run's `quarter_index` is at most 1, otherwise `ERR_QUARTER_CLOSED`. A `reason` of 1 to 500 characters is mandatory and is recorded on the run as `reversal_reason`. It subtracts `allocated_total_cents` from the plan's `revenue_credit_allocated_cents` and restores every pool fund's `levelized_credit_cents` and `accrued_revenue_credit_cents` line by line from the stored `allocation_lines`, so that accrued minus allocated still equals the balance. `reversed_at` is stamped. `REVERSED` is terminal, so a run can never be reversed twice. A `DRAFT` run whose quarter falls more than one quarter behind the plan's clock is voided by the quarter advance.

### 3.9. Provider Oversight, the Request-for-Proposal Cycle, and Advancing the Quarter

**Engaging a provider.** A service provider engagement is created with `plan_id`, a `provider_name`, a `provider_role` of `RECORDKEEPER`, `ADVISOR`, `AUDITOR` or `CUSTODIAN`, a closed contract term from `term_start_date` to `term_end_date` where the end is no earlier than the start, a non-negative `per_head_fee_cents` recording the annual per-participant hard-dollar fee, an `asset_based_bps` from 0 to 200 recording the annual asset-based fee, an optional `benchmark_percentile` from 1 to 100 where 100 is the most expensive versus peer plans, and an optional non-negative `last_rfp_quarter_index` seeding the quarter of the most recent completed competition for that role, which stays null when the request omits it. `provider_name` runs 1 to 160 characters and both dates are stored back exactly as the request spelled them. The term must not overlap another engagement on the same plan and `provider_role` whose `status` is `PENDING`, `ACTIVE` or `UNDER_RFP`, otherwise `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_TERM_OVERLAP`; the comparison is on closed intervals with no buffer of any kind, so two terms that merely share a single calendar day overlap while a term beginning the day after another's end does not, and at most one active or under-competition engagement exists per plan and role. A new engagement is `PENDING` with `gross_cost_bps`, `fee_credit_tier`, `total_cost_bps`, `last_benchmarked_quarter_index`, `successor_engagement_id` and `approving_motion_id` all null and `rfp_required` false.

**Benchmarking.** Benchmarking is the consultant's act and it derives the engagement's cost picture in three passes that must run in the order A, B, C. The request may carry a `benchmark_percentile` from 1 to 100, which overwrites the engagement's stored figure before the passes run; when the request omits it, the engagement's existing `benchmark_percentile` is reused, and when neither the request nor the engagement supplies one the call fails `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A terminal engagement — `TERMINATED` or `SUPERSEDED` — may not be benchmarked at all, answering `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`; that test runs once the percentile has been resolved and before any of the three passes. Pass A produces the gross cost in basis points of plan assets. The head cost in cents is `per_head_fee_cents` multiplied by the plan's `participant_account_count`; the head cost in basis points is that figure multiplied by 10,000, divided by the plan's `total_plan_assets_cents`, and rounded half up to two decimal places — a `total_plan_assets_cents` of zero is `ERR_NO_PLAN_ASSETS` rather than a division by zero. `gross_cost_bps` is `asset_based_bps` plus the head cost in basis points, rounded half up to two decimal places.

Pass B selects `fee_credit_tier` from `gross_cost_bps` **only**, never from the total. A `gross_cost_bps` of 25.00 or less is `TIER_A` with an offset of 0.00 basis points; 45.00 or less is `TIER_B` with an offset of 5.00; anything higher is `TIER_C` with an offset of 12.00. The offset is applied only when the plan's `revenue_credit_balance_cents` is strictly greater than zero; with a zero balance the tier is still recorded but the offset is forced to 0.00.

Pass C produces `total_cost_bps` as `gross_cost_bps` minus the offset, rounded half up to two decimal places, with a floor of 0.00 so it is never negative. `rfp_required` is then true when any one of four conditions holds: `benchmark_percentile` is 75.00 or more; `last_rfp_quarter_index` is null; the plan's `fiscal_quarter_index` minus `last_rfp_quarter_index` is 20 or more; or `total_cost_bps` is strictly greater than 60.00.

The pass order is not stylistic. Deriving the tier from `total_cost_bps` instead of `gross_cost_bps` would let a `TIER_C` engagement fall into `TIER_B` after its own offset had been applied, a self-referential loop the pinned order forbids. The credit-balance gate also means the identical engagement can be `TIER_C` with a 12.00 basis-point offset in one quarter and `TIER_C` with a 0.00 offset in the next, flipping `rfp_required` on the total-cost arm without any fee changing at all. The boundaries are exact: a `gross_cost_bps` of 25.00 is `TIER_A` while 25.01 is `TIER_B`, and 45.00 is `TIER_B` while 45.01 is `TIER_C`; a `benchmark_percentile` of exactly 75.00 triggers a competition because the comparison is inclusive; 20 quarters is exactly five years, so a gap of exactly 20 triggers while 19 does not; and a `total_cost_bps` of exactly 60.00 does not trigger while 60.01 does.

Benchmarking a `PENDING` engagement moves it to `ACTIVE`, after re-checking the term-overlap rule, and stamps `last_benchmarked_quarter_index` with the plan's `fiscal_quarter_index`. Benchmarking an already-`ACTIVE` engagement is a self-loop that recomputes the cost figures and `rfp_required` and restamps `last_benchmarked_quarter_index`; it requires the plan's `fiscal_quarter_index` to be strictly greater than the existing `last_benchmarked_quarter_index`, otherwise `ERR_BENCHMARK_ALREADY_CURRENT` — a guard that applies to the `ACTIVE` self-loop alone, and only when `last_benchmarked_quarter_index` is already set, so an `ACTIVE` engagement that has never been benchmarked passes it freely. A benchmark run that yields `rfp_required` true moves the engagement from `ACTIVE` to `UNDER_RFP`. Benchmarking an engagement already `UNDER_RFP` carries no quarter guard at all and may be repeated within a single quarter: finding `rfp_required` false returns it to `ACTIVE` — the incumbent has been retained — and stamps both `last_rfp_quarter_index` and `last_benchmarked_quarter_index` with the current `fiscal_quarter_index`, while finding `rfp_required` still true leaves the engagement `UNDER_RFP` and `last_rfp_quarter_index` untouched, restamping only `last_benchmarked_quarter_index` and the three cost figures.

**Awarding the competition.** An award is the sponsor's act and moves the incumbent from `UNDER_RFP` to `SUPERSEDED`; any other starting status is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, and that test comes first, ahead of every gate below. Both `approving_motion_id` and `successor_engagement_id` are required and must resolve. The gates then run in the order the priority ladder dictates rather than the order they read most naturally: first, no active committee member whose `affiliated_provider_role` equals this engagement's `provider_role` may have voted `FOR` on the motion, otherwise `ERR_RECUSAL_REQUIRED` — the closed tally is re-inspected at award time, not merely at vote time, and the scan looks only at `FOR` entries cast by members still `ACTIVE`, treating a null `affiliated_provider_role` as `NONE`; then `approving_motion_id` must reference a carried motion with `subject_type` of `RFP_AWARD` and `subject_id` equal to this engagement's `id`, otherwise `ERR_MOTION_NOT_CARRIED`; then that motion's meeting must be `MINUTES_APPROVED`, otherwise `ERR_MINUTES_NOT_APPROVED`; and last the named `successor_engagement_id` must be some other engagement, belonging to the same plan and `provider_role` and standing at `PENDING`, otherwise `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` — naming the incumbent itself takes that same arm. On success `successor_engagement_id` and `approving_motion_id` are both recorded on the incumbent, the successor moves from `PENDING` to `ACTIVE` with `last_rfp_quarter_index` set to the plan's `fiscal_quarter_index` and the same `approving_motion_id` stamped on it too, and the incumbent's `term_end_date` is clamped to one day before the successor's `term_start_date`, which preserves the no-overlap invariant by construction. The response body is the superseded incumbent, not the successor.

**Terminating an engagement.** Termination is the sponsor's act and is legal from `PENDING` or `ACTIVE`, any other status being `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. A `reason` of 1 to 500 characters is mandatory and is recorded on the engagement as `termination_reason`. If the engagement's `provider_role` is `RECORDKEEPER`, another engagement on the plan with that same role must be `PENDING` or `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_RECORDKEEPER_REQUIRED` — a plan may never be left without a recordkeeper. No replacement proposal on the plan may be `NOTICE_ISSUED`, otherwise `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_HAS_PENDING_MAPPING`, because a mapping already announced to participants must not lose the party that would execute it. Termination clamps `term_end_date` back to the current logical date whenever the stored end was later, and it does so from `PENDING` just as from `ACTIVE`, leaving an already-past end date alone. Both `TERMINATED` and `SUPERSEDED` are terminal.

**Advancing the quarter.** The quarter advance is the plan sponsor's periodic maintenance run and it is the only thing that moves the fiscal epoch. It requires no charter and no quorum, and it increments the plan's `fiscal_quarter_index` by 1 and then performs exactly five sweeps in the order below, the increment landing before any sweep so that every sweep compares against the new index. Every training record whose `completed_quarter_index` is set and is now at least `validity_quarters` behind the new index moves from `COMPLETED` or `WAIVED` to `EXPIRED` — a record still `ASSIGNED`, or one carrying no `completed_quarter_index`, is left exactly as it stands — and each affected member's `fiduciary_training_status` is recomputed; expiry alone does not change menu posture, but a member whose training has lapsed can no longer convene, vote, approve minutes or finalize, and counts toward neither quorum figure at future meetings. Every `SCHEDULED` meeting from a prior quarter is voided, and any quarterly review pointing at it has `meeting_id` reset to null. Every review in `DRAFT` or `SCORING` more than one quarter old is voided along with each of its scorecards still standing at `SUBMITTED`, and both `submitted_scorecard_count` and `scorecard_count` are then zeroed on that review, so a voided review reports no scorecards at all even though its superseded rows survive in the collection. Every `APPROVED` levelization run from a prior quarter moves to `RECALC_REQUIRED`, and every `DRAFT` run more than one quarter old is voided. Every member in a non-terminal state whose `term_end_date` is strictly before the current logical date — visited in `created_at` ascending order — moves to `TERM_EXPIRED` with `end_term_reason` set to `TERM_EXPIRY`, and its seat moves from `FILLED` to `VACANT`. The vacancy counter is then a *separate* pass over the plan's seats rather than part of that move: once the expiries have landed, `consecutive_vacant_quarters` is incremented by 1 on every seat whose `status` is `VACANT`, so a seat that was already vacant before the advance is incremented too, and a seat vacated by this very run is incremented in the same advance. Menu posture and the derived aggregates are recomputed last, and the plan's `version` increments by 1.

**Recomputing posture explicitly.** Either the sponsor or the consultant may force a posture recomputation without any other change. It recomputes `at_risk_slot_ratio` and `menu_posture`, and when the posture becomes `OPEN` it restores every `BLOCKED` proposal on the plan to its `pre_block_status` exactly and clears that snapshot, iterated by slot `slot_index` ascending and then proposal `created_at` ascending, a `BLOCKED` proposal carrying no snapshot falling back to `DRAFT`; and when at least one proposal was released the capacity partition is recomputed a second time, so `reserved_menu_slots`, `available_menu_slots` and `draft_proposal_count` all reflect the restored statuses in the same response. The plan's `version` increments by 1 on every call, whether or not any derived figure actually moved.

**Moving the clock.** The logical UTC clock is settable through a dedicated control endpoint carrying a `current_utc` value. It is the only endpoint in the system that performs no authentication of any kind: no user-context headers are consulted, no role is checked, and the roles named for it elsewhere in this document are documentary rather than enforced, so any caller may move the clock. `current_utc` is required and must parse as a timestamp; the response is HTTP 200 whose `data` is not a resource row but the single field `current_utc`, echoing the clock's new reading. Before any call to it the clock reads exactly 2026-08-01T00:00:00Z, the server never falls back to real wall-clock time, and time may be moved either forward or backward. Every notice-period, blackout-window and term-validity rule reads this clock, which is what makes those rules testable.
