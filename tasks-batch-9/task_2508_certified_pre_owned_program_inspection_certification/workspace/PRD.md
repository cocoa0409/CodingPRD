# Certified Pre-Owned Program API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

An automaker's certified pre-owned (CPO) programme is a promise made three times over: to the customer who pays a premium for a used car that carries a factory badge, to the franchised dealer who fronts the inspection and reconditioning cost in exchange for that badge, and to the automaker's own brand, which absorbs every warranty claim the badge generates. The programme fails quietly rather than loudly. A dealer who certifies a rental-fleet car, skips a brake measurement, or leaves a syndicated advertisement live three weeks after the car was wholesaled does not trigger an alarm — it simply erodes the value of the badge for everyone else. This service exists to make each of those failures a hard, auditable rejection rather than a discretionary judgement call.

The system this document specifies is the programme's system of record. It holds the programme rulebook (age and mileage ceilings, excluded title brands and prior uses, fee schedule, warranty extension terms, audit sampling rates, documentation retention periods), the versioned multipoint inspection checklist catalogue, the dealer and technician roster, every vehicle under evaluation, and the full evidentiary chain from an opened inspection through an approved certification, its advertisement, its warranty claims, its post-certification audit, and — where it comes to that — its decertification. Everything is mounted under the `/cpo` path prefix.

Three characteristics of the domain shape the design and should be read as requirements rather than as commentary. First, the programme is a **compliance-audit** system: a certification is not merely a status flag but a dated, priced, warranty-bearing claim pinned to the exact rulebook generation it was approved under, so that retuning the programme tomorrow cannot retroactively re-price a car certified today. Second, a vehicle carries **two independent state axes** — its programme standing and its commercial standing — which evolve separately and gate each other at exactly one declared point; a car can be sold while uncertified, and a car can be certified while a complaint against it is open. Third, almost every meaningful write in the system **cascades**: an audit finding of FAIL does not just mark a sample, it decertifies a certification, credits a fee, withdraws advertisements, dismisses complaints, voids in-flight re-inspections, and — if the dealer has now failed twice — suspends the dealer, which rejects its pending submissions and pulls its live listings. Implementers should treat those cascades as first-class deliverables, not as follow-up work.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the service must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that principal is acting under. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the five declared role names, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). The five roles are spelled exactly `ProgramAdministrator`, `ProgramAuditor`, `DealerPrincipal`, `InspectionTechnician`, and `CustomerAdvocate`. When the headers are well-formed but the acting role is not authorised for the requested operation, the rejection is `ERR_ROLE_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 403).

The five roles divide along a deliberate segregation-of-duties line. A `ProgramAdministrator` is the automaker's programme office: it owns the programme tunables, the checklist catalogue, dealer enrolment and privilege, certification adjudication, warranty-claim adjudication, and reconditioning waivers — and it deliberately cannot draw audit samples or rule on them. A `ProgramAuditor` is the independent compliance function: it draws audit samples and records findings, and it deliberately cannot approve the certifications it audits, enrol dealers, or move a dealer's privilege by hand, because any suspension it causes must arrive through the audit-failure threshold rather than by fiat. A `DealerPrincipal` is the certifying dealership: it opens inspections, submits certifications with their documentation, advertises and syndicates, files warranty claims, and every one of its writes is scoped to its own dealership. An `InspectionTechnician` is a credentialed inspector employed by a dealer: it records checklist outcomes for its own inspections and closes reconditioning items on *other* technicians' inspections, and it can neither open, submit, nor certify. A `CustomerAdvocate` is programme-side customer relations: it logs consumer complaints against certified vehicles and reads the consumer-facing certification, listing, and complaint record, and it can neither inspect, certify, adjudicate, nor audit.

Two of the five roles are **entity-backed**, which is what ties `X-User-ID` to resource ownership. When `X-User-Role` is `DealerPrincipal`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Dealer`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Dealer` compare that resource's dealer foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `InspectionTechnician`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Technician`; ownership checks compare the technician foreign-key field of the targeted resource — or, for a reconditioning item, the `dealer_id` of the technician's employer against the `dealer_id` of the item's inspection — to `X-User-ID`. If an entity-backed principal's `X-User-ID` does not resolve to an existing `Dealer` row (for `DealerPrincipal`) or an existing `Technician` row (for `InspectionTechnician`), the request is rejected with `ERR_IDENTITY_NOT_RECOGNIZED` (HTTP 403). If the identity resolves but the targeted inspection, certification, listing, claim, or technician belongs to a different dealership, the rejection is `ERR_DEALER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403).

The remaining three roles are opaque. When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramAdministrator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it — for example as the `closed_by_user_id` stamped on a reconditioning item excused by waiver — but never compares it to any entity's `id`. The same holds when `X-User-Role` is `ProgramAuditor`, whose opaque identifier is recorded as `reviewer_user_id` on the audit sample it rules on, and when `X-User-Role` is `CustomerAdvocate`, whose opaque identifier is not persisted on any row at all.

One operation carries **variant-scoped exclusivity** rather than endpoint-scoped exclusivity. Closing a reconditioning item accepts both `InspectionTechnician` and `ProgramAdministrator` as callers, but the two resolutions belong to different roles: a `resolution` of `COMPLETED` is `InspectionTechnician`-exclusive and a `resolution` of `WAIVED` is `ProgramAdministrator`-exclusive. An `InspectionTechnician` asking for `WAIVED`, or a `ProgramAdministrator` asking for `COMPLETED`, is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_FORBIDDEN` even though both roles appear on the operation's authorised list.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list-returning operation accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1, and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` outside the range from 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is a schema violation rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) — the service never silently clamps an out-of-range value.

Unless a specific listing states otherwise, results are ordered by `created_at` ascending, so that pagination is stable while a collection grows. Four pagination fields appear inside `meta` on every list response: the `limit` and `offset` actually applied, `total_count` giving the number of rows matching the filter before pagination, and `has_more`, which is true when rows remain beyond the current page.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties

Twelve of the system's resource types — the programme configuration, dealers, technicians, vehicles, checklist templates, inspections, reconditioning items, certifications, audit samples, warranty claims, listings, and complaints — carry the full set of system-managed common properties:

- `id` (string): a server-minted UUID. It carries no type prefix and no embedded meaning; clients must treat it as opaque.
- `created_at` (string): the ISO 8601 UTC timestamp, with three fractional digits, at which the row was first written.
- `updated_at` (string): the ISO 8601 UTC timestamp, in the same format, of the most recent mutation of the row. Recomputing a derived counter is a mutation and refreshes this field.
- `version` (integer): a monotonically increasing integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of the row, including mutations the row receives as a cascade from another entity's write.

Three resource types are configuration or append-only evidence rows and are never mutated after creation: checklist items, inspection results, and certification documents. These carry only `id` and `created_at`; both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from them. Certification documents are the one nuance here — the approval of their parent certification stamps a `retained_until` value onto them, which is a completion of the row rather than a business mutation, and they still expose neither `updated_at` nor `version`.

Because `version` is the substrate of the concurrency guards described in §2.8, implementers must be scrupulous about *which* rows a write touches. Where a cascade in §3 states that a vehicle, dealer, certification, listing, complaint, or inspection is affected, that row's `version` increments and its `updated_at` refreshes, even when the request that caused it named an entirely different resource.

### 2.5. The Logical Programme Clock

Every deadline, coverage window, retention date, credential expiry, technician slot, and claim window in this system is evaluated against a server-held **logical clock**, never against wall-clock time. Before any request moves it, the clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time. A `ProgramAdministrator` may advance or rewind it by supplying a `current_utc` instant to an administrative test-driving capability, which echoes back the `current_utc` now in force.

Moving the clock is a purely forward-looking act. It never rewrites a stored timestamp and never recomputes a window that has already been persisted: a certification whose coverage dates were computed under yesterday's clock keeps those dates after the clock moves, and a complaint's re-inspection deadline stays where it was written. Everything the clock affects is affected at evaluation time, not at storage time.

### 2.6. The Programme Rulebook and Version Pinning

The programme's tunables live in a single **programme configuration** row that the server seeds at startup with the documented defaults; there is exactly one such row and it is never created or deleted through the API. A `ProgramAdministrator` retunes the programme by supplying any subset of the tunable fields; every field is optional and an omitted field keeps the value of the current generation. Each successful retune publishes a **new generation** by incrementing the configuration row's `version`. All five roles may read the live configuration, including its `version`, so that dealers and tests can see the tunables in force.

Retuning is never retroactive. When a certification is approved, it records the configuration `version` in force at that moment in its own `program_config_version` field, and every fee, warranty, and retention figure on that certification row is computed from that pinned generation and is never recomputed afterwards. A later retune of the fee schedule therefore changes the price of the *next* certification and leaves every already-approved one alone. Rules that are evaluated at request time rather than stored — eligibility screens, audit sampling percentages, disclosure requirements, slot lengths, per-claim caps — always read the *live* generation, because there is no pinned generation to read before approval has happened.

The tunables, with the values the server seeds them to, are as follows. Eligibility: `max_age_days` is 2190 and must be at least 1; `max_odometer_miles` is 80000 and must be at least 1; `ineligible_title_brands` is `SALVAGE`, `FLOOD`, `LEMON`, `REBUILT`, `ODOMETER_DISCREPANCY` (any subset of the title-brand enumeration other than `CLEAN`); `ineligible_prior_uses` is `RENTAL`, `TAXI`, `DRIVING_SCHOOL`, `POLICE` (any subset of the prior-use enumeration). Fee schedule: `base_fee_cents` is 89900; `age_surcharge_free_years` is 3; `age_surcharge_cents_per_year` is 12500; `mileage_surcharge_free_miles` is 40000; `mileage_surcharge_cents_per_10k` is 9000; `surcharge_cap_pct` is 0.35; and `volume_discount_tiers` is the ordered list of `min_count`/`pct` pairs 0 at 0.0, 10 at 0.05, and 50 at 0.10. Reconditioning: `recon_reimbursement_share` is 0.6 and `recon_reimbursement_cap_cents` is 150000. Coverage: `factory_basic_months` is 36; `factory_basic_miles` is 36000; `warranty_extension_months` is 12; `warranty_extension_miles` is 12000; `warranty_total_cap_months` is 84; `warranty_total_cap_miles` is 100000. Claims and dealer behaviour: `claim_per_claim_cap_cents` is 250000; `claim_window_days` is 365; `claim_variant_volume_floor` is 20; `claim_ratio_probation_threshold` is 0.25; `claim_ratio_suspension_threshold` is 0.40; `probation_claim_share` is 0.8. Audit: `audit_sample_pct` is 0.2; `audit_sample_min_per_run` is 1; `audit_fail_suspension_count` is 2. Documentation and marketing: `doc_retention_years` is 7; `required_doc_types` is `TITLE_HISTORY_REPORT`, `INSPECTION_WORKSHEET`, `RECONDITIONING_INVOICE`, `CUSTOMER_DISCLOSURE`; `listing_required_disclosure_fields` is `CERTIFIED_BADGE`, `WARRANTY_END_DATE`, `INSPECTION_DATE`, `CERTIFICATION_NUMBER`; `listing_removal_sla_hours` is 48. Scheduling: `inspection_slot_minutes` is 120 and `technician_buffer_minutes` is 30. Each knob's exact effect is specified in the §3 subsection that consumes it.

### 2.7. Parallel Vehicle State Axes and Cascade Propagation

A vehicle in this system carries two state fields that are not two views of one lifecycle but two genuinely independent axes. `cpo_state` is the **programme standing** axis, with values `NOT_CERTIFIED`, `CERTIFICATION_PENDING`, `CERTIFIED`, and `DECERTIFIED`; it is driven by inspection creation, certification approval, certification rejection, and decertification. `inventory_state` is the **commercial standing** axis, with values `IN_INVENTORY`, `RETAIL_SOLD`, and `WHOLESALED`; it is driven exclusively by recorded vehicle history events and never by certification activity. Neither axis derives from the other. A vehicle may be `RETAIL_SOLD` while its `cpo_state` is still `NOT_CERTIFIED`, and its `cpo_state` may be `CERTIFIED` while an unrelated complaint sits open against it. Neither axis has a terminal state: `DECERTIFIED` on the programme axis is a resting state a returned vehicle may leave, and a wholesaled vehicle may come back to inventory.

The two axes touch at exactly two declared points, and nowhere else. Entering `CERTIFICATION_PENDING` requires `inventory_state` to be `IN_INVENTORY`, and approving a certification requires the same thing — the programme only certifies cars the dealer still holds. Conversely, the disposition history events that move `inventory_state` off `IN_INVENTORY` cascade into any live certification of that vehicle, which in turn moves `cpo_state`.

Cascades in this system are **immediate and multi-hop**: within the single request that triggers them, every downstream row is brought to its consistent state before the response is written. Where §3 describes a cascade in hops, each hop is caused by the hop before it, and a hop that does not apply (because no matching row exists, or because a threshold has not been reached) is simply skipped without failing the request. Every row a cascade touches has its `version` incremented and its `updated_at` refreshed, and the response body returns the resource named by the request, not the cascaded ones — cascaded rows are observed by reading them back.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Two write operations in this system are guarded by optimistic concurrency control on a **parent** entity's `version`. In both cases the guard field is a required, non-optional element of the request body, and in both cases it carries the client's last observed `version` of the parent row.

Submitting a certification requires `expected_vehicle_version`, carrying the `version` of the vehicle the submitting dealer last read. If that value does not equal the vehicle's current `version`, the submission is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VEHICLE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The guard exists because a submission legitimately supersedes an earlier still-pending certification on the same vehicle: a writer holding a stale view of the vehicle would silently displace a submission it never observed, and would also have missed any history event — an odometer update, a title branding — recorded since it last read the vehicle.

Syndicating a listing requires `expected_certification_version`, carrying the `version` of the certification the dealer last read. If that value does not equal the certification's current `version`, the syndication is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CERTIFICATION_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Re-syndication legitimately replaces the previously published snapshot, so a writer working from a stale certification generation would publish superseded fee, warranty, or audit content under a fresh publication timestamp.

No other operation in the system takes a version field, and none may be rejected for a version mismatch. Note also that both guarded operations *advance* the parent's `version` as part of their own success — a certification submission increments the vehicle's `version`, and a syndication increments the certification's `version` — so a client that wants to write twice in a row must re-read the parent between the two writes.

### 2.9. Money, Ratios, and Rounding Discipline

Every monetary amount in this system is an **integer number of cents**, named with a `_cents` suffix, and is never expressed as a fractional currency value. Fees, reconditioning costs, reimbursements, claim amounts, advertised prices, and ledger balances are all whole cents. Where a monetary computation involves a division or a percentage, the specification names the rounding mode explicitly and implementers must follow it exactly, because the modes are deliberately not uniform:

- The certification fee uses **floor** on every division and on every percentage multiplication, with no intermediate rounding to dollars.
- A per-item reconditioning reimbursement uses **round half up** to whole cents, applied to each item *individually before* the items are summed.
- A claim payout uses **floor** on the share multiplication.
- The mileage-block count in the fee surcharge and the whole-year vehicle age both use **floor**; the audit sample count uses **ceiling**.

Two ratio fields on a dealer — the effective claim ratio and the audit failure rate — are decimal numbers rounded to exactly 2 decimal places using round half up. The dealer's risk score is an integer from 0 through 100, produced by rounding a weighted sum half up and then capping it. Where a division might have a zero denominator, the specification substitutes 1 for the denominator rather than producing an undefined result, so a dealer with no activations and no claims scores a claim ratio of `0.00` rather than failing.

### 2.10. Validation Precedence

Requests are validated in a strict, single ascending ladder, and the **first** failing rung determines the response. This matters because many requests violate several rules at once, and only one error identifier can be returned. The ladder, in order, is:

1. Authentication: both headers present and `X-User-Role` a declared role — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. Role authorisation, including the variant-scoped exclusivity of the reconditioning-item close described in §2.1 — otherwise `ERR_ROLE_FORBIDDEN`.
3. Identity resolution for entity-backed roles — otherwise `ERR_IDENTITY_NOT_RECOGNIZED`.
4. Schema validity of the request body and query parameters: required fields present, enum members recognised, numbers in range, `limit` within 1 through 100, `offset` at least 0, arrays that must be non-empty actually non-empty — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
5. Existence of every resource named by a path segment, checked left to right — otherwise the not-found identifier for the first missing segment.
6. Existence of every foreign key supplied in the request body — otherwise `ERR_REFERENCED_ENTITY_NOT_FOUND`.
7. Dealer-scope ownership — otherwise `ERR_DEALER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`.
8. State legality: the target resource is in a state from which the requested transition is declared — otherwise the state-conflict identifier for that resource type.
9. The optimistic concurrency guard of §2.8 — otherwise the stale-version identifier for the parent entity.
10. Title-brand eligibility — otherwise `ERR_BRANDED_TITLE_INELIGIBLE`.
11. Prior-use eligibility — otherwise `ERR_PRIOR_USE_INELIGIBLE`.
12. Vehicle age ceiling — otherwise `ERR_VEHICLE_AGE_EXCEEDED`.
13. Odometer ceiling and floor — otherwise `ERR_MILEAGE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`.
14. Dealer certifying privilege — otherwise `ERR_DEALER_PRIVILEGE_SUSPENDED`.
15. No other open inspection on the same vehicle — otherwise `ERR_INSPECTION_ALREADY_OPEN`.
16. Segregation of duties — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
17. Checklist item resolution for recorded results — otherwise `ERR_UNKNOWN_CHECKLIST_ITEM`.
18. Measurement consistency with the recorded outcome — otherwise `ERR_MEASUREMENT_CONTRADICTS_OUTCOME`.
19. Checklist completeness at submission — otherwise `ERR_INCOMPLETE_CHECKLIST`.
20. No repair still outstanding on an otherwise clean attempt — otherwise `ERR_RECONDITIONING_INCOMPLETE`.
21. The vehicle's certification slot is free — otherwise `ERR_VEHICLE_ALREADY_CERTIFIED`.
22. Required documentation present — otherwise `ERR_DOCUMENTATION_INCOMPLETE`.
23. No inapplicable or duplicated documentation — otherwise `ERR_DOCUMENT_NOT_APPLICABLE`.
24. Marketing disclosure standards — otherwise `ERR_MARKETING_STANDARD_VIOLATION`.
25. Uniqueness constraints — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`.
26. Inspection start not in the future — otherwise `ERR_INSPECTION_DATE_IN_FUTURE`.
27. Technician credential validity — otherwise `ERR_TECHNICIAN_CREDENTIAL_EXPIRED`.
28. Technician slot availability — otherwise `ERR_TECHNICIAN_SLOT_CONFLICT`.
29. Re-inspection deadline — otherwise `ERR_REINSPECTION_DEADLINE_PASSED`.
30. Non-empty computed coverage interval — otherwise `ERR_NO_COVERAGE_REMAINING`.
31. Coverage interval non-overlap on the vehicle — otherwise `ERR_COVERAGE_WINDOW_OVERLAP`.
32. Claimed repair inside the coverage window — otherwise `ERR_OUTSIDE_WARRANTY_WINDOW`.
33. Per-claim amount ceiling — otherwise `ERR_CLAIM_AMOUNT_EXCEEDS_CAP`.
34. Per-item reconditioning cost ceiling — otherwise `ERR_RECONDITIONING_COST_EXCEEDS_CAP`.
35. Content-eligibility preconditions that no earlier rung covers: a checklist template is publishable only when it carries at least one point *and* at least one `CRITICAL` point, otherwise `ERR_TEMPLATE_NOT_PUBLISHABLE`; and a reconditioning item whose `severity` is `CRITICAL` may never be excused by waiver, otherwise `ERR_CRITICAL_RECONDITIONING_NOT_WAIVABLE`.

Rung 35 sits deliberately last: because the corresponding state checks live at rung 8, a request that targets a template which is no longer in draft, or an item which is already closed, still surfaces as the state conflict rather than as the content-eligibility error.

### 2.11. Nested Resource Retrieval

Many operations address a child through its parent, for example `/cpo/checklist-templates/{id}/items`, `/cpo/inspections/{id}/results`, or `/cpo/certifications/{id}/audit-samples`. Path segments are resolved strictly **left to right**, and the request is rejected at the first identifier that does not name an existing row, with the not-found identifier belonging to that resource type: `ERR_DEALER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VEHICLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TEMPLATE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RECONDITIONING_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CERTIFICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AUDIT_SAMPLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LISTING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, or `ERR_COMPLAINT_NOT_FOUND`. All ten are HTTP 404. A request for the items of a template that does not exist therefore reports the missing template, never an empty list.

Several rows in this system are created only as a cascade of some other write and have no creation operation of their own — reconditioning items, certification documents, and audit samples all fall into this category. For those, the parent-scoped listing is the *only* discovery channel, and it is also how a client learns the identifiers it will later need in order to close a repair or record an audit finding.

### 2.12. Interval Semantics

Two distinct interval comparisons appear in this system, and they use **opposite** adjacency conventions. Implementers who unify them will fail one of the two.

A certification's warranty coverage interval runs from its first covered date to its first *uncovered* date and is **half-open**: the start date is inside the interval and the end date is outside it. Two coverage intervals on the same vehicle conflict when the candidate's start falls strictly before the retained interval's end *and* the retained interval's start falls strictly before the candidate's end. **Adjacency is allowed**: a candidate whose start equals a retained interval's end is not a conflict, and neither is a candidate whose end equals a retained interval's start.

A technician's inspection slot, by contrast, is **closed on both ends** after a buffer is applied at each end, and **adjacency is forbidden**: two buffered slots that touch at a single instant do conflict. The conflict test is that the candidate's buffered low bound is at or before the occupied slot's buffered high bound *and* the occupied slot's buffered low bound is at or before the candidate's buffered high bound.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Inspection Catalogue: Checklist Templates and Points

Nothing can be inspected until the automaker has published a checklist to inspect against. A `ProgramAdministrator` authors a **checklist template** by supplying a `code` — a short label such as `MPI-172`, which must be unique across all templates — and an `effective_from` date given as `YYYY-MM-DD`, the date from which the published template may back inspections. A new template is born in `DRAFT` with `published_at` null, `point_count` 0, and `critical_item_count` 0. A second template reusing an existing `code` is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`.

Into a `DRAFT` template the administrator adds **inspection points**, one at a time. Each point carries an `item_code` such as `B1`, unique within its template and the natural key that recorded results will later resolve against; a `category` drawn from `POWERTRAIN`, `BRAKES`, `TIRES`, `SAFETY`, `ELECTRICAL`, `BODY`, `INTERIOR`, or `ROAD_TEST`; a `severity` of `CRITICAL`, `MAJOR`, or `MINOR` describing the consequence class of a failure on that point; and a human-readable `pass_criteria` string that is recorded verbatim on the worksheet. A measured point additionally declares `min_measure`, the inclusive lower bound a reading must meet to pass, and `max_measure`, the inclusive upper bound it must respect; either may be omitted, in which case it is null and that side of the band is unbounded. A point where both are null is not a measured point at all. Two more attributes govern what a failure *costs*: `reconditioning_allowed`, a boolean defaulting to true, says whether a failure on this point may be cured by repair, and `max_reconditioning_cost_cents`, defaulting to 100000 cents, is the per-item ceiling on the reimbursable repair amount for the point. Because inspection points are append-only configuration, they are never edited after creation; adding a point recomputes the template's `point_count` and its `critical_item_count` (the number of its points whose `severity` is `CRITICAL`) and increments the template's `version` by 1. Adding a point to a template that has left `DRAFT` is rejected with `ERR_TEMPLATE_NOT_DRAFT`, and a duplicate `item_code` within the same template with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`.

Publishing moves a template `DRAFT → PUBLISHED`, sets `published_at` to the logical clock, and increments the template's `version` by 1. It is refused with `ERR_TEMPLATE_NOT_DRAFT` when the template is not currently `DRAFT`, and with `ERR_TEMPLATE_NOT_PUBLISHABLE` when the template carries no points at all or carries no `CRITICAL` point — a multipoint inspection must have at least one point and at least one point whose failure is critical. Publication also has a catalogue-wide effect that is easy to miss: every *other* template that is currently `PUBLISHED` and whose `effective_from` is on or before the newly published template's `effective_from` is moved `PUBLISHED → RETIRED`, and each of those retired rows has its own `version` incremented by 1 as well. Inspections already opened against a retired template keep referencing it and are unaffected, but no *new* inspection may name it; naming a template that is `DRAFT` or `RETIRED` when opening an inspection is rejected with `ERR_TEMPLATE_NOT_PUBLISHED`. `RETIRED` is terminal for a template.

Both the single-template read and the point listing are open to a `ProgramAdministrator`, a `ProgramAuditor`, a `DealerPrincipal`, and an `InspectionTechnician`; the point listing accepts `category` and `severity` filters alongside the usual `limit` and `offset`.

### 3.2. Programme Participants: Dealers, Technicians, and Vehicle Records

A `ProgramAdministrator` enrols a franchised dealer by supplying its legal `name`, its automaker `franchise_code`, and the `region_code` it reports into. The `franchise_code` must be non-empty and unique across all dealers — a duplicate is `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE` — and it later appears inside every certification label the dealer mints. A newly enrolled dealer begins with `privilege_status` of `ACTIVE`, `privilege_reason` null, `claim_ratio_variant` of `LIFETIME_CUMULATIVE`, and every counter at zero.

A dealer's row is largely a set of derived counters that the programme uses to watch its behaviour, and every one of them is maintained by the system rather than supplied. `lifetime_submitted_count` counts every certification the dealer has ever submitted in any status; it increments on each submission, is never decremented, and doubles as the sequence number inside the certification label. `lifetime_activated_count` counts the dealer's certifications that have ever been activated — those whose activation timestamp is set, which is to say those now `ACTIVE`, `UNDER_AUDIT`, or `DECERTIFIED` — and is likewise never decremented. `window_activated_count` counts those activated inside the trailing claim window, meaning those whose activation instant falls within the inclusive span from the claim-window length before the logical clock up to the logical clock itself. `active_certification_count` counts the dealer's certifications currently `SUBMITTED`, `ACTIVE`, or `UNDER_AUDIT`. `approved_claim_count_lifetime` counts every approved warranty claim ever made under any of the dealer's certifications, and `approved_claim_count_window` counts those whose repair date falls within the same inclusive trailing span, measured in whole dates. `claim_ratio_variant` records which claim-ratio algorithm currently applies and `claim_ratio_effective` the ratio that algorithm produced. `audit_sample_count` counts every audit sample ever drawn against the dealer's certifications in any status, `audit_fail_count` counts those that failed, and `audit_fail_rate` is the failure count divided by the sample count with 1 substituted for a zero sample count, rounded half up to 2 decimals. `risk_score` is the ranking key audit sampling uses. Three fields form the dealer's fee ledger: `fee_billed_cents_total` sums the fee charged over every certification of the dealer that has been activated at least once, `fee_credited_cents_total` sums the fee credited over every certification of the dealer, and `fee_balance_cents` is the billed total minus the credited total. That balance is the conserved money quantity of the programme: no operation may change it except by billing a fee or crediting one, and the two totals must always reconcile to it.

Under a dealer, a `ProgramAdministrator` or the `DealerPrincipal` itself registers **technicians**. Each carries a `name`, a `credential_id` that is the automaker's inspector credential number and must be unique within the employing dealer (a duplicate is `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`), a `credential_expires_at` instant at which the credential lapses, and an optional `credential_status` of `ACTIVE` or `REVOKED` that defaults to `ACTIVE`. A technician's `dealer_id` is taken from the path segment naming the employer, not from the body. Its `completed_inspection_count` starts at 0 and counts the technician's inspections that have reached `PASSED` or `FAILED`. The technician listing under a dealer accepts a `credential_status` filter with `limit` and `offset`, and is the discovery channel for a dealer's roster.

Vehicles are registered by a `ProgramAdministrator` or a `DealerPrincipal` with a `vin` of exactly 17 characters and unique across all vehicles (a duplicate is `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`), a `model_year` as printed on the compliance label, an `in_service_date` given as `YYYY-MM-DD` recording when the car first entered service, an `odometer_miles` reading of at least 0 that is thereafter monotonically non-decreasing, a `title_brand`, a documented `prior_use`, and an `owner_count` giving the number of retail owners on the title history, defaulting to 1. The `title_brand` enumeration is `CLEAN`, `SALVAGE`, `FLOOD`, `LEMON`, `REBUILT`, and `ODOMETER_DISCREPANCY`, and only `CLEAN` — the default — may be supplied at registration; every other brand arrives later through a title-branding history event. The `prior_use` enumeration is `PERSONAL`, `LEASE`, `FLEET`, `RENTAL`, `TAXI`, `DRIVING_SCHOOL`, and `POLICE`. A new vehicle starts with `cpo_state` `NOT_CERTIFIED`, `inventory_state` `IN_INVENTORY`, `active_certification_count` 0, `certified_certification_id` null, `decertified_at` null, and both `last_history_event_type` and `last_history_event_at` null.

Two derived pointers on the vehicle deserve explicit statement because they are recomputed on every certification status change. `active_certification_count` counts the vehicle's certifications currently `SUBMITTED`, `ACTIVE`, or `UNDER_AUDIT`; it is the conserved *slot* quantity of the programme, and the invariant is that it never exceeds 1. `certified_certification_id` names the vehicle's certification that is `ACTIVE` or `UNDER_AUDIT`, or is null when there is none; it is not a foreign key but a derived pointer. The single-vehicle read exposes both axes, both derived pointers, and the `version` a concurrency-guarded certification submission must echo.

**History events** are how a vehicle's factual record and its commercial standing change. A `ProgramAdministrator` or `DealerPrincipal` records one by supplying an `event_type` of `ODOMETER_UPDATE`, `TITLE_BRANDED`, `RETAIL_SOLD`, `WHOLESALED`, or `RETURNED_TO_INVENTORY`, optionally with a new `odometer_miles` reading and optionally with a new `title_brand`. Every event sets `last_history_event_type` to the event just applied and `last_history_event_at` to the logical clock, and increments the vehicle's `version` by 1. An `ODOMETER_UPDATE` raises `odometer_miles`; where a supplied reading is present on any event it must be at least the vehicle's current `odometer_miles`, because the odometer never runs backwards. A `TITLE_BRANDED` event sets `title_brand` to the supplied brand and decertifies any live certification of the vehicle with a decertification reason of `TITLE_BRANDED`. An `event_type` that is not a declared transition out of the vehicle's current `inventory_state` — a retail sale on a car already `RETAIL_SOLD`, or a return to inventory on a car still `IN_INVENTORY` — is rejected with `ERR_VEHICLE_HISTORY_EVENT_INVALID`.

The two disposition events are the source of the programme's longest cascade, and every hop must land. Recording `RETAIL_SOLD` on a vehicle that is `IN_INVENTORY` moves `inventory_state` `IN_INVENTORY → RETAIL_SOLD`; as hop 2, any certification of that vehicle in `ACTIVE` or `UNDER_AUDIT` moves to `DECERTIFIED` with a decertification reason of `RETAIL_SOLD` and its `fee_credited_cents` left at 0, because the programme keeps the fee on a retail sale. Recording `WHOLESALED` on a vehicle that is `IN_INVENTORY` moves `inventory_state` `IN_INVENTORY → WHOLESALED`; hop 2 decertifies any `ACTIVE` or `UNDER_AUDIT` certification of that vehicle with a decertification reason of `WHOLESALED` and `fee_credited_cents` again 0; hop 3, caused by that decertification, moves every listing of that certification in `DRAFT` or `SYNDICATED` to `WITHDRAWN` with a withdrawal reason of `CERTIFICATION_DECERTIFIED` and a takedown deadline set, moves every complaint of that certification in `OPEN` or `REINSPECTION_ORDERED` to `DISMISSED` with its resolution instant set to the logical clock, and moves the vehicle's `cpo_state` `CERTIFIED → DECERTIFIED`; hop 4, caused by hop 3's complaint dismissal, moves every inspection linked to a dismissed complaint that is still `DRAFT`, `IN_PROGRESS`, or `RECONDITIONING_REQUIRED` to `VOIDED` with a `failure_reason` of `COMPLAINT_DISMISSED`. The retail-sale path drives the same hop 3 and hop 4 chain; the only difference between the two dispositions is the recorded decertification reason.

A `RETURNED_TO_INVENTORY` event is accepted from either `RETAIL_SOLD` or `WHOLESALED` and moves `inventory_state` back to `IN_INVENTORY`. It deliberately leaves `cpo_state` alone: a returned car stays `DECERTIFIED` on the programme axis until a fresh inspection is opened on it. That is the loop that makes `DECERTIFIED → CERTIFICATION_PENDING → CERTIFIED` a legal path, and the vehicle's `decertified_at` is left in place as the historical record of the earlier withdrawal rather than being cleared.

### 3.3. Eligibility Screening and Opening a Multipoint Inspection

Only a `DealerPrincipal` may open an inspection, and it does so by supplying the `vehicle_id` of the car, the `technician_id` of the assigned inspector, the `template_id` of the published checklist to be used, the `started_at` instant at which the technician began work, and the `odometer_at_inspection` reading transcribed from the worksheet. Two further fields are optional and are discussed in §3.10 because they belong to the re-inspection path: `parent_inspection_id`, naming a prior inspection this one supersedes, and `complaint_id`, naming the complaint that ordered this re-inspection. Both default to null. The inspection's `dealer_id` is not supplied but resolved from the acting principal's `X-User-ID`; a body naming a technician, or targeting a resource, belonging to another dealership is rejected with `ERR_DEALER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`, and any body foreign key that does not resolve at all yields `ERR_REFERENCED_ENTITY_NOT_FOUND`. The inspection's `purpose` is derived, not supplied: it is `RE_INSPECTION` when either `parent_inspection_id` or `complaint_id` is non-null, and `INITIAL` otherwise.

Opening an inspection is the programme's **eligibility gate**, and the screens are evaluated in the order §2.10 fixes. The vehicle's `inventory_state` must be `IN_INVENTORY`. Its `title_brand` must not appear in the programme's excluded brand list, which by default holds `SALVAGE`, `FLOOD`, `LEMON`, `REBUILT`, and `ODOMETER_DISCREPANCY` — a branded car is permanently outside the programme, and the rejection is `ERR_BRANDED_TITLE_INELIGIBLE`. Its `prior_use` must not appear in the programme's excluded prior-use list, which by default holds `RENTAL`, `TAXI`, `DRIVING_SCHOOL`, and `POLICE`; a car that spent its life as a rental or a taxi is rejected with `ERR_PRIOR_USE_INELIGIBLE`. The car must not be too old: the whole number of days between its `in_service_date` and the calendar date of `started_at` must be at most the programme's age ceiling, which defaults to six years counted as 2190 days, and the bound is inclusive — a car exactly at 2190 days is still eligible, one at 2191 days is rejected with `ERR_VEHICLE_AGE_EXCEEDED`. The worksheet reading must not be too high and must not be too low: `odometer_at_inspection` must be at most the programme's odometer ceiling, which defaults to 80000 miles inclusive, and at least the vehicle's own recorded `odometer_miles`; either violation is `ERR_MILEAGE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. The inspecting dealer's `privilege_status` must not be `SUSPENDED`, otherwise `ERR_DEALER_PRIVILEGE_SUSPENDED`.

Three further gates guard the technician and the calendar. A vehicle may have only one live inspection at a time: if another inspection of the same vehicle sits in `DRAFT`, `IN_PROGRESS`, or `RECONDITIONING_REQUIRED`, the request is rejected with `ERR_INSPECTION_ALREADY_OPEN` — *unless* the request names exactly that inspection as its `parent_inspection_id`, which is the sanctioned way to replace an in-flight inspection. The `started_at` instant must not be after the logical clock; a future-dated worksheet is `ERR_INSPECTION_DATE_IN_FUTURE`. And the named technician must be currently credentialed: `credential_status` must be `ACTIVE` and `credential_expires_at` must be strictly after the logical clock, so a credential lapsing at exactly the current instant is already lapsed. A revoked or lapsed credential yields `ERR_TECHNICIAN_CREDENTIAL_EXPIRED`.

The last gate is the technician's calendar, and its arithmetic is exact. An inspection occupies its technician from `started_at` for the programme's slot length, which defaults to 120 minutes, giving the inspection a derived `slot_end_at` equal to `started_at` plus that slot length. Before comparison, a buffer — 30 minutes by default — is applied to **both** ends of every slot, so the buffered interval of an inspection runs from 30 minutes before its `started_at` to 30 minutes after its `slot_end_at`, and both endpoints are included in the interval. The candidate is compared against every non-voided inspection of the same technician, regardless of which dealer it belongs to, how it turned out, or how long ago it happened; voided inspections release their slot entirely. A conflict exists when the candidate's buffered low bound is at or before an occupied buffered high bound and that occupied buffered low bound is at or before the candidate's buffered high bound, and — unlike coverage windows — **touching counts as conflicting**. With the default 120-minute slot and 30-minute buffer, an inspection begun at 09:00 buffers to the closed interval from 08:30 to 11:30; a second inspection by the same technician begun at 12:00 buffers to the closed interval from 11:30 to 14:30, touches the first at 11:30, and is rejected with `ERR_TECHNICIAN_SLOT_CONFLICT`, while one begun at 12:01 buffers from 11:31 and is accepted.

A successful open creates the inspection in `DRAFT` with `attempt_count` 0, `recorded_item_count` 0, `failed_critical_count` 0, `failed_recon_count` 0, `total_recon_cost_cents` 0, `total_reimbursable_cents` 0, `submitted_at` null, and `failure_reason` null. Two derived snapshots are frozen at this moment and never recomputed: `slot_end_at`, described above, and `vehicle_age_days_at_inspection`, the whole number of days between the vehicle's `in_service_date` and the calendar date of `started_at`. As a side effect the vehicle moves onto the programme axis: `cpo_state` goes `NOT_CERTIFIED → CERTIFICATION_PENDING`, or `DECERTIFIED → CERTIFICATION_PENDING` for a car that has been returned to inventory after an earlier withdrawal, and the vehicle's `version` increments by 1.

The inspection listing is the reverse-discovery channel for every edge an inspection carries and accepts filters on `vehicle_id`, `dealer_id`, `technician_id`, `template_id`, `parent_inspection_id`, `complaint_id`, `status`, and `purpose`, with `limit` and `offset`. It is readable by a `ProgramAdministrator`, a `ProgramAuditor`, a `DealerPrincipal`, and an `InspectionTechnician`, as is the single-inspection read that exposes the derived counters.

### 3.4. Executing the Checklist: Attempts, Outcomes, and Measurements

Recording checklist outcomes is reserved to the inspection's **own** technician, acting as an `InspectionTechnician` whose `X-User-ID` equals the inspection's `technician_id`; any other technician is turned away with `ERR_DEALER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. The technician's credential is re-checked at every recording, not only when the inspection was opened: `credential_status` must still be `ACTIVE` and `credential_expires_at` must still be strictly after the logical clock, otherwise `ERR_TECHNICIAN_CREDENTIAL_EXPIRED`.

The payload is a single field, `results`, holding an array of rows. Each row names an `item_code`, an `outcome` of `PASS`, `FAIL`, or `NOT_APPLICABLE`, and optionally a `measured_value`. An empty `results` array is a schema violation and yields `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Every supplied `item_code` must resolve to an inspection point of the inspection's own template — that is, the point whose template is the inspection's `template_id` and whose `item_code` matches the supplied value — and a code that resolves to nothing is rejected with `ERR_UNKNOWN_CHECKLIST_ITEM`. Where the resolved point declares a `min_measure` or a `max_measure`, the row must carry a `measured_value`, and the value must agree with the recorded outcome: a `PASS` requires a reading inside the inclusive band bounded below by `min_measure` and above by `max_measure`, and a `FAIL` requires a reading outside that band. A `PASS` with a reading outside the band, a `FAIL` with a reading inside it, or a measured point recorded with no reading at all is rejected with `ERR_MEASUREMENT_CONTRADICTS_OUTCOME`. On an unmeasured point, `measured_value` stays null.

Recording is **append-only and attempt-scoped**, which is the single most important thing to understand about this workflow. A recording call never edits the rows a previous call wrote. Instead it increments the inspection's `attempt_count` by exactly 1 and then writes one result row per supplied entry, each stamped with an `attempt_index` equal to the *new* `attempt_count`, a `recorded_by_technician_id` set to the acting `X-User-ID`, and a `recorded_at` set to the logical clock. Because only the inspection's own technician may record, that stamped technician identifier always equals the inspection's `technician_id`; the authoritative technician edge stays on the inspection itself, and a technician's recorded work is discovered by filtering the inspection listing on `technician_id` and then reading each inspection's results. Earlier attempts remain visible forever.

After each recording the inspection's `status` becomes `IN_PROGRESS` and its `version` increments by 1. Three derived counters are recomputed **over the new attempt only**, ignoring every earlier attempt: `recorded_item_count`, the number of distinct `item_code` values recorded under the current `attempt_count`; `failed_critical_count`, the number of results in the current attempt whose `outcome` is `FAIL` on a point whose resolved `severity` is `CRITICAL`; and `failed_recon_count`, the number of results in the current attempt whose `outcome` is `FAIL` on a point whose resolved `reconditioning_allowed` is true. This attempt-scoped recomputation is what allows a re-recording after repairs to erase the previous attempt's failures from the counters without deleting its evidence.

Results may be recorded from three starting states, and all three converge on `IN_PROGRESS`. A first recording moves the inspection `DRAFT → IN_PROGRESS`. A repeat recording moves it `IN_PROGRESS → IN_PROGRESS`, opening a fresh attempt. And a recording after repairs moves it `RECONDITIONING_REQUIRED → IN_PROGRESS`, which closes the inspection's cycle — that loop may repeat any number of times, and each pass bumps `attempt_count` so results of different attempts never collide. On this third path the state of the outstanding repair rows is deliberately **not** checked; repairs are checked at closing time, not at recording time, and the repair rows generated by the previous close survive the new recording untouched. Recording results on an inspection that has already reached `PASSED`, `FAILED`, or `VOIDED` is rejected with `ERR_INSPECTION_TERMINAL`.

The result listing for an inspection returns every recorded outcome across all attempts and accepts filters on `attempt_index`, `outcome`, and `item_code`, with `limit` and `offset`. It is readable by a `ProgramAdministrator`, a `ProgramAuditor`, a `DealerPrincipal`, and an `InspectionTechnician`.

### 3.5. Reconditioning and Closing the Inspection

Closing the current attempt is a `DealerPrincipal` act, and it takes **no request body at all** — the outcome is derived from the recorded evidence rather than asserted by the caller. One precondition is shared by every branch: the current attempt must cover the whole checklist, meaning the inspection's `recorded_item_count` must equal its template's `point_count`. A partial attempt is rejected with `ERR_INCOMPLETE_CHECKLIST`. Closing an inspection that is already `PASSED`, `FAILED`, or `VOIDED` is `ERR_INSPECTION_TERMINAL`.

Given a complete attempt, exactly one of three outcomes follows, and the branches are evaluated in this order of severity. If any result in the current attempt is a `FAIL` on a point whose `severity` is `CRITICAL` **and** whose `reconditioning_allowed` is false, the inspection moves `IN_PROGRESS → FAILED`: the `failure_reason` is set to `CRITICAL_ITEM_FAIL`, `submitted_at` is set to the logical clock, and the inspection's `version` increments by 1. A car that fails an uncurable critical point is out: the vehicle moves `CERTIFICATION_PENDING → NOT_CERTIFIED` on the programme axis. Otherwise, if no such uncurable critical failure exists but `failed_recon_count` is at least 1, the inspection moves `IN_PROGRESS → RECONDITIONING_REQUIRED` and the required repairs are generated. Otherwise — no failure of any kind in the current attempt — the inspection moves `IN_PROGRESS → PASSED`, provided no repair remains outstanding.

Generating repairs is the interesting half of the middle branch. For every `FAIL` of the current attempt on a point whose `reconditioning_allowed` is true, the system creates a **reconditioning item** in status `REQUIRED` with its `inspection_id` set to the inspection, its `item_code` set to the failed point's code, and its `severity` copied from the resolved point at generation time. Generation is **idempotent on `item_code` across attempts**: a point that already has a reconditioning item for this inspection does not receive a second one, so a car that fails the same brake point on two successive attempts carries one repair row, not two. A new reconditioning item begins with `actual_cost_cents` null, `reimbursable_cents` 0, `waiver_reason` null, `completed_at` null, and `closed_by_user_id` null. These rows have no creation operation of their own; the repair listing under an inspection, filterable by `status` and `severity` with `limit` and `offset`, is the only channel through which their identifiers are discovered.

A repair is closed with a `resolution`, and the two resolutions are two different acts by two different roles. A `resolution` of `COMPLETED` is performed by an `InspectionTechnician` and requires an `actual_cost_cents` amount; a `resolution` of `WAIVED` is performed by a `ProgramAdministrator` and requires a `waiver_reason`. Either way, the item must still be `REQUIRED` — closing an item already `COMPLETED` or `WAIVED` is `ERR_RECONDITIONING_ITEM_CLOSED` — and its inspection must be in `RECONDITIONING_REQUIRED` or `IN_PROGRESS`, so repairs on a terminal inspection are unreachable. Both resolutions set `completed_at` to the logical clock and `closed_by_user_id` to the acting `X-User-ID`, and both increment the reconditioning item's `version` and its inspection's `version` by 1 each. `COMPLETED` and `WAIVED` are both terminal for a repair.

The completion path carries the programme's sharpest segregation-of-duties rule. The acting `X-User-ID` must resolve to a technician employed by the same dealer as the inspection, and it must **not** be the inspection's own `technician_id`: the technician who recorded the failure may not sign off the repair that cures it. A self-signoff is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The supplied `actual_cost_cents` must also be present and at most the resolved point's `max_reconditioning_cost_cents`, otherwise `ERR_RECONDITIONING_COST_EXCEEDS_CAP`. On success, the item's `reimbursable_cents` is set to the programme's share of the repair cost, capped by the point's own ceiling: take `actual_cost_cents`, multiply by the programme's reimbursement share (60 percent by default), round that product half up to whole cents, and then take the smaller of that rounded figure and the point's `max_reconditioning_cost_cents`.

The waiver path is the automaker excusing a repair it does not require the dealer to perform. It demands a non-empty `waiver_reason`, and it is categorically forbidden on a `CRITICAL` repair — a critical repair must be performed and costed, never excused, and an attempt to waive one is rejected with `ERR_CRITICAL_RECONDITIONING_NOT_WAIVABLE`. A waived item keeps `actual_cost_cents` null and `reimbursable_cents` 0.

Closing either way recomputes the inspection's two money aggregates. `total_recon_cost_cents` is the plain sum of `actual_cost_cents` over the inspection's repairs whose status is `COMPLETED`. `total_reimbursable_cents` is the programme's aggregate obligation, and its **rounding order is pinned**: round each completed repair's share to whole cents *first*, then add the rounded per-item amounts together, iterating the completed repairs in `created_at` ascending order. Summing the raw costs and rounding once at the end is wrong and produces a different total — three repairs at 1001 cents each give 601 plus 601 plus 601, which is 1803 cents, not the 1802 cents a single rounding of the combined 3003 cents would yield. The summed figure is then capped by the programme's per-inspection reimbursement ceiling of 150000 cents by default, and the smaller of the two is stored. Waived repairs contribute nothing and never enter the sum; outstanding repairs contribute nothing either.

A clean attempt still cannot pass while a repair is outstanding. If any reconditioning item of the inspection remains `REQUIRED`, closing is rejected with `ERR_RECONDITIONING_INCOMPLETE`. When the attempt is clean and every repair is closed, the inspection reaches `PASSED`: `submitted_at` is set to the logical clock, `total_recon_cost_cents` and `total_reimbursable_cents` are recomputed one final time, the assigned technician's `completed_inspection_count` increments by 1, and the inspection's `version` increments by 1.

Two more paths lead an inspection out of play without a verdict, and both land on `VOIDED`. When a new inspection is created naming an existing inspection as its `parent_inspection_id`, that parent — from `DRAFT`, `IN_PROGRESS`, or `RECONDITIONING_REQUIRED` — moves to `VOIDED` with a `failure_reason` of `SUPERSEDED_BY_REINSPECTION`, and its `version` increments by 1. When the complaint an inspection was opened for is dismissed by a decertification cascade, that inspection — again from any of `DRAFT`, `IN_PROGRESS`, or `RECONDITIONING_REQUIRED` — moves to `VOIDED` with a `failure_reason` of `COMPLAINT_DISMISSED`, and its `version` increments by 1. Voiding from `RECONDITIONING_REQUIRED` leaves any outstanding repair rows sitting in `REQUIRED`, where they become permanently unreachable; that is intentional, because the evidence of what was found must survive even though the work will never be signed off. A voided inspection also releases its technician's calendar slot, which is why the slot conflict test in §3.3 ignores voided rows.

### 3.6. Certification Submission and Documentation Retention

A `DealerPrincipal` submits a certification once an inspection has passed, supplying the `vehicle_id` of the car, the `inspection_id` of the passed inspection that backs the claim, the `expected_vehicle_version` concurrency guard described in §2.8, and a `documents` array carrying the retained evidence. The certification's `dealer_id` is resolved from the acting `X-User-ID`, never supplied. The named inspection must be `PASSED`; naming one in any other status is rejected with `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_PASSED`. The eligibility screens of §3.3 are re-run here against the vehicle's *current* facts, so a car whose title was branded between inspection and submission is rejected with `ERR_BRANDED_TITLE_INELIGIBLE` and a car whose recorded prior use excludes it with `ERR_PRIOR_USE_INELIGIBLE`. The submitting dealer must not be `SUSPENDED`, otherwise `ERR_DEALER_PRIVILEGE_SUSPENDED`.

The vehicle's certification slot is the scarcest thing in the system. A submission is refused with `ERR_VEHICLE_ALREADY_CERTIFIED` when the vehicle already holds a certification that is `ACTIVE` or `UNDER_AUDIT` — those block outright. A vehicle holding a single still-`SUBMITTED` certification is a different matter: the new submission legitimately **supersedes** it, provided the concurrency guard passes. The earlier row moves `SUBMITTED → SUPERSEDED` and its `version` increments by 1, and the certification slot it occupied is handed to the new row within the same request, so the vehicle's `active_certification_count` remains 1 throughout rather than dipping to 0 or rising to 2. `SUPERSEDED` is terminal, no fee was ever billed on that row, and its `activated_at` stays null.

Documentation is checked at submission, not at approval. Each entry of the `documents` array names a `doc_type` and a `reference`, the dealer's own document-management pointer. The `doc_type` enumeration is `TITLE_HISTORY_REPORT`, `INSPECTION_WORKSHEET`, `RECONDITIONING_INVOICE`, and `CUSTOMER_DISCLOSURE`, and the programme's required-document list holds all four by default. Three of them are unconditionally required. The reconditioning invoice is **conditionally** required, and the condition cuts both ways: it is mandatory exactly when the backing inspection produced at least one reconditioning item, and it is *forbidden* when the inspection produced none. Omitting any required document type — including the invoice on an inspection that generated repairs — is rejected with `ERR_DOCUMENTATION_INCOMPLETE`. Supplying the invoice for an inspection that generated no repairs, or supplying the same `doc_type` twice, is rejected with `ERR_DOCUMENT_NOT_APPLICABLE`. An empty `documents` array is a schema violation and yields `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

A successful submission creates the certification in `SUBMITTED` with `submitted_at` set to the logical clock and a deterministic `certification_number`. That label is built as the literal prefix `CPO-`, then the certifying dealer's `franchise_code`, then a hyphen, then the dealer's `lifetime_submitted_count` *after* this submission has incremented it, zero-padded to four digits — so the first certification a dealer with franchise code `F123` ever submits is labelled `CPO-F123-0001`. The submission also creates one **certification document** row per `documents` entry, each with its `certification_id` set to the new certification, its `uploaded_at` set to the logical clock, and its `retained_until` still null; those rows are discoverable only through the documents listing under the certification, which accepts a `doc_type` filter with `limit` and `offset` and is readable by a `ProgramAdministrator`, a `ProgramAuditor`, or the owning `DealerPrincipal`. Finally, the submission recomputes the vehicle's `active_certification_count` and the dealer's `lifetime_submitted_count` and `active_certification_count`, increments the vehicle's `version` by 1 — which is the observable advance the concurrency guard defends, and the reason a dealer must re-read the vehicle before submitting again — and increments the dealer's `version` by 1.

A newly submitted certification carries no financial or coverage content whatsoever. Its `program_config_version`, `warranty_anchor_variant`, `warranty_start_date`, `warranty_end_date`, `warranty_end_miles`, and `retention_expires_at` are all null; its `fee_surcharge_cents`, `fee_discount_cents`, `fee_charged_cents`, and `fee_credited_cents` are all 0; its `activated_at`, `decertified_at`, `rejection_reason`, `decertification_reason`, and `listing_syndicated_at` are all null; `audit_sampled` is false; and `audit_sample_count`, `document_count`, `approved_claim_count`, and `approved_claim_amount_cents` are all 0 or derived from an empty set. Of those derived counters, `document_count` counts the certification's document rows, `audit_sample_count` counts its audit samples, `approved_claim_count` counts its approved warranty claims, and `approved_claim_amount_cents` sums the approved amount over those claims.

The certification listing is readable by a `ProgramAdministrator`, a `ProgramAuditor`, a `DealerPrincipal`, and a `CustomerAdvocate`, and accepts filters on `vehicle_id`, `inspection_id`, `dealer_id`, `status`, and `audit_sampled`, with `limit` and `offset`. The single-certification read exposes the pinned configuration generation, the fee breakdown, the warranty anchor variant, the coverage window, and the `version` a concurrency-guarded syndication must echo.

### 3.7. Adjudication: Pricing the Fee and Fixing the Coverage Terms

Adjudication is reserved to a `ProgramAdministrator`, who supplies a `decision` of either `APPROVE` or `REJECT` and, on a rejection, an optional `rejection_reason`. The certification must be `SUBMITTED`; adjudicating one in any other status is rejected with `ERR_CERTIFICATION_NOT_SUBMITTED`.

Rejection is the simpler branch and must be specified with equal care. The certification moves `SUBMITTED → REJECTED` with its `rejection_reason` set to the supplied value or defaulting to `ADMIN_DECISION`. No fee is billed, `activated_at` stays null, and no coverage is computed. The vehicle's `active_certification_count` is recomputed and falls to 0, its `certified_certification_id` stays null, and its `cpo_state` moves `CERTIFICATION_PENDING → NOT_CERTIFIED`. The dealer's `active_certification_count` is recomputed. The certification's `version`, the vehicle's `version`, and the dealer's `version` each increment by 1. `REJECTED` is terminal. The same transition also occurs without an administrator asking for it — as the cascade of a dealer suspension, in which case the recorded `rejection_reason` is `DEALER_SUSPENDED` rather than `ADMIN_DECISION`. The `rejection_reason` enumeration is therefore null, `DOCUMENTATION_INCOMPLETE`, `DEALER_SUSPENDED`, or `ADMIN_DECISION`.

Approval is where the programme commits money and coverage, and it has its own gates. The certifying dealer's `privilege_status` must be `ACTIVE` or `PROBATION` — approval is the one operation that tolerates probation, and a `SUSPENDED` dealer is refused with `ERR_DEALER_PRIVILEGE_SUSPENDED`. The vehicle's `title_brand` and `prior_use` are re-evaluated one last time against the excluded lists as they stand at approval time, yielding `ERR_BRANDED_TITLE_INELIGIBLE` or `ERR_PRIOR_USE_INELIGIBLE`. The vehicle's `inventory_state` must be `IN_INVENTORY`: approving a car already `RETAIL_SOLD` or `WHOLESALED` is rejected with `ERR_VEHICLE_NOT_IN_INVENTORY`, and this is the single point at which the commercial axis blocks the programme axis.

Pricing the fee reads exactly three things: the vehicle as it stands at approval, the passed inspection named by `inspection_id`, and the certifying dealer's `lifetime_activated_count` as it stands immediately *before* this approval increments it. All money figures are whole cents and every division floors, with no intermediate rounding to dollars. The vehicle's age in whole days is the number of days between its `in_service_date` and the calendar date of `activated_at`; whole age years are that day count divided by 365 and rounded down. Chargeable age years are the whole age years minus the programme's free-year allowance — three years by default — with a negative result treated as zero. The age component of the surcharge is those chargeable years multiplied by the per-year age surcharge, which is 12500 cents (125 dollars) per year by default. Excess mileage is the inspection's `odometer_at_inspection` minus the programme's free-mileage allowance of 40000 miles, again with a negative result treated as zero, and the number of chargeable mileage blocks is that excess divided by 10000 and rounded down — so a car 4000 miles into a block contributes nothing for that partial block. The mileage component is those whole blocks multiplied by the per-block mileage surcharge, 9000 cents by default. The raw surcharge is the age component plus the mileage component.

**The evaluation order of the remaining steps is pinned and must not be rearranged.** The surcharge cap is the programme's base fee multiplied by the surcharge cap fraction — 35 percent by default — rounded down to whole cents, and `fee_surcharge_cents` is the smaller of the raw surcharge and that cap. Only then is the subtotal formed, as the base fee (89900 cents by default) plus the *capped* `fee_surcharge_cents`. The applicable volume discount tier is the entry of the programme's tier list with the greatest `min_count` that is at or below the dealer's pre-approval `lifetime_activated_count`; the default tiers are a `min_count` of 0 at a `pct` of 0.00, a `min_count` of 10 at a `pct` of 0.05, and a `min_count` of 50 at a `pct` of 0.10, and ties are impossible because `min_count` is unique per tier. `fee_discount_cents` is the subtotal multiplied by the selected tier's `pct`, rounded down to whole cents. `fee_charged_cents` is the subtotal minus `fee_discount_cents`. The discount is therefore always computed on the *capped* subtotal and never on the uncapped surcharge sum.

Fixing the coverage terms is the other half of approval, and it selects between two anchor rules. Read from the pinned configuration generation, the factory basic warranty runs for a number of calendar months from the vehicle's `in_service_date` — 36 months by default — and up to a mileage limit of 36000 miles by default. The factory expiry date is the vehicle's `in_service_date` advanced by that many calendar months, where calendar-month addition clamps to the last day of the target month when the source day does not exist there. The factory basic warranty counts as still active when the activation date falls strictly before the factory expiry date **and** the inspection's `odometer_at_inspection` is strictly below the factory mileage limit. When it is still active, `warranty_start_date` is the factory expiry date and `warranty_anchor_variant` is `IN_SERVICE_ANCHORED`, so the CPO extension is contiguous with factory coverage and begins in the future. When the factory warranty has lapsed on either axis, `warranty_start_date` is the activation date and `warranty_anchor_variant` is `CERTIFICATION_ANCHORED`, so coverage begins immediately. For a car sitting exactly at the factory boundary the two variants differ by the remaining factory term, and the in-service-anchored answer always ends strictly later than the certification-anchored answer would for the same car.

From that start date the extension is measured out. A raw end date is `warranty_start_date` advanced by the extension length in calendar months — 12 months by default. An absolute cap date is the vehicle's `in_service_date` advanced by the total coverage cap in calendar months, 84 months (seven years) by default. `warranty_end_date` is the earlier of those two dates. On the mileage axis, a raw end mileage is the inspection's `odometer_at_inspection` plus the extension mileage of 12000 miles by default, and `warranty_end_miles` is the smaller of that figure and the absolute odometer cap of 100000 miles. `retention_expires_at` is `warranty_end_date` advanced by the documentation retention period in calendar years — seven years by default — and it is the date the retained documentation may be destroyed. Both `warranty_start_date` and `warranty_end_date` are dates in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, and the coverage interval they bound is half-open as §2.12 describes: `warranty_start_date` is the first covered date and `warranty_end_date` is the first *uncovered* date. If the programme cap collapses the interval — `warranty_end_date` at or before `warranty_start_date`, or `warranty_end_miles` at or below the inspection's `odometer_at_inspection` — the approval is rejected with `ERR_NO_COVERAGE_REMAINING`.

Immediately after the candidate interval is computed, it is tested for **overlap against coverage already retained on the same vehicle**. The retained set is every *other* certification of that vehicle that is `ACTIVE` or `UNDER_AUDIT`, plus every certification of that vehicle that is `DECERTIFIED` with a decertification reason of `RETAIL_SOLD`. That last clause is deliberate: a retail sale ends the dealer's certification but the coverage the customer bought survives on the car, so its interval is still occupied. Certifications that are `REJECTED` or `SUPERSEDED`, and those `DECERTIFIED` for any other reason, release their interval entirely. Every interval on both sides is half-open, and a conflict exists when the candidate's start falls strictly before a retained interval's end and that retained interval's start falls strictly before the candidate's end; adjacency at a shared endpoint is explicitly not a conflict. A conflict rejects the approval with `ERR_COVERAGE_WINDOW_OVERLAP`.

When every gate has passed, approval commits in one atomic sweep. The certification pins `program_config_version` to the configuration `version` in force; writes `fee_surcharge_cents`, `fee_discount_cents`, and `fee_charged_cents`; writes `warranty_anchor_variant`, `warranty_start_date`, `warranty_end_date`, `warranty_end_miles`, and `retention_expires_at`; stamps `retained_until` equal to `retention_expires_at` onto **every** certification document row of this certification; moves `SUBMITTED → ACTIVE`; and sets `activated_at` to the logical clock. That timestamp records when the certification *first* became active and is never cleared afterwards, even by decertification. On the vehicle, `cpo_state` moves `CERTIFICATION_PENDING → CERTIFIED` and `certified_certification_id` is set to the approved certification. On the dealer, `lifetime_activated_count`, `window_activated_count`, `fee_billed_cents_total`, `fee_balance_cents`, `claim_ratio_variant`, `claim_ratio_effective`, and `risk_score` are all recomputed. The certification's `version`, the vehicle's `version`, and the dealer's `version` each increment by 1.

### 3.8. Marketing Standards, Syndication, and Forced Withdrawal

A `DealerPrincipal` advertises a certified car by creating a **listing** against the certification, supplying the `certification_id`, a `channel`, an advertised `price_cents` of at least 1, and a `disclosure_fields` array of the disclosure tokens the advertisement actually carries. The `channel` enumeration is `OEM_SITE`, `DEALER_SITE`, and `THIRD_PARTY_AGGREGATOR`. The certification must be live — creating a listing against one that is not `ACTIVE` or `UNDER_AUDIT` is rejected with `ERR_CERTIFICATION_NOT_ACTIVE` — and a certification may hold at most one listing per channel that is not `WITHDRAWN`, a second live listing on the same channel being rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE`. The `disclosure_fields` array must be a **superset** of the programme's required disclosure tokens, which by default are `CERTIFIED_BADGE`, `WARRANTY_END_DATE`, `INSPECTION_DATE`, and `CERTIFICATION_NUMBER`; extra tokens are permitted, but omitting any required one is rejected with `ERR_MARKETING_STANDARD_VIOLATION`. A new listing begins in `DRAFT` with `syndicated_at`, `syndicated_snapshot_version`, `withdrawn_at`, `withdrawal_reason`, and `removal_due_at` all null.

Syndication publishes or refreshes the advertisement on its channel, and it is guarded. The listing must not be `WITHDRAWN` — syndicating a withdrawn listing is rejected with `ERR_LISTING_WITHDRAWN` — and its certification must be `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_CERTIFICATION_NOT_ACTIVE`. The `expected_certification_version` guard of §2.8 must match the certification's current `version`, otherwise `ERR_STALE_CERTIFICATION_VERSION`. The disclosure superset rule is re-checked here as well, so a listing drafted before a retune of the required-token list cannot be published against the new standard, and again the rejection is `ERR_MARKETING_STANDARD_VIOLATION`. One additional clause applies **only** at syndication and not at drafting: a listing whose `channel` is `THIRD_PARTY_AGGREGATOR` demands a dealer whose `privilege_status` is exactly `ACTIVE`. A dealer on `PROBATION` may therefore freely draft an aggregator listing but may never publish it, while it retains full access to `OEM_SITE` and `DEALER_SITE`; the refusal on the aggregator channel is `ERR_MARKETING_STANDARD_VIOLATION`, and a `SUSPENDED` dealer is stopped earlier and more bluntly with `ERR_DEALER_PRIVILEGE_SUSPENDED` on any channel.

Successful syndication moves the listing `DRAFT → SYNDICATED`, or `SYNDICATED → SYNDICATED` on a refresh, and in both cases sets `syndicated_at` to the logical clock and records `syndicated_snapshot_version` as the certification's `version` **as observed after** the increment described next — the published content generation must name the generation the publication itself produced. In the same request the certification's `listing_syndicated_at` is set to the logical clock and the certification's `version` increments by 1, which is precisely the observable advance that makes the concurrency guard meaningful: re-syndication legitimately supersedes the previously published snapshot, so a stale writer would otherwise republish superseded fee, warranty, or audit content under a fresh timestamp. The listing's own `version` increments by 1.

Withdrawal is never a voluntary state in this system's write surface — it arrives as a cascade. When a certification is decertified for any reason, or when a dealer is suspended, every affected listing in `DRAFT` **or** `SYNDICATED` moves to `WITHDRAWN` with `withdrawn_at` set to the logical clock, a `withdrawal_reason` of `CERTIFICATION_DECERTIFIED` or `DEALER_SUSPENDED` respectively, and a `removal_due_at` equal to `withdrawn_at` plus the programme's takedown window — 48 hours (two days) by default — which is the deadline by which the dealer must pull the syndicated content from the channel. Both the draft and the syndicated path behave identically, including writing `removal_due_at`: the deadline is recorded even when nothing was ever published, so the takedown obligation is auditable either way. The `withdrawal_reason` enumeration also admits `DEALER_ACTION` alongside those two, and `WITHDRAWN` is terminal. Each withdrawn listing has its `version` incremented by 1.

The listing collection of a certification is readable by a `ProgramAdministrator`, a `ProgramAuditor`, a `DealerPrincipal`, and a `CustomerAdvocate`, and accepts `channel` and `status` filters with `limit` and `offset`.

### 3.9. Warranty Claims and Dealer Behaviour Monitoring

The programme watches how a dealer's certified cars behave after the badge goes on, and warranty claims are the raw material. A `DealerPrincipal` files a claim against a certification by supplying the `certification_id`, a `component_category`, the `repair_date` as `YYYY-MM-DD`, the `repair_odometer_miles` at repair, and the `claimed_amount_cents` it is asking for, which must be at least 1. The `component_category` enumeration is `POWERTRAIN`, `BRAKES`, `TIRES`, `SAFETY`, `ELECTRICAL`, `BODY`, and `INTERIOR` — note that it omits `ROAD_TEST`, which exists as an inspection area but not as a repairable component group. The certification must be live, otherwise `ERR_CERTIFICATION_NOT_ACTIVE`.

Two coverage checks gate the filing. The `repair_date` must fall inside the certification's half-open coverage interval — at or after `warranty_start_date` and strictly before `warranty_end_date` — and `repair_odometer_miles` must be at most `warranty_end_miles`; a repair outside either bound is rejected with `ERR_OUTSIDE_WARRANTY_WINDOW`. And `claimed_amount_cents` must not exceed the programme's per-claim ceiling, 250000 cents (2500 dollars) by default, otherwise `ERR_CLAIM_AMOUNT_EXCEEDS_CAP`. That same constant *also* caps the certification's lifetime approved total, but that second use is a trim applied at adjudication rather than a reason to reject the filing. A new claim is created in `SUBMITTED` with `approved_amount_cents` 0, `dealer_ratio_snapshot_before` null, `denial_reason` null, and `adjudicated_at` null.

Adjudication is a `ProgramAdministrator` act taking a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `DENY`. The claim must still be `SUBMITTED`; adjudicating one already `APPROVED` or `DENIED` is rejected with `ERR_CLAIM_ALREADY_ADJUDICATED`. Denial requires a `denial_reason`, drawn from `NOT_COVERED_COMPONENT`, `PRE_EXISTING_DAMAGE`, or `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE`; the claim moves `SUBMITTED → DENIED` with that reason and `adjudicated_at` set to the logical clock, `approved_amount_cents` stays 0, and **every dealer counter is left untouched** — only approved claims enter the claim ratio. The claim's `version` increments by 1. Both `APPROVED` and `DENIED` are terminal.

Approval is permitted while the certification is `ACTIVE`, `UNDER_AUDIT`, **or** `DECERTIFIED`, because a retail sale does not void a claim whose repair fell inside the window. The payout calculation looks circular — the reimbursement share is keyed on the dealer's claim ratio, but the claim ratio counts the very claim being reimbursed — and the following **evaluation order is normative** and is what makes the value well-defined.

First, the claim pins `dealer_ratio_snapshot_before` to the certifying dealer's `claim_ratio_effective` as read *before* this claim is counted anywhere. Second, the remaining headroom on the certification is computed as the programme's per-claim ceiling minus the certification's `approved_claim_amount_cents` so far, with a negative result treated as zero, and the capped amount is the smaller of the claim's `claimed_amount_cents` and that headroom. Two individually legal claims on one certification may collectively exhaust the ceiling, and the later one is trimmed to what is left; **the trim happens before the probation share, never after**. Third, the share is selected from the snapshot: when `dealer_ratio_snapshot_before` is at or above the programme's probation threshold of 0.25, the share is the programme's probation claim share of 0.80, and otherwise the share is 1.00 — a dealer already behaving badly is reimbursed at eighty percent. Fourth, `approved_amount_cents` is the capped amount multiplied by that share, rounded **down** to whole cents. Fifth — and only now — the claim moves `SUBMITTED → APPROVED` with `adjudicated_at` set to the logical clock, the certification's `approved_claim_count` and `approved_claim_amount_cents` are recomputed, and the dealer's `approved_claim_count_lifetime`, `approved_claim_count_window`, `claim_ratio_variant`, `claim_ratio_effective`, and `risk_score` are recomputed with this claim included. Sixth — and only after that — the privilege thresholds are re-evaluated against the post-claim ratio. A dealer therefore never has this claim's own effect on its ratio reduce this claim's payout, but the very next claim does feel it. The claim's `version`, the certification's `version`, and the dealer's `version` each increment by 1.

The claim ratio itself comes in **two variants**, and which one applies is a property of the dealer's volume. When the dealer's `lifetime_activated_count` is at or above the programme's volume floor — 20 activations by default — `claim_ratio_variant` is `TRAILING_WINDOW`; below that floor it is `LIFETIME_CUMULATIVE`. Under `LIFETIME_CUMULATIVE`, `claim_ratio_effective` is `approved_claim_count_lifetime` divided by `lifetime_activated_count`, with 1 substituted when that count is zero, rounded half up to 2 decimals: old claims never age out, and a dealer with one approved claim over two lifetime activations scores `0.50`. Under `TRAILING_WINDOW`, `claim_ratio_effective` is `approved_claim_count_window` divided by `window_activated_count`, again with 1 substituted for a zero denominator and rounded half up to 2 decimals: numerator and denominator are both restricted to the trailing window, so the very same dealer with both its claim and its activation outside the window scores `0.00` on identical underlying rows. Both window counters use the **inclusive** span running from the claim-window length before the current logical-clock date up to the current logical-clock date — 365 days by default — evaluated against each certification's `activated_at` on the denominator side and each claim's `repair_date` on the numerator side. Decertified certifications stay in the denominator: decertifying a car must not flatter the dealer's claim behaviour. Claims that are `SUBMITTED` or `DENIED` are excluded from both variants. The ratio is recomputed whenever a certification is activated, whenever a claim is approved, and on every transition of a certification into `DECERTIFIED`.

The dealer's `risk_score` rides on the same recomputations — any recomputation of `claim_ratio_effective` or of `audit_fail_rate` refreshes it. It is a weighted blend of the two behaviour signals expressed on a 0-to-100 scale: take `claim_ratio_effective`, multiply by 100, and weight it at 60 percent; take `audit_fail_rate`, multiply by 100, and weight it at 40 percent; add the two weighted terms; round the sum half up to a whole number; and cap the result at 100. No cross-dealer normalisation is involved — a dealer's score depends only on its own counters.

Crossing a behaviour threshold moves the dealer's privilege, and the thresholds are checked in descending severity. When the post-claim `claim_ratio_effective` is at or above the programme's suspension threshold of 0.40, `privilege_status` becomes `SUSPENDED` with `privilege_reason` `CLAIM_RATIO_SUSPENSION`. Otherwise, when it is at or above the probation threshold of 0.25, `privilege_status` becomes `PROBATION` with `privilege_reason` `CLAIM_RATIO_PROBATION`. Otherwise the privilege is left unchanged. A move to `SUSPENDED` — and only a move to `SUSPENDED` — cascades: every certification of that dealer still in `SUBMITTED` moves to `REJECTED` with `rejection_reason` `DEALER_SUSPENDED`, each releasing its vehicle's certification slot and moving that vehicle's `cpo_state` to `NOT_CERTIFIED`, and every listing of that dealer in `SYNDICATED` moves to `WITHDRAWN` with `withdrawal_reason` `DEALER_SUSPENDED` and a `removal_due_at` deadline. Every row that cascade touches has its `version` incremented.

A `ProgramAdministrator` may also move a dealer's privilege by hand, supplying a `privilege_status` and an optional `privilege_reason` that defaults to `ADMIN_ACTION`. The `privilege_status` enumeration is `ACTIVE`, `PROBATION`, and `SUSPENDED`; the `privilege_reason` enumeration is null, `CLAIM_RATIO_PROBATION`, `CLAIM_RATIO_SUSPENSION`, `AUDIT_FAILURE_SUSPENSION`, and `ADMIN_ACTION`, and it is null while a dealer sits at its default `ACTIVE`. A manual move to `SUSPENDED` drives exactly the same cascade as a threshold-driven one — pending submissions rejected with `rejection_reason` `DEALER_SUSPENDED`, and listings in `DRAFT` or `SYNDICATED` withdrawn with `withdrawal_reason` `DEALER_SUSPENDED` and a `removal_due_at` deadline — and increments the dealer's `version` by 1 plus the `version` of every cascaded certification, vehicle, and listing. Notably, the `ProgramAuditor` has no access to this capability at all: the suspension an auditor causes must arrive through the audit-failure threshold rather than by fiat.

The claim collection of a certification is readable by a `ProgramAdministrator`, a `ProgramAuditor`, and the owning `DealerPrincipal`, and accepts `status` and `component_category` filters with `limit` and `offset`.

### 3.10. Post-Certification Audit Sampling, Findings, and Decertification

Documentation retention only has teeth if somebody occasionally asks to see it. A `ProgramAuditor` therefore draws an **audit run** — a capability that takes no request body and returns a summary object rather than an entity: an `audit_run_id`, the `pool_size` it drew from, the `selected_count` it drew, and a `selected_certification_ids` array naming the certifications it selected. The `audit_run_id` is a deterministic run label built as the literal prefix `AR-` followed by the 1-based count of audit runs executed so far, so the first run is `AR-1`; it groups the samples of one run and is not a foreign key, because no audit-run entity is persisted.

The eligible pool is every certification that is `ACTIVE`, whose `audit_sampled` flag is still false, whose `activated_at` is at or before the logical clock, and whose dealer's `privilege_status` is `ACTIVE` or `PROBATION`. Certifications that are `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_AUDIT`, `REJECTED`, `SUPERSEDED`, or `DECERTIFIED` are excluded, as are those belonging to a `SUSPENDED` dealer. The pool is ranked by three keys in order: the dealer's `risk_score` descending, then the certification's `activated_at` ascending, then the certification's `created_at` ascending as the final tie-break — the certification's `id` is never consulted. The number drawn is the smaller of the pool size and the greater of the programme's per-run minimum (1 by default) and the programme's sampling fraction (20 percent by default) multiplied by the pool size and rounded **up** to a whole number. An empty pool is not an error: a pool size of zero yields a `selected_count` of 0, creates no audit samples at all, and still returns a successful 201 Created response.

Each drawn certification receives an **audit sample** in status `SELECTED`, carrying the run's `audit_run_id` and a `selection_rank` giving its 1-based position in the ranked order, with `finding_code`, `reviewed_at`, and `reviewer_user_id` all null. The certification itself moves `ACTIVE → UNDER_AUDIT`, its `audit_sampled` flag is set to true, and its `version` increments by 1. Because that flag is never reset, a certification is sampled at most once in its life. For every dealer touched, `audit_sample_count`, `audit_fail_rate`, and `risk_score` are recomputed. The audit-sample collection of a certification is readable by a `ProgramAdministrator`, a `ProgramAuditor`, and the owning `DealerPrincipal`, accepts `status` and `audit_run_id` filters with `limit` and `offset`, and is the only channel through which the sample identifiers a run created become discoverable.

Recording an outcome is likewise a `ProgramAuditor` act, supplying a `finding` of `PASS` or `FAIL` and an optional `finding_code` drawn from `NONE`, `DOCUMENTATION_MISSING`, `ELIGIBILITY_VIOLATION`, or `INSPECTION_INCOMPLETE`. The sample must still be `SELECTED` — a finding on one already `PASSED` or `FAILED` is rejected with `ERR_AUDIT_SAMPLE_ALREADY_REVIEWED` — and its parent certification must still be `UNDER_AUDIT`, otherwise `ERR_CERTIFICATION_NOT_ACTIVE`. Both `PASSED` and `FAILED` are terminal for a sample.

A `PASS` finding moves the sample `SELECTED → PASSED` with its `finding_code` defaulting to `NONE` when none is supplied, `reviewed_at` set to the logical clock, and `reviewer_user_id` set to the auditor's opaque `X-User-ID`. The certification returns `UNDER_AUDIT → ACTIVE` with its `activated_at`, its fee fields, and its coverage fields **untouched** — clearing an audit does not re-price or re-date anything. The certification's `version` and the sample's `version` each increment by 1.

A `FAIL` finding requires a `finding_code` that is present and is not `NONE`, and it drives the programme's deepest cascade — five hops, each caused by the one before it, all inside the one request. Hop 1 moves the sample `SELECTED → FAILED` with the supplied `finding_code`, `reviewed_at`, and `reviewer_user_id`, and recomputes the dealer's `audit_fail_count` and `audit_fail_rate`. Hop 2, caused by the sample failing, moves the certification `UNDER_AUDIT → DECERTIFIED` with `decertification_reason` `POST_AUDIT_FAIL` and `decertified_at` set to the logical clock, and — uniquely among all decertification reasons — sets its `fee_credited_cents` equal to its `fee_charged_cents`, because the programme's own audit invalidated the certification and the dealer gets its money back; the dealer's `fee_credited_cents_total` and `fee_balance_cents` are recomputed accordingly. Hop 3, caused by hop 2, moves the vehicle's `cpo_state` `CERTIFIED → DECERTIFIED`, sets the vehicle's `decertified_at` to the logical clock, clears `certified_certification_id` to null, recomputes `active_certification_count` to 0, moves every listing of that certification in `DRAFT` or `SYNDICATED` to `WITHDRAWN` with `withdrawal_reason` `CERTIFICATION_DECERTIFIED` and a `removal_due_at` deadline, and moves every complaint of that certification in `OPEN` or `REINSPECTION_ORDERED` to `DISMISSED`. Hop 4, caused by hop 1's counter crossing its threshold, applies only when the dealer's `audit_fail_count` has reached the programme's audit-failure suspension count — two failures by default: the dealer's `privilege_status` moves to `SUSPENDED` with `privilege_reason` `AUDIT_FAILURE_SUSPENSION`. Hop 5, caused by hop 4, moves every certification of that dealer still in `SUBMITTED` to `REJECTED` with `rejection_reason` `DEALER_SUSPENDED`, each releasing its vehicle's certification slot, and moves every listing of that dealer in `SYNDICATED` to `WITHDRAWN` with `withdrawal_reason` `DEALER_SUSPENDED`. The certification's `version`, the sample's `version`, the vehicle's `version`, and the dealer's `version` each increment by 1, as does the `version` of every other row the cascade touched.

Decertification can also be requested directly, by either a `ProgramAdministrator` or the owning `DealerPrincipal`, supplying a `decertification_reason`. That request accepts only `ADMIN_ACTION` or `TITLE_BRANDED`; the disposition reasons `RETAIL_SOLD` and `WHOLESALED` arrive only through vehicle history events, and `POST_AUDIT_FAIL` only through an audit finding. The full `decertification_reason` enumeration is therefore null, `RETAIL_SOLD`, `WHOLESALED`, `POST_AUDIT_FAIL`, `TITLE_BRANDED`, `COMPLAINT_REINSPECTION_FAIL`, and `ADMIN_ACTION`. The certification must be `ACTIVE` or `UNDER_AUDIT`; one already `REJECTED`, `SUPERSEDED`, or `DECERTIFIED` is rejected with `ERR_CERTIFICATION_TERMINAL`. `DECERTIFIED` is itself terminal.

Every decertification, whatever triggered it, shares one side-effect set. The certification moves to `DECERTIFIED` with the triggering `decertification_reason` and `decertified_at` set to the logical clock. The fee is credited — meaning `fee_credited_cents` is set equal to `fee_charged_cents` — **only** when the reason is `POST_AUDIT_FAIL`; every other reason leaves `fee_credited_cents` at 0, so a dealer that sells the car keeps paying for the badge it used. The vehicle moves `CERTIFIED → DECERTIFIED` with `decertified_at` set, `certified_certification_id` cleared, and `active_certification_count` recomputed to 0. Every listing of that certification in `DRAFT` or `SYNDICATED` is withdrawn with `withdrawal_reason` `CERTIFICATION_DECERTIFIED` and a `removal_due_at` deadline. Every complaint of that certification in `OPEN` or `REINSPECTION_ORDERED` is dismissed — with one exception: when the reason is `COMPLAINT_REINSPECTION_FAIL`, the driving complaint has already reached `RESOLVED_DECERTIFIED` and is not touched again. The dealer's `active_certification_count`, `fee_credited_cents_total`, and `fee_balance_cents` are recomputed, and its `claim_ratio_effective` is refreshed because transitions into `DECERTIFIED` are one of the ratio's recomputation triggers. The certification's `version` and the vehicle's `version` each increment by 1, as does the `version` of every cascaded listing and complaint. Decertifying a certification that is `UNDER_AUDIT` behaves identically, with one consequence worth calling out: the open audit sample stays in `SELECTED` and can never receive a finding afterwards.

### 3.11. Customer Complaints and the Re-Inspection Path

A `CustomerAdvocate` — and only a `CustomerAdvocate` — logs a **complaint** against a certified vehicle, supplying the `certification_id` complained about, a `customer_reference` recording the customer's case number, a `category`, and a free-text `description` narrating what the customer reported. The `category` enumeration is `UNDISCLOSED_DAMAGE`, `INSPECTION_ITEM_FAILURE`, `WARRANTY_DENIAL`, and `MARKETING_MISREPRESENTATION`. The certification must be live, otherwise `ERR_CERTIFICATION_NOT_ACTIVE`. A new complaint is created in `OPEN` with `filed_at` set to the logical clock, `reinspection_ordered_at` and `resolved_at` null, and a derived `reinspection_deadline_at` equal to `filed_at` plus five days — after that instant no re-inspection may be opened for the complaint. Complaints are readable through a per-certification listing that accepts `status` and `category` filters with `limit` and `offset`, and through a single-complaint read that exposes `reinspection_deadline_at` and the resolution stamps; both are open to a `ProgramAdministrator`, a `ProgramAuditor`, a `DealerPrincipal`, and a `CustomerAdvocate`, and the single read is the view the advocate and the certifying dealer both work from while a re-inspection is pending.

Ordering a re-inspection is not a separate operation: it happens when a `DealerPrincipal` opens a new inspection and supplies the complaint's identifier as `complaint_id`. That makes the new inspection's `purpose` `RE_INSPECTION` and moves the complaint `OPEN → REINSPECTION_ORDERED` with `reinspection_ordered_at` set to the logical clock and the complaint's `version` incremented by 1. Four conditions guard the ordering. The complaint must be `OPEN`; one already ordered, resolved, or dismissed is rejected with `ERR_COMPLAINT_NOT_OPEN`. The logical clock must be at or before the complaint's `reinspection_deadline_at` — the bound is inclusive, so an order at exactly the deadline instant is accepted, and one past it is rejected with `ERR_REINSPECTION_DEADLINE_PASSED`. The new inspection's `vehicle_id` must be the vehicle of the complained-about certification. And the new inspection's `technician_id` must **not** be the `technician_id` of the inspection that backed the complained-about certification: a fresh pair of eyes is required, and reusing the original inspector is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Notably, opening a re-inspection leaves the vehicle's `cpo_state` at `CERTIFIED`, because the certification remains `ACTIVE` while it is being re-examined; every other eligibility screen, credential check, and technician-slot check of §3.3 still applies unchanged.

The re-inspection's own verdict resolves the complaint. When the re-inspection closes as `PASSED`, the complaint moves `REINSPECTION_ORDERED → RESOLVED_UPHELD` with `resolved_at` set to the logical clock and its `version` incremented by 1; the certification stays `ACTIVE` and its coverage fields are untouched — the customer's concern was examined and the badge held. When the re-inspection closes as `FAILED`, the complaint moves `REINSPECTION_ORDERED → RESOLVED_DECERTIFIED` with `resolved_at` set and its `version` incremented, and a four-hop cascade follows: hop 1 is the inspection reaching `FAILED` with `failure_reason` `CRITICAL_ITEM_FAIL` and `submitted_at` set; hop 2, caused by that, resolves the complaint as just described; hop 3, caused by the complaint resolution, moves the complaint's certification `ACTIVE → DECERTIFIED` with `decertification_reason` `COMPLAINT_REINSPECTION_FAIL` and `fee_credited_cents` left at 0; hop 4, caused by that decertification, withdraws every listing of that certification with `withdrawal_reason` `CERTIFICATION_DECERTIFIED` and a `removal_due_at` deadline and moves the vehicle's `cpo_state` to `DECERTIFIED`. Each affected row's `version` increments by 1.

A complaint can also be closed without ever being examined. Whenever the complained-about certification is decertified for any reason **other than** `COMPLAINT_REINSPECTION_FAIL`, every complaint of that certification in `OPEN` or `REINSPECTION_ORDERED` moves to `DISMISSED` with `resolved_at` set to the logical clock and its `version` incremented by 1. The two source states are treated identically, with one addition on the `REINSPECTION_ORDERED` path: because a re-inspection was already in flight, every inspection whose `complaint_id` names the dismissed complaint and whose status is `DRAFT`, `IN_PROGRESS`, or `RECONDITIONING_REQUIRED` is moved to `VOIDED` with `failure_reason` `COMPLAINT_DISMISSED`. `RESOLVED_UPHELD`, `RESOLVED_DECERTIFIED`, and `DISMISSED` are all terminal for a complaint.
