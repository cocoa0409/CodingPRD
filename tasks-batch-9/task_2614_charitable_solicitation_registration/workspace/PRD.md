# Charitable Solicitation Registration API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A nonprofit that raises money across state lines does not face one regulator; it faces fifty of them, each with its own registration trigger, its own renewal deadline arithmetic, its own audit threshold, its own disclosure language and its own appetite for penalties. Compliance offices today track this in spreadsheets, and the failure mode is always the same: a deadline computed one month off, a financial statement restated after a filing was already certified, a fundraising contract filed a week late in the one state whose window was ten days rather than thirty. The consequence is not a bookkeeping error but a cease-solicitation order that stops donations in a state where the charity has already spent money on appeals.

This document specifies the Charitable Solicitation Registration API, a system of record for a nonprofit compliance office and the state charities bureaus it answers to. The API models the full regulatory surface: per-state rule sets that drive every derived deadline and fee, solicitation-activity footprints that decide where registration is even required, initial and renewal filings backed by governing documents, financial statements and officer disclosures, extension requests against fiscal-year-based deadlines, professional fundraiser and fundraising-counsel registration with contract-filing obligations, disclosure clause requirements in solicitation materials, commercial co-venture and charitable sales promotion filings, and the delinquency, penalty and remediation machinery that follows when any of it slips.

Two properties shape the design. First, almost every number a user cares about is **derived** rather than entered — deadlines, assurance requirements, filing fees, penalties, settlement totals and disclosure gaps are all recomputed by the server from configuration and from cross-entity state, and a single write can legitimately move fields on four other records. Second, the system runs on a **logical clock** rather than wall-clock time, so that deadline, cure-window and promotion-window behaviour is exactly reproducible. Everything in this document should be read with those two properties in mind: clients supply facts, the server supplies consequences.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

All endpoints described in this document are mounted at the root of the service. This section defines the contracts that hold everywhere — authentication, envelope shape, pagination, precision, concurrency and the order in which failures are reported.

### 2.1. Actors and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which it acts. Five roles exist, spelled exactly as follows: `ComplianceOfficer`, `CertifyingOfficer`, `StateRegulator`, `FundraiserAgent`, and `SystemAdmin`. A request missing either header, carrying an empty value, or naming a role outside that set of five is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). A well-authenticated request whose role does not appear in the authorized-role list of the endpoint it targets (see §4) is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

Two of the five roles are **entity-backed**, meaning `X-User-ID` names a persisted row and ownership is checked against it:

- When `X-User-Role` is `CertifyingOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `OfficerDisclosure` row. Ownership checks on any resource that references an organization compare the organization of that disclosure against the resource's `organization_id`; a certifying officer may only submit filings and settle promotions belonging to the organization whose officer roster contains them. The same identifier is what the server stamps into `certified_by_officer_id` when a filing is submitted.
- When `X-User-Role` is `FundraiserAgent`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `ProfessionalFundraiser` firm. Ownership checks compare the target resource's `fundraiser_id` to `X-User-ID`, so an agent may register only its own firm and file only its own firm's contracts.

The other three roles are **opaque**. When `X-User-Role` is `ComplianceOfficer`, `StateRegulator`, or `SystemAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server accepts and may log it, but never compares it to any row's `id` and never derives ownership from it. A compliance officer therefore acts on behalf of any organization in the roster, a state regulator adjudicates any filing, and a platform administrator seeds configuration and drives the clock, all without an ownership relationship.

Where an entity-backed role acts on a resource it does not own, the rejection is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). This covers a `FundraiserAgent` registering a firm other than its own or filing a contract whose `fundraiser_id` is not its own identifier, and a `CertifyingOfficer` acting on a filing or promotion whose `organization_id` differs from the organization of the disclosure named by `X-User-ID`. It also covers the case where `X-User-ID` under the `CertifyingOfficer` role matches no `OfficerDisclosure` at all, which surfaces instead as `ERR_OFFICER_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) because there is no officer to compare against.

Segregation of duties is a first-class rule rather than an afterthought: the officer who certifies a filing may not have prepared any document inside it, and the officer who settles a co-venture promotion may not be the officer who certified that promotion's client filing for the same state. Both violations report `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints are paginated through two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of records returned; it defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` is the number of records to skip and defaults to 0. Both are echoed back in the `meta` object of the envelope alongside `total_count` (the number of records matching the filters before pagination is applied) and `has_more` (true when records remain beyond the current page).

Unless a specific endpoint documents a different ordering, every list is sorted by `created_at` ascending. One computed read does document a different ordering: the organization compliance calendar is ordered by `effective_due_date` ascending with `state_code` ascending as the tie-breaker, and it accepts no `limit` or `offset` at all.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock

The server never reads wall-clock time for business decisions. It maintains a single logical clock that reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` until a platform administrator moves it, by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant to the clock-setting capability; the response echoes the new instant as `current_utc`. Every subsequent comparison — renewal deadlines, extension windows, contract filing windows, cure deadlines, promotion windows, bond expiries and sweep eligibility — is evaluated against the logical clock's current value and never against the host's real time. Timestamps the server stamps onto records (`submitted_at`, `registered_at`, `decided_at`, `filed_at`, `issued_at`, and the sweep's own `swept_at` marker) likewise take the logical clock's value.

### 2.5. Common Resource Properties

Every resource carries a server-minted `id` formatted as a bare UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp in ISO 8601 UTC with three fractional digits (for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`). Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly one on every mutation of that row — including mutations the server performs on its own initiative while cascading a change that originated on some other resource. Recomputing a derived field such as `Organization.penalty_outstanding_cents` or `StateRegistration.effective_due_date` is a mutation for this purpose: `updated_at` moves and `version` increments.

`SolicitationActivity` is the single exception. It is an immutable event record: it carries `id` and `created_at` only, and both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from its representation. Organizations, state profiles, financial statements, officer disclosures, state registrations, registration documents, extension requests, professional fundraisers, fundraiser registrations, fundraising contracts, co-venture promotions, disclosure statements and enforcement actions all carry the full set of four.

### 2.6. Monetary Precision, Ratios and Whole-Day Arithmetic

Every monetary field in the system is an integer count of cents; there are no fractional currency values anywhere in a request or a response. Where a calculation multiplies cents by a fraction — the initial-filing multiplier, the monthly late-fee escalation, the reinstatement discount — the intermediate product is rounded **half up** to a whole number of cents, and each specification below states exactly how many times and at which step that rounding occurs. Rounding order is observable in this domain, so it is pinned rather than left to the implementation.

Two non-monetary precision rules apply. Ratios, of which `program_ratio` is the only instance, are rounded half up to four decimal places. Date arithmetic is performed in whole days and whole calendar months: derived deadlines land at midnight UTC (`00:00:00.000Z`), month arithmetic clamps an overflowing day-of-month down to the last day of the target month, and any "days late" or "months late" quantity is a whole number produced by the rounding direction its formula names.

### 2.7. State Profiles as the Configuration Backbone

Nothing in this system is hard-coded per jurisdiction. A `StateProfile` row, keyed by its two-letter `state_code`, is the sole source of every jurisdictional parameter: whether registration is required at all, how the renewal deadline is derived, which financial metric the assurance thresholds measure, what those thresholds are, what activity levels trigger registration, whether extensions are entertained and for how long, which disclosure clauses solicitation materials must carry, how quickly a fundraising contract must be filed, what bond a fundraiser must post, whether co-venture promotions must be filed and how far apart consecutive promotion windows must sit, the four components and two clamp bounds of the filing fee, the three parameters of the late-fee accrual, the length of the cure period, and the undiscounted reinstatement fee. A referenced `state_code` that matches no profile — whether it arrives as a path parameter, as a payload field, or as one element of a `state_codes` array — is rejected with `ERR_STATE_PROFILE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404).

Because profiles are configuration, only a platform administrator creates them, and only six of their fields are required on creation: `state_code`, `registration_required`, `renewal_due_rule`, `assurance_basis`, `review_threshold_cents` and `audit_threshold_cents`. Every other field falls back to a documented default, listed in §3 next to the rule that consumes it. `state_code` must be exactly two uppercase letters and must not collide with an existing profile; a collision reports `ERR_DUPLICATE_IDENTIFIER` (HTTP 409). `audit_threshold_cents` may not be lower than `review_threshold_cents`, and `review_threshold_cents` may not be negative.

### 2.8. Two Independent Posture Dimensions

An organization's compliance standing is not a single status. It is two derived dimensions living on the same row, each recomputed by the server from its children and each gating a different set of operations. Neither dimension is ever supplied by a client, and a change to one leaves the other untouched.

The first dimension is `solicitation_posture`, a function of `ceased_state_codes` — the distinct `state_code` values of the organization's registrations currently in `CEASED`, held in ascending order. The posture is `CLEAR` when that list is empty. It is `RESTRICTED` when the list holds exactly one code and that code is not the organization's `domicile_state_code`. It is `HALTED` when the list contains `domicile_state_code`, or when it holds two or more codes — so a cease order in the state of domicile jumps straight from `CLEAR` to `HALTED` without passing through `RESTRICTED`, and a second cease order anywhere escalates `RESTRICTED` to `HALTED`. The dimension is fully reversible: when a ceased registration is restored, the posture walks back down — to `RESTRICTED` when exactly one non-domicile code remains, and to `CLEAR` when the list empties.

The second dimension is `filing_posture`, a function of `delinquent_registration_count`. It reads `DELINQUENT` whenever that count is at least 1 and `CURRENT` whenever it is exactly 0. The transition from `CURRENT` to `DELINQUENT` is not merely a label change: it also denies every extension request still awaiting a decision anywhere on that organization's filings, as described in §3.9. The transition back from `DELINQUENT` to `CURRENT` fires the moment the last delinquent filing leaves that status.

Each recomputation of either dimension is a mutation of the organization row and increments `Organization.version` by one. Because both dimensions are recomputed inside the same server-driven cascades that move child records, a single client request can increment the organization's version while also incrementing versions on a registration, an enforcement action and a promotion. §3 states the full cascade for each operation; the discipline is that every entity the cascade touches has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented, and that no cascade is partial — either the whole chain applies or the request fails and nothing changes.

### 2.9. Resolving Path and Payload References

Requests that address a resource through the URL are validated left to right. The first identifier in the path that names no existing row determines the error, and the server stops there rather than continuing to evaluate deeper segments or the request body: a document read reports `ERR_REGISTRATION_DOCUMENT_NOT_FOUND`, an extension-request decision reports `ERR_EXTENSION_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, an enforcement decision reports `ERR_ENFORCEMENT_ACTION_NOT_FOUND`, a contract operation reports `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`, a promotion operation reports `ERR_PROMOTION_NOT_FOUND`, a filing operation reports `ERR_REGISTRATION_NOT_FOUND`, an organization read or amendment reports `ERR_ORGANIZATION_NOT_FOUND`, a fundraiser read reports `ERR_FUNDRAISER_NOT_FOUND`, and a profile read keyed on `state_code` reports `ERR_STATE_PROFILE_NOT_FOUND`. All nine carry HTTP 404.

Foreign keys arriving in a request body are resolved in a fixed order so that the reported error is deterministic when more than one is wrong: `organization_id` first, then `state_code` or every element of `state_codes`, then `financial_statement_id`, then `registration_id`, then `prepared_by_officer_id`, then `fundraiser_id`, then `coventurer_id`, and finally `supersedes_registration_id`. A dangling `organization_id` reports `ERR_ORGANIZATION_NOT_FOUND`; a dangling `financial_statement_id` reports `ERR_FINANCIAL_STATEMENT_NOT_FOUND`; a dangling `prepared_by_officer_id` reports `ERR_OFFICER_NOT_FOUND`; a dangling `fundraiser_id` or `coventurer_id` reports `ERR_FUNDRAISER_NOT_FOUND`; a dangling `registration_id` or `supersedes_registration_id` reports `ERR_REGISTRATION_NOT_FOUND`.

### 2.10. Optimistic Concurrency Control on Superseding Writes

Two creation operations in this system legitimately overwrite a picture the client has already reasoned about, and neither can be guarded by a state precondition, because the overwrite is exactly what the operation is for. Both are therefore guarded by an optimistic-concurrency check on the parent row's `version`.

`POST /extension-requests` requires a non-optional `expected_registration_version` field carrying the `version` of the target `StateRegistration` as the client last observed it. Creating the request drives every earlier extension request on that filing to `SUPERSEDED`, which can silently discard an already-granted extension and reset the filing's deadline, so the server compares `expected_registration_version` against the registration's current `version` and rejects any mismatch with `ERR_STALE_REGISTRATION_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A client that has not observed the filing's latest version cannot know which deadline it is about to replace.

`POST /financial-statements` requires a non-optional `expected_organization_version` field carrying the `version` of the target `Organization` as the client last observed it. A restatement for a fiscal year that already has a current statement supersedes that statement and repoints open filings onto the new one, so again no state precondition can block the write; instead the server compares `expected_organization_version` against the organization's current `version` and rejects a mismatch with `ERR_STALE_ORGANIZATION_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This is what tells a preparer that the organization's postures, penalty ledger or registration counters moved underneath the numbers being restated.

No other endpoint takes a version field. Requests to any other write operation neither require nor accept `expected_registration_version` or `expected_organization_version`.

### 2.11. Order of Validation

Checks are evaluated in a fixed ascending order and the first failure wins; no later check can mask an earlier one, and no earlier check is skipped because a later one would also fail. The full ladder, with the endpoints each rung guards, is:

1. Authentication — both headers present and `X-User-Role` one of the five declared roles → `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. Role authorization — the acting role appears in the target endpoint's role list → `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. Request shape — required fields present, enum values in range, integers non-negative where constrained, arrays non-empty where constrained, datetimes parseable as ISO 8601, `coverage_end` strictly after `coverage_start`, `contract_end` strictly after `contract_start`, `promo_end` at or after `promo_start`, `fiscal_year_end_month` within 1 through 12 → `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422). Applies to every endpoint.
4. Path existence, resolved left to right → the matching not-found error (404). Applies to every endpoint with a path parameter.
5. Payload foreign-key existence, resolved in the order given in §2.9 → the matching not-found error (404). Applies to every creation endpoint carrying foreign keys.
6. Ownership for entity-backed roles → `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). Applies to fundraiser registration creation, contract filing, filing submission, and promotion settlement.
7. `expected_registration_version` equals the target registration's `version` → `ERR_STALE_REGISTRATION_VERSION` (412). Applies to extension-request creation.
8. `expected_organization_version` equals the target organization's `version` → `ERR_STALE_ORGANIZATION_VERSION` (412). Applies to financial-statement creation.
9. The registration's `status` is in the requested transition's from-set → `ERR_INVALID_REGISTRATION_STATE` (409). Applies to filing submission, review, withdrawal, and registration-document creation.
10. The enforcement action's `status` is in the requested transition's from-set → `ERR_INVALID_ENFORCEMENT_STATE` (409). Applies to remediation, decision, and vacatur of an enforcement action.
11. The contract's `status` is `DRAFT` for a filing operation → `ERR_INVALID_CONTRACT_STATE` (409). Applies to contract filing.
12. The promotion's `status` is in the from-set — `DRAFT` to file, `FILED` to activate, `ACTIVE` or `DEFICIENT_REMITTANCE` to settle → `ERR_INVALID_PROMOTION_STATE` (409). Applies to promotion filing, activation, and settlement.
13. The extension request's `status` is `PENDING` → `ERR_EXTENSION_ALREADY_DECIDED` (409). Applies to extension-request decisions.
14. The organization's `solicitation_posture` is not `HALTED` → `ERR_SOLICITATION_HALTED` (409). Applies to registration creation, filing submission, and promotion creation.
15. The organization's `solicitation_posture` is exactly `CLEAR` → `ERR_SOLICITATION_RESTRICTED` (409). Applies to promotion activation.
16. No document on the filing was prepared by the acting certifying officer → `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). Applies to filing submission.
17. Every officer disclosure of the organization has `disclosure_status` equal to `COMPLETE` → `ERR_OFFICER_DISCLOSURE_INCOMPLETE` (422). Applies to filing submission.
18. The requested state's `registration_required` is true → `ERR_STATE_REGISTRATION_NOT_REQUIRED` (422). Applies to registration creation.
19. Every state on the contract or promotion has an active fundraiser registration for the firm → `ERR_FUNDRAISER_NOT_REGISTERED_IN_STATE` (422). Applies to contract filing and promotion filing.
20. `supersedes_registration_id`, when supplied, targets a registration of the same organization and state whose `status` is `REGISTERED` or `DELINQUENT` → `ERR_SUPERSEDE_TARGET_INVALID` (422). Applies to registration creation and review.
21. The statement named by `financial_statement_id` has `statement_status` equal to `CURRENT` and belongs to the same organization → `ERR_FINANCIAL_STATEMENT_SUPERSEDED` (422). Applies to registration creation and filing submission.
22. Coverage intervals do not overlap → `ERR_COVERAGE_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (409). Applies to registration creation and review.
23. Promotion windows for the same co-venturer are adequately separated → `ERR_PROMOTION_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409). Applies to promotion creation.
24. The contract filing hard stop has not passed → `ERR_CONTRACT_FILING_WINDOW_CLOSED` (422). Applies to contract filing.
25. The cure window is open for a remediation, and closed for an escalation taken directly from `OPEN` → `ERR_CURE_WINDOW_EXPIRED` (422). Applies to enforcement remediation and enforcement decisions.
26. The promotion window is in the right phase for the requested operation → `ERR_PROMOTION_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN` (422). Applies to promotion filing, activation, and settlement.
27. No non-terminal registration already exists for the same organization, state and fiscal year → `ERR_DUPLICATE_STATE_REGISTRATION` (409). Applies to registration creation.
28. `ein` is unused by another organization, and `state_code` unused by another profile → `ERR_DUPLICATE_IDENTIFIER` (409). Applies to organization creation and state-profile creation.
29. The required document set is complete → `ERR_REQUIRED_DOCUMENT_MISSING` (422). Applies to filing submission.
30. The state allows extensions and grants at least one day → `ERR_EXTENSION_NOT_ALLOWED` (422). Applies to extension-request creation and extension-request decisions.
31. The organization's `filing_posture` is `CURRENT` → `ERR_FILING_POSTURE_DELINQUENT` (409). Applies to extension-request creation.
32. The bond posted is sufficient and outlasts the registration → `ERR_BOND_INSUFFICIENT` (422). Applies to fundraiser-registration creation.
33. No other active fundraiser registration exists for the same firm and state → `ERR_DUPLICATE_FUNDRAISER_REGISTRATION` (409). Applies to fundraiser-registration creation.
34. The supporting statement's assurance level covers the requirement → `ERR_ASSURANCE_LEVEL_INSUFFICIENT` (422). Applies to filing submission.
35. `actual_units` and `remitted_cents` in a settlement are non-negative → `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422). Applies to promotion settlement. Note carefully what this rung does **not** do: a remittance smaller than the benefit owed is never an error. There is no shortfall rejection code; a shortfall routes the settlement into a different terminal state, as §3.7 describes.

The business meaning of rungs 14 through 35 — the conditions each one is really testing, and the domain reason it exists — is developed in §3 alongside the workflow it guards.

### 2.12. Conserved Quantities and Compensating Actions

Two invariants must hold after every request, and they are the system's own consistency check on the cascade machinery described above.

The first is the penalty ledger. `Organization.penalty_outstanding_cents` is at all times exactly the sum of `penalty_cents` over that organization's enforcement actions whose `status` is `OPEN`, `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED` or `ESCALATED`; actions in `CURED` or `VACATED` contribute nothing. Issuing an action for a penalty raises the ledger by exactly that amount, and vacating that same action lowers it by exactly that amount, so the ledger returns to its pre-issuance value with no drift even though the intervening transitions moved several other fields.

The second is a cardinality rather than a sum. `Organization.registered_state_count` is at all times exactly the number of that organization's registrations whose `status` is `REGISTERED`, and `delinquent_registration_count` is exactly the number whose `status` is `DELINQUENT`. Filings in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `DEFICIENT`, `EXPIRED`, `CEASED` or `WITHDRAWN` contribute to neither counter. Approving a filing raises the registered count by exactly one; withdrawing that filing lowers it by exactly one, restoring the pre-approval count even though `registered_at` and the accepted `review_status` values on its documents remain stamped in the record.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Onboarding a Charity and Mapping Its Solicitation Footprint

A compliance officer — or a platform administrator seeding data — enters a charity into the roster by supplying its `legal_name` (between 1 and 200 characters, and not blank), its `ein` (exactly nine digits, and unique across the roster, since a second organization claiming the same `ein` is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_IDENTIFIER`), its `domicile_state_code` naming the state of incorporation or principal office, and its `fiscal_year_end_month` as a calendar month number between 1 and 12 inclusive. Two optional flags round out the profile: `has_online_donation_page`, which defaults to false and declares whether the charity operates an interactive online giving page, and `lifecycle_status`, which defaults to `ACTIVE` and may otherwise be `DISSOLVED`. The `domicile_state_code` must name an existing state profile.

Creation initialises the derived side of the record and clients may not set any of it: `solicitation_posture` starts at `CLEAR`, `filing_posture` at `CURRENT`, `registered_state_count` and `delinquent_registration_count` at 0, `ceased_state_codes` at an empty list, and `penalty_outstanding_cents` at 0. Reading a single organization returns all of it — the two postures, the two counters, the ceased-state list and the penalty ledger — which is how a compliance officer sees at a glance whether solicitation is gated anywhere. The roster listing supports filtering by `solicitation_posture`, by `filing_posture` and by `domicile_state_code`, so an examiner can pull every organization currently halted, or every organization domiciled in one state.

Four profile fields remain amendable after creation, and amendment is restricted to a compliance officer: `legal_name`, `fiscal_year_end_month`, `has_online_donation_page` and `lifecycle_status`. Amending the fiscal year end has consequences that reach well beyond the organization row, because every renewal deadline in the system is derived from it. On amendment the server therefore recomputes `renewal_due_date` and `effective_due_date` on every one of that organization's registrations whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `DEFICIENT` or `REGISTERED` — registrations in `EXPIRED`, `DELINQUENT`, `CEASED` or `WITHDRAWN` are left alone — and increments `Organization.version` by one. Neither `ein` nor `domicile_state_code` may be amended; they are identity, not profile.

Before a charity can argue about *where* it must register, it must record *what it did*. A `SolicitationActivity` row captures one fiscal year of fundraising in one state: `organization_id` for the charity, `state_code` for the jurisdiction whose donors were solicited, `fiscal_year` (a year between 2000 and 2100 inclusive), `donor_count` as the number of distinct donors located in that state (never negative), `contributions_cents` as the money those donors gave (never negative), and `channels`, a non-empty array whose members are drawn from `MAIL`, `PHONE`, `EMAIL`, `WEB_PASSIVE`, `WEB_TARGETED`, `EVENT` and `GRANT_REQUEST`. These rows are immutable history: once written they are never amended, which is why they carry no `updated_at` and no `version`.

Each activity row is scored on creation against the state's own trigger parameters, and the scoring answers two computed fields. If the state's `registration_required` is false, the row reports `triggers_registration` as false with an empty `trigger_reasons` list and nothing further is evaluated. Otherwise three independent clauses are tested. The first fires when `donor_count` is at least the state's `donor_count_trigger`, which defaults to 25 donors, and it contributes the reason `DONOR_COUNT`. The second fires when `contributions_cents` is at least the state's `contribution_trigger_cents`, which defaults to $25,000 — that is, 2,500,000 cents — and contributes `CONTRIBUTION_TOTAL`. The third fires when the state's `online_presence_counts` flag is true (its default is false) and `channels` contains `WEB_TARGETED`, and contributes `TARGETED_ONLINE_PRESENCE`; note that this clause is about *targeted* web solicitation, so a passive donate button recorded as `WEB_PASSIVE` never fires it on its own. All three comparisons are inclusive at the trigger value. `trigger_reasons` lists whichever clauses fired in the canonical order `DONOR_COUNT`, then `CONTRIBUTION_TOTAL`, then `TARGETED_ONLINE_PRESENCE`, regardless of the order in which they were evaluated or of the order of `channels`, and `triggers_registration` is true when at least one fired. The reverse lookup of a charity's activity rows can be narrowed by `state_code` and by `fiscal_year`.

Officer and director disclosures are the third leg of onboarding. Each `OfficerDisclosure` names its `organization_id`, the `officer_name`, and a `title` drawn from `PRESIDENT`, `TREASURER`, `SECRETARY`, `EXECUTIVE_DIRECTOR` and `BOARD_CHAIR`. Three optional fields default quietly: `is_certifying_officer` defaults to false and marks the officer as authorized to certify state filings, `annual_compensation_cents` defaults to 0 and may not be negative, and `criminal_conviction_disclosed` defaults to false. A fourth, `conviction_explanation`, defaults to null and is the narrative a disclosed conviction demands. From these the server derives `disclosure_status`: the row is `INCOMPLETE` when `criminal_conviction_disclosed` is true while `conviction_explanation` is null or an empty string, and `COMPLETE` in every other case — including the ordinary case of no conviction at all, where the missing narrative is not a defect. An incomplete disclosure anywhere on the roster blocks certification of that organization's filings, as §3.4 explains. The officer roster of an organization can be listed and narrowed by `is_certifying_officer`, which is how a compliance officer finds the person entitled to certify.

### 3.2. Financial Statements, Restatements and the Assurance Ladder

Every filing must stand on a financial statement, and a statement is filed for one `organization_id` and one `fiscal_year` (again between 2000 and 2100). The preparer supplies `total_revenue_cents`, which must be at least as large as `contributions_cents`; `contributions_cents`, the money given by the public and excluding government grants; `program_expense_cents`, `fundraising_expense_cents` and `management_expense_cents`, none of which may be negative; and optionally `government_grant_cents`, which defaults to 0, may not be negative, and is understood to be *included* inside `total_revenue_cents` rather than added to it. One further optional field, `assurance_level`, records the level of independent assurance actually obtained; it defaults to `NONE` and may otherwise be `REVIEW` or `AUDIT`. The creation payload also carries the mandatory `expected_organization_version` described in §2.10.

Two figures are derived on creation. `functional_expense_total_cents` is the sum of the program, fundraising and management expense amounts. `program_ratio` is the program expense divided by that functional expense total, rounded half up to four decimal places, and is defined as `0.0` whenever the functional expense total is zero, so a statement with no expenses at all yields a ratio rather than an arithmetic failure. A newly created statement takes `statement_status` of `CURRENT`; the server, never the client, owns that field.

Filing a second statement for a `fiscal_year` that already has a current one is a **restatement**, and it is a three-link cascade rather than a simple insert. The prior statement moves `CURRENT` → `SUPERSEDED`. Every registration pointing at the superseded statement through `financial_statement_id` whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `DEFICIENT` is repointed to the new statement's identifier, has its `required_financial_attestation` re-derived against the new numbers, and has its `version` incremented. And where such a repointed registration was sitting in `SUBMITTED`, and its freshly re-derived attestation requirement now outranks the new statement's `assurance_level` on the ordered scale in which `NONE` is weakest, `REVIEW` sits above it and `AUDIT` is strongest, that registration is pushed back `SUBMITTED` → `DEFICIENT` with `deficiency_reasons` set to a single-element list holding `ASSURANCE_LEVEL_INSUFFICIENT` and `deficiency_round_count` incremented by one. The restating write also increments `Organization.version` by one. Registrations already in `REGISTERED`, `EXPIRED`, `DELINQUENT`, `CEASED` or `WITHDRAWN` are untouched by all three links: a restatement does not reopen a filing the state has already accepted.

The assurance requirement itself is derived from exactly two rows — the statement named by `financial_statement_id`, read regardless of its `statement_status`, and the single state profile for the filing's `state_code` — with no aggregation across sibling statements. The state chooses which figure it measures through `assurance_basis`. Under the `CONTRIBUTIONS_ONLY` basis the measured metric is the statement's `contributions_cents`, which excludes `government_grant_cents` and every other non-contribution dollar. Under the `GROSS_REVENUE` basis the measured metric is `total_revenue_cents`, which includes government grants and program revenue. The metric is then laddered: when it is at or above `audit_threshold_cents` the requirement is `AUDIT`; otherwise when it is at or above `review_threshold_cents` the requirement is `REVIEW`; otherwise it is `NONE`. Both comparisons are inclusive at the threshold. The consequence of the two bases is worth stating plainly, because it is the most common source of confusion in multi-state filing: one and the same statement can require an `AUDIT` in a gross-revenue state and `NONE` in a contributions-only state, purely because a large government grant counts in the first and not the second.

A charity's statements can be listed in creation order and narrowed by `fiscal_year` and by `statement_status`, which is how a preparer confirms which restatement is currently authoritative.

### 3.3. Opening a Filing: Coverage Windows, Derived Deadlines and Fee Assessment

A compliance officer opens a filing by declaring the `organization_id`, the `state_code` being registered in, the `registration_type` — either `INITIAL` to open a registration or `RENEWAL` to continue one — the `fiscal_year` the filing reports on, the coverage interval as `coverage_start` and `coverage_end`, and the `financial_statement_id` of the supporting statement. A renewal additionally names `supersedes_registration_id`, the prior filing in the same state that it replaces; the field defaults to null and must be null on an initial filing. The new record lands in `status` of `DRAFT` with `deficiency_round_count` at 0, `deficiency_reasons` an empty list, and `submitted_at`, `registered_at` and `certified_by_officer_id` all null.

Four gates apply to the create. A filing may not be opened at all for a state whose profile has `registration_required` set to false, which reports `ERR_STATE_REGISTRATION_NOT_REQUIRED`. A filing may not be opened while the organization's `solicitation_posture` is `HALTED`, which reports `ERR_SOLICITATION_HALTED`. The supporting statement must be `CURRENT` and must belong to the same organization, or the create is rejected with `ERR_FINANCIAL_STATEMENT_SUPERSEDED`. And where `supersedes_registration_id` is supplied, its target must belong to the same organization, sit in the same state, and be in `status` `REGISTERED` or `DELINQUENT` — anything else reports `ERR_SUPERSEDE_TARGET_INVALID`.

Two uniqueness rules protect the state's docket. First, at most one non-terminal filing may exist per organization, state and fiscal year: if another filing for the same triple exists in any status other than `EXPIRED` or `WITHDRAWN`, the create is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_STATE_REGISTRATION`. Second, coverage intervals may not overlap. The interval is treated as half-open — `coverage_start` is inside it and `coverage_end` is outside it — and it is compared against the coverage intervals of every other filing for the same organization and state whose `status` is `SUBMITTED`, `REGISTERED`, `DELINQUENT` or `CEASED`. Any genuine overlap reports `ERR_COVERAGE_PERIOD_OVERLAP`, while two intervals that merely abut, where the new `coverage_start` is exactly the existing `coverage_end`, are accepted as consecutive coverage years rather than a conflict. `coverage_end` must in all cases be strictly after `coverage_start`. The overlap rule is re-evaluated at approval time as well as at creation time, because a competing filing may have been approved in the interim.

**Deriving the renewal deadline.** `renewal_due_date` is computed on creation, and recomputed whenever the deadline picture changes, from three inputs: the filing's `fiscal_year`, the organization's `fiscal_year_end_month`, and the state's `renewal_due_rule`. The first step is the fiscal year end date: take the month named by `fiscal_year_end_month` in the year named by `fiscal_year`, and take its last calendar day at midnight UTC. The second step applies the state's rule, and the order of arithmetic inside it is pinned. Under `FYE_PLUS_4M_15D` the server first advances four calendar months from the fiscal year end date — clamping the day of month down to the last day of the target month when the original day does not exist there — and then, second, adds fifteen days to that anchor. Under `FYE_PLUS_6M` the server advances six calendar months with the same day clamping and performs no day arithmetic at all. Under `CALENDAR_MAY_15` the deadline ignores the fiscal month entirely and falls on May 15 of the calendar year following the year of the fiscal year end date, at midnight UTC. The pinned order is observable: for a December fiscal year end in 2025, the four-month-plus-fifteen-day rule advances 2025-12-31 to 2026-04-30 — the day clamped from 31 down to 30 — and then adds fifteen days to reach 2026-05-15. Adding the fifteen days first would have produced 2026-05-15 and then advanced four months to 2026-09-15, which is wrong.

The third step layers extensions on top. `extended_due_date` is `renewal_due_date` plus the `granted_days` of the one extension request on this filing whose `status` is `GRANTED`, and it is null while no request on the filing has been granted. At most one request per filing can hold `GRANTED`, because creating a new request supersedes every earlier one. `effective_due_date` is then simply `extended_due_date` when that is non-null, and `renewal_due_date` otherwise. Every deadline comparison in the system — delinquency sweeping, penalty accrual — reads `effective_due_date`, never the unextended date.

**Assessing the filing fee.** `filing_fee_cents` is computed on creation and recomputed on every submission, and it combines four inputs non-linearly. The first component is a tier selected on the supporting statement's `contributions_cents`: public support below $25,000 selects a base of $25.00; support from $25,000 up to but not including $100,000 selects $50.00; support from $100,000 up to but not including $500,000 selects $150.00; and support of $500,000 or more selects $400.00. (Read in cents, those tier boundaries are 2,500,000, 10,000,000 and 50,000,000, and the four bases are 2,500, 5,000, 15,000 and 40,000 cents.) The second component is the state's `online_surcharge_cents`, which defaults to 0 and is added only when **both** the organization's `has_online_donation_page` and the state's `online_presence_counts` are true; if either flag is false the surcharge contributes nothing. The third component is the state's `per_contract_fee_cents`, which defaults to 0, multiplied by the number of that organization's fundraising contracts whose `status` is `FILED` or `LATE_FILED` and whose `state_codes` array contains this filing's state; contracts still in `DRAFT` and contracts already `VOIDED` are excluded, and the count is taken at the moment of evaluation with no time window.

Those three components are summed into a raw amount, and only then are the last two operations applied, in this order. When `registration_type` is `INITIAL`, the raw amount is multiplied by 1.5 — an initial filing costs half again what a renewal costs — and the product is rounded half up to whole cents; a renewal skips the multiplier entirely. Finally the amount is clamped between the state's two bounds: take the larger of the amount and `base_filing_fee_cents` (default 2,500 cents), then take the smaller of that result and `filing_fee_cap_cents` (default 30,000 cents). The order matters whenever the cap binds — clamping before applying the multiplier would produce a different fee.

**Supporting documents.** A compliance officer attaches supporting documents to a filing while it is in `DRAFT` or `DEFICIENT`; an attempt to attach to a filing in any other status reports `ERR_INVALID_REGISTRATION_STATE`. Each `RegistrationDocument` names the `registration_id` it belongs to, a `doc_type` drawn from `GOVERNING_DOCUMENT`, `IRS_DETERMINATION_LETTER`, `FINANCIAL_STATEMENT`, `OFFICER_DISCLOSURE_FORM`, `REVIEW_REPORT` and `AUDIT_OPINION`, the `prepared_by_officer_id` of the officer who prepared it, and a `page_count` of at least 1. Its `review_status` is server-owned and begins at `ATTACHED`; the regulator's adjudication later moves it to `ACCEPTED` or `REJECTED`. The `prepared_by_officer_id` is not bookkeeping — it is the input to the segregation-of-duties rule in §3.4, and it is why the document listing can be filtered by `prepared_by_officer_id` as well as by `registration_id`, `doc_type` and `review_status`.

The filing listing itself can be narrowed by `organization_id`, `state_code`, `status`, `fiscal_year`, `financial_statement_id`, `supersedes_registration_id` and `certified_by_officer_id`, the last of which lets an examiner pull every filing a particular officer has certified. For a whole-organization view there is a computed calendar capability, optionally narrowed to one `fiscal_year`, which returns `organization_id`, an `entry_count`, an `entries` collection carrying one entry per registration with that entry's `state_code`, `fiscal_year`, `renewal_due_date`, `effective_due_date`, `status` and `required_financial_attestation`, plus a `next_effective_due_date` naming the soonest deadline still ahead and a `delinquent_state_codes` list naming the states where the charity is currently delinquent. Entries are ordered by `effective_due_date` ascending, with `state_code` ascending breaking ties.

### 3.4. Certification, Submission and the Regulator's Adjudication

Submission is the one operation in this system reserved for a certifying officer acting personally, because it is the point at which a human attests to the packet under penalty of perjury. The request carries at most an optional `certification_note`; everything else the server derives from the filing itself and from the acting `X-User-ID`. A filing advances `DRAFT` → `SUBMITTED` on its first pass and `DEFICIENT` → `SUBMITTED` on every subsequent pass through the revision loop, and both directions enforce an identical set of preconditions.

Those preconditions are, in the order §2.11 fixes: the acting `CertifyingOfficer` must resolve to an officer disclosure of *this* organization whose `is_certifying_officer` is true, or the request is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`; the filing must currently be in `DRAFT` or `DEFICIENT`, or it is `ERR_INVALID_REGISTRATION_STATE`; the organization's `solicitation_posture` must not be `HALTED`, or it is `ERR_SOLICITATION_HALTED`; no document attached to this filing may carry a `prepared_by_officer_id` equal to the acting officer's identifier, or it is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; every officer disclosure of the organization must have `disclosure_status` of `COMPLETE`, or it is `ERR_OFFICER_DISCLOSURE_INCOMPLETE`; the linked financial statement must still be `CURRENT`, or it is `ERR_FINANCIAL_STATEMENT_SUPERSEDED`; the required document set must be complete, or it is `ERR_REQUIRED_DOCUMENT_MISSING`; and the statement's `assurance_level` must be at or above `required_financial_attestation` on the ordered scale where `NONE` is weakest, `REVIEW` sits above it and `AUDIT` is strongest, or it is `ERR_ASSURANCE_LEVEL_INSUFFICIENT`. The state's profile must also still exist and still have `registration_required` true. Note the interaction the ladder creates deliberately: a segregation-of-duties breach surfaces before an incomplete-disclosure problem, and both surface before the document-set and assurance checks, so a packet with several defects reports the earliest rung rather than the most alarming one.

The required document set is itself derived. Every filing needs a document of `doc_type` `GOVERNING_DOCUMENT` and one of `doc_type` `FINANCIAL_STATEMENT`. A filing whose `required_financial_attestation` is `REVIEW` additionally needs a `REVIEW_REPORT`, and a filing whose requirement is `AUDIT` additionally needs an `AUDIT_OPINION`. A filing whose requirement is `NONE` needs neither. The remaining document types, `IRS_DETERMINATION_LETTER` and `OFFICER_DISCLOSURE_FORM`, are permitted attachments but are never required by this check.

A successful submission stamps `submitted_at` with the current logical clock, stamps `certified_by_officer_id` with the acting officer's identifier, recomputes `filing_fee_cents` and re-derives `required_financial_attestation` against the linked statement's current numbers, and increments the filing's `version` by one. When the submission is closing a revision loop — that is, when it comes out of `DEFICIENT` — it also clears `deficiency_reasons` back to an empty list. What it deliberately does not do is reset `deficiency_round_count`: that counter accumulates across every round of the loop, so a filing on its third pass still shows a count of 2 from its two prior rejections.

A state regulator then adjudicates, supplying a `decision` that is either `APPROVE` or `DEFICIENT`, and the two branches of that fork have entirely disjoint effects.

An `APPROVE` decision drives `SUBMITTED` → `REGISTERED`. Before it lands, the coverage-overlap rule of §3.3 is re-evaluated against sibling filings in `SUBMITTED`, `REGISTERED`, `DELINQUENT` or `CEASED`, reporting `ERR_COVERAGE_PERIOD_OVERLAP` on a genuine overlap, and where `supersedes_registration_id` is non-null its target is re-verified to belong to the same organization and state and to sit in `REGISTERED` or `DELINQUENT`, reporting `ERR_SUPERSEDE_TARGET_INVALID` otherwise. The transition then sets `registered_at` to the current logical clock, sets `review_status` to `ACCEPTED` on every document of the filing, and refreshes the organization's `registered_state_count` and `ceased_state_codes`. Where the approved filing names a supersede target, a three-link cascade runs. The first link expires the predecessor: a target in `REGISTERED` moves `REGISTERED` → `EXPIRED` and the organization's `registered_state_count` drops by one, while a target already swept into delinquency moves `DELINQUENT` → `EXPIRED` and it is `delinquent_registration_count` that drops by one instead. Either way the predecessor's `version` increments. The second link, caused by the first, cures the predecessor's enforcement history: every enforcement action whose `registration_id` is that expired predecessor and whose `status` is `OPEN`, `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED` or `ESCALATED` transitions to `CURED`. Filing the renewal is therefore itself a cure path for a lapsed predecessor. The third link, caused by the second, recomputes the organization's `penalty_outstanding_cents`, `delinquent_registration_count` and `filing_posture`, and increments `Organization.version` by one.

A `DEFICIENT` decision drives `SUBMITTED` → `DEFICIENT` and requires a non-empty `deficiency_reasons` array in the request; an empty or absent array is a request-shape failure. The transition records that array on the filing verbatim, increments `deficiency_round_count` by one, sets `review_status` to `REJECTED` on every document of the filing, and increments the filing's `version` by one. It touches none of the organization's aggregates: `registered_state_count`, `penalty_outstanding_cents`, `solicitation_posture` and `filing_posture` are all left exactly as they were, because a rejected packet was never counted as a registration in the first place.

Withdrawal is the compensating action for approval, is reserved for a compliance officer, and requires a `withdraw_reason`. It is available from exactly two statuses, and their bookkeeping differs. A `REGISTERED` filing moves `REGISTERED` → `WITHDRAWN`, decrements the organization's `registered_state_count` by one, recomputes `filing_posture`, and increments `Organization.version` by one. A `DRAFT` filing moves `DRAFT` → `WITHDRAWN` and leaves `registered_state_count` untouched, precisely because a draft was never counted. Both variants increment the filing's own `version` by one, and both are blocked while any enforcement action against that filing is still live: if any action with this `registration_id` sits in `OPEN`, `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED` or `ESCALATED`, the withdrawal is refused. A filing in any other status — `SUBMITTED`, `DEFICIENT`, `EXPIRED`, `DELINQUENT` or `CEASED` — cannot be withdrawn at all and reports `ERR_INVALID_REGISTRATION_STATE`. `EXPIRED` and `WITHDRAWN` are the two terminal statuses of a filing; no transition leads out of either.

### 3.5. Extension Requests and the Renewal Calendar

Some states entertain a request to push a renewal deadline back; many do not. A compliance officer asks for one by naming the `registration_id` of the filing whose deadline is at issue, a `basis` of either `IRS_990_EXTENSION` (the charity has an extension of its federal return) or `STATE_HARDSHIP`, a `requested_days` count of at least 1, and the `expected_registration_version` that §2.10 requires. The request is born in `status` `PENDING` with `granted_days` at 0 and `decided_at` null.

Two gates apply before the request may even be recorded. The state must actually allow extensions — its `extension_allowed` must be true (the default is false) and its `extension_max_days` must be at least 1 (the default is 0) — or the request is rejected with `ERR_EXTENSION_NOT_ALLOWED`; the same check is repeated when a regulator later grants the request, so a state whose configuration is corrected between request and decision cannot slip a grant through. And the organization's `filing_posture` must be `CURRENT`: a charity already carrying a delinquent filing anywhere may not ask for more time, and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_FILING_POSTURE_DELINQUENT`.

Creating a request supersedes the filing's extension history. Every earlier request on the same `registration_id` in `PENDING` or in `GRANTED` transitions to `SUPERSEDED` with `decided_at` stamped at the current logical clock and its own `version` incremented. Superseding a `PENDING` request has no effect on the deadline, since none had been granted. Superseding a `GRANTED` request, on the other hand, clears the filing's `extended_due_date` back to null, recomputes `effective_due_date` — which therefore snaps back to the unextended `renewal_due_date` — and increments the filing's `version` by one. This silent deadline reset is exactly why the create is version-guarded: without it, a client that had read a comfortable extended deadline could destroy it while believing it was merely asking for a little more.

A state regulator resolves a pending request with a `decision` of `GRANT` or `DENY`; a request whose `status` is anything other than `PENDING` reports `ERR_EXTENSION_ALREADY_DECIDED`. A `GRANT` additionally requires that the linked filing be in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `DEFICIENT` or `REGISTERED` — a filing already expired, delinquent, ceased or withdrawn is past extending. The grant length is then derived from the `basis`. Under `IRS_990_EXTENSION` the server grants the smaller of 180 days and the state's `extension_max_days`, and `requested_days` is ignored entirely — a charity riding its federal extension gets the statutory maximum the state permits, no more and no less. Under `STATE_HARDSHIP` the server grants the smaller of `requested_days` and `extension_max_days`. The resulting figure is written to `granted_days`, `status` becomes `GRANTED`, `decided_at` takes the current logical clock, and the linked filing has its `extended_due_date` set to `renewal_due_date` plus `granted_days`, its `effective_due_date` recomputed, and its `version` incremented; the request's own `version` increments too.

A `DENY` sets `status` to `DENIED` and `decided_at` to the current logical clock, increments the request's `version`, and leaves the filing's `extended_due_date` exactly as it was — a denial never disturbs a deadline, extended or not. There is also a third, system-driven route into `DENIED`, described in §3.9: when the delinquency sweep pushes an organization's `filing_posture` to `DELINQUENT`, every `PENDING` request anywhere on that organization's filings is denied in the same pass, with `decided_at` set to the sweep's clock. `GRANTED`, `DENIED` and `SUPERSEDED` are all terminal; nothing leaves them. A filing's extension history can be listed in creation order and narrowed by `status`, which is how an examiner distinguishes a live grant from a superseded one.

### 3.6. The Professional Fundraiser Registry and Contract Filing

Firms retained to raise money are regulated in their own right. A platform administrator enters a firm into the registry with its `legal_name`, a `fundraiser_type` of `PROFESSIONAL_SOLICITOR`, `FUNDRAISING_COUNSEL` or `COMMERCIAL_COVENTURER`, a `bond_expires_at` instant for its surety bond, and optionally a `bond_amount_cents` that defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A new firm's derived `registry_status` reads `UNREGISTERED`. Thereafter that field is a pure function of the firm's state registrations: `REGISTERED` when at least one of them is `ACTIVE`, `LAPSED` when the firm has at least one registration but none of them is `ACTIVE`, and `UNREGISTERED` only while the firm has no registrations at all — a distinction that matters, because a firm that never registered and a firm whose registrations all expired are treated differently by examiners.

A `FundraiserAgent` registers its own firm state by state, naming the `fundraiser_id` (which must equal the acting `X-User-ID`, or the request is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`), the `state_code`, the `bond_on_file_cents` actually filed with that state, and the `expires_at` instant of the state registration. Two constraints apply. The bond must be adequate: `bond_on_file_cents` must be at least the state's `fundraiser_bond_minimum_cents` (which defaults to 0), **and** the firm's own `bond_expires_at` must be strictly after the `expires_at` of the registration being created — a bond that lapses mid-registration is no bond at all. Either failure reports `ERR_BOND_INSUFFICIENT`. And a firm may hold only one live registration per state: an existing `ACTIVE` registration for the same firm and `state_code` makes the second attempt `ERR_DUPLICATE_FUNDRAISER_REGISTRATION`. A successful registration is born `ACTIVE` and immediately refreshes the firm's `registry_status`. `LAPSED` is the only other value of a registration's `status` and it is terminal; the sweep of §3.9 is what puts it there. A firm's registrations can be listed in creation order and narrowed by `state_code` and by `status`.

Contracts between a charity and a firm are drafted by the charity's compliance officer, who supplies `organization_id`, `fundraiser_id`, a non-empty `state_codes` array of the states where the contract will be performed, `contract_start` and `contract_end` bounding the term (the end strictly after the start), and a `compensation_type` of `FLAT_FEE`, `PERCENT_OF_GROSS` or `HOURLY`. Two optional money fields carry the terms: `percent_of_gross`, a fraction between 0 and 1 inclusive that defaults to 0 and must be greater than 0 when the compensation type is `PERCENT_OF_GROSS`, and `flat_fee_cents`, defaulting to 0, never negative, and required to be greater than 0 when the compensation type is `FLAT_FEE`. An `HOURLY` contract needs neither. The contract is born in `status` `DRAFT` with `filed_at` null and `late_filing_surcharge_cents` at 0.

The filing deadline is derived on creation and the rule is deliberately unforgiving in multi-state contracts: `filing_due_date` is `contract_start` plus the **smallest** `fundraiser_contract_filing_days` among the states listed in `state_codes`, counted in whole days. Since that parameter defaults to 10 days, a contract spanning a ten-day state and a thirty-day state must be filed within ten days of its start. Beyond `filing_due_date` there is a further sixty days of grace — the hard stop — after which no filing is accepted at all.

Filing is performed by the firm's own agent, and it is the agent's `X-User-ID` that must match the contract's `fundraiser_id`. Three conditions gate the operation. The contract must still be in `DRAFT`, or the attempt reports `ERR_INVALID_CONTRACT_STATE`. Every state in `state_codes` must have an `ACTIVE` registration for this firm, or it reports `ERR_FUNDRAISER_NOT_REGISTERED_IN_STATE`. And the logical clock must be at or before `filing_due_date` plus the sixty-day hard stop, or it reports `ERR_CONTRACT_FILING_WINDOW_CLOSED`. Within those bounds the timeliness of the filing selects between two outcomes with identical structure and different consequences. A clock at or before `filing_due_date` drives `DRAFT` → `FILED`, stamps `filed_at` with the current logical clock, and sets `late_filing_surcharge_cents` to 0. A clock strictly after `filing_due_date` but still within the sixty-day grace drives `DRAFT` → `LATE_FILED`, stamps `filed_at` identically, and sets `late_filing_surcharge_cents` to $100.00 — 10,000 cents — the flat late-contract surcharge. Both outcomes then recompute `compliance_status` on every disclosure statement of the client charity whose `state_code` appears in the contract's `state_codes`, because a newly filed solicitor engagement can add a required clause to material that was compliant a moment earlier, and both increment the contract's `version` by one.

A contract's `status` may also be driven to `VOIDED`, which is terminal, by the lapse cascade of §3.9; the sweep does this from `FILED` and from `LATE_FILED` alike. Contracts can be listed and narrowed by `organization_id`, `fundraiser_id`, `status` and `state_code`, the last matching against any element of the `state_codes` array.

### 3.7. Charitable Sales Promotions and Co-Venture Settlement

A commercial co-venture — "one dollar from every jar goes to the food bank" — is a cause-marketing arrangement that most states regulate as a distinct filing. A compliance officer creates one by naming the benefiting charity's `organization_id`, the `coventurer_id` of the commercial partner in the fundraiser registry, a non-empty `state_codes` array of the states where the promotion runs, and the promotion window as `promo_start` and `promo_end`. Unlike a coverage interval, the promotion window is **closed at both ends**: both endpoints are inside it, and `promo_end` may equal `promo_start` for a single-day promotion. Three optional economics fields default to 0 and may not be negative: `guaranteed_minimum_cents`, the benefit the charity receives regardless of how little sells; `per_unit_cents`, the benefit per unit sold; and `projected_units`, the co-venturer's own sales projection. The promotion is born in `status` `DRAFT` with `actual_units` at 0, `remitted_cents` at 0, `final_benefit_cents` at 0 and `shortfall_cents` at 0.

Creation is blocked while the organization's `solicitation_posture` is `HALTED`, reporting `ERR_SOLICITATION_HALTED`. Creation also enforces a separation rule between a co-venturer's consecutive promotions, so that a single commercial partner cannot run back-to-back campaigns in the same state under the guise of separate filings. For every state shared with an existing promotion of the same `coventurer_id` whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `FILED` or `ACTIVE`, the two closed windows must be separated by strictly more than that state's `coventure_buffer_days` (default 0). Concretely, the new promotion is rejected with `ERR_PROMOTION_WINDOW_CONFLICT` when the new `promo_start` falls at or before the existing `promo_end` advanced by the buffer **and** the existing `promo_start` falls at or before the new `promo_end` advanced by the buffer; when either half of that condition fails the windows are far enough apart and the create proceeds. Promotions already `SETTLED`, `DEFICIENT_REMITTANCE` or `HALTED` are ignored by the check.

Two fields are derived at creation. `disclosure_clause_required` is true when any state in `state_codes` has `coventure_filing_required` true (the default is false), and it is the flag a compliance officer reads to know whether solicitation material for this campaign needs a percentage-to-charity clause. `projected_benefit_cents` is the forward-looking benefit estimate: the larger of `guaranteed_minimum_cents` and the product of `per_unit_cents` and `projected_units`. Both terms are integer cents, so no rounding arises.

Filing the promotion drives `DRAFT` → `FILED`. It is available only from `DRAFT` (anything else is `ERR_INVALID_PROMOTION_STATE`), it requires that the logical clock be strictly before `promo_start` — a promotion already underway cannot be filed, and the attempt reports `ERR_PROMOTION_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN` — and it requires the co-venturer to hold an `ACTIVE` fundraiser registration in every state of `state_codes` whose profile has `coventure_filing_required` true, reporting `ERR_FUNDRAISER_NOT_REGISTERED_IN_STATE` otherwise. States that do not require a co-venture filing impose no registration requirement on the partner. The transition recomputes `projected_benefit_cents`, recomputes `compliance_status` on the client charity's disclosure statements for the promotion's states, and increments the promotion's `version`.

Activation drives `FILED` → `ACTIVE` and is the strictest gate in the system on solicitation posture: it demands `solicitation_posture` of exactly `CLEAR`, so a charity under a single non-domicile cease order — posture `RESTRICTED` — cannot start a new promotion, and the attempt reports `ERR_SOLICITATION_RESTRICTED`. The logical clock must also lie inside the closed window, at or after `promo_start` and at or before `promo_end`, or the attempt reports `ERR_PROMOTION_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN`. Activation from any status other than `FILED` is `ERR_INVALID_PROMOTION_STATE`.

Settlement is reserved for a certifying officer of the benefiting charity — the acting `X-User-ID` must resolve to an officer disclosure of that organization with `is_certifying_officer` true — and it must not be the same officer who certified that charity's filing for the state in question, which is the second limb of `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The request supplies `actual_units` and `remitted_cents`, both non-negative; a negative value is a request-shape failure reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The first settlement of a promotion requires the logical clock to be strictly after `promo_end`, since units cannot be counted before the campaign closes, and a premature attempt reports `ERR_PROMOTION_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN`.

The settlement then recomputes the benefit on the *actual* basis: `final_benefit_cents` is the larger of `guaranteed_minimum_cents` and the product of `per_unit_cents` and `actual_units`. This is the same formula as the projection with one operand swapped, and the swap is consequential — the guaranteed minimum can bind at settlement where it never bound at filing. With a guaranteed minimum of 500,000 cents, a per-unit benefit of 250 cents, 3,000 projected units and 1,600 actual units, the projection is 750,000 cents (the per-unit term dominating) while the settlement is 500,000 cents (the guarantee binding), from identical stored inputs.

The comparison of `remitted_cents` against `final_benefit_cents` forks the outcome, and both branches record the same fields. When the amount remitted is greater than or equal to the benefit owed, the promotion moves `ACTIVE` → `SETTLED`: `actual_units` and `remitted_cents` are written from the request, `final_benefit_cents` is written from the actual-basis computation, and `shortfall_cents` is set to 0. When the amount remitted falls short of the benefit owed, the promotion moves `ACTIVE` → `DEFICIENT_REMITTANCE` instead: the same three fields are written, and `shortfall_cents` is set to the benefit owed minus the amount remitted. A shortfall is emphatically **not** an error — there is no rejection code for under-remittance, and implementations must not invent one; the deficiency is a state, and the state is the record of the debt. A deficient remittance is cured by settling again: from `DEFICIENT_REMITTANCE` a certifying officer may resubmit, and when the newly supplied `remitted_cents` is at least the `final_benefit_cents` recomputed from the newly supplied `actual_units`, the promotion overwrites all three figures, resets `shortfall_cents` to 0 and moves `DEFICIENT_REMITTANCE` → `SETTLED`. This cure path is not gated on the promotion window, since the window has necessarily already closed. Both `SETTLED` and `HALTED` are terminal.

A promotion can also be stopped by force. When an escalation elsewhere in the system moves the charity's `solicitation_posture` to `RESTRICTED` or `HALTED`, every promotion of that charity in `FILED` or in `ACTIVE` whose `state_codes` contains the newly ceased state is driven to `HALTED`, each incrementing its own `version`; §3.9 sets out that cascade in full. Promotions can be listed and narrowed by `organization_id`, `coventurer_id`, `status` and `state_code`, and a single read returns both benefit figures — the projection and the settlement — side by side.

### 3.8. Disclosure Language in Solicitation Materials

States dictate what a solicitation must say, not merely who may say it. A `DisclosureStatement` registers one piece of material for one jurisdiction: `organization_id`, the `state_code` whose disclosure law governs it, a `material_type` of `WEB`, `PRINT`, `PHONE_SCRIPT` or `EMAIL`, and `included_clauses`, the array of clauses actually present in the copy. Clause identifiers come from a canonical list of five, and that list also fixes the order in which every derived clause array is emitted: `REGISTRATION_DISCLOSURE`, then `FINANCIAL_STATEMENT_AVAILABILITY`, then `PAID_SOLICITOR_IDENTITY`, then `TAX_DEDUCTIBILITY`, then `PERCENT_TO_CHARITY`.

The requirement itself is resolved from three sources. The base is the state's `disclosure_required_clauses`, which defaults to an empty list. To that base the server adds `PAID_SOLICITOR_IDENTITY` whenever the charity has a fundraising contract whose `status` is `FILED` or `LATE_FILED`, whose `state_codes` contains this statement's state, and whose firm's `fundraiser_type` is `PROFESSIONAL_SOLICITOR` — the identity of a paid solicitor must be disclosed only once one is actually engaged in that state, and a `FUNDRAISING_COUNSEL` or `COMMERCIAL_COVENTURER` engagement does not trigger it. It adds `PERCENT_TO_CHARITY` whenever the charity has a co-venture promotion whose `status` is `FILED` or `ACTIVE` and whose `state_codes` contains this state; promotions in `DRAFT`, `SETTLED`, `DEFICIENT_REMITTANCE` or `HALTED` are ignored, as are contracts in `DRAFT` or `VOIDED`. Both additions are idempotent: a clause the state already required is not duplicated.

The result is written to `required_clauses` in canonical order. `missing_clauses` is then the members of `required_clauses` absent from `included_clauses`, again in canonical order, and `compliance_status` is `COMPLIANT` when `missing_clauses` is empty and `NON_COMPLIANT` otherwise. The empty case is worth spelling out: in a state with no base clauses and no live solicitor or promotion engagement, `required_clauses` is empty, `missing_clauses` is empty, and the material is `COMPLIANT` even though `included_clauses` is itself empty — there is nothing the law demanded and nothing was omitted.

Because the requirement depends on contracts and promotions rather than on the material alone, previously compliant copy can fall out of compliance without anyone touching it. The server therefore re-resolves `required_clauses`, `missing_clauses` and `compliance_status` on the affected statements whenever a fundraising contract is filed or voided, and whenever a co-venture promotion is filed, in each case for those of the charity's statements whose `state_code` appears in the triggering record's `state_codes`. A charity's registered materials can be listed in creation order and narrowed by `state_code` and by `compliance_status`, which is the working queue a compliance officer uses to fix copy.

### 3.9. The Compliance Sweep, Enforcement and Remediation

Nothing in this system becomes delinquent on its own. Delinquency is discovered by a sweep, which a platform administrator or a state regulator runs at the current logical clock and which may optionally be narrowed to a single `state_code`. The sweep makes two independent passes, and within each pass candidates are processed in `created_at` ascending order so that the resulting record identifiers are deterministic.

The first pass looks for lapsed renewals. A registration in `REGISTERED` becomes a candidate when its `effective_due_date` is strictly before the sweep's clock — a filing due exactly now is not yet late — and when no renewal has already covered it, that is, when no registration for the same organization and state exists with `registration_type` of `RENEWAL`, `status` of `REGISTERED`, and `supersedes_registration_id` pointing at this filing. Each candidate moves `REGISTERED` → `DELINQUENT` and increments its own `version`, and a three-link cascade follows. The first link issues enforcement: a new enforcement action is created with `action_type` of `DELINQUENCY_NOTICE`, `status` of `OPEN`, `registration_id` naming the swept filing, `penalty_cents` derived by the late-penalty accrual at the sweep's clock, and `cure_deadline` set to the sweep's clock plus the state's `cure_period_days` (default 30 days) counted in whole days. The second link, caused by the first, raises the organization's `penalty_outstanding_cents` by exactly the new action's `penalty_cents`, raises `delinquent_registration_count` by one, recomputes `filing_posture` to `DELINQUENT`, and increments `Organization.version` by one. The third link fires only where that recomputation actually flipped the posture to `DELINQUENT`: every extension request in `PENDING` on *any* registration of that organization — not merely the swept one — transitions `PENDING` → `DENIED` with `decided_at` set to the sweep clock and its own `version` incremented. A charity that has gone delinquent forfeits all its pending pleas for more time.

The second pass looks for expired fundraiser credentials, and it is entirely independent of the first. A fundraiser registration in `ACTIVE` whose `expires_at` is at or before the sweep's clock — inclusive here, unlike the renewal test — moves `ACTIVE` → `LAPSED`, which refreshes the firm's `registry_status`, and increments its own `version`. Caused by that, every fundraising contract of the same firm whose `status` is `FILED` or `LATE_FILED` and whose `state_codes` contains the lapsed state is driven to `VOIDED`, each incrementing its own `version`. And caused in turn by each voiding, the client charity's disclosure statements for those states have `required_clauses`, `missing_clauses` and `compliance_status` re-resolved: a voided solicitor engagement can *remove* a clause that was previously required, so material that read as non-compliant may become compliant in the same sweep.

The sweep reports what it did: a `swept_at` instant, a `delinquent_registration_count` of registrations newly made delinquent, and five collections of identifiers — `delinquent_registration_ids`, `enforcement_action_ids` for the notices it issued, `lapsed_fundraiser_registration_ids`, `voided_contract_ids`, and `denied_extension_request_ids`.

A state regulator may also issue an enforcement action directly, naming the `organization_id`, the `registration_id` of the filing at issue, and an `action_type` of `DELINQUENCY_NOTICE`, `PENALTY_ASSESSMENT` or `CEASE_SOLICITATION_ORDER`. The action's `state_code` is copied from the linked filing rather than supplied, `issued_at` is stamped with the current logical clock, and `cure_deadline` is `issued_at` plus that state's `cure_period_days` in whole days. The penalty amount has two sources: an optional `penalty_cents_override` in the request is taken verbatim, and when it is omitted the server derives `penalty_cents` from the late-penalty accrual against the linked filing at the current clock. Either way, issuance raises the organization's `penalty_outstanding_cents` by exactly `penalty_cents` and increments `Organization.version` by one. A new action is born in `status` `OPEN` with `settlement_total_cents` at 0 and `remediation_note` null.

**Accruing the late penalty.** The accrual reads one state profile, the linked filing's `effective_due_date`, and the logical clock, and nothing else — no other enforcement action participates. First, the number of whole days late is the elapsed time from `effective_due_date` to the clock, discarding any partial day; when the clock is at or before the deadline the days late is zero or negative and `penalty_cents` is 0. Otherwise the number of months late is the days late divided by thirty and rounded **up** to the next whole number, so a single day late is already one month. Then, for each month index counting from 1 up to the number of months late, that month's charge is the state's `late_fee_base_cents` (default 2,000 cents) multiplied by one plus the state's `late_fee_escalation_pct` (default 0.1) times one less than the month index — so the first month is the plain base amount, the second is the base plus one escalation step, the third the base plus two steps, and so on. **Each month's charge is rounded half up to whole cents individually, before any summation.** The rounded monthly charges are summed, and the total is capped at the state's `late_fee_cap_cents` (default 50,000 cents) by taking the smaller of the two. The rounding order is observable and must not be re-arranged: with a base of 2,004 cents, an escalation of 0.125 and four months late, rounding each month first gives 2,004 plus 2,255 plus 2,505 plus 2,756, which is 9,520 cents, whereas summing the unrounded monthly amounts and rounding once gives 9,519 cents.

A compliance officer answers an action by submitting a remediation packet with a `remediation_note`. This is available from `OPEN` and from `ESCALATED` — an escalated cease-solicitation order can be remediated repeatedly, which is what makes the escalation loop cyclic — and in both cases the logical clock must be at or before the action's `cure_deadline`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_CURE_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. From any other status the request reports `ERR_INVALID_ENFORCEMENT_STATE`. The transition sets `status` to `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED`, records the supplied `remediation_note`, stamps `settlement_total_cents`, and increments the action's `version` by one. It deliberately leaves `penalty_outstanding_cents` alone, because a submitted remediation is still an outstanding matter: the penalty leaves the ledger only when the action is cured or vacated.

**Computing the settlement total.** The stamping is evaluated in a pinned four-step order. First, the penalty subtotal is the sum of `penalty_cents` over every enforcement action sharing this action's `registration_id` whose `status` is `OPEN`, `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED` or `ESCALATED`, this action included; actions in `CURED` or `VACATED` are excluded, and the sum is taken at transition time with no time window. Second, a discount rate is selected **on that subtotal**: a subtotal of $500.00 (50,000 cents) or more earns 20 percent, a subtotal of $200.00 (20,000 cents) or more but under $500.00 earns 10 percent, and anything below $200.00 earns no discount. Third, the reinstatement charge is the state's `reinstatement_fee_cents` (default 12,500 cents) multiplied by one minus that discount rate, rounded half up to whole cents — the single rounding step in this calculation. Fourth, `settlement_total_cents` is the penalty subtotal plus that reinstatement charge. The definition looks circular to a careless reader, who keys the discount on the final total and inflates the discount; the pinned order resolves it. With a penalty subtotal of 18,000 cents and an undiscounted reinstatement fee of 12,500 cents, the correct result is no discount, a reinstatement charge of 12,500 and a settlement total of 30,500 cents — not the 29,250 that keying the tier on the 30,500 total would have produced.

A state regulator disposes of an action with a `decision` of `CURE` or `ESCALATE`, and the two branches have disjoint cascades.

A `CURE` decision is available only from `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED` and only when `settlement_total_cents` is greater than 0 — there is nothing to cure before a settlement has been computed. The action moves to `CURED`, and the linked filing is restored: a filing in `CEASED` moves `CEASED` → `REGISTERED`, and a filing in `DELINQUENT` moves `DELINQUENT` → `REGISTERED`, each incrementing its own `version`. Caused by that restoration, the organization's `ceased_state_codes`, `solicitation_posture`, `delinquent_registration_count`, `filing_posture`, `registered_state_count` and `penalty_outstanding_cents` are all recomputed and `Organization.version` increments by one. The restoration from `CEASED` closes the full cease-order cycle `DELINQUENT` → `CEASED` → `REGISTERED`; the restoration from `DELINQUENT` closes the shorter delinquency cycle. `CURED` is terminal.

An `ESCALATE` decision is available from `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED` and from `OPEN`, and the two entries differ in exactly one precondition. From `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED` it requires only that the linked filing's `status` be `DELINQUENT` or `CEASED`. From `OPEN` it requires the same filing-status condition **and** that the logical clock be strictly after the action's `cure_deadline`, because escalating without giving the charity a chance to answer is only permitted once the cure window has closed; escalating an `OPEN` action while the window is still open reports `ERR_CURE_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. Both entries run an identical chain. The action's `status` becomes `ESCALATED` and its `action_type` is overwritten to `CEASE_SOLICITATION_ORDER`, whatever it was before. The first link ceases the filing: a linked filing in `DELINQUENT` moves `DELINQUENT` → `CEASED` and increments its `version`. The second link, caused by the first, adds that filing's `state_code` to the organization's `ceased_state_codes`, recomputes `solicitation_posture` from the §2.8 rules, and increments `Organization.version` by one. The third link, caused by the second and firing only where the posture actually became `RESTRICTED` or `HALTED`, halts cause marketing: every co-venture promotion of that organization in `FILED` or in `ACTIVE` whose `state_codes` contains the ceased state moves to `HALTED`. The action's own `version` increments by one.

Vacatur is the compensating action for issuance and requires a non-empty `vacate_reason`; an empty string is a request-shape failure. It is available from `OPEN`, from `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED` and from `ESCALATED`, and every one of the three lowers the organization's `penalty_outstanding_cents` by exactly this action's `penalty_cents`, recomputes `delinquent_registration_count` and `filing_posture`, increments `Organization.version` by one, and increments the action's own `version` by one. The variant from `ESCALATED` does more, because escalation had ceased a filing: it also restores that filing `CEASED` → `REGISTERED` and recomputes `ceased_state_codes`, `solicitation_posture` and `registered_state_count` in addition. Vacating from `OPEN` or from `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED` leaves the filing's own status alone, since neither of those statuses had moved it. `VACATED` is terminal, and vacating an action already `CURED` or `VACATED` reports `ERR_INVALID_ENFORCEMENT_STATE`.

Enforcement actions can be listed and narrowed by `organization_id`, `registration_id`, `status`, `action_type` and `state_code`, and a single read returns the action's `settlement_total_cents` alongside its `penalty_cents`, `issued_at`, `cure_deadline` and `remediation_note`.
