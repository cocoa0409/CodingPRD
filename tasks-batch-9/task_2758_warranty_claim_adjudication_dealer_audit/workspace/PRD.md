# Warranty Claim Adjudication & Dealer Audit API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

An automaker reimburses its franchised dealers for repairs performed under warranty, and the money involved is substantial enough that every step of the process is contested territory. Dealers want their labor rate and parts markup set as high as state law allows, and they want claims paid quickly. The manufacturer wants coverage determined strictly against the vehicle's published warranty terms, wants flat-rate labor times respected, wants the failed parts back for engineering analysis, and wants the right to audit a dealer's repair orders after the fact and charge back whatever the paperwork does not support. State warranty-reimbursement statutes sit on top of all of this, imposing markup ceilings, prescribed methods for establishing labor rates, deemed-approval clocks that punish manufacturer inaction, and appeal windows that protect the dealer.

This document specifies the Warranty Claim Adjudication & Dealer Audit service that arbitrates between those interests. The service is mounted under `/warranty` and owns the full lifecycle: statute configuration and dealer enrollment; labor-rate and parts-markup petitions; claim submission with causal-part identification and a three-part repair narrative; coverage determination against basic and powertrain terms; an automated edit engine that suspends duplicate, overlapping, excessive-time and high-value claims for human review; goodwill authorization with decaying authority limits for repairs the warranty no longer covers; the parts-return program with its non-return debit and its late-return reversal; on-site dealer audits with statistical extrapolation of the sampled error rate across the unsampled population; and the dealer's right to appeal every charged-back finding.

Two structural commitments run through the whole specification and should be read before anything else. First, all money movement against a dealer flows through a single append-only chargeback ledger, and the dealer's running balance and disciplinary posture are defined as functions of that ledger rather than as independently maintained numbers — every reversal appends an offsetting row rather than editing history, so a debit followed by its full reversal must return the dealer to exactly its prior balance and posture with zero drift. Second, several operations deliberately pin the inputs they consumed at the instant they consumed them: an audit's paid denominators, a rate request's statutory ceiling, a claim's provisional price. Later events do not retroactively rewrite those pinned values, and a good deal of the specification below exists to make that pinning observable.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the service must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that user is acting in. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the seven role names below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If both headers are well formed but the named role is not among the roles authorised for the target endpoint (see the table in §4), the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The seven roles are `WarrantyAdmin`, `ClaimAdjudicator`, `AdjudicationManager`, `FieldAuditor`, `AuditManager`, `AppealReviewer`, and `DealerWarrantyAdmin`.

A `WarrantyAdmin` is the manufacturer's warranty program administrator: it publishes all reference data, drives the parts-return program, runs the deemed-approval and non-return sweeps, and pays approved claims. A `ClaimAdjudicator` adjudicates submitted claims against coverage, flat-rate time and the statutory markup cap, and raises goodwill requests for out-of-warranty repairs. An `AdjudicationManager` decides dealer labor-rate and parts-markup petitions and holds the higher goodwill authority limit. A `FieldAuditor` performs the on-site visit and attaches unsupported-claim findings, but may neither issue nor close an audit. An `AuditManager` schedules audits, issues findings together with the extrapolated chargeback, and closes audits. An `AppealReviewer` independently grants, partially grants or denies a dealer's appeal. A `DealerWarrantyAdmin` is a dealership-side administrator who submits, corrects and appeals on behalf of one dealership.

Identity semantics differ between the manufacturer-side roles and the dealer-side role, and the difference is load-bearing. When `X-User-Role` is any of `WarrantyAdmin`, `ClaimAdjudicator`, `AdjudicationManager`, `FieldAuditor`, `AuditManager`, or `AppealReviewer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it where a transition says to (as `submitted_by_user_id` on a rate request, `requested_by_user_id` and `decided_by_user_id` on a goodwill authorization, `lead_auditor_user_id` on an audit, `reviewer_user_id` on an appeal) and compares it against those recorded values for segregation-of-duties purposes, but never compares it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `DealerWarrantyAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting dealership's `Dealer` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Dealer` compare the resource's dealer foreign key to `X-User-ID`.

Dealer scoping is enforced on ten operations: reading one dealer, listing that dealer's ledger entries, reading that dealer's expense analytics, submitting a claim, reading one claim, resubmitting a claim, listing a claim's edits, reading one audit, filing an appeal against an audit, and reading one appeal. In each case the dealer reached from the target resource — the `Dealer` row itself, a claim's `dealer_id`, an audit's `dealer_id`, an appeal's `filed_by_dealer_id`, or the `dealer_id` of the claim behind a parts-return request — must equal `X-User-ID`, and on claim submission and appeal filing the `dealer_id` or `filed_by_dealer_id` the body would establish must equal `X-User-ID` as well. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_DEALER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` controls how many records are skipped before the page begins: it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A value outside these bounds is a body/parameter shape violation and is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION`.

Results on every list endpoint are ordered by `created_at` ascending. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit` and `offset` as they were resolved for that request, `total_count` giving the number of records matching the filters before pagination was applied, and `has_more`, which is true exactly when records matching the filters remain beyond the current page.

### 2.4. Record Identity, Timestamps and Version Counters

Every persisted record carries a server-minted `id`, which is a bare UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp in ISO 8601 UTC form with exactly three fractional-second digits. Identifiers are never decision inputs: no rule anywhere in this specification branches on the content of an `id`.

Most records additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp refreshed on every mutation of that record, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the record is created and is incremented by exactly 1 on every mutation. The records that carry both are `Dealer`, `LaborRateRequest`, `WarrantyClaim`, `PartsReturnRequest`, `DealerAudit`, `AuditFinding`, `AuditAppeal` and `GoodwillAuthorization`.

Six record kinds are configuration or append-only history and therefore carry only `id` and `created_at` — both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from them entirely. Those are `StateLawProfile`, `Vehicle`, `LaborOperation`, `PartCatalogItem`, `ClaimEdit` and `ChargebackLedgerEntry`. A `StateLawProfile` in particular is never mutated after creation, and a `ClaimEdit` is only ever amended by stamping its `resolved_at`, which the append-only ledger analogy tolerates because the row's substance does not change.

When one operation mutates several records, each mutated record's own `version` advances by 1 and its own `updated_at` is refreshed. The cascades described in §3 are explicit about which records they touch, and every touched record's counter moves.

### 2.5. Monetary Units, Percentages and Rounding

All money in this system is expressed in whole cents as integers. There are no fractional cents anywhere: every calculation that could produce one resolves it immediately, and the rounding method is always round half up — a value exactly at the midpoint rounds away from zero to the larger magnitude.

Percentages and ratios — `markup_cap_pct`, `approved_parts_markup_pct`, `requested_parts_markup_pct`, `policy_adjustment_pct`, `out_of_warranty_margin_pct`, `error_rate`, `extrapolation_factor`, `outlier_ratio` — are decimal fractions, not whole-number percents, so a forty percent markup is expressed as 0.40. Markup percentages are carried to two decimal places. The three derived ratios `out_of_warranty_margin_pct`, `error_rate` and `outlier_ratio` are each rounded half up to four decimal places at the moment they are computed, and the rounded value is what every downstream step consumes. `extrapolation_factor` is not rounded at all; it is one of exactly three literal tier values.

Where a formula in §3 says a product or quotient is rounded, that rounding happens at exactly the point stated and nowhere else. Two places in this specification pin the rounding *order* rather than merely the precision, and both are called out explicitly in the narrative that defines them: the parts side of claim pricing, and the extrapolated portion of an audit chargeback.

Labor hours are decimal numbers carried to one decimal place. Odometer readings, mileage limits, month counts, day counts and record counts are exact integers and are never rounded.

### 2.6. The Logical Clock and Statutory Windows

Every deadline, retention window, appeal window and analytics window in this system is evaluated against a server-held logical clock, never against real wall-clock time. Before the clock has ever been set, it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time. An administrative test-driving capability sets the clock to a supplied `current_utc` instant and returns that instant; setting the clock mutates no entity and fires no sweep implicitly.

Three deadline fields are derived from the clock and from statute parameters. A parts-return request's `due_date` is its own `created_at` advanced by the causal part's `retention_days` days. An audit's `appeal_deadline` is its `findings_issued_at` advanced by the governing state's `appeal_window_days` days, and it is recomputed from scratch every time findings are re-issued. A rate request becomes eligible for deemed approval once the clock reaches its `created_at` advanced by the governing state's `deemed_approved_days` days.

All three of those comparisons are inclusive: a sweep or a deadline check that says "at or past" fires when the clock sits exactly on the boundary instant, and the appeal-window check admits an appeal filed exactly on the deadline instant.

### 2.7. Nested Path Resolution and Reference Integrity

Several endpoints address a child resource through its parent's path. Path parameters are always resolved strictly left to right. The outer identifier is looked up first, and if it names no live record the request fails immediately with that entity's not-found code — the inner identifier is not consulted at all. Only once the outer record resolves is the inner identifier examined, and it must both name a live record and actually belong to the outer record named in the path.

The twelve not-found codes are `ERR_STATE_LAW_PROFILE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DEALER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VEHICLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LABOR_OPERATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CATALOG_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LABOR_RATE_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PARTS_RETURN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AUDIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FINDING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPEAL_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_GOODWILL_AUTH_NOT_FOUND`, all HTTP 404.

The same left-to-right discipline governs foreign keys supplied in a request body. Each such reference is validated in the fixed order the endpoint's input list declares it, and the first unresolvable reference decides the error. On claim submission that order is `dealer_id`, then `vehicle_id`, then `labor_operation_id`, then `causal_part_id`, so a request naming both a nonexistent dealer and a nonexistent vehicle reports `ERR_DEALER_NOT_FOUND` and never mentions the vehicle. A `state_law_profile_id` that names no profile yields `ERR_STATE_LAW_PROFILE_NOT_FOUND`; a `resubmission_of_claim_id` or a finding's `claim_id` that names no claim yields `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`; a `causal_part_id` or `part_id` that names no catalog part yields `ERR_CATALOG_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`; and a finding identifier appearing in `contested_finding_ids` or `overturned_finding_ids` that names no finding yields `ERR_FINDING_NOT_FOUND`.

### 2.8. The Chargeback Ledger and Dealer Posture

All money the manufacturer claws back from a dealer, and every reversal of such a claw-back, is recorded as a `ChargebackLedgerEntry`. The ledger is append-only: nothing in this specification ever edits or deletes an existing row, and a reversal is always a new row carrying the opposite sign. Each row names the `dealer_id` whose balance it moves, a `source_type` identifying the mechanism that produced it, a `source_id` holding the identifier of the originating record, an `amount_cents` that is signed and never zero — positive increases the amount the dealer owes the manufacturer, negative reduces it — and a `balance_after_cents` recording the dealer's balance immediately after this row was appended, so that a reader can reconcile the running balance without re-summing the whole ledger. A row also carries a `claim_id` naming the claim whose money moved, which is null on the extrapolation rows because those are not attributable to any single claim.

`source_type` is one of exactly seven values: `PARTS_NON_RETURN`, `PARTS_NO_DEFECT`, `LATE_RETURN_REVERSAL`, `AUDIT_CHARGEBACK_SAMPLE`, `AUDIT_CHARGEBACK_EXTRAPOLATION`, `APPEAL_REVERSAL_SAMPLE` and `APPEAL_REVERSAL_EXTRAPOLATION`. The `source_id` points at a `PartsReturnRequest` for the two parts codes and for `LATE_RETURN_REVERSAL`, at a `DealerAudit` for the two audit chargeback codes, and at an `AuditAppeal` for the two appeal reversal codes.

A dealer's `chargeback_balance_cents` is *defined* as the signed sum of `amount_cents` over every ledger row naming that dealer, and this equality is an invariant that must hold at every observable instant. In practice the server maintains it incrementally: each append adds that row's `amount_cents` to the dealer's current balance, writes the result into the row's `balance_after_cents`, and stores it as the dealer's new `chargeback_balance_cents`. No rounding occurs anywhere in this calculation, so a forward debit of some amount followed by a reversal of exactly that amount returns the balance to its precise pre-debit value. When several rows are appended within a single request they are applied in the order the triggering operation lists them, and each row's `balance_after_cents` reflects the balance after that particular row.

Immediately after each append, and only there, the dealer's `status` is recomputed from the new balance. A balance of at least 200,000 cents — 2,000 dollars — puts the dealer in `SUSPENDED`. Otherwise, a balance of at least 90,000 cents — 900 dollars — puts the dealer in `PROBATION`. Below 900 dollars the dealer is `ACTIVE`. Every edge implied by those three bands is therefore reachable: a debit may drive `ACTIVE → PROBATION`, `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` or `PROBATION → SUSPENDED`, and a reversal may drive `PROBATION → ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED → PROBATION` or `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE`. Because the test is on the balance and not on the size of the movement, a single large debit may take a dealer from `ACTIVE` straight to `SUSPENDED` without ever passing through `PROBATION`, and a recomputation that lands the dealer in the band it was already in is not a transition at all and changes nothing. No posture is terminal. Dealer posture is never set directly by any endpoint; it is only ever a consequence of a ledger append.

### 2.9. Version Pinning on Dependent Writes

Four create operations depend on state they cannot lock, and each therefore requires the client to declare which version of the parent record it was looking at. In all four cases the version field is a mandatory, non-optional body field, and a value that does not exactly equal the parent record's current `version` at the moment of the request is rejected before any state check runs.

Creating a labor-rate request requires `expected_dealer_version` matching the petitioning `Dealer`'s current `version`. If the dealer's version has advanced since the client last read it, the client's view of the dealer's currently approved rate and of any pending petition is stale — an earlier writer may already have created a request that superseded the prior one — and the statutory ceiling this request would compute would be derived from obsolete inputs. The rejection carries `ERR_STALE_DEALER_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

Scheduling a dealer audit likewise requires `expected_dealer_version` matching the audited `Dealer`'s current `version`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_DEALER_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A second audit over a different, non-overlapping period is a perfectly legitimate operation that no state rule would block, so this check is what forces the scheduler to re-read the dealer's posture and `audit_hold` before committing another audit against it.

Creating a goodwill authorization requires `expected_claim_version` matching the target `WarrantyClaim`'s current `version`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CLAIM_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Because a new authorization supersedes any prior one that is still open, a second create would otherwise succeed against a claim whose `provisional_total_cents` — the input to both authority limits — had since been recomputed by a resubmission.

Filing an appeal requires `expected_audit_version` matching the appealed `DealerAudit`'s current `version`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_AUDIT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Since a partially granted or denied decision returns the audit to `FINDINGS_ISSUED` with a fresh deadline, a second appeal round is legitimate, and this check forces the filer to re-read which findings are still charged back and what the current `appeal_deadline` is before filing again.

No other endpoint in this system accepts a version field. Requests to endpoints outside these four must not be expected to carry one, and none of them performs a version comparison.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties

Four decisions may not be made by the person who set them up, and the check is always on `X-User-ID` rather than on role. Adjudicating a claim is refused when the acting user is recorded as `decided_by_user_id` on an existing goodwill authorization for that same claim. Deciding a goodwill authorization is refused when the acting user equals that authorization's `requested_by_user_id`. Deciding an appeal is refused when the acting user equals the appealed audit's `lead_auditor_user_id`. Deciding a labor-rate request is refused when the acting user equals that request's `submitted_by_user_id`. All four refusals carry `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403), and all four are evaluated ahead of any state check, so a conflicted user acting on a record that is also in the wrong state sees the segregation error rather than a state error.

### 2.11. Order of Rejection

Many operations are guarded by several rules at once, and which rule speaks first is part of the contract. Checks are evaluated in the following order, and the first one that fails determines the response; no later check is consulted.

1. Header presence and role validity — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. Role authorisation for the target endpoint — `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
3. Dealer scoping for a `DealerWarrantyAdmin` — `ERR_DEALER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`.
4. Segregation of duties — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
5. Path-parameter existence — the twelve not-found codes.
6. Body foreign-key existence, left to right — the not-found codes again.
7. Body shape: required fields present, enum values inside their declared domains, integers non-negative, decimal numerics parseable, dates in ISO-8601 calendar form, an audit's `period_start` strictly before its `period_end`, `requested_labor_rate_cents` strictly positive, `claimed_labor_hours` strictly positive, `claimed_parts_qty` at least 1 when a causal part is named and exactly 0 when none is, and a finding's `unsupported_amount_cents` non-negative — `ERR_VALIDATION`.
8. Version pinning on the four dependent creates — `ERR_STALE_DEALER_VERSION`, `ERR_STALE_CLAIM_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_AUDIT_VERSION`.
9. State admissibility of the requested transition — `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_ADJUDICABLE`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_PAYABLE`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_VOIDABLE`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_RESUBMITTABLE`, `ERR_CLAIM_STILL_SUSPENDED`, `ERR_PARTS_RETURN_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_AUDIT_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_APPEAL_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_GOODWILL_INVALID_STATE` or `ERR_LABOR_RATE_REQUEST_INVALID_STATE`.
10. Dealer posture — `ERR_DEALER_SUSPENDED`.
11. Dealer audit hold — `ERR_DEALER_UNDER_AUDIT_HOLD`.
12. Pending appeal blocking an audit close — `ERR_APPEAL_PENDING`.
13. Appeal window — `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_CLOSED`.
14. Contested findings belonging to the audit — `ERR_FINDING_NOT_IN_AUDIT`.
15. Claim eligibility for a finding — `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_ELIGIBLE_FOR_FINDING`.
16. Duplicate repair order — `ERR_DUPLICATE_REPAIR_ORDER`.
17. Overlapping repair on the same vehicle — `ERR_OVERLAPPING_REPAIR_CLAIM`.
18. Overlapping audit period on the same dealer — `ERR_AUDIT_PERIOD_OVERLAP`.
19. Repair narrative completeness — `ERR_NARRATIVE_INCOMPLETE`.
20. Causal-part configuration — `ERR_CAUSAL_PART_REQUIRED`.
21. Labor time against the operation's absolute maximum — `ERR_LABOR_TIME_EXCEEDS_MAX`.
22. Retail-sample sufficiency — `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_RATE_SAMPLE`.
23. Requested rate against the statutory ceiling — `ERR_LABOR_RATE_ABOVE_STATUTORY_CEILING`.
24. Requested markup against the state cap — `ERR_MARKUP_ABOVE_STATE_CAP`.
25. Goodwill amount against the applicable authority limit — `ERR_GOODWILL_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`.
26. Approval of a claim with no coverage and no approved goodwill — `ERR_NO_COVERAGE_WITHOUT_GOODWILL`.
27. Overdue parts return blocking payment — `ERR_PARTS_RETURN_OVERDUE`.

Two consequences of this ordering deserve emphasis. Retail-sample sufficiency is checked before the statutory ceiling is computed, because the ceiling formula consumes the very sample figure that check validates. And `ERR_CLAIM_STILL_SUSPENDED` is deliberately a state-level rejection rather than a business one: the claim in question *is* adjudicable by status, but it carries an open dealer-posture hold that only a posture recovery can lift.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Program Reference Data and Dealer Enrollment

Everything downstream is adjudicated against configuration that a `WarrantyAdmin` publishes first. A state's warranty-reimbursement statute is captured as a profile keyed by `state_code`, a two-letter US state code consisting of exactly two uppercase letters and unique across all profiles. The profile carries `markup_cap_pct`, the statutory ceiling on dealer parts markup expressed as a decimal fraction between 0.00 and 1.00 inclusive and carried to two decimal places; `labor_rate_method`, which is either `RETAIL_SAMPLE` or `TIME_AND_PRICE` and selects how the labor-rate ceiling is established; `min_sample_repair_orders`, the minimum number of customer-pay repair orders a retail-sample petition must cite, which may be zero or more; `max_labor_rate_cents`, an absolute statutory cap on the hourly warranty labor rate that must be strictly positive; `deemed_approved_days`, a strictly positive count of days after which an untouched petition is deemed approved; and `appeal_window_days`, a strictly positive count of days a dealer has to appeal issued findings. A profile is configuration and is never mutated once registered.

Enrolling a dealer establishes `dealer_code`, the manufacturer's non-empty and program-unique dealer code; `name`, the dealer's non-empty legal name; `state_law_profile_id`, naming the statute that governs this dealer's rates; and `vehicles_in_operation`, the non-negative count of vehicles in the dealer's territory that serves as the denominator for warranty-expense analytics. Enrollment also takes `initial_labor_rate_cents` and `initial_parts_markup_pct`, which are copied straight into the dealer's live `approved_labor_rate_cents` and `approved_parts_markup_pct` — the only two fields that describe what the dealer is paid today. A grandfathered markup captured at onboarding may legitimately sit above the state's cap; the cap is applied when a claim is finally adjudicated, and again when a petition is decided, but never retroactively to the enrolled figure. A newly enrolled dealer opens `ACTIVE`, with `chargeback_balance_cents` at 0, `open_parts_return_count` at 0, `audit_hold` false, `outlier_flag` false, `version` at 1, and `labor_rate_effective_date` left null until the dealer's first petition is approved.

A warranted vehicle is registered with its `vin`, exactly seventeen characters long and unique across the fleet; a `model_year` between 1980 and 2100 inclusive; a `warranty_start_date` in year-month-day form recording the in-service date; and an `in_service_odometer` reading in miles, zero or greater, which is the baseline every later mileage measurement is taken from. Coverage terms come in two tiers. Basic bumper-to-bumper coverage is bounded by `basic_months`, strictly positive, and `basic_miles`, strictly positive and measured from the in-service reading. Powertrain coverage is bounded by `powertrain_months`, which must be at least `basic_months`, and `powertrain_miles`, which must be at least `basic_miles`. Vehicle terms are immutable configuration; claims are validated against them and never change them.

Flat-rate labor operations are published with an `op_code` that is non-empty and unique, a non-empty human-readable `description`, and two time figures carried to one decimal place: `standard_time_hours`, strictly positive, which is what the manufacturer will actually reimburse, and `max_time_hours`, at least equal to standard time, which is the absolute ceiling above which a claim is not accepted at all. Each operation declares a `category` of either `BASIC` or `POWERTRAIN`, which decides the coverage bucket the repair is adjudicated under. Each operation also declares whether it `requires_causal_part`; when that flag is true the operation must name a non-empty `causal_failure_group` identifying the component family the causal part has to belong to, and when it is false `causal_failure_group` must be null. Finally, every operation carries a non-empty `overlap_group` label. Two claims on the same vehicle can only collide with each other — for duplicate-repair and repeat-repair purposes — when their operations share that label, which is what lets a brake job and a transmission job on the same vehicle in the same week coexist without complaint.

Catalog parts are published with a non-empty and unique `part_number`, a non-empty `description`, a strictly positive `dealer_net_cents` per-unit price that markup is applied to, and a non-empty `failure_group` naming the component family the part belongs to — the value that must match a labor operation's declared causal failure group for the part to be admissible as that operation's causal part. A part also declares `return_required`: when true, a claim naming this part as causal creates a return obligation for failed-component analysis. The companion `retention_days` field gives the number of days after the obligation is raised before the part is deemed not returned; it must be zero when `return_required` is false and may be zero or more otherwise.

Dealers can be listed with filters on `state_law_profile_id`, on posture via `status`, on `outlier_flag` and on `audit_hold`, alongside the usual `limit` and `offset`.

### 3.2. Establishing a Dealer's Labor Rate and Parts Markup

A `DealerWarrantyAdmin` petitions for better terms by filing a labor-rate request that names its own `dealer_id`, the `requested_labor_rate_cents` it is asking for, the `requested_parts_markup_pct` it is asking for as a two-decimal fraction between 0.00 and 1.00 inclusive, an `effective_date` in year-month-day form on which the new terms would take effect, and the `expected_dealer_version` described in §2.9. Where the governing statute uses the retail-sample method, the petition also cites `sample_repair_order_count`, the number of customer-pay repair orders in the sample, and `sample_median_rate_cents`, the median customer-pay hourly rate across that sample; both are non-negative integers and both are optional in body shape terms, because a time-and-price state has no use for them.

Filing is blocked outright while the petitioning dealer is `SUSPENDED`, with `ERR_DEALER_SUSPENDED`. On acceptance the request pins `state_law_profile_id` as a snapshot of the dealer's governing profile at that instant, so that later reassigning the dealer to a different state cannot retroactively change this petition's ceiling, and records `submitted_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID` for the segregation-of-duties check that will guard its decision. The request opens in `SUBMITTED` with `deemed_approved` false, and the petitioning dealer's `version` advances by 1. Any earlier request of the same dealer still sitting in `SUBMITTED` is superseded, moving `SUBMITTED → WITHDRAWN` with its own `version` advancing by 1; a request that a reviewer has already pulled into `UNDER_REVIEW` is deliberately left untouched.

In a retail-sample state the petition must cite at least as many repair orders as the statute's `min_sample_repair_orders` and must supply a non-null median rate; falling short of either yields `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_RATE_SAMPLE`. That check runs before the ceiling is computed, because the ceiling consumes the median.

The ceiling itself is written into `statutory_ceiling_cents` at creation and never recomputed thereafter. Under the retail-sample method the server first works out a step-up floor by taking the dealer's currently approved hourly rate, increasing it by twenty-five percent, and rounding the product half up to whole cents; it then takes the greater of the cited sample median and that step-up floor; and it finally takes the lesser of that result and the statute's absolute `max_labor_rate_cents`. Under the time-and-price method there is no sample to consider: the server increases the dealer's currently approved hourly rate by ten percent, rounds the product half up to whole cents, and takes the lesser of that figure and the statute's absolute maximum. Either way, a requested rate equal to the ceiling is inside it; only a rate strictly above the ceiling is refused, with `ERR_LABOR_RATE_ABOVE_STATUTORY_CEILING`. A requested markup is judged separately and directly against the statute: equal to `markup_cap_pct` passes, strictly above it fails with `ERR_MARKUP_ABOVE_STATE_CAP`.

A `WarrantyAdmin` manages the review track by supplying an `action`, optionally accompanied by a free-text `note`. An `action` of `START_REVIEW` moves the petition `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW` and requires it to be in `SUBMITTED`; an `action` of `RETURN_FOR_INFO` bounces it back `UNDER_REVIEW → SUBMITTED` and requires it to be in `UNDER_REVIEW`. Either way the request's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` advances by 1. A request in any other state is refused with `ERR_LABOR_RATE_REQUEST_INVALID_STATE`. Starting review has one consequence worth spelling out: it stops the statutory clock, because the deemed-approval sweep only ever promotes rows still sitting in `SUBMITTED`. Returning for information does *not* restart that clock — eligibility is always measured from the request's original `created_at`, so bouncing a petition back and forth cannot be used to buy the manufacturer more time.

An `AdjudicationManager` decides the petition by supplying an `action` of either `APPROVE` or `DENY`, and the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the recorded `submitted_by_user_id`. Both actions accept a request in either `SUBMITTED` or `UNDER_REVIEW`; anything else yields `ERR_LABOR_RATE_REQUEST_INVALID_STATE`. On approval the manager supplies `approved_labor_rate_cents` and `approved_parts_markup_pct`, which are what is actually granted and may be lower than what was asked for. The granted rate may not exceed the request's pinned `statutory_ceiling_cents` and the granted markup may not exceed the governing statute's `markup_cap_pct`, the two violations reporting `ERR_LABOR_RATE_ABOVE_STATUTORY_CEILING` and `ERR_MARKUP_ABOVE_STATE_CAP` respectively. Approval moves the request to `APPROVED`, writes both granted figures onto the request, then republishes them onto the dealer: the dealer's `approved_labor_rate_cents` and `approved_parts_markup_pct` are overwritten with the granted values and its `labor_rate_effective_date` is set to the request's `effective_date`, with the dealer's `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` advanced by 1. The request's own `version` advances by 1 as well. Claims already priced are not revisited: a claim's provisional and approved amounts are pinned at their own transition instants and a later rate change never rewrites them.

Denial requires a present, non-empty `denial_reason`, which is recorded on the request as it moves to `DENIED`; the request's `version` advances by 1 and the dealer's rate and markup are left exactly as they were.

Letting the statutory clock run out waives the manufacturer's objection. A `WarrantyAdmin` triggers the deemed-approval sweep, which walks every request still in `SUBMITTED` whose `created_at` is at or beyond the governing statute's `deemed_approved_days` before the logical clock, in ascending `created_at` order, and promotes each one `SUBMITTED → APPROVED`. A swept request has `deemed_approved` set true, has its `approved_labor_rate_cents` set equal to `requested_labor_rate_cents` and its `approved_parts_markup_pct` set equal to `requested_parts_markup_pct`, and applies exactly the same dealer-side writes a manual approval would — rate, markup, effective date, dealer `version` advanced by 1 — with the request's own `version` advancing by 1. The pinned `statutory_ceiling_cents` is deliberately *not* re-checked during the sweep: the whole point of a deemed-approval statute is that inaction concedes the dealer's number. The sweep reports how many rows it promoted as `promoted_count` and lists their identifiers as `promoted_request_ids`.

Petitions can be listed with filters on `dealer_id`, `state_law_profile_id`, `status` and `deemed_approved`, alongside `limit` and `offset`. `APPROVED`, `DENIED` and `WITHDRAWN` are terminal: no further transition leaves any of them.

### 3.3. Claim Intake and Coverage Determination

A `DealerWarrantyAdmin` submits a warranty claim on behalf of its own dealership. The submission names the `dealer_id` it is acting for, the `vehicle_id` that was repaired, the `labor_operation_id` performed, and — when the operation demands one — the `causal_part_id` identified as the failing component. It carries the dealer's own non-empty `repair_order_number`, the `repair_date` on which the repair was completed in year-month-day form, the `odometer_at_repair` reading in miles which must be zero or greater, the `claimed_labor_hours` the dealer wants paid for (strictly positive, one decimal place), and `claimed_parts_qty`, the number of units of the causal part consumed, which must be zero when no causal part is named. Three narrative fields complete the submission: `complaint_text` recording what the customer reported, `cause_text` recording what the technician determined, and `correction_text` recording what was actually done. A submission may also carry `resubmission_of_claim_id`, naming the earlier claim this one replaces after a dealer correction; it defaults to null for an original submission and, when supplied, must name a real claim or the request fails with `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`.

Submission is refused while the dealer is `SUSPENDED`, with `ERR_DEALER_SUSPENDED`. A dealer on `PROBATION`, by contrast, may still submit — posture suspends claims already in flight but does not close the intake door, and the posture hold described in §3.7 is applied only by the cascade that fires at the moment the posture changes, never by the intake path.

Two collision rules then run, in this order. First, no other *live* claim of the same dealer may already carry the same `repair_order_number`, where live means the claim's `status` is `SUBMITTED`, `SUSPENDED` or `APPROVED`; a collision is `ERR_DUPLICATE_REPAIR_ORDER`. Claims that have reached `DENIED` or `VOID` are dead and never collide. Second, no other live claim on the same `vehicle_id` whose labor operation shares this operation's `overlap_group` may sit within thirty days of this `repair_date` in either direction. That interval is closed on both ends: a gap of exactly thirty days collides, a gap of thirty-one days does not. The claim's own resubmission predecessor, if `resubmission_of_claim_id` names it, is exempt from this test. A collision is `ERR_OVERLAPPING_REPAIR_CLAIM`.

The repair narrative is then checked for substance. Each of `complaint_text`, `cause_text` and `correction_text` must be present and, after leading and trailing whitespace is trimmed, must be at least ten characters long. Any one of them falling short yields `ERR_NARRATIVE_INCOMPLETE`.

Causal-part configuration is checked next, and it cuts both ways. When the cited labor operation has `requires_causal_part` true, the claim must supply a `causal_part_id`, that part's `failure_group` must be exactly equal to the operation's `causal_failure_group`, and `claimed_parts_qty` must be at least 1. When the operation has `requires_causal_part` false, supplying a `causal_part_id` at all is a violation. All four of those failures — missing part, mismatched failure group, zero quantity where a part is required, and an unwanted part — report `ERR_CAUSAL_PART_REQUIRED`.

Finally, `claimed_labor_hours` is tested against the operation's absolute ceiling. Hours at or below `max_time_hours` are accepted; hours strictly above it are refused with `ERR_LABOR_TIME_EXCEEDS_MAX`. Note carefully that hours above `standard_time_hours` but at or below `max_time_hours` are perfectly acceptable at intake — they merely raise an excessive-time edit, described in §3.4.

Coverage is then determined and written into `coverage_type`, using nothing but the one vehicle and the one labor operation the claim names. No other claim participates: prior repair history influences the repeat-repair edit and nothing else. Two elapsed measures are computed first. The elapsed month count is derived from the vehicle's `warranty_start_date` and the claim's `repair_date` by taking the difference in calendar years multiplied by twelve, adding the difference in calendar months, and then subtracting one if the repair date's day-of-month is earlier than the warranty start date's day-of-month. Miles used is the claim's `odometer_at_repair` less the vehicle's `in_service_odometer`. Both are exact integers and neither is rounded.

Powertrain terms are tested first, and only for an operation whose `category` is `POWERTRAIN`: if the elapsed months are at most `powertrain_months` and the miles used are at most `powertrain_miles`, both bounds inclusive, `coverage_type` becomes `POWERTRAIN`. Otherwise — for a `BASIC`-category operation, and also for a `POWERTRAIN`-category operation that has run past its powertrain limits — basic terms are tested: if the elapsed months are at most `basic_months` and the miles used are at most `basic_miles`, both bounds inclusive, `coverage_type` becomes `BASIC`. If neither test passes, `coverage_type` becomes `NONE`. A `BASIC`-category operation never falls back onto powertrain terms, however much powertrain coverage the vehicle has left. The one and only way `coverage_type` ever leaves `NONE` is the goodwill route of §3.5, which flips it from `NONE` to `GOODWILL`; no other transition of `coverage_type` exists.

A newly created claim opens with `version` at 1, with `payment_status` at `UNPAID`, and with `status` at either `SUBMITTED` or `SUSPENDED` depending on what the edit engine finds. Claims can be read individually and listed with filters on `dealer_id`, `vehicle_id`, `labor_operation_id`, `causal_part_id`, `resubmission_of_claim_id`, `status`, `payment_status`, `coverage_type`, `repair_date_from` and `repair_date_to`, alongside `limit` and `offset`; that list is the only way to walk the self-referential resubmission chain, since no create response exposes it.

### 3.4. Provisional Pricing and the Automated Edit Engine

Every accepted submission is priced immediately, on the dealer's terms and on the dealer's numbers, and the result is persisted as the claim's provisional value. Four inputs are selected for this provisional pass: the hourly rate is the dealer's `approved_labor_rate_cents` as of this instant; the markup is the dealer's `approved_parts_markup_pct` as of this instant, taken **uncapped**, whatever the statute says; the hours are the dealer's `claimed_labor_hours`, also taken uncapped, whatever the flat-rate standard says; and the quantity is `claimed_parts_qty`.

Labor is priced with exactly one rounding, applied to the product: multiply the hours by the hourly rate in cents and round the result half up to a whole cent. That figure is `provisional_labor_amount_cents`.

Parts are priced with a **pinned rounding order** that must be implemented exactly as stated, because the alternative order produces a different number of cents. First, take the catalog part's `dealer_net_cents`, multiply it by one plus the markup fraction, and round that *unit* price half up to a whole cent. Then multiply the rounded unit price by the quantity as an exact integer multiplication, with no further rounding. That product is `provisional_parts_amount_cents`. Rounding after the multiplication instead of before it is forbidden: with a dealer net price of 24,799 cents, a markup of 0.40 and a quantity of three, the pinned order gives a unit price of 34,719 cents and a parts amount of 104,157 cents, whereas rounding the whole product would give 104,156 cents — a cent low. When the claim names no causal part, the parts amount is 0.

`provisional_total_cents` is then simply the labor amount plus the parts amount, with no further rounding of any kind. All three provisional figures are recomputed on every submission and on every resubmission, and are never overwritten by adjudication: their whole purpose is to remain visible next to the final numbers so that the divergence between what the dealer asked for and what the manufacturer granted can be inspected.

With the provisional total in hand, the automated edit engine runs. It can raise up to five edits on an intake, and it appends them in the fixed order below, so that the append ordering of the resulting `ClaimEdit` rows is deterministic. Each row records the `claim_id` it belongs to, the `edit_code` that fired, the `severity` that code carries, a human-readable `detail` naming the threshold that was crossed, a `resolved_at` stamp that stays null while the edit is open, and — for one code only — a `conflicting_claim_id`.

The three suspending edits come first. An `EXCESSIVE_TIME` edit fires when `claimed_labor_hours` is strictly greater than the operation's `standard_time_hours`. A `HIGH_VALUE` edit fires when `provisional_total_cents` reaches 150,000 cents — 1,500 dollars — or more, the boundary being inclusive. A `NO_COVERAGE` edit fires when the freshly determined `coverage_type` is `NONE`. All three carry `severity` `SUSPEND`.

Two informational edits follow. A `NARRATIVE_THIN` edit fires when `correction_text` is strictly shorter than twenty characters — note that this is a separate and stricter test than the ten-character narrative floor of §3.3, applies to the correction text alone, and is merely advisory. A `REPEAT_REPAIR` edit fires when a qualifying prior claim exists, and records that prior claim's identifier in `conflicting_claim_id`; on every other edit code `conflicting_claim_id` is null. Both carry `severity` `WARN`.

The prior claim behind a repeat-repair edit is selected precisely. Candidates are claims other than this one that share this claim's `dealer_id`, share its `vehicle_id`, have a labor operation in the same `overlap_group`, and are in `SUBMITTED`, `SUSPENDED` or `APPROVED` — claims in `DENIED` or `VOID` are excluded. A candidate qualifies only when this claim's `repair_date` falls strictly more than thirty days and at most one hundred and eighty days after the candidate's `repair_date`; the lower bound is exclusive and the upper bound is inclusive, which dovetails with the closed thirty-day collision buffer of §3.3 so that a prior repair either blocks the claim outright or, being between thirty-one and one hundred and eighty days old, merely flags it. Among qualifying candidates the one with the latest `repair_date` wins, and a tie on repair date is broken by taking the earliest `created_at`.

`suspend_edit_count` is then set to the number of edits just appended whose `severity` is `SUSPEND`, and the claim's `status` follows directly: `SUSPENDED` when that count is greater than zero, `SUBMITTED` when it is zero. A claim is `SUSPENDED` exactly while it holds at least one open suspending edit and has not reached a terminal status.

A sixth edit code exists, `DEALER_POSTURE_HOLD`, but the intake engine never raises it. It is appended only by the dealer-posture cascade of §3.7, and it too carries `severity` `SUSPEND`.

When the submission's causal part has `return_required` true, the same request also raises a parts-return obligation in `REQUESTED`, whose `due_date` is its own `created_at` advanced by the part's `retention_days` days, and increments the dealer's `open_parts_return_count` by 1 — re-establishing the invariant that the count always equals the number of parts-return rows in `REQUESTED` whose claim belongs to that dealer.

A dealer corrects a suspended claim in place rather than by filing a new one. Correction requires the claim to be in `SUSPENDED` and its `payment_status` to be `UNPAID`; a claim in any other `status` is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_RESUBMITTABLE`. The dealer's own posture must not be `SUSPENDED`, or the correction is refused with `ERR_DEALER_SUSPENDED`. The correction supplies fresh values for `claimed_labor_hours`, `claimed_parts_qty`, `complaint_text`, `cause_text` and `correction_text`, which overwrite the stored values, and the corrected hours are tested against `max_time_hours` exactly as at intake, with `ERR_LABOR_TIME_EXCEEDS_MAX` on breach. The duplicate-repair-order rule, the overlapping-repair rule, the narrative floor and the causal-part configuration rule all apply again to a correction, reporting the same four codes they report at intake.

A correction then stamps `resolved_at` on every edit of this claim that is still open, re-runs coverage determination and the provisional pricing pass, and appends a freshly computed edit set. `suspend_edit_count` is set to the number of suspending edits in that fresh set, and the claim's `version` advances by 1. Here is the subtlety: a correction that still trips a suspending edit — hours still above standard time, coverage still `NONE` — is **not** an error. The call succeeds with 200 OK, the fresh edits are recorded, and the claim simply remains in `SUSPENDED`. Only when the fresh set contains no suspending edit does the claim complete the cycle `SUSPENDED → SUBMITTED`. This is the first of the two cycle edges in the claim's adjudication lifecycle.

A claim's accumulated edits can be listed in append order, filtered by `edit_code` and by `severity` alongside `limit` and `offset`. That listing is the only channel through which a reader ever sees `conflicting_claim_id`, since no create response returns it.

### 3.5. Goodwill Authority for Out-of-Warranty Repairs

Sometimes a repair the warranty no longer covers should still be paid — a component that failed just past the mileage limit, a customer-retention judgement call. A `ClaimAdjudicator` handles that by raising a goodwill authorization against the claim. The claim must have `coverage_type` exactly `NONE` and its `status` must be `SUBMITTED` or `SUSPENDED`; the request carries a strictly positive `requested_amount_cents`, a `policy_adjustment_pct` between 0.00 and 0.50 inclusive carried to two decimal places representing the share of the authorized amount the dealer itself will absorb, a non-empty `justification`, and the `expected_claim_version` of §2.9. The raising adjudicator's `X-User-ID` is recorded as `requested_by_user_id`, and the claim's `version` advances by 1. Any earlier authorization on the same claim still sitting in `PENDING` or `ESCALATED` is superseded to `WITHDRAWN`, with that row's own `version` advancing by 1.

Two authority ceilings are computed at creation time and pinned onto the authorization. The computation begins with how far past the warranty the repair actually sits, recorded as `out_of_warranty_margin_pct`. Miles overrun is the claim's `odometer_at_repair` less the vehicle's `in_service_odometer` less the vehicle's `basic_miles`, floored at zero so a repair inside the mileage limit contributes nothing. Months overrun is the elapsed month count between the vehicle's `warranty_start_date` and the claim's `repair_date` — computed exactly as in §3.3 — less the vehicle's `basic_months`, likewise floored at zero. Each overrun is then expressed as a fraction of its own limit: miles overrun divided by `basic_miles`, and months overrun divided by `basic_months`. The larger of those two fractions, rounded half up to four decimal places, is `out_of_warranty_margin_pct`. Should this ever be evaluated for a claim whose `coverage_type` is not `NONE`, the margin is zero.

A decay multiplier is then formed by subtracting twice the margin from one and flooring the result at zero, so a repair ten percent past its warranty terms retains eighty percent of the base authority, a repair fifty percent past them retains none of it. Separately, a share cap is formed by taking eighty percent of the claim's `provisional_total_cents` and rounding half up to whole cents — goodwill never covers the entire provisional value of a repair. `adjudicator_limit_cents` is the lesser of the share cap and the adjudicator base authority of 50,000 cents (500 dollars) multiplied by the decay multiplier and rounded half up to whole cents. `manager_limit_cents` is the lesser of the same share cap and the manager base authority of 250,000 cents (2,500 dollars) multiplied by the same decay multiplier and rounded half up to whole cents. Neither limit is influenced by any other claim or by a superseded prior authorization.

The requested amount then selects the authorization's opening state. At or below `adjudicator_limit_cents` it opens `PENDING`. Above that but at or below `manager_limit_cents` it opens `ESCALATED`. Strictly above `manager_limit_cents` the request is refused outright with `ERR_GOODWILL_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422) and no authorization is created.

Deciding an authorization requires it to be in `PENDING` or `ESCALATED`; anything else is `ERR_GOODWILL_INVALID_STATE`. The decision supplies an `action` of `APPROVE` or `DENY`, and the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `requested_by_user_id` or the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. A `PENDING` authorization may be decided by either a `ClaimAdjudicator` or an `AdjudicationManager`. An `ESCALATED` authorization may be decided only by an `AdjudicationManager`; a `ClaimAdjudicator` attempting it is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED_FOR_ESCALATED_GOODWILL` (HTTP 403). On approval the decider supplies `approved_amount_cents`, which must be strictly positive and must not exceed the ceiling that belongs to the deciding role — `adjudicator_limit_cents` when a `ClaimAdjudicator` decides, `manager_limit_cents` when an `AdjudicationManager` decides — with `ERR_GOODWILL_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` on breach.

Approval records `approved_amount_cents` and stamps `decided_by_user_id` with the decider's `X-User-ID`, moving the authorization to `APPROVED` and advancing its `version` by 1. It then cascades onto the claim: the claim's `coverage_type` flips from `NONE` to `GOODWILL`, the claim's open `NO_COVERAGE` edit is stamped `resolved_at`, `suspend_edit_count` is recomputed from the edits that remain open, and the claim's `version` advances by 1. When that recount reaches zero the claim completes the second of its two cycle edges, `SUSPENDED → SUBMITTED`, which is what makes `GOODWILL` coverage reachable at all — without it a claim with no coverage would sit suspended forever. A claim that also holds an open `EXCESSIVE_TIME`, `HIGH_VALUE` or `DEALER_POSTURE_HOLD` edit keeps a non-zero count and therefore stays `SUSPENDED`, even though its coverage has been fixed.

Denial requires a present, non-empty `denial_reason`, which is recorded alongside `decided_by_user_id` as the authorization moves to `DENIED` and its `version` advances by 1. The claim's `coverage_type` stays `NONE`, and no edit is resolved. `APPROVED`, `DENIED` and `WITHDRAWN` are all terminal.

Authorizations can be read individually and listed with filters on `claim_id` and `status` alongside `limit` and `offset`; that listing is the only channel exposing which claim an authorization belongs to.

### 3.6. Adjudication, Payment and Voiding

Adjudication is where the manufacturer's own numbers replace the dealer's. A `ClaimAdjudicator` or an `AdjudicationManager` supplies a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `DENY`, and on approval also supplies `approved_labor_hours` and `approved_parts_qty` — the hours and part units it is willing to grant. The claim's `status` must be `SUBMITTED` or `SUSPENDED`; anything else is `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_ADJUDICABLE`. The acting `X-User-ID` must not already appear as `decided_by_user_id` on a goodwill authorization for this same claim, or the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

A suspended claim is genuinely adjudicable, and this is a deliberate design choice: an adjudicator may look at an `EXCESSIVE_TIME`, `HIGH_VALUE` or `NO_COVERAGE` suspension, decide it is fine, and approve straight through, at which point those edits are stamped resolved. One suspension is different. A claim holding an open edit whose `edit_code` is `DEALER_POSTURE_HOLD` cannot be adjudicated away: that request is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_STILL_SUSPENDED`, because a dealer-posture hold is lifted only by the dealer's balance recovering, never by an adjudicator's signature.

On approval, `approved_labor_hours` is tested against the operation's `max_time_hours` exactly as claimed hours were at intake, with `ERR_LABOR_TIME_EXCEEDS_MAX` when it is strictly above. `approved_parts_qty` may not exceed `claimed_parts_qty`; granting more units than the dealer claimed is a body-shape violation reported as `ERR_VALIDATION`. And `coverage_type` must not be `NONE`: a claim with no coverage requires an `APPROVED` goodwill authorization to have flipped it to `GOODWILL` first, and approving without one is refused with `ERR_NO_COVERAGE_WITHOUT_GOODWILL` (HTTP 409).

The final pricing pass then runs. It is the same two-step arithmetic as the provisional pass of §3.4 — one rounding on the labor product, then the pinned unit-first rounding order on parts — but its four inputs are chosen differently, and that difference is the whole point. The hourly rate is the dealer's `approved_labor_rate_cents` as of *this* instant, which may have moved since submission. The markup is the lesser of the dealer's `approved_parts_markup_pct` and the governing statute's `markup_cap_pct`, so a grandfathered markup is finally trimmed to what state law allows. The hours are the lesser of `approved_labor_hours` and the operation's `standard_time_hours`, so flat-rate discipline bites even when the adjudicator was generous. The quantity is `approved_parts_qty`. Labor is multiplied and rounded half up once to give `approved_labor_amount_cents`; the marked-up unit price is rounded half up once and then multiplied by the quantity to give `approved_parts_amount_cents`, which is 0 when the claim names no causal part; and their sum is the unadjusted total, with no further rounding.

Two goodwill adjustments follow, and only when `coverage_type` is `GOODWILL`. In that case `approved_total_cents` is the lesser of the unadjusted total and the approved goodwill authorization's `approved_amount_cents` — goodwill caps what the manufacturer will pay — and `dealer_participation_cents` is `approved_total_cents` multiplied by that authorization's `policy_adjustment_pct` and rounded half up to whole cents. For every other coverage type `approved_total_cents` is simply the unadjusted total and `dealer_participation_cents` is 0. In all cases `oem_payable_cents` is `approved_total_cents` less `dealer_participation_cents`, and that is the figure the manufacturer actually pays.

Approval moves the claim `SUBMITTED → APPROVED` or `SUSPENDED → APPROVED`, stamps `resolved_at` on every edit of the claim that is still open, sets `suspend_edit_count` to 0, and advances the claim's `version` by 1. Note that the provisional figures are left exactly as they were: after a normal approval the claim carries both stories side by side.

Denial requires a present, non-empty `denial_reason`, which is recorded as the claim moves `SUBMITTED → DENIED` or `SUSPENDED → DENIED`. Denial also stamps `resolved_at` on every open edit, sets `suspend_edit_count` to 0, advances the claim's `version` by 1, and cascades onto the parts-return program: every parts-return obligation of this claim still in `REQUESTED` moves to `WAIVED`, and the dealer's `open_parts_return_count` is decremented by one for each waived row. `DENIED` is terminal.

Voiding is for a claim that should never have entered the program at all. It requires a `void_reason`, which is recorded on the claim, and is available to a `WarrantyAdmin` or a `ClaimAdjudicator`. A claim in `SUBMITTED` or `SUSPENDED` may be voided freely; a claim in `APPROVED` may be voided only while its `payment_status` is still `UNPAID`. A claim already in `DENIED` or `VOID`, or an approved claim whose payment status has left `UNPAID`, is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_VOIDABLE`. Voiding stamps `resolved_at` on every open edit, sets `suspend_edit_count` to 0, advances the claim's `version` by 1, and runs the same parts-return waiver as denial — every `REQUESTED` obligation becomes `WAIVED`, the dealer's `open_parts_return_count` drops by one per waived row, and the dealer's `version` advances by 1. That compensating pair is deliberate: submitting a claim whose causal part must be returned and then voiding that claim leaves the dealer's open-return count at exactly its pre-submission value. `VOID` is terminal.

Money moves on a second, entirely independent dimension. A claim's `payment_status` walks `UNPAID → PAID → DEBITED → CREDIT_REINSTATED` without its `status` ever leaving `APPROVED`: a debited claim is still an approved claim, and the two dimensions must be reported and reasoned about separately. `UNPAID` is the opening value and `CREDIT_REINSTATED` is terminal on that dimension.

A `WarrantyAdmin` pays an approved claim, and the payment is for `oem_payable_cents`. Four gates stand in front of it. The claim's `status` must be `APPROVED` and its `payment_status` must be `UNPAID`, or the request is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_PAYABLE`; its `suspend_edit_count` must be 0. The dealer must not be `SUSPENDED`, or `ERR_DEALER_SUSPENDED`. The dealer's `audit_hold` must be false, or `ERR_DEALER_UNDER_AUDIT_HOLD` — payment of a claim is frozen for the duration of an audit, which is what stops the manufacturer from paying out money it is about to try to claw back. And where the claim's causal part requires a return, the claim cannot be paid while that obligation is still in `REQUESTED` and the logical clock has already reached or passed its `due_date`; that case is `ERR_PARTS_RETURN_OVERDUE`, and the non-return sweep must be run first so that the debit lands before any payment does. A successful payment moves `payment_status` from `UNPAID` to `PAID`, leaves `status` untouched at `APPROVED`, and advances the claim's `version` by 1.

### 3.7. The Parts Return Program and the Non-Return Cascade

The manufacturer's engineering organisation needs the failed components back, so every claim whose causal part is flagged for return carries an obligation to ship that part in. The obligation names the `claim_id` that created it and the `part_id` to be returned, both established by the cascade at claim submission rather than by any request body, and both visible only through the parts-return listing, which filters on `claim_id`, `part_id` and `status` alongside `limit` and `offset`. The obligation opens in `REQUESTED` with a `due_date` derived from its own creation instant and the part's retention window, with `received_date`, `analysis_result` and `analysis_note` all null, and with `debit_amount_cents` and `reversed_amount_cents` both at 0. `ANALYZED` and `WAIVED` are its terminal states.

A `WarrantyAdmin` records physical receipt by supplying the `received_date` on which the part arrived. Receipt is admissible from `REQUESTED` and from `NOT_RETURNED` and from nowhere else; any other state is `ERR_PARTS_RETURN_INVALID_STATE`. The linked claim must not be in `VOID`. A timely receipt moves `REQUESTED → RECEIVED`, stamps `received_date`, decrements the dealer's `open_parts_return_count` by 1, advances the dealer's `version` by 1, and advances the obligation's `version` by 1.

Analysis follows receipt. A `WarrantyAdmin` supplies an `analysis_result` — one of `DEFECT_CONFIRMED`, `NO_DEFECT_FOUND` or `NOT_AVAILABLE` — together with a free-text `analysis_note`, and both are recorded as the obligation moves `RECEIVED → ANALYZED`. The obligation must be in `RECEIVED`, or the request is `ERR_PARTS_RETURN_INVALID_STATE`, and `analysis_result` must be present. What happens to the money depends entirely on which of the three outcomes was recorded, and the debit is always computed against the linked claim, which must be in `APPROVED` — a claim that reached `DENIED` or `VOID` had its obligation waived and is never debited.

`DEFECT_CONFIRMED` is the honest outcome and no money moves: `debit_amount_cents` stays 0. `NO_DEFECT_FOUND` means the dealer replaced a part that was not actually faulty, and `debit_amount_cents` becomes the claim's entire `approved_parts_amount_cents` — the parts side of the claim is clawed back, the labor is not. `NOT_AVAILABLE` means the part arrived but could not be analysed, and the parties split the difference: `debit_amount_cents` becomes half of the claim's `approved_parts_amount_cents`, rounded half up to a whole cent. Whenever the resulting debit is greater than zero, the claim's `debited_amount_cents` is increased by it, the claim's `payment_status` moves to `DEBITED` from either `PAID` or `UNPAID`, the claim's `version` advances by 1, and a ledger row with `source_type` `PARTS_NO_DEFECT` is appended for that amount against the dealer, which recomputes the dealer's balance and posture and advances the dealer's `version` by 1. The obligation's own `version` advances by 1 in every case.

A part that simply never arrives is caught by the non-return sweep, which a `WarrantyAdmin` triggers and which is the deepest cascade in the system. The sweep considers every obligation still in `REQUESTED` whose `due_date` the logical clock has reached or passed, and whose linked claim is in `APPROVED`; it processes them in ascending order of their own `created_at`, so that the sequence of `balance_after_cents` values it writes onto the ledger is deterministic. Each swept obligation moves `REQUESTED → NOT_RETURNED`, has `debit_amount_cents` set to the linked claim's entire `approved_total_cents` — a non-return costs the dealer the whole claim, labor included, not merely the parts — decrements the dealer's `open_parts_return_count` by 1, and advances its own `version` by 1.

From there the cascade runs five hops deep. First, the linked claim's `debited_amount_cents` is increased by the debit and its `payment_status` moves to `DEBITED`, from `PAID` if it had been paid and from `UNPAID` if it had not; a debit against an unpaid claim still books a positive ledger amount, and that claim can never be paid afterwards because payment requires `payment_status` to be `UNPAID`. Second, a ledger row with `source_type` `PARTS_NON_RETURN` is appended for the positive debit amount against the dealer, raising `chargeback_balance_cents`. Third, the dealer's `status` is recomputed from the new balance and may rise `ACTIVE → PROBATION` or `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED`, with the dealer's `version` advancing by 1. Fourth, if that recomputation put the dealer into `PROBATION` or `SUSPENDED`, every claim of that dealer currently in `SUBMITTED` is driven `SUBMITTED → SUSPENDED`: each such claim receives an appended edit whose `edit_code` is `DEALER_POSTURE_HOLD` and whose `severity` is `SUSPEND`, has its `suspend_edit_count` incremented by 1, and has its `version` advanced by 1. Fifth and last, those newly suspended sibling claims are now unpayable and unapprovable — the fifth hop is what makes one dealer's unreturned part visible on every other claim that dealer has in flight. The sweep reports `swept_count`, the identifiers of the claims it debited as `debited_claim_ids`, and the summed positive debit as `total_debit_cents`.

A late return reverses all of that, and reversing it exactly is the point. Receipt on an obligation already in `NOT_RETURNED` moves `NOT_RETURNED → RECEIVED`, stamps `received_date`, and sets `reversed_amount_cents` equal to the obligation's `debit_amount_cents`. The dealer's `open_parts_return_count` is deliberately **not** decremented again here: the row already left `REQUESTED` when the sweep moved it to `NOT_RETURNED`, and decrementing twice would break the count invariant. Four compensating hops then run. The linked claim moves `DEBITED → CREDIT_REINSTATED` with its `reinstated_amount_cents` increased by the reversed amount and its `version` advanced by 1. A ledger row with `source_type` `LATE_RETURN_REVERSAL` is appended carrying the negative of the debit amount, so the dealer's `chargeback_balance_cents` falls by exactly what it rose by. The dealer's `status` is recomputed from the reduced balance and may fall `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE`, `PROBATION → ACTIVE`, or `SUSPENDED → PROBATION`, with the dealer's `version` advancing by 1. And if the dealer has returned all the way to `ACTIVE`, every claim of that dealer in `SUSPENDED` whose only open suspending edit is a `DEALER_POSTURE_HOLD` is released `SUSPENDED → SUBMITTED`: those hold edits are stamped `resolved_at`, `suspend_edit_count` is set to 0, and the claim's `version` advances by 1. That release is conditional, and the condition matters — a claim that also holds an open `EXCESSIVE_TIME`, `HIGH_VALUE` or `NO_COVERAGE` edit stays `SUSPENDED`, because only the posture component of its suspension was lifted. A dealer falling merely from `SUSPENDED` to `PROBATION` releases nothing at all, since `PROBATION` continues to hold that dealer's claims.

Because no rounding occurs anywhere in the balance arithmetic, a forward debit and its full reversal leave the dealer's `chargeback_balance_cents` at precisely its pre-debit value and its `status` at whatever posture that value implies, and leave the claim with `reinstated_amount_cents` exactly equal to `debited_amount_cents`. Zero drift is required.

The last route out of `REQUESTED` is the waiver already described in §3.6: when the linked claim reaches `DENIED` or `VOID`, its `REQUESTED` obligations move `REQUESTED → WAIVED`, the dealer's `open_parts_return_count` drops by one per waived row, and both the dealer's `version` and each waived obligation's `version` advance by 1. Taken together, the four movements that touch the count — creation into `REQUESTED` adding one, and departure from `REQUESTED` to any of `RECEIVED`, `NOT_RETURNED` or `WAIVED` subtracting one, with the late `NOT_RETURNED → RECEIVED` step changing nothing — keep `open_parts_return_count` exactly equal to the number of that dealer's obligations sitting in `REQUESTED` at every observable instant. Rows in `RECEIVED`, `ANALYZED`, `NOT_RETURNED` and `WAIVED` are all excluded from that count.

### 3.8. On-Site Dealer Audit, Extrapolated Chargeback and Appeal Rights

An `AuditManager` schedules an audit against one dealer over one repair-date window. The scheduling request names the `dealer_id` to be audited, the window's inclusive `period_start` and its exclusive `period_end` in year-month-day form, a strictly positive `sample_size` recording how many claims the auditor intends to pull — advisory only, never enforced against the number of findings actually raised — a non-empty `lead_auditor_user_id` holding the `X-User-ID` of the `FieldAuditor` who will lead the visit, and the `expected_dealer_version` of §2.9. `period_end` must be strictly after `period_start`, or the request fails with `ERR_VALIDATION`.

The window is half-open throughout the entire audit machinery: a claim belongs to the period when its `repair_date` is at or after `period_start` and strictly before `period_end`. That convention governs overlap too. No other audit of the same dealer whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`, `IN_FIELD`, `FINDINGS_ISSUED` or `APPEALED` may have a period intersecting the requested one, and because the intervals are half-open with no buffer whatsoever, a new period whose `period_start` equals an existing period's `period_end` is adjacent rather than overlapping and is accepted. A genuine intersection is refused with `ERR_AUDIT_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. A scheduled audit opens in `SCHEDULED` with `sample_paid_snapshot_cents`, `population_paid_snapshot_cents`, `sample_chargeback_cents`, `extrapolated_chargeback_cents` and `total_chargeback_cents` all at 0, with `error_rate` and `extrapolation_factor` at zero, and with `findings_issued_at` and `appeal_deadline` both null; the dealer's `version` advances by 1.

The lead auditor going on site moves the audit `SCHEDULED → IN_FIELD`, and any other starting state is `ERR_AUDIT_INVALID_STATE`. Going on site sets the dealer's `audit_hold` to true and advances the dealer's `version` by 1, alongside the audit's own. `audit_hold` is true exactly while at least one audit of that dealer sits in `IN_FIELD`, `FINDINGS_ISSUED` or `APPEALED`, and as §3.6 explained it freezes payment of that dealer's claims for the duration.

While the audit is in field the auditor attaches findings, one per unsupported claim. A finding names the `claim_id` held unsupported, a `finding_code` drawn from `NO_REPAIR_ORDER`, `NO_TECHNICIAN_SIGNATURE`, `PART_NOT_ON_HAND`, `TIME_NOT_DOCUMENTED` and `NARRATIVE_UNSUPPORTED`, a strictly positive `unsupported_amount_cents` that may not exceed the cited claim's `approved_total_cents`, and a non-empty `evidence_note`. The audit must be in `IN_FIELD` or the attempt is `ERR_AUDIT_INVALID_STATE`. The cited claim must satisfy five conditions at once: it must belong to the audited dealer, its `status` must be `APPROVED`, its `payment_status` must be either `UNPAID` or `PAID`, its `repair_date` must lie inside the half-open audit period, and it must not already be cited by another finding of the same audit. Any failure is `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_ELIGIBLE_FOR_FINDING`. A new finding opens in `OPEN` with `appeal_id` null; `OVERTURNED` and `UPHELD` are its terminal states. Findings can be read individually and listed with filters on `audit_id`, `claim_id`, `appeal_id` and `status`, which is the only channel exposing the cascade-set `audit_id` and `appeal_id` links.

Issuing the findings is where the audit becomes money, and its internal ordering is normative because the very fields it reads in its first steps are the fields its last step mutates. The `AuditManager` issues from `IN_FIELD` only; anything else is `ERR_AUDIT_INVALID_STATE`. Six computational steps run, in this exact order, before anything is written back.

The first step pins the sampled denominator. `sample_paid_snapshot_cents` is the sum of `approved_total_cents` over every claim that is named by one of this audit's findings, belongs to the audited dealer, is in `APPROVED`, has `payment_status` `PAID` at this instant — before any chargeback has touched it — and has a `repair_date` inside the half-open period. The second step pins the unsampled remainder: the same sum is taken over every claim of the dealer in `APPROVED` with `payment_status` `PAID` and a `repair_date` inside the period regardless of whether a finding names it, and `population_paid_snapshot_cents` is that figure less `sample_paid_snapshot_cents`. Both snapshots are persisted on the audit, and every later appeal decision recomputes against them rather than re-reading live payment statuses — which is precisely why the audit row carries them at all.

The third step totals the unsupported amount still standing. `sample_chargeback_cents` is the sum of `unsupported_amount_cents` over this audit's findings whose `status` is `OPEN`, `CHARGED_BACK` or `UPHELD`; findings in `OVERTURNED` are excluded. This is the only term an appeal ever changes. The fourth step derives `error_rate`: when the pinned sampled denominator is zero the rate is zero, and otherwise it is `sample_chargeback_cents` divided by `sample_paid_snapshot_cents`, rounded half up to four decimal places. The fifth step selects the extrapolation tier from that rate, with both boundaries inclusive on their lower edge: a rate of at least 0.10 gives an `extrapolation_factor` of 1.00, a rate of at least 0.05 but below 0.10 gives 0.50, and anything below 0.05 gives 0.00 — a dealer whose sampled error rate is under five percent is not extrapolated against at all. The sixth step multiplies `population_paid_snapshot_cents` by `error_rate` and then by `extrapolation_factor`, rounds that product half up to whole cents as `extrapolated_chargeback_cents`, and sets `total_chargeback_cents` to `sample_chargeback_cents` plus `extrapolated_chargeback_cents`.

Only now does anything mutate. Every finding of this audit in `OPEN` moves `OPEN → CHARGED_BACK`, each advancing its own `version` by 1. Every sampled claim currently in `PAID` moves `PAID → DEBITED` with its `debited_amount_cents` increased by its own finding's `unsupported_amount_cents` and its `version` advanced by 1. One ledger row with `source_type` `AUDIT_CHARGEBACK_SAMPLE` is appended per charged-back finding, each carrying that finding's `claim_id` and its positive `unsupported_amount_cents`, so the sampled rows sum to `sample_chargeback_cents`. Then, and only when `extrapolated_chargeback_cents` is greater than zero, a single further row with `source_type` `AUDIT_CHARGEBACK_EXTRAPOLATION` is appended for that positive amount with its `claim_id` left null, because extrapolated money is by construction not attributable to any one claim. Every append recomputes the dealer's `chargeback_balance_cents` and `status`, so issuing findings can push the dealer into `PROBATION` or `SUSPENDED` and thereby suspend all of that dealer's `SUBMITTED` claims with `DEALER_POSTURE_HOLD` edits exactly as the sweep of §3.7 does; the dealer's `version` advances by 1. The audit moves `IN_FIELD → FINDINGS_ISSUED`, sets `findings_issued_at` to the current logical-clock instant, sets `appeal_deadline` to that instant advanced by the governing statute's `appeal_window_days` days, and advances its own `version` by 1. An audit with no findings at all issues perfectly cleanly: every chargeback figure stays 0, no ledger row is appended, and the appeal window opens anyway.

The dealer answers by appealing. A `DealerWarrantyAdmin` files an appeal against an audit in `FINDINGS_ISSUED`, supplying non-empty written `grounds`, a `contested_finding_ids` array holding at least one finding identifier, and the `expected_audit_version` of §2.9. The filing must arrive while the logical clock is at or before `appeal_deadline` — the boundary instant itself is still open — and a late filing is refused with `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_CLOSED`. Every identifier in `contested_finding_ids` must name a finding belonging to the audit in question and must find that finding in `CHARGED_BACK`; otherwise the filing is refused with `ERR_FINDING_NOT_IN_AUDIT`. On acceptance the appeal opens in `FILED` with `filed_by_dealer_id` taken from `X-User-ID`, `overturned_finding_ids` an empty array, `reviewer_user_id` and `decision_note` null and `reinstated_amount_cents` at 0; each contested finding has its `appeal_id` stamped with the new appeal; and the audit moves `FINDINGS_ISSUED → APPEALED` with its `version` advanced by 1. Appeals can be read individually and listed with filters on `audit_id`, `filed_by_dealer_id` and `status`.

An `AppealReviewer` decides the appeal by supplying an `action` of `GRANT`, `PARTIAL` or `DENY`, optionally an `overturned_finding_ids` array, and optionally a written `decision_note` recording the rationale. The appeal must be in `FILED`, or the request is `ERR_APPEAL_INVALID_STATE`. Independence is mandatory: the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the appealed audit's `lead_auditor_user_id`, and a reviewer who led the audit is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

The three actions are symmetric in structure and differ only in which findings survive. `GRANT` moves the appeal `FILED → GRANTED`, sets `overturned_finding_ids` equal to `contested_finding_ids`, and moves every contested finding `CHARGED_BACK → OVERTURNED`. `PARTIAL` requires `overturned_finding_ids` to be a non-empty strict subset of `contested_finding_ids`, moves the appeal `FILED → PARTIALLY_GRANTED`, moves the listed findings `CHARGED_BACK → OVERTURNED` and every remaining contested finding `CHARGED_BACK → UPHELD`. `DENY` moves the appeal `FILED → DENIED`, moves every contested finding `CHARGED_BACK → UPHELD`, and leaves the appeal's `reinstated_amount_cents` at 0. In all three cases `reviewer_user_id` is stamped from `X-User-ID`, every touched finding advances its own `version` by 1, and the appeal's `version` advances by 1.

Every decision then re-runs the third through sixth computational steps described above, against the audit's *persisted* snapshots rather than against live data: `sample_chargeback_cents` is re-totalled over the findings that are still `OPEN`, `CHARGED_BACK` or `UPHELD`, `error_rate` is recomputed against the unchanged `sample_paid_snapshot_cents`, the tier is re-selected, `extrapolated_chargeback_cents` is recomputed against the unchanged `population_paid_snapshot_cents`, and `total_chargeback_cents` is rewritten. Two reversal rows are then considered. A row with `source_type` `APPEAL_REVERSAL_SAMPLE` carries the sampled delta — the previous `sample_chargeback_cents` less the new one — as a negative amount, and a row with `source_type` `APPEAL_REVERSAL_EXTRAPOLATION` carries the extrapolation delta — the previous `extrapolated_chargeback_cents` less the new one — likewise as a negative amount, each with `claim_id` null. Each row is omitted entirely when its own delta is zero, which is why a denied appeal typically appends nothing at all. Each claim named by a finding that has just become `OVERTURNED` has its `debited_amount_cents` reduced by that finding's `unsupported_amount_cents`, has its `reinstated_amount_cents` increased by the same figure, moves `DEBITED → CREDIT_REINSTATED`, and advances its `version` by 1. The appeal's own `reinstated_amount_cents` records the audit's `total_chargeback_cents` before the decision less its value after. The dealer's balance and posture are recomputed from the appended rows, and a dealer whose balance drops back below the probation threshold returns to `ACTIVE` and releases its posture-suspended claims back to `SUBMITTED` under exactly the conditions §3.7 states; the dealer's `version` advances by 1.

Where the audit goes next depends on the action, and this is what makes the audit lifecycle cyclic. A granted appeal — one whose reinstated set equals its contested set — drives the audit `APPEALED → CLOSED`, at which point the dealer's `audit_hold` is recomputed and becomes false when no other audit of that dealer remains open. A partially granted or a denied appeal instead drives the audit `APPEALED → FINDINGS_ISSUED`, stamping a fresh `findings_issued_at` at the current clock instant and a fresh `appeal_deadline` derived from it, so the dealer may appeal the re-issued findings again inside the new window. `FINDINGS_ISSUED → APPEALED → FINDINGS_ISSUED` may therefore repeat indefinitely, each round recomputing against the same two pinned denominators. The audit's `version` advances by 1 on every such move.

An `AuditManager` closes an audit manually once nothing is pending. The close accepts an audit in `FINDINGS_ISSUED` or in `APPEALED` — the latter deliberately, so that the pending-appeal rule rather than a generic state error is what speaks — and no appeal of that audit may still be in `FILED`, or the close is refused with `ERR_APPEAL_PENDING`. Since an audit in `APPEALED` always has an appeal in `FILED`, a manual close attempted from `APPEALED` always reports `ERR_APPEAL_PENDING`. A successful close moves the audit `FINDINGS_ISSUED → CLOSED`, moves every finding still in `CHARGED_BACK` to `UPHELD` with each such finding's `version` advancing by 1, recomputes the dealer's `audit_hold` and advances the dealer's `version` by 1, and advances the audit's own `version` by 1. `CLOSED` is terminal. Audits can be read individually and listed with filters on `dealer_id` and `status` alongside `limit` and `offset`.

### 3.9. Warranty Expense Analytics and Outlier Detection

The manufacturer detects dealers whose warranty spending is out of line with their peers through a computed read on one dealer, which optionally accepts a `window_start` and a `window_end`. When `window_start` is omitted it defaults to the logical clock less three hundred and sixty-five days; when `window_end` is omitted it defaults to the logical clock itself. The window is applied to each claim's `repair_date` and is half-open: a claim counts when its repair date is at or after the window start and strictly before the window end.

Two numerators are computed over that window on two different bases, and their divergence is the point of the report. The paid basis sums `approved_total_cents` over the dealer's claims that are in `APPROVED` and whose `payment_status` is exactly `PAID`, reporting the result as `paid_numerator_cents`; a claim that is approved but not yet paid is invisible on this basis, and a claim later charged back to `DEBITED` drops out of it entirely. The incurred basis sums `approved_total_cents` over the dealer's claims that are in `APPROVED` whatever their `payment_status` — `UNPAID`, `PAID`, `DEBITED` and `CREDIT_REINSTATED` all count — reporting the result as `incurred_numerator_cents`. On identical data the incurred figure is therefore always at least the paid figure. Alongside them the response reports `paid_claim_count` and `incurred_claim_count`, the number of claims each basis counted.

Each numerator is then divided by the dealer's `vehicles_in_operation` and rounded half up to whole cents, giving `warranty_expense_per_vehicle_paid_cents` and `warranty_expense_per_vehicle_incurred_cents`. A dealer with no vehicles in operation is a division by zero, and both per-vehicle figures are reported as 0 in that case rather than being computed.

The peer comparison always uses the paid basis and never the incurred one. The peer set is every dealer whose `vehicles_in_operation` is greater than one — more precisely, greater than zero — regardless of posture, so dealers in `ACTIVE`, `PROBATION` and `SUSPENDED` all participate, and the dealer under examination is included in its own peer set. Each peer's paid-basis expense per vehicle is computed over the same window, and the resulting values are sorted ascending by value with ties broken by the owning dealer's `created_at` ascending. With an odd number of peers, `peer_median_paid_cents` is the middle value of that sorted list. With an even number of peers, it is the mean of the two middle values, rounded half up to whole cents. An empty peer set yields a median of 0.

`outlier_ratio` is the dealer's own `warranty_expense_per_vehicle_paid_cents` divided by `peer_median_paid_cents`, rounded half up to four decimal places, and is reported as 0 when the median is 0. `outlier_flag` is true exactly when that ratio reaches 1.50 or more — a dealer spending half again what the median dealer spends per vehicle in operation is flagged — with the boundary inclusive. The computed flag is not merely reported: it is written back onto the dealer's persisted `outlier_flag`, and when that write actually changes the stored value the dealer's `version` advances by 1. The response also echoes `dealer_id`, the resolved `window_start` and `window_end`, and `vehicles_in_operation`, so a reader can see exactly which window and which denominator produced the figures.
