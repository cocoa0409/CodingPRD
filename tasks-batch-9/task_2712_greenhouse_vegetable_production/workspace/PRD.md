# Greenhouse Vegetable Production API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A controlled-environment greenhouse is a factory whose machinery is alive. Every decision a head grower makes — which cultivar goes into which compartment, how warm the air is held overnight, how much drain water is blended back into the feed, when bumblebee hives are swapped, which crew deleafs on Tuesday — lands eventually as pounds of graded fruit on a retailer's pallet, or as a shortfall penalty on an invoice. The commercial problem is that these decisions are currently tracked in a dozen disconnected places: yield curves in a spreadsheet, climate setpoints in the vendor's climate computer, scouting rounds on paper cards, labor hours in a payroll system, and weekly volume commitments in email threads with the retail buyer. Nobody can answer, on demand, whether the compartment that just went into a pest lock is the same compartment carrying next week's largest commitment.

This document specifies the Greenhouse Vegetable Production API, a single system of record that ties all of that together. The API models thirteen peer resource collections — growing compartments, cultivars, crop cycles, climate strategy windows, fertigation batches, scouting observations, biological-control releases, workers, labor tasks, harvest lots, retail programs, weekly commitments, and the allocation rows that link lots to weeks — and it enforces the agronomic, safety, fiscal and contractual rules that connect them. It is deliberately a *gating* system rather than an advisory one: a fertigation batch cannot be mixed into a compartment whose drainage has alarmed three times, a harvest crew cannot be sent into a compartment inside a beneficial-agent re-entry interval, and a week's pallets cannot be signed out by the same inspector who released the lots from cold storage.

Two design commitments run through the whole specification and are worth stating up front. First, **every derived figure is derived, never asserted**: postures, budgets, pack-out percentages, fill rates and penalties are computed by the server from primary inputs, and the only way to force a derived posture is an explicit, reason-carrying override. Second, **evaluation order is part of the contract**: when a request breaks several rules at once, the specification fixes exactly which rejection the caller sees, because growers use the returned error to decide what to fix first. Implementations that get the arithmetic right but the ordering wrong are not conformant.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the rules that hold everywhere in the API: authentication and role semantics, the response envelope, pagination, common resource properties, the logical clock, the posture-derivation machinery that couples compartments to their children, decimal discipline, stale-view protection, conserved-quantity invariants, and the global rejection precedence.

### 2.1. Operator Identity and Role-Based Access Control

Every request — reads included — must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting operator and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity in which they are acting. If either header is absent or present but empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). This check runs ahead of everything else, including path resolution, so a header-less call against an identifier that does not exist returns 401 and never 404.

Six roles are recognised, spelled exactly as follows: `GrowerManager`, `IrrigationTech`, `IPMScout`, `IPMCoordinator`, `HarvestSupervisor`, and `QualityInspector`. A request whose `X-User-Role` is not one of these six strings, or is one of them but is not listed among the roles authorised for the endpoint being called, is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403); an unrecognised role string is treated as an unauthorised role rather than as a malformed body. All six roles may read every collection and every single-resource fetch. Write authority is partitioned:

- `GrowerManager` is the head grower. This role owns compartment registration and posture overrides, cultivar registration, crop cycle planning and every crop-cycle lifecycle move, hive placements, pollination checks, climate strategy creation, energy-actuals recording and climate-window cancellation, worker registration, labor-task scheduling, retail program registration, commitment-week creation, allocation runs, and the logical clock. It shares labor-task transitions with `HarvestSupervisor`, owns the close-out of a commitment week (while shipment signing belongs to `QualityInspector`), and owns allocation revocation (while allocation confirmation belongs to `QualityInspector`).
- `IrrigationTech` mixes and delivers fertigation batches, reconciles or discards them, and logs drainage readings. It holds no other write authority.
- `IPMScout` files scouting observations, requests biological-control releases, and executes approved releases. A scout may also cancel a release request they raised; returning a request for revision and rejecting it belong to `IPMCoordinator`.
- `IPMCoordinator` approves release requests, optionally revising the agent count, and may return an approved request for revision or reject a requested one.
- `HarvestSupervisor` registers picked lots and owns the grading dimension of a lot, and shares labor-task transitions with `GrowerManager`.
- `QualityInspector` owns the cold chain dimension of a lot, confirms provisional allocations, and signs weekly shipments.

The relationship between `X-User-ID` and persisted data differs by role, and the difference is load-bearing. `IPMScout`, `IPMCoordinator`, and `HarvestSupervisor` are **entity-backed**: when `X-User-Role` is `IPMScout`, `IPMCoordinator`, or `HarvestSupervisor`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Worker` row, and ownership and segregation checks on any resource that references a `Worker` compare that resource's worker reference field to `X-User-ID`. For these three roles the header must resolve to a `Worker` whose `skill` matches the role: both `IPMScout` and `IPMCoordinator` must resolve to a `Worker` whose `skill` is `SCOUT`, and `HarvestSupervisor` must resolve to a `Worker` whose `skill` is `HARVEST`. When it does not resolve — no such `Worker`, or a `Worker` with the wrong `skill` — the request is rejected with `ERR_ACTOR_NOT_RECOGNIZED` (HTTP 403). This actor-resolution check applies to filing a scouting observation, requesting a biological-control release, approving one, executing one, driving a release through any of its other transitions, grading a lot, and regrading a lot.

The other three roles are **opaque**. When `X-User-Role` is `GrowerManager`, `IrrigationTech`, or `QualityInspector`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server never compares it to any entity's `id`. It may still be recorded — a `QualityInspector` identifier is stored as `cold_chain_released_by_user_id` on a lot when that inspector releases it from cold storage, and as `shipped_by_user_id` on a commitment week when that inspector signs the week's shipment — and those two recorded values are compared against each other for segregation of duties, but neither is resolved against a `Worker` row. Because they are opaque, these three roles skip the actor-resolution check entirely.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of rows returned; it defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` is the number of rows to skip before the first returned row; it defaults to 0.

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. The one deliberate exception is the allocation run, which returns the weeks it evaluated in waterfall order rather than creation order, as described in §3.9.

List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope. `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied, and `has_more` is true exactly when rows remain beyond the current page.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties, Timestamps and Versioning

Every resource carries a server-minted `id` formatted as a UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp rendered as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits (for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`).

Most resources also carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the resource is created and increments by one on every mutation of that resource. This includes mutations the server performs on its own initiative: when a cascade recomputes a compartment's budget split or a week's fill rate, that is a mutation of the affected resource and its `updated_at` and `version` move accordingly.

Two resource kinds are immutable once written and therefore carry only `id` and `created_at` — they have no `updated_at` field and no `version` field at all. A cultivar is one: cultivars are registered once and never edited, and there is no update operation for them. A scouting observation is the other: an observation is a permanent field record of what a scout saw at a moment in time, and nothing in the system ever rewrites it.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

Time in this system is a server-held **logical clock**, not wall-clock time. Before any client sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to the host's real time under any circumstance. Every temporal comparison in this specification — "is the harvest window open", "has the re-entry interval elapsed", "may this week be closed short" — is evaluated against the logical clock's current value.

A `GrowerManager` may set the clock to any instant by supplying a `current_utc` value. Setting the clock is not a passive operation: it has two documented consequences. First, every climate strategy window that is still `ACTIVE` and whose `window_end` is at or before the new instant transitions to `EXPIRED`. Second, every compartment's `ipm_posture` is re-derived, so a compartment whose `reentry_clear_at` has now passed drops back out of the clock-driven lock — to `SCOUT_ALERT` if open pest alerts remain, or to `NORMAL` if none do.

### 2.6. Compartment Posture Derivation and Cascade Discipline

A growing compartment carries three derived enumerations that no client ever writes directly: `status` (its occupancy posture), `ipm_posture` (the pest gate), and `irrigation_posture` (the fertigation gate). Each is re-derived from primary state whenever anything that feeds it changes — whenever `open_pest_alert_count`, `reentry_clear_at`, or `drain_alarm_count` moves, whenever any crop cycle in the compartment changes status, and whenever the logical clock is set. The derivation is deterministic and total:

- `ipm_posture` is `LOCKED` when `reentry_clear_at` is set and the current logical clock is strictly earlier than it. Otherwise it is `LOCKED` when `open_pest_alert_count` is 2 or more. Otherwise it is `SCOUT_ALERT` when `open_pest_alert_count` is 1 or more. Otherwise it is `NORMAL`. The two lock paths are independent: the clock-driven lock holds even when `open_pest_alert_count` has already fallen to 0 and clears by itself as the clock advances, while the counter-driven lock holds regardless of the re-entry clock and fires the harvest-task cancellation cascade once, on the crossing.
- `irrigation_posture` is `LOCKED` when `drain_alarm_count` is 3 or more, `WATCH` when it is 2, and `NORMAL` otherwise. A count of 1 leaves the compartment `NORMAL`.
- `status` is evaluated in this exact order, first match winning: `HARVESTING` when any crop cycle in the compartment is in `PRODUCTION`; otherwise `TURNAROUND` when any is in `TERMINATION_SCHEDULED`; otherwise `PLANTED` when any is in `PLANNED` or `TRANSPLANTED`; otherwise `IDLE`. A compartment whose every cycle has reached `TERMINATED` or `ABORTED` therefore reads `IDLE`, and a compartment holding one `PRODUCTION` cycle alongside one `PLANNED` cycle reads `HARVESTING`.

The only way to write a posture directly is a posture override, described in §3.5, and an override never reinstates whatever the posture destroyed on its way in.

Cascades in this system are **multi-hop and each hop is a real mutation**. When a drainage reading latches a batch alarm, that raises the compartment's `drain_alarm_count`; the raised count re-derives `irrigation_posture`; and an `irrigation_posture` that lands on `LOCKED` sets `nutrient_stress_flag` on every crop cycle in that compartment whose status is `TRANSPLANTED`, `PRODUCTION`, or `TERMINATION_SCHEDULED`. When a scouting observation pushes `open_pest_alert_count` to 2, the resulting `LOCKED` posture cancels the compartment's future scheduled harvest tasks, and each of those cancellations independently repays the compartment's labor budget and raises the owning cycle's `labor_shortfall_flag`. Implementations must run every hop, not just the first: the hardest failures to diagnose in a system like this are the ones where the primary effect landed and the secondary effect did not.

### 2.7. Decimal Discipline, Quantization Points and Rounding

All money, weight, volume, index and percentage arithmetic uses decimal arithmetic with **round half up**, never binary floating point. Values are returned in JSON as numbers, not strings.

The standard precision is two decimal places, and it governs every US-dollar amount, every pound figure, every kilowatt-hour and kilogram figure, every percentage, and every hour figure. Four exceptions exist and each is stated where it applies: a crop cycle's `density_factor` and a labor task's `productivity_ratio` are carried to four decimal places; a compartment's share of combined-heat-and-power gas cost between power and heat is carried to four decimal places; every electrical-conductivity figure in the fertigation blend — `provisional_blend_ec_ms` and `blended_ec_ms` — is carried to three decimal places, as is the pest pressure figure compared against an action threshold; and `injector_ml` is carried to one decimal place.

**Where a value is rounded matters as much as how many places it keeps.** Wherever this specification says a figure is rounded before it is used downstream, the rounded figure — not the full-precision one — is the input to the next step. The clearest case is the expected weekly yield curve: each week's pounds are rounded to two decimal places first, and the rounded weekly figures are then added to give the baseline total. Adding the unrounded products and rounding once at the end is a different answer, and it is the wrong one. Intermediate values that this document does not explicitly round stay at full precision until the next stated rounding point.

### 2.8. Stale-View Protection on Compartment and Lot Writes

Three write operations are protected by optimistic concurrency control against the *parent* resource they read before writing. In each case the client must send the parent's `version` as it last observed it, and a mismatch against the parent's current `version` is a rejection, not a merge.

Creating a climate strategy requires `expected_zone_version` as a non-optional input field, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target compartment. If that value does not equal the compartment's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ZONE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This guard is the only thing that can reject the request: because a climate window may legitimately supersede an overlapping active window, a second create carrying an already-superseded view of the compartment is a stale-view write rather than an invalid state transition.

A posture override likewise requires `expected_zone_version` as a non-optional input field carrying the client's last observed compartment `version`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ZONE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Overrides are legitimately repeatable — clearing a pest lock twice is not an invalid transition — so no state rule rejects the second write; only the stale version does.

Regrading a harvest lot requires `expected_lot_version` as a non-optional input field carrying the client's last observed `version` of that lot, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_LOT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Regrading is legitimately repeatable, and an allocation run against the lot advances the lot's `version` between two regrades; without this guard a client holding the pre-run view would overwrite a grade split that no longer matches the pounds already allocated out of it. Each successive regrade therefore needs a freshly read `expected_lot_version`.

No other endpoint accepts a version field, and no other endpoint performs a version check. Where a version field is required it is required absolutely: omitting it is a missing-required-field failure, not an opt-out of the check.

### 2.9. Conserved Quantities

Four pairs of figures in this system are conserved: the two halves must always add up to the whole, at every instant, after every cascade.

A compartment's `labor_budget_usd` is split into `labor_budget_committed_usd` and `labor_budget_remaining_usd`, and the two always sum to `labor_budget_usd`. The committed side is the sum of `planned_cost_usd` over the compartment's labor tasks in `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS`, plus `actual_cost_usd` over its tasks in `COMPLETED`. Whenever a task is scheduled, completed, cancelled, or marked a no-show, both halves move by exactly the same amount in opposite directions.

A harvest lot's graded pounds are similarly split per grade: `grade_a_lbs` always equals `allocated_a_lbs` plus `available_a_lbs`, and `grade_b_lbs` always equals `allocated_b_lbs` plus `available_b_lbs`. The allocated side counts allocation rows in `PROVISIONAL`, `CONFIRMED`, or `RELEASED`; a `REVOKED` row counts toward neither and its pounds return to the available pool by exactly the amount that left it.

A compartment's `hive_slots_used` is the sum of `hive_count_placed` over its crop cycles in `TRANSPLANTED`, `PRODUCTION`, or `TERMINATION_SCHEDULED`, and it can never exceed `hive_slot_capacity`.

A worker's `committed_hours` is the sum of `planned_hours` over that worker's labor tasks in `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS` — measured hours never enter this figure, and completion removes the planned hours rather than replacing them.

### 2.10. Rejection Precedence

Validation rules are evaluated in a strict ascending priority order, and **the first violated rule determines the response**. No later rule is evaluated once one has fired, so a request that breaks several rules at once always returns the error belonging to the lowest-numbered rule it broke. The order is deliberate, and several places in §3 depend on it.

1. Authentication headers present and non-empty — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`, 401. Runs before path resolution.
2. The acting role is one of the six declared roles and is authorised for the endpoint — `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`, 403.
3. For the entity-backed roles, `X-User-ID` resolves to a `Worker` with the matching `skill` — `ERR_ACTOR_NOT_RECOGNIZED`, 403.
4. Every field the operation declares as required is present and of the declared type — `ERR_VALIDATION`, 422. Computed and system-managed fields that appear in a payload are silently ignored rather than rejected.
5. Every enum-typed field carries a declared member of its enumeration — `ERR_INVALID_ENUM_VALUE`, 422. This covers `glazing_type`, `crop_type`, `regime`, `pest_code`, `agent_code`, `skill`, `task_type`, `grade_spec`, `target_field`, and every `to_status`.
6. Every identifier in the request path exists, validated left to right — the matching not-found error, 404. Path existence outranks body existence, so a call with both a bad path identifier and a bad body foreign key returns the path error.
7. Every foreign key in the request body references an existing row — the matching not-found error, 404. Body foreign keys are validated in the order the operation's input list declares them.
8. The supplied `expected_zone_version` or `expected_lot_version` equals the parent's current `version` — `ERR_STALE_ZONE_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_LOT_VERSION`, 412. The stale-view guard runs ahead of every state and business check, so a stale version on an otherwise doomed request still returns 412.
9. The entity is not already terminal for the dimension the request touches — `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE`, 409. The terminal sets are: a crop cycle in `TERMINATED` or `ABORTED`; a climate strategy in `SUPERSEDED`, `EXPIRED`, or `CANCELLED`; a fertigation batch in `RECONCILED` or `DISCARDED`; a release request in `RELEASED`, `REJECTED`, or `CANCELLED`; a labor task in `COMPLETED`, `CANCELLED`, or `NO_SHOW`; a lot whose `cold_chain_status` is `RELEASED` or `REJECTED`; a commitment week in `CLOSED_FULFILLED` or `CLOSED_SHORT`; an allocation row in `RELEASED` or `REVOKED`.
10. The requested from-state to to-state pair is one this specification declares for that entity — `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, 409. This also catches a repeated non-terminal trigger, such as grading a lot whose `grading_status` is already `GRADED` (a restatement must go through the regrade operation).
11. For an allocation-row action, the parent commitment week is in the state that action needs — `ERR_WEEK_NOT_ALLOCATED` or `ERR_WEEK_ALREADY_SHIPPED`, 409. Confirming requires the week to be `ALLOCATED`; revoking requires it to be `OPEN` or `ALLOCATED`. This cross-entity week check is evaluated *before* the lot's cold chain and grading checks at priority 16.
12. Shipping a week requires at least one allocation row on it in `CONFIRMED` — `ERR_NO_CONFIRMED_ALLOCATIONS`, 409. A week cannot ship on provisional pounds alone, and this is checked before the segregation-of-duties rule at priority 18.
13. The compartment's `ipm_posture` is not `LOCKED` — `ERR_ZONE_IPM_LOCKED`, 409.
14. The current logical clock is at or after the compartment's `reentry_clear_at` whenever that field is set — `ERR_REENTRY_INTERVAL_ACTIVE`, 409. This is kept separate from posture because a release can clear the alert counter while the interval is still open.
15. The compartment's `irrigation_posture` is not `LOCKED` — `ERR_IRRIGATION_LOCKED`, 409. A `WATCH` posture is permitted; only `LOCKED` blocks.
16. The lot's cold chain and grading dimensions allow the requested commercial action — `ERR_LOT_NOT_COLD_HELD` or `ERR_LOT_NOT_GRADED`, 409. Cold chain is checked before grading, so an ungraded ambient lot returns `ERR_LOT_NOT_COLD_HELD`.
17. The acting `X-User-ID` differs from the justifying observation's `observed_by_worker_id` — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, 403.
18. The acting `X-User-ID` differs from `cold_chain_released_by_user_id` on every lot allocated to the week being shipped — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, 403.
19. Natural-key uniqueness — `ERR_ZONE_CODE_DUPLICATE`, `ERR_PROGRAM_CODE_DUPLICATE`, or `ERR_ISO_WEEK_DUPLICATE`, 409.
20. Climate setpoints are inside range, and only then, that the compartment has the equipment those setpoints demand — `ERR_SETPOINT_OUT_OF_RANGE` or `ERR_ZONE_CAPABILITY_MISSING`, 422.
21. A cultivar's `yield_curve_lbs_per_sqft` has the arithmetically required length — `ERR_YIELD_CURVE_LENGTH_MISMATCH`, 422.
22. A grade split balances against gross weight — `ERR_MASS_BALANCE_MISMATCH`, 422.
23. A regrade does not push a grade below the pounds already allocated from it — `ERR_ALLOCATED_EXCEEDS_GRADE`, 409. The mass-balance rule at 22 runs first.
24. Pack-out is at or above the minimum unless the caller acknowledged a low pack-out — `ERR_PACK_OUT_BELOW_MINIMUM`, 422.
25. A release request's `revision_count` is below the revision cap — `ERR_MAX_REVISIONS_EXCEEDED`, 409.
26. An executed release's `actual_release_count` is inside tolerance of `approved_release_count` — `ERR_RELEASE_COUNT_OUT_OF_TOLERANCE`, 422.
27. A hive placement keeps `hive_slots_used` within `hive_slot_capacity` — `ERR_HIVE_SLOTS_EXCEEDED`, 409.
28. `window_end` is strictly after `window_start` — `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`, 422. Zero-length and inverted windows are both rejected.
29. No active climate strategy on the same compartment and `regime` overlaps the requested window unless `supersede` is true — `ERR_CLIMATE_WINDOW_OVERLAP`, 409. Evaluated after the stale-view check at priority 8.
30. The assigned worker is not double-booked once each window is widened by the worker buffer — `ERR_WORKER_DOUBLE_BOOKED`, 409.
31. `harvest_date` is on or after the cycle's `first_harvest_allowed_date` — `ERR_HARVEST_TOO_EARLY`, 422.
32. No other live crop cycle occupies the compartment over the requested date range — `ERR_ZONE_OCCUPIED`, 409.
33. An open week may only be closed once its delivery window has elapsed — `ERR_WEEK_STILL_OPEN`, 409.
34. The program behind the week is `ACTIVE` — `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_ACTIVE`, 409. This is a cross-entity check on the grandparent program, and it applies both to allocation runs and to commitment-week transitions.
35. The assigned worker is `ACTIVE`, and only then, that their `skill` equals the requested `task_type` — `ERR_WORKER_INACTIVE` or `ERR_WORKER_SKILL_MISMATCH`, 409. Employment status is checked before the skill match, so an inactive worker with the wrong skill returns `ERR_WORKER_INACTIVE`.
36. A batch's `target_ec_ms` is within recipe tolerance of the cultivar's `ec_target_ms` — `ERR_TARGET_EC_OFF_RECIPE`, 422.
37. The justifying observation is at or above threshold, and only then, that it belongs to the release's own compartment — `ERR_OBSERVATION_BELOW_THRESHOLD` or `ERR_OBSERVATION_ZONE_MISMATCH`, 409. Threshold status is checked before the compartment match, so a below-threshold observation from another compartment returns `ERR_OBSERVATION_BELOW_THRESHOLD`.
38. A child row's `zone_id` equals the `zone_id` of the crop cycle it references — `ERR_CROP_CYCLE_ZONE_MISMATCH`, 409. This applies to fertigation batches, labor tasks, and scouting observations.
39. The compartment's `labor_budget_remaining_usd` covers the task's `planned_cost_usd` — `ERR_LABOR_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`, 409. This is deliberately the last group, so a budget-busting harvest task in a pest-locked compartment reports the safety error at priority 13 rather than the fiscal one.
40. An allocation run finds at least one eligible pound — `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_LOTS`, 422.
41. The pounds being confirmed do not exceed the lot's available pounds for that grade — `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_AVAILABLE_LBS`, 409.

### 2.11. Flat Topology and Path Resolution

All thirteen collections are peers mounted at the API root; there are no nested creation paths. A child references its parents through foreign-key fields in the request body — a crop cycle names its compartment and its cultivar, a fertigation batch names its compartment and its crop cycle, a labor task names its compartment, its cycle and its worker — and the pairs must agree. Because a batch, a task, or an observation names both a compartment and a crop cycle, the system checks that the crop cycle actually grows in the named compartment and rejects a disagreement with `ERR_CROP_CYCLE_ZONE_MISMATCH`.

Where a path does carry an identifier, or carries an identifier followed by a sub-path segment, identifiers are resolved strictly left to right and the first one that does not exist determines the 404. The per-resource not-found codes are `ERR_ZONE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VARIETY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CROP_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLIMATE_STRATEGY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FERTIGATION_BATCH_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SCOUTING_OBSERVATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BIO_CONTROL_RELEASE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WORKER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LABOR_TASK_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_HARVEST_LOT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RETAIL_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COMMITMENT_WEEK_NOT_FOUND`, and `ERR_LOT_ALLOCATION_NOT_FOUND`. The same codes serve for body foreign keys: an unknown `variety_id` yields `ERR_VARIETY_NOT_FOUND`, an unknown `worker_id` yields `ERR_WORKER_NOT_FOUND`, an unknown `observation_id` yields `ERR_SCOUTING_OBSERVATION_NOT_FOUND`, an unknown `program_id` yields `ERR_RETAIL_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`, and an unknown `labor_task_id` yields `ERR_LABOR_TASK_NOT_FOUND`.

Allocation rows deserve a special note: no client ever creates one. They are minted only by an allocation run, and the only way to discover them is the filtered allocation list, which accepts `lot_id`, `commitment_week_id`, `grade`, and `status` filters.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

The narrative below follows the growing year rather than the resource list: a compartment is registered and a cultivar chosen, a cycle is planted and driven through production, its climate is planned and settled, it is fed and its drainage watched, it is scouted and treated, it is pollinated, it is worked by crews, it is picked and graded and chilled, and finally its pounds are allocated against retail commitments and paid out. Rules appear where the work happens.

### 3.1. Registering Compartments and Cultivars

A `GrowerManager` registers a growing compartment by supplying a `code` between 1 and 16 characters that must be unique across every compartment (a repeat is rejected with `ERR_ZONE_CODE_DUPLICATE`), an `area_sqft` of glazed growing area of at least 1000 and at most 200000 square feet, a `glazing_type` of either `GLASS` or `POLY`, three equipment booleans, a `hive_slot_capacity` between 0 and 40 inclusive, and a `labor_budget_usd` of at least 0 carried to two decimal places. The equipment booleans are `has_thermal_screen`, which declares whether a deployable thermal screen exists; `has_supplemental_lighting`, which declares whether HPS or LED supplemental lighting is installed; and `chp_connected`, which declares whether the compartment draws heat, power and carbon dioxide from the combined heat and power plant. That last flag is not decorative — it selects which of the two energy settlement variants applies to the compartment's climate windows, as §3.3 describes.

Newly registered compartments read `IDLE` for `status`, `NORMAL` for both `ipm_posture` and `irrigation_posture`, zero for `open_pest_alert_count`, `drain_alarm_count`, `hive_slots_used` and `climate_plan_revision`, `0.00` for `labor_budget_committed_usd` with `labor_budget_remaining_usd` equal to the full `labor_budget_usd`, a null `reentry_clear_at`, and `version` 1. The `area_sqft` figure is the single most load-bearing input in the system: it drives every per-square-foot yield projection and every energy demand figure. Compartments can be listed with optional `status`, `ipm_posture` and `irrigation_posture` filters, which is how a grower finds the compartments currently gated.

Cultivars are registered separately and are immutable once written. A cultivar carries a `name`, a `crop_type` of `TOMATO`, `CUCUMBER` or `PEPPER`, a `cycle_length_weeks` between 4 and 60 inclusive counting the whole span from transplant to termination, a `first_harvest_week` of at least 2 and at most `cycle_length_weeks` giving the one-based production week in which picking may first happen, a `plant_density_per_sqft` greater than 0 and at most 2 describing nominal plants per square foot at full density, a `yield_curve_lbs_per_sqft` array of per-week marketable pounds per square foot whose first element corresponds to `first_harvest_week`, a `grade_a_ratio_pct` between 0 and 100 that is an informational reference for graders and enters no calculation, a `requires_pollination` boolean declaring whether bumblebee pollination is agronomically necessary, and an `ec_target_ms` recommended feed electrical conductivity greater than 0 and at most 6 millisiemens per centimetre. Every element of `yield_curve_lbs_per_sqft` must be at least 0, and the array's length must equal `cycle_length_weeks` minus `first_harvest_week` plus one — a cultivar running 13 weeks with first harvest in week 4 must supply exactly 10 weekly figures. A length that does not match is rejected with `ERR_YIELD_CURVE_LENGTH_MISMATCH`, because the projected weekly yield array of every cycle built on this cultivar depends on that arithmetic holding. Cultivars can be listed with an optional `crop_type` filter.

### 3.2. Planning a Crop Cycle and Projecting Its Yield Curve

Planning a cycle means naming a compartment through `zone_id`, naming a cultivar through `variety_id`, giving a `transplant_date` as a calendar date in `YYYY-MM-DD` form on which seedlings enter the compartment, and stating `plants_transplanted`, the number of plants actually set, which must be at least 1. The cultivar reference is pinned at creation and is never mutated afterwards. From the cultivar the server derives two dates: `planned_end_date` is the `transplant_date` advanced by `cycle_length_weeks` multiplied by seven days and then pulled back one day, and `first_harvest_allowed_date` is the `transplant_date` advanced by `first_harvest_week` minus one, multiplied by seven days. A cycle transplanted on 2026-08-03 under a 13-week cultivar whose first harvest week is 4 therefore has a `planned_end_date` of 2026-10-31 and a `first_harvest_allowed_date` of 2026-08-24.

A compartment holds one live cycle at a time. A cycle occupies its compartment across the inclusive range from its `transplant_date` to its `planned_end_date`, and any cycle in `PLANNED`, `TRANSPLANTED`, `PRODUCTION`, or `TERMINATION_SCHEDULED` counts as live. A new cycle whose occupancy range touches a live cycle's range is rejected with `ERR_ZONE_OCCUPIED`, which means a replanting must transplant at least one full day after the previous cycle's `planned_end_date`. A freshly planned cycle sits in `PLANNED`, and the compartment it now occupies re-derives its occupancy `status` to `PLANTED`.

Creation also runs the **expected yield projection**, and the order of operations here is normative. First, the nominal plant population is the compartment's `area_sqft` multiplied by the cultivar's `plant_density_per_sqft`. Second, `density_factor` is `plants_transplanted` divided by that nominal population, rounded half up to four decimal places, and then capped at 1.2 — a grower who sets more plants than the nominal density supports gains no more than a twenty percent yield uplift from crowding. Third, each element of `expected_weekly_yield_lbs` is the corresponding element of the cultivar's `yield_curve_lbs_per_sqft`, multiplied by `area_sqft`, multiplied by `density_factor`, and rounded half up to two decimal places — **each week is rounded before anything is summed**. Fourth, `baseline_total_yield_lbs` is the sum of those already-rounded weekly figures, itself rounded half up to two decimal places. Fifth, `expected_total_yield_lbs` is `baseline_total_yield_lbs` multiplied by the quantity one minus `pollination_haircut_pct` divided by one hundred, rounded half up to two decimal places; since `pollination_haircut_pct` is `0.00` until a pollination check moves it, a newly planned cycle's `expected_total_yield_lbs` equals its `baseline_total_yield_lbs`. Summing the unrounded weekly products and rounding only once at the end produces a figure that can differ by a cent from the normative round-then-sum result; implementations must reproduce the round-then-sum figure. `baseline_total_yield_lbs` is retained permanently so that a pollination haircut can be applied and reversed without losing the original projection.

Two more derived figures track the cycle against its projection. `cumulative_harvested_lbs` is the sum of `gross_weight_lbs` over the cycle's harvest lots whose `cold_chain_status` is anything other than `REJECTED`, and it starts at `0.00`. `yield_variance_pct` is one hundred multiplied by the difference between `cumulative_harvested_lbs` and `expected_total_yield_lbs`, divided by `expected_total_yield_lbs`, rounded half up to two decimal places; it starts at `0.00` and is recomputed whenever either input moves. Cycles are listable with `zone_id`, `variety_id` and `status` filters.

### 3.3. Driving a Crop Cycle Through Its Lifecycle

A cycle's `status` walks `PLANNED` → `TRANSPLANTED` → `PRODUCTION` and then leaves either through `TERMINATION_SCHEDULED` → `TERMINATED` or directly to `ABORTED`. Only a `GrowerManager` may drive it, and each move names the desired `to_status`.

Moving `PLANNED` → `TRANSPLANTED` requires the cycle to be in `PLANNED` and requires that no other cycle in the same compartment is currently in `TRANSPLANTED`, `PRODUCTION`, or `TERMINATION_SCHEDULED`; a conflict is rejected with `ERR_ZONE_OCCUPIED`. The move may optionally carry a revised `plants_transplanted`, and when that value differs from the planned figure the entire expected yield projection of §3.2 is re-run from the new plant count. The compartment's occupancy `status` is re-derived and reads `PLANTED` while the cycle sits in `TRANSPLANTED`, and the compartment's `version` increments by one.

Moving `TRANSPLANTED` → `PRODUCTION` requires the cycle to be in `TRANSPLANTED` and requires the current logical clock date to be at or after the cycle's `first_harvest_allowed_date`; an earlier attempt is rejected with `ERR_PRODUCTION_TOO_EARLY`. Once in `PRODUCTION` the cycle becomes eligible to receive harvest lots, and the compartment's occupancy `status` re-derives to `HARVESTING`.

Scheduling termination moves `PRODUCTION` → `TERMINATION_SCHEDULED` and carries a `termination_date`, the requested crop-removal date. The cycle must be in `PRODUCTION`; the `termination_date` must be on or after the current logical clock date; and it must be no later than the `planned_end_date` advanced by a fourteen-day grace period, with a later date rejected as `ERR_TERMINATION_DATE_OUT_OF_RANGE`. The date is recorded on the cycle, the compartment's occupancy `status` re-derives to `TURNAROUND`, and from this point a newly scheduled labor task on this cycle whose `window_start` falls after the `termination_date` is rejected with `ERR_TASK_AFTER_TERMINATION`.

Termination scheduling is reversible. A cycle in `TERMINATION_SCHEDULED` may be moved back to `PRODUCTION`, which clears `termination_date` back to null; the pair `PRODUCTION` → `TERMINATION_SCHEDULED` → `PRODUCTION` may repeat without limit, and the compartment's occupancy posture follows it each time.

Completing the cycle moves `TERMINATION_SCHEDULED` → `TERMINATED`, and this exit is a *clean* one: it demands that nothing is left open. No labor task on the cycle may be in `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS`, or the move is rejected with `ERR_OPEN_LABOR_TASKS`; and no harvest lot on the cycle may still be in `grading_status` `UNGRADED`, or the move is rejected with `ERR_UNGRADED_LOTS_REMAIN`. On success the cycle's `hive_count_placed` is set to 0, which decrements the compartment's `hive_slots_used` by the number of hives just released; the compartment's occupancy `status` is re-derived; and the compartment's `version` increments by one.

`ABORTED` is the divergent branch, reachable from either `TRANSPLANTED` or `PRODUCTION`, and it is a *forceful* exit: unlike `TERMINATED` it requires neither open tasks nor ungraded lots to be cleared beforehand, because it clears the tasks itself. The abort cascade runs in hops. The first hop transitions every labor task on the cycle whose status is `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS` to `CANCELLED` with `cancel_reason` set to the literal `CROP_CYCLE_ABORTED`. The second hop is caused by those cancellations rather than by the abort itself: each cancelled task removes its own `planned_cost_usd` from the compartment's `labor_budget_committed_usd`, restores exactly the same amount to `labor_budget_remaining_usd`, and subtracts its `planned_hours` from the assigned worker's `committed_hours`. The abort then sets the cycle's `hive_count_placed` to 0, decrementing the compartment's `hive_slots_used`, re-derives the compartment's occupancy `status`, and increments the compartment's `version` by one. Both `TERMINATED` and `ABORTED` are terminal: any further transition request against the cycle is rejected with `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE`.

### 3.4. Climate Strategy Windows: Planning Setpoints and Settling Energy

Climate is planned as a series of **setpoint windows**. A `GrowerManager` creates a window against a compartment through `zone_id` and declares which diurnal `regime` it belongs to, either `DAY` or `NIGHT`. The two regimes are independent occupancy tracks on the same compartment: a day window and a night window may overlap each other freely, but two active windows on the same compartment *and* the same regime may not. The window itself is half-open on the interval from `window_start` inclusive to `window_end` exclusive, both given as ISO 8601 UTC instants, and `window_end` must be strictly after `window_start` or the create is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`. Because the interval is half-open, a new window that starts exactly at an existing window's `window_end` does not overlap it.

Five setpoints describe the environment the window holds. `temp_setpoint_c` is the air temperature in degrees Celsius and must be at least 5 and at most 35. `rh_setpoint_pct` is the relative humidity in percent and must be at least 40 and at most 95; it is recorded for the climate computer and does not enter any cost figure. `co2_setpoint_ppm` is the carbon dioxide enrichment target in parts per million and must be at least 400 and at most 1200. `lighting_hours` is the number of supplemental lighting hours inside the window and must be at least 0 and no greater than the window's own duration in hours. `screen_position_pct` is the thermal screen closure percentage held through the window, between 0 and 100. Any of these falling outside its stated range is rejected with `ERR_SETPOINT_OUT_OF_RANGE`, and that includes `lighting_hours` exceeding the window duration. Only once every value is in range does the capability rule apply: `lighting_hours` must be exactly 0 when the compartment's `has_supplemental_lighting` is false, and `screen_position_pct` must be exactly 0 when the compartment's `has_thermal_screen` is false. Demanding equipment the compartment does not have is rejected with `ERR_ZONE_CAPABILITY_MISSING`.

Overlap handling is governed by the optional `supersede` boolean, which defaults to false. When an `ACTIVE` window on the same compartment and the same `regime` overlaps the requested half-open window and `supersede` is absent or false, the create is rejected with `ERR_CLIMATE_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. When `supersede` is true, the create is accepted and the overlapped `ACTIVE` window transitions `ACTIVE` → `SUPERSEDED`; any energy actuals and `settled_energy_cost_usd` already recorded on that superseded window are left exactly as they were, because superseding a plan does not unmake the energy it consumed. Every create — superseding or not — must carry `expected_zone_version` matching the compartment's current `version` as described in §2.8, increments the compartment's `climate_plan_revision` by one, and increments the compartment's `version` by one. The `climate_plan_revision` counter exists precisely so that each accepted window is an observable mutation of the compartment, which is what makes the stale-view guard meaningful across successive creates.

Creation immediately values the window under the **planning variant**. `window_hours` is the elapsed time from `window_start` to `window_end` expressed in hours and rounded half up to two decimal places. A screen credit factor is one minus the product of 0.35 and `screen_position_pct` divided by one hundred — a screen held fully closed therefore cuts thermal demand by thirty-five percent, and a screen held at forty percent closure cuts it by fourteen percent. `planned_heat_kwh` is 0.00042 multiplied by `area_sqft`, multiplied by the greater of zero and the difference between `temp_setpoint_c` and the fixed outside temperature of 8.0 degrees Celsius, multiplied by `window_hours`, multiplied by the screen credit factor, and rounded half up to two decimal places; a setpoint at or below the outside temperature therefore demands no heat at all rather than a negative amount. `planned_light_kwh` is 0.012 multiplied by `area_sqft`, multiplied by `lighting_hours`, rounded half up to two decimal places. `planned_co2_kg` is 0.00018 multiplied by `area_sqft`, multiplied by the greater of zero and the difference between `co2_setpoint_ppm` and the ambient level of 420 parts per million, divided by one hundred, multiplied by `window_hours`, rounded half up to two decimal places — enriching to ambient or below purchases no gas. Finally `projected_energy_cost_usd` is `planned_heat_kwh` valued at 0.045 US dollars per kilowatt-hour of boiler gas, plus `planned_light_kwh` valued at the grid purchase price of 0.14 US dollars per kilowatt-hour, plus `planned_co2_kg` valued at 0.09 US dollars per kilogram, with the sum rounded half up to two decimal places. A newly created window sits in `ACTIVE` with a null `actuals_recorded_at`, a null `settled_energy_cost_usd`, and a null `cost_variance_usd`.

Recording energy actuals against a window is a separate act by the `GrowerManager`, carrying four metered figures: `gas_consumed_m3`, the natural gas burned during the window in cubic metres; `chp_generated_kwh`, the electricity the combined heat and power engine generated during the window; `grid_import_kwh`, the electricity imported from the grid; and `screen_deployed_hours`, the hours the thermal screen was actually deployed. `screen_deployed_hours` is stored for audit and deliberately does not enter the cost. Actuals may be recorded only once — a second attempt against a window whose `actuals_recorded_at` is already set is rejected with `ERR_ACTUALS_ALREADY_RECORDED` — and acceptance stamps `actuals_recorded_at` with the current logical-clock instant.

Settlement then runs in one of two variants, selected by the compartment's `chp_connected` flag rather than by anything on the window. Both begin with a gas cost: `gas_consumed_m3` multiplied by 0.42 US dollars per cubic metre, rounded half up to two decimal places; and a grid cost: `grid_import_kwh` multiplied by 0.14 US dollars per kilowatt-hour, rounded half up to two decimal places.

For a compartment where `chp_connected` is true, the engine's gas bill is shared between the electricity it made and the heat it made, and only the heat share is charged to the crop. The heat recovered is `chp_generated_kwh` multiplied by 1.15, rounded half up to two decimal places. The power share is `chp_generated_kwh` divided by the sum of `chp_generated_kwh` and that recovered heat figure, rounded half up to four decimal places. The allocated power cost is the gas cost multiplied by the power share, rounded half up to two decimal places, and the allocated heat cost is the gas cost minus the allocated power cost, rounded half up to two decimal places. Flue gas from the engine offsets purchased carbon dioxide: the offset in kilograms is the lesser of `planned_co2_kg` and the quantity `chp_generated_kwh` multiplied by 0.18 rounded half up to two decimal places. The carbon dioxide purchase cost is then the greater of zero and the difference between `planned_co2_kg` and that offset — the difference itself rounded half up to two decimal places before the comparison — multiplied by 0.09 US dollars per kilogram, rounded half up to two decimal places. Surplus engine output earns an export credit: the greater of zero and the difference between `chp_generated_kwh` and `planned_light_kwh`, that difference rounded half up to two decimal places before the comparison, multiplied by the grid sale price of 0.06 US dollars per kilowatt-hour, rounded half up to two decimal places. `settled_energy_cost_usd` is then the allocated heat cost plus the grid cost plus the carbon dioxide purchase cost minus the export credit, rounded half up to two decimal places.

For a compartment where `chp_connected` is false, there is no engine to share the bill with: `settled_energy_cost_usd` is the whole gas cost plus the grid cost plus the quantity `planned_co2_kg` multiplied by 0.09 US dollars per kilogram rounded half up to two decimal places, with the sum rounded half up to two decimal places. No export credit and no flue-gas offset apply. The same metered actuals therefore settle to a substantially larger figure on a compartment without a plant connection than on one with it, which is exactly the economics the flag is modelling.

Either way, `cost_variance_usd` is `settled_energy_cost_usd` minus `projected_energy_cost_usd`, rounded half up to two decimal places, and it stays null until actuals are recorded.

An `ACTIVE` window may be cancelled by the `GrowerManager`, moving it `ACTIVE` → `CANCELLED`, but only while `actuals_recorded_at` is still null; a window with actuals on it is rejected with `ERR_ACTUALS_ALREADY_RECORDED`. Cancelling frees the interval so a later non-superseding create can occupy it. `SUPERSEDED` and `EXPIRED` are system-driven outcomes and cannot be requested by a client: `SUPERSEDED` arrives only through another create carrying `supersede` true, and `EXPIRED` arrives only when the logical clock advances to or past the window's `window_end`, as described in §2.5. All three of `SUPERSEDED`, `EXPIRED`, and `CANCELLED` are terminal, and each of the three frees the interval for a subsequent non-superseding create. Windows can be listed with `zone_id`, `regime` and `status` filters.

### 3.5. Fertigation: Blending, Recirculation Caps and Drainage Monitoring

An `IrrigationTech` mixes a nutrient batch for a specific cycle in a specific compartment, naming both through `zone_id` and `crop_cycle_id` — and the cycle must actually grow in that compartment or the mix is rejected with `ERR_CROP_CYCLE_ZONE_MISMATCH`. The cycle must additionally be operationally active, meaning its status is `TRANSPLANTED`, `PRODUCTION`, or `TERMINATION_SCHEDULED`; a `PLANNED`, `TERMINATED`, or `ABORTED` cycle is rejected with `ERR_CROP_CYCLE_NOT_ACTIVE`. Mixing is also blocked outright while the compartment's `irrigation_posture` is `LOCKED`, which is rejected with `ERR_IRRIGATION_LOCKED`; a `WATCH` posture permits mixing.

The batch payload names a `batch_date` on which the solution is mixed and a `planned_volume_liters` total delivery volume of at least 100 and at most 200000 litres, comprising fresh water plus recirculated drain water. `requested_recirculated_liters` is how much drain water the technician asks to blend back in, at least 0 and no more than `planned_volume_liters`. `reservoir_ec_ms` is the measured electrical conductivity of the drain reservoir in millisiemens per centimetre, at least 0 and at most 12. The recipe itself is `target_ec_ms`, the feed conductivity the injectors must reach, greater than 0 and at most 6; `target_ph`, the feed pH target, at least 4.5 and at most 7.0; and three nutrient concentrations in parts per million — `n_ppm` for nitrogen between 0 and 400, `k_ppm` for potassium between 0 and 600, and `ca_ppm` for calcium between 0 and 400. Finally `drain_target_pct` is the target drain fraction as a percentage of delivered volume, at least 10 and at most 45. The batch's `target_ec_ms` must sit within 0.8 millisiemens per centimetre of the cultivar behind the named crop cycle — the check reaches through `crop_cycle_id` to `variety_id` and reads that cultivar's `ec_target_ms` — and a deviation beyond that tolerance is rejected with `ERR_TARGET_EC_OFF_RECIPE`. Because the tolerance is anchored to the cultivar, a recipe that is perfectly valid for one cycle can be invalid for another.

Blending is a deliberate **single-pass** calculation, and the single pass is the interesting part. Step one computes the blend the technician asked for: the provisional fresh volume is `planned_volume_liters` minus `requested_recirculated_liters`, and `provisional_blend_ec_ms` is the provisional fresh volume multiplied by the fresh-water conductivity of 0.4 millisiemens per centimetre, plus `requested_recirculated_liters` multiplied by `reservoir_ec_ms`, all divided by `planned_volume_liters` and rounded half up to three decimal places. Step two selects `recirc_cap_pct` from that provisional figure alone: 60 when `provisional_blend_ec_ms` is at most 2.5, otherwise 40 when it is at most 3.2, otherwise 0. Step three applies the cap: `recirculated_liters_applied` is the lesser of `requested_recirculated_liters` and the whole number obtained by taking `planned_volume_liters` multiplied by `recirc_cap_pct`, divided by one hundred, and discarding any fraction; `fresh_liters_applied` is `planned_volume_liters` minus `recirculated_liters_applied`. Step four recomputes the real blend and the dose: `blended_ec_ms` is `fresh_liters_applied` multiplied by 0.4, plus `recirculated_liters_applied` multiplied by `reservoir_ec_ms`, all divided by `planned_volume_liters` and rounded half up to three decimal places; and `injector_ml` is the greater of zero and the difference between `target_ec_ms` and `blended_ec_ms`, multiplied by 85 millilitres of stock solution per unit of conductivity per thousand litres, multiplied by `planned_volume_liters` divided by one thousand, rounded half up to one decimal place.

**The cap is never revisited.** When the request is inside the cap, the technician's volume is honoured in full and `blended_ec_ms` equals `provisional_blend_ec_ms`. When the cap binds, the applied volume is clipped and the final blend lands *below* the conductivity tier that chose the cap — often below enough that recomputing the cap from `blended_ec_ms` would move it up a tier and allow the full requested volume after all. That second pass must not happen. The tier is pinned to the provisional figure so that the answer is a function of the request rather than of an iteration, and implementations that loop until the cap and the blend agree produce a different and non-conformant result.

A new batch sits in `MIXED`. From there it may be delivered or discarded. Delivering carries `delivered_volume_liters`, the volume actually reaching the crop, which must sit within five percent of `planned_volume_liters` or the transition is rejected with `ERR_DELIVERY_VOLUME_VARIANCE`; delivery also re-checks the compartment's `irrigation_posture` and is blocked with `ERR_IRRIGATION_LOCKED` if the posture has locked since the batch was mixed. Only after `MIXED` → `DELIVERED` do drainage readings become acceptable. Discarding moves `MIXED` → `DISCARDED`, is terminal, and no drainage reading may ever be filed against a discarded batch.

Drainage readings are a repeatable self-loop on a delivered batch. Each reading carries `drain_volume_liters`, `drain_ec_ms`, and a `reading_at` instant. The batch must be in `DELIVERED` — a reading against any other status is rejected with `ERR_BATCH_NOT_DELIVERED` — and `reading_at` must fall between midnight UTC opening the batch's `batch_date` and forty-eight hours after that midnight, inclusive at both ends; anything outside that two-day window is rejected with `ERR_READING_OUT_OF_WINDOW`. An accepted reading increments `drain_reading_count` by one, adds `drain_volume_liters` to `cumulative_drain_volume_liters`, records `drain_ec_ms` as `last_drain_ec_ms`, and recomputes `drain_fraction_pct` as one hundred multiplied by `cumulative_drain_volume_liters`, divided by `delivered_volume_liters`, rounded half up to two decimal places. Before delivery `drain_fraction_pct` is null, and `last_drain_ec_ms` is likewise null until the first accepted reading. When several readings share a `reading_at`, `last_drain_ec_ms` is taken from the most recent reading ordered by `reading_at` and then by `created_at`.

Each accepted reading is then evaluated for a **drain alarm**. The acceptable band runs from `drain_target_pct` minus ten percentage points to `drain_target_pct` plus ten percentage points, each bound rounded half up to two decimal places; a batch targeting a 25.00 percent drain fraction therefore has a band from 15.00 to 35.00. Three independent breaches exist. A high breach occurs when `drain_fraction_pct` is strictly above the upper bound. An electrical-conductivity breach occurs when `last_drain_ec_ms` is strictly above the reuse ceiling of 5.5 millisiemens per centimetre. A low breach occurs when `drain_fraction_pct` is strictly below the lower bound *and* `drain_reading_count` has reached at least 2 — a single early reading naturally sits below the band and must not alarm on its own. If any of the three holds and the batch's `drain_alarm` was still false, `drain_alarm` latches to true and the compartment's `drain_alarm_count` increments by exactly one. The latch is permanent: a later in-band reading never clears it, and because the alarm is per batch, a batch that has already alarmed never raises the compartment counter a second time no matter how many further readings breach.

That single counter increment starts the drainage cascade described in §2.6. Its second hop re-derives `irrigation_posture`, taking the compartment to `WATCH` when `drain_alarm_count` reaches 2 and to `LOCKED` when it reaches 3. Its third hop, caused by the `LOCKED` posture rather than by the reading, sets `nutrient_stress_flag` to true on every crop cycle in that compartment whose status is `TRANSPLANTED`, `PRODUCTION`, or `TERMINATION_SCHEDULED`. The compartment's `version` increments by one on each accepted reading.

Finally, a delivered batch may be reconciled, moving `DELIVERED` → `RECONCILED`, but only once at least one drainage reading has been accepted against it; a batch with `drain_reading_count` of zero is rejected with `ERR_NO_DRAIN_READINGS`. Reconciliation freezes `drain_fraction_pct`, and any further reading against the reconciled batch is rejected with `ERR_BATCH_NOT_DELIVERED`. Batches can be listed with `zone_id`, `crop_cycle_id` and `status` filters.

### 3.6. Integrated Pest Management: Scouting, Release Decisions and Re-entry

Scouting rounds are filed by an `IPMScout` against a compartment and a cycle, named through `zone_id` and `crop_cycle_id`; as with fertigation, the cycle must grow in the named compartment or the observation is rejected with `ERR_CROP_CYCLE_ZONE_MISMATCH`. The observation records `observed_at`, the instant the round was performed; a `pest_code` of `WHITEFLY`, `THRIPS`, `SPIDER_MITE`, or `APHID`; `plants_inspected`, the number of plants looked at, which must be at least 1 and is recorded as scouting effort without entering any comparison; and `pest_count_per_plant`, the mean count per inspected plant, which must be at least 0. The scout is not named in the body — `observed_by_worker_id` is taken from the `X-User-ID` header of the filing scout and never from the payload. Observations are immutable field records and can be listed with `zone_id`, `crop_cycle_id`, `observed_by_worker_id` and `threshold_status` filters.

Filing an observation immediately compares pressure against the action threshold. The pressure figure is `pest_count_per_plant` rounded half up to three decimal places, **and the rounding happens before the comparison, not after it**. `action_threshold` is looked up from the pest: 0.500 for `WHITEFLY`, 1.000 for `THRIPS`, 0.250 for `SPIDER_MITE`, and 0.750 for `APHID`. `threshold_status` is `BELOW` when the rounded pressure is strictly less than the threshold, `AT` when the two are exactly equal, and `ABOVE` when the pressure is strictly greater. Because rounding precedes the comparison, a whitefly count of 0.4996 rounds to 0.500 and lands on `AT` rather than `BELOW`, while 0.4994 rounds to 0.499 and lands on `BELOW`. `release_required` is true exactly when `threshold_status` is `AT` or `ABOVE`.

A `release_required` observation is what drives the compartment's pest gate. When the compartment's `open_pest_alert_count` was 0 before the observation and either its `reentry_clear_at` is null or the current logical clock has reached it, the counter rises to 1 and the compartment moves `NORMAL` → `SCOUT_ALERT`, with the compartment's `version` incrementing by one. When `open_pest_alert_count` was already 1 — one short of the lock count of 2 — the next at-or-above observation takes the compartment `SCOUT_ALERT` → `LOCKED`, and that crossing fires the deepest cascade in the system. Its first hop raises `open_pest_alert_count` to 2, which re-derives `ipm_posture` to `LOCKED`. Its second hop, caused by the locked posture rather than by the observation, transitions every labor task in that compartment whose status is `SCHEDULED`, whose `task_type` is `HARVEST`, and whose `window_start` is at or after the current logical clock, to `CANCELLED` with `cancel_reason` set to the literal `IPM_POSTURE_LOCK`. Its third hop, caused by each of those cancellations, sets the owning cycle's `labor_shortfall_flag` to true and returns that task's `planned_cost_usd` to the compartment's `labor_budget_remaining_usd` exactly as a manual cancellation would. The compartment's `version` increments by one. A below-threshold observation is filed normally, sets `release_required` false, and leaves the compartment's posture entirely untouched.

`open_pest_alert_count` has a precise definition worth stating on its own: it is the number of scouting observations on the compartment whose `threshold_status` is `AT` or `ABOVE` and whose justifying biological-control release has not reached `RELEASED`. An alert stays open until the treatment it demands has physically happened.

A scout requests a treatment by naming the compartment through `zone_id` and the justifying observation through `observation_id`, choosing an `agent_code` of `ENCARSIA`, `AMBLYSEIUS`, or `APHIDIUS`, proposing a `planned_release_count` of at least 100 agents, and giving a `planned_release_date`. The justifying observation must have `release_required` true, or the request is rejected with `ERR_OBSERVATION_BELOW_THRESHOLD`; only if it passes that test is its compartment compared with the request's `zone_id`, a disagreement being rejected with `ERR_OBSERVATION_ZONE_MISMATCH`. The acting worker must have `skill` `SCOUT` or the request is rejected with `ERR_WORKER_SKILL_MISMATCH`. On acceptance, `requested_by_worker_id` is taken from `X-User-ID`, the request sits in `REQUESTED` with `revision_count` 0, and `approved_release_count`, `decided_by_worker_id`, `decision_reason`, `released_at`, `actual_release_count` and `reentry_hours` are all null. Requests can be listed with `zone_id`, `observation_id`, `requested_by_worker_id`, `decided_by_worker_id` and `status` filters.

Approval belongs to an `IPMCoordinator`, and it is where segregation of duties bites. The request must be in `REQUESTED`; the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the justifying observation's `observed_by_worker_id`, or the approval is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — the scout who found the pest may not sign off the treatment it justifies, and the role check earlier in the sequence does not catch this because both actors are legitimately IPM staff resolving to workers with `skill` `SCOUT`; and the acting worker must itself resolve to a worker with `skill` `SCOUT` or the approval is rejected with `ERR_WORKER_SKILL_MISMATCH`. The coordinator may optionally supply a `revised_release_count` and an optional `decision_reason`. `approved_release_count` becomes `revised_release_count` when one was supplied and `planned_release_count` otherwise, `decided_by_worker_id` records the coordinator, and the request moves `REQUESTED` → `APPROVED`.

Approval is not final. A coordinator may return an approved request for revision, moving `APPROVED` → `REQUESTED` with a `decision_reason`, which increments `revision_count` by one, resets `approved_release_count` to null, clears `decided_by_worker_id`, and is subject to the same segregation-of-duties comparison against the observation's `observed_by_worker_id`. This is a genuine cycle: the pair `REQUESTED` → `APPROVED` → `REQUESTED` may repeat while `revision_count` remains below 2, and the third attempt to send it back — when `revision_count` already equals 2 — is rejected with `ERR_MAX_REVISIONS_EXCEEDED`, closing the loop.

Two other exits leave the request. A coordinator may reject a `REQUESTED` request outright, moving it `REQUESTED` → `REJECTED`; a `decision_reason` is mandatory on this move and its absence is rejected with `ERR_DECISION_REASON_REQUIRED`, the segregation-of-duties comparison applies, and `decided_by_worker_id` is recorded. Critically, rejection leaves the compartment's `open_pest_alert_count` unchanged, so the compartment stays alerted — refusing to treat a pest does not make the pest go away. A scout may instead cancel their own request, moving either `REQUESTED` → `CANCELLED` or `APPROVED` → `CANCELLED` with a `decision_reason`; `decided_by_worker_id` is recorded, `approved_release_count` is retained for audit when cancelling from `APPROVED`, and the alert counter is likewise left untouched. All three of `REJECTED`, `RELEASED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal.

Executing an approved release is the scout's job and is the only thing that closes an alert. The request must be in `APPROVED`, and the execution carries `released_at`, the instant the agents actually went out, and `actual_release_count`, the agents actually released. The count must sit inside a ten percent tolerance of `approved_release_count`, evaluated as a closed interval whose bounds are nine tenths and eleven tenths of `approved_release_count` with each bound rounded to a whole number of agents; a count outside that interval is rejected with `ERR_RELEASE_COUNT_OUT_OF_TOLERANCE`. `released_at` must be at or after the justifying observation's `observed_at`, since agents cannot be released before the pest was seen; an earlier instant is rejected with `ERR_RELEASE_BEFORE_OBSERVATION`.

Execution then does four things. It sets `reentry_hours` from the agent released — 4 hours for `ENCARSIA`, 6 hours for `AMBLYSEIUS`, and 12 hours for `APHIDIUS` — and moves the request `APPROVED` → `RELEASED`. It sets the compartment's `reentry_clear_at` to `released_at` advanced by `reentry_hours`. It decrements the compartment's `open_pest_alert_count` by one, because this release closes the alert its observation opened. And it re-derives `ipm_posture`, which is where the two lock paths of §2.6 diverge in a way that surprises implementers: even though the alert counter just fell, possibly to zero, the freshly set `reentry_clear_at` sits in the future, so the compartment stays `LOCKED` for the whole re-entry interval. Expressed as a self-loop, this is `LOCKED` → `LOCKED`. The compartment's `version` increments by one. The lock releases by itself when the logical clock advances to or past `reentry_clear_at`, at which point the compartment re-derives to `SCOUT_ALERT` if alerts remain open or `NORMAL` if none do.

There is exactly one manual way out of a posture lock, and it belongs to the `GrowerManager`: a **posture override** against the compartment. The override names a `target_field` of either `ipm_posture` or `irrigation_posture`, a `target_value` of `NORMAL`, an `override_reason` explaining why the grower is overruling the gate, and an `expected_zone_version` matching the compartment's current `version` as described in §2.8. An override of `ipm_posture` clears `open_pest_alert_count` to 0 and `reentry_clear_at` to null and sets `ipm_posture` to `NORMAL`, and it works from either `LOCKED` or `SCOUT_ALERT`. An override of `irrigation_posture` clears `drain_alarm_count` to 0 and sets `irrigation_posture` to `NORMAL`, but it deliberately leaves every crop cycle's `nutrient_stress_flag` at true — that flag is an audit record of what the crop experienced, not a mirror of the current posture. Either override increments the compartment's `version` by one. And in neither case are harvest tasks that the lock already cancelled ever reinstated: the crew did not show up, and the override cannot un-ring that bell.

### 3.7. Pollination Management and the Yield Haircut

Cultivars whose `requires_pollination` is true need bumblebees, and hives are placed onto a crop cycle by the `GrowerManager`. A placement supplies a `hive_count` and a `placed_at` instant. The placement adds to the cycle's `hive_count_placed` and sets `hives_placed_at` to the supplied `placed_at`, which **restarts the strength decay clock** from that instant. Two gates apply: the placement is blocked while the compartment's `ipm_posture` is `LOCKED`, rejected with `ERR_ZONE_IPM_LOCKED`, since nobody may walk into a locked compartment to set hives; and the compartment's `hive_slots_used`, which is the sum of `hive_count_placed` across every cycle in the compartment whose status is `TRANSPLANTED`, `PRODUCTION`, or `TERMINATION_SCHEDULED`, must remain within `hive_slot_capacity` after the placement, or the request is rejected with `ERR_HIVE_SLOTS_EXCEEDED`. That capacity check is an aggregate across the whole compartment, so each individual placement can look modest while the set of them overruns the compartment.

Hive vigour is then re-scored on demand through a **pollination check**, also a `GrowerManager` action, which carries an `observed_at` instant, a `flower_visit_pct` figure recording the share of flowers showing bee visitation, and a `hive_activity_count` recording bees counted at the hive entrance. Neither `flower_visit_pct` nor `hive_activity_count` is persisted — they are transient inputs to the scoring and are discarded once the score is written.

Scoring starts from hive age. The number of whole weeks since placement is the elapsed time from `hives_placed_at` to the check's `observed_at`, divided by seven days, with any fraction discarded. Base strength is one hundred multiplied by 0.88 raised to the power of those whole weeks, rounded half up to two decimal places — so a one-week-old hive scores 88.00, a two-week-old hive 77.44, and a four-week-old hive 59.97. Three penalties may then subtract from that base, and they accumulate. Eight points are deducted when `flower_visit_pct` is at least 70 and strictly below the target of 85. Ten points are deducted when `flower_visit_pct` is strictly below 70 — this is the low band and it is scored instead of the mid band, since the two conditions cannot both hold. A further ten points are deducted when `hive_activity_count` is strictly below 40 bees, independently of whichever visitation band applied.

`pollination_strength_index` is the base strength minus the accumulated penalty, clamped to lie between 0 and 100 inclusive, rounded half up to two decimal places. `pollination_risk_score` is one hundred minus that index, rounded half up to two decimal places. `pollination_haircut_pct` then follows a three-way decision: it is `0.00` whenever the cultivar's `requires_pollination` is false, regardless of how poor the bees look; otherwise it is 12 when `pollination_risk_score` is at or above the risk threshold of 30; otherwise it is `0.00`. A four-week-old hive checked at 78 percent flower visitation with 31 bees counted therefore loses 8 for the mid visitation band and 10 for low activity, giving an index of 41.97, a risk score of 58.03, and a twelve percent haircut. The same visitation and a healthy 55-bee count on a one-week-old hive gives an index of 80.00, a risk score of 20.00, and no haircut at all.

Applying the haircut re-derives the cycle's yield projection without destroying it: `expected_total_yield_lbs` becomes `baseline_total_yield_lbs` multiplied by the quantity one minus `pollination_haircut_pct` divided by one hundred, rounded half up to two decimal places, and `yield_variance_pct` is recomputed from the new expectation. Because `baseline_total_yield_lbs` is retained untouched, a later check that finds the bees recovered — dropping the risk score back below 30 — restores `expected_total_yield_lbs` to the baseline exactly. Every subsequent harvest is measured against whichever expectation is current, so a haircut applied mid-season moves `yield_variance_pct` for the rest of the cycle.

### 3.8. Workers, Productivity Standards and Labor Budget Discipline

Workers are registered by the `GrowerManager` with a `full_name`, a `skill` of `DELEAF`, `TRELLIS`, `HARVEST`, or `SCOUT`, and an `hourly_rate_usd` greater than 0. Two fields are optional at registration: `productivity_index`, a multiplier applied to the productivity standard of any task this worker is given, which must be at least 0.5 and at most 2.0 and defaults to `1.00`; and `status`, either `ACTIVE` or `INACTIVE`, which defaults to `ACTIVE`. A newly registered worker has `committed_hours` of `0.00`. The `skill` field carries real authority: it constrains which labor tasks the worker may be assigned, and only workers whose `skill` is `SCOUT` may file scouting observations or take biological-control decisions. Workers can be listed with `skill` and `status` filters.

A labor task is scheduled by the `GrowerManager` and names its compartment through `zone_id`, its cycle through `crop_cycle_id`, and its worker through `worker_id`. It declares a `task_type` of `DELEAF`, `TRELLIS`, or `HARVEST` — note that `SCOUT` is a worker skill but never a task type — a `window_start` and `window_end` bounding the work, a `planned_units` figure greater than 0 measuring the work to be done (plants for deleafing and trellising, pounds for harvesting), and a `standard_units_per_hour` productivity standard greater than 0 for that kind of work. For worker-occupancy purposes the work window is treated as closed at both ends, from `window_start` through `window_end` inclusive — deliberately different interval semantics from the half-open climate windows of §3.4.

Seven conditions gate the creation, and their relative order matters because a request can break several at once. The crop cycle must be in `TRANSPLANTED` or `PRODUCTION`, and any other status — including `TERMINATION_SCHEDULED` — is rejected with `ERR_CROP_CYCLE_NOT_ACTIVE`. The compartment's `ipm_posture` must not be `LOCKED`, rejected with `ERR_ZONE_IPM_LOCKED`, and the current logical clock must have reached the compartment's `reentry_clear_at` whenever that field is set, rejected with `ERR_REENTRY_INTERVAL_ACTIVE`; both of these safety gates outrank everything below them. The named cycle must grow in the named compartment, rejected with `ERR_CROP_CYCLE_ZONE_MISMATCH`. The worker's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, rejected with `ERR_WORKER_INACTIVE`, and only then is the worker's `skill` compared with `task_type`, a mismatch being rejected with `ERR_WORKER_SKILL_MISMATCH` — so an inactive worker holding the wrong skill reports the employment problem, not the skill problem. `window_end` must be strictly after `window_start`, rejected with `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`. The worker must not be double-booked: no other task on this worker in `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS` may have a window that, once widened by thirty minutes on both sides, overlaps the requested window by a positive duration. Touching exactly at a widened endpoint is not a conflict, so a task starting twenty minutes after another ends conflicts and is rejected with `ERR_WORKER_DOUBLE_BOOKED`, while a task starting exactly thirty minutes after another ends is accepted. If the cycle has a `termination_date` set, `window_start` must be at or before it, rejected with `ERR_TASK_AFTER_TERMINATION`. And last of all, the compartment's `labor_budget_remaining_usd` must be at least the task's computed `planned_cost_usd`, rejected with `ERR_LABOR_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`. That fiscal check is deliberately the final gate, which is why a budget-busting harvest task in a pest-locked compartment reports `ERR_ZONE_IPM_LOCKED` rather than `ERR_LABOR_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`.

Costing a task before it runs uses the **planned variant** of the wage calculation. `planned_hours` is `planned_units` divided by the product of `standard_units_per_hour` and the worker's `productivity_index`, rounded half up to two decimal places. The wage cost of any hours figure is overtime-aware: the lesser of those hours and the eight-hour overtime threshold is paid at the worker's `hourly_rate_usd`, plus the greater of zero and the hours in excess of eight, paid at `hourly_rate_usd` multiplied by an overtime factor of 1.5, with the sum rounded half up to two decimal places. `planned_cost_usd` is that wage cost evaluated on `planned_hours`. A harvest task of 4200 pounds against a standard of 520 pounds per hour worked by someone with a `productivity_index` of 1.05 at 19.50 US dollars an hour therefore plans 7.69 hours at a `planned_cost_usd` of 149.96.

Scheduling the task commits the money and the time: `planned_cost_usd` is added to the compartment's `labor_budget_committed_usd` and subtracted from `labor_budget_remaining_usd`, `planned_hours` is added to the worker's `committed_hours`, the compartment's `version` increments by one, and the task sits in `SCHEDULED` with null `actual_hours`, `actual_units`, `productivity_ratio`, `bonus_usd`, `actual_cost_usd` and `cancel_reason`. Tasks can be listed with `zone_id`, `crop_cycle_id`, `worker_id`, `task_type` and `status` filters.

Starting a task moves `SCHEDULED` → `IN_PROGRESS` and may be driven by a `GrowerManager` or a `HarvestSupervisor`. It requires the compartment's `ipm_posture` not to be `LOCKED`, rejected with `ERR_ZONE_IPM_LOCKED`; it requires the current logical clock to have reached the compartment's `reentry_clear_at` when set, rejected with `ERR_REENTRY_INTERVAL_ACTIVE`; and it requires the current logical clock to be at or after the task's `window_start`, an early start being rejected with `ERR_TASK_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN`. Starting changes nothing about the budget commitment.

Completing a task moves `IN_PROGRESS` → `COMPLETED` and carries `actual_hours` and `actual_units`, both of which must be greater than zero or the request is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION`. This runs the **actual variant** of the wage calculation. `productivity_ratio` is `actual_units` divided by `actual_hours`, that quotient divided by `standard_units_per_hour`, rounded half up to four decimal places. `bonus_usd` is `actual_units` multiplied by 0.02 US dollars per unit, rounded half up to two decimal places, when `productivity_ratio` is at or above the bonus threshold of 1.10, and `0.00` otherwise — the threshold is inclusive, so a ratio of exactly 1.1000 earns the bonus. `actual_cost_usd` is the same overtime-aware wage cost evaluated on `actual_hours`, plus `bonus_usd`, rounded half up to two decimal places. A crew that worked 9.0 hours to pick 5100 pounds against a 520-pound standard reaches a ratio of 1.0897, just under the threshold, earning no bonus and an `actual_cost_usd` of 185.25 — eight hours at the base rate plus one hour at time and a half. A crew that produced 4400 units in exactly 8.0 hours against a 500-unit standard reaches exactly 1.1000, earning an 88.00 bonus on top of a 156.00 wage for a total of 244.00. Completion then **replaces** this task's `planned_cost_usd` with its `actual_cost_usd` inside the compartment's `labor_budget_committed_usd` and recomputes `labor_budget_remaining_usd` accordingly, subtracts `planned_hours` from the worker's `committed_hours`, and increments the compartment's `version` by one.

Two divergent terminal exits share one release behaviour. Cancelling a task, reachable from either `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS` and carrying a `cancel_reason`, removes exactly `planned_cost_usd` from the compartment's `labor_budget_committed_usd`, restores exactly `planned_cost_usd` to `labor_budget_remaining_usd`, subtracts `planned_hours` from the worker's `committed_hours`, and increments the compartment's `version` by one. Marking a task a no-show, reachable only from `SCHEDULED` and also carrying a `cancel_reason`, performs the identical budget and worker-hours release and additionally requires that the current logical clock is strictly past the task's `window_end` — a worker cannot be declared absent while there is still time to turn up, and an early attempt is rejected with `ERR_TASK_WINDOW_STILL_OPEN`. A no-show pays no bonus and computes no `actual_cost_usd`. `COMPLETED`, `CANCELLED` and `NO_SHOW` are all terminal.

Beyond these operator-driven moves, a task may be cancelled by the system: the pest-lock cascade of §3.6 cancels a `SCHEDULED` task whose `task_type` is `HARVEST` and whose `window_start` is at or after the current logical clock, setting `cancel_reason` to `IPM_POSTURE_LOCK`, releasing the budget commitment exactly as a manual cancellation does, and setting the owning cycle's `labor_shortfall_flag` to true. The crop-cycle abort cascade of §3.3 likewise cancels every `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS` task on the cycle with `cancel_reason` `CROP_CYCLE_ABORTED`.

### 3.9. Harvest Lots: Grading and the Cold Chain

A picked lot is registered by the `HarvestSupervisor` against a crop cycle through `crop_cycle_id`, with a `harvest_date` and a `gross_weight_lbs` greater than 0. An optional `labor_task_id` may credit the harvest task that produced the lot. The lot's `harvest_date` must not fall before the cycle's `first_harvest_allowed_date` from §3.2, and an early pick is rejected with `ERR_HARVEST_TOO_EARLY`. Registration is also blocked while the compartment's `ipm_posture` is `LOCKED`, rejected with `ERR_ZONE_IPM_LOCKED`, and while the compartment's `reentry_clear_at` is still in the future relative to the logical clock, rejected with `ERR_REENTRY_INTERVAL_ACTIVE` — nobody picks from a compartment that was just sprayed. A new lot lands with `grading_status` `UNGRADED`, `cold_chain_status` `AMBIENT`, and `allocated_a_lbs`, `allocated_b_lbs`, `available_a_lbs` and `available_b_lbs` all `0.00`, while `grade_a_lbs`, `grade_b_lbs`, `cull_lbs`, `pack_out_pct`, `pulp_temp_c`, `graded_by_worker_id` and `cold_chain_released_by_user_id` are all null and `regrade_count` is 0. Registration adds `gross_weight_lbs` to the cycle's `cumulative_harvested_lbs` and re-derives its `yield_variance_pct`. Lots can be listed with `crop_cycle_id`, `labor_task_id`, `graded_by_worker_id`, `grading_status`, `cold_chain_status` and `harvest_date` filters.

A lot then carries **two independent state dimensions that evolve in parallel and never gate each other's ordering**: `grading_status`, which moves through `UNGRADED`, `GRADED` and `REGRADED`, and `cold_chain_status`, which moves through `AMBIENT`, `PRECOOLED`, `COLD_HELD` and then either `RELEASED` or `REJECTED`. A lot may be pre-cooled before it is graded or graded before it is pre-cooled; the two dimensions cross-reference each other only at the specific points named below. Neither `UNGRADED`, `GRADED` nor `REGRADED` is terminal — the grading dimension has no terminal state at all — while `RELEASED` and `REJECTED` are both terminal on the cold-chain dimension.

#### Grading and pack-out

Grading is a `HarvestSupervisor` action that splits the lot into `grade_a_lbs`, `grade_b_lbs` and `cull_lbs`. Those three figures must sum to exactly `gross_weight_lbs` — this is an exact equality, not a tolerance — and any other total is rejected with `ERR_MASS_BALANCE_MISMATCH`, checked before anything is written. The lot's `cold_chain_status` must not be `REJECTED`, since a condemned lot cannot be graded, and an attempt is rejected with `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE`. `pack_out_pct` is one hundred multiplied by the sum of `grade_a_lbs` and `grade_b_lbs`, divided by `gross_weight_lbs`, rounded half up to two decimal places — cull pounds are excluded from the numerator because they are unsaleable. `available_a_lbs` is `grade_a_lbs` minus `allocated_a_lbs` and `available_b_lbs` is `grade_b_lbs` minus `allocated_b_lbs`, each rounded half up to two decimal places. On an initial grade, where `grading_status` is `UNGRADED`, both allocated pools are still `0.00`, so availability simply equals the graded pounds. A 2000-pound lot split 1200 A, 700 B and 100 cull reaches a `pack_out_pct` of 95.00; a 3000-pound lot split 1800 A, 1000 B and 200 cull reaches 93.33. The initial grade moves `UNGRADED` → `GRADED` and records `graded_by_worker_id` from the `X-User-ID` header. Attempting the initial grade again on a lot already `GRADED` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`: a restatement must use the regrade capability instead.

Regrading restates the split of a lot that has already been graded, and it is the same `HarvestSupervisor` capability whether the lot is currently `GRADED` or already `REGRADED` — the edge is repeatable, moving `GRADED` → `REGRADED` the first time and `REGRADED` → `REGRADED` on every pass after that, with identical preconditions and identical effects on each pass. The lot's `grading_status` must be `GRADED` or `REGRADED`, and a still-`UNGRADED` lot is rejected with `ERR_LOT_NOT_GRADED`. The request must carry an `expected_lot_version` matching the lot's current `version` as described in §2.8, and each pass therefore needs a freshly read version. Mass must balance exactly as before. The new `grade_a_lbs` must be at least the lot's `allocated_a_lbs` and the new `grade_b_lbs` at least its `allocated_b_lbs`, because pounds already promised to a retail commitment cannot be graded away; a violation is rejected with `ERR_ALLOCATED_EXCEEDS_GRADE`, and this conservation rule — not a negative availability — is what a shrinking regrade runs into. The lot's `cold_chain_status` must not be `RELEASED`, since a shipped lot's grade is settled history, and an attempt is rejected with `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE`. A successful regrade runs the **regrade variant** of the pack-out calculation, which subtracts the already-allocated pounds from the new split when computing `available_a_lbs` and `available_b_lbs`, recomputes `pack_out_pct`, increments `regrade_count` by one and increments the lot's `version` by one.

Both grading paths apply the same pack-out floor. `MIN_PACK_OUT_PCT` is `80.00`, and a split whose computed `pack_out_pct` falls below that floor is rejected with `ERR_PACK_OUT_BELOW_MINIMUM` unless the request carries `low_pack_out_acknowledged` set to true. This is a conditional gate in the purest sense: the identical set of pound figures passes or fails purely on that flag. The flag is a transient acknowledgement and is **never persisted** on the lot, so a reader of the graded lot cannot tell whether a low pack-out was acknowledged; there is no field recording it.

#### The cold chain

Every cold-chain move is a `QualityInspector` action naming a `to_status` and a measured `pulp_temp_c`. The forward path advances strictly in order. `AMBIENT` → `PRECOOLED` requires `pulp_temp_c` at or below `PRECOOL_MAX_C` of 12.0 degrees Celsius, and a warmer reading is rejected with `ERR_COLD_CHAIN_BREACH`. `PRECOOLED` → `COLD_HELD` requires `pulp_temp_c` at or below the stricter `COLD_HOLD_MAX_C` of 8.0 degrees, again rejected with `ERR_COLD_CHAIN_BREACH` if the fruit has warmed; reaching `COLD_HELD` is what makes the lot eligible for allocation confirmation in §3.10. `COLD_HELD` → `RELEASED` requires `pulp_temp_c` at or below 8.0 degrees and additionally requires the **other** dimension to be settled: `grading_status` must be `GRADED` or `REGRADED`, and an ungraded lot is rejected with `ERR_LOT_NOT_GRADED`. Release records `cold_chain_released_by_user_id` from the releasing inspector's `X-User-ID`, and that identifier is retained specifically so that it can be compared against whoever signs the shipment in §3.10. Skipping a rung — asking for `COLD_HELD` from `AMBIENT`, for instance — is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, and any move at all out of `RELEASED` or `REJECTED` is rejected with `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE`.

Rejection is reachable from every non-released state: `AMBIENT` → `REJECTED`, `PRECOOLED` → `REJECTED` and `COLD_HELD` → `REJECTED` are all permitted and all behave identically, and they fire the allocation-unwind cascade. Every `LotAllocation` on the lot whose status is `PROVISIONAL` or `CONFIRMED` moves to `REVOKED` with `revoked_reason` set to `COLD_CHAIN_REJECT`. Each revocation restores its `allocated_lbs` to the lot's `allocated_a_lbs` or `allocated_b_lbs` and correspondingly to `available_a_lbs` or `available_b_lbs` according to its `grade`. Each revocation also reduces the affected `CommitmentWeek`'s `allocated_lbs`, and any week that was `ALLOCATED` but now falls short of its `committed_lbs` reverts to `OPEN`, with its program's `at_risk_week_count` incremented by one. Finally, the rejection removes the lot's `gross_weight_lbs` from the owning cycle's `cumulative_harvested_lbs` and recomputes that cycle's `yield_variance_pct` — condemned fruit was never really harvested. That is four hops from one temperature reading: lot pools, allocations, commitment weeks and program risk counters, plus the crop cycle's yield ledger.

### 3.10. Retail Programs, the Allocation Waterfall and Weekly Settlement

The commercial end of the greenhouse is a set of retail volume programs, each registered by the `GrowerManager` with a `retailer_name`, a `program_code` unique across all programs, a `grade_spec` of either `A_ONLY` or `A_AND_B`, a `weekly_commitment_lbs` greater than 0, a `price_per_lb_usd` greater than 0, a `shortfall_penalty_per_lb_usd` of at least 0, and a `priority_rank` between 1 and 99 where **a lower number is served first**. A duplicate `program_code` is rejected with `ERR_PROGRAM_CODE_DUPLICATE`. An optional `status` of `ACTIVE` or `PAUSED` defaults to `ACTIVE`; a `PAUSED` program is skipped by every allocation run. A new program starts with `at_risk_week_count` 0 and `penalty_accrued_usd` `0.00`. Programs can be listed with `status` and `grade_spec` filters.

Each program's obligations are broken into weeks. A commitment week is opened by the `GrowerManager` naming its `program_id`, an `iso_week` string of the form four digits, a literal `-W`, then two digits, and a `week_start_date`. The `iso_week` must be unique inside the program, and a repeat is rejected with `ERR_ISO_WEEK_DUPLICATE`; the same `iso_week` is perfectly legal under a different program. An optional `committed_lbs` overrides the volume for that week; when it is omitted the week inherits the program's `weekly_commitment_lbs`. The week opens in `OPEN` with `allocated_lbs` `0.00`, `shipped_lbs` `0.00`, `fill_rate_pct` `0.00` and `provisional_shortfall_lbs` equal to `committed_lbs`, since nothing has been promised yet. `final_shortfall_lbs`, `final_penalty_usd`, `gross_revenue_usd`, `net_payout_usd`, `shipped_at` and `shipped_by_user_id` are all null. Weeks can be listed with `program_id`, `week_start_date` and `status` filters.

#### The allocation waterfall

An **allocation run**, driven by the `GrowerManager`, is the only thing that creates `LotAllocation` records. It takes a single `week_start_date` and evaluates every eligible week for that date across every active program in one fully deterministic pass. There is no create capability for an allocation row and no way to hand-place pounds; the run's output is the only source.

The run first builds its **target set**: every `CommitmentWeek` whose `week_start_date` equals the run's `week_start_date`, whose `status` is `OPEN`, and whose program's `status` is `ACTIVE`. Targets are then ordered by the program's `priority_rank` ascending, and where two programs share a rank, by the program's `created_at` ascending. Ties are broken **only** by those two declared sort keys and never by identifier.

It then builds its **candidate lot set**: every `HarvestLot` whose `grading_status` is `GRADED` or `REGRADED`, whose `cold_chain_status` is `PRECOOLED` or `COLD_HELD`, and whose `harvest_date` falls at or after the run's `week_start_date` and strictly before that date plus the `LOT_ELIGIBILITY_WINDOW_DAYS` window of 7 days — a half-open seven-day window, so a lot picked exactly seven days after the week start belongs to the next week, not this one. Note that an `AMBIENT` lot is not yet eligible, and a `RELEASED` or `REJECTED` lot is no longer eligible. Candidates are ordered by `harvest_date` ascending, then by `created_at` ascending, so **the oldest eligible fruit drains first**. If the candidate set is empty the entire run is rejected with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_LOTS` rather than returning an empty allocation set.

The waterfall then walks the targets in order, and each target is filled as completely as the remaining pounds allow before the next target receives anything — pounds are never spread evenly. For each target, the need is `committed_lbs` minus the week's current `allocated_lbs`, rounded half up to two decimal places. The grades to draw from are grade `A` alone when the program's `grade_spec` is `A_ONLY`, and grade `A` followed by grade `B` when it is `A_AND_B`, with **grade A fully exhausted across every candidate lot before grade B is touched at all**. For each grade in that order, the run walks the candidate lots in their order; it stops walking as soon as the need has fallen to zero or below; it reads the lot's `available_a_lbs` for grade `A` or `available_b_lbs` for grade `B` as the pool; the amount taken is the lesser of the remaining need and that pool; a take of zero or less is skipped and the run moves to the next lot without minting anything. Otherwise a `LotAllocation` is created naming its `lot_id`, its `commitment_week_id`, its `grade` of `A` or `B`, an `allocated_lbs` equal to the take, an `allocated_at` instant from the logical clock, and a `status` of `PROVISIONAL`. The lot's pool falls **immediately** by the take, so the very next target in the waterfall sees the reduced availability, and the need is recomputed as the previous need minus the take, rounded half up to two decimal places.

Minting an allocation adds its `allocated_lbs` to the lot's `allocated_a_lbs` or `allocated_b_lbs` according to its `grade` and subtracts the same amount from the matching available pool, and adds the same amount to the week's `allocated_lbs`. After a target has been walked, its `allocated_lbs` is restated as the sum of `allocated_lbs` across all of its allocation rows whose status is `PROVISIONAL`, `CONFIRMED` or `RELEASED`, rounded half up to two decimal places — revoked rows contribute nothing. The week's status becomes `ALLOCATED` when that total is at or above `committed_lbs`, and stays `OPEN` otherwise. When the candidate pounds cover every target's need, every week reaches `ALLOCATED` and every `provisional_penalty_usd` is `0.00`. When the pounds run out part-way down the target order, the earlier high-priority weeks are filled completely and the tail weeks remain `OPEN` carrying a positive `provisional_shortfall_lbs`. A week that stays `OPEN` after a run has taken the `OPEN` → `OPEN` self-loop; it is not an error, and because eligibility is evaluated afresh on every run, a later run against new fruit may top the very same week up. The run returns every week it evaluated, in the same `priority_rank` ascending then program `created_at` ascending order, with `allocated_lbs`, `fill_rate_pct`, `provisional_shortfall_lbs` and `provisional_penalty_usd` all recomputed. Consider two programs both committed for the week starting a given Monday: P1 at `priority_rank` 1 committed for 3000 pounds under `A_ONLY`, and P2 at `priority_rank` 2 committed for 2500 pounds under `A_AND_B`, with 4700 eligible pounds available in total. P1 takes 3000 pounds of grade A and reaches a `fill_rate_pct` of 100.00; the remaining 1700 pounds — grade A first, then grade B — go to P2, which lands at `allocated_lbs` 1700, a `fill_rate_pct` of 68.00, a `provisional_shortfall_lbs` of 800 and, at a penalty rate of 0.25 US dollars per pound, a `provisional_penalty_usd` of 200.00, and its week stays `OPEN`.

#### Confirming and revoking allocations

Allocation rows are discoverable only by listing them, filterable by `lot_id`, `commitment_week_id`, `grade` and `status`. A row begins `PROVISIONAL`, and `RELEASED` and `REVOKED` are both terminal.

Confirming a row moves `PROVISIONAL` → `CONFIRMED` and belongs to the `QualityInspector`. It requires the owning week's `status` to be `ALLOCATED`, rejected otherwise with `ERR_WEEK_NOT_ALLOCATED`; the lot's `cold_chain_status` to be exactly `COLD_HELD`, rejected otherwise with `ERR_LOT_NOT_COLD_HELD`, so a merely pre-cooled lot that the waterfall was happy to draw from cannot yet be confirmed; and the lot's `grading_status` to be `GRADED` or `REGRADED`, rejected otherwise with `ERR_LOT_NOT_GRADED`. The lot must also still hold enough availability behind the row, rejected otherwise with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_AVAILABLE_LBS` — a last line of defence for the mass invariant that can only fire when a regrade shrank the lot after the row was minted, since the waterfall itself never over-asks. Confirmation moves no pounds at all: `CONFIRMED` still counts toward the week's `allocated_lbs` and toward the lot's allocated pool exactly as `PROVISIONAL` did.

Revoking a row is a `GrowerManager` action reachable from both `PROVISIONAL` and `CONFIRMED`, with identical effects from either, and it requires a `revoked_reason`. The owning week's `status` must be `OPEN` or `ALLOCATED`; once the week has shipped its allocations are history, and an attempt is rejected with `ERR_WEEK_ALREADY_SHIPPED`. A revocation cascades in three hops. Hop one subtracts `allocated_lbs` from the lot's `allocated_a_lbs` or `allocated_b_lbs` according to the row's `grade` and adds exactly the same amount back to the matching available pool. Hop two, caused by hop one, drops the week's `allocated_lbs` by the same amount and recomputes its `fill_rate_pct`, `provisional_shortfall_lbs` and `provisional_penalty_usd`; a week that was `ALLOCATED` and is now short of its `committed_lbs` reverts `ALLOCATED` → `OPEN`. Hop three, caused by hop two's reversion and only by it, increments the program's `at_risk_week_count` by one — a week that was already `OPEN` and merely got shorter does not increment that counter.

Rows can also be revoked by the system rather than by a grower, and the `revoked_reason` records which mechanism did it: `AUTO_UNCONFIRMED_AT_SHIP` when a week ships and leaves an unconfirmed row behind, `COLD_CHAIN_REJECT` when the lot is condemned per §3.9, and `WEEK_CLOSED_SHORT` when a week is closed out directly from `OPEN`. Every one of these restores the lot's available pool by exactly `allocated_lbs`.

#### Shipping and closing a week

Shipping moves a week `ALLOCATED` → `SHIPPED`, carries a `shipped_at` instant, and belongs exclusively to the `QualityInspector`. At least one allocation row on the week must be `CONFIRMED`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_NO_CONFIRMED_ALLOCATIONS`. Segregation of duties applies: the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from `cold_chain_released_by_user_id` on every lot allocated to this week, and the inspector who released a lot from cold storage may not also sign the shipment that consumes it, rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The program must also still be `ACTIVE`, rejected otherwise with `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_ACTIVE`. Shipping promotes every `CONFIRMED` row on the week to `RELEASED` — which moves those pounds out of `allocated_lbs` accounting and into `shipped_lbs` accounting while leaving the lot's allocated pool untouched, because `RELEASED` still counts as allocated — and revokes every remaining `PROVISIONAL` row with `revoked_reason` `AUTO_UNCONFIRMED_AT_SHIP`, restoring `available_a_lbs` or `available_b_lbs` on each such lot. It then recomputes `shipped_lbs` from the `RELEASED` rows only and recomputes `fill_rate_pct` from `shipped_lbs`, and records `shipped_at` and `shipped_by_user_id`. Crucially, **the `ALLOCATED` → `OPEN` revert rule is suppressed for this transition**: shipping necessarily drops `allocated_lbs` when unconfirmed rows are dropped, but a shipped week does not fall back to `OPEN` and does not increment `at_risk_week_count`. That reversion happens only through a revocation or a cold-chain rejection.

Closing a week is a `GrowerManager` action and reaches one of two divergent terminal states depending purely on what actually shipped. From `SHIPPED`, the week lands in `CLOSED_FULFILLED` when `shipped_lbs` is at or above `committed_lbs`, and in `CLOSED_SHORT` when it falls below; both run identical computations, the short branch simply carrying a positive `final_penalty_usd`. A week may also be closed straight from `OPEN` to `CLOSED_SHORT`, which is how a week nobody could fill gets written off, but only once the delivery window has elapsed: the current logical clock date must be at or after `week_start_date` plus 7 days, and an early attempt is rejected with `ERR_WEEK_STILL_OPEN`. Closing from `OPEN` revokes every remaining `PROVISIONAL` or `CONFIRMED` row with `revoked_reason` `WEEK_CLOSED_SHORT`, restoring lot availability, and leaves `shipped_lbs` at `0.00` so the final penalty is the full committed shortfall.

#### Valuing a week twice

A commitment week is valued against the same contract terms twice over, from two different bases, and the two figures live side by side on the record.

The **provisional variant** runs whenever `allocated_lbs` changes — after an allocation run, after a revocation, after a cold-chain rejection. `fill_rate_pct` is one hundred multiplied by `allocated_lbs`, divided by `committed_lbs`, rounded half up to two decimal places. `provisional_shortfall_lbs` is the greater of zero and `committed_lbs` minus `allocated_lbs`, rounded half up to two decimal places. `provisional_penalty_usd` is `provisional_shortfall_lbs` multiplied by the program's `shortfall_penalty_per_lb_usd`, rounded half up to two decimal places. This penalty is **advisory only**: it never posts to the program's `penalty_accrued_usd`, no matter how large it grows or how long the week sits there.

The **final variant** runs on the closing transition and is based on `shipped_lbs`, which excludes every `PROVISIONAL` row dropped at ship time. It restates `fill_rate_pct` as one hundred multiplied by `shipped_lbs`, divided by `committed_lbs`, rounded half up to two decimal places, overwriting the provisional figure. `final_shortfall_lbs` is the greater of zero and `committed_lbs` minus `shipped_lbs`, rounded half up to two decimal places. `final_penalty_usd` is that shortfall multiplied by `shortfall_penalty_per_lb_usd`, rounded half up to two decimal places. `gross_revenue_usd` is `shipped_lbs` multiplied by the program's `price_per_lb_usd`, rounded half up to two decimal places. `net_payout_usd` is `gross_revenue_usd` minus `final_penalty_usd`, rounded half up to two decimal places, and it **may be negative and is not floored at zero** — a week that shipped almost nothing against a steep penalty rate genuinely costs the grower money. Closing then posts `final_penalty_usd` to the program's `penalty_accrued_usd`.

The two variants diverge for the same week whenever confirmed pounds are fewer than allocated pounds. A program committed for 3000 pounds at 1.85 US dollars a pound with a 0.40 US dollar penalty rate shows, after a full allocation, a `fill_rate_pct` of 100.00, a `provisional_shortfall_lbs` of 0 and a `provisional_penalty_usd` of `0.00`. If only 1200 of those pounds were ever confirmed and therefore shipped, closing restates `fill_rate_pct` to 40.00 and yields a `final_shortfall_lbs` of 1800, a `final_penalty_usd` of 720.00, a `gross_revenue_usd` of 2220.00 and a `net_payout_usd` of 1500.00. The second program above, committed for 2500 pounds at a 0.25 US dollar penalty rate, showed a `provisional_penalty_usd` of 200.00 while sitting at `allocated_lbs` 1700; closed short directly from `OPEN` with `shipped_lbs` `0.00`, its `final_penalty_usd` is 625.00 — the full commitment at the penalty rate. A separate advisory benchmark, `MIN_ACCEPTABLE_FILL_PCT` of `95.00`, marks the fill rate below which a week is considered commercially unacceptable; it gates nothing and rejects nothing.
