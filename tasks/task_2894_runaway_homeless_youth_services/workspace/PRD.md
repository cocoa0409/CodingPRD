# Runaway & Homeless Youth Services API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Runaway and homeless youth arrive at our doors through a street outreach van, a police drop-off, a school counsellor's referral, or on their own at two in the morning — and the moment they arrive, a dense web of federal obligations closes around them. A bed may only be offered if the youth falls inside the program's licensed age band. A guardian must be notified within twenty-four hours, unless a statute says the opposite. A trafficking screening must clear before transitional housing is offered. Every night slept must be counted for the federal report, and every licensed bed is a hard ceiling that no amount of compassion is allowed to breach. Today our case managers reconcile these obligations on paper, and the reconciliation fails in exactly the places that matter most: the notification nobody chased, the stay that quietly ran past the federal maximum, the trafficking indicator that was written on a clipboard and never scored.

This document specifies the Runaway & Homeless Youth Services API, a single system of record spanning the whole continuum of care: street engagement, emergency shelter admission, the parental-notification and runaway-reporting obligation, family reunification with mediation and aftercare, transitional living placement with its savings and life-skills requirements, education and employment support, safety screening for trafficking and exploitation, youth participation in program governance, and the federal compliance and outcome report that ties it all together. Every collection is mounted under the `/ryhs` prefix, with two administrative test-driving endpoints mounted outside it.

Two design commitments run through the entire specification. First, **compliance is computed, never asserted**: bed occupancy, utilization, stay duration, savings requirements, trafficking risk and outcome rates are derived by the server from the underlying records, so a caller can never simply declare that a program is compliant. Second, **posture gates access**: each shelter program and each youth carries a server-derived posture that opens, restricts or freezes downstream operations, so a capacity breach or an unresolved safety obligation propagates automatically into refusals rather than depending on a human remembering to stop.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the system-wide contracts, derived gates and cross-cutting conventions that apply to every endpoint in the API.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which that user acts. Both headers must be present and non-empty; a missing, empty or whitespace-only value, or a role string that is not one of the six recognised roles, is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS` (HTTP 401) before any other processing occurs. Once the headers are accepted, the acting role must appear in the authorised-role list of the endpoint being called; if it does not, the request is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

The six roles are `OutreachWorker`, `ShelterCaseManager`, `ReunificationSpecialist`, `TlpCoordinator`, `ProgramAdmin`, and `YouthMember`.

An `OutreachWorker` is street outreach staff: this role alone intakes new youths and logs street-outreach encounters, and it shares the submission of safety screenings with a `ShelterCaseManager`. A `ShelterCaseManager` is emergency-shelter staff: this role alone requests admissions, extends stays, discharges youths and records parental-notification attempts, and it shares notification resolution with a `ProgramAdmin` and shares education enrollment and records verification with a `TlpCoordinator`. A `ReunificationSpecialist` owns the whole family-reunification pathway exclusively: opening cases, scheduling mediation rounds, recording mediation outcomes, recording home assessments, deciding cases and logging aftercare contacts. A `TlpCoordinator` owns transitional living exclusively: creating placements, posting savings entries, crediting life-skills completions and ending tenancies, and shares the savings-compliance review with a `ProgramAdmin`. A `ProgramAdmin` licenses programs, dispositions safety screenings, closes governance votes, reads the federal compliance report, drives the logical clock and the nightly census, and may read every read endpoint in the system. A `YouthMember` is a resident youth participating in program governance: this role alone casts and retracts governance votes, shares proposal creation with a `ProgramAdmin`, and has access to no other read or write endpoint whatsoever.

Identity semantics differ between the staff roles and the youth role, and the difference is load-bearing. When `X-User-Role` is `OutreachWorker`, `ShelterCaseManager`, `ReunificationSpecialist`, `TlpCoordinator` or `ProgramAdmin`, the `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity: the server records it on the rows those roles create or resolve — as `intake_worker_user_id` on a youth, `worker_user_id` on an outreach contact, `admitting_user_id` on an admission, `resolving_user_id` on a guardian notification, `screener_user_id` and `disposition_user_id` on a safety screening, `scheduler_user_id` and `recorded_by_user_id` on a mediation session, `decision_user_id` on a reunification case, `coordinator_user_id` on a placement, `recorded_by_user_id` on a savings entry, `verified_by_user_id` on a life-skills completion, `liaison_user_id` on an education enrollment, and `decided_by_user_id` on a governance proposal — and compares those recorded identifiers against each other for the segregation-of-duties rules, but never compares an operator identifier to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `YouthMember`, by contrast, `X-User-ID` **is** the `id` of the acting `Youth`; ownership checks compare the youth-referencing foreign key on the addressed resource to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a `YouthMember` may read only the youth record whose `id` equals its own `X-User-ID`, may list only its own governance votes, may retract only a `GovernanceVote` whose `youth_id` equals its own `X-User-ID`, and casts votes that are stamped with its own `X-User-ID` as `youth_id`. Any attempt by a `YouthMember` to reach another youth's data or another youth's vote is rejected with `ERR_NOT_VOTE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). The `youth_id` of a cast vote is always taken from the header and never from the request body.

Two segregation-of-duties rules cut across the role model and are enforced with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). The user who records a mediation outcome may not be the user who decides that reunification case; the `ProgramAdmin` who waives a parental notification may not be the case manager who admitted the youth; and the administrator who dispositions a safety screening may not be the person who submitted it.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every endpoint that returns a collection accepts two optional query parameters. The `limit` parameter caps the number of records returned; when it is omitted it defaults to 10, its smallest accepted value is 1 and its largest accepted value is 100. The `offset` parameter states how many records to skip before the first returned record; when it is omitted it defaults to 0, and its smallest accepted value is 0. A `limit` below 1, a `limit` above 100, a negative `offset`, or a non-integer value for either parameter is a request-shape failure and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are ordered by the record's creation timestamp, `created_at`, in ascending order. Both list-based and paginated responses populate `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope, where `total_count` is the number of records matching the filter before the limit and offset window is applied, and `has_more` is true exactly when records matching the filter remain beyond the returned window.

### 2.4. Record Identity, Audit Fields and Mutation Semantics

Every resource in the system is addressed by an `id`, which is a server-minted UUID string carrying no type prefix of any kind. Clients never supply an `id`. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording the logical-clock instant at which the record was written.

Most resources additionally carry two mutation-tracking fields. `updated_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, refreshed on every change to the record, including changes the server makes on its own initiative as part of a cascade. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the record is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that record; a mutation of a derived field — an occupied-bed counter, a recomputed posture, a re-decayed risk score — is a mutation like any other and increments `version` accordingly. `ShelterProgram`, `Youth`, `Admission`, `GuardianNotification`, `SafetyScreening`, `ReunificationCase`, `MediationSession`, `TlpPlacement`, `EducationEnrollment`, `GovernanceProposal` and `GovernanceVote` all carry the full set of `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` and `version`.

Three record types are immutable event records: an `OutreachContact`, a `SavingsLedgerEntry` and a `LifeSkillsCompletion` are written once and never changed. These three carry only `id` and `created_at`; they have no `updated_at` field and no `version` field at all, and neither field appears in their responses.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock, Instants and Numeric Precision

The server runs on a **logical clock** rather than on wall-clock time. The clock starts at `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` and stays there until an administrator moves it; the server never falls back to real time for any purpose. Every age computation, every notification due instant, every mediation cooldown, every risk decay, every elapsed-days figure and every duration ceiling reads this clock. The administrative clock endpoint takes a single field, `to_iso8601`, and answers with the object `{ current_utc, previous_utc }`, reporting the new clock value and the value it replaced. The clock is strictly monotonic: a `to_iso8601` value equal to or earlier than the present clock value is refused with `ERR_CLOCK_MONOTONIC_VIOLATION` (HTTP 422), and moving the clock mutates no entity and runs no census.

All instants exchanged over the API are ISO 8601 UTC timestamps with three fractional digits. The single exception is a youth's `date_of_birth`, which is a calendar date in `YYYY-MM-DD` form with no time component at all. A youth's `age_years` is a whole number of years measured from `date_of_birth` to the current logical clock, computed as follows: the youth is treated as having already had this year's birthday when the clock's month-and-day pair is greater than or equal to the birth date's month-and-day pair; the age is then the clock's year minus the birth year, reduced by one when this year's birthday has not yet arrived. Because the comparison is inclusive, a youth turns the new age on the birthday itself rather than the day after, and the result is always floored to whole years. No entity other than the youth record and the clock participates in this computation.

The system stores no fractional quantities. Money is always an integer count of cents. Rates and ratios are always integer **basis points**, where one basis point is one ten-thousandth, so 10000 basis points is the whole and 9000 basis points is ninety percent. Every division that produces a ratio in basis points — bed utilization, duration consumption, governance support, positive-outcome rate — is truncated toward zero, that is, floored to a whole basis point. Elapsed days, elapsed months and billable nights are likewise floored to whole units. The one place a different rounding rule governs is the program savings match: each individual savings entry's match component is rounded **half up** to whole cents before any summation, and the per-entry rounding order is mandatory (see §3.8).

### 2.6. Program Posture: The Licensed-Capacity and Overdue-Notification Gate

Every `ShelterProgram` carries a derived `posture` whose value is one of `OPEN`, `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN`. Posture is the system's single gate over program-level intake and it is recomputed by the server on every mutation of the program's `occupied_beds` or `overdue_notification_count`, and again on every nightly census run. It is never set by a client.

Four counters feed the posture. `occupied_beds` is polymorphic in the program's `program_type`: for an `EMERGENCY_SHELTER` program it is the number of `Admission` records of that program whose status is `ACTIVE`, `EXTENDED` or `DISCHARGE_SCHEDULED`; for a `TRANSITIONAL_LIVING` program it is the number of `TlpPlacement` records of that program whose tenancy status is `PLACED` or `NOTICE_GIVEN`. `waitlisted_count` is the number of `Admission` records of that program whose status is `WAITLISTED`. `overdue_notification_count` is the number of `GuardianNotification` records in status `OVERDUE` whose admission belongs to that program. `utilization_bp` is the occupied-bed count multiplied by 10000, divided by `licensed_bed_capacity`, and floored to whole basis points.

The posture is then decided by evaluating three clauses from the top down, the first match winning. The posture is `FROZEN` when the occupied-bed count has reached or passed `licensed_bed_capacity`, or when the overdue-notification count has reached two. Otherwise the posture is `RESTRICTED` when the utilization has reached or passed 9000 basis points — ninety percent of licensed capacity, so a ten-bed program with nine beds filled sits at exactly 9000 basis points and is therefore `RESTRICTED` — or when the overdue-notification count is exactly one. Otherwise the posture is `OPEN`.

Posture gates three things. A `FROZEN` posture diverts a new emergency-shelter admission request onto the waitlist rather than into a bed, unless the requester explicitly opted out of waitlisting, in which case the request is refused with `ERR_BED_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409). A `FROZEN` posture that arises from beds being full also blocks a new transitional-living placement outright with `ERR_BED_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, because transitional living has no waitlist at all; a `FROZEN` posture that arises only from overdue notifications leaves transitional-living placement alone. A `RESTRICTED` posture admits only high-need youth: the admission's `priority_score` must be at least 60, and a lower score is refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_RESTRICTED_PRIORITY` (HTTP 422). The licensed bed capacity is a hard federal ceiling and is never exceeded.

### 2.7. Youth Risk Posture: The Safety Gate

Each `Youth` carries a second derived gate, `risk_posture`, again valued `OPEN`, `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN`. It is recomputed on every mutation of a safety screening or guardian notification touching that youth, on every logged outreach encounter, and on every nightly census run.

Two inputs decide it. The first is the youth's most recent `SafetyScreening`, meaning the screening row of that youth with the greatest `created_at`. The second is whether any open parental-notification obligation exists — that is, whether any `GuardianNotification` reachable through any of that youth's admissions sits in status `PENDING`, `ATTEMPTED` or `OVERDUE`. The three clauses are evaluated top to bottom and the first match wins: the posture is `FROZEN` when the most recent screening exists and is in status `ESCALATED`; otherwise it is `RESTRICTED` when an open notification obligation exists or the youth's `trafficking_risk_tier` is `CRITICAL`; otherwise it is `OPEN`.

A `FROZEN` risk posture means the youth is in an active safety situation and family contact is unsafe. It blocks opening a new reunification case and blocks approving a reunification decision, both with `ERR_YOUTH_POSTURE_FROZEN` (HTTP 422), and it blocks resolving a parental notification as contacted, with `ERR_NOTIFICATION_UNSAFE_CONTACT` (HTTP 422). A `RESTRICTED` risk posture blocks transitional-living placement with `ERR_YOUTH_POSTURE_RESTRICTED` (HTTP 422) until the obligation is discharged. Neither posture ever blocks education enrollment: the school-enrollment right of an unaccompanied youth is unconditional and is deliberately exempted from every posture gate in the system.

### 2.8. Cascade Discipline and Server-Initiated Transitions

Because compliance figures are derived, a single client request routinely mutates several records. The server performs these cascades itself, in a fixed order, within the same request, and each touched record independently refreshes its `updated_at` and increments its `version`. A client never has to issue a follow-up request to bring a derived counter into line, and a client can never bring one into line by hand.

Four cascade chains dominate the system and are specified in detail in §3. Activating an admission consumes a bed, which forces the program's utilization and posture to be recomputed, and creates the parental-notification obligation, whose existence flips the youth's risk posture to `RESTRICTED`, which in turn blocks that youth's transitional-living placement until the obligation is resolved. Discharging an admission releases the bed, recomputes the program's utilization and posture, and — if the posture has left `FROZEN` — promotes exactly one waitlisted admission, which re-consumes the bed, re-recomputes the posture, and creates that youth's own parental-notification obligation and consequent `RESTRICTED` risk posture. Approving a reunification case sets the aftercare deadline, moves the youth to `REUNIFIED_PENDING`, moves every `ACTIVE` or `EXTENDED` admission of that youth to `DISCHARGE_SCHEDULED`, and waives each of those admissions' still-open notification obligations, which recomputes the program's overdue count and posture. Marking a notification `OVERDUE` during the census raises the program's overdue count, which recomputes the program's posture and can drive it to `FROZEN`.

Server-initiated transitions are as real as client-initiated ones. When an admission reaches `ACTIVE` by cascade, the youth's status changes and the youth's `version` increments exactly as it would have on a direct write.

### 2.9. Stale-Version Protection on Youth and Placement Writes

Two write operations are protected by optimistic concurrency control, and each requires a non-optional version field in its request body.

Opening a reunification case — `POST /ryhs/reunification-cases` — requires `expected_youth_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Youth`. If that value does not equal the youth's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_YOUTH_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This guard exists because a new case legitimately supersedes any earlier non-terminal case for the same youth, so a second sequential open would otherwise succeed and silently discard mediation history the client never observed; the version check turns that into a deterministic refusal whenever the caller's view of the youth is out of date.

Posting a savings entry — `POST /ryhs/tlp-placements/{id}/savings-entries` — requires `expected_placement_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `TlpPlacement`. If that value does not equal the placement's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PLACEMENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This guard exists because deposits are inherently repeatable: a second identical deposit is a legitimate business operation rather than a state violation, so no state precondition could ever catch a caller posting against an out-of-date balance, compliance status or match total. The version check on the placement is the only guard available.

No other endpoint in the system accepts a version field, and no other endpoint performs a version check.

### 2.10. Path Resolution and the Validation Ladder

Identifiers embedded in a URL path are resolved strictly left to right, and the first identifier that fails to resolve determines the error. Each resource kind has its own not-found code, so a request naming an unknown youth fails with `ERR_YOUTH_NOT_FOUND` before the server ever looks at the nested segment. The eleven not-found codes are `ERR_YOUTH_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ADMISSION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SCREENING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_NOTIFICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CASE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SESSION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PLACEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ENROLLMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROPOSAL_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_VOTE_NOT_FOUND`, each returning HTTP 404. Foreign keys supplied in a request body are resolved in the order the endpoint declares them: an admission request resolves `youth_id` and then `program_id`; a transitional-living placement resolves `youth_id` and then `program_id`; a reunification case resolves `youth_id`.

When several checks could fail on the same request, they are evaluated in a fixed order and the first failure is the one reported. The ladder, from highest precedence to lowest, is:

1. Authentication headers present, non-empty, and the role recognised — `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS`, 401, on every endpoint.
2. The acting role is authorised for the endpoint — `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED_ROLE`, 403, on every endpoint.
3. A `YouthMember` is acting only on its own resources — `ERR_NOT_VOTE_OWNER`, 403 — on reading one youth, listing a youth's governance votes, casting a vote on a proposal, and retracting a vote.
4. Every identifier in the path resolves, left to right — the matching not-found code, 404, on every path-templated endpoint.
5. Every foreign key in the request body resolves, in declaration order — the matching not-found code, 404, on the admission-request, transitional-living-placement and reunification-case-open operations.
6. Request-body shape: required fields present, enum members legal, integers inside their declared bounds, arrays free of duplicates and drawn from their declared code sets, instants parseable as ISO 8601 UTC, and `limit` and `offset` inside the pagination bounds — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, 400, on every endpoint.
7. `expected_youth_version` equals the youth's current `version` — `ERR_STALE_YOUTH_VERSION`, 412, on opening a reunification case.
8. `expected_placement_version` equals the placement's current `version` — `ERR_STALE_PLACEMENT_VERSION`, 412, on posting a savings entry.
9. The admission is in an operable non-terminal state for the requested action — `ERR_ADMISSION_NOT_ACTIVE`, 409 — on extend and discharge. Extension requires status `ACTIVE` or `EXTENDED`; discharge requires status `ACTIVE`, `EXTENDED` or `DISCHARGE_SCHEDULED`. This rule is deliberately scoped to the five non-terminal statuses `PENDING_SCREENING`, `WAITLISTED`, `ACTIVE`, `EXTENDED` and `DISCHARGE_SCHEDULED` only; an already-terminal admission falls outside its scope and is caught by rule 10 instead, so the two codes never shadow one another.
10. The admission has not already reached `DISCHARGED` or `DENIED` — `ERR_ADMISSION_ALREADY_TERMINAL`, 409 — on extend and discharge.
11. The youth holds no other admission at the same program in status `ACTIVE`, `EXTENDED` or `DISCHARGE_SCHEDULED` — `ERR_YOUTH_ALREADY_SHELTERED`, 409 — on the admission request.
12. The guardian notification is not already `CONTACTED`, `WAIVED` or `LAW_ENFORCEMENT_REPORTED` — `ERR_NOTIFICATION_ALREADY_RESOLVED`, 409 — on recording an attempt and on resolving.
13. The requested screening disposition is a declared transition — `ERR_SCREENING_INVALID_TRANSITION`, 409 — on screening disposition. `SUBMITTED` may go to `CLEARED` or `ESCALATED`, `ESCALATED` may go to `CLEARED`, and `CLEARED` may go to `ESCALATED`; re-clearing an already-`CLEARED` screening and re-escalating an already-`ESCALATED` screening are not declared transitions.
14. The reunification case is in the state the requested step declares — `ERR_CASE_INVALID_STATE`, 409 — on scheduling a mediation session, recording a home assessment, deciding, and logging an aftercare contact. Scheduling requires status `OPENED` or `MEDIATION_HELD`; home assessment requires `MEDIATION_HELD`; decision requires `HOME_ASSESSMENT`; aftercare contacts require `APPROVED` or `AFTERCARE`.
15. The mediation session has no recorded outcome yet and its case is neither `SUPERSEDED` nor `CLOSED` — `ERR_SESSION_ALREADY_RECORDED`, 409 — on recording a mediation outcome.
16. The placement's tenancy is not `EXITED` — `ERR_PLACEMENT_ALREADY_EXITED`, 409 — on posting a savings entry, crediting a life-skills completion, running a compliance review, and exiting.
17. The proposal is still `OPEN_FOR_VOTE` — `ERR_PROPOSAL_ALREADY_DECIDED`, 409 — on closing the vote, casting a vote, and retracting a vote.
18. A screening in the `CRITICAL` tier may not be cleared — `ERR_SCREENING_CRITICAL_TIER`, 422 — on screening disposition.
19. The youth's most recent screening is not `ESCALATED` — `ERR_SCREENING_ESCALATED_BLOCKS_ACTION`, 422 — on the admission request and on transitional-living placement.
20. The youth's risk posture is not `FROZEN` — `ERR_YOUTH_POSTURE_FROZEN`, 422 — on opening a reunification case and on the reunification decision.
21. The admission's notification is not `OVERDUE` unless a runaway report has already been filed — `ERR_RUNAWAY_REPORT_REQUIRED`, 422 — on discharge.
22. A notification may not be resolved as contacted while the youth's risk posture is `FROZEN`, nor with zero recorded attempts — `ERR_NOTIFICATION_UNSAFE_CONTACT`, 422 — on resolving a notification.
23. Every notification of the youth's `ACTIVE` and `EXTENDED` admissions is at least `ATTEMPTED` — `ERR_NOTIFICATION_UNRESOLVED`, 422 — on the reunification decision.
24. Segregation of duties on the three paired operations — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, 403 — on the reunification decision, on notification resolution, and on screening disposition.
25. The youth's age in whole years lies inside the program's licensed age band, both bounds inclusive — `ERR_AGE_OUTSIDE_PROGRAM_BAND`, 422 — on the admission request and on transitional-living placement.
26. The program type matches the operation — `ERR_PROGRAM_TYPE_MISMATCH`, 422 — on the admission request and on transitional-living placement.
27. The requested stay does not overlap another stay of the same youth — `ERR_ADMISSION_OVERLAP`, 409 — on the admission request.
28. The mediation window does not collide with another window on the same case — `ERR_MEDIATION_SESSION_OVERLAP`, 409 — on scheduling a mediation session.
29. The mediation cooldown has elapsed — `ERR_MEDIATION_COOLDOWN_ACTIVE`, 409 — on scheduling a mediation session.
30. The youth's most recent screening exists and is `CLEARED` — `ERR_SCREENING_REQUIRED`, 422 — on transitional-living placement.
31. The youth holds no other open transitional-living placement — `ERR_PLACEMENT_OVERLAP`, 409 — on transitional-living placement.
32. Caller-supplied instants lie in the future and are internally ordered — `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`, 422 — on the admission request, on extend, on scheduling a mediation session, and on opening a governance proposal.
33. The logical clock only moves forward — `ERR_CLOCK_MONOTONIC_VIOLATION`, 422 — on the clock endpoint.
34. The recomputed projected billable nights do not exceed the program's stay ceiling — `ERR_MAX_STAY_EXCEEDED`, 422 — on extend.
35. The extension count is still below two — `ERR_MAX_EXTENSIONS_REACHED`, 422 — on extend.
36. The mediation round count is still below the case ceiling — `ERR_MEDIATION_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`, 422 — on scheduling a mediation session.
37. The placement has duration allowance left — `ERR_DURATION_LIMIT_REACHED`, 422 — on crediting a life-skills completion and on running a compliance review.
38. The youth has no counted vote on this proposal already — `ERR_DUPLICATE_VOTE`, 409 — on casting a vote.
39. The youth is vote-eligible and resident in the proposal's program — `ERR_YOUTH_NOT_VOTE_ELIGIBLE`, 422 — on casting a vote and on a `YouthMember` opening a proposal.
40. The clock has not passed the proposal's voting deadline — `ERR_PROPOSAL_VOTE_CLOSED`, 409 — on casting a vote and on retracting a vote.
41. The electorate computed at close time is non-empty — `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_ELECTORATE`, 422 — on closing the vote.
42. Under a `RESTRICTED` program posture the admission's priority score reaches the floor of 60 — `ERR_PROGRAM_RESTRICTED_PRIORITY`, 422 — on the admission request.
43. Licensed capacity is respected — `ERR_BED_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, 409 — on the admission request when waitlisting was opted out of against a `FROZEN` program, and on transitional-living placement when the program's beds are already full.
44. A withdrawal does not exceed the current savings balance — `ERR_SAVINGS_INSUFFICIENT_BALANCE`, 422 — on posting a savings entry.
45. A withdrawal is not attempted while the placement is on savings probation — `ERR_SAVINGS_WITHDRAWAL_BLOCKED`, 422 — on posting a savings entry.
46. A reversal does not exceed the deposits recorded on the placement — `ERR_SAVINGS_REVERSAL_UNMATCHED`, 422 — on posting a savings entry.
47. The first aftercare contact falls inside the aftercare planning window — `ERR_AFTERCARE_WINDOW_MISSED`, 422 — on logging an aftercare contact.
48. The youth's risk posture is `OPEN` at placement time — `ERR_YOUTH_POSTURE_RESTRICTED`, 422 — on transitional-living placement. This is the lowest-precedence gate in the entire system, so every other placement check above wins over it.

The full business condition behind each of these rules lives with the workflow it guards, in §3.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Street Outreach, Intake and Building Engagement

Everything begins on the street. An `OutreachWorker` who meets a young person creates a **Youth** record by supplying `legal_first_name`, `legal_last_name` and `date_of_birth`, plus an optional `unaccompanied` flag that declares whether the youth presented with no accompanying adult. When `unaccompanied` is omitted it defaults to true, on the operating assumption that a youth met through street outreach is unaccompanied unless the worker says otherwise. Each of the two names must be between 1 and 80 characters long, and the birth date must parse as an ISO calendar date with no time component. The freshly created youth starts in `status` `PROSPECT`, with `risk_posture` `OPEN`, `trafficking_risk_score` 0, `trafficking_risk_tier` `LOW`, `engagement_contact_count` 0, `basic_needs_provision_count` 0, `cumulative_shelter_nights` 0, `lifetime_savings_deposited_cents` 0, `governance_vote_eligible` false, and `version` 1. The acting worker's identifier is stored as `intake_worker_user_id` on the row, and `age_years` is not stored as a client input at all but computed against the logical clock on every read, as described in §2.5.

Trust is built one encounter at a time, and each encounter is logged as an **OutreachContact** against the youth. The worker records the `engagement_stage` reached — one of `FIRST_CONTACT`, `RAPPORT_BUILDING`, `SERVICE_OFFER` or `TRUST_ESTABLISHED` — together with `basic_needs_provided`, the list of items actually handed over during the encounter, and `indicator_flags`, the list of trafficking or exploitation indicators the worker observed. The basic-needs list is drawn from `FOOD`, `HYGIENE_KIT`, `CLOTHING`, `TRANSIT_PASS` and `MEDICAL_REFERRAL`; the indicator list is drawn from `THIRD_PARTY_CONTROL`, `QUOTA_DEBT`, `COMMERCIAL_SEX`, `DOCUMENT_WITHHELD`, `RECRUITER_PRESENT` and `CURFEW_ENFORCED`. Both lists must be free of duplicates, both may be empty, and any member outside the declared code set is a request-shape failure reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The server stamps `contacted_at` with the logical-clock instant of the encounter and `worker_user_id` with the acting worker's identifier; an outreach contact is an immutable event record and carries no version.

Logging an encounter is only permitted while the youth's `status` is `PROSPECT` or `ENGAGED`. A first encounter moves the youth `PROSPECT → ENGAGED`; every later encounter is a legitimate self-loop `ENGAGED → ENGAGED`, since street engagement is by nature repeated. Either way the same set of consequences fires: `engagement_contact_count` rises by one, `basic_needs_provision_count` rises by the number of items in the `basic_needs_provided` list of this encounter, the youth's standing trafficking risk is recomputed and re-tiered against the current clock, the youth's `risk_posture` is recomputed, `governance_vote_eligible` is recomputed, and the youth's `version` increments by one.

The youth-level trafficking figure is a **decaying history score**, and it is the number that later feeds admission priority and the safety gate. Each indicator code carries a weight: `THIRD_PARTY_CONTROL` is worth 30 points, `QUOTA_DEBT` 25, `COMMERCIAL_SEX` 40, `DOCUMENT_WITHHELD` 20, `RECRUITER_PRESENT` 15 and `CURFEW_ENFORCED` 10. The raw value of any one indicator set is the sum of the weights of its distinct codes. To turn a history of encounters into one figure, the server walks every `OutreachContact` of the youth in ascending `created_at` order, with no status filter of any kind — contacts are immutable and are never excluded from the walk. For each contact it takes the whole number of days between that contact's `contacted_at` and the current clock, divides by seven and floors to get the number of completed weeks, and derives a retained percentage of one hundred minus ten percentage points for each completed week, held at a floor of zero so a contact older than ten weeks contributes nothing. That contact's contribution is its raw indicator value multiplied by the retained percentage, divided by one hundred, and **floored to a whole point before being added to the running total**. The per-contact flooring order is mandatory: summing raw values first and decaying the total afterwards is the explicitly rejected alternative and produces different numbers. `trafficking_risk_score` is the running total, capped at 100.

`trafficking_risk_tier` follows from that score by two thresholds: the tier is `CRITICAL` at a score of 60 or more, `ELEVATED` at a score of 30 or more, and `LOW` below 30. The same two thresholds and the same indicator weights are reused for the screening-time figure described in §3.2, but the two numbers are computed differently and must not be conflated: because every completed week strips ten percentage points off each contribution and each contribution is floored individually, an identical indicator set scores strictly lower as history than it does at screening time once any contact is at least seven days old. The history figure is also recomputed from scratch on every nightly census run, so a youth's standing risk decays over time even when nobody touches the record.

`governance_vote_eligible` is likewise derived rather than declared: it is true exactly when the youth's `status` is `SHELTERED` or `TLP_ENROLLED` **and** `engagement_contact_count` is at least one. A youth admitted straight off the street with no prior outreach encounter is therefore sheltered but not vote-eligible.

A youth's whole lifecycle runs `PROSPECT` → `ENGAGED` → `SHELTERED` → either `TLP_ENROLLED` or `REUNIFIED_PENDING`, and terminates in `REUNIFIED` or `EXITED`. Two of those hops are worth naming here because they are driven by other entities rather than by outreach: a youth becomes `SHELTERED` when one of that youth's admissions reaches its active stay, which is legal from either `PROSPECT` or `ENGAGED`, and a youth returns from `REUNIFIED_PENDING` back to `SHELTERED` when a newer reunification case supersedes an approved one. The remaining hops are covered in §3.5, §3.6 and §3.7. Listing youths supports narrowing by `status`, by `trafficking_risk_tier` and by `risk_posture`, and reading one youth returns the computed `age_years`, both postures and every counter described above.

### 3.2. Safety Screening and the Specialized Anti-Trafficking Response

Screening for trafficking and exploitation is a formal, two-person process, and it is deliberately separated from the outreach encounter that may have prompted it. Either an `OutreachWorker` or a `ShelterCaseManager` submits a **SafetyScreening** against a youth, supplying `indicator_codes` — the same six-code vocabulary and the same no-duplicates, may-be-empty rules as the outreach indicator list. The server stamps `screened_at` with the clock instant, records the submitter as `screener_user_id`, and opens the screening in `status` `SUBMITTED` with `disposition_user_id` still null. A screening in `SUBMITTED` never freezes a youth on its own; the youth's `risk_posture` is recomputed on submission, but only a disposition can drive it to `FROZEN`.

The screening's own `score` is the **undecayed snapshot** of exactly one indicator set: the sum of the weights of the distinct codes supplied on this screening, capped at 100, with no decay of any kind applied and no other screening or contact participating. That figure is frozen onto the row and is never re-decayed afterwards. The screening's `tier` is read off the same two thresholds as the youth's tier — `CRITICAL` at 60 or more, `ELEVATED` at 30 or more, `LOW` below 30 — but it is read off the screening's own `score` rather than off the youth's history figure.

A `ProgramAdmin`, and only a `ProgramAdmin`, dispositions a screening by supplying `disposition` — either `CLEARED` or `ESCALATED` — and optionally `specialized_response_code`, which records the specialized anti-trafficking response attached at disposition time. The response code is one of `NONE`, `ADVOCATE_ASSIGNED`, `LAW_ENFORCEMENT_LIAISON` or `EMERGENCY_RELOCATION`, and it defaults to `NONE` when omitted. Whoever dispositions the screening must not be the person who submitted it: a matching identifier is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The accepted disposition stores `disposition_user_id` and the response code on the row.

Escalation and clearance are genuinely cyclic, because new indicators surface and specialized responses complete. From `SUBMITTED` a screening may move to `CLEARED` or to `ESCALATED`. A `CLEARED` screening may later be re-escalated `CLEARED → ESCALATED`, and an `ESCALATED` screening may be re-cleared `ESCALATED → CLEARED` once the specialized response has run. Re-clearing something already `CLEARED` and re-escalating something already `ESCALATED` are not declared transitions and are refused with `ERR_SCREENING_INVALID_TRANSITION`. Escalating in either direction — from `SUBMITTED` or from `CLEARED` — requires a `specialized_response_code` other than `NONE`, because an escalation without a specialized response is meaningless; and re-clearing an escalated screening requires that the escalated row already carries a response code other than `NONE`, evidencing that a specialized response actually ran. Clearing in either direction is additionally forbidden when the screening's `tier` is `CRITICAL`: such a screening is refused with `ERR_SCREENING_CRITICAL_TIER`, the specialized response must run, and a fresh screening must be submitted before the youth can be cleared.

The two dispositions have deliberately asymmetric consequences, and both are specified here in full. `ESCALATED` recomputes the youth's `risk_posture` to `FROZEN`, which from that moment blocks opening a reunification case for that youth, blocks approving a reunification decision, and blocks resolving any of that youth's parental notifications as contacted — family contact is unsafe while a screening stands escalated. `CLEARED` recomputes the youth's `risk_posture`, which leaves `FROZEN`, and then fires a further cascade: the youth's oldest admission still waiting on screening — the one with the earliest `created_at` among that youth's admissions in `PENDING_SCREENING` — advances to its active stay, which stamps that admission's `admitted_at`, computes its projected billable nights, increments the program's `occupied_beds` and recomputes that program's `utilization_bp` and `posture`, and creates that admission's parental-notification obligation in `PENDING`. Exactly one admission is activated per clearance: if the youth has several admissions in `PENDING_SCREENING`, only the earliest by `created_at` advances and the rest are left where they are. Activation also requires that the program's posture is not `FROZEN` at disposition time.

Screenings of a youth can be listed and narrowed by `status`, and the resulting rows expose the frozen `score`, the `tier`, the `specialized_response_code`, `screened_at`, `screener_user_id` and `disposition_user_id`.

### 3.3. Licensing Programs and Requesting Emergency Shelter

Before anyone can be sheltered, a `ProgramAdmin` licenses a **ShelterProgram**. The licence declares a `name` of 1 to 120 characters and a `program_type` of either `EMERGENCY_SHELTER` or `TRANSITIONAL_LIVING`, which fixes for all time which kind of child record is allowed to occupy that program's beds. It declares `licensed_bed_capacity`, the federally licensed bed count, which must be at least 1 and which is a hard ceiling on occupancy. It declares the admissible age band as `min_age_years` and `max_age_years`, where the youngest admissible age is between 0 and 24 inclusive and the oldest admissible age is between the youngest admissible age and 24 inclusive, both bounds of the band themselves being inclusive when a youth is measured against them. It declares two duration ceilings: `max_stay_nights`, the federal emergency-shelter ceiling expressed in billable nights, and `max_placement_days`, the federal transitional-living base duration ceiling expressed in days, each at least 1. Finally it declares two savings rates in basis points, each between 0 and 10000 inclusive: `savings_match_rate_bp`, the share of each youth deposit that the program matches, and `required_savings_rate_bp`, the share of documented monthly income that a transitional-living youth must bank. A capacity below 1, or a rate outside the basis-point range, is refused as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A newly licensed program starts with `occupied_beds`, `waitlisted_count` and `overdue_notification_count` all at 0, `utilization_bp` at 0, and `posture` computed as `OPEN`. Programs can be listed and narrowed by `program_type` and by `posture`, and reading one program returns its live counters and posture.

A `ShelterCaseManager` requests an **Admission** by supplying `youth_id`, `program_id` and `planned_discharge_at`, the caller-declared planned exit instant that forms the upper bound of the projected occupancy interval. The optional `waitlist_opt_out` flag declares that the caller wants an outright refusal rather than a waitlist slot when no bed is free; when omitted it behaves as false. On acceptance the server stamps `requested_at` with the clock instant, records the acting case manager as `admitting_user_id` — the value that later governs who may waive the parental notification — and freezes `priority_score` onto the row.

The disposition of the request is decided inside the creating request itself, and it resolves to exactly one of three statuses. The **capacity branch is evaluated before the screening branch**, and this ordering matters: a youth with no cleared screening arriving at a `FROZEN` program lands in `WAITLISTED`, never in `PENDING_SCREENING`. Concretely, when the program's posture is `FROZEN` and waitlisting was not opted out of, the admission is created `WAITLISTED`; the program must still be an `EMERGENCY_SHELTER` program and the youth must still fall inside the age band. When the program's posture is not `FROZEN` and the youth's most recent safety screening — the screening of that youth with the greatest `created_at` — exists and stands `CLEARED`, and no other non-terminal admission of that youth overlaps the requested interval, and the priority floor is satisfied where a `RESTRICTED` posture demands it, the admission is created `ACTIVE`. Otherwise the admission rests in its initial `PENDING_SCREENING` status and nothing further changes until a screening is dispositioned as cleared.

The three branches have sharply different consequences, and each is specified in full. The `ACTIVE` branch is the five-record cascade: `admitted_at` is stamped with the clock, `projected_billable_nights` is computed, the program's `occupied_beds` rises by one, which forces the program's `utilization_bp` and `posture` to be recomputed, a parental-notification obligation is created in `PENDING` with `attempt_count` 0 and a due instant twenty-four hours after `admitted_at`, the existence of that pending obligation recomputes the youth's `risk_posture` to `RESTRICTED` — which blocks that youth's transitional-living placement with `ERR_YOUTH_POSTURE_RESTRICTED` until the obligation is resolved — and the youth's `status` becomes `SHELTERED` with the youth's `version` incremented by one. The `WAITLISTED` branch sets `capacity_blocked` to true, leaves `admitted_at` null, still freezes `priority_score`, raises the program's `waitlisted_count` by one, and consumes no bed at all, so `occupied_beds`, `utilization_bp` and `posture` are all unchanged; no notification obligation is created and the youth's `status` is untouched. The `PENDING_SCREENING` branch leaves `admitted_at` null, consumes no bed, creates no obligation and changes no youth field.

Several gates guard the request, and their relative precedence is fixed by §2.10. The program must be an `EMERGENCY_SHELTER` program, or the request fails with `ERR_PROGRAM_TYPE_MISMATCH`. The youth's `age_years` at the current clock must lie between the program's `min_age_years` and `max_age_years`, both bounds inclusive, or the request fails with `ERR_AGE_OUTSIDE_PROGRAM_BAND`. The youth must not already hold another admission at the same program in status `ACTIVE`, `EXTENDED` or `DISCHARGE_SCHEDULED`, or the request fails with `ERR_YOUTH_ALREADY_SHELTERED`. The youth's most recent screening must not stand `ESCALATED`, or the request fails with `ERR_SCREENING_ESCALATED_BLOCKS_ACTION`. The `planned_discharge_at` instant must be strictly later than the current clock, or the request fails with `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`.

Stays of one youth may not overlap, and the overlap rule is **half-open with a zero buffer and a capacity of one youth**. The interval being requested runs from the current clock up to but not including `planned_discharge_at`. It must not intersect the interval of any other admission of the same youth in status `PENDING_SCREENING`, `WAITLISTED`, `ACTIVE`, `EXTENDED` or `DISCHARGE_SCHEDULED`, where the existing admission's interval runs from its `admitted_at` — falling back to its `requested_at` when it has not yet been admitted — up to but not including its `discharged_at`, falling back to its `planned_discharge_at` when it has not yet been discharged. Because both intervals exclude their end instants, exact adjacency is legal: a new stay whose start instant equals a prior stay's end instant does not collide. Any genuine intersection is refused with `ERR_ADMISSION_OVERLAP`.

The **priority score** is the waitlist's ranking key and the gate under a restricted posture, and it is computed once from the youth record as it stands at request time and then frozen: later changes to the youth's fields never revise it. Four components are added together. The age component is eighteen minus the youth's `age_years`, held at a floor of zero so that a youth aged eighteen or older contributes nothing, multiplied by four — so a fourteen-year-old contributes sixteen points. The risk component is the youth's `trafficking_risk_score`, capped at 40. The engagement component is the youth's `engagement_contact_count` multiplied by five, capped at 20, so a fourth and later encounter adds nothing further. The exposure component is 15 for a youth whose `cumulative_shelter_nights` is exactly zero — a first-time entrant to the system — and otherwise 15 minus the youth's `cumulative_shelter_nights`, held at a floor of zero. Those four are summed; then, if and only if the youth's `trafficking_risk_tier` is `CRITICAL`, a further 10 points are added; and the result is finally capped at 100. Nested caps and floors are evaluated innermost first, and the critical-tier bump is applied **before** the final cap at 100, never after it. Where a program's posture is `RESTRICTED`, the resulting `priority_score` must be at least 60 or the request is refused with `ERR_PROGRAM_RESTRICTED_PRIORITY`.

When a bed is freed, the waitlist is worked by a **selector with a pinned policy**. The candidate set is every admission of that program in status `WAITLISTED`. Candidates are ranked by `priority_score` in descending order, with earlier `created_at` breaking a tie in favour of the longer-waiting youth. Exactly one candidate is promoted per freeing event, and an empty candidate set is simply a no-op rather than an error. Promotion additionally requires that the program's posture is not `FROZEN` immediately after the freeing event, and that the youth's `age_years` still falls inside the program's age band — a youth who aged out while waiting is not promoted. Promotion clears `capacity_blocked` back to false, stamps `admitted_at` with the promotion clock instant, recomputes `projected_billable_nights` against the **original** `planned_discharge_at`, lowers the program's `waitlisted_count` by one, raises the program's `occupied_beds` by one — which again forces `utilization_bp` and `posture` to be recomputed and can drive the program straight back to `FROZEN` — creates that admission's own parental-notification obligation in `PENDING` with a due instant twenty-four hours after the promotion, which flips that youth's `risk_posture` to `RESTRICTED`, and moves that youth's `status` to `SHELTERED` with the youth's `version` incremented by one. Promotion fires from two places: a discharge at the same program, and the nightly census.

An admission that sits in `PENDING_SCREENING` too long is abandoned rather than left open forever: the nightly census moves any admission whose `requested_at` is more than seventy-two hours behind the clock to the terminal status `DENIED`, with no bed accounting change and no change to the youth's status. Admissions can be listed and narrowed by `youth_id`, by `program_id` and by `status`, and reading one admission exposes both billable-night figures, `extension_count`, `capacity_blocked`, `discharged_at`, `exit_destination` and `outcome_positive` alongside the fields above.

### 3.4. The Parental Notification Obligation and Runaway Reporting

The moment a youth occupies a bed, a statutory clock starts. The server itself creates a **GuardianNotification** obligation whenever an admission reaches its active stay — whether that happened inside the admission request, through a screening clearance, or through a waitlist promotion — and no client can create one directly. The obligation references its admission through `admission_id`, opens in `status` `PENDING` with `attempt_count` at 0, `last_attempt_at` null and `resolved_at` null, and carries a `due_at` computed as the admission's `admitted_at` plus twenty-four hours. Because the obligation is created by cascade rather than by a client call, listing the notifications of one admission is the only discovery channel for the new row, and that listing can be narrowed by `status`.

A `ShelterCaseManager` works the obligation by recording attempts. Each recorded attempt supplies `attempt_channel`, one of `PHONE`, `IN_PERSON`, `CERTIFIED_MAIL` or `EMAIL`, raises `attempt_count` by one, stamps `last_attempt_at` with the clock instant and stores the channel, overwriting whatever channel a previous attempt had recorded. The first attempt moves the obligation `PENDING → ATTEMPTED`; further attempts are a legitimate self-loop `ATTEMPTED → ATTEMPTED`, because chasing a guardian usually takes several tries. Attempts may also be recorded against an `OVERDUE` obligation, which stays `OVERDUE`. Attempts are refused once the obligation has reached one of its three terminal statuses — `CONTACTED`, `WAIVED` or `LAW_ENFORCEMENT_REPORTED` — with `ERR_NOTIFICATION_ALREADY_RESOLVED`.

An obligation left unresolved past its due instant becomes a program-level compliance problem. During the nightly census, any obligation in `PENDING` or `ATTEMPTED` whose `due_at` lies strictly before the current clock moves to `OVERDUE`. That transition raises the owning program's `overdue_notification_count` by one, which forces the program's `posture` to be recomputed — so a single overdue row pushes an otherwise open program to `RESTRICTED`, and a second overdue row on the same program drives it to `FROZEN`. The youth's `risk_posture` is recomputed as well, and stays `RESTRICTED`, since an overdue obligation is still an open obligation.

Resolution takes one of three `outcome` values, and the three are guarded very differently. `CONTACTED` records that the guardian was actually reached; it is available from `ATTEMPTED` and from `OVERDUE`, requires that at least one attempt has already been recorded, and requires that the youth's `risk_posture` is not `FROZEN` — an escalated safety screening forbids guardian contact outright. Resolving as contacted with zero recorded attempts, or while the youth is frozen, is refused with `ERR_NOTIFICATION_UNSAFE_CONTACT`. `WAIVED` records that a statute suppresses guardian contact altogether; it is available from `PENDING`, from `ATTEMPTED` and from `OVERDUE`, it may only be performed by a `ProgramAdmin`, it requires a `waiver_reason` of `ABUSE_DISCLOSED`, `TRAFFICKING_RISK` or `COURT_ORDER`, and the acting identifier must differ from the `admitting_user_id` of the admission — the administrator who waives may not be the case manager who admitted, and a match is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. `LAW_ENFORCEMENT_REPORTED` records that a runaway report was filed with law enforcement; it is available only from `OVERDUE` and requires at least one recorded attempt.

Every accepted resolution stamps `resolved_at` with the clock instant and stores the acting identifier as `resolving_user_id`; a law-enforcement resolution additionally stamps `runaway_report_filed_at` with the same instant. When the obligation being resolved was `OVERDUE`, the program's `overdue_notification_count` falls by one and the program's `posture` is recomputed, which can lift a program back out of `FROZEN` or `RESTRICTED`. The youth's `risk_posture` is recomputed on every resolution and drops to `OPEN` once no unresolved obligation remains and no escalated screening keeps the youth frozen — and that is precisely the moment transitional-living placement becomes available to the youth again.

One further path into `WAIVED` exists and is not client-driven: when a reunification case for the youth is approved, each of that youth's still-open obligations — those in `PENDING`, `ATTEMPTED` or `OVERDUE` — is waived by the server with `waiver_reason` set to `COURT_ORDER`, `resolved_at` set to the clock instant, and `resolving_user_id` set to the identifier that decided the case. If the waived row had been `OVERDUE`, the program's `overdue_notification_count` falls by one and the program's `posture` is recomputed exactly as it would be on a manual resolution.

Finally, the runaway-reporting obligation must be discharged before the youth is. An admission whose obligation stands `OVERDUE` with `runaway_report_filed_at` still null cannot be discharged; the attempt is refused with `ERR_RUNAWAY_REPORT_REQUIRED`. Once a runaway report has been filed, the discharge gate is satisfied from then on.

### 3.5. Extensions, Discharge and Billable-Night Accounting

Stays sometimes need to run longer than planned, and a `ShelterCaseManager` may extend one by supplying `new_planned_discharge_at`. The new instant must be strictly later than the admission's current `planned_discharge_at`, or the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`. Extension is available only from status `ACTIVE`, which it moves to `EXTENDED`, or from `EXTENDED`, which it leaves in `EXTENDED` as a self-loop for the second extension. Three ceilings apply. The admission's `extension_count` must still be below two, since a stay may be extended at most twice; a third attempt is refused with `ERR_MAX_EXTENSIONS_REACHED`. The projected billable nights recomputed against the new planned discharge instant must not exceed the program's `max_stay_nights`, with a figure exactly equal to the ceiling being allowed; a figure above it is refused with `ERR_MAX_STAY_EXCEEDED`. And on the first extension, the youth must have no guardian notification standing `OVERDUE`. An accepted extension replaces `planned_discharge_at` with the supplied instant, raises `extension_count` by one, and recomputes `projected_billable_nights`.

Billable nights are what the federal report counts, and the system keeps **two different figures** that are computed differently and must not be confused. Both rest on the same primitive: the whole number of calendar days between the UTC calendar date of one instant and the UTC calendar date of another.

`projected_billable_nights` is the **forward-looking** figure, and it is the only value available before the youth actually leaves. It is the number of whole calendar days from the calendar date of `admitted_at` to the calendar date of `planned_discharge_at`. The planned discharge date is never itself a billable night, and the clock-time component of both instants is ignored entirely, so this figure can never award the noon-cutoff night described below. It is computed when an admission becomes active, recomputed when a waitlisted admission is promoted — against the original planned discharge instant, not a new one — and recomputed after every accepted extension.

`final_billable_nights` is the **chronological actual** figure, and it is the only one that reaches the federal report. It is null until the admission is discharged. At discharge it is the number of whole calendar days from the calendar date of `admitted_at` to the calendar date of `discharged_at`, plus one further night when the hour of the discharge instant is at or after twelve — a youth still in the bed at or after 12:00:00Z on the discharge date has occupied that night's bed for federal reporting purposes. Consequently, a youth discharged on the planned date at 15:00Z ends with exactly one night more than was projected. For any admission that passed through the waitlist, both figures measure from the promotion instant recorded in `admitted_at` and never from `requested_at`. Both figures are whole nights with no rounding of any kind; both are pure calendar arithmetic.

Discharge is performed by a `ShelterCaseManager` supplying `exit_destination`, the federally reported destination, one of `FAMILY_REUNIFIED`, `TRANSITIONAL_LIVING`, `INDEPENDENT_HOUSING`, `OTHER_SHELTER` or `UNKNOWN`. It is available from `ACTIVE`, from `EXTENDED` and from `DISCHARGE_SCHEDULED`, and all three routes behave identically. An admission in `PENDING_SCREENING` or `WAITLISTED` cannot be discharged or extended and is refused with `ERR_ADMISSION_NOT_ACTIVE`; an admission already in `DISCHARGED` or `DENIED` is refused with `ERR_ADMISSION_ALREADY_TERMINAL`.

The discharge cascade has five hops and every one of them must fire. First, `discharged_at` is stamped with the clock instant, the supplied `exit_destination` is stored, `final_billable_nights` is computed by the chronological-actual rule, and `outcome_positive` is derived as true exactly when the destination is `FAMILY_REUNIFIED`, `TRANSITIONAL_LIVING` or `INDEPENDENT_HOUSING` and false otherwise — the field is null until discharge; the youth's `cumulative_shelter_nights` rises by the final billable nights, and the youth's `status` moves to `REUNIFIED` if the youth was in `REUNIFIED_PENDING` and the destination is `FAMILY_REUNIFIED`, and otherwise to `EXITED`, with the youth's `version` incremented by one and `governance_vote_eligible` recomputed to false either way. Second, because the admission has left the set of bed-holding statuses, the program's `occupied_beds` falls by one. Third, because occupied beds changed, the program's `utilization_bp` and `posture` are recomputed. Fourth, if the recomputed posture is no longer `FROZEN`, the waitlist selector promotes exactly one waitlisted admission of that program, which re-increments `occupied_beds` and re-recomputes the posture — possibly straight back to `FROZEN`. Fifth, the promoted admission's own parental-notification obligation is created in `PENDING`, which flips that second youth's `risk_posture` to `RESTRICTED`. Discharging from `DISCHARGE_SCHEDULED` with destination `FAMILY_REUNIFIED` leaves the youth's approved or aftercare reunification case untouched and moves the youth to `REUNIFIED`.

`DISCHARGE_SCHEDULED` is a holding state that only the server sets, and it exists to keep the bed reserved while a reunification is completed. When a youth's reunification case is approved, every admission of that youth in `ACTIVE` or `EXTENDED` moves to `DISCHARGE_SCHEDULED`. The bed is still held, so the program's `occupied_beds` is deliberately unchanged by that move; what does change is the admission's notification obligation, which is waived as described in §3.4. Should the approved case later be superseded, each `DISCHARGE_SCHEDULED` admission of the youth reverts to `ACTIVE`, with no bed accounting change, with `extension_count` and `admitted_at` untouched, and — importantly — with the waived notification **not** reopened.

### 3.6. Family Reunification: Mediation, Home Assessment, Decision and Aftercare

Reunification is pursued where it is safe and appropriate, and it is organised as a **ReunificationCase** owned end to end by a `ReunificationSpecialist`. Opening a case supplies `youth_id`, a `guardian_household_label` of 1 to 120 characters naming the household under mediation, and `expected_youth_version` as described in §2.9. The label is deliberately opaque: two cases for the same youth may name different households, which is exactly how a failed reunification with one household is retried against another. A new case opens in `status` `OPENED` with `opened_at` stamped from the clock, `mediation_round` at 0, `max_mediation_rounds` fixed by the server at 3, `safety_assessment_result` null, `aftercare_plan_due_at` null, `aftercare_contacts_completed` at 0 and `closed_at` null. Opening a case is refused with `ERR_YOUTH_POSTURE_FROZEN` while the youth's `risk_posture` is `FROZEN`.

Opening a case also **supersedes history**. Every earlier case of that youth that has not already reached `CLOSED` or `SUPERSEDED` moves to `SUPERSEDED` with `closed_at` stamped from the clock — this is legal from `OPENED`, `MEDIATION_SCHEDULED`, `MEDIATION_HELD`, `HOME_ASSESSMENT`, `APPROVED` and `AFTERCARE` alike. Any mediation session of a superseded case that was scheduled but never recorded keeps a null `outcome` forever and can never be recorded afterwards. Superseding a case that stood in `APPROVED` or `AFTERCARE` carries two additional consequences, and both must fire: the youth returns `REUNIFIED_PENDING → SHELTERED`, and every admission of that youth in `DISCHARGE_SCHEDULED` reverts to `ACTIVE`. Opening a case always increments the youth's `version` by one. Cases of a youth can be listed — including the superseded ones — and narrowed by `status`.

Mediation runs as a bounded negotiation loop. A specialist schedules a **MediationSession** by supplying `scheduled_start_at` and `scheduled_end_at`, the inclusive start and end of the scheduled window. The server stores the session against its case through `case_id`, sets `round_number` to the case's current `mediation_round` plus one, records `scheduler_user_id`, and leaves `outcome` and `held_at` null. Scheduling is available while the case stands `OPENED`, moving it `OPENED → MEDIATION_SCHEDULED`, and again while it stands `MEDIATION_HELD`, moving it `MEDIATION_HELD → MEDIATION_SCHEDULED` for the next round; from any other case status it is refused with `ERR_CASE_INVALID_STATE`. The loop `MEDIATION_HELD → MEDIATION_SCHEDULED → MEDIATION_HELD` is genuine and is bounded only by the round ceiling: the case's `mediation_round` must be strictly below its `max_mediation_rounds` of 3, and a fourth attempt is refused with `ERR_MEDIATION_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`.

Two temporal rules guard scheduling, and they use deliberately different interval semantics from the admission rule in §3.3. First, session windows on one case are **closed intervals** and touching endpoints do collide: the new window running from `scheduled_start_at` to `scheduled_end_at` inclusive must not intersect the window of any other session of the same case, and a new window whose start instant equals a prior window's end instant is rejected — unlike the half-open admission rule, where exact adjacency is legal. A collision is refused with `ERR_MEDIATION_SESSION_OVERLAP`. Second, a **cooldown buffer** of seventy-two hours applies between rounds: `scheduled_start_at` must be at least seventy-two hours after the `held_at` of the case's most recent held session, and the buffer is measured from that `held_at` instant rather than from the previous window's end. A shorter gap is refused with `ERR_MEDIATION_COOLDOWN_ACTIVE`. Both instants must also be in the future and correctly ordered — `scheduled_end_at` strictly after `scheduled_start_at`, and `scheduled_start_at` strictly after the current clock — or the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`. Sessions of a case can be listed in creation order.

Recording the result of a held session supplies `outcome`, one of `PROGRESS`, `IMPASSE`, `GUARDIAN_NO_SHOW` or `AGREEMENT`. It requires that the session's `outcome` is still null and that the current clock has reached or passed the session's `scheduled_start_at`; a second recording on the same session, or a recording on a session whose case has been superseded or closed, is refused with `ERR_SESSION_ALREADY_RECORDED`. An accepted recording stores the outcome, stamps `held_at` with the clock instant, records the acting identifier as `recorded_by_user_id` — the value that later governs who may decide the case — and raises the case's `mediation_round` by one, moving the case `MEDIATION_SCHEDULED → MEDIATION_HELD`.

Once at least one session has been held, the specialist records the home safety assessment by supplying `safety_assessment_result`, one of `SAFE`, `CONDITIONAL` or `UNSAFE`. This step requires the case to stand `MEDIATION_HELD` and requires the latest held session's outcome to be either `PROGRESS` or `AGREEMENT`; an impasse or a guardian no-show does not open the door to a home assessment. The accepted step stores the verdict and moves the case `MEDIATION_HELD → HOME_ASSESSMENT`.

The decision step is where the two branches of the case diverge, and they diverge sharply. The specialist supplies `decision`, either `APPROVE` or `DECLINE`, and the case must stand `HOME_ASSESSMENT`. Both branches require that the acting identifier differs from the `recorded_by_user_id` of the latest held session, or the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; whoever ran the mediation may not also sign off on its result. Both branches store the acting identifier as `decision_user_id`.

`APPROVE` additionally requires three things: the recorded `safety_assessment_result` must be `SAFE` or `CONDITIONAL`, so an unsafe home can never be approved; the youth's `risk_posture` must not be `FROZEN`, or the request is refused with `ERR_YOUTH_POSTURE_FROZEN`; and every guardian notification belonging to the youth's `ACTIVE` or `EXTENDED` admissions must already stand in `ATTEMPTED`, `CONTACTED`, `WAIVED` or `LAW_ENFORCEMENT_REPORTED` — a still-`PENDING` obligation with zero recorded attempts blocks approval and is refused with `ERR_NOTIFICATION_UNRESOLVED`. An accepted approval fires a four-hop cascade. The case's `aftercare_plan_due_at` is set to the clock instant plus seventy-two hours and the case moves to `APPROVED`. Because the case became approved, the youth moves `SHELTERED → REUNIFIED_PENDING`, the youth's `risk_posture` is recomputed and the youth's `version` increments by one. Because the youth became `REUNIFIED_PENDING`, every admission of the youth in `ACTIVE` or `EXTENDED` moves to `DISCHARGE_SCHEDULED`. And because each such admission left its bed-holding operational state, each of those admissions' guardian notifications standing in `PENDING`, `ATTEMPTED` or `OVERDUE` is waived with `waiver_reason` `COURT_ORDER`, which recomputes the owning program's `overdue_notification_count` and `posture`.

`DECLINE` fires no cascade at all, and the asymmetry is intentional. The case moves to `DECLINED`, `aftercare_plan_due_at` stays null, the youth's `status` is not changed, and no admission and no notification is touched. A declined case is not left open indefinitely: the nightly census closes it once at least one hundred and sixty-eight hours — a full week — have elapsed since the case was last updated, stamping `closed_at` with the clock instant.

Aftercare planning follows an approval and is bounded by the seventy-two-hour window the approval opened. Each logged aftercare contact may carry an optional `contact_note` and raises `aftercare_contacts_completed` by one. The **first** contact must be logged at or before `aftercare_plan_due_at`, and a first contact logged after the clock has passed that instant is refused with `ERR_AFTERCARE_WINDOW_MISSED`; later contacts are unconstrained by that window. The first contact moves the case `APPROVED → AFTERCARE`; the second is a self-loop `AFTERCARE → AFTERCARE`, permitted while `aftercare_contacts_completed` is still below the required total of three; and the third contact — the one logged when the completed count stands at exactly two — sets `aftercare_contacts_completed` to 3, stamps `closed_at` with the clock instant and moves the case `AFTERCARE → CLOSED`. Logging a contact against a case that is not `APPROVED` or `AFTERCARE` is refused with `ERR_CASE_INVALID_STATE`. The terminal statuses of a case are `CLOSED` and `SUPERSEDED`.

### 3.7. Transitional Living: Tenancy, Duration Allowance and Life-Skills Credit

Transitional living is the step between a shelter bed and independence, and a `TlpCoordinator` opens one by creating a **TlpPlacement** from `youth_id`, `program_id` and an optional `documented_monthly_income_cents`, the verified monthly earned income that drives the savings requirement. That income figure defaults to 0 when omitted and may never be negative. On acceptance the server stamps `placed_at` with the clock instant, records the acting coordinator as `coordinator_user_id`, opens the placement with `tenancy_status` `PLACED` and `compliance_status` `IN_COMPLIANCE`, sets `consecutive_probation_reviews` to 0, `life_skills_credit_hours` to 0, `required_savings_cents` to 0, `savings_balance_cents` to 0 and `match_awarded_cents` to 0, leaves `exited_at` and `exit_reason` null, copies `base_allowance_days` from the program's `max_placement_days`, raises the program's `occupied_beds` by one — which forces that program's `utilization_bp` and `posture` to be recomputed — and moves the youth `SHELTERED → TLP_ENROLLED` with the youth's `version` incremented by one and `governance_vote_eligible` recomputed, staying true.

Placement is the most heavily gated operation in the system, and the order of its gates is fixed by §2.10. The program must be a `TRANSITIONAL_LIVING` program, or the request fails with `ERR_PROGRAM_TYPE_MISMATCH`. The youth's `age_years` must lie inside the program's licensed age band, both bounds inclusive, or the request fails with `ERR_AGE_OUTSIDE_PROGRAM_BAND`. The youth's most recent safety screening must not stand `ESCALATED`, or the request fails with `ERR_SCREENING_ESCALATED_BLOCKS_ACTION`; it must furthermore exist and stand `CLEARED`, and a missing or non-cleared screening fails with `ERR_SCREENING_REQUIRED`. There is no waitlist in transitional living at all: the program's `occupied_beds` must be strictly below its `licensed_bed_capacity`, and a full program fails with `ERR_BED_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` — as does a program frozen because its beds are full, whereas a program frozen only because of overdue notifications does not block a placement. Placement exclusivity is **point-in-time with an open-ended interval**: the youth's placement occupies every instant from `placed_at` onwards with no end instant participating at all, so no adjacency case exists and the rule reduces to a simple check that the youth holds no other placement whose `tenancy_status` is `PLACED` or `NOTICE_GIVEN`; a second open placement fails with `ERR_PLACEMENT_OVERLAP`. And last of all, the youth's `risk_posture` must be `OPEN`: a `RESTRICTED` posture — meaning an unresolved parental-notification obligation on any of the youth's admissions, or a `CRITICAL` trafficking tier — fails with `ERR_YOUTH_POSTURE_RESTRICTED`. That posture gate sits at the very bottom of the ladder, so every other placement check listed here wins over it.

A placement carries **two independent state dimensions on the same row**, and the distinction is fundamental. `tenancy_status` is the occupancy dimension and runs `PLACED`, `NOTICE_GIVEN`, `EXITED`, with `EXITED` terminal. `compliance_status` is the savings dimension and runs `IN_COMPLIANCE`, `SAVINGS_DEFICIENT`, `PROBATION`. The two dimensions evolve independently: a compliance review never moves the tenancy dimension except in the single coupled case described below, and a tenancy change never alters `compliance_status` at all.

Elapsed time on a placement is derived, never stored by a client. `days_elapsed` is the whole number of twenty-four-hour days between `placed_at` and the current logical clock, floored, and it is recomputed on every read of the placement and on every census run.

The **duration allowance** is where life-skills work earns extra time, and its evaluation order is pinned in three steps that must be followed exactly. Step one computes `consumed_ratio_bp` as `days_elapsed` multiplied by 10000, divided by `base_allowance_days`, floored to whole basis points — and the divisor is always the **base** allowance copied from the program at placement time, never the final allowance. Step two computes the life-skills bonus from `life_skills_credit_hours`: the milestone bonus is 90 days at 60 or more credited hours, 60 days at 40 or more hours, 30 days at 20 or more hours, and 0 days below 20 hours; that milestone bonus is then subjected to a late-stage haircut, so `earned_bonus_days` is the milestone bonus halved and floored to whole days when step one's `consumed_ratio_bp` has reached or passed 7500 — three quarters of the base allowance consumed — and is the full milestone bonus otherwise. Step three sets `allowance_days` to `base_allowance_days` plus `earned_bonus_days`.

The dependency looks circular, since the final allowance depends on the bonus, which depends on a consumption ratio that is itself a ratio against an allowance. It is not circular, because the order above is pinned: step one always divides by the base allowance. Recomputing the consumption ratio against the final allowance after step three is the explicitly rejected alternative — it would push the ratio back below the haircut threshold and hand the youth the full bonus. A placement with a base allowance of 540 days, 420 days elapsed and 40 credited hours therefore has a consumed ratio of 7777 basis points, takes the haircut, earns 30 bonus days and reaches an allowance of 570 days, rather than earning 60 bonus days for an allowance of 600.

Life-skills credit is recorded as an immutable **LifeSkillsCompletion** against the placement, supplying `module_code` — one of `BUDGETING`, `TENANCY`, `EMPLOYMENT_READINESS`, `HEALTH_LITERACY`, `CONFLICT_RESOLUTION` or `CIVIC_ENGAGEMENT` — and `credit_hours`, the verified curriculum hours, which must be between 1 and 40 inclusive. The server records the owning placement as `placement_id`, stamps `completed_at` with the clock instant and records the acting identifier as `verified_by_user_id`. Crediting a completion adds the credited hours to the placement's `life_skills_credit_hours`, then recomputes `earned_bonus_days` and `allowance_days` by the three-step rule, and increments the placement's `version` by one. Completions of a placement can be listed. A placement that has already consumed its full duration allowance — one whose `days_elapsed` has reached or passed its `allowance_days` — can accept neither a new life-skills completion nor a compliance review and must simply be exited; either attempt is refused with `ERR_DURATION_LIMIT_REACHED`.

The tenancy dimension advances in three ways. The nightly census gives notice, moving a `PLACED` placement to `NOTICE_GIVEN`, once `days_elapsed` has reached or passed the allowance minus a thirty-day notice lead — that is, once thirty days or fewer of the allowance remain. A second consecutive savings-probation review also gives notice, and this is the **only** coupling between the two dimensions: when `consecutive_probation_reviews` reaches two while the tenancy is still `PLACED`, the tenancy moves to `NOTICE_GIVEN` while `compliance_status` stays `PROBATION`. The reverse coupling never holds. Finally, the census exits a placement outright, moving `NOTICE_GIVEN` to `EXITED` with `exit_reason` set to `DURATION_LIMIT` once `days_elapsed` has reached or passed `allowance_days`.

A coordinator ends a tenancy deliberately by supplying `exit_reason`, one of `DURATION_LIMIT`, `INDEPENDENT_HOUSING`, `REUNIFIED`, `NONCOMPLIANCE` or `YOUTH_INITIATED`. This is available from `PLACED` and from `NOTICE_GIVEN`, and both routes behave identically: `tenancy_status` becomes `EXITED`, `exited_at` is stamped with the clock instant, the reason is stored, the program's `occupied_beds` falls by one — which forces `utilization_bp` and `posture` to be recomputed — the youth moves `TLP_ENROLLED → EXITED` with the youth's `version` incremented by one and `governance_vote_eligible` recomputed to false, and `compliance_status` is frozen at whatever value it held and is no longer reviewable. Any savings entry, life-skills completion, compliance review or second exit attempted against an already-exited placement is refused with `ERR_PLACEMENT_ALREADY_EXITED`. Placements can be listed and narrowed by `youth_id`, by `program_id`, by `tenancy_status` and by `compliance_status`.

### 3.8. Savings Accounts, the Program Match and Savings-Compliance Review

Every placement carries a savings account built out of immutable **SavingsLedgerEntry** postings. A `TlpCoordinator` posts an entry by supplying `entry_type` — `DEPOSIT`, `WITHDRAWAL` or `REVERSAL` — an `amount_cents` magnitude that is always positive and therefore at least 1, the `expected_placement_version` required by §2.9, and an optional `source_note` of up to 200 characters recording provenance. The server records the owning placement as `placement_id`, stamps `posted_at` with the clock instant, records the acting identifier as `recorded_by_user_id`, and derives three figures onto the entry.

`signed_amount_cents` gives the posting its direction: it is the positive `amount_cents` for a `DEPOSIT`, and the negation of `amount_cents` for both a `WITHDRAWAL` and a `REVERSAL`. `match_component_cents` is the per-entry program match: for a `DEPOSIT` it is the entry's `amount_cents` multiplied by the program's `savings_match_rate_bp`, divided by 10000 and rounded **half up** to whole cents; for a `REVERSAL` it is the exact negation of that same quantity; and for a `WITHDRAWAL` it is 0. `balance_after_cents` is the placement's `savings_balance_cents` as it stands immediately after this entry posted.

**The rounding order is pinned and it is the crux of the calculation**: each entry's match is rounded to whole cents *first*, and only then are the per-entry matches summed. The placement's `match_awarded_cents` is the sum of `match_component_cents` over every entry of the placement, held at a floor of zero and capped at 50000 cents, with the cap comparison applied *after* the summation and any surplus above the cap never carried forward. The placement's `savings_balance_cents` is the sum of `signed_amount_cents` over every entry plus the capped `match_awarded_cents` — that identity is the conserved quantity of the account and must hold after every posting. Every entry of the placement participates in both sums with no status filter of any kind, because entries are immutable and are never excluded. The rejected alternative — summing the deposit amounts first and applying the rate once to the total — produces different cents and must not be used: with a match rate of 2500 basis points and three deposits of 1010 cents each, the pinned per-entry order yields 253 plus 253 plus 253 for a total of 759 cents, while the rejected order yields a single rounding of 757.5 to 758 cents. Posting an entry also recomputes the youth's `lifetime_savings_deposited_cents`, which is the sum of `amount_cents` over the youth's `DEPOSIT` entries minus the sum of `amount_cents` over the youth's `REVERSAL` entries, taken across every placement that youth has ever held; and it increments the placement's `version` by one.

Three fiscal guards protect the account. A `WITHDRAWAL` whose `amount_cents` exceeds the placement's current `savings_balance_cents` is refused with `ERR_SAVINGS_INSUFFICIENT_BALANCE`. A `WITHDRAWAL` attempted while the placement's `compliance_status` is `PROBATION` is refused with `ERR_SAVINGS_WITHDRAWAL_BLOCKED` — savings are locked while a youth is on probation, and they unlock again the moment a review moves the placement off probation. A `REVERSAL` whose `amount_cents` exceeds the sum of `DEPOSIT` amounts on that placement less the sum of prior `REVERSAL` amounts is refused with `ERR_SAVINGS_REVERSAL_UNMATCHED`, so a reversal can never invent savings that were never deposited. The ledger of a placement can be listed in posting order and narrowed by `entry_type`.

The **required savings** figure is what a review measures the balance against, and it grows in whole months. The number of full elapsed months is `days_elapsed` divided by thirty and floored. The monthly requirement in cents is the placement's `documented_monthly_income_cents` multiplied by the program's `required_savings_rate_bp`, divided by 10000 and floored to whole cents. `required_savings_cents` is that monthly requirement multiplied by the number of full elapsed months — so a youth in the first thirty days of a placement owes nothing at all. Both divisions floor, and the figure is recomputed on every compliance review and on every read of the placement.

A **savings-compliance review**, which a `TlpCoordinator` or a `ProgramAdmin` may run and which takes no request body at all, recomputes the required savings and then writes only the `compliance_status` dimension. Three outcomes are possible and they are evaluated as inclusive comparisons, so an exactly-met requirement counts as met. A balance at or above `required_savings_cents` lands on `IN_COMPLIANCE`. Otherwise a balance at or above half the required savings, that half being floored to whole cents, lands on `SAVINGS_DEFICIENT`. Otherwise the review lands on `PROBATION`.

The compliance dimension is genuinely cyclic and every transition out of every state is available on equal terms. From `IN_COMPLIANCE` a review may move the placement to `SAVINGS_DEFICIENT`, which resets `consecutive_probation_reviews` to 0 and leaves the tenancy dimension untouched, or to `PROBATION`, which raises `consecutive_probation_reviews` by one and blocks withdrawals from that point on. From `SAVINGS_DEFICIENT` a review may move the placement back to `IN_COMPLIANCE`, resetting `consecutive_probation_reviews` to 0, or on to `PROBATION`, raising `consecutive_probation_reviews` by one. From `PROBATION` a review may move the placement to `IN_COMPLIANCE`, resetting `consecutive_probation_reviews` to 0 and unblocking withdrawals, or to `SAVINGS_DEFICIENT`, likewise resetting `consecutive_probation_reviews` to 0 and unblocking withdrawals. In short, any review that does not land on `PROBATION` resets the consecutive-probation counter to zero, and any review that lands on `PROBATION` raises it by one. Reviews require the tenancy not to be `EXITED`, and reviews require duration allowance to remain. Every review increments the placement's `version` by one.

### 3.9. School Enrollment Rights and Youth Voice in Program Governance

Positive youth development rests on two rights that the system treats as non-negotiable: the right of an unaccompanied youth to enroll in school immediately, and the right of resident youth to a voice in how their program is run.

An **EducationEnrollment** is created against a youth by either a `TlpCoordinator` or a `ShelterCaseManager`, supplying `school_name`, the receiving school or program name of 1 to 120 characters, and `enrollment_type`, one of `PUBLIC_K12`, `GED_PROGRAM`, `POSTSECONDARY` or `VOCATIONAL`, which covers both education and employment-track placements. The server stamps `enrolled_at` with the clock instant, records the acting staff member as `liaison_user_id`, opens the enrollment in `status` `IMMEDIATE_ENROLLMENT` with `verified_at` null, and sets `records_waived` to the youth's `unaccompanied` value as it stands at enrollment time — recording that guardian-held records were waived under the school-enrollment right. This operation is deliberately exempt from every gate in the system: neither the program posture nor the youth's `risk_posture` ever blocks it, because the enrollment right of an unaccompanied youth is unconditional. When the receiving school later confirms the youth's records, a staff member records the verification, which moves the enrollment `IMMEDIATE_ENROLLMENT → VERIFIED` and stamps `verified_at` with the clock instant. Enrollments of a youth can be listed and narrowed by `status`.

Governance runs on **GovernanceProposal** records scoped to a program, whose residents form the electorate. A proposal may be opened by a `ProgramAdmin` or by a `YouthMember`, supplying `title` of 1 to 160 characters, a `category` of `HOUSE_RULES`, `PROGRAMMING`, `GRIEVANCE_PROCESS` or `FOOD_SERVICE`, and `voting_closes_at`, the instant after which no vote may be cast, which must be strictly later than the current clock or the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`. The proposal opens in `status` `OPEN_FOR_VOTE` with `support_vote_count` and `oppose_vote_count` at 0, `eligible_electorate_size` at 0 — it stays 0 until the vote is closed and the electorate is frozen — `support_ratio_bp` at 0, and `adopted_at` and `decided_by_user_id` null. When the opener is a `YouthMember` rather than an administrator, that youth must itself be a vote-eligible resident of the program, or the request is refused with `ERR_YOUTH_NOT_VOTE_ELIGIBLE`. Proposals of a program can be listed and narrowed by `status`, and reading one proposal returns its live tally.

Only a `YouthMember` votes. Casting a **GovernanceVote** supplies just `stance`, either `SUPPORT` or `OPPOSE`; the server takes `youth_id` from the acting header rather than from the body, stores the proposal reference as `proposal_id`, stamps `cast_at` with the clock instant, opens the vote in `status` `COUNTED` with `retracted_at` null, and raises the proposal's `support_vote_count` or `oppose_vote_count` by one according to the stance. Four gates apply. The proposal must still be `OPEN_FOR_VOTE`, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_PROPOSAL_ALREADY_DECIDED`. The youth must hold no existing vote on that proposal in status `COUNTED`, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_VOTE`; a previously retracted vote does not block a fresh one. The youth's `governance_vote_eligible` must be true and the youth must be resident in the proposal's program by the electorate test below, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_YOUTH_NOT_VOTE_ELIGIBLE`. And the current clock must be at or before the proposal's `voting_closes_at`; a clock strictly past that instant is refused with `ERR_PROPOSAL_VOTE_CLOSED`.

A youth may retract its own vote, which moves the vote `COUNTED → RETRACTED`, stamps `retracted_at` with the clock instant, and lowers the proposal's `support_vote_count` or `oppose_vote_count` by one, restoring the pre-vote tally exactly. Retraction is subject to the same proposal-decided and voting-deadline gates as casting, and to the ownership rule of §2.1: a vote may only be retracted by the youth who cast it, and any other youth is refused with `ERR_NOT_VOTE_OWNER`. Votes can be listed per proposal and per youth, narrowed by `status` in both cases.

Closing the vote is a `ProgramAdmin` action, and it computes the **adoption tally** and freezes it onto the row. `support_vote_count` is the number of votes on the proposal whose `stance` is `SUPPORT` and whose `status` is `COUNTED`; `oppose_vote_count` is the number whose `stance` is `OPPOSE` and whose `status` is `COUNTED`. Retracted votes are excluded from both counts. `eligible_electorate_size` is the number of youths whose `governance_vote_eligible` is true and who are resident in the proposal's program, where residency means the youth holds an admission at that program in status `ACTIVE`, `EXTENDED` or `DISCHARGE_SCHEDULED`, or holds a transitional-living placement at that program whose `tenancy_status` is `PLACED` or `NOTICE_GIVEN`. The electorate is evaluated at close time and then frozen onto the row. `support_ratio_bp` is 0 when the electorate is empty, and otherwise the support count multiplied by 10000, divided by the electorate size and floored to whole basis points.

The outcome then follows from three clauses. An empty electorate is not a decision at all: it is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_ELECTORATE`, nothing is frozen, nothing is changed, and the proposal remains `OPEN_FOR_VOTE`. Otherwise the proposal becomes `ADOPTED` when `support_ratio_bp` has reached or passed 5000 basis points — a simple majority of the electorate — **and** `support_vote_count` is at least 2, so a single supporter can never adopt a proposal however small the electorate. Because the ratio comparison is inclusive, an electorate of four with exactly two support votes sits at exactly 5000 basis points and is therefore adopted. Otherwise, when either the ratio falls short of 5000 basis points or the support count falls short of 2, the proposal becomes `REJECTED`. Both decided outcomes freeze `eligible_electorate_size` and `support_ratio_bp` onto the row and record the acting administrator as `decided_by_user_id`; adoption additionally stamps `adopted_at` with the clock instant, while rejection leaves `adopted_at` null. A second close attempt on an already-decided proposal is refused with `ERR_PROPOSAL_ALREADY_DECIDED`.

### 3.10. Federal Outcome Reporting and the Nightly Census Epoch

The federal compliance and outcome report is a computed read available only to a `ProgramAdmin`, and it is the artifact the whole system exists to produce honestly. It aggregates over the program's **discharged admissions only**, taken in ascending `created_at` order: admissions in `ACTIVE`, `EXTENDED`, `DISCHARGE_SCHEDULED`, `WAITLISTED`, `PENDING_SCREENING` or `DENIED` are excluded from every outcome and stay-duration aggregate, and admissions belonging to other programs never enter at all.

The report answers with sixteen figures. `discharged_count` is the number of discharged admissions of the program. `billable_nights_total` is the sum of `final_billable_nights` over those discharged admissions. `average_stay_nights_bp` is 0 when nothing has been discharged, and otherwise the billable-night total multiplied by one hundred, divided by the discharged count and floored — so it is nights expressed to two implied decimal places, and eight nights over three discharges reports as 266. `over_limit_admission_count` is the number of discharged admissions whose `final_billable_nights` exceeded the program's `max_stay_nights`. `positive_outcome_count` is the number of discharged admissions whose `outcome_positive` is true. `positive_outcome_rate_bp` is 0 when nothing has been discharged, and otherwise the positive-outcome count multiplied by 10000, divided by the discharged count and floored to whole basis points. `waived_notification_count` is the number of guardian notifications in status `WAIVED` whose admission belongs to the program, and `runaway_report_count` is the number in status `LAW_ENFORCEMENT_REPORTED` whose admission belongs to the program. `bed_capacity_compliant` is true exactly when the program's `occupied_beds` is at or below its `licensed_bed_capacity`. The remaining figures are the program's own live fields copied straight through: `licensed_bed_capacity`, `occupied_beds`, `waitlisted_count`, `utilization_bp`, `posture` and `overdue_notification_count`.

Because the system runs on a logical clock, the passage of a night is an explicit administrative act rather than something that happens on its own. Advancing the clock, as described in §2.5, moves time but mutates nothing. A second administrator-only capability then runs one **census epoch** against the current clock and performs, in this order, every deadline-driven transition in the system: guardian notifications in `PENDING` or `ATTEMPTED` whose `due_at` has passed move to `OVERDUE`, after which each affected program's `overdue_notification_count` and `posture` are recomputed; admissions in `PENDING_SCREENING` whose `requested_at` is more than seventy-two hours behind the clock move to `DENIED`; the waitlist selector runs for every program whose posture is not `FROZEN`, promoting one admission per program per epoch, ranked by `priority_score` descending with earlier `created_at` breaking ties; placements in `PLACED` reach `NOTICE_GIVEN` once `days_elapsed` has reached the allowance less the thirty-day notice lead, and placements in `NOTICE_GIVEN` reach `EXITED` with `exit_reason` `DURATION_LIMIT` once `days_elapsed` has reached `allowance_days`; reunification cases in `DECLINED` reach `CLOSED` once a hundred and sixty-eight hours have passed since they were last updated; and every youth's `trafficking_risk_score` is re-decayed against the new clock by the history rule of §3.1, with every program posture and youth posture recomputed at the end. The epoch answers with a count of the work it did in seven integers: `overdue_marked`, `admissions_denied`, `admissions_promoted`, `placements_noticed`, `placements_exited`, `cases_closed` and `postures_recomputed`.
