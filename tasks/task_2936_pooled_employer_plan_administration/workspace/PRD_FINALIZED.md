# Pooled Employer Plan Administration API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A Pooled Employer Plan (PEP) lets many unrelated employers participate in one retirement plan under a single plan document, a single trust and a single annual return. That structure is attractive to small employers precisely because it removes the administrative machinery they cannot afford — but it moves that machinery onto the **pooled plan provider**, which must now reconcile the elections, payrolls, contributions, coverage data and exits of dozens of independent employers without ever letting one employer's failure contaminate the plan-level record. The provider that gets this wrong does not merely produce a bad report; it produces a plan-level filing that misstates delinquent participant contributions, a single audit that was performed by a firm holding a fiduciary role, or a spin-off that moves money out of the trust without the corresponding deduction staying in it.

This document specifies the service that administers such a plan end to end. It carries a registered pooled plan provider from admission to the plan register, through authoring the single plan document and engaging the recordkeeper, third-party administrator, trustee, investment manager and audit firm, into the allocation of statutory fiduciary functions and the activation of the plan. It then governs the recurring operational loop: employers adopt the plan by electing eligibility, vesting and match terms inside the common structure; payroll batches arrive from their payroll systems and are reconciled against the elected match formula; contributions are remitted to the trust and are judged timely or late against a business-day deposit deadline; late deposits open a correction workflow that computes lost earnings, a restoration floor and a Form 5330 excise tax. In parallel, coverage and nondiscrimination tests are run across the pooled population or against a single employer treated as if it maintained its own plan, and the annual return is prepared, audited, certified and filed.

Two design commitments run through every requirement that follows. The first is **arithmetic auditability**: the plan document maintains money and headcount control totals whose deltas must be exactly zero after every operation that moves value or people, so that any drift is a defect rather than a rounding opinion. The second is **segregation of duties**: the pooled plan provider certifies employer-level data but may not prepare, attest or file the return; the service provider that prepared the return may not audit it; no firm holding an allocated fiduciary function — nor an affiliate of one — may supply the plan audit; and only the regulator-side operator accepts the filing. Every rule below is written so that a reader can determine, for any request, exactly which check rejects it and with which error identifier.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the API must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which it acts. If either header is absent, or is present with an empty-string value, the request is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS` (HTTP 401); an empty string counts as absent, not as a value. If `X-User-Role` carries anything other than one of the five declared roles, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE` (HTTP 401). The five roles are, with exact spelling: `RegulatorOps`, `PooledPlanProvider`, `AdoptingEmployer`, `ServiceProvider`, and `IndependentAuditor`. A syntactically valid role that is not among the roles authorised for the requested operation is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

`RegulatorOps` is the government-side operator. It admits pooled plan providers to the register, accepts the plan's annual return, and drives the server's logical clock; it never touches employer or contribution data, though it may read every resource in the domain for oversight purposes. When `X-User-Role` is `RegulatorOps`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity: the server may record it for audit purposes but never compares it to any entity's `id`, and no ownership check narrows what a `RegulatorOps` caller may reach.

`IndependentAuditor` is the independent qualified public accountant that performs the plan-level single audit. Its only mutating capability is attestation, and it may read the plan document, its service providers, its employers, its compliance tests and its filings. When `X-User-Role` is `IndependentAuditor`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the audit firm whose independence is actually tested is not inferred from the header but named explicitly in the request body as `audit_firm_service_provider_id`, and the server compares that named identifier — not `X-User-ID` — against the plan's engaged providers and fiduciary allocations.

The remaining three roles are entity-backed, meaning the header value must be the primary key of a persisted row. When `X-User-Role` is `PooledPlanProvider`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `PooledPlanProvider`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `PooledPlanProvider` compare the resource's provider foreign key to `X-User-ID`, and because plan documents, service providers, fiduciary allocations, employers, elections, payroll batches, deposits, compliance tests, filings, exit transfers and fee disclosures all descend from a plan document, a `PooledPlanProvider` caller reaches exactly the descendants of the plans whose `pooled_plan_provider_id` is its own `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `AdoptingEmployer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `AdoptingEmployer`; ownership checks compare the employer foreign key on the resource — directly on elections, payroll batches and exit transfers, and transitively through the parent batch on remittance deposits — to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `ServiceProvider`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `ServiceProvider`; a fiduciary role allocation may only be acknowledged by the provider whose `service_provider_id` the allocation names, and every other service-provider capability is limited to the plan on which that provider is engaged, that is, the plan whose `id` equals the provider's own `plan_document_id`. Where an entity-backed role acts on a resource that resolves to a different owner, the request is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403).

Ownership is checked after role authorisation and before any state or business rule. A caller who is both unauthorised for the endpoint and not the owner sees `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`, never `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: its default is 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: its default is 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0, with no upper bound. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 422) rather than being silently clamped.

List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending unless a specific listing states otherwise, so pagination is stable across pages. For every list response the `meta` object carries `limit` (the effective page size applied), `offset` (the effective starting position applied), `total_count` (the number of rows matching the request before the page window is applied) and `has_more` (true when rows remain beyond the current window). Single-resource responses omit all four.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Version Semantics

All persisted resources expose an `id`, which is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix. Clients never supply an `id`, and no business rule — no selector, ranking, tie-break or fallback — may be decided by comparing `id` values. Where a rule needs a deterministic ordering over otherwise equal rows, it uses `created_at`.

Most resources also carry three further common properties. `created_at` is the ISO 8601 UTC instant of creation, always rendered with exactly three fractional digits (for example, `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`). `updated_at` has the same format and is refreshed on every mutation, including mutations that the resource did not request itself but received as a cascaded side effect from a sibling or child operation. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly one on every mutation, again including cascaded ones. Every recomputation of a derived control total on a plan document, every suspension of an employer caused by someone else's late deposit, and every revocation of a fiduciary allocation caused by a newer allocation is a mutation in this sense: it refreshes `updated_at` and increments `version`.

One entity is deliberately exempt. A `FeeDisclosure` is an immutable event row: it carries only `id` and `created_at`, has neither `updated_at` nor `version`, and has no update capability of any kind. Everything a disclosure asserts is computed once, at issue time, and never restated.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock, Calendar Dates and the Business-Day Calendar

Every deadline, window, timeliness judgement and "not in the future" check in this specification reads a server-side **logical clock** rather than real wall-clock time. Before the clock has ever been set it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to wall-clock time. An administrative capability reserved to `RegulatorOps` moves the clock to a supplied `current_utc` instant; it changes no business state. Where a rule speaks of "the logical-clock calendar date", it means the UTC calendar date of that instant.

Two date shapes appear in payloads. Calendar dates are ISO 8601 `YYYY-MM-DD` strings with no time component: `registration_effective_date`, `effective_date`, `plan_year_end_date`, `effective_adoption_date`, `election_window_close_date`, `effective_from`, `effective_to`, `pay_period_start`, `pay_period_end`, `pay_date`, `withholding_date`, `deposit_due_date`, `deposit_date`, `recovery_date`, `requested_exit_date`, `blackout_start_date`, `blackout_end_date`, `disclosure_period_start`, `disclosure_period_end`, `filing_deadline_date` and `filed_date` are all of this kind. Instants are ISO 8601 UTC datetimes with three fractional digits, taken from the logical clock: `registered_at`, `acknowledged_at`, `revoked_at`, `approved_at`, `validated_at`, `posted_at`, `funded_at`, `run_at`, `prepared_at`, `audit_attested_at`, `employer_data_certified_at`, `settled_at`, `exited_at` and `disclosed_at` are all of this kind. A value of the wrong shape is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION`.

Several rules count **business days**. A date is a business day when its weekday is neither Saturday nor Sunday and it is not a recognised federal holiday. The recognised holidays are, for 2025: January 1, January 20, February 17, May 26, June 19, July 4, September 1, October 13, November 11, November 27 and December 25. For 2026: January 1, January 19, February 16, May 25, June 19, July 3, September 7, October 12, November 11, November 26 and December 25. For 2027: January 1, January 18, February 15, May 31, June 18, July 5, September 6, October 11, November 11, November 25 and December 24.

Three business-day primitives are used throughout. **Counting elapsed business days between two dates** means counting the dates that are strictly later than the earlier date, no later than the later date, and are business days — the interval is open on the left and closed on the right, so the starting date itself never contributes and the ending date always contributes when it is a business day. **The n-th business day after a start date** means walking forward from the day after the start date and taking the n-th business day encountered. **The n-th business day of a calendar month** means walking forward from the first day of that month and taking the n-th business day encountered. These primitives produce integer day counts or calendar dates; nothing here is rounded.

### 2.6. Cents, Percentages and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts are integers denominated in cents, and every field name carrying money ends in `_cents`. Wherever a monetary computation produces a fractional cent, the result is rounded **half up** to a whole cent — that is, a remainder of exactly one half cent rounds away from zero. Where a calculation is specified as a sequence of tiers, segments or buckets, the rounding happens at the step the calculation names and not at any other step; summing unrounded intermediate values and rounding once at the end is a different, incorrect answer, and the specification for each calculation states the pinned evaluation order explicitly.

Percentage-valued fields are decimal numbers rounded half up to two decimal places, with one exception: `expense_rebate_pct` is expressed to one decimal place. The two-decimal, round-half-up convention governs `hce_avg_deferral_pct`, `nhce_avg_deferral_pct`, `hce_benefiting_pct`, `nhce_benefiting_pct`, `ratio_pct`, `adp_limit_pct` and `basis_share_pct`. Integer counts are exact and never rounded.

### 2.7. Stale-Version Protection on Superseding Writes

Two create operations can silently destroy work the client never saw, and both are therefore guarded by optimistic concurrency control against the *parent* resource's `version`. No other endpoint takes a version field, and supplying one where it is not specified has no effect.

Creating a plan design election at `POST /pep/plan-design-elections` requires a non-optional `expected_adopting_employer_version` field carrying the `version` of the target `AdoptingEmployer` as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the employer's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_EMPLOYER_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The protection matters because a new election legitimately supersedes whatever in-flight election the employer already had and resets the employer's `adoption_status` back to `APPLIED`; a writer holding a stale view of the employer would discard election content it never read. A successful create advances the employer's `version`, so a second create still carrying the previously observed value is rejected.

Creating a fiduciary role allocation at `POST /pep/fiduciary-role-allocations` requires a non-optional `expected_plan_document_version` field carrying the `version` of the target `PlanDocument` as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the plan document's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PLAN_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Here the destructive effect is displacement rather than discard: a new allocation for the same statutory function revokes the incumbent holder, so a writer working from a stale plan view would remove a role holder it never observed. A successful create advances the plan document's `version`, so the next create carrying the earlier observed value is rejected.

### 2.8. Reverse Navigation and Left-to-Right Resolution of Path Identifiers

Every business resource is addressed at its own top-level collection beneath the `/pep` mount. Ownership is expressed through foreign-key fields on the child, never through nested write paths, so no create or action path embeds a parent identifier. Reverse navigation is instead provided by read-only sub-resource listings hanging off the parent: a provider's plan documents; a plan document's service providers, adopting employers, fiduciary role allocations, compliance tests, plan filings and fee disclosures; a service provider's affiliates, fiduciary role allocations and attested filings; an employer's design elections, payroll batches, compliance tests and exit transfers; and a payroll batch's remittance deposits. Because a service provider may name another provider on the same plan as its affiliate, the provider layer is a graph rather than a tree, and the affiliates listing returns the providers that name the addressed provider as *their* affiliate.

Identifiers in a path are resolved strictly left to right, and the first identifier that does not resolve produces that entity's own not-found error before any later identifier, body field, state condition or business rule is considered: `ERR_PLAN_PROVIDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PLAN_DOCUMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SERVICE_PROVIDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FIDUCIARY_ALLOCATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ADOPTING_EMPLOYER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DESIGN_ELECTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PAYROLL_BATCH_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REMITTANCE_DEPOSIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COMPLIANCE_TEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PLAN_FILING_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_EXIT_TRANSFER_NOT_FOUND`, each carrying HTTP 404.

Foreign-key identifiers in a request body are resolved after every path identifier, and among themselves in the order the fields are declared for the entity being created. A body foreign key that is well-formed but does not resolve produces the same not-found error the corresponding path identifier would produce — `ERR_PLAN_PROVIDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PLAN_DOCUMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SERVICE_PROVIDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ADOPTING_EMPLOYER_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_PAYROLL_BATCH_NOT_FOUND`, selected by the entity the field points at, including when the unresolvable value arrives as `pooled_plan_provider_id`, `affiliated_service_provider_id` or `audit_firm_service_provider_id`. One body foreign key is reached only through its own ownership comparison: the `pooled_plan_provider_id` of a new plan document names the sponsoring provider that is itself the ownership subject of the create, so a caller supplying a value other than its own `X-User-ID` is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` at priority 4 whether or not that value resolves, and the sponsor's existence is judged — reporting `ERR_PLAN_PROVIDER_NOT_FOUND` — only for a value equal to the caller's own identifier. A body foreign key that is absent altogether, or is not a string, is a shape defect and surfaces as `ERR_VALIDATION` instead.

### 2.9. Plan Control Totals, Derived Gates and Compensating Reversals

The plan document is the reconciliation point for the whole plan, and its derived fields are recomputed after **every** transition that moves money or headcount: payroll batch validation, deposit posting, deposit reversal, correction funding, exit settlement, exit reversal, employer admission and census restatement. On the money side, `total_validated_contributions_cents` is the sum of `batch_total_cents` over the plan's payroll batches in either `VALIDATED` or `REMITTED` status; `aggregate_receivable_cents` is the sum of `batch_total_cents` over the batches still in `VALIDATED` status; `total_assets_cents` is the sum of `account_balance_cents` over every employer of the plan plus `expense_recovered_cents`; `total_corrections_funded_cents` is the sum of `restoration_cents` over the plan's deposits whose `timeliness_status` is `CORRECTED`; `transferred_out_cents` is the sum of `transfer_amount_cents` over the plan's exit transfers in `SETTLED` status; and `expense_recovered_cents` is the sum of `deductions_applied_cents` over those same settled transfers. From these, `contribution_control_delta_cents` is obtained by adding `total_assets_cents`, `aggregate_receivable_cents` and `transferred_out_cents`, then subtracting `total_corrections_funded_cents` and subtracting `total_validated_contributions_cents`. It must be exactly zero after every one of the triggers listed above; a non-zero value is a defect, never a tolerance.

The headcount side mirrors this. `participant_count` on the plan document is the sum of each employer's own `participant_count` over the employers of the plan whose `adoption_status` is any of `APPLIED`, `ELECTIONS_SUBMITTED`, `RETURNED_FOR_REVISION`, `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED`, `EXIT_REQUESTED` or `SPINOFF_IN_PROGRESS`; employers in `EXITED` are excluded. `cumulative_enrolled_participant_count` is a monotone control total: it increases by the admitted headcount on every employer admission and by every positive census delta, decreases by every negative census delta, and is *never* reduced by an exit. `transferred_out_participant_count` is the sum of `transferred_participant_count` over the plan's exit transfers in `SETTLED` status. `headcount_control_delta` is `participant_count` plus `transferred_out_participant_count` minus `cumulative_enrolled_participant_count`, and it too must be exactly zero after every trigger above.

Three derived gates ride on the same recomputation. `active_employer_count` counts the plan's employers whose `adoption_status` is `ACTIVE`, `EXIT_REQUESTED` or `SPINOFF_IN_PROGRESS`. `suspended_employer_count` counts those whose `adoption_status` is `SUSPENDED`. From that count the plan's `compliance_posture` follows: zero suspended employers means `OPEN`, exactly one means `RESTRICTED`, and two or more means `FROZEN`. Separately, `audit_required` on the plan document is true when the plan's `participant_count` is at least 1000 — the plan-level single-audit threshold — and false below it.

Compensating operations must return the ledger to where it was, with no drift. Reversing a posted deposit restores the pre-posting balances exactly; reversing a settled exit transfer restores the employer's balance and recomputes every plan control total, so both `headcount_control_delta` and `contribution_control_delta_cents` return to zero. The written-off portion of an exit deduction (`unrecovered_deduction_cents`) is never converted into a receivable, and so never appears in any control total.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties Across the Provider Layer

The provider layer carries four independence rules that no other part of the system enforces, and each is checked at attestation of the annual return. First, the firm named for attestation must be engaged on the plan and must have `provider_type` `AUDITOR_FIRM`; anything else is rejected with `ERR_NOT_AN_AUDITOR_FIRM`. Second, the named audit firm may hold no fiduciary role allocation on the plan in `ALLOCATED` status, and may not be linked by `affiliated_service_provider_id` in either direction — neither naming, nor being named by — a provider that holds one; a violation is rejected with `ERR_AUDITOR_NOT_INDEPENDENT`. Third, the audit firm may not be the same provider that prepared the return: when the named `audit_firm_service_provider_id` equals the filing's recorded `prepared_by_user_id`, the attestation is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Fourth, the roles themselves are disjoint at the capability level: only a `ServiceProvider` prepares the return, only an `IndependentAuditor` attests it, only a `PooledPlanProvider` certifies the employer-level data attached to it, and only `RegulatorOps` files it.

### 2.11. Rejection Precedence

When a request violates more than one rule, exactly one error is returned: the violation with the lowest priority number in the ladder below. The ladder is global and its ordering is observable — a request that is both mistyped and stale reports the shape defect, and a request that is both stale and blocked by a state precondition reports the stale version.

Priorities 1 through 4 are authentication and authorisation, in this order: `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS`, `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE`, `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`, `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. Priority 5 is path-identifier existence and priority 6 is body foreign-key existence, both as described in §2.8. Priority 7 is request shape — required fields present, correct types, declared enum members, declared integer ranges, ISO-8601 date and datetime formats, and pagination bounds — reported as `ERR_VALIDATION`; unknown fields in a body are ignored rather than rejected. Priorities 8 and 9 are the two optimistic-concurrency checks of §2.7: `ERR_STALE_EMPLOYER_VERSION` then `ERR_STALE_PLAN_VERSION`.

Priorities 10 through 23 are state preconditions, in this order: `ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_PENDING`, `ERR_PLAN_ALREADY_ACTIVE`, `ERR_PLAN_NOT_ACTIVE`, `ERR_ALLOCATION_ALREADY_ACKNOWLEDGED`, `ERR_ELECTION_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_EMPLOYER_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_BATCH_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_DEPOSIT_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_DEPOSIT_NOT_REVERSIBLE`, `ERR_CORRECTION_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_TEST_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_FILING_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_AUDIT_NOT_REQUIRED`, `ERR_TRANSFER_INVALID_STATE`.

Priorities 24 through 30 are the safety and regulatory gates, in this order: `ERR_FIDELITY_BOND_BELOW_FLOOR`, `ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_REGISTERED`, `ERR_NAMED_FIDUCIARY_NOT_ACKNOWLEDGED`, `ERR_TRUSTEE_NOT_ACKNOWLEDGED`, `ERR_PLAN_COMPLIANCE_FROZEN`, `ERR_PLAN_LEVEL_TEST_OUTSTANDING`, `ERR_AUDIT_OPINION_UNACCEPTABLE`.

Priorities 31 through 39 are cross-entity referential rules, in this order: `ERR_NOT_AN_AUDITOR_FIRM`, `ERR_AUDITOR_NOT_INDEPENDENT`, `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, `ERR_EMPLOYER_ELECTIONS_INCOMPLETE`, `ERR_TEST_SCOPE_MISMATCH`, `ERR_EMPLOYER_PLAN_MISMATCH`, `ERR_NOT_PLAN_RECORDKEEPER`, `ERR_INVALID_AFFILIATION`, `ERR_NO_BILLABLE_SERVICE_PROVIDER`.

Priorities 40 through 51 are the temporal rules, in this order: `ERR_INVALID_PAY_PERIOD`, `ERR_PAY_PERIOD_OVERLAP`, `ERR_WITHHOLDING_DATE_OUT_OF_PERIOD`, `ERR_DEPOSIT_DATE_OUT_OF_RANGE`, `ERR_INVALID_RECOVERY_DATE`, `ERR_INVALID_ALLOCATION_WINDOW`, `ERR_ALLOCATION_WINDOW_OVERLAP`, `ERR_INVALID_BLACKOUT_WINDOW`, `ERR_BLACKOUT_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, `ERR_ELECTION_WINDOW_CLOSED`, `ERR_BLACKOUT_NOT_ENDED`, `ERR_INVALID_DISCLOSURE_PERIOD`.

Priorities 52 through 65 are uniqueness and constraint-satisfaction rules, in this order: `ERR_DUPLICATE_PROVIDER_EIN`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_PLAN_NUMBER`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_EMPLOYER_EIN`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_PAYROLL_REFERENCE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_FILING_YEAR`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_COMPLIANCE_TEST`, `ERR_TIER_FIELDS_MISMATCH`, `ERR_TIER_LIMITS_OUT_OF_ORDER`, `ERR_SIGNATORY_REQUIRED`, `ERR_ELIGIBILITY_MORE_RESTRICTIVE`, `ERR_VESTING_MORE_RESTRICTIVE`, `ERR_CENSUS_INCONSISTENT`, `ERR_NO_APPROVED_ELECTION`, `ERR_MATCH_MISMATCH`.

Priorities 66 through 71 close the ladder with the fiscal checks, in this order: `ERR_DEPOSIT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`, `ERR_OPEN_LATE_DEPOSIT_CORRECTION`, `ERR_UNREMITTED_CONTRIBUTIONS`, `ERR_FIDELITY_BOND_INSUFFICIENT`, `ERR_FORM_5330_REFERENCE_REQUIRED`, `ERR_FILING_ALREADY_SUBMITTED`. Money and quota checks are deliberately last, so that a request which is structurally or procedurally wrong never reports a financial reason.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up the Plan: Provider Registration, the Plan Document and the Provider Layer

Nothing in this domain exists until a firm has been admitted to the pooled plan provider register. A `RegulatorOps` operator opens a provider record by supplying the firm's `legal_name` (between 1 and 200 characters), its `ein` (exactly nine digits, and unique across every provider record in the system — a second record carrying an `ein` that already exists is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PROVIDER_EIN`), and the `fidelity_bond_amount_cents` face amount of the ERISA section 412 fidelity bond the firm carries, which may be zero or any larger whole number of cents at this stage. The new record opens with `registration_status` `PENDING_REGISTRATION`, and `registration_number`, `registration_effective_date` and `registered_at` all null.

Granting the registration advances `registration_status` from `PENDING_REGISTRATION` to `REGISTERED`, and is the only transition the provider record ever makes; `REGISTERED` is terminal. The request supplies a single `registration_effective_date`, the calendar date on which the registration takes effect, and that date may not be earlier than the logical-clock calendar date — a backdated effective date is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION`. Two further conditions guard the grant. Attempting to grant a registration that has already been granted is rejected with `ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_PENDING`, and this state check is evaluated before the money check that follows it. The money check is the statutory floor: `fidelity_bond_amount_cents` must be at least 100000 cents — one thousand dollars of bond face amount — and a provider below that floor is rejected with `ERR_FIDELITY_BOND_BELOW_FLOOR`. On success the server mints `registration_number` in the form `PPP-NNNN`, where `NNNN` is the one-based ordinal of this approval among all approvals, zero-padded to four digits and assigned in ascending order of approval, so the first firm admitted carries `PPP-0001` and the tenth carries `PPP-0010`; it copies `registration_effective_date` from the request, stamps `registered_at` from the logical clock, and increments the provider's `version` by one.

A registered — or, at this stage, merely existing — provider authors the single plan document that every adopting employer will later adopt. The payload names the sponsoring provider through `pooled_plan_provider_id` and carries the `plan_name` (1 to 200 characters), the three-digit `plan_number` used on the annual return, which must be unique among the plans of that same sponsoring provider (a repeat is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PLAN_NUMBER`), and the `plan_year_end_date`, the last calendar date of the plan year. The remaining four inputs define the **common structure** — the outer envelope inside which every adopting employer must fit. `base_eligibility_min_age` is the most restrictive age condition an employer may impose and must be between 18 and 21 inclusive; `base_eligibility_service_months` is the most restrictive service condition in months and must be between 0 and 12 inclusive; `base_vesting_schedule` is the least generous vesting schedule permitted and is one of `IMMEDIATE`, `GRADED_2_6` or `CLIFF_3YR`; and `base_match_formula_code` is the plan's default employer match formula, one of `SAFE_HARBOR_BASIC`, `SAFE_HARBOR_ENHANCED`, `DISCRETIONARY_TIERED` or `NONE`. A freshly authored plan document opens in `status` `DRAFT` with `effective_date` null, every control total described in §2.9 initialised to zero, and `compliance_posture` `OPEN`.

Activation is a **guarded** transition whose entire gate lives on sibling entities rather than on the plan document itself, and it moves `status` from `DRAFT` to `ACTIVE`, which is terminal. Four conditions apply, evaluated in this order. A plan document that is not in `DRAFT` is rejected with `ERR_PLAN_ALREADY_ACTIVE`. Next, the sponsoring provider must have `registration_status` `REGISTERED`; a plan whose sponsor is still `PENDING_REGISTRATION` is rejected with `ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_REGISTERED`. Then the plan must have an `ALLOCATED` fiduciary role allocation for the `NAMED_FIDUCIARY_3_16` function whose `acknowledged_at` is set, failing which the request is rejected with `ERR_NAMED_FIDUCIARY_NOT_ACKNOWLEDGED`; and finally it must have an `ALLOCATED` allocation for the `TRUSTEE_403A` function whose `acknowledged_at` is set, failing which it is rejected with `ERR_TRUSTEE_NOT_ACKNOWLEDGED`. An allocation that exists but has never been acknowledged does not satisfy either gate. On success the plan's `effective_date` is set to the logical-clock calendar date and its `version` is incremented by one.

Engaging a service provider on the plan records both sides of that firm's compensation. The payload carries `plan_document_id`, the firm's `legal_name` (1 to 200 characters), its `provider_type` — one of `RECORDKEEPER`, `TPA`, `TRUSTEE`, `INVESTMENT_MANAGER` or `AUDITOR_FIRM` — its `revenue_sharing_bps`, the indirect compensation it receives from plan investments expressed in basis points between 0 and 500 inclusive, and its `direct_fee_cents_per_participant`, the direct annual per-participant fee billed to the plan, zero or greater. An optional `affiliated_service_provider_id` links the firm to a commonly controlled provider on the same plan; when supplied it must reference a service provider engaged on that same plan and must not be the new row's own identifier, and a violation of either condition is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_AFFILIATION`. Newly engaged providers open with `holds_fiduciary_role` false.

Allocating a statutory fiduciary function is where the segregation-of-duties graph acquires its edges. The payload names the plan through `plan_document_id`, the accepting firm through `service_provider_id`, the `fiduciary_role` being allocated — one of `NAMED_FIDUCIARY_3_16`, `INVESTMENT_MANAGER_3_38`, `TRUSTEE_403A` or `NON_FIDUCIARY_MINISTERIAL` — and a closed effective window bounded by `effective_from`, the first calendar date the firm holds the function, and `effective_to`, the last. The window must be well formed: an `effective_to` earlier than `effective_from` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_ALLOCATION_WINDOW`. It must also not collide with the window of the incumbent `ALLOCATED` allocation for the same plan and the same `fiduciary_role`: touching at a single day counts as a collision, so an `effective_from` equal to the incumbent's `effective_to` is rejected with `ERR_ALLOCATION_WINDOW_OVERLAP` while an `effective_from` one day later is accepted. The stale-plan-version guard of §2.7 also applies here. A new allocation opens with `status` `ALLOCATED`, `acknowledged_at`, `acknowledgment_statement`, `revoked_at` and `revoked_reason` all null, and it supersedes the incumbent: when the new allocation's `effective_from` is strictly later than the incumbent's `effective_from`, the incumbent moves from `ALLOCATED` to `REVOKED`, has `revoked_at` stamped from the logical clock and `revoked_reason` set to `SUPERSEDED_BY_NEW_ALLOCATION`, and has its own `version` incremented; the outgoing firm's `holds_fiduciary_role` is then recomputed, as is the incoming firm's, and the plan document's `version` is incremented by one. A firm's `holds_fiduciary_role` is true exactly when at least one allocation naming it is in `ALLOCATED` status.

Acknowledgment is not a separate status but a required attribute of an allocated role: it is a self-transition from `ALLOCATED` to `ALLOCATED`. Only the firm the allocation names may acknowledge, and a `ServiceProvider` caller whose `X-User-ID` differs from the allocation's `service_provider_id` is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. The request supplies the verbatim `acknowledgment_statement` text; the server stamps `acknowledged_at` from the logical clock, stores the statement, and increments the allocation's `version` by one. Acknowledging twice, or acknowledging an allocation that has already been revoked, is rejected with `ERR_ALLOCATION_ALREADY_ACKNOWLEDGED`.

### 3.2. Admitting Adopting Employers and Maintaining Census Data

Once the plan document is `ACTIVE`, the pooled plan provider onboards adopting employers; onboarding against a plan still in `DRAFT` is rejected with `ERR_PLAN_NOT_ACTIVE`. Each employer arrives with `plan_document_id`, its `legal_name` (1 to 200 characters), its `ein` (exactly nine digits, unique among the employers of that same plan — a duplicate is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_EMPLOYER_EIN`), and an `election_window_close_date`, the last calendar date on which that employer may submit a plan design election. It also arrives with an opening census: `participant_count` (zero or greater), `hce_count`, the count of highly compensated employees (zero or greater), `nhce_count`, the count of non-highly compensated employees (at least one), `benefiting_hce_count` and `benefiting_nhce_count`, the two counts benefiting under the plan for coverage-testing purposes, and the two deferral averages `hce_avg_deferral_pct` and `nhce_avg_deferral_pct`, each a number between 0.00 and 25.00 carried to two decimal places.

Census arithmetic is checked as a unit: `benefiting_hce_count` may not exceed `hce_count`, `benefiting_nhce_count` may not exceed `nhce_count`, `nhce_count` must be at least one, and `hce_count` plus `nhce_count` must equal `participant_count` exactly. Any of those four failures is reported as `ERR_CENSUS_INCONSISTENT`.

A newly admitted employer opens in `adoption_status` `APPLIED` with `effective_adoption_date` and `exited_at` null, `account_balance_cents`, `contribution_receivable_cents`, `revenue_sharing_credit_cents` and `open_correction_count` all zero, and `contribution_hold` false. Two flags are derived from the census immediately: `is_small_plan` is true when the employer's `participant_count` is below 100, which is what selects the seven-business-day deposit safe harbour for that employer's payroll, and `employer_audit_required` is true when `participant_count` is 100 or more, meaning the employer must supply audited employer-level financial data for the plan's return. Admission also moves the plan: the plan document's `participant_count` and its `cumulative_enrolled_participant_count` both increase by the admitted headcount, its `audit_required` flag and `headcount_control_delta` are recomputed, and its `version` is incremented by one.

Census data is restated over the life of the adoption, and either the employer itself or the pooled plan provider may restate it. A restatement carries `participant_count`, `hce_count`, `nhce_count`, `benefiting_hce_count` and `benefiting_nhce_count`, and may optionally carry `hce_avg_deferral_pct` and `nhce_avg_deferral_pct`; omitting the two averages leaves the stored values untouched. The same four-part census arithmetic applies, again reported as `ERR_CENSUS_INCONSISTENT`. Restatement is only accepted while the employer's `adoption_status` is `ACTIVE`; from any other status it is rejected with `ERR_EMPLOYER_INVALID_STATE`. On success `is_small_plan` and `employer_audit_required` are recomputed — so a growing employer can cross the 100-participant line and lose the safe harbour for *future* batches — the plan document's `participant_count` and `cumulative_enrolled_participant_count` both move by the signed delta so that `headcount_control_delta` stays at zero, the plan's `audit_required` is recomputed, and the employer's `version` is incremented by one.

### 3.3. Plan Design Elections Inside the Common Structure

An adopting employer joins the plan by electing its own terms inside the common structure, and the review of those elections is deliberately cyclic: an employer may be sent back for revision and resubmit without bound. The employer's `adoption_status` tracks that review from the outside while the election's own `status` tracks it from the inside.

Creating an election is done by the employer itself and is accepted only while its `adoption_status` is `APPLIED`, `ELECTIONS_SUBMITTED` or `RETURNED_FOR_REVISION`; from `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED`, `EXIT_REQUESTED`, `SPINOFF_IN_PROGRESS` or `EXITED` it is rejected with `ERR_EMPLOYER_INVALID_STATE`. That restriction is what makes an already-`APPROVED` election permanent: an active employer cannot create a replacement. The payload names the employer through `adopting_employer_id`, carries `eligibility_min_age` (18 to 21 inclusive) and `eligibility_service_months` (0 to 12 inclusive), a `vesting_schedule` from `IMMEDIATE`, `GRADED_2_6` or `CLIFF_3YR`, a `match_formula_code` from `SAFE_HARBOR_BASIC`, `SAFE_HARBOR_ENHANCED`, `DISCRETIONARY_TIERED` or `NONE`, and the stale-employer-version guard `expected_adopting_employer_version` described in §2.7.

The four tier fields are conditional on the formula code. When `match_formula_code` is `DISCRETIONARY_TIERED`, all four of `match_tier_1_pct`, `match_tier_1_limit_pct`, `match_tier_2_pct` and `match_tier_2_limit_pct` must be present; for any other formula code all four must be absent or null. Either half of that rule failing — a tiered election missing a tier field, or a non-tiered election supplying one — is rejected with `ERR_TIER_FIELDS_MISMATCH`. The two rate fields `match_tier_1_pct` and `match_tier_2_pct` are percentages between 0.00 and 100.00; the two limit fields `match_tier_1_limit_pct` and `match_tier_2_limit_pct` are percentages of eligible compensation between 0.00 and 15.00, and the second tier's limit must be greater than or equal to the first tier's limit, failing which the request is rejected with `ERR_TIER_LIMITS_OUT_OF_ORDER`.

On creation the server assigns `election_sequence`, the one-based ordinal of this election among the same employer's elections in ascending `created_at` order, and derives `nondiscrimination_track`: it is `SAFE_HARBOR_EXEMPT` when `match_formula_code` is `SAFE_HARBOR_BASIC` or `SAFE_HARBOR_ENHANCED`, and `ADP_ACP_TESTED` for `DISCRETIONARY_TIERED` and `NONE`. The election opens in `status` `DRAFT` with `signatory_name`, `return_reason`, `deficiency_code` and `approved_at` null. Creation is also a **reset**: any prior election of the same employer whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `RETURNED` moves to `SUPERSEDED` and has its `version` incremented, and if the employer was sitting in `ELECTIONS_SUBMITTED` or `RETURNED_FOR_REVISION` it returns to `APPLIED`, because the in-flight election that put it there no longer exists. Either way the employer's `version` is incremented by one. Because a replacement is a fresh row, `election_sequence` advances on replacement but never on resubmission of the same row.

Submitting an election moves it from `DRAFT` to `SUBMITTED`, or from `RETURNED` back to `SUBMITTED`, and drags the employer to `ELECTIONS_SUBMITTED` in either case — from `APPLIED` on a first submission and from `RETURNED_FOR_REVISION` on a resubmission, which is the cycle edge `ELECTIONS_SUBMITTED → RETURNED_FOR_REVISION → ELECTIONS_SUBMITTED` that may repeat indefinitely. Submission from any status other than `DRAFT` or `RETURNED` is rejected with `ERR_ELECTION_INVALID_STATE`. It must land inside the employer's election window: the logical-clock calendar date must be on or before the employer's `election_window_close_date`, and a submission on a later date is rejected with `ERR_ELECTION_WINDOW_CLOSED`. The payload carries `signatory_name`, the name of the employer signatory, which is required and may not be blank; its absence is rejected with `ERR_SIGNATORY_REQUIRED`. A `DISCRETIONARY_TIERED` election must still carry all four tier values at submission time. On success `signatory_name` is stored, any `return_reason` and `deficiency_code` left over from a previous return are cleared, and both the election's `version` and the employer's `version` are incremented by one.

Returning a submitted election is the pooled plan provider's revision request. It is accepted only from `SUBMITTED` — from anywhere else it is rejected with `ERR_ELECTION_INVALID_STATE` — and moves the election to `RETURNED` while moving the employer to `RETURNED_FOR_REVISION`. The payload carries free-text `return_reason` and a structured `deficiency_code`, one of `MISSING_SIGNATORY`, `TIER_MISMATCH`, `ELIGIBILITY_TOO_RESTRICTIVE` or `VESTING_TOO_RESTRICTIVE`. Both values are stored on the election, and both the election's and the employer's `version` are incremented by one.

Approval is where the common structure is actually enforced, and it carries no payload at all. Four checks apply, in this order. The plan document must be `ACTIVE`, failing which the request is rejected with `ERR_PLAN_NOT_ACTIVE`. The election must be in `SUBMITTED`, failing which it is rejected with `ERR_ELECTION_INVALID_STATE`. Then the employer's eligibility terms must be no more restrictive than the common structure: `eligibility_min_age` must be at most the plan document's `base_eligibility_min_age` and `eligibility_service_months` at most its `base_eligibility_service_months`, so an employer may always be *more* generous than the plan but never stricter; a violation of either is rejected with `ERR_ELIGIBILITY_MORE_RESTRICTIVE`. Finally the elected vesting schedule must be at least as generous as the plan's base schedule, measured on a generosity rank where `IMMEDIATE` is 0, `GRADED_2_6` is 1 and `CLIFF_3YR` is 2: the rank of the election's `vesting_schedule` must be at most the rank of the plan's `base_vesting_schedule`, and a higher rank is rejected with `ERR_VESTING_MORE_RESTRICTIVE`. On success the election moves from `SUBMITTED` to `APPROVED` with `approved_at` stamped from the logical clock, the employer advances from `ELECTIONS_SUBMITTED` to `ACTIVE` with `effective_adoption_date` set to the logical-clock calendar date, the plan document's `active_employer_count` is recomputed, and both the election's and the employer's `version` are incremented by one.

Exactly one election per employer may be in `APPROVED` status at any time, and `SUPERSEDED`, `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` and `RETURNED` elections are never consulted by any downstream calculation. The only edge out of `APPROVED` is `APPROVED → SUPERSEDED`, driven by the settlement of that employer's exit; a new election can never displace an approved one, because an `ACTIVE` employer may not create one.

### 3.4. Payroll Delivery, Match Reconciliation and the Deposit Deadline

Payroll flows into the plan one period at a time, delivered by the employer from its own payroll system. Delivery is accepted only while the employer's `adoption_status` is `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_EMPLOYER_INVALID_STATE` — and only when the employer holds a `PlanDesignElection` in `APPROVED` status, because that election is what makes an expected match computable; an employer without one is rejected with `ERR_NO_APPROVED_ELECTION`. The payload names the employer through `adopting_employer_id` and describes the period with `pay_period_start`, `pay_period_end`, `pay_date`, `withholding_date` and a `payroll_provider_reference` of 1 to 64 characters issued by the employer's payroll system, which must be unique among that employer's batches — a repeat is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_PAYROLL_REFERENCE`. It carries the period's money as `total_eligible_compensation_cents`, `total_deferral_cents`, `total_match_cents` and `total_loan_repayment_cents`, each zero or greater, together with `participant_line_count`, the number of participant lines in the file, which must be at least one.

The period is treated as a half-open interval that includes its start date and excludes its end date. `pay_period_end` must be strictly after `pay_period_start`, and `pay_date` must be on or after `pay_period_start`; either failure is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAY_PERIOD`. The interval also may not overlap the interval of any other payroll batch of the same employer, and because the interval is half-open, exact adjacency is permitted: a batch ending on 2026-06-15 and the next starting on 2026-06-15 do not overlap, whereas any genuine overlap is rejected with `ERR_PAY_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. The `withholding_date`, the date elective deferrals were actually withheld from wages, must be on or after `pay_period_start` and no later than 31 calendar days after `pay_period_end`; outside that range it is rejected with `ERR_WITHHOLDING_DATE_OUT_OF_PERIOD`.

Three values are computed at delivery and stored on the batch. `batch_total_cents` is simply `total_deferral_cents` plus `total_match_cents` plus `total_loan_repayment_cents` — the whole amount the employer owes the trust for the period. `expected_match_cents` is the match the employer's own elected formula produces for this period's compensation and deferrals, described below. And the deposit deadline is fixed: `deposit_rule` is `SMALL_PLAN_7_BUSINESS_DAY` when the employer's `is_small_plan` flag was true at the moment the batch was created and `GENERAL_15TH_BUSINESS_DAY` otherwise, and `deposit_due_date` follows from it. Under the safe harbour the due date is the seventh business day after the `withholding_date`, counting forward from the day after withholding and skipping weekends and the federal holidays listed in §2.5; for a `withholding_date` of 2026-08-03 that lands on 2026-08-12. Under the general rule the due date is the fifteenth business day of the calendar month immediately following the month of the `withholding_date`; for a `withholding_date` of 2026-06-15 that lands on 2026-07-22. The batch's `deposit_rule` and `deposit_due_date` are frozen at creation and are never recomputed, so a census restatement that later flips the employer's `is_small_plan` flag does not move the deadline of a batch already delivered. A new batch opens in `status` `RECEIVED`, with `validated_at` and `reconciliation_note` null.

**Computing the expected match.** The formula reads the employer's `APPROVED` election, the batch's `total_eligible_compensation_cents` (call it the compensation) and its `total_deferral_cents` (call it the deferrals). Each branch produces a raw match, and the evaluation order is pinned: each tier is rounded half up to whole cents first, the rounded tiers are then summed, and only then is the plan-wide cap applied.

- For `SAFE_HARBOR_BASIC` the raw match is the sum of two tiers. The first tier is the lesser of the deferrals and three percent of compensation, rounded half up to whole cents. The second tier is one half of the amount by which the lesser of the deferrals and five percent of compensation exceeds three percent of compensation — and if that excess is negative it is treated as zero — again rounded half up to whole cents.
- For `SAFE_HARBOR_ENHANCED` the raw match is the lesser of the deferrals and four percent of compensation, rounded half up to whole cents; there is only one tier.
- For `DISCRETIONARY_TIERED` the raw match is again the sum of two tiers, this time built from the election's own rates and limits. The first tier is `match_tier_1_pct` percent of the lesser of the deferrals and `match_tier_1_limit_pct` percent of compensation, rounded half up to whole cents. The second tier is `match_tier_2_pct` percent of the amount by which the lesser of the deferrals and `match_tier_2_limit_pct` percent of compensation exceeds `match_tier_1_limit_pct` percent of compensation — treated as zero if that excess is negative — rounded half up to whole cents.
- For `NONE` the raw match is zero.

The plan-wide cap is six percent of compensation, rounded half up to whole cents, and `expected_match_cents` is the lesser of the raw match and that cap. Consider a period with compensation of 100000000 cents and deferrals of 4500000 cents: `SAFE_HARBOR_BASIC` yields 3000000 plus 750000, for 3750000; `SAFE_HARBOR_ENHANCED` yields 4000000; a `DISCRETIONARY_TIERED` election at one hundred percent up to a 3.0 percent limit and twenty-five percent up to a 6.0 percent limit yields 3000000 plus 375000, for 3375000; and `NONE` yields zero. Note that `expected_match_cents` is computed and returned on the delivered batch even when it disagrees with the employer's submitted `total_match_cents` — the disagreement is not a delivery failure.

**Reconciliation.** The plan's recordkeeper reconciles the delivered batch, moving it from `RECEIVED` to `VALIDATED`. Three checks apply in order. The batch must be in `RECEIVED`; a batch already `VALIDATED` or `REMITTED` is rejected with `ERR_BATCH_INVALID_STATE`. The acting service provider must be engaged on the batch's plan with `provider_type` `RECORDKEEPER`; any other firm, including a `TPA` or `TRUSTEE` on the same plan, is rejected with `ERR_NOT_PLAN_RECORDKEEPER`. And the employer's submitted `total_match_cents` must equal the computed `expected_match_cents` exactly; a discrepancy is rejected with `ERR_MATCH_MISMATCH`. The request carries a `reconciliation_note` from the recordkeeper. On success `validated_at` is stamped from the logical clock, the note is stored, the employer's `contribution_receivable_cents` is recomputed as the sum of `batch_total_cents` over that employer's batches still in `VALIDATED` status, the plan document's `total_validated_contributions_cents`, `aggregate_receivable_cents` and `contribution_control_delta_cents` are recomputed, and the batch's `version` is incremented by one.

### 3.5. Remittance Settlement, Timeliness and Late-Deposit Correction

A remittance deposit carries **two independent state dimensions**. `deposit_status` is the settlement dimension, advanced only by the recordkeeper, and runs `PENDING → POSTED → REVERSED` with `REVERSED` terminal. `timeliness_status` is the compliance dimension, evaluated at posting and afterwards advanced by the correction workflow, and runs across `UNDETERMINED`, `TIMELY`, `LATE`, `CORRECTION_COMPUTED` and `CORRECTED`, with `CORRECTED` terminal. The two dimensions move on different triggers and are driven by different actors, so a deposit can legitimately be `POSTED` and `LATE`, `POSTED` and `CORRECTED`, or `REVERSED` and `UNDETERMINED`. Neither dimension constrains the other's vocabulary.

The employer records the remittance itself. The payload names the batch through `payroll_batch_id`, states the `deposit_date` on which the money reached the trust, the `amount_cents` remitted (at least one cent), and a wire or ACH `deposit_reference` of 1 to 64 characters. Three checks apply in order: the named batch must be in `VALIDATED` status, and a batch still `RECEIVED` or already `REMITTED` is rejected with `ERR_BATCH_INVALID_STATE`; the `deposit_date` must not be later than the logical-clock calendar date and must be on or after the batch's `withholding_date`, failing which the request is rejected with `ERR_DEPOSIT_DATE_OUT_OF_RANGE`; and `amount_cents` must equal the batch's `batch_total_cents` exactly, since partial remittances are not accepted and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_DEPOSIT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`. A new deposit opens with `deposit_status` `PENDING` and `timeliness_status` `UNDETERMINED`, with `business_days_elapsed`, `late_by_business_days`, `posted_at`, `posting_reference`, `correction_method`, `recovery_date`, `lost_earnings_cents`, `restoration_cents`, `excise_tax_cents`, `form_5330_reference`, `funded_at` and `reversal_reason` all null, and `actual_investment_return_cents` at zero. Recording the deposit changes nothing else in the plan.

**Posting.** The recordkeeper posts the deposit to the trust, and timeliness is judged in the very same request. The batch-state check comes first, then the deposit must be in `PENDING` — a deposit already `POSTED` or `REVERSED` is rejected with `ERR_DEPOSIT_INVALID_STATE` — and then the acting firm must be the plan's `RECORDKEEPER`, failing which the request is rejected with `ERR_NOT_PLAN_RECORDKEEPER`. The request carries the trust `posting_reference`. On success `deposit_status` moves from `PENDING` to `POSTED`, `posted_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and the reference is stored. Two day counts are then computed. `business_days_elapsed` is the number of business days strictly after the batch's `withholding_date` through the `deposit_date` inclusive — the withholding date itself never counts and the deposit date counts whenever it is a business day. `late_by_business_days` is the number of business days strictly after the batch's `deposit_due_date` through the `deposit_date` inclusive, and it is zero whenever the deposit is timely. The compliance dimension then advances from `UNDETERMINED` to `TIMELY` when the `deposit_date` is on or before the batch's `deposit_due_date`, and from `UNDETERMINED` to `LATE` when it is strictly after it. Independently of the timeliness verdict, the batch advances from `VALIDATED` to `REMITTED`, the employer's `contribution_receivable_cents` and `account_balance_cents` are both recomputed — the amount leaves the receivable and enters the balance — and the plan document's `aggregate_receivable_cents`, `total_assets_cents` and `contribution_control_delta_cents` are recomputed. Both the deposit's and the batch's `version` are incremented by one.

**The late-deposit cascade.** A `LATE` verdict is the deepest cascade in the system and every level of it fires in the posting request. At the first level the deposit's `late_by_business_days` is set from the business-day count above. At the second level the employer is suspended: its `adoption_status` moves from `ACTIVE` to `SUSPENDED`, its `contribution_hold` is set to true, its `open_correction_count` is incremented, and its `version` is incremented by one. `contribution_hold` is true exactly when the employer has at least one deposit that is `POSTED` with a `timeliness_status` of either `LATE` or `CORRECTION_COMPUTED`, and `open_correction_count` is the count of such deposits. At the third level the plan document's `suspended_employer_count` is recomputed and `compliance_posture` follows from it — one suspended employer makes the plan `RESTRICTED`, a second makes it `FROZEN` — along with `active_employer_count`. At the fourth level the plan's annual return is touched: every plan filing that is not yet `FILED` has the late deposit's `amount_cents` added to its `late_deposit_schedule_h_line_cents`, the delinquent-participant-contributions line; and a filing sitting in `READY_TO_FILE` has its employer-data branch revoked, meaning `employer_data_certified_at`, `certified_by_user_id` and `certified_employer_count` are all cleared and its `version` is incremented. Where that revoked filing lands depends on its own `audit_required`: a filing with `audit_required` false falls all the way back to `PREPARED`, since the employer-data branch was the only branch it had, while a filing with `audit_required` true falls back only to `PARTIALLY_CLEARED`, because its audit branch stays cleared. A compliance event on a grandchild of the plan thereby unwinds a completed branch of the plan's return.

**Reversal.** A posted deposit can be unwound, but only before the correction workflow has touched it. The deposit must be `POSTED` — otherwise `ERR_DEPOSIT_INVALID_STATE` — and its `timeliness_status` must be `TIMELY`, `LATE` or `UNDETERMINED`; once a correction has been computed or funded the posting is frozen and reversal is rejected with `ERR_DEPOSIT_NOT_REVERSIBLE`. The acting firm must again be the plan's `RECORDKEEPER`. The request carries a `reversal_reason`, which is stored. On success `deposit_status` moves from `POSTED` to `REVERSED` while `timeliness_status` returns from `TIMELY` or from `LATE` to `UNDETERMINED`, and `business_days_elapsed` and `late_by_business_days` are cleared. The batch reverts from `REMITTED` to `VALIDATED`, the employer's `contribution_receivable_cents` is restored and its `account_balance_cents` is reduced by the reversed amount, and every plan control total is recomputed so that `contribution_control_delta_cents` returns to exactly zero. If the reversed deposit had been `LATE`, the suspension it caused is unwound too: the employer's `open_correction_count` is decremented and, when it reaches zero, the employer returns from `SUSPENDED` to `ACTIVE` with `contribution_hold` cleared, after which the plan's `suspended_employer_count` and `compliance_posture` are recomputed. Both the deposit's and the batch's `version` are incremented by one.

**Computing the correction.** For a deposit that is `POSTED` and `LATE`, the recordkeeper computes what the employer owes the plan. The deposit must be `POSTED`, its `timeliness_status` must be `LATE` — a deposit that is `TIMELY`, already `CORRECTION_COMPUTED` or `CORRECTED` is rejected with `ERR_CORRECTION_INVALID_STATE` — and the acting firm must be the plan's `RECORDKEEPER`. The payload chooses a `correction_method`, either `VFCP_RECOVERY_DATE` or `IRS_6621_UNDERPAYMENT`, supplies a `recovery_date`, and supplies `actual_investment_return_cents`, the actual investment return the plan would have earned on the money, used as a floor. The `recovery_date` must be on or after the deposit's `deposit_date` and must not be later than the logical-clock calendar date; outside that range it is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_RECOVERY_DATE`. On success `timeliness_status` advances from `LATE` to `CORRECTION_COMPUTED` and `lost_earnings_cents`, `restoration_cents` and `excise_tax_cents` are all set.

Both variants of the lost-earnings computation treat the deposit's `amount_cents` as the delinquent principal, apply the IRS section 6621 underpayment rate as an annual percentage on a 365-day basis, and start the interest period from the day after the batch's `deposit_due_date`. The quarterly annual rates are 7.0 percent for each of the four quarters of 2025 and for the first quarter of 2026, 8.0 percent for the second quarter of 2026, and 9.0 percent for the third and fourth quarters of 2026 and for all four quarters of 2027.

- Under `IRS_6621_UNDERPAYMENT` the employer self-corrects outside the DOL programme and owes interest only for the delinquency period. The period ends at the `deposit_date`, and the number of days is the plain calendar-day difference between the `deposit_date` and the `deposit_due_date`. A single rate applies to the whole period — the rate of the calendar quarter that contains the `deposit_date` — with no segmentation at all. `lost_earnings_cents` is the principal multiplied by that annual rate expressed as a fraction of one hundred, multiplied by the day count, divided by 365, and rounded half up to whole cents. The `recovery_date` is stored but never enters the arithmetic under this variant.
- Under `VFCP_RECOVERY_DATE` the employer corrects under the Voluntary Fiduciary Correction Program and must make the plan whole through the `recovery_date`, which may fall much later than the `deposit_date`. The interest period runs from the day after the `deposit_due_date` through the `recovery_date`, inclusive at both ends. That period is split into calendar-quarter segments in chronological order. For each segment the server counts the dates the segment contains, multiplies the principal by that quarter's own annual rate expressed as a fraction of one hundred, multiplies by the segment's day count, divides by 365, and rounds half up to whole cents. `lost_earnings_cents` is the sum of those per-segment amounts. Each segment is rounded before summation, so rounding deliberately accumulates across quarter boundaries and a single unsegmented computation over the same period gives a different answer.

The two variants diverge sharply on identical inputs. For a principal of 8500000 cents, a `deposit_due_date` of 2026-06-25, a `deposit_date` of 2026-07-20 and a `recovery_date` of 2026-08-31, `VFCP_RECOVERY_DATE` yields 139260 while `IRS_6621_UNDERPAYMENT` yields 52397.

`restoration_cents` — the amount the employer must actually restore — is the greater of `lost_earnings_cents` and the supplied `actual_investment_return_cents`. The excise tax follows immediately from it: if `restoration_cents` is strictly less than 10000 cents, `excise_tax_cents` is zero; otherwise `excise_tax_cents` is fifteen percent of `restoration_cents`, rounded half up to whole cents. The de-minimis test is strictly less-than, so a `restoration_cents` of exactly 10000 is taxable at 1500 while 9999 attracts no tax at all.

**Funding the correction.** The employer, not the recordkeeper, funds the restoration. The deposit must be `POSTED` and its `timeliness_status` must be `CORRECTION_COMPUTED`, failing which the request is rejected with `ERR_CORRECTION_INVALID_STATE`. The payload may carry `form_5330_reference`, the excise tax return reference; it is required whenever the deposit's `excise_tax_cents` is greater than zero, and its absence in that case is rejected with `ERR_FORM_5330_REFERENCE_REQUIRED`. On success `timeliness_status` advances from `CORRECTION_COMPUTED` to `CORRECTED`, `funded_at` is stamped from the logical clock and the reference is stored; `restoration_cents` is added to the employer's `account_balance_cents`; the employer's `open_correction_count` is decremented and, when it reaches zero — that is, when no other deposit of that employer is `POSTED` with `timeliness_status` `LATE` or `CORRECTION_COMPUTED` — the employer returns from `SUSPENDED` to `ACTIVE` with `contribution_hold` set to false; and the plan document's `total_corrections_funded_cents`, `total_assets_cents`, `suspended_employer_count`, `compliance_posture` and `contribution_control_delta_cents` are all recomputed. The deposit's `version` is incremented by one.

### 3.6. Coverage and Nondiscrimination Testing Across Adopting Employers

A pooled plan is tested both as one plan and, for some purposes, employer by employer, and the same census data can pass one way and fail the other. Scheduling a test is the pooled plan provider's act. The payload names the plan through `plan_document_id`, chooses a `test_type` from `COVERAGE_410B_RATIO`, `ADP` or `ACP`, chooses a `scope` of either `PLAN_LEVEL` or `EMPLOYER_LEVEL`, states the `plan_year` under test as an integer between 2020 and 2030 inclusive, and — only for an employer-level test — names the employer through `adopting_employer_id`.

Five conditions guard scheduling, in this order. The plan document must be `ACTIVE`, failing which the request is rejected with `ERR_PLAN_NOT_ACTIVE`. The scope and the employer reference must agree: `adopting_employer_id` must be present when `scope` is `EMPLOYER_LEVEL` and must be null when `scope` is `PLAN_LEVEL`, and either mismatch is rejected with `ERR_TEST_SCOPE_MISMATCH`. A named employer must belong to the named plan, failing which the request is rejected with `ERR_EMPLOYER_PLAN_MISMATCH`. At most one test that is not `CLOSED` may exist for a given combination of plan, `test_type`, `scope`, `plan_year` and named employer; a second is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_COMPLIANCE_TEST`. Finally, an `EMPLOYER_LEVEL` test requires that the named employer hold an `APPROVED` election, because otherwise its nondiscrimination track is undefined; an employer still in `APPLIED`, `ELECTIONS_SUBMITTED` or `RETURNED_FOR_REVISION` is rejected with `ERR_NO_APPROVED_ELECTION`. A scheduled test opens in `status` `SCHEDULED` with `run_at`, `failure_reason`, `closed_reason` and every computed population count and percentage null, and `exemption_applied` false.

Running the test is a service provider's act and takes no payload. The plan document must be `ACTIVE`, failing which the run is rejected with `ERR_PLAN_NOT_ACTIVE`; the test must still be `SCHEDULED`, and a test already `PASSED`, `FAILED` or `CLOSED` is rejected with `ERR_TEST_INVALID_STATE`. The tested population must contain at least one employer whose `adoption_status` is `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`; a test with an empty population cannot produce a verdict and is likewise refused with `ERR_TEST_INVALID_STATE`.

The population itself is defined identically for every test type and depends only on `scope`. Under `PLAN_LEVEL` the pooled plan is treated as a single plan and the population is every employer of the plan whose `adoption_status` is `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`. Under `EMPLOYER_LEVEL` the population is the single employer named by `adopting_employer_id`, tested as though it maintained its own plan. Employers in `APPLIED`, `ELECTIONS_SUBMITTED`, `RETURNED_FOR_REVISION` and `EXITED` are excluded from every population, at either scope. Running the test records `run_at` from the logical clock, sets `population_hce_count` to the sum of `hce_count` over the population, `population_nhce_count` to the sum of `nhce_count`, and the test's own `benefiting_hce_count` and `benefiting_nhce_count` to the sums of the employers' `benefiting_hce_count` and `benefiting_nhce_count` respectively, and increments the test's `version` by one.

**Coverage ratio testing.** For `COVERAGE_410B_RATIO` the server first computes `hce_benefiting_pct` as the aggregated benefiting HCE count multiplied by one hundred and divided by the aggregated HCE population, rounded half up to two decimal places, and `nhce_benefiting_pct` as the aggregated benefiting NHCE count multiplied by one hundred and divided by the aggregated NHCE population, again rounded half up to two decimal places. `ratio_pct` is then `nhce_benefiting_pct` multiplied by one hundred and divided by `hce_benefiting_pct`, rounded half up to two decimal places. The order matters and is pinned: each of the two benefiting percentages is rounded to two decimals *before* the ratio is taken, and taking the ratio of the unrounded percentages produces a different final hundredth of a percent. The verdict is `PASSED` when `ratio_pct` is at least 70.00 and `FAILED` when it is below 70.00, in which case `failure_reason` is set to `RATIO_BELOW_THRESHOLD`. The scope difference is observable: two employers with benefiting-over-population HCE and NHCE figures of ten out of ten and ninety out of one hundred, and ten out of ten and twelve out of twenty, aggregate to a `ratio_pct` of 85.00 and pass at `PLAN_LEVEL`, while the second employer tested alone yields 60.00 and fails at `EMPLOYER_LEVEL`. `hce_benefiting_pct`, `nhce_benefiting_pct` and `ratio_pct` are populated only for a coverage test that has been run, and remain null on `ADP` and `ACP` tests.

**Deferral-percentage testing.** For `ADP` and `ACP` the server first checks the safe-harbour exemption. `exemption_applied` is true when every employer in the tested population holds an `APPROVED` election whose `nondiscrimination_track` is `SAFE_HARBOR_EXEMPT`; when it is true the test passes without computing any averages, and `hce_avg_deferral_pct`, `nhce_avg_deferral_pct` and `adp_limit_pct` are all left null. Otherwise the server computes headcount-weighted averages over the population: `nhce_avg_deferral_pct` is the sum, over the population, of each employer's own `nhce_avg_deferral_pct` multiplied by its `nhce_count`, divided by the sum of those `nhce_count` values, rounded half up to two decimal places; `hce_avg_deferral_pct` is computed the same way from each employer's `hce_avg_deferral_pct` weighted by its `hce_count`. Because every employer's census must carry at least one non-highly compensated employee, the NHCE denominator can never be zero.

The permitted HCE average is a three-branch piecewise function of the weighted NHCE average. When the NHCE average is at most 2.00 percent, `adp_limit_pct` is that average multiplied by two. When the NHCE average is above 2.00 and at most 8.00 percent, `adp_limit_pct` is that average plus two percentage points. When the NHCE average is above 8.00 percent, `adp_limit_pct` is that average multiplied by 1.25. In every branch the result is rounded half up to two decimal places. Branch selection is observable even though the function is continuous at neither published boundary in the obvious way: an NHCE average of 2.50 gives 4.50 by the addend branch, not 5.00, and an NHCE average of 8.50 gives 10.63 by the 1.25 multiplier branch, not 10.50. The verdict is `PASSED` when `hce_avg_deferral_pct` is at most `adp_limit_pct` — the comparison is inclusive, so an HCE average exactly equal to the limit passes — and `FAILED` when it exceeds the limit, in which case `failure_reason` is set to `HCE_AVERAGE_EXCEEDS_LIMIT`.

Tests do not survive the departure of the employer they measure. When an employer's exit settles, every `EMPLOYER_LEVEL` test of the plan naming that employer moves to `CLOSED` with `closed_reason` set to `EMPLOYER_EXITED` and its `version` incremented, and this happens from every non-closed status alike: `SCHEDULED → CLOSED`, `PASSED → CLOSED` and `FAILED → CLOSED` are all driven by the same settlement event with the same effects. `PLAN_LEVEL` tests are never closed by an individual employer's exit, whatever their status.

### 3.7. The Plan-Level Annual Return

One annual return covers the whole pooled plan but reports employer-level data inside it, and clearing it is a **fork-join**: preparation forks into an audit branch and an employer-data branch, and the two branches rejoin before the return can be filed. `filing_status` runs `DRAFT → PREPARED → PARTIALLY_CLEARED → READY_TO_FILE → FILED`, with `FILED` terminal, and `PARTIALLY_CLEARED` is skipped whenever the fork degenerates to a single branch.

Opening the return is the pooled plan provider's act and needs only `plan_document_id` and the `plan_year` covered, an integer between 2020 and 2030 inclusive that must be unique among that plan's filings; a second filing for the same year is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_FILING_YEAR`. A new filing opens in `DRAFT` with `prepared_by_user_id`, `prepared_at`, `audit_attested_at`, `audit_opinion`, `audit_firm_service_provider_id`, `employer_data_certified_at`, `certified_by_user_id`, `certified_employer_count`, `late_deposit_schedule_h_line_cents`, `fidelity_bond_minimum_cents`, `filing_deadline_date`, `filed_date` and `acknowledgment_reference` all null, `audit_required` false and `is_late_filing` false.

**Preparation** is performed by a service provider engaged on the plan — a firm engaged elsewhere is rejected by the ownership check with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` — and takes no payload. Four further conditions apply, in this order. The plan document must be `ACTIVE`, failing which the request is rejected with `ERR_PLAN_NOT_ACTIVE`. The filing must be in `DRAFT`, failing which it is rejected with `ERR_FILING_INVALID_STATE`. The plan's `compliance_posture` must not be `FROZEN`; with two or more suspended adopting employers the return cannot be prepared at all, and the request is rejected with `ERR_PLAN_COMPLIANCE_FROZEN`. And no compliance test of the plan with `scope` `PLAN_LEVEL` and the same `plan_year` may still be sitting in `SCHEDULED`; an outstanding plan-level test is rejected with `ERR_PLAN_LEVEL_TEST_OUTSTANDING`.

Preparation records `prepared_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID` — which, for the entity-backed `ServiceProvider` role, is that provider's own identifier — stamps `prepared_at` from the logical clock, and snapshots four figures. `late_deposit_schedule_h_line_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` over every remittance deposit of the plan that is `POSTED` and whose `timeliness_status` is `LATE`, `CORRECTION_COMPUTED` or `CORRECTED`; a funded correction does not remove a deposit from this line. `audit_required` is copied from the plan document's own `audit_required` as evaluated at preparation time, so it reflects whether the plan's `participant_count` had reached 1000. `fidelity_bond_minimum_cents` is the minimum ERISA section 412 bond the plan requires: take the plan document's `total_assets_cents`, multiply by one tenth and round half up to whole cents, then raise the result to 100000 cents if it falls below that floor and cap it at 100000000 cents if it exceeds that ceiling. The floor therefore binds until plan assets reach 1000000 cents, ten percent of which is exactly the floor, and the ceiling binds from 1000000000 cents of assets upward. Note that `total_assets_cents` already includes `expense_recovered_cents` from settled exits. `filing_deadline_date` is derived from the plan document's `plan_year_end_date`, not from the filing's own `plan_year`: take the month seven months after the month of `plan_year_end_date`, take the last calendar day of that month, and if that day is not a business day roll *forward* one day at a time until it is. Rolling is forward only, never backward. For a `plan_year_end_date` of 2026-12-31 the target month is July 2027, whose last day is Saturday 2027-07-31, so the deadline rolls forward to 2027-08-02. Preparation increments the filing's `version` by one.

**The audit branch** is cleared by attestation, which only the independent auditor performs. The payload names the auditing firm through `audit_firm_service_provider_id` and records the `audit_opinion`, one of `UNMODIFIED`, `QUALIFIED` or `DISCLAIMER`. Six conditions apply, in this order. The filing must be in `PREPARED` or `PARTIALLY_CLEARED` and its `audit_attested_at` must still be null, because a branch that has already cleared cannot clear twice; a violation of either is rejected with `ERR_FILING_INVALID_STATE`. The filing's `audit_required` must be true, since a plan under the 1000-participant threshold has no audit branch to clear, and attestation on such a filing is rejected with `ERR_AUDIT_NOT_REQUIRED`. Then come the three independence rules of §2.10 in their stated order — `ERR_NOT_AN_AUDITOR_FIRM`, then `ERR_AUDITOR_NOT_INDEPENDENT`, then `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Last, the plan's bond must actually be adequate: the sponsoring provider's `fidelity_bond_amount_cents` must be at least the filing's computed `fidelity_bond_minimum_cents`, and a shortfall is rejected with `ERR_FIDELITY_BOND_INSUFFICIENT`. On success `audit_attested_at` is stamped from the logical clock, `audit_opinion` and `audit_firm_service_provider_id` are stored, and the filing's `version` is incremented by one. The filing then moves to `PARTIALLY_CLEARED` if the employer-data branch has not yet cleared — that is, if `employer_data_certified_at` is still null — and straight to `READY_TO_FILE` if it has.

**The employer-data branch** is cleared by the pooled plan provider's certification, which takes no payload. The filing must be in `PREPARED` or `PARTIALLY_CLEARED` with `employer_data_certified_at` still null, failing which the request is rejected with `ERR_FILING_INVALID_STATE`. Then every employer of the plan whose `adoption_status` is not `EXITED` must hold a plan design election in `APPROVED` status; the return reports employer-level data for every employer the plan has admitted and not yet released, so an employer still sitting in `APPLIED`, `ELECTIONS_SUBMITTED` or `RETURNED_FOR_REVISION` blocks certification just as surely as an `ACTIVE` one without an approved election, and the rejection is `ERR_EMPLOYER_ELECTIONS_INCOMPLETE`. This is easy to trip after the fact: an employer admitted *after* the filing was prepared is in `APPLIED` and will block a certification that would have succeeded a moment earlier. On success `employer_data_certified_at` is stamped from the logical clock, `certified_by_user_id` is recorded from the acting `X-User-ID`, `certified_employer_count` is set to the count of the plan's employers whose `adoption_status` is anything other than `EXITED` at that instant, and the filing's `version` is incremented by one. Where the filing lands depends on the other branch and on `audit_required`: with `audit_required` false the audit branch is pre-cleared and the join completes on this single branch, so the filing moves from `PREPARED` directly to `READY_TO_FILE`; with `audit_required` true and `audit_attested_at` still null it moves from `PREPARED` to `PARTIALLY_CLEARED`; and with the audit branch already cleared it moves from `PARTIALLY_CLEARED` to `READY_TO_FILE`.

**Filing** is reserved to the regulator-side operator and takes no payload. Three conditions apply, in this order: the filing must be in `READY_TO_FILE`, failing which the request is rejected with `ERR_FILING_INVALID_STATE`; the plan's `compliance_posture` must not be `FROZEN`, failing which it is rejected with `ERR_PLAN_COMPLIANCE_FROZEN`; and when `audit_required` is true the recorded `audit_opinion` must not be `DISCLAIMER`, failing which it is rejected with `ERR_AUDIT_OPINION_UNACCEPTABLE`. A `QUALIFIED` opinion is acceptable; only a disclaimer blocks the filing. On success `filing_status` moves to `FILED`, `filed_date` is set to the logical-clock calendar date, `is_late_filing` is set to true when `filed_date` is strictly later than `filing_deadline_date` and false otherwise, an `acknowledgment_reference` is minted by the regulator, and the filing's `version` is incremented by one.

### 3.8. Employer Exit, Spin-off and Asset Transfer

An employer leaves the pooled plan through a four-stage transfer whose `status` runs `REQUESTED → APPROVED → SETTLED → REVERSED`, and whose employer-side mirror runs `ACTIVE → EXIT_REQUESTED → SPINOFF_IN_PROGRESS → EXITED`. `EXITED` looks terminal and is escaped by exactly one route: explicitly unwinding the settlement that produced it.

The employer initiates its own exit. The payload names itself through `adopting_employer_id`, chooses an `exit_type` from `SPINOFF_TO_SINGLE_PLAN`, `TRANSFER_TO_OTHER_PEP` or `PLAN_TERMINATION_DISTRIBUTION`, states the `requested_exit_date` on which it asks to leave, and bounds the asset-transfer blackout window with `blackout_start_date` and `blackout_end_date`, a closed interval that includes both endpoints. A `successor_plan_name` of 1 to 200 characters naming the receiving plan is required for `SPINOFF_TO_SINGLE_PLAN` and `TRANSFER_TO_OTHER_PEP` and is not required for `PLAN_TERMINATION_DISTRIBUTION`, where there is no receiving plan; its absence when required is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION`. Three checks apply in order: the employer's `adoption_status` must be `ACTIVE`, so a `SUSPENDED` employer may not request an exit and is rejected with `ERR_EMPLOYER_INVALID_STATE`; the dates must be well ordered, with `blackout_end_date` on or after `blackout_start_date` and `requested_exit_date` on or after `blackout_end_date`, failing which the request is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_BLACKOUT_WINDOW`; and the employer must carry no remittance deposit that is `POSTED` with a `timeliness_status` of `LATE` or `CORRECTION_COMPUTED`, failing which it is rejected with `ERR_OPEN_LATE_DEPOSIT_CORRECTION`. A new transfer opens in `REQUESTED` with `approved_at`, `settled_at`, `reversal_reason` and every waterfall figure null, and the employer advances from `ACTIVE` to `EXIT_REQUESTED` with its `version` incremented by one.

**Approval** is the pooled plan provider's act, takes no payload, and opens the blackout window. Three checks apply in order: the transfer must be in `REQUESTED`, failing which it is rejected with `ERR_TRANSFER_INVALID_STATE`; the plan's `compliance_posture` must not be `FROZEN`, failing which it is rejected with `ERR_PLAN_COMPLIANCE_FROZEN`; and the blackout window must not collide with another transfer's window, because the plan services one asset transfer at a time. Collision is generous: the new window may neither overlap nor come within five calendar days of the blackout window of any other exit transfer of the same plan whose `status` is not `REVERSED`. The gap between two non-overlapping windows is the whole number of calendar days from the earlier window's `blackout_end_date` to the later window's `blackout_start_date`; a gap of five days or fewer conflicts and a gap of six days or more is accepted. A `SETTLED` transfer still holds the slot, and only a `REVERSED` one releases it. A conflict is rejected with `ERR_BLACKOUT_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. On success `approved_at` is stamped from the logical clock, the transfer moves to `APPROVED`, the employer advances from `EXIT_REQUESTED` to `SPINOFF_IN_PROGRESS`, and both `version` values are incremented by one. `SPINOFF_IN_PROGRESS` is the asset-movement phase for every `exit_type`, not only for a spin-off, and the blackout is in force for as long as the employer sits there.

**Settlement** moves the money and takes no payload. Four checks apply in order: the transfer must be in `APPROVED`, failing which it is rejected with `ERR_TRANSFER_INVALID_STATE`; the logical-clock calendar date must be strictly after `blackout_end_date`, since assets cannot move while the blackout is still open, and a settlement on or before that date is rejected with `ERR_BLACKOUT_NOT_ENDED`; the employer must carry no `POSTED` deposit whose `timeliness_status` is `LATE` or `CORRECTION_COMPUTED`, failing which it is rejected with `ERR_OPEN_LATE_DEPOSIT_CORRECTION`; and the employer must have no payroll batch left in `VALIDATED` status awaiting remittance, failing which it is rejected with `ERR_UNREMITTED_CONTRIBUTIONS`.

The settlement figures are captured at the settlement instant, not at request time. `gross_account_balance_cents` is the employer's `account_balance_cents` as it stands then, and `transferred_participant_count` is its `participant_count` as it stands then. Two deductions are computed: `admin_recovery_cents` is 1250 cents of administrative recovery per transferred participant, multiplied by `transferred_participant_count`; and `flat_exit_fee_cents` is 250000 cents, except for `PLAN_TERMINATION_DISTRIBUTION`, where the flat fee is waived and the figure is zero. The deductions are then satisfied out of the gross balance as ordered buckets: administrative recovery is satisfied first, taking the lesser of `admin_recovery_cents` and the gross balance; the flat fee is satisfied second out of whatever remains, taking the lesser of `flat_exit_fee_cents` and that remainder. `deductions_applied_cents` is the sum of the two amounts actually taken. `unrecovered_deduction_cents` is the shortfall — the sum of `admin_recovery_cents` and `flat_exit_fee_cents` less `deductions_applied_cents` — and it is written off by the plan rather than becoming a receivable. `transfer_amount_cents`, the amount that actually moves to the successor arrangement, is the gross balance less `deductions_applied_cents`, while `settled_amount_cents`, the total value removed from the pooled plan, equals the gross balance. Two worked cases: a gross balance of 8500000 cents for 13 participants under `SPINOFF_TO_SINGLE_PLAN` applies 16250 and 250000, leaves nothing unrecovered, and transfers 8233750; a gross balance of 100000 cents for 4 participants under the same exit type applies 5000 and then only 95000 of the flat fee, leaves 155000 unrecovered, and transfers nothing at all.

The ledger routing is deliberate: `deductions_applied_cents` stays inside the plan and accumulates into the plan document's `expense_recovered_cents`, `transfer_amount_cents` leaves the plan and accumulates into `transferred_out_cents`, and the employer's own `account_balance_cents` is zeroed — which is exactly why `contribution_control_delta_cents` remains at zero across a settlement. Settlement is also the deepest cascade on the exit side. The transfer moves to `SETTLED` with `settled_at` stamped from the logical clock. The employer advances from `SPINOFF_IN_PROGRESS` to `EXITED`, has `exited_at` stamped and its balance zeroed, and its `version` incremented. The employer's `APPROVED` plan design election moves to `SUPERSEDED` with its own `version` incremented. Every `EMPLOYER_LEVEL` compliance test naming that employer moves to `CLOSED` with `closed_reason` `EMPLOYER_EXITED`. And the plan document's `participant_count`, `transferred_out_participant_count`, `active_employer_count`, `audit_required`, `total_assets_cents`, `transferred_out_cents`, `expense_recovered_cents` and both control deltas are all recomputed — with `cumulative_enrolled_participant_count` deliberately left alone, since an exit never reduces the monotone enrolment total, which is precisely why `headcount_control_delta` stays at zero once `transferred_out_participant_count` absorbs the departed headcount.

**Reversal** unwinds a settlement. The transfer must be in `SETTLED`, failing which it is rejected with `ERR_TRANSFER_INVALID_STATE`, and no plan filing of the plan may be `FILED` for the plan year containing the transfer's `settled_at`; once the return is in, the settlement is history and reversal is rejected with `ERR_FILING_ALREADY_SUBMITTED`. The payload carries a `reversal_reason`, which is stored. On success the transfer moves to `REVERSED`, the employer returns from `EXITED` to `SPINOFF_IN_PROGRESS`, its `account_balance_cents` is restored to the pre-settlement gross and its `exited_at` is cleared, and every plan control total is recomputed so that both `headcount_control_delta` and `contribution_control_delta_cents` return to exactly zero with no drift. Both `version` values are incremented by one.

### 3.9. Fee Disclosure and Revenue Sharing Allocation

Periodically the pooled plan provider must tell adopting employers what the plan costs and hand back the indirect compensation the plan received from investment providers. A disclosure names its plan through `plan_document_id`, bounds the period with `disclosure_period_start` and `disclosure_period_end`, chooses an `allocation_method` of either `PRO_RATA_ASSETS` or `PER_CAPITA`, and states the `revenue_sharing_pool_cents` to be handed back, an integer of zero or more. Only the pooled plan provider may issue one. Three conditions apply: the plan document must be `ACTIVE`, failing which the request is rejected with `ERR_PLAN_NOT_ACTIVE`; at least one service provider engaged on the plan must charge a `direct_fee_cents_per_participant` greater than zero, since a plan whose every provider charges no direct fee has nothing to disclose and gives the rebate no base to reduce, and such a request is rejected with `ERR_NO_BILLABLE_SERVICE_PROVIDER`; and `disclosure_period_end` must fall on or after `disclosure_period_start`, failing which it is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_DISCLOSURE_PERIOD`. A disclosure is an immutable event row: it carries `disclosed_at` stamped from the logical clock, is readable afterwards by the regulator, the pooled plan provider and adopting employers, and — as noted in §2.4 — has no `updated_at` and no `version`, because nothing ever amends it. Correcting a disclosure means issuing another one; nothing prevents two disclosures covering the same period, and each allocates its own pool on top of the last.

Issuing a disclosure runs two calculations, and their order is pinned.

**The net plan expense** is computed first. `gross_direct_fees_cents` is the plan document's `participant_count` multiplied by the sum of `direct_fee_cents_per_participant` over *every* service provider whose `plan_document_id` is this plan, regardless of that provider's `provider_type` and regardless of whether it holds a fiduciary role — an unengaged auditor firm on the plan still contributes its per-participant fee to the gross. `expense_rebate_pct` is then selected by a two-tier schedule read off the gross figure: 5.0 when `gross_direct_fees_cents` is at least 5000000 cents, 2.0 when it is at least 1000000 cents, and 0.0 below that. The rebate itself is `gross_direct_fees_cents` multiplied by `expense_rebate_pct` divided by 100, rounded half up to whole cents, and the rebated amount is the gross less that rebate. Only then does the revenue-sharing pool come into play: `revenue_sharing_offset_cents` is the lesser of `revenue_sharing_pool_cents` and the rebated amount, so the pool can extinguish the plan's expense but can never push it negative, and `net_plan_expense_cents` is the rebated amount less that offset. The order is observable rather than cosmetic. With a gross of 1020000 cents and a pool of 1000000 cents, reading the tier off the gross selects 2.0 percent, produces a rebate of 20400, a rebated amount of 999600, an offset of 999600 and a `net_plan_expense_cents` of 0. Offsetting first would leave 20000, select the 0.0 percent tier, and report 20000 instead.

**The revenue-sharing allocation** runs second and divides the pool among eligible employers by largest remainder, so that not a single cent is created or lost. The eligible population is the plan's employers whose `adoption_status` is `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, taken in `created_at` ascending order; `eligible_employer_count` records how many there were. A suspended employer still shares in the pool, while an employer that has `EXITED` receives nothing even if it held assets throughout the disclosure period, and employers still in `APPLIED`, `ELECTIONS_SUBMITTED`, `RETURNED_FOR_REVISION`, `EXIT_REQUESTED` or `SPINOFF_IN_PROGRESS` are likewise outside the population. Each eligible employer's basis is its `account_balance_cents` under `PRO_RATA_ASSETS` and its `participant_count` under `PER_CAPITA`. If the total basis across the population is zero — every eligible employer holding a zero balance, say — the pool is not allocated at all: `allocation_lines` is the empty array, `total_revenue_sharing_allocated_cents` is 0 and `residual_cents_distributed` is 0. Otherwise each employer's raw share is `revenue_sharing_pool_cents` multiplied by its basis divided by the total basis, kept exact and then floored to whole cents, with the fractional part retained for ranking; its `basis_share_pct` is its basis multiplied by 100 divided by the total basis, rounded half up to two decimal places. The residual is `revenue_sharing_pool_cents` less the sum of the floors. The eligible employers are ranked by fractional remainder descending, ties broken by `created_at` ascending and never by `id`, and one extra cent goes to each of the first `residual` employers in that ranking, recorded as `residual_awarded` of 1 on the winners and 0 on everyone else. An employer's `credit_cents` is its floor plus its award, `residual_cents_distributed` is the residual, and `total_revenue_sharing_allocated_cents` is the sum of the credits, which equals the pool exactly. Every eligible employer gets a line even when its credit is zero: a zero-balance employer under `PRO_RATA_ASSETS` appears with a `basis_amount` of 0, a `basis_share_pct` of 0.00 and a `credit_cents` of 0. Each entry of `allocation_lines` is an object of `adopting_employer_id`, `basis_amount`, `basis_share_pct`, `credit_cents` and `residual_awarded`, and the array is ordered by `credit_cents` descending, ties broken by the named employer's `created_at` ascending. Finally, each named employer's `revenue_sharing_credit_cents` increases by its `credit_cents`, so that field is a running lifetime total across every disclosure the plan has ever issued rather than a single-period figure.

The two methods diverge sharply on the same population. With a pool of 1000000 cents split `PER_CAPITA` between employers of 13 and 4 participants, the raw shares are 764705.88 and 235294.11, the floors 764705 and 235294, the residual 1 cent, and the larger fractional remainder — 0.88 — takes it, giving credits of 764706 and 235294 with `residual_cents_distributed` of 1. For the same two employers holding balances of 8500000 and 0 cents, `PRO_RATA_ASSETS` sends the whole 1000000 to the first and 0 to the second, while `PER_CAPITA` sends 764706 and 235294.
