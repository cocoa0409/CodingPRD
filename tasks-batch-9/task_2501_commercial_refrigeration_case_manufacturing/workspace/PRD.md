# Commercial Refrigeration Case Manufacturing API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A refrigerated display case is one of the few products in a supermarket that is simultaneously an engineered thermodynamic system, a regulated appliance, a merchandising fixture and a warranted capital asset. Our company designs and builds those cases, and today the four concerns are tracked in four disconnected systems: a lab spreadsheet holds psychrometric chamber bookings and metered energy results, a compliance binder holds the energy labels we print on nameplates, a quoting tool holds store-by-store remodel configurations, and the plant floor runs off printed travellers. The consequence is a class of expensive failures that nobody detects until a trailer is already at a store: a case whose design changed after its energy test was certified, a store plan priced against a model that has since lost certification, a crew double-booked across two remodels on the same weekend, a warranty claim honoured on a unit that was never actually handed off.

This document specifies a single backend service that holds the whole chain — refrigerant catalogue, case design and airflow modelling, chamber scheduling and energy testing, compliance labelling, store-plan configuration and pricing, plant release and fabrication routing, shipment into the store remodel window, install-contractor handoff, and the warranty and field-service network behind the delivered fleet. Two ideas run through all of it. The first is **pinning**: whenever a downstream artefact depends on an upstream design, the service copies the upstream value at the moment of the dependency and later compares the copy against the live value, so a design change can never silently validate a document that was written against an older design. The second is **cascading holds**: when certification is lost, the loss propagates deterministically through ordered lines, plans and queued work orders, and reverses just as deterministically when certification is restored.

The service is also a compliance record. Every state change is attributable to the acting user, and two decisions — issuing an energy label and approving a store plan — are deliberately split between two different people. Because the regulatory and warranty windows are temporal, the service does not read wall-clock time; it reads a logical UTC clock that an operator sets explicitly, so that a remodel window, an install window and a warranty term can all be exercised deterministically.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the service must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that user is acting in. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the nine role names declared below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). This is the very first check the service performs — global rank 1 in the evaluation ladder described in §2.5 — and it runs before any body parsing, path resolution or business evaluation.

The nine roles are `PlatformAdmin`, `DesignEngineer`, `TestLabTechnician`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `SalesEngineer`, `PlantSupervisor`, `LogisticsCoordinator`, `InstallContractor` and `ServiceManager`. Each endpoint in §4 lists the roles authorised to call it. A syntactically valid, authenticated request whose `X-User-Role` is not on the called endpoint's list is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403) at global rank 2 — again before any other consideration, so an unauthorised caller learns nothing about whether the target resource exists.

`PlatformAdmin` is the operator who seeds the engineering reference data — refrigerant blends, psychrometric chambers, depot stock, install crews and energy standards — and who drives the logical clock. `DesignEngineer` designs case models, runs the airflow and charge models, submits designs to the lab, and opens refrigerant-transition engineering changes. `TestLabTechnician` schedules, starts and completes chamber tests. `ComplianceOfficer` issues and revokes energy labels, and may also register energy standards. `SalesEngineer` opens store plans, configures their lines, prices them and approves them. `PlantSupervisor` releases approved plans to the plant and drives each physical case through fabrication and final QA. `LogisticsCoordinator` creates and advances shipments, cancels them, registers install crews and reserves those crews against shipments. `InstallContractor` is the installation crew that accepts a delivered shipment on site. `ServiceManager` stocks regional depots and runs warranty claims from report through resolution or denial. Every role in the list may perform every read operation.

Identity semantics differ across roles, and the difference matters for ownership checks. When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `DesignEngineer`, `TestLabTechnician`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `SalesEngineer`, `PlantSupervisor`, `LogisticsCoordinator` or `ServiceManager`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a workflow demands attribution — as `submitted_by_user_id` on a case model when a `DesignEngineer` submits it for test, as `issued_by_user_id` on a compliance label when a `ComplianceOfficer` issues it, and as `priced_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` on a store plan when a `SalesEngineer` prices or approves it — but it never compares that value to any entity's `id`.

`InstallContractor` is the single exception. When `X-User-Role` is `InstallContractor`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting install crew; ownership checks on any resource that references an install crew compare the resource's crew reference to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, when an install contractor accepts a delivered shipment, the service requires that `X-User-ID` equal the `install_crew_id` of at least one install booking attached to that shipment, in **any** booking state — `BOOKED`, `RELEASED` or `COMPLETED` all satisfy the identity test. A contractor who is not on any booking of that shipment is rejected with `ERR_CREW_IDENTITY_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403) at global rank 3. Note carefully that this identity test is deliberately looser than the readiness test described in §3.9: satisfying rank 3 with a `RELEASED` booking does not make the handoff legal, it only proves the caller is one of the crews that has at some point been assigned to that shipment.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls how many rows are returned; it defaults to 10 when omitted, and its accepted range is a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100 inclusive. `offset` controls how many rows are skipped before the returned page begins; it defaults to 0 when omitted and its minimum is 0, with no upper bound. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is a request-shape failure and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending unless a specific endpoint description states otherwise, and no endpoint in this service overrides that default. The response `meta` block for a list carries `limit` and `offset` as they were applied, `total_count` giving the number of rows matching the filters before pagination was applied, and `has_more`, which is true exactly when rows matching the filters remain beyond the returned page.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Revision Counters and the Logical Clock

Every persisted resource carries an `id` that is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp in ISO 8601 UTC with three fractional digits. Most resources additionally carry an `updated_at` timestamp in the same format, refreshed on every mutation, and a `version` integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. Two catalogue resources are deliberately immutable reference data and therefore carry **only** `id` and `created_at`: refrigerant blends and energy standards have no `updated_at` field and no `version` field at all. Every other resource in the system — psychrometric chambers, case models, energy tests, compliance labels, store plans, store plan lines, work orders, shipments, install crews, install bookings, depot stock rows and warranty claims — carries the full set.

Version increments are load-bearing rather than cosmetic, because two write operations are guarded by the optimistic-concurrency rules of §2.7 and a caller must be able to observe the counter it is expected to echo. Note in particular that certain operations bump the `version` of an entity other than the one being written: scheduling an energy test increments the target case model's `version` by 1 even though the test itself is the new row, issuing a compliance label increments the certified case model's `version` by 1, and creating a shipment increments the source store plan's `version` by 1. Those bumps are what make a second, otherwise-legitimate write against the same parent visible as stale.

The service never reads wall-clock time. Every temporal comparison — the warranty coverage window, the install-booking handoff window — reads a **logical UTC clock** held by the service. Before any operator sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it must never fall back to real wall-clock time. A `PlatformAdmin` moves the clock by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant to the administrative clock-setting capability, which responds with HTTP 200 and an empty data object. Timestamps supplied in request bodies — remodel window boundaries, test windows, ship and delivery dates, booking dates, the reported date on a claim — are client-supplied data and are compared against each other and against the logical clock, never against the host's real time.

### 2.5. Request Evaluation Order

Every request walks a fixed evaluation ladder, and the **lowest-ranked** failing check is the one whose error is returned. The universal preamble occupies ranks 1 through 8 and applies in this order:

1. **Rank 1 — authentication.** `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` present and the role recognised, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. **Rank 2 — authorisation.** The acting role appears on the called endpoint's role list, else `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403).
3. **Rank 3 — crew identity.** Only on the install-contractor handoff: `X-User-ID` matches a booking of the shipment as described in §2.1, else `ERR_CREW_IDENTITY_MISMATCH` (403).
4. **Rank 4 — request shape.** Required fields present, enum members recognised, types correct, numeric fields inside their declared constraints, `limit` within 1 to 100 inclusive and `offset` at least 0, else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400).
5. **Rank 5 — path existence.** Every identifier appearing in the URL path resolves to an existing row, validated left to right, else the entity-specific not-found error at HTTP 404: `ERR_CASE_MODEL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ENERGY_TEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COMPLIANCE_LABEL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STORE_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STORE_PLAN_LINE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WORK_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SHIPMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INSTALL_BOOKING_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_WARRANTY_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`. Because resolution is strictly left to right, the leftmost unresolvable segment determines the error.
6. **Rank 6 — body reference existence.** Every foreign reference supplied in the request body resolves to an existing row, validated in the order the reference fields are declared on the endpoint, else the corresponding not-found error at HTTP 404: `ERR_REFRIGERANT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ENERGY_STANDARD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TEST_CHAMBER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CASE_MODEL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ENERGY_TEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STORE_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SHIPMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INSTALL_CREW_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PART_INVENTORY_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_WORK_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`.
7. **Rank 7 — state admissibility.** The target row's current state admits the requested transition, else `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409). This check guards every state-driving operation in the service: running the airflow model on a case model, submitting a case model for test, opening an engineering change on a case model, starting an energy test, completing an energy test, issuing a compliance label, revoking a compliance label, adding a line to a store plan, pricing a store plan, approving a store plan, releasing a store plan to the plant, advancing a work order, recording a QA result, advancing a shipment, cancelling a shipment, handing off a shipment, releasing an install booking, reserving parts on a warranty claim, resolving a warranty claim, and denying a warranty claim — twenty operations in all.
8. **Rank 8 — stale parent version.** The optimistic-concurrency check of §2.7, carrying HTTP 412.

Ranks 9 through 39 are the domain rules. Each is introduced in §3 alongside the workflow it guards, and each is quoted there with its global rank so that the relative order of two rules competing on the same request is never ambiguous. When two domain rules both fail on one request, the lower rank number wins.

### 2.6. Rounding Discipline Across the Engineering and Commercial Models

Every rounding in this service is **round half up** — a value exactly at the midpoint rounds away from zero — and the number of decimal places is fixed per quantity rather than chosen per call. Thermal loads in watts are carried to 2 decimal places. Refrigerant CO2e masses in kilograms are carried to 1 decimal place, while gram quantities are exact integers. Daily energy figures in kilowatt-hours and the energy indices derived from them are carried to 3 decimal places. Every monetary quantity in the system is an integer count of cents; there are no fractional cents anywhere.

More important than the number of places is the **order** of rounding, which is pinned by each calculation and is not interchangeable with rounding the final sum. Two patterns recur. *Round-per-row-then-sum*: where a total aggregates rows, each row's contribution is rounded first and the rounded contributions are then added, so plan subtotals, warranty accruals and warranty incurred balances are all built from already-rounded per-row figures and are never re-derived from unrounded base terms. *Round-the-component-then-combine*: where a total combines named components, each named component is rounded at its own precision before the combination, and any term derived from that combination is computed from the already-rounded value. Intermediate multipliers that are never persisted as fields — the ambient uplift on a field energy projection, for instance — are not rounded at all and enter their products at full precision. Where a formula in §3 marks a factor as unrounded, that is a hard instruction, not an optimisation.

### 2.7. Design-Revision Pinning and Stale-Parent Rejection

Two distinct mechanisms in this service protect a downstream document from an upstream change, and they must not be confused.

The first is **design-revision pinning**. Every case model carries a `design_revision` integer, which begins at 1 when the model is created and increases by exactly 1 on every engineering change. Documents that depend on a specific design copy that number at the moment they are created: an energy test copies it into `pinned_design_revision` at scheduling time, and a compliance label copies it into `pinned_design_revision` at issuance. Whenever the service later acts on such a document, it compares the pinned copy against the live `design_revision` on the model, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_CASE_MODEL_REVISION_STALE` (HTTP 409) at global rank 14. That comparison guards three operations: starting an energy test, completing an energy test, and issuing a compliance label.

The second is **optimistic concurrency control on the parent row**. Exactly two write operations in this service require the client to echo the `version` of a parent entity it last observed, and each names its own field:

- Scheduling an energy test requires `expected_case_model_version` as a non-optional field in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the case model named by `case_model_id`. If that value does not equal the case model's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CASE_MODEL_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A new test may legitimately supersede an earlier `SCHEDULED` test on the same model, so the second create would otherwise succeed on its own merits; the version check is precisely what tells the lab that the design it read has since been revised, re-submitted or re-tested.
- Creating a shipment requires `expected_store_plan_version` as a non-optional field in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the store plan named by `store_plan_id`. If that value does not equal the plan's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_STORE_PLAN_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A plan may legitimately carry several shipments, so a second create is valid product behaviour; the version check is what stops a coordinator from loading a unit set that has changed since the plan was read.

No other endpoint in this service accepts a version field of any kind, and supplying one elsewhere is not a recognised input. Both checks sit at global rank 8, after state admissibility and before every domain rule, so a request that is both in the wrong state and carrying a stale version reports `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.

### 2.8. Compliance Holds and the Cascade Discipline

Certification is not a property a case model holds privately; it is a precondition for everything downstream of it, and its loss must propagate. The service models that propagation as an ordered chain of **cascade hops** executed inside the single request that triggers them. Each hop reads the state the previous hop has already written — hop 4 fires *because* hop 3 raised a hold count above zero, and hop 5 fires *because* hop 4 moved a plan into its hold state — so the hops are sequential, not simultaneous, and a hop whose precondition is unmet is a silent no-op rather than an error.

The hold marker itself is a boolean `compliance_hold` on each store plan line, false when the line is created. When a line's model loses its issued label, the line's `compliance_hold` becomes true, and the owning plan's `held_line_count` — a count of that plan's lines whose `compliance_hold` is true — is recomputed. A plan with at least one held line cannot be priced, approved, released or shipped from, and any of those four attempts is rejected with `ERR_PLAN_ON_COMPLIANCE_HOLD` (HTTP 409) at global rank 16. When certification is restored, the reverse chain runs with equal determinism: holds clear on the lines of the newly certified model, the count is recomputed, and if it reaches zero the plan and its held work orders both unwind.

Two properties of the cascade deserve emphasis. First, a plan whose production has already finished is out of scope: lines are only held when the owning plan's `production_status` is anything other than `COMPLETE`. Second, the two lifecycle dimensions of a store plan are genuinely independent — a commercial hold never rewinds production, and production progress never clears a commercial hold — so a plan may legitimately sit in the pair (`HOLD_PENDING_RECERT`, `IN_PRODUCTION`) for as long as recertification takes.

Every mutation described anywhere in this document, including one reached only as a cascade hop, refreshes the mutated row's `updated_at` and increments its `version` by 1. A single engineering change can therefore bump the version of a case model, several compliance labels, several energy tests, many store plan lines, several store plans and many work orders, all within one request.

### 2.9. Segregation of Duties

Two decisions in this service are structurally split between two people, and the split is enforced by comparing `X-User-ID` against a previously recorded user id. Issuing a compliance label requires that the acting `ComplianceOfficer`'s `X-User-ID` differ from the `submitted_by_user_id` recorded on the case model when it was submitted for test. Approving a store plan requires that the acting `SalesEngineer`'s `X-User-ID` differ from the `priced_by_user_id` recorded when the plan was priced. Either violation is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403) at global rank 9 — ahead of every other domain rule, so a self-approval attempt reports the duty conflict rather than, say, a credit problem it also has.

### 2.10. Interval Conventions

Three different interval conventions coexist in this service and each is stated explicitly wherever it applies, because mixing them produces off-by-one-day defects that only surface at boundaries.

**Half-open intervals** are written as starting at their first instant and ending strictly before their end instant. An energy test's chamber occupation runs from `window_start` up to but not including `window_end`. An install booking occupies `start_date` and every day up to but not including `end_date`, so a booking whose `end_date` is a given day does not occupy that day. Two half-open intervals that merely touch — one ending exactly where the next begins — do not overlap.

**Closed intervals** include both boundaries. A chamber's maintenance closure runs from `closed_window_start` through `closed_window_end` inclusive, so a test window that begins exactly at `closed_window_end` does intersect the closure. A store plan's remodel window runs from `remodel_window_start` through `remodel_window_end` inclusive.

**Exclusive-end anchoring against a closed window** is the third case, and it appears once: an install booking's exclusive `end_date` is permitted to equal the day after the remodel window's last day, because a booking that occupies the final remodel day must express that with an end anchored one day later.

Independently of which convention applies, any interval whose end is not strictly after its start is invalid: the exclusive `end_date` of an install booking must be strictly after its `start_date`, and the `window_end` of an energy test must be strictly after its `window_start`. Either violation is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL` (HTTP 422) at global rank 29.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Seeding the Engineering and Service Catalogues

Before any design work can begin, a `PlatformAdmin` populates the reference data that the engineering and compliance models read. A **refrigerant blend** is registered with its ASHRAE designation as `refrigerant_code`, the commercial `trade_name` printed on the nameplate, its 100-year global warming potential as `gwp_100yr` (an integer of at least 1, and the multiplier behind every CO2e mass in the system), its ASHRAE flammability class as `safety_class` — one of `A1`, `A2L` or `A3` — the per-case installed charge ceiling `max_charge_grams` (an integer of at least 1), and `allowed_system_types`, a non-empty subset of the two circuit architectures `REMOTE` and `SELF_CONTAINED` that this blend may be applied to. Refrigerant codes are globally unique. Refrigerant rows are pure reference data: they carry an `id` and a `created_at` and nothing else — no `updated_at`, no `version`.

An **energy standard** is the regulatory yardstick a test is judged against, and it may be registered either by a `PlatformAdmin` or by a `ComplianceOfficer`. Its `standard_code` is a globally unique identifier of up to 48 characters, such as a DOE rule designation. Its `effective_date` is the ISO 8601 UTC instant from which the standard may be tested against; a test window that begins before it is not covered. Its `max_gwp` is the highest refrigerant global warming potential the standard permits on a certified case. Its `limit_coefficient_a` (strictly greater than zero) and `limit_constant_b` (at least zero) are the per-square-foot coefficient and the fixed offset of the daily-energy limit. Its `temperature_class` — one of `HT`, `MT` or `LT` — and its `system_type_scope` — `REMOTE`, `SELF_CONTAINED` or `ANY` — determine which cases fall under it. Like refrigerants, energy standards carry only `id` and `created_at`; they have neither `updated_at` nor `version`.

A **psychrometric test chamber** is registered by a `PlatformAdmin` with a unique `chamber_code`, a display `name`, `supported_temperature_classes` as a non-empty subset of `HT`, `MT` and `LT`, `turnaround_hours` giving the soak-and-reset hours the chamber needs on each side of an occupied test window (an integer of at least zero), and `max_tda_sqft`, the largest total display area the chamber can physically accept (strictly greater than zero). A chamber may also declare an optional maintenance closure through `closed_window_start` and `closed_window_end`; both default to absent, and when present they bound a closed interval during which no test may be scheduled.

An **install crew** belongs to an outside installation contractor and may be registered by a `PlatformAdmin` or a `LogisticsCoordinator`. It carries a unique `crew_code`, the `contractor_name` of the contracting company, `service_region_codes` as a non-empty array of two-letter region codes the crew will travel to, and `daily_case_capacity`, the maximum number of cases the crew can set on any single calendar day (at least 1). A newly registered crew's `committed_case_days` counter starts at exactly 0.

A **depot stock row** may be created by a `PlatformAdmin` or a `ServiceManager` and represents one service part stocked in one region for one case model. It carries the `part_number`, a free-text `description`, the `case_model_id` of the model the part is stocked for, the `region_code` of the depot holding it, the physical `on_hand_qty` (at least zero), the `unit_cost_cents` charged as the parts cost basis on a claim (at least zero), and the `min_stock_qty` stocking floor for that part in that region (at least zero). A part number must be unique within a region, though the same part number may legitimately be stocked in several regions. On creation the row's `reserved_qty` is set to 0, its `available_qty` is computed as `on_hand_qty` less `reserved_qty`, and its `below_min` flag is computed as true exactly when `available_qty` is strictly less than `min_stock_qty`.

All eight creation operations that mint a coded resource — refrigerant, energy standard, chamber, case model, compliance label, install crew, store plan and depot stock row — share one uniqueness rule at global rank 21. Refrigerant codes, standard codes, chamber codes, model codes, label codes and crew codes are each globally unique; a store plan's `store_number` is unique within its `banner_name`; and a depot row's `part_number` is unique within its `region_code`. Any collision is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CODE` (HTTP 409). Finally, an administrator drives the logical clock as described in §2.4, supplying a `to_iso8601` target instant; this is the only capability in the system that changes what "now" means.

### 3.2. Case Design: Configuration and the Airflow Model

A `DesignEngineer` opens a new design, which is born in `DRAFT` with `design_revision` set to 1 and `submitted_by_user_id` absent. The design declares a unique `model_code`, a merchandising `application` such as a dairy multideck or a frozen coffin, the design `temperature_class` (`HT`, `MT` or `LT`), the circuit `system_type` (`REMOTE` or `SELF_CONTAINED`), the `refrigerant_id` of the blend the circuit is charged with, and optionally a `predecessor_model_id` naming the model this design supersedes in a refrigerant transition — absent by default, and the link that lets engineering trace a lower-GWP successor back to the design it replaces. Geometry follows: `nominal_length_ft` must be exactly 4, 6, 8 or 12 feet, and `tda_sqft` is the total display area in square feet, strictly greater than zero, which every energy calculation divides by.

Circuit and closure configuration are declared at the same time and are cross-checked immediately. The nameplate `charge_grams` sealed inside the case must be at least 1. The `line_set_length_ft` is the field line-set run between the case and a remote rack; a `SELF_CONTAINED` case must declare exactly 0 feet of line set, while a `REMOTE` case must declare at least 1 foot, and a violation of either half is rejected with `ERR_LINE_SET_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 422) at global rank 18 — a rule that guards both the initial design creation and every later engineering change. The front closure `door_type` is either `OPEN` or `GLASS_DOOR`, and `glass_door_count` (default 0, never negative) counts the glass doors carrying anti-sweat heat. An `OPEN` case must carry exactly zero glass doors and a `GLASS_DOOR` case must carry at least one; either violation is rejected with `ERR_DOOR_CONFIG_INVALID` (HTTP 422) at global rank 17, and this rule is checked both when the design is created and again when the airflow model is run.

The airflow inputs complete the design. `curtain_layers` is the number of air-curtain layers across the display opening, at least 1. `discharge_velocity_mps` is the air-curtain discharge velocity in metres per second, strictly greater than zero. `case_air_temp_c` is the discharge air temperature inside the display volume and `ambient_dew_point_c` is the design store dew point; together they set the infiltration temperature spread. `lighting_w_per_ft` and `fan_w_per_ft` are the lighting and evaporator-fan loads per linear foot, each at least zero, and `defrost_cycles_per_day` is the number of design defrost cycles in 24 hours, at least zero. All eight computed airflow fields and all three computed charge fields are absent on a freshly created design.

Running the **airflow model** moves the design `DRAFT → MODELED` and takes no request body at all: everything it needs is already on the row. It is admissible only from `DRAFT`. Two further preconditions apply. The design's `system_type` must be a member of the `allowed_system_types` of the blend named by `refrigerant_id`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_REFRIGERANT_SYSTEM_TYPE_INCOMPATIBLE` (HTTP 422) at global rank 10 — the same rule that guards an engineering change. And the closure configuration must still hold: the transition requires that either the `door_type` be `GLASS_DOOR` or the `glass_door_count` be zero, with the stricter rank-17 rule described above also re-evaluated, so a `GLASS_DOOR` case that has somehow reached zero doors is rejected with `ERR_DOOR_CONFIG_INVALID`.

The model itself is a constraint propagation over the single case model named in the request and the one refrigerant blend it references; no other row participates. It proceeds as follows.

- The **temperature spread** is the greater of zero and the store dew point minus the discharge air temperature — that is, `ambient_dew_point_c` less `case_air_temp_c`, floored at zero so a case colder than its surroundings never earns a negative infiltration term.
- The **curtain factor** rewards additional curtain layers: it is one, reduced by twelve hundredths for each layer beyond the first, and then floored at fifty-five hundredths. A single-layer curtain therefore yields exactly 1, a two-layer curtain 0.88, and a five-layer curtain would compute to 0.52 but is held at the 0.55 floor. The result is stored as `curtain_factor` and is **not** rounded.
- The **velocity factor** penalises discharge above the reference velocity of forty-five hundredths of a metre per second: it is one, plus twenty hundredths multiplied by the excess of `discharge_velocity_mps` over that reference (the excess floored at zero), that excess having first been divided by the same reference velocity. The result is stored as `velocity_factor` and is likewise **not** rounded; both factors enter the infiltration product at full precision.
- The **infiltration component** `infiltration_load_w` is an infiltration coefficient for the case's temperature class, multiplied by `tda_sqft`, multiplied by the temperature spread, multiplied by the curtain factor, multiplied by the velocity factor, and then rounded half up to 2 decimal places. The coefficient is sixty-two hundredths for an `HT` case, seventy-eight hundredths for `MT`, and one and five hundredths for `LT`.
- The **lighting component** `lighting_load_w` is `lighting_w_per_ft` multiplied by `nominal_length_ft`, rounded half up to 2 decimals. The **fan component** `fan_load_w` is `fan_w_per_ft` multiplied by `nominal_length_ft`, rounded the same way. Both use the nominal design length, not any ordered length.
- The **defrost component** `defrost_load_w` averages defrost energy across the day: three and a half watts per cycle per square foot, multiplied by `defrost_cycles_per_day`, multiplied by `tda_sqft`, divided by the 24 hours in a day, then rounded half up to 2 decimals.
- The **anti-sweat component** `anti_sweat_load_w` is a per-door anti-sweat wattage for the temperature class, multiplied by `glass_door_count`, rounded half up to 2 decimals. That per-door wattage is zero watts for an `HT` case, 18 watts for `MT` and 42 watts for `LT`, so an open `HT` merchandiser contributes nothing here while a low-temperature glass-door case contributes heavily.
- The five components above are each rounded **first** and then added to form the load subtotal. A `SELF_CONTAINED` case then adds a condenser-heat rejection term, because its condenser dumps heat back into the space: eight hundredths of the already-rounded subtotal, itself rounded half up to 2 decimals. A `REMOTE` case's rejection term is exactly zero. Finally `total_design_load_w` is the subtotal plus the rejection term, rounded half up to 2 decimals.

The same operation also establishes the **refrigerant charge basis**, an aggregation over the case model and its blend. The `installed_charge_grams` is the nameplate `charge_grams` plus 26 grams for every foot of `line_set_length_ft`, and it is an exact integer with no rounding. The `nameplate_co2e_kg` is the nameplate charge converted from grams to kilograms — divided by one thousand — multiplied by the blend's `gwp_100yr`, rounded half up to 1 decimal place. The `installed_co2e_kg` applies the identical conversion and multiplication to `installed_charge_grams`, again rounded half up to 1 decimal.

Those two masses are deliberately different bases for different audiences, and the distinction has teeth. The **nameplate basis** counts only the charge sealed inside the case and ignores the field line set entirely; it is what gets printed on the nameplate and what feeds factory-level GWP reporting. The **installed basis** adds the line-set contribution, so a `REMOTE` case with a long line set can sit comfortably under its blend's ceiling on the nameplate basis and breach it on the installed basis. Because a self-contained circuit is required to declare zero line-set feet, the two bases coincide exactly for `SELF_CONTAINED` cases. Both computations, together with all eight airflow fields, are written in the single airflow-model request, which also increments the case model's `version` by 1.

Submitting a `MODELED` design to the lab moves it `MODELED → SUBMITTED_FOR_TEST`, takes no request body, and records the acting engineer's `X-User-ID` as `submitted_by_user_id` — the value §2.9 later compares against the label issuer. Two preconditions guard it beyond state admissibility: `total_design_load_w` must be present, which in practice means the airflow model has run, and the **installed** charge basis must be at or below the blend's `max_charge_grams`. A breach is rejected with `ERR_REFRIGERANT_CHARGE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422) at global rank 11. That same rank-11 rule is re-evaluated in an entirely different place — when a sales engineer adds a line to a store plan — so a model that would exceed its ceiling cannot be ordered even if it somehow slipped through submission. Submission also increments the case model's `version` by 1. A design listing can be filtered by `refrigerant_id`, by `predecessor_model_id` to walk a transition chain, or by `status`.

### 3.3. Chamber Scheduling and Energy Testing

A `TestLabTechnician` reserves chamber time by scheduling an energy test, naming the `case_model_id` under test, the `test_chamber_id` to be occupied, the `energy_standard_id` whose limit the measurement will be judged against, the half-open occupation interval `window_start` to `window_end`, the chamber ambient dry-bulb temperature `test_ambient_c` that will be held during the run, and the `store_ambient_c` of the destination store that the field projection will later assume. The request must also carry `expected_case_model_version` exactly as described in §2.7. A newly created test is `SCHEDULED`.

Scheduling is where the test's dependence on the design is frozen. Three values are copied from the case model in the same request: `pinned_design_revision` takes the model's current `design_revision`, `pinned_tda_sqft` takes its `tda_sqft`, and `pinned_defrost_cycles_per_day` takes its `defrost_cycles_per_day`. Every later index computation divides by the pinned display area and reasons about the pinned defrost count, never about the live model fields — this is what makes a completed test reproducible after the design has moved on. Scheduling also increments the case model's `version` by 1, which is precisely why a second schedule attempt built on a stale read fails the rank-8 version check.

The **daily energy limit** is pinned in the same request. It is the standard's `limit_coefficient_a` multiplied by the case model's `tda_sqft`, plus the standard's `limit_constant_b`, rounded half up to 3 decimal places, and stored as `limit_daily_kwh`. Because the limit is frozen at scheduling, a standard registered later or a design resized later cannot move a test's bar. Both the standard and the case model are read exactly once, at creation.

Four domain rules guard scheduling, and their relative order matters when a request breaks more than one. First at global rank 13, the standard must actually cover the case: the standard's `temperature_class` must equal the model's `temperature_class`, the standard's `system_type_scope` must be either `ANY` or exactly the model's `system_type`, and the requested `window_start` must fall at or after the standard's `effective_date`. Any of those three failures is reported as `ERR_STANDARD_NOT_APPLICABLE` (HTTP 422). Next at rank 22, the model's `temperature_class` must be a member of the chamber's `supported_temperature_classes`, else `ERR_CHAMBER_CLASS_UNSUPPORTED` (HTTP 422). Then at rank 23, the model's `tda_sqft` must be at or below the chamber's `max_tda_sqft`, else `ERR_CHAMBER_TDA_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). Finally at rank 24 comes the chamber conflict check. The interval-validity rule of §2.10 — `window_end` strictly after `window_start`, else `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL` — sits at rank 29 and is therefore evaluated after all four of these.

**Chamber occupation** is single-occupancy with a symmetric turnaround buffer. For every existing test on the same chamber whose status is `SCHEDULED` or `RUNNING`, the service builds a blocked interval by widening that test's occupation on **both** sides by the chamber's `turnaround_hours`: the blocked interval starts `turnaround_hours` before that test's `window_start` and ends, exclusively, `turnaround_hours` after its `window_end`. The candidate's own half-open interval must not overlap any such blocked interval. Because both intervals are half-open, mere touching is not overlap — a candidate whose `window_start` equals the end of a blocked interval is accepted. Tests whose status is `COMPLETED_PASS`, `COMPLETED_FAIL` or `INVALIDATED` release the chamber entirely and are excluded from the scan. Separately, the candidate interval must not intersect the chamber's closed maintenance interval running from `closed_window_start` through `closed_window_end` inclusive; because that closure is closed at both ends, a candidate starting exactly at `closed_window_end` **is** rejected. Either form of conflict is reported as `ERR_CHAMBER_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409). All of this arithmetic is done in whole hours.

Starting a scheduled test moves it `SCHEDULED → RUNNING`, takes no request body, and increments the test's `version` by 1. Beyond admissibility from `SCHEDULED`, it requires the case model to still be in `SUBMITTED_FOR_TEST` and the test's `pinned_design_revision` to still equal the model's live `design_revision`; a revision mismatch is the rank-14 rejection `ERR_CASE_MODEL_REVISION_STALE` (HTTP 409). This is the exact mechanism by which a test that kept its chamber slot through an engineering change is caught at the door.

Completing a running test is the technician's final act on it. The request supplies `measured_kwh_24h`, the metered 24-hour energy consumption (at least zero), and `measured_defrost_cycles`, the defrost cycles actually observed during the run (at least zero). Completion is admissible only from `RUNNING`, and it recomputes both index variants before deciding the outcome. **Pass or fail is decided by the laboratory variant alone**: the test moves `RUNNING → COMPLETED_PASS` when `measured_kwh_24h` is at or below `limit_daily_kwh`, and `RUNNING → COMPLETED_FAIL` when it is strictly above. A measurement exactly equal to the limit therefore passes. Completion increments the test's `version` by 1, and it drives the case model across in the same request: the model moves `SUBMITTED_FOR_TEST → TESTED_PASS` on the pass path and `SUBMITTED_FOR_TEST → TESTED_FAIL` on the fail path, with the model's `version` incremented either way. Both cross-entity moves require the model to be sitting in `SUBMITTED_FOR_TEST` and the test to belong to that model and to be `RUNNING`. The rank-14 revision check guards completion exactly as it guards starting.

The two indices are computed identically on the pass and fail paths — a failing test is fully evaluated, not abandoned — and both read only the test row's own pinned values. The **laboratory variant** sets `lab_rated_index` to `measured_kwh_24h` divided by `pinned_tda_sqft`, rounded half up to 3 decimal places; it applies no ambient or defrost correction whatsoever, and it is the number a compliance label will print. The **field-projected variant** answers a different question — what this case will actually draw in the destination store — and it applies two corrections. The ambient uplift is one, plus thirty-five thousandths for every degree Celsius by which `store_ambient_c` exceeds `test_ambient_c`, with that excess floored at zero; the uplift is not rounded. The missed defrost count is `pinned_defrost_cycles_per_day` less `measured_defrost_cycles`, floored at zero, so a chamber that ran more cycles than the design calls for earns no credit. Then `field_projected_daily_kwh` is `measured_kwh_24h` multiplied by the ambient uplift, plus twenty-eight hundredths of a kilowatt-hour for each missed defrost cycle, with the whole expression rounded half up to 3 decimals exactly once at the end. `field_projected_index` is `field_projected_daily_kwh` divided by `pinned_tda_sqft`, rounded half up to 3 decimals.

The last computed field on a completed test is the one compliance cares about most. `field_exceedance` is true exactly when both of two things hold: `measured_kwh_24h` is at or below `limit_daily_kwh` — the lab variant passed — **and** `field_projected_daily_kwh` is strictly above `limit_daily_kwh`. It flags the uncomfortable case of a design that earns its certification in the chamber and would miss the same limit in the store it is destined for. It changes no state and blocks nothing; it is a recorded fact. Test listings can be filtered by `case_model_id`, `test_chamber_id`, `energy_standard_id` or `status`.

### 3.4. Certification, Labelling and Revocation

A `ComplianceOfficer` certifies a design by issuing an energy label from a passing test, naming the `case_model_id` being certified, the `energy_test_id` the label is issued from, and the unique `label_code` that will be applied to the case nameplate. The label is created with `label_status` set to `ISSUED`, its `revocation_reason` absent, and `issued_by_user_id` set to the acting officer's `X-User-ID`. Three values are pinned at issuance: `pinned_design_revision` takes the model's current `design_revision`, `pinned_lab_rated_index` takes the source test's `lab_rated_index`, and `pinned_standard_code` takes the `standard_code` of the energy standard that test was run against. Those pinned values are the printed record; they never move afterwards.

Issuance is the gate where the most preconditions converge. The case model must be sitting in `TESTED_PASS` and moves `TESTED_PASS → CERTIFIED` in the same request, with the model's `version` incremented by 1. The source test must have status `COMPLETED_PASS`. The test's `pinned_design_revision` must still equal the model's live `design_revision`, else the rank-14 `ERR_CASE_MODEL_REVISION_STALE` (HTTP 409) applies — this is the third and last operation that revision check guards. The acting officer's `X-User-ID` must differ from the model's `submitted_by_user_id`, else the rank-9 `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403) applies. And the refrigerant's `gwp_100yr` must be at or below the `max_gwp` of the energy standard the test was run against, else `ERR_GWP_EXCEEDS_STANDARD` (HTTP 422) at global rank 12. Because rank 9 precedes rank 12 which precedes rank 14, an officer who both submitted the design and used an over-GWP blend on a stale revision is told about the duty conflict first. The `label_code` uniqueness rule of rank 21 applies here as well.

Issuing a label does more than certify a design: it is the recovery path out of a compliance hold. In the same request, `compliance_hold` is set back to false on every store plan line that orders this model, and the `held_line_count` of each touched plan is recomputed. For any such plan whose `commercial_status` is `HOLD_PENDING_RECERT` and whose `held_line_count` has now reached zero, the plan moves `HOLD_PENDING_RECERT → APPROVED`, and every work order of that plan sitting in `HELD` moves `HELD → QUEUED`. Each of those rows has its `version` incremented and its `updated_at` refreshed. A plan that still has held lines referencing some *other* uncertified model stays in `HOLD_PENDING_RECERT`, because the unwind is conditioned on the count reaching zero, not merely on the count decreasing.

Revoking an issued label is the mirror image and is the officer's other exclusive act. It requires a `revocation_reason`, which must be a non-empty string, and it is admissible only from `label_status` `ISSUED`. The label moves `ISSUED → REVOKED`, the reason is recorded in `revocation_reason`, and the label's `version` is incremented by 1. From there the cascade runs in three further hops. The case model, which must be in `CERTIFIED`, moves `CERTIFIED → DECERTIFIED` with its `version` incremented. Then `compliance_hold` is set to true on every store plan line whose ordered model is this one and whose owning plan's `production_status` is anything other than `COMPLETE`, and each touched plan's `held_line_count` is recomputed. Then, for each plan touched by that hop whose `commercial_status` is `APPROVED`, the plan moves `APPROVED → HOLD_PENDING_RECERT`, which in turn moves that plan's `QUEUED` work orders to `HELD` as described in §2.8. Label listings can be filtered by `case_model_id`, `energy_test_id` or `label_status`.

A model that has been decertified is not dead; it re-enters the design loop through an engineering change, and there is no terminal state anywhere on the case-model lifecycle.

### 3.5. Refrigerant Transition and the Engineering-Change Cascade

Regulatory pressure toward lower global warming potential means a case design is rarely finished. A `DesignEngineer` opens an **engineering change** on a case model to re-charge it with a different blend, supplying the new `refrigerant_id`, the new nameplate `charge_grams`, the new `line_set_length_ft`, and a `change_note` recording why. The change is admissible from any of the six non-draft states, and every one of those six lands the model back in `DRAFT`: `MODELED → DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED_FOR_TEST → DRAFT`, `TESTED_PASS → DRAFT`, `TESTED_FAIL → DRAFT`, `CERTIFIED → DRAFT` and `DECERTIFIED → DRAFT` are all legal moves. Attempting it on a model already in `DRAFT` is a rank-7 `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. The supplied blend must permit the model's existing `system_type` in its `allowed_system_types`, else the rank-10 `ERR_REFRIGERANT_SYSTEM_TYPE_INCOMPATIBLE` (HTTP 422) applies, and the supplied `line_set_length_ft` must satisfy the rank-18 architecture rule of §3.2 — zero feet for a `SELF_CONTAINED` circuit, at least one foot for a `REMOTE` one — else `ERR_LINE_SET_NOT_PERMITTED`.

The change then executes a five-hop cascade, in order, inside the one request.

**Hop 1 — the design itself.** The model's `design_revision` is incremented by exactly 1, `submitted_by_user_id` is cleared to absent, `status` becomes `DRAFT`, and every computed airflow and charge field is cleared to absent: `curtain_factor`, `velocity_factor`, `infiltration_load_w`, `lighting_load_w`, `fan_load_w`, `defrost_load_w`, `anti_sweat_load_w`, `total_design_load_w`, `installed_charge_grams`, `nameplate_co2e_kg` and `installed_co2e_kg`. The model's `version` is incremented by 1. Because the airflow fields are gone, the engineer must run the airflow model again before the design can be re-submitted.

**Hop 2 — labels and running tests.** Because `design_revision` now exceeds the `pinned_design_revision` of any label written against the previous revision, every compliance label of this model whose `label_status` is `ISSUED` moves `ISSUED → SUPERSEDED`. Every energy test of this model whose status is `RUNNING` moves `RUNNING → INVALIDATED`, on the reasoning that a test physically running on a design that just changed is void; invalidation releases the chamber interval so a later test may occupy it. Energy tests whose status is `SCHEDULED` are **deliberately left in `SCHEDULED`** — they keep their chamber slot and are instead rejected at start time with `ERR_CASE_MODEL_REVISION_STALE`. Every row touched in this hop has its `version` incremented.

**Hop 3 — ordered lines.** Because the model no longer holds an issued label, `compliance_hold` is set to true on every store plan line that orders this model and whose owning plan's `production_status` is anything other than `COMPLETE`, and each touched plan's `held_line_count` is recomputed.

**Hop 4 — the commercial dimension.** Because `held_line_count` has become greater than zero, each affected plan whose `commercial_status` is `APPROVED` moves `APPROVED → HOLD_PENDING_RECERT`. A plan in `DRAFT` or `PRICED` is not moved by this hop; it simply carries held lines that will block its pricing.

**Hop 5 — queued production.** Because the plan entered `HOLD_PENDING_RECERT`, every work order of that plan whose status is `QUEUED` moves `QUEUED → HELD`. Work orders already in fabrication, in final QA, complete, shipped or scrapped are untouched; only the queue is frozen.

The cascade is written identically for every one of the six admissible starting states, but its later hops are naturally no-ops in some of them. Starting from `MODELED`, `SUBMITTED_FOR_TEST` or `TESTED_FAIL`, the model never held an issued label, so hops 2 through 5 typically do nothing except invalidate a `RUNNING` test where one exists. Starting from `TESTED_PASS`, the passing test keeps its `COMPLETED_PASS` status, but its `pinned_design_revision` no longer matches the model, so no label can ever be issued from it — the test is stranded rather than voided. Starting from `CERTIFIED` or `DECERTIFIED`, the full chain runs. The engineer's route back is the same in every case: run the airflow model, submit for test, schedule and run a new test, and have a compliance officer issue a fresh label, which as described in §3.4 unwinds the holds this change created.

### 3.6. Store Plans: Configuration, Pricing and Approval

A `SalesEngineer` opens a store plan for one remodel, declaring the retail `banner_name` ordering it, the banner-scoped `store_number`, the two-letter `region_code` used later to match install crews and parts depots, the remodel window boundaries `remodel_window_start` and `remodel_window_end` as a closed interval of ISO 8601 UTC days, and the `credit_limit_cents` ceiling that the plan's total may not exceed. A new plan is born with `commercial_status` `DRAFT` and `production_status` `NOT_RELEASED`; those two dimensions evolve independently for the rest of the plan's life and neither is ever terminal. Its counters start at zero — `line_count`, `unit_count`, `held_line_count`, `units_completed_count`, `units_shipped_count`, `warranty_reserve_accrual_cents` and `warranty_reserve_incurred_cents` — its `priced_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` are absent, and all seven pricing roll-up fields are absent.

Adding a **configured line** to the plan is how a store plan expresses a store's fixture layout. Each line names the `case_model_id` being ordered, the `length_ft` of case being ordered, the `quantity` of identical cases on that line (at least 1), an optional `end_cap` selection defaulting to `NONE` and drawn from `NONE`, `LEFT`, `RIGHT` and `BOTH`, and an optional `merchandising_options` array defaulting to empty and drawn from `LED_CANOPY`, `NIGHT_CURTAIN`, `GLASS_DOOR_UPGRADE`, `SHELF_LIGHTING` and `DIGITAL_PRICE_RAIL`. A new line's `unit_price_cents` and `extended_price_cents` are absent until the plan is priced, and its `compliance_hold` is false.

Four domain rules guard a new line, and they are evaluated in ascending rank order. At rank 11, the ordered model's installed charge basis must be at or below its blend's `max_charge_grams`, else `ERR_REFRIGERANT_CHARGE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). At rank 15, the ordered model's `status` must be exactly `CERTIFIED`, else `ERR_MODEL_NOT_CERTIFIED` (HTTP 422) — a design still in test, or one that has been decertified, cannot be sold. At rank 19, the requested `length_ft` must equal the ordered model's `nominal_length_ft`, because we build only the lengths a model is engineered for; a mismatch is `ERR_LENGTH_NOT_OFFERED` (HTTP 422). At rank 20, two option compatibilities are enforced: `GLASS_DOOR_UPGRADE` may only be ordered on a model whose `door_type` is `GLASS_DOOR`, and `NIGHT_CURTAIN` may only be ordered on a model whose `door_type` is `OPEN` — a night curtain is meaningless on a case that already has doors. Either violation is `ERR_OPTION_NOT_COMPATIBLE` (HTTP 422).

Adding a line always recomputes the plan's `line_count`, the number of lines attached to it, and `unit_count`, the sum of `quantity` across those lines, and always increments the plan's `version` by 1. Lines may be added while the plan's `commercial_status` is `DRAFT` or `PRICED`; from `APPROVED` or `HOLD_PENDING_RECERT` the attempt is a rank-7 `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409). Adding a line to a plan that has already been priced deliberately invalidates the quote: the plan moves `PRICED → DRAFT` and `subtotal_cents`, `volume_discount_bps`, `volume_discount_cents`, `net_cents`, `freight_cents`, `install_allowance_cents`, `total_cents` and `priced_by_user_id` are all cleared to absent, forcing a re-price. The commercial dimension is cyclic in exactly this way.

**Pricing** the plan takes no request body and moves it `DRAFT → PRICED`. It is admissible only from `DRAFT`, and only when the plan has at least one line — pricing an empty plan fails the transition's precondition and is reported as `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409). Two domain rules also apply: at rank 15 every line's ordered model must still be `CERTIFIED`, else `ERR_MODEL_NOT_CERTIFIED`, and at rank 16 no line of the plan may carry `compliance_hold` true and the plan's `held_line_count` must be zero, else `ERR_PLAN_ON_COMPLIANCE_HOLD` (HTTP 409). Pricing records the acting engineer's `X-User-ID` as `priced_by_user_id` and increments the plan's `version` by 1.

Pricing evaluates every line of the plan, processed in `created_at` ascending order and never filtered by any status, and computes a **per-case price** in four additive parts.

- The **base** is a per-linear-foot rate keyed on the ordered model's `temperature_class`, multiplied by the line's `length_ft`, multiplied by a fractional length tier keyed on that same `length_ft`, with the product rounded half up to whole cents. The per-foot rates are 78000 cents for an `HT` case, 96000 cents for `MT` and 124000 cents for `LT`. The length tiers price short cases at a premium and long cases at a discount: a 4-foot case carries a factor of one and fifteen hundredths, a 6-foot case one and six hundredths, an 8-foot case exactly one, and a 12-foot case ninety-four hundredths.
- The **options adder** is the sum of the per-option adders for every code in `merchandising_options`: 42000 cents for `LED_CANOPY`, 28500 for `NIGHT_CURTAIN`, 165000 for `GLASS_DOOR_UPGRADE`, 51500 for `SHELF_LIGHTING` and 73500 for `DIGITAL_PRICE_RAIL`. An empty option array contributes nothing.
- The **end-cap adder** is 87000 cents per finished side, multiplied by the number of sides the `end_cap` selection implies: zero sides for `NONE`, one side for `LEFT`, one side for `RIGHT`, and two sides for `BOTH`.
- The **system adder** is keyed on the ordered model's `system_type`: 118000 cents for a `REMOTE` case, whose condensing unit sits on a remote rack, and 320000 cents for a `SELF_CONTAINED` case, which carries its own.

Only the base term is rounded, because only the base term is fractional; the option, end-cap and system adders are exact integer cents added **after** that rounding. Rounding the four-part sum instead of the base term alone would produce a different number and is not permitted. The result is stored as `unit_price_cents`, and `extended_price_cents` is `unit_price_cents` multiplied by the line's `quantity`.

The plan roll-up then executes as a **six-step waterfall**, in this order and no other. The order is pinned because the discount tier is keyed on the subtotal while freight and the install allowance are keyed on the post-discount net, so reading the six equations as one simultaneous system would be circular.

1. `subtotal_cents` is the sum of `extended_price_cents` over the plan's lines, taken in `created_at` ascending order, each line's contribution already rounded by the per-case computation above — the subtotal is never re-derived from unrounded base terms. In the same step the plan's total linear footage is computed as the sum, over the plan's lines, of `length_ft` multiplied by `quantity`.
2. `volume_discount_bps` is selected from the **subtotal**, not from the total: it is 0 basis points when `subtotal_cents` is strictly below 5000000, 300 basis points when it is strictly below 10000000, 550 basis points when it is strictly below 20000000, and 800 basis points otherwise. The tier boundaries are strict, so a subtotal of exactly 5000000 cents lands in the 300-basis-point tier and a subtotal of exactly 20000000 cents lands in the 800-basis-point tier.
3. `volume_discount_cents` is `subtotal_cents` multiplied by `volume_discount_bps`, divided by ten thousand, rounded half up to whole cents.
4. `net_cents` is `subtotal_cents` less `volume_discount_cents`.
5. `freight_cents` is the **lesser** of two candidates: a per-foot tariff of 9500 cents multiplied by the plan's total linear footage, rounded half up to whole cents; and a cap of four hundredths of `net_cents`, likewise rounded half up to whole cents. Each candidate is rounded before the comparison.
6. `install_allowance_cents` is twenty-five thousandths of `net_cents`, rounded half up to whole cents, and `total_cents` is `net_cents` plus `freight_cents` plus `install_allowance_cents`.

**Approval** likewise takes no request body and moves the plan `PRICED → APPROVED`, recording the acting engineer's `X-User-ID` as `approved_by_user_id` and incrementing the plan's `version` by 1. Three domain rules apply in ascending rank order. At rank 9, the approver's `X-User-ID` must differ from `priced_by_user_id`, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). At rank 16, `held_line_count` must be zero, else `ERR_PLAN_ON_COMPLIANCE_HOLD` (HTTP 409). At rank 39 — the last rule in the whole ladder — `total_cents` must be at or below `credit_limit_cents`, else `ERR_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 402). Line listings can be filtered by `store_plan_id` or by `case_model_id`.

### 3.7. Plant Release and Fabrication Routing

Releasing an approved plan to the plant is a `PlantSupervisor` action, takes no request body, and moves the plan's production dimension `NOT_RELEASED → IN_PRODUCTION`. It requires the plan's `commercial_status` to be `APPROVED` and its `production_status` to be `NOT_RELEASED`; it also re-checks at rank 15 that every line's ordered model is still `CERTIFIED`, else `ERR_MODEL_NOT_CERTIFIED` (HTTP 422), and at rank 16 that `held_line_count` is zero, else `ERR_PLAN_ON_COMPLIANCE_HOLD` (HTTP 409). The plan's `version` is incremented by 1.

Release is the only way work orders come into existence — there is no create operation for them, and listing them is their only discovery channel. One work order is created per physical case: for each line, as many rows as its `quantity`, generated line by line in `created_at` ascending order across the plan's lines and, within each line, in ascending `unit_sequence` order. Each row copies the plan reference as `store_plan_id`, the line reference as `store_plan_line_id`, and the ordered model as `case_model_id`; its `shipment_id` is absent; its `unit_sequence` is a 1-based index within its own line, so a line of quantity three yields sequences 1, 2 and 3; and its `unit_price_cents` copies the line's `unit_price_cents` at the moment of release, which becomes the immovable basis of the warranty accrual even if the line is later re-priced. `rework_count` starts at 0, `warranty_start_date` is absent, and `status` is `QUEUED`. The `serial_number` is stamped at release and is composed of three dash-separated parts: the plan's `store_number`, then the rank of the owning line among the plan's lines ordered by `created_at` ascending (1 for the earliest-created line, 2 for the next, and so on), then the unit's `unit_sequence`.

Release also establishes the plan's **warranty accrual**. `warranty_reserve_accrual_cents` is the sum, over the plan's work orders whose status is anything other than `SCRAPPED` and taken in `created_at` ascending order, of 275 basis points of each work order's `unit_price_cents` — that is, the unit price multiplied by 275 and divided by ten thousand — with each work order's contribution rounded half up to whole cents **before** the summation.

Fabrication then advances one case at a time, each advance moving exactly one step and incrementing that work order's `version` by 1. A `QUEUED` unit moves `QUEUED → SHEET_METAL`, and this first step additionally requires the owning plan's `production_status` to be `IN_PRODUCTION`. From there a unit moves `SHEET_METAL → FOAM_INJECTION`. The routing then **branches on the ordered model's `door_type`**, and the two branches are strictly symmetric in what they demand and what they skip. A unit whose model's `door_type` is `GLASS_DOOR` moves `FOAM_INJECTION → DOOR_ASSEMBLY` and afterwards `DOOR_ASSEMBLY → FINAL_QA`. A unit whose model's `door_type` is `OPEN` moves `FOAM_INJECTION → FINAL_QA` directly, skipping `DOOR_ASSEMBLY` entirely. Both branches rejoin at `FINAL_QA`, and requesting the branch that does not match the model's door type is a rank-7 `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. A unit in `REWORK` re-enters fabrication at the beginning, moving `REWORK → SHEET_METAL`.

Recording the final QA outcome supplies a `result` of `PASS`, `FAIL` or `SCRAP`, plus an optional `defect_note`. All three outcomes are admissible only from `FINAL_QA` and each increments the unit's `version` by 1.

- A `PASS` moves the unit `FINAL_QA → COMPLETE` and recomputes the plan's `units_completed_count`, which counts the plan's work orders whose status is `COMPLETE` or `SHIPPED`.
- A `FAIL` moves the unit `FINAL_QA → REWORK` and increments its `rework_count` by 1, but only while `rework_count` is strictly below the rework limit of 2. A `FAIL` recorded on a unit whose `rework_count` already equals 2 is rejected at rank 25 with `ERR_REWORK_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422): that unit has exhausted its allowance and must be scrapped instead.
- A `SCRAP` moves the unit `FINAL_QA → SCRAPPED`, which is terminal, and recomputes the plan's `units_completed_count` with the scrapped unit excluded. Scrapping is permitted only when `rework_count` equals exactly 2; a `SCRAP` recorded on a unit that still has rework attempts left is rejected at rank 26 with `ERR_SCRAP_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 422). The two rules are exact complements — below the limit only `FAIL` is legal, at the limit only `SCRAP` is.

A unit may also be frozen from outside the routing. As described in §3.5 and §2.8, a `QUEUED` unit moves `QUEUED → HELD` when its plan's commercial dimension falls into `HOLD_PENDING_RECERT` in the same request, and a `HELD` unit moves `HELD → QUEUED` when recertification clears the plan's holds and `held_line_count` reaches zero. Work-order listings can be filtered by `store_plan_id`, `store_plan_line_id`, `case_model_id`, `shipment_id` or `status`.

### 3.8. Shipment Coordination and Installation Handoff

A `LogisticsCoordinator` builds a shipment against a plan by naming the `store_plan_id`, the `ship_date` on which the trailer leaves the plant, the `delivery_date` on which it arrives at the store, the freight `carrier_code`, and the `expected_store_plan_version` required by §2.7. The shipment is created in `STAGED`, with `handoff_at` and `received_by_name` both absent.

Three domain rules guard creation, in ascending rank order. At rank 16 the plan must carry no held line, else `ERR_PLAN_ON_COMPLIANCE_HOLD` (HTTP 409). At rank 27 the plan must have at least one work order in status exactly `COMPLETE` to load, else `ERR_NO_SHIPPABLE_UNITS` (HTTP 422). At rank 28 the `delivery_date` must fall inside the plan's closed remodel window running from `remodel_window_start` through `remodel_window_end` inclusive, and must also be at or after the `ship_date`; a violation of either half is `ERR_DELIVERY_OUTSIDE_REMODEL_WINDOW` (HTTP 422).

The **manifest** is assembled from the plan's work orders whose status is exactly `COMPLETE`, taken in `created_at` ascending order. Units that are `QUEUED`, anywhere in fabrication, `HELD`, already `SHIPPED` or `SCRAPPED` are excluded, so a plan with no completed units yields the empty set and trips the rank-27 rejection. The shipment's own `unit_count` is the number of units loaded, and its `linear_feet` is the sum, over those loaded units, of the `length_ft` of each unit's originating line — whole feet, integer counts.

Loading is a cascade in itself. Every loaded work order moves `COMPLETE → SHIPPED` and has its `shipment_id` set to the new shipment, with each unit's `version` incremented. The plan's `units_shipped_count` — the number of the plan's work orders whose status is `SHIPPED` — is recomputed, and when it equals the plan's `unit_count` the plan's production dimension moves `IN_PRODUCTION → COMPLETE`. The plan's `version` is incremented by 1 in every case, whether or not the production dimension moved.

Moving the trailer is a three-step walk, one step per request: `STAGED → LOADED`, then `LOADED → IN_TRANSIT`, then `IN_TRANSIT → DELIVERED`, each incrementing the shipment's `version` by 1.

**Cancelling** a shipment requires a `cancel_reason` and is admissible from `STAGED`, `LOADED` or `IN_TRANSIT` — never from `DELIVERED`, `HANDED_OFF` or `CANCELLED`. All three starting states are treated identically: `STAGED → CANCELLED`, `LOADED → CANCELLED` and `IN_TRANSIT → CANCELLED` each run the same five-hop cascade with no branch differences whatsoever. Hop 1 sets the shipment's status to `CANCELLED` and increments its `version`. Hop 2 moves every work order whose `shipment_id` is this shipment `SHIPPED → COMPLETE` and clears that `shipment_id` to absent, incrementing each unit's `version`. Hop 3 recomputes the plan's `units_shipped_count`, and because it has now fallen below the plan's `unit_count`, the plan's production dimension moves `COMPLETE → IN_PRODUCTION` with the plan's `version` incremented. Hop 4, firing because the plan left production `COMPLETE`, moves every install booking of this shipment whose status is `BOOKED` to `BOOKED → RELEASED`. Hop 5, firing because those bookings were released, decreases the reserved crew's `committed_case_days` by that booking's `cases_committed` multiplied by its `day_count`.

**Reserving an install crew** is the coordinator's other exclusive act: a booking names the `shipment_id` it will set, the `install_crew_id` reserved, the first install day `start_date`, the exclusive `end_date`, and `cases_committed`, the number of cases the booking consumes from that crew on every day of the interval (at least 1). The booking is created in `BOOKED`. Its `day_count` is the number of whole UTC days that fall at or after `start_date` and strictly before `end_date`; because the exclusive end must be strictly later than the start, `day_count` is always at least 1. The set of days a booking occupies is therefore the start day and each following day up to but not including the end day.

Four domain rules guard a booking, in ascending rank order. At rank 29 the `end_date` must be strictly after the `start_date`, else `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL` (HTTP 422). At rank 30 the booking must sit inside the delivery-and-remodel envelope: `start_date` must be at or after the shipment's `delivery_date`, and `end_date` must be at or before the day following the plan's `remodel_window_end` — the exclusive end is allowed to equal the day after the window's last day precisely so that a crew can work the final remodel day. A violation is `ERR_BOOKING_OUTSIDE_REMODEL_WINDOW` (HTTP 422). At rank 31 the `region_code` of the shipment's plan must be a member of the crew's `service_region_codes`, else `ERR_CREW_REGION_NOT_SERVED` (HTTP 422). At rank 32 comes the capacity test.

Crew capacity is an **aggregate per-day** constraint, not single occupancy: two bookings may share a day as long as the day's total stays within the crew's limit. For every day the candidate booking would occupy, the service computes that day's load as the candidate's own `cases_committed` plus the `cases_committed` of every other booking of the same crew whose status is `BOOKED` and whose occupied days include that day. If any single day's load strictly exceeds the crew's `daily_case_capacity`, the booking is rejected with `ERR_CREW_DAILY_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409). Bookings whose status is `RELEASED` or `COMPLETED` are excluded from the scan and free their days entirely. A successful booking increases the crew's `committed_case_days` by its `cases_committed` multiplied by its `day_count`; that counter is always the sum of `cases_committed` multiplied by `day_count` over the crew's `BOOKED` bookings.

Releasing a booking requires a `release_reason`, moves it `BOOKED → RELEASED`, decreases the crew's `committed_case_days` by that booking's `cases_committed` multiplied by its `day_count`, and increments the booking's `version` by 1. `RELEASED` and `COMPLETED` are both terminal for a booking.

The **handoff** is the only operation an `InstallContractor` may perform, and it is the moment the product changes hands. It supplies `received_by_name`, the name of the crew member who signed for the delivery, and moves the shipment `DELIVERED → HANDED_OFF`. Beyond the rank-3 crew identity test of §2.1, two domain rules apply. At rank 33, an install booking with status exactly `BOOKED` must exist for this shipment, else `ERR_INSTALL_BOOKING_NOT_READY` (HTTP 409) — which is why a contractor whose only booking on the shipment is `RELEASED` passes the identity test at rank 3 and still cannot complete the handoff. At rank 34, the current logical clock instant must fall at or after that booking's `start_date` and strictly before its `end_date`, else `ERR_HANDOFF_OUTSIDE_BOOKING_WINDOW` (HTTP 422).

A successful handoff writes four things. The shipment's `handoff_at` is set to the current logical clock instant and its `received_by_name` to the supplied value, with the shipment's `version` incremented by 1. That install booking moves `BOOKED → COMPLETED` and the crew's `committed_case_days` decreases by its `cases_committed` multiplied by its `day_count`. And every work order whose `shipment_id` is this shipment has its `warranty_start_date` set to that same logical instant — which is the only way a `warranty_start_date` is ever stamped, and therefore the only way a unit becomes eligible for warranty coverage at all. Shipment listings can be filtered by `store_plan_id` or `status`; booking listings by `shipment_id`, `install_crew_id` or `status`; crew records are read individually.

### 3.9. Warranty, Field Service and the Reserve Ledger

A `ServiceManager` opens a warranty claim against a delivered unit, naming the `work_order_id` the failure was reported against, the `part_inventory_id` the replacement part will come from, the `install_crew_id` of the field service crew assigned to the visit, the `claim_type` failure category, the `reported_date` on which the store reported the failure, the `requested_qty` of replacement parts (at least 1), and `estimated_cost_cents`, the field estimate of the total repair cost (at least zero). The claim is created in `OPEN`, with `parts_cost_cents`, `labor_cost_cents`, `total_cost_cents` and `denial_reason` all absent.

Two referential rules guard claim creation, in ascending rank order. At rank 35 the depot stock row's `case_model_id` must equal the claimed unit's `case_model_id`, else `ERR_PART_MODEL_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422) — we will not ship a dairy-multideck controller to fix a frozen coffin. At rank 36 the depot stock row's `region_code` must equal the `region_code` of the store plan behind the claimed unit, else `ERR_PART_REGION_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422), because a part must come out of the depot serving that store.

Creation evaluates the **coverage window**. The `warranty_term_months` is selected from the `claim_type`: a `COMPRESSOR_FAILURE` carries a five-year term of 60 months, while `DOOR_GASKET`, `CONTROLLER` and `GLASS_FOG` each carry a one-year term of 12 months. The coverage end is the unit's `warranty_start_date` advanced by that many whole months. The claim's `in_warranty` flag is true when all three of the following hold: the unit's `warranty_start_date` is present at all, the `reported_date` is at or after that start instant, and the `reported_date` is at or before the coverage end. The end boundary is inclusive, so a failure reported exactly at the coverage end is in warranty; and because `warranty_start_date` is only ever stamped by a shipment handoff, a unit that has never been handed off yields `in_warranty` false.

**Reserving parts** takes no request body and moves the claim `OPEN → PARTS_RESERVED`. Two rules apply in ascending rank order: at rank 37 the claim's `in_warranty` must be true, else `ERR_CLAIM_OUT_OF_WARRANTY` (HTTP 422), and at rank 38 the depot row's `available_qty` must be at or above the claim's `requested_qty`, else `ERR_PARTS_UNAVAILABLE` (HTTP 409). On success the claim's `parts_cost_cents` is fixed at the depot row's `unit_cost_cents` multiplied by `requested_qty`, the depot row's `reserved_qty` increases by `requested_qty`, and that row's `available_qty` and `below_min` are recomputed — `available_qty` being `on_hand_qty` less `reserved_qty`, and `below_min` being true exactly when `available_qty` is strictly less than `min_stock_qty`. The depot row's `reserved_qty` is always the sum of `requested_qty` over the claims in status `PARTS_RESERVED` against that row. The claim's `version` is incremented by 1.

**Resolving** the claim after the field repair supplies `labor_cost_cents`, the labor billed by the field service crew (at least zero), and moves the claim `PARTS_RESERVED → RESOLVED`. The claim's `total_cost_cents` is fixed at `parts_cost_cents` plus `labor_cost_cents`. The reserved parts are now consumed: both the depot row's `on_hand_qty` and its `reserved_qty` decrease by `requested_qty`, and `available_qty` and `below_min` are recomputed. Note that consuming stock leaves `available_qty` unchanged in net terms — the reservation had already withheld the units — while `on_hand_qty` genuinely falls. The claim's `version` is incremented by 1.

**Denying** a claim requires a `denial_reason`, which must be a non-empty string, and is admissible from either `OPEN` or `PARTS_RESERVED`, so both `OPEN → DENIED` and `PARTS_RESERVED → DENIED` are legal moves. Both paths record the reason, land the claim in the terminal state `DENIED`, and increment its `version` by 1. The two paths differ in exactly one respect, and the difference is a compensation: denying a claim that had already reserved parts decreases the depot row's `reserved_qty` by `requested_qty` while leaving `on_hand_qty` untouched, recomputes `available_qty` and `below_min`, and clears the claim's `parts_cost_cents` back to absent. Denying a claim still in `OPEN` touches no depot row, because nothing was ever reserved.

Behind all of this sits the plan-level **warranty reserve ledger**, which has two halves and is recomputed on plan release, on claim creation, on claim resolution and on claim denial. The accrual half, `warranty_reserve_accrual_cents`, is described in §3.7. The incurred half, `warranty_reserve_incurred_cents`, sums the claims reachable from this plan — that is, every claim whose `work_order_id` points at a work order whose `store_plan_id` is this plan — taken in `created_at` ascending order, and it recognises each claim according to its status. A claim in `RESOLVED` contributes its full `total_cost_cents`, the actual parts and labor. A claim in `OPEN` or `PARTS_RESERVED` contributes 60 hundredths of its `estimated_cost_cents`, rounded half up to whole cents, because the repair scope is not yet fixed. A claim in `DENIED` contributes exactly zero. Each claim's contribution is rounded before it enters the sum, never after. The practical consequence is that the incurred balance moves when a claim resolves even if the field estimate turned out to be exactly right, because the ledger switches from recognising 60 hundredths of an estimate to recognising the whole actual.

Depot listings can be filtered by `case_model_id` or `region_code`, and claim listings by `work_order_id`, `part_inventory_id`, `install_crew_id` or `status`.
