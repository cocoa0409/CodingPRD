# Ballot Measure Petition Signature Verification API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Direct democracy in this state rests on a single administrative promise: that when a proponent committee files boxes of petition sheets with the Secretary of State, the office can say — defensibly, and in court if necessary — exactly how many of those signatures were valid, which counties contributed enough of them, and what the taxpayer paid the county elections offices to find out. The initiative program that makes that promise today runs on spreadsheets passed between the division of elections, the Attorney General's title unit, and fifty-odd county clerks. Every statutory gate in the process — the single-subject rule, the two-round limit on format review, the circulator training and paid-disclosure requirements, the random-sample projection that decides whether a full check is needed, the supplemental "cure" window, and the challenge and court-review track — is presently enforced by a human reading a statute. This document specifies the API that replaces those spreadsheets.

The system serves seven distinct constituencies whose interests are deliberately not aligned. Proponent committees want their measures cleared and certified. County clerks want to be reimbursed for the lines they actually examined, and only those lines. Elections examiners want a verification record that survives a challenge, which means the sample draw must be reproducible from the filed data alone and never from a random number generator. Challengers want a documented window in which to contest a determination and escalate it to court. And the Secretary of State needs the whole apparatus to be reversible: an examination performed in error must be undoable without leaving drift in either the signature ledger or the money ledger. Segregation of duties is therefore a first-class requirement, not a policy note — the examiner who tallied the round that produced a petition's outcome may not be the one who signs the determination, and the examiner who opened a fraud referral may not be the one who adjudicates it.

Three technical constraints shape everything that follows. First, the service is deterministic: it reads a server-controlled **logical clock** rather than wall-clock time, so every deadline, window and expiry in the specification is testable. Second, all arithmetic is integer arithmetic — statutory percentages are carried as **basis points** (hundredths of a percent, so 10000 basis points is 100 percent), county reimbursement rates are carried in **millicents**, and money is stored in whole cents; there are no floating-point quantities anywhere in the contract. Third, every destructive operation has a documented compensating operation, and the compensation must restore the affected aggregates to byte-identical values. The endpoint surface described in §4 is mounted at the server root; there is no versioned or domain prefix.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

Everything in this section is a contract. Where §3 narrates workflows and may state a rule from more than one angle, §2 fixes the structural shape of requests, responses, identifiers, ordering and precision. An implementation that diverges here diverges everywhere.

### 2.1. Actors, Identity Headers and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint — reads included — must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting principal. `X-User-Role` names the role that principal is acting in. If either header is absent, or is present but holds an empty string, the request is refused with `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS` and HTTP 401; no further processing of any kind occurs. If `X-User-Role` carries a value outside the seven roles enumerated below, the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ROLE`, also HTTP 401. Only once the role is recognised does the server ask whether that role is listed among the acting endpoint's authorised roles in §4; a recognised role attempting an operation it is not authorised for is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` and HTTP 403.

The seven roles, spelled exactly as they must appear in `X-User-Role`, are `SosAdmin`, `ElectionsExaminer`, `TitleExaminer`, `Proponent`, `CountyClerk`, `CirculatorAgent`, and `Challenger`.

- A `SosAdmin` is the Secretary of State's initiative program administrator. It owns statutory configuration, clearance for circulation, cure periods, batch invalidation and reinstatement, round voiding, circulator discipline, and entry of court rulings.
- An `ElectionsExaminer` belongs to the division of elections. It runs format review, opens and tallies verification rounds, opens and adjudicates fraud referrals, and may issue determinations for petitions it did not itself tally.
- A `TitleExaminer` sits in the Attorney General's office and is the only role permitted to write a petition's official title and summary.
- A `Proponent` is an initiative proponent committee. It files and submits its own petitions, may register circulators, may create and terminate circulation assignments, and may file batches and individual lines on a circulator's behalf.
- A `CountyClerk` is a county elections office. It verifies batch affidavits, strikes format-defective lines, validates individual signature lines against its own county roll, and may open fraud referrals.
- A `CirculatorAgent` is the registered circulator acting for itself: completing training, filing its disclosure and its affidavit, and filing the batches and lines it circulated.
- A `Challenger` is any elector or party contesting a pending determination. It files a challenge and escalates it to court review.

How `X-User-ID` binds to data differs by role, and this binding is what every ownership check compares against.

When `X-User-Role` is `CountyClerk`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `County`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `County` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a `CountyClerk` may only act on a `SignatureBatch` whose `county_id` equals `X-User-ID`, on a `SignatureRecord` whose owning batch has a `county_id` equal to `X-User-ID`, and on a `VoterRecord` whose `county_id` equals `X-User-ID`.

When `X-User-Role` is `CirculatorAgent`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Circulator`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Circulator` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. A `CirculatorAgent` may act on a `Circulator` only when that circulator's `id` equals `X-User-ID`, and may create a `SignatureBatch` only when the submitted `circulator_id` equals `X-User-ID`.

When `X-User-Role` is `SosAdmin`, `ElectionsExaminer`, `TitleExaminer`, `Proponent`, or `Challenger`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — for example as `proponent_user_id` on a `Petition` at filing, as `challenger_user_id` on a `Challenge` at filing, as `tallied_by_examiner_id` on a `VerificationRound` at tally, and as `opened_by_examiner_id` and `adjudicated_by_examiner_id` on a `FraudReferral` — but it never compares that identifier to any entity's `id`. Two of those recorded values do carry ownership weight of their own: a `Proponent` may act on a `Petition` only when the petition's `proponent_user_id` equals `X-User-ID`, and a `Challenger` may escalate a `Challenge` only when that challenge's `challenger_user_id` equals `X-User-ID`.

An ownership violation is refused with HTTP 403. The generic signal is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` — a `CountyClerk` reaching for another county's batch, line or voter roll, a `CirculatorAgent` reaching for another circulator, a `Proponent` reaching for another committee's petition. Challenge escalation has its own dedicated signal, `ERR_NOT_CHALLENGE_OWNER`, for the case where the escalating `Challenger` is not the one who filed. A third 403 signal, `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, is described in §2.12.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two query parameters that control the window returned. `limit` sets the maximum number of items in the page; it defaults to 10 when omitted, with a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100. `offset` sets the number of items to skip before the window starts; it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is a request-shape violation and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and HTTP 400.

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, list endpoints return their resources sorted by creation timestamp in ascending order.

List responses populate four fields inside the `meta` object of the envelope: `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more`. Single-resource responses omit all four.

All other query parameters on list endpoints are filters. A filter narrows the result set to rows whose corresponding field equals the supplied value; supplying several filters narrows on all of them together. The available filters per endpoint are the non-pagination query parameters named in the discussions in §3 and reachable through the paths in §4.

### 2.4. The Logical Election Clock

Nothing in this system reads wall-clock time. Deadlines, circulation windows, signing windows, assignment intervals, cure expiry and challenge review windows all compare against a single server-held **logical clock**, and that clock only moves when a `SosAdmin` moves it by supplying a `current_utc` value — an ISO 8601 UTC instant — to the clock-setting capability described in §4. Before the first such call the clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; it never falls back to real time, and it never advances on its own.

Every system-assigned instant in the data model is stamped from this clock: a petition's `cleared_at`, a batch's `filed_at`, a circulator's `training_completed_at` and `affidavit_filed_at`, a cure period's `opened_at`, and a challenge's `filed_at`. Moving the clock re-evaluates every subsequent window comparison, so a test can walk a petition past its circulation deadline simply by advancing the clock. Instants already stamped on stored resources are never rewritten when the clock moves.

### 2.5. The Jurisdiction Standard: Statutory Parameters and Fixed Gates

A `VerificationStandard` encodes one jurisdiction's statutory arithmetic and is registered by a `SosAdmin` before any petition can be filed against it. It carries a `jurisdiction_code` of between 2 and 8 characters that must be unique across all standards; a `gubernatorial_vote_total` of at least 1, being the votes cast for governor at the last general election and the base of the signature formula; and `signature_pct_bps`, from 1 to 10000, the share of that total required as valid signatures.

Sampling is parameterised by `sample_pct_bps`, from 1 to 10000, the share of the accepted pool drawn into a random-sample round, and `sample_min_count`, at least 1, the statutory floor on sample size. `stratification_mode` selects which of the two documented projection shapes applies and is either `POOLED` or `STRATIFIED`. The sufficiency bands are set by `sufficiency_upper_bps`, which must be at least 10000, and `sufficiency_lower_bps`, from 1 to 10000. County distribution is set by `county_distribution_pct_bps`, from 1 to 10000, the share of a county's registered voters that county must contribute, and `required_qualifying_counties`, at least 1, the number of counties that must qualify before certification is permitted.

Four further parameters govern the supplemental and circulation windows. `cure_allowed` is a boolean that defaults to `true` and states whether a supplemental signature period exists in this jurisdiction at all. `cure_window_days` is a whole number of days of at least 1 and defaults to 30. `cure_eligibility_floor_bps` defaults to 8000 and expresses, in basis points of the required signature threshold, how much a petition must already hold to deserve a cure at all. `circulation_window_days` is a whole number of days of at least 1 and defaults to 180. Finally, county reimbursement is priced by `verification_rate_millicents_per_signature`, at least 1, paid per signature examined, and `county_surcharge_bps`, from 0 to 10000, an administrative surcharge applied to the discounted invoice subtotal.

A handful of gates are set by statute rather than by jurisdiction, so they are fixed constants of the system and appear in no request body:

- A petition may pass through at most **2** format review rounds; the round counter is compared strictly against that ceiling.
- A challenge may be escalated to court only within **15** days of filing.
- A new circulation assignment must clear every other live interval of the same circulator by at least **3** days on each side.
- A single substantiated misconduct finding — a threshold of **1** — is enough to suspend a circulator and to bar its reinstatement.
- Only two training courses are on the approved list: `SOS-CIRC-101` and `SOS-CIRC-201`.
- A petition may hold at most **2** cure periods over its lifetime.
- When a cure target is computed, the observed validity rate used in the extrapolation is clamped to a floor of **2500** basis points and a ceiling of 10000 basis points.
- County reimbursement discounting has two tiers, entered at gross amounts of **20** cents and **100** cents respectively, discounting **1000** and **1500** basis points.
- Circulator credibility scoring weights the observed invalid rate at **5000** basis points before any misconduct is proven and at **10000** basis points afterwards, charges **500** basis points for each still-open referral in the unproven case and **2000** basis points for each substantiated strike in the proven case, and never reports a score below a floor of **1000** basis points.

### 2.6. Basis Points, Cents, Millicents and Rounding Discipline

Every proportion in this specification is an integer count of basis points, where 10000 basis points is exactly 100 percent and 1 basis point is one hundredth of one percent. Every stored monetary amount — `verification_cost_cents` on a batch, `accrued_cost_cents` on a round, `verification_cost_accrued_cents` on a petition, and the whole invoice breakdown — is an integer count of whole cents. County reimbursement is quoted in millicents, where 1000 millicents is one cent.

The rounding direction is not uniform, and it is not negotiable. It is fixed per computation as follows, and no computation may substitute a different direction:

- The required signature threshold rounds **up** to the next whole signature: a fractional signature always counts as a whole one.
- Sample sizing rounds the percentage draw **up**, while the systematic interval derived from the pool size rounds **down**.
- Every validity rate expressed in basis points rounds **down**, including each per-county rate computed inside a stratified projection before the strata are summed.
- The upper sufficiency gate rounds **up**; the lower sufficiency gate rounds **down**. Both comparisons against those gates are inclusive.
- Each county's distribution minimum rounds **up**; each county's credited projection rounds **down**; the qualification comparison is inclusive.
- Batch verification cost converts millicents to cents with **half-up** rounding, and does so once per batch — never once per round and never once per petition. Because rounding happens at the batch boundary, two batches of two examined lines each accrue more than one batch of four examined lines would at the same rate. At a rate of 4750 millicents per signature the per-batch amounts are 5 cents for one line, 10 cents for two, 14 cents for three and 19 cents for four; two batches of two lines therefore accrue 20 cents in total, whereas rounding a four-line total in one step would have produced 19.
- On the reimbursement invoice the discount rounds **down** and the surcharge rounds **up**.
- The cure target rounds **up**, and the clamp on the assumed validity rate is applied before the division, not after.
- The weighted invalid-rate term of the credibility score rounds **down**, and the result is then clamped into the inclusive range from the credibility floor to 10000.

### 2.7. Common Resource Properties

Nine of the twelve resource types in this system — `Petition`, `Circulator`, `CirculationAssignment`, `SignatureBatch`, `SignatureRecord`, `VerificationRound`, `CurePeriod`, `Challenge` and `FraudReferral` — carry the full set of system-managed properties:

- `id` (string): a server-minted unique identifier formatted as a UUID, with no type prefix of any kind.
- `created_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, for example `2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z`, recording when the resource was first created.
- `updated_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format, refreshed on every mutation of the resource — including mutations that only touch derived counters such as a petition's `accepted_raw_total` or a circulator's `credibility_score_bps`.
- `version` (integer): a server-managed counter that increments by one on every mutation of the resource, derived-counter mutations included. It is the value clients echo back for the optimistic concurrency checks of §2.9.

The three configuration types — `VerificationStandard`, `County` and `VoterRecord` — are different, and the difference is deliberate. Each carries only `id` and `created_at`. Neither `updated_at` nor `version` exists on them, and neither field appears in their responses. This holds even for `VoterRecord`, whose `registration_status` is mutable through a dedicated write: moving a voter between registration states changes the outcome of every later signature validation against that voter, but it does not stamp an `updated_at` and does not bump a `version`, because those fields are absent from the resource.

All datetime fields anywhere in the system — whether client-supplied, such as `effective_from`, `effective_to`, `signed_on` and `registration_date`, or server-stamped — are ISO 8601 UTC instants.

### 2.8. Left-to-Right Identifier Resolution

Only three paths in this API nest a child collection under a parent: opening a verification round, opening a cure period, and filing a challenge are all addressed under the petition that owns them. Everywhere else, relationships travel as foreign keys in the request body and are read back through filtered list endpoints.

Where a path does carry more than one identifier, the server resolves them strictly left to right and rejects on the **first** one that fails to resolve, with HTTP 404 and the not-found code belonging to that identifier's type. Path resolution always precedes body validation: a request naming a nonexistent petition is refused with `ERR_PETITION_NOT_FOUND` before any body field is inspected. The twelve not-found signals are `ERR_PETITION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CIRCULATOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BATCH_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SIGNATURE_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ROUND_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CURE_PERIOD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CHALLENGE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FRAUD_REFERRAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VERIFICATION_STANDARD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COUNTY_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_VOTER_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`, and they carry the same meaning whether the failing identifier came from the path or from the body. The twelve identifiers a path may carry are `standard_id`, `county_id`, `voter_record_id`, `petition_id`, `circulator_id`, `assignment_id`, `batch_id`, `signature_record_id`, `round_id`, `cure_period_id`, `challenge_id` and `referral_id`, each naming the resource of its own type.

Body foreign keys are resolved in a fixed order too — the order in which the fields are introduced across the data model: `standard_id`, then `petition_id`, then `circulator_id`, then `county_id`, then `batch_id`, then `cure_period_id`, and finally each entry of `assigned_county_codes` against a registered county code. That ordering governs which 404 surfaces when a single request names two nonexistent parents. It applies to petition filing, assignment creation, batch filing, voter-record creation, fraud-referral creation, and verification-round creation. A `cure_period_id` that resolves to a cure period belonging to a different petition is treated as unresolvable and reported as `ERR_CURE_PERIOD_NOT_FOUND`; a `county_code` entry in `assigned_county_codes` that matches no registered county is reported as `ERR_COUNTY_NOT_FOUND`.

### 2.9. Optimistic Concurrency: Rejecting Writes Made From a Stale View

Two writes in this system are legitimate to repeat. A revised official title and summary properly supersedes an earlier prepared pair while the petition sits in `TITLE_PENDING`, and a circulator may properly hold several assignments across different petitions and non-overlapping windows. Neither can therefore be refused by any state precondition, which means neither is protected from a client that read the resource, went away, and wrote back on top of a change it never saw. Both are guarded instead by an explicit version echo.

`PUT /petitions/{petition_id}/title-summary` requires `expected_petition_version` in its request body. The field is not optional — omitting it is a request-shape violation. Its value must equal the target petition's current `version`. If another writer has advanced the petition's `version` since the client last read it, the write is refused with `ERR_STALE_PETITION_VERSION` and HTTP 412, and neither `official_title` nor `official_summary` is touched. On success the petition's `version` increments by one, so a client that intends to revise the title twice must re-read the petition between the two writes.

`POST /circulation-assignments` requires `expected_circulator_version` in its request body, likewise non-optional, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `Circulator` named by `circulator_id`. A mismatch with that circulator's current `version` refuses the create with `ERR_STALE_CIRCULATOR_VERSION` and HTTP 412. A successful create increments the circulator's `version` by one, which is precisely why a client scripting several assignments for one circulator must re-read the circulator between them.

No other endpoint in this system takes a version field, and no other endpoint performs a version check. Where a caller supplies neither field on an endpoint that does not require one, nothing happens; where a caller omits a required one on either of the two endpoints above, the request is a shape violation.

### 2.10. Cascades, Compensation and the Two Conserved Ledgers

The petition is the reconciliation point of the whole system. Its derived counters — `raw_signature_count`, `accepted_raw_total`, `projected_valid_count`, `verified_valid_count`, `qualifying_county_count`, `verification_cost_accrued_cents`, `voided_signature_count` and `cure_period_count` — are never patched incrementally from a diff the caller supplies. Whenever an operation anywhere below the petition changes a batch status, a line's validation outcome, or a stored batch cost, the affected counters are recomputed in full from the surviving data. A recomputation is a mutation of the petition: it refreshes `updated_at` and increments `version` even when the recomputed number happens to be unchanged.

Cascades in this system run downward through as many as four levels and are described in full in §3. Two of them are worth naming here because they are the deepest: revoking a circulator terminates its assignments, invalidates the batches under those assignments, voids the lines in those batches, and then reconciles the counters of every petition thereby touched; substantiating a fraud referral invalidates the referred batch, voids its lines, charges the circulator a misconduct strike, may suspend the circulator, and thereby suspends that circulator's active assignments. Both cascades are transitive in one direction only — a cascade never runs back upward from a child to re-trigger its parent's own transition.

Two quantities are treated as conserved, and their conservation is the acceptance criterion for the compensating operations:

- The **signature ledger**, whose conserved quantity is `accepted_raw_total`. Invalidating a batch and then reinstating it must return `accepted_raw_total` to the identical value it held before the invalidation, along with `verified_valid_count`, `projected_valid_count`, `voided_signature_count` and `qualifying_county_count`.
- The **cost ledger**, whose conserved quantity is `verification_cost_accrued_cents`. Because each batch stores the cost that was actually charged for it in `verification_cost_cents`, invalidation subtracts exactly the stored amount rather than a freshly recomputed one, and reinstatement adds exactly the amount that was subtracted. When a batch is examined a second time — first sampled, then fully checked — the ledger moves by the **difference** between the newly computed batch cost and the stored one, never by the whole recomputed amount.

Restoration is verbatim, not approximate. A batch records the status it held immediately before invalidation in `status_before_invalidation` and each of its lines records the validation outcome it held immediately before voiding in `status_before_void`; reinstatement restores both from those recorded values and then clears them. Voiding a verification round likewise restores each batch the round touched to the status that batch held before the round examined it.

### 2.11. Order of Evaluation for Rejections

When a single request violates more than one rule, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fully determined. The server evaluates in the following order and stops at the first failure. This ordering is normative: an implementation that reports a later failure while an earlier one is also present is wrong, because the earlier check is what the caller is entitled to see first.

1. **Headers present.** Both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` present and neither an empty string, else `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Role recognised.** `X-User-Role` is one of `SosAdmin`, `ElectionsExaminer`, `TitleExaminer`, `Proponent`, `CountyClerk`, `CirculatorAgent`, `Challenger`, else `ERR_INVALID_ROLE` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Role authorised.** The acting role appears in the endpoint's authorised-role list, else `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
4. **Request shape.** Every required input field present and non-null; every enum value within its declared set; every integer within its declared range; every array within its declared cardinality and free of duplicate entries; every datetime a valid ISO 8601 UTC instant; `effective_to` strictly after `effective_from`; `limit` between 1 and 100 and `offset` not negative. Any failure is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Applies to every endpoint.
5. **Path identifiers resolve.** Left to right, as described in §2.8, each failure yielding that type's not-found code (404). Applies to every endpoint carrying a path parameter.
6. **Body foreign keys resolve.** In the declared order of §2.8 (404). Applies to petition filing, assignment creation, batch filing, voter-record creation, fraud-referral creation, and verification-round creation.
7. **Ownership.** The ownership comparisons of §2.1, yielding `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`, or `ERR_NOT_CHALLENGE_OWNER` for challenge escalation (403). Applies to every mutating endpoint reachable by a `CountyClerk`, `CirculatorAgent`, `Proponent` or `Challenger`.
8. **Segregation of duties.** `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403), as described in §2.12. Applies to issuing a petition determination and to adjudicating a fraud referral.
9. **Optimistic concurrency.** `expected_petition_version` against the petition's `version` on title-summary preparation, and `expected_circulator_version` against the circulator's `version` on assignment creation, yielding `ERR_STALE_PETITION_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_CIRCULATOR_VERSION` (412). Applies to those two endpoints only.
10. **State preconditions.** The target resource is in a state that admits the requested transition — petition status gates, batch status gates with their dedicated already-terminal signal, round status gates and the one-open-round-per-petition rule, circulator registration gates, challenge status gates, the requirement that a referral sit with the Attorney General before adjudication, and the rule that a petition may hold at most one non-`NONE` challenge dimension at a time. The signals are `ERR_INVALID_PETITION_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_BATCH_STATE`, `ERR_BATCH_ALREADY_TERMINAL`, `ERR_INVALID_ROUND_STATE`, `ERR_ROUND_ALREADY_OPEN`, `ERR_INVALID_CIRCULATOR_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_CHALLENGE_STATE`, `ERR_REFERRAL_NOT_WITH_AG` and `ERR_CHALLENGE_ALREADY_FILED`, all HTTP 409. Applies to every state-advancing endpoint.
11. **Uniqueness.** A standard's `jurisdiction_code`, a county's `county_code` and a voter's `voter_id_number` are each unique across the whole system, and a line's `line_number` is unique within its batch. The signals are `ERR_DUPLICATE_JURISDICTION_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_COUNTY_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_VOTER_ID_NUMBER` and `ERR_DUPLICATE_LINE_NUMBER`, all HTTP 409. Applies to standard registration, county registration, voter-record creation and adding a line to a batch.
12. **Statutory eligibility and compliance.** The single-subject rule at format approval; official title and summary present before clearance; the training course on the approved list; training complete before the affidavit; the paid-circulator disclosure on file before the affidavit; the affidavit sworn; the batch circulator registered; a revoked circulator blocking every write that touches it or its work; a circulator carrying a substantiated strike barred from reinstatement. The signals are `ERR_SINGLE_SUBJECT_VIOLATION`, `ERR_TITLE_SUMMARY_MISSING`, `ERR_TRAINING_COURSE_NOT_APPROVED`, `ERR_TRAINING_NOT_COMPLETE`, `ERR_PAID_DISCLOSURE_MISSING`, `ERR_AFFIDAVIT_NOT_SWORN`, `ERR_CIRCULATOR_NOT_REGISTERED`, `ERR_CIRCULATOR_REVOKED` and `ERR_CIRCULATOR_REINSTATEMENT_BARRED`, all HTTP 422. Applies to format decision, clearance for circulation, training, affidavit filing, circulator reinstatement, batch filing, and batch reinstatement.
13. **Cross-entity linkage.** The batch circulator holds an `ACTIVE` assignment for the batch petition; the batch county's code appears in that assignment's `assigned_county_codes`; a supplemental batch names an `OPEN` cure period of the same petition and a non-supplemental batch names none; the line under validation is in an open round's sample; every entry of `format_defect_line_numbers` matches a line of that batch; a batch named by a substantiated fraud referral cannot be reinstated. The signals are `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_ASSIGNMENT`, `ERR_COUNTY_NOT_IN_ASSIGNMENT`, `ERR_SUPPLEMENTAL_WITHOUT_CURE_PERIOD`, `ERR_CURE_PERIOD_NOT_OPEN`, `ERR_SIGNATURE_NOT_IN_OPEN_ROUND`, `ERR_LINE_NUMBER_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_FRAUD_SUBSTANTIATED_BATCH`, all HTTP 409. Applies to batch filing, batch affidavit verification, batch reinstatement, and line validation.
14. **Windows and intervals.** Signatures may only be filed while the logical clock is at or before the circulation deadline; each line's `signed_on` must lie inside the petition's circulation window and, for a supplemental batch, additionally inside its cure period's window; a new assignment interval must clear the circulator's other live intervals by the statutory buffer on both sides; a new cure period must not overlap an existing one of the same petition; a cure period may only be expired at or after its deadline; a challenge may only be escalated strictly before its review deadline and may only be filed while the petition holds a pending determination. The signals are `ERR_CIRCULATION_DEADLINE_PASSED`, `ERR_SIGNED_OUTSIDE_WINDOW`, `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, `ERR_CURE_PERIOD_OVERLAP`, `ERR_CURE_PERIOD_NOT_EXPIRED`, `ERR_CHALLENGE_REVIEW_WINDOW_EXPIRED` and `ERR_CHALLENGE_WINDOW_CLOSED`, all HTTP 409. Applies to filing signatures, adding a line to a batch, assignment creation, cure-period creation, cure-period expiry, challenge filing, and challenge escalation.
15. **Counting and quota gates, evaluated last.** Format review rounds not exhausted; cure period count below the statutory ceiling and the petition above the cure eligibility floor; the eligible pool non-empty at round creation; no selected line still `PENDING` at tally; at least one affidavit-verified batch before filing; at least one line in a batch before affidavit verification; county distribution satisfied before certification; no pending challenge and no open fraud referral before any determination. The signals are `ERR_FORMAT_REVIEW_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`, `ERR_CURE_LIMIT_REACHED`, `ERR_CURE_NOT_ELIGIBLE`, `ERR_EMPTY_ELIGIBLE_POOL`, `ERR_ROUND_HAS_PENDING_SIGNATURES`, `ERR_NO_VERIFIED_BATCHES`, `ERR_BATCH_EMPTY`, `ERR_COUNTY_DISTRIBUTION_UNMET`, `ERR_CHALLENGE_PENDING` and `ERR_FRAUD_REFERRAL_OPEN`, all HTTP 409. Applies to petition submission, cure-period creation, verification-round creation, round tally, filing signatures, batch affidavit verification, and issuing a determination.

The practical consequence worth internalising is that authorisation always precedes shape, shape always precedes existence, existence always precedes ownership, ownership precedes concurrency, concurrency precedes state, and state precedes every business rule. Within group 15, note in particular that a pending challenge or an open fraud referral is checked at determination time alongside the county distribution gate rather than as a state precondition, because the challenge dimension advances independently of the petition's lifecycle status.

### 2.12. Segregation of Duties

Two operations in this system may not be performed by the same operator who performed the operation that produced them.

Issuing a petition's determination compares the acting `X-User-ID` against the `tallied_by_examiner_id` of that petition's latest closed verification round. If they are equal, the determination is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` and HTTP 403 — the examiner who counted may not be the examiner who certifies or fails. Adjudicating a fraud referral compares the acting `X-User-ID` against that referral's `opened_by_examiner_id`, with the same code and status when they match: the examiner who alleged the misconduct may not be the one who finds it substantiated. Both bars apply to `SosAdmin` and `ElectionsExaminer` alike, because both roles may reach both operations; the bar is on the identifier, not on the role.

"Latest closed round" throughout this document means the petition's verification round whose status is `CLOSED` with the highest `round_index`, and where two closed rounds somehow share a `round_index`, the more recently created of the two. Rounds in `VOIDED` status are invisible to this selection, so voiding the round that was tallied by a given examiner frees that examiner to issue the determination once a different round has been closed.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Configuring a Jurisdiction: Standards, Counties and the Voter Roll

Nothing else in the system can exist until a `SosAdmin` has laid down the statutory arithmetic. Registering a `VerificationStandard` — whose parameters are enumerated in §2.5 — is the first act of program setup, and a second attempt to register a standard under a `jurisdiction_code` that is already taken is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_JURISDICTION_CODE`. Four of the sixteen parameters are optional on the way in: omitting `cure_allowed` accepts `true`, omitting `cure_window_days` accepts 30, omitting `cure_eligibility_floor_bps` accepts 8000, and omitting `circulation_window_days` accepts 180. The other twelve — `jurisdiction_code`, `gubernatorial_vote_total`, `signature_pct_bps`, `sample_pct_bps`, `sample_min_count`, `sufficiency_upper_bps`, `sufficiency_lower_bps`, `stratification_mode`, `county_distribution_pct_bps`, `required_qualifying_counties`, `verification_rate_millicents_per_signature` and `county_surcharge_bps` — must all be supplied. Standards are readable individually and as a list, and the list can be narrowed by `jurisdiction_code` or by `stratification_mode`.

County elections offices come next. Each `County` carries a `county_code` of between 2 and 12 uppercase characters, unique across all counties and refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_COUNTY_CODE` on collision; a display `name` for the office; and a `registered_voter_count` of at least 1, which is the base of that county's distribution minimum. That code is not decoration: it is the canonical key that must appear in a circulation assignment's `assigned_county_codes`, and it is the secondary sort key in every ordering rule the verification engine applies. Counties are readable individually and as a list, which may be narrowed by `county_code`.

The voter roll is the ground truth against which every signature is eventually judged. A `VoterRecord` is added by a `SosAdmin` or by the `CountyClerk` whose county it belongs to, and carries a `county_id` naming the county whose roll it appears in, a `voter_id_number` that must be unique across the entire state roll — a collision is `ERR_DUPLICATE_VOTER_ID_NUMBER` — the voter's registered `full_name`, a `signature_on_file_hash` holding the hash of the exemplar signature the county has on file, and a `registration_date` recording the ISO 8601 UTC instant at which the registration took effect. A voter's `registration_status` may be supplied at creation and accepts `ACTIVE` when omitted; the permitted values are `ACTIVE`, `INACTIVE`, `CANCELLED` and `PENDING`, and only `ACTIVE` will ever count a signature toward validity. Voter records are readable individually and as a list narrowable by `county_id`, `voter_id_number` or `registration_status`.

Rolls move. A voter dies, moves away, or re-registers, and the county needs to record that without rewriting history. Moving a voter between the four registration states is therefore a dedicated write, taking a single `registration_status`, available to a `SosAdmin` and to the owning `CountyClerk`. It changes the outcome of every signature validation performed against that voter **after** the change, and leaves every already-validated line exactly as it was: a line found `VALID` against an `ACTIVE` voter stays `VALID` when the voter is later moved to `CANCELLED`. Because `VoterRecord` is a configuration resource, this write neither stamps an `updated_at` nor bumps a `version` — see §2.7.

### 3.2. From Draft to Cleared: Format Review, Single Subject, Title and Clearance

A `Proponent` opens a measure by filing a petition, supplying the `standard_id` of the jurisdiction standard whose formula will govern it, the `proponent_name` of the committee as filed, the `submitted_title` the committee wrote for itself, and `measure_subject_codes` — an array of between 1 and 5 entries with no duplicates, each drawn from `TAX`, `ELECTIONS`, `EDUCATION`, `HEALTH`, `ENVIRONMENT` and `PUBLIC_SAFETY`. The server records the filer's identifier in `proponent_user_id`, which is thereafter the only thing that lets that committee touch this petition. A fresh petition begins with `status` at `DRAFT`, `challenge_status` at `NONE`, `format_review_round` at 0, and every derived counter at 0 — `required_valid_signatures`, `raw_signature_count`, `accepted_raw_total`, `projected_valid_count`, `verified_valid_count`, `qualifying_county_count`, `verification_cost_accrued_cents`, `voided_signature_count` and `cure_period_count` all start there — with `official_title`, `official_summary`, `cleared_at` and `circulation_deadline` all null. Petitions are readable individually and as a list narrowable by `standard_id`, by `status`, or by `challenge_status`.

Submitting the petition for review moves it from `DRAFT` to `FORMAT_REVIEW`, and the same act moves it from `FORMAT_REJECTED` back to `FORMAT_REVIEW` — this is the revision cycle. Submission takes no body. It requires `measure_subject_codes` to hold between 1 and 5 distinct valid codes, and it requires `format_review_round` to be strictly less than the statutory ceiling of two rounds. Because the counter is incremented by every format decision, whether the examiner approves or rejects, a committee gets two bites: submit, rejection, submit, and then the third submission is refused with `ERR_FORMAT_REVIEW_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED` because `format_review_round` has reached two.

An `ElectionsExaminer` records the outcome of format review by supplying a `decision` of either `APPROVE` or `REJECT`, optionally accompanied by a `reviewer_note`. A `REJECT` decision moves the petition from `FORMAT_REVIEW` to `FORMAT_REJECTED` and increments `format_review_round` by one. An `APPROVE` decision moves it from `FORMAT_REVIEW` to `TITLE_PENDING` and likewise increments `format_review_round` by one — the counter tracks rounds consumed, not rejections. Approval carries the single-subject rule with it: the petition's `measure_subject_codes` must hold exactly one code at the moment of approval, and a petition still carrying two or more subject codes is refused with `ERR_SINGLE_SUBJECT_VIOLATION`. A petition may be rejected for other reasons with several codes on file; it can only clear format review with one.

Title preparation belongs to the `TitleExaminer` alone, and it is a self-transition: the petition sits in `TITLE_PENDING` before the write and remains in `TITLE_PENDING` after it. The examiner supplies an `official_title`, an `official_summary`, and the `expected_petition_version` demanded by §2.9. A second preparation legitimately supersedes the first — which is exactly why no state precondition can protect this write and why the version echo is the only guard available. Each successful preparation increments the petition's `version` by one.

Clearance for circulation is a `SosAdmin` act, moves the petition from `TITLE_PENDING` to `CLEARED`, and takes no body. Both `official_title` and `official_summary` must be non-null by then; a petition sent for clearance while either is still null is refused with `ERR_TITLE_SUMMARY_MISSING`. Clearance does three things at once. It stamps `cleared_at` from the logical clock. It sets `circulation_deadline` to `cleared_at` advanced by the standard's `circulation_window_days` whole days — one hundred and eighty days of street circulation by default. And it freezes the signature threshold: `required_valid_signatures` is set to the standard's `gubernatorial_vote_total` multiplied by its `signature_pct_bps`, divided by 10000, with any fractional remainder rounded **up** to the next whole signature. Exactly one standard participates, resolved through the petition's `standard_id` at clearance time, and the value is frozen: editing the standard afterwards never moves an already-cleared petition's threshold.

### 3.3. Circulator Registration and Authorisation to Circulate

Registering a circulator is open to a `SosAdmin` and to a `Proponent`. The registration carries the circulator's legal `full_name` and a `residency_state_code` of exactly two uppercase characters as sworn in the registration. Two further fields describe compensation: `is_paid` accepts `false` when omitted, and `employer_name`, naming the employer or contractor paying the circulator, accepts null when omitted — but a null `employer_name` is only tenable while `is_paid` is `false`. A new circulator begins with `registration_status` at `PENDING_TRAINING`, with `training_course_code`, `training_completed_at`, `affidavit_filed_at` and `disclosure_document_id` all null, with `misconduct_strike_count` at 0, and with `credibility_score_bps` at its ceiling of 10000, because a circulator with nothing yet examined has nothing held against it. Circulators are readable individually and as a list narrowable by `registration_status` or by `is_paid`.

`paid_disclosure_on_file` is a derived flag and the gate on the whole paid-circulator disclosure regime. It is `true` when `is_paid` is `false`; it is also `true` when `is_paid` is `true` and both `employer_name` and `disclosure_document_id` are non-null; in every other case — that is, a paid circulator missing either the employer or the filed document — it is `false`. Filing the disclosure is a dedicated write available to a `SosAdmin` and to the `CirculatorAgent` acting for itself: it supplies the `disclosure_document_id` identifying the filed paid-circulator disclosure document and may supply an `employer_name` at the same time, and it recomputes `paid_disclosure_on_file` immediately.

Training comes before the affidavit, and only two courses count. Recording training moves the circulator from `PENDING_TRAINING` to `TRAINED`, stamps `training_completed_at` from the logical clock, and records the supplied `training_course_code`; a code outside the approved pair `SOS-CIRC-101` and `SOS-CIRC-201` is refused with `ERR_TRAINING_COURSE_NOT_APPROVED`, and attempting to record training for a circulator that has already moved past `PENDING_TRAINING` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CIRCULATOR_STATE`.

Filing the sworn affidavit completes registration, moving the circulator from `TRAINED` to `REGISTERED` and stamping `affidavit_filed_at` from the logical clock. Three gates guard it, and each has its own signal. The circulator must actually be in `TRAINED`, else `ERR_TRAINING_NOT_COMPLETE`. `paid_disclosure_on_file` must be `true`, else `ERR_PAID_DISCLOSURE_MISSING` — the case a paid circulator hits when either `employer_name` or `disclosure_document_id` is still null. And the request's `sworn` flag must be `true`, else `ERR_AFFIDAVIT_NOT_SWORN`.

Only a `REGISTERED` circulator can be authorised to circulate a particular measure, and that authorisation is a `CirculationAssignment` created by a `SosAdmin` or a `Proponent`. It names the `petition_id` it authorises work on and the `circulator_id` it authorises, bounds the work with an `effective_from` and an `effective_to` that must be strictly after it, and lists the `assigned_county_codes` the circulator may collect in — between 1 and 10 entries, no duplicates, each matching a registered county's `county_code`. The proponent's acknowledgement that it reviewed the paid-circulator disclosure travels as `paid_disclosure_ack`, which accepts `false` when omitted. The `expected_circulator_version` demanded by §2.9 also rides on this request. An assignment begins at `ACTIVE`; assignments are readable individually and as a list narrowable by `petition_id`, `circulator_id` or `status`.

An assignment **occupies** an interval on the circulator, and occupation is what prevents one person from being sworn to two overlapping circulation windows. The occupied interval is the closed span from `effective_from` through `effective_to`, extended by a three-day clearance on each side. A new assignment must clear every interval already occupied by an `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` assignment of the same circulator by at least those three days on both sides; a request that fails to is refused with `ERR_ASSIGNMENT_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Intervals of `TERMINATED` assignments do not occupy anything, which is the point of termination: ending an assignment early — permitted from `ACTIVE` and from `SUSPENDED`, by a `SosAdmin` or a `Proponent`, with an optional `reason` — moves it to `TERMINATED`, releases its interval so that a later overlapping assignment becomes admissible, and blocks any further batch filing under it, while leaving the circulator's own registration and every batch already filed completely untouched.

### 3.4. Filing Sheets: Batches, Lines and County Affidavit Verification

Signatures reach the state in **batches** — a bundle of petition sheets sworn by one circulator and filed with one county elections office. A `CirculatorAgent` or a `Proponent` files a batch naming the `petition_id` the sheets belong to, the `circulator_id` who swore the batch affidavit, the `county_id` of the clerk who will verify them, and an `affidavit_signed` attestation that the batch affidavit has been executed. Two further fields, `is_supplemental` and `cure_period_id`, belong to the supplemental regime and are discussed in §3.7. Filing stamps `filed_at` from the logical clock — the secondary key of the duplicate keeper ranking — and starts the batch at `RECEIVED` with `raw_count` 0, `accepted_raw_count` 0, `format_defect_count` 0, `checked_signature_count` 0 and `verification_cost_cents` 0, with `affidavit_notary_id`, `status_before_invalidation` and `invalidation_reason` all null. Batches are readable individually and as a list narrowable by `petition_id`, `circulator_id`, `county_id`, `cure_period_id`, `status` or `is_supplemental`.

Filing is heavily gated, because a batch is the point at which circulator eligibility becomes a signature-counting question. The named circulator's `registration_status` must be exactly `REGISTERED`; anything else — still `PENDING_TRAINING`, merely `TRAINED`, or `SUSPENDED` — is refused with `ERR_CIRCULATOR_NOT_REGISTERED`, and a circulator whose registration has reached `REVOKED` is refused with `ERR_CIRCULATOR_REVOKED`. That circulator must hold an `ACTIVE` `CirculationAssignment` for this petition; if none was ever created, or the only one is `SUSPENDED` or `TERMINATED`, the filing is refused with `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_ASSIGNMENT`. And the county the sheets are being filed with must be covered: the `county_code` of the batch's county must appear in that assignment's `assigned_county_codes`, else `ERR_COUNTY_NOT_IN_ASSIGNMENT`.

Individual signature lines are then added to a batch, one request per line, by a `CirculatorAgent` or a `Proponent`, and only while the batch is still `RECEIVED` — adding a line to a batch whose affidavit has already been verified is refused with `ERR_INVALID_BATCH_STATE`. Each line carries a `line_number` of at least 1 giving its position on the sheet, which must be unique within its batch or the request is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_LINE_NUMBER`; the `signer_name` as signed; the `claimed_voter_id_number` written on the line, which is what will later be matched against the roll; a `signed_on` instant; and a `signature_hash` of the signature image on the line. Each line begins with `validation_status` at `PENDING`, `in_sample` `false`, and `matched_voter_record_id`, `duplicate_of_signature_id`, `validated_in_round_id`, `status_before_void` and `void_reason` all null, with `manual_match_override_applied` `false`. Adding a line increments the owning batch's `raw_count` by one and recomputes the petition's `raw_signature_count`.

`signed_on` is the field that enforces the circulation window on a per-line basis, and it does so more strictly than the deadline check at filing time. Every line's `signed_on` must fall inside the petition's window, from `cleared_at` through `circulation_deadline` inclusive at both ends. For a line on a supplemental batch there is a second, tighter constraint described in §3.7. A line signed outside its applicable window is refused with `ERR_SIGNED_OUTSIDE_WINDOW`. `signed_on` is also the **primary** key of every ordering rule in this system: pool ordering, duplicate keeper ranking, and therefore the identity of every sampled line, all begin with it.

Affidavit verification is the county clerk's act and is where a batch stops being paper and becomes countable signatures. The clerk supplies the `affidavit_notary_id` recorded at verification and may supply `format_defect_line_numbers`, an array of the line numbers being struck as format-defective. The batch must be in `RECEIVED`, and it must carry at least one line — verifying the affidavit of a batch with no lines at all is refused with `ERR_BATCH_EMPTY`. Every entry of `format_defect_line_numbers` must match a `line_number` actually present in this batch; an entry that matches none is refused with `ERR_LINE_NUMBER_NOT_FOUND`. On success each listed line's `validation_status` becomes `INVALID_FORMAT_DEFECT` and `format_defect_count` is set to how many were struck; `accepted_raw_count` becomes `raw_count` less `format_defect_count`; and `affidavit_notary_id` is recorded.

Which state the batch lands in depends on whether anything survived. If at least one line survives, the batch moves from `RECEIVED` to `AFFIDAVIT_VERIFIED` and its accepted lines join the petition's countable pool. If `format_defect_line_numbers` covers every line in the batch — that is, the number of entries equals the batch's `raw_count` — the batch moves from `RECEIVED` to `REJECTED_FORMAT`, which is terminal, and `accepted_raw_count` is set to 0. A `REJECTED_FORMAT` batch keeps its struck lines on file, which produces a deliberate asymmetry worth stating plainly: those sheets were physically filed, so they still count toward the petition's `raw_signature_count`, but they contribute nothing to `accepted_raw_total`.

Those two petition-level counters are recomputed in full on every event that can move them — a line being added, an affidavit being verified, signatures being filed, a batch being invalidated or reinstated, and every cascade that changes a batch status. `raw_signature_count` is the sum of `raw_count` across all of the petition's batches whose `status` is anything other than `INVALIDATED`. `accepted_raw_total` is the sum of `accepted_raw_count` across only those batches whose `status` is `AFFIDAVIT_VERIFIED`, `SAMPLED` or `FULLY_CHECKED`. A batch's `accepted_raw_count` is exactly 0 while it sits in `RECEIVED`, `REJECTED_FORMAT` or `INVALIDATED`, so those three contribute nothing on the accepted side. Alongside them, `voided_signature_count` is recomputed as the count of the petition's lines whose `validation_status` is `VOIDED`. None of these three is ever patched incrementally.

Closing circulation is the proponent's final act before verification begins, and it moves the petition from `CLEARED` to `SIGNATURES_FILED` with no body at all. The logical clock must be at or before `circulation_deadline`; a filing attempted when the clock has moved strictly past that instant is refused with `ERR_CIRCULATION_DEADLINE_PASSED`. At least one of the petition's batches must have reached `AFFIDAVIT_VERIFIED`, else the filing is refused with `ERR_NO_VERIFIED_BATCHES` — a petition holding nothing but `RECEIVED` and `REJECTED_FORMAT` batches has nothing to verify. Filing recomputes `raw_signature_count` and `accepted_raw_total`, and also recomputes `qualifying_county_count` for the first time.

### 3.5. Verification Rounds: Building the Pool, Drawing the Sample, and Validating Lines

Verification proceeds in **rounds**, each opened by an `ElectionsExaminer` against a petition and each carrying a `round_type` of `RANDOM_SAMPLE`, `FULL_CHECK` or `CURE_RECHECK`. A round may optionally name a `cure_period_id`, which is required for and only meaningful to the cure recheck discussed in §3.7. Rounds are assigned a 1-based `round_index` in creation order within their petition, begin at `OPEN`, and are readable individually and as a list narrowable by `petition_id`, `cure_period_id`, `round_type`, `status` or `determination`. A petition may have at most one round `OPEN` at a time: opening a second while one is still `OPEN` is refused with `ERR_ROUND_ALREADY_OPEN`.

Opening a round drives the petition's lifecycle. From `SIGNATURES_FILED`, a `RANDOM_SAMPLE` round moves the petition to `SAMPLE_VERIFICATION` and a `FULL_CHECK` round moves it directly to `FULL_CHECK`. Once in `FULL_CHECK`, a further `FULL_CHECK` round is a self-transition that keeps the petition in `FULL_CHECK` — this is how the state is reached after a random sample landed in the middle band, and it additionally requires the eligible pool to be non-empty, refusing with `ERR_EMPTY_ELIGIBLE_POOL` when it is not. Once in `CURE_PERIOD`, a `CURE_RECHECK` round naming an `OPEN` cure period of that same petition is likewise a self-transition that keeps the petition in `CURE_PERIOD`.

The **eligible pool** is the set of lines a round is entitled to examine, and its definition is where the supplemental regime bites. A line is eligible when its batch's `status` is one of `AFFIDAVIT_VERIFIED`, `SAMPLED` or `FULLY_CHECKED`; its own `validation_status` is still `PENDING`; and its `validated_in_round_id` is null, meaning no earlier round has already claimed it. For a `RANDOM_SAMPLE` round and for a `FULL_CHECK` round there is one further exclusion: the line's batch must have `is_supplemental` equal to `false`, so supplemental sheets are invisible to both. A `CURE_RECHECK` round drops that exclusion and reaches supplemental lines and any still-unexamined ordinary lines alike. Whatever the type, an empty pool refuses the round with `ERR_EMPTY_ELIGIBLE_POOL`, and the pool's size is recorded on the round as `eligible_pool_size`.

Pool order is pinned before anything is drawn, because a challenge in court will ask the state to reproduce the draw from the filed data. Lines are ordered by `signed_on` ascending; where those tie, by the `county_code` of the line's batch's county ascending; where those tie, by the batch's `filed_at` ascending; and where those still tie, by `line_number` ascending. No identifier and no random value participates in the ordering, in the draw, or in any tie-break anywhere in this system.

Sizing the draw depends on the round type. A `FULL_CHECK` or `CURE_RECHECK` round takes the whole pool, so its size equals the pool size. A `RANDOM_SAMPLE` round takes the larger of the standard's `sample_min_count` and the pool size multiplied by `sample_pct_bps` divided by 10000 with any remainder rounded **up**, and then caps that at the pool size — a statutory minimum can never demand more lines than exist. The draw itself is a systematic interval walk: the interval is the pool size divided by the chosen size with any remainder discarded, and the round selects the line at position zero, then the line one interval further along, and so on for as many lines as the chosen size, counting positions from zero. The number of lines actually selected is recorded as `sample_size`, and every selected line has `in_sample` set to `true` and `validated_in_round_id` set to the round.

Where the governing standard's `stratification_mode` is `STRATIFIED`, that whole procedure — ordering, sizing and the interval walk — is performed separately inside each county stratum, with the strata visited in `county_code` ascending order and the per-stratum selections concatenated. The consequence is that `sample_min_count` is applied once per county rather than once per petition, and stratified draws are therefore larger. With two lines in each of two counties, a `sample_pct_bps` of 5000 and a `sample_min_count` of 2, a `POOLED` standard draws 2 lines in total while a `STRATIFIED` standard draws 4.

Validating a line is the `CountyClerk`'s work, one line at a time, and the clerk may only reach lines whose batch was filed with its own county. A line is only validatable while it is genuinely under examination: `in_sample` must be `true`, `validated_in_round_id` must name a round that is still `OPEN`, and the line's own `validation_status` must still be `PENDING`. A line that is not in a sample, or whose round has already been closed or voided, or that already carries a non-`PENDING` outcome, is refused with `ERR_SIGNATURE_NOT_IN_OPEN_ROUND`. The request body carries at most one field, the optional `manual_match_override` flag.

The per-line checks run as a cascade in a fixed order, and the first one that fails determines the outcome — which matters, because a line can fail several at once and only one outcome is recorded. First, the line's `claimed_voter_id_number` is resolved against the roll's `voter_id_number`. If no roll entry exists at all, the line becomes `INVALID_NOT_REGISTERED` and `matched_voter_record_id` stays null. Second, if a roll entry was found but its `registration_status` is anything other than `ACTIVE`, the line likewise becomes `INVALID_NOT_REGISTERED`, but `matched_voter_record_id` is set to the record that was found. Third, if the matched voter's `county_id` differs from the batch's `county_id`, the line becomes `INVALID_WRONG_COUNTY` with `matched_voter_record_id` set — the voter is real and active, just registered somewhere else. Fourth, the line's `signature_hash` is compared with the matched voter's `signature_on_file_hash`; if they differ and `manual_match_override` is absent or `false`, the line becomes `INVALID_SIGNATURE_MISMATCH` with `matched_voter_record_id` set, whereas if the clerk supplied `manual_match_override` as `true` the mismatch is accepted, `manual_match_override_applied` is set to `true` on the line, and the cascade continues.

Duplicate detection runs **last**, which is why a line that is both unregistered and duplicated is reported as `INVALID_NOT_REGISTERED` rather than as a duplicate. The candidate set is every line of the same petition whose `matched_voter_record_id` equals this line's matched voter, whose `validation_status` is either `PENDING` or already `VALID`, and whose batch's `status` is neither `REJECTED_FORMAT` nor `INVALIDATED` — so lines struck as format defects, lines already resolved to some other invalid reason, and lines sitting in a rejected or invalidated batch are all excluded. That exclusion is precisely what makes the outcome independent of the order in which the clerk happens to validate two competing lines. The candidates are ranked by `signed_on` ascending, then by their batch's `filed_at` ascending, then by `line_number` ascending, and the first-ranked candidate is the **keeper**. If the line being validated is the keeper, it becomes `VALID` and `matched_voter_record_id` is set. If it is not, it becomes `INVALID_DUPLICATE`, `matched_voter_record_id` is set, and `duplicate_of_signature_id` is set to the keeper's identifier — a self-referential pointer from the loser to the survivor. Ties beyond that triple cannot arise, because `line_number` is unique within a batch. The keeper is never chosen by identifier.

Every validation, whatever its outcome, also recomputes the credibility score of the circulator who swore the batch; that scoring rule lives in §3.9. Lines are readable individually and as a list narrowable by `batch_id`, `matched_voter_record_id`, `duplicate_of_signature_id`, `validated_in_round_id`, `validation_status` or `in_sample`.

### 3.6. Closing a Round: Projection, Sufficiency Bands, County Distribution and County Reimbursement

Tallying is the single densest operation in the system. An `ElectionsExaminer` closes a round, moving it from `OPEN` to `CLOSED`, and in doing so computes the round's arithmetic, advances the batches the round touched, accrues county reimbursement, recomputes the petition's counters and advances the petition's lifecycle. The round must be `OPEN` — tallying one that is not is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ROUND_STATE` — and every line the round selected must have moved off `PENDING`, else the tally is refused with `ERR_ROUND_HAS_PENDING_SIGNATURES`. The acting operator's identifier is recorded on the round as `tallied_by_examiner_id`, which is the value the segregation-of-duties bar of §2.12 later reads.

Two figures are computed for every round type. `sampled_valid_count` is the number of the round's selected lines whose `validation_status` is `VALID` at tally. `validity_rate_bps` is `sampled_valid_count` multiplied by 10000, divided by the round's `sample_size`, with any remainder discarded — a basis-point rate recorded for audit on every round regardless of type.

`projected_valid_count` is where the two documented variants of the standard diverge, and the divergence is the whole point of stratification. For a `RANDOM_SAMPLE` round under a `POOLED` standard, the projection is the petition's `accepted_raw_total` multiplied by the round's `validity_rate_bps`, divided by 10000, with the remainder discarded: one statewide rate extrapolated across the whole accepted pool, so a county with a poor validity rate is diluted by better counties. For a `RANDOM_SAMPLE` round under a `STRATIFIED` standard, the projection is instead the sum, taken across the participating counties in `county_code` ascending order, of each county's own accepted line total multiplied by that county's own rate and divided by 10000 with the remainder discarded, where the county's rate is that county's valid selected lines multiplied by 10000 and divided by that county's selected line count, remainder discarded. Because the remainder is discarded inside each stratum before the strata are summed, a poor county drags only its own share. For identical data — two accepted lines in each of two counties, one line in the first found invalid — a `POOLED` standard yields 4 and a `STRATIFIED` standard yields 3. Note that the round's own `validity_rate_bps` remains the round-level rate even under a stratified projection, so the audit field and the projection can legitimately disagree.

For a `FULL_CHECK` or `CURE_RECHECK` round nothing is projected at all: the result is a face-value count. `projected_valid_count` becomes the number of the petition's lines whose `validation_status` is `VALID` across every batch whose `status` is `AFFIDAVIT_VERIFIED`, `SAMPLED` or `FULLY_CHECKED`, supplemental batches included, and the petition's `verified_valid_count` is set to that same number. `verified_valid_count` is written only by `FULL_CHECK` and `CURE_RECHECK` tallies; a random-sample tally leaves it alone and writes only `projected_valid_count`. Conversely, `projected_valid_count` on the petition is refreshed from the latest closed round whenever `accepted_raw_total` changes, which is what makes invalidating a batch move a projection that no new examination produced.

The `determination` follows immediately from the projection, and it is banded. Two gates are derived from the petition's frozen `required_valid_signatures`: the upper gate is that requirement multiplied by the standard's `sufficiency_upper_bps` divided by 10000, with any remainder rounded **up**; the lower gate is the requirement multiplied by `sufficiency_lower_bps` divided by 10000, with any remainder discarded. For a `RANDOM_SAMPLE` round, a `projected_valid_count` at or above the upper gate yields `SUFFICIENT`; one at or below the lower gate yields `INSUFFICIENT`; anything strictly between the two yields `FULL_CHECK_REQUIRED`. Both comparisons are inclusive, so a projection landing exactly on the lower gate is `INSUFFICIENT` and one landing exactly on the upper gate is `SUFFICIENT`. For a `FULL_CHECK` or `CURE_RECHECK` round there is no middle band: the face-value count at or above `required_valid_signatures` yields `SUFFICIENT` and anything below it yields `INSUFFICIENT`. Only a `RANDOM_SAMPLE` round can ever return `FULL_CHECK_REQUIRED`, and a round's `determination` sits at `PENDING` until it is tallied.

The determination then drives the petition. From `SAMPLE_VERIFICATION`, a `SUFFICIENT` determination advances the petition to `PENDING_CERTIFICATION`, and a `FULL_CHECK_REQUIRED` determination advances it to `FULL_CHECK`; either way `projected_valid_count` is copied onto the petition from the round. An `INSUFFICIENT` determination splits on cure eligibility: a cure-eligible petition advances to `CURE_PERIOD`, and one that is not cure-eligible advances to `PENDING_FAILURE`. From `FULL_CHECK`, a `SUFFICIENT` determination advances the petition to `PENDING_CERTIFICATION`, setting both `verified_valid_count` and `projected_valid_count` to the face-value count and recomputing `qualifying_county_count`; an `INSUFFICIENT` determination sets `verified_valid_count` to the face-value count and then advances to `CURE_PERIOD` when the petition is cure-eligible or to `PENDING_FAILURE` when it is not. Cure eligibility is a conjunction of three conditions, all of which must hold: the standard's `cure_allowed` is `true`; the petition's `cure_period_count` is strictly less than the statutory ceiling of two; and the best available valid count multiplied by 10000 is at or above `required_valid_signatures` multiplied by the standard's `cure_eligibility_floor_bps`. Failing any one of the three makes the petition not cure-eligible.

Tallying also advances the batches the round examined, and this is the fork-join shape of the batch lifecycle. A batch in `AFFIDAVIT_VERIFIED` moves to `SAMPLED` when a `RANDOM_SAMPLE` round selected at least one of its lines. A batch in `AFFIDAVIT_VERIFIED` moves straight to `FULLY_CHECKED` when a `FULL_CHECK` or `CURE_RECHECK` round covered every remaining line of it. And a batch already in `SAMPLED` moves to `FULLY_CHECKED` when a later `FULL_CHECK` round covers its remaining lines — the join point of the diamond, reached only after a random sample returned `FULL_CHECK_REQUIRED` and a full check followed.

County reimbursement accrues at exactly those moments, once per batch the round touched. A batch's `checked_signature_count` is the number of its lines whose `validation_status` is neither `PENDING` nor `INVALID_FORMAT_DEFECT` — format-defect lines were struck before any examiner ever looked at them and are never billed, and lines still pending were never examined. The batch's `verification_cost_cents` is `checked_signature_count` multiplied by the standard's `verification_rate_millicents_per_signature`, divided by 1000 to convert millicents into cents, with the result rounded **half up**, and that conversion happens once per batch as described in §2.6. The ledger then moves by a **delta**: the difference between the newly computed `verification_cost_cents` and the amount already stored on the batch is added both to the petition's `verification_cost_accrued_cents` and to the round's `accrued_cost_cents`. At the join point — a batch re-examined from `SAMPLED` to `FULLY_CHECKED` — the ledger must move by that difference alone and never by the whole recomputed amount, or the county would be paid twice for the lines the sample already covered. The round's `accrued_cost_cents` is the sum of the deltas that round accrued, and it is the exact amount subtracted back if the round is ever voided.

Every tally recomputes `qualifying_county_count`, and county distribution compliance is also recomputed when signatures are filed, whenever a batch is invalidated or reinstated, and on demand through the petition's distribution report. Only counties reachable from the petition's non-terminal batches participate: a county whose only batch is `REJECTED_FORMAT` or `INVALIDATED` drops out of the numerator and out of the denominator alike, so it neither qualifies nor counts against the petition. For each county holding at least one batch of this petition in `AFFIDAVIT_VERIFIED`, `SAMPLED` or `FULLY_CHECKED`, that county's **minimum** is its `registered_voter_count` multiplied by the standard's `county_distribution_pct_bps`, divided by 10000, with any remainder rounded **up**. That county's **credited** count depends on the petition's latest closed round: when the petition has no closed round at all the credited count is 0; when the latest closed round is a `RANDOM_SAMPLE` round the credited count is that county's accepted line total multiplied by the applicable rate — that county's own stratum rate under a `STRATIFIED` standard, or the round's `validity_rate_bps` under a `POOLED` one — divided by 10000 with the remainder discarded; and when the latest closed round is a `FULL_CHECK` or `CURE_RECHECK` round the credited count is simply the number of `VALID` lines of this petition in that county. A county qualifies when its credited count is at or above its minimum, so a county sitting exactly on its minimum qualifies. `qualifying_county_count` is the number of counties that qualify. Because the credited count is derived from surviving batches, invalidating a single batch can un-qualify a county and block a petition that had already reached its statewide total.

The distribution report is a computed read available to a `SosAdmin`, an `ElectionsExaminer`, a `Proponent` or a `Challenger`. It returns the `petition_id`, the standard's `required_qualifying_counties`, the petition's `qualifying_county_count`, and a `counties` array in which each element carries that county's `county_code`, its `distribution_minimum`, its `credited_count`, and a `qualifies` flag.

The reimbursement invoice is a separate computed read, available to a `SosAdmin`, an `ElectionsExaminer` or a `CountyClerk`, and it is tiered. Its `gross_cents` is the sum of `verification_cost_cents` across only those batches of the petition whose `status` is `SAMPLED` or `FULLY_CHECKED`; a batch still merely `AFFIDAVIT_VERIFIED` never reached an examiner and an `INVALIDATED` batch was struck, so both are excluded even where they still hold a non-zero stored cost. The `tier` is keyed on `gross_cents` **before** any discount: a gross at or above 100 cents is `TIER_B` with a `discount_bps` of 1500, a gross at or above 20 cents but below 100 is `TIER_A` with a `discount_bps` of 1000, and anything below 20 cents is `TIER_NONE` with a `discount_bps` of 0. A gross of exactly 20 cents therefore selects `TIER_A`; the boundary is inclusive. `discount_cents` is `gross_cents` multiplied by `discount_bps` divided by 10000 with the remainder discarded; `subtotal_cents` is `gross_cents` less `discount_cents`; `surcharge_cents` is `subtotal_cents` multiplied by the standard's `county_surcharge_bps` divided by 10000 with any remainder rounded **up**; and `total_cents` is `subtotal_cents` plus `surcharge_cents`. The response returns the `petition_id` alongside `gross_cents`, `tier`, `discount_bps`, `discount_cents`, `subtotal_cents`, `surcharge_cents` and `total_cents`. Worked at a `county_surcharge_bps` of 250: a gross of 10 cents gives `TIER_NONE`, a discount of 0, a subtotal of 10, a surcharge of 1 and a total of 11; a gross of 20 cents gives `TIER_A`, a discount of 2, a subtotal of 18, a surcharge of 1 and a total of 19. The same four examined lines rounded in one step instead of once per batch would have produced a gross of 19, which slips below the tier-A floor and yields a total of 20; skipping the discount entirely would yield 21.

### 3.7. Cure Periods and Supplemental Signatures

Where a jurisdiction allows it, a petition that fell short but not hopelessly short gets a second chance to gather signatures. Opening a `CurePeriod` is a `SosAdmin` act against a petition sitting in `CURE_PERIOD`, takes no request body, and is gated four ways. The standard's `cure_allowed` must be `true` and the petition's best available valid count must clear the cure eligibility floor, both refused with `ERR_CURE_NOT_ELIGIBLE`; the petition's `cure_period_count` must be strictly below the statutory ceiling of two, refused with `ERR_CURE_LIMIT_REACHED`; and the new period must not overlap any existing cure period of the same petition, refused with `ERR_CURE_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. Cure periods are readable individually and as a list narrowable by `petition_id` or `status`.

Opening a period stamps `opened_at` from the logical clock and sets `deadline_at` to `opened_at` advanced by the standard's `cure_window_days` whole days — thirty days by default. The pair is treated as a **half-open** interval: `opened_at` is inclusive, `deadline_at` is exclusive, and it is those half-open intervals that the overlap check compares. Opening a period increments the petition's `cure_period_count` by one, and that counter counts periods in any status, expired and closed ones included.

The period also computes the target the proponent must hit. The single latest closed verification round of the petition is consulted — voided rounds are invisible here, so voiding the round that triggered the cure and closing a fresh one changes the target. The best available valid count is that round's `projected_valid_count` when the round was a `RANDOM_SAMPLE` round, and the petition's `verified_valid_count` otherwise. `shortfall_at_open` is then `required_valid_signatures` less that best available count, floored at zero so it is never negative. `assumed_validity_rate_bps` is the round's `validity_rate_bps` clamped into the inclusive range from 2500 basis points to 10000 basis points — the floor exists so that a pathological observed rate of zero cannot produce an infinite target. And `cure_target_raw_signatures`, the number of raw supplemental lines the proponent must file, is `shortfall_at_open` multiplied by 10000, divided by `assumed_validity_rate_bps`, with any remainder rounded **up**; the clamp is applied before the division, not after. A shortfall of 1 at an observed rate of 2500 basis points therefore yields a target of 4 raw signatures, while the same shortfall at 5000 basis points yields 2.

Supplemental sheets are filed as ordinary batches with two extra fields set: `is_supplemental` as `true` and `cure_period_id` naming the period. The pairing is strict in both directions — a batch declaring `is_supplemental` as `true` with no `cure_period_id`, and a batch supplying a `cure_period_id` while `is_supplemental` is `false`, are both refused with `ERR_SUPPLEMENTAL_WITHOUT_CURE_PERIOD`. The named period must belong to the same petition and must still be `OPEN`; a supplemental batch naming a period in any other status is refused with `ERR_CURE_PERIOD_NOT_OPEN`. Lines on a supplemental batch face a second temporal constraint on top of the petition's circulation window: each `signed_on` must additionally fall inside the period's half-open interval, at or after `opened_at` and strictly before `deadline_at`, or the line is refused with `ERR_SIGNED_OUTSIDE_WINDOW`. The period tracks what has arrived in `supplemental_raw_count`, the sum of `raw_count` across every batch naming this `cure_period_id` whose `status` is neither `REJECTED_FORMAT` nor `INVALIDATED`.

Closing the cure is done by a `CURE_RECHECK` round, whose `cure_period_id` names the period and whose pool reaches supplemental and still-unexamined ordinary lines alike. A `SUFFICIENT` determination on that round moves the period from `OPEN` to `CLOSED_SUFFICIENT`, sets the petition's `verified_valid_count` to the face-value valid count across its non-invalidated batches, and advances the petition from `CURE_PERIOD` to `PENDING_CERTIFICATION`. An `INSUFFICIENT` determination moves the period from `OPEN` to `CLOSED_INSUFFICIENT`, and what happens to the petition depends on how many periods it has already consumed: while `cure_period_count` remains below the ceiling of two and the face-value count is still at or above the cure eligibility floor, the petition stays at `CURE_PERIOD` so that a second period may be opened, and once `cure_period_count` has reached two the petition advances to `PENDING_FAILURE`.

A cure period can also simply run out. Expiring one is a `SosAdmin` act taking no body, permitted only while the period is `OPEN` and only once the logical clock has reached or passed its `deadline_at`; expiring it while the clock is still strictly before that instant is refused with `ERR_CURE_PERIOD_NOT_EXPIRED`. Expiry moves the period from `OPEN` to `EXPIRED`, advances the petition to `PENDING_FAILURE`, and causes any later supplemental batch naming that period to be rejected.

### 3.8. Determination, Challenges and Court Review

The determination is the act that ends a petition. A `SosAdmin` or an `ElectionsExaminer` submits a `determination` of either `CERTIFY` or `FAIL`. `CERTIFY` requires the petition to be in `PENDING_CERTIFICATION` and moves it to the terminal `CERTIFIED`; `FAIL` requires the petition to be in `PENDING_FAILURE` and moves it to the terminal `FAILED`. Four gates stand in front of it, and they are evaluated in the order given in §2.11.

The first gate is the challenge dimension: the petition's `challenge_status` must not be `CHALLENGE_FILED` or `COURT_REVIEW`, else the request is refused with `ERR_CHALLENGE_PENDING`. A `challenge_status` of `NONE`, `COURT_AFFIRMED` or `COURT_REVERSED` all pass, because in each of those states the litigation is either absent or concluded. The second gate is the fraud dimension: no `FraudReferral` naming this petition may have a `status` of `OPENED` or `REFERRED_TO_AG`, else the request is refused with `ERR_FRAUD_REFERRAL_OPEN`. This gate applies to both determinations — an open allegation stops a failure being recorded just as it stops a certification, because the referral may yet change the count. The third gate is county distribution, and it applies **only** to the `CERTIFY` path: the petition's `qualifying_county_count` must be at or above the standard's `required_qualifying_counties`, else the request is refused with `ERR_COUNTY_DISTRIBUTION_UNMET`. A petition with more than enough statewide signatures concentrated in too few counties is therefore stuck in `PENDING_CERTIFICATION`: it cannot be certified, and it cannot be failed either, because its status is not `PENDING_FAILURE`. The fourth gate is segregation of duties: the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the `tallied_by_examiner_id` of the petition's latest closed round, else the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, and this applies to both determinations.

A challenge is filed by a `Challenger` against a petition sitting in `PENDING_CERTIFICATION` or `PENDING_FAILURE`; filing against a petition in any other status, including one already `CERTIFIED` or `FAILED`, is refused with `ERR_CHALLENGE_WINDOW_CLOSED`. The petition's `challenge_status` must be `NONE`, else the filing is refused with `ERR_CHALLENGE_ALREADY_FILED` — one live challenge at a time. The filing supplies `challenger_name`, `grounds` — one of `COUNT_ERROR`, `FORGERY`, `CIRCULATOR_INELIGIBLE` or `FORMAT_DEFECT` — and a narrative `statement`. The server stores `challenger_user_id` from the `X-User-ID` header, stamps `filed_at` from the logical clock, and sets `review_deadline_at` to `filed_at` advanced by the fifteen-day statutory review window. The new `Challenge` starts in `FILED` and the petition's `challenge_status` moves from `NONE` to `CHALLENGE_FILED`. Filing does not change the petition's `status`; the challenge dimension runs independently and only blocks the determination endpoint.

A `Challenger` escalates a `FILED` challenge to court review by supplying a `court_docket_number`. The challenge must be `FILED` and the logical clock must be strictly before `review_deadline_at`; a clock at or past that instant refuses the escalation with `ERR_CHALLENGE_REVIEW_WINDOW_EXPIRED`. Escalation is owner-restricted: the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the challenge's `challenger_user_id`, else the request is refused with `ERR_NOT_CHALLENGE_OWNER`. Escalation moves the challenge from `FILED` to `COURT_REVIEW` and the petition's `challenge_status` from `CHALLENGE_FILED` to `COURT_REVIEW`.

A `SosAdmin` may instead dismiss a `FILED` challenge administratively, with an optional `reason`, before any court sees it. Dismissal moves the challenge from `FILED` to the terminal `DISMISSED` and returns the petition's `challenge_status` to `NONE` — which both re-opens the determination endpoint and permits a further challenge to be filed, since the one-live-challenge rule reads the dimension rather than a history.

A `SosAdmin` records the court's `ruling` on a challenge in `COURT_REVIEW`. An `AFFIRM` ruling upholds the agency: the challenge moves from `COURT_REVIEW` to `DISMISSED`, the petition's `challenge_status` becomes the terminal `COURT_AFFIRMED`, and the petition's `status` is left exactly as it was. A `REVERSE` ruling upholds the challenger: the challenge moves from `COURT_REVIEW` to `UPHELD`, the petition's `challenge_status` becomes the terminal `COURT_REVERSED`, and the petition's pending determination **flips** — a petition in `PENDING_CERTIFICATION` moves to `PENDING_FAILURE`, and a petition in `PENDING_FAILURE` moves to `PENDING_CERTIFICATION`. The court ruling never writes a terminal petition status directly; it only changes which determination the agency may then issue, and because the resulting `challenge_status` is `COURT_AFFIRMED` or `COURT_REVERSED` rather than `NONE`, the determination gate is satisfied and the flipped determination can be issued immediately. Attempting to escalate, dismiss or rule on a challenge whose `status` does not match the transition's requirement is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CHALLENGE_STATE`. Challenges are readable individually and as a list narrowable by `petition_id`, `status` or `grounds`, which is the only channel from a petition to the challenges filed against it.

### 3.9. Fraud Referrals, Circulator Discipline and Compensating Corrections

An `ElectionsExaminer`, a `SosAdmin` or a `CountyClerk` opens a `FraudReferral` naming a `petition_id`, a `circulator_id`, a `referral_type` of `FORGED_SIGNATURES`, `CIRCULATOR_MISCONDUCT` or `FALSE_AFFIDAVIT`, a `narrative`, and optionally a `batch_id` when the allegation is tied to specific sheets. The server stores `opened_by_examiner_id` from the `X-User-ID` header. The referral starts in `OPENED`, `referred_to_ag` is `false`, `finding` is null, `adjudicated_by_examiner_id` is null and `voided_signature_count` is 0. Opening a referral has two immediate consequences even before anything is proven: it recomputes the named circulator's `credibility_score_bps` under the provisional variant, applying the open-referral penalty, and it blocks the petition's determination endpoint until the referral is adjudicated. Referrals are readable individually and as a list narrowable by `petition_id`, `circulator_id`, `batch_id`, `referral_type` or `status`, which is the only channel from a petition, a circulator or a batch to the referrals naming it.

An `ElectionsExaminer` or a `SosAdmin` forwards an `OPENED` referral to the Attorney General, taking no request body; this sets `referred_to_ag` to `true` and moves the referral from `OPENED` to `REFERRED_TO_AG`. Adjudication is possible only from `REFERRED_TO_AG` — adjudicating a referral still merely `OPENED`, or one already adjudicated, is refused with `ERR_REFERRAL_NOT_WITH_AG` — and the adjudicating `X-User-ID` must differ from the referral's `opened_by_examiner_id`, else the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The adjudication supplies a `finding` of `SUBSTANTIATED` or `UNSUBSTANTIATED`, which is recorded alongside `adjudicated_by_examiner_id`, and the referral moves to the correspondingly named terminal status.

An `UNSUBSTANTIATED` finding cascades nowhere. It records the finding, and because the referral is no longer open the petition's determination unblocks and the circulator's provisional open-referral penalty falls away. A `SUBSTANTIATED` finding cascades four levels. When the referral named a `batch_id`, that batch is set to `INVALIDATED` and every one of its lines not already `VOIDED` is set to `VOIDED` with a `void_reason` of `FRAUD_SUBSTANTIATED`, with the number of lines so voided recorded on the referral as `voided_signature_count`. The circulator's `misconduct_strike_count` is incremented and its `credibility_score_bps` recomputed under the adjudicated variant. Once `misconduct_strike_count` reaches the suspension threshold of one, the circulator is moved to `SUSPENDED`, which in turn sets each of its `ACTIVE` assignments to `SUSPENDED`. And the affected petition's ledgers are recomputed. Because the threshold is one, the very first substantiated referral suspends the circulator.

The circulator's `credibility_score_bps` is recomputed at every line validation, at every referral opening and at every adjudication, and it is scored over every `SignatureRecord` in every `SignatureBatch` that circulator swore, across all petitions. Each line is scored on its **effective status**: the line's `status_before_void` when its `validation_status` is `VOIDED`, and its `validation_status` otherwise, so that voiding lines does not erase the history that produced the score. The checked base is the number of lines whose effective status is neither `PENDING` nor `INVALID_FORMAT_DEFECT`; the invalid count is the number of lines in that same base whose effective status begins with `INVALID_`; and the invalid rate in basis points is the invalid count multiplied by 10000 divided by the checked base with the remainder discarded, or 0 when the base is empty. The score then follows one of two variants selected by `misconduct_strike_count`. Under the **provisional** variant, used while `misconduct_strike_count` is 0, the raw score is 10000 less the invalid rate multiplied by 5000 and divided by 10000 with the remainder discarded, less a flat 500 basis points for each `FraudReferral` of this circulator whose `status` is `OPENED` or `REFERRED_TO_AG` — an unproven allegation weighs something, but the invalid rate weighs only half. Under the **adjudicated** variant, used once `misconduct_strike_count` is 1 or more, the raw score is 10000 less the invalid rate at full weight, that is multiplied by 10000 and divided by 10000 with the remainder discarded, less 2000 basis points for each substantiated strike; open-referral penalties drop away entirely. The raw value is then clamped into the inclusive range from 1000 to 10000. A brand-new circulator with nothing examined scores 10000. One examined valid line and one examined invalid line give an invalid rate of 5000 basis points, hence a score of 7500 under the provisional variant and 3000 under the adjudicated variant with a single strike. The floor is observable: with every examined line invalid and one strike the raw value is negative and the reported score is exactly 1000.

Discipline is also available directly to a `SosAdmin`, and the three levers differ sharply in reach. **Suspension** applies to a circulator in `REGISTERED`, takes an optional `reason`, moves `registration_status` to `SUSPENDED`, and sets every `ACTIVE` assignment of that circulator to `SUSPENDED` — which blocks new batches against those assignments — while leaving every existing `SignatureBatch` untouched. **Reinstatement** applies to a circulator in `SUSPENDED`, takes no body, moves `registration_status` back to `REGISTERED` and returns every `SUSPENDED` assignment to `ACTIVE`; but it is barred when the suspension came from proven misconduct, so a `misconduct_strike_count` at or above the threshold of one refuses the request with `ERR_CIRCULATOR_REINSTATEMENT_BARRED`. A fraud-driven suspension is consequently permanent in practice, while a discretionary one is reversible. **Revocation** applies from `PENDING_TRAINING`, `TRAINED`, `REGISTERED` or `SUSPENDED` — anything but the terminal `REVOKED` — takes an optional `reason`, and cascades four levels with a fifth level of derived-field reconciliation. It moves `registration_status` to the terminal `REVOKED`; it terminates every assignment not already `TERMINATED`; it sets every `SignatureBatch` of those assignments whose `status` is not already `REJECTED_FORMAT` or `INVALIDATED` to `INVALIDATED`, recording each batch's `status_before_invalidation`; it sets every line of those batches not already `VOIDED` to `VOIDED` with a `void_reason` of `CIRCULATOR_REVOKED`, recording each line's `status_before_void`; and it recomputes `accepted_raw_total`, `projected_valid_count`, `verified_valid_count`, `voided_signature_count`, `verification_cost_accrued_cents` and `qualifying_county_count` on every affected petition. Revoking a circulator who never got past `PENDING_TRAINING` terminates their assignments and stops there, because there are no batches to reach.

A single batch can also be struck on its own. A `SosAdmin` invalidates a batch with a mandatory `invalidation_reason`; the batch must not already be `REJECTED_FORMAT` or `INVALIDATED`, else the request is refused with `ERR_BATCH_ALREADY_TERMINAL`. Invalidation records `status_before_invalidation` and `invalidation_reason`, voids every line not already `VOIDED` with a `void_reason` of `BATCH_INVALIDATED` while recording each line's `status_before_void`, sets the batch's `accepted_raw_count` to 0, subtracts the batch's **stored** `verification_cost_cents` from the petition's `verification_cost_accrued_cents`, and recomputes the petition's `accepted_raw_total`, `verified_valid_count`, `projected_valid_count`, `voided_signature_count` and `qualifying_county_count`. Subtracting the stored figure rather than a freshly computed one is what keeps the cost ledger exact.

Reinstating a batch is the compensating undo, and it must leave no drift in either ledger. It applies to a batch in `INVALIDATED`, takes an optional `invalidation_reason`, and is blocked two ways: the batch circulator's `registration_status` must not be `REVOKED`, else the request is refused with `ERR_CIRCULATOR_REVOKED`, and no `FraudReferral` naming this batch may have a `status` of `SUBSTANTIATED`, else it is refused with `ERR_FRAUD_SUBSTANTIATED_BATCH`. Reinstatement restores the batch's `status` from `status_before_invalidation` and clears that field, so a batch that was `AFFIDAVIT_VERIFIED` returns to `AFFIDAVIT_VERIFIED`, one that was `SAMPLED` returns to `SAMPLED` and one that was `FULLY_CHECKED` returns to `FULLY_CHECKED`; it restores each line's `validation_status` from its `status_before_void` verbatim and clears `status_before_void` and `void_reason`, so a line that was `VALID` returns to `VALID`, one that was `INVALID_FORMAT_DEFECT` returns to `INVALID_FORMAT_DEFECT`, and one that was never examined returns to `PENDING` and becomes eligible for a later round again; it restores `accepted_raw_count` and adds the stored `verification_cost_cents` back to the petition's `verification_cost_accrued_cents`; and it recomputes the same five petition figures. Only `BATCH_INVALIDATED` voids compensate: a line voided with `CIRCULATOR_REVOKED` or `FRAUD_SUBSTANTIATED` is not restorable, which is precisely why the two blocking preconditions exist.

Finally, an examination itself can be withdrawn. A `SosAdmin` voids a `VerificationRound` with a mandatory `voided_reason`. A round in `OPEN` may always be voided; a round in `CLOSED` may be voided only while the petition's `status` is not `CERTIFIED` or `FAILED`, else the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PETITION_STATE`. Voiding clears `in_sample` and `validated_in_round_id` on every line the round selected and returns those lines to `PENDING`, so they re-enter the eligible pool of a later round; it restores every batch the round touched to the status that batch held before the round examined it and resets that batch's stored `verification_cost_cents` to 0; it subtracts the round's `accrued_cost_cents` from the petition's `verification_cost_accrued_cents`, which is exact because the round accrued only deltas; and it moves the round to the terminal `VOIDED`, after which the round is invisible to every "latest closed round" lookup. Voiding a `CLOSED` round additionally recomputes the petition's `projected_valid_count` and `verified_valid_count` from whatever closed round is now the latest, or sets both to 0 when none remains, and returns the petition's `status` to `SIGNATURES_FILED` when no other `CLOSED` round exists. Because the void is invisible to later lookups, voiding the round that triggered a cure period and closing a fresh one changes the cure target computed in §3.7.
