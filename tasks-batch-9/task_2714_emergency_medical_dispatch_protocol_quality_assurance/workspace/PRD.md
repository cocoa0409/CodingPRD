# Emergency Medical Dispatch QA API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

An emergency communications centre lives or dies by the first ninety seconds of a call. The dispatcher who answers a 911 line works a scripted card set — case entry, key questions, pre-arrival instructions, final coding — and the determinant code they land on decides whether an ambulance runs hot or cold and whether it arrives with paramedics or with basic life support. Accrediting bodies do not accept assurances that this is done well; they require documented, reproducible evidence that a sampled slice of every call type was reviewed against the protocol in force at the moment the call was answered, that compliance percentages cleared published thresholds, and that every dispatcher who fell short was coached and retrained.

This document specifies the service that carries that evidence. It is a quality-assurance system of record for emergency medical dispatch: it ingests handled calls, draws reproducible samples by call type, records section-by-section score sheets, grades determinant-code accuracy against a reviewer's judgement, isolates high-risk cardiac-arrest cases for clinical scrutiny of dispatcher-assisted CPR performance, delivers coaching plans and tracks remediation hours to completion, coordinates protocol version updates through retraining and go-live, and rolls the whole picture up into accreditation reports for the medical director and the oversight committee. Nothing in the system is allowed to drift: every derived percentage, hours ledger and privilege flag is recomputed from its source rows rather than nudged by deltas, so that voiding a review or cancelling a session returns the affected ledgers to exactly the value they held before.

Two constraints shape almost every rule that follows. The first is **segregation of duties**: reviewers score, supervisors finalize and adjudicate, the medical director alone approves protocol content and classifies clinical failures, and no single operator identity may both score a case and finalize it. The second is **auditability under change**: score sheets are append-only, protocol versions are pinned to a review at selection time and never re-pointed, and once an accreditation report has been filed the sampling periods it drew on are frozen so that the filed figure can always be reproduced. All resource collections are mounted at the service root; there is no single owning aggregate.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Centre Personnel and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting operator, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which they act. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the six declared roles, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If the headers are well formed but the named role is not among those authorized for the endpoint being called, the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403). The six roles are `ProgramAdmin`, `QaSupervisor`, `QaReviewer`, `MedicalDirector`, `AccreditationOfficer`, and `Dispatcher`.

A `ProgramAdmin` is the communications-centre administrator: they configure the QA program and its thresholds, register protocol versions and their rollouts, enroll and certify dispatchers, ingest call records, and own the logical clock. A `QaSupervisor` runs the audit unit: they open and close sampling periods, draw samples, adjudicate rebuttals, finalize and void reviews, and manage coaching plans and retraining sessions. A `QaReviewer` performs the protocol compliance review itself: they score sampled cases, open the clinical record on high-risk cases and grade dispatcher-assisted resuscitation, but they cannot finalize, adjudicate, or create coaching plans. A `MedicalDirector` approves protocol versions and signs off high-risk cases as sentinel events or coaching-only matters, and holds no scoring or finalization authority. An `AccreditationOfficer` builds and files the aggregate compliance reports and touches nothing else. A `Dispatcher` is the employee under review.

Identity semantics differ between the operator roles and the employee role, and the distinction matters for every ownership check. When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramAdmin`, `QaSupervisor`, `QaReviewer`, `MedicalDirector`, or `AccreditationOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it on the rows those roles touch — as `approved_by_user_id` on an approved protocol version, as `submitted_by_user_id` on a submitted score sheet, as `decided_by_user_id` on an adjudicated rebuttal, and as `signed_off_by_user_id` on a signed-off high-risk record — but never compares it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `Dispatcher`, by contrast, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting dispatcher row, and ownership checks compare the resource's dispatcher foreign key to it directly: a dispatcher filing a rebuttal must be the dispatcher whose work is under review, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_NOT_CASE_OWNER` (HTTP 403).

The segregation-of-duties rule that gives this section its teeth is enforced at finalization. The `X-User-ID` finalizing a review must differ from the `submitted_by_user_id` recorded on that review's current score sheet; a reviewer who has already been granted `QaSupervisor` capacity may not sign off their own scoring work, and an attempt to do so is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints accept two optional query parameters. `limit` caps the number of items returned; when omitted it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` is the number of items to skip before the page begins; when omitted it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. A supplied `limit` that is not an integer, is below 1 or above 100, or a supplied `offset` that is not an integer or is negative, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400).

Unless a subsection below says otherwise, every list endpoint returns its resources ordered by `created_at` ascending.

List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before the page window is applied and `has_more` indicates whether rows remain beyond it.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and the Logical Clock

Every resource carries an `id` that is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix; clients never supply one. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the row was first written. Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, in the same format, refreshed on every mutation of the row, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and is incremented by 1 on every mutation of that row — including mutations the server performs on its own initiative as part of a cascade, not only those a client requests directly on that resource. The single exception is the score sheet: because sheets are append-only audit artefacts that are never edited after submission, a score sheet carries only `id` and `created_at`, and both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from it entirely.

Time in this system is not wall-clock time. The server maintains a single logical UTC clock which every deadline, window, expiry and eligibility rule in this document reads. Its startup value is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and before the clock has been set the server reads that default rather than falling back to the host's real time. A `ProgramAdmin` may move the clock to any instant by supplying `to_iso8601`; the response reports the new value as `current_utc`. Moving the clock mutates no entity — it merely changes the instant against which all subsequent temporal evaluations are made.

Several timestamps recorded by the server are logical-clock readings rather than arrival times: `activated_at` and `superseded_at` on a protocol version, `went_live_at` on a rollout, `certified_at` on a dispatcher, `closed_at` and `locked_at` on a sampling period, `scored_at`, `finalized_at` and `voided_at` on a review, `signed_off_at` on a high-risk record, `verified_at` on a coaching plan, `completed_at` on a retraining session, and `submitted_at` on an accreditation report. Where a client supplies a datetime — `effective_from`, `effective_to`, `scheduled_golive_at`, `hire_date`, `received_at`, `period_start`, `period_end`, `starts_at`, `ends_at`, `due_at`, `window_start`, `window_end`, or an explicit `certified_at` on a certification request — it must parse as an ISO 8601 UTC instant, and a value that does not is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

### 2.5. Nested Path Resolution and Programme Scope

Many resources are addressed through a nested path — a program's protocol versions, a protocol version's rollouts, a dispatcher's call records, a review's score sheets, a coaching plan's retraining sessions. Path identifiers are resolved strictly left to right, and the first identifier that does not resolve determines the error: `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROTOCOL_VERSION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ROLLOUT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DISPATCHER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SAMPLING_PERIOD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REBUTTAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_HIGH_RISK_REVIEW_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COACHING_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SESSION_NOT_FOUND`, or `ERR_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`, each at HTTP 404. A missing parent is therefore never reported as a problem with the child. Identifiers supplied in a request body are resolved on the same principle and with the same codes — a `program_id`, `protocol_version_id`, `dispatcher_id`, `call_record_id` or `source_review_id` that names nothing yields the corresponding not-found code, with `ERR_CALL_RECORD_NOT_FOUND` covering an unknown call.

Resolution succeeding is not the same as the reference being usable. This service is multi-programme, and cross-aggregate references must agree on programme scope: the protocol version a dispatcher is certified on must belong to that dispatcher's program, a call targeted into a sampling period must have been handled by a dispatcher of the period's program, and the curriculum version taught in a retraining session must belong to the coached dispatcher's program. A reference that resolves but crosses a programme boundary is rejected with `ERR_PROGRAM_SCOPE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 409).

### 2.6. Precision and Rounding Discipline

All rounding in this system is **round half up** — a value exactly at the midpoint rounds away from zero — and the number of decimal places is fixed per quantity rather than chosen by the implementation.

Section percentages on a score sheet (`case_entry_pct`, `key_questions_pct`, `pre_arrival_pct`, `final_coding_pct`) are held to 2 decimal places. Composite scores and compliance percentages are held to 1 decimal place: `provisional_composite_score`, `pre_penalty_composite_score`, `penalty_points`, `final_composite_score`, `compliance_points_total`, `compliance_pct` on both a dispatcher and a report, `high_risk_compliance_pct`, `latest_compliance_pct`, and `dacpr_index`. Hours are held to 2 decimal places: `duration_hours`, `credited_hours`, `remediation_hours_owed`. Programme threshold percentages (`target_compliance_pct`, `watch_floor_pct`, `high_risk_target_pct`), the restriction threshold `hours_owed_restriction_threshold`, `required_training_hours` and `required_hours` are held to 1 decimal place. Protocol section weights (`case_entry_weight`, `key_questions_weight`, `pre_arrival_weight`, `final_coding_weight`) are held to 4 decimal places.

The **order** in which rounding is applied is part of the specification, not an implementation detail, because rounding early and rounding late give different answers. Section percentages are rounded to 2 decimal places first, and the composite is then computed from those already-rounded percentages and rounded once to 1 decimal place. The determinant penalty tier is chosen from the pre-penalty composite and never from the post-penalty result. In the dispatcher-equal-weight accreditation variant, each dispatcher's own compliant rate is kept unrounded and only the final programme mean is rounded. Sub-scores inside the resuscitation performance index are kept unrounded until the index itself is rounded, and the late-compression cap is then applied to the rounded index.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Reviews and Dispatcher Ledgers

Two write operations in this system remain legitimate on repetition, which means no state precondition can protect a client that is working from a view of the world it read some time ago. Both are therefore guarded by an explicit version comparand, and in both cases the version field is a required input, not an optional one.

Submitting a score sheet (`POST /reviews/{id}/scores`) requires `expected_review_version`, carrying the `version` of the target review as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the review's current `version` — because another reviewer appended a sheet, because a supervisor adjudicated a rebuttal, or because a cascade touched the row — the submission is rejected with `ERR_STALE_REVIEW_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Re-scoring a review that is already scored is an ordinary, permitted operation (the new sheet simply supersedes the old one as the current sheet), so this stale-version check is the only thing standing between a reviewer and silently overwriting a sheet, a rebuttal decision, or a ledger effect they never saw.

Creating a manual coaching plan (`POST /coaching-plans`) requires `expected_dispatcher_version`, carrying the `version` of the target dispatcher as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the dispatcher's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_DISPATCHER_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A manual plan supersedes the dispatcher's other open plans and rewrites their hours ledger, so a supervisor acting on a stale reading of that ledger would cancel commitments they never inspected.

No other endpoint takes a version field. Where this document does not name `expected_review_version` or `expected_dispatcher_version` as an input, the operation carries no version comparand and is guarded by its state preconditions alone.

### 2.8. Cascade Discipline and Derived-Field Reconciliation

A large fraction of the fields in this system are derived rather than supplied, and the governing rule for all of them is **full recomputation from source rows, never incremental adjustment**. When a review is finalized, the dispatcher's case count, point total and compliance percentage are recomputed by walking that dispatcher's finalized reviews; when the same review is later voided, the same walk runs again over the smaller set. This is what makes voiding an exact compensating action: the point ledger returns to precisely the value it held before finalization, with no accumulated drift. The same discipline governs a coaching plan's credited hours (recomputed over its completed sessions), a dispatcher's owed remediation hours (recomputed over their open plans), a sampling period's two review counters, and a rollout's trained-dispatcher count.

Four named cascades run through the system, and each is described in full in the workflow subsection that triggers it. Cascade A fires on finalization and runs from the review's own scoring fields out through the dispatcher's ledgers, posture, automatic coaching plan, owed hours and dispatch privilege. Cascade B fires on rollout go-live and runs from the version becoming active, through the supersession of the previous active version of the same card-set family, the lapsing of every dispatcher certified on it, the suspension of those dispatchers' open plans and the re-derivation of their privilege, and finally the regression of the superseded version's rollout training status. Cascade C fires when an accreditation report is filed and locks the closed sampling periods the report drew on, voiding the untouched selections inside them. Cascade D fires on voiding a finalized review and reverses Cascade A link by link.

Every row a cascade writes is a mutation of that row: its `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` is incremented, exactly as if a client had written it. A client that reads a dispatcher, then triggers an unrelated finalization that touches that dispatcher, and then attempts a version-guarded write with the stale number will be rejected — this is the intended serialization point, not an accident.

### 2.9. Order of Evaluation for Rejections

When a request violates more than one rule, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fully determined by the ladder below. Checks are evaluated in this order and the first violated rule wins:

1. **Authentication** — headers present and the role recognized, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. **Authorization** — the acting role appears in the endpoint's authorized list, else `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.
3. **Request shape** — the body parses, required fields are present, types match, enum values are declared members, and numeric fields are in range, else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. In-range means, among others, that section points earned and possible are both at least 0 and that earned never exceeds possible, that `recognition_time_sec` and `time_to_first_compression_sec` are at least 0, that `target_sample_count`, `requested_count` and `target_dispatcher_count` are at least 1, that `required_training_hours` is greater than 0, and that hours thresholds are at least 0.
4. **Pagination shape** — on list endpoints, `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`.
5. **Determinant-code shape** — `ERR_INVALID_DETERMINANT_CODE`.
6. **Programme threshold coherence** — `ERR_INVALID_THRESHOLD_CONFIG`.
7. **Protocol weight normalization** — `ERR_WEIGHTS_NOT_NORMALIZED`.
8. **Path existence**, left to right, then **body-reference existence** — the 404 family.
9. **Case ownership** for a dispatcher's rebuttal — `ERR_NOT_CASE_OWNER`.
10. **Segregation of duties** at finalization — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
11. **Stale version** — `ERR_STALE_REVIEW_VERSION`, then `ERR_STALE_DISPATCHER_VERSION`.
12. **Target state permits the transition** — the HTTP 409 state family.
13. **Programme scope agreement**, then **call-type agreement** — `ERR_PROGRAM_SCOPE_MISMATCH`, then `ERR_CALL_TYPE_MISMATCH`.
14. **Referenced protocol version is usable for the purpose** — `ERR_VERSION_NOT_CERTIFIABLE`, `ERR_CURRICULUM_VERSION_NOT_TRAINABLE`, `ERR_NO_PROTOCOL_VERSION_IN_FORCE`.
15. **Clinical safety gates** — `ERR_HIGH_RISK_SIGN_OFF_REQUIRED`, `ERR_SENTINEL_CLASSIFICATION_REQUIRED`.
16. **Window exclusivity** — `ERR_PROTOCOL_WINDOW_OVERLAP`, `ERR_SESSION_WINDOW_OVERLAP`.
17. **Go-live window open** — `ERR_GOLIVE_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN`.
18. **Temporal envelopes** — `ERR_CALL_RECEIVED_IN_FUTURE`, `ERR_CALL_OUTSIDE_PERIOD`, `ERR_REBUTTAL_WINDOW_CLOSED`, `ERR_REVIEW_DEADLINE_PASSED`, `ERR_INVALID_TIME_WINDOW`, `ERR_REPORT_WINDOW_INVALID`.
19. **Uniqueness and cardinality** — `ERR_DUPLICATE_CALL_REFERENCE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_EMPLOYEE_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_HIGH_RISK_REVIEW`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_COACHING_PLAN`, `ERR_CALL_ALREADY_SELECTED`, `ERR_SAMPLING_MODE_MISMATCH`, `ERR_REBUTTAL_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`.
20. **Quota and readiness gates** — `ERR_SAMPLE_QUOTA_FILLED`, `ERR_SAMPLE_QUOTA_NOT_MET`, `ERR_TRAINING_INCOMPLETE`, `ERR_ESCALATED_SAMPLING_REQUIRED`.
21. **Non-empty aggregate source sets** — `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_CALLS`, `ERR_NO_FINALIZED_REVIEWS_IN_WINDOW`.

The practical consequence worth internalising: a state violation always outranks a scope, safety, overlap, temporal, uniqueness or quota violation, and an authorization failure outranks everything except a missing header. A supervisor attempting to close a period that is already closed and also short of quota receives `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_OPEN`, not `ERR_SAMPLE_QUOTA_NOT_MET`. Conversely, the stale-version checks sit *above* the state family, so a score submission carrying a stale `expected_review_version` against a review that has since been finalized is rejected as a stale version, not as an unscorable review.

### 2.10. Determinant Codes and What They Commit the Centre To

A determinant code is the output of the card set and the single most consequential field a dispatcher produces. It is written as a chief-complaint number, a hyphen, a level letter, a hyphen, and a sub-determinant number — for example `10-C-1` or `9-E-1`. Structurally, a valid code is one or two decimal digits, then a hyphen, then exactly one uppercase letter drawn from `O`, `A`, `B`, `C`, `D`, `E`, then a hyphen, then one or two decimal digits, with nothing before or after. A two-part code, a lowercase level letter, a level letter outside that set, or a three-digit component is invalid, and any endpoint that accepts a code rejects it with `ERR_INVALID_DETERMINANT_CODE` (HTTP 422). Two endpoints accept codes: call ingestion, which takes `dispatched_determinant_code`, and score-sheet submission, which takes `reviewer_determinant_code`.

Only the middle level letter carries dispatch semantics, and the server decodes it identically wherever a code is written. The letter maps to a numeric priority rank ordering codes by clinical acuity: `O` is rank 1, `A` is rank 2, `B` is rank 3, `C` is rank 4, `D` is rank 5, and `E` — the ECHO level, the most acute — is rank 6. The response mode is `HOT` when the level letter is `D` or `E`, and `COLD` for every other letter. The resource tier is `ALS` when the level letter is `C`, `D` or `E`, and `BLS` for every other letter. On a call record these three decodings are stored as `dispatched_priority_rank`, `response_mode_assigned` and `resource_assigned`; on a score sheet the same decoding of the reviewer's code is stored as `reviewer_priority_rank`, `reviewer_response_mode` and `reviewer_resource`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up a QA Programme, the Roster and the Call Feed

A `ProgramAdmin` begins by registering the QA programme itself, supplying a `name` for the centre's programme and the `accreditation_body` it reports to — one of `IAED`, `NAED_LOCAL`, or `STATE_OEMS`. Everything else on the programme is a threshold or a window length, and each may be supplied or left to its default. `target_compliance_pct` is the compliance percentage the accrediting body demands and defaults to 90.0; `watch_floor_pct` is the floor separating a merely-watched dispatcher from one on probation and defaults to 80.0; `high_risk_target_pct` is the minimum pass rate demanded of dispatcher-assisted resuscitation cases and defaults to 85.0. The three must be mutually coherent: `watch_floor_pct` must be greater than 0.0 and strictly less than `target_compliance_pct`, `target_compliance_pct` must not exceed 100.0, and `high_risk_target_pct` must be greater than 0.0 and not exceed 100.0. A configuration that violates any of those relations is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_THRESHOLD_CONFIG`.

The remaining programme parameters govern the audit clock and the escalation posture. `review_deadline_days` is the number of days after a review is opened within which it must be finalized, defaulting to 30. `rebuttal_window_days` is the number of days after a score is submitted within which the dispatcher may dispute it, defaulting to 14; both must be at least 1. `max_rebuttal_rounds` caps how many times one case may be disputed and defaults to 2, with 0 through 3 the permitted range — a programme that sets it to 0 has disabled rebuttals entirely. `escalated_sample_floor` is the elevated sample size the programme insists on when a dispatcher under audit is in trouble, defaulting to 25 and required to be at least 1. `hours_owed_restriction_threshold` is the number of outstanding remediation hours a dispatcher may carry before their dispatch privilege is curtailed, defaulting to 6.0 hours.

Four programme fields are never supplied and are always derived. `accreditation_status` reads `SETUP` from the moment the programme is created and stays there until the first accreditation report is filed, after which it is either `ACCREDITED` or `AT_RISK`. `latest_compliance_pct` is null until the first filing. `submitted_report_count` starts at 0 and `sentinel_event_count` starts at 0.

Enrolling a dispatcher requires the owning `program_id`, a centre-issued `employee_code`, and the `full_name` that will appear on coaching paperwork; `hire_date` is optional and used only for reporting. The employee code must be unique within its programme, and a collision is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_EMPLOYEE_CODE`. A newly enrolled dispatcher starts with `certification_status` of `PROVISIONAL`, no `certified_protocol_version_id` and no `certified_at`, `dispatch_privilege` of `FULL`, `compliance_posture` of `UNRATED`, `compliance_pct` of null, and every counter and ledger at zero: `reviewed_case_count` 0, `compliance_points_total` 0.0, `remediation_hours_owed` 0.00, `sentinel_event_count` 0.

Calls flow in one at a time against the dispatcher who handled them. Ingestion carries a `call_reference` — the CAD incident reference, which must be globally unique across the whole service and which doubles as the deterministic tie-break key when samples are drawn; a repeat is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CALL_REFERENCE`. It carries the `call_type`, the chief-complaint bucket that sampling works by, drawn from `CARDIAC_ARREST`, `CHEST_PAIN`, `BREATHING_PROBLEM`, `TRAUMA`, and `UNCONSCIOUS`. It carries `received_at`, the instant the call was answered, which may not be after the logical clock — a call from the future is rejected with `ERR_CALL_RECEIVED_IN_FUTURE`. And it carries `dispatched_determinant_code`, the code the dispatcher actually assigned live, from which the server immediately derives the priority rank, response mode and resource tier described in §2.10.

Two optional outcome flags travel with the call and matter enormously later. `cardiac_arrest_confirmed` records whether the patient was in fact in arrest and defaults to false. `bystander_cpr_in_progress` records whether a bystander was already doing compressions when the line connected, defaults to false, and re-weights the whole resuscitation performance index because a dispatcher who inherits compressions in progress is judged more on how fast they recognised the arrest and less on how fast compressions began. A freshly ingested call has `status` of `INGESTED`; it becomes `SELECTED` once a non-voided review references it, and returns to `INGESTED` if that review is ever voided.

### 3.2. Protocol Content: Registration, Approval and the Governing Interval

Protocol content is versioned, and a version is registered as a draft before anyone may rely on it. A `ProgramAdmin` registers it under a programme with a `protocol_family` of either `MPDS_CARDSET` or `LOCAL_CARDSET`, a human `version_label` such as "v13.2" which must be unique within its programme and family, and the half-open interval during which it governs calls: `effective_from` is the inclusive lower bound and `effective_to` the exclusive upper bound, with `effective_to` omitted or null meaning the interval runs forward without end. An `effective_to` that is not strictly after `effective_from` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_TIME_WINDOW`.

The substance of a version is its four section weights — `case_entry_weight`, `key_questions_weight`, `pre_arrival_weight`, and `final_coding_weight` — each a non-negative value expressed to 4 decimal places, and the four together summing to exactly 1.0000 at that precision. A set of weights that does not sum to exactly one, or that contains a negative member, is rejected with `ERR_WEIGHTS_NOT_NORMALIZED`. The weights encode the centre's clinical judgement about what matters: a typical MPDS configuration weights case entry at 0.2000, key questions at 0.3500, pre-arrival instructions at 0.3000, and final coding at 0.1500, so that a failure to read pre-arrival instructions costs a dispatcher far more than a slip during case entry. A newly registered version has `status` of `DRAFT`, no `approved_by_user_id`, no `activated_at`, and no `superseded_at`; no interval-collision check runs at registration.

That collision check runs at approval, and approval belongs to the `MedicalDirector` alone. Approving a draft moves it `DRAFT → APPROVED`, stamps `approved_by_user_id` with the director's `X-User-ID`, and may carry an optional `approval_note`. Approval is refused unless the version is currently `DRAFT` — a second attempt on an already-approved, active or superseded version is rejected with `ERR_VERSION_NOT_APPROVABLE` — and unless the four weights still sum to exactly 1.0000. The interval check is the substantive gate: within one programme and one protocol family, the governing intervals of versions already in `APPROVED`, `ACTIVE` or `SUPERSEDED` must not overlap the interval of the version being approved. The comparison is half-open on both sides, so a version whose `effective_to` equals another's `effective_from` touches without colliding and is fine, while any genuine overlap — including an overlap with an open-ended interval that has no upper bound — is rejected with `ERR_PROTOCOL_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. Draft versions are invisible to this check, which is why two drafts may sit on top of each other and only the second approval fails.

Approval is what makes a version usable: from `APPROVED` onward it may be pinned to a review, it may certify a dispatcher, and it may serve as a retraining curriculum. Activation is a different matter and never happens by direct request — a version reaches `ACTIVE` only as the first link of the go-live cascade described next.

### 3.3. Retraining, Rollout Readiness and Go-Live

Putting a new card set into force is a coordination problem: the content must be approved, the room must be trained, and the switch must be thrown on a scheduled date. A `ProgramAdmin` schedules the switch by creating a rollout against a protocol version, supplying `scheduled_golive_at` — the earliest logical instant at which the switch may be thrown — and `target_dispatcher_count`, the headcount that must be certified on the version first, which must be at least 1. `required_training_hours` records how many hours of retraining the programme expects per dispatcher for this rollout, defaults to 2.0, and must be greater than 0. A new rollout has `golive_status` of `SCHEDULED`, no `went_live_at`, and a `trained_dispatcher_count` and `training_status` computed immediately from whoever is already certified — normally `NOT_STARTED`, but `IN_PROGRESS` or `COMPLETE` if certifications already exist on that version.

A rollout carries two independent state dimensions on one row, and it is important not to conflate them. The training dimension takes the values `NOT_STARTED`, `IN_PROGRESS` and `COMPLETE`, is entirely derived, and may move in both directions. Its input is `trained_dispatcher_count`, the number of dispatchers whose `certified_protocol_version_id` is this rollout's version and whose `certification_status` is exactly `CERTIFIED` — dispatchers who are `PROVISIONAL` or `LAPSED` do not count, no matter what version they were last certified on. When that count is 0 the training dimension reads `NOT_STARTED`; when it is at or above `target_dispatcher_count` it reads `COMPLETE`, so hitting the target exactly is enough; anything between reads `IN_PROGRESS`. The go-live dimension takes the values `SCHEDULED` and `LIVE`, advances only by an explicit go-live request, and is one-way. The go-live guard reads the training dimension, but the training dimension never reads go-live: a rollout that has already gone live will still see its `training_status` regress to `NOT_STARTED` if every certification on its version subsequently lapses.

Certification is a `ProgramAdmin` action naming a `protocol_version_id`, optionally with an explicit `certified_at`. The target version's `status` must be `APPROVED` or `ACTIVE`, and a draft or superseded version is refused with `ERR_VERSION_NOT_CERTIFIABLE`; the version must also belong to the dispatcher's own programme, or the request is refused for programme scope. A successful certification moves `certification_status` from `PROVISIONAL → CERTIFIED`, or from `LAPSED → CERTIFIED` when it is a re-certification onto a newer version, sets `certified_protocol_version_id` and `certified_at`, and then bubbles upward: every rollout on the target version — and, on a re-certification, every rollout on the version the dispatcher is leaving — recomputes its `trained_dispatcher_count` and re-derives its `training_status`, which is how a rollout crosses `TRAINING_NOT_STARTED → TRAINING_IN_PROGRESS` on the first certification and `TRAINING_IN_PROGRESS → TRAINING_COMPLETE` on the one that reaches the target. Re-certification does one thing more: every coaching plan of that dispatcher sitting in `SUSPENDED` reopens, returning to `IN_PROGRESS` when the plan has already accrued credited hours above 0.00 and to `ASSIGNED` when it has not. Finally, the dispatcher's `dispatch_privilege` is re-derived, because certification status is one of its three inputs.

Throwing the switch is also a `ProgramAdmin` action, optionally carrying a `golive_note`, and it is guarded on four fronts. The rollout's `golive_status` must still be `SCHEDULED` and its version must still be `APPROVED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_ROLLOUT_NOT_LIVEABLE`. The `training_status` must be `COMPLETE`, or the request is refused with `ERR_TRAINING_INCOMPLETE`. And the logical clock must be at or after `scheduled_golive_at` — the instant itself qualifies — or the request is refused with `ERR_GOLIVE_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN`. When all four hold, the rollout crosses `GOLIVE_SCHEDULED → GOLIVE_LIVE`, `went_live_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and Cascade B runs in five links.

The first link takes the rollout's own version `APPROVED → ACTIVE` and stamps its `activated_at`. The second link finds the version that was previously `ACTIVE` in the same programme and protocol family and takes it `ACTIVE → SUPERSEDED`, stamping `superseded_at` while leaving its declared `effective_to` untouched. The third link sweeps every dispatcher whose `certified_protocol_version_id` is that superseded version and whose `certification_status` is `CERTIFIED`, taking each `CERTIFIED → LAPSED` while retaining the old `certified_protocol_version_id` for audit. The fourth link, because those dispatchers are no longer certified, moves every one of their coaching plans in `ASSIGNED` or `IN_PROGRESS` to `SUSPENDED` — the unmet hours on a suspended plan stay in the owed ledger, and no session may be scheduled or completed while a plan is suspended — and re-derives each affected dispatcher's `dispatch_privilege`, which lands on `RESTRICTED` on the strength of the lapsed certification alone. The fifth link recomputes `trained_dispatcher_count` and `training_status` on every rollout of the superseded version, so a rollout that read `TRAINING_COMPLETE` regresses to `TRAINING_NOT_STARTED` once its count reaches zero, even though its own `golive_status` remains `LIVE`.

### 3.4. Sampling Periods and Getting Cases onto Reviewers' Desks

Sampling is organised into periods, and each period audits exactly one call type over exactly one window. A `QaSupervisor` opens a period under a programme with a `call_type` from the five chief-complaint buckets, a `period_start` and a `period_end` bounding the calls it may draw on — the window is half-open, so a call answered exactly at `period_start` is inside it and a call answered exactly at `period_end` is not, and a `period_end` that is not strictly after `period_start` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_TIME_WINDOW`. It also carries a `target_sample_count`, the number of cases the period must accumulate before it may be closed, which must be at least 1. A new period has `status` of `OPEN`, no `closed_at`, no `locked_at`, and both counters — `selected_review_count` and `finalized_review_count` — at 0.

The period's `sampling_mode` decides which of the two selection routes it will accept: `RANDOM` accepts only the systematic random draw, `TARGETED` accepts only the case-by-case targeted selection, and `MIXED` accepts both. Attempting the wrong route for the mode — a random draw against a targeted-only period, or a targeted selection against a random-only period — is refused with `ERR_SAMPLING_MODE_MISMATCH`. Both routes require the period to still be `OPEN`; once it is closed or locked, selection is refused with `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_OPEN`. Both routes also require that at least one sample slot remains: when `selected_review_count` has already reached `target_sample_count`, further selection is refused with `ERR_SAMPLE_QUOTA_FILLED`.

The random draw is called "random" by the accreditation standard but is deliberately **deterministic and reproducible**, because the accrediting body may ask the centre to demonstrate the same sample twice. No random number generator and no identifier ordering participate. The supervisor supplies `requested_count`, which must be at least 1. The candidate pool is assembled from call records whose handling dispatcher belongs to the period's programme, whose `call_type` equals the period's `call_type`, whose `status` is `INGESTED`, whose `received_at` falls inside the period's half-open window, and whose handling dispatcher's `dispatch_privilege` is anything other than `SUSPENDED` — a suspended dispatcher's calls are withheld from the systematic pool entirely. If that pool is empty, the draw is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_CALLS`. Otherwise the pool is sorted by `received_at` ascending and, where two calls share an instant, by `call_reference` ascending in plain ASCII string order, which is unambiguous because call references are globally unique.

From the ordered pool the server takes a systematic stride. The number of cases actually drawn is the smallest of three quantities: the `requested_count`, the quota still remaining (the period's `target_sample_count` less its current `selected_review_count`), and the size of the pool. The stride is the pool size divided by `requested_count`, discarding any fractional part, with a minimum stride of 1. Positions are then taken from the ordered pool at index zero, at one stride, at two strides, and so on for as long as the running index remains inside the pool, and the first however-many-are-needed of those positions are drawn. Should the stride run off the end of the pool before enough cases have been collected, the shortfall is made up from the still-unselected head of the same ordered pool, in the same `received_at`-then-`call_reference` order, so that exactly the intended number of reviews is always created whenever the pool is large enough to supply it. Each drawn call yields one review with `selection_mode` of `RANDOM` and `selection_reason` stamped as `SYSTEMATIC_DRAW`; each drawn call moves `INGESTED → SELECTED`; and the period's `selected_review_count` grows by the number drawn.

Targeted selection, available to a `QaSupervisor` and also to a `QaReviewer`, pulls one nominated case in. It carries the `call_record_id` of the call and a `selection_reason` documenting the justification — one of `COMPLAINT`, `HIGH_RISK_OUTCOME`, `SENTINEL_FLAG`, or `NEW_HIRE_AUDIT` (the fifth member, `SYSTEMATIC_DRAW`, is reserved for the random draw and is not a supervisor's to claim). Three checks are specific to this route. The nominated call's handling dispatcher must belong to the period's programme, or the request fails for programme scope. The call's `call_type` must equal the period's `call_type`, or it fails with `ERR_CALL_TYPE_MISMATCH`. The call's `received_at` must fall inside the period's half-open window, or it fails with `ERR_CALL_OUTSIDE_PERIOD`. And a call already in `status` `SELECTED` cannot be pulled in a second time — that is refused with `ERR_CALL_ALREADY_SELECTED`. A successful targeted selection creates one review with `selection_mode` of `TARGETED`, moves the call to `SELECTED`, and increments the period's `selected_review_count` by one.

Whichever route created it, a new review is born in `status` `SELECTED` with `current_sheet_round` at 0, with `provisional_composite_score`, `pre_penalty_composite_score`, `determinant_accuracy`, `penalty_points`, `final_composite_score` and `compliant` all null, with `rebuttal_round_count` at 0, and with no `scored_at`, `finalized_at`, `voided_at` or `void_reason`. Two derived references are captured at birth and never revisited. The first is `dispatcher_id`, copied from the call record so that the review is permanently attributed to the dispatcher who handled the call even if the roster later changes. The second is `high_risk`, which is true when any one of three conditions holds — the call's `call_type` is `CARDIAC_ARREST`, or the call's `cardiac_arrest_confirmed` flag is true, or the level letter of the call's `dispatched_determinant_code` is `E` — and false otherwise. A high-risk review cannot be finalized until a clinical record has been opened on it and signed off.

The third thing captured at birth is the protocol pin, and it is the reason this system can still reproduce a two-year-old score. Each new review resolves `protocol_version_id` by searching the `MPDS_CARDSET` family of the period's programme for the single version whose `status` is `APPROVED`, `ACTIVE` or `SUPERSEDED` and whose governing interval contains the call's `received_at` — `effective_from` at or before the instant and `effective_to` either null or strictly after it. Draft versions never pin. The approval-time interval-exclusivity rule guarantees that at most one version can match, so the search either finds exactly one row or none; when it finds none, the whole selection is refused with `ERR_NO_PROTOCOL_VERSION_IN_FORCE`. The four section weights this review will be graded on are read from the pinned row forever afterwards, and are **never** re-pointed to whatever version happens to be active when the review is eventually finalized.

Closing a period is a `QaSupervisor` action carrying an optional `closing_note`, and it moves the period `OPEN → CLOSED` with `closed_at` stamped from the logical clock. Closing demands that the quota has been met: `selected_review_count` must be at or above `target_sample_count`, with exact equality passing, and a premature close is refused with `ERR_SAMPLE_QUOTA_NOT_MET`. There is a second, sharper gate for centres with a struggling dispatcher: if any dispatcher whose cases the period selected currently sits in `compliance_posture` `PROBATION`, then the period's `target_sample_count` must be at or above the programme's `escalated_sample_floor` before the period may be closed, and a period sampled too thinly for a dispatcher in that condition is refused with `ERR_ESCALATED_SAMPLING_REQUIRED`. Closing shuts off further selection but nothing else: reviews already selected inside a closed period remain fully scorable, disputable and finalizable. Only the accreditation lock described in §3.9 freezes them.

### 3.5. Scoring the Sheet: Sections, Percentages and Determinant Accuracy

Scoring is the `QaReviewer`'s work and nobody else's. Submitting a score against a review in `SELECTED` takes it `SELECTED → SCORED`; submitting a further score against a review already in `SCORED` is also permitted and keeps it in `SCORED`, a self-loop that supersedes the previous sheet. Sheets themselves are **append-only**: each submission creates a new score sheet whose `sheet_round` is one greater than the review's `current_sheet_round` at the moment of submission, and no earlier sheet is ever mutated or deleted. The sheet with the highest round is "the current sheet", and the review's `current_sheet_round` is set to that round on every submission. Every sheet records `submitted_by_user_id` from the submitting reviewer's `X-User-ID`, which is the comparand the segregation-of-duties rule uses at finalization, and each sheet is linked to its parent by `review_id`.

The sheet body is four pairs of integers, one pair per protocol section: `case_entry_points_earned` with `case_entry_points_possible`, `key_questions_points_earned` with `key_questions_points_possible`, `pre_arrival_points_earned` with `pre_arrival_points_possible`, and `final_coding_points_earned` with `final_coding_points_possible`. Every one of the eight must be at least 0, and within each pair the points earned must not exceed the points possible — a sheet violating either is rejected as an invalid request. A points-possible of 0 is legitimate and carries a specific meaning: the section did not apply to this call, as when a chest-pain call indicated no pre-arrival instructions at all. The reviewer may also attach free-text `reviewer_notes`, and must supply `reviewer_determinant_code`, the code they judge to have been correct for the call.

From the four pairs the server derives four percentages — `case_entry_pct`, `key_questions_pct`, `pre_arrival_pct`, and `final_coding_pct`. Each is the points earned divided by the points possible, multiplied by one hundred, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. A section whose points possible is 0 yields exactly 100.00 rather than an undefined value, so an inapplicable section neither helps nor hurts the case under the unweighted view. The sheet's `provisional_composite_score` is then the arithmetic mean of those four already-rounded percentages, rounded half up to 1 decimal place — under this view each of the four sections contributes exactly one quarter, regardless of the weights on the pinned protocol version, and no determinant penalty is applied. The same value is written onto the review's own `provisional_composite_score`, and the review's `scored_at` is stamped from the logical clock. This provisional figure is what the reviewer sees and what the dispatcher sees during the rebuttal window; it is not the figure that enters any ledger.

The sheet also grades the dispatcher's coding decision. The reviewer's code is decoded exactly as the dispatched code was, yielding `reviewer_priority_rank`, `reviewer_response_mode` and `reviewer_resource`. Two deviation flags then compare the two decodings: `response_mode_deviation` is true when the reviewer's response mode differs from the call's `response_mode_assigned`, and `resource_deviation` is true when the reviewer's resource tier differs from the call's `resource_assigned`. From those the sheet's `determinant_accuracy` is classified, and the four classes are decided in this order. If the reviewer's code string is identical to the dispatched code string, the accuracy is `ACCURATE`. Otherwise, if neither deviation flag is set — the codes differ, but the response mode and the resource tier they commit the centre to are the same — the accuracy is `MINOR_MISCODE`, because the miscoding had no operational consequence. Otherwise there is a real deviation, and its direction decides: when the reviewer's priority rank is greater than the dispatched rank, the correct code was more acute than the one sent and the call was **under-triaged**, so the accuracy is `UNDER_TRIAGE`; when the reviewer's rank is lower, the call was sent hotter or heavier than it needed and the accuracy is `OVER_TRIAGE`.

Every score submission must carry `expected_review_version` matching the review's current `version`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_REVIEW_VERSION` as described in §2.7. Two further preconditions guard the write. The review's sampling period must not be `LOCKED`; a frozen work-product review can no longer be re-scored and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_PERIOD_LOCKED`. And no rebuttal on the review may still be sitting in `SUBMITTED` — while a dispute is undecided the sheet is out of the reviewer's hands, and an attempt to overwrite it, like any attempt to score a review that is not in `SELECTED` or `SCORED`, is rejected with `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_SCORABLE`. Each accepted submission increments the review's `version` by 1, which is precisely why a client must re-read the review before submitting a second sheet.

### 3.6. High-Risk Cases: Arrest Recognition and Dispatcher-Assisted CPR

A cardiac-arrest case is not judged on paperwork alone. When a review is flagged `high_risk`, a `QaReviewer` opens a clinical record on it, supplying the `outcome_category` the centre recorded for the patient — `ROSC_FIELD` when return of spontaneous circulation was achieved before transport, `ROSC_HOSPITAL` when it was achieved at hospital, `NO_ROSC` when it was not achieved, and `NOT_ARREST` when the patient turned out not to be in arrest at all. The record is linked by `review_id`, and at most one such record may exist per review; a second attempt is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_HIGH_RISK_REVIEW`. A newly opened record has `status` of `PENDING`, with every observation, index and classification field null. Opening the record has an immediate consequence for the parent review: from that moment it cannot be finalized until the record has been signed off.

Grading the resuscitation performance is a second `QaReviewer` step, permitted only while the record is `PENDING` — otherwise it is refused with `ERR_HRCR_NOT_SCORABLE` — and only while the parent review is in `SELECTED` or `SCORED`. It carries five observations. `cardiac_arrest_recognized` states whether the dispatcher recognised the arrest during case entry at all. `recognition_time_sec` is the number of seconds from call answer to that recognition, and `time_to_first_compression_sec` the number of seconds from call answer to the first bystander compression; both must be at least 0. `dacpr_instructions_delivered` states whether the dispatcher-assisted CPR instructions were actually read out, and `compressions_coached` whether the dispatcher coached compression tempo. Recording them moves the record `PENDING → DACPR_SCORED` and computes `compression_compliant`, `dacpr_index` and `dacpr_pass`.

The `dacpr_index` is a weighted performance index on a 0-to-100 scale, and its computation begins with a hard gate: **if the dispatcher did not recognise the arrest, nothing downstream can earn credit.** When `cardiac_arrest_recognized` is false, `compression_compliant` is set to false, `dacpr_index` is set to 0.0, `dacpr_pass` is set to false, and no further arithmetic is performed. Otherwise three sub-scores are computed, each on a 0-to-100 scale and each kept unrounded.

The recognition sub-score decays linearly against a 90-second limit: it is one hundred multiplied by the amount by which 90 seconds exceeds `recognition_time_sec`, divided by 90, with the shortfall treated as zero once the recognition time reaches or passes 90 seconds. Recognition at the instant of answer would therefore score the full one hundred, recognition at 30 seconds scores two thirds of it, and recognition at or beyond a minute and a half scores none.

The compression sub-score protects a 30-second grace floor and then decays to zero at a 2-minute limit. `compression_compliant` is true when `time_to_first_compression_sec` is at or below 120 seconds — exactly 120 seconds is compliant. The decay span is the 90 seconds between the 30-second floor and the 120-second limit. The sub-score is one hundred multiplied by the remaining time, divided by that 90-second span, where the remaining time is the amount by which 120 seconds exceeds the observed time, treated as zero once the observed time reaches or passes 120 seconds and capped at the full 90-second span so that any first compression inside 30 seconds earns the whole sub-score.

The instruction sub-score is the sum of two independent observations rather than a single flag: 60 points when `dacpr_instructions_delivered` is true and nothing when it is false, plus 40 points when `compressions_coached` is true and nothing when it is false. Reading the instructions without coaching the tempo therefore earns 60 of the 100 available points.

The three sub-scores are then combined under one of two weight sets, chosen by whether a bystander was already compressing when the call landed. When the call record's `bystander_cpr_in_progress` is false — the dispatcher is working a caller who is doing nothing — recognition carries a weight of 40, compression 45, and instruction 15. When it is true, the dispatcher's recognition speed matters more and the time to first compression matters less, so recognition carries 55, compression 30, and instruction 15. The raw index is each sub-score multiplied by its weight, the three products summed, and the total divided by one hundred; `dacpr_index` is that raw value rounded half up to 1 decimal place. One cap then applies after the rounding: when `compression_compliant` is false, a late first compression caps the index at 50.0 no matter how strong the rest of the performance was, so the stored `dacpr_index` becomes the lower of the rounded index and 50.0. Finally `dacpr_pass` is true when `dacpr_index` is at or above 75.0 and false otherwise.

Sign-off belongs exclusively to the `MedicalDirector`, is permitted only while the record is in `DACPR_SCORED` — otherwise it is refused with `ERR_HRCR_NOT_SIGNABLE` — and carries a `classification` of either `SENTINEL_EVENT` or `COACHING_ONLY` plus optional `director_notes`. Whichever branch is taken, the record's `classification`, `signed_off_by_user_id` and `signed_off_at` are written. The two branches then diverge sharply, and the difference is the whole point of the clinical review.

Classifying a case as a sentinel event takes the record `DACPR_SCORED → SENTINEL_EVENT`. Exclusively on this branch, an automatic coaching plan of `CRITICAL` severity carrying 8.0 required hours and a `focus_area` of `DACPR` is created or upgraded for the dispatcher against this review, and the sentinel counters are recomputed — both the dispatcher's own `sentinel_event_count` and the programme's `sentinel_event_count`. Because the hours ledger and the sentinel count have both moved, the dispatcher's `remediation_hours_owed` is recomputed and their `dispatch_privilege` re-derived, and a first sentinel event alone is sufficient to pull that privilege down to `RESTRICTED`. Sentinel counting deliberately does not wait for the parent review to be finalized: a signed-off sentinel event restricts the dispatcher from the moment of sign-off, and only voiding the underlying review removes it.

Classifying a case as coaching-only takes the record `DACPR_SCORED → COACHING_ONLY`, and it is gated: a record whose `dacpr_index` is below 50.0 is too poor a performance to close as a coaching matter, and an attempt to do so is refused with `ERR_SENTINEL_CLASSIFICATION_REQUIRED` — below that floor the classification must be `SENTINEL_EVENT`. Exclusively on this branch, an `ADVISORY` automatic coaching plan carrying 2.0 required hours and a `focus_area` of `DACPR` is created or upgraded **only when `dacpr_pass` is false**; when the case passed on the index, no plan is created at all. Also exclusively on this branch, the sentinel counters are not touched and `dispatch_privilege` is unaffected by the classification itself.

Both branches rejoin. When the parent review is finalized, a clinical record sitting in either `SENTINEL_EVENT` or `COACHING_ONLY` transitions to `CLOSED`, and a closed record accepts no further clinical edits.

### 3.7. Disputes, Adjudication, Finalization and Reversal

A dispatcher who believes a score is wrong may say so, and the right to do it is theirs alone — only the `Dispatcher` role may file, and only against a review whose `dispatcher_id` is their own `X-User-ID`, with any other dispatcher's attempt refused with `ERR_NOT_CASE_OWNER`. A dispute carries a `grounds_category` — `SCORING_DISPUTE` when the section marks are contested, `DETERMINANT_DISPUTE` when the reviewer's code is contested, `PROTOCOL_AMBIGUITY` when the card set itself is claimed to be unclear, or `AUDIO_QUALITY` when the recording is claimed to be unusable — together with a written `narrative`. Filing is possible only against a review in `SCORED`; a review in any other status is refused with `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_DISPUTABLE`. Two further gates apply. The review's `rebuttal_round_count` must still be below the programme's `max_rebuttal_rounds`, and a case that has exhausted its rounds is refused with `ERR_REBUTTAL_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`. And the dispute must arrive no later than the review's `scored_at` plus the programme's `rebuttal_window_days`, with the boundary instant itself still inside the window; a late dispute is refused with `ERR_REBUTTAL_WINDOW_CLOSED`. Note that this window runs from the score, not from selection, so an upheld dispute followed by a fresh sheet restarts it.

An accepted filing creates a rebuttal in `status` `SUBMITTED`, linked by `review_id`, with `round_index` one greater than the review's `rebuttal_round_count` at the time of filing and `submitted_by_user_id` recording the filing dispatcher — always the same identifier as the review's `dispatcher_id`. The review's `rebuttal_round_count` is set to that same round index, the review moves `SCORED → DISPUTED`, and its `version` is incremented. `decided_by_user_id` and `decision_notes` remain null while the dispute is open.

Adjudication belongs to a `QaSupervisor` and takes a `decision` of either `UPHELD` or `REJECTED`, with optional `decision_notes` recording the rationale. The rebuttal must still be in `SUBMITTED` — a second decision on an already-adjudicated rebuttal is refused with `ERR_REBUTTAL_ALREADY_DECIDED` — and its parent review must be in `DISPUTED`. Upholding takes the rebuttal `SUBMITTED → UPHELD`, records `decided_by_user_id`, and sends the review backwards: `DISPUTED → SELECTED`, with `provisional_composite_score` and `scored_at` both cleared to null because the case must be scored again from scratch. The review's `current_sheet_round` is deliberately **not** rewound — the invalidated sheet stays in the append-only audit log, and the replacement sheet will carry the next round number up. Rejecting takes the rebuttal `SUBMITTED → REJECTED`, records `decided_by_user_id`, and returns the review `DISPUTED → SCORED` with its `provisional_composite_score` and `scored_at` preserved intact, because the existing sheet stands. Either decision increments the review's `version`. This is the cycle referred to throughout: `SELECTED → SCORED → DISPUTED → SELECTED`, bounded only by the programme's round cap.

Finalization is a `QaSupervisor` action, optionally carrying a `finalization_note`, and it is the moment a review stops being an opinion and becomes a number in a ledger. Five preconditions guard it. The review must be in `SCORED`, and any other status is refused with `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_FINALIZABLE`. The finalizing `X-User-ID` must differ from the current sheet's `submitted_by_user_id`, or the request is refused for segregation of duties. The review's sampling period must not be `LOCKED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_PERIOD_LOCKED`. If the review is `high_risk`, a clinical record must exist on it in `SENTINEL_EVENT` or `COACHING_ONLY` — a record still sitting in `PENDING` or `DACPR_SCORED`, or no record at all, is refused with `ERR_HIGH_RISK_SIGN_OFF_REQUIRED`. And the logical clock must be no later than the review's `created_at` plus the programme's `review_deadline_days`, with the boundary instant still inside; a review left too long is refused with `ERR_REVIEW_DEADLINE_PASSED`.

The scoring arithmetic at finalization is a different view of the same sheet, and this is where the pinned protocol version finally earns its keep. The four already-rounded section percentages of the **current** sheet — the one whose `sheet_round` equals the review's `current_sheet_round`, superseded sheets being excluded entirely — are each multiplied by the corresponding weight on the review's pinned protocol version, the four products are summed, and the total is rounded half up to 1 decimal place to give `pre_penalty_composite_score`. Under the default weighting this makes key-question and pre-arrival failures dominate the accreditation figure, whereas the provisional figure the reviewer saw treated all four sections alike; the two numbers on the same sheet are routinely different, and that is intended. The current sheet's `determinant_accuracy` is copied onto the review at the same moment.

The penalty for miscoding is then subtracted, and the order of the two steps below is pinned because reversing them yields a different answer for identical inputs. First a base penalty is read from the accuracy class: `ACCURATE` carries 0.0, `MINOR_MISCODE` carries 2.0, `OVER_TRIAGE` carries 5.0, and `UNDER_TRIAGE` carries 10.0 — under-triage is punished five times as hard as an inconsequential miscode, because it is the error that sends a basic-life-support crew cold to a dying patient. Second, a tier multiplier is selected **from the pre-penalty score, never from the post-penalty result**: a pre-penalty score at or above 90.0 selects the high tier and multiplies the base by 1.5, a pre-penalty score at or above 75.0 but below 90.0 selects the mid tier and multiplies by 1.0, and anything below 75.0 selects the low tier and multiplies by 0.5. The base multiplied by the tier multiplier, rounded half up to 1 decimal place, is `penalty_points`; the effect is that a nearly-perfect sheet is punished harder for a coding error than a weak sheet, because the centre expects a dispatcher who scored 95 on the card to have coded it correctly. `final_composite_score` is the pre-penalty score less the penalty points, floored at 0.0 so it can never go negative, rounded half up to 1 decimal place. `compliant` is true when `final_composite_score` is at or above the programme's `target_compliance_pct` and false otherwise. The review's `status` becomes `FINALIZED` and `finalized_at` is stamped from the logical clock.

Cascade A then runs outward from that single number. The sampling period's `finalized_review_count` is recomputed as the count of finalized reviews in the period. Any clinical record on the review moves to `CLOSED`. The dispatcher's compliance figures are recomputed from scratch over every review of theirs in `FINALIZED` — reviews in `SELECTED`, `SCORED`, `DISPUTED` or `VOIDED` contribute nothing: `reviewed_case_count` is the number of such reviews, `compliance_points_total` is the sum of their `final_composite_score` values rounded half up to 1 decimal place, and `compliance_pct` is that total divided by the count, rounded half up to 1 decimal place. When the count is zero, `compliance_pct` is null rather than 0.0. From the percentage, `compliance_posture` follows: `GOOD` when it is at or above the programme's `target_compliance_pct`, `WATCH` when it is below that but at or above `watch_floor_pct`, and `PROBATION` when it is below the watch floor; a dispatcher with no finalized reviews is `UNRATED`. The dispatcher's `sentinel_event_count` is recomputed as the number of clinical records classified `SENTINEL_EVENT` whose parent review is not `VOIDED`. The automatic coaching plan described in §3.8 is then created or upgraded, `remediation_hours_owed` is recomputed, and `dispatch_privilege` is re-derived — landing on `RESTRICTED` as soon as the owed hours exceed the programme's `hours_owed_restriction_threshold`.

Voiding is the compensating action and belongs to a `QaSupervisor`. It carries a `void_reason` of `AUDIO_UNAVAILABLE`, `WRONG_DISPATCHER`, or `DUPLICATE_CASE` — the fourth member, `PERIOD_LOCKED`, is stamped only by the accreditation lock cascade and is not a supervisor's to supply. A review may be voided from `SELECTED`, `SCORED`, `DISPUTED` or `FINALIZED`; a second attempt on an already-voided review is refused with `ERR_REVIEW_ALREADY_VOIDED`, and voiding a finalized review inside a `LOCKED` period is refused with `ERR_PERIOD_LOCKED`. Every void sets `status` to `VOIDED`, stamps `voided_at` from the logical clock, records the `void_reason`, returns the underlying call record from `SELECTED` to `INGESTED`, and recomputes the period's `selected_review_count` — which is what returns a consumed sample slot to the quota, since that counter counts only reviews in `SELECTED`, `SCORED`, `DISPUTED` or `FINALIZED`. Voiding from `SELECTED` has no ledger consequences at all, because the review never contributed a compliance point. Voiding from `SCORED` additionally clears `provisional_composite_score` to null. Voiding from `DISPUTED` leaves any rebuttal in `SUBMITTED` exactly where it is, but that rebuttal can no longer be decided, because its review has reached a terminal state.

Voiding from `FINALIZED` reverses Cascade A link by link, and it is here that the full-recomputation discipline pays off. The review's `final_composite_score`, `pre_penalty_composite_score`, `penalty_points`, `determinant_accuracy` and `compliant` are all cleared to null, and both period counters are recomputed. Because the review has left the finalized set, the dispatcher's `reviewed_case_count`, `compliance_points_total`, `compliance_pct` and `compliance_posture` are recomputed over the remaining finalized reviews — and because the recomputation is a full walk rather than a subtraction, the point ledger returns to exactly the value it held before the finalization, with no drift. Because the source review is gone, every automatic coaching plan attributed to it — those whose `origin` is `AUTO`, whose `source_review_id` is this review, and whose `status` is `ASSIGNED`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `SUSPENDED` — moves to `CANCELLED` with `closure_reason` set to `SOURCE_VOIDED`; any `SCHEDULED` session already booked against such a plan is left exactly where it is, but it can no longer be completed, because completion requires an open plan. Because those plans have left the open set, `remediation_hours_owed` is recomputed and `dispatch_privilege` is re-derived, returning to `FULL` once both ledgers are clear. And if a clinical record hung off the voided review, the dispatcher's and the programme's `sentinel_event_count` are both recomputed, since sentinel counting excludes voided reviews.

### 3.8. Coaching, Retraining Delivery and the Remediation Hours Ledger

Coaching plans arise in two ways, and the difference is recorded in `origin`. A plan whose `origin` is `AUTO` was manufactured by the system as a consequence of a finalization or a clinical sign-off; a plan whose `origin` is `MANUAL` was written by a `QaSupervisor` who decided a dispatcher needed attention. Every plan names the coached `dispatcher_id`, a `severity` of `ADVISORY`, `STANDARD`, or `CRITICAL`, and a `focus_area` naming the protocol area the coaching targets — `CASE_ENTRY`, `KEY_QUESTIONS`, `PRE_ARRIVAL_INSTRUCTIONS`, `FINAL_CODING`, `DETERMINANT_CODING`, or `DACPR`. Severity is not decorative: it fixes both `severity_rank` — 1 for `ADVISORY`, 2 for `STANDARD`, 3 for `CRITICAL` — and `required_hours`, which is 2.0 hours for `ADVISORY`, 4.0 hours for `STANDARD`, and 8.0 hours for `CRITICAL`. A plan may also record the `source_review_id` of the review that prompted it, and a `due_at` by which the remediation is expected to be complete, which is used for reporting only.

The automatic path is an **upsert keyed on the pairing of `dispatcher_id` and `source_review_id`**, and at most one automatic plan exists per such pairing. Its severity is chosen by the first matching clause of an ordered ladder, arranged most-severe first. When the finalized review's `determinant_accuracy` is `UNDER_TRIAGE`, or the clinical record's `classification` is `SENTINEL_EVENT`, the plan is `CRITICAL`, with a `focus_area` of `DETERMINANT_CODING` in the under-triage case and `DACPR` in the sentinel case. Otherwise, when the dispatcher's freshly recomputed `compliance_posture` is `PROBATION`, the plan is `STANDARD` and targets the weakest section. Otherwise, when the posture is `WATCH`, the plan is `ADVISORY` and targets the weakest section. Otherwise, when the clinical record was classified `COACHING_ONLY` and its `dacpr_pass` is false, the plan is `ADVISORY` with a `focus_area` of `DACPR`. And if none of those clauses match, no plan is created at all — a dispatcher whose posture came out `GOOD` on an accurately coded case is left alone.

"The weakest section" means the lowest of the four section percentages on the review's current sheet, mapping `case_entry_pct` to `CASE_ENTRY`, `key_questions_pct` to `KEY_QUESTIONS`, `pre_arrival_pct` to `PRE_ARRIVAL_INSTRUCTIONS`, and `final_coding_pct` to `FINAL_CODING`. Ties are broken by that same fixed section order — case entry first, then key questions, then pre-arrival instructions, then final coding — so a sheet scoring 80.00 on both case entry and final coding coaches case entry.

When the upsert finds no existing automatic plan on the key — considering only plans in `ASSIGNED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `SUSPENDED` or `VERIFIED` — it creates one with `origin` `AUTO`, the chosen severity and focus, the matching `severity_rank` and `required_hours`, `credited_hours` at 0.00, `status` `ASSIGNED`, no `closure_reason`, no `verified_at`, and a `due_at` set 30 days after the logical clock. When it does find one, the plan **ratchets upward only**: if the newly chosen severity outranks the existing `severity_rank`, then `severity`, `severity_rank`, `focus_area` and `required_hours` are all raised to the new values while `credited_hours`, `status` and `due_at` are preserved untouched; if the existing plan already carries an equal or higher rank, nothing at all changes. A dispatcher who collects an advisory plan from a watch posture and then a critical plan from a sentinel sign-off on the same case ends up with one plan owing 8.0 hours, not two plans owing 10.0.

A `QaSupervisor` creating a plan by hand supplies the `dispatcher_id`, the `severity`, the `focus_area`, the `expected_dispatcher_version` demanded by §2.7, and optionally a `due_at` and the `source_review_id` of the review that prompted it. The plan is written with `origin` `MANUAL`, `status` `ASSIGNED`, `required_hours` from the severity table and `credited_hours` at 0.00. A manual create is a deliberate reset of the dispatcher's coaching: **every other open plan of that dispatcher is cancelled**, moving from `ASSIGNED`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `SUSPENDED` to `CANCELLED` with `closure_reason` set to `SUPERSEDED`. Consequently the dispatcher's `remediation_hours_owed` is recomputed, their `dispatch_privilege` re-derived, and the dispatcher row's `version` incremented. One collision is refused rather than absorbed: if a `source_review_id` is supplied and an automatic plan already exists on that same dispatcher-and-review pairing, the create is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_COACHING_PLAN`. Supplying no `source_review_id` leaves the field null and cannot collide.

Delivering the hours is what closes a plan. A `QaSupervisor` schedules a retraining session against a plan, supplying the `protocol_version_id` of the curriculum to be taught, a `starts_at`, an `ends_at` that must be strictly after it (otherwise `ERR_INVALID_TIME_WINDOW`), and a `modality` of `CLASSROOM`, `SIMULATION`, `ONLINE`, or `ONE_ON_ONE`. The curriculum version must be in `APPROVED` or `ACTIVE` — a draft or superseded curriculum is refused with `ERR_CURRICULUM_VERSION_NOT_TRAINABLE` — and must belong to the coached dispatcher's programme, or the request is refused for programme scope. The plan itself must be open: scheduling against a plan in `VERIFIED`, `SUSPENDED` or `CANCELLED` is refused with `ERR_PLAN_NOT_OPEN`. There is also a humane scheduling rule: a dispatcher cannot be double-booked, and the check is generous about travel and turnaround. The new session's window is widened by **45 minutes on each side** and compared, inclusive of the endpoints, against every other `SCHEDULED` or `COMPLETED` session of the same dispatcher; any touching or overlap is refused with `ERR_SESSION_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. Because the comparison is closed rather than half-open, two genuinely back-to-back sessions do collide — the buffer is a hard rest requirement, not a formality. Sessions already `CANCELLED` release their window and no longer participate in the check.

A successful scheduling writes the session against its plan through `coaching_plan_id`, with `status` `SCHEDULED`, no `attendance_confirmed`, no `cancel_reason` and no `completed_at`, and computes `duration_hours` as the elapsed time from `starts_at` to `ends_at` expressed in hours — the elapsed seconds divided by 3600 — rounded half up to 2 decimal places. If the plan was in `ASSIGNED`, scheduling moves it `ASSIGNED → IN_PROGRESS`.

Completing a session is a `QaSupervisor` attestation carrying `attendance_confirmed`, which must be true; a completion that asserts the dispatcher did not attend is rejected as an invalid request. The session must still be in `SCHEDULED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_SESSION_NOT_COMPLETABLE`; its plan must be in `ASSIGNED` or `IN_PROGRESS`; and the logical clock must be at or after the session's `ends_at`, since a class that has not finished cannot be attested. Completion moves the session `SCHEDULED → COMPLETED`, stamps `completed_at` from the logical clock, and recomputes the plan's `credited_hours` as the sum of `duration_hours` over every session of the plan in `COMPLETED`, rounded half up to 2 decimal places — sessions in `SCHEDULED` or `CANCELLED` contribute nothing. When `credited_hours` reaches `required_hours`, with exact equality sufficing, the plan bubbles up `IN_PROGRESS → VERIFIED` and stamps `verified_at` from the logical clock. Then, because a verified plan has left the open set, the dispatcher's `remediation_hours_owed` is recomputed and their `dispatch_privilege` re-derived.

Cancelling a session is the mirror image and carries a free-text `cancel_reason`. A session in `SCHEDULED` may be cancelled, in which case `credited_hours` never changed and no ledger moves at all; a session already in `COMPLETED` may also be cancelled, which is a genuine compensating action: `completed_at` is cleared to null and `credited_hours` is recomputed over the remaining completed sessions, returning it to exactly its pre-completion value. If that drops `credited_hours` back below `required_hours` on a plan that had reached `VERIFIED`, the plan cycles `VERIFIED → IN_PROGRESS` and its `verified_at` is cleared to null. Either way the dispatcher's owed hours and privilege are recomputed. Cancelling a session already in `CANCELLED` is refused with `ERR_SESSION_NOT_CANCELLABLE`.

The owed-hours ledger itself is a full recomputation over the dispatcher's **open** plans — those in `ASSIGNED`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `SUSPENDED`, with `VERIFIED` and `CANCELLED` plans contributing nothing. For each open plan the shortfall is the plan's `required_hours` less its `credited_hours`, floored at 0.00 so that over-delivery on one plan can never offset another plan's shortfall; `remediation_hours_owed` is the sum of those per-plan shortfalls, rounded half up to 2 decimal places.

`dispatch_privilege` is derived from three inputs — the owed-hours ledger, `certification_status`, and `sentinel_event_count` — and is re-evaluated after every recomputation of any of them. The clauses are evaluated top-down and the first match wins. When `sentinel_event_count` is 2 or more, the privilege is `SUSPENDED`. Otherwise, when `remediation_hours_owed` is **strictly greater** than the programme's `hours_owed_restriction_threshold`, the privilege is `RESTRICTED` — owing exactly the threshold leaves a dispatcher at `FULL`. Otherwise, when `certification_status` is `LAPSED`, the privilege is `RESTRICTED`. Otherwise, when `sentinel_event_count` is 1, the privilege is `RESTRICTED`. Otherwise it is `FULL`. The two grades of curtailment differ in effect: a `RESTRICTED` dispatcher keeps working and their calls stay sampleable, whereas a `SUSPENDED` dispatcher's `INGESTED` calls are withheld from the systematic-draw candidate pool described in §3.4.

### 3.9. Accreditation Reporting, Locking the Record, and Reading Back

Accreditation reporting is the `AccreditationOfficer`'s exclusive province. A report is built as a draft over a finalization window: `window_start` is the inclusive lower bound and `window_end` the exclusive upper bound on the review's `finalized_at`, and a `window_end` that is not strictly after `window_start` is refused with `ERR_REPORT_WINDOW_INVALID`. The `variant` selects the aggregation, and the two variants exist because two audiences ask different questions: `CASE_WEIGHTED` is the figure the accrediting body audits, and `DISPATCHER_EQUAL_WEIGHT` is the figure the oversight committee reads for staffing equity.

The report pool is every review in `FINALIZED` whose handling dispatcher belongs to the programme in question and whose `finalized_at` falls inside the half-open window. Voided reviews are excluded even when they were finalized earlier inside that same window. If the pool is empty the report is refused outright with `ERR_NO_FINALIZED_REVIEWS_IN_WINDOW` — the system will not file an empty attestation. From a non-empty pool, `review_count` is the size of the pool, `compliant_review_count` is how many of those reviews have `compliant` true, and `dispatcher_count` is the number of distinct dispatchers contributing at least one review to it.

`compliance_pct` is then computed one of two ways. Under the case-weighted variant every reviewed case counts once: the figure is one hundred multiplied by `compliant_review_count`, divided by `review_count`, rounded half up to 1 decimal place — so a dispatcher whose calls were sampled heavily dominates the programme number. Under the dispatcher-equal-weight variant every dispatcher counts once regardless of how many of their calls were sampled: for each distinct dispatcher in the pool, that dispatcher's own compliant rate is their compliant in-window review count divided by their total in-window review count and is kept **unrounded**; those per-dispatcher rates are summed, the sum is divided by `dispatcher_count`, the result is multiplied by one hundred, and only that final programme mean is rounded half up to 1 decimal place. The two variants can disagree about the same data, and a heavily sampled weak dispatcher who sinks the case-weighted figure may leave the dispatcher-equal-weight figure comfortably above target — which is exactly why both are reported.

The high-risk sub-figure is computed identically in both variants. `high_risk_review_count` is the number of pool reviews whose `high_risk` is true, and `high_risk_pass_count` is how many of those have a clinical record whose `dacpr_pass` is true. `high_risk_compliance_pct` is one hundred multiplied by the pass count, divided by the high-risk count, rounded half up to 1 decimal place; when the high-risk count is 0 the field is null rather than 0.0, because a window containing no cardiac-arrest cases is not a window in which the centre failed them. Finally `threshold_met` is true when `compliance_pct` is at or above the programme's `target_compliance_pct` **and** either `high_risk_compliance_pct` is null or it is at or above the programme's `high_risk_target_pct`. A freshly built report has `status` `DRAFT`, no `submitted_to` and no `submitted_at`.

Filing the report is the act that changes the programme's standing and freezes the evidence. The officer supplies `submitted_to`, naming either `MEDICAL_DIRECTOR` or `OVERSIGHT_COMMITTEE`; the report must still be in `DRAFT`, and a second filing is refused with `ERR_REPORT_ALREADY_SUBMITTED`. Filing takes the report `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`, records the recipient and stamps `submitted_at` from the logical clock, and then Cascade C runs.

Its first consequence is on the programme. `submitted_report_count` is recomputed as the number of the programme's reports in `SUBMITTED`; draft reports never count. `latest_compliance_pct` is copied verbatim from the winning report, and `accreditation_status` becomes `ACCREDITED` when that report's `threshold_met` is true and `AT_RISK` when it is false. The winning report is the submitted report with the **greatest `window_end`**, and where two tie on that, the one with the greatest `created_at`, and where two still tie, the one with the greatest `submitted_at` — never the one with the larger identifier. A programme with no submitted reports at all reads `SETUP` with a null `latest_compliance_pct`. The programme's own `sentinel_event_count` is recomputed at the same time as the number of clinical records classified `SENTINEL_EVENT` whose parent review is not `VOIDED` across the whole programme.

Its second consequence is the lock. Every sampling period of the programme that is in `CLOSED` and whose half-open window overlaps the report's half-open window transitions `CLOSED → LOCKED`, stamping `locked_at` from the logical clock. Locking then reaches into those periods' reviews, and it treats them asymmetrically **by design**. A review still sitting in exactly `SELECTED` was never worked and is voided, with `void_reason` stamped as `PERIOD_LOCKED`; its underlying call record returns to `INGESTED` and the period's `selected_review_count` is recomputed. A review in `SCORED` or `DISPUTED`, by contrast, is real audit work product and is deliberately preserved rather than discarded — it is simply frozen, so any later attempt to submit a score against it or to finalize it fails with `ERR_PERIOD_LOCKED`. Reviews already `FINALIZED` are of course untouched: they are the very rows the filed figure was computed from.

Reading the record back is available to most roles, with the narrowest surface belonging to a `Dispatcher`, who may read their own dispatcher row, a review, its score sheets and its rebuttals, and a coaching plan with its sessions. Beyond single-resource reads, the list surfaces accept filters that mirror the fields above, all of them optional and all combinable with `limit` and `offset`: a programme's protocol versions may be filtered by `status` and by `protocol_family`; a version's rollouts by `golive_status`; dispatchers by `program_id`, `certified_protocol_version_id`, `compliance_posture`, `dispatch_privilege`, and `certification_status`; a dispatcher's calls by `call_type` and `status`; a programme's sampling periods by `status` and `call_type`; reviews by `sampling_period_id`, `call_record_id`, `dispatcher_id`, `protocol_version_id`, `status`, and `high_risk`; a review's rebuttals and a review's clinical record by `status`; coaching plans by `dispatcher_id`, `source_review_id`, `status`, `severity`, and `origin`; a plan's sessions and a curriculum version's sessions by `status`; and a programme's accreditation reports by `status` and `variant`.
