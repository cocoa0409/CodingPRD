# Community Health Needs Assessment API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Every three years a nonprofit hospital must look outward rather than inward: measure the health of the community it serves, ask that community what it needs, choose which of those needs it will actually work on, commit money and staff to the work, and then account for all of it in public and to the regulator. Today that work is scattered across spreadsheets, shared drives and email threads. Nobody can answer, on demand, which health indicators are evidenced well enough to prioritize against, whether a partner organization has already pledged the money a new commitment is about to spend, or whether the assessment that was filed with the annual return is the same assessment the board actually adopted. The Community Health Needs Assessment API replaces that patchwork with one governed system of record for the community benefit office.

The system is built around a single organizing entity, the **assessment cycle**, which walks a fiscal year's work through a declared sequence of phases — from an empty draft, through epidemiological data collection, community input, prioritization and strategy development, into board review, adoption, publication and finally a regulatory filing. Everything else in the system hangs off that cycle: the health indicators and the observations that evidence them, the stakeholder interview sessions that capture community concern, the candidate needs that are scored and selected, the implementation strategies with their measurable objectives, the partnership agreements and resource commitments that fund and staff the work, the community benefit expense report that tallies what was spent, and the progress evaluations that report back annually and mid-cycle.

Two design pressures shape almost every rule that follows. The first is **segregation of duties**: the officer who assembles the assessment is deliberately not the person who certifies its epidemiological data, not the person who adopts it on the board's behalf, and not the person who locks or files the expense report. Two rules go further and compare acting user identities *within* a single role. The second is **derived-state honesty**: severity scores, disparity ratios, composite priority scores, budget availability, effort encumbrance and every expense total are computed by the server from primary records, never accepted from clients — and when a primary record is retracted, the system must walk the consequences all the way down, suspending work that was justified by evidence that no longer exists. The specifications below describe both pressures in detail, and an implementation is judged on how faithfully it honours them.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

Everything in this section applies system-wide. Where a rule here and a workflow narrative in §3 describe the same behaviour, they are two views of one requirement, not two requirements.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint — reads included — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that user is acting in. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the six role names declared below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` and HTTP 401. This check runs before anything else, including before the server attempts to resolve any identifier in the path.

There are exactly six roles, spelled exactly as follows:

- `CommunityBenefitOfficer` — runs the assessment cycle end to end: registering indicators, gathering community input, prioritizing needs, drafting strategies and objectives, negotiating partnerships, committing resources, opening the expense report, posting expenses, and drafting and publishing progress evaluations. This role cannot certify indicator observations, cannot adopt the assessment, and cannot lock or file the community benefit report.
- `DataSteward` — certifies the epidemiological evidence base. The sole author and the sole retractor of indicator observations, which is precisely what makes a data retraction a segregated action rather than a self-serve correction.
- `BoardChair` — records the board's decision on the assessment and its implementation strategies. The only role that may adopt or defer, and therefore the only role that can trigger the three-link adoption cascade.
- `PartnerRepresentative` — acts for a community partner organization and signs partnership agreements on the partner's behalf.
- `ComplianceOfficer` — owns the regulatory end of the cycle: locking the community benefit report against the audited cost-to-charge ratio, filing the assessment with the annual return, and closing the cycle out. This role cannot open the report it later locks.
- `PublicViewer` — the public availability surface. Read-only access to the cycle collection, a single cycle, the cycle-scoped progress-evaluation list and a single progress evaluation; every other read and every write is denied.

If the headers are well-formed but the acting role does not appear in the called endpoint's authorized-role list in §4, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` and HTTP 403. Two endpoints refine that check further rather than relying on the role list alone. On a cycle phase advance the acting role must equal the declared actor of the *requested* transition: a `to_status` of `CLOSED` requires `ComplianceOfficer`, and every other `to_status` requires `CommunityBenefitOfficer`; a mismatch is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`. On a partnership signature the acting role must match the `signatory_side` being recorded: a `HOSPITAL` signature requires `CommunityBenefitOfficer` and a `PARTNER` signature requires `PartnerRepresentative`; again the rejection is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.

Role identity binds to `X-User-ID` in two different ways, and the difference matters. When `X-User-Role` is `PartnerRepresentative`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `ResponsibleParty`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `ResponsibleParty` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a `PartnerRepresentative` may only sign a `PartnershipAgreement` whose `party_id` equals the caller's `X-User-ID`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_NOT_AGREEMENT_PARTY` and HTTP 403. When `X-User-Role` is `CommunityBenefitOfficer`, `DataSteward`, `BoardChair`, `ComplianceOfficer` or `PublicViewer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — as `adoption_board_user_id` on an adopted cycle, as `voided_by_user_id` on a retracted observation or a voided posting, as `hospital_signatory_user_id` on a hospital-signed agreement, as `created_by_user_id` and `finalized_by_user_id` on a community benefit report, and as `posted_by_user_id` on an expense posting — but it never compares that value to any entity's `id`. The one place an opaque identifier *is* compared is against another opaque identifier previously recorded on the same object, which is the segregation-of-duties mechanism described in §2.8.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Filtering

Every collection endpoint accepts two pagination parameters as query-string arguments. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, its minimum accepted value is 1, and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position within the result set: it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` outside its permitted range and a negative `offset` are both request-shape failures and are rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION` and HTTP 400.

Results are ordered by `created_at` ascending — oldest record first — on every collection endpoint, with one deliberate exception: the assessment-cycle collection is returned newest first, ordered by `created_at` descending, because the cycle a community benefit office is working on is almost always the most recently opened one.

For every list response the `meta` object carries `limit` and `offset` as they were applied, `total_count` as the number of records matching the filters *before* the page window was applied, and `has_more` as a boolean that is true when records matching the filters remain beyond the current window. Single-resource responses carry none of those four pagination fields.

Filters are additive: supplying two of them narrows the result set to records satisfying both, and supplying none returns everything the acting role is permitted to see. The assessment-cycle collection filters on `fiscal_year` and `status`. The health-indicator collection filters on `cycle_id`, `domain` and `data_state`. Observations are listed either under a single indicator, filtered by `status` and `population_subgroup`, or across indicators, where `indicator_id` joins those two filters. Responsible parties filter on `party_type`. Stakeholder interviews filter on `cycle_id`, `facilitator_party_id` and `status`. Priority candidates filter on `cycle_id`, `indicator_id` and `status`. Implementation strategies filter on `candidate_id`, on `superseded_by_strategy_id` — which is how the supersede chain is walked — and on both state axes independently, `approval_state` and `execution_state`. Objectives listed under one strategy filter on `status`; the cross-strategy objective list adds `strategy_id`. Partnership agreements filter on `cycle_id`, `party_id`, `signature_state` and `funding_state`. Resource commitments filter on `strategy_id`, `party_id`, `partnership_id` and `status`. Community benefit reports filter on `cycle_id` and `status`. Expense postings filter on `report_id`, `strategy_id`, `category` and `status`. Progress evaluations filter on `cycle_id`, `eval_type` and `status`, and the cycle-scoped evaluation list filters on `eval_type` and `status`.

### 2.4. Cycle Posture: the Gate Over Every Child Record

The assessment cycle carries a server-derived field named `posture` that is the single gate over every mutation of every record beneath it. It is one of `OPEN`, `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN`, it starts at `OPEN`, and it is recomputed synchronously inside the same request that could have changed it — on every cycle phase transition, and on every cascade that changes the number of strategies awaiting review.

Posture is derived from two inputs and nothing else. The first input is the cycle's own `status`. The second is `review_flag_count`, which is the number of implementation strategies reachable from the cycle — that is, every strategy whose priority candidate belongs to this cycle — whose `execution_state` is `SUSPENDED_PENDING_REVIEW`. The derivation is evaluated in this order and stops at the first match: if the cycle's `status` is `FILED` or `CLOSED`, the posture is `FROZEN`; otherwise, if the cycle's `status` is `BOARD_REVIEW`, the posture is `RESTRICTED`; otherwise, if `review_flag_count` is at least 1, the posture is `RESTRICTED`; otherwise the posture is `OPEN`.

A `FROZEN` posture blocks every child mutation on the cycle. That includes expense postings, releasing a resource commitment, changing a strategy's execution status, reporting objective progress, publishing a progress evaluation, retracting an indicator observation, and every other write beneath the cycle. The rejection is `ERR_CYCLE_FROZEN` with HTTP 409. A cycle phase advance is the one operation posture never gates, because it is a mutation of the cycle itself rather than of a child — which is exactly how a `FILED` cycle can still be advanced to `CLOSED`.

A `RESTRICTED` posture is narrower: it blocks only assessment-side work, on the reasoning that the board should not be deliberating over a moving target and that a strategy suspended for lost evidence should not be built upon further. Rejections carry `ERR_CYCLE_RESTRICTED` with HTTP 409, and the rule applies to exactly thirteen operations: creating a health indicator; recording an indicator observation; scheduling a stakeholder interview; completing a stakeholder interview; cancelling a stakeholder interview; creating a priority candidate; scoring a priority candidate; selecting a priority candidate; deferring a priority candidate; creating an implementation strategy; submitting an implementation strategy; adding an objective to a strategy; and creating a resource commitment. Report-side and evaluation-side endpoints stay open while `RESTRICTED`, as does an execution-status change on a strategy — the latter deliberately so, because clearing a suspended strategy back to `ON_TRACK` is the only way `review_flag_count` ever returns to zero. Retracting an observation is likewise exempt from the `RESTRICTED` gate.

### 2.5. Record Identity, Timestamps and Versioning

Every resource in this system — assessment cycles, health indicators, indicator observations, responsible parties, stakeholder interviews, priority candidates, implementation strategies, strategy objectives, partnership agreements, resource commitments, community benefit reports, expense postings and progress evaluations alike — carries the same four system-managed properties:

- `id` (string): a server-minted UUID. It carries no type prefix of any kind; it is a bare UUID string.
- `created_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with exactly three fractional-second digits, recording when the record was first stored.
- `updated_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format, refreshed on every mutation of the record, including mutations that only touch server-derived fields.
- `version` (integer): starts at 1 when the record is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that record.

No entity in this system is exempt: there is no record type that omits `updated_at` or `version`. That uniformity matters because `version` is both the audit trail and the concurrency token. A cascade that changes a derived counter on a parent is a mutation of the parent: when an observation is recorded and its indicator re-derives its computed fields, the indicator's `version` increments and its `updated_at` is refreshed; when a posting is created or voided, the owning report's `version` increments as well as the posting's own. Conversely, an operation that is rejected by any rule in this document changes nothing and increments no `version`.

### 2.6. Left-to-Right Resolution of Path and Body References

Identifiers arrive in two places, and both are resolved before any business rule runs. Path identifiers are resolved strictly left to right, so that the outermost segment's failure is what the client sees when several segments are wrong at once. Each path variable has its own dedicated error code: `{cycle_id}` yields `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND`, `{indicator_id}` yields `ERR_INDICATOR_NOT_FOUND`, `{data_point_id}` yields `ERR_DATA_POINT_NOT_FOUND`, `{party_id}` yields `ERR_PARTY_NOT_FOUND`, `{interview_id}` yields `ERR_INTERVIEW_NOT_FOUND`, `{candidate_id}` yields `ERR_CANDIDATE_NOT_FOUND`, `{strategy_id}` yields `ERR_STRATEGY_NOT_FOUND`, `{objective_id}` yields `ERR_OBJECTIVE_NOT_FOUND`, `{agreement_id}` yields `ERR_PARTNERSHIP_NOT_FOUND`, `{commitment_id}` yields `ERR_COMMITMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `{report_id}` yields `ERR_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`, `{posting_id}` yields `ERR_POSTING_NOT_FOUND`, and `{evaluation_id}` yields `ERR_EVALUATION_NOT_FOUND`. All thirteen are HTTP 404.

Foreign keys supplied in a request body are resolved after the body has passed its shape check, and in the order the body fields are declared for that endpoint. The mapping is the same family of codes: a `cycle_id` that resolves to nothing yields `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND`; an `indicator_id`, `ERR_INDICATOR_NOT_FOUND`; a `facilitator_party_id` or a `party_id`, `ERR_PARTY_NOT_FOUND`; a `candidate_id`, `ERR_CANDIDATE_NOT_FOUND`; a `strategy_id`, `ERR_STRATEGY_NOT_FOUND`; a `partnership_id`, `ERR_PARTNERSHIP_NOT_FOUND`; and a `report_id`, `ERR_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`. Body-level reference resolution applies to the nine creation endpoints that carry foreign keys: `POST /health-indicators`, `POST /stakeholder-interviews`, `POST /priority-candidates`, `POST /implementation-strategies`, `POST /partnership-agreements`, `POST /resource-commitments`, `POST /community-benefit-reports`, `POST /expense-postings` and `POST /progress-evaluations`.

Request-body shape validation itself covers required-field presence, JSON type correctness, enum membership, numeric ranges, and decimal-place limits, plus the four cross-field orderings the domain requires: a session's `ends_at` must be strictly later than its `starts_at`, a commitment's `effective_to` must be strictly later than its `effective_from`, an agreement's `term_end` must be the same day as or later than its `term_start`, and every entry in a `concern_ratings` object must be keyed by a health-domain enum member with an integer value between 1 and 5 inclusive. All of these failures are reported as `ERR_VALIDATION` with HTTP 400.

### 2.7. Stale-Read Protection on Strategy Drafting and Expense Posting

Exactly two write operations in this system take an optimistic-concurrency token, and both take it as a required, non-optional field naming the parent record's version the client last observed. No other endpoint accepts a version field of any kind, and supplying one elsewhere has no effect.

A `POST /implementation-strategies` request must carry `expected_candidate_version` matching the target `PriorityCandidate`'s current `version`. If the candidate's version has advanced since the client last read it, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CANDIDATE_VERSION` and HTTP 412. The protection exists because drafting a strategy against a candidate is a legitimate operation *on a stale view*: a newer strategy silently supersedes the candidate's existing `DRAFT` or `RETURNED` strategy and releases that strategy's pending commitments. The version token is what forces the client to re-read the candidate — including its `strategy_count` and its current score fields — before it supersedes work it has not seen.

A `POST /expense-postings` request must carry `expected_report_version` matching the target `CommunityBenefitReport`'s current `version`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_REPORT_VERSION` and HTTP 412. Here the reason is arithmetic rather than governance: a posting's valued amount is derived from the report's current cost-to-charge ratio and valuation basis, both of which the finalization step rewrites, so a posting computed against a stale view of the report would enter the ledger on the wrong valuation basis.

### 2.8. Segregation of Duties and Same-Role Identity Checks

Two operations compare the acting `X-User-ID` against an identifier recorded by an earlier, paired step, and reject the request when the two are equal. Locking a community benefit report requires that the acting `X-User-ID` differ from the `created_by_user_id` recorded when that report was opened. Voiding an expense posting requires that the acting `X-User-ID` differ from the `posted_by_user_id` recorded when that posting was created. Both rejections carry `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` with HTTP 403, and both apply irrespective of role — the posting-void rule bites even when both users hold the same role, because voiding a posting is available to both `CommunityBenefitOfficer` and `ComplianceOfficer`.

Beyond those two comparisons, segregation is enforced structurally through the role list of each endpoint: only a `DataSteward` may author or retract an observation, only a `BoardChair` may record a board decision, and only a `ComplianceOfficer` may lock the report, file the assessment or close the cycle.

### 2.9. Cents, Basis Points, Ratios and Rounding

Money is held exclusively in integer cents, in every field whose name ends in `_cents`. There are no fractional cents anywhere in the system, and no monetary value is ever transported as a string. One hundred cents make one dollar, and the tier thresholds described in §3.9 are stated in dollars precisely so that the conversion is explicit at the point of use.

Effort is held in **basis points of one full-time equivalent**, where 10,000 basis points equal one whole FTE. A party contributing a quarter of one person's time to a strategy commits 2,500 basis points. Both `fte_capacity_bp` and `fte_bp` use this unit, and the derived `committed_fte_bp` accumulates in the same unit with no rounding — basis-point arithmetic is exact integer arithmetic.

Cost-to-charge ratios are decimal numbers greater than 0 and no greater than 1, carrying exactly four decimal places; both `prior_year_cost_to_charge_ratio` on the cycle and `audited_cost_to_charge_ratio` on the report are validated to that precision, and a value with more or fewer than four decimal places is an `ERR_VALIDATION` failure.

Wherever a value must be rounded, the method is **round half up** — a value exactly on the halfway mark rounds away from zero — and the target precision depends on the quantity. Amounts in cents round to whole integers. `severity_score`, `disparity_ratio`, `disparity_points`, `community_concern_index`, `composite_score`, `attainment_pct`, `benefit_pct_of_expense` and `overhead_rate`-derived percentages round to 2 decimal places. Two rules about *when* rounding happens are load-bearing and are stated with each formula in §3: a single rounding is applied to a weighted sum rather than to each of its terms, and a ceiling or floor clamp is applied *after* rounding rather than before. `shared_accountability_pct` and `hospital_capacity_score` are client-supplied and are validated to at most 2 decimal places; `benchmark_value`, an observation's `value`, `baseline_value` and `target_value` are client-supplied and validated to at most 4 decimal places.

### 2.10. The Logical Clock and Calendar Windows

Every timestamp the server stamps and every date-window rule reads a single **logical clock** rather than real wall-clock time. Before any client sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it must never fall back to the host's real time. An operator capability accepts a supplied `to_iso8601` instant and moves the logical clock to it, returning HTTP 204 with no body; that capability is available to `CommunityBenefitOfficer`, `DataSteward`, `BoardChair` and `ComplianceOfficer`.

Every field this document describes as a stamped instant — `completed_at`, `adopted_at`, `published_at`, `filed_at`, `submitted_at`, `approved_at`, `baseline_locked_at`, `hospital_signed_at`, `partner_signed_at`, `committed_at`, `released_at`, `locked_at` — is taken from the logical clock at the moment the transition is applied, and each is null until its transition has occurred. Calendar-date fields (`collected_on`, `target_date`, `as_of_date`, `term_start`, `term_end`, `effective_from`, `effective_to`) are plain `YYYY-MM-DD` strings, not instants.

Two interval conventions run through the domain and they are not the same. A stakeholder session's window is `[starts_at, ends_at)` — the start is inclusive, the end exclusive — and a session may last at most 240 minutes. A resource commitment's window is the half-open day range `[effective_from, effective_to)`, so the last day the commitment actually covers is the day before `effective_to`. A partnership agreement's term, by contrast, is fully inclusive on both ends: `term_end` is itself a covered day. That asymmetry is the reason the containment rule in §3.6 compares a commitment's exclusive end against the day *after* `term_end`.

### 2.11. Order of Rule Evaluation

When one request violates several rules, exactly one error is returned, and it is the one from the earliest rung of the ladder below. Rungs are listed from first-evaluated to last-evaluated; rules sharing a rung cannot both fire on the same request because they guard disjoint operations, except where noted otherwise in §3.

1. Header presence and role-name validity — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. Endpoint authorization, including the transition-actor refinement on a phase advance and the signatory-side refinement on a signature — `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
3. Path identifier resolution, left to right — the thirteen `ERR_..._NOT_FOUND` codes.
4. Request-body shape, types, enums, ranges, decimal places and cross-field orderings — `ERR_VALIDATION`.
5. Body foreign-key resolution, in body-field declaration order — the `ERR_..._NOT_FOUND` codes again.
6. The partner-identity check on a signature (`ERR_NOT_AGREEMENT_PARTY`) and the party-type check on an interview facilitator or a partnership counterparty (`ERR_PARTY_TYPE_MISMATCH`).
7. Segregation of duties — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
8. The optimistic-concurrency token — `ERR_STALE_CANDIDATE_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_REPORT_VERSION`.
9. The `FROZEN` posture gate — `ERR_CYCLE_FROZEN`.
10. The `RESTRICTED` posture gate — `ERR_CYCLE_RESTRICTED`.
11. State legality: the target row's own state (`ERR_INVALID_STATE`), the declared-successor check on a phase advance (`ERR_INVALID_PHASE_TRANSITION`), the approval-axis lock on attaching objectives or commitments (`ERR_STRATEGY_LOCKED`), the closed interview-scheduling window (`ERR_INPUT_WINDOW_CLOSED`), and the creation-time parent-phase gates (also `ERR_INVALID_STATE`).
12. Cross-entity state prerequisites: indicator data sufficiency (`ERR_INDICATOR_DATA_INSUFFICIENT`), strategy approval (`ERR_STRATEGY_NOT_APPROVED`) and partnership execution (`ERR_PARTNERSHIP_NOT_EXECUTED`).
13. Public availability before filing — `ERR_PUBLIC_AVAILABILITY_NOT_MET`.
14. Referential completeness gates: a locked report before filing (`ERR_REPORT_NOT_LOCKED`), a complete strategy set before board review or on submission (`ERR_STRATEGY_SET_INCOMPLETE`), and a published annual evaluation before close (`ERR_ANNUAL_REPORT_MISSING`).
15. Facilitator window occupancy — `ERR_FACILITATOR_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.
16. The observation evidence window — `ERR_DATA_POINT_OUT_OF_WINDOW`.
17. Commitment containment inside the agreement term — `ERR_COMMITMENT_OUTSIDE_TERM`.
18. Uniqueness: `ERR_DUPLICATE_FISCAL_YEAR`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_INDICATOR_NAME`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_ORGANIZATION`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_REPORT` and `ERR_DUPLICATE_EVALUATION_PERIOD`.
19. Posting basis consistency — `ERR_POSTING_BASIS_MISMATCH`.
20. Party effort capacity — `ERR_PARTY_FTE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.
21. Partnership pledge load over the overlapping window — `ERR_PARTNERSHIP_PLEDGE_WINDOW_EXCEEDED`.
22. Strategy budget availability — `ERR_STRATEGY_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`.
23. Completeness and threshold gates whose inputs are counters or flags: `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_INDICATOR_COVERAGE`, `ERR_NO_COMMUNITY_INPUT`, `ERR_NO_SELECTED_PRIORITY`, `ERR_QUORUM_NOT_MET`, `ERR_NO_DOMAIN_INPUT`, `ERR_STALE_CONCERN_INPUT`, `ERR_REPORT_EMPTY` and `ERR_OBJECTIVE_PROGRESS_MISSING`.
24. Vote arithmetic and the selection score threshold: `ERR_ADOPTION_VOTE_FAILED` and `ERR_PRIORITY_SCORE_BELOW_THRESHOLD`.

Two pairs on this ladder share an endpoint and their relative order is therefore observable. On a board action, the quorum check at rung 23 runs before the vote-margin check at rung 24, so a board action with no quorum and a losing vote reports `ERR_QUORUM_NOT_MET`. On a candidate selection, the stale-community-input check at rung 23 runs before the score-threshold check at rung 24, so a candidate that is both dirty and under-scoring reports `ERR_STALE_CONCERN_INPUT`.

Seven creation endpoints also carry a parent-phase gate at rung 11 that has no state of its own to check, because the row being created does not exist yet. Registering a health indicator and recording an observation both require the owning cycle's `status` to be `DATA_COLLECTION`. Creating a priority candidate requires the cycle's `status` to be `PRIORITIZATION`. Creating an implementation strategy requires the cycle's `status` to be `STRATEGY_DEVELOPMENT` *and* the target candidate's `status` to be `SELECTED`. Adding an objective to a strategy and creating a resource commitment both require the cycle's `status` to be `STRATEGY_DEVELOPMENT`. Creating an expense posting requires the owning report's `status` to be `OPEN`. Each of these failures is `ERR_INVALID_STATE` with HTTP 409. Four creation endpoints carry no phase gate at all and are constrained only by the posture rules at rungs 9 and 10: registering a responsible party, drafting a partnership agreement, opening a community benefit report, and drafting a progress evaluation.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Opening a Cycle and Walking It Through Its Phases

A `CommunityBenefitOfficer` opens the year's work by creating an assessment cycle, naming the community it covers in `service_area_name` — between 1 and 120 characters — and declaring the `fiscal_year` it reports on as an integer no lower than 2000 and no higher than 2100. Only one cycle may ever exist for a given fiscal year; a second attempt is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_FISCAL_YEAR`. A new cycle lands in `DRAFT` with `posture` at `OPEN`, every derived counter at zero, and `version` at 1. The counters the cycle maintains are `sufficient_indicator_count`, `completed_interview_count`, `selected_priority_count`, `review_flag_count`, `board_deferral_count`, `mid_cycle_adjustment_count` and `annual_report_count`; each is described where the work that moves it is described.

The cycle's `status` walks a declared chain and never skips a rung. The permitted forward pairs are `DRAFT` to `DATA_COLLECTION`, `DATA_COLLECTION` to `INPUT_GATHERING`, `INPUT_GATHERING` to `PRIORITIZATION`, `PRIORITIZATION` to `STRATEGY_DEVELOPMENT`, `STRATEGY_DEVELOPMENT` to `BOARD_REVIEW`, and `FILED` to `CLOSED`. A phase advance names its destination in `to_status`. Any other combination of current status and requested `to_status` — including every backward pair, every skipped rung, and any attempt to reach `ADOPTED`, `PUBLISHED` or `FILED` this way — is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PHASE_TRANSITION`. The three statuses missing from that chain are reached by their own dedicated actions: `ADOPTED` by a board decision, `PUBLISHED` by publication, and `FILED` by the regulatory filing. Every successful advance recomputes `posture` and increments the cycle's `version` by 1, and returns the updated cycle with HTTP 200.

Four of the six advances carry a readiness gate, and each gate reads a counter the cycle has been maintaining all along. Leaving `DATA_COLLECTION` requires `sufficient_indicator_count` to be at least 1 — the assessment must rest on at least one adequately evidenced health indicator — and otherwise fails with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_INDICATOR_COVERAGE`. Leaving `INPUT_GATHERING` requires `completed_interview_count` to be at least 1, so that no prioritization happens without community voice; the failure is `ERR_NO_COMMUNITY_INPUT`. Leaving `PRIORITIZATION` requires `selected_priority_count` to be at least 1, failing with `ERR_NO_SELECTED_PRIORITY`. And the advance into `BOARD_REVIEW` requires that *every* priority candidate on the cycle whose `status` is `SELECTED` has at least one implementation strategy whose `approval_state` is `SUBMITTED` and which itself carries at least one objective whose `status` is either `DRAFT` or `ACTIVE`; if a single selected priority falls short, the advance fails with `ERR_STRATEGY_SET_INCOMPLETE`.

Three of the advances also carry consequences beyond the status change itself. Reaching `PRIORITIZATION` closes the interview scheduling window: from that point on, scheduling a *new* session on the cycle is rejected with `ERR_INPUT_WINDOW_CLOSED`, while completing or cancelling a session that was already `SCHEDULED` remains permitted. That deliberate asymmetry is what makes late community input — and therefore stale candidate scores — reachable. Reaching `STRATEGY_DEVELOPMENT` locks the priority list by cascade: every priority candidate on the cycle whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `SCORED` or `SCORE_INVALIDATED` moves to `DEFERRED` and has its `defer_reason` set to the literal string `NOT_SELECTED_AT_PRIORITY_LOCK`; candidates that are already `SELECTED` or already `DEFERRED` are left entirely untouched, and `selected_priority_count` is recomputed afterwards. Reaching `BOARD_REVIEW` drives `posture` to `RESTRICTED`, freezing assessment-side child mutations while the board deliberates.

The final advance, `FILED` to `CLOSED`, is the only one a `ComplianceOfficer` performs rather than the officer who built the assessment. It requires at least one progress evaluation on the cycle whose `eval_type` is `ANNUAL` and whose `status` is `PUBLISHED`, failing otherwise with `ERR_ANNUAL_REPORT_MISSING`. On success two cascades run in sequence. First, every implementation strategy reachable from the cycle whose `execution_state` is `ON_TRACK`, `AT_RISK`, `BEHIND` or `SUSPENDED_PENDING_REVIEW` moves to `CLOSED_OUT`; strategies already in `COMPLETED`, `DISCONTINUED` or `CLOSED_OUT` are untouched. Second, caused by that close-out, every objective of those strategies whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `REVISED` is finalized: it moves to `FINALIZED_MET` when its `attainment_pct` is not null and is at least 100.00, and to `FINALIZED_NOT_MET` in every other case — including the case where `attainment_pct` was never computed at all. The cycle's `posture` stays `FROZEN` throughout, because `CLOSED` is itself a frozen status.

### 3.2. The Evidence Base: Health Indicators and Their Observations

While the cycle sits in `DATA_COLLECTION`, either a `CommunityBenefitOfficer` or a `DataSteward` may register a **health indicator** against it, supplying the owning `cycle_id`, a `name` between 1 and 160 characters, the `domain` the indicator belongs to, and the `benchmark_value` — a number greater than zero, carrying at most four decimal places — that represents the state or national rate the community's own rate will be measured against. The `domain` is one of `BEHAVIORAL_HEALTH`, `CHRONIC_DISEASE`, `MATERNAL_CHILD_HEALTH`, `ACCESS_TO_CARE` or `HOUSING_AND_FOOD`, and those same five strings are the keys community concern ratings are recorded under later, which is how epidemiology and community voice are joined. Indicator names are unique within a cycle, so a repeat name on the same cycle is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_INDICATOR_NAME`. A fresh indicator starts with `data_state` at `INSUFFICIENT`, `subgroup_count` at 0 and `evidence_count` at 0.

Observations are the `DataSteward`'s exclusive territory. Each observation records the `population_subgroup` it describes — 1 to 60 characters in upper snake case, where the literal string `OVERALL` is reserved for the whole service area — the `source_type` establishing its provenance, the observed rate in `value` (greater than zero, at most four decimal places), and the calendar date `collected_on` on which it was collected. Provenance is one of `PUBLIC_DATASET`, `HOSPITAL_EHR`, `COMMUNITY_SURVEY` or `VITAL_STATISTICS`. Every observation must fall inside the cycle's evidence window: `collected_on` must be on or after the first of January three years before the cycle's `fiscal_year` — for a cycle reporting on fiscal year 2026, that boundary is `2023-01-01` — and on or before the logical clock's current calendar date. An observation outside that window is rejected with `ERR_DATA_POINT_OUT_OF_WINDOW`. Each observation starts with `status` at `ACTIVE`, and its `void_reason` and `voided_by_user_id` are both null while it remains active.

Storing an observation re-derives five fields on the owning indicator, and the derivation reads only observations whose `status` is `ACTIVE` — retracted observations are retained for audit but contribute nothing. `latest_overall_value` is taken from the newest active observation whose `population_subgroup` is exactly `OVERALL`, where "newest" means ordering candidates by `collected_on` descending and, for ties on that date, by `created_at` descending, then taking the first; identifiers are never used as a tie-break. When no active `OVERALL` observation exists, both `latest_overall_value` and `severity_score` are null. Otherwise `severity_score` is the community's rate divided by the indicator's `benchmark_value`, multiplied by 50, rounded half up to two decimal places, and then capped at 100.00 — the cap is applied *after* the rounding, never before. The multiplier of 50 encodes benchmark parity: an indicator whose community rate exactly equals its benchmark scores 50.00, and an indicator running at twice its benchmark reaches the ceiling.

Disparity is measured across the *other* subgroups. For each distinct `population_subgroup` other than `OVERALL` that has at least one active observation, the newest observation for that subgroup is selected by the same `collected_on` descending then `created_at` descending ordering, and its `value` is that subgroup's representative rate. `subgroup_count` is the number of such distinct subgroups. When fewer than two such subgroups exist, `disparity_ratio` is null; otherwise `disparity_ratio` is the largest representative rate divided by the smallest, rounded half up to two decimal places. Separately, `evidence_count` is the total number of active observations on the indicator, counting the `OVERALL` rows as well as the subgroup rows. Finally `data_state` is `SUFFICIENT` when `latest_overall_value` is not null *and* `subgroup_count` is at least 2, and `INSUFFICIENT` in every other case.

An indicator crossing from `INSUFFICIENT` to `SUFFICIENT` adds 1 to the cycle's `sufficient_indicator_count`. An observation stored against an indicator that already qualifies re-derives all five fields and increments the indicator's `version`, but changes no counter and — this is the important negative — does *not* invalidate any candidate score, even when the new observation moves `severity_score` substantially. Only a retraction has that power.

Retraction is the `DataSteward`'s other exclusive action and the entry point of the system's most consequential cascade. It requires the observation's `status` to be `ACTIVE`, rejecting a second attempt with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, and it records the supplied `reason` into `void_reason` alongside the acting user in `voided_by_user_id` before moving `status` to `VOID`. The retraction is permitted while the cycle's `posture` is `RESTRICTED` — a data-quality correction must not wait for the board — but it is not permitted once the posture is `FROZEN`.

What follows a retraction is a chain of three linked consequences, and each link is caused by the one above it. **Link one:** the owning indicator re-derives all five computed fields, and `severity_score` and `disparity_ratio` become null when their inputs disappear. If the re-derivation leaves `latest_overall_value` null or `subgroup_count` below 2, the indicator moves `SUFFICIENT` to `INSUFFICIENT` and the cycle's `sufficient_indicator_count` is reduced by 1. **Link two**, caused by that loss of sufficiency: every priority candidate whose `indicator_id` names this indicator and whose `status` is `SCORED` or `SELECTED` moves to `SCORE_INVALIDATED`, and its `composite_score`, `severity_input`, `disparity_points` and `community_concern_index` are all set to null; for each candidate that was `SELECTED` before this link ran, the cycle's `selected_priority_count` is reduced by 1. **Link three**, caused by link two: for every candidate that had been `SELECTED`, each implementation strategy whose `candidate_id` names that candidate and whose `execution_state` is anything other than `COMPLETED`, `DISCONTINUED` or `CLOSED_OUT` moves to `SUSPENDED_PENDING_REVIEW`; the cycle's `review_flag_count` is then recomputed and, because it is now at least 1, the cycle's `posture` becomes `RESTRICTED`. The whole chain is the intended serialization point between the epidemiological record and the work justified by it: pull the evidence, and the work built on it stops until somebody reviews it.

### 3.3. Community Input: Facilitators, Sessions and Concern Ratings

Before any community input can be gathered, the officer registers the organizations and internal departments that will do the work. A **responsible party** carries a `name` between 1 and 120 characters, a `party_type` of either `INTERNAL` or `PARTNER`, and an `fte_capacity_bp` — the effort capacity available for commitments, in basis points, no lower than 0 and no higher than 100,000, which is to say up to ten whole FTE. Its derived `committed_fte_bp` starts at 0. Party registration is not gated by any cycle phase; a party outlives any single assessment.

The two party types are not interchangeable, and the system enforces the distinction at the point of reference. A stakeholder session's `facilitator_party_id` must name a party whose `party_type` is `INTERNAL`, because the hospital facilitates its own community listening. A partnership agreement's `party_id` must name a party whose `party_type` is `PARTNER`, because a hospital department cannot be the counterparty to its own agreement. Either violation is rejected with `ERR_PARTY_TYPE_MISMATCH` and HTTP 409.

A **stakeholder interview** is a scheduled listening session with one community organization, and it may only be created while the owning cycle sits in `INPUT_GATHERING`; once the cycle has advanced past that phase the attempt is rejected with `ERR_INPUT_WINDOW_CLOSED`. Scheduling supplies the owning `cycle_id`, the facilitating `facilitator_party_id`, the `organization_name` being interviewed (1 to 120 characters), the `sector` that organization represents, and the session window as `starts_at` and `ends_at`. The `sector` is one of `SAFETY_NET_PROVIDER`, `PUBLIC_HEALTH_AGENCY`, `SCHOOL_DISTRICT`, `FAITH_BASED`, `RESIDENT_COALITION` or `PAYER`. The window's `starts_at` is inclusive and its `ends_at` is exclusive, `ends_at` must be strictly later than `starts_at`, and the elapsed time between them may not exceed 240 minutes — four hours is the longest session the system will book. A new session starts with `status` at `SCHEDULED`, with `concern_ratings`, `attendee_count`, `completed_at` and `cancel_reason` all null.

An organization gets one seat per cycle. `organization_name` is unique within a cycle across sessions whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `COMPLETED`; a duplicate is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ORGANIZATION`. A cancelled session releases the name, so the same organization can be rebooked after a cancellation.

Facilitators are protected from back-to-back bookings by a symmetric half-hour buffer. The occupancy set for a facilitator is every session sharing the requested `facilitator_party_id` whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `COMPLETED`, taken across every cycle and not merely the cycle being scheduled; cancelled sessions leave the set entirely and free their slot. Each existing session in that set is notionally widened by 30 minutes on *both* sides before the comparison, and the requested session conflicts with it when two conditions both hold: the requested `starts_at` falls strictly earlier than the existing session's `ends_at` advanced by 30 minutes, *and* the existing session's `starts_at` falls strictly earlier than the requested `ends_at` advanced by 30 minutes. Both comparisons are strict, which fixes the boundary precisely: a requested session beginning exactly at the instant an existing session ends does conflict, and so does one beginning 29 minutes later, while a requested session beginning exactly 30 minutes after an existing session ends does not conflict and is accepted. Any conflict is rejected with `ERR_FACILITATOR_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.

Completing a session is what turns a booking into evidence. It requires the session's `status` to be `SCHEDULED` and the owning cycle's `status` to be either `INPUT_GATHERING` or `PRIORITIZATION`; either failure is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The completion records `concern_ratings` — an object whose keys are health-domain enum members and whose values are integer community-concern ratings from 1 to 5 inclusive, with any malformed key or out-of-range value rejected as `ERR_VALIDATION` — and `attendee_count`, the number of community members present, which must be at least 1 and which becomes the weight those ratings carry in the concern index. On success `status` moves `SCHEDULED` to `COMPLETED`, `completed_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and the cycle's `completed_interview_count` increases by 1.

Completion has one further consequence that only bites when input arrives late. Because sessions scheduled during `INPUT_GATHERING` may still be completed after the cycle has moved to `PRIORITIZATION`, a completion can land *after* candidates have already been scored. When that happens, every priority candidate on the same cycle whose `status` is `SCORED` and whose indicator's `domain` appears as a key in the newly recorded `concern_ratings` has its `concern_input_dirty` flag set to true. The flag is a claim that the candidate's score no longer reflects all available community voice, and it blocks selection until the candidate is scored again.

Cancelling a session is the other terminal outcome. It requires the session's `status` to be `SCHEDULED` and the owning cycle's `status` to be `INPUT_GATHERING` or `PRIORITIZATION`, rejecting otherwise with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The supplied `reason` is stored in `cancel_reason`, `status` moves `SCHEDULED` to `CANCELLED`, and the row leaves both the facilitator occupancy set and the organization-name uniqueness set — so a cancellation genuinely frees the slot and the name for reuse.

### 3.4. Prioritization: Scoring, Selecting and Deferring Candidate Needs

Once the cycle reaches `PRIORITIZATION` the officer nominates **priority candidates**, each pairing one assessed need with the hospital's own judgement of what it can do about it. A candidate names its owning `cycle_id`, the `indicator_id` supplying its epidemiological inputs, and a `hospital_capacity_score` between 0 and 100 inclusive with at most two decimal places. It starts with `status` at `DRAFT`, `composite_score` null, `concern_input_dirty` false, `strategy_count` at 0, and `scoring_variant`, `severity_input`, `disparity_points` and `community_concern_index` all null.

Scoring a candidate is where the four statutory criteria — severity, disparities, community concern and hospital capacity — are combined into one number. A scoring run is permitted when the candidate's `status` is `DRAFT`, `SCORED` or `SCORE_INVALIDATED` and the owning cycle's `status` is `PRIORITIZATION`, rejecting otherwise with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; re-scoring an already-`SCORED` candidate is therefore a legitimate operation that simply overwrites the previous figures. Two further prerequisites protect the arithmetic from empty inputs. The candidate's indicator must have `data_state` equal to `SUFFICIENT`, or the run fails with `ERR_INDICATOR_DATA_INSUFFICIENT`. And at least one completed session on the cycle must carry a `concern_ratings` key equal to the indicator's `domain`, or the run fails with `ERR_NO_DOMAIN_INPUT` — without that, the concern term would have no input set at all.

The **community concern index** is computed first. Its input set is every session on the candidate's cycle whose `status` is `COMPLETED` and whose `concern_ratings` object contains a key equal to the candidate's indicator `domain`; sessions still `SCHEDULED` and sessions `CANCELLED` are excluded, and there is no time window, so a session completed during `PRIORITIZATION` counts exactly as much as one completed during `INPUT_GATHERING`. For each qualifying session, that domain's rating is multiplied by the session's `attendee_count`, and those products are summed; that sum is divided by the sum of the same sessions' `attendee_count` values, giving an attendance-weighted mean rating on the 1-to-5 scale. That mean is then multiplied by 20 to place it on the same 0-to-100 footing as the other three criteria, and the result is rounded half up to two decimal places. Rounding happens exactly once, on that final product — never per session and never on the intermediate mean. A unanimous rating of 5 from every session therefore yields an index of 100.00, and a unanimous 1 yields 20.00.

The **composite score** then combines four terms. The severity term is the indicator's current `severity_score`, snapshotted onto the candidate as `severity_input`. The disparity term, snapshotted as `disparity_points`, is 0.00 when the indicator's `disparity_ratio` is null; otherwise it is the `disparity_ratio` less 1, multiplied by 100, rounded half up to two decimal places, and then capped at 100.00 — so an indicator whose worst-off subgroup fares 1.4 times worse than its best-off subgroup contributes 40.00 disparity points, and any ratio at or beyond 2 contributes the full 100.00. The concern term is the `community_concern_index` just computed. The capacity term is the candidate's own `hospital_capacity_score`. Which weighting applies is chosen by the caller through `scoring_variant`, and the chosen value is stored on the candidate.

Under the `EQUAL_WEIGHT` variant — the statutory reading, in which severity, disparities, community concern and hospital capacity carry the same weight — every term carries a weight of 0.25: the composite score is one quarter of the severity term plus one quarter of the disparity term plus one quarter of the concern term plus one quarter of the capacity term. Under the `COMMUNITY_WEIGHTED` variant — the equity reading, in which community concern and disparities outrank hospital capacity — the weights differ by term: the severity term carries a weight of 0.20, the disparity term 0.30, the concern term 0.35 and the capacity term 0.15, and the composite score is the sum of those four weighted terms. In both variants a single round-half-up to two decimal places is applied to the finished weighted sum and never to the individual terms, and both variants therefore produce different composites from identical inputs whenever the four terms are not all equal. A successful run stores `scoring_variant`, `severity_input`, `disparity_points`, `community_concern_index` and `composite_score`, moves `status` to `SCORED`, and resets `concern_input_dirty` to false.

Selection is the officer's commitment that this need is one the hospital will act on. It requires the candidate's `status` to be exactly `SCORED` and the cycle's `status` to be `PRIORITIZATION`, failing otherwise with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. It then requires `concern_input_dirty` to be false, failing with `ERR_STALE_CONCERN_INPUT` when community input has arrived since the last scoring run. Finally it requires `composite_score` to be at least 60.00 — a score of exactly 60.00 passes — and rejects anything lower with `ERR_PRIORITY_SCORE_BELOW_THRESHOLD`. When a candidate fails both of the last two checks at once, the stale-input rejection is the one reported. On success the supplied `selection_rationale` is stored, `status` moves `SCORED` to `SELECTED`, and the cycle's `selected_priority_count` increases by 1.

Deferral is the counterweight. It is permitted from `DRAFT`, `SCORED`, `SELECTED` or `SCORE_INVALIDATED` while the cycle's `status` is `PRIORITIZATION`, and it stores the supplied `reason` in `defer_reason` before moving `status` to `DEFERRED`. Deferring a candidate that was `SELECTED` carries two extra consequences: the cycle's `selected_priority_count` is reduced by 1, and every implementation strategy whose `candidate_id` names this candidate and whose `approval_state` is `DRAFT` or `RETURNED` moves to `SUPERSEDED`, so the draft strategy work built on a deferred need is retired rather than left dangling.

A candidate that lost its score to a data retraction is not stranded. Once the underlying indicator regains `SUFFICIENT` data — a new observation restoring the `OVERALL` value or a second subgroup — the candidate may be scored again, moving `SCORE_INVALIDATED` back to `SCORED` with freshly computed score fields. That closes the loop `SCORED` → `SELECTED` → `SCORE_INVALIDATED` → `SCORED`, which is repeatable without bound. Re-scoring does **not** restore the candidate's selection: the officer must select it again explicitly, and the cycle's `selected_priority_count` only rises when that happens.

### 3.5. Implementation Strategies and Their Measurable Objectives

With the priority list locked and the cycle in `STRATEGY_DEVELOPMENT`, the officer drafts an **implementation strategy** for each selected need. A strategy names the `candidate_id` it addresses, a `title` between 1 and 160 characters, an `authorized_budget_cents` of at least 0 cents — the total resource envelope the officer is willing to see committed under this strategy — and the `expected_candidate_version` described in §2.7. The target candidate's `status` must be `SELECTED` and the cycle's `status` must be `STRATEGY_DEVELOPMENT`; either failure is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. A new strategy starts with `approval_state` at `DRAFT`, `execution_state` at `NOT_STARTED`, `budget_available_cents` equal to its full `authorized_budget_cents`, `active_objective_count` at 0, and `superseded_by_strategy_id`, `submitted_at`, `approved_at` and `execution_note` all null. The owning candidate's `strategy_count` rises by 1 and the candidate's `version` increments, which is precisely why the next client to draft against the same candidate needs a fresh read.

Drafting a second strategy for a candidate that already has one is a supported way to start over, and it retires the earlier attempt automatically. Any existing strategy on the same candidate whose `approval_state` is `DRAFT` or `RETURNED` moves to `SUPERSEDED`, its `superseded_by_strategy_id` is set to the new strategy's `id`, and every resource commitment of that superseded strategy whose `status` is `PENDING` moves to `RELEASED` with `release_reason` set to the literal string `STRATEGY_SUPERSEDED` — restoring the contributing party's `committed_fte_bp` and the superseded strategy's own `budget_available_cents`. A strategy that has already reached `SUBMITTED`, `APPROVED` or `SUPERSEDED` is never retired this way; `SUPERSEDED` is terminal on the approval axis.

Each strategy carries two **independent** state axes, and confusing them is the most common way to get this domain wrong. The `approval_state` axis is governance — `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `APPROVED`, `RETURNED` and `SUPERSEDED` — and it answers whether the board has blessed the strategy. The `execution_state` axis is delivery — `NOT_STARTED`, `ON_TRACK`, `AT_RISK`, `BEHIND`, `SUSPENDED_PENDING_REVIEW`, `COMPLETED`, `DISCONTINUED` and `CLOSED_OUT` — and it answers how the work is going. Every transition described in this document moves one axis and leaves the other untouched unless it explicitly says otherwise.

**Measurable objectives** hang off a strategy and are what make it accountable. Each objective supplies a `metric_name` between 1 and 160 characters, a `baseline_value` of at least 0 with at most four decimal places recording where the measure stood at the start of the cycle, a `target_value` on the same terms recording where the strategy commits to move it, and a `target_date` as a calendar date by which the target is to be reached. An objective is created with `status` at `DRAFT`, `attainment_status` at `PENDING`, and `attainment_pct`, `actual_value`, `as_of_date` and `baseline_locked_at` all null. Objectives may only be attached while the cycle's `status` is `STRATEGY_DEVELOPMENT`, and only to a strategy whose `approval_state` is still `DRAFT` or `RETURNED`; attaching to a `SUBMITTED`, `APPROVED` or `SUPERSEDED` strategy is rejected with `ERR_STRATEGY_LOCKED` and HTTP 409. The same lock governs resource commitments.

Submitting a strategy hands it to the board. Submission is permitted from `DRAFT` or from `RETURNED` — the second case being a resubmission after the board sent the strategy back — and requires the owning cycle's `status` to be `STRATEGY_DEVELOPMENT`; either failure is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. It further requires the strategy to carry at least one objective whose `status` is `DRAFT`, rejecting an objective-free submission with `ERR_STRATEGY_SET_INCOMPLETE`. On success `approval_state` moves to `SUBMITTED` and `submitted_at` is stamped from the logical clock; a resubmission re-stamps `submitted_at` with the newer instant. The `execution_state` axis is not touched — a resubmitted strategy that was already running keeps running.

### 3.6. Partners, Pledges and Resource Commitments

A **partnership agreement** formalizes what a community partner brings and what both sides are accountable for. It names the owning `cycle_id` and the counterparty `party_id`, a `pledged_cents` of at least 1 cent, an `accountability_metric` of 1 to 160 characters naming the shared measure both sides are held to, a `shared_accountability_pct` between 0 and 100 inclusive with at most two decimal places recording the partner's share of that metric, and an inclusive term bounded by `term_start` and `term_end`, where `term_end` must fall on or after `term_start`. A new agreement starts with `signature_state` at `NEGOTIATING`, `funding_state` at `UNFUNDED`, `funded_cents` at 0, and `hospital_signed_at`, `partner_signed_at`, `hospital_signatory_user_id`, `partner_signatory_user_id` and `terminate_reason` all null. Agreements are not phase-gated; they may be negotiated at any point the cycle's posture allows child mutations.

Signatures are a fork-join: the two sides sign independently, in either order, and only the second signature closes the join. Each signature names the side being recorded in `signatory_side`, which is either `HOSPITAL` or `PARTNER`. Recording a `HOSPITAL` signature on an agreement in `NEGOTIATING` moves `signature_state` to `HOSPITAL_SIGNED`, stamps `hospital_signed_at` from the logical clock, and stores the acting `X-User-ID` as `hospital_signatory_user_id`; `partner_signed_at` and `partner_signatory_user_id` stay null and `funding_state` stays `UNFUNDED`. Recording a `PARTNER` signature on an agreement in `NEGOTIATING` moves `signature_state` to `PARTNER_SIGNED`, stamps `partner_signed_at` from the logical clock, and stores the acting `X-User-ID` as `partner_signatory_user_id` — a value that, by the identity rule in §2.1, necessarily equals the agreement's `party_id`; `hospital_signed_at` and `hospital_signatory_user_id` stay null and `funding_state` stays `UNFUNDED`. The two branches touch strictly disjoint sets of fields.

The join is reached from either single-signed state. A `PARTNER` signature on an agreement in `HOSPITAL_SIGNED` stamps `partner_signed_at` and `partner_signatory_user_id` and moves `signature_state` to `EXECUTED`. A `HOSPITAL` signature on an agreement in `PARTNER_SIGNED` stamps `hospital_signed_at` and `hospital_signatory_user_id` and moves `signature_state` to `EXECUTED`. In both cases, and only on reaching the join, `funding_state` moves `UNFUNDED` to `PLEDGED` — the point at which the agreement becomes eligible to fund resource commitments. Signing an agreement that is already `EXECUTED` or `TERMINATED`, or re-signing the side that has already signed, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

Termination is available from `NEGOTIATING`, `HOSPITAL_SIGNED`, `PARTNER_SIGNED` or `EXECUTED`, rejecting a second termination with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The supplied `reason` is stored in `terminate_reason` and `signature_state` moves to `TERMINATED`. Termination then unwinds everything the agreement was funding: every resource commitment referencing this agreement whose `status` is `PENDING` or `COMMITTED` moves to `RELEASED` with `release_reason` set to the literal string `PARTNERSHIP_TERMINATED`, restoring each contributing party's `committed_fte_bp` and each affected strategy's `budget_available_cents`; the agreement's own `funded_cents` returns to 0 and its `funding_state` moves to `RELEASED`.

A **resource commitment** is the many-to-many join that actually spends money and people: it binds a strategy, a contributing party and — optionally — a funding agreement over a date window. It names the `strategy_id` receiving the resources, the `party_id` contributing them, an optional `partnership_id` naming the agreement the funding is drawn from, the effort committed as `fte_bp` (no lower than 0 and no higher than 100,000 basis points), the money committed as `funding_cents` (at least 0), and the half-open window `effective_from` to `effective_to`, where `effective_to` must be strictly later than `effective_from` and the last day actually covered is the day before `effective_to`. Commitments may only be created while the cycle's `status` is `STRATEGY_DEVELOPMENT`, and only against a strategy whose `approval_state` is `DRAFT` or `RETURNED` — otherwise `ERR_STRATEGY_LOCKED`. A commitment starts with `status` at `PENDING` and `committed_at`, `released_at` and `release_reason` all null.

Crucially, a `PENDING` commitment already encumbers both conserved quantities. A strategy's `budget_available_cents` is its `authorized_budget_cents` less the sum of `funding_cents` over every commitment on that strategy whose `status` is `PENDING` or `COMMITTED`; released commitments are excluded. The invariant is that the strategy's `budget_available_cents` plus that encumbered sum always equals its `authorized_budget_cents`, after every forward action and after every compensating release. A party's `committed_fte_bp` is likewise the sum of `fte_bp` over every commitment naming that `party_id` whose `status` is `PENDING` or `COMMITTED`, taken across all strategies and independent of dates; both sums are exact integer arithmetic with no rounding. For a party starting at zero, the sequence create-commitment, activate, release must return `committed_fte_bp` to exactly zero.

Four checks guard a commitment, and they run in a fixed order. First, when `partnership_id` is supplied, the named agreement's `signature_state` must be `EXECUTED` — funding may only be drawn from an agreement both sides have signed — or the create fails with `ERR_PARTNERSHIP_NOT_EXECUTED`. Second, still only when `partnership_id` is supplied, the commitment window must sit inside the agreement term, and the two interval conventions must be reconciled: `effective_from` must be on or after `term_start`, and `effective_to` must be on or before the day *after* `term_end`, because `term_end` is an inclusive covered day while `effective_to` is exclusive. A window escaping the term is rejected with `ERR_COMMITMENT_OUTSIDE_TERM`. Third, the contributing party's existing `committed_fte_bp` plus the requested `fte_bp` must not exceed that party's `fte_capacity_bp`, or the create fails with `ERR_PARTY_FTE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Fourth — and only when `partnership_id` is supplied — the agreement's pledge must cover the peak load inside the requested window: taking every sibling commitment on the same agreement whose `status` is `PENDING` or `COMMITTED` and whose window overlaps the requested one, the requested `funding_cents` plus the sum of those siblings' `funding_cents` must not exceed the agreement's `pledged_cents`, or the create fails with `ERR_PARTNERSHIP_PLEDGE_WINDOW_EXCEEDED`. Two windows overlap when the requested `effective_from` falls strictly before a sibling's `effective_to` *and* that sibling's `effective_from` falls strictly before the requested `effective_to`; there is no buffer, so exactly adjacent windows — one beginning on the very day another ends — do not overlap and do not accumulate, and the same pledge may legitimately be spent again in a later, disjoint window. Last of all, the requested `funding_cents` must not exceed the strategy's current `budget_available_cents`, or the create fails with `ERR_STRATEGY_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`.

Releasing a commitment is the compensating action for both conserved quantities. It is permitted while the commitment's `status` is `PENDING` or `COMMITTED`, rejecting a second release with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The supplied `reason` is stored in `release_reason`, `released_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and `status` moves to `RELEASED`. The contributing party's `committed_fte_bp` is reduced by the commitment's `fte_bp` and the owning strategy's `budget_available_cents` is recomputed, restoring the `funding_cents` to the envelope. When `partnership_id` is supplied *and* the row being released had reached `COMMITTED`, the agreement's `funded_cents` is reduced by the same `funding_cents`, and once `funded_cents` reaches 0 the agreement's `funding_state` moves `FUNDED` to `RELEASED`. Releasing a row that was still `PENDING` leaves `funded_cents` untouched, because a pending commitment never contributed to it in the first place: `funded_cents` is by definition the sum of `funding_cents` over commitments on the agreement whose `status` is `COMMITTED`.

### 3.7. Board Review, Adoption and the Three-Link Cascade

Once the cycle reaches `BOARD_REVIEW` its posture is `RESTRICTED` and the assessment stops moving underneath the board. Only a `BoardChair` may record what the board decided, and the record carries four required values plus one optional one: an `action_type` of either `ADOPT` or `DEFER`, a boolean `quorum_present`, the tallies `votes_for` and `votes_against`, and an optional free-text `note`. The cycle's `status` must be `BOARD_REVIEW`, and both action types require `quorum_present` to be true — a board action recorded without quorum is rejected with `ERR_QUORUM_NOT_MET` regardless of the tallies, and regardless of which `action_type` was requested.

A `DEFER` action sends the work back for revision. The cycle's `status` moves `BOARD_REVIEW` to `STRATEGY_DEVELOPMENT`, every implementation strategy reachable from the cycle whose `approval_state` is `SUBMITTED` moves to `RETURNED` while keeping its existing `submitted_at` intact, the cycle's `board_deferral_count` increases by 1, and `posture` is recomputed — returning to `OPEN` when `review_flag_count` stands at 0, or remaining `RESTRICTED` when a suspended strategy is still outstanding. Returned strategies keep their `execution_state` exactly as it was. The governance loop `BOARD_REVIEW` → `STRATEGY_DEVELOPMENT` → `BOARD_REVIEW` may be traversed any number of times, with `board_deferral_count` recording how often the board sent the work back.

An `ADOPT` action requires one more thing after quorum: `votes_for` must be strictly greater than `votes_against`, and a tie or a loss is rejected with `ERR_ADOPTION_VOTE_FAILED`. On success the cycle's `status` moves `BOARD_REVIEW` to `ADOPTED`; `adopted_at` is stamped from the logical clock, `adoption_board_user_id` records the acting `X-User-ID`, and `votes_for_at_adoption` and `votes_against_at_adoption` preserve the tallies exactly as recorded. The cycle's `posture` is then recomputed, becoming `OPEN` when `review_flag_count` is 0.

Adoption also drives the deepest cascade in the system, and its three links are strictly causal — each one runs on the records the previous link just changed, not on everything in sight.

**Link one — strategies are approved.** Every implementation strategy reachable from the cycle whose `approval_state` is `SUBMITTED` moves to `APPROVED` and has `approved_at` stamped from the logical clock. Strategies in `DRAFT`, `RETURNED` or `SUPERSEDED` are not approved by adoption; they were never before the board.

**Link two — objectives are activated, caused by link one.** For each strategy that link one just moved to `APPROVED`, every objective of that strategy whose `status` is `DRAFT` moves to `ACTIVE` and has `baseline_locked_at` stamped from the logical clock, at which point that objective's `baseline_value` becomes immutable — a strategy cannot be judged against a baseline it is allowed to move. The strategy's `active_objective_count` rises accordingly. In the same link, that strategy's `execution_state` moves `NOT_STARTED` to `ON_TRACK`, provided at least one of its objectives actually moved from `DRAFT` to `ACTIVE`; the delivery axis begins running only when there is something measurable to run against, and the `approval_state` axis is not touched a second time.

**Link three — commitments are activated, caused by link two.** For each strategy whose `active_objective_count` rose from 0 to at least 1 during link two, every resource commitment of that strategy whose `status` is `PENDING` moves to `COMMITTED` and has `committed_at` stamped from the logical clock. Activation is deliberately neutral on the two conserved quantities: because a `PENDING` commitment already encumbered both the party's `committed_fte_bp` and the strategy's `budget_available_cents`, neither figure changes when the commitment becomes `COMMITTED`. What does change is partnership funding, which counts only committed rows: for each activated commitment whose `partnership_id` is not null, that agreement's `funded_cents` increases by the commitment's `funding_cents` and the agreement's `funding_state` moves `PLEDGED` to `FUNDED`.

### 3.8. Execution Tracking, Progress Reporting and Mid-Cycle Adjustment

With the strategies approved and running, the officer keeps the delivery axis honest by recording execution-status changes. Each change names the destination in `to_execution_state` and may carry a free-text `execution_note`, which overwrites whatever note the previous change left. Every such change requires the strategy's `approval_state` to be `APPROVED`; an attempt against a `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `RETURNED` or `SUPERSEDED` strategy is rejected with `ERR_STRATEGY_NOT_APPROVED`, and the `approval_state` itself never changes as a result of an execution move.

Ordinary progress reporting moves freely among the three live states. From `ON_TRACK`, `AT_RISK` or `BEHIND`, a change to `AT_RISK`, `BEHIND` or `ON_TRACK` is always permitted — `AT_RISK` to `BEHIND`, `BEHIND` back to `AT_RISK`, and `BEHIND` straight back to `ON_TRACK` are all legal, because a delivery axis that could only degrade would be useless. From any other `execution_state`, including `NOT_STARTED`, `COMPLETED`, `DISCONTINUED` and `CLOSED_OUT`, those moves are rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

Two destinations carry extra conditions. A move to `COMPLETED` is permitted from `ON_TRACK`, `AT_RISK` or `BEHIND`, and it additionally requires that *every* objective of the strategy has a `status` of `ACTIVE` or `REVISED` and an `attainment_status` other than `PENDING` — in other words that progress has been reported at least once against each one. A strategy with an objective that was never measured cannot be declared complete, and the rejection is `ERR_OBJECTIVE_PROGRESS_MISSING`. A move to `DISCONTINUED` is permitted from a wider set — `ON_TRACK`, `AT_RISK`, `BEHIND` or `SUSPENDED_PENDING_REVIEW` — and unwinds the strategy's resourcing: every resource commitment of that strategy whose `status` is `PENDING` or `COMMITTED` moves to `RELEASED` with `release_reason` set to the literal string `STRATEGY_DISCONTINUED`, restoring each contributing party's `committed_fte_bp`, the strategy's own `budget_available_cents`, and — for rows drawing on an agreement — that agreement's `funded_cents`.

Clearing a suspension is the one move that changes the cycle's posture. A strategy sitting in `SUSPENDED_PENDING_REVIEW` because a data retraction invalidated its candidate's score may be moved back to `ON_TRACK`, but only once the underlying priority candidate's `status` is something other than `SCORE_INVALIDATED` — reviving a strategy whose justification is still missing is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. On success the cycle's `review_flag_count` is recomputed, and when it reaches 0 the cycle's `posture` returns to `OPEN`, reopening every assessment-side operation the `RESTRICTED` posture had been blocking. This is why an execution-status change is exempt from the `RESTRICTED` gate: it is the only route out.

Progress against an objective is reported by supplying the measured `actual_value` and the calendar date `as_of_date` on which it was measured. The objective's `status` must be `ACTIVE` or `REVISED`, rejecting a `DRAFT`, `FINALIZED_MET` or `FINALIZED_NOT_MET` objective with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Both values are stored, replacing any earlier report, and the attainment figures are recomputed from them.

Attainment measures how far the objective has travelled from its baseline toward its target. In the ordinary case, where `target_value` differs from `baseline_value`, the raw percentage is the reported `actual_value` less the `baseline_value`, divided by the `target_value` less the `baseline_value`, multiplied by 100; that raw figure is rounded half up to two decimal places, and the rounded figure is then clamped into the range from 0.00 to 150.00 — the clamp is applied after the rounding, never before, and 150.00 is the highest `attainment_pct` the system will record no matter how far the measure over-shot. In the degenerate case, where `target_value` exactly equals `baseline_value`, there is no distance to travel: `attainment_pct` is 100.00 when the reported `actual_value` is at least the `target_value`, and 0.00 otherwise. The derived `attainment_status` then reads off three inclusive bands: `MET` when `attainment_pct` is at least 100.00, `PARTIAL` when it is at least 50.00 but below 100.00, and `NOT_MET` when it is below 50.00. Exactly 100.00 is `MET` and exactly 50.00 is `PARTIAL`.

**Progress evaluations** are the cycle's reporting-back instrument. Each names the owning `cycle_id`, an `eval_type` of either `ANNUAL` or `MID_CYCLE`, the `period_year` it reports on as an integer between 2000 and 2100 inclusive, and an optional `findings_summary` narrative. A cycle may hold at most one evaluation for any combination of `eval_type` and `period_year`; a duplicate is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_EVALUATION_PERIOD`. A new evaluation starts with `status` at `DRAFT`, `objectives_revised_count` at 0, and `public_url` and `published_at` null. Evaluations carry no cycle-phase gate at creation time.

Publication is what makes an evaluation count. It requires the evaluation's `status` to be `DRAFT` and the owning cycle's `status` to be `ADOPTED`, `PUBLISHED` or `FILED`; either failure is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The supplied `public_url` is stored, `published_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and `status` moves `DRAFT` to `PUBLISHED`. What happens next depends entirely on `eval_type`. Publishing an `ANNUAL` evaluation increases the cycle's `annual_report_count` by 1, and that counter is what the close-out gate reads. Publishing a `MID_CYCLE` evaluation increases the cycle's `mid_cycle_adjustment_count` by 1 and additionally triggers objective revision: every objective reachable from the cycle whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `attainment_pct` is not null and strictly below 50.00 moves to `REVISED`; each such revision moves its owning strategy's `execution_state` from `ON_TRACK` to `BEHIND`, and increments this evaluation's `objectives_revised_count`. Objectives whose `attainment_pct` is null — never measured — are not revised, and neither are objectives already sitting in `REVISED`.

### 3.9. Community Benefit Accounting, Publication, Filing and Close-Out

Everything the hospital spends in the community has to be valued, totalled and filed, and that is what the community benefit report is for. A report names the owning `cycle_id` and the `fiscal_year` it accounts for, given as an integer between 2000 and 2100 inclusive. A cycle may hold at most one report per `fiscal_year`; a second attempt at the same year under the same cycle is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_REPORT`.

Two figures on the assessment cycle exist only to serve this accounting, and they are supplied when the cycle is first opened. The `prior_year_cost_to_charge_ratio` is a decimal between 0.0001 and 1.0000 inclusive, carried to four decimal places, expressing how many dollars of cost sit behind each dollar of charges on the hospital's books. The `total_operating_expense_cents` is the hospital's whole-organisation operating expense for the year, in integer cents, and must be at least 1 — it is the denominator against which community benefit is expressed as a share of spending.

A new report starts with `status` at `OPEN` and `valuation_basis` at `PROVISIONAL`. Its `cost_to_charge_ratio` is copied from the owning cycle's `prior_year_cost_to_charge_ratio` at the moment of creation, so that charge-based valuation can begin before the auditors have signed off on anything; `audited_cost_to_charge_ratio` is null. All six money totals — `financial_assistance_cents`, `subsidized_services_cents`, `community_health_improvement_cents`, `cash_and_inkind_cents`, `community_building_cents` and `total_valued_cents` — start at 0, as do `indirect_allocation_cents`, `grand_total_cents` and `benefit_pct_of_expense`. The `overhead_rate` starts at 0.05, `locked_at` and `filed_at` are null, and `created_by_user_id` records the `X-User-ID` of whoever created the report — a value that matters later, because that person is barred from finalizing it.

**Posting an expense.** Each expense posting names its `report_id`, a `category`, the two money inputs `charges_cents` and `direct_cost_cents` (each an integer of at least 0), and optionally the `strategy_id` of the implementation strategy it funds. The owning report's `status` must be `OPEN`. Because postings change the report's totals and two people may be entering expenses against the same report, every posting must also carry `expected_report_version` as described in §2.7.

The five categories divide into two valuation families, and which family a category belongs to determines which of the two money inputs must be populated. `FINANCIAL_ASSISTANCE` and `SUBSIDIZED_SERVICES` are *charge-based*: they are recorded at gross charges and converted to cost, so a posting in either category requires `charges_cents` to be at least 1 and `direct_cost_cents` to be exactly 0. `COMMUNITY_HEALTH_IMPROVEMENT`, `CASH_AND_INKIND` and `COMMUNITY_BUILDING` are *cost-based*: they are recorded at actual cost already, so a posting in any of the three requires `direct_cost_cents` to be at least 1 and `charges_cents` to be exactly 0. A posting that populates the wrong input for its category, or populates both, or populates neither, is rejected with `ERR_POSTING_BASIS_MISMATCH`.

Valuation follows directly from the family. For a charge-based posting, `valued_cents` is the posting's `charges_cents` multiplied by the report's current `cost_to_charge_ratio`, rounded half up to a whole number of cents, and `ratio_applied` stores the exact ratio used. For a cost-based posting, `valued_cents` is simply the posting's `direct_cost_cents` unchanged, and `ratio_applied` is null — no ratio was involved and none is recorded. Every posting also freezes `valuation_basis_at_posting` to the report's `valuation_basis` as it stood at the moment of posting, so a reader can tell which numbers were provisional when they were entered. A new posting is created with `status` at `POSTED`, `posted_by_user_id` set to the acting `X-User-ID`, and `void_reason` and `voided_by_user_id` null.

When a `strategy_id` is supplied it must name an implementation strategy whose `approval_state` is `APPROVED`; attaching community benefit spending to a strategy the board never approved is rejected with `ERR_STRATEGY_NOT_APPROVED`. The `strategy_id` may equally be omitted, for spending that is not tied to any single strategy.

**Category totals.** Each of the five category totals is the sum of `valued_cents` across every posting of the report whose `status` is `POSTED`, restricted to postings in that category: `financial_assistance_cents` over `FINANCIAL_ASSISTANCE` postings, `subsidized_services_cents` over `SUBSIDIZED_SERVICES`, `community_health_improvement_cents` over `COMMUNITY_HEALTH_IMPROVEMENT`, `cash_and_inkind_cents` over `CASH_AND_INKIND`, and `community_building_cents` over `COMMUNITY_BUILDING`. Voided postings contribute nothing. The `total_valued_cents` is the sum of those five category totals. The order of rounding is fixed and is not an implementation detail: each posting's `valued_cents` is rounded to a whole number of cents when the posting is valued, and the totals are sums of those already-rounded integers. Multiplying a category's raw charges by the ratio and rounding the sum is a different computation and is not what this specification asks for — the two can differ by one or more cents, and the per-posting rounding is the correct reading.

**Indirect allocation and the grand total.** The base for indirect allocation is `total_valued_cents` — the sum of the five direct category totals, never `grand_total_cents`, which is derived from the allocation and would make the calculation circular. The `overhead_rate` is selected from that base by three tiers: 0.05 when the base is below 5,000,000 cents (50,000 dollars), 0.08 when the base is at least 5,000,000 cents but below 25,000,000 cents (250,000 dollars), and 0.11 when the base is 25,000,000 cents or more. The tier boundaries belong to the higher tier, so a base of exactly 5,000,000 cents selects 0.08 while 4,999,999 selects 0.05, and a base of exactly 25,000,000 selects 0.11. The `indirect_allocation_cents` is the base multiplied by the selected `overhead_rate`, rounded half up to a whole number of cents, and `grand_total_cents` is the base plus that allocation. Finally, `benefit_pct_of_expense` is `grand_total_cents` divided by the owning cycle's `total_operating_expense_cents`, multiplied by 100, and rounded half up to two decimal places. All four of these figures are recomputed from scratch whenever the set of `POSTED` postings changes or the report's ratio changes.

**Voiding a posting.** A posting entered in error is voided rather than deleted, with a mandatory `reason`. The posting's `status` must be `POSTED` and the owning report's `status` must be `OPEN`, so a locked report can never be altered. Voiding is a segregation-of-duties operation: the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the posting's own `posted_by_user_id`, and a self-void is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Either a `CommunityBenefitOfficer` or a `ComplianceOfficer` may perform it. On success the supplied reason is stored in `void_reason`, `voided_by_user_id` records the actor, `status` moves `POSTED` to `VOID`, and every one of the report's totals — the five categories, `total_valued_cents`, `overhead_rate`, `indirect_allocation_cents`, `grand_total_cents` and `benefit_pct_of_expense` — is recomputed so that the report returns to exactly the values it held before that posting was ever entered. Both the posting's and the report's `version` increase by 1.

**Finalizing the report.** Finalization is where the provisional ratio is replaced by the audited one, and only a `ComplianceOfficer` may do it. The single input is `audited_cost_to_charge_ratio`, a decimal between 0.0001 and 1.0000 inclusive carried to four decimal places. Three conditions must hold: the report's `status` must be `OPEN`; the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the report's `created_by_user_id`, rejecting a self-finalization with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; and the report must hold at least one posting whose `status` is `POSTED`, because an empty report has nothing to certify — that rejection is `ERR_REPORT_EMPTY`, and a report whose only postings have all been voided counts as empty.

On success the supplied figure is stored in `audited_cost_to_charge_ratio` and also replaces the report's working `cost_to_charge_ratio`, and `valuation_basis` moves `PROVISIONAL` to `FINAL`. Every posting of the report whose `status` is `POSTED` is then revalued under the new ratio: each charge-based posting's `valued_cents` becomes its `charges_cents` multiplied by the audited ratio, rounded half up to whole cents, with `ratio_applied` updated to the audited ratio; cost-based postings are unaffected, since no ratio ever entered their valuation. Both aggregations then run again over the revalued postings — the five category totals and `total_valued_cents` first, then the tier selection, `indirect_allocation_cents`, `grand_total_cents` and `benefit_pct_of_expense`, in that order, because the tier depends on the new base. Finally `locked_at` is stamped from the logical clock, `finalized_by_user_id` records the actor, and `status` moves `OPEN` to `LOCKED`. A `LOCKED` report accepts no further postings and no further voids.

**Publishing the assessment.** With the board's adoption in hand, a `CommunityBenefitOfficer` publishes the assessment to the community by supplying the `public_url` at which it can be read and the `public_comment_channel` through which comments will be received. The cycle's `status` must be `ADOPTED` and its `adopted_at` must not be null — the record of the board's decision has to exist, not merely the phase. On success the `public_url` and `public_comment_channel` are stored, `published_at` is stamped from the logical clock, the boolean `public_availability_met` is set to true, and `status` moves `ADOPTED` to `PUBLISHED`.

**Filing the return.** Filing is the compliance act that closes the assessment out with the tax authority, and only a `ComplianceOfficer` may perform it. The single input is `return_form_id`, the identifier of the filed form. Filing is deliberately reachable from `ADOPTED` as well as from `PUBLISHED`, so that an attempt to file an unpublished assessment is not turned away as a phase error but is caught by the substantive compliance check: `public_availability_met` must be true, and when it is not the rejection is `ERR_PUBLIC_AVAILABILITY_NOT_MET`. The second check is that the cycle has exactly one community benefit report whose `status` is `LOCKED`; a cycle with no locked report, or with a report still sitting `OPEN`, is rejected with `ERR_REPORT_NOT_LOCKED`.

When both checks pass the `return_form_id` is stored on the cycle, the cycle's `filed_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and `status` moves to `FILED`. The locked report moves in step: its `status` goes `LOCKED` to `FILED` and its own `filed_at` is stamped from the same logical clock reading. Filing also drives the cycle's `posture` to `FROZEN`, and from that moment the entire assessment beneath the cycle is read-only — every child write is refused with `ERR_CYCLE_FROZEN`, as described in §2.4. The one thing a `FROZEN` posture does not block is a further advance of the cycle itself, which is what allows a `FILED` cycle to be walked on to `CLOSED` once its three published annual evaluations are on the record, retiring every approved strategy to `CLOSED_OUT` as described in §3.1.
