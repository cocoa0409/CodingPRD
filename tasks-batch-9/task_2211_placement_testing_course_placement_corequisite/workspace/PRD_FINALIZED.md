# Placement Testing and Corequisite Remediation API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Community college assessment offices no longer place students by a single test score. State policy now obliges them to weigh a *portfolio* of measures — a high-school transcript GPA, a proctored placement test result, and the student's own self-guided pathway declaration — and to place every student into the highest course the evidence supports, attaching a corequisite support course rather than sending the student down a remedial sequence. This API is the system of record for that mandate. It owns the catalogue of courses and the prerequisite graph that connects them, the cut-score policy that translates measures into placements, the proctored test sessions in which scores are produced, the appeal and instructor-override channel that lets a student contest a placement, the term sections and corequisite pairings that make a placement enrollable, and the registration ledger where prerequisite enforcement actually bites.

Three pressures shape the design. First, **auditability**: a placement is a consequential, contestable decision, so every determination records which rule produced it, which measure it read, how confident the office was, and when that confidence expires — and no historical determination is ever silently re-derived when policy changes. Second, **segregation of duties**: the person who proposes a cut-score change may not approve it, the registrar who authored a placement may not review the appeal against it, and the instructor who endorses an appeal may not be the registrar reviewing it. Third, **equity accountability**: cut scores must be validated against real course-success outcomes before they take effect, and both the validation study and the standing course reports are disaggregated by race/ethnicity, English-learner status, first-generation status and Pell eligibility, with a disproportionate-impact test that can block an otherwise statistically successful cut score.

The system is deliberately built on a graph rather than a hierarchy: every resource is a root-level collection joined by identifier references, courses point at other courses through requisite edges, sections point at their corequisite partners, and registrations point at their companion registrations. Consistency is immediate — a single request that touches one entity may legitimately fan out through four or five further entities before it returns — and time is governed by an explicit logical clock so that waiting periods, expiry windows and observation windows are all reproducible.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contract-level rules that hold for every endpoint in the API. Domain behaviour is described in §3.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which they act. Exactly seven roles exist, and their spellings are `SystemAdmin`, `AssessmentDirector`, `AssessmentStaff`, `Proctor`, `Instructor`, `Registrar`, and `Student`.

A request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401 Unauthorized) when `X-User-Role` is absent or is not one of those seven strings, when `X-User-ID` is absent or empty, or when `X-User-Role` is `Student` but `X-User-ID` is not the `id` of an existing student record. A request that authenticates but whose role does not appear in the authorized-role list published for the target operation in §4 is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403 Forbidden).

The seven roles divide responsibility as follows:

- A `SystemAdmin` is the catalogue and roster steward. They own course creation and retirement, requisite-edge creation and retirement, the shared student-roster entry point, section planning and section status changes, the logical clock, and read access across students, courses, requisites, sections, registrations and overrides.
- An `AssessmentDirector` owns placement policy. They alone draft, activate and retire placement rules, and they alone decide cut-score validation studies. They also read the disaggregated course equity report and have read access to students, determinations, appeals, attempts, administrations and studies.
- An `AssessmentStaff` operator runs the assessment office day to day: scheduling test sessions, recording a student's measures, running the multiple-measures selector, driving a determination's evidence axis and its promotion to a final placement, proposing and running validation studies, booking attempts on a student's behalf, voiding attempts, driving session status, and reading the equity report.
- A `Proctor` supervises one room. Checking a candidate in, recording a score, and marking a no-show are theirs exclusively; they also drive session status and void attempts alongside assessment staff.
- An `Instructor` is discipline faculty. They exclusively endorse placement appeals for their course and post final grades, and they share the issuing and revoking of prerequisite overrides with the registrar's office.
- A `Registrar` is the enrolment authority. They exclusively pair and unpair corequisite sections, confirm held registrations, and run the appeal review chain (taking an appeal under review, requesting more information, and deciding it). They share section creation and status, registration creation and dropping, appeal filing, resubmission and withdrawal, and override issuance and revocation.
- A `Student` is the person being placed and acts only on their own records: updating their own measures, booking their own attempts, filing, resubmitting and withdrawing their own appeals, holding and dropping their own registrations, and reading their own student record, attempts, determinations, appeals, registrations and overrides in addition to the public course and section catalogues. A `Student` can never confirm a registration, post a grade, run the selector, or take any step in the appeal review chain.

**Identity semantics.** When `X-User-Role` is `Student`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting student record; ownership checks on any resource that references a student compare that resource's `student_id` to `X-User-ID`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403 Forbidden). When `X-User-Role` is `SystemAdmin`, `AssessmentDirector`, `AssessmentStaff`, `Proctor`, `Instructor`, or `Registrar`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity: the server records it where a transition writes it — as `proctor_user_id` on a test session, `determined_by_user_id` on a determination, `reviewed_by_user_id` or `endorsed_by_user_id` on an appeal, `issued_by_user_id` on a prerequisite override, and `proposed_by_user_id` or `decided_by_user_id` on a validation study — and compares it against those recorded strings, but never against any entity's `id`.

Holding a role is necessary but frequently not sufficient. A `Proctor` may act on a session or an attempt only when `X-User-ID` equals that session's `proctor_user_id`, otherwise the request fails with `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_PROCTOR` (403 Forbidden). A `Registrar` may request information on, or decide, an appeal only when `X-User-ID` equals the appeal's `reviewed_by_user_id`, otherwise `ERR_NOT_APPEAL_REVIEWER` (403 Forbidden) — a second registrar cannot hijack a colleague's appeal. An `Instructor` may revoke only an override whose `issued_by_user_id` equals their `X-User-ID`, otherwise `ERR_NOT_OVERRIDE_ISSUER` (403 Forbidden); a `Registrar` revoking an override is under no such restriction.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints are paginated with two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of items returned; it defaults to 10 when omitted, must be at least 1, and must not exceed 100. `offset` is the number of items to skip; it defaults to 0 and must not be negative. A `limit` outside the range 1 to 100 inclusive, or a negative `offset`, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400 Bad Request).

Unless a specific listing states otherwise, list results are sorted by `created_at` ascending. Two listings state otherwise and are called out where they are described in §3: the per-student determination listing orders by `determined_at` ascending and then `created_at` ascending, and the disaggregated equity report orders its groups by group key ascending.

Every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the envelope's `meta` object.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties

All twelve persisted resource types — students, courses, requisite edges, placement rules, test administrations, test attempts, placement determinations, placement appeals, sections, registrations, prerequisite overrides and cut-score validation studies — carry the same four system-managed properties:

- `id` (string): a server-minted UUID. It carries no type prefix of any kind.
- `created_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, recording when the row was first created.
- `updated_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format, refreshed on every mutation of the row, including mutations produced by a cascade the client did not request directly.
- `version` (integer): a monotonically increasing counter that increments by one on every mutation of the row. No resource type in this API omits `updated_at` or `version`.

Because cascades are pervasive, it bears stating plainly: when an operation on one resource propagates a change to another resource, the second resource's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` incremented exactly as if it had been modified directly. Recomputation of a derived field is a mutation for this purpose.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

Every deadline, expiry, waiting period and observation window in this specification is evaluated against a server-maintained **logical UTC clock**, never against real wall-clock time. Before any client sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; it never falls back to the host's real time. An administrative test-driving capability accepts a `current_utc` instant and moves the logical clock to it.

Advancing the clock performs exactly one declared sweep and nothing else: every prerequisite override in state `ISSUED` whose `expires_at` is at or before the new clock value moves to `EXPIRED`, and every registration in state `PENDING` or `WAITLISTED` that presented such an override has its `prerequisite_status` recomputed. Nothing else is swept. Appeal filing and resubmission windows, validation-study observation windows, determination validity horizons, and section and session statuses are all evaluated lazily at the moment they are read or written, never eagerly on a clock move.

### 2.6. Numeric Precision and Rounding

All rounding in this system is **round half up**, and every quantity has a pinned scale:

- Confidence figures on a determination — `base_confidence_score`, `staleness_penalty` and `confidence_score` — are stored at exactly 1 decimal place. The intermediate per-measure component scores and the per-measure weighted terms that feed them are each rounded to exactly 2 decimal places *before* they are summed, and the sum is then rounded to 1 decimal place. Summing unrounded weighted terms and rounding once at the end is a different algorithm and is not what this system specifies.
- `hs_gpa`, `min_hs_gpa` and `max_hs_gpa` are carried and compared at exactly 2 decimal places, on a scale from 0.00 to 4.50 inclusive.
- Course `units` and a registration's `term_units_at_confirm` are carried at exactly 1 decimal place; `units` must lie between 0.5 and 6.0 inclusive.
- `raw_score` is an integer from 0 to 160 inclusive. `scaled_score`, `min_score` and `max_score` are integers from 0 to 1000 inclusive; a derived `scaled_score` is rounded to a whole number and then clamped into that range.
- Success rates and the disproportionate-impact index — `success_rate` on a course, `strict_success_rate` and `inclusive_success_rate` and `disproportionate_impact_index` on a study, and the per-group rates and `throughput_gap` in the equity report — are stored at exactly 4 decimal places.
- `level_rank` (1 to 100 inclusive), `priority_rank` (1 to 999 inclusive), `seat_capacity`, `duration_minutes`, `validity_days`, `revision_round` and `run_count` are exact integers with no rounding.
- Seat counters and attempt counters are exact integers; both variants of the available-seat calculation floor at zero, and `remaining_attempts` likewise floors at zero.

All datetimes in request bodies and responses are ISO 8601 UTC. In responses, `created_at`, `updated_at`, the envelope's `api_request_timestamp` and the `current_utc` echoed back by the clock-setting operation always carry exactly three fractional digits; every other datetime field is rendered at the precision its own instant carries — with no fractional part at all when the instant falls exactly on a whole second, and with three fractional digits (any finer sub-second component truncated to milliseconds) when it does not. A non-conforming datetime is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400 Bad Request).

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Three write operations mint a child row against a parent whose observable state the client must have read first. Each of them requires a non-optional version echo in the request body, and each rejects a stale echo with a distinct identifier and HTTP 412 Precondition Failed. These are the only three operations in the API that take a version field; no other endpoint accepts one.

- Minting a placement determination requires `expected_student_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the student named by `student_id`. If it does not equal that student's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_STUDENT_VERSION` (412 Precondition Failed). A new determination legitimately supersedes the student's earlier determination in the same subject, so the operation is not blocked by state; the stale echo is the only signal that the counsellor's view of the student's measures — transcript verification posture, attempt counts, remaining retakes — has gone out of date.
- Holding a seat requires `expected_section_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the section named by `section_id`. A mismatch against the section's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SECTION_VERSION` (412 Precondition Failed). Seat holds, waitlist promotions and pairing changes all advance a section's `version`, so a stale echo means the client's seat-availability picture no longer matches the server's. A hold for a different student would otherwise legitimately succeed, which is exactly why this is resolved by a version echo rather than by a state precondition.
- Filing a placement appeal requires `expected_determination_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the determination named by `determination_id`. A mismatch against that determination's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_DETERMINATION_VERSION` (412 Precondition Failed). A new appeal legitimately supersedes any earlier non-terminal appeal on the same determination, so the create is not state-blocked; the stale echo catches the case where the determination's `evidence_state` or `placement_state` moved — for example to `DISPUTED` by an attempt-void cascade — after the client read it.

### 2.8. The Ordered Enforcement Stack

Requests are validated in a single, strictly ascending priority order. **The first rule that is violated determines the response, and no later rule is evaluated.** Text order is not incidental here: it is the contract. The thirty priority bands are:

1. `X-User-Role` is present and equals one of the seven role names — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. `X-User-ID` is present and non-empty, and when the role is `Student` it names an existing student record — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
3. The acting role appears in the target operation's authorized-role list — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
4. Request shape: every required input field is present, no unknown field is supplied, every enum value is a declared member, numeric values respect their range and decimal precision, datetimes are ISO 8601 UTC, `limit` lies in 1 to 100 and `offset` is at least 0 — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Applies to every endpoint.
5. Every path parameter resolves to an existing row, validated left to right, with 404 Not Found reported for the *first* unresolvable segment: `ERR_STUDENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COURSE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REQUISITE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RULE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ADMINISTRATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ATTEMPT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DETERMINATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPEAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SECTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REGISTRATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OVERRIDE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STUDY_NOT_FOUND`. Applies to every endpoint.
6. Every identifier reference supplied in a request body resolves to an existing row, checked in the order the endpoint declares its input fields, reported from the same twelve-code 404 catalogue. This band applies to the eleven creating operations: requisite-edge creation, placement-rule drafting, test-session scheduling, attempt booking, determination minting, appeal filing, section planning, section pairing, seat holding, override issuance, and validation-study proposal.
7. The version echo required by §2.7 matches the parent's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_STUDENT_VERSION`, `ERR_STALE_SECTION_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_DETERMINATION_VERSION` (412). Applies to determination minting, seat holding, and appeal filing.
8. The target resource sits in a state from which the requested transition is declared. Violations report `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409) unless a more specific state code applies, in which case that code is used: `ERR_ADMINISTRATION_NOT_BOOKABLE`, `ERR_ADMINISTRATION_NOT_OPEN`, `ERR_SECTION_NOT_OPEN`, `ERR_SECTION_NOT_CLOSED`, `ERR_OVERRIDE_CONSUMED`, `ERR_APPEAL_NOT_ENDORSED`, or `ERR_DETERMINATION_SUPERSEDED` (all 409). Applies to every operation that drives a declared transition.
9. Cross-entity state preconditions hold: the parent session's `status` for attempt check-in and scoring; the course's `status` being `ACTIVE` for section opening, rule activation and appeal endorsement; the governing determination's `placement_state` and `evidence_state` for registration confirmation; the candidate rule's `status` being `DRAFT` for study running and study approval; and the appealed determination's `placement_state` being `APPEALED` for taking an appeal under review. Violations report `ERR_ADMINISTRATION_NOT_OPEN`, `ERR_COURSE_RETIRED`, `ERR_PLACEMENT_NOT_ACTIONABLE`, `ERR_CANDIDATE_RULE_NOT_DRAFT` or `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409). This band applies to exactly nine operations: attempt check-in, attempt scoring, section status changes, rule activation, registration confirmation, taking an appeal under review, appeal endorsement, study running, and study deciding.
10. Segregation of duties holds — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). Applies to four operations: study deciding, taking an appeal under review, appeal endorsement, and appeal deciding.
11. Actor-to-resource binding holds: `X-User-ID` equals the session's `proctor_user_id` for proctor actions, equals the appeal's `reviewed_by_user_id` for requesting information and for deciding, equals the override's `issued_by_user_id` for an `Instructor` revoke, and equals the owning `student_id` (or the student's own `id`) for `Student`-role actions. Violations report `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_PROCTOR`, `ERR_NOT_APPEAL_REVIEWER`, `ERR_NOT_OVERRIDE_ISSUER` or `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (all 403). This band applies to exactly nine operations: session status changes, attempt check-in, attempt scoring, appeal information requests, appeal resubmission, appeal deciding, override revocation, registration dropping, and student measure updates.
12. Prerequisite enforcement: the prerequisite closure must not evaluate to `UNMET` — otherwise `ERR_PREREQUISITE_NOT_MET` (422). Applies to registration confirmation.
13. English-learner pathway integrity — otherwise `ERR_EL_PATHWAY_VIOLATION` (422). Applies to determination minting and appeal filing.
14. A new `PREREQUISITE` requisite edge must not close a cycle — otherwise `ERR_REQUISITE_CYCLE` (409). Applies to requisite-edge creation.
15. Corequisite pairing integrity — reported as `ERR_COREQ_SECTION_MISMATCH`, `ERR_COREQ_PAIRING_REQUIRED` or `ERR_COREQ_COURSE_INVALID` (all 422). Applies to section pairing, section status changes, and rule activation.
16. The section's course must be the governing determination's `placed_course_id` or its `coreq_course_id` — otherwise `ERR_PLACEMENT_COURSE_MISMATCH` (422). Applies to registration confirmation.
17. Room turnaround geometry — otherwise `ERR_ROOM_TIME_CONFLICT` (409). Applies to test-session scheduling.
18. Corequisite meeting geometry — otherwise `ERR_COREQ_TIME_CONFLICT` (409). Applies to section pairing. Because this band sits below band 15, a pairing request that is both mismatched and time-conflicted reports the mismatch.
19. Proctor daily aggregate — otherwise `ERR_PROCTOR_DAILY_CAP_EXCEEDED` (409). Applies to test-session scheduling.
20. Retake waiting period — otherwise `ERR_RETAKE_WAITING_PERIOD` (422). Applies to attempt booking.
21. Expiry windows: the appeal filing window on appeal filing, the appeal resubmission window on resubmission, and an override's `expires_at` when an override is presented. Violations report `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_CLOSED` or `ERR_OVERRIDE_EXPIRED` (both 422). Applies to appeal filing, appeal resubmission, and seat holding.
22. A validation study's observation window must already be closed — otherwise `ERR_STUDY_WINDOW_OPEN` (422). Applies to study running.
23. Retake budget and session capacity, evaluated in this internal order: the student must have at least one remaining attempt in the session's subject (`ERR_ATTEMPT_LIMIT_REACHED`, 422), the student must not already hold an attempt in state `BOOKED` or `CHECKED_IN` in the same subject (`ERR_ATTEMPT_ALREADY_OPEN`, 422), and the session must not be full (`ERR_ADMINISTRATION_FULL`, 409). Applies to attempt booking.
24. The appeal revision cap — otherwise `ERR_APPEAL_ROUND_LIMIT` (422). Applies to appeal information requests. Note that because band 11 sits above this one, an information request from the wrong registrar reports `ERR_NOT_APPEAL_REVIEWER` even when the round cap is also exhausted.
25. Uniqueness: `student_number` across all students, `course_code` across all courses, the combination of `course_id` with `requisite_course_id` with `requisite_type` among `ACTIVE` requisite edges, and one live registration per student-and-section pair. Violations report `ERR_DUPLICATE_STUDENT_NUMBER`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_COURSE_CODE`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_REQUISITE` or `ERR_DUPLICATE_REGISTRATION` (all 409). Applies to student creation, course creation, requisite-edge creation, and seat holding.
26. Measure completeness and selector outcome: the selector's candidate set must be non-empty (`ERR_NO_PLACEMENT_RULE_MATCH`, 422), and a rule being activated must carry the bounds its `measure_type` requires (`ERR_RULE_MEASURE_INCOMPLETE`, 422). Applies to determination minting and rule activation.
27. Validation-study viability: the strict cohort must contain at least one row after computation (`ERR_STUDY_COHORT_EMPTY`, 422), and on approval the strict success rate and the disproportionate-impact index must each clear their floor (`ERR_VALIDATION_RATE_TOO_LOW` and `ERR_DISPROPORTIONATE_IMPACT`, both 422). Applies to study running and study deciding.
28. A course may not be retired while any of its sections is in status `PLANNED` or `OPEN` — otherwise `ERR_COURSE_HAS_LIVE_SECTIONS` (409). Applies to course retirement.
29. The term unit cap, with corequisite support units included and never dropped to fit — otherwise `ERR_TERM_UNIT_CAP_EXCEEDED` (422). Applies to registration confirmation.
30. Seat capacity: when at least one seat is available the confirmation enrols; when no seat is available but the waitlist still has room the confirmation waitlists instead; when no seat is available and the waitlist is at its cap the request is rejected with `ERR_SECTION_FULL` (409). Applies to registration confirmation.

### 2.9. Reference Resolution and Nested Reads

Although no collection is nested underneath another in the URL space, several read endpoints scope a collection to a parent identifier and several write endpoints accept identifier references in the body. Path segments are always resolved left to right, and the first segment that fails to resolve determines the 404 code, before any body field is examined. Only once every path segment resolves are body references resolved, and those are checked in the order the operation declares its inputs — so, for instance, a rule draft naming both a missing `target_course_id` and a missing `coreq_course_id` reports the course-not-found code once, for the first of the two.

The path identifiers used across the API are `student_id`, `course_id`, `requisite_id`, `rule_id`, `administration_id`, `attempt_id`, `determination_id`, `appeal_id`, `section_id`, `registration_id`, `override_id` and `study_id`; each names the resource whose absence produces that resource type's own not-found code.

The scoped read collections are: a student's determinations, a course's requisite edges (those the course *carries*, meaning edges whose `course_id` is the path course), a session's attempts, and a section's registrations. Each accepts its own filters plus `limit` and `offset`.

### 2.10. Segregation of Duties

Three separation constraints run through the system, and all three surface as `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403 Forbidden):

- **SoD-1.** The `AssessmentDirector` who decides a cut-score validation study must not be the operator who proposed it: the deciding `X-User-ID` must differ from the study's `proposed_by_user_id`. Because only assessment staff can propose a study, this bites when a director also holds the proposer's identifier.
- **SoD-2.** The `Registrar` who takes an appeal under review, and the one who decides it, must not be the operator who authored the appealed placement: the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the appealed determination's `determined_by_user_id`. This is checked both when the appeal is taken under review and again when it is decided.
- **SoD-3.** The `Instructor` who endorses an appeal must not be the operator reviewing it: the endorsing `X-User-ID` must differ from the appeal's `reviewed_by_user_id`.

### 2.11. Cascade Propagation

Consistency is immediate: a single request completes all of its downstream effects before it returns, and there is no deferred reconciliation. Several operations propagate through four or five successive hops, and each hop is caused by the hop before it rather than by the original request — retiring a course retires requisite edges, which revokes overrides, which invalidates prerequisite status; voiding a scored attempt restores retake budget, which disputes a determination's evidence, which denies registrations, which denies their companion registrations and releases their overrides, which frees seats, which promotes waitlists. Each entity touched anywhere in such a chain has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented, and every derived counter recomputed along the way is recomputed from the underlying rows rather than adjusted incrementally where the two could disagree.

Two general ordering rules hold across every cascade. Waitlist promotion, wherever it appears, always processes a section's waitlisted registrations in `created_at` ascending order and stops as soon as no seat remains. Attempt renumbering, wherever it appears, always renumbers a student's surviving non-voided attempts in a subject by `created_at` ascending. Neither ordering ever consults a row's `id`.

### 2.12. Conserved Quantities and Compensating Actions

Three ledgers are conserved, and each has a compensating action pair that must return the system exactly to its prior figures.

**The section seat ledger.** For any section, the sum of `seats_taken`, `seats_held` and `waitlist_count` plus the number of its registrations in state `DENIED` or `DROPPED` equals the total number of registrations attached to that section. Independently, `seats_taken` always equals the number of that section's registrations in state `ENROLLED` or `COMPLETED`, and `seats_taken` never exceeds `seat_capacity`. Holding a seat and then confirming it into `ENROLLED` is compensated by dropping that registration: afterwards `seats_taken` and `seats_held` return to exactly their pre-confirmation values and the ledger total is unchanged, even though the drop's waitlist promotion may have reassigned which registration occupies the freed seat. These invariants are asserted after every registration creation, confirmation, drop, denial and waitlist promotion, and after every section cancellation.

**The retake budget.** For every student and subject pair, `attempt_counts` plus `remaining_attempts` for that subject always equals three, and `attempt_counts` for that subject always equals the number of that student's attempts in that subject whose `state` is `SCORED`. Scoring an attempt is compensated by voiding it: afterwards both figures return to exactly their pre-scoring values with no drift, even though sibling `attempt_ordinal` values and `next_eligible_at` values are renumbered and recomputed in between. These invariants are asserted after every attempt scoring, voiding and no-show marking, and after every session closure and cancellation.

**The override redemption balance.** The number of prerequisite overrides in state `CONSUMED` always equals the number of registrations that name an `override_id` and are in state `ENROLLED` or `COMPLETED`. For every override in state `CONSUMED`, its `consumed_by_registration_id` names a registration in state `ENROLLED` or `COMPLETED`; for every override in state `ISSUED`, `consumed_by_registration_id` is null. Confirming a registration that presents an override moves that override from `ISSUED` to `CONSUMED`, and dropping the registration moves it back from `CONSUMED` to `ISSUED`, restoring the balance and the null invariant. These invariants are asserted after every registration confirmation, drop and denial, and after every override revocation and expiry.
## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Course Catalogue and the Requisite Graph

Everything downstream depends on the catalogue, so a `SystemAdmin` builds it first. A course is created with a `course_code` (2 to 16 characters, unique across the whole catalogue — a repeat is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_COURSE_CODE`), a `title`, a `subject_area` drawn from `MATH`, `ENGLISH`, `ESL` and `OTHER`, a `level_rank` integer from 1 to 100, and `units` between 0.5 and 6.0 carried at one decimal place. Two optional booleans complete the picture: `is_support_course`, which marks the course as a corequisite support course and defaults to false, and `transfer_level`, which marks it as degree-applicable and defaults to false. By office convention `level_rank` encodes curricular altitude, higher being more advanced: transfer-level courses sit at 100, corequisite support courses at 90, and below-transfer courses at 80 or 70. A new course starts with `status` equal to `ACTIVE`, and its three outcome figures initialise to `enrolled_count` of 0, `success_count` of 0 and `success_rate` of null.

Those three figures are derived, never written directly. `enrolled_count` is the number of registrations against sections of this course whose `state` is `ENROLLED` or `COMPLETED`. `success_count` is the number of registrations against sections of this course whose `state` is `COMPLETED` and whose `final_grade` is one of `A`, `B`, `C` or `P`. `success_rate` is null whenever no registration for the course has reached `COMPLETED`; otherwise it is `success_count` divided by the count of the course's `COMPLETED` registrations, rounded to four decimal places. The catalogue listing can be narrowed by `subject_area`, `is_support_course`, `transfer_level` and `status`.

Retiring a course is the single most consequential catalogue action, and the office is protected from doing it carelessly: the course must currently be `ACTIVE`, and no section of it may sit in status `PLANNED` or `OPEN`, or the request is refused with `ERR_COURSE_HAS_LIVE_SECTIONS`. When the retirement proceeds, `ACTIVE → RETIRED` fans out through three further hops. In the first hop, every requisite edge that is `ACTIVE` and names this course as either its `course_id` or its `requisite_course_id` moves `ACTIVE → RETIRED`, and every placement rule in status `DRAFT`, `ACTIVE` or `SUPERSEDED` that names this course as either its `target_course_id` or its `coreq_course_id` moves to `RETIRED`. The second hop is caused by that rule retirement rather than by the retire request itself: every cut-score validation study in state `DRAFT`, `RUNNING` or `COMPLETED` whose `candidate_rule_id` points at one of the just-retired rules moves to `WITHDRAWN`, with `decision_note` set to `candidate rule retired`. The third hop is caused by the requisite retirement: every prerequisite override in state `ISSUED` whose `requisite_id` points at one of the just-retired edges moves `ISSUED → REVOKED`, and each affected registration's `prerequisite_status` is recomputed. The retired course's own `version` increments by one, and so does the `version` of every row mutated anywhere in those three hops.

Requisite edges are declared separately from the courses they join. Creating one supplies the `course_id` that carries the requirement, the `requisite_course_id` that must be satisfied, and a `requisite_type` of `PREREQUISITE`, `COREQUISITE` or `ADVISORY`. Two optional fields tune enforcement: `min_grade`, the lowest grade in the requisite course that counts as satisfaction, drawn from `A`, `B`, `C` and `P` and defaulting to `C`; and `enforcement`, either `BLOCKING` or `WARNING`, defaulting to `BLOCKING`. Only `BLOCKING` `PREREQUISITE` edges are actually enforced when a student registers — `WARNING` edges and `ADVISORY` edges are recorded for advising purposes and never block anyone. A new edge begins with `status` equal to `ACTIVE`. Duplicating the combination of `course_id`, `requisite_course_id` and `requisite_type` among edges that are still `ACTIVE` is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_REQUISITE`.

Prerequisite edges form a directed graph, and the graph must stay acyclic. When a new `PREREQUISITE` edge is declared, the system walks the existing `ACTIVE` `PREREQUISITE` edges depth-first from the proposed `requisite_course_id`; if that walk can reach the proposed `course_id`, the edge would close a cycle and is refused with `ERR_REQUISITE_CYCLE`. The degenerate case where `course_id` and `requisite_course_id` are the same course is a cycle of length one and is refused by the same rule. Cycle detection is the *only* use of transitive reachability in this system; enforcement at registration time considers direct edges only. Creating an edge also recomputes `prerequisite_status` on every registration in state `PENDING` for the edge's `course_id`, so a requirement added mid-term takes hold immediately. The edge listing can be filtered from either end of the edge, by `course_id`, by `requisite_course_id`, by `requisite_type`, by `enforcement` and by `status`.

Retiring a requisite edge directly moves it `ACTIVE → RETIRED` and propagates in two hops: every prerequisite override in state `ISSUED` whose `requisite_id` is this edge moves `ISSUED → REVOKED`, and then, caused by that revocation, every registration in state `PENDING` or `WAITLISTED` whose section belongs to this edge's `course_id` has its `prerequisite_status` recomputed. A registration that had been `OVERRIDDEN` solely by an override revoked in the first hop becomes `UNMET`. The edge's own `version` increments by one. The identical two-hop chain runs when an edge is retired as a cascade of a course retirement rather than by direct request.

### 3.2. The Student Record and the Multiple Measures

A student enters the roster with a `student_number` of 1 to 20 characters that must be unique across the roster — a repeat is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_STUDENT_NUMBER` — and a `full_name`. A `race_ethnicity_code` is required and is one of `AIAN`, `ASIAN`, `BLACK`, `HISPANIC`, `MULTI`, `NHPI`, `WHITE` or `UNKNOWN`; together with `first_generation` and `pell_eligible`, both booleans defaulting to false, and `el_status`, it forms the disaggregation keys on which every equity report is grouped. `el_status` is one of `NOT_EL`, `EL_ACTIVE` or `EL_EXITED` and defaults to `NOT_EL`.

The remaining four inputs are the multiple measures themselves, all optional at creation. `hs_gpa` is either null or a value from 0.00 to 4.50 at exactly two decimal places, and defaults to null. `transcript_recorded_at` is the instant at which the sending high school recorded that GPA; it is client-supplied and is normally *earlier* than the logical clock, because transcript staleness is measured forward from it. `hs_transcript_status` records the verification posture of the transcript measure and is one of `NONE`, `SELF_REPORTED` or `VERIFIED`, defaulting to `NONE`. `self_guided_pathway` is the pathway the student declared for themselves — `STEM`, `BSTEM` or `NON_STEM` — and defaults to null.

Measures change over time, and an `AssessmentStaff` operator or the student themselves may revise them. The revision accepts `hs_gpa`, `hs_transcript_status`, `transcript_recorded_at`, `self_guided_pathway` and `el_status`, all optional, and changes **only** the fields actually supplied; unsupplied fields keep their current values. When the actor is a `Student`, `X-User-ID` must equal the student's own `id`, or the request fails with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. Every such revision increments the student's `version` by one, which invalidates any `expected_student_version` a client had previously observed. Critically, revising measures does **not** re-derive any existing placement determination — a determination is a historical decision, and reflecting new measures requires minting a new determination.

Four fields on the student record are computed and read-only. `attempt_counts` is an object shaped as `{ MATH: integer, ENGLISH: integer, ESL: integer }` giving, per subject, the number of the student's test attempts whose `state` is `SCORED`; attempts that are `VOIDED` or `NO_SHOW` are excluded from it. `remaining_attempts` has the same shape and holds three minus the corresponding entry of `attempt_counts` for each subject, floored at zero — so a fresh record has three remaining attempts in each of `MATH`, `ENGLISH` and `ESL`. `placement_status` is `FINAL` when the student holds at least one determination whose `placement_state` is `FINAL`; otherwise it is `PROVISIONAL` when the student holds at least one determination whose `placement_state` is `PROVISIONAL` or `APPEALED`; otherwise it is `UNPLACED`, which is what a newly created record shows. `advising_hold` is true exactly when the student holds at least one determination whose `evidence_state` is `DISPUTED`, or at least one determination whose `placement_state` is `APPEALED`, and false otherwise; a newly created record shows false. The roster listing may be filtered by `student_number`, `el_status`, `placement_status` and `hs_transcript_status`, and a student's determinations can be read as a scoped collection, filtered by `subject_area` and `placement_state`, returned in every `placement_state` and ordered by `determined_at` ascending and then `created_at` ascending.

### 3.3. Test Sessions, Proctoring and the Retake Budget

An `AssessmentStaff` operator schedules a proctored session by naming the `room_code` whose time is exclusively occupied, the `subject_area` being tested (`MATH`, `ENGLISH` or `ESL`), the `proctor_user_id` of the operator who will supervise it, the inclusive `starts_at` and the exclusive `ends_at` of the sitting, and a `seat_capacity` between 1 and 200. `ends_at` must be strictly after `starts_at`, and the sitting may not run longer than eight hours; a longer or inverted window is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Because `Proctor` is an opaque operator role rather than a persisted entity, `proctor_user_id` records an identifier string and is not a reference to any row. A new session opens in `status` `SCHEDULED`, exposes `duration_minutes` as the whole number of minutes between `starts_at` and `ends_at`, and exposes `seats_booked` as the number of attempts against it whose `state` is `BOOKED`, `CHECKED_IN` or `SCORED`.

Two capacity protections apply at scheduling time. The first is **room turnaround**: rooms must be reset between sittings, and the office allows exactly half an hour to do it. Two sessions sharing a `room_code`, both in status `SCHEDULED`, `OPEN` or `CLOSED`, conflict when the first one's `starts_at` falls strictly before the second one's `ends_at` advanced by thirty minutes *and* the second one's `starts_at` falls strictly before the first one's `ends_at` advanced by thirty minutes. A gap of exactly thirty minutes between them is therefore permitted; a gap of twenty-nine is not. Sessions already in status `CANCELLED` never conflict with anything. A conflict is refused with `ERR_ROOM_TIME_CONFLICT`. The second protection is the **proctor daily aggregate**: for the named `proctor_user_id`, the office sums the minutes of every session in status `SCHEDULED`, `OPEN` or `CLOSED`, plus the proposed session, *clipped to each UTC calendar day* so that a sitting spanning midnight contributes its own minutes to each of the two days it touches. If that sum exceeds eight hours on any single UTC calendar day, the request is refused with `ERR_PROCTOR_DAILY_CAP_EXCEEDED`. The session listing may be filtered by `room_code`, `subject_area`, `status`, `proctor_user_id`, and a start-instant range expressed as `starts_at_from` and `starts_at_to`.

A session's lifecycle is driven by supplying a `target_status`. `SCHEDULED → OPEN` is a proctoring action: `X-User-ID` must equal the session's `proctor_user_id` when the acting role is `Proctor`, otherwise `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_PROCTOR`, and the logical clock must still be strictly before `ends_at`, otherwise the sitting has already passed and the request fails with `ERR_ADMINISTRATION_WINDOW_PASSED`. `OPEN → CLOSED` likewise requires the assigned proctor's identifier. `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED` and `OPEN → CANCELLED` are administrative actions taken by an `AssessmentDirector`. Each of these transitions increments the session's `version` by one.

Closing an open session resolves the candidates who never sat or never finished. In the first hop, every attempt against the session whose `state` is `BOOKED` moves `BOOKED → NO_SHOW`, and every attempt whose `state` is `CHECKED_IN` moves `CHECKED_IN → VOIDED` with `void_reason` set to `PROCTOR_ERROR`. The second hop follows from those voids: because voided attempts leave the ordinal pool, `attempt_ordinal` is renumbered across each affected student's surviving non-voided attempts in the same `subject_area`, in `created_at` ascending order. The session's `seats_booked` is then recomputed. Cancelling a session — whether from `SCHEDULED` or from `OPEN`, with an identical chain in both cases — instead moves every attempt whose `state` is `BOOKED` or `CHECKED_IN` to `VOIDED` with `void_reason` set to `ADMIN_CORRECTION`; the second hop recomputes `attempt_counts` and `remaining_attempts` for every affected student in the session's subject and renumbers `attempt_ordinal` over their remaining non-voided attempts by `created_at` ascending; and the third hop, caused by that renumbering, recomputes `next_eligible_at` for every renumbered attempt still in state `SCORED`, from its own unchanged `scored_at` and its **new** `attempt_ordinal`.

Booking a seat is available to an `AssessmentStaff` operator or to the student themselves, and takes only a `student_id` and an `administration_id`. The new attempt begins in `state` `BOOKED`, copies `subject_area` from the session at booking time (it never changes afterwards), and receives an `attempt_ordinal`. Booking is refused with `ERR_ADMINISTRATION_NOT_BOOKABLE` when the session's `status` is `CLOSED` or `CANCELLED`, and with `ERR_ADMINISTRATION_FULL` when `seats_booked` has already reached `seat_capacity`. The attempt listing may be filtered by `student_id`, `administration_id`, `state`, `subject_area` and `irregularity_flag`.

The **retake budget** is what keeps placement testing from becoming score shopping. Each student may hold at most three scored attempts in each subject; booking when `remaining_attempts` for the session's subject has fallen to zero is refused with `ERR_ATTEMPT_LIMIT_REACHED`, and booking while the student already holds an attempt in state `BOOKED` or `CHECKED_IN` in that same subject is refused with `ERR_ATTEMPT_ALREADY_OPEN`. Only `SCORED` attempts consume budget; a `NO_SHOW` never does, and a void gives budget back.

Separately, a **waiting period** stands between successive sittings, and it lengthens sharply. The tier is keyed on the attempt's `attempt_ordinal`: a first attempt imposes 14 days, a second attempt imposes 30 days, and a third or later attempt imposes 180 days. Ordinals are assigned by taking the student's attempts in the subject whose `state` is not `VOIDED`, sorting them by `created_at` ascending — never by `id` — and numbering them from one; that pool deliberately retains `NO_SHOW` attempts, so a missed sitting still advances the tier applied to the next scored attempt. For each attempt in the pool whose `state` is `SCORED`, `next_eligible_at` is its own `scored_at` advanced by the whole number of days its tier prescribes; for every attempt in the pool whose `state` is not `SCORED`, `next_eligible_at` is null. Booking a new attempt then consults the latest `next_eligible_at` across the student's `SCORED` attempts in the session's subject: the booking is allowed when there is no such value at all, or when that latest value falls at or before the session's `starts_at`. Equality is permitted — a gate landing exactly on `starts_at` still books. A gate strictly after `starts_at` is refused with `ERR_RETAKE_WAITING_PERIOD`.

Inside the room, the assigned `Proctor` checks a candidate in, advancing `BOOKED → CHECKED_IN`. That requires the parent session's `status` to be `OPEN`, or the request fails with `ERR_ADMINISTRATION_NOT_OPEN`; requires `X-User-ID` to equal the session's `proctor_user_id`; and requires the logical clock to sit at or after `starts_at` and strictly before `ends_at`. Scoring then advances `CHECKED_IN → SCORED` and again requires the session to be `OPEN` and the actor to be the assigned proctor. Scoring supplies a `raw_score`, which must be an integer from 0 to 160 inclusive, and optionally `irregularity_flag`, the proctor's record of a testing irregularity, which defaults to false. The transition stores `raw_score` and `irregularity_flag`, sets `scored_at` to the logical clock, and derives `scaled_score` as follows: multiply `raw_score` by 6.25 and round the product half up to a whole number; subtract a penalty of 25 points if `irregularity_flag` is true and nothing otherwise; then raise the result to 0 if it went below 0, and lower it to 1000 if it went above 1000. Scoring also assigns `attempt_ordinal` as one plus the number of the student's non-voided attempts in the same subject created strictly earlier, computes `next_eligible_at` from the tier table above, increases the student's `attempt_counts` for that subject by one, decreases `remaining_attempts` for that subject by one, and increments the student's `version` by one.

Two terminal outcomes remain. Marking a `BOOKED` attempt as a no-show advances `BOOKED → NO_SHOW`, sets `next_eligible_at` to null, and leaves the attempt in the ordinal pool while touching neither `attempt_counts` nor `remaining_attempts`. Voiding a `SCORED` attempt — available to the assigned `Proctor` or to an `AssessmentStaff` operator, and requiring a `void_reason` of `IRREGULARITY`, `PROCTOR_ERROR` or `ADMIN_CORRECTION` — is the deepest cascade in the system, and it is described where its consequences land, in §3.6.

### 3.4. Placement Policy: Rules, Cut Scores and Validation Studies

An `AssessmentDirector` writes placement policy as a set of **placement rules**, each of which reads one of the three measures and names the course a qualifying student lands in. A rule is drafted with a `subject_area` of `MATH`, `ENGLISH` or `ESL`; a `measure_type` of `TRANSCRIPT_GPA`, `TEST_SCORE` or `SELF_GUIDED`; a `target_course_id` naming the course the student is placed into; a `priority_rank` integer from 1 to 999; an inclusive `effective_from` instant; and a human-readable `version_label` identifying the cut-score generation. Optional fields carry the band itself and the pathway gates: `coreq_course_id` names the corequisite support course attached to the placement and defaults to null, `coreq_required` defaults to false and marks a rule whose placements must carry that support course, `effective_to` is the *exclusive* end of the rule's effective window and defaults to null meaning open-ended, and `required_el_status` restricts the rule to students whose `el_status` equals the named value. A rule drafted this way starts with `status` equal to `DRAFT` and `activated_at` equal to null, and a `DRAFT` rule is never consulted when a student is placed.

The band fields differ by measure, and their boundary inclusivity is not symmetric — this is deliberate, so that adjacent GPA bands tile without overlap. For a rule reading transcript GPA, `min_hs_gpa` is an **inclusive** lower bound and `max_hs_gpa` is an **exclusive** upper bound; both are null or values from 0.00 to 4.50 at two decimal places, and when both are supplied `min_hs_gpa` must be strictly less than `max_hs_gpa`. For a rule reading a test score, `min_score` is an **inclusive** lower bound and `max_score` is an **inclusive** upper bound; both are null or integers from 0 to 1000, and when both are supplied `min_score` must be less than or equal to `max_score`. For a rule reading a self-guided declaration, `required_self_guided_pathway` names the `STEM`, `BSTEM` or `NON_STEM` pathway the student must have declared.

Activation is what puts a rule into service. `DRAFT → ACTIVE` requires that the rule currently be `DRAFT`, that the course named by `target_course_id` have `status` `ACTIVE`, and that the rule's measure be completely specified for its `measure_type`: a transcript rule needs at least one of `min_hs_gpa` and `max_hs_gpa` to be non-null, a test-score rule needs at least one of `min_score` and `max_score` to be non-null, and a self-guided rule needs `required_self_guided_pathway` to be non-null. A one-sided band — only a floor, or only a ceiling — is complete and activates; a rule with both bounds null on its own measure is rejected with `ERR_RULE_MEASURE_INCOMPLETE`. Where `coreq_required` is true, `coreq_course_id` must be non-null, must name a course whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `is_support_course` is true, and there must be an `ACTIVE` `COREQUISITE` requisite edge joining `target_course_id` to that support course; any failure here is rejected with `ERR_COREQ_COURSE_INVALID`. Where `coreq_required` is false no support course is needed at all. Referencing a retired course from either `target_course_id` or `coreq_course_id` is rejected with `ERR_COURSE_RETIRED`. On success the rule's `activated_at` is set to the logical clock, and one hop follows: **every other** placement rule that is currently `ACTIVE` and shares the same combination of `subject_area`, `measure_type` and `target_course_id` moves `ACTIVE → SUPERSEDED`. `version_label` is *not* part of that supersession key, so relabelling does not protect a rule from being superseded; conversely, rules that target a *different* course coexist happily as separate placement bands and never supersede one another. When a rule becomes `SUPERSEDED`, existing determinations keep their `applied_rule_id` untouched — historical placements are never re-derived.

Retirement is available from `DRAFT`, from `ACTIVE` and from `SUPERSEDED`, and beyond being in one of those statuses it carries no precondition; the same terminal `RETIRED` status is also reached as a cascade of a course retirement, in which case the trigger is that the course being retired is the rule's `target_course_id` or its `coreq_course_id`. A retired rule leaves the selector's candidate set immediately. Retiring a rule from `DRAFT` or from `ACTIVE` also withdraws its studies: every cut-score validation study in state `DRAFT`, `RUNNING` or `COMPLETED` whose `candidate_rule_id` is this rule moves to `WITHDRAWN` with a `decision_note` recorded. Retiring a rule that was already `SUPERSEDED` has no effect beyond refreshing `updated_at` and incrementing `version`. The rule listing may be filtered by `subject_area`, `measure_type`, `status`, `target_course_id`, `coreq_course_id` and `required_el_status`.

**Validation studies** are the evidence gate a new cut score must pass. An `AssessmentStaff` operator proposes one against a `candidate_rule_id` that must still be in status `DRAFT` — a candidate in any other status is refused with `ERR_CANDIDATE_RULE_NOT_DRAFT` — naming a `subject_area` that must match the candidate rule's own subject, an inclusive `window_start_at` and an exclusive `window_end_at` bounding the outcome observation window. A window whose `window_end_at` is at or before its `window_start_at`, or a `subject_area` that disagrees with the rule's, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The new study begins in state `DRAFT` with `run_count` of 0, with `proposed_by_user_id` set to the proposing `X-User-ID`, with `decided_by_user_id` and `decision_note` null, with `disproportionate_impact_index` and `reference_group_key` null, with `strict_cohort_size`, `strict_success_count`, `inclusive_cohort_size` and `inclusive_success_count` all 0, and with `strict_success_rate` and `inclusive_success_rate` null. Proposing a study for a candidate rule that already has a live study withdraws the earlier one: a study in state `DRAFT` or `COMPLETED` for the same `candidate_rule_id` moves to `WITHDRAWN` with a `decision_note` recorded. The study listing may be filtered by `subject_area`, `candidate_rule_id` and `state`.

Running a study computes its metrics. The transition sequence `DRAFT → RUNNING → COMPLETED` happens inside the single request: the run begins, both cohort variants and the impact index are computed, and the study lands in `COMPLETED` before the response is returned. A re-run is the same request against a study already in `COMPLETED`, giving the loop `COMPLETED → RUNNING → COMPLETED`; on a re-run every strict figure, every inclusive figure and the impact index are recomputed from scratch rather than accumulated on top of the previous run. Either entry into `RUNNING` requires the candidate rule to still be in status `DRAFT`, failing with `ERR_CANDIDATE_RULE_NOT_DRAFT`, and requires the observation window to be closed, meaning `window_end_at` must be at or before the logical clock; a window whose `window_end_at` is strictly after the clock is refused with `ERR_STUDY_WINDOW_OPEN`. Reaching `COMPLETED` additionally requires the strict cohort to contain at least one row: a strict cohort of size zero aborts the whole request with `ERR_STUDY_COHORT_EMPTY` and leaves the study in whatever state it held before the call, which is `DRAFT` on a first run and `COMPLETED` on a re-run. That abort applies **even when the inclusive cohort is non-empty**. A completed run increments `run_count` by one.

The cohort is assembled from graded registrations. Let the candidate rule's `target_course_id` be the course under study. A registration is a candidate row when its `state` is `COMPLETED`, its section belongs to that course, and its `graded_at` falls inside the half-open observation window — at or after `window_start_at` and strictly before `window_end_at`. For each candidate row the system also identifies the *governing determination*: among that student's determinations whose `subject_area` matches the study's subject, whose `placed_course_id` is the course under study, and whose `placement_state` is any of `PROVISIONAL`, `FINAL`, `SUPERSEDED` or `APPEALED`, it takes the one with the greatest `determined_at`, breaking a tie by the greatest `created_at`. A row counts as a success when its `final_grade` is one of `A`, `B`, `C` or `P`.

Two cohort variants are then computed and stored side by side, and they differ in exactly two respects. The **strict** variant admits a candidate row only when a governing determination exists, when that determination's `measure_type_used` is `TEST_SCORE`, and when the row's `final_grade` is not `W` — so withdrawals are excluded from the denominator altogether and only test-placed students participate. Its size is stored as `strict_cohort_size`, its successes as `strict_success_count`, and `strict_success_rate` is `strict_success_count` divided by `strict_cohort_size` rounded to four decimal places, or null when the size is zero. The **inclusive** variant admits a candidate row whenever a governing determination exists, whatever measure placed the student, so students placed by `TRANSCRIPT_GPA`, `SELF_GUIDED` or `APPEAL_OVERRIDE` are all counted, and `W` grades stay in the denominator as non-successes. Its figures are stored as `inclusive_cohort_size`, `inclusive_success_count` and `inclusive_success_rate`, computed the same way. The inclusive variant is always computed and always stored, but the approval gate never consults it — it exists so the office can see how much of its apparent success rate comes from cohort definition.

Immediately after the cohorts, the **disproportionate impact index** is computed over the strict cohort only; rows that appear solely in the inclusive cohort never participate. Group the strict cohort by the `race_ethnicity_code` of each row's student. For each distinct code, count the group's rows, count the group's successes, and take the group rate as successes divided by rows rounded to four decimal places. A group is eligible for the impact test only when it holds at least ten rows — small subgroups are statistically meaningless and are set aside. When no group is eligible, `reference_group_key` is null and `disproportionate_impact_index` is 1.0000, meaning no impact is detectable. Otherwise the reference group is the eligible group with the highest rate, breaking a tie in favour of the larger group and, if still tied, in favour of the lexicographically earlier code; its code is stored as `reference_group_key`. If the reference group's rate is exactly zero, `disproportionate_impact_index` is 1.0000. Otherwise the index is the smallest value of any eligible group's rate divided by the reference group's rate, rounded to four decimal places.

The `AssessmentDirector` closes the loop by deciding a `COMPLETED` study, supplying a `decision` and a `decision_note`. The decider's `X-User-ID` must differ from the study's `proposed_by_user_id` under SoD-1, and `decided_by_user_id` records the deciding identifier. A decision to approve carries `COMPLETED → APPROVED` and is gated twice: `strict_success_rate` must be at least 0.7000, failing which the request is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION_RATE_TOO_LOW`, and `disproportionate_impact_index` must be at least 0.8000, failing which it is refused with `ERR_DISPROPORTIONATE_IMPACT`. `inclusive_success_rate` is never consulted at this gate. Approval also requires the candidate rule to still be in status `DRAFT`. On approval two hops follow: the candidate rule moves `DRAFT → ACTIVE` with `activated_at` set to the logical clock, and then, caused by that activation, every other placement rule that is `ACTIVE` and shares the same `subject_area`, `measure_type` and `target_course_id` moves `ACTIVE → SUPERSEDED` — again regardless of `version_label`, and again leaving rules that target a different course untouched. A decision to reject carries `COMPLETED → REJECTED`, records `decided_by_user_id` and `decision_note` under the same SoD-1 constraint, and leaves the candidate rule in `DRAFT`.

### 3.5. Running the Multiple-Measures Selector

Minting a placement is the heart of the system. An `AssessmentStaff` operator asks for a placement by naming a `student_id`, a `subject_area` and the `expected_student_version` they last read; a mismatch against the student's current `version` is refused with `ERR_STALE_STUDENT_VERSION` before any measure is evaluated. The operation then runs a four-part selector policy over the placement rules.

**The eligibility filter** assembles the candidate set. A rule is a candidate only when *all* of the following hold: its `status` is `ACTIVE` — rules in `DRAFT`, `SUPERSEDED` or `RETIRED` never enter the candidate set at all; its `subject_area` equals the requested subject; its `effective_from` is at or before the logical clock; its `effective_to` is either null or strictly after the logical clock; its `required_el_status` is either null or equal to the student's `el_status`; and the student satisfies the rule's measure. Measure satisfaction is judged per `measure_type`. A `TRANSCRIPT_GPA` rule is satisfied when the student's `hs_transcript_status` is `SELF_REPORTED` or `VERIFIED` — a status of `NONE` disqualifies every transcript rule — and `hs_gpa` is not null, and, where the rule sets `min_hs_gpa`, the student's `hs_gpa` is at or above it, and, where the rule sets `max_hs_gpa`, the student's `hs_gpa` is strictly below it. A `TEST_SCORE` rule is satisfied when the student has a best scaled score at all and, where the rule sets `min_score`, that best is at or above it, and, where the rule sets `max_score`, that best is at or below it — note that unlike the GPA band, both score bounds are inclusive. A `SELF_GUIDED` rule is satisfied when the student's `self_guided_pathway` is not null and equals the rule's `required_self_guided_pathway`.

The **best scaled score** is drawn from the student's attempts in the requested subject whose `state` is `SCORED`; attempts that are `VOIDED` or `NO_SHOW` contribute nothing. If that pool is empty the best score is absent, and every test-score rule fails its measure. Otherwise the best is the greatest `scaled_score` in the pool. The pool member holding that greatest score is the *cited* attempt, and when two pool members tie on `scaled_score` the cited attempt is the one with the earliest `created_at`.

**The ranking expression** then sorts the candidates so that the highest placement the student qualifies for wins. The first key is the `level_rank` of the rule's `target_course_id`, in *descending* order, so the most advanced course wins. The second key breaks ties among equally advanced courses by measure precedence, in ascending order, where transcript GPA precedes test score and test score precedes self-guided declaration — the office trusts a verified transcript over a single sitting, and a sitting over a student's own pathway declaration. The third key is `priority_rank`, ascending, so a smaller number wins. When all three keys tie, the fourth and final key is `created_at`, ascending: the rule written first prevails. The winner is the first candidate after this sort.

**The empty-pool fallback** is a rejection rather than a default placement: when the candidate set is empty, the request is refused with `ERR_NO_PLACEMENT_RULE_MATCH` and no determination is created.

The winner writes the new determination's core references: `applied_rule_id` is the winning rule, `measure_type_used` is that rule's `measure_type`, and `placed_course_id` is its `target_course_id`. `coreq_course_id` is copied from the winning rule *only when* that rule's `coreq_required` is true, and is null otherwise — so a rule that names a support course without requiring it does not attach one to the placement. `source_attempt_id` is the cited attempt when `measure_type_used` is `TEST_SCORE`, and null for every other measure. `determined_by_user_id` records the operator's `X-User-ID` and `determined_at` records the logical clock.

Immediately after the winner is chosen — and never before — the **English learner pathway gate** runs. When the student's `el_status` is `EL_ACTIVE` and the requested subject is `ENGLISH` or `ESL`, the winning rule's `required_el_status` must itself be `EL_ACTIVE`, or the request is refused with `ERR_EL_PATHWAY_VIOLATION`. This gate never causes re-ranking: if the top-ranked rule is not pathway-appropriate for an actively-served English learner, the whole request is rejected rather than quietly falling through to the runner-up. A gate on the winner, applied after ranking, is what makes the placement auditable.

**Confidence** is then scored on a 0-to-100 scale from three components. The transcript component is worth its GPA as a percentage of a 4.00 scale: when `hs_transcript_status` is `SELF_REPORTED` or `VERIFIED` and `hs_gpa` is not null, take `hs_gpa` divided by 4.00, multiply by 100, cap the result at 100.00, and round to two decimal places; otherwise the transcript component is 0.00. The test component is worth the best scaled score as a percentage of the 1000-point scale: when a best scaled score exists, divide it by 1000, multiply by 100, cap at 100.00 and round to two decimal places; otherwise the test component is 0.00. The self-guided component is a flat 60.00 when `self_guided_pathway` is not null and 0.00 when it is null. The three components are weighted at one half for the transcript, thirty-five hundredths for the test and fifteen hundredths for the self-guided declaration.

The rounding order here is mandated and is not a matter of taste. **Each weighted term is rounded to two decimal places first, and only then are the three rounded terms added; the sum is finally rounded to one decimal place** and stored as `base_confidence_score`. Summing the unrounded products and rounding once at the end is a different figure and is wrong. Note also that all three components are always evaluated: a student placed by test score still earns transcript credit if they have a usable GPA, because confidence measures how much evidence stands behind the placement, not how the placement was reached.

**Validity** is tiered off `base_confidence_score` — the figure *before* any staleness penalty. A base of at least 85.0 earns `validity_days` of 730; a base of at least 70.0 but below 85.0 earns 365; anything below 70.0 earns 180. `expires_at` is `determined_at` advanced by that many days. Both `validity_days` and `expires_at` are fixed at this moment and are never recomputed afterwards, even though the penalty applied next may drag the stored `confidence_score` below the tier boundary that produced them.

**Staleness** then penalises placements resting on old transcripts. When `transcript_recorded_at` is null the `staleness_penalty` is 0.0. Otherwise, take the whole number of complete days from `transcript_recorded_at` to `expires_at`, subtract 1095 days — three years of grace — divide the remainder by 73.0, floor the result at 0.0, cap it at 20.0, and round to one decimal place. So a transcript younger than three years at the placement's expiry attracts no penalty at all, and every further 73 days of age costs one tenth of a point up to a ceiling of twenty. `confidence_score` is `base_confidence_score` less `staleness_penalty`, floored at 0.0 and rounded to one decimal place.

The determination's opening states follow. `placement_state` is always `PROVISIONAL`. `evidence_state` is `VERIFIED` when `confidence_score` is at least 70.0 **and** the student's `hs_transcript_status` is `VERIFIED`; in every other case it is `UNVERIFIED`. Both conditions are required — a very confident placement on a merely self-reported transcript still opens `UNVERIFIED`. Finally, the student's previous non-terminal determination in the same `subject_area` is superseded: a determination in `placement_state` `PROVISIONAL` or `FINAL` moves to `SUPERSEDED` with its `superseded_by_id` pointing at the new row, and the student's `version` increments by one. Determinations may be listed and filtered by `student_id`, by `subject_area`, by `measure_type_used`, by either state axis using `placement_state` and `evidence_state`, and by any of the six references `applied_rule_id`, `placed_course_id`, `coreq_course_id`, `source_attempt_id` and `superseded_by_id`.

### 3.6. The Determination's Two Axes

A determination carries two state fields that move independently. `placement_state` tracks how settled the placement is; `evidence_state` tracks how well-founded it is. The finalize action touches only the first; the evidence action touches only the second. They are coupled at exactly one point, described below.

On the **placement axis**, an `AssessmentStaff` operator promotes `PROVISIONAL → FINAL`. That promotion is where the two axes meet: it requires `evidence_state` to be `VERIFIED`, and an `UNVERIFIED` or `DISPUTED` determination is refused with `ERR_EVIDENCE_NOT_VERIFIED`. It further requires the logical clock to be strictly before `expires_at`, since a lapsed placement cannot be settled; an expired determination is refused with `ERR_PLACEMENT_EXPIRED`. Promotion recomputes the student's `placement_status` to `FINAL` and increments the student's `version` by one. Filing an appeal moves the determination `PROVISIONAL → APPEALED` or `FINAL → APPEALED`, in each case recomputing `advising_hold` to true and incrementing the determination's `version` by one. Deciding that appeal returns it: on a denial the determination leaves `APPEALED` for `FINAL` when its `evidence_state` is `VERIFIED`, and for `PROVISIONAL` when its `evidence_state` is `UNVERIFIED` or `DISPUTED` — a genuine cycle, since a determination that was `FINAL`, was appealed and had the appeal denied is `FINAL` again. Withdrawing an appeal releases the determination from `APPEALED` by the same return rule. On a grant the determination instead moves `APPEALED → SUPERSEDED`. `SUPERSEDED` is terminal on this axis, and it is also where a determination lands when a newer determination for the same student and subject is minted.

On the **evidence axis**, an `AssessmentStaff` operator supplies a `target_evidence_state` and an optional `note`. Moving to `VERIFIED` from `UNVERIFIED` requires that the student's `hs_transcript_status` be `VERIFIED`, or that the student hold at least one attempt in state `SCORED` whose `subject_area` matches the determination's; when neither holds, the request is refused with `ERR_NO_VERIFIABLE_EVIDENCE`. Moving to `VERIFIED` from `DISPUTED` is harder, because a dispute usually means the cited attempt was thrown out: it requires the student to hold at least one attempt in state `SCORED` in the determination's subject whose `created_at` is strictly later than the voided attempt named by `source_attempt_id` — fresh evidence, not the same evidence re-presented — and fails with `ERR_NO_VERIFIABLE_EVIDENCE` otherwise. Moving to `DISPUTED`, whether from `UNVERIFIED` or from `VERIFIED`, needs no evidence test at all. Any evidence move requires that `placement_state` not be `SUPERSEDED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_DETERMINATION_SUPERSEDED`. Every evidence move recomputes the student's `advising_hold` and increments the determination's `version` by one. Because `advising_hold` is derived when the student is read rather than stored, an evidence move — including one that lands on `DISPUTED` and fans out through the enrolment consequences below — does not itself mutate the student row: the student's `version` and `updated_at` are left exactly as they were, and the determination is the only row the move itself touches. This is a deliberate exception to the general cascade rule of §2.4, and it is confined to the evidence axis: every other operation that reaches into a student's placement or attempt picture still increments that student's `version` — minting and finalizing a determination, scoring and voiding an attempt, closing and cancelling a session, and the appeal transitions that drive a determination onto or off `APPEALED` (filing, deciding and withdrawing).

Landing on `DISPUTED` — by direct action or by cascade — has consequences for enrolment. Every registration held by that determination's own `student_id`, in state `PENDING`, `ENROLLED` or `WAITLISTED`, whose section belongs to the determination's `placed_course_id` or its `coreq_course_id` moves to `DENIED` with `denial_reason` set to `EVIDENCE_DISPUTED`; the seats those rows held are released; any override those rows had consumed returns from `CONSUMED` to `ISSUED`; and each affected section then promotes its waitlist in `created_at` ascending order. Registrations belonging to any *other* student are left untouched even when they sit on those same courses.

**Voiding a scored attempt** is the deepest chain in the system, because it removes the evidence a placement was built on. The action names a `void_reason` and runs in five hops. The first hop sets `void_reason` and moves the attempt `SCORED → VOIDED`; the student's `attempt_counts` for that subject decreases by one and `remaining_attempts` increases by one, giving the retake budget back; the student's surviving non-voided attempts in that subject are renumbered by `created_at` ascending; and each renumbered sibling still in state `SCORED` has its `next_eligible_at` recomputed from its own unchanged `scored_at` and its **new** `attempt_ordinal`, which can move a sibling's gate either earlier or later. The second hop follows from the attempt leaving the countable set: every determination whose `source_attempt_id` is this attempt and whose `placement_state` is `PROVISIONAL` or `FINAL` has its `evidence_state` set to `DISPUTED`, and the student's `advising_hold` recomputes to true. A determination already `SUPERSEDED` is left alone. The third hop follows from that dispute: every registration held by that determination's own `student_id`, in state `PENDING`, `ENROLLED` or `WAITLISTED`, whose section belongs to that determination's `placed_course_id` or `coreq_course_id` moves to `DENIED` with `denial_reason` set to `EVIDENCE_DISPUTED`, and the affected sections decrement `seats_taken`, `seats_held` or `waitlist_count` according to the state each row was in. The fourth hop follows from the third: for each registration denied in the third hop, its `coreq_registration_id` counterpart, if that counterpart is in state `PENDING`, `ENROLLED` or `WAITLISTED`, also moves to `DENIED` with the same `denial_reason`, and every override in state `CONSUMED` whose `consumed_by_registration_id` names any row in the denied set moves `CONSUMED → ISSUED` with `consumed_by_registration_id` reset to null. The fifth hop follows from the seat releases in the third and fourth hops: every section that released a seat promotes its `WAITLISTED` registrations to `ENROLLED` in `created_at` ascending order, one at a time, for as long as `available_seats` remains at least one. Each hop only runs on the rows the previous hop actually changed, so a void of an attempt no determination cites stops after the first hop.

### 3.7. Appeals, Endorsement and the Override Determination

A student who believes their placement is wrong — or a `Registrar` acting for them — files an appeal naming the `determination_id` under challenge, the `requested_course_id` they believe they belong in, the `grounds` (`NEW_TRANSCRIPT`, `TEST_IRREGULARITY`, `EL_PATHWAY` or `OTHER`), a `statement` of 1 to 2000 characters, and the `expected_determination_version` last read; a mismatch against the determination's current `version` is refused with `ERR_STALE_DETERMINATION_VERSION`. The appeal opens in `state` `SUBMITTED` with `revision_round` of 0, `filed_at` set to the logical clock, `student_id` copied from the determination being appealed, and `reviewed_by_user_id`, `endorsed_by_user_id`, `info_request_note`, `decision_note`, `decided_at` and `minted_determination_id` all null. Filing drives the determination onto `APPEALED` and increments its `version` by one.

Three filing gates apply. The determination must be appealable: filing against one whose `placement_state` is already `APPEALED`, or is `SUPERSEDED`, is refused with `ERR_PLACEMENT_NOT_ACTIONABLE`. The filing window is thirty days from the determination: once the logical clock reaches `determined_at` advanced by thirty days, filing is refused with `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_CLOSED`. And `grounds` of `EL_PATHWAY` is available only to a student whose `el_status` is `EL_ACTIVE` or `EL_EXITED`; a student who is `NOT_EL` claiming that ground is refused with `ERR_EL_PATHWAY_VIOLATION`. Filing a second appeal against the same determination withdraws the earlier one: an appeal in `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `INFO_REQUESTED` or `INSTRUCTOR_ENDORSED` moves to `WITHDRAWN` with `decided_at` and a `decision_note` recorded. Appeals may be listed and filtered by `determination_id`, `student_id`, `requested_course_id`, `minted_determination_id`, `state` and `grounds`.

A `Registrar` takes ownership, moving `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW`. That requires the appealed determination to actually be in `placement_state` `APPEALED`, and it enforces SoD-2: the reviewer's `X-User-ID` must differ from the determination's `determined_by_user_id`, or the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Ownership is recorded once, in `reviewed_by_user_id`, on the *first* entry into `UNDER_REVIEW`; later re-entries after an information cycle leave it unchanged, so the case does not change hands mid-review.

The review may cycle. The owning reviewer sends the case back with an `info_request_note`, moving `UNDER_REVIEW → INFO_REQUESTED`; only the owner may do so, checked against `reviewed_by_user_id` and refused with `ERR_NOT_APPEAL_REVIEWER`, and only while `revision_round` is still below two, refused with `ERR_APPEAL_ROUND_LIMIT` once two rounds have already been spent. Because the ownership check sits at a higher priority band than the round cap, a non-owner asking for information on an appeal that has already exhausted its rounds is told they are not the reviewer, not that the rounds are gone. The student then supplies a replacement `statement`, moving `INFO_REQUESTED → UNDER_REVIEW`, which increments `revision_round` by one and clears `info_request_note`. A student doing this themselves must match `X-User-ID` to the appeal's `student_id` or be refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`; a `Registrar` may resubmit on the student's behalf, in which case that ownership check does not apply, but the round counter still increments and the resubmission window still binds. That window is fourteen days from filing: once the logical clock reaches `filed_at` advanced by fourteen days, resubmission is refused with `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_CLOSED`. Note that the resubmission clock runs from `filed_at` and is *not* restarted by an information request, so two full rounds must fit inside the same fourteen days.

A grant needs an instructor's professional judgement first. The `Instructor` of the requested course endorses, moving `UNDER_REVIEW → INSTRUCTOR_ENDORSED` with an optional `decision_note`, which records `endorsed_by_user_id`. The course named by `requested_course_id` must have `status` `ACTIVE`, or the endorsement is refused with `ERR_COURSE_RETIRED`. SoD-3 applies: the endorsing `X-User-ID` must differ from `reviewed_by_user_id`, so the reviewer cannot endorse their own case, and a collision is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

The owning `Registrar` closes the appeal with a `decision` and a `decision_note`. The decider must be the owner, checked against `reviewed_by_user_id` and refused with `ERR_NOT_APPEAL_REVIEWER`, and SoD-2 is re-checked here against the determination's `determined_by_user_id`. A decision of `DENY` is available from `UNDER_REVIEW` and from `INSTRUCTOR_ENDORSED`; it sets `decided_at` and `decision_note`, moves the appeal to `DENIED`, mints nothing, and returns the determination off `APPEALED` by the rule given in §3.6. A decision of `GRANT` is available **only** from `INSTRUCTOR_ENDORSED`; attempting it from any other state, `UNDER_REVIEW` included, is refused with `ERR_APPEAL_NOT_ENDORSED`.

A grant runs the system's second five-hop chain. The first hop sets `decided_at` and `decision_note` and moves the appeal to `GRANTED`. The second hop, caused by the first, moves the appealed determination `APPEALED → SUPERSEDED` and mints a new determination for the same student and subject with `measure_type_used` of `APPEAL_OVERRIDE`, `applied_rule_id` of null — the only circumstance in which a determination has no rule behind it — `placed_course_id` set to the appeal's `requested_course_id`, `coreq_course_id` set to the `requisite_course_id` of the `ACTIVE` `COREQUISITE` requisite edge on the requested course when one exists and null when none does, `source_attempt_id` of null, `placement_state` of `PROVISIONAL`, `determined_by_user_id` set to the deciding `X-User-ID` and `determined_at` set to the logical clock. The override determination bypasses the confidence machinery entirely: `base_confidence_score` and `confidence_score` are both 90.0, `staleness_penalty` is 0.0 regardless of how old the student's transcript is, `validity_days` is 365 with `expires_at` 365 days after `determined_at`, and `evidence_state` is `VERIFIED` regardless of `hs_transcript_status` — the granted appeal *is* the evidence. The appeal's `minted_determination_id` and the old determination's `superseded_by_id` are cross-set so the pair is auditable from either end.

The third hop, caused by the supersession, unwinds enrolments that the old placement authorised and the new one does not: every registration held by that determination's own `student_id`, in state `PENDING`, `ENROLLED` or `WAITLISTED`, whose section belongs to the old determination's `placed_course_id` or `coreq_course_id` **and does not** belong to the new determination's `placed_course_id` or `coreq_course_id` moves to `DENIED` with `denial_reason` set to `PLACEMENT_SUPERSEDED`, and the corresponding section seat counters are decremented. A course that appears on both the old and the new placement keeps its registrations untouched. The fourth hop, caused by the third, denies each denied row's `coreq_registration_id` counterpart when that counterpart is in state `PENDING`, `ENROLLED` or `WAITLISTED`, with the same `denial_reason`, and returns every override in state `CONSUMED` whose `consumed_by_registration_id` is in the denied set to `ISSUED` with `consumed_by_registration_id` reset to null. The fifth hop, caused by the seat releases in the third and fourth, promotes `WAITLISTED` registrations to `ENROLLED` in `created_at` ascending order on every section that released a seat, while `available_seats` remains at least one. The student's `placement_status` and `advising_hold` recompute and the student's `version` increments by one.

An appeal may also be withdrawn from `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `INFO_REQUESTED` or `INSTRUCTOR_ENDORSED`, by the student — matching `X-User-ID` to `student_id`, else `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` — or by a `Registrar`. Withdrawal sets `decided_at`, records a `decision_note`, moves the appeal to `WITHDRAWN`, and releases the determination from `APPEALED`. `GRANTED`, `DENIED` and `WITHDRAWN` are all terminal.

### 3.8. Section Planning and Corequisite Pairing

A `Registrar` or `SystemAdmin` plans a section by naming its `course_id`, a `term_code` of 5 to 10 characters, the `room_code` it meets in, the inclusive `meets_start_at` and exclusive `meets_end_at` of its meeting block, and a `seat_capacity` between 1 and 300. `meets_end_at` must be strictly after `meets_start_at`. Planning against a course whose `status` is `RETIRED` is refused with `ERR_COURSE_RETIRED`. The new section opens with `status` `PLANNED`, `pairing_state` `UNPAIRED`, `role_in_pair` `NONE`, `linked_section_id` null, and `seats_taken`, `seats_held` and `waitlist_count` all zero. Sections may be listed and filtered by `course_id`, `term_code`, `room_code`, `status`, `pairing_state`, `role_in_pair` and `linked_section_id`.

The three seat counters are derived from the section's registrations: `seats_taken` counts those in state `ENROLLED` or `COMPLETED`, `seats_held` counts those in state `PENDING`, and `waitlist_count` counts those in state `WAITLISTED`. A graded registration still occupies its seat — completing a course does not free it.

`available_seats` is the figure the registration gate actually reads, and it is computed by one of **two variants**, chosen by the section's `pairing_state`. For a section that is `UNPAIRED` or `PAIR_BROKEN`, the *committed-only* variant applies: `available_seats` is `seat_capacity` less `seats_taken`, floored at zero. Pending holds are deliberately ignored, because an unpaired section can afford to oversubscribe its holds. For a section that is `PAIRED`, the *paired-conservative* variant applies to **both** halves: for each of the two sections compute `seat_capacity` less `seats_taken` less `seats_held`, take the smaller of the two results, and floor it at zero. Holds now count against supply on both sides, because a seat confirmed in the primary section obliges a seat in the support section, and the office will not promise a corequisite pair it cannot deliver. The practical consequence is that the *same* section reports a different `available_seats` before and after pairing, with no registration having changed. One refinement applies whichever variant is in force: when `available_seats` is being evaluated as the gate for confirming a specific held registration, that registration's own `PENDING` hold is removed from `seats_held` before the arithmetic, so a row never blocks itself. A plain read of a section never applies that exclusion.

Pairing is a `Registrar` action naming a `support_section_id` in the body; the section in the path becomes the `PRIMARY` half and the section named in the body becomes the `SUPPORT` half. Pairing runs from `UNPAIRED → PAIRED`, and also from `PAIR_BROKEN → PAIRED` when a broken pair is re-formed against a different support section — a genuine loop, since a section whose partner was cancelled can be re-paired to another. Both the primary and the support section must have `status` `PLANNED`; otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The pairing integrity check requires that the primary section's course have `is_support_course` false, that the support section's course have `is_support_course` true, that the two sections share the same `term_code`, that the support section's `pairing_state` be `UNPAIRED`, and that an `ACTIVE` requisite edge of `requisite_type` `COREQUISITE` join the primary course to the support course; any failure is refused with `ERR_COREQ_SECTION_MISMATCH`. Only after that check passes is the meeting geometry examined: the two meeting blocks must not overlap under half-open, **zero-buffer** semantics, meaning they conflict exactly when the primary's `meets_start_at` falls strictly before the support's `meets_end_at` and the support's `meets_start_at` falls strictly before the primary's `meets_end_at`. Exact adjacency — one block ending at the very instant the other begins — is allowed, since a student can walk between rooms; unlike test sessions, corequisite meetings get no turnaround buffer at all. A conflict is refused with `ERR_COREQ_TIME_CONFLICT`. On success both sections set `pairing_state` to `PAIRED`, cross-set `linked_section_id` at each other, set `role_in_pair` to `PRIMARY` and `SUPPORT` respectively, and recompute `available_seats` under the paired-conservative variant. Re-pairing a `PAIR_BROKEN` section repoints `linked_section_id` at the new support section.

Unpairing is the deliberate `Registrar` reversal, `PAIRED → UNPAIRED`. Only the `PRIMARY` half may dissolve a pair — an attempt from the `SUPPORT` half is refused with `ERR_COREQ_SECTION_MISMATCH` — and the section's `status` must be `PLANNED` or `OPEN`, else `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. No registration on the linked support section may still hold a non-null `coreq_registration_id` while in state `PENDING`, `ENROLLED` or `WAITLISTED`; those companion rows must be dropped first, and an attempt to unpair over them is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Unpairing sets both sections' `pairing_state` to `UNPAIRED`, `role_in_pair` to `NONE` and `linked_section_id` to null, increments both sections' `version` by one, and reverts both to the committed-only variant, which stops pending holds from suppressing supply and can make `available_seats` jump upward with no registration having changed.

`PAIR_BROKEN` is the involuntary counterpart, reached when the linked section is cancelled. Both halves move `PAIRED → PAIR_BROKEN`, and each **retains** its `linked_section_id` so the broken pair stays auditable and re-pairable — the contrast with a deliberate unpairing, which clears the link, is intentional. `available_seats` reverts to the committed-only variant on both halves.

The section's `status` axis is driven by a `target_status`. `PLANNED → OPEN` requires the section's course to have `status` `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_COURSE_RETIRED`; and it requires corequisite readiness: when the section's course has `is_support_course` false and at least one `ACTIVE` placement rule targets that course with `coreq_required` true, the section's `pairing_state` must already be `PAIRED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_COREQ_PAIRING_REQUIRED`. A support section, or a section of a course no coreq-requiring rule targets, opens freely. `OPEN → CLOSED` transitions no registrations at all: the section simply stops accepting new holds, and anyone attempting one thereafter is refused with `ERR_SECTION_NOT_OPEN`, while its `ENROLLED` rows remain gradeable. `CLOSED → OPEN` is the cyclic return edge, reopening a closed section for late registration; it requires the section to actually be `CLOSED`, else `ERR_SECTION_NOT_CLOSED`.

Cancelling a section is available from `PLANNED`, from `OPEN` and from `CLOSED`, and the chain is identical in each case. The first hop moves every registration on the section in state `PENDING`, `ENROLLED` or `WAITLISTED` to `DENIED` with `denial_reason` set to `SECTION_CANCELLED`, zeroing the section's seat counters, and, when the section's `pairing_state` is `PAIRED`, moves **both** this section and its linked section `PAIRED → PAIR_BROKEN` with `linked_section_id` retained on each. The second hop, caused by that break, forces a linked section whose `role_in_pair` is `PRIMARY` and whose `status` is `OPEN` to move `OPEN → CLOSED`, since its corequisite support is gone and it must take no further corequisite-bearing registrations. The third hop, caused by the first, moves each denied registration's `coreq_registration_id` counterpart in state `PENDING`, `ENROLLED` or `WAITLISTED` to `DENIED` with the same `denial_reason`, and returns every override in state `CONSUMED` whose `consumed_by_registration_id` is in the denied set to `ISSUED`. `CLOSED` and `CANCELLED` are both terminal on the status axis. A section's registrations may be read as a scoped collection, filtered by `state` and returned in `created_at` ascending order — the same order the waitlist is promoted in.

### 3.9. Registration, Prerequisite Enforcement, Grading and Equity Review

Registration is deliberately two-step: a hold, then a confirmation that runs the full enforcement stack. A `Registrar` or the student holds a seat by naming the `student_id`, the `section_id`, the `expected_section_version` last read, and optionally an `override_id` presenting a prerequisite waiver. A version mismatch is refused with `ERR_STALE_SECTION_VERSION`. The section's `status` must be `OPEN`, else `ERR_SECTION_NOT_OPEN`. A student who already holds a registration for that same section in state `PENDING`, `ENROLLED`, `WAITLISTED` or `COMPLETED` is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_REGISTRATION` — a `DROPPED` or `DENIED` row does not block a fresh attempt. A presented `override_id` naming an override whose `state` is `CONSUMED`, `REVOKED` or `EXPIRED` is refused with `ERR_OVERRIDE_CONSUMED`, and one whose `expires_at` is at or before the logical clock is refused with `ERR_OVERRIDE_EXPIRED`. The hold is created in `state` `PENDING`, increments the section's `seats_held` by one, and computes `prerequisite_status`. Registrations may be listed and filtered by `student_id`, `section_id`, `coreq_registration_id`, `override_id`, `state`, `term_code` and `final_grade`.

**Prerequisite satisfaction** looks only at *direct* edges. For the section's course, gather every requisite edge whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, whose `requisite_type` is `PREREQUISITE` and whose `enforcement` is `BLOCKING`; advisory edges, corequisite edges, warning-only edges and retired edges are all ignored, and no transitive closure is walked — a prerequisite of a prerequisite is not this student's problem. If that set is empty, `prerequisite_status` is `SATISFIED`. Otherwise each edge is tested: the student satisfies it when they hold a registration in state `COMPLETED` for a section of the edge's `requisite_course_id` whose `final_grade` ranks at or above the edge's `min_grade`. Grades rank as follows: `A` is worth 4, `B` is worth 3, `C` and `P` are each worth 2, `D` is worth 1, and `F`, `NP`, `W` and a null grade are worth 0 — so a passing `P` is exactly equivalent to a `C`, and `NP`, `W` and an ungraded row never satisfy anything. An unsatisfied edge may still be waived by a valid override: one whose `student_id`, `course_id` and `requisite_id` all match the student, the section's course and that edge, whose `state` is `ISSUED` or `CONSUMED`, and whose `expires_at` is strictly after the logical clock. `prerequisite_status` is then `UNMET` if any edge is unsatisfied and unwaived; otherwise `OVERRIDDEN` if any edge was unsatisfied but waived; otherwise `SATISFIED`. The status is recomputed whenever the graph or the waivers underneath it change — when an edge is created, when an edge is retired, when an override is revoked or expires.

**Confirming** the hold is a `Registrar` action and is where every gate lands. The student must hold a governing determination for the subject area of the section's course whose `placement_state` is `PROVISIONAL` or `FINAL` and whose `evidence_state` is not `DISPUTED`, or the confirmation is refused with `ERR_PLACEMENT_NOT_ACTIONABLE`. The section's course must be that determination's `placed_course_id` or its `coreq_course_id`, or the confirmation is refused with `ERR_PLACEMENT_COURSE_MISMATCH`. `prerequisite_status` must be `SATISFIED` or `OVERRIDDEN`, and a status of `UNMET` is refused with `ERR_PREREQUISITE_NOT_MET`. The section's `status` must still be `OPEN`, else `ERR_SECTION_NOT_OPEN`.

The **term unit load** is then checked. Sum the `units` of the courses behind the student's *other* registrations in the same `term_code` whose `state` is `ENROLLED`, `WAITLISTED` or `COMPLETED` — a waitlisted row counts against the cap even though it holds no seat, because it may be promoted at any moment, while `PENDING`, `DROPPED` and `DENIED` rows do not count. Add the `units` of the course being confirmed. Then, when the section's `pairing_state` is `PAIRED` and its `role_in_pair` is `PRIMARY`, add the `units` of the linked support section's course as well, because confirming the primary automatically enrols the companion. Round the total to one decimal place. That total must not exceed eighteen units; a total of exactly eighteen is allowed, and only a total above eighteen is refused with `ERR_TERM_UNIT_CAP_EXCEEDED`. The accepted total is stored on the registration as `term_units_at_confirm`, which stays null until confirmation.

Finally, seat availability decides which branch the confirmation takes. When `available_seats` for the applicable variant is at least one, the registration moves `PENDING → ENROLLED`: the section's `seats_held` decreases by one and `seats_taken` increases by one, the section's `version` increments by one, `term_units_at_confirm` is written, any presented override moves `ISSUED → CONSUMED` with `consumed_by_registration_id` set to this registration, and, when the section is `PAIRED` with `role_in_pair` `PRIMARY`, a companion registration is created on the linked support section **directly in state `ENROLLED`**, cross-linked through `coreq_registration_id` on both rows and consuming one support seat. When `available_seats` is zero and the section's `waitlist_count` is below ten, the registration instead moves `PENDING → WAITLISTED`: `waitlist_count` increases by one and `seats_held` decreases by one, no seat is consumed, **no companion registration is created** — the corequisite pair is materialised only at `ENROLLED` — and any presented override stays `ISSUED` rather than being consumed, because a waiver is redeemed only against an actual seat. When `available_seats` is zero and `waitlist_count` has already reached ten, the confirmation is refused with `ERR_SECTION_FULL`. Every placement, prerequisite and unit-cap gate above applies identically to the waitlisted branch; only the seat arithmetic differs.

A waitlisted row is promoted by the system, never by request. Whenever a seat is released on a section, the earliest `WAITLISTED` registration by `created_at` ascending is promoted `WAITLISTED → ENROLLED` provided the section's `status` is `OPEN` and `available_seats` is at least one, and promotion repeats while seats remain. A promotion carries exactly the same side effects as a confirmation into `ENROLLED`, including override consumption and companion-registration creation.

**Dropping** is the compensating action. A student may drop their own `PENDING` or `WAITLISTED` hold, matching `X-User-ID` to the registration's `student_id` or being refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`; dropping from `PENDING` decrements `seats_held` and dropping from `WAITLISTED` decrements `waitlist_count`. Dropping an `ENROLLED` registration is a `Registrar` action and requires the section's `status` to be `OPEN` or `CLOSED`, else `ERR_SECTION_NOT_OPEN`. It runs in three hops: the first decrements the section's `seats_taken`, increments the section's `version` by one, and, when the registration presented an `override_id` whose override is in state `CONSUMED`, returns that override `CONSUMED → ISSUED` with `consumed_by_registration_id` cleared, restoring the redemption balance to exactly its pre-confirmation value; the second, caused by the first, moves the `coreq_registration_id` counterpart in state `PENDING`, `ENROLLED` or `WAITLISTED` to `DROPPED` and releases its seat too; and the third, caused by the seat releases in the first two, promotes waitlists on both affected sections in `created_at` ascending order while `available_seats` remains at least one.

**Grading** closes the loop. The `Instructor` posts a `final_grade` from `A`, `B`, `C`, `D`, `F`, `P`, `NP` or `W`, moving the registration `ENROLLED → COMPLETED`. The section's `status` must be `CLOSED`, else `ERR_SECTION_NOT_CLOSED`, and the logical clock must be at or after the section's `meets_end_at` — a course cannot be graded before it has finished meeting. Grading sets `graded_at` to the logical clock and derives `success` as true exactly when `final_grade` is `A`, `B`, `C` or `P`. It does **not** release the seat: a `COMPLETED` registration still counts in `seats_taken`. One hop follows: the course's `enrolled_count`, `success_count` and `success_rate` are recomputed and the course's `version` increments by one. A graded row also becomes eligible for the validation cohorts of §3.4 and for the equity report below. `DENIED`, `DROPPED` and `COMPLETED` are the terminal registration states, and a row lands on `DENIED` only by cascade, with `denial_reason` recording which cascade did it: `EVIDENCE_DISPUTED`, `PLACEMENT_SUPERSEDED` or `SECTION_CANCELLED`.

**Prerequisite overrides** are the escape valve. An `Instructor` or `Registrar` issues one naming the `student_id`, the `course_id` whose requirement is being waived, the `requisite_id` of the specific edge, a `justification` of 10 to 1000 characters, and an exclusive `expires_at`. The named edge must belong to the body's `course_id`, must have `requisite_type` `PREREQUISITE` and must have `enforcement` `BLOCKING` — a waiver of an advisory or warning-only edge is meaningless and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and the course must not be `RETIRED`, else `ERR_COURSE_RETIRED`. The override opens in `state` `ISSUED` with `issued_by_user_id` set to the issuing `X-User-ID` and `consumed_by_registration_id` null. It may be listed and filtered by `student_id`, `course_id`, `requisite_id`, `consumed_by_registration_id`, `state` and `issued_by_user_id`.

An override's life is a redemption loop: `ISSUED → CONSUMED` when a registration presenting it reaches `ENROLLED`, and `CONSUMED → ISSUED` when that registration is dropped or denied. Consumption requires the logical clock to be strictly before `expires_at`. Revocation, `ISSUED → REVOKED`, is available to an `Instructor` — but only for an override they themselves issued, checked against `issued_by_user_id` and refused with `ERR_NOT_OVERRIDE_ISSUER` — or to a `Registrar`, who may revoke any override. A `CONSUMED` override cannot be revoked: the consuming registration must be dropped first, which returns the override to `ISSUED`, and a direct attempt is refused with `ERR_OVERRIDE_CONSUMED`. Revocation recomputes `prerequisite_status` on every `PENDING` or `WAITLISTED` registration that presented the override, typically turning `OVERRIDDEN` into `UNMET`. The same revocation and recomputation happen as a cascade when the underlying requisite edge is retired. `EXPIRED` is reached only by the clock sweep of §2.5, and both `REVOKED` and `EXPIRED` are terminal.

The **equity report** is the office's accountability instrument, read for one course over a closed observation window. It takes a `disaggregation_key`, an inclusive `window_start_at` and an exclusive `window_end_at`. The `disaggregation_key` selects which student attribute forms the groups: `RACE_ETHNICITY` groups on `race_ethnicity_code`, `EL_STATUS` groups on `el_status`, `FIRST_GENERATION` groups on `first_generation` rendered as the strings `TRUE` and `FALSE`, and `PELL` groups on `pell_eligible` rendered the same way. The set of groups is drawn from the **whole student roster**, not from the students the window placed: every student on the roster contributes their rendered key, so the `groups` array carries exactly one entry for each distinct key present across all students — a key whose students hold no qualifying determination for this course appears with `placed` of zero rather than being omitted — and `group_count` is simply the number of entries in `groups`. A roster that renders only one key therefore yields one group, and an empty roster yields none.

For each group key, `placed` is the number of *distinct* students in that group holding at least one determination whose `placed_course_id` is the requested course and whose `determined_at` falls inside the half-open window, at or after `window_start_at` and strictly before `window_end_at`; determinations in every `placement_state` count, superseded ones included, because the report asks who was ever directed into this course. `through` is the number of *those same* students holding at least one registration whose `state` is `COMPLETED`, whose section belongs to the requested course, whose `final_grade` is `A`, `B`, `C` or `P`, and whose `graded_at` falls strictly before `window_end_at` advanced by two terms of 120 days each, that is, 240 days — a student is given two terms past the window's close to get through the course. `rate` is null when `placed` is zero, and otherwise `through` divided by `placed` rounded to four decimal places.

A group is *scored* when its `placed` is at least one. `throughput_gap` is null when fewer than two groups are scored, since a gap needs two sides; otherwise it is the greatest `rate` among scored groups less the smallest `rate` among scored groups, rounded to four decimal places. The response echoes `course_id`, `disaggregation_key`, `window_start_at` and `window_end_at`, reports `group_count` and the `groups` array sorted by `group_key` ascending in lexicographic order on the rendered key, each group carrying its `group_key`, `placed`, `through` and `rate`, and reports `throughput_gap` together with `min_group_key` and `max_group_key`, which name the scored groups attaining the smallest and greatest `rate` respectively, tie-broken by the lexicographically earliest key, and are null exactly when `throughput_gap` is null.
