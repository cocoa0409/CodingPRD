# Neighborhood Swim Club API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

For fifty years the club has run its membership book, its swim team and its pool deck out of three-ring binders, and every spring the same three failures repeat. A household is offered a membership slot out of turn because nobody could reconstruct the waitlist order; a guest is waved through the gate on a free pass that had already been spent; and a swim meet is hosted with fewer volunteers on the deck than the roster promised, with no penalty ever collected. The board has asked for a single system of record that makes each of those outcomes impossible by construction rather than by diligence.

This document specifies the Neighborhood Swim Club API, mounted under `/swim-club`. It covers the whole operational surface of a community swim club: membership categories with their refundable bonds and one-time initiation fees, a household-level waitlist with priority tiers, seasonal dues with two competing proration bases, guest admission with a free-pass ledger and a cash guest-fee ledger, lifeguard hiring and certification tracking, deck staffing measured against a bather-load ratio, swim-team registration with squad capacity and multi-swimmer discounts, meet hosting with volunteer credit obligations and buyouts, private party reservations priced per facility zone, health-department inspection findings with a closure protocol, a capital reserve for pool resurfacing and pump replacement, and conduct enforcement up to expulsion.

Two constraints shape every rule that follows. First, the club operates a public-health-regulated facility: when the health department cites a critical violation the pool shuts, and no fiscal, scheduling or convenience consideration may override that. Second, the club is a non-profit that must be able to reconcile its books to the cent at the end of every season — so every money figure is carried as an integer number of cents, every derived counter is defined as an exact function of the rows that feed it, and the order in which the arithmetic is performed is part of the specification rather than an implementation detail.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the API must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the caller, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which the caller is acting. If either header is absent or empty, or if `X-User-Role` names a role outside the enumeration below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). A header that is present but holds only whitespace counts as empty for that test, and surrounding whitespace is trimmed from both values before either is used, so a `X-User-Role` sent as `" PoolManager "` authenticates as `PoolManager`. If both headers are well formed but the named role is not one of the roles authorised for the endpoint being called, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The system recognises exactly eight roles: `Member`, `MembershipChair`, `PoolManager`, `SwimTeamCoach`, `HealthInspector`, `Treasurer`, `BoardPresident`, and `SystemOperator`. Their remits are as follows. A `Member` is a member household acting for itself — requesting waitlist entries, paying dues, recording its own guests, registering swimmers, booking parties, working volunteer jobs and appealing conduct sanctions. The `MembershipChair` runs the membership book: categories, households, the waitlist and membership creation. The `PoolManager` runs the facility day to day: opening and closing the season, hiring and certifying lifeguards, scheduling shifts, admitting guests at the gate, approving parties and reporting conduct incidents. The `SwimTeamCoach` runs the swim team: registrations, squads, meet scheduling and volunteer rostering. The `HealthInspector` is the health department's officer: the only role that may record an inspection finding, and the only role that may reopen or verify-close one. The `Treasurer` runs the money: invoicing, payments, write-offs, the billing checkpoint, reserve contributions and capital-project funding. The `BoardPresident` is the board's decision maker: creating seasons, deciding conduct cases, moving household standing, and approving, rejecting or cancelling capital projects — and never deciding what it proposed itself. The `SystemOperator` is an operational identity that exists only to drive the server's logical clock for deterministic testing; it carries no business authority of any kind.

Only one role is tied to a persisted entity. When `X-User-Role` is `Member`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Household`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Household` compare the resource's household reference to `X-User-ID`. That comparison is direct for a `Household` or a `WaitlistEntry` (whose `household_id` is compared), it runs through the addressed membership's `household_id` for a `Membership`, `GuestVisit`, `SwimTeamRegistration` or `PartyReservation`, and it runs through the case's `household_id` for a `ConductCase`. A `Member` that addresses a resource owned by a different household is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403).

When `X-User-Role` is `MembershipChair`, `PoolManager`, `SwimTeamCoach`, `HealthInspector`, `Treasurer`, `BoardPresident` or `SystemOperator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — as `recorded_by_user_id` and `verified_by_user_id` on an `InspectionFinding`, as `reported_by_user_id` and `decided_by_user_id` on a `ConductCase`, and as `requested_by_user_id` and `approved_by_user_id` on a `CapitalProject` — but it never compares such an identifier to any entity's `id`. Those recorded identifiers exist to support the two-person controls described in §2.8.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`: that prefix followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the uppercase letters `A`–`Z` and the digits `0`–`9`, minted afresh for every response including every error response.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. It is read from the logical clock of §2.5 rather than from wall-clock time, so every response produced without an intervening clock move carries the same value.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters, `limit` and `offset`. `limit` defaults to 10 when omitted, must be an integer, and must lie between a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100 inclusive. `offset` defaults to 0 when omitted, must be an integer, and must be at least 0. A `limit` outside that range, or a negative `offset`, or a non-integer value for either, is rejected with `ERR_PAGINATION_INVALID` (HTTP 422).

List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending unless a specific narrative in §3 pins a different order for a particular computation. Because the logical clock of §2.5 advances only when a caller moves it, rows created at the same clock position share an identical `created_at`; such ties are broken by the order in which the rows were inserted, so the ordering is always total and never falls back on `id`. Four pagination fields appear in the `meta` object of every list response: `limit` (the effective page size), `offset` (the effective starting position), `total_count` (the number of rows matching the request's filters before the page window is applied) and `has_more` (true when rows remain beyond the current page). Single-resource reads carry neither of those four fields.

Filters supplied to a list endpoint narrow the matched set before pagination is applied, and filters combine conjunctively — a request that supplies both a status filter and a season filter returns only rows matching both. A filter value is compared exactly and case-sensitively against the stored field, and a value that cannot be interpreted is never a validation error: an unrecognised enum or identifier value simply matches no rows, a non-integer value for an integer filter such as `year` matches no rows, and a boolean filter reads `true`, `1` and `yes` as true and `false`, `0` and `no` as false while any other spelling matches no rows. Filters are also the only narrowing a list endpoint performs — a list read is never scoped to the caller's identity, so a `Member` authorised for a list endpoint sees every matching row and must filter on its own `household_id` to see only its own.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps, Versioning and Cascade Propagation

Every persisted resource carries a server-minted `id`, which is a UUID string with no type prefix of any kind, and a `created_at` timestamp recorded as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with exactly three fractional digits. Client-supplied identifiers are never accepted as an `id`.

That three-digit rendering is the system's only instant format, and it applies to every instant-valued field the API returns — `created_at` and `updated_at`, every server-stamped business instant (`invoiced_at`, `offered_at`, `offer_expires_at`, `requeued_at`, `queue_key_at`, `sanctioned_at`, `roster_closed_at`, `voided_at`, `closure_started_at`, `remediation_submitted_at`) and every instant echoed back from a client-supplied input (`visit_start_iso`, a shift's, a party's or a meet's `start_iso` and `end_iso`, the `at_iso` of the staffing-window read). Whatever UTC offset or fractional width the request wrote, the value is normalised to UTC, its sub-millisecond precision is dropped, and it is stored and returned with exactly three fractional digits and a trailing `Z`; comparisons against it read that normalised value.

Every entity in this system additionally carries `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits refreshed on every mutation of that row, and `version`, an integer that begins at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. There are no exceptions: `Season`, `MembershipCategory`, `Household`, `WaitlistEntry`, `Membership`, `GuestVisit`, `Lifeguard`, `Certification`, `GuardShift`, `SwimTeamRegistration`, `Meet`, `VolunteerAssignment`, `PartyReservation`, `InspectionFinding`, `ConductCase` and `CapitalProject` all expose `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` and `version`.

The club's data is a graph rather than a tree, and a single request routinely mutates several rows at once. The governing principle is that **every row the request actually changes has its own `version` incremented and its own `updated_at` refreshed** — not merely the row named in the URL. When admitting a guest changes the sponsoring household's pass balance, the household's `version` moves as well as the visit's. When drawing capital funds changes a season's unallocated reserve, the season's `version` moves as well as the project's. When a conduct sanction suspends a household, and that suspension revokes swim-team registrations, and those revocations release volunteer assignments, and those releases lower a meet's filled-slot count, every one of those rows advances its own `version`. Rows that a cascade inspects but does not change do not advance. A row that one request changes more than once — reached twice by the same cascade, or changed by the endpoint and then again by a cascade beneath it — advances its `version` by exactly 1 for that request rather than once per hop; the counter measures mutating requests, not writes.

Cascades are executed depth-first and to completion inside the originating request; there is no deferred or eventual processing. Where a cascade touches a set of sibling rows, the processing order is pinned in the §3 narrative that describes it, and that order is always a deterministic ordering on business columns — never on `id`.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

Every deadline, expiry, season window, lookback and "is this instant in the past" comparison in this specification reads a single server-side logical UTC clock rather than wall-clock time. The clock's startup value is exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`: before any caller has moved it, the logical clock reads that instant, and it must never fall back to real wall-clock time. That default sits at or before every date the club's fixtures use, so an unset clock is a fixed, well-defined earliest instant.

A `SystemOperator` may move the clock by calling `POST /swim-club/_clock/set_current_utc` with a single field `to_iso8601`. From that call onwards, every subsequent request reads the supplied instant as "now". Setting the clock mutates no entity and recomputes no derived field by itself; it changes only what later requests observe as the current instant. The clock may be moved backwards as freely as forwards, and to the same instant repeatedly: no monotonicity is enforced, and the supplied `to_iso8601` is normalised exactly as §2.4 describes before it becomes "now". The endpoint returns HTTP 200 with an empty data object.

Two vocabularies are used throughout §3 and the distinction matters. "The logical clock" means the full instant. "The logical clock's date" means the calendar date of that instant in UTC, and it is the value compared against date-typed fields such as a season's `open_date`, `close_date` and `dues_due_date`, or a certification's `expires_on`.

### 2.6. Fiscal Precision and Rounding Discipline

All money in this system is an integer number of cents. There are no fractional-cent fields, no floating-point money in responses, and no currency codes. Volunteer credits, guest passes, conduct points, guard counts and bather headcounts are likewise integers.

Wherever a monetary rule multiplies by a rate, the result is rounded **half up** to a whole number of cents — a value whose fractional part is exactly one half rounds away from zero, to the larger magnitude. Two other rounding operators appear and are never interchangeable with rounding half up: **rounding up to the next whole number** (used for guard counts derived from a bather ratio, for billable duration in whole hours, and for swim weeks) and plain integer truncation, which is used only where the text says so explicitly.

The **order** in which rounding is applied is part of the specification. Where §3 describes a figure as being assembled from a list of lines, each line is rounded to whole cents as it is produced and later lines are computed from the sum of the already-rounded lines. Summing exact unrounded values and rounding once at the end is a different and incorrect answer, and the difference is observable to the cent.

Durations expressed in hours as a decimal — a guard shift's worked length, for example — are rounded half up to two decimal places.

### 2.7. Stale-Version Guards on Ledger-Bearing Writes

Two write operations depend on a counter that another writer may have moved since the client last read it, and both therefore require the client to declare the version it believes it is working from. This is optimistic concurrency control on the parent row's `version`; no other concurrency mechanism exists in this system, and no other endpoint accepts a version field.

`POST /swim-club/guest-visits` requires the non-optional field `expected_household_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the sponsoring household. The household is reached through the `membership_id` on the request. If `expected_household_version` does not equal that household's current `version`, the visit is not recorded and the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_HOUSEHOLD_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Recording several visits for the same household in sequence is entirely legitimate, so no state precondition blocks a repeated request; the stale-version check is the only thing standing between a client working from an out-of-date free-pass balance and a corrupted guest-fee ledger, because whether a visit is settled with passes or with cash depends on that balance.

`POST /swim-club/capital-projects/{id}/fund` requires the non-optional field `expected_capital_project_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the addressed project. If it does not equal the project's current `version`, no draw is made and the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PROJECT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Successive draws against the same project are legitimate and are not blocked by any state precondition, so this check is what prevents a client working from an out-of-date `funded_cents` figure from over-drawing the season's reserve.

### 2.8. Two-Person Control

Three decisions in this system are subject to segregation of duties: the same human may not both raise an item and decide it. Approving or rejecting a capital project requires that `X-User-ID` differ from the project's `requested_by_user_id`. Sanctioning or dismissing a conduct case requires that `X-User-ID` differ from the case's `reported_by_user_id`. Verify-closing an inspection finding requires that `X-User-ID` differ from the finding's `recorded_by_user_id`. A violation of any of the three is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403), and the check is an authorisation concern evaluated before any state or business rule on the same request.

### 2.9. The Rejection Ladder

Most write operations in this system are guarded by several rules at once, and which error a caller sees when more than one rule is broken is part of the contract. The club operates a regulated facility, so the ladder is ordered on the principle that a closed pool outranks an unpaid bill and an uncertified guard outranks a scheduling inconvenience: no fiscal complaint may be reported in place of a compliance failure. Checks are evaluated strictly in the following order, and the first one that fails determines the response.

1. **Authentication.** Both headers present and non-empty, and `X-User-Role` naming a declared role — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. **Endpoint authorisation.** The acting role appears in the endpoint's authorised list — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403).
3. **Resource ownership.** For a `Member` addressing an owned resource, `X-User-ID` matches the owning household — otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). Where the request itself names the household — a `{id}` path segment on a household route, or a `household_id` in the payload — the comparison is made against that value before anything is looked up, so a `Member` naming another household's id is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` whether or not that id exists. Where ownership can only be read from the addressed row — a membership, guest visit, waitlist entry, registration, reservation or conduct case — a row that does not exist leaves nothing to compare against, the ownership check is skipped, and the request falls through to the existence check of band 9 and its 404. The comparison also waits on the payload wherever it must: where the resource carrying the ownership is named in the body rather than in the path, or where a shared transitions endpoint has to read the requested `to_status` before it can tell which roles the edge belongs to, bands 5, 6 and 7 are settled over the whole payload first and the ownership comparison follows them.
4. **Two-person control**, on capital-project transitions, conduct-case transitions and inspection-finding transitions — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
5. **Payload shape.** Every required field present, of the declared JSON type and non-empty; money and count fields integers of at least 0; date fields formatted `YYYY-MM-DD` and instant fields formatted as ISO 8601 UTC — otherwise `ERR_VALIDATION` (422). A body that is absent, empty or unparseable as JSON is treated as an empty object, so its required fields are simply missing; a body that parses to anything other than a JSON object is itself `ERR_VALIDATION`. A field sent explicitly as `null` is treated exactly as an omitted field: required means `ERR_VALIDATION`, optional means the declared default takes effect. A string field holding only whitespace is not non-empty. JSON types are taken literally — a boolean is not an integer, a number is not a string — so a value of the wrong type fails here rather than anywhere lower. A date must be a real calendar date, and an instant must carry a zone designator, either `Z` or a `±HH:MM` offset that is converted to UTC, so an instant written without any zone at all fails this band. Fields the endpoint does not declare are ignored rather than rejected.
6. **Enumerations.** Every enum-valued input holds one of its declared values — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_ENUM` (422). Band 5 is exhausted over the whole payload before band 6 begins on any field, so a payload that sends a number where an enum is declared answers `ERR_VALIDATION`, and `ERR_INVALID_ENUM` appears only once every field's shape is sound. This covers the membership category `code`, a household's `residency`, a waitlist `priority_tier`, a membership's `bond_payment_plan`, a season's `proration_basis` and `guest_settlement_policy`, a waitlist `decision`, the `allocation` on either payment endpoint, a party `zone`, a shift's `shift_role`, a finding's or case's `severity`, a `sanction_type`, a `job_code`, a `squad`, a `cert_type`, a `project_kind`, a `to_standing` and any `to_status`.
7. **Paired time ranges.** Within a single payload, a start is strictly earlier than its paired end — a guard shift's `start_iso` before its `end_iso`, a party's `start_iso` before its `end_iso`, a meet's `start_iso` before its `end_iso`, a season's `open_date` before its `close_date`, a certification's `issued_on` before its `expires_on` — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_TIME_RANGE` (422). Date-typed pairs are compared as calendar dates and instant-typed pairs as instants, but the band is the same one and the identifier is the same one.
8. **Pagination arguments**, on list endpoints — otherwise `ERR_PAGINATION_INVALID` (422).
9. **Existence**, for every resource addressed in the path or referenced by a foreign key in the payload, each with its own code: `ERR_SEASON_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CATEGORY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_HOUSEHOLD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WAITLIST_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MEMBERSHIP_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_GUEST_VISIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LIFEGUARD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CERTIFICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_GUARD_SHIFT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REGISTRATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MEET_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VOLUNTEER_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PARTY_RESERVATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INSPECTION_FINDING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONDUCT_CASE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CAPITAL_PROJECT_NOT_FOUND` — all HTTP 404. Within this band the season is resolved first, then the membership category, then the household, then the waitlist entry, then the membership, then the remaining resources in the order just listed.
10. **Stale-version guards**, as described in §2.7 — `ERR_STALE_HOUSEHOLD_VERSION` then `ERR_STALE_PROJECT_VERSION`, both HTTP 412.
11. **Resource state**, in this internal order: the referenced season is `OPEN` (`ERR_SEASON_NOT_OPEN`, 409); the season's facility posture is not `CLOSED` (`ERR_FACILITY_CLOSED`, 409); a `RESTRICTED` posture's operating envelope is respected (`ERR_FACILITY_RESTRICTED`, 409); the requested `to_status` is a declared outgoing edge from the resource's current status (`ERR_INVALID_TRANSITION`, 409); the resource is not already in a terminal state (`ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`, 409); the acting or referenced household is not suspended or expelled (`ERR_HOUSEHOLD_SUSPENDED`, 409); the referenced membership's access posture is adequate (`ERR_MEMBERSHIP_NOT_IN_GOOD_STANDING`, 409); the meet's roster is still open (`ERR_ROSTER_ALREADY_CLOSED`, 409); the waitlist offer is live (`ERR_OFFER_EXPIRED`, 409); the deferral budget is not exhausted (`ERR_MAX_DEFERRALS_REACHED`, 409). Where one request trips both the transition test and the terminal test at once — a requested move that is not a declared edge out of a status that is itself terminal — the terminal complaint is the one reported: `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` precedes `ERR_INVALID_TRANSITION` everywhere in this system, so a move aimed at an already-resolved row always reads as terminal.
12. **Safety and compliance**, ahead of every scheduling and fiscal check below it: the assigned guard's certification wall (`ERR_CERT_NOT_VALID_FOR_SHIFT`, 422), then the bather-load guard ratio (`ERR_GUARD_RATIO_NOT_MET`, 422).
13. **Temporal windows.** The operation falls inside the referenced season's window (`ERR_OUTSIDE_SEASON_WINDOW`, 422), then a guest visit is not recorded before it happened (`ERR_VISIT_IN_FUTURE`, 422).
14. **Constraint satisfaction**, in this internal order: the guard rest buffer (`ERR_SHIFT_REST_BUFFER_VIOLATION`, 409), zone double-booking (`ERR_ZONE_DOUBLE_BOOKED`, 409), aggregate bather capacity (`ERR_BATHER_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, 409), duplicate membership (`ERR_DUPLICATE_MEMBERSHIP`, 409), duplicate waitlist entry (`ERR_DUPLICATE_WAITLIST_ENTRY`, 409), the per-visit guest limit (`ERR_GUEST_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, 422), the category's household-size cap (`ERR_HOUSEHOLD_SIZE_EXCEEDED`, 422), meet volunteer oversubscription (`ERR_VOLUNTEER_SLOTS_FULL`, 409) and duplicate volunteer assignment (`ERR_DUPLICATE_VOLUNTEER_ASSIGNMENT`, 409).
15. **Referential agreement**, in this internal order: an accepted waitlist entry where the category demands one (`ERR_WAITLIST_OFFER_REQUIRED`, 409), agreement between the requested category and the entry being converted (`ERR_CATEGORY_MISMATCH`, 422), agreement among all season-scoped references in one request (`ERR_SEASON_MISMATCH`, 422), and a non-empty eligible waitlist when an offer is being extended (`ERR_WAITLIST_EMPTY`, 409). The squad-capacity check on confirming a waitlisted swimmer (`ERR_SQUAD_FULL`, 409) is evaluated immediately after these.
16. **Fiscal, last of all**, in this internal order: household delinquency (`ERR_DUES_DELINQUENT`, 402), an unsettled bond before a party may be booked (`ERR_BOND_NOT_PAID`, 402), a payment exceeding the counter it settles (`ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_BALANCE`, 422), a reserve draw exceeding the unallocated reserve (`ERR_INSUFFICIENT_RESERVE`, 402) and a draw exceeding the project's budget (`ERR_DRAW_EXCEEDS_BUDGET`, 422).

### 2.10. Path Resolution and Read Conventions

Nested paths are resolved strictly left to right. The parent segment is resolved before the child segment, so a request for a resource nested under a season that does not exist yields `ERR_SEASON_NOT_FOUND` rather than a not-found for the child, and a request that names an existing parent but a non-existent child yields the child's own not-found code. The same left-to-right discipline applies to foreign keys inside a payload: the season is resolved before the category, the category before the household, and the household before any narrower reference. A path this API does not define at all, and a method the addressed path does not support, are answered with the standard error envelope of §2.2 carrying `ERR_VALIDATION` — with HTTP 404 for the unknown path and HTTP 405 for the unsupported method, the only two places where an identifier is returned with a status other than the one listed against it in §5 — and an unexpected server fault answers HTTP 500 with the same identifier. A trailing slash is insignificant: `/swim-club/seasons/` addresses the same route as `/swim-club/seasons`.

Creating a new resource returns HTTP 201; every other successful operation, including every state transition, every payment, every computed read and the clock-setting call, returns HTTP 200. A successful create or modify returns the created or updated resource in the `data` field, with one exception: the staffing-window read returns a computed object rather than a stored entity, and the clock-setting call returns an empty object.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Season, the Facility Posture and the Health Department

Everything the club does happens inside a **season**. A `BoardPresident` opens the year by creating one, supplying the `year` it covers, the `open_date` and `close_date` that bracket the swimming window, and the `dues_due_date` after which an invoiced-but-unpaid membership becomes delinquent at the next billing checkpoint. The `year` must be an integer between 2026 and 2099 inclusive and must be unique across all seasons — a second season claiming a year already taken is rejected as invalid input with `ERR_VALIDATION`; `close_date` must fall strictly after `open_date`; both dates and the due date are plain calendar dates in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, and `dues_due_date` is not itself required to fall inside the season window. Two headcount figures are recorded at the same time: `baseline_bather_load`, the standing bather headcount the board assumes is present at any open moment before party reservations are added, which defaults to 10 and may not be negative, and `bather_load_capacity`, the hard aggregate ceiling on bathers anywhere in the facility at any instant, which defaults to 120 and must be at least 1. `baseline_bather_load` is recorded for the board's reference and nothing more: it enters neither the ratio computation of §3.8 nor the aggregate capacity comparison of §3.7, both of which count reservation loads alone.

The season also carries the two policy switches that decide which variant of two different money rules the club will use for the whole year. `proration_basis` is either `CALENDAR_DAY` or `SWIM_WEEK` and defaults to `CALENDAR_DAY` when omitted; `guest_settlement_policy` is either `PASS_FIRST` or `FEE_FIRST_ON_WEEKEND` and defaults to `PASS_FIRST` when omitted. Both switches are read from the season, never from the request that triggers the calculation. A newly created season is born with `status` `PLANNED`, `facility_posture` `CLOSED`, `open_critical_finding_count` and `open_major_finding_count` at 0, `closure_started_at` null, and both reserve counters — `reserve_contributions_cents` and `reserve_unallocated_cents` — at 0.

A `PoolManager` opens the season for swimming, moving `status` from `PLANNED` to `OPEN`; the operation is available only while the season is still `PLANNED`. Opening recomputes the finding counters and re-derives the facility posture, so an `OPEN` season with no open findings emerges with `facility_posture` `OPEN`. Season lifecycle runs `PLANNED → OPEN → CLOSED` and `CLOSED` is terminal. An attempt to open a season already `OPEN`, like an attempt to close one still `PLANNED`, is `ERR_INVALID_TRANSITION`, while either operation aimed at a season already `CLOSED` is `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`.

**Facility posture is derived, never set by hand.** It is recomputed after every write to an inspection finding — creation or transition — and on both season-lifecycle moves. The derivation first counts the season's **open findings**, meaning findings whose `status` is `OPEN`, `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED` or `REOPENED`; findings in `VERIFIED_CLOSED` never contribute no matter how severe they were. Among those open findings, `open_critical_finding_count` is the number whose `severity` is `CRITICAL` and `open_major_finding_count` is the number whose `severity` is `MAJOR`. Findings of `severity` `MINOR` are counted in neither and never affect posture at all. The posture is then decided by the first matching case: if the season's own `status` is anything other than `OPEN`, the posture is `CLOSED`, because a season that is not open is never swimmable; otherwise, if at least one critical finding is open, the posture is `CLOSED`; otherwise, if two or more major findings are open, the posture is `RESTRICTED`; otherwise the posture is `OPEN`. Two major findings restrict the facility — the threshold is inclusive.

The moment the derivation lands on `CLOSED` while the season's previous posture was something else, `closure_started_at` is stamped with the logical clock and a two-hop cascade fires: every `CONFIRMED` party reservation in the season whose `end_iso` is still in the future moves to `CANCELLED_BY_CLOSURE`, and every guard shift of the season in `SCHEDULED` or `CONFIRMED` whose `end_iso` is still in the future moves to `CANCELLED_BY_CLOSURE`. Each hop walks its own rows in `created_at` ascending order, and the first hop runs to completion — every cancellation refunded and auto-applied as §3.7 describes — before the second begins; a guard who loses two shifts to the same closure has their `scheduled_hours` recomputed once, after every shift of theirs has been cancelled. Whenever the derivation lands on any posture other than `CLOSED`, `closure_started_at` is set back to null.

A `RESTRICTED` posture does not close the pool; it narrows what may be booked. While the posture is `RESTRICTED`, a guest visit may bring at most 2 guests and a party reservation may not book the `WHOLE_FACILITY` zone. A request that breaks the restricted envelope is rejected with `ERR_FACILITY_RESTRICTED`, and only two operations are subject to it: recording a guest visit and requesting a party reservation. A `CLOSED` posture is stricter and blocks four operations with `ERR_FACILITY_CLOSED`: recording a guest visit, requesting a party reservation, confirming a requested reservation, and hosting a meet. Separately, six creation operations require the referenced season's `status` to be `OPEN` and are rejected with `ERR_SEASON_NOT_OPEN` otherwise: creating a membership, recording a guest visit, scheduling a guard shift, registering a swimmer, scheduling a meet, and requesting a party reservation.

The health department's officer files a finding against the season with `inspected_on` (the date of the visit), `inspector_name`, `finding_code` (the health-code citation, for example `HC-410`), `severity` — one of `CRITICAL`, `MAJOR` or `MINOR` — and a `narrative`. A new finding is born `OPEN`, its `recorded_by_user_id` stamped from `X-User-ID`, its `reopen_count` at 0, its `remediation_note`, `remediation_submitted_at` and `verified_by_user_id` all null. Because the posture derivation runs immediately, filing a critical finding shuts the facility on the same request and drags the closure cascade — the reservation cancellations and the shift cancellations — behind it, refunding each affected household in full as described in §3.7 and crediting the household account, which may in turn auto-settle an open membership balance. That chain is four hops deep and completes inside the filing request.

Remediation is a loop rather than a line. The finding's transitions endpoint is shared between the two offices: the `HealthInspector` and the `PoolManager` both clear its endpoint authorisation, and the edge is then narrowed from the `to_status` the payload names. `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED` belongs to the `PoolManager` alone; every other `to_status`, the reopen and the verify-close included, belongs to the `HealthInspector` alone. That narrowing is decided from the payload before the finding is looked up, so an inspector submitting a remediation, or a manager reopening or verifying one, is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` whether or not the finding exists. The `PoolManager` submits a fix, carrying `to_status` `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED` together with a `remediation_note` that must be present and non-empty; because the note is required on that one edge only, its absence can be judged only once the finding has been resolved, so a remediation naming a finding that does not exist answers `ERR_INSPECTION_FINDING_NOT_FOUND` and never `ERR_VALIDATION`, while the missing note is in turn reported ahead of every state test, so a note-less remediation aimed at a finding already `VERIFIED_CLOSED` is `ERR_VALIDATION` rather than `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`. The note is stored on the finding and `remediation_submitted_at` is stamped with the logical clock. Submission is available from `OPEN` and from `REOPENED` alike, and both edges behave identically. A submitted remediation is still an open finding, so a critical citation keeps the season `CLOSED` while it is under review. The inspector may reject the fix, moving the finding `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED → REOPENED`, which raises `reopen_count` by one and leaves the posture unchanged because the finding is still open. Where the derivation changes nothing, neither does the season: filing a finding, submitting a remediation and reopening one all advance the season's `version` only if the derivation actually moved one of the four values it maintains — `open_critical_finding_count`, `open_major_finding_count`, `facility_posture` or `closure_started_at` — so a `MINOR` citation filed against an open season touches the finding alone, while a `MAJOR` one advances the season's `version` for the counter alone even where the posture stays `OPEN`. Verify-close is the single exception, advancing the season's `version` whether the posture moved or not, because the closing of a citation is itself part of the season's health record. The pool manager may then submit again, and the `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED → REOPENED → REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED` cycle may repeat without limit. Only the inspector may verify-close: moving `REMEDIATION_SUBMITTED → VERIFIED_CLOSED` requires that `X-User-ID` differ from the finding's `recorded_by_user_id` — the two-person control of band 4, settled as soon as the finding resolves and therefore ahead of the terminal and edge tests, so the inspector who filed the citation is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` whatever status the finding currently holds, an already `VERIFIED_CLOSED` one included — stamps `verified_by_user_id` with the acting user, recomputes both finding counters, re-derives the posture — which reopens the facility to `OPEN` or `RESTRICTED` once no critical finding remains open — clears `closure_started_at` when the recomputed posture is not `CLOSED`, and advances the season's `version` as well as the finding's. `VERIFIED_CLOSED` is the finding's only terminal state.

**The billing checkpoint.** Time in this system does not age anything by itself; a `Treasurer` runs an explicit checkpoint against the season to age it, optionally supplying `as_of_date` to age the season against a specific calendar date instead of the logical clock's own date. The checkpoint performs four steps in a fixed order. Step one expires certifications: every certification in `VALID` whose `expires_on` is strictly earlier than the effective date moves to `EXPIRED`, which recomputes its guard's certification flags and can drive that guard inactive. Step two expires stale waitlist offers: every entry in `OFFERED` whose `offer_expires_at` is earlier than the logical clock moves to `EXPIRED`. Step three refills each slot vacated in step two by re-running the offer selector for that entry's `membership_category_id`, so a lapsed offer is immediately passed down the queue; where that category holds no eligible candidate the vacated slot is simply left unfilled and the checkpoint carries on, an empty queue never being an error inside the checkpoint. Step four ages the money: every membership whose `dues_status` is `INVOICED`, whose `balance_due_cents` is greater than 0, and whose season's `dues_due_date` is strictly earlier than the effective date moves `dues_status` to `DELINQUENT` — the due date itself is not delinquent. The checkpoint advances the season's `version` and returns the season.

**Closing the season** is the year's accounting event and the `PoolManager` performs it from `OPEN`. The season's `status` becomes `CLOSED` and `facility_posture` is forced to `CLOSED`. Because the posture derivation runs on this lifecycle move as well, and a season that is not `OPEN` derives `CLOSED`, a season closed while its facility posture was `OPEN` or `RESTRICTED` stamps `closure_started_at` and drags the whole two-hop closure cascade above behind it: the season's future `CONFIRMED` reservations are cancelled as `CANCELLED_BY_CLOSURE` and refunded in full with the auto-apply of §3.4 running, and its future `SCHEDULED` or `CONFIRMED` shifts are cancelled. A season whose facility was already `CLOSED` on an open critical finding repeats neither the stamp nor the cascade. Either way that work completes before the memberships lapse, so a balance those refunds settle is already `PAID` by the time the buyout pass below reaches it. Every membership of the season whose `access_status` is `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` moves to `LAPSED`, processed in `created_at` ascending order. Every swim-team registration of the season in `CONFIRMED` moves to `COMPLETED`, also in `created_at` ascending order. Then, for each membership of the season in `created_at` ascending order — skipping outright any membership whose `dues_status` is `WRITTEN_OFF`, which this pass leaves entirely alone — both the required and the earned volunteer credit counts are recomputed, the volunteer buyout is assessed as described in §3.6 and folded into `balance_due_cents`, which is floored at 0 like every other balance move — and any membership whose recomputed `balance_due_cents` is greater than zero while its `dues_status` was `PAID` moves `dues_status` backwards to `INVOICED`. That backwards edge exists only here: the buyout is assessed only at season close, so a household that had settled in full can legitimately acquire a fresh balance after the fact. On that edge, `invoice_total_cents`, `amount_paid_cents` and `assessed_dues_cents` are **not** re-derived; only the buyout line and the balance move. The season's `version` advances by one.

### 3.2. The Membership Book: Categories, Households, Bonds and the Waitlist Queue

The club sells membership in categories, and the `MembershipChair` defines them. A category is identified by its `code`, which must be unique across categories and must be one of `FAMILY`, `COUPLE`, `SINGLE`, `SENIOR` or `SWIM_TEAM_ONLY` — a second category claiming a code already taken is rejected as invalid input with `ERR_VALIDATION` — and it carries a human-readable `label`. Its money terms are three separate figures, none of which may be negative: `bond_cents`, the refundable capital bond a joining household owes; `initiation_fee_cents`, a one-time non-refundable fee charged only to households that have never been members before; and `seasonal_dues_cents`, the full-season dues before any proration. A category also grants `guest_pass_allotment` free guest passes to the household each time a membership in that category is created, caps the household at `max_household_size` people (at least 1), and levies `volunteer_credits_required` volunteer credits for every confirmed swim-team registration held under a membership in that category.

Three boolean switches complete the category. `waitlist_required`, which defaults to `false` when omitted at creation, makes a membership in that category purchasable only against an accepted waitlist entry. `proration_allowed`, which defaults to `true`, governs whether a mid-season joiner gets a reduced dues assessment: when it is false, the assessment charges the full `seasonal_dues_cents` regardless of the join date. `party_privileges`, which also defaults to `true`, governs whether memberships in the category may reserve the facility for a private party at all; a party request naming a membership whose category has no party privileges is rejected as invalid input with `ERR_VALIDATION`.

A household is the unit of membership, of the waitlist, of the guest-pass ledger and of conduct enforcement. The `MembershipChair` registers one with a `primary_contact_name`, an `address_line`, a `residency` of either `IN_DISTRICT` or `OUT_OF_DISTRICT` recording whether the address lies inside the club's assessment district, and a `member_count` of at least 1 (defaulting to 1) recording how many people the membership will cover. A household is born in `GOOD_STANDING` with `conduct_points` at 0, with `bond_status` `NONE` and `bond_paid_cents` at 0, with all three guest-pass counters — `guest_pass_allotment_granted`, `guest_passes_consumed` and `guest_pass_balance` — at 0, and with `guest_fee_owed_cents`, `party_charges_owed_cents` and `account_credit_cents` all at 0.

Bonds may travel between households, and this is the club's oldest tradition: a departing family's paid bond can be handed to the family moving into the same house. Supplying `transferred_from_household_id` at registration names the household whose paid bond is carried forward; the new household is then born with `bond_status` `TRANSFERRED` rather than `NONE`, and its `bond_paid_cents` copies the source household's figure. A bond transfer also makes the incoming household a returning member for fee purposes. `prior_member` may be supplied directly at registration and otherwise defaults to false; it becomes true when a bond is transferred in, and it becomes true once the household has ever held a membership. Its only effect is to suppress the initiation fee. If `transferred_from_household_id` names a household that does not exist, the registration is rejected with `ERR_HOUSEHOLD_NOT_FOUND`. Households can be listed and filtered by `standing`, `residency` and `bond_status`, and filtering on `transferred_from_household_id` finds every household that inherited a bond from a given one.

Household-level money is settled at the front desk with an `amount_cents` figure and an `allocation` naming which counter is being retired. An `allocation` of `GUEST_FEES` lowers `guest_fee_owed_cents` by the amount paid; `PARTY_CHARGES` lowers `party_charges_owed_cents`; `BOND` raises `bond_paid_cents` and moves `bond_status` from `NONE` or `INSTALLMENTS` to `PAID` once `bond_paid_cents` has reached the `bond_cents` of the category of this household's membership — a `bond_status` of `TRANSFERRED` is never overwritten by a payment. This is the only way guest fees and party charges are ever cleared with money: voiding a visit or refunding a reservation is a reversal, not a payment. It is also, in practice, what a household must do before it may sponsor another guest or book another party, because the club tolerates exactly zero outstanding household charges before those privileges resume. No payment may exceed the counter it settles: for `GUEST_FEES` the ceiling is `guest_fee_owed_cents`, for `PARTY_CHARGES` it is `party_charges_owed_cents`, and for `BOND` it is the category's `bond_cents` less the household's `bond_paid_cents` already credited, the category in question being that of the household's most recently created membership — so a household that holds no membership at all has a bond ceiling of 0 and any bond payment it tenders is refused. Exceeding any of those is rejected with `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_BALANCE`.

**The queue.** In a category that requires one, a household reaches a membership only through the waitlist, and either the `MembershipChair` or the household itself may join the queue by naming the `household_id` and the `membership_category_id`. An optional `priority_tier` claims a queue tier — `LEGACY`, `SIBLING` or `STANDARD` — and defaults to `STANDARD`. From that tier a numeric `priority_tier_rank` is derived and stored as the queue's primary sort key: `LEGACY` ranks 0, `SIBLING` ranks 1, `STANDARD` ranks 2, so a lower rank is served first. A new entry is born `WAITING` with `deferral_count` at 0, `offered_at`, `offer_expires_at` and `requeued_at` all null, and its `queue_key_at` seeded from its own `created_at`. Joining is refused when the household already holds an entry for that same category in `WAITING`, `OFFERED` or `ACCEPTED` (`ERR_DUPLICATE_WAITLIST_ENTRY`), when the household's `member_count` exceeds the category's `max_household_size` (`ERR_HOUSEHOLD_SIZE_EXCEEDED`), and when the household's standing is `SUSPENDED` or `EXPELLED` (`ERR_HOUSEHOLD_SUSPENDED`).

Extending the next offer for a category is a `MembershipChair` operation that names only the `membership_category_id`, and the winner is chosen by a total order rather than by judgement. The candidate set is every entry in `WAITING` for the requested category whose household is in `GOOD_STANDING`; entries whose household is on `PROBATION`, `SUSPENDED` or `EXPELLED` are skipped and their status is left untouched, as are entries already in `OFFERED`, `ACCEPTED`, `DECLINED`, `WITHDRAWN` or `EXPIRED`. If the candidate set is empty the request is rejected with `ERR_WAITLIST_EMPTY`. Otherwise the winner is the candidate with the lowest `priority_tier_rank`; ties are broken by the earliest `queue_key_at`; remaining ties are broken by the earliest `created_at`, which is the final tie-break and is never the row's `id`. The winning entry moves `WAITING → OFFERED`, `offered_at` is stamped with the logical clock, and `offer_expires_at` is set to fourteen days after that instant. The offer remains acceptable for as long as the logical clock is at or before `offer_expires_at` — the boundary instant itself is still live.

A household responds to its own entry, and the response carries a `decision` of `ACCEPT`, `DECLINE` or `WITHDRAW`, plus an optional `requeue` flag that defaults to false and is meaningful only alongside a decline. Only the charged household may respond on its own behalf, so a `Member`'s `X-User-ID` must equal the entry's `household_id`; the `MembershipChair` may also respond, and being an operator identity its `X-User-ID` is never compared against the entry. Accepting moves `OFFERED → ACCEPTED` and is the entry a membership creation later consumes; an acceptance attempted after the offer window has passed is rejected with `ERR_OFFER_EXPIRED`. The offer-live test is not the acceptance's alone: it guards every `ACCEPT` and every `DECLINE` — requeueing or not — aimed at an entry in `OFFERED`, and it is evaluated ahead of the deferral-budget check below, so a requeueing decline against a lapsed offer answers `ERR_OFFER_EXPIRED` even when the deferral budget is exhausted as well. A `WITHDRAW` is not subject to it, since withdrawal acts on a `WAITING` entry rather than on a live offer. Declining with `requeue` true is the queue's cycle edge: the entry returns `OFFERED → WAITING`, `deferral_count` rises by one, `requeued_at` is stamped with the logical clock — which makes the entry's `queue_key_at` later than every un-requeued sibling in the same tier, so the household keeps its tier but loses its place to later arrivals — and both `offered_at` and `offer_expires_at` are cleared to null. A household may defer at most twice: a requeueing decline sent when `deferral_count` has already reached 2 is rejected with `ERR_MAX_DEFERRALS_REACHED`. Declining with `requeue` false or omitted moves `OFFERED → DECLINED` and leaves the queue for good. Withdrawing applies to an entry still in `WAITING` and moves it to `WITHDRAWN`; a withdrawal aimed at an entry in `OFFERED`, or an acceptance or a decline aimed at one still `WAITING`, is `ERR_INVALID_TRANSITION`, while any response at all to an entry already resolved is `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`, which is tested ahead of the offer-live check above. `ACCEPTED`, `DECLINED`, `EXPIRED` and `WITHDRAWN` are all terminal; every one of these responses advances the entry's `version` by one. Entries are listed and filtered by `household_id`, `membership_category_id`, `status` and `priority_tier`, which is how a household discovers its position in the queue.

### 3.3. Selling a Membership: Eligibility Gates and the Dues Assessment

Once a household has cleared the queue — or where the category never required a queue at all — the `MembershipChair` sells it the season. Creating a membership names the `household_id`, the `membership_category_id`, the `season_id` and the `join_date` on which the household's access begins, and optionally a `bond_payment_plan` of `FULL`, `INSTALLMENTS` or `TRANSFERRED` recording how the household intends to settle the category bond. The referenced season must be `OPEN`. `join_date` is a plain calendar date that must fall on or between the season's `open_date` and its `close_date`, both endpoints included; a join date outside that window is `ERR_OUTSIDE_SEASON_WINDOW`.

Five business gates stand between the request and a new membership row, and they are evaluated in the order of the ladder in §2.9 rather than the order they are listed here. A household whose `standing` is `SUSPENDED` or `EXPELLED` cannot buy in at all (`ERR_HOUSEHOLD_SUSPENDED`). A household that already holds a membership for the same season whose `access_status` is anything other than `LAPSED` or `TERMINATED` is refused as a duplicate (`ERR_DUPLICATE_MEMBERSHIP`) — a lapsed or terminated membership from the same season does not block a fresh purchase. A household whose `member_count` exceeds the category's `max_household_size` is refused with `ERR_HOUSEHOLD_SIZE_EXCEEDED`. Where the category's `waitlist_required` is true, the household must already hold a waitlist entry for that same category in `ACCEPTED` status, and its absence is reported as `ERR_WAITLIST_OFFER_REQUIRED`. Finally, when an accepted entry is being converted, the `membership_category_id` on the request must name the same category the entry sits in; a disagreement is `ERR_CATEGORY_MISMATCH`. The gate is satisfied by any `ACCEPTED` entry the household holds, whatever season the purchase is for, and the sale consumes it only in the sense that it unlocks the purchase: the entry itself is not mutated, stays `ACCEPTED` and does not advance its `version`.

A membership is born with `dues_status` `UNBILLED` and `access_status` `PENDING`. These are two genuinely independent state dimensions carried on the same row, and neither is a function of the other: a membership can be `PAID` on the money dimension while `SUSPENDED` on the access dimension, or `DELINQUENT` on the money dimension while still `ACTIVE` on the access dimension. Three figures are stamped at creation and one household ledger is moved. `assessed_dues_cents` receives the prorated seasonal dues described below. `initiation_fee_cents` receives the category's `initiation_fee_cents` when the household's `prior_member` flag is false at that moment, and 0 when it is true — which is how a returning family and a bond-inheriting family both escape the one-time fee. The household's `guest_pass_allotment_granted` rises by the category's `guest_pass_allotment` and its `guest_pass_balance` rises by the same number, so the passes are in hand before the gate ever opens. When the plan is `INSTALLMENTS`, the household's `bond_status` moves to `INSTALLMENTS` — and only from `NONE`, since `PAID` and `TRANSFERRED` are never walked backwards. Every other money and credit figure on the new row is born at 0 — `bond_installment_cents`, `swim_team_fee_cents`, `late_penalty_cents`, `invoice_total_cents`, `amount_paid_cents`, `balance_due_cents`, `volunteer_credits_required`, `volunteer_credits_earned` and `volunteer_buyout_cents` — with `invoiced_at` null and `bond_payment_plan` null when the request omitted it. The household's `version` advances along with the new membership's own.

**The seasonal dues assessment.** How much a mid-season joiner owes is decided by the season's `proration_basis`, never by anything on the request. The arithmetic begins with two measures of the season itself: the total number of calendar days in the season, counted from `open_date` to `close_date` with both endpoints included, and the total number of swim weeks, which is that day count divided by the seven days in a swim week and rounded up to the next whole number. It then computes the household's effective start, which is the later of the membership's `join_date` and the season's `open_date`, and from that the remaining number of calendar days, counted from the effective start to `close_date` with both endpoints included, and the remaining number of swim weeks, again the remaining day count divided by seven and rounded up to the next whole number.

Three cases follow, tested in this order. If the remaining day count is zero or less, `assessed_dues_cents` is 0. Otherwise, if the effective start is at or before the season's `open_date`, the household is charged the category's full `seasonal_dues_cents` with no proration whatsoever. Otherwise the variant decides: under `CALENDAR_DAY` the assessment is the category's `seasonal_dues_cents` multiplied by the remaining calendar days and divided by the total calendar days, with the single final result rounded half up to a whole cent; under `SWIM_WEEK` it is `seasonal_dues_cents` multiplied by the remaining swim weeks and divided by the total swim weeks, again with one half-up rounding applied to the final result. No rounding is applied to the intermediate ratio in either variant. Because both the numerator and the denominator of the swim-week variant are rounded up, a partial week is billed as a whole week, so for the same category, the same season window and the same `join_date` the `SWIM_WEEK` figure is greater than the `CALENDAR_DAY` figure whenever the join date is not a week boundary — and that difference flows straight into `invoice_total_cents` and `balance_due_cents` on the invoice that follows. A category whose `proration_allowed` is false short-circuits all of this and is charged the full `seasonal_dues_cents` irrespective of the join date.

`assessed_dues_cents` is stamped exactly once, while the membership is still `UNBILLED`, and is never re-derived. A later invoice, payment, write-off or season close leaves that stamped figure frozen. Categories themselves can be listed and filtered by `code` and by `waitlist_required`, and memberships by `household_id`, `membership_category_id`, `season_id`, `dues_status` and `access_status` — the last two independently, which is how the treasurer finds every membership that is `DELINQUENT` on money while still `ACTIVE` at the gate.

### 3.4. Invoicing, Settlement and the Delinquency Consequence

Cutting the invoice is the single most consequential act on a membership, and the `Treasurer` performs it exactly once per membership: it is the only edge from `UNBILLED` to `INVOICED`, it takes no payload at all, and it is available only while the membership is still `UNBILLED` and its season's `status` is `PLANNED` or `OPEN`. Invoicing a membership whose `dues_status` has already moved on answers `ERR_INVALID_TRANSITION`, except that a membership already `WRITTEN_OFF` — the money dimension's terminal state — answers `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`. It is also what flips the access dimension `PENDING → ACTIVE`, provided the owning household's `standing` is `GOOD_STANDING` at that instant, and that flip is what makes the membership eligible to sponsor guest visits and to reserve the facility for a party. The ordering consequence is deliberate and easy to get wrong: because swim-team fees are billed on the invoice, every swimmer the household intends to have billed must already be registered before the invoice is cut.

**The invoice is assembled as an ordered list of five lines, and the rounding order is part of the answer.** Line 1 is the membership's already-stamped `assessed_dues_cents`. Line 2 is its already-stamped `initiation_fee_cents`. Line 3 is the bond installment, written to `bond_installment_cents` and keyed off the `bond_payment_plan`: a plan of `FULL` bills the category's whole `bond_cents`; a plan of `INSTALLMENTS` bills one quarter of `bond_cents`, rounded half up to a whole cent; any other case — a plan of `TRANSFERRED`, or a membership created without a plan at all — bills nothing. Line 4 is the swim-team fee, written to `swim_team_fee_cents`, and it is the sum of the **already-rounded** per-swimmer `assessed_fee_cents` figures over the membership's registrations whose `status` is `PENDING`, `CONFIRMED` or `COMPLETED`; registrations in `WAITLISTED`, `WITHDRAWN` or `REVOKED` contribute nothing to Line 4. Line 5 is the late penalty, written to `late_penalty_cents`: take the sum of the four already-rounded lines above as the penalty base, and when the logical clock's date is strictly later than the season's `dues_due_date`, the penalty is that base multiplied by three and one half per cent, rounded half up to a whole cent; when the clock's date is at or before the due date the penalty is 0. `invoice_total_cents` is the penalty base plus the penalty. Summing the exact unrounded swim-team values and rounding once at the end produces a different total, and the difference survives all the way into `balance_due_cents` — the pinned order is the specification.

`balance_due_cents` is at all times the membership's `invoice_total_cents` plus its `volunteer_buyout_cents` minus its `amount_paid_cents`, floored at 0 so it never goes negative. At invoice time `volunteer_buyout_cents` is still 0 (the buyout is assessed only at season close, as §3.6 explains), so the balance is simply the invoice total less anything already paid. `invoiced_at` is stamped with the logical clock, the household's `bond_status` becomes `INSTALLMENTS` when the plan is `INSTALLMENTS`, and the membership's `version` advances.

Payments against a membership carry an `amount_cents` and an optional `allocation`, which defaults to `DUES` and may otherwise be `BOND`. A `BOND` allocation raises the household's `bond_paid_cents` and flips its `bond_status` to `PAID` once `bond_paid_cents` has reached the category's `bond_cents` — and as at the front desk, a `bond_status` of `TRANSFERRED` is never overwritten by a payment. The only ceiling on the amount tendered here is `balance_due_cents`: the bond ceiling of §3.2 governs front-desk household payments alone, so a bond allocation against a membership may carry `bond_paid_cents` past the category's `bond_cents`. Either allocation raises `amount_paid_cents` by the amount tendered and lowers `balance_due_cents` correspondingly. No payment may exceed `balance_due_cents`; one that does is rejected with `ERR_PAYMENT_EXCEEDS_BALANCE`. Payments are accepted while `dues_status` is `INVOICED` or `DELINQUENT`; one tendered against a membership still `UNBILLED` or already `PAID` is `ERR_INVALID_TRANSITION`, and one against a `WRITTEN_OFF` membership is `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`. The state consequence depends on the amount: a payment strictly smaller than the balance leaves `dues_status` exactly where it was, while a payment equal to the balance — driving `balance_due_cents` to 0 — moves `INVOICED → PAID` or `DELINQUENT → PAID`. Both edges carry identical side effects. Among them is the club's capital habit: the first time a given membership arrives at `PAID`, five thousand cents of capital-reserve contribution are booked onto its season, raising both `reserve_contributions_cents` and `reserve_unallocated_cents`. That booking happens exactly once per membership; a membership that leaves `PAID` and returns never books it twice.

When a balance proves uncollectible, the `Treasurer` writes it off. Writing off is available only from `DELINQUENT`, requires a `reason`, sets `balance_due_cents` to 0, and moves `dues_status` to the terminal `WRITTEN_OFF`. In the same request the access dimension moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` when it was `ACTIVE`, which drags the full suspension cascade of §3.9 behind it: every swim-team registration of that membership in `PENDING`, `CONFIRMED` or `WAITLISTED` is revoked and every future reservation still in `REQUESTED` or `CONFIRMED` is cancelled as `CANCELLED_BY_SUSPENSION`. The season's `reserve_contributions_cents` is **not** reduced by a write-off — an already-booked contribution is never clawed back.

Refund money reaches a membership by a second, indirect route. Whenever a party refund raises a household's `account_credit_cents`, an auto-apply rule runs immediately: if the household holds a membership in that reservation's season whose `dues_status` is `INVOICED` or `DELINQUENT`, and `account_credit_cents` is greater than or equal to that membership's `balance_due_cents`, then the whole balance is settled from the credit — `amount_paid_cents` rises by the balance, `dues_status` moves to `PAID`, `account_credit_cents` falls by the amount applied, and the season's per-membership reserve contribution is booked if it has not been booked for that membership already. A credit smaller than the balance is left sitting on the household account and applies nothing; the rule is all-or-nothing. The rule also reaches exactly one membership: it walks the household's memberships in `created_at` ascending order, stops at the first one belonging to that reservation's season whose `dues_status` is `INVOICED` or `DELINQUENT`, and goes no further whether or not the credit covered that membership's balance — so a credit large enough to settle two such memberships still settles only the earliest of them, and a household whose earliest matching membership is too expensive to clear has its later ones left untouched.

**Delinquency is contagious.** Three operations refuse to proceed for a household that is behind: recording a guest visit, requesting a party reservation and registering a swimmer. The household is treated as delinquent when it holds any membership in the season whose `dues_status` is `DELINQUENT` or `WRITTEN_OFF`, or when the sum of its `guest_fee_owed_cents` and its `party_charges_owed_cents` is greater than zero — the club's tolerance for outstanding household charges is exactly zero cents. Any of those three requests is then rejected with `ERR_DUES_DELINQUENT`, and because fiscal checks sit at the very bottom of the ladder in §2.9, that code appears only when every compliance, state and constraint check above it has already passed.

### 3.5. The Gate: Guest Passes, Guest Fees and Voided Visits

At the gate, either the sponsoring household itself or the `PoolManager` records a guest visit. The payload names the `membership_id` sponsoring the guests, the `sponsor_name` of the household person escorting them, a `guest_count` of at least 1, the `visit_start_iso` instant at which they entered, and — because the settlement outcome depends on a counter another writer may have moved — the `expected_household_version` described in §2.7. Recording a visit whose `expected_household_version` does not equal the sponsoring household's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_HOUSEHOLD_VERSION`, and nothing about the household's pass ledger changes.

The gate's own checks are these. `guest_count` must lie between 1 and 6 inclusive, and the two bounds are enforced in different bands: the floor of 1 is part of the field's declared shape, so a `guest_count` of 0 or less is `ERR_VALIDATION` at band 5, while a party of seven clears the shape band and is `ERR_GUEST_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. The visit's date must fall inside the season's window, from `open_date` to `close_date` inclusive, or the request is `ERR_OUTSIDE_SEASON_WINDOW`. A `visit_start_iso` later than the logical clock is a visit that has not happened yet and is rejected with `ERR_VISIT_IN_FUTURE`. The sponsoring membership's `access_status` must be `ACTIVE`, so a membership still `PENDING` an invoice, or one `SUSPENDED`, `LAPSED` or `TERMINATED`, is refused with `ERR_MEMBERSHIP_NOT_IN_GOOD_STANDING`. The household's `standing` must not be `SUSPENDED` or `EXPELLED` (`ERR_HOUSEHOLD_SUSPENDED`), the household must not be delinquent (`ERR_DUES_DELINQUENT`), and the season must be `OPEN` with a facility posture that is not `CLOSED` and, where the posture is `RESTRICTED`, a `guest_count` of at most 2.

**How a visit is settled** depends on the day of the week and on the season's `guest_settlement_policy`, which is copied onto the visit as its `settlement_variant` so the historical record survives a later policy change. `is_weekend` is derived from `visit_start_iso` and is true when that instant falls on a Saturday or a Sunday in UTC. The unit fee follows from it: eight hundred cents per guest on a weekend, five hundred cents per guest on a weekday. Under the `PASS_FIRST` variant, `passes_used` is the smaller of the household's current `guest_pass_balance` and the visit's `guest_count`, on every day of the week alike. Under the `FEE_FIRST_ON_WEEKEND` variant, a weekend visit consumes no pass at all — `passes_used` is 0 and every guest is charged the weekend fee — while a weekday visit behaves exactly as `PASS_FIRST` does. The billable guest count is `guest_count` less `passes_used`, and `fee_charged_cents` is that billable count multiplied by the unit fee; every term is an integer, so no rounding arises. The household's ledgers then move together: `guest_pass_balance` falls by `passes_used`, `guest_passes_consumed` rises by `passes_used`, and `guest_fee_owed_cents` rises by `fee_charged_cents`. The household's `version` advances alongside the visit's. The two variants can diverge sharply on identical inputs — the same weekend party of six may consume four passes and be billed for two guests, or consume none and be billed for all six — and they leave different surviving pass balances behind.

A visit recorded in error is voided by the `PoolManager`, who supplies a `reason` that must be present and non-empty. Nothing but the terminal test below guards the void: it is available whatever the season's `status` and `facility_posture` are and whatever the sponsoring household's `standing` is, because a correction of the record is not itself an admission. Voiding moves the visit `RECORDED → VOIDED`, stamps `voided_at` with the logical clock, and reverses the ledger exactly: because a `VOIDED` visit no longer sits inside the `RECORDED` filter, `guest_passes_consumed` falls by that visit's `passes_used` and `guest_pass_balance` rises by the same number, while `guest_fee_owed_cents` falls by that visit's `fee_charged_cents`. `guest_pass_allotment_granted` is deliberately left untouched, which preserves the club's conservation identity: a household's `guest_pass_balance` plus its `guest_passes_consumed` always equals its `guest_pass_allotment_granted`. Both the visit's `version` and the household's `version` advance. `VOIDED` is terminal; a voided visit cannot be un-voided, and settling the fee is not a reversal — cash owed at the gate is retired only by a household payment allocated to `GUEST_FEES`, as §3.2 describes. Visits are listed and filtered by `membership_id`, by `status` and by `settlement_variant`, the last of which is how the treasurer separates pass-settled from fee-settled admissions when the board changes policy mid-decade.

### 3.6. The Swim Team, Meet Hosting and the Volunteer Credit Ledger

Registering a swimmer is open both to the `SwimTeamCoach` and to the household itself. The payload names the `membership_id` the swimmer belongs to, the `season_id` being swum, the `swimmer_name`, the `swimmer_birthdate` and the `squad` — one of `MINI`, `JUNIOR` or `SENIOR`. The `season_id` on the request must be the same season as the membership's own `season_id`; a disagreement between two season-scoped references in one request is `ERR_SEASON_MISMATCH`. The season must be `OPEN`, the household must not be `SUSPENDED` or `EXPELLED`, and the household must not be delinquent. The access-posture rule is deliberately looser here than anywhere else: registration accepts a membership whose `access_status` is `PENDING` **or** `ACTIVE`, because swimmers must be on the roster before the invoice is cut — the invoice is both what bills the swim-team fees and what flips access from `PENDING` to `ACTIVE` — but a membership that is `SUSPENDED`, `LAPSED` or `TERMINATED` may never register a swimmer, and such a request is `ERR_MEMBERSHIP_NOT_IN_GOOD_STANDING`.

Two figures are computed at registration. `squad_seq` is the swimmer's 1-based rank **within its own membership**, obtained by ordering that membership's registrations whose `status` is `PENDING`, `CONFIRMED`, `WAITLISTED` or `COMPLETED` by `swimmer_birthdate` ascending and then, for identical birthdates, by `created_at` ascending — never by `id`. From that rank comes the multi-swimmer discount: each swimmer past the first earns a further six and one quarter per cent off the base swim-team fee of fourteen thousand five hundred cents, and the accumulated discount rate is capped at twenty-five per cent, so the rate runs 0 per cent for the first swimmer, six and one quarter for the second, twelve and one half for the third and eighteen and three quarters for the fourth, and the fifth and every later swimmer share the same capped twenty-five per cent. `assessed_fee_cents` is the base fee multiplied by one minus that capped rate, rounded half up to a whole cent, and it is that already-rounded per-swimmer figure that Line 4 of the invoice sums. Each squad has a standing capacity — twenty places in `MINI`, thirty in `JUNIOR` and thirty in `SENIOR` — counted across the season's registrations in `PENDING`, `CONFIRMED` or `COMPLETED`. A full squad does not reject the registration: the swimmer is simply born `WAITLISTED` instead of `PENDING`. The membership's `swim_team_fee_cents` is recomputed either way — and a recomputation of `swim_team_fee_cents`, here or on any later registration move, rewrites that one field alone: `invoice_total_cents` and `balance_due_cents` were fixed when the invoice was cut and are never re-derived from it, so a swimmer registered after the invoice raises `swim_team_fee_cents` without raising by one cent what the household owes. The same is true of `volunteer_credits_required`, which no registration move ever turns into money before season close.

Confirming a swimmer onto a squad is the coach's act, and it tightens the posture rules again: the membership must be `access_status` `ACTIVE` **and** `dues_status` `INVOICED` or `PAID`, and the season must still be `OPEN`, so no swimmer is confirmed from an uninvoiced or suspended membership. The closed-season complaint outranks even the terminal one, band 11 of §2.9 putting `ERR_SEASON_NOT_OPEN` ahead of everything else it carries: a confirmation aimed at an already `WITHDRAWN` registration in a season that has closed reads `ERR_SEASON_NOT_OPEN` rather than `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`. The posture checks that follow it are the membership's alone, and they are reached only on the confirmation edge — a withdrawal is never refused for the state of the membership's dues. A `PENDING → CONFIRMED` move needs no capacity re-check, since the registration already held its slot. A `WAITLISTED → CONFIRMED` move does: the count of the season's registrations in that same squad with `status` `PENDING`, `CONFIRMED` or `COMPLETED` must be strictly below the squad's capacity, and this is the only edge in the whole system that raises `ERR_SQUAD_FULL`; when it succeeds, the registration takes the free slot directly without passing through `PENDING`. Either confirmation recomputes the membership's `volunteer_credits_required` and its `swim_team_fee_cents`.

Withdrawal is available from `PENDING`, from `CONFIRMED` and from `WAITLISTED`, and the three branches differ in exactly one respect, the promotion. Withdrawal from any of the three releases every volunteer assignment of that registration still in `ASSIGNED`, a `WAITLISTED` registration included, since nothing stopped it being rostered for a job. From `PENDING` and from `CONFIRMED` the freed squad slot additionally promotes the earliest `WAITLISTED` registration for the same season and squad — ordered by `created_at` ascending — back to `PENDING`; that promotion happens only while the squad count is strictly below capacity, and exactly one sibling is promoted however many slots the count leaves free. The promoted registration's own membership — very often another household's entirely — has its `volunteer_credits_required` and `swim_team_fee_cents` recomputed and its `version` advanced inside the same request, because moving that swimmer from `WAITLISTED` to `PENDING` moves its `assessed_fee_cents` into the set Line 4 sums. From `WAITLISTED`, no slot frees and no sibling is promoted, because a `WAITLISTED` row never held a slot in the first place. All three branches recompute the membership's `volunteer_credits_required` and `swim_team_fee_cents` and advance the registration's `version`. `REVOKED` is reached only by the suspension cascade of §3.9 and behaves like withdrawal in every respect — releasing `ASSIGNED` assignments, and freeing a slot only where the revoked row was `PENDING` or `CONFIRMED` rather than `WAITLISTED`. `COMPLETED` is reached only at season close. `WITHDRAWN`, `REVOKED` and `COMPLETED` are all terminal. Registrations are listed and filtered by `membership_id`, `season_id`, `squad` and `status`.

**Hosting a meet.** The `SwimTeamCoach` schedules a home meet against a season that is `OPEN`, supplying the `season_id`, a `name`, the `start_iso` and `end_iso` bracketing the competition, `expected_swimmers` as the projected swimmer headcount the meet is expected to draw, and `volunteer_slots_required` — the number of volunteer **credits** the host club must field, which may legitimately be 0. `expected_swimmers` is recorded for the coach's reference and nothing more: exactly like the season's `baseline_bather_load`, it enters neither the ratio computation of §3.8 nor the aggregate capacity comparison of §3.7, both of which count reservation loads alone, so a meet never adds a single bather to either figure. The meet's window must fall inside the season's own window, compared at day granularity — the `start_iso`'s UTC date at or after `open_date` and the `end_iso`'s UTC date at or before `close_date`, both endpoints included — or the request is `ERR_OUTSIDE_SEASON_WINDOW`. A new meet is `SCHEDULED`, with `volunteer_slots_filled` at 0, `roster_closed_at` null and `understaffed_penalty_cents` at 0. Meets are listed and filtered by `season_id` and `status`.

Volunteer jobs are taken by naming the `meet_id`, the `swim_team_registration_id` whose household is working the job, and a `job_code` of `TIMER`, `STARTER`, `CONCESSIONS`, `MARSHAL` or `SCORER`. Jobs are not equal: a `credit_value` of 2 is stamped for `STARTER` and for `SCORER`, and of 1 for `TIMER`, `CONCESSIONS` and `MARSHAL`. **Slots are counted in credits, not in bodies, and a new assignment counts the instant it is created** — it is born `ASSIGNED` and immediately raises the meet's `volunteer_slots_filled` by its `credit_value`. Three checks guard the creation. The meet's roster must still be open, meaning its `roster_closed_at` is null; a closed roster accepts no further assignment and yields `ERR_ROSTER_ALREADY_CLOSED`. The meet may not be oversubscribed in credits: the meet's current `volunteer_slots_filled` plus the new job's credit value must not exceed `volunteer_slots_required`, and an attempt to go beyond is `ERR_VOLUNTEER_SLOTS_FULL`. And a single registration may not hold two jobs at the same meet, so a registration that already has an assignment for this meet in `ASSIGNED` or `COMPLETED` is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_VOLUNTEER_ASSIGNMENT`. The meet named and the registration named must also belong to the same season, or the request is `ERR_SEASON_MISMATCH`. The registration is resolved before the meet, so a request naming neither answers `ERR_REGISTRATION_NOT_FOUND`, and the registration's own `status` is not a gate at all: the four checks just listed are the whole of the creation's business guard. Assignments are listed and filtered by `meet_id`, `swim_team_registration_id`, `job_code` and `status`. Either the `SwimTeamCoach` or a `Member` may move an assignment, and a `VolunteerAssignment` is not one of the ownership-bearing resources §2.1 enumerates: no household comparison is made on it anywhere, so any authorised `Member` may complete or no-show any assignment and may read any of them.

Closing the roster is the fork in the meet's life, and both branches are reachable from `SCHEDULED` only. When `volunteer_slots_filled` is greater than or equal to `volunteer_slots_required` the meet becomes `STAFFED`; the comparison is inclusive, so equality is sufficient and a meet requiring 0 credits qualifies with no assignments at all. On that branch `roster_closed_at` is stamped with the logical clock and `understaffed_penalty_cents` is set to 0. When `volunteer_slots_filled` is strictly below `volunteer_slots_required` the meet becomes `UNDERSTAFFED`; `roster_closed_at` is stamped identically, and `understaffed_penalty_cents` is set to the credit shortfall — the requirement less the filled count — multiplied by seven thousand five hundred cents per missing credit. Either way the meet's `version` advances, and no further assignment may be created for it. A second closure attempt is answered on the roster test rather than on the edge test: a meet already `STAFFED` or `UNDERSTAFFED` necessarily carries a stamped `roster_closed_at`, and that reads as `ERR_ROSTER_ALREADY_CLOSED`, reported on this one operation ahead of the `ERR_INVALID_TRANSITION` the closed edge would otherwise raise — while a meet already `HOSTED` or `CANCELLED` is `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` ahead of both.

A closed roster is not a frozen one. Moving an assignment to `NO_SHOW` — available only while the meet is `STAFFED`, `UNDERSTAFFED` or `HOSTED`, and `ERR_INVALID_TRANSITION` against a meet still `SCHEDULED` or already `CANCELLED` — lowers the meet's `volunteer_slots_filled` by that assignment's `credit_value`, because `NO_SHOW` leaves the counted set. The suspension and withdrawal cascades produce `RELEASED`, which is never reachable by a direct request, and it lowers `volunteer_slots_filled` by the same rule. In either case, when the meet was `STAFFED` and the recomputed `volunteer_slots_filled` falls strictly below `volunteer_slots_required`, the meet slides back `STAFFED → UNDERSTAFFED` and `understaffed_penalty_cents` is recomputed from the new shortfall. That slide is the only recomputation: a credit that leaves a meet already sitting in `UNDERSTAFFED` still lowers `volunteer_slots_filled`, but the `understaffed_penalty_cents` figure stays exactly as the roster closure or the earlier slide stamped it and does not follow the deeper shortfall. So where a cascade releases two credits from the same `STAFFED` meet one row at a time, the first release stamps the penalty and the second leaves that stamped figure alone. `roster_closed_at` is **not** cleared on that slide — the roster stays closed, so no replacement volunteer may be assigned, and the penalty is the club's remedy rather than a re-opened sign-up sheet. Moving an assignment to `COMPLETED` leaves `volunteer_slots_filled` unchanged, since `COMPLETED` remains inside the counted set, and raises the owning membership's `volunteer_credits_earned` by the assignment's `credit_value` — raises it, but not by incrementing the stored figure: the counter is recomputed from scratch over every `COMPLETED` assignment beneath the membership and re-capped at ten exactly as the derivation below defines it, so an assignment completed once the membership already holds ten credits moves neither the counter nor the membership's `version`, and a `COMPLETED` assignment hanging off a registration that was later withdrawn or revoked still counts. `COMPLETED`, `NO_SHOW` and `RELEASED` are all terminal.

Both fork branches join at `HOSTED`, and hosting is available only once the logical clock has reached or passed the meet's `end_iso` — attempted before the meet has finished it is `ERR_INVALID_TRANSITION` — and only while the season's `facility_posture` is `OPEN`. Hosting also re-checks the deck: the staffing ratio of §3.8 must report the window compliant at the same two probes a party confirmation uses, at the meet's `start_iso` and at one minute before its `end_iso`, or the request is `ERR_GUARD_RATIO_NOT_MET`. On both branches, every volunteer assignment for the meet still in `ASSIGNED` moves to `COMPLETED`, processed in `created_at` ascending order, and each one raises its owning membership's `volunteer_credits_earned` by its `credit_value`. The branches then diverge on money. Hosting from `STAFFED` books no penalty at all. Hosting from `UNDERSTAFFED` divides the accrued `understaffed_penalty_cents` evenly across the memberships holding a `CONFIRMED` registration in that meet's season, ordered by the membership's `created_at` ascending: the share is the integer quotient, and the remainder cents are handed out one each to the earliest memberships in that order. Each share is added to that membership's `balance_due_cents`, and a membership holding two or three confirmed swimmers is still one membership and is billed one share. A membership whose share works out to 0 cents — which happens at the tail of the order whenever the penalty is smaller than the number of memberships sharing it — is left entirely alone: neither its `balance_due_cents` nor its `version` moves, because nothing was billed to it. Cancelling a meet is available from `SCHEDULED`, from `STAFFED` and from `UNDERSTAFFED`; all three release every assignment still in `ASSIGNED`, processed in `created_at` ascending order, and set `understaffed_penalty_cents` to 0, so a cancelled understaffed meet has its accrued penalty zeroed rather than billed. A release produced by a cancellation is the one release that leaves `volunteer_slots_filled` exactly where it stood: the counter is not recomputed on this path and the meet does not slide between `STAFFED` and `UNDERSTAFFED` on the way out, because the figure now stands as the historical record of what had been rostered when the meet was called off. `HOSTED` and `CANCELLED` are the meet's terminal states.

**The volunteer obligation and its buyout.** A membership's `volunteer_credits_required` is not a fixed number but a derived one: the category's `volunteer_credits_required` multiplied by the count of that membership's registrations whose `status` is `CONFIRMED` or `COMPLETED`, with the product capped at ten credits. Its `volunteer_credits_earned` is the sum of the `credit_value` of every assignment belonging to that membership's registrations whose `status` is `COMPLETED`, likewise capped at ten credits — the cap is applied to earned credits **before** the shortfall is taken, so over-volunteering can never produce a negative buyout. The shortfall is the required figure less the earned figure, floored at 0, and `volunteer_buyout_cents` is that shortfall multiplied by three thousand five hundred cents per unworked credit. The buyout is assessed only when the `PoolManager` closes the season, as §3.1 describes: at that moment each membership's earned credits are recomputed, the buyout is set and folded into `balance_due_cents`, and a membership that had already settled in full moves `dues_status` backwards from `PAID` to `INVOICED` — the only backwards edge on the money dimension in the whole system.

### 3.7. Private Parties: Zone Booking, Tiered Pricing and Refunds

A household books the facility for a private party itself, or the `PoolManager` books it on the household's behalf. The request names the `membership_id` hosting the party, the `zone` reserved — `PAVILION`, `SHALLOW_END` or `WHOLE_FACILITY` — the `start_iso` and `end_iso` of the booking, a `guest_count` of non-member guests expected (which may be 0) and a `host_party_size` of household attendees of at least 1. Reservation windows are treated as half-open intervals that include the start instant and exclude the end instant, so two parties that merely abut — where one ends at the exact instant the next begins — do not overlap.

Before anything is priced, the booking must survive a wall of gates, answered in one fixed order. The hosting membership's category must carry `party_privileges`, and a request naming a membership whose category does not is rejected as invalid input with `ERR_VALIDATION` — a complaint about the request rather than about the state of the club, so it is settled as soon as the membership and its category resolve and ahead of every state, temporal, constraint and fiscal test below it. Then the season must be `OPEN`; the facility posture must not be `CLOSED`; a `RESTRICTED` posture forbids the `WHOLE_FACILITY` zone specifically. Then the household's `standing` must not be `SUSPENDED` or `EXPELLED`, and only after that must the membership's `access_status` be `ACTIVE`, the household's posture outranking the membership's exactly as band 11 of §2.9 orders them. The window must lie inside the season's own window (`ERR_OUTSIDE_SEASON_WINDOW`), compared at day granularity — the `start_iso`'s UTC date at or after `open_date` and the `end_iso`'s UTC date at or before `close_date`, both endpoints included, so a party held on the opening day or the closing day is inside the window whatever the hour. Only then are the neighbouring bookings consulted, and both of the constraints that follow are read off a single pass in `created_at` ascending order over the reservations of this membership's own season and no other: a party in one season never conflicts with, nor contributes load to, a party in another. No other reservation in `REQUESTED` or `CONFIRMED` may hold a conflicting zone over an overlapping half-open window: a `WHOLE_FACILITY` booking conflicts with every zone including itself, while `PAVILION` and `SHALLOW_END` conflict only with themselves, and a conflict is `ERR_ZONE_DOUBLE_BOOKED`, reported on the first conflicting row the pass reaches rather than after the load has been totalled, so a request that both double-books a zone and overruns the capacity reads as the double-booking. The aggregate bather load of every `REQUESTED` and `CONFIRMED` reservation overlapping the requested half-open window, plus this reservation's own load, must not exceed the season's `bather_load_capacity`, or the request is `ERR_BATHER_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. And last of all come the two fiscal gates: the household must not be delinquent (`ERR_DUES_DELINQUENT`), and its bond must be settled — `bond_status` of `PAID` or `TRANSFERRED`, or `bond_paid_cents` having reached the category's `bond_cents` — before it may reserve the facility at all, with an unsettled bond reported as `ERR_BOND_NOT_PAID`.

**Pricing looks circular and is not.** The guard surcharge depends on the bather load, the discount tier depends on a subtotal that already contains the guard surcharge, and the total depends on the discount. Nine steps are executed in exactly this sequence. First, `projected_bather_load` is the reservation's `guest_count` plus its `host_party_size`. Second, `required_guard_count` is derived from that load: divide the load by the twenty-five bathers each guard may cover and round up to the next whole number, take the larger of that figure and the standing minimum of one guard whenever the facility is open, and then add one further guard when the reserved `zone` is `WHOLE_FACILITY`, because the deep end needs its own eyes. Third, `duration_hours_ceil` is the reservation's length in minutes divided by sixty and rounded up to the next whole hour, so a four-and-a-half-hour party is billed as five hours. Fourth, `lifeguard_surcharge_cents` is `required_guard_count` multiplied by `duration_hours_ceil` multiplied by two thousand two hundred cents per guard-hour. Fifth, `base_facility_fee_cents` is the flat fee of the reserved zone: six thousand cents for `PAVILION`, nine thousand for `SHALLOW_END`, twenty thousand for `WHOLE_FACILITY`. Sixth, `guest_overage_fee_cents` charges seven hundred cents for each guest beyond the ten guests included in every booking, and is 0 when `guest_count` is ten or fewer. Seventh, `subtotal_cents` is `base_facility_fee_cents` plus `guest_overage_fee_cents` plus `lifeguard_surcharge_cents` — the guard surcharge sits **inside** the discount base, and that is the step most easily got wrong. Eighth, the member discount rate is chosen from `subtotal_cents` against two inclusive lower bounds: a subtotal of at least forty-five thousand cents earns fifteen per cent, a subtotal of at least twenty-five thousand cents earns ten per cent, and anything below twenty-five thousand cents earns nothing. Ninth, `member_discount_cents` is `subtotal_cents` multiplied by that rate and rounded half up to a whole cent, and `total_charge_cents` is `subtotal_cents` less `member_discount_cents`. Evaluating the tier against the zone fee and guest overage alone, without the guard surcharge, selects a lower tier and produces a different and wrong total.

A new reservation is born `REQUESTED` with `refunded_cents` at 0, and its `total_charge_cents` is booked onto the household immediately: `party_charges_owed_cents` rises by that amount and the household's `version` advances. Nothing about overlap or capacity feeds the price — those are validation concerns, and no other reservation contributes a cent to this one's charge. Reservations are listed and filtered by `membership_id`, `zone` and `status`.

A requested reservation waits on the `PoolManager`, who moves it by naming a `to_status`. The transitions endpoint is shared with the host: both the `PoolManager` and a `Member` clear its endpoint authorisation, and the edges are then narrowed one by one from the `to_status` the payload names. Confirming, rejecting and completing belong to the `PoolManager` alone, and a `Member` requesting any of the three is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`; only `CANCELLED_BY_HOST` is open to both roles. That narrowing is decided from the payload alone, before the reservation is looked up and before the ownership comparison runs, so a `Member` naming `CONFIRMED` against a reservation that does not exist, or against another household's reservation, is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` rather than `ERR_PARTY_RESERVATION_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`. Ownership is then compared for a `Member` on whichever edge survives, so a `Member` may host-cancel only their own household's party. Confirming — `REQUESTED → CONFIRMED` — is permitted only while the season's `facility_posture` is `OPEN`, and only when the staffing ratio of §3.8 reports the deck compliant at two probes: at the reservation's `start_iso`, and at one minute before its `end_iso`. A failure at either probe is `ERR_GUARD_RATIO_NOT_MET`. Confirmation moves no money, because the charge was booked when the party was requested. Rejecting — `REQUESTED → REJECTED` — refunds in full on exactly the terms every other refund of this section uses: `refunded_cents` is set to the reservation's `total_charge_cents`, the household's `party_charges_owed_cents` falls by that amount, its `account_credit_cents` rises by the same amount, and both the reservation's and the household's `version` advance. A rejection is the club's own decision and not the member's, but it is still not a settlement: the account-credit auto-apply rule of §3.4 does **not** run on a rejection, so the credit sits on the household until a later refund's auto-apply reaches it — `account_credit_cents` is moved by nothing else in the system, no payment at the front desk or against a membership ever drawing on it. Completing — `CONFIRMED → COMPLETED` — is available only once the logical clock has reached or passed the reservation's `end_iso`, refused with `ERR_INVALID_TRANSITION` while the party is still under way, and moves nothing but the status.

Four cancellation outcomes exist and they refund on three different rules, which is the club's way of pricing whose fault the cancellation was. The host may cancel a `CONFIRMED` reservation — either the hosting household itself or the `PoolManager` — but only while the logical clock is strictly before `start_iso`; a party already under way or already past has no host-cancellation edge left and the request is `ERR_INVALID_TRANSITION`. Cancelling at least seven days ahead of `start_iso` refunds the whole `total_charge_cents`; cancelling later than that refunds half the `total_charge_cents`, rounded half up to a whole cent. A closure-driven cancellation, which the posture cascade of §3.1 produces for every `CONFIRMED` reservation whose `end_iso` is still in the future, always refunds the whole `total_charge_cents` no matter how close to the party it lands, because the club caused it. A suspension-driven cancellation, which the conduct cascade of §3.9 produces for every reservation in `REQUESTED` or `CONFIRMED` whose `start_iso` is still in the future, always refunds half the `total_charge_cents`, rounded half up, because the member caused it.

Every one of those refunds moves the same pair of household counters — `party_charges_owed_cents` falls by `refunded_cents` and `account_credit_cents` rises by the same amount — and advances both the reservation's `version` and the household's `version`. That subtraction is plain and is **not** floored at 0, unlike a membership's `balance_due_cents`: a household that had already settled its party charges at the front desk before the refund lands is left with a negative `party_charges_owed_cents`, and that negative figure enters the delinquency sum of §3.4 exactly as it stands, where it can offset a positive `guest_fee_owed_cents`. What happens next differs by exactly one rule. On a host cancellation and on a closure cancellation, the account-credit auto-apply rule of §3.4 then runs, which is how the closure cascade reaches four hops deep: a critical inspection finding closes the facility, the closure cancels the party, the refund credits the household account, and the credit settles an open membership balance. On a suspension cancellation the auto-apply rule does **not** run, because a suspended household may not settle its dues out of a refund its own conduct produced. `COMPLETED`, `REJECTED`, `CANCELLED_BY_HOST`, `CANCELLED_BY_CLOSURE` and `CANCELLED_BY_SUSPENSION` are all terminal.

### 3.8. The Deck: Guards, Certifications and Ratio Compliance

Hiring a guard is the simplest write in the system and the least useful on its own. The `PoolManager` records a `full_name` and a `hire_date`, and the new guard starts with `employment_status` `ACTIVE`, `scheduled_hours` at 0, and both derived certification flags — `has_valid_lifeguard_cert` and `has_valid_cpr_cert` — false. Because no shift can be confirmed without certifications on file, a freshly hired guard cannot yet stand on the deck. Guards are listed and filtered by `employment_status` and by `has_valid_lifeguard_cert`, which is how the manager finds who is legally deployable.

Certifications are recorded against a guard by naming the `lifeguard_id`, the `cert_type` — `LIFEGUARD`, `CPR_AED`, `WSI` or `POOL_OPERATOR` — the `issued_on` date and the `expires_on` date, which must fall strictly after `issued_on` on pain of `ERR_INVALID_TIME_RANGE` — a paired-range failure of band 7, settled over the payload before the `lifeguard_id` is resolved, so a reversed pair naming a guard who does not exist reads as the time-range complaint and never as `ERR_LIFEGUARD_NOT_FOUND`. A new certification is born `VALID` with `revoked_reason` null. Recording a second certification of the same `cert_type` for the same guard is the renewal path: an existing row of that type in `VALID` moves to `SUPERSEDED`, and so does an existing row of that type in `EXPIRED`, in both cases advancing the superseded row's own `version` while the new row stands as the live one. Both certification flags are recomputed after every such write — `has_valid_lifeguard_cert` is true when at least one of the guard's certifications has `cert_type` `LIFEGUARD` and `status` `VALID`, and `has_valid_cpr_cert` is the same test for `CPR_AED` — and a guard sitting at `INACTIVE` returns to `ACTIVE` the moment a valid lifeguard certification exists for them again.

Revocation runs the other way. The manager supplies a `revoked_reason`, which is stored on the row as it moves `VALID → REVOKED`; both flags are recomputed; and when the revoked row was the guard's last `VALID` `LIFEGUARD` certification, the guard's `employment_status` moves `ACTIVE → INACTIVE`, after which no shift of theirs may be confirmed. Revocation also clears the schedule: every shift of that guard in `SCHEDULED` or `CONFIRMED` whose `end_iso` is still in the future moves to `CANCELLED`, taken in `created_at` ascending order and each advancing its own `version`. Those cancellations are unconditional — the ratio precondition that protects a manually cancelled shift is not consulted here, because a revoked certification leaves the club no lawful choice, so a revocation strands a confirmed party rather than being refused with `ERR_GUARD_RATIO_NOT_MET`. Once the schedule is cleared the guard's `scheduled_hours` is recomputed over what survives. Both the certification's `version` and the guard's `version` advance. `REVOKED` and `SUPERSEDED` are terminal, so a second revocation of the same row is `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`; `EXPIRED` is not terminal, since an expired row can still be superseded by a renewal, but neither may it be revoked — revocation is an edge out of `VALID` alone, and aimed at an `EXPIRED` row it is `ERR_INVALID_TRANSITION`. Expiry itself is never spontaneous — it happens only in step one of the billing checkpoint described in §3.1, which moves every `VALID` certification whose `expires_on` is strictly earlier than the effective date to `EXPIRED` and recomputes the affected guard's flags, and which can therefore drive a guard `ACTIVE → INACTIVE` by the same last-lifeguard-certification rule. Certifications are listed and filtered by `lifeguard_id`, `cert_type` and `status`, which is how a guard's certification wall is discovered; the health department's own reads cover inspection findings filtered by `season_id`, `severity` and `status`, and seasons filtered by `year`, `status` and `facility_posture`.

**The certification wall.** A shift is scheduled by naming the `lifeguard_id`, the `season_id`, a `shift_role` of `PRIMARY`, `SECONDARY` or `SUPERVISOR`, and the `start_iso` and `end_iso` bounding the watch. Two derived figures are stamped: `duration_hours`, the length of the interval expressed in hours and rounded half up to two decimal places, and `counts_toward_ratio`, true when the `shift_role` is `PRIMARY` or `SECONDARY` and false for `SUPERVISOR`. The guard's `scheduled_hours` — the sum of the already-rounded `duration_hours` figures of their shifts in `SCHEDULED`, `CONFIRMED` or `COMPLETED`, itself carried to two decimal places rather than as an integer, so a guard holding a single four-hour watch reads `4.0` while a guard who has never held a shift reads the plain `0` they were hired with — is recomputed and the guard's `version` advances. Every later recomputation is that same total sum taken afresh rather than an adjustment of the stored figure, and where the recomputed total equals what is already stored the guard is not touched at all: confirming a shift leaves the guard's row entirely alone, `SCHEDULED` and `CONFIRMED` both being inside the counted set. Scheduling is refused outright unless the guard holds a certification with `cert_type` `LIFEGUARD` and `status` `VALID` **and** another with `cert_type` `CPR_AED` and `status` `VALID`, each with an `expires_on` at or after the date of the shift's `end_iso`; a guard short either one is `ERR_CERT_NOT_VALID_FOR_SHIFT`. That check is evaluated ahead of every scheduling and fiscal check, so an uncertified guard is always reported as a compliance failure and never as a scheduling conflict. The scheduling conflict itself is the rest buffer: the guard may hold no other shift in `SCHEDULED` or `CONFIRMED` whose closed interval comes within thirty minutes of the new one. Two shifts that merely touch, where the earlier `end_iso` equals the new `start_iso`, are rejected; a gap of exactly thirty minutes is allowed. A violation is `ERR_SHIFT_REST_BUFFER_VIOLATION`. The shift's window must also lie inside the season's window (`ERR_OUTSIDE_SEASON_WINDOW`), compared at day granularity — the `start_iso`'s UTC date at or after `open_date` and the `end_iso`'s UTC date at or before `close_date`, both endpoints included, so a shift standing on the opening day or the closing day is inside the window whatever the hour — and the season must be `OPEN`.

Shift lifecycle moves are all the manager's, each naming a `to_status`. `SCHEDULED → CONFIRMED` requires the guard's `employment_status` to be `ACTIVE` and re-runs the whole certification wall against the date of `end_iso`, because a certification may have expired between scheduling and confirmation; both halves of that requirement are the same compliance complaint, so an `INACTIVE` guard is `ERR_CERT_NOT_VALID_FOR_SHIFT` exactly as a lapsed wall is, the roster posture being tested first and the wall second. `CONFIRMED → COMPLETED` requires the logical clock to have reached or passed `end_iso`, and a shift completed early is `ERR_INVALID_TRANSITION` rather than a temporal-window complaint, because the club treats a watch that has not finished as having no completion edge yet. `CONFIRMED → NO_SHOW` requires the logical clock to have reached or passed `start_iso`, refused the same way with `ERR_INVALID_TRANSITION` while the shift has not begun, and because a `NO_SHOW` shift leaves the counted set, it lowers the guard's `scheduled_hours`. Cancelling is available from `SCHEDULED` and from `CONFIRMED` alike, and both edges carry the same protective precondition: for every reservation in `CONFIRMED` whose half-open window overlaps the shift — overlap being strict at both ends, so a party that merely abuts the watch is not consulted, while a party already in the past is, the test never asking whether the party has happened yet — the guard count that would remain after the cancellation must still satisfy the ratio rule below, and a cancellation that would strand a confirmed party without coverage is refused with `ERR_GUARD_RATIO_NOT_MET`. Each such reservation contributes up to two probe instants, taken from the party and then pulled inside the watch: the reservation's `start_iso` and the instant one minute before its `end_iso`, each clamped into the shift's closed interval so that a candidate before the shift's `start_iso` becomes that `start_iso` and one after its `end_iso` becomes that `end_iso`. A clamped instant that no longer falls inside the reservation's own half-open window is discarded, and where both candidates clamp onto the same instant that instant is tested once rather than twice. Every surviving instant is then put to the ratio rule below with this shift's own coverage removed, and the first instant that is not compliant refuses the cancellation. Cancellation likewise lowers `scheduled_hours`. The sixth outcome, `CANCELLED_BY_CLOSURE`, is reachable only through the closure cascade of §3.1, which takes every shift of the season in `SCHEDULED` or `CONFIRMED` whose `end_iso` is still in the future and recomputes the affected guards' `scheduled_hours`. `COMPLETED`, `CANCELLED`, `NO_SHOW` and `CANCELLED_BY_CLOSURE` are terminal. Shifts are listed and filtered by `lifeguard_id`, `season_id`, `status` and `shift_role`.

**The bather-load ratio.** Compliance is always evaluated at a single instant, and the `PoolManager`, the `BoardPresident` and the `HealthInspector` may all ask the question directly through a computed read that takes a mandatory `at_iso` query parameter — absent, empty or not a well-formed ISO 8601 UTC instant, the read is `ERR_VALIDATION`, and that check is settled before the season in the path is resolved — and returns an object rather than a stored entity: the `season_id` and `at_iso` it was asked about, the `bather_load` it found, the `required_guard_count` that load demands, the `scheduled_guard_count` actually covering the instant, and the boolean `compliant` verdict. The load is the sum of `guest_count` plus `host_party_size` over the season's reservations in `REQUESTED` or `CONFIRMED` whose window contains the instant, where a reservation's window is half-open: it includes `start_iso` and excludes `end_iso`, so back-to-back parties do not stack their loads. Reservations in `REJECTED`, `COMPLETED` or any cancelled status contribute nothing. The requirement is that load divided by the twenty-five bathers one guard may cover, rounded up to the next whole number, taken against the standing minimum of one guard, whichever is larger — plus one further guard when any of the overlapping reservations books `WHOLE_FACILITY`. Coverage counts the distinct `lifeguard_id` values over the season's shifts that are in `CONFIRMED`, that have `counts_toward_ratio` true, and whose interval contains the instant, where a shift's interval is **closed**: it includes both `start_iso` and `end_iso`, so a guard standing at the exact instant of handover is counted. That asymmetry between the two intervals is deliberate. Shifts merely `SCHEDULED` do not count toward coverage, because an unconfirmed guard may not show, and `SUPERVISOR` shifts never count at all. The verdict is compliant when the covering guard count is greater than or equal to the required count; exactly meeting the requirement is compliant.

### 3.9. Conduct: Reporting, Sanction, Appeal and the Suspension Cascade

A conduct incident is reported by the `PoolManager` or the `BoardPresident` against a household, naming the `household_id` charged, the `subject_name` of the individual involved as free text, a `severity` of `MINOR`, `MAJOR` or `SEVERE`, and a `narrative` of what happened. The case opens at `REPORTED` with the reporter's identity captured in `reported_by_user_id`, `points_assessed` at 0 and `appeal_count` at 0; `decided_by_user_id`, `sanction_type` and `sanctioned_at` are all null until the board decides. Reporting a case is the one conduct write that is never blocked by the household's own standing — a household already suspended can be reported again, and must be, or the second incident would never reach the points ledger. Cases are read and listed by the `BoardPresident`, the `PoolManager` and the charged `Member`, filterable by `household_id`, `severity` and `status`; a `Member` reaching for a case belonging to another household is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`.

Every subsequent move names a `to_status` and, when sanctioning, a `sanction_type`. The case's transitions endpoint is shared between the board and the household: the `BoardPresident` and a `Member` both clear its endpoint authorisation, and the edge is then narrowed from the `to_status` the payload names. `APPEALED` belongs to the `Member` alone; `UNDER_REVIEW`, `SANCTIONED`, `DISMISSED` and `CLOSED` belong to the `BoardPresident` alone, so a president filing an appeal on the household's behalf is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` and so is a `Member` sanctioning, dismissing, reviewing or closing. That narrowing is decided from the payload before the case is looked up and before the ownership comparison runs, so a `Member` naming `SANCTIONED` against another household's case, or against no case at all, is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` rather than `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` or `ERR_CONDUCT_CASE_NOT_FOUND`; the ownership comparison then follows on the appeal edge that survives it. A case at `REPORTED` moves to `UNDER_REVIEW` when the board takes it up. Sanctioning from `UNDER_REVIEW` requires the `sanction_type` to be present and stamps it alongside `decided_by_user_id` and a `sanctioned_at` set to the logical clock, then sets `points_assessed` to the point value of the case's `severity`. Dismissal from `UNDER_REVIEW` also records `decided_by_user_id` but leaves `points_assessed` at 0, and recomputes the household's points so that a dismissal of a previously sanctioned-then-reheard case cannot leave stale points behind. Both decisions are subject to two-person control: the deciding `X-User-ID` may not equal the case's own `reported_by_user_id`, and a board president who tries to decide the case they themselves reported is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. That comparison is band 4's and is settled as soon as the case resolves — ahead of the missing-`sanction_type` complaint and ahead of the terminal and edge tests alike — so the reporter attempting to sanction their own case with no `sanction_type` in the payload, or against a case already `CLOSED`, still reads as `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The `sanction_type` complaint sits immediately behind it and still ahead of the state tests, so a `SANCTIONED` request carrying no `sanction_type` is `ERR_VALIDATION` even where the case is terminal or the edge does not exist. A `to_status` that is not a declared outgoing edge from the case's current status is `ERR_INVALID_TRANSITION`, and a case already at `DISMISSED` or `CLOSED` — the two terminal states — is `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`.

Appeal is the household's own remedy and the reason the case machine is a cycle rather than a line. From `SANCTIONED` the charged `Member` — and only the charged household — may move the case to `APPEALED`, which increments `appeal_count` by one and leaves every other field, including `points_assessed` and `sanctioned_at`, exactly as the sanction left them. Two conditions bound the remedy: `appeal_count` must be below two, so a household gets at most two appeals of the same case, and the logical clock must be at or before ten days after `sanctioned_at`, the boundary being inclusive so an appeal filed at exactly the tenth day is accepted. A third appeal, or one filed past the window, is not an available edge and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TRANSITION`. Accepting the appeal for re-hearing moves the case `APPEALED → UNDER_REVIEW`, and this is the one edge that gives points back: `points_assessed` is reset to 0 and the household's `conduct_points` are recomputed, which can drop the household below the suspension threshold. Standing is emphatically **not** restored by that drop — a re-hearing reopens the question, it does not reopen the pool — and the household stays suspended until the board reinstates it explicitly. The alternative ending is closure: from `SANCTIONED` a case moves to `CLOSED` once the logical clock is strictly past ten days after `sanctioned_at`, the appeal window having elapsed unused — closure attempted at or before that boundary is refused with `ERR_INVALID_TRANSITION`, the same identifier a premature appeal earns, because the club treats an unexpired window as an edge that does not yet exist — and closure deliberately leaves the case's points inside the lookback window, so closing a case is an administrative tidy rather than a pardon.

**How points accrue.** After any case transition that changes `points_assessed` — a sanction, a dismissal, or an appeal accepted for re-hearing — the charged household's `conduct_points` is recomputed from scratch rather than adjusted. The cutoff is the logical clock less seven hundred and thirty days, that is two years of incidents, and the sum runs over that household's cases whose `status` is `SANCTIONED` or `CLOSED` and whose `sanctioned_at` falls at or after the cutoff, adding the `points_assessed` of each. The boundary is inclusive, so a case sanctioned exactly seven hundred and thirty days ago still counts, and a case one day older has decayed out of the ledger entirely without any event ever being recorded against it. Cases in `REPORTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `APPEALED` or `DISMISSED` contribute zero. The point values are two for a `MINOR` incident, four for a `MAJOR` one and eight for a `SEVERE` one, all integers, so a single severe incident sits one point below the expulsion line and two minor incidents sit two points below suspension.

**How standing hardens.** Immediately after the recomputation, and only on a sanction, the household's `standing` is re-derived from three inputs at once. The sanction just handed down maps directly: `WARNING` to `GOOD_STANDING`, `PROBATION` to `PROBATION`, `SUSPENSION` to `SUSPENDED` and `EXPULSION` to `EXPELLED`. The recomputed point total maps independently: ten points or more means `EXPELLED`, six or more but fewer than ten means `SUSPENDED`, and anything below six means `GOOD_STANDING`, both comparisons inclusive so exactly six suspends and exactly ten expels. The household's existing `standing` is the third input. Ranking the four values as `GOOD_STANDING` below `PROBATION` below `SUSPENDED` below `EXPELLED`, the new standing is whichever of the three candidates ranks most severe. The rule is therefore monotone: a `WARNING` handed to a household already on `PROBATION` leaves it on `PROBATION`, a `MINOR` incident that carries a household's total to six suspends it even though the sanction was only a `WARNING`, and no sanction of any kind ever softens standing. Softening happens on exactly one path, described below.

**The cascade.** A sanction of `PROBATION` moves a household in `GOOD_STANDING` to `PROBATION` and does nothing else: probation is a warning with a memory, it recomputes `conduct_points`, it advances the household's `version`, and it leaves every membership, registration and booking alone. Suspension is where the consequence propagates, and it propagates identically whether the household arrives at `SUSPENDED` from `GOOD_STANDING` or from `PROBATION`. It propagates only on arrival: where the re-derivation lands on the standing the household already holds — a second `SUSPENSION` handed to a household already `SUSPENDED`, or any sanction the monotone rule cannot harden past where the household already sits — the standing does not move, no cascade runs at all, nothing beneath the household is reached a second time, and the household's `version` advances only if its recomputed `conduct_points` changed. Every membership of that household whose `access_status` is `ACTIVE` moves to `SUSPENDED`, processed in `created_at` ascending order so the household's oldest membership is dealt with first. Each such membership then takes every swim-team registration beneath it that is in `PENDING`, `CONFIRMED` or `WAITLISTED` to `REVOKED`, again in `created_at` ascending order, and each revoked registration releases its volunteer assignments, which lowers the affected meets' `volunteer_slots_filled` and can slide a meet `STAFFED → UNDERSTAFFED` exactly as described in §3.6. Each membership also takes every party reservation beneath it that is in `REQUESTED` or `CONFIRMED` and whose `start_iso` is strictly later than the logical clock to `CANCELLED_BY_SUSPENSION`, processed in `start_iso` ascending order and, for reservations sharing a start instant, `created_at` ascending; a party already under way or already past is left where it stands. Version counters advance at every hop of the chain. This is the same cascade the delinquency write-off of §3.4 fires, which is why a household can lose its squad places to unpaid dues as surely as to misconduct.

Expulsion is suspension's terminal form and reaches the same objects one step further. From `GOOD_STANDING`, from `PROBATION` or from `SUSPENDED`, an `EXPULSION` sanction takes every membership of the household whose `access_status` is `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` to `TERMINATED` in `created_at` ascending order — a membership arriving from `ACTIVE` runs the full suspension cascade over its registrations and future reservations on the way through, so an expelled household's swimmers are revoked and its parties cancelled exactly as a suspension would have done, and only then is the membership terminated. Expulsion additionally clears the household out of the queue: every waitlist entry of that household in `WAITING` or `OFFERED` moves to `WITHDRAWN`, which is why an expelled household cannot lurk at the head of a category's queue waiting for the ban to be forgotten. Termination is fiscally silent and fiscally final: `balance_due_cents` is left untouched, so an expelled household still owes what it owed, and the bond is forfeited with `bond_status` left at whatever value it held, so a `PAID` bond reads `PAID` forever and is simply never returned. `EXPELLED` is the household's only terminal standing; there is no path back for an expelled household, and its memberships can go no further than `TERMINATED`.

**Reinstatement.** The single path that softens a standing belongs to the `BoardPresident`, who names the `to_standing` the household is to hold and a `reason` for the record. The conduct pipeline can only harden, so this operation exists to undo what it hardened: a household at `SUSPENDED` returns to `GOOD_STANDING`, and the board may equally place it at `PROBATION` rather than fully clearing it. Reinstatement to `GOOD_STANDING` is refused while any case against that household is still live — that is, while any of its cases sits in `REPORTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `APPEALED` — because a household with an open hearing has not yet earned its standing back; the board must first dismiss or decide the case. That refusal is `ERR_INVALID_TRANSITION`, as is any `to_standing` that fails to soften the household's standing strictly — the target must rank strictly less severe than where the household currently sits, so `SUSPENDED → SUSPENDED` and every hardening move are refused here — while a household already `EXPELLED` answers `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` whatever target is named. When the transition takes, every membership of the household whose `access_status` is `SUSPENDED` returns to `ACTIVE`, processed in `created_at` ascending order, and the household's `version` advances. Reinstatement reopens facility access and nothing more: revoked swim-team registrations are **not** restored, released volunteer assignments are **not** restored, and cancelled party reservations are **not** revived. A reinstated household that wants its swimmers back on a squad registers them again, at the back of whatever `squad_seq` order and whatever remaining capacity the squad now has, and re-earns its volunteer credits from zero. Standing governs the front door everywhere else in the club: a household at `SUSPENDED` or `EXPELLED` may not join a waitlist, be sold a membership, sponsor a guest, register a swimmer or book a party, all of which report `ERR_HOUSEHOLD_SUSPENDED`. At season close, suspended and active memberships alike fall to `LAPSED`, so a suspension that is never lifted simply expires with the season rather than following the household into the next year — though its conduct points, which live on the household and not the membership, follow it for two full years.

### 3.10. The Capital Reserve: Resurfacing, Pumps and the Two-Counter Identity

A neighborhood pool is a depreciating asset, and the club funds its resurfacing and its pump replacements out of a reserve carried on the season rather than out of the operating dues. The reserve is tracked with two integer counters, and the whole of §3.10 is really one invariant restated from several directions: `reserve_unallocated_cents` plus the sum of `funded_cents` over the season's capital projects in `FUNDED`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `COMPLETED` always equals `reserve_contributions_cents`. Projects in `PROPOSED`, `APPROVED` or `REJECTED` contribute nothing to the allocated side because they hold no money, and a `CANCELLED` project contributes nothing because its `funded_cents` has been zeroed, so the identity holds arithmetically for every project in the system without special-casing any status. All reserve arithmetic is in whole cents with no rounding anywhere, which is what lets the identity hold exactly rather than nearly.

Money enters the reserve two ways. The `Treasurer` may book a lump special assessment against the season by naming an `amount_cents` and, optionally, a `note` explaining what the assessment is for; both `reserve_contributions_cents` and `reserve_unallocated_cents` rise by that amount and the season's `version` advances; the season's own lifecycle `status` does not gate the booking, so a lump assessment may be recorded against a `PLANNED`, an `OPEN` or a `CLOSED` season alike. The other way is automatic and per household: the first time a membership's `dues_status` reaches `PAID`, five thousand cents — fifty dollars of every fully-paid membership — is booked to the season's reserve, raising both counters by that amount, as described in §3.4. That contribution is booked exactly once per membership, so a membership that pays, is written off, and somehow pays again never contributes twice. `reserve_contributions_cents` is append-only in the strictest sense: it never decreases. A draw does not reduce it, a project cancellation does not reduce it, and writing off a delinquent membership's dues does not claw back a contribution already booked from that membership. It is a lifetime total, and the club's history of capital saving is not rewritten by later events.

A project begins as a proposal from the `Treasurer`, naming the `funding_season_id` whose reserve will pay for it, a `project_kind` of `POOL_RESURFACING` or `PUMP_REPLACEMENT`, a `title`, and a `budget_cents` of at least one cent that stands as the board-authorised ceiling on cumulative draws. It opens at `PROPOSED` with `funded_cents` at 0 and the proposer's identity in `requested_by_user_id`. From `PROPOSED` the `BoardPresident` either approves the project — which records their own `X-User-ID` in `approved_by_user_id` — or rejects it, and both decisions are under the same two-person control that governs conduct sanctions: the deciding user may not be the `requested_by_user_id`, and a treasurer-turned-president approving their own proposal is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. That answer survives the fact that approving and rejecting are the board's edges rather than the treasurer's: the `Treasurer` is on the transition endpoint's authorised role list, so it clears the endpoint authorisation of §2.9 band 2, and on an `APPROVED` or `REJECTED` request the two-person control is settled before the narrowing that reserves those two edges for the `BoardPresident`. A `Treasurer` who is *not* the proposer, and any other caller the edge does not belong to, is still `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`. Once work is funded the `Treasurer` starts it, moving `FUNDED → IN_PROGRESS`, which requires `funded_cents` to be strictly greater than zero because no contractor starts on an unfunded authorisation — a start attempted against a `FUNDED` project holding nothing is `ERR_INVALID_TRANSITION`, the same identifier the edge itself would raise, so the emptiness of the project reads as a missing edge rather than as a fiscal complaint — and later completes it with `IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED`. Projects are listed and filtered by `funding_season_id`, `project_kind` and `status`.

**Drawing on the reserve.** A draw is a separate operation from the lifecycle moves, belongs to the `Treasurer`, and carries two required inputs and one optional one: the `draw_amount_cents` being taken, which may be 0, an `expected_capital_project_version` that must equal the project's current `version`, failing which the draw is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PROJECT_VERSION`, and an optional `season_id` naming the reserve the caller believes is being charged. The season actually charged is always the project's own `funding_season_id` and never the one in the payload: `season_id` is a cross-check and nothing more, compared when it is supplied and ignored entirely when it is omitted, so a draw that names no season is perfectly well formed. The version guard exists because successive draws against the same project are entirely legitimate — no state precondition blocks a second draw, since the operation is available from `APPROVED` and from `FUNDED` alike, though a project still `PROPOSED` has no authorisation to draw against and is `ERR_INVALID_TRANSITION`, and one already `COMPLETED`, `REJECTED` or `CANCELLED` is `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`, both of those tested after the version guard and not before it — so nothing but the version check stands between a client working from a stale `funded_cents` figure and an over-drawn reserve. Three further conditions apply in ascending order of leniency about money: any `season_id` the payload does name must be the project's `funding_season_id` (`ERR_SEASON_MISMATCH`), the `draw_amount_cents` may not exceed the season's `reserve_unallocated_cents` (`ERR_INSUFFICIENT_RESERVE`), and the project's `funded_cents` plus the `draw_amount_cents` may not exceed its `budget_cents` (`ERR_DRAW_EXCEEDS_BUDGET`). A draw that passes raises `funded_cents` by the drawn amount and lowers `reserve_unallocated_cents` by the same amount while leaving `reserve_contributions_cents` untouched — money moves across the identity, it is not created or destroyed — and advances both the project's `version` and the season's `version`. The first successful draw against an `APPROVED` project also moves it to `FUNDED`; subsequent draws leave it there. A draw of 0 cents is a legal draw: it satisfies all three money conditions trivially, moves nothing across the identity, and still promotes an `APPROVED` project to `FUNDED` and advances both the project's and the season's `version` — which is the one way a `FUNDED` project comes to hold no cents at all.

Cancellation is the compensating action for the draw and must restore the identity exactly. The `BoardPresident` may cancel a project from `APPROVED`, from `FUNDED` or from `IN_PROGRESS`; every cent the project holds returns to `reserve_unallocated_cents`, `funded_cents` is set to 0, `reserve_contributions_cents` is again untouched, and the project's `version` advances. The season's `version` advances only where cents actually returned: a cancellation that gives nothing back leaves the season's counters and its `version` exactly as they stood, because nothing about the season changed. Cancelling an `APPROVED` project that never drew anything is therefore a no-op on the money and on the season alike, and a real transition on the status. `COMPLETED`, `REJECTED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal, and a completed project keeps its `funded_cents` on the allocated side of the identity permanently, because the money was genuinely spent on the pool. A capital project is never touched by season close, facility closure, or any conduct or fiscal cascade: the reserve is the one ledger in the club that outlives the swimming.

Creating a brand-new resource returns 201 Created; every other successful call — state transitions, payments, checkpoint runs, reserve bookings, computed reads, list and single reads, and the clock-setting call — returns 200 OK. Request payload fields, response payloads, derived figures, ordering rules and the conditions under which each error is raised live in the §2 and §3 narratives above, and the error identifiers themselves are catalogued in §5.
