# Nonprofit Social Enterprise & Transitional Employment API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A social enterprise is two businesses wearing one coat. On the outside it is a cafe, a print shop or a landscaping crew that must hit quality, cost and customer-service standards with a workforce that is, by design, still learning. On the inside it is a workforce-development program that recruits people facing serious employment barriers, pays them a transitional wage, wraps job coaching and case management around the work, and measures itself on how many of them reach — and keep — an outside job. The tension between those two identities is not a bug to be engineered away; it is the entire point. This API exists to make that tension auditable.

The system described here governs the full arc of a transitional-employment program: selecting which business lines are worth trading in at all, recruiting candidates through community referral partners and screening them against eligibility criteria and verified barriers, seating trainees on real trading shifts under real supervision limits, layering coaching and support services onto the work, splitting every dollar of cost between mission subsidy and business operations so that funders and the board see the same true cost, placing graduates with outside employers, and following each placement at 30, 90, 180 and 365 days to produce retention numbers a funder will accept.

Two design commitments run through every requirement below. First, **money and hours must reconcile to the cent and to the third decimal of an hour** — the subsidy ledger, the shift calendar and the retention clock all read each other's derived fields, so a single period close legitimately moves five different entities, and the order in which it moves them is part of the specification rather than an implementation detail. Second, **the program cannot grade its own homework**: the job coach who certifies a participant ready to graduate may not be the case manager who graduates them, employers rather than staff attest to post-placement wages, and every rejection carries a specific machine-readable code so that a funder audit can replay exactly why an action was refused.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section fixes the contract-level behaviour that applies to every endpoint: authentication, envelope shape, pagination, identity and timestamps, the logical clock, the order in which rules are evaluated, numeric precision, version guards, interval arithmetic, and the cascade discipline that keeps derived fields honest.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which they act. Seven roles exist, and their names are spelled exactly as follows: `ProgramDirector`, `IntakeSpecialist`, `JobCoach`, `CaseManager`, `FunderAnalyst`, `ReferralPartner`, and `EmployerPartner`. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of those seven strings, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If both headers are well-formed but the declared role is not authorized for the endpoint being called (see the authorization column in §4), the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

Five of the seven roles are **operator roles** whose identity is opaque. When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramDirector`, `IntakeSpecialist`, `JobCoach`, `CaseManager`, or `FunderAnalyst`, the value of `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — for example as `decided_by_user_id` on a `Referral` when an `IntakeSpecialist` records a screening decision, as `readiness_certified_by_user_id` on a `Participant` when a `JobCoach` certifies readiness, as `delivered_by_user_id` on a `SupportActivity` when a `JobCoach` marks it delivered, or as `supervisor_user_id` when a shift is scheduled — but it never compares it to any entity's `id`.

The remaining two roles are **entity-backed** and therefore carry ownership scoping. When `X-User-Role` is `ReferralPartner`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `ReferralPartner` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a referral partner compare the resource's `referral_partner_id` to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `EmployerPartner`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Employer` row; ownership checks on a `Placement` compare the placement's `employer_id` to `X-User-ID`, and ownership checks on a `RetentionCheckpoint` compare the `employer_id` of the checkpoint's parent placement to `X-User-ID`. An entity-backed role reaching a resource it does not own is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). This ownership check guards exactly five operations: referral submission, single-referral read, placement separation, placement wage verification, and retention-checkpoint confirmation.

`FunderAnalyst` is strictly read-only and appears in no mutating operation anywhere in the system. Segregation of duties is enforced within the program roles as well: a `JobCoach` may certify graduation readiness but may never graduate a participant, and a `CaseManager` may graduate a participant but only if that case manager's `X-User-ID` is not the user recorded as having certified the readiness.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints are paginated through two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of items returned; it defaults to 10, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` is the number of items to skip and defaults to 0. Values outside the permitted range are a request-shape failure and are rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list responses are sorted by `created_at` in ascending order. The single documented exception is the retention-checkpoint listing for a placement, which is ordered by `interval_days` ascending — an ordering that happens to coincide with creation order because the four checkpoints are created in ascending interval order.

Every paginated response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope, where `total_count` is the number of matching rows before pagination is applied.

Collections also accept equality filters, all optional and all combinable with pagination. Referrals may be filtered by `referral_partner_id` and `status`. Participants may be filtered by `business_line_id`, `referral_id`, and `status`. Work shifts may be filtered by `business_line_id` and `status`. Shift assignments may be filtered by `participant_id`, `work_shift_id`, and `status`. Support activities may be filtered by `participant_id`, `status`, and `category`. Placements may be filtered by `participant_id`, `employer_id`, and `employment_status`. The retention-checkpoint listing for a placement accepts `status`. Subsidy periods may be filtered by `business_line_id` and `status`. Cost entries may be filtered by `subsidy_period_id`, `source_activity_id`, `classification`, and `status`.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Versions

All twelve persisted resource types in this system — business lines, referral partners, referrals, participants, work shifts, shift assignments, support activities, employers, placements, retention checkpoints, subsidy periods and cost entries — carry the same four system-managed properties:

- **`id`** (string): a server-minted UUID. It carries no type prefix and no domain-flavoured formatting; a caller must never construct one.
- **`created_at`** (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, recording when the row was first written, taken from the logical clock described in §2.5.
- **`updated_at`** (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, refreshed on every mutation of the row, including mutations that only change derived fields.
- **`version`** (integer): a counter that increments by one on every mutation of the row. It is the value clients quote back in the version guards of §2.9.

No entity in this system opts out of `updated_at` or `version`; every row has all four properties. A recomputation of a derived field counts as a mutation: when a shift completion causes a business line's `quality_incident_count` and `intake_posture` to be re-derived, that business line's `updated_at` is refreshed. Where a state machine or a cascade explicitly states that a particular entity's `version` is incremented, that increment is part of the contract and a test may read it; the transitions and cascades in §3 name those increments individually.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

Nothing in this system reads the wall clock. Every deadline, ISO-week window, accounting-period boundary, retention due date, rest buffer and grace period is evaluated against a server-held **logical clock**. Before any client sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time.

A `ProgramDirector` moves the clock by submitting `to_iso8601`, an ISO 8601 UTC instant; the response reports the resulting clock as `current_utc`. Setting the clock mutates no entity and triggers no sweep — advancing time does not by itself expire a checkpoint or exit a participant. Time-driven state changes happen only when the ops-run sweep described in §3.8 is invoked, which is what makes the program's epoch advance observable and replayable.

### 2.6. The Rejection Ladder

When a request would fail more than one rule, exactly one error is returned, and which one is not a matter of taste. Rules are evaluated strictly in the order below, and the **first** failing rule decides the response; no later rule is evaluated, and no state is written. This ordering matters in practice: a case manager who tries to graduate a participant using the same operator identity that certified readiness, on a participant who is not in the certified state at all, sees the state error rather than the segregation-of-duties error, because state precedes safety on the ladder.

1. **Authentication.** Both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` present, and `X-User-Role` one of the seven declared roles — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Role authorization.** The acting role appears in the endpoint's authorized-role list — otherwise `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Existence.** Every identifier the request references must resolve to a row. Path identifiers are checked first, left to right, then body foreign keys in the order they are declared in the endpoint's input list. The failure carries the code for the missing type: `ERR_BUSINESS_LINE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REFERRAL_PARTNER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REFERRAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PARTICIPANT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WORK_SHIFT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SHIFT_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SUPPORT_ACTIVITY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EMPLOYER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PLACEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RETENTION_CHECKPOINT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SUBSIDY_PERIOD_NOT_FOUND`, or `ERR_COST_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`, each with HTTP 404. Applies to every endpoint carrying a path identifier or a foreign key in its body.
4. **Ownership.** For the two entity-backed roles, `X-User-ID` must equal the owning entity id of the target resource — otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). Applies to referral submission, single-referral read, placement separation, placement wage verification, and retention-checkpoint confirmation.
5. **Request shape.** The body conforms to the endpoint's input schema: required fields present, enum members drawn from the declared sets, integers inside their declared ranges, datetimes valid ISO 8601 UTC, a shift's `ends_at` strictly after its `starts_at`, a period's `period_end` strictly after its `period_start`, `duration_minutes` exactly 0 for the two voucher categories and strictly greater than 0 for the two contact categories, `actual_hours` strictly greater than 0, a `REJECTED` review decision accompanied by a `rejection_reason`, and a `VERIFY` wage-verification action accompanied by a `verified_wage_cents_per_hour`. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (422). Applies to every endpoint.
6. **Declared transition.** The target entity is in a state from which the requested transition is declared — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (409). Applies to every action endpoint: review, launch, screen, certify-readiness, graduate, re-enter, deliver, void, wage-verification, separate, confirm, and close.
7. **Shift still schedulable.** The work shift behind a shift-assignment create, complete or release is in `SCHEDULED` status — otherwise `ERR_SHIFT_NOT_SCHEDULED` (409). Applies to those three operations.
8. **Period still open.** Any subsidy period a cost movement would touch is in `OPEN` status, whether the movement is a direct manual post or the compensating void of a delivered support activity — otherwise `ERR_PERIOD_CLOSED` (409). Applies to manual cost posting and support-activity voiding.
9. **Version guard.** The supplied expected-version value equals the parent's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_BUSINESS_LINE_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_SUBSIDY_PERIOD_VERSION` (412). Applies to participant enrollment and manual cost posting; see §2.9.
10. **Segregation of duties.** The case manager graduating a participant is not the user who certified readiness — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (409). Applies to graduation.
11. **Graduation readiness criteria.** All three readiness thresholds are met — otherwise `ERR_GRADUATION_CRITERIA_NOT_MET` (422). Applies to readiness certification.
12. **Viability gate.** An `APPROVED` review decision clears both the composite floor and the per-dimension floor — otherwise `ERR_VIABILITY_GATE_NOT_MET` (422). Applies to business-line review.
13. **Eligibility floor.** An `ELIGIBLE` screening decision clears the verified-barrier, availability and score floors — otherwise `ERR_ELIGIBILITY_SCORE_BELOW_FLOOR` (422). Applies to referral screening.
14. **Checkpoint due.** The logical clock is at or after the checkpoint's `due_date` — otherwise `ERR_CHECKPOINT_NOT_DUE` (409). Applies to checkpoint confirmation.
15. **Period ended.** The logical clock is strictly after the period's `period_end` — otherwise `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_ENDED` (409). Applies to period close.
16. **Hire date not before graduation.** A placement's `hire_date` is at or after the participant's `graduated_at` — otherwise `ERR_HIRE_DATE_BEFORE_GRADUATION` (422). Applies to placement creation.
17. **Shift started.** The logical clock is at or after the shift's `starts_at` when an assignment is completed — otherwise `ERR_SHIFT_NOT_STARTED` (409). Applies to assignment completion.
18. **Partner MOU in force.** The referring partner's `mou_status` is `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_PARTNER_MOU_LAPSED` (409). Applies to referral submission and referral screening.
19. **Intake open.** The business line's `status` is `LAUNCHED` and its `intake_posture` is `OPEN` — otherwise `ERR_INTAKE_CLOSED` (409). Applies to participant enrollment and participant re-entry.
20. **Employer active.** The employer's `partnership_status` is `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_EMPLOYER_NOT_ACTIVE` (409). Applies to placement creation.
21. **An open period exists.** The open-period selector of §3.9 returns a period for the anchor instant — otherwise `ERR_NO_OPEN_SUBSIDY_PERIOD` (409). Applies to assignment completion and support-activity delivery.
22. **Revenue available.** A revenue-basis true-cost analysis is only answerable when the period actually has `revenue_cents` — otherwise `ERR_REVENUE_NOT_AVAILABLE` (409). Applies to the true-cost analysis read.
23. **Candidate reference unique.** The pair of `referral_partner_id` and `candidate_reference` does not already exist on another referral — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_CANDIDATE_REFERENCE` (409). Applies to referral submission.
24. **Referral not already consumed.** No participant row already references the supplied `referral_id` — otherwise `ERR_REFERRAL_ALREADY_ENROLLED` (409). Applies to participant enrollment.
25. **Re-entry limit.** The participant's `re_entry_count` is strictly below the re-entry limit of two — otherwise `ERR_RE_ENTRY_LIMIT_REACHED` (409). Applies to participant re-entry.
26. **No duplicate seat.** No assignment in `ASSIGNED` or `COMPLETED` status already links this participant to this shift — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_SHIFT_ASSIGNMENT` (409). Applies to seating.
27. **Participant rest buffer.** The buffered pairwise exclusion of §2.10 holds — otherwise `ERR_PARTICIPANT_REST_BUFFER_CONFLICT` (409). Applies to seating.
28. **Line-wide supervision capacity.** The concurrent-seat ceiling of §2.10 holds — otherwise `ERR_SUPERVISION_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409). Applies to seating.
29. **Seats remaining.** The shift's `assigned_count` is strictly below its `trainee_capacity` — otherwise `ERR_SHIFT_CAPACITY_FULL` (409). Applies to seating.
30. **Checkpoint sequence.** Every checkpoint of the same placement with a smaller `interval_days` is already in `CONFIRMED` or `VOIDED` status — otherwise `ERR_CHECKPOINT_OUT_OF_SEQUENCE` (409). Applies to checkpoint confirmation.
31. **Period non-overlap.** The new accounting period's closed interval does not intersect any existing period of the same line — otherwise `ERR_SUBSIDY_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (409). Applies to period creation.
32. **No open seats blocking close.** No assignment in `ASSIGNED` status remains on a shift of this line whose `starts_at` falls inside the period interval — otherwise `ERR_OPEN_ASSIGNMENTS_BLOCK_CLOSE` (409). Applies to period close.
33. **Manually postable category.** A manual cost entry uses one of `SUPERVISION`, `MATERIALS` or `OVERHEAD` — otherwise `ERR_CATEGORY_NOT_MANUALLY_POSTABLE` (422). Applies to manual cost posting.
34. **Hours within shift plus grace.** `actual_hours` is at most the shift's `duration_hours` plus the half-hour grace allowance — otherwise `ERR_ACTUAL_HOURS_EXCEED_SHIFT` (422). Applies to assignment completion.
35. **Trainee wage band.** `wage_cents_per_hour` is at least 1500 and at most 2200 — otherwise `ERR_TRAINEE_WAGE_OUT_OF_RANGE` (422). Applies to participant enrollment and participant re-entry.
36. **Employer wage floor.** `starting_wage_cents_per_hour` is at least the employer's `min_starting_wage_cents_per_hour` — otherwise `ERR_WAGE_BELOW_EMPLOYER_FLOOR` (422). Applies to placement creation.
37. **Wage-progression floor.** `starting_wage_cents_per_hour` is at least the participant's frozen graduation wage uplifted by ten percent — otherwise `ERR_WAGE_PROGRESSION_FLOOR_NOT_MET` (422). Applies to placement creation.
38. **Weekly hours cap.** The participant's already-committed hours in the ISO week of the new shift plus the new shift's `duration_hours` stay within the weekly cap — otherwise `ERR_WEEKLY_HOURS_CAP_EXCEEDED` (409). Applies to seating.
39. **Partner quota.** The partner's referral count inside the trailing 30-day window is strictly below `referral_quota_per_period` — otherwise `ERR_PARTNER_QUOTA_EXCEEDED` (409). Applies to referral submission.

Note two consequences of this ordering that are easy to get wrong. Because request-shape validation (rung 5) precedes the declared-transition check (rung 6), a malformed body on a terminal entity yields `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. And because the version guard (rung 9) precedes every business rule, a client holding a stale business-line version learns that fact before it learns that intake had closed.

### 2.7. Path and Reference Resolution

Path identifiers are resolved left to right, and the first one that fails to resolve determines the 404 that comes back. The identifiers that appear in paths are `businessLineId`, `referralPartnerId`, `referralId`, `participantId`, `workShiftId`, `shiftAssignmentId`, `supportActivityId`, `employerId`, `placementId`, `retentionCheckpointId`, `subsidyPeriodId`, and `costEntryId`; the retention-checkpoint listing nested under a placement uses the shorter path variable `id` for the placement it belongs to. Only that one listing is nested; every other collection is mounted as a flat top-level path.

Body foreign keys are resolved after all path identifiers, in the declaration order of the endpoint's input list. A cost-entry post that names both a non-existent `subsidy_period_id` and an unusable category therefore reports `ERR_SUBSIDY_PERIOD_NOT_FOUND` and not `ERR_CATEGORY_NOT_MANUALLY_POSTABLE`. Filter values behave the same way where they are identifiers: a cost-entry listing filtered by a `source_activity_id` that matches no support activity reports `ERR_SUPPORT_ACTIVITY_NOT_FOUND`.

### 2.8. Numeric Precision and Rounding

Every rounding operation in this system uses **round half up**, and every rounding operation happens at the decimal place named below. Where a formula rounds at more than one step, the number of rounding steps is part of the specification: collapsing two roundings into one produces a different, wrong answer.

- **Money** is always integer cents. There are no fractional cents anywhere in the persisted model. `amount_cents`, `wage_cost_cents`, `billable_labor_cost_cents`, `allocated_mission_cents`, `allocated_business_cents`, `mission_cost_cents`, `business_operating_cost_cents`, `total_cost_cents`, `subsidy_draw_cents`, `cumulative_subsidy_cents`, `subsidy_cap_cents`, `household_income_cents`, `cost_cents`, `revenue_cents`, `wage_cents_per_hour`, `graduation_wage_cents_per_hour`, `starting_wage_cents_per_hour`, `min_starting_wage_cents_per_hour` and `verified_wage_cents_per_hour` are all integers.
- **Two per-hour cost ratios** — `true_cost_per_trainee_hour_cents` and `subsidy_per_trainee_hour_cents` — are numbers carrying 2 decimal places.
- **Hours** carry 3 decimal places: `duration_hours`, `actual_hours`, `transitional_hours_worked`, `scheduled_hours_current_week`, `trainee_hours`, and `supervisor_hours`.
- **Scores** carry 2 decimal places: `composite_viability_score` and `eligibility_score`.
- **Rates, shares and ratios** carry 4 decimal places: `fpl_ratio`, `conversion_rate`, `mission_share`, `margin_pct`, `wage_progression_pct`, `retention_reliability_pct`, `target_gross_margin_pct`, `placement_rate`, `retention_rate`, and `average_wage_progression_pct`.
- **`subsidy_rate`** is one of exactly three tier values with 2 decimal places: 0.50, 0.70 or 0.90.
- **Counts** are integers throughout, and the boundary comparisons that use them are stated explicitly wherever they appear.

A ratio whose denominator is zero is reported as `null`, never as zero and never as an error. This applies to `conversion_rate` when a partner has submitted nothing, to `retention_reliability_pct` when an employer has no resolved retention outcomes, to both per-hour cost ratios when a period recorded no completed trainee hours, and to all three funder-reporting rates when their respective denominators are empty.

### 2.9. Version Guards on Enrollment and Ledger Posting

Two write operations require the client to prove it is acting on a state of the world it has actually observed, because in both cases the write would otherwise succeed quietly while silently invalidating data the client had already read.

**Participant enrollment** requires `expected_business_line_version` as a non-optional input field, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target business line. If that value does not match the business line's current `version`, the enrollment is rejected with `ERR_STALE_BUSINESS_LINE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The guard exists precisely because enrollment against a line with free slots legitimately succeeds for any number of distinct referrals: no state precondition would catch a client whose picture of the line's slot ledger has moved on, so the version comparison is the only thing standing between that client and an enrollment it never intended.

**Manual cost posting** requires `expected_subsidy_period_version` as a non-optional input field, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target subsidy period. If that value does not match the period's current `version`, the post is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SUBSIDY_PERIOD_VERSION` (HTTP 412). An open period accepts an unlimited number of posted entries, so this request too would otherwise succeed; the guard is there because every accepted entry re-derives the period's `mission_share` and therefore silently re-allocates every other shared entry the client had already read.

No other endpoint takes a version field. Endpoints that are not named above must not require one and must not reject a request for supplying one out of date, because they do not read one.

### 2.10. Interval Conventions

Three different interval semantics coexist in this system, and confusing them is the single most common source of wrong answers.

**Work shifts are half-open.** A shift occupies the interval from `starts_at` inclusive to `ends_at` exclusive. `ends_at` must be strictly after `starts_at` and at most twelve hours later. Two shifts that merely touch — where one ends at exactly the instant the other begins — do not overlap and never contend for supervision capacity. A shift's `duration_hours` is the elapsed time from `starts_at` to `ends_at` expressed in hours to 3 decimal places.

**The participant rest buffer is a buffered pairwise exclusion.** When seating a trainee, the system looks at every other assignment of that same participant in `ASSIGNED` or `COMPLETED` status and inflates the other shift's interval by 60 minutes on **both** sides. The new seating is rejected when the new shift's `starts_at` is earlier than the other shift's `ends_at` plus 60 minutes **and** the other shift's `starts_at` is earlier than the new shift's `ends_at` plus 60 minutes. A gap of exactly 60 minutes between two shifts is therefore accepted, a gap of 59 minutes is rejected, and back-to-back shifts with no gap at all are rejected.

**Line-wide supervision capacity is an aggregate concurrency ceiling with no buffer.** For every instant inside the new shift's half-open interval, the number of `ASSIGNED` seats across **all** `SCHEDULED` shifts of the same business line whose half-open intervals contain that instant must remain at or below the line's `supervisor_capacity`. Adjacency is explicitly allowed here: touching shifts never contend, because the shared instant belongs to only one of the two half-open intervals.

**Accounting periods are closed on both ends.** A subsidy period covers `period_start` through `period_end` inclusive, both of which sit at `00:00:00Z` — `period_end` is midnight at the start of the final day. Periods of the same business line may never intersect, whatever their status: a new period is rejected when its `period_start` is at or before an existing period's `period_end` and that existing period's `period_start` is at or before the new period's `period_end`. Abutting periods must therefore begin at least one day after the previous period's `period_end`.

**Retention dates live on a whole-day grid.** A `hire_date` is supplied at `00:00:00Z`, and each checkpoint's `due_date` is that instant plus a whole number of days, also landing on `00:00:00Z`. `separation_days_after_hire` is the count of whole days between `hire_date` and `separation_date`.

**ISO weeks run Monday to Sunday in UTC.** The week window used for weekly hour accounting starts at Monday `00:00:00Z` of the ISO week containing the logical clock's current instant and runs to the same instant seven days later, exclusive.

### 2.11. Cascade Discipline

Many operations in this system move more than one entity. When they do, three things are guaranteed. First, the cascade is **immediate and complete within the request**: there is no deferred reconciliation, and a read issued after the response reflects every hop. Second, the cascade is **ordered deterministically** — wherever a cascade touches a set of rows, the processing order is stated with the transition that owns it, and secondary sort keys are given so the order is total. Third, a cascade that cannot complete does not partially complete: when a precondition fails anywhere in the chain, the whole request is rejected and nothing is written.

Derived fields are never client-supplied. Slot counts, quality counters, intake posture, participant progress metrics, partner conversion, employer retention counters, ledger allocations, true-cost ratios and every rate in the funder report are recomputed from their inputs whenever an input changes, and a recomputation refreshes the owning row's `updated_at` exactly as any other mutation does. Where a specific cascade hop increments a specific entity's `version`, §3 says so at the point the hop is described; hops that only refresh derived values on the same row do not additionally bump a foreign row's `version` unless the text says they do.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Selecting and Launching a Business Line

Everything begins with the question of what business the enterprise should be in. A `ProgramDirector` proposes a candidate line by supplying its trading `name` (between 1 and 120 characters) together with the three findings of the feasibility study: `market_viability_score`, `training_relevance_score`, and `mission_alignment_score`, each an integer from 0 through 100 measuring, respectively, whether the market will pay, whether the work teaches transferable skills, and whether the line fits the nonprofit's mission and target population. The same request declares the operating envelope: `training_slots_total`, the number of funded transitional-employment slots on the line (at least 1); `supervisor_capacity`, the maximum number of trainees one supervisor can cover at any single instant across all shifts of the line (at least 1); `target_gross_margin_pct`, the board-approved gross-margin target for the trading operation, a number from 0.0000 through 0.9000; and `subsidy_cap_cents`, the lifetime mission-subsidy ceiling for the line, a non-negative integer whose breach will later suspend the line outright.

On creation the system derives the line's **composite viability score** and stores it as `composite_viability_score`: forty percent of `market_viability_score`, plus thirty-five percent of `training_relevance_score`, plus twenty-five percent of `mission_alignment_score`, with the sum rounded half up to two decimal places. A newly created line begins in `DRAFT` `status` with `intake_posture` set to `CLOSED`, `training_slots_filled` at 0, `cumulative_subsidy_cents` at 0, `quality_incident_count` at 0, and both `rejection_reason` and `launched_at` null.

Review is a genuine fork, and the two branches are not symmetric. The `ProgramDirector` records a `decision` on a line still in `DRAFT`. On the approving branch, `DRAFT → APPROVED`, the viability gate is enforced: the line's `composite_viability_score` must be at least 70.00 **and** the smallest of the three individual dimension scores must be at least 50. A line can therefore clear the composite floor on the strength of two excellent dimensions and still be refused because of a single weak one, which is exactly the intent — the gate fails with `ERR_VIABILITY_GATE_NOT_MET`. Approval sets `status` to `APPROVED`, re-derives `intake_posture` (which stays `CLOSED`, because posture only opens for a launched line), increments the line's `version` by one, and writes no `rejection_reason`. On the rejecting branch, `DRAFT → REJECTED`, the viability gate is **not** evaluated at all; instead `rejection_reason` is mandatory and must be present and non-empty, a missing one being a request-shape failure. Rejection stores the supplied `rejection_reason`, leaves `intake_posture` at `CLOSED`, increments `version` by one, and is terminal: a rejected line never moves again.

Launching is the act that turns an approved line into a trading operation. The transition `APPROVED → LAUNCHED` requires the line to be in `APPROVED` status with `training_slots_total` at least 1 and `supervisor_capacity` at least 1. It sets `status` to `LAUNCHED`, stamps `launched_at` from the logical clock, increments `version` by one, and re-derives `intake_posture`, which now becomes `OPEN` provided `training_slots_filled` is strictly below `training_slots_total` and `quality_incident_count` is strictly below the quality-incident limit of ten.

**Intake posture** is the gate on new enrollment, and it is recomputed after every mutation of the line's `status`, `training_slots_filled` or `quality_incident_count` — that is, on review, on launch, on suspension, on every participant enrollment, graduation, re-entry and exit, and on every shift-assignment completion. Its inputs are counted narrowly. `training_slots_filled` is the number of participants on the line whose `status` is `TRANSITIONAL_EMPLOYED` or `READY_FOR_GRADUATION`; no other participant status occupies a slot. `quality_incident_count` is the sum of `quality_defect_count` plus `customer_complaint_count` across all `COMPLETED` shift assignments on shifts of the line; assignments still `ASSIGNED` or already `RELEASED` contribute nothing. `intake_posture` is `OPEN` when the line's `status` is `LAUNCHED` **and** `training_slots_filled` is strictly less than `training_slots_total` **and** `quality_incident_count` is strictly less than ten; in every other case it is `CLOSED`. Both comparisons are strict, so a line whose slots are exactly full closes intake, and a line that has accumulated exactly ten incidents closes intake. The five possible values of a line's `status` are `DRAFT`, `APPROVED`, `REJECTED`, `LAUNCHED` and `SUSPENDED`; `REJECTED` and `SUSPENDED` are terminal.

### 3.2. Referral Intake and Eligibility Screening

Candidates reach the program through community organizations. A `ProgramDirector` registers each one as a referral partner by supplying its `organization_name` (1 to 120 characters), its `mou_tier` — one of `TIER_1`, `TIER_2` or `TIER_3` — and its `referral_quota_per_period`, the maximum number of referrals it may submit in the trailing 30 logical-clock days (at least 1). The partner's `mou_status` may be supplied and defaults to `ACTIVE`; the only other permitted value is `LAPSED`. A freshly registered partner has `referrals_submitted_count` at 0, `enrolled_referral_count` at 0, and `conversion_rate` null.

Submission is the one operation a `ReferralPartner` performs, and it is ownership-scoped: the `referral_partner_id` in the body must equal the acting `X-User-ID`. Alongside it the partner supplies `candidate_reference`, its own handle for the candidate (1 to 64 characters), which must be unique for that partner — a repeat is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CANDIDATE_REFERENCE`. The partner also declares what the candidate has told them: `declared_barrier_codes`, between zero and seven distinct codes drawn from `JUSTICE_INVOLVED`, `HOUSING_INSTABILITY`, `LONG_TERM_UNEMPLOYED`, `DISABILITY`, `LIMITED_ENGLISH`, `SUBSTANCE_RECOVERY` and `YOUTH_AGING_OUT`; `household_income_cents`, gross monthly household income as a non-negative integer; `household_size`, at least 1; and `hours_available_per_week`, from 0 through 40. Two gates guard submission: a partner whose `mou_status` has gone `LAPSED` is refused with `ERR_PARTNER_MOU_LAPSED`, and a partner that has already submitted as many referrals as `referral_quota_per_period` within the trailing 30 logical-clock days is refused with `ERR_PARTNER_QUOTA_EXCEEDED` — the count considered is the referrals of that partner created strictly after the instant 30 days before the logical clock's current value, and the comparison against the quota is strict. A new referral lands in `SUBMITTED` `status` with `verified_barrier_codes` an empty list and `fpl_ratio`, `eligibility_score`, `priority_band`, `screened_at` and `decided_by_user_id` all null. Submission also refreshes the partner's `referrals_submitted_count` — which counts every referral of that partner regardless of status — and its `conversion_rate`.

**Screening** is where an `IntakeSpecialist` replaces what was declared with what was verified. The screening request carries a `decision` — one of `ELIGIBLE`, `WAITLISTED` or `INELIGIBLE` — together with `verified_barrier_codes`, `verified_household_income_cents`, `verified_household_size` and `verified_hours_available_per_week`. Those verified values **overwrite** the submitted ones on the referral row before anything is scored: `verified_barrier_codes` is stored as the confirmed subset of the barrier enumeration, and `household_income_cents`, `household_size` and `hours_available_per_week` take the verified figures. `declared_barrier_codes` is preserved as submitted and never enters the score. Whatever the decision, the referral's `screened_at` is stamped from the logical clock and `decided_by_user_id` records the acting operator's `X-User-ID`. Screening is refused for a partner whose MOU has lapsed, again with `ERR_PARTNER_MOU_LAPSED`.

The **eligibility score** is computed on the verified inputs before the decision branch is applied, in six steps. First, barrier points: count the distinct verified barrier codes, cap that count at three, and multiply by twelve — so one barrier scores 12, three barriers score 36, and five barriers also score 36. Second, the federal-poverty-level ratio. The annual poverty base depends on `household_size`: a household of one uses 1 509 000 cents, of two 2 043 000 cents, of three 2 577 000 cents, and of four 3 111 000 cents; each person beyond the fourth adds 534 000 cents to the four-person base. `fpl_ratio` is the verified monthly `household_income_cents` multiplied by twelve, divided by that annual base, rounded half up to four decimal places. Third, income points, chosen by the first matching band when read top to bottom: an `fpl_ratio` at or below 1.0000 scores 35; above 1.0000 and at or below 1.5000 scores 25; above 1.5000 and at or below 2.0000 scores 15; above 2.0000 scores 0. Fourth, availability points: divide `hours_available_per_week` by two, discard any fraction, and cap the result at twenty. Fifth, the partner multiplier, taken from the referring partner's `mou_tier`: `TIER_1` gives 1.10, `TIER_2` gives 1.00, and `TIER_3` gives 0.90. Sixth and last, `eligibility_score` is the sum of the three integer point components multiplied by that single multiplier, rounded half up to two decimal places. There is exactly one rounding at this stage: the multiplier is applied to the whole integer point sum and never to the components individually. `priority_band` follows from the rounded score — `BAND_A` at 70.00 or above, `BAND_B` from 50.00 up to but not including 70.00, and `BAND_C` below that.

Three branches leave `SUBMITTED`, and two of them may also be taken from `WAITLISTED`, which makes the referral lifecycle a branch with a revisit. The **eligible** branch, `SUBMITTED → ELIGIBLE` or `WAITLISTED → ELIGIBLE`, is the only one that enforces floors: it requires at least one verified barrier code, `hours_available_per_week` of at least 20, and an `eligibility_score` of at least 50.00. The floor comparison uses the **rounded** score, so a raw 49.995 that rounds to 50.00 passes while a raw 49.994 that rounds to 49.99 fails, and any of the three failures reports `ERR_ELIGIBILITY_SCORE_BELOW_FLOOR`. The **waitlist** branch, `SUBMITTED → WAITLISTED` or a re-screen that lands on `WAITLISTED` again, deliberately does **not** enforce the score floor: a below-floor candidate can be parked and re-screened once new evidence arrives, and re-screening simply overwrites `verified_barrier_codes`, the three verified inputs, all three derived scoring fields, `screened_at` and `decided_by_user_id`. The **ineligible** branch, `SUBMITTED → INELIGIBLE` or `WAITLISTED → INELIGIBLE`, enforces no floors either but is terminal; it still overwrites the verified inputs, recomputes `fpl_ratio`, `eligibility_score` and `priority_band`, and stamps `screened_at` and `decided_by_user_id`. A referral's `status` is therefore one of `SUBMITTED`, `WAITLISTED`, `ELIGIBLE`, `INELIGIBLE` or `ENROLLED`, the last two being terminal.

A partner's **conversion rate** is recomputed after every referral creation and after every enrollment. `referrals_submitted_count` counts all of the partner's referrals in any status; `enrolled_referral_count` counts only those whose `status` is `ENROLLED`. `conversion_rate` is `enrolled_referral_count` divided by `referrals_submitted_count`, rounded half up to four decimal places, and is `null` — never 0 — while the partner has submitted nothing at all.

### 3.3. Enrollment, Slot Occupancy and the Program Cycle

Enrollment is the moment the program takes responsibility for a person. An `IntakeSpecialist` enrolls by naming the `referral_id` of an `ELIGIBLE` referral and the `business_line_id` of the line to place them on, setting `wage_cents_per_hour` — the transitional wage the trading operation will pay, which must be at least 1500 and at most 2200 or the request is refused with `ERR_TRAINEE_WAGE_OUT_OF_RANGE` — and quoting `expected_business_line_version` as described in §2.9. Optionally the specialist may set `max_transitional_weeks`, the duration limit on this person's transitional employment, which defaults to 26 and must be between 8 and 52. Enrollment is refused when the line's `status` is not `LAUNCHED` or its `intake_posture` is `CLOSED`, with `ERR_INTAKE_CLOSED`, and refused when a participant already references that referral, with `ERR_REFERRAL_ALREADY_ENROLLED` — at most one participant exists per referral.

A successful enrollment writes a participant in `TRANSITIONAL_EMPLOYED` `status` with `enrolled_at` stamped from the logical clock, all four progress metrics at their zero values, `re_entry_count` at 0, and `readiness_certified_by_user_id`, `readiness_certified_at`, `graduated_at`, `graduation_wage_cents_per_hour` and `eligible_re_entry_at` all null. It simultaneously drives the referral `ELIGIBLE → ENROLLED`, which is terminal, and refreshes the referring partner's `enrolled_referral_count` and `conversion_rate`. On the business-line side, `training_slots_filled` and `intake_posture` are re-derived and the line's `version` is incremented by one.

A participant's `status` takes one of seven values — `TRANSITIONAL_EMPLOYED`, `READY_FOR_GRADUATION`, `GRADUATED`, `PLACED`, `RE_ENTRY_ELIGIBLE`, `ALUMNI_RETAINED`, `EXITED_UNSUCCESSFUL` — and the interesting thing about them is that they form a **cycle**, not a line. The happy path runs `TRANSITIONAL_EMPLOYED → READY_FOR_GRADUATION → GRADUATED → PLACED`, and from `PLACED` a placement that fails early sends the person to `RE_ENTRY_ELIGIBLE`, from which they can return to `TRANSITIONAL_EMPLOYED` and go round again. Only `ALUMNI_RETAINED` and `EXITED_UNSUCCESSFUL` are terminal.

**Re-entry** closes that cycle. An `IntakeSpecialist` returns a `RE_ENTRY_ELIGIBLE` participant to transitional employment by supplying a fresh `wage_cents_per_hour`, subject to the same 1500-to-2200 band. Three preconditions apply: the participant must be in `RE_ENTRY_ELIGIBLE` status, their `re_entry_count` must be strictly below the re-entry limit of two — a third attempt is refused with `ERR_RE_ENTRY_LIMIT_REACHED` — and the business line must be `LAUNCHED` with `intake_posture` `OPEN`. The transition `RE_ENTRY_ELIGIBLE → TRANSITIONAL_EMPLOYED` increments `re_entry_count` by one, restamps `enrolled_at` from the logical clock, overwrites `wage_cents_per_hour` with the supplied value, and clears the whole previous cycle's evidence: `readiness_certified_by_user_id`, `readiness_certified_at`, `graduated_at`, `graduation_wage_cents_per_hour` and `eligible_re_entry_at` all become null. The line's `training_slots_filled` and `intake_posture` are re-derived and its `version` is incremented by one. Note the asymmetry deliberately built into the cycle: the re-entry limit is enforced here and nowhere else, so a participant who has already re-entered twice still becomes `RE_ENTRY_ELIGIBLE` after a third early failure and simply parks there until the ops sweep of §3.8 exits them.

The duration limit is the other way out. A participant who is still `TRANSITIONAL_EMPLOYED` or `READY_FOR_GRADUATION` when the logical clock passes `enrolled_at` plus `max_transitional_weeks` multiplied by seven days is swept to `EXITED_UNSUCCESSFUL`, which is terminal. Both of those sweeps carry the same side effects: every one of that participant's assignments still in `ASSIGNED` status moves to `RELEASED` with `released_reason` set to `PARTICIPANT_EXITED`, processed in order of shift `starts_at` ascending and then assignment `created_at` ascending, and the line's `training_slots_filled` and `intake_posture` are re-derived because the exiting participant has vacated a slot.

### 3.4. The Trading Calendar: Shifts, Seating and Supervision Limits

The trading operation runs on shifts. A `ProgramDirector` schedules one against a `business_line_id`, giving `starts_at` and `ends_at` as ISO 8601 UTC instants, `trainee_capacity` — the number of seats on this single shift, at least 1 — and `supervisor_user_id`, the opaque operator identifier of the staffer supervising it. The shift may also declare its `customer_service_standard`, either `STANDARD` or `PREMIUM`, defaulting to `STANDARD`; this is the service level the trading operation promises customers on that shift, and it is the visible reminder that customers do not receive a discount for being served by a training workforce. On creation the shift is `SCHEDULED`, its `duration_hours` is derived from the half-open interval, `assigned_count` is 0, both quality counters are 0, and `billable_labor_cost_cents` is null. A shift's `status` is one of `SCHEDULED`, `CLOSED` or `CANCELLED`, and the latter two are terminal.

**Seating** a trainee is the busiest validation point in the system. A `CaseManager` submits the `work_shift_id` and the `participant_id`; nothing else. The shift must be `SCHEDULED`, or the request fails with `ERR_SHIFT_NOT_SCHEDULED`. Five business rules then apply in the ladder order of §2.6. The participant must not already hold an `ASSIGNED` or `COMPLETED` assignment on this exact shift (`ERR_DUPLICATE_SHIFT_ASSIGNMENT`). The rest buffer of §2.10 must hold against every other `ASSIGNED` or `COMPLETED` assignment of the same participant, so a trainee cannot be scheduled back-to-back across two shifts and cannot be given less than a full hour between them (`ERR_PARTICIPANT_REST_BUFFER_CONFLICT`). The line-wide concurrency ceiling of §2.10 must hold at every instant inside the new shift's interval, counting `ASSIGNED` seats across all `SCHEDULED` shifts of that line against the line's `supervisor_capacity` (`ERR_SUPERVISION_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`) — this is the rule that stops a well-intentioned scheduler from stacking three shifts in parallel under one supervisor. The shift's own `assigned_count` must be strictly below its `trainee_capacity` (`ERR_SHIFT_CAPACITY_FULL`). And the weekly hours cap must hold: the participant's already-committed hours in the ISO week containing the new shift — the sum of `duration_hours` over that participant's assignments in `ASSIGNED` or `COMPLETED` status whose shift `starts_at` falls in the same ISO week — plus the new shift's `duration_hours` must come to no more than 28 hours. Exactly 28.000 is accepted; anything beyond it is refused with `ERR_WEEKLY_HOURS_CAP_EXCEEDED`. That cap exists because transitional employment is meant to be paired with coaching, appointments and, often, a second life; it is not a full-time job.

A successful seating creates an assignment in `ASSIGNED` status with `actual_hours`, `wage_cost_cents` and `released_reason` all null and both `quality_defect_count` and `customer_complaint_count` at 0, then re-derives the shift's `assigned_count` and the participant's `scheduled_hours_current_week`. The latter is the sum of `duration_hours` over that participant's `ASSIGNED` assignments whose shift `starts_at` falls inside the current ISO week — Monday `00:00:00Z` of the week containing the logical clock's instant, through the same instant seven days later, exclusive — rounded half up to three decimal places. An assignment's `status` is one of `ASSIGNED`, `COMPLETED` or `RELEASED`, and `released_reason`, when set, is one of `MANUAL_RELEASE`, `SHIFT_CANCELLED` or `PARTICIPANT_EXITED`.

**Releasing** a seat is the compensating action for seating one. The `CaseManager` who releases an assignment must find it `ASSIGNED` on a shift that is still `SCHEDULED`. The transition `ASSIGNED → RELEASED` sets `released_reason` to `MANUAL_RELEASE`, re-derives the shift's `assigned_count` because the seat has come back, and re-derives the participant's `scheduled_hours_current_week` because the hours have come back. Crucially, **no cost entry is created and none is voided** — releasing an unworked seat leaves the mission ledger completely untouched, because nothing was ever spent. The shift's auto-close condition is then evaluated.

### 3.5. Completing Work, Recording Quality, and the Wage Cost Trail

Completion is where the training workforce meets the business's cost structure. A `CaseManager` records what actually happened on a seated shift by supplying `actual_hours`, together with the optional `quality_defect_count` and `customer_complaint_count`, each defaulting to 0 and each a non-negative integer attributing defects and customer complaints to that trainee's output on that shift. Completion requires the assignment to be `ASSIGNED` on a shift that is still `SCHEDULED`, requires the logical clock to have reached the shift's `starts_at` — completing a shift that has not begun is refused with `ERR_SHIFT_NOT_STARTED` — and requires `actual_hours` to be strictly greater than 0 and no more than the shift's `duration_hours` plus a grace allowance of 0.5 hours, a breach reporting `ERR_ACTUAL_HOURS_EXCEED_SHIFT`. Finally, an open accounting period must exist for the shift's business line covering the shift's `starts_at`; if none does, the whole request is refused with `ERR_NO_OPEN_SUBSIDY_PERIOD` and no state whatsoever is written — not the completion, not the cost, not the counters.

The transition `ASSIGNED → COMPLETED` stores `actual_hours`, `quality_defect_count` and `customer_complaint_count`, and computes the assignment's `wage_cost_cents` as `actual_hours` multiplied by the participant's `wage_cents_per_hour`, rounded half up to whole cents. That per-assignment rounding is the first half of a two-step labour cost, and the order is pinned.

From there the cascade runs. One cost entry is auto-posted into the selected accounting period with `category` `TRAINEE_WAGES`, `classification` `SHARED`, `amount_cents` equal to the assignment's `wage_cost_cents`, `source_activity_id` null and `status` `POSTED`, and the period's `version` is incremented by one. Because a new posted entry has landed, the period re-derives `trainee_hours`, `mission_share`, every entry's allocation, `mission_cost_cents`, `business_operating_cost_cents`, `total_cost_cents` and both per-trainee-hour ratios, exactly as §3.9 describes. The participant's `transitional_hours_worked` and `scheduled_hours_current_week` are re-derived. In parallel the quality chain fires: the shift's own `quality_defect_count` and `customer_complaint_count` are re-summed from its `COMPLETED` assignments, the business line's `quality_incident_count` is re-summed from all `COMPLETED` assignments on its shifts, and the line's `intake_posture` is re-derived — which flips it to `CLOSED` the moment `quality_incident_count` reaches ten. A training business that is generating ten defects and complaints has stopped being a good training environment, and the system stops admitting people to it without anyone having to remember to.

The shift's **auto-close** is then evaluated, and this is the one state machine in the system whose parent state is a pure function of its children. A shift moves `SCHEDULED → CLOSED` when it is still `SCHEDULED`, has at least one assignment row, every assignment on it is in `COMPLETED` or `RELEASED` status, and at least one of them is `COMPLETED`. A shift on which every seat was released therefore stays `SCHEDULED` rather than closing, because there is nothing to bill. On close, the shift's `billable_labor_cost_cents` becomes the **sum of the already-rounded `wage_cost_cents`** across its `COMPLETED` assignments, and its `quality_defect_count` and `customer_complaint_count` are aggregated from those same assignments. Rounding per assignment first and summing second is the specification, and it does not agree with summing raw products and rounding once: 3.335 hours at 1650 cents an hour and 2.125 hours at 1725 cents an hour give 5503 and 3666, totalling 9169 under the pinned order, where sum-then-round would give 9168. `RELEASED` assignments contribute nothing and keep `wage_cost_cents` null. The evaluation runs after every completion and after every release, since a release can be the event that leaves no `ASSIGNED` seats behind.

A participant's four progress metrics are re-derived after every assignment creation, completion or release and after every support-activity delivery or void, and their status filters are mutually exclusive. `transitional_hours_worked` sums `actual_hours` over that participant's `COMPLETED` assignments, rounded half up to three decimals. `scheduled_hours_current_week` sums shift `duration_hours` over their `ASSIGNED` assignments inside the current ISO week, rounded half up to three decimals. `support_activity_minutes` sums `duration_minutes` over their `DELIVERED` support activities. `delivered_coaching_count` counts their `DELIVERED` support activities whose `category` is `JOB_COACHING`. Voided activities and released assignments never contribute to anything.

### 3.6. Support Services, Coaching, and Graduation Readiness

Transitional employment without support is just a low-wage job. Both a `JobCoach` and a `CaseManager` may plan a support service for a participant, naming the `participant_id`, the `category` — one of `JOB_COACHING`, `CASE_MANAGEMENT`, `TRANSPORT_VOUCHER` or `CHILDCARE_VOUCHER` — the `duration_minutes` of contact time, from 0 through 480, and the `cost_cents` of delivering it, which must be strictly greater than 0. Contact time and vouchers are shaped differently: `duration_minutes` must be strictly greater than 0 for `JOB_COACHING` and `CASE_MANAGEMENT`, and must be exactly 0 for `TRANSPORT_VOUCHER` and `CHILDCARE_VOUCHER`, a mismatch being a request-shape failure. A planned activity is created in `PLANNED` `status` with `delivered_by_user_id`, `delivered_at` and `voided_reason` null, and no ledger movement whatsoever — planning something costs nothing.

**Delivery** is reserved to a `JobCoach`. The activity must be `PLANNED`, the participant must be in `TRANSITIONAL_EMPLOYED`, `READY_FOR_GRADUATION` or `PLACED` status — a graduate awaiting placement and an exited participant cannot receive services — and an open accounting period must exist for the participant's business line covering the logical clock's current instant, failing which the request is refused with `ERR_NO_OPEN_SUBSIDY_PERIOD`. The transition `PLANNED → DELIVERED` stamps `delivered_at` from the logical clock, records the acting operator as `delivered_by_user_id`, and auto-posts one cost entry into the selected period with `classification` `MISSION`, `amount_cents` equal to the activity's `cost_cents`, `source_activity_id` pointing at this activity, `status` `POSTED`, and `category` `COACHING` when the activity's `category` is `JOB_COACHING` or `CASE_MANAGEMENT` and `SUPPORT_SERVICE` otherwise. The period's `version` is incremented by one and its `mission_cost_cents`, `business_operating_cost_cents`, `total_cost_cents` and both true-cost ratios are re-derived. Finally the participant's `support_activity_minutes` and `delivered_coaching_count` are re-derived. Support cost is always wholly mission cost: a coaching hour is never charged to the trading operation.

**Voiding** is a `ProgramDirector` action and has two shapes with deliberately different consequences. Voiding a `PLANNED` activity, `PLANNED → VOIDED`, simply stores the supplied `voided_reason` and moves on — no cost entry exists, so there is no ledger movement at all. Voiding a `DELIVERED` activity, `DELIVERED → VOIDED`, is the compensating action for the mission ledger: it requires that the cost entry whose `source_activity_id` is this activity belongs to a period still in `OPEN` status, refusing with `ERR_PERIOD_CLOSED` otherwise, and it moves that entry `POSTED → VOIDED` with `voided_at` stamped from the logical clock. A voided entry drops out of every period aggregate immediately, the period's `mission_cost_cents`, `business_operating_cost_cents`, `total_cost_cents` and both true-cost ratios are re-derived, the period's `version` is incremented by one, and the participant's `support_activity_minutes` and `delivered_coaching_count` are re-derived downward. `VOIDED` is terminal for a support activity, whose `status` is one of `PLANNED`, `DELIVERED` or `VOIDED`.

**Readiness certification** is the gate between working and graduating, and it belongs to the `JobCoach` alone. The participant must be `TRANSITIONAL_EMPLOYED`, and three thresholds must all be met: `transitional_hours_worked` of at least 24.000 hours — the equivalent of two full twelve-hour shifts actually worked — `support_activity_minutes` of at least 180, which is three hours of delivered contact time, and `delivered_coaching_count` of at least 2, meaning at least two delivered job-coaching sessions specifically. A shortfall on any one of the three is refused with `ERR_GRADUATION_CRITERIA_NOT_MET`. The transition `TRANSITIONAL_EMPLOYED → READY_FOR_GRADUATION` records the certifying operator's `X-User-ID` in `readiness_certified_by_user_id` and stamps `readiness_certified_at` from the logical clock. The participant's slot is **not** released — `READY_FOR_GRADUATION` still occupies a training slot, so `training_slots_filled` is unchanged, though `intake_posture` is re-derived as always.

**Graduation** is a `CaseManager` action, and it is where segregation of duties bites. The participant must be `READY_FOR_GRADUATION`; the acting `X-User-ID` must **not** equal the participant's `readiness_certified_by_user_id`, a violation reporting `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; and the participant must hold no assignment still in `ASSIGNED` status, because a person cannot graduate out of a shift they are still rostered on. The transition `READY_FOR_GRADUATION → GRADUATED` stamps `graduated_at` from the logical clock and freezes `graduation_wage_cents_per_hour` as a copy of the participant's current `wage_cents_per_hour`. That frozen figure is the baseline every later wage-progression check is measured against, and it is deliberately a snapshot: a subsequent re-entry at a different wage does not retroactively change what a past placement had to beat. Graduation releases the training slot, so the line's `training_slots_filled` and `intake_posture` are re-derived and its `version` is incremented by one.

### 3.7. Placement with Outside Employers and Wage Evidence

Placement is the outcome the funders paid for. A `ProgramDirector` registers each partner employer with its `organization_name` (1 to 120 characters), an `industry_code` label used in funder reporting (1 to 32 characters), and `min_starting_wage_cents_per_hour`, the wage floor the employer commits to for program placements, which must be at least 1500. A new employer starts in `ACTIVE` `partnership_status` with `failed_retention_count`, `confirmed_retention_count` and `placement_count` all at 0 and `retention_reliability_pct` null. `partnership_status` is one of `ACTIVE`, `PROBATION` or `TERMINATED`, the last being terminal.

A `CaseManager` creates a placement by naming the `participant_id` of a graduate and the `employer_id` of the receiving employer, with `hire_date` — the first day of outside employment, at `00:00:00Z` — `starting_wage_cents_per_hour` of at least 1500, and `weekly_hours`, the contracted weekly hours at the outside employer, from 1 through 60. Five gates apply. The participant must be in `GRADUATED` status. The `hire_date` may not predate the participant's `graduated_at`, which reports `ERR_HIRE_DATE_BEFORE_GRADUATION`. The employer's `partnership_status` must be `ACTIVE`, so a placement into an employer on probation or terminated is refused with `ERR_EMPLOYER_NOT_ACTIVE`. And two wage floors must both be cleared: `starting_wage_cents_per_hour` must be at least the employer's `min_starting_wage_cents_per_hour`, reporting `ERR_WAGE_BELOW_EMPLOYER_FLOOR`, and it must also be at least the participant's frozen `graduation_wage_cents_per_hour` multiplied by 1.10 and rounded half up to whole cents, reporting `ERR_WAGE_PROGRESSION_FLOOR_NOT_MET`. The second floor is the program's promise that leaving transitional employment is a raise of at least ten percent, not a lateral move.

A created placement carries **two independent state dimensions on one row**, and neither gates the other. `employment_status` tracks the job and is one of `ACTIVE`, `SEPARATED_VOLUNTARY` or `SEPARATED_INVOLUNTARY`, starting at `ACTIVE`. `wage_verification_status` tracks the wage evidence and is one of `UNVERIFIED`, `VERIFIED` or `DISPUTED`, starting at `UNVERIFIED`. A placement may perfectly legitimately be `SEPARATED_INVOLUNTARY` with `VERIFIED` wage evidence, or still `ACTIVE` with `DISPUTED` evidence. Alongside them the row starts with `retention_outcome` at `PENDING`, `verified_wage_cents_per_hour` null, `separation_date` null, `separation_days_after_hire` null, `wage_progression_pct` null and `at_risk_flag` false. Creation drives the participant `GRADUATED → PLACED`, re-derives the employer's `placement_count` — which counts every placement of that employer regardless of outcome — and creates the four retention checkpoints described in §3.8.

**Wage verification** is where the two dimensions show their independence, and the two actions on it are performed by different roles. An `EmployerPartner` attests to a wage by supplying `action` `VERIFY` together with `verified_wage_cents_per_hour`, which must be present and at least 1500. The attestation is permitted when `wage_verification_status` is `UNVERIFIED` or `DISPUTED` — so an employer can answer a dispute with better evidence — and requires the acting `X-User-ID` to equal the placement's `employer_id`. It sets `wage_verification_status` to `VERIFIED`, stores `verified_wage_cents_per_hour`, recomputes `wage_progression_pct`, and does **not** touch `employment_status`. A `CaseManager` raises a dispute by supplying `action` `DISPUTE`, permitted when `wage_verification_status` is `UNVERIFIED` or `VERIFIED`; it sets `wage_verification_status` to `DISPUTED`, clears `verified_wage_cents_per_hour` to null and `wage_progression_pct` to null, and again leaves `employment_status` alone.

**Wage progression** is measured against the frozen graduation wage and never against the participant's current `wage_cents_per_hour`. When a placement's `wage_verification_status` becomes `VERIFIED`, whether by employer attestation or by the checkpoint cascade of §3.8, `wage_progression_pct` becomes the verified wage minus the participant's `graduation_wage_cents_per_hour`, divided by that same `graduation_wage_cents_per_hour`, rounded half up to four decimal places. It is `null` whenever the frozen graduation wage is null or the placement's `wage_verification_status` is anything other than `VERIFIED`, and it may legitimately be negative if a verified wage has fallen below the graduation wage. A dispute resets it to null; a later verification recomputes it from the new evidence.

**Separation** is reported by the employer who did the separating, and it is the most far-reaching cascade in the placement pipeline. An `EmployerPartner` whose `X-User-ID` equals the placement's `employer_id` supplies `separation_type`, either `VOLUNTARY` or `INVOLUNTARY`, and `separation_date`, which must be at or after the placement's `hire_date` and at or before the logical clock's current instant. The placement must be in `ACTIVE` `employment_status`. The two branches are identical in every respect except the value written: the voluntary branch sets `employment_status` to `SEPARATED_VOLUNTARY` and the involuntary branch sets it to `SEPARATED_INVOLUNTARY`, and both then run the same five hops.

The first hop stores `separation_date`, derives `separation_days_after_hire` as the whole days between `hire_date` and `separation_date`, and sets `retention_outcome`: `FAILED_EARLY` when `separation_days_after_hire` is fewer than 90 days, `FAILED_LATE` when it is at least 90 and fewer than 180, and `RETAINED_180` when it is 180 or more. Every retention checkpoint of that placement still in `SCHEDULED` status whose `due_date` falls after the `separation_date` moves `SCHEDULED → VOIDED`, processed in order of `interval_days` ascending — a checkpoint that was already due before the separation is left alone to be answered or missed on its own terms. The second hop, triggered because `retention_outcome` moved to a failure value, re-derives the employer's `failed_retention_count` and `retention_reliability_pct`. The third hop moves the employer's own standing if a threshold has been crossed: `ACTIVE → PROBATION` when `failed_retention_count` has reached at least 3 while remaining below 5, and `PROBATION → TERMINATED` when it has reached 5 or more. A `TERMINATED` employer may never receive a new placement. The fourth hop, triggered because the employer entered probation or termination, sets `at_risk_flag` to true on **every** placement of that employer whose `employment_status` is still `ACTIVE`, processed in order of `hire_date` ascending and then `created_at` ascending — one employer's pattern of failures marks all of that employer's remaining program placements as needing attention. The fifth and last hop applies only when `retention_outcome` came out as `FAILED_EARLY`: the participant moves `PLACED → RE_ENTRY_ELIGIBLE` with `eligible_re_entry_at` stamped from the logical clock. That hop is unconditional on `re_entry_count`, which is why a participant who has exhausted the re-entry limit still lands in `RE_ENTRY_ELIGIBLE` and waits there.

An employer's **retention reliability** is re-derived after every change to a placement's `retention_outcome`, whether from the separation cascade or from a 180-day confirmation. `failed_retention_count` counts that employer's placements whose `retention_outcome` is `FAILED_EARLY` or `FAILED_LATE`. `confirmed_retention_count` counts those whose `retention_outcome` is `RETAINED_180`. `retention_reliability_pct` is `confirmed_retention_count` divided by the sum of the two counts, rounded half up to four decimal places, and is `null` when that sum is zero. Placements still `PENDING` are excluded from that denominator but are counted in `placement_count`.

### 3.8. Retention Follow-Up and the Ops Sweep

Funders do not pay for job starts; they pay for jobs that last. Creating a placement therefore creates exactly four retention checkpoints and no operation ever creates one any other way. One row exists per follow-up interval, at 30, 90, 180 and 365 days, and each row records its `placement_id`, its `interval_days` and a `due_date` equal to the placement's `hire_date` plus that many whole days, landing on `00:00:00Z`. The rows are created in ascending `interval_days` order, so creation order matches interval order, which is what lets the listing of a placement's checkpoints be read positionally: the 30-day row first, then 90, then 180, then 365. A checkpoint begins in `SCHEDULED` `status` with `still_employed` null, `verified_wage_cents_per_hour` null and `confirmed_at` null; its `status` is one of `SCHEDULED`, `CONFIRMED`, `MISSED` or `VOIDED`, and the last three are terminal.

**Confirmation** belongs to the `EmployerPartner` who owns the placement. Five preconditions apply. The checkpoint must be `SCHEDULED`; the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the placement's `employer_id`; the placement's `employment_status` must still be `ACTIVE`; the logical clock must have reached the checkpoint's `due_date`, inclusive, with an earlier attempt refused as `ERR_CHECKPOINT_NOT_DUE`; and every checkpoint of the same placement with a smaller `interval_days` must already be in `CONFIRMED` or `VOIDED` status, so the 180-day answer cannot be given while the 90-day one is still outstanding or was missed — that failure reports `ERR_CHECKPOINT_OUT_OF_SEQUENCE`. The employer supplies `still_employed`, their answer at this follow-up, and may supply `verified_wage_cents_per_hour` as the wage evidenced at this checkpoint.

The confirmation cascade has four hops. The first moves the checkpoint `SCHEDULED → CONFIRMED`, stamps `confirmed_at` from the logical clock, and stores `still_employed` and `verified_wage_cents_per_hour`. The second runs in parallel on the wage dimension: when the confirmation carried `still_employed` as true and the placement's `wage_verification_status` is not `DISPUTED`, that status moves `UNVERIFIED → VERIFIED`, the placement's `verified_wage_cents_per_hour` takes the checkpoint's evidenced wage, and `wage_progression_pct` is recomputed. A placement whose wage evidence is under dispute is left in `DISPUTED` — a checkpoint answer does not overrule a case manager's open dispute. The third hop fires only at the 180-day checkpoint: the placement's `retention_outcome` becomes `RETAINED_180` and the employer's `confirmed_retention_count` and `retention_reliability_pct` are re-derived. The fourth follows from the third: because the placement reached `RETAINED_180`, the participant moves `PLACED → ALUMNI_RETAINED`, which is terminal and is the program's definition of success. The 365-day checkpoint exists for reporting continuity and does not itself change any retention outcome.

**The ops sweep** is how the program's epoch advances. Setting the logical clock changes no entity; it is this operation, invoked by a `ProgramDirector` and optionally scoped to a single line through `business_line_id`, that turns elapsed time into state. It processes three passes in a fixed order. First, overdue checkpoints: every checkpoint still in `SCHEDULED` status whose `due_date` plus the 14-day grace period has been passed by the logical clock moves `SCHEDULED → MISSED` and sets `at_risk_flag` to true on its placement, processed in order of `due_date` ascending and then `created_at` ascending. Note the asymmetry between the two thresholds: a checkpoint becomes answerable the instant the clock reaches `due_date` but is only written off two weeks later, which gives an employer a fortnight of slack without letting the record drift indefinitely. Second, duration-limit exits: participants still in `TRANSITIONAL_EMPLOYED` or `READY_FOR_GRADUATION` whose `enrolled_at` plus `max_transitional_weeks` multiplied by seven days has been passed by the logical clock move to `EXITED_UNSUCCESSFUL`, releasing their `ASSIGNED` assignments with `released_reason` `PARTICIPANT_EXITED` and forcing the line's `training_slots_filled` and `intake_posture` to be re-derived. Third, stale re-entry exits: participants in `RE_ENTRY_ELIGIBLE` status whose `eligible_re_entry_at` plus 60 days has been passed by the logical clock move to `EXITED_UNSUCCESSFUL`. That third pass is how a participant who has exhausted the re-entry limit finally leaves the cohort. When `business_line_id` is supplied, only participants enrolled on that line, and only checkpoints belonging to placements of those participants, are considered. The sweep reports what it did: `missed_checkpoint_count`, `exited_participant_count`, `released_assignment_count`, and `swept_at`, the logical-clock instant at which the sweep ran.

### 3.9. Subsidy Accounting, True Cost and Funder Reporting

The whole point of separating mission cost from business operating cost is to be able to answer one question honestly: what does an hour of training actually cost, and how much of it is the business earning back? That answer is assembled inside accounting periods.

A `ProgramDirector` opens a period on a `business_line_id` with `period_start` and `period_end`, both at `00:00:00Z` and `period_end` strictly after `period_start`, and with `supervisor_hours`, the budgeted non-trainee supervision hours for the period — a non-negative number to three decimal places, and the denominator partner of trainee hours under the hours allocation basis. Periods of the same line may never intersect, on the closed-interval rule of §2.10, and a collision reports `ERR_SUBSIDY_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. A new period is `OPEN`, has `allocation_basis` `HOURS`, `revenue_cents` null, every ledger aggregate at 0, `mission_share` at 0, both per-trainee-hour ratios null, and `margin_pct`, `subsidy_rate`, `subsidy_draw_cents` and `closed_at` all null. `status` is `OPEN` or `CLOSED`, and `allocation_basis` is `HOURS` or `REVENUE`.

**Choosing the period a cost lands in** is a pinned selection, not a search. Whenever a cost must be auto-posted, the candidate set is the periods of exactly one business line — the line of the shift for a completion, the line of the participant for a delivery — whose `status` is `OPEN` and whose closed interval from `period_start` through `period_end` contains the anchor instant. The anchor instant is the shift's `starts_at` for an assignment completion and the logical clock's current instant for a support-activity delivery. Candidates are ranked by `period_start` descending, ties broken by `created_at` ascending, and the first row wins. `CLOSED` periods are never selected even when their interval covers the anchor. When the candidate set is empty the entire request is rejected with `ERR_NO_OPEN_SUBSIDY_PERIOD` and no partial state is written.

**Cost entries** are the ledger's atoms. A `ProgramDirector` posts one manually against a `subsidy_period_id`, giving its `category`, its `classification`, an `amount_cents` strictly greater than 0, and `expected_subsidy_period_version` per §2.9. Six categories exist — `TRAINEE_WAGES`, `SUPERVISION`, `MATERIALS`, `COACHING`, `SUPPORT_SERVICE`, `OVERHEAD` — but only `SUPERVISION`, `MATERIALS` and `OVERHEAD` may be posted by hand; `TRAINEE_WAGES`, `COACHING` and `SUPPORT_SERVICE` are system-posted only, arriving from shift completion and support delivery respectively, and a manual attempt at one of them is refused with `ERR_CATEGORY_NOT_MANUALLY_POSTABLE`. `classification` is `MISSION`, `BUSINESS` or `SHARED`. A manually posted entry has `source_activity_id` null; a system-posted entry from a support delivery carries the originating activity in `source_activity_id`, while one from a shift completion leaves it null. An entry's `status` is `POSTED` or `VOIDED`, with `voided_at` null while posted; posted entries count toward every aggregate and voided entries are excluded from all of them, though a voided entry keeps the last `allocated_mission_cents` and `allocated_business_cents` values it was given.

**Allocation** runs on every cost-entry post, on every cost-entry void, and again on the period close, and it always operates on the period's `POSTED` entries only. The period's `trainee_hours` is the sum of `actual_hours` over `COMPLETED` assignments whose shift belongs to this line and whose shift `starts_at` falls inside the closed period interval, rounded half up to three decimal places — note that the shift must *start* inside the period, not merely overlap it. The period's gross cost is the sum of `amount_cents` over its posted entries.

`mission_share` is where the two variants diverge. Under the **hours basis**, which is what an `OPEN` period always uses and what a what-if analysis requested on the hours basis reports, `mission_share` is `trainee_hours` divided by the sum of `trainee_hours` and `supervisor_hours`, rounded half up to four decimal places; when both hour figures are zero, `mission_share` is 0.0000. That basis ignores trading revenue entirely and is therefore stable against a bad trading month. Under the **revenue basis**, used when a period closes on that basis and when a what-if analysis on a closed period requests it, `mission_share` is one minus `revenue_cents` divided by gross cost, floored at zero and rounded half up to four decimal places; when gross cost is zero, `mission_share` is 0.0000. That basis reads mission cost as the unfunded fraction of what the operation spent. The same ledger genuinely splits differently under the two: with `trainee_hours` of 12.000, `supervisor_hours` of 40.0, `revenue_cents` of 250000, and a ledger of one shared trainee-wage entry of 19800, one shared supervision entry of 300000, one business materials entry of 40000 and one mission coaching entry of 12000 — a gross cost of 371800 — the hours basis yields a `mission_share` of 0.2308 and `mission_cost_cents` of 85810, while the revenue basis yields a `mission_share` of 0.3276 and `mission_cost_cents` of 116766.

Each posted entry is then allocated, and the rounding is **per entry, then summed**, never the other way round. An entry classified `MISSION` puts its whole `amount_cents` into `allocated_mission_cents` and 0 into `allocated_business_cents`. An entry classified `BUSINESS` puts 0 into `allocated_mission_cents` and its whole `amount_cents` into `allocated_business_cents`. An entry classified `SHARED` sets `allocated_mission_cents` to `amount_cents` multiplied by `mission_share`, rounded half up to whole cents, and sets `allocated_business_cents` to `amount_cents` minus that figure — the exact complement, which is what guarantees the two sides reconcile to the cent. The period's `mission_cost_cents` is the sum of `allocated_mission_cents` over its posted entries, `business_operating_cost_cents` is the sum of `allocated_business_cents`, and `total_cost_cents` is the sum of those two, which always equals the gross cost.

**True cost** falls straight out of those aggregates. `true_cost_per_trainee_hour_cents` is `total_cost_cents` divided by `trainee_hours`, rounded half up to two decimal places, and `subsidy_per_trainee_hour_cents` is `mission_cost_cents` divided by `trainee_hours` on the same rounding. Both are `null` when `trainee_hours` is zero — never 0, and never an error. The read-only true-cost analysis reports `subsidy_period_id`, the `allocation_basis` it applied, `mission_share`, `mission_cost_cents`, `business_operating_cost_cents`, `total_cost_cents`, `trainee_hours`, `true_cost_per_trainee_hour_cents` and `subsidy_per_trainee_hour_cents`. An `allocation_basis` may be supplied to ask the what-if question, defaulting to the period's own stored basis; the answer never persists, so asking the question does not move the ledger. Asking for the revenue basis on a period that has no `revenue_cents` at all is refused with `ERR_REVENUE_NOT_AVAILABLE`.

**Closing a period** is the single heaviest operation in the system, and it moves five entities. A `ProgramDirector` supplies the `allocation_basis` to freeze and the final `revenue_cents`, which must be present and non-negative. Four preconditions apply: the period must be `OPEN`; the logical clock must be strictly after `period_end`, with an earlier attempt refused as `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_ENDED`; `revenue_cents` must be present; and no assignment in `ASSIGNED` status may remain on a shift of this line whose `starts_at` falls inside the period interval, since an unresolved seat means an unknown labour cost — that failure reports `ERR_OPEN_ASSIGNMENTS_BLOCK_CLOSE`.

The first hop sets `status` to `CLOSED`, stamps `closed_at` from the logical clock, stores `revenue_cents`, freezes `allocation_basis` to the requested basis, re-derives `mission_share` and every posted entry's `allocated_mission_cents` and `allocated_business_cents` under that final basis, re-derives `mission_cost_cents`, `business_operating_cost_cents`, `total_cost_cents` and both per-trainee-hour ratios, and increments the period's `version` by one. It then computes the subsidy draw, and **the evaluation order here is pinned**. The margin is taken from the *pre-subsidy* position: `margin_pct` is negative 1.0000 when `revenue_cents` is zero, and otherwise `revenue_cents` minus `business_operating_cost_cents`, divided by `revenue_cents`, rounded half up to four decimal places — where `business_operating_cost_cents` is the post-allocation business side, not the raw sum of business-classified entries. `subsidy_rate` is chosen from that **rounded** margin: 0.50 when `margin_pct` is at least 0.2000, 0.70 when `margin_pct` is at least 0.0500 but below 0.2000, and 0.90 when `margin_pct` is below 0.0500. Because the comparison uses the rounded value, a raw margin of 0.19999958 rounds to 0.2000 and buys the top tier. `subsidy_draw_cents` is then `mission_cost_cents` multiplied by `subsidy_rate`, rounded half up to whole cents. Computing the margin from revenue minus business cost *plus* the drawn subsidy would select a different tier and is explicitly forbidden: the subsidy is the consequence of the margin, never an input to it.

The second hop follows from the draw becoming non-null: the business line's `cumulative_subsidy_cents` is re-derived as the sum of `subsidy_draw_cents` over all `CLOSED` periods of that line. The third hop fires only when that running total now **strictly exceeds** the line's `subsidy_cap_cents`: the line moves `LAUNCHED → SUSPENDED`, its `intake_posture` is re-derived to `CLOSED` because posture only opens for a launched line, and its `version` is incremented by one. `SUSPENDED` is terminal — a line that has burned through its lifetime subsidy ceiling does not come back. The fourth hop follows from the suspension: every work shift of that line still in `SCHEDULED` status whose `starts_at` is after the logical clock's current instant moves `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED`, processed in order of `starts_at` ascending and then `created_at` ascending. Shifts that have already started are left alone so that work genuinely performed can still be recorded. The fifth hop follows from those cancellations: on each cancelled shift, every assignment still in `ASSIGNED` status moves `ASSIGNED → RELEASED` with `released_reason` set to `SHIFT_CANCELLED`, and each affected participant's `scheduled_hours_current_week` is re-derived.

**Funder reporting** is a pure read over a window. The outcome summary answers for one business line over a closed window from `period_start` through `period_end`, both at `00:00:00Z`, defaulting to the line's `launched_at` through the logical clock's current instant when either is omitted. The enrolled cohort is the participants of that line whose `enrolled_at` falls inside the window, so a re-entered participant is selected by their most recent enrollment; `enrolled_count` is the size of that cohort and `placed_count` is the number of them whose `status` is `PLACED` or `ALUMNI_RETAINED`. `placement_rate` is `placed_count` divided by `enrolled_count`, rounded half up to four decimals, and null when the cohort is empty. The window's placements are those belonging to cohort participants whose `hire_date` falls inside the window; separated placements stay in that set. `verified_placement_count` counts the window placements whose `wage_verification_status` is `VERIFIED`, and `average_wage_progression_pct` is the sum of `wage_progression_pct` across exactly those placements divided by their count, rounded half up to four decimals and null when there are none.

Retention is reported under one of two measures, selected by `retention_measure` and defaulting to `COHORT_BASED`. Under `COHORT_BASED`, `retention_denominator` is the number of window placements whose `hire_date` plus 180 days falls at or before the window's `period_end` — that is, only placements that had a full 180-day chance inside the window — and `retention_numerator` is the number of those whose `retention_outcome` is `RETAINED_180`; a placement still `ACTIVE` but never confirmed at 180 days therefore scores as a non-retention. Under `POINT_IN_TIME`, `retention_denominator` is all window placements regardless of elapsed time and `retention_numerator` is those whose `employment_status` is `ACTIVE` as of the logical clock's current instant. `retention_rate` is the numerator divided by the denominator, rounded half up to four decimals and null when the denominator is empty. The two measures answer genuinely different questions, and the report says which one it used: a single active-but-unconfirmed placement scores a `retention_rate` of 0.0000 under `COHORT_BASED` and 1.0000 under `POINT_IN_TIME`. The response reports `business_line_id`, the resolved `period_start` and `period_end`, the `retention_measure` applied, `enrolled_count`, `placed_count`, `placement_rate`, `retention_denominator`, `retention_numerator`, `retention_rate`, `verified_placement_count` and `average_wage_progression_pct`.
