# Demand Planning & Sales And Operations Planning API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Consumer packaged goods businesses run a monthly rhythm that nobody in the organisation can escape: demand planners rebuild a statistical baseline, sales leaders argue that the baseline understates what they can sell, supply planners explain which of those numbers the plants and the component supply base can actually build, and finally an executive committee reconciles the resulting volume plan against a revenue number that was committed to the board months earlier. Today that rhythm lives in spreadsheets. Each hand-off loses the arithmetic that produced the previous number, promotional lifts get double-counted, discontinued items keep drawing forecast, and nobody can answer the question that matters most at the end of the quarter — which planner owned the forecast that missed, and by how much.

This document specifies the Demand Planning & Sales and Operations Planning API, a server-side system of record for the full S&OP cycle. The API is mounted under the `/sop` path prefix. It owns eleven kinds of business record — planning cycles, product families, items, trade customers, capacity blocks, trade promotions, demand forecast lines, consensus adjustments, supply plans, customer allocations, gap-closing scenarios and immutable accuracy records — and it enforces the phase discipline that keeps them mutually consistent. A cycle marches forward through demand review, supply review, scenario review and executive review before it is published; each phase admits only its own class of write, and the system rejects out-of-phase attempts rather than silently accepting them.

Three design commitments shape almost every rule that follows. First, **every derived number is computed by the server, never supplied by the client** — baselines, consensus totals, safety stock, constrained supply, allocations, gap closures and accuracy metrics are all read-only outputs whose formulas are pinned in this document, because two implementations that round in different places produce two different plans. Second, **money and volume are conserved**: units handed out to customers plus units left over always equal the units the supply plan authorised, and gap-closing money that is superseded leaves the cycle's residual gap in the same request that admits its replacement. Third, **the people who propose and the people who approve are never the same person** — the demand review lead who reviews an adjustment must not be the planner who proposed it, and the executive who selects a scenario must not be the planner who authored it. These are enforced on the acting user's identity, not merely on their role.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contracts, envelopes and cross-cutting principles that hold for every endpoint in the surface. Where a rule here and a narrative in §3 describe the same behaviour, they are reinforcing each other, not competing.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which that user is acting. If either header is missing, empty or entirely whitespace, or if `X-User-Role` names a role that this specification does not declare — role names are matched exactly, so a differently-cased spelling is an undeclared role — the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If the named role is present and valid but is not authorised for the target endpoint, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). The single exception is the logical-clock capability described in §2.5, which is exempt from both header authentication and the role check.

Six roles exist, spelled exactly as follows.

- `SopAdmin` — the S&OP process owner. Maintains master data (product families, items, customers, capacity blocks), opens and drives planning cycles, and repositions the logical clock for horizon testing. An `SopAdmin` may never review a consensus adjustment nor select a scenario.
- `DemandPlanner` — owns the statistical baselines and proposes consensus adjustments. Accountable for forecast accuracy at product-family level. A `DemandPlanner` may never review or endorse an adjustment, including one it proposed itself.
- `DemandReviewLead` — chairs the demand review. The sole approver of trade promotions and the sole reviewer of consensus adjustments, able to endorse, reject, or request a revision.
- `SupplyPlanner` — runs the supply review: publishes capacity, builds constrained supply plans, executes the rationing waterfall, and drafts capacity-investment scenarios. A `SupplyPlanner` may neither select a scenario nor publish a cycle.
- `ExecutiveSponsor` — chairs the executive review. The sole selector of gap-closing scenarios and, jointly with `SopAdmin`, the driver of cycle phase transitions including publication and reopening.
- `CustomerRep` — the external representative of exactly one trade customer. Read-only.

Identity semantics differ between the internal roles and the external one, and the difference matters because ownership checks compare against `X-User-ID` directly. When `X-User-Role` is `SopAdmin`, `DemandPlanner`, `DemandReviewLead`, `SupplyPlanner` or `ExecutiveSponsor`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it where a rule requires it — as `proposed_by_user_id` and `reviewed_by_user_id` on a consensus adjustment, and as `created_by_user_id` and `selected_by_user_id` on a scenario — but it is never matched against any row's `id`, and no check anywhere compares it against the `accountable_planner_user_id` recorded on a product family: family accountability is a recorded attribution and a list filter, never an authorisation test, so a planner may write against a family it does not own. When `X-User-Role` is `CustomerRep`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Customer`; ownership checks on any resource that references a customer compare that resource's `customer_id` field to `X-User-ID`.

A `CustomerRep` may read only rows scoped to its own customer. On the list reads of demand forecast lines, allocations and accuracy records it must supply a `customer_id` filter equal to its own customer's `id`; omitting the filter, or supplying any other customer's `id`, is rejected with `ERR_CUSTOMER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). On the corresponding single-resource reads, the targeted row's `customer_id` must be the caller's own customer, and the same error is returned otherwise. This restriction covers exactly six reads: the list and single-resource reads of demand forecast lines, of allocations, and of accuracy records.

Segregation of duties is enforced on `X-User-ID` in two places. The user whose identifier was captured as an adjustment's `proposed_by_user_id` may not review that adjustment, and the user whose identifier was captured as a scenario's `created_by_user_id` may not select that scenario. Either attempt is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.
- Two envelope-level rejections sit outside the business catalogue of §5 and travel in this same error envelope: a request to a path this document does not declare answers `error_id` `ERR_NOT_FOUND` with HTTP 404, and a declared path invoked with a method it does not accept answers `error_id` `ERR_METHOD_NOT_ALLOWED` with HTTP 405. A trailing slash on any declared path is tolerated and addresses the same endpoint as the slashless form.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint is paginated through two query parameters. The `limit` parameter caps the number of rows returned; it defaults to 10 when omitted, may be no smaller than 1, and may be no larger than 100. The `offset` parameter is the number of rows to skip before the first returned row; it defaults to 0. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is a request-shape failure and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and so is a `limit` or `offset` whose text is not a plain base-ten integer literal — a decimal such as `1.5`, an empty value, or any other non-numeric string.

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are ordered by the resource's creation timestamp, `created_at`, in ascending order. Because the logical clock advances only when a request repositions it, many rows legitimately share a `created_at`; rows that tie are ordered by the sequence in which the server created them, so the ordering is total and stable and a row's `id` is never consulted as a tie-break. Apart from the `customer_id` filter a `CustomerRep` is obliged to supply per §2.1, every filter query parameter is optional, and several supplied together combine conjunctively. A value supplied for a filter whose domain is an enumeration must belong to that enumeration, or the read is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; a filter whose domain is an identifier is matched literally and is never resolved as a foreign key, so one naming no existing row yields an empty page rather than a 404. Query parameters a given list read does not declare are ignored rather than rejected.

List responses carry four pagination fields inside the envelope's `meta` object: `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`. `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied, and `has_more` is true exactly when rows remain beyond the returned page.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Addressing and the Immutable Accuracy Ledger

Almost every record in this system is a peer: planning cycles, product families, items, customers, capacity blocks, promotions, demand forecast lines, consensus adjustments, supply plans, allocations, scenarios and accuracy records each live at their own top-level collection beneath the `/sop` prefix, and relationships between them travel as identifier fields in request bodies and as filters on list reads rather than as nested URL segments.

All of those records carry a standard set of server-managed properties:

- **`id`** (string) — a server-minted UUID. Identifiers carry no type prefix; a cycle's `id` and an item's `id` are indistinguishable by shape alone.
- **`created_at`** (string) — an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`, taken from the logical clock at the moment the row was created.
- **`updated_at`** (string) — an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format, refreshed on every mutation of the row, including mutations the server performs on its own initiative as a cascade of some other request.
- **`version`** (integer) — a counter that starts at 1 on creation and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of the row. It is the token that the optimistic-concurrency rules in §2.7 compare against.

`AccuracyRecord` is the sole exception and it is deliberate. A posted actual is an audit fact that nothing may ever rewrite, so an accuracy record carries only `id` and `created_at`: it has **no** `updated_at` field and **no** `version` field, and no endpoint exists that could modify one once written.

Where an identifier does appear in a URL path, the path variable names are `{cycle_id}` for a planning cycle, `{family_id}` for a product family, `{item_id}` for an item, `{customer_id}` for a trade customer, `{block_id}` for a capacity block, `{promotion_id}` for a promotion, `{forecast_id}` for a demand forecast line, `{adjustment_id}` for a consensus adjustment, `{supply_plan_id}` for a supply plan, `{allocation_id}` for an allocation, `{scenario_id}` for a scenario and `{record_id}` for an accuracy record. Path identifiers are resolved strictly left to right, and the first segment that resolves to nothing determines the rejection: the response is the 404 that belongs to that segment's entity type — `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FAMILY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CUSTOMER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CAPACITY_BLOCK_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROMOTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FORECAST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ADJUSTMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SUPPLY_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ALLOCATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SCENARIO_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_ACCURACY_RECORD_NOT_FOUND` — and no later validation runs. The surface contains exactly one two-segment read, the cycle accuracy summary described in §3.9, and it is a computed projection rather than a child collection: the cycle in its path must resolve first, and a request naming an unknown cycle there fails with `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND` before the summary is attempted.

Foreign-key identifiers supplied in a request **body** are resolved after path identifiers, and each resolves to the 404 of the entity it names. A `family_id` that matches no product family yields `ERR_FAMILY_NOT_FOUND`; an `item_id`, `successor_item_id` or `target_item_id` that matches no item yields `ERR_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`; an unknown `customer_id` yields `ERR_CUSTOMER_NOT_FOUND`; an unknown `cycle_id` yields `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND`; an unknown `demand_forecast_id` yields `ERR_FORECAST_NOT_FOUND`; and an unknown `promotion_id` yields `ERR_PROMOTION_NOT_FOUND`.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock and Calendar Conventions

Time in this system is a logical clock, never the host's wall clock. Before any request repositions it, the clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to wall-clock time under any circumstance. An `SopAdmin` capability repositions the clock to a supplied `to_iso8601` instant and echoes the new setting back as `current_utc` — the sole field of that response's `data` object. A trailing `Z` is accepted, an explicit UTC offset is honoured and converted to UTC, and an instant carrying no zone at all is read as already being UTC; the echoed `current_utc` is always the normalised three-fractional-digit UTC rendering of the resulting instant rather than the caller's own spelling of it. A `to_iso8601` that is missing, empty or not a parseable ISO 8601 instant is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Repositioning the clock modifies no business rows. This capability is exempt from the header and role checks of §2.1, and the only business rule that consults the clock directly is the promotion frozen-horizon rule of §3.4. The clock is also what stamps `created_at`, `updated_at` and a cycle's `published_at`.

Three calendar formats and three interval conventions recur, and mixing them up produces wrong plans:

- A **month string** has the form `YYYY-MM`, for example `2026-09`. Month strings are compared lexicographically, which for this format is the same as chronological comparison. `horizon_start_month`, `horizon_end_month`, `month`, `start_month`, `end_month` and `runout_end_month` are all month strings. A month string is well-formed only when its month part lies between `01` and `12`, so `2026-13` or `2026-00` is an `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than a month that merely sorts oddly.
- A **date string** has the form `YYYY-MM-DD`. `start_date` and `end_date` on a capacity block are date strings. Beyond matching the shape, a date string must name a day that exists on the calendar, so `2026-02-30` is an `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
- A promotion's month window `start_month` through `end_month` is **closed and inclusive** on both ends: a promotion running `2026-09` to `2026-11` covers September, October and November. Two closed windows overlap when they share at least one month; windows that merely abut, such as one ending `2026-09` and another starting `2026-10`, do not overlap.
- A capacity block's date window `start_date` through `end_date` is **half-open**: the start date is included and the end date is excluded. `end_date` must be strictly later than `start_date`.
- A cycle's horizon window, used when deciding which capacity blocks a supply plan may consume, is likewise half-open: it begins on the first calendar day of `horizon_start_month` and ends on the first calendar day of the month immediately following `horizon_end_month`.

### 2.6. Numeric Precision and Rounding Discipline

Rounding is a contract, not an implementation detail, because the same inputs rounded in a different order yield different integers. Wherever this document says a value is *rounded*, the method is **round half up**: a fractional part of exactly one half rounds away from zero, so 2.5 becomes 3 and 0.125 rounded to two decimals becomes 0.13. Wherever it says a value is *ceilinged*, the value is raised to the next integer unless it is already an integer. Wherever it says a value is *floored*, the value is truncated down to the next integer.

The specific commitments are:

- **Unit quantities are integers.** `baseline_units`, `consensus_units`, `applied_delta_units`, `gross_requirement_units`, `target_build_units`, `available_units`, `material_cap_units`, `demand_side_units`, `constrained_units`, `shortfall_units`, `unallocated_units`, `requested_units`, `allocated_units`, `incremental_units`, `safety_stock_units`, `reorder_point_units`, `actual_units`, `forecast_units_snapshot`, `abs_error_units` and `signed_error_units` are all integer units. `signed_error_units` is the one routinely negative figure here, but it is not the only one that can be: nothing clamps `baseline_units`, `consensus_units` or `applied_delta_units`, so a negative `delta_units`, a cannibalisation term larger than its lift term, or a `trend_units_per_month` steep enough to drive the level term below zero each carry straight through to a negative integer.
- **Money is integer cents.** `financial_commitment_cents`, `unit_price_cents`, `unit_cost_cents`, `plan_value_cents`, `gross_gap_cents`, `selected_closure_cents`, `residual_gap_cents`, `gross_uplift_cents`, `investment_cost_cents`, `pre_trade_closure_cents`, `trade_spend_cents` and `net_gap_closure_cents` are whole cents. Gap and closure figures may be negative.
- **Percentages that the server derives are carried to exactly two decimal places**, rounded half up: `fill_rate_pct`, `ape_pct`, `rolling_wmape_pct` and `rolling_bias_pct`, and likewise the `mape_pct`, `wmape_pct` and `bias_pct` figures reported by the cycle accuracy summary. `trade_rate_pct` is a server-selected tier value taken from a fixed set.
- **Rates, factors and hour quantities the client supplies keep the precision they were sent with**: `npi_ramp_pct`, `service_level_target_pct`, `demand_cv`, `seasonal_index`, `causal_uplift_pct`, `trend_units_per_month`, `lift_pct`, `cannibalization_pct`, `price_increase_pct`, `units_per_hour`, `max_monthly_hours`, `available_hours` and `added_capacity_hours` are stored as given.
- **Round once, and round where this document says to round.** A baseline applies exactly one rounding, at the very end of its expression. A consensus adjustment's delta is rounded per adjustment line and the already-integer results are then summed — summing unrounded percentage terms across adjustment lines and rounding the total once is wrong and produces a different integer. A reorder point ceilings its cycle-stock term first and adds the already-ceilinged safety stock to it, rather than ceilinging the sum in one pass. The hours-to-units conversion rounds the product of hours and throughput to six decimal places to defuse binary floating-point drift, and only then floors it.
- **Percentage inputs are percentages, not fractions.** A `causal_uplift_pct` of 10 means the raw value is multiplied by 1 plus ten hundredths; a `lift_pct` of 20 means twenty hundredths of the baseline.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on the Parent's Version

Two writes in this surface can legitimately discard work that the caller may never have seen, so both require the caller to prove it read the parent record's latest state. In each case the check is a comparison against the parent row's current `version`, and the parent is a different record from the one being written.

Creating a consensus adjustment requires a non-optional `expected_demand_forecast_version` field in the request body, carrying the `version` of the target `DemandForecast` as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the forecast's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_FORECAST_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This guard exists because a second adjustment of the same `adjustment_type` from the same proposer on the same forecast legitimately supersedes and voids the earlier proposed one, so no state precondition could catch the mistake — the client's stale view of the forecast line is the only evidence that it never saw the adjustment it is about to discard.

Selecting a scenario requires a non-optional `expected_cycle_version` field in the request body, carrying the `version` of the governing `PlanningCycle` as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the cycle's current `version`, the selection is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CYCLE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Here too the write is legitimate on its own terms — selecting a scenario supersedes the cycle's other selected scenario of the same `scenario_type` and rewrites `selected_closure_cents` and `residual_gap_cents` — so a stale version is the only way to detect that the selecting executive never saw the closure it is replacing.

Resubmitting a consensus adjustment likewise requires `expected_demand_forecast_version` against the forecast's current `version`, and a mismatch there is also `ERR_STALE_FORECAST_VERSION`. No other endpoint in this surface accepts a version field, and no other endpoint performs a version comparison; where this document does not name a version field, none is expected and none may be required.

### 2.8. Cycle Posture, Phase Gating and the Frozen Cycle

Every planning cycle carries a derived gate called `posture`, one of `GREEN`, `AT_RISK` or `FROZEN`, and it defaults to `GREEN` on creation. Posture is recomputed immediately after every recomputation of the cycle's money fields, and it is evaluated in this order with the first matching rule winning: if the cycle's `status` is `PUBLISHED`, posture is `FROZEN`; otherwise, if the cycle's `residual_gap_cents` is greater than zero **or** its `constrained_plan_count` is at least 1, posture is `AT_RISK`; otherwise posture is `GREEN`.

A `FROZEN` posture is an absolute write barrier over the cycle's planning artefacts. While a cycle is frozen, no forecast line may be created or transitioned, no adjustment may be created, reviewed or resubmitted, no supply plan may be created or rationed, and no scenario may be created, evaluated or selected within that cycle; every such attempt is rejected with `ERR_CYCLE_FROZEN` (HTTP 409). That is ten operations in total, and it is the reason publication is a one-way door.

Separately from the freeze, each phase of the cycle admits only its own class of write, and this shows up as two distinct rejections. The first is a phase-actor mismatch: consensus adjustments may only be created, resubmitted or reviewed while the cycle's `status` is `DEMAND_REVIEW`; supply plans may only be created or rationed while it is `SUPPLY_REVIEW`; scenarios may only be created or evaluated while it is `SCENARIO_REVIEW`; a scenario may only be selected while it is `EXECUTIVE_REVIEW`; and accuracy records may only be created while it is `PUBLISHED`. An operation belonging to a different phase than the cycle currently occupies is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED_PHASE_ACTOR` (HTTP 403), and this check covers exactly those nine operations: adjustment create, adjustment review, adjustment resubmit, supply-plan create, supply-plan ration, scenario create, scenario evaluate, scenario select and accuracy-record create. For those nine, **any** mismatch between the cycle's `status` and the status the operation belongs to is a phase-actor rejection, whatever the acting role: role ownership of the phase is no excuse and never softens the rejection. The second rejection, `ERR_CYCLE_PHASE_CLOSED` (HTTP 409), belongs to the two writes the phase-actor check omits — forecast-line creation and the `BASELINE → UNDER_CONSENSUS` forecast-line transition, the only forecast-line transition that carries a cycle-phase precondition of its own per §3.3 — and it fires when the cycle has moved past `DEMAND_REVIEW`, the phase that admits those writes, whose owning roles are the only ones authorised for them anyway. Because the phase-actor check sits earlier in the rejection ladder of §2.9, it wins wherever both could apply, so none of the nine phase-actor operations can ever observably return `ERR_CYCLE_PHASE_CLOSED`.

### 2.9. Rejection Precedence

When a single request violates several rules, exactly one error is returned, and which one is not a matter of taste. Checks are evaluated in the following order, and the first violation encountered ends the request:

1. **Authentication.** `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` are both present, non-empty, and the role is one of the six declared in §2.1 — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. Applies to every endpoint except the logical-clock capability.
2. **Authorisation.** The named role appears among the target endpoint's authorised roles — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`. Applies to every endpoint except the logical-clock capability.
3. **Request shape.** The body parses; every required field is present; every field has its declared type; every enum value belongs to its declared set; every declared numeric range holds; every month string matches `YYYY-MM` and every date string matches `YYYY-MM-DD`; no window is inverted. Conditionally required fields are checked here too. Any failure is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). Unknown fields in a body are ignored rather than rejected. An explicitly `null` value is treated exactly as if the field had been omitted: on an optional field the declared default applies, and on a required field the rejection is the ordinary missing-field `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A field declared as an integer accepts only a JSON integer — a value carrying a fractional part, a numeric string or a boolean is rejected — while a field declared as a number accepts an integer or a decimal but never a boolean, and a field declared as a string accepts only a string. A request whose body is absent or empty is read as the empty JSON object, so its required fields simply report as missing, while a body that parses to anything other than a JSON object, or that is not valid JSON at all, is itself `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
4. **Path existence.** Every identifier in the URL path resolves, left to right, per §2.4.
5. **Body reference existence.** Every foreign-key identifier in the body resolves, per §2.4. This applies to item creation, item lifecycle transitions, capacity-block creation, promotion creation, forecast creation, adjustment creation, supply-plan creation, scenario creation and accuracy-record creation.
6. **Phase actor.** `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED_PHASE_ACTOR`, per §2.8.
7. **Customer scope.** `ERR_CUSTOMER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`, per §2.1.
8. **Segregation of duties.** `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, per §2.1.
9. **Optimistic concurrency.** The supplied `expected_demand_forecast_version` or `expected_cycle_version` equals the parent's current `version`, per §2.7.
10. **Frozen cycle.** `ERR_CYCLE_FROZEN`, per §2.8.
11. **Phase closed.** `ERR_CYCLE_PHASE_CLOSED`, per §2.8.
12. **Transition legality.** The requested transition is declared for the subject row's current state. Depending on the subject, the rejection is `ERR_CYCLE_TRANSITION_INVALID`, `ERR_FORECAST_TRANSITION_INVALID`, `ERR_ADJUSTMENT_NOT_REVIEWABLE`, `ERR_ADJUSTMENT_NOT_RESUBMITTABLE`, `ERR_ITEM_LIFECYCLE_TRANSITION_INVALID`, `ERR_SUPPLY_PLAN_NOT_RATIONABLE`, `ERR_SCENARIO_NOT_EVALUATABLE`, `ERR_SCENARIO_NOT_SELECTABLE` or `ERR_PROMOTION_NOT_APPROVABLE`.
13. **Cross-entity referential eligibility**, checked before any temporal or capacity arithmetic: `ERR_ITEM_NOT_PROMOTABLE`, `ERR_PROMOTION_NOT_APPROVED`, `ERR_ADJUSTMENT_TYPE_NOT_PERMITTED`, `ERR_SUCCESSOR_NOT_ELIGIBLE`, `ERR_FORECAST_NOT_LOCKED`, `ERR_OPEN_ADJUSTMENTS`, `ERR_STALE_SUPPLY_PLAN` and `ERR_UNEVALUATED_SCENARIO`. The individual conditions are stated in §3 alongside the workflows they guard.
14. **Interval and calendar checks**: `ERR_PROMOTION_WINDOW_OVERLAP`, `ERR_CAPACITY_BLOCK_OVERLAP`, `ERR_PROMOTION_HORIZON_FROZEN` and `ERR_MONTH_OUTSIDE_HORIZON`.
15. **Aggregate capacity and uniqueness**: `ERR_FAMILY_MONTHLY_HOURS_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_FORECAST`, `ERR_SUPPLY_PLAN_EXISTS`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACCURACY_RECORD`, and the global-uniqueness violations of `family_code`, `sku`, `customer_code` and `cycle_code`, which surface as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
16. **Rate and volume safety limits**: `ERR_REOPEN_LIMIT_REACHED`, `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED` and `ERR_RUNOUT_INVENTORY_REMAINS`.
17. **Financial guardrails**, evaluated last because they depend on the arithmetic of the calculations in §3: `ERR_ADJUSTMENT_SWING_EXCEEDED` and `ERR_UNCLOSED_GAP`.

The declared numeric ranges enforced at step 3 are: `unit_price_cents` greater than zero; `unit_cost_cents` greater than zero; `on_hand_units` at least zero; `material_supply_cap_units` at least zero; `avg_monthly_demand_units` at least zero; `npi_ramp_pct` at least 0 and at most 200; `priority_rank` at least 1; `lift_pct` greater than 0 and at most 300; `cannibalization_pct` at least 0 and at most 100; `service_level_target_pct` greater than 0 and at most 100; `demand_cv` at least 0 and at most 3; `lead_time_days` at least 1; `seasonal_index` greater than 0 and at most 5; `causal_uplift_pct` at least -100 and at most 500; `level_units` at least zero; `financial_commitment_cents` at least zero; `units_per_hour` greater than zero; `max_monthly_hours` greater than zero; `available_hours` greater than zero; `added_capacity_hours` greater than zero when present; `price_increase_pct` greater than 0 and at most 100 when present; `actual_units` at least zero; `horizon_end_month` at or after `horizon_start_month`; `end_month` at or after `start_month`; and `end_date` strictly after `start_date`.

The conditionally required fields enforced at step 3 are: `promotion_id` is required when `adjustment_type` is `PROMOTION` and must be absent or null for `SALES_INTELLIGENCE` and `NEW_ITEM_PLAN`, while `delta_units` is required on every adjustment create regardless of type; `target_item_id` and `added_capacity_hours` are required when `scenario_type` is `CAPACITY_INVESTMENT` and must be absent or null for `PRICE_REALIZATION`, while `price_increase_pct` is required for `PRICE_REALIZATION` and must be absent or null for `CAPACITY_INVESTMENT`; `runout_end_month` is required when an item's `target_state` is `RUNOUT` and must be absent or null for every other target state; and `reopen_reason` is required when a cycle's `target_status` is `DEMAND_REVIEW`.

### 2.10. Cascades, Version Bumps and Conserved Quantities

Consistency in this system is immediate: by the time a request returns, every record its action touched — directly or through a cascade — already reflects the new reality. Cascades run to their full depth in the same request, and the runout cascade of §3.8 is three hops deep. Each record a cascade rewrites is an independent mutation: its `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increases by 1, exactly as if a client had written it. A single item lifecycle transition can therefore bump the versions of promotions, adjustments, forecast lines, supply plans, allocations and a planning cycle alongside the item's own.

Two quantities are conserved exactly, with no tolerance, and every implementation must preserve them.

**Allocation units on a single supply plan.** The sum of `allocated_units` across all of that plan's allocation rows, plus the plan's `unallocated_units`, always equals the plan's `constrained_units`. Every allocation row participates in that sum regardless of its `allocation_status` — rows in `ALLOCATED`, rows in `WITHHELD_CREDIT_HOLD` and rows in `VOIDED` all count, the latter two contributing zero. The identity holds in all three states of a plan's life: before rationing, when nothing is allocated and the leftover equals the constrained quantity; after rationing, when granted units plus leftover equal it; and after a staleness compensation has voided the rows, when the leftover has been restored to the full constrained quantity. Forward action and compensation therefore leave zero drift.

**Gap-closing money on a single planning cycle.** A cycle's `residual_gap_cents` always equals its `financial_commitment_cents`, minus its `plan_value_cents`, minus the sum of `net_gap_closure_cents` over that cycle's scenarios in `SELECTED` or `EXECUTED` status. Superseded scenarios contribute nothing even though their own `net_gap_closure_cents` field retains the value it was evaluated at. When a second scenario of the same `scenario_type` is selected, the first one's contribution is subtracted out in the very same request that adds the second one's, so the residual gap lands exactly on the gross gap less the new scenario's closure, with no residue from the old one.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

The narrative below follows the S&OP calendar: master data first, then capacity, then the demand review, the supply review, rationing, scenario and executive review, and finally the two things that outlive a single cycle — the item lifecycle and forecast accountability.

### 3.1. Master Data: Product Families, Items and Trade Customers

Everything in this system hangs off a **product family**, the asset-group-plus-accountability unit that a `SopAdmin` registers before anything else can be planned. A family is created with a `family_code` such as `FAM-SNACK` — non-empty and globally unique across all families — a display `name` that is required but, unlike the code, may be sent as the empty string, an `accountable_planner_user_id` naming the `DemandPlanner` who carries the family's forecast accuracy, a `units_per_hour` throughput rate greater than zero that converts scheduled run hours into producible units, and a `max_monthly_hours` ceiling greater than zero that caps how many hours may be scheduled against the family's asset group in any one calendar month. A newly registered family starts with no accuracy history at all: its `rolling_wmape_pct` and `rolling_bias_pct` are both null, its `bias_flag` is `NONE`, its `accuracy_grade` is `UNGRADED`, and its `scored_record_count` is 0. Families can be listed with an optional filter on `accountable_planner_user_id` or on `accuracy_grade`, which is how a review lead finds every family a given planner owns or every family currently graded `D`.

Inside a family, an `SopAdmin` creates **items**. An item is identified by a `sku` that is non-empty and globally unique, and it belongs to a family through `family_id`. Its commercial parameters are `unit_price_cents`, the net selling price per unit used to value plans, and `unit_cost_cents`, the standard cost per unit; both must be greater than zero. Its supply-side parameters are `on_hand_units`, the current finished-goods inventory position, and `material_supply_cap_units`, a hard ceiling on producible units imposed by component and material availability; both must be at least zero. Its demand-side parameter is `avg_monthly_demand_units`, the demand rate that drives inventory policy, also at least zero. An optional `npi_ramp_pct` scales new-item volumes and defaults to 100 — meaning no scaling — and is constrained to the range from 0 through 200 inclusive.

Item creation also seeds the three inventory-policy parameters, each of which may be supplied on the create and each of which has a default: `service_level_target_pct` defaults to 95.0 and must be greater than 0 and at most 100; `demand_cv`, the coefficient of variation of demand, defaults to 0.30 and must be at least 0 and at most 3; and `lead_time_days`, the replenishment lead time, defaults to 30 and must be at least 1. One further optional input deserves attention because its default is deliberately not the enum's own default: `lifecycle_state` may be supplied on the create, and **when it is omitted the new item is created as `MATURE`**, not as `PRELAUNCH`. That makes the common case — registering an item that is already selling — a single call, while a genuine new-product introduction is created by explicitly passing `PRELAUNCH`. The lifecycle values are `PRELAUNCH`, `RAMP`, `MATURE`, `RUNOUT`, `DISCONTINUED` and `CANCELLED_NPI`, and the create accepts any one of the six as the seed state without imposing any reachability or gating rule of its own — the transition graph of §3.8 governs movement between states, not the choice of the initial one. A freshly created item has no `successor_item_id` and no `runout_end_month`; both are null until the item enters runout, and neither can be seeded on the create even when the seed state is `RUNOUT`.

**Inventory policy** is derived, never supplied. Whenever an item is created, and again whenever an `SopAdmin` or `SupplyPlanner` resets the item's policy by supplying a new `service_level_target_pct`, `demand_cv` and `lead_time_days` together — all three are required on the reset, which is admitted whatever the item's `lifecycle_state`, a `DISCONTINUED` or `CANCELLED_NPI` item included — the server recomputes two integers. First it selects a service-level safety factor from a tiered table, testing the bands in descending order and taking the first that matches: a target of 99.0 percent or higher uses a factor of 2.33; failing that, 97.5 percent or higher uses 1.96; failing that, 95.0 percent or higher uses 1.65; failing that, 90.0 percent or higher uses 1.28; and anything below 90.0 percent uses a floor factor of 0.84. Then `safety_stock_units` is the safety factor multiplied by `demand_cv`, multiplied by `avg_monthly_demand_units`, multiplied by the square root of `lead_time_days` divided by thirty — the whole product raised to the next integer by a single ceiling applied once at the end, with the intermediate multiplications kept at full precision. Finally `reorder_point_units` is computed in a pinned order: the cycle-stock term, `avg_monthly_demand_units` multiplied by `lead_time_days` and divided by thirty, is ceilinged to an integer **first**, and the already-ceilinged `safety_stock_units` is then added to it. Ceilinging the sum of the two terms in one pass is forbidden and yields a different integer. The thirty in both expressions is the standard month length this system uses to convert a lead time expressed in days into months.

Resetting an item's inventory policy is not a harmless master-data edit, because supply plans snapshot safety stock at plan time. Every supply plan of that item which is in `PLANNED` or `RATIONED` is therefore invalidated by the reset: it transitions to `STALE`, its `requires_replan` flag is set to true, and the consequences described in §3.6 and §3.7 follow; a plan already `COMMITTED` is left exactly as it stands, as is one already `STALE`.

Finally, an `SopAdmin` registers the **trade customers** that demand is planned against. A customer carries a `customer_code` such as `NATL-A` that is non-empty and globally unique, a display `name` under the same required-but-possibly-empty rule as a family's, and a `channel` drawn from `GROCERY`, `CLUB`, `DISCOUNT` and `ECOMMERCE`. Its `priority_rank` is an integer of at least 1 and governs rationing: lower ranks are served first, and the very lowest ranks are protected outright as described in §3.6. An optional `credit_hold` boolean defaults to false; when it is true the customer is withheld from every allocation pass. Customers can be listed filtered by `channel` and by `credit_hold`.

### 3.2. Publishing Capacity

Before a supply review can bind anything, a `SopAdmin` or `SupplyPlanner` declares the run hours available on a family's asset group. Each **capacity block** names its `family_id`, an inclusive `start_date`, an exclusive `end_date`, and `available_hours` greater than zero — the scheduled run hours on that asset group over that interval.

Two rules police the capacity calendar, and both are aggregate checks rather than per-row ones. The first is a changeover buffer. Production lines need two days of changeover between scheduled blocks, so an incoming block is accepted only if it clears every existing block of the same family by at least two days in one direction or the other: either the incoming `start_date` falls on or after the existing block's `end_date` plus two days, or the existing block's `start_date` falls on or after the incoming `end_date` plus two days. Anything closer than that — including two blocks that are exactly adjacent, where one block's `end_date` is the next block's `start_date` — is rejected with `ERR_CAPACITY_BLOCK_OVERLAP`. Note the asymmetry against the promotion rule of §3.4: abutting promotion windows are fine, abutting capacity blocks are not.

The second rule is the family's monthly hour ceiling. Blocks are bucketed into calendar months by their `start_date` alone, regardless of how far the block's interval extends. If adding the incoming block would push the total `available_hours` of all blocks of that family whose `start_date` falls in the same calendar month above the family's `max_monthly_hours`, the block is rejected with `ERR_FAMILY_MONTHLY_HOURS_EXCEEDED`. Capacity blocks are readable individually and as a list filtered by `family_id`.

### 3.3. Opening a Cycle and Building the Statistical Baseline

An `SopAdmin` opens a monthly S&OP cycle. The cycle carries a `cycle_code` such as `CY-2026-09`, non-empty and globally unique; a horizon spanning `horizon_start_month` through `horizon_end_month` inclusive, where the end month may not precede the start month; a `financial_commitment_cents` figure of at least zero, being the revenue the executive team has already committed for that horizon; and a `supply_review_policy` of either `NET_INVENTORY_FIRST` or `GROSS_CAPACITY_FIRST`, which selects the netting variant the supply review will use and cannot be changed afterwards.

A new cycle opens in `DEMAND_REVIEW` with `reopen_count` at 0, `plan_value_cents` at 0 and `constrained_plan_count` at 0, and its `published_at` is null. The server then runs the money roll-up and the posture rule of §2.8 immediately, so a freshly opened cycle has `gross_gap_cents` equal to its whole `financial_commitment_cents`, `selected_closure_cents` at 0, `residual_gap_cents` equal to `gross_gap_cents`, and consequently a `posture` of `AT_RISK` whenever the committed revenue is positive. Cycles can be listed filtered by `status` and by `posture`, and a single cycle read returns its money reconciliation fields, its posture and its `version` — the last of which is the token an executive needs before selecting a scenario.

Within a cycle in `DEMAND_REVIEW`, an `SopAdmin` or `DemandPlanner` lays down one **forecast line** per combination of cycle, item, customer and month. The line's subject is fixed by `cycle_id`, `item_id`, `customer_id` and `month`; the three body identifiers are resolved in exactly that order — `cycle_id`, then `item_id`, then `customer_id` — so a create naming more than one unknown row reports the earliest of the three. At most one line may exist for that quadruple — a second attempt is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_FORECAST`. The forecast `month` must fall inside the cycle's horizon, at or after `horizon_start_month` and at or before `horizon_end_month`; a month outside that span is rejected with `ERR_MONTH_OUTSIDE_HORIZON`.

The statistical inputs on a line are four: `level_units`, the de-seasonalised level term, at least zero; `trend_units_per_month`, the linear trend per month of horizon offset, which may be negative; `seasonal_index`, the multiplicative seasonal index for that month, greater than 0 and at most 5; and `causal_uplift_pct`, the causal-factor uplift expressed as a percent, at least -100 and at most 500. The server derives the rest.

**Method selection** happens first, and it reads the item's state as it stands at the moment the line is created. Three candidates are tested in order and the first match wins. If the item's `lifecycle_state` is `PRELAUNCH`, the `method` is `ANALOG_PROFILE` — a new item has no history of its own, so an analogue's shape is used. Otherwise, if the item's `demand_cv` is at least 0.75, the `method` is `CROSTON_FLAT`, the treatment reserved for intermittent, lumpy demand. Otherwise the `method` is `HOLT_WINTERS`. A `demand_cv` of exactly 0.75 selects `CROSTON_FLAT`; 0.74 selects `HOLT_WINTERS`.

**Horizon offset** is next: a whole number of months, zero-based, counting from the cycle's horizon start to the forecast's month. It is the difference in years between the forecast `month` and `horizon_start_month` multiplied by twelve, plus the difference in month numbers between the two. A line for the cycle's first month has an offset of 0; a line thirteen months later has an offset of 13.

**The raw level** then depends on which method was selected, and the three variants differ in exactly which terms survive:

- Under `HOLT_WINTERS`, both trend and seasonality participate: the raw level is `level_units` plus `trend_units_per_month` multiplied by the horizon offset, and that whole sum is multiplied by `seasonal_index`.
- Under `CROSTON_FLAT`, both trend and seasonality are suppressed entirely: the raw level is simply `level_units`. Neither the trend term nor the seasonal index touches it.
- Under `ANALOG_PROFILE`, trend alone is suppressed and the level is scaled by the item's ramp factor: the raw level is `level_units` multiplied by `npi_ramp_pct` divided by one hundred, and that product is then multiplied by `seasonal_index`.

**The causal factor closes the calculation**, and there is exactly one rounding in the whole computation. `baseline_units` is the raw level multiplied by one plus `causal_uplift_pct` divided by one hundred, rounded half up to a whole number of units. No intermediate result is rounded before that point. A `causal_uplift_pct` of 10 therefore multiplies by 1.1, a value of -100 collapses the baseline to zero, and a value of 0 leaves the raw level untouched apart from the rounding.

A new line is created in `BASELINE` status with `endorsed_adjustment_count` at 0 and `consensus_units` equal to `baseline_units`, because no adjustment exists yet. Forecast lines are readable individually — the read exposes `baseline_units`, `consensus_units` and the line's `version`, which is the concurrency token adjustment writes must carry — and as a list filtered by `cycle_id`, `item_id`, `customer_id`, `month` and `status` — the `month` filter must itself be a well-formed `YYYY-MM` month string, or the read is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — subject to the customer-scoping rule of §2.1.

A forecast line moves through four statuses: `BASELINE`, `UNDER_CONSENSUS`, `CONSENSUS_LOCKED` and the terminal `RUNOUT_TRUNCATED`. An `SopAdmin`, `DemandPlanner` or `DemandReviewLead` may drive a line by naming a `target_status`, and each such transition increments the line's `version` by 1. Moving `BASELINE → UNDER_CONSENSUS` requires the line to currently be in `BASELINE` and the governing cycle to be in `DEMAND_REVIEW`; it also happens implicitly, driven by the server, when the first adjustment is created against a line still in `BASELINE`. Moving `UNDER_CONSENSUS → CONSENSUS_LOCKED` is permitted only when the line carries no adjustment in `PROPOSED` or `REVISION_REQUESTED`; an attempt with an open adjustment outstanding is rejected with `ERR_OPEN_ADJUSTMENTS`. That lock freezes `consensus_units` at its then-current rolled-up value and commits every `ENDORSED` adjustment on the line to `COMMITTED`, re-running the roll-up as it does so. A line may also be locked straight from `BASELINE → CONSENSUS_LOCKED`, which has no precondition beyond the line being in `BASELINE`, and freezes `consensus_units` at a value equal to `baseline_units` since nothing was ever adjusted. Moving `CONSENSUS_LOCKED → UNDER_CONSENSUS` is the rework edge and carries no precondition of its own: the line becomes adjustable again, its already-`COMMITTED` adjustments return to `ENDORSED` and continue contributing to `consensus_units` either way, and every supply plan of that line's item that is in `PLANNED` or `RATIONED` — in whatever cycle it sits, not merely this line's own — is marked `STALE`, while a plan already `COMMITTED` is left exactly as it stands. Because a line can be locked, reopened and locked again, this edge makes the consensus workflow cyclic; the number of round trips is bounded in practice by the cycle's reopen limit. Any `target_status` that is not reachable from the line's present status — including any attempt to leave the terminal `RUNOUT_TRUNCATED` — is rejected with `ERR_FORECAST_TRANSITION_INVALID`.

### 3.4. Trade Promotions and the Consensus Adjustment Loop

Consensus is where sales intelligence, promotional plans and new-item plans are folded into the statistical baseline, and it is the most heavily governed part of the cycle.

**Promotions come first**, because an adjustment that cites one can only cite an approved one. An `SopAdmin` or `DemandPlanner` drafts a promotion naming the promoted `item_id`, the trade `customer_id` it runs at, an inclusive `start_month` and `end_month` where the end may not precede the start, a `lift_pct` greater than 0 and at most 300, and a `cannibalization_pct` of at least 0 and at most 100 representing the share of baseline lost to cannibalised sibling products. The two body identifiers are resolved `item_id` first and `customer_id` second, so a draft naming an unknown item and an unknown customer at once fails with `ERR_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`. Drafting and approving a promotion consult no planning cycle at all, so neither operation is gated on any cycle's `status` or `posture`. A promotion is created in `DRAFT`, and a `DRAFT` promotion is invisible to the consensus roll-up — citing one has no effect on any forecast.

Approval is the exclusive province of the `DemandReviewLead` and moves the promotion `DRAFT → APPROVED`, incrementing its `version`. Four things are checked. The promotion must currently be in `DRAFT`; one already `APPROVED` or already `CANCELLED_RUNOUT` is rejected with `ERR_PROMOTION_NOT_APPROVABLE`. The promoted item must be sellable, meaning its `lifecycle_state` is either `RAMP` or `MATURE`; a `PRELAUNCH`, `RUNOUT`, `DISCONTINUED` or `CANCELLED_NPI` item yields `ERR_ITEM_NOT_PROMOTABLE`. The promotion's month window must not overlap the window of any other `APPROVED` promotion for the same item and customer pair, under the closed-inclusive semantics of §2.5 — sharing even a single month is an overlap and yields `ERR_PROMOTION_WINDOW_OVERLAP`, while two windows that merely abut at adjacent months are accepted. And the promotion's horizon must still be open: `start_month` must be strictly later than the current logical month, so a promotion starting in the current logical month or any earlier month is rejected with `ERR_PROMOTION_HORIZON_FROZEN`. Once approved, the promotion becomes citable as an adjustment's `promotion_id`. Promotions can be listed filtered by `item_id`, `customer_id` and `status`.

**Adjustments** are proposed exclusively by a `DemandPlanner` against a forecast line, and only while the governing cycle is in `DEMAND_REVIEW`. A proposal names the `demand_forecast_id` of the line being adjusted, an `adjustment_type` of `SALES_INTELLIGENCE`, `PROMOTION` or `NEW_ITEM_PLAN`, a `delta_units` integer, a non-empty `rationale` justifying the change, optionally a `promotion_id`, and the `expected_demand_forecast_version` token described in §2.7. The two body identifiers are resolved `demand_forecast_id` first and `promotion_id` second, so a proposal naming an unknown line and an unknown promotion at once fails with `ERR_FORECAST_NOT_FOUND`. The `delta_units` field is required on every proposal regardless of type, but for a `PROMOTION` adjustment it is **ignored**: that type's delta is derived entirely from the cited promotion's `lift_pct` and `cannibalization_pct`, and the convention is to send 0.

Eligibility of the adjustment type against the item is checked before any arithmetic. A `PROMOTION` adjustment's `promotion_id` must reference a promotion that is `APPROVED` **and** whose item is the same item as the target forecast line's; a promotion that is still `DRAFT`, one that has been cancelled, or one attached to a different item is rejected with `ERR_PROMOTION_NOT_APPROVED`. A `NEW_ITEM_PLAN` adjustment is permitted only when the target line's item is in `PRELAUNCH` or `RAMP`, and a `SALES_INTELLIGENCE` adjustment is forbidden on an item in `PRELAUNCH`; either misuse is rejected with `ERR_ADJUSTMENT_TYPE_NOT_PERMITTED`.

A proposal is created in `PROPOSED` with `revision_round` at 0, `reviewed_by_user_id` and `review_note` both null, and `proposed_by_user_id` captured from the acting `X-User-ID`. The target line's own `status` is no precondition — a line already `CONSENSUS_LOCKED` or `RUNOUT_TRUNCATED` admits a proposal exactly as one in `UNDER_CONSENSUS` does. If the target line was still in `BASELINE`, the creation carries it to `UNDER_CONSENSUS`. Either way the forecast line's `version` increments by 1 — which is precisely why a client proposing two adjustments in a row must re-read the line between them.

**Per-line delta arithmetic** is where the three adjustment types diverge, and each branch is computed when the row is created and recomputed when it is resubmitted:

- For `PROMOTION`, two terms are computed independently and each is rounded half up to a whole number of units on its own: the lift term is the target line's `baseline_units` multiplied by the promotion's `lift_pct` divided by one hundred; the cannibalisation term is the same `baseline_units` multiplied by the promotion's `cannibalization_pct` divided by one hundred. `applied_delta_units` is then the rounded lift term minus the rounded cannibalisation term. Rounding the netted percentage in a single step instead of rounding the two terms separately is forbidden, because on many inputs it produces a different integer.
- For `SALES_INTELLIGENCE`, no rounding occurs at all: `applied_delta_units` is the planner's `delta_units` used verbatim, since it is already an integer.
- For `NEW_ITEM_PLAN`, `applied_delta_units` is `delta_units` multiplied by the item's `npi_ramp_pct` divided by one hundred, rounded half up to a whole number of units. A ramp factor of 100 therefore reproduces the planner's number exactly, a factor of 50 halves it, and a factor of 200 doubles it.

**The swing guardrail** is applied to that computed delta, and because it depends on the arithmetic it is the very last check on a proposal or a resubmission. A single adjustment's `applied_delta_units`, taken in absolute value, may not exceed a quarter of the target line's `baseline_units` — computed as `baseline_units` multiplied by twenty-five, divided by one hundred, rounded half up to a whole number of units. A delta exactly equal to that ceiling is accepted; one unit beyond it is rejected with `ERR_ADJUSTMENT_SWING_EXCEEDED`. No planner may move a single forecast line by more than a quarter of its statistical baseline in one stroke.

**The forecast roll-up** is what a reader of `consensus_units` actually sees. Only adjustments in `ENDORSED` or `COMMITTED` status contribute; rows in `PROPOSED`, `REVISION_REQUESTED`, `REJECTED` and `VOIDED` contribute nothing. `consensus_units` is the line's `baseline_units` plus the sum of `applied_delta_units` over the contributing rows, and `endorsed_adjustment_count` is how many rows contributed. Because each contribution is already an integer before it enters the sum — round per line, then sum — the order of summation is irrelevant and the result is deterministic; summing unrounded percentage terms across adjustments and rounding once at the end is forbidden. One override sits above all of this: if the line's status is `RUNOUT_TRUNCATED`, `consensus_units` is 0 regardless of what the sum would have produced, while `baseline_units` retains its original value and `endorsed_adjustment_count` remains the plain count of the contributing rows — the override suppresses the units alone.

**Review** belongs solely to the `DemandReviewLead`, who supplies a `decision` of `ENDORSE`, `REJECT` or `REQUEST_REVISION` together with an optional `review_note` which, when it is present at all, must be a string but may be the empty one. All three decisions require the adjustment to currently be in `PROPOSED` — anything else, whether already endorsed, rejected, committed, voided or awaiting revision, is rejected with `ERR_ADJUSTMENT_NOT_REVIEWABLE` — require the governing cycle to be in `DEMAND_REVIEW`, otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED_PHASE_ACTOR` under the phase-actor check of §2.8, and require the acting `X-User-ID` to differ from the adjustment's `proposed_by_user_id`, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Each decision records `reviewed_by_user_id` from the acting user and increments the adjustment's `version` by 1, and the three branches differ in what else they do:

- `ENDORSE` moves the row `PROPOSED → ENDORSED`. The target line's `consensus_units` and `endorsed_adjustment_count` are recomputed by the roll-up above, and the line's `version` increments by 1.
- `REJECT` moves the row `PROPOSED → REJECTED`, which is terminal. `consensus_units` is unchanged, because a rejected adjustment never contributed anything, and the forecast line is not written at all, so its `version` does not move.
- `REQUEST_REVISION` moves the row `PROPOSED → REVISION_REQUESTED` and stores the supplied `review_note`. `consensus_units` is likewise unchanged and the forecast line's `version` likewise stands still.

`REQUEST_REVISION` is the only branch that ever writes `review_note`. The `ENDORSE` and `REJECT` branches leave the field exactly as they found it and never record the note the reviewer supplied, so on an endorsed or rejected row `review_note` reads back null — a reviewable row is always in `PROPOSED`, where the field is null either from creation or from the clearing a resubmission performs.

**Resubmission** closes the loop and belongs to the `DemandPlanner`. A resubmission supplies a fresh `rationale`, optionally a new `delta_units` — omitted or null, the adjustment keeps the `delta_units` it already carried — and the `expected_demand_forecast_version` token; it moves the row `REVISION_REQUESTED → PROPOSED`. The adjustment must be in `REVISION_REQUESTED`, otherwise `ERR_ADJUSTMENT_NOT_RESUBMITTABLE`; the governing cycle must be in `DEMAND_REVIEW`; and the row's `revision_round` must be below three, otherwise `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_REACHED`. On success `revision_round` increases by 1, the supplied `delta_units` and `rationale` replace the previous values, `applied_delta_units` is recomputed from the inputs as they now stand — for a `PROMOTION` row, from the cited promotion's current `lift_pct` and `cannibalization_pct`, with no re-test of that promotion's status, since the eligibility rules above are applied on the create alone — and re-checked against the swing guardrail, `reviewed_by_user_id` and `review_note` are both cleared back to null, and both the adjustment's `version` and the forecast line's `version` increment by 1. Three round trips are therefore available — `revision_round` climbing 0 to 1 to 2 to 3 — and a fourth resubmission is refused.

A `PROPOSED` adjustment can also be voided by the server without any review. When a `DemandPlanner` creates a second adjustment of the same `adjustment_type` on the same forecast line while an earlier `PROPOSED` adjustment of that type from the same proposer is still outstanding, the earlier row is superseded: it transitions `PROPOSED → VOIDED`, its `applied_delta_units` is set to 0, its `version` increments, and the line's `consensus_units` is recomputed — a value that does not actually move, since a `PROPOSED` row was contributing nothing. The supersession shares the single line `version` bump the creation already applies, so the line advances by 1 in total and not by 2. This is the write that §2.7's concurrency token exists to protect. Adjustments are readable individually, exposing `applied_delta_units` and `revision_round`, and as a list filtered by `demand_forecast_id`, `promotion_id`, `status` and `adjustment_type`.

### 3.5. Closing the Demand Review and Constraining Supply

Advancing a cycle `DEMAND_REVIEW → SUPPLY_REVIEW` is the act of declaring consensus finished, and either an `SopAdmin` or an `ExecutiveSponsor` may drive it by naming the `target_status`. It requires the cycle to currently be in `DEMAND_REVIEW`, and it requires that no adjustment anywhere in the cycle is still open — that is, no adjustment attached to any of the cycle's forecast lines may be in `PROPOSED` or `REVISION_REQUESTED`, or the advance is rejected with `ERR_OPEN_ADJUSTMENTS`. On success the server sweeps the cycle: every forecast line still in `BASELINE` or `UNDER_CONSENSUS` is carried to `CONSENSUS_LOCKED`, and every `ENDORSED` adjustment sitting on those lines is carried `ENDORSED → COMMITTED` with its `applied_delta_units` untouched, so no line's `consensus_units` moves as a result of the commitment. The cycle's money fields and posture are then recomputed and the cycle's `version` increments by 1.

Once the cycle is in `SUPPLY_REVIEW`, a `SupplyPlanner` builds one **supply plan** per item by naming only `cycle_id` and `item_id`; everything else on the plan is derived. The two body identifiers are resolved `cycle_id` first and `item_id` second, so a create naming an unknown cycle and an unknown item at once fails with `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND`. Two preconditions guard the create. The demand side must be settled: at least one forecast line must exist for that cycle-and-item pair, and every such line must be in either `CONSENSUS_LOCKED` or `RUNOUT_TRUNCATED`. If no line exists at all, or if even one line is still in `BASELINE` or `UNDER_CONSENSUS`, the create is rejected with `ERR_FORECAST_NOT_LOCKED`. And the pair must not already be planned: at most one plan may exist per cycle-and-item pair in a status other than `STALE`, so a second create while a `PLANNED`, `RATIONED` or `COMMITTED` plan exists is rejected with `ERR_SUPPLY_PLAN_EXISTS`. A plan whose predecessor has gone `STALE`, by contrast, may be rebuilt freely — that is what `requires_replan` is signalling.

The plan's derivation runs in four movements.

**The demand side.** `gross_requirement_units` is the sum of `consensus_units` over the cycle's forecast lines for that item whose status is `CONSENSUS_LOCKED`, taken across every customer and every horizon month. Lines in `RUNOUT_TRUNCATED`, `BASELINE` or `UNDER_CONSENSUS` are excluded from the sum entirely. `target_build_units` is the inventory-target contribution and equals the item's `safety_stock_units` as it stands at plan-creation time — this is the snapshot that makes an inventory-policy reset invalidate the plan. `material_cap_units` is likewise a snapshot, of the item's `material_supply_cap_units`.

**The capacity side.** Only capacity blocks belonging to the item's family and **fully contained** within the cycle's horizon window count. Using the half-open horizon window of §2.5, a block qualifies only if its `start_date` falls on or after the window's first day **and** its `end_date` falls on or before the window's end. A block that pokes out of either end of the horizon is excluded in its entirety; there is no proration of partially overlapping blocks. The qualifying blocks' `available_hours` are summed, that sum is multiplied by the family's `units_per_hour`, the product is rounded half up to six decimal places to defuse binary floating-point drift, and the result is then floored to give `available_units`.

**The netting policy**, which is where the cycle's `supply_review_policy` finally bites. The two variants can produce different answers from identical inputs, and they can even report different binding constraints.

- Under `NET_INVENTORY_FIRST`, on-hand inventory is netted off the requirement *before* the ceilings are applied. `demand_side_units` is the **net** requirement: `gross_requirement_units` plus `target_build_units` minus the item's `on_hand_units`, floored at zero so it is never negative. `constrained_units` is then the smallest of three numbers — that `demand_side_units`, `available_units`, and `material_cap_units`. Because netting happens first, on-hand stock can never be wasted against a binding capacity ceiling.
- Under `GROSS_CAPACITY_FIRST`, the ceilings are applied to the **gross** requirement first and on-hand is subtracted afterwards. `demand_side_units` is `gross_requirement_units` plus `target_build_units`, with no netting. The smallest of that figure, `available_units` and `material_cap_units` is taken, and `constrained_units` is that capped figure minus the item's `on_hand_units`, floored at zero. Whenever a ceiling actually binds, this ordering yields a strictly lower `constrained_units` than the other policy would, and because `demand_side_units` is the gross rather than the net requirement it can flip the reported constraint from `INVENTORY_TARGET` to `CAPACITY` on identical inputs.

Whichever policy applies, `shortfall_units` is `demand_side_units` minus `constrained_units`, floored at zero so it is never negative, and `unallocated_units` starts equal to `constrained_units` because nothing has been rationed yet.

**Constraint identification** closes the derivation. Three tests are evaluated in order and the first match determines `constraint_type`. If `material_cap_units` is less than or equal to `available_units` and is also strictly less than `demand_side_units`, the constraint is `MATERIAL` — components are scarcer than machine time and they bind. Failing that, if `available_units` is strictly less than `demand_side_units`, the constraint is `CAPACITY`. Failing that, if `constrained_units` is strictly less than `gross_requirement_units`, the constraint is `INVENTORY_TARGET`, meaning nothing external bound but the inventory-target arithmetic still left the plan short of the raw requirement. Otherwise `constraint_type` is `NONE`. Note the tie: when material and capacity ceilings are exactly equal and both bind, `MATERIAL` is reported, because its test is evaluated first and uses a non-strict comparison against `available_units`.

A new plan is created in `PLANNED` with `requires_replan` false, and the cycle's money fields and posture are recomputed immediately, incrementing the cycle's `version` by 1 — which is how a single constrained plan drags a cycle's `posture` to `AT_RISK`. Plans are readable individually and as a list filtered by `cycle_id`, `item_id`, `status` and `constraint_type`.

A plan's four statuses are `PLANNED`, `RATIONED`, and the two terminal ones, `STALE` and `COMMITTED`. Four distinct events push a plan `PLANNED → STALE` or `RATIONED → STALE`: an inventory-policy reset on the plan's item, any lifecycle transition of the plan's item, the manual unlocking of one of the item's forecast lines from `CONSENSUS_LOCKED` back to `UNDER_CONSENSUS`, and the reopening of the plan's cycle from executive review. In every case `requires_replan` is set to true and the plan's `version` increments by 1. Because a `STALE` plan no longer contributes to `plan_value_cents`, the owning cycle's money fields and posture are recomputed in the same request and the cycle's `version` increments by 1. When the plan that goes stale was already `RATIONED`, its allocation rows are compensated as described in §3.6.

### 3.6. Rationing Scarce Supply Across Customers

When constrained supply falls short of what customers asked for, somebody has to decide who goes short. A `SupplyPlanner` rations a plan while the governing cycle is in `SUPPLY_REVIEW`, moving it `PLANNED → RATIONED`. The plan must currently be in `PLANNED` — one already `RATIONED`, `STALE` or `COMMITTED` is rejected with `ERR_SUPPLY_PLAN_NOT_RATIONABLE` — and an attempt while the cycle sits in any other phase is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED_PHASE_ACTOR` under the phase-actor check of §2.8. Rationing takes no request body at all, and what it returns is the updated supply plan object rather than the allocation rows it just created.

Rationing creates exactly one **allocation** row per customer that owns at least one `CONSENSUS_LOCKED` forecast line for the plan's cycle-and-item pair, so the resulting set of rows is a complete picture of who was in the running. The rows are written in ascending `created_at` order of the customers they belong to, with the customers' own creation sequence breaking ties — not in the order of the waterfall passes below — so that is the order the allocation list read returns them in. Each row carries `supply_plan_id`, the receiving `customer_id`, and a `requested_units` figure that is the sum of `consensus_units` over that customer's `CONSENSUS_LOCKED` lines for the plan's cycle and item, aggregated across all horizon months.

The waterfall then distributes the plan's `constrained_units` in a fixed sequence, with a running remainder that starts at `constrained_units`.

**Customers on credit hold never enter any pass.** Every candidate whose `credit_hold` is true receives `allocated_units` of 0, an `allocation_status` of `WITHHELD_CREDIT_HOLD`, and an `allocation_pass` of `NONE`. Their requests do not consume the remainder and do not enter any denominator.

**Customers who asked for nothing get a row but no units.** A candidate not on credit hold whose `requested_units` is 0 receives `allocated_units` of 0, an `allocation_status` of `ALLOCATED` and an `allocation_pass` of `NONE`, and is excluded from the pro-rata denominator described below.

**Pass one protects the strategic accounts.** Every remaining candidate whose `priority_rank` is at most 2 is filled in full, in order of ascending `priority_rank` and, among customers of equal rank, ascending `created_at`. Each such customer receives the smaller of its own `requested_units` and whatever remains, with `allocation_status` `ALLOCATED` and `allocation_pass` `PROTECTED`, and the remainder is reduced by what was granted. Rank 1 and rank 2 are protected; rank 3 is not.

**Pass two splits the leftover pro rata.** The pool for this pass is every remaining candidate not on credit hold, with a positive `requested_units`, and a `priority_rank` greater than 2. Let the pool's total request be the sum of their `requested_units`. If that total is zero, or if nothing at all remains after pass one, then no units are distributed and the remainder is kept intact, but every pool member still receives a row with `allocated_units` of 0, an `allocation_status` of `ALLOCATED` and an `allocation_pass` of `NONE`, so the plan's customer set stays complete.

Otherwise the distributable amount is the smaller of the remainder and the pool's total request. Capping at the pool's total request is deliberate and pinned: it guarantees no customer is ever granted more than it asked for, and any surplus above the pool's total request stays in `unallocated_units` rather than inflating fill rates. Each pool member's exact share is the distributable amount multiplied by that member's `requested_units` and divided by the pool's total request, kept as an exact fraction; its `allocated_units` is that share floored to an integer, with `allocation_status` `ALLOCATED` and `allocation_pass` `PRO_RATA`. Flooring loses units, so the difference between the distributable amount and the sum of the floored grants is redistributed one unit at a time by the largest-remainder method: the pool is ordered by the fractional part of the exact share descending, then by `priority_rank` ascending, then by `created_at` ascending, and the leading members receive one extra unit each until the difference is exhausted. A row's `id` is never consulted as a tie-break at any point. The remainder is then reduced by the distributable amount.

Whatever remains at the end becomes the plan's `unallocated_units`, and the plan's `version` increments by 1. Each row's `fill_rate_pct` is `allocated_units` divided by `requested_units`, expressed as a percentage and rounded half up to two decimal places — except that a row whose `requested_units` is 0 carries a `fill_rate_pct` of exactly 100.00 rather than an undefined ratio. Allocations are readable individually, exposing the fill rate and which pass produced the row, and as a list filtered by `supply_plan_id`, `customer_id` and `allocation_status`, subject to the customer-scoping rule of §2.1. `allocation_status` takes the values `ALLOCATED`, `WITHHELD_CREDIT_HOLD` and `VOIDED`; `allocation_pass` takes `PROTECTED`, `PRO_RATA` and `NONE`.

Allocations are compensated, never deleted. When a `RATIONED` plan is invalidated and goes `STALE`, every one of its rows whose `allocation_status` is `ALLOCATED` transitions to `VOIDED` with `allocated_units` reset to 0, `fill_rate_pct` reset to 0.00 and `allocation_pass` reset to `NONE`, while the plan's `unallocated_units` is restored to the full `constrained_units`. Rows already in `WITHHELD_CREDIT_HOLD` are left as they are. The conservation identity of §2.10 therefore survives the compensation exactly.

A plan's other terminal exit is commitment. When the cycle publishes, a plan in `PLANNED` or `RATIONED` transitions to `COMMITTED` and its `version` increments by 1; for a plan that had been rationed, its allocation rows are frozen at that moment as the committed customer allocations.

### 3.7. Scenario Planning, Gap Closure and the Executive Review

**Cycle money is a roll-up, recomputed rather than accumulated.** Five events trigger it — every cycle phase transition, the creation of a supply plan, a plan going `STALE` from either `PLANNED` or `RATIONED`, a scenario being selected, and a scenario being superseded — and it always recomputes all five figures from scratch. `plan_value_cents` is the sum, over the cycle's supply plans whose status is `PLANNED`, `RATIONED` or `COMMITTED`, of each plan's `constrained_units` multiplied by that plan's item's `unit_price_cents`; plans in `STALE` are excluded. `gross_gap_cents` is the cycle's `financial_commitment_cents` minus `plan_value_cents`, and it is positive when the plan falls short of what was committed. `selected_closure_cents` is the sum of `net_gap_closure_cents` over the cycle's scenarios whose status is `SELECTED` or `EXECUTED`; scenarios in `DRAFT`, `EVALUATED` or `SUPERSEDED` contribute nothing. `residual_gap_cents` is `gross_gap_cents` minus `selected_closure_cents`. And `constrained_plan_count` is the number of the cycle's supply plans in `PLANNED` or `RATIONED` whose `constraint_type` is anything other than `NONE`. All five are whole cents or whole counts and no rounding occurs, because every term entering them is already an integer. Immediately after the roll-up, the posture rule of §2.8 runs.

Advancing the cycle `SUPPLY_REVIEW → SCENARIO_REVIEW` requires the cycle to be in `SUPPLY_REVIEW` and requires that none of its supply plans is in `STALE` — an unreplanned plan blocks the advance with `ERR_STALE_SUPPLY_PLAN`. The advance recomputes the cycle's money and posture and increments the cycle's `version` by 1. Any `target_status` that is not reachable from the cycle's present status is rejected with `ERR_CYCLE_TRANSITION_INVALID`.

**Scenarios model the levers available to close a gap**, and a `SupplyPlanner` or `ExecutiveSponsor` drafts them while the cycle is in `SCENARIO_REVIEW`. A scenario names its `cycle_id`, a non-empty display `name`, and a `scenario_type` of either `CAPACITY_INVESTMENT` or `PRICE_REALIZATION`. A capacity-investment scenario additionally requires `target_item_id`, the item whose shortfall it addresses, and `added_capacity_hours`, the incremental run hours being purchased, which must be greater than zero; it must not carry `price_increase_pct`. A price-realization scenario requires `price_increase_pct`, the list-price realisation percentage, greater than 0 and at most 100; it must carry neither `target_item_id` nor `added_capacity_hours`. The body identifiers are resolved `cycle_id` first and, on a capacity-investment scenario, `target_item_id` second, so a draft naming an unknown cycle and an unknown target item at once fails with `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND`. A scenario is created in `DRAFT` with all seven of its derived figures at 0 — `incremental_units`, `gross_uplift_cents`, `investment_cost_cents`, `pre_trade_closure_cents`, `trade_rate_pct`, `trade_spend_cents` and `net_gap_closure_cents` — with `created_by_user_id` captured from the acting `X-User-ID`, and with `selected_by_user_id` null. Scenarios are readable individually and as a list filtered by `cycle_id`, `target_item_id`, `status` and `scenario_type`. Their five statuses are `DRAFT`, `EVALUATED`, `SELECTED`, and the two terminal ones, `SUPERSEDED` and `EXECUTED`.

**Evaluation** is performed by a `SupplyPlanner` or `ExecutiveSponsor` and moves a scenario `DRAFT → EVALUATED`, incrementing its `version` by 1. The scenario must be in `DRAFT`, otherwise `ERR_SCENARIO_NOT_EVALUATABLE`; the governing cycle must be in `SCENARIO_REVIEW`, which surfaces as `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED_PHASE_ACTOR` under the phase-actor check of §2.8 whatever the acting role; and for a capacity-investment scenario a supply plan in `PLANNED` or `RATIONED` must exist for the scenario's cycle and `target_item_id`, otherwise `ERR_SUPPLY_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`. That plan-existence test is the last of the three, so a scenario that is no longer in `DRAFT` reports `ERR_SCENARIO_NOT_EVALUATABLE` even when its target plan has gone stale as well. Evaluating a scenario deliberately does **not** touch the cycle's money fields — nothing moves until an executive selects it.

The gap-closure arithmetic looks circular at first glance, because the trade-spend rate is a tier over the closure while the closure is net of trade spend. It is resolved by pinning seven steps in a fixed order and forbidding any iteration or fixed-point search. All seven outputs are written in that order.

1. **`incremental_units`.** For a capacity-investment scenario, the purchased hours are converted to units the same way plan capacity is: `added_capacity_hours` multiplied by the target item's family's `units_per_hour`, the product rounded half up to six decimal places, then floored. `incremental_units` is the smaller of that figure and the matching supply plan's `shortfall_units` — buying capacity beyond the shortfall unlocks nothing sellable. For a price-realization scenario, `incremental_units` is 0.
2. **`gross_uplift_cents`.** For a capacity-investment scenario it is `incremental_units` multiplied by the target item's `unit_price_cents`. For a price-realization scenario it is the cycle's `plan_value_cents`, as it stands at evaluation time, multiplied by `price_increase_pct` divided by one hundred, rounded half up to a whole cent.
3. **`investment_cost_cents`.** For a capacity-investment scenario it is `added_capacity_hours` multiplied by the standing charge of 25,000 cents — 250 dollars — for every added run hour, rounded half up to a whole cent. For a price-realization scenario it is 0, since raising a price costs no hours.
4. **`pre_trade_closure_cents`.** `gross_uplift_cents` minus `investment_cost_cents`. For a price-realization scenario this necessarily equals `gross_uplift_cents`, because there is no investment cost to deduct.
5. **`trade_rate_pct`.** The trade-spend tier is selected from `pre_trade_closure_cents` and from nothing else — selecting it from `gross_uplift_cents` or from the final net closure is forbidden. Three bands are tested in order, first match winning: a pre-trade closure of at least 5,000,000 cents (50,000 dollars) attracts a rate of 8.0; failing that, at least 1,000,000 cents (10,000 dollars) attracts 6.0; anything below that attracts 4.0. Bigger plays buy more trade support, and the boundaries are inclusive at the bottom of each band.
6. **`trade_spend_cents`.** The selected rate is applied to **gross** uplift, not to the pre-trade closure: `gross_uplift_cents` multiplied by `trade_rate_pct` divided by one hundred, rounded half up to a whole cent.
7. **`net_gap_closure_cents`.** `pre_trade_closure_cents` minus `trade_spend_cents`. This is the figure that reaches the cycle, and only while the scenario is `SELECTED` or `EXECUTED`.

Advancing the cycle `SCENARIO_REVIEW → EXECUTIVE_REVIEW` requires the cycle to be in `SCENARIO_REVIEW` and requires that none of its scenarios is still in `DRAFT`; an unevaluated scenario blocks the advance with `ERR_UNEVALUATED_SCENARIO`. Money and posture are recomputed and the cycle's `version` increments by 1.

**Selection** is the `ExecutiveSponsor`'s exclusive act and moves a scenario `EVALUATED → SELECTED`. The scenario must be in `EVALUATED` — still `DRAFT`, or already `SELECTED`, `SUPERSEDED` or `EXECUTED`, all yield `ERR_SCENARIO_NOT_SELECTABLE`; the cycle must be in `EXECUTIVE_REVIEW`, otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED_PHASE_ACTOR` under the phase-actor check of §2.8; the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the scenario's `created_by_user_id`, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`; a capacity-investment scenario's target supply plan must not have gone `STALE` since the scenario was evaluated, otherwise `ERR_STALE_SUPPLY_PLAN`; and the supplied `expected_cycle_version` must equal the cycle's current `version`, otherwise `ERR_STALE_CYCLE_VERSION`. On success `selected_by_user_id` records the acting user, any **other** scenario of the same cycle with the same `scenario_type` and a status of `SELECTED` transitions to `SUPERSEDED`, the cycle's `selected_closure_cents` and `residual_gap_cents` are recomputed followed by the posture rule, the cycle's `version` increments by 1, and the scenario's own `version` increments by 1. One capacity-investment plan and one price-realization play can therefore both be live at once; a second of either kind displaces its own kind only.

The two exits from `SELECTED` are deliberately asymmetric in what happens to the money. `SELECTED → SUPERSEDED` — caused either by another scenario of the same cycle and `scenario_type` being selected, or by the cycle being reopened — **removes** the scenario's `net_gap_closure_cents` from the cycle's `selected_closure_cents`, forcing `residual_gap_cents` to be recomputed, and increments the scenario's `version` by 1. The scenario's own `net_gap_closure_cents` field keeps the value it was evaluated at, and so does the `selected_by_user_id` the selection recorded; the closure simply stops counting. `SELECTED → EXECUTED` — caused only by the cycle being published — **retains** the scenario's `net_gap_closure_cents` inside the cycle's `selected_closure_cents`, and increments the scenario's `version` by 1. Both branches preserve the money conservation identity of §2.10.

**Publication** closes the cycle and is driven by an `ExecutiveSponsor` or an `SopAdmin`, the two roles authorised for every cycle phase transition. It requires the cycle to be in `EXECUTIVE_REVIEW` and requires the gap to be genuinely closed: `residual_gap_cents` must be zero or negative, and publishing a cycle whose residual gap is still greater than zero is rejected with `ERR_UNCLOSED_GAP`. Overachievement is fine; a shortfall is not. On success, every supply plan of the cycle in `PLANNED` or `RATIONED` transitions to `COMMITTED`, every scenario of the cycle in `SELECTED` transitions to `EXECUTED`, `published_at` is stamped with the current logical-clock instant, the cycle's money and posture are recomputed — which lands `posture` on `FROZEN` because the cycle's `status` is now `PUBLISHED` — and the cycle's `version` increments by 1. `PUBLISHED` is terminal, and the resulting `FROZEN` posture blocks every subsequent cycle-scoped child mutation with `ERR_CYCLE_FROZEN`.

**Reopening** is the one backward edge in the phase machine, and it is bounded. An `ExecutiveSponsor` or an `SopAdmin` may drive a cycle `EXECUTIVE_REVIEW → DEMAND_REVIEW` by naming that `target_status` together with a non-empty `reopen_reason`, which is mandatory for this transition and is read for validation only — no field on the cycle retains it. The cycle must be in `EXECUTIVE_REVIEW`, and its `reopen_count` must be below two; a third attempt is rejected with `ERR_REOPEN_LIMIT_REACHED`. Reopening is the widest cascade in the system. `reopen_count` increases by 1. Every forecast line of the cycle in `CONSENSUS_LOCKED` returns to `UNDER_CONSENSUS`, and unlike the manual rework edge of §3.3 this sweep leaves the adjustments sitting on those lines exactly as they stand, so an adjustment already `COMMITTED` stays `COMMITTED` and goes on contributing to `consensus_units`. Every scenario of the cycle in `SELECTED` transitions to `SUPERSEDED` and its `net_gap_closure_cents` leaves `selected_closure_cents`. Every supply plan of the cycle in `PLANNED` or `RATIONED` transitions to `STALE` with `requires_replan` set to true; and because those plans became stale, each of their allocation rows in `ALLOCATED` transitions to `VOIDED` with `allocated_units` set to 0 and `fill_rate_pct` set to 0.00, while each plan's `unallocated_units` is restored to its `constrained_units`. The cycle's money fields are then recomputed — with the newly stale plans no longer contributing to `plan_value_cents` — the posture rule runs, and the cycle's `version` increments by 1. Every record touched along that cascade has its own `updated_at` refreshed and its own `version` incremented.

### 3.8. Item Lifecycle, Runout and the Discontinuation Cascade

New products arrive and old products leave, and both events have to reach the plans that reference them. An `SopAdmin` or `DemandPlanner` drives an item's lifecycle by naming a `target_state`, optionally accompanied by `successor_item_id` and `runout_end_month`. Only the `MATURE → RUNOUT` branch ever consults `successor_item_id`: supplied on any other transition it must still resolve to an existing item — and does so before transition legality is judged, so an unknown one yields `ERR_ITEM_NOT_FOUND` even when the transition itself is illegal — but it is then ignored rather than stored, leaving the item's `successor_item_id` null. Any `target_state` that is not reachable from the item's current `lifecycle_state` — including any attempt to move out of the terminal `DISCONTINUED` or `CANCELLED_NPI` states — is rejected with `ERR_ITEM_LIFECYCLE_TRANSITION_INVALID`. Every successful transition increments the item's `version` by 1, and every successful transition, whichever one it is, also marks each of the item's supply plans that is in `PLANNED` or `RATIONED` as `STALE` — a plan already `COMMITTED`, like one already `STALE`, is left exactly as it stands — which in turn recomputes the owning cycle's money fields and posture and increments that cycle's `version` by 1.

A `PRELAUNCH` item forks two ways, and the two branches could hardly be less alike.

`PRELAUNCH → RAMP` is the launch. Its only precondition is that the item is currently in `PRELAUNCH`, and beyond the item's own `version` bump it touches nothing else at all — no forecast, no adjustment, no promotion is disturbed. What it changes is eligibility: a `RAMP` item is promotable, which is what a promotion approval requires.

`PRELAUNCH → CANCELLED_NPI` is the cancellation of a new product that never launched, and it clears the item out of the plan in three sweeps. Every consensus adjustment of type `NEW_ITEM_PLAN` sitting on any of the item's forecast lines in `PROPOSED`, `ENDORSED` or `COMMITTED` status transitions to `VOIDED` with its `applied_delta_units` set to 0, exactly as in the runout cascade below, and the line it sat on has its consensus roll-up re-run. Every forecast line of the item in `BASELINE`, `UNDER_CONSENSUS` or `CONSENSUS_LOCKED` transitions to `RUNOUT_TRUNCATED` with `consensus_units` set to 0 — **every** month of the horizon, not merely months past some boundary, because a cancelled new product will never ship at all. And every promotion of the item in `DRAFT` or `APPROVED` transitions to `CANCELLED_RUNOUT`. Each row rewritten by those sweeps has its own `version` incremented.

`RAMP → MATURE` is the graduation to steady state. Its only precondition is that the item is in `RAMP`, and like the launch it touches nothing beyond the item's own `version`.

`MATURE → RUNOUT` is the beginning of the end and the deepest cascade in the system. It requires the item to be in `MATURE` and requires a `runout_end_month` in `YYYY-MM` form — the last month the item may be planned for — which is stored on the item; a missing or malformed value is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A `successor_item_id` may optionally be supplied, recording the item that takes over the shelf space, and when it is present the nominated successor must exist, must be a **different** item from the one running out, must sit in the **same** product family, and must itself be in `PRELAUNCH` or `RAMP`. Nominating the item itself, an item from another family, or an item that is already `MATURE`, `RUNOUT`, `DISCONTINUED` or `CANCELLED_NPI` is rejected with `ERR_SUCCESSOR_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. Because the successor pointer is a link from one item to another in the same collection, the item list read accepts a `successor_item_id` filter, which is how a planner finds every predecessor running out into a given item; the list also accepts `family_id` and `lifecycle_state` filters.

The runout cascade then runs three hops deep in the same request. **Hop one:** every promotion of the running-out item in `DRAFT` or `APPROVED` whose `start_month` is later than the item's `runout_end_month` transitions to `CANCELLED_RUNOUT` — a promotion that would begin after the item stops shipping cannot run. **Hop two**, caused by each promotion's cancellation rather than by the item transition itself: every consensus adjustment of type `PROMOTION` whose `promotion_id` is that cancelled promotion and whose status is `PROPOSED`, `ENDORSED` or `COMMITTED` transitions to `VOIDED` with its `applied_delta_units` set to 0, and the adjustment's forecast line has its `consensus_units` and `endorsed_adjustment_count` recomputed by the consensus roll-up of §3.4, incrementing that line's `version`. **Hop three, alongside:** every forecast line of the item whose `month` is later than `runout_end_month` **and** whose status is `UNDER_CONSENSUS` or `CONSENSUS_LOCKED` transitions to `RUNOUT_TRUNCATED` with `consensus_units` set to 0 and with that line's `version` incremented, while a line beyond the boundary that is still in `BASELINE` — or already `RUNOUT_TRUNCATED` — is left exactly as it stands, in contrast with the new-item cancellation above, which sweeps `BASELINE` lines too; `baseline_units` is left untouched, so the statistical history remains auditable. Month comparison here is the plain lexicographic comparison of `YYYY-MM` strings described in §2.5, and the boundary is strict: a line for exactly the `runout_end_month` survives, a line for the month after it is truncated.

`RUNOUT → DISCONTINUED` is the final step, and it is gated on inventory. The item must be in `RUNOUT` and its `on_hand_units` must be exactly 0; an item with even one unit of finished goods remaining is rejected with `ERR_RUNOUT_INVENTORY_REMAINS`. Zero passes, one fails. There is no tolerance band.

### 3.9. Forecast Accuracy, Bias Detection and Planner Accountability

Accountability is the reason the whole cycle is worth recording. Once a cycle is `PUBLISHED`, an `SopAdmin` or `DemandPlanner` posts the realised shipments against it, one **accuracy record** per combination of `cycle_id`, `item_id` and `customer_id`, carrying the `actual_units` that actually shipped, a figure of at least zero. The three body identifiers are resolved in exactly that order — `cycle_id`, then `item_id`, then `customer_id` — so a post naming more than one unknown row reports the earliest of the three. At most one record may exist for that triple, and a second attempt is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACCURACY_RECORD`. Records may only be posted while the governing cycle is `PUBLISHED`, and the months implied by the scored triple — those carried by that triple's `CONSENSUS_LOCKED` forecast lines, the only lines this test examines — must fall inside the cycle's horizon, at or after `horizon_start_month` and at or before `horizon_end_month`, or the post is rejected with `ERR_MONTH_OUTSIDE_HORIZON`. Records are immutable once written — see §2.4 — and readable individually or as a list filtered by `cycle_id`, `item_id` and `customer_id`, subject to the customer-scoping rule of §2.1.

Posting a record derives five values. `forecast_units_snapshot` is the sum of `consensus_units` over the `CONSENSUS_LOCKED` forecast lines matching that exact cycle, item and customer triple, summed across every horizon month — this is the number the plan actually promised, frozen at the moment the actual is posted. `signed_error_units` is `forecast_units_snapshot` minus `actual_units`, so a positive value means the plan over-forecast and a negative value means it under-forecast. `abs_error_units` is the magnitude of that signed error, always at least zero. `ape_pct` is `abs_error_units` divided by `actual_units`, expressed as a percentage and rounded half up to two decimal places — except that when `actual_units` is 0 the ratio is undefined and `ape_pct` is null. And `accountable_planner_user_id` is copied from the `accountable_planner_user_id` of the scored item's product family, pinning ownership of the miss to a named planner at the moment it is recorded.

Posting a record then rolls the item's product family forward — and only that one family, whose row is rewritten in the same request so that its `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increments by 1. The roll-up is computed over a **record set**, and the two scopes matter because they usually disagree: the family's own `rolling_wmape_pct`, `rolling_bias_pct`, `bias_flag`, `accuracy_grade` and `scored_record_count` roll up **every** accuracy record whose item belongs to that family, across **all** cycles, whereas the cycle accuracy summary read described below rolls up only the records whose `cycle_id` is the cycle in its path, optionally narrowed further to a single family. The two scopes coincide only when a family has been scored in exactly one cycle.

If the record set is empty, everything is null or neutral: the record count is 0, all three percentages are null, `bias_flag` is `NONE`, `accuracy_grade` is `UNGRADED`, the two supporting unit totals are 0 rather than null, and the worst-offending item is null. Otherwise two deliberately different accuracy metrics are produced from the same records, and they will not agree.

- **The unweighted metric, MAPE.** Only records whose `ape_pct` is non-null participate; records with `actual_units` of 0 are excluded outright. The already-rounded per-record `ape_pct` values are summed, divided by the number of participating records, and the mean is then rounded half up to two decimal places. It is a round-then-average metric, which means every forecast line counts equally and small-volume lines dominate the result. If no record has a non-null `ape_pct`, this metric is null.
- **The volume-weighted metric, WMAPE.** The **full** record set participates, including the zero-actual records. The `abs_error_units` of every record are summed, that sum is divided by the sum of `actual_units` over every record, the quotient is expressed as a percentage, and only then is it rounded half up to two decimal places. It is an aggregate-then-round metric that weights each line by the volume it actually shipped, so large-volume lines dominate. If the total of `actual_units` across the set is 0, this metric is null. On identical records the two metrics produce materially different percentages, and it is the volume-weighted figure — not the unweighted one — that lands in a family's `rolling_wmape_pct`.

**Bias** is computed the same way as the weighted metric but keeps its sign: the sum of `signed_error_units` over every record in the set, divided by the sum of `actual_units` over every record, expressed as a percentage and rounded half up to two decimal places, and null when the total of `actual_units` is 0. The verdict is read off that already-rounded percentage against a five-point threshold: a bias of 5.00 or greater means the planner is systematically over-forecasting and the flag is `OVER_FORECAST`; a bias of -5.00 or lower means systematic under-forecasting and the flag is `UNDER_FORECAST`; anything strictly between those two bounds, and any null bias, gives a flag of `NONE`. Exactly 5.00 flags; 4.99 does not.

**The accountability grade** is a tier over the volume-weighted metric and over nothing else. Four bands are tested in order, first match winning: a null weighted metric gives `UNGRADED`; a weighted metric of 10.00 or below gives `A`; of 20.00 or below gives `B`; of 35.00 or below gives `C`; and anything above 35.00 gives `D`. Each boundary belongs to the better grade, so exactly 20.00 is a `B`, not a `C`. A family's `scored_record_count` is the number of records in the family's full set that the rolling metrics were computed over.

**The worst offender** is identified by computing, for each distinct item in the record set, that item's own volume-weighted error — the sum of its records' `abs_error_units` divided by the sum of its records' `actual_units` — and taking the item with the highest such ratio. Items whose records sum to zero actual units are ranked last, behind every item with a computable ratio, and among themselves by that same earliest-record tie-break. When two items tie on the ratio, the tie is broken in favour of the item with the earliest `created_at` among the tied items' accuracy records; a row's `id` is never consulted as a tie-break.

The cycle accuracy summary is a computed read available to `SopAdmin`, `DemandPlanner`, `DemandReviewLead` and `ExecutiveSponsor`, optionally narrowed to one product family with a `family_id` query parameter, which is matched literally against each scored item's `family_id` and is never resolved as a foreign key, so one naming no existing family narrows the scope to nothing rather than answering `ERR_FAMILY_NOT_FOUND`. It reports both variants side by side plus the supporting totals: `record_count`, the number of records in scope; `mape_pct`, the unweighted metric; `wmape_pct`, the volume-weighted metric; `bias_pct`, the signed bias; `bias_flag`; `accuracy_grade`; `total_actual_units`, the sum of `actual_units` over the scoped records; `total_abs_error_units`, the sum of `abs_error_units` over the same records; and `worst_item_id`, the identifier of the worst-offending item or null when the scope is empty. Reading it changes nothing.
