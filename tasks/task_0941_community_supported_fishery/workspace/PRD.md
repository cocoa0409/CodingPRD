# Community-Supported Fishery API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A Community-Supported Fishery sells a season of fish before the fish exists. Members buy a share in the spring, the boats go out through the summer, and every week the CSF partitions whatever actually came over the rail across the shares it has already sold. That inversion — revenue first, supply second, with a variable and legally capped supply — is what this API has to model. Nothing about a landing is predictable: the pounds vary trip to trip, the species that comes up is not necessarily the species a member's plan entitles them to, a regulator can close a management area overnight, and a temperature logger can condemn an entire lot after the boxes have already left the dock. The system's job is to keep the pound arithmetic honest, the member's money honest, and the vessel's settlement honest, all at the same time and all under an audit trail.

This document specifies the back-office service that runs a CSF end to end: season and catalogue setup, member subscriptions and their two payment bases, vessel entitlements and landings, the weekly catch allocation, substitution when a box comes up short, the dock-to-door cold chain across two structurally different fulfillment pipelines (hub pickup and home delivery), quality disposition, skips and share transfers, vendor settlement with a quality holdback, product recalls, and the season-end true-up. It is written for the engineers who will build the service and for the operations staff who will live inside it. Where the domain has a hard rule — a regulatory quota, a mass-conservation identity, a segregation-of-duties gate — the rule is stated as a rule, with the exact threshold and the exact rejection.

Five kinds of user share the system, and they deliberately do not trust each other. A CSF administrator runs the business but cannot resolve a quality hold. A quality officer can condemn a lot but cannot be the same person who logged the temperature excursion that caused the hold. A vessel operator reports their own landing but cannot verify it. A hub operator moves fish through custody but touches no money decisions. A member acts only on their own subscription. The API is mounted under `/csf`, and every route below is relative to that mount.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Actors, Identity and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the service must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the caller, and `X-User-Role`, naming the caller's role. Both must be present and non-empty, and `X-User-Role` must name one of exactly five roles: `CsfAdmin`, `Member`, `Vendor`, `HubOperator`, or `QualityOfficer`. A request that omits either header, sends either as an empty string, or names a role outside that list of five is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). A request whose role is recognised but is not listed among the authorised roles for the endpoint being called is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

Two of the five roles are entity-backed, meaning `X-User-ID` is a real primary key and ownership is checked against it. When `X-User-Role` is `Member`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting member; ownership checks compare the `member_id` recorded on the subscription behind the addressed resource to `X-User-ID`, and this applies to subscription actions, skip requests, pickups, substitution responses and share-transfer responses alike. When `X-User-Role` is `Vendor`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting vessel; ownership checks compare the `vessel_id` recorded on the addressed trip to `X-User-ID`. An entity-backed actor addressing a resource it does not own is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403), the same code used for the role-list check, and this ownership rung applies to every endpoint whose authorised-role list includes `Member` or `Vendor`.

The other three roles are opaque. When `X-User-Role` is `CsfAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server does not compare it to any entity's `id`. The same holds when `X-User-Role` is `HubOperator` and when `X-User-Role` is `QualityOfficer`, with one consequential exception: a cold-chain leg record stamps the logging operator's `X-User-ID` into its `recorded_by_user_id` field, and that stored value is later compared against the `X-User-ID` of whoever tries to disposition the lot. That comparison is the system's only use of an opaque identifier as data, and it exists to enforce segregation of duties.

Responsibilities divide as follows. A `CsfAdmin` creates and advances seasons, creates the entire catalogue (share plans, delivery zones, pickup hubs, pickup windows, members, vessels, quota ledgers), creates and activates subscriptions, runs catch allocations and lot splits, verifies landings, creates and finalizes and pays settlement statements, raises and remediates recall notices, proposes substitutions, moves vessel compliance status, and sets the logical clock. A `Member` files and revokes their own skip requests, accepts or rejects a substitution offer, and accepts or declines a share transfer; jointly with a `CsfAdmin` they may pause, resume, cancel and pay their own subscription, update their own preference profile, and create a share transfer out of their own subscription; jointly with a `CsfAdmin` and a `HubOperator` they may record collection of their own staged box. A `Vendor` alone records a landing against a planned trip, and shares trip creation with a `CsfAdmin`. A `HubOperator` alone records sub-lot arrival and packs, dispatches, delivers and stages boxes; they share window opening and closing with a `CsfAdmin`, cold-chain logging with a `QualityOfficer`, and pickup recording with a `CsfAdmin` and `Member`. A `QualityOfficer` alone issues a quality disposition, and shares cold-chain logging with a `HubOperator`. Read access is broad but not universal — the authorised-role column of the endpoint table in §4 is definitive for every route.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and counts from the start of the result set. List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending unless a subsection below says otherwise. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` (the number of rows matching the filters before the window is applied) and `has_more` (true when rows remain beyond the current window).

Filter parameters are additive: supplying more than one narrows the result set, and supplying none returns everything the caller is authorised to see. The filters available on each list route are named in the workflow narratives of §3 and are, without exception, exact-match filters on the named field.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps, Versioning and the Logical Clock

Every resource carries a server-minted `id` — a UUID string with no type prefix — and a `created_at` timestamp. All timestamps in this system, whether stored or returned, are ISO 8601 instants in UTC rendered with exactly three fractional-second digits and a trailing `Z`.

Almost every resource additionally carries an `updated_at` timestamp and an integer `version`. `version` starts at 1 when the resource is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that resource; `updated_at` is refreshed to the current instant on the same mutations. There is exactly one exception in this system: a cold-chain leg record is an append-only observation and is never edited after it is written, so it carries `id` and `created_at` and carries neither `updated_at` nor `version`. Its own `recorded_at` field holds the logical-clock instant at which the leg was logged, and its `recorded_by_user_id` holds the operator identity described in §2.1.

Time in this system is a **logical clock**, not wall-clock time. Before any client sets it, the clock reads exactly its declared default `CLOCK_DEFAULT_UTC`, which is the instant `2026-05-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time. An administrator advances it by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant; the response reports the new setting as `current_utc`. Every deadline in the specification — a pickup window's opening and closing, the grace tail, a skip cutoff, a species open and close date, the instant a landing is recorded — is evaluated against the logical clock, and every system-written timestamp (`landed_at`, `requested_at`, `recorded_at`, `true_up_run_at`) is stamped from it.

Path parameters are validated left to right. When a route addresses a nested resource, the outer identifier is resolved before the inner one, and the first identifier that fails to resolve determines the error returned; a request naming a hub that does not exist never reaches the question of whether the window under it exists.

### 2.5. The Screening Ladder

Every request is screened by an ordered ladder of checks. The order matters and is normative: when a request would fail more than one rung, the response carries the error of the **lowest-numbered** rung it fails, and no later rung is evaluated. Rungs that do not apply to the endpoint being called are skipped without affecting the order of the rest.

1. Both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` are present and non-empty, on every endpoint — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. `X-User-Role` names one of the five declared roles, on every endpoint — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
3. The acting role appears in the called endpoint's authorised-role list, on every endpoint — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
4. On every write endpoint, every field the endpoint marks as required is present and non-null — otherwise `ERR_VALIDATION`. Unknown fields in a request body are ignored rather than rejected.
5. On every write endpoint carrying one of them, each supplied enum-typed field holds a declared member — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_ENUM`. This rung owns exactly and only the following fields, and no other field in the system routes an out-of-list value here: a share plan's `share_size`, `delivery_frequency` and `species_tier`; a season's `ambient_band`; a delivery zone's `fee_tier`; a subscription's `fulfillment_mode` and `payment_basis`; the members of a vessel's `gear_types`; a quota ledger's `species` and the members of its `permitted_gears`; a trip's `gear_type`; a landing lot's `species`; a sub-lot's `destination_type`; a cold-chain leg's `custody_leg`; a recall notice's `reason`; the `disposition` supplied when a quality hold is resolved; and the `target_status` supplied when a vessel's compliance standing is moved.
6. On every write endpoint, numeric and format constraints hold — otherwise `ERR_VALIDATION`. Pounds are strictly positive with at most two decimal places; cents are non-negative integers; a `week_index` is between 1 and the season's `total_weeks` inclusive; minute counts are non-negative integers; datetimes parse as ISO 8601 UTC; a window's `closes_at` is strictly after its `opens_at`; and an entitlement's `species_close_date` is at or after its `species_open_date`.
7. The season named in the path, or in a `season_id` body field, exists — otherwise `ERR_SEASON_NOT_FOUND`. This applies to every endpoint carrying a season identifier.
8. The share plan, delivery zone, pickup hub or pickup window named in the path or in a `share_plan_id`, `delivery_zone_id`, `pickup_hub_id` or `pickup_window_id` body field exists — otherwise `ERR_CATALOG_NOT_FOUND`. This applies to every endpoint carrying a catalogue identifier.
9. The member named in the path or in a `member_id` body field exists — otherwise `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND`, on every endpoint carrying a member identifier.
10. The subscription named in the path or in a `subscription_id`, `from_subscription_id`, `to_subscription_id` or `renewed_from_subscription_id` body field exists — otherwise `ERR_SUBSCRIPTION_NOT_FOUND`, on every endpoint carrying a subscription identifier.
11. The vessel named in the path or in a `vessel_id` body field exists — otherwise `ERR_VESSEL_NOT_FOUND`, on every endpoint carrying a vessel identifier.
12. The quota ledger named in a `quota_ledger_id` body field exists — otherwise `ERR_QUOTA_LEDGER_NOT_FOUND`. This rung guards trip creation and the single-ledger read.
13. The trip named in the path or in a `trip_id` body field exists — otherwise `ERR_TRIP_NOT_FOUND`, on every endpoint carrying a trip identifier.
14. The landing lot named in the path or in a `landing_lot_id` or `substitute_landing_lot_id` body field exists — otherwise `ERR_LANDING_LOT_NOT_FOUND`, on every endpoint carrying a lot identifier.
15. The sub-lot named in the path or in a `sub_lot_id` body field exists — otherwise `ERR_SUB_LOT_NOT_FOUND`, on every endpoint carrying a sub-lot identifier.
16. The share delivery named in the path or in a `share_delivery_id` body field exists — otherwise `ERR_SHARE_DELIVERY_NOT_FOUND`, on every endpoint carrying a delivery identifier.
17. The substitution offer named in the path exists — otherwise `ERR_SUBSTITUTION_NOT_FOUND`. This rung guards offer acceptance, offer rejection and the single-offer read.
18. The skip request named in the path exists — otherwise `ERR_SKIP_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, guarding skip revocation and the single-skip read.
19. The settlement statement named in the path exists — otherwise `ERR_SETTLEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, guarding statement finalization, statement payment and the single-statement read.
20. The share transfer named in the path exists — otherwise `ERR_SHARE_TRANSFER_NOT_FOUND`, guarding transfer acceptance, transfer decline and the single-transfer read.
21. The recall notice named in the path exists — otherwise `ERR_RECALL_NOT_FOUND`, guarding recall remediation and the single-recall read.
22. An entity-backed actor owns the resource it is acting on, as described in §2.1 — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`. This applies to every endpoint whose authorised-role list includes `Member` or `Vendor`.
23. Declared cross-references agree — otherwise `ERR_REFERENCE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). The agreements are enumerated in §2.8 and this rung guards trip creation, landing recording, subscription creation, transfer creation, substitution proposal and hub staging.
24. The season behind the addressed resource is not `CLOSED` — otherwise `ERR_SEASON_CLOSED` (HTTP 409). This applies to every write endpoint whose addressed resource resolves to a season, with four carve-outs that exist so the rung cannot make its own successors unreachable: the season true-up call itself, the logical-clock setter, every read endpoint, and the member preference update.
25. The season is in the status the operation requires — otherwise `ERR_SEASON_NOT_OPEN` (HTTP 409). Subscription creation, subscription activation, subscription resumption, trip creation, landing recording, catch allocation and transfer creation all require the season to be `OPEN`; moving the season to landings-closed requires it to be `OPEN`; running the true-up requires it to be `LANDINGS_CLOSED`; and opening the season requires it to be `PLANNING`.
26. The acting or referenced member does not have standing `RESTRICTED` — otherwise `ERR_MEMBER_RESTRICTED` (HTTP 403). This guards subscription creation, subscription activation and subscription resumption.
27. The vessel behind the trip has compliance standing `COMPLIANT` or `PROBATION` — otherwise `ERR_VESSEL_SUSPENDED` (HTTP 403), on trip creation and landing recording.
28. The addressed resource sits in a from-state the requested transition declares, including terminal-state lockdown: a resource in a terminal state accepts no further transition. Failure is `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). This rung also owns the gate that a substitution may only be proposed against a box whose `fill_status` is `UNDER_FILLED`. It applies to every action endpoint that drives a state transition.
29. The subscription behind the operation has `lifecycle_status` equal to `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_SUBSCRIPTION_NOT_ACTIVE` (HTTP 409). This guards skip filing, transfer creation, box pickup and substitution proposal.
30. Money owed is in place — otherwise `ERR_PAYMENT_REQUIRED` (HTTP 402). A `PREPAID_FULL_SEASON` activation requires `paid_cents` to be at least `commitment_cents`, and staging or dispatching a box requires the owning subscription's `lifecycle_status` to be anything other than `PAST_DUE`. This guards subscription activation, hub staging and dispatch.
31. The supplied expected-version field equals the parent's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This guards catch allocation, transfer creation and settlement creation, and is detailed in §2.7.
32. The entitlement's `area_closed` flag is false — otherwise `ERR_AREA_CLOSED` (HTTP 422), on landing recording.
33. The logical clock is at or after the entitlement's `species_open_date` and at or before its `species_close_date` — otherwise `ERR_SPECIES_OUT_OF_SEASON` (HTTP 422), on landing recording.
34. The trip's `gear_type` is a member of the entitlement's `permitted_gears` and also a member of the vessel's `gear_types` — otherwise `ERR_GEAR_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 422), on landing recording.
35. The sustainability band computed for the trip is not `RED` — otherwise `ERR_SUSTAINABILITY_BLOCKED` (HTTP 422), on landing recording.
36. `landed_pounds_gross` is at most the entitlement's `remaining_pounds`, or `allow_partial_fill` is true; and a ledger already in status `EXHAUSTED` or `SUSPENDED` accepts no landing at all. Failure is `ERR_QUOTA_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422), on landing recording.
37. The substitute species is not a member of the member's `allergy_exclusions` — otherwise `ERR_ALLERGY_EXCLUSION` (HTTP 422), on substitution proposal.
38. The member's `never_substitute` flag is false — otherwise `ERR_NEVER_SUBSTITUTE` (HTTP 422), on substitution proposal.
39. The disliked-species test described in §3.5 passes — otherwise `ERR_DISLIKE_ALTERNATIVE_AVAILABLE` (HTTP 422), on substitution proposal.
40. The substitute lot has enough uncommitted product to cover the offer, as described in §3.5 — otherwise `ERR_NO_SUBSTITUTE_AVAILABLE` (HTTP 422), on substitution proposal.
41. The lot behind the box has `quality_status` of `CLEAR`, `RELEASED` or `DISCOUNTED` — otherwise `ERR_LOT_ON_QUALITY_HOLD` (HTTP 409). This guards home delivery completion and hub pickup.
42. The custody evidence the pipeline requires exists — otherwise `ERR_COLD_CHAIN_EVIDENCE_MISSING` (HTTP 422). Delivering a home box requires a `HUB_TO_DOOR` leg logged on the lot; recording a hub pickup requires a `HUB_HOLD` leg logged on the lot.
43. The requested disposition is inside its cold-chain budget — otherwise `ERR_COLD_CHAIN_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422), on quality disposition.
44. The acting `X-User-ID` differs from the `recorded_by_user_id` of every breaching cold-chain leg on the lot — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403), on quality disposition.
45. The pickup window is in a status that admits the operation — otherwise `ERR_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN` (HTTP 409). Staging requires the window to be `SCHEDULED` or `OPEN`; recording a pickup requires it to be `OPEN` or `GRACE`; closing it requires it to be `OPEN` or `GRACE`.
46. The pickup instant is at or before the window's `closes_at` plus its `grace_minutes`; one second later is refused with `ERR_WINDOW_GRACE_EXPIRED` (HTTP 409), on pickup.
47. The window's `slots_free` is at least 1 before the reservation is taken — otherwise `ERR_HUB_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409), on hub staging.
48. The split's destination pounds reconcile exactly with the allocation, as specified in §3.4 — otherwise `ERR_MASS_CONSERVATION_VIOLATION` (HTTP 422), on lot splitting.
49. The subscription's `skip_count` is strictly below the seasonal skip cap before the new skip is counted — otherwise `ERR_SKIP_CAP_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422), on skip filing.
50. A skip revocation arrives at or before the skip's `cutoff_at` — otherwise `ERR_SKIP_WINDOW_CLOSED` (HTTP 409).
51. The transfer is eligible: the receiving subscription is `WAITLISTED`, the transferring subscription is `ACTIVE`, the two belong to different members, and `remaining_delivery_count` is strictly greater than 0. Failure is `ERR_TRANSFER_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (HTTP 422), and this rung guards both transfer creation and transfer acceptance.
52. The lot's quality disposition has resolved before a statement is finalized, meaning its `quality_status` is not `HOLD` — otherwise `ERR_HOLDBACK_NOT_CLEARED` (HTTP 409), on statement finalization.
53. Scoped uniqueness holds — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_RESOURCE` (HTTP 409). A season's `season_code`, a member's `email`, a vessel's `permit_number`, a hub's `hub_code` and a zone's `zone_code` are each globally unique. A plan's `plan_code` is unique within its `season_id`. A quota ledger is unique on the combination of `vessel_id`, `season_id`, `species` and `management_area`. A pickup window is unique on the combination of `pickup_hub_id`, `season_id` and `week_index`. And a member may hold at most one subscription per season whose `lifecycle_status` is `PENDING`, `WAITLISTED`, `ACTIVE`, `PAUSED` or `PAST_DUE`. This rung guards season creation, member creation, vessel creation, hub creation, zone creation, plan creation, ledger creation, window creation and subscription creation.

Note the consequence of rungs 28 and 31 sitting where they do: a request that is both aimed at a resource in the wrong state and carrying a stale version is answered with the state error, not the version error.

### 2.6. Pounds, Cents and Rounding Discipline

Two quantities dominate this system and they round differently.

**Pounds** are decimal numbers carried to exactly two decimal places. Every pound-valued input must be strictly positive and must not carry more than two decimals. Pound arithmetic that partitions a quantity is exact subtraction with no rounding at all: what comes out of a partition sums back to what went in, to the cent-pound. Where a proportional share has to be reduced to two decimals, the specification says explicitly whether the reduction is a truncation downward or a rounding, and the residue left by a truncation is always redistributed rather than discarded. The smallest pound step the system ever hands out is `ALLOCATION_POUND_PRECISION`, which is 0.01 pounds.

**Cents** are integers. Every money field in the system ends in `_cents` and holds a whole number of cents. Where a money figure is derived from a multiplication, the rounding method is **round half up** — a value ending in exactly one half cent rounds away from zero to the next whole cent. The number of times rounding is applied is as significant as the method, and the specification pins it per calculation: some chains multiply several factors together and round once at the very end, while others round each line item separately and then sum the already-rounded lines. Where the two orders would produce different totals, §3 says which one governs, and the difference is not a matter of implementer discretion.

Comparisons against instants are exact to the second, and a boundary instant belongs to whichever side the prose names. Throughout this document, "at or before" and "at or after" include the boundary instant; "strictly after" and "strictly before" exclude it.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Superseding Writes

Three operations in this system legitimately supersede work that already exists, and all three therefore require the client to prove it has seen the current state of the parent resource before it overwrites it. In each case the request body carries a non-optional integer field naming the parent's expected `version`. If the value supplied does not equal the parent's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` at HTTP 412 and nothing is written.

The first is the catch allocation. A request that runs the weekly allocation against a landing lot must carry `expected_landing_lot_version` equal to that lot's current `version`. A second allocation run against the same lot legitimately discards the pound assignments the first run made, so a client whose view of the lot predates an earlier run would silently overwrite assignments it never saw; the version check converts that into a deterministic rejection. A successful allocation run increments the lot's `version` by 1, which means a client that wants to run the allocation twice must re-read the lot in between.

The second is the share transfer. A request that creates a transfer must carry `expected_subscription_version` equal to the current `version` of the transferring subscription. A new transfer offer supersedes the delivery ownership recorded on that subscription, and a client whose view predates a pause, a payment or an earlier transfer would price the transfer off a stale remaining-delivery count. A successful create increments the transferring subscription's `version` by 1.

The third is the vendor settlement. A request that creates a settlement statement for a trip must carry `expected_trip_version` equal to that trip's current `version`. A restated statement voids the statement the trip already carries, so a client holding a stale view of the trip would void a statement it never read and republish superseded numbers. A successful create increments the trip's `version` by 1.

No other endpoint in this system takes an expected-version field, and no other endpoint may reject on version grounds.

### 2.8. Referential Agreements

A number of cross-references in this system must agree, and disagreement is a single failure with a single code: `ERR_REFERENCE_MISMATCH` at HTTP 422. The agreements are these. A trip's `management_area` must equal the `management_area` of the quota ledger it is planned against, and the trip's `vessel_id` must equal that ledger's `vessel_id`. A landing lot's `species` must equal the ledger's `species`. A subscription on the hub-pickup pipeline must supply a `pickup_hub_id` and must not supply a `delivery_zone_id`; a subscription on the home-delivery pipeline must supply a `delivery_zone_id` and must not supply a `pickup_hub_id`, and the zone it names must have `is_serviceable` set to true. A subscription's plan must belong to the same season as the subscription itself. The two subscriptions named on a share transfer must belong to the same season. A substitution's substitute lot must belong to the same season as the delivery it is offered against. And a box being staged into a pickup window requires that window's `week_index` to equal the delivery's `week_index` and that window's `pickup_hub_id` to equal the `pickup_hub_id` recorded on the subscription.

### 2.9. Conservation Invariants

Nine equalities hold at all times, and they are the system's own audit. They are not merely descriptive: each is written by a specific pair of operations, one of which puts a quantity into a bucket and the other of which takes it out again.

`INV_LANDED_POUNDS_PARTITION` — for every landing lot, `landed_pounds_gross` equals `landed_pounds_net` plus `discarded_over_quota_pounds`. The partial-fill path on landing recording is what can make the second term non-zero, and it does so in the same request as the ledger debit.

`INV_ALLOCATION_PARTITION` — for every allocated lot, `landed_pounds_net` equals `allocated_pounds_total` plus `surplus_secondary_pounds` plus `surplus_donated_pounds`. The allocation run and the surplus routing that follows it write both sides.

`INV_SPLIT_MASS` — for every split lot, the sum of `pounds` across the sub-lots created from it equals `split_pounds_total`, which in turn equals `allocated_pounds_total`. The split consumes exactly the allocation it was handed.

`INV_QUOTA_LEDGER` — for every quota ledger, `entitlement_pounds` equals `committed_pounds` plus `remaining_pounds`. A landing debits the ledger and a rejected verification credits exactly the same pounds back, leaving `entitlement_pounds` and `committed_pounds` at their pre-landing values.

`INV_HUB_SLOT_PARTITION` — for every pickup window, `cooler_slots_total` equals `slots_free` plus `slots_reserved` plus `slots_occupied` plus `slots_forfeited`. Staging reserves a slot and either a pickup or the window-close no-show sweep consumes exactly that reservation.

`INV_VENDOR_MONEY` — on every statement that has reached `FINAL`, `gross_cents` equals `deductions_cents` plus `net_payable_cents` plus `holdback_forfeited_cents` plus `offset_cents`. Finalization resolves the holdback that statement creation withheld, and `holdback_released_cents` plus `holdback_forfeited_cents` always equals `holdback_cents`.

`INV_MEMBER_CREDIT` — for every member, `credit_balance_cents` equals the sum of `credit_cents` over every delivery of every subscription that member holds. The below-value substitution branch, the discount disposition and the recall walk each write both sides of this equality inside the same request.

`INV_PROMISED_COUNT_PARTITION` — for every subscription evaluated after the true-up sweep, `promised_delivery_count` equals `delivered_count` plus `credited_count` plus `member_fault_count` plus `skip_credited_count` plus `undelivered_count`. Filing a skip moves one unit out of the undelivered bucket into either the skip-credited or the member-fault bucket, and revoking that skip moves it straight back.

`INV_TRANSFER_NET_TAKE` — on every accepted transfer, the CSF's net cash take equals `transfer_fee_cents` exactly. Concretely: `transferee_charge_cents` minus `transferor_refund_cents`, and minus the assumed liability (`remaining_delivery_count` multiplied by the transferring subscription's `price_per_delivery_cents`) on the installment variant only, equals `transfer_fee_cents`. The equality holds on both settlement variants.

### 2.10. Domain Vocabulary and State Axes

Nine species are landable: `COD`, `HADDOCK`, `POLLOCK`, `HALIBUT`, `SALMON`, `TUNA`, `LOBSTER`, `SCALLOP` and `OYSTER`. Each maps to exactly one of three commercial tiers, and the mapping is fixed for the life of the system: `COD`, `HADDOCK` and `POLLOCK` are `WHITEFISH`; `HALIBUT`, `SALMON` and `TUNA` are `PREMIUM_FINFISH`; `LOBSTER`, `SCALLOP` and `OYSTER` are `SHELLFISH`. This is the `SPECIES_TIER_MAP`, and it decides both which shares a landing can fill and what a pound of it is worth at retail. Retail value is set by `RETAIL_CENTS_PER_LB`: 1150 cents per pound for `WHITEFISH`, 1850 for `PREMIUM_FINFISH` and 2650 for `SHELLFISH`.

Five gear types exist: `HOOK_AND_LINE`, `TRAP`, `DIVE`, `GILLNET` and `TRAWL`. Sustainability bands are `GREEN`, `YELLOW`, `ORANGE` and `RED`. Cold-chain legs are logged against one of three custody legs: `DOCK_TO_HUB`, `HUB_HOLD` and `HUB_TO_DOOR`. A sub-lot's `destination_type` is either `HUB` or `ROUTE`. A recall's `reason` is one of `PATHOGEN`, `MISLABEL`, `COLD_CHAIN` or `REGULATORY`. A season's `ambient_band` is `COOL`, `MILD` or `HOT`, defaulting to `MILD` when a season is created without one. A delivery zone's `fee_tier` is `TIER_1`, `TIER_2` or `TIER_3`. A share plan's `share_size` is `SMALL`, `MEDIUM` or `LARGE`, its `delivery_frequency` is `WEEKLY` or `BIWEEKLY`, and its `species_tier` is one of the three commercial tiers. A subscription's `fulfillment_mode` is `HUB_PICKUP` or `HOME_DELIVERY` and its `payment_basis` is `PREPAID_FULL_SEASON` or `INSTALLMENT`.

Twenty independent status axes drive the system. Several resources carry more than one at once, and axes on the same resource evolve independently unless a rule explicitly couples them.

- A season's `status` runs `PLANNING`, `OPEN`, `LANDINGS_CLOSED`, `TRUE_UP` and `CLOSED`, with `CLOSED` terminal.
- A subscription's `lifecycle_status` runs across `PENDING`, `WAITLISTED`, `ACTIVE`, `PAUSED`, `PAST_DUE`, `TRANSFERRED`, `CANCELLED` and `COMPLETED`; the last three are terminal.
- A subscription's `billing_status` runs across `UNBILLED`, `CURRENT`, `DUNNING`, `PREPAID_SETTLED`, `REFUND_PENDING` and `SETTLED`, with `SETTLED` terminal.
- A subscription's `true_up_status` runs `NOT_DUE`, `SHORTFALL_FLAGGED`, `REFUNDED` and `CREDITED`; the last two are terminal.
- A member's `standing` cycles `GOOD`, `WARNED` and `RESTRICTED`, with no terminal state.
- A vessel's `compliance_status` cycles `COMPLIANT`, `PROBATION` and `SUSPENDED`, with no terminal state.
- A quota ledger's `status` cycles `OPEN`, `WARNING`, `EXHAUSTED` and `SUSPENDED`, with no terminal state.
- A trip's `status` runs `PLANNED`, `LANDED`, then either `VERIFIED` or `REJECTED`; both endings are terminal.
- A landing lot's `status` runs `RECORDED`, `ALLOCATED`, `SPLIT`, `DISPATCHED`, and ends in `CLOSED` or `RECALLED`; both endings are terminal.
- A landing lot's `quality_status` runs `CLEAR`, `HOLD`, then one of `RELEASED`, `DISCOUNTED` or `DISCARDED`; those three are terminal.
- A sub-lot's `status` runs `IN_TRANSIT`, `ARRIVED`, and ends in `CONSUMED`, `RETURNED` or `DESTROYED`; those three are terminal.
- A pickup window's `status` runs `SCHEDULED`, `OPEN`, `GRACE`, `CLOSED` and `RECONCILED`, with `RECONCILED` terminal.
- A share delivery's `fulfillment_status` covers `SCHEDULED`, `SKIPPED`, `ALLOCATED`, `PACKED`, `IN_TRANSIT`, `DELIVERED`, `AT_HUB`, `PICKED_UP`, `NO_SHOW`, `DONATED` and `VOIDED`; `DELIVERED`, `PICKED_UP`, `DONATED` and `VOIDED` are terminal.
- A share delivery's `fill_status` covers `PENDING`, `FULL_FILL`, `UNDER_FILLED`, `SUBSTITUTED` and `VOID`, with `VOID` terminal.
- A share delivery's `billing_status` covers `PENDING`, `CHARGED`, `PARTIALLY_CREDITED`, `CREDITED`, `REFUNDED` and `FORFEITED`; `REFUNDED` and `FORFEITED` are terminal.
- A substitution offer's `status` runs `PROPOSED` and ends in `ACCEPTED`, `REJECTED` or `CREDIT_ISSUED`.
- A skip request's `status` runs `REQUESTED` and ends in `CREDITED`, `FORFEITED` or `REVOKED`; `FORFEITED` and `REVOKED` are terminal, and `CREDITED` is the one non-terminal outcome because it can still be revoked.
- A settlement statement's `status` runs `DRAFT`, `PROVISIONAL`, `HELD`, `FINAL` and ends in `PAID` or `VOID`.
- A share transfer's `status` runs `REQUESTED` and ends in `ACCEPTED`, `DECLINED` or `EXPIRED`.
- A recall notice's `status` runs `INITIATED`, `SCOPED`, `REMEDIATED` and `CLOSED`, with `CLOSED` terminal.

Four further enumerations are computed rather than supplied and record which branch of a forked calculation ran: a lot's `allocation_variant` (`NONE` before allocation, then `NORMAL_FILL` or `SHORT_FILL`), a substitution's `settlement_variant` (`EQUAL_OR_GREATER` or `BELOW_VALUE`), a skip's `settlement_variant` (`CREDIT` or `FORFEIT`), a transfer's `settlement_variant` (`PREPAID` or `INSTALLMENT`), and a settlement statement's `variant` (`PROVISIONAL` or `FINAL`). A cold-chain leg records a `breach_type` of `NONE`, `SINGLE_EXCURSION`, `CUMULATIVE` or `BOTH`, and a packed box records a `pack_size` of `NONE`, `SMALL`, `MEDIUM` or `LARGE`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up a Season and Its Catalogue

Everything in a CSF hangs off a season. An administrator creates one by supplying a `season_code` of between 1 and 32 characters that no other season already uses, a `start_date` and an `end_date` as ISO 8601 UTC instants, and a `total_weeks` count between 1 and 40 that fixes the delivery calendar: week indices run from 1 through `total_weeks`, and `start_date` is the scheduled instant of the week-1 delivery while `end_date` is the instant after which nothing more is scheduled. Two optional inputs shape the season's physics. The `ambient_band` records the temperature band the region sits in for the summer and governs how long a gel pack lasts; when it is omitted the season is created as `MILD`. The `landing_commit_lead_hours` value records how many hours before a week's scheduled delivery instant the CSF commits its landing plan; it must be an integer between 0 and 336, and when it is omitted the season is created with the standard lead `DEFAULT_LANDING_COMMIT_LEAD_HOURS` of 72 hours. Finally, `offered_species` is a non-empty array of species codes naming what may be landed and what may be offered as a substitute during this season.

A newly created season sits in `PLANNING`, its `shortfall_flag_count` at 0 and its `true_up_run_at` null. Opening it — the `PLANNING → OPEN` move, available to an administrator — requires that the season currently be in `PLANNING` and that at least one share plan already exist for it; an open season is what permits subscriptions to be created, landings to be recorded and allocations to be run. The remaining season transitions belong to the end of the season and are described in §3.9.

**Share plans** are the product matrix. A plan is created against a `season_id` with a `plan_code` between 1 and 32 characters that is unique within that season, and three axes: `share_size`, `delivery_frequency` and `species_tier`. Those three axes fully determine the price of one delivery. The base is set by size — `SHARE_SIZE_BASE_CENTS` is 2400 cents for `SMALL`, 3600 for `MEDIUM` and 5200 for `LARGE`. That base is multiplied by the species-tier multiplier, `SPECIES_TIER_MULTIPLIER`, which is 1.00 for `WHITEFISH`, 1.25 for `PREMIUM_FINFISH` and 1.55 for `SHELLFISH`, and then by the frequency factor, `FREQUENCY_FACTOR`, which is 1.00 for `WEEKLY` and 1.10 for `BIWEEKLY`. The three factors are multiplied together first and the result is rounded half up to whole cents exactly once, at the end; `price_per_delivery_cents` is that single rounded figure. A `MEDIUM` × `PREMIUM_FINFISH` × `WEEKLY` plan therefore lists at 4500 cents, and a `LARGE` × `SHELLFISH` × `BIWEEKLY` plan at 8866 cents.

The same three axes fix the physical box. `SHARE_SIZE_WEIGHT_UNITS` gives a plan its `weight_units`: 1 for `SMALL`, 2 for `MEDIUM`, 3 for `LARGE`. Those units are the currency of the weekly allocation. One weight unit is worth `LB_PER_WEIGHT_UNIT`, which is 2.5 pounds, so `target_pounds_per_delivery` — the pound figure at which a box counts as completely full — is the plan's `weight_units` multiplied by 2.5, giving 2.50 pounds for a `SMALL` plan, 5.00 for a `MEDIUM` and 7.50 for a `LARGE`. Independently, `SHARE_MIN_FLOOR_LB` gives each size a `min_floor_pounds` below which shipping a box is not worth the packaging: 1.00 pound for `SMALL`, 2.00 for `MEDIUM`, 3.00 for `LARGE`. Lastly, `scheduled_delivery_count` records how many deliveries a member who joins at week 1 would receive across the whole season: for a `WEEKLY` plan it is simply the season's `total_weeks`, and for a `BIWEEKLY` plan it is `total_weeks` divided by two and rounded up to the next whole number. A twenty-week season yields 20 for weekly plans and 10 for biweekly ones. Plans can be listed, optionally narrowed by `season_id`, alongside the usual `limit` and `offset`.

**Delivery zones** describe the home-delivery road network. A zone carries a globally unique `zone_code` of 1 to 32 characters, a `fee_tier` that sets its base fee, a `transit_hours` figure that must be greater than zero with at most two decimals and represents door-to-door transit time, and an `is_serviceable` flag that defaults to true and, when false, bars the zone from being sold into.

**Pickup hubs** describe the other pipeline. A hub carries a globally unique `hub_code` of 1 to 32 characters, a `cooler_slots_default` of at least 1, and a `grace_minutes` value between 0 and 480 that defaults to `DEFAULT_GRACE_MINUTES`, 90 minutes, when omitted — the tail after a window's stated close during which a member may still collect a box that is waiting for them.

**Pickup windows** are created under a hub, one per delivery week. A window names a `season_id` and a `week_index` between 1 and the season's `total_weeks`, and no two windows may share the same hub, season and week. It carries an `opens_at` and a `closes_at`, the latter strictly after the former. Two values are inherited at creation: `cooler_slots_total`, which is taken from the hub's `cooler_slots_default` when the request omits it and otherwise from the supplied value, which must be at least 1; and `grace_minutes`, which is copied from the hub's `grace_minutes` at the moment the window is created and frozen there, so that later edits to the hub do not retroactively move a window's grace tail. A new window sits in `SCHEDULED` with `slots_reserved`, `slots_occupied` and `slots_forfeited` all at 0 and `slots_free` equal to `cooler_slots_total`; `slots_free` is always `cooler_slots_total` less the reserved, occupied and forfeited counts. Hub staff move the window `SCHEDULED → OPEN` once the logical clock has reached or passed `opens_at`, which is what makes collection possible for the boxes waiting in it. Windows can be listed and narrowed by `pickup_hub_id`, `season_id` and `week_index`.

### 3.2. Members, Subscriptions and the Two Payment Bases

A member is created with a `full_name` of 1 to 120 characters and an `email` that must contain an at-sign and must be unique across all members. Three preference fields shape how the CSF may treat their box, and all three may be supplied at creation or amended later through the preference update, which accepts any subset of them. `allergy_exclusions` is an array of species codes that must never be placed in this member's box under any circumstance — a hard block. `dislikes` is an array of species codes the member would rather avoid — a soft block that yields when nothing else is available. `never_substitute` is a boolean, false by default, that when true means the member refuses substitution outright and takes money instead. The preference update also accepts a `standing_reset` flag; it is an administrator's instrument, and setting it true resets the member's `season_no_show_count` to 0 and returns `standing` to `GOOD` from either `WARNED` or `RESTRICTED`. Subscriptions that were paused because of a restriction are not automatically resumed by the reset. A member also carries a `credit_balance_cents` figure, which starts at 0 and accumulates every credit the CSF issues them.

Creating a subscription is an administrative act. The request names a `member_id`, a `share_plan_id`, a `season_id`, a `fulfillment_mode`, a `payment_basis` and a `join_week_index` between 1 and the season's `total_weeks`. The fulfillment mode decides which of `pickup_hub_id` and `delivery_zone_id` must accompany it, per §2.8. A `renewed_from_subscription_id` may name the member's subscription from a prior season; when it is supplied, that prior subscription's `rollover_credit_cents` is carried across into the new subscription's `paid_cents`, which is how last season's unspent credit becomes this season's opening payment. A `waitlist_requested` flag, when true, puts the new subscription straight into `WAITLISTED` instead of `PENDING`; no boxes are created for it and it becomes eligible to receive a share transfer. Otherwise the subscription is created in `PENDING` with `billing_status` `UNBILLED` and `true_up_status` `NOT_DUE`.

Creation is also where the season commitment is priced, and the two payment bases price it very differently. Under `INSTALLMENT`, the subscription's `price_per_delivery_cents` is the plan's undiscounted list price, and `commitment_cents` is that unit price multiplied by the number of deliveries promised from the join week onward — the commitment is prorated to what the member will actually receive. Under `PREPAID_FULL_SEASON`, every unit is discounted: `PREPAY_DISCOUNT_PCT` is 0.08, so `price_per_delivery_cents` is the list price multiplied by 0.92 and rounded half up to whole cents, that rounding happening once on the unit price and never on the season total. But the prepaid commitment is *not* prorated: `commitment_cents` is the discounted unit price multiplied by the plan's full-season `scheduled_delivery_count`, regardless of when the member joins. A late prepaid joiner therefore pays for weeks they will never receive, and that gap is exactly what the season-end true-up exists to settle. To make the contrast concrete, a `MEDIUM` × `PREMIUM_FINFISH` × `WEEKLY` plan listing at 4500 cents, joined at week 11 of a 20-week season, produces a unit price of 4500 and a commitment of 45000 on the installment basis, and a unit price of 4140 and a commitment of 82800 on the prepaid basis — with ten deliveries promised either way.

The set of delivery weeks a subscription participates in is derived from its `join_week_index` and its plan's `delivery_frequency`. A weekly subscription takes every week from the join week through `total_weeks` inclusive. A biweekly subscription takes every other week starting at the join week — formally, every week between the join week and `total_weeks` inclusive whose distance from the join week is an even number. Note that the parity of a biweekly schedule is anchored on the join week, not on the season, so two biweekly subscriptions joining one week apart never share a delivery week. Joining at week 11 of a 20-week season gives a weekly subscriber ten deliveries and a biweekly subscriber five, in weeks 11, 13, 15, 17 and 19. `promised_delivery_count` is the size of that set.

Activation moves the subscription `PENDING → ACTIVE` and is what turns the schedule into rows. It requires the subscription to be `PENDING`, the season to be `OPEN`, the member's `standing` to be `GOOD` or `WARNED`, and — on the prepaid basis only — `paid_cents` to be at least `commitment_cents`. It creates one share delivery per week in the delivery-week set, each in `fulfillment_status` `SCHEDULED`, `fill_status` `PENDING` and `billing_status` `PENDING`, each stamped with its `week_index`, its `season_id` and a `scheduled_at` instant computed as the season's `start_date` advanced by seven days for each week beyond the first — that is, `start_date` plus seven days multiplied by one less than the week index — preserving the time of day of `start_date`. A season starting `2026-05-04T00:00:00Z` therefore schedules its week-11 box at `2026-07-13T00:00:00.000Z`. Activation records `promised_delivery_count` as the number of rows it created, and sets the money axis: `billing_status` moves `UNBILLED → PREPAID_SETTLED` for a prepaid subscription and `UNBILLED → CURRENT` for an installment one.

Payments are recorded against a subscription by supplying an `amount_cents` figure, which is added to `paid_cents`. Payments are accepted while the subscription is `PENDING`, while it is `ACTIVE` and while it is `PAST_DUE`, and only the last of those moves the lifecycle axis. In `PENDING` the subscription stays `PENDING` — this is how a prepaid member reaches the paid-in-full condition that activation demands. In `ACTIVE` it stays `ACTIVE`, building headroom against future charges. In `PAST_DUE`, the subscription returns `PAST_DUE → ACTIVE` provided that `paid_cents` after the payment is at least the cumulative charged amount, defined as the sum of `charged_cents` over this subscription's deliveries whose `billing_status` is `CHARGED` or `PARTIALLY_CREDITED`; the money axis moves `DUNNING → CURRENT` alongside it.

A member or an administrator may pause an `ACTIVE` subscription, which moves it `ACTIVE → PAUSED` and makes every one of its `SCHEDULED` deliveries in a week strictly later than the current delivery week ineligible for catch allocation. Resuming moves it `PAUSED → ACTIVE`, requires the season to be `OPEN` and the member's `standing` to be `GOOD` or `WARNED`, and restores eligibility. That `ACTIVE → PAUSED → ACTIVE` cycle is re-entrant and may be walked any number of times, as is the `ACTIVE → PAST_DUE → ACTIVE` cycle above it. There is also a second, system-driven route into `PAUSED`: when a member's `standing` becomes `RESTRICTED`, every `ACTIVE` subscription that member holds is paused, and while the standing remains `RESTRICTED` an attempt to resume is refused with `ERR_MEMBER_RESTRICTED`.

Cancellation is available from `ACTIVE`, from `PAUSED` and — as an administrative act — from `PAST_DUE`, and in every one of those three cases it behaves identically: the subscription moves to `CANCELLED`, every one of its deliveries still in `SCHEDULED` moves to `VOIDED` with `fill_status` `VOID` and *no* member credit, and `billing_status` moves to `SETTLED`. Cancellation is deliberately unsympathetic; a member who wants their money back must skip within the cutoff instead.

A subscription's counters are readable as a computed ledger that reports `promised_delivery_count`, `delivered_count`, `credited_count`, `member_fault_count`, `skip_credited_count`, `undelivered_count`, `skip_credit_balance_cents`, `expired_credit_cents`, `commitment_cents`, `paid_cents`, `rollover_credit_cents` and `true_up_amount_cents` in one place. `delivered_count` counts this subscription's deliveries that reached `DELIVERED` or `PICKED_UP`. `credited_count` counts promised deliveries the CSF failed to supply and refunded — it counts voided deliveries only and never counts skips. `skip_credited_count` counts skip requests standing in `CREDITED`. `member_fault_count` counts deliveries lost to the member's own fault, which is to say hub no-shows plus forfeited skips. `undelivered_count` is written only by the true-up sweep. Subscriptions can be listed and narrowed by `member_id`, `share_plan_id`, `season_id`, `pickup_hub_id`, `delivery_zone_id`, `renewed_from_subscription_id` and `lifecycle_status`.

### 3.3. Vessels, Entitlements and the Landing

A vessel is registered with a `vessel_name` of 1 to 120 characters, a `permit_number` of 1 to 32 characters that is unique across the fleet, a `home_port`, and a non-empty `gear_types` array drawn from the five declared gear types. It starts `COMPLIANT` with a `violation_count` of 0. An administrator moves a vessel around its compliance cycle by supplying a `target_status`. Moving `COMPLIANT → PROBATION` increments `violation_count` by 1 and nothing else. Moving `PROBATION → SUSPENDED` increments `violation_count` by 1 again, and additionally suspends the vessel's entitlements: every quota ledger of that vessel standing in `OPEN` or `WARNING` moves to `SUSPENDED`, and both trip creation and landing recording for that vessel are refused with `ERR_VESSEL_SUSPENDED`. Coming back is asymmetric. Moving `PROBATION → COMPLIANT` changes nothing but the axis. Moving `SUSPENDED → COMPLIANT` re-opens each of the vessel's `SUSPENDED` ledgers, and each one lands where its own consumption puts it: back to `OPEN` when its `committed_pounds` divided by its `entitlement_pounds` is strictly less than `QUOTA_WARNING_PCT`, which is 0.85, and to `WARNING` otherwise. `COMPLIANT → PROBATION → SUSPENDED → COMPLIANT` is a full cycle and may be walked repeatedly, `violation_count` accumulating each time.

**Quota ledgers** are the regulatory ceiling. One is created per vessel, season, species and `management_area` — a code of 1 to 16 characters — and that four-part combination must be unique. The ledger carries an `entitlement_pounds` figure greater than zero with at most two decimals, and its `committed_pounds` starts at 0 while `remaining_pounds` starts equal to the entitlement. Four more inputs describe the fishery: an `area_closed` boolean defaulting to false, a `species_open_date` and a `species_close_date` bounding the period during which the species may be landed in that area (both boundaries inclusive), and a non-empty `permitted_gears` array. Two integers tune the sustainability score without touching the pounds: `area_sensitivity_penalty`, between 0 and 40, records how sensitive the habitat is, and `species_stock_adjustment`, between −20 and 10, records the health of the stock. Ledgers are readable individually and listable with `vessel_id` and `season_id` filters.

A ledger's `status` tracks consumption and is re-entrant. It opens in `OPEN`. Immediately after a landing debits it, it moves `OPEN → WARNING` when `committed_pounds` divided by `entitlement_pounds` has reached or passed 0.85 while `remaining_pounds` is still greater than zero, and moves to `EXHAUSTED` from either `OPEN` or `WARNING` the moment `remaining_pounds` reaches exactly zero. An exhausted ledger refuses further landings with `ERR_QUOTA_EXCEEDED`. It is not, however, a dead end: rejecting a landing report credits the pounds back, and the ledger returns from `EXHAUSTED` to `WARNING` when the post-credit ratio is still at or above 0.85, or from `EXHAUSTED` to `OPEN` when it has fallen strictly below it; a `WARNING` ledger likewise returns to `OPEN` when the credit drops the ratio below the threshold. `EXHAUSTED → WARNING → EXHAUSTED` is a genuine loop, driven entirely by rejected landings.

**Trips** are planned before they are fished. A trip names a `vessel_id`, a `season_id`, a `quota_ledger_id`, the `gear_type` that will be deployed and the `management_area` that will be fished, and it starts `PLANNED` with `landed_at` null, `offload_minutes` 0, `sustainability_score` 0 and `sustainability_band` `GREEN`. Trips are readable individually and listable with `vessel_id`, `season_id` and `quota_ledger_id` filters.

Recording the landing — the `PLANNED → LANDED` move, and the only operation in the system reserved exclusively to a `Vendor` — is where most of the domain's regulatory weight falls. The vendor supplies the `species` landed, the `landed_pounds_gross` that came over the rail, the `ex_vessel_price_cents_per_lb` agreed for the lot as a positive integer, the `offload_minutes` of labour the dock spent on it as a non-negative integer, and optionally an `allow_partial_fill` flag. The call requires the trip to be `PLANNED`, the season to be `OPEN` and the vessel to be `COMPLIANT` or on `PROBATION`; a season that has moved past open refuses the landing with `ERR_SEASON_NOT_OPEN`.

Four regulatory gates then apply in the order given in §2.5. The entitlement's area must not be administratively closed, or the landing is refused with `ERR_AREA_CLOSED`. The logical clock must sit at or after `species_open_date` and at or before `species_close_date`, or it is refused with `ERR_SPECIES_OUT_OF_SEASON`. The trip's `gear_type` must appear both in the ledger's `permitted_gears` and in the vessel's own `gear_types`, or it is refused with `ERR_GEAR_NOT_PERMITTED`.

The fourth gate is the sustainability score, which is computed at this moment and written to the trip. It starts from the gear: `GEAR_BASE_SCORE` awards 90 to `HOOK_AND_LINE`, 85 to `DIVE`, 78 to `TRAP`, 60 to `GILLNET` and 45 to `TRAWL`. From that base the ledger's `area_sensitivity_penalty` is subtracted and its `species_stock_adjustment` is added; the result is then clamped into the range 0 through 100, meaning any value below 0 becomes 0 and any value above 100 becomes 100, and that clamped integer is `sustainability_score`. The band follows from the score with lower-inclusive boundaries: `GREEN` at `SUSTAINABILITY_GREEN_MIN`, which is a score of 80 or more; `YELLOW` from `SUSTAINABILITY_YELLOW_MIN` of 60 up to but not including 80; `ORANGE` from `SUSTAINABILITY_ORANGE_MIN` of 40 up to but not including 60; and `RED` below 40. A `RED` band blocks the landing outright with `ERR_SUSTAINABILITY_BLOCKED`. A trap-caught trip against an area carrying a penalty of 38 with no stock adjustment scores exactly 40 and squeaks into `ORANGE`; a trawl trip against a penalty of 10 scores 35 and is refused.

Only then are the pounds debited. Let the ledger's available pounds be its `entitlement_pounds` less its `committed_pounds`. When `landed_pounds_gross` is at most that available figure, `landed_pounds_net` equals the gross and `discarded_over_quota_pounds` is 0.00. When the gross exceeds it, behaviour forks on `allow_partial_fill`: if the flag is true, `landed_pounds_net` is trimmed to exactly the available pounds and the excess — the gross less the available figure — is written to `discarded_over_quota_pounds` as a regulatory discard; if the flag is false or absent, the landing is refused with `ERR_QUOTA_EXCEEDED`. The ledger's `committed_pounds` then rises by `landed_pounds_net` and `remaining_pounds` is recomputed as `entitlement_pounds` less `committed_pounds`. All of this is exact two-decimal subtraction; nothing here is rounded. A ledger with a 500.00 pound entitlement and 480.00 already committed has 20.00 available: a 25.00 pound gross landing is refused without the flag and yields a net of 20.00 with 5.00 discarded with it, driving the ledger to `EXHAUSTED`, while a gross of exactly 20.00 is accepted either way.

The landing creates the **landing lot** — a single row in `status` `RECORDED` and `quality_status` `CLEAR`, carrying the `species`, the gross and net pounds, the discard figure, the agreed `ex_vessel_price_cents_per_lb`, and a `retail_value_cents_per_lb` looked up from the species' commercial tier, so that a lot of `COD` carries 1150 and a lot of `SALMON` carries 1850. Its `allocation_week_index` is null, its `allocated_pounds_total`, `surplus_secondary_pounds`, `surplus_donated_pounds` and `split_pounds_total` are all 0, its cold-chain roll-ups `cold_chain_cumulative_minutes`, `cold_chain_max_single_minutes` and `peak_temp_c` are all 0, and its `allocation_variant` is `NONE`. The trip's `landed_at` is stamped from the logical clock and its `status` becomes `LANDED`. Lots are readable individually and listable with a `trip_id` filter.

Verification is the administrator's counter-signature and takes a single boolean, `accepted`. Accepting moves the trip `LANDED → VERIFIED`, which is the precondition for creating a settlement statement. Rejecting moves the trip `LANDED → REJECTED`, requires the trip's lot to still be in `RECORDED`, returns `landed_pounds_net` to the ledger by reducing `committed_pounds` by exactly that figure — restoring `committed_pounds` and `remaining_pounds` to their pre-landing values — recomputes the ledger's status per the thresholds above, and moves the lot `RECORDED → CLOSED`, leaving its `quality_status` untouched. A rejected lot is never allocated.

### 3.4. Allocating the Catch of the Week

The weekly allocation is the heart of the CSF and the point at which a finite, variable pool of pounds meets a fixed set of promises. An administrator runs it against one landing lot for one delivery week, supplying that `week_index` and the `expected_landing_lot_version` described in §2.7. The lot must be in `RECORDED` or in `ALLOCATED`, its `quality_status` must be `CLEAR`, its trip must be `LANDED`, its `sustainability_band` must be `GREEN`, `YELLOW` or `ORANGE`, and the season must be `OPEN`.

Eligibility is narrow and every clause matters. A share delivery participates only if its `season_id` matches the season of the lot's trip; its `week_index` equals the week supplied on the call; its `fulfillment_status` is `SCHEDULED`; its subscription's `lifecycle_status` is `ACTIVE`; and its subscription's plan has a `species_tier` exactly equal to the commercial tier of the lot's species under the `SPECIES_TIER_MAP`. Everything else is out: paused, past-due, cancelled and transferred subscriptions do not receive fish, and neither does a whitefish plan when a shellfish lot lands.

The eligible set is then put in a fixed order — by the plan's `weight_units` descending, and among equal weights by the delivery's `created_at` ascending. Call the lot's `landed_pounds_net` the pool and call the sum of `weight_units` across the eligible set the total units. Each delivery's exact proportional share is the pool multiplied by its own `weight_units` and divided by the total units, kept as an exact rational number and not rounded at this stage. Which of two branches runs depends on those exact shares alone.

**The normal-fill branch** runs when every delivery's exact proportional share reaches its plan's `min_floor_pounds`. Each delivery is allocated its exact share truncated downward to two decimal places, capped at its plan's `target_pounds_per_delivery` so that no box is overfilled. Truncation and capping together leave a residue — the pool less the sum of what was allocated — and that residue is handed out one hundredth of a pound at a time, walking the fixed order repeatedly, to deliveries still strictly below their target. The lot's `allocation_variant` is recorded as `NORMAL_FILL`.

**The short-fill branch** runs when at least one delivery's exact proportional share falls below its plan's `min_floor_pounds`. Here floors come first. Walking the fixed order, each delivery whose `min_floor_pounds` is less than or equal to the pounds still unassigned receives exactly its `min_floor_pounds`; each delivery for which it is not receives 0.00 pounds and is left empty. Whatever remains unassigned after that walk is then spread across only the deliveries that did receive their floor, in proportion to their `weight_units`, truncated downward to two decimals, and the residue from that truncation is again handed out one hundredth of a pound at a time in the fixed order to deliveries still below their target. The lot's `allocation_variant` is recorded as `SHORT_FILL`. The deliveries left at 0.00 pounds are precisely the ones that will open the substitution ladder in §3.5.

Consider three eligible deliveries created in the order d1 (`MEDIUM`, 2 units, target 5.00, floor 2.00), d2 (`LARGE`, 3 units, target 7.50, floor 3.00) and d3 (`SMALL`, 1 unit, target 2.50, floor 1.00) — six total units and fifteen pounds of combined target, with the fixed order running d2, d1, d3. A pool of 8.00 pounds takes the normal branch and yields 2.66, 4.01 and 1.33, the single hundredth of residue landing on d2 because it comes first in the order. A pool of exactly 6.00 pounds also takes the normal branch, landing precisely on the floors at 2.00, 3.00 and 1.00. A pool of 5.99 pounds takes the short branch and yields 2.39, 3.60 and 0.00. A pool of 5.00 pounds takes the short branch and yields 2.00, 3.00 and 0.00. A pool of 18.00 pounds takes the normal branch, fills all three to target at 5.00, 7.50 and 2.50, and leaves 3.00 pounds of surplus.

Each delivery the run fills is stamped with its `landing_lot_id` and its `allocated_pounds`, and moves `SCHEDULED → ALLOCATED` on the fulfillment axis. On the fill axis it moves `PENDING → FULL_FILL` when its `allocated_pounds` has reached or passed its plan's `target_pounds_per_delivery`, and `PENDING → UNDER_FILLED` when it has not. The lot records `allocated_pounds_total` as the sum of what it handed out, records the `allocation_week_index` it was run for, moves `RECORDED → ALLOCATED`, and increments its `version` by 1.

Whatever the allocation did not hand out is **surplus**, and it is routed immediately in the same request. Take the lot's `landed_pounds_net` less its `allocated_pounds_total`. `SURPLUS_SECONDARY_MAX_PCT` is 0.60, so `surplus_secondary_pounds` is 60 percent of that surplus truncated downward to two decimals, and `surplus_donated_pounds` is everything left after that — the donation bucket deliberately absorbs the truncation residue so that allocated, secondary and donated pounds sum exactly back to `landed_pounds_net`. A surplus of 3.00 pounds routes 1.80 to the secondary market and 1.20 to donation; a surplus of 1.00 pound routes 0.60 and 0.40.

An allocation may be re-run against a lot already in `ALLOCATED`, and that re-run is a genuine supersede rather than an increment. It requires the lot's `quality_status` to still be `CLEAR` and requires that no sub-lot yet exist for the lot — once the fish has been physically split, the allocation is frozen. The re-run first discards the prior allocation completely: every delivery carrying this lot's identifier returns to `fulfillment_status` `SCHEDULED`, `fill_status` `PENDING`, `allocated_pounds` 0.00, and a null `landing_lot_id`. It then re-runs both the allocation and the surplus routing against the eligible set for the `week_index` supplied on the new call, and increments the lot's `version` by 1 again. This supersede is exactly the hazard the stale-version rejection in §2.7 exists to make safe.

**Splitting** converts one allocated lot into the physical consignments that leave the dock. The request supplies a `destinations` array; each entry names a `destination_type` of `HUB` or `ROUTE`, a `destination_id` identifying the pickup hub or the delivery zone, and the `pounds` going to it. The lot must be `ALLOCATED` with `quality_status` `CLEAR`, and mass conservation is checked with no tolerance whatsoever. First, the sum of the requested destination pounds must equal the lot's `allocated_pounds_total` exactly to two decimals. Second, each individual destination's `pounds` must equal the sum of `allocated_pounds` across the deliveries routed to it — a delivery routes to a hub destination when its subscription's `pickup_hub_id` matches that destination and to a route destination when its subscription's `delivery_zone_id` matches. Any disagreement, in either test, is refused with `ERR_MASS_CONSERVATION_VIOLATION`; a lot with 9.00 allocated pounds split as 4.50 to one hub and 4.50 to one zone is accepted only when the deliveries actually routed to that hub were allocated 4.50 pounds between them, and 4.49 or 4.51 is refused. On success one sub-lot is created per destination in `IN_TRANSIT`, each carrying its `landing_lot_id`, its `destination_type`, its `pounds` and either a `pickup_hub_id` or a `delivery_zone_id` according to its type; every routed delivery is stamped with its `sub_lot_id`; `split_pounds_total` is set to the requested total; and the lot moves `ALLOCATED → SPLIT`.

Hub staff record a consignment's arrival, moving that sub-lot `IN_TRANSIT → ARRIVED`; the move is refused while the parent lot's `quality_status` is `DISCARDED`. When no sub-lot of a lot remains `IN_TRANSIT`, the lot itself moves `SPLIT → DISPATCHED`. Later, when every delivery carried by a sub-lot has come to rest in `DELIVERED`, `PICKED_UP` or `DONATED` — that is, when none is still in `PACKED`, `IN_TRANSIT`, `AT_HUB` or `NO_SHOW` — that sub-lot moves `ARRIVED → CONSUMED`; and when every delivery of a whole lot has come to rest in `DELIVERED`, `PICKED_UP`, `DONATED` or `VOIDED`, with none left in `ALLOCATED`, `PACKED`, `IN_TRANSIT`, `AT_HUB` or `NO_SHOW`, the lot moves `DISPATCHED → CLOSED`. Sub-lots are readable individually and listable with `landing_lot_id`, `pickup_hub_id` and `delivery_zone_id` filters.

### 3.5. Making an Under-Filled Box Whole

When the catch runs short, the CSF offers the member fish of a different species instead. Only an administrator may propose one, and only against a box whose `fill_status` is `UNDER_FILLED` — a proposal against a box in any other fill state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, and a proposal against a box whose subscription is not `ACTIVE` is refused with `ERR_SUBSCRIPTION_NOT_ACTIVE`. The proposal names a `substitute_landing_lot_id` — which must belong to the same season as the delivery — and the `substitute_pounds` being offered, a positive figure with at most two decimals. The offer records the `substitute_species` drawn from that lot and is created in `PROPOSED`.

Four member-protection tests guard the proposal, and they are evaluated in the order the ladder gives them. If the substitute species appears in the member's `allergy_exclusions`, the proposal is refused with `ERR_ALLERGY_EXCLUSION` — this block is absolute and has no override. If the member has set `never_substitute` to true, it is refused with `ERR_NEVER_SUBSTITUTE`; such a member takes money rather than unfamiliar fish. If the substitute species appears in the member's `dislikes`, the CSF looks for an alternative: the proposal is refused with `ERR_DISLIKE_ALTERNATIVE_AVAILABLE` when at least one other species in the season's `offered_species` is in neither the member's `allergy_exclusions` nor their `dislikes` and is actually carried by some landing lot of the same season whose `status` is `RECORDED`, `ALLOCATED` or `SPLIT` and whose `quality_status` is `CLEAR`. When no such alternative exists, the disliked substitute is allowed through — a dislike is a preference, not a prohibition. Finally the substitute lot must be able to supply what is being offered: `substitute_pounds` must be at most that lot's `landed_pounds_net`, less its `allocated_pounds_total`, less the pounds already committed to substitutions drawn from that lot which stand in `ACCEPTED` or `CREDIT_ISSUED`. A lot that cannot cover the offer produces `ERR_NO_SUBSTITUTE_AVAILABLE`.

The offer is priced at retail on both sides. The `shortfall_pounds` is the plan's `target_pounds_per_delivery` less the delivery's `allocated_pounds` at the moment of proposal — note that this measures the hole the allocation left, not any pounds a previous substitution added. The `owed_value_cents` is that shortfall multiplied by the retail rate of the *plan's own* species tier, rounded half up once. The `substitute_value_cents` is `substitute_pounds` multiplied by the substitute lot's `retail_value_cents_per_lb`, rounded half up once. Comparing the two fixes the `settlement_variant`. When the substitute is worth at least what is owed, the variant is `EQUAL_OR_GREATER` and `member_credit_cents` is 0 — the member is made whole in kind and receives no money, even if the substitute is worth considerably more. When the substitute is worth strictly less, the variant is `BELOW_VALUE` and `member_credit_cents` is the value gap — `owed_value_cents` less `substitute_value_cents` — capped at the delivery's own `subtotal_cents`, so that a substitution can never refund more than the share charge itself. Take a `SMALL` `PREMIUM_FINFISH` box allocated 0.00 pounds against a 2.50 pound target: the shortfall is 2.50 pounds and the owed value is 4625 cents. Offering 4.03 pounds of `COD` is worth 4635 cents and settles as `EQUAL_OR_GREATER` with no credit; offering 4.02 pounds is worth 4623 and settles as `BELOW_VALUE` with a credit of 2 cents; offering 2.00 pounds is worth 2300 and settles as `BELOW_VALUE` with a credit of 2325 cents.

The member decides. Accepting an `EQUAL_OR_GREATER` offer moves it `PROPOSED → ACCEPTED`; accepting a `BELOW_VALUE` offer moves it `PROPOSED → CREDIT_ISSUED`. Both outcomes add the offer's `substitute_pounds` to the delivery's `substituted_pounds` and move the delivery's fill axis `UNDER_FILLED → SUBSTITUTED`. Only the second issues money: `member_credit_cents` is added to the delivery's `credit_cents` and to the member's `credit_balance_cents`, and if the box has already been charged its money axis moves `CHARGED → PARTIALLY_CREDITED`. Rejecting the offer moves it `PROPOSED → REJECTED`, leaves the delivery in `UNDER_FILLED`, and means the box will simply be packed short. Offers are readable individually and listable with `share_delivery_id` and `substitute_landing_lot_id` filters.

### 3.6. Cold Chain Custody and Quality Disposition

Fish is measured against a temperature threshold on every leg of its journey, and the record of that measurement is the CSF's evidence. A hub operator or a quality officer logs one leg at a time by naming the `landing_lot_id`, the `custody_leg` it was measured on, the `minutes_above_threshold` as a non-negative whole number, and the `peak_temp_c` reached on that leg as a number with at most one decimal. A `sub_lot_id` accompanies the record when the leg belongs to a particular consignment and is omitted for the dock leg. The record is stamped with a `recorded_at` from the logical clock and a `recorded_by_user_id` taken from the caller's `X-User-ID`. Cold-chain records are append-only observations: they carry no `updated_at` and no `version`, and once written they are never excluded from any later evaluation. They are readable individually and listable with `landing_lot_id` and `sub_lot_id` filters.

Logging a leg refreshes three roll-ups on the parent lot across every leg ever logged against it, this one included: `cold_chain_cumulative_minutes` is the sum of `minutes_above_threshold`, `cold_chain_max_single_minutes` is the largest single such figure, and the lot's `peak_temp_c` is the highest `peak_temp_c` any leg reached. Two budgets are then tested, and both breach on a strict excess so that a figure sitting exactly on the budget is still inside it. The single-excursion budget, `COLD_CHAIN_SINGLE_EXCURSION_MAX_MIN`, is 20 minutes and is tested against *this* leg's own `minutes_above_threshold`. The cumulative budget, `COLD_CHAIN_CUMULATIVE_BUDGET_MIN`, is 45 minutes and is tested against the refreshed `cold_chain_cumulative_minutes`. The leg's `breach_type` is `BOTH` when both are exceeded, `SINGLE_EXCURSION` when only the leg's own figure is, `CUMULATIVE` when only the running total is, and `NONE` when neither is. Legs of 18 then 22 minutes make the second `SINGLE_EXCURSION`, because 22 exceeds 20 while the running 40 is still inside 45. Legs of 20, 20 then 6 minutes make the third `CUMULATIVE`, because the running 46 exceeds 45 while 6 is well inside 20. Legs of 20, 20 then 21 make the third `BOTH`.

Any `breach_type` other than `NONE` moves the lot's quality axis `CLEAR → HOLD`, and a hold has immediate consequences on both sides of the business. On the member side, handing over a box from a held lot — completing a home delivery or recording a hub pickup — is refused with `ERR_LOT_ON_QUALITY_HOLD`. On the vendor side, any settlement statement of that lot standing in `PROVISIONAL` moves to `HELD`, and finalizing a held statement is refused with `ERR_HOLDBACK_NOT_CLEARED` for as long as the lot remains on hold.

A hold is resolved by a quality officer, and by nobody else, through a `disposition` of `RELEASE`, `DISCOUNT` or `DISCARD`. Two gates apply first. The budget gate asks whether the requested disposition is defensible given how bad the excursion was: `RELEASE` requires `cold_chain_cumulative_minutes` to be at most `COLD_CHAIN_RELEASE_LIMIT_MIN`, which is 60 minutes, *and* the lot's `peak_temp_c` to be at most `QUALITY_RELEASE_TEMP_C`, which is 7.0 degrees Celsius; `DISCOUNT` requires `cold_chain_cumulative_minutes` to be at most `COLD_CHAIN_DISCOUNT_LIMIT_MIN`, which is 90 minutes; and `DISCARD` has no budget test at all, because condemning fish is always permissible. A disposition outside its budget is refused with `ERR_COLD_CHAIN_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`. The segregation gate then asks who is signing: the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the `recorded_by_user_id` of every cold-chain leg on this lot whose `breach_type` is not `NONE`. An officer who logged the breach may not also clear it, and an attempt to do so is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The gate looks only at breaching legs; an officer who logged a clean leg on the same lot is not disqualified.

`RELEASE` moves the quality axis `HOLD → RELEASED`. Deliveries of that lot may resume, and no member money moves at all. Any settlement statement of the lot standing in `HELD` returns to `PROVISIONAL`, which makes `PROVISIONAL → HELD → PROVISIONAL` a genuine re-entrant loop on the settlement axis.

`DISCOUNT` moves the quality axis `HOLD → DISCOUNTED` and compensates the members who have already been billed. `QUALITY_DISCOUNT_CREDIT_PCT` is 0.30, so every delivery of that lot whose `billing_status` is `CHARGED` or `PARTIALLY_CREDITED` receives a credit of 30 percent of its own `invoice_total_cents`, rounded half up per delivery; that amount is added to the delivery's `credit_cents` and to the owning member's `credit_balance_cents`, and the delivery's money axis moves to `PARTIALLY_CREDITED`. A delivery already sitting in `REFUNDED` or `FORFEITED` receives nothing. A box whose `invoice_total_cents` is 6250 therefore receives 1875 cents back. A delivery may accumulate more than one credit — a below-value substitution credit followed by a quality discount credit, for instance — and the money axis stays on `PARTIALLY_CREDITED` through each addition as long as the accumulated `credit_cents` remains strictly below `charged_cents`. On the vendor side, a `HELD` statement becomes eligible for finalization under the discount holdback split described in §3.9.

`DISCARD` moves the quality axis `HOLD → DISCARDED` and starts the longest cascade in the system, six hops deep, all inside the one request. The disposition itself is hop one. Hop two destroys the physical consignments: every sub-lot of that lot standing in `IN_TRANSIT` or `ARRIVED` moves to `DESTROYED`. Hop three, caused by hop two, voids the boxes those consignments were carrying: every delivery whose `sub_lot_id` names one of them and whose `fulfillment_status` is `ALLOCATED`, `PACKED`, `IN_TRANSIT` or `AT_HUB` moves to `VOIDED` with `fill_status` `VOID`, and a box that was sitting `AT_HUB` releases the cooler slot it had reserved as it goes. Hop four, caused by hop three, refunds them: each such delivery whose money axis stands in `CHARGED` or `PARTIALLY_CREDITED` moves to `REFUNDED` with `credit_cents` raised to its full `invoice_total_cents`, that amount is added to the member's `credit_balance_cents`, and the owning subscription's `credited_count` rises by 1. Hop five, caused by the `credited_count` written in hop four, flags the subscription for the season-end reckoning: a subscription whose `true_up_status` is `NOT_DUE` moves to `SHORTFALL_FLAGGED`. Hop six, caused by hop five, increments the season's `shortfall_flag_count` by 1. On the vendor side, a `HELD` statement becomes eligible for finalization with the whole holdback forfeited and an offset charged back, again per §3.9.

### 3.7. Packing, Pricing, and the Two Fulfillment Pipelines

Packing is where a box acquires its price, and it is a hub operator's job. A box may be packed once its `fulfillment_status` is `ALLOCATED` and its `sub_lot_id` has been stamped by the split — a box that has not been assigned to a consignment cannot be packed. Its `fill_status` may be `FULL_FILL`, `UNDER_FILLED` or `SUBSTITUTED`: an under-filled box whose substitution was rejected, or never proposed at all, is simply packed short. Packing is refused with `ERR_PAYMENT_REQUIRED` when the owning subscription is in `PAST_DUE`.

The physical box comes first. `box_pounds` is the delivery's `allocated_pounds` plus its `substituted_pounds` — the actual weight going into the container. On the hub-pickup pipeline there is no insulated pack at all: `pack_size` is `NONE` and `gel_pack_count` is 0. On the home-delivery pipeline the pack tier is chosen by weight, with each boundary belonging to the lower tier: `PACK_SMALL_MAX_LB` is 6.00 pounds, so a box at or below 6.00 pounds takes a `SMALL` pack; `PACK_MEDIUM_MAX_LB` is 14.00 pounds, so a box above 6.00 and at or below 14.00 takes a `MEDIUM`; and anything above 14.00 takes a `LARGE`. Exactly 6.00 pounds is `SMALL`, 6.01 is `MEDIUM`, 14.00 is `MEDIUM` and 14.01 is `LARGE`.

Gel packs follow from the pack tier, the season's `ambient_band` and the zone's `transit_hours`. `GEL_PACK_HOURS` states how many hours one gel pack holds a given pack tier in a given band. In the `COOL` band a pack lasts 3.0 hours in a `SMALL` container, 6.0 in a `MEDIUM` and 8.0 in a `LARGE`. In the `MILD` band it lasts 2.0, 4.0 and 5.5 hours respectively. In the `HOT` band it lasts 1.5, 3.0 and 4.0 hours. The `gel_pack_count` is the zone's `transit_hours` divided by the applicable figure and rounded *up* to a whole number, with a floor of one pack — a home-delivery box always carries at least one. There is a counter-intuitive consequence worth stating plainly: a smaller container has less thermal mass and therefore burns gel packs faster, so in the `HOT` band over a 6.0 hour transit a `SMALL` pack needs four gel packs while a `MEDIUM` pack needs only two, and a `LARGE` also two. In the `MILD` band over a 5.0 hour transit a `SMALL` pack needs three.

The invoice is then assembled, and **its evaluation order is pinned and must be followed exactly** because one of its steps reads a running total that later steps change.

1. The `subtotal_cents` is the subscription's `price_per_delivery_cents` — the discounted unit price for a prepaid subscription, the list price for an installment one.
2. On the hub-pickup pipeline, both the pack cost and the zone base are 0. On the home-delivery pipeline, the pack cost is the base fee for the chosen pack tier plus the gel-pack fee for each pack: `PACK_BASE_FEE_CENTS` is 300 cents for `SMALL`, 450 for `MEDIUM` and 600 for `LARGE`, and `GEL_PACK_FEE_CENTS` is 175 cents per pack. The zone base is set by the zone's `fee_tier`: `ZONE_BASE_FEE_CENTS` is 500 cents for `TIER_1`, 800 for `TIER_2` and 1250 for `TIER_3`.
3. The `surcharge_cents` is decided next. It is 0 whenever the subscription's `fulfillment_mode` is `HUB_PICKUP`. It is also 0 whenever `subtotal_cents` has reached or passed `FREE_SURCHARGE_THRESHOLD_CENTS`, which is 6000 cents — a large enough share earns free carriage. Otherwise it is `REMOTE_SURCHARGE_CENTS`, which is 250 cents. **The waiver test reads `subtotal_cents` and nothing else.** It must not read the zone base, the pack cost, `delivery_fee_cents` or `invoice_total_cents`, even though the surcharge ultimately lands inside `invoice_total_cents`. This is what breaks the otherwise circular dependency between a fee component and a fee-derived total, and evaluating the waiver against any running total that already includes fees produces a different number on the same inputs.
4. The `delivery_fee_cents` is the zone base plus the pack cost plus the surcharge.
5. The `invoice_total_cents` is `subtotal_cents` plus `delivery_fee_cents`.
6. The `charged_cents` is set equal to `invoice_total_cents`.

Every term above is already a whole number of cents, so no rounding is applied anywhere in this chain. A `MEDIUM` × `PREMIUM_FINFISH` weekly home delivery of a 3.00 pound box into a `TIER_1` zone with 6.0 hours of transit in a `HOT` season prices out as: subtotal 4500, a `SMALL` pack with four gel packs, surcharge 250, `delivery_fee_cents` of 1750 and `invoice_total_cents` of 6250. The same plan on hub pickup prices out as subtotal 4500, pack `NONE`, no gel packs, no surcharge, `delivery_fee_cents` 0 and `invoice_total_cents` 4500.

The fee chain is not monotone in weight, and operations should expect complaints about it. Take a `HOT` season, a `TIER_1` zone with 6.0 hours of transit, and a `LARGE` × `WHITEFISH` × `WEEKLY` plan whose subtotal is 5200 on both runs. A 6.00 pound box takes a `SMALL` pack and four gel packs, costing 300 plus 700, and with the 500 zone base and the 250 surcharge produces a `delivery_fee_cents` of 1750 and an `invoice_total_cents` of 6950. A 6.01 pound box takes a `MEDIUM` pack and only two gel packs, costing 450 plus 350, producing a `delivery_fee_cents` of 1550 and an `invoice_total_cents` of 6750. One extra hundredth of a pound of fish makes the box 200 cents cheaper, both in absolute fee and per pound.

Packing moves the delivery `ALLOCATED → PACKED` and its money axis `PENDING → CHARGED`. It is also the moment arrears are tested on an installment subscription: when `paid_cents` is less than the sum of `charged_cents` across that subscription's deliveries standing in `CHARGED` or `PARTIALLY_CREDITED`, the subscription moves `ACTIVE → PAST_DUE` and its `billing_status` moves `CURRENT → DUNNING`.

**On the home-delivery pipeline**, a packed box is dispatched, moving `PACKED → IN_TRANSIT`. Dispatch requires the subscription's `fulfillment_mode` to be `HOME_DELIVERY`, the carrying sub-lot's `destination_type` to be `ROUTE`, and the box to carry at least one gel pack; it is refused with `ERR_PAYMENT_REQUIRED` when the subscription is `PAST_DUE`. Completing the delivery moves `IN_TRANSIT → DELIVERED` and requires two things beyond the state itself: the lot's `quality_status` must be `CLEAR`, `RELEASED` or `DISCOUNTED`, and at least one cold-chain leg with `custody_leg` `HUB_TO_DOOR` must already have been logged against that lot. A missing leg is refused with `ERR_COLD_CHAIN_EVIDENCE_MISSING`. Completing a delivery may be the event that moves the carrying sub-lot `ARRIVED → CONSUMED` and the lot `DISPATCHED → CLOSED`.

**On the hub-pickup pipeline**, a packed box is staged into a cooler slot by naming the `pickup_window_id` it goes into. Staging requires the subscription's `fulfillment_mode` to be `HUB_PICKUP`, the carrying sub-lot's `destination_type` to be `HUB`, the window to be `SCHEDULED` or `OPEN` — once a window has slipped into `GRACE` staging into it is refused with `ERR_WINDOW_NOT_OPEN` — and the window to have at least one free slot. The window's `week_index` must match the delivery's, and its hub must match the subscription's, per §2.8. Staging stamps `pickup_window_id` onto the delivery and moves it `PACKED → AT_HUB`.

Cooler slots are strictly accounted. Staging raises `slots_reserved` by 1. A pickup lowers `slots_reserved` by 1 and raises `slots_occupied` by 1. A no-show lowers `slots_reserved` by 1 and raises `slots_forfeited` by 1. Voiding a box that was staged simply releases its reservation, lowering `slots_reserved` by 1. `slots_free` is at all times `cooler_slots_total` less the reserved, occupied and forfeited counts, and staging is refused with `ERR_HUB_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` when `slots_free` is below 1 before the increment. A window with two slots therefore accepts two staged boxes and refuses the third; if one of those two is collected and the other is forfeited, the window ends with 0 reserved, 1 occupied, 1 forfeited and 0 free.

Collection moves the delivery `AT_HUB → PICKED_UP`. It requires the window to be `OPEN` or `GRACE`, the logical clock to be at or before the window's `closes_at` plus its frozen `grace_minutes` — one second past that and the collection is refused with `ERR_WINDOW_GRACE_EXPIRED` — the lot's `quality_status` to be `CLEAR`, `RELEASED` or `DISCOUNTED`, and at least one cold-chain leg with `custody_leg` `HUB_HOLD` to have been logged against the lot. Collection increments the owning subscription's `delivered_count` by 1. A collection made strictly after `closes_at` additionally moves the window `OPEN → GRACE`, which is the only way that state is reached.

Closing a window is the reckoning. Hub staff may close a window standing in `OPEN` or `GRACE`, and only once the logical clock is strictly past `closes_at` plus `grace_minutes`. The close runs a four-hop cascade in one request. Hop one: every delivery in that window still standing in `AT_HUB` moves to `NO_SHOW`. Hop two, caused by hop one: each such delivery moves `NO_SHOW → DONATED`, its money axis moves `CHARGED → FORFEITED` with `credit_cents` left at 0 — a no-show is not refunded — its cooler slot moves from reserved to forfeited, the owning member's `season_no_show_count` rises by 1 and the owning subscription's `member_fault_count` rises by 1. Hop three, caused by the member counter written in hop two: the member's `standing` escalates. `NO_SHOW_WARN_THRESHOLD` is 1 and `NO_SHOW_RESTRICT_THRESHOLD` is 2, and both fire on reaching the threshold rather than exceeding it, so a `season_no_show_count` of 1 moves `GOOD → WARNED` while a count of 2 or more moves `WARNED → RESTRICTED`; a count of 0 leaves the standing alone. Hop four, caused by hop three: when a member has become `RESTRICTED`, every subscription of theirs standing in `ACTIVE` moves to `PAUSED`, and it stays paused — clearing the restriction later does not auto-resume it. The window itself moves `OPEN → CLOSED` or `GRACE → CLOSED`, and then, once no delivery in it remains `AT_HUB`, moves `CLOSED → RECONCILED` inside the same request, with `slots_reserved` down to 0 and `slots_free` recomputed.

Deliveries are readable individually and listable, narrowed by `subscription_id`, `season_id`, `landing_lot_id`, `sub_lot_id`, `pickup_window_id`, `week_index` and `fulfillment_status`.

### 3.8. Skips, Transfers and the Waitlist

A member who will be away files a **skip** against one scheduled box by naming its `share_delivery_id`. The box must be in `SCHEDULED` and the subscription must be `ACTIVE`. `SKIP_CAP_PER_SEASON` is 4, and the subscription's `skip_count` must be strictly below that before this skip is counted, or the request is refused with `ERR_SKIP_CAP_EXCEEDED`; the count includes every skip standing in `CREDITED` and every skip standing in `FORFEITED`, and excludes revoked ones.

Everything then turns on one instant. The skip's `cutoff_at` is the target delivery's `scheduled_at` less the season's `landing_commit_lead_hours`, and the skip's `requested_at` is the logical clock at filing. Filing **at or before** `cutoff_at` takes the credit branch: `settlement_variant` is `CREDIT`, the skip's `credit_cents` is the subscription's full `price_per_delivery_cents`, that amount is banked on the subscription's `skip_credit_balance_cents`, `skip_credited_count` rises by 1, the skip is created directly in `CREDITED` and the delivery moves `SCHEDULED → SKIPPED` with its money axis moving `PENDING → CREDITED`. Filing **strictly after** `cutoff_at` takes the forfeit branch: the landing has already been committed against that share, so `settlement_variant` is `FORFEIT`, `credit_cents` is 0, the subscription's `member_fault_count` rises by 1, the skip is created directly in `FORFEITED` and the delivery moves `SCHEDULED → SKIPPED` with its money axis moving `PENDING → FORFEITED`. On both branches the subscription's `skip_count` rises by 1. A week-11 box scheduled for `2026-07-13T00:00:00Z` under a 72-hour lead has a cutoff of `2026-07-10T00:00:00Z`; filing at exactly that instant earns the credit, and filing one second later does not.

A credited skip may be withdrawn while the cutoff has still not passed. Revocation requires the skip to be standing in `CREDITED` and the logical clock to be at or before `cutoff_at`, and a late attempt is refused with `ERR_SKIP_WINDOW_CLOSED`. It moves the skip `CREDITED → REVOKED`, removes `credit_cents` from the subscription's `skip_credit_balance_cents`, decrements both `skip_count` and `skip_credited_count` by 1, and returns the delivery `SKIPPED → SCHEDULED` with its money axis returning to `PENDING`. `SCHEDULED → SKIPPED → SCHEDULED` on the fulfillment axis and `PENDING → CREDITED → PENDING` on the money axis are the two re-entrant loops this creates. A forfeited skip cannot be revoked. Skips are readable individually and listable with `subscription_id` and `share_delivery_id` filters.

A **share transfer** hands the rest of a season to somebody on the waitlist. It is created by a member out of their own subscription, or by an administrator, naming a `from_subscription_id`, a `to_subscription_id`, the `season_id` both belong to, the `effective_week_index` at which the handover takes effect, and the `expected_subscription_version` of §2.7. Eligibility is checked at creation and again at acceptance: the receiving subscription must be `WAITLISTED`, the transferring one must be `ACTIVE`, the two must belong to different members, and `remaining_delivery_count` must be strictly greater than 0, or the request is refused with `ERR_TRANSFER_NOT_ELIGIBLE`.

The money is computed when the offer is created. `remaining_delivery_count` counts the transferring subscription's deliveries whose `week_index` is at or after `effective_week_index` and whose `fulfillment_status` is still `SCHEDULED` — boxes already allocated, packed, skipped or finished are not transferable and are not counted. `transfer_fee_cents` is the flat `TRANSFER_FEE_CENTS` of 2500 cents. The rest forks on the transferring subscription's `payment_basis`. On the `PREPAID` variant, `transferee_charge_cents` is `remaining_delivery_count` multiplied by the transferor's `price_per_delivery_cents`, and `transferor_refund_cents` is that same figure less the transfer fee — the transferor gets back the unused prepaid value minus the fee, and the transferee pays exactly the unused value. On the `INSTALLMENT` variant, nothing was prepaid, so `transferor_refund_cents` is 0 and `transferee_charge_cents` is `remaining_delivery_count` multiplied by the unit price *plus* the transfer fee — the transferee assumes the liability and pays the fee on top. With ten remaining weekly deliveries and a prepaid unit price of 4140, the transferee is charged 41400 and the transferor refunded 38900; with an installment unit price of 4500, the transferee is charged 47500 and the transferor refunded nothing. Either way the CSF's net take is exactly 2500 cents.

Acceptance is the receiving member's act and requires the acting `X-User-ID` to equal the member behind the receiving subscription, both subscriptions to be in the states above, and the season to be `OPEN`. It moves the offer `REQUESTED → ACCEPTED`, moves the transferring subscription `ACTIVE → TRANSFERRED` and the receiving one `WAITLISTED → ACTIVE`. Every delivery of the transferring subscription whose `week_index` is at or after `effective_week_index` and whose `fulfillment_status` is `SCHEDULED` is re-parented to the receiving subscription, and the receiving subscription gets one share delivery for each delivery week from `effective_week_index` onward, its `commitment_cents` set to `transferee_charge_cents` and its `billing_status` set to `CURRENT`. On the transferor's money axis, a prepaid subscription moves `PREPAID_SETTLED → REFUND_PENDING` — the refund owed being recorded in `transferor_refund_cents` — and then to `SETTLED` once that refund is disbursed inside the same request; an installment subscription moves straight to `SETTLED`. Declining moves the offer `REQUESTED → DECLINED` and leaves both subscriptions exactly where they were. An offer that is never answered dies with the season: when the season moves `OPEN → LANDINGS_CLOSED`, every transfer of that season still standing in `REQUESTED` moves to `EXPIRED` and no money moves. Transfers are readable individually and listable with `from_subscription_id`, `to_subscription_id` and `season_id` filters.

### 3.9. Paying the Boat, Recalling the Fish, and Closing the Season

**Vendor settlement** begins once a landing has been verified. An administrator creates a statement against the trip, supplying the `expected_trip_version` of §2.7; the trip must be `VERIFIED`. The statement is computed inside the create call — `DRAFT` is only the pre-computation waypoint, and the statement comes back already in `PROVISIONAL` with its `variant` recorded as `PROVISIONAL`. A statement carries its `trip_id`, its `landing_lot_id` and the payee `vessel_id`. Creating a statement voids any earlier statement of the same trip standing in `DRAFT` or `PROVISIONAL`, moving it to `VOID`, and increments the trip's `version` by 1 — which is why a restatement needs a fresh read of the trip.

Gross pay has two lines. The `ex_vessel_cents` is the lot's `ex_vessel_price_cents_per_lb` multiplied by its `landed_pounds_net`, rounded half up once on its own. The `premium_cents` is the CSF's premium above the dock price, and it is a marginal bracket calculation. The first `CSF_PREMIUM_BRACKET_1_MAX_LB` of 100.00 pounds earn `CSF_PREMIUM_RATE_1_CENTS_PER_LB`, which is 60 cents per pound. Pounds beyond 100.00 and up to `CSF_PREMIUM_BRACKET_2_MAX_LB` of 300.00 earn `CSF_PREMIUM_RATE_2_CENTS_PER_LB`, which is 45 cents per pound. Pounds beyond 300.00 earn `CSF_PREMIUM_RATE_3_CENTS_PER_LB`, which is 30 cents per pound. The three bracket amounts are summed exactly, without rounding, and the raw sum is then multiplied by the trip's band factor: `BAND_PREMIUM_FACTOR` pays 1.00 for `GREEN`, 0.85 for `YELLOW`, 0.50 for `ORANGE` and 0.00 for `RED`. Only that final product is rounded, half up, once. A 350.00 pound lot earns a raw premium of 16500 cents — 100 pounds at 60, 200 at 45, 50 at 30 — which a `GREEN` band keeps in full, a `YELLOW` band cuts to 14025 and an `ORANGE` band cuts to 8250. At exactly 100.00 pounds the raw premium is 6000, and at 100.01 pounds it is 6000.45, which rounds back to 6000. The `gross_cents` is `ex_vessel_cents` plus `premium_cents`.

Deductions come off gross, and **the rounding order here is pinned**: each deduction line is rounded to whole cents on its own, and only the already-rounded lines are summed. Summing the raw lines and rounding once at the end is wrong and produces a different total. The `dock_fee_cents` is `DOCK_FEE_CENTS`, a flat charge of 800 cents levied on every landing regardless of its size. The `ice_handling_cents` is the lot's `landed_pounds_net` multiplied by `ICE_HANDLING_CENTS_PER_LB`, which is 12 cents per pound, rounded half up. The `offload_labor_cents` is the trip's `offload_minutes` multiplied by `OFFLOAD_LABOR_CENTS_PER_MINUTE`, which is 95 cents per minute, rounded half up. The `handling_levy_cents` is `gross_cents` multiplied by `HANDLING_LEVY_PCT`, which is 0.0125, rounded half up. The `deductions_cents` is the sum of those individually rounded lines. Worked through: for a net landing of 10.04 pounds, 12 offload minutes and a gross of 9136 cents, the raw lines are 800, 120.48, 1140 and 114.20; rounding each first gives 800 plus 120 plus 1140 plus 114, which is 2174. Summing first would give 2174.68 and round to 2175, and 2174 is the figure this system publishes.

A quality holdback is retained against every statement. `HOLDBACK_PCT` is 0.10, so `holdback_cents` is 10 percent of `gross_cents`, rounded half up. While the statement is `PROVISIONAL` the entire holdback is simply withheld: `holdback_released_cents`, `holdback_forfeited_cents` and `offset_cents` are all 0, and `net_payable_cents` is `gross_cents` less `deductions_cents` less `holdback_cents`. This is the vessel's floor, the least it can be paid.

Finalization resolves the holdback against whatever the lot's quality disposition turned out to be, and the `variant` becomes `FINAL`. A statement in `PROVISIONAL` may be finalized when the lot's `quality_status` is `CLEAR` or `RELEASED`; a statement in `HELD` may be finalized when the lot's `quality_status` is `DISCOUNTED` or `DISCARDED`; and a statement whose lot is still on `HOLD` cannot be finalized at all, being refused with `ERR_HOLDBACK_NOT_CLEARED`. Three splits are possible. When the lot is `CLEAR` or `RELEASED`, `holdback_released_cents` is the whole `holdback_cents`, `holdback_forfeited_cents` is 0 and `offset_cents` is 0. When the lot is `DISCOUNTED`, `VENDOR_DISCOUNT_HOLDBACK_RELEASE_PCT` is 0.50, so `holdback_released_cents` is half the holdback rounded half up, `holdback_forfeited_cents` is whatever is left of the holdback after that release, and `offset_cents` is 0. When the lot is `DISCARDED`, `holdback_released_cents` is 0, `holdback_forfeited_cents` is the whole holdback, and the vessel is additionally charged an offset: `DISCARD_OFFSET_PCT` is 0.25, so `offset_cents` is 25 percent of `ex_vessel_cents` rounded half up, capped at the provisional net payable — that is, capped at `gross_cents` less `deductions_cents` less `holdback_cents` — so that an offset can never drive the settlement below what was already provisionally payable. On every final split, `net_payable_cents` is `gross_cents` less `deductions_cents` less `holdback_forfeited_cents` less `offset_cents`. Continuing the worked figures: a gross of 9136 with deductions of 2174 carries a holdback of 914. Provisionally the vessel is payable 6048. Finalized against a `CLEAR` lot it is 6962. Finalized against a `DISCOUNTED` lot, 457 is released, 457 is forfeited, and the payable is 6505. Finalized against a `DISCARDED` lot, all 914 is forfeited, the offset is 2134, and the payable is 3914.

Payment is the last act: it moves the statement `FINAL → PAID` and sets `net_paid_cents` equal to `net_payable_cents`. Statements are readable individually and listable with `trip_id`, `landing_lot_id` and `vessel_id` filters.

**Recalls** run the supply chain backwards. An administrator raises one against a lot by naming the `landing_lot_id` and a `reason`; the lot must be standing in `SPLIT`, `DISPATCHED` or `CLOSED`. The notice is created in `INITIATED` — again only a waypoint — and comes back in `SCOPED`, having walked four hops inside the one request. Hop one moves the lot to `RECALLED`. Hop two, caused by hop one, moves every sub-lot of that lot standing in `IN_TRANSIT` or `ARRIVED` to `RETURNED`. Hop three, caused by hop two, reaches the boxes: every delivery carried by one of those sub-lots is visited in `created_at` ascending order, and for each the credit issued is its `invoice_total_cents` less whatever `credit_cents` it has already received. A delivery that had already reached `DELIVERED` or `PICKED_UP` has its money axis moved to `CREDITED`, whether it was standing in `CHARGED` or in `PARTIALLY_CREDITED`; a delivery that had not yet reached the member instead has its `fulfillment_status` moved to `VOIDED` with `fill_status` `VOID`. Hop four, caused by hop three, records the damage: each credit is added to the delivery's `credit_cents` and to the owning member's `credit_balance_cents`, and the notice records `impacted_sub_lot_count` (the number of sub-lots walked), `impacted_delivery_count` (the number of deliveries reached through them), `impacted_member_count` (the number of *distinct* members behind those deliveries) and `total_credit_cents` (the sum of the credits issued during the walk). A lot split into two sub-lots carrying three deliveries for three distinct members with invoice totals of 6250, 4500 and 4500 and no prior credits produces counts of 2, 3 and 3 and a total credit of 15250 cents. Deliveries whose money axis already stood in `REFUNDED` or `FORFEITED` are visited by the walk and receive nothing.

Remediation closes a recall out. It requires the notice to be `SCOPED` and no sub-lot of the recalled lot to remain `IN_TRANSIT`, records that every impacted member has been notified, and moves the notice `SCOPED → REMEDIATED` and then `REMEDIATED → CLOSED` inside the same request; a closed notice accepts no further mutation. Notices are readable individually and listable with `landing_lot_id` and `season_id` filters.

**Closing the season** happens in two administrative steps. The first moves it `OPEN → LANDINGS_CLOSED`: from that point landings for the season are refused with `ERR_SEASON_NOT_OPEN`, and every share transfer of the season still standing in `REQUESTED` expires.

The second is the true-up, which requires the season to be `LANDINGS_CLOSED` and its `true_up_run_at` to still be null, and which walks `LANDINGS_CLOSED → TRUE_UP → CLOSED` inside one request — `TRUE_UP` is an intra-request waypoint, and the response of the true-up call already shows the season `CLOSED`. Every subscription of the season whose `lifecycle_status` is `ACTIVE`, `PAUSED` or `PAST_DUE` is processed, in `created_at` ascending order; cancelled and transferred subscriptions are not touched.

For each such subscription, banked skip credit is spent first. The outstanding balance is `commitment_cents` less `paid_cents`, floored at zero — a subscription that has paid at or beyond its commitment has an outstanding balance of 0. The applied credit is the smaller of `skip_credit_balance_cents` and that outstanding balance; it is added to `paid_cents`. Whatever skip credit was not applied is written to `expired_credit_cents` and lost, and `skip_credit_balance_cents` is zeroed. A prepaid subscription has nothing outstanding, so a banked 4140 cents applies nothing and expires all of it; an installment subscription still owing 9000 applies the whole of a banked 4500 and expires nothing.

Then the counts are reconciled. `undelivered_count` is `promised_delivery_count` less `delivered_count`, less `credited_count`, less `member_fault_count`, less `skip_credited_count` — the promised boxes that reached season close in no other bucket at all. The base amount owed back to the member is the sum of `credited_count` and `undelivered_count`, multiplied by that subscription's own `price_per_delivery_cents`, which on a prepaid subscription is the discounted unit price. `TRUE_UP_MIN_REFUND_CENTS` is 5000 cents, and it decides which of two variants runs. A `PREPAID_FULL_SEASON` subscription whose base owed has reached or passed 5000 cents takes the refund variant: `true_up_status` becomes `REFUNDED`, `true_up_amount_cents` is the base owed, `rollover_credit_cents` stays 0, and the money axis moves `PREPAID_SETTLED → REFUND_PENDING` and then to `SETTLED` as the refund is disbursed in the same request. Everything else takes the credit variant — an `INSTALLMENT` subscription always, and a prepaid subscription whose base owed is strictly below 5000 cents. On the credit variant no cash moves at all: `true_up_status` becomes `CREDITED`, `true_up_amount_cents` is the base owed, and the same figure is written to `rollover_credit_cents` for the member to carry into next season, which is what a renewing subscription picks up when it names a `renewed_from_subscription_id`. A prepaid subscription at 4140 cents a delivery with one credited box owes 4140 and therefore takes the credit branch; with two credited boxes it owes 8280 and takes the refund branch. An installment subscription at 4500 a delivery with one credited box always takes the credit branch, rolling 4500 forward. A subscription that a discard cascade had already flagged as `SHORTFALL_FLAGGED` lands in `REFUNDED` or `CREDITED` by exactly the same test.

Once every processed subscription carries a `true_up_status` of `REFUNDED` or `CREDITED`, each moves its `lifecycle_status` to `COMPLETED` and its `billing_status` to `SETTLED` — including a subscription that was standing in `PAUSED` or `PAST_DUE` when the sweep began. The season's `true_up_run_at` is stamped from the logical clock and its `status` becomes `CLOSED`. From that moment no further mutation is accepted on any resource belonging to that season, and an attempt is refused with `ERR_SEASON_CLOSED` — with only the four carve-outs listed in §2.5 remaining open.
