# Hydrogen Fueling Ops API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Hydrogen fuel operators sit at the junction of three businesses that rarely share a single system of record. Upstream, electrolysers and reformers produce hydrogen whose carbon intensity determines whether a kilogram is worth a low-carbon-fuel credit or nothing at all. In the middle, tube trailers and liquid tankers move that hydrogen across distances long enough that the losses in transit — a residual heel left in the tubes, or boil-off from a cryogenic tanker — are material to the mass balance. Downstream, a fueling station must dispense fuel-cell-grade hydrogen into vehicle tanks under a fill protocol that ties precool temperature to ambient conditions, while a funding authority audits whether the station was actually available to customers for the fraction of the reporting period the grant agreement promised.

This document specifies the Hydrogen Fueling Ops API, the operational backbone that unifies those three businesses behind one contract. The system tracks production lots from the moment a pathway consumes electricity or natural gas through purity testing and carbon-intensity certification, follows each lot onto a trailer and into a station's pressure-vessel cascade, meters every fill against a temperature- and pressure-corrected mass, and closes the loop by converting settled dispensing volume into low-carbon fuel credits and by reporting station uptime against contractual floors. Safety is not a side channel in this design: leak detection, ventilation, compressor availability and permitted setback distances all feed a single derived station posture that gates dispensing, offload and inspection operations, and every safety event doubles as the raw material for uptime accounting.

Two properties of the domain drive most of the complexity below. First, almost every business-relevant number on a station is **derived** rather than stored — on-site inventory is the replay of an append-only ledger, deliverable mass is a function of live vessel pressures, and posture is a constraint propagation over open safety events and vessel inspection status. Because a single write can therefore ripple through four or five entities, the specification is explicit about cascade order. Second, the domain is adversarial in the auditing sense: the same operator must not both test a lot and release it, the same auditor must not both submit a claim and certify it, and a settled meter reading must be reversible without leaving the inventory ledger inconsistent. Segregation of duties and exact compensating reversals are therefore first-class requirements, not conveniences.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint — reads included — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting person, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role they are acting in. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` does not name one of the roles below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If the headers are well formed but the named role is not among those authorised for the target endpoint, the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403). These two checks run before any other validation in the system: authentication first, then endpoint authorisation.

Eight roles exist, and the access model is one of **segregation of duties** — no role is a superset of any other, and three specific operation pairs additionally forbid the same `X-User-ID` from performing both halves.

- **`PlatformAdmin`** configures the estate. Funding agreements, stations and the logical clock are its exclusive province; it shares pressure-vessel commissioning and responder-certification recording with `SafetyOfficer`, and production-asset and power-contract registration with `ProductionEngineer`. It cannot dispense, test purity, release lots or adjudicate claims.
- **`StationOperator`** is the station-side operator. It exclusively runs dispensing sessions (opening, fuelling, completing, aborting) and station-side delivery handling (offload and refusal); it shares meter settlement with `ComplianceAdmin`, safety-event opening with `SafetyOfficer`, and uptime-report drafting with `ComplianceAdmin`.
- **`ProductionEngineer`** owns production. Lot creation is exclusive to it; asset and power-contract registration are shared with `PlatformAdmin`. It cannot test purity, certify carbon intensity or dispense.
- **`QualityAnalyst`** performs fuel-cell-grade purity testing exclusively, and shares lot release with `ComplianceAdmin`.
- **`SafetyOfficer`** owns pressure-vessel inspections and the safety-event lifecycle — acknowledgement and clearing are exclusive to it — and shares vessel commissioning, responder-certification recording and safety-event opening as described above.
- **`ComplianceAdmin`** owns carbon-intensity certification of lots, incentive-claim submission and resubmission, meter voids, and uptime-report finalisation and amendment. It shares lot release with `QualityAnalyst`, meter settlement with `StationOperator`, and uptime-report drafting with `StationOperator`.
- **`LogisticsDispatcher`** books, dispatches and rebooks deliveries exclusively. It may not offload or refuse a load at the station.
- **`ProgramAuditor`** is the independent adjudicator of incentive claims: requesting revisions, certifying and issuing credits are exclusive to it. It never submits a claim.

Reads are shared broadly: every one of the eight roles holds read access to some part of the estate, and the per-endpoint authorised-role lists in §4 are authoritative for reads exactly as they are for writes.

Identity binding differs by role, and this is what ties `X-User-ID` to resource ownership. When `X-User-Role` is `StationOperator`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Station`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Station` compare the resource's station foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a `StationOperator` acting on a delivery offload, a delivery refusal, or any dispensing-session operation (creation, fuelling, completion or abort) must present an `X-User-ID` equal to the `station_id` of the targeted resource; a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STATION_OWNERSHIP_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403), which is evaluated after endpoint authorisation and before any existence check on path identifiers.

When `X-User-Role` is any of `PlatformAdmin`, `ProductionEngineer`, `QualityAnalyst`, `SafetyOfficer`, `ComplianceAdmin`, `LogisticsDispatcher` or `ProgramAuditor`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a workflow demands an audit trail — as `performed_by_user_id` on a purity test, `released_by_user_id` on a production lot, `opened_by_user_id` and `cleared_by_user_id` on a safety event, and `submitted_by_user_id` and `certified_by_user_id` on an incentive claim — but never compares it to any entity's `id`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, may be no smaller than 1, and may be no larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` outside the inclusive range from 1 to 100, or a negative `offset`, is a schema failure and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. The one documented exception is the purity-test listing for a lot, which is ordered newest-first — by `sampled_at` descending, with `created_at` descending as the tie-break — because a reader of that collection almost always wants the test that currently governs the lot's quality verdict.

For list responses the `meta` object carries `limit` and `offset` as applied, `total_count` as the number of rows matching the filter before pagination, and `has_more` as a boolean indicating whether rows remain beyond the current page.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Version Semantics

Every resource carries an `id`: a server-minted UUID with no type prefix, never supplied by the client. Every resource also carries a `created_at` timestamp, an ISO 8601 UTC instant rendered with three fractional digits.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at` — same format as `created_at`, refreshed on every mutation — and `version`, an integer that begins at 1 on creation and increments by one on every mutation of the row. Two entity types are exceptions because they are append-only records that are never updated after creation: a `PurityTest` and an `InventoryLedgerEntry` each carry only `id` and `created_at`. For both of these, `updated_at` and `version` are absent from the resource entirely.

The `version` field is not merely informational. It is the coordination token for the optimistic concurrency rules in §2.7, and several writes that appear to touch only a child resource deliberately increment a parent's `version` so that any client holding a stale snapshot of that parent is forced to re-read. §3 states, workflow by workflow, exactly which writes bump which parent.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

The server does not read wall-clock time for any business decision. Instead it maintains a **logical clock** that defaults to `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` and is moved only by an admin-only test-driving capability that accepts a single field, `to_iso8601`, carrying the target instant. That capability's response is not a resource envelope but a small object reporting `current_utc` (the newly set instant), `vessels_recomputed` (how many pressure vessels had their inspection status re-derived) and `stations_recomputed` (how many stations had their posture re-derived). Both counts tally the rows the re-derivation covered, not the rows whose derived value happened to change: `vessels_recomputed` is the number of pressure vessels in the whole system that are not condemned, and `stations_recomputed` is the number of stations in the whole system.

Every date-sensitive computation in the system reads the logical clock: pressure-vessel inspection status, responder-certification currency, delivery-window arrival checks, transit-hour computation, the calendar month that scopes month-to-date metering rollups, and the timestamps recorded on ledger entries and safety events. Moving the clock is therefore a mutation with real consequences — it re-derives `inspection_status` for every vessel that is not condemned, re-derives `is_current` for every responder certification, and re-derives `safety_posture` for every station, which in turn refreshes the trained-responder count that gates dispensing and offload.

### 2.6. Numeric Precision, Rounding and Unit Conventions

Rounding in this system is always **round half up** — a value exactly at the midpoint rounds away from zero — and it is applied at the precision each quantity declares. Masses in kilograms and percentages derived from masses or minutes are carried to three decimal places. Pressures in bar are carried to two decimal places. Carbon intensities in grams CO2e per megajoule are carried to two decimal places. Penalties are integer cents, rounded to a whole number of cents before any clamping is applied. Precool and ambient temperatures are carried to one decimal place. The capture-rate fraction on a production asset is carried to four decimal places.

Two rounding subtleties are load-bearing and are called out where they apply, because getting either backwards produces answers that differ from the specification by a milligram or more. The density correction factor applied to a meter reading is **not** rounded before it multiplies the reading; only the product is rounded. And a month-to-date corrected-mass rollup sums the already-rounded per-session corrected masses rather than rounding the sum of unrounded products — summing first and rounding once is explicitly not the required behaviour.

The unit conventions worth stating once: mass is kilograms except for credits, which are metric tonnes of CO2e; vessel volume is litres; pressure is bar; energy is kilowatt-hours for electricity, MMBtu for natural-gas feedstock, and megajoules for the energy content of dispensed hydrogen; carbon intensity of a power contract is expressed per megawatt-hour while carbon intensity of a lot is expressed per megajoule.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Station and Agreement Snapshots

Two write operations reserve or draft against a parent whose derived state the client has necessarily just read, and both are guarded by optimistic concurrency control.

Opening a dispensing session — the `POST /dispense-sessions` endpoint — requires the non-optional field `expected_station_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Station`. If that value does not equal the station's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_STATION_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The rationale is that a session create reserves mass against a snapshot of available inventory, posture and already-reserved mass; if another writer has advanced the station's version since the client last read it, that snapshot is obsolete and reserving against it would over-commit inventory the operator never saw.

Drafting an uptime report — the `POST /uptime-reports` endpoint — requires the non-optional field `expected_funding_agreement_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `FundingAgreement`. If that value does not equal the agreement's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_AGREEMENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A report draft supersedes the client's view of the holdback balance, so the server refuses to draft against an out-of-date snapshot of the agreement.

No other endpoint accepts a version field. Endpoints not named in the two paragraphs above take no `expected_*_version` input at all, and supplying one is not part of their contract.

### 2.8. Station Safety Posture as a Universal Gate

Every station carries a derived `safety_posture` that takes exactly one of three values — `OPEN`, `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN` — and that value gates operations across the whole station. Posture is recomputed after every mutation that touches its inputs: opening, acknowledging or clearing a safety event; recording a vessel inspection; accepting a delivery offload; completing a fill; and moving the logical clock.

The derivation is a top-down band selection in which the **first** matching band wins. The station is `FROZEN` when any of these hold: `leak_detection_status` is `ALARM`, or `ventilation_status` is `FAULT`, or `setback_compliant` is false, or at least one `SafetyEvent` on the station has severity `CRITICAL` and status either `OPEN` or `ACKNOWLEDGED`. Otherwise the station is `RESTRICTED` when any of these hold: `compressor_status` is `OFFLINE`, or `high_bank_deliverable_kg` is strictly less than 10.000 kg, or at least one `PressureVessel` on the station has `inspection_status` of `OVERDUE` or `CONDEMNED`, or at least one `SafetyEvent` on the station has severity `MAJOR` and status either `OPEN` or `ACKNOWLEDGED`. When neither band matches, the posture is `OPEN`. Only safety events whose status is `OPEN` or `ACKNOWLEDGED` participate; `CLEARED` events are excluded from the input set. Pressure vessels participate in every inspection status. The high-bank comparison is made at three decimal places.

A `FROZEN` posture is an absolute bar. Five operations refuse to proceed against a frozen station and return `ERR_STATION_FROZEN` (HTTP 409): opening a dispensing session, starting fuelling, completing a fill, offloading a delivery, and recording a pressure-vessel inspection. A `RESTRICTED` posture is narrower — it does not abort work in flight and does not block offload or inspection — but it does refuse any session whose `target_pressure_class` is `H70`, both at session creation and again at the fuelling step, with `ERR_POSTURE_RESTRICTS_H70` (HTTP 422); and it withholds compressor makeup, since a restricted posture caused by an offline compressor makes makeup physically impossible.

### 2.9. The Append-Only Inventory Ledger

On-site hydrogen inventory is never stored as a mutable number. Each station owns an append-only ledger whose entries are the sole record of mass movement, and `onsite_inventory_kg` on the station is the `resulting_onsite_kg` of the latest entry in replay order — 0.000 when the ledger is empty. Replay order is pinned: entries are ordered by `occurred_at` ascending, then by `sequence_no` ascending, where `sequence_no` is assigned per station in append order, beginning at 1 and incrementing by one for each subsequent entry, so that the secondary key fully disambiguates entries sharing a single logical-clock instant. Replaying the ledger means starting a running total at 0.000 and, for each entry in that order, adding the entry's `delta_kg` to the running total, rounding the result half-up to three decimal places, and recording that rounded total as the entry's `resulting_onsite_kg`. The final running total is the station's on-site inventory, and it must equal the sum of every `delta_kg` in the ledger rounded half-up to three decimal places.

No ledger entry is ever mutated or deleted. A movement that must be undone is undone by appending its exact additive inverse, which is why the void of a metered settlement appends a reversal rather than editing the correction. Each entry records a `reason` drawn from `OPENING_BALANCE`, `DELIVERY_RECEIPT`, `HEEL_RETURNED`, `BOILOFF_LOSS`, `DISPENSE_DRAW`, `METER_CORRECTION`, `METER_VOID_REVERSAL` and `INSPECTION_VENT`, and a polymorphic pointer to whatever caused it: `source_ref_type` names the entity kind, one of `PRESSURE_VESSEL`, `DELIVERY` or `DISPENSE_SESSION`, and `source_ref_id` holds that entity's identifier. Because an opening balance is seeded by commissioning rather than by a movement of an existing thing, `source_ref_id` is null for an `OPENING_BALANCE` entry. The ledger is readable per station and may be filtered by `reason`.

### 2.10. Path Resolution and Validation Ordering

Nested paths are resolved left to right. When a request addresses a child through its parent, the parent identifier is validated first, and a failure at that position produces the parent's not-found error rather than the child's — a request naming a station that does not exist yields `ERR_STATION_NOT_FOUND` even if the child identifier further right is also unknown. Each entity type has its own 404: `ERR_STATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FUNDING_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRESSURE_VESSEL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRODUCTION_ASSET_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRODUCTION_LOT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DELIVERY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DISPENSE_SESSION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SAFETY_EVENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INCENTIVE_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_UPTIME_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`.

The system evaluates checks in a fixed global order, and the first failure is the one reported. Authentication comes first, then endpoint role authorisation, then station-ownership binding for a `StationOperator`, then existence of every identifier in the request path, then schema validation of the body and query string, then existence of every identifier referenced inside the request body. Only after all of those does state-sensitive logic run: first the optimistic-concurrency version check, then the state-machine from-state check, then the frozen-posture gate, and then the domain-specific business rules in the order §3 describes for each workflow.

Schema validation covers required fields, correct types, valid enum members, numeric ranges and declared decimal precision, and pagination bounds; it also covers a set of conditional shape rules. A tube-trailer delivery must supply both `trailer_water_volume_l` and `trailer_final_pressure_bar`, and a liquid-tanker delivery must omit both. A lot produced on an electrolysis pathway must report `feedstock_consumed_mmbtu` of 0. An asset's `ccs_capture_rate` must be 0 unless its `pathway` is `SMR_CCS`. A lot's `carryover_kg` must be 0 when `predecessor_lot_id` is absent, and may never exceed `mass_kg`. Inclusive date ranges must have `period_end` no earlier than `period_start`, and booked windows must have `window_end` strictly later than `window_start`. Any of these failing yields `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

Body-referenced identifiers are checked as a group after schema validation: `funding_agreement_id`, `station_id`, `production_asset_id`, `production_lot_id`, `predecessor_lot_id`, `receiving_vessel_id`, `amended_from_report_id`, and each element of `production_lot_ids` must resolve to an existing row, failing with that entity's not-found error.

### 2.11. Segregation of Duties and Uniqueness of Codes

Three operation pairs forbid one person from performing both halves, and all three are enforced by comparing the acting `X-User-ID` against a user identifier the system recorded at the earlier step. A lot may not be released by anyone whose `X-User-ID` matches the `performed_by_user_id` of any purity test on that lot. A safety event may not be cleared by whoever opened it. An incentive claim may not be certified by whoever submitted it. All three violations report `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 409).

Human-facing codes are unique within their own tables. A station's `code` must be unique across all stations, and a collision is reported as `ERR_STATION_CODE_TAKEN` (HTTP 409). A funding agreement's `agreement_code`, a production asset's `asset_code` and a power contract's `contract_code` are each unique within their own table, and a collision on any of the three is reported as `ERR_CODE_TAKEN` (HTTP 409). A production lot's `lot_code` is unique across all lots, with its own code `ERR_LOT_CODE_TAKEN` (HTTP 409).

### 2.12. Cascading Recomputation Discipline

Because station-level figures are derived, a single write frequently propagates through several hops, and the specification treats those hops as part of the operation rather than as eventual consistency. Consistency is immediate: by the time a write returns, every derived field it touches has already been recomputed, and every state transition that its recomputation triggers has already been applied.

The general shape recurs throughout §3. A write mutates a row; that mutation changes one or more station aggregates; a changed aggregate may cross a threshold and move `safety_posture`; a posture move may force child rows into new states; and those forced child transitions may in turn change the aggregates that fed the posture. Whenever a station's derived fields are recomputed as part of a write, `updated_at` is refreshed and `version` is incremented by one on the affected rows, which is precisely what makes a client's earlier `expected_station_version` stale. The recomputation set is never left to inference: each workflow in §3 enumerates which aggregates are refreshed.

An entity's own state machine is also authoritative about which transitions exist. A request that asks a row to make a transition its current state does not declare — including repeating an already-applied action and acting on a terminal state — is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409).

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Estate Configuration: Funding Agreements, Stations and Vessel Banks

Everything in this system hangs off a funding agreement. A `PlatformAdmin` registers one by supplying the funder's own `agreement_code`, the `funder_name` of the authority, the contractual `min_uptime_pct` floor that stations under the agreement must meet in each reporting period, a `penalty_per_point_cents` rate assessed per whole percentage point of shortfall, an `initial_holdback_cents` pool from which those penalties are deducted, and the `uptime_method` that decides which of two uptime variants is contractually binding — either `NOZZLE_WEIGHTED` or `STATION_LEVEL`. The uptime floor is a percentage between 0 and 100 inclusive carried to three decimal places; both money fields are whole cents and may not be negative. At creation the agreement's live `holdback_balance_cents` equals `initial_holdback_cents`, because no penalty has yet been charged. Thereafter it is derived, never stored: it is the initial pool minus the sum of `penalty_cents` across every uptime report filed under the agreement whose status is `FINAL`, floored at zero. Reports sitting in `DRAFT` and reports superseded into `AMENDED` are excluded from that sum, which is exactly what makes superseding a report reverse its penalty. Agreements can be listed with optional `funder_name` and `uptime_method` filters.

A `PlatformAdmin` then creates each station under an agreement, naming the agreement through its `funding_agreement_id`, giving the station an operator `code` and a human-readable `name`, and declaring its physical configuration: `nozzle_count` (at least one dispensing nozzle), `offload_bay_count` (at least one bay available for concurrent delivery windows), `storage_capacity_kg` as the maximum permitted on-site inventory, `reserve_floor_kg` as the supply-alert floor, and `max_pressure_class` as the highest dispensing pressure class the equipment supports — either `H35` or `H70`. Capacity must be strictly positive and the reserve floor may not be negative; both are carried to three decimal places. An optional `setback_compliant` flag records whether the station currently satisfies its permitted setback distances and defaults to true.

A newly created station is not immediately usable, and the reason is instructive. Its three subsystem fields start clean — `ventilation_status` is `NORMAL`, `leak_detection_status` is `NORMAL` and `compressor_status` is `ONLINE` — and every inventory and metering aggregate starts at 0.000. But with no pressure vessels commissioned, `high_bank_deliverable_kg` is 0.000, which is below the 10.000 kg high-bank minimum, so posture is computed as `RESTRICTED` at birth. `supply_alert` is computed at the same moment. Stations may be listed with optional `funding_agreement_id` and `safety_posture` filters.

Pressure vessels are commissioned onto a station by a `SafetyOfficer` or a `PlatformAdmin`, one at a time, each declaring the `bank_tier` it serves — `HIGH`, `MEDIUM` or `LOW` — along with `water_volume_l`, the `mawp_bar` maximum allowable working pressure, the `min_residual_bar` pressure that must remain in the vessel after any draw, the `initial_pressure_bar` at commissioning, and the `next_inspection_due_date` on which its next periodic inspection falls due. Volume and maximum working pressure must be strictly positive; the residual floor may not be negative and must be strictly below the maximum working pressure; the commissioning pressure must be at least the residual floor and no more than the maximum working pressure. Volumes carry three decimals, pressures two, and the due date is a plain `YYYY-MM-DD` calendar date.

Commissioning seeds inventory. The vessel's `current_pressure_bar` starts at its `initial_pressure_bar`, and its `stored_mass_kg` is derived as the current pressure multiplied by the water volume multiplied by the mass constant of 0.00008 kilograms per litre per bar, rounded half-up to three decimal places. `inspection_count` starts at 0, `last_inspection_date` is null until the first inspection is recorded, and `inspection_status` is derived immediately. An `OPENING_BALANCE` ledger entry is appended whose `delta_kg` equals the vessel's stored mass, whose `source_ref_type` is `PRESSURE_VESSEL` and whose `source_ref_id` is null, exactly as §2.9 requires for an `OPENING_BALANCE` entry. That append forces recomputation of the station's `onsite_inventory_kg`, `available_kg`, `total_deliverable_kg`, `high_bank_deliverable_kg`, `projected_available_kg`, `supply_alert` and `safety_posture`, and increments the station's `version` by one. Once enough high-bank mass exists, that same recomputation is what lifts the station out of its birth restriction.

The deliverable aggregates deserve their own definition because they, not raw inventory, decide whether a fill can be served. `total_deliverable_kg` is the sum, taken over the station's pressure vessels whose `inspection_status` is `CURRENT` or `DUE`, of the greater of zero and the quantity formed by subtracting the vessel's `min_residual_bar` from its `current_pressure_bar` and multiplying that difference by the vessel's `water_volume_l` and by 0.00008 kilograms per litre per bar. `high_bank_deliverable_kg` is that same sum restricted to vessels whose `bank_tier` is `HIGH`. Vessels that are `OVERDUE` or `CONDEMNED` contribute to neither figure. Alongside them, `reserved_kg` is the sum of `planned_kg` across the station's dispensing sessions whose `fill_status` is `PRECOOLING` or `FUELING`; `available_kg` is `onsite_inventory_kg` minus `reserved_kg`; `inbound_planned_kg` is the sum of `planned_kg` across the station's deliveries whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `ARRIVED`; `projected_available_kg` is `available_kg` plus `inbound_planned_kg`; and `supply_alert` is true exactly when `projected_available_kg` is strictly less than the station's `reserve_floor_kg`, and false otherwise. A station's vessel roster can be listed with optional `bank_tier` and `inspection_status` filters.

Inspection status is a cyclic derivation against the logical-clock date, with a grace period of thirty days. A vessel that has been condemned stays `CONDEMNED` permanently — the state is terminal and sticky, and no later clock movement or inspection changes it. Otherwise, if today's logical date is strictly earlier than `next_inspection_due_date`, the vessel is `CURRENT`; if today's date is on or after the due date but no later than thirty days after it, the vessel is `DUE`; and if today's date is more than thirty days after the due date, the vessel is `OVERDUE`. The two boundaries are worth stating explicitly: a vessel whose due date is exactly today is `DUE`, not `CURRENT`, and a vessel exactly thirty days past due is still `DUE`, not `OVERDUE`. Moving the logical clock across the first boundary takes a vessel `CURRENT → DUE`; a `DUE` vessel is still deliverable and remains inside both deliverable sums, so nothing else changes. Moving the clock across the second boundary takes the vessel `DUE → OVERDUE`, drops it out of `total_deliverable_kg` and `high_bank_deliverable_kg`, and forces the station's posture to be recomputed, because an overdue vessel is a restricting condition; a station that was `OPEN` and holds at least one vessel newly gone overdue therefore moves `OPEN → RESTRICTED`, and its `version` is incremented by one.

A `SafetyOfficer` closes the cycle by recording an inspection against a vessel, supplying an `outcome` and, when the outcome is a pass, a `next_due_date`. A passing inspection is accepted while the vessel's `inspection_status` is `DUE` or `OVERDUE` and the station is not `FROZEN`; it sets `last_inspection_date` to the logical-clock date, replaces `next_inspection_due_date` with the supplied `next_due_date`, increments `inspection_count` by one, sets `inspection_status` back to `CURRENT`, and triggers a posture recomputation. From `DUE` that is the `DUE → CURRENT` leg of the cycle; from `OVERDUE` it is the `OVERDUE → CURRENT` leg, and it additionally re-enters the vessel into both deliverable sums and drops the overdue restriction from the station's posture. A condemning inspection is accepted from either `DUE` or `OVERDUE` — the `DUE → CONDEMNED` and `OVERDUE → CONDEMNED` legs are specified identically — and it sets `inspection_status` to `CONDEMNED`, zeroes `current_pressure_bar` to 0.00, and appends an `INSPECTION_VENT` ledger entry whose `delta_kg` is the negation of the vessel's stored mass at that moment, recording the vented contents. A condemning inspection leaves `inspection_count` unchanged: only a passing inspection ever advances that counter, so a vessel condemned on its first inspection still reports an `inspection_count` of 0. Either outcome recomputes the station's `onsite_inventory_kg`, `total_deliverable_kg`, `high_bank_deliverable_kg`, `projected_available_kg`, `supply_alert` and `safety_posture`, and increments the station's `version` by one. An inspection attempted at a frozen station is refused with `ERR_STATION_FROZEN`.

### 3.2. Production Pathways, Power Supply and the Carbon Intensity of a Lot

A `ProductionEngineer` or `PlatformAdmin` registers each hydrogen production asset with a unique `asset_code`, the `pathway` it runs — one of `PEM_ELECTROLYSIS`, `ALKALINE_ELECTROLYSIS`, `SMR` or `SMR_CCS` — its `nameplate_kg_per_day` output (strictly positive, three decimal places), the `operator_org` running it, and, where the pathway captures carbon, a `ccs_capture_rate`. The capture rate is a fraction between 0 and 1 inclusive carried to four decimal places, defaults to 0, and must be 0 for every pathway other than `SMR_CCS`; supplying a non-zero rate on an electrolysis or unabated-reforming asset is a schema failure.

Power supply is contracted against an asset rather than against the estate, because the grid an electrolyser draws from is what determines most of its carbon intensity. Each contract, attached by a `ProductionEngineer` or `PlatformAdmin`, carries a unique `contract_code`, the `grid_ci_gco2e_per_mwh` carbon intensity of the non-renewable balance of the supply (non-negative, two decimal places), an `annual_renewable_pct` annual-average renewable share and an `hourly_matched_renewable_pct` hour-by-hour matched renewable share (each a percentage between 0 and 100 inclusive carried to three decimal places), an optional `hourly_matching_enabled` flag defaulting to false, and the interval over which the contract governs: `effective_from` inclusive and `effective_to` exclusive, the latter strictly after the former. A colliding `contract_code` is rejected with `ERR_CODE_TAKEN`.

Contracts on one asset must not merely avoid overlapping — they must be separated by a changeover buffer of a full day, twenty-four hours. For every power contract already attached to the same asset, the new contract is refused when the new interval's `effective_from` is strictly earlier than the existing interval's `effective_to` advanced by twenty-four hours **and** the existing interval's `effective_from` is strictly earlier than the new interval's `effective_to` advanced by twenty-four hours. Both conditions must hold simultaneously for a conflict to exist. The practical consequence is that back-to-back contracts still conflict: an interval that begins the instant its predecessor ends is inside the buffer, and only a separation of at least twenty-four hours clears it. A conflict returns `ERR_POWER_CONTRACT_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). Contracts for one asset can be listed in their own collection.

Recording production is exclusive to `ProductionEngineer`. The payload names the asset through `production_asset_id`, supplies a unique `lot_code`, the `mass_kg` of hydrogen produced (strictly positive), the `energy_consumed_kwh` of electricity drawn (non-negative), the production window as `production_start_at` inclusive and `production_end_at` exclusive with the end strictly after the start, and optionally `feedstock_consumed_mmbtu` for the natural gas consumed, a `predecessor_lot_id`, and a `carryover_kg`. Masses and energies carry three decimal places. Feedstock defaults to 0 and must be 0 for both electrolysis pathways. Carryover defaults to 0, must be 0 when no `predecessor_lot_id` is supplied, may not exceed the lot's own `mass_kg`, and — this is the self-referential part of the model — the named predecessor must exist and must belong to the same `production_asset_id`. A duplicate `lot_code` is rejected with `ERR_LOT_CODE_TAKEN`. A fresh lot begins with `qa_status` of `PENDING`, `ci_status` of `PENDING`, `status` of `PRODUCED` and `accepted_delivered_kg` of 0.000; lots can be listed with optional `production_asset_id`, `predecessor_lot_id`, `qa_status` and `status` filters.

Two things happen at lot creation that the engineer does not control. First, the server selects the governing power contract and records it as `selected_power_contract_id` — an identity reference the server owns, never a request input. The selector is deterministic. Its candidate set is every power contract whose `production_asset_id` equals the lot's asset and whose interval covers the lot's `production_end_at`, meaning the contract's `effective_from` is at or before that instant and its `effective_to` is strictly after it. Candidates are ranked by `effective_from` descending — the most recently commenced contract wins — with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break, and the first candidate is selected. When the candidate set is empty, the lot cannot be recorded at all and the request fails with `ERR_NO_POWER_CONTRACT` (HTTP 422); the same check guards carbon-intensity certification later.

Second, the server stamps `ci_provisional_gco2e_per_mj`, the provisional carbon intensity, using the `ANNUAL_AVERAGE` variant unconditionally — regardless of whether the selected contract has hourly matching enabled. Certification later stamps `ci_certified_gco2e_per_mj`, which stays null until then, using whichever variant the contract selects, and records that choice in `ci_variant_applied`.

The carbon-intensity computation itself is shared by both stamps and works in four steps. Begin by determining the renewable share: under the `HOURLY_MATCHED` variant it is the contract's `hourly_matched_renewable_pct`; under `ANNUAL_AVERAGE` it is the contract's `annual_renewable_pct`. Next, compute the lot's own emissions, all in grams of CO2e. The **fresh mass** is the lot's `mass_kg` minus its `carryover_kg`. The **electricity term** is the `energy_consumed_kwh` multiplied by the contract's `grid_ci_gco2e_per_mwh` divided by one thousand, and that product multiplied by one minus the renewable share expressed as a fraction (that is, the renewable percentage divided by one hundred). The **feedstock term** is the `feedstock_consumed_mmbtu` multiplied by 61,260 grams of CO2e per MMBtu of natural gas, and that product multiplied by one minus the asset's `ccs_capture_rate`. The **compression term** is the fresh mass multiplied by a compression adder of 300 grams of CO2e per kilogram. The lot's own emissions are the sum of the electricity, feedstock and compression terms.

Blending across the predecessor link is the third step, and it exists because carried-over mass carries the predecessor's carbon, not this lot's. When the lot has no `predecessor_lot_id`, or when its `carryover_kg` is zero, the carryover emissions term is zero. Otherwise the carryover emissions term is the `carryover_kg` multiplied by the lower heating value of hydrogen, 120 megajoules per kilogram, multiplied by the predecessor's carbon intensity — and which of the predecessor's two figures is used depends on which figure is being computed: computing this lot's provisional intensity uses the predecessor's `ci_provisional_gco2e_per_mj`, while computing this lot's certified intensity uses the predecessor's `ci_certified_gco2e_per_mj`. Finally, the carbon intensity is the sum of the lot's own emissions and the carryover emissions term, divided by the lot's `mass_kg` multiplied by 120 megajoules per kilogram, rounded half-up to two decimal places.

Because certification of a lot reads the predecessor's certified figure, a lot whose `carryover_kg` is greater than zero cannot be certified until its predecessor has been certified. Attempting it returns `ERR_LOT_CI_NOT_CERTIFIED` (HTTP 422) — the same code the incentive-claim workflow uses when a claimed lot is uncertified.

The two variants differ in exactly one input, and the difference is directional. `ANNUAL_AVERAGE` applies when the selected contract's `hourly_matching_enabled` is false, and always when the provisional figure is being stamped; it uses `annual_renewable_pct`, crediting the annual-average renewable contract volume against every production hour. `HOURLY_MATCHED` applies when the selected contract's `hourly_matching_enabled` is true, and only when the certified figure is being stamped; it uses `hourly_matched_renewable_pct` instead, crediting only renewable generation matched hour by hour. On identical lot data, the hourly-matched variant yields a strictly higher carbon intensity whenever the hourly-matched renewable share is smaller than the annual-average share — an operator who over-contracts annual renewables but draws grid power at night is penalised precisely when the contract opts into hourly matching.

Certifying a lot's carbon intensity is exclusive to `ComplianceAdmin` and takes no payload. It is accepted only while the lot's `status` is `PRODUCED`, its `ci_status` is `PENDING`, and its `selected_power_contract_id` is not null. It sets `ci_variant_applied` from the selected contract's hourly-matching flag, computes and stores `ci_certified_gco2e_per_mj`, moves `ci_status` from `PENDING` to `CERTIFIED`, and increments the lot's `version` by one. The lot's own `status` is untouched: this is progress along one branch of a two-branch lifecycle, not a lifecycle transition, so the lot remains `PRODUCED` throughout.

### 3.3. Fuel-Cell-Grade Quality Assurance and the Two-Branch Lot Release

A production lot's lifecycle is a fork-join. Two branches advance independently — the quality-assurance branch, tracked by `qa_status`, and the carbon-intensity branch, tracked by `ci_status` — and the join is release. Progress along either branch leaves the lot's `status` at `PRODUCED`, so a lot may make several `PRODUCED → PRODUCED` moves before it ever leaves that state.

Purity testing is exclusive to `QualityAnalyst`. Each test records the `sampled_at` instant the sample was drawn and seven mandatory analyte readings — `total_hydrocarbons_ppm` on a C1 basis, `water_ppm`, `oxygen_ppm`, `nitrogen_ppm`, `carbon_monoxide_ppm`, `carbon_dioxide_ppm` and `total_sulfur_ppm` — each non-negative and carried to three decimal places. Two further analytes, `ammonia_ppm` and `helium_ppm`, are optional and are omitted entirely when the analyte was not measured; both default to null. The server records `performed_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`, which is the value the release step later refuses to match. A purity test is an immutable laboratory record: it carries `id` and `created_at` and nothing else in the way of common properties — no `updated_at`, no `version`.

Timing matters. A sample cannot predate the end of the window that produced the hydrogen, so the test's `sampled_at` must be at or after the lot's `production_end_at`; an earlier sample is rejected with `ERR_SAMPLE_BEFORE_PRODUCTION_END` (HTTP 422). That check runs after the state-machine check that the lot's `status` is still `PRODUCED` — a lot already rejected or already released cannot accept a new test, and the attempt surfaces as `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` rather than as a timing complaint.

The verdict is a threshold evaluation against the fuel-cell-grade specification, and the analytes are evaluated in a fixed order that also governs the ordering of the `failing_analytes` array: total hydrocarbons first, then water, oxygen, nitrogen, carbon monoxide, carbon dioxide, total sulfur, ammonia and finally helium. Only analytes whose value was supplied participate; an omitted optional analyte is excluded both from failure detection and from the margin computation. The limits are: total hydrocarbons no more than 2.000 ppm, water no more than 5.000 ppm, oxygen no more than 5.000 ppm, nitrogen no more than 100.000 ppm, carbon monoxide no more than 0.200 ppm, carbon dioxide no more than 2.000 ppm, total sulfur no more than 0.004 ppm, ammonia no more than 0.100 ppm and helium no more than 300.000 ppm. An analyte fails only when its measured value is **strictly greater** than its limit; a value exactly equal to the limit passes. `failing_analytes` holds the field names of the failing analytes in the fixed order above.

Three verdicts follow, and the precedence is explicit: failure outranks incompleteness. If `failing_analytes` is non-empty the `verdict` is `FAIL`, even when one of the optional analytes was never measured. Otherwise, if either `ammonia_ppm` or `helium_ppm` was omitted, the `verdict` is `INDETERMINATE` — nothing exceeded a limit, but the specification cannot be certified complete. Only when every measured analyte is within limits **and** both optional analytes were supplied is the `verdict` `PASS`. Alongside the verdict, `purity_margin_pct` reports the tightest remaining headroom: for each measured analyte, subtract the measured value from its limit, divide by that limit, multiply by one hundred, and round half-up to three decimal places; the reported margin is the smallest of those per-analyte figures.

Each verdict drives the quality branch differently, and all three branches carry equal weight. A `PASS` sets the lot's `qa_status` to `PASSED` and leaves `status` at `PRODUCED`. An `INDETERMINATE` leaves `qa_status` at `PENDING` and leaves `status` at `PRODUCED`, so the analyst may draw a fresh, complete sample and test again — the lot's quality verdict always follows the latest test, selected by `sampled_at` with `created_at` as tie-break, which is why the per-lot test listing is ordered newest-first and may be filtered by `verdict`. A `FAIL` sets `qa_status` to `FAILED` and takes the lot `PRODUCED → REJECTED`, a terminal state from which no release, certification or delivery is possible. In every case the created test row is stored with its computed `verdict`, `failing_analytes` and `purity_margin_pct`, and the lot's `version` is incremented by one.

Release is the join, performed by a `QualityAnalyst` or a `ComplianceAdmin` with no payload, and it enforces its preconditions in a fixed order. First the lot must still be in `PRODUCED`; a lot already `RELEASED`, `REJECTED` or `CONSUMED` fails with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Then both branches must be complete — `qa_status` must be `PASSED` and `ci_status` must be `CERTIFIED` — and a lot missing either is refused with `ERR_QA_NOT_PASSED` (HTTP 422); the single code covers both an untested-or-indeterminate quality branch and an uncertified carbon branch. Only then does segregation of duties apply: the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the `performed_by_user_id` of every purity test recorded against the lot, and a match yields `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 409). Ordering these checks this way means a lot that is genuinely not ready reports its readiness gap rather than a duties complaint. A successful release moves the lot `PRODUCED → RELEASED`, records `released_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`, and increments the lot's `version` by one.

The final leg of the lot lifecycle is driven by delivery rather than by anyone's request. A lot tracks `accepted_delivered_kg`, the sum of `net_receipt_kg` over every delivery of that lot whose `status` is `ACCEPTED`. When an offload lifts that figure to at least ninety percent of the lot's `mass_kg` — the consumption fraction is 0.900, applied at three decimal places — the lot moves `RELEASED → CONSUMED`, which is terminal, and no further delivery may reference it. A lot below that threshold stays `RELEASED` and remains available for further deliveries.

### 3.4. Delivery Logistics: Booking, Arrival, Offload and Refusal

Deliveries are booked by a `LogisticsDispatcher` against an offload-bay window. The booking names the receiving station through `station_id`, the lot being moved through `production_lot_id`, and the vessel the offload will be routed into through `receiving_vessel_id`; it declares the `mode` of transport, either `TUBE_TRAILER` or `LIQUID_TANKER`; it states the `planned_kg` gross mass loaded (strictly positive, three decimal places); and it books the bay window as `window_start` inclusive and `window_end` exclusive, the latter strictly after the former. It also records `departed_at`, the instant the load left the production site, which must be at or before `window_start`. For a tube trailer the booking must additionally supply `trailer_water_volume_l` (strictly positive) and `trailer_final_pressure_bar` (non-negative, two decimal places); for a liquid tanker both fields must be omitted. A new delivery is created with `status` of `SCHEDULED` and `reschedule_count` of 0, and the write recomputes the station's `inbound_planned_kg`, `projected_available_kg` and `supply_alert` and increments the station's `version` by one. Deliveries can be listed with optional `station_id`, `production_lot_id`, `receiving_vessel_id`, `status` and `mode` filters.

Booking enforces three domain rules beyond schema. The receiving vessel must belong to the receiving station: a `receiving_vessel_id` naming a vessel whose `station_id` differs from the delivery's `station_id` is a cross-aggregate ownership breach and is refused with `ERR_AGREEMENT_MISMATCH` (HTTP 409). The receiving vessel must be fit to accept hydrogen, meaning its `inspection_status` is `CURRENT` or `DUE`; an `OVERDUE` or `CONDEMNED` vessel is refused with `ERR_VESSEL_INSPECTION_OVERDUE` (HTTP 422). And the station must have a free bay for the requested window. That last check is an aggregate-capacity test rather than a pairwise one: count the station's other deliveries whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `ARRIVED` and whose half-open window from `window_start` inclusive to `window_end` exclusive intersects the requested window; the booking is accepted only while that count is strictly less than the station's `offload_bay_count`. Windows that merely touch — where an existing window's `window_end` exactly equals the new window's `window_start` — do not intersect and do not consume a bay. A station whose bays are all taken for an intersecting window refuses the booking with `ERR_OFFLOAD_BAY_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409).

Recording arrival is also the dispatcher's job, takes no payload, and is accepted only while the delivery is `SCHEDULED` and the logical-clock instant lies inside the half-open booked window — at or after `window_start` and strictly before `window_end`. An arrival recorded outside that window is refused with `ERR_ARRIVAL_OUTSIDE_WINDOW` (HTTP 422). On success the delivery moves `SCHEDULED → ARRIVED`, `arrived_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and the transit and loss terms are computed. `transit_hours` is the elapsed time from `departed_at` to `arrived_at` expressed in hours and rounded half-up to three decimal places; it is null until arrival. An `ARRIVED` delivery is still counted inside the station's `inbound_planned_kg`, because the mass has reached the site but not yet the vessel.

The loss terms are where the two transport modes diverge, and they are mirror images of one another. Under `TUBE_TRAILER`, the loss is the residual **heel** left in the tubes: `heel_kg` is the `trailer_final_pressure_bar` multiplied by the `trailer_water_volume_l` and by 0.00008 kilograms per litre per bar, rounded half-up to three decimal places, and `boiloff_kg` is 0.000. This loss is a function of trailer geometry and final pressure alone and is completely independent of how long the trailer was on the road. Under `LIQUID_TANKER`, the loss is **boil-off** accumulating linearly with transit time: `heel_kg` is 0.000, and `boiloff_kg` is the `planned_kg` multiplied by a daily boil-off rate of 0.003, multiplied by `transit_hours`, divided by twenty-four, and rounded half-up to three decimal places. This loss is completely independent of trailer geometry. In both modes, `net_receipt_kg` — the mass that will actually be credited to station inventory — is the `planned_kg` minus `heel_kg` minus `boiloff_kg`, rounded half-up to three decimal places.

Offloading is exclusive to the receiving station's own `StationOperator` and takes no payload, but it is the most heavily guarded write in the system, and its checks run in a fixed order. The delivery must be `ARRIVED`. The station must not be `FROZEN`, or the attempt fails with `ERR_STATION_FROZEN`. The station must have at least two current emergency-response certifications on file, or the attempt fails with `ERR_RESPONDER_TRAINING_LAPSED` (HTTP 422). The receiving vessel's `inspection_status` must be `CURRENT` or `DUE`, or `ERR_VESSEL_INSPECTION_OVERDUE` applies — the same rule enforced at booking, re-checked at offload because the logical clock may have moved in between. The delivery's lot must have reached `RELEASED`; a lot still `PRODUCED`, already `REJECTED`, or already `CONSUMED` blocks the receipt with `ERR_LOT_NOT_RELEASED` (HTTP 422). Then two physical limits: the receiving vessel's `current_pressure_bar` plus `net_receipt_kg` divided by the product of the vessel's `water_volume_l` and 0.00008 must be no greater than the vessel's `mawp_bar`, or `ERR_VESSEL_PRESSURE_LIMIT` (HTTP 422) applies; and the station's `onsite_inventory_kg` plus `net_receipt_kg` must be no greater than its `storage_capacity_kg`, or `ERR_STORAGE_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422) applies.

A successful offload cascades in four hops, and every hop is part of the same write. First, the delivery moves `ARRIVED → ACCEPTED` and `accepted_at` is stamped from the logical clock. Second, because the delivery was accepted, the receiving vessel's `current_pressure_bar` increases by `net_receipt_kg` divided by the product of its `water_volume_l` and 0.00008, and its `stored_mass_kg` is recomputed from the new pressure; at the same time two ledger entries are appended — a `DELIVERY_RECEIPT` entry whose `delta_kg` is the positive `net_receipt_kg` with `source_ref_type` of `DELIVERY`, and immediately after it a second entry whose `delta_kg` is 0.000 and whose `reason` is `HEEL_RETURNED` for a tube trailer or `BOILOFF_LOSS` for a liquid tanker, recording the loss term for audit without moving inventory. Third, because the ledger and the vessel pressure changed, the station's `onsite_inventory_kg`, `available_kg`, `total_deliverable_kg`, `high_bank_deliverable_kg`, `inbound_planned_kg`, `projected_available_kg`, `supply_alert` and `safety_posture` are all recomputed — and a station that was `RESTRICTED` purely because its high bank had fallen below 10.000 kg can move `RESTRICTED → OPEN` on the strength of this receipt. Fourth, because the lot's `accepted_delivered_kg` has risen, the lot may make its `RELEASED → CONSUMED` transition as described above. The station's `version` is incremented by one.

Refusal is also exclusive to the station's own operator, is accepted only from `ARRIVED`, and requires a `reject_reason` drawn from `PURITY_FAIL`, `LOT_NOT_RELEASED`, `DOCUMENTATION` and `SEAL_BROKEN`. The reason chooses between two structurally different outcomes and both are specified in full. A `PURITY_FAIL` refusal takes the delivery `ARRIVED → QUARANTINED`, which is terminal and not reschedulable, and recomputes the station's `inbound_planned_kg`, `projected_available_kg` and `supply_alert`. Any other reason takes the delivery `ARRIVED → REJECTED`, which is reschedulable, and cascades likewise: the reason is recorded; because the delivery has left the set of `SCHEDULED` and `ARRIVED` rows, the station's `inbound_planned_kg` decreases by the delivery's `planned_kg`; because inbound planned mass decreased, `projected_available_kg` decreases by the same amount; and if that fall takes projected availability below the station's `reserve_floor_kg`, `supply_alert` flips from false to true. Under either outcome no ledger entry is appended, because nothing was received, and the station's `version` is incremented by one. `reject_reason` is null unless the delivery's `status` is `REJECTED` or `QUARANTINED`.

Rebooking a refused load belongs to the dispatcher, is accepted only from `REJECTED`, and supplies a fresh `window_start` and `window_end`. Two guards apply: the delivery's `reschedule_count` must be strictly less than the reschedule ceiling of two, so a delivery that has already been rebooked twice is refused with `ERR_RESCHEDULE_LIMIT_REACHED` (HTTP 409); and the new window must not exhaust the station's `offload_bay_count` under exactly the aggregate-capacity rule described for booking, failing with `ERR_OFFLOAD_BAY_CONFLICT`. On success the delivery moves `REJECTED → SCHEDULED`: the window fields are replaced with the supplied values, `reschedule_count` is incremented by one, and `arrived_at`, `transit_hours`, `boiloff_kg`, `heel_kg`, `net_receipt_kg` and `reject_reason` are all reset to null so that the next arrival recomputes them from scratch. Because the delivery's `planned_kg` re-enters the inbound set, the station's `inbound_planned_kg`, `projected_available_kg` and `supply_alert` are recomputed and the station's `version` is incremented by one.

### 3.5. Dispensing: Reservation, Fill-Protocol Compliance and Cascade Allocation

A dispensing session carries two independent state dimensions on the same row. `fill_status` tracks the physical fill and runs `PRECOOLING → FUELING → COMPLETED`, with `ABORTED` reachable from either of the first two; `COMPLETED` and `ABORTED` are the terminal values of that axis. `billing_status` tracks settlement and runs `PENDING → METERED`, with `VOIDED` reachable from either. The two axes advance independently, and §3.6 covers the billing axis; this subsection covers the fill.

Opening a session is exclusive to the station's own `StationOperator`. The payload names the station through `station_id`, declares the `vehicle_class` being fuelled — `LIGHT_DUTY`, `TRANSIT_BUS`, `HEAVY_TRUCK` or `FORKLIFT` — the `target_pressure_class` requested (`H35` or `H70`), the `fill_protocol` family governing precool requirements (`SAE_J2601_TABLE` or `MC_FORMULA`), the `planned_kg` mass the session reserves against inventory (strictly positive, three decimal places), the `ambient_temp_c` at fill start (between −40 and 55 degrees Celsius inclusive, one decimal place), and the `expected_station_version` described in §2.7.

Session creation is the most heavily gated create in the system, and the order of its checks is deliberate so that the most fundamental obstacle is the one reported. Station ownership is verified first, as §2.1 describes. Then the optimistic-concurrency check on `expected_station_version` runs, so a client working from a stale snapshot is told to re-read before any business rule is consulted. Then the frozen gate: a `FROZEN` station accepts no new session, and the attempt returns `ERR_STATION_FROZEN`. Then the restricted gate on high-pressure fills: a `RESTRICTED` station accepts no session whose `target_pressure_class` is `H70`, returning `ERR_POSTURE_RESTRICTS_H70`. Then emergency-response readiness: the station must hold at least two current `EMERGENCY_RESPONSE` certifications, or `ERR_RESPONDER_TRAINING_LAPSED` applies — the same readiness floor that guards offload. Then nozzle availability: the count of the station's sessions whose `fill_status` is `PRECOOLING` or `FUELING` must be strictly less than the station's `nozzle_count`, and a station whose every nozzle is occupied refuses with `ERR_NO_NOZZLE_AVAILABLE` (HTTP 409). Then equipment capability: an `H70` request at a station whose `max_pressure_class` is `H35` is refused with `ERR_PRESSURE_CLASS_UNSUPPORTED` (HTTP 422), a permanent configuration limit distinct from the temporary posture restriction above. Finally inventory: `planned_kg` must be no greater than the station's `available_kg` at the moment of creation, and an over-reservation is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_AVAILABLE_INVENTORY` (HTTP 422).

On success the session is created with `fill_status` of `PRECOOLING`, `billing_status` of `PENDING`, and `started_at` stamped from the logical clock — the instant that decides which calendar month the session's metering rolls up into. `required_precool_c` is stamped at the same moment. Because the reservation now counts, the station's `reserved_kg`, `available_kg`, `projected_available_kg` and `supply_alert` are recomputed and the station's `version` is incremented by one, which is exactly what invalidates any other client's `expected_station_version`. Sessions can be listed per station with optional `fill_status` and `billing_status` filters.

The precool ceiling is a table lookup with a protocol adjustment. Ambient temperature selects one of three bands, and the bands are half-open at the top: an `ambient_temp_c` strictly greater than 30.0 degrees Celsius is the hot band; otherwise, an `ambient_temp_c` strictly greater than 10.0 degrees is the mild band; otherwise it is the cold band. Note the boundary — an ambient of exactly 30.0 degrees lands in the mild band, not the hot band, and an ambient of exactly 10.0 degrees lands in the cold band. For an `H70` fill the table value is −40.0 degrees in the hot band, −33.0 in the mild band and −26.0 in the cold band. For an `H35` fill it is −33.0 degrees in the hot band, −26.0 in the mild band and −17.0 in the cold band. Under the `SAE_J2601_TABLE` protocol the table value stands as `required_precool_c` unmodified. Under the `MC_FORMULA` protocol the requirement is relaxed by 5.0 degrees — the relief is added to the table value, so a warmer dispenser satisfies it. All precool figures carry one decimal place.

Starting the fill belongs to the same station operator and supplies a single field, `precool_temp_achieved_c`, the dispenser precool temperature actually reached (between −40 and 25 degrees Celsius inclusive, one decimal place). The transition is accepted only while `fill_status` is `PRECOOLING`; the station must not be `FROZEN`, or `ERR_STATION_FROZEN` applies; a `RESTRICTED` posture again blocks an `H70` session with `ERR_POSTURE_RESTRICTS_H70`, re-checked here because posture may have degraded during precooling; and fill-protocol compliance requires that `precool_temp_achieved_c` be no warmer than `required_precool_c`, meaning numerically less than or equal to it. Equality complies. A dispenser that failed to reach the ceiling is refused with `ERR_FILL_PROTOCOL_VIOLATION` (HTTP 422). On success the achieved temperature is stored, `fill_status` moves `PRECOOLING → FUELING`, `billing_status` remains `PENDING`, and the session's `planned_kg` stays inside the station's `reserved_kg`.

Completing the fill supplies three readings: `metered_kg`, the mass indicated by the dispenser meter (strictly positive, three decimal places); `avg_dispense_pressure_bar`, the average dispensing pressure over the fill (strictly positive, two decimal places); and `final_pressure_bar`, the vehicle tank pressure at end of fill (strictly positive, two decimal places), which is retained as an operational record. The transition is accepted only while `fill_status` is `FUELING` and the station is not `FROZEN`. Then `metered_kg` must be no greater than the session's `planned_kg`, with `ERR_METERED_EXCEEDS_PLANNED` (HTTP 422) otherwise. Then the cascade allocation runs, and its two failure modes are reported in the order the allocation encounters them: a required compressor contribution at a station whose `compressor_status` is `OFFLINE` yields `ERR_COMPRESSOR_OFFLINE` (HTTP 422), and an allocation that still cannot serve the reserved mass yields `ERR_CASCADE_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 422).

Cascade allocation is a two-pass waterfall over the station's pressure vessels, and it allocates the session's `planned_kg`. Only vessels whose `inspection_status` is `CURRENT` or `DUE` are candidates; vessels that are `OVERDUE` or `CONDEMNED` are excluded from both passes. The fill's terminal pressure is 875.00 bar for an `H70` session and 438.00 bar for an `H35` session.

The first pass is the gravity cascade, taken high bank first because that is the fastest fill. Candidates are visited in bank order — every `HIGH` vessel, then every `MEDIUM`, then every `LOW` — and within one tier in `created_at` ascending order. For each vessel, its floor pressure is the greater of its own `min_residual_bar` and the fill's terminal pressure; its usable pressure is the greater of zero and the amount by which its `current_pressure_bar` exceeds that floor; and its usable mass is that usable pressure multiplied by its `water_volume_l` and by 0.00008 kilograms per litre per bar. The amount taken from the vessel is the smaller of the mass still outstanding and that usable mass, rounded half-up to three decimal places. When the amount taken is greater than zero, the vessel's `current_pressure_bar` is reduced by the amount taken divided by the product of its `water_volume_l` and 0.00008; an allocation record is appended to `cascade_plan` carrying that vessel's `bank_tier`, its `vessel_id`, the `kg` taken and the `pressure_after_bar` reached; and the outstanding mass is reduced by the amount taken. `direct_cascade_kg` is the sum of the `kg` values allocated in this pass, rounded half-up to three decimal places.

The second pass is compressor makeup, and it deliberately runs in the **opposite** bank order to preserve high-bank pressure for the next fast fill. `compressor_makeup_kg` is the session's `planned_kg` minus `direct_cascade_kg`, rounded half-up to three decimal places; when it is greater than zero and the station's `compressor_status` is `OFFLINE`, the completion is refused with `ERR_COMPRESSOR_OFFLINE`. Otherwise candidates are visited `LOW` bank first, then `MEDIUM`, then `HIGH`, again in `created_at` ascending order within a tier, and the arithmetic is identical to the first pass with one change: the floor is each vessel's own `min_residual_bar` rather than the fill's terminal pressure, so the compressor can draw a vessel down below the pressure a gravity cascade could use. Records are appended to `cascade_plan` in the same shape. If mass remains outstanding after the second pass — meaning the two passes together cannot serve `planned_kg` without taking some vessel below its own `min_residual_bar` — the completion is refused with `ERR_CASCADE_INSUFFICIENT`. Kilogram amounts in the plan are carried at three decimal places and pressures at two.

Metering correction runs on the same write and is a pure function of three readings. The `density_correction_factor` is one, plus a temperature term, plus a pressure term. The temperature term is the amount by which the reference temperature of 20.0 degrees Celsius exceeds `ambient_temp_c`, multiplied by a coefficient of 0.00040 per degree. The pressure term is a coefficient of negative 0.00002 per bar multiplied by the difference obtained by subtracting the reference pressure of 700.00 bar from `avg_dispense_pressure_bar`. That factor is used exactly as computed, with no intermediate rounding. `corrected_kg` is `metered_kg` multiplied by the factor, rounded half-up to three decimal places. `metering_error_pct` is `metered_kg` minus `corrected_kg`, divided by `corrected_kg`, multiplied by one hundred, and rounded half-up to three decimal places. `metering_within_tolerance` is true when the absolute value of `metering_error_pct` is no greater than the meter tolerance of 1.500 percent; a session sitting exactly on the tolerance is within it.

A successful completion therefore stores `density_correction_factor`, `corrected_kg`, `metering_error_pct` and `metering_within_tolerance`; stores `cascade_plan`, `direct_cascade_kg` and `compressor_makeup_kg`; decrements `current_pressure_bar` and recomputes `stored_mass_kg` on every vessel named in the plan; appends a `DISPENSE_DRAW` ledger entry whose `delta_kg` is the negation of `metered_kg` and whose `source_ref_type` is `DISPENSE_SESSION`; moves `fill_status` `FUELING → COMPLETED` while leaving `billing_status` at `PENDING`; and recomputes the station's `onsite_inventory_kg`, `reserved_kg`, `available_kg`, `total_deliverable_kg`, `high_bank_deliverable_kg`, `projected_available_kg`, `supply_alert` and `safety_posture`, incrementing the station's `version` by one. That last recomputation carries a consequence worth stating on its own: a station that was `OPEN` with at least 10.000 kg of high-bank deliverable mass before the draw, and whose `high_bank_deliverable_kg` falls below 10.000 kg as a result of the draw, moves `OPEN → RESTRICTED` on the completion itself — the fill that emptied the high bank is the fill that restricts the next `H70` customer.

Abandoning a session releases its reservation. The operator's abort takes no payload and is accepted from either `PRECOOLING` or `FUELING`, and both legs behave identically: `fill_status` becomes `ABORTED`, `abort_reason` is set to `OPERATOR_ABORT`, `billing_status` moves `PENDING → VOIDED`, the session's `planned_kg` leaves the station's `reserved_kg` so `available_kg` rises again, and no ledger entry is appended because no mass moved. The station's `reserved_kg`, `available_kg`, `projected_available_kg` and `supply_alert` are recomputed and its `version` is incremented by one. The same two legs are also driven by the freeze cascade in §3.7, in which case `abort_reason` is `STATION_FROZEN` instead. `abort_reason` is null unless `fill_status` is `ABORTED`.

### 3.6. Metering Settlement, Month-to-Date Accuracy and Compensating Voids

Settlement is the billing axis of a dispensing session, and it moves on its own schedule. Either the station's own `StationOperator` or a `ComplianceAdmin` may settle a fill, with no payload; the transition is accepted only while the session's `billing_status` is `PENDING` **and** its `fill_status` is `COMPLETED`. A session still precooling or fuelling has no corrected mass to settle, and an aborted session was already voided, so either case is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.

Settling moves `billing_status` `PENDING → METERED` and books the difference between the meter and physics to the ledger. A `METER_CORRECTION` entry is appended whose `delta_kg` is the session's `metered_kg` minus its `corrected_kg`, reconciling the raw meter reading — which the completion already removed from inventory — to the corrected mass. When the correction factor was above one, the corrected mass exceeds the reading and the entry is negative, removing the extra mass; when the factor was below one, the entry is positive, returning mass. The write then recomputes the station's `onsite_inventory_kg`, `available_kg`, `projected_available_kg` and `supply_alert`, refreshes the month-to-date metering rollup described next, and increments the station's `version` by one.

The rollup is where a station's metering accuracy becomes visible. Its input set is the station's sessions whose `billing_status` is `METERED` and whose `started_at` falls inside the same UTC calendar month as the current logical clock, taken in `created_at` ascending order; `PENDING` and `VOIDED` sessions are excluded entirely, and ties are impossible because each row's `created_at` is unique. `metered_kg_mtd` is the sum of `metered_kg` across that set, rounded half-up to three decimal places. `corrected_kg_mtd` is the sum of the per-session `corrected_kg` values — that is, of the figures already rounded to three decimal places and stored on each session. The rounding order here is pinned and consequential: each session's corrected mass is rounded first and the rounded values are then summed. Rounding the sum of the unrounded products instead is explicitly not the required behaviour; the two answers can differ by up to a milligram per session. `metering_error_pct_mtd` is `metered_kg_mtd` minus `corrected_kg_mtd`, divided by `corrected_kg_mtd`, multiplied by one hundred, and rounded half-up to three decimal places. `metering_accuracy_flag` is `WITHIN_TOLERANCE` when the absolute value of `metering_error_pct_mtd` is no greater than 1.500 percent, and `OUT_OF_TOLERANCE` otherwise. When the input set is empty, `metered_kg_mtd`, `corrected_kg_mtd` and `metering_error_pct_mtd` are all 0.000 and the flag is `WITHIN_TOLERANCE`.

Voiding a settlement is the system's compensating action and is exclusive to `ComplianceAdmin`. It supplies a `void_reason` drawn from `METER_FAULT`, `BILLING_DISPUTE` and `AUDIT_ADJUSTMENT`, and that field is null on any session that has not been voided from a metered state. The transition is accepted only while `billing_status` is `METERED`, and only while no incentive claim whose `status` is `CERTIFIED` or `CREDITS_ISSUED` covers the date of the session's `started_at`; a session already baked into an adjudicated credit claim cannot be unwound, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.

A void moves `billing_status` `METERED → VOIDED` and appends a `METER_VOID_REVERSAL` entry whose `delta_kg` is the session's `corrected_kg` minus its `metered_kg` — the exact additive inverse of the `METER_CORRECTION` entry the settlement appended. Because the ledger is append-only, this is how a movement is undone, and the invariant it guarantees is checkable: after the void, the station's `onsite_inventory_kg` returns precisely to its pre-settlement value. The write also recomputes `available_kg`, `projected_available_kg` and `supply_alert`, and refreshes the metering rollup, which now excludes the voided session from `metered_kg_mtd`, `corrected_kg_mtd`, `metering_error_pct_mtd` and `metering_accuracy_flag`. The station's `version` is incremented by one.

### 3.7. Safety Events, Emergency-Response Readiness and Posture Recovery

Before a station may dispense or receive hydrogen, it must have trained people on site. A `SafetyOfficer` or `PlatformAdmin` records each responder's qualification with a `responder_ref` the operator assigns, a `cert_type` of `EMERGENCY_RESPONSE`, `HAZMAT_AWARENESS` or `CONFINED_SPACE`, and a validity range given as `valid_from` and `expires_on`, both plain `YYYY-MM-DD` dates with the expiry no earlier than the start. The derived `is_current` flag is true exactly when the logical-clock date is at or after `valid_from` and at or before `expires_on` — both bounds inclusive — and false otherwise, so a certification expiring today is still current today and lapses tomorrow. The readiness floor referenced by session creation and by offload counts only certifications on the station whose `cert_type` is `EMERGENCY_RESPONSE` and whose `is_current` is true; when that count falls below two, both operations are refused with `ERR_RESPONDER_TRAINING_LAPSED`. Moving the logical clock re-derives `is_current` for every certification in the system, which is how a station silently loses dispensing capability the day its second responder's certification lapses. Certifications can be listed per station with an optional `cert_type` filter.

Safety events may be opened by a `SafetyOfficer` or by the station's own `StationOperator`. The payload names the station through `station_id`, declares the `kind` of condition — `LEAK_DETECTED`, `VENTILATION_FAULT`, `COMPRESSOR_FAULT`, `OVERPRESSURE` or `SETBACK_BREACH` — the `severity` band of `MINOR`, `MAJOR` or `CRITICAL`, the `affected_nozzle_count` taken out of service (non-negative and no greater than the station's `nozzle_count`), and the `detected_by` channel of `SENSOR`, `OPERATOR_ROUND` or `THIRD_PARTY`. The server records `opened_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`, stamps `opened_at` from the logical clock — the inclusive start of the outage interval that uptime reporting will later measure — and sets `status` to `OPEN`. Events can be listed per station with optional `status` and `severity` filters.

Opening an event also writes through to the station's subsystem fields, and the mapping is one-to-one: a `LEAK_DETECTED` event sets `leak_detection_status` to `ALARM`; a `VENTILATION_FAULT` event sets `ventilation_status` to `FAULT`; a `COMPRESSOR_FAULT` event sets `compressor_status` to `OFFLINE`; and a `SETBACK_BREACH` event sets `setback_compliant` to false. An `OVERPRESSURE` event touches no subsystem field and influences posture only through its `severity`. The station's `safety_posture` is then recomputed against §2.8, and the station's `version` is incremented by one.

The freeze path is the system's most consequential cascade, and it applies whether the station was `OPEN → FROZEN` or `RESTRICTED → FROZEN`; the two legs are specified identically. It fires when the opened event's `kind` is `LEAK_DETECTED`, `VENTILATION_FAULT` or `SETBACK_BREACH`, or when its `severity` is `CRITICAL`. Hop one creates the event row with `status` of `OPEN` and `opened_at` stamped. Hop two sets the corresponding subsystem field as described above. Hop three, because that subsystem field (or the presence of a `CRITICAL` open event) is a freezing condition, recomputes `safety_posture` to `FROZEN`. Hop four, because the posture became `FROZEN`, takes every dispensing session on the station whose `fill_status` is `PRECOOLING` or `FUELING` to `fill_status` of `ABORTED` with `abort_reason` of `STATION_FROZEN`. Hop five, because those sessions have left the reserving set, moves each of them whose `billing_status` was `PENDING` to `VOIDED`, and recomputes the station's `reserved_kg`, `available_kg`, `projected_available_kg` and `supply_alert`. Completed fills are untouched — only in-flight ones are aborted.

The restricting path is narrower and does not abort work in flight. A `COMPRESSOR_FAULT` event of `MAJOR` severity opened against an `OPEN` station sets `compressor_status` to `OFFLINE` and recomputes the posture to `RESTRICTED`, taking the station `OPEN → RESTRICTED`; in-flight sessions continue, because a restricted posture only gates *new* `H70` sessions and *new* compressor makeup. The station's `version` is incremented by one. Two other routes reach the same posture without any safety event at all, and both are described where they occur: a fill completion whose cascade draw takes `high_bank_deliverable_kg` below 10.000 kg (§3.5), and a clock advance that takes a vessel past its due date plus thirty days (§3.1).

Acknowledgement belongs exclusively to `SafetyOfficer`, takes no payload, and is accepted only while the event's `status` is `OPEN`. It stamps `acknowledged_at` from the logical clock and moves the event `OPEN → ACKNOWLEDGED`. Crucially, the station's `safety_posture` does **not** change: an `ACKNOWLEDGED` event still counts as outstanding for every posture band, so acknowledging a critical leak does not thaw the station.

Clearing is also exclusive to `SafetyOfficer` and is the only step that requires a narrative: a non-empty `corrective_action` describing what was done. It is accepted only while the event's `status` is `ACKNOWLEDGED` — an `OPEN` event must be acknowledged first — and the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the event's `opened_by_user_id`, with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` otherwise. On success `cleared_at` is stamped from the logical clock as the **exclusive** end of the outage interval, so the accrued outage length is the span from `opened_at` to `cleared_at`; `cleared_by_user_id` records the acting user; the `corrective_action` is stored; and the event moves `ACKNOWLEDGED → CLEARED`, which is terminal. When no other event of the same `kind` on the station remains `OPEN` or `ACKNOWLEDGED`, the matching subsystem field returns to its healthy value — `ventilation_status` to `NORMAL`, `leak_detection_status` to `NORMAL`, `compressor_status` to `ONLINE`, `setback_compliant` to true. The station's `safety_posture` is then recomputed and its `version` incremented by one. Sessions aborted by an earlier freeze are never resumed.

Posture recovery therefore has three distinct shapes, and which one occurs depends entirely on what remains outstanding. Clearing the last freezing event while a `MAJOR` `COMPRESSOR_FAULT` event is still `OPEN` or `ACKNOWLEDGED` takes the station `FROZEN → RESTRICTED`: no event of kind `LEAK_DETECTED`, `VENTILATION_FAULT` or `SETBACK_BREACH` and no `CRITICAL`-severity event remains outstanding, but at least one restricting condition still holds. Clearing the last outstanding event of any kind, with no restricting condition left — no offline compressor, high-bank deliverable mass at or above 10.000 kg, no `OVERDUE` or `CONDEMNED` vessel and no outstanding `MAJOR` event — takes the station `FROZEN → OPEN`. And a station already `RESTRICTED` returns `RESTRICTED → OPEN` as soon as no restricting or freezing condition remains, which may be achieved by clearing an event, by recording a passing vessel inspection, or by an offload that restores high-bank deliverable mass. Every one of these recomputations increments the station's `version` by one.

### 3.8. Incentive Programme Participation and Credit Netting

Settled dispensing volume is what a low-carbon fuel programme pays for, and a `ComplianceAdmin` files each claim. The submission names the station through `station_id`, the `program` under which it is filed — `LCFS`, `CFP_OREGON` or `CFS_CANADA` — the claim period as `period_start` and `period_end` (plain `YYYY-MM-DD` dates, both bounds **inclusive**, with the end no earlier than the start), and a non-empty `production_lot_ids` array declaring which lots' certified carbon intensity backs the claim. A new claim is created with `status` of `SUBMITTED`, `revision_round` of 0, and `submitted_by_user_id` recorded from the acting `X-User-ID`. Claims can be listed per station with optional `program` and `status` filters.

Three domain rules guard submission, evaluated in this order. Every lot named in `production_lot_ids` must have `ci_status` of `CERTIFIED`, or the claim is refused with `ERR_LOT_CI_NOT_CERTIFIED` — an uncertified lot has no carbon intensity to weight. Every named lot must also have at least one delivery to *this* station whose `status` is `ACCEPTED` and whose `accepted_at` falls inside the inclusive claim period, or the claim is refused with `ERR_LOT_NOT_DELIVERED_IN_PERIOD` (HTTP 422); a lot the station never received cannot back its dispensing. Finally, no other claim for the same `station_id` and the same `program` whose `status` is anything other than `DENIED` may have an inclusive date range from `period_start` to `period_end` that intersects the requested range, or the claim is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). Denied claims free their period for a fresh filing; submitted, revision-requested, certified and credit-issued claims all hold it.

Credit netting runs at submission and again at every resubmission, and its evaluation order is pinned to break the apparent circularity between the fee tier and the netted total. Step one establishes volume: `dispensed_kg` is the sum of `corrected_kg` across the station's dispensing sessions whose `billing_status` is `METERED` and whose `started_at` date lies within the inclusive claim period, rounded half-up to three decimal places; `energy_mj` is `dispensed_kg` multiplied by the lower heating value of hydrogen, 120 megajoules per kilogram, rounded half-up to three decimal places.

Step two establishes the pathway carbon intensity backing the claim. `pathway_ci_gco2e_per_mj` is a mass-weighted mean over the declared lots, weighting each lot's `ci_certified_gco2e_per_mj` by its `accepted_delivered_kg`: sum the products of each lot's certified intensity and its accepted delivered mass, divide by the total accepted delivered mass across the declared lots, and round half-up to two decimal places. When that total accepted delivered mass is zero the weights are undefined, and the specification falls back to the unweighted arithmetic mean — the sum of the declared lots' certified intensities divided by the number of declared lots, rounded half-up to two decimal places.

Step three converts intensity into credits in a strictly ordered sequence. First, `gross_credits_mt` is computed as follows: divide `pathway_ci_gco2e_per_mj` by the energy economy ratio of 3.4, subtract that quotient from the benchmark carbon intensity of 79.10 grams CO2e per megajoule, multiply the difference by `energy_mj`, divide by one million to convert grams into metric tonnes, round half-up to three decimal places, and take the greater of that result and 0.000 — a pathway dirtier than the benchmark earns no credits rather than negative ones. Second, `admin_fee_rate` is selected from a tier table keyed on **gross** credits: a gross of at least 3.600 metric tonnes takes the high rate of 0.030, otherwise a gross of at least 1.200 metric tonnes takes the mid rate of 0.020, otherwise the low rate of 0.010 applies. The tier is keyed on gross credits and never on net credits; keying it on the net figure would be self-referential and is forbidden. Third, `admin_fee_mt` is `gross_credits_mt` multiplied by `admin_fee_rate`, rounded half-up to three decimal places. Fourth, `net_credits_mt` is `gross_credits_mt` minus `admin_fee_mt`, rounded half-up to three decimal places.

Adjudication belongs to `ProgramAuditor`, and the revision loop is cyclic but bounded. Requesting a revision supplies a non-empty `revision_note` and is accepted only while the claim's `status` is `SUBMITTED`. It always stores the note and always increments `revision_round` by one, but the resulting state depends on the round count *before* the increment. When `revision_round` was strictly less than the ceiling of two, the claim moves `SUBMITTED → REVISION_REQUESTED` and the compliance team may try again. When `revision_round` was already exactly two, the claim moves `SUBMITTED → DENIED` instead — the bound is enforced by automatic denial rather than by an error, so a third revision request succeeds as a request and terminates the claim. `DENIED` is terminal and no credits are ever issued against it; `revision_round` is left at three in that case.

Resubmission belongs to `ComplianceAdmin`, is accepted only while the claim's `status` is `REVISION_REQUESTED`, and supplies a replacement `production_lot_ids` array. Every lot in the supplied array must have `ci_status` of `CERTIFIED`, or the resubmission is refused with `ERR_LOT_CI_NOT_CERTIFIED`, and every lot must likewise satisfy the delivered-in-period rule, failing with `ERR_LOT_NOT_DELIVERED_IN_PERIOD`. On success the array is replaced, the claim moves `REVISION_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED`, and `dispensed_kg`, `energy_mj`, `pathway_ci_gco2e_per_mj`, `gross_credits_mt`, `admin_fee_rate`, `admin_fee_mt` and `net_credits_mt` are all recomputed from scratch. The period-overlap rule is not re-applied on resubmission, since the claim already holds its own period.

Certification is the auditor's approval, takes no payload, and is accepted only while the claim's `status` is `SUBMITTED`. The acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the claim's `submitted_by_user_id`, or the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. On success `certified_by_user_id` records the acting user and the claim moves `SUBMITTED → CERTIFIED`. That transition has an effect outside the claim: from this point the dispensing sessions whose `started_at` dates fall inside the claim period become ineligible for a meter void, as §3.6 describes.

Issuing credits is the last step and is accepted only while the claim's `status` is `CERTIFIED` and its `net_credits_mt` is strictly greater than zero; a claim netting nothing is refused with `ERR_NO_CREDITS_TO_ISSUE` (HTTP 422). On success the claim moves `CERTIFIED → CREDITS_ISSUED`, which is terminal, and the station's `credits_issued_total_mt` is recomputed as the sum of `net_credits_mt` across every claim for that station whose `status` is `CREDITS_ISSUED`; the station's `version` is incremented by one.

### 3.9. Uptime Reporting, Holdback Penalties and Amendments

Funding agreements pay for availability, so each reporting period ends with a report on how much of it the station was actually able to serve. A `ComplianceAdmin` or the station's own `StationOperator` drafts one, naming the station through `station_id` and the agreement through `funding_agreement_id`, bounding the period with `period_start` inclusive and `period_end` exclusive (both ISO 8601 UTC instants, the end strictly after the start), and supplying the `expected_funding_agreement_version` described in §2.7. Reports can be listed with optional `station_id`, `funding_agreement_id`, `amended_from_report_id` and `status` filters.

Drafting checks the concurrency token first, then two domain rules in order. No other report for the same `station_id` whose `status` is `FINAL` may have a half-open period from `period_start` to `period_end` intersecting the requested period, or the draft is refused with `ERR_REPORT_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409) — a rule re-applied at finalisation, so a second draft covering the same period may be created but only one of them can ever be made final. And the report must be filed under the station's own agreement: the supplied `funding_agreement_id` must equal the station's `funding_agreement_id`, or the draft is refused with `ERR_AGREEMENT_MISMATCH` (HTTP 409), the same cross-aggregate ownership code the delivery-booking rule uses.

A successful draft is created with `status` of `DRAFT`, snapshots `nozzle_count_snapshot` from the station's current `nozzle_count` — the denominator of the nozzle-weighted variant, frozen at report creation so that later nozzle expansion does not rewrite history — computes both uptime variants along with `uptime_pct` and `shortfall_points`, leaves `penalty_cents` at 0 because nothing is charged against the holdback until finalisation, and increments the agreement's `version` by one.

Downtime is measured from cleared safety events, and the input set is narrow. Only events on the station whose `severity` is `MAJOR` or `CRITICAL` and whose `status` is `CLEARED` participate; `MINOR` events never accrue downtime, and events still `OPEN` or `ACKNOWLEDGED` have no `cleared_at` and are excluded entirely. Of those, only events whose half-open outage interval from `opened_at` to `cleared_at` intersects the half-open reporting period are considered, and each interval is clipped to the period before use. The period's own length in minutes is the span from `period_start` to `period_end`.

The two variants treat the same event set very differently, and the divergence is the whole point of letting an agreement choose. Under the **nozzle-weighted** variant there is no interval merging at all: `downtime_nozzle_minutes` is the sum, over every qualifying event, of that event's clipped length in minutes multiplied by its `affected_nozzle_count`. Overlapping events accrue independently, because each names its own affected nozzle count, so a partial outage overlapping a full outage still reduces uptime. `uptime_pct_nozzle_weighted` is one hundred minus the quantity formed by dividing `downtime_nozzle_minutes` by the product of the period's minutes and `nozzle_count_snapshot` and multiplying by one hundred, rounded half-up to three decimal places. Under the **station-level** variant only full outages count: keep only those qualifying events whose `affected_nozzle_count` exactly equals `nozzle_count_snapshot`, then merge their clipped half-open intervals into a union — adjacent intervals with no gap between them merge into one, overlapping intervals are counted once, and disjoint intervals stay separate. `full_outage_minutes` is the total length of that union. `uptime_pct_station_level` is one hundred minus the quantity formed by dividing `full_outage_minutes` by the period's minutes and multiplying by one hundred, rounded half-up to three decimal places. On identical event data the station-level variant reports a strictly higher uptime whenever any partial outage exists, because partial-nozzle outages are invisible to it and overlaps are counted once.

Both variants are always computed and both are always stored, but only one binds. `uptime_pct` is `uptime_pct_nozzle_weighted` when the governing agreement's `uptime_method` is `NOZZLE_WEIGHTED`, and `uptime_pct_station_level` when it is `STATION_LEVEL`. `shortfall_points` is the greater of zero and the agreement's `min_uptime_pct` minus `uptime_pct`, rounded half-up to three decimal places. All four percentages carry three decimal places.

Finalisation is exclusive to `ComplianceAdmin`, takes no payload, and is accepted only while the report's `status` is `DRAFT` and no other report for the station with `status` of `FINAL` has a period intersecting this one, failing with `ERR_REPORT_PERIOD_OVERLAP` otherwise. It stamps `penalty_cents` and moves the report `DRAFT → FINAL`. The penalty is computed in two steps against a holdback figure evaluated **excluding** this report — that is, over the agreement's other reports whose `status` is `FINAL`. The raw penalty is `shortfall_points` multiplied by the agreement's `penalty_per_point_cents`, rounded half-up to a whole number of cents. The stamped `penalty_cents` is the smaller of that raw figure and the holdback remaining before this report, so a penalty can never drive the pool negative. A report with `shortfall_points` of 0.000 stamps `penalty_cents` of 0 and moves no money. After the write, the agreement's `holdback_balance_cents` is recomputed as the greater of zero and `initial_holdback_cents` minus the sum of `penalty_cents` across every report under the agreement whose `status` is `FINAL`, and the agreement's `version` is incremented by one.

Amending a final report supersedes it rather than editing it, and it is also exclusive to `ComplianceAdmin`. The request supplies a non-empty `amend_reason` together with `period_start` and `period_end`, which restate the period of the report being superseded; it is accepted only while the cited report's `status` is `FINAL`. The cited report moves `FINAL → AMENDED`, which is terminal, and stores the `amend_reason`; its `penalty_cents` value is retained for audit but, because the holdback derivation counts only `FINAL` reports, it immediately stops counting toward the charge. In its place a replacement report is created with `status` of `DRAFT`, `amended_from_report_id` pointing at the cited report, and every uptime figure recomputed from current safety-event data — which is the point of amending, since events cleared after the original finalisation now fall inside the period. The cited report must exist, share the same `station_id`, and have been `FINAL`. Finally the agreement's `holdback_balance_cents` is recomputed, which restores the superseded penalty because `AMENDED` reports are excluded from the charge, and the agreement's `version` is incremented by one. The replacement draft must itself be finalised before its own penalty is charged. The amendment's own response is that replacement — 201 Created carrying the new `DRAFT` report, not the superseded `AMENDED` one.
