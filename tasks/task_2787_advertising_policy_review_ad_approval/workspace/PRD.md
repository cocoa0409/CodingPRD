# Advertising Policy Review API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Advertising platforms live or die by the credibility of their policy enforcement. An advertiser who slips a prohibited claim past screening damages every other advertiser on the network; an advertiser who is wrongly disapproved, with no reasoned notice and no route to challenge it, takes their budget elsewhere. The Advertising Policy Review API is the operational backbone that resolves this tension. It carries a written policy framework with country-level variation, an automated pre-approval screening layer whose classifier decisions are graded against per-rule confidence thresholds, human review queues staffed by reviewers with declared specialities and country authorizations, and a full appeal path that can reverse an assessment and compensate the advertiser's violation ledger.

Two design commitments shape everything that follows. The first is that a creative's fate is never decided by a single axis: policy review and destination integrity advance independently, on different triggers, from different operators, and a creative becomes servable only when both axes and the owning account's enforcement posture agree. The second is that every points movement is written down. Assessments, appeals and reversals all land as append-only ledger entries, from which the advertiser's balances, severity index and account posture are derived rather than patched — so an operator can always reconstruct why an account was warned, restricted or suspended.

The service is mounted under `/ad-policy`. It runs on a logical clock rather than wall-clock time, and every time-gated rule — certification validity, appeal windows, review SLAs, enforcement expiry and points decay — is re-evaluated only when a deterministic policy sweep is invoked. This makes the entire compliance timeline reproducible: the same sequence of writes and clock advances always produces the same enforcement outcome.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section fixes the contracts that hold across every endpoint: authentication, envelope shape, pagination, resource identity, numeric discipline, version pinning, the logical clock, cascade semantics and the precedence in which validations are evaluated.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which it acts. Exactly four roles exist, spelled `Advertiser`, `PolicyReviewer`, `IntegrityAnalyst` and `PolicyAdmin`. A request whose `X-User-Role` header is missing or empty, whose `X-User-ID` header is missing or empty, or whose `X-User-Role` value is not one of those four spellings, is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). A syntactically valid request whose role is not among those authorized for the addressed operation — the authorized set for each operation is given in the §4 table — is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

The meaning of `X-User-ID` depends on the role. When `X-User-Role` is `Advertiser`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Advertiser` record; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Advertiser` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, an `Advertiser` may act only where `X-User-ID` equals the `advertiser_id` of the addressed `AdCreative`, of the addressed `Certification`, of the creative behind an addressed `DisapprovalNotice`, or of the creative behind the notice behind an addressed `Appeal`. When `X-User-Role` is `PolicyReviewer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Reviewer` record, and it must resolve to an existing `Reviewer`; task ownership compares a task's `assigned_reviewer_id` to `X-User-ID`, and appeal segregation compares a notice's `decided_by_reviewer_id` to `X-User-ID`. A violation of either the advertiser-ownership rule or the reviewer-identity rule yields `ERR_ADVERTISER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). This scope rule guards creative creation, creative revision, creative submission, creative withdrawal, certification submission, appeal filing, and every `PolicyReviewer` action on review tasks and appeals.

When `X-User-Role` is `IntegrityAnalyst`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record or log it but never compares it to any entity's `id`. The same holds when `X-User-Role` is `PolicyAdmin`: the identifier is opaque, is not matched against any stored row, and no ownership scoping is applied to it — a `PolicyAdmin` reads the whole estate unscoped.

The role model is one of segregation of duties, and it is deliberately restrictive. A `PolicyAdmin` maintains the policy framework, onboards advertisers and reviewers, ingests classifier screening runs, records verification outcomes, revokes certifications, requeues escalated tasks, mints manual enforcement actions, and drives the clock and sweep capabilities — but may never claim, release or decide a review task, and may never adjudicate an appeal. A `PolicyReviewer` claims and decides queued work and adjudicates appeals — but may never author policy rules, country overrides or enforcement actions. An `IntegrityAnalyst` is the sole source of landing-page and destination evidence and holds no other write capability. An `Advertiser` authors, revises, submits and withdraws its own creatives, submits certifications (a capability shared with `PolicyAdmin`) and files appeals. All four roles may read; `Advertiser` reads are scoped to that advertiser's own creatives, certifications, notices, appeals, archive entries and ledger entries.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints are paginated through two query parameters. The `limit` parameter caps the number of items returned; it defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. The `offset` parameter is the number of items to skip and defaults to 0; it may not be negative. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100, a non-integer `limit`, a negative `offset`, or a non-integer `offset` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400).

Unless a specific list operation declares a different ordering in the §4 table, list results are sorted by `created_at` ascending. Several lists do declare their own ordering, and where they do it overrides the default: the policy framework list is ordered by `code` ascending, a rule's country variations by `country_code` ascending, a creative's screening runs by `sequence_index` ascending, a ledger listing by `entry_index` ascending, archive entries by `archived_at` ascending, certifications by `valid_from` ascending and then `created_at` ascending, enforcement actions by `effective_from` ascending and then `created_at` ascending, and review tasks by `priority_score` descending and then `created_at` ascending.

Every list response repeats four pagination fields inside the envelope's `meta` object: `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties

Every resource in the system carries a server-minted `id`, formatted as a UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp rendered as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits (for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`). The instant recorded in `created_at` is read from the logical clock described in §2.8, never from wall-clock time.

Most resources additionally carry two revision fields. An `updated_at` timestamp, in the same ISO 8601 UTC format, is refreshed on every mutation of the row, including mutations that only touch derived fields. A `version` integer starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. Recomputing a derived value counts as a mutation: when a cascade rewrites an `AdCreative`'s risk scores or serving gate, or re-derives an `Advertiser`'s posture, that row's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` advances. The resources carrying `updated_at` and `version` are `PolicyRule`, `CountryPolicyOverride`, `Advertiser`, `Reviewer`, `AdCreative`, `ReviewTask`, `Certification`, `DisapprovalNotice`, `Appeal` and `EnforcementAction`.

Five record types are append-only evidence rather than mutable state, and they carry only `id` and `created_at` — both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from them entirely: `ScreeningRun`, `ClassifierDecision`, `DestinationCheck`, `PoliticalArchiveEntry` and `ViolationLedgerEntry`. Nothing in the system rewrites or deletes a row of any of these five types; corrections are expressed by writing a further row, which is why an appeal reversal is a new ledger entry rather than an edit of the original assessment.

### 2.5. Left-to-Right Resolution of Path Identifiers

Where a path names more than one resource, identifiers are resolved strictly left to right and the request fails on the first identifier that resolves to nothing. A request addressing the country variations of a policy rule that does not exist fails on the rule; a request addressing the screening runs of a creative that does not exist fails on the creative. Each identifier position has its own dedicated not-found error, all of them HTTP 404: an unresolvable `advertiser_id` yields `ERR_ADVERTISER_NOT_FOUND`, an unresolvable `ad_creative_id` yields `ERR_AD_CREATIVE_NOT_FOUND`, an unresolvable `policy_rule_id` yields `ERR_POLICY_RULE_NOT_FOUND`, an unresolvable `reviewer_id` yields `ERR_REVIEWER_NOT_FOUND`, an unresolvable `screening_run_id` yields `ERR_SCREENING_RUN_NOT_FOUND`, an unresolvable `review_task_id` yields `ERR_REVIEW_TASK_NOT_FOUND`, an unresolvable `certification_id` yields `ERR_CERTIFICATION_NOT_FOUND`, an unresolvable `disapproval_notice_id` yields `ERR_DISAPPROVAL_NOTICE_NOT_FOUND`, an unresolvable `appeal_id` yields `ERR_APPEAL_NOT_FOUND`, and an unresolvable `enforcement_action_id` yields `ERR_ENFORCEMENT_ACTION_NOT_FOUND`.

The same discipline applies to foreign keys arriving in a request body. A body-supplied `advertiser_id`, `policy_rule_id`, `prior_action_id`, `disapproval_notice_id` or `evidence_certification_id` must resolve, and every element of `declared_category_codes` must match the `code` of an existing policy rule — a miss there reports `ERR_POLICY_RULE_NOT_FOUND`. Within a screening ingest, the `policy_rule_id` of each submitted decision is validated in the order the decisions were submitted, and the first miss rejects the whole run: no partial run, no partial decisions.

### 2.6. Points, Scores and Numeric Discipline

All points, scores, weights and multipliers are decimal numbers carried to exactly 2 decimal places, and every rounding step in this document uses round-half-up. They are returned in response bodies as JSON numbers, never as strings.

Because several of the scoring rules in §3 sum many terms, the *order* of rounding is part of the contract and is stated explicitly at each formula. Two orders appear in this system and they are not interchangeable: risk scoring keeps its per-finding products unrounded and rounds the sum once, while the points assessment on a disapproval notice rounds each line to 2 decimals first and then sums the already-rounded lines. The two orders can differ by a cent on the same inputs, and each rule uses the one named in its own description.

Ranges are enforced as declared: a policy rule's `base_severity_weight` lies between 0.01 and 20.00, a country override's `severity_multiplier` between 0.50 and 3.00, both confidence thresholds and every classifier `confidence` between 0.01 and 1.00, and both risk scores between 0.00 and 100.00. A numeric value outside its declared range, or carrying more than 2 decimal places where 2 are specified, is a request-shape failure and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). Every threshold comparison in this system is an inclusive lower bound unless the text says otherwise: a value exactly equal to a threshold satisfies it.

### 2.7. Version Pinning on Classifier Ingest and Manual Enforcement

Exactly two write operations are guarded by optimistic concurrency control, and on both of them the version field is a required input rather than an optional hint.

Ingesting a screening run — `POST /ad-policy/ad-creatives/{id}/screening-runs` — requires `expected_ad_creative_version` in the body, carrying the `version` of the `AdCreative` that the classifier observed when it fetched the creative text. If that value does not equal the creative's current `version`, the run is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CREATIVE_VERSION` (HTTP 412) and nothing is written. Re-screening the same creative is legitimate and repeatable — a later run supersedes the earlier one and routing is recomputed from the newest run — so the version pin is the only sound discriminator: without it, a run could be ingested against text the advertiser has already revised.

Minting a manual enforcement action — `POST /ad-policy/advertisers/{id}/enforcement-actions` — requires `expected_advertiser_version` in the body, carrying the `version` of the `Advertiser` the operator read before deciding. If that value does not equal the advertiser's current `version`, the action is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ADVERTISER_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A new action legitimately supersedes the prior active one, so the write is repeatable by design; the version pin is what stops an operator from stacking an action onto a posture that a cascade has already changed beneath them.

No other endpoint accepts a version field. In particular, review-task claims, releases, decisions, requeues, appeal filings, appeal decisions, certification submissions, revocations, creative revisions, submissions and withdrawals take no version input and perform no version check.

### 2.8. The Logical Clock and the Deterministic Policy Sweep

The server keeps a logical clock and reads every "now" from it. Before any clock write the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it must never fall back to wall-clock time. A `PolicyAdmin` moves it forward by supplying `to_iso8601`, and the response reports `current_utc` alongside `previous_utc`. The clock never runs backwards: a `to_iso8601` earlier than the current logical clock, or one that is not a valid ISO 8601 UTC instant, is rejected with `ERR_CLOCK_REWIND` (HTTP 409). A value equal to the current clock is accepted. Advancing the clock mutates no entity by itself — it merely changes what "now" means.

Time-gated consequences are realized only by the policy sweep, an admin-only capability that runs eight phases in this fixed order at the current logical clock: (1) activation of certifications whose validity interval now contains the clock; (2) expiry of certifications whose validity has ended, together with the re-routing of creatives that relied on them; (3) expiry of disapproval notices whose appeal window has closed; (4) SLA expiry of review tasks, together with creation of their replacement tasks; (5) expiry of enforcement actions whose window has ended; (6) refresh of every advertiser's decayed points balance and severity index; (7) lifting of enforcement actions whose threshold the refreshed severity index no longer reaches; and (8) release of creatives held under suspension once the account is no longer suspended. Phase order matters: the decay refresh in phase 6 must precede the lifting decision in phase 7, and enforcement must have expired in phase 5 before phase 8 can conclude that no suspension remains active.

The sweep reports a count for each phase: `activated_certifications`, `expired_certifications`, `expired_notices`, `expired_review_tasks`, `requeued_review_tasks`, `expired_enforcement_actions`, `lifted_enforcement_actions`, `released_hold_creatives` and `decay_refreshed_advertisers`. Every entity the sweep touches has its `version` incremented by 1.

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline

Many single writes in this system move several rows. The cascades are specified hop by hop in §3, and three global rules govern all of them. First, a cascade is immediate and complete within the request that triggered it — there is no deferred or eventually-consistent tail, and a read issued after the response reflects every hop. Second, each row the cascade touches independently refreshes its own `updated_at` and increments its own `version` by 1, including rows reached only indirectly; the one deliberate exception is escalation of a review task, which leaves the creative untouched and does not advance the creative's `version`. Third, derived fields are recomputed from their inputs rather than incrementally patched: an advertiser's `violation_points_raw`, `violation_points_decayed`, `severity_index`, `account_status` and `ledger_entry_count`, and a creative's `risk_score_provisional`, `risk_score_final`, `serving_eligible` and `blocking_reason_codes`, are always the output of replaying their defining rule over current data.

Denormalized counters follow the same discipline. A policy rule's `country_override_count` tracks the number of country variations attached to it and starts at 0. A creative's `screening_run_count` tracks its screening runs. A reviewer's `claimed_task_count` tracks exactly how many of their tasks are currently held, and is conserved by the claim/release pair. An advertiser's `ledger_entry_count` tracks its ledger rows.

### 2.10. Validation Precedence

When one request breaks several rules, the server reports exactly one error — the one earliest in the following order — and performs no side effect whatsoever. The ordering places safety and compliance ahead of capacity, because a prohibited-category ad must be refused even when the queue is full:

1. Authentication of the headers — `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. Role authorization for the addressed operation — `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`.
3. Ownership scoping of the acting principal — `ERR_ADVERTISER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`.
4. Request-body shape: valid JSON, no unknown fields, every required field present, and every value satisfying its declared type, enum membership, string length, decimal precision, non-empty-array and no-duplicate-element constraints, and the `https://` prefix requirement on URL fields — `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
5. Pagination parameter validity — `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`.
6. Resolution of path identifiers, left to right, as described in §2.5.
7. Resolution of foreign keys supplied in the body, as described in §2.5.
8. The one-appeal-per-notice duplicate check — `ERR_APPEAL_ALREADY_EXISTS`. This deliberately runs ahead of the notice-state check below, because filing the first appeal moves the notice out of `ISSUED`: a second attempt must be reported as a duplicate, not as an un-appealable notice.
9. State preconditions on the addressed resource — `ERR_INVALID_CREATIVE_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_TASK_STATE`, `ERR_NOTICE_NOT_APPEALABLE`, `ERR_APPEAL_NOT_PENDING`, `ERR_INVALID_CERTIFICATION_STATE`.
10. Version pinning — `ERR_STALE_CREATIVE_VERSION`, `ERR_STALE_ADVERTISER_VERSION`.
11. Prohibited-category and blocked-country safety — `ERR_PROHIBITED_CATEGORY_BLOCKED`.
12. Account suspension — `ERR_ADVERTISER_SUSPENDED`.
13. Political eligibility — `ERR_POLITICAL_ELIGIBILITY_MISSING`.
14. Disclosure text presence — `ERR_POLITICAL_DISCLOSURE_MISSING`.
15. Segregation of duties on appeal adjudication — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
16. Reviewer country authorization — `ERR_REVIEWER_NOT_AUTHORIZED_COUNTRY`.
17. Reviewer availability — `ERR_REVIEWER_OFFLINE`.
18. Reviewer queue speciality — `ERR_REVIEWER_QUEUE_MISMATCH`.
19. Reviewer claim ceiling — `ERR_REVIEWER_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.
20. Certification coverage for a limited approval — `ERR_CERTIFICATION_MISSING`.
21. Shape of the retained country set on a limited approval — `ERR_INVALID_LIMITED_COUNTRIES`. Note the order: coverage is judged before the country set's own shape.
22. Certification validity window ordering — `ERR_INVALID_VALIDITY_WINDOW`.
23. Appeal window still open — `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_CLOSED`.
24. Certification interval overlap — `ERR_CERTIFICATION_INTERVAL_OVERLAP`.
25. Enforcement cooldown blackout — `ERR_ENFORCEMENT_COOLDOWN_CONFLICT`.
26. Clock monotonicity — `ERR_CLOCK_REWIND`.
27. Restricted-account category ban — `ERR_ADVERTISER_RESTRICTED_CATEGORY`.
28. Recognition of a fetched content category — `ERR_UNKNOWN_POLICY_CATEGORY`.
29. Consistency of supplied reason codes — `ERR_INVALID_REASON_CODE`.
30. Ownership of an evidence certification — `ERR_CERTIFICATION_OWNER_MISMATCH`.
31. Uniqueness of a policy code or of a rule-and-country pair — `ERR_DUPLICATE_POLICY_CODE`.
32. Ordering of the two confidence thresholds on a policy rule — `ERR_INVALID_THRESHOLD_ORDER`.

### 2.11. The Serving Eligibility Gate

Whether a creative may actually serve is never stored by hand; it is derived. The boolean `serving_eligible` and the ordered array `blocking_reason_codes` are recomputed on every write to a creative's `review_status`, on every write to its `destination_status`, and on every write to the owning advertiser's `account_status`. Four conjuncts are evaluated in this fixed order, and each one that fails appends its code to `blocking_reason_codes` in that same order — the array is never sorted:

1. `REVIEW_NOT_APPROVED` — satisfied when `review_status` is either `AUTO_APPROVED` or `APPROVED_LIMITED`.
2. `DESTINATION_NOT_CLEARED` — satisfied when `destination_status` is `DESTINATION_CLEARED`.
3. `ACCOUNT_ENFORCEMENT` — satisfied when the owning advertiser's `account_status` is either `ACTIVE` or `WARNED`.
4. `POLITICAL_ARCHIVE_MISSING` — satisfied when the creative's `political_flag` is false, or else when a political archive entry already exists for this creative and the owning advertiser's `political_eligibility_status` is `ELIGIBLE`.

`serving_eligible` is true only when all four conjuncts hold, in which case `blocking_reason_codes` is the empty array. A freshly created creative therefore reports `serving_eligible` false with `blocking_reason_codes` exactly `[REVIEW_NOT_APPROVED, DESTINATION_NOT_CLEARED]`, since it is neither approved nor destination-cleared but its account is in good standing and it is not political.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Policy Framework: Rules and Country Variation

Everything the platform enforces begins with a written **policy rule**, authored by a `PolicyAdmin`. A rule is identified by its `code` — a stable token of 1 to 40 characters drawn only from uppercase letters A through Z, digits and the underscore, unique across the whole framework — and carries a human-readable `title` of 1 to 160 characters that will later be printed on notices. Its `category_class` places it in one of the three framework tiers: `PROHIBITED` for content that may never run, `RESTRICTED` for content that may run only under review and licence, and `REGULATED` for content that is permitted but tracked. The rule's `base_severity_weight`, a value between 0.01 and 20.00 carried to exactly 2 decimals, is the points weight the rule contributes before any country multiplier or reason factor is applied. Two booleans state what an approval demands: `requires_certification`, which when true means an approval of a matched creative must be backed by a covering licence, and `requires_disclaimer`, which when true means an approval demands non-empty disclosure text on the creative. Both default to false.

A rule also configures how the classifier's output is graded and where routed work lands. `confidence_auto_disapprove_threshold` is the classifier confidence at or above which a match is treated as an automatic disapproval; it defaults to 0.90. `confidence_manual_threshold` is the confidence at or above which a match instead routes to a human; it defaults to 0.55. Both are values between 0.01 and 1.00 carried to exactly 2 decimals. Their *ordering* is a separate business rule rather than a type constraint: the auto-disapprove threshold must be strictly greater than the manual threshold, and a rule submitted with an auto-disapprove threshold less than or equal to the manual threshold is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_THRESHOLD_ORDER` (HTTP 422). `review_sla_hours`, an integer from 1 to 336 defaulting to 48, is how many hours a routed task on this rule gets before its deadline. `default_queue`, one of `STANDARD`, `REGULATED_CERT` or `POLITICAL` and defaulting to `STANDARD`, names the queue a routed task lands in when this rule is the primary finding. A newly registered rule begins with `country_override_count` at 0. Registering a rule whose `code` already exists is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_POLICY_CODE` (HTTP 409). The framework can be listed in whole or filtered by `category_class`.

Policy is not uniform across the world, so a `PolicyAdmin` may attach **country variations** to a rule. Each variation names the `country_code` it refines — exactly two uppercase letters, ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 — and carries the `policy_rule_id` of the rule it belongs to. Its `disposition` states the country-level treatment and is one of `BLOCKED`, `RESTRICTED_CERT_REQUIRED`, `ALLOWED_WITH_DISCLAIMER` or `ALLOWED`. `requires_license`, defaulting to false, means that in this country an approval demands a certification whose `issuing_country_code` equals this `country_code`. `requires_disclaimer`, defaulting to false, means approval in this country demands non-empty disclosure text. `severity_multiplier`, a value between 0.50 and 3.00 carried to exactly 2 decimals and defaulting to 1.00, scales the rule's base weight for this country. At most one variation may exist for any one rule and country: a second attempt on the same pair is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_POLICY_CODE` (HTTP 409). Attaching a variation increments the parent rule's `country_override_count` by 1 and advances the rule's `version` by 1.

Four derived notions built on this framework recur throughout the rest of this document, and they are defined once here. The **matched rule set** of a creative is the set of policy rules whose `code` appears in the creative's `declared_category_codes`. The **country multiplier** of a rule for a creative is the largest `severity_multiplier` found among the variations of that rule for the creative's target countries, counting 1.00 for every target country that has no variation on that rule; when the creative has no target countries at all the multiplier is 1.00, a case the non-empty constraint on the country list makes unreachable. The **strictest multiplier** of a creative is the largest `severity_multiplier` found across every combination of a rule in its matched rule set and a country in its target set, again counting 1.00 where no variation exists, and 1.00 when the matched rule set is empty. Finally, the **disposition of a rule in a country** is that country variation's `disposition` when a variation exists; when none exists it falls back to the rule's tier — `BLOCKED` for a `PROHIBITED` rule, `RESTRICTED_CERT_REQUIRED` for a `RESTRICTED` rule and `ALLOWED` for a `REGULATED` rule. The **strictest disposition** of a rule for a creative is the harshest of those dispositions across the creative's target countries, where harshness runs `BLOCKED` first, then `RESTRICTED_CERT_REQUIRED`, then `ALLOWED_WITH_DISCLAIMER`, then `ALLOWED`.

### 3.2. Advertiser Accounts, Verification Axes and Posture

A `PolicyAdmin` onboards an advertiser with three declared facts: its `legal_name`, of 1 to 200 characters, which is the registered business name; its `country_code` of incorporation, exactly two uppercase letters; and its self-declared `industry_code`, of 1 to 40 characters, against which industry authorization is later judged. Onboarding also fixes the account's starting posture: `identity_status` and `business_standing_status` both begin at `PENDING`, `industry_authorization_status` begins at `NOT_REQUIRED`, `political_eligibility_status` begins at `NOT_REQUESTED`, `account_status` begins at `ACTIVE`, `violation_points_raw`, `violation_points_decayed` and `severity_index` all begin at 0.00, and `ledger_entry_count` begins at 0.

Four verification axes advance independently, and only a `PolicyAdmin` recording a verification outcome may advance them. The request names the `dimension` being recorded — one of `IDENTITY`, `BUSINESS_STANDING`, `INDUSTRY_AUTHORIZATION` or `POLITICAL_ELIGIBILITY` — together with the `outcome`, and may attach an optional `evidence_ref`. The permitted outcomes are constrained to the axis being written: the identity axis writes `identity_status` to `PENDING`, `VERIFIED` or `FAILED`; the business-standing axis writes `business_standing_status` to `PENDING`, `VERIFIED` or `FAILED`; the industry axis writes `industry_authorization_status` to `NOT_REQUIRED`, `PENDING`, `GRANTED` or `DENIED`; and the political axis writes `political_eligibility_status` to `NOT_REQUESTED`, `PENDING`, `ELIGIBLE` or `INELIGIBLE`. An `outcome` outside the addressed axis's enumeration is a request-shape failure and reports `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Recording an outcome writes the one matching status field and increments the advertiser's `version` by 1.

Account posture, held in `account_status`, is different in kind: it is never written directly but is derived — a pure function of the advertiser's enforcement actions that are currently in force. When no action on the account is in force, the posture is `ACTIVE`. When the most severe action in force is a warning, the posture is `WARNED`; when it is a restriction, the posture is `RESTRICTED`; when it is a suspension, the posture is `SUSPENDED`, with severity running warning first, then restriction, then suspension. Because the posture is recomputed from scratch after any change to any enforcement row, it can move any distance in either direction in a single re-derivation — `ACTIVE` straight to `SUSPENDED`, or `SUSPENDED` straight back to `ACTIVE` — and each such re-derivation increments the advertiser's `version` by 1.

Posture has teeth. A `RESTRICTED` account may not author a creative whose `declared_category_codes` include any rule of tier `PROHIBITED` or `RESTRICTED`, and may not author a creative with `political_flag` true; either attempt is refused with `ERR_ADVERTISER_RESTRICTED_CATEGORY` (HTTP 409). A `SUSPENDED` account may not author a creative, revise one, submit one for review, or submit a certification; all four are refused with `ERR_ADVERTISER_SUSPENDED` (HTTP 409). Reads are never blocked by posture: an advertiser can always inspect its own account, including `account_status`, both points balances, `severity_index` and `ledger_entry_count`.

### 3.3. Creative Authoring, Revision, Submission and Withdrawal

An `Advertiser` authors a creative by supplying its own `advertiser_id`, the `headline` of 1 to 200 characters and the `body_text` of 1 to 2000 characters that the classifier will screen, the non-empty `declared_category_codes` array naming the policy codes it self-declares, the non-empty `target_country_codes` array of distinct two-letter uppercase country codes it intends to serve in, the `landing_page_url` a click resolves to, and the `display_url` shown in the ad — both URLs must begin with the literal text `https://` and must contain a host segment. Two further fields are optional: `political_flag`, a boolean defaulting to false by which the advertiser declares the creative to be political or social-issue advertising, and `disclaimer_text`, the paid-for-by disclosure, which defaults to null and when present is 1 to 300 characters. A creative is born with `review_status` `DRAFT` and `destination_status` `NOT_CHECKED`, with both `risk_score_provisional` and `risk_score_final` at 0.00, `limited_country_codes` empty, `approved_under_certification_id` null, `screening_run_count` at 0, `serving_eligible` false and `blocking_reason_codes` exactly `[REVIEW_NOT_APPROVED, DESTINATION_NOT_CLEARED]`.

The creative lifecycle runs on two independent axes that live on the same row. The `review_status` axis takes the values `DRAFT`, `SCREENING`, `AUTO_APPROVED`, `PENDING_MANUAL_REVIEW`, `APPROVED_LIMITED`, `DISAPPROVED`, `SUSPENDED_HOLD` and `WITHDRAWN`, of which `WITHDRAWN` is terminal. The `destination_status` axis takes the values `NOT_CHECKED`, `DESTINATION_PENDING`, `DESTINATION_CLEARED`, `MISMATCH_FLAGGED` and `CLOAKING_CONFIRMED`, and has no terminal value at all. The two axes advance on different triggers, are moved by different roles, and are read together by the serving gate of §2.11.

Submitting a creative for policy review is the owning advertiser's act, and it advances `DRAFT → SCREENING`. It is admissible only while `review_status` is `DRAFT`, only when `X-User-ID` equals the creative's `advertiser_id`, only when the owning advertiser's `account_status` is anything other than `SUSPENDED`, and only when every element of `declared_category_codes` resolves to an existing policy rule `code`. As a side effect the destination axis moves `NOT_CHECKED → DESTINATION_PENDING` when it was still `NOT_CHECKED`, and the creative's `version` increments by 1 with `updated_at` refreshed. Submission does not itself screen anything — screening arrives separately as an ingested classifier run. Attempting submission from any `review_status` other than `DRAFT` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CREATIVE_STATE` (HTTP 409); attempting it while the account is suspended is refused with `ERR_ADVERTISER_SUSPENDED`.

A disapproved creative is not a dead end. The owning advertiser may revise the creative, changing any of `headline`, `body_text`, `declared_category_codes`, `target_country_codes`, `landing_page_url`, `display_url`, `political_flag` and `disclaimer_text`, and a revision is admissible while `review_status` is either `DRAFT` or `DISAPPROVED` — from `DISAPPROVED` the revision returns the creative to `DRAFT`, closing the revision loop so that a corrected creative can be re-submitted and re-screened. Revision resets the destination axis to `NOT_CHECKED`, resets both `risk_score_provisional` and `risk_score_final` to 0.00, resets `limited_country_codes` to the empty array and `approved_under_certification_id` to null, expires any queued review task standing on this creative, and increments the creative's `version` by 1. As with submission, the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the creative's `advertiser_id`, the account must not be `SUSPENDED`, and a revision attempted from any other `review_status` — `APPROVED_LIMITED`, for instance — reports `ERR_INVALID_CREATIVE_STATE`.

Withdrawal is the advertiser's escape hatch and is available from every non-terminal review state: `DRAFT`, `SCREENING`, `PENDING_MANUAL_REVIEW`, `AUTO_APPROVED`, `APPROVED_LIMITED`, `DISAPPROVED` and `SUSPENDED_HOLD` all admit it, and it moves `review_status` to the terminal `WITHDRAWN`. Withdrawal expires every queued or claimed review task on the creative and decrements the `claimed_task_count` of each reviewer who was holding one, recomputes `serving_eligible` to false, and increments the creative's `version` by 1. Any political archive entry already written for the creative survives withdrawal untouched and stays searchable — the archive is a public-interest record, not a convenience for the advertiser. Only the owning advertiser may withdraw, and a withdrawal attempted on an already-`WITHDRAWN` creative reports `ERR_INVALID_CREATIVE_STATE`.

Creatives can be listed and filtered by owner through `advertiser_id`, by either axis through `review_status` and `destination_status`, by `political_flag`, and by the certification they were approved under through `approved_under_certification_id`. A single creative read returns both axes, both risk scores, the serving gate and its blocking reasons.

### 3.4. Automated Pre-Approval Screening

Screening is ingested rather than performed: an external classifier reads the creative text and a `PolicyAdmin` submits the resulting **screening run** against the creative. The submission carries `classifier_version`, a build identifier of 1 to 40 characters recorded purely for audit; `decisions`, a non-empty array in which each element is an object with a `policy_rule_id`, a `confidence` between 0.01 and 1.00 carried to exactly 2 decimals, and a `matched_snippet` of 1 to 300 characters quoting the creative text that triggered the match; and `expected_ad_creative_version`, the version pin described in §2.7. A run is admissible only while the creative's `review_status` is `SCREENING`; ingesting one against a `DRAFT` creative reports `ERR_INVALID_CREATIVE_STATE`. Every `policy_rule_id` in `decisions` is checked in submitted order and the first unresolvable one rejects the entire run with `ERR_POLICY_RULE_NOT_FOUND`, leaving no run and no decisions behind.

An accepted run records `ad_creative_id`, takes `sequence_index` as one greater than the highest index already used on that creative — so indices are contiguous from 1 and the greatest index is always the latest run — and snapshots the creative's `version` at acceptance into `creative_version_at_run`. It then materializes one **classifier decision** row per submitted element, each carrying `screening_run_id`, the submitted `policy_rule_id`, `confidence` and `matched_snippet`, plus three derived fields. The run itself records `decision_count` as the number of decision rows it created, `max_confidence` as the highest `confidence` among them (0.00 when the run carried no decisions), `provisional_risk_score` as a snapshot of the creative's provisional risk score at run time, and `outcome`. Ingesting a run increments the creative's `screening_run_count` and its `version` by 1. A creative's runs can be listed in `sequence_index` order, a single run read on its own, and a run's decisions listed; decisions can also be listed per policy rule, which is how an admin sees every creative a given rule has ever caught.

Grading happens in three steps, and the first grades each decision on its own. Comparing the decision's `confidence` against the thresholds of the rule it matched, and treating every threshold as an inclusive lower bound, the decision's `disposition` is `AUTO_DISAPPROVE` when `confidence` is at least that rule's `confidence_auto_disapprove_threshold`, otherwise `ROUTE_MANUAL` when `confidence` is at least that rule's `confidence_manual_threshold`, and otherwise `BELOW_THRESHOLD`. Alongside the disposition each decision records `country_multiplier_applied`, which is the country multiplier of its rule for this creative as defined in §3.1, and `assessed_points`, which is the rule's `base_severity_weight` multiplied by that `country_multiplier_applied` and then multiplied by the decision's own `confidence`, with the product rounded half-up to 2 decimals. This per-row `assessed_points` is an audit figure only; it is never summed to produce a risk score, because summing already-rounded rows is the wrong rounding order for that purpose.

The second step derives the run's `outcome`, and the six clauses below are evaluated in exactly this order with the first match winning. First, the outcome is `AUTO_DISAPPROVED` when some decision in the run has disposition `AUTO_DISAPPROVE` and the strictest disposition of that decision's rule for this creative is `BLOCKED`. Second, failing that, the outcome is `ROUTED_MANUAL` when the creative's `political_flag` is true — a political creative is never auto-approved. Third, the outcome is `ROUTED_MANUAL` when some decision has disposition `AUTO_DISAPPROVE` or `ROUTE_MANUAL`; this is the clause that catches an auto-disapprove finding whose strictest country disposition stopped short of `BLOCKED`. Fourth, the outcome is `ROUTED_MANUAL` when the owning advertiser's `identity_status` is anything other than `VERIFIED`, or its `business_standing_status` is anything other than `VERIFIED`. Fifth, the outcome is `ROUTED_MANUAL` when the advertiser's `account_status` is `RESTRICTED` or `WARNED` and at least one rule in the creative's matched rule set has `category_class` `PROHIBITED` or `RESTRICTED`. Sixth and last, the outcome is `AUTO_APPROVED` — and that includes a run whose `decisions` produced only `BELOW_THRESHOLD` rows.

Each outcome drives the review axis. An `AUTO_APPROVED` outcome advances `SCREENING → AUTO_APPROVED`, which additionally requires that the advertiser's `identity_status` and `business_standing_status` are both `VERIFIED` and that `political_flag` is false — conditions the fourth and second grading clauses already guarantee, which is precisely why this transition is unreachable for a political creative. A `ROUTED_MANUAL` outcome advances `SCREENING → PENDING_MANUAL_REVIEW` and creates exactly one review task in status `QUEUED`. An `AUTO_DISAPPROVED` outcome advances `SCREENING → DISAPPROVED` and opens the disapproval cascade: hop one creates a disapproval notice in status `ISSUED` whose `decided_by_reviewer_id` is null and whose `reason_codes` is exactly `[AUTO_SCREENING]`; hop two writes a violation ledger entry for that notice with `reason` `DISAPPROVAL_ASSESSED` and `delta_points` equal to the notice's `assessed_points`; hop three recomputes the advertiser's `violation_points_raw`, `violation_points_decayed` and `severity_index` from the ledger; and hop four lets that refreshed `severity_index` possibly create an enforcement action, which in turn re-derives `account_status`. Every outcome, including auto-approval, writes both `risk_score_provisional` and `risk_score_final`, recomputes `serving_eligible` and `blocking_reason_codes`, and increments the creative's `version` by 1.

The third step applies only to a `ROUTED_MANUAL` outcome and settles where the single new task lands. Its `queue` is `POLITICAL` when the creative's `political_flag` is true; otherwise it is `REGULATED_CERT` when some decision with disposition `AUTO_DISAPPROVE` or `ROUTE_MANUAL` matched a rule whose `requires_certification` is true, or when such a decision's rule has a country variation with `requires_license` true for one of the creative's target countries; otherwise it is `STANDARD`. Its `primary_policy_rule_id` is null when no decision in the run reached `AUTO_DISAPPROVE` or `ROUTE_MANUAL`; otherwise it is the rule of the qualifying decision ranked by `confidence` descending, ties broken by that rule's `code` ascending — and since codes are unique the ranking is total. Its `sla_deadline_at` is the task's `created_at` plus the `review_sla_hours` of the primary rule, or plus a default of 48 hours when `primary_policy_rule_id` is null.

Risk scoring runs off the same evidence but with a narrower input set: only the decision rows of the creative's *latest* run count, and among those only the rows whose `confidence` is at least the `confidence_manual_threshold` of the rule they matched. Rows below that threshold are excluded, and every row belonging to an earlier run is excluded outright. Call those surviving rows the findings. Two scores are then published from the findings, and they are deliberately different quantities.

The provisional score, stored as `risk_score_provisional` and mirrored onto the run's `provisional_risk_score`, is what the screening layer publishes before any destination evidence or licence credit exists. For each finding, take the matched rule's `base_severity_weight`, multiply it by the country multiplier of that rule for this creative, and multiply the result by the finding's own `confidence`; keep each of those products unrounded, add them all together, round the single total half-up to 2 decimals, and cap the outcome at 100.00. The rounding happens once, on the sum — not per finding.

The final score, stored as `risk_score_final`, is written alongside the provisional score at every run and then rewritten on every destination check and on every review decision that moves the creative's `review_status`. It starts from the provisional score, adds a destination penalty, and subtracts a certification credit. The destination penalty is 40.00 when the creative's `destination_status` is `CLOAKING_CONFIRMED`, 15.00 when it is `MISMATCH_FLAGGED`, and 0.00 in the three remaining cases `NOT_CHECKED`, `DESTINATION_PENDING` and `DESTINATION_CLEARED`. The certification credit rewards licences actually held: count the distinct rules among the findings for which the licence-coverage test of §3.5 returns at least one candidate certification, at the current logical clock, for at least one of the creative's target countries; multiply that count by 8.00; and cap the credit at 24.00, so three covered rules already exhaust it. Add the penalty to the provisional score, subtract the credit, round the result half-up to 2 decimals, floor it at 0.00 and cap it at 100.00. Because the provisional score is rounded before it enters this expression while the two adjustments are exact, the two scores may diverge by more than a rounding artifact — that divergence is intended, and either score may be the larger of the two depending on whether penalty or credit dominates.

### 3.5. Certifications and Licence Coverage

Restricted and regulated categories are frequently approvable only against a licence, and the platform models those licences as **certifications**. Either the advertiser itself or a `PolicyAdmin` may submit one, naming the `advertiser_id` that holds it, the `policy_rule_id` it certifies against, the `license_number` issued by the authority (1 to 60 characters), the `issuing_country_code` of the authority (exactly two uppercase letters), and the half-open validity interval `valid_from` to `valid_until`. The interval is inclusive at `valid_from` and exclusive at `valid_until`, and `valid_until` must be strictly after `valid_from` — a submission where it is equal or earlier is refused with `ERR_INVALID_VALIDITY_WINDOW` (HTTP 422). Instants are compared at whole-second ISO 8601 UTC resolution. A certification is born with `status` `SUBMITTED` and a null `revocation_reason`, except that when the logical clock is already at or after `valid_from` and strictly before `valid_until` it is born `ACTIVE` outright. Submitting while the owning account is `SUSPENDED` is refused with `ERR_ADVERTISER_SUSPENDED`.

A holder may not hold two overlapping licences for the same rule in the same country. On submission, the new interval is rejected when there already exists a certification for the same combination of `advertiser_id`, `policy_rule_id` and `issuing_country_code` whose `status` is `SUBMITTED` or `ACTIVE` and whose interval intersects the new one — intersection meaning that the new `valid_from` is strictly before the existing `valid_until` and the existing `valid_from` is strictly before the new `valid_until`. The rejection is `ERR_CERTIFICATION_INTERVAL_OVERLAP` (HTTP 409). Adjacency is therefore expressly legal: a new interval starting exactly at an existing interval's `valid_until`, or ending exactly at an existing interval's `valid_from`, does not intersect it and is admitted. Rows whose `status` is `EXPIRED` or `REVOKED` are ignored by this check entirely, so a fresh licence may be filed over a revoked one's dates.

A certification lives through four states, `SUBMITTED`, `ACTIVE`, `EXPIRED` and `REVOKED`, of which the last two are terminal. A future-dated `SUBMITTED` licence is promoted `SUBMITTED → ACTIVE` by the policy sweep's first phase once the logical clock has reached `valid_from` and is still strictly before `valid_until`, and provided no other certification for the same holder, rule and issuing country with status `SUBMITTED` or `ACTIVE` has an interval intersecting this one. The sweep's second phase moves a certification whose `valid_until` has been reached or passed to `EXPIRED`, from either `SUBMITTED` or `ACTIVE`. A `PolicyAdmin` may instead revoke a certification whose `status` is `SUBMITTED` or `ACTIVE`, supplying `revocation_reason`, which moves it to `REVOKED`; revoking one already `EXPIRED` or `REVOKED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CERTIFICATION_STATE` (HTTP 409). Each of these three transitions increments the certification's `version` by 1.

Losing a certification reaches back into the creatives that leaned on it. When a certification expires, every creative whose `approved_under_certification_id` names it and whose `review_status` is `AUTO_APPROVED` or `APPROVED_LIMITED` is re-routed to `PENDING_MANUAL_REVIEW`, gains a fresh `QUEUED` review task in the `REGULATED_CERT` queue, has its `approved_under_certification_id` cleared, has `serving_eligible` and `blocking_reason_codes` recomputed, and has its `version` incremented by 1. Revocation is narrower in scope but heavier in effect: every creative whose `approved_under_certification_id` names the revoked licence and whose `review_status` is `APPROVED_LIMITED` moves to `PENDING_MANUAL_REVIEW` with a new `REGULATED_CERT` task, and has both `approved_under_certification_id` and `limited_country_codes` cleared, along with the same recomputation and `version` bump.

Whether a creative is *covered* for a rule in a country at a given instant is a single test used in three places — by a limited approval, by the certification credit in the final risk score, and by the coverage validation on a reviewer decision. The candidate set for that question is every certification whose `advertiser_id` is the creative's owner, whose `policy_rule_id` is the rule in question, whose `issuing_country_code` equals the country in question, whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, and whose interval contains the instant — that is, `valid_from` at or before the instant and the instant strictly before `valid_until`. Rows in `SUBMITTED`, `EXPIRED` or `REVOKED` never qualify as coverage. When exactly one certification must be pinned onto a creative, the candidates are ranked by `valid_until` descending so that the longest remaining coverage wins, with ties broken by `created_at` ascending; identifiers never participate in the ranking. An empty candidate set where coverage is demanded rejects the operation with `ERR_CERTIFICATION_MISSING`. A country demands coverage for a rule when that rule's `requires_certification` is true, or when the rule's variation for that country has `requires_license` true. Certifications can be listed filtered by `advertiser_id`, by `policy_rule_id` or by `status`.

### 3.6. The Human Review Queue: Reviewers, Claiming, Escalation and SLA

Human review is staffed by **reviewers** registered by a `PolicyAdmin`. A reviewer carries a `display_name` of 1 to 120 characters that appears on notices and audit trails, a non-empty `queue_specialties` array of distinct values drawn from `STANDARD`, `REGULATED_CERT` and `POLITICAL` naming the queues they may pull from, a non-empty `country_authorizations` array of distinct two-letter uppercase codes naming the countries they are competent to adjudicate, and `max_concurrent_claims`, an integer from 1 to 10 defaulting to 3 that ceilings how many tasks they may hold at once. A new reviewer begins with `claimed_task_count` at 0 and `status` `ACTIVE`; the only other availability value is `OFFLINE`. A `PolicyAdmin` may later amend `status`, `max_concurrent_claims`, `queue_specialties` or `country_authorizations`, which increments the reviewer's `version` by 1; narrowing specialties or authorizations does not release tasks the reviewer already holds, so a reviewer can legitimately finish work they could no longer claim today.

A **review task** is never created by a client — it is always the by-product of routing, re-routing, reinstatement or replacement. It records the `ad_creative_id` it concerns, the `primary_policy_rule_id` of the highest-confidence qualifying finding (null when the creative routed with no qualifying finding at all), the `assigned_reviewer_id` of whoever holds it (null while `QUEUED`, `ESCALATED` or `EXPIRED`, and null again after a release), the `queue` it sits in, its `status`, the reviewer's `resolution`, the derived `priority_score`, the derived `sla_deadline_at`, the `cycle_count` loop counter, the `claimed_at` instant of the last successful claim (null while never claimed) and the `escalation_note` (null unless an escalation wrote one). Task `status` runs over `QUEUED`, `CLAIMED`, `RESOLVED`, `ESCALATED` and `EXPIRED`, with `RESOLVED` and `EXPIRED` terminal; `resolution` starts at `PENDING` and may become `APPROVE`, `APPROVE_WITH_LIMITS`, `DISAPPROVE` or `ESCALATE`. Tasks are listed ordered by `priority_score` descending and then `created_at` ascending, and can be filtered by `ad_creative_id`, `primary_policy_rule_id`, `assigned_reviewer_id`, `status` or `queue`.

Priority is computed when a task is created — by screening routing, by a destination re-route, by a certification loss, by appeal reinstatement or by SLA replacement — and again when an escalated task is requeued. Start from a queue base of 30.00 for `STANDARD`, 45.00 for `REGULATED_CERT` and 60.00 for `POLITICAL`. Compute a severity component: 0.00 when the task has no `primary_policy_rule_id`, and otherwise the primary rule's `base_severity_weight` multiplied by the creative's strictest multiplier and then multiplied by 2.00, that product rounded half-up to 2 decimals and then capped at 30.00. Compute a history component from the owning advertiser's decayed points balance: divide `violation_points_decayed` by 10.00, discard the fractional part, multiply the whole number by 5.00, and cap the result at 15.00 — so every complete ten decayed points buys five priority points, up to three steps. Take the *larger* of the severity component and the history component; the two are alternatives, never a sum. Then add a verification gap of 10.00 when the advertiser's `identity_status` is anything other than `VERIFIED` or its `business_standing_status` is anything other than `VERIFIED`, and 0.00 otherwise. Then add a disclosure gap of 12.00 when the creative's `political_flag` is true while its `disclaimer_text` is null or the empty string, and 0.00 otherwise. Add the queue base, the larger of the two components, the verification gap and the disclosure gap; round the total half-up to 2 decimals and cap it at 100.00. That value is `priority_score`. The deadline `sla_deadline_at` is the task's `created_at` plus the primary rule's `review_sla_hours`, or plus 48 hours when the task has no primary rule.

Claiming a `QUEUED` task is a `PolicyReviewer` act that advances `QUEUED → CLAIMED`, and it is gated by four checks that are evaluated in a fixed order so that the reported error is predictable. Competence comes first: every element of the creative's `target_country_codes` must appear in the acting reviewer's `country_authorizations`, and a gap reports `ERR_REVIEWER_NOT_AUTHORIZED_COUNTRY` (HTTP 403). Availability comes second: the reviewer's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, and an `OFFLINE` reviewer is refused with `ERR_REVIEWER_OFFLINE` (HTTP 409). Speciality comes third: the task's `queue` must appear in the reviewer's `queue_specialties`, failing which the claim reports `ERR_REVIEWER_QUEUE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403). Capacity comes last: the reviewer's `claimed_task_count` must be strictly less than their `max_concurrent_claims`, and a reviewer already at their ceiling is refused with `ERR_REVIEWER_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409) — an aggregate check, in that each held task is individually legal while the collection may not exceed the ceiling. A claim on a task that is not `QUEUED` reports `ERR_INVALID_TASK_STATE` (HTTP 409). A successful claim writes `assigned_reviewer_id` from `X-User-ID` and `claimed_at` from the logical clock, increments the reviewer's `claimed_task_count` by 1 and the reviewer's `version` by 1, and increments the task's `version` by 1.

Releasing is the exact compensating action for claiming and advances `CLAIMED → QUEUED`. It requires that the task is `CLAIMED` and that `assigned_reviewer_id` equals the acting `X-User-ID`; it clears both `assigned_reviewer_id` and `claimed_at`, decrements the reviewer's `claimed_task_count` by 1 and increments the reviewer's `version` by 1, and increments the task's `version` by 1. Release deliberately does *not* recompute `priority_score` or `sla_deadline_at` — the task returns to its queue holding the position and the deadline it already had, so releasing is not a way to buy time. A release on a task that is not `CLAIMED` reports `ERR_INVALID_TASK_STATE`.

Escalation is the reviewer's way of handing a hard case back, and it is the one decision branch that leaves the creative entirely alone. Choosing the `ESCALATE` resolution requires the task to be `CLAIMED` and held by the acting reviewer; it advances `CLAIMED → ESCALATED`, records `resolution` `ESCALATE`, writes the supplied `note` into `escalation_note`, clears `assigned_reviewer_id` and `claimed_at`, decrements the holder's `claimed_task_count` by 1 and increments the reviewer's `version` by 1, and increments the task's `version` by 1. The creative keeps its `review_status` of `PENDING_MANUAL_REVIEW` and its `version` is *not* incremented, because nothing about the creative changed.

An escalated task returns to circulation only when a `PolicyAdmin` requeues it, which advances `ESCALATED → QUEUED` and requires both that the task is `ESCALATED` and that the creative is still in `PENDING_MANUAL_REVIEW`. Requeueing increments `cycle_count` by 1, resets `resolution` to `PENDING`, recomputes `priority_score` against the advertiser's *current* state rather than the state at original routing, recomputes `sla_deadline_at` as the logical clock plus the primary rule's `review_sla_hours`, and increments the task's `version` by 1. This closes the loop `QUEUED → CLAIMED → ESCALATED → QUEUED`; `cycle_count` counts the laps and is never reset. A requeue attempted on a task that is not `ESCALATED` reports `ERR_INVALID_TASK_STATE`.

Tasks also die of old age. The policy sweep's fourth phase expires every `QUEUED` or `CLAIMED` task whose `sla_deadline_at` has been reached or passed by the logical clock, moving it to the terminal `EXPIRED`, clearing `assigned_reviewer_id` and — when the task was `CLAIMED` — decrementing the holding reviewer's `claimed_task_count` by 1 and incrementing that reviewer's `version` by 1. Only on this SLA path does the sweep create a replacement task: a fresh `QUEUED` task with the same `queue` and the same `primary_policy_rule_id`, whose `cycle_count` is one greater than the expired task's. The other routes to `EXPIRED` — a cascade from creative withdrawal, from an account suspension, or from a revision of a disapproved creative — expire the task without any replacement, because in those cases there is no longer anything to review.

### 3.7. Reviewer Decisions and Their Cascades

A reviewer records a verdict on a task they hold by naming a `resolution`, and the four branches — `APPROVE`, `APPROVE_WITH_LIMITS`, `DISAPPROVE` and `ESCALATE` — take different additional inputs. A `DISAPPROVE` verdict requires `reason_codes`; an `APPROVE_WITH_LIMITS` verdict requires `limited_country_codes`; an `ESCALATE` verdict requires `note`. All three resolving branches — `APPROVE`, `APPROVE_WITH_LIMITS` and `DISAPPROVE` — require that the task is `CLAIMED`, that `assigned_reviewer_id` equals the acting `X-User-ID`, and that the creative's `review_status` is `PENDING_MANUAL_REVIEW`; each advances the task `CLAIMED → RESOLVED`, writes the chosen `resolution`, decrements the holder's `claimed_task_count` by 1, increments the reviewer's `version` by 1, increments the task's `version` by 1, and drives the matching move on the creative's review axis together with that move's full cascade.

Before any of that lands, the compliance gates fire, and they fire in the order §2.10 fixes. The hardest gate is first: an `APPROVE` or `APPROVE_WITH_LIMITS` verdict is refused outright when any rule matched by a qualifying classifier decision of the creative's latest screening run has `category_class` `PROHIBITED`, and equally when the strictest disposition of any matched rule is `BLOCKED` for a country the decision retains — every element of `target_country_codes` under a plain approval, and every element of `limited_country_codes` under a limited approval. Either way the refusal is `ERR_PROHIBITED_CATEGORY_BLOCKED` (HTTP 422). Next, approving a creative whose `political_flag` is true while the owning advertiser's `political_eligibility_status` is anything other than `ELIGIBLE` is refused with `ERR_POLITICAL_ELIGIBILITY_MISSING` (HTTP 422). Next, approving while `disclaimer_text` is null or empty is refused with `ERR_POLITICAL_DISCLOSURE_MISSING` (HTTP 422) whenever disclosure is demanded — that is, whenever `political_flag` is true, or any matched rule has `requires_disclaimer` true, or any retained country's variation on a matched rule has `requires_disclaimer` true. Only after those does licence coverage come under scrutiny, and only then the shape of the retained country set.

`APPROVE` advances `PENDING_MANUAL_REVIEW → AUTO_APPROVED`. Beyond the gates above it demands nothing further: when `political_flag` is true the creative must carry non-empty `disclaimer_text` and the advertiser must be `ELIGIBLE`, and when any matched rule or any applicable country variation has `requires_disclaimer` true the creative must carry non-empty `disclaimer_text`. On success the task becomes `RESOLVED` with `resolution` `APPROVE`, a political creative gains exactly one archive entry, and the creative's `risk_score_final`, `serving_eligible` and `blocking_reason_codes` are recomputed before its `version` increments by 1.

`APPROVE_WITH_LIMITS` advances `PENDING_MANUAL_REVIEW → APPROVED_LIMITED` and is the mechanism for clearing a creative in some countries but not all. Its `limited_country_codes` must be non-empty, must contain no duplicates and must be a subset of the creative's `target_country_codes`; a set that is empty, duplicated or not a subset is refused with `ERR_INVALID_LIMITED_COUNTRIES` (HTTP 422). No country whose strictest disposition is `BLOCKED` may be retained. Every retained country that demands a licence — because a matched rule's `requires_certification` is true, or because that country's variation on a matched rule has `requires_license` true — must have a non-empty coverage candidate set at the logical clock, and a shortfall is refused with `ERR_CERTIFICATION_MISSING` (HTTP 422). The same political disclosure and eligibility demands as a plain approval apply. On success the creative's `limited_country_codes` is written from the request sorted ascending, its `approved_under_certification_id` is written from the coverage selection — the candidate ranked first by `valid_until` descending with ties broken by `created_at` ascending — the task becomes `RESOLVED` with `resolution` `APPROVE_WITH_LIMITS`, a political creative gains exactly one archive entry, and `risk_score_final`, `serving_eligible` and `blocking_reason_codes` are recomputed before the creative's `version` increments by 1.

Approving a political creative writes a **political archive entry**, and that entry is append-only public record. It snapshots the `ad_creative_id` and the `advertiser_id`, copies the creative's `disclaimer_text` into `disclosure_text`, copies the advertiser's `legal_name` into `payer_name` as the payer of record, copies the creative's `headline` into `headline_snapshot`, and records into `target_country_codes_snapshot` the countries the creative was cleared for — the full `target_country_codes` under a plain approval and the `limited_country_codes` under a limited approval. Its `archived_at` is the logical clock and its `retention_until` is `archived_at` plus 2555 whole days, which is exactly seven 365-day years; nothing is ever deleted before that instant, and in fact no endpoint deletes or mutates an archive entry at all. Its `searchable` flag is true and stays true through a later withdrawal or disapproval of the creative. Because entries are never rewritten, a political creative that is approved, withdrawn and approved again accumulates one entry per approval. Archive entries can be listed per creative or per advertiser, in `archived_at` order.

`DISAPPROVE` advances `PENDING_MANUAL_REVIEW → DISAPPROVED` and carries the heaviest cascade in the system. First the supplied `reason_codes` are checked against the creative's current state, and each code has its own precondition: `CLOAKING` demands that `destination_status` is `CLOAKING_CONFIRMED`; `DESTINATION_MISMATCH` demands that `destination_status` is `MISMATCH_FLAGGED`; `POLITICAL_DISCLOSURE` demands that `political_flag` is true; `CERT_MISSING` demands that at least one matched rule has `requires_certification` true; `MANUAL_REVIEW` is always permitted; and `AUTO_SCREENING` is never permitted on a reviewer's decision, since it is reserved for system-issued notices. Any inconsistent code is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REASON_CODE` (HTTP 422). The array must be non-empty and free of duplicates.

Once the reason codes pass, the cascade runs in six hops. Hop one moves the task to `RESOLVED` with `resolution` `DISAPPROVE` and decrements the holding reviewer's `claimed_task_count` by 1. Hop two creates a disapproval notice in status `ISSUED` whose `decided_by_reviewer_id` is the acting reviewer and whose `reason_codes` are the supplied ones. Hop three has that notice write a violation ledger entry with `reason` `DISAPPROVAL_ASSESSED`, `delta_points` equal to the notice's `assessed_points` and `balance_after` set to the advertiser's new raw balance. Hop four recomputes the advertiser's `violation_points_raw`, `violation_points_decayed` and `severity_index` from the whole ledger. Hop five lets the refreshed `severity_index` possibly create an enforcement action, supersede whatever action was previously in force, and re-derive `account_status`. Hop six applies only when that re-derivation lands on `SUSPENDED`: the advertiser's sibling creatives are swept to `SUSPENDED_HOLD`, which expires their `QUEUED` and `CLAIMED` review tasks and decrements the `claimed_task_count` of every reviewer who was holding one. The disapproved creative's own `version` increments by 1, as does the `version` of every other row the cascade touched.

Account suspension is what puts a creative into `SUSPENDED_HOLD`, and the hold is reversible. The sweep moves a creative to `SUSPENDED_HOLD` whenever the owning advertiser's posture has become `SUSPENDED` and the creative's `review_status` is `SCREENING`, `AUTO_APPROVED`, `PENDING_MANUAL_REVIEW` or `APPROVED_LIMITED`; that move expires every `QUEUED` or `CLAIMED` review task on the creative, decrements each holding reviewer's `claimed_task_count`, forces `serving_eligible` to false with `ACCOUNT_ENFORCEMENT` among the `blocking_reason_codes`, and increments the creative's `version` by 1. Once every suspension on the advertiser has left the in-force state, the sweep's eighth phase returns each `SUSPENDED_HOLD` creative to `PENDING_MANUAL_REVIEW` and gives it one fresh `QUEUED` review task. That replacement task starts with `cycle_count` 0 and copies its `queue` and `primary_policy_rule_id` from the creative's most recently created review task whatever that task's status, ranked by `created_at` descending; when the creative has no review task at all, the replacement defaults to the `STANDARD` queue with a null `primary_policy_rule_id`. Its `sla_deadline_at` is the logical clock plus the primary rule's `review_sla_hours`, or plus 48 hours when there is no primary rule, and its `priority_score` is computed as in §3.6. The creative's `version` increments by 1.

### 3.8. Destination and Landing-Page Integrity

The text of an ad is only half the promise; the page behind the click is the other half. An `IntegrityAnalyst`, and nobody else, records a **destination check** against a creative, reporting what a fetch actually found. The submission names the `check_type`, either `INITIAL` for a first pass or `RECHECK` for a later re-examination; the `user_agent_variant` the fetch used, either `CRAWLER` or `USER`, whose divergence is the cloaking signal; the `fetched_final_url` the fetch finally resolved to after redirects, which must begin with the literal text `https://` and contain a host segment; the `fetched_content_category` classified from the fetched page, of 1 to 40 characters; and the `http_status` of the final response, an integer from 100 to 599. The stored check also carries the `ad_creative_id` it concerns. A `fetched_content_category` that is neither the literal `GENERAL` nor the `code` of an existing policy rule is refused with `ERR_UNKNOWN_POLICY_CATEGORY` (HTTP 422). A check recorded against a creative whose `review_status` is `DRAFT` or `WITHDRAWN` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CREATIVE_STATE`. A creative's checks are listed in `created_at` order.

Two derived fields are written on the check: `result`, one of `CLEARED`, `MISMATCH` or `CLOAKING`, and `mismatch_detail`, which names the clause that produced a non-cleared result and is null when the result is `CLEARED`. Deriving them needs two notions. The **host** of a URL is the text lying between the first occurrence of the colon-slash-slash separator and the next forward slash, taken to the end of the string when no further forward slash follows, and compared only after lowercasing — comparison is exact, with no substring or suffix matching. The **reference crawler check** is the most recent earlier check on this same creative whose `user_agent_variant` is `CRAWLER`, ranked by `created_at` descending, with ties broken first by the greater `http_status` and then by the lexicographically greater `fetched_final_url`; identifiers never break the tie, and the reference is simply absent when the creative has no crawler check yet. Checks recorded on other creatives never participate.

Five clauses are then evaluated in order, and the first one that matches decides the outcome. First, the result is `MISMATCH` with `mismatch_detail` `DEST_UNREACHABLE` when the check's `http_status` is 400 or greater. Second, the result is `CLOAKING` with `mismatch_detail` `UA_DIVERGENCE` when this check's `user_agent_variant` is `USER`, a reference crawler check exists, and the host of that reference check's `fetched_final_url` differs from the host of this check's `fetched_final_url` — the classic bait-and-switch, where the crawler and the human are shown different destinations. Third, the result is `MISMATCH` with `mismatch_detail` `HOST_MISMATCH` when the host of the check's `fetched_final_url` differs from the host of the creative's `display_url`, which is the declared reference host. Fourth, the result is `MISMATCH` with `mismatch_detail` `CATEGORY_MISMATCH` when the `fetched_content_category` is not the literal `GENERAL` and is also not an element of the creative's `declared_category_codes`. Fifth and otherwise, the result is `CLEARED` with a null `mismatch_detail`.

The destination axis then moves according to the result. From `DESTINATION_PENDING`, a `CLEARED` result advances `DESTINATION_PENDING → DESTINATION_CLEARED` and a `MISMATCH` result advances `DESTINATION_PENDING → MISMATCH_FLAGGED`. From `DESTINATION_CLEARED`, a later `MISMATCH` reopens the question with `DESTINATION_CLEARED → MISMATCH_FLAGGED`, and a `RECHECK` yielding `CLEARED` closes it again with `MISMATCH_FLAGGED → DESTINATION_CLEARED`. The destination axis is therefore genuinely cyclic: a destination that was cleared can be caught mismatching later and cleared again afterwards, any number of times. A `CLOAKING` result advances `DESTINATION_CLEARED → CLOAKING_CONFIRMED`, and that move additionally requires a prior `CRAWLER` check to exist on the creative — without a crawler baseline there is nothing for a `USER` fetch to diverge from. Every one of these moves rewrites `risk_score_final` with the destination penalty appropriate to the new `destination_status` — 15.00 once `MISMATCH_FLAGGED`, 40.00 once `CLOAKING_CONFIRMED`, and 0.00 once `DESTINATION_CLEARED` — recomputes `serving_eligible` and `blocking_reason_codes`, and increments the creative's `version` by 1.

The review axis reacts to destination findings, but selectively. A `MISMATCH` re-routes the review axis only when the creative was `AUTO_APPROVED` or `APPROVED_LIMITED`, in which case the creative moves to `PENDING_MANUAL_REVIEW` and gains one new `QUEUED` review task in the `STANDARD` queue with `cycle_count` 0. A creative already sitting in `PENDING_MANUAL_REVIEW` keeps the review task it already has and gains no second one, and a creative in `DISAPPROVED` or `SUSPENDED_HOLD` has its review axis left alone entirely.

A `CLOAKING` result is treated as proven bad faith and runs its own cascade from any of `AUTO_APPROVED`, `APPROVED_LIMITED` or `PENDING_MANUAL_REVIEW`. Hop one sets `destination_status` to `CLOAKING_CONFIRMED`. Hop two forces `review_status` to `DISAPPROVED` and creates a disapproval notice whose `reason_codes` is exactly `[CLOAKING]` and whose `decided_by_reviewer_id` is null, since no human decided it. Hop three has that notice write a violation ledger entry with `reason` `DISAPPROVAL_ASSESSED`. Hop four recomputes the advertiser's `violation_points_raw`, `violation_points_decayed` and `severity_index`. Hop five lets the refreshed `severity_index` possibly create an enforcement action and re-derive `account_status`. Alongside the hops, every `QUEUED` or `CLAIMED` review task on the creative is expired and each holding reviewer's `claimed_task_count` is decremented. The creative's `version` increments by 1.

### 3.9. Disapproval Notices, Appeals, the Violation Ledger and Account Enforcement

Every disapproval, whether decided by a reviewer or forced by the system, produces a **disapproval notice** — the document the advertiser is owed. A notice records the `ad_creative_id` it concerns and the `decided_by_reviewer_id` of the reviewer who disapproved, which is null for the two system-issued paths (an automatic screening disapproval and a cloaking confirmation). Its `reason_codes` is a non-empty duplicate-free array drawn from `AUTO_SCREENING`, `MANUAL_REVIEW`, `DESTINATION_MISMATCH`, `CLOAKING`, `CERT_MISSING` and `POLITICAL_DISCLOSURE`. Its `policy_rule_codes` holds the `code` values of the findings carried into the notice, sorted ascending. Its `political_at_issue` snapshots the creative's `political_flag` at the moment of issue. Its `status` runs over `ISSUED`, `APPEALED`, `APPEAL_GRANTED`, `APPEAL_UPHELD` and `EXPIRED`, of which the last three are terminal, and it begins at `ISSUED`. Notices can be listed filtered by `ad_creative_id`, by `decided_by_reviewer_id` or by `status`, and a single notice read returns its assessment lines, reason codes and appeal deadline.

The points a notice assesses are computed in two phases, and the *reason source* is decided first: the source reason is `AUTO_SCREENING` when the notice's `decided_by_reviewer_id` is null, and `MANUAL_REVIEW` otherwise. Phase one writes one finding line per qualifying classifier decision of the creative's latest screening run — qualifying meaning the decision's `disposition` is `AUTO_DISAPPROVE` or `ROUTE_MANUAL`, so `BELOW_THRESHOLD` rows are excluded and earlier runs are ignored entirely — taken in ascending order of the matched rule's `code`. Each finding line records the matched rule's code as its `policy_rule_code`, the source reason as its `reason_code`, and as its `line_points` the matched rule's `base_severity_weight` multiplied by the country multiplier of that rule for this creative and then multiplied by the reason factor of the source reason, rounded half-up to 2 decimals. Confidence does *not* enter the assessment at all; it belongs only to risk scoring.

Phase two writes one integrity line for each non-finding reason code the notice carries, taken in the fixed sequence `CERT_MISSING`, then `CLOAKING`, then `DESTINATION_MISMATCH`, then `POLITICAL_DISCLOSURE`, and skipping any of those four the notice does not carry. Each integrity line has a null `policy_rule_code`, the reason code itself as its `reason_code`, and as its `line_points` an integrity base weight of 10.00 multiplied by the creative's strictest multiplier and then multiplied by that reason code's reason factor, rounded half-up to 2 decimals. The reason factors are fixed: 0.80 for `AUTO_SCREENING`, 1.00 for `MANUAL_REVIEW`, 0.60 for `CERT_MISSING`, 2.00 for `CLOAKING`, 1.25 for `DESTINATION_MISMATCH` and 1.50 for `POLITICAL_DISCLOSURE`.

The notice's `assessment_lines` is phase one's lines followed by phase two's, in exactly that order, and its `assessed_points` is the sum of the `line_points` of every line, capped at 100.00. The rounding order here is pinned and is the opposite of the risk-score order: each line is rounded to 2 decimals **before** the sum, and the already-rounded lines are then added — summing unrounded products and rounding once at the end is not what this rule does, and the two orders can differ by a cent. The notice also records `disclosed_points_total`, which is the advertiser's `violation_points_raw` immediately after this notice's ledger entry has landed, so the advertiser sees the balance the notice actually produced.

Every notice opens an appeal window. Its `appeal_window_hours` is 168 hours — seven days — when `political_at_issue` is true, and 72 hours — three days — otherwise; its `appeal_deadline_at` is the notice's `created_at` plus that many whole hours. An appeal is admissible only while the logical clock is *strictly* before `appeal_deadline_at`: at the deadline instant the window is already shut, and an attempt then or later is refused with `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_CLOSED` (HTTP 409). The sweep's third phase moves an `ISSUED` notice whose `appeal_deadline_at` has been reached or passed to `EXPIRED`, which changes no ledger row and no advertiser field — it simply makes the disapproval final — and increments the notice's `version` by 1.

Filing an appeal is the owning advertiser's act. The request names the `disapproval_notice_id` under challenge, a `justification` of 10 to 2000 characters, and optionally an `evidence_certification_id` offering a certification as evidence, which defaults to null; when supplied, that certification's `advertiser_id` must equal the advertiser behind the appealed notice's creative, and a mismatch is refused with `ERR_CERTIFICATION_OWNER_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). At most one appeal may exist per notice: a second attempt is refused with `ERR_APPEAL_ALREADY_EXISTS` (HTTP 409), and as §2.10 explains that duplicate check runs *ahead* of the notice-state check precisely so the second attempt is reported as a duplicate rather than as an un-appealable notice. The notice must be `ISSUED`; one already `APPEALED`, `APPEAL_GRANTED`, `APPEAL_UPHELD` or `EXPIRED` is refused with `ERR_NOTICE_NOT_APPEALABLE` (HTTP 409). A successful filing creates the appeal in status `SUBMITTED` with a null `decided_by_reviewer_id`, a null `decided_at` and a null `reinstated_review_status`, and advances the notice `ISSUED → APPEALED` with its `version` incremented by 1. Appeals can be listed filtered by `disapproval_notice_id`, by `evidence_certification_id`, by `decided_by_reviewer_id` or by `status`.

Adjudication is a `PolicyReviewer` act and it forks. The request names the `outcome`, either `GRANTED` or `DENIED`, and may carry a `note`. Both branches require that the appeal's `status` is `SUBMITTED` and its notice's `status` is `APPEALED` — otherwise `ERR_APPEAL_NOT_PENDING` (HTTP 409) — and both require that the acting reviewer's identifier differs from the notice's `decided_by_reviewer_id`, since no reviewer may sit in judgement on their own disapproval; a self-adjudication attempt is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Both branches write `decided_by_reviewer_id` and set `decided_at` to the logical clock, and both increment the appeal's `version` by 1. Beyond that the branches diverge deliberately.

A `GRANTED` outcome advances the appeal `SUBMITTED → GRANTED` and runs a five-hop reinstatement. Hop one moves the notice `APPEALED → APPEAL_GRANTED`. Hop two writes a compensating violation ledger entry with `reason` `APPEAL_REVERSAL` and `delta_points` equal to the negative of the notice's `assessed_points`. Hop three recomputes the advertiser's `violation_points_raw`, `violation_points_decayed` and `severity_index`. Hop four moves any enforcement action currently in force to `LIFTED` when the refreshed `severity_index` has fallen below the threshold of that action's own `action_type`, re-deriving `account_status`. Hop five reinstates the creative `DISAPPROVED → PENDING_MANUAL_REVIEW`, which additionally requires that the creative's `review_status` is indeed `DISAPPROVED`. The reinstatement resets `destination_status` to `DESTINATION_PENDING` when it stood at `MISMATCH_FLAGGED` or `CLOAKING_CONFIRMED`, rewrites `risk_score_final` with a destination penalty of 0.00, and writes `PENDING_MANUAL_REVIEW` into the appeal's `reinstated_review_status`. It also creates one fresh review task in status `QUEUED` with `cycle_count` 0, copying its `queue` and `primary_policy_rule_id` from the creative's most recently created review task whatever that task's status, ranked by `created_at` descending, and defaulting to the `STANDARD` queue with a null `primary_policy_rule_id` when the creative has no review task at all; its `sla_deadline_at` is the logical clock plus the primary rule's `review_sla_hours`, or plus 48 hours when there is no primary rule, and its `priority_score` is computed as in §3.6. The creative's `version` increments by 1.

A `DENIED` outcome advances the appeal `SUBMITTED → DENIED` and moves the notice `APPEALED → APPEAL_UPHELD`, and it does deliberately less. No ledger entry is written, the advertiser's `violation_points_raw` is unchanged, the creative stays `DISAPPROVED`, the destination axis is untouched, enforcement is untouched, and `reinstated_review_status` stays null. The asymmetry is the point: granting an appeal compensates the ledger, denying one does not.

The **violation ledger** is where all of this is written down. Each entry records the `advertiser_id` whose balance moved, the `source_notice_id` that produced the delta, the signed non-zero `delta_points` — positive on an assessment, negative on an appeal reversal — the `reason`, either `DISAPPROVAL_ASSESSED` or `APPEAL_REVERSAL`, the `balance_after` giving the advertiser's `violation_points_raw` immediately after applying this delta, and an `entry_index` that is a 1-based per-advertiser insertion counter, contiguous, and that defines the chronological replay order. Entries are append-only and carry no status, so nothing is ever filtered out of a replay. A ledger can be listed per advertiser in `entry_index` order, and also per notice, which surfaces that notice's assessment together with its reversal if one exists.

Two balances are replayed from the ledger in ascending `entry_index` order, and they answer different questions. The raw balance, stored as `violation_points_raw` and disclosed to the advertiser through a notice's `disclosed_points_total`, is the sum of every entry's `delta_points` at full weight forever, rounded half-up to 2 decimals and floored at 0.00. The decayed balance, stored as `violation_points_decayed`, weights each entry by its own age: compute the entry's age in whole days by taking the difference between the logical clock and the entry's `created_at` in seconds, dividing by 86400 and discarding the fraction; the entry then counts at full weight while its age is under 90 days, at half weight once its age is at least 90 days but still under 180 days, and at zero weight once its age is 180 days or more. Multiply each entry's `delta_points` by its own weight, sum the unrounded products, round the single total half-up to 2 decimals and floor it at 0.00. Because each entry ages independently, a compensating `APPEAL_REVERSAL` entry written today counts at full weight even when the `DISAPPROVAL_ASSESSED` entry it reverses has already decayed to half or zero — so a late reversal can drive the decayed balance to 0.00 through the floor while the raw balance remains positive. The decayed balance is refreshed by every ledger insert and again by the sweep's sixth phase, and it is the only one of the two that can create or lift an enforcement action.

The **severity index** turns the decayed balance into an enforcement decision, in four steps whose order is pinned and non-iterative. Step one computes a base index: count the advertiser's enforcement actions whose `status` is `SUPERSEDED`, `LIFTED` or `EXPIRED` — actions still in force are excluded, so a currently effective action never inflates its own successor's index — multiply that closed-action count by 5.00, add the advertiser's `violation_points_decayed`, and round the total half-up to 2 decimals. Step two picks an escalation surcharge keyed on the base index *alone*: 0.00 when the base index is below 20.00, 3.50 when it is at least 20.00 but below 35.00, and 9.00 when it is 35.00 or more. Step three sets `severity_index` to the base index plus that surcharge, rounded half-up to 2 decimals. Step four selects an action type keyed on the post-surcharge `severity_index`: `SUSPENSION` when it is at least 40.00, otherwise `RESTRICTION` when it is at least 25.00, otherwise `WARNING` when it is at least 10.00, and otherwise none at all. The tier chosen in step two is never re-chosen after the surcharge is added, even when the finished index lands in a higher tier — that re-evaluation is expressly not part of this rule.

An **enforcement action** is created from that selection only when an action type was selected and either no action is currently in force on the advertiser or the selected type is strictly more severe than the type of the action in force, severity running warning, then restriction, then suspension. An action records the `advertiser_id` under enforcement, the `action_type` — one of `WARNING`, `RESTRICTION` or `SUSPENSION`, listed here in ascending order of severity — the `origin` — `SYSTEM_CASCADE` when raised by a cascade and `MANUAL` when minted by an operator — the free-text `trigger_reason` of 1 to 300 characters, the `severity_index_at_issue` capturing the index at the moment of creation, the `effective_from` instant taken from the logical clock, the `effective_until` instant ending the window, and a `status` of `ACTIVE`, `LIFTED`, `SUPERSEDED` or `EXPIRED` beginning at `ACTIVE` with the last three terminal. Its `prior_action_id` points at the action it supersedes and is null for the first action on an account. Its `source_notice_id` names the notice whose ledger entry triggered it and is null for a manual action. When `effective_until` is not supplied it defaults to `effective_from` plus the duration for the type: 30 days for a warning, 60 days for a restriction and 180 days for a suspension. Creating an action moves any existing `ACTIVE` action on the advertiser to `SUPERSEDED`, points the new action's `prior_action_id` at it, re-derives `account_status` and increments the advertiser's `version` by 1; a `SUSPENSION` additionally sweeps the advertiser's creatives to `SUSPENDED_HOLD` and expires their review tasks. A `SUPERSEDED` action begins counting toward the closed-action count from that moment on, and its own `version` increments by 1.

A `PolicyAdmin` may also mint an action by hand, supplying `action_type`, `trigger_reason`, optionally `effective_until`, optionally `prior_action_id`, and the required `expected_advertiser_version` of §2.7. A manual action is additionally blocked by a cooldown: the new `effective_from` — the logical clock — may not fall inside the blackout window of any existing action on the advertiser, where that window runs from that action's `effective_from` through its `effective_until` extended by a further 72 hours, inclusive at both ends, and where existing actions in every status — `ACTIVE`, `LIFTED`, `EXPIRED` and `SUPERSEDED` — are considered. A collision is refused with `ERR_ENFORCEMENT_COOLDOWN_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409).

Actions leave force in three ways. An action is `LIFTED` when the severity index has fallen strictly below the threshold for its own `action_type` — 10.00 for a `WARNING`, 25.00 for a `RESTRICTION`, 40.00 for a `SUSPENSION` — which happens either at the moment an appeal is granted or during the sweep's seventh phase after the decay refresh; lifting re-derives `account_status`, increments the advertiser's `version` by 1, increments the action's `version` by 1, and a lifted suspension is what allows the sweep to return `SUSPENDED_HOLD` creatives to `PENDING_MANUAL_REVIEW`. An action is `EXPIRED` by the sweep's fifth phase once its `effective_until` has been reached or passed, which likewise re-derives `account_status` and bumps both versions. An action is `SUPERSEDED` when a strictly more severe action is created on the same advertiser. All three terminal statuses count toward the closed-action count from the moment they are reached, which is why a repeat offender's index climbs by 5.00 for every enforcement chapter already closed on their account. Enforcement actions can be listed filtered by `advertiser_id`, by `prior_action_id`, by `source_notice_id` or by `status`, and a single action read returns `severity_index_at_issue` and its window.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Operations that bring a new resource into existence — a policy rule, a country variation, an advertiser, an enforcement action, a reviewer, a creative, a screening run, a destination check, a certification or an appeal — return `201 Created`. Operations that modify an existing resource, including every state transition, every verification recording, the clock advance and the policy sweep, return `200 OK`. Every request carries `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role`; every list operation additionally accepts `limit` and `offset`. Request payloads and response shapes are not repeated here — they are specified in the §2 and §3 narratives.
