# Tower Colocation Master Lease API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A communications tower company does not sell square feet; it sells structural capacity, elevation and compound ground space on assets it usually does not own the dirt under. Every dollar of colocation revenue therefore sits on top of two stacked contracts — a ground lease with the landlord who owns the site, and a master lease agreement with the carrier who hangs equipment on the structure — and every technical decision (can this antenna go on at 180 feet? does the tower need reinforcing first? who pays for the reinforcement?) is also a legal and a financial decision. Today those three views live in three different places: the structural engineers keep loading spreadsheets, the lease administrators keep amendment binders, and the revenue team keeps a receivables ledger that nobody reconciles against either.

This document specifies the API for a single system of record that closes that gap. It manages the tower asset inventory and its live structural picture, the ground leases and landlord remittances underneath each site, the carrier master leases and their site-level amendments, the colocation application pipeline with its structural-review service-level commitments, structural modification projects and the cost sharing that funds them, compliance audits that surface unauthorized installations and bill them retroactively, and finally tenant departure with its site-restoration obligations. The unifying idea is that a tower has one authoritative capacity posture, derived from what is actually standing on the structure plus what has been contractually promised, and that this posture gates every downstream operation — a submission may enter review or be diverted to a capacity hold, an amendment may or may not be executable, and a held application may be promoted the instant capacity is released.

The system is deliberately conservative about two things. First, money: all amounts are integer cents, all rounding is explicit and the order of operations in every escalation and allocation formula is pinned so that two implementations cannot drift apart by a cent. Second, time: the server runs on an operator-settable logical clock rather than wall-clock time, because rent anniversaries, review deadlines, renewal windows, construction windows and lease expiries all have to be reproducible.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

All endpoints described in this document are mounted under the `/tower-colo` prefix.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that principal is acting in. Five roles exist, spelled exactly as follows: `PortfolioAdmin`, `StructuralEngineer`, `ComplianceAuditor`, `Tenant`, and `Landlord`. If either header is absent — an `X-User-ID` that is empty or consists only of whitespace counts as absent — or if `X-User-Role` is not one of those five strings, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401) before anything else is examined. If both headers are well formed but the acting role is not among the roles listed for the requested endpoint in §4, the rejection is `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403).

Three of the five roles are **operator roles** whose identity is opaque. When `X-User-Role` is `PortfolioAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server never compares it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `StructuralEngineer`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier; the server records it — as `engineer_user_id` on the `StructuralAnalysis` it creates, as `proposing_engineer_user_id` on the `ModificationProject` it proposes, and as `certified_by_user_id` on the project it certifies — and it compares those recorded values against each other to enforce segregation of duties, but it never resolves any of them to an entity row. When `X-User-Role` is `ComplianceAuditor`, `X-User-ID` is again an opaque operator identifier, recorded as `auditor_user_id` on the `EquipmentAudit` that operator schedules and not compared to any entity's `id`.

The other two roles are **entity-backed**. When `X-User-Role` is `Tenant`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Tenant` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Tenant` compare the resource's tenant foreign key to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `Landlord`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Landlord` row; ownership checks compare a ground lease's `landlord_id` to `X-User-ID`. For both of these roles, if `X-User-ID` does not resolve to an existing row of the backing entity, the request fails with `ERR_IDENTITY_NOT_FOUND` (403).

Scope enforcement follows resolution. A `Tenant` may act only on resources whose owning tenant equals `X-User-ID` — for colocation applications, equipment lines and site amendments the owning tenant is the `tenant_id` of the governing master lease agreement; for cost shares it is the cost share's own `tenant_id`. A violation yields `ERR_TENANT_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (403). A `Landlord` may read only ground leases whose `landlord_id` equals `X-User-ID`; a violation yields `ERR_LANDLORD_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (403). This scope check applies to exactly eleven operations: creating a colocation application, adding an equipment line to an application, deleting an equipment line, submitting an application, revising an application, withdrawing an application, giving termination notice on a site amendment, accepting a cost share, declining a cost share, applying a tenant payment, and listing ground leases. No other operation is scope-checked: every single-resource read, and every list endpoint other than the ground-lease list, is open to any principal whose role appears in that endpoint's role list, so a `Tenant` listing applications, equipment lines, amendments, cost shares or audit findings sees every row rather than only its own, and a `Landlord` may read any single ground lease and any lease's landlord-payment list. On the ground-lease list itself, a `Landlord` that supplies a `landlord_id` query filter naming some other landlord is rejected with `ERR_LANDLORD_SCOPE_VIOLATION`, while a `Landlord` that omits the filter simply has the page narrowed to its own leases.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. It is taken from the host's real wall clock, not from the logical clock of §2.4, and it is the one timestamp in the system that does not read the logical clock.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints are paginated through two query parameters. The `limit` parameter caps the number of items returned; it defaults to 10, has a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100. The `offset` parameter is the number of items to skip and defaults to 0, with no upper bound but no negative values allowed. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (400); a `limit` or `offset` whose text is not a plain integer at all (`abc`, `10.5`) fails the earlier request-shape check instead, with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400).

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list endpoints return their items sorted by `created_at` ascending. Two endpoints override this: the payment list for a ground lease is ordered by `period_start_date` ascending, and the analysis list for a colocation application is ordered by `created_at` descending, newest first. Because the logical clock lets many rows share one `created_at`, every ordering in this document — these list orders, the reconciliation and promotion rankings of §2.7 and §3.5, the cost-share remainder ranking of §3.6, and the selection of a "newest" or "earliest" record anywhere — breaks remaining ties by the server's own monotonic creation sequence, so records created earlier in the same instant sort earlier. Filter query parameters that name an identifier are matched literally: a well-formed but unknown `tower_id`, `tenant_id`, `master_lease_agreement_id`, `colocation_application_id`, `installed_amendment_id`, `supersedes_amendment_id`, `landlord_id`, `modification_project_id`, `site_amendment_id`, `originating_analysis_id` or `equipment_audit_id` yields an empty page with `total_count` 0, never a 404, whereas an enum-valued filter (`status`, `execution_status`, `billing_status`, `capacity_posture`, `structure_type`) that is not a declared member is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Every list response includes `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` in the `meta` object of the envelope.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties, the Logical Clock and Time-Driven Expiry

Every resource carries an `id`, which is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at` timestamp, which is an ISO 8601 UTC instant rendered with three fractional digits. Almost every resource additionally carries an `updated_at` timestamp with the same format, refreshed on any mutation of that resource, and a `version` integer that starts at 1 when the resource is created and increments by one on every mutation. The full set of resources carrying all four properties is: towers, landlords, ground leases, tenants, master lease agreements, colocation applications, equipment lines, structural analyses, site amendments, modification projects, cost shares, equipment audits and audit findings. The single exception is the landlord payment record, which is immutable once written: a `LandlordPayment` carries only `id` and `created_at`, and both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from it entirely. Calendar-date fields — `commencement_date`, `term_end_date`, `paid_through_date`, `period_start_date`, `period_end_date`, `expires_on`, `effective_date`, `construction_window_start_date`, `construction_window_end_date`, `audit_date` and `installed_on_date` — are echoed back exactly as the caller rendered them on the request that set them, and are rendered as bare `YYYY-MM-DD` only when the server itself derived the value; a date supplied as a full instant is therefore read back as that same instant string.

Recomputation of a derived field counts as a mutation. When the system recalculates a tower's occupancy after an amendment is executed, or recalculates a tenant's balance after a finding is billed, that resource's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` is incremented.

**The logical clock.** Every deadline, anniversary, expiry, construction window and date comparison in this specification reads a server-held logical clock rather than the host's wall clock. On a cold start the clock is fixed at `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and the implementation must never fall back to real time. A `PortfolioAdmin` may advance the clock by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant; the requested instant must be strictly after the current logical clock instant, otherwise the request is rejected with `ERR_CLOCK_NOT_MONOTONIC` (422). The response to a clock advance is not a stored entity but a summary object with four members: `current_utc` (the new clock instant), `expired_ground_lease_count`, `expired_master_lease_count` and `frozen_tower_count`. The first two are the number of ground leases and of master leases that this sweep actually moved to `EXPIRED`; `frozen_tower_count` is the number of towers whose `capacity_posture` is `FROZEN` after the sweep and was something other than `FROZEN` immediately before it, so a tower that was already frozen is not counted again.

Advancing the clock runs two expiry sweeps. First, every ground lease whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `RENEWAL_NEGOTIATION` and whose `term_end_date` falls before the new instant moves `ACTIVE → EXPIRED` or `RENEWAL_NEGOTIATION → EXPIRED`. Because an expired ground lease means the tower company no longer has the right to be on the site, this cascades: the tower's `capacity_posture` is recomputed to `FROZEN`; because the posture became `FROZEN`, every colocation application on that tower whose `status` is `UNDER_STRUCTURAL_REVIEW` moves `UNDER_STRUCTURAL_REVIEW → CAPACITY_HOLD` and the tower's `open_review_count` consequently drops to 0; and the tower's `version` is incremented by one. Second, every master lease agreement whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `expires_on` falls before the new instant moves `ACTIVE → EXPIRED`, its `application_privilege` is set to `SUSPENDED`, and every application under it whose `status` is `SUBMITTED` or `UNDER_STRUCTURAL_REVIEW` moves to `ON_HOLD`.

### 2.5. Path-Scoped and Body-Scoped Resource Resolution

When a path contains more than one identifier, the system resolves them strictly left to right and rejects on the first one that does not exist, with the 404 code belonging to that resource type: `ERR_TOWER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_GROUND_LEASE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TENANT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MLA_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EQUIPMENT_LINE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ANALYSIS_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AMENDMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROJECT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COST_SHARE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AUDIT_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_FINDING_NOT_FOUND`. A finding recorded under a non-existent audit fails with `ERR_AUDIT_NOT_FOUND` before the system considers anything about the finding itself.

Foreign keys supplied in a request body are resolved after the path but before any business rule, and they are resolved in the order the fields are listed for that endpoint in §4's request contract. The applicable codes are `ERR_TOWER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LANDLORD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TENANT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MLA_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ANALYSIS_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AMENDMENT_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_GROUND_LEASE_NOT_FOUND`. A request that names both an unknown tower and an unknown landlord surfaces the tower error, because `tower_id` is listed first.

### 2.6. Validation Precedence

Because a single request routinely trips more than one rule, the order in which rules are evaluated is part of the contract. Checks are evaluated in the sequence below, and the first failure is the response; later checks are never reached. Where a check is scoped to particular operations, that scope is exhaustive.

1. Authentication — both headers present and `X-User-Role` a declared role → `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). All endpoints.
2. Role authorization — the acting role appears in the endpoint's role list → `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403). All endpoints.
3. Request shape — body and query parameters parse, required fields present, enum members valid, numeric ranges and the per-field constraints described in §3 satisfied → `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). All endpoints.
4. Pagination bounds → `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (400). All list endpoints.
5. Path resources exist, left to right → the matching 404 code from §2.5. All endpoints with path parameters.
6. Body foreign keys exist, in declaration order → the matching 404 code from §2.5. All creating endpoints that carry foreign keys.
7. Entity-backed identity resolves → `ERR_IDENTITY_NOT_FOUND` (403). All endpoints whose role list includes `Tenant` or `Landlord`.
8. Ownership scope → `ERR_TENANT_SCOPE_VIOLATION` or `ERR_LANDLORD_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (403), on the eleven operations enumerated in §2.1.
9. Optimistic concurrency → `ERR_STALE_TOWER_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_PROJECT_VERSION` (412), on structural-analysis creation and cost-share reallocation respectively.
10. The target resource is in a state from which the requested transition is declared → `ERR_INVALID_APPLICATION_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_AMENDMENT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_BILLING_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_PROJECT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_LEASE_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_AUDIT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_COST_SHARE_STATE` or `ERR_INVALID_ANALYSIS_STATE` (409). All state-changing endpoints.
11. The application's master lease agreement is `ACTIVE` → `ERR_MLA_NOT_ACTIVE` (409), on application submit and application revise. This same code, rather than any `ERR_INVALID_*_STATE` code, also carries the lifecycle-state rejection of the master lease agreement's own two transitions: activating a master lease that is not `DRAFT`, and reinstating a master lease that is not `ACTIVE`. `ERR_INVALID_LEASE_STATE` is reserved for the ground lease.
12. The master lease agreement's `application_privilege` is `ENABLED` → `ERR_MLA_PRIVILEGE_SUSPENDED` (409), on application submit and application revise.
13. The amendment's tower has a ground lease in `ACTIVE` status → `ERR_GROUND_LEASE_NOT_ACTIVE` (409), on amendment execution.
14. The tower's `capacity_posture` is not `FROZEN` → `ERR_TOWER_POSTURE_FROZEN` (409), on four operations: amendment execution, application approval, application revision, and structural-analysis creation.
15. Post-execution structural loading is strictly below the capacity limit → `ERR_STRUCTURAL_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (422), on amendment execution.
16. Structural clearance exists for the amendment's application → `ERR_STRUCTURAL_CLEARANCE_MISSING` (422), on amendment execution.
17. Segregation of duties on certification → `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403), on project completion certification.
18. Elevation-band separation against occupied bands → `ERR_ELEVATION_BAND_CONFLICT` (409), on amendment execution.
19. The requested band lies inside the tower's usable elevation range → `ERR_ELEVATION_OUT_OF_RANGE` (422), on application submit.
20. The application has at least one equipment line → `ERR_NO_EQUIPMENT_LINES` (422), on application submit and application revise.
21. Ground space is available → `ERR_GROUND_SPACE_UNAVAILABLE` (422), on amendment execution.
22. No construction-window conflict on the tower → `ERR_CONSTRUCTION_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409), on project proposal and on cost-share allocation.
23. The amendment term does not outlast the ground lease term → `ERR_TERM_EXCEEDS_GROUND_LEASE` (422), on amendment execution.
24. The renewal window is open → `ERR_RENEWAL_WINDOW_CLOSED` (422), on opening a ground-lease renewal.
25. A renewal option remains → `ERR_NO_RENEWAL_OPTIONS_REMAINING` (422), on opening a renewal and on exercising a renewal.
26. The payment period does not re-cover paid ground → `ERR_PAYMENT_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (409), on landlord payment creation.
27. The payment period is contiguous with what has been paid → `ERR_PAYMENT_PERIOD_GAP` (422), on landlord payment creation.
28. The payment period spans whole calendar months → `ERR_PAYMENT_PERIOD_NOT_WHOLE_MONTHS` (422), on landlord payment creation.
29. The audit date is not in the future → `ERR_AUDIT_DATE_IN_FUTURE` (422), on audit scheduling.
30. The finding's installation date is not after the audit date → `ERR_FINDING_DATE_AFTER_AUDIT` (422), on recording a finding.
31. The supersede target is valid → `ERR_SUPERSEDE_TARGET_INVALID` (422), on amendment creation.
32. The cost-share beneficiary set is usable → `ERR_COST_SHARE_BENEFICIARY_INVALID` (422), on cost-share allocation and reallocation.
33. The landlord has tax documentation on file → `ERR_LANDLORD_W9_MISSING` (422), on landlord payment creation.
34. The acting or owning tenant is not delinquent → `ERR_TENANT_DELINQUENT` (402), on four operations: application submit, application approval, amendment execution, and master-lease reinstatement.
35. A buyout right exists → `ERR_BUYOUT_NOT_AVAILABLE` (422), on ground-lease buyout.
36. The tenant payment amount is positive → `ERR_INVALID_PAYMENT_AMOUNT` (422), on tenant payment.
37. The originating analysis carries a quotation → `ERR_QUOTATION_MISSING` (422), on project proposal and on cost-share allocation.
38. The clock advance is monotonic → `ERR_CLOCK_NOT_MONOTONIC` (422), on setting the logical clock.

Two consequences of this ladder are worth naming because they are easy to get backwards. On amendment execution, the ground-lease status check precedes the frozen-posture check, which precedes the structural-capacity check, which precedes the structural-clearance check, which precedes the elevation-band check, which precedes the ground-space check, which precedes the term check, which finally precedes the delinquency check — so an amendment that would fail four of these at once reports the earliest one in that order. On landlord payment creation, the overlap check precedes the contiguity check, which precedes the whole-months check, which precedes the tax-documentation check.

### 2.7. Tower Capacity Reconciliation

A tower's occupancy is never stored by increment; it is always recomputed as a pure function of the amendments standing on it. The reconciliation runs whenever a site amendment's `execution_status` changes, whenever a ground lease's `status` changes, whenever a modification project's `status` changes, and whenever `rated_capacity_load_units` changes.

The contributing set is every site amendment on the tower whose `execution_status` is either `EXECUTED` or `TERMINATION_NOTICED`. An amendment that has merely given notice is still physically bolted to the structure, so it keeps holding load, ground space and its elevation band until decommissioning completes. Amendments in `DRAFT`, `PENDING_SIGNATURE`, `VOIDED` or `TERMINATED` contribute nothing.

From that set:

- `reserved_load_units` is `baseline_reserved_load_units` plus the sum of `reserved_load_units` over the contributing amendments.
- `reserved_ground_space_sqft` is `baseline_reserved_ground_sqft` plus the sum of `ground_space_sqft` over the contributing amendments.
- `available_ground_space_sqft` is `ground_space_total_sqft` less `reserved_ground_space_sqft`.
- `reserved_load_pct` is one hundred times `reserved_load_units` divided by `rated_capacity_load_units`, rounded half up to three decimal places.
- `occupied_elevation_bands` is an array with one entry per contributing amendment, each entry carrying `amendment_id`, `bottom_ft`, `top_ft` and `tenant_id`, ordered by `bottom_ft` ascending and, where two bands start at the same elevation, by the amendment's `created_at` ascending.
- `open_review_count` is the number of colocation applications on the tower whose `status` is `UNDER_STRUCTURAL_REVIEW`.

Because reserved quantities are a pure function of the contributing set rather than a running total, they are a conserved quantity: executing an amendment and then completing its decommissioning restores `reserved_load_units` and `reserved_ground_space_sqft` to their exact pre-execution values, with no accumulated drift.

### 2.8. The Capacity Posture Gate

Each tower carries a single derived gate, `capacity_posture`, which is one of `OPEN`, `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN`. It is evaluated as an ordered ladder, and the first matching condition wins. If any ground lease on the tower has `status` equal to `EXPIRED`, the posture is `FROZEN`. Otherwise, if any modification project on the tower has `status` equal to `FUNDED` or `IN_CONSTRUCTION`, the posture is `FROZEN`. Otherwise, if `reserved_load_pct` is strictly greater than 90.000 percent, or `available_ground_space_sqft` is zero or negative, the posture is `RESTRICTED`. Otherwise it is `OPEN`.

The comparison directions matter at the boundary. A `reserved_load_pct` of exactly 90.000 is not `RESTRICTED`; anything above it is. An `available_ground_space_sqft` of exactly 0 **is** `RESTRICTED`. A ground lease in `BOUGHT_OUT` or `RENEWAL_NEGOTIATION` never freezes the tower — only an `EXPIRED` one does.

Operationally, `OPEN` is the only posture that admits a new submission into structural review; both `RESTRICTED` and `FROZEN` divert submissions to a capacity hold. `FROZEN` goes further and additionally blocks amendment execution, application approval, application revision and the creation of a new structural analysis.

### 2.9. Parallel Status Axes and Cascade Discipline

Most entities in this system carry a single lifecycle. The site amendment is the exception: it carries two independent axes on one row. `execution_status` tracks signature and occupancy and runs `DRAFT`, `PENDING_SIGNATURE`, `EXECUTED`, `TERMINATION_NOTICED`, `TERMINATED`, `VOIDED`, with `TERMINATED` and `VOIDED` terminal. `billing_status` tracks revenue and runs `NOT_BILLING`, `BILLING`, `SUSPENDED`, `CLOSED`, with `CLOSED` terminal. Between `EXECUTED` and `TERMINATED` the two axes evolve independently: billing can be suspended and resumed any number of times while occupancy continues untouched.

Cascades in this system are transitive and must be run to completion inside the request that starts them. A billing event changes a tenant's balance; a changed balance changes that tenant's credit standing; a changed credit standing changes the application privilege on that tenant's active master leases; a changed privilege moves applications between `ON_HOLD` and `SUBMITTED`. Likewise, a capacity event changes a tower's reserved load; a changed reserved load changes the posture; a changed posture moves applications between `UNDER_STRUCTURAL_REVIEW` and `CAPACITY_HOLD`. Each entity touched along the chain has its own `updated_at` refreshed and its own `version` incremented, and where the specification calls it out explicitly, the tower's `version` is incremented by exactly one for the whole request rather than once per derived field. Where it is *not* called out explicitly the tower's `version` is left untouched even though its derived counters move, because those counters are re-derived on every read rather than stored: submitting, revising, approving, rejecting or withdrawing an application, allocating or reallocating cost shares, declining a cost share, voiding an amendment, giving termination notice, finalizing an audit and the master-lease half of the clock sweep all change `open_review_count` or the posture inputs without bumping the tower.

### 2.10. Precision and Rounding

All monetary amounts are integer cents; there are no fractional cents anywhere in the system. Unless a rule says otherwise, monetary rounding is **round half up** to whole cents. Structural load values (`load_units`, `total_load_units`, `included_load_units`, `reserved_load_units`) are rounded half up to three decimal places, and percentages (`reserved_load_pct`, `projected_loading_pct`) are likewise rounded half up to three decimal places. Every threshold comparison against a percentage is made against the **rounded** value, never against the unrounded intermediate.

Four rounding orders are pinned and differ from each other deliberately; getting them backwards produces off-by-one-cent and off-by-one-thousandth results.

- **Aggregate load** is rounded per line and then summed, never summed and then rounded.
- **Master-lease site rent** compounds over already-rounded cents: the running amount is rounded to whole cents at each anniversary before the next anniversary is applied.
- **Ground rent** escalates simply and is rounded exactly once, at the end.
- **Landlord payment totals** round each covered month's rent to whole cents and then sum the rounded monthly figures.

Cost-share allocation is the one place that does not round half up: each beneficiary's share is floored to whole cents, and the entire leftover remainder is then assigned to a single beneficiary rather than spread across several.

### 2.11. Optimistic Concurrency on Shared Structural State

Two write operations read a picture that other writers can legitimately move, and both are guarded by an optimistic-concurrency check on the **parent** resource's `version`.

Creating a structural analysis requires `expected_tower_version` as a non-optional field in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target tower. If it does not equal the tower's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_TOWER_VERSION` (412). The create itself is always product-legal — a new analysis legitimately supersedes the prior analysis of the same application — but the tower's structural picture (its reserved load, its posture, its rated capacity) may have moved since the engineer read it, which would silently change the analysis result.

Reallocating a project's cost shares requires `expected_modification_project_version` as a non-optional field, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the modification project. If it does not equal the project's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PROJECT_VERSION` (412). Reallocation legitimately supersedes the previous allocation, so the operation is product-legal from the allocated state; the check exists because the beneficiary set and the total cost may have changed since the version the client read.

No other endpoint in this system accepts a version field, and no other endpoint performs an optimistic-concurrency check.

### 2.12. Tenant Receivables and the Arrears Interlock

Each tenant carries `outstanding_balance_cents`, which begins at 0 and is the single money figure the arrears interlock reads. It increases when a cost share is billed, when an audit finding is billed, and when a restoration deposit is charged at amendment execution. It decreases when the tenant pays cash, when a service-level rent credit is issued, when a restoration deposit is released on completed decommissioning, and when a billed cost share is reversed by project cancellation. The balance is allowed to go negative, representing a credit standing to the tenant's account.

`credit_status` is derived from that balance on every mutation of it: it is `DELINQUENT` when `outstanding_balance_cents` is greater than or equal to 5,000,000 cents (50,000 dollars), and `CURRENT` otherwise. The comparison is inclusive at the boundary — a balance of exactly 5,000,000 cents is `DELINQUENT`, and 4,999,999 cents is `CURRENT`.

Credit standing gates the ability to file work. Each master lease agreement carries `application_privilege`, which is `SUSPENDED` while its tenant is `DELINQUENT` or while the master lease itself is `EXPIRED`, and `ENABLED` otherwise. When a balance change flips a tenant from `CURRENT` to `DELINQUENT`, every master lease agreement of that tenant whose `status` is `ACTIVE` has `application_privilege` set to `SUSPENDED`, and every application under those leases whose `status` is `SUBMITTED` or `UNDER_STRUCTURAL_REVIEW` moves to `ON_HOLD` — with the tower's `open_review_count` decremented once for each application that was leaving review. When a balance change flips a tenant from `DELINQUENT` back to `CURRENT`, those same `ACTIVE` master leases get `application_privilege` set to `ENABLED`, and each of their applications in `ON_HOLD` moves back to `SUBMITTED`.

A master lease agreement also publishes `executed_amendment_count`, the number of site amendments under it whose `execution_status` is `EXECUTED`, and it likewise publishes `application_privilege` as a derived value. Both are recomputed on every read of the lease, so an amendment being executed or terminated changes the published `executed_amendment_count` without refreshing the master lease's own `updated_at` or incrementing its `version`; the master lease is bumped only by its own transitions (activation, reinstatement, expiry) and by an arrears flip that suspends or restores its privilege.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Building the Site Inventory

Everything begins with the asset. A `PortfolioAdmin` registers a tower by supplying a `site_name` between 1 and 120 characters and not blank, a `structure_type` that is one of `MONOPOLE`, `SELF_SUPPORT` or `GUYED`, a `rated_capacity_load_units` figure greater than zero, a `min_colocation_elevation_ft` of zero or more, a `max_elevation_ft` strictly greater than `min_colocation_elevation_ft`, and a `ground_space_total_sqft` greater than zero. Two optional inputs describe what the anchor or owner equipment already consumes before any colocation exists: `baseline_reserved_load_units`, which defaults to 0 and must be at least zero and strictly less than `rated_capacity_load_units`, and `baseline_reserved_ground_sqft`, which defaults to 0 and must be at least zero and strictly less than `ground_space_total_sqft`.

On registration the derived picture is seeded from those baselines: `reserved_load_units` and `reserved_ground_space_sqft` start at the baseline figures, `reserved_load_pct` and `available_ground_space_sqft` follow from them, `occupied_elevation_bands` starts as an empty array, `open_review_count` starts at 0, `capacity_posture` is computed by the posture ladder, and the tower's `version` is initialised to 1.

Towers can be listed with optional `capacity_posture` and `structure_type` filters alongside `limit` and `offset`, and every role may read them. Reading a single tower is the canonical way for an engineer to obtain the tower `version` that the optimistic-concurrency check on analysis creation will compare against. There is also a dedicated computed read — a capacity summary for one tower — whose response is not a stored entity but an object with nine members: `tower_id`, `rated_capacity_load_units`, `reserved_load_units`, `reserved_load_pct`, `available_ground_space_sqft`, `capacity_posture`, `open_review_count`, `occupied_band_count` (the number of entries in `occupied_elevation_bands`) and `capacity_hold_application_count` (the number of applications on the tower currently sitting in `CAPACITY_HOLD`).

Before a site can have a ground lease it needs a payee. A landlord is registered with a `legal_name` between 1 and 160 characters and not blank, a `remit_contact_email` that must be a non-blank string of at most 320 characters and must contain an `@` character, and an optional `w9_on_file` boolean that defaults to `false`. That boolean is not decorative: it is the gate on landlord payment creation.

### 3.2. Ground Rent Over the Term: Escalation, Renewal, Buyout and Remittance

A ground lease is created by a `PortfolioAdmin` against a specific site and payee, supplying `tower_id`, `landlord_id`, a `base_monthly_rent_cents` greater than zero, an `escalator_pct` between 0 and 25 inclusive, a `commencement_date` that is the current escalation baseline, a `term_end_date` strictly after it, a `renewal_option_count` of zero or more, a `renewal_term_years` of at least one, and a `buyout_price_cents` of zero or more where zero means no buyout right was negotiated. On creation the lease's `status` is `ACTIVE`, `renewals_exercised` is 0, `paid_through_date` is null because no payment exists yet, `buyout_paid_cents` is 0, and `current_monthly_rent_cents` is computed. Creating a lease also recomputes the tower's `capacity_posture` and increments the tower's `version` by one.

Ground rent escalates **simply**, not compounding. To evaluate the rent as of some instant, count the anniversaries of `commencement_date` that fall at or before that instant — that is, the largest whole number of years that have elapsed since commencement, which is **0** for any instant before the first anniversary, so a freshly created lease's `current_monthly_rent_cents` equals its `base_monthly_rent_cents`; an anniversary is the same month and day as `commencement_date`, and a 29 February baseline takes 28 February as its anniversary in non-leap years — then multiply `base_monthly_rent_cents` by one plus `escalator_pct` divided by one hundred multiplied by that anniversary count, and round the product half up to whole cents in exactly one rounding step. `current_monthly_rent_cents` is that figure evaluated at the current logical clock instant. Leases are listed with optional `tower_id`, `landlord_id` and `status` filters, and a `Landlord` reading the list sees only leases whose `landlord_id` matches its own identifier.

Renewal is a cycle rather than a one-way door. Opening a renewal moves the lease `ACTIVE → RENEWAL_NEGOTIATION` and requires two things: that `renewals_exercised` is still strictly less than `renewal_option_count`, otherwise `ERR_NO_RENEWAL_OPTIONS_REMAINING`; and that the logical clock date has reached the point one year — 365 days — before `term_end_date`, meaning the clock date must be at or after `term_end_date` minus 365 days, otherwise `ERR_RENEWAL_WINDOW_CLOSED`. Per the ladder of §2.6 the lease-state check comes first and the window check is evaluated before the option-count check, so a lease that is both outside its window and out of options reports `ERR_RENEWAL_WINDOW_CLOSED`. Exercising the renewal then moves the lease `RENEWAL_NEGOTIATION → ACTIVE`, and again requires an unconsumed option. Exercising it rebases the escalation entirely: `base_monthly_rent_cents` is set to the rent evaluated at the **old** `term_end_date`, `commencement_date` is set to the day after the old `term_end_date`, `term_end_date` is pushed out by `renewal_term_years` years, `renewals_exercised` is incremented by one, and `current_monthly_rent_cents` is recomputed from the reset baseline — which restarts the anniversary count at zero. The tower's `capacity_posture` is recomputed and its `version` incremented by one. Each pass through the cycle consumes exactly one option.

Buying out the landlord's interest is available from either `ACTIVE` or `RENEWAL_NEGOTIATION` and requires `buyout_price_cents` to be strictly greater than zero, otherwise `ERR_BUYOUT_NOT_AVAILABLE`. On buyout the lease moves `ACTIVE → BOUGHT_OUT` or `RENEWAL_NEGOTIATION → BOUGHT_OUT`, `buyout_paid_cents` is set to `buyout_price_cents`, and `paid_through_date` is set to `term_end_date` so that no further landlord rent accrues. The tower's posture is recomputed and its `version` incremented; a bought-out lease never freezes the tower. `EXPIRED` and `BOUGHT_OUT` are both terminal for a ground lease.

Remittances are recorded against a lease by naming the `ground_lease_id` together with a `period_start_date` and a `period_end_date` that must be strictly after the start, a plain shape rule enforced with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` before the lease is even resolved. The period rules are strict and are checked in the order given in §2.6: the period must not re-cover ground already paid, so when `paid_through_date` is not null, `period_start_date` must be strictly after it, otherwise `ERR_PAYMENT_PERIOD_OVERLAP`; the period must be contiguous, so when `paid_through_date` is not null, `period_start_date` must be exactly the day after it with no month skipped, otherwise `ERR_PAYMENT_PERIOD_GAP`; and the period must cover whole calendar months, so `period_start_date` must be the first calendar day of a month and `period_end_date` must be the last calendar day of a month, otherwise `ERR_PAYMENT_PERIOD_NOT_WHOLE_MONTHS`. Finally, the lease's landlord must have `w9_on_file` set to `true`, otherwise `ERR_LANDLORD_W9_MISSING`.

A recorded payment computes `months_covered` as the count of whole calendar months contained in the inclusive-to-inclusive interval running from `period_start_date` through `period_end_date`, and computes `amount_cents` by evaluating the lease rent at the first day of each of those months, rounding each month's figure to whole cents, and summing the rounded monthly figures — never by summing first and rounding once. Recording the payment sets the lease's `paid_through_date` to `period_end_date`. The payment record itself is immutable and, as noted in §2.4, carries neither `updated_at` nor `version`. Payments for one lease can be listed with `limit` and `offset`, in `period_start_date` order, by a `PortfolioAdmin` or by any `Landlord` whose identity resolves — this list is one of the reads that carries no ownership scope check, so a landlord who does not own the lease still gets the page.

### 3.3. Carrier Onboarding, Receivables and the Arrears Interlock

A carrier is registered as a tenant with a `legal_name` between 1 and 160 characters and not blank and a `carrier_code` of 3 to 12 uppercase alphanumeric characters that must be unique across all tenants, a collision being a request-shape failure reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. A new tenant starts with `outstanding_balance_cents` at 0 and `credit_status` at `CURRENT`.

Cash coming in is applied as a tenant payment carrying `amount_cents`, which must be strictly greater than zero, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PAYMENT_AMOUNT`. The payment decreases `outstanding_balance_cents` by that amount and recomputes `credit_status`. If the tenant thereby flips from `DELINQUENT` to `CURRENT`, `application_privilege` is set to `ENABLED` on every `ACTIVE` master lease of that tenant and each of their applications sitting in `ON_HOLD` moves `ON_HOLD → SUBMITTED`. Either the tenant itself or a `PortfolioAdmin` may apply a payment; when the tenant applies it, the ownership scope check of §2.1 applies. No payment entity is persisted for the carrier side of the ledger: the response body is the updated `Tenant` resource, with its new `outstanding_balance_cents`, its recomputed `credit_status` and its incremented `version`.

The master lease agreement is the umbrella contract. It is created against a `tenant_id` with a `base_monthly_rent_cents` greater than zero that new site amendments will inherit as their default site rent, an `escalator_pct` between 0 and 25 inclusive that compounds annually on those site amendments, an `initial_term_years` of at least one, a `renewal_terms_count` of zero or more, an `expires_on` date, and an optional `expedited_review_days`. That last field defaults to null, meaning the carrier bought no expedited-review commitment; when it is supplied it must be at least 1 and at most 45. A new master lease starts in `status` `DRAFT` with `application_privilege` at `ENABLED` and `executed_amendment_count` at 0.

Activation moves the lease `DRAFT → ACTIVE`; a lease that is not `DRAFT` is rejected with `ERR_MLA_NOT_ACTIVE`, and only then is `expires_on` required to be strictly after the current logical clock instant, a request-shape failure reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Activation recomputes `application_privilege`, which lands on `ENABLED` when the tenant's `credit_status` is `CURRENT`. `EXPIRED` is terminal for a master lease agreement.

When an arrears suspension has bitten and the tenant has since caught up, a `PortfolioAdmin` can reinstate the lease. Reinstatement sets `application_privilege` to `ENABLED`, requires the lease's `status` to be `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_MLA_NOT_ACTIVE` — and then the tenant's `credit_status` to be `CURRENT`, otherwise `ERR_TENANT_DELINQUENT` (402), and moves every application under that lease in `ON_HOLD` back to `SUBMITTED`. A tenant's leases can be listed with an optional `status` filter.

An application that has been un-held re-enters `SUBMITTED` and is **not** automatically pushed back into review; it is re-branched against the tower posture on the tenant's next submit-equivalent action. Being un-held restamps `submitted_at` to the current logical clock and recomputes `review_deadline_at`.

### 3.4. Filing an Application and Costing Its Equipment Schedule

A carrier opens a colocation application against a specific site and a specific umbrella contract, supplying `tower_id`, `master_lease_agreement_id`, a `requested_elevation_bottom_ft` of zero or more, a `requested_elevation_top_ft` strictly greater than the bottom, a `requested_ground_space_sqft` greater than zero for cabinets and generators, and an optional `expedited_requested` boolean that defaults to `false` and invokes the master lease's expedited-review commitment. The requested band is half-open: the bottom is inclusive and the top is exclusive. A fresh application starts in `DRAFT` with `total_load_units` at 0, `revision_count` at 0, `sla_breached` at `false`, and both `submitted_at` and `review_deadline_at` null. Applications can be listed with optional `tower_id`, `master_lease_agreement_id` and `status` filters.

The equipment schedule is built line by line. Each equipment line named against its parent `colocation_application_id` carries an `equipment_type` of `ANTENNA`, `RRU`, `TMA` or `DISH`, a `model_code` between 1 and 40 characters and not blank, a `quantity` of at least one, a `unit_weight_lbs` greater than zero, a `wind_area_sqft` of zero or more, and a `mount_elevation_ft` of zero or more. A line also carries `installed_amendment_id`, which is null while the line is unbuilt and is set later when the line is carried onto an executed amendment. Adding a line is not gated on the application's `status` at all — a line may be appended to an application in any state, including one already submitted, approved or terminal — and adding one immediately recomputes the parent application's `total_load_units` and increments the application's `version`.

Structural load is not weight. A line's `load_units` is derived by first selecting an elevation multiplier from a strict three-tier ladder on `mount_elevation_ft`: below 100 feet the multiplier is 1.00; from 100 feet up to but not including 200 feet it is 1.25; at 200 feet and above it is 1.50. The raw figure is then the sum of `unit_weight_lbs` and thirty times `wind_area_sqft` — thirty being the wind coefficient in pounds per square foot — multiplied by `quantity`, multiplied by the elevation multiplier. That raw figure is rounded half up to three decimal places to give the line's `load_units`. The application's `total_load_units` is the sum of the **already rounded** per-line figures, so each line is rounded to three decimals first and only then are the lines added together.

A tenant may remove an equipment line while the application is still editable — that is, while its `status` is `DRAFT` or `MODIFICATION_REQUIRED`. Deletion returns no body and immediately recomputes the application's `total_load_units` from the surviving lines; the sibling lines' own `load_units` are unchanged. Equipment lines can be listed with optional `colocation_application_id` and `installed_amendment_id` filters, which is how one recovers the historical equipment schedule of a given amendment.

Submitting the application is the tenant's commitment. Beyond the ownership check, submission requires that the application has at least one equipment line, otherwise `ERR_NO_EQUIPMENT_LINES`; that its master lease agreement is `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_MLA_NOT_ACTIVE`; that the master lease's `application_privilege` is `ENABLED`, otherwise `ERR_MLA_PRIVILEGE_SUSPENDED`; that the requested band lies inside the tower's usable range, meaning `min_colocation_elevation_ft` is at or below `requested_elevation_bottom_ft` and `requested_elevation_top_ft` is at or below `max_elevation_ft`, otherwise `ERR_ELEVATION_OUT_OF_RANGE`; and that the tenant's `credit_status` is `CURRENT`, otherwise `ERR_TENANT_DELINQUENT`. Per §2.6, the two lease-state checks are evaluated before the elevation check, which is evaluated before the equipment-line check, which is evaluated before the delinquency check.

A successful submission moves the application `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`, stamps `submitted_at` with the current logical clock instant, recomputes `total_load_units` over the application's lines, and computes `review_deadline_at`. The review clock runs a number of days determined by the contract: if `expedited_requested` is `true` **and** the master lease's `expedited_review_days` is not null, the commitment is that many days; in every other case — including an expedited request against a master lease that bought no commitment — the standard 45-day review period applies. `review_deadline_at` is `submitted_at` advanced by that number of days, counted as twenty-four-hour periods.

Submission then branches inside the same request, and which branch is taken depends entirely on the tower's `capacity_posture`. If the posture is `OPEN`, the application moves `SUBMITTED → UNDER_STRUCTURAL_REVIEW` and the tower's `open_review_count` is incremented by one. If the posture is `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN`, the application moves `SUBMITTED → CAPACITY_HOLD` instead, the tower's `open_review_count` is left unchanged because no review slot was consumed, and no structural analysis may be created for the application while it is held.

### 3.5. Structural Review, the Dual-Variant Analysis, and Application Disposition

A `StructuralEngineer` runs an analysis by naming the `colocation_application_id`, choosing a `variant` of either `PRELIMINARY` or `DETAILED`, and supplying `expected_tower_version`. The optimistic-concurrency check on that field is described in §2.11. The application must be in `UNDER_STRUCTURAL_REVIEW`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_APPLICATION_STATE`, which is what makes a held application unanalysable. The tower's posture must not be `FROZEN`, otherwise `ERR_TOWER_POSTURE_FROZEN`.

The two variants differ in exactly one term, and that difference is the whole point of offering both. The base term is always the tower's current `reserved_load_units` — the load already standing on the structure. The subject term is always the application's own `total_load_units`. The pipeline term is where they diverge. For a `PRELIMINARY` desktop screen the pipeline term is zero, so reserved-but-unbuilt demand from other carriers is invisible and the same tower with the same equipment can pass. For a `DETAILED` sealed analysis the pipeline term is the sum of `total_load_units` over every **other** application on the same tower whose `status` is `APPROVED` or `MODIFICATION_REQUIRED`, plus every other application on the tower whose `status` is `AMENDMENT_ISSUED` and whose site amendment has an `execution_status` of `DRAFT` or `PENDING_SIGNATURE`. Applications on the tower in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_STRUCTURAL_REVIEW`, `CAPACITY_HOLD`, `ON_HOLD`, `REJECTED` or `WITHDRAWN` are excluded from both variants. Adding the pipeline term can push an identical subject application from a pass to requiring modification, or all the way to exceeding capacity.

The analysis resource publishes all three terms alongside their sum, as `base_load_units`, `subject_load_units` and `pipeline_load_units`, each rounded half up to three decimal places, with `pipeline_load_units` rendered as 0 on a `PRELIMINARY` screen. `included_load_units` is the sum of the base, subject and pipeline terms. `projected_loading_pct` is one hundred times `included_load_units` divided by the tower's `rated_capacity_load_units`, rounded half up to three decimal places. The `result` is then read off that rounded percentage against two limits: at or below 95.000 percent the result is `PASS`; above 95.000 percent but strictly below 100.000 percent the result is `MODIFICATION_REQUIRED`; at 100.000 percent or above the result is `CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.

Creating an analysis sets its `status` to `ISSUED`, records `engineer_user_id` as the acting operator identifier and `issued_at` as the current logical clock instant, sets every earlier analysis of the same application whose `status` is `ISSUED` or `QUOTED` to `SUPERSEDED`, and increments the tower's `version` by one. A `PASS` leaves the application in `UNDER_STRUCTURAL_REVIEW`. A result of `MODIFICATION_REQUIRED` moves the application `UNDER_STRUCTURAL_REVIEW → MODIFICATION_REQUIRED`, decrements the tower's `open_review_count` by one, and recomputes `sla_breached`; this branch is only taken when the analysis just created is the only non-superseded analysis of the application. A result of `CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` — that is, when `projected_loading_pct` reaches 100.000 percent or more — moves the application `UNDER_STRUCTURAL_REVIEW → REJECTED` and decrements the tower's `open_review_count` by one.

**The review-time commitment.** Analysis creation is also where the service-level promise is settled, and it is settled on **every** analysis creation, before the result branch above is taken and irrespective of whether the result is `PASS`, `MODIFICATION_REQUIRED` or `CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. A breach occurs when the analysis's `issued_at` is **strictly** after `review_deadline_at`; an analysis issued at exactly the deadline instant is not a breach, and an application whose `review_deadline_at` is still null cannot breach. Only the first analysis issued after the latest review entry can trigger the consequence, and `sla_breached` is a latch that can fire only once per application. When a breach is detected and `sla_breached` was still `false`, `sla_breached` is set to `true` and a rent credit is issued: the credit is twenty-five percent of the master lease's `base_monthly_rent_cents`, rounded half up to whole cents, and that amount is subtracted from the tenant's `outstanding_balance_cents`, which may take the balance negative. The tenant's `credit_status` is then recomputed.

When an analysis says the structure needs work, the engineer issues the quotation on it by supplying `modification_construction_cost_cents`, which must be greater than zero. The quotation is only accepted on an analysis whose `status` is `ISSUED` and whose `result` is not `PASS`; otherwise the rejection is `ERR_INVALID_ANALYSIS_STATE`. Issuing it moves the analysis `ISSUED → QUOTED` and stamps `quoted_at` with the current logical clock instant. `SUPERSEDED` is terminal for an analysis. An application's analyses can be listed newest first with an optional `status` filter.

**Revision.** A carrier whose application landed in `MODIFICATION_REQUIRED` may change its equipment lines and re-enter review, moving `MODIFICATION_REQUIRED → UNDER_STRUCTURAL_REVIEW` and optionally attaching a `revision_note`, which when supplied must be a non-blank string of at most 500 characters and which is left at its previous value when omitted. Revision requires the ownership check, at least one surviving equipment line, an `application_privilege` of `ENABLED`, an `ACTIVE` master lease agreement, and a tower posture of `OPEN`; a posture of `FROZEN` or `RESTRICTED` blocks the revision with `ERR_TOWER_POSTURE_FROZEN`. Revising increments `revision_count` by one, recomputes `total_load_units` from the surviving lines, restamps `submitted_at` and recomputes `review_deadline_at`, sets every analysis of the application whose `status` is `ISSUED` or `QUOTED` to `SUPERSEDED`, and increments the tower's `open_review_count` by one. The loop has no limit: equipment can be dropped or added and re-analysed any number of times.

**Approval.** A `PortfolioAdmin` can approve from either of two states — `UNDER_STRUCTURAL_REVIEW → APPROVED` or `MODIFICATION_REQUIRED → APPROVED` — and the evidence required differs. From `UNDER_STRUCTURAL_REVIEW`, the application's newest analysis whose `status` is `ISSUED` or `QUOTED` must have `result` equal to `PASS`; the tower posture must not be `FROZEN`; the tenant's `credit_status` must be `CURRENT`. This path decrements the tower's `open_review_count` by one. From `MODIFICATION_REQUIRED`, a certified reinforcement replaces the pass requirement: there must exist a modification project whose `status` is `CERTIFIED` and whose `originating_analysis_id` is the newest non-superseded analysis of this application; the tower posture must again not be `FROZEN` and the tenant must again be `CURRENT`. This second path has no side effect on `open_review_count`, because the application was not occupying a review slot. Failures map to `ERR_TOWER_POSTURE_FROZEN`, `ERR_TENANT_DELINQUENT` and — where the required structural evidence is simply absent from a state that does allow approval — `ERR_INVALID_APPLICATION_STATE`.

**Rejection and withdrawal.** Rejection by a `PortfolioAdmin` carries an optional `rejection_reason` — omitting it is legal, and when supplied it must be a non-blank string of at most 500 characters — and is available as `UNDER_STRUCTURAL_REVIEW → REJECTED` or `MODIFICATION_REQUIRED → REJECTED` with no further preconditions; when the application was in review, the tower's `open_review_count` is decremented by one, and when it was in `MODIFICATION_REQUIRED` no counter changes. Withdrawal is the carrier's own escape hatch, available as `DRAFT → WITHDRAWN`, `SUBMITTED → WITHDRAWN`, `UNDER_STRUCTURAL_REVIEW → WITHDRAWN`, `CAPACITY_HOLD → WITHDRAWN` or `MODIFICATION_REQUIRED → WITHDRAWN`, subject only to the ownership check. Withdrawing from `UNDER_STRUCTURAL_REVIEW` decrements `open_review_count` by one **and** sets every `ISSUED` or `QUOTED` analysis of the application to `SUPERSEDED`; withdrawing from `MODIFICATION_REQUIRED` supersedes those analyses but touches no counter; withdrawing from `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `CAPACITY_HOLD` has no side effects at all. `REJECTED`, `WITHDRAWN` and `AMENDMENT_ISSUED` are the three terminal states of an application.

**Promotion out of a capacity hold.** Whenever a posture-clearing event recomputes a tower's `capacity_posture` to `OPEN` — completing a decommissioning, certifying a reinforcement, or cancelling a **funded** project — exactly **one** held application is promoted, and the rest stay held. Those are the only three events that promote; cancelling a project from `PROPOSED`, `COST_ALLOCATED` or `STALLED` never promotes, because no such project was freezing the tower in the first place, and neither does any other posture recomputation. The winner is determined by ranking the tower's applications in `CAPACITY_HOLD` by `created_at` ascending, then by `requested_ground_space_sqft` ascending, then by `requested_elevation_bottom_ft` ascending, and taking the first. Promoting it moves it `CAPACITY_HOLD → UNDER_STRUCTURAL_REVIEW`, restamps `submitted_at` to the current logical clock instant, recomputes `review_deadline_at` so the review commitment starts afresh, increments the tower's `open_review_count` by one, and sets every analysis of that application whose `status` is `ISSUED` or `QUOTED` to `SUPERSEDED`, because those analyses were computed against a structural picture that has since moved.

The cycle closes in the other direction too. Whenever a tower's posture is recomputed to `FROZEN` — by a ground-lease expiry sweep or by a reinforcement project becoming funded — every application on that tower in `UNDER_STRUCTURAL_REVIEW` is pushed back `UNDER_STRUCTURAL_REVIEW → CAPACITY_HOLD`, with `open_review_count` decremented by one for each. And whenever a tenant's `application_privilege` becomes `SUSPENDED` — by audit finalization, by cost-share billing, or by the master-lease expiry sweep — that lease's applications move `SUBMITTED → ON_HOLD` and `UNDER_STRUCTURAL_REVIEW → ON_HOLD`, the latter decrementing `open_review_count` by one.

### 3.6. Reinforcing the Structure: Projects, Cost Sharing and Certification

When an analysis has been quoted, a `StructuralEngineer` proposes the reinforcement work by naming the `tower_id` and the `originating_analysis_id`, reserving a `construction_window_start_date` and a `construction_window_end_date` that is on or after the start, and optionally choosing an `allocation_basis` of `PRO_RATA_LOAD` or `EQUAL_SHARE`, which defaults to `PRO_RATA_LOAD`. The originating analysis must have `status` equal to `QUOTED` with a `modification_construction_cost_cents` greater than zero, otherwise `ERR_QUOTATION_MISSING`. The construction window must not conflict: no other modification project on the same tower whose `status` is `PROPOSED`, `COST_ALLOCATED`, `FUNDED` or `IN_CONSTRUCTION` may have a closed date interval from its own `construction_window_start_date` through its own `construction_window_end_date` that intersects this project's window on any calendar day, otherwise `ERR_CONSTRUCTION_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. A proposed project starts in `PROPOSED` with `proposing_engineer_user_id` set to the acting operator identifier, `allocated_cents` at 0, and `base_construction_cost_cents` copied from the originating analysis; the three pipeline outputs `volume_discount_pct`, `engineering_fee_cents` and `total_cost_cents` are null until the first allocation computes them, as are `certified_by_user_id`, `funded_at`, `added_capacity_load_units` and `cancellation_reason`. Per the ladder of §2.6 the construction-window conflict is checked before the quotation, so a proposal that both collides with another window and names an unquoted analysis reports `ERR_CONSTRUCTION_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Projects can be listed with optional `tower_id`, `originating_analysis_id` and `status` filters, and reading one project is how a client obtains the project `version` for the reallocation concurrency check.

**The pinned cost pipeline.** Allocating cost shares moves the project `PROPOSED → COST_ALLOCATED` and first computes the money. The dependency here looks circular — the engineering fee is a percentage of construction cost, the volume discount tier is keyed on the total including the fee, and the discount changes the construction cost the fee is computed from — so the evaluation order is pinned and must **not** be iterated to a fixed point. It runs in exactly seven steps. First, the base construction figure is the originating analysis's `modification_construction_cost_cents`, and this is what is stored as `base_construction_cost_cents`. Second, a provisional engineering fee is twelve percent of that base construction figure, rounded half up to whole cents. Third, the pre-discount total is the base construction figure plus that provisional fee. Fourth, `volume_discount_pct` is selected **once** from that pre-discount total: it is 0.03 when the pre-discount total is at least 25,000,000 cents (250,000 dollars), otherwise 0.015 when the pre-discount total is at least 10,000,000 cents (100,000 dollars), otherwise 0; the tier is never re-derived from the post-discount total. Fifth, the discounted construction figure is the base construction figure less the product of the base construction figure and `volume_discount_pct`, that product rounded half up to whole cents. Sixth, `engineering_fee_cents` is twelve percent of the **discounted** construction figure, rounded half up to whole cents. Seventh, `total_cost_cents` is the discounted construction figure plus `engineering_fee_cents`. Only the originating analysis's quoted construction cost feeds this pipeline; no other project on the tower contributes anything to it.

**Who pays.** The beneficiary set is resolved explicitly, and it has exactly two sources. Every site amendment on the project's tower whose `execution_status` is `EXECUTED` produces one beneficiary entry, whose `basis_load_units` is that amendment's `reserved_load_units`, whose `basis_created_at` is that amendment's `created_at`, whose `tenant_id` is the `tenant_id` of the amendment's master lease agreement, and whose `site_amendment_id` is that amendment's identifier. In addition, the originating analysis's own application produces one beneficiary entry when its `status` is `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_STRUCTURAL_REVIEW`, `CAPACITY_HOLD`, `MODIFICATION_REQUIRED` or `APPROVED`; its `basis_load_units` is the application's `total_load_units`, its `basis_created_at` is the application's `created_at`, its `tenant_id` comes from that application's master lease agreement, and its `site_amendment_id` is null because the applicant has no amendment yet. If the resolved set is empty, or if the total basis load summed over the set is not greater than zero, the allocation is rejected with `ERR_COST_SHARE_BENEFICIARY_INVALID`. The construction-window conflict check of §2.6 and the quotation check are both re-applied at allocation time.

The total basis is the sum of `basis_load_units` across the beneficiary set. Under `PRO_RATA_LOAD`, a beneficiary's `share_cents` is `total_cost_cents` multiplied by that beneficiary's `basis_load_units`, divided by the total basis, with the result floored to whole cents. Under `EQUAL_SHARE`, it is `total_cost_cents` divided by the number of beneficiaries, floored to whole cents. Flooring leaves a remainder equal to `total_cost_cents` less the sum of the floored shares, and that **entire** remainder is added to exactly one beneficiary's share — never spread across several. The remainder recipient is chosen by ranking beneficiaries by `basis_created_at` ascending, then — because the logical clock makes exact ties routine — by the creation sequence of the basis row itself (the amendment or the application), then by the beneficiary tenant's own `created_at` ascending, then by the tenant's creation sequence, then by `basis_load_units` ascending, and taking the first.

Allocation mints one cost share per beneficiary in `status` `PENDING` and sets the project's `allocated_cents` to `total_cost_cents`. The invariant that holds from allocation until cancellation is that the sum of `share_cents` over the project's shares whose `status` is `PENDING`, `ACCEPTED` or `BILLED` equals the project's `total_cost_cents`; `allocated_cents` is precisely that sum, so shares that were voided or declined by an earlier allocation contribute nothing. Allocation on its own does not change the tower's posture. The response body of both allocation and reallocation is the updated `ModificationProject` resource — with its freshly computed pipeline outputs and its `allocated_cents` — not the minted shares, which are recovered from the cost-share list. Cost shares can be listed with optional `modification_project_id`, `tenant_id`, `site_amendment_id` and `status` filters.

**Reallocation.** An engineer may supersede the current allocation with a fresh one under a different `allocation_basis`, supplying `expected_modification_project_version` as described in §2.11. `allocation_basis` is itself optional here: when it is omitted the project keeps the basis it already had and the allocation is simply recomputed. This is a self-loop, `COST_ALLOCATED → COST_ALLOCATED`: it is only available while the project is in `COST_ALLOCATED`, and it additionally requires that no cost share of the project has `status` equal to `BILLED` — both of those rejections carry `ERR_INVALID_PROJECT_STATE`. Unlike the first allocation, reallocation re-checks neither the construction window nor the quotation; only the beneficiary-set check of §2.6 is re-applied. Reallocating moves each affected share `PENDING → VOIDED` or `ACCEPTED → VOIDED`, updates `allocation_basis`, mints a fresh set of `PENDING` shares from a freshly resolved beneficiary set, restores `allocated_cents` to `total_cost_cents`, and increments the project's `version` by one.

**The fork and the join.** With shares outstanding, the project waits on the carriers, and the two possible joins have deliberately different consequences. A carrier accepting its own share — permitted only while the share is `PENDING` and the parent project is `COST_ALLOCATED`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_COST_SHARE_STATE` — moves the share `PENDING → ACCEPTED` and stamps `accepted_at` with the logical clock; the response body of both accept and decline is the updated `CostShare` resource rather than the project. When that acceptance leaves no non-voided sibling share still `PENDING`, the project joins `COST_ALLOCATED → FUNDED`, and a substantial cascade fires: `funded_at` is stamped with the logical clock; every `ACCEPTED` share moves `ACCEPTED → BILLED` and its tenant's `outstanding_balance_cents` is increased by that share's `share_cents`; each affected tenant's `credit_status` is recomputed, and any flip to `DELINQUENT` sets `application_privilege` to `SUSPENDED` on that tenant's `ACTIVE` master leases and moves their `SUBMITTED` and `UNDER_STRUCTURAL_REVIEW` applications to `ON_HOLD`; the tower's `capacity_posture` is recomputed to `FROZEN`, because a funded reinforcement blocks new occupancy; every application on the tower in `UNDER_STRUCTURAL_REVIEW` is pushed `UNDER_STRUCTURAL_REVIEW → CAPACITY_HOLD`; and the tower's `version` is incremented by one.

A carrier declining its share, with an optional `decline_reason` that when supplied must be a non-blank string of at most 500 characters, is subject to the same state preconditions and moves the share `PENDING → DECLINED`. The project then joins `COST_ALLOCATED → STALLED`, and the side-effect set is deliberately much thinner than the funded branch: every remaining `PENDING` share of the project is set to `VOIDED`; `ACCEPTED` shares are left exactly as they are and are **not** billed; and the tower's posture is recomputed but does not freeze, because a stalled project reserves nothing. There is no billing, no freeze and no push-back of reviewing applications on this branch. `DECLINED`, `BILLED` and `VOIDED` are the terminal states of a cost share.

**Construction and certification.** Starting construction moves the project `FUNDED → IN_CONSTRUCTION` and requires that the logical clock date has reached `construction_window_start_date`, that is, the clock date is at or after the window start; a window that has not opened yet is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PROJECT_STATE` (409), the same code as the state check itself. The tower's posture stays `FROZEN` throughout. Certifying completion moves the project `IN_CONSTRUCTION → CERTIFIED` and carries `added_capacity_load_units`, which must be greater than zero. Certification enforces segregation of duties: the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the project's `proposing_engineer_user_id` **and** from the `engineer_user_id` of the originating structural analysis, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. It also requires that every cost share of the project whose `status` is not `VOIDED` has `status` equal to `BILLED` — the work cannot be signed off while money is still outstanding, and a `DECLINED` share therefore blocks certification as surely as a `PENDING` one does. That share check is evaluated **after** the segregation-of-duties check and is reported as `ERR_INVALID_PROJECT_STATE`, so an engineer who is disqualified *and* faces an unbilled share sees `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Certification records `certified_by_user_id` as the acting operator identifier, increases the tower's `rated_capacity_load_units` by `added_capacity_load_units`, recomputes `reserved_load_pct`, `available_ground_space_sqft` and `capacity_posture` so the freeze is lifted, promotes the single held-application winner if the posture lands on `OPEN`, and increments the tower's `version` by one.

**Cancellation and compensation.** A `PortfolioAdmin` may cancel a project as `PROPOSED → CANCELLED`, `COST_ALLOCATED → CANCELLED`, `STALLED → CANCELLED` or `FUNDED → CANCELLED`, optionally supplying a `cancellation_reason` that when supplied must be a non-blank string of at most 500 characters; `CERTIFIED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal. Cancelling from `PROPOSED` only recomputes the tower's posture, and leaves the tower's `version` untouched — as do the `COST_ALLOCATED` and `STALLED` paths. Cancelling from `COST_ALLOCATED` or from `STALLED` additionally moves every outstanding share `PENDING → VOIDED` or `ACCEPTED → VOIDED` and sets `allocated_cents` to 0. Cancelling from `FUNDED` performs an exact compensation, and also voids any share still sitting in `PENDING` or `ACCEPTED`: every cost share whose `status` is `BILLED` moves `BILLED → VOIDED` and its tenant's `outstanding_balance_cents` is decreased by that share's `share_cents`, reversing the funding billing to the cent; each affected tenant's `credit_status` is recomputed, and a flip from `DELINQUENT` back to `CURRENT` restores `application_privilege` to `ENABLED` on that tenant's `ACTIVE` master leases and moves their `ON_HOLD` applications back to `SUBMITTED`. In all cancellation paths that had shares, `allocated_cents` becomes 0; from `FUNDED` the tower's posture is recomputed with the freeze lifted because no funded project remains, the single held-application winner is promoted if the posture becomes `OPEN`, and the tower's `version` is incremented by one.

### 3.7. Papering, Executing and Superseding Site Amendments

An approved application becomes a contract when a `PortfolioAdmin` papers the site amendment, naming the `master_lease_agreement_id`, the `tower_id` and the `colocation_application_id` it papers, together with an `effective_date` that will serve as both the escalation baseline and the occupancy start, and a `term_end_date` strictly after it. Four inputs are optional. `base_monthly_rent_cents`, which must be greater than zero, defaults to the master lease's own `base_monthly_rent_cents` when omitted. `escalator_pct`, between 0 and 25 inclusive, defaults to the master lease's `escalator_pct` when omitted. `restoration_deposit_cents`, which must be at least zero, defaults to 0. And `supersedes_amendment_id` names the executed amendment that an equipment-swap amendment replaces; it is resolved last of the four foreign keys, after `master_lease_agreement_id`, `tower_id` and `colocation_application_id` in that order, so an unknown value surfaces `ERR_AMENDMENT_NOT_FOUND`, and when it resolves it must reference a site amendment whose `execution_status` is `EXECUTED`, with the same `tower_id` and the same `master_lease_agreement_id` as the new amendment, otherwise `ERR_SUPERSEDE_TARGET_INVALID`. The chain is self-referential: a swap amendment can itself later be superseded.

Papering the amendment requires that the application's `status` is `APPROVED` and that no site amendment already references the application — either failure is `ERR_INVALID_APPLICATION_STATE`, evaluated before the supersede-target check — and it moves the application `APPROVED → AMENDMENT_ISSUED`. The new amendment starts with `execution_status` at `DRAFT`, `billing_status` at `NOT_BILLING` and `restoration_status` at `NOT_REQUIRED`, with `notice_given_at` and `terminated_at` both null. Four fields are copied straight off the application at creation and are not client-supplied: `elevation_bottom_ft` from `requested_elevation_bottom_ft` as an inclusive bound, `elevation_top_ft` from `requested_elevation_top_ft` as an exclusive bound, `ground_space_sqft` from `requested_ground_space_sqft`, and `reserved_load_units` from `total_load_units` — the last being the quantity this amendment will hold on the tower once executed. Amendments can be listed with optional `master_lease_agreement_id`, `tower_id`, `colocation_application_id`, `supersedes_amendment_id`, `execution_status` and `billing_status` filters.

**Execution is two-stage.** The execute operation carries a single, mandatory `signature_received` boolean. When it is `false`, the amendment moves `DRAFT → PENDING_SIGNATURE`: the source state must be exactly `DRAFT`, so re-parking an amendment already in `PENDING_SIGNATURE` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_AMENDMENT_STATE`; the only other precondition is that the amendment's tower has a ground lease whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, otherwise `ERR_GROUND_LEASE_NOT_ACTIVE`, and the amendment parks awaiting the countersignature without claiming any capacity. When `signature_received` is `true`, the amendment moves `DRAFT → EXECUTED` or `PENDING_SIGNATURE → EXECUTED`, and the full precondition set is evaluated against current state on either path — the parked path re-evaluates everything rather than trusting what was true when it parked.

Those preconditions, in the evaluation order of §2.6, are: the tower must have a ground lease whose `status` is `ACTIVE` (`ERR_GROUND_LEASE_NOT_ACTIVE`); the tower's `capacity_posture` must not be `FROZEN` (`ERR_TOWER_POSTURE_FROZEN`); the post-execution structural loading must be strictly below the capacity limit (`ERR_STRUCTURAL_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`); structural clearance must exist (`ERR_STRUCTURAL_CLEARANCE_MISSING`); the elevation band must clear the separation buffer (`ERR_ELEVATION_BAND_CONFLICT`); the ground space must be available (`ERR_GROUND_SPACE_UNAVAILABLE`); the amendment's `term_end_date` must be at or before that active ground lease's own `term_end_date` (`ERR_TERM_EXCEEDS_GROUND_LEASE`); and the tenant's `credit_status` must be `CURRENT` (`ERR_TENANT_DELINQUENT`). Where a tower carries more than one `ACTIVE` ground lease, the one consulted — both for the existence check and for the term comparison — is the earliest-created of them.

Three of those deserve their arithmetic spelled out. **Post-execution loading** is one hundred multiplied by the quantity formed as the tower's current `reserved_load_units`, less the `reserved_load_units` of the amendment named by `supersedes_amendment_id` (zero when nothing is being superseded), plus this amendment's own `reserved_load_units`, all divided by the tower's `rated_capacity_load_units`, rounded half up to three decimal places; that rounded percentage must be strictly below 100.000 percent. **Ground space** is available when the tower's `available_ground_space_sqft` plus the superseded amendment's `ground_space_sqft` (zero when `supersedes_amendment_id` is null) is greater than or equal to this amendment's `ground_space_sqft`. **Band separation** is checked against every other site amendment on the same tower whose `execution_status` is `EXECUTED` or `TERMINATION_NOTICED`, excluding the one named by `supersedes_amendment_id`: for each such neighbour, either this amendment's `elevation_top_ft` plus a ten-foot separation buffer must be at or below the neighbour's `elevation_bottom_ft`, or the neighbour's `elevation_top_ft` plus that same ten-foot buffer must be at or below this amendment's `elevation_bottom_ft`. If neither holds for any neighbour, the bands conflict.

**Structural clearance** is satisfied in either of two ways: the amendment's application has a non-superseded structural analysis whose `result` is `PASS`, or there exists a modification project whose `status` is `CERTIFIED` and whose `originating_analysis_id` is that application's newest non-superseded analysis.

Reaching `EXECUTED` fires a broad set of effects inside the same request. The billing axis moves `NOT_BILLING → BILLING`. `restoration_status` is set to `PENDING` when `restoration_deposit_cents` is greater than zero, and to `NOT_REQUIRED` otherwise. The tenant's `outstanding_balance_cents` is increased by `restoration_deposit_cents` and `credit_status` recomputed accordingly. Every equipment line of the amendment's application has its `installed_amendment_id` set to this amendment's identifier. When `supersedes_amendment_id` is set, the superseded amendment moves `EXECUTED → TERMINATED` with its `restoration_status` set to `COMPLETE` and its `billing_status` set to `CLOSED`; its `terminated_at` stays null, because this is a paper replacement rather than a decommissioning, and no restoration deposit of its own is released. The tower's `reserved_load_units`, `reserved_load_pct`, `reserved_ground_space_sqft`, `available_ground_space_sqft`, `occupied_elevation_bands` and `capacity_posture` are all recomputed, the master lease's `executed_amendment_count` is recomputed, and the tower's `version` is incremented by one.

An amendment that has not yet been executed can be voided with no preconditions, as `DRAFT → VOIDED` or `PENDING_SIGNATURE → VOIDED` on the execution axis, which also sets `billing_status` to `CLOSED`.

**The billing axis, driven directly.** A `PortfolioAdmin` may move the billing axis without touching occupancy by supplying a `to_status`. Moving `BILLING → SUSPENDED` requires the amendment's `execution_status` to be `EXECUTED` or `TERMINATION_NOTICED` and has no further effects — billing may be suspended while the equipment stays on the tower. Moving `SUSPENDED → BILLING` requires the same `execution_status` set and recomputes `current_monthly_rent_cents`. Moving `BILLING → CLOSED` on this explicit path requires the `execution_status` to be `TERMINATION_NOTICED`. Any billing move not declared from the amendment's current `billing_status` — including `SUSPENDED → CLOSED`, which the system only ever performs for itself — is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_BILLING_STATE`, and that check runs first; a declared move whose `execution_status` precondition is unmet is then rejected with `ERR_INVALID_AMENDMENT_STATE` instead. The system itself drives three further billing moves: `NOT_BILLING → BILLING` as a side effect of the execution axis reaching `EXECUTED`, which also recomputes `current_monthly_rent_cents`; `BILLING → CLOSED` and `SUSPENDED → CLOSED` as side effects of the execution axis reaching `TERMINATED`; and `NOT_BILLING → CLOSED` as a side effect of the execution axis reaching `VOIDED`.

**Site rent compounds, and it is capped.** `current_monthly_rent_cents` is recomputed whenever the billing axis enters `BILLING`, whether from execution or from un-suspension, and on every read of the amendment. To evaluate it, first count the anniversaries of `effective_date` — anniversaries landing on the same month and day as `effective_date` — that fall at or before the current logical clock instant. Then, starting from `base_monthly_rent_cents`, walk those anniversaries in order and at each one multiply the running amount by one plus `escalator_pct` divided by one hundred, rounding the result half up to whole cents **before** moving to the next anniversary. Separately compute the cap as `base_monthly_rent_cents` multiplied by 1.50, rounded half up to whole cents. `current_monthly_rent_cents` is the lesser of the compounded running amount and that cap.

The rounding order here is pinned and is not the same as raising one plus the escalator to a power and rounding once at the end. Compounding happens over already-rounded cents. For a base of 250,000 cents at an escalator of 3.5 percent across four anniversaries, the pinned per-anniversary order yields 286,880 cents, whereas the single-shot power form would yield 286,881; the implementation must produce 286,880.

### 3.8. Departure: Notice, Restoration and Capacity Release

Departure is a two-step process, and the gap between the steps is where a great deal of money and capacity sits. A carrier gives notice on its own executed amendment, moving `EXECUTED → TERMINATION_NOTICED`, subject to the ownership check, to the source state being exactly `EXECUTED` (otherwise `ERR_INVALID_AMENDMENT_STATE`) and to the amendment's `billing_status` not already being `CLOSED` (otherwise `ERR_INVALID_BILLING_STATE`, checked second). Giving notice stamps `notice_given_at` with the current logical clock instant and sets `restoration_status` to `PENDING`. Critically, it releases nothing: the equipment is still on the structure, so the amendment keeps holding its load, its ground space and its elevation band on the tower until decommissioning actually completes.

Closing out the decommissioning is a `PortfolioAdmin` action carrying a mandatory `restoration_confirmed` boolean that must be `true` — a `false` value is a request-shape failure, `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, rejected before the amendment is even resolved — and it requires the amendment's `execution_status` to be `TERMINATION_NOTICED` and then its `restoration_status` to be `PENDING`, both failures reported as `ERR_INVALID_AMENDMENT_STATE`. An amendment that noticed termination while carrying no restoration deposit therefore still has `restoration_status` `PENDING`, because giving notice sets it unconditionally. It moves the amendment `TERMINATION_NOTICED → TERMINATED`, stamps `terminated_at` with the logical clock, sets `restoration_status` to `COMPLETE` and sets `billing_status` to `CLOSED`.

From there a four-level cascade runs to completion. Because the amendment terminated with restoration complete, the tenant's `outstanding_balance_cents` is **decreased** by `restoration_deposit_cents` — the deposit charged at execution is released. Because the balance dropped, the tenant's `credit_status` is recomputed and may flip from `DELINQUENT` to `CURRENT`. Because the credit standing became `CURRENT`, every master lease agreement of that tenant whose `status` is `ACTIVE` has `application_privilege` recomputed to `ENABLED`. And because those privileges were restored, each of those leases' applications in `ON_HOLD` moves `ON_HOLD → SUBMITTED`.

Alongside the money cascade, the capacity cascade runs. The tower's `reserved_load_units`, `reserved_ground_space_sqft`, `available_ground_space_sqft`, `occupied_elevation_bands`, `reserved_load_pct` and `capacity_posture` are all recomputed, which is where the released load and ground space actually come back. If that recomputation lands the posture on `OPEN`, the single promotion winner among the tower's `CAPACITY_HOLD` applications is advanced into `UNDER_STRUCTURAL_REVIEW` by the ranking rule of §3.5. Every equipment line whose `installed_amendment_id` points at this amendment keeps that reference as a historical record but no longer contributes any load to the tower. The master lease's `executed_amendment_count` is recomputed, and the tower's `version` is incremented by one.

### 3.9. Compliance Audits and Retroactive Billing of Unauthorized Installations

Carriers do not always ask before bolting something to a tower, so a `ComplianceAuditor` schedules physical walk-downs. Scheduling an audit names the `tower_id`, an `audit_date` that is the as-of date of the inspection and must not be after the current logical clock instant (otherwise `ERR_AUDIT_DATE_IN_FUTURE`), and a `basis` of either `INSTALL_DATE_BASIS` or `DISCOVERY_LIMITED`. The basis is fixed up front, before anything is found, and it governs the retroactive billing of every finding on that audit. A scheduled audit starts in `SCHEDULED` with `auditor_user_id` set to the acting operator identifier, `finding_count` at 0 and `retroactive_billing_total_cents` at 0. Audits on one tower can be listed with an optional `status` filter.

Starting the walk-down moves the audit `SCHEDULED → IN_PROGRESS` and requires `audit_date` to be at or before the current logical clock instant. Each unauthorized installation is then recorded as a finding under its parent `equipment_audit_id`, carrying the `tenant_id` of the carrier that owns the gear, a `description` between 1 and 240 characters and not blank, an `observed_load_units` measurement greater than zero, an `installed_on_date` giving the best-evidence installation date, a `mount_elevation_ft` of zero or more, and an optional `site_amendment_id` naming the tenant's executed amendment the gear was found next to — left null when that tenant has no amendment on the tower at all. That reference is only resolved for existence, with `ERR_AMENDMENT_NOT_FOUND` when it is unknown; the server does not additionally require it to be executed, to belong to the named tenant, or to sit on the audited tower. The `installed_on_date` must not be after the parent audit's `audit_date`, otherwise `ERR_FINDING_DATE_AFTER_AUDIT`. A new finding starts with `status` `OPEN`, `months_billable` at 0 and `retroactive_billing_cents` at 0, and it increments the audit's `finding_count` by one. Findings can be listed with optional `equipment_audit_id`, `tenant_id`, `site_amendment_id` and `status` filters.

Finalizing the audit moves it `IN_PROGRESS → FINALIZED` with no preconditions and is where the money is computed. For every finding whose `status` is `OPEN` — findings already `BILLED` are never re-billed — the whole-month arithmetic runs as follows. Start with twelve multiplied by the difference between the audit date's year and the installation date's year, plus the difference between the audit date's month and the installation date's month. If the audit date's day of the month is earlier than the installation date's day of the month, subtract one, because a partial month is never billed. If the result is negative, treat it as zero. That figure is the gross month count. Under `INSTALL_DATE_BASIS`, `months_billable` is the gross count outright, with no ceiling — an installation forty-one months old bills forty-one months. Under `DISCOVERY_LIMITED`, `months_billable` is the lesser of the gross count and a two-year ceiling of 24 months, so the identical finding against the identical carrier bills a strictly smaller amount whenever the gross count exceeds 24.

The rate is then applied in two rounding steps. First, the monthly rate is 25 cents per load unit multiplied by `observed_load_units`, rounded half up to whole cents. Second, `retroactive_billing_cents` is `months_billable` multiplied by that monthly rate multiplied by a penalty multiplier of 1.5, rounded half up to whole cents. Each finding then moves `OPEN → BILLED`, and the audit's `retroactive_billing_total_cents` is the sum of `retroactive_billing_cents` across the audit's findings, which is 0 when the audit recorded none. `FINALIZED` is terminal for an audit, and `BILLED` is terminal for a finding.

Finalization then cascades four levels deep, exactly as described in §2.12. Because the findings were billed, each affected tenant's `outstanding_balance_cents` is increased by that tenant's own finding total — as a single aggregate movement per tenant, so a tenant with three findings on the audit is bumped once rather than three times, and the tenants are visited in the order in which their first billed finding was recorded. Because the balance rose, each affected tenant's `credit_status` is recomputed and may flip from `CURRENT` to `DELINQUENT`. Because the credit standing became `DELINQUENT`, every master lease agreement of that tenant whose `status` is `ACTIVE` has `application_privilege` set to `SUSPENDED`. And because those privileges were suspended, each of those leases' applications whose `status` is `SUBMITTED` or `UNDER_STRUCTURAL_REVIEW` moves to `ON_HOLD`, with the relevant tower's `open_review_count` decremented once for every application that was leaving review.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Operations that register a new stand-alone resource, or add a new distinct resource to a collection, return `201 Created`. Operations that modify an existing resource — including all state transitions, all cost and billing operations, and the logical-clock control — return `200 OK`. The single delete operation returns `204 No Content` with no response body. All read operations return `200 OK`. Request and response payloads are not restated here; they are established by the narratives in §2 and §3. A path that matches no route in the table, or a method that the matched path does not declare, is answered with the standard error envelope carrying `error_id` `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` at HTTP `404` and `405` respectively — the only two places where that identifier is paired with a status other than 400.
