# Digital Curriculum Rostering & Single Sign-On API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Every August, a school district's educational-technology office faces the same compressed problem: several dozen digital curriculum vendors each expect a roster file, each expects it in a different shape, each counts licensed seats differently, and each expects the district's single sign-on identities to appear the moment the school year opens. The source of truth is the student information system — buildings, terms, staff, students, course sections and enrollments — but the source of truth is never clean. Teachers resign mid-year, students transfer in on the fifteenth instructional day, the same child is registered twice under two spellings, and a building whose grade band was recorded as elementary turns out to house seventh graders. Today this work is done with spreadsheets, ad-hoc scripts and a great deal of email, and the most expensive failures are silent ones: a vendor invoice for seats nobody used, or a withdrawn student who still has a working password in March.

This document specifies the Digital Curriculum Rostering & Single Sign-On service, mounted under `/rostering`, which owns that pipeline end to end. The service holds the extracted district roster, records the per-vendor rostering standard and transformation rules that turn that roster into a vendor-consumable feed, enforces license entitlement by seat count and by building allocation, provisions and deprovisions single sign-on identities against each building's password policy, and maintains a triage queue for the roster defects that cannot be resolved automatically. It also carries the money: what the district has committed per seat, what a mid-year change costs when it is prorated against the remaining term, and what the renewal conversation should look like when the license comes up again.

Two design commitments run through the whole specification and are worth stating before the details. First, **every consequential action is a two-person control or a versioned write** — the officer who publishes a roster is never the operator who extracted it, the officer who activates a license is never the manager who registered it, and every write that reshapes the seat pool carries the license version the client last observed. Second, **the license seat axis and the single sign-on identity axis are independent**: giving a seat back is not the same act as switching off an identity, and the specification never couples them except where a cascade explicitly says so.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Authentication is header-based. Every request to every endpoint — reads included, and the logical-clock endpoint included — must carry both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role`. If either header is absent or blank, the request is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before anything else is examined. If both headers are present but the value of `X-User-Role` is not among the roles the target endpoint admits, the request is refused with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403). A header whose value is only whitespace counts as blank. The one route outside this rule is the unauthenticated liveness probe `GET /_health`, which sits outside the `/rostering` business surface, takes no headers and answers HTTP 200 with the bare body `{"status": "ok", "now": "<clock instant>"}` rather than the envelope of §2.2.

There are exactly five roles, spelled exactly as follows:

- `RosterOperator` — the district SIS data steward. Maintains buildings, terms, staff, students, sections and enrollments, and drives the extraction and transform half of the rostering pipeline.
- `DataGovernanceOfficer` — the district data-governance authority and the second pair of eyes. Publishes rosters, triages the error queue, records withdrawals and staff separations, rolls the school year over and activates licenses.
- `VendorIntegrationManager` — the vendor-facing integration owner. Registers the license, chooses the rostering standard and the transformation rules, consumes the roster feed and posts usage telemetry.
- `BuildingLicenseAdmin` — a school-level license administrator that re-cuts its own building's seat allocation.
- `IdentityAdmin` — the single sign-on identity administrator. Provisions and deprovisions accounts, sets and resets passwords, and releases seats. It is the only role that may set an account password, which is also the act that advances the identity axis from `PENDING_PROVISION` to `PROVISIONED`.

Four of the five roles are **opaque operator identities**. When `X-User-Role` is `RosterOperator`, `DataGovernanceOfficer`, `VendorIntegrationManager` or `IdentityAdmin`, the `X-User-ID` value is an opaque operator identifier that is not the `id` of any persisted entity and is never compared to one. The server does record it where the specification says so — as `created_by_user_id` and `activated_by_user_id` on a vendor license, as `initiated_by_user_id` and `published_by_user_id` on a sync run, and as `resolved_by_user_id` on a triage item — and it compares recorded operator identifiers to each other for the two-person controls described below, but it never resolves one to an entity row.

`BuildingLicenseAdmin` is the single **entity-backed** role. When `X-User-Role` is `BuildingLicenseAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `School`; ownership checks compare the `school_id` of the resource being written to `X-User-ID`. A `BuildingLicenseAdmin` may therefore re-cut the seat allocation of its own building and no other, and an attempt to write another building's allocation is refused with `ERR_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). This ownership check is evaluated **before** the allocation identifier in the path is resolved, so a `BuildingLicenseAdmin` naming an allocation that does not exist at all is also refused with `ERR_SCOPE_VIOLATION` and never with `ERR_BUILDING_ALLOCATION_NOT_FOUND`.

`VendorIntegrationManager` carries a second, weaker form of binding that is not entity-backed but is still scoped: an operator acting as `VendorIntegrationManager` is bound to exactly those licenses whose `created_by_user_id` equals its `X-User-ID`. Amending a license, reading a license's roster feed, reading a license's renewal summary, and posting usage telemetry for an account under that license are all refused with `ERR_SCOPE_VIOLATION` when the governing license was registered by somebody else. Registering a brand-new license always passes this check, because that is the act which establishes the binding. This scope check is evaluated **before** existence checks, so a probe against another operator's license cannot be used to enumerate identifiers: the caller learns that it is out of scope, not whether the identifier exists.

Two operations are **two-person controls**. Publishing a sync run is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403) when the publishing officer's `X-User-ID` equals the run's `initiated_by_user_id`, and activating a license is refused with the same error when the activating officer's `X-User-ID` equals the license's `created_by_user_id`. Both checks are evaluated in the authentication and authorization tier, ahead of any state or existence check.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`, followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the upper-case letters `A`–`Z` and the digits `0`–`9`. It is minted freshly for every response, success or failure, and is never echoed from the request.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. It is read from the logical clock of §2.4 like every other instant the server writes, so two responses taken between two clock moves carry the identical timestamp.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints, and only for them: the `meta` of a single-object success response — a creation, a state transition, an amendment, a single-record read or a computed read — carries `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp` and nothing else. `response_type` is correspondingly `"array"` only for the paginated collection reads; every computed read answers `"object"`.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination, Filtering and List Ordering

Every collection read is paginated with two query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1 and may not be larger than 100; `offset` defaults to 0, has no upper bound, and counts records from the start of the ordered collection. Both arrive as text and are read as decimal integers; a value that is not one, a `limit` outside 1 through 100, and a negative `offset` are each **refused** with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) rather than clamped to the nearest legal figure, while a parameter supplied with an empty value is treated exactly as if it had been omitted and takes its default. An `offset` at or beyond `total_count` is legal and answers an empty `data` array with `has_more` false. List responses set `response_type` to `"array"`, place the page of records in `data`, and populate four pagination fields in `meta`: `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` (the number of records matching the filters before the page window is applied) and `has_more` (true when records remain beyond the current window). Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, every list is ordered by `created_at` ascending. Because the clock is logical and only moves when a client moves it, records routinely share a `created_at`; ties are therefore broken by the order in which the server created the records, so a list is always in creation order.

Collection reads also accept equality filters drawn from the records they return, and filters combine conjunctively. A filter matches on exact equality against the stored field value with no case-folding and no partial matching; a filter supplied with an empty value is ignored rather than matched against the empty string; a query parameter the endpoint does not recognise is ignored entirely; and the two integer-valued filters, `school_year` on terms and `grade_level` on students, are read as decimal integers, so a non-numeric value for either is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). A filter value that is well-formed but matches nothing answers an empty page with `total_count` of 0, never a not-found error, even when the value is an identifier no record carries. Buildings may be filtered by `status` and `grade_band`. Terms may be filtered by `state` and `school_year`. Teachers may be filtered by `school_id` and `employment_status`. Students may be filtered by `school_id`, `duplicate_of_student_id`, `enrollment_state` and `grade_level`. Sections may be filtered by `school_id`, `term_id`, `primary_teacher_id`, `status` and `roster_health`. Enrollments may be filtered by `section_id`, `student_id`, `roster_status` and `add_drop_class`. Vendor licenses may be filtered by `term_id`, `status`, `rostering_standard` and `sync_health`. Building allocations may be filtered by `vendor_app_id` and `school_id`. App accounts may be filtered by `building_allocation_id`, `student_id`, `teacher_id`, `subject_type`, `seat_status`, `identity_status` and `password_state`. Sync runs may be filtered by `vendor_app_id`, `state` and `run_type`. Triage items may be filtered by `sync_run_id`, `subject_section_id`, `subject_student_id`, `duplicate_candidate_student_id`, `category`, `severity` and `status`.

Two computed reads take a single query parameter of their own rather than a filter set: the vendor roster feed accepts `include_archived`, and the renewal summary accepts `basis`. Both are described where their business meaning lives. `include_archived` is read as a boolean query value: `true`, `1` or `yes` in any letter case mean true, `false`, `0` or `no` mean false, an omitted or empty value means false, and anything else is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). Neither computed read is paginated, so `limit` and `offset` are ignored on both.

### 2.4. Common Record Properties, Identifiers and the Logical Clock

Every persisted record carries a server-minted `id` in UUID form with no type prefix — a random version-4 UUID in the canonical lower-case hyphenated 36-character spelling. Clients never supply an `id`, and identifiers are never derived from a business key. Every record also carries `created_at`, the ISO 8601 UTC instant of creation with exactly three fractional-second digits (for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`). Every record in this system additionally carries `updated_at`, in the same format, and `version`, an integer that begins at 1 when the record is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that record. Whenever the specification says that a record is mutated — a state change, a counter recomputation, a field overwrite, or a cascade reaching it from another record's transition — both `updated_at` and `version` move: `updated_at` is refreshed to the current instant and `version` is incremented by one. A brand-new record is stamped with `created_at` and `updated_at` equal to each other and to the clock instant of its creation. Whenever a record is serialised, `id` is the first field of the object and `created_at`, `updated_at` and `version` are its last three fields in that order, with the entity's own fields between them in a fixed order; keys are never alphabetised, and the server's private creation-sequence bookkeeping is never part of the emitted object. Every field an entity declares appears in every serialisation of that entity, carrying `null` when it holds no value — a field is never omitted merely because it is unset, and the same field set is emitted by the creating response, by the list read and by the single-record read alike.

All date-only fields are ISO 8601 calendar dates in `YYYY-MM-DD` form; all instant-valued fields are ISO 8601 UTC timestamps with three fractional digits. A malformed date or instant is a request-shape failure and yields `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). On the way in the server is tolerant about the fractional part and the zone marker of an instant: it accepts none through nine fractional digits, accepts either a `Z` suffix or a numeric `±HH:MM` offset — normalising the latter to UTC before the value is used — accepts a lower-case `t` or `z`, and ignores surrounding whitespace. A date, by contrast, must be exactly four-digit year, two-digit month and two-digit day; a calendar-impossible value such as a thirty-first of February is malformed. Everything the server writes back is normalised: dates as `YYYY-MM-DD`, instants always with exactly three fractional digits and a `Z` suffix.

Time in this service is a **logical clock**, not wall-clock time. Before any client sets it, the clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` — emitted as `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`; this startup default is mandatory and the service never falls back to real time. An administrative test-driving capability sets the clock to a supplied `now` instant and answers with that instant: its `data` is an object carrying the single field `now`, holding the accepted instant re-normalised to three fractional digits with a `Z` suffix, and nothing else. The clock may be set to any instant, forwards or backwards, as often as a client likes; nothing else in the service moves it. All state — the clock included — lives in the process, and the process starts with every collection empty: nothing is seeded, so a freshly booted service holds no building, term, licence, account or run at all, and restarting it both empties the district and returns the clock to the startup default. Boot is driven by `start.sh` in the service directory, which installs the Python dependencies only when they are missing and then serves the application single-threaded — one request handled at a time, in arrival order, with no debug mode and no auto-reloader — on the port named by the `PORT` environment variable, or 8000 when that variable is unset. Every rule in this document that speaks of "the clock instant" or "the clock date" reads the logical clock: password expiry and lockout evaluation, the archived-access retention window, per-seat proration and rate selection, the usage-recency window of the renewal score, and every `*_at` stamp the server writes. The "clock date" always means the date part of the logical clock instant.

### 2.5. Path Resolution and Request-Shape Screening

Identifiers appearing in a URL path are resolved **left to right**, and the first segment that fails to resolve decides the response with HTTP 404: `school_id` yields `ERR_SCHOOL_NOT_FOUND`, `term_id` yields `ERR_TERM_NOT_FOUND`, `teacher_id` yields `ERR_TEACHER_NOT_FOUND`, `student_id` yields `ERR_STUDENT_NOT_FOUND`, `section_id` yields `ERR_SECTION_NOT_FOUND`, `enrollment_id` yields `ERR_ENROLLMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `vendor_app_id` yields `ERR_VENDOR_APP_NOT_FOUND`, `building_allocation_id` yields `ERR_BUILDING_ALLOCATION_NOT_FOUND`, `app_account_id` yields `ERR_APP_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND`, `sync_run_id` yields `ERR_SYNC_RUN_NOT_FOUND` and `error_queue_item_id` yields `ERR_ERROR_QUEUE_ITEM_NOT_FOUND`. A path identifier is matched as an opaque string against the stored `id` values, so a value that is not even UUID-shaped is simply an identifier that resolves to nothing and takes the same not-found error. A trailing slash is insignificant — `/rostering/schools/` and `/rostering/schools` are the same endpoint — and a request whose path matches no endpoint of the service at all, or whose method is not one the matched path defines, is answered in the envelope of §2.2 with `error_id` of `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` under HTTP 404 and HTTP 405 respectively — and that is so even when the request carries no authentication headers, because routing fails before there is any endpoint whose role list could be consulted.

After path resolution, a write request's body is screened for shape. Every non-optional input field must be present, every value of an enumerated field must be one of that field's declared values compared exactly and case-sensitively, every integer must lie inside its declared range, dates must be ISO calendar dates and instants must be ISO UTC instants, `meeting_days` must be a non-empty duplicate-free selection from `MON`, `TUE`, `WED`, `THU` and `FRI`, `grade_band_scope` must be a non-empty duplicate-free selection from `ELEMENTARY`, `MIDDLE` and `HIGH`, and an account-provisioning body must carry exactly one of `student_id` or `teacher_id`, the one that agrees with its `subject_type`. Any of these failures is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). Screening reads the body field by field, and the following conventions apply throughout. A field supplied as JSON `null` counts as **absent**: a required field that is null is refused exactly as a missing one is, and an optional field that is null falls back to its declared default exactly as omission does. A string field is refused when it is the empty string, just as if it had been omitted, and it is refused when its value is of any other JSON type — a number is not coerced to a string. An integer field must be a JSON integer: a boolean, a fractional number and a numeric string are all refused, and only then is the declared range compared, inclusive at both ends. A boolean field must likewise be a JSON boolean, so the strings `"true"` and `"false"` and the integers 1 and 0 are refused. A duplicate-free selection array is stored and echoed back in exactly the order the client supplied it and is never sorted into a canonical order. Finally, body keys the endpoint does not declare are **ignored** rather than refused, so an unexpected extra field never by itself fails a request. A body that is absent or empty is read as the empty object, so its missing required fields are what the refusal reports; a body that is not parseable JSON, or that parses to a JSON array, number, string or boolean rather than to an object, is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400), while a literal `null` body is read as the empty object. Four transitions declare no input field at all — activating a term, rolling a term over, activating a license and releasing a seat — and they do not screen the body in any way, so a body that is malformed JSON or of the wrong JSON type is simply ignored there rather than refused; transform and publish, which also declare no input field, do screen it and refuse a malformed one. The `Content-Type` header is not examined — a well-formed JSON object body is accepted whatever the client declares.

Only after shape screening are foreign keys carried in the request **body** resolved, in the order the fields are declared in that endpoint's input list, again with HTTP 404 and the same per-entity error identifiers listed above (`ERR_SCHOOL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TERM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TEACHER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STUDENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SECTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VENDOR_APP_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BUILDING_ALLOCATION_NOT_FOUND`). A `primary_teacher_id` or `replacement_teacher_id` that does not resolve is an `ERR_TEACHER_NOT_FOUND`; a `surviving_student_id` that does not resolve is an `ERR_STUDENT_NOT_FOUND`.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency on the Vendor License

Two writes reshape the district's seat arithmetic, and both are guarded by optimistic concurrency control on the **vendor license record** — never on the record being written. Both take `expected_vendor_app_version` as a required, non-optional input field carrying the `version` of the governing `VendorApp` that the client last observed. It is read as a JSON integer of at least 1, so a missing, non-integer or non-positive value is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` at rung 6 and never a version mismatch.

- `POST /rostering/app-accounts` must carry `expected_vendor_app_version` equal to the current `version` of the license reached through the supplied `building_allocation_id`. If the license's `version` has advanced since the client read it, the provisioning request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VENDOR_APP_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Provisioning a seat for a different subject is a perfectly legitimate act at any time, so no state precondition can stand in for this check; the request is refused purely because the client's view of the license — its `total_seats`, its `overage_policy`, its allocation split, its `consumed_seats` — is out of date.
- `PATCH /rostering/building-allocations/{building_allocation_id}` must carry `expected_vendor_app_version` equal to the current `version` of the license that owns the allocation named in the path. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VENDOR_APP_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Re-cutting two different buildings are both legitimate operations that no state precondition would refuse; only the parent-version check makes the seat-pool arithmetic safe.

The version check is evaluated **after** path resolution and body shape screening, so a stale-version reply never reveals whether a malformed body would also have been rejected. No other endpoint takes a version field of any kind: every other write is decided entirely by its state preconditions.

### 2.7. The Order in Which a Request Is Refused

A single request frequently violates more than one rule at once. The service therefore evaluates its rules in one fixed global order, and **the first violated rule decides the response; no later rule is evaluated**. Implementers should treat the following ladder as normative, because a request that breaks rules at rungs 11 and 39 must report the rung-11 error and not the rung-39 one. The ladder runs in nine tiers.

**Tier A — authentication and authorization.** (1) Both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` present and non-blank, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. (2) The role is admitted by the target operation, else `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`. (3) Scope binding for the two scoped roles, else `ERR_SCOPE_VIOLATION` — this guards exactly five operations: amending a vendor license, re-cutting a building allocation, posting usage telemetry, reading a vendor's roster feed and reading a vendor's renewal summary. (4) Segregation of duties, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — this guards exactly two operations: publishing a sync run and activating a license.

**Tier B — existence and request shape.** (5) Path identifiers resolve left to right, else the matching not-found error. (6) Request shape, else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; this applies to every write. (7) Body foreign keys resolve in declaration order, else the matching not-found error.

**Tier C — optimistic concurrency.** (8) `expected_vendor_app_version` matches the governing license's `version`, else `ERR_STALE_VENDOR_APP_VERSION`.

**Tier D — state preconditions.** (9) The governing building has `status` of `ACTIVE` when a teacher, a student or a section is created under it, else `ERR_SCHOOL_CLOSED`. (10) The governing term is in the state the operation requires, else `ERR_TERM_NOT_ACTIVE`: `ACTIVE` for creating a section, creating an enrollment, requesting a drop, and opening a `FULL` or `DELTA` sync run; `ACTIVE` also to roll the term over; `PLANNED` to activate it — six operations in total. (11) The student is `ENROLLED` when being enrolled, withdrawn, or given an app account, else `ERR_STUDENT_NOT_ENROLLED`; a `WITHDRAWN` or `MERGED_DUPLICATE` student is inert. (12) The teacher's `employment_status` is `ACTIVE` when the teacher is named as a section's teacher of record, when the teacher is separated, and when the teacher is given an app account, else `ERR_TEACHER_SEPARATED`. (13) The enrollment's `roster_status` is `PENDING_ADD` or `ACTIVE` when a drop is requested, else `ERR_ENROLLMENT_NOT_DROPPABLE`. (14) The license `status` is `ACTIVE` when a sync run is opened, when an app account is provisioned against one of its allocations, and when one of its building allocations is re-cut, else `ERR_VENDOR_APP_NOT_ACTIVE` — `DRAFT`, `SUSPENDED` and `TERMINATED` licenses accept none of the three. (15) Activation requires license `status` of `DRAFT`, else `ERR_VENDOR_APP_ALREADY_ACTIVATED`. (16) A run that is not `SUPERSEDED` must be in the state the operation requires — transform requires a run in `EXTRACTED` or `HELD` and publish requires a run in `TRANSFORMED` — else `ERR_SYNC_RUN_STATE_INVALID`; a `SUPERSEDED` run is decided by rung 17 instead and never reports this error, even though `SUPERSEDED` is not one of the states either verb admits. (17) The run is not `SUPERSEDED`, else `ERR_SYNC_RUN_SUPERSEDED`; this guards four operations — transform, publish, and both triage resolutions. (18) The account still holds its seat, with `seat_status` of `RESERVED` or `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_SEAT_ALREADY_RELEASED`; this guards exactly three operations — releasing a seat, posting usage telemetry and deprovisioning an identity. Password setting is deliberately **exempt** from rung 18, because the identity axis is independent: a summer-suspended identity whose seat has already been released can still be reactivated. (19) The account's `identity_status` is not `DEPROVISIONED` for password setting, usage telemetry and further deprovision calls, else `ERR_ACCOUNT_DEPROVISIONED`; a deprovisioned identity is frozen. (20) A triage item has `status` of `OPEN` and its `category` matches the resolution verb, else `ERR_ERROR_QUEUE_ITEM_NOT_OPEN`. (21) Archived-class access is inside the retention window, else `ERR_ARCHIVE_WINDOW_CLOSED`; this guards three operations — reading an `ARCHIVED` section, requesting a roster feed with `include_archived` set to true, and opening a `ROLLOVER` sync run.

**Tier E — identity and password safety.** (22) A `LOCKED` password is terminal for password setting and the attempt is refused with `ERR_ACCOUNT_LOCKED` (HTTP 403); there is no unlock flag on the request body, and no role other than `IdentityAdmin` may even attempt a reset. (23) The supplied `new_password` is at least as long as the subject building's `min_password_length`, else `ERR_PASSWORD_TOO_SHORT`. (24) When the subject building's `password_requires_mixed_case` is true, `new_password` contains at least one lower-case and at least one upper-case letter, else `ERR_PASSWORD_COMPLEXITY`; because length is checked at rung 23, a short all-lower-case password reports `ERR_PASSWORD_TOO_SHORT` and not `ERR_PASSWORD_COMPLEXITY`. (25) Publish is refused while the run's `blocking_error_count` is greater than zero, with `ERR_BLOCKING_ERRORS_UNRESOLVED`. (26) An explicit deprovision is never post-dated, else `ERR_DEPROVISION_NOT_DUE`.

**Tier F — temporal constraints.** (27) A new term's dates are ordered so that `start_date` is on or before `add_drop_cutoff_date`, which is on or before `end_date`, and `end_date` is strictly later than `start_date`, else `ERR_TERM_DATE_ORDER`. (28) Term lifecycle windows, else `ERR_TERM_WINDOW_CLOSED`. (29) An enrollment's `requested_effective_date` falls inside the section's term window, else `ERR_EFFECTIVE_DATE_OUT_OF_TERM`. (30) A drop's `drop_effective_date` is not earlier than the enrollment's effective date and not later than the term's `end_date`, else `ERR_DROP_BEFORE_ADD`. (31) No student double-booking, else `ERR_STUDENT_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT`. (32) No teacher double-booking across the travel buffer, else `ERR_TEACHER_PERIOD_CONFLICT`; this guards two operations — creating a section and resolving an unmatched-teacher triage item by attaching a replacement.

**Tier G — referential coherence.** (33) Building coherence, else `ERR_SCHOOL_MISMATCH`, over three operations: an enrollment requires the student's `school_id` to equal the section's `school_id`; provisioning an app account requires the subject's `school_id` to equal the allocation's `school_id`; attaching a replacement teacher requires that teacher's `school_id` to equal the section's `school_id`. (34) A merge's `surviving_student_id` is one of the triage item's two duplicate candidates and the two candidates are distinct students of the same building, else `ERR_MERGE_SUBJECT_MISMATCH`. (35) A username can be derived for this subject under this vendor's `identifier_strategy`, else `ERR_IDENTIFIER_UNAVAILABLE`.

**Tier H — uniqueness and capacity.** (36) `sis_student_id` is unique across the district and `sis_staff_id` is unique across the district, else `ERR_DUPLICATE_SIS_ID`. (37) At most one enrollment that is not `DROPPED` exists for a given section-and-student pair, else `ERR_DUPLICATE_ENROLLMENT`. (38) At most one seat-holding app account exists for a given license-and-subject pair, else `ERR_DUPLICATE_APP_ACCOUNT`. (39) The section has a free physical seat, else `ERR_SECTION_AT_CAPACITY`. (40) The subject's own grade band is in the license's `grade_band_scope`, else `ERR_GRADE_BAND_OUT_OF_SCOPE`. (41) Activation finds at least one eligible building, else `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_BUILDINGS`. (42) A re-cut that raises `allocated_seats` keeps the license inside its purchased total, else `ERR_ALLOCATION_EXCEEDS_LICENSE`. (43) A re-cut that lowers `allocated_seats` does not fall below that building's `consumed_seats`, else `ERR_ALLOCATION_BELOW_CONSUMED`.

**Tier I — fiscal seat admission, always last.** (44) The target building admits the seat, else `ERR_BUILDING_ALLOCATION_EXHAUSTED`. (45) Under a district-pool license whose target building is already full, the district pool is non-empty, else `ERR_DISTRICT_SEATS_EXHAUSTED`.

### 2.8. Integer Money, Integer Seats and Rounding Discipline

All money in this system is expressed in **integer cents**; no monetary field is ever fractional and no monetary value is ever stored as a decimal string. All seat counts are integers; no seat is ever fractional. Rounding, therefore, is not a presentation concern but a specification concern, and each calculation names its own direction:

- Per-seat proration rounds **up** (ceiling) and rounds up **once per subject**, before any summation.
- The volume discount on an invoice projection rounds **down** (floor); the platform fee rounds **up** (ceiling); the shelfware credit rounds **down** (floor).
- Proportional seat allocation takes the **floor** of every raw share first and then distributes the leftover seats one at a time.
- The renewal score is the only non-integer stored figure: it is rounded **half up to exactly two decimal places**, once, at the very end of its computation. `utilization`, `engagement` and `cost_efficiency` are exposed as unrounded ratios on the renewal summary read.

Where a calculation pins an evaluation order, that order is part of the contract. Reordering the steps of a pinned calculation produces different integers even when every individual step is implemented correctly, and the differences are not rounding noise — they are wrong answers.

### 2.9. Cascades, Counters and Derived Fields

Several transitions in this system are not local. A student withdrawal reaches the student's enrollments, from the enrollments to the affected app accounts, from the accounts to the building allocations that lent them seats, and from the allocations to the license counters — four hops deep, each hop caused by the hop before it rather than by the original call. Summer rollover has the same shape. Whenever a cascade reaches a record and changes something on it, that record's own `updated_at` and `version` move, exactly as if it had been written directly — and, symmetrically, a hop that finds every field it would write already holding the value it would write leaves that record completely untouched, with no `version` bump at all. Counter maintenance is change-detected in exactly that way: recomputing `School.enrolled_student_count`, `Section.active_enrollment_count`, `Student.active_enrollment_count`, `BuildingAllocation.consumed_seats` or `borrowed_seats`, or a sync run's two error counts, to the figure the record already held is invisible on that record. The `VendorApp` is the one exception: its five derived figures — `consumed_seats`, `unallocated_seats`, `committed_charge_cents`, `renewal_score` and `renewal_recommendation` — form a single block that every cascade reaching the license recomputes as a whole, so wherever this document names only some of them at a cascade's last hop it is abbreviating rather than restricting; the two writes that move one figure of the block on its own are the activation split and the allocation re-cut of §3.5, each of which resets `unallocated_seats` and nothing else derived. Every recomputation of that block moves the license's `updated_at` and `version` whether or not any figure actually changed, which is why every write that recomputes it — an account provisioned, a seat released, an identity deprovisioned, a usage report, a publish, an amendment — advances the `version` that §2.6 makes clients quote back.

Four families of derived values are maintained by the service and are never supplied by clients:

- **Building headcount.** `School.enrolled_student_count` is the number of students whose `school_id` is that building and whose `enrollment_state` is `ENROLLED`. It is recomputed whenever a student is created, withdrawn, or merged away as a duplicate.
- **Roster counts.** `Section.active_enrollment_count` is the number of enrollments of that section whose `roster_status` is `PENDING_ADD` or `ACTIVE` — `PENDING_DROP` and `DROPPED` rows are not counted, which is why a pending drop does not free a physical seat. `Student.active_enrollment_count` is the number of enrollments of that student whose `roster_status` is `PENDING_ADD`, `ACTIVE` or `PENDING_DROP` — here a pending drop *is* counted, because the student is still scheduled into the class.
- **Seat counters.** `BuildingAllocation.consumed_seats` is the number of app accounts drawing on that allocation whose `seat_status` is `RESERVED` or `ACTIVE`; `BuildingAllocation.borrowed_seats` is the number of those accounts that took their seat from the district pool rather than from the building's own grant. `VendorApp.consumed_seats` is the number of accounts under all of that license's allocations whose `seat_status` is `RESERVED` or `ACTIVE`. `VendorApp.unallocated_seats` is the license's `total_seats` minus the sum of `allocated_seats` across its allocations minus the sum of `borrowed_seats` across its allocations; while the license is still `DRAFT` it equals `total_seats`.
- **Committed money.** `VendorApp.committed_charge_cents` is the sum, over **every** app account of that license regardless of `seat_status`, of that account's `seat_charge_cents` minus its `refunded_charge_cents`. It is defined by nothing else. No sync-run charge figure is ever posted into it.

Nested resources are reached through flat, peer-level collections filtered by foreign key rather than through nested URLs, so an enrollment is found by filtering enrollments on `section_id` or `student_id` rather than by walking a section's subtree. Where a path does carry more than one identifier, resolution remains left to right as described in §2.5.

### 2.10. Boundary Conventions

Boundary directions in this domain are deliberately not uniform, and the differences are load-bearing. Three conventions must be implemented exactly as written:

1. **Password expiry is inclusive.** An account whose password would expire at a given instant is still usable one millisecond before that instant and is expired exactly on it — the comparison is that the clock instant is at or after `password_expires_at`.
2. **The archived-access window is strict.** An archived class is readable while the clock instant is strictly earlier than `archived_access_expires_at`; at that instant the window is already shut. This is the opposite boundary direction from password expiry, and both must be honoured.
3. **Thresholds compare with "at least".** Lockout occurs when the cumulative `failed_login_count` reaches the building's `password_lockout_threshold`, not when it exceeds it. Renewal tiers, discount tiers and seat-admission comparisons are likewise inclusive on their floors, as each calculation states.

Date arithmetic on a retention or age window is **calendar-day addition on a date**, not a multiplication of hours: ninety retention days after `2026-12-18` is `2027-03-18`, anchored at `00:00:00.000Z`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The District Backbone: Buildings, Password Policy and the Academic Term

Everything in this system hangs off two pieces of reference data that the district registers before a school year opens: its buildings and its terms.

A `RosterOperator` registers a building with its display `name`, the `sis_school_id` that identifies it in the source-of-truth extract, the `building_code` that will appear in vendor transformation output, and its `grade_band`, which is one of `ELEMENTARY`, `MIDDLE` or `HIGH`. The SIS identifier and the building code are each expected to be unique across the district, but neither is enforced by the service — no uniqueness rule guards buildings at all, so a second registration that reuses an existing `sis_school_id` or `building_code` is accepted and simply mints another building; rung 36 of §2.7 covers the student and staff identifiers and nothing else. The display name runs from 1 to 120 characters, the SIS identifier from 1 to 32 and the building code from 1 to 16. Buildings are born with `status` of `ACTIVE` and an `enrolled_student_count` of zero; the only way that status ever changes is an explicit amendment, whose optional `status` field accepts `ACTIVE` and `CLOSED` and nothing else — any other value, `ARCHIVED` or a mis-cased spelling included, is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — so the same amendment both closes a building by moving `status` to `CLOSED` and reopens a closed one by moving it back to `ACTIVE`. A closed building is excluded from the candidate set when a license is activated, but it keeps supplying its stored policy values to accounts that were already provisioned out of it.

Buildings also carry the district's **password policy**, and they carry it per building rather than centrally, because a K–2 primary school and a comprehensive high school do not tolerate the same rules. Five policy fields may be supplied at registration and each falls back to a district default when omitted: `min_password_length` defaults to 8 and must lie between 6 and 64; `password_requires_mixed_case` defaults to true; `password_max_age_days` defaults to 180 and must lie between 30 and 365 — a single month at the low end and a full calendar year at the high end; `password_lockout_threshold` defaults to 5 and must lie between 3 and 20 failed logins; and `temp_password_ttl_hours` defaults to 72 — three days — and must lie between 1 hour and 336 hours, which is a fortnight. A later amendment may rewrite any of these five values or close the building; every field of the amendment body is optional and an omitted or null one leaves the stored value exactly as it stood rather than resetting it to its district default, the amendment is never refused merely because the building is already `CLOSED`, and each amendment increments the building's `version` — including one whose body names none of the six amendable fields at all. Creating a teacher, a student or a section under a `CLOSED` building is refused with `ERR_SCHOOL_CLOSED`.

Terms are the other half of the backbone. A term is created with its `name`, its `school_year` (the starting calendar year, between 2000 and 2100), its `start_date` and `end_date` as ISO calendar dates with the end strictly after the start, its `add_drop_cutoff_date` — the last date on which an add or a drop still counts as being inside the add/drop window — and its `instructional_days`, an integer from 1 to 250 recorded as the term's instructional day count and reported back on the term for reference only — it is stored and echoed and never enters any computation, proration included, whose denominator is always the inclusive **calendar**-day span of §3.6. Optionally the term carries `archive_retention_days`, which defaults to 90 and may run from 0 to 730; this is how long archived-class reads stay permitted after the term ends. Terms are created in `PLANNED` state with `archived_access_expires_at` left null. The three dates must be ordered so that the start is on or before the cutoff and the cutoff is on or before the end; a term whose dates violate that ordering is refused with `ERR_TERM_DATE_ORDER`.

Before any scheduling can happen, a `RosterOperator` activates the term, taking `state` from `PLANNED` to `ACTIVE` and incrementing the term's `version`. Activation requires the term to be in `PLANNED`, and it requires the clock date to be on or before the term's `end_date` — a term that has already ended cannot be opened, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_TERM_WINDOW_CLOSED`. Because section creation, enrollment creation, drop requests and ordinary sync runs are all gated on an `ACTIVE` term, this single transition is the prerequisite of the entire roster surface. The mirror-image transition — `ACTIVE` to `ROLLED_OVER` — belongs to the summer and is described in §3.8.

### 3.2. Staff, Students and the Records of Record

A `RosterOperator` registers a teacher against a building with `school_id`, the `sis_staff_id` from the extract (1 to 32 characters), the teacher's `full_name` as the SIS delivers it (1 to 120 characters), and a district `email` of 3 to 160 characters that must contain exactly one at-sign, which may be neither the first nor the last character of the value; each of those is a request-shape rule, so a violation is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` at rung 6, screened before `school_id` is even resolved. Nothing requires the email to be unique. Teachers begin with `employment_status` of `ACTIVE` and a null `separation_effective_date`. Students are registered with `school_id`, a `sis_student_id` of 1 to 32 characters, a `first_name` and a `last_name` of 1 to 60 characters each, a `grade_level` between 0 and 12 where 0 means kindergarten, a `date_of_birth` — which is compared against neither the clock nor any term, so even a future date of birth is accepted — and optionally a `state_student_id` of 1 to 32 characters, which is null whenever the state has not yet issued one. A student begins `ENROLLED` with an `active_enrollment_count` of zero, a null `withdrawal_effective_date` and a null `duplicate_of_student_id`, and the owning building's `enrolled_student_count` is recomputed on the spot. Both source-of-truth identifiers are unique district-wide: a second attempt to register the same `sis_student_id`, or the same `sis_staff_id`, is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_SIS_ID`.

Staff separations are handled by a `DataGovernanceOfficer` and are far more disruptive than they look. The transition takes the teacher from `ACTIVE` to `SEPARATED`, stores the supplied `separation_effective_date` — which is required, but is constrained by nothing beyond being a well-formed calendar date: it is compared against neither the clock nor any term window, so a back-dated and a post-dated separation are accepted alike — and increments the teacher's `version`; a teacher who is already `SEPARATED` cannot be separated again, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_TEACHER_SEPARATED`. From there the effects fan out three levels. Every section whose teacher of record was this teacher has its `roster_health` set to `UNMATCHED_TEACHER`, while its `primary_teacher_id` goes on pointing at the now-separated teacher — the separation never clears that link, and a section that already carried `UNMATCHED_TEACHER` is left entirely untouched with no `version` bump. For every such section that sits inside the resolved scope of an in-flight sync run — the in-scope section set of §3.7, never the narrowed feed set, so a license whose `unmatched_teacher_policy` is `SKIP_SECTION` has its runs held exactly as a `HOLD_RUN` license does — a `BLOCKING` triage item of category `UNMATCHED_TEACHER` is raised against that run, and any such run sitting in `EXTRACTED` or `TRANSFORMED` is parked in `HELD` with its `projected_seat_delta` and `projected_charge_cents` reset to null, because the projection it published to the operator is no longer trustworthy; a run already `HELD` stays `HELD` and has those same two figures nulled. Each affected run recomputes its `blocking_error_count`, and the governing license's `sync_health` becomes `BLOCKED` with its `version` incremented. Finally, the teacher's own app accounts — every one of them not already `DEPROVISIONED`, whatever its `seat_status` — have their `identity_status` moved to `DEPROVISIONED`, their `password_state` set to `EXPIRED` and their `deprovision_scheduled_date` stamped with the supplied `separation_effective_date`; the identity never goes to `SUSPENDED`, which is reserved exclusively for summer rollover. The seat axis is deliberately left alone here, so a separated teacher's account keeps the seat it holds and no license seat counter or charge figure moves as a result of the separation.

Withdrawals are the deepest cascade in the system, and they are also handled by a `DataGovernanceOfficer`. The request carries a single `withdrawal_effective_date`. The student must be `ENROLLED`, else `ERR_STUDENT_NOT_ENROLLED`; and the supplied date must fall inside the window running from `start_date` to `end_date` of every term in which the student currently holds a section — where holding a section means an enrollment whose `roster_status` is `PENDING_ADD`, `ACTIVE` or `PENDING_DROP`, `DROPPED` rows being ignored entirely — else `ERR_EFFECTIVE_DATE_OUT_OF_TERM`. On success the student's `enrollment_state` becomes `WITHDRAWN`, `withdrawal_effective_date` is stored and the student's own `active_enrollment_count` is recomputed on that one same write, so the student record is written exactly once and its `version` advances by exactly one however many of its enrollments the withdrawal touched; because §2.9 counts `PENDING_DROP` rows in that figure, a post-dated withdrawal whose drops merely pend leaves the count non-zero. The building's `enrolled_student_count` is recomputed as well — and then four hops run, each caused by the hop before it rather than by the withdrawal call itself.

On the second hop, every enrollment of this student whose `roster_status` is `PENDING_ADD` or `ACTIVE` is wound down: it becomes `DROPPED` when the withdrawal date is on or before the clock date, and `PENDING_DROP` when the withdrawal date is still in the future. In both cases `drop_effective_date` is stamped with the withdrawal date, and the affected sections recompute `active_enrollment_count`. On the third hop, every enrollment that actually reached `DROPPED` releases the student's seat-holding app accounts — those whose `seat_status` is `RESERVED` or `ACTIVE` — for each license that no longer retains the student in scope: `seat_status` becomes `RELEASED`, `refunded_charge_cents` is set to the account's `seat_charge_cents` when the clock date is on or before the term's `add_drop_cutoff_date` and to zero otherwise, `identity_status` becomes `DEPROVISIONED`, `password_state` becomes `EXPIRED` alongside it, and `deprovision_scheduled_date` is stamped with the withdrawal date. On the fourth hop, each release decrements the owning allocation's `consumed_seats` by one and then recomputes the license's `consumed_seats`, `unallocated_seats`, `committed_charge_cents`, `renewal_score` and `renewal_recommendation`, incrementing that license's `version` by one.

A withdrawal is not the only way a student leaves the roster. The other exit is a duplicate merge, which moves `enrollment_state` to `MERGED_DUPLICATE` and points `duplicate_of_student_id` at the surviving record; the two exits are disjoint, because a withdrawal keeps the student's roster rows attached to that same student while a merge re-points or drops them. Neither exit hides the record: a `WITHDRAWN` or `MERGED_DUPLICATE` student is still served in full by the student collection read and by its own single read, with no window, gate or extra error of any kind — among the plain entity reads the single-section read of §3.8 is the only one that carries a gate at all. Merges are triage work and are described with the error queue in §3.7.

### 3.3. Sections and the Master Schedule

A section is one meeting of one course in one building in one term, and a `RosterOperator` creates it with `school_id`, `term_id`, an optional `primary_teacher_id`, the `sis_section_id` from the extract, 1 to 64 characters and expected to be unique across the district though no uniqueness rule enforces it — a repeated `sis_section_id` is accepted and simply mints another section — the district catalog's `course_code` of 1 to 32 characters, a `subject` drawn from `MATH`, `ELA`, `SCIENCE`, `SOCIAL_STUDIES` and `ELECTIVE`, a `period_index` from 1 to 8 naming the bell-schedule period the class meets in, a `meeting_days` array selected without repetition from `MON`, `TUE`, `WED`, `THU` and `FRI`, and a physical `max_seats` capacity from 1 to 60. Sections are created with `status` of `ACTIVE` and `active_enrollment_count` of zero. Their `roster_health` is set to `UNMATCHED_TEACHER` when `primary_teacher_id` was omitted and to `OK` when a teacher was named; a section left without a teacher of record is exactly what the rostering pipeline will later complain about. Creation requires an `ACTIVE` term (`ERR_TERM_NOT_ACTIVE`), an `ACTIVE` building (`ERR_SCHOOL_CLOSED`), and, when a teacher is named, that teacher to be `ACTIVE` rather than `SEPARATED` (`ERR_TEACHER_SEPARATED`).

Teacher scheduling carries a **travel buffer**, and this is the one rule most likely to be implemented too loosely. A teacher may not be the teacher of record of two `ACTIVE` sections in the same term whose periods fall within one period of each other and which share at least one meeting day. Concretely, the section being created occupies the closed period interval running from its `period_index` minus one to its `period_index` plus one — the travel buffer is one period — and if any existing `ACTIVE` section taught by the same teacher in the same term has a `period_index` inside that interval and shares at least one entry of `meeting_days`, the request is refused with `ERR_TEACHER_PERIOD_CONFLICT`. Adjacent periods therefore **do** conflict for teachers: a teacher assigned to period 3 on Monday and Wednesday cannot also take period 2 or period 4 on Monday. The same rule is re-evaluated when a replacement teacher is attached during triage.

Sections can be amended after the fact. An amendment may resize the section by rewriting `max_seats`, attach or replace its `primary_teacher_id`, or retire it by setting `status`; all three fields are optional and an omitted or null one leaves the stored value untouched, so `primary_teacher_id` can be attached or swapped but never cleared back to null by an amendment, and `max_seats` may be lowered below the section's current `active_enrollment_count` because no capacity rule is evaluated on an amendment at all. Attaching a teacher clears `roster_health` back to `OK`, and the travel-buffer rule is *not* re-evaluated here — rung 32 of §2.7 guards only section creation and the triage match verb, so an amendment may park a teacher in a conflicting period. Retiring a section by moving `status` to `CANCELLED` takes every enrollment of that section that is not already `DROPPED` — `PENDING_DROP` rows included — to `DROPPED`, stamping `drop_effective_date` with the clock date on every such row that does not already carry one and leaving an already-stamped date exactly as it stood; the section then recomputes its `active_enrollment_count`, each affected student recomputes its own, and each affected student runs the same conditional seat-release cascade described in §3.4, with the refund read against the **clock date** at which the cancellation is run rather than against each row's own `drop_effective_date`, and with `identity_status` and `password_state` left exactly as they were, because a cancellation releases seats without touching the identity axis. That cascade runs only when the amendment actually moves `status` into `CANCELLED` from some other value: re-sending `CANCELLED` against an already-`CANCELLED` section runs no cascade and moves nothing but the `version`. Every amendment increments the section's `version`, including one whose body names no amendable field at all. The third value of `status`, `ARCHIVED`, is never set by an amendment — it is written only by summer rollover, and a body supplying `status` of `ARCHIVED` is refused at rung 6 with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — but the amendment itself applies no status precondition of its own, so an `ARCHIVED` or `CANCELLED` section can still be resized, re-teachered, cancelled or moved back to `ACTIVE`; the archived-access window of §3.8 gates only the three operations rung 21 of §2.7 names, and amending a section is not one of them.

### 3.4. Add, Drop and the Mid-Year Schedule Change

Adding a student to a section is a `RosterOperator` action carrying `section_id`, `student_id`, a `requested_effective_date`, and optionally `source`, which records the provenance of the row as either `SIS_EXTRACT` or `MANUAL` and defaults to `MANUAL`. The new row is created with `roster_status` of `PENDING_ADD`, a null `drop_effective_date`, and an `effective_date` stamped from `requested_effective_date`; that stamp is not rewritten again except by the publish transition described in §3.7.

The add's timing is then classified and, in the common case, immediately honoured. Its `add_drop_class` is computed once, at creation, from the term that owns the section: `IN_WINDOW` when the date it was stamped with is on or before the term's `add_drop_cutoff_date`, and `LATE_ADD` otherwise. This classification is never recomputed later, even if a publish moves the effective date past the cutoff. Separately, when `requested_effective_date` is on or before the clock date, the row advances from `PENDING_ADD` to `ACTIVE` **in the same request**. Both the section's and the student's `active_enrollment_count` are then recomputed whichever of the two statuses the new row settled in, because §2.9 counts `PENDING_ADD` rows in both figures, so even a future-dated add that stays `PENDING_ADD` moves both counters. The boundary is exact: a request whose effective date equals the clock date lands `ACTIVE`, and a request one day in the future stays `PENDING_ADD` until a publish picks it up.

Six things can go wrong on an add, and the ladder in §2.7 decides which one is reported. The term must be `ACTIVE` (`ERR_TERM_NOT_ACTIVE`) and the student must be `ENROLLED` (`ERR_STUDENT_NOT_ENROLLED`). The `requested_effective_date` must fall inside the section's term window, from `start_date` through `end_date` inclusive, else `ERR_EFFECTIVE_DATE_OUT_OF_TERM`. The student may not be double-booked: taking the half-open date interval from the new row's `requested_effective_date` up to but not including its drop date — or up to but not including the term's `end_date` when no drop has been requested — that interval must not overlap the corresponding interval of any other enrollment of the same student that is not `DROPPED` and whose section has the **same** `period_index` and at least one shared entry of `meeting_days`; a violation is `ERR_STUDENT_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT`. The other row's interval runs from its own `effective_date` — the stamped one, which a publish may have moved later than the date originally requested — up to but not including its `drop_effective_date`, or up to but not including the `end_date` of that other section's term when it carries no drop date; the other section's own `status` is not consulted, and neither is its term, because the date intervals already do that work. Two intervals that merely touch, where one ends on the day the next begins, do not overlap. Note the asymmetry with teachers: the student rule uses exact period equality over a date interval, while the teacher rule of §3.3 uses a one-period buffer and ignores dates. The student and the section must belong to the same building, comparing the student's `school_id` to the section's `school_id`, else `ERR_SCHOOL_MISMATCH`. At most one enrollment that is not `DROPPED` may exist for a given section-and-student pair, else `ERR_DUPLICATE_ENROLLMENT`. And the section must have a free physical seat: its `active_enrollment_count` must be strictly less than its `max_seats`, else `ERR_SECTION_AT_CAPACITY` — remembering from §2.9 that this count includes `PENDING_ADD` and `ACTIVE` rows but not `PENDING_DROP` or `DROPPED` ones, so a pending drop does not free a chair. The section's own `status` is not among the preconditions of an add — the state gate is on the term, not on the section — so an add against a `CANCELLED` section whose term is still `ACTIVE` is accepted and takes a chair like any other.

Drops are requested with a single `drop_effective_date` against an enrollment that is `PENDING_ADD` or `ACTIVE`; a row that is already `PENDING_DROP` or `DROPPED` cannot be dropped again and yields `ERR_ENROLLMENT_NOT_DROPPABLE`. The date must not precede the row's effective date — for a row still in `PENDING_ADD`, its `requested_effective_date` — and must not fall after the term's `end_date`; either violation is `ERR_DROP_BEFORE_ADD`. What happens next depends entirely on whether the drop is post-dated. A drop dated after the clock date — on an `ACTIVE` row and on a `PENDING_ADD` row alike — records `drop_effective_date` and moves `roster_status` to `PENDING_DROP`: **no seat is released and no refund is issued yet**, and because a `PENDING_DROP` row leaves the section's `active_enrollment_count`, the chair becomes available immediately even though the license seat does not. A drop dated on or before the clock date takes the row straight to `DROPPED`, from `PENDING_ADD` or from `ACTIVE` alike, and runs the seat-release cascade. Both `active_enrollment_count` figures are recomputed on either branch of the drop, the student's own count simply coming out unchanged on the pending branch because §2.9 counts `PENDING_DROP` rows in it.

That cascade is **conditional**, and the condition is the subtlest piece of seat accounting in the system. For each of the student's app accounts whose `seat_status` is `RESERVED` or `ACTIVE`, look at the license behind that account's `building_allocation_id`. The account is released **only** when the student, after this drop, holds zero enrollments with `roster_status` of `PENDING_ADD`, `ACTIVE` or `PENDING_DROP` whose section simultaneously belongs to that license's term, is itself `ACTIVE`, and sits in a building whose `grade_band` is listed in that license's `grade_band_scope`. In other words, a student who drops one of two math sections that both feed the same vendor keeps the seat; a student who drops the last one loses it. A release sets `seat_status` to `RELEASED` and sets `refunded_charge_cents` to the account's `seat_charge_cents` when `drop_effective_date` is on or before the term's `add_drop_cutoff_date`, and to zero otherwise. On the next hop, each release decrements the owning allocation's `consumed_seats` by one and recomputes the license's `consumed_seats`, `committed_charge_cents`, `renewal_score` and `renewal_recommendation`, incrementing that license's `version` by one.

### 3.5. Vendor Licenses, Seat Entitlement and Building Allocation

A `VendorIntegrationManager` registers a vendor license, and in doing so fixes both the *shape* of the roster the vendor will receive and the *entitlement* the district has bought. The registration payload carries the product `name` of 1 to 120 characters, a short `vendor_code` of 1 to 32 characters used in feed filenames and expected to be unique across all licenses though no uniqueness rule enforces it — a repeated `vendor_code` is accepted and simply mints another license — the `term_id` of the term this license and its rosters cover, and then the per-vendor transformation rules: `rostering_standard`, one of `ONEROSTER_CSV_1P1`, `ONEROSTER_REST_1P2` or `VENDOR_NATIVE_JSON`, which drives the shape of the roster feed; `identifier_strategy`, one of `SIS_ID`, `EMAIL_LOCALPART` or `STATE_ID`, which derives each account's username; a `username_domain` of 3 to 64 lower-case characters containing no at-sign, appended to every derived username; and an optional `name_format` of `LAST_FIRST` or `FIRST_LAST`, defaulting to `LAST_FIRST`, which shapes the display name in the feed. Entitlement is described by `grade_band_scope`, a non-empty duplicate-free selection over `ELEMENTARY`, `MIDDLE` and `HIGH`; `seat_model`, either `PER_STUDENT` or `PER_STUDENT_AND_TEACHER`, which decides whether teacher seats are licensed alongside student seats; `allocation_policy`, either `PROPORTIONAL_HEADCOUNT` or `FIXED_BUILDING_BLOCKS`; `block_seats`, an integer from 1 to 10000 that is **required when and only when** the policy is `FIXED_BUILDING_BLOCKS` and must be omitted otherwise; `overage_policy`, either `BLOCK` or `POOL_FALLBACK`, defaulting to `BLOCK`; `unmatched_teacher_policy`, either `HOLD_RUN` or `SKIP_SECTION`, defaulting to `HOLD_RUN`; `total_seats`, from 1 to 100000, the seats actually purchased; and `price_per_seat_cents`, from 1 to 10000000, the full-term list price of a single seat in cents.

A newly registered license is `DRAFT`. Its `created_by_user_id` is stamped with the registering operator's `X-User-ID`, its `activated_by_user_id` is null, its `unallocated_seats` equals `total_seats`, its `consumed_seats` and `committed_charge_cents` are zero, its `sync_health` is `OK`, and both `renewal_score` and `renewal_recommendation` are null until at least one app account exists under it. A `DRAFT` license rosters nothing and provisions nothing: opening a sync run against it, or provisioning an account against one of its allocations, is refused with `ERR_VENDOR_APP_NOT_ACTIVE`.

Activation is the two-person control. A `DataGovernanceOfficer` — necessarily somebody other than the operator recorded in `created_by_user_id`, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` — activates a license that must still be in `DRAFT`, else `ERR_VENDOR_APP_ALREADY_ACTIVATED`. Activation moves `status` from `DRAFT` to `ACTIVE`, stamps `activated_by_user_id` with the activating officer's `X-User-ID`, increments the license's `version`, and — most importantly — **splits the purchased seats across the district's eligible buildings**, creating one building allocation per eligible building.

The candidate buildings are every building whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `grade_band` appears in the license's `grade_band_scope`, whether or not that building currently holds any sections or students; `CLOSED` buildings are excluded. If the candidate set is empty the activation is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_BUILDINGS`. The candidates are then put in a fixed order — by `enrolled_student_count` descending, and where two buildings tie, by `created_at` ascending, and where they tie on that too, by the order in which the buildings were registered — and the split proceeds by policy. One allocation row is then written per candidate, in exactly that candidate order, so the allocations of a freshly activated license list in headcount order rather than in building-registration order.

Under `PROPORTIONAL_HEADCOUNT`, let the district headcount be the sum of `enrolled_student_count` over the candidates. When that headcount is zero — a brand-new district, or a license scoped to buildings with no students yet — the seats are divided evenly: each building gets the whole-number quotient of `total_seats` divided by the number of candidates, and the leftover seats, one each, go to the first buildings in the candidate order until the leftovers run out. When the headcount is greater than zero, each building's exact share is `total_seats` multiplied by that building's `enrolled_student_count` and divided by the district headcount, held as an exact fraction with no rounding at all. Each building is then granted the **floor** of its exact share. The leftover — `total_seats` minus the sum of those floors, which is always at least zero and always fewer than the number of candidates — is then handed out one seat at a time to buildings ranked by the fractional part of their exact share descending, breaking ties first by `enrolled_student_count` descending, then by `created_at` ascending, and finally by registration order.

**This rounding order is pinned and rounding each raw share to the nearest whole seat is forbidden**, because per-share rounding does not conserve the purchased total. Consider ten seats spread over three buildings with headcounts of 7, 7 and 1, a district headcount of 15. The exact shares are 4.6667, 4.6667 and 0.6667; the floors are 4, 4 and 0, summing to 8, so two seats are left over. All three fractional parts are equal, so the ranking falls through to headcount descending and then to creation order, and the two leftover seats go to the two seven-student buildings, giving 5, 5 and 0 — exactly ten seats. Rounding each share half up would have given 5, 5 and 1, which is eleven seats and over-allocates the license by one.

Under `FIXED_BUILDING_BLOCKS` the split is a waterfall instead: walking the candidates in that same order, each building is granted the smaller of `block_seats` and the seats still unallocated, and the remaining pool shrinks by whatever was granted. A district whose license does not stretch to a full block for every building therefore leaves the tail buildings with a partial block or with nothing at all.

Each created allocation records the `vendor_app_id` of its license and the `school_id` of its building — neither of which a client ever supplies — together with its `allocated_seats`, a `consumed_seats` and `borrowed_seats` of zero, a `hard_cap` flag that is true exactly when the license's `overage_policy` was `BLOCK` at the moment the row was written, and an `eligible_headcount_snapshot` holding the building's `enrolled_student_count` as observed during the split. That snapshot is a historical record of the headcount the split was based on and nothing more: it is written once, at activation, never refreshed when the building's roll moves, and never read back by any later rule — a re-cut is judged against the license's `total_seats` and the building's own `consumed_seats`, not against it. Once the split is done, the license's `unallocated_seats` is set to `total_seats` minus the sum of the granted `allocated_seats` — the district pool remainder. Activation answers with the license record itself; the allocation rows it minted are read back from the building-allocations collection.

Seat allocations can be re-cut afterwards by a `BuildingLicenseAdmin` for its own building or by a `DataGovernanceOfficer` for any building. The request carries the new `allocated_seats`, an integer from 0 to 100000 — a building may legitimately be cut back to no seats at all — together with the mandatory `expected_vendor_app_version` described in §2.6. The license must be `ACTIVE` (`ERR_VENDOR_APP_NOT_ACTIVE`). A re-cut upward must keep the sum of `allocated_seats` plus the sum of `borrowed_seats` across all of that license's allocations no greater than the license's `total_seats`, else `ERR_ALLOCATION_EXCEEDS_LICENSE`. A re-cut downward must leave `allocated_seats` no lower than that building's own `consumed_seats`, because seats already in use cannot be reclaimed by arithmetic, else `ERR_ALLOCATION_BELOW_CONSUMED`. On success the allocation's `allocated_seats` is overwritten, its `hard_cap` is re-stamped from the license's current `overage_policy`, the allocation's `version` is incremented, and the license's `unallocated_seats` is recomputed with the license's own `version` incremented as well — both versions moving even when the figure supplied equals the one already stored, because neither guard is evaluated in that case and the write is not change-detected. The re-cut answers with the allocation record, never with the license.

Licenses also have a mid-life. An amendment may rewrite `total_seats`, `price_per_seat_cents`, `overage_policy` or `unmatched_teacher_policy`, or move `status` — and `status` is where the license state machine lives. Every one of the five amendable fields is optional and an omitted or null one leaves the stored value exactly as it stood, and a `status` of `DRAFT` is never accepted at all, because a license can never be moved back to `DRAFT`: the attempt is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` at rung 6, exactly as a status value outside the four is. An `ACTIVE` license may be suspended, which moves it to `SUSPENDED`: while suspended, new account provisioning is refused with `ERR_VENDOR_APP_NOT_ACTIVE`, but every existing account keeps its `seat_status` and its `identity_status` untouched. Re-sending `SUSPENDED` against a license that is already `SUSPENDED` is accepted as a no-op that moves nothing but the `version`, while suspending one that is `DRAFT` or `TERMINATED` is refused with `ERR_VENDOR_APP_NOT_ACTIVE`. A `SUSPENDED` license may be resumed back to `ACTIVE`, and this loop is legal and repeatable; on the way back the allocation guard is re-evaluated, so a resume is refused with `ERR_ALLOCATION_EXCEEDS_LICENSE` when the sum of `allocated_seats` across the license's allocations now exceeds `total_seats` — the figure compared being the `total_seats` this very amendment supplies when it supplies one and the stored figure otherwise, and the guard being evaluated before any field is written — which is exactly what happens if `total_seats` was lowered while the license was suspended. Resuming a license that is already `ACTIVE` is `ERR_VENDOR_APP_ALREADY_ACTIVATED`, and naming `ACTIVE` on a license still in `DRAFT` — where activation is the only route out — or already `TERMINATED` is `ERR_VENDOR_APP_NOT_ACTIVE`, as is naming `TERMINATED` on a license that is `DRAFT` or already `TERMINATED`. Both transitions increment the license's `version`, and so does an amendment that names no field at all.

Termination is the terminal state and it is reachable from `ACTIVE` and from `SUSPENDED` alike, with an **identical** effect set in both cases. Every app account under the license whose `seat_status` is `RESERVED` or `ACTIVE` is released — `seat_status` becomes `RELEASED`, with `refunded_charge_cents` set by the same refund rule used everywhere else, read here against the **clock date** — and its `identity_status` becomes `DEPROVISIONED`, its `password_state` `EXPIRED` and its `deprovision_scheduled_date` stamped with that same clock date. Each release decrements the owning allocation's `consumed_seats`, and the license's `consumed_seats` and `committed_charge_cents` are recomputed. Every sync run of the license still sitting in `EXTRACTED`, `TRANSFORMED` or `HELD` becomes `SUPERSEDED`, and every `OPEN` triage item on those runs becomes `DISMISSED` with `resolved_at` stamped with the clock instant and with `resolved_by_user_id` and `resolution_note` both left null — termination writes no note, unlike the supersession of §3.7 — after which each of those runs recomputes its two error counts down to zero. Finally `status` becomes `TERMINATED`, the license's `renewal_score` and `renewal_recommendation` are recomputed, its `sync_health` is left exactly as it stood, and its `version` is incremented — once for the whole termination, however many accounts and runs it reached.

### 3.6. Single Sign-On Identities: Provisioning, Passwords, Usage and Wind-Down

Every licensed seat is represented by one app account, and an app account is a junction between a seat pool and a **polymorphic subject**. An `IdentityAdmin` provisions one with the `building_allocation_id` of the seat pool it draws from, a `subject_type` of either `STUDENT` or `TEACHER`, exactly one of `student_id` or `teacher_id` — the one that agrees with `subject_type`, the other omitted — and the mandatory `expected_vendor_app_version`. A body carrying both subject identifiers, or neither, or the one that disagrees with `subject_type`, is an `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Admission is decided by a chain of checks. The license behind the allocation must be `ACTIVE` (`ERR_VENDOR_APP_NOT_ACTIVE`); a student subject must be `ENROLLED` (`ERR_STUDENT_NOT_ENROLLED`) and a teacher subject must not be `SEPARATED` (`ERR_TEACHER_SEPARATED`); the subject's `school_id` must equal the allocation's `school_id` (`ERR_SCHOOL_MISMATCH`); a username must be derivable (`ERR_IDENTIFIER_UNAVAILABLE`); at most one seat-holding account may exist per license-and-subject pair, so a second attempt while the first account still has `seat_status` of `RESERVED` or `ACTIVE` is `ERR_DUPLICATE_APP_ACCOUNT` — though once that account has been `RELEASED`, a fresh one may be created.

**Grade-band eligibility is checked against the subject, not the building.** For a student, the band is derived from `grade_level` by the district's standard mapping: grades 0 through 5 are `ELEMENTARY`, grades 6 through 8 are `MIDDLE`, and grades 9 through 12 are `HIGH`. For a teacher, the band is the `grade_band` of the teacher's own building. That band must appear in the license's `grade_band_scope`, else `ERR_GRADE_BAND_OUT_OF_SCOPE`. This is deliberately stricter than the building-coherence rule: a seventh grader housed in a K–8 building whose `grade_band` is recorded as `ELEMENTARY` cannot take a seat on an elementary-only license, even though that building holds an allocation and the subject and the allocation agree on `school_id`.

Seat admission itself is pure arithmetic against the allocation. Let the building's free seats be its `allocated_seats` minus its `consumed_seats` — and remember that `consumed_seats` already includes accounts that took a borrowed seat. When there is at least one free seat the account is admitted and nothing is borrowed. When there is none, the license's `overage_policy` as it stands at that moment decides — the allocation's own `hard_cap` snapshot is never consulted here, so an amendment that flips the policy changes admission immediately while the stale flag on the row goes on saying what it said: under `BLOCK` the request is refused with `ERR_BUILDING_ALLOCATION_EXHAUSTED` **even if the district pool still holds unallocated seats**, because district seats may not cross a building boundary under a hard cap; under `POOL_FALLBACK` the account is admitted by borrowing exactly one seat from the district pool, which increments the allocation's `borrowed_seats` by one, and only an empty pool — a license whose `unallocated_seats` is zero — yields `ERR_DISTRICT_SEATS_EXHAUSTED`. The comparison is inclusive on the last seat: a building whose `consumed_seats` is one below its `allocated_seats` admits, and a building whose `consumed_seats` equals its `allocated_seats` does not. On admission the allocation's `consumed_seats` is incremented by one and the license's `unallocated_seats` is recomputed as `total_seats` minus the summed `allocated_seats` minus the summed `borrowed_seats`. The identical request against identical data therefore succeeds or fails purely on `overage_policy` — that is the whole point of the flag.

The account is created with `seat_status` of `RESERVED` on the seat axis and `identity_status` of `PENDING_PROVISION` on the identity axis. `PROVISIONED` is **never** reached by creation; only a later password set gets there. Its `password_state` is `TEMP_SET`, its `failed_login_count` is zero, its `last_password_set_at`, `last_activity_date` and `deprovision_scheduled_date` are null, its `active_minutes_total` and `session_count` and `refunded_charge_cents` are zero, and its `seat_charge_cents` is frozen at the per-seat rate for the provisioning clock date. Creation also increments the allocation's counters as described, recomputes the license's `consumed_seats` and `committed_charge_cents`, re-scores the license's `renewal_score` and `renewal_recommendation`, and increments the license's `version` by one.

**The per-seat rate** deserves stating precisely here, because it is frozen into `seat_charge_cents` at provisioning and reused by the pipeline projections of §3.7. Let the term's total days be the inclusive count of calendar days from its `start_date` through its `end_date` — a term running from 10 August 2026 to 18 December 2026 has 131 total days. Let the remaining days for a given date be the inclusive count from that date through the term's `end_date`, never fewer than one. A seat priced on a date on or before the term's `add_drop_cutoff_date` costs the full `price_per_seat_cents`. A seat priced after the cutoff is prorated: take `price_per_seat_cents`, multiply by the remaining days, divide by the total days, and **round the result up** to the next whole cent. With a list price of 1000 cents and the term above, a seat priced on 1 October 2026 has 79 remaining days and costs the ceiling of 603.0534, which is 604 cents.

The `username` is not supplied by the client — it is **derived**, and the derivation is the per-vendor transformation rule that most often surprises implementers. It runs in three steps. First, the raw identifier is picked according to the license's `identifier_strategy`. Under `SIS_ID` it is the student's `sis_student_id` or the teacher's `sis_staff_id`. Under `EMAIL_LOCALPART` it is, for a teacher, the part of the teacher's `email` before the first at-sign, and for a student — who carries no district email — the student's `last_name`, then a single period, then the first character of the student's `first_name`. Under `STATE_ID` it is the student's `state_student_id`, and a student whose `state_student_id` is null is refused with `ERR_IDENTIFIER_UNAVAILABLE`; a teacher under `STATE_ID` is refused outright with the same error, as is a teacher with a blank `email` under `EMAIL_LOCALPART`.

Second, the raw identifier is normalised in exactly this order: lower-case it; replace every maximal run of characters outside the set of lower-case letters and digits with a single period; strip leading periods; **truncate to 20 characters**; and only then strip trailing periods. An empty result is `ERR_IDENTIFIER_UNAVAILABLE`. Third, collisions are resolved within the same license: the candidate username is the normalised local part, an at-sign, and the license's `username_domain`. A username is considered taken when another account of the **same** license holds it with a `seat_status` other than `RELEASED` — a released seat frees its username for reuse, an account that is `DEPROVISIONED` but still holding a seat keeps occupying its username, and the identical username under a different license is never a collision. When the candidate is taken, the smallest whole number not below 2 is found such that the local part, a plus sign, that number, an at-sign and the domain is free, and that becomes the username.

The order of those steps is pinned: truncation happens **before** the trailing-period strip and **before** the collision suffix, so a suffixed username may legitimately be longer than 20 characters in its local part. Take a student named Maria-Jo Vandenberghe-Mwangi under an `EMAIL_LOCALPART` license with a `username_domain` of `apps.district.k12`. The raw identifier is `Vandenberghe-Mwangi.M`; lower-casing and run-replacement give `vandenberghe.mwangi.m`, which is 21 characters; truncation to 20 gives `vandenberghe.mwangi.`; the trailing-period strip gives the 19-character `vandenberghe.mwangi`, so the username is `vandenberghe.mwangi@apps.district.k12`. A second student, Maria Vandenberghe Mwangi, normalises to the same 19 characters and therefore receives `vandenberghe.mwangi+2@apps.district.k12` — a 21-character local part, which is correct. Note also how strategy changes the collision picture: under `SIS_ID` the opaque identifier is normalised verbatim, so identically-named siblings never collide, while under `EMAIL_LOCALPART` with student subjects the username comes from mutable name fields and identically-named siblings do collide and do consume the numeric suffix sequence.

Passwords are governed by **the subject's own building**, not by the building that lent the seat, and that distinction matters whenever a student attends a program housed elsewhere. At provisioning the account is minted with a temporary password: `password_state` is `TEMP_SET` and `password_expires_at` is the account's `created_at` plus the subject building's `temp_password_ttl_hours`. An `IdentityAdmin` then sets a real password by supplying `new_password`, a string of 1 to 128 characters as a matter of request shape, so a longer value is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` at rung 6 before any policy comparison is reached. The account's `identity_status` must not be `DEPROVISIONED` (`ERR_ACCOUNT_DEPROVISIONED`) and its `password_state` must not be `LOCKED` (`ERR_ACCOUNT_LOCKED`); the new password must be at least `min_password_length` characters (`ERR_PASSWORD_TOO_SHORT`) and, when the subject building's `password_requires_mixed_case` is true, must contain at least one lower-case and at least one upper-case **ASCII** letter — one character drawn from `a` through `z` and one from `A` through `Z`, cased letters outside those two ranges satisfying neither half of the test (`ERR_PASSWORD_COMPLEXITY`). On acceptance the identity axis advances from `PENDING_PROVISION` to `PROVISIONED`, or from `SUSPENDED` to `PROVISIONED` when the account is being reactivated after a summer suspension; `password_state` becomes `USER_SET`, `failed_login_count` is reset to zero, `last_password_set_at` is stamped with the clock instant, and `password_expires_at` is recomputed as `last_password_set_at` plus the subject building's `password_max_age_days`. **The seat axis is not touched** by a password set — which is precisely why password setting is exempt from the released-seat rung of §2.7 and a summer-suspended identity whose seat has already gone back can still be reactivated.

Password expiry is evaluated lazily but reliably: on every single-account read, and on every usage report before any other effect, an account whose `password_state` is `TEMP_SET` or `USER_SET` and whose `password_expires_at` is at or before the clock instant is moved to `EXPIRED`. That move is a mutation like any other, so it advances the account's own `updated_at` and `version` even though the caller issued nothing but a read, while an evaluation that finds nothing to change leaves the record untouched. The collection read of app accounts does **not** run this evaluation, and neither does its `password_state` filter, which matches the stored value alone: an account whose password has lapsed but which no single read has visited yet still lists as `TEMP_SET` or `USER_SET`. Once a password is `EXPIRED` or `LOCKED`, its `password_expires_at` is frozen at whatever value was current when that state was entered. Worked through with the defaults: an account created at `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z` under a 72-hour temporary lifetime expires at `2026-08-04T00:00:00.000Z`, so at `2026-08-03T23:59:59.999Z` it is still `TEMP_SET` and at `2026-08-04T00:00:00.000Z` it is `EXPIRED`; a successful set at `2026-08-04T09:00:00.000Z` under a 180-day maximum age carries the account to `2027-01-31T09:00:00.000Z`. Accounts whose `identity_status` is `DEPROVISIONED` are frozen entirely: neither expiry nor lockout is re-evaluated for them, and a password set against them is refused.

Vendor telemetry arrives through usage reports, which an `IdentityAdmin` or the license's own `VendorIntegrationManager` may post. The report carries the `activity_date` it covers — a well-formed calendar date constrained by nothing else, compared against neither the clock nor any term window, so a back-dated and a post-dated report are accepted alike — the `active_minutes` observed as an integer from 0 to 100000, and optionally `session_count` and `failed_logins`, each an integer from 0 to 100000 and each defaulting to zero, which is what reporting nothing means. The account's `active_minutes_total` grows by the reported `active_minutes`, its `session_count` grows by the reported sessions, and its `last_activity_date` is overwritten with the reported `activity_date`. When the reported `active_minutes` is greater than zero and the seat axis is still `RESERVED`, the seat advances to `ACTIVE` — the seat has been proven consumed by telemetry rather than merely held. A report of zero `active_minutes` leaves `seat_status` exactly where it was. Reported `failed_logins` accumulate into `failed_login_count`, and the moment that cumulative count reaches the subject building's `password_lockout_threshold` the `password_state` becomes `LOCKED`; with the default threshold of 5, a cumulative count of 4 leaves the account usable and 5 locks it. That comparison is made on every report, whether or not this particular report carried any failed logins at all, and it is the last word on `password_state`: a report that both lapses the password by expiry and finds the cumulative count at the threshold leaves the account `LOCKED` rather than `EXPIRED`. The whole report is a single write of the account, so a usage report that also applies the lazy expiry described above advances the account's `version` by exactly one. A `LOCKED` password changes neither `identity_status` nor `seat_status`, because a locked password still occupies its licensed seat. Every usage report re-scores the license's `renewal_score` and `renewal_recommendation` and increments the license's `version`.

Winding an account down is two separate acts, and conflating them is the classic error. **Releasing** the seat is an `IdentityAdmin` action taking no payload: the seat axis moves from `RESERVED` or `ACTIVE` to `RELEASED` (a second attempt on an already-released seat is `ERR_SEAT_ALREADY_RELEASED`), `refunded_charge_cents` is set to the account's `seat_charge_cents` when the clock date is on or before the term's `add_drop_cutoff_date` and to zero otherwise, the owning allocation's `consumed_seats` is decremented by one — decrementing `borrowed_seats` too, and only, when this very account was the one that took a borrowed seat, because whether a seat was borrowed is recorded on the account at admission and is never re-attributed afterwards: releasing an account that drew on its building's own grant leaves `borrowed_seats` standing even while a borrowed account of the same building is still live — and the license's `consumed_seats`, `unallocated_seats`, `committed_charge_cents`, `renewal_score` and `renewal_recommendation` are recomputed with its `version` incremented. `identity_status` is deliberately left alone. **Deprovisioning** the identity is the mirror image: it carries an `effective_date`, moves `identity_status` to `DEPROVISIONED` from `PENDING_PROVISION`, `PROVISIONED` or `SUSPENDED` alike, stamps `deprovision_scheduled_date` with that `effective_date` — overwriting any date an earlier cascade had put there — and sets `password_state` to `EXPIRED` from `TEMP_SET`, `USER_SET`, an already-`EXPIRED` value or even `LOCKED` alike, since deprovisioning overwrites the password axis unconditionally and a locked password is no bar to it — while leaving the seat axis untouched, so a deprovisioned identity may keep a `RESERVED` or `ACTIVE` seat until somebody releases it. An explicit deprovision is never post-dated: the supplied `effective_date` must not be later than the clock date, and when the account already carries a non-null `deprovision_scheduled_date` from an earlier cascade, the clock date must also have reached that scheduled date; otherwise the request is refused with `ERR_DEPROVISION_NOT_DUE`. A second deprovision of an already-deprovisioned identity is `ERR_ACCOUNT_DEPROVISIONED`. A deprovision also re-scores the license's `renewal_score` and `renewal_recommendation` and increments the license's `version`, even though the seat the identity still holds leaves `consumed_seats`, `unallocated_seats` and `committed_charge_cents` exactly where they were. The cascades of §3.2, §3.4, §3.5, §3.7 and §3.8 reach the same `DEPROVISIONED` state without an explicit call, using the cascade's own effective date.

### 3.7. The Rostering Pipeline: Extract, Transform, Triage, Publish

The pipeline is a four-stage conveyor with a deliberate loop in the middle, and one sync run is one pass down it. A `RosterOperator` opens a run against a license by supplying `vendor_app_id` and a `run_type` of `FULL`, `DELTA` or `ROLLOVER`. The license must be `ACTIVE` (`ERR_VENDOR_APP_NOT_ACTIVE`). A `FULL` or `DELTA` run rosters the live term and therefore requires that term to be `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_TERM_NOT_ACTIVE`; a `ROLLOVER` run rosters an already-archived term for archived-class access and requires that term to be `ROLLED_OVER` with its retention window still open, else `ERR_ARCHIVE_WINDOW_CLOSED`. The new run is created in `EXTRACTED` with `initiated_by_user_id` stamped from the opening operator's `X-User-ID` and `extracted_at` stamped with the clock instant; `transformed_at`, `published_at` and `published_by_user_id` are null, `transform_count` is zero, and every projected and published figure is null. `transformed_record_count`, `blocking_error_count` and `advisory_error_count` are born at zero rather than null.

Opening a run is also an act of pre-emption: **every other run of the same license** sitting in `EXTRACTED`, `TRANSFORMED` or `HELD` becomes `SUPERSEDED`, and each of those runs' `OPEN` triage items becomes `DISMISSED` with `resolution_note` set to `run superseded`, `resolved_at` stamped with the clock instant and `resolved_by_user_id` left null, after which those runs' `blocking_error_count` and `advisory_error_count` recompute to zero. A `SUPERSEDED` run is inert: transforming it, publishing it, or resolving one of its items is refused with `ERR_SYNC_RUN_SUPERSEDED`.

**Scope resolution** is the same computation everywhere it is needed — at extraction, at each transform, at publish, and when the vendor reads its feed — and it is pinned down to its ordering so that a feed is byte-stable. The sections in scope are those whose `term_id` matches the license's `term_id`, whose `status` is `ACTIVE` for a `FULL` or `DELTA` run and `ARCHIVED` — and `ARCHIVED` alone, never `ACTIVE` as well — for a `ROLLOVER` run, whose building has `status` of `ACTIVE`, and whose building's `grade_band` appears in the license's `grade_band_scope`; they are ordered by the building's `building_code` ascending, then by `period_index` ascending, then by the section's `created_at` ascending, and finally, where all three of those tie, by the order in which the server created the sections. On the feed read, which belongs to no run and therefore has no `run_type`, the admitted status set is `ACTIVE` alone when `include_archived` is false and `ACTIVE` *together with* `ARCHIVED` when it is true — the flag widens the set rather than replacing it, so an archived-inclusive feed still carries the live sections. `CANCELLED` sections and closed buildings are never in scope. The **feed sections** are the in-scope sections minus those removed by policy: when the license's `unmatched_teacher_policy` is `SKIP_SECTION`, every in-scope section whose `roster_health` is `UNMATCHED_TEACHER` is omitted from the feed. The enrollments in scope are those of in-scope sections whose `roster_status` is `PENDING_ADD`, `ACTIVE` or `PENDING_DROP`, ordered by `effective_date` ascending, then by `created_at` ascending, and then by creation order; `DROPPED` rows are excluded. The students in scope inherit that same order, each appearing at the position of the first in-scope enrollment that names it. The students in scope are the distinct subjects of those enrollments whose `enrollment_state` is `ENROLLED` — a `WITHDRAWN` or `MERGED_DUPLICATE` student is excluded even when a stale enrollment row still points at them. The teachers in scope are empty unless the license's `seat_model` is `PER_STUDENT_AND_TEACHER`, in which case they are the distinct non-null values of `primary_teacher_id` over the feed sections whose teacher has `employment_status` of `ACTIVE`.

From that resolution the run's counters follow. `extracted_section_count` is the number of in-scope sections and `extracted_enrollment_count` the number of in-scope enrollments, both written at extraction. `transformed_record_count`, written at each transform, is the number of feed sections, plus the number of in-scope students restricted to feed sections, plus the number of in-scope teachers.

Transforming a run applies the vendor's transformation rules and, in the same pass, **triages the roster**. Transform is permitted only from `EXTRACTED` or `HELD`, else `ERR_SYNC_RUN_STATE_INVALID`. The triage pass raises exactly one triage item per defect, and it deduplicates within the run on the pair of `category` and `source_ref`: when a re-transform re-detects a defect for which that run still holds an `OPEN` item of the same category and the same `source_ref`, no second item is written and the existing one is left exactly as it stands, its `severity` included and its `version` unmoved — even if the license's policy flags have since changed the severity that a fresh item would have carried. An item that has already left `OPEN` never suppresses a new one, so a defect that comes back after its item was resolved or dismissed is raised again as a separate item. Each item records the `sync_run_id` of the run that raised it — never supplied by a client — along with its `category`, its `severity`, a `source_ref` holding the raw feed key that could not be resolved, and whichever subject references the category calls for. Every item is born with `status` of `OPEN` and with `resolved_by_user_id`, `resolution_note` and `resolved_at` all null; those three are written only when the item leaves `OPEN`. The pass works the four categories below in the order they are listed here, and inside each category in the scope order of the subject it names, so a run's items are created — and therefore list — in exactly that order. The four categories are:

- **`UNMATCHED_TEACHER`** is raised for every in-scope section whose `primary_teacher_id` is null or whose teacher of record has `employment_status` of `SEPARATED`. Its `severity` is `BLOCKING` when the license's `unmatched_teacher_policy` is `HOLD_RUN` and `ADVISORY` when it is `SKIP_SECTION`. Its `source_ref` is the section's `sis_section_id` and its `subject_section_id` points at the offending section; `subject_student_id` and `duplicate_candidate_student_id` are null.
- **`DUPLICATE_STUDENT`** is always `BLOCKING` and is raised once per duplicate key, the key being the student's `last_name` lower-cased, a pipe, and the student's `date_of_birth`. Of the two colliding students ordered by `created_at` ascending, the earlier-created one is recorded in `subject_student_id` and the later-created one in `duplicate_candidate_student_id` — and where three or more in-scope students share one key, the single item names the earliest two of them in that order and no other; `source_ref` is that duplicate key and `subject_section_id` is null.
- **`MISSING_GRADE_BAND`** is always `ADVISORY` and is raised for each in-scope student whose `grade_level` falls outside the license's grade-band mapping. Its `source_ref` is the student's `sis_student_id` and `subject_student_id` names the offending student.
- **`SEAT_SHORTFALL`** is raised once when the run's projected seat delta exceeds the license's `unallocated_seats` plus the free capacity of **every** allocation of that license — each allocation's `allocated_seats` minus its `consumed_seats`, counted only where that difference is positive, whether or not the building it belongs to holds a section in the run's scope. Its `severity` is `BLOCKING` when the license's `overage_policy` is `BLOCK` and `ADVISORY` when it is `POOL_FALLBACK`; its `source_ref` is the license's `vendor_code`, and both subject references are null.

Where the run lands is decided by **one category only**. The run reaches `TRANSFORMED` whenever triage produced no `BLOCKING` item of category `UNMATCHED_TEACHER`; a `BLOCKING` `DUPLICATE_STUDENT` or `SEAT_SHORTFALL` item does **not** hold the run, so the run transforms with those items still open and it is publish that refuses later with `ERR_BLOCKING_ERRORS_UNRESOLVED`. When triage did produce at least one `BLOCKING` `UNMATCHED_TEACHER` item, the run goes to `HELD` instead. Either way, `transform_count` increases by one, `transformed_at` is stamped with the clock instant, `transformed_record_count` is recomputed — on the `HELD` branch exactly as on the `TRANSFORMED` one, because the record count describes the data the transform saw rather than the run's landing — and the run's `blocking_error_count` and `advisory_error_count` are recomputed as the numbers of its `OPEN` items of `BLOCKING` and `ADVISORY` severity respectively. A run that reached `TRANSFORMED` also writes `projected_seat_delta` and `projected_charge_cents` from the provisional charge variant below; a run that went to `HELD` leaves both projected figures null. In both cases the license's `sync_health` becomes `OK` when no `OPEN` `BLOCKING` item remains on the run and `BLOCKED` when one does, and the license's `version` is incremented.

The loop back is explicit. A `HELD` run may be transformed again, but only once the triage that held it has been cleared: there must be **zero** items of this run with `category` of `UNMATCHED_TEACHER`, `severity` of `BLOCKING` and `status` of `OPEN`, else the re-transform is refused with `ERR_BLOCKING_ERRORS_UNRESOLVED`. A permitted re-transform re-runs triage against current data, and if the data has drifted such that a `BLOCKING` `UNMATCHED_TEACHER` item is raised again, the run stays `HELD` — a self-loop. `transform_count` records how many laps a run made around the `TRANSFORMED` → `HELD` → `TRANSFORMED` cycle. A run can also fall backwards into the loop without an operator doing anything: as §3.2 describes, a staff separation that raises a new `BLOCKING` `UNMATCHED_TEACHER` item against a section inside a `TRANSFORMED` run's scope drags that run back to `HELD` and nulls both projected figures.

Triage is `DataGovernanceOfficer` work and has two resolution verbs with sharply divergent effects. **Attaching a replacement teacher** carries a `replacement_teacher_id` and an optional `resolution_note` of 1 to 500 characters when one is supplied at all. The item must have `status` of `OPEN` and `category` of `UNMATCHED_TEACHER`, else `ERR_ERROR_QUEUE_ITEM_NOT_OPEN`; the replacement teacher's `employment_status` must be `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_TEACHER_SEPARATED`; the replacement's `school_id` must equal the subject section's `school_id`, else `ERR_SCHOOL_MISMATCH`; and the replacement must have no period conflict with the subject section under the one-period travel buffer of §3.3, else `ERR_TEACHER_PERIOD_CONFLICT`. On success the subject section's `primary_teacher_id` is set to the replacement and its `roster_health` returns to `OK`, and the item moves from `OPEN` to `RESOLVED_MATCHED` with `resolved_by_user_id` stamped from the officer's `X-User-ID`, `resolved_at` stamped with the clock instant and `resolution_note` recorded when one was supplied. The run's `blocking_error_count` is recomputed — but **the run stays `HELD`**: returning to `TRANSFORMED` is an explicit operator action, and triage never re-transforms on its own. This resolution mutates a section and no student, and it does not write the license at all: clearing even the very item that held the run leaves the license's `sync_health` and its `version` exactly as the last transform or staff separation left them, so a license reported `BLOCKED` stays `BLOCKED` until the run is transformed again.

**Merging a duplicate** carries a `surviving_student_id` and an optional `resolution_note` of 1 to 500 characters when one is supplied at all. The item must have `status` of `OPEN` and `category` of `DUPLICATE_STUDENT`, else `ERR_ERROR_QUEUE_ITEM_NOT_OPEN`; the supplied survivor must be one of the item's own two candidates — the one in `subject_student_id` or the one in `duplicate_candidate_student_id` — and the two candidates must be distinct students of the same building, else `ERR_MERGE_SUBJECT_MISMATCH`; and the surviving student must have `enrollment_state` of `ENROLLED`, else `ERR_STUDENT_NOT_ENROLLED`. The candidate that is *not* the survivor is the loser, and the loser must itself be `ENROLLED`, reported with the same `ERR_STUDENT_NOT_ENROLLED`. Both of those two checks are made only once the pairing has been established, so they follow the subject-mismatch guard of rung 34 rather than sitting at rung 11 with the enrolment, withdrawal and provisioning checks — a merge naming a `WITHDRAWN` survivor who is not one of the item's candidates reports `ERR_MERGE_SUBJECT_MISMATCH`, not `ERR_STUDENT_NOT_ENROLLED`. On success the loser takes the student transition to `MERGED_DUPLICATE` with `duplicate_of_student_id` pointed at the survivor, and the loser's roster is folded into the survivor's: each of the loser's enrollments whose `roster_status` is `PENDING_ADD`, `ACTIVE` or `PENDING_DROP` is re-pointed to the surviving student when the survivor holds no enrollment in that same section that is not `DROPPED`, and is otherwise moved to `DROPPED` with `drop_effective_date` stamped with the clock date. Every section that lent one of those rows then recomputes its own `active_enrollment_count` — once per section however many of the loser's rows it held — and the surviving student recomputes its `active_enrollment_count` over the folded roster, while the loser's `active_enrollment_count` is recomputed on the same single write that records `MERGED_DUPLICATE`, and necessarily lands at zero because the fold has left the loser no `PENDING_ADD`, `ACTIVE` or `PENDING_DROP` row at all. Every app account of the loser whose `seat_status` is `RESERVED` or `ACTIVE` becomes `RELEASED` under the standard refund rule read against the **clock date** — the full `seat_charge_cents` back when the clock date is on or before the term's `add_drop_cutoff_date` and zero otherwise — with `identity_status` set to `DEPROVISIONED`, `password_state` set to `EXPIRED` and `deprovision_scheduled_date` stamped with that same clock date, after which the owning allocations' `consumed_seats` and the counters of each owning license — `consumed_seats`, `unallocated_seats`, `committed_charge_cents`, `renewal_score` and `renewal_recommendation` — are recomputed, once for each seat released and so once more for each further account the loser held, that license's `version` moving each time while its `sync_health` is left exactly as it stood; and the loser's building recomputes `enrolled_student_count`. The item moves to `RESOLVED_MERGED` with `resolved_by_user_id`, `resolved_at` and `resolution_note` recorded exactly as above, and the run's `blocking_error_count` is recomputed while the run again stays `HELD`. This resolution mutates two students, their enrollments and their app accounts, and it reaches a section only through that recomputed `active_enrollment_count` — never through a section's `primary_teacher_id` or its `roster_health`, which is the mirror image of the match verb.

Publishing is the second two-person control and the only stage that changes the district's roster of record. It is a `DataGovernanceOfficer` action on a run that must be `TRANSFORMED` (`ERR_SYNC_RUN_STATE_INVALID`), by an officer whose `X-User-ID` differs from the run's `initiated_by_user_id` (`ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`), on a run with **zero** `OPEN` items of `BLOCKING` severity in any category (`ERR_BLOCKING_ERRORS_UNRESOLVED`). Publish then does five things in a fixed order. First, **mid-year adds land**: every in-scope enrollment whose `roster_status` is `PENDING_ADD` and whose `requested_effective_date` is on or before the clock date becomes `ACTIVE`, with `effective_date` stamped as the later of `requested_effective_date` and the clock date — so an add the district asked for in September but only published in October takes effect on the publish date — while a row whose requested date is still in the future stays `PENDING_ADD` and is simply skipped by this publish. Second, **mid-year drops land**: every in-scope enrollment whose `roster_status` is `PENDING_DROP` and whose `drop_effective_date` is on or before the clock date becomes `DROPPED`, running the same conditional seat-release cascade over app accounts, building allocations and license counters that §3.4 describes. Third, `published_seat_delta` and `published_charge_cents` are written from the final charge variant, **evaluated after** those two enrollment cascades have been applied. Fourth, every remaining `OPEN` triage item of the run — necessarily `ADVISORY`, since a `BLOCKING` one would have refused the publish — becomes `DISMISSED` with `resolved_by_user_id` stamped from the publishing officer and `resolved_at` stamped with the clock instant, while `resolution_note` is left exactly as it was — publish records no note of its own, unlike the pre-emption of §3.7 which writes one. Fifth, the run's `state` becomes `PUBLISHED`, `published_at` is stamped with the clock instant and `published_by_user_id` with the officer's `X-User-ID`, and its `blocking_error_count` and `advisory_error_count` are recomputed — necessarily down to zero, that dismissal having emptied the run's `OPEN` set; the license's `consumed_seats`, `committed_charge_cents`, `renewal_score` and `renewal_recommendation` are recomputed, its `sync_health` returns to `OK`, and its `version` is incremented.

**Seat and charge projection** is one calculation with two variants, and the difference between them is the district's whole negotiating position at renewal time. In both variants the priced population is the set of **subjects** who will hold a seat after the run — the in-scope students plus the in-scope teachers, both taken over the **feed** sections rather than over every in-scope section — and each subject is priced exactly once, no matter how many sections that subject appears in. Each subject has a charge date: for a teacher it is the term's `start_date`; for a student it is the earliest charge date over that student's in-scope enrollments *that survived into the feed*, using the field the variant nominates, so a section removed by `SKIP_SECTION` cannot even contribute a date. The seat delta is the number of subjects minus the number of existing app accounts of this license whose `seat_status` is `RESERVED` or `ACTIVE` and whose subject is still in that population — in other words, only genuinely new seats count toward the delta, while subjects who already hold a live account are re-priced without inflating it. The charge is the sum, over every subject, of the per-seat rate for that subject's charge date as defined in §3.6. Enrollments that are `DROPPED`, and sections dropped from the feed by `SKIP_SECTION`, contribute nothing at all.

The rounding order here is pinned and it is per-subject: **round each subject's prorated rate up first, then add the integers together.** Summing the exact fractions and rounding once at the end is forbidden. With a list price of 1000 cents, a term running from 10 August 2026 to 18 December 2026 (131 total days) and a charge date of 1 October 2026 (79 remaining days), each subject's rate is the ceiling of 603.0534, which is 604 cents, so three subjects commit 1812 cents. Ceiling the pooled fraction instead — the ceiling of 1809.1603 — would give 1810 cents and understate the commitment by two cents.

The **provisional** variant is evaluated at each transform and written to `projected_seat_delta` and `projected_charge_cents`. It dates a student subject by the earliest `requested_effective_date` among that student's in-scope enrollments — the date the source system asked for — and it still counts `PENDING_DROP` enrollments as seat-holding, because the drop has not been published yet. The **final** variant is evaluated at publish, after the enrollment cascades, and written to `published_seat_delta` and `published_charge_cents`. It dates a student subject by the earliest `effective_date` among that student's in-scope enrollments — the date the add actually took effect, which for a late add is the publish clock date — and by then the `PENDING_DROP` rows have become `DROPPED` and have dropped out of the population altogether. The consequence is worth spelling out: an add whose `requested_effective_date` is on or before the term's `add_drop_cutoff_date` but whose `effective_date` lands after it is billed at full price provisionally and prorated finally.

Reconciliation, finally, is an **observation and not a posting**. The reconciliation figure is the run's `published_charge_cents` minus its `projected_charge_cents`, and nothing else moves as a result of it. `VendorApp.committed_charge_cents` remains defined solely as the summed `seat_charge_cents` of the license's app accounts less their summed `refunded_charge_cents`, and no sync-run charge figure is ever written into it. With the fixture above — three subjects requested for 1 September 2026 and published on 1 October 2026 — the projection is three times 1000, or 3000 cents, the final figure is three times 604, or 1812 cents, and the reconciliation delta is therefore negative 1188 cents.

The vendor sees the result of all this through a **roster feed** read, available to the `RosterOperator`, the `DataGovernanceOfficer` and the license's own `VendorIntegrationManager`. The feed applies the same scope resolution and the same username derivation described above, shaped by the license's `rostering_standard`, `identifier_strategy` and `name_format`. It answers with the `vendor_app_id`, the `rostering_standard` in force, a `section_count`, a `student_count`, a `teacher_count`, a `skipped_section_count` counting the sections `SKIP_SECTION` removed from the feed, a `sections` array, a `usernames` array and a `display_names` array. The three counts are taken over the **feed** population of §3.7: `section_count` is the number of feed sections, `student_count` the number of in-scope students restricted to feed sections, and `teacher_count` the number of in-scope teachers — zero, therefore, under a `PER_STUDENT` license. The `sections` array carries those feed sections as complete section records, the same shape a section read returns, in scope order. The `usernames` and `display_names` arrays are parallel to each other and are laid out feed students first, in scope order, and then in-scope teachers, in scope order: one entry per subject, so each array holds exactly `student_count` plus `teacher_count` entries and the *n*-th entry of one describes the same subject as the *n*-th entry of the other. A subject that already holds an app account of this license whose `seat_status` is not `RELEASED` is reported under that account's stored `username` exactly as stored, however far the derivation rules have since drifted from it; every other subject's username is derived on the spot under the rules of §3.6, de-duplicated against the usernames live on the license **and** against the ones this same feed has already emitted, so two never-provisioned namesakes in one feed are reported as a base name and a `+1` variant. Nothing derived here is persisted — the feed mints no accounts and no `username` — and a subject whose identifier normalises away to an empty local part is reported as a `null` username in its position rather than refusing the whole read with `ERR_IDENTIFIER_UNAVAILABLE`. Display names are the second per-vendor transformation rule, and it shapes **student** subjects only, because a student is the one subject carried as a separate `first_name` and `last_name`: under `LAST_FIRST` a student's display name is the `last_name`, a comma, a space and the `first_name`; under `FIRST_LAST` it is the `first_name`, a space and the `last_name`. A teacher has no such split, so a teacher's display name is that teacher's stored `full_name` exactly as the SIS delivered it, whatever the license's `name_format` says. Unlike usernames, display names are emitted **raw** — they are not lower-cased, not run-replaced and not truncated. Setting `include_archived` to true reaches `ARCHIVED` sections and is gated by the retention window of §3.8, so the same request that succeeds inside the window is refused with `ERR_ARCHIVE_WINDOW_CLOSED` outside it.

### 3.8. Summer Rollover, Archived-Class Access and the Renewal Decision

When the instructional year ends, a `DataGovernanceOfficer` rolls the term over. The term must be `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_TERM_NOT_ACTIVE`, and the clock date must have reached the term's `end_date` — a term cannot be rolled over before it ends, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_TERM_WINDOW_CLOSED`. Rollover then computes the archived-access deadline and walks four hops outward, exactly as the withdrawal cascade does.

`archived_access_expires_at` is set to the term's `end_date` plus its `archive_retention_days`, anchored at `00:00:00.000Z` on that date — calendar-day addition on a date, not an arithmetic of hours. On the second hop, every section of this term whose `status` is `ACTIVE` becomes `ARCHIVED`; sections already `CANCELLED` are left untouched, which makes this a conditional rather than a blanket cascade. On the third hop, every enrollment of those newly archived sections whose `roster_status` is `PENDING_ADD`, `ACTIVE` or `PENDING_DROP` becomes `DROPPED` with `drop_effective_date` stamped with the term's `end_date`, after which each newly archived section and each affected student recomputes its `active_enrollment_count`. On the fourth hop, each of those drops releases the **student's** seat-holding app accounts — only student subjects are reached, so a teacher's account under the same license keeps its seat and its `identity_status` right through the summer — under licenses whose `term_id` is this term and whose scope no longer retains the student — `seat_status` becomes `RELEASED` with `refunded_charge_cents` set by the standard refund rule read against the **clock date** at which rollover is run, not against the term's `end_date`, and `deprovision_scheduled_date` is left exactly as it was — and sets `identity_status` to `SUSPENDED` whatever that axis previously held, an already-`DEPROVISIONED` identity that was still holding its seat included. **Summer suspension is not deprovisioning**: the identity is parked, not destroyed, `password_state` is left exactly as it was, and a password set in August reactivates the account straight back to `PROVISIONED` as §3.6 describes. Each release then decrements the owning allocation's `consumed_seats` by one and recomputes the license's `consumed_seats`, `committed_charge_cents`, `renewal_score` and `renewal_recommendation`, incrementing that license's `version` once per affected license. Finally the term's `state` becomes `ROLLED_OVER` and its own `version` is incremented.

Archived-class access is then permitted **while the clock instant is strictly earlier than** `archived_access_expires_at`, and only for a term whose `state` is `ROLLED_OVER`. Three operations are gated on that window: reading a **single** section whose `status` is `ARCHIVED`, requesting a roster feed with `include_archived` set to true, and opening a `ROLLOVER` sync run. Each is refused with `ERR_ARCHIVE_WINDOW_CLOSED` outside the window, and the gate is reached only after the path identifier has resolved, so an unknown section id is still `ERR_SECTION_NOT_FOUND`. Nothing else is gated: the sections collection read is never refused and keeps listing `ARCHIVED` sections in full, in the ordinary page and under a `status=ARCHIVED` filter, whether the window is open or shut, and amending an `ARCHIVED` section is not gated either. While the term has not yet rolled over, `archived_access_expires_at` is null and the window is considered shut, so an archived-inclusive feed request is refused with the same error. The boundary is strict, and it is the opposite of the password-expiry boundary: with an `end_date` of 18 December 2026 and 90 retention days, the deadline is `2027-03-18T00:00:00.000Z`, so at `2027-03-17T23:59:59.999Z` the archived roster is served and at `2027-03-18T00:00:00.000Z` the identical request is refused. A term configured with `archive_retention_days` of 0 has a window that is open for no time at all: its deadline equals its `end_date` at midnight, which is already in the past the moment rollover runs. The retention figure is read from the term at rollover time, and a later edit of the term does not move an already-computed deadline. Only `ARCHIVED` sections are served by this path; `CANCELLED` sections never are.

The renewal conversation is served by a computed **renewal summary** read on a license, available to the `RosterOperator`, the `DataGovernanceOfficer`, the `BuildingLicenseAdmin` and the license's own `VendorIntegrationManager`. It carries a `basis` query parameter selecting the scoring variant: the default `final` basis is the renewal decision of record, taken when the parameter is omitted or empty, and `provisional` is offered for an in-flight pilot that has not yet accumulated a full usage window; those two spellings are the only ones accepted, and any other value — including a differently-cased `FINAL` — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The read answers with the `vendor_app_id`, the `basis` in force, and then the scoring figures — `consumed_seats`, `provisioned_seats`, `engaged_seats`, `utilization`, `engagement`, `cost_per_engaged_cents`, `cost_efficiency`, `renewal_score` and `renewal_recommendation` — followed by the invoice figures `gross_seat_cents`, `discount_rate`, `discount_cents`, `net_seat_cents`, `platform_fee_cents`, `shelfware_credit_cents` and `invoice_total_cents`.

**Scoring** counts only app accounts belonging to this license's building allocations. The consumed population is the accounts whose `seat_status` is `RESERVED` or `ACTIVE`; `RELEASED` seats never count. The provisioned population is the accounts whose `identity_status` is `PROVISIONED`. The engaged population is the accounts that are `PROVISIONED`, have an `active_minutes_total` of at least 120 minutes — two hours of real classroom use — and satisfy the variant's recency test; a `DEPROVISIONED` or `SUSPENDED` account is never engaged no matter how many minutes it accumulated. Utilization is the consumed count divided by the license's `total_seats`, or zero when the license has no seats at all. Engagement is the engaged count divided by the variant's denominator, or zero when that denominator is zero. Cost per engaged seat is the license's `committed_charge_cents` divided by the engaged count and **rounded up** to a whole cent, and it is null when nothing is engaged. Cost efficiency is the smaller of 1 and the ratio of a 1500-cent target to that cost per engaged seat, and it is zero when nothing is engaged; when something *is* engaged but the cost per engaged seat works out to zero or less — every seat of the license having been refunded, so that `committed_charge_cents` is zero — the ratio is not taken at all and cost efficiency is 1. The three terms are then weighted — utilization at 0.50, engagement at 0.30 and cost efficiency at 0.20, weights that sum to exactly 1.00 — and the weighted sum is multiplied by 100 and **rounded half up to two decimal places exactly once, at the very end**, giving `renewal_score`. No intermediate ratio is rounded, so rounding cannot accumulate across the three terms. The recommendation tiers off that score with inclusive floors: `RENEW` at a score of 70 or above, `RENEGOTIATE` at 40 or above but below 70, and `DROP` below 40. Both comparisons are "at least", never "greater than". Both `renewal_score` and `renewal_recommendation` are also stored on the license, always on the **final** basis whatever `basis` a later reader asks for, and are recomputed whenever an account is provisioned, a usage report arrives, a seat is released — by an explicit release or by any of the cascades of §3.2, §3.4, §3.5, §3.7 and §3.8 — an identity is deprovisioned, a run is published, or the license itself is amended, an amendment re-scoring the pair even when it touched nothing but the price. They are not re-scored by a transform, which moves the license's `sync_health` and `version` and nothing else derived. Both stay null until the license has at least one app account of any `seat_status` whatever, a wholly `RELEASED` account being quite enough to keep them populated. That null rule governs the pair **stored on the license record**, and it alone; the computed summary read is unconditional and always answers with the figures it has just derived, so a license that has never provisioned an account answers a `renewal_score` of zero and a `renewal_recommendation` of `DROP` on either basis while the license record read beside it still shows both fields as null.

Work one boundary through. A license with `total_seats` of 5 and `price_per_seat_cents` of 1000, with 4 seats consumed, `committed_charge_cents` of 4000 and 2 engaged accounts, has a utilization of 0.8 contributing 0.40, an engagement of 2 divided by 4 contributing 0.15, and a cost per engaged seat of 2000 cents giving a cost efficiency of 0.75 and contributing 0.15 — a weighted sum of 0.70 and a `renewal_score` of exactly 70.00, which lands on `RENEW`. Drop either engaged account below 120 minutes and engagement falls to 0.25 while cost efficiency falls to 0.375, giving 0.55 and a score of 55.00, which is `RENEGOTIATE`.

The two scoring variants differ on precisely two points. Under the **final** basis — the decision of record for an `ACTIVE` license whose term is `ACTIVE` or `ROLLED_OVER` — the recency test requires the account's `last_activity_date` to be no earlier than 30 days before the clock date, so stale accounts stop counting as engaged, and the engagement denominator is the consumed count, meaning every seat the district is paying for. Under the **provisional** basis the recency test always passes, so lifetime minutes count whenever they were logged, and the engagement denominator is the provisioned count, excluding seats that are merely `RESERVED`. The same stored data can therefore produce two different scores and two different recommendations: with `total_seats` of 5, a list price of 1000, four consumed seats of which three are `PROVISIONED` and one is merely `RESERVED` with `PENDING_PROVISION`, `committed_charge_cents` of 4000, and two accounts holding 120 minutes each whose `last_activity_date` is 40 days before the clock, the final basis scores 40.00 and recommends `RENEGOTIATE` — nothing is engaged, which zeroes both the engagement and cost-efficiency terms — while the provisional basis scores 0.40 plus 0.30 times two-thirds plus 0.20 times 0.75, which is 0.75, or 75.00, and recommends `RENEW`.

**Invoice projection** shares the read and is evaluated in seven pinned steps, in exactly this sequence. (1) The gross seat charge is the license's `committed_charge_cents`, already net of refunds. (2) The discount rate is chosen from that gross figure by tier, with inclusive floors: 0.15 at 5000000 cents or more, 0.10 at 2000000 cents or more, 0.05 at 500000 cents or more, and 0.00 below that. (3) The discount amount is the gross figure multiplied by that rate, **rounded down**. (4) The net seat charge is the gross figure minus the discount. (5) The platform fee is the larger of a 25000-cent floor and the net seat charge multiplied by 0.03 **rounded up**. (6) The shelfware credit is the smaller of the net seat charge and the license's `unallocated_seats` multiplied by its `price_per_seat_cents` multiplied by 0.25, **rounded down** — the district gets a quarter of the list value of the seats it never allocated back as a negotiating credit. (7) The invoice total is the net seat charge plus the platform fee minus the shelfware credit.

The order is the contract: the volume discount is applied **before** the platform fee, and the shelfware credit is applied **last**, after the fee floor has already been resolved. Worked through: a gross of 2000000 cents with 3 unallocated seats at a list price of 1000 cents lands in the 0.10 tier, so the discount is 200000, the net is 1800000, the fee is the larger of 25000 and the ceiling of 54000.0 — that is 54000 — the credit is the smaller of 1800000 and the floor of 750.0, which is 750, and the invoice total is 1800000 plus 54000 minus 750, or 1853250. Computing the fee on the gross figure first, or subtracting the credit before the fee is resolved, produces different totals and is wrong. Tier floors are inclusive, and the boundary is worth asserting in both directions: a gross of 2000000 takes 0.10, while a gross of 1999999 takes 0.05, giving a discount of the floor of 99999.95 — that is 99999 — a net of 1900000 and a fee of 57000, so one cent less gross produces a *larger* net. A license still in `DRAFT` has a `committed_charge_cents` of zero and therefore lands in the 0.00 tier.
