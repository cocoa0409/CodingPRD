# Vendor Chargeback & Supplier Compliance Program API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A national retailer's supplier compliance office sits between two organisations that both dislike surprises. On one side, distribution centres receive thousands of vendor shipments a week against a published routing guide that dictates how goods must be shipped, labelled, packaged and documented; every deviation costs the retailer real money in re-work, re-labelling and dock congestion. On the other side, suppliers experience the retailer's recovery of those costs as unexplained deductions from their remittance advice, and the single most common complaint in vendor relationship reviews is not the amount but the absence of an auditable trail from a photograph taken at a receiving door to a line item netted off an invoice. This API exists to make that trail the primary artefact rather than an afterthought.

The service governs the complete chargeback lifecycle. Compliance officers draft and publish routing guide editions, each carrying a fee schedule that prices every violation type with a flat administrative fee plus a cost-recovery component. Distribution-centre inspectors receive shipments, file violations against the guide edition that was effective at the moment of arrival, and attach photographic, scan, measurement and document evidence at the door. The compliance office then assesses a chargeback, computed by a tiered pricing rule that treats a supplier's first slip of a given kind more leniently than a repeat. Suppliers may dispute an assessment within a bounded window and a bounded number of evidence rounds; adjudication is subject to a segregation-of-duties rule that forbids the officer who detected a violation from ruling on the dispute it produced. Accounts payable then posts deductions against payables or applies vendor remittance cash directly, with every recovery recorded as an allocation on a many-to-many recovery graph so that a single deduction spanning several chargebacks, or a single chargeback recovered from several sources, reconciles exactly.

Two further concerns are woven through the design. First, performance measurement: on-time in-full scorecards measure a supplier against tolerance windows and a fill-rate calculation, and a repeat-offender rule escalates a failing supplier into a corrective action plan whose non-remediation ultimately reaches the buying organisation as a range review or a sourcing hold. Second, financial governance: the retailer must be able to answer, at any instant and from source rows rather than running totals, exactly how much a vendor currently owes, how much has ever been recovered, and how much has been over-recovered and is therefore refundable. Every derived money field in this system is a full recomputation from the underlying allocations, because a chargeback programme that drifts is a chargeback programme that ends up in litigation.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the system-wide contracts: authentication, response shape, pagination, identity and audit conventions, arithmetic precision, the order in which requests are rejected, and the cross-cutting invariants that hold across every workflow in §3. All endpoints described in this document are mounted under the `/compliance` prefix, with the single exception of the test-driving clock capability described in §2.5.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which that principal acts. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the five roles the programme recognises, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). The five roles are `ComplianceOfficer`, `DCInspector`, `Vendor`, `APAccountant` and `CategoryBuyer`. Once a caller is authenticated, the acting role must appear in the authorised-role list of the endpoint being called, as tabulated in §4; a role acting outside its remit is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403). All five roles may read every collection and every single-resource view in the programme — read access is deliberately universal so that a supplier, an inspector, an accountant and a buyer all look at the same numbers — while every state-changing operation is restricted.

The `ComplianceOfficer` is the retailer's supplier-compliance operator: it registers vendors, drafts and publishes routing guides and their requirements, assesses and voids violations, requests information on and adjudicates vendor disputes, opens, measures and finalizes scorecards, decides and closes corrective action plans, and is the only role permitted to drive the programme's logical clock. The `DCInspector` works the receiving door: receiving shipments, filing compliance violations and capturing evidence. The `APAccountant` owns the money movement: drafting deductions, earmarking allocations against them, posting and reversing deductions, and applying vendor remittance cash to chargebacks. The `CategoryBuyer` raises purchase orders and owns the buying-decision escalation on repeat-offender corrective action plans; it never adjudicates disputes, which is the structural half of the segregation-of-duties rule described in §2.11. The `Vendor` role acts on its own records only: advising shipments against its own purchase orders, opening and resubmitting disputes against its own chargebacks with supporting evidence, and submitting corrective action plans issued against it.

When `X-User-Role` is `Vendor`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Vendor` entity, and ownership checks compare that value against the vendor-referencing foreign key of the targeted resource. Five operations carry this check: advising a shipment compares the owning purchase order's `vendor_id`; opening a dispute compares the chargeback's `vendor_id`; attaching evidence to a dispute and resubmitting a dispute both compare the dispute's `vendor_id`; and submitting a corrective action plan compares the plan's `vendor_id`. Any mismatch is rejected with `ERR_VENDOR_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (403). When `X-User-Role` is `ComplianceOfficer`, `DCInspector`, `APAccountant` or `CategoryBuyer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a workflow demands an audit stamp — as `published_by_user_id` on a routing guide, `detected_by_user_id` on a violation, `captured_by_user_id` on an evidence item, `decided_by_user_id` on a dispute, `posted_by_user_id` on a deduction and `applied_by_user_id` on a recovery allocation — but it never compares those values against any entity's `id`. The one comparison the server does make between two opaque identifiers is the segregation-of-duties check of §2.11, which compares one operator identifier against another rather than against an entity key.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from uppercase letters and digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, read from the program logical clock of §2.5 rather than from wall-clock time, so two responses taken at the same logical instant carry the same value.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.
- Failures raised by the transport rather than by the rules of §3 use this same envelope with an `error_id` of `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`: a path matching no endpoint answers 404, a known path called with an unsupported method answers 405, a malformed HTTP request answers 400, and an unexpected server fault answers 500. A trailing slash on a path is not significant.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection read accepts two optional query parameters. The `limit` parameter caps the number of items returned; it defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. The `offset` parameter is the number of items to skip before the first returned item; it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` or `offset` that is not an integer, or that falls outside those bounds, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400); on a collection read nested under a path identifier that identifier is resolved before the query string is inspected, so an unresolvable parent answers its own not-found error even when the query string is also invalid.

Unless a specific collection declares a different ordering in §2.4, list results are sorted by `created_at` in ascending order. Every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` in the envelope's `meta` object, where `total_count` is the size of the filtered result set before the pagination window is applied and `has_more` states whether any item remains beyond that window.

### 2.4. Collection Reads: Ordering and Filters

Because compliance work is reconciliation work, each collection is ordered by the timestamp that matters to the people who read it, and each accepts the filters that make a reconciliation tractable. Vendors are returned newest first by `created_at` descending and may be narrowed by `status`, `repeat_offender_flag` and `exposure_alert_flag`. Routing guides are ordered by `effective_from` and then by `created_at`, filterable by `status`, `scope_code` and `supersedes_guide_id`. The requirements owned by one guide are ordered by `requirement_code` and may be narrowed by `domain` and `severity_tier`. Purchase orders are ordered by `created_at` descending and filterable by `vendor_id` and `status`; the shipments raised against one purchase order are ordered by `created_at` ascending and filterable by `status`.

Violations are ordered by `detected_at` and then `created_at`, filterable by `shipment_id`, `requirement_id` and `status`. The evidence attached to a violation and the evidence attached to a dispute are each ordered by `captured_at` ascending and may be narrowed by `kind`. Chargebacks are ordered by `assessed_at` and then `created_at`, and because a chargeback carries two independent state dimensions they may be filtered on either or both — `dispute_status` and `settlement_status` — as well as by `vendor_id`, `violation_id` and `requirement_id`. Disputes are ordered by `submitted_at` and then `created_at`, filterable by `vendor_id`, `chargeback_id` and `status`. Scorecards are ordered by `period_start` ascending and then `created_at`, filterable by `vendor_id`, `period_code` and `status`. Corrective action plans are ordered by `issued_at` and then `created_at`, filterable by `vendor_id`, `trigger_scorecard_id`, `predecessor_cap_id` and `status`. Deductions are ordered by `created_at` descending and filterable by `vendor_id` and `status`. Recovery allocations — the junction read that reconciles the many-to-many graph of chargebacks against deductions — are ordered by `applied_at` and then `created_at`, filterable by `chargeback_id`, `deduction_id`, `source_type` and `effective_status`.

Two conventions apply to every collection above. Each ordering is made total by a final tiebreak on the server's row-creation sequence, applied in the same direction as the ordering itself, and where the leading sort key is a nullable timestamp — as `applied_at` is for an allocation still `PENDING` — rows holding null sort after every row that carries a value. Filter values are compared for exact equality against the stored field and are not themselves checked against the enumeration they name, so a filter carrying a value no row holds yields an empty page rather than an error, and query parameters a collection does not declare are ignored; the two boolean vendor filters are the exception, accepting only `true`, `false`, `1` or `0` case-insensitively and refusing anything else with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

### 2.5. Resource Identity, Audit Stamps and the Program Logical Clock

Every resource in the programme carries an `id`, which is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix, and a `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. Most resources additionally carry an `updated_at`, in the same timestamp format, refreshed on every mutation, and a `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by one on every mutation — including mutations the client did not request directly, such as a parent whose derived totals were recomputed by a cascade. The one exception is the evidence item: evidence is append-only audit history, so an evidence item carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has no `updated_at` and no `version`, there is no endpoint that modifies it, and nothing in the programme ever deletes one.

All time in this system comes from a single service-wide logical clock. It is initialised to `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z` when the service starts and never advances on its own; wall-clock time is irrelevant. Every timestamp the server stamps — `created_at`, `updated_at`, `published_at`, `arrived_at`, `detected_at`, `captured_at`, `assessed_at`, `submitted_at`, `decided_at`, `measured_at`, `finalized_at`, `issued_at`, `escalated_at`, `closed_at`, `posted_at`, `reversed_at` and `applied_at` — reads that clock, and every window comparison in the programme (the violation filing window, the dispute window, guide effectivity, scorecard periods, corrective-action due dates and the grace and repeat lookbacks) is evaluated against it. A `ComplianceOfficer` may move the clock with a test-driving capability that accepts a single field, `to_iso8601`, carrying the instant to move to, and answers with the resulting `current_utc`. The instant supplied may be earlier than, equal to or later than the instant the clock currently holds — the capability assigns it unconditionally rather than advancing a monotonic counter, and it neither rewrites nor revalidates any timestamp already stamped on a stored row. This capability exists so the time-boxed rules of §3 are reachable in a test harness; it is the last resort of the endpoint ladder and has no business meaning.

### 2.6. Cents, Percentages and the Rounding Discipline

Money in this programme is always an integer number of cents, and every field whose name ends in `_cents` is an integer. Because the units are already indivisible, sums and differences of money need no rounding at all; the exposure and settlement reconciliations of §3.9 are exact integer arithmetic. Rounding enters only where a percentage meets an amount, and in exactly three places, each of which rounds half up to a whole number of cents and none of which is ever applied to an intermediate sum: the handling-cost component of a chargeback assessment, the reason ceiling of a dispute determination, and the goodwill uplift of a dispute determination.

Percentages are decimal numbers carried to two decimal places and rounded half up, and each is emitted in a response as a JSON number carrying that two-decimal value — so a percentage of zero reads as `0.0` — and as null only where the field has not been resolved yet, as with a dispute's `reason_ceiling_pct` before adjudication. This covers the fee-schedule input `handling_pct`, the scorecard inputs `fill_tolerance_pct`, the scorecard outputs `on_time_pct`, `fill_rate_pct`, `otif_pct` and `composite_score`, the vendor's `otif_score`, and the dispute's resolved `reason_ceiling_pct`. Where a threshold compares a score against a constant — the repeat-offender score bands and the goodwill eligibility band — the comparison is made on the two-decimal value, and the direction of every such comparison is stated explicitly in §3 rather than left to the reader.

### 2.7. The Rejection Ladder: Order of Evaluation

Several rules can be violated by one request at the same time, and which error the caller sees is part of the contract, not an implementation detail. The server evaluates rules in the following fixed order and returns the first failure it finds. Later rungs are never evaluated once an earlier rung has failed.

1. Authentication — `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` are present and `X-User-Role` is one of the five declared roles; otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. Authorisation — the acting role appears in the target endpoint's authorised-role list; otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. Request shape — every required body field is present, every value has the correct type, every enumerated value is a declared member, and every numeric range is honoured; otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Applies to every write endpoint. The ranges checked at this rung include: `allocated_cents` at least 1, `amount_cents` at least 1, `claimed_reversal_cents` at least 1, `ordered_units` at least 1, `handling_pct` between 0.00 and 25.00 inclusive, `min_evidence_count` between 0 and 5 inclusive, `cap_cents` not less than `min_fee_cents`, `ship_window_end` strictly later than `ship_window_start`, `must_arrive_by` no earlier than `ship_window_end`, and `period_end` strictly later than `period_start`. A request carrying no body at all, or a body that is not valid JSON, is treated as an empty JSON object, while a body that is valid JSON but not an object is refused at this rung; a field present with an explicit JSON null is treated exactly as if it had been omitted, so a required field supplied as null fails as missing and an optional field supplied as null takes its documented default. An instant is accepted in any ISO 8601 form, with a trailing `Z` or an explicit offset normalised to UTC and an offset-less value read as UTC. A decimal input has its range checked against the value exactly as supplied and is only then rounded half up to two decimals.
4. Path existence — every identifier embedded in the URL resolves, checked left to right as described in §2.8; otherwise the not-found error for the first unresolved segment, at 404.
5. Body reference existence — every foreign key supplied in the request body resolves. This rung guards purchase-order creation (`vendor_id`), violation filing (`requirement_id`), scorecard creation (`vendor_id`), deduction creation (`vendor_id`) and deduction allocation (`chargeback_id`), answering with `ERR_VENDOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REQUIREMENT_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_CHARGEBACK_NOT_FOUND` at 404.
6. Vendor self-scope — when the acting role is `Vendor`, `X-User-ID` matches the owning vendor of the targeted resource, per the five comparisons listed in §2.1; otherwise `ERR_VENDOR_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (403).
7. Optimistic concurrency — the supplied `expected_vendor_version` or `expected_chargeback_version` matches the parent's current `version`; otherwise `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412). See §2.9.
8. Transition legality — the target resource is in a state from which the requested transition exists, per the state machines described throughout §3; otherwise the 409 conflict error appropriate to that resource and operation. This rung is the home of `ERR_GUIDE_NOT_DRAFT`, `ERR_SHIPMENT_ALREADY_RECEIVED`, `ERR_SHIPMENT_NOT_RECEIVED`, `ERR_VIOLATION_NOT_OPEN`, `ERR_CHARGEBACK_WRITTEN_OFF`, `ERR_DISPUTE_NOT_AWAITING_DECISION`, `ERR_DISPUTE_NOT_INFO_REQUESTED`, `ERR_SCORECARD_NOT_MEASURED`, `ERR_SCORECARD_ALREADY_FINALIZED`, `ERR_CAP_NOT_AWAITING_SUBMISSION`, `ERR_CAP_NOT_DECIDABLE`, `ERR_CAP_NOT_ESCALATABLE`, `ERR_CAP_NOT_CLOSABLE`, `ERR_DEDUCTION_NOT_DRAFT`, `ERR_DEDUCTION_ALREADY_POSTED` and `ERR_DEDUCTION_NOT_POSTED`. One carve-out matters: on chargeback assessment this rung refuses only the two terminal violation states, `VOIDED` and `CHARGED`, with `ERR_VIOLATION_NOT_OPEN`. A violation still in `DETECTED` deliberately passes this rung and is refused two rungs later at rung 10, because `DETECTED` is by construction the under-evidenced state and the caller deserves to be told so.
9. Segregation of duties — on dispute adjudication, the acting officer's `X-User-ID` differs from the `detected_by_user_id` of the violation behind the disputed chargeback; otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
10. Evidence sufficiency — on chargeback assessment, the violation's `evidence_count` is at least the pinned requirement's `min_evidence_count`; otherwise `ERR_EVIDENCE_INSUFFICIENT` (422). When `min_evidence_count` is 0 the violation was born `EVIDENCE_COMPLETE` and this rung passes on an empty evidence set.
11. Guide effectivity — the requirement pinned by the violation belongs to a guide whose `status` is `PUBLISHED` or `SUPERSEDED` and whose effectivity window contains the shipment's `arrived_at`; otherwise `ERR_GUIDE_NOT_EFFECTIVE` (422). This rung guards both violation filing and chargeback assessment.
12. Filing window — violation filing occurs strictly earlier than 72 hours after the shipment's `arrived_at`; otherwise `ERR_VIOLATION_FILING_WINDOW_CLOSED` (422).
13. Dispute window — dispute creation occurs strictly earlier than 30 days after the chargeback's `assessed_at`; otherwise `ERR_DISPUTE_WINDOW_CLOSED` (422).
14. Guide effectivity overlap — publication does not collide with an existing published edition on the same scope timeline; otherwise `ERR_GUIDE_EFFECTIVE_OVERLAP` (409). On publication this rung is reached only after the publishable-guide check of rung 24 and after the `effective_from`-in-past check, both of which §3.2 pins ahead of it; that in-past check, answering `ERR_GUIDE_EFFECTIVE_FROM_IN_PAST` (422), is not a rung of its own.
15. Scorecard period overlap — a new measurement period clears every existing period of the same vendor; otherwise `ERR_SCORECARD_PERIOD_OVERLAP` (409).
16. One dispute per chargeback — the chargeback's `dispute_status` is `NONE`; otherwise `ERR_DISPUTE_ALREADY_EXISTS` (409).
17. Dispute round bound — the dispute's `round_count` is strictly less than 2, checked on both information request and resubmission; otherwise `ERR_DISPUTE_ROUND_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (422).
18. Corrective-plan submission bound — the plan's `submission_round` is strictly less than 2; otherwise `ERR_CAP_SUBMISSION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (422).
19. Duplicate violation — no other violation on the same `shipment_id` cites the same `requirement_code` while in `DETECTED`, `EVIDENCE_COMPLETE` or `CHARGED`; otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_VIOLATION` (409).
20. Receipt quantity — the posted `received_units` does not exceed the shipment's `shipped_units`; otherwise `ERR_RECEIPT_EXCEEDS_SHIPPED` (422).
21. Affected quantity — the filed `affected_units` does not exceed the shipment's `received_units` and the filed `affected_cartons` does not exceed the shipment's `carton_count`; otherwise `ERR_AFFECTED_UNITS_EXCEED_RECEIPT` (422).
22. Vendor code uniqueness — the submitted `vendor_code` is not already held by another vendor; otherwise `ERR_VENDOR_CODE_TAKEN` (409).
23. Requirement code uniqueness — the submitted `requirement_code` is not already used within the owning `guide_id`; otherwise `ERR_REQUIREMENT_CODE_TAKEN` (409).
24. Publishable guide — the guide being published owns at least one requirement, that is, its `requirement_count` is at least 1; otherwise `ERR_GUIDE_HAS_NO_REQUIREMENTS` (422).
25. Measurable period — the measurement input set is non-empty, that is, `measured_po_count` is at least 1; otherwise `ERR_SCORECARD_NO_MEASURABLE_ORDERS` (422).
26. Commitment date — the submitted `committed_completion_at` is no later than the plan's `due_at`; otherwise `ERR_CAP_COMMITMENT_PAST_DUE` (422).
27. Escalation authority — a `buying_decision` of `SOURCING_HOLD` requires the plan's `escalation_level` to be at least 2, and a `buying_decision` of `NONE` is never accepted at escalation; otherwise `ERR_CAP_ESCALATION_LEVEL_TOO_LOW` (422).
28. Sourcing hold — purchase-order creation is blocked while the vendor's `status` is `SOURCING_HOLD`; otherwise `ERR_VENDOR_SOURCING_HOLD` (409).
29. Open dispute freeze — deduction posting is blocked when any allocated chargeback carries a `dispute_status` outside `NONE`, `UPHELD` and `PARTIALLY_REVERSED`, that is, one still `OPEN` or one already fully `REVERSED`; otherwise `ERR_CHARGEBACK_DISPUTE_OPEN` (409).
30. Claim ceiling — the submitted `claimed_reversal_cents` does not exceed the chargeback's `gross_assessed_cents` less its `reversal_cents`, checked on both dispute creation and resubmission; otherwise `ERR_CLAIM_EXCEEDS_ASSESSMENT` (422).
31. Allocation ceiling — the submitted `allocated_cents` does not exceed the target chargeback's `outstanding_cents`, with equality permitted; otherwise `ERR_ALLOCATION_EXCEEDS_OUTSTANDING` (422).
32. Cash ceiling — the submitted `amount_cents` does not exceed the target chargeback's `outstanding_cents`. Exact equality is accepted and drives the chargeback to `RECOVERED`; one cent more than `outstanding_cents` is refused with `ERR_OVER_APPLICATION` (422).
33. Exposure ceiling — a deduction's `amount_cents` does not exceed the vendor's `open_chargeback_cents` at the instant of posting, with equality permitted; otherwise `ERR_DEDUCTION_EXCEEDS_EXPOSURE` (422).

### 2.8. Path Resolution and Referential Integrity

When a URL contains more than one identifier, the server resolves them strictly left to right and fails on the first one that does not exist, so a request for a requirement under a guide that does not exist answers `ERR_ROUTING_GUIDE_NOT_FOUND` and never reaches the requirement lookup. Each resource family has its own not-found error, all at 404: `ERR_VENDOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ROUTING_GUIDE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REQUIREMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PURCHASE_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SHIPMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VIOLATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CHARGEBACK_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DISPUTE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SCORECARD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CAP_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DEDUCTION_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_ALLOCATION_NOT_FOUND`. A `supersedes_guide_id` supplied in a body that does not resolve is likewise answered with `ERR_ROUTING_GUIDE_NOT_FOUND`.

Parent-child ownership is never supplied by the client where the path already carries it. A requirement takes its `guide_id` from the path, a shipment takes its `po_id` from the path, a violation takes its `shipment_id` from the path, a dispute takes its `chargeback_id` from the path, and an evidence item takes both its polymorphic `subject_type` and the matching one of `violation_id` or `dispute_id` from the path it was created under. The other reference of the polymorphic pair is null: an item captured against a violation has `subject_type` `VIOLATION` and a null `dispute_id`, and an item attached to a dispute has `subject_type` `DISPUTE` and a null `violation_id`.

### 2.9. Optimistic Concurrency on Exposure-Sensitive Writes

Two write operations in the programme read a derived money field, then act on the strength of what they read, and both therefore require the client to declare which version of the parent it observed.

Opening a draft deduction against a vendor requires `expected_vendor_version`, a non-optional field carrying the client's last observed `version` of that `Vendor`. If the vendor's current `version` differs — because a chargeback was assessed, a dispute was decided, another deduction was drafted or posted, or a scorecard was finalized since the client last read the vendor — the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412). Several deductions legitimately stack against one payable, so a second draft is perfectly valid product behaviour; the check exists because every draft re-reads the vendor's open exposure, and a stale view of that exposure would let an accountant net more than the vendor actually owes.

Applying vendor cash to a chargeback requires `expected_chargeback_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of that `Chargeback`. A mismatch with the chargeback's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412). Partial cash applications legitimately stack on one chargeback, so a second application is valid product behaviour; the check exists because the over-application ceiling of rung 32 is computed from `outstanding_cents`, and a stale view of that field would let cash exceed the amount actually owed.

Both fields are integers of at least 1, so a value of 0 or a negative value is a shape failure refused at rung 3 with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400) rather than a stale view refused at rung 7. No other endpoint in the programme accepts a version field, and no other endpoint performs a concurrency check. Successful writes advance `version` on the resources they touch as described in §2.10, but only these two endpoints ask the client to prove what it saw.

### 2.10. Cascade Discipline and Derived-Field Reconciliation

Almost every write in this programme mutates more than one row. The design principle is that cascades are numbered, ordered and complete: link 1 is the operation the caller asked for, and each subsequent link is caused by the one before it. Each entity a cascade touches has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by one, whether or not the caller named that entity in the request. A vendor whose exposure totals were recomputed as the fourth link of a chargeback assessment is, from an optimistic-concurrency point of view, a modified vendor.

Derived fields are never maintained incrementally. Whenever a trigger fires, every affected derived field is recomputed in full from its source rows: a vendor's exposure figures from all of its chargebacks, a chargeback's recovery figures from all of its allocations, a purchase order's receipt roll-up from all of its received shipments, a guide's `requirement_count` from its requirements. This is deliberately more work than a delta update, and it is the reason a posted deduction that is later reversed returns every affected total to exactly its pre-posting value with no drift.

Parent state also follows child state. A purchase order's `status` is not set by any caller; it is derived from the receipt posture of its shipments. A vendor's `status` is not set by any caller either; it follows the issuance, escalation and closure of corrective action plans. A violation's promotion out of `DETECTED` follows the arrival of evidence rather than an explicit request. Where a state change is triggered this way the state machine is still authoritative: the server performs the transition only when the transition's preconditions hold.

### 2.11. Segregation of Duties and Vendor Self-Scope

The compliance office may not sit in judgement of its own detection. When a dispute is adjudicated, the acting `ComplianceOfficer`'s `X-User-ID` is compared against the `detected_by_user_id` recorded on the compliance violation that produced the disputed chargeback, and if the two are equal the adjudication is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). This is the one place where two opaque operator identifiers are compared to each other. Note that the check reaches back two hops — from the dispute to its chargeback, and from the chargeback to its violation — so an officer who has never touched the dispute may still be barred from deciding it.

The buying consequence is separated the same way. Escalating a rejected corrective action plan into a range review or a sourcing hold is reserved to the `CategoryBuyer` and is never available to the `ComplianceOfficer`, so the office that measures a supplier cannot unilaterally stop buying from it. Conversely, the decisions that keep a plan inside the compliance process — accepting, rejecting and closing it — are reserved to the `ComplianceOfficer` and are never available to the `CategoryBuyer`. On the supplier side, the `Vendor` role is confined to its own records by the ownership comparisons of §2.1, which means a supplier can never read a competitor's exposure into its own dispute or attach evidence to a case that is not its own.

### 2.12. Conserved Quantities, Monotonic Audit Counters and Compensating Actions

Three classes of counter behave differently, and conflating them is the most common source of reconciliation error.

Conserved counters must return to their prior value when the event that raised them is undone. A shipment's `open_violation_count` and a vendor's `open_violation_count` both rise by one when a violation is filed and fall by one when that violation leaves the open set, whether it leaves by being voided or by being charged; after either exit the counters read exactly what they read before the filing. A vendor's `open_cap_count` behaves the same way across plan issuance and plan closure. The vendor's money figures — `open_chargeback_cents`, `recovered_lifetime_cents` and `refund_due_cents` — are conserved in the stronger sense that they are recomputed from source rows rather than adjusted, so reversing a posted deduction restores them exactly.

Monotonic audit counters never fall. A shipment's `evidence_capture_count` counts every evidence item ever captured against any violation on that shipment, and it is deliberately not decremented when a violation is voided — the photographs were taken, the inspection happened, and the audit record of that work survives the withdrawal of the finding.

Compensating actions are the programme's answer to irreversible-looking events. Voiding a violation compensates a detection. Reversing a posted deduction compensates a posting, unwinding every one of its side effects. Closing a corrective action plan successfully compensates a sourcing hold by returning the vendor to probation, and then to active status once no plan remains open. A dispute reversal compensates an assessment, up to and including writing the chargeback off entirely. In every case the compensating action is a forward transition to a new state rather than an erasure of history: the original row remains readable, with the state that records what happened to it.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Suppliers and the Sourcing Posture

A supplier enters the programme when a `ComplianceOfficer` registers it with three pieces of information: a `vendor_code`, the retailer-assigned supplier code of between 1 and 16 characters drawn from uppercase letters and digits and unique across the whole programme; a `legal_name` of between 1 and 160 characters naming the legal entity; and `payment_terms_days`, an integer between 1 and 120 recording the net payment terms that apply when a deduction is later netted against that supplier's payables. A code already held by another supplier is refused with `ERR_VENDOR_CODE_TAKEN`.

A newly registered supplier begins in `ACTIVE` sourcing posture with a clean sheet: `otif_score` 0.00, `open_chargeback_cents`, `recovered_lifetime_cents`, `refund_due_cents`, `open_violation_count` and `open_cap_count` all zero, and both governance flags — `repeat_offender_flag` and `exposure_alert_flag` — false. The `otif_score` is not a free-standing number; it is the `composite_score` of the supplier's most recently finalized scorecard, chosen by `period_end` descending and, where two finalized periods share an end instant, by `created_at` descending. Until a scorecard has been finalized the score reads 0.00.

Sourcing posture is one of `ACTIVE`, `PROBATION` and `SOURCING_HOLD`, and no caller sets it directly. A supplier moves `ACTIVE → PROBATION` as the fourth link of the scorecard finalization cascade described in §3.7 — specifically when a measured scorecard whose `cap_required` is true is finalized against a supplier that is currently `ACTIVE`, causing a corrective action plan to be issued. It moves `PROBATION → SOURCING_HOLD` when a `CategoryBuyer` escalates a rejected corrective action plan with a buying decision of `SOURCING_HOLD`, which requires the supplier to be on `PROBATION` at that instant. It moves `SOURCING_HOLD → PROBATION` when a plan that was escalated with a `SOURCING_HOLD` buying decision is closed successfully, and `PROBATION → ACTIVE` when an accepted plan is closed successfully and it was the supplier's only plan in the open set — that is, when `open_cap_count` stands at 1 and therefore falls to 0. Each of these movements increments the supplier's `version` by one and refreshes its `updated_at`. The cycle is re-enterable: a later failing scorecard can put a restored supplier back on probation.

The practical consequence of a `SOURCING_HOLD` is commercial. While a supplier's `status` is `SOURCING_HOLD` the buying organisation may not raise new purchase orders against it, and any attempt is refused with `ERR_VENDOR_SOURCING_HOLD`. Existing orders, shipments and chargebacks are unaffected; the hold stops new commitment, not settlement of old commitment.

Four derived counters on the supplier record answer the questions a relationship review actually asks. `open_violation_count` is the number of compliance violations, across every shipment of every purchase order belonging to this supplier, whose `status` is `DETECTED` or `EVIDENCE_COMPLETE`. `open_cap_count` is the number of corrective action plans for this supplier whose `status` is one of `ISSUED`, `VENDOR_SUBMITTED`, `REJECTED`, `ACCEPTED` or `ESCALATED`. `repeat_offender_flag` records the repeat-offender determination written at scorecard finalization. `exposure_alert_flag` is recomputed every single time `open_chargeback_cents` changes and is true exactly when that figure has reached 500,000 cents — five thousand currency units of unrecovered assessment — or more.

### 3.2. Publishing the Routing Guide and Its Chargeback Schedule

The routing guide is the contract a supplier is measured against, and the programme treats it as a versioned publication rather than a mutable document. A `ComplianceOfficer` drafts an edition with a `guide_code` of 1 to 32 characters identifying the edition — a label the programme does not require to be unique, so two editions may legitimately share a code — a `title` of 1 to 160 characters, a `scope_code` naming the requirement family the edition governs, and an `effective_from` instant in ISO 8601 UTC with three fractional digits, which is the inclusive lower bound of the edition's effectivity interval. The `scope_code` is one of `ALL`, `SHIPMENT`, `LABELING`, `PACKAGING` or `DOCUMENTATION`. Editions sharing a `scope_code` form a single effectivity timeline, and an edition scoped `ALL` shares a timeline with every other scope — two editions collide when their `scope_code` values are equal or when either one of them is `ALL`. An optional `supersedes_guide_id` names the currently published edition this one is intended to retire; it is left null for the first edition on a timeline, and because it points at another guide the chain it forms is self-referential. The reference is resolved at drafting time, so an identifier matching no guide is already refused there with `ERR_ROUTING_GUIDE_NOT_FOUND`; what is deferred to publication is the check that the edition it names is itself `PUBLISHED` and on a colliding scope, so a draft may legitimately declare a predecessor that is still `DRAFT` at the moment it is declared. A freshly drafted edition is `DRAFT`, with `requirement_count` 0, `effective_until` null, and `published_at` and `published_by_user_id` both null.

Requirements are added to an edition while it is still `DRAFT`; once published, its schedule is frozen, and an attempt to add a requirement to a non-`DRAFT` edition is refused with `ERR_GUIDE_NOT_DRAFT`. Each requirement is one violation type with its price. The `requirement_code` — values such as `ASN_LATE` or `GS1_LABEL_MISSING` — is 1 to 32 characters and must be unique within the owning edition, with a collision refused as `ERR_REQUIREMENT_CODE_TAKEN`. The `domain` places the requirement in one of `SHIPMENT`, `LABELING`, `PACKAGING` or `DOCUMENTATION`. The `severity_tier` is one of `TIER_1`, `TIER_2` or `TIER_3` and selects which cost-recovery branch prices a breach. `min_evidence_count`, an integer from 0 to 5, is the number of evidence items that must exist before a breach of this requirement may be assessed; a value of 0 means evidence is optional for this requirement. The five money and rate columns are the chargeback schedule proper: `fixed_fee_cents`, a non-negative flat administrative fee charged per breach; `per_unit_recovery_cents`, a non-negative rate applied to the number of affected units; `handling_pct`, a percentage from 0.00 to 25.00 carried to two decimals and applied to the receipt's invoice value as recoverable handling cost; `min_fee_cents`, a non-negative floor applied to the assessed amount; and `cap_cents`, a ceiling of at least 1 that must also be no smaller than `min_fee_cents`. A new requirement is created with `effective_until` null and raises its owning edition's `requirement_count` by one.

Publication is where the effectivity timeline is enforced. A `ComplianceOfficer` publishes an edition that is still `DRAFT`; publishing anything else is refused with `ERR_GUIDE_NOT_DRAFT`. The edition must own at least one requirement, so a `requirement_count` of 0 is refused with `ERR_GUIDE_HAS_NO_REQUIREMENTS`. The edition's `effective_from` must be strictly later than the current logical clock — a guide may not begin governing arrivals that have already happened — and an `effective_from` at or before the clock is refused with `ERR_GUIDE_EFFECTIVE_FROM_IN_PAST`. Timeline collision is then checked: publication is refused with `ERR_GUIDE_EFFECTIVE_OVERLAP` when any colliding edition that is already `PUBLISHED` has an `effective_from` at or later than the candidate's `effective_from`. Effectivity intervals are half-open, running from `effective_from` inclusive to `effective_until` exclusive, so adjacency at a shared instant is allowed: a successor edition may begin at exactly the instant its predecessor's window ends. Finally, when `supersedes_guide_id` is supplied it must resolve — an unresolvable value answers `ERR_ROUTING_GUIDE_NOT_FOUND` — and the edition it names must itself be `PUBLISHED` and must collide with the candidate on scope; a predecessor that is not published or that governs an unrelated scope is refused with `ERR_GUIDE_EFFECTIVE_OVERLAP`, since it cannot be part of this timeline.

A successful publication is a three-link cascade. The first link moves the edition `DRAFT → PUBLISHED`, stamps `published_at` with the current logical clock and records the acting operator in `published_by_user_id`. The second link, caused by the first, applies only when `supersedes_guide_id` was supplied: the named predecessor moves `PUBLISHED → SUPERSEDED` and its `effective_until` is stamped with the successor's `effective_from`, closing its half-open window at exactly the instant the new edition opens. The third link, caused by the second, copies that same `effective_until` down onto every requirement owned by the superseded predecessor, retiring the old fee schedule without deleting a single row — an assessment computed under the old schedule must remain explicable years later. Both editions have their `version` incremented by one, and so does every requirement the third link retires. A `SUPERSEDED` edition is terminal; while an edition is `DRAFT` or `PUBLISHED` its `effective_until` and its requirements' `effective_until` remain null, meaning the window is open-ended above.

### 3.3. Purchase Orders, Advance Shipping Notices and Door Receipt

A `CategoryBuyer` raises a purchase order naming the supplier through a payload containing that supplier's `vendor_id`, together with a `po_number` of 1 to 32 characters, which is a reference label the programme does not require to be unique, `ordered_units` of at least 1, and `unit_cost_cents` of at least 1 representing landed cost per unit. Three instants frame the commitment: `ship_window_start`, the inclusive start of the supplier's permitted ship window; `ship_window_end`, the exclusive end of that window, which must fall strictly after the start; and `must_arrive_by`, the contractual arrival deadline, which may not fall earlier than `ship_window_end`. The `must_arrive_by` instant is the centre of the on-time tolerance window used in scorecard measurement, and `ordered_units` is the denominator of the fill-rate calculation, which is why both are validated at creation rather than at measurement time. A new order is `OPEN`, with `received_units` 0 and `first_receipt_at` null. An unresolvable `vendor_id` answers `ERR_VENDOR_NOT_FOUND`, and a supplier on sourcing hold answers `ERR_VENDOR_SOURCING_HOLD`.

The supplier then advises its shipments. Acting in the `Vendor` role against its own order — the owning order's `vendor_id` must equal the caller's `X-User-ID`, or the request is refused with `ERR_VENDOR_SCOPE_VIOLATION` — a supplier declares an `asn_number` of 1 to 32 characters carrying the advance shipping notice reference, which likewise need not be unique, `shipped_units` of at least 1 that it claims to have despatched, and `carton_count` of at least 1 as declared on the notice, which becomes the denominator for carton-scoped findings. A shipment is created `IN_TRANSIT`, taking its owning `po_id` from the path, with `received_units` null, `arrived_at` null, `invoice_value_cents` 0, `open_violation_count` 0 and `evidence_capture_count` 0. Nothing about the owning order's own receipt posture gates the notice, so a further shipment may be advised against an order that is already `PARTIALLY_RECEIVED` or `RECEIVED`, and neither the order's `status` nor its roll-up fields move when a notice is merely advised. Invoice value is deliberately not derived from what the supplier claims to have shipped; it is derived at the door from what was actually counted.

Receipt is the `DCInspector`'s act and the pivot of the whole programme. The inspector posts the counted quantity as `received_units`, which may be zero or more but may never exceed the shipment's `shipped_units`; an excess is refused with `ERR_RECEIPT_EXCEEDS_SHIPPED`. The shipment must still be `IN_TRANSIT`; receiving one that has already been received is refused with `ERR_SHIPMENT_ALREADY_RECEIVED`. On success the shipment advances `IN_TRANSIT → RECEIVED`, stores the counted `received_units`, stamps `arrived_at` with the current logical clock, and computes `invoice_value_cents` as the counted `received_units` multiplied by the owning order's `unit_cost_cents`. That `arrived_at` instant does two further jobs: it opens the 72-hour window inside which findings against this shipment may be filed, and it is the instant against which routing-guide effectivity is tested.

Receipt then bubbles up to the owning order, which is a compound state derived entirely from its children. The order's `received_units` is recomputed as the sum of `received_units` over its shipments whose `status` is `RECEIVED`, and its `first_receipt_at` as the earliest `arrived_at` among those same shipments, remaining null while none has been received. The order's `status` follows: it moves `OPEN → PARTIALLY_RECEIVED` when the recomputed `received_units` is at least 1 but still below `ordered_units`, and it advances to `RECEIVED` once the recomputed `received_units` reaches or exceeds `ordered_units`. A single shipment large enough to satisfy the whole order therefore takes the order `OPEN → RECEIVED` directly, skipping `PARTIALLY_RECEIVED` altogether; an order already `PARTIALLY_RECEIVED` advances `PARTIALLY_RECEIVED → RECEIVED` on a later receipt that closes the gap. `RECEIVED` is terminal for both the shipment and the order. Each of these movements increments the affected resource's `version` by one: the receipt always takes one increment on the shipment itself, while the owning order is incremented only where the recomputation actually changes one of its three derived fields — `received_units`, `first_receipt_at` or `status` — so the ordinary receipt costs one increment on the order, and a receipt of zero units against an order that already carries an earlier `first_receipt_at` leaves the order's `version` and `updated_at` untouched.

### 3.4. Detecting Violations at the Door and Capturing Evidence

A `DCInspector` files a finding against a received shipment through a payload containing the `requirement_id` of the breached routing-guide requirement, the `affected_units` and `affected_cartons` quantities, and a `narrative` of 1 to 500 characters describing what was found. `affected_units` must be zero or more and no greater than the shipment's `received_units`; `affected_cartons` must be zero or more and no greater than the shipment's `carton_count`; a breach of either bound is refused with `ERR_AFFECTED_UNITS_EXCEED_RECEIPT`. Affected units are the multiplier for per-unit cost recovery, while affected cartons are recorded for the benefit of the evidence reviewer and do not enter any calculation.

Four gates guard the filing beyond the quantity bounds. The shipment must already be `RECEIVED`; a finding against a shipment still `IN_TRANSIT` is refused with `ERR_SHIPMENT_NOT_RECEIVED`, because there is no counted quantity and no arrival instant to reason about. The `requirement_id` must resolve, or the answer is `ERR_REQUIREMENT_NOT_FOUND`. The requirement's owning edition must have been governing arrivals at the moment this shipment arrived: its `status` must be `PUBLISHED` or `SUPERSEDED`, its `effective_from` must be at or before the shipment's `arrived_at`, and the shipment's `arrived_at` must fall strictly before its `effective_until` — an edition whose `effective_until` is null is treated as unbounded above, so it governs every arrival at or after its start. A requirement drawn from a `DRAFT` edition, or from an edition whose window does not contain the arrival, is refused with `ERR_GUIDE_NOT_EFFECTIVE`. And the filing window must still be open: the current logical clock must be strictly earlier than 72 hours — three days — after the shipment's `arrived_at`. At exactly 72 hours the window is already closed, and a late filing is refused with `ERR_VIOLATION_FILING_WINDOW_CLOSED`. These gates are evaluated in the ladder order of §2.7 rather than in the order they are narrated here, so the `requirement_id` is resolved at rung 5 before the shipment's receipt posture is tested at rung 8, and both of those precede the duplicate check at rung 19 and the quantity bounds at rung 21.

Duplicate findings are blocked at the requirement-code level rather than the requirement-row level. A filing is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_VIOLATION` when another violation on the same shipment already cites the same `requirement_code` and is in `DETECTED`, `EVIDENCE_COMPLETE` or `CHARGED`. Because the comparison is on the code and not the row identifier, re-citing the same violation type from a newer guide edition is still a duplicate. Violations that have been voided do not block anything: a withdrawn finding may be re-filed, which is precisely why voiding exists.

A newly filed violation records `detected_at` from the current logical clock and `detected_by_user_id` from the acting inspector's `X-User-ID`, takes its `shipment_id` from the path, pins the supplied `requirement_id`, starts with `evidence_count` 0 and `void_reason` null, and raises both the shipment's `open_violation_count` and the supplier's `open_violation_count` by one. Its initial state depends on the requirement it cites: when the pinned requirement's `min_evidence_count` is 0 the violation is born directly in `EVIDENCE_COMPLETE`, and otherwise it starts in `DETECTED`.

Evidence is captured by the `DCInspector` against a violation that has not yet reached a terminal state; capture against a violation whose `status` is `VOIDED` or `CHARGED` is refused with `ERR_VIOLATION_NOT_OPEN`. Each item declares a `kind` — one of `PHOTO`, `SCAN`, `MEASUREMENT` or `DOCUMENT` — a `reference_uri` of 1 to 300 characters pointing immutably at the stored artefact, and an optional `note` annotation which defaults to null when omitted. The stored item records `captured_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID` and `captured_at` from the current logical clock, carries `subject_type` `VIOLATION` with the owning `violation_id` set and `dispute_id` null, and is never modified thereafter. Each capture raises the violation's `evidence_count` by one and the shipment's `evidence_capture_count` by one. As soon as `evidence_count` reaches or exceeds the pinned requirement's `min_evidence_count`, the violation is promoted `DETECTED → EVIDENCE_COMPLETE` in the same request and its `version` is incremented.

Withdrawal is the compliance office's compensating action. A `ComplianceOfficer` voids a violation with a mandatory `void_reason`, permitted while the violation's `status` is `DETECTED` or `EVIDENCE_COMPLETE`; from `VOIDED` or `CHARGED` the request is refused with `ERR_VIOLATION_NOT_OPEN`. The void takes the violation `DETECTED → VOIDED` or `EVIDENCE_COMPLETE → VOIDED` with an identical side-effect set from either origin: the recorded `void_reason` is stored, the shipment's `open_violation_count` and the supplier's `open_violation_count` each fall by one to exactly their pre-filing values, and the violation's `version` increments. The shipment's `evidence_capture_count` is pointedly not decremented — the evidence remains, and so does the count of it. Voiding mints no chargeback and touches no money field on the supplier; that asymmetry with the other terminal branch is the whole point of the fork.

### 3.5. Assessing the Chargeback

Assessment is the `ComplianceOfficer`'s decision to turn an evidenced finding into money owed, and it takes the violation `EVIDENCE_COMPLETE → CHARGED`. An optional `assessment_note` of 1 to 1000 characters may accompany the request for the file; it is validated at the request-shape rung like any other optional string and is then discarded, so neither the violation nor the minted chargeback carries a field recording it and it is absent from every read. Three preconditions apply, and the order in which they refuse matters. A violation already in `VOIDED` or `CHARGED` is refused with `ERR_VIOLATION_NOT_OPEN` at the transition-legality rung, and the shipment's receipt posture is tested at that same rung immediately after: the shipment must be `RECEIVED`, so that `invoice_value_cents` is already final, or the answer is `ERR_SHIPMENT_NOT_RECEIVED`. Only once both of those pass is evidence weighed — a violation still in `DETECTED` deliberately passes the transition-legality rung and is refused instead with `ERR_EVIDENCE_INSUFFICIENT` (422), because `DETECTED` means the pinned requirement's `min_evidence_count` has not yet been met and the caller should be told that rather than a generic conflict. And guide effectivity is re-tested at assessment exactly as it was at filing — the pinned requirement's edition must be `PUBLISHED` or `SUPERSEDED` with `effective_from` at or before the shipment's `arrived_at` and `arrived_at` strictly before `effective_until` where that bound exists — with a failure refused as `ERR_GUIDE_NOT_EFFECTIVE`.

The amount is computed from exactly three rows: the violation being assessed, its shipment, and the single requirement the violation pinned. No other finding on the same shipment participates, and the requirement is copied onto the resulting chargeback so that a later guide supersession can never re-price an assessment that has already been made. The computation proceeds in a fixed order. The flat component, `fixed_fee_cents`, is copied straight from the pinned requirement's `fixed_fee_cents`. A unit-recovery amount is formed by multiplying the requirement's `per_unit_recovery_cents` by the violation's `affected_units`. A handling-recovery amount is formed by multiplying the shipment's `invoice_value_cents` by the requirement's `handling_pct`, dividing by 100, and rounding the result half up to a whole number of cents before it is used anywhere.

The severity tier then selects the cost-recovery branch, and the three branches are genuinely different rather than differently parameterised. For a `TIER_1` requirement, `cost_recovery_cents` is the unit-recovery amount plus the handling-recovery amount — both components are charged. For a `TIER_2` requirement, `cost_recovery_cents` is whichever of the two amounts is larger, and the smaller one is discarded. For a `TIER_3` requirement, `cost_recovery_cents` is zero and only the flat fee survives.

Repeat behaviour then decides which of two assessment variants applies, and the chosen variant is recorded on the chargeback as `assessment_variant`. The variant is `GRACE_FIRST_OCCURRENCE` when the pinned requirement's `severity_tier` is `TIER_2` or `TIER_3` **and** the supplier has no other chargeback at all whose pinned requirement carries this same `requirement_code` with an `assessed_at` falling within the 180 days immediately before the current logical clock. That lookback is inclusive at its lower bound, so a prior chargeback whose `assessed_at` sits exactly 180 days before the current instant still defeats the grace variant, and it takes no account of the prior chargeback's `dispute_status` — even one that was fully reversed on dispute counts as a prior occurrence. Otherwise the variant is `STANDARD` — which is always the case for a `TIER_1` requirement, and is also the case for a `TIER_2` or `TIER_3` requirement once a matching prior chargeback exists inside that 180-day lookback. Under `STANDARD`, `cost_recovery_cents` keeps the value the severity-tier branch produced, the raw amount is the flat fee plus that cost recovery, the requirement's `min_fee_cents` is applied as a floor to the raw amount, and the requirement's `cap_cents` is applied as a ceiling to the floored result; `gross_assessed_cents` is that floored-then-capped figure. Under `GRACE_FIRST_OCCURRENCE`, `cost_recovery_cents` is forced to zero and the `min_fee_cents` floor is not applied at all, so `gross_assessed_cents` is simply whichever is smaller of the flat fee and `cap_cents`. Identical violation data therefore yields a different `gross_assessed_cents` under the two variants, and a supplier's second breach of the same requirement type inside that 180-day lookback is materially more expensive than its first.

A successful assessment is a four-link cascade. The first link moves the violation to `CHARGED` and lowers both the shipment's `open_violation_count` and the supplier's `open_violation_count` by one, restoring them to their pre-filing values just as a void would. The second link, caused by the first, mints the chargeback: it records `violation_id` for the finding it prices, `vendor_id` denormalised from the shipment's purchase order so that exposure queries need not traverse three hops, `requirement_id` as the frozen copy of the pinned requirement, the computed `fixed_fee_cents`, `cost_recovery_cents` and `gross_assessed_cents`, the selected `assessment_variant`, `evidence_at_assessment_count` as a snapshot of the violation's `evidence_count` at this instant, and `assessed_at` from the current logical clock, which starts the supplier's dispute window. It opens with `reversal_cents` 0, `recovered_cents` 0, `outstanding_cents` equal to `gross_assessed_cents`, `deduction_posted` false, `dispute_status` `NONE` and `settlement_status` `ASSESSED`. That opening posture is written directly rather than derived: assessment does not run the settlement reconciliation of §3.9, which re-derives the settlement dimension only on a later money or dispute event, so in the degenerate case where the computed `gross_assessed_cents` is 0 the chargeback is still minted `ASSESSED` with `outstanding_cents` 0. The third link, caused by the second, recomputes the supplier's `open_chargeback_cents`. The fourth link, caused by the third, re-derives `exposure_alert_flag` as true exactly when `open_chargeback_cents` has reached 500,000 cents or more, and increments the supplier's `version` by one.

The snapshot taken in the second link is not decoration. `evidence_at_assessment_count` freezes how well evidenced the assessment was at the moment it was made, and it is the input that decides whether a later dispute pleading insufficient evidence is entitled to a full reversal or only a half one. Because it is a snapshot, evidence attached after assessment cannot retroactively strengthen the retailer's position.

### 3.6. Vendor Disputes: Rounds, Evidence Review and the Reversal Determination

A chargeback carries two state dimensions on the same row, and they evolve independently of one another. The `dispute_status` dimension records the adjudication story and moves only through dispute handling; the `settlement_status` dimension records the money story and moves only through deduction posting, deduction reversal and cash application. A chargeback that has already been partly recovered can still open a dispute, and a chargeback whose dispute was upheld can still be recovered afterwards. Neither dimension constrains the other except where this document says so explicitly.

The `dispute_status` dimension begins at `NONE`. A supplier acting in the `Vendor` role opens a dispute against its own chargeback — the chargeback's `vendor_id` must equal the caller's `X-User-ID`, or the request is refused with `ERR_VENDOR_SCOPE_VIOLATION` — through a payload containing a `reason_code`, a `claimed_reversal_cents` amount of at least 1, and a `narrative` of 1 to 1000 characters arguing the case for this round. The `reason_code` is one of `NOT_OUR_LABEL`, `CARRIER_CAUSED`, `DUPLICATE_ASSESSMENT`, `EVIDENCE_INSUFFICIENT` or `FEE_MISCALCULATED`, and it selects the reversal ceiling applied at decision time. Four gates guard the opening. The chargeback's `settlement_status` must not be `WRITTEN_OFF`; disputing a chargeback that has already been written off is refused with `ERR_CHARGEBACK_WRITTEN_OFF`. The dispute window must still be open: the current logical clock must be strictly earlier than 30 days after the chargeback's `assessed_at`, and at exactly 30 days the window is already closed, refused with `ERR_DISPUTE_WINDOW_CLOSED`. There may be at most one dispute per chargeback, so the chargeback's `dispute_status` must be `NONE`, and a second attempt is refused with `ERR_DISPUTE_ALREADY_EXISTS`. And the claim may not exceed what is still assessed: `claimed_reversal_cents` must be no greater than the chargeback's `gross_assessed_cents` less its `reversal_cents`, with an excess refused as `ERR_CLAIM_EXCEEDS_ASSESSMENT`.

A successful opening creates the dispute in `SUBMITTED` with `round_count` 1, `submitted_at` from the current logical clock, `chargeback_id` taken from the path and `vendor_id` denormalised from the chargeback. Its decision fields are all empty at this point: `reason_ceiling_pct` null, `approved_reversal_cents` 0, `goodwill_cents` 0, `decided_at` null, `decided_by_user_id` null, `review_note` null and `information_request_note` null. The same request moves the chargeback's `dispute_status` `NONE → OPEN` and increments the chargeback's `version` by one; its `settlement_status` is untouched, so a chargeback that was `PARTIALLY_RECOVERED` or `RECOVERED` stays exactly where it was on that dimension.

A supplier may attach supporting material to its own live dispute. Dispute evidence carries the same `kind`, `reference_uri` and optional `note` fields as door evidence, and is stored with `subject_type` `DISPUTE`, the owning `dispute_id` set, `violation_id` null, `captured_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID` and `captured_at` from the logical clock. Attachment requires the dispute's `status` to be `SUBMITTED` or `INFO_REQUESTED`; attaching to an already-decided dispute is refused with `ERR_DISPUTE_NOT_AWAITING_DECISION`. Dispute evidence never affects `evidence_count` or `evidence_capture_count` — those counters belong to the detection side of the file. Unlike a door capture, which promotes and re-versions the violation it evidences, a dispute attachment writes exactly one row and no more: the dispute itself is not touched, so neither its `updated_at` nor its `version` moves, and the chargeback behind it is not touched either.

The review cycle is bounded at two rounds. A `ComplianceOfficer` who needs more from the supplier bounces the case back with a mandatory `information_request_note` of 1 to 1000 characters, taking the dispute `SUBMITTED → INFO_REQUESTED`, storing the note and incrementing the dispute's `version`. This requires the dispute to be `SUBMITTED` — from `INFO_REQUESTED` or from any decided state the request is refused with `ERR_DISPUTE_NOT_AWAITING_DECISION` — and requires `round_count` to be strictly less than 2, with a second information request on the same case refused as `ERR_DISPUTE_ROUND_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. The supplier answers by resubmitting with a fresh `claimed_reversal_cents` and a fresh `narrative`, which replace the previous round's values; this takes the dispute `INFO_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED`, raises `round_count` by one, clears `information_request_note` and increments the dispute's `version`. Resubmission requires the dispute to be exactly `INFO_REQUESTED`, refused otherwise with `ERR_DISPUTE_NOT_INFO_REQUESTED`; requires `round_count` to be strictly less than 2, refused otherwise with `ERR_DISPUTE_ROUND_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`; and re-checks the claim against `gross_assessed_cents` less `reversal_cents`, refused otherwise with `ERR_CLAIM_EXCEEDS_ASSESSMENT`. The loop `SUBMITTED → INFO_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED` therefore closes once and only once; a third presentation of the case is impossible. Neither move in the loop touches the chargeback: its `dispute_status` stands at `OPEN` and its `version` and `updated_at` do not move again until adjudication.

Adjudication is performed by a `ComplianceOfficer` supplying a `decision` of either `APPROVE` or `DENY` together with a `review_note` of 1 to 1000 characters recording the evidence review. The dispute must be `SUBMITTED` or `INFO_REQUESTED`, refused otherwise with `ERR_DISPUTE_NOT_AWAITING_DECISION`, and the acting officer's `X-User-ID` must differ from the `detected_by_user_id` of the violation behind the disputed chargeback, refused otherwise with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

A `DENY` decision short-circuits the arithmetic: `reason_ceiling_pct` is set to 0.00, `goodwill_cents` to 0 and `approved_reversal_cents` to 0, the dispute lands in `DENIED`, and the chargeback moves `OPEN → UPHELD` on its dispute dimension with `reversal_cents` left exactly as it was. No money field on the supplier changes, and the `settlement_status` dimension does not move. From either origin — `SUBMITTED → DENIED` or `INFO_REQUESTED → DENIED` — the side-effect set is identical.

An `APPROVE` decision runs the reversal determination, whose seven steps each run exactly once in the following pinned order. The order matters because the dependency looks circular — the goodwill uplift is granted only when the outcome is partial, yet the outcome is classified from an amount the uplift changes — and the pinned order resolves it: goodwill is decided against the pre-goodwill classification, and the post-goodwill amount then re-classifies the outcome without withdrawing the uplift.

- **Step one** establishes the eligible cap as the chargeback's `gross_assessed_cents` less its `reversal_cents`, so any reversal already granted on this chargeback is excluded from what can be granted again.
- **Step two** resolves `reason_ceiling_pct` from the dispute's `reason_code`. A `reason_code` of `NOT_OUR_LABEL`, `DUPLICATE_ASSESSMENT` or `FEE_MISCALCULATED` yields 100.00. A `reason_code` of `CARRIER_CAUSED` yields 60.00. A `reason_code` of `EVIDENCE_INSUFFICIENT` yields 100.00 when the chargeback's `evidence_at_assessment_count` was strictly below the pinned requirement's `min_evidence_count` at assessment, and 50.00 when that snapshot was at or above `min_evidence_count`. The resolved percentage is stored on the dispute.
- **Step three** converts the ceiling into cents by multiplying the eligible cap by `reason_ceiling_pct`, dividing by 100, and rounding half up to whole cents.
- **Step four** takes the requested amount as whichever is smaller of the supplier's `claimed_reversal_cents` and the step-three ceiling in cents.
- **Step five** classifies the pre-goodwill outcome as full when the requested amount is at or above the eligible cap and partial otherwise, then computes `goodwill_cents`. Goodwill is granted only when all three of the following hold: the pre-goodwill outcome is partial, the supplier's `otif_score` is at or above 95.00, and the dispute's `round_count` is exactly 1. When they all hold, `goodwill_cents` is 25 per cent of the gap between the eligible cap and the requested amount, rounded half up to whole cents; otherwise `goodwill_cents` is 0. A supplier that needed a second round to make its case therefore forfeits goodwill, as does a supplier whose on-time in-full performance is below the 95.00 threshold.
- **Step six** sets `approved_reversal_cents` to the requested amount plus `goodwill_cents`, capped at the eligible cap so the uplift can never carry the reversal past what is assessed.
- **Step seven** classifies the outcome from `approved_reversal_cents`: the dispute lands in `DENIED` when the approved amount is 0, in `APPROVED_FULL` when it equals the eligible cap exactly, and in `APPROVED_PARTIAL` in every other case. An approved decision that computes to zero — a claim that survives no ceiling at all — therefore ends as a denial despite the officer's `APPROVE`.

Rounding is applied independently at step three and at step five and never to an intermediate sum; the two roundings are separate events and neither compounds into the other.

Adjudication is a four-link cascade. The first link writes the dispute, and because a decision may be taken from either awaiting state, each of the six movements `SUBMITTED → APPROVED_FULL`, `SUBMITTED → APPROVED_PARTIAL`, `SUBMITTED → DENIED`, `INFO_REQUESTED → APPROVED_FULL`, `INFO_REQUESTED → APPROVED_PARTIAL` and `INFO_REQUESTED → DENIED` is permitted and carries an identical side-effect set: the dispute's `status` becomes `APPROVED_FULL`, `APPROVED_PARTIAL` or `DENIED`, and `reason_ceiling_pct`, `goodwill_cents` and `approved_reversal_cents` are stored alongside `decided_at` from the logical clock, `decided_by_user_id` from the acting officer and the supplied `review_note`. The second link, caused by the first, adds `approved_reversal_cents` to the chargeback's `reversal_cents` and moves its dispute dimension: `OPEN → REVERSED` when the determination classified a full approval, `OPEN → PARTIALLY_REVERSED` when it classified a partial one, and `OPEN → UPHELD` on a denial. The chargeback's `outstanding_cents` and `settlement_status` are then re-derived by the settlement reconciliation described in §3.9; where the accumulated `reversal_cents` has reached or exceeded `gross_assessed_cents` the chargeback is driven to `WRITTEN_OFF` — `ASSESSED → WRITTEN_OFF` when nothing had been recovered, and `PARTIALLY_RECOVERED → WRITTEN_OFF` when cash or a deduction had already been applied — with `outstanding_cents` set to 0 in either case. The third link, caused by the second, makes every deduction holding an effective allocation on that chargeback recompute its three derived figures — chiefly `over_allocated_cents`, because a reversal shrinks the balance an already-effective allocation was netted against — exactly once each however many of its allocations point at this chargeback, and increments such a deduction's `version` only where the recomputation actually changes one of the three, so a deduction whose figures were already correct is left with its `version` and `updated_at` untouched. A deduction still in `DRAFT` is never reached by this link at all, since its allocations are merely pending. The fourth link, caused by the third, re-runs the exposure reconciliation of §3.9 over the supplier, updating `open_chargeback_cents`, `refund_due_cents` and `exposure_alert_flag`; the supplier's `version` and `updated_at` move on this link only where the determination approved a reversal greater than zero, so an adjudication that granted nothing — a `DENY`, or an `APPROVE` whose arithmetic computed to zero — leaves the supplier record exactly where it stood. Where a chargeback is written off while holding recovered cash, that cash becomes fully refundable and lands in `refund_due_cents` — the retailer has reversed the assessment but is still holding the supplier's money.

### 3.7. On-Time In-Full Measurement: Tolerance Windows and Fill Rate

Scorecards measure a supplier over a bounded period. A `ComplianceOfficer` opens one by naming the supplier's `vendor_id` — unresolvable values answer `ERR_VENDOR_NOT_FOUND` — together with a `period_code` label of 1 to 16 characters such as `2026-M08`, a `period_start` marking the inclusive start of the measurement period, and a `period_end`, strictly later than the start, marking its inclusive end. Three tolerance inputs are optional and default when omitted: `tolerance_early_days`, the number of days before the contractual deadline that still counts as on time, defaulting to 2 and constrained to 0 through 14; `tolerance_late_days`, the number of days after the deadline that still counts as on time, defaulting to 0 and likewise constrained to 0 through 14; and `fill_tolerance_pct`, the shortfall percentage tolerated before an order fails in full, defaulting to 2.00, constrained to 0.00 through 10.00 and carried to two decimals. The asymmetric defaults are deliberate: arriving two days early is acceptable, arriving one day late is not.

Periods of the same supplier may neither overlap nor touch, and must in fact clear each other by a full day. Both endpoints of a period are treated as occupied, and a mandatory clearance of one day is added on top, so a candidate period is refused with `ERR_SCORECARD_PERIOD_OVERLAP` when any existing scorecard of the same supplier in `OPEN`, `MEASURED` or `FINALIZED` satisfies both of these conditions at once: the candidate's `period_start` is at or before that scorecard's `period_end` plus one day, and that scorecard's `period_start` is at or before the candidate's `period_end` plus one day. Two periods whose endpoints are a single day apart therefore collide. The clearance rule constrains time and only time: `period_code` is never checked for uniqueness anywhere in the programme, so two well-separated periods of the same supplier may legitimately carry the same label. A new scorecard opens in `OPEN` with `measurement_variant` null, `measured_at` null, `finalized_at` null, `cap_required` false, and all measurement outputs — `measured_po_count`, `on_time_count`, `in_full_count`, `otif_count`, `on_time_pct`, `fill_rate_pct`, `otif_pct` and `composite_score` — at zero.

Measurement is requested by a `ComplianceOfficer` supplying a `measurement_variant` of either `ORDER_WEIGHTED` or `UNIT_WEIGHTED`. It is permitted from `OPEN` and from `MEASURED`, taking the scorecard `OPEN → MEASURED` on the first run and `MEASURED → MEASURED` on any re-run; a scorecard already `FINALIZED` is refused with `ERR_SCORECARD_ALREADY_FINALIZED`. The input set is the supplier's purchase orders whose `must_arrive_by` falls inside the closed interval running from `period_start` through `period_end` inclusive and whose `status` is `OPEN`, `PARTIALLY_RECEIVED` or `RECEIVED`. Note that membership is decided by the contractual deadline and not by when anything actually arrived, and that orders still open count against the supplier rather than being excused. Only shipments whose `status` is `RECEIVED` contribute their `received_units` and `arrived_at`; shipments still in transit are excluded entirely. The set is walked in a pinned order — `must_arrive_by` ascending, and where two orders share a deadline, `created_at` ascending — which does not change any aggregate but guarantees both variants agree on membership. The count of that set is `measured_po_count`, and a period containing no measurable orders is refused with `ERR_SCORECARD_NO_MEASURABLE_ORDERS`.

Each order in scope is then judged twice. It is on time when it has a `first_receipt_at` at all — an order never received is never on time — and that instant falls no earlier than `must_arrive_by` less `tolerance_early_days` days and no later than `must_arrive_by` plus `tolerance_late_days` days; both bounds are inclusive. It is in full when its fill percentage reaches the tolerated floor: the order's fill percentage is 100 multiplied by its `received_units`, divided by its `ordered_units`, rounded half up to two decimals, and the order passes when that value is at or above 100.00 less `fill_tolerance_pct` — at exactly the floor the order passes. An order is on time in full when it satisfies both tests. `on_time_count`, `in_full_count` and `otif_count` are the counts of orders passing the first test, the second test and both tests respectively. `on_time_pct` is 100 multiplied by `on_time_count`, divided by `measured_po_count`, rounded half up to two decimals, and `otif_pct` is the same expression built from `otif_count`.

The two variants differ in one place only — where the rounding sits relative to the aggregation of fill rate — and that single difference is enough to move the headline score. Under `ORDER_WEIGHTED` the rule is round-then-aggregate: each order's fill percentage is rounded to two decimals first, those already-rounded per-order values are summed, the sum is divided by `measured_po_count`, and the average is rounded half up to two decimals again. Every order carries equal weight regardless of its size, so a five-unit order missing one unit hurts as much as a five-thousand-unit order missing one thousand. Under `UNIT_WEIGHTED` the rule is aggregate-then-round: `received_units` is summed across the whole scope, `ordered_units` is summed across the whole scope, `fill_rate_pct` is 100 multiplied by the first sum and divided by the second, and only that final quotient is rounded half up to two decimals. Large orders dominate. For identical underlying data the two variants therefore produce different `fill_rate_pct` values and, through it, different `composite_score` values. The composite is 0.60 multiplied by `otif_pct` plus 0.40 multiplied by `fill_rate_pct`, rounded half up to two decimals — on-time-in-full performance carries three fifths of the weight and raw fill rate the remaining two fifths.

Re-measurement exists precisely so a reviewer can see both readings. A re-run recomputes every counter from scratch rather than adjusting the previous figures, and it increments the scorecard's `version` each time. Across a change of variant, `measured_po_count`, `on_time_count`, `in_full_count` and `otif_count` are invariant — they depend only on the scope and the tolerance windows — while `fill_rate_pct` and `composite_score` change. Each measurement also stamps `measured_at` from the logical clock, records the requested `measurement_variant`, and re-derives `cap_required` from the repeat-offender rule of §3.8 without yet creating any plan. Measurement writes nothing at all onto the supplier: neither `otif_score` nor `repeat_offender_flag` moves, and the supplier's `version` and `updated_at` stay where they were until the period is finalized.

Finalization locks the figures and publishes them into the relationship record. It requires the scorecard to be `MEASURED`: a scorecard still `OPEN` is refused with `ERR_SCORECARD_NOT_MEASURED`, and one already `FINALIZED` with `ERR_SCORECARD_ALREADY_FINALIZED`. The cascade has four links. The first moves the scorecard `MEASURED → FINALIZED` and stamps `finalized_at`. The second, caused by the first, re-derives the supplier's `otif_score` exactly as §3.1 defines it — from the `composite_score` of whichever of the supplier's finalized scorecards now ranks first by `period_end` descending, which is this scorecard unless the supplier already holds a finalized scorecard with a later `period_end` — and writes the supplier's `repeat_offender_flag` from a fresh evaluation of the repeat-offender rule taken at the finalization instant rather than from anything the measurement run left behind. One output is deliberately not re-evaluated: whether a plan is issued at all is read from the `cap_required` value stored on the scorecard at measurement, while the `escalation_level`, the `repeat_count` and the `repeat_offender_flag` all come from the fresh evaluation, so a window that moved between measurement and finalization can issue a plan whose level and repeat count differ from those the measurement run would have produced. The third, caused by the second, applies only when that stored `cap_required` is true: a corrective action plan is issued as described in §3.8, carrying `trigger_scorecard_id` pointing at this scorecard, and the supplier's `open_cap_count` is recomputed. The fourth, caused by the third, moves an `ACTIVE` supplier to `PROBATION` because a plan is now open against it. The supplier's `version` is incremented by one. `FINALIZED` is terminal for a scorecard: no further measurement is possible, which is why the variant choice must be settled before finalization.

### 3.8. Repeat Offenders: Corrective Action Plans and Buying Decisions

Whether a period's performance demands a corrective action plan is decided by a threshold rule evaluated at measurement time and then, as §3.7 pins, evaluated a second time over the window as it stands at finalization. The rule looks back over a half-open window that begins exactly 180 days before the scorecard's `period_end` and runs up to but does not include `period_end` itself. Inside that window it counts the supplier's chargebacks whose `assessed_at` falls in the window and whose `dispute_status` is anything other than `REVERSED`; a chargeback that was only partially reversed still counts against the supplier, and a fully reversed one does not count at all. Voided findings never appear in this count because they mint no chargeback. That count is the charged-violation count. Separately the rule counts how many of those same chargebacks pinned a requirement whose `severity_tier` is `TIER_1`, and how many of the supplier's corrective action plans reached `CLOSED_UNREMEDIATED` with a `closed_at` inside the same half-open window.

From those three counts the rule derives four outputs. The supplier is flagged as a repeat offender when the charged-violation count has reached 3 or more, **or** when at least one `TIER_1` chargeback sits in the window and the period's `composite_score` is strictly below 90.00. A plan is required — `cap_required` becomes true — only when the supplier is flagged as a repeat offender **and** the period's `composite_score` is strictly below 92.00, so a repeat offender that nonetheless scored 92.00 or better is flagged without being placed under a plan. The `escalation_level` is one plus the number of prior unremediated plans in the window, plus one more when the period's `composite_score` is strictly below 80.00, and the whole sum is capped at 3. The plan's `repeat_count` records the charged-violation count that justified it. All score comparisons are made on the two-decimal value and all three are strict: a score of exactly 92.00 does not trigger a plan, and a score of exactly 80.00 does not add the critical-performance level.

When finalization issues a plan, that plan opens in `ISSUED` with `vendor_id` naming the supplier, `trigger_scorecard_id` naming the scorecard that produced it, the computed `escalation_level` and `repeat_count`, `submission_round` 0, `buying_decision` `NONE`, `milestone_text` and `committed_completion_at` null, `issued_at` stamped from the logical clock, and `decided_at`, `escalated_at`, `closed_at`, `review_note`, `escalation_note` and `closure_note` all null. Its `predecessor_cap_id` is set by explicit selection rather than by any identifier arithmetic: it points at the supplier's corrective action plan in `CLOSED_UNREMEDIATED` ranked first by `closed_at` descending and, where two share a closure instant, by `created_at` descending, and it is null when the supplier has never failed to remediate. That self-referential chain is what makes a second failure demonstrably worse than a first. The deadline `due_at` is derived from `issued_at` plus a level-dependent allowance: 30 days at escalation level 1, 21 days at level 2 and 14 days at level 3. A supplier that has failed before therefore gets less time, not more.

The supplier responds by submitting its remediation commitment: a `milestone_text` of 1 to 1000 characters describing the committed milestones and a `committed_completion_at` instant by which the work will be done. Submission is available in the `Vendor` role against the supplier's own plan — the plan's `vendor_id` must equal the caller's `X-User-ID`, or the answer is `ERR_VENDOR_SCOPE_VIOLATION` — and requires the plan to be in `ISSUED` or `REJECTED`, refused otherwise with `ERR_CAP_NOT_AWAITING_SUBMISSION`. It requires `submission_round` to be strictly less than 2, refused otherwise with `ERR_CAP_SUBMISSION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, and it requires `committed_completion_at` to be no later than `due_at`, refused otherwise with `ERR_CAP_COMMITMENT_PAST_DUE`. A submission takes the plan `ISSUED → VENDOR_SUBMITTED` on the first attempt or `REJECTED → VENDOR_SUBMITTED` on the rework attempt, stores the supplied `milestone_text` and `committed_completion_at`, raises `submission_round` by one and increments the plan's `version`.

A `ComplianceOfficer` then rules on the submission with a `decision` of `ACCEPT` or `REJECT` and a `review_note` of 1 to 1000 characters, permitted only while the plan is `VENDOR_SUBMITTED` and refused otherwise with `ERR_CAP_NOT_DECIDABLE`. An acceptance takes the plan `VENDOR_SUBMITTED → ACCEPTED`; a rejection takes it `VENDOR_SUBMITTED → REJECTED`. Either way `decided_at` and `review_note` are stamped and `version` increments. The rework loop `VENDOR_SUBMITTED → REJECTED → VENDOR_SUBMITTED` closes once: the submission bound of two means a third submission is refused with `ERR_CAP_SUBMISSION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, leaving a twice-rejected plan with escalation as its only forward path.

Escalation is the buying organisation's move and belongs exclusively to the `CategoryBuyer`. It requires the plan to be `REJECTED`, refused otherwise with `ERR_CAP_NOT_ESCALATABLE`, and takes a `buying_decision` together with an `escalation_note` of 1 to 1000 characters recording the rationale. A `buying_decision` of `RANGE_REVIEW` is permitted at any escalation level and leaves the supplier's `status` untouched — the assortment is reconsidered but purchasing continues. A `buying_decision` of `SOURCING_HOLD` requires the plan's `escalation_level` to be at least 2, and additionally a `buying_decision` of `NONE` is never accepted at escalation; either failing is refused with `ERR_CAP_ESCALATION_LEVEL_TOO_LOW`. Escalation takes the plan `REJECTED → ESCALATED`, records the `buying_decision` and `escalation_note`, stamps `escalated_at`, increments the plan's `version`, and when the decision is `SOURCING_HOLD` moves the supplier `PROBATION → SOURCING_HOLD`, from which point new purchase orders against that supplier are refused with `ERR_VENDOR_SOURCING_HOLD`. That posture movement is the only reason escalation ever writes to the supplier record, and it carries a `version` increment with it. A `SOURCING_HOLD` escalation against a supplier that does not stand on `PROBATION` at that instant — one already held, for instance — is accepted rather than refused and simply leaves the posture it found; and in that case, as on every `RANGE_REVIEW` escalation, the supplier's derived figures are re-reconciled, none of them changes, and its `version` and `updated_at` are therefore left alone.

Closure is the `ComplianceOfficer`'s final act on a plan and takes an `outcome` of either `SUCCESS` or `FAILED` together with a `closure_note` of 1 to 1000 characters. It is permitted only from `ACCEPTED` or `ESCALATED`, refused otherwise with `ERR_CAP_NOT_CLOSABLE`. A `SUCCESS` outcome takes the plan `ACCEPTED → CLOSED_SUCCESS` or `ESCALATED → CLOSED_SUCCESS`, stamps `closed_at` and `closure_note`, recomputes the supplier's `open_cap_count`, and restores sourcing posture: a plan that had been escalated with a `SOURCING_HOLD` buying decision returns the supplier `SOURCING_HOLD → PROBATION` as a compensating restoration, and an accepted plan that was the supplier's only open plan returns the supplier `PROBATION → ACTIVE` with `open_cap_count` falling to 0. No other successful closure restores anything: a plan escalated with a `RANGE_REVIEW` buying decision leaves the supplier's `status` exactly as it found it even when it was the last open plan, and only a plan closed out of `ACCEPTED` can drive the `PROBATION → ACTIVE` restoration. A `FAILED` outcome takes the plan `ACCEPTED → CLOSED_UNREMEDIATED` or `ESCALATED → CLOSED_UNREMEDIATED` with the same stamping and the same `open_cap_count` recomputation, but the supplier's `status` is explicitly not restored — it remains on `PROBATION` or on `SOURCING_HOLD` as it stood. In addition, a plan closed unremediated becomes eligible as the `predecessor_cap_id` of the supplier's next plan, which raises that plan's `escalation_level` and shortens its `due_at`. Both closure branches increment the plan's `version`, and both closed states are terminal. Because a closure always removes the plan from the open set, both branches also increment the supplier's `version` by one — the `open_cap_count` recomputation alone guarantees it — whether or not sourcing posture was restored.

### 3.9. Deductions Against Payables, Cash Application and Exposure Reconciliation

Recovery has two channels, and both write rows into the same junction: a recovery allocation always names the `chargeback_id` it recovers, and its `source_type` records which channel produced it. Deduction-sourced allocations are drafted under a deduction document and become effective only when that document is posted; cash-sourced allocations are created directly against a chargeback from a vendor remittance and are effective immediately. A single deduction may spread across many chargebacks and a single chargeback may be recovered from many sources, which is why the junction exists as a first-class resource rather than as a column.

An `APAccountant` opens a deduction against a supplier's payable by naming the supplier's `vendor_id` and the `invoice_reference` of 1 to 32 characters that the deduction will be netted against, together with the `expected_vendor_version` demanded by §2.9. The deduction opens in `DRAFT` with `amount_cents` 0, `allocation_count` 0, `over_allocated_cents` 0, and `posted_at`, `posted_by_user_id`, `reversed_at` and `reversal_reason` all null; creating it increments the supplier's `version` by one, which is exactly why a client drafting a second deduction must re-read the supplier first.

Allocations are earmarked against a draft. The accountant supplies the `chargeback_id` to be recovered and an `allocated_cents` amount of at least 1. The `chargeback_id` must resolve, and a chargeback belonging to a different supplier than the deduction's is likewise answered with `ERR_CHARGEBACK_NOT_FOUND`, since a deduction may only net its own supplier's chargebacks; both of those are settled at the body-reference rung and therefore ahead of the state check, so an allocation naming another supplier's chargeback against a deduction that is no longer a draft answers `ERR_CHARGEBACK_NOT_FOUND` and not `ERR_DEDUCTION_NOT_DRAFT`. The deduction must then still be `DRAFT`, refused otherwise with `ERR_DEDUCTION_NOT_DRAFT`. The amount may not exceed the target chargeback's `outstanding_cents`, with exact equality permitted and an excess refused as `ERR_ALLOCATION_EXCEEDS_OUTSTANDING`. A new allocation is stored with `source_type` `DEDUCTION`, `effective_status` `PENDING`, `remittance_reference` null, `applied_at` null and `applied_by_user_id` from the acting accountant, and it raises the deduction's `amount_cents` by the allocated amount and its `allocation_count` by one. A pending allocation contributes nothing to any chargeback's recovery; it is an intention, not a recovery.

Posting turns intentions into money. It requires the deduction to be `DRAFT` — a deduction already `POSTED` or `REVERSED` is refused with `ERR_DEDUCTION_ALREADY_POSTED` — and it requires at least one allocation, so an `allocation_count` of 0 is refused with `ERR_DEDUCTION_NO_ALLOCATIONS`. Every allocated chargeback must be free of a live dispute: each must have a `dispute_status` of `NONE`, `UPHELD` or `PARTIALLY_REVERSED`, and any allocated chargeback outside those three — one still `OPEN`, and equally one already fully `REVERSED` on the dispute dimension — freezes the whole document with `ERR_CHARGEBACK_DISPUTE_OPEN`. And the document may not net more than the supplier currently owes: the deduction's `amount_cents` must be no greater than the supplier's `open_chargeback_cents` at the instant of posting, with equality permitted and an excess refused as `ERR_DEDUCTION_EXCEEDS_EXPOSURE`. Posting is a four-link cascade. The first link moves the deduction `DRAFT → POSTED` and stamps `posted_at` and `posted_by_user_id`. The second link, caused by the first, flips every child allocation `PENDING → EFFECTIVE` and stamps each one's `applied_at` with the posting instant. The third link, caused by the second, makes each allocated chargeback recompute `recovered_cents` and `outstanding_cents`, set `deduction_posted` to true, and re-derive `settlement_status`. The fourth link, caused by the third, re-runs the exposure reconciliation over the supplier, lowering `open_chargeback_cents`, raising `recovered_lifetime_cents` and re-deriving `exposure_alert_flag`. The deduction's `version` and each affected chargeback's `version` are incremented by exactly one for the whole posting — the recomputation of the deduction's own three derived figures deliberately does not count as a second increment on it — and each chargeback takes its increment whether or not the recomputation moved one of its figures.

Reversal unwinds a posting completely. It requires the deduction to be `POSTED`, refused otherwise with `ERR_DEDUCTION_NOT_POSTED`, and takes a `reversal_reason` of at least one character on which no maximum length is imposed. The deduction moves `POSTED → REVERSED`, stamping `reversed_at` and recording the reason; every child allocation flips `EFFECTIVE → REVERSED`; the deduction's `over_allocated_cents` is reset to 0. Each affected chargeback then recomputes `recovered_cents` with the reversed allocations no longer counted, sets `deduction_posted` back to false when no effective deduction-sourced allocation remains on it, re-derives `outstanding_cents`, and re-derives `settlement_status` — which takes a chargeback `PARTIALLY_RECOVERED → ASSESSED` or `RECOVERED → ASSESSED` once its `recovered_cents` returns to 0, restoring `outstanding_cents` to `gross_assessed_cents` less `reversal_cents`. The supplier's `open_chargeback_cents` and `recovered_lifetime_cents` return to exactly their pre-posting values, with no drift, because both are recomputed from source rows rather than adjusted by a delta. The deduction takes exactly one `version` increment for the whole reversal, and every chargeback the document had targeted takes one of its own. `REVERSED` is terminal for a deduction; the compensating document is never un-reversed, and a fresh draft is required to try again.

Cash application is the second channel and bypasses payables entirely. An `APAccountant` applies a vendor remittance directly to one chargeback by supplying a `remittance_reference` identifying the remittance advice, of at least one character and with no maximum length imposed, an `amount_cents` of at least 1, and the `expected_chargeback_version` demanded by §2.9. The chargeback's `settlement_status` must not be `WRITTEN_OFF`, refused otherwise with `ERR_CHARGEBACK_WRITTEN_OFF`, and the amount may not exceed the chargeback's `outstanding_cents`: exact equality is accepted and drives the chargeback to full recovery, while one cent more is refused with `ERR_OVER_APPLICATION`. The application creates an allocation with `source_type` `CASH`, `deduction_id` null, `effective_status` `EFFECTIVE` and `applied_at` stamped from the logical clock at creation, storing the applied amount in the allocation's `allocated_cents` field and the supplied reference in `remittance_reference`. The chargeback's `recovered_cents` rises by the applied amount, `outstanding_cents` and `settlement_status` are re-derived, the chargeback's `version` increments, and the supplier's exposure figures are recomputed.

The settlement dimension of a chargeback follows from those recomputations rather than from any caller's instruction. Its net assessed balance is `gross_assessed_cents` less `reversal_cents`; its `recovered_cents` is the sum of `allocated_cents` over its allocations whose `effective_status` is `EFFECTIVE`, with pending allocations under a draft deduction and reversed allocations under a reversed deduction both contributing nothing; its `outstanding_cents` is the net assessed balance less `recovered_cents`, floored at 0 so it never reads negative; and its `deduction_posted` is true exactly when at least one of its allocations has `source_type` `DEDUCTION` and belongs to a deduction whose `status` is `POSTED`, a flag that is independent of the settlement dimension. The `settlement_status` is then chosen by the first matching branch of four, tested in this order: the chargeback is `WRITTEN_OFF` when the net assessed balance is zero or below; otherwise `RECOVERED` when `recovered_cents` has reached or exceeded the net assessed balance; otherwise `PARTIALLY_RECOVERED` when `recovered_cents` is above zero; otherwise `ASSESSED`. Because the second branch tests "reached or exceeded" rather than "exceeded", an exact-payoff cash application lands on `RECOVERED` and not on `PARTIALLY_RECOVERED`. The reachable movements on this dimension are `ASSESSED → PARTIALLY_RECOVERED`, `ASSESSED → RECOVERED`, `PARTIALLY_RECOVERED → RECOVERED`, `PARTIALLY_RECOVERED → ASSESSED` and `RECOVERED → ASSESSED` on a deduction reversal, and `ASSESSED → WRITTEN_OFF` or `PARTIALLY_RECOVERED → WRITTEN_OFF` when a dispute reversal extinguishes the assessment. `WRITTEN_OFF` is terminal on this dimension.

The deduction document maintains three derived figures of its own. Its `amount_cents` is the sum of `allocated_cents` over its allocations whose `effective_status` is anything other than `REVERSED`. Its `allocation_count` is simply the number of allocations it holds, reversed ones included. Its `over_allocated_cents` is the sum, over its allocations whose `effective_status` is `EFFECTIVE`, of the excess of each allocation's `allocated_cents` over the net assessed balance of the chargeback it targets, counting zero where there is no excess; it is recomputed whenever a dispute reversal shrinks a chargeback the document had already netted, which is how an accountant discovers that a posted deduction has become too large after the fact.

Supplier exposure is reconciled in full from source rows on every one of these money triggers — chargeback assessment, dispute adjudication, deduction posting, deduction reversal and cash application — and never by incremental delta. The reconciliation is not selective: it re-derives all seven of the supplier's derived fields together — the four figures below plus `open_violation_count`, `open_cap_count` and `otif_score` — and the same whole-record pass therefore also runs on the triggers that move only a non-money figure: violation filing and voiding, scorecard finalization, corrective-plan escalation and closure, and the mere drafting of a deduction. On those the money figures simply come out unchanged, which is why a supplier's `version` may advance without any of its cents moving. `open_chargeback_cents` is the sum, over the supplier's chargebacks whose `settlement_status` is `ASSESSED` or `PARTIALLY_RECOVERED` only, of `gross_assessed_cents` less `reversal_cents` less `recovered_cents`; chargebacks that are `RECOVERED` or `WRITTEN_OFF` are excluded from this figure entirely. `recovered_lifetime_cents` is the sum of `allocated_cents` over every allocation on any of the supplier's chargebacks whose `effective_status` is `EFFECTIVE`, with no filter on the chargeback's settlement state. `refund_due_cents` is the sum, over every one of the supplier's chargebacks with no settlement filter at all, of the excess of `recovered_cents` over `gross_assessed_cents` less `reversal_cents`, counting zero where there is no excess — so a written-off chargeback that still holds the supplier's cash contributes its whole recovered amount to the refund owed. Finally `exposure_alert_flag` is true exactly when `open_chargeback_cents` has reached 500,000 cents or more. Those four figures, read together with `otif_score`, `repeat_offender_flag`, `open_violation_count` and `open_cap_count`, are the whole of what a vendor relationship review needs, and all eight are visible on a single supplier read.
