# Kids Clothing Rent & Swap Subscription API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Children outgrow clothes faster than households can buy them, and the wardrobe that fitted in March is landfill by September. This service turns that problem into a subscription: a family subscribes to a plan, receives a **box** of house-owned garments sized to their child, wears them for a monthly cycle, swaps a few pieces mid-cycle if something does not work out, and ships the rest back to be graded, cleaned, repaired and re-circulated. When a garment is loved too much to give back, the household may buy it out of the box at a wear-adjusted price. When the household has its *own* outgrown garments, it sends them in as a **trade-in submission** and receives account credit that can be spent against any future charge.

Two pipelines therefore run through the same warehouse, the same grading bench and the same money ledger, and they must not be confused with each other. **Pipeline R — rental circulation** moves house-owned `GarmentUnit` rows out in a `Box`, back through a return shipment, across the grading bench, through cleaning or repair, and back into stock; ownership never leaves the service and money flows *from* the household (plan charge, protection fee, late fee, damage fee, buy-out price, lost-parcel liability). **Pipeline T — trade-in intake** moves member-owned garments *in* as `TradeInItem` rows, across the *same* grading bench under the *same* rubric, and converts them into `CreditLedgerEntry` credit; ownership transfers *to* the service and money flows *to* the household. The two pipelines share the condition rubric, the cleaning and repair routing, the whole shipping-label and shipment machinery, and the ordering rules of the ledger. They differ in who ends up owning the garment, in which direction the money moves, in the standing preconditions a member must satisfy, and in which subsequence of the platform's write gates each operation must clear.

This document specifies the complete HTTP contract for that platform: entity shapes, the twelve-gate write ladder with its sixty-eight numbered rungs, sixteen state machines (eight of them non-linear), sixty-one derivations, and the exact error identifier and status code every refusal carries. Where a boundary is inclusive, this document says so; where an order of operations is pinned, this document pins it; where two readings of a rule would produce different money, this document names the reading that is correct. Everything is mounted under the `/kidswear` prefix.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Actors, Identity Headers and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint — reads included — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role`. If either header is absent or present but empty, the request is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If `X-User-Role` carries a string that is not one of the eight role names below, the request is refused with `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE` (HTTP 401). These two checks are the first two rungs of the platform's write ladder and they run before anything else, including before the server attempts to parse or resolve any identifier in the URL.

The eight declared roles are `PlatformAdmin`, `Member`, `WarehouseOperator`, `GradingTechnician`, `CleaningVendor`, `LogisticsCoordinator`, `BillingAgent`, and `MerchandisePlanner`.

- A `PlatformAdmin` operates the platform itself: it authors plans, registers and activates members, schedules cycles, opens them, closes the swap window, applies the default disposition to an un-elected trade-in garment, denies pause requests, and drives the logical clock.
- A `Member` is the subscribing household. It owns its cycles, boxes, box items, swap requests, buy-out orders, trade-in submissions, pause requests and lost-shipment claims.
- A `WarehouseOperator` performs physical intake and outbound handling: building boxes, allocating units into slots, packing, receiving inbound parcels, and approving, rejecting and fulfilling swaps.
- A `GradingTechnician` applies the shared condition rubric to rental units and to trade-in garments alike, and may never confirm a grade it authored itself.
- A `CleaningVendor` executes the routed cleaning and repair work and reports pass, fail, or a rework start.
- A `LogisticsCoordinator` mints, voids, expires and reissues shipping labels; creates, departs, marks-lost and returns shipments; ships packed boxes; attaches inbound labels to trade-in submissions; and runs lost-shipment claims up to the approve/deny fork.
- A `BillingAgent` runs the money side: late assessment, cycle closing, the credit and charge ledgers, trade-in finalisation, buy-out payment, pause activation and claim settlement.
- A `MerchandisePlanner` owns the catalog and the stock: styles, unit acquisition, unit retirement, capsule waves, absorption of trade-ins into rental stock, grade confirmation, and scrapping.

Role identity binds to `X-User-ID` in exactly one of two ways. When `X-User-Role` is `Member`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Member` row; every ownership check on a member-scoped resource compares that resource's owning member — reached directly through its `member_id`, or indirectly through `cycle_id` to the cycle's owner, through `box_id` to the box's owner, or through `submission_id` to the submission's owner — against `X-User-ID`, and a mismatch is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). When `X-User-Role` is any of `PlatformAdmin`, `WarehouseOperator`, `GradingTechnician`, `CleaningVendor`, `LogisticsCoordinator`, `BillingAgent` or `MerchandisePlanner`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it where a transition says so — for example as `graded_by_user_id` on a `ConditionAssessment` submitted by a `GradingTechnician`, or as `confirmed_by_user_id` on the same row when a `MerchandisePlanner` confirms it — but never compares it to any entity's `id`.

If the acting role is a declared role but is not one of the roles listed for the endpoint in §4, the request is refused with `ERR_ROLE_FORBIDDEN` (HTTP 403). One further authorization rule is not about *which* role acts but about *which operator*: grade confirmation is segregated from grade authorship. The operator confirming a `ConditionAssessment` must present an `X-User-ID` different from the `graded_by_user_id` already stored on that assessment; presenting the same identifier is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Because a regrade rewrites `graded_by_user_id` with the regrading technician's identifier, the segregation rule always compares the confirmer against the *most recent* grader, not the original one.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and counts rows to skip from the front of the ordered result. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is a field-shape failure and is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 422).

Unless a specific narrative in §3 pins a different ordering for a *derivation* (the selector policies, the credit waterfall and the trade-in cap waterfall each pin their own orderings, and those orderings govern the computation, not the list response), every list endpoint returns its rows sorted by `created_at` ascending. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` — the number of rows matching the filters before `limit` and `offset` are applied — and `has_more`, which is true exactly when `offset` plus the number of returned rows is strictly less than `total_count`. Filter parameters named on each list endpoint in §4 are all optional; supplying several narrows the result by conjunction.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps, Versions and the Logical Clock

Every persisted resource carries a server-minted `id` that is a UUID string with no type prefix, and a `created_at` instant. All twenty-one entity types in this system also carry `updated_at` and an integer `version`: `version` starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row, and `updated_at` is refreshed to the logical clock on the same mutation. No entity in this system is exempt from `updated_at` or `version`.

All instants — `created_at`, `updated_at`, and every domain instant such as `start_at`, `swap_by_at`, `return_due_at`, `grace_ends_at`, `size_effective_at`, `issued_at`, `expires_at`, `shipped_at`, `delivered_at`, `quoted_at`, `quote_expires_at`, `granted_at`, `release_at`, `close_at`, `embargo_until`, `election_deadline_at`, `filed_at`, `deadline_at`, `acquired_at` and `end_at` — are rendered as ISO 8601 UTC with exactly three fractional digits and a trailing `Z`. An instant supplied by a client in a request body is compared semantically, as an instant, not as a string; the response, however, always renders the platform's own canonical three-fractional-digit form.

Time in this system is a **logical clock**, never wall-clock time. Before any client sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-05-01T00:00:00Z` — the platform default recorded as `DEFAULT_CLOCK_UTC` — and it never falls back to real time. An administrative test-driving capability sets the clock to a supplied `to_iso8601` instant and answers with the resulting `current_utc`; every window, expiry, deadline, grace and TTL evaluation made afterwards uses that instant. Every derived instant written by the platform (a label's `expires_at`, a quote's `quote_expires_at`, a credit's `granted_at`) is derived from the logical clock at the moment of the write.

A mutation's response body always reflects the state of the resource *after* the mutation has fully completed, including the incremented `version` and every field the transition's side effects computed. A client that reads a `version` from a mutation response may immediately use it as the `expected_…_version` of a following optimistically-guarded write.

### 2.5. The Twelve Write Gates and Their Total Order

Every write on this platform is screened by a fixed sequence of twelve **gates**. The gates form a total order, and they are evaluated strictly in ascending order. The **first** gate that fails decides the error the caller receives; no later gate is consulted once an earlier one has refused. The order is:

1. **G1_AUTH** — `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` are present, and `X-User-Role` names a declared role.
2. **G2_ROLE** — the acting role appears in the endpoint's authorized-role list, and for an entity-backed role the acting `X-User-ID` owns the addressed resource. Grade-confirmation segregation of duties is enforced here.
3. **G3_EXISTENCE** — every identifier in the path resolves, then every foreign key in the body resolves, validated left-to-right through the path and then in body-declaration order.
4. **G4_STANDING** — the owning member's standing permits the write: `CANCELLED` blocks every member-scoped write; `PENDING` blocks every circulation or intake write; `DELINQUENT` blocks allocation, swap creation, buy-out quoting, capsule allocation and trade-in label issuance, but deliberately does **not** block returns, grading, processing or payment.
5. **G5_PAUSE** — a member whose `status` is `PAUSED` may not perform Pipeline R circulation writes or Pipeline T intake writes; pause-request writes themselves are exempt, otherwise a paused member could never resume.
6. **G6_OCC** — the supplied `expected_…_version` matches the parent entity's current `version`.
7. **G7_STATE** — the addressed entity is in a declared from-state for the requested transition, on whichever status axis the transition targets.
8. **G8_WINDOW** — every temporal window holds: cycle swap, return and grace boundaries, label validity, capsule release, embargo and close instants, buy-out quote lifetime, trade-in election deadline, lost-claim filing window, and size-up cooling-off.
9. **G9_ENTITLEMENT** — period-scoped budgets hold: box slots, swap entitlements, capsule quota, pause days per period and lifetime, label reissue cap, regrade cap, rework cap and trade-in item-count bounds.
10. **G10_STOCK** — physical-stock feasibility holds: size compatibility on the ordered ladder, unit availability, exclusion of retirement-eligible and `RETIRE`-graded stock, capsule membership of the requested style.
11. **G11_MONEY** — monetary feasibility holds: credit sufficiency and unexpired credit, charge settlement state, the buy-out price floor, waiver availability and the delinquency rule on cycle closing.
12. **G12_CONSERVATION** — the write would not break a declared conservation invariant.

Each write endpoint in §4 is screened only by the **subsequence** of these gates that is relevant to it, but that subsequence is always evaluated in the same ascending order. Two endpoints that share a gate share the numbered rungs that hang from it. Read endpoints are screened by `G1_AUTH` and `G2_ROLE`, plus `G3_EXISTENCE` whenever they address a resource by identifier in the path.

Four consequences of this ordering are load-bearing and are called out here because they are the places where a plausible alternative ordering would return a different error:

- **G6_OCC precedes G7_STATE.** A buy-out quote request carrying a stale `expected_box_item_version` against a `BoxItem` whose `settlement_status` is already `BOUGHT_OUT` is answered `ERR_STALE_BOX_ITEM_VERSION` (HTTP 412), *not* `ERR_BOX_ITEM_ALREADY_SETTLED` (HTTP 409). The stale-version refusal is reachable on an already-settled item only because the concurrency gate runs first.
- **G4_STANDING precedes G5_PAUSE.** A `CANCELLED` member who also holds an `ACTIVE` pause receives `ERR_MEMBER_CANCELLED`, never `ERR_MEMBER_PAUSED`.
- **G8_WINDOW precedes G9_ENTITLEMENT.** A swap submitted after the cycle's `swap_by_at` by a member who has *also* exhausted the cycle's swap entitlement receives `ERR_SWAP_WINDOW_CLOSED`, not `ERR_SWAP_ENTITLEMENT_EXHAUSTED`.
- **G10_STOCK precedes G11_MONEY.** A buy-out quote requested for a `RETIRE`-graded unit whose price would also fall below the floor receives `ERR_UNIT_RETIRE_GRADED`, not `ERR_BUYOUT_BELOW_FLOOR`.

Request-shape validation — a missing required body field, a null where a value is required, a wrong JSON type, or a numeric value outside a declared bound — is performed as part of the authorization stage, before the ladder proper reaches existence checks, and is reported as `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 422) unless §2.9 routes the particular field to `ERR_INVALID_ENUM`.

### 2.6. The Sixty-Eight Numbered Rungs

The gates above are subdivided into sixty-eight numbered **rungs**, and the rung number *is* the evaluation order. Rungs 1 through 68 are evaluated in ascending numeric order, and because every rung belongs to exactly one gate and the rung numbering follows the gate ordering, evaluating the rungs in numeric order and evaluating the gates in gate order are the same thing. A rung whose `applies_to` scope does not cover the endpoint under evaluation is skipped without effect.

Each rung below is tagged with the pipeline or pipelines that cite it. **R** means the rental-circulation pipeline, **T** means the trade-in intake pipeline, and **R+T** means the rung is shared — the *same numbered rung*, with the same error identifier, governs both pipelines. Twenty-five of the sixty-eight rungs are shared; this is what it means for the two pipelines to run over one ladder.

**G1_AUTH — rungs 1 to 2 (both R+T, both applying to every endpoint):**

1. **[R+T]** `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` are both present and non-empty. Otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. **[R+T]** `X-User-Role` names one of the eight declared roles. Otherwise `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE` (401).

**G2_ROLE — rungs 3 to 5 (all R+T):**

3. **[R+T]** The acting role appears in the endpoint's declared role list. Otherwise `ERR_ROLE_FORBIDDEN` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
4. **[R+T]** For an entity-backed role, `X-User-ID` equals the `id` of the `Member` that owns the addressed resource, reached directly or through `member_id`, through `cycle_id` to the cycle's owner, through `box_id` to the box's owner, or through `submission_id` to the submission's owner. Otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). Applies to every member-scoped endpoint.
5. **[R+T]** On grade confirmation, the acting `X-User-ID` differs from the assessment's `graded_by_user_id`. Otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). Applies to `POST /kidswear/condition-assessments/{assessment_id}/confirm` only.

**G3_EXISTENCE — rungs 6 to 13:**

6. **[R+T]** The `Member` identifier in the path or body resolves. Otherwise `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND` (404). Applies to every endpoint carrying a member identifier.
7. **[R]** The `Cycle`, `Box` or `BoxItem` identifier in the path resolves, validated left-to-right. Otherwise `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND` (404). Applies to all cycle, box and box-item endpoints.
8. **[R+T]** The `GarmentStyle` or `GarmentUnit` identifier in the path or body resolves. Otherwise `ERR_STYLE_NOT_FOUND` (404). Applies to garment-style, garment-unit, box allocation and trade-in absorption endpoints.
9. **[R+T]** The `Shipment` or `ShippingLabel` identifier in the path or body resolves. Otherwise `ERR_SHIPMENT_NOT_FOUND` (404). Applies to shipment, label, box-ship and trade-in issue-label endpoints.
10. **[T]** The `TradeInSubmission` or `TradeInItem` identifier in the path resolves. Otherwise `ERR_TRADE_IN_NOT_FOUND` (404). Applies to trade-in endpoints.
11. **[R+T]** The `ConditionAssessment` or `ProcessingJob` identifier in the path resolves. Otherwise `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FOUND` (404). Applies to condition-assessment and processing-job endpoints.
12. **[R]** The `Capsule`, `CapsuleAllocation`, `BuyOutOrder`, `PauseRequest`, `LostShipmentClaim`, `CreditLedgerEntry` or `ChargeLedgerEntry` identifier in the path resolves. Otherwise `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (404). Applies to capsule, allocation, buy-out, pause, claim and ledger endpoints.
13. **[R+T]** Every identifier inside an array body field — `return_box_item_ids` and `requested_style_ids` — resolves and belongs to the addressed member's own resources. Otherwise `ERR_REFERENCE_NOT_OWNED` (422). Applies to `POST /kidswear/cycles/{cycle_id}/swap-requests`.

**G4_STANDING — rungs 14 to 16 (all R+T):**

14. **[R+T]** The owning member's `status` is not `CANCELLED`. Otherwise `ERR_MEMBER_CANCELLED` (409). Applies to every member-scoped write.
15. **[R+T]** The owning member's `status` is not `PENDING`. Otherwise `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_ACTIVATED` (409). Applies to cycle, box, swap, buy-out, capsule and trade-in write endpoints.
16. **[R+T]** The owning member's `status` is not `DELINQUENT`. Otherwise `ERR_MEMBER_DELINQUENT` (409). Applies to exactly these five endpoints and no others: `POST /kidswear/boxes/{box_id}/allocate`, `POST /kidswear/cycles/{cycle_id}/swap-requests`, `POST /kidswear/box-items/{box_item_id}/buy-out`, `POST /kidswear/capsules/{capsule_id}/allocations`, and `POST /kidswear/trade-in-submissions/{submission_id}/issue-label`. Returns, grading, processing and payment remain permitted to a delinquent member — that is deliberate, because a delinquent member must still be able to send garments back and pay off the balance.

**G5_PAUSE — rungs 17 to 18:**

17. **[R]** The member's `status` is not `PAUSED` for a Pipeline R circulation write — box allocation, packing, shipping, swap creation and submission, buy-out quoting and acceptance, and capsule allocation. Otherwise `ERR_MEMBER_PAUSED` (409).
18. **[T]** The member's `status` is not `PAUSED` for a Pipeline T intake write. Otherwise `ERR_INTAKE_BLOCKED_WHILE_PAUSED` (409). Applies to `POST /kidswear/trade-in-submissions`, `POST /kidswear/trade-in-submissions/{submission_id}/items` and `POST /kidswear/trade-in-submissions/{submission_id}/issue-label`. Note that the two pause rungs carry *different* error identifiers: a paused household that tries to circulate garments and a paused household that tries to send garments in are told apart.

**G6_OCC — rungs 19 to 21:**

19. **[R]** `expected_cycle_version` equals the addressed `Cycle`'s current `version`. Otherwise `ERR_STALE_CYCLE_VERSION` (412). Applies to `POST /kidswear/cycles/{cycle_id}/swap-requests`.
20. **[R]** `expected_box_item_version` equals the addressed `BoxItem`'s current `version`. Otherwise `ERR_STALE_BOX_ITEM_VERSION` (412). Applies to `POST /kidswear/box-items/{box_item_id}/buy-out`. Evaluated before any settlement-state check, so a stale writer addressing an already `BOUGHT_OUT` item receives 412 here.
21. **[R+T]** `expected_member_version` equals the addressed `Member`'s current `version`. Otherwise `ERR_STALE_MEMBER_VERSION` (412). Applies to `POST /kidswear/members/{member_id}/credit-applications`.

**G7_STATE — rungs 22 to 32:**

22. **[R]** `Cycle.status` is a declared from-state for the requested cycle transition. Otherwise `ERR_CYCLE_INVALID_STATE` (409). Applies to cycle transition endpoints.
23. **[R]** `Cycle.status` is `OPEN` for swap creation, swap submission and box allocation. Otherwise `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_OPEN` (409). Applies to `POST /kidswear/cycles/{cycle_id}/swap-requests`, `POST /kidswear/swap-requests/{swap_request_id}/submit` and `POST /kidswear/boxes/{box_id}/allocate`.
24. **[R]** `Box.status` is a declared from-state for the requested box transition. Otherwise `ERR_BOX_INVALID_STATE` (409). Applies to box transition endpoints.
25. **[R]** `BoxItem.fulfillment_status` is a declared from-state for the requested fulfillment-axis transition. Otherwise `ERR_BOX_ITEM_INVALID_FULFILLMENT_STATE` (409). Applies to box-item transition endpoints.
26. **[R]** `BoxItem.settlement_status` is `OPEN` for buy-out quoting and for damage settlement. Otherwise `ERR_BOX_ITEM_ALREADY_SETTLED` (409). Applies to `POST /kidswear/box-items/{box_item_id}/buy-out` and `POST /kidswear/condition-assessments/{assessment_id}/confirm`.
27. **[R]** `GarmentUnit.state` is a declared from-state for the requested circulation transition. Otherwise `ERR_UNIT_INVALID_STATE` (409). Applies to allocation, shipping, return-start, receiving, retirement and processing endpoints.
28. **[R+T]** `Shipment.status` is a declared from-state for the requested shipment transition. Otherwise `ERR_SHIPMENT_INVALID_STATE` (409). Applies to shipment transition endpoints.
29. **[R+T]** `ShippingLabel.status` is a declared from-state for the requested label transition. Otherwise `ERR_LABEL_INVALID_STATE` (409). Applies to label transition endpoints and to departure and box shipping. This one rung governs `OUTBOUND`, `RETURN`, `TRADE_IN_INBOUND` and `REJECT_RETURN` labels alike.
30. **[R+T]** `ConditionAssessment.status` and `ProcessingJob.status` are declared from-states for the requested transition. Otherwise `ERR_ASSESSMENT_INVALID_STATE` (409). Applies to condition-assessment and processing-job transition endpoints, identically for `RENTAL` and `TRADE_IN` assessments.
31. **[T]** `TradeInSubmission.status`, and `TradeInItem.grading_status` or `TradeInItem.disposition_status` on whichever axis the transition addresses, are declared from-states. Otherwise `ERR_TRADE_IN_INVALID_STATE` (409). Applies to trade-in transition endpoints.
32. **[R]** `Member.status`, `Capsule.status`, `CapsuleAllocation.status`, `BuyOutOrder.status`, `PauseRequest.status` and `LostShipmentClaim.status` are declared from-states for the requested transition. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409). Applies to member lifecycle (activation, cancellation), capsule, allocation, buy-out, pause and claim transition endpoints.

**G8_WINDOW — rungs 33 to 43:**

33. **[R]** A swap may be created or submitted only while the logical clock is on or before the cycle's `swap_by_at`; the boundary is **inclusive**, so a swap submitted at exactly `swap_by_at` is accepted. Otherwise `ERR_SWAP_WINDOW_CLOSED` (409). Applies to `POST /kidswear/cycles/{cycle_id}/swap-requests` and `POST /kidswear/swap-requests/{swap_request_id}/submit`.
34. **[R]** A cycle may be opened only when the logical clock is at or after the cycle's `start_at`. Otherwise `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_STARTED` (409). Applies to `POST /kidswear/cycles/{cycle_id}/open`.
35. **[R]** The swap window may be closed only when the logical clock is strictly after `swap_by_at`. Otherwise `ERR_SWAP_WINDOW_STILL_OPEN` (409). Applies to `POST /kidswear/cycles/{cycle_id}/close-swap-window`.
36. **[R]** Lateness accrues only when the logical clock is **strictly after** the cycle's `grace_ends_at`; the boundary is **exclusive**, and this is deliberately the opposite inclusivity from rung 33. Assessment attempted at or before `grace_ends_at` on the transition into `GRACE` is refused with `ERR_GRACE_NOT_EXPIRED` (409). Applies to `POST /kidswear/cycles/{cycle_id}/assess-late`.
37. **[R+T]** A shipping label may authorise a departure only while the logical clock is on or before its `expires_at`; the boundary is **inclusive**. Otherwise `ERR_LABEL_EXPIRED` (409). Applies to `POST /kidswear/shipments/{shipment_id}/depart`, `POST /kidswear/boxes/{box_id}/ship` and `POST /kidswear/box-items/{box_item_id}/start-return`. Shared by both pipelines.
38. **[R]** A capsule box item may not be swapped or bought out while the logical clock is strictly before that item's `embargo_until`. Otherwise `ERR_CAPSULE_EMBARGOED` (409). Applies to `POST /kidswear/cycles/{cycle_id}/swap-requests` and `POST /kidswear/box-items/{box_item_id}/buy-out`.
39. **[R]** A capsule allocation is permitted only when the logical clock is at or after the capsule's `release_at` and strictly before its `close_at`. Otherwise `ERR_CAPSULE_WINDOW_CLOSED` (409). Applies to `POST /kidswear/capsules/{capsule_id}/allocations`.
40. **[R]** A buy-out quote may be accepted only while the logical clock is strictly before `quote_expires_at`. Otherwise `ERR_BUYOUT_QUOTE_EXPIRED` (409). Applies to `POST /kidswear/buy-out-orders/{buy_out_order_id}/accept`.
41. **[T]** A disposition election is accepted only while the logical clock is on or before the submission's `election_deadline_at`; the boundary is **inclusive**. Otherwise `ERR_ELECTION_DEADLINE_PASSED` (409). Applies to `POST /kidswear/trade-in-items/{trade_in_item_id}/elect-disposition`.
42. **[R+T]** A lost-shipment claim may be filed only while the logical clock is on or before the shipment's `shipped_at` plus ten days; when `shipped_at` is null — the never-scanned loss — the same ten-day window is measured from the shipment's `created_at` instead. Otherwise `ERR_CLAIM_WINDOW_CLOSED` (409). Applies to `POST /kidswear/lost-shipment-claims`. Shared by both pipelines.
43. **[R]** A size-up event requires at least thirty days to have elapsed since the member's `size_effective_at`. Otherwise `ERR_SIZE_UP_TOO_SOON` (409). Applies to `POST /kidswear/members/{member_id}/size-up`.

**G9_ENTITLEMENT — rungs 44 to 52:**

44. **[R]** Allocating a unit must not push the box's occupied slots above its `slot_capacity`. Otherwise `ERR_BOX_SLOTS_EXHAUSTED` (409). Applies to `POST /kidswear/boxes/{box_id}/allocate`.

Rung 68 is different: it is an ordinary reachable check, and the overlap conditions it enforces are described in §3.

### 2.7. Money, Precision and the Pinned Order of Operations

All monetary amounts on this platform are integer **cents**. Wherever a derivation produces a fractional cent, the result is rounded **half up** to the nearest whole cent — a value exactly halfway between two cents rounds away from zero, to the larger cent. Percentage and multiplier inputs are exact decimal fractions; the effective discount fraction is carried to four decimal places before it is used.

Two rounding disciplines are pinned and must not be traded for each other:

- **Round per item, then sum.** Whenever a total is an aggregate of per-row amounts — a cycle's `late_fee_cents` over its box items, a claim's `assessed_value_cents` over its lost units, a submission's `uncapped_total_cents` over its graded items — each row's amount is rounded to the cent *first*, and the rounded row amounts are then added. Summing raw fractional products and rounding once at the end is forbidden and produces a different total.
- **Round at the pinned steps only.** The cycle invoice rounds at exactly two points, described below; it does not round at intermediate additions.

The invoice for a cycle is assembled in a **pinned four-step order**, and this ordering is the single most consequential arithmetic decision in the system:

1. **Proration.** The plan's `monthly_fee_cents` is reduced by the cycle's `proration_credit_cents`, the amount that an overlapping pause removed.
2. **Discount.** The prorated amount is multiplied by one minus the effective discount fraction and rounded half up to the cent, giving `plan_charge_cents`. The same effective discount fraction is applied to the flat protection fee of 900 cents, rounded half up separately, giving `protection_fee_cents`. The `discount_cents` field records the money the discount removed across the plan charge and the protection fee together.
3. **Credit application.** The discounted charges, plus `late_fee_cents` and `damage_fee_cents`, are reduced by `credit_applied_cents` — the banked credit consumed against this cycle's charges.
4. **Surcharge.** A surcharge of 8.75 percent is computed on the **post-credit** taxable base and rounded half up to the cent, giving `surcharge_cents`. The final `invoice_total_cents` is the post-credit base, floored at zero so it can never be negative, plus the surcharge.

The alternative reading — computing the surcharge on the pre-credit base and *then* subtracting credit — is explicitly **forbidden**. It would produce a strictly larger invoice for any member holding credit, and the divergence between the two readings is exactly why the order is pinned. Likewise, applying the volume-discount tier to a total that has already been discounted is forbidden: the tier is selected from the *undiscounted* gross and applied once.

Percentage constants used across the platform, with their literal values, are: the surcharge rate `SURCHARGE_RATE` of 0.0875; the first volume-discount tier `VOLUME_DISCOUNT_TIER_1_PCT` of 0.05, which engages when the undiscounted gross reaches `VOLUME_DISCOUNT_TIER_1_MIN_CENTS` of 12000 cents; the second tier `VOLUME_DISCOUNT_TIER_2_PCT` of 0.10, which engages at `VOLUME_DISCOUNT_TIER_2_MIN_CENTS` of 20000 cents; the buy-out late surcharge `BUYOUT_LATE_SURCHARGE_PCT` of 0.10; the retire-grade damage percentage `DAMAGE_FEE_RETIRE_PCT_OF_RETAIL` of 0.55; and the lost-parcel assessed-value percentage `LOST_ASSESSED_VALUE_PCT_OF_RETAIL`, also 0.55. The platform's rounding convention is fixed by `MONEY_ROUNDING`, which is "round_half_up to 1 cent" everywhere.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Three write endpoints are guarded optimistically against a parent entity whose state they depend on. In each case the guard field is a required, non-optional input; omitting it is a field-shape failure reported as `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 422), and supplying a value that does not equal the parent's current `version` is a stale-write refusal.

- **Creating a swap request against a cycle.** A `POST /kidswear/cycles/{cycle_id}/swap-requests` request must carry `expected_cycle_version` holding the client's last observed `version` of the target `Cycle`. If the cycle's `version` has advanced since the client read it, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CYCLE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A prior swap creation legitimately consumes part of the cycle's period-scoped swap entitlement, so a writer holding a stale view of the cycle would size its partial swap against an entitlement that no longer exists. A successful create increments the cycle's `version` by 1.
- **Minting a buy-out quote against a box item.** A `POST /kidswear/box-items/{box_item_id}/buy-out` request must carry `expected_box_item_version` holding the client's last observed `version` of the target `BoxItem`. A mismatch with the item's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_BOX_ITEM_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The quoted price is derived from the item's lateness and from its unit's grade and wear count, all of which a version bump may have changed, so a stale quote would price a state of the world that no longer exists. Because this check sits at gate G6 and the settlement-state check sits at gate G7, a stale request against an already `BOUGHT_OUT` item is answered with the 412, not the 409. A successful quote increments the box item's `version` by 1.
- **Applying credit on a member.** A `POST /kidswear/members/{member_id}/credit-applications` request must carry `expected_member_version` holding the client's last observed `version` of the target `Member`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_MEMBER_VERSION` (HTTP 412), because a prior application consumes credit from the same ledger and a stale writer would compute its application against a balance that has already been spent. A successful application increments the member's `version` by 1.

No other endpoint on the platform accepts a version field, and none should be invented. Endpoints not named above take no `expected_…_version` input at all.

### 2.9. Field-Shape Validation and Enum Routing

A body field that is required and missing, is null where a value is required, carries the wrong JSON type, or falls outside a declared numeric bound is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 422). This is the **default route for every field-shape failure**, and it is the correct answer unless the field appears in the closed list below.

A value outside a declared member set routes to `ERR_INVALID_ENUM` (HTTP 422) for exactly these fields and **no others**: a plan's `tier`, `proration_basis` and `late_fee_mode`; a member's `child_size_code`; a garment style's `brand_tier`, `size_code`, `season` and `demand_band`; a shipment's `direction`; a shipping label's `direction`; a condition assessment's `pipeline` and `grade`; a processing job's `post_repair_grade`; a trade-in item's `declared_brand_tier` and `declared_size_code`; the `disposition` body field of a disposition election; the `quote_variant` body field of a buy-out quote request; a capsule's `season`; the `reason` supplied to unit retirement, which is a `retired_reason` value; a swap request's `rejection_reason`; and a pause request's `denial_reason`. Every other field with a constrained value set — including `charge_type` on an operator-raised charge, `status` filters on list endpoints, and every boolean or integer bound — routes to `ERR_VALIDATION` instead. There is no other field that routes to `ERR_INVALID_ENUM`.

Two uniqueness constraints are enforced at creation and carry their own codes. A member whose `email` duplicates an existing member's is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_EMAIL` (HTTP 409), and a garment style whose `sku` duplicates an existing style's is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_SKU` (HTTP 409).

### 2.10. Conservation Invariants

Four quantities are conserved and are re-checked after every write that could disturb them.

- **Physical garments.** For each `GarmentStyle`, its `units_ever_acquired` equals the total number of that style's units counted across all eight circulation states: `IN_STOCK`, `RESERVED`, `OUT`, `INBOUND`, `PROCESSING`, `LOST_IN_TRANSIT`, `RETIRED` and `SOLD`. A garment never disappears; it only changes state. Re-checked after every `GarmentUnit` transition.
- **Box slot entitlements.** For each `Box`, its `slot_capacity` equals its `slots_free` plus the number of its items whose `fulfillment_status` is one of `ALLOCATED`, `SHIPPED`, `WITH_MEMBER` or `RETURNING`, plus the number of its items whose `settlement_status` is `BOUGHT_OUT`. Re-checked after every box-item transition on either axis.
- **Monetary credit.** For each `Member`, the sum of `amount_cents` over every one of that member's credit entries equals the sum of `remaining_cents` over entries whose `status` is `AVAILABLE` or `PARTIALLY_APPLIED`, plus the sum of `credit_applied_cents` over all of that member's charges, plus the sum of `remaining_cents` over entries whose `status` is `EXPIRED` or `REVOKED`. Credit is neither created nor destroyed by an application; it changes bucket. Re-checked after every credit grant, application, expiry or revocation.
- **Period-scoped swap entitlements.** For each `Cycle`, the plan's `swaps_per_cycle` equals the cycle's `swaps_used`, plus the number of that cycle's swap requests whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `APPROVED`, plus the swaps still remaining. Re-checked after every swap-request transition.

### 2.11. Two Pipelines Over One Ladder

The design principle that shapes this whole specification is that Pipeline R and Pipeline T are structurally different journeys that nevertheless run over one set of numbered rungs. The grading rubric and its grade ladder are identical for both. The cleaning and repair routing table is identical for both. The label and shipment machinery — minting, departing, receiving, marking lost, voiding, expiring, reissuing — is identical for both, and rungs 29, 37, 42 and 50 govern the labels of all four directions. The money ordering rungs 58 through 60 are identical for both, which is precisely how credit earned by sending garments *in* becomes usable against charges raised by taking garments *out*.

Where they diverge, the divergence is explicit: ownership transfers to the service only in Pipeline T (and only at absorption does a trade-in garment become house stock); credit flows to the member only in Pipeline T while every other money movement in Pipeline R flows from the member; the pause gate carries a different error identifier on each side (rung 17 versus rung 18); and each endpoint names its own gate subsequence. When reading §3, treat any statement about grading, cleaning, labels or the ledger as applying to both pipelines unless the text names a pipeline explicitly.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Households, Plans and the Size Ladder

Commercial terms live on a **plan**, and a plan is authored by a `PlatformAdmin`. A plan carries a `tier`, which is one of `STARTER`, `CORE`, `PLUS` or `CAPSULE_PLUS`; a `slots_per_box` count of at least 1 and at most 8, which fixes how many garments a box carries; a `swaps_per_cycle` entitlement of at least 0 and at most 6; and a `monthly_fee_cents` of at least 0, the base plan charge before proration, discount, credit and surcharge. Four further switches shape the plan's behaviour and each has a default when the field is omitted at creation: `protection_available` defaults to true and says whether the damage-waiver add-on may be enrolled at all; `proration_basis` defaults to `THIRTY_DAY` and selects between the two pause-proration variants, the other being `ACTUAL_DAYS`; `late_fee_mode` defaults to `PER_DAY` and selects between the two late-fee variants, the other being `FLAT_TIER`; and `capsule_quota_per_wave` defaults to 2 and is bounded between 0 and 6. A `discount_pct` fraction defaults to 0 and may not exceed 0.50; it discounts the plan charge *and*, by a deliberate coupling described in §3.10, the net protection fee as well. Every plan is born with `status` `ACTIVE` and may later be moved to `RETIRED`. Only `discount_pct` and `status` may be updated after creation, and an administrator changing `discount_pct` should understand that it changes what the damage waiver is worth on every cycle opened afterwards.

Registering a household creates a `Member` against a plan. The registration payload carries `plan_id`, `email`, `display_name` and `child_size_code`, and may optionally carry `protection_enrolled`, which defaults to false, and `auto_apply_credit`, which defaults to true. The `email` must be unique across all members; a duplicate is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_EMAIL`. A `plan_id` that does not resolve is refused with `ERR_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, and registering against — or activating on — a plan whose `status` is `RETIRED` is refused with `ERR_PLAN_RETIRED`. A new member begins in `status` `PENDING` with `size_effective_at` set to the logical clock at registration.

Activation by a `PlatformAdmin` moves the member `PENDING → ACTIVE`. It requires that the member is still `PENDING` and that the referenced plan is `ACTIVE`; on success it sets `size_effective_at` to the current logical clock and increments the member's `version` by 1. Attempting the transition from any other state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The member lifecycle beyond activation is genuinely non-linear: `ACTIVE` and `PAUSED` form a bidirectional pair driven by pause activation and pause ending, and `ACTIVE` and `DELINQUENT` form a second bidirectional pair driven by the outstanding balance crossing a threshold in either direction. `CANCELLED` is the single terminal state.

Cancellation may be requested by the household itself or by a `PlatformAdmin`, and it is allowed from `ACTIVE` and from `PAUSED`. In both cases the member must have no box item whose `fulfillment_status` is `SHIPPED`, `WITH_MEMBER` or `RETURNING` — garments physically in the household's possession must come back first. From `ACTIVE`, cancellation revokes every credit entry whose `status` is `AVAILABLE` or `PARTIALLY_APPLIED`, recording `MEMBER_CANCELLED` as the entry's `revoke_reason`, closes every `SCHEDULED` cycle of the member, and increments the member's `version` by 1. From `PAUSED`, cancellation additionally ends the member's `ACTIVE` pause request, revokes the same credit entries with the same reason, and increments `version` by 1; the scheduled-cycle closure is the one asymmetry between the two paths, because a paused member's forward cycles have already been rescheduled rather than left in place.

The damage-waiver add-on is toggled by the household or by a `BillingAgent` through a single boolean input named `enrolled`, which writes `protection_enrolled` on the member and increments `version` by 1.

Sizes are an **ordered ladder**, never a set of equal labels. The eleven rungs of `SIZE_LADDER`, in order, are `2T`, `3T`, `4T`, `5`, `6`, `6X`, `7`, `8`, `10`, `12` and `14`, and their ordinals under `SIZE_LADDER_ORDINALS` run from 0 for `2T` to 10 for `14`. Compatibility between a garment and a child is judged on that ordinal, not on equality: a unit is compatible when its style's size ordinal minus the member's `child_size_code` ordinal is at least 0 — a unit may never sit *below* the child's size, because `SIZE_COMPAT_STEPS_BELOW_MAX` is 0 — and at most 1, because `SIZE_COMPAT_STEPS_ABOVE_MAX` is 1. A difference of exactly 0 and a difference of exactly 1 are both compatible; a difference of 2 is not, and a difference of −1 is not.

When a child grows, the household or a `PlatformAdmin` advances `child_size_code` by supplying a `new_size_code`. The event is valid only if it moves **up** the ladder by at least one rung and by no more than `SIZE_UP_MAX_STEPS_PER_EVENT`, which is 2 rungs; a move down the ladder, a move of zero rungs, or a move of three or more rungs is refused with `ERR_SIZE_UP_INVALID_STEP` (HTTP 422). Independently, at least `SIZE_UP_MIN_DAYS_BETWEEN_EVENTS` — thirty days — must have elapsed since the member's `size_effective_at`, and a sooner attempt is refused with `ERR_SIZE_UP_TOO_SOON`. On success the member's `child_size_code` is advanced, `size_effective_at` is reset to the logical clock, and `version` increments by 1. Garments already out with the household that are now incompatible become **stranded**: the count of the member's items whose `fulfillment_status` is `SHIPPED` or `WITH_MEMBER` and which fail the compatibility test against the *new* size is exactly what stranding means. Stranded garments are **not** force-returned and remain perfectly returnable; what they lose is the ability to be re-allocated or to be swapped *for*.

A computed entitlements read summarises where a household stands. It answers with `slots_total`, taken from the plan's `slots_per_box`; `slots_occupied`, the occupied-slot count over the member's non-closed box; `slots_permanently_consumed`, taken from the member's `permanent_slots_consumed`; `swaps_remaining` for the member's open cycle; `pause_days_remaining_period` and `pause_days_remaining_lifetime`; and the two money aggregates `credit_balance_cents` and `outstanding_balance_cents`. Those two aggregates are themselves derived and always current: `credit_balance_cents` is the sum of `remaining_cents` across the member's credit entries whose `status` is `AVAILABLE` or `PARTIALLY_APPLIED`, and `outstanding_balance_cents` is the sum of `net_due_cents` across the member's charge entries whose `status` is `ASSESSED` or `PARTIALLY_SETTLED`. Two more member-level counters are maintained the same way: `completed_cycles` counts the member's cycles whose `status` is `CLOSED`, and `total_paused_days` — which always equals `pause_days_used_lifetime` — is the cumulative paused-day figure used to extend credit expiry.

Standing is re-evaluated on **every** change to `outstanding_balance_cents`, and the two comparators are deliberately asymmetric. An `ACTIVE` member whose outstanding balance reaches or exceeds `DELINQUENCY_THRESHOLD_CENTS`, which is 15000 cents, becomes `DELINQUENT`. A `DELINQUENT` member whose outstanding balance falls **strictly below** 15000 cents returns to `ACTIVE`. A balance of exactly 15000 cents therefore keeps a delinquent member delinquent. Falling into `DELINQUENT` cascades: every `OPEN` or `SWAP_CLOSED` cycle of the member moves to `HELD`; every box item of the member still in `ALLOCATED` moves to `CANCELLED`; each such item's reserved unit returns from `RESERVED` to `IN_STOCK`; a `DRAFT` box moves to `CANCELLED` as its allocated items are cancelled; and the member's `version` increments by 1. Recovering to `ACTIVE` reverses the hold: every `HELD` cycle returns to the status implied by the current clock — `OPEN` while the clock is on or before `swap_by_at`, `SWAP_CLOSED` while it is on or before `return_due_at`, `RETURN_DUE` while it is on or before `grace_ends_at`, and `GRACE` thereafter — and the member's `version` increments again.

### 3.2. The Cycle Calendar, Its Boundaries and Its Charges

A **cycle** is the thirty-day unit of subscription. `CYCLE_LENGTH_DAYS` is 30. A `PlatformAdmin` or a `BillingAgent` schedules one for a household by supplying `member_id` and a `start_at` instant; the new row is born `SCHEDULED`, and its `cycle_index` is assigned as the member's previous highest index plus one, starting at 1 and never exceeding `CYCLE_MAX_INDEX`, which is 240.

Three boundaries are derived from `start_at` at creation and re-derived whenever the cycle is rescheduled. `swap_by_at` is `start_at` plus `SWAP_BY_OFFSET_DAYS`, twenty days. `return_due_at` is `start_at` plus `RETURN_DUE_OFFSET_DAYS`, thirty days. `grace_ends_at` is `return_due_at` plus `GRACE_PERIOD_DAYS`, a further five days — so grace ends thirty-five days after the cycle starts. The **inclusivity of these three boundaries is not uniform, and the difference is deliberate**: a swap is allowed while the clock is *on or before* `swap_by_at`, so a swap submitted at exactly `swap_by_at` succeeds; a return is on time while the clock is *on or before* `return_due_at`; but lateness accrues only *strictly after* `grace_ends_at`, so a garment whose return starts at exactly `grace_ends_at` accrues zero late days.

The derivation has two variants. In the ordinary case the three offsets are measured from the supplied `start_at` unchanged. In the post-pause case — a cycle that is still `SCHEDULED` when the household resumes from a pause — `start_at` is first advanced to the pause's `end_at`, and only then are the three offsets measured from the advanced `start_at`. Rescheduling never shortens a cycle: the thirty-day span is preserved and pushed forward. When a pause ends, every one of the member's `SCHEDULED` cycles is walked in ascending `cycle_index` order and each one's `start_at` becomes the later of its existing `start_at` and the pause's `end_at`, after which the three boundaries are re-derived.

Opening a cycle is a `PlatformAdmin` action that moves it `SCHEDULED → OPEN`. It requires that the cycle is `SCHEDULED`, that the member is `ACTIVE`, and that the logical clock has reached `start_at`; opening early is refused with `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_STARTED`. Opening is where the money for the cycle is assessed, in the pinned order of §2.7. First the **effective discount fraction** is resolved: an undiscounted gross is formed from the plan's `monthly_fee_cents` plus, when the member has `protection_enrolled` set, the flat `PROTECTION_FEE_CENTS_PER_CYCLE` of 900 cents per cycle; if that gross is at least 20000 cents a tier fraction of 0.10 applies, otherwise if it is at least 12000 cents a tier fraction of 0.05 applies, otherwise the tier fraction is zero; the effective discount fraction is the plan's `discount_pct` plus that tier fraction, capped at 0.50. The tier is chosen from the **undiscounted** gross and is never re-evaluated against the discounted total — the definition looks circular because the gross that keys the tier contains the very protection fee the discount will later reduce, and the evaluation order resolves that circularity once and for all.

With the fraction resolved, `plan_charge_cents` is the plan's `monthly_fee_cents` reduced by the cycle's `proration_credit_cents` and then multiplied by one minus the effective discount fraction, rounded half up to the cent. When the member is enrolled in protection, `protection_fee_cents` is 900 cents multiplied by one minus the same fraction, rounded half up separately. The cycle's `discount_cents` records the money the discount removed. Opening creates a charge ledger entry of `charge_type` `PLAN_CHARGE`, and — only when `protection_enrolled` is true — a second entry of `charge_type` `PROTECTION_FEE`. The cycle's `version` increments by 1.

Closing the swap window is a `PlatformAdmin` action moving the cycle `OPEN → SWAP_CLOSED`. It is permitted only when the logical clock is **strictly after** `swap_by_at`; attempting it at or before that instant is refused with `ERR_SWAP_WINDOW_STILL_OPEN`. Closing the window rejects every one of the cycle's swap requests still in `DRAFT` or `SUBMITTED`, writing `MEMBER_STANDING` as their `rejection_reason`, and bumps the cycle's `version`. A swap request that was merely `DRAFT` when the window closed is additionally treated as cancelled on its own machine.

Late assessment is a `BillingAgent` action and it is the same operation regardless of which of three edges it takes. From `SWAP_CLOSED` it moves the cycle to `RETURN_DUE` when the clock has passed `return_due_at` but has not yet passed `grace_ends_at`; on that edge **no** late fee accrues, precisely because the grace boundary has not been crossed. From `RETURN_DUE` it moves the cycle to `GRACE` once the clock is strictly past `grace_ends_at`. From `SWAP_CLOSED` it moves the cycle directly to `GRACE` when assessment happens only after the grace boundary has already been passed — the cycle skips `RETURN_DUE` entirely, and the side effects on this edge are identical to those of the `RETURN_DUE → GRACE` edge. Attempting to move into `GRACE` at or before `grace_ends_at` is refused with `ERR_GRACE_NOT_EXPIRED`.

On either edge into `GRACE`, `days_late` is computed for every box item whose `fulfillment_status` is `SHIPPED` or `WITH_MEMBER`, and `late_fee_cents` is computed per item under the plan's variant. The number of late days is the whole number of days between `grace_ends_at` and the effective instant, counted only when that instant is strictly after `grace_ends_at`, with **any partial day rounded up to a whole day**; the effective instant is the return-start instant for an item that has already started returning, and otherwise the current logical clock. Under the `PER_DAY` variant the fee is `LATE_FEE_PER_UNIT_PER_DAY_CENTS`, 150 cents, for each late day, capped at `LATE_FEE_CAP_PER_UNIT_CENTS`, 4500 cents per unit — the cap binds at thirty late days. Under the `FLAT_TIER` variant the fee is a step function: zero when there are no late days, `LATE_FEE_FLAT_TIER_1_CENTS` of 500 cents while the late days are at most `LATE_FEE_TIER_1_MAX_DAYS`, which is 3, `LATE_FEE_FLAT_TIER_2_CENTS` of 2000 cents while they are at most `LATE_FEE_TIER_2_MAX_DAYS`, which is 10, and `LATE_FEE_FLAT_TIER_3_CENTS` of 4500 cents beyond that. The two variants disagree on identical input by construction: four days late costs 2000 cents under `FLAT_TIER` but 600 cents under `PER_DAY`. The cycle's `late_fee_cents` is the sum of the per-item fees, taken over the cycle's items whose `fulfillment_status` is `SHIPPED`, `WITH_MEMBER`, `RETURNING`, `RECEIVED` or `LOST`, walked in ascending `slot_index` order; each per-item fee is rounded to the cent first and the rounded amounts are then added. A `LATE_FEE` charge ledger entry is created for the cycle whenever any fee is non-zero, and the cycle's `version` increments.

Closing a cycle is performed by a `BillingAgent` or a `PlatformAdmin` and has three destinations. From `GRACE` and from `RETURN_DUE` the cycle reaches `CLOSED`, and both edges require that every one of the cycle's box items has a `fulfillment_status` of `RECEIVED`, `CANCELLED` or `LOST`, or else a `settlement_status` of `BOUGHT_OUT`, and that the member is not `DELINQUENT`. The two edges have identical side effects; the only difference is that a cycle closing from `RETURN_DUE` carries a `late_fee_cents` of zero because the grace boundary was never crossed. Closing computes the invoice in the pinned four-step order, creates a charge ledger entry of `charge_type` `SURCHARGE`, increments the member's `completed_cycles` by 1, and bumps the cycle's `version`. From `HELD` a cycle owned by a `DELINQUENT` member closes instead to `DELINQUENT_CLOSED`: **no** surcharge entry is created, `completed_cycles` is **not** incremented, and only the `version` bump is shared with the ordinary path. Attempting the ordinary close while the member is `DELINQUENT` is refused with `ERR_CYCLE_CLOSE_BLOCKED_BY_DELINQUENCY`, and attempting any cycle transition from a state that does not declare it is refused with `ERR_CYCLE_INVALID_STATE`.

The taxable base for the surcharge is the sum of `plan_charge_cents`, `protection_fee_cents`, `late_fee_cents` and `damage_fee_cents`, reduced by `credit_applied_cents` and floored at zero; `surcharge_cents` is 8.75 percent of that base, rounded half up. `invoice_total_cents` is the floored post-credit base plus `surcharge_cents`. A computed invoice read exposes every ordered step separately — `plan_charge_cents`, `proration_credit_cents`, `discount_cents`, `protection_fee_cents`, `late_fee_cents`, `damage_fee_cents`, `credit_applied_cents`, `surcharge_cents` and `invoice_total_cents` — so that the effect of each step is observable rather than inferred. Alongside these the cycle also carries `swaps_used`, the count of its swap requests that reached `FULFILLED`; `slots_occupied`, the count of its box's items whose `fulfillment_status` is `ALLOCATED`, `SHIPPED`, `WITH_MEMBER` or `RETURNING`; `paused_days`, the days of the cycle window covered by an `ACTIVE` or `ENDED` pause; and `protection_waiver_used_cents`, the damage money the waiver has absorbed this cycle. `swaps_used`, `paused_days`, `late_fee_cents`, `damage_fee_cents`, `credit_applied_cents`, `surcharge_cents` and `protection_waiver_used_cents` all begin at 0.

### 3.3. The Catalog, House Stock, and Filling a Box

The catalog is owned by a `MerchandisePlanner`. A **garment style** is a catalog entry, not a physical thing: it carries a `sku` that must be unique across styles, a human-readable `name`, a `brand_tier` of `PREMIUM`, `STANDARD` or `VALUE`, a `retail_price_cents` of at least 100, a `size_code` drawn from the size ladder, a `season` of `SS`, `AW` or `ALL_SEASON`, a free-text `category` such as OUTERWEAR, TOP, BOTTOM or DRESS, and a `demand_band` of `HIGH`, `MEDIUM` or `LOW` that defaults to `MEDIUM`. Its `units_ever_acquired` counter starts at 0 and counts every physical unit ever created for that style from either acquisition source. A style is born `ACTIVE` and may be moved to `DISCONTINUED`; only `demand_band` and `status` may be updated later, and the planner should know that `demand_band` is a live input to the trade-in credit multiplier, so editing it changes what future trade-ins are worth. The `retail_price_cents` figure is the base of three separate calculations: the buy-out price, the lost-shipment assessed value, and the damage fee on a written-off garment.

A **garment unit** is one physical piece of clothing. Acquiring one supplies `style_id` and `acquired_at`, and may optionally supply `capsule_id` to enrol the unit in a seasonal wave, `current_grade` to record the condition it arrived in, and `cycles_completed` to seed its wear history; a unit acquired against a `DISCONTINUED` style is refused with `ERR_STYLE_DISCONTINUED`. Every acquired unit begins with `state` `IN_STOCK`, `acquisition_source` `PURCHASED`, `current_grade` `A` when none is supplied, `cycles_completed` and `cumulative_cleaning_cost_cents` at 0, `retirement_eligible` false, `retired_reason` null and `capsule_id` null, and it increments its style's `units_ever_acquired` by 1. Its `acquired_at` instant is the base of the tenure-decay buy-out variant.

The circulation loop is the heart of Pipeline R and it is **re-entrant**: `IN_STOCK → RESERVED → OUT → INBOUND → PROCESSING → IN_STOCK`, traversed once per wear cycle by the same unit. Two threshold-driven edges and one sale edge break the loop, and one loss edge diverts it. A unit's `retired_reason` names why it left: `CYCLE_CAP` when its wear count reached the cap, `COST_CAP` when its cleaning spend did, `GRADE_RETIRE` when the grading bench wrote it off, `REWORK_FAILED` when cleaning could not save it, and `LOST_WRITE_OFF` when a settled claim wrote off a parcel. A `MerchandisePlanner` may also retire a unit directly out of stock by supplying a `reason`, which moves it `IN_STOCK → RETIRED` and bumps its `version`; that is the only edge into `RETIRED` that does not pass through processing or a claim. `RETIRED` and `SOLD` are the two terminal circulation states.

A **box** is the physical shipment container for one cycle. A `WarehouseOperator` or a `PlatformAdmin` opens one against a `cycle_id`; the new box is `DRAFT`, its `member_id` is denormalised from the cycle's owner, its `slot_capacity` is copied from the member's plan `slots_per_box` and then **frozen for the life of the box** — a later plan edit does not resize an open box — and its `outbound_shipment_id` is null until it ships. The `slots_free` figure is always `slot_capacity` minus the count of the box's items whose `fulfillment_status` is `ALLOCATED`, `SHIPPED`, `WITH_MEMBER` or `RETURNING`, minus the count of items whose `settlement_status` is `BOUGHT_OUT`. That second subtraction is what makes a bought-out slot **permanently consumed**.

Allocation is a `WarehouseOperator` action against an `OPEN` cycle's `DRAFT` box, and it takes either an explicit `unit_id` or a `style_id` that hands the choice to the box-fill selector. Allocation moves the chosen unit `IN_STOCK → RESERVED`, creates a `BoxItem` with `fulfillment_status` `ALLOCATED` and `settlement_status` `OPEN`, assigns `slot_index` as a zero-based position in ascending order of allocation `created_at`, sets `is_capsule_item` true exactly when the allocated unit's `capsule_id` is non-null, copies `embargo_until` from that capsule when it is, and decrements the box's `slots_free` by 1. The item's `return_shipment_id` starts null, its `days_late`, `late_fee_cents` and `damage_fee_cents` start at 0, and its `assessed_grade` starts null. Allocating past the frozen capacity is refused with `ERR_BOX_SLOTS_EXHAUSTED`; allocating a unit whose size fails the ladder test is refused with `ERR_SIZE_INCOMPATIBLE`; allocating a unit whose `current_grade` is `RETIRE` or whose `retirement_eligible` is true is refused with `ERR_UNIT_RETIRE_GRADED`; and allocating a capsule unit without an `ACTIVE` allocation on an `OPEN` or `EMBARGO_LIFTED` capsule is refused with `ERR_CAPSULE_ALLOCATION_MISSING`. Attempting to allocate while the cycle is not `OPEN` is refused with `ERR_CYCLE_NOT_OPEN`, and attempting to move a unit that is not `IN_STOCK` is refused with `ERR_UNIT_INVALID_STATE`.

The **box-fill selector** runs when a style is named rather than a unit, and it has two variants that pick *different units from identical stock*. The eligibility filter is common to both: the unit's `state` is `IN_STOCK`, its `style_id` matches the requested style, its `current_grade` is one of `A`, `B`, `C` or `D`, its `retirement_eligible` is false, and its size is compatible with the member's `child_size_code` on the ordered ladder. The **standard fill** variant applies when the requested style carries no capsule membership; it restricts the pool to units whose `capsule_id` is null and ranks them by `cycles_completed` ascending, then by `cumulative_cleaning_cost_cents` ascending, breaking ties by `created_at` ascending — least-worn first, spreading wear evenly across stock. The **capsule fill** variant applies when the requested style's units carry a `capsule_id` and the member holds an `ACTIVE` allocation on that capsule; it restricts the pool to units whose capsule is `OPEN` or `EMBARGO_LIFTED` and ranks them by `created_at` ascending, breaking ties by `cycles_completed` ascending — oldest-acquired first, because capsule stock is issued in arrival order and not least-worn-first. If either variant's pool is empty, the whole request is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_STOCK`. Neither variant ever uses a row's `id` as a decision input; selection is fully deterministic from the declared ranking.

Packing is a `WarehouseOperator` action that moves the box `DRAFT → PACKED`. It requires that the count of the box's `ALLOCATED` items equals the box's `slot_capacity` — a partially filled box is refused with `ERR_BOX_NOT_FULL` — and that the owning cycle is `OPEN`. Shipping is a `LogisticsCoordinator` action that moves the box `PACKED → SHIPPED` under a supplied `label_id`. The label must have `direction` `OUTBOUND` and `status` `ISSUED`, and the logical clock must not have passed the label's `expires_at`; a label of the wrong direction is refused with `ERR_LABEL_DIRECTION_MISMATCH`, a label in the wrong status with `ERR_LABEL_INVALID_STATE`, and an expired label with `ERR_LABEL_EXPIRED`. Shipping creates a `Shipment` with `direction` `OUTBOUND` and `status` `CREATED`, sets the box's `outbound_shipment_id`, advances every `ALLOCATED` item to `SHIPPED`, advances every `RESERVED` unit to `OUT`, and bumps versions. A box transition attempted from a state that does not declare it is refused with `ERR_BOX_INVALID_STATE`.

The remainder of the box's life is driven from elsewhere. When the outbound parcel is received by the household, the box moves `SHIPPED → DELIVERED` and its items move `SHIPPED → WITH_MEMBER`, at which point a capsule item's `embargo_until` is (re)set from its capsule. When the first item of a delivered box starts its return the box moves `DELIVERED → RETURN_STARTED`. When the last non-terminal item of the box reaches `RECEIVED`, `CANCELLED` or `LOST` — that is, when no item of the box remains in `ALLOCATED`, `SHIPPED`, `WITH_MEMBER` or `RETURNING` — the box moves `RETURN_STARTED → CLOSED` on its own. A `DRAFT` box whose owner falls into delinquency moves `DRAFT → CANCELLED`, cancelling every `ALLOCATED` item and returning every `RESERVED` unit to `IN_STOCK`. `CLOSED` and `CANCELLED` are the box's terminal states.

### 3.4. Swapping Garments Inside a Cycle

Not every garment works. A household that dislikes two of five pieces should not have to wait a month, so each plan grants `swaps_per_cycle` swap entitlements and a household may spend them on **partial** swaps: send some items back early, receive replacements, and leave the rest of the box alone.

A swap request is created by the household against an `OPEN` cycle and is guarded optimistically on that cycle, as §2.8 describes. The payload carries `return_box_item_ids`, the identifiers of the box items being sent back, which may be a strict subset of the box; `requested_style_ids`, the catalog styles wanted as replacements; and `expected_cycle_version`. The two arrays must be the same length, each must hold at least one entry, and neither may hold more entries than the plan's `slots_per_box`; a violation of any of those three conditions is refused with `ERR_SWAP_SHAPE_INVALID`. Every identifier inside either array must resolve *and* belong to the addressed member's own resources; a stranger's box item or an unresolvable style is refused with `ERR_REFERENCE_NOT_OWNED`. A request that names a box item whose `embargo_until` is still in the future is refused with `ERR_CAPSULE_EMBARGOED`. A request created after the cycle's `swap_by_at` has passed is refused with `ERR_SWAP_WINDOW_CLOSED`. The new row is born `DRAFT` with `member_id` denormalised from the cycle's owner, `allocated_unit_ids` empty, `slot_delta` at 0 and `rejection_reason` null, and creating it increments the *cycle's* `version` by 1.

Submission is the household's second step and it is where the entitlement is actually claimed. Submission requires the request to be `DRAFT`, the cycle to be `OPEN`, and the logical clock to be on or before `swap_by_at` — at exactly `swap_by_at` submission still succeeds. The remaining entitlement for a cycle is the plan's `swaps_per_cycle` minus the count of that cycle's requests already `FULFILLED` minus the count still in flight as `SUBMITTED` or `APPROVED`; in-flight requests are counted precisely so a household cannot open five requests against a two-swap plan and have them all approved. If no entitlement remains, submission is refused with `ERR_SWAP_ENTITLEMENT_EXHAUSTED`. The entitlement is **period-scoped to the cycle** and does not carry forward: an unspent swap expires with the cycle. Note the interaction pinned in §2.5 — a household that is both past `swap_by_at` and out of entitlements is told `ERR_SWAP_WINDOW_CLOSED`, because the window gate is evaluated first.

Approval is a `WarehouseOperator` action moving the request `SUBMITTED → APPROVED`, and it runs the **replacement selector** over the requested styles. The styles are processed in the order they were supplied in `requested_style_ids`. For each one, the candidate pool is the units whose `state` is `IN_STOCK`, whose `style_id` matches, whose `current_grade` is one of `A`, `B`, `C` or `D`, whose `retirement_eligible` is false, whose size is compatible with the member on the ordered ladder, and which are either not capsule stock at all — a null `capsule_id` — or belong to a capsule whose `status` is `OPEN` or `EMBARGO_LIFTED`. Candidates are ranked by `cycles_completed` ascending, then by `cumulative_cleaning_cost_cents` ascending, with ties broken by `created_at` ascending, and the first candidate wins. Units already picked earlier **within the same request** are excluded from later picks, so a request naming the same style twice draws two different units. If any requested style yields an empty pool the whole request is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_STOCK`; a size failure surfaces as `ERR_SIZE_INCOMPATIBLE` and a retirement-grade failure as `ERR_UNIT_RETIRE_GRADED`, while a capsule unit without a live allocation surfaces as `ERR_CAPSULE_ALLOCATION_MISSING`. On success `allocated_unit_ids` is populated, each selected unit moves `IN_STOCK → RESERVED`, `slot_delta` is computed and pinned to 0, and the request's `version` increments by 1.

Rejection is the sibling branch and is also a `WarehouseOperator` action, moving the request `SUBMITTED → REJECTED` with a supplied `rejection_reason` of `NO_ELIGIBLE_STOCK`, `SIZE_MISMATCH`, `EMBARGOED_ITEM` or `MEMBER_STANDING`. Rejection consumes **no** slot and **no** entitlement — the in-flight count that a `SUBMITTED` request contributed simply disappears — and it bumps the request's `version` by 1. Any allocated box item covered by a rejected swap moves `ALLOCATED → CANCELLED`, which returns its unit `RESERVED → IN_STOCK` and settles the item at zero.

Fulfilment closes the loop. A `WarehouseOperator` moves the request `APPROVED → FULFILLED` only once **every** identifier in `return_box_item_ids` names an item whose `fulfillment_status` has reached `RECEIVED`; a premature attempt is refused with `ERR_RETURN_ITEM_NOT_RECEIVED`. Fulfilment creates one new box item per entry in `allocated_unit_ids`, each with `fulfillment_status` `ALLOCATED`, increments the cycle's `swaps_used` by 1, and bumps the request's `version`. The **conserving** property of a partial swap is enforced here: `slot_delta` is the number of allocated units minus the number of returned items, and it must be exactly zero, so returning two of five items and requesting two replacements leaves the box's `slots_free` unchanged. A non-zero `slot_delta`, or any breach of the cycle's swap-entitlement identity, is refused with `ERR_SWAP_CONSERVATION_VIOLATION`. `FULFILLED`, `REJECTED` and `CANCELLED` are the request's terminal states, and a request still sitting in `DRAFT` when the cycle's swap window closes is cancelled outright.

### 3.5. Labels, Shipments and Lost Parcels

Every physical movement in either pipeline travels under a **shipping label**, and the label machinery is one of the pieces both pipelines share outright. A `LogisticsCoordinator` mints a label for a `member_id` in one of four directions: `OUTBOUND` and `RETURN` belong to Pipeline R, `TRADE_IN_INBOUND` and `REJECT_RETURN` belong to Pipeline T. A minted label is `ISSUED`, records `issued_at` at the logical clock, carries a `tracking_code` minted with it, starts with `reissue_count` at 0 and `replaces_label_id` null, and expires at `issued_at` plus `LABEL_VALID_DAYS`, fourteen days. A label may authorise a departure while the clock is **on or before** its `expires_at`; that boundary is inclusive.

Labels have a genuinely cyclic life. `ISSUED` advances to `IN_TRANSIT` when its shipment departs, then to `DELIVERED` when the shipment is received, or to `LOST` when the shipment is marked lost. An unused `ISSUED` label may be **voided** by the coordinator, but only when no shipment referencing it has reached `IN_TRANSIT` or `DELIVERED`. An `ISSUED` label whose window has passed may be **expired**, and only then: expiring a label at or before its `expires_at` is refused with `ERR_LABEL_NOT_EXPIRED`. Both `VOIDED` and `EXPIRED` re-enter `ISSUED` through **reissue**, and reissue is where the cycle is made finite. Reissue does not resurrect the old row: it mints a **new** label with `status` `ISSUED`, `replaces_label_id` pointing at the predecessor, `reissue_count` equal to the predecessor's `reissue_count` plus one, and `expires_at` re-derived as fourteen days from the *current* logical clock; the predecessor stays in its terminal `VOIDED` or `EXPIRED` status. The two reissue edges — from `VOIDED` and from `EXPIRED` — have identical side effects. Because the count is carried forward along the `replaces_label_id` chain and `LABEL_REISSUE_CAP` is 2, the chain terminates: a reissue attempted on a label whose `reissue_count` has already reached 2 is refused with `ERR_LABEL_REISSUE_CAP_REACHED`. `DELIVERED` and `LOST` are the label's terminal states.

A **return label may expire before the cycle's `return_due_at`**, and when it does the household must obtain a reissue or the item accrues late fees on the cycle clock even though it was the label clock, not the household, that caused the delay. This is a real and intended asymmetry between two independent calendars, not an oversight.

A **shipment** is created by a coordinator against a `member_id`, a `label_id` and a `direction`, optionally declaring `item_count`, which defaults to 0 and may not be negative. It begins `CREATED` with `shipped_at` and `delivered_at` null and `ever_scanned` false. **Departure** moves it `CREATED → IN_TRANSIT`, requires the label to be `ISSUED` and unexpired, sets `shipped_at` to the logical clock, sets `ever_scanned` true, and advances the label `ISSUED → IN_TRANSIT`. A `TRADE_IN_INBOUND` departure additionally advances its submission from `LABEL_ISSUED` to `IN_TRANSIT`.

**Receipt** moves a shipment `IN_TRANSIT → DELIVERED`, sets `delivered_at`, advances the label to `DELIVERED`, and fires a cascade chosen by `direction`. An `OUTBOUND` receipt advances the box's `SHIPPED` items to `WITH_MEMBER` and the box itself `SHIPPED → DELIVERED`. A `RETURN` receipt advances `RETURNING` items to `RECEIVED`, moves their units `INBOUND → PROCESSING`, increments each unit's `cycles_completed` by 1, and opens a `DRAFT` condition assessment of `pipeline` `RENTAL` per unit. A `TRADE_IN_INBOUND` receipt advances the submission `IN_TRANSIT → RECEIVED` and transfers ownership of every declared garment to the service.

**Loss** is where the two liability variants are born, and the distinction is subtle enough to be worth stating twice. A shipment marked lost from `IN_TRANSIT` has already scanned, so its `ever_scanned` is true. A shipment marked lost from `CREATED` — the parcel was handed to the carrier but never produced a departure scan — leaves `shipped_at` null and `ever_scanned` false. **This second edge is the only way a never-scanned loss can occur**, and every other side effect of the two edges is identical: the label moves to `LOST` (from `ISSUED` on the never-scanned edge and from `IN_TRANSIT` on the scanned edge), every `RETURNING` box item on the shipment moves to `LOST` with its `settlement_status` moving to `LOST_CHARGED`, every `INBOUND` unit moves to `LOST_IN_TRANSIT`, and a charge ledger entry of `charge_type` `LOST_LIABILITY` is raised for the **full** assessed value — which may itself push the member `ACTIVE → DELINQUENT`. **No claim is created automatically**; filing a claim is a separate, explicit act and it is the only producer of a `LostShipmentClaim` row.

A shipment may also be **recovered**. Receipt of a shipment whose `status` is `LOST` is permitted while no claim against that shipment has reached `SETTLED`; it moves the shipment `LOST → DELIVERED`, moves every `LOST_IN_TRANSIT` unit to `PROCESSING`, moves every `LOST` box item to `RECEIVED` with its `settlement_status` reset to `OPEN`, and denies every claim still `OPENED` or `INVESTIGATING` with a `denial_reason` of `SHIPMENT_RECOVERED`. This edge is what makes the circulation loop re-entrant even from a loss. Separately, a `REJECT_RETURN` shipment carrying rejected trade-in garments back to the household moves `IN_TRANSIT → RETURNED_TO_SENDER`, advancing its label to `DELIVERED` and leaving each covered item's `RETURN_TO_MEMBER` disposition intact; attempting that operation on a shipment of any other direction is refused with `ERR_SHIPMENT_DIRECTION_MISMATCH`, and any shipment transition attempted from an undeclared from-state is refused with `ERR_SHIPMENT_INVALID_STATE`.

Filing a **lost-shipment claim** is open to the household itself or to a coordinator, and it takes only a `shipment_id`. The shipment must be `LOST`; filing against anything else is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_SHIPMENT_NOT_LOST`. The filing window is `LOST_CLAIM_FILING_WINDOW_DAYS`, ten days, measured from the shipment's `shipped_at` — or, when `shipped_at` is null because the parcel never scanned, from the shipment's `created_at`. The resulting `deadline_at` is that base plus ten days, and filing is allowed **on or before** it; a later filing is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_WINDOW_CLOSED`. A filed claim is `OPENED`, records `filed_at`, and denormalises `member_id` from the shipment.

Investigation moves the claim `OPENED → INVESTIGATING` and requires the shipment to still be `LOST`. Investigation is where the money is computed. The claim's `variant` resolves from `ever_scanned`: `SCANNED` when the parcel entered `IN_TRANSIT` at least once, `NEVER_SCANNED` otherwise. Its `assessed_value_cents` is the sum, over every unit on the shipment whose `state` is `LOST_IN_TRANSIT`, of that unit's style `retail_price_cents` multiplied by 0.55 and rounded half up to the cent — rounded per unit first, then summed, walking the units in ascending `created_at` order. Its `member_liability_pct` is `LOST_LIABILITY_MEMBER_PCT_SCANNED`, 0.30, under the scanned variant, because the carrier is presumed to hold the parcel; and `LOST_LIABILITY_MEMBER_PCT_NEVER_SCANNED`, 0.60, under the never-scanned variant, because the parcel is presumed never to have been handed over. Its `member_liability_cents` is `assessed_value_cents` multiplied by that fraction, rounded half up. On identical assessed value the never-scanned household therefore bears exactly double the scanned household's share.

`INVESTIGATING` and `AWAITING_MEMBER_INFO` form a **bidirectional pair** that may be traversed any number of times: a coordinator requests information, the household answers by supplying an `info_note` which is appended to the claim and returns it to `INVESTIGATING`, and the cycle may repeat. From `INVESTIGATING` the claim forks. **Approval** by a coordinator finalises `member_liability_cents` and moves the claim to `APPROVED`. **Denial** by a coordinator records a `denial_reason` and moves it to `DENIED`, and denial leaves the household liable for the **full** assessed value — the `LOST_LIABILITY` charge is not reduced. **Settlement** by a `BillingAgent` moves an `APPROVED` claim to `SETTLED`: the `LOST_LIABILITY` charge entry's amount is reduced to `member_liability_cents`, every `LOST_IN_TRANSIT` unit on the shipment is retired with `retired_reason` `LOST_WRITE_OFF`, the member's `outstanding_balance_cents` is recomputed, and if the recomputed balance now falls strictly below 15000 cents the member returns `DELINQUENT → ACTIVE`. `SETTLED` and `DENIED` are the claim's terminal states.

### 3.6. Coming Back: Return, Grading, Cleaning and Retirement

A household returns garments **one at a time**, not as a block. Starting a return is a member action on a single box item that supplies a `return_shipment_id`. It requires the item's `fulfillment_status` to be `SHIPPED` or `WITH_MEMBER`, its `settlement_status` to still be `OPEN`, and the return label to be `ISSUED` with the logical clock on or before its `expires_at`. It moves the item to `RETURNING`, writes `return_shipment_id` onto the item, computes `days_late` against the cycle's `grace_ends_at` using the return-start instant, moves the unit `OUT → INBOUND`, and advances the box `DELIVERED → RETURN_STARTED` if it has not advanced already. An item addressed in a fulfillment state that does not declare the transition is refused with `ERR_BOX_ITEM_INVALID_FULFILLMENT_STATE`.

The **condition rubric is one rubric**. A `GradingTechnician` opens a grading record by naming a `pipeline` of `RENTAL` or `TRADE_IN` together with exactly one target: `unit_id` for a rental unit, or `trade_in_item_id` for a trade-in garment. Supplying both, supplying neither, or supplying the one that contradicts the declared pipeline is refused with `ERR_ASSESSMENT_TARGET_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422); confirming a `RENTAL` assessment against a trade-in garment, or a `TRADE_IN` assessment against a rental unit, is refused with `ERR_PIPELINE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 409). A record is born `DRAFT` with `grade` null, `defect_codes` empty, `regrade_count` at 0, and `graded_by_user_id`, `confirmed_by_user_id` and `dispute_reason` all null.

Grades live on an ordered ladder of their own. `GRADE_LADDER` runs `A`, `B`, `C`, `D`, `RETIRE`, and the ordinals under `GRADE_ORDINALS` are 0 for `A`, 1 for `B`, 2 for `C`, 3 for `D` and 4 for `RETIRE`. Submitting a grade moves the record `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`, requires the supplied `grade` to be a member of that ladder, optionally records `defect_codes` such as STAIN, TEAR, PILLING or MISSING_BUTTON, and writes the acting technician's identifier into `graded_by_user_id`. A `TRADE_IN` submission's parent additionally advances `RECEIVED → GRADING` at this moment.

A submitted grade may be **disputed** — by the household or by a `MerchandisePlanner` — with a free-text `dispute_reason`, moving the record `SUBMITTED → DISPUTED`. A disputed record may be **regraded** by a technician with a replacement `grade`, moving it `DISPUTED → SUBMITTED`, replacing the stored grade, incrementing `regrade_count` by 1, and rewriting `graded_by_user_id` with the regrading technician's identifier. This is a genuine loop and it is capped: both disputing and regrading require `regrade_count` to be strictly below `REGRADE_MAX_ATTEMPTS`, which is 2, and an attempt at or above the cap is refused with `ERR_RETRY_CAP_REACHED`. A record still `DRAFT` when its covered unit reaches `RETIRED` is voided outright. `CONFIRMED` and `VOIDED` are the record's terminal states.

Confirmation is a `MerchandisePlanner` action, permitted from `SUBMITTED` and equally from `DISPUTED` — confirming a disputed record upholds the dispute at whatever grade currently stands, and the side effects of the two edges are identical. The confirmer's identifier is written into `confirmed_by_user_id` and must differ from `graded_by_user_id`, as §2.1 requires. For a `RENTAL` record, confirmation writes `assessed_grade` onto the returned box item, creates a processing job, and advances the item's settlement axis. For a `TRADE_IN` record, confirmation drives the trade-in item's two axes as described in §3.9.

**Cleaning routing is shared and table-driven.** The confirmed grade selects a `route` through `GRADE_ROUTE_MAP`: an `A` or a `B` routes to `STANDARD_LAUNDER`, a `C` routes to `STAIN_TREATMENT`, a `D` routes to `REPAIR`, and a `RETIRE` routes to `RETIRE`. The job is created `QUEUED` with `attempt_count` and `cost_cents` at 0, `post_repair_grade` null, and its `assessment_id` and `unit_id` copied from the confirmed record. Base costs are fixed per route: `CLEAN_COST_STANDARD_LAUNDER_CENTS` is 320, `CLEAN_COST_STAIN_TREATMENT_CENTS` is 900, `CLEAN_COST_REPAIR_CENTS` is 2400, and `CLEAN_COST_RETIRE_CENTS` is 0; the same four numbers are what `ROUTE_BASE_COST_CENTS` holds for `STANDARD_LAUNDER`, `STAIN_TREATMENT`, `REPAIR` and `RETIRE` respectively.

A `CleaningVendor` starts a job, moving it `QUEUED → IN_PROGRESS`, which requires the unit to be in `PROCESSING` and increments `attempt_count` by 1, setting `cost_cents` to the route's base cost. A job may **fail**, moving `IN_PROGRESS → FAILED`, and a failed job may be **restarted**, moving `FAILED → IN_PROGRESS` — a re-entrant rework loop capped at `REWORK_MAX_ATTEMPTS`, which is 2. Each rework increments `attempt_count` again and **adds** `REWORK_COST_MULTIPLIER` — 0.50, that is half — of the route's base cost to `cost_cents`, rounded half up. A start attempted at or above the cap is refused with `ERR_RETRY_CAP_REACHED`. The rework loop therefore feeds the retirement threshold directly: money spent chasing a stubborn stain is money that counts toward writing the garment off.

Passing a job moves it `IN_PROGRESS → PASSED` and is where the unit's ledger is settled. The unit's `cumulative_cleaning_cost_cents` becomes the sum of `cost_cents` over that unit's jobs whose `status` is `PASSED` or `SCRAPPED`, walked in ascending `created_at` order. The unit's `current_grade` is then recomputed. If the unit is already `RETIRED`, the effective grade is `RETIRE` — retirement is **absorbing** and a unit is never resurrected. If the job's route is not `REPAIR`, or the job supplies no `post_repair_grade`, the effective grade is simply the confirmed assessment grade. Otherwise the job supplies a `post_repair_grade` of `A`, `B`, `C` or `D`, and the uplift — the assessment grade's ordinal minus the `post_repair_grade`'s ordinal — must be at most `REPAIR_UPLIFT_MAX_STEPS`, which is 2; an uplift beyond that, or a `post_repair_grade` supplied on a route that is not `REPAIR`, is refused with `ERR_REPAIR_UPLIFT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 422). A confirmed grade of `RETIRE` stays `RETIRE` no matter what the repair claims. Grade is therefore **non-monotone against wear**: a `D` garment repaired well becomes a `B`, and only `RETIRE` is one-way.

After the cost has accrued, retirement eligibility is re-evaluated: a unit is `retirement_eligible` when its `cycles_completed` has reached `UNIT_MAX_CYCLES`, 8, **or** its `cumulative_cleaning_cost_cents` has reached `UNIT_MAX_CLEANING_COST_CENTS`, 6000, **or** its `current_grade` is `RETIRE`. Both counters use an at-or-above comparison, so a unit standing at exactly 8 completed cycles, or at exactly 6000 cents of cleaning spend, retires. A passed job on a unit that is not eligible returns it `PROCESSING → IN_STOCK` and it re-enters circulation. A passed job on a unit that *is* eligible moves it `PROCESSING → RETIRED` and writes `retired_reason` by a fixed precedence: `CYCLE_CAP` when the wear count has reached 8; otherwise `COST_CAP` when the cleaning spend has reached 6000; otherwise `GRADE_RETIRE`.

Scrapping is the `MerchandisePlanner`'s counterpart and has two edges. A `FAILED` job may be scrapped, retiring its unit with `retired_reason` `REWORK_FAILED`. A `QUEUED` job whose `route` is `RETIRE` may be scrapped, retiring its unit with `retired_reason` `GRADE_RETIRE`; that is the **only** path a `RETIRE`-routed job may ever take, because such a job can never be started and never be passed. `PASSED` and `SCRAPPED` are the job's terminal states, and any job or assessment transition attempted from an undeclared from-state is refused with `ERR_ASSESSMENT_INVALID_STATE`.

The other half of confirmation on the rental side is **damage money**. The gross damage fee follows the confirmed grade: `DAMAGE_FEE_BY_GRADE_CENTS` gives 0 for `A`, 0 for `B`, 1500 for `C` and 4000 for `D`, while a `RETIRE` grade instead charges the style's `retail_price_cents` multiplied by 0.55 and rounded half up. The **damage waiver** then applies, and it is priced against what the household actually paid for protection. The waiver's effective cap for the cycle is `PROTECTION_WAIVER_CAP_PER_CYCLE_CENTS`, 5000 cents, scaled by the ratio of the cycle's own `protection_fee_cents` to the undiscounted 900-cent protection fee, rounded half up; the remaining cap is that effective cap less the cycle's `protection_waiver_used_cents`, floored at zero. The coupling is deliberate and it costs the household money: a 10 percent discount that reduces the protection fee to 810 cents also shrinks a 5000-cent waiver cap to 4500 cents.

The waived amount is zero when the member is not enrolled in protection, and zero when the confirmed grade's ordinal exceeds `PROTECTION_WAIVER_GRADE_ORDINAL_MAX`, which is 3 — so grades `A` through `D` are inside the waiver band and `RETIRE`, at ordinal 4, is **never** waived no matter how much cap remains. Otherwise the waived amount is the smaller of the gross fee and the remaining cap. The item's `damage_fee_cents` is the gross fee less the waived amount, and the cycle's `protection_waiver_used_cents` grows by the waived amount. The settlement axis records the outcome: the item moves `OPEN → WAIVED` exactly when the member is enrolled, the grade ordinal is at most 3, the gross fee is strictly greater than zero, and the gross fee is less than or equal to the remaining cap — that is, when the waiver absorbs the fee **entirely**, in which case `damage_fee_cents` is 0 and **no** `DAMAGE_FEE` charge entry is created. In every other case the item moves `OPEN → SETTLED`, `assessed_grade` is written, `damage_fee_cents` is computed as above — which may still be a *partially* waived amount when the gross fee exceeded the remaining cap — and a charge ledger entry of `charge_type` `DAMAGE_FEE` is created whenever the resulting `damage_fee_cents` is greater than zero. Confirming an assessment on an item whose `settlement_status` is no longer `OPEN` is refused with `ERR_BOX_ITEM_ALREADY_SETTLED`, and confirming an assessment that would draw on the waiver while the cycle's `PROTECTION_FEE` charge has itself been waived is refused with `ERR_WAIVER_NOT_AVAILABLE`.

### 3.7. Buying a Garment Out of the Box

Sometimes a garment simply cannot go back. A household may buy the unit occupying one of its slots, and the price it is offered depends on how that garment has aged — but there are two competing notions of ageing, and the household chooses which one prices its purchase.

Minting a quote is a member action on a single box item, guarded optimistically on that item as §2.8 describes, and it takes a `quote_variant` of either `CYCLES_WORN` or `MONTHS_HELD` alongside `expected_box_item_version`. The base price is the unit's style `retail_price_cents` multiplied by the base rate for the unit's `current_grade`: `BUYOUT_BASE_RATE_BY_GRADE` gives 0.62 for `A`, 0.50 for `B`, 0.38 for `C` and 0.22 for `D`. A decay factor is then applied. Under the `CYCLES_WORN` variant the decay factor is one minus the unit's `cycles_completed` multiplied by `BUYOUT_CYCLE_DECAY_PER_CYCLE`, 0.04 — so a garment worn through six cycles has lost 24 percent regardless of how long the service has owned it. Under the `MONTHS_HELD` variant the decay factor is one minus the number of whole months between the unit's `acquired_at` and the logical clock, multiplied by `BUYOUT_MONTH_DECAY_PER_MONTH`, 0.015 — so a garment acquired six months ago has lost 9 percent regardless of how often it has been worn. In both variants the decay factor is floored at `BUYOUT_DECAY_FLOOR_FACTOR`, 0.60, so no garment ever decays below sixty percent of its graded base. The raw price is the graded base multiplied by the floored decay factor, and `quoted_price_cents` is that raw price rounded **half up to the nearest 25 cents**, the step fixed by `BUYOUT_ROUNDING_STEP_CENTS`. The two variants price the *same* unit differently on purpose.

If the item is already late — its `days_late` is greater than zero — a `late_surcharge_cents` of 10 percent of `quoted_price_cents`, rounded half up, is added; otherwise `late_surcharge_cents` is 0. The `final_price_cents` is `quoted_price_cents` plus `late_surcharge_cents`, raised to `BUYOUT_MIN_PRICE_CENTS`, 900 cents, if it would otherwise fall below it. **The order here is pinned**: the 25-cent step rounding is applied to `quoted_price_cents` *before* the surcharge, and the 900-cent floor is applied *after* the surcharge. Applying the floor first, or rounding after the surcharge, gives a different answer. Buying out a late garment does **not** erase the `LATE_FEE` already accrued — the surcharge is additional — though the obligation to return that garment is extinguished the moment the buy-out completes, so no further late days accrue on that slot. A quote may not be minted at all for a unit whose style `retail_price_cents` is below 900 cents, because the floor would then exceed the garment's own retail value; that is refused with `ERR_BUYOUT_BELOW_FLOOR` (HTTP 422). A second quote against a box item that already carries an order in `QUOTED`, `ACCEPTED`, `PAID` or `COMPLETED` is refused with `ERR_BUYOUT_ALREADY_EXISTS`.

A minted order is `QUOTED`, records `quoted_at` at the logical clock, denormalises `member_id`, and sets `quote_expires_at` to `quoted_at` plus `BUYOUT_QUOTE_TTL_HOURS`, seventy-two hours. Acceptance is a member action and is allowed only while the logical clock is **strictly before** `quote_expires_at`; at or after that instant acceptance is refused with `ERR_BUYOUT_QUOTE_EXPIRED`. Acceptance additionally requires the member to be `ACTIVE`, and requires the covered item to be out of embargo — either its `embargo_until` is null, or the clock has reached it. A `BillingAgent` may instead expire a stale quote, moving it `QUOTED → EXPIRED` once the clock has reached `quote_expires_at`; and the household may cancel, moving `QUOTED → CANCELLED`, or moving `ACCEPTED → CANCELLED` provided no `BUYOUT_PRICE` charge has yet been raised for the order.

Payment is a `BillingAgent` action and it takes **two calls**. The first moves the order `ACCEPTED → PAID` and raises a charge ledger entry of `charge_type` `BUYOUT_PRICE` for `final_price_cents`. The second moves it `PAID → COMPLETED` and performs the ownership transfer: the box item's `settlement_status` becomes `BOUGHT_OUT`, its `fulfillment_status` becomes `RECEIVED` **without any physical return**, the unit moves `OUT → SOLD`, the member's `permanent_slots_consumed` increments by 1, and — the point of the whole mechanism — the box's `slots_free` is **not** incremented. Contrast this with an ordinary return, which does give the slot back. `COMPLETED`, `EXPIRED` and `CANCELLED` are the order's terminal states, and any buy-out transition from an undeclared from-state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

### 3.8. Seasonal Capsule Waves

Twice a year the merchandising team releases a **capsule** — a curated seasonal drop that longer-standing households get first call on. A `MerchandisePlanner` creates a wave by supplying a `season`, which is one of the two members of `CAPSULE_SEASONS`, `SS` or `AW`; a `name`; a `release_at` instant; a `close_at` instant; and optionally a `quota_per_member` between 0 and 6 that defaults to `CAPSULE_QUOTA_PER_MEMBER_DEFAULT`, which is 2. The wave begins `DRAFT`.

Two capsules **of the same season** may not overlap, and the interval semantics here are **closed at both ends**: a new wave conflicts with an existing non-closed wave of the same season whenever the new `release_at` is at or before the existing `close_at` and the existing `release_at` is at or before the new `close_at`. Adjacency is therefore a **conflict** — a wave opening at exactly another wave's `close_at` collides. This is deliberately the opposite of the pause-window rule in §3.10, which uses half-open intervals and allows adjacency. A collision is refused with `ERR_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. Waves of *different* seasons never conflict with each other.

The wave's life is linear but its calendar is not the cycle calendar. Announcing it moves it `DRAFT → ANNOUNCED`, is permitted only while the logical clock is strictly before `release_at`, and computes `embargo_until` as `release_at` plus `CAPSULE_EMBARGO_DAYS`, twenty-one days. Opening it moves it `ANNOUNCED → OPEN` and is permitted only when the clock has reached `release_at` and has not reached `close_at`; from that moment the wave's units are allocatable. Lifting the embargo moves it `OPEN → EMBARGO_LIFTED` and is permitted only when the clock has reached `embargo_until`; an earlier attempt is refused with `ERR_CAPSULE_EMBARGO_NOT_REACHED`. Closing is permitted from `EMBARGO_LIFTED` and equally from `OPEN`, in both cases only once the clock has reached `close_at`; an earlier attempt is refused with `ERR_CAPSULE_CLOSE_NOT_DUE`. Both closing edges release every `ACTIVE` allocation on the wave to `RELEASED` and bump the wave's `version`; the difference is that when a wave closes straight out of `OPEN`, **its items remain embargoed**, because `embargo_until` has not yet been reached. The capsule calendar and the embargo calendar are genuinely not the same calendar: `close_at` may precede `embargo_until`, and a capsule item can stay embargoed past its own cycle's `return_due_at`.

While an item is embargoed, it may be neither swapped nor bought out; the check compares the logical clock against the *item's* `embargo_until`, which is copied from its capsule when the item is a capsule item and is null otherwise, and a strictly-before-embargo attempt is refused with `ERR_CAPSULE_EMBARGOED`.

A household claims its share of a wave by creating an **allocation** naming `member_id` and a `units_allocated` count of at least 1. Eligibility has four parts, all required: the member's `status` is `ACTIVE` — a `PAUSED` member is not eligible even mid-window, so a pause silently costs capsule access for its whole duration; the member's `completed_cycles` is at least `CAPSULE_ELIGIBILITY_MIN_COMPLETED_CYCLES`, which is 1, and a household short of that is refused with `ERR_CAPSULE_ELIGIBILITY_NOT_MET`; the wave's `status` is `OPEN`, and any other status is refused with `ERR_CAPSULE_NOT_OPEN`; and the logical clock is at or after `release_at` and strictly before `close_at`, with any other instant refused with `ERR_CAPSULE_WINDOW_CLOSED`. The quota available to a household is the **smaller** of the wave's `quota_per_member` and the plan's `capsule_quota_per_wave`, less the sum of `units_allocated` over the household's `ACTIVE` allocations on that same wave; exceeding it is refused with `ERR_CAPSULE_QUOTA_EXCEEDED`. A created allocation is `ACTIVE`; a planner may release one directly, moving it `ACTIVE → RELEASED`, and the wave's closure releases the rest.

### 3.9. Pipeline T: From Outgrown Garment to Account Credit

The household's own outgrown clothes are an asset, and Pipeline T turns them into spendable credit. A **submission** is a batch, not a single garment. The household opens one by naming its own `member_id` and a `declared_item_count`; that count must be at least `TRADEIN_MIN_ITEMS_PER_SUBMISSION`, three, and at most `TRADEIN_MAX_ITEMS_PER_SUBMISSION`, twelve. A count outside those bounds, adding more garments than declared, or issuing a label while the number of created garment rows does not equal `declared_item_count`, is refused with `ERR_TRADE_IN_ITEM_COUNT_INVALID` (HTTP 422). A new submission is `DRAFT` with `inbound_shipment_id` null, `credited_total_cents` and `uncapped_total_cents` at 0, and `election_deadline_at` null.

Each declared garment is added individually, carrying a `declared_brand_tier` of `PREMIUM`, `STANDARD` or `VALUE` and a `declared_size_code` from the size ladder. A trade-in garment is born with `grading_status` `PENDING` and `disposition_status` `NONE`, `assessed_grade` null, `demand_band` `MEDIUM`, `absorbed_unit_id` null, and `credit_cents` and `granted_credit_cents` at 0. **These two axes advance independently**: the grading axis answers "does this garment earn credit", the disposition axis answers "where does this garment physically end up", and a garment can be fully credited while still awaiting a home, or fully rejected while still needing an election.

A `LogisticsCoordinator` attaches a `label_id` of `direction` `TRADE_IN_INBOUND` and `status` `ISSUED` to a `DRAFT` submission whose member is `ACTIVE`; that creates the inbound shipment, writes `inbound_shipment_id`, and moves the submission `DRAFT → LABEL_ISSUED`. The parcel's departure moves it `LABEL_ISSUED → IN_TRANSIT`, and its receipt moves it `IN_TRANSIT → RECEIVED`, at which moment **ownership of every declared garment transfers to the service** — the single clearest structural difference from Pipeline R, where ownership never moves at all. The first grade submitted against any garment in the batch moves the submission `RECEIVED → GRADING`.

Grading uses the same bench, the same technician, the same rubric and the same segregation-of-duties rule as the rental side; only the outcome differs. Confirming a `TRADE_IN` assessment writes `assessed_grade` onto the garment and forks on the grade's ordinal. A confirmed grade whose ordinal is at most 3 — that is, `A`, `B`, `C` or `D` — moves the grading axis `PENDING → GRADED`, resolves `demand_band`, and computes `credit_cents`. A confirmed grade of `RETIRE` instead moves the grading axis `PENDING → REJECTED`, sets `credit_cents` to 0, and simultaneously moves the disposition axis `NONE → AWAITING_ELECTION`, because a garment the service will not take must go somewhere.

The `demand_band` is resolved from the live catalog, not from anything the household declared. The candidate set is every `ACTIVE` garment style whose `size_code` equals the garment's `declared_size_code`. If that set is empty the band is `MEDIUM`. Otherwise the candidates are ranked by demand-band ordinal ascending, using `DEMAND_BAND_ORDINALS` in which `HIGH` ranks 0, `MEDIUM` ranks 1 and `LOW` ranks 2, with ties broken by `created_at` ascending, and the first candidate's `demand_band` is taken. The ranking is therefore biased toward `HIGH`: a single high-demand style in the child's size lifts the whole valuation.

Per-garment credit is a product of three independent tables, rounded half up to the cent. The grade base comes from `TRADEIN_GRADE_BASE_CENTS`: 1400 cents for `A`, 900 for `B`, 450 for `C` and 0 for `D`. The brand multiplier comes from `BRAND_TIER_MULTIPLIER`: 1.40 for `PREMIUM`, 1.00 for `STANDARD`, 0.70 for `VALUE`. The size-demand multiplier comes from `SIZE_DEMAND_BAND_MULTIPLIER`: 1.25 for `HIGH`, 1.00 for `MEDIUM`, 0.80 for `LOW`. `credit_cents` is the grade base multiplied by the brand multiplier multiplied by the demand multiplier, rounded once at the end. A `RETIRE` grade never reaches this calculation, because such a garment has already been rejected.

Finalisation is a `BillingAgent` action moving the submission `GRADING → CREDITED`, and it requires **every** garment in the batch to have a `grading_status` of `GRADED` or `REJECTED` — no garment may still be `PENDING`. Finalisation walks the batch's `GRADED` garments ordered by `credit_cents` **descending**, ties broken by `created_at` ascending. `uncapped_total_cents` is the sum of the already-rounded per-garment `credit_cents`, exposed on the submission precisely so the cap's effect is observable. The per-submission cap is `TRADEIN_CREDIT_CAP_PER_SUBMISSION_CENTS`, 6000 cents, and it is applied by one of **two waterfalls chosen by the plan's tier**. Under the **sequential** waterfall — which applies whenever the plan's `tier` is not `CAPSULE_PLUS` — a running remainder starts at 6000 and each garment in the pinned order receives the smaller of its own `credit_cents` and whatever remains, with the remainder reduced by what it just granted; the highest-value garments are paid in full and the tail is truncated to zero, so the cap falls entirely on the cheapest pieces. Under the **pro-rata** waterfall — which applies when the plan's `tier` is `CAPSULE_PLUS` — a single factor is computed as the smaller of 1 and the cap divided by `uncapped_total_cents`, and every garment's `granted_credit_cents` is its own `credit_cents` multiplied by that factor, rounded half up per garment; no garment is zeroed. The two waterfalls split identical inputs differently, which is the whole point of offering the second one. `credited_total_cents` is the sum of the granted amounts; per-garment rounding happens first and the rounded amounts are then summed.

Finalisation also moves every `GRADED` garment's grading axis to `CREDITED`, creates **one** credit ledger entry for the batch with `status` `AVAILABLE`, `amount_cents` and `remaining_cents` equal to `credited_total_cents`, `source_submission_id` pointing at the batch, `granted_at` at the logical clock and `expires_at` derived; recomputes the member's `credit_balance_cents`; applies the new credit immediately to the member's open charges under the standing waterfall when `auto_apply_credit` is true; and sets `election_deadline_at` to the finalisation instant plus `TRADEIN_DISPOSITION_ELECTION_DAYS`, seven days. The submission's `version` increments by 1.

The disposition axis is settled separately. A **credited** garment may be **absorbed** into house rental stock by a `MerchandisePlanner`, who supplies a `style_id`; that style must be `ACTIVE` and its `size_code` must equal the garment's `declared_size_code`, and any other style is refused with `ERR_ABSORB_STYLE_MISMATCH`. Absorption mints a new `GarmentUnit` with `acquisition_source` `TRADE_IN_ABSORBED`, `state` `IN_STOCK` and `current_grade` equal to the garment's `assessed_grade`, writes `absorbed_unit_id` back onto the trade-in garment, moves its disposition axis `NONE → ABSORBED`, and increments the chosen style's `units_ever_acquired` by 1. This edge is where Pipeline T feeds Pipeline R: a household's outgrown coat becomes another household's rental stock. Absorption requires the garment's `grading_status` to have reached `CREDITED`, so a rejected garment is never absorbed.

A **rejected** garment sits in `AWAITING_ELECTION` until the household elects a `disposition`. Electing `RETURN_TO_MEMBER` ships the garment back and raises a charge ledger entry of `charge_type` `SHIPPING_FEE` for `TRADEIN_RETURN_SHIP_FEE_CENTS`, 1200 cents — the household pays to have its own rejected garment sent home. Electing `DONATE` or electing `RECYCLE` creates **no** charge at all. All three elections are accepted only while the logical clock is **on or before** `election_deadline_at`; a later election is refused with `ERR_ELECTION_DEADLINE_PASSED`, and an election attempted on a garment whose `disposition_status` is not `AWAITING_ELECTION` is refused with `ERR_TRADE_IN_ITEM_NOT_REJECTED`. Once the deadline has passed, a `PlatformAdmin` applies `TRADEIN_DEFAULT_DISPOSITION`, which is `DONATE`, without any member election and without any `SHIPPING_FEE`; applying the default at or before the deadline is refused with `ERR_ELECTION_NOT_DUE`. Any trade-in transition attempted from an undeclared from-state on the addressed axis is refused with `ERR_TRADE_IN_INVALID_STATE`.

The batch reaches its own terminal state when no garment is left hanging: a `CREDITED` submission closes to `CLOSED` once **no** garment carries a `disposition_status` of `NONE` or `AWAITING_ELECTION`. The declared terminal pairings for a trade-in garment are `CREDITED` with `ABSORBED`, `CREDITED` with `DONATE`, `REJECTED` with `RETURN_TO_MEMBER`, `REJECTED` with `DONATE`, and `REJECTED` with `RECYCLE`; note that the only declared edge leaving `NONE` on the disposition axis is absorption, so a credited garment reaches a donated resting state only through the same election machinery a rejected garment uses.

### 3.10. Pausing the Subscription

Life interrupts subscriptions, so a household may pause. A pause request names a `start_at` and a `days_requested` count of whole days that must be at least `PAUSE_MIN_DAYS`, seven, and at most `PAUSE_MAX_DAYS_PER_PERIOD`, thirty; a value outside that range is refused with `ERR_PAUSE_DAYS_OUT_OF_RANGE` (HTTP 422). The request's `end_at` is derived as `start_at` plus `days_requested` days, and the pause window is **half-open**: it includes `start_at` and excludes `end_at`. A new request is born `REQUESTED` with `proration_credit_cents` at 0 and `denial_reason` null.

Activation is a `BillingAgent` or `PlatformAdmin` action and it is the gate through which the pause becomes real. It requires the request to be `REQUESTED` and the member to be `ACTIVE`. It requires that **no** shipment of that member is currently `IN_TRANSIT`, otherwise it is refused with `ERR_SHIPMENT_IN_TRANSIT_BLOCKS_PAUSE`, and that **no** box item of that member has a `days_late` greater than zero, otherwise it is refused with `ERR_UNIT_PAST_DUE_BLOCKS_PAUSE`. Two budgets are then checked. The member's `pause_days_used_period` — the sum of `days_requested` over the member's pauses whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `ENDED` and whose `start_at` falls inside the trailing `PAUSE_PERIOD_LENGTH_DAYS` window of 365 days — plus this request's `days_requested` must not exceed thirty, or the activation is refused with `ERR_PAUSE_PERIOD_CAP_EXCEEDED`. The member's `pause_days_used_lifetime`, the same sum over all time with no window at all, plus this request's `days_requested` must not exceed `PAUSE_MAX_DAYS_LIFETIME`, ninety, or the activation is refused with `ERR_PAUSE_LIFETIME_CAP_EXCEEDED`.

Finally, the new window must not overlap another `ACTIVE` or `ENDED` pause of the same member. Overlap is judged under **half-open** semantics: two windows conflict when the new `start_at` is strictly before the other's `end_at` and the other's `start_at` is strictly before the new `end_at`. **Adjacency is allowed** — a pause starting at exactly a prior pause's `end_at` does not conflict, because the prior interval excludes its own endpoint. A conflict is refused with `ERR_WINDOW_OVERLAP`, the same code the capsule rule uses even though the capsule rule closes both of its endpoints and therefore treats adjacency as a collision. The four `denial_reason` values a `PlatformAdmin` may record on an explicit denial — `SHIPMENT_IN_TRANSIT`, `UNIT_PAST_DUE`, `PERIOD_CAP` and `LIFETIME_CAP` — mirror those same four obstacles.

A successful activation moves the request `REQUESTED → ACTIVE`, moves the member `ACTIVE → PAUSED`, computes the proration credit, and grants every one of the member's credit entries whose `status` is `AVAILABLE` or `PARTIALLY_APPLIED` a `paused_days_extension` equal to `days_requested`. **Credit does not age during a pause.**

Proration is computed against the member's cycle whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `OPEN` and whose window overlaps the pause. The overlap is the whole number of days shared by the half-open pause window and the half-open cycle window running from the cycle's `start_at` up to but not including its `return_due_at`. Under the `THIRTY_DAY` variant the credit is the plan's `monthly_fee_cents` multiplied by the overlap days and divided by `PAUSE_PRORATION_DAY_BASIS`, a fixed 30-day basis that ignores the cycle's real length, rounded half up. Under the `ACTUAL_DAYS` variant the credit is the same product divided instead by `CYCLE_LENGTH_DAYS`, the cycle's real length, also 30, rounded half up. The two variants coincide numerically only because both divisors are currently 30; they are distinct rules, and the `THIRTY_DAY` basis is by definition the one that ignores cycle length. In both variants the credit is capped at the plan's `monthly_fee_cents` — a pause can zero a cycle's plan charge but can never turn it into a payment to the household.

Ending a pause is a member or admin action moving the request `ACTIVE → ENDED` and the member `PAUSED → ACTIVE`, and it re-derives the dates of every `SCHEDULED` cycle as §3.2 describes. A `REQUESTED` pause may instead be cancelled by the household or denied by a `PlatformAdmin` with a `denial_reason`; in both cases **no pause days are consumed**, which is why the period and lifetime budgets count only pauses that reached `ACTIVE` or `ENDED`. `ENDED`, `CANCELLED` and `DENIED` are the request's terminal states.

### 3.11. The Two Ledgers: Charges, Credits and How Money Is Applied

Every amount a household owes is a **charge ledger entry**, and every amount the household holds is a **credit ledger entry**. The two ledgers are shared by both pipelines, which is exactly how credit earned by sending garments in pays for garments taken out.

A charge entry carries `member_id`, an optional `cycle_id` that is null for charges not tied to a cycle, a `charge_type` drawn from `PLAN_CHARGE`, `PROTECTION_FEE`, `LATE_FEE`, `DAMAGE_FEE`, `SHIPPING_FEE`, `LOST_LIABILITY`, `BUYOUT_PRICE` and `SURCHARGE`, a gross `amount_cents`, a `credit_applied_cents` that starts at 0, and a `net_due_cents` that is `amount_cents` less `credit_applied_cents`, floored at zero. It is born `ASSESSED` and may reach `PARTIALLY_SETTLED`, `SETTLED` or `WAIVED`. A `BillingAgent` may raise one directly — a goodwill reversal, a manual liability, a corrected fee — by supplying `charge_type`, `amount_cents` and optionally `cycle_id`; such an entry is created `ASSESSED` with `net_due_cents` equal to `amount_cents`, the member's `outstanding_balance_cents` is recomputed, and the member may fall `ACTIVE → DELINQUENT` with the full cascade of §3.1. A `BillingAgent` may also waive an open charge by supplying a `waive_reason`, which moves it to `WAIVED` with `net_due_cents` set to 0, recomputes the outstanding balance, and may return the member `DELINQUENT → ACTIVE`. Waiving or crediting a charge whose `status` is already `SETTLED` or `WAIVED` is refused with `ERR_CHARGE_NOT_OPEN`.

A credit entry carries `member_id`, an optional `source_submission_id` that points at the trade-in batch that produced it and is null for operator grants, an `amount_cents` that is strictly greater than zero, a `remaining_cents` holding the unconsumed portion, a `granted_at` instant, an `expires_at` instant, a `paused_days_extension` that starts at 0, and a `revoke_reason` that starts null. It is born `AVAILABLE`, becomes `PARTIALLY_APPLIED` while `remaining_cents` is still above zero — a status it may re-enter many times — and becomes `APPLIED` when `remaining_cents` reaches zero. It may also be `EXPIRED` or `REVOKED`. Expiry is `granted_at` plus `CREDIT_EXPIRY_DAYS`, 180 days, plus `paused_days_extension` days; credit is usable **strictly before** that instant. Where no pause intervened the 180-day window simply runs on wall time; where a pause was activated while the entry stood `AVAILABLE` or `PARTIALLY_APPLIED`, the expiry instant moves out by exactly that pause's `days_requested`. A `BillingAgent` may expire an entry whose instant has passed, or revoke one with a `revoke_reason`; both recompute the member's `credit_balance_cents`. A `BillingAgent` may also grant goodwill credit directly by supplying `amount_cents` and an optional `reason`; such an entry is created `AVAILABLE` with `remaining_cents` equal to `amount_cents`, `granted_at` at the logical clock and `expires_at` derived — and **auto-apply deliberately does not fire for operator grants**, unlike a trade-in finalisation, which does auto-apply when the member's `auto_apply_credit` is true.

Applying credit is a `BillingAgent` or member action naming a single `charge_entry_id` together with `expected_member_version`. The waterfall is fully deterministic and its two orderings are pinned. **Charges** are taken in the order of their `charge_type` within `CREDIT_APPLICATION_ORDER` — `PLAN_CHARGE` first, then `PROTECTION_FEE`, then `LATE_FEE`, then `DAMAGE_FEE`, then `SHIPPING_FEE`, then `LOST_LIABILITY`, then `BUYOUT_PRICE` — with ties inside a bucket broken by `created_at` ascending. That bucket order is a total order and is **never** re-sorted by amount. **Credits** are taken oldest-expiry-first: entries whose `status` is `AVAILABLE` or `PARTIALLY_APPLIED` and whose `expires_at` the logical clock has not yet reached, ordered by `expires_at` ascending with ties broken by `created_at` ascending. For each charge in turn, credit entries are drawn in that order while the charge still has money due, each draw taking the smaller of the credit's `remaining_cents` and the charge's `net_due_cents`. The drawn amount is subtracted from `remaining_cents` and added to `credit_applied_cents`; a credit whose `remaining_cents` reaches zero becomes `APPLIED`, otherwise it becomes `PARTIALLY_APPLIED`. A charge whose `net_due_cents` reaches zero becomes `SETTLED`; a charge that received some credit but still owes becomes `PARTIALLY_SETTLED`; a charge that received nothing stays `ASSESSED`. The member's `outstanding_balance_cents` is recomputed afterwards and the member may return `DELINQUENT → ACTIVE`. An application against an entry that is expired, `APPLIED`, `EXPIRED` or `REVOKED` is refused with `ERR_CREDIT_EXPIRED`, and an application attempted while the member's `credit_balance_cents` is zero is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_CREDIT`.
