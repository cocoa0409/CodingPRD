# LTCH Admission & Length-of-Stay Compliance API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A long-term acute care hospital (LTCH) occupies an unusual position in the post-acute landscape: it accepts the patients an acute-care hospital can no longer manage — those on prolonged mechanical ventilation, those with multiple non-healing complex wounds — and it is paid under a prospective system whose every dollar is conditioned on documentation the hospital itself produces. Two compliance regimes press on the same admission. The first is patient-level: unless the patient arrived directly from a qualifying acute stay and either spent enough days in an intensive care unit or accrued enough ventilator hours inside the LTCH, the stay is paid at a *site-neutral* rate benchmarked to what a general hospital would have received, not at the full MS-LTC-DRG amount. The second is facility-level: if more than a quarter of a period's admissions arrive from any single referring hospital, the LTCH's status as an independent provider comes into question, and the admission mix must be monitored continuously rather than discovered at audit.

This API is the operational system of record for both regimes. It carries a referral from intake through screening against a versioned admission criteria set, admits qualifying patients into exactly one open cost reporting period, tracks covered length of stay against the DRG's average length of stay while netting out interruptions of stay, prices each discharge under **both** payment variants so that the difference is auditable, computes short-stay-outlier reductions and high-cost-outlier add-ons, scores each chart for denial risk, and issues physician documentation queries where the chart is thin. It also handles the awkward cases that make LTCH accounting genuinely hard: a patient who leaves for nine days and comes back, a patient who is discharged and readmitted three days later and must be folded back into the original stay with a combined length of stay and a reversed payment, and a period whose incrementally maintained ledger must reconcile to the penny against a full recomputation before it may be closed.

Two organizational constraints shape the design throughout. Money and clinical judgement are segregated: the analyst who prices a determination may never be the analyst who finalizes it, and the nurse who issues a documentation query may never be the nurse who closes it. And time is explicit: because every window, deadline and length-of-stay computation in this domain is a function of "now", the server reads a logical clock that test harnesses and back-office operators advance deliberately, never the wall clock.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Personnel, Headers and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to this API, without exception, carries two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role under which the user acts. There are exactly five roles, spelled exactly as follows: `ComplianceOfficer`, `IntakeCoordinator`, `UtilizationReviewNurse`, `AttendingPhysician`, and `RevenueIntegrityAnalyst`. If `X-User-Role` is absent, holds a value that is not one of these five spellings, or names a role that the requested endpoint does not admit, the request is refused with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` and HTTP status 403. Authorization is decided from the header alone; the API does not consult a user directory.

The `ComplianceOfficer` owns reference data and the fiscal envelope: referring hospitals, physicians and their credentials, MS-LTC-DRG rate entries, admission criteria sets, and cost reporting periods including their recomputation and close. The same role resolves denial-risk reviews and performs stay recombination, but may neither price payment nor file clinical records. An `IntakeCoordinator` receives referrals, screens them, supplies late data, withdraws them and admits qualifying patients — and does nothing else. A `UtilizationReviewNurse` documents the stay: interdisciplinary notes, interruptions and returns, the ventilator weaning plan and its trial records, discharge, and the issuing and closing of documentation queries; a nurse may not answer a query, because only a physician may. An `AttendingPhysician` closes weaning plans and answers documentation queries. A `RevenueIntegrityAnalyst` prices and re-prices discharged stays and moves determinations through the period ledger.

Identity semantics differ by role, and the difference matters. When `X-User-Role` is `AttendingPhysician`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Physician` row; ownership and credential checks on any resource that references a `Physician` compare the resource's physician foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`, and it is this value that gets stamped as `closed_by_physician_id` when a weaning plan is closed and as `answered_by_physician_id` when a query is answered. If `X-User-ID` for an `AttendingPhysician` request does not correspond to any `Physician` row, the request is refused with `ERR_PHYSICIAN_NOT_FOUND`. When `X-User-Role` is `ComplianceOfficer`, `IntakeCoordinator`, `UtilizationReviewNurse` or `RevenueIntegrityAnalyst`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a transition writes it — as `priced_by_user_id` and `finalized_by_user_id` on a `PaymentDetermination`, as `opened_by_user_id` and `resolved_by_user_id` on a `DenialRiskReview`, and as `issued_by_user_id` and `closed_by_user_id` on a `DocumentationQuery` — and compares it to those recorded values when enforcing segregation of duties, but never compares it to any entity's `id`.

Segregation of duties is enforced on three operations and produces `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` with HTTP status 403. The `X-User-ID` finalizing a determination must differ from that determination's `priced_by_user_id`. The `X-User-ID` voiding a determination that is currently `FINAL` must differ from that determination's `finalized_by_user_id`. And the `X-User-ID` closing a documentation query must differ from that query's `issued_by_user_id`. In all three cases both actors necessarily hold the same role, so only the identity distinguishes them. Where a cascade rather than a human produced a record, the recorded actor is the literal string `SYSTEM`, which no real `X-User-ID` may equal.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is a format violation and is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`.

Unless a specific endpoint's description states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. The one documented exception is the per-stay list of payment determinations, which is returned newest first. Every list response populates all four pagination fields in `meta`: `limit` and `offset` echo the effective values used for the query, `total_count` reports the number of records matching the filter before pagination, and `has_more` is true when records remain beyond the current page.

Filter query parameters, where an endpoint declares them, combine conjunctively — a request that supplies both `patient_id` and `status` returns only records matching both.

### 2.4. Record Identity, Audit Fields and the Logical Clock

Every persisted resource carries an `id` that is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix; clients never supply an `id`. Every resource also carries a `created_at` timestamp, an ISO 8601 UTC instant rendered with exactly three fractional digits, stamped from the logical clock at the moment the row was written.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that begins at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. Two entities are deliberately exempt because they are append-only and never change once written: a `DrgRateEntry` and an `IdtNote` carry only `id` and `created_at` — for those two, `updated_at` and `version` are absent from the response entirely. All twelve remaining entities (`ReferringHospital`, `CostReportingPeriod`, `Patient`, `Physician`, `AdmissionCriteriaSet`, `Referral`, `Stay`, `WeaningPlan`, `InterruptionEvent`, `PaymentDetermination`, `DenialRiskReview`, `DocumentationQuery`) carry the full set of four common properties.

Time in this system is a server-held logical instant, not the wall clock. Before any operator sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time. An operator in any of the five roles may advance the clock by supplying a `current_utc` instant; the response reports the new `current_utc` alongside the `previous_utc` it replaced. The clock is strictly monotonic: a `current_utc` that is not strictly after the current logical instant is refused with `ERR_CLOCK_NOT_MONOTONIC`. Advancing the clock mutates no entity — expiries and window lapses are evaluated lazily, at the moment the next relevant operation reads them. Every system-stamped datetime field in this specification (`closed_at`, `discharged_at`, `resumed_at`, `documented_at`, `returned_at`, `last_progression_at`, `issued_at`, `answered_at`) reads from this logical instant, as do all deadline and window comparisons.

### 2.5. Day Counting, Rounding and Monetary Precision

Two arithmetic conventions are used pervasively and must be implemented exactly as described, because nearly every threshold in the domain sits on one of them.

**Whole-day counting.** The day count between two instants is the difference between their UTC *calendar dates*, expressed in whole days; the later day is not itself counted. A discharge on the same calendar date as the admission therefore yields a day count of zero, and a discharge on the following calendar date yields one. Time-of-day within a date never contributes a partial day. This convention governs covered length of stay, interruption duration, the recombination window and the interdisciplinary-note week index.

**Half-up rounding.** All monetary amounts in this domain are integers of whole cents; there are no fractional cents anywhere in a stored field or a response. Where a monetary computation produces a fractional cent, the result is rounded to the nearest whole cent with ties rounded away from zero. Percentages — `share_pct`, `threshold_pct`, `discharge_payment_pct` — are stored and returned with exactly two decimal places, likewise rounded half up with ties away from zero. Ventilator hours are decimal numbers carried to one decimal place. Denial-risk scores are integers, produced by rounding the weighted total half up and then clamping the result into the inclusive range from 0 through 100.

The place where the rounding *order* is observable is the period ledger, and the rule is pinned. Each determination's `total_payment_cents` is rounded to whole cents when the determination is priced. A period's `total_payment_cents` is then the sum of those already-rounded integers — never a re-derivation from unrounded per-diem products with a single rounding applied at the end. The two orders genuinely differ: two short-stay standard determinations whose unrounded per-diem products are 1,384,615.3846 and 1,661,538.4615 round individually to 1,384,615 and 1,661,538 for a period total of 3,046,153, whereas summing the unrounded products first and rounding once yields 3,046,154. Only 3,046,153 is correct under this specification.

### 2.6. Stale-Version Protection on Superseding Writes

Two write operations in this API deliberately destroy work that a client may not have observed, and both are therefore guarded by an optimistic-concurrency check against the parent record's `version`.

The first is referral creation. A request to `POST /ltch/referrals` must carry `expected_patient_version` — a non-optional integer field holding the `version` of the target `Patient` as the intake coordinator last read it. Creating a referral supersedes any prior open referral for that patient, so a coordinator working from a stale view of the patient would silently cancel a referral it never saw. If `expected_patient_version` does not equal the patient's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PATIENT_VERSION` and HTTP status 412. On success the patient's `version` advances by 1, which invalidates every other in-flight referral create for that patient.

The second is re-pricing. A request to `POST /ltch/stays/{id}/payment-determinations` must carry `expected_stay_version` — a non-optional integer field holding the `version` of the target `Stay` as the analyst last read it. The write supersedes whatever `DRAFT` determination the stay already holds, including one produced by the discharge cascade or by an earlier re-price, so an analyst holding an out-of-date view of the stay would otherwise discard a determination it never observed. If `expected_stay_version` does not equal the stay's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_STAY_VERSION` and HTTP status 412. On success the stay's `version` advances by 1.

No other endpoint in this API accepts a version field. Requests to endpoints outside these two must not be expected to supply one, and supplying one is simply ignored rather than validated.

### 2.7. The Cost Reporting Period as Fiscal Envelope

Every stay belongs to exactly one cost reporting period, recorded on the stay as `cost_reporting_period_id` and selected once, at admission, as the single period whose `status` is `OPEN` and whose `start_date` and `end_date` bracket the admission date inclusively. At most one period may hold `status` `OPEN` at any moment, and periods may not overlap in calendar coverage.

A period's `status` is `OPEN` while it accrues admissions and payments and `CLOSED` once an operator closes it; `closed_at` records the logical instant of the close and is null while the period is `OPEN`. Once a period is `CLOSED`, every further mutation touching a stay in that period — discharge, re-pricing, finalization, void, and share recomputation — is refused with `ERR_PERIOD_CLOSED` (HTTP 409), as is an attempt to close an already-closed period. Admission is the one operation where the period is not yet known: when no period exists whose `status` is `OPEN` and whose date range covers the admission date, whether because none covers that date or because the covering period is already `CLOSED`, the admission is refused with `ERR_NO_OPEN_PERIOD` (HTTP 422) rather than `ERR_PERIOD_CLOSED`.

Opening a new period resets facility-wide monitoring counters: every `ReferringHospital` has its `admissions_in_period` reset to 0 and its `share_pct` reset to 0.00. A newly opened period begins with `total_admissions` 0, `total_payment_cents` 0 and `final_determination_count` 0.

### 2.8. Validation Precedence

Requests frequently violate more than one rule. The server evaluates its checks in a fixed order and the **first** violated rule determines the response; no later check is evaluated once one has fired. The complete order is:

1. **Payload shape.** The request body carries every non-optional field named in the endpoint's input contract, each field parses to its declared type, numeric fields fall inside their declared range, and every enum-typed field holds one of that field's declared literals — this covers `hospital_type`, `primary_payer`, `specialty`, `discipline`, `destination`, `discharge_disposition`, `query_type`, `credential_status` and the `outcome` values of the weaning and review operations. Violations return `ERR_VALIDATION` (400). Applies to all write endpoints.
2. **Authorization.** `X-User-Role` names a declared role and that role is permitted to invoke this endpoint. Violations return `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403). Applies to all endpoints.
3. **Path existence.** Every entity `id` appearing in the request path resolves to an existing row, returning the type-specific 404: `ERR_REFERRAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STAY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WEANING_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INTERRUPTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DETERMINATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_QUERY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_HOSPITAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PATIENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CRITERIA_SET_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_PHYSICIAN_NOT_FOUND`. Applies to every endpoint that has a path id.
4. **Body foreign keys.** Every foreign key supplied in the body resolves to an existing row — `patient_id`, `referring_hospital_id`, `drg_rate_id`, `physician_id`, `cost_reporting_period_id`, `interrupted_parent_stay_id`, `referral_id`, `stay_id` and `review_id` — returning `ERR_PATIENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_HOSPITAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DRG_RATE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PHYSICIAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PERIOD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STAY_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_REFERRAL_NOT_FOUND` as appropriate. Applies to all write endpoints that carry foreign keys.
5. **Optimistic concurrency.** Where the endpoint declares a version field, the supplied `expected_stay_version` or `expected_patient_version` equals the parent's current `version`, else `ERR_STALE_STAY_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_PATIENT_VERSION` (412). Applies to `POST /ltch/referrals` and `POST /ltch/stays/{id}/payment-determinations`.
6. **State legality.** The subject entity occupies a state from which the requested transition is declared: screening only from `RECEIVED`, information submission only from `INFO_REQUESTED`, admission only from `CRITERIA_MET`, interruption only from `ADMITTED`, return only from an `OPEN` interruption, trial recording only from `SBT_TRIAL`, `PROGRESSING` or `REGRESSED`, finalization only from `DRAFT`, answering only from `ISSUED`, query closure only from `ANSWERED`, and review resolution only from `OPEN` or `QUERIES_ISSUED`. Violations return `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409). There is one deliberate carve-out: an admission attempt on a referral whose `status` is `CRITERIA_NOT_MET` is **not** reported here, but deferred to rule 9 so the caller learns the clinical reason rather than a generic state error. Admission attempted on a referral in `RECEIVED`, `INFO_REQUESTED`, `ADMITTED`, `WITHDRAWN` or `SUPERSEDED` is still `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Applies to all transition endpoints.
7. **Period openness.** As described in §2.7, yielding `ERR_PERIOD_CLOSED` or, for admission, `ERR_NO_OPEN_PERIOD`. Applies to `POST /ltch/referrals/{id}/admit`, `POST /ltch/stays/{id}/discharge`, `POST /ltch/stays/{id}/payment-determinations`, `POST /ltch/payment-determinations/{id}/finalize`, `POST /ltch/payment-determinations/{id}/void` and `POST /ltch/cost-reporting-periods/{id}/recompute-referral-shares`.
8. **An effective criteria set exists.** At least one `AdmissionCriteriaSet` has `status` `ACTIVE` and an `effective_from` on or before the current logical date, else `ERR_NO_EFFECTIVE_CRITERIA_SET` (422). Applies to `POST /ltch/referrals/{id}/screen` and `POST /ltch/referrals/{id}/submit-info`.
9. **Referral eligibility.** Admission is permitted only when the referral's `qualifying_track` is `PMV` or `COMPLEX_WOUND`, else `ERR_REFERRAL_NOT_ELIGIBLE` (422). This is a patient-safety gate and deliberately outranks the duplicate-admission check below it, so a request that violates both returns this error rather than `ERR_PATIENT_ALREADY_ADMITTED`. Applies to `POST /ltch/referrals/{id}/admit`.
10. **No concurrent admission.** The patient has no `Stay` whose `clinical_status` is `ADMITTED` or `INTERRUPTED`, else `ERR_PATIENT_ALREADY_ADMITTED` (409). A readmission is legal only after the prior stay reached `DISCHARGED`; folding it back into the earlier stay is a separate recombination call. Applies to `POST /ltch/referrals/{id}/admit`.
11. **No occupancy overlap.** The new stay's occupancy interval must not overlap any existing non-recombined stay of the same patient, else `ERR_PATIENT_STAY_OVERLAP` (422). Applies to `POST /ltch/referrals/{id}/admit`.
12. **Referral-source breach override.** When the referral's `ReferringHospital` has `monitoring_state` `BREACHED`, the admit request must carry a non-empty `override_reason`; without it the admission is refused with `ERR_REFERRAL_SOURCE_BREACHED` (422). Applies to `POST /ltch/referrals/{id}/admit`.
13. **Admission instant sanity.** `admitted_at`, whether supplied or defaulted from the logical clock, must be greater than or equal to the referral's `prior_acute_discharge_at`, else `ERR_ADMISSION_BEFORE_ACUTE_DISCHARGE` (422); and must not be after the current logical instant, else `ERR_ADMISSION_IN_FUTURE` (422). Applies to `POST /ltch/referrals/{id}/admit`.
14. **Interruption instant sanity.** `left_at`, whether supplied or defaulted, must be greater than or equal to the stay's `admitted_at` and, when the stay has been resumed, greater than or equal to its `resumed_at`, else `ERR_INTERRUPTION_BEFORE_ADMISSION` (422); it must also not be after the current logical instant, in which case `ERR_ADMISSION_IN_FUTURE` (422) is returned. Applies to `POST /ltch/stays/{id}/interrupt`.
15. **Interruption buffer.** A new interruption must clear every earlier interruption of the same stay by at least 24 hours, else `ERR_INTERRUPTION_BUFFER_VIOLATION` (422). This temporal rule deliberately outranks the per-stay count cap below it. Applies to `POST /ltch/stays/{id}/interrupt`.
16. **Interruption count cap.** The stay has fewer than three interruption events, else `ERR_INTERRUPTION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (422). Applies to `POST /ltch/stays/{id}/interrupt`.
17. **Liberation progression.** Closing a weaning plan as `LIBERATED` requires `liberation_eligible` to be true, else `ERR_WEANING_PROGRESSION_INSUFFICIENT` (422). Applies to `POST /ltch/weaning-plans/{id}/close`.
18. **Ventilator-dependence attempts.** Closing a weaning plan as `VENT_DEPENDENT` requires `sbt_attempt_count` of at least 3, else `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_SBT_ATTEMPTS` (422). Applies to `POST /ltch/weaning-plans/{id}/close`.
19. **Physician credentialing.** The physician closing a weaning plan must have `specialty` `PULMONOLOGY` and `credential_status` `ACTIVE`; the physician answering a documentation query must have `credential_status` `ACTIVE` regardless of specialty. Violations return `ERR_PHYSICIAN_NOT_CREDENTIALED` (422). Applies to `POST /ltch/weaning-plans/{id}/close` and `POST /ltch/documentation-queries/{id}/answer`.
20. **Weaning plan closed before discharge.** A stay that has a `WeaningPlan` cannot be discharged while that plan is in `SBT_TRIAL`, `PROGRESSING` or `REGRESSED`, else `ERR_WEANING_PLAN_OPEN` (422). Applies to `POST /ltch/stays/{id}/discharge`.
21. **One live determination.** A stay may hold at most one `PaymentDetermination` whose `status` is `DRAFT` or `FINAL`; an attempt to add another while a `FINAL` one exists returns `ERR_DETERMINATION_EXISTS` (409). Applies to `POST /ltch/stays/{id}/payment-determinations`.
22. **Review resolved before money.** Finalization requires the stay's `DenialRiskReview` to be resolved, meaning its `status` is `CLEARED` or `ESCALATED`; a review still `OPEN` or `QUERIES_ISSUED` returns `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_RESOLVED` (422). Applies to `POST /ltch/payment-determinations/{id}/finalize`.
23. **No open queries before money.** Finalization requires zero `DocumentationQuery` rows in `status` `ISSUED` on the stay's review, else `ERR_OPEN_QUERIES_BLOCK_FINALIZE` (422). Because this fiscal gate sits below the `DRAFT`-state check at rule 6, finalizing an already-`FINAL` determination that also has an open query returns `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, not this error. Applies to `POST /ltch/payment-determinations/{id}/finalize`.
24. **Segregation of duties**, as described in §2.1, returning `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). Applies to `POST /ltch/payment-determinations/{id}/finalize`, `POST /ltch/payment-determinations/{id}/void` and `POST /ltch/documentation-queries/{id}/close`.
25. **Query response window.** A query may be answered only within 72 hours of its `issued_at`, measured inclusively; at exactly 72 hours the answer is accepted, one second later it is refused with `ERR_QUERY_RESPONSE_WINDOW_EXPIRED` (422) and the query is marked `EXPIRED`. Applies to `POST /ltch/documentation-queries/{id}/answer`.
26. **Recombination window and parentage.** `interrupted_parent_stay_id` must reference a *different* stay of the *same* patient whose `clinical_status` is `DISCHARGED` and whose `discharged_at` is no more than 3 whole days before the child stay's `admitted_at`; otherwise `ERR_RECOMBINATION_WINDOW_EXCEEDED` (422). Applies to `POST /ltch/stays/{id}/recombine`.
27. **No drafts at period close.** Closing a period requires zero `PaymentDetermination` rows in `status` `DRAFT` on its stays, else `ERR_PERIOD_HAS_DRAFT_DETERMINATIONS` (422). Applies to `POST /ltch/cost-reporting-periods/{id}/close`.
28. **Ledger reconciliation at period close.** Closing a period requires the incrementally maintained ledger to equal a full recompute: `total_payment_cents` equals the sum of the `total_payment_cents` of every `FINAL` determination on the period's stays, and `final_determination_count` equals their count. A disagreement returns `ERR_PERIOD_LEDGER_MISMATCH` (409). Applies to `POST /ltch/cost-reporting-periods/{id}/close`.
29. **Clock monotonicity.** The requested `current_utc` must be strictly after the current logical instant, else `ERR_CLOCK_NOT_MONOTONIC` (422). Applies to `POST /ltch/_clock/set_current_utc`.
30. **Uniqueness and non-overlap.** `DrgRateEntry.drg_code`, `ReferringHospital.npi`, `Physician.npi`, `Patient.mrn`, `AdmissionCriteriaSet.label` and `CostReportingPeriod.period_label` are each unique; a new criteria set's `effective_from` must be strictly after the `effective_from` of every `ACTIVE` set; a new period's `start_date` through `end_date` range must not overlap an existing period and only one period may be `OPEN` at a time. Violations return `ERR_DUPLICATE_KEY` (409). Applies to `POST /ltch/drg-rates`, `POST /ltch/referring-hospitals`, `POST /ltch/physicians`, `POST /ltch/patients`, `POST /ltch/admission-criteria-sets` and `POST /ltch/cost-reporting-periods`.
31. **Pinned criteria set still active.** A referral may not be admitted when its `pinned_criteria_set_id` references an `AdmissionCriteriaSet` whose `status` is `SUPERSEDED`; the referral must be re-screened first. Violations return `ERR_CRITERIA_SET_SUPERSEDED` (422). Because this check sits below the eligibility gate at rule 9, a `CRITERIA_NOT_MET` referral pinned to a superseded set still returns `ERR_REFERRAL_NOT_ELIGIBLE`. Applies to `POST /ltch/referrals/{id}/admit`.
32. **Fresh interdisciplinary documentation.** A tolerated spontaneous-breathing trial may be recorded only when the interdisciplinary team has documented the stay since the last progression, else `ERR_IDT_DOCUMENTATION_STALE` (422). A failed trial carries no documentation precondition. Applies to `POST /ltch/weaning-plans/{id}/record-sbt`.

### 2.9. Cascades, Compensating Reversals and Version Bumps

Several operations in this domain mutate more than one entity, and the secondary effects are as much part of the contract as the primary one. Discharge is the deepest: it drives a four-level cascade in which the stay is discharged, a payment determination is created, a denial-risk review is opened and scored, and — when that score lands in the highest band — a documentation query is auto-issued. Recombination runs the same chain backwards, voiding determinations, unwinding the period ledger, voiding the review and voiding its outstanding queries. Publishing a new admission criteria set flips every older active set to superseded, which in turn invalidates admission for referrals pinned to those sets. Opening a new cost reporting period resets every referring hospital's period counters.

Two invariants apply to all of it. First, every entity the cascade touches has its `updated_at` refreshed and, where it carries one, its `version` incremented by 1 — a cascade is not a silent write. Second, a compensating reversal must restore the ledger exactly: voiding a determination that was `FINAL` decrements the period's `total_payment_cents` by that determination's `total_payment_cents` and decrements `final_determination_count` by 1, returning both to their pre-finalization values, because integer cent addition is exact and the per-determination rounding already happened.

### 2.10. Nested Path Resolution

Several collections are addressed beneath a parent resource — a stay's interruptions, a stay's interdisciplinary notes, a stay's weaning plan, a stay's payment determinations, a stay's denial-risk reviews, and a review's documentation queries. Path parameters are validated strictly left to right. The parent `id` is resolved first, and if it does not exist the parent's own not-found error is returned (for example `ERR_STAY_NOT_FOUND` for an unknown stay, `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_FOUND` for an unknown review) without any attempt to interpret the remainder of the path or the body. Only once the parent resolves is the child segment considered.

Where a child is addressed by its own top-level path — an interruption, a determination, a query, a weaning plan — the child `id` is resolved directly, and the parent relationships it carries are read from the stored row rather than from the URL.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Reference Data: Referral Sources, People, Rates and Criteria

Before a single referral can be taken, a `ComplianceOfficer` must seed the reference world. Registering a referring acute-care hospital records its `name` (1 to 120 characters, required), its `npi` (exactly 10 digits, unique across all referring hospitals), and a `hospital_type` that is either `ACUTE_CARE` or `CRITICAL_ACCESS`. An optional `is_colocated` flag, defaulting to false, marks hospitals that host the LTCH as a hospital-within-a-hospital. Two of those inputs determine how much of the LTCH's admission mix this hospital is allowed to supply: a hospital's `threshold_pct` is 50.00 when `is_colocated` is true **or** its `hospital_type` is `CRITICAL_ACCESS`, and 25.00 otherwise. A newly registered hospital starts with `admissions_in_period` at 0, `share_pct` at 0.00 and `monitoring_state` at `COMPLIANT`; the live posture is readable at any time and by every role.

Patients are registered by either a `ComplianceOfficer` or an `IntakeCoordinator` with an `mrn` (1 to 24 characters, unique across patients), a `full_name` (1 to 120 characters), a `birth_date` in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, and a `primary_payer` drawn from `MEDICARE_FFS`, `MEDICARE_ADVANTAGE` or `COMMERCIAL`. A new patient's `open_referral_count` begins at 0. Because referral creation depends on the patient's `version`, an intake coordinator normally reads the patient record first — that read is the observation the concurrency check later compares against.

Physicians are registered with an `npi` (exactly 10 digits, unique across physicians), a `full_name` (1 to 120 characters) and a `specialty` from `PULMONOLOGY`, `INFECTIOUS_DISEASE`, `WOUND_CARE` or `INTERNAL_MEDICINE`. Every new physician begins with `credential_status` `ACTIVE`. A `ComplianceOfficer` may later toggle that field to `SUSPENDED` or back to `ACTIVE` by submitting the desired `credential_status`; a suspended physician can no longer close weaning plans or answer documentation queries.

MS-LTC-DRG rate entries are loaded immutably. Each entry names a `drg_code` (3 to 5 characters, unique), a `relative_weight` (strictly greater than zero, at most four decimal places), an `alos_days` figure (the DRG's average length of stay, from 1 to 120 days), a `standard_federal_rate_cents` amount (strictly positive whole cents), an `ipps_comparable_per_diem_cents` amount (strictly positive) and an `ipps_comparable_full_cents` amount (strictly positive). There is deliberately no way to update a rate entry: determinations snapshot the values they used instead, so a later rate correction never silently rewrites history. The `alos_days` figure does double duty — it sets the short-stay-outlier threshold and it is the divisor of the LTCH per-diem computation.

Admission criteria sets are versioned, and only the newest one is live. Publishing a set names a `label` (1 to 40 characters, unique), an `effective_from` calendar date from which the set may be pinned by screening, and four clinical thresholds: `pmv_min_vent_hours` (from 1 to 1000) and `pmv_min_icu_days` (from 0 to 60) govern the prolonged-mechanical-ventilation track, while `wound_min_stage` (from 1 to 4) and `wound_min_count` (from 1 to 20) govern the complex-wound track. The new set's `effective_from` must be strictly later than the `effective_from` of every set currently `ACTIVE`. On publication, the new set takes `status` `ACTIVE` and every previously `ACTIVE` set flips to `SUPERSEDED` — which is exactly why a referral that pinned an older set can no longer be admitted until it is re-screened. Any role may read a criteria set and observe whether a newer publication has superseded it.

Cost reporting periods are opened with a `period_label` (1 to 40 characters, unique), a `start_date` and an `end_date`, where the `end_date` must be strictly later than the `start_date`. The mechanics of the period as a fiscal envelope are set out in §2.7, and its reconciliation and close in §3.9.

### 3.2. Referral Intake and the Screening Loop

An `IntakeCoordinator` opens the workflow by creating a referral. The payload names the referred patient through `patient_id`, the source hospital through `referring_hospital_id`, and the anticipated MS-LTC-DRG through `drg_rate_id`; alongside those it carries `expected_patient_version` as described in §2.6. Five further inputs are optional at creation because referring hospitals rarely send a complete packet on the first fax: `vent_hours_at_referral` (ventilator hours accrued during the immediately preceding acute stay, at least zero when present), `icu_days_at_referral` (ICU days in that same preceding stay, at least zero when present), `wound_stage` (the highest wound stage documented, from 0 to 4 when present), `wound_count` (the number of qualifying complex wounds, from 0 to 20 when present) and `prior_acute_discharge_at` (the UTC discharge instant of the preceding acute stay). Any of the five that is omitted is stored as null and treated as missing.

A newly created referral enters `RECEIVED` with `qualifying_track` `NONE`, `screened_count` 0, `data_complete` false and `pinned_criteria_set_id` null. Creation also does something destructive, which is what §2.6's version check protects: any other referral of the same patient whose `status` is `RECEIVED`, `INFO_REQUESTED` or `CRITERIA_MET` is flipped to `SUPERSEDED` (a terminal status), the patient's `version` advances by 1, and the patient's `open_referral_count` settles at 1 — one decrement for the superseded referral, one increment for the new one, so the net stays at 1. That is why `open_referral_count` never exceeds 1 in practice.

Screening a `RECEIVED` referral is the coordinator's next act, and it does three things in a fixed order. **First, it selects and pins a criteria set.** The candidates are every `AdmissionCriteriaSet` whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `effective_from` falls on or before the current logical calendar date. Those candidates are ranked by `effective_from` in descending order, and where two share the same `effective_from`, by `created_at` in descending order; the first candidate in that ranking is the winner and its `id` is written to the referral's `pinned_criteria_set_id`. If there are no candidates at all, screening is refused with `ERR_NO_EFFECTIVE_CRITERIA_SET` and nothing is pinned.

**Second, it evaluates completeness.** Exactly five inputs are required for a screening verdict: `vent_hours_at_referral`, `icu_days_at_referral`, `wound_stage`, `wound_count` and `prior_acute_discharge_at`. The names of whichever of those five are null are written into `missing_fields` as an array of strings, and `data_complete` is set to true only when `missing_fields` is empty. If the data is incomplete, the referral moves `RECEIVED → INFO_REQUESTED`, `qualifying_track` is left at `NONE`, and evaluation stops there.

**Third, when the data is complete, it resolves a track.** The prolonged-mechanical-ventilation criterion is met when `vent_hours_at_referral` is at least the pinned set's `pmv_min_vent_hours` **and** `icu_days_at_referral` is at least its `pmv_min_icu_days`. The complex-wound criterion is met when `wound_stage` is at least the pinned set's `wound_min_stage` **and** `wound_count` is at least its `wound_min_count`. Precedence is fixed and not symmetric: the ventilation criterion is evaluated first, so a referral meeting both criteria resolves to `qualifying_track` `PMV`, and only a referral that fails the ventilation criterion but meets the wound criterion resolves to `COMPLEX_WOUND`. A referral meeting neither takes `qualifying_track` `NONE`. The referral then moves `RECEIVED → CRITERIA_MET` when the track is `PMV` or `COMPLEX_WOUND`, or `RECEIVED → CRITERIA_NOT_MET` when the track is `NONE`. Ventilator hours are compared as decimal numbers; the remaining comparisons are on integers. Every screening evaluation, whatever its verdict, increases `screened_count` by 1.

`CRITERIA_NOT_MET` is terminal: the patient no longer holds an open referral, so `Patient.open_referral_count` is decreased by 1 on that transition.

A referral sitting in `INFO_REQUESTED` is completed by submitting the late data. The submission accepts the same five optional inputs — `vent_hours_at_referral`, `icu_days_at_referral`, `wound_stage`, `wound_count`, `prior_acute_discharge_at` — and whichever are supplied overwrite the referral's stored values. The evaluation then re-runs in full, and here is the subtlety: **the criteria set is re-selected against the current logical date, not against the date of the original screen.** A referral that bounced through `INFO_REQUESTED` while a newer criteria set took effect is therefore judged by the newer set, and `pinned_criteria_set_id` is re-pinned accordingly. Three outcomes are possible and each increases `screened_count` by 1. If at least one required input is still null, the referral takes the self-loop `INFO_REQUESTED → INFO_REQUESTED` with `missing_fields` recomputed — a referral may bounce through this state any number of times. If the data is now complete and a track matches, the referral moves `INFO_REQUESTED → CRITERIA_MET`. If the data is complete and no track matches, the referral moves `INFO_REQUESTED → CRITERIA_NOT_MET`, `qualifying_track` is set to `NONE`, and `Patient.open_referral_count` is decreased by 1. Submitting information on a referral in any status other than `INFO_REQUESTED` is refused as an illegal state transition, and submission is subject to the same effective-criteria-set precondition as the initial screen.

A coordinator may abandon a referral at any point before admission. Withdrawal is legal from `RECEIVED`, from `INFO_REQUESTED` and from `CRITERIA_MET`; it moves the referral to the terminal status `WITHDRAWN` and decreases `Patient.open_referral_count` by 1, settling it at 0.

Referrals are readable individually with their full screening outcome, and listable with optional filters on `patient_id`, `referring_hospital_id`, `drg_rate_id`, `pinned_criteria_set_id` and `status`, alongside `limit` and `offset`.

### 3.3. Admission: Eligibility, Occupancy and the Referral-Mix Ceiling

Admitting a referral is the single most heavily guarded operation in the API, because it is the point at which a clinical judgement becomes a billable stay. The transition is `CRITERIA_MET → ADMITTED` on the referral, and it is performed by an `IntakeCoordinator`. Only two inputs are accepted, both optional. `admitted_at` supplies the admission instant as an ISO 8601 UTC value with three fractional digits; when omitted it defaults to the current logical instant, and when supplied it may be backdated to record a late data entry. `override_reason` is a free-text justification whose role is explained below.

The preconditions, in the precedence order established in §2.8, are these. The referral must be in `CRITERIA_MET`; an attempt from `RECEIVED`, `INFO_REQUESTED`, `ADMITTED`, `WITHDRAWN` or `SUPERSEDED` is an illegal state transition. Exactly one cost reporting period must have `status` `OPEN` with the admission date falling inside its `start_date` through `end_date` range inclusively; if none does, admission fails with `ERR_NO_OPEN_PERIOD`. The referral's `qualifying_track` must be `PMV` or `COMPLEX_WOUND` — this is the deferred verdict for a `CRITERIA_NOT_MET` referral, and it fires before the duplicate-admission check so that a doubly-invalid request names the clinical cause. The patient must hold no `Stay` in `clinical_status` `ADMITTED` or `INTERRUPTED`. The new stay's occupancy interval must not overlap any existing non-recombined stay of the same patient. The referral source must not be in breach, or the request must justify the exception. `admitted_at` must be on or after the referral's `prior_acute_discharge_at` — an LTCH stay cannot begin before the acute discharge that feeds it — and must not be after the current logical instant. Finally, the referral's `pinned_criteria_set_id` must reference a set whose `status` is still `ACTIVE`, not `SUPERSEDED`.

Patient occupancy deserves a precise statement because backdating makes true overlaps reachable. A stay occupies the **half-open** interval that begins at its `admitted_at` and runs up to but does not include its `discharged_at`. A new admission whose `admitted_at` is exactly equal to a prior stay's `discharged_at` is adjacent, not overlapping, and is allowed; an admission one second earlier than that instant overlaps and is refused with `ERR_PATIENT_STAY_OVERLAP`. A stay whose `discharged_at` is null occupies its interval out to positive infinity, so nothing may be admitted after it while it remains open. Stays that were folded into an earlier stay by recombination are excluded from this overlap test entirely.

**The referral-mix ceiling.** No single referring hospital may supply too large a share of the LTCH's admissions, and the system tracks that share continuously rather than at audit time. The denominator is the period's `total_admissions`, which counts every `Stay` in the period whose `counts_toward_admission_threshold` is true. The numerator for each hospital is its `admissions_in_period`, which counts the stays in the open period whose referral pointed at that hospital and whose `counts_toward_admission_threshold` is true. A hospital's `share_pct` is its `admissions_in_period` divided by the period's `total_admissions`, multiplied by 100 and rounded half up to two decimal places; when the denominator is zero, `share_pct` is stored as 0.00. Its `threshold_pct` is recomputed at the same time from the rule in §3.1 — 50.00 for a co-located or critical-access source, 25.00 otherwise.

`monitoring_state` then follows from three tests applied in order. If the period's `total_admissions` is fewer than 4, the mix is too thin to judge and `monitoring_state` is `COMPLIANT` regardless of the computed share; note that `share_pct` is still stored in that case, so a hospital may legitimately show a share of 100.00 while sitting at `COMPLIANT`. Otherwise, if `share_pct` is **strictly greater** than `threshold_pct`, `monitoring_state` becomes `BREACHED` — a share of exactly 25.00 against a threshold of 25.00 is not a breach. Otherwise, if `share_pct` is strictly greater than `threshold_pct` reduced by a warning margin of 5.00 percentage points, `monitoring_state` becomes `WARNING`. In every other case it is `COMPLIANT`.

When the referring hospital of the referral being admitted has `monitoring_state` `BREACHED`, admission is refused with `ERR_REFERRAL_SOURCE_BREACHED` unless the request supplies a non-empty `override_reason`. Supplying one lets the admission proceed, records `admitted_with_override` as true on the resulting stay and stores the justification in `override_reason`; admissions that did not need an override carry `admitted_with_override` false and `override_reason` null.

A successful admission creates a `Stay`. The new stay's `patient_id` and `referral_id` are copied from the admitting referral, its `cost_reporting_period_id` is the selected open period, its `clinical_status` is `ADMITTED`, its `payment_review_status` is `PENDING`, its `admitted_at` is the supplied value or the current logical instant, and its `counts_toward_admission_threshold` is true. Its `carried_los_days` starts at 0, its `interruption_days_total` at 0, its `interruption_count` at 0, and its `interrupted_parent_stay_id`, `resumed_at`, `discharged_at`, `covered_los_days`, `sso_threshold_days`, `short_stay_outlier_applied` and `discharge_payment_pct` all start null. The admission also does four things beyond the stay itself: the referral's `status` becomes `ADMITTED`, `Patient.open_referral_count` is recomputed to 0, the period's `total_admissions` is increased by 1, and the referring hospital's `admissions_in_period` is increased by 1. Because the denominator moved, `share_pct`, `threshold_pct` and `monitoring_state` are then recomputed for every referring hospital with a non-zero `admissions_in_period` plus any hospital whose counter just changed. A single admission can therefore push a *different* hospital into `WARNING` or out of `BREACHED`.

A `ComplianceOfficer` may also re-run that whole recomputation on demand against an `OPEN` period, an idempotent sweep that rewrites `share_pct`, `threshold_pct` and `monitoring_state` for every referring hospital from the period's stored `total_admissions`. The sweep exists precisely so that the incrementally maintained counters can be checked against a full recompute — the two must always agree.

Stays are readable individually with both status dimensions, their length-of-stay counters and their `version` for concurrency purposes, and listable with optional filters on `patient_id`, `referral_id`, `cost_reporting_period_id`, `interrupted_parent_stay_id`, `clinical_status` and `payment_review_status`, alongside `limit` and `offset`.

### 3.4. Interdisciplinary Documentation and the Ventilator Weaning Protocol

Two kinds of clinical record accumulate against a live stay, and they interlock: interdisciplinary-team notes are what license progression on the weaning protocol, and the weaning protocol's cumulative ventilator hours are what decide which payment variant the stay eventually receives.

**Interdisciplinary notes.** Either a `UtilizationReviewNurse` or an `AttendingPhysician` may file a note against a stay. A note names its `discipline` — one of `PHYSICIAN`, `NURSING`, `RESPIRATORY_THERAPY`, `CASE_MANAGEMENT`, `NUTRITION` or `PT_OT` — carries `note_text` of 1 to 2000 characters, and may set the optional `goals_documented` flag, which defaults to false and records whether measurable weekly goals appear in the note. The system stamps `documented_at` from the logical clock and computes `week_index` as the whole-day count from the stay's `admitted_at` to `documented_at`, divided by 7 and rounded down, so the admission week is week 0. Notes are strictly append-only: there is no update and no delete, and a filed note never changes. A stay's notes are listable, optionally filtered by `discipline`. Filing a note in a discipline that was previously absent is the ordinary way to lower a stay's denial-risk score on the next rescore.

**Opening the weaning plan.** A `UtilizationReviewNurse` opens at most one ventilator weaning plan per stay; a second attempt on the same stay is refused with `ERR_WEANING_PLAN_EXISTS`. Two optional inputs are accepted: `protocol_label`, a 1-to-40-character protocol identifier that defaults to `STANDARD_SBT`, and `vent_hours_total`, which seeds the plan's cumulative ventilator-hour counter and defaults to 0. That default is deliberate and consequential — the ventilator hours accrued in the *prior acute stay*, recorded on the referral as `vent_hours_at_referral`, are **not** carried over into the plan. Only hours accrued inside the LTCH accumulate here. A new plan carries `stay_id`, enters `status` `SBT_TRIAL` with `current_step` 0, `sbt_attempt_count` 0, `consecutive_failed_sbt` 0, `max_tolerated_sbt_hours` 0, `liberation_eligible` false, and `last_progression_at`, `closed_at` and `closed_by_physician_id` all null.

**Recording spontaneous-breathing trials.** Each trial record supplies three required values: an `outcome` of either `TOLERATED` or `FAILED`, the trial's duration in hours as `sbt_hours`, and `vent_hours_since_last`, the ventilator hours accrued since the previous record. Every record, whatever its outcome, increases `sbt_attempt_count` by 1 and adds `vent_hours_since_last` to `vent_hours_total`. The cumulative total is therefore the create-time seed plus the sum of `vent_hours_since_last` across every trial record on the plan, failed trials included; there is no decay and regression never subtracts ventilator hours. Hours are carried to one decimal place.

A `TOLERATED` record is a progression. It increases `current_step` by 1, resets `consecutive_failed_sbt` to 0, sets `max_tolerated_sbt_hours` to the larger of its previous value and this trial's `sbt_hours`, stamps `last_progression_at` with the current logical instant, and recomputes `liberation_eligible` as true exactly when `current_step` has reached at least 2. From `SBT_TRIAL` the plan moves to `PROGRESSING`; from `PROGRESSING` it takes the self-loop back to `PROGRESSING`; from `REGRESSED` it moves back to `PROGRESSING`. All three tolerated paths carry the identical effects. A tolerated record is, however, gated on fresh interdisciplinary documentation: the stay must have at least one note with `discipline` `RESPIRATORY_THERAPY` **and** at least one note with `discipline` `PHYSICIAN` whose `documented_at` is on or after the plan's `last_progression_at`, or — before the first progression, while `last_progression_at` is still null — on or after the plan's `created_at`. Missing either note yields `ERR_IDT_DOCUMENTATION_STALE`. Because each tolerated record re-stamps `last_progression_at`, the documentation window restarts every time: two progressions require two rounds of respiratory-therapy and physician notes.

A `FAILED` record carries no documentation precondition and its effect depends on the running count. It increases `sbt_attempt_count` by 1, increases `consecutive_failed_sbt` by 1, and adds `vent_hours_since_last` to `vent_hours_total`. If the resulting `consecutive_failed_sbt` is still below 2, the plan simply stays where it was — `SBT_TRIAL` self-loops to `SBT_TRIAL`, `PROGRESSING` self-loops to `PROGRESSING` — and `current_step` is untouched. If the resulting `consecutive_failed_sbt` reaches 2 or more, the plan regresses: `current_step` is reduced by 1 but never below 0, `liberation_eligible` is recomputed against the 2-step requirement, and the plan moves `SBT_TRIAL → REGRESSED` or `PROGRESSING → REGRESSED`. A plan that fails twice before ever progressing regresses exactly like one that had progressed, with its `current_step` simply staying at 0. A plan already in `REGRESSED` takes the self-loop `REGRESSED → REGRESSED` on every further failed record, decreasing `current_step` by 1 each time with the same floor at 0. Because a tolerated record resets the consecutive counter, a plan may oscillate `PROGRESSING → REGRESSED → PROGRESSING` indefinitely. Recording a trial on a plan already in `LIBERATED` or `VENT_DEPENDENT` is an illegal state transition.

**Closing the plan.** Only an `AttendingPhysician` closes a weaning plan, supplying an `outcome` of either `LIBERATED` or `VENT_DEPENDENT`. Both closes stamp `closed_at` from the logical clock and write the acting physician's `X-User-ID` into `closed_by_physician_id`, and both require the acting physician to have `specialty` `PULMONOLOGY` and `credential_status` `ACTIVE`. A `LIBERATED` close is reachable only from `PROGRESSING` and only when `liberation_eligible` is true, meaning `current_step` has reached at least 2; otherwise it is refused with `ERR_WEANING_PROGRESSION_INSUFFICIENT`. A `VENT_DEPENDENT` close is reachable from `SBT_TRIAL`, from `PROGRESSING` and from `REGRESSED`, and requires `sbt_attempt_count` of at least 3; a plan with fewer attempts is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_SBT_ATTEMPTS`. Both `LIBERATED` and `VENT_DEPENDENT` are terminal. A closed plan no longer blocks discharge, which is the practical reason the close exists.

Weaning plans are readable individually and listable with optional filters on `stay_id`, `closed_by_physician_id` and `status`, alongside `limit` and `offset`.

### 3.5. Interruptions of Stay and Recombination of Readmissions

Patients leave. Some go to an acute-care hospital for a procedure, some to a rehabilitation or skilled-nursing facility, some briefly home. Whether that absence interrupts the stay or ends it depends entirely on how long it lasts, and the tolerance depends on where the patient went.

A `UtilizationReviewNurse` records the departure, moving the stay `ADMITTED → INTERRUPTED` and creating an `InterruptionEvent` carrying that `stay_id`. The request names a required `destination` — one of `ACUTE_CARE`, `IRF`, `SNF` or `HOME` — and may supply an optional `left_at`. When `left_at` is omitted it defaults to the current logical instant; when supplied it may be backdated to record an interruption discovered late, but it must be on or after the stay's `admitted_at` and, for a stay that has been resumed by recombination, on or after its `resumed_at`, and it must not be after the current logical instant. The destination sets the event's `window_days` directly: 9 days for `ACUTE_CARE`, 27 days for both `IRF` and `SNF`, and 3 days for `HOME`. The new event enters `status` `OPEN` with `returned_at`, `interruption_days` and `within_window` all null, and the stay's `interruption_count` increases by 1.

Three constraints limit interruptions. The stay must currently be in `ADMITTED`; interrupting a `DISCHARGED`, `INTERRUPTED` or `RECOMBINED` stay is an illegal state transition. The stay must carry fewer than three interruption events — and every event counts toward that cap regardless of its `status`, so an `EXPIRED` one still occupies a slot. And a new interruption must clear every earlier interruption of the same stay by at least 24 hours. That buffer is measured treating interruption intervals as **closed on both ends**, from `left_at` through `returned_at` inclusive, so a `left_at` falling exactly 24 hours after a previous event's `returned_at` is rejected while one second later is accepted. The buffer check is evaluated before the count cap, so a request that violates both returns `ERR_INTERRUPTION_BUFFER_VIOLATION` rather than `ERR_INTERRUPTION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`.

Recording the patient's return is where the window is judged. The nurse's return call takes no body. The system stamps `returned_at` with the current logical instant and computes `interruption_days` as the whole-day count from `left_at` to `returned_at`. The comparison is inclusive: `within_window` is true when `interruption_days` is less than or equal to `window_days`, so an absence of exactly 9 days falls inside a 9-day window while 10 days does not.

When the return is inside the window, the event moves `OPEN → RETURNED` with `within_window` true, its `interruption_days` are added to the stay's `interruption_days_total`, and the stay moves `INTERRUPTED → ADMITTED`. A stay may leave and re-enter `ADMITTED` this way up to three times.

When the return is outside the window, the event moves `OPEN → EXPIRED` with `within_window` false and the stay is discharged retroactively rather than resumed: the stay moves `INTERRUPTED → DISCHARGED`, its `discharged_at` is set to the interruption's `left_at` — **not** to the return instant — and its `discharge_disposition` is stamped as `INTERRUPTION_EXPIRED`, a value only the system may write. An expired event's `interruption_days` are deliberately **not** added to `interruption_days_total`, because those days now fall outside the stay entirely. The covered length of stay, the short-stay-outlier threshold and its flag are then computed, and the full discharge cascade of §3.6 runs. Because no `estimated_cost_cents` is ever supplied on a window-expiry discharge, that field stays null and the high-cost outlier add-on comes out at zero for such stays. Returning an interruption whose `status` is not `OPEN` is an illegal state transition, and the current logical instant must be on or after `left_at`.

A stay's interruption events are listable, optionally filtered by `status`.

**Recombination.** A readmission that falls close on the heels of a discharge is, for payment purposes, not a second stay at all but a continuation of the first. Only a `ComplianceOfficer` performs the fold, and the operation is addressed to the *child* — the later stay — naming the earlier stay through `interrupted_parent_stay_id`. The named parent must be a different stay, belong to the same patient, sit in `clinical_status` `DISCHARGED`, belong to the same cost reporting period, and have a `discharged_at` no more than 3 whole days before the child's `admitted_at`; at exactly 3 days the fold is accepted, at 4 days it is refused with `ERR_RECOMBINATION_WINDOW_EXCEEDED`. A parent id that resolves to no stay at all yields `ERR_STAY_NOT_FOUND`. The child must itself be in `clinical_status` `ADMITTED` with `payment_review_status` `PENDING` and must hold no interruption event still in `OPEN`.

The fold has two halves. The **child** moves `ADMITTED → RECOMBINED`, which is terminal — no further clinical transition is possible on it — records the parent's id in `interrupted_parent_stay_id`, and sets `counts_toward_admission_threshold` to false. Because the child stopped counting as an admission, the child's referring hospital has its `admissions_in_period` decreased by 1 and the period's `total_admissions` is decreased by 1; and because the denominator moved, `share_pct`, `threshold_pct` and `monitoring_state` are recomputed for every referring hospital exactly as at admission. A recombination can therefore lift a hospital *out* of `BREACHED`.

The **parent** is reopened, moving `DISCHARGED → ADMITTED`. Its `carried_los_days` is frozen at the whole-day count from its `admitted_at` to its `discharged_at` — the covered days already banked from the pre-recombination segment — its `resumed_at` is stamped with the child's `admitted_at`, and both its `discharged_at` and its `discharge_disposition` are cleared to null. Its `payment_review_status` reverts to `PENDING`. Reverting to `PENDING` unwinds the money: every `PaymentDetermination` on the parent whose `status` is `DRAFT` or `FINAL` becomes `VOIDED` with `void_reason` set to the literal `RECOMBINED`, and for each one that was `FINAL`, the period's `total_payment_cents` is decreased by that determination's `total_payment_cents` and its `final_determination_count` is decreased by 1. Voiding the determinations in turn voids the compliance work built on them: the parent's `DenialRiskReview` becomes `VOIDED`, and every `DocumentationQuery` on that review whose `status` is `ISSUED` or `ANSWERED` becomes `VOIDED` as well. Both stays have their `version` increased by 1. The reopened parent is discharged again later, and that second discharge produces a combined length of stay via `carried_los_days`.

One consequence is worth stating explicitly: a review that had already reached `CLEARED` or `ESCALATED` is **not** immune to this. Because finalization requires a resolved review, every `FINAL` determination that recombination has to unwind necessarily hangs off a resolved review — so `CLEARED → VOIDED` and `ESCALATED → VOIDED` are real transitions, and `VOIDED` is the only genuinely terminal state of the review lifecycle.

### 3.6. Discharge, Covered Length of Stay and the Four-Level Cascade

Discharge is performed by a `UtilizationReviewNurse` and takes two required inputs: a `discharge_disposition` drawn from `HOME`, `HOME_HEALTH`, `SNF`, `IRF`, `ACUTE_TRANSFER` or `EXPIRED` — the seventh literal, `INTERRUPTION_EXPIRED`, is reserved for the system and may not be supplied by a client — and `estimated_cost_cents`, the estimated cost of the stay in whole cents, which must be at least 0 and which drives the high-cost outlier. The stay must be in `clinical_status` `ADMITTED`, no interruption event on it may still be `OPEN`, its cost reporting period must be `OPEN`, and if the stay has a weaning plan that plan must already have reached `LIBERATED` or `VENT_DEPENDENT`; a plan still in `SBT_TRIAL`, `PROGRESSING` or `REGRESSED` blocks the discharge with `ERR_WEANING_PLAN_OPEN`. The transition is `ADMITTED → DISCHARGED` and `discharged_at` is stamped from the logical clock.

**Covered length of stay** is computed at every discharge, whether the ordinary one or the retroactive one driven by a lapsed interruption window. The segment start is the stay's `resumed_at` when that field is non-null, and otherwise its `admitted_at`. The segment length is the whole-day count from that segment start to `discharged_at`. The stay's `covered_los_days` is then `carried_los_days` plus that segment length, minus `interruption_days_total`. Since `carried_los_days` is 0 for any stay that was never reopened, and `interruption_days_total` sums `interruption_days` only over this stay's events whose `status` is `RETURNED` — open and expired events contribute nothing — a simple uninterrupted stay yields exactly the whole-day count from admission to discharge.

Alongside it, `sso_threshold_days` is computed as five-sixths of the `alos_days` of the DRG in force, rounded **down** to a whole number and never rounded to nearest. The precise fraction used for that multiplication is 0.8333333333333334. The stay's `short_stay_outlier_applied` flag is then true exactly when `covered_los_days` is less than or equal to `sso_threshold_days`. Before discharge all three of `covered_los_days`, `sso_threshold_days` and `short_stay_outlier_applied` are null.

The "DRG in force" means the `DrgRateEntry` identified by the pricing request's `override_drg_rate_id` when one was supplied, and otherwise the `DrgRateEntry` named by the admitting referral's `drg_rate_id`.

**The four-level cascade.** A single discharge call produces four levels of effect, each caused by the previous one.

*Level one* is the discharge itself: `clinical_status` becomes `DISCHARGED`, `discharged_at` is stamped, `discharge_disposition` and `estimated_cost_cents` are stored, the three length-of-stay fields above are computed, and the stay's `version` increases by 1.

*Level two* follows because the stay became `DISCHARGED`: a `PaymentDetermination` is created for it with `status` `DRAFT` and `priced_by_user_id` set to the literal `SYSTEM` rather than to any human `X-User-ID`. That determination is priced by the full dual-variant calculation of §3.7, its `recalculation_reason` is null because no analyst asked for it, and its `discharge_payment_pct` is mirrored onto the stay's own `discharge_payment_pct` field. The stay's second status dimension advances `PENDING → PRICED`. This second dimension is genuinely independent: `payment_review_status` moves while `clinical_status` stays at `DISCHARGED`.

*Level three* follows because a determination now exists: a `DenialRiskReview` is opened for the stay with `status` `OPEN`, `opened_by_user_id` recording the user whose action triggered the cascade (or `SYSTEM` where no human acted), `resolved_by_user_id` and `resolution_note` null, and its `as_documented_score`, `missing_discipline_count`, `answered_query_count`, `effective_score` and `risk_band` computed as set out in §3.8. At most one live review exists per stay.

*Level four* follows only when that review's `risk_band` came out `HIGH`: a `DocumentationQuery` with `query_type` `IDT_PARTICIPATION` is auto-issued against the review with `issued_by_user_id` set to the literal `SYSTEM`, and the review moves `OPEN → QUERIES_ISSUED`. The same auto-issue applies to both cascade entry points — the ordinary discharge and the retroactive discharge driven by a lapsed interruption window. When the band is `MODERATE` or `LOW`, no query is issued and the review stays `OPEN`.

### 3.7. Payment Determination: Dual-Variant Pricing, Short-Stay Outliers and the High-Cost Add-On

Every determination stores **both** payment variants, always, whichever one is selected. That is the whole point of the design: an auditor comparing what the LTCH was paid against what a general hospital would have received must be able to read both numbers off the same row without re-running anything.

**Re-pricing.** Beyond the determination the discharge cascade creates automatically, a `RevenueIntegrityAnalyst` may re-price a discharged stay at any time while its period is `OPEN`. The request carries `expected_stay_version` as described in §2.6, a required `recalculation_reason` explaining why the stay is being re-priced, and an optional `override_drg_rate_id` naming a different `DrgRateEntry` to price against — useful when a coding review changed the DRG. An `override_drg_rate_id` that resolves to no rate entry yields `ERR_DRG_RATE_NOT_FOUND`. Before the new row is written, any existing determination on that stay whose `status` is `DRAFT` is flipped to `VOIDED` with `void_reason` set to the literal `SUPERSEDED`. If the stay already holds a determination whose `status` is `FINAL`, the re-price is refused with `ERR_DETERMINATION_EXISTS`, because a stay may hold at most one determination in `DRAFT` or `FINAL` at a time. The new determination is written with `status` `DRAFT`, `priced_by_user_id` set to the acting analyst's `X-User-ID`, its `stay_id` copied from the path, the stay's `payment_review_status` set to `PRICED`, and the stay's `version` increased by 1. The stay's `DenialRiskReview` is rescored afterwards, because the new pricing changed the inputs the score reads.

**Value snapshots.** Pricing freezes the inputs it used onto the determination, so a later rate load or a later interruption cannot retroactively change a priced amount. The snapshot fields are `drg_code_used`, `relative_weight_used`, `alos_days_used`, `ipps_per_diem_used_cents`, `ipps_full_used_cents` — copied from the DRG in force — and `covered_los_days_used`, copied from the stay's `covered_los_days` at pricing time.

**The full standard amount.** The full MS-LTC-DRG amount, `full_standard_amount_cents`, is the DRG's `standard_federal_rate_cents` multiplied by its `relative_weight`, with that single product rounded half up to whole cents.

**Step one: variant selection from patient-level criteria.** Three facts decide the variant. The stay's admitting referral must record a prior acute discharge, meaning its `prior_acute_discharge_at` is non-null. Then at least one of two intensity criteria must hold: the referral's `icu_days_at_referral` is at least 3, **or** the stay has a weaning plan whose `vent_hours_total` has reached at least 96 ventilator hours — four full days of ventilation accrued inside the LTCH, inclusive at the boundary, so 95.0 hours does not qualify and 96.0 does. A stay with no weaning plan at all contributes no ventilator qualification. When the referral records a prior acute discharge and at least one intensity criterion holds, `payment_variant` is `STANDARD_LTCH_PPS`; in every other case it is `SITE_NEUTRAL`.

**Step two: the standard base.** When the stay's `short_stay_outlier_applied` is false, the standard base is simply `full_standard_amount_cents`. When it is true, the base is reduced to the **smallest of three candidates**. The first candidate is the smaller of `full_standard_amount_cents` and the LTCH per-diem component, where that component is `full_standard_amount_cents` divided by the DRG's `alos_days` and multiplied by the stay's `covered_los_days`, with exactly **one** rounding — half up to whole cents — applied to the whole product; the per-diem quotient must never be rounded before it is multiplied by the length of stay. The second candidate is the DRG's `ipps_comparable_per_diem_cents` multiplied by the `covered_los_days` and then by 1.20, rounded half up to whole cents — that is, 120 percent of the IPPS-comparable per diem for the days actually delivered. The third candidate is the DRG's `ipps_comparable_full_cents` unchanged. The standard base is the minimum of those three.

**Step three: the site-neutral base.** The site-neutral base is the smaller of the DRG's `ipps_comparable_full_cents` and the stay's `estimated_cost_cents`, with a null `estimated_cost_cents` treated as 0. The site-neutral variant ignores the LTCH per-diem and the 120-percent IPPS cap entirely, which is precisely why the same stay data yields a different number under each variant.

**Step four: the high-cost outlier add-on.** The add-on is computed once per variant, from that variant's base, and its evaluation order is pinned. First, a threshold is formed as the variant's base payment plus a fixed-loss amount of 3,000,000 cents (30,000 dollars). Second, an excess is formed as the stay's `estimated_cost_cents`, with null treated as 0, minus that threshold. Third, if the excess is less than or equal to zero the add-on is 0; otherwise the add-on is 0.80 multiplied by the excess, rounded half up to whole cents. The add-on must never re-enter its own threshold: reading the threshold as base plus add-on plus fixed loss and solving it as a fixed point is forbidden. For a base of 6,000,000 cents against an estimated cost of 12,000,000 cents, the pinned order yields an add-on of 2,400,000 cents; the fixed-point reading would yield 1,333,333, and only 2,400,000 is correct.

**Step five: storage and selection.** `standard_variant_amount_cents` is the standard base plus the add-on computed from the standard base. `site_neutral_variant_amount_cents` is the site-neutral base plus the add-on computed from the site-neutral base. Both are stored on every determination regardless of which variant was selected. Then `base_payment_cents` is the standard base when `payment_variant` is `STANDARD_LTCH_PPS` and the site-neutral base when it is `SITE_NEUTRAL`; `outlier_payment_cents` is the add-on computed from `base_payment_cents`; and `total_payment_cents` is `base_payment_cents` plus `outlier_payment_cents`, in whole cents.

**The discharge payment percentage.** Finally, `discharge_payment_pct` is `base_payment_cents` divided by `full_standard_amount_cents`, multiplied by 100 and rounded half up to two decimal places. Note that the numerator deliberately **excludes** `outlier_payment_cents`, so the percentage reads exactly 100.00 for a full-length standard stay with no short-stay reduction and never exceeds 100.00 for such a stay. The determination's own `short_stay_outlier_applied` mirrors whether `covered_los_days_used` was less than or equal to the stay's `sso_threshold_days`. The percentage is mirrored onto the stay's `discharge_payment_pct`, which is null until a determination exists.

**Finalization.** A determination moves `DRAFT → FINAL` when a `RevenueIntegrityAnalyst` accepts it, and the request takes no body. The determination must be in `DRAFT`, its stay must be in `clinical_status` `DISCHARGED`, the stay's cost reporting period must be `OPEN`, the stay's denial-risk review must already be resolved to `CLEARED` or `ESCALATED`, no `DocumentationQuery` on that review may still be in `status` `ISSUED`, and the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the determination's `priced_by_user_id`. On success `finalized_by_user_id` records the acting `X-User-ID`, the stay's `payment_review_status` moves `PRICED → FINALIZED`, the period's `total_payment_cents` is increased by this determination's `total_payment_cents`, and the period's `final_determination_count` is increased by 1. Note that a determination the discharge cascade priced carries `priced_by_user_id` `SYSTEM`, so any analyst may finalize it.

**Voiding.** A `RevenueIntegrityAnalyst` may reverse a determination in either `DRAFT` or `FINAL`, supplying a required `void_reason`. The stay's period must be `OPEN`. Voiding a `DRAFT` determination is `DRAFT → VOIDED` and touches no ledger; when no other determination on the stay remains in `DRAFT` or `FINAL`, the stay's `payment_review_status` reverts `PRICED → PENDING` and its `discharge_payment_pct` is cleared to null. Voiding a `FINAL` determination is `FINAL → VOIDED`, requires that the acting `X-User-ID` differ from the determination's `finalized_by_user_id`, sets the stay's `payment_review_status` back to `PENDING` and clears `discharge_payment_pct`, and unwinds the ledger exactly: the period's `total_payment_cents` is decreased by this determination's `total_payment_cents` and its `final_determination_count` is decreased by 1, returning both to their pre-finalization values. `VOIDED` is terminal. The `void_reason` field holds the analyst's supplied reason for a human void, the literal `SUPERSEDED` for a draft displaced by a re-price, and the literal `RECOMBINED` for one unwound by a recombination; it is null on a determination that was never voided.

A stay's determinations are listable newest-first with an optional `status` filter, and each determination is readable individually with both variant amounts, the add-on and the percentage.

### 3.8. Denial-Risk Review, Documentation Queries and Physician Responses

Every discharged stay gets exactly one live denial-risk review, and its score is what stands between the stay and its money. The score is a weighted count of documentation and payment red flags, and it is computed in two variants from the same raw total.

**The raw total.** Four disciplines are treated as mandatory on every stay: `PHYSICIAN`, `NURSING`, `RESPIRATORY_THERAPY` and `CASE_MANAGEMENT`. The review's `missing_discipline_count` is how many of those four have zero interdisciplinary notes on the stay — a number from 0 through 4, counting notes in any state since notes are immutable. A weaning gap counts as present, contributing 1, in exactly two situations: the stay has no weaning plan at all, or the stay's plan reached `VENT_DEPENDENT` with `max_tolerated_sbt_hours` still at 0, meaning the patient never tolerated a single trial. Otherwise the weaning gap contributes 0. Note that a low attempt count cannot itself be the gap, because a `VENT_DEPENDENT` close already requires at least 3 attempts. Three further flags come from the stay's determination — the one whose `status` is `DRAFT` or `FINAL`, of which at most one exists. A short-stay-outlier flag contributes 1 when that determination's `short_stay_outlier_applied` is true and 0 otherwise. A site-neutral flag contributes 1 when its `payment_variant` is `SITE_NEUTRAL` and 0 otherwise. And a length-of-stay excess is the determination's `covered_los_days_used` minus its `alos_days_used`, floored at 0 so that a stay shorter than the DRG's average contributes nothing.

The raw total is then 8 points per missing discipline, plus 15 points if the weaning gap is present, plus 20 points if the short-stay-outlier flag is set, plus 10 points if the site-neutral flag is set, plus half a point per day of length-of-stay excess.

**The two variants.** The `as_documented_score` is the raw total rounded half up to an integer and then clamped into the inclusive range 0 through 100; it is the risk implied by the chart as filed and ignores query responses entirely. The `query_adjusted_score` starts from the same raw total, subtracts 12 points for each answered query, then rounds half up and clamps into the same 0-through-100 range. The count of answered queries, `answered_query_count`, counts the review's `DocumentationQuery` rows whose `status` is `ANSWERED` or `CLOSED`; queries in `ISSUED`, `EXPIRED` or `VOIDED` earn no credit. `query_adjusted_score` is null until a rescore observes a non-zero `answered_query_count`. The review's `effective_score` is the `query_adjusted_score` when `answered_query_count` is greater than 0, and the `as_documented_score` otherwise. Identical chart data can therefore yield a lower effective score once queries come back, which is exactly how a review drops out of the highest band.

**The bands.** `risk_band` is `HIGH` when `effective_score` is at least 70, `MODERATE` when `effective_score` is at least 40 but below 70, and `LOW` below 40.

**Rescoring.** A `ComplianceOfficer` may re-run the score at any time; the request takes no body. Rescoring recomputes both variants, refreshes `effective_score`, `risk_band`, `missing_discipline_count` and `answered_query_count`, and additionally performs a lazy expiry sweep: every `DocumentationQuery` on the review whose `status` is `ISSUED` and whose `due_at` has already passed the current logical instant moves `ISSUED → EXPIRED`. An expired query stops blocking payment finalization but earns no answered-query credit, so it keeps the review out of `CLEARED` unless the rescore also drops the band below `HIGH`. When the review was in `QUERIES_ISSUED` and no query remains in `ISSUED` after the sweep, the rescore returns the review to `OPEN` — the cycle `OPEN → QUERIES_ISSUED → OPEN` may repeat for as many rounds of querying as the case needs.

**Issuing a query.** A `UtilizationReviewNurse` issues a physician documentation query from a review, supplying a required `query_type` — one of `VENT_WEANING_RATIONALE`, `WOUND_STAGING`, `IDT_PARTICIPATION` or `SITE_NEUTRAL_JUSTIFICATION` — and a required `prompt_text` of 1 to 1000 characters posing the question. The review must be in `OPEN` or `QUERIES_ISSUED` and the stay must be in `clinical_status` `DISCHARGED`. The new query carries `denial_risk_review_id` naming the issuing review and `stay_id` copied from that review for direct filtering, enters `status` `ISSUED` with `issued_at` stamped from the logical clock and `due_at` computed as `issued_at` plus 72 hours — three full days — and records the acting nurse's `X-User-ID` as `issued_by_user_id`. Its `response_text`, `answered_at`, `answered_by_physician_id` and `closed_by_user_id` all start null. A review in `OPEN` moves `OPEN → QUERIES_ISSUED`; a review already in `QUERIES_ISSUED` stays there.

**Answering a query.** Only an `AttendingPhysician` answers, supplying a required `response_text` of 1 to 2000 characters. The query must be in `ISSUED`, the current logical instant must be at or before `due_at` — measured inclusively, so an answer at exactly 72 hours is accepted and one at 72 hours and one second is refused with `ERR_QUERY_RESPONSE_WINDOW_EXPIRED` and the query flips to `EXPIRED` — and the acting physician must have `credential_status` `ACTIVE`, regardless of specialty. On success the query moves `ISSUED → ANSWERED`, `answered_at` is stamped from the logical clock, `answered_by_physician_id` records the acting physician's `X-User-ID`, the review's `answered_query_count` increases by 1, and the denial-risk score is recomputed, so the `risk_band` may fall.

**Closing a query.** A `UtilizationReviewNurse` closes an answered query as adequate; the request takes no body. The query must be in `ANSWERED` and the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the query's `issued_by_user_id`. The transition is `ANSWERED → CLOSED`, `closed_by_user_id` records the acting nurse's `X-User-ID`, and the query keeps its answered-query credit in the score. `CLOSED`, `EXPIRED` and `VOIDED` are all terminal for a query.

**Resolving the review.** Only a `ComplianceOfficer` resolves, supplying a required `outcome` of either `CLEARED` or `ESCALATED` and a required `resolution_note`. A `CLEARED` resolution is permitted only from `OPEN`, only when `risk_band` is not `HIGH`, and only when no query on the review is still in `ISSUED`; a high-risk chart cannot simply be waved through. An `ESCALATED` resolution is permitted from either `OPEN` or `QUERIES_ISSUED` and carries no band restriction — escalation is the honest exit for a chart that cannot be cleared. Both outcomes record the acting officer's `X-User-ID` as `resolved_by_user_id` and store the `resolution_note`. Resolving the review is what unblocks finalization of the stay's determination.

Reviews are listable per stay with an optional `status` filter and readable individually with both score variants, the effective score and the band. Queries are readable individually and listable with optional filters on `denial_risk_review_id`, `stay_id`, `answered_by_physician_id` and `status`, alongside `limit` and `offset`.

### 3.9. Period Reconciliation and Close

Closing a cost reporting period is the compliance officer's final act, and it is a reconciliation, not a formality. Two gates guard it, evaluated in that order. First, no stay in the period may still hold a `PaymentDetermination` in `status` `DRAFT`; an unfinished pricing means the period's total is provisional, and the close is refused with `ERR_PERIOD_HAS_DRAFT_DETERMINATIONS`. Second, the incrementally maintained ledger must equal a full recomputation: the period's `total_payment_cents` must equal the sum of the `total_payment_cents` of every determination whose `status` is `FINAL` and whose stay belongs to this period, and its `final_determination_count` must equal the count of those same determinations. Determinations in `DRAFT` or `VOIDED` contribute nothing to either figure. A disagreement is refused with `ERR_PERIOD_LEDGER_MISMATCH`. The order of summation is irrelevant because integer cent addition is exact — which is precisely why the per-determination rounding of §2.5 must happen first.

There is also a third precondition, structural rather than fiscal: every stay in the period whose `clinical_status` is `DISCHARGED` must carry a determination whose `status` is `FINAL`. A discharged stay with no accepted price cannot be left inside a closed period.

On a successful close the period moves `OPEN → CLOSED`, `closed_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and `share_pct`, `threshold_pct` and `monitoring_state` are recomputed one final time for every referring hospital so that the closed period's admission mix is frozen at its audited values. `CLOSED` is terminal. From that moment, every mutation touching a stay, determination, note or interruption of that period is refused with `ERR_PERIOD_CLOSED`, as is a second close attempt on the same period. A period's admission counters and payment ledger remain readable after the close by a `ComplianceOfficer`, a `RevenueIntegrityAnalyst` or an `IntakeCoordinator`.
