# Privatized Military Family Housing API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Privatized military family housing sits in an uncomfortable seam. The homes are owned and operated by a private project company, the rent is paid out of a service member's Basic Allowance for Housing through a payroll allotment rather than a cheque, and the government housing office at the installation retains an oversight role that can override the landlord. When a family's roof leaks, when mold is found behind a bathroom wall, when a set of permanent-change-of-station orders lands three weeks before a lease was due to renew, three organisations with different incentives all have a legitimate claim on the same record. The system specified here is the shared record.

This document defines the API for a privatized family housing management platform operated on the Balfour Beatty Communities model. It covers the full tenancy arc: joining an installation's waitlist and earning a position on it through an orders-derived control date, being matched to a unit whose bedroom count and designated rank category fit the family, signing a lease whose rent is derived from a published BAH rate table, walking the unit at move-in and acknowledging the Tenant Bill of Rights, living in it through maintenance requests and habitability escalations and environmental hazard responses, disputing a charge with the landlord and, if necessary, with the installation housing office, and finally terminating — sometimes early, sometimes with a statutory right that waives the fee. Layered over the tenancy arc are two ops-run checkpoints the project company depends on: the monthly allotment run that posts rent charges to each active lease's ledger, and the metrics run that scores occupancy performance and computes the owner's incentive fee net of habitability penalties.

Two properties of the domain drive most of the difficulty in this specification, and implementers should read the rest of the document with them in mind. First, almost every meaningful operation cascades: a certified hazard assessment can, in a single request, grade a severity, spawn a remediation work order, place a unit under hold, suspend a lease's rent collection, post a negative ledger row, and thereby move a fiscal-year reinvestment account. Second, almost every quantity here is money or a deadline, so the order in which steps are evaluated and the point at which a value is rounded are part of the contract, not an implementation detail. Where this document pins an evaluation order, that order is authoritative.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request other than the logical-clock test hook must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role the user is acting in. If either header is absent or holds an empty string, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the six declared role names, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If both headers are present and well formed but the named role does not appear in the authorised-role list for the endpoint being called, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

The six roles are `PlatformAdmin`, `PropertyManager`, `ServiceMember`, `MaintenanceTech`, `EnvironmentalSpecialist`, and `InstallationHousingOffice`.

A `PlatformAdmin` is the project company's corporate systems operator: it loads installations, BAH rate tables, unit inventory, sponsor records and fiscal-year reinvestment accounts, and it holds the compensating power to void a hazard case. A `PropertyManager` is the on-site project manager: it runs the offer and allotment cycles, writes leases, conducts move-in inspections, dispatches maintenance, authorises displacement lodging and proposes dispute resolutions. A `ServiceMember` is the sponsor — the uniformed resident or applicant — who applies for housing, accepts or declines offers, signs the inspection and the Bill of Rights, submits and reopens work orders, reports hazards, gives termination notice and opens disputes. A `MaintenanceTech` is the assigned technician who starts and completes work orders and may raise new ones discovered on site, but never assigns, escalates, prices or leases. An `EnvironmentalSpecialist` is the certified mold and lead assessor who grades hazard severity, certifies clearance and may authorise displacement lodging. An `InstallationHousingOffice` user is the government housing office at the installation: it maintains sponsor and orders records, recertifies waitlist applications, co-runs the offer cycle, escalates habitability failures and renders the binding decision on an escalated resident dispute.

Identity semantics differ between the resident role and the five operator roles, and this distinction governs every ownership check in the system. When `X-User-Role` is `ServiceMember`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `ServiceMember` record; ownership checks on any resource that references a `ServiceMember` compare the resource's sponsor reference to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, a resident may act only on a waitlist application whose `service_member_id` equals `X-User-ID`, a lease whose `service_member_id` equals `X-User-ID`, a dispute case whose `service_member_id` equals `X-User-ID`, a move-in inspection whose lease belongs to that sponsor, a work order whose `lease_id` resolves to a lease belonging to that sponsor, or a dependent whose `service_member_id` equals `X-User-ID`. A resident acting on another sponsor's resource is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). That list is exhaustive, and ownership is enforced only on the single-resource reads and the action endpoints that address one such row. It is deliberately **not** enforced on top-level collection list endpoints, which return every matching row to a resident caller unfiltered, and it is not enforced on the sponsor read, the sponsor's dependent listing, the hazard-case read or the displacement-lodging read — those resources are readable by any authorised role.

When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `PropertyManager`, `MaintenanceTech`, `EnvironmentalSpecialist`, or `InstallationHousingOffice`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it wherever a transition in this specification stamps an actor — as `conducted_by_user_id` on a `MoveInInspection`, as `assessed_by_user_id` or `cleared_by_user_id` on a `HazardCase`, as `proposed_by_user_id` or `decided_by_user_id` on a `DisputeCase` — but it never compares such an identifier to any entity's `id`, and no ownership rule constrains these five roles. Two of those stamped operator identifiers are compared against each other, however: see the segregation-of-duties principle in §2.9.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the uppercase letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. It is the one timestamp in the system taken from real wall time rather than from the logical clock of §2.4, so setting the clock does not change it and it never matches a resource's `created_at`.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong. Message text is advisory only and is never part of the contract; the `error_id` and the HTTP status are.
- Transport-level failures use this same envelope. A request to a path this document does not define returns `error_id` `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` with HTTP 404, a request using a method the path does not support returns `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` with HTTP 405, and a body that is not parseable JSON, that parses to something other than a JSON object, or that arrives without a JSON content type returns `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` with HTTP 400. An unexpected server fault returns `error_id` `ERR_INTERNAL` with HTTP 500. A trailing slash is not significant: `/installations` and `/installations/` address the same endpoint.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1, and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0, with no upper bound. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) rather than being silently clamped. Either parameter supplied as an empty string is treated as omitted and takes its default; either supplied as anything that is not a plain base-ten integer — including an integer padded with surrounding whitespace — is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending unless a section of this document pins a different order for a specific collection. Because `created_at` is stamped from the logical clock, many rows share an identical value, and every such tie is broken by insertion sequence — the order in which the rows were written — using a server-internal counter that is never exposed in any response. Row identifiers are never part of any ordering. Filter parameters are validated with the request shape: a filter supplied as an empty string is treated as omitted, while a filter value outside its declared enumeration, or a non-integer where an integer is expected, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than yielding an empty page. Boolean filters accept `true`, `True`, `TRUE` and `1` for true and `false`, `False`, `FALSE` and `0` for false, and reject anything else. The `meta` block of every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`; `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before the page window is applied, and `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond the current window.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps, and the Logical Clock

Every persisted resource carries an `id` that is a server-minted UUID string with no type prefix and no embedded meaning; clients never supply it. Every resource also carries a `created_at` timestamp, an ISO 8601 UTC instant rendered with exactly three fractional digits, stamped once when the row is inserted and never changed.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits refreshed on every mutation of that row, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on each mutation. Two entities are deliberate exceptions because they are immutable once written: a `BahRate` row and an `AllotmentTransaction` row carry only `id` and `created_at`. Neither has `updated_at` and neither has `version`. A published BAH rate is never corrected in place — a corrected rate is a new row for a new `effective_year` — and a ledger row is never corrected in place either: a correction is a new, oppositely signed row.

All time in this domain is read from a server-side **logical clock**, never from wall time. Deadlines, SLA windows, offer expiry, notice periods, escalation thresholds, abatement day counts, period-month boundaries and future-period checks all consult the logical clock. Before the clock has ever been set it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; this startup default is mandatory and the server must never fall back to real wall-clock time. An administrative test-driving capability sets the clock to a supplied `current_utc` instant and modifies no business entity; it is the single endpoint that performs no authentication and no role check at all, so it succeeds with no headers, with unknown headers, or under any role, it requires `current_utc` to be present and parseable or answers `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and it answers HTTP 200 with an empty `data` object rather than with any resource. Fields stamped from the clock — `reported_at`, `first_response_at`, `completed_at`, `escalated_at`, `assessed_at`, `posted_at`, `opened_at`, `tenant_signature_at`, `bill_of_rights_ack_at`, `last_offer_run_at`, `last_metrics_run_at`, `offer_expires_at`, `response_due_at`, `completion_due_at`, `resolution_due_at` — all take their value from that clock.

Date-only fields are ISO calendar dates in `YYYY-MM-DD` form and carry no time component: `start_date`, `end_date`, `actual_end_date`, `scheduled_date`, `last_lease_end_date`, `date_of_birth`, `pcs_orders_date`, `expected_arrival_date`, `expected_return_date`, `pcs_report_date`, `termination_notice_date`, `termination_effective_date`, and `effective_control_date` are all of this kind, and each must be supplied as exactly ten characters in that form. Datetime-valued request fields are accepted in ISO 8601 form with either a trailing `Z` or a numeric UTC offset and are normalised to UTC before use; a datetime carrying no zone designator at all is read as already being UTC. Billing periods are expressed as `period_month` strings in `YYYY-MM` form, which must be exactly seven characters with a month between 01 and 12.

### 2.5. The Validation Ladder

Every request is evaluated against a single, globally ordered ladder of forty-six checks. The ladder is walked in ascending priority order and **the first violated rung determines the response**; the remaining rungs are not evaluated. No later rung may pre-empt an earlier one, so when a request violates two rules the caller always sees the lower-numbered one. Implementers should treat this ordering as part of the contract: a semantically correct implementation that reports a 422 where the ladder demands a 409 is wrong.

**Rungs 1 to 3 — authentication and ownership.** Rung 1 requires both `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` to be present and `X-User-Role` to be one of the six declared roles, failing with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401); it applies to every endpoint except the logical-clock setter, which is unauthenticated. Rung 2 requires the acting role to appear in the called endpoint's authorised-role list, failing with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED_ROLE` (403); it applies to every endpoint except the logical-clock setter, which performs no role check either. Rung 3 applies to the resident-facing single-resource reads and action endpoints enumerated in §2.1 and requires, when the acting role is `ServiceMember`, that `X-User-ID` equal the sponsor identifier owning the addressed resource — the application's `service_member_id`, the lease's `service_member_id`, the dispute's `service_member_id`, the sponsor of the lease behind an addressed move-in inspection or work order, or the sponsoring member of a dependent being created — failing with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). Rung 3 never fires on a top-level collection list endpoint, on the sponsor read or the sponsor's dependent listing, or on the hazard-case or displacement-lodging read. When the sponsor reference the comparison needs cannot be obtained — the body field carrying it is absent or is not a string, or the addressed row does not exist — rung 3 is skipped and evaluation falls through, so a resident naming a row that does not exist sees the shape or existence error rather than an ownership error.

**Rung 4 — request shape.** Before any lookup, the request body and query string are checked for structural validity: every required field present, every enum-typed value a declared member of its enumeration, every integer-typed value an actual integer, monetary fields non-negative integers unless this document explicitly declares them signed, every ISO date and datetime parseable, and `limit` within 1 through 100 with `offset` at least 0. A violation is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). A field supplied explicitly as JSON `null` is treated exactly as if it had been omitted: a required field so supplied fails as missing, and an optional field so supplied silently takes its declared default and clears nothing. String fields must be non-empty once surrounding whitespace is stripped; boolean fields must be JSON booleans rather than strings or numbers, and a JSON boolean is never accepted where an integer or a number is required. An absent request body is read as an empty object, so an endpoint whose every body field is optional accepts a request carrying no body at all, while an endpoint with any required field rejects one. Cross-field bounds this document states between two body fields — such as a BAH rate's `utility_allowance_cents` having to be strictly below its `monthly_rate_cents` — are rung-4 checks as well, and are therefore reported before any entity is resolved. Fields this document does not declare for an endpoint are ignored rather than rejected.

**Rungs 5 to 15 — existence, resolved left to right.** Path parameters are resolved before body references, and body references are resolved in the order the entity families are listed here. Rung 5 requires the addressed `Installation` to exist and covers installation reads, the installation's BAH rate listing, the waitlist offer run, the allotment run, and any request body carrying `installation_id`; failure is `ERR_INSTALLATION_NOT_FOUND` (404). Rung 6 requires the addressed `HousingUnit` to exist and covers unit reads, unit status changes, and any body carrying `housing_unit_id`; failure is `ERR_HOUSING_UNIT_NOT_FOUND` (404). Rung 7 requires the addressed `ServiceMember` to exist and covers sponsor reads, the sponsor's dependent listing, orders recording, and any body carrying `service_member_id`; failure is `ERR_SERVICE_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND` (404). Rung 8 requires the addressed `WaitlistApplication` to exist and covers application reads, every application action, and lease creation carrying `application_id`; failure is `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_FOUND` (404). Rung 9 requires the addressed `Lease` to exist and covers lease reads, the lease's sub-collection listings, every lease action, and any body carrying `lease_id`; failure is `ERR_LEASE_NOT_FOUND` (404). Rung 10 requires the addressed `MoveInInspection` to exist and covers inspection reads, deficiency logging and tenant acknowledgment; failure is `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_FOUND` (404). Rung 11 requires the addressed `WorkOrder` to exist and additionally requires any supplied `parent_work_order_id` or `source_work_order_id` to resolve; it covers work-order reads, every work-order action, work-order submission carrying `parent_work_order_id`, and hazard-case reporting carrying `source_work_order_id`; failure is `ERR_WORK_ORDER_NOT_FOUND` (404). Rung 12 requires the addressed `HazardCase` to exist and covers hazard-case reads, every hazard-case action, and lodging authorisation carrying `hazard_case_id`; failure is `ERR_HAZARD_CASE_NOT_FOUND` (404). Rung 13 requires the addressed `DisplacementLodging` to exist and covers lodging reads and lodging closure; failure is `ERR_LODGING_NOT_FOUND` (404). Rung 14 requires the addressed `DisputeCase` to exist and covers dispute reads and every dispute action; failure is `ERR_DISPUTE_NOT_FOUND` (404). Rung 15 requires the addressed `ReinvestmentAccount` to exist and covers account reads, expenditure authorisation and the metrics run; failure is `ERR_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND` (404).

**Rungs 16 to 18 — optimistic concurrency.** These three rungs are the version checks described in §2.6. Rung 16 requires `expected_lease_version` to equal the stored `version` of the addressed lease on a rent redetermination, failing with `ERR_STALE_LEASE_VERSION` (412). Rung 17 requires `expected_application_version` to equal the stored `version` of the addressed waitlist application on a recertification, failing with `ERR_STALE_APPLICATION_VERSION` (412). Rung 18 requires `expected_account_version` to equal the stored `version` of the addressed reinvestment account on an expenditure authorisation, failing with `ERR_STALE_ACCOUNT_VERSION` (412). Because these rungs sit above every state and business rung, a stale version is reported even when the request would also have failed a later business check.

**Rungs 19 to 28 — state preconditions.** Rung 19 requires that the requested transition be declared in this document for the current state of the request's **primary** target resource, failing with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (409); it applies to every action endpoint that drives a state machine. Rung 19 is evaluated **only** when the primary target's current state is not one of that entity's terminal states. A resource already parked in a terminal state is not judged at rung 19 at all — it falls through to rung 20 — so that the caller learns the tenancy or case is finished rather than that the verb was wrong. Cascade targets are also not judged at rung 19; the business rungs further down the ladder govern them. Rung 20 requires that the primary target not already be in a terminal state, failing with `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE_LOCKED` (409); the terminal states are `HOUSED` and `EXPIRED` for a waitlist application, `TERMINATED` for a lease's occupancy dimension and `CLOSED` for its allotment dimension, `COMPLETED` for a move-in inspection, `CLOSED` for a work order, `CLOSED` and `VOIDED` for a hazard case, `CLOSED` for a displacement lodging, and `CLOSED_RESOLVED` for a dispute case. Rung 21 requires, on offer acceptance, that the logical clock be at or before the application's `offer_expires_at`, failing with `ERR_OFFER_WINDOW_CLOSED` (409). Rung 22 requires, on lease creation, that the referenced application's `status` be `ACCEPTED`, failing with `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_ACCEPTED` (409). Rung 23 requires, on lease creation, that the referenced unit's `status` be `ASSIGNED` and that it be the very unit the application was offered, failing with `ERR_UNIT_NOT_AVAILABLE` (409). Rung 24 requires, on move-in, that the lease have a move-in inspection whose `status` is `TENANT_ACKNOWLEDGED` or `COMPLETED`, failing with `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_ACKNOWLEDGED` (409). Rung 25 requires the lease's `occupancy_status` to be `OCCUPIED` or `NOTICE_GIVEN` and guards three operations — termination notice, termination, and dispute opening — failing with `ERR_LEASE_NOT_OCCUPIED` (409). Rung 26 requires the hazard case to have been assessed, meaning its `status` is anything other than `REPORTED`, and guards two operations — lodging authorisation and clearance certification — failing with `ERR_HAZARD_NOT_ASSESSED` (409); on clearance certification rung 19 always answers first, since the only declared clearance edge leaves `CLEARANCE_PENDING`, so lodging authorisation is the only place this rung is observable. Rung 27 requires that a dispute credit greater than zero be awarded only while the lease's `allotment_status` is `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`, since a `CLOSED` ledger accepts no further credit; it guards a resident response with `accepted` true and `credit_offer_cents` greater than 0, and an installation decision with `credit_award_cents` greater than 0, failing with `ERR_ALLOTMENT_NOT_ACTIVE` (409). Rung 28 requires the work order's `reopen_count` to be below the reopen budget of the requesting actor — below 1 when the actor is a `ServiceMember`, below 2 when the actor is a `PropertyManager` — and guards work-order reopen, failing with `ERR_REOPEN_LIMIT_REACHED` (409).

**Rungs 29 to 31 — habitability and compliance safety.** Rung 29 requires that the housing unit not be in `HAZARD_HOLD`, because a unit under an environmental hazard hold may neither be leased nor moved into; it guards exactly two operations, lease creation and move-in, failing with `ERR_HAZARD_HOLD_ACTIVE` (409). On lease creation this rung is the single declared exception to strict ladder order: it is evaluated **ahead of** rung 23, so an offered unit that has since gone onto a hazard hold — and is therefore no longer `ASSIGNED` either — reports `ERR_HAZARD_HOLD_ACTIVE` rather than `ERR_UNIT_NOT_AVAILABLE`. On move-in the rung keeps its ladder position, answering after rung 24 and before rung 30. The offer run is deliberately **not** in that list: `HAZARD_HOLD` units are simply not `AVAILABLE`, so the run skips them without raising this error. Rung 30 requires the lease's move-in inspection to have `critical_open_count` equal to 0 — no deficiency entry of severity `HABITABILITY_CRITICAL` still sitting in entry status `OPEN` or `WORK_ORDER_CREATED` — and guards move-in only, failing with `ERR_UNRESOLVED_CRITICAL_DEFICIENCY` (409). Rung 31 enforces segregation of duties on two operations: the `X-User-ID` certifying a hazard clearance must differ from the case's `assessed_by_user_id`, and the `X-User-ID` rendering an installation dispute decision must differ from the case's `proposed_by_user_id`. Either violation is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).

**Rungs 32 to 34 — business referential integrity.** Rung 32 requires a supplied `lease_id` and `housing_unit_id` to agree, meaning the lease must be that unit's current non-`TERMINATED` lease; it guards work-order submission, hazard-case reporting and lodging authorisation, failing with `ERR_LEASE_UNIT_MISMATCH` (422). Rung 33 requires a supplied `parent_work_order_id` or `source_work_order_id` to belong to the same housing unit as the row being created; it guards work-order submission and hazard-case reporting, failing with `ERR_WORK_ORDER_UNIT_MISMATCH` (422). Rung 34 requires that a `termination_reason` of `PCS` be backed by `pcs_orders_ref` on the sponsor's record, and that a `termination_reason` of `DEPLOYMENT` be backed by `deployment_orders_ref` together with a `deployment_status` of `DEPLOYED`; it guards termination notice, failing with `ERR_ORDERS_NOT_ON_FILE` (422).

**Rungs 35 to 39 — business constraints.** Rung 35 requires the sponsor to have at least one full-time-custody dependent, because unaccompanied members are not eligible for family housing; it guards waitlist application creation, failing with `ERR_NOT_ELIGIBLE_FAMILY_HOUSING` (422). Rung 36 requires the sponsor to have no other waitlist application at the same installation in `PENDING`, `OFFERED` or `ACCEPTED` status; it guards waitlist application creation, failing with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTIVE_APPLICATION` (422). Rung 37 requires the unit's `bedroom_count` to equal the application's `bedroom_eligibility`; it guards lease creation, failing with `ERR_BEDROOM_MISMATCH` (422). Rung 38 requires the unit's `designated_rank_category` to equal the application's `rank_category`; it guards lease creation, failing with `ERR_RANK_CATEGORY_MISMATCH` (422). Rung 39 requires the lodging's `occupants_count` not to exceed two plus the sponsor's full-time-custody dependent count; it guards lodging authorisation, failing with `ERR_OCCUPANTS_EXCEED_AUTHORIZED` (422).

**Rungs 40 to 44 — temporal constraints.** Rung 40 requires the requested lodging interval, which runs from `start_date` inclusive to `end_date` exclusive, not to overlap any `AUTHORIZED` or `CLOSED` lodging interval already on the same lease; adjacency is explicitly allowed, so a new lodging may begin on the same date a previous one ended. It guards lodging authorisation, failing with `ERR_LODGING_INTERVAL_OVERLAP` (409). Rung 41 requires the new lease's `start_date` to be at least the installation's `turn_buffer_days` whole days after the unit's `last_lease_end_date`; it guards lease creation, failing with `ERR_UNIT_TURN_BUFFER_VIOLATION` (409). Rung 42 requires `termination_effective_date` to be at least 30 whole days after `termination_notice_date`, except for reasons `PCS` and `DEPLOYMENT`, which permit 14; it guards termination notice, failing with `ERR_NOTICE_PERIOD_TOO_SHORT` (422). Rung 43 requires the work order to be overdue by at least the escalation threshold hours for its `priority_class` and target level; it guards work-order escalation, failing with `ERR_ESCALATION_NOT_DUE` (409). Rung 44 requires that no metrics run has already been recorded for this reinvestment account and `period_month`, and that the requested period is not in the future relative to the logical clock; it guards the metrics run, failing with `ERR_PERIOD_ALREADY_POSTED` (409).

**Rungs 45 and 46 — fiscal checks, evaluated last.** Rung 45 requires that a `BahRate` row exist for the combination of `installation_id`, `pay_grade`, `with_dependents` and `effective_year` that the active rent variant selects; it guards lease creation, move-in and rent redetermination, failing with `ERR_BAH_RATE_NOT_ON_FILE` (422). Rung 46 requires the requested expenditure not to exceed the account's `closing_balance_cents`; it guards operator-initiated expenditure authorisation only and is never applied to the expenditure that the lodging-closure cascade writes, failing with `ERR_REINVESTMENT_BALANCE_INSUFFICIENT` (422).

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency on Version-Guarded Writes

Three operations in this system are legitimately repeatable — calling them twice in sequence from fresh reads is correct behaviour, not an error — and therefore cannot be guarded by a state precondition. For these three, the client's view of the parent record is the thing being checked.

A rent redetermination request must carry `expected_lease_version`, a non-optional field holding the client's last observed `version` of the addressed lease. If it does not match the lease's current `version`, the redetermination is rejected with `ERR_STALE_LEASE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A redetermination against an up-to-date view is allowed to run again and may produce the same rent or a new one, so only the stale-version check distinguishes a writer whose view of `gross_rent_cents` predates an intervening move-in, hazard abatement or dispute award.

A recertification request must carry `expected_application_version`, a non-optional field holding the client's last observed `version` of the addressed waitlist application. If it does not match the application's current `version`, the recertification is rejected with `ERR_STALE_APPLICATION_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Recertification is legitimately repeatable while the application is `PENDING`, so a second sequential recertification from a fresh read succeeds; the version check is what catches a writer who did not observe an intervening offer, decline penalty or earlier recertification.

An expenditure authorisation must carry `expected_account_version`, a non-optional field holding the client's last observed `version` of the addressed reinvestment account. If it does not match the account's current `version`, the expenditure is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ACCOUNT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Expenditures are legitimately repeatable while `closing_balance_cents` remains sufficient, so a second sequential expenditure from a fresh read succeeds; the version check is what stops a writer from spending against a balance that an intervening allotment run, termination settlement or earlier expenditure has already moved.

No other endpoint in this system accepts a version field. Requests to endpoints outside these three must not be required to supply one, and supplying one elsewhere has no effect.

### 2.7. Money, Rounding, and the Append-Only Allotment Ledger

Every monetary amount in this system is an integer number of cents. There are no floating-point money fields and no fractional cents anywhere in a stored value or a response. Wherever a computation produces a fractional cent, the result is rounded **half up** to the nearest whole cent: a value of exactly one half cent rounds away from zero to the next whole cent. Percentages that this document expresses as fractions — the incentive base fraction, the historic premium, the unit-type adjustments, the fee rates, the occupancy multipliers, the habitability penalty steps and the reinvestment contribution fraction — participate in the arithmetic as exact decimal fractions and are never pre-rounded. The single non-money computed quantity that carries decimals is the occupancy rate, which is rounded half up to exactly 2 decimal places and stored as a number such as 90.00; it is serialised as an ordinary JSON number, so trailing zeros are not preserved on the wire and that value appears as `90.0`.

Where a value is derived once and then multiplied by a day or night count, the rounding happens **once, at derivation**, and the already-rounded integer is what gets multiplied. This applies with particular force to the per-day rent figure: it is rounded to whole cents when rent is set, and every downstream day-count multiplication — final-month proration, hazard abatement, displacement reconciliation — multiplies that stored integer. Summing an unrounded daily fraction across a span of days and rounding the total once is a different answer and is forbidden.

A lease's rent collection is recorded as an append-only ledger of `AllotmentTransaction` rows. Each row belongs to exactly one lease through `lease_id`, is stamped with a `period_month`, a `type`, a signed `amount_cents`, a `source_ref` describing what produced it, a `posted_at` instant from the logical clock, and a `running_balance_cents`. Rows are never updated and never deleted; a correction is always a new, oppositely signed row. The five row kinds are `CHARGE`, `ABATEMENT`, `REFUND`, `ARREARS_ADJUSTMENT` and `EARLY_TERMINATION_FEE`. `CHARGE`, `ARREARS_ADJUSTMENT` and `EARLY_TERMINATION_FEE` rows carry positive amounts; `ABATEMENT` and `REFUND` rows carry negative amounts. The `source_ref` string identifies the producing process, in forms such as `ALLOTMENT_RUN:2026-09` for a monthly charge, `HAZARD_ABATEMENT:` followed by the hazard case identifier, `DISPUTE_AWARD:` followed by the dispute case identifier, and `TERMINATION_SETTLEMENT:` followed by the lease identifier.

Two derived quantities are conserved against that ledger after every insertion, and this is a hard invariant rather than a convenience cache. Ordering the lease's rows by `posted_at` ascending and breaking ties by `created_at` ascending, each row's `running_balance_cents` equals the previous row's `running_balance_cents` plus that row's own `amount_cents`, where the first row's predecessor value is zero. The lease's `allotment_balance_cents` equals the `running_balance_cents` of the last row, which is the same thing as the sum of `amount_cents` over every row of the lease. The lease's `abatement_total_cents` equals the negated sum of `amount_cents` restricted to rows whose `type` is `ABATEMENT` or `ARREARS_ADJUSTMENT` — so it is reported as a positive magnitude even though the underlying abatement rows are negative, and because it is a **net** figure a compensating positive `ARREARS_ADJUSTMENT` cancels the `ABATEMENT` it reverses and drives `abatement_total_cents` back to exactly 0. `REFUND` rows arising from dispute awards are deliberately excluded from `abatement_total_cents`: they are credits, not habitability abatements.

### 2.8. Inventory Conservation and Cascade Discipline

An installation publishes six live counters over its unit inventory plus one over its waitlist. `total_unit_count` is the number of `HousingUnit` rows carrying that installation's `installation_id`. `available_unit_count`, `assigned_unit_count`, `occupied_unit_count`, `offline_unit_count` and `hazard_hold_unit_count` are the counts of those rows whose `status` is `AVAILABLE`, `ASSIGNED`, `OCCUPIED`, `OFFLINE_MAINTENANCE` and `HAZARD_HOLD` respectively. `pending_application_count` is the number of the installation's `WaitlistApplication` rows whose `status` is `PENDING`.

After every request that creates a unit or changes a unit's `status`, the five per-status counters must each equal the live count of the installation's units in the matching status, and `total_unit_count` must equal their sum across all five tracked statuses. This is a compensating invariant: moving a unit from `HAZARD_HOLD` back to `AVAILABLE` moves one count from `hazard_hold_unit_count` to `available_unit_count` and leaves `total_unit_count` unchanged. All counters are exact integers.

Cascades in this system are **synchronous and immediate**. When a single request touches several entities, every affected row is updated within that request before the response is emitted; there is no deferred or eventual reconciliation. Each distinct row that a cascade mutates has its own `updated_at` refreshed and its own `version` incremented by 1, including rows the caller did not address. The increment is counted once per row per request rather than once per write, so a row that a single request touches several times still advances its `version` by exactly 1 for that request, even though its `updated_at` is refreshed on each touch; conversely, a row a request leaves genuinely unchanged advances neither. Continuing the examples — a hazard assessment that suspends a lease increments that lease's `version`, and a unit status change that the assessment triggers increments the unit's `version`. Where a cascade hop targets a row that does not exist, this document says explicitly whether the hop is skipped silently or the request fails; unless stated otherwise, a missing cascade target is a failure.

### 2.9. Segregation of Duties

Two decisions in this domain must be made by two different people, and the system enforces this by comparing stamped operator identifiers rather than by any role check. On a hazard case, the `EnvironmentalSpecialist` whose `X-User-ID` was stamped as `assessed_by_user_id` at assessment may not certify that same case's clearance; the certifying `X-User-ID` must differ. On a dispute case, the `PropertyManager` whose `X-User-ID` was stamped as `proposed_by_user_id` on the most recent proposal may not be the `InstallationHousingOffice` user who renders the escalated decision; the deciding `X-User-ID` must differ. Both violations surface as `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` with HTTP 403, and both are evaluated at rung 31 — above the referential and constraint rungs, below the state rungs.

### 2.10. Sub-Collection Reads and Left-to-Right Path Resolution

Business paths in this system are flat top-level collections; ownership is expressed through foreign-key fields rather than nested URLs. A handful of sub-collection reads exist so that children which are never returned by a create response remain reachable from their parent — an installation's BAH rate rows, a sponsor's dependents, a lease's move-in inspections, and a lease's allotment ledger. For any such path, the segments are resolved strictly left to right: the parent named by the leading path parameter is looked up and must exist before the child collection is filtered, so addressing the ledger of a non-existent lease yields `ERR_LEASE_NOT_FOUND` rather than an empty page. The same left-to-right discipline applies between path and body: path parameters resolve before body-carried references.

Sub-collection reads accept the standard `limit` and `offset` parameters plus their own filters, and they return the standard array envelope. The lease's allotment ledger is returned in `created_at` ascending order and accepts `period_month` and `type` filters; a lease's inspection listing accepts a `status` filter; an installation's BAH rate listing accepts `pay_grade`, `with_dependents` and `effective_year` filters; a sponsor's dependent listing accepts `relationship` and `is_full_time_custody` filters.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Standing Up a Housing Project

A housing project begins as an `Installation` record registered by a `PlatformAdmin`. Registration supplies the installation's `name` (for example, Fort Cavazos Family Housing), its owning service `branch` — one of `ARMY`, `NAVY`, `AIR_FORCE` or `MARINE_CORPS` — the `mha_code` naming the Military Housing Area used for BAH lookup (for example, TX-FTC), and the `bill_of_rights_version` string identifying the currently published Tenant Bill of Rights that residents must acknowledge at move-in (for example, 2026.1). Five policy knobs may be supplied and each has a default when omitted. `turn_buffer_days` is the number of whole days of unit turn time required between a prior lease's termination effective date and the start of the next lease on the same unit; it defaults to 5 and must be at least 0. `offer_response_hours` is the number of hours a waitlist offer stays open before it can no longer be accepted; it defaults to 72 and must be at least 1. `decline_limit` is the number of declines after which an application becomes `EXPIRED`; it defaults to 2 and must be at least 1. `decline_penalty_days` is the number of whole days added to an application's control date each time an offer is declined and the application returns to the pending pool; it defaults to 15 and must be at least 0. `incentive_base_pct` is the fraction of net collected allotments forming the base performance incentive fee; it defaults to 0.03 — three percent — and must be between 0 and 1 inclusive. At registration, `total_unit_count`, `available_unit_count`, `assigned_unit_count`, `occupied_unit_count`, `offline_unit_count`, `hazard_hold_unit_count` and `pending_application_count` all initialise to 0, `offer_run_epoch` initialises to 0, and `last_offer_run_at` initialises to null.

Rent in this domain is not negotiated; it is looked up. A `PlatformAdmin` loads the published BAH table one row at a time, each row naming the `installation_id` whose housing area it prices, the `pay_grade` it applies to — a string in the families `E1` through `E9`, `W1` through `W5`, or `O1` through `O10` — a `with_dependents` boolean distinguishing the with-dependents rate from the without-dependents rate, the `effective_year` the table is effective for (a calendar year from 2024 through 2030), the full monthly entitlement as `monthly_rate_cents` (which must be greater than 0), and the `utility_allowance_cents` carved out of that entitlement as the tenant utility allowance (which must be at least 0 and strictly less than `monthly_rate_cents`). Both of those bounds, including the comparison between the two fields, are request-shape checks, so a violation answers `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` before the named installation is resolved. No uniqueness constraint is enforced over the selector formed by `installation_id`, `pay_grade`, `with_dependents` and `effective_year`: loading a second row for the same selector is accepted rather than refused. These rows are configuration, not transactions: a rate row has no `version` and no `updated_at`, cannot be updated, and a corrected rate is loaded as a new row for a new `effective_year`. The installation's rate rows can be listed back for auditing, filtered by `pay_grade`, `with_dependents` and `effective_year`.

Units are added to inventory by a `PlatformAdmin` or a `PropertyManager`. Each `HousingUnit` names its owning `installation_id` and a `unit_code` giving the street address code, which must be unique within its installation; a duplicate `unit_code` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and because the check has to read the installation's existing inventory it is evaluated after the installation itself has been resolved. Its `bedroom_count` is between 1 and 5 inclusive. Its `unit_type` is one of `SFH`, `DUPLEX`, `TOWNHOME` or `APARTMENT`, and drives the rent adjustment percentage described in §3.5. Its `designated_rank_category` — one of `JUNIOR_ENLISTED`, `SENIOR_ENLISTED`, `WARRANT_OFFICER`, `JUNIOR_OFFICER` or `SENIOR_OFFICER` — marks the inventory pool the unit belongs to; the offer run only offers it to applications of a matching rank category. The optional `historic_flag` defaults to false and, when true, adds a rent premium before the statutory BAH cap is applied. The optional `accessibility_features` array defaults to empty and holds zero or more of the codes `ROLL_IN_SHOWER`, `ZERO_STEP_ENTRY` and `WIDENED_DOORWAYS`; a value that is not an array, or an array containing anything other than those three codes, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and a repeated code is collapsed to a single stored occurrence in first-supplied order. A newly created unit enters inventory with `status` `AVAILABLE`, a null `hazard_hold_reason` and a null `last_lease_end_date`, and the creation raises the installation's `total_unit_count` by one and `available_unit_count` by one, preserving the counter invariant of §2.8. Units can be listed and filtered by `installation_id`, `status`, `bedroom_count` and `unit_type`.

Operators also move units by hand. A `PlatformAdmin` or `PropertyManager` may drive a unit's `status` directly along five operator-visible edges — the four in this paragraph plus the hold release in the next — supplying the target `status` and, where required, a `hazard_hold_reason`. The supplied target may name any of the five declared unit statuses without failing rung 4; a target that is not the far end of one of those five edges, including a no-op request naming the unit's own current status, is refused at rung 19 with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. `hazard_hold_reason` is a rung-4 requirement whenever the target is `HAZARD_HOLD`, so its absence is reported before the unit is resolved, and any transition to a status other than `HAZARD_HOLD` leaves `hazard_hold_reason` null. A `HousingUnit` declares no terminal state, so rung 20 never fires on this endpoint. `AVAILABLE → OFFLINE_MAINTENANCE` takes a unit out of the offer pool for planned work, and `OFFLINE_MAINTENANCE → AVAILABLE` puts it back; each edge recomputes `available_unit_count` and `offline_unit_count` and raises the unit's `version` by 1. Note carefully that an `OFFLINE_MAINTENANCE` unit stays in **both** occupancy denominators described in §3.9: taking a unit offline lowers the installation's occupancy rate rather than shrinking the denominator it is measured against. `AVAILABLE → HAZARD_HOLD` is the operator-placed hold — a suspected mold finding during turn, for instance — and requires `hazard_hold_reason` to be present in the request body; it stamps that reason, recomputes `available_unit_count` and `hazard_hold_unit_count`, and raises the unit's `version` by 1. `ASSIGNED → HAZARD_HOLD` is the same operator hold placed on a unit already reserved for a sponsor; it likewise requires `hazard_hold_reason`, stamps it, recomputes `assigned_unit_count` and `hazard_hold_unit_count`, and raises the unit's `version` by 1. Any lease already written on that unit stays in occupancy status `PENDING_MOVE_IN`, and the family's move-in is now blocked by rung 29 with `ERR_HAZARD_HOLD_ACTIVE` until the hold is released. Both operator holds are distinct from the system hold that a certified hazard assessment places on an `OCCUPIED` unit, described in §3.8.

Releasing an operator-placed hold is the compensating edge: `HAZARD_HOLD → AVAILABLE` clears `hazard_hold_reason`, recomputes `hazard_hold_unit_count` and `available_unit_count` with the five-counter sum unchanged, and raises the unit's `version` by 1. It is permitted only when no `HazardCase` on that unit is in any of the states `REPORTED`, `ASSESSED`, `REMEDIATION_IN_PROGRESS`, `DISPLACEMENT_ACTIVE` or `CLEARANCE_PENDING`; a release attempted while such a case still stands is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (409), the same error the undeclared edges raise. One consequence deserves emphasis: a unit that was `ASSIGNED` before the hold returns to `AVAILABLE`, not to `ASSIGNED`. Releasing a hold never re-reserves inventory.

### 3.2. Sponsors, Dependents, and the Orders of Record

A `ServiceMember` record is the sponsor — the uniformed member whose entitlement pays the rent. A `PlatformAdmin` or an `InstallationHousingOffice` user registers one by supplying the `dodid`, which is the DoD ID number and must be globally unique across sponsors — a duplicate is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — the sponsor's `last_name` and `first_name`, the service `branch`, and the current `pay_grade`. On registration `deployment_status` initialises to `NOT_DEPLOYED`, `dependent_count` initialises to 0, and `deployment_orders_ref`, `expected_return_date`, `pcs_orders_ref` and `pcs_report_date` all initialise to null.

The sponsor's `rank_category` is never supplied by a client; it is derived from `pay_grade` alone, and any value a client offers for it is ignored. Take the letter prefix of the pay grade and the integer that follows it. For a grade beginning with `E`, the category is `JUNIOR_ENLISTED` when that integer is at most 6, and `SENIOR_ENLISTED` from 7 upward — so `E6` is junior enlisted and `E7` is senior enlisted. For a grade beginning with `W`, the category is always `WARRANT_OFFICER`. For a grade beginning with `O`, the category is `JUNIOR_OFFICER` when the integer is at most 3, and `SENIOR_OFFICER` from 4 upward. The derivation reads nothing but the pay-grade string; no other entity participates.

Orders arrive through their own recording operation, available to a `PlatformAdmin` or an `InstallationHousingOffice` user, and every one of its fields is optional so that a single set of orders can be filed without disturbing the others. Supplying `deployment_status` sets it to `NOT_DEPLOYED`, `DEPLOYED` or `RETURNED`. `deployment_orders_ref` records the order number backing a deployed status, which the deployment-based lease termination right requires, and `expected_return_date` records the date the member is expected home. `pcs_orders_ref` records the order number backing permanent-change-of-station orders, which the PCS termination right requires, and `pcs_report_date` records the report-no-later-than date on those orders. Supplying `pay_grade` records a promotion: it replaces the stored pay grade and `rank_category` is immediately re-derived from the new value. Only the fields actually supplied are written, and a field supplied as JSON `null` counts as not supplied, so there is no way to blank an orders field once it has been written. The sponsor's `version` is incremented by exactly 1 on every recording, including a recording that supplies no fields at all and therefore changes nothing.

The retroactivity boundary here matters, and it is deliberately narrow. Recording orders does **not** reach back into waitlist applications. An application's already-stamped `effective_control_date` is not retroactively adjusted, and an application's already-stamped `rank_category` snapshot is not retroactively adjusted, even by a promotion that changes the sponsor's live rank category. Both only move when the application is recertified.

Dependents are added one at a time by a `PlatformAdmin`, an `InstallationHousingOffice` user or the sponsor themselves. Each `Dependent` names its sponsoring `service_member_id`, a `relationship` of `SPOUSE`, `CHILD` or `OTHER_AUTHORIZED`, a `date_of_birth` used for age-at-control-date bedroom sharing, a `gender` of `MALE` or `FEMALE` used only for same-gender child bedroom sharing, and an optional `is_full_time_custody` flag defaulting to true. A dependent with `is_full_time_custody` false is excluded entirely — from the sponsor's `dependent_count` and from bedroom eligibility. Adding a dependent recomputes the sponsor's `dependent_count` as the number of that sponsor's full-time-custody dependents and increments the sponsor's `version` by 1. A `Dependent` row itself carries the usual `updated_at` and `version`, and there is no read endpoint for a single dependent — dependents are reached only through the sponsor's listing. As with orders, existing waitlist applications are untouched: their `bedroom_eligibility` snapshots are not recomputed until a recertification runs.

### 3.3. Joining the Waitlist: Control Dates, Bedroom Eligibility, and Recertification

A sponsor, or an `InstallationHousingOffice` user acting on their behalf, joins an installation's family-housing waitlist by creating a `WaitlistApplication` naming the `installation_id` whose list is being joined, the `service_member_id` of the sponsor, the `pcs_orders_date` on the orders that establish the housing control date, and the `expected_arrival_date` on which the family expects to reach the installation. An optional `accessibility_required` flag defaults to false; when true, it restricts the candidate unit pool to units whose `accessibility_features` contain `ROLL_IN_SHOWER`. Two eligibility gates apply before anything is computed: the sponsor must have at least one full-time-custody dependent, since unaccompanied members are not eligible for family housing and are turned away with `ERR_NOT_ELIGIBLE_FAMILY_HOUSING`, and the sponsor must not already hold another application at this installation in `PENDING`, `OFFERED` or `ACCEPTED` status, or the request is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ACTIVE_APPLICATION`.

Creation then snapshots the family as it stands. The application's `rank_category` is copied from the sponsor's currently derived rank category and is never re-derived afterwards except by recertification. Its `dependent_count_at_application` is copied from the sponsor's current `dependent_count` and is likewise re-snapshotted only by recertification. The `status` initialises to `PENDING`, `decline_count` and `offer_count` initialise to 0, and `offered_unit_id` and `offer_expires_at` initialise to null. The installation's `pending_application_count` rises by one.

Two computed values are then produced, and their order is fixed: the control date is computed first, because bedroom eligibility measures dependents' ages against it. The **effective control date** begins as the supplied `pcs_orders_date`. If, at the moment of creation, the sponsor's `deployment_status` is `DEPLOYED`, the sponsor earns a deployment credit of 30 whole days, which is applied by moving the date 30 days **earlier** — an earlier control date sorts ahead of a later one, so the credit improves the family's waitlist position. The result is stored as `effective_control_date` as an ISO calendar date. Each subsequent decline that returns the application to the pending pool advances `effective_control_date` later by the installation's `decline_penalty_days` whole days.

**Bedroom eligibility** answers how many bedrooms the family is entitled to, and it is computed against the control date just established. Consider only the sponsor's dependents whose `is_full_time_custody` is true; part-time-custody dependents are excluded from the calculation entirely, and so are dependents added after the snapshot was taken unless a recertification re-runs the rule. A dependent's age in whole years is the number of times their birthday has occurred on or before the control date, computed as the control date's year minus the birth year, reduced by one when the control date's month-and-day pair falls before the birth date's month-and-day pair. A birthday landing exactly on the control date counts as having been reached. The entitlement starts at a base of one bedroom, which covers the sponsor together with a `SPOUSE` dependent if one is present — a spouse therefore never adds a bedroom. Among the dependents whose `relationship` is `CHILD`, every child whose age is at least 10 requires a private bedroom and contributes one bedroom each; the children under 10 share, two same-gender children to a bedroom. The sharing is counted gender by gender in the fixed order `MALE` then `FEMALE`: for each gender, count the under-ten children of that gender, divide by two, and round **up** to a whole number of bedrooms, then add the two results together. Every dependent whose `relationship` is `OTHER_AUTHORIZED` contributes one bedroom each. The raw entitlement is the base bedroom plus the shared-bedroom total plus the count of private-bedroom children plus the count of other authorised dependents. The stored `bedroom_eligibility` is that raw figure floored at 1 and capped at 5 bedrooms — it is always a whole number of bedrooms in that range. The age comparison is inclusive at ten, so a child whose tenth birthday falls exactly on the control date requires a private bedroom rather than a shared one.

Recertification is the only way a stamped application snapshot moves, and it is reserved to a `PlatformAdmin` or an `InstallationHousingOffice` user. The request carries `expected_application_version` — the client's last observed application `version`, checked as described in §2.6 — and may optionally carry a replacement `pcs_orders_date`, which when supplied overwrites the application's stored `pcs_orders_date` before anything is recomputed and is what every later read and recertification sees; when it is omitted the stored value stands unchanged. Recertification recomputes the control date from the **current** `pcs_orders_date` and the sponsor's **current** `deployment_status`, then re-applies the accumulated decline penalty by advancing the result later by `decline_count` multiplied by the installation's `decline_penalty_days` whole days. Because the penalty is re-applied from the recomputed base rather than added to the old stored value, a recertification never double-counts declines. Recertification then re-runs bedroom eligibility against the freshly computed control date and the sponsor's **current** full-time-custody dependents, and re-snapshots both `rank_category` and `dependent_count_at_application` from the sponsor's live record. The application's `version` is incremented by 1. Recertification drives no state machine, so it never moves the application's `status` and rung 19 never applies to it; rung 20 does apply, so a recertification addressed to an application already parked in a terminal state — `HOUSED` or `EXPIRED` — is refused with `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE_LOCKED` (409) even when the supplied `expected_application_version` matches, while `PENDING`, `OFFERED` and `ACCEPTED` applications are all recertifiable.

Applications can be read individually and listed for reverse lookup, filterable by `installation_id`, `service_member_id`, `offered_unit_id` and `status`.

### 3.4. The Offer Run, Acceptance, and Decline

Offers are not made one at a time. A `PropertyManager` or an `InstallationHousingOffice` user triggers an **offer run** against an installation, and the run is an ops-run checkpoint that walks the installation's inventory in a pinned order and matches each free unit to the best-qualified waiting family. The run is a read of the installation followed by a batch of writes, and it always returns a success response and the installation record — even when it creates no offer at all.

The run considers only that installation's units whose `status` is `AVAILABLE`, and it visits them ordered by `bedroom_count` ascending, breaking ties by `created_at` ascending. Row identifiers are never part of the ordering. Units in `OFFLINE_MAINTENANCE`, `HAZARD_HOLD`, `ASSIGNED` or `OCCUPIED` never participate, and a `HAZARD_HOLD` unit is skipped silently rather than raising the hazard-hold error.

For each unit in that order the run assembles a candidate set from the installation's applications. An application qualifies when its `status` is `PENDING`, its `bedroom_eligibility` is **exactly equal** to the unit's `bedroom_count` — not merely large enough, and not merely no larger — its `rank_category` is **exactly equal** to the unit's `designated_rank_category`, and either its `accessibility_required` is false or the unit's `accessibility_features` contains `ROLL_IN_SHOWER`. Applications in `OFFERED`, `ACCEPTED`, `EXPIRED` or `HOUSED` status never participate. An application already offered a unit earlier in the same run is excluded from every later unit's candidate set: a single application receives at most one offer per run.

Candidates are then ranked by `effective_control_date` ascending — the earliest control date wins — with ties broken by `bedroom_eligibility` descending, and any remaining ties broken by `created_at` ascending. Row identifiers are never a tie-break. The first candidate in that ranking wins the unit. If the candidate set is empty, the run **skips that unit and continues**: no error is raised, no offer is created, and the unit keeps `status` `AVAILABLE`.

Winning an offer advances the application `PENDING → OFFERED`, writes the unit's identifier into `offered_unit_id`, sets `offer_expires_at` to the logical clock plus the installation's `offer_response_hours` hours, raises `offer_count` by 1, and increments the application's `version` by 1. In the same step the unit advances `AVAILABLE → ASSIGNED` and its `version` is incremented by 1. Once the walk finishes, the installation's `available_unit_count`, `assigned_unit_count` and `pending_application_count` are recomputed, `offer_run_epoch` is raised by exactly 1, and `last_offer_run_at` is stamped with the logical clock. A run that made no offers still advances the epoch and stamps the timestamp. The installation's own `version` is therefore incremented by exactly 1 on every run, whether or not any offer was created, and the recount refreshes all seven counters rather than only the three the walk can move. The run takes no request body, and `offer_expires_at` is computed once from the clock at the start of the run, so every offer a single run creates carries the identical expiry instant.

A sponsor — or an `InstallationHousingOffice` user acting for them — accepts by advancing the application `OFFERED → ACCEPTED`. Acceptance is permitted while the logical clock is at or before `offer_expires_at`; acceptance landing exactly on `offer_expires_at` is allowed, and one instant later it is refused with `ERR_OFFER_WINDOW_CLOSED`. The acting sponsor must own the application. On acceptance the unit remains `ASSIGNED` — it is now held for lease creation rather than returned to the pool — the installation's `pending_application_count` is recomputed, and the application's `version` is incremented by 1.

Declining is the loop edge, and its outcome forks on the installation's decline budget. A decline may carry an optional `decline_reason`, which is checked as an ordinary non-empty string when it is present but is not persisted on the application and appears in no response field; the application record carries no reason column. In every case `decline_count` rises by 1, `offered_unit_id` and `offer_expires_at` are cleared, the released unit moves `ASSIGNED → AVAILABLE` with its `version` incremented by 1, the installation's `available_unit_count`, `assigned_unit_count` and `pending_application_count` are recomputed — leaving the five-counter sum unchanged, as §2.8 requires — and the application's `version` is incremented by 1. What differs is where the application lands. When the incremented `decline_count` is still below the installation's `decline_limit`, the application returns `OFFERED → PENDING` and its `effective_control_date` is pushed later by `decline_penalty_days` whole days, so the family keeps its place in line but pays for the refusal; it becomes eligible again in a later run. When the incremented `decline_count` reaches the `decline_limit`, the application instead goes `OFFERED → EXPIRED`, which is terminal, and `effective_control_date` is **not** advanced — the penalty exists only to reposition an application that is returning to the pending pool, and an expired application is not returning to it.

### 3.5. Writing the Lease and Setting the Rent

Lease creation is exclusive to the `PropertyManager`. The payload names the `application_id` of the accepted application the lease realises, the `housing_unit_id` of the unit being leased, the `start_date` on which the lease term begins, and the `term_months` initial term, which must be between 1 and 24 whole months inclusive. The lease's `service_member_id` is not supplied; it is copied from the application at creation. Six business gates apply, in exactly this evaluation order — the ladder order of §2.5 with the one hazard-hold exception §2.5 declares: the referenced application must be in `ACCEPTED` status or the request fails with `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_ACCEPTED`; the unit must not be in `HAZARD_HOLD`, or the request fails with `ERR_HAZARD_HOLD_ACTIVE`, and because this hazard gate answers before the availability gate a hold placed on the offered unit surfaces as `ERR_HAZARD_HOLD_ACTIVE` and never as `ERR_UNIT_NOT_AVAILABLE`; the referenced unit must otherwise be `ASSIGNED` and must be the very unit this application was offered, or the request fails with `ERR_UNIT_NOT_AVAILABLE`; the unit's `bedroom_count` must equal the application's `bedroom_eligibility`, or the request fails with `ERR_BEDROOM_MISMATCH`; the unit's `designated_rank_category` must equal the application's `rank_category`, or the request fails with `ERR_RANK_CATEGORY_MISMATCH`; and the new `start_date` must fall at least `turn_buffer_days` whole days after the unit's `last_lease_end_date`, or the request fails with `ERR_UNIT_TURN_BUFFER_VIOLATION`. A unit that has never been leased carries a null `last_lease_end_date` and the turn-buffer rule does not constrain it.

A successful creation initialises the lease's two independent status dimensions — `occupancy_status` to `PENDING_MOVE_IN` and `allotment_status` to `NOT_STARTED` — drives the application `ACCEPTED → HOUSED`, which is terminal for the application and increments its `version` by 1, leaves the unit `ASSIGNED` until the family actually arrives, and recomputes the installation's `pending_application_count`. The lease's money fields are then set from the BAH table under the provisional variant described next, and `early_termination_fee_cents`, `settlement_total_cents`, `allotment_balance_cents` and `abatement_total_cents` all initialise to 0 while `bill_of_rights_version_ack`, `bill_of_rights_ack_at`, `termination_reason`, `termination_notice_date` and `termination_effective_date` all initialise to null.

**Gross rent determination** runs in one of two variants, and the two variants can legitimately read different BAH rate rows for the same lease. In both variants the first step is to select exactly one rate row by a four-part key: the `installation_id` of the unit's installation, a `pay_grade` equal to the sponsor's current pay grade, a `with_dependents` value that is true when the variant's dependent-count source is at least 1 and false otherwise, and an `effective_year` equal to the variant's year source. If no such row is on file the operation is rejected with `ERR_BAH_RATE_NOT_ON_FILE`. Because §3.1 enforces no uniqueness over that selector, the table may legitimately hold several rows matching it; the row used is then the **most recently loaded** matching row — the last one in `created_at` ascending order with insertion sequence as the tie-break — so re-loading a corrected rate for a selector already on file supersedes the earlier row for every later pricing run. Next, a unit-type adjustment is applied to the monthly entitlement: an `SFH` unit takes no adjustment, a `DUPLEX` unit is reduced by two percent, a `TOWNHOME` unit by three percent, and an `APARTMENT` unit by five percent. The entitlement `monthly_rate_cents` is multiplied by one plus that adjustment and the product is rounded half up to whole cents **at this point**, before anything else happens. If the unit's `historic_flag` is true, a historic premium of one and one half percent of the unadjusted `monthly_rate_cents`, itself rounded half up to whole cents, is then added to that intermediate figure. Finally the statutory cap is applied: the stored `gross_rent_cents` is the smaller of the figure just computed and the rate row's `monthly_rate_cents`, because rent may never exceed the BAH entitlement. The lease's `utility_allowance_cents` is copied verbatim from the selected rate row's `utility_allowance_cents`. The lease's `net_rent_cents` is `gross_rent_cents` less `utility_allowance_cents`, and it is the amount actually collected by allotment. The lease's `rent_effective_year` records the variant's year source.

The two variants differ only in the year source and the dependent-count source, and each stamps a different `rent_basis`. The **`OFFER_PROVISIONAL`** variant runs at lease creation and produces the rent quoted at signing, before the family arrives: its year source is the calendar year of the application's `effective_control_date`, and its dependent-count source is the application's `dependent_count_at_application` snapshot. It sets `rent_basis` to `OFFER_PROVISIONAL`. The **`MOVE_IN_FINAL`** variant runs at move-in and at rent redetermination and produces the rent actually collected: at move-in its year source is the calendar year of the lease's `start_date`, at redetermination its year source is the calendar year of the supplied `effective_date`, and in both cases its dependent-count source is the sponsor's **current** `dependent_count`. It sets `rent_basis` to `MOVE_IN_FINAL`. The divergence is real and intended: for a control date in 2025 and a start date in 2026 the two variants read different rate rows and produce different `gross_rent_cents` for identical lease inputs.

Every run of gross rent determination also recomputes the lease's per-day rent. The stored `daily_rent_cents` is `net_rent_cents` multiplied by 12 and divided by 365, rounded half up to whole cents. That rounding happens exactly once, here. Every later day-count multiplication — final-month proration, hazard abatement, displacement reconciliation — multiplies this already-rounded integer and never re-derives a fractional daily rate from `net_rent_cents`.

Re-pricing an existing lease is the job of the **rent redetermination**, available to a `PropertyManager` or a `PlatformAdmin`. It carries `expected_lease_version` and an `effective_date`, re-runs the `MOVE_IN_FINAL` variant for the calendar year of that `effective_date` against the sponsor's current `dependent_count`, overwrites `gross_rent_cents`, `utility_allowance_cents`, `net_rent_cents` and `daily_rent_cents`, sets `rent_basis` to `MOVE_IN_FINAL` and `rent_effective_year` to the `effective_date`'s year, and increments the lease's `version` by 1. It posts no ledger row at all: the new rent applies to subsequent allotment runs only, and nothing already charged is retroactively adjusted. Redetermination is repeatable, which is precisely why it is guarded by the stale-version check of §2.6 rather than by a state precondition. It drives no state machine at all, so neither rung 19 nor rung 20 is evaluated for it and the lease's `occupancy_status` is never inspected: a lease in `PENDING_MOVE_IN`, `NOTICE_GIVEN` or even `TERMINATED` is re-priced exactly like an `OCCUPIED` one, and a matching `expected_lease_version` is the only thing a terminated lease's re-pricing needs. Leases can be read individually and listed for reverse lookup, filterable by `application_id`, `housing_unit_id`, `service_member_id`, `occupancy_status` and `allotment_status`.

### 3.6. Move-In: The Walkthrough, the Bill of Rights, and Critical Deficiencies

Before a family may take possession, the `PropertyManager` schedules a `MoveInInspection` against a lease that is still in `PENDING_MOVE_IN`, supplying the `lease_id` and the `scheduled_date` of the walkthrough, which is accepted whatever its relation to the logical clock or the lease's `start_date`. The addressed lease is the primary target of the scheduling request, so a lease already `TERMINATED` is refused at rung 20 with `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE_LOCKED` while a lease in any other non-`PENDING_MOVE_IN` occupancy state — `OCCUPIED` or `NOTICE_GIVEN` — is refused at rung 19 with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Nothing limits a lease to one walkthrough: a second inspection may be scheduled against the same lease while it remains `PENDING_MOVE_IN`, and each is an independent row with its own deficiency list and counters. The inspection is created in `status` `SCHEDULED` with `deficiency_count` and `critical_open_count` both 0, an empty deficiency list, and `tenant_signature_name`, `tenant_signature_at` and `bill_of_rights_version_ack` all null. The acting operator's `X-User-ID` is stamped as `conducted_by_user_id`, recording who scheduled and conducted the walkthrough.

Findings from the walkthrough are logged by the `PropertyManager` as a `deficiencies` payload. The deficiency list on an inspection is **append-only**: logging may be performed while the inspection is `SCHEDULED` or already `DEFICIENCIES_LOGGED`, and the first such call advances the inspection `SCHEDULED → DEFICIENCIES_LOGGED`. Logging against a `COMPLETED` inspection is refused at rung 20 with `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE_LOCKED`, and logging against one already `TENANT_ACKNOWLEDGED` is refused at rung 19 with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` — once the resident has signed, the list is frozen. The `deficiencies` field itself is required and must be a JSON array of objects; an array supplied empty is accepted and still advances `SCHEDULED → DEFICIENCIES_LOGGED` with both counters left at 0. Each supplied entry carries a `deficiency_code`, the `room` it was found in, and a `severity` of `COSMETIC`, `FUNCTIONAL` or `HABITABILITY_CRITICAL`. A `deficiency_code` must be unique within the inspection — the same code may not be logged twice, whether the earlier occurrence is already on the inspection or merely earlier in the same payload — and a violation is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400) with nothing appended at all, so a batch carrying one duplicate leaves the inspection exactly as it was; and it must be one of the trade categories a work order can carry, because a critical entry's code becomes the spawned work order's category verbatim. Each appended entry is stored with an entry-level `status` of `OPEN` and a `work_order_id` of null; the entry status later moves to `WORK_ORDER_CREATED` and then `RESOLVED`, and `work_order_id` later holds the identifier of the work order spawned for that entry. That handle is a cross-entity reference resolved together with the inspection's lease, not a declared foreign key on the inspection. Logging recomputes `deficiency_count`, which is simply the length of the deficiency list, and `critical_open_count`, which is the number of entries whose `severity` is `HABITABILITY_CRITICAL` and whose entry status is anything other than `RESOLVED`. The inspection's `version` is incremented by 1. Crucially, **no work order is created at logging time**; critical entries spawn their work orders later, at tenant acknowledgment.

Acknowledgment is the resident's act and is exclusive to the `ServiceMember` who owns the lease. It carries the `tenant_signature_name` the resident prints and the `bill_of_rights_version_ack` version string being acknowledged, and it is accepted while the inspection is `SCHEDULED` or `DEFICIENCIES_LOGGED` — an inspection with zero logged deficiencies acknowledges straight from `SCHEDULED`. An acknowledgment addressed to a `COMPLETED` inspection is refused at rung 20 with `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE_LOCKED` and one addressed to an inspection already `TENANT_ACKNOWLEDGED` at rung 19 with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`; the signature is a once-only act. The acknowledged version must name exactly the currently published `bill_of_rights_version` of the installation owning the lease's unit; an acknowledgment carrying any other version string is not an acceptable request and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. That comparison is the one shape-flavoured check in this document that cannot run at rung 4, because the value it compares against has to be reached through the inspection's lease, its unit and that unit's installation; it is therefore evaluated **after** the two state gates just described, so an inspection that is `COMPLETED` or already signed answers with its state error even when the supplied version string is also wrong. On success the inspection advances to `TENANT_ACKNOWLEDGED`, stamping `tenant_signature_name`, `tenant_signature_at` from the logical clock, and `bill_of_rights_version_ack`. The acknowledged version is also copied onto the lease as the lease's own `bill_of_rights_version_ack` with `bill_of_rights_ack_at` stamped from the same clock reading, and the lease's `version` is incremented by 1.

Acknowledgment then triggers the deficiency cascade, and this is where the habitability gate on move-in acquires teeth. As a second hop, every deficiency entry whose `severity` is `HABITABILITY_CRITICAL` and whose entry status is still `OPEN` flips to `WORK_ORDER_CREATED`, and a work order is created on the lease's unit for each one — with `category` copied verbatim from the entry's `deficiency_code`, `habitability_impact` set to true, and `inspection_deficiency_code` set to that entry's `deficiency_code`. The spawned order also carries the inspection's lease in its `lease_id`, a null `parent_work_order_id` and a null `assigned_tech_user_id`, and its `description` is generated by the system in the form `Move-in inspection deficiency <code> in <room>`, naming the entry's code and its `room`. Each newly created order is classified by the priority rules of §3.7 like any other — and because the lease is still `PENDING_MOVE_IN` the unit has no `OCCUPIED` lease yet, so the under-six vulnerability escalation of §3.7 can never fire on one of these orders and a `HABITABILITY_CRITICAL` entry in a tier-1 or tier-2 category lands on `URGENT` — and its identifier is written back into the originating entry's `work_order_id`. As a third hop, `critical_open_count` is recomputed; because `WORK_ORDER_CREATED` is not `RESOLVED`, the count does not drop, so move-in stays blocked until every spawned order actually completes. The inspection's `version` is incremented by 1. An inspection acknowledged with no `HABITABILITY_CRITICAL` entries has a `critical_open_count` of 0 already and therefore auto-advances `TENANT_ACKNOWLEDGED → COMPLETED` inside the very same acknowledgment request.

The remaining edge into `COMPLETED` is driven by the system rather than by a caller: when the last non-`RESOLVED` critical deficiency's work order completes and that entry flips to `RESOLVED`, `critical_open_count` becomes 0 and the inspection advances `TENANT_ACKNOWLEDGED → COMPLETED` with its `version` incremented by 1.

Move-in itself is a `PropertyManager` action on the lease and takes no request body. It requires the lease's `occupancy_status` to be `PENDING_MOVE_IN` — a lease already `TERMINATED` answers `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE_LOCKED` and a lease in any other occupancy state answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` — requires **at least one** inspection on that lease in `TENANT_ACKNOWLEDGED` or `COMPLETED` status — otherwise `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_ACKNOWLEDGED` — requires the unit not to be in `HAZARD_HOLD`, which is the only unit-status gate the arrival carries and which answers `ERR_HAZARD_HOLD_ACTIVE` ahead of the habitability count, and requires at least one of those acknowledged or completed inspections to carry a `critical_open_count` of 0 — otherwise `ERR_UNRESOLVED_CRITICAL_DEFICIENCY`. Where a lease carries several inspections, one clear inspection is enough even if another still has criticals outstanding. No unit status other than `HAZARD_HOLD` blocks the arrival: a unit left `AVAILABLE`, `OFFLINE_MAINTENANCE` or already `OCCUPIED` is driven to `OCCUPIED` exactly as an `ASSIGNED` one is. A successful move-in does four things at once. It re-runs gross rent determination under the `MOVE_IN_FINAL` variant and overwrites `gross_rent_cents`, `utility_allowance_cents`, `net_rent_cents`, `daily_rent_cents` and `rent_effective_year`, setting `rent_basis` to `MOVE_IN_FINAL`. It advances the occupancy dimension `PENDING_MOVE_IN → OCCUPIED`. It advances the independent allotment dimension `NOT_STARTED → ACTIVE`, which is what makes the lease eligible for monthly charge posting. And it advances the unit `ASSIGNED → OCCUPIED`, recomputing the installation's `assigned_unit_count` and `occupied_unit_count` and incrementing the unit's `version` by 1. The lease's `version` is incremented by 1. The waitlist application is not touched; it is already `HOUSED`.

### 3.7. Maintenance: Priority Classification, SLA Clocks, Reopens, and Habitability Escalation

A `WorkOrder` may be raised by a `PropertyManager`, by a resident `ServiceMember`, or by a `MaintenanceTech` who finds something else while on site. A resident submitting one must own the lease they name: a `ServiceMember` whose `X-User-ID` is not the sponsor of the supplied `lease_id` is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`, while a submission carrying no `lease_id` at all is subject to no ownership check. The submission names the `housing_unit_id` the work is performed in, optionally the `lease_id` under which the work was requested — null for work on a vacant unit — optionally a `parent_work_order_id` naming the order whose incomplete repair spawned this follow-on, a `category`, a free-text `description` from the resident or inspector, and an optional `habitability_impact` flag defaulting to false which is set true when the condition makes any part of the dwelling uninhabitable. The `category` is one of `NO_HEAT`, `NO_WATER`, `GAS_LEAK`, `SEWAGE_BACKUP`, `ELECTRICAL`, `PLUMBING_LEAK`, `HVAC`, `APPLIANCE`, `COSMETIC`, `GROUNDS`, `MOLD_SUSPECTED` or `LEAD_SUSPECTED`. Where both a lease and a unit are supplied they must agree — the lease must be the unit's current non-`TERMINATED` tenancy, or the request fails with `ERR_LEASE_UNIT_MISMATCH` — and a supplied `parent_work_order_id` must belong to the same unit as the order being created, or the request fails with `ERR_WORK_ORDER_UNIT_MISMATCH`. On creation the order enters `status` `SUBMITTED` with `escalation_level` and `reopen_count` at 0, `sla_breached` false, and `first_response_at`, `completed_at`, `escalated_at`, `assigned_tech_user_id` and `inspection_deficiency_code` all null. Its `reported_at` is stamped from the logical clock and anchors both SLA clocks. Orders can be listed for reverse lookup, filterable by `housing_unit_id`, `lease_id`, the self-referential `parent_work_order_id`, `status` and `priority_class`.

**Priority classification** happens once, at submission, and produces `priority_class` together with both deadlines. Start from a base tier determined by the reported category, where tier 3 means emergency, tier 2 means urgent and tier 1 means routine. `NO_HEAT`, `NO_WATER`, `GAS_LEAK` and `SEWAGE_BACKUP` start at tier 3. `ELECTRICAL`, `PLUMBING_LEAK`, `HVAC`, `MOLD_SUSPECTED` and `LEAD_SUSPECTED` start at tier 2. `APPLIANCE`, `COSMETIC` and `GROUNDS` start at tier 1. Two independent upward floors are then applied, each of which raises the tier to at least the floor value and can never lower it: if `habitability_impact` is true the tier becomes at least 2, and if the category is `MOLD_SUSPECTED` or `LEAD_SUSPECTED` the tier likewise becomes at least 2. This is why a suspected mold or lead order is never routine even when the reporter left `habitability_impact` false. A third rule then applies, and it fires **only when both** of two conditions hold together: `habitability_impact` must be true, and the sponsor on the unit's `OCCUPIED` lease must have a full-time-custody dependent whose `relationship` is `CHILD` and whose age as of `reported_at` is under six years. When both hold, the tier becomes 3 outright. A unit with no `OCCUPIED` lease can never take this vulnerability escalation. The resulting `priority_class` is `EMERGENCY` at tier 3, `URGENT` at tier 2 and `ROUTINE` at tier 1.

Both SLA deadlines are measured in whole hours from `reported_at`. An `EMERGENCY` order must be responded to within 4 hours and completed within 24. An `URGENT` order must be responded to within 24 hours and completed within 72. A `ROUTINE` order must be responded to within 120 hours — five days — and completed within 720 hours, which is thirty days. The stored `response_due_at` and `completion_due_at` are `reported_at` plus those hour counts. One further rule is applied **after** that table lookup: when `habitability_impact` is true, `completion_due_at` is the earlier of the value just computed and `reported_at` plus 72 hours. A habitability-affecting routine-category order therefore inherits a three-day completion deadline rather than a thirty-day one. Both due timestamps are stored to whole seconds in ISO-8601 UTC form.

Dispatch is the `PropertyManager`'s exclusive act: assignment supplies the `assigned_tech_user_id` of the technician taking the job — recorded verbatim as supplied and never checked against any user or role record — and advances the order `SUBMITTED → ASSIGNED` or, on the loop path, `REOPENED → ASSIGNED`. Assignment stamps `first_response_at` from the logical clock **only when it is still null**, so the first assignment records the first response and a re-assignment after a reopen leaves the original first-response instant intact. The order's `version` is incremented by 1. A `MaintenanceTech` then advances `ASSIGNED → IN_PROGRESS`, which requires `assigned_tech_user_id` to be non-null — an order somehow sitting in `ASSIGNED` without one is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` — incrementing `version` by 1. The acting `X-User-ID` is **not** compared against `assigned_tech_user_id` on either the start or the completion call: the role check is the only gate, so any `MaintenanceTech` may start or complete an order another technician was assigned.

Completion is likewise the technician's act and may carry an optional `completion_note`, which is validated as a non-empty string when supplied but is not persisted on the order and never appears in any response; the same holds for the `reopen_reason` a reopen must carry. It stamps `completed_at` from the logical clock and recomputes `sla_breached`, which is true when `completed_at` is **strictly after** `completion_due_at` — landing exactly on the deadline is not a breach. Completion then forks on the reopen budget, and both branches carry the identical cascade set. When `reopen_count` is still below 2 the order advances `IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED`, leaving it re-enterable. When `reopen_count` has already reached 2 the reopen budget is spent, so the same request instead advances `IN_PROGRESS → CLOSED`, which is terminal. Either way the order's `version` is incremented by 1.

Two cascades hang off completion. If the order carries an `inspection_deficiency_code`, the matching deficiency entry on the lease's move-in inspection flips to `RESOLVED` as a second hop, `critical_open_count` is recomputed, and if that drives the count to 0 the inspection advances `TENANT_ACKNOWLEDGED → COMPLETED` as a third hop. If instead the order is the one a hazard case is tracking as its remediation order, the case advances out of `REMEDIATION_IN_PROGRESS` or `DISPLACEMENT_ACTIVE` into `CLEARANCE_PENDING` as a second hop; and when the case had been in `DISPLACEMENT_ACTIVE`, every `AUTHORIZED` lodging on it is closed with `actual_end_date` set to the logical clock's date, computing `nights_displaced`, `per_night_authorized_cents`, `lodging_authorized_cents` and `rent_abatement_cents` as a third hop, after which the reinvestment account for that installation and the calendar year of the close date has its `expenditures_cents` raised by the sum of those closed lodgings' `lodging_authorized_cents` as a fourth hop, and its `closing_balance_cents` recomputed with its `version` incremented by 1 as a fifth hop. §3.8 describes these figures in detail.

Residents who find the problem unfixed may reopen. Reopening is available to the `PropertyManager` and to the owning `ServiceMember`, requires a `reopen_reason`, and applies only to an order in `COMPLETED`. The reopen budgets are asymmetric on purpose: a resident may reopen only an order whose `reopen_count` is still 0 — one reopen per order, after which they must ask the housing office — while a manager may reopen an order whose `reopen_count` is still below 2. A resident acting on an order must own the lease named by the order's `lease_id`. When the caller's budget is spent the request is refused with `ERR_REOPEN_LIMIT_REACHED`. A successful reopen advances `COMPLETED → REOPENED`, raises `reopen_count` by 1, clears `completed_at`, and clears `assigned_tech_user_id` so the order must be dispatched again. `response_due_at` and `completion_due_at` are deliberately **not** recomputed: the original SLA clock keeps running, so a reopened order can already be past due the moment it reopens. If the order carries an `inspection_deficiency_code`, the matching deficiency entry returns from `RESOLVED` to `WORK_ORDER_CREATED`, `critical_open_count` is recomputed, and an inspection sitting in `COMPLETED` returns to `TENANT_ACKNOWLEDGED`. The order's `version` is incremented by 1.

**Habitability escalation** is the resident's lever against a landlord who lets a deadline slip, and it is open to the `ServiceMember`, the `PropertyManager` and the `InstallationHousingOffice`. Escalation is a self-loop: it is available from `SUBMITTED`, `ASSIGNED`, `IN_PROGRESS` and `REOPENED`, and it preserves the order's `status` while advancing the escalation ladder. Eligibility is measured in overdue hours: take the number of hours between `completion_due_at` and the current logical clock reading, which is negative while the order is not yet due. The target level is the order's current `escalation_level` plus one, and it is capped at 2. The required overdue hours depend on the priority class and the target level: an `EMERGENCY` order needs 0 overdue hours to reach level 1 and 24 to reach level 2; an `URGENT` order needs 24 overdue hours for level 1 and 96 for level 2; a `ROUTINE` order needs 240 overdue hours — ten days — for level 1 and 480 hours, twenty days, for level 2. The comparison is inclusive: escalation is eligible once the overdue hours are **at least** the required figure. When they are not, the request is refused with `ERR_ESCALATION_NOT_DUE`. A successful escalation raises `escalation_level` by 1 up to the maximum of 2, stamps `escalated_at` from the logical clock, recomputes `sla_breached`, and increments `version` by 1. A resident escalating must own the lease named by the order's `lease_id`, exactly as on a reopen. Because the target level is capped at 2, an order already standing at `escalation_level` 2 is measured against the level-2 threshold again and, when that threshold is met, escalates successfully rather than being refused: the level stays at 2 while `escalated_at` is re-stamped, `sla_breached` recomputed and `version` incremented. The inclusive threshold has a visible consequence at the emergency boundary: with a required overdue figure of 0 hours, a clock standing exactly at `completion_due_at` makes escalation eligible, while `sla_breached` at that same instant would still be false because the breach comparison is strict. One second earlier, escalation is refused with `ERR_ESCALATION_NOT_DUE`. Escalation never looks at sibling work orders, and orders escalated to level 1 or 2 count against the installation's habitability penalty at the next metrics run, as §3.9 describes; an order left at `escalation_level` 0 never penalises.

### 3.8. Environmental Hazards: The Assessment Fork, Displacement, Clearance, and Reversal

Mold and lead are handled by a protocol rather than by an ordinary work order, because a finding can displace a family and suspend their rent. A `HazardCase` is opened by a `PropertyManager`, a resident `ServiceMember` or an `EnvironmentalSpecialist`, naming the `housing_unit_id` where the hazard was reported, optionally the `lease_id` of the affected household, optionally the `source_work_order_id` of the `MOLD_SUSPECTED` or `LEAD_SUSPECTED` order that surfaced it, the `hazard_type` — one of `MOLD`, `LEAD_PAINT` or `LEAD_WATER`, which selects the severity threshold ladder — and the reporter's `reported_symptoms` narrative. A resident opening a case must own the lease they name — a `ServiceMember` supplying another sponsor's `lease_id` is refused with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`, while a case carrying no `lease_id` takes no ownership check. A supplied lease and unit must agree or the request fails with `ERR_LEASE_UNIT_MISMATCH`, and a supplied `source_work_order_id` must belong to the same unit or the request fails with `ERR_WORK_ORDER_UNIT_MISMATCH`. The case enters `status` `REPORTED` with `affected_area_sqft`, `lead_level_ppm`, `assessment_severity`, `assessed_by_user_id`, `assessed_at`, `cleared_by_user_id`, `clearance_test_passed` and `remediation_work_order_id` all null, and `child_under_six_present` and `displacement_required` both false. Reporting has **no** effect on the unit and no effect on the ledger: raising a concern is not yet a finding, and the unit only enters `HAZARD_HOLD` if a certified assessment concludes that displacement is required. Cases can be listed for reverse lookup, filterable by `housing_unit_id`, `lease_id`, `source_work_order_id`, `remediation_work_order_id`, `hazard_type` and `status`.

Assessment is exclusive to the `EnvironmentalSpecialist` and is the pivot of the whole protocol. It requires the case to be in `REPORTED`, and it carries the measurement appropriate to the hazard family plus a `child_under_six_present` declaration. For a `MOLD` case the request must supply `affected_area_sqft`, the visible mold area in square feet, and `lead_level_ppm` is ignored and stays null. For a `LEAD_PAINT` or `LEAD_WATER` case the request must supply `lead_level_ppm` — a concentration in parts per million for paint and parts per billion for water — and `affected_area_sqft` is ignored and stays null. The stored `child_under_six_present` reflects the family composition on file: it is true exactly when the sponsor on the case's lease has at least one full-time-custody dependent whose age at the assessment date is under six years, so a declared value that disagrees with the dependents on record does not change the outcome. A case carrying no `lease_id` has no family to read and therefore always stores `child_under_six_present` false, whatever the request declared.

**Severity grading** reads one measurement against the ladder for the hazard family. For `MOLD`, an affected area of at most 10 square feet is `SEVERITY_1`, an area greater than 10 but at most 30 square feet is `SEVERITY_2`, and anything greater than 30 square feet is `SEVERITY_3`; the mold bounds are inclusive upper bounds, so exactly 10 square feet is `SEVERITY_1` and exactly 30 square feet is `SEVERITY_2`. For `LEAD_PAINT`, a level below 5000 is `SEVERITY_1`, a level from 5000 up to but not including 10000 is `SEVERITY_2`, and 10000 or more is `SEVERITY_3`; the lead bounds are inclusive lower bounds, so exactly 5000 is `SEVERITY_2` and exactly 10000 is `SEVERITY_3`. For `LEAD_WATER`, a level below 10 is `SEVERITY_1`, a level from 10 up to but not including 15 is `SEVERITY_2`, and 15 or more is `SEVERITY_3`; again the bounds are inclusive lower bounds, so exactly 15 is `SEVERITY_3`. The stored `displacement_required` is true when the graded `assessment_severity` is `SEVERITY_3`, and also when it is `SEVERITY_2` **and** `child_under_six_present` is true. `SEVERITY_2` on its own does not displace a family; a child under six is what turns a moderate finding into a displacement.

Assessment stamps `assessment_severity`, `child_under_six_present` and `displacement_required`, records `assessed_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID` and `assessed_at` from the logical clock, and creates the remediation work order on the unit — with `category` `MOLD_SUSPECTED` for a mold case or `LEAD_SUSPECTED` for either lead case, and `habitability_impact` set to whatever `displacement_required` came out as — storing the new order's identifier in `remediation_work_order_id`. The case advances `REPORTED → ASSESSED` and then, inside the same request, forks along one of two system edges. The case's `version` is incremented by 1.

**Branch A** is taken when `displacement_required` is false: the case advances `ASSESSED → REMEDIATION_IN_PROGRESS`, the unit's `status` is **not** changed, the household stays in place, the lease's `allotment_status` is **not** changed, and **no** `ABATEMENT` row is posted. Rent continues to be collected while the repair proceeds.

**Branch B** is taken when `displacement_required` is true: the case advances `ASSESSED → DISPLACEMENT_ACTIVE`, and a four-deep cascade fires that branch A does not run. As a second hop the unit advances `OCCUPIED → HAZARD_HOLD` and its `hazard_hold_reason` is stamped with the hazard type together with the reason for displacement — the severity being `SEVERITY_3`, or `SEVERITY_2` with a child under six — while the installation's `occupied_unit_count` and `hazard_hold_unit_count` are recomputed and the unit's `version` is incremented by 1. As a third hop, because the unit entered `HAZARD_HOLD`, every lease on that unit whose `allotment_status` is `ACTIVE` advances `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` with the lease's `version` incremented by 1; leases in `NOT_STARTED`, `SUSPENDED` or `CLOSED` are left untouched and receive no abatement. That second hop fires only when the unit actually stands in `OCCUPIED`: a unit in any other status keeps its status, its `hazard_hold_reason` and its `version`, and the installation's counters are not recomputed — while the suspension and the abatement are driven by `displacement_required` alone and run even then. As a fourth hop, because the allotment was suspended, a habitability rent abatement is posted to the lease's ledger and the lease's `allotment_balance_cents` and `abatement_total_cents` are recomputed from that ledger. The household also becomes eligible for temporary lodging.

The **abatement posting** credits the resident for the remainder of the month in which the hazard was assessed. Take the date of `assessed_at` and the `period_month` it falls in. Count the whole days from that date to the last calendar day of that same month and add one, so the assessment day itself is included in the credit. The posted row is an `ABATEMENT` whose `period_month` is that month, whose `amount_cents` is the lease's stored `daily_rent_cents` multiplied by that day count and then **negated** — the negative sign is part of the amount, so the running balance decreases — and whose `source_ref` is the string `HAZARD_ABATEMENT:` followed by the hazard case's identifier. Prior abatement rows for the same period are not netted out; each qualifying hazard posts its own row.

Temporary lodging is authorised by a `PropertyManager` or an `EnvironmentalSpecialist` once the case has been assessed — a case still in `REPORTED` is refused with `ERR_HAZARD_NOT_ASSESSED`, and that is the only status gate the authorisation applies: `REPORTED` is the sole refused status, so a case sitting in `CLOSED` or `VOIDED` still accepts a new lodging authorisation. A `DisplacementLodging` names the `hazard_case_id` authorising the displacement, the `lease_id` of the displaced household, a `start_date` giving the first lodging night, an `end_date` giving the planned checkout, a `nightly_rate_cents` contract rate greater than 0, and an `occupants_count` of at least 1 giving the authorised occupants drawing per diem. The interval is **half-open**: a night is counted for each date from `start_date` inclusive up to `end_date` exclusive. No ordering constraint is enforced between the two at authorisation — `end_date` is a planned figure that never enters a money computation, so an `end_date` on or before `start_date` is accepted rather than rejected, and only the overlap rule reads it. Two constraints apply beyond the shared referential check that lease and case agree: the occupants may not exceed two plus the sponsor's full-time-custody dependent count, or the request fails with `ERR_OCCUPANTS_EXCEED_AUTHORIZED`; and the requested interval may not overlap any `AUTHORIZED` or `CLOSED` lodging interval already on the same lease, or the request fails with `ERR_LODGING_INTERVAL_OVERLAP`. Adjacency is explicitly permitted — a new lodging may begin on the exact date a previous one ended. A new lodging is created in `status` `AUTHORIZED` with `actual_end_date` null and `nights_displaced`, `per_night_authorized_cents`, `lodging_authorized_cents` and `rent_abatement_cents` all 0 until it closes. Lodgings can be listed for reverse lookup, filterable by `hazard_case_id`, `lease_id` and `status`.

Closing a lodging is where its money is computed. A `PropertyManager` closes it explicitly by supplying the `actual_end_date`, which is the exclusive checkout date and must be on or after `start_date` — an earlier date is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and because that comparison reads the stored lodging it is evaluated **after** the state rungs, so an already-`CLOSED` lodging answers `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE_LOCKED` even when the supplied date is also out of range; the system also closes every `AUTHORIZED` lodging automatically when its case leaves `DISPLACEMENT_ACTIVE`. Either way the lodging advances `AUTHORIZED → CLOSED`, which is terminal, and four figures are computed. `nights_displaced` is the number of whole days from `start_date` inclusive to `actual_end_date` exclusive, so a lodging closed on its own start date yields 0 nights. `per_night_authorized_cents` is the smaller of `nightly_rate_cents` and the nightly room-rate cap of 25000 cents — two hundred and fifty dollars a night — plus a per-occupant meal allowance of 6900 cents for each authorised occupant; the cap applies to the room rate only, and the meal allowance is added after the cap and is itself uncapped. `lodging_authorized_cents` is `per_night_authorized_cents` multiplied by `nights_displaced`, so a zero-night lodging authorises 0 cents. `rent_abatement_cents` is the lease's stored `daily_rent_cents` multiplied by `nights_displaced`, and the rounding order here is pinned: the per-day figure was rounded once when rent was set and is multiplied as an integer. The forbidden alternative — summing an unrounded daily fraction across the nights and rounding the total once — differs by as much as one cent short of the night count and must not be used. For a lease whose `net_rent_cents` is 218150, giving a `daily_rent_cents` of 7172, ten nights yield exactly 71720; the unrounded-then-rounded route would yield 71721, which is wrong. The lodging's `version` is incremented by 1.

Closing a lodging also moves the project's capital account. The reinvestment account for that installation and the calendar year of the `actual_end_date` has its `expenditures_cents` raised by the closed lodging's `lodging_authorized_cents` and its `closing_balance_cents` recomputed, with its `version` incremented by 1. This cascaded expenditure is deliberately **not** subject to the balance-sufficiency check, which guards only an operator-initiated expenditure; a displacement the project is obliged to pay for cannot be blocked by a thin account. When no account row exists for that installation and year the hop is skipped silently rather than failing the closure. No `AllotmentTransaction` row is posted for the lodging at all: the ledger abatement was already posted when the allotment was suspended, and `rent_abatement_cents` is the reconciliation figure the installation compares against it.

Both fork branches converge on `CLEARANCE_PENDING`, driven by the system when the work order named by `remediation_work_order_id` reaches `COMPLETED` or `CLOSED`. From `REMEDIATION_IN_PROGRESS` the transition to `CLEARANCE_PENDING` carries no lodging and no ledger effects, because branch A never created any. From `DISPLACEMENT_ACTIVE` the same transition closes every `AUTHORIZED` lodging on the case with `actual_end_date` set to the logical clock's date — recomputing `nights_displaced`, `per_night_authorized_cents`, `lodging_authorized_cents` and `rent_abatement_cents` for each — and then raises the `expenditures_cents` of the reinvestment account for that installation and the calendar year of the close date by the sum of those closed lodgings' `lodging_authorized_cents`, recomputing `closing_balance_cents` and incrementing that account's `version` by 1; when no such account row exists the hop is skipped silently.

Clearance is certified by an `EnvironmentalSpecialist` who is **not** the assessor: the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the case's `assessed_by_user_id` or the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The request carries a single `clearance_test_passed` boolean and requires the case to be in `CLEARANCE_PENDING`. The state answer comes first: a case in `REPORTED`, `ASSESSED`, `REMEDIATION_IN_PROGRESS` or `DISPLACEMENT_ACTIVE` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` and never with `ERR_HAZARD_NOT_ASSESSED`, a `CLOSED` or `VOIDED` case is refused with `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE_LOCKED`, and both of those outrank the segregation-of-duties comparison, which is only reached by a case genuinely standing in `CLEARANCE_PENDING`. On a **pass**, `cleared_by_user_id` and `clearance_test_passed` are stamped, the case advances `CLEARANCE_PENDING → CLOSED`, which is terminal, the unit advances `HAZARD_HOLD → OCCUPIED` with `hazard_hold_reason` cleared — but only when no other hazard case on that unit is in `DISPLACEMENT_ACTIVE`, `REMEDIATION_IN_PROGRESS` or `CLEARANCE_PENDING`, since a second open case keeps the hold in place — and the lease advances `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` with **no reinstating ledger row**; the lease reinstatement travels with the hold release, so when the hold is kept for a second open case, or when the unit was never on a hold at all, the lease is left in `SUSPENDED` and its `version` does not move: the abatement was earned by a real habitability failure and stays on the ledger. The installation's `occupied_unit_count` and `hazard_hold_unit_count` are recomputed and the case's `version` is incremented by 1. On a **fail**, `clearance_test_passed` is stamped false, `cleared_by_user_id` is cleared, the case cycles back `CLEARANCE_PENDING → REMEDIATION_IN_PROGRESS`, a follow-on work order is created on the same unit and lease with its `parent_work_order_id` pointing at the old remediation order and `remediation_work_order_id` repointed to the new one — its `category` is again `MOLD_SUSPECTED` or `LEAD_SUSPECTED` by hazard family and its `habitability_impact` is the case's stored `displacement_required`, so it is priced and clocked by §3.7 exactly as the first remediation order was — the unit stays in `HAZARD_HOLD` when the case had displaced the household, and the case's `version` is incremented by 1.

Voiding is the compensating action for a case opened in error, and it belongs to a `PlatformAdmin` or an `InstallationHousingOffice` user. It carries a `void_reason` and is available from `ASSESSED`, `REMEDIATION_IN_PROGRESS`, `DISPLACEMENT_ACTIVE` or `CLEARANCE_PENDING`, driving the case to `VOIDED`, which is terminal. A void unwinds every side effect the case caused. Any still-open remediation work order is driven to `CLOSED`. Every `AUTHORIZED` lodging on the case is closed with `actual_end_date` set equal to its own `start_date`, which yields `nights_displaced` of 0 and `lodging_authorized_cents` of 0; because the authorised figure is 0 these forced closures move no reinvestment account at all — no `expenditures_cents` changes and no account `version` advances — even though an ordinary lodging closure does. Every `ABATEMENT` row this case posted is reversed by an equal and opposite `ARREARS_ADJUSTMENT` row — one such row per affected lease, carrying a positive amount equal to the absolute value of the sum of the `ABATEMENT` rows whose `source_ref` names this case, reusing that same `HAZARD_ABATEMENT:` `source_ref` and taking its `period_month` from the last of the rows it reverses, and posted only when that sum is non-zero — which restores the lease's `allotment_balance_cents` to its pre-abatement value and drives `abatement_total_cents` back to exactly 0 with no drift. The unit advances `HAZARD_HOLD → OCCUPIED` with `hazard_hold_reason` cleared, the lease advances `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE`, the installation's unit counters are recomputed, and the case's `version` is incremented by 1. The contrast with clearance is the whole point of the design: a clearance leaves the abatement standing because the hazard was real, and a void reverses it because the hazard was not.

### 3.9. Collection, Disputes, Termination, and Project Performance

Rent is collected by allotment, and the collection is a monthly ops-run checkpoint rather than a per-lease operation. A `PropertyManager` or a `PlatformAdmin` triggers an **allotment run** against an installation for a supplied `period_month`. The run gathers the installation's leases whose `allotment_status` is `ACTIVE` and processes them ordered by `created_at` ascending, which fixes the posting order deterministically. Leases in `NOT_STARTED`, `SUSPENDED` or `CLOSED` are skipped entirely — a family that has not yet moved in is not charged, and a family displaced by a hazard is not charged while the hold stands. For each eligible lease, if a `CHARGE` row already exists for that lease and that `period_month` the lease is skipped, which makes a repeat run for the same period a no-op and guarantees at most one charge row per lease per period. Otherwise a `CHARGE` row is posted whose `amount_cents` is the lease's current `net_rent_cents`, whose `period_month` is the supplied period, and whose `source_ref` is the string `ALLOTMENT_RUN:` followed by that period — so a September 2026 run stamps `ALLOTMENT_RUN:2026-09`. Each posting advances the lease's `allotment_balance_cents` to the new running balance as §2.7 requires and increments that lease's `version` by exactly 1, while a lease skipped for either reason is left entirely untouched. The duplicate test looks only at row `type` and `period_month`, so any pre-existing `CHARGE` row for the period suppresses the run's own charge regardless of what `source_ref` produced it. The run applies no future-period check: any well-formed `period_month` is accepted, including one later than the logical clock, which is where it differs from the metrics run. The run returns the installation record, whose own `version` it does not change.

Residents who believe a charge or a repair was wrong open a `DisputeCase`, and only a `ServiceMember` may open one. The payload names the `lease_id` the dispute concerns, the `service_member_id` of the sponsor — which must exist and must be the lease's own sponsor, enforced by the ownership rung rather than by any mismatch code, since only the owning `ServiceMember` may open a case and both the supplied sponsor and the supplied lease's sponsor are compared against `X-User-ID`; the stored value is copied from the lease itself — a `category` of `RENT_CHARGE`, `MAINTENANCE_QUALITY`, `HABITABILITY`, `DAMAGE_CLAIM` or `UTILITY_BILLING`, and the resident's `description`. The lease's `occupancy_status` must be `OCCUPIED` or `NOTICE_GIVEN`, or the request is refused with `ERR_LEASE_NOT_OCCUPIED`. The case opens in `status` `UNDER_REVIEW` with `round_count` 0, `credit_offer_cents` and `credit_award_cents` both 0, and `proposed_remedy`, `proposed_by_user_id`, `decided_by_user_id` and `escalated_at` all null. Its `opened_at` is stamped from the logical clock and its `resolution_due_at` is set to `opened_at` plus 30 days. Cases can be listed for reverse lookup, filterable by `lease_id`, `service_member_id`, `category` and `status`.

Negotiation is a bounded loop. A `PropertyManager` — and only a `PropertyManager` — proposes a remedy against a case in `UNDER_REVIEW`, supplying the `proposed_remedy` text and a `credit_offer_cents` rent credit which may be 0. A proposal is permitted only while `round_count` is below the round cap of 3; a proposal against a case whose `round_count` has already reached 3 is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (409) rather than any cap-specific code. It raises `round_count` by 1, stores the remedy and the offer, stamps `proposed_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`, advances the case `UNDER_REVIEW → PROPOSED_RESOLUTION`, and increments `version` by 1.

The resident then responds — again exclusively — with a single `accepted` boolean. **Accepting** advances the case `PROPOSED_RESOLUTION → CLOSED_RESOLVED`, which is terminal, and sets `credit_award_cents` equal to the standing `credit_offer_cents`. When the awarded credit is greater than 0 a `REFUND` row is posted to the lease's ledger with `amount_cents` equal to the negated award, `source_ref` equal to `DISPUTE_AWARD:` followed by the dispute case's identifier, and a `period_month` taken from the calendar month of the logical clock at the moment of the award rather than from any period the dispute concerns, after which the lease's `allotment_balance_cents` is recomputed and the lease's `version` is incremented by 1. An award of exactly 0 posts no row and leaves the lease entirely untouched. The same `period_month` rule governs the `REFUND` an installation decision posts. A positive credit may only be awarded while the lease's `allotment_status` is `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED`; against a `CLOSED` ledger the acceptance is refused with `ERR_ALLOTMENT_NOT_ACTIVE`. **Rejecting** advances the case `PROPOSED_RESOLUTION → RESIDENT_REJECTED` and then immediately forks on the round count inside the same request. When `round_count` is still below the cap of 3 the case cycles back `RESIDENT_REJECTED → UNDER_REVIEW` for another round, the `credit_offer_cents` is left on the record as the standing offer, and no ledger row is posted. When `round_count` has reached 3 the case instead goes `RESIDENT_REJECTED → ESCALATED_INSTALLATION` with `escalated_at` stamped from the logical clock; the case leaves the property manager's authority and no further landlord proposal is accepted. Either path increments the case's `version` by 1. The cap exists so that a resident cannot be held in an endless negotiation.

An escalated case is decided by the government housing office, and only an `InstallationHousingOffice` user may decide it. The decision carries the `credit_award_cents` finally awarded and an optional `decision_note`, requires the case to be in `ESCALATED_INSTALLATION`, and requires the acting `X-User-ID` to differ from the case's `proposed_by_user_id` or it is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. It stores `decided_by_user_id` and `credit_award_cents`, posts a `REFUND` row of the negated award to the lease's ledger when the award is greater than 0 and recomputes `allotment_balance_cents` — again refused with `ERR_ALLOTMENT_NOT_ACTIVE` when the lease's ledger is `CLOSED` — advances the case `ESCALATED_INSTALLATION → CLOSED_RESOLVED`, and increments `version` by 1. An award granted here has a long tail: it reduces the fee base of any later termination settlement on the same lease.

Leaving privatized housing begins with a **termination notice**, which a `PropertyManager`, the owning `ServiceMember`, or an `InstallationHousingOffice` user may give against a lease whose `occupancy_status` is `OCCUPIED`. The lease is the notice's primary target, so the state answer comes from rungs 19 and 20 rather than from rung 25: a `TERMINATED` lease answers `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE_LOCKED` and a lease in `PENDING_MOVE_IN` or already in `NOTICE_GIVEN` answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, since `OCCUPIED → NOTICE_GIVEN` is the only notice edge this document declares. Rung 25 is therefore never the observed answer on a notice or on a termination — it reaches only the dispute opening, where the lease is a referenced record rather than the primary target. The notice carries a `termination_reason` of `PCS`, `DEPLOYMENT`, `SEPARATION`, `CONVENIENCE` or `LANDLORD`, the `termination_notice_date` on which notice was given, and the `termination_effective_date` on which the tenancy ends — the last day the unit is occupied under this lease. Two reasons are statutory rights and must be documented: a `PCS` reason requires the sponsor to have `pcs_orders_ref` on file, and a `DEPLOYMENT` reason requires both `deployment_orders_ref` on file and a `deployment_status` of `DEPLOYED`; a claim without its backing orders is refused with `ERR_ORDERS_NOT_ON_FILE`. The notice period is also reason-dependent: `termination_effective_date` must be at least 30 whole days after `termination_notice_date`, except for `PCS` and `DEPLOYMENT`, which permit as few as 14 whole days. A shorter notice is refused with `ERR_NOTICE_PERIOD_TOO_SHORT`. A valid notice advances the occupancy dimension `OCCUPIED → NOTICE_GIVEN`, records all three supplied values, and increments the lease's `version` by 1. The notice deliberately leaves `allotment_status` untouched: rent keeps being collected right up to termination.

**Termination** itself is the `PropertyManager`'s closing act and takes no request body. It requires the lease's `occupancy_status` to be `NOTICE_GIVEN` — an already `TERMINATED` lease answers `ERR_TERMINAL_STATE_LOCKED`, and a lease in `PENDING_MOVE_IN` or still merely `OCCUPIED` answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, because `NOTICE_GIVEN → TERMINATED` is the only termination edge declared — and requires the logical clock's date to have reached or passed `termination_effective_date`, a premature closing also being refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (409) rather than any date-specific code; the clock's date landing exactly on `termination_effective_date` is permitted. It advances the occupancy dimension `NOTICE_GIVEN → TERMINATED`, which is terminal, and advances the allotment dimension from `ACTIVE` or from `SUSPENDED` to `CLOSED` — both source states are permitted and both run the identical settlement and reinvestment cascade, the only difference being that abatement rows already sitting on a suspended lease's ledger reduce the settlement base. The unit is driven to `AVAILABLE` from whatever status it held — normally `OCCUPIED`, but a unit still parked in `HAZARD_HOLD` behind the suspended-allotment path is released to `AVAILABLE` too, and its `hazard_hold_reason` is left standing since termination is not a clearance — with `last_lease_end_date` set to `termination_effective_date`, which arms the turn-buffer rule for whoever leases the unit next, and the installation's `occupied_unit_count` and `available_unit_count` are recomputed with the unit's `version` incremented by 1. The lease's `version` is incremented by 1.

The **settlement calculation** runs in five pinned steps and each step consumes the previous step's output; applying the credit later, or choosing the tier earlier, produces a different total and is wrong. Step one prorates the final partial month: take the first day of the calendar month containing `termination_effective_date`, count the whole days from that first day to `termination_effective_date` and add one so both endpoints are included, and multiply that day count by the lease's stored `daily_rent_cents` to get the prorated rent. Step two sums the dispute credits already awarded on this lease, adding up `credit_award_cents` across every `DisputeCase` on the lease whose `status` is `CLOSED_RESOLVED`; disputes in any other status contribute nothing. Step three computes the fee base as the prorated rent less those awarded credits, floored at 0 — credits reduce the base **before** the tier is chosen. Step four selects the tier rate on that reduced base: a fee base below 100000 cents, that is below one thousand dollars, takes the low rate of five percent, and a fee base of 100000 cents or more takes the high rate of eight percent. The reason rate is then zero for `PCS`, `DEPLOYMENT`, `SEPARATION` and `LANDLORD` — the statutory and landlord-caused exemptions — and equal to the selected tier rate only for `CONVENIENCE`. Only a convenience termination pays a fee at all. Step five multiplies the fee base by the reason rate and rounds the product half up to whole cents, once, storing the result as `early_termination_fee_cents`; `settlement_total_cents` is the fee base plus that fee.

Termination then posts the settlement to the ledger and moves the capital account, in a cascade three hops deep. As a second hop, because the allotment dimension became `CLOSED`, settlement rows are posted: a `CHARGE` row for the prorated final period, whose amount is the prorated rent from step one, and — only when `early_termination_fee_cents` is greater than 0 — an `EARLY_TERMINATION_FEE` row for the fee amount. Both carry a `source_ref` of `TERMINATION_SETTLEMENT:` followed by the lease's identifier, and the lease's `allotment_balance_cents` is recomputed. As a third hop, because settlement rows were posted, **every** reinvestment account row for that installation whose `fiscal_year` equals the calendar year of the `termination_effective_date`, taken in `created_at` ascending order, has its `contributions_cents` raised by ten percent of the settlement charges just posted — the sum of those settlement rows' amounts — rounded half up to whole cents. Like the lodging-closure expenditure of §3.8, this contribution is a silently skipped hop when no account row matches that installation and year: the termination still succeeds. It is also skipped when the rounded contribution comes to 0 cents, in which case no account row is touched and no account `version` moves. As a fourth hop, because `contributions_cents` changed, that account's `closing_balance_cents` is recomputed and its `version` is incremented by 1.

The project's capital pool is a `ReinvestmentAccount`, opened by a `PlatformAdmin` for an installation and a fiscal year. Creation names the `installation_id`, the `fiscal_year` covered (2024 through 2030), an `opening_balance_cents` carried in at the start of the year which must be at least 0, and an `occupancy_basis` of `UNIT_DAY_WEIGHTED` or `MONTH_END_SNAPSHOT`. The basis is pinned at creation and is immutable thereafter: every metrics run on the account uses the variant named here. On creation `contributions_cents` and `expenditures_cents` initialise to 0 and `closing_balance_cents` to `opening_balance_cents`; `occupancy_rate_pct`, `net_collected_cents`, `habitability_penalty_pct` and `performance_incentive_fee_cents` all initialise to 0; `metrics_run_epoch` initialises to 0 and `last_metrics_run_at` to null. The account's `closing_balance_cents` is always `opening_balance_cents` plus `contributions_cents` less `expenditures_cents`. An installation may carry more than one account row — two fiscal years, or two rows measuring the same period under different bases — and where a lodging closure cascades into an account it targets the account rows whose `fiscal_year` equals the calendar year of the close date, in `created_at` ascending order. Accounts can be listed, filterable by `installation_id`, `fiscal_year` and `occupancy_basis`.

Drawing on the account is an **expenditure authorisation** by a `PropertyManager` or a `PlatformAdmin`. It carries `expected_account_version`, the `amount_cents` being drawn, and a `purpose` describing the capital project. The request is refused with `ERR_REINVESTMENT_BALANCE_INSUFFICIENT` when `amount_cents` exceeds the account's current `closing_balance_cents`. On success `expenditures_cents` rises by `amount_cents`, `closing_balance_cents` is recomputed, and the account's `version` is incremented by 1. An `amount_cents` of 0 is a well-formed authorisation: it passes the balance gate, leaves both money figures where they were, and still increments the account's `version`. This balance gate applies here and nowhere else: the expenditure that a displacement-lodging closure cascades into the account bypasses it, as §3.8 states.

The **metrics run** is the second ops-run checkpoint and is triggered by a `PropertyManager` or a `PlatformAdmin` against one account for a supplied `period_month`. A period that already has a metrics run recorded on this account, or a period that lies in the future relative to the logical clock, is refused with `ERR_PERIOD_ALREADY_POSTED`. The run computes the occupancy rate first, then the incentive fee.

**Occupancy** is measured over every `HousingUnit` of the account's installation — including units in `OFFLINE_MAINTENANCE` and `HAZARD_HOLD`, because taking a unit out of service is supposed to lower the measured rate rather than shrink the denominator it is measured against. Let the period run from the first day to the last day of the supplied `period_month`, and let the day count of that month be its number of calendar days. Under the `UNIT_DAY_WEIGHTED` basis, the numerator is the sum, over all of the installation's units, of the number of days in the period on which that unit had a lease whose `occupancy_status` was `OCCUPIED` or `NOTICE_GIVEN`, where a lease counts from its `start_date` through the earlier of its `termination_effective_date` and the period's last day, inclusive at both ends; the denominator is the unit count multiplied by the month's day count. A mid-month move-in therefore contributes only the tail of the month. Under the `MONTH_END_SNAPSHOT` basis, the numerator is the count of units whose stored `status` is `OCCUPIED` at the moment the run executes and the denominator is simply the unit count, so any occupied unit counts fully no matter when the family arrived. The snapshot reads the live inventory rather than reconstructing the period's last day — the system keeps no unit-status history, so a unit occupied now counts even for a run over an earlier period, and a unit since taken to `HAZARD_HOLD` or `AVAILABLE` does not count even though it was occupied at month end. An installation carrying no units at all yields an `occupancy_rate_pct` of 0.00 under either basis rather than failing on a zero denominator. In both cases the stored `occupancy_rate_pct` is 100 multiplied by the numerator, divided by the denominator, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. The divergence is material: for two units where one was occupied all 30 days of 2026-09 and the other from 2026-09-07 onward, which is 24 days, the unit-day basis yields 90.00 while the month-end snapshot yields 100.00 — ten full points apart, which lands the installation in different incentive multiplier bands.

The run then computes the period's collections and the owner's fee. The stored `net_collected_cents` is the sum of `amount_cents` across **every** `AllotmentTransaction` row belonging to a lease of that installation and carrying the supplied `period_month`, of every row type — so negative `ABATEMENT` and `REFUND` rows net against positive `CHARGE` rows and the figure can fall below the gross amount charged, and can itself go negative for a period whose credits exceed its charges. No flooring is applied at any point: a negative `net_collected_cents` carries its sign straight through the base fee, the incentive fee and the reinvestment contribution, all of which are then negative figures. The base fee is `net_collected_cents` multiplied by the installation's `incentive_base_pct`, rounded half up to whole cents. An occupancy multiplier is then selected from the rate just computed, with inclusive lower band edges: a rate below 85 takes a multiplier of 0.00, a rate from 85 up to but not including 92 takes 0.50, a rate from 92 up to but not including 96 takes 1.00, and a rate of 96 or above takes 1.25. Exactly 92.00 therefore pays the 1.00 multiplier while 91.99 pays 0.50. Next comes the habitability penalty: count the installation's work orders whose `escalation_level` is at least 1 and whose `reported_at` falls within the period, multiply that count by five percent, and cap the result at fifty percent; the result is stored as `habitability_penalty_pct` as a decimal **fraction** rather than as percentage points, so three escalated orders store `0.15` and any count of ten or more stores the capped `0.5`. It is serialised as an ordinary JSON number, so a penalty of zero appears as `0.0`. The occupancy multiplier is used in the fee arithmetic but is not stored on the account. Orders left at `escalation_level` 0 never penalise. The stored `performance_incentive_fee_cents` is the base fee multiplied by the occupancy multiplier and then by one minus the habitability penalty fraction, rounded half up to whole cents — so a project with an occupancy rate below 85 earns nothing regardless of collections, and a project with ten escalated orders in the period loses half of what it would otherwise earn.

Finally the run contributes to the capital pool and records itself. The account's `contributions_cents` grows by ten percent of `net_collected_cents`, rounded half up to whole cents, and `closing_balance_cents` is recomputed as the opening balance plus contributions less expenditures. `metrics_run_epoch` is raised by exactly 1, `last_metrics_run_at` is stamped from the logical clock, and the account's `version` is incremented by 1.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

All paths below are top-level; there is no versioning or namespace prefix. Every request must carry the `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` headers described in §2.1. Endpoints that create a new resource return HTTP 201 with the created resource in `data`; endpoints that read, list, or modify an existing resource return HTTP 200 with the resulting resource — or, for list endpoints, the page of resources plus the pagination `meta` block of §2.3 — in `data`. Every list endpoint accepts `limit` and `offset` and returns its rows in `created_at` ascending order unless the workflow text pins a different order. Failures return the error envelope of §2.2 with the `error_id` and status selected by the validation ladder of §2.5. One route sits outside this table and outside the envelope contract entirely: `GET /health` is unauthenticated and answers HTTP 200 with the bare body `{"status": "ok"}`, carrying neither a `meta` block nor a `data` wrapper.
