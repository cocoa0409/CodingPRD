# Police Recruit Academy & Field Training API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A municipal police academy carries a legal burden that few training organisations share: every hour of instruction, every examination result, every daily observation of a probationary officer, and every adverse decision may be re-read years later by a state certification commission or by opposing counsel in litigation. Today that burden is discharged with spreadsheets, paper daily observation reports and a filing cabinet, which means the academy cannot answer two questions that matter most to the chief: *is this recruit actually certifiable against the state standard*, and *how many applicants must we recruit next cycle to field the officers the city has budgeted for*.

This document specifies the Police Recruit Academy & Field Training API, a single service that owns the whole pipeline from applicant intake through state certification. It models the selection screening pipeline (background investigation, medical, psychological and physical fitness), an academy curriculum mapped hour-by-hour to a state POST commission standard, examinations with a bounded remediation budget and dismissal criteria, a field training programme with deliberately rotating trainers and standardized observation reports, and a probationary period whose length flexes according to measured competency. Underneath all of it sits an append-only training record: an immutable evidentiary chain that is written by the system as a side effect of ordinary work, never by an operator directly, and never by the custodian who keeps it.

Two design commitments run through every section that follows. The first is **segregation of duties** — the operator who records a screening's findings may not adjudicate them, the instructor who writes a lesson plan may not approve it, and an instructor may serve as a recruit's Field Training Officer at most once across the entire field-training programme. The second is **arithmetic determinism** — hours, competency scores, attrition rates and probation dates are all derived quantities, and this document pins the evaluation order and rounding step of each one, because a cent-level or day-level difference in a certification record is a defect, not a rounding preference. Every path in this API is mounted at the top level; there is no shared URL prefix.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contracts that hold everywhere: authentication, response shape, pagination, the logical clock, arithmetic precision, the order in which rejections are decided, concurrency control, and the cascade discipline that keeps derived values honest.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request — without exception, including reads and including the test-driving clock endpoint — must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting user and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity in which they act. If either header is absent or empty, or if `X-User-Role` names anything other than one of the eight roles below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). A header carrying only whitespace counts as empty. Both values are trimmed of surrounding whitespace before use — including before an ownership comparison — and the role name is matched exactly and case-sensitively, so `academydirector` is not a recognised role. If the headers are well formed but the declared role is not authorized for the target operation, the rejection is `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The eight roles are `AcademyDirector`, `TrainingCoordinator`, `BackgroundInvestigator`, `MedicalExaminer`, `AcademyInstructor`, `FieldTrainingOfficer`, `RecordsCustodian` and `Recruit`.

- An **`AcademyDirector`** is the commanding officer of the academy: sole authority for certification standards, cohort creation, instructor certification and revocation, lesson plan approval and revision requests, adverse actions (dismissal, probation decisions, delivery voids) and graduation certification.
- A **`TrainingCoordinator`** runs the day-to-day pipeline: enrols applicants, orders screenings, advances pipeline stages, schedules course sessions and examinations, assigns remediation, and plans and activates field training rotations.
- A **`BackgroundInvestigator`** conducts and adjudicates the background investigation leg of selection.
- A **`MedicalExaminer`** is the contract physician or psychologist who conducts and adjudicates the medical and psychological legs.
- An **`AcademyInstructor`** is a certified classroom instructor who authors lesson plans, records attendance, closes out taught sessions and grades examinations, and who never approves their own plan.
- A **`FieldTrainingOfficer`** is a certified FTO who supervises one probationary recruit during a rotation and files the standardized daily observation reports.
- A **`RecordsCustodian`** is the custodian of training records for certification audit and future litigation, and is read-only by design: it may inspect every record and every report but may never mutate one, so the evidentiary chain cannot be altered by its keeper.
- A **`Recruit`** is the recruit themself, who may read only their own record, their own certification progress, their own examinations, their own rotations and their own observation reports. Only the first two of those are enforced by rejection, because the ownership gate below is armed on those two reads alone: a `Recruit` reading an examination, a rotation or an observation report that belongs to another recruit is answered normally rather than refused.

**How `X-User-ID` is interpreted.** Five of the roles are *opaque*: when `X-User-Role` is `AcademyDirector`, `TrainingCoordinator`, `BackgroundInvestigator`, `MedicalExaminer` or `RecordsCustodian`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a rule needs a durable actor — as `findings_recorded_by_user_id` and `adjudicated_by_user_id` on a screening record, as `approved_by_user_id` on a lesson plan, as `graded_by_user_id` on an examination, and as `actor_user_id` (alongside `actor_role`) on a training record entry — but it is never compared against any entity's `id`.

The remaining three roles are *entity-backed*, and for them `X-User-ID` is a resource identifier that ownership checks compare against:

- When `X-User-Role` is `AcademyInstructor`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Instructor`; ownership checks compare a lesson plan's `instructor_id` to `X-User-ID`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `FieldTrainingOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Instructor`; ownership checks compare a rotation's `fto_instructor_id` to `X-User-ID`.
- When `X-User-Role` is `Recruit`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Recruit`; ownership checks compare the target recruit's own `id` — or, for a row that hangs off a recruit, the `recruit_id` chain that resolves to it — to `X-User-ID`.

An entity-backed role acting on a resource it does not own is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). This ownership gate is armed on exactly four operations: submitting a lesson plan revision (`POST /lesson-plans/{lessonPlanId}/revisions`), filing an observation report (`POST /field-training-rotations/{rotationId}/observation-reports`), reading a single recruit (`GET /recruits/{recruitId}`), and reading a recruit's certification progress (`GET /recruits/{recruitId}/certification-progress`). On the two recruit reads the comparison is made against the path identifier exactly as written, *before* that identifier is resolved, so a `Recruit` naming someone else's recruit id — or an id that names nothing at all — answers `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` rather than `ERR_RECRUIT_NOT_FOUND`; and because the gate is armed only for the entity-backed `Recruit`, an opaque role reading a nonexistent recruit still answers the 404. On the other two operations the comparison is made the other way round — *after* the path identifier has resolved — so a revision or a report aimed at an identifier that names nothing answers `ERR_LESSON_PLAN_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_ROTATION_NOT_FOUND` rather than the 403. One further ownership comparison exists outside this gate and is decided much later than rung 4: `POST /lesson-plans` refuses an `AcademyInstructor` whose `X-User-ID` is not the `instructor_id` they are authoring for, but only after the body foreign key has resolved at rung 12 and the body has passed shape at rung 20 (§3.3).

**Subject-scoped screening authority.** Recording findings on a screening and adjudicating one are shared operations, but each acting role owns only its own leg of the selection pipeline. A `BackgroundInvestigator` may act only on a screening record whose `screening_type` is `BACKGROUND`; a `MedicalExaminer` only on `MEDICAL` or `PSYCHOLOGICAL`; a `TrainingCoordinator` only on `PHYSICAL_FITNESS`. Acting outside one's own subject is `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`, but because that check needs the screening record in hand it is necessarily decided *after* the path identifier has resolved at rung 10 and before body shape at rung 20 — so a subject-scope violation aimed at a screening id that names nothing answers `ERR_SCREENING_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`, not the 403. The retest decision narrows further still: only a `TrainingCoordinator` may order a retest, and a caller in another role naming that decision is refused `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` — a narrowing keyed to the decision in the body, and therefore decided after body shape rather than at the role rung. The waiver decision carries no separate role gate of its own: subject scope alone is what confines a waiver to a `BackgroundInvestigator` or a `MedicalExaminer`, because the only `screening_type` a `TrainingCoordinator` owns is `PHYSICAL_FITNESS` and waiving that one is refused later, at rung 54, as `ERR_DISQUALIFIER_UNWAIVED`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`, followed by exactly 20 characters drawn from the mixed-case ASCII letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. It is read from the logical clock of §2.5 rather than from wall time, so two requests taken without moving the clock carry the same value.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints only; a single-resource or action response carries a `meta` object of just `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`. `has_more` is true exactly when `offset` plus the number of rows returned in `data` is strictly less than `total_count`, so an `offset` past the end of the result set reads `false`.
- Envelope keys are emitted in the order written above — `meta`, `response_type`, `data`, and inside `meta` the two request keys followed by `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more` — and are never re-sorted alphabetically. The same holds inside a resource object (§2.4).

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong. Its wording is not part of the contract; only `error_id` and the HTTP status are.
- Failures raised outside the declared endpoint surface use the same envelope but are not part of the catalogue of §5: a request to an unrouted path, or one using a method that path does not declare, answers `error_id` `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` carrying the transport status of the failure (404 or 405 respectively) rather than 400, and any otherwise-unclassified server-side failure answers `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` with 400. A trailing slash on any declared path is tolerated and resolves to the same endpoint.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint is paginated through two query parameters. `limit` is the maximum number of rows returned; it defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` is the number of rows to skip; it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` that is not an integer in the range 1 through 100 inclusive, or an `offset` that is not an integer of at least 0, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400). Only an entirely absent parameter takes its default: a parameter supplied with an empty value is a rejection, not an omission. The value must be a bare run of decimal digits with an optional leading sign — surrounding whitespace is trimmed, but a fractional or non-numeric form such as `10.0` is rejected — and `limit` is examined before `offset`. Neither parameter has any effect on a single-resource endpoint, which never rejects them.

Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending, with rows sharing a `created_at` — the common case, because the logical clock only moves when an operator moves it — ordered by ascending order of creation, so any page is a stable prefix of insertion order. The response envelope's `meta` object carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` on every list response. `total_count` is the number of rows that survived the filters, counted before `limit` and `offset` are applied; an `offset` at or beyond that count yields an empty `data` array with the same `total_count` rather than an error.

Filter query parameters are additive and are applied before pagination. They exist so that a caller can walk any foreign key backwards: certification standards may be filtered by `state_code`; cohorts by `certification_standard_id` and `status`; recruits by `academy_class_id`, `pipeline_stage`, `clearance_status`, `academy_status` and `probation_status`; screening records by `recruit_id`, `screening_type` and `status`; instructors by `certification_status` and `fto_certified`; lesson plans by `instructor_id`, `topic_code` and `status`; course sessions by `academy_class_id`, `lesson_plan_id`, `instructor_id`, `topic_code` and `status`; attendance records by `course_session_id`, `recruit_id` and `ledger_status`; examinations by `recruit_id`, `topic_code` and `status`; field training rotations by `recruit_id`, `fto_instructor_id`, `prior_rotation_id` and `status`; observation reports by `field_training_rotation_id`, `author_instructor_id` and `voided`; and training record entries by `recruit_id`, `academy_class_id`, `kind` and `stage_code`. A filter supplied with an empty value is treated as absent — the two exceptions being `state_code` on certification standards and `certification_standard_id` on cohorts, which are matched literally and so select nothing. A filter over an enumerated field (`status`, `pipeline_stage`, `clearance_status`, `academy_status`, `probation_status`, `screening_type`, `certification_status`, `ledger_status`, `kind`, `stage_code`) whose value is not a member of that enumeration is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400), decided at rung 20 and therefore *before* pagination is examined at rung 22; the two boolean filters, `fto_certified` and `voided`, accept `true`, `false`, `1` and `0` in any letter case and reject anything else the same way. Identifier-valued and code-valued filters are compared literally and are never resolved, so a filter naming a row that does not exist is an empty result rather than a 404.

### 2.4. Common Record Properties and the Immutable Training Record

Every resource in this system carries a server-minted `id`, formatted as a UUID with no type prefix — 36 characters, five groups of lowercase hexadecimal digits of length 8, 4, 4, 4 and 12 joined by hyphens — and a `created_at` timestamp: an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits, read from the logical clock of §2.5 at the moment the row was first written.

All resources except one also carry `updated_at` and `version`. `updated_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, refreshed on every mutation of the row; a newly created row carries `version` 1 and an `updated_at` identical to its `created_at`. `version` is an integer that increments by one on every mutation of the row, and it is the value optimistic concurrency control compares against (§2.8). Re-deriving a computed field counts as a mutation of the row that owns it: when a delivery credits hours to a recruit, the recruit's `updated_at` and `version` both move, and the same is true of a cohort whose projected hours were recomputed or an instructor whose trainee load changed. The increment is **once per row per request**, not once per touch: a single request that re-derives the same recruit twice still advances that recruit from version 4 to version 5, and stamps its `updated_at` once. Within a resource object the entity's own fields are emitted first with `id` leading, and `created_at`, `updated_at` and `version` are emitted last, in that order.

The single exception is the **training record entry**. `TrainingRecordEntry` rows are append-only and immutable, so they carry only `id` and `created_at` — they have neither `updated_at` nor `version`, and neither field appears in their representation. Nothing in this API updates or deletes a training record entry once written; the litigation record is grow-only.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

The service runs on a logical UTC clock rather than wall time, so that certification expiry, session delivery windows, rotation completion and probation milestones can all be driven deterministically. At process start the clock reads `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. Every timestamp the server stamps, every certification-expiry comparison and every probation date derivation reads from this clock.

An operator in any of the seven roles listed for that endpoint in §4 may move the clock forward by supplying a `current_utc` instant; `Recruit` is the one role excluded from it, and a `Recruit` naming it is refused `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` at rung 3. The clock is monotonic: a target instant earlier than the clock's current value is rejected with `ERR_CLOCK_NOT_MONOTONIC` (HTTP 400); an instant equal to the current value is accepted. The response body of that call is the object `{ "current_utc": ... }`, carrying the newly set instant at second precision (for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`).

Advancing the clock performs exactly one sweep and no other work: every `Instructor` whose `certification_status` is `CERTIFIED` and whose `certification_expires_at` is strictly earlier than the new instant moves to `EXPIRED`. No lesson plan, course session, recruit or cohort row is mutated by this call, and no other derived field is recomputed by it. Expiry is deliberately *not* propagated to scheduled work; it is enforced at the next scheduling or delivery attempt instead.

Domain instants — `certification_expires_at`, `approved_at`, `graded_at`, `starts_at`, `ends_at`, `delivered_at` and `recorded_at_utc` — are ISO 8601 UTC instants at second precision, for example `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. `findings_recorded_at` is the single exception on the way out: it is emitted as an RFC 1123 UTC date-time, for example `Sat, 01 Aug 2026 00:00:00 GMT`, and reads null until findings are first recorded. On the way *in*, an instant may be written with one to nine fractional digits and with either a `Z` suffix or a numeric `±HH:MM` offset; the value is normalised to UTC and **truncated**, never rounded, to whole seconds before it is stored or compared, so `2026-08-01T00:00:00.999Z` and `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` are the same instant to this service. A bare date, or a date-time carrying no zone designator at all, is not an instant and fails body shape at rung 20. Domain *dates* — `start_date`, `end_date`, `date_of_birth`, `observed_on`, `probation_start`, `probation_milestone_due_date` and `probation_scheduled_end` — are ISO 8601 calendar dates in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, written with a four-digit year and two-digit month and day and naming a real calendar day: `2027-2-1` and `2027-02-30` both fail at rung 20. Where a rule compares a date to the clock, it compares against the clock instant's calendar date.

### 2.6. Arithmetic Precision and Rounding Discipline

Hours and scores are the currency of a certification record, and their arithmetic is pinned rather than left to the implementer.

- **Hours and competency scores** are carried to exactly 2 decimal places and rounded half up. This covers `scheduled_hours`, `present_hours`, `credited_hours`, `counted_absence_hours`, `credited_hours_total`, `scheduled_basis_hours_total`, `credited_topic_hours_total`, every value inside `per_topic_credited_hours`, `unmet_topic_hours` and `projected_topic_hours`, `unexcused_absence_hours`, `make_up_hours_owed`, `dor_mean`, `competency_score`, `rolling_mean_score`, `probation_milestone_score`, `min_total_hours`, `min_passing_score`, `max_absence_hours`, `contact_hours`, `required_hours`, `remediation_hours` and `raw_score`.
- **Rounding position matters and is specified per calculation.** In the hours ledger the rounding is applied to each attendance row *before* the rows are summed, never once at the end (§3.4). In the two competency variants the rounding is applied *once at the very end* of each variant, and the intermediate bucket means are left unrounded (§3.6). Where these two disciplines differ, the difference is intentional and produces different cent values.
- **Attrition arithmetic** is carried to 4 decimal places, rounded half up: this covers each stage's attrition rate and the survival-to-certification factor. The applicant requirement derived from them is an integer produced by rounding *up* to the next whole applicant, and each per-stage expected head-count in the forward pass is produced by discarding the fractional part (rounding *down*).
- **The physical fitness composite is an integer.** The per-event ratios that feed it are exact unrounded quotients and are compared against their tier bounds unrounded.
- **Date arithmetic is exact calendar addition.** Adding a number of days to a calendar date yields the calendar date that many days later, with no time-zone or hour component involved. Where a fraction of a day-count is taken, the result is floored to a whole number of days before it is added.
- **Numeric values are emitted as JSON numbers**, not strings. A quantity specified at 2 decimal places is emitted at 2 decimal places, including trailing zeros in value terms (a credited total of three and a half hours is `3.50`) — the equality is numeric rather than textual, so the same value may be rendered `3.5` in the JSON text. Symmetrically, a numeric field must be *supplied* as a JSON number: a decimal written as a string is a body-shape failure, and so is a value carrying more decimal places than the field's specified precision — an over-precise input is rejected at rung 20, never silently rounded.

### 2.7. Rejection Precedence: the Validation Ladder

A single request frequently violates more than one rule, and which error the caller sees is part of the contract, not an implementation detail. Checks are evaluated in the fixed ascending order below; the **first** check that fails determines the response, and no later check is consulted. The numbers are the ladder's rungs — they are not returned to the caller, they only fix the order.

1. **(rung 1)** `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` are both present and non-empty — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`, 401. Applies to every endpoint.
2. **(rung 2)** `X-User-Role` names one of the eight declared roles — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`, 401. Applies to every endpoint.
3. **(rung 3)** The acting role is authorized for the target operation — otherwise `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`, 403. Applies to every endpoint. The subject-scoped screening authority of §2.1 answers the same identifier and the same status, but cannot be settled here because it reads the target record: it is decided immediately after rung 10 instead, and the retest role narrowing after rung 20.
4. **(rung 4)** For an entity-backed role, `X-User-ID` equals the owning foreign key of the target resource — otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`, 403. Applies to `POST /lesson-plans/{lessonPlanId}/revisions`, `POST /field-training-rotations/{rotationId}/observation-reports`, `GET /recruits/{recruitId}` and `GET /recruits/{recruitId}/certification-progress`.
5. **(rung 10)** Every identifier templated into the path resolves to an existing row, validated left to right — otherwise the 404 for the *first* unresolvable segment (§2.10). Applies to every endpoint with a path identifier.
6. **(rung 12)** Every foreign-key identifier supplied in the request body resolves to an existing row — otherwise the matching 404. Resolution is attempted only for a value that is present as a non-empty string: a body foreign key that is absent, `null`, an empty string, or of any other JSON type is not a 404 and instead falls through to the shape check at rung 20. Where an endpoint carries more than one body foreign key they are resolved in a fixed declared order and the first unresolvable one decides the 404: `academy_class_id`, then `lesson_plan_id`, then `instructor_id` on `POST /course-sessions`; and `recruit_id`, then `fto_instructor_id`, then `prior_rotation_id` on `POST /field-training-rotations`. Applies to `POST /academy-classes`, `POST /recruits`, `POST /lesson-plans`, `POST /course-sessions`, `POST /course-sessions/{courseSessionId}/attendance`, `POST /examinations`, `POST /field-training-rotations` and `POST /field-training-rotations/{rotationId}/observation-reports`.
7. **(rung 20)** Request body shape: required fields present, types correct, enum members recognised, numeric ranges honoured, ISO 8601 dates and instants well formed, and `dimension_ratings` carrying the five declared keys with integer values from 1 through 7 — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, 400. Applies to every endpoint. Four conventions make this rung decidable. A field explicitly supplied as `null` is treated exactly as an absent field, so a required field written `null` is a shape failure while an optional one written `null` takes its documented default. A field the endpoint does not declare is ignored rather than rejected, and that holds inside `dimension_ratings` too: a sixth rating key is dropped, not refused. A missing or empty request body is read as the empty object, so a create sent with no body fails on its first required field. And a body that parses as JSON but is not an object — an array, a number, a bare string — is itself the shape failure. Types are strict in both directions: a boolean is never accepted where an integer or a number is required, and `1` or `"true"` is never accepted where a boolean is required. Every free-text narrative an action endpoint takes is a required string of 1 to 2000 characters, the same bound §3 states explicitly for a report `narrative` and a probation or dismissal `justification`: this also covers the `reason` of a certification revocation, of a delivery void and of a rotation abort, the `revision_notes` of a lesson plan revision, the `reviewer_notes` of a revision request, and the `final_narrative` of a rotation completion.
8. **(rung 21)** A course session's `ends_at` is strictly after its `starts_at`, and a rotation's `end_date` is not earlier than its `start_date` — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`, 400. Applies to `POST /course-sessions` and `POST /field-training-rotations`.
9. **(rung 22)** Pagination arguments are in range — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`, 400. Applies to every list endpoint.
10. **(rung 23)** The clock target is not earlier than the current logical clock — otherwise `ERR_CLOCK_NOT_MONOTONIC`, 400. Applies to `POST /_clock/set_current_utc`.
11. **(rung 24)** Uniqueness of `code` on a certification standard, `class_code` on a cohort, `applicant_number` on a recruit and `badge_number` on an instructor — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_IDENTIFIER`, 409. Applies to `POST /certification-standards`, `POST /academy-classes`, `POST /recruits` and `POST /instructors`.
12. **(rung 30)** Optimistic concurrency: the supplied expected version equals the parent's current `version` — otherwise the matching 412 (§2.8). Applies to `POST /field-training-rotations` and `POST /lesson-plans/{lessonPlanId}/revisions`.
13. **(rung 39)** The subject recruit's `pipeline_stage` is none of the terminal values `CERTIFIED`, `DISMISSED` or `WITHDRAWN` — otherwise `ERR_RECRUIT_TERMINAL`, 409. Applies to `POST /recruits/{recruitId}/screenings`, `POST /recruits/{recruitId}/advance-stage`, `POST /recruits/{recruitId}/dismiss`, `POST /recruits/{recruitId}/probation-decisions`, `POST /examinations`, `POST /field-training-rotations` and `POST /course-sessions/{courseSessionId}/attendance`. This rung sits deliberately **above** the per-entity state family on rung 40: once a recruit is terminal, every mutation naming that recruit answers `ERR_RECRUIT_TERMINAL` rather than the narrower `ERR_STAGE_TRANSITION_NOT_ALLOWED`, `ERR_PROBATION_NOT_ACTIVE` or `ERR_INVALID_STATE` that the same request would also have violated.
14. **(rung 40)** The target row is in a state from which the requested transition is declared — otherwise the applicable member of the state family: `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_SCREENING_ALREADY_ADJUDICATED`, `ERR_SESSION_NOT_DELIVERABLE`, `ERR_SESSION_NOT_VOIDABLE`, `ERR_ROTATION_NOT_ACTIVE`, `ERR_PROBATION_NOT_ACTIVE` or `ERR_STAGE_TRANSITION_NOT_ALLOWED`, all 409. Applies to every action endpoint. Where a narrower member exists for the entity being acted on, it is preferred over the generic `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.
15. **(rung 42)** The examinee's `academy_status` is `IN_PROGRESS` or `REMEDIATION`, and not `NOT_STARTED`, `DISMISSAL_PENDING` or `COMPLETE` — otherwise `ERR_RECRUIT_NOT_IN_ACADEMY`, 409. Applies to `POST /examinations`, `POST /examinations/{examinationId}/grade`, `POST /examinations/{examinationId}/assign-remediation` and `POST /course-sessions/{courseSessionId}/attendance`.
16. **(rung 50)** The instructor of record has `certification_status` equal to `CERTIFIED` and the relevant `topic_code` present in their `authorized_topic_codes` — otherwise `ERR_INSTRUCTOR_NOT_AUTHORIZED`, 422. Applies to `POST /course-sessions`, `POST /course-sessions/{courseSessionId}/deliver` and `POST /lesson-plans/{lessonPlanId}/approve`.
17. **(rung 51)** The session instructor's `certification_expires_at` is strictly after the current logical clock — otherwise `ERR_INSTRUCTOR_CERT_EXPIRED`, 422. Applies to `POST /course-sessions` and `POST /course-sessions/{courseSessionId}/deliver`.
18. **(rung 52)** The referenced lesson plan's `status` is `APPROVED` and the session's `pinned_lesson_plan_revision` still equals that plan's `approved_revision_number` — otherwise `ERR_LESSON_PLAN_NOT_APPROVED`, 422. Applies to `POST /course-sessions` and `POST /course-sessions/{courseSessionId}/deliver`.
19. **(rung 53)** The recruit's `clearance_status` is `CLEARED` before the academy phase is entered — otherwise `ERR_CLEARANCE_INCOMPLETE`, 422. Applies to `POST /recruits/{recruitId}/advance-stage`.
20. **(rung 54)** A screening carrying a non-empty `disqualifier_codes` array is not adjudicated as a clearance, a waiver is not adjudicated without a non-empty `waiver_justification`, and a `PHYSICAL_FITNESS` screening is never adjudicated as a waiver at all — otherwise `ERR_DISQUALIFIER_UNWAIVED`, 422. Applies to `POST /screening-records/{screeningRecordId}/adjudicate`.
21. **(rung 55)** On a clearance decision for a `PHYSICAL_FITNESS` screening, `composite_score` is at least the standard's `fitness_pass_score` — otherwise `ERR_FITNESS_STANDARD_NOT_MET`, 422. Applies to `POST /screening-records/{screeningRecordId}/adjudicate`. The same error identifier also documents the case of a recruit being advanced past the screening stage while a physical fitness composite remains below the pass score. A single fitness event scoring below `fitness_event_floor_points` raises no error of its own: it only caps the composite at 59 as §3.2 describes, and so reaches this identifier indirectly, through that capped composite failing the same `fitness_pass_score` comparison. Both callers of this identifier test the composite against the pass score and nothing else.
22. **(rung 56)** Segregation of duties: the `X-User-ID` adjudicating a screening differs from that screening's `findings_recorded_by_user_id`, and the `X-User-ID` approving a lesson plan differs from the authoring instructor's identifier — otherwise `ERR_SAME_ACTOR_ADJUDICATION`, 422. Applies to `POST /screening-records/{screeningRecordId}/adjudicate` and `POST /lesson-plans/{lessonPlanId}/approve`.
23. **(rung 57)** The assigned FTO has `certification_status` equal to `CERTIFIED` and `fto_certified` true — otherwise `ERR_FTO_NOT_CERTIFIED`, 422. Applies to `POST /field-training-rotations` and `POST /field-training-rotations/{rotationId}/activate`; the credential is tested at both planning and activation.
24. **(rung 60)** The candidate rotation's closed date interval does not intersect any other non-aborted rotation of the same trainee — `PLANNED`, `ACTIVE`, `REMEDIAL` or `COMPLETED` all count, an `ABORTED` row never does — otherwise `ERR_ROTATION_DATE_OVERLAP`, 409. Applies to `POST /field-training-rotations`.
25. **(rung 61)** The report's `observed_on` falls inside the rotation's closed date window — otherwise `ERR_DOR_OUTSIDE_ROTATION_WINDOW`, 422. Applies to `POST /field-training-rotations/{rotationId}/observation-reports`.
26. **(rung 62)** The candidate session's half-open instant interval does not conflict with another live session of the same instructor — live meaning a `status` of `SCHEDULED`, `UNDER_MIN_ENROLLMENT` or `DELIVERED`, so a `CANCELLED` or `VOIDED` session neither overlaps nor imposes a room turnaround — otherwise `ERR_INSTRUCTOR_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT`, 409. Applies to `POST /course-sessions`.
27. **(rung 63)** The candidate `fto_instructor_id` has not already trained this recruit — otherwise `ERR_TRAINER_ROTATION_VIOLATION`, 409. Applies to `POST /field-training-rotations`.
28. **(rung 64)** The report's `author_instructor_id` equals the rotation's `fto_instructor_id` — otherwise `ERR_DOR_AUTHOR_NOT_ASSIGNED_FTO`, 422. Applies to `POST /field-training-rotations/{rotationId}/observation-reports`.
29. **(rung 65)** At most one non-voided observation report exists per rotation and `observed_on` date — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_DOR`, 409. Applies to `POST /field-training-rotations/{rotationId}/observation-reports`.
30. **(rung 66)** At most one attendance record exists per course session and recruit, whatever its `ledger_status` — otherwise `ERR_ATTENDANCE_ALREADY_RECORDED`, 409. Applies to `POST /course-sessions/{courseSessionId}/attendance`.
31. **(rung 67)** At most one screening record exists per recruit and `screening_type` — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_SCREENING_TYPE`, 409. Applies to `POST /recruits/{recruitId}/screenings`.
32. **(rung 68)** `present_hours` is consistent with the declared attendance `status` — otherwise `ERR_PRESENT_HOURS_INCONSISTENT`, 422. Applies to `POST /course-sessions/{courseSessionId}/attendance`.
33. **(rung 70)** The `topic_code` exists as a `topic_requirements` entry on the certification standard of the relevant cohort — otherwise `ERR_TOPIC_NOT_IN_STANDARD`, 422. Applies to `POST /course-sessions` and `POST /examinations`.
34. **(rung 71)** That `topic_requirements` entry has `exam_required` true — otherwise `ERR_EXAM_NOT_REQUIRED_FOR_TOPIC`, 422. Applies to `POST /examinations`.
35. **(rung 72)** `prior_rotation_id`, when supplied, points at a rotation of the same recruit whose `phase_number` is exactly one less than the candidate's — otherwise `ERR_PRIOR_ROTATION_MISMATCH`, 422. Applies to `POST /field-training-rotations`.
36. **(rung 80)** The assigned FTO is within the aggregate concurrent-trainee ceiling for the candidate's date interval — otherwise `ERR_FTO_LOAD_EXCEEDED`, 409. Applies to `POST /field-training-rotations`.
37. **(rung 81)** The recruit's remediation budget is not yet exhausted — otherwise `ERR_REMEDIATION_ATTEMPTS_EXHAUSTED`, 409. Applies to `POST /examinations/{examinationId}/assign-remediation`.
38. **(rung 82)** The probation milestone date has been reached before a pass decision is accepted — otherwise `ERR_MILESTONE_NOT_REACHED`, 409. Applies to `POST /recruits/{recruitId}/probation-decisions`.

Two orderings inside this ladder are worth calling out because they are frequently guessed the other way round. Existence of a path identifier (rung 10) is decided **before** body shape (rung 20), so a syntactically invalid body against a non-existent recruit answers 404, not 400. And optimistic concurrency (rung 30) is decided **before** any state or business check, so a stale expected version answers 412 even when the target row would also have failed a later rung.

### 2.8. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Two write operations require the client to prove which version of a parent record they were looking at. Both compare against the parent's own `version` field as described in §2.4, and both reject a mismatch with HTTP 412.

**Planning a field training rotation.** A `POST /field-training-rotations` request must carry `expected_recruit_version`, a non-optional field holding the client's last observed `version` of the trainee named by `recruit_id`. If that value does not equal the recruit's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_RECRUIT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This matters precisely because a second rotation against the same trainee is *legitimate* product behaviour — a later phase supersedes the earlier assignment and `prior_rotation_id` chains them — so nothing else in the specification would stop a client from writing an assignment against a trainee snapshot that has since moved. A recruit's `version` is advanced by every stage advance, every screening adjudication, every probation re-derivation, every graded examination and every rotation create, so an assignment written against an out-of-date observation of the trainee is refused rather than silently accepted.

**Revising a lesson plan.** A `POST /lesson-plans/{lessonPlanId}/revisions` request must carry `expected_lesson_plan_version`, a non-optional field holding the client's last observed `version` of that lesson plan. A value that does not equal the plan's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_LESSON_PLAN_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Again the point is that a new revision legitimately supersedes the currently approved one, so a second revision submission is valid on its own terms; the check pins which revision the author was actually editing. Approval, a revision request and the instructor-revocation cascade all advance a plan's `version`, so a revision written against a superseded snapshot is refused.

No other endpoint takes a version field, and no other endpoint performs a version comparison. Where an expected-version field is specified above it is required, not optional: omitting it is a body-shape failure at rung 20.

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline and Conserved Quantities

Several single operations in this system mutate a chain of rows. Cascades are described in full where they occur in §3, but three properties hold for all of them. First, a cascade is **immediate and complete within the triggering request** — there is no deferred reconciliation and no eventual consistency. Second, every row a cascade touches has its own `updated_at` refreshed and its own `version` incremented, including rows whose only change was a re-derived computed field. Third, every cascade has a **conserved quantity** that a compensating action must restore exactly:

- Voiding a delivered session restores `credited_topic_hours_total` on the cohort to its exact pre-delivery value.
- Aborting a rotation restores `active_trainee_count` on the assigned instructor to its exact pre-activation value.
- Voiding or re-adding an attendance row leaves `enrolled_count` on the session equal to the count of that session's non-voided attendance rows at all times.

Three named cascade chains recur throughout §3 and are cross-referenced by name there: **cascade A** begins at a terminal examination failure, **cascade B** begins at a filed observation report, and **cascade C** begins at an instructor certification revocation.

### 2.10. Resolving Path and Body Identifiers

Identifiers templated into a path are resolved strictly left to right, and the response reports the **first** segment that does not resolve, with the 404 that belongs to that segment's entity type: `ERR_CERTIFICATION_STANDARD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ACADEMY_CLASS_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RECRUIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SCREENING_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INSTRUCTOR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LESSON_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COURSE_SESSION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ATTENDANCE_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EXAMINATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ROTATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OBSERVATION_REPORT_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_TRAINING_RECORD_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`. All twelve carry HTTP 404.

The same twelve identifiers are reused when a foreign key supplied in a *request body* fails to resolve, which is why the not-found error is keyed to the entity type rather than to the position in the URL. A `certification_standard_id` that names nothing answers `ERR_CERTIFICATION_STANDARD_NOT_FOUND`; an `academy_class_id` answers `ERR_ACADEMY_CLASS_NOT_FOUND`; a `recruit_id` answers `ERR_RECRUIT_NOT_FOUND`; an `instructor_id`, `fto_instructor_id` or `author_instructor_id` answers `ERR_INSTRUCTOR_NOT_FOUND`; a `lesson_plan_id` answers `ERR_LESSON_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`; and a `prior_rotation_id` answers `ERR_ROTATION_NOT_FOUND`.

### 2.11. Interval Conventions

This system uses two different interval conventions, and confusing them produces wrong answers rather than errors.

**Field training rotation dates are closed intervals.** A rotation occupies `[start_date, end_date]` inclusive of both endpoints, so two rotations that merely touch — one ending on the very day the next begins — *do* intersect. Adjacency counts as overlap.

**Course session instants are half-open intervals.** A session occupies `[starts_at, ends_at)`, including its start instant and excluding its end instant, so a session that begins exactly when another ends does *not* intersect it. On top of that plain intersection test the academy adds a room-turnaround rule: two sessions of the same instructor that sit in the *same* `room_code` may be perfectly adjacent with a zero gap, but two sessions in *different* `room_code` values separated by a gap of less than fifteen minutes are a conflict, because the instructor cannot cross the campus in that time.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The State Standard and the Cohort It Governs

Everything the academy does is measured against a **certification standard** published by a state POST commission, and registering one is the first act in the system. An `AcademyDirector` registers a standard with its commission `code` (1 to 40 characters, unique across all standards), the `state_code` it belongs to (exactly two uppercase letters), the `min_total_hours` of credited academy contact time a recruit must accumulate (greater than zero, carried to 2 decimal places), the `min_passing_score` that counts as a pass on an examination (greater than zero and no more than 100, carried to 2 decimal places, and an *inclusive* lower bound — a score landing exactly on it passes), the `max_remediation_attempts` a recruit may take per topic after a first failure (zero or more, so the total number of sittings permitted is one plus that number), and the `max_absence_hours` of cumulative unexcused absence a recruit may accrue before mandatory dismissal review (zero or more, 2 decimal places).

The heart of the standard is its `topic_requirements` array — the curriculum map. It carries between 1 and 20 entries, each shaped as a `topic_code` (1 to 24 uppercase characters, unique within the array), the `required_hours` for that topic (greater than zero) and an `exam_required` boolean. Two further fields tune the fitness standard: `fitness_event_floor_points`, the per-event points floor, which defaults to 10 and must lie between 0 and 25 inclusive, and `fitness_pass_score`, the minimum fitness composite that clears the physical fitness screening, which defaults to 60 and must lie between 0 and 100 inclusive. A repeated `code` is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_IDENTIFIER`.

Against a standard the director opens a **cohort**: an `AcademyClass` created with the `certification_standard_id` it is certified against — pinned at creation and immutable thereafter — a `class_code` (1 to 24 characters, unique across cohorts), the `start_date` on which the academy phase opens, and the `planned_seats` the agency has budgeted (at least one). A new cohort's `status` begins at `FORMING` and nothing else happens at creation. A repeated `class_code` is likewise `ERR_DUPLICATE_IDENTIFIER`.

A cohort exposes four derived readings that together answer "is this class on track". `credited_topic_hours_total` is the cohort's conserved hours ledger: the sum of the `hours_delta` of every training record entry whose `kind` is `HOURS_CREDIT` or `HOURS_REVERSAL` and whose `academy_class_id` is this cohort, carried to 2 decimal places. `unmet_topic_hours` maps each curriculum topic to the class-wide hours still owed — for each topic, the greater of zero and the topic's `required_hours` less the delivered hours for that topic, where delivered hours are the sum of `scheduled_hours` over this cohort's sessions whose `status` is `DELIVERED` and whose `topic_code` matches. `projected_topic_hours` maps each topic to delivered-plus-scheduled hours: the sum of `scheduled_hours` over this cohort's sessions with that `topic_code` whose `status` is `DELIVERED`, `SCHEDULED` or `UNDER_MIN_ENROLLMENT`. Both maps always carry exactly one entry per `topic_requirements` entry of the cohort's standard, in the order those entries were declared; a topic with no delivered session reads its full `required_hours` in the unmet map, and a topic with no session at all reads `0.00` in the projected map. Both maps carry values at 2 decimal places. `graduated_count` is simply the number of this cohort's recruits whose `pipeline_stage` is `CERTIFIED`.

Cohort posture moves without anyone asking for it. A `FORMING` cohort becomes `IN_SESSION` the moment one of its sessions is first closed out as delivered. An `IN_SESSION` cohort drops to `AT_RISK` whenever a re-derivation leaves *any* curriculum topic with projected hours below that topic's `required_hours` — which happens when scheduled sessions are cancelled by cascade C, and when a delivered session is voided. An `AT_RISK` cohort climbs back to `IN_SESSION` as soon as a replacement session restores every topic's projected hours to at least its `required_hours`, closing the `IN_SESSION` ↔ `AT_RISK` posture loop. Finally, a cohort that is `IN_SESSION` or `AT_RISK` becomes `GRADUATED` on a graduation run in which at least one recruit was actually certified; `GRADUATED` is terminal for the cohort.

### 3.2. Applicant Intake and the Selection Screening Pipeline

A `TrainingCoordinator` or an `AcademyDirector` enrols an applicant into a cohort by supplying the `academy_class_id` (immutable after creation), the agency's `applicant_number` for the file (1 to 24 characters, unique across all recruits — a repeat is `ERR_DUPLICATE_IDENTIFIER`), the recruit's `full_name` as it will be printed on the certification record (1 to 120 characters), their `date_of_birth`, and the `age_bracket` whose fitness standard applies to them: `AGE_20_29`, `AGE_30_39` or `AGE_40_49`. A newly enrolled recruit's probation block is written at its pre-probation values rather than left absent: `probation_base_days` is `365` and `probation_extension_days` is `0` from the moment of intake, while `probation_start`, `probation_milestone_due_date`, `probation_milestone_score` and `probation_scheduled_end` are all null, as is `dismissal_reason_code`.

A recruit carries **four independent state dimensions on the same row**, and understanding that they move independently is essential. `pipeline_stage` tracks position in the selection and training pipeline and begins at `APPLICANT`, running through `SCREENING`, `ACADEMY`, `FIELD_TRAINING`, `PROBATION` to the terminal `CERTIFIED`, `DISMISSED` or `WITHDRAWN`. `clearance_status` aggregates the recruit's four screening records and begins at `PENDING`, moving through `PARTIAL` to `CLEARED` or to the terminal `DISQUALIFIED`. `academy_status` tracks academy-phase standing and begins at `NOT_STARTED`, moving among `IN_PROGRESS`, `REMEDIATION`, `DISMISSAL_PENDING` and `COMPLETE`. `probation_status` tracks probationary standing and begins at `NOT_APPLICABLE`, moving among `ACTIVE` and `EXTENSION_REQUIRED` to the terminal `PASSED` or `SEPARATED`. A recruit's `clearance_status` can reach `DISQUALIFIED` while `pipeline_stage` is still `SCREENING`; `academy_status` can reach `DISMISSAL_PENDING` while `pipeline_stage` is still `ACADEMY`. Neither dimension drags the other along except where this document says so explicitly.

Selection begins when a `TrainingCoordinator` orders a screening for the recruit, naming the `screening_type`: `BACKGROUND`, `MEDICAL`, `PSYCHOLOGICAL` or `PHYSICAL_FITNESS`. The new `ScreeningRecord` starts at `status` `ORDERED` with `attempt_number` 1, a null `raw_metrics`, a null `composite_score`, an empty `disqualifier_codes` array, and `waiver_justification`, `findings_recorded_by_user_id`, `adjudicated_by_user_id` and `findings_recorded_at` all null. The **first** screening ordered for a recruit — that is, the one ordered while `pipeline_stage` is still `APPLICANT` and no screening record for that recruit yet exists — also advances `pipeline_stage` from `APPLICANT` to `SCREENING`, appends a training record entry of `kind` `STAGE_ENTRY` with `stage_code` `SCREENING`, a `hours_delta` of `0.00`, both `actor_role` and `actor_user_id` recorded as the literal `system`, `subject_entity` `ScreeningRecord` with the just-created screening row's own `id` as `subject_entity_id` — the screening, not the recruit — and a `detail` of `pipeline stage advanced to SCREENING on first screening order`, and increments the recruit's `version` by one. There is no separate call for that hop; screening is reached by ordering work, not by requesting a stage change. Only one screening record may ever exist per recruit and `screening_type`: a retest re-enters the *same* row and increments its `attempt_number`, so a second row of the same type is never legitimate — not even after the first reached `CLEARED`, `WAIVED` or `DISQUALIFIED` — and an attempt to create one answers `ERR_DUPLICATE_SCREENING_TYPE`.

Recording findings moves a screening from `ORDERED` to `FINDINGS_RECORDED`. The payload carries `raw_metrics` and `disqualifier_codes`. For a `PHYSICAL_FITNESS` screening `raw_metrics` must carry all four event keys — `push_ups` and `sit_ups` as integers of at least zero, `run_1_5_mile_seconds` as an integer strictly greater than zero, and `vertical_jump_inches` as a non-negative number of at most 2 decimal places; for every other screening type it carries a single `summary` string. `raw_metrics` must itself be a JSON object, and it is retained **as supplied**: any key beyond the four fitness events, or beyond `summary`, is stored unvalidated and echoed back verbatim in the record's `raw_metrics`. `disqualifier_codes` is an array of uppercase finding codes, each of 1 to 24 characters, and an empty array means no adverse finding was recorded. Both fields are **required**: an absent or `null` `raw_metrics`, or an absent or `null` `disqualifier_codes`, is a shape failure at rung 20 — an empty array must be sent explicitly to record no adverse finding. Findings are accepted only from `ORDERED` or `RETEST_REQUIRED`; re-recording them against a record already at `FINDINGS_RECORDED` answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409). Along with the payload the server stores `findings_recorded_by_user_id` as the acting `X-User-ID` and `findings_recorded_at` as the current logical clock, and for a physical fitness screening it computes `composite_score` (below). `composite_score` is null for every screening type other than `PHYSICAL_FITNESS`. Recording findings touches the screening record alone: the recruit's own `updated_at` and `version` do not move until the screening is adjudicated.

Adjudication is a separate act by a *different* person, and this is the system's first segregation-of-duties boundary: the adjudicating `X-User-ID` must differ from the record's `findings_recorded_by_user_id`, or the request is refused with `ERR_SAME_ACTOR_ADJUDICATION`. Adjudication is legal only from `FINDINGS_RECORDED`; against a record already at `CLEARED`, `WAIVED` or `DISQUALIFIED` — all three terminal — both findings and adjudication answer `ERR_SCREENING_ALREADY_ADJUDICATED`, while from the two non-terminal statuses `ORDERED` and `RETEST_REQUIRED` adjudication answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409) instead. The `decision` field selects one of four outcomes, and each writes `adjudicated_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID` and increments the recruit's `version` by one:

- A **clear** decision moves the record to `CLEARED`. It requires that `disqualifier_codes` be empty — a non-empty list is `ERR_DISQUALIFIER_UNWAIVED` — and, for a physical fitness screening, that `composite_score` be at least the standard's `fitness_pass_score`, failing which the answer is `ERR_FITNESS_STANDARD_NOT_MET`. It re-derives the recruit's `clearance_status` and appends a training record entry of `kind` `SCREENING_ADJUDICATION` with `stage_code` `SCREENING`, `hours_delta` `0.00`, `subject_entity` `ScreeningRecord`, the screening's own `id` as `subject_entity_id` and a `detail` of `screening adjudicated CLEAR`; the waive and disqualify entries below are shaped identically, each carrying its own decision word at the end of that `detail`.
- A **waive** decision moves the record to `WAIVED`, and in practice only a `BackgroundInvestigator` or a `MedicalExaminer` can reach it — there is no role gate on the decision itself, as §2.1 explains; subject scope alone confines it, and a `TrainingCoordinator` waiving the one type they own is refused as `ERR_DISQUALIFIER_UNWAIVED` rather than `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`. It requires a non-empty `waiver_justification` — an optional body field of at most 2000 characters whose absence, `null`, empty value or whitespace-only value all count alike as missing — which is stored on the record, and it is permitted only when `screening_type` is `BACKGROUND`, `MEDICAL` or `PSYCHOLOGICAL`: physical fitness shortfalls are never waivable, and attempting to waive one — or waiving anything without a justification — is `ERR_DISQUALIFIER_UNWAIVED`. The fitness-type refusal is decided before the missing-justification refusal, though both answer the same identifier. Nothing requires `disqualifier_codes` to be non-empty for a waiver: a clean record may be waived. `waiver_justification` is null on any record that was not waived. It re-derives `clearance_status` and appends a `SCREENING_ADJUDICATION` entry.
- A **disqualify** decision moves the record to `DISQUALIFIED` and sets the recruit's `clearance_status` directly to `DISQUALIFIED`, which is terminal for that dimension. It appends a `SCREENING_ADJUDICATION` entry.
- A **retest** decision moves the record to `RETEST_REQUIRED`. It is open only to a `TrainingCoordinator` — any other role naming it is refused `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`, decided after body shape — and only for a `PHYSICAL_FITNESS` screening whose `composite_score` is strictly below the standard's `fitness_pass_score`: named against any other `screening_type`, or against a fitness screening whose `composite_score` is null or already at or above the pass score, the answer is `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409). Both of those two tests are decided **after** the rung 54, 55 and 56 checks rather than at rung 40, so a retest named by the same user who recorded the findings answers `ERR_SAME_ACTOR_ADJUDICATION` first. The recruit's `clearance_status` is left unchanged and no training record entry is appended for this decision, but `adjudicated_by_user_id` is still written from the acting `X-User-ID` and the recruit's `version` still advances by one.

A record at `RETEST_REQUIRED` accepts a fresh findings submission, which returns it to `FINDINGS_RECORDED`, increments `attempt_number` by one, replaces `raw_metrics` and `disqualifier_codes` wholesale, recomputes `composite_score`, records the new `findings_recorded_by_user_id` and `findings_recorded_at`, and resets `adjudicated_by_user_id` to null — re-arming the segregation-of-duties comparison against whoever recorded the retest. This closes the `FINDINGS_RECORDED` ↔ `RETEST_REQUIRED` loop.

The recruit's `clearance_status` is re-derived after every adjudication rather than being set by hand. From `PENDING` it becomes `PARTIAL` when at least one of the recruit's screenings sits at `CLEARED` or `WAIVED`, fewer than four of them do, and none sits at `DISQUALIFIED`; a `PARTIAL` recruit stays in `pipeline_stage` `SCREENING`. From `PARTIAL` it becomes `CLEARED` when all four screening types — `BACKGROUND`, `MEDICAL`, `PSYCHOLOGICAL` and `PHYSICAL_FITNESS` — each have a record of this recruit at `CLEARED` or `WAIVED`. From either `PENDING` or `PARTIAL` it becomes `DISQUALIFIED` the moment any one of the recruit's screenings is adjudicated to `DISQUALIFIED`.

**Scoring the physical fitness test.** When findings are recorded on a `PHYSICAL_FITNESS` screening — on the initial attempt and again on every retest — the server derives `composite_score` from the four event results in `raw_metrics` together with the recruit's `age_bracket`, and only from the current attempt: earlier attempts are ignored entirely. Each bracket carries its own reference standard. For `AGE_20_29` the references are 30 push-ups, 38 sit-ups, 750 seconds on the 1.5-mile run and 18 inches of vertical jump. For `AGE_30_39` they are 24 push-ups, 32 sit-ups, 810 seconds and 16 inches. For `AGE_40_49` they are 18 push-ups, 26 sit-ups, 900 seconds and 14 inches.

Each event yields a performance ratio. For `push_ups`, `sit_ups` and `vertical_jump_inches` the ratio is the recruit's result divided by the bracket reference. For `run_1_5_mile_seconds` the ratio is **inverted** — the bracket reference divided by the recruit's elapsed seconds — because a lower time is a better run. Ratios are exact quotients and are compared against the tier bounds without rounding. Each ratio earns points on a five-tier scale: 25 points at a ratio of at least 1.20; 20 points at a ratio of at least 1.00 but below 1.20; 12 points at a ratio of at least 0.85 but below 1.00; 6 points at a ratio of at least 0.70 but below 0.85; and 0 points at a ratio below 0.70. The raw total is the sum of the four events' points.

A non-linear gate then applies, and it is what makes a single weak event decisive: if the *smallest* of the four per-event point values is below the standard's `fitness_event_floor_points`, the composite is the lesser of the raw total and 59 — a hard cap that sits below the default pass mark no matter how strong the other three events were. Otherwise the composite is the lesser of the raw total and 100. The composite is an integer, and the screening clears the fitness standard when it is at least `fitness_pass_score`.

### 3.3. Instructor Certification, Lesson Plan Approval, and Cascade C

Instruction may only be delivered by a credentialed instructor, and the academy tracks that credential as a first-class lifecycle. An `AcademyDirector` registers an instructor with a `badge_number` (1 to 16 characters, unique across instructors — a repeat is `ERR_DUPLICATE_IDENTIFIER`) and a `full_name` (1 to 120 characters). A newly registered instructor sits at `certification_status` `UNCERTIFIED` with a null `certification_expires_at`, an empty `authorized_topic_codes` list, `fto_certified` false and `revocation_reason` null.

Certification is granted, and later renewed, by the director supplying the `certification_expires_at` instant at which the credential lapses, the `authorized_topic_codes` list of curriculum topics the instructor may teach (each an uppercase code of 1 to 24 characters), and the `fto_certified` boolean that marks them as a Field Training Officer. The call is legal from `UNCERTIFIED` and from `EXPIRED`, and it requires `certification_expires_at` to be strictly after the current logical clock — a credential cannot be issued already lapsed, and an instant at or before the clock answers `ERR_INSTRUCTOR_CERT_EXPIRED` (422), decided after the status test just described. It moves `certification_status` to `CERTIFIED` and replaces `certification_expires_at`, `authorized_topic_codes` and `fto_certified` *wholesale* from the request rather than merging them, and it increments the instructor's `version` by one. The `EXPIRED` to `CERTIFIED` path is what closes the `CERTIFIED` → `EXPIRED` → `CERTIFIED` renewal loop. Attempting to certify an instructor whose `certification_status` is `REVOKED` answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, because `REVOKED` is terminal.

A `CERTIFIED` instructor lapses to `EXPIRED` only through the clock sweep of §2.5, and lapsing touches nothing else: no lesson plan and no session is altered, because expiry is enforced at the next scheduling or delivery attempt instead.

Revocation for cause is the head of **cascade C**, and it is the widest cascade in the system. The director supplies a `reason`, which is stored as `revocation_reason`. From `CERTIFIED` or from `EXPIRED`, the instructor moves to the terminal `REVOKED`, `fto_certified` is forced false and `authorized_topic_codes` is cleared. Then, in order:

- **Cascade C, level 2** — because the credential became `REVOKED`, every lesson plan authored by this instructor whose `status` is `APPROVED` moves `APPROVED` → `REVISION_REQUESTED`, has its `approved_revision_number` set to null, and has `reviewer_notes` set to record that the authoring instructor's certification was revoked; `approved_at` and `approved_by_user_id` are left exactly as they stand, unlike the request-revision route below, which clears all three.
- **Cascade C, level 3** — because the instructor may no longer teach, every course session naming this instructor as its own `instructor_id` of record — whatever lesson plan it references, and whoever authored that plan — whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `UNDER_MIN_ENROLLMENT` moves to `CANCELLED`, taken in the sessions' ascending `created_at` order, and every attendance row of such a session whose `ledger_status` is `PROVISIONAL` moves to `VOIDED` with its `void_reason` set to the literal `session cancelled` and its `credited_hours` and `counted_absence_hours` reset to `0.00`. Selection is by instructor of record, not by lesson plan: a session taught by *another* instructor against one of the revoked author's plans keeps its `SCHEDULED` posture, and what stops it is the plan no longer being `APPROVED` at delivery.
- **Cascade C, level 4** — because those sessions were cancelled, every affected cohort re-derives `projected_topic_hours`, and a cohort at `IN_SESSION` for which any curriculum topic now projects fewer hours than that topic's `required_hours` drops `IN_SESSION` → `AT_RISK`.
- Independently of the plan chain, every field training rotation naming this instructor as its FTO whose `status` is `PLANNED`, `ACTIVE` or `REMEDIAL` moves to `ABORTED` with `abort_reason` recording that the FTO's certification was revoked, every non-voided observation report of such a rotation is marked `voided` true exactly as an explicit abort does (§3.6), and the instructor's `active_trainee_count` is re-derived, which necessarily leaves it at zero.

`active_trainee_count` is a conserved reading rather than a stored counter: it is always the number of field training rotations naming this instructor as FTO whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `REMEDIAL`.

**Lesson plans.** An `AcademyInstructor` or a `TrainingCoordinator` submits a **lesson plan** with the authoring `instructor_id` (immutable after creation), the `topic_code` the plan teaches (1 to 24 uppercase characters, and it must match a topic in the certification standard of any cohort that later schedules it), a `title` (1 to 160 characters) and the `contact_hours` the plan is written for (greater than zero, 2 decimal places). A plan begins at `status` `SUBMITTED` with `revision_number` 1, a null `approved_revision_number`, a null `approved_at`, a null `approved_by_user_id` and null `reviewer_notes`. When the acting role is `AcademyInstructor`, the plan's `instructor_id` must equal the acting `X-User-ID`, or the answer is `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403) — a comparison decided after the `instructor_id` has resolved at rung 12 and after the body has passed shape at rung 20, not at rung 4, so a create naming a nonexistent instructor answers `ERR_INSTRUCTOR_NOT_FOUND` and one carrying a malformed `title` answers `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` even when the author is also the wrong user. A `TrainingCoordinator` may author on any instructor's behalf.

Approval is the second segregation-of-duties boundary. An `AcademyDirector` approves a plan from `SUBMITTED` only, and the approval requires that the authoring instructor's `certification_status` be `CERTIFIED`, that the plan's `topic_code` appear in that instructor's `authorized_topic_codes` — otherwise `ERR_INSTRUCTOR_NOT_AUTHORIZED` — and that the approving `X-User-ID` differ from the authoring instructor's identifier, otherwise `ERR_SAME_ACTOR_ADJUDICATION`. Approval sets `status` to `APPROVED`, captures `approved_revision_number` as the plan's *current* `revision_number`, stamps `approved_at` from the logical clock and `approved_by_user_id` from the acting user, and increments the plan's `version`. Approving a plan that is already `APPROVED` answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

The director may instead send a plan back, supplying `reviewer_notes`, which moves it `SUBMITTED` → `REVISION_REQUESTED`, stores those notes, clears `approved_revision_number`, `approved_at` and `approved_by_user_id` to null, and increments `version`. This route is legal from `SUBMITTED` **only**: an approved plan is never sent back this way, and asking for it answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. An approved plan leaves `APPROVED` by exactly two routes — an author-driven revision, or cascade C. Sessions already scheduled against a plan that has been sent back keep their `SCHEDULED` posture, but cannot be delivered while the plan sits outside `APPROVED`.

The author closes the loop by submitting a revision, which is legal from `REVISION_REQUESTED` and from `APPROVED`. The submission carries `expected_lesson_plan_version` (the optimistic concurrency guard of §2.8), the replacement `contact_hours`, and a `revision_notes` narrative describing what changed — required by body shape, but not retained on the plan and absent from every representation of it. When the acting role is `AcademyInstructor` the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the plan's `instructor_id`. The revision sets `status` back to `SUBMITTED`, increments `revision_number` by one, replaces `contact_hours`, clears `reviewer_notes` to null, and increments the plan's `version` by one — closing the `SUBMITTED` → `REVISION_REQUESTED` → `SUBMITTED` revision loop.

Revising an `APPROVED` plan deserves its own sentence, because what it *does not* change is the load-bearing part: `approved_revision_number`, `approved_at` and `approved_by_user_id` are all **left unchanged**, so course sessions created earlier still hold a pin that resolves to a real revision number. What breaks their delivery is that the plan itself is no longer `APPROVED`, and separately that its `approved_revision_number` no longer matches the newer content the author has since written. Either way, a session whose pin is stale is refused at delivery with `ERR_LESSON_PLAN_NOT_APPROVED`. A new revision legitimately supersedes the approved one; the pin exists so that supersession is *visible* rather than silent.

### 3.4. Delivering the Curriculum: Sessions, Attendance and the Hours Ledger

A `TrainingCoordinator` schedules a **course session** — one delivery of an approved lesson plan to a cohort — by supplying the `academy_class_id` receiving it, the `lesson_plan_id` being delivered, the `instructor_id` of record for this delivery (which may legitimately differ from the plan's author), the `room_code` in which it will be taught (1 to 16 uppercase characters), the `starts_at` and `ends_at` instants, and optionally a `min_enrollment` floor, which defaults to 3 and may not be negative.

Scheduling is gated on six things, in the ladder order of §2.7: `ends_at` must be strictly after `starts_at`, or the answer is `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL`; the named instructor must be `CERTIFIED` with the plan's topic in their `authorized_topic_codes`, or `ERR_INSTRUCTOR_NOT_AUTHORIZED`; their `certification_expires_at` must be strictly after the current logical clock, or `ERR_INSTRUCTOR_CERT_EXPIRED`; the lesson plan must be `APPROVED`, or `ERR_LESSON_PLAN_NOT_APPROVED`; the instructor must be free, per the half-open-interval and room-turnaround rules of §2.11, or `ERR_INSTRUCTOR_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT`; and the plan's topic must be a `topic_requirements` entry of the cohort's own certification standard, or `ERR_TOPIC_NOT_IN_STANDARD`. The last two are in that order because the schedule conflict sits at rung 62 and the topic test at rung 70, so a session that both double-books the instructor and teaches an off-curriculum topic answers `ERR_INSTRUCTOR_SCHEDULE_CONFLICT`.

A new session begins at `status` `SCHEDULED`. Three values are derived at creation and never supplied by the caller. `scheduled_hours` is the elapsed time from `starts_at` to `ends_at` expressed in hours — the number of seconds between them divided by 3600, rounded half up to 2 decimal places. `topic_code` is copied from the referenced lesson plan and is immutable thereafter. `pinned_lesson_plan_revision` captures the plan's `approved_revision_number` at this instant, and it is this pin that delivery will later insist still matches. `enrolled_count` and `credited_hours_total` both start at zero, and `void_reason` and the system-managed `delivered_at` instant both start null. Creating a session also re-derives the cohort's `projected_topic_hours`, which is how a replacement session pulls an `AT_RISK` cohort back to `IN_SESSION`. That re-derivation runs the posture test of §3.1 in both directions, so scheduling a session against an `IN_SESSION` cohort that is still short of projected hours on some *other* topic moves that cohort `IN_SESSION` → `AT_RISK` in the same request; the cohort row is touched either way.

An `AcademyInstructor` or a `TrainingCoordinator` records one recruit's attendance against a session by supplying the `recruit_id`, the attendance `status` — `PRESENT`, `PARTIAL`, `ABSENT_EXCUSED` or `ABSENT_UNEXCUSED` — and the `present_hours` actually spent in the room. Those two must agree, or the answer is `ERR_PRESENT_HOURS_INCONSISTENT`: `PRESENT` requires `present_hours` to equal the session's `scheduled_hours`; `PARTIAL` requires `present_hours` to be strictly greater than zero and strictly less than `scheduled_hours`; and both absence values require `present_hours` to be exactly zero. A negative `present_hours`, or one exceeding `scheduled_hours`, is refused the same way. At most one attendance row may exist per session and recruit whatever its `ledger_status`, so a second answers `ERR_ATTENDANCE_ALREADY_RECORDED`. The recruit must be in the academy phase, with `academy_status` at `IN_PROGRESS` or `REMEDIATION`, or the answer is `ERR_RECRUIT_NOT_IN_ACADEMY`. The session's own `status` is not tested at all on this route: a row may be recorded against a session already at `DELIVERED`, `CANCELLED` or `VOIDED` exactly as against a `SCHEDULED` one, and it simply stays `PROVISIONAL`, because only a delivery converts a row to `CREDITED`.

Every new attendance row begins at `ledger_status` `PROVISIONAL` with `credited_hours` and `counted_absence_hours` both `0.00` and `void_reason` null: nothing is credited until the session is closed out. Adding a row re-derives the session's `enrolled_count`, which is always the count of that session's attendance rows whose `ledger_status` is not `VOIDED`; a session sitting at `UNDER_MIN_ENROLLMENT` whose `enrolled_count` reaches or exceeds its `min_enrollment` after the new row is added returns to `SCHEDULED`, closing the `SCHEDULED` ↔ `UNDER_MIN_ENROLLMENT` loop. The opposite direction is driven by cascade: whenever a session's `enrolled_count` is re-derived *downward* — because a recruit exited the pipeline, or because their `academy_status` moved to `DISMISSAL_PENDING` — a session at `SCHEDULED` whose `enrolled_count` has fallen below `min_enrollment` moves to `UNDER_MIN_ENROLLMENT`.

**Closing out a taught session** is the act that converts attendance into curriculum credit. It may be performed by an `AcademyInstructor` or a `TrainingCoordinator`, from `SCHEDULED` or from `UNDER_MIN_ENROLLMENT` — a roster that fell below its enrolment floor does not forfeit the hours the recruits who did attend actually earned. Against a session in any other status, or a session whose `ends_at` is later than the current logical clock (a class cannot be closed out before it has finished), the answer is `ERR_SESSION_NOT_DELIVERABLE`. Closing out further requires that the lesson plan still be `APPROVED` and that the session's `pinned_lesson_plan_revision` still equal that plan's `approved_revision_number`, or `ERR_LESSON_PLAN_NOT_APPROVED`; and that the session's instructor still be `CERTIFIED` with the session's `topic_code` in their `authorized_topic_codes` and a `certification_expires_at` strictly after the clock, or `ERR_INSTRUCTOR_NOT_AUTHORIZED` and `ERR_INSTRUCTOR_CERT_EXPIRED` respectively. Those three later tests run in the ladder order of §2.7 rather than the order just narrated — rung 50, then rung 51, then rung 52 — so a close-out whose instructor's credential has lapsed *and* whose pinned plan revision has been superseded answers `ERR_INSTRUCTOR_CERT_EXPIRED`.

The session then moves `SCHEDULED` → `DELIVERED`, and `delivered_at` is stamped from the logical clock. Every attendance row of the session at `PROVISIONAL` moves to `CREDITED`, with `credited_hours` and `counted_absence_hours` computed as described below. For each credited row the system appends a training record entry of `kind` `HOURS_CREDIT` with `stage_code` `ACADEMY`, the session's `topic_code`, and a `hours_delta` equal to that row's positive `credited_hours`, together with `subject_entity` `CourseSession`, the session's own `id` as `subject_entity_id` and a `detail` of `hours credited from delivered session`; the attendance rows are processed, and so these entries appended, in the rows' ascending `created_at` order. The session's `credited_hours_total` becomes the sum of its rows' `credited_hours`. The cohort's `credited_topic_hours_total` rises by that amount and its `unmet_topic_hours` and `projected_topic_hours` are re-derived, and a cohort at `FORMING` moves to `IN_SESSION` on its first delivered session. Each attending recruit re-derives `credited_hours_total`, `scheduled_basis_hours_total`, `per_topic_credited_hours`, `unexcused_absence_hours` and `make_up_hours_owed`. Each attending recruit's academy standing is then re-evaluated in that same order, and the two tests are themselves ordered: the absence ceiling below is applied first, and it moves `academy_status` to `DISMISSAL_PENDING` only from `IN_PROGRESS` or `REMEDIATION` — a recruit already at `DISMISSAL_PENDING`, or one at `COMPLETE`, is left exactly as it stands — after which nothing further is evaluated for that recruit; otherwise a recruit at `IN_PROGRESS` whose curriculum is now satisfied on the test of §3.5 moves `IN_PROGRESS` → `COMPLETE`, while a recruit at `REMEDIATION` never completes on a delivery.

**Crediting hours: the pinned rounding order.** Each attendance row earns an attendance factor from its `status`: a full `1.00` for `PRESENT`, `0.75` for `PARTIAL` — the partial-credit factor, so a partially attended block loses a quarter of the time actually spent — and `0.00` for both `ABSENT_EXCUSED` and `ABSENT_UNEXCUSED`. That row's credited hours are its `present_hours` multiplied by its attendance factor, **rounded half up to 2 decimal places at the row**. That same row's `counted_absence_hours` is set at the same moment, and from the session rather than from `present_hours`: its session's whole `scheduled_hours` when the row's attendance `status` is `ABSENT_UNEXCUSED`, and `0.00` for `PRESENT`, `PARTIAL` and `ABSENT_EXCUSED` alike. A recruit's `per_topic_credited_hours` for a topic is then the sum of those already-rounded per-row values across every attendance row of that recruit whose `ledger_status` is `CREDITED`, whose session's `status` is `DELIVERED`, and whose session's `topic_code` is that topic. The recruit's `credited_hours_total` is the sum of those per-topic values, with no further rounding applied. Like the cohort's two maps, `per_topic_credited_hours` carries exactly one entry per `topic_requirements` entry of the cohort's standard, in the order those entries were declared, and a topic with no qualifying row reads `0.00`. Rows at `PROVISIONAL` or `VOIDED`, and rows whose session sits at `SCHEDULED`, `UNDER_MIN_ENROLLMENT`, `CANCELLED` or `VOIDED`, contribute nothing at all.

The ordering here is the specification, not an optimisation: rounding is applied *per row and then summed*, never summed and rounded once at the end. Three delivered two-hour sessions of one topic, each attended `PARTIAL` for one and a half hours, credit one and thirteen hundredths of an hour each and total three and thirty-nine hundredths hours — not the three and thirty-eight hundredths that summing first would give.

**The two hour bases.** The same set of rows — `CREDITED` rows of `DELIVERED` sessions — is read two different ways, and the two readings are both persisted. The **attended basis** is the attendance-weighted total just described, written to `credited_hours_total`; `PARTIAL` rows lose a quarter of their time and `ABSENT_EXCUSED` rows contribute nothing. The **scheduled basis** is seat-time-on-roster: every qualifying row whose attendance `status` is `PRESENT`, `PARTIAL` or `ABSENT_EXCUSED` contributes its session's *whole* `scheduled_hours` regardless of `present_hours`, and the total is written to `scheduled_basis_hours_total`. The same three partially attended two-hour sessions read three and thirty-nine hundredths hours on the attended basis and six hours flat on the scheduled basis. Only the **attended** basis gates academy completion and certification; the scheduled basis exists for state reporting.

**Absence accrual.** A recruit's `unexcused_absence_hours` is the sum of the session `scheduled_hours` of every attendance row of theirs whose `status` is `ABSENT_UNEXCUSED`, whose `ledger_status` is `CREDITED` and whose session is `DELIVERED`; rows at `PROVISIONAL` or `VOIDED` contribute nothing, which is exactly why voiding a delivery walks the accrual back down. Mandatory dismissal review is triggered when that accrual is **strictly greater than** the standard's `max_absence_hours`; landing exactly on the limit does not trigger it. With a limit of four hours, two delivered two-hour sessions both marked unexcused-absent leave the recruit at four hours and `academy_status` untouched; a third delivered half-hour session marked the same way reaches four and a half hours and moves `academy_status` to `DISMISSAL_PENDING`, carrying its own void cascade over the recruit's `PROVISIONAL` attendance rows — each of which takes `void_reason` set to the literal `academy dismissal pending`, exactly as cascade A does. Separately, `make_up_hours_owed` is the sum of the session `scheduled_hours` of the recruit's `CREDITED` rows whose `status` is `ABSENT_EXCUSED` — excused time still has to be made up, even though it never counts as unexcused absence.

**Reversing a delivery.** Only an `AcademyDirector` may void a delivery, supplying a `reason`, and only against a session whose `status` is `DELIVERED`; anything else answers `ERR_SESSION_NOT_VOIDABLE`. The session moves `DELIVERED` → `VOIDED`, which is terminal, with `void_reason` recording the supplied reason and `delivered_at` cleared back to null. Every attendance row of the session at `CREDITED` moves to `VOIDED` with `credited_hours` and `counted_absence_hours` both reset to `0.00`, and with its own `void_reason` set to the same supplied reason the session carries. For each reversed row the system appends a training record entry of `kind` `HOURS_REVERSAL` with `stage_code` `ACADEMY`, the session's `topic_code` and a `hours_delta` equal to the *negative* of that row's previous `credited_hours`, together with `subject_entity` `CourseSession`, the session's own `id` as `subject_entity_id` and a `detail` of `hours reversed by voided delivery`, appended in the reversed rows' ascending `created_at` order. The session's own `credited_hours_total` returns to `0.00`, the cohort's `credited_topic_hours_total` falls by the amount the delivery had added — returning to its exact pre-delivery value, which is the conserved quantity of this compensation — and the cohort's `unmet_topic_hours` and `projected_topic_hours` are re-derived, moving an `IN_SESSION` cohort to `AT_RISK` when any topic now projects short. Each affected recruit re-derives every hour aggregate, and a recruit whose `academy_status` was `COMPLETE` but whose curriculum is no longer satisfied reverts `COMPLETE` → `IN_PROGRESS`. That is the *only* `academy_status` movement a void performs: the absence ceiling is not re-tested on this path, so a recruit at `DISMISSAL_PENDING` stays there even though the reversal walks its `unexcused_absence_hours` back below `max_absence_hours`.

**Reading progress.** A recruit's curriculum progress is available as a report that is recomputed from current state on every read and persisted nowhere. The `basis` query parameter selects which hour basis to report — `ATTENDED` when omitted or supplied with an empty value, or `SCHEDULED`; any other value is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400), and the comparison is exact and case-sensitive — and the selected value is echoed back in the response's `basis`. The report carries the `recruit_id`, the `total_hours` on the selected basis, a `per_topic_hours` map and an `unmet_topic_hours` map that each carry exactly one entry per `topic_requirements` entry of the cohort's standard, in the order those entries were declared, where each unmet value is the greater of `0.00` and that topic's `required_hours` less the reported per-topic hours *on the selected basis*. It also carries an `exam_status` map with one entry per `topic_requirements` entry whose `exam_required` is true, in that same declared order, whose value is that topic's examination `status` — the status of the **most recently created** examination row for the topic when the recruit has sat it more than once, so a fresh sitting scheduled after a pass reads `SCHEDULED` rather than `PASSED` (and rows sharing a `created_at`, the usual case under a logical clock that only moves when asked, are separated by insertion order, so the row written last wins) — or the literal `NOT_TAKEN` when the recruit has no examination row for it. Every exam verdict in this document reads a topic that way. It reports `unexcused_absence_hours` and `make_up_hours_owed` as held on the recruit. Finally, `eligible_for_certification` is a curriculum verdict that **always reads the attended basis** regardless of the requested `basis`: it is true only when the attended-basis total reaches the standard's `min_total_hours`, every attended-basis unmet topic hour is `0.00`, and every `exam_status` entry reads `PASSED`. Asking for the scheduled basis can never flip that verdict.

### 3.5. Examinations, Remediation and Academy Standing

A `TrainingCoordinator` or an `AcademyInstructor` schedules a topic examination for a recruit by supplying the `recruit_id` and the `topic_code` being examined (1 to 24 uppercase characters). The topic must be a `topic_requirements` entry of the cohort's certification standard, or the answer is `ERR_TOPIC_NOT_IN_STANDARD`, and that entry's `exam_required` must be true, or the answer is `ERR_EXAM_NOT_REQUIRED_FOR_TOPIC` — the service refuses to create an examination that could never be part of the certification verdict. The recruit's `academy_status` must be `IN_PROGRESS` or `REMEDIATION`, or the answer is `ERR_RECRUIT_NOT_IN_ACADEMY`.

A new examination starts at `status` `SCHEDULED` with `raw_score`, `graded_at` and `graded_by_user_id` all null and `remediation_hours` at zero. Two values are derived at creation and immutable thereafter. `passing_score` is a **snapshot** of the cohort standard's `min_passing_score` taken at this moment: later editing of a standard could never move the bar under an examination already scheduled. `attempt_number` is one plus the count of the recruit's existing examination rows carrying the same `topic_code`, so the first sitting of a topic is attempt 1 and a second row scheduled for the same topic opens at attempt 2.

**Grading.** An `AcademyInstructor` or a `TrainingCoordinator` grades an examination by supplying a `raw_score` between 0 and 100 with at most 2 decimal places. Grading is legal only from `SCHEDULED` or `REMEDIATION_ASSIGNED`; from `PASSED`, `FAILED` or `FAILED_FINAL` the answer is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The grade is stored, `graded_at` is stamped from the logical clock and `graded_by_user_id` from the acting `X-User-ID`, and the outcome is decided by two comparisons in order.

A `raw_score` **greater than or equal to** `passing_score` — the lower bound is inclusive, so a score landing exactly on the bar passes — moves the row to `PASSED`, which is terminal. A `raw_score` strictly below `passing_score` moves the row to `FAILED` while `attempt_number` is strictly less than one plus the standard's `max_remediation_attempts`, and to `FAILED_FINAL` — also terminal — once `attempt_number` has reached that bound. With `max_remediation_attempts` of 2, attempts 1 and 2 fail recoverably and attempt 3 fails finally. Every grading appends a training record entry of `kind` `EXAM_RESULT` with `stage_code` `ACADEMY`, the examination's `topic_code` and a `hours_delta` of `0.00`, `subject_entity` `Examination`, the examination's own `id` as `subject_entity_id` and a `detail` of `examination graded ` followed by the status the row just reached — so `examination graded PASSED`, `examination graded FAILED` or `examination graded FAILED_FINAL` — and re-derives the recruit's `remediation_attempts_used`, incrementing the recruit's own `version`. The entry is appended for every grading, including the final failure, and is written before cascade A runs.

Passing carries two further consequences. A recruit at `academy_status` `REMEDIATION` returns to `IN_PROGRESS` when — and only when — no other examination of that recruit remains at `FAILED` or `REMEDIATION_ASSIGNED`: one topic clearing does not lift a remediation posture that another topic is still holding down. And when the pass completes the curriculum, `academy_status` moves `IN_PROGRESS` → `COMPLETE`, on exactly the criteria set out for delivery in §3.4: every `topic_requirements` entry satisfied on the attended basis, every exam-required topic at `PASSED`, and `unexcused_absence_hours` no greater than `max_absence_hours`.

**Cascade A: final failure.** Reaching `FAILED_FINAL` — from either `SCHEDULED` or `REMEDIATION_ASSIGNED` — runs the same four-level chain. Level 1 is the examination itself reaching `FAILED_FINAL`. Level 2, because the examination became `FAILED_FINAL`, moves the examinee's `academy_status` to `DISMISSAL_PENDING`, from `IN_PROGRESS` or from `REMEDIATION` alike. Level 3, because `academy_status` became `DISMISSAL_PENDING`, sets every attendance row of that recruit whose `ledger_status` is `PROVISIONAL` to `VOIDED` with `void_reason` set to the literal `academy dismissal pending` and `credited_hours` and `counted_absence_hours` reset to `0.00`, and re-derives `enrolled_count` on each course session that lost a row. Level 4, because those counts dropped, moves every affected session whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` and whose `enrolled_count` is now below its `min_enrollment` to `UNDER_MIN_ENROLLMENT`. All four levels land in the same request. Note what level 3 does *not* touch: rows already at `CREDITED` are left alone, so hours already earned survive a pending dismissal.

**Remediation.** Only a `TrainingCoordinator` may assign remedial instruction, supplying `remediation_hours` — required, a number of at least zero and carried to at most two decimal places, with no upper bound of its own — and only against an examination at `FAILED`; anything else is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The gate is a **per-recruit budget shared across topics**: the recruit's `remediation_attempts_used` must be strictly less than the standard's `max_remediation_attempts`, or the answer is `ERR_REMEDIATION_ATTEMPTS_EXHAUSTED`. Because `remediation_attempts_used` is the maximum over all of that recruit's examinations of `attempt_number` minus one, a budget spent on one topic blocks a *first* remediation on another: with `max_remediation_attempts` of 2, a recruit who has already reached attempt 3 on one topic is refused remediation on a freshly failed topic even though that topic is only at attempt 1.

Assigning remediation moves the examination `FAILED` → `REMEDIATION_ASSIGNED`, increments its `attempt_number` by one, and **adds** the requested hours to the row's `remediation_hours`, which accumulates across every assignment on that row rather than being replaced. It also moves the examinee's `academy_status` `IN_PROGRESS` → `REMEDIATION` and increments the recruit's `version`. Grading the row again closes the `FAILED` → `REMEDIATION_ASSIGNED` → `FAILED` remediation loop on that single examination row, and each pass through it consumes one increment of `attempt_number` — which is the mechanism by which the loop eventually walks the row into `FAILED_FINAL` rather than looping forever.

### 3.6. Field Training: Rotations and Daily Observation Reports

Field training is served in numbered phases, each under a different Field Training Officer. A `TrainingCoordinator` plans one by supplying the `recruit_id` of the trainee, the `fto_instructor_id` of the assigned officer, the `phase_number` (1 to 4), the `start_date` and `end_date` of the phase, the `expected_recruit_version` demanded by §2.8, and — for every phase after the first — the `prior_rotation_id` of the rotation this one follows.

Six business gates apply, in the ladder order of §2.7. The named officer must have `fto_certified` true and `certification_status` `CERTIFIED`, or the answer is `ERR_FTO_NOT_CERTIFIED`. The candidate's closed date interval must not intersect another non-aborted rotation of the same recruit — and adjacency counts as intersection per §2.11 — or the answer is `ERR_ROTATION_DATE_OVERLAP`. The **rotating-trainer requirement** forbids the same officer from serving the same recruit twice: if that officer already holds a non-aborted rotation for this recruit, the answer is `ERR_TRAINER_ROTATION_VIOLATION`. The predecessor link must be coherent, or the answer is `ERR_PRIOR_ROTATION_MISMATCH` — which covers four distinct faults: `prior_rotation_id` naming a rotation of a *different* recruit, naming one whose `phase_number` is not exactly one less than the candidate's, naming one whose `status` is not `COMPLETED`, and being null while `phase_number` is greater than 1. Because "exactly one less" than phase 1 is a phase 0 that can never exist, a `prior_rotation_id` supplied alongside `phase_number` 1 is always refused this way; only omitting it, or passing it as null, is legitimate at the first phase. Finally the officer's concurrent load is capped at two: if they already hold two or more rotations at `PLANNED`, `ACTIVE` or `REMEDIAL` whose closed intervals intersect the candidate's, the answer is `ERR_FTO_LOAD_EXCEEDED`. A `phase_number` must also be unique per recruit across rows whose `status` is not `ABORTED`, which is what lets an aborted phase 2 be re-planned as a fresh phase 2. That uniqueness test answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409) and is decided **last** — after every gate above it, the load ceiling of rung 80 included — so a duplicate phase proposed to an officer who is already at the ceiling of two answers `ERR_FTO_LOAD_EXCEEDED`.

A new rotation starts at `status` `PLANNED` with `competency_score` and `rolling_mean_score` both null, `final_narrative` and `abort_reason` null, and `dor_count` and `remedial_flag_count` at zero. Creating it increments the trainee's `version`.

**Putting a rotation into service.** A `TrainingCoordinator` activates a `PLANNED` rotation. The assigned officer must *still* be `CERTIFIED` with `fto_certified` true at this moment, the trainee's `pipeline_stage` must be `FIELD_TRAINING` or `PROBATION` — later phases legitimately run while the recruit is already probationary, which is what lets a rotation flipping remedial push a probation schedule — and no other rotation of the same recruit may be at `ACTIVE` or `REMEDIAL`. Of those three, only the officer's credential has an identifier of its own, `ERR_FTO_NOT_CERTIFIED`; the trainee's stage and the sibling-rotation test both answer the generic `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409), and so does activation attempted from any status other than `PLANNED` — this route never returns `ERR_ROTATION_NOT_ACTIVE`. The status test is decided first, before the credential, so activating an already-`ACTIVE` rotation whose officer has meanwhile lapsed still answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The rotation moves `PLANNED` → `ACTIVE` and the officer's `active_trainee_count` rises by one. `PLANNED` never counted toward that load; activation is the reservation.

**Filing a daily observation report.** Only a `FieldTrainingOfficer` may file one, and only against a rotation at `ACTIVE` or `REMEDIAL`; against any other status the answer is `ERR_ROTATION_NOT_ACTIVE`. The report carries the `author_instructor_id`, which must equal the rotation's `fto_instructor_id` or the answer is `ERR_DOR_AUTHOR_NOT_ASSIGNED_FTO`; the `observed_on` calendar date of the observed shift, which must fall inside the rotation's closed date window and must not be later than the logical clock's date, or the answer is `ERR_DOR_OUTSIDE_ROTATION_WINDOW`; a `dimension_ratings` object carrying exactly the five standardized evaluation guideline dimensions `officer_safety`, `driving`, `report_writing`, `decision_making` and `demeanor`, each an integer from 1 to 7 where 4 is the acceptable level — any further key in that object is ignored, and the stored `dimension_ratings` carries exactly those five keys in the order just listed; a `narrative` of 1 to 2000 characters retained for certification and litigation; and optionally `not_responding_to_training`, which defaults to false. At most one non-voided report may exist per rotation and `observed_on` date, or the answer is `ERR_DUPLICATE_DOR`. Each report stores its own `dor_mean` — the arithmetic mean of its five ratings, rounded half up to 2 decimal places — and starts with `voided` false.

Filing a report re-derives four values on the rotation. `dor_count` is the count of that rotation's reports whose `voided` is false. `remedial_flag_count` is the count of those whose `not_responding_to_training` is true. And both competency variants are recomputed.

**The two competency variants.** Both read the rotation's non-voided reports ordered by `observed_on` ascending with `created_at` ascending as tie-break, and both are null while `dor_count` is zero. Each report contributes a rating sum: the total of its five dimension values. The **rolling mean** pools every rating equally — the sum of all rating sums divided by five times the report count, rounded half up to 2 decimal places once at the end — and is written to `rolling_mean_score`. The **terminally weighted** variant is a recency-weighted two-bucket mean: the recent bucket is the last three reports in that ordering (or all of them when fewer than three exist), the earlier bucket is everything before it, each bucket's mean is the sum of its rating sums divided by five times its row count and is left **unrounded**, and the result is 0.60 of the recent mean plus 0.40 of the earlier mean, rounded half up to 2 decimal places once at the end. When the earlier bucket is empty the earlier mean is taken to be the recent mean, so with three or fewer reports the weighting collapses to the plain mean of them. The result is written to `competency_score`.

The two variants deliberately disagree, and only the terminally weighted one carries authority. Four reports whose ratings are uniform within each report at 6, 6, 3 and 3 — rating sums 30, 30, 15, 15 — give a rolling mean of 90 divided by 20, or 4.50; the recent bucket is the last three (30, 15, 15) for a recent mean of 4.0, the earlier bucket is the single 30 for an earlier mean of 6.0, and the terminally weighted score is 0.60 times 4.0 plus 0.40 times 6.0, or 4.80. A late slump therefore dominates.

**Cascade B: the remedial flip.** After every report is filed, the recomputed **terminally weighted** `competency_score` is compared against the remedial threshold of 4.00. A rotation at `ACTIVE` whose recomputed score is **strictly less than** 4.00 moves to `REMEDIAL` — level 2 of the cascade. Because the rotation became `REMEDIAL`, level 3 moves the trainee's `probation_status` from `ACTIVE` to `EXTENSION_REQUIRED` (only when it was `ACTIVE`). Because `probation_status` changed, level 4 re-derives the probation schedule of §3.7, moving `probation_milestone_score`, `probation_extension_days` and `probation_scheduled_end`, and increments the trainee's `version`. In the other direction, a rotation at `REMEDIAL` whose recomputed score reaches 4.00 or above returns to `ACTIVE`, closing the loop — but this direction does **not** restore the trainee's `probation_status`: only an explicit extension decision or the abort path closes that loop. The `not_responding_to_training` flag only increments `remedial_flag_count`; it never gates the flip on its own, and a rotation with several flags raised stays `ACTIVE` while its score holds at or above 4.00. The officer's `active_trainee_count` is unchanged by the flip, because a `REMEDIAL` rotation still occupies the officer's load. Every filed report appends a training record entry of `kind` `DOR_FILED` with `stage_code` `FIELD_TRAINING`, a `hours_delta` of `0.00`, `subject_entity` `ObservationReport`, the new report's own `id` as `subject_entity_id` and a `detail` of the literal `daily observation report filed`; the entry is appended before the cascade above is evaluated, and it is appended whether or not the score moves.

**Closing a rotation.** A `FieldTrainingOfficer` or a `TrainingCoordinator` completes a rotation from `ACTIVE` **or** `REMEDIAL`, supplying the `final_narrative`. Completing a remedial rotation is legal — the 4.00 threshold gates the `ACTIVE` ↔ `REMEDIAL` loop, not this transition — and it does **not** clear a trainee's `EXTENSION_REQUIRED` posture. Completion requires at least one non-voided report, so `dor_count` must be 1 or more — tested first — and requires the logical clock's date to have reached or passed the rotation's `end_date`; both failures answer `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, while completion from any other status answers `ERR_ROTATION_NOT_ACTIVE` and is decided ahead of both. The rotation moves to `COMPLETED`, which is terminal, the `final_narrative` is stored, the officer's `active_trainee_count` falls by one, and the trainee re-derives the probation schedule whenever `probation_start` is not null.

**Aborting a rotation: the compensation.** An `AcademyDirector` or a `TrainingCoordinator` aborts a rotation from `PLANNED`, `ACTIVE` or `REMEDIAL`, supplying a `reason` stored as `abort_reason`. Against a rotation already `COMPLETED` or `ABORTED` the answer is `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409), not `ERR_ROTATION_NOT_ACTIVE`. The rotation becomes `ABORTED`, which is terminal. From `ACTIVE` or `REMEDIAL` the officer's `active_trainee_count` falls by one, restoring exactly the value it held before activation — the conserved quantity of this compensation; from `PLANNED` it is unchanged, because `PLANNED` never counted. The officer's own row is touched — its `updated_at` and `version` move — only on an abort from `ACTIVE` or `REMEDIAL`; an abort from `PLANNED` leaves that row alone. Every non-voided report of the rotation is marked `voided` true, and `dor_count`, `remedial_flag_count`, `competency_score` and `rolling_mean_score` all return to their empty-set values, meaning both scores become null. And the compensation proper: when the trainee's `probation_status` is `EXTENSION_REQUIRED` and no rotation of that trainee remains at `REMEDIAL`, `probation_status` returns to `ACTIVE` and the probation schedule is re-derived, restoring `probation_extension_days` and `probation_scheduled_end` to their pre-cascade values. Aborting the rotation that caused a cascade-B extension therefore undoes it exactly; aborting one while another remedial rotation is still open does not. That probation compensation is armed on this endpoint alone: the aborts cascade C performs on a revocation (§3.3), and the aborts a pipeline exit or a probation separation performs (§3.7), void the reports and release the officer's load in exactly the same way, but never return a trainee's `probation_status` from `EXTENSION_REQUIRED` to `ACTIVE`.

### 3.7. Advancing the Pipeline, Probation and Certification

**Advancing one hop.** A `TrainingCoordinator` advances a recruit's `pipeline_stage` by naming the `to_stage`. Exactly one successor is legal from each stage, and naming anything else — including a stage the recruit has already passed, or a hop that skips one — answers `ERR_STAGE_TRANSITION_NOT_ALLOWED`. `to_stage` must first *be* one of the eight declared `pipeline_stage` values, or the body fails shape at rung 20 with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; it is only a well-formed stage that is not the single legal successor which reaches `ERR_STAGE_TRANSITION_NOT_ALLOWED`. The `APPLICANT` → `SCREENING` hop is *not* performed this way at all: it happens automatically when the recruit's first screening is ordered, as described in §3.2. That leaves three hops driven by this action.

`SCREENING` → `ACADEMY` requires the recruit's cohort to be at `FORMING`, `IN_SESSION` or `AT_RISK` — tested **first**, at rung 40, so a hop into a `GRADUATED` cohort answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE` even when the clearance is also short — and then requires `clearance_status` to be exactly `CLEARED`, answering `ERR_CLEARANCE_INCOMPLETE` while it is `PENDING` or `PARTIAL`. A `DISQUALIFIED` clearance is diagnosed further, and the substantive screening faults of §2.7 are decided in exactly this order: any screening record of the recruit carrying a non-empty `disqualifier_codes` array — whatever that record's own status, so codes sitting on an already waived or cleared record still count — answers `ERR_DISQUALIFIER_UNWAIVED`; failing that, any `PHYSICAL_FITNESS` screening of the recruit whose `composite_score` is non-null and strictly below the standard's `fitness_pass_score` answers `ERR_FITNESS_STANDARD_NOT_MET`; failing both, the answer falls back to `ERR_CLEARANCE_INCOMPLETE`. It sets `academy_status` to `IN_PROGRESS` from `NOT_STARTED`, and leaves it alone from any other value. `ACADEMY` → `FIELD_TRAINING` requires `academy_status` to be exactly `COMPLETE`; because `COMPLETE` is reached only by the curriculum test of §3.4 and §3.5, this hop can never be used to bypass unfinished hours or an unpassed examination. `FIELD_TRAINING` → `PROBATION` requires at least one rotation of the recruit at `COMPLETED` and no rotation of the recruit still at `PLANNED` — an unstarted phase must be activated and closed, or aborted, before probation begins. Every successful hop appends a training record entry of `kind` `STAGE_ENTRY` carrying the destination stage as its `stage_code`, `subject_entity` `Recruit`, the recruit's own `id` as `subject_entity_id` and a `detail` of `pipeline stage advanced to ` followed by that destination stage, and increments the recruit's `version`. Any hop attempted on a recruit whose `pipeline_stage` is already `CERTIFIED`, `DISMISSED` or `WITHDRAWN` answers `ERR_RECRUIT_TERMINAL`.

The probation hop is the only one that accepts data: `probation_start` is the calendar date probation begins and is **required** whenever `to_stage` is `PROBATION`, an absent or `null` value being a shape failure at rung 20, and `probation_base_days` is the base probationary length, an optional integer of at least zero defaulting to 365 when omitted. Both are read at rung 20 — before the terminal check of rung 39 and before the successor test — so a `to_stage` of `PROBATION` sent without `probation_start` answers `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` even against an already terminal recruit or a recruit for whom the hop would have been illegal anyway. Both are honoured only for this hop and ignored on the others. The hop sets `probation_status` to `ACTIVE` from `NOT_APPLICABLE` and runs the probation schedule.

**The probation schedule.** Four derived fields — `probation_milestone_due_date`, `probation_milestone_score`, `probation_extension_days` and `probation_scheduled_end` — are re-derived at exactly five points: when a recruit enters probation on the `FIELD_TRAINING` → `PROBATION` hop; on every probation decision, whichever of `PASS`, `EXTEND` and `SEPARATE` it is; when a rotation of the trainee reaches `COMPLETED`, provided `probation_start` is not null; when cascade B moves the trainee's `probation_status` from `ACTIVE` to `EXTENSION_REQUIRED`; and when an abort returns `probation_status` from `EXTENSION_REQUIRED` to `ACTIVE`. Nothing else re-derives them — in particular a rotation flipping `REMEDIAL` → `ACTIVE`, an abort that does not restore `probation_status`, and a separation recorded through the dismissal endpoint all leave the four fields untouched — and when `probation_start` is null the derivation is a no-op that writes none of them. Read casually the four look circular: the milestone date seems to depend on the scheduled end, the scheduled end depends on the extension, the extension depends on the milestone score, and the milestone score depends on the milestone date. It is not circular, because the evaluation order is **pinned as a single forward pass with no iteration and no fixed-point search**.

Step 1 derives `probation_milestone_due_date` from `probation_base_days` **only**: it is `probation_start` plus the floor of `probation_base_days` multiplied by one half, counted in days by exact calendar addition. It must not be derived from `probation_scheduled_end`.

Step 2 selects `probation_milestone_score` by an explicit selector over the trainee's rotations. Eligible rows are those of this recruit whose `status` is `COMPLETED` or `REMEDIAL`, whose `end_date` is at or before `probation_milestone_due_date`, and whose `competency_score` is not null; `PLANNED`, `ACTIVE` and `ABORTED` rotations are excluded, which is precisely why aborting a remedial rotation restores the pre-cascade extension. Eligible rows are ranked by `end_date` descending, with `created_at` descending as tie-break — never by identifier — and `probation_milestone_score` becomes the terminally weighted `competency_score` of the top-ranked row. When no row qualifies, `probation_milestone_score` is null.

Step 3 sets `probation_extension_days` from that score alone: zero days when the score is null, zero when it is 5.00 or above, 30 days when it is at least 4.00 and below 5.00, and 60 days when it is below 4.00. Scores are compared at 2 decimal places.

Step 4, **last**, sets `probation_scheduled_end` to `probation_start` plus `probation_base_days` plus `probation_extension_days`, in days, by exact calendar addition. Nothing in steps 1 through 3 reads this value.

A recruit whose `probation_start` is 2027-01-01 with 365 base days gets a milestone date of 2027-07-02, because the floor of 365 halved is 182 days. If one completed rotation ends 2027-03-31 with a terminally weighted score of 4.80, it is selected, the 4.00-to-5.00 tier grants 30 days, and `probation_scheduled_end` becomes 2028-01-31. With no qualifying rotation the extension is zero and the scheduled end is 2028-01-01. Deriving step 1 from the scheduled end instead would give a milestone date of 2027-07-17 and can then select a different rotation altogether — which is why the order is pinned.

**Probation decisions.** Only an `AcademyDirector` records one, supplying the `decision` and a `justification` of 1 to 2000 characters — required by body shape, but retained nowhere and absent from every representation of the recruit. The recruit's `probation_status` must be `ACTIVE` or `EXTENSION_REQUIRED`, or the answer is `ERR_PROBATION_NOT_ACTIVE`. `PASS` is legal from `ACTIVE` only and moves `probation_status` to `PASSED`, which is terminal; it requires `probation_milestone_score` not to be null, without which the trigger is not declared and the answer is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, and only **then** requires the logical clock's date to have reached `probation_milestone_due_date`, or the answer is `ERR_MILESTONE_NOT_REACHED`. That order is the specification: a recruit with a null milestone score and an unreached milestone date answers `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, not `ERR_MILESTONE_NOT_REACHED`. `EXTEND` is legal from `EXTENSION_REQUIRED` only and returns `probation_status` to `ACTIVE`, closing the `ACTIVE` ↔ `EXTENSION_REQUIRED` loop; from `ACTIVE` there is nothing to extend and the answer is `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. `SEPARATE` is legal from either and moves `probation_status` to `SEPARATED`, which is terminal, while also setting `pipeline_stage` to `DISMISSED` with `dismissal_reason_code` `PROBATION_SEPARATION` and aborting every rotation of the recruit at `PLANNED`, `ACTIVE` or `REMEDIAL` — each such rotation taking `abort_reason` set to the literal `probation separation`, and its non-voided reports marked `voided` true — so the affected officers re-derive `active_trainee_count`. Neither `EXTEND` nor `SEPARATE` is gated by the milestone date. Every decision re-derives the probation schedule, increments the recruit's `version`, and appends a training record entry of `kind` `PROBATION_DECISION` with `stage_code` `PROBATION` — except a separation, which is recorded as an `ADVERSE_EXIT`. Either entry carries `subject_entity` `Recruit`, the recruit's own `id` as `subject_entity_id` and a `detail` of `probation decision ` followed by the decision word, so a separation's `ADVERSE_EXIT` reads `probation decision SEPARATE` rather than the dismissal wording below.

**Dismissal and voluntary withdrawal.** Only an `AcademyDirector` records an exit, supplying a `reason_code` and a `justification` of 1 to 2000 characters — again required by body shape, retained nowhere and absent from every representation. The `reason_code` is one of `ACADEMIC_FAILURE`, `ABSENCE_LIMIT`, `INTEGRITY`, `MEDICAL`, `FIELD_TRAINING_FAILURE`, `PROBATION_SEPARATION` or `VOLUNTARY_WITHDRAWAL`, and it alone chooses the destination: `VOLUNTARY_WITHDRAWAL` routes `pipeline_stage` to `WITHDRAWN`, every other code routes it to `DISMISSED`. The exit is legal from `SCREENING`, `ACADEMY`, `FIELD_TRAINING` or `PROBATION`; against a recruit already `CERTIFIED`, `DISMISSED` or `WITHDRAWN` the answer is `ERR_RECRUIT_TERMINAL`, and against one still at `APPLICANT` — no screening having been ordered yet, so there is nothing to exit from — the answer is `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409). The stored `dismissal_reason_code` is the supplied code.

What the exit cascades depends on the stage it leaves. From `SCREENING`, screening rows at `ORDERED`, `FINDINGS_RECORDED` or `RETEST_REQUIRED` are **left untouched** — this cascade only ever reaches terminal-bound work. From `ACADEMY`, every attendance row of the recruit at `PROVISIONAL` is set to `VOIDED` with `void_reason` set to the literal `recruit exited pipeline`, each affected session re-derives `enrolled_count`, and a `SCHEDULED` session that falls below `min_enrollment` moves to `UNDER_MIN_ENROLLMENT`; the recruit's `CREDITED` rows are left alone, so hours already earned survive the exit. From `FIELD_TRAINING`, every rotation of the recruit at `PLANNED`, `ACTIVE` or `REMEDIAL` becomes `ABORTED` with `abort_reason` set to that same literal `recruit exited pipeline`, each such rotation's non-voided reports are marked `voided` true, and each affected officer re-derives `active_trainee_count`. From `PROBATION`, `probation_status` moves to `SEPARATED` from `ACTIVE` or `EXTENSION_REQUIRED` and the same rotation aborts run — but the academy void of the `ACADEMY` branch does not, so a probationary recruit's `PROVISIONAL` attendance rows are untouched by this exit. Every exit appends one training record entry of `kind` `ADVERSE_EXIT` carrying the departed stage as its `stage_code`, `subject_entity` `Recruit`, the recruit's own `id` as `subject_entity_id` and a `detail` of `pipeline exit recorded with reason ` followed by the supplied `reason_code` — for voluntary withdrawals too, which is why the attrition arithmetic of §3.8 has to filter on `dismissal_reason_code` rather than on entry `kind` — and increments the recruit's `version`.

**Certifying graduates.** Only an `AcademyDirector` runs certification, and it is a bulk operation over the whole cohort. The cohort's `status` must be `IN_SESSION` or `AT_RISK`; on a `FORMING` or `GRADUATED` cohort the whole call is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, which is what makes a *second* certification run on the same cohort a conflict rather than a zero-count success.

Every recruit of the cohort is assessed against four preconditions: `probation_status` must be `PASSED` (and `pipeline_stage` must be `PROBATION`); `credited_hours_total` on the attended basis must reach the standard's `min_total_hours`; every `topic_requirements` entry must be satisfied by the recruit's `per_topic_credited_hours`; and every exam-required topic must have an examination at `PASSED`. Recruits who satisfy all four move `pipeline_stage` `PROBATION` → `CERTIFIED`, which is terminal, and each gets a training record entry of `kind` `CERTIFICATION_ISSUED` with `stage_code` `PROBATION`, `subject_entity` `Recruit`, the recruit's own `id` as `subject_entity_id`, a `hours_delta` of `0.00` and a `detail` of `certification issued on cohort certification run`; the entry's `actor_role` and `actor_user_id` are the running director's, not `system`.

Recruits who fail any precondition are **skipped, not rejected** — one ineligible recruit never spoils the run. The response reports the `academy_class_id`, the resulting `class_status`, the `certified_count`, the `certified_recruit_ids` of those newly certified, and a `skipped` array whose entries are each shaped as a `recruit_id` and a `reason_code`. The `reason_code` is the **first applicable** of exactly four values, evaluated in exactly this order: `PROBATION_NOT_PASSED` when `pipeline_stage` is not `PROBATION` or `probation_status` is not `PASSED`; `HOURS_SHORTFALL` when the recruit's own attended-basis `credited_hours_total` is below `min_total_hours` — reachable whenever `min_total_hours` exceeds the sum of the `topic_requirements` hours, because academy completion only gates the per-topic minima; `TOPIC_SHORTFALL` when any topic requirement is unmet; and `EXAM_NOT_PASSED` when any required topic lacks a passed examination. Both the `skipped` array and `certified_recruit_ids` are ordered by the recruit's `created_at` ascending, because the cohort's recruits are walked in that single order and each is appended to whichever list it lands in.

The cohort's `graduated_count` is re-derived, and its `status` moves to `GRADUATED` — from `IN_SESSION` or from `AT_RISK` — **only when at least one recruit was certified in this request**. A run in which zero recruits qualify leaves the cohort posture completely unchanged and reports a `certified_count` of zero: the empty-set edge is a success, not an error, and the same cohort may be run again later once recruits qualify. It is the cohort's `status` that a zero-certified run leaves alone; the cohort row itself is touched by every certification run, so its `updated_at` and `version` each advance once even when nobody was certified.

### 3.8. The Immutable Training Record, Attrition and Pipeline Forecasting

Every consequential act in the pipeline writes an append-only training record entry, retained as certification and litigation-hold evidence. Entries are never updated and never deleted; as noted in §2.4, a training record entry carries only an `id` and a `created_at` and has no `updated_at` or `version`. No endpoint creates one directly — they are written exclusively as side effects.

Each entry names its subject through `recruit_id` and the `academy_class_id` the entry rolls up to, and classifies itself through `kind`, which is one of nine values: `STAGE_ENTRY` for a pipeline stage being entered, `ADVERSE_EXIT` for a dismissal or withdrawal, `HOURS_CREDIT` and `HOURS_REVERSAL` for the two directions of curriculum crediting, `SCREENING_ADJUDICATION` for a screening decision, `EXAM_RESULT` for a grading, `DOR_FILED` for a daily observation report, `PROBATION_DECISION` for a probationary milestone decision, and `CERTIFICATION_ISSUED` for a certification. `stage_code` is the pipeline stage the entry refers to — `SCREENING`, `ACADEMY`, `FIELD_TRAINING` or `PROBATION` — and is null for entries that are not stage-scoped. `topic_code` is the curriculum topic it refers to and is null when not topic-scoped. `hours_delta` is the signed hours movement: positive on an hours credit, negative on an hours reversal, and exactly `0.00` for every other kind. `actor_role` and `actor_user_id` record the acting request's `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID`, and they do so even for entries a cascade writes rather than the operator asking for them directly — the `HOURS_CREDIT` rows of a delivery carry the delivering instructor or coordinator, not `system`. Exactly one entry in the system is attributed to the literal `system` in both fields: the automatic `APPLICANT` → `SCREENING` stage entry of §3.2. `subject_entity` names the entity the entry documents, such as the literal `CourseSession`, and `subject_entity_id` identifies the documented row — identity only, never used in any business decision. `detail` is a human-readable summary retained verbatim in the litigation record, and `recorded_at_utc` is the logical-clock instant of the entry.

**Attrition analysis.** Because stage entries and adverse exits are recorded as immutable entries, per-stage attrition is computed from the record rather than from current recruit state. The four stages are read in one fixed order — `SCREENING`, then `ACADEMY`, then `FIELD_TRAINING`, then `PROBATION` — and the window is every entry ever written up to the current logical clock, inclusive.

For each stage, the number **entered** is the count of *distinct* `recruit_id` values over the cohort's entries whose `kind` is `STAGE_ENTRY` and whose `stage_code` is that stage; counting distinct recruits rather than rows means a recruit who somehow accumulated two entries for one stage still counts once. The number of **adverse exits** is the count of distinct `recruit_id` values over the cohort's entries whose `kind` is `ADVERSE_EXIT` and whose `stage_code` is that stage **and whose recruit's `dismissal_reason_code` is not** `VOLUNTARY_WITHDRAWAL`. **Voluntary exits** are counted the same way with the reason code equal to `VOLUNTARY_WITHDRAWAL`; they are reported separately and never enter the attrition numerator. The **attrition rate** of a stage is its adverse exits divided by its entered count, rounded half up to 4 decimal places, and is null when nothing entered that stage.

`survival_to_certification` is the product, over the stages whose entered count is greater than zero, of one minus each stage's attrition rate, rounded half up to 4 decimal places. Stages whose rate is null contribute a factor of exactly one. Each factor is built from that stage's *already-rounded* 4-decimal rate, and the running product is carried exactly and left unrounded until that final rounding. The rounding order is pinned: **the product is rounded to 4 decimal places before it is divided into** by the forecast below. `voluntary_exits_total` is the sum of the per-stage voluntary exits.

The attrition report returns the `academy_class_id`, a `stages` array carrying exactly one entry per stage in that fixed order — each shaped as a `stage_code`, an `entered` count, an `adverse_exits` count, a `voluntary_exits` count and an `attrition_rate` that is a number or null — together with `survival_to_certification`, `voluntary_exits_total`, and `rolling_mean_scores`. That last is an object keyed by rotation identifier whose value is that rotation's rolling-mean score at 2 decimal places, or null while its `dor_count` is zero, covering every rotation of the cohort whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `REMEDIAL`, keyed in those rotations' ascending `created_at` order.

**Pipeline forecasting.** The recruiting forecast answers how many applicants must be taken in to yield a target number of sworn officers. The caller supplies `target_sworn_officers` as a **required** query parameter: a bare run of decimal digits with an optional leading sign, surrounding whitespace trimmed, evaluating to an integer of at least zero. An absent, empty, non-integer (`25.0`) or negative value is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400), and that rejection is decided *after* the cohort's path identifier has resolved, so a forecast aimed at a cohort that does not exist answers `ERR_ACADEMY_CLASS_NOT_FOUND` first. `required_applicants` is that target divided by `survival_to_certification`, rounded **up** to the next whole applicant, and is null when `survival_to_certification` is zero — an entirely non-surviving pipeline has no finite answer. When it is null, every value in `per_stage_expected` is null too, one per stage in the same fixed order.

`per_stage_expected` is then a pinned forward pass evaluated in the same fixed stage order, each step **floored** to a whole recruit: the screening expectation is `required_applicants` itself, and each later stage's expectation is the previous stage's expectation multiplied by one minus the *previous* stage's attrition rate, floored. A stage whose attrition rate is null contributes a factor of one, carrying the previous stage's value through unchanged. Flooring at every step, rather than once at the end, is the specification.

A cohort in which screening saw 10 entered and 2 adverse exits, the academy 8 and 1, field training 7 and 0, and probation nothing at all, has rates of 0.2000, 0.1250, 0.0000 and null. Survival is 0.8 times 0.875 times 1.0 times 1.0, rounded to 0.7000. A target of 25 sworn officers requires the ceiling of 25 divided by 0.7000, which is 36 applicants; a target of 40 requires the ceiling of 40 divided by 0.7000, which is 58. The forward pass for a target of 25 then reads 36 at screening, the floor of 36 times 0.8 or 28 at the academy, the floor of 28 times 0.875 or 24 at field training, and the floor of 24 times 1.0 or 24 at probation. Had one of those three screening exits carried `dismissal_reason_code` `VOLUNTARY_WITHDRAWAL`, it would not appear in the screening adverse exits at all: it would show only as one voluntary exit at screening and a `voluntary_exits_total` of one, and every rate and expectation above would change accordingly. The forecast response returns the `academy_class_id`, the echoed `target_sworn_officers`, `survival_to_certification`, `required_applicants` and `per_stage_expected`.
