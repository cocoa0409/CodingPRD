# Network Operations Circuit Fault API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

An enterprise Network Operations Center (NOC) watches thousands of leased access and transport circuits, the routers and switches that terminate them, and the business application paths that ride across both. When a fibre is cut at three in the morning, the monitoring platform does not report one problem — it reports forty, because every device behind the failed uplink screams at the same time. The people on shift must decide, in minutes, which single condition is the root cause, how much business impact it carries, whether the affected application still has a surviving redundant leg, which carrier owns the fault, and how long the carrier has under contract before the outage becomes a billable service-level breach. Today that reasoning is done in spreadsheets and chat threads, and the evidence needed to win a credit claim three weeks later is usually gone.

This service is the system of record for that reasoning. It ingests raw alarms and correlates them into a single root condition with its suppressed noise, derives circuit and device health from the surviving root conditions, propagates that health through the application-path graph to answer "is this application still protected?", classifies incidents against a weighted impact model with response and escalation deadlines, and drives carrier trouble tickets through carrier test evidence and tier escalation to a confirmed restoration. It then closes the commercial loop: restoration durations are measured on two independent clocks, credit claims are computed from contracted percentages and capped, and the carrier's open credit exposure and chronic-circuit count feed a periodic vendor performance review that can approve circuit replacement.

Two further concerns are first-class rather than bolted on. **Change coordination** ensures that carrier-announced maintenance and internal change windows are approved with explicit acknowledgement of the redundancy risk they create, that they suppress monitoring only for their duration, and that deliberate failover tests never run while a maintenance window is in effect on the same topology. **Capacity monitoring** watches utilization samples and, when a circuit sustains high utilization, moves it into an upgrade posture and drafts the upgrade order automatically, holding the committed bandwidth on the carrier's books until the order either completes or is cancelled. Every rule below is a contract: the derived values, the ordering of checks, and the exact rejection codes are all observable through the API and are all tested.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Operator Identity, Roles and Carrier Scope

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that principal is acting in. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the six roles defined below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If both headers are well formed but the named role is not permitted to invoke the requested operation — the authorization matrix is the Authorized Role(s) column of the endpoint table in Section 4 — the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The six roles are `NocAnalyst`, `NocShiftLead`, `CarrierLiaison`, `VendorManager`, `CapacityPlanner` and `PlatformAdmin`. A `NocAnalyst` is the front-line operator: they ingest alarms, clear them, record circuit test evidence, triage incidents and open carrier trouble tickets. A `NocShiftLead` carries shift authority: incident escalation and resolution, carrier tier escalation, restoration confirmation, maintenance-window review and execution, failover-test scheduling and result attestation. A `CarrierLiaison` acts on the carrier's behalf, posting carrier-side ticket updates, announcing planned maintenance and answering credit claims. A `VendorManager` owns the commercial relationship: filing, submitting, settling and withdrawing credit claims, running vendor performance reviews and replacement decisions, and approving upgrade orders. A `CapacityPlanner` owns utilization: ingesting samples and driving upgrade orders from draft through completion. A `PlatformAdmin` is the operational superuser who seeds carriers, circuits, devices, application paths and path segments, drives the logical clock, and may additionally perform any operational write that another role can perform.

When `X-User-Role` is `NocAnalyst`, `NocShiftLead`, `VendorManager`, `CapacityPlanner` or `PlatformAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it where a workflow demands an audit trail — as `opened_by_user_id` and `restoration_confirmed_by_user_id` on a carrier ticket, `recorded_by_user_id` on a test result, `submitted_by_user_id` on a credit claim, `approved_by_user_id` on a maintenance window and on an upgrade order, `created_by_user_id` on an upgrade order, `scheduled_by_user_id` and `attested_by_user_id` on a failover test, and `decided_by_user_id` on a vendor review — but it never compares that value to any entity's `id`.

`CarrierLiaison` is different: when `X-User-Role` is `CarrierLiaison`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting carrier, and every ownership check compares a resource's carrier reference to that value. A liaison posting an update on a carrier ticket, or answering a credit claim, must be the carrier named by that ticket's or claim's `carrier_id`; a liaison announcing maintenance must own the circuit the window applies to, meaning the circuit's `carrier_id` must equal `X-User-ID`; a liaison reviewing a window is likewise scoped to the window's `carrier_id`. Any mismatch is rejected with `ERR_CARRIER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). Read access granted to a `CarrierLiaison` is scoped the same way — a liaison sees its own carrier, its own carrier's circuits, tickets, test evidence, claims and windows.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Collection reads accept `limit` and `offset` as query-string parameters. `limit` defaults to 10, may be no smaller than 1 and no larger than 100; `offset` defaults to 0 and may be no smaller than 0. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). The four pagination fields `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` are reported in the `meta` object of every list response: `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before the page window is applied, and `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond the current window.

Unless a subsection below states otherwise, every collection is returned in ascending `created_at` order — oldest row first — so that a client walking pages with a growing `offset` observes a stable sequence. Collections also accept filters named after the fields they narrow; those filters are introduced alongside the workflows they serve in Section 3. A filter value that is not a member of the corresponding enumeration is likewise an `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock and Temporal Coherence

Nothing in this service reads the host machine's wall clock. Every timestamp the server stamps, every dwell measurement, every soak measurement, every window-in-effect test and every service-level duration is evaluated against a **logical clock** that starts at `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. An administrative test-driving capability moves that clock to a supplied `to_iso8601` instant and answers with the resulting `current_utc` value; the clock only ever moves forward, and moving it mutates no business entity. Because the clock is explicit, an outage that lasted nine hours can be replayed in a second, and every derived duration in this document is reproducible.

Instant and interval inputs must be internally coherent, and incoherence is reported as `ERR_TIMESTAMP_RANGE_INVALID` (HTTP 422). Concretely: a maintenance window's `window_end` must be strictly after its `window_start`; a failover test's `scheduled_end` must be strictly after its `scheduled_start`; a vendor review's `period_end` must be strictly after its `period_start`; an availability query's `to_iso8601` must be strictly after its `from_iso8601`; an alarm's `raised_at` must be at or before the current logical clock instant; a clearing `cleared_at` must fall at or after the alarm's `raised_at` and at or before the current clock instant; and a carrier-asserted `restored_at` must fall strictly after the ticket's `opened_at` and at or before the current clock instant. Those seven operations — alarm ingestion, alarm clearing, carrier ticket updates, maintenance-window creation, failover-test scheduling, vendor-review creation and the path availability query — are exactly the operations this check guards.

### 2.5. Record Identity, Timestamps and Versioning

Every persisted record carries an `id` that is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix and no client-supplied component. Records carry a `created_at` timestamp stamped from the logical clock at insertion, formatted as ISO 8601 UTC with three fractional digits. Most records additionally carry an `updated_at` timestamp, refreshed on every mutation, and an integer `version` that begins at 1 when the record is created and increases by exactly 1 on each mutation of that record. The `version` field is not decorative: it is the observable evidence of the cascades described throughout Section 3, and it is the value the stale-parent checks in Section 2.6 compare against.

Three record types are deliberately append-only and therefore carry only `id` and `created_at` — both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from them entirely. Those types are the alarm, the carrier test result, and the capacity reading. An alarm's `correlation_state` and `cleared_at` are still updated by correlation and clearing, but the alarm record exposes no `version` and no `updated_at`; test results and capacity readings are never modified after insertion at all.

### 2.6. Stale-Parent Rejection When Deriving Records From a Parent

Two create operations derive their entire content from a parent record that other workflows are also mutating, so both require the client to prove it has observed the parent's latest state.

A manual credit-claim create must carry `expected_carrier_ticket_version` holding the `version` of the carrier ticket the client last read. If that value does not equal the ticket's current `version` — because a carrier update, a restoration confirmation, a test result or an earlier claim create has advanced it since the client's read — the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CARRIER_TICKET_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The check is not a state guard: a manual claim legitimately supersedes a withdrawn automatic draft on the same ticket, so no ticket state forbids the operation, and the stale expected version is the only thing the server refuses. On success the ticket's `version` advances by 1.

An upgrade-order create must likewise carry `expected_circuit_version` holding the `version` of the target circuit as the client last read it, and a mismatch against the circuit's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CIRCUIT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Again the operation itself is not state-guarded — a manual order legitimately supersedes a cancelled automatic order on the same circuit — and on success the circuit's `version` advances by 1. In the formal endpoint vocabulary of Section 4 these two rules attach to `POST /sla-credit-claims` and `POST /upgrade-orders` respectively. No other operation in this service accepts an expected-version field.

### 2.7. The Order in Which Requests Are Refused

When a request violates several rules at once, the response must name the violation the server reached first, and the order is fixed. Checks are evaluated strictly in the ascending sequence below; the first failure short-circuits the request and no later check runs.

1. **Authentication.** Both headers present and `X-User-Role` one of the six declared roles, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every operation.
2. **Role authorization.** The acting role appears in the operation's authorized-role list, else `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403). Applies to every operation.
3. **Carrier scope.** For a `CarrierLiaison`, `X-User-ID` must equal the `carrier_id` of the targeted carrier ticket, credit claim or maintenance window — and, when a window is being created with `origin` `CARRIER_NOTIFIED`, the `carrier_id` of the circuit named by `circuit_id` — else `ERR_CARRIER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (403). This guards exactly four operations: posting a carrier ticket update, answering a credit claim, creating a maintenance window with carrier origin, and reviewing a maintenance window.
4. **Path resource existence,** validated left to right along the URL. A missing carrier, circuit, device, application path, path segment, alarm, incident, carrier ticket, credit claim, maintenance window, failover test, upgrade order or vendor review yields `ERR_CARRIER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CIRCUIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DEVICE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PATH_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SEGMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ALARM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TICKET_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WINDOW_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TEST_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_FOUND` respectively, all HTTP 404. Applies to every operation that takes a path parameter.
5. **Body reference existence.** Every reference supplied in a request body must resolve to an existing row — `carrier_id`, `circuit_id`, `device_id`, `application_path_id`, `incident_id`, `carrier_ticket_id`, `source_circuit_id` and `source_device_id` — reported with the same not-found identifiers as above. This guards circuit creation, device creation, path-segment creation, alarm ingestion, carrier-ticket creation, credit-claim creation, maintenance-window creation, failover-test scheduling, upgrade-order creation and vendor-review creation.
6. **Request schema.** Required fields present, enumeration members recognised, integers integral, `utilization_pct` between 0 and 100 inclusive, `sequence_index` at least 1, `min_surviving_legs` between 1 and 2 inclusive, `escalation_tier_count` between 1 and 3 inclusive, a supplied `monitoring_state` restricted to `UNMONITORED` or `MONITORED`, `limit` between 1 and 100 inclusive and `offset` at least 0. Violations are `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400), and this check applies to every operation.
7. **Interval and instant coherence,** as specified in Section 2.4, reported as `ERR_TIMESTAMP_RANGE_INVALID` (422).
8. **The target record's own state.** Each of these is HTTP 409 and each guards a named set of operations: `ERR_ALARM_ALREADY_CLEARED` when clearing an alarm that already has a `cleared_at`; `ERR_RESPONSE_SEQUENCE_INVALID` when a requested response step is not exactly one step ahead of the incident's `response_state`, guarding both the response advance and the resolve operation; `ERR_INCIDENT_ALREADY_RESOLVED` when an escalation is requested on a `RESOLVED` incident; `ERR_ESCALATION_SEQUENCE_INVALID` when the requested escalation is not exactly one step above the current `escalation_state`; `ERR_TICKET_STATE_INVALID` on the four carrier-ticket operations — recording a test result, escalating a tier, posting a carrier update and confirming restoration; `ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID` on the four claim operations — submit, carrier response, settle and withdraw; `ERR_WINDOW_STATE_INVALID` on window review and window state changes; `ERR_TEST_STATE_INVALID` on starting a failover test and recording its result; `ERR_ORDER_STATE_INVALID` on upgrade-order submit, approve, state change and cancel; and `ERR_REVIEW_ALREADY_DECIDED` when a vendor review that already carries a decision is decided again.
9. **The state of related records.** All HTTP 409. `ERR_TICKET_NOT_CLOSED` when the claim's parent ticket is not `CLOSED_RESTORED`, guarding both claim creation and claim submission. `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_MITIGATED` when the incident behind the claim's ticket is neither `MITIGATED` nor `RESOLVED`, guarding claim submission. `ERR_OPEN_ALARMS_PRESENT`, guarding two operations: incident resolution requires every alarm attached to the incident to be cleared, and restoration confirmation requires that no uncleared `ROOT` alarm remains on the ticket's circuit. `ERR_CARRIER_TICKET_OPEN` when a carrier ticket on the resolving incident is in any state other than `CLOSED_RESTORED` or `CLOSED_NO_FAULT`. `ERR_CIRCUIT_SLATED_FOR_REPLACEMENT` when the circuit's `chronic_state` is `REPLACEMENT_APPROVED`, guarding both upgrade-order creation and upgrade-order submission.
10. **Stale parent versions,** as specified in Section 2.6: `ERR_STALE_CARRIER_TICKET_VERSION` on claim creation and `ERR_STALE_CIRCUIT_VERSION` on upgrade-order creation, both HTTP 412.
11. **Reference shape and coherence,** all HTTP 422. `ERR_SEGMENT_TARGET_INVALID` when a path segment does not supply exactly one of `circuit_id` and `device_id`. `ERR_ALARM_SOURCE_INVALID` when an alarm does not supply exactly one of `source_circuit_id` and `source_device_id`. `ERR_CIRCUIT_INCIDENT_MISMATCH` when a carrier ticket's `circuit_id` is not the `primary_circuit_id` of the incident it is filed against. `ERR_LEG_NOT_FOUND` when the requested `tested_leg_group` is not among the distinct `leg_group` values of the path's `ACTIVE` segments.
12. **Uniqueness and admissibility of the new record,** all HTTP 409. `ERR_CARRIER_CODE_DUPLICATE` when `carrier_code` is already held by another carrier; `ERR_CIRCUIT_IDENTIFIER_DUPLICATE` when the pairing of `carrier_id` and `carrier_circuit_identifier` already exists; `ERR_DEVICE_HOSTNAME_DUPLICATE` when `hostname` is already held; `ERR_SEGMENT_POSITION_TAKEN` when the combination of `application_path_id`, `leg_group` and `sequence_index` is already occupied by an `ACTIVE` segment; `ERR_CARRIER_REFERENCE_DUPLICATE` when the pairing of `carrier_id` and `carrier_reference` already exists; `ERR_DUPLICATE_OPEN_TICKET` when another ticket on the same `circuit_id` is in any state other than `CLOSED_RESTORED` or `CLOSED_NO_FAULT`; `ERR_CLAIM_ALREADY_OPEN` when another claim on the same `carrier_ticket_id` is in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `ACKNOWLEDGED` or `PAID`; `ERR_CIRCUIT_NOT_MONITORED` when the ticket's circuit does not have `monitoring_state` `MONITORED`; and `ERR_CIRCUIT_NOT_FAULTED` when the ticket's circuit has `operational_state` other than `DEGRADED` or `DOWN`.
13. **Operation-specific business rules.** `ERR_TIER_SEQUENCE_INVALID` (422) when `target_tier` is not exactly one above the ticket's `tier_level`; `ERR_TIER_CEILING_REACHED` (409) when `target_tier` exceeds the carrier's `escalation_tier_count`; `ERR_TEST_EVIDENCE_MISSING` (422) when the ticket carries no test result whose `outcome` is `FAIL` or `INCONCLUSIVE`; `ERR_TEST_EVIDENCE_CONTRADICTS_NO_FAULT` (409) when a no-fault carrier update is posted while `fail_test_count` is greater than zero; `ERR_EXEC_ESCALATION_NOT_ALLOWED` (422) when executive escalation is requested for an incident whose `severity` is neither `SEV1` nor `SEV2`; `ERR_REDUNDANCY_RISK_UNACKNOWLEDGED` (422) on window approval as described in Section 3.7; `ERR_PATH_NOT_PROTECTED` (409) when a failover test is started while the path's `posture` is anything other than `PROTECTED`; `ERR_MAINTENANCE_IN_EFFECT` (409) when a maintenance window in `APPROVED` or `IN_PROGRESS` covers the current instant on any circuit reachable from the path's `ACTIVE` segments; `ERR_FAILOVER_RESULT_INCONSISTENT` (422) when a recorded outcome disagrees with `surviving_leg_count`; and `ERR_UPGRADE_TARGET_INVALID` (422) when `target_bandwidth_mbps` is not a bandwidth-ladder rung strictly greater than the circuit's current `bandwidth_mbps`.
14. **Temporal exclusivity and elapsed-time rules,** all HTTP 409: `ERR_WINDOW_OVERLAP` on window approval, `ERR_FAILOVER_TEST_CONFLICT` on failover-test scheduling, `ERR_READING_OUT_OF_ORDER` on capacity-reading ingestion, `ERR_TIER_DWELL_NOT_MET` on carrier-tier escalation, `ERR_SOAK_WINDOW_NOT_ELAPSED` on restoration confirmation, `ERR_WINDOW_NOT_IN_EFFECT` on starting a window, `ERR_TEST_WINDOW_NOT_IN_EFFECT` on starting a failover test, and `ERR_ESCALATION_PREMATURE` on the escalation step from `L1` to `L2`. Each of these is specified with its exact boundary semantics in Section 3.
15. **Segregation of duties,** `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403), guarding credit-claim submission and upgrade-order approval. Note carefully that although this is a 403, it is evaluated late — after state, reference, uniqueness and temporal checks — because it compares the acting `X-User-ID` against a value stored on the target record rather than against the request's own credentials.
16. **Fiscal admissibility,** all HTTP 422 and evaluated last: `ERR_NO_SLA_BREACH` when the claim's `credit_amount_cents` is not strictly greater than zero, guarding both claim creation and claim submission; `ERR_CLAIM_BELOW_MINIMUM` when the claim's `credit_amount_cents` is below the carrier's `min_claim_amount_cents`, guarding submission; `ERR_CREDIT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH` when `paid_amount_cents` does not equal the claim's `credit_amount_cents` exactly; `ERR_REPLACEMENT_NOT_JUSTIFIED` when a replacement decision is not supported by the review's numbers; and `ERR_RETAIN_NOT_JUSTIFIED` when a retention decision is not supported by the review's score.

### 2.8. Nested Reads and Reference Resolution

Several collections are read through a parent — a carrier's circuits, a carrier's vendor reviews, a circuit's devices, a circuit's capacity readings, a circuit's upgrade orders, an application path's failover tests, a ticket's test results. In all of these the parent identifier in the URL is resolved before the child collection is assembled, so a request naming a carrier that does not exist answers `ERR_CARRIER_NOT_FOUND` rather than an empty list. When a URL carries more than one identifier, resolution proceeds left to right and the leftmost failure is the one reported. The same discipline applies to references supplied in a request body, and a body reference that resolves to nothing is a 404 rather than a 422: the client named a row that is not there.

### 2.9. Numeric Precision and Rounding

All money in this service is held in whole cents as integers; there is no fractional-cent representation. All bandwidth is held in whole Mbps as integers. Counts — leg counts, alarm counts, reopen counts, fault counts, consecutive-reading counts, impact scores — are integers.

Wherever a value must be rounded, the method is **round half up**: a value exactly halfway between two representable results rounds away from zero, so 0.5 becomes 1 and 2.345 at two decimal places becomes 2.35. Restoration durations, excluded-maintenance minutes and mean-time-to-restore figures are carried to two decimal places. Availability percentages are carried to three decimal places. A carrier's credit ratio is carried to four decimal places. A vendor review's net performance score is carried to two decimal places and floored at 0.00. Utilization percentages are compared at two decimal places. Where a calculation applies rounding more than once — per line before summation, for example — the order is stated explicitly in Section 3 and must be followed exactly, because rounding once at the end produces a different total.

### 2.10. Conserved Ledgers and Full Re-derivation of Derived Fields

Two quantities on a carrier are **conserved ledgers**, and every operation that could change them is responsible for leaving them consistent. `open_credit_exposure_cents` is the sum of `credit_amount_cents` over that carrier's credit claims whose `state` is `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `ACKNOWLEDGED`; claims in `REJECTED`, `PAID` or `WITHDRAWN` are outside the open set and contribute nothing. `committed_upgrade_mbps` is the sum of `delta_mbps` over upgrade orders on that carrier's circuits whose `state` is `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `APPROVED` or `IN_PROGRESS`; orders in `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED` are outside the committed set. The two ledgers are distinct in unit as well as in meaning — one is money, one is bandwidth — and neither is ever patched by guesswork: after any claim or order mutation the affected ledger is recomputed in full from the qualifying rows.

Derived topology fields follow the same discipline. A circuit's `spof_flag`, an application path's `posture`, `active_leg_count`, `healthy_leg_count` and `monitored_segment_count`, and a carrier's `chronic_circuit_count` and `watchlist_state` are all recomputed in full from their inputs whenever an input changes, never adjusted incrementally. This matters most for `spof_flag`: a circuit that stops being a single point of failure because a segment was retired must have the flag cleared, and the only reliable way to get that right is to re-derive it from the current graph.

Cascades are ordered, and the order is observable. When an operation changes a source's health, the affected application paths recompute their leg counts and `posture` first, then single-point-of-failure flags are re-derived from the resulting postures, and only then is incident classification evaluated — so the impact score reads post-cascade redundancy values, not the values that held before the alarm arrived. Every record touched by a cascade has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by 1, including records the client did not name in the request.

### 2.11. Segregation of Duties

Two decisions in this service may not be made by the same person who created the thing being decided. The operator who confirmed a restoration may not submit the credit claim that arises from it: on submission, `X-User-ID` is compared against the ticket's `restoration_confirmed_by_user_id`, and equality is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). The operator who created an upgrade order may not approve it: on approval, `X-User-ID` is compared against the order's `created_by_user_id`, and equality is refused with the same identifier and status. Automatically generated orders record the literal user identifier `system` as their creator, so any human approver differs from it by construction.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Building the Monitored Estate: Carriers, Circuits and Devices

Everything in this service hangs off a carrier, and a carrier is far more than a name: it is the contract. A `PlatformAdmin` registers one by supplying its legal `name` and a short operator code, `carrier_code`, which must be between 2 and 12 characters and is compared case-sensitively and exactly — `ATT` and `att` are two different codes, and reusing an existing code is refused with `ERR_CARRIER_CODE_DUPLICATE`. The remaining inputs are contractual parameters, all optional, each with a default that applies when the field is omitted. `escalation_tier_count` states how many carrier escalation tiers the contract offers and defaults to 3, with 1 as the floor and 3 as the ceiling. `sla_restore_minutes_critical` defaults to 240 minutes — four hours — and is the restoration target that applies whenever the driving incident was classified `SEV1` or `SEV2`; `sla_restore_minutes_standard` defaults to 480 minutes and applies to `SEV3` and `SEV4`. Both must be at least 1 minute.

The commercial terms follow the same pattern. `credit_pct_per_breach_hour` is the percentage of a circuit's monthly recurring charge credited for each full hour of breach; it defaults to 5.0 and must be greater than zero and no more than 25. `credit_pct_cap` is the ceiling on what a single outage can claim, defaulting to 50.0, greater than zero and no more than 100. `min_claim_amount_cents` is the contractual floor beneath which a claim may not be submitted at all and defaults to 5000 cents, with zero permitted. `tier_escalation_min_minutes` is the minimum dwell time in the current carrier tier before the next tier may be demanded and defaults to 60 minutes, with zero permitted. `watchlist_exposure_threshold_cents` is the open-exposure level at which vendor management flags the carrier and defaults to 100000 cents, at least 1. A freshly created carrier starts with `open_credit_exposure_cents` at 0, `committed_upgrade_mbps` at 0, `chronic_circuit_count` at 0 and `watchlist_state` `ACTIVE`; the alternative posture, `WATCHLIST`, is only ever reached by the derivations in Sections 3.6 and 3.8.

A circuit is registered against a carrier by naming `carrier_id`, the carrier-assigned `carrier_circuit_identifier` quoted on trouble tickets, the two endpoint site codes `site_a_code` and `site_z_code`, the provisioned `bandwidth_mbps`, and `mrc_cents`, the monthly recurring charge in cents that forms the base of every credit computation and must be at least 1. Provisioned bandwidth is not free-form: it must be one of the six rungs on the standard bandwidth ladder — 100, 200, 300, 500, 1000 or 10000 Mbps. The pairing of `carrier_id` with `carrier_circuit_identifier` must be unique, so the same identifier may legitimately be reused by a different carrier but not twice by the same one; a collision is `ERR_CIRCUIT_IDENTIFIER_DUPLICATE`. `monitoring_state` may be supplied and defaults to `UNMONITORED`; the only other value accepted on input is `MONITORED`. The third value the field can hold, `MAINTENANCE_SUPPRESSED`, is never accepted from a client and is reachable only through the maintenance cascade of Section 3.7. Every other circuit attribute is derived and starts at a known value: `operational_state` `UP`, `chronic_state` `NORMAL`, `capacity_state` `NORMAL`, `consecutive_high_readings` 0, `spof_flag` false, `fault_count_period` 0, and `last_incident_at` null until the circuit's first incident stamps it with that incident's detection instant.

Devices are the second kind of monitored object. Registration supplies a `hostname`, which must be globally unique across all devices — a duplicate is `ERR_DEVICE_HOSTNAME_DUPLICATE` — the `site_code` where the box lives, and a `device_role` of `EDGE`, `CORE` or `ACCESS`. The optional `primary_uplink_circuit_id` is the topology edge that makes alarm correlation possible: it names the uplink circuit whose root conditions explain this device's own alarms, and it defaults to null for a device with no monitored uplink. A device's `monitoring_state` defaults to `UNMONITORED` and accepts only `UNMONITORED` or `MONITORED`, and its `operational_state` is derived, starting at `UP`.

Reading the estate back is deliberately filterable. A carrier's circuits may be narrowed by `monitoring_state`, `operational_state`, `chronic_state` and `capacity_state`, so a shift lead can ask for every monitored circuit currently `DOWN`, or every circuit whose `capacity_state` is `UPGRADE_REQUIRED`. A carrier's vendor reviews may be narrowed by `decision`. The devices hanging off a circuit — those whose uplink is that circuit — may be narrowed by `device_role`.

### 3.2. Application Paths, Legs and the Derivation of Redundancy Posture

An application path is the business-visible object: "payroll to the data centre", not "circuit 12". Creating one supplies its `name`, a `service_tier` of `GOLD`, `SILVER` or `BRONZE` that drives impact weighting, a `protection_scheme` of `ACTIVE_ACTIVE`, `ACTIVE_STANDBY` or `UNPROTECTED` documenting the redundancy design, and `min_surviving_legs`, the number of healthy legs the path needs to be considered protected, which defaults to 1 and may be 1 or 2. A new path begins with `posture` `PROTECTED` and `active_leg_count`, `healthy_leg_count` and `monitored_segment_count` all at 0 — a path with no topology yet is vacuously protected.

Topology is added one hop at a time. Each hop names its `application_path_id`, the `leg_group` it belongs to (`LEG_A` or `LEG_B`), its `sequence_index` within that leg, which must be at least 1, and exactly one target: either `circuit_id` or `device_id`. Supplying both, or neither, is refused with `ERR_SEGMENT_TARGET_INVALID`. The triple of path, leg and sequence position must be unoccupied among `ACTIVE` hops, and a collision is `ERR_SEGMENT_POSITION_TAKEN`. Every hop is created with `segment_state` `ACTIVE`. Retiring a hop does not delete the row — historical alarms must stay explicable — it flips `segment_state` to `RETIRED`, which removes the hop from every count and from every derivation from that moment on. Both adding and retiring a hop re-derive the parent path's leg counts and `posture` and re-derive `spof_flag` on every circuit reachable from the path's remaining `ACTIVE` hops; adding a hop advances the path's `version` by 1, and retiring one advances both the hop's `version` and the path's `version` by 1 each. Hops may be listed and narrowed by `application_path_id`, `circuit_id`, `device_id`, `leg_group` and `segment_state`.

Posture derivation is the heart of the redundancy model, and it considers only `ACTIVE` hops. `active_leg_count` is the number of distinct `leg_group` values among them. A hop's target counts as *up* under two different rules depending on what it points at: a circuit target counts as up unless its `operational_state` is `DOWN`, which means a `DEGRADED` circuit still counts as up; a device target counts as up only when its `operational_state` is exactly `UP`. `healthy_leg_count` is the number of distinct `ACTIVE` legs in which every single `ACTIVE` hop's target counts as up — one down target anywhere in a leg makes the whole leg unhealthy. `monitored_segment_count` is the number of `ACTIVE` hops whose target has `monitoring_state` `MONITORED`, counted per hop rather than per leg.

`posture` is then chosen by walking six conditions strictly from the top and stopping at the first that holds. If `active_leg_count` is zero, the posture is `PROTECTED`. Otherwise, if `healthy_leg_count` is zero, the posture is `DOWN`. Otherwise, if `healthy_leg_count` is less than the path's `min_surviving_legs`, the posture is again `DOWN`. Otherwise, if `healthy_leg_count` equals `active_leg_count` — nothing is broken — the posture is `PROTECTED`. Otherwise, if `healthy_leg_count` is strictly greater than `min_surviving_legs`, the posture is `DEGRADED_REDUNDANT`: something is broken but spare capacity remains. Otherwise the posture is `DEGRADED_NO_REDUNDANCY`, the dangerous case where the path is still carrying traffic on exactly the minimum it needs.

`DEGRADED_NO_REDUNDANCY` is what makes a circuit a single point of failure. A circuit's `spof_flag` is true when, and only when, there exists at least one path whose `posture` is `DEGRADED_NO_REDUNDANCY` that has an `ACTIVE` hop pointing at that circuit inside a leg all of whose `ACTIVE` hop targets currently count as up. In plain terms: the circuit is carrying a surviving leg of a path that has no spare. The flag is recomputed in full from the current graph every time postures are re-derived, so it clears itself as soon as the condition stops holding.

Alongside the live posture, the service answers a historical question: how available was this path over an arbitrary window? The availability report takes `from_iso8601` and `to_iso8601`, treating the period as including its start instant and excluding its end instant, together with a `basis` selecting one of two accounting variants. Downtime evidence is narrow by design: only alarms that are root conditions count, meaning an alarm whose `correlation_state` is `ROOT` or `CLEARED` *and* whose `correlation_parent_alarm_id` is null, and only those whose `raw_severity` is `CRITICAL`. Suppressed alarms and non-critical alarms never contribute downtime. Each qualifying alarm on a hop's target yields one interval that begins at the later of the alarm's `raised_at` and the period start, and ends at the earlier of the period end and either the alarm's `cleared_at` or, for an alarm still open, the current logical clock instant; intervals that come out empty are discarded. All intervals belonging to hops in the same leg are merged into a disjoint union, and the total length of that union is the leg's entry in `leg_down_minutes`, which is reported per leg.

The two variants differ only in how leg downtime becomes path downtime, and they can differ sharply on the same data. Under `INTERSECTION`, `path_down_minutes` is the total length of the intersection of every leg's merged union — the path is down only over instants when every leg was simultaneously down — and `availability_pct` is 100 minus the ratio of `path_down_minutes` to `period_minutes` expressed as a percentage, rounded half up to three decimal places. Under `BEST_LEG`, `path_down_minutes` is instead the smallest `leg_down_minutes` across the legs: each leg is scored independently and the healthiest leg's total is attributed to the path regardless of whether the outages overlapped in time, which for non-overlapping leg outages yields a strictly larger `path_down_minutes` than `INTERSECTION` on identical data. `availability_pct` is computed the same way in both variants. The report echoes the `basis` used and reports `period_minutes`, the length of the requested window in minutes; minute quantities are carried to two decimal places and the percentage to three.

### 3.3. Alarm Ingestion, Correlation and the Suppression of Noise

Alarms are the raw input. Ingesting one supplies the `alarm_type` that fired — `LOSS_OF_SIGNAL`, `ERRORED_SECONDS`, `PATH_LATENCY` or `DEVICE_UNREACHABLE` — the `raw_severity` the monitoring platform assigned before any correlation (`CRITICAL`, `MAJOR`, `MINOR` or `WARNING`), the `raised_at` instant at which the condition was observed, and exactly one source: either `source_circuit_id` or `source_device_id`. Supplying both or neither is `ERR_ALARM_SOURCE_INVALID`. An alarm arrives with `cleared_at` null, and the two derived correlation fields are decided immediately.

Correlation asks a single question: is there already an open root condition that explains this alarm? For a device-sourced alarm the search looks at the device's `primary_uplink_circuit_id`; when that uplink is null nothing can possibly explain the alarm and the candidate set is empty, and otherwise the candidates are alarms on that uplink circuit. For a circuit-sourced alarm the candidates are alarms on the same source circuit, which is how duplicate flapping on one circuit is collapsed. In both cases a candidate qualifies only if its `correlation_state` is `ROOT`, its `cleared_at` is still null, its `raised_at` is at or before the new alarm's `raised_at`, and the gap between the two `raised_at` instants is no more than 900 seconds — fifteen minutes — with the boundary inclusive, so a gap of exactly fifteen minutes still suppresses. Comparisons are made at whole-second resolution, and cleared or already-suppressed alarms are never eligible as parents.

If the candidate set is empty, the new alarm becomes a root condition: `correlation_state` `ROOT` and `correlation_parent_alarm_id` null. If it is not empty, the candidates are ranked by `raised_at` descending — the most recent qualifying root wins — with ties broken by `created_at` ascending, never by identifier. The winner becomes the parent: the new alarm takes `correlation_state` `SUPPRESSED`, records the parent's identifier in `correlation_parent_alarm_id`, and inherits the parent's `incident_id` so that noise volume is still filed against the right incident. Suppressed noise never opens an incident of its own; it increments the incident's `alarm_count` by 1 and can only ratchet severity upward, never re-classify it downward. One special case overrides all of this: an alarm raised on a source whose `monitoring_state` is `MAINTENANCE_SUPPRESSED` is stored with `correlation_state` `SUPPRESSED` and creates no incident at all.

A root condition, by contrast, drives health. A circuit's `operational_state` is derived from the set of uncleared root alarms on it: the circuit is `DOWN` while any of them has `raw_severity` `CRITICAL`, `DEGRADED` while any of them has `raw_severity` `MAJOR` or `MINOR` and none is `CRITICAL`, and `UP` when none remains. Alarms of `WARNING` severity never change `operational_state` at all. So a first critical root alarm moves the circuit `UP → DOWN`; a first major or minor root alarm moves it `UP → DEGRADED`; a critical root alarm arriving at an already-degraded circuit moves it `DEGRADED → DOWN`. A device is simpler because it exposes only `UP` and `DOWN`: it drops to `DOWN` while it carries at least one uncleared root alarm whose `raw_severity` is `CRITICAL` or whose `alarm_type` is `DEVICE_UNREACHABLE`, and no lesser root alarm can degrade it because no degraded device state exists.

Every health change fans out in a fixed order. First, every application path with an `ACTIVE` hop pointing at the changed source recomputes `active_leg_count`, `healthy_leg_count`, `monitored_segment_count` and `posture`. Second, `spof_flag` is set true on every circuit that is an `ACTIVE` hop target inside a healthy leg of a path whose posture has just become `DEGRADED_NO_REDUNDANCY`. Third and last, an incident is opened on the anchor circuit or the alarm is attached to the incident already open there, with the classification of Section 3.4 evaluated **after** the first two steps so that its redundancy and single-point-of-failure terms read post-cascade values. Because a degraded circuit still counts as up for leg health, a `UP → DEGRADED` transition leaves path postures `PROTECTED` unless some other target in the same leg is already down. Opening a new incident stamps the circuit's `last_incident_at`, and each circuit, path and incident touched by the cascade has its `version` advanced by 1.

Clearing is the mirror image, and it is where the correlation tree gets interesting. Clearing supplies `cleared_at`, which must fall at or after the alarm's `raised_at` and at or before the current clock instant; an alarm that already carries a `cleared_at` cannot be cleared again and yields `ERR_ALARM_ALREADY_CLEARED`. The alarm's `correlation_state` becomes `CLEARED`. When the cleared alarm was suppressed noise, that is the entire effect — no circuit, device, path or incident state changes, because the alarm was never driving anything. When the cleared alarm was a root condition, four things follow. Its still-open suppressed children are ordered by `raised_at` ascending with ties broken by `created_at` ascending, and the earliest of them — considering only children whose `cleared_at` is still null — is promoted to `correlation_state` `ROOT` with its `correlation_parent_alarm_id` set to null; the remaining open children are re-parented to the newly promoted alarm. Because a root condition now exists on the promoted alarm's own source, that source's `operational_state` is recomputed, which can take a device from `UP` to `DOWN` or a circuit from `UP` to `DEGRADED` or `DOWN`. Because the source's health changed, every path with an `ACTIVE` hop on that source recomputes its leg counts and `posture`, and `spof_flag` is re-derived on the surviving legs' circuits. Finally the promoted alarm attaches to the incident open on the anchor circuit — advancing `alarm_count` and ratcheting severity — or opens a new incident when none is open there.

When the cleared root alarm has no open suppressed child to promote, the source's `operational_state` is instead recomputed from whatever uncleared root alarms remain on it, becoming `UP` when none remain, and the affected path postures and `spof_flag` values are re-derived from that. This is the path by which a circuit travels `DOWN → UP` or `DEGRADED → UP`: the precondition is simply that no uncleared root alarm remains on the circuit, and the effect is a full re-derivation of affected postures plus the clearing of `spof_flag` on circuits that are no longer sole surviving legs. Every circuit and path touched has its `version` advanced by 1; alarms themselves, being append-only records, carry no `version` to advance.

Alarms are searchable by `source_circuit_id`, `source_device_id`, `correlation_parent_alarm_id`, `incident_id`, `correlation_state` and `alarm_type`, which is how an analyst pulls up the whole suppressed family under a single root condition.

### 3.4. Incidents: Classification, the Response Clock and Escalation

An incident is opened by the system, never by a client. It is anchored to a circuit — recorded as `primary_circuit_id`, which is the root alarm's own circuit for a circuit-sourced alarm and the alarming device's `primary_uplink_circuit_id` for a device-sourced one — and it remembers the alarm that opened it in `root_alarm_id`. Opening stamps `detected_at` from the logical clock, sets `response_state` to `DETECTED` and `escalation_state` to `NONE`, starts `alarm_count` at 1 and `reopen_count` at 0, and leaves `triaged_at`, `mitigated_at` and `resolved_at` null. The two lifecycle dimensions are genuinely parallel: `response_state` advances through triage, mitigation and resolution while `escalation_state` moves independently through management levels, and each advances on the same record without disturbing the other.

Classification is a weighted score over the paths the anchor circuit actually carries. A path counts as **affected** when it has an `ACTIVE` hop whose `circuit_id` is the incident's `primary_circuit_id`; a hop that points at a device never makes a path affected, even if that device sits on the circuit. The number of affected paths is recorded as `affected_path_count`. Business criticality is weighted 4 for `GOLD`, 2 for `SILVER` and 1 for `BRONZE`. From these, four terms are formed. The tier term is the greatest weight among the affected paths, or zero when there are none. The breadth term is the number of affected paths capped at five and then doubled, so six affected paths score the same breadth as five. The redundancy term looks at the smallest `healthy_leg_count` among the affected paths — treated as 99 when there are no affected paths at all — and contributes 8 when that smallest value is zero, 4 when it is exactly 1, and nothing otherwise. The single-point-of-failure term contributes 3 when the anchor circuit's `spof_flag` is true and nothing when it is false. `impact_score` is the tier term multiplied by ten, plus the breadth term, plus the redundancy term, plus the single-point-of-failure term, and it is an integer.

`severity` follows from `impact_score` by four bands with inclusive lower bounds: 50 or more is `SEV1`, 30 or more is `SEV2`, 12 or more is `SEV3`, and anything below 12 is `SEV4`. When an alarm attaches to an incident that is already open, severity **ratchets**: `SEV1` is the strongest classification and `SEV4` the weakest, and the stored value becomes the stronger of the stored and the freshly computed one, so an incident never quietly downgrades itself while it is still running. Classification also sets two deadlines from `detected_at`: `response_due_at` is `detected_at` plus 15 minutes for `SEV1`, 30 minutes for `SEV2`, 120 minutes for `SEV3` and 480 minutes for `SEV4`, while `escalation_due_at` is `detected_at` plus 30 minutes for `SEV1`, 60 minutes for `SEV2`, 240 minutes for `SEV3` and 960 minutes for `SEV4`. Both are ISO 8601 UTC instants with three fractional digits. Remember from Section 3.3 that classification runs only after posture and single-point-of-failure derivation, so the redundancy and single-point-of-failure terms describe the network as it is *after* the alarm landed.

Advancing the response dimension names the `target_response_state` and may carry a free-text `note`. Only single steps forward are legal: `DETECTED → TRIAGED` stamps `triaged_at` with the current clock instant, and `TRIAGED → MITIGATED` stamps `mitigated_at`. Anything that is not exactly one step ahead of the incident's current `response_state` is refused with `ERR_RESPONSE_SEQUENCE_INVALID`. Reaching `MITIGATED` also relaxes management attention by exactly one step, without touching `response_state`: an incident at `EXEC` moves to `L2` and one at `L2` moves to `L1`, while `NONE` and `L1` are left where they are. The incident's `version` advances by 1.

Escalation is the other dimension and is a `NocShiftLead` prerogative. The request names a `target_escalation_state` and must climb exactly one rung of `NONE → L1 → L2 → EXEC`; anything else is `ERR_ESCALATION_SEQUENCE_INVALID`, and any escalation attempt on an incident whose `response_state` is already `RESOLVED` is `ERR_INCIDENT_ALREADY_RESOLVED`. Two rungs carry extra conditions. Climbing `L1 → L2` requires that the current clock instant has reached or passed the incident's `escalation_due_at`; escalating earlier is `ERR_ESCALATION_PREMATURE`, which exists so that management levels track the response matrix rather than the loudest voice on the bridge. Climbing `L2 → EXEC` requires the incident's `severity` to be `SEV1` or `SEV2`; an executive page for a `SEV3` or `SEV4` is refused with `ERR_EXEC_ESCALATION_NOT_ALLOWED`. Escalation leaves `response_state` untouched and advances `version` by 1.

Resolution is the final response step and is guarded three ways. The incident must be in `MITIGATED`, or the request is `ERR_RESPONSE_SEQUENCE_INVALID`. Every alarm attached to the incident must carry a `cleared_at`, or the request is `ERR_OPEN_ALARMS_PRESENT` — an incident cannot be declared over while the monitoring platform still says otherwise. No carrier ticket raised on the incident may be in any state other than `CLOSED_RESTORED` or `CLOSED_NO_FAULT`, or the request is `ERR_CARRIER_TICKET_OPEN`, because closing internally while the carrier is still working the fault destroys the evidence trail. The request may carry a `resolution_note`. On success `resolved_at` is stamped with the current instant, `escalation_state` is forced back to `NONE` regardless of where it stood, and the anchor circuit's chronic posture is re-evaluated; the incident's `version`, the circuit's `version` and the carrier's `version` each advance by 1.

That chronic re-evaluation is how repeat offenders surface. A cutoff instant is computed as the current clock instant minus 90 days, and the boundary is inclusive. `fault_count_period` becomes the number of incidents anchored on that circuit whose `response_state` is `RESOLVED` and whose `resolved_at` is at or after the cutoff — a reopened incident, having had its `resolved_at` reset to null, drops out of the count entirely. From that count the circuit's `chronic_state` is chosen: three or more faults makes it `CHRONIC`, two makes it `CHRONIC_CANDIDATE`, fewer than two returns it to `NORMAL`. Because the window slides, the movement runs in both directions: `NORMAL → CHRONIC_CANDIDATE` when the count reaches 2 while remaining below 3, `CHRONIC_CANDIDATE → CHRONIC` when it reaches 3, `CHRONIC_CANDIDATE → NORMAL` when older incidents age past the ninety-day boundary and the count falls below 2, and `CHRONIC → CHRONIC_CANDIDATE` when the count falls below 3 while remaining at least 2. Entering `CHRONIC` raises the carrier's `chronic_circuit_count` by 1 and leaving it lowers that count by 1; more precisely, `chronic_circuit_count` is the number of that carrier's circuits whose `chronic_state` is `CHRONIC` or `REPLACEMENT_APPROVED`. The fourth chronic value, `REPLACEMENT_APPROVED`, is sticky: once a circuit has been slated for replacement this re-evaluation never moves it again.

Incidents can come back. When a new root alarm lands on an incident's `primary_circuit_id` within 120 minutes of that incident's `resolved_at`, the incident is reopened rather than a second incident being created: `response_state` returns `RESOLVED → TRIAGED`, `resolved_at` is set back to null, `reopen_count` advances by 1, the new alarm is attached with `alarm_count` advancing and severity ratcheting, and `version` advances by 1. A root alarm arriving more than 120 minutes after resolution opens a brand-new incident instead. Incidents may be listed and narrowed by `primary_circuit_id`, `root_alarm_id`, `severity`, `response_state` and `escalation_state`.

### 3.5. Carrier Trouble Tickets: Evidence, Tier Escalation and Restoration

Once the NOC believes the fault belongs to the carrier, it opens a trouble ticket. The request names the `incident_id` it belongs to, the `circuit_id` being reported and the carrier's own `carrier_reference` for the case. Five conditions must hold, and they are evaluated in the order of Section 2.7. The reported `circuit_id` must be exactly the incident's `primary_circuit_id`, or the request is `ERR_CIRCUIT_INCIDENT_MISMATCH`. The pairing of carrier and `carrier_reference` must be unique, or it is `ERR_CARRIER_REFERENCE_DUPLICATE`. No other ticket on the same circuit may be in a state other than `CLOSED_RESTORED` or `CLOSED_NO_FAULT`, or it is `ERR_DUPLICATE_OPEN_TICKET`. The circuit's `monitoring_state` must be `MONITORED`, since an unmonitored or maintenance-suppressed circuit carries no trustworthy fault evidence, or it is `ERR_CIRCUIT_NOT_MONITORED`. And the circuit's `operational_state` must be `DEGRADED` or `DOWN`, or it is `ERR_CIRCUIT_NOT_FAULTED` — the NOC does not open carrier cases on healthy circuits.

A new ticket is created in `OPEN` with `tier_level` 1, and both `tier_entered_at` and `opened_at` set to the current clock instant. Its `carrier_id` is copied from the circuit rather than supplied, and `reported_circuit_identifier` snapshots the circuit's `carrier_circuit_identifier` at open time so that a later inventory rename cannot rewrite what was quoted to the carrier. `opened_by_user_id` records the acting `X-User-ID`. Everything downstream starts empty: `restored_at`, `confirmed_at`, `restoration_confirmed_by_user_id`, `restoration_minutes_carrier_clock` and `restoration_minutes_noc_clock` are null, `excluded_minutes` is 0, and `reopen_count` and `fail_test_count` are 0. The parent incident's `version` advances by 1. Tickets may be listed and narrowed by `incident_id`, `circuit_id`, `carrier_id`, `state` and `tier_level`.

Test evidence is what makes carrier escalation possible. Recording a result supplies the `test_type` performed — `BERT`, `LOOPBACK`, `OTDR` or `PING_SWEEP` — the `outcome` (`PASS`, `FAIL` or `INCONCLUSIVE`), and optional free-text `notes` that default to null. The server stamps `performed_at` from the clock and `recorded_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID`; the record is append-only, carrying only its identifier and creation timestamp, and is never modified afterwards. Results may be recorded while the ticket is in `OPEN`, `REOPENED`, `TESTING`, `ESCALATED_T2` or `ESCALATED_T3`; any other state is `ERR_TICKET_STATE_INVALID`. The state effect is asymmetric on purpose: from `OPEN` or from `REOPENED` the ticket moves to `TESTING`, whereas a ticket already in `ESCALATED_T2` or `ESCALATED_T3` keeps the state it has, so recording more evidence never walks a tier back. Whenever the `outcome` is `FAIL` or `INCONCLUSIVE`, `fail_test_count` advances by 1; a `PASS` leaves it alone. The ticket's `version` advances by 1. Test evidence may be filtered by `outcome` and `test_type`.

Escalating the carrier tier names the `target_tier` and may carry a `justification`. Five preconditions apply, evaluated in the priority order of Section 2.7: the ticket must be in `TESTING` or `ESCALATED_T2`, otherwise `ERR_TICKET_STATE_INVALID`; `target_tier` must be exactly one greater than the ticket's current `tier_level`, otherwise `ERR_TIER_SEQUENCE_INVALID`; `target_tier` must not exceed the carrier's `escalation_tier_count`, otherwise `ERR_TIER_CEILING_REACHED`; `fail_test_count` must be at least 1, so at least one failing or inconclusive test exists as evidence, otherwise `ERR_TEST_EVIDENCE_MISSING`; and the elapsed time from `tier_entered_at` to the current clock instant must have reached the carrier's `tier_escalation_min_minutes`, otherwise `ERR_TIER_DWELL_NOT_MET`. Success sets `tier_level` to `target_tier`, moves the ticket `TESTING → ESCALATED_T2` or `ESCALATED_T2 → ESCALATED_T3` accordingly, resets `tier_entered_at` to the current instant so the dwell clock restarts for the new tier, and advances `version` by 1.

The carrier answers through its liaison, and there are exactly two answers. The `update` field carries either `RESTORED` or `NO_FAULT_FOUND`. A `RESTORED` update additionally requires `restored_at`, the instant the carrier asserts service came back, which must fall strictly after `opened_at` and at or before the current clock instant. It is accepted while the ticket is in `OPEN`, `TESTING`, `ESCALATED_T2`, `ESCALATED_T3` or `REOPENED` — anything else is `ERR_TICKET_STATE_INVALID` — and only from the carrier that owns the ticket. It moves the ticket to `RESTORED_PENDING_CONFIRM` from whichever of those states it held, computes the three duration fields described next, retains `tier_level` so the eventual credit claim records which tier restored the circuit, and advances `version` by 1. A `NO_FAULT_FOUND` update is accepted only while the ticket is in `OPEN` or `TESTING`, and only while `fail_test_count` is exactly zero: a carrier cannot claim there is no fault when the NOC holds failing test evidence, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_TEST_EVIDENCE_CONTRADICTS_NO_FAULT`. It closes the ticket as `CLOSED_NO_FAULT`, which is terminal; no credit claim is ever synthesised from a no-fault closure, and the incident and the circuit's `operational_state` are left exactly as they were, on the reasoning that the fault, if real, is internal.

Restoration duration is measured on **two independent clocks**, and both are computed at the instant the carrier reports restoration and then snapshotted — they are never retro-adjusted afterwards. The NOC clock is wall-to-wall from customer-visible detection: `restoration_minutes_noc_clock` is the elapsed minutes from the driving incident's `detected_at` to `restored_at`, with no exclusions at all. The carrier clock is the carrier-facing duration less contractually excluded carrier maintenance. It starts from the raw elapsed minutes between the ticket's `opened_at` and `restored_at`, then subtracts `excluded_minutes`, computed in three fixed steps. Step one gathers every maintenance window on the ticket's circuit whose `origin` is `CARRIER_NOTIFIED` and whose `state` is `APPROVED`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `COMPLETED` — proposed, rejected and cancelled windows and every `INTERNAL_CHANGE` window are excluded — and merges their intervals into a disjoint union, each interval including its `window_start` and excluding its `window_end`, processed in ascending `window_start` order with ties broken by ascending `created_at`, and intervals that merely touch end-to-end merged into one. Step two intersects that union with the interval that includes `opened_at` and excludes `restored_at`. Step three sums the lengths of those intersections, and that sum is `excluded_minutes`. `restoration_minutes_carrier_clock` is the raw elapsed minutes minus `excluded_minutes`. Both minute figures are rounded half up to two decimal places. Because the carrier clock starts later and subtracts maintenance, it is never larger than the NOC clock for the same ticket, which is exactly why the choice of clock is a commercial decision in Section 3.6.

Restoration is not believed until it has soaked. Confirmation carries a single boolean, `soak_passed`, and may only be applied to a ticket in `RESTORED_PENDING_CONFIRM`; any other state is `ERR_TICKET_STATE_INVALID`. When `soak_passed` is true, two further conditions guard the closure: at least 30 minutes must have elapsed from `restored_at` to the current clock instant, or the request is refused with `ERR_SOAK_WINDOW_NOT_ELAPSED`, and no uncleared root alarm may remain on the ticket's circuit, or the request is refused with `ERR_OPEN_ALARMS_PRESENT`. Passing both moves the ticket to `CLOSED_RESTORED`, stamps `confirmed_at` with the current instant and `restoration_confirmed_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`, and triggers the automatic credit draft of Section 3.6. This operation reads the `restoration_minutes_carrier_clock`, `restoration_minutes_noc_clock` and `excluded_minutes` snapshot that the carrier update already stamped; it never recomputes them. When `soak_passed` is false the fault has returned: the ticket moves to `REOPENED`, `reopen_count` advances by 1, and `restored_at` is cleared back to null along with `excluded_minutes` back to zero and both restoration-minute fields back to null, because the measurement they held is now meaningless. No claim is synthesised on that branch. A reopened ticket re-enters the cycle at `REOPENED → TESTING` as soon as fresh test evidence is recorded, and the whole loop may run repeatedly. Either branch advances the ticket's `version` by 1, and the branch that drafts a claim also advances the carrier's `version` by 1.

### 3.6. Service-Level Credit Claims and the Carrier's Open Exposure

Confirming a restoration is also a commercial event. At the moment a ticket closes as `CLOSED_RESTORED`, the service computes what the outage is worth on the carrier clock and, when that amount comes out strictly greater than zero, synthesises a claim automatically: `origin` `AUTO_DRAFT`, `state` `DRAFT`, `basis` `CARRIER_CLOCK`. When the computed amount is exactly zero — the carrier beat its target — no claim row is created at all. A drafted claim immediately enters the carrier's open exposure, raising `open_credit_exposure_cents` by its `credit_amount_cents`, and if that raises exposure to or above the carrier's `watchlist_exposure_threshold_cents`, `watchlist_state` flips `ACTIVE → WATCHLIST`.

A `VendorManager` may also file a claim by hand, which is how a ticket gets a second bite after an automatic draft was withdrawn. The request names the `carrier_ticket_id`, carries `expected_carrier_ticket_version` as described in Section 2.6, and may specify `basis` — either `CARRIER_CLOCK` or `NOC_CLOCK` — defaulting to `CARRIER_CLOCK`. Such a claim is created with `origin` `MANUAL` in `DRAFT`, with `carrier_id` copied from the ticket, `submitted_by_user_id` and `submitted_at` null, `paid_amount_cents` and `rejection_reason` null, and `revision_count` 0. The parent ticket must be in `CLOSED_RESTORED`, otherwise `ERR_TICKET_NOT_CLOSED`; no other claim on the same ticket may sit in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `ACKNOWLEDGED` or `PAID`, otherwise `ERR_CLAIM_ALREADY_OPEN`; and the computed `credit_amount_cents` must be strictly greater than zero, otherwise `ERR_NO_SLA_BREACH`. Creation raises the carrier's exposure and re-evaluates `watchlist_state` exactly as the automatic draft does, and advances the ticket's `version` and the carrier's `version` by 1 each.

The credit computation is the same in both cases and runs in a pinned order. First the applicable target is selected: `sla_target_minutes` is the carrier's `sla_restore_minutes_critical` when the driving incident's `severity` is `SEV1` or `SEV2`, and its `sla_restore_minutes_standard` otherwise. Then the restoration figure is taken from the ticket according to the claim's `basis` — `restoration_minutes_carrier_clock` for `CARRIER_CLOCK`, `restoration_minutes_noc_clock` for `NOC_CLOCK`. `breach_minutes` is the amount by which the restoration figure exceeds the target, and zero when it does not exceed it. `breach_hours` is zero when `breach_minutes` is zero, and otherwise `breach_minutes` divided by 60 and rounded **up** to the next whole hour, so a breach of 61 minutes is two breach hours. The credit percentage is the carrier's `credit_pct_per_breach_hour` multiplied by `breach_hours`, held down to the carrier's `credit_pct_cap` when the product exceeds it.

That percentage is then applied per affected path. The lines are the paths reachable through an `ACTIVE` hop on the ticket's circuit, using the same definition of *affected* as incident classification, and retired hops contribute no line. Each line's share is its own service-tier weight — 4 for `GOLD`, 2 for `SILVER`, 1 for `BRONZE` — divided by the total weight of all lines. **The rounding order is pinned:** each line's amount is the circuit's `mrc_cents` multiplied by that line's weight share, multiplied by the credit percentage, divided by 100, and rounded half up to whole cents *for that line*; only then are the rounded line amounts added together. Summing unrounded shares and rounding once at the end is explicitly not the rule and produces a different total. When there are no lines at all — total weight zero — the amount is instead the circuit's `mrc_cents` multiplied by the credit percentage, divided by 100, rounded half up to whole cents. A separate ceiling is computed as the circuit's `mrc_cents` multiplied by the carrier's `credit_pct_cap`, divided by 100 and rounded half up, and `credit_amount_cents` is the smaller of the line total and that ceiling.

Submission is where the commercial controls bite, and its six checks run in the order Section 2.7 fixes. The claim must be in `DRAFT` or `REJECTED`, otherwise `ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID`. Its parent ticket must be in `CLOSED_RESTORED`, otherwise `ERR_TICKET_NOT_CLOSED`. The incident behind that ticket must have `response_state` `MITIGATED` or `RESOLVED`, otherwise `ERR_INCIDENT_NOT_MITIGATED`. The acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the ticket's `restoration_confirmed_by_user_id`, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The `credit_amount_cents` must be strictly greater than zero, otherwise `ERR_NO_SLA_BREACH`, and must be at least the carrier's `min_claim_amount_cents`, otherwise `ERR_CLAIM_BELOW_MINIMUM`. The request may carry a `basis` and an `attestation_note`; when a `basis` is supplied it replaces the stored `basis` and every derived amount — `breach_minutes`, `breach_hours` and `credit_amount_cents` — is recomputed under the new variant **before** the two fiscal checks run, and the carrier's exposure is adjusted by the difference. Because the carrier clock is never larger than the NOC clock, switching a claim from `CARRIER_CLOCK` to `NOC_CLOCK` can only raise or hold the amount, and switching the other way can push a claim below the minimum. Success stamps `submitted_by_user_id` and `submitted_at` and advances the claim's `version` and the carrier's `version` by 1 each. The two entry paths differ in one respect: a `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` claim was already inside the open-exposure set, whereas a `REJECTED → SUBMITTED` resubmission advances `revision_count` by 1, clears `rejection_reason` back to null, and re-adds its `credit_amount_cents` to the carrier's exposure, because a rejected claim had left the open set.

The carrier answers a submitted claim through its liaison, and the `response` field is either `ACKNOWLEDGED` or `REJECTED`. Either answer requires the claim to be in `SUBMITTED`, otherwise `ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID`, and requires the liaison to own the claim's carrier. `ACKNOWLEDGED` moves the claim `SUBMITTED → ACKNOWLEDGED` and leaves the exposure ledger untouched, because an acknowledged claim is still open money. `REJECTED` requires a non-empty `rejection_reason`, without which the request is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; it records that reason, moves the claim `SUBMITTED → REJECTED`, lowers the carrier's `open_credit_exposure_cents` by the claim's `credit_amount_cents` and re-evaluates `watchlist_state`. Both answers advance the claim's `version` and the carrier's `version` by 1 each.

Settlement closes the money. It applies only to a claim in `ACKNOWLEDGED`, otherwise `ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID`, and the supplied `paid_amount_cents` must equal the claim's `credit_amount_cents` exactly — partial settlement is not modelled, and a mismatch in either direction is `ERR_CREDIT_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`. Success records `paid_amount_cents`, moves the claim `ACKNOWLEDGED → PAID`, and lowers the carrier's exposure by the claim amount because `PAID` sits outside the open set; both `version` values advance by 1.

Withdrawal is the compensating action and is available from `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `REJECTED`; any other state is `ERR_CLAIM_STATE_INVALID`. Withdrawing from `DRAFT` or from `SUBMITTED` subtracts from `open_credit_exposure_cents` exactly the amount that was added when the claim entered the open set, restoring the ledger — and, when exposure falls back below the threshold, flipping `watchlist_state` `WATCHLIST → ACTIVE`. Withdrawing from `REJECTED` changes no exposure at all, because a rejected claim had already left the open set; the difference between the branches is precisely that. All three branches advance the claim's `version` and the carrier's `version` by 1 each. `WITHDRAWN` and `PAID` are the two terminal claim states. The invariant to preserve across all of this is the one stated in Section 2.10: `open_credit_exposure_cents` always equals the sum of `credit_amount_cents` over that carrier's claims in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `ACKNOWLEDGED`, and `watchlist_state` is `WATCHLIST` exactly when that sum has reached or passed `watchlist_exposure_threshold_cents`. Claims may be listed and narrowed by `carrier_ticket_id`, `carrier_id`, `state`, `basis` and `origin`.

### 3.7. Change Coordination: Maintenance Windows and Verified Failover

Planned work is announced as a maintenance window. Creation names the `circuit_id`, an `origin` of `CARRIER_NOTIFIED` for carrier-announced work or `INTERNAL_CHANGE` for NOC-driven change, the interval bounds `window_start` and `window_end` — the start is included, the end excluded, and the end must be strictly after the start — and optionally `suppresses_monitoring`, which defaults to true. A window is created in `PROPOSED` with `carrier_id` copied from the circuit, `risk_acknowledged` false, `approved_by_user_id` null and `monitoring_state_before_start` null. A `CarrierLiaison` may only create windows with `CARRIER_NOTIFIED` origin on its own carrier's circuits. Windows may be listed and narrowed by `circuit_id`, `carrier_id`, `state` and `origin`.

Review is where the risk is priced. The request carries a `decision` of `APPROVED` or `REJECTED` and may carry `risk_acknowledged`. The window must be in `PROPOSED`, otherwise `ERR_WINDOW_STATE_INVALID`. Then the redundancy check runs: for every application path that has an `ACTIVE` hop referencing the window's circuit, the reviewer's projected leg health is computed as the number of legs in which every `ACTIVE` hop's target currently counts as up *and* no `ACTIVE` hop references the window's circuit — in other words, how many legs would still be healthy with this circuit taken out. If for any such path that projected count is at or below the path's `min_surviving_legs`, then `risk_acknowledged` must be true in the request body, and an approval without it is refused with `ERR_REDUNDANCY_RISK_UNACKNOWLEDGED`. Finally the exclusivity check runs against other windows on the same circuit whose `state` is `APPROVED` or `IN_PROGRESS`, using a buffer of 30 minutes on both sides: approval is refused when the candidate window starts before an existing window's `window_end` plus 30 minutes *and* that existing window starts before the candidate's `window_end` plus 30 minutes. Two consequences follow from that arithmetic and both are intended: windows separated by exactly 30 minutes of quiet do **not** conflict, while windows that merely touch — one starting at the instant the other ends — **do** conflict. A refusal is `ERR_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. Approving moves the window `PROPOSED → APPROVED`, stamps `approved_by_user_id` and persists the supplied `risk_acknowledged`; rejecting moves it `PROPOSED → REJECTED`, which is terminal, has no effect on any schedule, and still records the reviewer of record in `approved_by_user_id`. Either decision advances the window's `version` by 1.

Execution is driven by a `target_state`. Moving `APPROVED → IN_PROGRESS` requires the current clock instant to be at or after `window_start` and strictly before `window_end`, otherwise `ERR_WINDOW_NOT_IN_EFFECT`; it captures the circuit's current `monitoring_state` into `monitoring_state_before_start` and, when `suppresses_monitoring` is true, sets the circuit's `monitoring_state` to `MAINTENANCE_SUPPRESSED` — which is what makes the alarms raised during the work land as suppressed noise with no incident, as described in Section 3.3. Moving `IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED` restores the circuit's `monitoring_state` to exactly the captured `monitoring_state_before_start` value, neither guessing `MONITORED` nor defaulting to `UNMONITORED`, and re-runs the posture cascade. Cancellation is available from `PROPOSED` or from `APPROVED`, is terminal, and if the window had already suppressed monitoring that suppression is reversed; any other originating state is `ERR_WINDOW_STATE_INVALID`. A cancelled window also leaves the maintenance-exclusion set used by the carrier clock — but tickets whose restoration durations were already computed against it keep their stored values, because those clocks are snapshotted at carrier-update time and never retro-adjusted. Each state change advances the window's `version` by 1 and, where the circuit was touched, the circuit's `version` by 1.

Redundancy that has never been tested is a hypothesis. A `NocShiftLead` schedules a failover test by naming the `application_path_id`, the `tested_leg_group` to be deliberately failed, and the reserved interval `scheduled_start` to `scheduled_end`, the end strictly after the start. The requested `tested_leg_group` must appear among the distinct `leg_group` values of the path's `ACTIVE` hops, otherwise `ERR_LEG_NOT_FOUND`. The server derives `carrier_id_set`, the distinct carrier identifiers of the circuits reachable from the path's `ACTIVE` hops, and uses it to limit blast radius: at most **one** test may be reserved per carrier at a time, so scheduling is refused with `ERR_FAILOVER_TEST_CONFLICT` when at least one existing test in `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS` both strictly overlaps the requested interval — each interval including its start and excluding its end — and shares at least one carrier with the requested path's `carrier_id_set`. Adjacency never conflicts here: an existing test ending exactly when the new one starts is fine, in deliberate contrast to the buffered maintenance rule above. A scheduled test starts in `SCHEDULED` with `scheduled_by_user_id` recording the acting `X-User-ID`, `surviving_leg_count` null and `attested_by_user_id` null. Tests may be listed per path and narrowed by `state` and `tested_leg_group`.

Starting a test is guarded four times, in this order: the test must be in `SCHEDULED`, otherwise `ERR_TEST_STATE_INVALID`; the path's `posture` must be exactly `PROTECTED`, since deliberately failing a leg of an already-degraded path is how a test becomes an outage, otherwise `ERR_PATH_NOT_PROTECTED`; no maintenance window in `APPROVED` or `IN_PROGRESS` may cover the current instant on any circuit reachable from the path's `ACTIVE` hops, otherwise `ERR_MAINTENANCE_IN_EFFECT`; and the current clock instant must be at or after `scheduled_start` and strictly before `scheduled_end`, otherwise `ERR_TEST_WINDOW_NOT_IN_EFFECT`. A started test moves `SCHEDULED → IN_PROGRESS`, and for its duration the circuits of the tested leg are treated as unavailable so that the path's `posture` re-derives against the surviving legs; the test's `version` and each affected path's `version` advance by 1.

Recording the result carries an `outcome` of `PASSED`, `FAILED` or `ABORTED` and, for the first two, the observed `surviving_leg_count`. The result must agree with the number: `PASSED` requires `surviving_leg_count` to be at least the path's `min_surviving_legs`, `FAILED` requires it to be strictly fewer, and a contradiction between the narrative and the number is `ERR_FAILOVER_RESULT_INCONSISTENT`. `PASSED` and `FAILED` may only be recorded for a test in `IN_PROGRESS`, while `ABORTED` is accepted from `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS`; anything else is `ERR_TEST_STATE_INVALID`. All three outcomes stamp `attested_by_user_id`. `IN_PROGRESS → PASSED` and `IN_PROGRESS → FAILED` both record `surviving_leg_count`, whereas an aborted test leaves `surviving_leg_count` null because nothing was observed. A `FAILED` result additionally marks `spof_flag` true on every circuit reachable from the path's `ACTIVE` hops in legs other than the `tested_leg_group` — the test just proved those legs are carrying the path alone — and leaves the path's declared `protection_scheme` unchanged, because the design on paper did not change, only the truth about it. Recording any result restores the tested leg to its pre-test availability and re-derives the path's `posture` from live health. The test's `version`, the path's `version` and every touched circuit's `version` advance by 1.

### 3.8. Capacity Pressure, Bandwidth Upgrades and the Vendor Scorecard

Utilisation telemetry arrives one reading at a time, and the platform deliberately keeps no re-sortable history: each reading carries a `sample_at` that must be strictly later than the `sample_at` of the circuit's most recent reading, and a reading that arrives out of order is refused with `ERR_READING_OUT_OF_ORDER` rather than being inserted into the past. The body also carries `utilization_pct`, a percentage between 0 and 100 inclusive, and a `direction` of `INGRESS` or `EGRESS`. Because ordering is guaranteed, the accumulator on the circuit is sufficient and no historical row is ever rescanned. The reading's `threshold_state` is `HIGH` when `utilization_pct` is at or above 80.00 and `NORMAL` otherwise, compared at two decimal places, so exactly 80.00 is `HIGH` while 79.99 is `NORMAL`. A `HIGH` reading raises the circuit's `consecutive_high_readings` by 1; a `NORMAL` reading resets it to 0 outright, with no decay and no partial credit. Readings may be listed per circuit and narrowed by `direction` and `threshold_state`.

The circuit's `capacity_state` is then re-derived from that counter alone: `UPGRADE_REQUIRED` once the counter has reached 3, `WATCH` while it stands at 1 or 2, and `NORMAL` when it is 0. All four movements follow from that rule — `NORMAL → WATCH` on the first `HIGH` reading, `WATCH → UPGRADE_REQUIRED` on the third consecutive one, and `WATCH → NORMAL` or `UPGRADE_REQUIRED → NORMAL` on any `NORMAL` reading. Dropping back to `NORMAL` leaves any upgrade orders already raised entirely untouched; the pressure reading changed, not the commercial commitment. Every reading advances the circuit's `version` by 1.

Crossing into `UPGRADE_REQUIRED` for the first time — that is, when the previous `capacity_state` was something else — raises an upgrade order without anyone asking. The bandwidth ladder is 100, 200, 300, 500, 1000 and 10000 Mbps, and the target chosen is the lowest rung strictly greater than the circuit's current `bandwidth_mbps`. When the circuit already sits on the top rung, no rung qualifies, and then **no order is created at all while `capacity_state` still becomes `UPGRADE_REQUIRED`** — the pressure is recorded even though nothing can be ordered. Otherwise the order is created in `DRAFT` with `origin` `AUTO_THRESHOLD`, `expedite` false, `target_bandwidth_mbps` set to that rung, and `created_by_user_id` holding the literal string `system`, since no human raised it. Its `delta_mbps` immediately joins the carrier's `committed_upgrade_mbps`.

A `CapacityPlanner` may also raise an order by hand, naming the `circuit_id`, the `target_bandwidth_mbps`, `expected_circuit_version` for the stale-parent check of Section 2.6, and optionally `expedite`, which defaults to false. The target must be a member of the ladder and strictly greater than the circuit's present `bandwidth_mbps`; a value off the ladder, a sideways move or a downgrade is `ERR_UPGRADE_TARGET_INVALID`. A circuit whose `chronic_state` is `REPLACEMENT_APPROVED` accepts no new order at all, because money spent widening a circuit that is being ripped out is money wasted: the request is refused with `ERR_CIRCUIT_SLATED_FOR_REPLACEMENT`. A manual order is created in `DRAFT` with `origin` `MANUAL` and `created_by_user_id` holding the acting `X-User-ID`, adds its `delta_mbps` to the carrier's `committed_upgrade_mbps`, and advances the circuit's `version` and the carrier's `version` by 1 each. Orders may be listed per circuit and narrowed by `state` and `origin`.

Costing runs identically for both origins and its evaluation order is pinned, because read carelessly it looks circular. First, `delta_mbps` is the `target_bandwidth_mbps` less the circuit's `bandwidth_mbps` at the moment of creation. Second, `subtotal_cents` is 250000 plus `delta_mbps` multiplied by 900. Third, an expedite tier percentage is selected **from `subtotal_cents`**: 0.25 when `subtotal_cents` is below 500000, 0.15 when it is below 1500000, and 0.08 otherwise, with each band's lower bound included. Fourth, `expedite_fee_cents` is `subtotal_cents` multiplied by that tier percentage and rounded half up to whole cents when `expedite` is true, and exactly 0 when it is false. Fifth, `total_cost_cents` is `subtotal_cents` plus `expedite_fee_cents`. The tier is never re-selected from `total_cost_cents`, and a later completed upgrade never restates the figures on an order created earlier.

The order lifecycle then runs `DRAFT → SUBMITTED → APPROVED → IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED`, with cancellation available from any of the first three. Any operation attempted from a state that does not admit it is `ERR_ORDER_STATE_INVALID`. Submission additionally re-checks that the circuit is not `REPLACEMENT_APPROVED`, since the slating may have happened after the draft was raised, and refuses with `ERR_CIRCUIT_SLATED_FOR_REPLACEMENT`; it leaves the committed-bandwidth ledger unchanged, because `SUBMITTED` is still an open commitment. Approval is a `VendorManager` act and enforces the separation of Section 2.11: the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the order's `created_by_user_id`, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, which also means an `AUTO_THRESHOLD` order — created by `system` — can always be approved by any authorised manager. Approval stamps `approved_by_user_id`. Progression to `IN_PROGRESS` and completion are both driven by a `target_state`. `IN_PROGRESS` keeps the commitment open; `COMPLETED` is where the estate actually changes — the circuit's `bandwidth_mbps` is raised to the order's `target_bandwidth_mbps`, its `consecutive_high_readings` resets to 0 and its `capacity_state` to `NORMAL`, and the order's `delta_mbps` is released from the carrier's `committed_upgrade_mbps` because `COMPLETED` sits outside the committed set. Cancellation is the compensating action, optionally recording a `cancel_reason`; it subtracts exactly the `delta_mbps` that was added at creation, restoring `committed_upgrade_mbps` to its pre-order value, and deliberately leaves both `bandwidth_mbps` and `capacity_state` alone, since abandoning an upgrade does not relieve the utilisation pressure that prompted it. Each of these steps advances the order's `version` by 1, plus the circuit's `version` and the carrier's `version` by 1 each wherever those rows were touched. The invariant to hold is the one in Section 2.10: `committed_upgrade_mbps` is always the sum of `delta_mbps` across that carrier's orders in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `APPROVED` or `IN_PROGRESS`.

Periodically a `VendorManager` scores a carrier over a closed period. The review names the `carrier_id`, `period_start` and `period_end`, the end strictly after the start, and the period is treated as half-open throughout: an event at `period_start` counts, an event at `period_end` belongs to the next period. Four figures are computed at creation and then frozen. The credit side gathers the carrier's claims whose `state` is `ACKNOWLEDGED` or `PAID` and whose `submitted_at` falls in the period; their `credit_amount_cents` are summed and divided by the total `mrc_cents` of every circuit of that carrier whose `created_at` is before `period_end`, giving `credit_ratio` rounded half up to four decimals — or exactly 0.0 when that revenue base is zero, which is the division-guard rather than an error. The repair side gathers the carrier's tickets whose `state` is `CLOSED_RESTORED` and whose `confirmed_at` falls in the period, and averages their `restoration_minutes_carrier_clock` into `mttr_minutes` at two decimals, or exactly 0.00 when no ticket qualifies. `chronic_circuit_count` is snapshotted from the carrier as it stands at creation. The raw score starts at 100 and subtracts three penalties: `credit_ratio` multiplied by 100 and then by 2; `chronic_circuit_count` multiplied by 5; and the smaller of `mttr_minutes` divided by 60 and the cap of 10 hours, multiplied by 3 — so a carrier that takes a week per repair is penalised no more heavily than one that takes ten hours. `net_performance_score` is that raw figure rounded half up to two decimals, held up to a floor of 0.00 so it never goes negative. The review is created in `PENDING`.

Deciding a review is a one-way fork and each branch carries a different consequence, which is the point of separating them. A review already carrying any decision other than `PENDING` is `ERR_REVIEW_ALREADY_DECIDED`. The request carries the `decision` and optionally a `justification`, and both are recorded together with `decided_by_user_id`. `RETAIN` requires `net_performance_score` to be at or above 60, otherwise `ERR_RETAIN_NOT_JUSTIFIED`, and beyond stamping the decision it changes nothing else in the estate. `REMEDIATION_PLAN` carries no score precondition and sets the carrier's `watchlist_state` to `WATCHLIST`. `REPLACE` requires both a `net_performance_score` strictly below 60 and a `chronic_circuit_count` of at least 1 — a bad score alone is not enough, there must be a chronic circuit to replace — otherwise `ERR_REPLACEMENT_NOT_JUSTIFIED`; it likewise sets `watchlist_state` to `WATCHLIST`, and then drives the estate cascade in three linked steps. Every circuit of that carrier whose `chronic_state` is `CHRONIC` moves to `REPLACEMENT_APPROVED`. Because those circuits are now slated for replacement, every upgrade order on them in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `APPROVED` moves to `CANCELLED` with `cancel_reason` set to the literal string `SUPERSEDED_BY_REPLACEMENT`. Because those orders have left the committed set, the carrier's `committed_upgrade_mbps` falls by the sum of their `delta_mbps`. Orders already `IN_PROGRESS` are not swept up — physical work underway is not abandoned by a paperwork decision. The decision advances the review's `version`, the carrier's `version` and each touched circuit's `version` by 1. All three decisions are terminal. Reviews may be listed per carrier and narrowed by `decision`.

Endpoints that bring a brand-new resource into existence respond with HTTP 201 and return the created object; every other successful call — including the action endpoints that advance a state machine, the deletion that retires a hop, and all reads — responds with HTTP 200 and returns the affected or requested object. All responses, successful or not, use the envelope of Section 2.2. Every request must carry `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role`, and the role must appear in the endpoint's authorised list; the two authentication and authorisation failures are described in Section 2.1 and take precedence over everything else. List endpoints accept `limit` and `offset` as described in Section 2.3 in addition to the filters named in their own subsection above.
