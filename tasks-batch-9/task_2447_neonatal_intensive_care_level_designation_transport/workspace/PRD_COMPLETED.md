# Neonatal Network API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A regional neonatal network is a promise made across hospital walls: that every newborn who needs more than a community nursery can safely give will reach a unit staffed, equipped and designated to give it, and that no infant will be moved further from home than their condition requires. Today that promise is kept by telephone. A referring obstetrician calls a regional centre, a neonatologist guesses at bed availability from memory, a transport team is scrambled without knowing whether the isolette is out of decontamination, and the designation paperwork that is supposed to guarantee the receiving unit can actually deliver Level III care sits in a filing cabinet that nobody re-reads when a ventilator goes out of service. The consequences are measurable — infants back-transferred to hospitals that cannot support them, maternal transfers refused in favour of postnatal transports that were riskier, and central-line infection rates reported to the network months after the cohort has gone home.

This document specifies the API for the network's operational backbone. It carries four intertwined responsibilities. First, **level-of-care designation**: the network declares a standard for each level, units record the credentials and equipment they hold, and a designation review scores the unit against the standard it is asking for — with the deliberate property that a designation on paper is worthless if the underlying capability has evaporated, so the level a unit may actually act on is always the lower of what it was granted and what it can currently prove. Second, **referral and bed allocation**: every referral is scored for the level of care it needs, both on the findings that already exist and on the needs that can be anticipated, and acceptance consumes a bed from a strictly conserved ledger under optimistic concurrency control. Third, **transport and bedside care**: activation of a transport team against a held bed, mode-dependent isolette occupation and decontamination, a physiologic stabilization bundle that gates departure, and then the cot-side record of care days, developmental rounds, milk handling and infection surveillance. Fourth, **network oversight**: surge escalation when the census outruns the staffed bed target, risk-adjusted infection reporting back to the region, and follow-up clinic enrollment for the graduates who need it.

Two design commitments run through every requirement below. Time never mutates state on its own — the system runs on a logical clock, derived statuses are recomputed on every read, and every other time-driven consequence is applied by one explicit operational sweep. And capacity is never optimistic: a bed that is held is as unavailable as a bed that is occupied, a forecast bed is never accepted against, and when an escalation stands down while its beds are still full the ledger is allowed to go negative rather than pretend otherwise.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to this API must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting person, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capability set the caller is exercising. If either header is absent, or present but empty or consisting only of whitespace, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before anything else is examined. If both headers are present but the value of `X-User-Role` is not one of the roles listed against the endpoint in §4, the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

Seven roles exist, spelled exactly as follows: `NetworkAdmin`, `UnitDirector`, `IntakeNeonatologist`, `ReferringClinician`, `TransportCoordinator`, `BedsideNurse`, and `FollowUpCoordinator`.

Three of these roles are unit-scoped and are backed by a persisted `NicuUnit`. For `UnitDirector`, `IntakeNeonatologist` and `ReferringClinician`, `X-User-ID` must take the form `<unit_id>:<person-label>` — the text before the first colon is the `id` of the `NicuUnit` the caller acts for, and the label after the colon identifies the individual person. The unit portion is what ownership checks compare against: for endpoints addressed at a unit, it must equal the unit named in the path; for a creation that names the unit it acts for by a `unit_id` in the request body — a credential, an equipment asset, a designation review, a surge escalation or a surveillance report — it must equal that body `unit_id`; for intake actions on a referral, it must equal that referral's `receiving_unit_id`; for referral creation and referral withdrawal, it must equal the referral's `referring_unit_id`; and for bedside or intake actions on an admission, it must equal that admission's `unit_id`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_UNIT_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403). The comparison guards mutating requests only: a `GET` is never refused with `ERR_UNIT_SCOPE_VIOLATION`, so any role §4 lists against a read may read any unit's rows. The person portion after the colon is what the segregation-of-duties rules compare, so two directors of the same unit are distinct actors even though they share a unit prefix.

The remaining four roles carry opaque identity. When `X-User-Role` is `NetworkAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it where a transition demands it — as `verified_by_user_id` or `certified_by_user_id` on a `DesignationReview`, or as `authorized_by_user_id` on a `SurgeEscalation` — but never compares it to any entity's `id`. The same holds for `TransportCoordinator`, for `BedsideNurse` (whose identifier is recorded as `received_by_user_id` and `verified_by_user_id` on a `MilkBatch`), and for `FollowUpCoordinator`: each carries any non-empty `X-User-ID`, is never unit-bound, and is never subject to `ERR_UNIT_SCOPE_VIOLATION`.

Role responsibilities divide as follows. `NetworkAdmin` is the regional network authority: it registers units, declares standards, returns, verifies and certifies designation reviews, authorizes and denies surge escalations, accepts and rejects surveillance reports, and alone drives the logical clock and the operational capacity sweep. `UnitDirector` is accountable for one unit's designation readiness — credentials, equipment, opening and submitting designation reviews, requesting surge escalations, and opening and submitting surveillance reports — and shares the unit capacity edit and the credential and equipment writes with `NetworkAdmin`, standing an escalation down with `NetworkAdmin`, and posting infection events with `BedsideNurse`. `IntakeNeonatologist` decides what the receiving unit takes: requesting information, accepting, declining, deferring, revoking an acceptance, admitting, attesting back-transfer readiness, creating back-transfer referrals, and discharging. `ReferringClinician` sends cases out of the referring hospital, answers information requests, and withdraws cases. `TransportCoordinator` runs the regional transport service across every unit and shares the stabilization recording with `BedsideNurse`. `BedsideNurse` owns cot-side work: care updates, developmental rounds, and the whole milk-handling chain. `FollowUpCoordinator` runs the high-risk graduate clinic. Read operations are broadly shared; §4 is authoritative on which roles may call which endpoint.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`, followed by twenty uppercase alphanumeric characters.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. It is the one timestamp in the API read from real wall-clock time rather than from the logical clock of §2.4, because it describes the transport rather than the domain.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be below 1, and may not exceed 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` outside the range of 1 to 100, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400), as is a value that is not an integer at all; either parameter supplied as an empty string, however, is treated as omitted and falls back to its default, and the same holds for any filter query parameter supplied empty.

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are sorted by `created_at` in ascending order, with rows sharing an identical `created_at` instant held in the order they were created. One endpoint deliberately departs from this: the deferral-queue read returns every one of a unit's `DEFERRED` referrals — including those whose `required_level` outranks the unit's `effective_level`, which promotion itself would skip over — sorted by the promotion ranking described in §3.4 rather than in creation order.

For list responses the `meta` object carries `limit` and `offset` echoing the effective values used, `total_count` giving the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied, and `has_more` indicating whether any rows remain beyond the current page — true exactly when `offset` plus the number of rows actually returned is still below `total_count`. Filter query parameters, where an endpoint offers them, combine conjunctively — a row must match every supplied filter to appear.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Versioning and the Logical Clock

Every stored resource is identified by `id`, a server-minted UUID with no type prefix — no `unit_` or `ref_` decoration, just the UUID. An `id` identifies a resource in URL paths and in foreign-key fields, and nowhere else: it must never participate in a selector, a ranking, a tie-break, an allocation order, a cascade order or a replay order. Wherever a precise fallback ordering is needed, this specification pins `created_at` ascending instead.

Alongside `id`, resources carry `created_at` and `updated_at` — ISO 8601 UTC timestamps rendered with exactly three fractional-second digits — and `version`, an integer that begins at 1 when the resource is created and increases by one on every mutation of that resource. `updated_at` is refreshed to the current clock instant by the same mutation that advances `version`. One entity is deliberately exempt: a designation standard is immutable once declared, so it carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has no `updated_at` and no `version` field at all, there is no edit endpoint for it, and it never participates in optimistic concurrency control.

Server-stamped time comes from a **logical clock**, never from wall-clock time. The clock begins at `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and the only thing that moves it is an administrator-only test-driving capability that sets it to a supplied `current_utc` instant, required in the body, and returns a bare object carrying that single `current_utc` key as its `data`. Every timestamp the server itself stamps is read from that clock: `created_at`, `updated_at`, `held_at`, `revoked_at`, `out_of_service_at`, `assessment_at`, `submitted_at`, `infection_confirmed_at`, `discharged_at`, `attendance_recorded_at`, and the base instants from which the various `expires_at` deadlines are computed. Client-supplied instants — `expires_on`, `last_service_at`, `serviced_at`, `depart_at`, `planned_return_at`, `arrived_at`, `expressed_at`, `effective_from`, `effective_to`, `period_start`, `period_end`, `target_appointment_date`, `confirmed_appointment_date` — are taken as given and are not clamped to the clock. Rendering, however, is uniform rather than verbatim: every instant the API returns — whether the server stamped it, derived it, or was handed it by the caller — is written in the same ISO 8601 UTC form with exactly three fractional-second digits, normalised to UTC and shown to whole milliseconds, rather than echoed back character for character as it was sent. So a `confirmed_appointment_date` supplied as `2026-09-01T09:00:00Z` reads back as `2026-09-01T09:00:00.000Z`, while the instant retained for every comparison and every derivation is exactly the one supplied.

### 2.5. Derived-on-Read Status and the Operational Capacity Sweep

Advancing the clock never, by itself, rewrites a stored row. Two statuses are pure functions of the clock and are therefore re-derived on every read and before every decision that consults them, so no background process is required. A credential's `status` is `REVOKED` when its `revoked_at` is set to a non-null instant; otherwise it is `EXPIRED` when its `expires_on` is at or before the current clock instant; otherwise it is `VALID`. An equipment asset's `status` is `OUT_OF_SERVICE` when its `out_of_service_at` is non-null; otherwise it is `SERVICE_DUE` when its `next_service_due_at` is at or before the current clock instant; otherwise it is `IN_SERVICE`. The asset's `next_service_due_at` is itself derived — the `last_service_at` instant advanced by `service_interval_days` days.

Every other time-driven consequence is applied only by an explicit, administrator-only operational capability — the **capacity sweep** — or by an explicitly declared transition. The sweep accepts an optional `unit_id` in its request body to narrow its scope to a single unit — supplied, it must resolve, else `ERR_UNIT_NOT_FOUND` (404), and it narrows the first two passes by the reservation's or escalation's own `unit_id` and the third by the enrollment's `clinic_unit_id`; with the parameter omitted it works across the whole network. It performs four passes, in this order. First, every bed reservation still in `HELD` whose `expires_at` is at or before the current clock instant is moved to `EXPIRED` with `released_reason` set to `HOLD_EXPIRED`, and its referral is moved to `DIVERTED` with `decision_reason` set to `HOLD_EXPIRED` — but only where that referral is still `ACCEPTED`, so a referral already carried onward to `ADMITTED` or already withdrawn keeps the status it has while its stale hold still expires. Second, every surge escalation in `ACTIVE` whose `effective_to` is strictly before the current clock instant is moved to `EXPIRED` and the unit's `escalation_bed_count` is re-derived. Third, every follow-up enrollment still in `ENROLLED` whose `appointment_window_end` is strictly before the current clock instant, and which was never scheduled, is moved to `LOST_TO_FOLLOWUP`. Fourth, for every unit whose bed picture the first three passes changed, the deferral-queue promotion of §3.4 is run. The sweep reports `holds_expired`, `escalations_expired`, `enrollments_lapsed`, `referrals_promoted` and `units_touched` as integer counts, and it advances `version` by one on each unit it changed — once per unit, no matter how many rows at that unit it touched. `units_touched` counts exactly those units whose bed picture the first two passes changed: a sweep that only lapses enrollments reports zero units touched and advances no unit's `version`, and the promotions of the fourth pass add no further unit to the count and no further version increment beyond that single one.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Three write operations are contested enough that they may only be performed against a picture of the world the caller has actually read. Each declares a non-optional integer version field in its request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of a parent resource; if the stored `version` differs, the write is refused and nothing at all is persisted.

Accepting a referral contests `available_bed_count` on the receiving unit — the count of free staffed and escalation beds. The acceptance request must carry `expected_unit_version` equal to the receiving unit's current `version`. If any other writer has advanced that unit's `version` since the client read it, the acceptance is rejected with `ERR_UNIT_VERSION_CONFLICT` (HTTP 412) and no reservation is created. Concretely: an intake neonatologist accepts referral A quoting version 5, which succeeds and takes the unit to version 6; a subsequent acceptance of referral B still quoting version 5 is rejected even though a bed happens to be free, because B's clinical decision was made against a stale bed picture. The operations that advance a unit's `version`, and therefore invalidate a quoted value, are: accepting a referral; revoking an acceptance; withdrawing a referral in the case where that withdrawal releases a held reservation; admitting an infant, both for the hold-to-occupied conversion and for the back-transfer cascade at the source unit; discharging an admission; authorizing a surge escalation; standing a surge escalation down; editing the unit's capacity profile; certifying a designation review; and the capacity sweep, once per unit it changes.

Editing a unit's capacity profile contests `staffed_bed_target` and `followup_clinic_daily_capacity` — the denominators every allocation decision divides by. That edit likewise requires `expected_unit_version` matching the unit's current `version`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_UNIT_VERSION_CONFLICT` (HTTP 412) with no field written. Concretely: a director shrinks the target from 6 to 4 quoting version 3, taking the unit to version 4; a second edit still quoting version 3 is rejected rather than silently overwriting the shrink.

Creating a back-transfer referral from an admission contests the admission itself — the single infant whose onward disposition is being decided. That request must carry `expected_admission_version` equal to the admission's current `version`. Because a back-transfer referral encodes the infant's weight, support and readiness at the instant it is written, a care update or an infection event that has advanced the admission since the client's read invalidates the decision: the write is rejected with `ERR_ADMISSION_VERSION_CONFLICT` (HTTP 412) and no referral is created. Concretely: the first back-transfer referral is created quoting version 4, taking the admission to version 5; a second creation still quoting version 4 is rejected. The operations that advance an admission's `version` are: posting a care update; posting a developmental round; posting an infection event; attesting back-transfer readiness; creating a back-transfer referral; discharging; and recording or reversing a milk administration.

No other endpoint takes a version field. Where a version field is declared, it is checked before any state or business rule is evaluated, so that a caller working from a stale read is told to re-read rather than being told its stale decision was illegal.

### 2.7. The Level-of-Care Ladder, Capability and Effective Level

Levels of care form a strict four-rung ladder: `LEVEL_I` ranks 1, `LEVEL_II` ranks 2, `LEVEL_III` ranks 3, and `LEVEL_IV` ranks 4. Every comparison in this specification that speaks of one level being "at or above" another, or takes the higher or the lower of two levels, is a comparison of those ranks. "One level below `LEVEL_II`" means `LEVEL_I`, and there is nothing below `LEVEL_I` — a demotion that would fall beneath `LEVEL_I` stays at `LEVEL_I`.

Each unit carries three level fields that must not be confused. `designated_level` is what the regional network has granted: it defaults to `LEVEL_I` and is set at registration, and thereafter only a designation certification may change it. `capability_level` is what the unit can currently prove — the highest-ranking level among the declared standards that the unit satisfies, falling back to `LEVEL_I` when it satisfies none of them — a level for which no standard has been declared at all is not a candidate, since there is nothing to satisfy — because a newborn nursery baseline is statutory and a unit is never rated below `LEVEL_I`. A unit **satisfies** a standard when every code listed in that standard's `required_credential_codes` is held by a credential of that unit whose derived `status` is `VALID` and whose `fte` is at least 0.5, and every code in that standard's `required_equipment_codes` is held by an asset of that unit whose derived `status` is `IN_SERVICE`. `effective_level` is the lower-ranking of `designated_level` and `capability_level`, and it — not the designated level — gates every referral acceptance.

Two booleans expose the halves of the satisfaction test against the standard for the unit's own `designated_level`: `staffing_compliant` is true when every required credential code of that standard is held with derived `status` `VALID` and `fte` at least 0.5, and `equipment_compliant` is true when every required equipment code of that standard is held with derived `status` `IN_SERVICE`. Where no standard has been declared for the unit's `designated_level`, there is nothing to fail and both booleans read true. All four derived values are recomputed on every read of the unit and again before every acceptance decision, which means a credential revocation or an equipment fault propagates into `effective_level` with no explicit write to the unit at all.

A unit's `designation_status` is separate from its level and takes one of four values: `PROVISIONAL` on registration, `DESIGNATED` after a fully passing certification, `CONDITIONAL` after a certification that granted one level less than requested or after an equipment loss that broke the standard for the designated level, and `SUSPENDED` after a review exhausts its submission rounds. A `SUSPENDED` unit may not accept referrals; the attempt is refused with `ERR_UNIT_SUSPENDED` (HTTP 422).

### 2.8. The Bed Ledger, Waterfall Allocation and Census Posture

Bed capacity is a conserved ledger, not a counter. A unit's `staffed_bed_target` is the base staffed bed count it declares, and must be at least 1. Its `escalation_bed_count` is the sum of `authorized_beds` over the unit's surge escalations in status `ACTIVE`. Its `occupied_bed_count` is the number of the unit's admissions whose `care_status` is one of `ADMITTED`, `STABILIZED`, `BACK_TRANSFER_READY` or `BACK_TRANSFER_REQUIRED`. Its `held_bed_count` is the number of the unit's bed reservations in status `HELD`. And `available_bed_count` is the base staffed bed target plus the escalation bed count, minus the occupied bed count, minus the held bed count. The invariant that must hold after every single operation is that `staffed_bed_target` plus `escalation_bed_count` equals `occupied_bed_count` plus `held_bed_count` plus `available_bed_count`.

Two consequences follow and are easy to get wrong. First, allocation is a **waterfall**: a new hold takes `bed_class` `STAFFED` while the pre-operation sum of `occupied_bed_count` and `held_bed_count` is strictly below `staffed_bed_target`, and `ESCALATION` otherwise. Base staffed beds always fill before escalation beds. Second, `available_bed_count` is never clamped at zero. Standing an escalation down while its beds are still occupied leaves the value negative, and the negative value is exactly what the closed posture and the "no new holds" rule read.

`census_posture` is derived from the ledger by evaluating three bands from the top down. When `available_bed_count` is at or below zero, the posture is `CLOSED`. Otherwise, when `available_bed_count` is at or below the `staffed_bed_target` multiplied by 0.20 and rounded down to a whole bed, the posture is `CONSTRAINED`. Otherwise it is `OPEN`. Because the constrained band uses an at-or-below comparison, a unit with a target of 5 and exactly 1 free bed is `CONSTRAINED` while the same unit with 2 free beds is `OPEN`; and because the threshold is rounded down, a unit with a target of 4 has an empty constrained band — one free bed there is already `OPEN`.

Nursing requirement is derived per acuity band. For each level, the unit's census band is the number of its admissions whose `care_status` is one of `ADMITTED`, `STABILIZED`, `BACK_TRANSFER_READY` or `BACK_TRANSFER_REQUIRED` and whose `current_care_level` equals that level. The waiver in force is the largest `staffing_waiver_ratio` among the unit's `ACTIVE` surge escalations, or 1.00 when the unit has none. For each level, divide that level's census band by the product of the `nurse_patient_ratio_denominator` declared by the standard for that level and the waiver in force, then round the quotient **up** to a whole nurse; `required_nurse_count` is the sum of those per-band whole numbers; a level for which no standard has been declared has no ratio denominator to divide by and contributes nothing to the sum, however many infants sit in its band. The order is pinned: the rounding up happens per acuity band and only then are the bands summed. Adding the fractional requirements first and rounding up once is wrong, because a single `LEVEL_III` infant and a single `LEVEL_I` infant cannot share a nurse. An empty census requires zero nurses.

A separate advisory read publishes capacity in two variants. The committed variant, `committed_available`, is simply `available_bed_count`. The forecast variant, `forecast_available`, is `committed_available` plus `back_transfer_ready_count` — the number of the unit's admissions whose `care_status` is `BACK_TRANSFER_READY` — plus `expiring_hold_count` — the number of `HELD` reservations whose `expires_at` is at or before the current clock instant advanced by 60 minutes. Variant selection is absolute: every gate that creates an obligation, meaning acceptance, deferral-queue promotion and surge authorization, reads the committed figure. Only the advisory capacity read publishes the forecast figure alongside `census_posture`, `required_nurse_count` and `deferral_queue_depth`, and a forecast bed is never accepted against. That read is not a unit representation: its `data` is a bare object carrying exactly `unit_id`, `committed_available`, `forecast_available`, `back_transfer_ready_count`, `expiring_hold_count`, `census_posture`, `required_nurse_count` and `deferral_queue_depth`, with no `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` or `version`. `deferral_queue_depth` is itself the count of referrals whose `receiving_unit_id` is this unit and whose status is `DEFERRED`.

### 2.9. Precision, Rounding and Score Conventions

Unless a rule says otherwise, monetary-style and scored quantities are rounded **half up** to two decimal places. That applies to `triage_priority`, to each of `staffing_subscore`, `equipment_subscore` and `volume_subscore`, to `compliance_score` — which is rounded at each subscore and again on the weighted total — to `developmental_bundle_score`, and to both `crude_clabsi_rate` and `device_adjusted_clabsi_rate`. Two quantities are carried at four decimal places, half up: `census_ratio_at_request` and `birth_weight_risk_weight`. Milk volumes — `volume_ml`, `administered_ml`, `discarded_ml`, `remaining_ml` and the admission's `milk_administered_ml` roll-up — are carried at one decimal place. `stabilization_score` is an integer arithmetic result and needs no rounding.

Two integer derivations round in a fixed direction rather than half up. `required_nurse_count` rounds each acuity band's fractional requirement **up**, as described in §2.8. `authorized_beds` derives its staffing capacity by rounding **down**, as described in §3.5.

### 2.10. Rejection Precedence and Path Resolution

When a request violates more than one rule, exactly one error is returned, and which one is not a matter of implementation convenience. Checks are evaluated in the following fixed order, and the first failure is returned immediately.

1. Authentication: `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` must both be present and non-empty, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. Role authorization: `X-User-Role` must be one of the roles listed against the endpoint, else `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403).
3. Path existence: every `id` appearing in the URL path must resolve to a stored row, resolved strictly left to right, else the matching not-found code for that entity (404). A request naming a unit that does not exist fails with `ERR_UNIT_NOT_FOUND` before anything further along the path is examined at all.
4. Unit scope: for the three unit-scoped roles, the unit parsed from `X-User-ID` must match the unit the operation acts on as described in §2.1, else `ERR_UNIT_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (403). This is the position of the comparison whenever the unit is named by the URL path itself or is recorded on an entity the path has already resolved. Where the operation instead learns the unit from the request body — from a body `unit_id` when recording a credential or an equipment asset, opening a designation review, requesting a surge escalation or opening a surveillance report, or from the `receiving_unit_id` of a body-supplied `referral_id` when admitting an infant — the comparison necessarily waits until that body value has cleared the schema check of band 5 and resolved in band 6, and is then made ahead of band 7 and everything after it: a malformed body or a body `id` that does not resolve is answered with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` or the matching not-found code rather than with a scope violation. Referral creation is the exception §2.1 names explicitly: when its `referring_unit_id` is present in the body as a string it is compared here, at band 4, before the schema of the rest of the body is checked and before either unit id is resolved; when that field is absent or not a string the band 5 schema failure is returned instead.
5. Request schema: required fields present, types correct, enum values in range, numbers inside their declared bounds, `period_end` strictly after `period_start`, `planned_return_at` strictly after `depart_at`, `effective_to` strictly after `effective_from`, `limit` between 1 and 100 inclusive and `offset` at least 0 — else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). A request body that is absent, unparseable, or parses to something other than a JSON object fails the same way. So too does a request against a path this specification does not expose, or with a method that path does not accept: both are answered with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, carrying the transport's own 404 or 405 status rather than 400.
6. Body existence: every `id` supplied in the request body must resolve — `unit_id`, `referral_id`, `admission_id`, `isolette_asset_id`, `clinic_unit_id`, `reservation_id` — else the matching not-found code (404). Where a body carries two of them, they resolve in the order the endpoint declares them, so referral creation resolves `referring_unit_id` before `receiving_unit_id` and reports the first of the two that is unknown.
7. Optimistic concurrency: where the endpoint declares `expected_unit_version` or `expected_admission_version`, the supplied value must equal the parent's stored `version`, else `ERR_UNIT_VERSION_CONFLICT` or `ERR_ADMISSION_VERSION_CONFLICT` (412).
8. Own state: the entity's own status must permit the trigger, including terminal-state lockdown and rejection of a repeated trigger, else `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409).
9. Dependent state: cross-entity state preconditions on an entity other than the one being written, else `ERR_DEPENDENT_STATE_INVALID` (409). These are: revoking an acceptance requires no in-flight transport run; departing requires the referral behind the reservation still `ACCEPTED`; creating a back-transfer referral requires the source admission in `BACK_TRANSFER_READY` or `BACK_TRANSFER_REQUIRED`; follow-up enrollment requires the admission `DISCHARGED` or `TRANSFERRED`; and a milk administration requires the admission not to be `DISCHARGED` or `DECEASED`.
10. Level capability: the receiving unit's `effective_level` must rank at or above the referral's `required_level`, else `ERR_LEVEL_CAPABILITY_INSUFFICIENT` (422).
11. Clinical safety gates, each with its own code (422): a transport crew must include `TRANSPORT_RN`, else `ERR_CREW_COMPOSITION_INVALID`; the isolette must be serviceable, else `ERR_ISOLETTE_NOT_SERVICEABLE`; air modes require weather minimums attested, else `ERR_WEATHER_MINIMUMS_NOT_MET`; departure requires a complete stabilization bundle, else `ERR_STABILIZATION_INCOMPLETE`; back-transfer readiness requires a developmental bundle score of at least 60, else `ERR_DEVELOPMENTAL_BUNDLE_INCOMPLETE`; a milk administration requires a verified, unexpired batch, else `ERR_MILK_NOT_ADMINISTRABLE` or `ERR_MILK_EXPIRED`; and a suspended unit may not accept referrals, else `ERR_UNIT_SUSPENDED`. Follow-up enrollment of an ineligible graduate belongs to this band and is refused with `ERR_NOT_ELIGIBLE_FOR_FOLLOWUP`.
12. Referential coherence between entities that both already exist (409): the isolette must belong to the run's receiving unit, else `ERR_ISOLETTE_UNIT_MISMATCH`; a presented milk label must match the stored one, else `ERR_MILK_LABEL_MISMATCH`; the maternal-transfer preference of §3.4 must not be violated, else `ERR_MATERNAL_TRANSFER_PREFERENCE`; and an admission must not already hold an open follow-up enrollment, else `ERR_DUPLICATE_ENROLLMENT`. A second back-transfer referral from the same admission supersedes the earlier one rather than being rejected as a duplicate.
13. Interval and window rules (409): an isolette occupation interval must not overlap another run's including its decontamination buffer, else `ERR_ISOLETTE_UNAVAILABLE`; a unit's surge escalation windows must not overlap inclusively, else `ERR_ESCALATION_WINDOW_OVERLAP`; a follow-up appointment must fall inside the graduate's appointment window, else `ERR_APPOINTMENT_OUTSIDE_WINDOW`; and a surveillance period must not begin before the unit was created, else `ERR_PERIOD_INVALID`.
14. Uniqueness and segregation of duties (409): one standard per level, else `ERR_DUPLICATE_STANDARD`; one open designation review per unit, else `ERR_REVIEW_ALREADY_OPEN`; and the segregation rules of §2.11, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. This band also enforces one active follow-up enrollment per admission.
15. Capacity and quota rules, evaluated last precisely because they depend on every derived quantity above: `available_bed_count` must be at least 1 to accept, else `ERR_NO_BED_AVAILABLE` (409); `census_ratio_at_request` must be at least 0.80 to authorize an escalation, else `ERR_SURGE_THRESHOLD_NOT_MET` (409); `authorized_beds` must come out at least 1, else `ERR_SURGE_NO_STAFFED_CAPACITY` (422); a follow-up clinic's calendar-day bookings must be strictly below `followup_clinic_daily_capacity`, else `ERR_CLINIC_DAY_FULL` (409); and a milk administration must not exceed `remaining_ml` nor a reversal exceed `administered_ml`, else `ERR_MILK_VOLUME_EXCEEDED` (422).

Each identifier the API accepts has exactly one not-found code, and the same code is used whether the identifier arrived in the path or in the body. A `unit_id` — and equally a `clinic_unit_id`, a `referring_unit_id` or a `receiving_unit_id` — that does not resolve yields `ERR_UNIT_NOT_FOUND`. A `standard_id` yields `ERR_STANDARD_NOT_FOUND`; a `credential_id` yields `ERR_CREDENTIAL_NOT_FOUND`; an `asset_id`, or an `isolette_asset_id` supplied in a body, yields `ERR_ASSET_NOT_FOUND`; a `review_id` yields `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_FOUND`; a `referral_id` yields `ERR_REFERRAL_NOT_FOUND`; a `reservation_id` yields `ERR_RESERVATION_NOT_FOUND`; a `run_id` yields `ERR_RUN_NOT_FOUND`; an `admission_id`, including a `source_admission_id` or a `back_transfer_source_admission_id`, yields `ERR_ADMISSION_NOT_FOUND`; a `batch_id` yields `ERR_MILK_BATCH_NOT_FOUND`; an `escalation_id` yields `ERR_ESCALATION_NOT_FOUND`; a `report_id` yields `ERR_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`; and an `enrollment_id` yields `ERR_ENROLLMENT_NOT_FOUND`. All of them are HTTP 404.

### 2.11. Cascade Propagation and Segregation of Duties

Because entities in this domain are addressed at top-level collections and related only through foreign-key fields, consistency is maintained by explicit cascades rather than by nesting. Whenever a cascade touches an entity, that entity's `updated_at` is refreshed to the current clock instant and its `version` advances by one, exactly as if it had been written directly. Cascades are immediate: by the time a response is returned, every consequence described in §3 has already been applied and every derived figure on every touched unit reflects it. Where an operation changes both a child and its unit — releasing a reservation while freeing a bed, for example — the unit's counters, `census_posture` and `required_nurse_count` are all re-derived within the same operation.

Segregation of duties is enforced on three chains, and in each case the comparison is between person identities, not between roles. On a designation review, the user recorded in `submitted_by_user_id` may not be the user who returns it and may not be the user who verifies it, and the user recorded in `verified_by_user_id` may not be the user who certifies it — the certifier is recorded as `certified_by_user_id`. On a milk batch, the user in `received_by_user_id` may not be the user who verifies the label; the verifier is recorded as `verified_by_user_id`. On a surveillance report, the user who submitted it may not be the user who accepts it. Every violation on any of these chains is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 409).

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Bringing a Unit into the Regional Network

A `NetworkAdmin` registers each participating hospital's neonatal unit, supplying a display `name`, a `hospital_type` of either `REGIONAL_CENTER` or `COMMUNITY`, and a `staffed_bed_target` that is a whole number of beds of at least one. Four further inputs are optional at registration and default in the caller's absence: `designated_level` defaults to `LEVEL_I`; `transport_capable`, which declares that the unit operates its own neonatal transport team, defaults to false; `annual_admission_volume`, the rolling annual admission volume the unit declares for designation scoring, a whole number of admissions of at least zero, defaults to 0; and `followup_clinic_daily_capacity`, the number of high-risk follow-up clinic slots the unit can fill per calendar day, likewise a whole number of at least zero, defaults to 0. A newly registered unit begins at `designation_status` `PROVISIONAL` and at `quality_flag` `WITHIN_BENCHMARK`, the latter being replaced only when the network accepts a surveillance report as described in §3.9. Units may be listed and filtered by `hospital_type`, by `designation_status` and by `census_posture`, and reading a single unit recomputes every derived capability and bed figure described in §2.7 and §2.8 before responding.

Later edits to the capacity profile are narrow and guarded. A `NetworkAdmin` or the unit's own `UnitDirector` may revise `staffed_bed_target`, `followup_clinic_daily_capacity`, `annual_admission_volume` and `transport_capable`, each optionally, but the request must carry `expected_unit_version` as described in §2.6 — that version field is the edit's only required input, and the unit's `version` advances by one on a matching quote even when the body carries no editable field at all, so an empty revision is still a contested write that invalidates every other quoted version. Nothing else about a unit is editable — `designated_level` moves only by certification, and every counter and posture field is derived rather than stored input.

The network's expectations are expressed as **designation standards**, one per level of care. Declaring a standard requires the `level` it governs, the `required_credential_codes` and `required_equipment_codes` arrays that define the satisfaction test of §2.7, a `nurse_patient_ratio_denominator` greater than zero giving the number of patients one nurse may cover at that level (the network's scale runs from 1.0 at `LEVEL_IV` up to 4.0 at `LEVEL_I`), a `min_annual_volume` of at least one admission expected of a `REGIONAL_CENTER` at that level, a `transport_capability_required` boolean, a `min_compliance_score` needed for full designation at the level, and a `conditional_floor_score` needed for a conditional designation one level below. Only one standard may exist per level; a second declaration for the same `level` is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_STANDARD`. Standards are immutable and carry no version, as noted in §2.4, and may be listed filtered by `level`. An empty `required_credential_codes` array scores full marks for credential coverage, and an empty `required_equipment_codes` array scores full marks for equipment coverage — this is how `LEVEL_I`, which demands nothing, is satisfied by every unit.

Against those standards a unit records what it holds. A credential is recorded with the `unit_id` that holds it, a `credential_code` drawn from `NEO_BOARD`, `NNP`, `RN_NICU_CORE`, `RT_NEO`, `TRANSPORT_RN`, `LACTATION_IBCLC` or `DEV_CARE_CERT`, a de-identified `holder_reference`, an `fte` strictly greater than zero giving the full-time-equivalent that holder contributes, and an `expires_on` expiry instant. Its `status` is derived as described in §2.5, and `revoked_at` is null until a revocation stamps it. Revoking a credential sets `revoked_at` to the current clock instant, which makes the derived `status` `REVOKED`, and then re-derives the unit's `capability_level`, `effective_level` and `staffing_compliant`; if `effective_level` drops as a result, the capability cascade of §3.8 runs and the unit's `version` advances by one — where the effective level did not fall, no version is spent on the unit at all. Revocation is permitted whatever the credential's derived `status`, so revoking an already-expired or already-revoked credential is accepted and simply re-stamps `revoked_at` to the current instant. Credentials may be listed filtered by `unit_id`, by `credential_code` and by `status`, and reading one re-derives its `status` against the clock.

Equipment is recorded the same way: the owning `unit_id`, an `equipment_code` drawn from `CONVENTIONAL_VENT`, `HFOV`, `INHALED_NO`, `THERAPEUTIC_HYPOTHERMIA`, `TRANSPORT_ISOLETTE` or `ECMO_CIRCUIT`, a biomed `asset_tag`, the `last_service_at` instant at which preventive maintenance was last completed, and a `service_interval_days` interval of at least one day. Completing preventive maintenance supplies a `serviced_at` instant, which becomes the asset's new `last_service_at`, and clears both `out_of_service_at` and `out_of_service_reason` — after which `next_service_due_at` and the derived `status` are recomputed along with the unit's `capability_level`, `effective_level` and `equipment_compliant`. Both postings are permitted whatever the asset's derived `status`, so servicing an asset that was never out of service, or taking out of service one that already is, is accepted rather than refused; and completing maintenance writes nothing to the unit itself and leaves its `version` alone, however much the derived capability picture improves. Taking an asset out of service supplies a `reason`, which is recorded in `out_of_service_reason`; the accepted values are `BIOMED_FAULT`, `RECALL`, `CONTAMINATION`, `AWAITING_PARTS` and `SCHEDULED_SERVICE`. Assets may be listed filtered by `unit_id`, by `equipment_code` and by `status`. One further asset field is written only by transport activity: `decontamination_ready_at` records the instant an isolette becomes re-available, is stamped by transport dispatch, arrival and abort as described in §3.6, and is null on any asset that has never carried a run.

### 3.2. Designation Review: Assessment, Verification and Certification

A `UnitDirector` opens a designation review for its own unit by naming the `unit_id`, the `requested_level` it is asking the network to grant, and the `assigned_nurse_count` — the number of nurses the unit reports as assigned to its current census, which may be zero but not negative. The review starts in `DRAFT` with `submission_round` at 0 and every score field at 0. Only one review may be open per unit at a time: if the unit already has a review in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `RETURNED` or `VERIFIED`, the attempt is refused with `ERR_REVIEW_ALREADY_OPEN`. Reviews may be listed filtered by `unit_id`, by `status` and by `requested_level`, and a single read exposes the frozen subscores described below.

Submitting the review moves it `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`, or `RETURNED → SUBMITTED` on a later round, and may optionally revise `assigned_nurse_count` in the same call. Two preconditions guard it: `submission_round` must be strictly below 2, and the unit's `designation_status` must not be `SUSPENDED` — a submission at a suspended unit is refused with `ERR_DEPENDENT_STATE_INVALID` (409), the unit being an entity other than the review. The rounds-exhausted branch described next is evaluated ahead of that suspension test, so a `RETURNED` review at round 2 takes the denial path even though the unit that exhausted its rounds is itself already suspended. A permitted submission increments `submission_round` by one, stamps `assessment_at` with the current clock instant, records the caller's `X-User-ID` as `submitted_by_user_id`, and freezes `required_nurse_count`, `staffing_subscore`, `equipment_subscore`, `volume_subscore` and `compliance_score` onto the review as they stand at that instant. Freezing matters: a later credential expiry does not retroactively change a submitted review's score, and the network assesses the snapshot.

The third submission is not a submission at all. When a review sitting in `RETURNED` already has `submission_round` equal to 2, the call moves it `RETURNED → DENIED` instead of to `SUBMITTED`: `submission_round` is set to 3, `denial_basis` is set to `ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`, `outcome_level` is left null, the unit's `designation_status` becomes `SUSPENDED`, and every referral whose `receiving_unit_id` is that unit and whose status is `PENDING`, `INFO_REQUESTED` or `DEFERRED` is moved to `DIVERTED` with `decision_reason` `CAPABILITY_LOST`. A unit that cannot get its paperwork right in two rounds stops receiving referrals.

The score the network assesses is built from four parts against the standard for the review's `requested_level`. Credential coverage is the count of that standard's `required_credential_codes` which the unit holds with derived `status` `VALID` and `fte` of at least 0.5, divided by the number of codes the standard lists; when the list is empty the coverage is treated as complete. A ratio factor is 1.0 when `required_nurse_count` is zero, and otherwise the lower of 1.0 and the reported `assigned_nurse_count` divided by `required_nurse_count`. `staffing_subscore` is 100 multiplied by the credential coverage and again by the ratio factor, rounded half up to two decimals. `equipment_subscore` is 100 multiplied by the count of the standard's `required_equipment_codes` held with derived `status` `IN_SERVICE` and divided by the number of codes listed, rounded half up to two decimals, and is 100.00 when the list is empty. For volume, the effective minimum is the standard's `min_annual_volume` for a unit whose `hospital_type` is `REGIONAL_CENTER`, and half of that figure for a `COMMUNITY` unit; `volume_subscore` is 100 multiplied by the lower of 1.0 and the unit's `annual_admission_volume` divided by that effective minimum, rounded half up to two decimals. `compliance_score` is then 0.45 of the staffing subscore plus 0.35 of the equipment subscore plus 0.20 of the volume subscore, rounded half up to two decimals. Finally, and only after that total has been computed, one cap applies: when the standard sets `transport_capability_required` to true and the unit's `transport_capable` is false, `compliance_score` becomes the lower of its computed value and 69.99. A level that owes the region a transport team can never be fully designated without one, and the cap sits deliberately just beneath a conditional floor of 70.00. Where no standard has been declared for the review's `requested_level` there is nothing to score against: every subscore and `compliance_score` alike read 100.00, the transport cap does not apply, and both the `min_compliance_score` and the `conditional_floor_score` the later gates compare against read 0.

Returning a submission moves it `SUBMITTED → RETURNED` and records the supplied `return_reason` free text; the caller's `X-User-ID` must differ from `submitted_by_user_id`. Verification moves it `SUBMITTED → VERIFIED` and records the caller as `verified_by_user_id`; it requires that `compliance_score` be at or above the `conditional_floor_score` of the standard for `requested_level`, and again that the caller differ from `submitted_by_user_id`. A review whose frozen score sits below the conditional floor cannot be verified at all — the network refuses it with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409) rather than passing it forward, and that score test is made after the `SUBMITTED` status check but ahead of the segregation-of-duties comparison, so a below-floor review presented by its own author is reported as unverifiable rather than as a duties violation.

Certification has two outcomes, decided by where the frozen score sits. From `VERIFIED`, certification moves the review `VERIFIED → CERTIFIED` and records the caller as `certified_by_user_id`, which must differ from `verified_by_user_id`. `outcome_level` becomes `requested_level` when `compliance_score` is at or above the standard's `min_compliance_score`, and otherwise the level one rung below `requested_level` — which, because nothing sits below `LEVEL_I`, means a `LEVEL_I` request scoring under its minimum has an outcome equal to its request. The unit's `designated_level` is then written to that `outcome_level`, and its `designation_status` becomes `DESIGNATED` when the outcome equals the requested level and `CONDITIONAL` when it is the rung below, so that `LEVEL_I` case is certified `DESIGNATED` however low its score, having nowhere further to fall. The unit's `effective_level` is re-derived from the new designated level, and — because a certification can lower a unit as well as raise it — every referral of that unit already in `ACCEPTED` whose `required_level` now outranks the new `effective_level` is moved to `DIVERTED` with `decision_reason` `CAPABILITY_LOST`, and its `HELD` reservation is moved to `RELEASED` with `released_reason` `CAPABILITY_LOST`. The unit's `version` advances by one. From `SUBMITTED`, the same certification call instead moves the review `SUBMITTED → DENIED` when `compliance_score` is strictly below the standard's `conditional_floor_score`: `denial_basis` becomes `SCORE_BELOW_FLOOR`, `outcome_level` stays null, and the caller must differ from `submitted_by_user_id`. A `SUBMITTED` review whose score is at or above that floor cannot be certified directly: the call is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409), because such a review must be verified first. So a review requesting `LEVEL_III` against a standard with a minimum of 85.00 and a floor of 70.00 is designated at `LEVEL_III` on a score of 96.00, granted `CONDITIONAL` at `LEVEL_II` on a score of 84.75, and denied on either 55.00 or on the 69.99 the transport cap produces. `CERTIFIED` and `DENIED` are both terminal.

### 3.3. Referral Intake and Risk-Appropriate Level Determination

A `ReferringClinician` opens a case by naming the `referring_unit_id` it acts for and the `receiving_unit_id` it is asking to accept, a `referral_kind` of `INBOUND_NEONATAL`, `MATERNAL_ANTENATAL` or `BACK_TRANSFER`, a de-identified `case_reference`, a `gestational_age_weeks` decimal between 20.0 and 44.0 inclusive, a `birth_weight_grams` that is a whole number of grams of at least 300 (for an antenatal case this is the estimated fetal weight), and a `support_modality` of `ROOM_AIR`, `CPAP`, `CONVENTIONAL_VENT`, `HFOV`, `INHALED_NO` or `ECMO_CONSULT`. Two inputs are optional: `diagnoses`, an array drawn from `RDS_SEVERE`, `HIE_MODERATE`, `PROM`, `CHD_DUCTAL` and `SURGICAL_NEC`, defaulting to an empty array; and `planned_surgery`, a boolean defaulting to false. The new referral starts at status `PENDING` with `info_request_rounds` at 0 and `decision_reason`, `requested_items`, `source_admission_id`, `superseded_by_referral_id` and `reservation_id` all null.

The weight the level calculation actually consults is `weight_basis_grams`: it is the source admission's `current_weight_grams` when `referral_kind` is `BACK_TRANSFER`, and the referral's own `birth_weight_grams` otherwise. Four intermediate terms are then derived. The **weight term** is `LEVEL_III` when the basis weight is below 1500 grams, `LEVEL_II` when it is at least 1500 and below 1800 grams, and `LEVEL_I` at 1800 grams or more. The **support term** maps `ROOM_AIR` to `LEVEL_I`, `CPAP` to `LEVEL_II`, `CONVENTIONAL_VENT` to `LEVEL_III`, `HFOV` to `LEVEL_III`, `INHALED_NO` to `LEVEL_IV` and `ECMO_CONSULT` to `LEVEL_IV`. The **gestational-age term** is `LEVEL_III` below 32.0 weeks, `LEVEL_II` from 32.0 weeks up to but not including 35.0 weeks, and `LEVEL_I` at 35.0 weeks or more. The **diagnosis term** maps `RDS_SEVERE` to `LEVEL_III`, `HIE_MODERATE` to `LEVEL_IV`, `CHD_DUCTAL` to `LEVEL_IV`, `SURGICAL_NEC` to `LEVEL_IV` and `PROM` to `LEVEL_I`.

Two level variants are then computed from those terms. `current_support_level` — the level demanded by findings that already exist — is the higher-ranking of the support term and the weight term, except that for a `MATERNAL_ANTENATAL` referral the weight contribution is forced to `LEVEL_I`, because an unborn infant has no current weight-driven support need and its estimated fetal weight belongs to the anticipated variant only. `anticipated_need_level` — the level demanded once anticipated needs are projected forward — is the highest-ranking of: `current_support_level`; the weight term; the gestational-age term, but only when `referral_kind` is not `BACK_TRANSFER`; the diagnosis term for every code present in `diagnoses`; and `LEVEL_IV` when `planned_surgery` is true. The gestational-age suppression for back-transfers is deliberate: a back-transfer is judged on how the infant is now, never on how premature it was born, or no ex-preemie could ever go home to a community hospital.

`required_level` is always `anticipated_need_level`. `level_basis` records which variant drove the answer: it is `ANTICIPATED_NEED` when `anticipated_need_level` outranks `current_support_level`, and `CURRENT_SUPPORT` otherwise — including when the two are equal. `maternal_transfer_preferred` is true exactly when `referral_kind` is `MATERNAL_ANTENATAL` and `required_level` ranks at `LEVEL_III` or above. So an inbound neonatal case at 35.0 weeks and 2400 grams on `CPAP` with surgery planned computes `LEVEL_II` current, `LEVEL_IV` anticipated and a basis of `ANTICIPATED_NEED`; an inbound case at 29.0 weeks and 1200 grams in `ROOM_AIR` computes `LEVEL_III` on both variants and a basis of `CURRENT_SUPPORT`; an antenatal case at 30.0 weeks with an estimated fetal weight of 1300 grams computes `LEVEL_I` current and `LEVEL_III` anticipated with `maternal_transfer_preferred` true; a back-transfer at 29.0 weeks now weighing 1900 grams in `ROOM_AIR` computes `LEVEL_I`; and an antenatal case at 33.0 weeks and 2000 grams in `ROOM_AIR` computes `LEVEL_II` with `maternal_transfer_preferred` false, because the level sits below `LEVEL_III`.

`triage_priority`, which ranks the deferral queue, is a weighted score out of 100. Level points are 25 for `LEVEL_I`, 50 for `LEVEL_II`, 75 for `LEVEL_III` and 100 for `LEVEL_IV`, taken from `required_level`. Prematurity points are 100 when `gestational_age_weeks` is below 28.0, 75 from 28.0 up to but not including 32.0, 50 from 32.0 up to but not including 35.0, and 25 at 35.0 weeks or more. Weight points, read from `weight_basis_grams`, are 100 below 1000 grams, 75 from 1000 up to but not including 1500, 50 from 1500 up to but not including 1800, and 25 at 1800 grams or more. Kind points are 100 for `MATERNAL_ANTENATAL`, 50 for `INBOUND_NEONATAL` and 0 for `BACK_TRANSFER`. The priority is 0.40 of the level points plus 0.25 of the prematurity points plus 0.20 of the weight points plus 0.15 of the kind points, rounded half up to two decimals. An antenatal `LEVEL_III` case at 30.0 weeks and 1300 grams scores 78.75; an inbound `LEVEL_III` case at 29.0 weeks and 1200 grams scores 71.25, as does an inbound `LEVEL_III` case at 30.0 weeks and 1250 grams — ties are real and are broken by the rules in §3.4. An inbound `LEVEL_III` case at 31.0 weeks and 1600 grams scores 66.25, an inbound `LEVEL_IV` case at 26.0 weeks and 850 grams scores 92.50, and a back-transfer at `LEVEL_I`, 29.0 weeks and 1900 grams scores 33.75.

Where the receiving unit needs more data before deciding, its `IntakeNeonatologist` asks for it, moving the referral `PENDING → INFO_REQUESTED` or `DEFERRED → INFO_REQUESTED` and recording the free-text `requested_items` it wants. This loop is bounded at two completed rounds: the request is only permitted while `info_request_rounds` is strictly below 2, and a request made when `info_request_rounds` already equals 2 does not enter `INFO_REQUESTED` at all — it moves the referral to `DIVERTED` with `decision_reason` `INFO_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED` — `requested_items` is still required in the body of that call, but on the diverting path it is discarded rather than recorded, and the referral's own `requested_items` is left null. The referring side answers by supplying any of `support_modality`, `birth_weight_grams`, `gestational_age_weeks`, `diagnoses` and `planned_surgery`, each optional — an entirely empty body is a valid answer that consumes a round and changes no clinical value — which moves the referral `INFO_REQUESTED → PENDING`, increments `info_request_rounds` by one, clears `requested_items` back to null, applies the supplied clinical values, and then recomputes `required_level`, `level_basis`, `maternal_transfer_preferred` and `triage_priority` from the revised inputs — so an answer can raise or lower the level the case demands.

Two closing paths belong to this stage. The receiving unit may decline outright, moving the referral `PENDING → DECLINED` or `INFO_REQUESTED → DECLINED` and recording a `decision_reason` supplied by the caller as either `NO_CAPABILITY` or `CLINICAL_DECLINE`. The referring side may withdraw, moving the referral to `WITHDRAWN` from `PENDING`, `INFO_REQUESTED`, `DEFERRED` or `ACCEPTED` with `decision_reason` `FAMILY_CHOICE`; if a `HELD` reservation exists it is moved to `RELEASED` with `released_reason` `REFERRAL_WITHDRAWN`, and the receiving unit's `version` advances by one in exactly that case. `DECLINED`, `DIVERTED`, `WITHDRAWN`, `REVOKED`, `SUPERSEDED` and `COMPLETED` are all terminal statuses for a referral. Referrals may be listed filtered by `referring_unit_id`, `receiving_unit_id`, `source_admission_id`, `superseded_by_referral_id`, `status`, `referral_kind` and `required_level` — every foreign key is exposed as a filter so that the referring and receiving sides can each find their own work.

### 3.4. Acceptance, Bed Holds and the Deferral Queue

Acceptance is the moment a bed leaves the ledger, so it is the most heavily guarded operation in the system. An `IntakeNeonatologist` acting for the receiving unit accepts a referral sitting in `PENDING`, quoting `expected_unit_version` as the only body field. Five conditions must hold. The receiving unit's `designation_status` must not be `SUSPENDED`, or the acceptance is refused with `ERR_UNIT_SUSPENDED`. The unit's `effective_level` must rank at or above the referral's `required_level`, or the acceptance is refused with `ERR_LEVEL_CAPABILITY_INSUFFICIENT` — note that this reads the *effective* level of §2.7, so a unit designated `LEVEL_III` whose ventilator is out of service cannot accept a `LEVEL_III` case. The unit's `available_bed_count` must be at least 1, or the acceptance is refused with `ERR_NO_BED_AVAILABLE`. No strictly-higher-priority antenatal transfer may be waiting on the last free bed, as set out two paragraphs below. And `expected_unit_version` must match, per §2.6. They are not evaluated in the order narrated here but in the band order of §2.10: the version quote first of all, then the referral's own `PENDING` status, then the level test, then the suspension test, then the maternal preference, and the bed count last of all — so an acceptance at a suspended unit that is also out of beds is answered with `ERR_UNIT_SUSPENDED`, and one blocked by a waiting maternal transfer with `ERR_MATERNAL_TRANSFER_PREFERENCE` rather than with `ERR_NO_BED_AVAILABLE`.

A permitted acceptance moves the referral `PENDING → ACCEPTED`, clears any `decision_reason` a previous abort had left on it back to null, and creates a bed reservation for it, carrying the receiving unit as `unit_id` and the referral as `referral_id`, starting at status `HELD`, taking its `bed_class` from the waterfall rule of §2.8, stamping `held_at` with the current clock instant, and computing `expires_at` from the referral's kind: an antenatal hold lasts 720 minutes — twelve hours — from `held_at`, because a mother in labour needs a bed that will still be there tomorrow morning, while every other kind of hold lasts 240 minutes, four hours. The unit's `version` advances by one and its counters and `census_posture` are re-derived. The referral's `reservation_id` is a read-only pointer, derived from the reservations that name the referral rather than stored as a foreign key: it names the referral's current `HELD` or `OCCUPIED` reservation — the latest by `held_at` should more than one be live — and, when none is live, whichever of the referral's reservations has the latest `held_at`, `RELEASED` and `EXPIRED` counting alike for that fallback; it stays null while the referral has never been accepted. Exposing it on the acceptance response saves the caller a second read. Reservations themselves are read-only through the API and may be listed filtered by `unit_id`, `referral_id`, `status` and `bed_class`; a reservation's `status` is one of `HELD`, `OCCUPIED`, `RELEASED` or `EXPIRED`, and its `released_reason` — null while the hold is live — takes one of `ADMITTED`, `REVOKED`, `REFERRAL_WITHDRAWN`, `CAPABILITY_LOST`, `TRANSPORT_ABORTED` or `HOLD_EXPIRED`.

**The maternal transfer preference.** In utero transport is safer than postnatal transport, so the last free bed at a unit is reserved for the mother who can still be moved. When an acceptance would consume the last free bed — meaning `available_bed_count` immediately before the operation is exactly 1 — and the referral being accepted is not of kind `MATERNAL_ANTENATAL`, the unit must first look for a blocked maternal transfer: any referral at the same receiving unit whose status is `PENDING` or `DEFERRED`, whose `referral_kind` is `MATERNAL_ANTENATAL`, whose `required_level` ranks at or below the unit's `effective_level`, and whose `triage_priority` is **strictly greater** than the incoming referral's. If such a referral exists the acceptance is refused with `ERR_MATERNAL_TRANSFER_PREFERENCE`. Two boundaries are deliberate: equal priority does not block, and the rule does not apply at all when two or more beds are free. With one bed free, an inbound case scoring 66.25 is refused while an antenatal case scoring 78.75 waits; with two beds free the same pair is accepted; and with one bed free an incoming antenatal case scoring 66.25 is accepted even though a higher-scoring antenatal case waits, because the rule only guards non-maternal acceptances.

When no bed is free, the case is parked rather than refused. Deferral moves a referral `PENDING → DEFERRED`, is permitted only when the unit's `available_bed_count` is at or below zero — a deferral attempted while a bed is still free is refused with `ERR_DEPENDENT_STATE_INVALID` (409), the bed picture belonging to the unit rather than to the referral — records `decision_reason` `NO_BED`, and causes the unit's `deferral_queue_depth` to be re-derived. The queue can be read in the exact order in which it will be drained.

**Promotion ranking.** The candidate pool for promotion is every referral whose status is `DEFERRED`, whose `receiving_unit_id` is the unit in question, and whose `required_level` ranks at or below the unit's current `effective_level`. Referrals needing more than the unit can deliver are skipped — never promoted, and never turned into an error. The ranking over that pool is total and pinned: `triage_priority` descending first; then, among ties, referrals with `maternal_transfer_preferred` true ahead of those with it false; then, among remaining ties, `created_at` ascending. The resource `id` is never consulted as a tie-break. Promotion then loops: while the unit's committed `available_bed_count` is at least 1 and the pool is non-empty, the top-ranked candidate is promoted `DEFERRED → ACCEPTED`, a `HELD` reservation is created for it exactly as an ordinary acceptance would (same waterfall `bed_class`, same `held_at`, same kind-dependent `expires_at`), its `decision_reason` is cleared back to null, the unit's `version` advances by one, the ledger is re-derived, and the loop continues. An empty pool promotes nothing and is not an error. With a pool scoring 78.75, 71.25, 71.25 and 66.25 and one free bed, only the 78.75 referral is promoted and the rest stay `DEFERRED`; with two non-maternal referrals tied at 71.25, the one created earlier goes first.

Promotion is never called directly. It fires from exactly four places: the operational capacity sweep of §2.5; admission, at the source unit of a back-transfer; discharge; and surge escalation authorization. It deliberately does **not** fire from a transport abort or from a revocation of acceptance. In both of those cases the case that just freed the bed returns to the queue itself, and it must not be leapfrogged in the very call that released its own hold.

Revoking an acceptance gives the held bed back before transport is under way. It moves the referral `ACCEPTED → REVOKED`, requires `expected_unit_version`, and is refused with `ERR_DEPENDENT_STATE_INVALID` when any transport run against that referral's reservation is in `GROUND_ROLLING`, `AIR_LAUNCHED`, `ON_SCENE`, `STABILIZING` or `EN_ROUTE` — a crew already on the road cannot have its destination taken away. A permitted revocation moves the `HELD` reservation to `RELEASED` with `released_reason` `REVOKED`, aborts any run still sitting in `ACTIVATED` on that reservation with `abort_reason` `CAPABILITY_LOST`, and advances the unit's `version` by one. The referral's `decision_reason` is left exactly as it stood: `REVOKED` is recorded as the referral's status and as the reservation's `released_reason`, not as a decision reason.

Holds expire on the clock but are not rewritten by it. A reservation whose `expires_at` is at or before the current clock instant is expired — at exactly `expires_at` it is expired, one second earlier it is not — yet its stored `status` stays `HELD` until the capacity sweep moves it to `EXPIRED` with `released_reason` `HOLD_EXPIRED` and diverts its referral `ACCEPTED → DIVERTED` with `decision_reason` `HOLD_EXPIRED`. Until then the held bed still counts against `held_bed_count`, because a promise that has not yet been formally broken is still a promise.

### 3.5. Surge Escalation During a Census Surge

When a unit's census outruns its staffed bed target, its `UnitDirector` requests extra beds by naming the `unit_id`, the `requested_beds` count of at least one bed, and a window given by `effective_from` (inclusive) and `effective_to` (inclusive, and strictly after the start). The request opens at status `REQUESTED` and grants nothing until the network authorizes it: `census_ratio_at_request`, `escalation_tier`, `staffing_waiver_ratio` and `authorized_beds` are all null on a requested escalation, and a refused authorization writes none of them — every one stays null on an escalation that never reached `ACTIVE`. Escalations may be listed filtered by `unit_id`, by `status` and by `escalation_tier`.

Authorization is a `NetworkAdmin` decision and computes three things in order. First, `census_ratio_at_request` — the unit's `occupied_bed_count` divided by the sum of its `staffed_bed_target` and the `authorized_beds` of every *other* `ACTIVE` escalation at that unit, rounded half up to four decimals. Held beds are deliberately excluded from this ratio: a hold is a promise, not a patient, and the region does not open surge capacity for promises. Second, the tier: a ratio at or above 1.00 is `TIER_3`, a ratio at or above 0.90 is `TIER_2`, a ratio at or above 0.80 is `TIER_1`, and a ratio strictly below 0.80 refuses the authorization with `ERR_SURGE_THRESHOLD_NOT_MET`. The tier fixes `staffing_waiver_ratio` — 1.10 at `TIER_1`, 1.25 at `TIER_2`, 1.50 at `TIER_3` — which is the multiplier applied to the acuity-band denominators in the nurse calculation of §2.8, and it also fixes a bed cap of 2 beds at `TIER_1`, 4 beds at `TIER_2` and 6 beds at `TIER_3`.

Third, `authorized_beds`. The unit's nursing capacity is the sum of `fte` over its credentials whose `credential_code` is `RN_NICU_CORE` and whose derived `status` is `VALID`; that figure is multiplied by the tier's `staffing_waiver_ratio` and again by two beds per registered-nurse full-time equivalent, then rounded **down** to a whole number of beds. From that staffing capacity the unit's currently committed beds — its `occupied_bed_count` plus its `held_bed_count` — are subtracted. `authorized_beds` is then the lowest of three quantities: the `requested_beds` asked for, that staffing headroom, and the tier's bed cap — floored at zero so it is never negative. When `authorized_beds` comes out as zero the authorization is refused with `ERR_SURGE_NO_STAFFED_CAPACITY`. A unit with a target of 4, four occupied beds, 3.0 registered-nurse full-time equivalents and a request for 6 beds lands at a ratio of 1.0000, `TIER_3`, a staffing capacity of 9 beds and 5 authorized beds; a unit with a target of 10, eight occupied beds, 6.0 full-time equivalents and a request for 4 lands at a ratio of exactly 0.8000, `TIER_1`, a staffing capacity of 13 beds and 2 authorized beds — the tier cap binds, not the request. A unit with a target of 10 and seven occupied beds is at 0.7000 and is refused; a unit with a target of 4, three occupied beds and one held bed is at 0.7500 and is also refused, which is exactly the held-bed exclusion at work. A unit with a target of 4, four occupied beds, 1.0 full-time equivalent and a request for 2 reaches a staffing capacity of 3 beds, computes zero authorized beds, and is refused.

A unit's escalation windows may not overlap. If the requested `[effective_from, effective_to]` interval touches or overlaps — inclusively at both ends — the window of another escalation already `ACTIVE` at that unit, the authorization is refused with `ERR_ESCALATION_WINDOW_OVERLAP`. That overlap test is evaluated ahead of the ratio, tier and bed arithmetic just described, per §2.10 — an overlapping window is reported as such even where the ratio would also have fallen short of its threshold. A permitted authorization moves the escalation `REQUESTED → ACTIVE`, records the caller's `X-User-ID` as `authorized_by_user_id`, then re-derives the unit's `escalation_bed_count`, `available_bed_count`, `census_posture` and `required_nurse_count`, runs the deferral-queue promotion of §3.4, and advances the unit's `version` by one. The network may instead refuse the request outright, moving it `REQUESTED → DENIED` and recording the supplied `denial_reason` free text.

Two endings close an active escalation. Standing it down — available to both `NetworkAdmin` and the unit's own `UnitDirector` — moves it `ACTIVE → STOOD_DOWN` and re-derives the unit's `escalation_bed_count`, advancing the unit's `version` by one. This is the case where `available_bed_count` may legitimately go negative: with a target of 4, an active escalation of 2 beds and 6 occupied beds, a stand-down leaves the unit at minus 2 available beds and a `census_posture` of `CLOSED`. Alternatively the escalation simply runs out: the capacity sweep moves any `ACTIVE` escalation whose `effective_to` is strictly before the current clock instant to `EXPIRED` and re-derives the unit's `escalation_bed_count`. `DENIED`, `EXPIRED` and `STOOD_DOWN` are all terminal.

### 3.6. Neonatal Transport from Activation to Arrival

A `TransportCoordinator` activates a transport team against a bed that is already held. Activation names the `reservation_id` of the hold the run will fulfil, the `isolette_asset_id` of the transport isolette assigned to it, a `mode` of `GROUND`, `ROTOR_WING` or `FIXED_WING`, a `crew_composition` array of credential codes carried by the crew — any array of strings is accepted here, the entries not being checked against the credential-code enumeration of §3.1 — a planned `depart_at` instant and a planned `planned_return_at` instant strictly after it, and optionally `weather_minimums_met`, an aviation attestation defaulting to true. The run's `receiving_unit_id` is copied from the reservation rather than supplied, and the run opens at status `ACTIVATED`. The reservation's own status is not examined: a run may be activated against a hold that has already been released, expired or converted to `OCCUPIED`. Its `decontamination_minutes` is derived from the mode: 45 minutes for a `GROUND` run, and 90 minutes — an hour and a half — for both `ROTOR_WING` and `FIXED_WING`, because an aircraft cabin takes longer to turn round than an ambulance. Runs may be listed filtered by `reservation_id`, `receiving_unit_id`, `isolette_asset_id`, `status` and `mode`.

Three checks guard the isolette at activation. The asset must be a `TRANSPORT_ISOLETTE` whose derived `status` is `IN_SERVICE` — an asset that is `OUT_OF_SERVICE` or `SERVICE_DUE`, or one whose `equipment_code` is something other than a transport isolette, is refused with `ERR_ISOLETTE_NOT_SERVICEABLE`. The asset must belong to the same unit as the run's receiving unit, or the activation is refused with `ERR_ISOLETTE_UNIT_MISMATCH`; per §2.10 that ownership test sits a band later than the serviceability one, so an out-of-service isolette belonging to another unit is reported as unserviceable. And the isolette must be free for the whole interval the run will occupy it.

**The isolette occupation interval** runs from `depart_at` up to `planned_return_at` advanced by the run's `decontamination_minutes`, and it is half-open: the start instant is inside the interval and the end instant is not, because the isolette is clean again at exactly that moment. Two runs conflict when their occupation intervals overlap, and a conflict refuses the later activation with `ERR_ISOLETTE_UNAVAILABLE`. Only a run in status `ABORTED` drops out of the test outright. A run that has `ARRIVED` still holds the isolette, but over a shortened real interval: one that ends at its `arrived_at` advanced by `decontamination_minutes` rather than at the planned return, so an early arrival frees the isolette early and a late one holds it longer than planned. A ground run planned from 08:00 to 12:00 on a given day occupies the isolette through 12:45, so a new run departing at 12:30 conflicts while a new run departing at exactly 12:45 is allowed; a rotor-wing run over the same 08:00-to-12:00 window occupies through 13:30, so a new run departing at 13:00 conflicts.

Dispatch forks on mode, and the two branches are specified symmetrically. One endpoint serves both: it carries no body fields at all and reads the fork from the run's own stored `mode`, so there is no way to ask for the wrong branch and no mode-mismatch error exists. A ground dispatch moves the run `ACTIVATED → GROUND_ROLLING`; it requires `crew_composition` to contain `TRANSPORT_RN`, and requires the assigned isolette's derived `status` to be `IN_SERVICE`; it then sets that isolette's `decontamination_ready_at` to `planned_return_at` advanced by 45 minutes. An air dispatch — taken for a `mode` of either `ROTOR_WING` or `FIXED_WING` — moves the run `ACTIVATED → AIR_LAUNCHED`; it requires `crew_composition` to contain `TRANSPORT_RN`, requires the isolette's derived `status` to be `IN_SERVICE`, and requires `weather_minimums_met` to be true, tested in that order; it then sets that isolette's `decontamination_ready_at` to `planned_return_at` advanced by 90 minutes. A crew missing `TRANSPORT_RN` is refused with `ERR_CREW_COMPOSITION_INVALID`, an unserviceable isolette with `ERR_ISOLETTE_NOT_SERVICEABLE`, and an air dispatch attempted with `weather_minimums_met` false with `ERR_WEATHER_MINIMUMS_NOT_MET`.

Both branches join at the referring hospital: arriving on scene moves the run `GROUND_ROLLING → ON_SCENE` or `AIR_LAUNCHED → ON_SCENE` and has no side effects of its own. The crew then stabilizes the infant, which moves the run `ON_SCENE → STABILIZING` and may be repeated from `STABILIZING → STABILIZING` as the numbers improve. Each stabilization posts all four of `temp_c`, the axillary temperature; `glucose_mg_dl`, the point-of-care glucose; `mean_bp_mmhg`, the mean arterial pressure; and `spo2_pct`, the pre-ductal saturation — every one required on every posting, and each accepted as any number, unbounded above and below, since a physiologically implausible reading simply fails its target. Those four values overwrite whatever was stored before — this is a replacement, not an accumulation — and `stabilization_score` and `stabilization_bundle_complete` are then recomputed. Both start out null and false respectively on a newly activated run.

**The stabilization score** begins at 100 and loses 8 points for each of four targets the infant misses. The temperature target is satisfied when `temp_c` lies between 36.5 and 37.5 degrees inclusive at both ends. The glucose target is satisfied when `glucose_mg_dl` is at least 45. The blood-pressure target is satisfied when `mean_bp_mmhg` is at least the numeric value of the referral's `gestational_age_weeks` — the mean-pressure-equals-gestation rule of neonatal transport. The saturation target is satisfied when `spo2_pct` lies between 90 and 95 inclusive; it has an upper bound as well as a lower one, because hyperoxia harms a preterm retina. `stabilization_bundle_complete` is true when `stabilization_score` reaches at least 85, which means at most one failed target may be carried into transport. At 30.0 weeks gestation, values of 36.5, 45, 30 and 90 — every one of them exactly on its boundary — score 100 and the bundle is complete; 36.4, 44, 26 and 89 score 68 and it is not; 36.8, 60, 28 and 92 score 92 and the bundle is complete with only the mean pressure failing; and 37.6, 60, 31 and 96 score 84 and the bundle is incomplete, because temperature and saturation both overshoot.

Departure moves the run `STABILIZING → EN_ROUTE` and carries no body fields. It requires the referral behind the reservation still to be in `ACCEPTED`, otherwise `ERR_DEPENDENT_STATE_INVALID` — a crew must not set off toward a bed that has since been given away — and it requires `stabilization_bundle_complete` to be true, otherwise `ERR_STABILIZATION_INCOMPLETE`; per §2.10 the dependent-state test comes first, so a departure toward a diverted referral is reported as such even when the bundle is also incomplete. Arrival moves the run `EN_ROUTE → ARRIVED`, records the supplied `arrived_at` instant exactly as given — it is not required to fall after `depart_at` — and sets the isolette's `decontamination_ready_at` to that `arrived_at` advanced by the run's `decontamination_minutes`.

Aborting is available from every non-terminal status — `ACTIVATED`, `GROUND_ROLLING`, `AIR_LAUNCHED`, `ON_SCENE`, `STABILIZING` and `EN_ROUTE` — and moves the run to `ABORTED`. The caller supplies `abort_reason` as one of `WEATHER`, `CREW_UNAVAILABLE` or `PATIENT_DEMISE`; the same field also carries `CAPABILITY_LOST` and `HOLD_EXPIRED` when a cascade rather than a coordinator ends the run. An abort releases the reservation to `RELEASED` with `released_reason` `TRANSPORT_ABORTED`, returns its referral `ACCEPTED → PENDING` with `decision_reason` `TRANSPORT_ABORTED` so the case can be placed again — only where that referral is still `ACCEPTED`, a referral already carried elsewhere keeping the status it has — and sets the isolette's `decontamination_ready_at` to the current clock instant advanced by `decontamination_minutes`. The receiving unit's `version` does not advance on an abort, even though releasing the hold gives the bed back. As stated in §3.4, an abort deliberately does not run the deferral-queue promotion. `ARRIVED` and `ABORTED` are terminal.

### 3.7. Admission, Bedside Care, Milk Handling and Infection Surveillance

Admitting an infant converts a promise into a patient. The receiving unit's `IntakeNeonatologist` supplies only the `referral_id`, which must be a referral in `ACCEPTED`. The referral is moved `ACCEPTED → COMPLETED` and its `HELD` reservation to `OCCUPIED` with `released_reason` `ADMITTED`; the bed has not been freed, it has changed category, and the ledger invariant of §2.8 holds throughout. The new admission takes its `unit_id` from the referral's `receiving_unit_id`, copies `gestational_age_weeks` and `birth_weight_grams` from the referral, initialises `current_weight_grams` to that birth weight and `support_modality` from the referral's, and sets `transport_run_id` to the run that brought the infant — the latest by `created_at` among the `ARRIVED` runs standing against the referral's live `HELD` reservation, and null both when no such run exists and when the referral has no live hold to look against at all. It opens at `care_status` `ADMITTED` and `infection_status` `SURVEILLANCE_CLEAR`, with `patient_days`, `central_line_days`, `ventilator_days`, `developmental_round_count`, `kangaroo_care_sessions`, `family_presence_minutes`, `cluster_care_compliant_count`, `light_cycling_compliant_count`, `developmental_bundle_score` and `milk_administered_ml` all at zero, `high_risk_graduate` false, and `back_transfer_source_admission_id`, `back_transfer_attestation_note`, `infection_confirmed_at`, `discharged_at` and `discharge_disposition` all null. The unit's counters are re-derived and its `version` advances by one. Admissions may be listed filtered by `unit_id`, `referral_id`, `transport_run_id`, `back_transfer_source_admission_id`, `care_status`, `infection_status` and `current_care_level`.

An admission carries **two independent state dimensions**, and this separation is deliberate. `care_status` tracks the clinical journey through `ADMITTED`, `STABILIZED`, `BACK_TRANSFER_READY`, `BACK_TRANSFER_REQUIRED`, `TRANSFERRED`, `DISCHARGED` and `DECEASED`, of which the last three are terminal. `infection_status` tracks surveillance through `SURVEILLANCE_CLEAR`, `SUSPECTED`, `CONFIRMED_CLABSI` and `RESOLVED`, none of which is terminal. Any value of one dimension may coexist with any value of the other, and a transition on one dimension never moves the other.

`current_care_level` is re-derived from the bedside record as the higher-ranking of the weight term applied to `current_weight_grams` and the support term applied to `support_modality`, using exactly the weight and support mappings of §3.3. This is the level that the unit's acuity bands count, and the level that the capability cascade of §3.8 compares against a fallen `effective_level`.

A `BedsideNurse` posts a day of care by supplying `current_weight_grams`, a whole number of grams of at least one, and `support_modality`, plus two optional booleans, `central_line_present` and `ventilated`, each defaulting to false. The posting is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409) once the admission has reached `TRANSFERRED`, `DISCHARGED` or `DECEASED` — the three terminal care statuses take no further bedside record. Each posting increments `patient_days` by one; it increments `central_line_days` by one when `central_line_present` was posted true, and `ventilator_days` by one when `ventilated` was posted true. The posted weight and support replace the stored values, `current_care_level` is re-derived, and the admission's `version` advances by one. One care-status consequence rides along: when the posted `support_modality` is `ROOM_AIR` or `CPAP` and the admission is in `ADMITTED`, the posting moves it `ADMITTED → STABILIZED`. An infant already `STABILIZED` stays there, and a posting of a heavier respiratory support does not push a stabilized infant back.

Developmental care is recorded as rounds. Each round posts all four of `kangaroo_care_minutes`, `family_presence_minutes`, `cluster_care_compliant` and `light_cycling_compliant`, none of them optional, the two minute counts being whole numbers of at least zero; and, exactly as with a care update, the round is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409) once the admission is `TRANSFERRED`, `DISCHARGED` or `DECEASED`. Posting a round increments `developmental_round_count` by one; it increments `kangaroo_care_sessions` by one only when the round's `kangaroo_care_minutes` is at least 60 — a session that short of an hour does not count as skin-to-skin care; it adds the round's `family_presence_minutes` to the admission's running total of the same name; it increments `cluster_care_compliant_count` by one when `cluster_care_compliant` was true; and it increments `light_cycling_compliant_count` by one when `light_cycling_compliant` was true. The admission's `version` advances by one.

**The developmental bundle score** is then recomputed from those aggregates, and it is 0.00 whenever `developmental_round_count` is zero. Kangaroo points are the lower of 1.0 and `kangaroo_care_sessions` divided by 3, multiplied by 100. Family points are the lower of 1.0 and the total `family_presence_minutes` divided by 720 minutes — twelve hours of presence earns full marks — multiplied by 100. Cluster points are `cluster_care_compliant_count` divided by `developmental_round_count`, multiplied by 100, and light points are `light_cycling_compliant_count` divided by `developmental_round_count`, multiplied by 100. `developmental_bundle_score` is 0.35 of the kangaroo points plus 0.25 of the family points plus 0.20 of the cluster points plus 0.20 of the light points, rounded half up to two decimals. Two rounds each of 60 kangaroo minutes, 240 family minutes, cluster compliance true and light cycling false produce exactly 60.00 — the boundary at which back-transfer readiness passes. The same two rounds with light cycling true produce 80.00. A single such round produces 40.00 and readiness is refused. One round of no kangaroo care, 120 family minutes, cluster true and light false produces 24.17. Three rounds of 60 kangaroo minutes and 240 family minutes with cluster true throughout and light cycling true on the last produce 86.67.

The infection dimension advances on an `event` supplied as `SUSPECT`, `CONFIRM_CLABSI` or `RESOLVE`, and may be posted by a `BedsideNurse` or by the unit's `UnitDirector`. `SUSPECT` moves the admission `SURVEILLANCE_CLEAR → SUSPECTED` or `RESOLVED → SUSPECTED`, and quarantines the infant's feed: every milk batch for that admission whose status is `RECEIVED`, `VERIFIED` or `PARTIALLY_ADMINISTERED` is moved to `QUARANTINED`, with its previous status preserved in `pre_quarantine_status`. `CONFIRM_CLABSI` moves the admission `SUSPECTED → CONFIRMED_CLABSI` and requires `central_line_days` to be at least 1 — a central-line-associated infection is definitionally impossible without a line day on the record, and a confirmation against a line-free record is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409) — and stamps `infection_confirmed_at` with the current clock instant, which is the timestamp surveillance periods filter on. `RESOLVE` moves the admission `SUSPECTED → RESOLVED` or `CONFIRMED_CLABSI → RESOLVED` and replays the quarantine: every `QUARANTINED` batch returns to the status recorded in its `pre_quarantine_status` — falling back to `RECEIVED` where no previous status was ever recorded — after which `pre_quarantine_status` is cleared back to null and the volume ledger of the paragraphs below re-derives the batch's status from the restored one. Each infection event advances the admission's `version` by one.

Milk is received against an infant, not against a unit. A `BedsideNurse` records a batch with the `admission_id` the milk is labelled for, a `donor_type` of `MOTHERS_OWN` or `DONOR_PASTEURIZED`, a `volume_ml` strictly greater than zero at one decimal place, the `expressed_at` instant of expression (or of thaw, for donor milk), and the `label_code` printed on the container for two-person verification. The caller's `X-User-ID` is stored as `received_by_user_id`, the batch opens at status `RECEIVED` with `verified_by_user_id` null, and `expires_at` is derived from the donor type: 96 hours — four days — after `expressed_at` for `MOTHERS_OWN`, and 24 hours after `expressed_at` for `DONOR_PASTEURIZED`. Receiving milk does not examine the admission's `care_status` at all: a batch may be booked in against an admission that has already been transferred, discharged or died, the gate on that sitting at administration rather than at receipt. Batches may be listed filtered by `admission_id`, `status` and `donor_type`.

A second nurse verifies the batch by presenting the `label_code`. The batch must still be in exactly `RECEIVED`, and a batch that has since been quarantined, discarded or already verified is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409) ahead of both checks that follow. If the presented code differs from the stored one the verification is refused with `ERR_MILK_LABEL_MISMATCH`; if the verifying `X-User-ID` equals `received_by_user_id` it is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, the label comparison being made first of the two. A successful verification records the caller as `verified_by_user_id` and moves the batch `RECEIVED → VERIFIED`.

**The milk volume ledger** is a conserved three-way split. `administered_ml` is the sum of volumes administered less the sum of volumes reversed; `discarded_ml` is the sum of volumes discarded; and `remaining_ml` is `volume_ml` less `administered_ml` less `discarded_ml`, at one decimal place. After every operation, including after a reversal, `volume_ml` must equal `administered_ml` plus `discarded_ml` plus `remaining_ml`. The batch's `status` is derived from that split: `DISCARDED` when `discarded_ml` is above zero and `remaining_ml` is zero; otherwise the stored status where that stored status is `QUARANTINED`, a quarantine outranking the two administration arms below it and persisting until a discard empties the container; otherwise `ADMINISTERED` when `remaining_ml` is zero and `administered_ml` is above zero; `PARTIALLY_ADMINISTERED` when `administered_ml` is above zero while `remaining_ml` is still above zero; and otherwise whatever status was last stored, meaning `RECEIVED` or `VERIFIED`. A reversal that brings `administered_ml` back to zero restores the batch to `VERIFIED`, so the compensation leaves no drift.

Administering a volume from a batch posts `volume_ml` and is guarded four ways, evaluated in the order the four are given here — admission first, then administrability, then expiry, then volume. The batch must be in `VERIFIED` or `PARTIALLY_ADMINISTERED`; a batch still `RECEIVED` and therefore unverified, or `QUARANTINED`, `DISCARDED` or already fully `ADMINISTERED`, is refused with `ERR_MILK_NOT_ADMINISTRABLE`. The batch must not have expired: an `expires_at` at or before the current clock instant is refused with `ERR_MILK_EXPIRED`, and at exactly `expires_at` the batch is already expired. The posted volume must not exceed `remaining_ml`, and a reversal's posted volume must not exceed `administered_ml`; either overrun is refused with `ERR_MILK_VOLUME_EXCEEDED`. And the admission must not be `DISCHARGED` or `DECEASED`, or the administration is refused with `ERR_DEPENDENT_STATE_INVALID`. Only the first of those four guards the compensating reversal, which is a correction of the record rather than a feed: a reversal is refused only when its volume exceeds `administered_ml`, and is otherwise permitted whatever the batch's status, whether or not the batch has expired, and whatever the admission's `care_status`. Every administration and every reversal recomputes the batch's ledger and status, recomputes the admission's `milk_administered_ml` as the sum of `administered_ml` over that admission's batches at one decimal place, and advances the admission's `version` by one. A 120.0 ml batch with 40.0 ml administered reports 40.0 administered, 80.0 remaining, status `PARTIALLY_ADMINISTERED` and an admission total of 40.0; reversing that 40.0 ml returns 0.0 administered, 120.0 remaining, status `VERIFIED` and an admission total of 0.0; a subsequent discard of the remainder reports 120.0 discarded, 0.0 remaining and status `DISCARDED`.

Discarding takes a `reason` of `EXPIRED`, `LABEL_MISMATCH`, `QUARANTINE_DISPOSAL` or `FAMILY_REQUEST` — validated against that enumeration but not retained on the batch, which exposes no discard-reason field — moves the whole `remaining_ml` into `discarded_ml`, leaves `remaining_ml` at zero, clears `pre_quarantine_status` back to null so a later resolution has nothing to replay, and lands the batch at `DISCARDED`. It is permitted from every other status, `QUARANTINED` included; only a batch already in `DISCARDED` is refused, with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409).

### 3.8. Back-Transfer, Discharge and the Capability Cascade

Risk-appropriate care means moving an infant back toward home as soon as the regional centre is no longer necessary. The receiving unit's `IntakeNeonatologist` attests that readiness, moving the admission `STABILIZED → BACK_TRANSFER_READY`. Four conditions must all hold, and they are tested in the order given: `developmental_bundle_score` must be at least 60, and a lower score is refused with `ERR_DEVELOPMENTAL_BUNDLE_INCOMPLETE`; `support_modality` must be `ROOM_AIR` or `CPAP`, and heavier support is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409); `infection_status` must not be `CONFIRMED_CLABSI`, because an active central-line infection is not something to hand to a community nursery, and such an admission is likewise refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409); and a `back_transfer_attestation_note` must be present in the request, which is then recorded on the admission as the risk-appropriateness attestation.

There is a second, involuntary route into the back-transfer family of states. When a unit loses capability and an admitted infant's `current_care_level` now outranks the unit's new `effective_level`, the admission is moved `ADMITTED → BACK_TRANSFER_REQUIRED` or `STABILIZED → BACK_TRANSFER_REQUIRED` by the cascade described below. `BACK_TRANSFER_READY` is a clinical achievement; `BACK_TRANSFER_REQUIRED` is a capability failure. Both are equally valid starting points for the onward referral.

Creating that onward referral requires `expected_admission_version` — see §2.6 — together with the `receiving_unit_id` of the community hospital being asked to take the infant and a `case_reference` for the new case. The source admission must be in `BACK_TRANSFER_READY` or `BACK_TRANSFER_REQUIRED`, or the creation is refused with `ERR_DEPENDENT_STATE_INVALID`. The operation creates a referral of kind `BACK_TRANSFER` with `source_admission_id` pointing back at the admission and `referring_unit_id` set to the admission's own unit, copying `gestational_age_weeks`, `birth_weight_grams` and `support_modality` from the admission and opening with an empty `diagnoses` array and `planned_surgery` false — the original case's diagnoses and surgical plan are not carried across — and it answers with the new referral itself, at HTTP 201, rather than with the admission it was created from; its `weight_basis_grams` is taken from the admission's `current_weight_grams` rather than from the birth weight, and the gestational-age term is suppressed in its level calculation exactly as §3.3 specifies. Any earlier back-transfer referral from the same admission still sitting in `PENDING` or `DEFERRED` is moved to `SUPERSEDED`, with its `superseded_by_referral_id` set to the new referral's `id` and any `HELD` reservation it holds released with `released_reason` `REVOKED` — a release that gives a bed back at the superseded referral's receiving unit without advancing that unit's `version`, since the contested resource here is the admission. A second onward plan supersedes the first rather than competing with it. The admission's `version` advances by one.

From there the back-transfer referral behaves like any other referral — it is accepted, deferred, declined or diverted by the community hospital under the rules of §3.4 — with one difference at admission time. When the community hospital admits against a `BACK_TRANSFER` referral, the new admission's `back_transfer_source_admission_id` is set to the regional-centre admission it continues, that pointer being written whenever the referral's `source_admission_id` resolves at all, whatever state the source is in. The source admission itself, however, moves only from the two back-transfer statuses: `BACK_TRANSFER_READY → TRANSFERRED` or `BACK_TRANSFER_REQUIRED → TRANSFERRED`, with `discharged_at` stamped at the current clock instant and `discharge_disposition` set to `BACK_TRANSFERRED`, while a source already `TRANSFERRED` or otherwise closed is left exactly as it stands and its unit's queue is not drained a second time. The source unit — not just the admitting one — then has its bed counters, `census_posture` and `required_nurse_count` re-derived, has its deferral queue promoted, and has its `version` advanced by one. That is the moment a regional bed genuinely frees, so that is where the queue drains.

Discharge closes an admission and takes a `disposition` of `HOME` or `DECEASED`. A discharge home moves the admission `STABILIZED → DISCHARGED` or `BACK_TRANSFER_READY → DISCHARGED`, and is permitted only when every milk batch belonging to the admission has been dealt with: each must be in status `ADMINISTERED`, in status `DISCARDED`, or still in `RECEIVED` with `remaining_ml` at zero. An unsettled fridge is refused with `ERR_DEPENDENT_STATE_INVALID` (409), the batches being entities other than the admission, and that test is made after the admission's own status has been checked. No milk may be left unaccounted for in a fridge after the infant has gone home. The discharge stamps `discharged_at` with the current clock instant, sets `discharge_disposition` to `HOME`, re-derives `high_risk_graduate` from the eligibility rules of §3.9, then re-derives the unit's counters, promotes its deferral queue, and advances its `version` by one.

Recording a death is the sibling branch of the same operation, taken when `disposition` is `DECEASED`, and it is reachable from a wider set of states: `ADMITTED`, `STABILIZED`, `BACK_TRANSFER_READY` and `BACK_TRANSFER_REQUIRED` all move to `DECEASED`. It likewise stamps `discharged_at` at the current clock instant and sets `discharge_disposition` to `DECEASED`, but it does not re-derive `high_risk_graduate` as the discharge-home branch does: whatever value that flag already carried is left standing, since there is no graduate to follow up. It carries three further consequences that the discharge-home branch does not: every milk batch of that admission which is not already in a terminal state is moved to `DISCARDED` with its `remaining_ml` transferred into `discarded_ml`; every follow-up enrollment for that admission sitting in `ENROLLED` or `SCHEDULED` is moved to `LOST_TO_FOLLOWUP`; and, as with a discharge home, the unit's counters are re-derived, its deferral queue is promoted and its `version` advances by one. Note that a death does not require the milk-accounting precondition — it settles the milk itself.

**The capability cascade.** Taking an equipment asset out of service is the sharpest edge in this system, because it can invalidate commitments the unit has already made. In one operation, the asset's `out_of_service_at` is stamped at the current clock instant, making its derived `status` `OUT_OF_SERVICE`; the unit's `equipment_compliant`, `capability_level` and `effective_level` are re-derived; a `designation_status` of `PROVISIONAL` or `DESIGNATED` becomes `CONDITIONAL` when the asset was required for the unit's `designated_level`; every referral at that unit whose status is `PENDING`, `INFO_REQUESTED`, `DEFERRED` or `ACCEPTED` and whose `required_level` now outranks the new `effective_level` is moved to `DIVERTED` with `decision_reason` `CAPABILITY_LOST`, with its `HELD` reservation moved to `RELEASED` with `released_reason` `CAPABILITY_LOST` and any non-terminal transport run against that reservation moved to `ABORTED` with `abort_reason` `CAPABILITY_LOST`; every admission at that unit whose `care_status` is `ADMITTED` or `STABILIZED` and whose `current_care_level` now outranks the new `effective_level` is moved to `BACK_TRANSFER_REQUIRED`; and the unit's `version` advances by one. That version increment is unconditional on this path: an out-of-service posting advances the unit's `version` whether or not `effective_level` actually fell and whether or not any referral or admission was disturbed. Revoking a credential runs the same propagation: `capability_level`, `effective_level` and `staffing_compliant` are re-derived, and where `effective_level` drops, the same referral, reservation, transport and admission consequences follow. Returning the asset to service reverses the derivation — `capability_level`, `effective_level` and `equipment_compliant` climb back — but it does not un-divert the referrals or un-require the back-transfers that the loss already caused.

### 3.9. Outcome Reporting and High-Risk Graduate Follow-Up

A `UnitDirector` opens a surveillance report for its unit by naming the `unit_id`, a `period_start` (inclusive) and a `period_end` (inclusive and strictly after the start), and the two denominators the unit has counted itself: `reported_patient_days` and `reported_central_line_days`, each at least zero. A period that begins before the unit itself was created is refused with `ERR_PERIOD_INVALID`. The report opens at status `DRAFT` with every metric of the next two paragraphs already computed against the census as it stands at that instant, rather than left null until submission, and reports may be listed filtered by `unit_id`, `status`, `network_benchmark_flag` and `variant_applied`.

Every metric on the report is computed rather than supplied. The **cohort** is the unit's admissions whose `created_at` falls inside the period, both ends inclusive, and `cohort_size` is its count. `clabsi_count` is deliberately not cohort-scoped: it is the count of the unit's admissions whose `infection_confirmed_at` falls inside the period, inclusive at both ends, whenever they were admitted. `mortality_count` is the count of the unit's admissions whose `care_status` is `DECEASED` and whose `discharged_at` falls inside the period. `birth_weight_risk_weight` is the mean, over the cohort, of a band weight applied to each admission's `birth_weight_grams`: 1.30 below 1000 grams, 1.15 from 1000 up to but not including 1500, 1.00 from 1500 up to but not including 2500, and 0.85 at 2500 grams or above — rounded half up to four decimals, and reported as 1.0000 for an empty cohort.

Two rate variants are then computed side by side. `crude_clabsi_rate` is `clabsi_count` multiplied by 1000 and divided by `reported_patient_days`, rounded half up to two decimals, and is 0.00 when that denominator is zero. `device_adjusted_clabsi_rate` is `clabsi_count` multiplied by `birth_weight_risk_weight`, multiplied by 1000, divided by `reported_central_line_days`, rounded half up to two decimals, and is likewise 0.00 when that denominator is zero. `variant_applied` records which of the two the network will judge: `DEVICE_ADJUSTED` when `reported_central_line_days` is above zero, and `CRUDE` otherwise. `data_completeness_flag` is `COMPLETE` when both `reported_patient_days` and `reported_central_line_days` are above zero, and `DENOMINATOR_MISSING` otherwise. `network_benchmark_flag` is `ABOVE_BENCHMARK` when the rate of the applied variant is strictly greater than the network benchmark of 1.50 infections per 1000 days, and `WITHIN_BENCHMARK` otherwise. One infection over 500 patient-days and 120 central-line-days, with a cohort of two infants weighing 900 and 2000 grams, yields a risk weight of 1.1500, a crude rate of 2.00, a device-adjusted rate of 9.58, a variant of `DEVICE_ADJUSTED`, a completeness of `COMPLETE` and a flag of `ABOVE_BENCHMARK`. The same data with zero central-line-days yields a device-adjusted rate of 0.00, a variant of `CRUDE`, a completeness of `DENOMINATOR_MISSING`, and still `ABOVE_BENCHMARK`, because the crude 2.00 exceeds 1.50. Zero infections over 500 patient-days and 120 line-days yields 0.00 on both rates and `WITHIN_BENCHMARK`. Two infections over 400 patient-days and 200 line-days with a single cohort infant of 850 grams yields a risk weight of 1.3000, a crude rate of 5.00 and a device-adjusted rate of 13.00.

Submission moves the report `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`, or `REJECTED → SUBMITTED` on a resubmission, stamps `submitted_at` with the current clock instant, and recomputes every metric against the census as it stands at that moment. It is permitted when the recomputed `data_completeness_flag` is `COMPLETE`, or when the caller passes `acknowledge_incomplete` as true — defaulting to false — and thereby accepts publishing a rate built on a missing denominator; an incomplete submission made without that acknowledgement is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409), and because the recomputation is judged before anything is written, such a refusal leaves the report's stored metrics exactly as they stood. The submitting user is recorded as `submitted_by_user_id` for the segregation comparison below but is not exposed on the report. The network then either accepts or rejects. Acceptance moves the report `SUBMITTED → ACCEPTED`, requires that the accepting `X-User-ID` differ from the submitting user, and writes the report's `network_benchmark_flag` onto the unit's `quality_flag`, which therefore mirrors the most recently accepted report and is either `WITHIN_BENCHMARK` or `ABOVE_BENCHMARK`; the unit's `version` advances only where that flag actually changed, an acceptance that confirms the flag already in force spending no version at all. Rejection moves the report `SUBMITTED → REJECTED` and records the supplied `rejection_reason` free text; from `REJECTED` the unit may submit again, and this loop is unbounded. `ACCEPTED` is the only terminal status.

Follow-up begins with eligibility, which is derived from the closed admission rather than declared — and re-derived, never frozen: an enrollment's `eligibility_basis` and `risk_tier` are recomputed from the admission behind it on every read and on every filtered listing, so a care update that changes the admission changes what the enrollment reports. `eligibility_basis` is the **first** match in a pinned order: `BW_UNDER_1500` when `birth_weight_grams` is below 1500; otherwise `GA_UNDER_32` when `gestational_age_weeks` is below 32.0; otherwise `HIE_COOLED` when the originating referral's `diagnoses` contain `HIE_MODERATE`; otherwise `SURGICAL_NEC` when those `diagnoses` contain `SURGICAL_NEC`; otherwise `VENT_OVER_7D` when `ventilator_days` is strictly greater than 7; and otherwise `NOT_ELIGIBLE`. `high_risk_graduate` on the admission is true exactly when `eligibility_basis` is anything other than `NOT_ELIGIBLE`. `risk_tier` is `HIGH` when `birth_weight_grams` is below 1000 or `eligibility_basis` is `HIE_COOLED`, and `MODERATE` otherwise. Because the order is pinned, a 1400-gram infant who was also cooled reports `BW_UNDER_1500` and therefore a `MODERATE` tier — the cooling never surfaces as the basis, so it never lifts the tier — unless that infant also weighed under 1000 grams. An 850-gram infant reports `BW_UNDER_1500` with a `HIGH` tier. A 2400-gram infant at 36.0 weeks with `HIE_MODERATE` recorded reports `HIE_COOLED` and a `HIGH` tier. The same infant with no diagnoses and 8 ventilator days reports `VENT_OVER_7D` and a `MODERATE` tier, while at exactly 7 ventilator days it reports `NOT_ELIGIBLE` and `high_risk_graduate` false.

A `FollowUpCoordinator` enrols a graduate by naming the `admission_id`, the `clinic_unit_id` of the unit running the clinic, and a `target_appointment_date` the family has asked for. The admission must be in `DISCHARGED` or `TRANSFERRED`, or the enrollment is refused with `ERR_DEPENDENT_STATE_INVALID`. An admission whose `eligibility_basis` is `NOT_ELIGIBLE` is refused with `ERR_NOT_ELIGIBLE_FOR_FOLLOWUP`. The nominated clinic must be either the admission's own unit or a `COMMUNITY` unit in the network, and a clinic that is neither is refused with `ERR_DEPENDENT_STATE_INVALID` (409) — a test made after the eligibility gate above it, so an ineligible graduate nominated at an impossible clinic is reported as ineligible. And an admission that already holds an enrollment in `ENROLLED` or `SCHEDULED` is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ENROLLMENT` — one active enrollment per graduate. A permitted enrollment opens at status `ENROLLED` with `confirmed_appointment_date` and `attendance_recorded_at` null, and with its appointment window derived from the admission: `appointment_window_start` is the admission's `discharged_at` advanced by 14 days, inclusive, and `appointment_window_end` is that same `discharged_at` — not the window start — advanced by 90 days, also inclusive, so the window spans 76 days. Enrollments may be listed filtered by `admission_id`, `clinic_unit_id`, `status`, `risk_tier` and `eligibility_basis`.

Scheduling confirms a slot. The coordinator supplies a `confirmed_appointment_date`, which must fall inside the window from `appointment_window_start` to `appointment_window_end` with both ends inclusive; outside it, the request is refused with `ERR_APPOINTMENT_OUTSIDE_WINDOW`. It must also fit the clinic's day. Bookings are counted per UTC calendar date: the count for a clinic and a date is the number of that clinic's enrollments in status `SCHEDULED` or `ATTENDED` whose `confirmed_appointment_date` falls on that calendar date, and scheduling is permitted only while that count is strictly below the clinic unit's `followup_clinic_daily_capacity`. A full day is refused with `ERR_CLINIC_DAY_FULL`, and because the comparison is strict a clinic that has declared a capacity of zero can never be scheduled into on any date at all. The limit is an aggregate per calendar day rather than per hour: appointments at 09:00 and at 17:00 on the same date consume the same day's capacity even though they never overlap in time. A permitted scheduling moves the enrollment `ENROLLED → SCHEDULED` and records the confirmed instant.

Attendance closes the enrollment. The coordinator posts `attended`: true moves the enrollment `SCHEDULED → ATTENDED`, false moves it `SCHEDULED → NO_SHOW`, and either branch stamps `attendance_recorded_at` with the current clock instant. An enrollment that was never scheduled lapses instead — the capacity sweep of §2.5 moves any enrollment still in `ENROLLED` whose `appointment_window_end` has fallen strictly behind the current clock instant to `LOST_TO_FOLLOWUP`. `ATTENDED`, `NO_SHOW` and `LOST_TO_FOLLOWUP` are all terminal.
