# Supply Chain Finance Dynamic Discounting API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Large corporate buyers sit on approved payables that will not be paid for another thirty, sixty or ninety days, while the suppliers who raised those payables are financing the same working-capital gap at rates far above the buyer's own cost of money. Dynamic discounting closes that gap: the buyer publishes the payables it has already approved, suppliers name the annualised rate at which they would accept cash today instead of on net terms, and whichever party finds the trade attractive takes it. This document specifies the backend service behind such a marketplace — a platform on which a buyer configures discounting programmes, ingests approved-payables extracts from its accounts-payable system, runs time-boxed auction windows in which rates are named, countered and accepted, and then moves cash either from its own balance sheet or from a third-party funder's.

The service is deliberately opinionated about the two failure modes that make these platforms expensive to operate. The first is money leaking through partial failure: a payment reserved against a funder's capital, executed, and then returned by the receiving bank must leave no residual encumbrance behind, and an invoice whose payable turns out to be disputed must release every rate, every reservation and every cent of committed capital that was standing behind it. Every quantity in this system that can be encumbered is therefore also fully recomputed from its constituent rows rather than adjusted by deltas, and every compensating action is specified down to the field. The second failure mode is a rate discovery process that quietly overpays. Accordingly, the platform measures its own adoption, derives a rate posture from it, and tightens each programme's acceptable APR ceiling as adoption proves the market will clear lower — a ceiling that, once tightened, never loosens again.

A third concern runs through the whole design: segregation of duties. Rate discovery and cash movement are separated into two buyer-side roles that authenticate as the same corporate identity but cannot perform each other's exclusive operations. A programme owner may negotiate a rate but never reserve the money; a treasury officer may move the money but never negotiate the rate. Identity verification, compliance holds and the platform's logical clock belong to a third, platform-side operator. Readers implementing this specification should treat the role boundaries as load-bearing business rules, not as decorations on top of the endpoint table.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Participants, Identity and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to this API carries two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the capacity in which that principal is acting. If either header is missing, empty, or if `X-User-Role` is not exactly one of the five recognised role names, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before anything else is examined. The five roles are `BuyerAdmin`, `BuyerTreasury`, `Supplier`, `Funder` and `PlatformOps`. When the headers are well formed but the acting role is not among the roles authorised for the endpoint being called, the rejection is `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

`BuyerAdmin` is the buyer-side programme owner: it configures programmes and auction windows, ingests approved-payables files, counters supplier rates, accepts supplier-named rates, and raises or resolves disputes. It owns rate discovery and never cash movement. `BuyerTreasury` is the buyer-side treasury officer: it reserves, executes, settles and classifies early payments, and owns cash movement and never rate discovery — it cannot configure a programme, counter an offer or accept a rate. `Supplier` is the selling party: it completes onboarding, registers and verifies a settlement bank account, names discount rates, re-names after a buyer counter, and accepts a buyer counter. `Funder` is a third-party capital provider: it commits capital tranches and pauses or withdraws its own participation, and never touches invoices, offers or programmes. `PlatformOps` is the platform operator: it onboards buyers, suppliers and funders, adjudicates identity verification, places and lifts compliance holds, drives the logical clock, and acts as settlement back-office; it cannot create programmes, name or accept rates, or reserve or execute payments.

Identity binds to persisted rows for four of the five roles. When `X-User-Role` is `BuyerAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting buyer, and ownership checks on any resource that references a buyer compare the resource's buyer foreign key to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `BuyerTreasury`, `X-User-ID` is likewise the `id` of the acting buyer, resolved through the same ownership chain — the two buyer-side roles share one corporate identity and differ only in their permitted operations. When `X-User-Role` is `Supplier`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting supplier, and ownership checks compare the addressed resource's supplier foreign key to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `Funder`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting funder, and ownership checks compare the addressed funder record's own `id`, or the funder foreign key on an addressed early payment, to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `PlatformOps`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it but never compares it to any entity's `id`, and a platform operator has read access to every collection.

One authorisation rule is not purely role-based and deserves separate emphasis. Acceptance of a discount offer is permitted to two roles, but which of the two may act depends on the offer's current negotiation state: a `BuyerAdmin` may accept an offer that is in `NAMED`, a `Supplier` may accept an offer that is in `COUNTERED`, and neither may accept the other's. A `BuyerAdmin` attempting to accept a `COUNTERED` offer, or a `Supplier` attempting to accept a `NAMED` offer, is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination, Filtering and List Ordering

Every endpoint that returns a collection accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum accepted value is 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and its minimum accepted value is 0. A value outside those bounds is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Ordering never uses `id`. Rows that were created inside a single request retain the order in which they were submitted, so the invoices of one approved-payables file appear in submitted-row order and the lines of one invoice appear in ascending `line_no` order. Cascades that touch several sibling rows in one request also process them in ascending `created_at` order, and this document states that ordering explicitly wherever it is observable.

For list responses, the envelope's `meta` object carries `limit` and `offset` as applied, plus `total_count` — the number of rows matching the request's filters before pagination — and `has_more`, which is true when rows remain beyond the current page. Filter parameters are all optional and combine with logical AND. A filter whose value is syntactically acceptable but matches nothing returns an empty collection rather than an error; for instance, filtering invoices by a `status` value that no invoice currently holds yields an empty array with `total_count` 0.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and the Logical Clock

Each persisted resource carries an `id` that is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix; clients never supply it. Each resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recorded when the row was first persisted. Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, refreshed to the current instant on every mutation of that row, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. Three resource kinds are deliberately narrower: an approved-payables file, an invoice line, and a remittance advice are immutable records of something that happened, and they carry only `id` and `created_at` — neither `updated_at` nor `version` exists on them at all, and no operation in this specification mutates them after creation.

Time in this system is not wall-clock time. The server maintains a **logical clock**, and every temporal comparison in this document — whether an auction window is open, whether an acceptance deadline has passed, how many days early a payment is, whether a capital tranche still covers today — reads that logical clock rather than the host's real time. Before the clock has been set for the first time it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; this is a fixed startup default and the server never falls back to real time. A platform operator may move the clock to any UTC instant through the test-driving clock endpoint, and doing so runs a five-step sweep whose behaviour is specified in §3.5. Timestamps that the platform stamps onto rows — the instant identity verification passed, the instant a supplier became payment-ready, the instant an offer was accepted — are always taken from the logical clock at the moment of the writing call.

### 2.5. Money, Rates and Rounding Conventions

All monetary amounts in this API are integer numbers of cents; no field carries a fractional currency value and no monetary field is ever expressed as a decimal string. All annualised rates are integer numbers of basis points, where one basis point is one hundredth of a percentage point. All ratios exposed as percentages carry exactly two decimal places.

Wherever this document says an amount is rounded, the rounding method is **round half up**: the value is scaled to the target precision, and a value whose fractional part is exactly one half is rounded away from zero, so 2916.5 becomes 2917 and negative 2916.5 becomes negative 2917. Monetary rounding produces whole cents, percentage rounding produces exactly two decimals, and APR rounding produces whole basis points. Two calculations in this specification round with a different function and say so explicitly: the rate-optimisation ceiling truncates toward zero rather than rounding, and the funder available-capital figure is floored at zero rather than being allowed to go negative.

Interest-style pricing uses a **360-day year**. Wherever a discount is derived from an annualised rate and a number of days, the annual rate is divided by 360 days, never by 365. Days are whole calendar days with no business-day adjustment: a Saturday counts exactly as much as a Tuesday.

Rounding order is pinned in two places where a different but superficially reasonable order yields a different cent total, and implementations must follow the order stated. Invoice face value is computed by rounding each line's tax to whole cents first and only then summing the lines — never by summing the pre-tax line amounts and applying a blended rate once at the end. Funder fees are computed against the pre-fee net amount — face minus discount — never against the final net payment after the fee has been deducted, because keying the fee tier on the post-fee figure would be circular and, at amounts near a bracket edge, would resolve to a different tier.

### 2.6. Stale-Version Guards on Rate Naming and Payment Reservation

Two write operations in this system can silently destroy work the client never observed, and both are therefore guarded by optimistic concurrency control against the `version` of a parent row. In each case the guard field is a mandatory, non-optional member of the request body; omitting it is a malformed request rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Naming a discount rate on an invoice — the create operation on the discount-offers collection, `POST /discount-offers` — requires `expected_invoice_version` in its body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the invoice identified by `invoice_id`. If that value does not equal the invoice's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_INVOICE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This guard matters because a newer offer legitimately supersedes any offer already sitting on the same invoice: a client whose view of the invoice has fallen behind does not know which offer it is about to supersede, so the platform refuses the write instead of guessing.

Reserving an early payment against an accepted offer — the create operation on the early-payments collection, `POST /early-payments` — requires `expected_offer_version` in its body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the offer identified by `offer_id`. If that value does not equal the offer's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_OFFER_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A new reservation legitimately voids a prior reserved or deployed payment on the same offer, which is how a treasury officer switches funding source before the money moves; without the guard, a second create carrying a version the client had already seen superseded would silently void a reservation the client never observed.

No other endpoint in this specification takes a version field. In particular, execution, settlement confirmation, settlement failure, accounting classification, countering, re-naming and acceptance all operate without one, and supplying an unexpected version field to them has no defined effect.

### 2.7. Rejection Precedence

Many requests in this system violate more than one rule at once, and the platform's answer must be deterministic. Every check below is evaluated in the numbered order given, and the first violated check decides both the `error_id` and the HTTP status; later checks are never reached. The numbering is the authoritative precedence, not a suggestion, and text order elsewhere in this document carries no precedence meaning.

1. **Authentication** — `X-User-Role` is present and is one of `BuyerAdmin`, `BuyerTreasury`, `Supplier`, `Funder`, `PlatformOps`, and `X-User-ID` is present and non-empty. Otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Role authorisation** — the acting role appears in the called endpoint's authorised-role list; on offer acceptance the permitted role additionally depends on the offer's current status, as described in §2.1. Otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Request shape** — every required body field is present, types are correct, enum values are within range, integers declared non-negative are non-negative, ISO 8601 timestamps parse, and every declared window has its start strictly before its end. This check runs before any database lookup. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Applies to every endpoint.
4. **Path-parameter existence** — every path parameter resolves to a live row, validated left to right along the path. The first segment that fails to resolve decides the error identifier, chosen from `ERR_BUYER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SESSION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SUPPLIER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_BANK_ACCOUNT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DISPUTE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OFFER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FUNDER_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_EARLY_PAYMENT_NOT_FOUND`, each carrying HTTP 404. Applies to every endpoint with a path parameter.
5. **Resource scope** — when `X-User-Role` is `Supplier`, `X-User-ID` must equal the supplier foreign key on the addressed supplier, bank account, invoice, discount offer or early payment; when it is `Funder`, `X-User-ID` must equal the addressed funder's own `id` or the `funder_id` on the addressed early payment; when it is `BuyerAdmin` or `BuyerTreasury`, `X-User-ID` must equal the `buyer_id` reachable from the addressed programme, auction session, payable file, invoice, dispute, discount offer or early payment. Otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_RESOURCE_SCOPE` (403). Because a scope test is only meaningful once the row exists, it is evaluated strictly after the addressed identifier has resolved: an identifier that resolves to no row is the 404 of check 4 or check 6, never this 403. Applies to every endpoint that addresses a scoped resource by path parameter or by body foreign key.
6. **Body foreign-key existence** — every top-level foreign key supplied in the request body resolves to a live row, covering `program_id`, `session_id`, `supplier_id`, `bank_account_id`, `invoice_id`, `offer_id` and `funder_id`, and reported with the same not-found identifiers as check 4. Applies to `POST /buyer-programs`, `POST /auction-sessions`, `POST /payable-files`, `POST /discount-offers`, `POST /early-payments` and `POST /funders/{id}/commitments`. One exception is deliberate: a `supplier_id` nested inside a payable-file row is not covered here, because an unresolvable row-level supplier is a row-level rejection carrying reason `SUPPLIER_UNKNOWN` rather than a 404 on the whole file.
7. **Invoice not suppressed** — the target invoice's `status` is not `SUPPRESSED` and no dispute on it holds `status` `OPEN`. Otherwise `ERR_INVOICE_DISPUTE_SUPPRESSED` (409). Applies to five operations: `POST /discount-offers`, `POST /discount-offers/{id}/counter`, `POST /discount-offers/{id}/rename`, `POST /discount-offers/{id}/accept` and `POST /early-payments`. This check deliberately precedes the generic wrong-state check at position 11, because a suppressed invoice state is a derived consequence of an open dispute and the dispute-specific error is the actionable one.
8. **Programme open** — the buyer programme governing the request holds `status` `OPEN`; `DRAFT`, `PAUSED` and `CLOSED` all reject with `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_OPEN` (409). Applies to seven operations: `POST /auction-sessions`, `POST /payable-files`, `POST /discount-offers`, `POST /discount-offers/{id}/counter`, `POST /discount-offers/{id}/rename`, `POST /discount-offers/{id}/accept` and `POST /early-payments`.
9. **Session live** — the auction session referenced by the request holds `status` `LIVE` and the logical clock lies inside the window that starts at `opens_at` inclusive and ends at `closes_at` exclusive. Otherwise `ERR_SESSION_NOT_LIVE` (409). Applies to `POST /discount-offers`, `POST /discount-offers/{id}/counter` and `POST /discount-offers/{id}/rename`.
10. **Not already terminal** — the addressed resource is not already terminal on the axis this operation writes. Terminal for this purpose means: a discount offer in `ACCEPTED`, `EXPIRED`, `SUPERSEDED` or `VOIDED_BY_SUPPRESSION`; a dispute in `WITHDRAWN`, `RESOLVED_UPHELD` or `RESOLVED_REJECTED`; an early payment whose `funding_status` is `VOID` or whose `settlement_status` is `CONFIRMED` or `FAILED`; a bank account in `VERIFIED`, `FAILED` or `REPLACED`; an auction session in `CLOSED` or `CANCELLED`; a buyer programme in `CLOSED`. Otherwise `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` (409). Applies to every action endpoint that drives a state machine. Note the consequence: repeating an identical trigger on a resource that has already reached terminal reports this error rather than succeeding idempotently.
11. **Correct source state** — the addressed resource satisfies the preconditions of the requested transition, whether that means being in the right non-terminal source state or having a fork-join precondition met such as identity verification already having passed. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409). Applies to every action endpoint that drives a state machine. The suppressed-invoice case is excluded here because check 7 already owns it.
12. **Invoice version guard** — `expected_invoice_version` equals the current `version` of the invoice named in the body. Otherwise `ERR_STALE_INVOICE_VERSION` (412). Applies to `POST /discount-offers`.
13. **Offer version guard** — `expected_offer_version` equals the current `version` of the offer named in the body. Otherwise `ERR_STALE_OFFER_VERSION` (412). Applies to `POST /early-payments`.
14. **Compliance hold** — the supplier that owns the invoice has `sanctions_hold` false. Otherwise `ERR_SUPPLIER_SANCTIONS_HOLD` (403). Applies to eight operations: `POST /suppliers/{id}/onboarding/submit`, `POST /suppliers/{id}/bank-accounts`, `POST /discount-offers`, `POST /discount-offers/{id}/counter`, `POST /discount-offers/{id}/rename`, `POST /discount-offers/{id}/accept`, `POST /early-payments` and `POST /early-payments/{id}/execute`. A supplier under a compliance hold is necessarily also not active, so this rule and the next one fire together; the compliance cause is the one reported.
15. **Payment readiness** — the supplier that owns the invoice has `onboarding_status` `ACTIVE`, `bank_status` `VERIFIED` and a non-null `payment_ready_at`. Otherwise `ERR_SUPPLIER_NOT_PAYMENT_READY` (409). Applies to `POST /discount-offers`, `POST /discount-offers/{id}/accept`, `POST /early-payments` and `POST /early-payments/{id}/execute`.
16. **Acceptance window** — the logical clock is strictly before the offer's `acceptance_deadline`; the window is half-open, so an instant exactly equal to the deadline is already closed. Otherwise `ERR_ACCEPTANCE_WINDOW_CLOSED` (409). Applies to `POST /discount-offers/{id}/accept`.
17. **Session window exclusivity** — the requested window neither overlaps nor falls within 60 minutes of any auction session on the same programme whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `LIVE`. Otherwise `ERR_SESSION_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409). Applies to `POST /auction-sessions`.
18. **File control totals** — the payable file's declared totals match the accepted rows. Otherwise `ERR_FILE_TOTALS_MISMATCH` (422). Applies to `POST /payable-files`.
19. **APR within programme bounds** — the APR being named, countered or accepted lies at or above 200 basis points and at or below the programme's current `max_acceptable_apr_bps`, and on a `FIXED_RATE` programme equals that programme's `fixed_apr_bps`. Otherwise `ERR_APR_OUT_OF_PROGRAM_BOUNDS` (422). Applies to `POST /discount-offers`, `POST /discount-offers/{id}/counter`, `POST /discount-offers/{id}/rename` and `POST /discount-offers/{id}/accept`. The re-check at acceptance reads the programme's bounds as they stand at that moment, which the rate-optimisation ceiling may have tightened after the offer was originally named.
20. **Sufficient days early** — days early measured on the pay-date basis is at least the programme's `min_days_early`, and the pay date is not after the invoice's `net_due_date`. Otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_DAYS_EARLY` (422). Applies to `POST /discount-offers`, `POST /early-payments` and `POST /early-payments/{id}/execute`.
21. **Counter rounds remaining** — the offer's `counter_round` is strictly below 3 before a counter is recorded. Otherwise `ERR_COUNTER_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED` (409). Applies to `POST /discount-offers/{id}/counter`.
22. **Micro-deposit attempts remaining** — the bank account's `micro_deposit_attempts` is strictly below 3 before a confirmation code is evaluated. Otherwise `ERR_MICRO_DEPOSIT_ATTEMPTS_EXHAUSTED` (409). Applies to `POST /bank-accounts/{id}/micro-deposit/confirm`.
23. **Override justification** — when the requested accounting classification differs from the payment's `default_accounting_status`, `override_reason` is present and non-empty after trimming whitespace. Otherwise `ERR_RECLASS_OVERRIDE_REASON_REQUIRED` (422). Applies to `POST /early-payments/{id}/accounting-classification`.
24. **Self-funding enabled** — when the funding waterfall lands on buyer self-funding as the only candidate source, the buyer's `self_funding_enabled` is true. Otherwise `ERR_SELF_FUNDING_DISABLED` (409). Applies to `POST /early-payments`.
25. **Concentration cap** — the forward-looking face-basis supplier share, including the invoice about to be awarded, is not strictly greater than the programme's `concentration_cap_pct`; a projected share exactly equal to the cap passes. Otherwise `ERR_CONCENTRATION_CAP_EXCEEDED` (409). Applies to `POST /discount-offers/{id}/accept`.
26. **Funder capital ceiling** — the new tranche does not push the funder's summed committed capital above its `capital_limit_cents` at any instant inside the tranche's interval. Otherwise `ERR_FUNDER_CAPITAL_OVERCOMMITTED` (409). Applies to `POST /funders/{id}/commitments`.
27. **An eligible funder exists** — the funder selector returned a winner when a funder is the last candidate source in the waterfall. Otherwise `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_FUNDER` (422). Applies to `POST /early-payments`.
28. **Buyer credit headroom** — the buyer's `credit_used_cents` plus the pre-fee net of this payment is at most the buyer's `credit_limit_cents` when the resolved funding source is buyer self-funding; exact equality is allowed. Otherwise `ERR_BUYER_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (402). Applies to `POST /early-payments` and `POST /early-payments/{id}/execute`.

Three precedence outcomes are worth spelling out because they are the ones implementations most often get backwards. A reservation request on an accepted offer whose programme has since been paused, submitted for a self-funding buyer whose remaining headroom is smaller than the pre-fee net, violates both check 8 and check 28; the reported error is `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_OPEN`, because programme state outranks credit capacity. A rate-naming request for a supplier whose compliance hold is set — which also forced that supplier out of `ACTIVE` and therefore fails payment readiness — violates both check 14 and check 15; the reported error is `ERR_SUPPLIER_SANCTIONS_HOLD`. An acceptance submitted after the clock has passed the offer's acceptance deadline, on an offer whose named APR now sits above a programme ceiling that was tightened in the meantime, violates both check 16 and check 19; the reported error is `ERR_ACCEPTANCE_WINDOW_CLOSED`.

### 2.8. Segregation of Duties and Exclusive Operations

Because both buyer-side roles authenticate as the same corporate `id`, the boundary between them is enforced purely by the endpoint authorisation list, and it is worth stating which operations belong exclusively to whom. Creating a programme, opening it, changing its lifecycle status, scheduling an auction session, closing an auction session early, ingesting an approved-payables file, countering an offer, and accepting an offer that is in `NAMED` are exclusive to `BuyerAdmin`. Reserving an early payment, executing it, and recording its accounting classification are exclusive to `BuyerTreasury`. Naming a rate, re-naming a rate after a counter, and accepting an offer that is in `COUNTERED` are exclusive to `Supplier`. Registering a buyer, registering a supplier, adjudicating identity verification, placing or lifting a compliance hold, registering a funder, and driving the logical clock are exclusive to `PlatformOps`. `Funder` holds no exclusive operation of its own.

Several operations are deliberately shared. Raising a dispute on an invoice and resolving one are open to `BuyerAdmin` and `PlatformOps`. Submitting supplier onboarding, adding a bank account and confirming a micro-deposit code are open to `Supplier` and `PlatformOps`. Changing funder participation and committing a capital tranche are open to `Funder` and `PlatformOps`. Confirming or failing a settlement is open to `BuyerTreasury` and `PlatformOps`. Read access follows ownership: a buyer-side role reads its own buyer's programmes, sessions, files, invoices, disputes, offers and early payments; a supplier reads its own supplier record, bank accounts, invoices, disputes, offers, early payments and remittance advices; a funder reads its own funder record, its commitments and the early payments it funds; a platform operator reads everything.

### 2.9. Nested Reads, Reverse Lookups and Path Resolution

The resource graph here is flat: fourteen peer entity types linked by explicit foreign keys, addressed by single-collection paths such as an invoice by its own identifier or an offer by its own identifier. No resource is reachable only through a multi-level ownership chain. Nesting in the URL space appears for exactly two reasons — reverse-reachability reads, where a nested collection lists the children of a parent by foreign key, and action sub-paths, where a verb hangs off the resource it acts on.

When a path carries more than one segment that must resolve, resolution proceeds strictly left to right and the first unresolved segment decides the error. Asking for the bank accounts of a supplier that does not exist yields `ERR_SUPPLIER_NOT_FOUND`, not an empty list; asking for the commitments of a funder that does not exist yields `ERR_FUNDER_NOT_FOUND`; asking for the lines of an invoice that does not exist yields `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_FOUND`. Reverse-lookup collections that do resolve but have no children return an empty array with `total_count` 0.

### 2.10. Cascade Discipline and Conserved Quantities

A single call in this system frequently mutates several rows across several entity types, and this specification treats those secondary effects as part of the contract rather than as implementation detail. Whenever a cascade touches more than one sibling row, the rows are processed in ascending `created_at` order. Every row a cascade mutates has its own `updated_at` refreshed and its own `version` incremented by one — including rows the caller did not address. Conversely, a row a cascade merely re-reads without changing does not have its `version` touched; the retry path on a wrong micro-deposit code is the canonical example, where the bank account's `version` advances but the supplier's does not, because no supplier field changed.

Two quantities in this system are conserved, and every operation that disturbs them must have a compensating counterpart that restores them exactly.

The first is **gross invoice face**. For every invoice, `face_amount_cents` must always equal `remaining_face_cents` plus the sum of `face_covered_cents` over that invoice's early payments whose `funding_status` is `RESERVED`, `DEPLOYED` or `RELEASED`. Reserving and then executing an early payment drives `face_covered_cents` up to the full face and `remaining_face_cents` down to zero. Recording a failed settlement is the compensating action: the payment's `funding_status` leaves that three-value set, so `face_covered_cents` returns to zero and `remaining_face_cents` returns to `face_amount_cents`. After a forward action followed by its compensation the equality must hold with `remaining_face_cents` back at its original value; a residual of even one cent is a defect.

The second is **funder capital covering the clock date**. For every funder, the capital committed at the logical clock date must always equal `available_capital_cents` plus `encumbered_capital_cents`, where `encumbered_capital_cents` is the sum of `net_payment_cents` over that funder's early payments whose `funding_status` is `RESERVED` or `DEPLOYED`. Reserving a funder-financed payment raises `encumbered_capital_cents` by the payment's `net_payment_cents` and lowers `available_capital_cents` by the same amount. Both the dispute-suppression cascade, which drives `funding_status` to `VOID`, and a recorded settlement failure are compensating actions that give the capital back. One subtlety must be honoured or the sum drifts: the recomputation of the discount at execution time changes `net_payment_cents`, and the encumbrance must be adjusted by exactly that delta rather than left at its reservation-time figure.

Both encumbrance figures — the buyer's `credit_used_cents` and each funder's `encumbered_capital_cents` — are always recomputed in full from the qualifying payment rows rather than adjusted by incremental deltas, precisely so that no drift is possible.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Onboarding a Buyer and Configuring a Discounting Programme

A platform operator brings a buyer onto the platform first. Registration takes the buyer's registered `legal_name`, between 1 and 200 characters, its `tax_id`, which must be exactly nine digits and must be unique across all buyers — a collision is reported as a malformed request with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and its `credit_limit_cents`, the ceiling on the total of self-funded early payments the buyer may hold open, which must be zero or greater. Three optional inputs shape later behaviour: `self_funding_enabled`, which defaults to true and which, when false, forbids the buyer from ever using its own balance sheet as a funding source; `reclass_days_threshold`, defaulting to 30 days and constrained to zero or greater; and `reclass_apr_threshold_bps`, defaulting to 900 basis points and likewise constrained to zero or greater. The last two govern accounting classification and are discussed in §3.9. A freshly registered buyer opens in `status` `ACTIVE` with `credit_used_cents` at 0, and the two buyer states are `ACTIVE` and `SUSPENDED`.

Programme creation belongs to the buyer's own programme owner and takes a `buyer_id` naming the funding buyer, a `name` between 1 and 120 characters that suppliers will see, and a `program_type` that is either `SUPPLIER_NAMED` or `FIXED_RATE`. The distinction drives everything downstream. Under `SUPPLIER_NAMED`, suppliers propose their own rates and the buyer may negotiate; `fixed_apr_bps` must be null on such a programme. Under `FIXED_RATE`, `fixed_apr_bps` is mandatory and must lie between 1 and 5000 basis points, every offer on the programme must carry exactly that APR, and there is nothing to negotiate. Every programme also carries `max_acceptable_apr_bps`, between 200 and 5000 basis points, as the upper bound on any rate named, countered or accepted. Three further inputs are optional: `min_days_early`, defaulting to 5 and at least 1, is the minimum number of days early a payment must achieve to be executable; `concentration_cap_pct`, defaulting to 60.00 percent and constrained between 0.01 and 100.00 with two decimals, caps the share of programme face value that one supplier may hold; and `funding_policy`, defaulting to `SELF_THEN_FUNDER`, is one of `SELF_ONLY`, `FUNDER_ONLY` or `SELF_THEN_FUNDER` and determines which balance sheets may pay and in which order. A new programme is persisted in `status` `DRAFT` with every analytics counter at zero — `eligible_invoice_count` and `adopted_invoice_count` at 0, `adoption_rate_pct` at 0.00, `weighted_avg_accepted_apr_bps` at 0 — and `rate_posture` at `DISCOVERY`.

Opening a programme moves it from `DRAFT` to `OPEN`. The transition requires that the programme is currently in `DRAFT`, that the owning buyer's `status` is `ACTIVE`, and, on a `FIXED_RATE` programme, that `fixed_apr_bps` is non-null and not greater than `max_acceptable_apr_bps`. Opening also runs the adoption recomputation described in §3.10, which on a fresh programme leaves both counters at 0 and `adoption_rate_pct` at exactly 0.00, and bumps the programme's `version` by one. A programme that is not in `DRAFT` when opening is attempted is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, unless it has already reached `CLOSED`, in which case the terminal-state check reports `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL` first.

Thereafter the programme's lifecycle is driven by an `action` field taking one of three values. `PAUSE` moves `OPEN` to `PAUSED` and requires the programme to be in `OPEN`; while paused, no new rate may be named and no new payment reserved, but offers that already reached `ACCEPTED` remain executable. `RESUME` moves `PAUSED` back to `OPEN` and requires the programme to be in `PAUSED`; this edge is what makes the programme lifecycle cyclic, and a programme may be paused and resumed any number of times. `CLOSE` moves either `OPEN` or `PAUSED` to `CLOSED`, which is terminal. Each of the three actions increments the programme's `version` by one.

Closing is the widest cascade in the configuration layer, and it runs in three hops. First, every auction session of this programme whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `LIVE` becomes `CANCELLED`, processed in ascending `created_at` order. Second, because those sessions have left the scheduled-or-live set, every discount offer bound to them whose `status` is `NAMED` or `COUNTERED` becomes `EXPIRED`, again in ascending `created_at` order. Third, if after this close no programme of that buyer holds `status` `DRAFT`, `OPEN` or `PAUSED`, the buyer itself moves from `ACTIVE` to `SUSPENDED`. Each of those cascaded rows has its own `version` incremented and its `updated_at` refreshed.

### 3.2. Supplier Onboarding: Two Verification Axes That Must Both Complete

Suppliers are registered by a platform operator with a `legal_name` between 1 and 200 characters, a `tax_id_last4` that must be exactly four digits and which identity verification later consumes, and an optional `remittance_email`, which defaults to null. A newly registered supplier sits in `onboarding_status` `DRAFT` with `idv_status` `NOT_STARTED`, `bank_status` `NOT_STARTED`, `sanctions_hold` false, `identity_score` 0, and `idv_verified_at`, `payment_ready_at` and `verified_account_last4` all null. The five onboarding states are `DRAFT`, `VERIFYING`, `ACTIVE`, `REJECTED` and `SUSPENDED`, of which `REJECTED` is terminal.

Submitting onboarding — an action the supplier itself or a platform operator may take — moves `onboarding_status` from `DRAFT` to `VERIFYING`, and it does two things at once. It sets `idv_status` to `PENDING`, opening the identity axis, and it deliberately leaves `bank_status` at `NOT_STARTED`, because the bank axis opens only when a settlement account is registered. Submission requires that the supplier is in `DRAFT` and that `sanctions_hold` is false. This is the fork point of a diamond: the two axes advance through entirely disjoint operations and write entirely disjoint field sets, and the supplier becomes usable only when both have completed.

On the identity axis, a platform operator adjudicates the verification outcome with a `decision` of `PASSED`, `MANUAL_REVIEW` or `FAILED`, optionally accompanied by an `identity_score` between 0 and 100 which is written to the supplier and which remains 0 until `idv_status` leaves `PENDING`. Adjudication requires that `onboarding_status` is `VERIFYING` and that `idv_status` is currently either `PENDING` or `MANUAL_REVIEW`. A decision of `PASSED` or `MANUAL_REVIEW` writes only identity-axis fields and leaves `onboarding_status` at `VERIFYING`; the five identity states are `NOT_STARTED`, `PENDING`, `MANUAL_REVIEW`, `PASSED` and `FAILED`. A decision of `PASSED` additionally stamps `idv_verified_at` with the logical-clock instant. A decision of `FAILED` sets `idv_status` to `FAILED` and drives `onboarding_status` straight to the terminal `REJECTED`, and it cascades: the supplier's bank account row that is not already `REPLACED`, if one exists, becomes `FAILED` and `bank_status` mirrors `FAILED`. The identity branch failing short-circuits the join, so the bank branch is abandoned rather than joined. Each of these adjudication paths increments the supplier's `version` by one.

On the bank axis, the supplier — or a platform operator acting for it — registers a settlement account by supplying a `routing_number` of exactly nine digits and an `account_last4` of exactly four digits; the account row records the owning `supplier_id`. The platform pushes a micro deposit carrying a six-digit `micro_deposit_code`, and that code is fully determined by the account itself: it is the zero-padded decimal formed by concatenating the last three digits of `routing_number` with the first three digits of `account_last4`. The new row is persisted in `status` `MICRO_DEPOSIT_SENT` with `micro_deposit_attempts` at 0, and the supplier's `bank_status` is set to `MICRO_DEPOSIT_SENT`. The four bank-account states are `MICRO_DEPOSIT_SENT`, `VERIFIED`, `FAILED` and `REPLACED`, the last being terminal.

Registering a newer account displaces the older one. Any earlier account row of the same supplier whose `status` is `MICRO_DEPOSIT_SENT`, `VERIFIED` or `FAILED` becomes `REPLACED`; the supplier's `bank_status` is set to `MICRO_DEPOSIT_SENT` to mirror the new row and `verified_account_last4` is cleared to null; and if the supplier's `onboarding_status` had already reached `ACTIVE`, it drops back to `VERIFYING` and `payment_ready_at` is cleared to null. The displaced account row and the supplier row each have their `version` incremented by one. A supplier whose `sanctions_hold` is true may not register an account at all.

Confirming the micro deposit means submitting a `code` for evaluation. Before the code is even compared, the attempt budget is checked: `micro_deposit_attempts` must be strictly below three, and a supplier's `sanctions_hold` must be false. Three outcomes follow. If the submitted `code` equals the row's `micro_deposit_code`, `micro_deposit_attempts` increments by one, the account moves `MICRO_DEPOSIT_SENT → VERIFIED`, the supplier's `bank_status` becomes `VERIFIED` and its `verified_account_last4` is set to the account's `account_last4`; both the account's `version` and the supplier's `version` increment by one. If the code differs and the incremented attempt count is still below three, `micro_deposit_attempts` increments by one and the account stays in `MICRO_DEPOSIT_SENT`, re-armed for another try — this self-edge is the cycle in the bank-account lifecycle, and a supplier retries against the same account row rather than creating a new one. On that retry path the account's `version` increments but the supplier's does not, because `bank_status` remains `MICRO_DEPOSIT_SENT` and no supplier field changed. If the code differs and the incremented attempt count reaches three, `micro_deposit_attempts` increments to three, the account moves `MICRO_DEPOSIT_SENT → FAILED`, the supplier's `bank_status` mirrors `FAILED`, and both `version` fields increment; any further confirmation attempt on that row is rejected with `ERR_MICRO_DEPOSIT_ATTEMPTS_EXHAUSTED`, and the supplier's only route forward is a replacement account row.

The join is evaluated at the end of both axis-advancing operations — after identity adjudication and after a successful micro-deposit confirmation. Whenever `idv_status` is `PASSED`, `bank_status` is `VERIFIED` and `sanctions_hold` is false, the supplier moves `VERIFYING → ACTIVE` and `payment_ready_at` is stamped with the logical-clock instant; the join is reachable from either branch completing last, and the supplier's `version` increments. Until that moment the supplier cannot be part of any rate or payment activity, and attempts are rejected with `ERR_SUPPLIER_NOT_PAYMENT_READY`.

Compliance holds cut across both axes. A platform operator setting `hold` to true writes `sanctions_hold` true and forces `onboarding_status` to `SUSPENDED`, permitted whenever the supplier is currently in `DRAFT`, `VERIFYING` or `ACTIVE`. The hold blocks new actions only: offers that already reached `ACCEPTED` and early payments already in `RESERVED` are not voided by it. Setting `hold` to false requires the supplier to be in `SUSPENDED` and returns it to whichever state its two axes justify — to `ACTIVE` when `idv_status` is `PASSED` and `bank_status` is `VERIFIED`, and to `VERIFYING` when that combination does not hold. Release never pushes a supplier past the join it has not earned. Either direction increments the supplier's `version` by one, and any blocked operation attempted while the hold is in force is rejected with `ERR_SUPPLIER_SANCTIONS_HOLD`.

### 3.3. Ingesting Approved Payables and Pricing Invoice Face Value

Payables enter the platform in bulk. A programme owner submits an extract from the buyer's accounts-payable system against a programme that must currently be `OPEN`, naming the target `program_id`, a buyer-supplied `filename` between 1 and 200 characters, the control totals `declared_row_count` — at least 1 — and `declared_total_cents` — at least 1 — and a `rows` array. Each element of `rows` carries an `invoice_number`, an `approval_date`, a `net_due_date`, a `supplier_id`, an optional `suppression_flag`, and a `lines` array whose elements each carry a `line_no`, a `line_amount_cents` and a `tax_rate_bps`. Ingestion is synchronous: the file record is persisted directly in its final outcome state, and there is no intermediate received-or-validating state for a client to observe.

Every row is validated and priced independently, and a row that fails validation produces no invoice at all. Five row-level failure reasons exist. A row whose `supplier_id` matches no supplier is rejected with reason `SUPPLIER_UNKNOWN` — note that this is a row-level rejection and never a 404 on the file as a whole. A row whose `invoice_number` duplicates an invoice number already present for the same combination of programme and supplier is rejected with reason `DUPLICATE_INVOICE_NUMBER`; `invoice_number` is between 1 and 40 characters and is unique per programme-and-supplier pair. A row containing any line whose `line_amount_cents` is zero or negative is rejected with reason `NON_POSITIVE_AMOUNT`, since every line amount must be strictly greater than zero. A row whose `net_due_date` is not strictly after its `approval_date` is rejected with reason `DUE_DATE_NOT_AFTER_APPROVAL`; both dates are ISO 8601 UTC instants at midnight. A row whose `lines` array is empty is rejected with reason `EMPTY_LINES`, and this check runs before any pricing so that no face calculation is ever attempted on a line-less row.

Pricing a row's face value has a **pinned rounding order** that implementations must reproduce exactly. Walk the row's lines in ascending `line_no` order. For each line, compute `line_tax_cents` as the line's `line_amount_cents` multiplied by its `tax_rate_bps`, divided by ten thousand, and rounded half up to a whole cent. Then compute `line_total_cents` as `line_amount_cents` plus `line_tax_cents`. Then accumulate that line total into a running face figure. The invoice's `face_amount_cents` is the accumulated total over all lines. Rounding happens once per line and never to the sum. The alternative order — summing the pre-tax amounts first and applying a blended rate once at the end — is explicitly not the rule and produces a different cent total for the same input: three lines of 33333, 33333 and 33334 cents each at 875 basis points yield per-line taxes of 2917, 2917 and 2917 cents and line totals of 36250, 36250 and 36251 cents, for a face of 108751 cents, whereas rounding after summation would yield 108750. A line's `tax_rate_bps` lies between 0 and 3000 basis points, and each line records the `invoice_id` it belongs to plus a `line_no` that is at least 1, unique within the invoice, and contiguous from 1 upward — the line order in the submitted row is the pinned iteration order, and the stored lines are read back in that same order.

Once the rows have been priced, the file's own figures are written. `accepted_row_count` is the number of rows that produced an invoice; `rejected_row_count` is the number that failed row-level validation and produced none; `computed_total_cents` is the sum of the computed face over the accepted rows, so rejected rows contribute nothing to it; and `rejection_reasons` is an ordered array of objects, each pairing a `row_index` — the zero-based position of the failing row inside the submitted `rows` array — with a `reason` drawn from the five values above, sorted by `row_index` ascending. The control-total reconciliation then runs: `declared_row_count` must equal `accepted_row_count` and `declared_total_cents` must equal `computed_total_cents`, and a mismatch on either side rejects the whole submission with `ERR_FILE_TOTALS_MISMATCH`. Rows rejected for row-level reasons are excluded from both sides of the comparison, so a file whose rejected rows account for exactly the difference between the buyer's raw extract and the platform's accepted set still passes.

The file's `ingest_status` is derived rather than supplied, and it takes one of three values: `ACCEPTED` when `rejected_row_count` is zero, `REJECTED` when `accepted_row_count` is zero, and `PARTIALLY_ACCEPTED` in every other case. A wholly `REJECTED` file creates no invoices, records every failure in `rejection_reasons`, and leaves the programme's counters untouched. An `ACCEPTED` or `PARTIALLY_ACCEPTED` file creates one invoice per accepted row, in submitted-row order, each with its lines in ascending `line_no` order, and then recomputes the programme's `eligible_invoice_count`, `adopted_invoice_count`, `adoption_rate_pct` and `rate_posture` and increments the programme's `version` by one.

Each created invoice records the `file_id` of the ingestion event that produced it, the `program_id` copied from that file, and the `supplier_id` resolved from the row. It opens with `face_covered_cents` at 0 — so `remaining_face_cents` equals `face_amount_cents` — with `accepted_apr_bps`, `provisional_discount_cents`, `final_discount_cents` and `discount_true_up_cents` all at 0, and with `dispute_suppressed` false. The six invoice states are `ELIGIBLE`, `SUPPRESSED`, `OFFERED`, `AWARDED`, `EARLY_PAID` and `MATURED`, of which the last two are terminal.

A row may arrive already flagged as contested. When a row's `suppression_flag` is true, its invoice is created directly in `SUPPRESSED` with `dispute_suppressed` true, and the platform simultaneously raises a dispute on it with `reason_code` `BUYER_FILE_SUPPRESSION`, `status` `OPEN` and `disputed_amount_cents` equal to the full invoice face. The file's `suppressed_row_count` counts exactly those accepted rows. Rows without the flag, or with it set to false, produce invoices in `ELIGIBLE`. That `BUYER_FILE_SUPPRESSION` reason code is reserved for this ingestion cascade and may never be supplied by a client raising a dispute directly.

Invoices and their lines are readable afterwards. The invoice collection can be filtered by `file_id`, by `program_id`, by `supplier_id` and by `status`, in any combination; a single invoice read exposes its face ledger and both discount figures; and an invoice's priced lines are listed through its own nested lines collection. A programme's ingested files are listed through the programme's nested payable-files collection, optionally filtered by `ingest_status`.

### 3.4. Contesting a Payable: Disputes, Suppression and Release

A payable whose amount or delivery the buyer contests must not be discounted while the argument is unresolved. A programme owner or a platform operator raises a dispute on an invoice by supplying a `reason_code`, a `disputed_amount_cents` and an optional `resolution_note` — the note is null while the dispute is `OPEN` and is normally supplied only at resolution time. The dispute records the `invoice_id` of the payable it contests. `reason_code` is one of `BUYER_FILE_SUPPRESSION`, `SHORT_SHIPMENT`, `PRICE_VARIANCE` or `GOODS_REJECTED`, and both buyer-file suppressions and operator-raised disputes live on the same dispute record, discriminated by that code. `disputed_amount_cents` is at least 1 and at most the invoice's `face_amount_cents`; a dispute arising from file suppression always carries the full face. Raising a dispute requires the invoice to be in `ELIGIBLE`, `OFFERED` or `AWARDED` with `dispute_suppressed` currently false. The four dispute states are `OPEN`, `WITHDRAWN`, `RESOLVED_UPHELD` and `RESOLVED_REJECTED`; the last three are terminal, and a second resolution attempt on an already-resolved dispute reports `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`.

Raising a dispute triggers the deepest cascade in this specification, and it is worth walking hop by hop because each hop is observable. Hop one creates the dispute in `OPEN`, sets the invoice's `dispute_suppressed` to true and drives the invoice to `SUPPRESSED` — so `ELIGIBLE → SUPPRESSED`, `OFFERED → SUPPRESSED` and `AWARDED → SUPPRESSED` are all reachable, and the invoice's `version` increments. Hop two applies when the invoice carried offers: every discount offer on that invoice whose `status` is `NAMED`, `COUNTERED` or `ACCEPTED` becomes `VOIDED_BY_SUPPRESSION`, processed in ascending `created_at` order, each with its own `version` increment. Hop three applies when one of those offers had been `ACCEPTED` and financed: every early payment on it whose `funding_status` is `RESERVED` or `DEPLOYED` becomes `VOID`, and its encumbrance is released according to that payment's own funding source — a funder-financed payment decrements its funder's `encumbered_capital_cents` by that payment's `net_payment_cents` and triggers a recomputation of the funder's `available_capital_cents`, while a self-funded payment decrements the buyer's `credit_used_cents` by the same amount. Hop four is the ledger consequence: because those payments have left the reserved-deployed-released set, the invoice's `face_covered_cents` drops back to 0 and `remaining_face_cents` returns to `face_amount_cents`, restoring face conservation. The programme's adoption figures are recomputed as part of the same call. The whole chain therefore runs dispute, then invoice, then offers, then early payments together with the funder or buyer ledger, then the invoice face ledger.

Resolution takes a `resolution` value and an optional `resolution_note`, which is stored on the dispute whichever branch is taken, and requires the dispute to be `OPEN`. Three branches exist, and they are not symmetric in who may act or in what happens to the invoice. A `resolution` of `WITHDRAWN`, available to a programme owner, moves the dispute `OPEN → WITHDRAWN`. A `resolution` of `REJECTED`, available to a platform operator, moves it `OPEN → RESOLVED_REJECTED`. Both of those outcomes mean the payable was not genuinely contested, and both cascade identically: once no dispute on that invoice still holds `status` `OPEN`, the invoice's `dispute_suppressed` returns to false and the invoice moves `SUPPRESSED → ELIGIBLE`. This revert edge is what makes the invoice lifecycle non-linear — a payable may be suppressed and released repeatedly — but it does not resurrect anything: offers voided by the suppression stay `VOIDED_BY_SUPPRESSION`, and the supplier must name a fresh rate. If another dispute on the same invoice is still `OPEN`, the invoice stays `SUPPRESSED`. A `resolution` of `UPHELD`, likewise available to a platform operator, moves the dispute `OPEN → RESOLVED_UPHELD` and takes the opposite view: the payable genuinely was wrong, so the invoice moves `SUPPRESSED → MATURED`, leaving the discounting programme altogether to be paid on net terms. Because the invoice has left the eligible set, the programme's `eligible_invoice_count`, `adoption_rate_pct` and `rate_posture` are recomputed. Every branch increments the dispute's `version`, and the cascaded invoice's `version`, by one. The disputes raised on one invoice are listable through that invoice's nested disputes collection, optionally filtered by `status`.

### 3.5. Auction Windows, the Logical Clock and Session Clearing

Rate discovery happens inside time-boxed windows. A programme owner schedules one against a programme that must be `OPEN`, naming the `program_id` and the window's `opens_at` and `closes_at`, both ISO 8601 UTC instants, with `closes_at` strictly after `opens_at`. The window is half-open: it includes its `opens_at` instant and excludes its `closes_at` instant. A new session is persisted in `status` `SCHEDULED` with `named_offer_count` at 0 and `clearing_apr_bps` at 0. The four session states are `SCHEDULED`, `LIVE`, `CLOSED` and `CANCELLED`, of which `CLOSED` and `CANCELLED` are terminal.

Windows on one programme occupy the timeline exclusively, and they must be separated by a cooldown of **one hour**. Concretely, a requested window conflicts with an existing `SCHEDULED` or `LIVE` session on the same programme when the requested `opens_at` falls before the existing window's `closes_at` advanced by sixty minutes and, at the same time, the existing window's `opens_at` falls before the requested `closes_at` advanced by sixty minutes. Two consequences follow from that formulation and both are intended: windows that merely abut, where the new `opens_at` equals the existing `closes_at`, still conflict; and a gap of exactly sixty minutes between the end of one window and the start of the next does not conflict. A conflicting request is rejected with `ERR_SESSION_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. A programme's windows are listable through the programme's nested auction-sessions collection, optionally filtered by `status`.

Sessions normally change state by themselves as time passes. Because time here is logical, the transitions are driven by a platform operator moving the clock, which is the platform's test-driving capability: it accepts a `current_utc` instant and performs a five-step sweep in exactly the order given. Step one stores the new logical-clock instant. Step two opens and closes sessions: every `SCHEDULED` session whose `opens_at` has arrived becomes `LIVE`, and every `SCHEDULED` or `LIVE` session whose `closes_at` has passed becomes `CLOSED`, with sessions processed in ascending `opens_at` order. Step three expires offers: every `NAMED` or `COUNTERED` offer bound to a session that step two closed becomes `EXPIRED`, processed in ascending `created_at` order. Step four matures invoices: every invoice in `ELIGIBLE` or `OFFERED` whose `net_due_date` has passed becomes `MATURED`, processed in ascending `net_due_date` order and then ascending `created_at` order. Step five expires capital: every `ACTIVE` funder commitment whose `effective_to` has passed becomes `EXPIRED`, and each affected funder's available capital is recomputed.

Two details of that sweep are load-bearing. A session becomes `LIVE` only when the new clock instant is at or after its `opens_at` and strictly before its `closes_at`, and only while the governing programme is `OPEN` or `PAUSED`. A session whose whole window is skipped in one clock jump — the new instant is at or after `closes_at` while the session is still `SCHEDULED` — goes straight from `SCHEDULED` to `CLOSED` and its `clearing_apr_bps` is 0, because no offer on it was ever accepted. Separately, invoices in `SUPPRESSED` are held out of the maturity sweep entirely: a suppressed payable matures only when its dispute is resolved as upheld. Maturing an invoice also expires every `NAMED` or `COUNTERED` offer on it, recomputes the programme's `eligible_invoice_count`, `adoption_rate_pct` and `rate_posture`, and increments the invoice's `version`.

Closing a session writes the window's clearing rate. `clearing_apr_bps` is the face-weighted mean accepted APR of the window: take every offer on the session whose `status` is exactly `ACCEPTED` at the instant of close, multiply each one's locked APR by the `face_amount_cents` of the invoice it prices, sum those products, divide by the sum of those same invoice faces, and round the quotient half up to a whole basis point. Offers in `EXPIRED`, `SUPERSEDED` or `VOIDED_BY_SUPPRESSION` are excluded. When no offer on the session ever reached `ACCEPTED`, the clearing rate is exactly 0 rather than null. Closing also cascades: every `NAMED` or `COUNTERED` offer on the session becomes `EXPIRED`, in ascending `created_at` order, and the session's `version` increments.

A programme owner may also close a window early rather than waiting for the clock, supplying a `mode`. With `mode` set to `SETTLE`, the session must currently be `LIVE`, and it moves `LIVE → CLOSED` with exactly the same effects as the clock-driven close: the clearing rate is written and outstanding offers expire. With `mode` set to `CANCEL`, the session may be `SCHEDULED` or `LIVE` and it moves to `CANCELLED`, expiring outstanding offers in ascending `created_at` order but writing no clearing rate. Cancellation carries one extra precondition that settlement does not: no offer on the session may currently hold `status` `ACCEPTED`. That is the essential difference between the two modes — settlement permits accepted offers to exist and records what they cleared at, whereas cancellation is only available while nothing has been awarded. Attempting to cancel a session that has an accepted offer, or to settle a session that is not `LIVE`, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; acting on a session that is already `CLOSED` or `CANCELLED` reports `ERR_ALREADY_TERMINAL`.

The session's `named_offer_count` tracks live and settled negotiation activity: it is a figure stored on the session row, and it counts the offers on that session whose `status` is `NAMED`, `COUNTERED` or `ACCEPTED`. Because `ACCEPTED` is already inside that set, acceptance does not increment the counter a second time. The stored figure is not derived at read time; it is re-derived by recounting those offers at the end of every operation that creates an offer on the session or writes the `status` of one — naming a rate, including the supersede self-edge, countering, re-naming, acceptance, and the session-close, session-cancel, programme-close and dispute-suppression cascades. Per §2.10 a recount that leaves the figure unchanged is not a mutation of the session row and therefore does not touch its `version`, while a recount that changes the figure is such a mutation; so naming the first offer on a session moves the stored figure from 0 to 1 and increments the session's `version`, whereas countering, re-naming and accepting that offer leave the figure alone and do not. The invoice-maturity sweep of step four above is the single exception to the recount: it expires the outstanding offers on a matured invoice without recounting them out of the session's stored figure, which may therefore keep counting an offer that maturity has expired until the next recount on that session.

### 3.6. Naming, Countering and Accepting a Discount Rate

A supplier names a rate on one of its eligible payables inside a live window. The submission carries the `invoice_id` being priced, the `session_id` of the window it is bound to, the `offered_apr_bps` the supplier is willing to pay for early settlement, the `requested_pay_date` on which it asks to be paid, an `acceptance_deadline` after which the offer may no longer be taken, and the `expected_invoice_version` guard described in §2.6. The offer copies the invoice's `supplier_id` onto itself at creation and opens in `status` `NAMED` with `counter_round` 0, `counter_apr_bps` null, `accepted_apr_bps` 0 and `accepted_at` null. The six offer states are `NAMED`, `COUNTERED`, `ACCEPTED`, `EXPIRED`, `SUPERSEDED` and `VOIDED_BY_SUPPRESSION`; the last three are terminal, and `ACCEPTED` is terminal for the purposes of further negotiation.

Naming a rate requires a long conjunction of conditions to hold. The invoice must be in `ELIGIBLE` — or in `OFFERED`, for the re-naming case below — with `dispute_suppressed` false. The programme must be `OPEN` and the session must be `LIVE` with the logical clock inside the half-open window. The supplier must have `onboarding_status` `ACTIVE` and `sanctions_hold` false. `offered_apr_bps` is a positive number of basis points, and the programme-bounds rule requires it to be at least 200 and at most the programme's `max_acceptable_apr_bps`; on a `FIXED_RATE` programme it must in addition equal the programme's `fixed_apr_bps` exactly. Any rate below 200 basis points, above the programme's current ceiling, or differing from a fixed programme's rate is rejected with `ERR_APR_OUT_OF_PROGRAM_BOUNDS`. `requested_pay_date` must fall strictly before the invoice's `net_due_date` and not before the logical clock's date. `acceptance_deadline` must fall strictly after the logical clock at creation and not after the session's `closes_at`; the acceptance window is half-open, running from the offer's `created_at` up to but not including its `acceptance_deadline`. Finally, the days-early floor of §3.8 must be satisfied on the pay-date basis, or the submission is rejected with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_DAYS_EARLY`.

Naming a rate on an invoice that is already `ELIGIBLE` moves it `ELIGIBLE → OFFERED` and increments the invoice's `version` by one. Naming a fresh rate on an invoice that already carries a `NAMED` or `COUNTERED` offer takes the invoice's self-edge `OFFERED → OFFERED` instead: the prior offers on that invoice whose `status` is `NAMED` or `COUNTERED` become `SUPERSEDED`, in ascending `created_at` order, the new offer is created in `NAMED`, and the invoice's `status` stays `OFFERED` while its `version` still increments by one. That version bump is exactly what renders any subsequent create still carrying the older `expected_invoice_version` stale. The self-edge additionally requires that no offer on that invoice currently holds `status` `ACCEPTED` — once a rate has been locked, the supplier can no longer re-price the payable. Either way the session's `named_offer_count` reflects the new offer.

Negotiation is a cycle, and only `SUPPLIER_NAMED` programmes have one. A programme owner counters a `NAMED` offer by supplying a `counter_apr_bps`, which moves the offer `NAMED → COUNTERED` and stores the countered rate. Countering requires the offer to be `NAMED`, the session to be `LIVE` with the clock inside the window, the programme to be `OPEN`, the programme's `program_type` to be `SUPPLIER_NAMED` — a fixed-rate programme has nothing to negotiate — the countered rate to be strictly greater than the offer's current `offered_apr_bps` and at most the programme's `max_acceptable_apr_bps`, and the offer's `counter_round` to be strictly below three. A counter attempted once three rounds have been completed is rejected with `ERR_COUNTER_ROUNDS_EXHAUSTED`. The counter increments the offer's `version` by one, which is the anchor of the offer-version guard on payment reservation.

The supplier answers a counter by re-naming, supplying a new `offered_apr_bps` and optionally a revised `requested_pay_date`. Re-naming requires the offer to be `COUNTERED`, the session to be `LIVE` with the clock inside the window, the new rate to be strictly greater than the previous `offered_apr_bps` and at most the programme's `max_acceptable_apr_bps`, and the offer's `counter_round` incremented by one to be no greater than three. It overwrites `offered_apr_bps` with the new rate, increments `counter_round` by one, clears `counter_apr_bps` back to null, and returns the offer `COUNTERED → NAMED`, incrementing its `version`. This edge closes one negotiation cycle, and the counter-and-rename pair may repeat until `counter_round` reaches three. Note the direction of travel in both edges: each round the supplier's offered rate rises and the buyer's counter sits above it, so the negotiation converges upward from the supplier's opening bid rather than downward.

Acceptance locks the rate and awards the payable, and which party may accept depends on where the offer stands. From `NAMED`, only a programme owner may accept — the buyer is taking the supplier's named rate — and the locked `accepted_apr_bps` is copied from the offer's `offered_apr_bps`. From `COUNTERED`, only the supplier may accept — the supplier is taking the buyer's counter — and the locked `accepted_apr_bps` is copied from `counter_apr_bps` instead. Both edges are otherwise identical in their preconditions: the logical clock must lie inside the half-open window bounded by the session's `opens_at` and `closes_at` with the session `LIVE`; the clock must be strictly before the offer's `acceptance_deadline`, or the request is rejected with `ERR_ACCEPTANCE_WINDOW_CLOSED`; the programme must be `OPEN`; the supplier must have `onboarding_status` `ACTIVE`, `bank_status` `VERIFIED` and `sanctions_hold` false; the invoice's `dispute_suppressed` must be false; the APR being locked must still lie within the programme's current bounds, re-checked against a ceiling that may have tightened since the offer was named; and the concentration gate below must pass. Both edges stamp `accepted_at` with the logical-clock instant, set the offer's `status` to `ACCEPTED`, increment the offer's `version` by one, and drive the invoice `OFFERED → AWARDED`, copying the locked APR onto the invoice's own `accepted_apr_bps` and incrementing the invoice's `version`.

The concentration gate is forward-looking, which is the point of it. Before an award is allowed, the platform measures what share of the programme's face value that supplier would hold once this invoice is added. The denominator is the sum of `face_amount_cents` over every invoice on the programme whose `status` is `ELIGIBLE`, `OFFERED`, `AWARDED` or `EARLY_PAID`. The numerator is the sum of `face_amount_cents` over that same programme's invoices belonging to this supplier whose `status` is `AWARDED` or `EARLY_PAID`, **plus** the `face_amount_cents` of the invoice about to be awarded. The projected share is one hundred times the numerator divided by the denominator, rounded half up to exactly two decimals, and is 0.00 when the denominator is zero. The award is refused with `ERR_CONCENTRATION_CAP_EXCEEDED` when that projected share is strictly greater than the programme's `concentration_cap_pct`; a projected share exactly equal to the cap is allowed. This face-basis measure counts an awarded invoice at its full gross face, before any discount or funder fee is deducted and before any cash has moved.

Offers are readable individually and as a collection filtered by `invoice_id`, `session_id`, `supplier_id` and `status`. Offers reach their terminal states from several directions, all covered elsewhere: a session closing or being cancelled and the invoice maturity sweep both drive `NAMED → EXPIRED` and `COUNTERED → EXPIRED`; a newer offer on the same invoice drives `NAMED → SUPERSEDED` and `COUNTERED → SUPERSEDED`; and the dispute cascade drives `NAMED → VOIDED_BY_SUPPRESSION`, `COUNTERED → VOIDED_BY_SUPPRESSION` and `ACCEPTED → VOIDED_BY_SUPPRESSION`. Every one of those transitions increments the offer's `version` by one.

### 3.7. Funder Capital, Tranches and Choosing Who Pays

Not every early payment comes off the buyer's own balance sheet. A platform operator registers a third-party funder with its `legal_name` between 1 and 200 characters, a `capital_limit_cents` strictly greater than zero that is the hard ceiling on how much committed capital may cover any single instant, a `min_apr_bps` between 1 and 5000 that is the lowest accepted APR the funder is willing to finance, a `fee_spread_bps` between 0 and 500 that is added to the platform's tier rate when the funder's fee is computed, and a `max_single_invoice_cents` strictly greater than zero that is the largest invoice face the funder will finance in one payment. A new funder opens in `status` `ACTIVE` with `encumbered_capital_cents` at 0 and `available_capital_cents` at 0, since no tranche has been committed yet. The three funder states are `ACTIVE`, `PAUSED` and `WITHDRAWN`, the last being terminal.

Capital is pledged in tranches. A tranche carries the owning `funder_id`, a `committed_amount_cents` strictly greater than zero, and a closed interval bounded by `effective_from` and `effective_to`, where `effective_to` may equal but must not precede `effective_from`. The interval is closed on both ends: a tranche whose `effective_to` falls on a given date still covers that date. Tranches are persisted in `status` `ACTIVE`, and the only other tranche state is the terminal `EXPIRED`. Tranches on one funder are explicitly permitted to overlap in time — the funder's exposure at an instant is the sum of the tranches covering it, which is the aggregate-capacity semantics this platform assumes.

Because overlap is legal, the capital ceiling is enforced on the summed exposure rather than on any single tranche. When a new tranche is submitted, the platform tests a set of candidate instants consisting of the new tranche's `effective_from` together with every boundary point of existing tranches that falls inside the new interval. At each candidate instant, the already-committed capital of that funder at that instant, plus the new `committed_amount_cents`, must be at most the funder's `capital_limit_cents`. If any candidate instant would breach the ceiling, the tranche is refused with `ERR_FUNDER_CAPITAL_OVERCOMMITTED`. Overlap alone never fails; only summed exposure does.

A funder's `available_capital_cents` is derived, never supplied. Committed capital at a date is the sum of `committed_amount_cents` over that funder's tranches whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose closed interval contains that date; expired tranches contribute nothing. `available_capital_cents` is then that committed figure at the logical clock's current date minus the funder's `encumbered_capital_cents`, floored at zero so it never goes negative. `encumbered_capital_cents` is itself the sum of `net_payment_cents` over that funder's early payments whose `funding_status` is `RESERVED` or `DEPLOYED`. Both figures are recomputed after every tranche creation or expiry, after every change to an early payment's `funding_status`, and on every clock advance. A funder's own record and its tranche collection are both readable, the tranche collection optionally filtered by `status`.

Participation changes are driven by an `action` field taking one of three values. `PAUSE` moves `ACTIVE → PAUSED` and requires the funder to be `ACTIVE`; a paused funder drops out of the selector's eligibility filter but its `encumbered_capital_cents` is left untouched, so reservations already standing against it continue to settle normally. `RESUME` moves `PAUSED → ACTIVE` and requires the funder to be `PAUSED`; this edge is what makes the funder lifecycle cyclic. `WITHDRAW` moves either `ACTIVE` or `PAUSED` to the terminal `WITHDRAWN`, and it carries one hard precondition: the funder's `encumbered_capital_cents` must be exactly zero, so a funder cannot walk away while money is still standing behind live payments. Withdrawal cascades — every `ACTIVE` tranche of that funder becomes `EXPIRED`, in ascending `created_at` order, each with its own `version` increment — and the funder's `available_capital_cents` is recomputed to 0. All three actions increment the funder's `version` by one. Tranches also expire on their own through the clock sweep of §3.5, when the clock's date passes a tranche's `effective_to`; that path increments both the tranche's `version` and the funder's `version` by one and recomputes available capital identically.

Deciding who pays for a given early payment is a strict waterfall over the programme's `funding_policy`. Under `SELF_ONLY` the candidate order is buyer self-funding alone. Under `FUNDER_ONLY` it is a third-party funder alone. Under `SELF_THEN_FUNDER` it is buyer self-funding first and a third-party funder second. The candidates are tried strictly left to right, and the first one that can pay wins. Buyer self-funding can pay when the buyer's `self_funding_enabled` is true and the buyer's `credit_used_cents` plus the pre-fee net of this payment is at most the buyer's `credit_limit_cents`; exact equality is acceptable. In that case the payment's `funding_source` is set to `BUYER_SELF` and its `funder_id` stays null. A third-party funder can pay when the selector described below returns a winner, in which case `funding_source` is set to `FUNDER` and `funder_id` is set to the winner's identifier. The pre-fee net used throughout this decision is the invoice's `face_amount_cents` minus the payment's provisional `discount_amount_cents`, computed before any funder fee exists.

When the waterfall exhausts its candidates without a payer, the error reported is the one belonging to the **last** candidate in the order, never the first. If the last candidate was a third-party funder, the answer is `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_FUNDER` (HTTP 422). Otherwise, if the buyer's `self_funding_enabled` is false, the answer is `ERR_SELF_FUNDING_DISABLED` (HTTP 409); and failing that, the answer is `ERR_BUYER_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 402). The practical consequence is worth stating plainly: a `SELF_THEN_FUNDER` programme whose buyer has insufficient credit headroom and which then finds no eligible funder reports `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_FUNDER`, not the credit-limit error, because the funder was the last candidate tried.

The funder selector runs over every funder row and works in three stages. It first fixes two reference values: the projected value date, which is the offer's `requested_pay_date` advanced by the platform's two-day settlement lag, and the pre-fee net described above. Stage one is an eligibility filter, and a funder passes it only if all four of these hold — its `status` is `ACTIVE`; its `min_apr_bps` is at most the payment's `accepted_apr_bps`; its `max_single_invoice_cents` is at least the invoice's `face_amount_cents`; and its committed capital at the projected value date minus its `encumbered_capital_cents` is at least the pre-fee net. Stage two scores each surviving funder by summing three terms. The first term rewards rate headroom: take the payment's `accepted_apr_bps` minus that funder's `min_apr_bps`, cap the result at 400 basis points by taking the smaller of the two, and double it. The second term penalises cost: three times the funder's `fee_spread_bps`, subtracted. The third term is a depth bonus of 150 points, added only when that funder's committed capital at the projected value date minus its `encumbered_capital_cents` is at least twice the pre-fee net, and zero otherwise. Stage three ranks the survivors and picks the first: the highest score wins; ties are broken by the lower `fee_spread_bps`; remaining ties are broken by the earlier `created_at`, ascending — never by `id`. If the eligible pool is empty the selector returns no winner, and the waterfall handles that as described above.

The non-linearity in that score is deliberate, and a small illustration shows why. Consider a payment whose `accepted_apr_bps` is 900 on an invoice of 1000000 cents with a provisional discount of 7500 cents, so the pre-fee net is 992500 cents. A first funder with `min_apr_bps` 600, `fee_spread_bps` 30 and 60000000 cents of free capital scores 600 for rate headroom, loses 90 to fee drag, and earns the 150-point depth bonus, for 660. A second funder with `min_apr_bps` 500, `fee_spread_bps` 18 and only 1500000 cents of free capital scores 800 for rate headroom — its headroom of 400 basis points is exactly at the cap — loses 54 to fee drag, and earns no depth bonus because its free capital is below twice the pre-fee net, for 746. The second funder wins despite having a small fraction of the first funder's capital, because the depth bonus is worth less than the rate headroom and the wider spread costs three points per basis point.

### 3.8. Reserving, Executing and Settling an Early Payment

An early payment is the cash leg of an awarded payable, and it is the only entity in this system that carries three independent lifecycles on one record. The **cash axis**, `funding_status`, tracks which balance sheet is standing behind the payment right now and takes the values `RESERVED`, `DEPLOYED`, `RELEASED` and `VOID`, of which the last two are terminal. The **remittance axis**, `settlement_status`, tracks whether the money has left and whether the bank confirmed it, and takes the values `UNSENT`, `REMITTED`, `CONFIRMED` and `FAILED`, of which the last two are terminal. The **classification axis**, `accounting_status`, records the trade-payable-versus-debt treatment and takes the values `UNCLASSIFIED`, `TRADE_PAYABLE` and `DEBT_RECLASSIFIED`; it has no terminal state at all and advances entirely independently of the other two, including after the cash axis has reached `VOID`.

A treasury officer reserves the payment by naming the `offer_id` of the accepted offer to be settled and the `expected_offer_version` guard from §2.6. Reservation requires the offer's `status` to be `ACCEPTED`, the invoice's `status` to be `AWARDED` with `dispute_suppressed` false, the programme to be `OPEN`, and the supplier to have `onboarding_status` `ACTIVE`, `bank_status` `VERIFIED` and `sanctions_hold` false. It also requires that the days-early figure on the pay-date basis is at least the programme's `min_days_early` — otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_DAYS_EARLY` — and that the funding waterfall of §3.7 actually resolves to a payer.

The reservation copies a good deal of state onto itself and then freezes it. `invoice_id` is copied from the offer. `accepted_apr_bps` is copied from the offer's locked rate at creation and **never re-read from the offer afterwards**, so a later change on the offer cannot silently re-price a payment in flight. `face_covered_cents` is always the whole `face_amount_cents` of the invoice, because partial early payment is out of scope for this platform. `requested_pay_date` is copied from the offer and is the projected execution date the provisional pricing works from. The row is created with `funding_status` `RESERVED`, `settlement_status` `UNSENT` and `accounting_status` `UNCLASSIFIED`, and `execution_date` and `value_date` both null. `funding_source`, `funder_id`, `days_early`, `discount_amount_cents`, `funder_fee_cents`, `net_payment_cents` and `default_accounting_status` are all written at this moment. Reservation encumbers whichever balance sheet won the waterfall — a funder's `encumbered_capital_cents` rises by `net_payment_cents` and its available capital is recomputed, or the buyer's `credit_used_cents` rises by the same amount — and it moves the invoice's face ledger, setting `face_covered_cents` to the full face and `remaining_face_cents` to 0. It also increments the offer's `version` by one, which is what makes any further reservation carrying the old `expected_offer_version` stale.

Reserving a second time on the same offer is legal and is how a treasury officer switches funding source before the money moves. Any prior payment on that offer whose `funding_status` is `RESERVED` or `DEPLOYED` is driven to `VOID` — leaving its own `settlement_status` and `accounting_status` untouched — and its encumbrance is released according to that row's own `funding_source`, giving back either the funder's capital or the buyer's credit. The invoice's `face_covered_cents` and `remaining_face_cents` are restored in the same step, and the voided row's `version` increments.

**Days early** is a whole count of calendar days with no business-day adjustment: it is the invoice's `net_due_date` minus a basis date, both taken as dates. The basis date depends on which variant is in force. On the **pay-date basis**, the basis date is the offer's `requested_pay_date` at reservation time and the payment's actual `execution_date` at execution time; this variant ignores the settlement lag, so the supplier is charged for the days between the payment instruction and maturity. On the **value-date basis**, used only at settlement confirmation, the basis date is the payment's `value_date`, which is the `execution_date` advanced by the two-day settlement lag; this variant charges only for the days the funds were genuinely out, and is therefore exactly two days shorter than the pay-date figure. Days early may be 0 — which fails any programme whose `min_days_early` is 1 or more — but is never negative, because both the requested pay date and the execution date are constrained to fall no later than `net_due_date`.

**The discount** is simple interest on the gross face over those days. Multiply the invoice's `face_amount_cents` by the payment's frozen `accepted_apr_bps`, multiply by the days-early figure for the variant in force, then divide by the product of ten thousand — converting basis points to a rate — and the 360-day year, and round the result half up once, to a whole cent. As an illustration, a face of 108751 cents at 800 basis points with a `net_due_date` of 2026-09-30 and an `execution_date` of 2026-08-05 gives 56 days on the pay-date basis and a discount of 1353 cents, and 54 days on the value-date basis and a discount of 1305 cents.

**The funder fee** is computed immediately after the discount, and its tier is keyed on the **pre-fee net** — the invoice's `face_amount_cents` minus the payment's `discount_amount_cents` — never on the final net after the fee has been taken out. A payment whose `funding_source` is `BUYER_SELF` has a `funder_fee_cents` of 0, always. Otherwise the tier rate is 45 basis points when the pre-fee net is below 5000000 cents, 30 basis points when it is at least 5000000 and below 25000000 cents, and 18 basis points at 25000000 cents and above; the brackets are half-open at the top, so a pre-fee net exactly at a bracket boundary belongs to the higher bracket. The effective rate is that tier rate plus the chosen funder's `fee_spread_bps`, and the fee is the pre-fee net multiplied by the effective rate, divided by ten thousand, rounded half up once. Why the ordering is pinned is easiest to see near a boundary: a face of 25661600 cents at 1000 basis points over 90 days gives a discount of 641540 cents and a pre-fee net of 25020060 cents, which sits in the 18-basis-point tier and yields a fee of 45036 cents and a net payment of 24975024 cents. Keying the tier on the post-fee figure instead would drop it below 25000000, select the 30-basis-point tier, and produce a materially different answer — that is not the rule.

**The net payment** follows immediately: `net_payment_cents` is the invoice's `face_amount_cents` minus the payment's `discount_amount_cents` minus its `funder_fee_cents`. All three terms are already whole cents, so no further rounding is applied. The buyer's `credit_used_cents` is likewise recomputed in full after every change to a payment's `funding_status` and after every recomputation of a net payment: it is the sum of `net_payment_cents` over the buyer's early payments whose `funding_source` is `BUYER_SELF` and whose `funding_status` is `RESERVED` or `DEPLOYED`, restricted to invoices belonging to that buyer's programmes. Payments in `RELEASED` or `VOID` are excluded, which is precisely why settling or voiding a payment frees the buyer's credit again.

Execution is the moment cash is instructed, and it moves two axes at once. It requires `funding_status` `RESERVED`, `settlement_status` `UNSENT`, the invoice's `dispute_suppressed` false, the supplier's `sanctions_hold` false, and the logical clock's date to be no later than the invoice's `net_due_date`. The execution date is never client-supplied: `execution_date` is stamped with the logical clock's date at the moment of the call, and a caller who wants a particular execution date moves the clock first. `value_date` is then set to `execution_date` advanced by two days. The cash axis moves `RESERVED → DEPLOYED` and, on the very same call, the remittance axis moves `UNSENT → REMITTED`. Because the actual execution date usually differs from the originally requested pay date, `days_early`, `discount_amount_cents` — still on the pay-date basis, now measured from the real `execution_date` — `funder_fee_cents` and `net_payment_cents` are all recomputed, and the encumbrance standing on the funder or the buyer is adjusted by exactly the resulting net delta. The invoice's `provisional_discount_cents` is written from the recomputed discount. The payment's `version` increments by one. The two axes remain independent even though both moved here: settlement can subsequently fail while the cash axis stays `DEPLOYED` until the failure path voids it, and `accounting_status` is untouched throughout.

Execution also produces exactly one **remittance advice**, an immutable record of what was sent. It records the `early_payment_id` it belongs to and an `advice_number` formed as the literal concatenation of `RA-`, the invoice's `invoice_number`, a hyphen, and a zero-padded two-digit number equal to the count of remittance advices already existing on that same invoice plus one. It snapshots the supplier's `legal_name` into `supplier_legal_name` and the supplier's `verified_account_last4` into `supplier_account_last4`, and neither is ever refreshed afterwards. It snapshots the invoice's `face_amount_cents` into `gross_face_cents`, the payment's `discount_amount_cents` — the pay-date-basis figure — into `discount_cents`, its `funder_fee_cents` into `fee_cents`, its `net_payment_cents` into `net_paid_cents`, and its `value_date` into the advice's own `value_date`. Its `delivery_status` is `SENT` when the supplier had a non-null `remittance_email` at the moment of execution and `BOUNCED` otherwise — so a supplier registered without a remittance address receives no advice, and every advice raised for it is recorded as bounced. The advices raised for one payment are listable through that payment's nested remittance-advices collection.

Confirming settlement is the successful ending, and it triggers the deepest calculation-driven cascade in the platform. It requires `funding_status` `DEPLOYED`, `settlement_status` `REMITTED`, and the logical clock's date to be no earlier than the payment's `value_date`. The remittance axis moves `REMITTED → CONFIRMED` and the cash axis moves `DEPLOYED → RELEASED`, releasing the encumbrance in full — a funder's `encumbered_capital_cents` falls by `net_payment_cents`, or the buyer's `credit_used_cents` falls by the same amount. The discount is then recomputed on the value-date basis, and `days_early`, `funder_fee_cents` and `net_payment_cents` are rewritten from it. Because the remittance axis reached `CONFIRMED`, the invoice moves `AWARDED → EARLY_PAID`, its `final_discount_cents` is written from the value-date-basis discount, and its `discount_true_up_cents` is written as `final_discount_cents` minus `provisional_discount_cents` — a signed figure that is negative whenever the APR is positive, because the value-date basis always spans two fewer days. In the illustration above, the true-up is 1305 minus 1353, or negative 48 cents. Because the invoice reached `EARLY_PAID`, the programme's `adopted_invoice_count` increments and its `adoption_rate_pct`, `weighted_avg_accepted_apr_bps` and `rate_posture` are recomputed; and because the rate posture may have left `DISCOVERY`, the programme's `max_acceptable_apr_bps` is rewritten by the ceiling rule of §3.10. The payment's `version` and the invoice's `version` each increment by one, and so does the programme's.

Recording a failed settlement is the compensating path, and it is the only operation here that takes a `failure_reason`. It requires `funding_status` `DEPLOYED` and `settlement_status` `REMITTED`. The remittance axis moves `REMITTED → FAILED` and the cash axis moves `DEPLOYED → VOID`, releasing the funder's `encumbered_capital_cents` or the buyer's `credit_used_cents` in full. The invoice's `face_covered_cents` returns to 0 and its `remaining_face_cents` returns to `face_amount_cents`, restoring face conservation exactly. Deliberately, the invoice's `status` stays `AWARDED`, its `provisional_discount_cents` returns to 0, and the offer stays `ACCEPTED` — so a replacement reservation can be created against the same award without renegotiating the rate. The remittance advice row is retained unchanged as the audit record of the failed remittance. Both the payment's `version` and the invoice's `version` increment by one.

Early payments are readable individually and as a collection filtered by `offer_id`, by `invoice_id`, by `funder_id`, by `funding_status` and by `settlement_status`, in any combination.

### 3.9. Accounting Treatment: Trade Payable or Reclassified Debt

Whether an early-paid payable stays a trade payable or becomes disclosed debt is a judgement with real consequences for a buyer's balance sheet, and this platform records both a default and the controller's decision. The default is computed once, at reservation, written to `default_accounting_status`, and never recomputed afterwards. A payment funded from the buyer's own balance sheet — `funding_source` `BUYER_SELF` — always defaults to `TRADE_PAYABLE`, because no third party has interposed itself. For a funder-financed payment, the default is `DEBT_RECLASSIFIED` when either the payment's `days_early` is strictly greater than the buyer's `reclass_days_threshold` or the payment's `accepted_apr_bps` is strictly greater than the buyer's `reclass_apr_threshold_bps`, and `TRADE_PAYABLE` otherwise. Both comparisons are strict, so a payment whose days early exactly equals the buyer's threshold — 30 days on a default configuration — stays `TRADE_PAYABLE`, and the same holds for an accepted APR exactly at the buyer's threshold of 900 basis points on a default configuration. The days-early input to this decision is the pay-date-basis figure frozen at reservation, not the value-date figure computed later.

A treasury officer records the actual treatment by submitting a `classification` of either `TRADE_PAYABLE` or `DEBT_RECLASSIFIED`, with an optional `override_reason`. This axis carries no funding or settlement precondition whatever — the payment need only exist, and classification is permitted at any `funding_status` including `VOID` and at any `settlement_status` including `FAILED`. That absence of preconditions is exactly what makes the classification axis independent of the other two. Whenever the submitted `classification` differs from the payment's `default_accounting_status`, `override_reason` must be present and non-empty after trimming whitespace; a missing or blank justification on a diverging classification is rejected with `ERR_RECLASS_OVERRIDE_REASON_REQUIRED`. When the submitted classification matches the default, `override_reason` is not required, and any value supplied is stored as given.

All four movements on this axis are permitted and behave identically in structure: `UNCLASSIFIED → TRADE_PAYABLE`, `UNCLASSIFIED → DEBT_RECLASSIFIED`, `TRADE_PAYABLE → DEBT_RECLASSIFIED` and `DEBT_RECLASSIFIED → TRADE_PAYABLE`. Each writes `accounting_status`, stores `override_reason` when one was supplied, and increments the payment's `version` by one. Re-classification is permitted without limit, because this axis has no terminal state — a controller may revisit the judgement as often as the auditors require.

### 3.10. Programme Adoption, Rate Optimisation and Concentration Reporting

A dynamic-discounting programme that nobody uses is worthless, and one everybody uses at a generous rate is expensive. The platform therefore measures its own take-up continuously and lets that measurement tighten the price. Adoption is recomputed whenever payables are ingested, when a programme is opened, and on every invoice status change, and it is read back through the programme's analytics view.

`eligible_invoice_count` is the number of invoices on the programme whose `status` is `ELIGIBLE`, `OFFERED`, `AWARDED` or `EARLY_PAID`. `adopted_invoice_count` is the number whose `status` is exactly `EARLY_PAID`. `adoption_rate_pct` is one hundred times the adopted count divided by the eligible count, rounded half up to exactly two decimals — and when the eligible count is zero it is exactly 0.00, never null and never a division error. Note which invoices are excluded from **both** the numerator and the denominator: invoices in `SUPPRESSED` and invoices in `MATURED`. A consequence follows that implementers should expect rather than treat as a bug — suppressing an invoice raises the adoption rate of the remainder, because the denominator shrinks.

`rate_posture` is a band derived from that percentage, and its edges are inclusive at the lower end. An adoption rate below 25.00 percent is `DISCOVERY`. An adoption rate at 25.00 percent or above but below 60.00 percent is `OPTIMISED` — so 25.00 is `OPTIMISED` and 59.99 is still `OPTIMISED`. An adoption rate at 60.00 percent or above is `THROTTLED`. A brand-new programme with no invoices therefore sits at 0.00 percent in `DISCOVERY`.

`weighted_avg_accepted_apr_bps` is the face-weighted mean rate the programme has actually paid, recomputed whenever an invoice of the programme enters or leaves `EARLY_PAID`. Take every invoice on the programme whose `status` is `EARLY_PAID`, multiply each one's `accepted_apr_bps` by its `face_amount_cents`, sum those products, divide by the sum of those same faces, and round the quotient half up to a whole basis point. When no invoice on the programme is `EARLY_PAID`, the figure is 0. Because the weighting is by face value, the result is order-independent and needs no tie-break.

The ceiling rule then runs at the end of every adoption recomputation, and it is the mechanism by which proven adoption buys the buyer a better price. While `rate_posture` is `DISCOVERY`, the candidate ceiling is the programme's existing `max_acceptable_apr_bps`, left alone. Under `OPTIMISED`, the candidate is the weighted average accepted APR multiplied by 90 percent, truncated toward zero to a whole basis point, and then raised to 200 basis points if that product fell below it. Under `THROTTLED`, the candidate is the same computation at 75 percent instead of 90, likewise floored at 200 basis points. The programme's `max_acceptable_apr_bps` is then set to the smaller of its current value and that candidate. This last step makes the ceiling **monotone**: it can only ever tighten, and no adoption movement, band change or invoice reversal ever loosens it again. As an illustration, a programme whose `max_acceptable_apr_bps` is 1200 and which has a single `EARLY_PAID` invoice at 1000 basis points, at an adoption rate of exactly 25.00 percent and therefore in `OPTIMISED`, produces a candidate of 900 basis points and a new ceiling of 900. Suppliers naming rates after that point are bound by the tightened figure, and an offer named before the tightening is re-checked against the new ceiling at acceptance time — that is the scenario the acceptance-time APR re-check of §3.6 exists to catch.

The analytics view also reports supplier concentration on two different bases, and they deliberately disagree. `concentration_top_supplier_face_pct` is the largest face-basis share held by any single supplier on the programme, computed exactly as the acceptance gate of §3.6 computes it but without the forward-looking addition: the numerator is that supplier's invoices on the programme in `AWARDED` or `EARLY_PAID` summed at `face_amount_cents`, and the denominator is every invoice on the programme in `ELIGIBLE`, `OFFERED`, `AWARDED` or `EARLY_PAID` summed at `face_amount_cents`. `concentration_top_supplier_funded_pct` is the largest funded-basis share: the numerator is the sum of `net_payment_cents` over that supplier's early payments on the programme whose `funding_status` is `DEPLOYED` or `RELEASED`, and the denominator is the sum of `net_payment_cents` over every early payment on the programme with those same funding statuses. Both are one hundred times numerator over denominator, rounded half up to exactly two decimals, and both are exactly 0.00 when their denominator is zero. The face basis measures gross commitment and counts an awarded invoice at full face before any cash moves; the funded basis measures net cash actually deployed, so discounts and funder fees shrink each supplier's numerator by a different proportion, and awarded-but-unexecuted invoices are excluded from it entirely. The same portfolio therefore yields two different percentages, and neither is wrong. The analytics view returns `eligible_invoice_count`, `adopted_invoice_count`, `adoption_rate_pct`, `rate_posture`, `weighted_avg_accepted_apr_bps`, `max_acceptable_apr_bps`, `concentration_top_supplier_face_pct`, `concentration_top_supplier_funded_pct` and `concentration_cap_pct`, and it writes nothing.

Beyond analytics, the platform exposes the read surfaces each participant needs. A buyer record can be read with its live self-funding utilisation, and the programmes of one buyer are listable with an optional `status` filter. A programme can be read with its current APR ceiling and rate posture. A supplier record can be read with both onboarding axes and the compliance flag visible, and a supplier's bank accounts are listable with an optional `status` filter. A funder record can be read with its live available and encumbered capital.
