# Lateral Partner Hiring API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Lateral partner recruiting is the single largest discretionary investment a law firm makes, and it is almost always made on numbers that nobody has audited. A candidate arrives through a retained legal search firm, hands over a questionnaire describing a book of business worth several million dollars a year, and the firm must decide — inside an exclusivity window, under confidentiality, and before the candidate's current firm notices — how much of that book will actually move, whether the firm is ethically permitted to accept it, what the hire will cost once the recruiter's placement fee and the office build-out are counted, and what the partner should be measured against a year later. Today that work is done in spreadsheets that disagree with one another, in conflicts memoranda that never feed back into the compensation model, and in committee meetings whose quorum arithmetic is reconstructed from memory.

This document specifies the Lateral Partner Hiring API: a single system of record for the whole arc from confidential approach to post-hire performance tracking. It carries the search firm engagement and its fee exposure, the lateral partner questionnaire and the disclosed client book, the historical collection evidence that either substantiates or contradicts the candidate's own figures, the conflicts clearance and waiver strategy across that client base, the compensation offer and the committee's vote on it, the integration plan with its practice group assignment and cross-selling schedule, and finally the periodic performance review against the business plan. Every one of those artifacts is linked, and — critically — every change to an upstream artifact is propagated to the downstream ones inside the same request, so that no committee ever votes on economics that a declined matter has already invalidated.

Two design commitments run through the entire specification. The first is that valuations are **dual-basis**: the same client book yields a self-reported total and a validated total, and the assessment records both while nominating exactly one as governing. The second is that staleness is a **state**, not a delay. Where a downstream artifact has been overtaken by an upstream change, the system marks it `STALE` or `REPRICE_REQUIRED` and refuses to price against it, rather than silently letting the two drift apart. All API paths described here are mounted under the `/lateral-hiring` prefix.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the API must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role under which the request is made. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the seven role names enumerated below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If both headers are well-formed but the named role is not among the roles authorized for the target endpoint in the endpoint table of §4, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). These two checks precede every other form of validation, including the existence of the resources named in the URL.

The seven roles are `SystemAdmin`, `RecruitingCoordinator`, `ConflictsCounsel`, `CommitteeChair`, `CommitteeMember`, `PracticeGroupLeader`, and `Candidate`.

A `SystemAdmin` is the firm's systems operator: it seeds practice groups and search firms, ingests collection data from the accounting system, and drives the logical clock. A `RecruitingCoordinator` runs the lateral pipeline — engaging search firms, opening candidate records and questionnaires, reviewing diligence, and producing portability assessments. A `ConflictsCounsel` acts for the general counsel's office, running conflicts clearance across the candidate's client base, pursuing waivers, and rescinding declinations. A `CommitteeChair` models and submits compensation offers, may vote, and finalizes the tally — but never on an offer they themselves drafted. A `CommitteeMember` reads the diligence package and votes, and cannot draft, submit, or finalize anything. A `PracticeGroupLeader` leads the receiving practice group and owns the integration plan, the cross-selling introductions, and the post-hire performance reviews for that group. A `Candidate` is the lateral partner under consideration, who fills in the questionnaire, discloses and withdraws client book entries, submits the questionnaire, and accepts the offer.

**How `X-User-ID` is interpreted differs by role, and this distinction is load-bearing.** When `X-User-Role` is `SystemAdmin`, `RecruitingCoordinator`, `ConflictsCounsel`, `CommitteeChair`, or `CommitteeMember`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — for example as `sponsor_user_id` when a candidate record is opened, as `created_by_user_id` when an offer is drafted, or as `voter_user_id` when a committee vote is cast — but it never compares such an identifier to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `PracticeGroupLeader`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `PracticeGroup`; ownership checks on any resource that references a practice group compare that resource's practice-group foreign key to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `Candidate`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Candidate`; ownership checks on any resource that references a candidate compare that resource's candidate foreign key to `X-User-ID`.

Ownership failures on those two entity-backed roles are reported as `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). Concretely: a `Candidate` acting on a `LateralQuestionnaire`, a `ClientBookEntry`, or a `CompensationOffer` must be the candidate that owns it, reached through the questionnaire's `candidate_id` for entries and through the offer's `candidate_id` for offers; a `PracticeGroupLeader` acting on an `IntegrationPlan`, a `CrossSellIntroduction`, or a `PerformanceReview` must match that plan's `practice_group_id`. The ownership check sits *after* schema validation and version pinning but *before* any state-machine or business rule, so a request that is both unowned and in the wrong state reports the ownership failure.

Two further separations are enforced against `X-User-ID` even when the role is correct, and both are described in §2.9: the sponsoring user recorded on a candidate may not vote on that candidate's offers, and the chair who created an offer may not finalize it.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls the page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, must be at least 1, and must be at most 100. `offset` controls the number of records skipped before the page begins: it defaults to 0 and must be 0 or greater. A `limit` outside the inclusive range 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is a schema violation and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400) rather than being silently clamped.

List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending unless a specific narrative in §3 pins a different order for a particular computation. The `meta` object of every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` — the number of records matching the filters across all pages, not merely the number returned on this page — and `has_more`, a boolean that is true exactly when records matching the filters remain beyond the current page.

Filter parameters on list endpoints are named after the foreign-key field they narrow on the child entity, so that every relationship in the model is reverse-reachable. Engagements can be narrowed by `search_firm_id`, `practice_group_id`, or `status`; candidates by `target_practice_group_id`, `sourcing_engagement_id`, `pipeline_status`, or `clearance_status`; questionnaires by `candidate_id` or `status`; client book entries by `questionnaire_id`, `status`, or `include_withdrawn`; collection records by `candidate_id`, `client_book_entry_id`, `record_type`, or `status`; portability assessments by `questionnaire_id` or `status`; conflicts checks by `client_book_entry_id`, `status`, or `severity`; waivers by `conflict_check_id` or `status`; matter declinations by `conflict_check_id`, `client_book_entry_id`, or `status`; offers by `candidate_id`, `portability_assessment_id`, or `status`; committee votes by `offer_id`, `status`, or `round_number`; integration plans by `candidate_id`, `practice_group_id`, `offer_id`, or `status`; cross-selling introductions by `integration_plan_id` or `status`; and performance reviews by `candidate_id`, `integration_plan_id`, or `performance_flag`.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and the Logical Clock

Every resource is identified by an `id` that the server mints as a UUID. Identifiers carry no type prefix and no embedded semantics: a caller must never parse an `id` to infer what kind of resource it points at. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with exactly three fractional-second digits, recorded when the row is first written.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format that is refreshed on every mutation of the row, and `version`, an integer that begins at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of the row. There is one exception: a `CollectionRecord` is an append-only observation. It carries only `id` and `created_at` — both `updated_at` and `version` are absent from its representation, because the row is never modified after it is written, has no update endpoint and no delete endpoint.

All date-sensitive behaviour in this system is evaluated against a **logical clock** rather than the host machine's wall clock. Before any caller sets it, the logical clock reads `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`. A `SystemAdmin` may move the clock to any instant by supplying a `to_iso8601` value; the clock is then set to that instant, and the move itself triggers three sweeps of system-driven transitions that are described where they belong: search firm engagements reaching the end of their fee-protection tail (§3.1), undecided conflict waivers passing their response deadline (§3.5), and active integration plans passing the end of their onboarding window (§3.7). All three sweeps land inside the clock-advance request.

### 2.5. Money, Shares and Rounding Discipline

All monetary amounts are decimal numbers carried to **2 decimal places**, and every monetary rounding in this specification is **round half up** — a value exactly at the midpoint rounds away from zero. All ratios, percentages and shares that the system computes (`variance_pct`, `top_client_share`, `top3_client_share`, `attainment_pct`, and the rate-alignment factor of the expected-contribution model) are carried to **4 decimal places**, also rounded half up. Percentage-typed inputs supplied by callers — `fee_pct`, `max_fee_pct`, `claimed_portability_pct`, `claimed_realization_rate`, `declared_realization_rate` — are likewise 4-decimal values, and `leverage_ratio`, `guarantee_budget_total_usd`, and every rate field are 2-decimal values.

Where a rounding step is significant, this document says so at the point of the calculation, and the implementer must not reorder it. Two patterns recur and are worth stating once. First, **per-line rounding precedes summation**: each client book entry's portable amount is rounded to 2 decimal places on its own, and the assessment totals are plain sums of those already-rounded amounts with no second rounding applied to the sum. Summing at full precision and rounding the total is a different answer in cents and is wrong. Second, one deliberate exception uses **round down** rather than round half up: the per-matter split of a declined entry's revenue floors each share to whole cents, and the entire leftover remainder is allocated to a single named share (§3.5).

### 2.6. Version Pinning on Diligence Writes

Two write operations in this system take a caller-supplied version of their parent resource, and both reject the write when the caller's view of that parent is out of date. The value is a required input field on those two requests, never optional.

Creating a `CompensationOffer` for a candidate requires `expected_candidate_version`, carrying the `version` of that `Candidate` as the client last observed it. If the candidate's current `version` differs from the supplied value, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CANDIDATE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This matters because offer creation itself rewrites the candidate — it sets `open_offer_id` and bumps the candidate's `version` — so a client working from a stale read may be about to supersede an offer it has never seen, on a candidate whose clearance dimension may also have moved.

Creating a `PortabilityAssessment` on a questionnaire requires `expected_questionnaire_version`, carrying the `version` of that `LateralQuestionnaire` as the client last observed it. If the questionnaire's current `version` differs, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_QUESTIONNAIRE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A new assessment supersedes every prior assessment on the same questionnaire and rewrites `latest_assessment_id`, so a caller holding an outdated version is valuing a client book it has not fully read.

No other endpoint in this API accepts a version field. In particular, re-drafting an offer, submitting it, voting on it, finalizing it, countering it, accepting it, adding client book entries, appending collection records, and every conflicts, integration and performance operation take no version input at all, and must not be made to require one.

### 2.7. Immediate Consistency and Multi-Hop Cascades

The consistency model is immediate. Every cascade, every ledger adjustment and every derived-field recomputation described in this document lands inside the request that triggered it, before that request returns. There is no background worker, no eventual reconciliation, and no window in which a parent and its children disagree. Where a downstream artifact has been overtaken by an upstream change, the system does not model that as lag; it models it as a status. A portability assessment that no longer reflects its client book becomes `STALE`, and an offer priced against such an assessment becomes `REPRICE_REQUIRED`.

Cascades in this domain run up to four and five hops deep, and this document numbers the hops wherever it describes one, because the hops are what make the arithmetic reproducible. Withdrawing a candidate abandons the questionnaire and lapses the open offer (hop 2), which releases the guarantee reservation and abandons the integration plan (hop 3), which cancels that plan's proposed introductions and returns the onboarding slot (hop 4). Refusing a conflict waiver blocks the conflicts check (hop 2), which declines the client book entry and creates one matter declination per matter type (hop 3), which turns the current assessment `STALE` and recomputes its totals (hop 4), which in turn drives any offer under committee review against that assessment to `REPRICE_REQUIRED` (hop 5).

Whenever any entity's field is written by any hop of a cascade, that entity's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increments by 1 — a cascade-driven write is a mutation like any other. This applies to entities the caller never named: the `SearchFirm` whose `engagement_count` moves, the `PracticeGroup` whose budget ledger or `active_onboarding_count` moves, the `LateralQuestionnaire` whose `entry_count` moves, and the `CompensationOffer` whose vote counters move.

### 2.8. Conserved Quantities

Three arithmetic identities hold at every observable instant in this system, and they are the cheapest way to check an implementation. They are not advisory; a mutation that leaves any of them broken is a defect.

The **guarantee budget identity** holds on every `PracticeGroup`: `guarantee_budget_total_usd` always equals `available_guarantee_budget_usd` plus `reserved_guarantee_budget_usd` plus `committed_guarantee_total_usd`. `reserved_guarantee_budget_usd` is the sum of `guaranteed_comp_usd` over every `CompensationOffer` in status `COMMITTEE_REVIEW` whose candidate targets this group; `committed_guarantee_total_usd` is the same sum over offers in status `ACCEPTED` for candidates targeting this group; and `available_guarantee_budget_usd` is `guarantee_budget_total_usd` less both of those. Offers in every other status contribute nothing to either. The ledger is maintained incrementally as offers move, but it must always agree with those definitions, and it must agree after every release path as well as after every reservation. Every summand is already a 2-decimal amount, so maintaining the ledger introduces no rounding of its own.

The **portable book identity** holds on every `PortabilityAssessment`: `validated_portable_total_usd` plus `declined_portable_total_usd` always equals `gross_baseline_total_usd`, exactly, to the cent. This is why each client book entry keeps two amounts rather than one — `baseline_portable_amount_usd` records what the entry is worth on the validated basis regardless of whether it has been declined, and `validated_portable_amount_usd` is that same figure zeroed out when the entry stands declined. A declination moves value from one side of the identity to the other; a rescission moves it back with no drift.

The **vote ledger identity** holds on every `CompensationOffer`: `votes_cast_total` always equals `active_vote_count` plus `voided_vote_count`. `active_vote_count` is the number of `CommitteeVote` rows on the offer whose `status` is `ACTIVE`; `voided_vote_count` is the number whose `status` is `VOIDED`; and `votes_cast_total` counts every vote row on the offer across all rounds and both statuses. Because a negotiation round reset voids votes rather than deleting them, `votes_cast_total` never decreases. These three counters are plain integers with no rounding.

### 2.9. Segregation of Duties

Two independent separations of duty are enforced on committee work, and both compare `X-User-ID` against a user identifier stored on a row — never against an entity `id`.

The first protects the vote. The user recorded as `sponsor_user_id` on a candidate is the internal partner who championed that hire, and that user may not cast a committee vote on any offer for that candidate. An attempt is rejected with `ERR_SPONSOR_CANNOT_VOTE` (HTTP 409), regardless of whether the acting role is `CommitteeMember` or `CommitteeChair`.

The second protects the tally. The chair whose `X-User-ID` was recorded as `created_by_user_id` when an offer was drafted may not be the one who closes voting on it; that attempt is rejected with `ERR_OFFER_CREATOR_CANNOT_FINALIZE` (HTTP 409). Note the asymmetry: the creating chair *may* still submit the offer to committee, may counter it, and may re-draft it. Only finalization is barred.

### 2.10. Rejection Precedence

When a single request violates more than one rule, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fully determined. Checks are evaluated in the ascending order below and the **first** failure wins; no later check can mask an earlier one, and no earlier check may be skipped because a later one would also fail. The ordering reflects the firm's own priorities: professional-responsibility and segregation-of-duties questions outrank everything, because a firm may not price or vote on a hire it is ethically barred from making; referential integrity of cross-aggregate pointers comes next; then temporal and exclusivity geometry; then structural caps; and money last, because a partner's guarantee budget is the final thing that should stop a lateral hire.

1. **Authentication** — `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` present, and `X-User-Role` one of the seven declared roles. Failure: `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. **Authorization** — `X-User-Role` listed among the target endpoint's roles. Failure: `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. **Existence** — every path-templated resource is resolved **left to right in path order**, and only then are body-referenced foreign keys resolved, in the order the endpoint's input list declares them. Failure yields the 404 code for the resource that could not be found: `ERR_PRACTICE_GROUP_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SEARCH_FIRM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CANDIDATE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_QUESTIONNAIRE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLIENT_BOOK_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COLLECTION_RECORD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONFLICT_CHECK_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WAIVER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DECLINATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OFFER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VOTE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INTEGRATION_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INTRODUCTION_NOT_FOUND`, or `ERR_PERFORMANCE_REVIEW_NOT_FOUND`. Applies to every endpoint carrying a path or body resource reference.
4. **Schema** — required body fields present, types correct, enum members recognised, numeric ranges honoured, array cardinalities honoured, ISO 8601 datetimes parseable, and pagination `limit` within 1 through 100 with `offset` at least 0. Failure: `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Applies to every endpoint.
5. **Version pinning** — `expected_candidate_version` equals the candidate's `version` on offer creation; `expected_questionnaire_version` equals the questionnaire's `version` on assessment creation. Failure: `ERR_STALE_CANDIDATE_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_QUESTIONNAIRE_VERSION` (412). Applies to `POST /lateral-hiring/candidates/{candidate_id}/offers` and `POST /lateral-hiring/questionnaires/{questionnaire_id}/portability-assessments`.
6. **Resource ownership** for the entity-backed roles, as described in §2.1. Failure: `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). Applies to nine endpoints: `POST /lateral-hiring/questionnaires/{questionnaire_id}/client-book-entries`, `DELETE /lateral-hiring/client-book-entries/{entry_id}`, `POST /lateral-hiring/questionnaires/{questionnaire_id}/submit`, `POST /lateral-hiring/offers/{offer_id}/accept`, `POST /lateral-hiring/candidates/{candidate_id}/integration-plans`, `POST /lateral-hiring/integration-plans/{plan_id}/activate`, `POST /lateral-hiring/integration-plans/{plan_id}/introductions`, `POST /lateral-hiring/introductions/{introduction_id}/complete`, and `POST /lateral-hiring/candidates/{candidate_id}/performance-reviews`.
7. **State admissibility** — the target resource's state field currently holds a value from which the requested transition departs. Failure yields the invalid-state code for that entity: `ERR_CANDIDATE_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_QUESTIONNAIRE_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_ASSESSMENT_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_CONFLICT_CHECK_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_WAIVER_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_DECLINATION_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_OFFER_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_INTEGRATION_PLAN_INVALID_STATE`, or `ERR_INTRODUCTION_INVALID_STATE` (all 409). Applies to every state-transition endpoint.
8. **Sponsor may not vote** — `ERR_SPONSOR_CANNOT_VOTE` (409) on `POST /lateral-hiring/offers/{offer_id}/votes`.
9. **Creator may not finalize** — `ERR_OFFER_CREATOR_CANNOT_FINALIZE` (409) on `POST /lateral-hiring/offers/{offer_id}/finalize`.
10. **Clearance complete** — the candidate's `clearance_status` is `CLEARED`. Failure: `ERR_CLEARANCE_INCOMPLETE` (409). Applies to two endpoints: `POST /lateral-hiring/offers/{offer_id}/submit` and `POST /lateral-hiring/offers/{offer_id}/accept`.
11. **Direct-adverse handling** — a check whose `severity` is `DIRECT_ADVERSE` may not be cleared outright, and may not be waived with an `ETHICAL_WALL` strategy. Failure: `ERR_DIRECT_ADVERSE_REQUIRES_WAIVER` (409). Applies to two endpoints: `POST /lateral-hiring/conflict-checks/{conflict_check_id}/clear` and `POST /lateral-hiring/conflict-checks/{conflict_check_id}/waivers`.
12. **Prior-firm constraints** on a cross-selling slot — the non-solicitation window and the confidential-approach restriction. Failure: `ERR_NON_SOLICIT_RESTRICTION` or `ERR_CONFIDENTIAL_APPROACH_RESTRICTION` (409). Applies to `POST /lateral-hiring/integration-plans/{plan_id}/introductions`.
13. **Notice period** — a plan's `onboarding_start_date` is not earlier than the candidate's `earliest_start_date`. Failure: `ERR_NOTICE_PERIOD_NOT_ELAPSED` (409). Applies to `POST /lateral-hiring/candidates/{candidate_id}/integration-plans`.
14. **Cross-aggregate agreement** — the referenced assessment's questionnaire belongs to the path candidate; the referenced offer belongs to the path candidate; a client book entry belongs to the path questionnaire; and a check's `affected_matter_types` is a subset of the entry's `matter_types`. Failure: `ERR_ASSESSMENT_CANDIDATE_MISMATCH`, `ERR_OFFER_CANDIDATE_MISMATCH`, or `ERR_MATTER_TYPE_NOT_ON_ENTRY` (all 422). Applies to three endpoints: `POST /lateral-hiring/candidates/{candidate_id}/offers`, `POST /lateral-hiring/candidates/{candidate_id}/integration-plans`, and `POST /lateral-hiring/client-book-entries/{entry_id}/conflict-checks`.
15. **Vote round agreement** — the submitted `round_number` equals the offer's current `round_number`. Failure: `ERR_VOTE_ROUND_MISMATCH` (409). Applies to `POST /lateral-hiring/offers/{offer_id}/votes`.
16. **Assessment currency** — the referenced assessment's `status` is `CURRENT`; a `STALE` assessment yields `ERR_ASSESSMENT_STALE` and a `SUPERSEDED` one yields `ERR_ASSESSMENT_INVALID_STATE` (both 409). Applies to two endpoints: `POST /lateral-hiring/offers/{offer_id}/submit` and `POST /lateral-hiring/candidates/{candidate_id}/offers`.
17. **Practice-group exclusivity geometry** — `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_EXCLUSIVITY_CONFLICT` (409) on `POST /lateral-hiring/search-firm-engagements`.
18. **Search-firm concurrency capacity** — `ERR_SEARCH_FIRM_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409) on `POST /lateral-hiring/search-firm-engagements`.
19. **Introduction slot geometry** — containment in the onboarding window and pairwise non-overlap. Failure: `ERR_INTRODUCTION_OUTSIDE_WINDOW` or `ERR_INTRODUCTION_SLOT_CONFLICT` (409). Applies to `POST /lateral-hiring/integration-plans/{plan_id}/introductions`.
20. **Window sanity** — `ERR_LOOKBACK_WINDOW_INVALID` (422) whenever a supplied window is degenerate or inverted, or a baseline or deadline instant sits on the wrong side of the logical clock. Applies to six endpoints: `POST /lateral-hiring/search-firm-engagements`, `POST /lateral-hiring/questionnaires/{questionnaire_id}/portability-assessments`, `POST /lateral-hiring/conflict-checks/{conflict_check_id}/waivers`, `POST /lateral-hiring/candidates/{candidate_id}/integration-plans`, `POST /lateral-hiring/integration-plans/{plan_id}/introductions`, and `POST /lateral-hiring/candidates/{candidate_id}/performance-reviews`.
21. **Practice group open** — `ERR_PRACTICE_GROUP_FROZEN` (409) on the three operations that add lateral commitment: `POST /lateral-hiring/candidates`, `POST /lateral-hiring/offers/{offer_id}/submit`, and `POST /lateral-hiring/integration-plans/{plan_id}/activate`.
22. **Fee ceiling** — `ERR_FEE_PCT_ABOVE_FIRM_MAX` (422) on `POST /lateral-hiring/search-firm-engagements`.
23. **Client name uniqueness** — `ERR_DUPLICATE_CLIENT_ENTRY` (409) on `POST /lateral-hiring/questionnaires/{questionnaire_id}/client-book-entries`.
24. **Client-book data sufficiency** — `ERR_EMPTY_CLIENT_BOOK` or `ERR_UNVERIFIED_COLLECTION_DATA` (both 422). Applies to two endpoints: `POST /lateral-hiring/questionnaires/{questionnaire_id}/submit` and `POST /lateral-hiring/questionnaires/{questionnaire_id}/portability-assessments`.
25. **Round caps** — `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` or `ERR_NEGOTIATION_ROUND_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (409). Applies to two endpoints: `POST /lateral-hiring/questionnaires/{questionnaire_id}/request-revision` and `POST /lateral-hiring/offers/{offer_id}/counter`.
26. **Committee integrity** — `ERR_COMMITTEE_TOO_SMALL`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_VOTE`, or `ERR_VOTE_ROLL_FULL` (409). Applies to two endpoints: `POST /lateral-hiring/offers/{offer_id}/submit` (the committee-size floor) and `POST /lateral-hiring/offers/{offer_id}/votes` (the duplicate-voter and roll-full checks).
27. **Quorum** — `ERR_QUORUM_NOT_MET` (409) on `POST /lateral-hiring/offers/{offer_id}/finalize`.
28. **Onboarding capacity** — `ERR_ONBOARDING_CAPACITY_REACHED` (409) on `POST /lateral-hiring/integration-plans/{plan_id}/activate`.
29. **Guarantee budget** — `ERR_GUARANTEE_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` (402) on `POST /lateral-hiring/offers/{offer_id}/submit`.

### 2.11. Nested Path Resolution and Soft-Deleted Rows

Many URLs in this API nest a child under its parent. Resolution is strictly left to right: the outer segment is looked up first, and if it does not exist the request fails with that resource's own not-found code, even when the inner identifier is also unknown. So a request against a questionnaire's client book entries under an unknown `questionnaire_id` reports `ERR_QUESTIONNAIRE_NOT_FOUND`, never a client-book error. The path variables used across the API are `practice_group_id`, `search_firm_id`, `engagement_id`, `candidate_id`, `questionnaire_id`, `entry_id`, `record_id`, `assessment_id`, `conflict_check_id`, `waiver_id`, `declination_id`, `offer_id`, `vote_id`, `plan_id`, `introduction_id`, and `review_id`.

Only one entity in this system is soft-deleted rather than removed: a `ClientBookEntry` whose `status` has become `WITHDRAWN`. Such a row remains readable by its own identifier and remains referenceable by rows that already point at it, but it is excluded from every aggregation the system performs and it is hidden from list responses unless the caller passes `include_withdrawn` as true. All other entities reach terminal states rather than being deleted.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Practice Groups, Search Firms and the Exclusivity Calendar

Nothing else in this system can be created until a `SystemAdmin` has registered the two configuration entities that anchor it. A practice group is registered with a display `name` between 1 and 120 characters that must be unique across all practice groups, a `guarantee_budget_total_usd` of zero or more carried to two decimal places, an `onboarding_capacity` of at least 1, a `leverage_ratio` greater than zero carried to two decimal places, and a `target_standard_rate_usd` greater than zero carried to two decimal places. The `leverage_ratio` expresses associates per partner and later decides whether the group earns a productivity uplift in the expected-contribution model; `target_standard_rate_usd` is the group's own standard partner rate and becomes the numerator of the rate-alignment factor. On registration the group's `status` is `OPEN`, `available_guarantee_budget_usd` is set equal to `guarantee_budget_total_usd`, `reserved_guarantee_budget_usd` and `committed_guarantee_total_usd` are both `0.00`, and `active_onboarding_count` is `0`.

A group's lateral activity can be gated. Freezing it, which requires a `reason` in the payload, moves `status` from `OPEN` to `HIRING_FROZEN`; reopening it moves `status` back from `HIRING_FROZEN` to `OPEN` and takes no payload at all. A freeze does not disturb anything already in flight — offers already drafted against candidates targeting the group are left exactly as they are — but three later operations will be refused while the group is frozen: opening a new candidate, submitting an offer to committee, and activating an integration plan. All three carry `ERR_PRACTICE_GROUP_FROZEN`.

Search firms are registered with a `name` between 1 and 120 characters, unique across all search firms; a `max_fee_pct` greater than zero and no more than `0.40`, carried to four decimal places, which is the highest placement-fee rate this recruiter is permitted to charge on any engagement; and a `max_concurrent_engagements` of at least 1. A new firm's `engagement_count` begins at `0` and thereafter counts every engagement row naming that firm, in any status whatsoever.

**Retaining a recruiter.** A `RecruitingCoordinator` creates a `SearchFirmEngagement` — an engagement, throughout this document — by naming the `search_firm_id` of the recruiter and the `practice_group_id` whose lateral search will be exclusive to it, together with the commercial terms: a `fee_pct` greater than zero carried to four decimal places, a `fee_cap_usd` greater than zero carried to two decimal places that hard-ceilings whatever fee eventually crystallises, an `exclusivity_start_date`, an `exclusivity_end_date`, and a `tail_off_days` between 0 and 365 inclusive. The exclusivity window is **half-open**: the start instant is inside the window and the end instant is not. The `tail_off_days` value does double duty — it is the fee-protection tail that survives the end of exclusivity, and it is also the mandatory quiet gap that must elapse before the same practice group may be given to another recruiter. A new engagement starts with `status` `ACTIVE`, `fee_status` `NOT_DUE`, `fee_basis` `NONE`, and `fee_amount_usd` `0.00`, and it increments the named firm's `engagement_count` by 1 (which, being a mutation of the firm row, also advances that firm's `version` by 1).

Two independent geometry rules guard the create, and they use **different interval conventions on different resources** — a distinction that has caught more than one implementer.

On the **practice group** side, the requested half-open window must not overlap the half-open window of any existing engagement on the same `practice_group_id` whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, and in addition the requested `exclusivity_start_date` must fall at or after that existing engagement's `exclusivity_end_date` advanced by its own `tail_off_days` days. In other words the new search may not begin until the previous recruiter's quiet gap has fully elapsed, even if the raw windows themselves are disjoint. Violation of either half of this rule is `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_EXCLUSIVITY_CONFLICT`.

On the **search firm** side, the rule is a capacity count evaluated under **closed** interval semantics. Count the engagements naming the same `search_firm_id` whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose closed window, from `exclusivity_start_date` through `exclusivity_end_date` inclusive of both endpoints, intersects the requested closed window. That count must be strictly less than the firm's `max_concurrent_engagements`; if it has already reached that number, the create is refused with `ERR_SEARCH_FIRM_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Because the intervals are closed here, two windows that merely touch — one ending at the exact instant another begins — *do* intersect and *do* both count. Separately, the requested `fee_pct` must not exceed the firm's `max_fee_pct`, or the create is refused with `ERR_FEE_PCT_ABOVE_FIRM_MAX`; and `exclusivity_end_date` must be strictly later than `exclusivity_start_date`, or the create is refused with `ERR_LOOKBACK_WINDOW_INVALID`.

**Ending an engagement early.** A coordinator may terminate an `ACTIVE` engagement, supplying a `reason`; the engagement moves `ACTIVE → TERMINATED`, its `fee_status` is set to `FEE_WAIVED`, its `fee_basis` is reset to `NONE`, and its `fee_amount_usd` is reset to `0.00`. Termination deliberately cascades nowhere: candidates whose `sourcing_engagement_id` names this engagement and whose `pipeline_status` is `SOURCED` or `APPROACHED` are left untouched and remain sourceable. Terminating an engagement that is not `ACTIVE` is refused with `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_INVALID_STATE`.

**Aging out.** Engagements also end by themselves. When the logical clock is moved, every engagement whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `exclusivity_end_date` advanced by its `tail_off_days` days falls **at or before** the new clock instant transitions `ACTIVE → EXPIRED`. Note that this boundary is inclusive: landing exactly on the end of the tail expires the engagement. Expiry leaves `fee_status` untouched, so a fee that had already crystallised as `FEE_DUE` survives the engagement's own expiry. Where several engagements qualify in one clock move, they are processed in `created_at` ascending order.

### 3.2. Confidential Sourcing and the Candidate Record

A candidate record is the pipeline's spine, and unusually it tracks **two independent status dimensions on the same row**. The recruiting dimension, `pipeline_status`, runs `SOURCED`, `APPROACHED`, `DILIGENCE`, `OFFER_OUT`, and terminates in `HIRED`, `DECLINED`, or `WITHDRAWN`. The conflicts dimension, `clearance_status`, runs `NOT_STARTED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `CLEARED`, and `BLOCKED` and has no terminal value at all. Neither dimension's transitions read the other's current value except where this document says so explicitly, and the clearance dimension is entirely derived — it is recomputed from the candidate's conflicts checks and is never set directly by a caller.

A `RecruitingCoordinator` opens the record with the candidate's `full_name` and `prior_firm_name` (each 1 to 120 characters), the `target_practice_group_id` of the group that would receive the lateral, the `sourcing_engagement_id` of the engagement that surfaced them, a `sponsor_user_id` of 1 to 64 characters naming the internal partner championing the hire, a `current_standard_rate_usd` greater than zero carried to two decimal places (the candidate's standard hourly rate at the prior firm, which becomes the denominator of the rate-alignment factor), a `notice_period_days` between 0 and 365 inclusive, a `non_solicit_days` between 0 and 730 inclusive, and optionally `approach_confidential`, which defaults to true when omitted. The target practice group must exist and its `status` must be `OPEN`, and the sourcing engagement must exist and its `status` must be `ACTIVE`. The new row lands with `pipeline_status` `SOURCED` and `clearance_status` `NOT_STARTED`, and with `resignation_date`, `earliest_start_date`, `hire_date`, and `open_offer_id` all null.

Recording the confidential approach advances `SOURCED → APPROACHED`. Two conditions guard it beyond the state itself: the sourcing engagement's `status` must still be `ACTIVE`, and the current logical clock must fall inside that engagement's **half-open** exclusivity window — at or after `exclusivity_start_date` and strictly before `exclusivity_end_date`. Approaching a candidate outside that window, or through an engagement that has expired or been terminated, is refused. The `approach_confidential` flag is retained exactly as it was recorded at creation; the approach step never rewrites it. Much later, that flag will decide whether the incoming partner may be introduced to a prior-firm client that has not yet confirmed it would move.

Beyond the approach, the recruiting dimension advances only as a consequence of other people's work. Issuing the questionnaire moves the candidate `APPROACHED → DILIGENCE`. A committee finalization that resolves in the offer's favour moves the candidate `DILIGENCE → OFFER_OUT` and records the approved offer's identifier in `open_offer_id`. A finalization that resolves against the offer moves the candidate to `DECLINED` — from `DILIGENCE` or from `OFFER_OUT`, whichever the candidate currently holds — and clears `open_offer_id` back to null. Acceptance of an approved offer moves the candidate `OFFER_OUT → HIRED`, and that hop is what sets `resignation_date` and `hire_date` to the current logical clock instant (they are always equal) and computes `earliest_start_date` as the `resignation_date` advanced by `notice_period_days` days. While `resignation_date` is null, `earliest_start_date` is null too.

**Withdrawal is the deepest cascade in the system.** A coordinator may withdraw a candidate from any non-terminal position — that is, whenever `pipeline_status` is not already `HIRED`, `DECLINED`, or `WITHDRAWN` — supplying a `withdrawal_reason`. The candidate's `pipeline_status` becomes `WITHDRAWN`, `open_offer_id` is cleared to null, and then four hops run inside the same request:

- **Hop 2** — every `LateralQuestionnaire` of that candidate whose `status` is not already `ACCEPTED` or `ABANDONED` becomes `ABANDONED`, whichever of `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, or `REVISION_REQUESTED` it held. In the same hop, the candidate's `CompensationOffer` becomes `LAPSED` if its `status` is any of `DRAFTED`, `COMMITTEE_REVIEW`, `APPROVED`, `COUNTER_REQUESTED`, or `REPRICE_REQUIRED`; an offer already `ACCEPTED`, `REJECTED`, or `LAPSED` is left alone.
- **Hop 3**, caused by that offer lapsing — the guarantee reservation is released: the target practice group's `reserved_guarantee_budget_usd` is reduced by the lapsed offer's `guaranteed_comp_usd` and its `available_guarantee_budget_usd` is increased by the same amount. This release happens only when the offer was in `COMMITTEE_REVIEW` or `APPROVED`, the two statuses that hold a live reservation; a `DRAFTED`, `COUNTER_REQUESTED`, or `REPRICE_REQUIRED` offer holds no reservation and releases nothing. `committed_guarantee_total_usd` is never touched by a withdrawal, because a withdrawn candidate's offer was by definition never accepted. Also in this hop, an `IntegrationPlan` naming that offer whose `status` is `DRAFT` or `ACTIVE` becomes `ABANDONED`.
- **Hop 4**, caused by that plan being abandoned — every `CrossSellIntroduction` on the plan whose `status` is `PROPOSED` becomes `CANCELLED`, and if the plan had been `ACTIVE` the practice group's `active_onboarding_count` is decremented by 1. A plan that was still `DRAFT` never consumed a slot, so nothing is returned for it.

### 3.3. The Lateral Partner Questionnaire and the Disclosed Client Book

The lateral partner questionnaire — the LPQ — is the candidate's own account of their practice, and it is deliberately allowed to be wrong; the firm's job is to measure the gap. A `RecruitingCoordinator` issues it against a candidate whose `pipeline_status` is `APPROACHED`, and only when that candidate has no questionnaire outstanding — that is, no existing questionnaire whose `status` is anything other than `ACCEPTED` or `ABANDONED`. The payload carries `declared_prior_year_billings_usd` of zero or more, `declared_prior_year_collections_usd` of zero or more that must not exceed the declared billings, a `declared_realization_rate` greater than zero and no more than `1.0000` carried to four decimal places, and `primary_matter_types`, an array of 1 to 10 matter-type labels each 1 to 60 characters. Issuing the LPQ writes it with `status` `DRAFT`, `revision_round` `0`, `entry_count` `0`, and `latest_assessment_id` null, and — as a side effect on the parent — advances the candidate `APPROACHED → DILIGENCE`.

The LPQ's lifecycle contains a **bounded revision loop**: `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW → REVISION_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED`, closing back on itself. Submission is the candidate's own act: the acting `Candidate` must be the questionnaire's own candidate, the questionnaire's `status` must be `DRAFT` or `REVISION_REQUESTED`, `entry_count` must be at least 1, and the candidate's sourcing engagement must still be `ACTIVE`. Submitting with an empty book is refused with `ERR_EMPTY_CLIENT_BOOK`; submitting through a dead engagement is refused with `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_INVALID_STATE`. On success the status becomes `SUBMITTED` and `entry_count` is refreshed from the count of the questionnaire's `ACTIVE` client book entries. Both the `DRAFT → SUBMITTED` edge and the loop-closing `REVISION_REQUESTED → SUBMITTED` edge carry exactly the same preconditions and the same refresh.

A coordinator then begins review, moving `SUBMITTED → UNDER_REVIEW`; that step takes no payload and has no effect beyond the status write. From `UNDER_REVIEW` the coordinator may send the LPQ back, supplying a `revision_note`, which moves it `UNDER_REVIEW → REVISION_REQUESTED`, increments `revision_round` by 1, and makes the client book mutable again — while the LPQ sits in `REVISION_REQUESTED`, entries may be created and soft-deleted. The loop is capped: revisions may be requested only while `revision_round` is strictly less than 3, so the third revision is the last one permitted and a fourth attempt is refused with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. Alternatively the coordinator accepts the LPQ as the diligence of record, moving `UNDER_REVIEW → ACCEPTED`, which requires that at least one portability assessment on this questionnaire is in `CURRENT` status. Acceptance freezes the client book: once the LPQ is `ACCEPTED`, creating an entry and soft-deleting an entry are both refused with `ERR_QUESTIONNAIRE_INVALID_STATE`.

**Disclosing a client relationship.** Each line of the client book is one `ClientBookEntry`, created by the `Candidate` against their own questionnaire. The payload carries a `client_name` of 1 to 120 characters; a `relationship_role` of `BILLING_ATTORNEY`, `RELATIONSHIP_PARTNER`, or `SERVICE_PARTNER`; optionally a `client_consent_status` of `CONSENT_PENDING` or `CONSENT_CONFIRMED`, defaulting to `CONSENT_PENDING` when omitted; a `claimed_annual_billings_usd` greater than zero carried to two decimal places; a `claimed_portability_pct` greater than zero and no more than `1.0000` carried to four decimal places; a `claimed_realization_rate` greater than zero and no more than `1.0000` carried to four decimal places; and `matter_types`, an array of 1 to 6 distinct strings each 1 to 60 characters. **The order of `matter_types` is significant** and must be preserved as supplied: if this relationship is ever declined, the rounding remainder of the revenue split lands on the first element of that array.

Within one questionnaire, `client_name` must be unique among entries whose `status` is `ACTIVE`; a collision is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CLIENT_ENTRY`. A withdrawn entry's name is therefore reusable. A new entry lands with `status` `ACTIVE`, `portable_status` `UNVALIDATED`, `baseline_portable_amount_usd` and `validated_portable_amount_usd` both `0.00`, and `verified_record_count` `0`; it increments the questionnaire's `entry_count` by 1, and it turns any assessment on that questionnaire whose `status` is `CURRENT` to `STALE`, because the book it valued has just grown.

Soft-deleting an entry sets its `status` to `WITHDRAWN`, decrements the questionnaire's `entry_count` by 1, and likewise turns any `CURRENT` assessment on that questionnaire `STALE`. The row itself survives — its `id` remains resolvable and rows already pointing at it remain valid — but it is excluded from every aggregation from that moment on. Only the owning candidate may soft-delete, and only while the questionnaire's `status` permits mutation — the statuses that permit book mutation are exactly `DRAFT` and `REVISION_REQUESTED`. Creating an entry and soft-deleting an entry are alike refused with `ERR_QUESTIONNAIRE_INVALID_STATE` while the questionnaire is `SUBMITTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `ACCEPTED`, or `ABANDONED`; a book that has gone out for review is frozen until a revision is requested to reopen it.

The entry's third computed field, `verified_record_count`, counts the collection records attached to this entry whose `record_type` is `HISTORICAL` and whose `status` is `VERIFIED`; it is the evidence counter that the validation step in §3.4 tests against a floor of three.

### 3.4. Collection Evidence and Portable-Business Validation

Claimed billings are not evidence. What substantiates a book of business is cash actually collected, and the firm records that as an append-only stream of observations against the candidate. Either a `SystemAdmin` or a `RecruitingCoordinator` may append one, naming the candidate and supplying a `record_type` of `HISTORICAL` or `POST_HIRE`, a `period_end_date` marking the end instant of the period the figure covers, an `amount_usd` of zero or more carried to two decimal places, a `source` of `PRIOR_FIRM_STATEMENT`, `CANDIDATE_ATTESTATION`, or `FIRM_LEDGER`, optionally a `status` of `UNVERIFIED` or `VERIFIED` that defaults to `UNVERIFIED`, and optionally a `client_book_entry_id` tying the figure to one disclosed relationship. That last field is left null for post-hire firm-ledger rows that do not correspond to any declared client line. `HISTORICAL` rows feed portable-business validation; `POST_HIRE` rows feed the performance tracking of §3.8. Provenance is decisive: **only rows whose `status` is `VERIFIED` ever enter any aggregation**, and because the row is immutable once written there is no way to verify it later — a record's `status` is fixed at the moment it is appended.

Appending a `HISTORICAL` row whose `status` is `VERIFIED` turns any assessment in `CURRENT` status on that candidate's questionnaire to `STALE`, since the evidence base has changed underneath it. Appending an `UNVERIFIED` row, or a `POST_HIRE` row, leaves assessments alone.

**Producing an assessment.** A `RecruitingCoordinator` values the client book by creating a portability assessment on the questionnaire. The payload supplies a `baseline_date`, optionally a `governing_basis` of `SELF_REPORTED` or `VALIDATED` that defaults to `VALIDATED`, and the pinned `expected_questionnaire_version` described in §2.6. The `baseline_date` is the **exclusive** upper bound of the evidence window, and it may not lie in the future — a `baseline_date` later than the current logical clock is refused with `ERR_LOOKBACK_WINDOW_INVALID`. From it the system derives `lookback_start_date` as the `baseline_date` moved back by three calendar years, equivalently thirty-six months, and that lower bound is **inclusive**. The evidence window is therefore half-open: a collection record counts when its `period_end_date` is at or after `lookback_start_date` and strictly before `baseline_date`.

The questionnaire's `status` must be `UNDER_REVIEW` or `ACCEPTED`. Its `entry_count` must be at least 1, or the request is refused with `ERR_EMPTY_CLIENT_BOOK`. And **every** client book entry on the questionnaire whose `status` is `ACTIVE` must have at least three verified historical collection records inside that half-open window; an entry short of three is refused with `ERR_UNVERIFIED_COLLECTION_DATA`. Three is a floor, not a target: exactly three qualifies. The assessment records how many entries it covered in `entry_count_in_scope`, which is the count of `ACTIVE` entries on the questionnaire at the moment the assessment ran. Entries whose `status` is `WITHDRAWN` are excluded from the scope entirely and are not tested for evidence.

**Valuing one relationship.** For each in-scope entry the system computes a validated portable amount, and this is where the diligence actually bites. Begin with the *historical base*: the sum of `amount_usd` across that entry's collection records whose `record_type` is `HISTORICAL`, whose `status` is `VERIFIED`, and whose `period_end_date` lies in the half-open evidence window. Divide that base by three — the number of lookback years — and round the quotient half up to two decimal places to obtain the entry's average annual collections. Next select two factors. The **role factor** is `1.00` when `relationship_role` is `BILLING_ATTORNEY`, `0.85` when it is `RELATIONSHIP_PARTNER`, and `0.55` when it is `SERVICE_PARTNER` — a partner who merely services a relationship carries barely half of it out the door. The **consent factor** is `1.00` when `client_consent_status` is `CONSENT_CONFIRMED` and `0.60` when it is `CONSENT_PENDING`. The **realization** input is the lesser of the entry's `claimed_realization_rate` and a firm-wide realization ceiling of `0.95`, so a candidate claiming full realization is trimmed to that ceiling.

Multiply the average annual collections by the role factor, by the consent factor, and by that realization figure, all at full precision. Then apply a hard portability ceiling: the result is replaced by the smaller of itself and 90 percent of the entry's average annual collections. That final value, and only that value, is rounded — half up, to two decimal places, exactly once — and written to `baseline_portable_amount_usd`. The step order is pinned: the factors are multiplied at full precision and the single rounding happens here, before any summation upstream. `validated_portable_amount_usd` is then set to `0.00` if the entry's `portable_status` is `DECLINED`, and to the same value as `baseline_portable_amount_usd` otherwise. Computing an entry this way advances its `portable_status` `UNVALIDATED → VALIDATED`, which requires that the entry's `status` is `ACTIVE` and that its verified historical record count within the window is at least three.

**Valuing the whole book, twice.** The assessment then aggregates over its scope — the `ACTIVE` entries of the questionnaire, processed in `created_at` ascending order — and produces four money totals. `gross_baseline_total_usd` is the plain sum of every in-scope entry's `baseline_portable_amount_usd`. `validated_portable_total_usd` is the plain sum of every in-scope entry's `validated_portable_amount_usd`. `declined_portable_total_usd` is the sum of `baseline_portable_amount_usd` restricted to in-scope entries whose `portable_status` is `DECLINED`. And `self_reported_total_usd` is the sum, over the same entries, of each entry's `claimed_annual_billings_usd` multiplied by its `claimed_portability_pct` and rounded half up to two decimal places **per line** before being added in. In every one of those four totals the summands are already rounded to two decimal places and **the total itself is not rounded again**; summing at full precision and rounding once at the end produces a different number of cents and is not what this specification asks for.

`variance_pct` measures how far the candidate's own story sits from the evidence: it is `self_reported_total_usd` less `validated_portable_total_usd`, divided by `self_reported_total_usd`, rounded half up to four decimal places. When `self_reported_total_usd` is zero the quotient is undefined and `variance_pct` is `0.0000`.

The two totals are the system's **dual valuation basis**, and `governing_basis` nominates which one drives money downstream. Under `SELF_REPORTED` the governing total is `self_reported_total_usd`: it is built purely from `claimed_annual_billings_usd` and `claimed_portability_pct`, and it therefore ignores the role factor, the consent factor, the realization ceiling, the portability ceiling, and every collection record in the system. That basis is for the case where no verified prior-firm statements exist and the committee is knowingly pricing off the candidate's own numbers — same client book, materially larger number. Under `VALIDATED`, the default, the governing total is `validated_portable_total_usd`, built from verified historical collections through the role, consent and realization factors and the 90-percent ceiling, and zeroed for any declined entry — same client book, materially smaller number.

**Grading concentration.** A book worth ten million dollars concentrated in one client is worth less than the same ten million spread across twenty, so the assessment discounts for concentration. Rank the in-scope entries by `baseline_portable_amount_usd` descending, breaking ties by `created_at` ascending — never by `id`. `top_client_share` is the highest-ranked entry's `baseline_portable_amount_usd` divided by `gross_baseline_total_usd`, rounded half up to four decimal places; `top3_client_share` is the sum of the three highest-ranked entries' baseline amounts (or all of them, if fewer than three exist) divided by the same denominator and rounded the same way. When `gross_baseline_total_usd` is zero both shares are `0.0000`. `top3_client_share` is **reported only** — it is published for the committee's benefit and is not an input to anything.

`concentration_grade` is decided by `top_client_share` alone, on **inclusive** boundaries: `HIGH` when the share is at least `0.30`, otherwise `MEDIUM` when it is at least `0.20`, otherwise `LOW`. A share of exactly `0.30` is `HIGH`, and a share of exactly `0.20` is `MEDIUM`. From the grade follows `concentration_haircut_pct`: `0.15` for `HIGH`, `0.07` for `MEDIUM`, and `0.00` for `LOW`. Note that concentration is computed from baseline amounts rather than validated ones, deliberately — a declination must not be allowed to flatter the concentration profile by removing the largest client from the numerator.

Finally, `adjusted_portable_total_usd` is the governing total multiplied by one minus `concentration_haircut_pct`, rounded half up to two decimal places. This single figure is what the compensation model in §3.6 prices against.

**Supersession and staleness.** Creating an assessment writes it with `status` `CURRENT`, turns every earlier assessment on the same questionnaire whose `status` is `CURRENT` or `STALE` to `SUPERSEDED`, writes the new assessment's identifier into the questionnaire's `latest_assessment_id`, and increments the questionnaire's `version` by 1. `SUPERSEDED` is terminal; a superseded assessment never moves again.

A `CURRENT` assessment goes `CURRENT → STALE` whenever an in-scope entry's `validated_portable_amount_usd` changes underneath it — the fourth hop of the declination cascade in §3.5, and also the consequence of adding an entry, soft-deleting an entry, or appending a verified historical record. On that transition the assessment recomputes `validated_portable_total_usd`, `declined_portable_total_usd`, and `adjusted_portable_total_usd`, while `gross_baseline_total_usd` stays exactly as it was — that is the conserved quantity of §2.8 doing its work. And the transition carries a fifth hop of its own: every compensation offer that priced against this assessment and whose `status` is `COMMITTEE_REVIEW` is driven to `REPRICE_REQUIRED`. A `STALE` assessment can still be superseded by a newer one, `STALE → SUPERSEDED`; both the `CURRENT → SUPERSEDED` and `STALE → SUPERSEDED` edges write nothing beyond the status.

### 3.5. Conflicts Clearance, Waiver Strategy and Matter Declination

A book of business the firm cannot ethically accept is worth nothing, so conflicts clearance runs across the candidate's disclosed relationships in parallel with the valuation work. A `ConflictsCounsel` opens a `ConflictCheck` — a check, below — against one client book entry, supplying an `adverse_party_name` of 1 to 120 characters naming the existing firm client or matter that is adverse, an `affected_matter_types` array of 1 to 6 strings identifying which of the relationship's matters the conflict touches, and a `severity` of `DIRECT_ADVERSE`, `POSITIONAL`, or `IMPUTED`. The entry's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, its questionnaire's `status` must be `UNDER_REVIEW`, `REVISION_REQUESTED`, or `ACCEPTED`, and **every element of `affected_matter_types` must appear in that entry's own `matter_types`** — a matter type absent from the entry is refused with `ERR_MATTER_TYPE_NOT_ON_ENTRY`. The new check lands with `status` `PENDING` and `resolution_note` null.

The clearance dimension of the candidate row is driven entirely from these checks and is recomputed on every check status change. The candidate's very first check moves `clearance_status` `NOT_STARTED → IN_PROGRESS`. The candidate becomes `IN_PROGRESS → CLEARED` at the moment a check status change leaves zero non-cleared checks — that is, when every check on every `ACTIVE` entry of the candidate's questionnaire has `status` `CLEAR`, and the questionnaire's `entry_count` is at least 1. Any check reaching `BLOCKED` drives the candidate to `BLOCKED`, from `IN_PROGRESS` or from `CLEARED` alike, and that is true whenever at least one check on the candidate's active entries is `BLOCKED`. Conversely, opening a fresh check after clearance was already complete moves the candidate `CLEARED → IN_PROGRESS`, which applies when at least one check on those entries is `PENDING` or `WAIVER_PENDING` and none is `BLOCKED`. That reopening does **not** retract anything already granted downstream: an offer that reached `COMMITTEE_REVIEW` while the candidate was `CLEARED` stays in `COMMITTEE_REVIEW`, because the clearance test is evaluated only when an offer is submitted to committee and when an offer is accepted. Finally, a candidate can travel back the other way — `BLOCKED → CLEARED` — when a rescission returns the last blocked check to `CLEAR` and every check on the candidate's active entries is then `CLEAR`. Those two edges together make the clearance dimension cyclic.

**Clearing outright.** A counsel may clear a `PENDING` check directly, recording a `resolution_note` of up to 500 characters; the check moves `PENDING → CLEAR` and the candidate's `clearance_status` is recomputed, possibly becoming `CLEARED`. But a check whose `severity` is `DIRECT_ADVERSE` may **not** be cleared this way — a directly adverse conflict cannot be waved through without a client waiver — and the attempt is refused with `ERR_DIRECT_ADVERSE_REQUIRES_WAIVER`. Clearing a check that is not `PENDING` is refused with `ERR_CONFLICT_CHECK_INVALID_STATE`.

**Pursuing a waiver.** The alternative is to ask the client to sign. A counsel opens a `ConflictWaiver` — a waiver, below — on a `PENDING` check, supplying a `waiver_strategy` of `ADVANCE_CONSENT`, `ETHICAL_WALL`, or `MATTER_CARVE_OUT`, a `requested_from_client` name of 1 to 120 characters, and a `response_deadline_date` that must be strictly later than the current logical clock — a deadline already in the past is refused with `ERR_LOOKBACK_WINDOW_INVALID`. One strategy is barred by severity: an `ETHICAL_WALL` may not be used to cure a `DIRECT_ADVERSE` check, because a screen cannot fix direct adversity, and that combination is refused with `ERR_DIRECT_ADVERSE_REQUIRES_WAIVER`. The waiver lands with `status` `REQUESTED` and `decided_at` null, and the parent check moves `PENDING → WAIVER_PENDING`.

A waiver resolves three ways. **Granted**: the counsel records the client's consent with a `resolution_note`, provided the waiver's `status` is still `REQUESTED` and the current logical clock is at or before the `response_deadline_date`; `decided_at` is set to the current clock instant, the waiver becomes `REQUESTED → GRANTED`, the parent check moves `WAIVER_PENDING → CLEAR`, and the candidate's `clearance_status` is recomputed and may become `CLEARED`. **Refused**: the counsel records the client's refusal with a `resolution_note`, `decided_at` is set to the current clock instant, and the waiver becomes `REQUESTED → REFUSED` — which starts a four-hop cascade. **Expired**: on a clock move, any waiver still in `REQUESTED` whose `response_deadline_date` is strictly earlier than the new clock instant becomes `REQUESTED → EXPIRED` with `decided_at` set to the new clock, and it runs the *identical* downstream cascade as a refusal. Where several waivers qualify in one clock move they are processed in `response_deadline_date` ascending order, breaking ties by `created_at` ascending. Granting or refusing a waiver that is not `REQUESTED` is refused with `ERR_WAIVER_INVALID_STATE`.

**The declination cascade.** Refusal and expiry both run the same chain, and the equal treatment is deliberate — only the trigger differs:

- **Hop 2** — the parent check moves `WAIVER_PENDING → BLOCKED`.
- **Hop 3**, caused by the check blocking — the client book entry moves `portable_status` `VALIDATED → DECLINED`; its `validated_portable_amount_usd` is set to `0.00` while its `baseline_portable_amount_usd` is left **unchanged**, which is precisely how the portable-book identity survives; one `MatterDeclination` row is created per element of the entry's `matter_types` array; and the candidate's `clearance_status` becomes `BLOCKED`.
- **Hop 4**, caused by the entry being declined — every assessment on that entry's questionnaire whose `status` is `CURRENT` moves to `STALE`, recomputing `validated_portable_total_usd`, `declined_portable_total_usd`, and `adjusted_portable_total_usd`.
- **Hop 5**, caused by the assessment going stale — every compensation offer priced against that assessment whose `status` is `COMMITTEE_REVIEW` moves to `REPRICE_REQUIRED`, voiding its live votes and releasing its guarantee reservation as described in §3.6.

**Splitting the foregone revenue.** Each declination row records the `conflict_check_id` of the blocked check that forced it, the `client_book_entry_id` of the relationship whose matter is declined, a `matter_type` copied verbatim from one element of the entry's `matter_types`, a `foregone_annual_revenue_usd` share, `status` `ACTIVE`, and `rescinded_at` null. The share is a waterfall allocation over the entry's `baseline_portable_amount_usd`. Let the entry have some number of matter types. Divide the baseline amount by that count and **round the quotient down** to whole cents — this is the per-matter share. Multiply that per-matter share back by the count and subtract from the baseline amount to get the leftover remainder. Each declination row then receives the per-matter share, and the row corresponding to the **first element of the `matter_types` array** additionally receives the entire remainder. Allocation follows the array's index order, never `created_at` and never `id`. The invariant this guarantees is exact: the `foregone_annual_revenue_usd` figures across the rows created for one entry sum precisely to that entry's `baseline_portable_amount_usd`, to the cent.

**Rescinding.** Clients change their minds, and the system provides a true compensating action. A `ConflictsCounsel` rescinds an `ACTIVE` declination, supplying a `rescission_note`; the row moves `ACTIVE → RESCINDED` and `rescinded_at` is set to the current logical clock. Two preconditions apply: the declination's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, and the parent check's `status` must be `BLOCKED`; rescinding an already-rescinded row is refused with `ERR_DECLINATION_INVALID_STATE`, and a parent check in the wrong state is refused with `ERR_CONFLICT_CHECK_INVALID_STATE`. When — and only when — this was the **last** `ACTIVE` declination on the entry, so that zero remain active afterwards, the reversal chain runs: the entry moves `portable_status` `DECLINED → VALIDATED` with `validated_portable_amount_usd` restored exactly to `baseline_portable_amount_usd` with no drift; the parent check moves `BLOCKED → CLEAR`; every assessment on that questionnaire whose `status` is `CURRENT` or `STALE` recomputes `validated_portable_total_usd`, `declined_portable_total_usd`, and `adjusted_portable_total_usd`; and the candidate's `clearance_status` is recomputed, which may return it to `CLEARED`. Rescinding one of several active declinations on the same entry moves only that row; the entry stays `DECLINED`. The portable-book identity holds both before and after the rescission.

### 3.6. Offer Modelling, Committee Negotiation and the Hire

**Drafting the model.** A `CommitteeChair` prices a candidate by creating a compensation offer. The payload names the `portability_assessment_id` whose valuation the offer prices against, a `guaranteed_comp_usd` greater than zero carried to two decimal places, a `signing_bonus_usd` of zero or more carried to two decimal places, and the pinned `expected_candidate_version` of §2.6. The candidate's `pipeline_status` must be `DILIGENCE`. The referenced assessment's questionnaire must belong to that same candidate, or the request is refused with `ERR_ASSESSMENT_CANDIDATE_MISMATCH`. And the assessment's `status` must be `CURRENT`: an assessment that has gone `STALE` is refused with `ERR_ASSESSMENT_STALE`, and one that has been `SUPERSEDED` is refused with `ERR_ASSESSMENT_INVALID_STATE`. The distinction matters — staleness means the book moved, supersession means a newer assessment exists.

The new offer records the acting chair's `X-User-ID` in `created_by_user_id`, lands with `status` `DRAFTED` and `round_number` `1`, and starts with `eligible_voter_count` null and all three vote counters — `votes_cast_total`, `active_vote_count`, `voided_vote_count` — at `0`. It copies the assessment's `adjusted_portable_total_usd` into its own `adjusted_portable_total_snapshot_usd`, and that snapshot is **pinned for the life of the offer**: it is never refreshed, not even when the assessment it came from goes `STALE`. Creation also writes the offer's identifier into the candidate's `open_offer_id` and increments the candidate's `version` by 1 — which is exactly why the next writer's `expected_candidate_version` must be refreshed.

**Expected contribution.** From the pinned snapshot the system derives `expected_contribution_usd`, the revenue the firm believes this partner will actually generate in year one. Start with a **ramp factor**, which penalises a long notice period because a partner who cannot start for months contributes less in the first year: the ramp factor is `0.70` when the candidate's `notice_period_days` is **strictly greater than** 60 days, and `0.85` otherwise — a notice period of exactly 60 days takes the more favourable `0.85`. Then add a **practice uplift** of `0.05` when the receiving group's `leverage_ratio` is **at least** `3.00`, and `0.00` when it is below that; a ratio of exactly three associates per partner earns the uplift. The sum of the ramp factor and the uplift is then capped at `0.92` — the effective multiplier is whichever is smaller, the sum or that cap.

Separately compute a **rate-alignment factor**: divide the receiving group's `target_standard_rate_usd` by the candidate's `current_standard_rate_usd`, then clamp the quotient into the band from `0.80` to `1.10` — that is, raise it to `0.80` if it falls below and lower it to `1.10` if it rises above — and round the clamped value half up to four decimal places before it is used anywhere. A candidate whose prior rate was far below the group's own rate is credited for the repricing headroom, but only up to ten percent.

`expected_contribution_usd` is then the pinned snapshot multiplied by the effective multiplier and by the rounded rate-alignment factor, evaluated left to right and rounded half up to two decimal places exactly once at the end.

**Net first-year economics, in five pinned steps.** The remaining economics fields look circular — the true-up is keyed on the net, the net depends on the integration cost, the integration cost depends on the search fee, and the search fee depends on the total compensation, which a true-up would otherwise feed back into. The dependency is cut by excluding the true-up from step 1, and the evaluation order below is mandatory.

1. `total_first_year_comp_usd` is `guaranteed_comp_usd` plus `signing_bonus_usd`. It **excludes** `guarantee_true_up_amount_usd` entirely; this exclusion is the cut that makes the graph acyclic.
2. `search_fee_amount_usd` is the placement fee this offer would crystallise, computed by the search-fee rule below with the current logical clock standing in for the hire instant.
3. `integration_cost_total_usd` is the sum of four terms: `search_fee_amount_usd`, a fixed office build-out charge of `50000.00`, a fixed paralegal support charge of `35000.00`, and an onboarding overhead of four percent — a factor of `0.04` — of `total_first_year_comp_usd` rounded half up to two decimal places before being added.
4. `net_first_year_economics_usd` is `expected_contribution_usd` less `total_first_year_comp_usd` less `integration_cost_total_usd`, rounded half up to two decimal places.
5. `guarantee_true_up_amount_usd` is keyed on that net, on **inclusive** boundaries: ten percent of `guaranteed_comp_usd` — a factor of `0.10` — when the net is at least `500000.00`, otherwise five percent of `guaranteed_comp_usd` — a factor of `0.05` — when the net is at least `0.00`, otherwise `0.00`. Each percentage is rounded half up to two decimal places. Nothing in steps 1 through 4 reads this field.

**The placement fee, two ways.** The search fee is owed on exactly one engagement — the one named by the candidate's `sourcing_engagement_id` — regardless of that engagement's current `status`. The rule tests a *hire instant* against the engagement's calendar. When the offer's economics are being modelled the hire instant is taken to be the current logical clock, a speculative probe; when the fee actually crystallises on acceptance the hire instant is the candidate's `hire_date`.

If the hire instant falls inside the **half-open** exclusivity window — at or after `exclusivity_start_date` and strictly before `exclusivity_end_date` — the `fee_basis` is `DIRECT_PLACEMENT`. The fee base is `total_first_year_comp_usd`, that is guaranteed compensation *and* signing bonus together, multiplied by the engagement's `fee_pct` and rounded half up to two decimal places. If instead the hire instant falls in the **tail-off period** — at or after `exclusivity_end_date` and strictly before `exclusivity_end_date` advanced by `tail_off_days` days — the `fee_basis` is `TAIL_OFF_CLAIM`. Here the fee base is `guaranteed_comp_usd` alone, with the signing bonus **excluded**, multiplied by `fee_pct` and then halved by a tail-off discount of `0.50`, and rounded half up to two decimal places. Outside both intervals the `fee_basis` is `NONE` and the raw fee is `0.00`. In every case the final `fee_amount_usd` is the smaller of the rounded raw fee and the engagement's `fee_cap_usd` — the cap is applied to the already-rounded figure, not before rounding. Because a terminated engagement had its `fee_status` set to `FEE_WAIVED` and its terms zeroed, a later hire through it yields basis `NONE` and a fee of `0.00`.

The two variants differ substantially on identical offer inputs. Take a guaranteed compensation of `850000.00`, a signing bonus of `100000.00`, and a `fee_pct` of `0.2500`. Under `DIRECT_PLACEMENT` the fee is `237500.00`. Under `TAIL_OFF_CLAIM` — same offer, same recruiter, a hire landing a few days later — it is `106250.00`.

**Re-drafting after a counter.** An offer that came back `COUNTER_REQUESTED` can be re-priced: the chair supplies a fresh `guaranteed_comp_usd` and `signing_bonus_usd`, which replace the stored values, and the offer moves `COUNTER_REQUESTED → DRAFTED`. All seven computed economics fields — `expected_contribution_usd`, `total_first_year_comp_usd`, `search_fee_amount_usd`, `integration_cost_total_usd`, `net_first_year_economics_usd`, `guarantee_true_up_amount_usd`, and the derived fee figures — are recomputed from the new inputs against the **same** pinned `adjusted_portable_total_snapshot_usd`. Attempting to re-draft an offer in any other status is refused with `ERR_OFFER_INVALID_STATE`.

**Submitting to committee.** Submission moves `DRAFTED → COMMITTEE_REVIEW` and takes a single input, `eligible_voter_count`, which snapshots the size of the committee roll for this round and becomes the quorum denominator. Five conditions guard it beyond the status: the candidate's `clearance_status` must be `CLEARED`, or `ERR_CLEARANCE_INCOMPLETE`; the referenced assessment's `status` must still be `CURRENT`, or `ERR_ASSESSMENT_STALE` / `ERR_ASSESSMENT_INVALID_STATE` as above; the submitted `eligible_voter_count` must be at least 3, or `ERR_COMMITTEE_TOO_SMALL`; the receiving practice group's `status` must be `OPEN`, or `ERR_PRACTICE_GROUP_FROZEN`; and the offer's `guaranteed_comp_usd` must not exceed the group's `available_guarantee_budget_usd`, or `ERR_GUARANTEE_BUDGET_EXCEEDED` with HTTP 402. That budget boundary is inclusive — a guarantee exactly equal to the available budget is permitted, and exceeding it by a single cent is not. On success the group's `reserved_guarantee_budget_usd` increases by `guaranteed_comp_usd` and its `available_guarantee_budget_usd` decreases by the same amount, so the budget identity of §2.8 is preserved.

**Voting.** A `CommitteeMember` or a `CommitteeChair` casts a vote on an offer in `COMMITTEE_REVIEW`, supplying a `decision` of `APPROVE`, `REJECT`, or `ABSTAIN`, the `round_number` (at least 1) the voter believes is open, and optionally a `rationale` of up to 500 characters. The submitted `round_number` must equal the offer's current `round_number`, or the vote is refused with `ERR_VOTE_ROUND_MISMATCH` — this is what stops a voter from unknowingly voting on a superseded round. The acting user must not be the candidate's `sponsor_user_id` (`ERR_SPONSOR_CANNOT_VOTE`), must not already hold an `ACTIVE` vote on this offer (`ERR_DUPLICATE_VOTE`), and the offer's `active_vote_count` must be strictly below its `eligible_voter_count` (`ERR_VOTE_ROLL_FULL`). Casting a vote records `voter_user_id` from `X-User-ID`, lands the vote with `status` `ACTIVE`, increments both `votes_cast_total` and `active_vote_count` by 1, and increments the offer's `version` by 1. Voting on an offer that is not in `COMMITTEE_REVIEW` is refused with `ERR_OFFER_INVALID_STATE`.

The three vote counters are always reconcilable against the vote rows themselves: `active_vote_count` is the number of votes on the offer whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, `voided_vote_count` the number whose `status` is `VOIDED`, and `votes_cast_total` the number of vote rows on the offer across every round and both statuses.

**Closing the tally.** Finalization is the three-way fork, and the chair who drafted the offer may not perform it (`ERR_OFFER_CREATOR_CANNOT_FINALIZE`). The tally reads only those votes on this offer whose `round_number` equals the offer's current `round_number` and whose `status` is `ACTIVE`; voided votes and votes from earlier rounds are excluded. Count the approvals, the rejections, and the abstentions, and let the cast count be the sum of all three. The quorum minimum is sixty percent of `eligible_voter_count`, **rounded up to the next whole voter**; quorum is met when the cast count is at least that minimum. If quorum is not met the request itself fails with `ERR_QUORUM_NOT_MET` and the offer does not move.

If quorum is met, approval is decided by a two-thirds rule over the non-abstaining votes, evaluated in whole numbers with no floating-point comparison anywhere: the approval count multiplied by three must be at least twice the sum of the approval count and the rejection count. Abstentions count toward quorum but are excluded from that ratio. The boundary is inclusive, so exactly two thirds approves.

The two branches of the fork have deliberately different consequences. On approval the offer moves `COMMITTEE_REVIEW → APPROVED` and the candidate moves `DILIGENCE → OFFER_OUT` with the approved offer's identifier recorded in `open_offer_id`; the guarantee stays **reserved** and is not yet committed. On rejection the offer moves `COMMITTEE_REVIEW → REJECTED`, the group's `reserved_guarantee_budget_usd` is reduced by `guaranteed_comp_usd` and its `available_guarantee_budget_usd` increased by the same amount, the candidate's `pipeline_status` becomes `DECLINED`, and `open_offer_id` is cleared to null. Rejection releases the money and terminates the candidate; approval does neither. Finalizing an offer that is not in `COMMITTEE_REVIEW` is refused with `ERR_OFFER_INVALID_STATE`.

**Countering.** Rather than finalize, the chair may send the offer back for repricing, supplying a `counter_note`. A counter is permitted from `COMMITTEE_REVIEW` and from `REPRICE_REQUIRED`, and both edges move the offer to `COUNTER_REQUESTED` with an identical side-effect set: every `ACTIVE` vote on the offer becomes `VOIDED`, `active_vote_count` is set to `0` and `voided_vote_count` grows by the number just voided while `votes_cast_total` stays exactly where it was; the group's `reserved_guarantee_budget_usd` is reduced by `guaranteed_comp_usd` and `available_guarantee_budget_usd` increased by the same amount; `round_number` is incremented by 1; and `eligible_voter_count` is reset to null, so the next submission must re-declare the roll. The negotiation loop `COMMITTEE_REVIEW → COUNTER_REQUESTED → DRAFTED → COMMITTEE_REVIEW` is bounded: a counter is permitted only while `round_number` is strictly less than 3, and an attempt beyond that is refused with `ERR_NEGOTIATION_ROUND_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`.

The system can also force a reprice without anyone asking. When the assessment an offer priced against goes `STALE`, an offer in `COMMITTEE_REVIEW` is driven to `REPRICE_REQUIRED`, and that transition carries the same two housekeeping effects as a counter: every `ACTIVE` vote becomes `VOIDED` with `active_vote_count` reset to `0`, `voided_vote_count` grown by the number voided and `votes_cast_total` unchanged; and the guarantee reservation is released from `reserved_guarantee_budget_usd` back into `available_guarantee_budget_usd`. Unlike a counter, this transition does not touch `round_number` and does not clear `eligible_voter_count`.

**Acceptance and the hire.** Acceptance belongs to the candidate: the acting `Candidate` must be the offer's own candidate, the offer's `status` must be `APPROVED`, and the candidate's `clearance_status` must still be `CLEARED` — a candidate blocked by a conflict discovered after approval cannot accept, and is refused with `ERR_CLEARANCE_INCOMPLETE`. On success the offer moves `APPROVED → ACCEPTED` and `accepted_at` is set to the current logical clock. The money then converts rather than releases: the group's `reserved_guarantee_budget_usd` is reduced by `guaranteed_comp_usd`, its `committed_guarantee_total_usd` is increased by the same amount, and `available_guarantee_budget_usd` is **unchanged** — the reservation becomes a commitment. Two further hops follow inside the same request. **Hop 2**: the candidate moves `OFFER_OUT → HIRED`, with `resignation_date` and `hire_date` both set to the current logical clock and `earliest_start_date` computed as that instant advanced by `notice_period_days` days. **Hop 3**, caused by hop 2: the sourcing engagement's fee crystallises — `fee_basis` and `fee_amount_usd` are computed by the search-fee rule above using the candidate's `hire_date` as the hire instant, and `fee_status` is set to `FEE_DUE`.

Offers also die passively. When a candidate is withdrawn, an offer in `DRAFTED`, `COMMITTEE_REVIEW`, `APPROVED`, `COUNTER_REQUESTED`, or `REPRICE_REQUIRED` moves to `LAPSED` and the candidate's `open_offer_id` is cleared; only the `COMMITTEE_REVIEW` and `APPROVED` cases release a reservation, and only those two also abandon a linked integration plan, as §3.2 describes. `ACCEPTED`, `REJECTED`, and `LAPSED` are the offer's terminal statuses.

### 3.7. Integration Planning, Onboarding Capacity and Cross-Selling

A signed partner who is not integrated is a write-off, so the receiving group's leader drafts a plan before the partner walks in. A `PracticeGroupLeader` creates it against a candidate whose `pipeline_status` is `HIRED`, naming the `practice_group_id` that will receive them, the `offer_id` of the accepted offer the plan implements, an `onboarding_start_date` and an `onboarding_end_date` that must be strictly later than the start, a `planned_standard_rate_usd` greater than zero carried to two decimal places, and a `cross_sell_target_count` between 0 and 50 inclusive that records how many introductions the business plan commits to. The onboarding window is a **closed** interval — both endpoints are inside it.

Three guards apply beyond the candidate's status. The referenced offer must belong to that same candidate and its `status` must be `ACCEPTED`; an offer belonging to someone else is refused with `ERR_OFFER_CANDIDATE_MISMATCH`. The acting leader's `X-User-ID` must equal the candidate's `target_practice_group_id`, and the submitted `practice_group_id` must equal it too — a group may not draft a plan for a lateral it was not assigned. And the `onboarding_start_date` must fall at or after the candidate's `earliest_start_date`, which is the notice period the candidate still owes the prior firm; an earlier start is refused with `ERR_NOTICE_PERIOD_NOT_ELAPSED`. The new plan lands with `status` `DRAFT` and both `introduction_count` and `completed_introduction_count` at `0`.

**Onboarding slots are scarce.** Activating a plan moves it `DRAFT → ACTIVE` and consumes one unit of the group's onboarding capacity: the group's `active_onboarding_count` is incremented by 1 and the group's `version` advances. Two conditions guard it: the group's `status` must be `OPEN`, or `ERR_PRACTICE_GROUP_FROZEN`; and the group's `active_onboarding_count` must be **strictly less than** its `onboarding_capacity`, or `ERR_ONBOARDING_CAPACITY_REACHED`. Equality is a rejection — a group with a capacity of two that already carries two active plans cannot take a third. Activating a plan that is not `DRAFT` is refused with `ERR_INTEGRATION_PLAN_INVALID_STATE`.

A leader may abandon a plan, supplying an `abandonment_reason`, from either `DRAFT` or `ACTIVE`; both edges set `status` to `ABANDONED`, and both require that the acting `X-User-ID` equals the plan's own `practice_group_id`, failing with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` otherwise. In both cases a second hop cancels every `CrossSellIntroduction` on the plan whose `status` is `PROPOSED`. The two edges then diverge in exactly one respect: when the plan had been `ACTIVE`, a third hop decrements the group's `active_onboarding_count` by 1 and advances the group's `version`; when it was still `DRAFT`, that count is left untouched, because a draft plan never consumed a slot in the first place. Abandoning a plan already `COMPLETED` or `ABANDONED` is refused with `ERR_INTEGRATION_PLAN_INVALID_STATE`. The same `ABANDONED` state is also reached without a caller, as hop 3 of a candidate withdrawal.

Plans also finish by themselves. When the logical clock is moved strictly past a plan's `onboarding_end_date` while its `status` is `ACTIVE`, the plan moves `ACTIVE → COMPLETED`, the group's `active_onboarding_count` is decremented by 1, and every introduction on the plan still in `PROPOSED` becomes `CANCELLED`. Where several plans qualify in one clock move they are processed in `onboarding_end_date` ascending order, breaking ties by `created_at` ascending.

**Scheduling an introduction.** Cross-selling is the point of the exercise: the new partner is walked round the firm's existing clients. A leader schedules a slot on an `ACTIVE` plan, supplying a `target_client_name` and an `introducing_partner_name` (each 1 to 120 characters) and a `slot_start_at` and `slot_end_at`, the latter strictly after the former. Slots use **closed** interval semantics at both ends, which drives two geometry rules. The slot must be closed-contained in the plan's onboarding window — `slot_start_at` at or after `onboarding_start_date` and `slot_end_at` at or before `onboarding_end_date` — or the request is refused with `ERR_INTRODUCTION_OUTSIDE_WINDOW`. And it must not intersect any other slot on the same plan whose `status` is `PROPOSED` or `COMPLETED`, again under closed semantics, so two slots that merely touch at an endpoint **do** conflict; the refusal is `ERR_INTRODUCTION_SLOT_CONFLICT`. Cancelled slots do not block anything.

Two prior-firm restrictions guard the same operation, and they are the reason the candidate record carries what it does. First, non-solicitation: `slot_start_at` must fall at or after the candidate's `resignation_date` advanced by `non_solicit_days` days, since until that moment the partner is contractually barred from soliciting prior-firm clients; an earlier slot is refused with `ERR_NON_SOLICIT_RESTRICTION`. Second, the confidential approach: when the candidate's `approach_confidential` is true, the `target_client_name` must not equal the `client_name` of any `ACTIVE` client book entry on that candidate's questionnaire whose `client_consent_status` is `CONSENT_PENDING`. Introducing a confidentially approached partner to a prior-firm client that has not yet confirmed it would move risks exposing the approach itself, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_CONFIDENTIAL_APPROACH_RESTRICTION`. A client whose `client_consent_status` is `CONSENT_CONFIRMED` is fair game, and so is any client not on the disclosed book at all. The candidate's `pipeline_status` must also still be `HIRED`.

A scheduled slot lands with `status` `PROPOSED` and `completed_at` null, and increments the plan's `introduction_count` by 1 along with the plan's `version`. Marking it held moves `PROPOSED → COMPLETED`, which requires that the current logical clock has reached or passed the slot's `slot_start_at` — an introduction cannot be reported as held before it was due to begin — and sets `completed_at` to the current clock instant while incrementing the plan's `completed_introduction_count` by 1. Completing a slot that is not `PROPOSED` is refused with `ERR_INTRODUCTION_INVALID_STATE`. When a slot is cancelled, whether by an abandonment, by a plan completing on a clock move, or by a candidate withdrawal, the plan's `introduction_count` is decremented by 1 — so `introduction_count` always reflects the slots that are still proposed or held, while `completed_introduction_count` counts only the held ones.

### 3.8. Post-Hire Performance Tracking

The last act is measurement: a year of collections against the business plan the committee bought. A `PracticeGroupLeader` opens a review against a hired candidate, naming the `integration_plan_id` whose plan the actuals are measured against, a `period_start_date` and a `period_end_date` strictly later than it, a `variance_basis` of `PLAN_BASIS` or `RATE_ADJUSTED_BASIS`, and a `realized_standard_rate_usd` greater than zero carried to two decimal places. The review period is a **closed** interval; the leader must own the named plan, or the request fails with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER`, and an inverted or degenerate period fails with `ERR_LOOKBACK_WINDOW_INVALID`.

Everything else on the review is computed. `period_days` is the inclusive day count of the period: the difference in calendar days between the `period_end_date` and the `period_start_date`, plus one — so a period whose start and end fall on consecutive days is two days long. `actual_collections_usd` is the sum of `amount_usd` across the candidate's collection records whose `record_type` is `POST_HIRE`, whose `status` is `VERIFIED`, and whose `period_end_date` falls inside the closed review period, that is at or after `period_start_date` and at or before `period_end_date`. Unverified rows and `HISTORICAL` rows contribute nothing, and a period with no qualifying rows at all yields `actual_collections_usd` of `0.00` — that is a legitimate, poor result, not an error.

`plan_target_usd` pro-rates the committed business plan across the period: take the `expected_contribution_usd` of the accepted offer the plan implements, multiply by `period_days`, divide by 365 days, and round half up to two decimal places. `plan_target_adjusted_usd` then neutralises rate slippage: take `plan_target_usd`, multiply by the review's `realized_standard_rate_usd`, divide by the plan's `planned_standard_rate_usd`, and round half up to two decimal places. **`plan_target_adjusted_usd` is populated for both variants**, always, and is reported whichever basis was requested; the basis decides only which target the variance is measured against.

`variance_usd` is where the dual variant lands. Under `PLAN_BASIS` it is `actual_collections_usd` less `plan_target_usd`, rounded half up to two decimal places, and it ignores `realized_standard_rate_usd` entirely — the partner is measured against the plan exactly as committed. Under `RATE_ADJUSTED_BASIS` it is `actual_collections_usd` less `plan_target_adjusted_usd`, rounded the same way, which measures the partner on volume rather than on discounting: for identical collections over an identical period, a realized rate below the planned rate makes this variance strictly less negative than the plan basis would.

`attainment_pct` is `actual_collections_usd` divided by `plan_target_usd`, rounded half up to four decimal places, and is `0.0000` when `plan_target_usd` is zero. Note carefully that `attainment_pct` — and therefore the flag below — **always** uses `plan_target_usd` and never the rate-adjusted target, no matter which `variance_basis` was requested; only `variance_usd` is basis-dependent. `performance_flag` is set on **inclusive** boundaries: `ON_PLAN` when `attainment_pct` is at least `0.90`, otherwise `WATCH` when it is at least `0.70`, otherwise `BELOW_PLAN`.

Finally, `cross_sell_credit_count` counts the cross-selling introductions on the named plan whose `status` is `COMPLETED` and whose `completed_at` is at or before the review's `period_end_date`, giving the committee a non-financial read on integration alongside the collections figure.
