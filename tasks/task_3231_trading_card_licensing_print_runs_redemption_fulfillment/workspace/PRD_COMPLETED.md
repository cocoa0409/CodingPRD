# Trading Card Licensing, Print Runs & Redemption Fulfillment API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

This document specifies the product requirements for a trading-card manufacturing and licensing platform serving a collectible-card publisher. The platform covers the full commercial chain of a card release: registering the league and player-association licenses under which cards may legally be produced, planning product lines and their card sets across base, insert and premium tiers, clearing artwork through the licensor's approval rights, freezing print-run quantities against observed demand evidence, publishing the pack odds a collector will see on the wrapper, acquiring autographs through player and celebrity signing sessions with their card-stock logistics and authentication, issuing redemption cards when an autograph is not available at packout, and allocating production across hobby, mass and direct distribution channels.

The commercial stakes of this domain are unusual: every quantity is a public commitment, every unfulfilled promise becomes a collector-facing liability, and the publisher's right to produce a given card at all is bounded by a license term negotiated with a third party. The API therefore treats licensing authority as the outermost gate — a resource produced outside its license is void regardless of how well-formed the rest of the request is — and treats collector truth-in-advertising and product-safety evidence as the next layer down, ahead of internal fiscal windows. Secondary-market sentiment, expressed through collector complaints, feeds back into future print-run decisions, so the system deliberately treats complaint and claim records as inputs to quantity arithmetic rather than as reporting-only artifacts.

A final note on the operating model: the platform runs against a server-managed logical clock that is fixed at startup and advanced only by an operational call. Every term window, approval SLA, decision validity window, ship window and consumer deadline in this document is evaluated against that clock, never against wall-clock time.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting principal and `X-User-Role` declares the operational capacity the principal is acting in. A request that omits either header, or that supplies a `X-User-Role` value the platform does not recognise, is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. A request whose declared role is not permitted to perform the requested operation is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN`.

The platform recognises exactly eleven roles, spelled as follows: `LicensingManager`, `ProductManager`, `ProductionPlanner`, `SigningCoordinator`, `AuthenticationOfficer`, `LicensorApprover`, `RedemptionFulfilmentAnalyst`, `DistributionManager`, `QualityAnalyst`, `CollectorSupportSpecialist`, and `PlatformAdmin`.

Ten of these roles present an opaque operator identifier. When `X-User-Role` is `LicensingManager`, `ProductManager`, `ProductionPlanner`, `SigningCoordinator`, `AuthenticationOfficer`, `RedemptionFulfilmentAnalyst`, `DistributionManager`, `QualityAnalyst`, `CollectorSupportSpecialist`, or `PlatformAdmin`, the `X-User-ID` value is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it for audit purposes but never compares it against any entity's `id` to derive an ownership relationship.

The eleventh role is different. When `X-User-Role` is `LicensorApprover`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `License`. This role models the league or player association that holds the approval right, and it is the only role whose identity is entity-backed. Any resource that carries a reference to a `License` as its approving authority — most importantly the `approver_id` field of an artwork approval — is subject to an ownership check that compares that foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. A `LicensorApprover` therefore acts for exactly one licensor instance at a time, and an operation attempted against another licensor's artwork is refused.

`PlatformAdmin` is the operational role. It holds no business exclusives but is the only role permitted to drive the logical clock, and it may read across every resource in the platform. The remaining roles partition the business exclusives: `LicensingManager` owns license registration, activation, suspension and expiry; `ProductManager` owns product-line and card-set planning, artwork submission, and pack configuration; `ProductionPlanner` owns print-run decisions and the production and packout schedule; `SigningCoordinator` books and ships signing sessions; `AuthenticationOfficer` completes them; `RedemptionFulfilmentAnalyst` issues redemption cards, applies the substitution policy and serves collector notices; `DistributionManager` reserves, ships and recalls channel allocations; `QualityAnalyst` handles print defects and reclaim credits; and `CollectorSupportSpecialist` receives and resolves collector complaints.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints accept `limit` and `offset` as query parameters. `limit` bounds how many items are returned and defaults to 10; its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum is 100. `offset` declares how many items to skip and defaults to 0. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100, an `offset` below 0, or a value of either that is not an integer, is a malformed request refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` rather than being clamped into range.

Unless a specific list endpoint is documented otherwise, list results are ordered by creation timestamp ascending, with the earliest-created resource first.

Responses to list endpoints carry the pagination fields in the `meta` object of the envelope: `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more`.

### 2.4. The Logical Clock and Temporal Boundary Semantics

The platform does not read wall-clock time. At startup the server's logical clock reads exactly `2026-09-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time; until it is moved, every temporal evaluation in this document sees that instant. The clock is moved only by the clock-driving capability, available to `PlatformAdmin` and `LicensingManager`, which accepts a `to_iso8601` instant and reports the resulting `current_utc`. That call is the only thing it writes — it mutates no business field — but it is not inert: advancing it past an artwork approval's deadline is itself the event that expires that approval, as described in §3.2.

Every term window, approval SLA, decision validity window, ship window and consumer deadline referenced anywhere in this document is evaluated against this clock rather than against the record's own timestamps in isolation. Where a boundary is expressed as a half-open interval, the lower bound is excluded and the upper bound is included; where a rule states that a comparison is inclusive, an instant landing exactly on the boundary satisfies the rule rather than violating it. Two of the licensing checks are deliberately phrased as strict inequalities — a license may not be activated before its term begins, and may not be expired at or before its term ends — and the specific direction of each is stated at the point the rule is described.

### 2.5. Common Resource Properties and the Version Ledger

Every resource in the platform — licenses, product lines, card sets, pack configurations, signing sessions, artwork approvals, print-run decisions, redemptions, collector notifications, distribution allocations, quality claims and collector complaints — carries the same four system-managed properties.

- `id` is a server-minted UUID. It carries no type prefix and no human-readable component.
- `created_at` is an ISO 8601 timestamp in UTC with exactly three fractional digits, recording when the resource was first created.
- `updated_at` is an ISO 8601 timestamp in the same format, recording when the resource was last modified.
- `version` is an integer that increments on every mutation to the resource. It is the value clients compare against when they supply a version expectation, and it is the value the platform exposes for optimistic concurrency control.

All twelve resources carry all four properties; none of them is immutable after creation and none omits `updated_at` or `version`. Derived and aggregate fields do not escape this discipline: recalculating `set_count` on a license, or `autograph_secured_count` on a card set, is a field write and refreshes `updated_at`.

The important qualification is that recomputing an aggregate is not by itself a mutation of the entity the aggregate *describes*. Refreshing `quality_claim_count` on a card set because a quality claim moved does not increment that card set's `version`, and refreshing `collector_notification_count` on a redemption because a notification moved does not increment that redemption's `version`. Only the transitions enumerated in §3 increment a resource's own `version`. Where a transition additionally increments a *different* entity's `version` — for example, approving a card set advances the parent license's version — the transition says so explicitly, and those explicitly named bumps are the complete set of cross-entity version increments the platform performs.

### 2.6. Nested Resource Retrieval and Path Binding

Requests that name a resource by an id in the path are validated in a fixed order. The platform first confirms that the id resolves; if it does not, the request is rejected with the entity-specific not-found identifier for that resource — `ERR_LICENSE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRODUCT_LINE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CARD_SET_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PACK_CONFIGURATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SIGNING_SESSION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ARTWORK_APPROVAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRINT_RUN_DECISION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REDEMPTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COLLECTOR_NOTIFICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DISTRIBUTION_ALLOCATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_QUALITY_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, or `ERR_COLLECTOR_COMPLAINT_NOT_FOUND` — all of which carry the same status, `404`.

This applies in two shapes. An entity-addressed operation — one whose path ends in the resource's own id, such as an approval or a state transition — returns that entity's not-found identifier. A collection endpoint whose path contains a parent id as a path segment, such as the list of card sets inside a product line or the list of allocations reserved against a line, returns the not-found identifier of the *path-segment* entity. In both shapes resolution is left to right across the path, so the first identifier that fails to resolve determines the error, and the platform does not attempt to read anything to the right of it.

Where the request body supplies identifiers rather than the path, a reference that does not resolve to an existing instance is rejected with `ERR_REFERENCE_NOT_FOUND` (`404`). This covers `product_line_id`, `card_set_id`, `license_id`, `approver_id`, `redemption_id` and `player_id` wherever they appear as inputs.

### 2.7. Licensing Authority as the Outermost Gate

The platform's most consequential gate is not about the resource being operated on — it is about whether a license exists and is currently in force that authorises the operation at all. A license counts as in-force when three things hold together: its `status` is `ACTIVE` as of the logical clock, the clock has reached or passed its `term_starts_on`, and the clock has not passed its `term_ends_on`. All three are evaluated against the same clock value, and a request that fails any of them is refused with `ERR_LICENSE_NOT_IN_FORCE` (`403`).

Six operations sit behind that gate and are refused with that identifier when the license chain behind the target resource is not in force: moving a product line into readiness, starting production on a product line, submitting a card set for review, approving a card set, preparing card stock for a signing session, and recording a licensor's artwork decision.

A stricter variant applies to card-set approval alone. It is not enough that the license is in force *now*; the license must have been continuously `ACTIVE` across the entire interval that begins at the moment the artwork was submitted (`ArtworkApproval.submitted_on`) and ends at the current logical clock. A license that was suspended at any point inside that window, and later reactivated, fails this test even though it is in force at the moment of approval. The rejection is `ERR_LICENSE_TERM_WINDOW_BREACH` (`403`).

The license's own lifecycle is also guarded by temporal rules, and these are expressed as strict comparisons rather than windows. Activation — whether of a fresh `DRAFT` license or of a `SUSPENDED` one — is refused with `ERR_LICENSE_TERM_NOT_STARTED` (`422`) when the clock has not yet reached `term_starts_on`, and with `ERR_LICENSE_TERM_EXPIRED` (`422`) when the clock has advanced past `term_ends_on`. Expiry of an `ACTIVE` license is refused with `ERR_LICENSE_TERM_NOT_ENDED` (`422`) when the clock is at or before `term_ends_on`.

Finally, the rights a card set was approved under are pinned, and a pinned set cannot silently ride on rights that have since moved. A card set records the license rights version it was approved against, and three operations re-check that pin against the live license: approving a card set, starting production on an approved set, and fulfilling a redemption against a set. A mismatch is refused with `ERR_RIGHTS_VERSION_MISMATCH` (`409`), and the mechanics of the pin are described in §2.8.

### 2.8. Rights Version Pinning and Optimistic Concurrency Control

A license carries a `rights_version` integer that is distinct from its ordinary `version`. The `rights_version` is bumped specifically whenever a license mutation occurs that changes the substantive rights or the license's standing — activation, reactivation, suspension and expiry each advance it, and so do several of the downstream events described in §3. A card set, once approved, records a snapshot of the license's rights version in its own `rights_pinned_version` field. That snapshot is what protects the publisher from a set silently continuing to be produced under rights that were later suspended, expired or otherwise amended: every downstream mutation that matters re-reads the live license and compares the two values, and any divergence is refused with `ERR_RIGHTS_VERSION_MISMATCH` (`409`). A card set that has never been approved holds `rights_pinned_version` of 0, which will not match any live license.

Optimistic concurrency control is applied narrowly rather than universally. Two operations require the client to supply the card set's last observed version as a non-optional input field named `expected_card_set_version`:

- approving a card set, where the field carries the `version` the client last read from that card set;
- approving a print-run decision, where the field carries the `version` the client last read from the card set the decision is being frozen against.

If the supplied `expected_card_set_version` does not match the card set's current `version`, the request is refused with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (`412`) and no mutation occurs. This is the mechanism that stops a client from approving a set or freezing a print run based on a picture of the set that another writer has already moved past — for card-set approval, the writers that move it are the ones that return it to draft or attach new artwork; for print-run approval, the writer that moves it is the one that has just frozen a competing decision. Both rejections are stale-version rejections; neither depends on the two requests overlapping in time.

A closely related but distinct expectation governs the print-run decision's evidence base. A proposed decision records the product line version it was built against in `proposed_against_line_version`, and at approval the client must supply `expected_product_line_version`. If that expectation does not match the product line's current `version`, the approval is refused with `ERR_AUTHORITY_OUTDATED` (`412`). This check exists because the decision's projected quantity is derived from the line's observed activity, and a line that has since moved — a new allocation reserved, for instance — invalidates the evidence the projection rested on. It is a state precondition evaluated against a field the writer reads, not a concurrency race, and it is enforced as part of the ordered validation cascade described in §3.5.

No other operation in this API accepts a version expectation. Operations that are not named above do not take an `expected_card_set_version` or `expected_product_line_version` field, and supplying one has no effect.

### 2.9. Numeric Precision, Rounding and Monetary Representation

Quantities in this platform are whole cards, whole packs or whole cases, and every quantity arithmetic rule returns an integer. Where a division is required, this system never uses round-half-up; it uses a ceiling — the result is rounded upward to the next whole unit whenever the division is not exact. This direction is deliberate and is stated again at each rule that depends on it, because rounding downward would under-commit a print run against demand and would under-state a licensor's compliance obligation.

The same direction governs monetary amounts, which are stored and transmitted as integer cents rather than as decimal currency. Royalties, allocative prices and redemption compensation are all whole-cent integers. Where a substitution ratio is applied to a scratch value, the product is floored to a whole cent rather than rounded half-up — this is the single place in the platform where the rounding direction is downward, and it is downward because the ratio multiply produces a settlement credit that must never exceed the ratio's exact mathematical value.

Severity weights and count aggregates are plain integers with no rounding at all. Aggregate counters — how many sets sit under a license, how many redemptions remain outstanding against a set, how many claims have been filed — are recomputed by full traversal rather than by incremental adjustment, so a value that moves backwards (a set rolled out of approval, a claim credited rather than approved) immediately lowers the counter rather than leaving a stale increment behind.

### 2.10. Supersession, Cascade and Rollback Discipline

Several of this platform's operations are not additive. Approving a new print-run decision for a set does not sit alongside the previous decision; it supersedes it. Publishing a new pack configuration does not extend the previous one; the ladder is strictly ordered. And a license that loses its standing does not merely stop new work — it unwinds work already in flight. These behaviours share a single shape, and it is worth stating the shape once rather than restating it at each site.

The first shape is **supersession**. When a resource in an active state is replaced by a successor, the predecessor is moved into a terminal superseded state rather than deleted or left live. This applies to pack configurations, to print-run decisions, and to the artwork approval set as a whole when a card set is returned to draft.

The second shape is **downward cascade**. When a license is suspended or expired, every product line produced under it is returned to the earliest planning state, and every card set under those lines has its print-run status reset to the `UNDECIDED` state, discarding the frozen decision that had previously been in effect. The same reset fires when a product line in the packed-out state is deliberately re-entered into the approval cycle: its card sets lose their frozen print run, their `announced` flag is cleared to false while their stored `print_run_quantity` is left standing at the frozen value, and both their pack configurations and their print-run decisions are superseded wholesale.

The third shape is **compensation**. Where a device that has already happened must be undone, the platform reverses it explicitly rather than editing history. The clearest example is the quality-claim credit, which reverses a fulfilment; the recall cascade is another. Compensation always names the value it restores, and the restored value is the one the record held before the compensated event began.

Throughout all three shapes the version-bump discipline of §2.5 holds: a cascade that touches a child entity's aggregate does not bump that child's `version`, but a cascade that explicitly changes a child entity's own state field does. Where a transition in §3 bumps an entity other than its own target, it names that entity.

### 2.11. Quantity Arithmetic and Tier Quantisation

Two quantity disciplines run through the platform and are worth isolating before the workflows that use them.

The first is **per-set-then-sum**, which is the pinned evaluation order for any total derived from the card sets under a product line. Each card set's own quantity is first snapped to that set's own tier quantum — rounded up to the next multiple of that tier's quantum, where the quantum is a different number for each of the three tiers — and only then are the snapped per-set quantities added together into the line total. Applying a single snap to the summed total instead produces a different, lower number for the same inputs, and it is wrong. The counter-example that makes the difference concrete is given alongside the rule in §3.2.

The second is **floor-then-cap-then-snap**, the fixed order used whenever a demand projection is converted into a declared quantity. The projected value is first raised to no less than the tier's floor, then lowered to no more than the tier's ceiling, and only then rounded up to the tier's quantum. Applying the snap before the clamp, or the ceiling before the floor, produces different results at the boundaries, so the order is a contract rather than an implementation detail. Neither the floor nor the ceiling is optional, and no tier is exempt from either.

Both disciplines return integers. Neither ever rounds downward. The specific floor, ceiling and quantum values for each of the three tiers, and the remaining constants that feed them, are given with the rules that consume them in §3.

### 2.12. Compound Derived State

One field in the platform is not a scalar that a workflow writes directly but a status synthesised from other records. A card set's `print_run_status` is compound: while no print-run decision has been frozen for the set it reads as `UNDECIDED`, and once a decision is frozen it reads as `LOCKED` — and thereafter resolves to either `DISCLOSED` or `UNDISCLOSED` according to whether the frozen decision published its quantity. This field is recomputed on every mutation of the card set itself, on every transition of a print-run decision, and on every read of the card set, so a client can never observe a stale value. The corresponding read capability returns the compound status together with the frozen quantity, the announced flag and the decision record it was derived from, in one response.

Aggregate counters follow the same read-consistency expectation even though they are not compound in the same sense: a count exposed on a parent resource reflects the children as they stand at the moment of the read, not as they stood when the parent was last written.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. The Licensing Relationship: Standing, Terms and the Rights Pin

Everything in this platform is downstream of a license. A `LicensingManager` registers one by naming the granting body in `grantor_name` and declaring the commercial shape of the arrangement: whether the grantor may license a competing manufacturer, expressed as `exclusivity_scope` and taking one of exactly two values, `EXCLUSIVE` or `NON_EXCLUSIVE`, and settling to `EXCLUSIVE` when the caller does not supply it; an inclusive term start in `term_starts_on` and an inclusive term end in `term_ends_on`; and a licensor service level for artwork decisions, `approval_window_days`, which defaults to 10 days when the caller does not supply it. Registration is immediate and the new license is not yet usable: it opens its life in `DRAFT`, with `rights_version` at 0, and with both of its aggregate counters — `set_count` and `approved_set_count` — at 0. Licenses can be browsed as a collection, filtered by `status` or by `grantor_name`, and read individually; a license's product lines are browsable as a nested collection.

Three transitions move a license between states, and all three are the exclusive province of the `LicensingManager`. The first is activation, which serves double duty: it takes a `DRAFT` license live, and it also brings a `SUSPENDED` license back. The temporal preconditions are shared and expressed as strict comparisons against the logical clock — the clock must have reached or passed `term_starts_on`, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_LICENSE_TERM_NOT_STARTED`; and the clock must not have passed `term_ends_on`, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_LICENSE_TERM_EXPIRED`. Activation from `DRAFT` carries no further precondition. Activation from `SUSPENDED` carries one additional requirement: the license must already govern at least one card set in `APPROVED` status. Reactivating a license that has never had a set approved is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. In both cases the effect is the same — the license's `status` becomes `ACTIVE`, its `version` advances by one, and its `rights_version` advances by one.

Suspension is the platform's bluntest instrument, and its blast radius is the reason it exists. The precondition is simply that the license is currently `ACTIVE`; anything else is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The visible effect is that `status` becomes `SUSPENDED` and both `version` and `rights_version` advance by one. The invisible effect is what matters commercially: every product line whose `license_id` points at this license is returned to `PLANNING`, with each of those lines having its `sets_per_case` recomputed as part of the cascade; and every card set belonging to one of those product lines has its print-run status reset to the `UNDECIDED` value, discarding whatever print-run decision had been frozen for it — the reset clears the status alone and leaves the set's stored `print_run_quantity` and its `announced` flag exactly as they stood. A suspension does not reverse approvals — sets stay approved — but it does cancel the quantity commitments that had been made under them, because those commitments are not sound to honour while the rights are in doubt.

The third transition is expiry, which ends the arrangement permanently. It is available only from `ACTIVE`, and only once the clock has moved strictly past `term_ends_on` — an attempt at exactly `term_ends_on`, or before it, is refused with `ERR_LICENSE_TERM_NOT_ENDED`. Expiry's cascade is the same shape as suspension's but is not identical in a way that is easy to miss: the license's `status` becomes `EXPIRED`, its `version` advances by one, and its `rights_version` advances by one; every product line under it returns to `PLANNING`; and every card set under those lines has its print-run status reset to the `UNDECIDED` value, again clearing only that status while leaving the set's stored `print_run_quantity` and `announced` flag untouched. Expiry is terminal — there is no path back out of `EXPIRED` — so a publisher that expires a license by mistake has to register a new one, and the sets under the old license will need to be re-approved against the new rights pin.

That pin is worth restating as a workflow rather than as a field definition. When a card set is approved, the platform captures the license's rights version at that instant into the set's `rights_pinned_version`. From then on, three distinct operations re-read the live license and compare: the set cannot start production, and a redemption against the set cannot be fulfilled, and the set cannot be re-approved, unless `rights_pinned_version` still equals the license's current `rights_version`. A mismatch is refused with `ERR_RIGHTS_VERSION_MISMATCH` (`409`). Because suspension, expiry and every other license mutation advances `rights_version`, a suspended-then-reactivated license leaves every set that was approved before the suspension holding a stale pin — those sets will be refused at every subsequent mutation until they are returned to draft and approved again. This is intentional: the pin is the platform's record of the exact rights regime a set was cleared under.

The two aggregate counters on a license are maintained by full traversal rather than by arithmetic on deltas. `set_count` is the number of card sets, in any status whatsoever, whose parent product line names this license. `approved_set_count` is the subset of those whose `status` is `APPROVED`. Both are recomputed whenever anything under the license moves — a card set changing status, artwork being decided, a redemption being fulfilled, card stock being shipped — and recomputing them never advances the card set's own `version`, only the license's.

### 3.2. Product Lines and Card Sets: Planning, Artwork Clearance and Pack Configuration

A `ProductManager` opens a product line by placing it under a license. The request names the license in `license_id`, gives the line its commercial identity in `line_name`, assigns its assortment tier in `line_kind` — one of `BASE`, `INSERT` or `PREMIUM` — and declares the physical hierarchy the run will be manufactured in: `packs_per_box`, `cards_per_pack` and `boxes_per_case`. The run's scale is declared separately as `cases_printed`, and the schedule it is planned against is `run_planned_on`. A new line opens in `PLANNING` and has its `sets_per_case` derived immediately. That derived value is the product of the three physical factors — boxes per case, multiplied by packs per box, multiplied by cards per pack — and it is required to be an exact multiple of `CARDS_PER_SET`, the platform's standard set size of 100 cards; a configuration whose product is not divisible by 100 is refused with `ERR_BOX_CONFIGURATION_INVALID`. Note that `packs_per_box`, `cards_per_pack`, `boxes_per_case`, `cases_printed`, `run_planned_on` and `line_name` all remain editable — but only while the line is still `PLANNING`. Once the line leaves planning it is frozen; this is the only update-shaped operation in the platform, and it recomputes `sets_per_case` and advances the line's `version`.

Moving a line into readiness is the `ProductManager`'s declaration that the line is fit to be manufactured. From `PLANNING` it requires that the license named by `license_id` be in force, and that the physical configuration satisfy the divisibility rule above; the effect is `status` of `READY_FOR_APPROVAL` and a recomputed `sets_per_case`. The same readiness move is also the re-entry path from `PACKED_OUT`, and there the preconditions are entirely different: the license must be in force, and every card set under the line must carry at least one published pack configuration, or the move is refused with `ERR_PACK_CONFIG_MISSING`. Re-entering readiness from a packed-out line is a destructive reset. Every card set under the line has its print-run status set to the `UNDECIDED` value and its `announced` flag cleared to false, while its stored `print_run_quantity` is left at the value the discarded decision had frozen onto it; every pack configuration belonging to those sets is superseded; and every print-run decision belonging to those sets is superseded. This is how a publisher pulls a shipped line back for a re-cut without deleting the audit trail of what was previously declared.

From there the line moves to production and then to packout. Starting production on a line requires the license in force, and — because no transition in the platform ever lands a line in the `APPROVED` state, readiness being the furthest a line advances before production — it accepts a line whose status is either `APPROVED` or `READY_FOR_APPROVAL`; it also requires that at least one card set under the line already carry a locked print run — a line whose sets have no frozen quantities cannot be manufactured, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_NO_LOCKED_PRINT_RUN` — and it applies the same channel-capacity arithmetic as reserving an allocation, refusing with `ERR_ALLOCATION_EXCEEDS_PRINT_RUN` when the cases already allocated to the line exceed its `cases_printed`. Besides setting `status` to `IN_PRODUCTION`, this transition is one of the few that reaches across to the license: the license's `version` advances by one and its `set_count` and `approved_set_count` are recomputed. Packing a line out is the last line-level move, and its precondition is a coverage check across the line's autograph commitments: every card set under the line whose `autograph_planned_count` exceeds zero must by then have `autograph_secured_count` at least equal to that plan, and a shortfall is refused with `ERR_AUTOGRAPH_QUANTITY_SHORT`. Packing out sets the line to `PACKED_OUT`, closes every redemption under the line's sets that is in `FULFILLED` status, and recomputes `autograph_redemptions_outstanding` on those sets.

Card sets carry the physical reality that a line's configuration only describes in the abstract. A set is created inside a line by naming `product_line_id`, giving it a `set_name`, assigning its own `set_tier` — again `BASE`, `INSERT` or `PREMIUM`, and the tier may differ from the line's — and declaring three intent flags: `serial_numbered`, whether every produced card carries a serial number; `announced`, whether its quantity will be published; and `autograph_planned_count`, how many autographed cards the set intends to pack out. A new set opens in `DRAFT` with its print-run status `UNDECIDED` and its rights pin at 0. Its `print_run_quantity` is the interesting input: it is required for `BASE` sets and forbidden for `INSERT` and `PREMIUM` sets, which is enforced not at creation but at approval, where a `BASE` set whose `print_run_quantity` is below 1 is refused with `ERR_PRINT_RUN_REQUIRED` and an `INSERT` or `PREMIUM` set carrying a non-zero `print_run_quantity` is refused with `ERR_PRINT_RUN_NOT_ALLOWED`. The interpretation is that base sets are quantity-manufactured and premium and insert sets are not.

A set's route to approval runs through the licensor. The `ProductManager` submits artwork first, creating an artwork approval that names its `card_set_id` implicitly, carries an internal `artwork_code`, declares the licensor holding the right in `approver_id`, and records the submission instant in `submitted_on`. Newly submitted artwork is in `SUBMITTED` status and the submission advances the card set's `version`. Submission of the set for review requires that its parent line already be `READY_FOR_APPROVAL` — otherwise `ERR_PRODUCT_LINE_NOT_READY` — that at least one piece of the set's artwork already be `APPROVED` (`ERR_ARTWORK_NOT_APPROVED`), and that no piece of its artwork still be sitting in `SUBMITTED` (`ERR_ARTWORK_APPROVAL_PENDING`). A set meeting those conditions moves to `UNDER_REVIEW`.

The `LicensorApprover` decides the artwork. The decision payload carries a `decision` taking one of `APPROVE` or `REJECT`, and, when it is a decision at all, a `decided_on` instant; a `decision` value outside that pair is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Approving artwork requires the clock to be at or before the deadline computed as `submitted_on` plus the license's `approval_window_days`; past that deadline the request is refused with `ERR_APPROVAL_WINDOW_CLOSED`. The same deadline governs rejection: a rejection after the window is also refused with `ERR_APPROVAL_WINDOW_CLOSED`. Approval sets the artwork to `APPROVED`, records `decided_on`, advances the artwork's `version`, advances the license's `version`, and recomputes the license's two counters. Rejection sets the artwork to `REJECTED`, records `decided_on`, and advances the artwork's `version` — and it carries a further side effect that is easy to under-implement: if the rejection leaves the card set with no remaining `APPROVED` artwork at all, that card set is returned to `DRAFT`. Note that no explicit license-in-force gate appears on the rejection branch even though approval carries one; the asymmetry is deliberate and reflects that a licensor refusing artwork is not producing anything under the rights.

A third, system-driven outcome exists for artwork nobody decides. Advancing the logical clock past an artwork's deadline is itself the trigger that expires it: any artwork still sitting in `SUBMITTED` whose `submitted_on` plus the license's `approval_window_days` has been passed by the new clock value is moved to `EXPIRED`. Expiry advances the artwork's `version` and deliberately leaves the parent card set's status exactly as it was — an expired artwork is not a rejection, and it does not silently un-approve the set it belongs to.

From `UNDER_REVIEW`, and also from `APPROVED` — the recovery path §3.1 names for a set whose rights pin went stale after a suspension or expiry — a set can be returned to draft by the `ProductManager`, which reverses the review and additionally rejects every one of the set's artwork approvals that is still in `SUBMITTED`. A set in any other status is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The subsequent approval of a set is the platform's most heavily gated single operation and is described in §3.8, but its outcome is straightforward: the set becomes `APPROVED`, `rights_pinned_version` is set to the license's current `rights_version`, and the license's counters are recomputed. An approved set cannot enter production until its print run is locked — starting production on a set whose print-run status is not locked is refused with `ERR_PRINT_RUN_NOT_LOCKED` — nor while the parent line's status is outside the set of `READY_FOR_APPROVAL`, `APPROVED` and `IN_PRODUCTION`, which is refused with `ERR_PRODUCT_LINE_STATE_BLOCKED`. A set that passes both gates moves to `IN_PRODUCTION`, advances its own `version`, and has `quality_claim_count` and `settled_complaint_count` recomputed as part of the transition. Packing a set out requires the parent line to have already reached `PACKED_OUT` (`ERR_PRODUCT_LINE_NOT_PACKED_OUT`) and requires the set to carry at least one published pack configuration (`ERR_PACK_CONFIG_MISSING`); packing out closes the set's fulfilled redemptions, marks its autograph allocations as delivered and recomputes `autograph_secured_count`, and refreshes `autograph_redemptions_outstanding`, `quality_claim_count` and `settled_complaint_count` on the set.

Pack configuration is where a set's stated odds are set, and it is governed by a strict ladder. A `ProductManager` proposes a configuration by naming `card_set_id`, giving the configuration its `config_serial` — an ordinal in the range 1 through 30 — and declaring the composition of one pack in three parts: `base_packs`, `insert_packs` and `premium_packs`. The proposal computes `total_cards` as the sum of those three. Publishing is where the checks bite. The parent set must be `APPROVED` or `IN_PRODUCTION`, or the attempt is refused with `ERR_SET_NOT_APPROVED`. The `config_serial` must lie within 1 through 30 inclusive, or it is refused with `ERR_CONFIG_SERIAL_OUT_OF_RANGE` — and separately, the ladder must hold: the configuration being published must be exactly one greater than the number of configurations already published for that set, so a gap or a repeat is refused with `ERR_CONFIG_LADDER_VIOLATION`. The composition must match the line: `base_packs` plus `insert_packs` plus `premium_packs` must equal the parent product line's `cards_per_pack`, and a mismatch is refused with `ERR_PACK_CONFIG_TOTAL_MISMATCH`. Finally, a set whose `announced` flag is false may not publish a configuration carrying any insert-tier cards at all: a non-zero `insert_packs` on an unannounced set is refused with `ERR_PACK_ODDS_NOT_PUBLISHABLE`, because insert odds cannot be quoted in a wrapper that does not disclose its run. Publication sets the configuration to `PUBLISHED`, derives `odd_value` as the quantity quoted for every physical pack in the set — which is the set's `print_run_quantity` — and advances both the configuration's and the card set's `version`.

Superseding a published configuration reverses the set's stated odds back to draft revision. It requires the configuration to currently be `PUBLISHED` (`ERR_INVALID_STATE`), sets it to `SUPERSEDED`, and — this is the part implementations commonly miss — returns every *other* pack configuration for the same card set that is currently `PUBLISHED` back to `PROPOSED`. The card set's `version` advances.

### 3.3. Freezing the Print Run: Demand Evidence and the Quantity Projection

A card set's manufacturing quantity is never simply accepted from the client. It is projected from evidence, and the projection is the platform's most involved calculation. A `ProductionPlanner` proposes a print-run decision against an approved set, supplying the `card_set_id`, an `authority` naming which evidence the projection is built from — one of `SALES_HISTORY`, `MARKET_DEMAND` or `BRAND_DIRECTIVE` — a `proposed_against_line_version` recording the version of the parent product line the proposal was built against, a `demand_multiplier` such as 1.05, a `declared_quantity` the planner asserts, an `announced` flag, and a `proposed_on` instant. The proposal is accepted in `PROPOSED` status and its `final_quantity` is computed immediately so the planner can see it. That immediate computation is a real evaluation, not a placeholder: an authority whose evidence is absent — fewer than three qualifying complaints for `SALES_HISTORY`, or no `HOBBY`- or `MASS`-channel allocation for `MARKET_DEMAND` — refuses the proposal itself with the authority's identifier (`ERR_INSUFFICIENT_SALES_HISTORY` or `ERR_MARKET_SIGNAL_UNAVAILABLE`), and the same check is re-run and enforced again at approval.

The projection proceeds in four ordered stages, and every one of them is tier-sensitive. The set's tier is read from its own `set_tier`, and each of the three tiers has its own entries in the platform's three per-tier tables. Keyed on the tier, the `PROJECTION_FLOOR` entry is 1000 for `BASE`, 100 for `INSERT` and 25 for `PREMIUM`; the `PROJECTION_CEILING` entry is 50000 for `BASE`, 10000 for `INSERT` and 2000 for `PREMIUM`; and the `PROJECTION_MULTIPLE_OF` quantum is 500 for `BASE`, 100 for `INSERT` and 25 for `PREMIUM`. These are the only three tiers and every lookup is keyed on the enum, never on a notion of a "best" or "next" tier.

The first stage establishes a base quantity from the chosen authority, and the three authorities differ in kind, not merely in constant. Under `SALES_HISTORY` the base is derived from collector complaints. The platform counts the complaints whose `card_set_id` is this set, whose `status` is either `APPROVED` or `SETTLED`, and whose `filed_on` lies strictly after the instant `CLAIM_BACKDATE_WINDOW_DAYS` — thirty days — before the clock and at or before the clock itself; the window is half-open, so a complaint filed exactly thirty days ago does not qualify while one filed exactly now does. Complaints in `FILED`, `UNDER_REVIEW` or `DENIED` status are excluded entirely. If fewer than `SALES_HISTORY_MIN_RECORDS` — three — such complaints exist the projection cannot be built and the request is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_SALES_HISTORY`; otherwise the base is that count multiplied by `SALES_HISTORY_UNITS_PER_CLAIM`, which is 1000 cards per complaint. Under `MARKET_DEMAND` the base is instead built from channel reservations: the platform sums `quantity_allocated` across all distribution allocations for the set's parent product line whose `channel` is either `HOBBY` or `MASS` — direct-channel reservations are excluded — and multiplies that sum by `MARKET_DEMAND_UNITS_PER_CASE`, which is 10 cards per reserved case; if no such allocation exists at all the request is refused with `ERR_MARKET_SIGNAL_UNAVAILABLE`. Under `BRAND_DIRECTIVE` the platform reads no observed activity whatsoever: the base is simply the `BRAND_DIRECTIVE_BASE` entry for the tier, which is 12000 for `BASE`, 3000 for `INSERT` and 400 for `PREMIUM`. This third variant is why two identical requests can produce different numbers: the directive bases for `INSERT` and `PREMIUM` sit above those tiers' floors while the `BASE` directive base sits below the `BASE` floor, so a `BRAND_DIRECTIVE` projection on a base set collapses to the floor whenever the multiplier is small, whereas the two observation-driven authorities produce whatever the observed window happens to justify.

The second stage multiplies the base by the decision's `demand_multiplier`. The third stage clamps, floor first and then ceiling: the scaled value is raised to no less than the tier's `PROJECTION_FLOOR`, and the result of that is then lowered to no more than the tier's `PROJECTION_CEILING`. The fourth stage snaps the clamped value upward to the tier's `PROJECTION_MULTIPLE_OF` quantum using a ceiling division — never a round-half-up — so the returned quantity is always an exact multiple of the tier's quantum and always an integer. The value returned by this four-stage process is the `final_quantity`, and 0 is not a valid projection output for any tier.

A separate safety floor is interposed between the third and fourth stages, and it exists to make defect evidence bind on future manufacturing. The platform takes all quality claims whose `card_set_id` is this set and whose `status` is `APPROVED` — a `FILED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `DENIED` or `CREDITED` claim contributes nothing — sums their `affected_quantity` into a verifiable total, multiplies that total by `REORDER_HIT_RATE`, the reorder hit rate of 0.005, to obtain a threshold, and computes a safety floor by ceiling that threshold up to the tier's `PROJECTION_MULTIPLE_OF` quantum. The value carried into the fourth stage is then the greatest of three numbers: the clamped value from the third stage, the threshold itself, and the safety floor. The consequence is that a set with even a modest approved defect exposure gets its projection lifted by at least one whole quantum, and a set with no approved claims at all falls through to exactly the third-stage value — so the same multiplier and tier produce two different quantities depending only on whether a claim has been approved against the set. Because that dependence runs through claim review state, crediting a claim (which moves it out of `APPROVED`) lowers the floor again on the next projection.

Approval of the decision is where the assertions are tested, not where the evidence is first read — and this ordering is the point of the design. Approval requires the card set to be `APPROVED` (`ERR_INVALID_STATE`); requires the supplied `expected_card_set_version` to match the card set's current `version`, else `ERR_STALE_VERSION`; requires the freshness precondition of the chosen authority — at least 3 qualifying complaints in the back-date window for `SALES_HISTORY`, else `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_SALES_HISTORY`, or at least one `HOBBY`- or `MASS`-channel allocation for `MARKET_DEMAND`, else `ERR_MARKET_SIGNAL_UNAVAILABLE`; requires `expected_product_line_version` to equal the parent line's current `version`, else `ERR_AUTHORITY_OUTDATED`; requires the recomputed `final_quantity` to equal the `declared_quantity` the proposal asserted, else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; and requires the clock to be at or before `proposed_on` plus the decision validity window, `PRINT_DECISION_VALIDITY_DAYS` being 30 days, else `ERR_PRINT_DECISION_WINDOW_CLOSED`. A proposal whose evidence moved between proposal and approval is therefore rejected rather than silently rewritten.

An approved decision is the freeze. It supersedes every other decision for the same set that is currently `APPROVED`, sets the set's print-run status to `LOCKED`, sets the set's `print_run_quantity` to the decision's `final_quantity`, sets the set's `announced` flag to the decision's `announced`, and advances the set's `version`. Note the compound status resolves further than `LOCKED`: once the freeze lands, a set whose frozen decision announced its quantity reads as `DISCLOSED`, and one whose decision did not reads as `UNDISCLOSED`, per §2.12.

Freezing a run is reversible only while the line has not yet shipped. Superseding an approved decision requires the decision to be `APPROVED` (`ERR_INVALID_STATE`) and the parent line's status to be either `READY_FOR_APPROVAL` or `APPROVED` — a line already in production or booked out cannot have its decisions unwound this way, and the attempt is refused with `ERR_PRODUCT_LINE_STATE_BLOCKED`. The effect is that the decision becomes `SUPERSEDED`, the card set's print-run status returns to the `UNDECIDED` value, the set's `print_run_quantity` returns to 0 and its `announced` flag to false, and the set's `version` advances.

### 3.4. Autograph Acquisition: Signing Sessions, Card Stock Logistics and Authentication

Autographed cards are not manufactured; they are obtained, and the obtaining is a logistics operation in its own right. A `SigningCoordinator` books a session by naming the `signer_name`, declaring the `signer_kind`, tying the session to the license that authorises it through `license_id`, setting the local calendar date in `scheduled_on`, and attaching the card stock the session will draw on as `allocation_lines`. Each allocation line names the `card_set_id` the stock comes from, the `player_id` whose cards are being signed, and `quantity_delivered` — a client-declared literal count of how many of that player's cards the line puts in the signer's hands. A new session opens in `SCHEDULED`, and booking derives `ship_date` as the `scheduled_on` date pulled back by the `SIGNER_SHIP_LEAD_DAYS` shipping lead time the platform applies to signer stock, a lead time of 30 days. That derived date matters later: it is the date the card stock must leave the printer, and it does not move when the session moves.

`signer_kind` is the field that determines which of two governing regimes applies, and the two are genuinely different. A signer of kind `PLAYER_ASSOCIATION` is a member of an association whose rights are administered under a license, so the session is bound by that license's term window: preparing its stock requires the license named in `license_id` to be in force, and the session is refused when it is not. A signer of kind `INDEPENDENT` carries no license tie, so the license-in-force gate does not apply to it at all; an independent celebrity books, prepares and ships under the overlap rule alone.

Preparing card stock is the first move after booking, and it is refused unless the license is in force when the signer is association-bound. It also carries two checks that apply to signers of either kind. The first is the signer-availability rule: no other session for the same `signer_name` whose status is `SCHEDULED`, `STOCK_PREPARED` or `STOCK_SHIPPED` may have a window that overlaps this one, where each session's window is the half-open day starting at its `scheduled_on` and ending one day later — meaning a session on the same calendar day always overlaps, back-to-back days do not, and sessions already in `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED` status are out of the way. An overlap is refused with `ERR_SIGNER_DOUBLE_BOOKED`, and this rule applies to independent signers exactly as it applies to association-bound ones. The second check is that every allocation line's `card_set_id` must resolve to a card set that is currently `APPROVED`; a line pointing at a set in any other status is refused with `ERR_CARD_SET_NOT_APPROVED`. Passing both, the session moves to `STOCK_PREPARED` and its `version` advances. A session can also be abandoned here: cancellation is available while the session is `SCHEDULED` and only then, its precondition being exactly that status (`ERR_INVALID_STATE`), and it moves the session to the terminal `CANCELLED` state.

Shipping the stock is where the session becomes expensive and where the royalty ledger is written. The gate is the clock: the session cannot ship before its `ship_date`, and an attempt earlier than that is refused with `ERR_SIGNER_SHIP_WINDOW_CLOSED`. Two further conditions protect the publisher's autograph commitments. For every allocation line, the card set must still be `APPROVED`, and the arithmetic `autograph_secured_count` plus that line's `quantity_delivered` must not exceed the set's `autograph_planned_count` — a line that would push the set past what it planned to autograph is refused with `ERR_AUTOGRAPH_ALLOCATION_EXCEEDS_PLAN`. And for every allocation line, the platform tests the line against that player's produced tally for the card set, read across the rest of the platform rather than against the line itself: the count of that player's cards produced for that set as of the logical clock is the sum of `quantity_delivered` across every allocation line naming that set and that player on any other session that has reached `STOCK_SHIPPED` or `COMPLETED`, and the line is refused with `ERR_CARD_STOCK_NOT_PRODUCED_AT_SIGNING` when that tally falls below the number the line delivers; a line delivering no more than the produced tally passes. What survives all three checks moves the session to `STOCK_SHIPPED` and advances its `version`.

The royalty charge is computed per allocation line and summed. Each line's charge is the platform's flat `SIGNER_ROYALTY_CENTS` rate of 750 cents per card, multiplied by that line's `quantity_delivered`, and the session's royalty total is the sum of those per-line charges, recorded on the session. Every allocation line is charged, including lines whose card set is merely approved and has not shipped, and including lines whose set later ships. Lines are processed in ascending order of the pair (`card_set_id`, `player_id`) so that the per-line ledger entries are reproducible run to run; the sum itself does not depend on the order, and the ordering pin exists only to make the line-item trail stable. The shipping transition also recomputes `autograph_secured_count` and `autograph_planned_count` on every referenced card set, and recomputes `total_allocated_count` on the session as the sum of `quantity_delivered` across its lines. It reaches the license as well: the license's `version` advances by one and its `set_count` and `approved_set_count` are recomputed.

Completion is the `AuthenticationOfficer`'s act and is where the autographs are certified. The only input is `signer_attendance`, and the sole precondition is that this value is anything other than `NO_SHOW`; completing a session whose signer failed to appear is refused with `ERR_AUTOGRAPH_AUTHENTICATION_FAILED`. A successful completion sets the session to `COMPLETED` and cascades outward to every redemption that was waiting on this signer: each redemption whose status is `ISSUED`, whose `signer_name` matches the session's signer, and whose `card_set_id` is named by one of the session's allocation lines is moved to `FULFILLED`, and `autograph_redemptions_outstanding` is recomputed on every referenced card set. `COMPLETED` is terminal, and so is `CANCELLED`; there is no path from either back into the working states.

### 3.5. Redemption Fulfillment and the Substitution Policy

When a signer's autograph is not available at packout, the publisher's obligation does not disappear — it becomes a redemption card. A `RedemptionFulfilmentAnalyst` issues one against the set it was found in, supplying a `redemption_code` that is globally unique, the `signer_name` whose autograph the card promises, an `estimated_value_cents` representing the scratch value of that promised autograph, and the `opened_on` date on which the collector opened it. A redemption code already in use by another redemption is refused with `ERR_REDEMPTION_CODE_DUPLICATE`. New redemptions open in `ISSUED`, and issuing one recomputes `autograph_redemptions_outstanding` on the set.

The fulfilment deadline is not a configured constant but a function of the promised value, and it is fixed at issuance. The threshold that separates the two regimes is `SUBSTITUTION_DIVERGENCE_CENTS`, 250000 cents. A redemption whose `estimated_value_cents` is at least that threshold is granted `SUBSTITUTION_DEADLINE_DAYS_HIGH`, 730 days; one below it is granted `SUBSTITUTION_DEADLINE_DAYS_LOW`, 365 days. The boundary is inclusive on the high side, so a scratch value of exactly 250000 already takes the longer window. The absolute deadline is then `opened_on` plus `deadline_days` days, evaluated against the logical clock rather than against real time.

A redemption can be fulfilled either by the system or by an analyst. The system fires it when the redemption's signer reaches session completion, as described in §3.4. An analyst can also fulfil one directly once the signer has delivered, and that route carries a rights check: the redemption's card set must still be in force with respect to its pinned rights — `rights_pinned_version` must equal the license's current `rights_version` — or the fulfilment is refused with `ERR_RIGHTS_VERSION_MISMATCH`. A successful fulfilment sets the redemption to `FULFILLED`, advances the license's `version`, recomputes the license's `set_count` and `approved_set_count`, and recomputes `autograph_redemptions_outstanding` on the set.

Two terminal routes follow from `FULFILLED`. Closing a fulfilled redemption requires the clock to be at or before `opened_on` plus `deadline_days`; closing after that is refused with `ERR_REDEMPTION_DEADLINE_EXCEEDED`. Closing sets the status to `CLOSED` and recomputes the set's outstanding count. Closing is also driven in bulk: packing out a card set or a product line closes every redemption under the affected sets that is already `FULFILLED`.

The other terminal route is cancellation, and it is reserved for the case where the publisher failed to deliver in time. Cancellation requires the redemption to still be `ISSUED` (`ERR_INVALID_STATE`) and requires the clock to have moved strictly past `opened_on` plus `deadline_days` — a cancellation at or before the deadline is refused with `ERR_REDEMPTION_DEADLINE_NOT_EXCEEDED`, because a redemption that is still inside its window must be fulfilled, not abandoned. Cancellation sets the status to `CANCELLED` and sets `estimated_value_cents` to 0, reflecting that the promised autograph is no longer the consideration; the outstanding count on the set is recomputed.

Cancellation is what makes the substitution policy live, and the substitution arithmetic is driven off the scratch value the redemption held before it was cancelled. The policy distinguishes two regimes by the same `SUBSTITUTION_DIVERGENCE_CENTS` boundary of 250000 used for the deadline, inclusive on the high side. Below the boundary the `SUBSTITUTION_RATIO_LOW` ratio of 1.00 applies — leaving the compensation equal to the scratch value itself — and at or above it the `SUBSTITUTION_RATIO_HIGH` ratio of 1.25 applies, raising the compensation a quarter above scratch. The compensation amount is the ratio multiplied by the scratch value, truncated downward to a whole cent; it is never rounded half-up, so a ratio multiply can never produce a credit larger than the ratio's exact value. The two regimes therefore produce two different credited amounts, and they equally produce two different consumer-facing deadlines of `SUBSTITUTION_DEADLINE_DAYS_LOW` (365 days) and `SUBSTITUTION_DEADLINE_DAYS_HIGH` (730 days) respectively, from the same issuance-time input. The compensation amount is the ledger credit that the settlement notice books, and when that notice is settled the platform compares it for exact equality against the scratch the redemption carried.

### 3.6. Collector Notices, Settlement and Compensating Reversals

The collector-facing side of a failed redemption is a notice. Serving one on a redemption is a `RedemptionFulfilmentAnalyst` action that takes a `notice_code` the collector selected — the codes carry their own meaning, of which `SUBST-90` (a substitution offer) and `MISS-120` (a missed card) are representative — and the `opened_on` date the notice was served. The server renders the matching `notice_text` for the code, and that rendered text includes the computed deadline date so the collector sees an absolute date rather than a relative window. A newly served notice sits in `ISSUED` status, and serving it recomputes `collector_notification_count` on the redemption.

Acknowledging a served notice is available while the notice is `ISSUED` and only then (`ERR_INVALID_STATE`), and it stops the missed-card escalation path from running further: the notice moves to `ACKNOWLEDGED`, and — unusually for a notice-level action — the parent redemption's `version` is advanced by one as well. The notice's own `version` advances too, and the redemption's notification count is recomputed.

Settlement is the notice-level act that books the substitution credit, and it is available directly from `ISSUED` rather than only from `ACKNOWLEDGED`. Two conditions must hold, and both fail with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. The first is that the parent redemption must already be in `CANCELLED` status — a substitution notice cannot settle against a redemption that is still live or that was fulfilled. The second is that the compensation value must equal the scratch value exactly, expressed through the `compensation_value_equals_scratch` input: the analyst asserts this, and the platform requires the assertion to be true before the settlement is recorded. Because the redemption's status is part of that check, settlement also takes the redemption's current status as an input, supplied as `redemption_status`; a request that presents a redemption status other than `CANCELLED` is refused with the same `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. Settling moves the notice to the terminal `SETTLED` state and records on the notice the booked credit in `compensation_value_cents`, computed by floor-truncating the applicable substitution ratio against the scratch value the redemption carried before it was cancelled; it then recomputes the redemption's `collector_notification_count`, and recomputes the parent card set's `settled_complaint_count`. Settlement is also evaluated against the same consumer-facing deadline that governs redemption closure — the clock must be at or before `opened_on` plus `deadline_days`, with the boundary treated as inclusive — and a settlement past that deadline is refused with `ERR_REDEMPTION_DEADLINE_EXCEEDED`. A notice that was acknowledged and carries no substitution credit can instead be resolved, which requires the `ACKNOWLEDGED` status and moves the notice to the terminal `RESOLVED` state, recomputing the notification count. `SETTLED` and `RESOLVED` are the two terminal ends of the notice lifecycle and there is no path between them or back out of either.

The platform's compensation discipline reaches further than notices. Quality claims, described in §3.7, can reverse a fulfilment that has already been recorded: crediting a claim against a set returns every redemption under that set that is currently `FULFILLED` back to `ISSUED`, restoring each redemption's `estimated_value_cents` to the value it held when the reversal began, recomputing the set's outstanding count, and advancing the set's `version`. The reverse direction exists too and is equally deliberate: fulfilling, cancelling, closing or acknowledging a redemption all recompute counts but do not touch the set's `version`, because a count refresh is not a state change on the set — only the compensating reversal, which explicitly changes the set's own status field, bumps it.

### 3.7. Defect Handling: Quality Claims, Credited Reclaims and Collector Complaints

Print defects are recorded as quality claims and they are not inert — an approved claim raises the safety floor of the next print-run projection, as described in §3.3. A `QualityAnalyst` files one against a set, classifying it in `claim_type` as one of `MISPRINT`, `DAMAGE` or `MISSING_HIT`, grading it in `severity` as one of `MINOR`, `MAJOR` or `CRITICAL`, stating how many cards it affects in `affected_quantity`, and dating it in `filed_on`. Filing opens the claim in `FILED` status, computes its `claim_weight` from the severity — `SEVERITY_WEIGHT_MINOR` of 1 for `MINOR`, `SEVERITY_WEIGHT_MAJOR` of 2 for `MAJOR` and `SEVERITY_WEIGHT_CRITICAL` of 3 for `CRITICAL`, with the `severity` enum closed and every value weighted — and recomputes `quality_claim_count` on the set. The weight is recomputed whenever severity is changed before approval, and it is what feeds both the print-run safety floor and the recall threshold in §3.8.

Opening review on a filed claim imposes the evidence window, and the window is expressed as a strict comparison rather than as a span: the claim's `filed_on` must be strictly after the instant thirty days before the clock. A claim filed exactly thirty days ago, or earlier, is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_BACKDATE_EXCEEDED`. Opening review moves the claim to `UNDER_REVIEW` and recomputes the set's claim count.

From `UNDER_REVIEW` the claim forks. Approval requires the claim to still be in `UNDER_REVIEW` (`ERR_INVALID_STATE`) and requires `affected_quantity` to be at least 1 — a claim asserting zero affected cards is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_QUANTITY_INVALID`. Approval moves the claim to `APPROVED`, and this is the state that makes it visible to the print-run safety floor and to the recall cascade. Denial requires the same `UNDER_REVIEW` status and moves the claim to the terminal `DENIED` state without any of those downstream consequences. Neither branch advances anything on the parent card set beyond a count recompute.

Crediting is the compensation step at the claim level. It requires the claim to be in `APPROVED` status (`ERR_INVALID_STATE`) and does three things at once: the claim moves to the terminal `CREDITED` state; every redemption whose `card_set_id` is this claim's card set and whose status is `FULFILLED` is returned to `ISSUED`; the set's `autograph_redemptions_outstanding` is recomputed; and the set's `version` is advanced by one. A separate guard applies when the platform is asked to reverse a fulfilment through the redemption side: a fulfilment reversal requires that at least one quality claim for the set already be in `APPROVED` status, and a request without one is refused with `ERR_QUALITY_CLAIM_NOT_APPROVED`. Because crediting moves claims out of `APPROVED` and into `CREDITED`, crediting a claim also lowers the set's defect exposure on the next projection — the same set can therefore project a higher quantity before the credit and a lower one after it, on identical demand inputs.

Collector complaints are the secondary-market channel and, unlike quality claims, they are directly consumed by the `SALES_HISTORY` projection authority. A `CollectorSupportSpecialist` files one against a set with a `complaint_label` for display, a `breach_reason` classified as either `UNDISCLOSED_PRINT_RUN` or `NO_REPLACEMENT_POLICY`, and a `claim_quantity` asserting how many cards the collector says were affected. Filing takes no `filed_on` input: the complaint's `filed_on` is stamped from the logical clock at the moment of creation, and it is that instant which the `SALES_HISTORY` projection later reads against its back-date window. A new complaint opens in `OPEN` status. Starting review requires exactly that status (`ERR_INVALID_STATE`) and moves it to `UNDER_REVIEW`.

Approval of a complaint tests the asserted breach against the set's actual state, and the two breach reasons are tested completely differently. A complaint alleging `UNDISCLOSED_PRINT_RUN` is approved only if the card set's compound print-run status is currently `UNDISCLOSED` — the complaint is refused with `ERR_UNDISCLOSED_RUN_DECLARED` when the set's run was in fact `DISCLOSED`. A complaint alleging `NO_REPLACEMENT_POLICY` is approved only if the set carries no quality claims at all, `quality_claim_count` being zero — a set that already has claims on record plainly does have a replacement policy in effect, and the complaint is refused with `ERR_REPLACEMENT_POLICY_EXISTS`. Both branches independently require `claim_quantity` to be at least 1, and a complaint asserting fewer is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_QUANTITY_INVALID` regardless of which breach reason it names. Approval moves the complaint to `APPROVED` and recomputes `settled_complaint_count` on the parent set — note that the counter tracks approved complaints while its name refers to settlement, and the settlement transition itself does not recompute it. Rejection requires the `UNDER_REVIEW` status and moves the complaint to the terminal `REJECTED` state. Settlement requires the `APPROVED` status (`ERR_INVALID_STATE`) and moves the complaint to the terminal `SETTLED` state. Approval of a complaint is what makes it countable in the back-date window that a `SALES_HISTORY` print-run projection reads, so the complaint lifecycle and the quantity arithmetic are joined at exactly this point.

### 3.8. Distribution: Channel Allocation, Capacity Conservation and the Recall Cascade

A finished line has to be placed, and placement is a conserved quantity. A `DistributionManager` reserves cases of a line for a channel by naming the `product_line_id`, declaring the `channel` — one of `HOBBY`, `MASS` or `DIRECT` — stating the number of cases in `quantity_allocated`, and recording the per-case realized price for that channel in `effective_price_cents`. A new allocation opens in `RESERVED` with `shipped_quantity` of 0, reflecting that nothing has physically moved yet.

Before the reservation is written, the platform performs a capacity pre-check, and it is conservative in a specific way: it counts every allocation for the line regardless of status, including allocations that have been recalled and allocations that are still merely reserved. The arithmetic is this. The projected case total is the sum of `quantity_allocated` across every existing allocation for this line other than the one under check, plus the case count this request itself is asking for. The line's capacity is its `cases_printed` multiplied by its `sets_per_case`, and because each case corresponds to one `CASES_PER_SET` unit of that capacity, that unit being a single set per case, the projected case total is multiplied by `sets_per_case` to obtain a projected number of sets. If that projected number of sets is strictly greater than the capacity, the reservation is refused with `ERR_ALLOCATION_EXCEEDS_PRINT_RUN`. The comparison is deliberately strict: an allocation that lands exactly on the capacity is accepted, and only one that overshoots it is refused. Because the check runs before the write, a refused allocation never becomes part of any subsequent allocation's other-cases sum, so the conserved channel total is restored to exactly the value it held before the refused call.

The capacity that the channel arithmetic is measured against is itself a derived quantity rather than a stored one, and its derivation order is a pinned contract. The line's producible card total — the field the platform exposes as `allocated_cards` — is obtained by snapping each of the line's card sets to that set's *own* tier quantum first, and only then summing the snapped per-set quantities into the line total. Snapping the summed total once, against a single quantum, produces a lower and incorrect number for the same inputs. The difference is not hypothetical: for a line carrying three `BASE` sets of 1234 cards each, the correct per-set-then-sum result is 1500 plus 1500 plus 1500, which is 4500, whereas snapping the summed 3702 once against the `BASE` quantum of 500 yields 4000. The per-set evaluation is required, so the rule performs one ceiling division per card set and never one division across the line. Every card set under the line participates in this total regardless of its status, because the line's producible total is what each set *will* run; a set whose quantity is 0 contributes 0. The total is recomputed whenever a card set's print run or tier changes.

Marking an allocation unable-to-fill is a deliberate admission that the channel cannot take the stock it reserved. It requires the parent line to have already reached `PACKED_OUT`, and an attempt before that is refused with `ERR_PRODUCT_LINE_NOT_PACKED_OUT`. It also enforces one unable-to-fill allocation per channel per line: if another allocation for the same product line and the same channel is already in `UTF` status, the move is refused with `ERR_CHANNEL_ALREADY_UTF`. Passing both, the allocation moves to `UTF` and its `version` advances.

Shipping is gated on the calendar, not on demand. It is available from `UTF` and only then (`ERR_INVALID_STATE`), so a reserved allocation must be marked unable-to-fill before it can ship. The clock must be at or after the parent line's `run_planned_on`, and an attempt before the run date is refused with `ERR_DISTRIBUTION_WINDOW_CLOSED`. The allocation must also still fit within the run: its `quantity_allocated` must not exceed the parent line's `cases_printed`, and exceeding it is refused with `ERR_ALLOCATION_EXCEEDS_PRINT_RUN`. Shipping sets the status to `SHIPPED`, records `shipped_quantity` as exactly the allocated quantity, advances the allocation's `version`, advances the parent product line's `version`, and recomputes that line's `sets_per_case`.

Recall is the platform's most destructive operation, and it is here that the quality-claim evidence from §3.7 comes due. It is available from `SHIPPED` and only then (`ERR_INVALID_STATE`). The allocation itself moves to the terminal `RECALLED` state with `shipped_quantity` cleared to 0, and its `version` advances. The line-level cascade is then driven by a loop rather than a single test: the platform returns the parent product line to `PLANNING` at least once, whether or not any approved defect exposure remains, and, while the total `affected_quantity` summed over the quality claims for that line's card sets that are in `APPROVED` status exceeds the platform's `QUALITY_RECALL_THRESHOLD`, which is 0, performs a further such reset — the comparison being a strict greater-than, any approved claim at all extends the cascade. The first pass is unconditional, so a recall with no approved claims still returns its line to `PLANNING`; because nothing in the call writes a claim, the exposure total is fixed and the line ends the call in `PLANNING` in every case. The comparison is a strict greater-than, so a total that lands exactly on the threshold does not extend the cascade, and each pass of the loop drives exactly one further recursion. Each recursion of the loop also supersedes every print-run decision belonging to the line's card sets and returns those card sets' print-run status to the `UNDECIDED` value, so a recalled line loses not just its schedule but its frozen quantities. The line's `version` advances by one, and the call returns the line rather than the allocation, because the line is what has materially changed. Note that the recall loop reads the *sum* of affected quantities rather than the severity-weighted `claim_weight` values, and that only `APPROVED` claims contribute — a claim that has been credited or denied stops feeding the loop.

### 3.9. The Validation Cascade and the Ordering of Approval Checks

Every mutating request passes through the same ordered validation cascade before its business effect is applied and before any side effect lands. The order is a contract, because the same request can fail more than one rule and the identifier a client receives must be the one belonging to the highest-priority failure.

Authorization is checked first: the presence of both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role`, and the recognition of the declared role (`ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`), with a role that is recognised but not permitted to perform the operation refused separately (`ERR_FORBIDDEN`). A malformed request — an unparseable body, a value of the wrong JSON type, or a required input field missing entirely — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` and is likewise settled before business rules are evaluated.

Licensing authority is the next gate, and it deliberately precedes whether the resource being operated on even exists, because a resource produced outside its license is void whether or not the id was right. The in-force test described in §2.7 is evaluated before resource resolution, and the continuous-activeness window test that applies to card-set approval is evaluated immediately after it. Only then does the cascade resolve identifiers: first the id named in the path (returning that entity's not-found identifier for an entity-addressed operation, or the path-segment entity's identifier for a collection endpoint), and then any foreign key carried in the request body (`ERR_REFERENCE_NOT_FOUND`).

With identity and authority established, the cascade moves through resource state. The rights pin is re-checked before the transition's own state precondition, so a card set whose pinned rights have moved reports the rights mismatch rather than a state complaint. The state precondition — that the resource occupy the source state its transition requires — is evaluated next, and its identifier is `ERR_INVALID_STATE` for most transitions, with the more specific state identifiers used where the platform defines them: a signing-session allocation line naming a set that is not approved reports `ERR_CARD_SET_NOT_APPROVED`; a pack configuration or print-run decision acted on while its set is not approved reports `ERR_SET_NOT_APPROVED`; card-set submission against a line that is not ready reports `ERR_PRODUCT_LINE_NOT_READY`; a card-set or decision transition against a line in a forbidden status reports `ERR_PRODUCT_LINE_STATE_BLOCKED`; packout or unable-to-fill marking before the line is packed out reports `ERR_PRODUCT_LINE_NOT_PACKED_OUT`; production start on a set whose run is unlocked reports `ERR_PRINT_RUN_NOT_LOCKED`; line production start with no locked run anywhere under it reports `ERR_NO_LOCKED_PRINT_RUN`; submission with no approved artwork reports `ERR_ARTWORK_NOT_APPROVED`; submission with artwork still pending reports `ERR_ARTWORK_APPROVAL_PENDING`; packout or line re-approval without a published pack configuration reports `ERR_PACK_CONFIG_MISSING`; and a fulfilment reversal without an approved quality claim reports `ERR_QUALITY_CLAIM_NOT_APPROVED`.

Collector-facing safety and truth-in-advertising checks come next, ahead of the internal financial windows, because they guard the product the collector actually receives. The back-dating window on claim review (`ERR_CLAIM_BACKDATE_EXCEEDED`) and the licensor's artwork decision SLA (`ERR_APPROVAL_WINDOW_CLOSED`) are evaluated in this band, followed by the print-run decision's validity window (`ERR_PRINT_DECISION_WINDOW_CLOSED`) and, immediately after it, the equality of the decision's declared quantity to the recomputed final quantity (`ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`).

The remaining business constraints follow in a fixed sequence: the physical-production check at the signing (that the allocated player's produced card count covers what the line delivers, `ERR_CARD_STOCK_NOT_PRODUCED_AT_SIGNING`) and the autograph plan check (`ERR_AUTOGRAPH_ALLOCATION_EXCEEDS_PLAN`) come first; then channel capacity (`ERR_ALLOCATION_EXCEEDS_PRINT_RUN`), which is the last of the fiscal band and applies both to reserving an allocation, to shipping one, and to starting line production; then the signer-availability overlap rule (`ERR_SIGNER_DOUBLE_BOOKED`); then the pack-configuration ladder (`ERR_CONFIG_LADDER_VIOLATION`) and the pack-composition total (`ERR_PACK_CONFIG_TOTAL_MISMATCH`); and finally the consumer deadline that governs redemption closure and notice settlement (`ERR_REDEMPTION_DEADLINE_EXCEEDED`). The relative order within this band matters when a single request could satisfy more than one failure condition, and the ordering above is the one the platform enforces.

Three operations deserve to be called out individually because their internal check sequences are themselves ordered.

Card-set approval is the platform's most gated single act. It first requires the client's `expected_card_set_version` to match the card set's current `version`, and a divergence is a stale-version rejection carrying `ERR_STALE_VERSION` per §2.8 — this is the check that keeps a client from approving a set that another writer has just returned to draft or attached new artwork to. It then requires the license behind the parent product line to be in force (`ERR_LICENSE_NOT_IN_FORCE`) and to have been continuously `ACTIVE` across the whole span from the artwork's `submitted_on` to now (`ERR_LICENSE_TERM_WINDOW_BREACH`), then requires the set's `rights_pinned_version` to equal the license's current `rights_version` (`ERR_RIGHTS_VERSION_MISMATCH`) — this pin test binds only a set that already carries a non-zero pin, since a set that has never been approved holds 0 and this same approval is what captures the license's current `rights_version` into it — and finally applies the tier rule: a `BASE` set with a `print_run_quantity` below 1 is refused with `ERR_PRINT_RUN_REQUIRED`, while an `INSERT` or `PREMIUM` set carrying a non-zero `print_run_quantity` is refused with `ERR_PRINT_RUN_NOT_ALLOWED`. The order matters most for the last pair, because they are mutually exclusive branches keyed on the very same tier field.

Print-run decision approval carries its own ordered sequence, and it is the one place where the platform both recomputes a projection and tests an assertion about it. The decision's card set must be `APPROVED` (`ERR_INVALID_STATE`); the supplied `expected_card_set_version` must match the card set's `version` (`ERR_STALE_VERSION`); the authority's freshness precondition must hold — the qualifying-complaint count for `SALES_HISTORY` (`ERR_INSUFFICIENT_SALES_HISTORY`) or the presence of a `HOBBY`- or `MASS`-channel allocation for `MARKET_DEMAND` (`ERR_MARKET_SIGNAL_UNAVAILABLE`); the supplied `expected_product_line_version` must equal the product line's current `version` (`ERR_AUTHORITY_OUTDATED`); the recomputed `final_quantity` must equal the decision's `declared_quantity` (`ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`); and the clock must be at or before `proposed_on` plus the platform's `PRINT_DECISION_VALIDITY_DAYS` window (`ERR_PRINT_DECISION_WINDOW_CLOSED`). Only when all six hold does the freeze described in §3.3 land.

The clock-driving capability is the third operation with behaviour that is not obvious from its input. It holds no business exclusives beyond itself, it writes no business field, and yet advancing the clock is what executes the artwork-expiry sweep of §3.2: any artwork approval still in `SUBMITTED` whose deadline has been passed by the new clock value is expired as a consequence of the call. Setting the clock is therefore never a purely observational act.
