# Crane Rigging Lift API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Heavy-lift contracting is a business in which a single arithmetic mistake is a fatality. A crane that is charted for 180,000 lb at a 40-foot radius is charted for far less at 60 feet; a sling rated for 12,000 lb carries twice its share of the load when its legs hang at thirty degrees off horizontal; and a machine whose outriggers sink into unconfirmed soil takes the load, the operator and the customer's building down with it. The operators who run this work — dispatch desks, lift directors, licensed professional engineers, certified operators, safety managers and billing clerks — currently reconcile load charts, certification expiries, permit lead times, gear inspection logs and hourly billing across spreadsheets, PDFs and phone calls. This document specifies the API for a system that replaces that reconciliation with an enforced, auditable workflow.

The product's governing idea is that a lift plan is not a document but a **state machine with two independent dimensions**. One dimension tracks readiness — has the ground been surveyed, has a machine been configured against its load chart, has an engineer stamped the analysis, has a permit been granted, has a lift director released the job. The other tracks what is physically happening in the field — mobilized, lifting, held for wind, secured, demobilized. The two dimensions advance separately, and the system's most important guarantee is that a readiness regression (a crane pulled out of service, a sling that failed inspection) reaches back into every plan, permit, review and invoice that depended on it, but only where the crew has not yet rolled onto site.

Money is the second thing this system conserves. Crane work is billed either portal-to-portal against an hourly rate with overtime, mobilization and mileage, or as a lump sum that absorbs mobilization and grants a short free standby allowance. The same job data therefore yields two legally different bills, and the difference is not cosmetic — it changes which volume discount tier applies and what fuel surcharge is assessed. Every invoice line is rounded independently before it is summed, every discount is keyed on the pre-surcharge subtotal, and a customer's open receivable is a conserved ledger: issuing an invoice and then voiding it must leave the account balance exactly where it started.

Everything in this API is mounted flat — every collection is a top-level path segment under the server root, with no nested resource trees except the two read-only reverse lookups that hang off a crane and off a gear item.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Crew Identity and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint — including reads and including the clock-driving capability — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role`. If either header is absent or empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). The same code and status are returned when `X-User-Role` carries a value outside the seven roles the system recognizes, which are, spelled exactly: `Dispatcher`, `LiftDirector`, `Engineer`, `Operator`, `SafetyManager`, `BillingClerk`, and `Customer`. Once the role is recognized, it is checked against the list of roles authorized for the endpoint being called (see the table in §4); a role that does not appear there is refused with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (HTTP 403).

The seven roles divide the work by segregation of duties. A `Dispatcher` runs the setup and scheduling surface — registering customers, sites, cranes, load-chart rows and personnel, opening lift plans, recording site surveys, configuring machines, committing and withdrawing rigging gear, and filing permit paperwork. A `LiftDirector` signs off standard lifts and releases every plan to the field, but may not configure a plan, may not stamp an engineering analysis, and may not inspect gear. An `Engineer` is a licensed professional engineer who authors critical-lift analyses and decides on *other* engineers' analyses. An `Operator` is a certified crane operator who drives the operational dimension of the one plan they are assigned to and nothing else. A `SafetyManager` owns gear integrity and weather holds: registering gear, recording inspections and retirements, opening and closing standby windows, and pulling cranes out of service or returning them. A `BillingClerk` owns the money surface: credit holds, invoice drafting, issuance, voiding and payment. A `Customer` is the paying account and may read only what belongs to it, plus dispute an invoice that has been issued to it.

Three of the seven roles are **entity-backed**, meaning `X-User-ID` names a real stored row and ownership checks compare that value against a foreign key on the resource being touched:

- When `X-User-Role` is `Engineer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Personnel` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Personnel` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, an engineer requesting changes on, stamping, or rejecting a review must have an `X-User-ID` different from that review's `author_personnel_id`, and an engineer resubmitting a review must have an `X-User-ID` equal to it. Either violation is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).
- When `X-User-Role` is `Operator`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Personnel` row; driving the operational dimension of a lift plan requires `X-User-ID` to equal that plan's `operator_id`, and any other operator attempting it is refused with `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_OPERATOR` (HTTP 403).
- When `X-User-Role` is `Customer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Customer` row; reading a customer record, reading an invoice, or disputing an invoice all require the target's `customer_id` (or, for the customer record itself, its own `id`) to equal `X-User-ID`. A mismatch is `ERR_CUSTOMER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (HTTP 403).

The other four roles are **opaque**. When `X-User-Role` is `Dispatcher`, `LiftDirector`, `SafetyManager`, or `BillingClerk`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may accept and log it, but it never compares it against any entity's `id`, and no ownership check keys on it.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint is paginated through two optional query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` outside the 1-to-100 band or a negative `offset` is a request-shape failure and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400).

List results are always ordered by `created_at` ascending unless a specific listing states otherwise. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`, where `total_count` is the number of rows matching the filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` is true when rows remain beyond the current page. Single-resource reads carry no pagination fields in `meta`.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties and the Logical Clock

Every stored resource carries an `id` that is a server-minted UUID. Identifiers are bare UUIDs with no type prefix, no embedded entity name, and no client-supplied component; a client that supplies its own `id` has it ignored. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with exactly three fractional-second digits, recorded at the instant the row is first persisted and never changed afterwards.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at` — same ISO 8601 UTC format with three fractional digits, refreshed on every mutation of that row — and `version`, an integer that begins at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. Two resource kinds are deliberately excluded from that pair, because both are append-only records of a moment rather than living objects: a load-chart row and a gear-inspection record carry only `id` and `created_at`. Neither has an `updated_at` and neither has a `version`, and no endpoint exists that would update or delete either of them.

The server keeps a **logical UTC clock** that is the sole time source for every expiry, lead-time, window and inspection-due comparison in this specification. It starts at exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` when the server boots and never falls back to wall-clock time. An operational test-driving capability moves it: the caller supplies `to_iso8601`, the new instant, and the response body reports the resulting `current_utc`. Moving the clock is not a passive act — it re-derives every person's certification posture against their `certification_expires_on`, moves any serviceable gear item to a due-for-inspection posture once the clock reaches its `next_inspection_due_at`, and pulls any crane out of service once the clock reaches its `annual_inspection_expires_on`.

### 2.5. Stale-View Protection on Configuration, Commitment and Billing

Three write operations can silently destroy work that the caller never observed, and all three are therefore guarded by optimistic concurrency control keyed on the parent resource's `version`. There is no other concurrency mechanism in this system, and no endpoint outside these three accepts a version field.

Configuring a lift plan must carry `expected_lift_plan_version` as a required, non-optional field whose value is the plan's `version` as the client last read it. A configure legitimately supersedes the plan's entire prior configuration — releasing the previously reserved crane and superseding any engineering review in flight — so a caller working from a stale view would discard a configuration it never saw. If `expected_lift_plan_version` does not equal the plan's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PLAN_VERSION` and HTTP status 412.

Creating an invoice likewise requires `expected_lift_plan_version` matching the target lift plan's current `version`. A newly created invoice voids any prior draft invoice on the same plan, so a caller holding a stale view would retire a bill it never observed. A mismatch is again `ERR_STALE_PLAN_VERSION` with HTTP status 412.

Committing a piece of rigging gear to a plan requires `expected_gear_version` matching that gear item's current `version`. The gear's derate, its inspection posture and its commitment ledger all move its `version`, and the assignment permanently snapshots the gear's working load limit at commit time — so a stale writer would freeze an obsolete limit onto the plan. A mismatch is `ERR_STALE_GEAR_VERSION` with HTTP status 412.

### 2.6. Measurement Precision and Rounding Discipline

Rounding in this domain is not a formatting concern; it is part of the contract, and "round half up" is the only rounding rule used anywhere in the system unless a specific figure is explicitly described as a ceiling or a floor.

Percentages and hour ledgers are carried to **two decimal places, rounded half up**: this covers a plan's utilization percentage, its per-outrigger load and bearing pressure, a crane's total reserved hours, and a person's peak daily assigned hours. A standby event's raw duration and the merged standby total are carried to **four decimal places, rounded half up**, before the billable figure is rounded *up* to the next quarter hour. Weights and tensions are whole pounds: a sling leg tension is rounded half up to the nearest whole pound, while a gear item's derated working load limit is **floored** to a whole pound. Mat area and mat count are integer **ceilings**. A crane's allowable wind speed is **floored** to a whole mile per hour. Every monetary figure in the system is an integer count of cents; each derived cent figure is rounded half up at the point the formula names, and no monetary field is ever carried as a fractional value.

One consequence deserves stating outright: wherever a threshold is compared against a rounded figure, the comparison uses the **already-rounded** value, never the raw quotient behind it.
### 2.7. The Order of Rejection

When a request violates more than one rule, the response is never ambiguous: checks run in a strict ascending priority order, and the **first** rule that fails determines both the `error_id` in the body and the HTTP status. A later, more interesting failure never masks an earlier one, and an earlier failure never yields to a later one because it seems less important. The complete ladder, from first-evaluated to last-evaluated, is:

1. Both `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` are present and non-empty on every request — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. `X-User-Role` names one of `Dispatcher`, `LiftDirector`, `Engineer`, `Operator`, `SafetyManager`, `BillingClerk`, `Customer` — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). This too applies to every request.
3. The supplied role appears among the roles authorized for the endpoint being called — otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403). Applies to every request.
4. Every identifier embedded in the URL path resolves to a stored row, checked left to right. Depending on which segment fails, the code is one of `ERR_CUSTOMER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SITE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CRANE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PERSONNEL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_GEAR_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LIFT_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_GEAR_ASSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PERMIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_STANDBY_EVENT_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_FOUND`, always with HTTP 404. Applies to every endpoint carrying a path identifier.
5. Request-body shape: every required field is present, every value has the correct type, every enum value is a legal member, `leg_count` lies between 1 and 4 inclusive, `sling_angle_deg` is one of 30, 45, 60 or 90, all hour and mile quantities are at least 0, any supplied soil bearing capacity is strictly greater than 0, `parts_of_line` is at least 1, and pagination `limit` lies within 1 to 100 with `offset` at least 0. Any violation is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). This rule applies to every POST endpoint and to the query parameters of every list endpoint.
6. Every foreign key supplied in the request body resolves to a stored row, yielding `ERR_CUSTOMER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SITE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CRANE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PERSONNEL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_GEAR_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_LIFT_PLAN_NOT_FOUND` at HTTP 404. This rule guards exactly these ten operations: `POST /sites`, `POST /load-chart-entries`, `POST /gear-inspections`, `POST /lift-plans`, `POST /lift-plans/{id}/configure`, `POST /gear-assignments`, `POST /engineering-reviews`, `POST /permits`, `POST /standby-events` and `POST /invoices`.
7. Entity-backed identity and segregation of duties, as described in §2.1, producing `ERR_CUSTOMER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`, `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_OPERATOR` or `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` at HTTP 403. This rule guards exactly six operations: `GET /customers/{id}`, `GET /invoices/{id}`, `POST /invoices/{id}/dispute`, `POST /lift-plans/{id}/field-status`, `POST /engineering-reviews/{id}/decision` and `POST /engineering-reviews/{id}/resubmit`.
8. The optimistic-concurrency check of §2.5, producing `ERR_STALE_PLAN_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_GEAR_VERSION` at HTTP 412, on exactly `POST /lift-plans/{id}/configure`, `POST /invoices` and `POST /gear-assignments`.
9. The target resource's own current state is a legal starting state for the requested transition, producing `ERR_INVALID_PLAN_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_FIELD_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_GEAR_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_REVIEW_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_PERMIT_STATE` or `ERR_INVALID_INVOICE_STATE` at HTTP 409. Applies to every state-changing endpoint.
10. Cross-entity state gates at HTTP 409: the assigned crane is not out of service (`ERR_CRANE_OUT_OF_SERVICE`), every active gear commitment on the plan references gear in a serviceable posture and gear may only be committed while it is serviceable (`ERR_GEAR_NOT_SERVICEABLE`), a plan that requires a permit holds an approved one (`ERR_PERMIT_NOT_APPROVED`), and a review may only be stamped while its plan is awaiting engineering (`ERR_PLAN_NOT_PENDING_ENGINEERING`). This rule guards exactly five operations: `POST /lift-plans/{id}/configure`, `POST /lift-plans/{id}/release`, `POST /lift-plans/{id}/field-status`, `POST /gear-assignments` and `POST /engineering-reviews/{id}/decision`.
11. Personnel discriminator match — `operator_id` references a person whose `role_type` is `OPERATOR`, `rigger_id` references one whose `role_type` is `RIGGER`, and `author_personnel_id` references one whose `role_type` is `ENGINEER` — otherwise `ERR_PERSONNEL_ROLE_MISMATCH` (422). Guards `POST /lift-plans/{id}/configure` and `POST /engineering-reviews`.
12. Certification and qualification at release, at HTTP 422: `ERR_OPERATOR_CERT_EXPIRED`, `ERR_OPERATOR_ENDORSEMENT_INSUFFICIENT` and `ERR_RIGGER_QUALIFICATION_INSUFFICIENT`. Guards `POST /lift-plans/{id}/release` only.
13. Rigging and capacity at configure, at HTTP 422: `ERR_NO_RIGGING_ASSIGNED`, `ERR_LOAD_CHART_NO_ROW`, `ERR_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` and `ERR_SLING_WLL_EXCEEDED`. Guards `POST /lift-plans/{id}/configure` only.
14. A standby event whose `cause` is `WIND` is only accepted when its reported wind speed is at or above the plan's allowable wind; otherwise `ERR_WIND_BELOW_HOLD_THRESHOLD` (422). Guards `POST /standby-events` only. Causes other than `WIND` skip this check entirely.
15. Crane effective-window exclusivity, producing `ERR_CRANE_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409) on `POST /lift-plans/{id}/configure`.
16. The operator daily hour cap, producing `ERR_OPERATOR_HOURS_EXCEEDED` (409) on `POST /lift-plans/{id}/configure`.
17. Uniqueness constraints at HTTP 409: `ERR_DUPLICATE_GEAR_TAG` on `POST /rigging-gear`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_CHART_ROW` on `POST /load-chart-entries`, and `ERR_DUPLICATE_GEAR_ASSIGNMENT` on `POST /gear-assignments`.
18. A permit's `jurisdiction_code` equals the `jurisdiction_code` of the site owning the permit's lift plan, otherwise `ERR_JURISDICTION_MISMATCH` (422). Guards `POST /permits/{id}/submit`.
19. Permit lead time, otherwise `ERR_PERMIT_LEAD_TIME_SHORT` (422). Guards `POST /permits/{id}/submit`. Note that jurisdiction is checked *before* lead time, so a filing that is both mis-addressed and too late reports the jurisdiction failure.
20. Lift window sanity, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW` (422). Guards `POST /lift-plans` only.
21. The engineering revision limit, otherwise `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (409). Guards `POST /engineering-reviews/{id}/resubmit` only.
22. The owning customer is not on credit hold, otherwise `ERR_CUSTOMER_CREDIT_HOLD` (402). Guards exactly two operations: `POST /lift-plans/{id}/release` and `POST /invoices/{id}/issue`.
23. A lump-sum invoice carries a positive agreed price, otherwise `ERR_LUMP_SUM_REQUIRED` (422). Guards `POST /invoices` only.

Three consequences of this ordering are worth calling out because they are easy to get backwards. First, the stale-version check at step 8 runs **before** the target's own state check at step 9 — so configuring a plan that is both stale and in an illegal state reports the stale version, not the state. Second, the credit-hold check at step 22 runs **after** every certification, permit and gear gate — so a release that fails both the operator's endorsement and the customer's credit standing reports the endorsement failure. Third, the release-time capacity gates at step 12 run before the scheduling constraints at steps 15 and 16, which in turn run before the fiscal check.

### 2.8. Path Resolution for Reverse Lookups

The topology is flat: `/customers`, `/sites`, `/cranes`, `/load-chart-entries`, `/personnel`, `/rigging-gear`, `/gear-inspections`, `/lift-plans`, `/gear-assignments`, `/engineering-reviews`, `/permits`, `/standby-events` and `/invoices` are all top-level collections. Only two read paths nest a child under a parent — the load-chart listing under a crane and the inspection log under a gear item — and in both cases the parent identifier is resolved first. A request to list the chart rows of a crane that does not exist reports `ERR_CRANE_NOT_FOUND`, and a request to list inspections for a gear item that does not exist reports `ERR_GEAR_NOT_FOUND`; neither returns an empty page. This left-to-right rule is general: wherever a URL carries more than one identifier, the leftmost unresolvable one determines the 404 code.

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline and Conserved Ledgers

A great deal of this system's behaviour is second-order: an action on one resource changes another, which changes a third. Four such chains exist, and each is specified in full in §3 where its trigger lives. The discipline they share is this: **every row a cascade touches has its own `updated_at` refreshed and its own `version` incremented by exactly 1**, even when the caller never named that row. A cascade that demotes four plans bumps four plan versions; a cascade that suspends two permits bumps two permit versions.

Cascades are also **conditional, not blanket**. The recurring guard is the plan's operational dimension: a plan whose crew has not yet mobilized is safe to demote, and a plan whose crew is already on site is deliberately left untouched. Implementations that cascade unconditionally will corrupt live jobs; implementations that never cascade will release unsafe lifts.

Three quantities in this system are **conserved ledgers** — figures that are always recomputed from their source rows rather than incremented in place, so that a compensating action returns them exactly to their prior value. A crane's reserved hours and active plan count are recomputed from the set of plans currently holding that machine, so configuring a plan and then cancelling it must return the crane's reserved hours to the exact figure it held before. A gear item's committed assignment count is recomputed from its active commitments. A customer's open receivable and invoice count are recomputed from that customer's invoice rows, so issuing an invoice and then voiding it must return the open receivable to its exact pre-issue value while leaving the invoice count at its post-creation value. Recomputation, not arithmetic drift, is the contract.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Accounts, Ground, Iron and Crew

Work begins at the dispatch desk, which registers the four kinds of static record every lift depends on. A customer account is opened with a legal `account_name` and, optionally, `billing_terms` chosen from `NET_15`, `NET_30` and `NET_45`; when the terms are omitted the account is opened on `NET_30`. A newly registered account has `credit_hold` false, `credit_hold_reason` null, `open_receivable_cents` at 0 and `invoice_count` at 0. Credit standing is not a dispatch concern — only a `BillingClerk` may toggle it, supplying the new `credit_hold` value and an optional `reason` that is stored as `credit_hold_reason` when the hold is being set. Clearing a hold leaves `credit_hold_reason` null. Accounts can be listed and narrowed by `credit_hold`.

Each account owns one or more sites. Registering a site requires the owning `customer_id`, a human `address_label`, the `jurisdiction_code` of the permitting authority that governs that address (for example a county-level code such as `NJ-BERGEN`), and an initial `soil_bearing_capacity_psf` — the allowable soil bearing pressure in pounds per square foot, which must be strictly greater than zero. Two optional site attributes carry outsized safety weight and are covered in §3.5: `proximity_to_energized_lines_ft`, the distance in feet to the nearest energized power line, which defaults to 999 when unstated and may not be negative; and `over_water`, false unless the site stages a lift over open water. A fresh site has `survey_status` at `NOT_SURVEYED`, `last_surveyed_at` null, `access_notes` null and `mat_inventory_available` false — the last three are written only by a site survey. Sites may be listed filtered by `customer_id` and by `survey_status`.

Registering a crane captures the machine's physical envelope in ten required attributes: a `model_code`, the nominal capacity class `class_tons` in US tons, the machine weight `gross_weight_lb` excluding counterweight, the installed `counterweight_lb`, the bearing area `outrigger_pad_area_sqft` of **one** outrigger float in square feet, the single-part hoist limit `max_line_pull_lb`, the manufacturer's rated in-service wind ceiling `rated_max_wind_mph`, the instant `annual_inspection_expires_on` at which the annual inspection lapses, the flat round-trip charge `mob_demob_cents` covering mobilization plus demobilization, and the bare-rental `hourly_rate_cents`. Every weight, pull and rate must be positive except `counterweight_lb` and `mob_demob_cents`, which may be zero. A newly registered machine has `out_of_service_flag` false, `reserved_hours_total` at 0, `active_plan_count` at 0 and `status` `AVAILABLE`.

A crane's `status` is never written directly; it is **derived on every read and after every mutation**, by a strict precedence. The machine is `OUT_OF_SERVICE` if `out_of_service_flag` is true *or* the logical clock has reached or passed `annual_inspection_expires_on`. Failing that, it is `IN_SERVICE` if any lift plan on it has an operational dimension of `MOBILIZED`, `LIFTING`, `WIND_HOLD` or `SECURED`. Failing that, it is `RESERVED` if `reserved_hours_total` is greater than zero. Otherwise it is `AVAILABLE`. That precedence is what makes the fleet's lifecycle a genuine cycle: `AVAILABLE → RESERVED` the moment a configuration puts reserved hours on the books, `RESERVED → AVAILABLE` when the last reserving plan is cancelled, closed or demoted and the reserved hours fall back to zero, `RESERVED → IN_SERVICE` when a crew mobilizes, and `IN_SERVICE → AVAILABLE` when the last plan on the machine demobilizes with no other plan still mobilized, lifting, held or secured and no reservation hours remaining. Cranes may be listed filtered by their derived `status`.

Pulling a machine out of service is a `SafetyManager` action that takes a `reason` and is legal from either `AVAILABLE` or `RESERVED`. It sets `out_of_service_flag` true and moves `status` to `OUT_OF_SERVICE`, and it launches a three-deep cascade. The second hop reaches every lift plan on that crane whose readiness dimension is `APPROVED` or `RELEASED` **and** whose operational dimension is still `NOT_MOBILIZED`, demoting each of them back to `CONFIGURED`. The third hop reaches every permit on those demoted plans that stood at `APPROVED` and moves it to `SUSPENDED`. Plans whose crew is already mobilized, lifting, on wind hold or secured are deliberately untouched — the cascade only claws back work that has not left the yard. The logical clock reaching a crane's `annual_inspection_expires_on` drives the same `OUT_OF_SERVICE` posture and the same cascade, but without setting `out_of_service_flag`. Returning a machine to service requires `new_annual_inspection_expires_on` strictly after the logical clock; it clears `out_of_service_flag`, writes the fresh date onto `annual_inspection_expires_on`, and then re-derives `status` by the precedence above — so a machine that still holds reservations lands on `RESERVED` rather than `AVAILABLE`.

A crane without a load chart is unusable, so each machine accumulates chart rows. One chart row names its `crane_id`, the `configuration` it was charted for (`MAIN_BOOM`, `LUFFING_JIB` or `FIXED_JIB`), the charted `boom_length_ft`, the charted working `radius_ft`, and the `gross_capacity_lb` the manufacturer publishes at that combination. All four numeric attributes must be positive. Chart rows are immutable once written — there is no update and no delete — and the combination of crane, configuration, boom length and radius must be unique; a duplicate is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CHART_ROW`. The chart for a single machine can be listed and narrowed by `configuration` and by `boom_length_ft`.

Crew records are polymorphic. A person is registered with a `full_name`, a `role_type` discriminator that is one of `OPERATOR`, `RIGGER` or `ENGINEER`, a `certification_number` (an NCCCO, NCCER or PE certificate), and the instant `certification_expires_on` at which that certificate lapses. Three further attributes are meaningful only for one discriminator each and default when omitted: `endorsement_class_tons`, the largest crane class in tons this person may operate, defaulting to 0 and meaningful only for an `OPERATOR`; `rigger_level`, defaulting to `NONE` and meaningful only for a `RIGGER`, drawn from a ladder ordered `NONE`, then `BASIC`, then `INTERMEDIATE`, then `ADVANCED`, then `MASTER`; and `pe_license_state`, defaulting to null and meaningful only for an `ENGINEER`. A person's `status` is derived, not stored: it reads `EXPIRED` once the logical clock has reached or passed `certification_expires_on`, and `ACTIVE` otherwise. Their `peak_daily_assigned_hours` starts at 0 and is maintained by the scheduling rules in §3.4. Recertification is a dispatch action that refreshes `certification_number` and `certification_expires_on` and may also refresh `endorsement_class_tons` and `rigger_level`; the derived `status` is recomputed against the logical clock immediately. Personnel can be listed filtered by `role_type` and by derived `status`.

### 3.2. Rigging Gear Integrity, Derate and Retirement

Rigging gear is the only class of asset in this system that degrades. A `SafetyManager` registers a piece of gear with a `gear_tag` that must be globally unique across the whole inventory — a collision is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_GEAR_TAG` — a `gear_type` drawn from `SLING`, `SHACKLE` and `SPREADER_BAR`, the manufacturer's `rated_wll_lb` working load limit in pounds, the dead `weight_lb` of the gear itself (which may be zero but not negative), and optionally an `inspection_interval_days` cadence that defaults to 30 days when omitted and must be positive. Newly registered gear starts at `DUE_INSPECTION` with `repair_count` at 0, `effective_wll_lb` equal to `rated_wll_lb`, `committed_assignment_count` at 0, and both `last_inspection_at` and `next_inspection_due_at` null. **New gear may not be committed to a plan until it has passed an inspection** — that is the whole point of starting it at `DUE_INSPECTION`. Gear may be listed and narrowed by `status` and by `gear_type`.

An inspection is recorded against a `gear_id` with a `result` drawn from `PASS`, `FAIL`, `REPAIR_PASS` and `RETIRE`, plus a free-text `inspector_note` capturing the finding. The inspection record is append-only: it stamps `inspected_at` with the logical clock, records the `resulting_status` it drove the gear to, and snapshots `repair_count_after` — the gear's repair tally immediately after this inspection was applied. Inspections are legal against gear in any posture except `RETIRED`; targeting retired gear is refused with `ERR_INVALID_GEAR_STATE`. Every inspection, whatever its result, refreshes the gear's `last_inspection_at` to the logical clock, and every inspection that leaves the gear serviceable also recomputes `next_inspection_due_at` as `last_inspection_at` advanced by `inspection_interval_days` days. The inspection log for a single gear item is readable, filterable by `result`, and — unusually for this API — is returned **newest first**.

The four results diverge sharply. A `PASS` moves the gear to `SERVICEABLE`, whether it came from `DUE_INSPECTION`, from `QUARANTINED`, or from `SERVICEABLE` (re-inspecting already-serviceable gear is legal and simply refreshes the cadence). A `REPAIR_PASS` also lands on `SERVICEABLE` from any of those three postures, but it additionally increments `repair_count` by one and therefore permanently lowers the gear's rating. A `FAIL` moves the gear to `QUARANTINED`. A `RETIRE` moves it to `RETIRED`, which is terminal.

The derate is a decay function, recomputed every time `repair_count` moves. The derate multiplier is one minus five hundredths for each recorded repair, but never allowed to fall below seventy hundredths — that is, the greater of the two. The gear's `effective_wll_lb` is then the manufacturer's `rated_wll_lb` multiplied by that multiplier, **floored** to a whole pound. Only `REPAIR_PASS` inspections count toward `repair_count`; a `PASS`, a `FAIL` and a `RETIRE` never move it. Because the derate loses five percent per repair and stops at seventy percent, the floor is reached at the sixth repair and the rating never moves again after that. One consequence matters operationally: a gear commitment snapshots the working load limit at the moment it is made, so a repair recorded afterwards does **not** retroactively lower a commitment that already exists.

Two of the four results launch the system's deepest cascade. When an inspection returns `FAIL` — or when a `SafetyManager` retires a gear item outright with a supplied `reason`, or an inspection returns `RETIRE` — the gear leaves the serviceable set, and everything downstream that depended on it unwinds in four hops. The first hop is the gear's own posture change to `QUARANTINED` or `RETIRED`. The second hop voids every commitment of that gear that was still active, moving each to `VOIDED` and recomputing the gear's `committed_assignment_count`. The third hop reaches every lift plan that owned one of those voided commitments, whose readiness dimension stands at `CONFIGURED`, `PENDING_ENGINEERING`, `APPROVED` or `RELEASED`, and whose operational dimension is still `NOT_MOBILIZED`; each such plan is demoted back to `SURVEYED` with `rigging_verified` cleared to false. A plan still at `DRAFT` or already at `SURVEYED`, `CLOSED` or `CANCELLED` is not a demotion candidate. The fourth hop reaches every engineering review on those demoted plans that stood at `UNDER_REVIEW`, `CHANGES_REQUESTED` or `STAMPED`, and moves each to `SUPERSEDED`. A plan whose crew is already mobilized, lifting, held or secured is left alone at every hop.

Demotion to `SURVEYED` is a full teardown of the plan's engineering state, and it must be complete. The plan's `rigging_verified` goes false; `lift_class` returns to `NOT_CLASSIFIED`; `utilization_pct`, `net_capacity_lb`, `chart_gross_capacity_lb`, `hoist_line_capacity_lb` and `total_load_lb` all return to 0, while `rigging_weight_lb` is recomputed across whatever commitments survived; `bearing_variant` returns to `NONE` with `per_outrigger_load_lb` and `bearing_pressure_psf` at 0, `matting_required` false, and `mat_area_required_sqft` and `mat_count` at 0; and the crew and machine assignments `crane_id`, `operator_id` and `rigger_id` are all cleared to null, which in turn forces a recomputation of the released crane's `reserved_hours_total`, `active_plan_count` and derived `status`. A gear item may also fall out of the serviceable set with no cascade at all: when the logical clock reaches its `next_inspection_due_at`, a `SERVICEABLE` item quietly returns to `DUE_INSPECTION`, which stops it being assignable and stops it satisfying the release gate, but voids nothing.

### 3.3. Opening a Lift Plan and Confirming the Ground

A lift plan is opened by a `Dispatcher` against an existing site. The payload names the `site_id`, a customer-facing `job_reference`, the on-site crane window as `window_start` and `window_end`, and the `load_weight_lb` — the weight of the load itself in pounds, excluding rigging, which must be positive. Four further inputs are optional and each defaults: `sail_area_sqft`, the projected wind sail area of the load in square feet, defaulting to 0 and never negative; `personnel_lift`, false unless the pick suspends people; `multi_crane`, false unless this is a tandem or multi-crane pick; and `road_closure_requested`, false unless the job needs a lane or road closure. The plan's `customer_id` is not supplied by the caller — it is copied from the owning site's `customer_id` at creation and never changes afterwards, even if the site is later re-pointed.

Window sanity is enforced at creation: `window_end` must be strictly after `window_start`, and `window_start` must be at or after the logical clock. A window that fails either test is refused with `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`. A newly opened plan sits at `DRAFT` on its readiness dimension and `NOT_MOBILIZED` on its operational dimension, with `lift_class` at `NOT_CLASSIFIED`, `bearing_variant` at `NONE`, and `crane_id`, `operator_id` and `rigger_id` all null. Plans are listable and can be narrowed by `site_id`, `customer_id`, `crane_id`, `operator_id`, `rigger_id`, `plan_status` and `field_status`.

Nothing may be configured until somebody has stood on the ground. The site survey is a dispatch action that advances the plan from `DRAFT` to `SURVEYED`, and it is the only route out of `DRAFT` other than cancellation. It carries three required inputs: `confirmed_soil_bearing_capacity_psf`, the allowable bearing pressure the surveyor actually measured, which overwrites the site's stored `soil_bearing_capacity_psf`; `access_notes`, recorded onto the site; and `mat_inventory_available`, a boolean recording whether crane mats are already stocked at that address, likewise recorded onto the site. The survey also flips the site's `survey_status` to `SURVEYED` — the first survey against any plan on that site does it, and subsequent surveys leave it there — and stamps the site's `last_surveyed_at` with the logical clock. Because the survey writes onto the plan as well as the site, the plan's `version` increments by one.

### 3.4. Committing Rigging, Configuring the Machine and Holding the Schedule

Before a machine can be configured, gear must be committed to the plan. A `Dispatcher` commits one item at a time, naming the `lift_plan_id`, the `gear_id`, the `leg_count` — how many sling legs share the load, between 1 and 4 inclusive — and the `sling_angle_deg`, the horizontal sling angle, which is accepted only at the four tabulated values 30, 45, 60 and 90. The commitment also carries `expected_gear_version` per §2.5. Gear may only be committed while its posture is `SERVICEABLE`; committing gear that is due for inspection, quarantined or retired is refused with `ERR_GEAR_NOT_SERVICEABLE`. A plan may hold at most one active commitment of any given gear item at a time, and a second attempt is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_GEAR_ASSIGNMENT`.

A new commitment is created `ACTIVE`. It permanently snapshots the gear's current derated limit into its own `effective_wll_lb`, resolves its `load_angle_factor` from the sling angle, and leaves `leg_tension_lb` at 0 until the owning plan is next configured. The angle factor table is fixed and has exactly four entries: an angle of 90 degrees gives a factor of 1.000, 60 degrees gives 1.155, 45 degrees gives 1.414, and 30 degrees gives 2.000 — a shallow sling doubles what each leg carries. Creating the commitment recomputes the gear item's `committed_assignment_count` and increments that gear item's `version` by one. Commitments can be listed and narrowed by `lift_plan_id`, `gear_id` and `status`.

Withdrawing a commitment moves it from `ACTIVE` to `RELEASED` and recomputes the gear's `committed_assignment_count`; withdrawing anything that is not currently `ACTIVE` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_GEAR_STATE`. Withdrawal deliberately does **not** recompute the owning plan's `rigging_weight_lb` — the plan keeps its stale weight until the next configure recomputes it from scratch. A commitment may also be moved to `VOIDED`, but only by the system, and only through the gear-integrity cascade of §3.2.

Configuring the plan is where the engineering actually happens. The `Dispatcher` supplies the `crane_id` to be used, the `operator_id` and `rigger_id` who will run the pick, the boom `configuration` to be flown, the `boom_length_ft` and working `radius_ft` for the pick, the `parts_of_line` reeved on the hoist (at least 1), and `expected_lift_plan_version`. Three optional deduction inputs each default to 0 when omitted: `block_weight_lb` for the hook block, `jib_weight_lb` for an erected jib, and `aux_head_weight_lb` for an auxiliary head. Configuration is legal while the plan's readiness dimension is `SURVEYED`, `CONFIGURED` or `PENDING_ENGINEERING` — re-configuring an already-configured plan is expressly permitted and supersedes everything the previous configuration established. The assigned crane may not be `OUT_OF_SERVICE` (`ERR_CRANE_OUT_OF_SERVICE`), and the two crew references must match their discriminators: `operator_id` must name a person whose `role_type` is `OPERATOR` and `rigger_id` must name one whose `role_type` is `RIGGER`, or the request is refused with `ERR_PERSONNEL_ROLE_MISMATCH`.

The capacity calculation proceeds in four steps, and the order matters because each step's failure has its own code. **Step one is the load-chart lookup**, which is deliberately conservative. The candidate rows are those belonging to the chosen crane, charted for the chosen `configuration`, whose `boom_length_ft` matches the requested boom length **exactly** — there is no interpolation between charted boom lengths — and whose charted `radius_ft` is greater than or equal to the requested radius. Among those candidates the system takes the one with the **smallest** `radius_ft`, which is the next charted radius at or beyond where the load actually sits; should two candidates somehow tie on radius, the earlier `created_at` wins. If no candidate exists at all, the configure fails with `ERR_LOAD_CHART_NO_ROW`. The chosen row's `gross_capacity_lb` becomes the plan's `chart_gross_capacity_lb`.

**Step two is the nested capacity ceiling.** The reeving imposes its own limit: the plan's `hoist_line_capacity_lb` is the crane's `max_line_pull_lb` multiplied by `parts_of_line`. The governing gross figure is the smaller of `chart_gross_capacity_lb` and `hoist_line_capacity_lb`. From that smaller figure the system subtracts the sum of the three deductions `block_weight_lb`, `jib_weight_lb` and `aux_head_weight_lb` to arrive at `net_capacity_lb`. If `net_capacity_lb` is zero or negative, the configure fails with `ERR_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.

**Step three is the load side.** The plan's `rigging_weight_lb` is the sum of the `weight_lb` of the gear behind every commitment on this plan whose status is `ACTIVE`; commitments that are `RELEASED` or `VOIDED` contribute neither weight nor a tension check. If there is not a single active commitment, the configure fails with `ERR_NO_RIGGING_ASSIGNED`. The plan's `total_load_lb` is its `load_weight_lb` plus that `rigging_weight_lb`.

**Step four is utilization.** The plan's `utilization_pct` is `total_load_lb` divided by `net_capacity_lb`, multiplied by 100, rounded half up to two decimal places. A utilization above 100.00 percent fails with `ERR_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`; exactly 100.00 percent is permitted.

Sling tension is checked alongside, once per active commitment. Each commitment's `leg_tension_lb` is the plan's `total_load_lb` divided by that commitment's `leg_count`, multiplied by its `load_angle_factor`, rounded half up to a whole pound. If any single commitment's resulting `leg_tension_lb` is **strictly greater** than that commitment's snapshotted `effective_wll_lb`, the whole configure fails with `ERR_SLING_WLL_EXCEEDED` — the check is aggregate, one violating leg condemns the entire request, and **no field on the plan is persisted** when it fails. When every leg passes, the plan's `rigging_verified` becomes true, which it remains only while the plan holds at least one active commitment and every one of them passed this check at the most recent configure.

With capacity settled, the configure classifies the pick into its `lift_class`. A baseline is taken from utilization alone: a plan whose `utilization_pct` is at or above 90.00 percent is baselined `ENGINEERED_CRITICAL`; failing that, one at or above 75.00 percent is baselined `CRITICAL`; anything below 75.00 percent is baselined `STANDARD`. The comparison uses the already-rounded `utilization_pct`, so a raw quotient of 74.996 percent rounds to 75.00 and **is** critical, while 74.99 percent is standard. Two categories of override then apply. Three conditions force `ENGINEERED_CRITICAL` outright regardless of the baseline: the plan's `personnel_lift` being true, the plan's `multi_crane` being true, or the site's `over_water` being true. A fourth condition — the site's `proximity_to_energized_lines_ft` being strictly less than 20 feet — is weaker: it raises the class to `CRITICAL` only if the baseline was lower, and leaves an `ENGINEERED_CRITICAL` baseline alone. In rank order, `NOT_CLASSIFIED` sits below `STANDARD`, which sits below `CRITICAL`, which sits below `ENGINEERED_CRITICAL`, and the proximity override takes whichever of the baseline and `CRITICAL` ranks higher. The forced overrides take precedence over the proximity override.

The resulting class forks the plan's readiness dimension. A configure that resolves to `STANDARD` advances the plan `SURVEYED → CONFIGURED`, and that plan will never see an engineer. A configure that resolves to `CRITICAL` or `ENGINEERED_CRITICAL` advances the plan `SURVEYED → PENDING_ENGINEERING` instead, and that plan cannot reach `APPROVED` until a stamped engineering analysis exists. Both branches run the identical calculation set — capacity, utilization, classification, provisional ground bearing, allowable wind and sling tension — and differ only in the class they land on and the state they advance to. Re-configuring from `CONFIGURED` or `PENDING_ENGINEERING` follows the same fork, so a plan can move between the two branches as its numbers change; and on either branch, any engineering review already on the plan standing at `UNDER_REVIEW`, `CHANGES_REQUESTED` or `STAMPED` is moved to `SUPERSEDED` by the new configuration.

A configure also settles the plan's transport posture. The plan's `escort_required` becomes true when the assigned crane's `class_tons` is greater than or equal to 200 tons, and is false while no crane is assigned. The plan's `permit_required` is true when `road_closure_requested` is true **or** `escort_required` is true, and false otherwise. Both flags are derived, never supplied. Finally, the configure increments the plan's `version` by one.

Assigning a machine puts hours on that machine's books. A crane's `reserved_hours_total` is the sum, over every plan on that crane whose readiness dimension is `CONFIGURED`, `PENDING_ENGINEERING`, `APPROVED` or `RELEASED`, of the plan's window length — `window_end` minus `window_start`, expressed in hours — rounded half up to two decimal places, and its `active_plan_count` is the number of plans in that same set. Plans sitting at `DRAFT` or `SURVEYED`, and plans that have reached `CLOSED` or `CANCELLED`, are excluded. Because a configure supersedes the prior configuration, it must first release whatever crane the plan previously held and recompute that machine's ledgers before recomputing them for the newly chosen one. This is a conserved ledger in the sense of §2.9: configure a plan then cancel it, and the crane's `reserved_hours_total` must return to precisely the value it had before.

Two scheduling constraints are evaluated at configure time, and they are independent of each other. The first is **crane effective-window exclusivity**: the new plan's window, treated as a half-open interval that includes its `window_start` and excludes its `window_end`, may not intersect any other plan's window on the same crane extended by a trailing release buffer of twelve hours — that is, the other plan's interval runs from its `window_start` up to but not including twelve hours after its `window_end`. Only plans in the reserving set (`CONFIGURED`, `PENDING_ENGINEERING`, `APPROVED`, `RELEASED`) are considered. An intersection is refused with `ERR_CRANE_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.

The second is the **operator daily hour cap**. For each UTC calendar day touched by any of the operator's plans in that same reserving set, the system sums the hours each plan occupies *on that day*, clipping each plan's window to the day boundaries: the day opens inclusively at midnight UTC and closes exclusively at the following midnight, and a plan contributes the hours between the later of its `window_start` and the day's opening and the earlier of its `window_end` and the day's close, or nothing at all if that difference is not positive. A plan spanning midnight therefore contributes only its clipped share to each of the two days it touches. The person's `peak_daily_assigned_hours` is the largest of those per-day sums across every day they touch, rounded half up to two decimal places, and is 0 when they hold no reserving plans. If any single day's sum would come out **strictly greater** than 12.0 hours, the configure is refused with `ERR_OPERATOR_HOURS_EXCEEDED`; exactly 12.0 hours is allowed. Two plans that never overlap each other in time can still be rejected together by this rule, because it aggregates capacity per day rather than testing for collisions.

### 3.5. Ground Bearing, Matting and the Wind Envelope

The ground carries the machine, the load and the counterweight together, and this system computes what the ground must hold **twice** — once provisionally from the survey, and once again, more harshly, if and when a licensed engineer stamps the pick. The two computations share every input and diverge on exactly one line, which is why the plan carries `bearing_variant` to record which of them produced the figures currently stored on it.

Both variants begin from the same total vertical load: the crane's `gross_weight_lb`, plus its `counterweight_lb`, plus the plan's `total_load_lb` as computed in §3.4. Under the **`PROVISIONAL_SURVEY`** variant — written at configure time, before any engineering stamp — the machine is assumed to be sitting level with all four outriggers sharing the vertical load equally and with no dynamic amplification, so the plan's `per_outrigger_load_lb` is that total divided by four, rounded half up to two decimal places, and `bearing_variant` is set to `PROVISIONAL_SURVEY`. Under the **`FINAL_ENGINEERED`** variant — written when an engineering review on the plan reaches `STAMPED` — the engineer assumes instead that the governing outrigger carries sixty hundredths of the *whole* vertical load and applies a dynamic and impact factor of 1.10 on top, so `per_outrigger_load_lb` is the total vertical load multiplied by sixty hundredths and then by 1.10, rounded half up to two decimal places, and `bearing_variant` is set to `FINAL_ENGINEERED`. The same inputs therefore yield a materially higher figure under the engineered variant, and the engineered figures **overwrite the provisional ones in place** on the plan rather than sitting beside them.

From `per_outrigger_load_lb` onward the two variants are identical. The plan's `bearing_pressure_psf` is `per_outrigger_load_lb` divided by the crane's `outrigger_pad_area_sqft` — the bearing area of a single float — rounded half up to two decimal places. The plan's `matting_required` is true when that `bearing_pressure_psf` is **strictly greater** than the site's `soil_bearing_capacity_psf` as most recently confirmed by a survey; equal pressure needs no mats. When matting is required, the plan's `mat_area_required_sqft` is `per_outrigger_load_lb` divided by the site's `soil_bearing_capacity_psf`, rounded **up** to the next whole square foot, and its `mat_count` is four times the number of standard mat panels needed to cover that area, where one standard panel covers 32 square feet and a partial panel counts as a whole one — four, because every outrigger needs the same treatment. When matting is not required, both `mat_area_required_sqft` and `mat_count` are 0.

Wind is handled by derating the machine rather than by refusing the pick. At configure time, the system computes a sail-area ratio from the plan's own numbers: it is the plan's `sail_area_sqft` multiplied by 1000 and divided by its `load_weight_lb`, rounded half up to two decimal places. A plan with a `load_weight_lb` of zero — which the positivity constraint forbids at creation but which the calculation must still define — takes a ratio of 0.00. That ratio selects one of four derate factors: a ratio at or below 1.00 keeps the full rating with a factor of 1.00; a ratio above 1.00 but at or below 2.00 takes 0.85; a ratio above 2.00 but at or below 3.00 takes 0.70; and anything above 3.00 takes 0.55. The plan's `allowable_wind_mph` is the crane's `rated_max_wind_mph` multiplied by that factor and then **floored** to a whole mile per hour. Tier selection reads the rounded ratio, so a raw ratio of 2.004 rounds to 2.00 and stays in the 0.85 band.

### 3.6. Engineering Review of Critical Lifts

A plan sitting at `PENDING_ENGINEERING` waits for a licensed professional engineer. An `Engineer` opens a review by naming the `lift_plan_id` under review, the `author_personnel_id` of the engineer who prepared the analysis, and the `analysis_notes` narrative itself. The `author_personnel_id` must reference a person whose `role_type` is `ENGINEER`, or the submission is refused with `ERR_PERSONNEL_ROLE_MISMATCH`. A new review is created at `UNDER_REVIEW` with `revision_round` at 1, `stamped_by_personnel_id` null, and both `change_request_note` and `decision_note` null. Reviews may be listed and narrowed by `lift_plan_id`, `author_personnel_id`, `stamped_by_personnel_id` and `status`.

Deciding on a review is a **second** engineer's job, and this is the hardest-enforced segregation boundary in the system: the caller's `X-User-ID` must differ from the review's `author_personnel_id` on every decision, and must equal it on every resubmission. The decision carries an `action` and an optional `note`, and is only legal while the review stands at `UNDER_REVIEW`; any other posture is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REVIEW_STATE`. The three actions diverge as follows.

`REQUEST_CHANGES` moves the review `UNDER_REVIEW → CHANGES_REQUESTED` and records the supplied `note` as `change_request_note`, leaving the owning plan exactly where it is.

`REJECT` moves the review `UNDER_REVIEW → REJECTED`, which is terminal, and records the supplied `note` as `decision_note`. The plan stays at `PENDING_ENGINEERING` and can only progress by way of a fresh review or a re-configuration.

`STAMP` is the consequential one and carries an extra gate: the owning plan must be sitting at `PENDING_ENGINEERING` at that moment, or the stamp is refused with `ERR_PLAN_NOT_PENDING_ENGINEERING`. A successful stamp moves the review `UNDER_REVIEW → STAMPED`, writes the stamping engineer's identifier into `stamped_by_personnel_id`, records the `note` as `decision_note`, and then runs the `FINAL_ENGINEERED` ground-bearing variant of §3.5. The engineered results are written twice: onto the review itself as `final_bearing_pressure_psf` and `final_mat_count`, and onto the plan, where they overwrite `bearing_variant`, `per_outrigger_load_lb`, `bearing_pressure_psf`, `matting_required`, `mat_area_required_sqft` and `mat_count` in place. In the same request the plan advances `PENDING_ENGINEERING → APPROVED` — no lift director sign-off is needed on a critical lift, because the stamp *is* the sign-off — and the plan's `version` increments alongside the review's.

Revisions cycle, but only three deep. When changes have been requested, the original author — and only the author — resubmits with fresh `analysis_notes`, which moves the review `CHANGES_REQUESTED → UNDER_REVIEW` and increments `revision_round` by one. That cycle may repeat while `revision_round` is below 3. A resubmission attempted while `revision_round` already stands at 3 is a hard stop with an unusual shape: the request **fails** with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` at HTTP 409, and yet the review is still persisted as `REJECTED` with `decision_note` recording that the revision limit was exceeded, and its `version` still increments. Callers must not read the error as "nothing happened".

Reviews also end without anyone deciding on them. Whenever the owning plan is re-configured, demoted back to `SURVEYED` by the gear-integrity cascade, or cancelled, every review on that plan standing at `UNDER_REVIEW`, `CHANGES_REQUESTED` **or** `STAMPED` is moved to `SUPERSEDED` by the system, with its `version` incremented. `SUPERSEDED`, like `STAMPED` and `REJECTED`, is terminal — a superseded analysis is dead paper and cannot be resubmitted or decided upon.

### 3.7. Permits, Escorts and Road Closures

Any plan whose `permit_required` came out true in §3.4 needs at least one granted authorization before it can go to the field. A `Dispatcher` opens a permit application naming the `lift_plan_id` it clears, the `permit_type` sought — `ROAD_CLOSURE`, `CRANE_PLACEMENT` or `OVERSIZE_ESCORT` — and the `jurisdiction_code` the application will be filed with. A new application is created at `DRAFT` with `submitted_at`, `decision_at`, `agency_reference` and `denial_reason` all null.

Two figures are derived at creation and never re-derived afterwards. The permit's `escort_required` is a snapshot of the owning plan's `escort_required` at that instant — which, per §3.4, is true when the assigned crane's `class_tons` is at least 200 tons. The permit's `required_lead_days` is a base lead time determined by `permit_type` — 10 days for a `ROAD_CLOSURE`, 7 days for an `OVERSIZE_ESCORT`, and 3 days for a `CRANE_PLACEMENT` — plus a further 5 days when `escort_required` is true. Its `permit_fee_cents` is likewise a base fee by type — 85,000 cents for a `ROAD_CLOSURE`, 45,000 cents for an `OVERSIZE_ESCORT`, and 25,000 cents for a `CRANE_PLACEMENT` — plus a further 60,000 cents when `escort_required` is true. Its `cancellation_fee_cents` starts at 0 and is only ever charged in the circumstance described in §3.9. Permits can be listed and narrowed by `lift_plan_id` and `status`.

Filing the application with the authority is a separate act, legal from `DRAFT`, from `DENIED` and from `SUSPENDED` — the last two are what make the permit lifecycle cyclic, since a refused or suspended application can be re-filed without being re-created. Any other posture is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PERMIT_STATE`. Two checks run at filing time, in this order. First, the permit's own `jurisdiction_code` must equal the `jurisdiction_code` of the site owning the permit's lift plan; a mismatch is `ERR_JURISDICTION_MISMATCH`. Second, there must be enough runway: the interval from the logical clock to the plan's `window_start` must be at least `required_lead_days` multiplied by 24 hours, measured inclusively, so a filing that lands exactly on the lead time is accepted and one that lands a minute inside it is refused with `ERR_PERMIT_LEAD_TIME_SHORT`. Because jurisdiction is checked first, an application that is both mis-addressed and too late reports the jurisdiction failure. A successful filing moves the permit to `SUBMITTED` and stamps `submitted_at` with the logical clock; when the prior posture was `DENIED`, the stale `denial_reason` is cleared back to null in the same step.

The authority's written response is recorded back into the system by the dispatch desk, carrying a `decision` of either `APPROVE` or `DENY`, an optional `agency_reference` tracking number, and an optional `reason`. Recording a decision is legal only from `SUBMITTED`. An approval moves the permit `SUBMITTED → APPROVED`, stamps `decision_at` with the logical clock and stores the `agency_reference`. A denial moves it `SUBMITTED → DENIED`, stamps `decision_at`, and stores the supplied `reason` as `denial_reason`; from there the application can be re-filed and the cycle `DRAFT → SUBMITTED → DENIED → SUBMITTED` may repeat.

Permits also move without anyone touching them. When a crane is pulled out of service and the plans on it are demoted, as described in §3.1, every `APPROVED` permit on those demoted plans is moved to `SUSPENDED` — the authorization is not destroyed, but the job no longer holds it, and re-filing is required before release becomes possible again. When a plan is cancelled, every permit on it standing at `SUBMITTED`, `APPROVED` or `SUSPENDED` is moved to `VOIDED`, which is terminal.

### 3.8. Sign-Off, Release and the Day of the Lift

For a standard pick, sign-off belongs to the `LiftDirector`. Approval takes no payload at all and moves the plan `CONFIGURED → APPROVED`, but only when three things hold at once: the plan's readiness dimension really is `CONFIGURED`, its `lift_class` really is `STANDARD` — a critical or engineered-critical plan can never be approved this way, since it reaches `APPROVED` only through an engineering stamp — and its `rigging_verified` is true.

Release is the last gate before iron moves, and it is where every certification, permit, gear and credit condition is finally tested. It too takes no payload, and moves the plan `APPROVED → RELEASED` only if all of the following hold. The assigned crane must not be `OUT_OF_SERVICE`, or the release fails with `ERR_CRANE_OUT_OF_SERVICE`. Every commitment on the plan still standing at `ACTIVE` must reference gear whose posture is `SERVICEABLE`, or the release fails with `ERR_GEAR_NOT_SERVICEABLE` — this is what makes the quiet drift from `SERVICEABLE` to `DUE_INSPECTION` bite. If the plan's `permit_required` is true, at least one permit on the plan must stand at `APPROVED`, or the release fails with `ERR_PERMIT_NOT_APPROVED`; a plan whose `permit_required` is false skips this check entirely. The assigned operator's `certification_expires_on` must be strictly after the plan's `window_start` — a certificate that lapses partway through the window is not good enough — or the release fails with `ERR_OPERATOR_CERT_EXPIRED`. That operator's `endorsement_class_tons` must be greater than or equal to the assigned crane's `class_tons`, or it fails with `ERR_OPERATOR_ENDORSEMENT_INSUFFICIENT`. The assigned rigger's `rigger_level` must sit at or above the rank the lift class demands on the `NONE`, `BASIC`, `INTERMEDIATE`, `ADVANCED`, `MASTER` ladder: a `STANDARD` lift demands at least `BASIC`, a `CRITICAL` lift demands at least `ADVANCED`, and an `ENGINEERED_CRITICAL` lift demands `MASTER`; a shortfall fails with `ERR_RIGGER_QUALIFICATION_INSUFFICIENT`. Finally the owning customer's `credit_hold` must be false, or the release fails with `ERR_CUSTOMER_CREDIT_HOLD` at HTTP 402. As §2.7 sets out, the credit check runs last of all, so a plan failing both a safety gate and the credit gate reports the safety gate.

Once released, the plan's operational dimension becomes the `Operator`'s to drive, and only the operator named in the plan's `operator_id` may drive it. Each step is requested by naming an `action`. `MOBILIZE` moves `NOT_MOBILIZED → MOBILIZED` and additionally requires that the plan's readiness dimension is `RELEASED` and that the crane is not `OUT_OF_SERVICE`; it causes the crane's derived `status` to be recomputed to `IN_SERVICE`. `START_LIFT` moves `MOBILIZED → LIFTING`. `RESUME` moves `WIND_HOLD → LIFTING`. `SECURE` moves `LIFTING → SECURED`. `DEMOBILIZE` moves `SECURED → DEMOBILIZED`, which is terminal on that dimension. Three of those five — `START_LIFT`, `RESUME` and `SECURE` — additionally require that **no** standby event on the plan is currently `OPEN`; an open hold blocks all three. A request whose current operational posture is not a legal starting point for the requested action, or which is blocked by an open standby, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_FIELD_STATE`.

Demobilizing is the plan's closing act and does four things beyond the state move itself. The readiness dimension advances `RELEASED → CLOSED`. The plan's `standby_hours_billable` is recomputed from every closed standby event on the plan. The crane's `reserved_hours_total`, `active_plan_count` and derived `status` are recomputed, the plan having left the reserving set. And the assigned operator's `peak_daily_assigned_hours` is recomputed for the same reason.

Standby is opened by a `SafetyManager`, not by the crew. The event names its `lift_plan_id`, a `cause` drawn from `WIND`, `WEATHER`, `CUSTOMER_DELAY` and `SITE_ACCESS`, and the `started_at` instant at which the standby clock began; a `wind_speed_mph` measurement in miles per hour may also be supplied, defaults to 0, may not be negative, and is consulted only when the cause is `WIND`. A `WIND` event is accepted only when its `wind_speed_mph` is at or above the plan's `allowable_wind_mph` as derived in §3.5 — reporting a breeze below the plan's own derated envelope is refused with `ERR_WIND_BELOW_HOLD_THRESHOLD`, while every other cause skips this check entirely. A new event opens at `OPEN` with `ended_at` null, `raw_duration_hours` at 0 and `forced_wind_hold` false. Opening an event against a plan that is currently `LIFTING` drives that plan `LIFTING → WIND_HOLD` and increments the plan's `version`; when the cause was `WIND`, the event's `forced_wind_hold` is set true to record that this particular event is what stopped the pick. The `LIFTING → WIND_HOLD → LIFTING` loop is a genuine cycle and may repeat any number of times in a single job. Standby events can be listed and narrowed by `lift_plan_id`, `status` and `cause`.

Closing a standby window supplies the `ended_at` instant, which must be strictly after `started_at`. The event moves `OPEN → CLOSED` and its `raw_duration_hours` is computed as the elapsed time from `started_at` to `ended_at` expressed in hours, rounded half up to four decimal places. Closing then re-merges the plan's whole standby history, because overlapping and back-to-back holds must never be billed twice. Every closed event on the plan is taken as a half-open interval that includes its start and excludes its end; the intervals are sorted by `started_at` ascending with the earlier `created_at` breaking ties; and two neighbours are merged whenever the later one starts at or before the earlier one ends — so a zero-gap, perfectly adjacent pair merges into one. The merged total is the sum of the merged interval lengths in hours, rounded half up to four decimal places, and the plan's `standby_hours_billable` is that total rounded **up** to the next quarter of an hour. Every cause counts toward the merge, not just wind, and an event that is still `OPEN` contributes nothing until it closes.

### 3.9. Cancellation, Billing and the Receivable Ledger

A plan can be abandoned at any point before it reaches its own terminal states: cancellation is legal from every readiness posture except `CLOSED` and `CANCELLED`, and either a `Dispatcher` or a `LiftDirector` may do it, supplying a `reason` that is stored as the plan's `cancellation_reason`. The immediate effects are that every commitment on the plan still standing at `ACTIVE` is moved to `RELEASED` with each affected gear item's `committed_assignment_count` recomputed, every engineering review on the plan standing at `UNDER_REVIEW`, `CHANGES_REQUESTED` or `STAMPED` is moved to `SUPERSEDED`, the crane's `reserved_hours_total`, `active_plan_count` and derived `status` are recomputed as the plan leaves the reserving set, and the plan's `version` increments.

Then a four-deep chain runs, in which each hop is caused by the hop before it rather than by the cancellation directly. **Hop two** reaches the permits: every permit on the plan standing at `SUBMITTED`, `APPROVED` or `SUSPENDED` is moved to `VOIDED`, and a permit that was standing at `APPROVED` at that instant has its `cancellation_fee_cents` set to forty hundredths of its own `permit_fee_cents`, rounded half up to whole cents. A permit that was only `SUBMITTED` or was `SUSPENDED` keeps a `cancellation_fee_cents` of 0, because the authority never granted it. **Hop three** fires only because at least one voided permit now carries a `cancellation_fee_cents` greater than zero: the system mints exactly one invoice against the plan, with `system_generated` true, `billing_mode` `LUMP_SUM`, `lump_sum_cents` at 0 and `line_permit_cancellation_cents` set to the sum of those fees, and moves that invoice straight to `ISSUED` without anyone drafting it. **Hop four** fires only because that invoice reached `ISSUED`: the owning customer's `open_receivable_cents` and `invoice_count` are recomputed. When no voided permit carried a fee, hops three and four do not run and no invoice is minted.

Ordinary invoicing is the `BillingClerk`'s work. A draft is created against a `lift_plan_id` in a chosen `billing_mode` of either `HOURLY` or `LUMP_SUM`, carrying `expected_lift_plan_version` per §2.5. Four further inputs are optional and each defaults to 0: `on_site_hours` and `travel_hours`, both portal-to-portal claims that may not be negative; `travel_miles`, the round-trip mileage claimed; and `lump_sum_cents`, the agreed fixed price. A `LUMP_SUM` invoice must carry a `lump_sum_cents` strictly greater than 0 or it is refused with `ERR_LUMP_SUM_REQUIRED`; an `HOURLY` invoice ignores `lump_sum_cents` entirely. On creation the invoice copies `customer_id` from the plan's `customer_id`, snapshots the plan's `standby_hours_billable` into its own `standby_hours_source`, opens at `DRAFT`, causes the plan's `invoice_count` to be recomputed and the plan's `version` to increment, and — because the newest bill for a job retires the last unsent one — moves any prior invoice on the same plan still standing at `DRAFT` to `VOIDED`. Invoices can be listed and narrowed by `lift_plan_id`, `customer_id` and `status`.

The two billing modes are genuinely different bills computed from the same job. Under `HOURLY`, the invoice's `billable_hours` is the sum of `on_site_hours` and `travel_hours`, or 4.0 hours if that sum is smaller — a four-hour minimum applies whether or not the crew was there four hours. Its `regular_hours` is the smaller of `billable_hours` and 8.0 hours, and its `overtime_hours` is whatever part of `billable_hours` exceeds 8.0 hours, or zero if none does. The billable standby figure is the full `standby_hours_source` with **no** free allowance. Under `LUMP_SUM`, `billable_hours`, `regular_hours` and `overtime_hours` are all 0, and the billable standby figure is `standby_hours_source` reduced by a free allowance of 2.0 hours, floored at zero — the first two hours of standby are absorbed into the fixed price.

Seven line figures are then produced, each an integer count of cents. `line_regular_cents` is `regular_hours` multiplied by the assigned crane's `hourly_rate_cents`, rounded half up, and is 0 under `LUMP_SUM`. `line_overtime_cents` is `overtime_hours` multiplied by that same rate and then by 1.5, rounded half up, and is likewise 0 under `LUMP_SUM`. `line_standby_cents` is the mode-specific billable standby hours multiplied by the rate and then by seventy-five hundredths, rounded half up, and applies under both modes. `line_travel_cents` is `travel_miles` multiplied by 285 cents per mile, rounded half up, and applies under both modes. `line_mobdemob_cents` is the crane's `mob_demob_cents` under `HOURLY` and 0 under `LUMP_SUM`, because mobilization is inside the fixed price. `line_lump_sum_cents` is the supplied `lump_sum_cents` under `LUMP_SUM` and 0 under `HOURLY`. `line_permit_cancellation_cents` is the sum of `cancellation_fee_cents` across every permit on the plan standing at `VOIDED`, and is non-zero only on the system-generated cancellation invoice.

The order of rounding is part of the contract and is observable. Each of those seven lines is **rounded to whole cents first, and only then are the seven rounded lines added together** to give `subtotal_cents`. Summing the unrounded lines and rounding once at the end produces a different answer and is wrong.

Discount and surcharge are then applied in a pinned five-step order, because they look mutually dependent and are not. Step one is `subtotal_cents` as just computed. Step two selects `discount_pct` from a volume tier keyed on that **pre-surcharge** subtotal: a subtotal at or above 6,000,000 cents takes eight hundredths; failing that, one at or above 2,500,000 cents takes five hundredths; failing that, one at or above 1,000,000 cents takes two hundredths; anything below takes zero. Step three sets `discount_cents` to `subtotal_cents` multiplied by `discount_pct`, rounded half up to whole cents. Step four sets `fuel_surcharge_cents` to seven hundredths of the discounted subtotal — that is, of `subtotal_cents` less `discount_cents` — rounded half up to whole cents. Step five sets `total_cents` to `subtotal_cents`, less `discount_cents`, plus `fuel_surcharge_cents`. Keying the tier on the subtotal *after* the surcharge is wrong and changes the answer whenever the surcharge alone would carry the bill across a tier boundary.

Invoices then move through a short lifecycle with one cycle in it. Issuing moves a `DRAFT` — or a `DISPUTED` — invoice to `ISSUED`, and requires the owning customer's `credit_hold` to be false, or it is refused with `ERR_CUSTOMER_CREDIT_HOLD`. A `Customer` may dispute an invoice that stands at `ISSUED`, supplying a `dispute_reason`, which moves it `ISSUED → DISPUTED`; re-issuing it returns it to `ISSUED`, so the `ISSUED → DISPUTED → ISSUED` loop may repeat. Payment is recorded from either `ISSUED` or `DISPUTED` and moves the invoice to `PAID`, which is terminal. Voiding, which takes a `reason`, is legal from `DRAFT`, `ISSUED` or `DISPUTED` and moves the invoice to `VOIDED`, also terminal. Any of these requested from a posture that does not permit it is refused with `ERR_INVALID_INVOICE_STATE`.

The customer's ledger follows mechanically. `open_receivable_cents` is the sum of `total_cents` across every invoice belonging to that customer whose status is `ISSUED` **or** `DISPUTED`; drafts, paid invoices and voided invoices are excluded. `invoice_count` is the number of that customer's invoices in any status whatsoever. Both are recomputed on every invoice status change — issue, dispute, payment, void, and the cancellation invoice minted by hop three above. Because a disputed invoice stays inside the open set, disputing changes nothing about the balance; because the ledger is recomputed rather than adjusted, issuing an invoice and then voiding it returns `open_receivable_cents` to exactly its pre-issue value while leaving `invoice_count` where it stood after the invoice was created.
