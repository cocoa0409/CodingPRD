# Environmental Justice Screening & Permit Review API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

State environmental agencies are increasingly required to look past the single-facility, single-pollutant permit file and ask a harder question: what does this authorisation do to a community that is already carrying more than its share of pollution, illness and disadvantage? This API is the system of record for that question. It maintains a screening tool over census block groups, combining demographic, health and pollution-burden indicators into a composite score that designates **overburdened communities**; it decides which permit applications must leave the standard review lane and enter enhanced review; it computes cumulative impact by combining the community's existing burden with the applicant's proposed emissions; and it carries the resulting file through translated public participation, permit condition development, and a final issue-or-deny decision that the agency must be able to defend on the record.

Three properties of the domain shape the design. First, screening data is never final: a newly filed indicator supersedes an older one, an enforcement finding at a nearby facility degrades the area's data confidence, and both events silently move the ground under permit files that are mid-review. The system therefore treats designation and data freshness as two independent, live dimensions of the same screening area and cascades their changes forward into applications, assessments and comment periods rather than freezing a snapshot at intake. Second, the agency's denial authority — the power to refuse a permit whose disproportionate impact mitigation cannot cure — is only credible if the numbers behind it are reproducible to the cent and the ton. Every score, credit, surcharge and comment-period length in this specification has a pinned evaluation order and a pinned rounding point. Third, the review chain is a segregation-of-duties chain: the analyst who authors a cumulative impact assessment may not quality-assure it, and the manager who issues or denies may be neither its author nor its reviewer.

A companion set of community-facing workflows completes the picture: complaint intake in identified areas, priority scoring that pushes inspections toward the most burdened block groups, and a program-evaluation report that lets the agency show what its permitting decisions actually did in overburdened communities. All resource endpoints described here are mounted under the `/ej` prefix; a single administrative logical-clock control endpoint sits outside that mount so that deadlines, vintage windows and comment periods are deterministic under test.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contract-level rules that hold across every endpoint: authentication, response shape, pagination, identity and versioning, the logical clock, numeric precision, concurrency guards, cascade discipline, resolution order and rejection precedence.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which they are acting. If either header is absent or empty, the request is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS`. If `X-User-Role` is present but is not one of the eight roles below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE`. If the role is well known but is not among the roles authorised for the requested endpoint, the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.

The eight recognised roles are:

- `ProgramAdmin` — program office staff who maintain screening reference data, facilities, venues and the logical clock.
- `EJAnalyst` — the screening analyst who runs applicability determinations, authors cumulative impact assessments, drafts permit conditions, and triages, resolves and queues complaints for inspection.
- `QAReviewer` — the independent reviewer who accepts or returns cumulative impact assessments, and who may never review an assessment they authored.
- `PermitManager` — the decision authority who issues, denies and rescinds permits.
- `CommunityLiaison` — the community engagement officer who runs comment periods, publishes translated materials, books accessible meetings and reopens complaints.
- `Inspector` — the field inspector who completes assigned inspections.
- `ApplicantRepresentative` — the facility representative who files, submits, resubmits and withdraws that facility's permit applications.
- `PublicCommenter` — a member of the public filing comments and complaints.

Seven of the eight roles carry an **opaque** identity. When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramAdmin`, `EJAnalyst`, `QAReviewer`, `PermitManager`, `CommunityLiaison`, `Inspector` or `PublicCommenter`, the `X-User-ID` value is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity and is never compared against any entity's `id`. The server does record such identifiers where a workflow demands provenance — `determined_by_user_id` on an applicability determination, `author_user_id` and `qa_user_id` on an assessment, `decided_by_user_id` on a decision, and `inspector_user_id` on an inspection — and it compares recorded operator identifiers against each other to enforce segregation of duties and inspector assignment, but it never resolves them to a row.

`ApplicantRepresentative` is the one exception: its identity is **entity-backed by a facility**. When `X-User-Role` is `ApplicantRepresentative`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting facility, and ownership checks on any permit application compare that application's `facility_id` field to `X-User-ID`. Filing an application for a facility other than the caller's own, or submitting, resubmitting or withdrawing an application whose `facility_id` is not the caller's `X-User-ID`, is rejected with `ERR_NOT_APPLICATION_OWNER`. Similarly, an `Inspector` may only complete an inspection whose `inspector_user_id` equals their `X-User-ID`; any other inspector is rejected with `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_INSPECTOR`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints accept two query parameters. `limit` bounds the number of items returned; it defaults to 10, may be no smaller than 1 and no larger than 100. `offset` is the number of items to skip and defaults to 0, with 0 as its minimum. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. There is exactly one documented exception in this API: the list of an application's applicability determinations is returned newest first, ordered by `revision_round` descending and then by `created_at` descending, so that the determination governing the current revision round appears first.

Every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Versioning

Every persisted resource receives an `id`: a server-minted UUID string with no type prefix of any kind. Clients never supply an `id`.

Most resources also carry a full audit triple. `created_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, fixed when the row is written. `updated_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp in the same format, refreshed on every mutation of the resource. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that resource — including mutations that only touch derived fields such as a score, a ledger balance or a counter. Screening areas, burden indicators, facilities, permit applications, cumulative impact assessments, participation plans, venues, public meetings, permit conditions, permit decisions, community complaints and inspections all carry `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` and `version`.

Two resources are deliberately write-once and carry **only** `id` and `created_at`: the **ApplicabilityDetermination** and the **PublicComment**. Neither exposes `updated_at` and neither exposes `version`; both fields are absent from their payloads entirely. An applicability determination is an immutable record of what the screen found at one moment, and a public comment is an immutable submission from the public.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

All temporal reasoning in this system — vintage windows, comment-period boundaries, repeat-complaint windows, meeting elapsed-ness — reads a server-held **logical UTC clock**, never the host's wall clock. Before any client sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time. A `ProgramAdmin` may move the clock by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant; the response reports the resulting `current_utc`. Moving the clock mutates no entity, but every subsequent calculation reads the new instant.

Where a rule speaks of the "clock date", it means the UTC calendar date of the current logical clock instant; where it speaks of the "clock year", it means that instant's UTC calendar year. Calendar-date fields (`comment_start_date`, `comment_end_date`, `scheduled_date`) are ISO dates in UTC; timestamp fields (`starts_at`, `ends_at`) are full ISO 8601 UTC instants compared at minute precision.

### 2.6. Numeric Precision, Rounding and Pinned Evaluation Order

Money, tons, percentiles and index values are decimal quantities and must be computed with decimal arithmetic. The rounding method everywhere in this specification is **round half up**.

Stored precision is as follows. Screening component means, screening scores, cumulative impact indices, residual indices, tons-per-year quantities, per-condition credits, currency amounts and priority scores are all stored and returned with 2 decimal places. Two intermediate quantities are pinned at 4 decimal places: an assessment's `exposure_factor`, and the mitigation credit ratio used to derive the residual impact index. The program-evaluation `denial_rate` is reported with 4 decimal places. Day counts and date arithmetic are in whole UTC calendar days; distances are whole metres; `proposed_operating_hours_per_year` is a whole number of hours.

Two disciplines matter more than the digit counts. **Rounding is applied exactly at the points this document names, and nowhere else** — a sum of per-item values that this document says are individually rounded must be formed from the rounded values, not from the raw products. And **evaluation order is pinned wherever one term feeds a tier or cap selection**: the tier is always chosen from the input term the rule names, never from the output the tier helps produce. Both disciplines are called out again at the calculations they govern.

### 2.7. Screening Data Provenance and Version Guards

Three write operations sit on top of a parent record whose derived state they rewrite, and each therefore requires the client to prove it has read the parent's current state. In all three cases the guard field is a **required, non-optional** input, and a mismatch against the parent's current `version` rejects the request before any state changes.

- Filing a burden indicator (`POST /ej/burden-indicators`) requires `expected_screening_area_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `ScreeningArea`. If it does not equal that area's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_AREA_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The guard exists because a newly filed indicator supersedes the prior active row for the same indicator code and rewrites both screening score variants; a writer working from a stale view of the area would silently supersede data it never saw.
- Creating a cumulative impact assessment (`POST /ej/cumulative-impact-assessments`) requires `expected_application_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `PermitApplication`. A mismatch against the application's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_APPLICATION_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A new assessment supersedes an existing draft or returned assessment and rewrites the application's `pre_mitigation_index`, so a stale view means the author is superseding an assessment or an index they never read.
- Attaching a permit condition (`POST /ej/permit-conditions`) likewise requires `expected_application_version` and is rejected with `ERR_STALE_APPLICATION_VERSION` (HTTP 412) on mismatch. Every condition rewrites `total_mitigation_credit_tpy` and `residual_impact_index`, so an author working from a stale mitigation picture would size a control requirement against a residual index that no longer exists.

No other endpoint takes a version field, and no other endpoint performs a version check. Provenance stamps complement these guards: `area_version_after` on a burden indicator records the screening area's `version` observed immediately after that indicator's most recent status change, and `application_version_after` on an applicability determination, a cumulative impact assessment and a permit condition records the permit application's `version` observed immediately after that row's most recent state change.

### 2.8. Cascade Discipline Across the Screening Hierarchy

Screening areas, facilities, applications, assessments, plans, meetings, conditions, decisions, complaints and inspections form one connected graph, and this system is **immediately consistent** across it: when an operation invalidates a premise that another row depends on, the dependent row is moved in the same request, not on a later sweep. Each row the server touches in a cascade is a real mutation of that row — its `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increases by 1 — even though the client asked about a different resource.

A screening area carries **two independent state dimensions on the same row**. The `designation` dimension (`UNDESIGNATED`, `OVERBURDENED`, `SEVERELY_OVERBURDENED`) expresses how burdened the community is and is derived purely from the statewide screening score. The `data_status` dimension (`CURRENT`, `STALE`) expresses whether the area's burden data can still be relied on, and moves only on enforcement events and recertification. The two dimensions never gate each other: an area may be `SEVERELY_OVERBURDENED` and `CURRENT`, or `UNDESIGNATED` and `STALE`, and a change in one is not a change in the other except insofar as a recomputed score happens to cross a designation threshold.

Cascades in this system are directional and run downstream: from an indicator to its area, from an area's designation to the applications at its facilities, from an application's regression to the assessments and comment periods that supported it, and from a confirmed inspection violation all the way through facility, area, assessment and application. De-designation is deliberately asymmetric — an area falling out of designation never downgrades an in-flight review, and an application already on the enhanced track stays there.

### 2.9. Path and Payload Resolution Order

Endpoints that carry an identifier in the path resolve it before doing anything else, and endpoints whose path carries more than one identifier resolve them strictly left to right; the **first** identifier that does not resolve decides the error. Each resource family has its own not-found code: `ERR_SCREENING_AREA_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FACILITY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_VENUE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MEETING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONDITION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DECISION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COMPLAINT_NOT_FOUND` and `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_FOUND`. A request for an area's facilities under a non-existent area therefore fails with `ERR_SCREENING_AREA_NOT_FOUND` before the server considers the facilities at all.

Foreign keys carried in a request body are resolved after path identifiers and before any business rule, and they are checked in this declaration order: `screening_area_id`, then `facility_id`, then `permit_application_id`, then `participation_plan_id`, then `venue_id`, then `prior_application_id`. The first unresolved key decides the error, using the same per-family codes; both `permit_application_id` and `prior_application_id` report `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_FOUND`. This body-key resolution applies to the nine creating operations that carry foreign keys: burden-indicator filing, facility registration, permit-application filing, assessment creation, meeting booking, comment filing, condition attachment, permit decision creation, and complaint intake.

### 2.10. Rejection Precedence

When a single request violates more than one rule, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fully determined. The server evaluates checks in the following order and returns the first failure it finds; a later check never masks an earlier one.

1. Both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` are present and non-empty — otherwise `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
2. `X-User-Role` names one of the eight declared roles — otherwise `ERR_UNKNOWN_ROLE` (401). Applies to every endpoint.
3. The role is authorised for the endpoint — otherwise `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
4. The body and query string are well formed: required fields present, types correct, enum members recognised, numeric ranges respected (`proposed_emissions_tpy` greater than 0, `population` at least 1, `capacity` at least 1, `ends_at` strictly later than `starts_at`, `limit` within 1 through 100, `offset` at least 0, dates and timestamps valid ISO 8601) — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Applies to every endpoint.
5. Path identifiers resolve, left to right, per §2.9 — otherwise the matching 404 code. Applies to every path-parameterised endpoint.
6. Body foreign keys resolve, in the declaration order given in §2.9 — otherwise the matching 404 code. Applies to the nine body-foreign-key creators listed there.
7. An `ApplicantRepresentative` acting on a permit application presents an `X-User-ID` equal to that application's `facility_id` — otherwise `ERR_NOT_APPLICATION_OWNER` (403). Applies to four operations: filing an application, submitting it, resubmitting it and withdrawing it.
8. An `Inspector` completing an inspection presents an `X-User-ID` equal to that inspection's `inspector_user_id` — otherwise `ERR_NOT_ASSIGNED_INSPECTOR` (403). Applies to inspection completion.
9. The target resource is in a state from which the requested transition is declared — otherwise the per-family state code: `ERR_INVALID_APPLICATION_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_ASSESSMENT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_PLAN_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_MEETING_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_CONDITION_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_DECISION_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_COMPLAINT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_INSPECTION_STATE`, or `ERR_AREA_NOT_STALE` when recertification is asked of an area whose `data_status` is already `CURRENT`. All are HTTP 409.
10. The version guards of §2.7 match — otherwise `ERR_STALE_AREA_VERSION` on burden-indicator filing, or `ERR_STALE_APPLICATION_VERSION` on assessment creation and condition attachment (both 412).
11. Segregation of duties holds: a `QAReviewer`'s `X-User-ID` differs from the assessment's `author_user_id`, and a deciding `PermitManager`'s `X-User-ID` is neither the governing assessment's `author_user_id` nor its `qa_user_id` — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). Applies to two operations: the QA decision on an assessment, and permit decision creation.

Beyond this common ladder, endpoint-specific business rules are evaluated in the order below (each item still yields exactly one error):

12. Burden indicator: `category` equals the canonical category of `indicator_code` — otherwise `ERR_INDICATOR_CATEGORY_MISMATCH` (422).
13. Burden indicator: `statewide_percentile` and `county_percentile` both lie between 0.00 and 100.00 inclusive — otherwise `ERR_PERCENTILE_OUT_OF_RANGE` (422).
14. Burden indicator: `vintage_year` lies within the validity window described in §3.1 — otherwise `ERR_INDICATOR_VINTAGE_INVALID` (422).
15. Screening area: `area_code` is not already in use — otherwise `ERR_AREA_CODE_DUPLICATE` (409).
16. Screening area: `required_translation_languages` is non-empty when the translation trigger fires — otherwise `ERR_TRANSLATION_LANGUAGES_REQUIRED` (422).
17. Assessment creation and assessment revision: the refined methodology carries both of its modelling inputs — otherwise `ERR_REFINED_INPUTS_REQUIRED` (422).
18. Condition attachment: a monitoring condition carries `monitoring_frequency` and a community-benefit condition carries `benefit_value_usd` — otherwise `ERR_CONDITION_INPUTS_REQUIRED` (422).
19. Advance-to-decision and permit decision creation: on the enhanced track the governing assessment exists and is `ACCEPTED` — otherwise `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_ACCEPTED` (409).
20. Advance-to-decision: on the enhanced track the application's participation plan is `CLOSED` — otherwise `ERR_PARTICIPATION_NOT_CLOSED` (409).
21. Opening a comment period: `comment_start_date` is on or after the clock date — otherwise `ERR_COMMENT_START_IN_PAST` (422).
22. Extending a comment period: the day cap of §3.4 is respected — otherwise `ERR_COMMENT_DAYS_CAP_EXCEEDED` (422).
23. Opening or extending a comment period: the area's concurrent-engagement cap of §3.4 is respected — otherwise `ERR_AREA_ENGAGEMENT_CAPACITY` (409).
24. Closing a comment period: the clock date is strictly later than `comment_end_date` — otherwise `ERR_COMMENT_PERIOD_NOT_ELAPSED` (409).
25. Comment filing: the plan is `OPEN` or `EXTENDED` and the clock date lies within the comment window inclusive of both ends — otherwise `ERR_COMMENT_PERIOD_CLOSED` (409).
26. Meeting booking: the meeting's `starts_at` date and `ends_at` date both lie inside the plan's comment window, inclusive of both ends — otherwise `ERR_MEETING_OUTSIDE_COMMENT_WINDOW` (422).
27. Meeting booking: the venue's `status` is `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_VENUE_DECOMMISSIONED` (409).
28. Meeting booking: the venue has both `ada_accessible` and `transit_accessible` set to true — otherwise `ERR_VENUE_NOT_ACCESSIBLE` (422).
29. Meeting booking: the buffered candidate interval of §3.5 does not overlap a scheduled meeting at that venue — otherwise `ERR_VENUE_SLOT_CONFLICT` (409).
30. Issuance: on the enhanced track the plan's `adequacy_status` is `COMPLIANT` — otherwise `ERR_PARTICIPATION_INADEQUATE` (409).
31. Issuance: `residual_impact_index` is below the denial threshold — otherwise `ERR_DISPROPORTIONATE_IMPACT_UNMITIGATED` (409).
32. Denial: `denial_basis` is supported by the record — otherwise `ERR_DENIAL_BASIS_UNSUPPORTED` (422).
33. Issuance: `community_benefit_base_usd` is present when the area is designated — otherwise `ERR_BENEFIT_BASE_REQUIRED` (422).
34. Issuance: the community benefit total meets the per-ton minimum when the area is designated — otherwise `ERR_BENEFIT_COMMITMENT_INSUFFICIENT` (422).
35. Issuance: the area's permitted-load ledger stays under its cap — otherwise `ERR_AREA_LOAD_CAP_EXCEEDED` (409).
36. Inspection assignment: when the request narrows to a single complaint that is not inspectable — its status is not `TRIAGED`, it has no `facility_id`, or it already has a scheduled inspection — the request fails with `ERR_COMPLAINT_NOT_INSPECTABLE` (422).
37. Inspection assignment: the named inspector is under their per-day capacity on the requested date — otherwise `ERR_INSPECTOR_DAY_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409). This capacity check is evaluated **before** the selector picks a complaint.
38. Inspection assignment: the selector's candidate set is non-empty — otherwise `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_COMPLAINT` (409).
39. Complaint resolution: an enforcement referral is supported by a completed inspection whose finding confirmed a violation — otherwise `ERR_REFERRAL_UNSUPPORTED` (422).
40. Condition attachment and condition voiding: the owning application's `status` is `STANDARD_REVIEW`, `ENHANCED_REVIEW` or `PENDING_DECISION` — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_APPLICATION_STATE` (409).

### 2.11. Segregation of Duties in the Review Chain

The integrity of the enhanced-review file rests on three people being three people. The analyst who authored an assessment is recorded in `author_user_id`; the reviewer who accepted or returned it is recorded in `qa_user_id`, which is null until a QA decision is taken. A `QAReviewer` whose `X-User-ID` equals the assessment's `author_user_id` may not take a QA decision on it. A `PermitManager` whose `X-User-ID` equals either the governing assessment's `author_user_id` or its `qa_user_id` may not issue or deny that application. Both refusals carry `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Note that authorship follows the last person to touch the draft: revising a returned assessment re-stamps `author_user_id` with the reviser's `X-User-ID`, so a reviewer who returns an assessment and then revises it has disqualified themselves from reviewing it again.

### 2.12. Emissions Ledgers and the Area Load Cap

Two running ledgers track authorised pollution. A facility's `existing_load_tpy` is the sum of approved net emissions over that facility's issued applications. A screening area's `permitted_load_tpy` is the same sum taken over every issued application at every facility in the area. Both start at 0.00, both move only on permit issuance and permit rescission, and both are maintained by increment and decrement rather than by recomputation from scratch: issuance adds the application's `approved_net_emissions_tpy` to both ledgers, and rescission subtracts the same amount from both before zeroing the application's own `approved_net_emissions_tpy`.

The ledgers exist to enforce a hard community-level ceiling. An area may never be permitted beyond **1.15 times** its existing aggregate load: issuance is refused when the area's `permitted_load_tpy` plus the application's `approved_net_emissions_tpy` would exceed `baseline_load_tpy` multiplied by 1.15 and rounded half up to 2 decimals. The refusal carries `ERR_AREA_LOAD_CAP_EXCEEDED`. Denial books nothing: a denied application leaves both `existing_load_tpy` and `permitted_load_tpy` untouched and records 0.00 as its own approved net emissions.

Alongside the emissions ledgers, a screening area maintains three live counters that the workflows below keep in step: `active_indicator_count`, the number of qualifying active indicators behind its current score; `open_complaint_count`, the number of complaints in the area that are still open; and `open_plan_count`, the number of comment periods running in the area. A venue maintains its own occupancy ledger, `booked_meeting_count`, and a participation plan maintains `meeting_count` and `comment_count`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Building the Screening Baseline: Areas, Indicators, Scores and Designation

A `ProgramAdmin` registers a census block group as a screening unit by supplying its `area_code` (a code such as "BG-06-101", between 1 and 32 characters, unique across every registered area — a repeat is refused with `ERR_AREA_CODE_DUPLICATE`), the `county` it sits in, its resident `population` (at least 1), its `limited_english_pct` (a percentage between 0.0 and 100.0 carried to one decimal), the `baseline_load_tpy` figure for the area's existing aggregate criteria-pollutant load in tons per year (strictly greater than 0, two decimals), and optionally `required_translation_languages`, an array of two-character lower-case ISO 639-1 codes the agency must translate materials into, which defaults to an empty array. Language access is not optional where it matters: when `limited_english_pct` is **at least 10.0**, `required_translation_languages` must be non-empty, and an empty list in that case is refused with `ERR_TRANSLATION_LANGUAGES_REQUIRED`. That same 10.0 percent threshold sets the boolean `translation_required` on the area, which is recomputed on every scoring run.

A freshly registered area has no indicators, so its scoring pass runs over an empty input set: `demographic_component_statewide`, `health_component_statewide` and `pollution_component_statewide` are all 0.00, `ej_screen_score_statewide` and `ej_screen_score_county` are both 0.00, `active_indicator_count` is 0, `enforcement_burden_points` is 0, `permitted_load_tpy` is 0.00, `open_complaint_count` and `open_plan_count` are both 0, `designation` is `UNDESIGNATED` and `data_status` is `CURRENT`.

Burden data arrives one indicator at a time. Either a `ProgramAdmin` or an `EJAnalyst` files an indicator against an area by supplying `screening_area_id`, the `indicator_code` being measured, its `category`, the `raw_value` in the indicator's native unit, the `statewide_percentile` and `county_percentile` positions of this block group, the `vintage_year` the measurement is attributed to, and the `expected_screening_area_version` guard from §2.7. Ten indicator codes exist, and each belongs to exactly one canonical category: `PM25`, `DIESEL_PM`, `TOXIC_RELEASE` and `DRINKING_WATER` are `POLLUTION` indicators; `ASTHMA_ED_RATE`, `LOW_BIRTH_WEIGHT` and `CARDIOVASCULAR` are `HEALTH` indicators; and `POVERTY_RATE`, `LINGUISTIC_ISOLATION` and `EDUCATIONAL_ATTAINMENT` are `DEMOGRAPHIC` indicators. A submitted `category` that disagrees with the code's canonical family is refused with `ERR_INDICATOR_CATEGORY_MISMATCH`. Both percentiles must lie between 0.00 and 100.00 inclusive, or the filing is refused with `ERR_PERCENTILE_OUT_OF_RANGE`. And measurements age out: `vintage_year` must be no earlier than the clock year minus **five** and no later than the clock year itself, both bounds inclusive, or the filing is refused with `ERR_INDICATOR_VINTAGE_INVALID`.

Filing an indicator supersedes its predecessor. Each indicator carries a `status` of `ACTIVE` or `SUPERSEDED`; a newly filed row lands `ACTIVE`, and if an `ACTIVE` row already exists for the same area and the same `indicator_code`, that row moves to `SUPERSEDED` and the new row records the displaced row's identifier in `superseded_indicator_id`. When nothing was displaced, `superseded_indicator_id` is null. Each row's `area_version_after` records the screening area's `version` observed immediately after that row's most recent status change, so the newly filed row and the row it displaced both carry the area version that this filing produced.

**Both screening score variants are then recomputed from scratch.** The qualifying input set is identical for both: the area's indicator rows whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `vintage_year` is at least the clock year minus five and at most the clock year, both bounds inclusive. Superseded rows and out-of-window rows are excluded. Within that set, a category component is 0.00 when no qualifying row belongs to that category, and is otherwise the sum of the qualifying rows' percentile values in that category divided by the number of those rows, rounded half up to two decimals — an unweighted mean, so no tie-break is needed. The statewide variant reads each row's `statewide_percentile`; the county variant reads each row's `county_percentile`. The number of qualifying rows is written to `active_indicator_count` and is shared by both variants.

The combination step is deliberately non-linear: the pollution and health burden is **scaled by** the demographic burden. Take the pollution component multiplied by 0.55, add the health component multiplied by 0.45, then multiply the result by the demographic component divided by 100; round that half up to two decimals to obtain the pre-penalty score. Then add the enforcement penalty — the area's `enforcement_burden_points` multiplied by 0.50 — take the smaller of 100.00 and that sum, and round half up to two decimals for the final score. The statewide pass writes `ej_screen_score_statewide` and also stores its three category means in `pollution_component_statewide`, `health_component_statewide` and `demographic_component_statewide`; the county pass writes `ej_screen_score_county`. Because the two passes read different percentile columns from identical rows, they generally produce different numbers, and an area can be designated on the statewide basis while its complaints score modestly on the county basis. The statewide score is the only one that drives designation, applicability snapshots and cumulative impact; the county score is used for nothing but complaint inspection priority.

Designation follows immediately from the statewide score, compared against the stored two-decimal value with no re-rounding. A score of **at least 80.00** designates the area `SEVERELY_OVERBURDENED`; otherwise a score of **at least 65.00** designates it `OVERBURDENED`; otherwise it is `UNDESIGNATED`. Both comparisons are inclusive, so a score of exactly 65.00 designates the area.

Four designation transitions are declared, and each carries its own consequences. Moving `UNDESIGNATED → OVERBURDENED` (statewide score at least 65.00 and below 80.00) escalates every application in `STANDARD_REVIEW` at a facility in the area onto the enhanced track, recomputes the required comment-period length on every participation plan of the area's applications whose status is `PLANNED`, `OPEN` or `EXTENDED`, and increments the area's `version` by 1. Moving `OVERBURDENED → SEVERELY_OVERBURDENED` (score at least 80.00) recomputes the required comment-period length on those same plans, lowers the area's concurrent-open-period cap to one for future openings, and increments the area's `version` by 1. Moving `SEVERELY_OVERBURDENED → OVERBURDENED` (score back to at least 65.00 and below 80.00, as happens when a superseding indicator lowers the score) recomputes the required comment-period length on those plans and increments the area's `version` by 1. Moving `OVERBURDENED → UNDESIGNATED` (score below 65.00) leaves applications that are already on the enhanced track with `review_track` `ENHANCED` — de-designation never downgrades an in-flight review — and increments the area's `version` by 1. Because a superseding indicator can push the score either way, the cycle from `UNDESIGNATED` to `OVERBURDENED` and back to `UNDESIGNATED` is reachable.

Filing an indicator therefore fans out: the predecessor row is superseded, both score variants and `active_indicator_count` are recomputed, designation is re-evaluated with the escalation consequences above (each newly escalated application also acquires an applicability determination and a participation plan, as described in §3.2 and §3.4), the priority score of every complaint in the area whose status is `RECEIVED` or `TRIAGED` is recomputed against the new county score, and the area's `version` increments by 1.

The second screening dimension, `data_status`, tracks whether the burden data can still be trusted. It begins `CURRENT`. A confirmed enforcement violation at any facility in the area drives it `CURRENT → STALE` through the cascade described in §3.8. Only a `ProgramAdmin` can bring it back: **recertifying** a screening area moves `data_status` `STALE → CURRENT`, recomputes both screening score variants, re-evaluates designation, and increments the area's `version` by 1. Recertification of an area whose `data_status` is already `CURRENT` is refused with `ERR_AREA_NOT_STALE`. An area's indicators can be listed for review, filtered by indicator `status` or by `indicator_code`.

### 3.2. Facilities, Filing, and the Applicability Screen

Regulated sources are registered by a `ProgramAdmin`, who supplies the `screening_area_id` of the block group containing the facility, the facility `name`, its `facility_type`, and `distance_to_area_centroid_m` — the whole number of metres from the facility's stack to the block-group centroid, at least 0. Six facility types are recognised: `MAJOR_SOURCE`, `INCINERATOR`, `LANDFILL`, `CHEMICAL_BULK_STORAGE`, `MINOR_SOURCE` and `AUTO_BODY_SHOP`. A facility starts with `existing_load_tpy` at 0.00, `compliance_status` at `IN_COMPLIANCE` and `confirmed_violation_count` at 0. Facilities in an area can be listed and filtered by `facility_type`.

An `ApplicantRepresentative` files a permit application for its own facility by supplying `facility_id`, the `permit_type` requested (`NEW_SOURCE`, `MODIFICATION` or `RENEWAL`), `proposed_emissions_tpy` (strictly greater than 0, two decimals), `proposed_operating_hours_per_year` (a whole number from 1 through 8760 inclusive), and optionally `prior_application_id` naming the predecessor filing this application replaces, which is null for a first filing. A new application lands in `status` `DRAFT` with `review_track` `UNDETERMINED`, `revision_round` 0, `screening_stale_flag` false, `pre_mitigation_index` 0.00, `total_mitigation_credit_tpy` 0.00, `residual_impact_index` 0.00 and `approved_net_emissions_tpy` 0.00. Applications naming a given application as their predecessor can be listed as that application's revisions, and a facility's applications can be listed and filtered by `status` or by `review_track`.

The applicant then **submits** the application, moving it `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`; only the owning representative may do so, only from `DRAFT`, and the transition increments the application's `version` by 1. Any other originating state yields `ERR_INVALID_APPLICATION_STATE`.

Screening is the fork in the road, and it belongs to the `EJAnalyst`. Running the screen on a `SUBMITTED` application evaluates five enhanced-review triggers against the application's facility, that facility's screening area, and the application's own proposed emissions at screen time. The triggers are appended to `trigger_codes` in exactly this order:

- `FACILITY_TYPE`, when the facility's `facility_type` is one of `MAJOR_SOURCE`, `INCINERATOR`, `LANDFILL` or `CHEMICAL_BULK_STORAGE`. The two remaining types, `MINOR_SOURCE` and `AUTO_BODY_SHOP`, are not facility-type triggers.
- `PROXIMITY`, when `distance_to_area_centroid_m` is **at most 800** metres — a facility exactly 800 metres from the centroid fires the trigger.
- `DESIGNATION`, when the area's `designation` is `OVERBURDENED` or `SEVERELY_OVERBURDENED`.
- `EMISSIONS_THRESHOLD`, when `proposed_emissions_tpy` is **at least 25.00** tons per year — exactly 25.00 fires the trigger.
- `PRIOR_VIOLATION`, when the facility's `compliance_status` is `VIOLATION_ON_RECORD`.

The screen writes an **ApplicabilityDetermination** recording `permit_application_id`, the fired `trigger_codes`, the `result`, an `area_designation_snapshot` of the area's designation and an `ej_screen_score_snapshot` of its statewide score at determination time, the application's `revision_round` at determination time, `determined_by_user_id` set to the screening analyst's `X-User-ID`, and `application_version_after`. If no trigger fires, `result` is `STANDARD` with an empty `trigger_codes` list, the application's `review_track` becomes `STANDARD`, and it moves `SUBMITTED → STANDARD_REVIEW`. On that branch neither a participation plan nor a cumulative impact assessment is required, and the application can go straight on to a decision. If at least one trigger fires, `result` is `ENHANCED_REQUIRED`, `review_track` becomes `ENHANCED`, the application moves `SUBMITTED → ENHANCED_REVIEW`, and the divergent side effect kicks in: a participation plan is created in `PLANNED` with its required comment days computed, unless the application already has a participation plan whose status is anything other than `CANCELLED`, in which case that existing plan's required comment days are recomputed instead. Either branch increments the application's `version` by 1. Screening an application that is not `SUBMITTED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_APPLICATION_STATE`.

The same escalation can arrive without an analyst asking for it. When a screening area's designation escalates while an application at one of its facilities sits in `STANDARD_REVIEW`, the server itself writes an applicability determination with `result` `ENHANCED_REQUIRED`, `trigger_codes` including `DESIGNATION`, and `determined_by_user_id` set to the literal string `"system"`; sets `review_track` to `ENHANCED`; creates a participation plan in `PLANNED` with its required comment days computed; moves the application `STANDARD_REVIEW → ENHANCED_REVIEW`; and increments the application's `version` by 1.

Advancing to a decision is also the analyst's act. A `STANDARD_REVIEW` application whose `review_track` is `STANDARD` moves `STANDARD_REVIEW → PENDING_DECISION` with nothing more than a version increment. An `ENHANCED_REVIEW` application moves `ENHANCED_REVIEW → PENDING_DECISION` only when its governing cumulative impact assessment exists and is `ACCEPTED` — otherwise `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_ACCEPTED` — and only when the application's non-cancelled participation plan is `CLOSED` — otherwise `ERR_PARTICIPATION_NOT_CLOSED`. The enhanced advance recomputes the application's `residual_impact_index` before incrementing its `version` by 1.

### 3.3. Cumulative Impact Assessment and Independent QA

The heart of enhanced review is the **cumulative impact assessment**, which combines what the community already carries with what the applicant proposes to add. An `EJAnalyst` authors one by naming the `permit_application_id`, choosing a `method`, and passing the `expected_application_version` guard. Two methods exist. `SCREENING_LEVEL` is the default methodology and needs no modelling inputs. `REFINED` is used when dispersion modelling has been submitted, and it requires **both** `dispersion_factor` (a modelled attenuation between 0.10 and 1.00 inclusive) and `receptor_distance_m` (the whole number of metres to the nearest sensitive receptor, at least 0); a refined assessment missing either input is refused with `ERR_REFINED_INPUTS_REQUIRED`. Both fields default to null and stay null for a screening-level assessment. The creating analyst's `X-User-ID` is stamped into `author_user_id`, `qa_user_id` starts null, and the assessment lands in `status` `DRAFT`.

The index is computed in a pinned order. First, the assessment snapshots the area's current statewide screening score into `baseline_burden_score`. Second, `proposed_increment_pct` is the application's `proposed_emissions_tpy` divided by the area's `baseline_load_tpy`, multiplied by 100, rounded half up to two decimals. Third, `exposure_factor` is determined by method: for `SCREENING_LEVEL` it is pinned at exactly 1.0000, so the full proposed increment is weighted; for `REFINED` a distance decay is chosen first — 1.00 when `receptor_distance_m` is at most 300 metres, otherwise 0.75 when it is at most 800 metres, otherwise 0.50 — and `exposure_factor` is `dispersion_factor` multiplied by that decay, rounded half up to four decimals. Fourth, the weighted increment is `proposed_increment_pct` multiplied by `exposure_factor` multiplied by 1.50, rounded half up to two decimals. Fifth, the pre-clamp index is `baseline_burden_score` multiplied by 0.70, plus the weighted increment, rounded half up to two decimals. Finally `cumulative_impact_index` is the smaller of 100.00 and that pre-clamp value, rounded half up to two decimals. Rounding happens at exactly those four marked points and nowhere else. Because the refined variant attenuates the increment, identical application and area inputs yield a strictly lower index under `REFINED` than under `SCREENING_LEVEL`, and the refined variant can flip a disproportionate finding from true to false.

Two derived findings accompany the index. `disproportionate_flag` is true when `cumulative_impact_index` is **at least 70.00**. `data_confidence` is `LOW` when the area has no qualifying indicators at all — an `active_indicator_count` of 0 — or when the area's `data_status` is `STALE`; otherwise it is `HIGH`. Low confidence is not itself a bar to assessment, but it is one of the three grounds on which a permit may later be denied.

Creating a new assessment for an application that already has one in `DRAFT` or `RETURNED` moves that older row `DRAFT → SUPERSEDED` or `RETURNED → SUPERSEDED` and increments its `version` by 1; the new row then rewrites the application's `pre_mitigation_index` and `residual_impact_index`, increments the application's `version` by 1, and stamps `application_version_after`.

The QA loop is a three-state cycle. The authoring analyst submits a `DRAFT` assessment for review, moving it `DRAFT → SUBMITTED_FOR_QA`; this is permitted only while the owning application's `status` is `ENHANCED_REVIEW` or `PENDING_DECISION`, and it increments the assessment's `version` by 1. A `QAReviewer` then records an `outcome` of either `ACCEPT` or `RETURN`, optionally with a `note`, and in either case their `X-User-ID` is written to `qa_user_id` and the assessment's `version` increments by 1. On `RETURN`, the assessment moves `SUBMITTED_FOR_QA → RETURNED` and nothing else changes. On `ACCEPT`, the assessment moves `SUBMITTED_FOR_QA → ACCEPTED` and four further things happen: any other assessment of the same application that was `ACCEPTED` moves `ACCEPTED → SUPERSEDED`; the application's `pre_mitigation_index` is set to this assessment's `cumulative_impact_index` and its `residual_impact_index` is recomputed; the required comment days on the application's participation plan are recomputed when that plan's status is `PLANNED`, `OPEN` or `EXTENDED`; and the application's `version` increments by 1 alongside the assessment's. An analyst closes the cycle by **revising** a `RETURNED` assessment, moving it `RETURNED → DRAFT`, re-stamping `author_user_id` with the reviser's `X-User-ID`, and recomputing the index against the current method inputs (`method`, `dispersion_factor` and `receptor_distance_m` may all be re-supplied) and the area's current scores. Submitting, deciding on or revising an assessment from any other state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_ASSESSMENT_STATE`.

Because several assessments can coexist, the system needs one rule for **which** assessment governs the file. Candidates are the application's assessments whose `status` is `ACCEPTED`, `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED_FOR_QA` or `RETURNED`; rows in `SUPERSEDED`, `INVALIDATED` or `VOIDED` are never candidates. Candidates are ranked with `ACCEPTED` ahead of all three of the in-progress statuses, and within a rank by `created_at` descending — newest first — with a final tie broken by `version` descending. The first row under that ordering governs. When no candidate exists there is no governing assessment, and both `pre_mitigation_index` and `residual_impact_index` are 0.00. The governing assessment supplies `pre_mitigation_index` by direct copy of its `cumulative_impact_index`, with no re-rounding.

Two events can knock a live assessment out from under a file. When the analyst returns the application for revision, every assessment of that application in `SUBMITTED_FOR_QA` or `ACCEPTED` moves to `INVALIDATED`. When an enforcement violation drives the area's `data_status` to `STALE`, every `ACCEPTED` assessment belonging to an application at a facility in that area moves `ACCEPTED → INVALIDATED`. In both cases each invalidated row increments its own `version` by 1, and the owning application's `pre_mitigation_index` and `residual_impact_index` are recomputed against whatever now governs. Assessments in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED_FOR_QA`, `RETURNED`, `ACCEPTED` or `INVALIDATED` all move to `VOIDED` when the application is resubmitted or withdrawn, again with a version increment on each, and again recomputing the application's indices. `SUPERSEDED` and `VOIDED` are terminal.

### 3.4. Enhanced Public Participation: Comment Periods and Translated Materials

Every enhanced-track application gets a **participation plan**, created by the system rather than by a person: it names the application through `permit_application_id` and lands in `status` `PLANNED` with `granted_extension_days` 0, `comment_start_date` and `comment_end_date` both null, `translated_languages` an empty array, `materials_translated` false, `meeting_count` 0, `comment_count` 0, `adequacy_status` `PENDING` and `deficiency_codes` an empty array.

The plan's `required_comment_days` is where community burden turns into procedural time. Start from a base of **30 days**. Add 15 days when the area's `designation` is `OVERBURDENED`. Add 30 days when it is `SEVERELY_OVERBURDENED`. Add a further 15 days when the area's `translation_required` flag is true. Add 10 more days when a governing assessment exists and its `cumulative_impact_index` is at least 70.00. Then take the smaller of the accumulated total and the ceiling of **120 days**. This figure is recomputed at plan creation, on the designation cascade, on assessment acceptance, and when an extension is granted.

The write scope of the end date is pinned, and this distinction matters. `comment_end_date` is written by exactly three things: opening the period, extending it, and the reopen cascade. A later recomputation of `required_comment_days` — from a screen, a designation cascade or an assessment acceptance — rewrites `required_comment_days` **only** and never moves an already-set `comment_end_date`.

A `CommunityLiaison` runs the period. Before or during it, the liaison records language access on the plan by supplying `translated_languages` (the ISO 639-1 codes the plan's materials are translated into) and `materials_translated` (the liaison's attestation that the translated materials were actually published); each such update increments the plan's `version` by 1, and the update is unavailable once the plan is `CANCELLED`, which is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PLAN_STATE`. **Opening** the period moves the plan `PLANNED → OPEN`: the liaison supplies `comment_start_date`, which must be on or after the clock date — an earlier date is refused with `ERR_COMMENT_START_IN_PAST` — and the server derives `comment_end_date` as `comment_start_date` plus `required_comment_days` plus `granted_extension_days`, minus one day, so that the window is a closed interval inclusive of both its first and last day. Opening increments the area's `open_plan_count` by 1 and the plan's `version` by 1.

Comment periods compete for the community's attention, so the area caps how many can run at once over overlapping windows. The cap is **two** concurrent periods normally and drops to **one** when the area's `designation` is `SEVERELY_OVERBURDENED`. The count is taken over other participation plans — excluding the plan being opened or extended — whose `status` is `OPEN` or `EXTENDED` and whose application's facility sits in the same screening area, and whose own closed comment window intersects the candidate window. This geometry is a **closed** date interval on both sides, so two windows that merely share a single endpoint day **do** collide. If the count of intersecting concurrent plans plus one would exceed the cap, the request is refused with `ERR_AREA_ENGAGEMENT_CAPACITY`. The check runs both on opening and on extension.

**Extending** a period is the plan's self-loop: from `OPEN` it moves `OPEN → EXTENDED`, and from `EXTENDED` it moves `EXTENDED → EXTENDED`, so extensions may be granted repeatedly. The liaison supplies `additional_days` and a `reason`. The sum of `required_comment_days`, the current `granted_extension_days` and the requested `additional_days` may not exceed the 120-day ceiling; exceeding it is refused with `ERR_COMMENT_DAYS_CAP_EXCEEDED`. A permitted extension adds `additional_days` to `granted_extension_days`, adds the same number of days to `comment_end_date`, and increments the plan's `version` by 1.

**Closing** the period moves the plan `OPEN → CLOSED` or `EXTENDED → CLOSED`, and is permitted only once the clock date is strictly later than `comment_end_date`; closing on or before the last day of the window is refused with `ERR_COMMENT_PERIOD_NOT_ELAPSED`. Closing decrements the area's `open_plan_count` by 1, increments the plan's `version` by 1, and — critically — evaluates participation adequacy.

Adequacy is a four-part audit whose findings are appended to `deficiency_codes` in exactly this order. First, the actual window length, computed as `comment_end_date` minus `comment_start_date` plus one day, is compared with `required_comment_days`; if the actual window is shorter than required, `SHORT_COMMENT_PERIOD` is appended. Second, the plan's accessible meetings are counted: public meetings belonging to this plan whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`, whose `accessibility_verified` is true, and whose `ends_at` is at or before the current logical clock instant — that is, meetings that have actually happened. If that count is zero, `NO_ACCESSIBLE_MEETING` is appended. Third, when the area's `translation_required` is true, `MISSING_TRANSLATION` is appended if `materials_translated` is false or if any code in the area's `required_translation_languages` is absent from the plan's `translated_languages`. Fourth, if any public comment filed against this plan has `in_required_language` false, `LANGUAGE_ACCESS_GAP` is appended. If `deficiency_codes` ends up empty, `adequacy_status` is `COMPLIANT`; otherwise it is `DEFICIENT`. An enhanced-track permit cannot be issued unless the plan closed `COMPLIANT`, and a `DEFICIENT` finding is one of the three supported grounds for denial.

A closed period can be reopened, but never by a person directly: reopening is a cascade from an applicant's resubmission or from a manager's rescission of an issued permit, and it moves the plan `CLOSED → OPEN`. The reopen grants headroom-limited extra time. Compute the remaining headroom as 120 minus the sum of `required_comment_days` and `granted_extension_days`; the days added are the smaller of **15** and that headroom, floored at zero so headroom can never subtract. Those added days are added to `granted_extension_days`, and the new `comment_end_date` is the later of the current clock date and the existing `comment_end_date`, plus the added days. When headroom is zero the plan reopens with no added days and the end date becomes the later of the clock date and the existing end date, unchanged by addition. Reopening also resets `adequacy_status` to `PENDING` and `deficiency_codes` to an empty array, increments the area's `open_plan_count` by 1, and increments the plan's `version` by 1 — the `OPEN → CLOSED → OPEN` cycle is a normal part of a contested file's life.

Finally, a withdrawal of the underlying application cancels the plan outright, from `PLANNED`, `OPEN`, `EXTENDED` or `CLOSED` to `CANCELLED`, which is terminal. Every scheduled meeting of the plan is cancelled with it, each releasing its venue slot; the area's `open_plan_count` is decremented by 1 when the plan's prior status was `OPEN` or `EXTENDED` (and is left alone when the plan was `PLANNED` or `CLOSED`); and the plan's `version` increments by 1. Opening, extending or closing a plan that is not in the declared originating state is refused with `ERR_INVALID_PLAN_STATE`.

### 3.5. Accessible Meeting Locations and Comment Intake

Venues are reference data maintained by a `ProgramAdmin`, who registers each with a `name`, an `address`, a seated `capacity` of at least 1, and three accessibility attributes: `transit_accessible` (within 400 metres of a scheduled transit stop), `ada_accessible` (meets ADA physical-access requirements) and `interpretation_capable` (equipped for simultaneous interpretation). A venue's `status` may be supplied at registration and defaults to `ACTIVE`; the alternative is `DECOMMISSIONED`. Each venue carries a slot-occupancy ledger, `booked_meeting_count`, which starts at 0 and always equals the number of meetings currently `SCHEDULED` at that venue. A venue's meetings can be listed and filtered by meeting `status`.

A `CommunityLiaison` books a public meeting by naming the `participation_plan_id` whose comment period it serves and the `venue_id` reserved, together with `starts_at`, `ends_at` (which must be strictly later than `starts_at`) and optionally `interpretation_languages`, the ISO 639-1 codes to be interpreted live, defaulting to an empty array. Four gates apply, in the precedence given in §2.10. The meeting must sit inside the comment window: both the `starts_at` date and the `ends_at` date must fall within the plan's closed comment window, inclusive of both ends, or the booking is refused with `ERR_MEETING_OUTSIDE_COMMENT_WINDOW`. The venue's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, or the booking is refused with `ERR_VENUE_DECOMMISSIONED`. The venue must be genuinely reachable: both `ada_accessible` and `transit_accessible` must be true, or the booking is refused with `ERR_VENUE_NOT_ACCESSIBLE`. And the slot must be free.

Slot conflict uses a **half-open** interval geometry with a symmetric turnover buffer of **60 minutes**. The candidate interval runs from 60 minutes before the requested `starts_at` up to, but not including, 60 minutes after the requested `ends_at`. Each existing meeting at the same venue whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` occupies the interval from its own `starts_at` up to, but not including, its own `ends_at` — no buffer is added on the stored side. If the candidate interval and any occupied interval overlap on a non-empty span, the booking is refused with `ERR_VENUE_SLOT_CONFLICT`. Because the intervals are half-open, touching endpoints do not collide: a candidate whose buffered start falls exactly on an existing meeting's `ends_at` is accepted, so a gap of exactly 60 minutes between two meetings is legal. Note that this is deliberately different arithmetic from the area engagement capacity check of §3.4, which uses closed date intervals in which touching endpoints do collide.

A booked meeting lands in `status` `SCHEDULED` with `accessibility_verified` set from the venue's flags — true when the venue had both `ada_accessible` and `transit_accessible` true at booking time. Booking increments the venue's `booked_meeting_count` by 1 and the plan's `meeting_count` by 1, and later mutations of the meeting increment its own `version` by 1. The liaison may cancel a scheduled meeting, optionally supplying a `reason`; the meeting moves `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED`, the venue's `booked_meeting_count` and the plan's `meeting_count` are each decremented by 1, and the buffer-expanded interval is released immediately so a previously conflicting booking now succeeds. Cancelling an already-cancelled meeting is refused with `ERR_INVALID_MEETING_STATE`. A withdrawal cascade cancels scheduled meetings the same way, with the same two decrements and the same version increment.

Public comments come from either a `PublicCommenter` or a `CommunityLiaison` filing on the public's behalf. A comment names the `participation_plan_id`, the `submitter_name` given by the commenter, the `language` it was written in (a two-character lower-case ISO 639-1 code), the `body` text (between 1 and 4000 characters), and optionally `requires_response`, the commenter's request for a written agency response, defaulting to false. Comments are accepted only while the plan's status is `OPEN` or `EXTENDED` **and** the clock date lies within the comment window inclusive of both ends; anything else is refused with `ERR_COMMENT_PERIOD_CLOSED`. On acceptance the server computes `in_required_language`, which is true when the comment's `language` is `"en"` or is one of the plan's `translated_languages` at filing time, and false otherwise; the default for the field is true. Filing increments the plan's `comment_count` by 1. A plan's comments can be listed and filtered by `language`. Public comments are immutable records: like applicability determinations, they carry only `id` and `created_at`.

### 3.6. Permit Conditions and Residual Impact Mitigation

Enhanced review exists to produce conditions, and conditions exist to reduce impact. An `EJAnalyst` attaches a condition to an application by naming `permit_application_id`, choosing a `condition_type`, and passing the `expected_application_version` guard. Three condition families are recognised, and the family determines how much credit the condition earns. An `ADDITIONAL_CONTROL` condition is an emissions control requirement. A `MONITORING` condition requires a `monitoring_frequency` of `CONTINUOUS`, `QUARTERLY` or `ANNUAL`. A `COMMUNITY_BENEFIT` condition requires a `benefit_value_usd` figure of at least 0 with two decimals, recording the committed community benefit value. Both of those requirements are strict: a monitoring condition without `monitoring_frequency`, or a community-benefit condition without `benefit_value_usd`, is refused with `ERR_CONDITION_INPUTS_REQUIRED`. Both fields default to null. Every condition also carries `reduction_tpy`, the claimed emissions reduction in tons per year (at least 0, two decimals, defaulting to 0.00) and `enforceable`, a boolean recording whether the condition is written as a federally enforceable limit, defaulting to false. A condition lands in `status` `ACTIVE` and stamps `application_version_after`. Conditions may only be attached or voided while the owning application's `status` is `STANDARD_REVIEW`, `ENHANCED_REVIEW` or `PENDING_DECISION`; outside that set the request is refused with `ERR_INVALID_APPLICATION_STATE`.

Credit is earned per condition and stored on the condition as `credit_tpy`. Multiply the condition's `reduction_tpy` by its family's efficacy factor — **1.00** for `ADDITIONAL_CONTROL`, **0.20** for `MONITORING` and **0.10** for `COMMUNITY_BENEFIT` — and then by an enforceability factor of 1.00 when `enforceable` is true or **0.50** when it is false; round the product half up to two decimals. The application's `total_mitigation_credit_tpy` is then the sum of the `credit_tpy` values of the application's `ACTIVE` conditions only, accumulated in `created_at` ascending order with ties broken by `version` ascending, and rounded half up to two decimals. **The rounding order is pinned: round each condition's credit first, then sum the rounded values.** The alternative reading — summing the raw products and rounding once at the end — is not used and produces a different number whenever the raw products carry a third decimal. Voided conditions are excluded from the sum entirely.

From the credit total comes the application's `approved_net_emissions_tpy` figure: the application's `proposed_emissions_tpy` minus `total_mitigation_credit_tpy`, floored at 0.00, rounded half up to two decimals. That figure is derived when a decision is taken rather than carried live on the application row: the application's stored `approved_net_emissions_tpy` stays 0.00 from filing until an issuance sets it (§3.7), and a rescission returns it to 0.00. Recomputing the credit aggregation — on condition attachment, on condition voiding, on withdrawal, or in any other cascade — rewrites `total_mitigation_credit_tpy` and `residual_impact_index` only, and never the stored `approved_net_emissions_tpy`.

The **residual impact index** is the number the agency's denial authority rests on, and it is computed in a pinned order that resolves what looks like a circular dependency. Begin with the application's `pre_mitigation_index`, taken from the governing assessment. Compute the credit ratio as `total_mitigation_credit_tpy` divided by `proposed_emissions_tpy`, rounded half up to four decimals, then capped at 1.0000. Select the mitigation cap from the **pre-mitigation** index, never from the residual it produces: the cap is 0.60 when `pre_mitigation_index` is at least 75.00, otherwise 0.75 when it is at least 50.00, otherwise 1.00; both comparisons are inclusive. The effective reduction is the smaller of the credit ratio and the cap. Finally, `residual_impact_index` is `pre_mitigation_index` multiplied by one minus the effective reduction, rounded half up to two decimals. In plain terms: a community already carrying a very high pre-mitigation burden can have at most 60 percent of that burden mitigated away on paper, no matter how much credit the conditions claim.

Recomputation is aggressive by design. Attaching a condition recomputes the credit aggregation and the residual index, increments the application's `version` by 1 and stamps `application_version_after`. Voiding a condition moves it `ACTIVE → VOIDED`, recomputes the same two figures and increments the application's `version` by 1; voiding an already-voided condition is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CONDITION_STATE`. The residual index is also recomputed after any change to the governing assessment and on advance-to-decision. An application's conditions can be listed and filtered by condition `status`.

### 3.7. Issuance, Denial Authority and Rescission

A `PermitManager` decides a `PENDING_DECISION` application by naming its `permit_application_id` and a `disposition` of `ISSUE` or `DENY`. The manager must be independent of the assessment chain: an `X-User-ID` equal to the governing assessment's `author_user_id` or its `qa_user_id` is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The decision record carries `residual_index_at_decision`, a snapshot of the application's `residual_impact_index` at decision time, and `decided_by_user_id`, the deciding manager's `X-User-ID`.

**Issuance** must clear five substantive gates in addition to the common ladder. On the enhanced track the governing assessment must exist and be `ACCEPTED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_ACCEPTED`. On the enhanced track the participation plan's `adequacy_status` must be `COMPLIANT`, so both a `DEFICIENT` finding and a still-`PENDING` one block issuance, refused with `ERR_PARTICIPATION_INADEQUATE`. The application's `residual_impact_index` must be **strictly below 60.00**; an impact that mitigation could not bring under that threshold may not be permitted, and the refusal carries `ERR_DISPROPORTIONATE_IMPACT_UNMITIGATED`. In a designated area — `OVERBURDENED` or `SEVERELY_OVERBURDENED` — the request must carry `community_benefit_base_usd`, or it is refused with `ERR_BENEFIT_BASE_REQUIRED`, and the resulting community benefit obligation must meet its minimum, or it is refused with `ERR_BENEFIT_COMMITMENT_INSUFFICIENT`. Finally the area load cap of §2.12 must hold, or the request is refused with `ERR_AREA_LOAD_CAP_EXCEEDED`.

The community benefit obligation is computed in a pinned order, and its tier selection is deliberately keyed on the input rather than the output. Begin from the requested `community_benefit_base_usd` and compute the application's `approved_net_emissions_tpy` from the mitigation credit aggregation. **Select the surcharge rate from the base**, never from the total the surcharge feeds: 0.12 when the base is at least 500000.00 US dollars, otherwise 0.08 when it is at least 100000.00, otherwise 0.05. Then choose a designation multiplier of 1.25 when the area's `designation` is `SEVERELY_OVERBURDENED`, 1.10 when it is `OVERBURDENED`, and 1.00 when it is `UNDESIGNATED`. `community_benefit_surcharge_usd` is the base multiplied by the surcharge rate multiplied by the designation multiplier, rounded half up to two decimals, and `community_benefit_total_usd` is the base plus that surcharge, rounded half up to two decimals. The minimum required commitment is **1,500.00 US dollars per approved net ton** — that is, 1500.00 multiplied by `approved_net_emissions_tpy`, rounded half up to two decimals — and the fiscal gate applies only when the area's `designation` is not `UNDESIGNATED`: a `community_benefit_total_usd` below the minimum is refused with `ERR_BENEFIT_COMMITMENT_INSUFFICIENT`. When the area is undesignated the surcharge is still computed with a multiplier of 1.00 and the gate does not apply.

A cleared issuance moves the application `PENDING_DECISION → ISSUED` and writes a decision in `status` `ISSUED` carrying the approved net emissions, both benefit amounts and the residual snapshot. The application's own `approved_net_emissions_tpy` is set to its `proposed_emissions_tpy` minus `total_mitigation_credit_tpy`, floored at 0.00 and rounded half up to two decimals; the facility's `existing_load_tpy` and the area's `permitted_load_tpy` are each increased by that amount; and the application's `version` increments by 1.

**Denial** is the agency's substantive power, and it must be justified. A `DENY` disposition requires a `denial_basis`, and the basis must be supported by the record on exactly one of three grounds: `UNMITIGABLE_DISPROPORTIONATE_IMPACT` requires the application's `residual_impact_index` to be **at least 60.00**; `INADEQUATE_PARTICIPATION` requires the participation plan's `adequacy_status` to be `DEFICIENT`; and `INCOMPLETE_ASSESSMENT` requires the governing assessment's `data_confidence` to be `LOW`. A basis the record does not support is refused with `ERR_DENIAL_BASIS_UNSUPPORTED`. A supported denial moves the application `PENDING_DECISION → DENIED`, which is terminal, and writes a decision in `status` `DENIED` with `approved_net_emissions_tpy` 0.00 and the residual snapshot recorded. Denial books nothing: the facility's `existing_load_tpy` and the area's `permitted_load_tpy` are left exactly as they were. The application's `version` increments by 1. Note that `community_benefit_base_usd` is not required on a denial, and both `denial_basis` and `community_benefit_base_usd` default to null.

An issued permit is not beyond recall. Only a decision in `status` `ISSUED` may be **rescinded** — a decision created directly in `DENIED` is terminal, and rescinding anything other than an `ISSUED` decision is refused with `ERR_INVALID_DECISION_STATE`. Rescission requires a `reason`, moves the decision `ISSUED → RESCINDED`, and reverses both emissions ledgers: the facility's `existing_load_tpy` and the area's `permitted_load_tpy` are each decreased by the application's `approved_net_emissions_tpy`, which is then set to 0.00. The application returns to review along the track it was on: an `ENHANCED` application moves `ISSUED → ENHANCED_REVIEW` and a `STANDARD` one moves `ISSUED → STANDARD_REVIEW`. On the enhanced track only, two further cascades fire: the governing assessment moves `ACCEPTED → INVALIDATED`, and the application's `CLOSED` participation plan reopens to `OPEN` under the headroom-limited reopen rule of §3.4. Both the decision's `version` and the application's `version` increment by 1. An application's decisions can be listed for the file record.

### 3.8. Community Complaints, Priority Scoring and Inspections

Complaint intake is open to both a `PublicCommenter` and a `CommunityLiaison`. A complaint names the `screening_area_id` it concerns, optionally a `facility_id` for the suspected source (null when the complainant could not name one), a `complaint_type` from `ODOR`, `DUST`, `NOISE`, `WATER_DISCHARGE`, `ILLEGAL_DUMPING` or `SMOKE`, a `severity` of `LOW`, `MEDIUM` or `HIGH` as recorded by the intake taker, a `description` narrative between 1 and 2000 characters, and optionally `submitter_language`, the ISO 639-1 code the complaint was filed in, defaulting to `"en"`. A new complaint lands in `status` `RECEIVED` with `reopen_count` 0, is scored immediately, and increments the area's `open_complaint_count` by 1.

The priority score decides where an inspector goes next, and it is what ties inspection effort to community burden. First compute `repeat_count_180d`: the number of other complaints in the same `screening_area_id` with the same `complaint_type` whose `status` is not `DISMISSED`, excluding this complaint itself, whose `created_at` is at or after the current logical clock instant minus **180 days** (inclusive at that boundary) and strictly before this complaint's own `created_at` (so later filings never count toward an earlier complaint's repeat history). Then `priority_score` is the sum of five terms, rounded half up to two decimals: the area's **county** screening score multiplied by 0.40; severity points of 5 for `LOW`, 15 for `MEDIUM` or 30 for `HIGH`; the smaller of 20 and four points per prior repeat, so the repeat contribution saturates at five priors; a flat 10 points when a `facility_id` was named and that facility's `compliance_status` is `VIOLATION_ON_RECORD`; and a flat 5 points when the area's `designation` is `SEVERELY_OVERBURDENED`. From the score comes `priority_tier`: `TIER_1` when the score is at least 70.00, otherwise `TIER_2` when it is at least 45.00, otherwise `TIER_3`. Both comparisons are inclusive, and `priority_tier` defaults to `TIER_3`. Note that this is the one place the county screening variant is used; an area that is `OVERBURDENED` on the statewide basis may still see its complaints score modestly here. Complaints are rescored on intake, on triage acceptance, on reopening, and whenever an indicator filing or recertification changes the area's county score while the complaint's `status` is `RECEIVED` or `TRIAGED`. Complaints can be listed with filters on `screening_area_id`, `facility_id`, `status` and `priority_tier`.

An `EJAnalyst` triages a `RECEIVED` complaint with an `outcome` of `ACCEPT` or `DISMISS`. Accepting moves it `RECEIVED → TRIAGED`, recomputes `priority_score` and `priority_tier`, and increments the complaint's `version` by 1. Dismissing requires a `dismissal_reason`, moves the complaint `RECEIVED → DISMISSED` (terminal), decrements the area's `open_complaint_count` by 1, and increments the complaint's `version` by 1. Triaging a complaint that is not `RECEIVED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_COMPLAINT_STATE`.

Inspections are pulled from the queue rather than pushed. An `EJAnalyst` asks for the next assignment by supplying an `inspector_user_id` — the opaque identifier of the `Inspector` the visit is assigned to — a `scheduled_date` for the visit as an ISO date in UTC, and optionally a `screening_area_id` to narrow the queue to one block group. **Inspector day capacity is validated before selection:** if the named inspector already has three or more inspections with `status` `SCHEDULED` on that `scheduled_date`, the request is refused with `ERR_INSPECTOR_DAY_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` and nothing is selected. The candidate set is then the complaints whose `status` is `TRIAGED`, whose `facility_id` is not null, and for which no inspection row is currently `SCHEDULED`, narrowed to the requested `screening_area_id` when that field was supplied. Candidates are ranked by `priority_score` descending, ties broken by `created_at` ascending so the oldest filing wins, and any remaining tie broken by `version` ascending. If the candidate set is empty the request is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_COMPLAINT`, no inspection row is created and no complaint is mutated. When the request narrows to a single complaint that fails the inspectability test — not `TRIAGED`, no `facility_id`, or already carrying a scheduled inspection — the refusal is `ERR_COMPLAINT_NOT_INSPECTABLE`.

A successful assignment creates an inspection in `status` `SCHEDULED` naming the `community_complaint_id` that earned it, copying the selected complaint's `facility_id` into the inspection's own `facility_id`, recording `priority_score_at_assignment` as a snapshot of the winning complaint's `priority_score`, and leaving `finding` null. The complaint moves `TRIAGED → INSPECTION_ASSIGNED` and its `version` increments by 1. Inspections can be listed by the complaint that raised them or by the facility they visit, filtered by inspection `status`.

Only the assigned `Inspector` may complete the visit, recording a `finding` of `NO_VIOLATION` or `VIOLATION_CONFIRMED` and optionally `notes`. Completion moves the inspection `SCHEDULED → COMPLETED`, writes the finding, moves the complaint `INSPECTION_ASSIGNED → INSPECTED`, and increments both rows' `version` by 1. Completing an inspection that is not `SCHEDULED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_INSPECTION_STATE`.

A confirmed violation is the system's longest cascade, and it runs in four linked steps. **First**, the inspected facility's `compliance_status` flips to `VIOLATION_ON_RECORD` and its `confirmed_violation_count` increases by 1. **Second**, that facility change adds **5 enforcement points** to the area's `enforcement_burden_points`, which feeds the penalty term of both screening score variants; both scores are recomputed, `designation` is re-evaluated with all the consequences of §3.1, and `data_status` is driven to `STALE`. **Third**, the `STALE` data status invalidates every `ACCEPTED` cumulative impact assessment belonging to an application at any facility in that area, moving each `ACCEPTED → INVALIDATED`. **Fourth**, each application whose governing assessment was just invalidated sets `screening_stale_flag` to true, and any such application sitting in `PENDING_DECISION` regresses `PENDING_DECISION → ENHANCED_REVIEW` on the enhanced track, recomputing its `pre_mitigation_index` and `residual_impact_index` and incrementing its `version` by 1. This is the intended serialisation point between enforcement and permitting: a permit cannot be issued on the strength of screening data that an enforcement finding has just called into question. A `NO_VIOLATION` finding fires none of these four steps.

After inspection, the analyst closes the complaint with a `resolution` of `RESOLVED` or `REFERRED_ENFORCEMENT`, optionally with a `note`. `RESOLVED` moves the complaint `INSPECTED → RESOLVED`. `REFERRED_ENFORCEMENT` moves it `INSPECTED → REFERRED_ENFORCEMENT`, which is terminal, and is permitted only when the complaint's latest `COMPLETED` inspection recorded a `finding` of `VIOLATION_CONFIRMED`; an unsupported referral is refused with `ERR_REFERRAL_UNSUPPORTED`. Both resolutions decrement the area's `open_complaint_count` by 1 and increment the complaint's `version` by 1. A `CommunityLiaison` may reopen a `RESOLVED` complaint with a `reason`, moving it `RESOLVED → TRIAGED`, incrementing `reopen_count` by 1, incrementing the area's `open_complaint_count` by 1, recomputing `priority_score` and `priority_tier`, and incrementing the complaint's `version` by 1 — the `TRIAGED` through `RESOLVED` back to `TRIAGED` cycle lets a community push back on a closed file.

### 3.9. Revision Cycles, Withdrawal and Program Evaluation

Enhanced review is iterative. An `EJAnalyst` who finds the file inadequate returns it to the applicant with a `reason`, moving the application `ENHANCED_REVIEW → RETURNED_FOR_REVISION` or `PENDING_DECISION → RETURNED_FOR_REVISION`. The return invalidates the live analytical work: every assessment of the application whose `status` is `SUBMITTED_FOR_QA` or `ACCEPTED` moves to `INVALIDATED`, the application's `pre_mitigation_index` and `residual_impact_index` are recomputed against whatever now governs, and its `version` increments by 1.

The applicant closes the cycle by **resubmitting**, optionally revising `proposed_emissions_tpy` and `proposed_operating_hours_per_year` in the process. Only the owning representative may resubmit, and only from `RETURNED_FOR_REVISION`. Resubmission moves the application `RETURNED_FOR_REVISION → SUBMITTED`, increments `revision_round` by 1, clears `screening_stale_flag` to false, moves every assessment of the application in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED_FOR_QA`, `RETURNED`, `ACCEPTED` or `INVALIDATED` to `VOIDED`, reopens the application's `CLOSED` participation plan to `OPEN` under the headroom-limited reopen rule of §3.4, recomputes `pre_mitigation_index` and `residual_impact_index`, and increments the application's `version` by 1. The application is then screened afresh, so the full cycle reads `RETURNED_FOR_REVISION → SUBMITTED → ENHANCED_REVIEW`, with a new applicability determination stamped with the incremented `revision_round`. The self-referential `prior_application_id` link is a separate device: it records a wholly new filing that replaces an earlier one, rather than a revision round within a single file.

**Withdrawal** is the applicant's exit and is terminal. It is permitted from `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `STANDARD_REVIEW`, `ENHANCED_REVIEW`, `PENDING_DECISION` or `RETURNED_FOR_REVISION`, takes an optional `reason`, and moves the application to `WITHDRAWN`. On the standard track, where there is no plan, no assessment and possibly no condition, the effect is just the state change and a `version` increment. On the enhanced track the full cascade runs: the application's participation plan moves from `PLANNED`, `OPEN`, `EXTENDED` or `CLOSED` to `CANCELLED`; every `SCHEDULED` public meeting of that plan moves to `CANCELLED`, each decrementing its venue's `booked_meeting_count` by 1 and the plan's `meeting_count` by 1; every assessment in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED_FOR_QA`, `RETURNED` or `ACCEPTED` moves to `VOIDED`; every `ACTIVE` permit condition moves to `VOIDED`; the application's `total_mitigation_credit_tpy`, `pre_mitigation_index` and `residual_impact_index` are recomputed over what remains; the area's `open_plan_count` is recomputed; and the application's `version` increments by 1. Withdrawing from `ISSUED`, `DENIED` or `WITHDRAWN` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_APPLICATION_STATE`.

Finally, the program office must be able to show what all this achieved. A `ProgramAdmin` or `EJAnalyst` requests a program evaluation over decided applications, optionally narrowing it with `basis`, `period_start`, `period_end` and `designation`. The population is the applications whose `status` is `ISSUED` or `DENIED` whose facility's screening area carries a designation in the filter; the filter is the single supplied `designation` when one is given, and otherwise both `OVERBURDENED` and `SEVERELY_OVERBURDENED`. An application's governing decision is its decision whose `status` is `ISSUED` or `DENIED`, of which there is at most one; a `RESCINDED` decision is excluded from the population entirely, so a rescinded permit stops counting as issued.

The `basis` parameter selects **which date** the reporting window filters on, and the two variants can report different metrics over identical data. `DECISION_DATE`, the default used when `basis` is omitted, filters on the governing decision's `created_at` date. `APPLICATION_DATE` filters on the application's own `created_at` date, so an application filed before the window but decided inside it is excluded under this variant while being included under the other. The window is closed on both ends — a date equal to `period_start` or to `period_end` is inside it — and the defaults are 1970-01-01 for `period_start` and 9999-12-31 for `period_end`.

The report echoes the `basis` in use and returns seven figures. `total_decided` counts the population inside the window. `issued_count` and `denied_count` split it by the application's `status`. `denial_rate` is 0.0000 when `total_decided` is zero, and otherwise `denied_count` divided by `total_decided`, rounded half up to four decimals. `mean_residual_index` is 0.00 when `total_decided` is zero, and otherwise the sum of the in-window applications' `residual_impact_index` values divided by `total_decided`, rounded half up to two decimals. `total_mitigation_credit_tpy` is the sum of those applications' `total_mitigation_credit_tpy` values, rounded half up to two decimals. `total_community_benefit_usd` is the sum of `community_benefit_total_usd` over the issued decisions in the window, rounded half up to two decimals. And `mean_comment_days` is 0.00 when no enhanced-track application falls in the window, and otherwise the sum, over the in-window enhanced-track applications, of each plan's `required_comment_days` plus its `granted_extension_days`, divided by the count of those applications, rounded half up to two decimals.
