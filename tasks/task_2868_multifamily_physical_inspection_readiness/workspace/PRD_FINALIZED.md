# Multifamily Physical Inspection Readiness API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Assisted-housing owners live under a physical-inspection regime that is unforgiving in exactly the places where their own record-keeping is weakest. An inspection cycle begins with a published standard that fixes which defects are scoreable and how heavily each inspectable area weighs on the final number; it continues through a statistically drawn sample of dwelling units, tenant notices that must reach households far enough in advance to be lawful, and an on-site walk that can be derailed by a single household refusing entry; and it ends in a score that determines whether the owner walks away clean or owes a corrective action plan, a follow-up inspection, and a capital-needs justification for the recurring problems the agency has now seen twice. Owners who go into that cycle blind lose points they could have prevented. Owners who sweep their own portfolio first, generate work orders from what they find, and prove those work orders were independently verified closed, go in with a defensible posture.

This document specifies the Multifamily Physical Inspection Readiness API: the system of record for that entire arc. It serves four constituencies whose interests deliberately do not align. The housing agency's inspectors need an unfalsifiable record of what was observed, in which unit, against which published defect definition, with the arithmetic of the score reproducible from the stored rows alone. Owners need a self-assessment channel that is genuinely theirs — sweeps, findings, work orders, corrections — plus a real avenue to contest inspector error. Independent reviewers need the assurance that nobody verified their own repair and nobody adjudicated their own plan. And the platform operator needs a curated catalogue of standards and defect definitions that cannot be quietly edited under a live inspection.

Three design commitments run through everything that follows. First, **pinning**: an inspection captures the standard that scores it at creation and never re-reads it, so publishing a newer standard mid-cycle cannot retroactively move a score. Second, **posture-driven gating**: a property carries a continuously re-derived readiness posture, computed from its open and overdue deficiencies, and that posture is what blocks or permits sampling and inspection start, and what escalates outstanding work orders. Third, **explicit evaluation order**: wherever two rules could plausibly fire on the same request, or wherever rounding could be applied at two different points in a formula, this document pins the order rather than leaving it to the implementer. Every threshold, boundary inclusivity, tie-break and rounding step below is normative.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

Every collection in this API is mounted directly under the service root: there are no nested collection paths, and each resource is addressed by its own identifier. Relationships between resources travel through request-body identifiers and query filters, never through URL nesting.

### 2.1. Actors and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers. `X-User-ID` identifies the acting principal and `X-User-Role` declares the capacity in which it acts. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the six declared roles, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If the headers are well-formed but the declared role is not listed among the roles authorised for the target endpoint, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403). These two checks run before any other validation in the system: authentication first, then endpoint authorisation.

The six roles are `PropertyOwner`, `HousingAgencyInspector`, `MaintenanceTechnician`, `QualityAssuranceReviewer`, `AppealsOfficer` and `PlatformAdmin`.

A `PropertyOwner` is the assisted-housing owner accountable for self-assessment, tenant notification, deficiency correction, appeals, corrective action plans and capital-needs planning on their own property. When `X-User-Role` is `PropertyOwner`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Property`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Property` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, for a `Property` the comparison is against the property's own `id`; for a `Unit`, an `Inspection` or a `SelfAssessmentSweep` it is against that row's `property_id`; for a `SampleSelection`, an `Appeal` or a `CorrectiveActionPlan` it is against the `property_id` of the inspection they hang from; for a `Deficiency` it is against the `property_id` reached through whichever of `inspection_id` or `sweep_id` is populated; and for a `WorkOrder` it is against the `property_id` of the sweep named by its `sweep_id`. When the comparison fails, the request is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_NOT_OWNER` (HTTP 403). This ownership check applies to every endpoint whose authorised roles include `PropertyOwner`, with one carve-out: collection listings have no single target resource, and instead silently narrow their result set to rows reachable from the caller's own property, so a listing never raises `ERR_FORBIDDEN_NOT_OWNER`.

`HousingAgencyInspector` is the agency inspector who schedules inspections, draws the unit sample, coordinates and records access, records deficiencies, publishes scores, finalizes, cancels, verifies claimed corrections and schedules follow-ups. `MaintenanceTechnician` is maintenance staff who record self-assessment findings and complete the resulting work orders. `QualityAssuranceReviewer` is the independent reviewer who verifies work-order closure and adjudicates corrective action plans. `AppealsOfficer` adjudicates technical-error and score-correction appeals and holds no operational authority over inspections or repairs. `PlatformAdmin` operates the readiness platform: it curates inspection standards, defect definitions, properties and units, and drives the logical clock and the historical-inspection injection capability. When `X-User-Role` is any of `HousingAgencyInspector`, `MaintenanceTechnician`, `QualityAssuranceReviewer`, `AppealsOfficer` or `PlatformAdmin`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it — a work order stores the completing operator in `completed_by_user_id` and the verifying operator in `verified_by_user_id` — but it never compares such an identifier to any entity's `id`.

Segregation of duties is enforced within these roles as well as across them, and is specified in §2.11.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection listing accepts two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of rows returned; when omitted it defaults to 10, and a supplied value must be at least 1 and at most 100. `offset` states how many rows to skip before the returned page begins; when omitted it defaults to 0.

All list endpoints return their rows sorted by creation timestamp in ascending order unless a specific endpoint narrative states otherwise.

List responses carry `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope, where `total_count` is the size of the full filtered result set before pagination and `has_more` states whether rows remain beyond the returned page.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties

Every persisted resource in this system — inspection standards, defect definitions, properties, units, inspections, sample selections, deficiencies, self-assessment sweeps, work orders, appeals, corrective action plans and capital need items alike — carries the same four system-managed properties:

- `id` (string): a server-minted UUID. It carries no type prefix and no embedded semantics; it is opaque to clients.
- `created_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with exactly three fractional digits, recording when the row was first created.
- `updated_at` (string): an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with exactly three fractional digits, refreshed on every mutation of the row.
- `version` (integer): a system-managed counter that increments by exactly 1 on every mutation of the row.

All twelve resource types carry all four properties; none of them is exempt. This matters because derived-counter recomputation is itself a mutation: when recording a deficiency forces an inspection to re-add its area point sums, the inspection's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increments even though the client never wrote a field on the inspection directly. The §3 narratives state, for each operation, which rows have their `version` incremented; those enumerations are normative for the rows they name, and they are read together with the cross-cutting cascade rules of §2.8 and §2.9 rather than in place of them — whenever an operation re-derives a property's `readiness_posture` on one of the §2.8 triggers, that property's `version` increments, and whenever the posture sweep of §2.9 moves a work order, that work order and the sweep owning it have their `version` incremented, whether or not the §3 narrative that set the cascade off repeats the fact. Read that way, the enumerations are what make optimistic concurrency (§2.7) predictable.

Every timestamp the API accepts or emits is an ISO 8601 UTC instant. Instants supplied in request bodies are stored as given and are the values the arithmetic in §3 operates on; the server does not silently substitute its own clock reading for a supplied instant.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

The server keeps a **logical clock** rather than reading wall-clock time. Before any client sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it must never fall back to real wall-clock time. A `PlatformAdmin` moves it by supplying a `current_utc` instant through the platform's clock-setting capability, which returns no resource payload of its own.

Advancing the clock is not inert. Every `Deficiency` still in `OPEN` status whose `correction_due_at` lies strictly before the new clock value transitions to `OVERDUE`. The boundary is exclusive: a clock value exactly equal to a deficiency's `correction_due_at` does **not** trip that deficiency. Each deficiency that flips has its `version` incremented, and every property touched by such a flip recomputes `overdue_life_threatening_count` and re-derives `readiness_posture`, incrementing that property's `version` too. Because an overdue life-threatening deficiency freezes a property (§2.8), a single clock advance can lock a property out of sampling and inspection start.

### 2.6. Numeric Precision and Rounding

Scores, point values, percentages and rates in this system are decimal numbers, and the rounding discipline is pinned per quantity rather than left to a global convention.

- Deficiency point deductions, inspectable-area sub-scores, weighted area products, sweep closure rates, notice lead hours and capital-need priority scores are all carried at **2 decimal places**, rounded **half up**.
- Composite inspection scores — `provisional_score` and `final_score` — are carried at **1 decimal place**, rounded **half up**.
- Sample sizes, tier values, day counts, recurrence counts and ranks are integers.
- Money is never a decimal. `capital_reserve_cents`, `total_estimated_cost_cents`, `allocated_reserve_cents` and `allocated_reserve_total_cents` are integer counts of cents, and reserve distribution uses floor-then-remainder arithmetic (§3.9) rather than per-item rounding.

Where a formula rounds more than once, the order of rounding is part of the specification and is stated in the workflow that owns the formula. Two rules in particular are load-bearing and are repeated here: a deficiency's deduction is rounded to two decimals **before** the per-deficiency cap is applied, and an inspection's composite score is formed by rounding each weighted area product to two decimals **before** the three products are summed and the sum rounded to one decimal. Numeric values are emitted in JSON as numbers, not strings.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Parent Aggregates

Three write operations in this system legitimately repeat against the same parent — a second deficiency on the same inspection, a second finding on the same sweep, a second capital-needs recompute on the same property are all valid on their own terms — so no state precondition can tell a well-formed repeat apart from a writer working off a stale view of the parent's accumulated totals. Those three operations therefore carry a mandatory optimistic-concurrency token naming the parent's `version` as the client last observed it. The token is a required input, not an optional one; a request that omits it fails request-shape validation.

- Recording a deficiency against an inspection requires `expected_inspection_version` to equal the target `Inspection`'s current `version`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_INSPECTION_VERSION` (HTTP 412), so a client cannot add points to an inspection whose accumulated area sums it has not observed.
- Filing a finding against a self-assessment sweep requires `expected_sweep_version` to equal the target `SelfAssessmentSweep`'s current `version`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SWEEP_VERSION` (HTTP 412), so a client cannot add another work order to a sweep whose work-order counters it has not observed.
- Recomputing a property's capital needs requires `expected_property_version` to equal the target `Property`'s current `version`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PROPERTY_VERSION` (HTTP 412), which is what stops a client from superseding a capital-plan generation it never saw.

No other endpoint in this API accepts a version field, and no other endpoint performs a version comparison. Because cascades increment the `version` of rows the client did not write (§2.4, §2.9), a client that reads an inspection, records one deficiency, and then attempts a second deficiency with the same `expected_inspection_version` will be rejected — the first record advanced the inspection. The correct client behaviour is to re-read the parent, or to use the `inspection_version_after_record` value returned on the deficiency it just created, which snapshots the parent inspection's `version` after that record and all of its cascades had been applied.

### 2.8. Readiness Posture: The Property-Wide Gate

Each `Property` carries a computed `readiness_posture` drawn from `READY`, `WATCH`, `RESTRICTED` and `FROZEN`. It is not a stored decision an operator makes; it is re-derived from the property's live deficiency population every time a deficiency is created, changes status, is voided, or is swept up by a clock advance, every time an inspection is scored or rescored, and every time a sweep closes.

The derivation reads three counts over the property's deficiencies. A deficiency reaches a property through either foreign-key path: through `inspection_id` to that inspection's `property_id`, or through `sweep_id` to that sweep's `property_id`. Both sourcing paths count — an owner's own self-assessment findings weigh on posture exactly as an inspector's observations do. The counts are: `overdue_life_threatening_count`, the number of deficiencies whose `severity` is `LIFE_THREATENING` and whose `status` is `OVERDUE`; `open_life_threatening_count`, the number whose `severity` is `LIFE_THREATENING` and whose `status` is either `OPEN` or `OVERDUE`; and `open_severe_count`, the number whose `severity` is `SEVERE` and whose `status` is either `OPEN` or `OVERDUE`. Deficiencies in `CORRECTED`, `VERIFIED` or `VOIDED` status contribute to none of the three.

The posture is then selected by the first matching rule, in this order:

1. If `overdue_life_threatening_count` is at least 1, the posture is `FROZEN`.
2. Otherwise, if `open_life_threatening_count` is at least 1, the posture is `RESTRICTED`.
3. Otherwise, if `open_severe_count` is at least 5, the posture is `WATCH`.
4. Otherwise, if `last_final_score` is not null and is below 60.00, the posture is `WATCH`. The comparison is made against the score as stored, at one decimal place.
5. Otherwise, the posture is `READY`.

Posture has two distinct effects. As a **gate**, a `FROZEN` posture blocks exactly two operations — drawing an inspection's unit sample and starting an inspection on site — each rejected with `ERR_PROPERTY_FROZEN` (HTTP 409). `RESTRICTED` blocks neither of those operations. `WATCH` and `READY` gate nothing at all. As an **escalation trigger**, a re-derived posture of `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN` raises every `OPEN` work order on that property to `URGENT` priority, and a re-derived posture outside those two demotes them back; the mechanics are in §2.9.

### 2.9. Cascade Discipline

Because the entities here are peers linked by identifiers rather than a URL hierarchy, consistency is maintained by explicit cascades rather than by containment. Four cascade chains matter enough to be named, and each is described end to end in the workflow that triggers it: the deficiency-record chain (§3.6), the appeal-grant chain (§3.7), the closure-verification chain (§3.3), and the capital-recompute chain (§3.9). Three cross-cutting rules govern all of them.

**Counters are always recomputed, never incrementally adjusted.** Every derived count and sum named in §3 — an inspection's area point totals, a sweep's work-order counters, a property's deficiency counters, a unit's deficiency counters — is re-derived from the rows that currently satisfy its stated predicate. This is why voiding a deficiency, which sets its `points_deducted` to `0.00` and its `status` to `VOIDED`, removes it from every sum in one step.

**Work-order escalation and de-escalation are symmetric.** Whenever a re-derivation lands a property's `readiness_posture` on `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN`, every `WorkOrder` on that property whose `repair_status` is `OPEN` and whose `priority_tier` is not already `URGENT` has its `priority_tier` raised to `URGENT` and its `due_at` pulled 24 hours earlier. Whenever a re-derivation lands the posture on any value other than `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN`, every `WorkOrder` on that property whose `repair_status` is `OPEN` and whose `priority_tier` is `URGENT` is demoted back to `ROUTINE` and has its `due_at` pushed 24 hours later, restoring the original deadline. The direction is decided by the posture value **after** the re-derivation, not by whether this particular request is the one that changed it — a request that re-derives the posture and finds it already `READY` still performs the demotion sweep. Each pass moves only the rows that are not already where it would put them: a work order it skips is not written at all, so neither its `due_at` nor its `version` moves, and repeated passes in the same direction therefore never drift a deadline by a further 24 hours. Every work order the pass does move has its own `version` incremented by 1, because `priority_tier` and `due_at` were written on it (§2.4). Every sweep that owns a work order touched by either direction recomputes its `urgent_work_order_count` and has its own `version` incremented.

**Bubble-up transitions are server-initiated.** Several parent transitions have no endpoint of their own: an inspection moves from `SAMPLED` to `NOTIFIED` when the last of its active sample slots receives its tenant notice; a self-assessment deficiency moves from `OPEN` to `CORRECTED` when the last non-void work order remediating it is marked repaired; an inspection returns from `APPEALED` to `SCORED` when the last non-terminal appeal against it reaches a terminal decision; a corrective action plan moves to `COMPLETED` when its follow-up inspection closes. In each case the server performs the parent transition as part of the child's request, and increments the parent's `version`.

### 2.10. Resolution Order and Validation Precedence

When two rules could both reject a request, the one with the lower priority number below wins, and the response carries that rule's error. The full precedence ladder is:

1. **Authentication** — headers present and `X-User-Role` recognised, else `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. **Endpoint authorisation** — the declared role appears in the endpoint's role list, else `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (403).
3. **Request shape** — every required body field is present and correctly typed, and every numeric field lies inside its declared range, else `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). This applies to every request-bearing operation.
4. **Enum membership** — every enum-typed body field carries a declared member of its enumeration, else `ERR_INVALID_ENUM_VALUE` (422). This applies to every request-bearing operation that accepts an enum input.
5. **Path identifiers** — identifiers embedded in the URL are resolved left to right, and the first one that names no existing row produces its own 404: `ERR_STANDARD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROPERTY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_UNIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SAMPLE_SELECTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DEFICIENCY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SWEEP_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_WORK_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_APPEAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CAP_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_CAPITAL_NEED_NOT_FOUND` as appropriate to the resource type.
6. **Body identifiers** — foreign-key identifiers carried in the body are resolved in the order the endpoint declares its inputs, each producing the 404 for its own resource type: `ERR_STANDARD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROPERTY_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_UNIT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DEFECT_DEFINITION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_INSPECTION_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_DEFICIENCY_NOT_FOUND`.
7. **Ownership** — the `PropertyOwner` reachability check of §2.1, else `ERR_FORBIDDEN_NOT_OWNER` (403).
8. **Segregation of duties** — the verifier-differs-from-completer check of §2.11, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
9. **State admissibility** (priority 20) — the target row's `status`, `repair_status`, `verification_status` or `adoption_status` admits the requested transition, else the state error for that resource type: `ERR_STANDARD_ALREADY_ADOPTED`, `ERR_INSPECTION_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_SELECTION_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_DEFICIENCY_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_SWEEP_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_WORK_ORDER_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_APPEAL_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_CAP_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_APPEAL_PENDING` or `ERR_FOLLOW_UP_NOT_CLOSED`, all at HTTP 409.
10. **Optimistic concurrency** (priority 25) — the version comparison of §2.7, else `ERR_STALE_INSPECTION_VERSION`, `ERR_STALE_SWEEP_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_PROPERTY_VERSION` at HTTP 412. Note that this runs **after** state admissibility: a stale token against an inspection that is not `IN_PROGRESS` surfaces as `ERR_INSPECTION_INVALID_STATE`, not as a version error.
11. **Life-safety gating** (priorities 30, 32, 34) — the frozen-property gate, the uncorrected-life-threatening gate on scoring, and the twenty-four-hour correction obligation, in that order.
12. **Referential business rules** (priorities 40 through 43) — standard pinning, adoption status, area agreement, and appeal targeting, in that order.
13. **Temporal rules** (priorities 50 through 55) — appeal window, interval well-formedness, inspection-window conflict, unit-access-window conflict, sweep overlap capacity, and follow-up timing, in that order.
14. **Remaining business constraints** (priorities 60 through 71) — sampling, access, weighting, uniqueness, limit and scope rules.
15. **Fiscal constraints** (priorities 80 through 82) — plan cost against reserve, plan target against deadline, and appeal adjustment bounds, last of all.

Each individual rule's condition and error are stated in the §3 workflow that owns it. Two orderings inside this ladder are worth restating because they are easy to get backwards: the frozen-property gate is evaluated before any sampling-eligibility arithmetic, so a frozen property with no eligible units reports `ERR_PROPERTY_FROZEN` rather than `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_UNITS`; and the uncorrected-life-threatening gate on scoring is evaluated before the access-recorded gate, so an inspection with both problems reports `ERR_LIFE_THREATENING_UNCORRECTED`.

### 2.11. Segregation of Duties

Two independence rules are structural rather than incidental. First, on a single work order, the operator who records the repair may never be the operator who verifies its closure. The completing operator's `X-User-ID` is stored as `completed_by_user_id`; at closure verification the acting `X-User-ID` is compared against it, and an exact match is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). The successful verifier's identifier is stored as `verified_by_user_id`. A `QualityAssuranceReviewer` may therefore complete a repair or verify a closure, but never both on the same work order. Second, authorship and adjudication are held apart by role: a `PropertyOwner` files appeals and submits corrective action plans but reviews neither; an `AppealsOfficer` reviews appeals but cannot file one; a `QualityAssuranceReviewer` reviews corrective action plans but cannot submit one.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Publishing an Inspection Standard and its Defect Catalogue

Nothing in this system can be inspected until a `PlatformAdmin` has published a standard. Registering one creates an `InspectionStandard` in `DRAFT` — the initial state of its lifecycle — carrying a short `code` such as `NSPIRE-2026` of between 1 and 32 characters, a human-readable `name`, an `effective_date` giving the ISO 8601 UTC instant from which the standard may legitimately be pinned to an inspection, and the three inspectable-area weights that will later apportion a score. `unit_weight_pct` weighs the dwelling-unit interiors, `inside_weight_pct` weighs the inside common areas, and `outside_weight_pct` weighs the site exterior; each is a percentage between 0.00 and 100.00 carried at two decimals. The three weights must sum to exactly 100.00, checked at registration and again at adoption; a submission that misses is rejected with `ERR_WEIGHTS_NOT_NORMALIZED` (HTTP 422). A newly registered standard has `adoption_status` `DRAFT`, `adopted_at` null, `superseded_at` null, and both of its derived catalogue counters — `defect_definition_count` and `life_threatening_definition_count` — at 0.

Standard codes are scope-unique in a deliberately narrow sense. At most one `InspectionStandard` bearing a given `code` may be in `DRAFT` or `ADOPTED` at any time; a second registration under a code already held by a `DRAFT` or `ADOPTED` row is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_STANDARD_CODE` (HTTP 409). Rows in `SUPERSEDED` do not occupy the code, which is what allows a `NSPIRE-2026` successor to be drafted after its predecessor has been retired.

Under a standard, the admin builds the catalogue of scoreable defects. Each `DefectDefinition` names its owning standard through `standard_id`, carries a `code` that must be unique among the definitions sharing that `standard_id` — a collision is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_DEFECT_CODE` (HTTP 409) — plus a `name`, an `inspectable_area` of `UNIT`, `INSIDE` or `OUTSIDE` fixing which area the defect scores against, and a `severity` of `LIFE_THREATENING`, `SEVERE`, `MODERATE` or `LOW`. Severity drives both the deduction multiplier and the correction clock. `base_point_value` is the raw deduction before any multiplier, between 0.01 and 40.00 at two decimals. `correction_deadline_hours` is the number of whole hours from observation to the correction deadline and must be at least 1; when the `severity` is `LIFE_THREATENING` it must be exactly 24, because the twenty-four-hour correction obligation is not negotiable. A life-threatening definition declaring anything else is rejected with `ERR_LT_DEADLINE_MUST_BE_24H` (HTTP 422). The system derives `is_life_threatening` on the definition, setting it to true exactly when `severity` is `LIFE_THREATENING` and false otherwise, and the owning standard's `defect_definition_count` and `life_threatening_definition_count` are recomputed — the first counting every definition under that standard, the second counting only those whose `severity` is `LIFE_THREATENING` — with the standard's `version` incremented by 1.

Adoption is the moment a standard becomes usable, and it is the only producer of `ADOPTED`; a `DRAFT` standard can never be pinned to an inspection. A `PlatformAdmin` adopts by supplying the `adopted_at` instant. Three conditions must hold: the standard's `adoption_status` is `DRAFT`, its three area weights still sum to exactly 100.00, and its `life_threatening_definition_count` is at least 1 — a standard that defines no life-threatening defect cannot be adopted, and the attempt is rejected with `ERR_NO_LIFE_THREATENING_DEFINITION` (HTTP 422). An adoption requested against a standard already in `ADOPTED` or in `SUPERSEDED` is rejected with `ERR_STANDARD_ALREADY_ADOPTED` (HTTP 409). On success the standard moves `DRAFT → ADOPTED`, its `adoption_status` becomes `ADOPTED` and its `adopted_at` is set to the supplied instant. In the same operation, every other `InspectionStandard` sharing the same `code` and currently in `ADOPTED` moves `ADOPTED → SUPERSEDED`, taking `adoption_status` `SUPERSEDED` and a `superseded_at` equal to the newly adopted standard's `adopted_at`. The `version` of the newly adopted row and of every row it supersedes each increments by 1. `SUPERSEDED` is terminal: nothing transitions out of it.

Supersession deliberately does not reach backwards. Inspections already pinned to a standard keep that standard's weights, because an inspection reads `standard_id` once at creation and never re-reads it. Where a standard's adoption status *is* re-read is at the point of citation: scheduling an inspection, filing a self-assessment finding, and injecting historical inspection data all require the cited standard to be in `ADOPTED`, and a citation of a `DRAFT` or `SUPERSEDED` standard is rejected with `ERR_STANDARD_NOT_ADOPTED` (HTTP 409).

Any authenticated role may read a single standard, which returns its derived definition counts, or list standards filtered by `code` and by `adoption_status`. Definitions may be read singly or listed filtered by `standard_id`, `inspectable_area` and `severity`.

### 3.2. The Property Portfolio and Unit Occupancy

A `PlatformAdmin` registers each assisted-housing `Property` with a `name`, a `total_unit_count` of at least 1, a `capital_reserve_cents` of at least 0 expressed in integer cents, and an `owner_contact_email` serving as the owner's notification mailbox. The property's `id` is significant beyond addressing: it is the value a `PropertyOwner` presents as `X-User-ID`, which is what makes the ownership reachability check of §2.1 work. A freshly registered property starts at `readiness_posture` `READY`, with `open_life_threatening_count`, `overdue_life_threatening_count`, `open_severe_count` and `allocated_reserve_total_cents` all 0, and with `last_final_score` and `last_sweep_closed_at` both null.

Those six derived fields each have a fixed definition. `open_life_threatening_count` counts the property's deficiencies whose `severity` is `LIFE_THREATENING` and whose `status` is `OPEN` or `OVERDUE`; `overdue_life_threatening_count` narrows that to `status` `OVERDUE` alone; `open_severe_count` counts those whose `severity` is `SEVERE` and whose `status` is `OPEN` or `OVERDUE`. `last_final_score` mirrors the `final_score` of the property's most recently scored inspection, selected by taking the latest `scored_at` and breaking ties on the earlier `created_at`, and stays null while no inspection of the property has been scored. `last_sweep_closed_at` mirrors the `closed_at` of the property's most recently `CLOSED` self-assessment sweep and stays null while none has closed. `allocated_reserve_total_cents` is the sum of `allocated_reserve_cents` across the property's capital need items whose `status` is `ACTIVE`.

Either the owner or a `PlatformAdmin` may amend a property, supplying any subset of `name`, `total_unit_count`, `capital_reserve_cents` and `owner_contact_email`; the amendment increments the property's `version` by 1. Raising `capital_reserve_cents` is the lever that widens what a corrective action plan may cost and what the capital plan may distribute.

Dwelling units are registered individually by a `PlatformAdmin`. Each `Unit` names its `property_id`, carries a `unit_number` that must be unique among the units sharing that `property_id` — a collision is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_UNIT_NUMBER` (HTTP 409) — and a `bedroom_count` between 0 and 6. Its `occupancy_status` is one of `OCCUPIED`, `VACANT` or `DOWN`, defaulting to `OCCUPIED` when omitted, and `occupant_display_name` gives the household name printed on the tenant notice, which is null for a unit that is `VACANT` or `DOWN`. A new unit begins with `prior_deficiency_count` and `open_deficiency_count` both 0. Occupancy is not cosmetic: `DOWN` units are excluded outright from sampling eligibility, and an `OCCUPIED` unit raises the deduction for a life-threatening defect found inside it.

The two unit counters have distinct scopes. `open_deficiency_count` counts the deficiencies citing this `unit_id` whose `status` is `OPEN` or `OVERDUE`, and it moves as corrections and verifications land. `prior_deficiency_count` is the unit's inspection history: it counts the deficiencies citing this `unit_id` whose `source` is `INSPECTION` and whose `status` is anything other than `VOIDED`, restricted to those belonging to an inspection whose own `status` is `CLOSED` or `FOLLOW_UP_SCHEDULED`. It is therefore a lagging figure, refreshed only at the two moments an inspection's findings become history — when an inspection closes and when a follow-up is scheduled — and it is the primary sort key of the sampling policy in §3.4, which is precisely why the units that gave trouble last cycle are the units drawn this cycle.

Either the owner or a `PlatformAdmin` may amend a unit, supplying any subset of `bedroom_count`, `occupancy_status` and `occupant_display_name`; the amendment increments the unit's `version` by 1. Because eligibility is read at draw time, flipping a unit to `DOWN` before the sample is drawn removes it from consideration, while flipping it afterwards does not disturb a slot already held.

Properties may be read singly by any role and listed, filtered by `readiness_posture`, by the four non-owner reviewing roles and the platform operator; the listing of all properties is the one read surface an owner does not hold. Units may be read singly or listed filtered by `property_id` and `occupancy_status` by any role.

### 3.3. Self-Assessment Sweeps and the Work-Order Loop

The owner's preparation channel is the **self-assessment sweep**: a bounded window during which the owner walks their own property, records what they find, and remediates it before an inspector arrives. A `PropertyOwner` or a `PlatformAdmin` plans a `SelfAssessmentSweep` by naming the `property_id`, a `window_start` and `window_end` bounding the sweep, and a `planned_area_scope` of `UNIT_ONLY`, `INSIDE_OUTSIDE` or `ALL_AREAS` declaring which inspectable areas the sweep covers. The `window_end` must be strictly after the `window_start`; an empty or inverted interval is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_TIME_WINDOW` (HTTP 422). A planned sweep begins in `PLANNED` with `started_at` and `closed_at` null, with `generated_work_order_count`, `open_work_order_count`, `verified_work_order_count`, `void_work_order_count` and `urgent_work_order_count` all 0, and — because a sweep with no work orders is vacuously fully verified — with `closure_verified_rate` at `100.00` rather than at `0.00` or null.

Sweeps may overlap, but only up to a point. At most two sweeps on a single property whose `status` is `PLANNED` or `IN_PROGRESS` may cover any single instant, measured on half-open windows that include `window_start` and exclude `window_end`. A third live sweep covering an instant already covered by two is rejected with `ERR_SWEEP_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409); a sweep that merely begins at the exact instant another ends does not share an instant with it and is therefore admitted. Sweeps in `CLOSED` or `ABANDONED` do not consume capacity.

Starting a sweep moves it `PLANNED → IN_PROGRESS` and stamps the supplied `started_at` onto the row, incrementing its `version` by 1; it is available to the owner and to a `PlatformAdmin`, and the owner's `X-User-ID` must be the sweep's `property_id`. Any sweep transition requested from a status that does not admit it is rejected with `ERR_SWEEP_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409).

Filing a finding is the sweep's central operation and is available to the `PropertyOwner` and to a `MaintenanceTechnician`. The request carries `expected_sweep_version` for the concurrency check of §2.7, the `defect_definition_id` being cited, the `inspectable_area` the finding is scored against, the `observed_at` instant, and optionally the `unit_id` when the finding is inside a dwelling unit, a `supersedes_deficiency_id` naming the earlier deficiency this finding recurs from, and a free-text `note`. Four preconditions apply: the sweep's `status` is `IN_PROGRESS`; the supplied `expected_sweep_version` equals the sweep's current `version`; the cited definition belongs to an `InspectionStandard` whose `adoption_status` is `ADOPTED`, else `ERR_STANDARD_NOT_ADOPTED`; and the submitted `inspectable_area` lies inside the sweep's `planned_area_scope`. Scope admission is exact: a `UNIT_ONLY` sweep admits `UNIT` alone, an `INSIDE_OUTSIDE` sweep admits `INSIDE` and `OUTSIDE`, and an `ALL_AREAS` sweep admits all three. An area outside scope is rejected with `ERR_AREA_OUT_OF_SCOPE` (HTTP 422). Independently of scope, the submitted `inspectable_area` must equal the `inspectable_area` on the cited definition, and `unit_id` must be present exactly when the area is `UNIT` and absent otherwise; a disagreement is rejected with `ERR_AREA_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422).

A successful finding writes two rows and returns the work order. The `Deficiency` is created with `source` `SELF_ASSESSMENT`, its `sweep_id` set to this sweep, its `inspection_id` left null, `status` `OPEN`, `severity` copied from the cited definition, `points_deducted` computed by the deduction rule of §3.6, and `correction_due_at` set to the `observed_at` instant advanced by the definition's `correction_deadline_hours`. The `WorkOrder` is created against the same `sweep_id` with `deficiency_id` pointing at the new deficiency, `repair_status` `OPEN`, `verification_status` `UNVERIFIED`, `priority_tier` `ROUTINE`, and `due_at` equal to the new deficiency's `correction_due_at`. The sweep then recomputes `generated_work_order_count`, `open_work_order_count`, `urgent_work_order_count` and `closure_verified_rate`, and its `version` increments by 1. Finally the owning property recomputes `open_life_threatening_count` and `open_severe_count` and re-derives `readiness_posture` — an owner's own finding of a life-threatening defect restricts their property exactly as an inspector's would, which in turn may raise this very sweep's open work orders to `URGENT`. When the resulting posture sweep of §2.9 does move a work order this sweep owns — the one just created included — that section's recompute and increment land on top of the increment just described, so this sweep's `version` advances by 2 across such a request.

**Two independent dimensions.** A work order advances along two axes that never gate each other: `repair_status` runs `OPEN`, `REPAIRED` and `VOID`, while `verification_status` runs `UNVERIFIED`, `VERIFIED` and `REJECTED`. Its initial position is `repair_status` `OPEN` paired with `verification_status` `UNVERIFIED`, and it is finished either at `repair_status` `VOID` or at the pairing of `repair_status` `REPAIRED` with `verification_status` `VERIFIED`.

A `MaintenanceTechnician` records the repair, supplying the `completed_at` instant and a `resolution_note` narrative. This is permitted only when `repair_status` is `OPEN` and the owning sweep's `status` is `IN_PROGRESS`; anything else is rejected with `ERR_WORK_ORDER_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). The transition sets `completed_at`, records the acting `X-User-ID` as `completed_by_user_id`, stores the `resolution_note`, and moves `repair_status` `OPEN → REPAIRED` while leaving `verification_status` untouched. If, after this write, every non-`VOID` work order sharing the same `deficiency_id` has `repair_status` `REPAIRED`, the linked deficiency bubbles `OPEN → CORRECTED` with its `corrected_at` set to the supplied `completed_at`. The sweep recomputes `open_work_order_count` and `closure_verified_rate`, and the `version` of the work order, of the sweep and — when the bubble fired — of the deficiency each increments by 1. That bubble is a deficiency status change, so it carries the §2.8 re-derivation with it: the cited unit recomputes `open_deficiency_count`, and the property recomputes `open_life_threatening_count`, `overdue_life_threatening_count` and `open_severe_count`, re-derives `readiness_posture` with its `version` incremented by 1, and runs the symmetric work-order sweep of §2.9 — which is how repairing the last work order against a life-threatening deficiency demotes that property's other urgent work orders inside this same request. A completion that leaves the deficiency where it stands, because a sibling non-`VOID` work order is still `OPEN`, reaches neither the unit nor the property.

Closure verification is the independence checkpoint, open to the `PropertyOwner` and to a `QualityAssuranceReviewer`. The request carries a `verification_result` of either `PASSED` or `FAILED` together with the `verified_at` instant. The work order's `repair_status` must be `REPAIRED` and its `verification_status` must be `UNVERIFIED` or `REJECTED`; and the acting `X-User-ID` must differ from the stored `completed_by_user_id`, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

On `PASSED` the system runs a four-link chain. First, `verification_status` moves `UNVERIFIED → VERIFIED`, `verified_at` is stamped, the acting `X-User-ID` is stored as `verified_by_user_id`, and the sweep recomputes `verified_work_order_count` and `closure_verified_rate`. Second, because every non-`VOID` work order sharing that `deficiency_id` now has `verification_status` `VERIFIED`, the linked deficiency transitions to `VERIFIED` with its `verified_at` set to the supplied instant. Third, because that deficiency has left the `OPEN`-or-`OVERDUE` population, the property recomputes `open_life_threatening_count`, `overdue_life_threatening_count` and `open_severe_count` and re-derives `readiness_posture`. Fourth, the posture value produced by the third link is inspected: when it is neither `RESTRICTED` nor `FROZEN`, every work order on that property whose `repair_status` is `OPEN` and whose `priority_tier` is `URGENT` is demoted to `ROUTINE` and has its `due_at` pushed 24 hours later, and every sweep owning such a work order recomputes `urgent_work_order_count`. The judgement in the fourth link is made on the posture as it stands after the third link, not on whether this request is the one that changed it. The `version` of the work order, of the deficiency, of the property and of every sweep touched each increments by 1.

On `FAILED` the chain is different in shape and equally explicit. `verification_status` moves `UNVERIFIED → REJECTED`, `verified_at` is stamped, the acting `X-User-ID` is stored as `verified_by_user_id`, and `rejection_count` increments by 1. The repair dimension is reopened: `repair_status` returns `REPAIRED → OPEN` and both `completed_at` and `completed_by_user_id` are cleared to null, which frees the work order to be completed again — potentially by a different technician, and the segregation check will then be measured against whoever completes it next. The linked deficiency is sent back `CORRECTED → OPEN` and its `corrected_at` is cleared to null. The sweep recomputes `open_work_order_count`, `verified_work_order_count` and `closure_verified_rate`, and the `version` of the work order, of the deficiency and of the sweep each increments by 1. This is the repair loop: a rejected closure returns the work order to an open, unverified-eligible position, and the complete-then-verify pair may repeat without limit.

Voiding retires a work order raised in error and is open to the `PropertyOwner` and to a `PlatformAdmin`. It requires a `void_reason` and is permitted only when `repair_status` is `OPEN` or `REPAIRED` and `verification_status` is not `VERIFIED`. It sets `repair_status` to `VOID`, stores the `void_reason`, and causes the sweep to recompute `void_work_order_count`, `open_work_order_count`, `urgent_work_order_count` and `closure_verified_rate` — while leaving `generated_work_order_count` unchanged, because that counter deliberately includes voided rows as a record of everything the sweep ever raised. `urgent_work_order_count` is indifferent to the repair dimension in the same way: it counts the sweep's work orders whose `priority_tier` is `URGENT`, with no filter on `repair_status` or `verification_status`. Since the escalation and demotion passes of §2.9 only ever move a work order whose `repair_status` is `OPEN`, a row escalated while it was `OPEN` keeps its `URGENT` tier once it is repaired or voided, and so stays inside this count. If no non-`VOID` work order remains against the linked deficiency, that deficiency transitions to `VOIDED` and its `points_deducted` is set to `0.00`. The work order's `version` increments by 1, as does the owning sweep's.

**The closure rate.** A sweep's `closure_verified_rate` is derived over its work orders whose `repair_status` is not `VOID`. The denominator is the count of those rows; the numerator is the count of those rows whose `verification_status` is `VERIFIED`. When the denominator is zero the rate is pinned to `100.00`. Otherwise the rate is the numerator multiplied by one hundred, divided by the denominator, rounded half up to two decimal places. Voided rows leave both the numerator and the denominator, which is why voiding an unverified work order raises the rate rather than lowering it.

Closing a sweep is available to the `PropertyOwner` and to a `PlatformAdmin` and carries only a `closed_at` instant, yet it lands on two different terminal states depending on where the sweep stands. From `IN_PROGRESS` it moves `IN_PROGRESS → CLOSED`, but only if `closure_verified_rate` is at least 90.00 — measured on the rounded value, and inclusive at the boundary, so a rate of exactly `90.00` closes — otherwise the request is rejected with `ERR_SWEEP_CLOSURE_RATE_TOO_LOW` (HTTP 409). A successful close stamps `closed_at`, copies that same instant onto the property's `last_sweep_closed_at`, and increments both the sweep's `version` and the property's `version` by 1. From `PLANNED`, meaning a sweep that never started, the same operation instead moves `PLANNED → ABANDONED`, stamping `closed_at` and incrementing the sweep's `version` by 1; the closure-rate floor is evaluated only on the `IN_PROGRESS` branch, and the abandonment branch touches neither the property's `last_sweep_closed_at` nor the property's `version`. Both `CLOSED` and `ABANDONED` are terminal.

Sweeps may be read singly — returning `generated_work_order_count`, `open_work_order_count`, `verified_work_order_count`, `void_work_order_count`, `urgent_work_order_count` and `closure_verified_rate` — or listed filtered by `property_id` and `status`. Work orders may be read singly or listed filtered by `sweep_id`, `deficiency_id`, `repair_status`, `verification_status` and `priority_tier`.

### 3.4. Scheduling an Inspection and Drawing the Unit Sample

A `HousingAgencyInspector` schedules an `Inspection` by naming the `property_id` under inspection, the `standard_id` whose weights will score it, and the `scheduled_start` and `scheduled_end` bounding the on-site window, optionally declaring an `inspection_kind` of `INITIAL` or `FOLLOW_UP` which defaults to `INITIAL` when omitted. `INITIAL` denotes a scheduled cycle inspection; `FOLLOW_UP` denotes one created by a corrective action plan, and in practice inspectors do not supply it directly because follow-ups are created for them (§3.8). The cited standard must be in `ADOPTED`, else `ERR_STANDARD_NOT_ADOPTED`. The `scheduled_end` must be strictly after the `scheduled_start`, else `ERR_INVALID_TIME_WINDOW`. A new inspection begins in `SCHEDULED` with `drawn_at`, `started_at`, `scored_at`, `rescored_at` and `finalized_at` all null, `sample_tier_value` and `sample_size` at 0, `inspected_unit_count` and `refused_unit_count` at 0, the three area point sums and `total_points_deducted` at `0.00`, `life_threatening_count` at 0, `provisional_score`, `final_score`, `appeal_deadline_at`, `cap_window_days`, `cap_due_at` and `followup_due_at` all null, and `cap_required` false.

Pinning happens here and only here. The `standard_id` is captured at creation and never re-read for the life of the inspection, so a later adoption that pushes that standard into `SUPERSEDED` leaves this inspection's weights exactly as they were.

Inspections on one property must not crowd each other. A requested window conflicts if the interval from `scheduled_start` to `scheduled_end`, expanded by a 72-hour cooldown buffer at each end, intersects the identically expanded window of any other inspection on the same property whose `status` is anything other than `CANCELLED` or `CLOSED`. The arithmetic is closed-interval: two expanded intervals that merely touch at an endpoint **do** conflict. A conflict is rejected with `ERR_INSPECTION_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409). Cancelled and closed inspections are invisible to this check, so a cancelled visit does not block its own replacement.

**Drawing the sample.** With the property notified of nothing yet, the inspector draws the unit sample by supplying the `drawn_at` instant. Three preconditions hold: the inspection's `status` is `SCHEDULED`; the property's `readiness_posture` is not `FROZEN`, else `ERR_PROPERTY_FROZEN`; and the eligibility filter below yields at least one candidate unit, else `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_UNITS` (HTTP 422). The draw itself runs in four pinned steps.

Step one derives the tier value from the property's **declared** `total_unit_count`, not from the number of `Unit` rows actually registered. For a declared count of 4 or fewer, `sample_tier_value` is the declared count itself. For a declared count of 5 through 30, it is the larger of 5 and the declared count multiplied by 0.20 then rounded up to the next whole number. For a declared count of 31 through 100, it is the larger of 8 and the declared count multiplied by 0.15 then rounded up to the next whole number. For a declared count above 100, it is the smaller of 35 and the larger of 15 and the declared count multiplied by 0.10 then rounded up to the next whole number. The rounding-up is applied to the exact product, never to a product that has already been rounded to some intermediate precision.

Step two builds the candidate pool from the `Unit` rows actually on record. A unit is a candidate when its `property_id` equals the inspection's `property_id`, its `occupancy_status` is `OCCUPIED` or `VACANT` — units that are `DOWN` are excluded outright — and no `SampleSelection` of this same inspection already holds that unit. If the pool is empty, the draw fails with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_UNITS`.

Step three clamps: `sample_size` is the smaller of `sample_tier_value` and the number of candidates. A property that declares 200 units but has registered only 6 eligible rows therefore draws 6, while `sample_tier_value` still records the 20 the tier policy asked for. The two fields are both persisted precisely so this gap is visible.

Step four ranks the candidates and takes the first `sample_size` of them. Candidates are ordered by `prior_deficiency_count` descending — the units with the worst inspection history first — and ties are broken by `created_at` ascending, the earlier-registered unit first. The `id` is never consulted as a tie-break. `selection_rank` is assigned 1 upward in that order.

Each drawn unit yields one `SampleSelection` naming the `inspection_id` and the `unit_id`, with `selection_basis` `SAMPLED`, `selection_status` `ACTIVE`, `access_status` `NOT_NOTIFIED`, `replaced_selection_id` null, and its `selection_rank` from step four. Its `notice_method`, `notified_at`, `access_window_start`, `access_window_end`, `notice_lead_hours`, `access_recorded_at` and `refusal_reason` all begin null. The inspection moves `SCHEDULED → SAMPLED`, stores `drawn_at`, `sample_tier_value` and `sample_size`, and increments its `version` by 1. Thereafter `sample_size` is maintained as the count of this inspection's sample slots whose `selection_status` is `ACTIVE`, which is what makes refusal handling in §3.5 able to move it.

An inspection that has not yet begun on site may be withdrawn by the inspector. Cancellation carries no request body and is permitted while the `status` is `SCHEDULED`, `SAMPLED` or `NOTIFIED`; from anywhere else it is rejected with `ERR_INSPECTION_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). It sets every `SampleSelection` of the inspection to `selection_status` `REPLACED`, releasing each held unit, and increments the inspection's `version` by 1. `CANCELLED` is terminal — no transition leads out of it — and a cancelled inspection no longer occupies its window for conflict purposes.

Inspections may be read singly, which returns `sample_tier_value`, `sample_size`, `inspected_unit_count`, `refused_unit_count`, `unit_area_points`, `inside_area_points`, `outside_area_points`, `total_points_deducted`, `life_threatening_count`, `provisional_score`, `final_score`, `appeal_deadline_at`, `cap_required`, `cap_window_days`, `cap_due_at` and `followup_due_at`, or listed filtered by `property_id`, `standard_id`, `status` and `inspection_kind`.

### 3.5. Tenant Notification, Access Coordination and Refusal Replacement

A household cannot be walked into unannounced, so the notice on each drawn slot is the owner's obligation, not the inspector's. Only a `PropertyOwner` may record it, and only for their own property: the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the `property_id` of the inspection owning the slot. The request carries a `notice_method` of `POSTED`, `MAILED`, `POSTED_AND_MAILED` or `EMAIL` describing how the notice reached the household named in the unit's `occupant_display_name`, the `notified_at` instant of delivery, and the `access_window_start` and `access_window_end` bounding the entry window agreed with that household.

Four further conditions guard the notice. The slot's `access_status` must be `NOT_NOTIFIED` and its `selection_status` must be `ACTIVE`, else `ERR_SELECTION_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). The owning inspection's `status` must be `SAMPLED`, `NOTIFIED` or `IN_PROGRESS` — the third case exists because a replacement slot born mid-inspection still needs its notice. The `access_window_end` must be strictly after the `access_window_start`, else `ERR_INVALID_TIME_WINDOW`. And the requested entry window must not overlap the entry window of any other sample slot with `selection_status` `ACTIVE` on the same `unit_id`; here the arithmetic is half-open, including the start instant and excluding the end instant, and with no buffer at all, so a window that begins exactly when another ends is **accepted**. A genuine overlap is rejected with `ERR_UNIT_ACCESS_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409). Note the deliberate contrast with the inspection-window rule of §3.4: inspection windows use closed intervals with a 72-hour buffer and treat touching endpoints as a conflict, while unit entry windows use half-open intervals with no buffer and treat touching endpoints as fine.

A successful notice moves the slot `NOT_NOTIFIED → NOTIFIED`, stores the four supplied values, and derives `notice_lead_hours` as the interval from `notified_at` to the owning inspection's `scheduled_start`, expressed in hours and rounded half up to two decimal places. The value may be negative — a replacement slot noticed after the on-site window has already opened legitimately produces a negative lead — and that is not itself an error. The slot's `version` increments by 1. If the owning inspection is in `SAMPLED` and, after this write, every one of its slots with `selection_status` `ACTIVE` has `access_status` `NOTIFIED`, the inspection bubbles `SAMPLED → NOTIFIED` with its `version` incremented by 1. There is no separate operation for that parent transition; it is a consequence of the last outstanding notice landing.

**Starting on site.** The inspector begins the walk by supplying the `started_at` instant. Three conditions hold: the inspection's `status` is `NOTIFIED`; the property's `readiness_posture` is not `FROZEN`, else `ERR_PROPERTY_FROZEN`; and every slot with `selection_status` `ACTIVE` and `selection_basis` `SAMPLED` has a `notice_lead_hours` of at least 48.00 — two full days of advance notice — else `ERR_NOTICE_WINDOW_TOO_SHORT` (HTTP 422). The boundary is inclusive: exactly `48.00` passes. Slots whose `selection_basis` is `REPLACEMENT` are exempt from this gate entirely, because they are created mid-inspection and could never satisfy it. The transition moves `NOTIFIED → IN_PROGRESS`, stamps `started_at`, and increments the inspection's `version` by 1.

**Recording access.** At each unit the inspector records the outcome, supplying an `access_result` of `GRANTED` or `REFUSED`, the `recorded_at` instant, and, on a refusal, a free-text `refusal_reason`. Both branches require the slot's `access_status` to be `NOTIFIED`, its `selection_status` to be `ACTIVE`, and the owning inspection's `status` to be `IN_PROGRESS`. `GRANTED` and `REFUSED` are both terminal positions for a slot's access dimension, and their side-effect sets diverge sharply.

On `GRANTED` the slot moves `NOTIFIED → GRANTED`, its `access_recorded_at` is set to the supplied `recorded_at`, and the inspection recomputes `inspected_unit_count` as the number of its slots with `selection_status` `ACTIVE` and `access_status` `GRANTED`. The slot's `version` and the inspection's `version` each increment by 1. Granting is what unlocks dwelling-unit deficiency recording on that unit.

On `REFUSED` the slot moves `NOTIFIED → REFUSED`, its `access_recorded_at` is set to the supplied `recorded_at`, its `refusal_reason` is stored, and the **replacement selector** runs. The selector re-applies the eligibility filter of §3.4 — units of the same property whose `occupancy_status` is `OCCUPIED` or `VACANT` and which hold no sample slot of this inspection — and takes exactly one unit from it under the same ranking, highest `prior_deficiency_count` first with the earlier `created_at` breaking ties. Neither the tier step nor the clamp step is re-run, because the sample's size is already fixed. When such a unit exists, the refused slot becomes `selection_status` `REPLACED` and a fresh slot is created on the substitute unit with `selection_basis` `REPLACEMENT`, `selection_status` `ACTIVE`, `access_status` `NOT_NOTIFIED`, `replaced_selection_id` pointing back at the refused slot, and a `selection_rank` one greater than the highest `selection_rank` currently on this inspection, continuing the sequence rather than reusing the vacated rank. When the candidate pool is empty the refusal is simply recorded without a replacement: the refused slot keeps `selection_status` `ACTIVE` alongside `access_status` `REFUSED`, so it remains in the final-score denominator and attracts the refusal penalty of §3.6. In that case no `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_UNITS` is raised — the empty-pool outcome that fails a fresh draw is a legitimate outcome here. Either way, the inspection recomputes `refused_unit_count` as the number of its slots with `selection_status` `ACTIVE` and `access_status` `REFUSED`, recomputes `sample_size` as the number with `selection_status` `ACTIVE`, keeps its `status` at `IN_PROGRESS`, and increments its `version` by 1, as does the refused slot. A replacement slot must still receive its own tenant notice before its access can be recorded.

Sample slots may be read singly, returning `selection_rank`, `selection_basis`, `access_status` and `notice_lead_hours` among the rest, or listed filtered by `inspection_id`, `unit_id`, `replaced_selection_id`, `selection_basis`, `selection_status` and `access_status`.

### 3.6. Recording Deficiencies, Deductions and the Correction Clock

A `Deficiency` is a single observation citing a single published defect definition, and it arises from exactly one of two origins. Its `source` is `INSPECTION` when an inspector recorded it during an official inspection, in which case its `inspection_id` names that inspection and its `sweep_id` is null; or `SELF_ASSESSMENT` when an owner raised it during a sweep (§3.3), in which case its `sweep_id` names that sweep and its `inspection_id` is null. Exactly one of the two identifiers is populated on any row.

Recording an inspection deficiency belongs to the `HousingAgencyInspector` and carries `expected_inspection_version` for the concurrency check of §2.7, the `defect_definition_id` cited, the `inspectable_area` observed, the `observed_at` instant, and optionally the `unit_id`, a `supersedes_deficiency_id` and a `note`. The preconditions are evaluated in a fixed order. The inspection's `status` must be `IN_PROGRESS`, else `ERR_INSPECTION_INVALID_STATE`. The supplied `expected_inspection_version` must equal the inspection's current `version`, else `ERR_STALE_INSPECTION_VERSION`. The cited definition's `standard_id` must equal the `standard_id` pinned on the inspection at creation; a definition drawn from any other standard — including a newer standard that is now the `ADOPTED` one — is rejected with `ERR_DEFECT_DEFINITION_STANDARD_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). The submitted `inspectable_area` must equal the cited definition's `inspectable_area`, and `unit_id` must be present exactly when the area is `UNIT`, else `ERR_AREA_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). Then, for a `UNIT`-area observation, the cited unit must be held by a sample slot of this inspection whose `selection_status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `access_status` is `GRANTED`; a slot in any other access position yields `ERR_UNIT_ACCESS_NOT_GRANTED` (HTTP 409), and this check precedes the broader membership check, so a notified-but-not-yet-entered unit reports the access error. The membership check that follows requires the cited unit's `property_id` to equal the inspection's `property_id` and the unit to be held by an `ACTIVE` slot of that inspection; a unit belonging to another property, or one never drawn at all, is rejected with `ERR_UNIT_NOT_IN_SAMPLE` (HTTP 422).

**The deduction.** Both origins compute the deduction identically, at creation time, from four multipliers applied to the definition's `base_point_value`. The severity multiplier is 3.0 for `LIFE_THREATENING`, 2.0 for `SEVERE`, 1.0 for `MODERATE` and 0.5 for `LOW`. The area multiplier is 1.0 for `UNIT`, 0.8 for `INSIDE` and 0.6 for `OUTSIDE`. The recurrence multiplier is 1.5 when the observation carries a non-null `supersedes_deficiency_id`, naming the earlier deficiency this one recurs from, and 1.0 otherwise. The occupancy multiplier is 1.2 when all three of the following hold — the cited definition's `severity` is `LIFE_THREATENING`, the observation carries a non-null `unit_id`, and that unit's `occupancy_status` is `OCCUPIED` — and 1.0 in every other case. The raw deduction is the `base_point_value` multiplied by the severity multiplier, by the area multiplier, by the recurrence multiplier and by the occupancy multiplier. That raw value is rounded half up to two decimal places, and only then is the per-deficiency cap of `40.00` applied by taking the smaller of the cap and the rounded value; the result is stored as `points_deducted`. Rounding strictly precedes the cap. All four multiplier inputs are read once, at creation: a unit later flipped from `OCCUPIED` to `VACANT` never moves a deduction that has already been recorded. The row also stores `recurrence_applied`, true exactly when `supersedes_deficiency_id` is non-null and the 1.5 recurrence multiplier was therefore applied, and false otherwise.

The new row is created with `status` `OPEN`, its `severity` copied from the cited definition, and `correction_due_at` set to the `observed_at` instant advanced by the definition's `correction_deadline_hours` — which for a `LIFE_THREATENING` defect is always exactly 24 hours, the twenty-four-hour obligation. Its `corrected_at` and `verified_at` begin null.

**The record cascade.** An inspection deficiency sets off a five-link chain, and every link is part of the same request. The first link creates the deficiency row as described. The second link makes the owning inspection recompute `unit_area_points` as the sum of `points_deducted` over its deficiencies whose `inspectable_area` is `UNIT` and whose `status` is not `VOIDED`, `inside_area_points` and `outside_area_points` the same way for `INSIDE` and `OUTSIDE`, `total_points_deducted` as the sum of those three sums rounded to two decimal places, and `life_threatening_count` as the number of its non-`VOIDED` deficiencies whose `severity` is `LIFE_THREATENING`; the inspection's `version` then increments by 1, and that post-increment value is written back onto the new deficiency as `inspection_version_after_record`, giving the client the exact token it needs for its next record. The third link makes the property recompute `open_life_threatening_count` and `open_severe_count` and re-derive `readiness_posture` by the rules of §2.8 — one open life-threatening observation is enough to make the property `RESTRICTED`. The fourth link, triggered when that re-derivation lands on `RESTRICTED` or `FROZEN`, raises every work order on the property whose `repair_status` is `OPEN` and whose `priority_tier` is not already `URGENT` to `priority_tier` `URGENT` and pulls each such `due_at` 24 hours earlier, on the terms of §2.9. The fifth link makes every sweep owning one of those escalated work orders recompute `urgent_work_order_count` and increment its own `version` by 1. Separately, the cited unit recomputes `open_deficiency_count`.

**Correcting.** Only the `PropertyOwner` records a correction directly, and only on their own property: the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the `property_id` reached through the deficiency's `inspection_id` or `sweep_id`. The request carries the `corrected_at` instant and an optional `note`. The deficiency's `status` must be `OPEN` or `OVERDUE`, else `ERR_DEFICIENCY_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). For a deficiency whose `severity` is `LIFE_THREATENING`, the supplied `corrected_at` must be no later than the row's `correction_due_at`; a correction instant strictly after that deadline is rejected with `ERR_LT_CORRECTION_DEADLINE_PASSED` (HTTP 422). The consequence is deliberate and severe: a life-threatening deficiency that has already tipped into `OVERDUE` can never be corrected at all, which freezes its property and blocks its inspection's scoring permanently. Non-life-threatening deficiencies carry no such deadline check and may be corrected from `OVERDUE` freely.

A successful correction moves the deficiency `OPEN → CORRECTED` (or `OVERDUE → CORRECTED`) and stamps `corrected_at`. The property recomputes `open_life_threatening_count`, `overdue_life_threatening_count` and `open_severe_count` and re-derives `readiness_posture`; the cited unit recomputes `open_deficiency_count`; and the `version` of the deficiency and of the property each increment by 1. The same `OPEN → CORRECTED` transition is also reached without a direct request, by the work-order bubble-up of §3.3, in which case `corrected_at` takes the work order's supplied `completed_at` instead.

**Verifying.** A `HousingAgencyInspector` adjudicates a claimed correction, supplying a `verification_result` of `PASSED` or `FAILED`, the `verified_at` instant, and an optional `note`. The deficiency's `status` must be `CORRECTED`, else `ERR_DEFICIENCY_INVALID_STATE`. On `PASSED` the deficiency moves `CORRECTED → VERIFIED` and `verified_at` is stamped. On `FAILED` it moves `CORRECTED → OPEN` and `corrected_at` is cleared to null; crucially, `correction_due_at` is **not** extended, so a failed verification can leave a deficiency immediately eligible to tip overdue. Both branches make the property recompute its three deficiency counters and re-derive `readiness_posture`, and both increment the `version` of the deficiency and of the property by 1. The `CORRECTED → OPEN` cycle may repeat without limit. `VERIFIED` is terminal, as is `VOIDED`.

**Going overdue.** A deficiency in `OPEN` moves to `OVERDUE` only through a clock advance, on the exclusive boundary described in §2.5, and the property's `overdue_life_threatening_count` and `readiness_posture` follow. A `FROZEN` posture is exactly the state where one life-threatening item went overdue.

**Being voided.** The one route to `VOIDED` is a granted technical-error appeal (§3.7), which may take a deficiency out of `OPEN`, `OVERDUE`, `CORRECTED` or `VERIFIED` alike. A voided row keeps its identity but its `points_deducted` becomes `0.00` and it leaves every sum and count that filters on non-`VOIDED` status.

Deficiencies may be read singly — returning `severity`, `points_deducted`, `recurrence_applied`, `correction_due_at` and `inspection_version_after_record` among the rest — or listed filtered by `inspection_id`, `sweep_id`, `unit_id`, `defect_definition_id`, `supersedes_deficiency_id`, `source`, `status` and `severity`.

### 3.7. Scoring, the Appeal Window and Rescoring

Scoring is the inspector's act of publishing, and it is gated on two things being true. First, no deficiency of the inspection may have `severity` `LIFE_THREATENING` together with a `status` of `OPEN` or `OVERDUE`; if one does, the request is rejected with `ERR_LIFE_THREATENING_UNCORRECTED` (HTTP 409). Second, every sample slot with `selection_status` `ACTIVE` must have an `access_status` of either `GRANTED` or `REFUSED`; a slot still sitting at `NOT_NOTIFIED` or `NOTIFIED` means the walk is unfinished, and the request is rejected with `ERR_ACCESS_NOT_RECORDED` (HTTP 409). The life-safety gate is evaluated before the access gate, so an inspection failing both reports the life-threatening error. The inspection's `status` must of course be `IN_PROGRESS`. The request carries only the `scored_at` instant.

**The composite score.** Two scores are published from the same deficiency data, and the single difference between them is the denominator applied to the dwelling-unit area. Both begin from three raw area sums taken over the inspection's deficiencies whose `status` is not `VOIDED` — `OPEN`, `OVERDUE`, `CORRECTED` and `VERIFIED` rows all count, and only `VOIDED` rows leave the sums. The unit raw sum is the total `points_deducted` of those rows whose `inspectable_area` is `UNIT`; the inside raw sum and the outside raw sum are the same totals for `INSIDE` and `OUTSIDE`.

For the **provisional** variant, the per-unit dwelling figure is the unit raw sum divided by `inspected_unit_count`, rounded half up to two decimal places, or `0.00` when `inspected_unit_count` is zero. Refusals are ignored entirely by this variant, so a refused unit neither dilutes nor penalises the dwelling-unit area.

For the **final** variant, the per-unit dwelling figure is the unit raw sum plus 12.00 refusal-penalty points for each refused unit — that is, `12.00` multiplied by `refused_unit_count` — divided by `sample_size`, rounded half up to two decimal places, or `0.00` when `sample_size` is zero. Because `sample_size` still counts refusals that were never replaced, and because the penalty is added into the numerator, the same deficiency data yields a different number from the provisional variant whenever `refused_unit_count` is at least 1.

From there both variants proceed identically. The dwelling-unit area score is one hundred minus the per-unit dwelling figure, floored at `0.00` so it never goes negative, rounded half up to two decimal places. The inside area score is one hundred minus the inside raw sum, floored at `0.00`, rounded half up to two decimal places, and the outside area score is one hundred minus the outside raw sum treated the same way. Each area score is then weighted by its share taken from the standard **pinned** in the inspection's `standard_id` — never from whichever standard happens to be `ADOPTED` at scoring time: the dwelling-unit area score is multiplied by `unit_weight_pct` divided by one hundred, the inside area score by `inside_weight_pct` divided by one hundred, and the outside area score by `outside_weight_pct` divided by one hundred. **The rounding order here is pinned and load-bearing:** each of those three weighted products is rounded half up to two decimal places individually, the three rounded products are then added together, and only that sum is rounded half up to one decimal place to give the published score. Summing unrounded products and rounding once at the end is not equivalent and is not the specified behaviour.

A successful scoring moves the inspection `IN_PROGRESS → SCORED`, stamps `scored_at`, writes the provisional variant to `provisional_score` and the final variant to `final_score`, and sets `appeal_deadline_at` to the `scored_at` instant advanced by 360 hours — fifteen days. That deadline is **frozen at first scoring and is never re-derived**, no matter how many times the inspection is later rescored. The property's `last_final_score` is set to the new `final_score` and its `readiness_posture` is re-derived, which is where a final score below 60.00 can pull an otherwise clean property into `WATCH`. The inspection's `version` and the property's `version` each increment by 1.

**Filing an appeal.** Only a `PropertyOwner` may appeal, and only on their own inspection. The request names the `inspection_id` being appealed, an `appeal_type` of `TECHNICAL_ERROR` or `SCORE_CORRECTION`, a written `rationale` giving the owner's basis, the `filed_at` instant, and — as the appeal-targeting rule requires — a `contested_deficiency_id`, plus a `requested_point_adjustment` on a score-correction appeal. `TECHNICAL_ERROR` asserts the observation should not exist at all and, if granted, voids the contested deficiency outright; `SCORE_CORRECTION` accepts the observation but disputes its arithmetic and, if granted, reduces the contested deficiency's points by the amount the reviewer allows.

The appeal-targeting rule is evaluated as a single check: `contested_deficiency_id` is required for both appeal types, must name a `Deficiency` whose `inspection_id` equals the inspection being appealed, and that deficiency's `status` must be anything other than `VOIDED`; and `requested_point_adjustment` must be present exactly when `appeal_type` is `SCORE_CORRECTION` and absent otherwise. Any violation of that composite condition — a missing contested deficiency, one drawn from a different inspection, one already voided, or a misplaced requested adjustment — is rejected with `ERR_DEFICIENCY_NOT_IN_INSPECTION` (HTTP 422). Separately, the `filed_at` instant must be no later than the inspection's frozen `appeal_deadline_at`; an instant strictly after it is rejected with `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_CLOSED` (HTTP 422). The inspection's `status` must be `SCORED`.

A filed appeal is created in `SUBMITTED` with `resubmission_count` 0, `granted_point_adjustment` null, `decided_at` null, `reviewer_note` null and `additional_evidence` null, and the inspection moves `SCORED → APPEALED` with its `version` incremented by 1. Because `appeal_deadline_at` is frozen, a second appeal filed after a first has been resolved is still measured against the original window.

**Adjudicating.** Only an `AppealsOfficer` reviews, supplying a `decision` of `GRANTED`, `DENIED` or `RETURNED_FOR_INFO`, the `decided_at` instant, an optional `reviewer_note`, and a `granted_point_adjustment` where the decision calls for one. A review requested on an appeal already in `GRANTED` or `DENIED` — both terminal — is rejected with `ERR_APPEAL_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). `GRANTED` and `DENIED` are reachable from `SUBMITTED` and from `RETURNED_FOR_INFO` alike; `RETURNED_FOR_INFO` is reachable from `SUBMITTED` only.

Granting a `SCORE_CORRECTION` appeal requires `granted_point_adjustment` to be present, else `ERR_ADJUSTMENT_REQUIRED` (HTTP 422), and to be strictly greater than `0.00` while being no greater than the contested deficiency's current `points_deducted`, else `ERR_ADJUSTMENT_EXCEEDS_POINTS` (HTTP 422). The reviewer's figure is what is applied; the owner's `requested_point_adjustment` is a request, not a binding amount.

A grant sets the appeal's `status` to `GRANTED`, stamps `decided_at`, stores the `reviewer_note`, and stores `granted_point_adjustment` for a score-correction grant. Then the appeal-grant chain runs. On a `TECHNICAL_ERROR` grant, the contested deficiency's `status` becomes `VOIDED` and its `points_deducted` becomes `0.00`; because that row has now left every area sum, the inspection recomputes `unit_area_points`, `inside_area_points`, `outside_area_points`, `total_points_deducted` and `life_threatening_count`, recomputes `provisional_score` and `final_score` from the surviving deficiencies by the same two variants described above, sets `rescored_at` to the appeal's `decided_at`, and returns `APPEALED → SCORED`; and because `final_score` changed, the property's `last_final_score` is rewritten and its `readiness_posture` re-derived, which is what can move a property `RESTRICTED → READY` when the voided row was its last open life-threatening deficiency. On a `SCORE_CORRECTION` grant, the contested deficiency's `points_deducted` is reduced by `granted_point_adjustment`, floored at `0.00` so it never goes negative, and the identical inspection rescore, `rescored_at` stamp and property posture re-derivation follow. The `version` of the appeal, of the contested deficiency, of the inspection and of the property each increment by 1.

A denial sets the appeal's `status` to `DENIED`, stamps `decided_at` and stores the `reviewer_note`, and returns the inspection `APPEALED → SCORED` with every score field left exactly as it was and `rescored_at` left exactly as it was — a denial never writes `rescored_at`, not even to the decision instant. The `version` of the appeal and of the inspection each increment by 1.

A return for more information sets the appeal's `status` to `RETURNED_FOR_INFO`, stamps `decided_at` and stores the `reviewer_note`, and leaves the inspection in `APPEALED` precisely because a non-terminal appeal still exists. The return of the inspection to `SCORED` happens only once no appeal of that inspection remains in `SUBMITTED` or `RETURNED_FOR_INFO`; the inspection's `version` increments by 1 when that return occurs.

**Resubmitting.** Only the `PropertyOwner` resubmits, and only their own: the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the appealed inspection's `property_id`. The request carries `additional_evidence`, the appeal's `status` must be `RETURNED_FOR_INFO`, and `resubmission_count` must be strictly less than 2; a third attempt is rejected with `ERR_APPEAL_RESUBMIT_LIMIT` (HTTP 409), after which the appeal can only be granted or denied. A successful resubmission moves the appeal `RETURNED_FOR_INFO → SUBMITTED`, stores `additional_evidence`, increments `resubmission_count` by 1, clears `decided_at` to null, and increments the appeal's `version` by 1. The submitted-returned-submitted cycle therefore runs at most twice.

Appeals may be read singly, returning `resubmission_count` and `granted_point_adjustment` among the rest, or listed filtered by `inspection_id`, `contested_deficiency_id`, `appeal_type` and `status`.

### 3.8. Finalization, Corrective Action Plans and Follow-Up Inspections

Finalization is where a published score becomes a consequence. The inspector supplies the `finalized_at` instant, and the operation is refused while any appeal of the inspection remains in `SUBMITTED` or `RETURNED_FOR_INFO`, with `ERR_APPEAL_PENDING` (HTTP 409). From `SCORED` the operation forks on whether a corrective action plan is owed: `cap_required` evaluates true when the inspection's `final_score` is below 90.00 **or** its `life_threatening_count` is at least 1, and false only when the `final_score` is at least 90.00 **and** the `life_threatening_count` is exactly 0.

On the plan-required branch the inspection moves `SCORED → CAP_REQUIRED`, sets `cap_required` to true, stamps `finalized_at`, and derives the deadline trio. The derivation order is pinned because the dependencies look circular — the appeal window bounds when an appeal may be filed, a granted appeal moves `final_score`, and `final_score` selects the plan window — so it is stated as an explicit sequence. The appeal deadline was derived at first scoring from `scored_at` alone and never reads any score (§3.7). Each granted appeal recomputes `final_score` and stamps `rescored_at`, leaving `appeal_deadline_at` untouched. At finalization the window tier reads the **current** `final_score`, meaning the post-rescore value when an appeal was granted and the original value otherwise: a `final_score` of at least 90.00 gives `cap_window_days` 60, at least 80.00 gives 45, at least 60.00 gives 30, and anything below 60.00 gives 15. Each boundary is inclusive on the upper side, so exactly 90.00 lands in the 60-day tier and exactly 80.00 in the 45-day tier. The anchor instant is `rescored_at` when it is non-null and `scored_at` otherwise; `cap_due_at` is that anchor advanced by `cap_window_days` multiplied by 24 hours. `followup_due_at` is `cap_due_at` advanced by a further 10 days when the inspection's `life_threatening_count` is at least 1, or by 30 days when it is 0 — a life-safety history buys a tighter follow-up, not a looser one. Both are ISO 8601 UTC instants with three fractional digits, and `cap_window_days` is an integer. The inspection's `version` increments by 1.

On the no-plan branch the inspection moves `SCORED → CLOSED`, sets `cap_required` to false, stamps `finalized_at`, and leaves `cap_window_days`, `cap_due_at` and `followup_due_at` all null. This branch additionally freezes unit history: every `Unit` carrying a non-`VOIDED` deficiency of this inspection recomputes its `prior_deficiency_count`, which is what feeds the next cycle's sampling ranking. And when the closing inspection's own `inspection_kind` is `FOLLOW_UP`, the `CorrectiveActionPlan` whose `follow_up_inspection_id` names it moves `FOLLOW_UP_SCHEDULED → COMPLETED`, closing the remediation loop. The inspection's `version` increments by 1.

A third finalization path exists on the parent side. An inspection sitting in `FOLLOW_UP_SCHEDULED` moves `FOLLOW_UP_SCHEDULED → CLOSED` once the inspection named by its plan's `follow_up_inspection_id` has itself reached `CLOSED`; attempting it earlier is rejected with `ERR_FOLLOW_UP_NOT_CLOSED` (HTTP 409). It stamps `finalized_at` and increments the inspection's `version` by 1. `CLOSED` is terminal.

**Submitting a plan.** Only the `PropertyOwner` submits, naming the `inspection_id` the plan addresses, a `narrative` describing the remediation, a `total_estimated_cost_cents` in integer cents of at least 0, a `milestone_count` of at least 1, a `target_completion_at` giving the owner's committed completion instant, and the `submitted_at` instant of the submission itself. Two fiscal constraints bind: `total_estimated_cost_cents` must be no greater than the property's `capital_reserve_cents`, else `ERR_CAP_COST_EXCEEDS_RESERVE` (HTTP 422); and `target_completion_at` must be no later than the inspection's `cap_due_at`, else `ERR_CAP_TARGET_AFTER_DEADLINE` (HTTP 422). The plan is created in `SUBMITTED` with `revision_count` 0, `follow_up_inspection_id` null, `decided_at` null and `reviewer_note` null.

**Reviewing a plan.** Only a `QualityAssuranceReviewer` adjudicates, supplying a `decision` of `APPROVED` or `REJECTED`, the `decided_at` instant and an optional `reviewer_note`. Both branches require the plan's `status` to be `SUBMITTED` and the owning inspection's `status` to be `CAP_REQUIRED`; a plan transition not admitted from the plan's current status, or one whose owning inspection is in the wrong status, is rejected with `ERR_CAP_INVALID_STATE` (HTTP 409). On `APPROVED` the plan moves `SUBMITTED → APPROVED`, stamps `decided_at`, stores the `reviewer_note`, and drives the inspection `CAP_REQUIRED → CAP_APPROVED`; the `version` of the plan and of the inspection each increment by 1. On `REJECTED` the plan moves `SUBMITTED → REJECTED`, stamps `decided_at`, stores the `reviewer_note`, and leaves the inspection sitting in `CAP_REQUIRED`; only the plan's `version` increments by 1.

**Resubmitting a plan.** Only the `PropertyOwner` resubmits, and only their own: the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the inspection's `property_id`. The request supplies a revised `narrative`, `total_estimated_cost_cents`, `milestone_count` and `target_completion_at`; the plan's `status` must be `REJECTED`; and the same two fiscal constraints re-apply, with `total_estimated_cost_cents` checked against the property's current `capital_reserve_cents` under `ERR_CAP_COST_EXCEEDS_RESERVE` and `target_completion_at` checked against the inspection's `cap_due_at` under `ERR_CAP_TARGET_AFTER_DEADLINE`. A successful resubmission moves the plan `REJECTED → SUBMITTED`, overwrites those four fields, increments `revision_count` by 1, clears both `decided_at` and `reviewer_note` to null, and increments the plan's `version` by 1. The submitted-rejected-submitted cycle is unbounded — unlike the appeal cycle, there is no revision limit.

**Scheduling the follow-up.** Only a `HousingAgencyInspector` schedules it, supplying the `scheduled_start` and `scheduled_end` of the new on-site window. The plan's `status` must be `APPROVED`. The `scheduled_end` must be strictly after the `scheduled_start`, else `ERR_INVALID_TIME_WINDOW`. The `scheduled_start` must be no earlier than the plan's `target_completion_at` and no later than the parent inspection's `followup_due_at`; a window that opens before the owner's own committed completion instant, or after the agency's follow-up deadline, is rejected with `ERR_FOLLOW_UP_TOO_EARLY` (HTTP 422). And the new window must clear the same 72-hour-buffered, closed-interval conflict test against every other live inspection window on the property described in §3.4, else `ERR_INSPECTION_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.

On success a fresh `Inspection` is created with `inspection_kind` `FOLLOW_UP`, `status` `SCHEDULED`, and the same `property_id` and `standard_id` as its parent — the follow-up inherits the parent's pinned standard rather than picking up whatever is currently `ADOPTED`. Its identifier is written to the plan's `follow_up_inspection_id`, the plan moves `APPROVED → FOLLOW_UP_SCHEDULED`, and the parent inspection moves `CAP_APPROVED → FOLLOW_UP_SCHEDULED`. Every `Unit` carrying a non-`VOIDED` deficiency of the parent inspection recomputes its `prior_deficiency_count` at this point, so the follow-up's own sample draw ranks units on the history the parent inspection just made. The `version` of the plan and of the parent inspection each increment by 1. `COMPLETED` is the plan's terminal state, reached when the follow-up closes.

Plans may be read singly, returning `revision_count` and `follow_up_inspection_id` among the rest, or listed filtered by `inspection_id`, `follow_up_inspection_id` and `status`.

### 3.9. Capital Needs Planning and Reserve Allocation

The last stage turns inspection history into a funding plan. A defect that the agency has now cited across more than one closed inspection is not a maintenance incident; it is a capital need, and the owner's reserve should be pointed at it in proportion to how badly it recurs. Only the `PropertyOwner` may recompute, naming the `property_id`, the `expected_property_version` token of §2.7, and optionally a `recurrence_basis` of `INSPECTION_SCOPED` or `CALENDAR_SCOPED`; when the basis is omitted it defaults to `CALENDAR_SCOPED`. The operation returns the newly created plan generation.

**Establishing recurrence.** The history considered is the property's inspections whose `status` is `CLOSED` and whose `finalized_at` is non-null, ordered by `finalized_at` descending with the later `created_at` breaking ties first; the `id` is never consulted as a tie-break. Under `INSPECTION_SCOPED` the scope is the first 2 entries of that ordering, regardless of how old they are — which means a defect appearing in three closed inspections can never score a recurrence count above 2 on this basis. Under `CALENDAR_SCOPED` the scope is every entry of that history whose `finalized_at` falls at or after the instant 730 days before the current logical clock reading; the lower bound is inclusive, so an inspection finalized exactly on the boundary instant is inside the window. Because the calendar basis admits however many in-window inspections exist, the same defect history yields a strictly larger recurrence count than the inspection-scoped basis whenever more than 2 in-window inspections cite the defect.

Within the chosen scope, for each `DefectDefinition` cited by at least one deficiency of a scoped inspection, `recurrence_count` is the number of **distinct** inspections in the scope holding a deficiency whose `defect_definition_id` names that definition, whose `source` is `INSPECTION`, and whose `status` is not `VOIDED`. Distinctness is taken over the inspection, so two observations of the same defect inside one inspection count once. Self-assessment deficiencies are excluded outright — an owner's own sweep findings can never inflate a capital need. Only definitions whose `recurrence_count` reaches 2 survive; the rest produce no item at all.

**Priority.** Each surviving definition yields one `CapitalNeedItem` naming the `property_id` and the `defect_definition_id`, carrying the `recurrence_basis` that produced it, the `recurrence_count` just derived, `status` `ACTIVE`, and a `computed_at` set to the logical-clock instant of this recompute. Its `priority_score` is the `recurrence_count` multiplied by the definition's `base_point_value` multiplied by the same severity multiplier used for deductions — 3.0 for `LIFE_THREATENING`, 2.0 for `SEVERE`, 1.0 for `MODERATE`, 0.5 for `LOW` — rounded half up to two decimal places, and then capped by taking the smaller of `100.00` and that rounded value. As with the deduction rule, rounding precedes the cap.

**Allocating the reserve.** The items of this generation are ordered by `priority_score` descending with the definition's `code` ascending as the tie-break, and `allocation_rank` is assigned 1 upward in that order. Let the priority total be the sum of `priority_score` across the generation. When that total is zero, or when the generation contains no items at all, every `allocated_reserve_cents` is 0. Otherwise the distribution runs in two pinned steps. First, each item's share is the property's `capital_reserve_cents` multiplied by that item's `priority_score`, divided by the priority total, then **truncated downward** to whole cents. Per-item half-up rounding is explicitly wrong here and produces different per-item cents than this order. Second, because flooring under-allocates, the leftover — the property's `capital_reserve_cents` minus the sum of the floored shares — is handed out one cent at a time, walking the items in `allocation_rank` order and wrapping around to rank 1 again if the leftover exceeds the number of items, until nothing remains. The invariant this guarantees is exact: the sum of `allocated_reserve_cents` across the `ACTIVE` generation equals the property's `capital_reserve_cents` precisely.

**Superseding the previous generation.** Every `CapitalNeedItem` of the property that was `ACTIVE` before this recompute moves `ACTIVE → SUPERSEDED` and has its `allocated_reserve_cents` set to 0, so superseded rows leave the sum entirely; `SUPERSEDED` is terminal. The property then recomputes `allocated_reserve_total_cents` over the new `ACTIVE` generation only, and the `version` of each superseded item and of the property each increment by 1.

Capital need items may be read singly, returning `recurrence_basis`, `recurrence_count`, `priority_score`, `allocation_rank` and `allocated_reserve_cents`, or listed filtered by `property_id`, `defect_definition_id`, `status` and `recurrence_basis`.

**Seeding history.** Exercising recurrence-driven planning would otherwise require replaying an entire inspection lifecycle for every historical cycle, so the platform exposes an admin-only seeding capability. A `PlatformAdmin` supplies the `property_id`, the `standard_id` — which must be in `ADOPTED`, else `ERR_STANDARD_NOT_ADOPTED` — a `finalized_at` instant, a `defect_definition_ids` collection naming the definitions the historical inspection cited, and optionally a `unit_id` to attribute the observations to. It creates one `Inspection` with `inspection_kind` `INITIAL`, `status` `CLOSED`, `scored_at` equal to the supplied `finalized_at`, `finalized_at` as supplied, and `cap_required` false; and one `Deficiency` per entry of `defect_definition_ids`, each with `source` `INSPECTION`, `status` `VERIFIED`, and `observed_at`, `corrected_at` and `verified_at` all equal to the supplied `finalized_at`. The cited unit recomputes `prior_deficiency_count`. The property's `readiness_posture` is deliberately left untouched, because no injected deficiency is left open.
