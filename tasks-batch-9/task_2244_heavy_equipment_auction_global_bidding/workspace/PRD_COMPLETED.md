# Heavy Equipment Auction API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Industrial equipment auctioneers sit at the intersection of three businesses that rarely share a system of record: a yard operation that receives, inspects, refurbishes and photographs multi-tonne machines; a global sales floor that must admit bidders from dozens of jurisdictions with different deposit, credit, tax and sanctions profiles; and a settlement desk that has to reconcile buyer money, seller money, customs paperwork and physical custody before a machine may roll off the property. Today those three functions are typically stitched together with spreadsheets, and the seams show: a lot gets catalogued with an unverifiable hour meter, a bidder from an embargoed country clears registration because the deposit arrived first, an excavator is released through the gate while its title is still encumbered, or a seller is paid on a sale the buyer later abandons.

This document specifies the Heavy Equipment Auction API, a single transactional backbone for consignment intake through load-out. It governs the whole chain: consignment agreements and the unreserved-sale disclosure; equipment inspection with hour-meter verification and lien search; refurbishment work orders with independent quality assurance; auction scheduling across live on-site rings and timed online events; global bidder registration with deposits, leverage, credit approval and country-derived payment terms; the bidding discipline that keeps a paddle inside its cap; hammer, reserve outcomes and consignor negotiation; buyer premium, taxes, documentation fees and export clearance; seller-side settlement waterfalls; and finally load-out bay scheduling, gate release, storage fees and the remedies applied when a buyer walks away from a purchase.

Two design commitments run through every requirement below. First, **money is never approximate**: every monetary amount is an integer number of cents, every rate application is rounded at a specified step, and the step at which rounding happens is part of the contract rather than an implementation detail. Second, **physical custody and financial obligation are tracked on separate axes**: a machine can be fully paid for and still be locked in the yard because customs has not cleared, and it can be released from the yard while a re-billing later reopens a shortfall. Implementers should expect the state model to reflect that separation rather than collapsing it into one status field.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the system-wide contracts: authentication, response shape, pagination, resource conventions, arithmetic discipline, concurrency control, and the fixed order in which the server rejects a bad request. These rules apply to every endpoint unless a later section explicitly narrows them.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting user, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which that user is acting. If either header is missing or empty, or if `X-User-Role` does not name a role the system recognises, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before the body is inspected at all.

Seven roles exist, and their names must be spelled exactly as follows: `PlatformAdmin`, `Consignor`, `IntakeInspector`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `AuctionOperator`, `SettlementOfficer`, and `Bidder`. Authorisation is decided from the endpoint alone: if the acting role is not one of the roles listed for the endpoint in §4, the request fails with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403) regardless of whether the payload is well formed.

The roles divide along a segregation-of-duties boundary. A `PlatformAdmin` is the back-office administrator who owns reference data — yards, load-out bays, consignor records, bidder records — and the logical clock. A `Consignor` is the equipment seller. An `IntakeInspector` is the yard inspector who performs inspection, hour-meter verification, photography and refurbishment quality assurance. A `ComplianceOfficer` is the title, lien and customs specialist. An `AuctionOperator` runs the sale itself: intake decisions, scheduling, catalogue assignment, opening, hammering and closing. A `SettlementOfficer` staffs the money desk: deposits, credit decisions, invoice finalisation, release authorisation, default and abandonment remedies, and seller settlements. A `Bidder` is the buyer, who owns its own registrations, bids, payments and load-out bookings. Every role may read the resources relevant to its function; the read grants per endpoint are listed in §4.

Two of the seven roles are **entity-backed**, meaning `X-User-ID` names a persisted row:

- When `X-User-Role` is `Consignor`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Consignor`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Consignor` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID` — directly for a consignment, and transitively through the consignment for a lot, a condition report, a sale or a settlement.
- When `X-User-Role` is `Bidder`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Bidder`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Bidder` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID` — directly for a registration, and transitively through the registration for a bid, an invoice, a payment or a load-out slot.

The remaining five roles are **opaque**: when `X-User-Role` is `PlatformAdmin`, `IntakeInspector`, `ComplianceOfficer`, `AuctionOperator` or `SettlementOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity, and the server never compares it to any entity's `id`. The server does record it where a transition says so — `inspector_user_id` on a condition report authored by an `IntakeInspector`, `qa_signed_off_by_user_id` on a refurbishment order passed by an `IntakeInspector`, and `credit_approved_by_user_id` on a registration approved by a `SettlementOfficer` — and those recorded values are later compared against the acting `X-User-ID` for segregation-of-duties purposes (§2.10), but they are never treated as entity identifiers.

Ownership enforcement is separate from role enforcement. When an entity-backed role acts on a resource belonging to a different `Consignor` or `Bidder`, the request fails with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403). This applies to consignment submission, the consignor's policy acknowledgement, consignor counteroffers, outright rejection of a provisional sale, the buyer's response to a counteroffer, bid placement, payment recording, load-out slot booking, slot check-in, slot cancellation, and registration cancellation.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints accept two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of records returned; it defaults to 10, has a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100. `offset` skips a number of leading records; it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` outside the range 1 through 100, a negative `offset`, or a non-numeric value for either, is a request-shape error and yields `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; a parameter present but empty counts as absent and takes its default.

Unless a specific endpoint states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending, ties being broken by the order in which the rows were written. The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`, where `total_count` counts every record matching the filters before pagination is applied.

Most list endpoints additionally accept filter parameters, which narrow the result set without changing the ordering. The available filters are: `status` on the auctions of a yard, on a consignor's consignments, on a lot's refurbishment orders, on registrations, on bids, on sales, on the payments of an invoice, and on load-out slots; `is_active` on the load-out bays of a yard; `seller_type` on consignors; `lifecycle_status` and `title_status` on lots, together with `consignment_id`, `auction_id` and `replacement_of_lot_id`; `yard_id` and `kind` on auctions; `country_code` and `country_tier` on bidders; `bidder_id` and `auction_id` on registrations; `registration_id` and `lot_id` on bids; `auction_id`, `lot_id`, `winning_bid_id` and `registration_id` on sales; `sale_id`, `bidder_id`, `registration_id`, `payment_status` and `release_status` on invoices; `lifecycle_status` on the lots of a consignment; and `invoice_id`, `bay_id` and `supersedes_slot_id` on load-out slots. A filter supplied but empty counts as absent; a filter whose value is not a member of the enumeration it draws on is a request-shape error yielding `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and a query parameter the endpoint does not recognise is ignored rather than refused.

### 2.4. Flat Resource Topology and Identifier Resolution

The API is deliberately flat. Every collection is a top-level path segment and every action hangs off the resource that owns the state being changed, so there are no parent/child URL chains. Relationships are carried by explicit foreign keys in request bodies instead: a lot names its `consignment_id`, a registration names its `bidder_id` and `auction_id`, a bid names its `registration_id` and `lot_id`, a payment names its `invoice_id`, a packet names its `invoice_id`, a slot names its `invoice_id` and `bay_id`, and a consignor settlement names its `sale_id`.

Identifiers are nevertheless resolved in a strict order, and the order determines which 404 a caller sees. Path identifiers are resolved first, left to right, and each has a dedicated error: `yard_id` yields `ERR_YARD_NOT_FOUND`, `bay_id` yields `ERR_LOADOUT_BAY_NOT_FOUND`, `consignor_id` yields `ERR_CONSIGNOR_NOT_FOUND`, `consignment_id` yields `ERR_CONSIGNMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `lot_id` yields `ERR_LOT_NOT_FOUND`, `report_id` yields `ERR_CONDITION_REPORT_NOT_FOUND`, `order_id` yields `ERR_REFURBISHMENT_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `auction_id` yields `ERR_AUCTION_NOT_FOUND`, `bidder_id` yields `ERR_BIDDER_NOT_FOUND`, `registration_id` yields `ERR_REGISTRATION_NOT_FOUND`, `bid_id` yields `ERR_BID_NOT_FOUND`, `sale_id` yields `ERR_SALE_NOT_FOUND`, `invoice_id` yields `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_FOUND`, `payment_id` yields `ERR_PAYMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `packet_id` yields `ERR_EXPORT_PACKET_NOT_FOUND`, `slot_id` yields `ERR_LOADOUT_SLOT_NOT_FOUND`, and `settlement_id` yields `ERR_SETTLEMENT_NOT_FOUND`. A handful of reverse-lookup list endpoints spell their owning identifier simply as `id` in the path; those resolve to the same error as the named form for that collection. Every one of these errors carries HTTP 404.

Only after every path identifier resolves does the server resolve the foreign keys carried in the request body, and it resolves them in the order the fields are declared for that entity. The same error catalogue applies. Consequently a request that names both a non-existent path resource and a non-existent body foreign key always reports the path resource's 404, and a bidder registration naming a bad `bidder_id` and a bad `auction_id` reports `ERR_BIDDER_NOT_FOUND` because `bidder_id` is declared first. Body foreign keys are checked on consignment creation, lot creation, auction creation, load-out bay creation, bidder registration, bid placement, payment recording, export packet creation, load-out slot booking, consignor settlement posting, condition report filing, and lot assignment. Note that `replacement_of_lot_id` on lot creation is itself a foreign key to another lot and produces `ERR_LOT_NOT_FOUND` when it does not resolve.

### 2.5. Common Resource Properties and the Logical Clock

Every resource carries a server-minted `id`: a UUID with no type prefix. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the row was first written. `created_at`, and the `updated_at` described just below, are stamped from the server's own real time of day rather than from the logical clock introduced later in this section, so setting the clock does not change what a subsequent write stamps into them.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the resource is created and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation the requested transition performs on that resource in its own right — but, as §2.9 sets out, not when the resource is merely on the receiving end of a derived-counter recomputation triggered by a write elsewhere. Two resources are exceptions because they are append-only records of an event rather than mutable state: a `Bid` and a `Payment` carry only `id` and `created_at`. Neither has an `updated_at` field and neither has a `version` field; a bid that is later marked `LOSING` and a payment that is later `REVERSED` are re-stamped without any version accounting, and no caller may supply a version for them.

All temporal rules — storage accrual, payment due dates, auction window checks, and slot check-in windows — read a **logical clock** rather than wall-clock time. The clock reads `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` until it is set. A `PlatformAdmin` may advance or rewind it through an administrative test-driving capability that accepts a single `current_utc` instant and echoes back `{ current_utc }` with HTTP 200 — the echoed value is the accepted instant exactly as the caller supplied it, echoed back verbatim rather than reformatted to the three-fractional-digit shape used for stored resource timestamps; the call mutates no entity, but every subsequent request reads the new value. Three system-stamped timestamps are taken directly from this clock at the moment of acceptance: `placed_at` on a bid, `received_at` on a payment, and `hammered_at` on a sale. `hammered_at` is additionally the ordering key for the deposit waterfall and for the chronological bid-cap replay, so it is normative, not merely informational.

### 2.6. Cents, Rates and Rounding

All monetary amounts in this system are **integer numbers of cents**. There are no fractional-cent amounts anywhere in a request body, a stored field, or a response.

Whenever a monetary amount is multiplied by a rate, the product is rounded to a whole number of cents using the **round-half-up** convention: a value whose fractional part is exactly one half rounds away from zero. Crucially, the *step* at which rounding is applied is normative because it changes the answer. Where a formula below describes rounding per tier and then summing the tiers, an implementation that sums first and rounds once will produce a different figure and is wrong. The same holds for the four weighted components of a condition grade, which are each rounded before they are added.

Three non-monetary quantities have their own precision. Condition component scores are supplied with at most one decimal place; a score with two or more decimals is a request-shape error. The derived `grade_score` is carried to 2 decimal places, round half up. `hour_meter_variance_pct` is carried to 4 decimal places, round half up. `tax_rate_applied` is stored to 4 decimal places. Each of these non-integer quantities is carried in JSON as a bare number rather than a string, and a JSON number preserves no trailing zeros, so a `tax_rate_applied` held as 0.1300 is emitted as `0.13` and a `grade_score` held as 82.00 as `82.0`. Chargeable storage days are computed with a **floor**, never a round: a partial day accrues nothing. Bid-cap and exposure arithmetic is exact integer arithmetic throughout; no rounding occurs at any intermediate step.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Sales and Invoices

Two write operations are guarded by optimistic concurrency control, and only those two. In both cases the guard exists because the write is *legitimate on its own merits* — it supersedes an earlier action rather than conflicting with it — so state inspection alone cannot tell the server that the client is acting on data it has not seen.

The first is the consignor counteroffer on a sale. A counteroffer request must carry `expected_sale_version`, a non-optional field holding the client's last observed `version` of the target `Sale`. If it does not equal the sale's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SALE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Without this guard, the counter-price monotonicity rule would be evaluated against a negotiation round the consignor never saw.

The second is load-out slot booking. A slot-booking request must carry `expected_invoice_version`, a non-optional field holding the client's last observed `version` of the target `Invoice`. If it does not equal the invoice's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_INVOICE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Because a new booking legitimately supersedes the invoice's existing booked slot, a stale version is the only deterministic signal that the client is rebooking on top of a cancellation, a finalisation restamp, or a release change it has not observed.

No other endpoint accepts a version field. Callers must not send one, and the server must not require one.

### 2.8. Rejection Order: the Validation Ladder

When a request violates several rules at once, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fixed rather than arbitrary. The server evaluates checks in the ascending order listed below and returns the **first** violation it finds. This ordering is part of the contract: a bid that is both placed on a closed auction and above the paddle's cap must report the closed auction, and an embargoed bidder whose deposit is also undersized must report the embargo.

1. Identity headers are present and `X-User-Role` names a known role — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Evaluated before the body is inspected.
2. The acting role is listed for the endpoint — otherwise `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403). Decided from the endpoint alone, so a malformed payload sent by the wrong role still returns 403.
3. The request body and query parameters satisfy the declared types, enum domains, string lengths, numeric ranges, ISO 8601 datetime formats and required-field lists — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). This covers pagination bounds, `slot_end` strictly after `slot_start`, `window_end` strictly after `window_start`, condition scores with more than one decimal place, and unknown enum members. Two families of field rule are deliberately *not* settled at this rung. A field that becomes mandatory only because of a discriminator value carried in the same payload — `correction_note` on a `RETURN` intake decision, `decline_reason` on a `DECLINE` credit decision, `actual_cost_cents` on `SUBMIT_QA`, `qa_note` on `QA_REWORK`, `lienholder_name` when `encumbrance_found` is true, and `title_document_ref`, `customs_entry_ref` and `rejection_reason` on the `ATTACH_TITLE`, `FILE_CUSTOMS` and `REJECT` packet transitions — is checked only after the target's own state guard at rung 8 has passed, and its absence (an omitted value, an explicit null, or an empty string) is reported as `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409) rather than 400. And `tonnage_kg`'s upper bound cannot be evaluated here because the bound is the lot's own `weight_kg`, which is only reachable once the body foreign keys have resolved at rung 5; it is therefore enforced immediately after that resolution, and a tonnage above the lot's weight is still `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). A field that is unconditionally required by its endpoint stays at this rung and yields 400 as usual, including `reject_reason` on sale rejection, `withdrawal_reason` on lot withdrawal, `reason` on invoice abandonment, `gate_pass_ref` on load-out completion and `lien_search_reference` on a lien search.
4. Every path identifier resolves — otherwise the matching not-found error (404), per §2.4.
5. Every body foreign key resolves — otherwise the matching not-found error (404), per §2.4.
6. For an entity-backed role, the acting `X-User-ID` matches the owning consignor or bidder reachable from the target — otherwise `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403). Deliberately after existence, so probing a stranger's identifier yields 403 rather than leaking a 404 about someone else's row.
7. `expected_sale_version` or `expected_invoice_version` equals the parent's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_SALE_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_INVOICE_VERSION` (412). Checked before any state precondition, so a stale version on a resource that is also in the wrong state still returns 412.
8. The target is in a state from which the requested transition is declared — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409). This is the generic guard; every terminal-state lockdown and every replayed one-shot action lands here. The more specific state guards that follow are only reached once this one passes, with three deliberate exceptions, each called out again where it is specified: the duplicate-condition-report and duplicate-settlement guards of rung 17, and — on catalogue assignment only — the already-assigned-elsewhere limb of rung 23. Those three are evaluated ahead of this generic guard, because the earlier action each of them detects has itself already moved the target out of the state this guard would require, which would otherwise make the named error unreachable. Conversely, wherever a workflow narrative in §3 states a precondition without naming the refusal it carries, that precondition is enforced at this rung and its refusal is `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409). The complete list is: the at-least-one-lot rule on consignment submission; the sibling-lifecycle rule on intake acceptance; the no-sale-yet rule on lot withdrawal; the not-already-withdrawn rule on ordering a lien search; the parent-lot-`INSPECTED` rule on starting refurbishment out of `DRAFT`; the elapsed-window and at-least-one-assigned-lot rules on opening an auction; the published-condition-report rule on catalogue assignment; the standing-high-bid rule on hammering; the no-live-bid rule on registration cancellation; the `CONFIRMED`-or-`SETTLED` sale rule on release authorisation; the `UNPAID`-or-`PARTIALLY_PAID` rule on recording a payment; the not-yet-`LOADED_OUT` rule on defaulting an invoice; the custody-axis rules on abandonment and on slot booking; the exactly-one-`BOOKED`-slot rule on rebooking; and the `LOADOUT_BOOKED` rule on load-out completion.
9. The auction is `OPEN` when a bid is placed or a lot is hammered — otherwise `ERR_AUCTION_NOT_OPEN` (409).
10. The registration is `APPROVED` when a bid is placed or a counteroffer is answered — otherwise `ERR_REGISTRATION_NOT_APPROVED` (409).
11. The lot's consignment is `CATALOGED` when the lot is assigned to an auction — otherwise `ERR_CONSIGNMENT_NOT_CATALOGED` (409).
12. Every refurbishment order of the lot is `COMPLETED` when its condition report is published — otherwise `ERR_REFURBISHMENT_INCOMPLETE` (409). Vacuously satisfied when the lot has no refurbishment orders.
13. The lot's `title_status` is `LIEN_CLEAR` or `LIEN_RELEASED` — otherwise `ERR_TITLE_NOT_CLEAR` (409). This guards three operations: catalogue assignment, release authorisation, and attaching a title to an export packet.
14. The invoice's `payment_status` is `PAID` when release is authorised — otherwise `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_PAID` (409). A shortfall of one cent is still a shortfall.
15. For a cross-border invoice, an export packet has reached `EXPORT_CLEARED` when release is authorised — otherwise `ERR_EXPORT_NOT_CLEARED` (409). Domestic invoices skip this rule entirely.
16. Every `CONFIRMED` sale of the auction has a `POSTED` settlement and no sale is still `PROVISIONAL` or `CONSIGNOR_COUNTERED` when the auction is reconciled — otherwise `ERR_SETTLEMENT_INCOMPLETE` (409).
17. Duplicate-create guards, all 409: the lot has no condition report yet, otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_CONDITION_REPORT`; the sale has no `POSTED` settlement yet, otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_SETTLEMENT`; the bidder-and-auction pair has no registration in `REQUESTED`, `APPROVED` or `SUSPENDED`, otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_REGISTRATION`. Replaying a create that already produced a row is a conflict, not a second row.
18. The bidder's `country_code` is not embargoed — otherwise `ERR_COUNTRY_EMBARGOED` (403). Guards registration creation, the credit decision, and export packet creation.
19. The bidder's `default_strike_count` is below the strike limit — otherwise `ERR_BIDDER_SUSPENDED` (403). Guards registration creation, the credit decision, and bid placement.
20. The acting `X-User-ID` differs from the segregation anchor on the target's chain — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403). Guards refurbishment QA pass and invoice fee waiver.
21. The condition report verified the hour meter, or the lot carries the hours-not-warranted disclaimer — otherwise `ERR_HOUR_METER_UNVERIFIED` (422). Guards catalogue assignment.
22. The chosen bay belongs to the yard that held the auction — otherwise `ERR_BAY_YARD_MISMATCH` (409). Guards slot booking.
23. The lot's consignment has been accepted at intake and the lot is not already assigned elsewhere — otherwise `ERR_LOT_AUCTION_MISMATCH` (409). Guards catalogue assignment and single-lot hammering.
24. The packet's `destination_country_code` equals the buyer's `country_code` and the invoice is cross-border — otherwise `ERR_DESTINATION_COUNTRY_MISMATCH` (422). Guards export packet creation.
25. `serial_number` is unique across all lots — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_SERIAL_NUMBER` (409). Guards lot creation.
26. A reserve price is only allowed where both the auction and the consignment agreement permit one — otherwise `ERR_RESERVE_NOT_ALLOWED` (422). Guards catalogue assignment.
27. An unreserved consignment may only be submitted once the consignor has signed the disclosure — otherwise `ERR_UNRESERVED_POLICY_NOT_ACK` (422). Guards consignment submission.
28. Cyclic lap limits, all 422: `rework_round` below its limit, otherwise `ERR_MAX_REWORK_ROUNDS_EXCEEDED`; `counter_round` below its limit, otherwise `ERR_MAX_COUNTER_ROUNDS_EXCEEDED`; `reopen_count` below its limit, otherwise `ERR_MAX_PACKET_REOPENS_EXCEEDED`. The lap that would exceed the limit is refused rather than silently capped.
29. `counter_price_cents` is at least the hammer price, at most the reserve, and no higher than the previous counteroffer — otherwise `ERR_COUNTER_PRICE_NOT_MONOTONIC` (422).
30. The chosen bay has `is_active` set to true — otherwise `ERR_BAY_INACTIVE` (409). Guards slot booking.
31. Aggregate reserved tonnage across time-overlapping slots of the bay stays within the crane rating — otherwise `ERR_BAY_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (409). Guards slot booking.
32. A live on-site window, extended by the yard's teardown buffer, does not overlap another live on-site auction at that yard — otherwise `ERR_AUCTION_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409). Guards auction creation and rescheduling.
33. Slot interval rules, both guarding slot booking: the requested interval does not overlap another live slot of the same invoice, otherwise `ERR_SLOT_OVERLAP` (409); and the interval sits inside the auction's load-out window, otherwise `ERR_SLOT_OUTSIDE_DEADLINE` (422).
34. Slot timing rules: check-in happens inside the booked window and a no-show is only recorded once the window has closed — otherwise `ERR_SLOT_NOT_IN_WINDOW` (409). Guards slot check-in and slot cancellation.
35. The logical clock is strictly past the invoice's `payment_due_at` when the invoice is defaulted — otherwise `ERR_PAYMENT_NOT_OVERDUE` (409). Exactly at the due instant is still on time.
36. Deposit admission, both 422 and both guarding registration creation: `deposit_hold_cents` reaches the tier minimum, otherwise `ERR_DEPOSIT_BELOW_MINIMUM`; and the bidder has at least that much free, otherwise `ERR_DEPOSIT_UNAVAILABLE`. This family is deliberately evaluated last, so an embargoed bidder with an undersized deposit never reaches it.
37. The bid amount is within the paddle's live cap — otherwise `ERR_BID_CAP_EXCEEDED` (422).
38. The bid amount beats the standing high bid, and clears the opening-bid floor on a first bid — otherwise `ERR_BID_NOT_HIGHER` (422).
39. A payment does not exceed the invoice's outstanding balance — otherwise `ERR_OVERPAYMENT` (422).
40. A payment's `currency_code` matches the auction yard's settlement currency — otherwise `ERR_CURRENCY_MISMATCH` (422).
41. Cumulative fee waivers stay within their ceiling — otherwise `ERR_WAIVER_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (422).

### 2.9. Derived Counters, Cascades and Parallel State Axes

A large fraction of the fields in this system are **derived**: they are never supplied by a client and are recomputed by the server whenever anything they depend on is written. Recomputation is immediate and synchronous — there is no eventual-consistency window — but a recomputation is not by itself a versioned mutation. Only a resource whose own state or scalar fields the requested transition writes in its own right has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by 1; a resource that merely has a derived counter refreshed as a side effect keeps the `updated_at` and `version` it already carried. So a lot's `high_bid_cents` moves without versioning the lot when a bid is placed; a consignor's `open_consignment_count` and `lifetime_net_proceeds_cents`, a consignment's `unsold_lot_count`, `reoffer_lot_count` and `advance_outstanding_cents`, an auction's counters on a single-lot hammer or a counteroffer, a bay's `reserved_tonnage_kg`, a bidder's `open_exposure_cents` and `lifetime_purchase_cents`, a registration's restamped `bid_cap_cents` and `deposit_applied_total_cents`, and the `deposit_applied_cents`, `total_due_cents` and `outstanding_cents` that the deposit waterfall restamps on a *sibling* invoice of the same registration all change silently. The one deliberate exception is the bidder, which is versioned whenever its deposit counters are moved by a deposit receipt, by the locking of a hold at registration creation, or by the release of a hold on a declined or cancelled registration — though not when a hold is forfeited by the abandonment cascade. Where one operation writes several resources in their own right, every one of those resources' `version` advances.

The derived counters are as follows. On a consignor, `open_consignment_count` counts that consignor's consignments whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `INTAKE_ACCEPTED` or `CATALOGED`, and `lifetime_net_proceeds_cents` sums `net_proceeds_cents` across that consignor's settlements whose `status` is `POSTED`. On a consignment, `advance_outstanding_cents` is `advance_paid_cents` less the total `advance_recovery_cents` of its `POSTED` settlements, `unsold_lot_count` counts its lots whose `lifecycle_status` is `UNSOLD`, and `reoffer_lot_count` counts its lots whose `lifecycle_status` is `REOFFER_QUEUED`. On a lot, `high_bid_cents` is the largest `amount_cents` among that lot's bids whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, and is 0 when no such bid exists. On an auction, `assigned_lot_count` counts lots pointing at it whose `lifecycle_status` is anything other than `WITHDRAWN`, `sold_lot_count` counts its sales whose `status` is `PROVISIONAL`, `CONSIGNOR_COUNTERED`, `CONFIRMED`, `SETTLED` or `DEFAULTED`, `unsold_lot_count` counts its lots whose `lifecycle_status` is `UNSOLD`, and `gross_hammer_cents` sums `hammer_price_cents` across its sales whose `status` is `CONFIRMED` or `SETTLED`. On a load-out bay, `reserved_tonnage_kg` sums `tonnage_kg` across that bay's slots whose `status` is `BOOKED` or `CHECKED_IN`. On a bidder, `default_strike_count` counts abandoned purchases charged to that bidder and `lifetime_purchase_cents` sums `hammer_price_cents` across that bidder's sales whose `status` is `SETTLED`. On a registration, `deposit_applied_total_cents` sums `deposit_applied_cents` across that registration's invoices. On an invoice, `amount_paid_cents` sums `amount_cents` across its payments whose `status` is `SETTLED`, and `outstanding_cents` is the larger of 0 and `total_due_cents` less `amount_paid_cents`.

Some transitions are **compound**, meaning a parent's state is a function of its children's states and the server moves the parent without a client request naming it. The clearest case is a consignment reaching `CATALOGED`: publishing the last outstanding condition report of the batch bubbles the parent up automatically. Reconciliation runs the other way, as an aggregate precondition: an auction may only close its books once every one of its child sales is resolved and settled.

Finally, two entities carry **two independent state axes**, and the independence is deliberate. A lot has a `lifecycle_status` describing its physical and commercial progress and a `title_status` describing its lien and title position; the lien pipeline advances entirely independently of whether the machine has been inspected, catalogued or sold, and neither axis constrains the other except where a rule explicitly says so. An invoice has a `payment_status` describing money and a `release_status` describing custody; the money axis can regress from `PAID` back to `PARTIALLY_PAID` while the custody axis stays exactly where it was, and custody can advance while the money axis is untouched.

### 2.10. Sanctions, Segregation of Duties and Deposit Conservation

Three cross-cutting protections apply regardless of workflow.

**Sanctions screening.** Bidders whose `country_code` is `IR`, `KP`, `SY` or `RU` are embargoed. Registration creation, the credit decision on a registration, and export packet creation all screen for this and refuse with `ERR_COUNTRY_EMBARGOED`. Sanctions screening outranks every money rule in the ladder of §2.8.

**Default strikes.** A bidder who has abandoned two purchases is locked out. Once `default_strike_count` reaches 2, registration creation, the credit decision, and bid placement all refuse with `ERR_BIDDER_SUSPENDED`, and every one of that bidder's `APPROVED` registrations is moved to `SUSPENDED` by the abandonment cascade itself.

**Segregation of duties.** No operator may approve their own work. The inspector who authored a lot's condition report — recorded as `inspector_user_id` — may not be the acting user who passes quality assurance on that lot's refurbishment order. The settlement officer who approved a bidder's credit — recorded as `credit_approved_by_user_id` — may not be the acting user who later waives fees on that bidder's invoice. Either violation is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, and the refusal stands even though the acting role is correct for the endpoint.

**Deposit conservation.** A bidder's four deposit counters form a conserved system. `deposit_total_cents` is the lifetime sum of cash received; `deposit_held_cents` is the portion currently locked by non-terminal registrations; `deposit_available_cents` is the portion free to be locked by a new registration; and `deposit_forfeited_cents` is the portion lost through abandoned purchases. At all times `deposit_total_cents` equals `deposit_held_cents` plus `deposit_available_cents` plus `deposit_forfeited_cents`. Recording new cash raises the total and the available amount together; locking a hold moves value from available to held; releasing a hold moves it back; forfeiting a hold moves it from held to forfeited. No operation in this system ever changes the total except the receipt of new cash.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Consignment Intake and Seller Expectations

Everything begins with reference data owned by a `PlatformAdmin`. A **yard** is a physical auction and load-out site, registered with a human-readable `code` — nominally unique across yards, between 3 and 32 characters, though only the length is machine-enforced: there is no duplicate-code error in the catalogue and a second yard registered with an existing `code` is accepted — and a `country_code`, exactly two uppercase letters naming the ISO-3166 country of the site. The yard's country is what later decides whether a given sale is domestic or an export. Three operational settings may be supplied and each has a default: `teardown_buffer_minutes`, the ring teardown time required between two live on-site events at that yard, defaults to two hours and may be anything from zero up to 2880 minutes; `loadout_deadline_days`, the number of days after an event closes by which all load-out must finish, defaults to 10 and must be between 1 and 60; and `currency_code`, the ISO-4217 alpha-3 settlement currency for auctions held there, defaults to `USD`.

Inside a yard, a `PlatformAdmin` creates **load-out bays**. Each bay names its `yard_id`, carries a `bay_number` that is nominally unique within the yard and lies between 1 and 99 — as with the yard `code`, only the numeric range is machine-enforced, and a repeated `bay_number` inside one yard is accepted rather than refused — and declares a `crane_capacity_kg` between 1,000 and 500,000 — the maximum aggregate tonnage that may be reserved across time-overlapping bookings in that bay. A bay is created with `is_active` true unless the request says otherwise, and its derived `reserved_tonnage_kg` starts at zero. A bay may later be taken in or out of service by supplying a new `is_active` value; doing so bumps the bay's `version` and does not disturb bookings that already exist, though an inactive bay accepts no new ones.

A **consignor** is registered by a `PlatformAdmin` with a `legal_name` of 2 to 120 characters, a two-uppercase-letter `country_code`, and a `seller_type` drawn from `DEALER`, `FLEET_OWNER`, `BANK_REPO` and `GOVERNMENT`. The record also carries `unreserved_policy_ack`, a boolean that may be supplied in the creation payload and defaults to false when it is not, and that records whether the seller has signed the unreserved-sale disclosure. A consignor may set that flag themselves at any time by supplying a new `unreserved_policy_ack` value through the policy-acknowledgement capability, which bumps the consignor's `version`. Both `open_consignment_count` and `lifetime_net_proceeds_cents` begin at zero and are maintained by the server thereafter.

The seller then opens a **consignment**, the intake batch under which machines arrive. The payload names the `consignor_id` and fixes the contractual terms for every lot in the batch: `agreement_type` is either `RESERVE` or `UNRESERVED`; `commission_schedule_code` is either `RESERVE_STD` or `UNRESERVED_STD` and selects the commission table used at seller settlement; `refurb_authorization_cap_cents`, between 0 and 100,000,000, caps the refurbishment cost rechargeable to the seller across the whole batch; and the optional `advance_paid_cents`, also between 0 and 100,000,000 and defaulting to 0, records cash advanced to the seller at intake. A new consignment is created in `DRAFT` with `advance_outstanding_cents` equal to `advance_paid_cents`, `correction_note` null, and its `unsold_lot_count` and `reoffer_lot_count` both zero; the consignor's `open_consignment_count` is recomputed on the spot.

Machines are added to the batch as **lots**. A lot names its `consignment_id` and describes the unit: `make` and `model`, each 1 to 60 characters; `model_year` between 1960 and 2030; `serial_number`, the manufacturer serial or PIN, 4 to 40 characters and unique across every lot in the system, so that the same machine cannot be consigned twice — a collision is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_SERIAL_NUMBER`; `hour_meter_reading`, the hour-meter value the seller declares, between 0 and 200,000; and `weight_kg`, the shipping weight, between 500 and 200,000, which later drives both the storage daily rate and the load-out tonnage reservation. Four optional fields complete the picture: `title_required`, defaulting to false, marks an on-road titled unit and triggers a title transfer fee at settlement; `reserve_price_cents` is the minimum acceptable hammer price and defaults to null, meaning the lot sells unreserved, and when present must be between 1 and 1,000,000,000; `sold_as_hours_unverified`, defaulting to false, records that the seller accepts cataloguing behind an hours-not-warranted disclaimer; and `replacement_of_lot_id`, defaulting to null, points at the earlier lot whose equipment this one re-offers after a buyer abandoned it. Every new lot starts with `lifecycle_status` `INTAKE`, `title_status` `TITLE_PENDING`, `high_bid_cents` of 0, and `auction_id`, `lien_search_reference`, `lienholder_name` and `withdrawal_reason` all null.

While a lot is still in `INTAKE` or `INSPECTED`, its consignor may revise the catalogue-relevant data by supplying any of `reserve_price_cents`, `sold_as_hours_unverified` or `weight_kg`; each is judged on key presence, so an absent key leaves its field untouched, and only `reserve_price_cents` is nullable — sending it as an explicit null clears the reserve and returns the lot to unreserved, while an explicit null for either of the other two is a request-shape error. The edit bumps the lot's `version`, and it is not ownership-scoped: any `Consignor` may edit any lot in `INTAKE` or `INSPECTED`. Once the lot has advanced past `INSPECTED` the edit is refused as an invalid state.

Submitting the batch for intake review moves the consignment `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`. Submission requires that at least one lot exists under the consignment, and — when `agreement_type` is `UNRESERVED` — that the consignor's `unreserved_policy_ack` is true, without which the request is refused with `ERR_UNRESERVED_POLICY_NOT_ACK`. This is seller-expectation management made mechanical: nobody enters an unreserved sale without having signed the disclosure first. Submission refreshes `updated_at` and increments `version` by 1.

An `AuctionOperator` then records the intake decision, supplying a `decision` of either `ACCEPT` or `RETURN`. Accepting requires that every lot of the consignment is in `INTAKE`, `INSPECTED` or `WITHDRAWN`, and moves the consignment `SUBMITTED → INTAKE_ACCEPTED` while recomputing the consignor's `open_consignment_count`. Returning the batch requires a `correction_note` in the request, records that note on the consignment, and moves it `SUBMITTED → DRAFT`; the same batch may be corrected and resubmitted any number of times. Either decision increments the consignment's `version` by 1.

An `AuctionOperator` may **withdraw** a lot from sale at any point while its `lifecycle_status` is `INTAKE`, `INSPECTED`, `CATALOG_READY` or `ASSIGNED`, provided no sale exists for it, by supplying a `withdrawal_reason`. Withdrawal moves the lot to `WITHDRAWN`, which is terminal on the lifecycle axis, marks every `ACTIVE` bid on the lot `LOSING`, and — when the lot had been `ASSIGNED` — recomputes the auction's `assigned_lot_count`. The lot's `version` increments by 1. Withdrawn lots are excluded from the sibling checks that gate intake acceptance and catalogue bubble-up, which is what allows a batch to progress around a machine that turned out to be unsellable.

### 3.2. Inspection, Grading, Refurbishment and Catalogue Publication

An `IntakeInspector` files a **condition report** against a lot, and that filing is what advances the lot `INTAKE → INSPECTED`. It is permitted only when no condition report already exists for that lot — a replay is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_CONDITION_REPORT`, and this duplicate check is evaluated before either state guard below, since the first filing has itself moved the lot out of `INTAKE` — only while the lot is in `INTAKE`, and only while the parent consignment is `SUBMITTED` or `INTAKE_ACCEPTED`. The payload carries four component scores, each between 0 and 100 with at most one decimal place: `structural_score` for the frame and structure, `powertrain_score` for engine and transmission, `hydraulics_score` for the hydraulic system, and `tires_score` for tires and undercarriage. It also carries `observed_hour_meter_reading`, the value the inspector actually read on the machine, between 0 and 200,000; `hour_meter_verified`, a boolean recording whether the inspector could corroborate the reading against ECM data; and the optional `hour_meter_photo_ref`, a photo asset reference for the meter itself, which defaults to null. The report is created in `DRAFT` with `inspector_user_id` set to the acting `X-User-ID`, and the lot's `version` increments by 1.

Filing the report computes three derived values. **`hour_meter_variance_pct`** measures how far the seller's declaration was from reality: take the absolute difference between `observed_hour_meter_reading` and the lot's declared `hour_meter_reading`, divide it by the larger of the lot's declared `hour_meter_reading` and 1, and round the quotient half up to four decimal places. The divisor floor of 1 is what keeps a lot declared at zero hours from producing a division by zero.

**`grade_score`** is the weighted condition score, and the order of operations is normative. Multiply `structural_score` by 0.35 and round the product half up to two decimals; multiply `powertrain_score` by 0.30 and round half up to two decimals; multiply `hydraulics_score` by 0.20 and round half up to two decimals; multiply `tires_score` by 0.15 and round half up to two decimals. Add those four already-rounded components together; the sum needs no further rounding because each term already carries two decimals. Summing the unrounded products and rounding once at the end gives a different number and is not the specified behaviour. If `hour_meter_verified` is false, subtract a flat penalty of 15 points from that sum and take the larger of the result and 0.00; if `hour_meter_verified` is true, the sum stands as computed. The outcome is stored in `grade_score` to two decimal places.

**`grade_band`** is the letter published in the catalogue, one of `A`, `B`, `C` or `D`. A `grade_score` of at least 85 bands as `A`; failing that, at least 70 bands as `B`; failing that, at least 55 bands as `C`; anything below 55 bands as `D`. One override sits on top: when `hour_meter_verified` is false, a band that would otherwise have come out `A` or `B` is published as `C` instead. An unverifiable hour meter therefore caps the catalogue grade at `C` even when the penalised score still lands in the upper ranges, while a penalised score that already fell to `C` or `D` is left where the bands put it.

A draft report may be corrected. An `IntakeInspector` may resubmit any of `structural_score`, `powertrain_score`, `hydraulics_score`, `tires_score`, `hour_meter_verified`, `observed_hour_meter_reading` or `hour_meter_photo_ref` on a report whose `status` is still `DRAFT`, each omitted field keeping the value it already had; only `hour_meter_photo_ref` is nullable, so sending it as an explicit null clears the reference while an explicit null in any of the other six is a request-shape error. The whole derivation is re-run and `grade_score`, `grade_band` and `hour_meter_variance_pct` are all restamped from the revised inputs, and the report's `version` increments by 1. Once a report has been published it is frozen and a rescore attempt is an invalid state.

Refurbishment, cleaning and photography are tracked as **refurbishment orders**, opened by an `IntakeInspector` against a `lot_id` with a `scope_note` of 3 to 500 characters describing the work and an `estimated_cost_cents` between 0 and 100,000,000. Opening an order imposes no precondition of its own on the lot — the `lot_id` need only resolve, and an order may be raised against a lot in any `lifecycle_status`, the inspection requirement biting later when work actually starts. A new order is created in `DRAFT` with `rework_round` at 0 and `actual_cost_cents`, `qa_note` and `qa_signed_off_by_user_id` all null. The order then walks a cyclic workflow driven by an `action` field taking one of four values, and every step increments the order's `version` by 1.

`START` moves the order `DRAFT → IN_PROGRESS`, and is permitted only while the parent lot is in `INSPECTED` — work happens after inspection and before publication. That lot check belongs to the `DRAFT` leg alone: the `REWORK → IN_PROGRESS` leg described below re-starts work without re-testing the lot's `lifecycle_status`, so a rework lap continues even after publication has moved the lot on to `CATALOG_READY`. `SUBMIT_QA` moves it `IN_PROGRESS → QA_REVIEW` and requires `actual_cost_cents` in the request, which overwrites the field on the order and becomes the basis of the seller recharge at settlement. `QA_REWORK` moves it `QA_REVIEW → REWORK`, requires a `qa_note` in the request, records that note, and increments `rework_round` by 1; it is only available while `rework_round` is still below 2, so a third rework decision is refused with `ERR_MAX_REWORK_ROUNDS_EXCEEDED` rather than being silently capped. From `REWORK`, another `START` returns the order to `IN_PROGRESS`, closing the `QA_REVIEW → REWORK → IN_PROGRESS → QA_REVIEW` loop for at most two laps. `QA_PASS` moves it `QA_REVIEW → COMPLETED`, which is terminal; it requires that `actual_cost_cents` is not null and that the acting `X-User-ID` differs from the `inspector_user_id` recorded on the lot's condition report, and it stamps `qa_signed_off_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`; a `qa_note` supplied alongside `QA_PASS` is optional but is recorded on the order when present. Passing QA on work whose inspection you authored yourself is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, and the separation check is vacuous — hence passed — when the lot has no condition report at all to compare against.

**Publication** is the gate onto the catalogue, and it advances two entities at once. Publishing a report moves its own `status` `DRAFT → PUBLISHED` and moves the lot `INSPECTED → CATALOG_READY`. It requires the lot to be in `INSPECTED`, the report to still be in `DRAFT`, the parent consignment to be in `INTAKE_ACCEPTED`, and every refurbishment order of the lot to have reached `COMPLETED` — a condition that is vacuously satisfied when the lot has no orders at all, and that is otherwise refused with `ERR_REFURBISHMENT_INCOMPLETE`. The lot's `version` and the report's `version` each increment by 1.

Publication also carries the batch's compound transition. When the published lot was the last one needed — that is, when every non-`WITHDRAWN` lot of the consignment is now `CATALOG_READY`, and at least one of them actually is `CATALOG_READY` rather than the batch consisting only of withdrawn units — the parent consignment moves `INTAKE_ACCEPTED → CATALOGED` on its own, with its `version` incremented by 1. `CATALOGED` is terminal for a consignment. Because catalogue assignment requires the parent to be `CATALOGED`, one unpublished sibling blocks the assignment of an already-published lot; that refusal surfaces as `ERR_CONSIGNMENT_NOT_CATALOGED`.

### 3.3. The Compliance Desk: Liens, Titles and Export Documentation

The lien and title pipeline runs on the lot's second state axis and is the sole province of a `ComplianceOfficer`. It advances entirely independently of the lifecycle axis: a search can be ordered on a machine still sitting in `INTAKE`, and a machine can be inspected and photographed while its title position is unresolved. The one lifecycle interaction is negative — a search may not be ordered on a lot whose `lifecycle_status` is already `WITHDRAWN`.

Ordering a search moves the lot `TITLE_PENDING → LIEN_SEARCH_ORDERED` and records the supplied `lien_search_reference`, the reference number of the ordered search. Recording the outcome then branches on a boolean `encumbrance_found`. When it is false, the lot moves `LIEN_SEARCH_ORDERED → LIEN_CLEAR`. When it is true, the request must also carry `lienholder_name`, which is recorded on the lot, and the lot moves `LIEN_SEARCH_ORDERED → LIEN_ENCUMBERED`. An encumbrance is cleared by recording the lienholder's release, which supplies a `release_document_ref` and moves the lot `LIEN_ENCUMBERED → LIEN_RELEASED`. Each of these four steps increments the lot's `version` by 1. There is no terminal state on this axis; both `LIEN_CLEAR` and `LIEN_RELEASED` are acceptable resting positions, and wherever the specification requires a clear title it accepts either one.

Title clearance gates three separate operations, all of them refusing with `ERR_TITLE_NOT_CLEAR` when the lot's `title_status` is neither `LIEN_CLEAR` nor `LIEN_RELEASED`: assigning the lot to an auction catalogue, authorising the physical release of the machine after sale, and attaching a title document to the buyer's export packet.

For cross-border sales, the same officer drives the **export document packet**. A packet is opened against an `invoice_id` and a `destination_country_code` of exactly two uppercase letters. Two conditions must hold: the destination must equal the buyer's `country_code`, and the invoice must actually be cross-border. A packet aimed at a country other than the buyer's, or opened against a domestic invoice, is refused with `ERR_DESTINATION_COUNTRY_MISMATCH`. Sanctions screening applies here too, so an embargoed buyer's packet is refused with `ERR_COUNTRY_EMBARGOED`. Nothing else about the invoice is inspected: neither its `payment_status`, nor its `release_status`, nor its `settlement_variant` gates the opening of a packet, and an invoice may carry several packets at once — the release rule of §3.8 asks only that *one* of them has cleared. A new packet is created in `PREPARING` with `reopen_count` at 0 and `title_document_ref`, `customs_entry_ref` and `rejection_reason` all null.

The documentation pipeline is driven by an `action` field with five values, and each step increments the packet's `version` by 1. `ATTACH_TITLE` moves the packet `PREPARING → TITLE_ATTACHED` and requires `title_document_ref` in the request — the title or manufacturer's statement of origin reference — as well as a clear title on the underlying lot. `FILE_CUSTOMS` moves it `TITLE_ATTACHED → CUSTOMS_FILED` and requires `customs_entry_ref`, the customs entry or export filing reference. `CLEAR` moves it `CUSTOMS_FILED → EXPORT_CLEARED`, which is terminal. `REJECT` moves it `CUSTOMS_FILED → CUSTOMS_REJECTED` and requires a `rejection_reason`. `REOPEN` moves it `CUSTOMS_REJECTED → PREPARING`, increments `reopen_count` by 1, and clears both `title_document_ref` and `customs_entry_ref` back to null so the whole pipeline is walked again from the start; it is only available while `reopen_count` is still below 2, and a third reopening is refused with `ERR_MAX_PACKET_REOPENS_EXCEEDED`.

Reaching `EXPORT_CLEARED` has a fiscal consequence that is worth stating precisely: it makes the buyer's final settlement zero-rated for tax, but it does **not** itself restamp the invoice. The invoice's stored money fields change only when the invoice is explicitly finalised, so an export clearance recorded after finalisation has no retroactive effect on the figures already stamped.

### 3.4. Scheduling Events and Assembling the Catalogue

An `AuctionOperator` schedules an **auction** at a yard, naming the `yard_id` and choosing four things. `kind` is either `LIVE_ONSITE`, which occupies the yard's physical ring, or `TIMED_ONLINE`, which does not. `reserve_policy` is either `UNRESERVED`, which forbids any lot in the catalogue from carrying a reserve price at all, or `RESERVE_ALLOWED`. `premium_schedule_code` selects the buyer premium table applied to every sale in the event and is either `STANDARD` or `LOW_VALUE_FLAT`. Finally `window_start` and `window_end` are ISO 8601 UTC instants bounding the bidding window as a closed interval, with `window_end` required to be strictly later than `window_start`. The auction is created in `SCHEDULED` with `assigned_lot_count`, `sold_lot_count`, `unsold_lot_count` and `gross_hammer_cents` all at zero.

Creation also computes `loadout_deadline_at`, the instant by which all load-out must be finished: it is `window_end` advanced by the yard's `loadout_deadline_days` days. No load-out slot may end after it.

Live on-site events compete for a physical ring, so their windows are checked against each other. For a `LIVE_ONSITE` auction, take its bidding window and extend it by the yard's `teardown_buffer_minutes` at **both** ends — `window_start` moved that many minutes earlier and `window_end` that many minutes later; that extended interval must not overlap the raw, unextended window of any other `LIVE_ONSITE` auction at the same yard whose `status` is anything other than `RECONCILED`. Only the candidate is padded, so the buffer is applied once per comparison rather than twice. Because the comparison uses closed intervals, a candidate that begins at exactly the existing event's `window_end` plus the buffer conflicts, while one that begins a single second later does not. `TIMED_ONLINE` events are exempt from the check entirely — any number of them may hold overlapping bidding windows at one yard, and they never block a live event. A conflict is refused with `ERR_AUCTION_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.

A `SCHEDULED` auction may be **rescheduled** by supplying a new `window_start` and `window_end`. Rescheduling recomputes `loadout_deadline_at` from the new `window_end` and re-runs the yard overlap check, excluding the auction being moved from the set it is compared against — otherwise every event would conflict with its own former self. The auction's `version` increments by 1. Once the event has left `SCHEDULED` the window is fixed.

**Catalogue assignment** brings a lot into an event. The request names the `lot_id` and may optionally supply a `reserve_price_cents`, which overwrites whatever reserve the lot carried; the field is judged on key presence, so omitting it leaves the lot's existing reserve alone while sending it as an explicit null clears the reserve outright and the lot then sells unreserved. Assignment is a dense gate; eight conditions must hold, and one of them is checked out of ladder order: a lot whose `auction_id` is already set to some other auction is refused with `ERR_LOT_AUCTION_MISMATCH` before any state guard is evaluated, because such a lot has necessarily left `CATALOG_READY` and the named error would otherwise be unreachable. The lot must be in `CATALOG_READY`, the auction in `SCHEDULED`, and the lot's condition report must exist and have reached `PUBLISHED` — three generic rung-8 guards, each refusing with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, evaluated in exactly that order and therefore all reached before the consignment, title, hour-meter and reserve rules that the remainder of this paragraph describes. The lot's consignment must be `CATALOGED`, failing which the refusal is `ERR_CONSIGNMENT_NOT_CATALOGED`. The lot's `title_status` must be `LIEN_CLEAR` or `LIEN_RELEASED`, failing which the refusal is `ERR_TITLE_NOT_CLEAR`. Either the report's `hour_meter_verified` must be true or the lot's `sold_as_hours_unverified` must be true, failing which the refusal is `ERR_HOUR_METER_UNVERIFIED` — an unverifiable meter reaches the catalogue only behind the seller's hours-not-warranted disclaimer. The lot must not already be assigned to another auction and its consignment must have been accepted at intake, failing which the refusal is `ERR_LOT_AUCTION_MISMATCH`. And a reserve price, whether already on the lot or supplied with the assignment, is only permitted when the auction's `reserve_policy` is `RESERVE_ALLOWED` **and** the consignment's `agreement_type` is `RESERVE`; either an unreserved event or an unreserved seller agreement forbids it, and the refusal is `ERR_RESERVE_NOT_ALLOWED`.

A successful assignment stamps the lot's `auction_id`, moves the lot `CATALOG_READY → ASSIGNED`, recomputes the auction's `assigned_lot_count`, and increments both the lot's `version` and the auction's `version` by 1. Although the operation is a create — it answers 201 — the resource it returns in `data` is the updated **lot**, not a separate assignment record; assignments have no identity of their own and no read endpoint. The lot's `auction_id` is never cleared afterwards.

**Opening** the event moves the auction `SCHEDULED → OPEN`. It requires that the logical clock is at or before `window_end` — an event whose window has already elapsed can no longer be opened — and that `assigned_lot_count` is at least 1, since there is nothing to sell in an empty catalogue. The auction's `version` increments by 1.

### 3.5. Global Bidder Admission: Deposits, Country Tiers and Credit

A **bidder** is registered by a `PlatformAdmin` with a `legal_name` of 2 to 120 characters, a two-uppercase-letter `country_code`, and an optional `tax_id` holding the VAT or tax registration number, defaulting to null. All six money counters — `deposit_total_cents`, `deposit_held_cents`, `deposit_available_cents`, `deposit_forfeited_cents`, `open_exposure_cents` and `lifetime_purchase_cents` — start at zero, as does `default_strike_count`.

Registration also derives `country_tier`, the risk tier that governs deposit leverage, minimum deposit and default payment terms. `TIER_A` covers bidders in the United States, Canada, Germany, Australia, the United Kingdom and Japan — that is, `country_code` of `US`, `CA`, `DE`, `AU`, `GB` or `JP`. `TIER_B` covers Mexico, the United Arab Emirates, Brazil and South Africa — `MX`, `AE`, `BR` and `ZA`. Every other country falls into `TIER_C`.

Cash arrives through the settlement desk. A `SettlementOfficer` records a deposit against a bidder with an `amount_cents` of at least 1 and no declared ceiling, and a mandatory non-empty `reference` string of any length identifying the wire or instrument; the resource returned is the updated bidder. Recording a deposit raises both `deposit_total_cents` and `deposit_available_cents` by `amount_cents`, leaving `deposit_held_cents` and `deposit_forfeited_cents` untouched, and increments the bidder's `version` by 1. The conservation identity of §2.10 continues to hold.

A `Bidder` then registers for one specific event, creating a **bidder registration** that names the `bidder_id` and `auction_id`, declares a `deposit_hold_cents` between 1 and 1,000,000,000 to be locked for that event, and may set `financing_requested` to ask for equipment financing on the event; that flag defaults to false. Two fiscal admission checks apply, and they are deliberately the last checks in the whole ladder. The requested hold must reach the minimum for the bidder's tier: 100,000 cents for `TIER_A`, 500,000 cents for `TIER_B`, and 1,500,000 cents for `TIER_C` — the less familiar the jurisdiction, the larger the stake required. A hold below the tier floor is refused with `ERR_DEPOSIT_BELOW_MINIMUM`. Separately, the bidder's `deposit_available_cents` must be at least the requested `deposit_hold_cents`; asking to lock cash that is not free is refused with `ERR_DEPOSIT_UNAVAILABLE`. Because the sanctions screen and the strike check sit far higher in the ladder, an embargoed bidder with an undersized deposit reports the embargo and never reaches either fiscal check.

Only one live registration may exist per bidder-and-auction pair: if a registration for that pair is already in `REQUESTED`, `APPROVED` or `SUSPENDED`, the create is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_REGISTRATION`. A successful create puts the registration in `REQUESTED` at `version` 1, moves `deposit_hold_cents` from the bidder's `deposit_available_cents` to its `deposit_held_cents`, and allocates a `paddle_number`: one greater than the highest paddle already issued at that auction — counting every earlier registration whatever `status` it now holds, so a cancelled or declined paddle number is never reused — starting at 101 when the auction has none. The create inspects nothing about the auction beyond the resolution of its identifier: a registration may be opened against an event in any `status`, including one already `CLOSED` or `RECONCILED`, since only the credit decision's approval branch tests the auction's `status`. `approved_credit_limit_cents` starts at 0, `financing_approved` at false, `bid_cap_cents` at 0, `deposit_applied_total_cents` at 0, and `payment_terms_code`, `leverage_multiplier`, `credit_approved_by_user_id` and `decline_reason` all at null.

The **credit decision** belongs to a `SettlementOfficer` and carries a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `DECLINE`. Approval requires that the registration is still in `REQUESTED`, that the auction's `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `OPEN`, that the bidder's `country_code` is not embargoed, and that the bidder's `default_strike_count` is below 2. Of those four, only the auction-status requirement is peculiar to approval; the `REQUESTED` requirement, the sanctions screen and the strike check are evaluated before the decision branches and so bind a `DECLINE` equally, meaning a decline aimed at an embargoed or twice-struck bidder is itself refused with `ERR_COUNTRY_EMBARGOED` or `ERR_BIDDER_SUSPENDED`, while a registration may still be declined after its event has closed. Approval moves the registration `REQUESTED → APPROVED`, records the optional `approved_credit_limit_cents` from the request as the granted credit line — omitted or null, it is recorded as 0, which by itself forecloses financing approval — stamps `credit_approved_by_user_id` with the acting `X-User-ID`, and derives three fields.

`financing_approved` becomes true when the officer approved a positive credit limit and the bidder had set `financing_requested`; otherwise it stays false. `payment_terms_code` follows from that outcome and the tier: an approved financing arrangement earns `NET_30`; failing that, a `TIER_A` bidder earns `NET_7`; every other bidder is `PREPAY`. `leverage_multiplier` is the deposit leverage granted by the tier: 10 for `TIER_A`, 5 for `TIER_B`, and 2 for `TIER_C`. Declining instead requires a `decline_reason` in the request, moves the registration `REQUESTED → DECLINED`, and releases the hold — `deposit_held_cents` falls and `deposit_available_cents` rises by `deposit_hold_cents`, with `deposit_total_cents` unchanged. Either decision increments the registration's `version` by 1.

Approval also stamps `bid_cap_cents` from the **registration forecast** view of the paddle's headroom. Start from a base headroom equal to `deposit_hold_cents` multiplied by `leverage_multiplier`. Compute the forecast exposure as the sum of `total_due_cents` across the registration's invoices whose `payment_status` is `UNPAID` or `PARTIALLY_PAID` and whose underlying sale's `status` is `CONFIRMED` or `SETTLED`; this is the same quantity the bidder carries as `open_exposure_cents`. The forecast cap is then the base headroom plus `approved_credit_limit_cents` less that exposure, floored at zero. This forecast is restamped on the registration whenever any invoice of the registration is written, so `bid_cap_cents` tracks the money actually owed.

A `Bidder` may **cancel** its own registration while it is in `REQUESTED` or `APPROVED`, provided no bid of that registration is in `ACTIVE` or `WINNING`. Cancellation moves the registration to `CANCELLED`, which is terminal, and performs the compensating deposit move: `deposit_held_cents` falls and `deposit_available_cents` rises by `deposit_hold_cents`, with `deposit_total_cents` untouched. Cancellation also zeroes the registration's `bid_cap_cents`, since a terminal paddle has no headroom left to report. The behaviour is identical whichever of the two states the registration was in. The registration's `version` increments by 1. `DECLINED`, `CANCELLED` and `SUSPENDED` are all terminal.

An `APPROVED` registration can also be **suspended** by the system, never by a client. When an abandonment pushes the owning bidder's `default_strike_count` to 2, every `APPROVED` registration of that bidder moves `APPROVED → SUSPENDED` and has its `bid_cap_cents` set to 0, with `version` incremented by 1 on each.

The desk and the ring measure headroom differently, and the difference is intentional. A computed exposure read exposes both views for one registration: it takes `auction_id` and `variant` as **query parameters**, both mandatory, `variant` being either `REGISTRATION_FORECAST` or `LIVE_CHRONOLOGICAL`; a missing parameter or an unrecognised variant is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` before the identifiers are resolved. The read returns the `bidder_id`, `auction_id`, `registration_id`, the `variant` requested, the `base_headroom_cents`, the `exposure_cents`, the resulting `bid_cap_cents`, and a `breached_at_sale_id`, and it mutates nothing. If the bidder has no registration for the requested auction, the read fails with `ERR_REGISTRATION_NOT_FOUND`; where the pair has accumulated several registrations over time, the read uses the most recently created one whose `status` is `REQUESTED`, `APPROVED` or `SUSPENDED`, falling back to the most recently created row of any status when none of them is live. Any of the four roles listed for the endpoint may read any bidder's exposure — this particular read is not ownership-scoped. The forecast variant is the one described above: it values each open obligation at its currently stamped `total_due_cents` — net of deposit already applied and inclusive of premium, tax and accrued storage — and it adds the credit line to the headroom. The live variant, described in §3.6, does neither. The two are expected to disagree, and the reported `exposure_cents` therefore means different things in each: under the forecast variant it is the money still owed, identical to the bidder's `open_exposure_cents`, while under the live variant it is the gross hammer actually consumed — the sum of `hammer_price_cents` over every non-`NO_SALE` sale the replay walked, whether or not that sale has been invoiced or paid. `breached_at_sale_id` is meaningful only for the live variant and is always returned as null for the forecast variant.

### 3.6. The Ring: Bidding, Hammering and Closing the Event

A `Bidder` places a **bid** by naming its `registration_id`, the `lot_id` being bid on, and an `amount_cents` between 1 and 1,000,000,000. A bid is created in `ACTIVE` with `placed_at` taken from the logical clock, and the lot's `high_bid_cents` is recomputed immediately. Bids are append-only: their `status` moves to `WINNING` or `LOSING` by system action alone, and there is no endpoint that edits or deletes a bid.

Four checks police a bid, and their relative order matters. The auction must be `OPEN`, failing which the bid is refused with `ERR_AUCTION_NOT_OPEN`; the auction in question is the one stamped on the **lot**, so a lot not yet catalogued into any event — its `auction_id` still null — is refused with the same `ERR_AUCTION_NOT_OPEN`, and the registration's own `auction_id` is never compared against the lot's, leaving a paddle admitted to one event free to bid on a lot catalogued into another. The registration must be `APPROVED` — a `REQUESTED`, `DECLINED`, `SUSPENDED` or `CANCELLED` registration cannot bid — failing which the refusal is `ERR_REGISTRATION_NOT_APPROVED`. Only then are the fiscal checks reached: `amount_cents` must not exceed the paddle's live cap, failing which the refusal is `ERR_BID_CAP_EXCEEDED`; and `amount_cents` must be strictly greater than the lot's current `high_bid_cents`, with a first bid on a lot additionally required to reach the opening floor of 10,000 cents, failing which the refusal is `ERR_BID_NOT_HIGHER`. A bid equal to the standing high bid is refused; the increment must be strictly positive. The strike lock-out applies here as well, so a bidder at 2 strikes is refused with `ERR_BIDDER_SUSPENDED` before any of the ring-specific fiscal checks.

The **live cap** that governs bidding is not the forecast cap stamped on the registration. On the ring, the auctioneer replays the paddle's own sales in chronological order and charges each one at its gross hammer price, because premiums, taxes, deposits and payments have not happened yet and are irrelevant to whether the paddle is still good for another machine. Start from a headroom equal to `deposit_hold_cents` multiplied by `leverage_multiplier`; the approved credit line is deliberately **not** available on the ring. Then walk the registration's sales in ascending `hammered_at` order, breaking ties by ascending `created_at` and any remaining tie by insertion order, skipping any sale whose `status` is `NO_SALE` and no other status — a `DEFAULTED` sale is still charged against the ring headroom — and subtract each sale's `hammer_price_cents` from the running headroom. The first step at which the running headroom reaches zero or falls below it is recorded as `breached_at_sale_id`, and later steps do not overwrite it. The live cap is the larger of the final running headroom and zero, and a bid whose `amount_cents` exceeds it is refused. Because the replay is ordered rather than aggregated, `breached_at_sale_id` depends on the sequence and not merely on the sum, which is why the live view can name a specific machine as the point of breach while the forecast view can only report a number.

**Hammering** a single lot moves it `ASSIGNED → HAMMERED` and creates the `Sale`. It requires that the lot is `ASSIGNED`, that the auction is `OPEN`, and that the lot's `high_bid_cents` is greater than zero — there is nothing to hammer without a standing bid. The request names only the lot, so "the auction" here is whatever the lot's own `auction_id` points at, and the mismatch guard consequently fires only in the degenerate case of an `ASSIGNED` lot that carries no `auction_id` at all, which is refused with `ERR_LOT_AUCTION_MISMATCH`; that guard sits between the lot-state check and the auction-open check. The sale is created with its `lot_id`, an `auction_id` copied from the lot, a `winning_bid_id` naming the highest `ACTIVE` bid on the lot, the `registration_id` of that winning bid, a `hammer_price_cents` equal to the lot's `high_bid_cents`, a `hammered_at` taken from the logical clock, `counter_round` at 0, and `counter_price_cents` and `reject_reason` at null. The winning bid's `status` becomes `WINNING` and every other `ACTIVE` bid on that lot becomes `LOSING`. The lot's `version` increments by 1.

The sale's `reserve_met` is evaluated at that moment and drives everything that follows. It is true when the auction's `reserve_policy` is `UNRESERVED`, or when the lot's `reserve_price_cents` is null, or when `hammer_price_cents` is greater than or equal to the lot's `reserve_price_cents`. A hammer price exactly equal to the reserve satisfies the comparison and confirms the sale. When `reserve_met` is true, the sale moves straight from `PROVISIONAL` to `CONFIRMED` and an `Invoice` is created at the provisional variant; the bidder's `open_exposure_cents`, the registration's `bid_cap_cents`, and the auction's `sold_lot_count` and `gross_hammer_cents` are all recomputed. When `reserve_met` is false, the sale stays in `PROVISIONAL` awaiting the seller's judgement, no invoice exists yet, and §3.7 takes over.

**Closing** the event does the same work for the whole ring in a single call, and the cascade has four depths. First, the auction moves `OPEN → CLOSED`. Second, every still-`ASSIGNED` lot of the auction is processed in ascending `created_at` order: a lot whose `high_bid_cents` is greater than zero is auto-hammered exactly as above, moving `ASSIGNED → HAMMERED` with a `Sale` created; a lot whose `high_bid_cents` is zero moves `ASSIGNED → UNSOLD` and no sale row is created for it at all. Third, every sale created with `reserve_met` true becomes `CONFIRMED` and an invoice is created at the provisional variant — the confirmation and its invoice are in fact stamped inside each lot's own knock-down as that lot is reached, rather than in a separate later pass, which changes no figure because the premium's volume discount counts only strictly earlier sales. Fourth, the derived aggregates are recomputed: the bidder's `open_exposure_cents`, the registration's `deposit_applied_total_cents` and `bid_cap_cents`, the auction's `sold_lot_count`, `unsold_lot_count` and `gross_hammer_cents`, and the consignment's `unsold_lot_count`. The auction's `version` increments by 1, as does the `version` of every lot and sale the cascade touched. The processing order is normative because the deposit waterfall and the chronological replay both depend on it.

### 3.7. Reserve Outcomes and the Consignor Negotiation Loop

A sale that missed its reserve sits in `PROVISIONAL` and belongs to the seller. Three paths lead out of it, and all three are the consignor's or the buyer's to take, never the auctioneer's.

The seller may **reject** the provisional result outright by supplying a `reject_reason`. Rejection is available only while the sale is `PROVISIONAL` with `reserve_met` false. It moves the sale `PROVISIONAL → NO_SALE`, which is terminal, moves the lot `HAMMERED → UNSOLD`, flips the winning bid `WINNING → LOSING`, and recomputes both the consignment's `unsold_lot_count` and the auction's `unsold_lot_count`. The sale's `version` increments by 1.

The seller may instead **counter**. A counteroffer is available only while the sale is `PROVISIONAL` with `reserve_met` false, and it is the one negotiation step guarded by optimistic concurrency: the request must carry `expected_sale_version` matching the sale's current `version`, or it is refused with `ERR_STALE_SALE_VERSION`. Beyond that, `counter_round` must still be below 3 — a fourth counteroffer is refused with `ERR_MAX_COUNTER_ROUNDS_EXCEEDED` — and the proposed `counter_price_cents` must satisfy all three of the following inequalities: it must be at least the sale's current `hammer_price_cents`, it must be no greater than the lot's `reserve_price_cents`, and when `counter_round` is already above zero it must be no greater than the previous `counter_price_cents`. Concessions may only move down; a second counteroffer higher than the first is refused with `ERR_COUNTER_PRICE_NOT_MONOTONIC`. A valid counteroffer moves the sale `PROVISIONAL → CONSIGNOR_COUNTERED`, increments `counter_round` by 1, records `counter_price_cents`, and increments the sale's `version` by 1. This is exactly why the version guard exists: evaluating the monotonicity comparison against a round the seller never observed would let a client walk the price back up.

The buyer answers with a `decision` of `ACCEPT` or `DECLINE`, and may do so only while the sale is `CONSIGNOR_COUNTERED`, only when the acting `X-User-ID` is the bidder behind the sale's registration, and only when that registration is `APPROVED`. Accepting additionally requires that `counter_price_cents` is no greater than the registration's `bid_cap_cents` — the buyer cannot accept its way past its own credit ceiling, and an acceptance above it is refused with `ERR_BID_CAP_EXCEEDED`. Acceptance overwrites the sale's `hammer_price_cents` with `counter_price_cents`, sets `reserve_met` to true, moves the sale `CONSIGNOR_COUNTERED → CONFIRMED`, creates the `Invoice` at the provisional variant, and recomputes the bidder's `open_exposure_cents` together with the auction's `sold_lot_count` and `gross_hammer_cents`.

Declining branches on how many rounds have already been used, and the two branches are genuinely different outcomes. While `counter_round` is still below 3, a decline moves the sale `CONSIGNOR_COUNTERED → PROVISIONAL`, returning it to the seller so another concession may be offered; the loop `PROVISIONAL → CONSIGNOR_COUNTERED → PROVISIONAL` may be walked for at most three laps. When `counter_round` has reached 3, the same decline instead moves the sale `CONSIGNOR_COUNTERED → NO_SALE`, moves the lot `HAMMERED → UNSOLD`, flips the winning bid `WINNING → LOSING`, and recomputes the consignment's and the auction's `unsold_lot_count`. Either branch increments the sale's `version` by 1.

A sale's full state set is `PROVISIONAL`, `CONSIGNOR_COUNTERED`, `CONFIRMED`, `NO_SALE`, `SETTLED` and `DEFAULTED`, of which `NO_SALE` and `DEFAULTED` are terminal. `CONFIRMED` advances to `SETTLED` when the seller settlement is posted (§3.9), and to `DEFAULTED` through the abandonment cascade.

### 3.8. Buyer Settlement: Premium, Tax, Storage, Payment and Release

An `Invoice` is created by the server the moment a sale becomes `CONFIRMED`, whether through a hammer that met its reserve, a close cascade, or a buyer's acceptance of a counteroffer. It records its `sale_id`, copies the `bidder_id` and `registration_id` from the winning registration, denormalises `hammer_price_cents` from the sale, and sets `settlement_variant` to `PROVISIONAL`. Its `is_cross_border` flag is true exactly when the buyer's `country_code` differs from the auction yard's `country_code`. Its `payment_status` starts at `UNPAID` and its `release_status` at `HELD`, and — as §2.9 stresses — those two axes move independently.

**Buyer premium.** `buyer_premium_cents` is charged on top of the hammer price, and which schedule applies comes from the auction's `premium_schedule_code`.

Under `LOW_VALUE_FLAT` the computation is short: take ten percent of `hammer_price_cents`, rounded half up to whole cents, and use the larger of that figure and 50,000 cents. The flat schedule applies no online surcharge, no volume discount and — importantly — no ceiling. A 100,000-cent machine therefore yields 10,000 cents of raw premium, which the floor lifts to 50,000 cents.

Under `STANDARD` the premium is built in four order-sensitive steps, and the order below is normative. Step one applies marginal tiers, each rounded to whole cents before the tiers are summed: twelve percent of the portion of the hammer price up to 5,000,000 cents; eight percent of the portion between 5,000,000 and 25,000,000 cents; and five percent of everything above 25,000,000 cents. Step two adds an online surcharge of one and one-half percent — rounded half up to whole cents — computed on the **hammer price**, not on the premium, and only when the auction's `kind` is `TIMED_ONLINE`; a live on-site event adds nothing. Step three applies a same-auction volume discount, evaluated against prior hammer volume only. The prior volume is the sum of `hammer_price_cents` across sales of the **same auction and same registration** whose `status` is `CONFIRMED` or `SETTLED` and whose `hammered_at` is strictly earlier than this sale's, with ties broken by ascending `created_at`; the sale being priced never counts toward its own discount. A prior volume of at least 100,000,000 cents earns a twenty percent discount; failing that, at least 50,000,000 cents earns ten percent; below that there is no discount. The discount is taken by rounding the discount percentage of the post-surcharge premium half up to whole cents and subtracting it. Step four clamps the result: the premium is at least 25,000 cents and at most 4,000,000 cents. Applying the clamp before the surcharge or before the discount produces different figures and is not the specified behaviour.

**The invoice total.** Two variants of the same formula are stamped at different points in the lifecycle, and they differ in exactly three ways: whether storage has accrued, whether an export clearance zero-rates the tax, and — through the finalisation itself — which fee waivers are already recognised. Both variants share the following spine.

`documentation_fee_cents` is 45,000 cents when `is_cross_border` is true and 15,000 cents when it is false. `title_transfer_fee_cents` is 12,500 cents when the lot's `title_required` is true and 0 otherwise. `financing_fee_cents` is two percent of the hammer price, rounded half up to whole cents, when the registration's `financing_approved` is true, and 0 otherwise. `subtotal_cents` is then the sum of `hammer_price_cents`, `buyer_premium_cents`, `documentation_fee_cents`, `title_transfer_fee_cents` and `financing_fee_cents`.

`tax_rate_applied` is keyed to the buyer's country: 0.0625 for `US`, 0.13 for `CA`, 0.16 for `MX`, 0.19 for `DE`, 0.10 for `AU`, 0.05 for `AE`, 0.20 for `GB`, 0.17 for `BR`, 0.10 for `JP`, 0.15 for `ZA`, and a default of 0.10 for any country not in that table. One exception overrides it: at the final variant, a cross-border invoice whose export packet has reached `EXPORT_CLEARED` is zero-rated, with `tax_rate_applied` set to 0.0000. `tax_cents` is `tax_rate_applied` applied to `subtotal_cents`, rounded half up to whole cents. Note carefully that the tax base is the subtotal; storage fees are never taxed.

`storage_fee_cents` is forced to 0 at the provisional variant, since nothing can have accrued at hammer time, and is computed by the storage rules below at the final variant. Subtracting `credit_memo_cents` — the cumulative approved fee waivers — from the sum of `subtotal_cents`, `tax_cents` and `storage_fee_cents` gives the gross amount before any deposit is applied.

`deposit_applied_cents` then draws down the registration's deposit hold. Because one registration may buy several machines, the hold is consumed by a waterfall across every invoice of that registration, ordered by ascending `hammered_at` of the underlying sale with ties broken by ascending `created_at` of the invoice. Each invoice in turn consumes the smaller of the remaining hold and its own gross-before-deposit amount, so the earliest machines absorb the deposit first and later ones may get nothing. `total_due_cents` is the gross-before-deposit amount less `deposit_applied_cents`, and `outstanding_cents` is the larger of 0 and `total_due_cents` less `amount_paid_cents`.

The provisional variant is stamped at invoice creation and always applies the buyer's own domestic tax rate — even on a cross-border sale — because no customs clearance can exist at hammer time; it also forces `storage_fee_cents` and `chargeable_storage_days` to zero. `deficiency_claim_cents` is 0 until an abandonment occurs.

Two due instants are stamped alongside the money. `payment_due_at` is the sale's `hammered_at` advanced by the number of days that the registration's `payment_terms_code` allows: 2 days for `PREPAY`, 7 days for `NET_7`, and 30 days for `NET_30`. `grace_end_at` is the sale's `hammered_at` advanced by the free-storage allowance of the same terms code: 3 days for `PREPAY`, 5 days for `NET_7`, and 10 days for `NET_30`. The financed buyer therefore gets both the longest credit and the longest free storage, and the prepay buyer the shortest of each.

**Storage accrual.** Beyond `grace_end_at`, the yard charges by the day. Take the elapsed time from `grace_end_at` to the logical clock's current instant, floored at zero, and divide by a full day, discarding any remainder: `chargeable_storage_days` counts **whole** days only, so a machine collected 23 hours and 59 minutes past the grace end accrues nothing, and one collected exactly 24 hours past accrues a single day. The daily rate is keyed to the machine's `weight_kg`: 2,500 cents per day for a light machine of at most 10,000 kg, 6,000 cents per day for a medium machine of at most 30,000 kg, and 12,000 cents per day for anything heavier. Multiply days by rate, then cap the result at twenty-five percent of `hammer_price_cents` rounded half up to whole cents; `storage_fee_cents` is the smaller of the uncapped accrual and that cap. A six-tonne machine and a thirty-six-tonne machine sitting for the same single day therefore accrue 2,500 and 12,000 cents respectively.

**Finalisation** restamps the invoice at the final variant. A `SettlementOfficer` triggers it; it has no precondition beyond the variant itself — neither the money axis nor the custody axis is consulted, so an `UNPAID` invoice and a `LOADOUT_BOOKED` one are equally finalisable — and it is one-shot, a replay against an already-`FINAL` invoice being refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. It moves `settlement_variant` `PROVISIONAL → FINAL` and restamps `storage_fee_cents`, `chargeable_storage_days`, `tax_rate_applied`, `tax_cents`, `deposit_applied_cents` and `total_due_cents`, recomputes the bidder's `open_exposure_cents`, and increments the invoice's `version` by 1. Because accrued storage can push `total_due_cents` above what the buyer has already tendered, the money axis may **regress**: an invoice whose `payment_status` was `PAID` moves back to `PARTIALLY_PAID` with `outstanding_cents` set to the new `total_due_cents` less `amount_paid_cents`. The regression is conditional on having been `PAID` and on the new total genuinely exceeding what was tendered; an invoice that entered finalisation `UNPAID` or `PARTIALLY_PAID` keeps that status regardless of how the restamped total compares with `amount_paid_cents`, though `outstanding_cents` is recomputed in every case as the larger of 0 and `total_due_cents` less `amount_paid_cents`. The custody axis is untouched by finalisation — this is the clearest illustration of the two axes being independent.

A read-only settlement preview lets the desk compare the two variants before committing. It takes `variant` as a mandatory **query parameter**, either `PROVISIONAL` or `FINAL`, a missing or unrecognised value being refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` before the invoice identifier is resolved, and returns, without mutating anything, exactly the following fifteen fields and no others: the `invoice_id`, the `variant` requested, `hammer_price_cents`, `buyer_premium_cents`, `documentation_fee_cents`, `title_transfer_fee_cents`, `financing_fee_cents`, `subtotal_cents`, `tax_rate_applied`, `tax_cents`, `chargeable_storage_days`, `storage_fee_cents`, `credit_memo_cents`, `deposit_applied_cents` and `total_due_cents`.

**Fee waivers.** A `SettlementOfficer` may waive part of the buyer's fees by supplying a `waiver_cents` amount of at least 0 — a zero waiver is accepted and simply changes nothing — and a mandatory non-empty `reason`. A waiver has no state precondition of its own: it may be granted at either settlement variant and whatever the invoice's `payment_status` and `release_status`. The waiver raises `credit_memo_cents` by `waiver_cents` and recomputes `total_due_cents` and `outstanding_cents`, incrementing the invoice's `version` by 1. Cumulative waivers on one invoice may not exceed five percent of its `subtotal_cents`: the sum of the existing `credit_memo_cents` and the requested `waiver_cents` must be no greater than five percent of `subtotal_cents` rounded half up to whole cents, and a request that would breach the ceiling is refused with `ERR_WAIVER_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. Segregation of duties applies: an officer who is recorded as the registration's `credit_approved_by_user_id` may not waive that buyer's fees. That separation check sits above the ceiling in the ladder, so an officer barred by segregation is told `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` even when the amount requested would also have breached the five-percent ceiling; and the check is vacuous when `credit_approved_by_user_id` is still null.

**Payments.** A `Bidder` tenders against an invoice by naming the `invoice_id`, an `amount_cents` between 1 and 1,000,000,000, a `method` of `WIRE`, `CARD` or `FINANCED_DRAW`, and a `currency_code`. The currency must equal the auction yard's `currency_code`; cross-currency tenders are refused with `ERR_CURRENCY_MISMATCH` rather than converted. The amount may not exceed `outstanding_cents` — the house holds no buyer credit balances, so settlement is exact or short, and an overpayment is refused with `ERR_OVERPAYMENT`. The invoice's `payment_status` must be `UNPAID` or `PARTIALLY_PAID`. Those three refusals are reached in ladder order rather than in the order this paragraph introduces them: the state guard first, then the overpayment test, then the currency test, so a wrong-currency tender against an already-`PAID` invoice reports the state and a wrong-currency overpayment reports the overpayment. A payment is created in `SETTLED` with `received_at` taken from the logical clock, after which the invoice's `amount_paid_cents`, `outstanding_cents` and `payment_status` are recomputed, the registration's `bid_cap_cents` is restamped as §3.5 requires of any write to an invoice of the registration, the bidder's `open_exposure_cents` is recomputed, and the invoice's `version` increments by 1.

The money axis follows from the arithmetic. A tender smaller than `outstanding_cents` moves the invoice `UNPAID → PARTIALLY_PAID`, or leaves it in `PARTIALLY_PAID`. A tender exactly equal to `outstanding_cents` moves the invoice to `PAID` — from `UNPAID` when the whole balance is settled in one instrument, and from `PARTIALLY_PAID` when the final instalment closes it out — and sets `outstanding_cents` to 0. One boundary case follows from this and is deliberate: when the deposit draw-down already covers an invoice in full, `total_due_cents` and `outstanding_cents` are both 0 while `payment_status` stays `UNPAID`, because only a payment advances the money axis and any tender of at least one cent would exceed a zero outstanding balance and be refused with `ERR_OVERPAYMENT`. Such an invoice can only be reopened to a tender by a finalisation that raises `total_due_cents` through accrued storage.

**Default.** Once the logical clock is strictly past `payment_due_at`, a `SettlementOfficer` may mark an `UNPAID` or `PARTIALLY_PAID` invoice as `IN_DEFAULT`, provided its `release_status` is not already `LOADED_OUT`. Exactly at `payment_due_at` the invoice is still on time and the request is refused with `ERR_PAYMENT_NOT_OVERDUE`. The transition increments the invoice's `version` by 1 and leaves the custody axis alone.

**Release authorisation.** Moving the custody axis `HELD → RELEASE_AUTHORIZED` is a `SettlementOfficer` action with four preconditions, each with its own refusal, and they are evaluated strictly in ladder order, which is: the custody axis must still be `HELD`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; the underlying sale's `status` must be `CONFIRMED` or `SETTLED`, otherwise the same `ERR_INVALID_STATE`; the lot's `title_status` must be `LIEN_CLEAR` or `LIEN_RELEASED`, otherwise `ERR_TITLE_NOT_CLEAR`; the invoice's `payment_status` must be `PAID`, otherwise `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_PAID`; and either the invoice is not cross-border, or one of its export packets has reached `EXPORT_CLEARED`, otherwise `ERR_EXPORT_NOT_CLEARED`. Title therefore outranks payment: an unpaid invoice on an encumbered lot reports `ERR_TITLE_NOT_CLEAR`, not `ERR_INVOICE_NOT_PAID`. A domestic invoice skips the export condition entirely. The invoice's `version` increments by 1.

### 3.9. Load-Out, Gate Release, Seller Settlement and Abandoned-Purchase Remedies

With release authorised, the buyer books a **load-out slot**. The request names the `invoice_id`, the `bay_id` of the chosen bay, the mandatory `expected_invoice_version` described in §2.7, a `slot_start` and `slot_end` forming a **half-open** interval — start inclusive, end exclusive, with `slot_end` strictly later than `slot_start` — a `transport_mode` of `BUYER_ARRANGED`, `HOUSE_BROKERED`, `RAIL` or `OCEAN_CONTAINER`, and optionally a `tonnage_kg` to reserve against the bay's crane. When `tonnage_kg` is omitted — or sent as an explicit null — it defaults to the lot's `weight_kg`; when supplied it must be at least 1 and no more than the lot's `weight_kg`. The lower bound is an ordinary rung-3 range check, but the upper bound can only be tested once the body foreign keys have resolved and the lot is in hand, so it is evaluated immediately after that resolution and before the ownership, version and state guards; the refusal is nevertheless `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The new slot is created in `BOOKED` with `gate_pass_ref`, `supersedes_slot_id` and `cancel_reason` all null, and it moves the invoice `RELEASE_AUTHORIZED → LOADOUT_BOOKED`.

Five constraints police a booking beyond the version guard. They are listed here in narrative order, but they are evaluated in the ladder order of §2.8, which puts the crane rating **before** the two interval rules even though it is described last below. The bay must belong to the same yard as the auction that sold the machine — equipment leaves only from the yard that held the event — otherwise `ERR_BAY_YARD_MISMATCH`. The bay's `is_active` must be true, otherwise `ERR_BAY_INACTIVE`; bays taken out of service keep their existing bookings but accept no new ones. The requested interval must not overlap another `BOOKED` or `CHECKED_IN` slot of the **same invoice**, otherwise `ERR_SLOT_OVERLAP`; because the intervals are half-open, a slot starting at exactly another slot's `slot_end` is adjacent rather than overlapping and is accepted. The interval must sit inside the auction's load-out window: `slot_start` at or after the auction's `window_end`, and `slot_end` at or before the auction's `loadout_deadline_at`, otherwise `ERR_SLOT_OUTSIDE_DEADLINE`. Finally the bay's crane rating must hold: sum `tonnage_kg` across the bay's `BOOKED` and `CHECKED_IN` slots that overlap the requested interval in time, add this slot's own tonnage, and the total must not exceed the bay's `crane_capacity_kg`, otherwise `ERR_BAY_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`. Each individual booking may fit comfortably while the overlapping set does not — that is the point of the aggregate check. One subtlety: when the booking supersedes the invoice's own existing `BOOKED` slot, that superseded slot is excluded from the tonnage sum, so a buyer moving a machine from one time window to another is not made to compete against itself. That indulgence is confined to the tonnage sum — the superseded slot is **not** excluded from the same-invoice overlap test, because it is still `BOOKED` at the moment that test runs, so a rebooking must genuinely move to a window that does not overlap the one it replaces or it is refused with `ERR_SLOT_OVERLAP`.

**Rebooking** is the self-loop on the custody axis: a slot booking made while the invoice is already `LOADOUT_BOOKED` leaves the invoice in `LOADOUT_BOOKED` and supersedes the prior booking, provided the version guard passes and exactly one slot of that invoice is currently `BOOKED`. The prior slot becomes `CANCELLED` with its `cancel_reason` set to `SUPERSEDED`, and this happens **only after every validation has passed** — a rejected rebooking leaves the original booking intact. The new slot records the cancelled slot's identifier in `supersedes_slot_id`, `reserved_tonnage_kg` is recomputed for both bays involved when the move crosses bays, and the invoice's `version` increments by 1.

The buyer's transporter then **checks in**, moving the slot `BOOKED → CHECKED_IN`. Check-in is only permitted when the logical clock is at or after `slot_start` and strictly before `slot_end`; arrival before the window opens or after it closes is refused with `ERR_SLOT_NOT_IN_WINDOW`. The slot's `version` increments by 1.

**Completion** is a `SettlementOfficer` action requiring a `gate_pass_ref`, a slot in `CHECKED_IN` and — checked immediately after the slot's own state — an invoice whose `release_status` is still `LOADOUT_BOOKED`. It moves the slot `CHECKED_IN → COMPLETED`, the invoice `LOADOUT_BOOKED → LOADED_OUT`, and the lot `SETTLED → RELEASED` — the terminal state of the lot lifecycle. That last move is conditional on the lot actually being in `SETTLED`: where no seller settlement has been posted and the lot is still `HAMMERED`, the machine leaves the gate and the invoice reaches `LOADED_OUT` while the lot's `lifecycle_status` and `version` are both left exactly as they were. The bay's `reserved_tonnage_kg` is recomputed, since a completed slot no longer reserves tonnage, the bidder's `lifetime_purchase_cents` is recomputed, and the `version` of the slot, the invoice and the lot each increment by 1.

A booking may also end without a load-out, through a cancellation carrying an `outcome` of `CANCELLED` or `NO_SHOW` and an optional `cancel_reason`. A `CANCELLED` outcome is available from `BOOKED` or `CHECKED_IN`; the buyer may cancel its own `BOOKED` slot, while cancelling a slot already checked in is a `SettlementOfficer` action. A `NO_SHOW` outcome is a `SettlementOfficer` action available only from `BOOKED` and only once the logical clock has reached `slot_end`; recording a no-show before the window has closed is refused with `ERR_SLOT_NOT_IN_WINDOW`. Both officer-only restrictions are enforced against an acting `Bidder` as `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403), decided after the ownership check and before any state guard, so a buyer asking for a `NO_SHOW`, or asking to cancel a `CHECKED_IN` slot, is told the role is wrong rather than the state. The optional `cancel_reason` is written onto the slot only when the request actually supplies one; omitting it leaves whatever the field already held, which for a first cancellation is null. Either outcome performs the compensating tonnage move — the bay's `reserved_tonnage_kg` returns to its pre-booking value — and moves the invoice `LOADOUT_BOOKED → RELEASE_AUTHORIZED` once no non-terminal slot of that invoice remains, so the buyer may book again. `COMPLETED`, `CANCELLED` and `NO_SHOW` are all terminal for a slot.

**Seller settlement.** In parallel with the buyer side, a `SettlementOfficer` posts the **consignor settlement** for a sale, naming only the `sale_id`. It requires that no `POSTED` settlement exists for it already — a replay is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_SETTLEMENT`, and this duplicate check is evaluated before the state guard, since the first posting has itself moved the sale out of `CONFIRMED` — and it requires the sale to be `CONFIRMED`. Posting creates the settlement in `POSTED` and returns it with 201, moves the sale `CONFIRMED → SETTLED` and the lot `HAMMERED → SETTLED` — the lot move being conditional on the lot actually being in `HAMMERED`, so a lot already moved on by an earlier remedy is left untouched — reduces the consignment's `advance_outstanding_cents` by the recovered advance, and recomputes the consignor's `lifetime_net_proceeds_cents`.

The settlement is a four-bucket waterfall over `gross_proceeds_cents`, which while `POSTED` equals the sale's `hammer_price_cents`. Bucket one is `commission_cents`, a marginal two-tier schedule with a floor, selected by the consignment's `commission_schedule_code`. Under `RESERVE_STD`, take twelve percent of the portion of gross up to 10,000,000 cents plus nine percent of everything above it, rounding each tier half up to whole cents before adding them, then use the larger of that sum and a floor of 100,000 cents. Under `UNRESERVED_STD`, the same shape applies with eight percent on the first 10,000,000 cents, six percent above it, and a floor of 50,000 cents — the unreserved seller pays less because the house takes less risk on an unreserved lot.

Bucket two is the refurbishment recharge. Sum `actual_cost_cents` across the lot's refurbishment orders whose `status` is `COMPLETED`; call that the actual spend. The remaining authorisation is the consignment's `refurb_authorization_cap_cents` less whatever refurbishment has already been recharged by earlier `POSTED` settlements of the same consignment, floored at zero — the cap is a batch-level budget, not a per-lot one. `refurb_recharge_cents` is the smaller of the actual spend and the remaining authorisation, and `house_absorbed_refurb_cents` is the difference the auctioneer eats: the actual spend less the recharge.

Bucket three is `advance_recovery_cents`, which claws back the cash advanced at intake. Compute what is left in the waterfall — gross less `commission_cents` less `refurb_recharge_cents` — and recover the smaller of the consignment's `advance_outstanding_cents` and the larger of zero and that remainder. The advance is therefore never recovered beyond what the sale can bear.

Bucket four is `no_sale_fee_cents`, which is 0 for as long as the settlement is `POSTED`. `net_proceeds_cents` is the residual: gross less `commission_cents`, less `refurb_recharge_cents`, less `advance_recovery_cents`, less `no_sale_fee_cents`.

**Abandoned purchases.** When a buyer in default walks away, the `SettlementOfficer` applies the abandonment remedy, supplying a `reason`. It requires the invoice's `payment_status` to be `IN_DEFAULT` and its `release_status` to be `HELD`, `RELEASE_AUTHORIZED` or `LOADOUT_BOOKED` — never `LOADED_OUT`, since a machine already through the gate cannot be abandoned. This is the deepest cascade in the system and it runs in a fixed order.

At the first depth, the invoice's `payment_status` moves `IN_DEFAULT → WRITTEN_OFF` and its `release_status` moves to `ABANDONED`, both terminal. `storage_fee_cents` and `chargeable_storage_days` are both restamped under the final-variant storage rules as of the current logical clock, and `deficiency_claim_cents` is computed. Still at the first depth, every `SETTLED` payment of the invoice becomes `REVERSED` and `amount_paid_cents` is recomputed to 0, every `BOOKED` or `CHECKED_IN` slot of the invoice becomes `CANCELLED` with its `cancel_reason` set to `ABANDONED` and the affected bays' `reserved_tonnage_kg` recomputed, and the registration's deposit hold is forfeited in full — the bidder's `deposit_held_cents` falls and `deposit_forfeited_cents` rises by `deposit_hold_cents`, with `deposit_total_cents` unchanged.

The **deficiency claim** is the figure the house books against the walking buyer, and it is deliberately a compensating calculation rather than a penalty: add `hammer_price_cents`, the storage accrued as of abandonment under the final-variant rules, and a re-offer administration fee of 75,000 cents; then subtract the forfeited deposit hold and the cash the buyer had already tendered, measured as `amount_paid_cents` **before** the payments of this invoice are reversed; and take the larger of the result and zero. A buyer who had already paid most of the balance can therefore leave a claim of exactly zero rather than a negative one, and `deficiency_claim_cents` is never negative.

At the second depth, the sale moves to `DEFAULTED` — from `SETTLED` when a settlement had been posted, and from `CONFIRMED` when none ever was — and the bidder's `default_strike_count` increments by 1, which in turn suspends every `APPROVED` registration of that bidder once the count reaches 2. The auction's `gross_hammer_cents` is recomputed, since a defaulted sale no longer contributes.

At the third depth, the sale's `POSTED` consignor settlement is reversed. Reversal restamps the settlement's figures rather than deleting the row: `gross_proceeds_cents`, `commission_cents`, `refurb_recharge_cents` and `advance_recovery_cents` all become 0 while `house_absorbed_refurb_cents` is deliberately left at the figure the original posting stamped — the refurbishment the house ate is a historical fact that the reversal does not undo — `no_sale_fee_cents` becomes 25,000 cents, and `net_proceeds_cents` becomes negative 25,000 cents — the seller now owes the house the no-sale fee. The settlement's `status` moves `POSTED → REVERSED`, the consignment's `advance_outstanding_cents` is restored to what it was before the recovery, and the consignor's `lifetime_net_proceeds_cents` is recomputed.

At the fourth depth, and only when a settlement was actually reversed, the lot moves `SETTLED → REOFFER_QUEUED` and the consignment's `reoffer_lot_count` is recomputed, putting the machine back in line for a future event where it will be consigned as a fresh lot carrying `replacement_of_lot_id` pointing at this one. When no settlement had ever been posted, this fourth depth is not taken: the lot stays in `HAMMERED`, because there was nothing to reverse. Last of all the deposit waterfall is re-run across the registration's invoices, which restamps this invoice's `deposit_applied_cents`, `total_due_cents` and `outstanding_cents` from the newly accrued storage against an `amount_paid_cents` of 0. The invoice's `version`, and the `version` of every other entity the cascade wrote in its own right — the cancelled slots, the sale, any suspended registration, the reversed settlement and, at the fourth depth, the lot — each increment by 1; the bidder is the exception noted in §2.9, its forfeited deposit and incremented `default_strike_count` moving without a version bump, as do the payments, which carry no `version` at all.

**Reconciliation.** Closing an auction's books is the last act. A `SettlementOfficer` moves the auction `CLOSED → RECONCILED`, which is terminal, and this is permitted only when every one of the auction's `CONFIRMED` sales has a `POSTED` consignor settlement and no sale of the auction is still `PROVISIONAL` or `CONSIGNOR_COUNTERED`. An unresolved negotiation or an unsettled confirmed sale refuses the request with `ERR_SETTLEMENT_INCOMPLETE`. Reconciliation recomputes `gross_hammer_cents` and increments the auction's `version` by 1. A `RECONCILED` auction also stops competing for the yard's ring, so its window no longer blocks the scheduling of a new live on-site event.

## 5. Error Handling

Every failure returns the standard error envelope of §2.2 with the `error_id` listed below. When a request violates more than one rule, the single error returned is the one the ladder in §2.8 reaches first. Three transport-level `error_id` values sit outside this domain catalogue but use the same envelope: a request for a path that matches no endpoint answers `ERR_NOT_FOUND` with 404, a request whose method is not defined for an otherwise recognised path answers `ERR_METHOD_NOT_ALLOWED` with 405, and any unanticipated server fault answers `ERR_INTERNAL` with 500.
