# OMS Anesthesia Permitting API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

An oral and maxillofacial surgery practice occupies an unusual regulatory position: it operates a licensed office anesthesia facility, a surgical schedule, an implant consignment cabinet, and a diagnostic imaging suite, all under one roof and all under separate oversight regimes. The state dental board grants the office an anesthesia permit at a specific depth of sedation, inspects the facility, and can restrict or revoke the privilege on the strength of a single critical deficiency or a single sentinel adverse event. Meanwhile the same practice must schedule third-molar extractions and orthognathic cases against surgeon block time, verify that each patient fasted and arranged an escort, decide whether a pathology or trauma procedure bills to the medical or the dental plan, and prove seven years later that a cone-beam study was clinically justified.

This document specifies the OMS Anesthesia Permitting API, a system of record that treats the anesthesia permit as the master gate over everything clinical. The recurring failure mode in practices today is that the schedule and the permit drift apart — cases stay confirmed after the office loses its privilege, emergency drills lapse quietly, consigned fixtures stay reserved for surgery that will never happen, and nobody notices until the board's next visit. The API closes that gap by deriving a single practice-level privilege state from the permit's status and by cascading every enforcement action downward through cases, evaluations, implant reservations, and inventory counts in one atomic step.

Two design commitments follow from that. First, compliance is computed, never asserted: drill coverage, inspection scores, risk bands, recovery-bay occupancy, benefit allocations, and consignment exposure are all derived from primitive records by rules stated in this document, so no operator can hand-set a compliance figure. Second, time is explicit: every window, deadline, and expiry is evaluated against a server-held logical clock rather than wall-clock time, which makes a two-year permit term, a thirty-day remediation window, and a seven-year imaging retention obligation all testable inside a single session.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contracts that hold for every endpoint in the system: authentication, response shape, pagination, resource identity, time, validation ordering, concurrency, numeric precision, and the cascade discipline that keeps the practice's derived state consistent with its permit.

### 2.1. Operators, Identity, and Role-Based Access Control

Every request — reads included — must carry the `X-User-Role` header naming the acting operator's capacity. The system recognises exactly seven roles: `PracticeAdministrator`, `SchedulingCoordinator`, `BenefitsSpecialist`, `InventoryManager`, `Surgeon`, `AnesthesiaCoordinator`, and `QualityReviewer`. A request whose `X-User-Role` is missing or is not one of those seven spellings is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). Once the role is recognised, the system checks it against the authorised-role list published for that endpoint in §4; a role that does not appear there is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403). These two checks run before any other work: before path identifiers are resolved, before the body is parsed, and before any business rule is consulted.

The second header, `X-User-ID`, carries the acting operator's identity, and its meaning depends on the role. Three of the roles are backed by a persisted entity. When `X-User-Role` is `Surgeon`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `StaffMember`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `StaffMember` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID` — a surgeon may only move a case into recovery or complete it when `X-User-ID` equals that case's `primary_surgeon_id`. When `X-User-Role` is `AnesthesiaCoordinator`, `X-User-ID` is likewise the `id` of the acting `StaffMember`, and signing an evaluation requires `X-User-ID` to equal that evaluation's `evaluator_staff_id`. When `X-User-Role` is `QualityReviewer`, `X-User-ID` is again the `id` of the acting `StaffMember`, and signing off a morbidity review requires `X-User-ID` to equal the event's `reviewer_staff_id`. For all three of these entity-backed roles `X-User-ID` is mandatory; its absence is an `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` condition, and a present-but-wrong value on an operation bound to a specific staff member yields `ERR_ACTOR_NOT_ASSIGNED` (HTTP 403).

The remaining four roles are administrative and are not tied to a persisted row. When `X-User-Role` is `PracticeAdministrator`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it for audit purposes but never compares it to any entity's `id`. The same holds when `X-User-Role` is `SchedulingCoordinator`, when `X-User-Role` is `BenefitsSpecialist`, and when `X-User-Role` is `InventoryManager`: in each of those four cases the header is an opaque operator identifier, and no ownership comparison is performed against it.

Because this is a surgical practice, identity is also a safety control rather than merely an access control. The practice operates under strict segregation of duties: the staff member who performs a case may not be the staff member who reviews or verifies it. Concretely, the morbidity reviewer of an adverse event must not be the `primary_surgeon_id` of the case the event was filed against, and the staff member performing a day-of verification must not be the `evaluator_staff_id` who authored the evaluation being verified — nor may that verifier be the case's operating surgeon. A violation of either rule is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403), which is a distinct condition from `ERR_ACTOR_NOT_ASSIGNED`: the former means the right person is doing the wrong job, the latter means the wrong person is doing the job.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint is paginated with the same two query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A value outside those bounds is a field-value violation and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_FIELD_VALUE`.

Unless a specific endpoint's description in §4 says otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending — oldest record first — so that a paged walk over a collection is stable while records are being added. List responses carry four pagination fields inside the `meta` object: `limit` and `offset` echo the effective values applied to the query, `total_count` reports how many records matched the filter before the page window was applied, and `has_more` is true whenever records remain beyond the current page. Single-resource reads and action endpoints omit all four.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps, and the `version` Counter

Every resource in the system is identified by `id`, a server-minted UUID. Identifiers carry no type prefix and no embedded meaning; clients must never parse or construct them. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording the instant the row was written. That same rendering — normalised to UTC, with exactly three fractional digits and a trailing `Z` — is used for every other instant the server itself derives, whether it is read off the logical clock or computed by date arithmetic: a permit's `issued_at`, `expires_at`, and `remediation_deadline_at`, an inspection's `outcome_recorded_at`, an evaluation's `signed_at` and `verified_at`, a case's `recovery_started_at`, an adverse event's `reported_at` and `reporting_deadline_at`, an imaging study's `retention_until`, the sweep's `swept_at`, and the `current_utc` and `previous_utc` the clock endpoint reports are all rendered that way. An instant supplied by the caller and retained on a row — `applied_on`, `scheduled_for`, `conducted_at`, `date_of_birth`, `acls_expires_on`, `block_start` and `block_end`, `planned_start` and `planned_end`, `expiry_date`, `acquired_at`, `occurred_at`, and the two `fasting_*_last_at` instants — is instead stored and echoed back exactly as the caller spelled it and is never re-rendered, so two instants that denote the same moment may legitimately be spelled differently on the same response.

Most resources are mutable and therefore carry two further common properties. `updated_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits that is refreshed on every mutation of the row, and `version` is an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increments by exactly 1 on every mutation. The `version` counter is the anchor of the concurrency scheme described in §2.7, and it is bumped whether the mutation was requested directly by an operator or applied indirectly by a cascade — a `BlockTime` whose minute ledger was rewritten because some other case was cancelled has an advanced `version` just as surely as one an operator released by hand. Practices, anesthesia permits, facility inspections, staff members, patients, block times, surgical cases, pre-anesthesia evaluations, benefit determinations, implant lots, implant reservations, and adverse events all behave this way.

Two record types are deliberately immutable and are therefore exceptions. An `EmergencyDrill` is a permanent log line: once written it is never edited, and a mistake is corrected by logging a fresh drill rather than by amending the old one. An `ImagingStudy` is likewise write-once, because its retention obligation is computed at acquisition and must not be recomputed later. Both of these carry only `id` and `created_at`; neither `updated_at` nor `version` exists on them, and neither appears in their responses.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock

All temporal reasoning in this system — permit expiry, remediation windows, the trailing drill window, fasting intervals, block release deadlines, implant sterility dates, adverse-event reporting deadlines, and imaging retention — is evaluated against a server-held logical clock rather than against wall-clock time. Before any client sets it, the clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z` — reported, like every other server-rendered instant, with three fractional digits, so a first read or a first displacement shows it as `2026-08-01T00:00:00.000Z`; this startup default is mandatory, and the server never falls back to the host's real time. Any operator role may advance the clock by supplying a `current_utc` instant, and the response reports both the newly effective `current_utc` and the `previous_utc` value it displaced.

The clock is monotonic: a requested value that is earlier than the current value is rejected with `ERR_CLOCK_MONOTONIC_VIOLATION` (HTTP 409), while a value equal to the current one is accepted and leaves the clock where it is. Setting the clock mutates no entity — nothing expires, suspends, or releases merely because time passed. Deadlines are *observed* by the operations that read them and are *enforced* in bulk only by the compliance sweep described in §3.10.

### 2.6. Validation Order

When a request violates more than one rule, the response is decided by exactly one of them: the system evaluates its checks in a fixed ascending priority order and the first violated rule wins. Because much of the discriminating behaviour of this API lies in *which* of several plausible errors comes back, this ordering is normative, not advisory.

Ten checks form the universal prefix and run, in this sequence, on every request they apply to. First, header authentication: `X-User-Role` must be present and one of the seven roles, and `X-User-ID` must be present whenever the acting role is one of the three entity-backed ones, failing which the answer is `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. Second, endpoint authorisation against the role list of §4, failing which the answer is `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`. Third, resolution of every identifier embedded in the request path, failing which the answer is `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`. Fourth, on every write endpoint, structural validation of the body — well-formed JSON, all required fields present, no unknown fields, and a `service_lines` array that is neither empty nor longer than eight entries — failing which the answer is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Fifth, still on every write endpoint, per-field type, enum, and range validation, failing which the answer is `ERR_INVALID_FIELD_VALUE`. Sixth, on every write carrying a foreign key, resolution of that key to a live row, failing which the answer is `ERR_REFERENCED_ENTITY_NOT_FOUND`. Seventh, practice-scope agreement between the referenced rows, failing which the answer is `ERR_PRACTICE_SCOPE_MISMATCH`. Eighth, the actor-binding check for entity-backed roles, failing which the answer is `ERR_ACTOR_NOT_ASSIGNED`. Ninth, the segregation-of-duties check, failing which the answer is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Tenth, the single-open-permit rule, failing which the answer is `ERR_PERMIT_ALREADY_OPEN`.

After that prefix, the optimistic-concurrency checks of §2.7 are evaluated, then the clock-monotonicity check of §2.5, then the state-transition legality check that asks whether the anchor row's current state declares the requested transition at all (`ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, HTTP 409 — which is also what a repeat call of a one-shot action receives). Only once a request has cleared all of that does the system evaluate the endpoint's domain gates, and each workflow narrative in §3 states the order of its own gates explicitly.

The fifth check in the prefix deserves its concrete inventory, because these are declared constraints rather than judgement calls. A practice `name` is 1 to 120 characters and required; `state_code` is exactly two uppercase letters; `recovery_bay_count` is at least 1 and at most 12; `turnover_buffer_minutes` is at least 0 and at most 120; `consignment_credit_limit_cents` is at least 0. A staff member's `full_name` is 1 to 120 characters. A patient's `asa_class` and `airway_class` are each an integer from 1 to 4, `weight_kg` is greater than 0, and `dental_annual_remaining_cents` is at least 0. A block's `room_code` is 1 to 16 characters, `release_deadline_hours` is at least 0 and at most 336, and `block_end` must be strictly later than `block_start`. A case's `planned_end` must be strictly later than `planned_start`, and its `total_charge_cents` is at least 0. An evaluation's `asa_class_recorded` and `airway_class_recorded` are each an integer from 1 to 4 and its `comorbidity_count` is at least 0. A reservation's `quantity` is at least 1. An implant lot's `diameter_mm` and `length_mm` are each greater than 0 and its `unit_cost_cents` and `quantity_on_hand` are each at least 0. An imaging study's `dose_mgy_cm2` is greater than 0. A drill's `participant_staff_ids` array must hold at least two entries. Each service line's `charge_cents` is at least 0. Violations of any of these — including `block_end` at or before `block_start` and `planned_end` at or before `planned_start` — return `ERR_INVALID_FIELD_VALUE` (HTTP 422).

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Shared Parents

Three write operations in this system reach into a parent row that other operators are also editing, and each one therefore requires the client to declare which version of that parent it was looking at. In every case the field is mandatory, not optional, and a mismatch against the parent's current `version` rejects the write outright rather than merging it.

Confirming a surgical case must carry `expected_block_version`, holding the client's last observed `version` of the case's parent `BlockTime`. If the block's `version` has advanced since the client read it — because another case held minutes against it, another case was confirmed on it, another case on it was cancelled, or the block's open minutes were released — the confirmation is rejected with `ERR_STALE_BLOCK_VERSION` (HTTP 412). This exists because holding and confirming legitimately rewrite the block's committed and open minutes, so a confirmation computed against a roster the client never observed would otherwise be admitted against stale capacity.

Creating an explicit implant reservation must carry `expected_lot_version`, holding the client's last observed `version` of the target `ImplantLot`. If the lot's `version` has advanced since the client read it — because another reservation was placed against it, a reservation on it was consumed or released, or the lot was quarantined — the creation is rejected with `ERR_STALE_LOT_VERSION` (HTTP 412), because each of those actions supersedes the caller's availability snapshot.

Creating a pre-anesthesia evaluation for a case must carry `expected_case_version`, holding the client's last observed `version` of the parent `SurgicalCase`. If the case's `version` has advanced since the client read it, the creation is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CASE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Creation is destructive by design — it supersedes any prior signed or verified evaluation on the case and resets the case's clearance — so a writer holding a stale view of the case would silently discard an evaluation it never saw.

No other endpoint takes a version field. In particular, the auto-sizing reservation endpoint takes none, because the caller never named a lot and therefore cannot have observed one; the selector reads availability itself at the moment of the call.

### 2.8. Numeric Precision and Rounding

Money is held in whole cents as integers throughout: `unit_cost_cents`, `line_subtotal_cents`, `restocking_fee_cents`, `consignment_credit_cents`, `consignment_liability_cents`, `consignment_committed_cents`, `consignment_credit_limit_cents`, `total_charge_cents`, `medical_allowed_cents`, `dental_allowed_cents`, `patient_responsibility_cents`, `dental_annual_remaining_cents`, and `dental_annual_remaining_snapshot_cents` are all integer cent quantities, never fractional currency. Wherever a percentage or coinsurance rate is applied to a cent amount, the product is rounded to whole cents using round-half-up.

Three computed quantities are decimals carried to exactly two decimal places, also with round-half-up: `drill_compliance_ratio`, `utilization_pct`, and both risk scores (`provisional_risk_score` and `final_risk_score`). Rounding position matters and is specified per calculation: the risk score is rounded once after its multiplier is applied and only then receives its integer penalties, and every coinsurance product in a benefit allocation is rounded per service line before any summation. Summing first and rounding once at the end is incorrect and can differ by a cent.

Minute counters — `block_duration_minutes`, `committed_minutes`, `released_minutes`, `open_minutes`, `duration_minutes`, `recovery_minutes_required`, `npo_solids_minutes`, and `npo_clears_minutes` — are whole integers computed as whole minutes between two instants. `inspection_score` is produced by integer arithmetic. Ages in whole years, both `age_years` and the age used for imaging retention, are floored — a patient three days short of a birthday is still the younger age.

### 2.9. Practice Scope and Path Resolution

The `Practice` is the tenancy boundary of this system. Every clinical record belongs to exactly one practice, either directly through its own `practice_id` or transitively through the case, patient, or lot it hangs from, and cross-practice references are refused rather than silently ignored. When a surgical case is drafted, its patient, primary surgeon, monitor, and block must all belong to the same practice as the case; when a reservation is placed, the lot must belong to the case's practice; when a drill is logged, every member of `participant_staff_ids` must be employed by the named practice; when a morbidity review is opened, the reviewer must work at the practice that owns the event's case; when an imaging study is registered, the patient and — if one is named — the case must belong to the same practice; and when a block is created, its surgeon must be employed by the named practice. Any such mismatch is rejected with `ERR_PRACTICE_SCOPE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 409).

Two collections in this system are reached through a parent path — a case's pre-anesthesia evaluations and a case's auto-sized implant reservations — and their path identifiers are resolved strictly left to right. The case identifier is resolved to a live row before the request body is read at all, so a request naming a non-existent case returns `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404) even if its body is also malformed. All remaining collections are top-level and are linked to their parents by foreign keys carried in the request body; those keys resolve at priority six with `ERR_REFERENCED_ENTITY_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404), which is a body-level failure and is deliberately distinguished from the path-level `ERR_RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`.

### 2.10. Privilege Cascades and Hierarchical Consistency

The practice carries a derived gate, `sedation_privilege_state`, which is the single authority over whether sedation may be delivered. It is never set by an operator. It is recomputed from the practice's open anesthesia permit on every change to that permit's `status`, by a fixed mapping: when there is no open permit at all, or the permit's `status` is `APPLIED` or `INSPECTION_PENDING`, or the permit's `status` is `EXPIRED` or `REVOKED`, the privilege is `UNPERMITTED`; an `ACTIVE` permit yields `PERMITTED`; a permit on `PROBATION` yields `RESTRICTED`; and a `SUSPENDED` permit yields `SUSPENDED`.

Enforcement is hierarchical. When a permit falls to `SUSPENDED` or `EXPIRED`, a five-hop cascade runs in one step, and every hop is a consequence of the hop above it. Hop one sets `sedation_privilege_state` on the practice. Hop two, because the privilege no longer permits sedation, reverts every surgical case of that practice whose `scheduling_status` is `CONFIRMED` and whose `planned_anesthesia_level` is anything other than `LOCAL_ONLY` back to `BLOCK_HELD` and sets its `clearance_status` to `DEFERRED`. Hop three, because those cases left the confirmed roster, moves each of their implant reservations still in `HELD` to `RELEASED`, applying the tiered restocking fee and dropping the consignment credit to zero. Hop four, because those reservations left `HELD`, decrements each parent lot's `quantity_reserved` by the released quantity, recomputes that lot's `available_quantity`, and increments the lot's `version`. Hop five, because reservation liabilities changed, recomputes the practice's `consignment_committed_cents`. Every row touched along the chain has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented.

A permit falling to `PROBATION` runs a narrower cascade: only cases whose `planned_anesthesia_level` is `DEEP_SEDATION_GENERAL` and whose `clearance_status` is `CLEARED` are pushed to `DEFERRED`, because a `RESTRICTED` practice may still deliver the shallower levels. Suspension and expiry, by contrast, defer every case whose `planned_anesthesia_level` is not `LOCAL_ONLY`. In both shapes the affected cases' pre-anesthesia evaluations keep their own `status` of `VERIFIED` — the evaluation was validly performed and is not retroactively falsified — but the case can no longer be confirmed until a fresh evaluation is created, signed, and verified.

Reinstatement is deliberately forward-only. Recovering a permit to `ACTIVE`, whether by remediating a probation or by passing a re-inspection out of suspension, restores `sedation_privilege_state` to `PERMITTED` and reopens scheduling for the levels it unlocks, but it does **not** undo the cascade: cases already pushed to `clearance_status` `DEFERRED` stay deferred, reservations already `RELEASED` are not re-held, and restocking fees already charged are not refunded. Nor does reinstatement extend the permit term — `expires_at` is stamped once and is never moved by remediation or re-inspection.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Practice Foundations: Office, Roster, and Patient Intake

Everything in this system hangs from a registered practice. A `PracticeAdministrator` registers the office by supplying its legal `name` (1 to 120 characters) and the two-letter `state_code` of the licensing jurisdiction whose office anesthesia permit will govern it, together with `recovery_bay_count` — the number of physical post-anesthesia recovery bays the office actually has, at least one and at most twelve. Two further inputs are optional and carry defaults that matter later: `turnover_buffer_minutes`, the decontamination and turnover gap applied around every case sharing an operatory, defaults to 15 minutes, and `consignment_credit_limit_cents`, the ceiling on the practice's outstanding consignment liability, defaults to 500,000 cents — five thousand dollars of unpaid manufacturer stock. A newly registered practice has no permit, so `sedation_privilege_state` is stamped `UNPERMITTED`, `open_adverse_event_count` is 0, and `consignment_committed_cents` is 0.

Three of the practice's properties are derived counters that no operator writes. `sedation_privilege_state` follows the permit mapping of §2.10 and takes one of `UNPERMITTED`, `PERMITTED`, `RESTRICTED`, or `SUSPENDED`. `open_adverse_event_count` is the number of adverse events filed against cases of this practice whose own `status` is anything other than `CLOSED`. `consignment_committed_cents` is the practice's rolling consignment exposure: the sum of `consignment_liability_cents` across every reservation on any lot of this practice whose reservation `status` is `HELD` or `CONSUMED`. Reservations that were released or shorted drop out of that sum entirely, and an owned — that is, non-consignment — lot still contributes its full subtotal, because the consignment flag suppresses only the credit, never the exposure.

For operational oversight there is a computed compliance read on the practice that no other endpoint duplicates. It answers with the practice's own `practice_id` and current `sedation_privilege_state`; the `open_permit_id`, `permit_status`, and `permit_level` of the practice's open permit; the `required_drill_types` that permit level demands alongside the `covered_drill_types` actually satisfied inside the trailing window; the `drill_compliance_ratio` and the boolean `drill_compliant` derived from it; the `open_adverse_event_count`; and both `consignment_committed_cents` and `consignment_credit_limit_cents` so the remaining consignment headroom is visible at a glance. Reading this view recomputes the drill ratio against the current logical clock but persists nothing — it is a pure projection, and a practice whose drills have silently aged out of the window will show a lapsed ratio here before any enforcement action has been taken against it.

The administrator also builds the clinical roster. Each `StaffMember` is created against a `practice_id` with a `full_name` of 1 to 120 characters and a `role_type` drawn from `SURGEON`, `ANESTHESIA_MONITOR`, `RECOVERY_NURSE`, and `INVENTORY_TECH`. Two credential fields ride along: `monitoring_certified`, a boolean defaulting to false that records whether the member holds the anesthesia-assistant monitoring certificate, and `acls_expires_on`, the ISO 8601 UTC expiry instant of the member's ACLS or PALS card, where a null means the member has never been certified at all. Both credentials are consulted by the staffing matrix at confirmation time, never at hire time — the roster records facts, and the schedule enforces them. Staff may be listed with `practice_id` and `role_type` filters, which is how an operator answers the question the staffing matrix asks about recovery nurses.

Patients are registered by a `PracticeAdministrator` or a `SchedulingCoordinator` against a `practice_id`, with `full_name`, `date_of_birth` as an ISO 8601 UTC birth instant, an intake `asa_class` and `airway_class` each from 1 to 4, and `weight_kg` greater than zero. Coverage is captured with three optional inputs: `medical_plan_active` and `dental_plan_active`, both booleans defaulting to false, and `dental_annual_remaining_cents`, the unspent dental annual maximum, defaulting to 150,000 cents. The single derived patient field is `age_years`, the whole years elapsed from `date_of_birth` to the logical clock, floored. Note carefully that the intake `asa_class` and `airway_class` are a snapshot for the chart only; the risk score never reads them, preferring the classes recorded at the pre-anesthesia visit. Patients may be listed filtered by `practice_id`.

### 3.2. Office Anesthesia Permitting: Application, Inspection, Drill Readiness, and Remediation

The permit is the spine of the compliance story. A `PracticeAdministrator` applies for one by naming the `practice_id`, the `permit_level` sought — one of `MINIMAL_SEDATION`, `MODERATE_SEDATION`, or `DEEP_SEDATION_GENERAL` — and `applied_on`, the ISO 8601 UTC instant the application was filed, which may not be later than the logical clock. A practice may hold at most one permit whose `status` is outside `EXPIRED` and `REVOKED`; a second application while one is open is refused with `ERR_PERMIT_ALREADY_OPEN` (HTTP 409). The new permit is stamped `status` `APPLIED`, and two derived fields are populated immediately: `required_drill_types`, fixed by the level applied for, and `drill_compliance_ratio`, computed from whatever drills the practice has already logged. `issued_at`, `expires_at`, `remediation_deadline_at`, and `last_enforcement_reason` are all null on a fresh application, and `probation_count` is 0. A permit's `status` moves through `APPLIED`, `INSPECTION_PENDING`, `ACTIVE`, `PROBATION`, and `SUSPENDED`, terminating in either `EXPIRED` or `REVOKED`; permits may be read individually or listed with `practice_id` and `status` filters.

Drill obligations scale with sedation depth, and the sets are cumulative. A `MINIMAL_SEDATION` permit requires the practice to have rehearsed `AIRWAY_OBSTRUCTION` and `LOCAL_ANESTHETIC_TOXICITY`. A `MODERATE_SEDATION` permit requires those two plus `LARYNGOSPASM` and `CARDIAC_ARREST` — four scenarios. A `DEEP_SEDATION_GENERAL` permit requires those four plus `MALIGNANT_HYPERTHERMIA` — five. Either a `PracticeAdministrator` or an `AnesthesiaCoordinator` logs a completed rehearsal as an `EmergencyDrill`, naming the `practice_id`, the `drill_type` rehearsed (one of `AIRWAY_OBSTRUCTION`, `LARYNGOSPASM`, `MALIGNANT_HYPERTHERMIA`, `CARDIAC_ARREST`, and `LOCAL_ANESTHETIC_TOXICITY`), the `conducted_at` instant, which may not be later than the logical clock, and `participant_staff_ids`, a list of at least two staff members who must all be employed by that same practice. The optional `deficiency_noted` boolean defaults to false and is the debrief's verdict: a drill that recorded an unresolved deficiency is excluded from compliance arithmetic entirely, so logging a failed rehearsal never improves the practice's standing. Logging any drill recomputes the open permit's `drill_compliance_ratio` and increments that permit's `version`. Drills are immutable and carry no version of their own; they may be read individually or listed with `practice_id`, `drill_type`, and `deficiency_noted` filters, oldest first by `conducted_at` and then by `created_at`.

The ratio itself is a distinct-coverage measure over a trailing window, not a count of rehearsals. Begin with the required drill-type set for the open permit's `permit_level`. A drill qualifies when it belongs to that practice, its `deficiency_noted` is false, its `conducted_at` falls strictly later than the instant exactly 365 days before the logical clock and at or before the logical clock itself, and its `drill_type` is a member of the required set. Count the distinct drill types among the qualifying drills — a type rehearsed five times counts once, and a type outside the required set counts not at all — divide that count by the number of types in the required set, and round the quotient half-up to two decimal places. That value is `drill_compliance_ratio`, and it lies between 0.00 and 1.00. The practice is drill-compliant when the ratio is at or above 0.75; because the comparison is inclusive, a ratio of exactly 0.75 passes. A `MODERATE_SEDATION` practice with three of its four types covered sits at 0.75 and is compliant; a `DEEP_SEDATION_GENERAL` practice with three of five sits at 0.60 and is not. The ratio is recomputed whenever a drill is logged, whenever an inspection result is posted, on every read of the practice compliance view, and during the compliance sweep.

Inspections are the mechanism that turns an application into a permit. A `PracticeAdministrator` schedules one by naming the `permit_id` under inspection and the `scheduled_for` instant the inspector is booked for, which must be strictly later than the logical clock. Scheduling is admitted only when the permit's `status` is `APPLIED`, `ACTIVE`, `PROBATION`, or `SUSPENDED`, and only when no inspection of that permit is already sitting in `SCHEDULED` — one open inspection per permit at a time. The new inspection is stamped `status` `SCHEDULED`, with `inspection_score` and `outcome_recorded_at` both null and its three deficiency counters — `minor_deficiency_count`, `major_deficiency_count`, and `critical_deficiency_count` — defaulting to 0. Scheduling from `APPLIED` is the one origin that moves the permit: it advances the permit from `APPLIED` to `INSPECTION_PENDING`, increments the permit's `version`, and recomputes the practice's `sedation_privilege_state`, which remains `UNPERMITTED` because neither `APPLIED` nor `INSPECTION_PENDING` confers privilege. Booking a routine or complaint inspection while the permit is `ACTIVE`, on `PROBATION`, or `SUSPENDED` creates the `SCHEDULED` inspection and leaves the permit's `status` untouched — a re-inspection does not reopen a permit. Inspections may be read individually or listed filtered by `permit_id` and `status`, oldest first by `created_at`.

Posting the result is where the scoring happens. The administrator submits the inspector's three counts, which overwrite whatever the inspection row held before the score is computed, and the call is admitted only while the inspection is still `SCHEDULED` and the logical clock has reached or passed its `scheduled_for` instant. The score starts at 100; each minor deficiency costs 2 points, each major deficiency costs 7 points, and each critical deficiency costs 25 points. The raw figure is the deduction of all three products from 100, and `inspection_score` is that figure floored at 0 so it is never negative. The outcome then branches on two tests taken in a fixed order. If `critical_deficiency_count` is one or more, the inspection ends `FAILED_CRITICAL` regardless of the score — a single immediate-jeopardy finding dominates, so an otherwise pristine facility with one critical deficiency and a score of 75 still fails critically. Otherwise, if `inspection_score` is at or above 70 the inspection is `PASSED`; because that comparison is inclusive, a score of exactly 70 passes. Otherwise it is `FAILED_CORRECTABLE`. In every branch the computed `inspection_score` is persisted and `outcome_recorded_at` is stamped with the logical clock, and the practice's `sedation_privilege_state` is recomputed with the practice's `version` incremented. All three inspection outcomes are terminal for the inspection row itself; a mistaken result is corrected by scheduling a fresh inspection, not by re-posting.

The permit's response to that outcome depends on where the permit already stood, and the branches are not symmetrical. A `PASSED` result on a permit in `INSPECTION_PENDING` whose `drill_compliance_ratio`, recomputed at that instant, is at or above 0.75 advances the permit to `ACTIVE`: `issued_at` is set to the logical clock if it was null, `expires_at` is set to `issued_at` plus 24 calendar months if it was null, `last_enforcement_reason` and `remediation_deadline_at` are both cleared to null, `sedation_privilege_state` becomes `PERMITTED`, and both the permit's and the practice's `version` increment. A `PASSED` result on a permit in `INSPECTION_PENDING` whose ratio is below 0.75 instead lands on `PROBATION`: `issued_at` and `expires_at` are stamped exactly as on the `ACTIVE` branch — the permit really was issued — but `last_enforcement_reason` becomes `DRILL_LAPSE`, `probation_count` increases by 1, `remediation_deadline_at` is set to the logical clock plus 30 days, `sedation_privilege_state` becomes `RESTRICTED`, and the narrow cascade runs: every case of that practice whose `planned_anesthesia_level` is `DEEP_SEDATION_GENERAL` and whose `clearance_status` is `CLEARED` moves to `clearance_status` `DEFERRED`. A `FAILED_CORRECTABLE` result on a permit in `INSPECTION_PENDING` sends it back to `APPLIED` with `last_enforcement_reason` `DEFICIENCY_SCORE`, the privilege recomputed to `UNPERMITTED`, and the permit's `version` incremented; this is the revision cycle, and the applicant may schedule a fresh inspection from `APPLIED` again. A `FAILED_CRITICAL` result on a permit in `INSPECTION_PENDING` that was never issued — meaning its `issued_at` is still null — drives the permit straight to the terminal `REVOKED` with `last_enforcement_reason` `CRITICAL_DEFICIENCY`, the privilege recomputed to `UNPERMITTED`, and the permit's `version` incremented.

Once a permit is live the same results bite harder. A `FAILED_CRITICAL` result against a permit that is `ACTIVE` or on `PROBATION` drives it to `SUSPENDED` with `last_enforcement_reason` `CRITICAL_DEFICIENCY`, increments the permit's `version`, and runs the full five-hop suspension cascade of §2.10 over the practice's privilege, its confirmed sedated cases, their held implant reservations, those reservations' parent lots, and finally the practice's consignment exposure. A `FAILED_CORRECTABLE` result against an `ACTIVE` permit drops it to `PROBATION` with `last_enforcement_reason` `DEFICIENCY_SCORE`, increases `probation_count` by 1, sets `remediation_deadline_at` to the logical clock plus 30 days, moves the privilege to `RESTRICTED`, and defers the practice's `CLEARED` `DEEP_SEDATION_GENERAL` cases. And a `PASSED` result against a `SUSPENDED` permit whose `drill_compliance_ratio` is at or above 0.75 reinstates it to `ACTIVE`, clearing `last_enforcement_reason` to null, restoring the privilege to `PERMITTED`, and incrementing the permit's `version` — but `expires_at` is not extended, so a permit suspended late in its term returns with whatever time remained. `last_enforcement_reason` always holds the reason of the most recent enforcement entry and is drawn from `CRITICAL_DEFICIENCY`, `DEFICIENCY_SCORE`, `SENTINEL_EVENT`, `DRILL_LAPSE`, and `PERMIT_EXPIRY`; it is null only on a permit that has never been enforced against.

Probation is escapable, but only inside its window and only by fixing the underlying lapse. The `PracticeAdministrator` closes out a probation by attesting that the deficiencies were corrected, supplying an `attestation_note` with the attestation. Three gates run in ascending order. First, the permit's `status` must actually be `PROBATION`; anything else is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Second, the logical clock must be at or before `remediation_deadline_at`; a clock past the deadline yields `ERR_REMEDIATION_WINDOW_EXPIRED` (HTTP 409). Third, the `drill_compliance_ratio`, recomputed at that instant, must be at or above 0.75; a still-lapsed practice is refused with `ERR_DRILL_COMPLIANCE_LAPSED` (HTTP 409). A successful attestation returns the permit to `ACTIVE`, clears both `remediation_deadline_at` and `last_enforcement_reason` to null, restores `sedation_privilege_state` from `RESTRICTED` to `PERMITTED` — which reopens `DEEP_SEDATION_GENERAL` scheduling — and increments the practice's `version`. `probation_count` is permanent history and is never decremented, so a permit reinstated twice still reports a count of 2. Consistent with §2.10, cases already deferred by the probation cascade are not restored by the reinstatement.

### 3.3. Operating-Room Block Time and the Minute Ledger

Surgeon block time is the currency of the schedule, and the system tracks it as a conserved minute ledger rather than as a calendar entry. A `SchedulingCoordinator` or a `PracticeAdministrator` reserves a block against a `practice_id` for a single `surgeon_id`, who must be a staff member of that practice whose `role_type` is `SURGEON`. The block occupies one operatory, named by `room_code` (1 to 16 characters), over the interval from `block_start` inclusive to `block_end` exclusive, where `block_end` must be strictly later than `block_start`. Room conflicts are keyed on the pair of practice and `room_code`, so two practices may use the same operatory label without colliding. The optional `release_deadline_hours` — how many hours before `block_start` unused minutes become releasable — defaults to 48 and may not exceed 336, which is two weeks.

At creation the block is stamped `status` `RESERVED`, and its ledger is initialised: `block_duration_minutes` is the whole number of minutes from `block_start` to `block_end`, `committed_minutes` and `released_minutes` are both 0, `open_minutes` equals `block_duration_minutes`, and `utilization_pct` is 0.00. Thereafter `committed_minutes` is always the sum of `duration_minutes` over the surgical cases attached to this block whose `scheduling_status` is one of the four committing states — `BLOCK_HELD`, `CONFIRMED`, `IN_RECOVERY`, or `COMPLETED`. Cases in `DRAFT` or `CANCELLED` contribute nothing, which is why cancelling a case returns its minutes to `open_minutes` rather than to `released_minutes`. `released_minutes` is a stored counter that only ever increases, and only by a release action. `open_minutes` is `block_duration_minutes` less `committed_minutes` less `released_minutes`, and is never negative. `utilization_pct` is one hundred multiplied by `committed_minutes`, divided by `block_duration_minutes`, rounded half-up to two decimal places. The invariant that holds after every write is that `committed_minutes`, `released_minutes`, and `open_minutes` sum to exactly `block_duration_minutes`; a two-hundred-minute block carrying ninety committed minutes therefore reports a utilization of 45.00 with 110 minutes still open. Blocks are readable individually and listable with `practice_id`, `surgeon_id`, and `status` filters.

Releasing a block hands its still-open minutes back to the practice, and a `SchedulingCoordinator` may do so with an optional `release_note` for the record. The action is admitted only while the block's `status` is `RESERVED`, and it branches on whether anything is committed. When `committed_minutes` is greater than zero the block becomes `PARTIALLY_RELEASED`: the current `open_minutes` are added to `released_minutes`, `open_minutes` is set to 0, and `utilization_pct` is recomputed against the unchanged `block_duration_minutes` — the committed minutes are untouched, so the percentage does not move, and the conservation identity still holds. When `committed_minutes` is zero the block goes straight to the terminal `RELEASED`: `released_minutes` is set to the full `block_duration_minutes` and `open_minutes` to 0. A `RELEASED` block can never again be held by a case, which is the point of the distinction. The compliance sweep applies the same two branches automatically once a block's release deadline has arrived, as described in §3.10.

### 3.4. Case Drafting, Block Holds, and Staged Treatment Chains

A `SchedulingCoordinator` drafts every surgical case. The mandatory payload names the `practice_id`, the `patient_id` of the subject, the `primary_surgeon_id` — who must be a `StaffMember` of that practice whose `role_type` is `SURGEON` — the `planned_anesthesia_level`, the `procedure_category`, the operative window as `planned_start` inclusive and `planned_end` exclusive with `planned_end` strictly later, and `total_charge_cents`, the gross professional charge that the benefit machinery must later conserve exactly. `planned_anesthesia_level` is one of `LOCAL_ONLY`, `MINIMAL_SEDATION`, `MODERATE_SEDATION`, or `DEEP_SEDATION_GENERAL`. `procedure_category` is one of `THIRD_MOLAR`, `ORTHOGNATHIC`, `PATHOLOGY`, `TRAUMA`, `IMPLANT`, or `TMJ`, and it drives both benefit orientation and referral coordination.

Four inputs are optional at drafting and each changes the case's downstream obligations. `monitor_staff_id` names the dedicated sedation monitor; leaving it null is legal only for a case whose `planned_anesthesia_level` is `LOCAL_ONLY`, because every deeper level requires a monitor at confirmation. `block_id` may be supplied at drafting or left null until the case takes block minutes. `prerequisite_case_id` points at a staged-treatment predecessor — the classic example being third-molar removal that must finish before orthognathic surgery — and stays null on an unstaged case. `referring_provider_npi` records the ten-digit NPI of the referring dentist or orthodontist; supplying it on a `THIRD_MOLAR` or `ORTHOGNATHIC` case is precisely what makes referral coordination mandatory before confirmation, and a case filed without it is simply uncoordinated.

Drafting derives four fields. `duration_minutes` is the whole minutes from `planned_start` to `planned_end`. `recovery_minutes_required` follows the anesthesia level: zero minutes for `LOCAL_ONLY`, 20 for `MINIMAL_SEDATION`, 45 for `MODERATE_SEDATION`, and 90 for `DEEP_SEDATION_GENERAL`; this figure is what makes a case occupy a recovery bay. `scheduling_status` starts at `DRAFT` and `clearance_status` at `NOT_EVALUATED`. `referral_coordination_status` starts at `PENDING` when the `procedure_category` is `THIRD_MOLAR` or `ORTHOGNATHIC` **and** a `referring_provider_npi` was supplied, and at `NOT_REQUIRED` in every other combination — a `TRAUMA` case with a referring NPI, or a `THIRD_MOLAR` case without one, are both `NOT_REQUIRED`. `recovery_started_at` and `cancellation_reason` are null at drafting.

The two status dimensions on a case are genuinely independent. `scheduling_status` walks `DRAFT`, `BLOCK_HELD`, `CONFIRMED`, and `IN_RECOVERY` toward the terminal `COMPLETED` or `CANCELLED`, while `clearance_status` cycles among `NOT_EVALUATED`, `EVALUATED`, `CLEARED`, and `DEFERRED` with no terminal state at all. Neither dimension's transitions read or write the other except where a rule says so explicitly: a case may be `CANCELLED` while its `clearance_status` reads `CLEARED`, and a case may sit at `BLOCK_HELD` with clearance `DEFERRED` indefinitely. The two dimensions meet in exactly two places — the confirmation gate, which demands `CLEARED`, and the privilege cascades of §2.10, which force `CLEARED` down to `DEFERRED`. Cases may be listed with filters on `practice_id`, `patient_id`, `primary_surgeon_id`, `monitor_staff_id`, `block_id`, `prerequisite_case_id`, `scheduling_status`, and `clearance_status`, which is the reverse-reachability channel for all six of a case's foreign keys including the self-referential staged link.

Staged treatment is a chain, and the chain must stay acyclic. Whether a `prerequisite_case_id` arrives with the initial draft or is attached later, the system walks the chain outward from the proposed predecessor before writing anything; if the walk reaches the case being edited — meaning the link would make a case its own transitive prerequisite — the write is refused with `ERR_PREREQUISITE_CYCLE` (HTTP 409). The predecessor may also be re-pointed after drafting: a `SchedulingCoordinator` may attach or replace a case's `prerequisite_case_id` while the case's `scheduling_status` is still `DRAFT` or `BLOCK_HELD`, supplying the new predecessor, which must belong to the same practice and must not close a cycle. A successful re-point increments the case's `version`. Once a case is confirmed its staged predecessor is frozen; attempting to re-point from `CONFIRMED` or beyond is an `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.

Referral coordination is a one-shot acknowledgement track. A `SchedulingCoordinator` records the referring dentist's or orthodontist's sign-off on the coordinated plan by supplying `acknowledged_by_npi`, which advances `referral_coordination_status` from `PENDING` to `ACKNOWLEDGED`. Attempting the same acknowledgement on a case whose `referral_coordination_status` is `NOT_REQUIRED` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409), as is a repeat acknowledgement of a case already `ACKNOWLEDGED`.

Taking block minutes is the move from `DRAFT` to `BLOCK_HELD`. The coordinator names the `block_id` to hold against, and the request is evaluated through six gates in ascending order. The case must currently be in `DRAFT`, or the answer is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. The practice's `sedation_privilege_state` must permit the case's `planned_anesthesia_level`, or the answer is `ERR_SEDATION_PRIVILEGE_UNAVAILABLE` (HTTP 409) — the same privilege test described under confirmation below, applied here so that an unpermitted office cannot even reserve minutes for sedation it may not deliver. The block must belong to the case's practice, its `status` must not be `RELEASED`, and the case's half-open window from `planned_start` to `planned_end` must be wholly contained inside the block's half-open window from `block_start` to `block_end`; a case that starts before the block opens or ends after it closes is refused with `ERR_CASE_OUTSIDE_BLOCK_WINDOW` (HTTP 409). The case's `primary_surgeon_id` must equal the block's `surgeon_id`, since a block belongs to one surgeon; a mismatch is `ERR_BLOCK_SURGEON_MISMATCH` (HTTP 409). Then comes the turnover test, and finally the capacity test.

Room turnover is where the practice's `turnover_buffer_minutes` earns its keep. No other case of the same practice in the same `room_code` whose `scheduling_status` is `BLOCK_HELD`, `CONFIRMED`, `IN_RECOVERY`, or `COMPLETED` may overlap this case's window once the buffer is appended — and the buffer is appended to the incumbent case's window *and* to the candidate's, so the effective comparison is between the interval from each case's `planned_start` up to its `planned_end` plus `turnover_buffer_minutes`. The consequence is that a gap shorter than the buffer is a conflict while a gap exactly equal to the buffer is not: with the default 15-minute buffer, a new case starting 15 minutes after an incumbent ends is admitted, and one starting 14 minutes after is refused with `ERR_ROOM_TURNOVER_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409). The last gate is arithmetic: the case's `duration_minutes` may not exceed the block's current `open_minutes`, or the hold is refused with `ERR_BLOCK_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409).

A successful hold persists `block_id` on the case, advances `scheduling_status` from `DRAFT` to `BLOCK_HELD`, adds the case's `duration_minutes` to the block's `committed_minutes`, recomputes the block's `open_minutes` and `utilization_pct`, and increments the block's `version` — which is exactly why a client that read the block before someone else's hold will find its `expected_block_version` stale at confirmation time.

### 3.5. Pre-Anesthesia Evaluation, Risk Scoring, and the Day-of Gate

Anesthesia clearance is a two-signature process owned by the `AnesthesiaCoordinator` role, and it is deliberately separated from scheduling. An evaluation is opened against a specific case, and because opening one destroys any clearance the case already had, the caller must present `expected_case_version` matching the case's current `version`; a stale value is refused with `ERR_STALE_CASE_VERSION` as described in §2.7. The mandatory payload names `evaluator_staff_id` — the author, who must be a staff member of the case's practice whose `role_type` is either `SURGEON` or `ANESTHESIA_MONITOR` — together with `asa_class_recorded` and `airway_class_recorded`, the ASA physical status and Mallampati airway classes observed at the pre-anesthesia visit, each from 1 to 4. These recorded classes are what the risk score reads; they may legitimately differ from the patient's intake `asa_class` and `airway_class`, and the score never falls back to the intake values. Four further inputs are optional: `comorbidity_count`, the number of active comorbidities documented, defaults to 0; and `fasting_solids_last_at`, `fasting_clears_last_at`, `escort_confirmed` (defaulting to false), and `escort_name` may be pre-populated at creation, though in practice they are supplied at day-of verification. `escort_name` is null whenever `escort_confirmed` is false.

Creating an evaluation is the revision loop of the clearance dimension. The new row is stamped `status` `DRAFT` with its `provisional_risk_score` and `risk_band` computed immediately by the provisional variant of the risk score, while `final_risk_score`, `npo_solids_minutes`, `npo_clears_minutes`, `signed_at`, and `verified_at` are all null. Any prior evaluation on the same case whose `status` is `SIGNED` or `VERIFIED` moves to the terminal `SUPERSEDED`, and the case's `clearance_status` resets to `NOT_EVALUATED` — whether it stood at `EVALUATED`, at `CLEARED`, or at `DEFERRED` — with the case's `version` incremented. That reset is what lets a deferred case be re-cleared: the operator opens a fresh evaluation, signs it, and verifies it again. An evaluation's `status` walks `DRAFT`, `SIGNED`, and `VERIFIED`, with `SUPERSEDED` and `VOIDED` as its two terminal states. Evaluations may be read individually or listed with `case_id`, `evaluator_staff_id`, and `status` filters.

Signing is the author's own act. The evaluation must be in `DRAFT`, the acting `X-User-ID` must equal its `evaluator_staff_id` — anyone else is refused with `ERR_ACTOR_NOT_ASSIGNED` — and the parent case's `scheduling_status` must be neither `COMPLETED` nor `CANCELLED`. Signature stamps `signed_at` with the logical clock, recomputes `provisional_risk_score` and re-derives `risk_band` from it against the case's *current* `planned_anesthesia_level` (so a case whose planned depth changed between drafting and signature is rescored, not left stale), advances the evaluation from `DRAFT` to `SIGNED`, moves the case's `clearance_status` from `NOT_EVALUATED` to `EVALUATED`, and increments the case's `version`.

The score itself is a weighted factor block scaled by the planned sedation depth. Start from the recorded ASA class: base points are ten times `asa_class_recorded`. Airway points are eight times the amount by which `airway_class_recorded` exceeds 2, and never negative, so classes 1 and 2 contribute nothing while class 3 contributes 8 and class 4 contributes 16. Age points are 6 when the patient's `age_years` is 70 or more, otherwise 4 when `age_years` is under 3 — the very young and the very old both carry risk — and otherwise 0. Comorbidity points are three times `comorbidity_count`, capped at 12, so the fifth and later comorbidity add nothing. Weight points are 5 when the patient's `weight_kg` is 120 kilograms or more, otherwise 0. Those five contributions are added into a factor subtotal, which is then multiplied by the level multiplier of the case's `planned_anesthesia_level`: one half for `LOCAL_ONLY`, eight tenths for `MINIMAL_SEDATION`, exactly one for `MODERATE_SEDATION`, and one and three tenths for `DEEP_SEDATION_GENERAL`. That product, rounded half-up to two decimal places, is the core score, and it is the whole of `provisional_risk_score`.

The final variant runs at day-of verification and adds three flat penalties to the already-rounded core score, never to the subtotal — because they are applied after the multiplier, they are never scaled by it, which is why an otherwise identical evaluation can sit two bands apart between the two variants. Fifteen points are added when `npo_solids_minutes` is under 480. Ten points are added when `escort_confirmed` is false and the case's `planned_anesthesia_level` is anything other than `LOCAL_ONLY`. Six points are added when the practice's `sedation_privilege_state` is `RESTRICTED` at that moment. The sum of the core score and the accumulated penalties, rounded half-up to two decimal places, is `final_risk_score`.

`risk_band` is derived from whichever score the variant produced — from `final_risk_score` from the moment the final variant has run at day-of verification, including a verification a clinical gate then refused, and from `provisional_risk_score` before that — using three floors: a score below 25 is `LOW`, a score from 25 up to but not including 40 is `ELEVATED`, a score from 40 up to but not including 55 is `HIGH`, and a score of 55 or more is `PROHIBITIVE`. Evaluations whose `status` is `SUPERSEDED` or `VOIDED` are never rescored, whatever else changes around them.

Day-of verification is performed by a second person and is the point at which the clinical gates fire. The verifier supplies `intent`, which is either `CLEAR` or `DEFER`, along with the actual `fasting_solids_last_at` and `fasting_clears_last_at` instants, the `escort_confirmed` boolean, and, when an escort was confirmed, the `escort_name`. Three access conditions precede everything: the evaluation must be in `SIGNED`, the parent case must be neither `COMPLETED` nor `CANCELLED`, and the acting `X-User-ID` must be neither the evaluation's `evaluator_staff_id` nor the case's `primary_surgeon_id` — the office may not have one person write, verify, and operate. A violation of either identity rule is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

Verification then computes the two fasting intervals: `npo_solids_minutes` is the whole minutes from `fasting_solids_last_at` to the logical clock at verification, and `npo_clears_minutes` is the whole minutes from `fasting_clears_last_at` to that same instant. `final_risk_score` and `risk_band` are recomputed by the final variant **before** any gate is evaluated, so the published score reflects reality even on a rejected attempt: when one of the three clinical gates below refuses the call, the recomputed `final_risk_score`, the `risk_band` derived from it, the two supplied fasting instants, `escort_confirmed` and `escort_name`, and both `npo_*_minutes` counters are all persisted on the evaluation and its `updated_at` and `version` are refreshed, while `status` stays `SIGNED`, `verified_at` stays null, and the case is left untouched. The three access conditions named above, by contrast, reject before anything is written at all. The gates then run only when `intent` is `CLEAR`, in ascending order. First fasting: for any case whose `planned_anesthesia_level` is above `LOCAL_ONLY`, `npo_solids_minutes` must be at least 480 — eight hours without solids — and `npo_clears_minutes` at least 120, two hours without clear liquids; a shortfall in either is `ERR_FASTING_REQUIREMENT_UNMET` (HTTP 409). Then escort: again for any level above `LOCAL_ONLY`, `escort_confirmed` must be true and `escort_name` must be non-empty, since a sedated patient may not be discharged without a responsible adult; otherwise the answer is `ERR_ESCORT_REQUIRED` (HTTP 409). Then the band: the recomputed `risk_band` must not be `PROHIBITIVE`, or the answer is `ERR_RISK_BAND_PROHIBITIVE` (HTTP 409).

Either intent completes the verification itself: `status` moves from `SIGNED` to `VERIFIED`, `verified_at` is stamped with the logical clock, `final_risk_score` and the re-derived `risk_band` are published, and the case's `version` increments. The intents differ in what they do to the case's clearance and in whether the gates ran at all. `CLEAR` moves the case's `clearance_status` from `EVALUATED` to `CLEARED`, which is the only route to a confirmable case. `DEFER` moves it from `EVALUATED` to `DEFERRED` and skips every clinical gate — no fasting, escort, or band check is enforced on that branch. This is deliberate and is the only lawful way to record a `PROHIBITIVE` patient: the coordinator publishes the honest final score, parks the case at `DEFERRED`, and the case simply cannot be confirmed until a fresh evaluation clears it.

Cancelling a case voids its evaluations, and how much score survives depends on how far the evaluation had progressed. An evaluation still in `DRAFT` moves to `VOIDED` with no score published at all. One in `SIGNED` moves to `VOIDED` with its `provisional_risk_score` retained for the record while `final_risk_score` stays null. One in `VERIFIED` moves to `VOIDED` with both scores retained. In all three cases the case's own `clearance_status` is deliberately left exactly where it was — cancellation is a scheduling act, not a clinical one.

### 3.6. Confirmation, Recovery, Completion, and Cancellation

Confirmation is the heaviest gate in the system: it is the moment the practice commits to operating, and it is where privilege, permit rank, drill readiness, staffing, clearance, staged prerequisites, referral coordination, monitor availability, and recovery-bay capacity are all tested together. The `SchedulingCoordinator` confirms a case by presenting `expected_block_version`, and nothing else. Because so many rules converge here, their order is normative and is stated in full below; the first violated rule decides the response.

The stale-block check runs first: `expected_block_version` must equal the parent block's current `version`, or the answer is `ERR_STALE_BLOCK_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Next the case's `scheduling_status` must be `BLOCK_HELD`, or the answer is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409) — a `DRAFT` case has no block minutes and an already-`CONFIRMED` case cannot be confirmed twice. Third, the case's `clearance_status` must read exactly `CLEARED`; `NOT_EVALUATED`, `EVALUATED`, and `DEFERRED` are all refused with `ERR_CASE_NOT_CLEARED` (HTTP 409), which is what a case pushed to `DEFERRED` by a privilege cascade will see even though it was confirmable an hour earlier.

Fourth is the sedation privilege test. The practice's `sedation_privilege_state` must admit the case's `planned_anesthesia_level`: `UNPERMITTED` and `SUSPENDED` both block every level above `LOCAL_ONLY`, `RESTRICTED` blocks `DEEP_SEDATION_GENERAL` while admitting `MINIMAL_SEDATION` and `MODERATE_SEDATION`, and `PERMITTED` admits all four. A `LOCAL_ONLY` case is admitted under every privilege state, including `UNPERMITTED` and `SUSPENDED`, because no sedation is being delivered. A blocked combination is refused with `ERR_SEDATION_PRIVILEGE_UNAVAILABLE` (HTTP 409). Fifth is permit rank, which is a different question from privilege: the practice's open permit — the one whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `PROBATION` — must be licensed at least as deep as the case requires. Ranking `LOCAL_ONLY` at zero, `MINIMAL_SEDATION` at one, `MODERATE_SEDATION` at two, and `DEEP_SEDATION_GENERAL` at three, the open permit's `permit_level` rank must be at or above the rank the case's `planned_anesthesia_level` demands; a `LOCAL_ONLY` case demands rank zero and therefore needs no permit level at all. An office holding only a `MODERATE_SEDATION` permit that tries to confirm a `DEEP_SEDATION_GENERAL` case is refused with `ERR_PERMIT_LEVEL_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 409). Sixth, the open permit's `drill_compliance_ratio`, recomputed at that instant, must be at or above 0.75, or the answer is `ERR_DRILL_COMPLIANCE_LAPSED` (HTTP 409) — drills that quietly aged out of the trailing 365-day window stop the schedule here, before any sweep has run.

Seventh is the staffing matrix, which is level-dependent and is checked in five steps that all report the same code. For a `LOCAL_ONLY` case nothing is required: no monitor, no certification, no surgeon ACLS card, and no recovery nurse. For a `MINIMAL_SEDATION` case a monitor is required but need not be certified, and neither a current surgeon ACLS card nor a dedicated recovery nurse is required. For a `MODERATE_SEDATION` case a monitor is required and must be certified, and a current surgeon ACLS card is required, but no dedicated recovery nurse is. For a `DEEP_SEDATION_GENERAL` case all four are required — certified monitor, current surgeon ACLS card, and a recovery nurse on the roster. The five steps run in this order: whether a monitor is required and `monitor_staff_id` is null; whether a monitor is required and that monitor's `role_type` is something other than `ANESTHESIA_MONITOR`; whether certification is required and the monitor's `monitoring_certified` is false; whether a surgeon ACLS card is required and the `primary_surgeon_id`'s `acls_expires_on` is at or before the case's `planned_start`, which a null `acls_expires_on` also fails since it means never certified; and whether a dedicated recovery nurse is required and the practice has no `StaffMember` at all whose `role_type` is `RECOVERY_NURSE`. Note that the recovery-nurse test counts the practice's whole roster and is indifferent to whether that nurse is busy on another case. Any of the five failing yields `ERR_STAFFING_RATIO_UNMET` (HTTP 409).

Eighth, if the case carries a `prerequisite_case_id`, that predecessor's `scheduling_status` must be `COMPLETED`; a predecessor still in flight is refused with `ERR_PREREQUISITE_CASE_INCOMPLETE` (HTTP 409). A case with a null `prerequisite_case_id` skips this test. Ninth, referral coordination: a case whose `referral_coordination_status` is still `PENDING` — meaning a `THIRD_MOLAR` or `ORTHOGNATHIC` case with a `referring_provider_npi` whose referring provider has not signed off — is refused with `ERR_REFERRAL_ACKNOWLEDGEMENT_REQUIRED` (HTTP 409); `NOT_REQUIRED` and `ACKNOWLEDGED` both pass. Tenth, the monitor must not be double-booked: the `monitor_staff_id` may not already be assigned to another case in `BLOCK_HELD`, `CONFIRMED`, or `IN_RECOVERY` whose half-open window from `planned_start` to `planned_end` overlaps this case's. No turnover buffer applies to people — a monitor may finish one case and begin another at the same instant — so back-to-back monitoring is legal while any true overlap is refused with `ERR_MONITOR_DOUBLE_BOOKED` (HTTP 409).

Eleventh and last is recovery-bay capacity, and it is a peak-concurrency test rather than a simple count. A case occupies a bay over the half-open interval that begins at its `planned_end` and runs for `recovery_minutes_required` minutes; a `LOCAL_ONLY` case, whose `recovery_minutes_required` is zero, never occupies a bay and never triggers this test. The candidate interval is compared against the recovery intervals of every *other* case in the same practice whose `scheduling_status` is `CONFIRMED` or `IN_RECOVERY` and whose `recovery_minutes_required` is greater than zero; cases in `DRAFT`, `BLOCK_HELD`, `COMPLETED`, or `CANCELLED` are excluded entirely. Because the intervals are half-open, a recovery that ends exactly when another begins is not an overlap. Occupancy is then evaluated at a finite set of instants: the candidate interval's own start, plus the start of each overlapping interval that falls inside the candidate window, taken in chronological order of `planned_end` with `created_at` breaking exact ties. At each such instant, occupancy is the candidate itself plus the number of overlapping intervals that contain that instant, and the peak is the largest occupancy found. If the case requires recovery time at all and that peak exceeds the practice's `recovery_bay_count`, the confirmation is refused with `ERR_RECOVERY_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409). A two-bay office can therefore hold two overlapping recoveries but not three, and staggering the third case's `planned_end` past the others' recovery windows is the remedy.

A confirmation that survives all eleven gates advances the case's `scheduling_status` from `BLOCK_HELD` to `CONFIRMED` and increments the case's `version`. The parent block's `utilization_pct` is re-published against the confirmed roster and the block's `version` increments as well — the minutes themselves were already committed at hold time and do not move again, so `committed_minutes` and `open_minutes` are unchanged by confirmation.

Confirmation is also reversible without cancellation. When the practice's `sedation_privilege_state` leaves `PERMITTED` — through suspension or expiry — every `CONFIRMED` case whose `planned_anesthesia_level` is not `LOCAL_ONLY` reverts from `CONFIRMED` to `BLOCK_HELD` and has its `clearance_status` forced to `DEFERRED`, each of its implant reservations still in `HELD` moves to `RELEASED` (which returns the quantity to its lot's `quantity_reserved` and increments that lot's `version`), and the block's `committed_minutes` is left alone, because a `BLOCK_HELD` case still counts as committed. The case keeps its block, loses its clearance, and must be re-cleared and re-confirmed.

Once the logical clock has reached the case's `planned_start`, the operating surgeon moves the case into recovery. Only the case's own primary surgeon may do so: the acting `X-User-Role` must be `Surgeon` and `X-User-ID` must equal the case's `primary_surgeon_id`, or the answer is `ERR_ACTOR_NOT_ASSIGNED`. The case must be `CONFIRMED`, and the logical clock must be at or after `planned_start`. The transition stamps `recovery_started_at` with the logical clock, moves `scheduling_status` from `CONFIRMED` to `IN_RECOVERY`, increments the case's `version`, and starts the interval during which the case genuinely occupies a recovery bay for `recovery_minutes_required` minutes.

Closing the case out is likewise the primary surgeon's act, and it has two substantive preconditions beyond the state itself. The case must be `IN_RECOVERY`, and the logical clock must have reached `recovery_started_at` plus `recovery_minutes_required` minutes — a `DEEP_SEDATION_GENERAL` patient cannot be discharged 30 minutes into a 90-minute recovery. In addition, no implant reservation of the case may still be sitting in `HELD`: the inventory must be reconciled first, every fixture either consumed, released, or reported short. Completion moves `scheduling_status` from `IN_RECOVERY` to the terminal `COMPLETED`, increments the case's `version`, frees the recovery bay for future capacity projections, and makes the case eligible to serve as the `prerequisite_case_id` of a staged successor — which is exactly what unblocks an orthognathic case waiting on a third-molar extraction.

Cancellation is available from `DRAFT`, `BLOCK_HELD`, and `CONFIRMED`, and the `SchedulingCoordinator` supplies a `cancellation_reason` of `PATIENT_REQUEST`, `CLINICAL`, or `PRIVILEGE_SUSPENDED`, which is persisted on the case. What the cancellation unwinds depends on how far the case had progressed. Cancelling from `DRAFT` moves every pre-anesthesia evaluation of the case whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `SIGNED`, or `VERIFIED` to `VOIDED` and touches nothing else. Cancelling from `BLOCK_HELD` runs a four-hop chain: hop one subtracts the case's `duration_minutes` from the block's `committed_minutes`, recomputes `open_minutes` and `utilization_pct`, and increments the block's `version`; hop two, because the case left the committed roster, moves each of its implant reservations in `HELD` to `RELEASED` with the tiered restocking fee applied and `consignment_credit_cents` reset to 0; hop three, because those reservations left `HELD`, decrements each parent lot's `quantity_reserved` and increments that lot's `version`; hop four, because lot liabilities changed, recomputes the practice's `consignment_committed_cents`. The case's evaluations in `DRAFT`, `SIGNED`, or `VERIFIED` are voided as before. Cancelling from `CONFIRMED` runs that identical four-hop chain, voids the evaluations with their computed scores retained, and additionally drops the case's recovery-bay projection so the freed capacity is immediately available to other confirmations. In all three origins the case's `clearance_status` is deliberately left untouched, and cancelling an already-`CANCELLED` or `COMPLETED` case is an `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.

### 3.7. Implant Inventory, Sizing, and Consignment Exposure

The implant cabinet is managed by the `InventoryManager`, who receives stock as lots. A lot is booked against a `practice_id` with its `manufacturer_code` (the manufacturer's short code, such as "NBL" or "STM"), its `catalog_reference`, the fixture geometry as `diameter_mm` and `length_mm` in millimetres — both greater than zero — the `unit_cost_cents` per fixture, the `quantity_on_hand` physically in the cabinet, and the `expiry_date` at which sterility lapses. The optional `is_consignment` boolean, defaulting to false, distinguishes manufacturer consignment stock — unpaid until consumed — from stock the practice owns outright. A received lot is stamped `status` `AVAILABLE`, with `quantity_reserved` at 0 and `available_quantity` equal to `quantity_on_hand`. Thereafter `quantity_reserved` is always the sum of `quantity` over the lot's reservations whose `status` is `HELD`, and `available_quantity` is `quantity_on_hand` less `quantity_reserved`, never negative. Lots are readable individually and listable with `practice_id`, `manufacturer_code`, `is_consignment`, and `status` filters.

A recall or a sterility concern is handled by quarantining the lot with a `quarantine_reason`. Quarantine moves the lot's `status` from `AVAILABLE` to `QUARANTINED` and forces its `available_quantity` to 0 so nothing further can be drawn from it. Every reservation on the lot still in `HELD` moves to `RELEASED`, but — because a manufacturer recall is not the practice's fault — with `restocking_fee_cents` set to 0 rather than to the tiered fee, `consignment_credit_cents` reset to 0, and `consignment_liability_cents` therefore 0; each released quantity is returned to the lot's `quantity_reserved`. The practice's `consignment_committed_cents` is recomputed afterwards and the practice's `version` increments.

Fixtures are held for a case in one of two ways. In the explicit path the inventory manager names the `case_id`, the `lot_id`, the `quantity` of fixtures, and `expected_lot_version`; the reservation's `selection_mode` is recorded as `EXPLICIT` and `requested_diameter_mm` and `requested_length_mm` are both null, because no sizing search was performed. Four gates run in ascending order: the stale-lot check of §2.7 answering `ERR_STALE_LOT_VERSION`; then the lot's `status` must not be `QUARANTINED`, or the answer is `ERR_LOT_UNAVAILABLE` (HTTP 409); then the lot's `available_quantity` must be at least the requested `quantity` and the lot's `expiry_date` must fall after the case's `planned_start`, or the answer is `ERR_IMPLANT_STOCK_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 409) — stock that expires before the operation is no stock at all; then the consignment credit test described below.

In the auto-sizing path the manager describes the fixture instead of naming it, supplying `requested_diameter_mm`, `requested_length_mm`, and `quantity`, and the selector chooses the lot. No lot version is presented, because the caller never observed a lot. The candidate pool is every lot of the case's practice whose `status` is `AVAILABLE`, whose `expiry_date` falls after the case's `planned_start`, whose `available_quantity` is at least the requested `quantity`, whose `diameter_mm` differs from `requested_diameter_mm` by at most half a millimetre, and whose `length_mm` differs from `requested_length_mm` by at most two millimetres. Both tolerance comparisons are inclusive and dimensions are compared to one decimal place, so a lot exactly 0.5 mm off the requested diameter is eligible while one 0.6 mm off is not. If the pool is empty the request is refused with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_IMPLANT_LOT` (HTTP 409); the quarantine test still answers `ERR_LOT_UNAVAILABLE` where it applies. Otherwise the pool is ordered by `diameter_mm` ascending, then `length_mm` ascending, then `expiry_date` ascending, then `created_at` ascending, and the first row wins — a four-key total ordering, so the selection is fully deterministic and prefers the narrowest, then shortest, then oldest-expiring fixture that fits. The chosen lot becomes the reservation's `lot_id`, `selection_mode` is recorded as `AUTO_SIZED`, and the requested dimensions are retained on the reservation. Every pricing and ledger effect is identical to the explicit path. Reservations may be read individually or listed with `case_id`, `lot_id`, `selection_mode`, and `status` filters.

Pricing is pinned at the moment a reservation is created and never re-priced by later changes to the lot. `line_subtotal_cents` is the lot's `unit_cost_cents` multiplied by the reservation's `quantity`. The restocking tier is then selected once from `line_subtotal_cents` — not from the liability, and not marginally across tiers: a subtotal at or below 50,000 cents attracts fifteen percent, a subtotal above that but at or below 150,000 cents attracts ten percent, and anything above 150,000 cents attracts five percent. `restocking_fee_cents` is `line_subtotal_cents` multiplied by that single rate, rounded half-up to whole cents. `consignment_credit_cents` is twenty percent of `line_subtotal_cents`, rounded half-up to whole cents, when the lot's `is_consignment` is true, and 0 when it is false.

`consignment_liability_cents` then depends on the reservation's `status`. While `HELD`, and still after it becomes `CONSUMED`, the liability is `line_subtotal_cents` less `consignment_credit_cents`. On `RELEASED` the liability is exactly `restocking_fee_cents`, and `consignment_credit_cents` is reset to 0 — the practice pays only the restocking penalty for stock it sent back. On `SHORTED` the liability is the full `line_subtotal_cents`, with both `restocking_fee_cents` and `consignment_credit_cents` at 0, because fixtures that cannot be accounted for must be paid for in full. The practice's rolling `consignment_committed_cents` is the sum of `consignment_liability_cents` over the practice's reservations whose `status` is `HELD` or `CONSUMED`; released and shorted rows drop out. Before any new reservation is admitted, the practice's exposure including that reservation's liability is compared against `consignment_credit_limit_cents`, and a request that would push the total past the limit is refused with `ERR_CONSIGNMENT_CREDIT_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (HTTP 409). This test applies to both the explicit and the auto-sizing paths.

Creating a reservation in either path stamps its `status` `HELD`, computes all four money fields, adds the `quantity` to the lot's `quantity_reserved`, recomputes the lot's `available_quantity`, increments the lot's `version`, recomputes and re-checks the practice's `consignment_committed_cents`, and increments the practice's `version`.

Reservations end in one of three terminal dispositions. Recording that the fixtures were actually placed requires the reservation to be `HELD`, the parent case's `scheduling_status` to be `CONFIRMED` or `IN_RECOVERY` — fixtures cannot be placed in a case that has not been committed — and the lot's `status` to be `AVAILABLE`. Consumption moves the reservation to `CONSUMED`, subtracts the `quantity` from both the lot's `quantity_on_hand` and its `quantity_reserved`, recomputes `available_quantity`, increments the lot's `version`, leaves `consignment_liability_cents` exactly as it stood while `HELD`, recomputes the practice's `consignment_committed_cents` — a consumed reservation still counts toward exposure — and increments the practice's `version`.

The other two dispositions share one action, differentiated by a `disposition` input of `RELEASED` or `SHORTED`, and both require the reservation to be `HELD`. On `RELEASED` the fixtures come back to the shelf: `quantity_on_hand` is deliberately unchanged while `quantity_reserved` is decremented by the reservation's `quantity`, the tiered `restocking_fee_cents` is charged, `consignment_credit_cents` drops to 0, and `consignment_liability_cents` becomes the restocking fee. On `SHORTED` the fixtures are physically unaccounted for, so both `quantity_on_hand` and `quantity_reserved` are decremented, `restocking_fee_cents` and `consignment_credit_cents` are both 0, and `consignment_liability_cents` becomes the full `line_subtotal_cents`. Either disposition increments the lot's `version`, recomputes the practice's `consignment_committed_cents`, and increments the practice's `version`. The system-driven releases described in §2.10 and §3.6 — a cancelled case, a privilege-driven reversion — apply exactly the same field arithmetic as the operator-driven `RELEASED` disposition, restocking fee included; only the recall quarantine waives the fee.

### 3.8. Benefit Determination and the Coordination-of-Benefits Waterfall

Whether a procedure bills to the medical or the dental plan is the practice's most consequential billing decision, and the `BenefitsSpecialist` resolves it per case. A determination is opened against a `case_id` with `service_lines`, an ordered array of one to eight entries, each carrying a `code`, a `charge_cents` amount of at least zero, and a `benefit_class` of `MEDICAL` or `DENTAL`. The array's order is not cosmetic: it *is* the waterfall order of the allocation, and the system never re-sorts it. An empty array or one longer than eight entries is a structural failure answering `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). The lines must also sum, in `charge_cents`, to exactly the parent case's `total_charge_cents`; a mismatch is refused with `ERR_CHARGE_TOTAL_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422), and that rule is enforced both at creation and at every submission.

Opening a determination stamps `status` `DRAFT` and `submission_count` 0, sets `dental_annual_remaining_snapshot_cents` from the patient's current `dental_annual_remaining_cents`, and immediately computes `route`, `primary_payer`, and the full allocation, so the specialist can see the split before committing to it. `pend_reason` and `denial_reason` are both null at creation. Determinations may be read individually or listed with `case_id`, `route`, and `status` filters.

Routing reads only the patient's two coverage flags. When both `medical_plan_active` and `dental_plan_active` are true the `route` is `SPLIT`; when only `medical_plan_active` is true it is `MEDICAL_PRIMARY`; when only `dental_plan_active` is true it is `DENTAL_PRIMARY`; and when neither is true it is `SELF_PAY`. `primary_payer` then follows from the route, and only on the split route does the procedure matter: on `SPLIT` the `primary_payer` is `MEDICAL` when the case's `procedure_category` is `PATHOLOGY`, `TRAUMA`, `TMJ`, or `ORTHOGNATHIC`, and `DENTAL` otherwise — which in practice means `THIRD_MOLAR` and `IMPLANT` cases are dental-first. On `MEDICAL_PRIMARY` the `primary_payer` is `MEDICAL`, on `DENTAL_PRIMARY` it is `DENTAL`, and on `SELF_PAY` it is null.

The allocation is a per-line waterfall against two coinsurance rates: the medical plan allows eighty percent and the dental plan fifty percent of whatever charge each is presented with. Every coinsurance product is rounded half-up to whole cents **per service line, before any summation**; summing first and rounding once at the end is wrong and can differ by a cent. The dental side is additionally bounded by a running remainder that begins at `dental_annual_remaining_snapshot_cents` and is drawn down line by line in the client's supplied order.

On the `SELF_PAY` route both `medical_allowed_cents` and `dental_allowed_cents` are 0 and the patient owes everything. When `primary_payer` is `MEDICAL`, medical pays first on each line's full `charge_cents`: `medical_allowed_cents` is the sum, across lines, of eighty percent of the line charge rounded to whole cents. The dental residual pass then runs **only** when the `route` is `SPLIT`; on `MEDICAL_PRIMARY` the dental plan is inactive, so `dental_allowed_cents` stays 0 no matter what the snapshot holds. In the split case, for each line in order, the residual is the line's `charge_cents` less the rounded medical allowance for that same line, the dental want is fifty percent of that residual rounded to whole cents, the amount actually paid is the smaller of that want and the remaining dental balance, the remaining balance is reduced by the amount paid, and the amount paid is added to `dental_allowed_cents`.

When `primary_payer` is `DENTAL` — which covers both the `DENTAL_PRIMARY` route and a dental-first `SPLIT` — the order reverses, and this is what makes the two variants produce genuinely different numbers from identical service lines. Dental pays first on each line's full `charge_cents`: for each line in order, the want is fifty percent of the line charge rounded to whole cents, the amount paid is the smaller of that want and the remaining dental balance, the remaining balance is reduced by it, and it accumulates into `dental_allowed_cents` while also being remembered per line. Because dental is applied to the gross charge here, it is dental that consumes the annual maximum. Medical then sees only the post-dental residual, and again only when the `route` is `SPLIT`: `medical_allowed_cents` is the sum, across lines, of eighty percent — rounded to whole cents per line — of the line's `charge_cents` less the dental amount actually paid for that same line. On the pure `DENTAL_PRIMARY` route `medical_allowed_cents` stays 0.

`patient_responsibility_cents` is then the case's `total_charge_cents` less `medical_allowed_cents` less `dental_allowed_cents`. The resulting identity — medical allowance plus dental allowance plus patient responsibility equals the case's `total_charge_cents` — is conserved from creation onward in every status, which is precisely why the service lines are forced to sum to the case total in the first place: it holds even for a `DRAFT` determination that has never been submitted.

Submission is a revision loop. A determination may be submitted from `DRAFT` or resubmitted from `PENDED`, and either way the case must already carry a pre-anesthesia evaluation whose `status` is `VERIFIED` — a claim cannot precede documented clearance — or the answer is `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` (HTTP 409). The service-line total is re-checked against the case's `total_charge_cents`. Submission then moves `status` to `SUBMITTED`, increases `submission_count` by 1, clears `pend_reason` to null, refreshes `dental_annual_remaining_snapshot_cents` from the patient's current `dental_annual_remaining_cents`, and recomputes the route, the payer, and the whole allocation against that fresh snapshot. The snapshot matters: the allocation is always computed against the frozen figure, never against the live patient field, which may already have been drawn down by another determination that was approved in the meantime. After every recompute the conserved money identity holds again.

The payer's answer arrives as an `outcome` of `APPROVED`, `DENIED`, or `PENDED`, and is accepted only while the determination is `SUBMITTED`. A `PENDED` outcome must carry a `pend_reason` of `MISSING_OP_REPORT`, `MEDICAL_NECESSITY_REVIEW`, or `COORDINATION_OF_BENEFITS`; it is persisted, the allowances and `patient_responsibility_cents` are frozen exactly as computed at submission, and the determination returns to `PENDED` for another round — it may bounce between `SUBMITTED` and `PENDED` any number of times. An `APPROVED` outcome is terminal and settles the annual maximum: the patient's `dental_annual_remaining_cents` is reduced by `dental_allowed_cents`, never below 0, and the patient's `version` increments. A `DENIED` outcome must carry a `denial_reason` of `NOT_COVERED_BENEFIT`, `EXCEEDS_ANNUAL_MAX`, or `NO_MEDICAL_NECESSITY`; it is also terminal, and it zeroes both `medical_allowed_cents` and `dental_allowed_cents` while setting `patient_responsibility_cents` to the case's full `total_charge_cents`, so the money identity survives the denial. On a denial the patient's `dental_annual_remaining_cents` is **not** decremented — nothing was allowed, so nothing was consumed.

### 3.9. Adverse Events, Morbidity Review, and the Regulator Track

An adverse event may be filed by a `QualityReviewer`, an `AnesthesiaCoordinator`, or a `Surgeon` against the `case_id` during which it occurred. The report carries an `event_type` drawn from `AIRWAY_OBSTRUCTION`, `HYPOXEMIA`, `PROLONGED_RECOVERY`, `UNPLANNED_INTUBATION`, `NERVE_INJURY`, and `DEATH`; a `severity` of `MINOR`, `MODERATE`, `SEVERE`, or `SENTINEL`; the `occurred_at` instant, which may not be later than the logical clock; and a free-text `narrative` describing what happened. Filing stamps `status` `REPORTED`, sets `regulator_notified` to false, records `reported_at` as the logical clock, and leaves `reviewer_staff_id` and `corrective_action_required` null.

Reporting deadlines scale inversely with severity and are anchored to when the event happened, not to when it was noticed. `reporting_deadline_at` is `occurred_at` plus 24 hours for a `SENTINEL` event, plus 72 hours for a `SEVERE` one, plus 7 days for a `MODERATE` one, and plus 30 days for a `MINOR` one. `late_report` is then true exactly when `reported_at` falls after `reporting_deadline_at` — a permanent, computed mark of a missed obligation that no operator can clear. Filing also increments the practice's `open_adverse_event_count` by 1 and increments the practice's `version`. Events may be read individually or listed with `case_id`, `reviewer_staff_id`, `severity`, and `status` filters.

A `SENTINEL` event does more than open a file: it enforces against the permit immediately, and how hard depends on where the permit stood. When the practice's permit is `ACTIVE`, the event drives it to `PROBATION` with `last_enforcement_reason` `SENTINEL_EVENT`, increases `probation_count` by 1, sets `remediation_deadline_at` to the logical clock plus 30 days, and increments the permit's `version`. The cascade then runs in two visible hops: the practice's `sedation_privilege_state` becomes `RESTRICTED`, and because the practice is restricted, every case of that practice whose `planned_anesthesia_level` is `DEEP_SEDATION_GENERAL` and whose `clearance_status` is `CLEARED` moves to `clearance_status` `DEFERRED`. Those cases' `VERIFIED` evaluations keep their own `status` of `VERIFIED` — the clinical work stands — but the cases can no longer be confirmed, and the confirmation attempt now fails on the clearance gate with `ERR_CASE_NOT_CLEARED` rather than on any privilege check. When the permit is already on `PROBATION`, a `SENTINEL` event escalates it to `SUSPENDED` with `last_enforcement_reason` `SENTINEL_EVENT` and runs the full five-hop suspension cascade of §2.10 across privilege, confirmed sedated cases, their held reservations, those reservations' lots, and the practice's consignment exposure.

Review and notification then proceed on two independent tracks that meet only at closure. On the review track, a `QualityReviewer` opens the morbidity and mortality review with an `action` of `OPEN`, naming the `reviewer_staff_id` who will conduct it. The event must be in `REPORTED`, and the nominated reviewer must be employed by the same practice as the event's case and must not be that case's `primary_surgeon_id` — a surgeon may not review their own morbidity, and an attempt is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Opening moves `status` from `REPORTED` to `UNDER_MORBIDITY_REVIEW` and persists the `reviewer_staff_id` on the event. The same action with an `action` of `SIGN_OFF` closes the review out: the event must be in `UNDER_MORBIDITY_REVIEW`, the acting `X-User-ID` must equal the event's recorded `reviewer_staff_id` (otherwise `ERR_ACTOR_NOT_ASSIGNED`), and the supplied `corrective_action_required` boolean is persisted as the review's finding. `status` moves from `UNDER_MORBIDITY_REVIEW` to `REVIEW_SIGNED_OFF`.

On the notification track, either a `QualityReviewer` or a `PracticeAdministrator` records the notification sent to the state dental board, supplying the `notified_at` instant and the board's `notification_reference`. This is admitted while the event's `status` is `REPORTED`, `UNDER_MORBIDITY_REVIEW`, or `REVIEW_SIGNED_OFF`, and only while `regulator_notified` is still false — it is a one-shot flag, and a second attempt is an `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Recording it sets `regulator_notified` to true and increments the event's `version`, and it deliberately does **not** advance `status`. That independence is the point: notifying the board is a legal obligation that runs on its own clock, unrelated to how far the internal review has progressed.

Closure is the join point of the two tracks, and only a `QualityReviewer` may perform it. The event must be in `REVIEW_SIGNED_OFF`, and the join condition is that an event whose `severity` is `SEVERE` or `SENTINEL` must already have `regulator_notified` true; a signed-off severe event with no recorded notification is refused with `ERR_REGULATOR_NOTIFICATION_REQUIRED` (HTTP 409), while `MINOR` and `MODERATE` events may close with the flag still false. Closure moves `status` to the terminal `CLOSED`, recomputes the practice's `open_adverse_event_count` so this event drops out of it, and increments the practice's `version`.

### 3.10. Radiographic Protocol, Retention, and the Periodic Compliance Sweep

Imaging is registered by a `Surgeon`, an `AnesthesiaCoordinator`, or a `PracticeAdministrator` against a `patient_id`, optionally naming the `case_id` the study was taken for — a purely diagnostic study leaves `case_id` null, which is why a study attaches always to a patient and only sometimes to a case. The study records its `modality`, one of `PANORAMIC`, `CEPHALOMETRIC`, `CBCT_SMALL_FOV`, and `CBCT_LARGE_FOV`; its `justification_code`, one of `IMPLANT_PLANNING`, `THIRD_MOLAR_ASSESSMENT`, `PATHOLOGY_STAGING`, `TRAUMA_SURVEY`, and `ORTHOGNATHIC_PLANNING`; the `acquired_at` instant, which may not be later than the logical clock; and `dose_mgy_cm2`, the dose-area product the unit reported, which must be greater than zero. Studies are immutable, carry no `version`, and may be read individually or listed with `patient_id`, `case_id`, `modality`, and `dose_flag` filters.

Cone-beam imaging is dose-intensive and is therefore restricted to justifications that genuinely need volumetric data. A `CBCT_LARGE_FOV` study accepts only `ORTHOGNATHIC_PLANNING`, `PATHOLOGY_STAGING`, or `TRAUMA_SURVEY` — planning a single implant does not warrant a large field of view. A `CBCT_SMALL_FOV` study accepts `IMPLANT_PLANNING`, `THIRD_MOLAR_ASSESSMENT`, `PATHOLOGY_STAGING`, or `TRAUMA_SURVEY`, but not `ORTHOGNATHIC_PLANNING`, whose skeletal analysis needs the larger volume. `PANORAMIC` and `CEPHALOMETRIC` studies accept any of the five codes. A mismatched pairing is rejected with `ERR_CBCT_JUSTIFICATION_INVALID` (HTTP 422).

Dose is judged against a per-modality reference level: 100.0 for `PANORAMIC`, 60.0 for `CEPHALOMETRIC`, 250.0 for `CBCT_SMALL_FOV`, and 900.0 for `CBCT_LARGE_FOV`, all in the same units as `dose_mgy_cm2`. `dose_flag` is `ABOVE_REFERENCE_LEVEL` when the recorded dose is strictly greater than its modality's reference level, and `WITHIN_PROTOCOL` otherwise; because the comparison is strict, a dose exactly at the reference level is `WITHIN_PROTOCOL`. Exceeding the reference level does not block the study — the record exists precisely so that protocol drift is visible.

Retention is computed once at registration and never recomputed, which is why the logical clock is irrelevant to it: the obligation is anchored to acquisition, not to the moment of upload. First determine the patient's age in whole years from `date_of_birth` to `acquired_at`. The acquisition floor is `acquired_at` plus seven years. For a patient who was already 18 or older at acquisition, `retention_until` is that acquisition floor. For a patient who was under 18 at acquisition, compute a second date — the patient's eighteenth birthday, being `date_of_birth` plus eighteen years, plus a further seven years — and `retention_until` is whichever of the two dates is later. A study taken on a 4-year-old must therefore be kept until that child is 25, far beyond the seven-year floor, while a study taken on a 17-year-old is kept until age 25 as well because the majority-based date still dominates. All of this year arithmetic is calendar arithmetic: the same month and day, clamped to the last day of the month where the source date is 29 February.

The compliance sweep is the only mechanism that enforces elapsed time in bulk, and only a `PracticeAdministrator` may run it, optionally narrowing it to a single `practice_id`. Within each class of records the sweep processes rows in ascending `created_at` order so that the outcome never depends on identifiers. Permits are handled in two passes, and the order between them is significant: every permit is tested for expiry **first**, and only permits still `ACTIVE` after that pass are re-checked for drill compliance, so an expired permit is never also counted as newly on probation. In the expiry pass, any permit — whether `ACTIVE` or on `PROBATION` — whose `expires_at` is at or before the logical clock moves to the terminal `EXPIRED` with `last_enforcement_reason` `PERMIT_EXPIRY` and the practice's `sedation_privilege_state` recomputed to `UNPERMITTED`. In the drill pass, an `ACTIVE` permit whose `drill_compliance_ratio`, recomputed at sweep time, has fallen below 0.75 drops to `PROBATION` with `last_enforcement_reason` `DRILL_LAPSE`, `probation_count` increased by 1, `remediation_deadline_at` set to the logical clock plus 30 days, the privilege moved to `RESTRICTED`, and the practice's `CLEARED` `DEEP_SEDATION_GENERAL` cases deferred. Separately, a permit on `PROBATION` whose `remediation_deadline_at` has passed — the logical clock is strictly beyond it — is escalated to `SUSPENDED`, and its `last_enforcement_reason` is pinned to `DRILL_LAPSE` because the reason enumeration has no dedicated remediation-lapse value.

Each suspension or expiry the sweep performs runs the privilege cascade in its sweep form, in this order: the practice's `sedation_privilege_state` is recomputed first; then every non-terminal case of that practice whose `planned_anesthesia_level` is above `LOCAL_ONLY` is moved to `scheduling_status` `CANCELLED` with `cancellation_reason` `PRIVILEGE_SUSPENDED`; then those cases' pre-anesthesia evaluations in `DRAFT`, `SIGNED`, or `VERIFIED` move to `VOIDED`; then those cases' implant reservations still in `HELD` move to `RELEASED`, returning each lot's `quantity_reserved` and incrementing each lot's `version`; and finally the practice's `consignment_committed_cents` and `open_adverse_event_count` are recomputed. Because cancellation removes those cases from the committed roster, each affected block's `committed_minutes`, `open_minutes`, and `utilization_pct` are recomputed and its `version` incremented as part of the same step.

Block time is swept last. A block whose release deadline has arrived — meaning the logical clock is at or after `block_start` less `release_deadline_hours` hours — is released according to what is committed against it. When `committed_minutes` is zero the block moves to `RELEASED`, with `released_minutes` set to the full `block_duration_minutes` and `open_minutes` set to 0. When `committed_minutes` is greater than zero but `utilization_pct` is strictly below 60.00, the block moves to `PARTIALLY_RELEASED`, with the still-open minutes added to `released_minutes` and `open_minutes` set to 0; a block already at or above 60.00 percent utilisation is left alone, because it is being used well enough to keep. The sweep answers with `swept_at` recording the logical clock it ran at, the counts `permits_expired` and `permits_suspended` alongside the corresponding `permits_expired_ids` and `permits_suspended_ids` arrays, the counts `blocks_released` and `blocks_partially_released`, and finally `cases_cancelled` and `reservations_released` summarising the collateral effects of the cascades.
