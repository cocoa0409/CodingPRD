# Hyperscale Site Selection & Power Procurement API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Choosing where to build a hyperscale data center is no longer a real-estate decision; it is a power-procurement decision wearing a real-estate costume. A single campus commits hundreds of megawatts of firm load to one balancing authority, tens of millions of gallons of cooling water to one basin, a decade of property-tax abatement to one county board, and a construction craft workforce that a rural labor shed may simply not contain. Our siting team currently manages this in spreadsheets that disagree with each other: the interconnection queue tracker does not know that the land option under phase two lapsed last Thursday, the incentive model still assumes a capital investment figure the authority countered three rounds ago, and nobody can answer "is this program still on track to energize by the contractual date?" without a two-day reconciliation exercise.

This service replaces that reconciliation exercise with a single system of record for the whole funnel. It carries a siting program from an initial load target through candidate-site screening on power, fiber, hazard, labor and water; through the serving utility's load study, transmission reservation and energization commitment; through zoning hearings, community objections and mitigation escrow; through state and local incentive negotiation rounds to a ratified term sheet; through phased land assembly under option agreements; and through renewable procurement matched to the program's annual load. Every one of those tracks feeds a small number of derived figures — a screening score, a risk tier, a land-control percentage, a renewable coverage percentage, a capital commitment — and the system's central obligation is that those figures are never stale. When a basin escalates to a drought stage, the feasibility of every site drawing on it is re-tested inside the same request. When a utility revises its deliverable capacity downward, reservations that no longer fit are walked back into the queue and every affected program's status is re-derived before the response is written.

The design bias throughout is toward refusing an action rather than recording an optimistic one. Committing a program to a site is deliberately the most heavily gated operation in the system: ten independent conditions are evaluated in a fixed order, and the operator who screened the site is barred from being the operator who commits it. Money and megawatts are conserved quantities, not free text — a released transmission reservation restores the utility's available capacity exactly, and a forfeited land premium moves between ledger buckets without ever being created or destroyed. Engineers implementing this specification should read the derived fields as invariants to be maintained, not as columns to be written.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

Every resource collection in this API is mounted as a top-level path segment; there are no compound identity paths. Where a URL such as a site's zoning listing appears to nest one resource inside another, the nesting is a reverse-reachability read path only, and never the child's identity. The subsections below define the contract that all endpoints share.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Authentication is header-based. Every request — reads included — must carry both `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role`. If either header is absent, or present but empty or whitespace-only, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` before any other consideration. If `X-User-Role` is not one of the six role names below, or is a valid role name that the addressed endpoint does not list as authorized, the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.

The six roles are `SiteScreeningAnalyst`, `PortfolioDirector`, `UtilityPlanner`, `IncentiveNegotiator`, `CapitalCommitteeChair` and `PermittingAuthority`. A `SiteScreeningAnalyst` is the development analyst who assembles candidate sites, files screening packages, drafts and submits interconnection applications, files zoning applications and signs land options. A `PortfolioDirector` owns the program: opening it, registering serving utilities, procuring power purchase agreements, exercising land options, running the option-expiry sweep, disqualifying sites, and driving the logical clock. A `UtilityPlanner` acts on behalf of one serving utility, filing load studies, reserving transmission capacity, committing energization dates and revising deliverable capacity. An `IncentiveNegotiator` is the economic-development lead who opens incentive negotiations, files the company's rounds and signs the standing term sheet. A `CapitalCommitteeChair` ratifies signed term sheets and commits the program to a site. A `PermittingAuthority` represents the state and local side: it registers water sources, declares drought stages, records community objections, decides zoning dockets, and files the authority's counter-offer rounds.

Five of the six roles carry an opaque identity. When `X-User-Role` is `SiteScreeningAnalyst`, `PortfolioDirector`, `IncentiveNegotiator`, `CapitalCommitteeChair` or `PermittingAuthority`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity. The server records it wherever a transition stamps an actor — as `screened_by_user_id` and `committed_by_user_id` on a candidate site, and as `signed_by_user_id` and `ratified_by_user_id` on an incentive negotiation — but it never compares such an identifier to any entity's `id`. Because these identifiers are opaque, one operator may legitimately present under more than one of these roles across requests; that is precisely why the segregation-of-duties comparisons in §2.9 are written against the stamped identifier and not against the role.

`UtilityPlanner` is the one entity-backed role. When `X-User-Role` is `UtilityPlanner`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Utility`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `Utility` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. Concretely, for a capacity revision the utility named in the path must be the caller's own utility, and for a load study, a capacity reservation or an energization commitment the addressed application's `utility_id` must equal `X-User-ID`. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_UTILITY_IDENTITY_MISMATCH`. When a `UtilityPlanner` files a load study, that same identifier is stamped onto the study row as `filed_by_user_id`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the uppercase letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits, read from the logical clock of §2.6 rather than from the host's wall clock, so it repeats the same instant until the clock is moved.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10 when omitted, may be no smaller than 1 and no larger than 100. `offset` defaults to 0 when omitted and may be no smaller than 0; it has no upper bound. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is a request-shape failure and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, as is either parameter carrying a value that is not parseable as an integer; a parameter present but empty is treated as omitted and takes its default.

List results are ordered by `created_at` ascending unless a subsection states otherwise. Where two rows carry the same `created_at`, the row that was inserted first sorts first, so ordering is total and repeatable. The `meta` object of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more`: `total_count` counts the rows matching the request's filters before the page window is applied, and `has_more` is true exactly when rows remain beyond the current window. Every documented list filter is matched by exact string equality against the stored field; a filter value is never checked against the field's declared value set, so an unrecognized value simply matches no rows, and a filter supplied empty is ignored rather than applied.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Timestamps and Record Versioning

Every resource is identified by an `id` that the server mints as a UUID. Identifiers carry no type prefix and no embedded semantics; clients must treat them as opaque. Two timestamp fields accompany most resources: `created_at`, set once when the row is inserted, and `updated_at`, refreshed on every mutation of that row. Both are ISO 8601 UTC strings with exactly three fractional-second digits. Every other instant the server itself stamps onto a row — an interconnection application's `submitted_at`, a land parcel option's `exercised_at` and its `lapsed_at` — is rendered in exactly that same form.

Mutable resources also carry a `version` integer. It is 1 when the resource is created and increases by exactly 1 on each request that mutates the resource, including mutations that a cascade performs rather than the caller addressing the resource directly. Any request that changes a siting program's derived rollups, a utility's capacity ledger, a water source's drought stage, a candidate site's status or derived figures, an interconnection application, an incentive negotiation, a zoning application, a power purchase agreement or a land parcel option increments that record's `version` by one and refreshes its `updated_at`. A water source's supply and demand figures sit outside that trigger list: its `stage_curtailment_pct`, its `effective_supply_mgal` and its `committed_annual_demand_mgal` are kept current as their inputs move — a sibling site's screening or disqualification changes the basin's committed demand, for instance — yet a request that changes only those three figures neither increments the basin's `version` nor refreshes its `updated_at`, so a basin's record advances only when a drought declaration moves its stage.

Load study rows are the single exception. A study, once filed, is an immutable historical artifact: it carries only `id` and `created_at`. It has no `updated_at` and no `version` field at all, and neither field should appear in its representation.

### 2.5. Reverse-Reachability Reads and Path Resolution Order

Because the topology is flat, foreign keys travel in request bodies rather than in URLs, and each entity's parentage is discoverable through a dedicated read path: the power purchase agreements of a program, the incentive negotiations and the zoning applications of a candidate site, and the load study rounds of an interconnection application. The load-study listing deserves particular attention: because study rows have no top-level collection of their own, listing them under their application is the only discovery channel for them.

Path parameters are resolved strictly left to right, and each segment must resolve before the next segment is examined or any body field is inspected. Each collection has its own not-found error: a `siting-programs` segment that does not resolve yields `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`, a `utilities` segment yields `ERR_UTILITY_NOT_FOUND`, a `water-sources` segment yields `ERR_WATER_SOURCE_NOT_FOUND`, a `candidate-sites` segment yields `ERR_SITE_NOT_FOUND`, an `interconnection-applications` segment yields `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_FOUND`, an `incentive-negotiations` segment yields `ERR_NEGOTIATION_NOT_FOUND`, a `zoning-applications` segment yields `ERR_ZONING_APPLICATION_NOT_FOUND`, a `power-purchase-agreements` segment yields `ERR_PPA_NOT_FOUND`, and a `parcel-options` segment yields `ERR_PARCEL_OPTION_NOT_FOUND`.

Foreign keys supplied in a request body are resolved after all path parameters, in a fixed declaration order: `program_id` first, then `utility_id`, then `water_source_id`, then `site_id`, then `application_id`, then `replaces_option_id`. Each unresolved reference raises the not-found error belonging to the collection it points at, so a create carrying both an unknown `program_id` and an unknown `utility_id` reports `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`.

### 2.6. The Logical Clock and Interval Conventions

The server keeps a logical UTC clock, and every window test, expiry decision and system-stamped instant reads that clock rather than real wall-clock time. At startup the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it must continue to read that instant until it is explicitly moved; it must never fall back to the host's wall clock. A `PortfolioDirector` may reposition it by supplying a target instant as `to_iso8601`. Repositioning the clock mutates no entity and runs no sweep — in particular, land options whose windows have closed do not lapse as a side effect of moving the clock, because the expiry sweep is a separate, explicit operation. Because no resource is affected, the clock operation returns no resource in `data`.

Two interval conventions coexist and must not be conflated. An interconnection application's requested energization window, delimited by `window_start` and `window_end`, is a **closed** interval: both endpoints belong to the window. A land parcel option's exclusivity window, also delimited by `window_start` and `window_end`, is **half-open**: `window_start` is inclusive and `window_end` is an exclusive upper bound, so two options that abut — one ending exactly where the next begins — do not overlap. In all three interval-bearing resources the end must be strictly after the start: an interconnection application's `window_end` after its `window_start`, a power purchase agreement's `delivery_end` after its `delivery_start`, and a land option's `window_end` after its `window_start`. A violation is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`. A new land option additionally may not be backdated: its `window_start` may not fall earlier than the current logical clock, and an attempt to do so is rejected with `ERR_OPTION_WINDOW_BACKDATED`.

### 2.7. Megawatts, Money and Rounding Discipline

All rounding in this system is half-up: a value exactly at the midpoint rounds away from zero. Monetary amounts are carried and reported at 2 decimal places. Power and energy quantities — megawatts and megawatt-hours alike — are carried at 3 decimal places, and MW comparisons against a capacity ceiling or a load target are performed at that same 3-decimal precision. Percentages such as a program's renewable coverage, a site's land control and a zoning application's weighted opposition are reported at 2 decimal places. Two figures have their own precision: a site's `workforce_availability_ratio` is rounded to 4 decimal places, and a site's `screening_score` to 2.

Where a formula sums several intermediate terms, the rounding point matters and is specified with the formula. Two cases are load-bearing. The weighted components of a screening score are **not** rounded individually; they are summed at full precision and only the final score is rounded. Conversely, a property-tax abatement's present value rounds **each** annual figure to cents before the years are summed; summing unrounded annual figures and rounding once at the end is incorrect and can differ by cents.

### 2.8. Derived-Field Reconciliation

A large share of this system's fields are derived, and the contract is that no response ever exposes a derived field that lags the mutation that request performed. Derived fields are recomputed inside the request that invalidates them, in dependency order, and the response body reflects the post-cascade state.

Clients cannot write derived fields. A request body may name a computed or system-maintained field, or a field the endpoint does not recognize at all; in both cases the extra key is silently ignored rather than rejected. Request-shape validation therefore concerns itself only with the fields the endpoint declares: required fields must be present, types must match, enum-typed values must fall inside their declared value sets, and numeric ranges must be respected — a program's `target_load_mw` strictly above zero, each hazard index between 0.0 and 1.0 inclusive, a load study's `diversity_factor` strictly above 0 and no greater than 1, an abatement term between 1 and 30 whole years, every percentage expressed as a fraction between 0.0 and 1.0 inclusive, and every `state_code` exactly two uppercase letters. A declared field present with an explicit `null` is treated exactly as an omission, so a required field sent as `null` fails while an optional field sent as `null` takes its documented default. Count-like figures must be whole numbers and not merely non-negative — a site's `fiber_route_count`, `craft_labor_supply_index` and `construction_peak_headcount`, a negotiation's `abatement_years` and `jobs_commitment`, an option's `phase_index` and `expected_site_version`, and a utility's `commissioning_buffer_days` and `energization_lead_days` — and a JSON boolean is never accepted where a number or an integer is declared. Any failure here is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.

Two conserved quantities must hold after every single request, including after a withdrawal, an expiry sweep or a disqualification cascade. A utility's `reserved_capacity_mw` plus its `available_capacity_mw` always equals its `total_transmission_capacity_mw`, and `available_capacity_mw` is never negative. A program's `option_exposure_usd` plus its `forfeited_premium_usd` plus its `capitalized_premium_usd` always equals its `premium_committed_total_usd`. Releasing a reservation or forfeiting a premium moves value between buckets; it never creates or destroys value, and it never leaves residual drift.

### 2.9. Segregation of Duties

Two decisions in this system are structurally two-person decisions, and the system enforces that separation on the acting operator identifier rather than on the role, because an opaque operator may hold more than one role. First, the operator committing a candidate site must not be the operator who screened it: at commit time, `X-User-ID` is compared against the site's `screened_by_user_id`, and an equal comparison is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Second, the operator ratifying an incentive term sheet must not be the operator who signed it: at ratify time, `X-User-ID` is compared against the negotiation's `signed_by_user_id`, and an equal comparison is likewise rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Both rejections carry HTTP 403.

### 2.10. Optimistic Concurrency on Land Option Creation

Exactly one write in this system carries an optimistic-concurrency token. A request creating a land parcel option — `POST /parcel-options` — must include `expected_site_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the `CandidateSite` named by `site_id`. The value is not optional and has no default. If `expected_site_version` does not equal that site's current `version` integer, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_SITE_VERSION` and HTTP 412.

The reason this particular write needs the token is supersession. An option create that names `replaces_option_id` moves the prior option to `RELEASED` and rewrites the site's `land_control_pct` in the same request. A client holding an older view of the site would therefore release an option it never observed and re-state land control from a footprint it never saw. The version check makes the site the serialization point: any writer whose view of the site predates another writer's land-control mutation is turned away rather than allowed to overwrite it. The check runs after path and body references have been resolved and before any state-machine or exclusivity condition is evaluated. No other endpoint in this API accepts a version field, and none should be added. Requests are otherwise serialized end to end — each request, cascades and derived-field reconciliation included, runs to completion before the next begins — so no reader ever observes a half-applied cascade and two concurrent option creates against the same site are decided one after the other, the second seeing the version the first left behind.

### 2.11. Request Admission Order

When several conditions could reject the same request, the order of evaluation is fixed, because the error the client receives must be predictable. The full ladder, from first-evaluated to last, is as follows.

1. **Header presence** — `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` present and non-empty, on every endpoint; otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. **Role authorization** — `X-User-Role` is one of the six declared roles and is listed on the addressed endpoint, on every endpoint; otherwise `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`.
3. **Utility identity binding** — for the entity-backed `UtilityPlanner` role, `X-User-ID` equals the `Utility` `id` that owns the addressed resource. This applies to exactly four operations: `POST /utilities/{id}/capacity-revisions`, `POST /interconnection-applications/{id}/load-studies`, `POST /interconnection-applications/{id}/reserve-capacity` and `POST /interconnection-applications/{id}/commit-energization`. Otherwise `ERR_UTILITY_IDENTITY_MISMATCH`. For the capacity revision the comparison is made against the path segment itself and needs no persisted row, so a `UtilityPlanner` addressing an id that is not its own fails on identity even when no utility carries that id; for the three application-scoped writes the binding can only be tested once the application row is found, so an addressed application that does not exist answers `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_FOUND` rather than the identity error.
4. **Request shape** — required fields, types, enum membership, numeric ranges and pagination bounds as described in §2.8, on every endpoint; otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
5. **Interval ordering** — end strictly after start, on `POST /interconnection-applications`, `POST /power-purchase-agreements` and `POST /parcel-options`; otherwise `ERR_INVALID_WINDOW`.
6. **Option backdating** — a new option's `window_start` not earlier than the logical clock, on `POST /parcel-options`; otherwise `ERR_OPTION_WINDOW_BACKDATED`.
7. **Path existence** — every path id resolves, left to right, on every endpoint that has a path parameter, with the per-collection errors listed in §2.5.
8. **Body reference existence** — every foreign key in the body resolves, in declaration order, on `POST /candidate-sites`, `POST /interconnection-applications`, `POST /incentive-negotiations`, `POST /zoning-applications`, `POST /power-purchase-agreements` and `POST /parcel-options`.
9. **Site version match** — `expected_site_version` equals the addressed site's current `version`, on `POST /parcel-options`; otherwise `ERR_STALE_SITE_VERSION`.
10. **Candidate site state** — the site's `status` permits the operation, on `POST /candidate-sites/{id}/screen`, `POST /candidate-sites/{id}/commit`, `POST /candidate-sites/{id}/disqualify`, `POST /interconnection-applications`, `POST /zoning-applications`, `POST /incentive-negotiations` and `POST /parcel-options`; otherwise `ERR_SITE_INVALID_STATE`.
11. **Application state** — the application's `status` permits submit, load-study filing, energization commitment or withdrawal, on `POST /interconnection-applications/{id}/submit`, `POST /interconnection-applications/{id}/load-studies`, `POST /interconnection-applications/{id}/commit-energization` and `POST /interconnection-applications/{id}/withdraw`; otherwise `ERR_APPLICATION_INVALID_STATE`.
12. **Study completeness for reservation** — the application's `status` is exactly `STUDY_COMPLETE`, on `POST /interconnection-applications/{id}/reserve-capacity`; otherwise `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_STUDIED`.
13. **Negotiation state** — rounds, signature and ratification each require their permitted status, on `POST /incentive-negotiations/{id}/rounds`, `POST /incentive-negotiations/{id}/sign` and `POST /incentive-negotiations/{id}/ratify`; otherwise `ERR_NEGOTIATION_INVALID_STATE`.
14. **Negotiation turn** — the round's `submitted_by_party` is the party the status expects, on `POST /incentive-negotiations/{id}/rounds`; otherwise `ERR_WRONG_NEGOTIATION_PARTY`.
15. **Zoning state** — the docket is still open, on `POST /zoning-applications/{id}/decide` and `POST /zoning-applications/{id}/community-objections`; otherwise `ERR_ZONING_INVALID_STATE`.
16. **Continuance budget** — on `POST /zoning-applications/{id}/decide`; otherwise `ERR_ZONING_CONTINUANCE_EXHAUSTED`.
17. **Agreement state** — the agreement is still a draft, on `POST /power-purchase-agreements/{id}/execute`; otherwise `ERR_PPA_INVALID_STATE`.
18. **Option state** — the option being exercised, or the option named by `replaces_option_id`, is `OPTIONED`, on `POST /parcel-options/{id}/exercise` and `POST /parcel-options`; otherwise `ERR_OPTION_INVALID_STATE`.
19. **Option window still open** — on `POST /parcel-options/{id}/exercise`; otherwise `ERR_OPTION_EXPIRED`.
20. **Cross-entity state** — a submit requires its site to be `SCREENED` and an exercise requires the site's live application to hold capacity, on `POST /interconnection-applications/{id}/submit` and `POST /parcel-options/{id}/exercise`; otherwise `ERR_SITE_NOT_SCREENED`.
21. **Segregation of duties** — on `POST /candidate-sites/{id}/commit` and `POST /incentive-negotiations/{id}/ratify`; otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.
22. **Hazard prohibition** — on `POST /candidate-sites/{id}/commit`; otherwise `ERR_HAZARD_TIER_PROHIBITED`.
23. **Zoning approval prerequisite** — on both `POST /candidate-sites/{id}/commit` and `POST /incentive-negotiations/{id}/ratify`; otherwise `ERR_ZONING_NOT_APPROVED`.
24. **Mitigation sufficiency** — on `POST /zoning-applications/{id}/decide`; otherwise `ERR_MITIGATION_TIER_INSUFFICIENT`.
25. **Referential agreement** — an application's `utility_id` matches its site's, and a superseding option matches its predecessor's site and parcel, on `POST /interconnection-applications` and `POST /parcel-options`; otherwise `ERR_APPLICATION_SITE_UTILITY_MISMATCH`.
26. **Application uniqueness** — on `POST /interconnection-applications`; otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_APPLICATION`.
27. **Zoning uniqueness** — on `POST /zoning-applications`; otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_ZONING_APPLICATION`.
28. **Negotiation uniqueness per authority tier** — on `POST /incentive-negotiations`; otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_NEGOTIATION`.
29. **Option exclusivity** — on `POST /parcel-options`; otherwise `ERR_OPTION_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.
30. **Restudy tolerance** — on `POST /interconnection-applications/{id}/reserve-capacity`; otherwise `ERR_STUDY_LOAD_MISMATCH`.
31. **Single committed site per program** — on `POST /candidate-sites/{id}/commit`; otherwise `ERR_SITE_COMMITMENT_EXISTS`.
32. **Complete land control** — on `POST /candidate-sites/{id}/commit`; otherwise `ERR_LAND_CONTROL_INCOMPLETE`.
33. **Ratified state incentives** — on `POST /candidate-sites/{id}/commit`; otherwise `ERR_INCENTIVES_NOT_RATIFIED`.
34. **Aggregate transmission capacity** — on `POST /interconnection-applications/{id}/reserve-capacity`; otherwise `ERR_TRANSMISSION_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`.
35. **Water sufficiency** — on `POST /candidate-sites/{id}/commit`; otherwise `ERR_WATER_SUPPLY_INSUFFICIENT`.
36. **Renewable coverage floor** — on `POST /candidate-sites/{id}/commit`; otherwise `ERR_RENEWABLE_COVERAGE_INSUFFICIENT`.
37. **Energization deadline** — on `POST /interconnection-applications/{id}/commit-energization`; otherwise `ERR_ENERGIZATION_DEADLINE_MISSED`.
38. **Capital ceiling** — on `POST /candidate-sites/{id}/commit`; otherwise `ERR_CAPITAL_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`. The fiscal ceiling is deliberately the last gate of all.

Two consequences of this ordering are worth stating outright. On a site commitment, the ten domain gates fire in the order segregation of duties, hazard prohibition, zoning approval, single-committed-site, land control, ratified state incentives, water sufficiency, renewable coverage, capital ceiling — after the site's own state has been checked — so a caller who is both the screener and short of land control learns about the duty conflict first. On a capacity reservation, the restudy-tolerance check precedes the aggregate-transmission check, so an application whose study drifted too far from its request is refused for the drift even when the utility also lacks the headroom.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Establishing a Program and its Physical Environment

A `PortfolioDirector` opens the funnel by creating a **siting program**, supplying a program label as `name`, the contracted IT load the program must eventually energize as `target_load_mw` in megawatts, the contractual date by which the chosen site must be energized as `required_energization_date`, and the capital ceiling for the whole endeavour as `capital_budget_usd`. The load target must be strictly greater than zero and the capital ceiling must be zero or greater. From the load target the system immediately derives `program_annual_load_mwh`, the denominator against which all renewable procurement is later measured: the target load is multiplied by the 8,760 hours in a year and then by a program load factor of 0.90, and the result is rounded to 3 decimal places. A fresh program reports `program_status` of `SCREENING`, and every rollup starts at zero — `committed_load_mw`, `renewable_coverage_pct`, `option_exposure_usd`, `forfeited_premium_usd`, `capitalized_premium_usd`, `premium_committed_total_usd` and `full_land_control_site_count` alike. Reading the program back is the canonical way to inspect both the compound status and the land-premium ledger.

Registering a **serving utility** is likewise the director's job. The registration carries the utility's `name`, the two-letter `state_code` of its service territory, the `balancing_authority` code it settles into, its `total_transmission_capacity_mw` at the point of interconnection, an `interconnect_study_fee_per_mw_usd` rate charged per requested megawatt when an application is submitted, a `network_upgrade_rate_usd_per_mw` rate applied to diversified peak load when a study finds an upgrade necessary, and an `energization_lead_days` figure stating the minimum number of days that must separate a capacity reservation from the energization date the utility will commit to. Two fields are optional. `commissioning_buffer_days` defaults to 90 when omitted and expresses how much commissioning slack the utility adds to **both** ends of **every** energization window when testing whether two reservations collide. `queue_policy` defaults to `FIRST_COME_FIRST_SERVED` when omitted and may otherwise be `FIRST_READY_FIRST_SERVED`. Both rate fields, the buffer and the lead-day figure must be zero or greater, the buffer and the lead-day figure expressed as whole numbers of days; the transmission capacity must be strictly positive. A newly registered utility reports `reserved_capacity_mw` of 0 and an `available_capacity_mw` equal to its total transmission capacity.

Cooling water enters through the `PermittingAuthority`, which registers a **water source** with its `name`, its regulator `basin_code`, the two-letter `state_code` of the permitting authority, and the `annual_permitted_mgal` withdrawal allowance in million gallons before any drought curtailment applies, which must be strictly positive. At registration the basin sits at `drought_stage` `NORMAL` with a `stage_curtailment_pct` of 0.00, so its `effective_supply_mgal` equals its permitted withdrawal exactly, its `committed_annual_demand_mgal` is 0, and its `last_declaration_effective_date` is null because no declaration has yet been issued.

### 3.2. Candidate Site Intake and the Screening Package

A `SiteScreeningAnalyst` adds a **candidate site** to a program by naming the program as `program_id`, the serving utility whose transmission capacity the campus will draw on as `utility_id`, the basin supplying cooling water as `water_source_id`, a site label as `name`, its two-letter `state_code`, its `county` or local jurisdiction, and the `gross_acres` the campus needs, which must be strictly positive and which later serves as the denominator of land control. A new site enters at `status` `DRAFT` with `land_control_pct` of 0 and `land_control_status` of `NONE`; its `energization_risk_tier` is derived immediately from the risk rules in §3.5, while `screening_score`, `hazard_tier`, `workforce_availability_ratio`, `water_headroom_mgal`, `screened_by_user_id` and the ten screening measurements including `transmission_headroom_mw` stay null until the site is screened, its `site_capital_commitment_usd` starts at 0.00, and its `water_feasible` flag reads false until screening establishes a demand to test. Its `disqualification_reason` is likewise null unless and until the site is dropped. Sites can be listed and filtered by `program_id`, `utility_id`, `water_source_id` or `status`, which is how the analyst reaches every site attached to a program, a utility or a basin.

Screening is a single operation that advances a site from `DRAFT` to `SCREENED` and files ten measurements at once: the `transmission_headroom_mw` the developer measured at the nearest substation, the `fiber_route_count` of physically diverse long-haul routes reaching the parcel, three normalized hazard exposures as `seismic_hazard_index`, `flood_hazard_index` and `wind_hazard_index`, the `craft_labor_supply_index` giving available construction craft headcount within the labor shed, the `construction_peak_headcount` the build requires at its peak, the `wage_premium_pct` over the local prevailing craft wage needed to staff the build, the `cooling_type` design, and the `annual_water_demand_mgal` of cooling water. The hazard indices are fractions between 0.0 and 1.0 inclusive; the headroom, the route count and the labor supply must be zero or greater; the peak headcount must be strictly greater than zero; the wage premium must be zero or greater. The cooling design is one of `EVAPORATIVE`, `AIR_COOLED` or `HYBRID`, and an `AIR_COOLED` campus must declare an annual water demand of exactly 0 — a non-zero demand paired with an air-cooled design is a contradiction the screening operation refuses with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`, and because that contradiction belongs to request-shape validation it is tested before the site named in the path has been resolved. Screening a site that is not in `DRAFT` is rejected with `ERR_SITE_INVALID_STATE`. On success the acting operator's identifier is stamped as `screened_by_user_id`, which becomes the comparand for the segregation-of-duties rule at commitment time.

The screening operation derives five figures on the site. First, `hazard_tier` is set from the largest of the three hazard indices: `SEVERE` when that largest value is at least 0.70, `MODERATE` when it is at least 0.40 but below 0.70, and `LOW` otherwise. Second, `workforce_availability_ratio` is the craft labor supply divided by the peak construction headcount, rounded to 4 decimal places. Third and fourth, the site's `water_headroom_mgal` and its boolean `water_feasible` flag are computed as described in §3.3. Fifth, the `screening_score` is assembled as a weighted sum out of a maximum of 100 points, and it is worth walking through slowly because it is the figure the utility's readiness queue can later rank on.

The score has five positive components and one penalty. The **power** component is worth up to 30 points: it is 30 multiplied by the transmission headroom divided by the program's target load, with that ratio capped at 1.0 so that headroom beyond the program's need earns nothing extra. The **fiber** component is worth up to 15 points: 15 multiplied by the site's diverse route count divided by a reference of 3 routes, again capped at 1.0. The **water** component is worth up to 15 points, and it has two readings. A site whose annual water demand is exactly 0 — an air-cooled campus — earns the full 15 points outright. Any other site earns 15 multiplied by its water headroom divided by its annual water demand, where that ratio is first capped at 1.0 above and floored at 0.0 below, so a negative headroom scores zero rather than a negative number. The **workforce** component is worth up to 25 points: 25 multiplied by the workforce availability ratio capped at 1.0, and then — only when the wage premium is strictly greater than 0.15, that is strictly more than a fifteen percent premium over the prevailing craft wage — that component is multiplied by a wage penalty factor of 0.8. The **land** component is worth up to 15 points: 15 multiplied by the gross acreage divided by a reference campus footprint of 200 acres, capped at 1.0. The **hazard penalty** is then subtracted, and it depends on the tier just derived: 0 points for `LOW`, 6 points for `MODERATE` and 15 points for `SEVERE`. None of the five components is rounded before the sum is taken. The sum minus the penalty is finally clamped into the range 0.0 to 100.0 and rounded to 2 decimal places to give the score.

One property of the screening score is deliberate and easy to get wrong: it is a point-in-time artifact. It is computed at screening and never re-derived afterwards. When a later drought declaration or a sibling site's screening changes this site's water headroom, the `water_headroom_mgal` and `water_feasible` fields **are** brought up to date, but the `screening_score` and the `hazard_tier` frozen at screening time stay exactly as they were.

Screening also reaches outward. The serving basin's `committed_annual_demand_mgal` is recomputed to include the newly screened demand, and then every **other** site on that same basin whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `SCREENED` or `COMMITTED` has its `water_headroom_mgal` and `water_feasible` re-evaluated, because the new site has just started consuming shared headroom — a `DRAFT` sibling therefore acquires a headroom figure of its own even though it still contributes no demand, and its `water_feasible` flag stays false while its own demand is unknown. Finally the owning program's `program_status` is re-derived and the site's `version` is incremented by one.

### 3.3. Cooling Water: Basin Supply, Drought Stages and Feasibility

Water availability in this system is a shared, curtailable pool rather than a per-site allowance. A basin's `effective_supply_mgal` is its annual permitted withdrawal multiplied by one minus its `stage_curtailment_pct`, rounded to 3 decimal places. The curtailment fraction is a direct lookup on the current drought stage: `NORMAL` curtails nothing at 0.00, `STAGE_1` withholds a tenth of the permit at 0.10, `STAGE_2` withholds nearly a third at 0.30, and `STAGE_3` withholds more than half at 0.55.

A site's headroom is what the basin can still deliver after its *siblings* have taken their share. For a given site, the demand of every **other** site on the same basin whose `status` is `SCREENED` or `COMMITTED` is summed, and that total is subtracted from the basin's effective supply, with the result rounded to 3 decimal places, to give the site's `water_headroom_mgal`. Sites in `DRAFT` and sites that have been `DISQUALIFIED` consume no headroom at all and never appear in that sum. The site's `water_feasible` flag is then true exactly when its own `annual_water_demand_mgal` is less than or equal to that headroom — equality is feasible, not marginal. The basin's own `committed_annual_demand_mgal` is the parallel figure computed over **all** its `SCREENED` and `COMMITTED` sites including the one under examination, rounded to 3 decimal places.

A `PermittingAuthority` moves the basin between stages by issuing a **drought declaration**, supplying the new `drought_stage` and the `effective_date` from which it applies. Stages are not a ratchet: a declaration may advance the basin, relax it, or skip stages in either direction, so `NORMAL` to `STAGE_1`, `STAGE_1` to `STAGE_2`, `NORMAL` straight to `STAGE_2`, `STAGE_2` to `STAGE_3`, `STAGE_3` back to `STAGE_1` and `STAGE_2` back to `NORMAL` are all legal single declarations, and a basin may cycle from `NORMAL` up to `STAGE_2` and back to `NORMAL` over its lifetime. Two conditions govern every declaration regardless of direction: the requested stage must differ from the stage the basin currently occupies, and where a prior declaration exists the new `effective_date` may not fall earlier than the recorded `last_declaration_effective_date`. A declaration failing either condition is not a legal declaration and is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`; both conditions are nevertheless evaluated only after the basin named in the path has resolved, so an unknown basin reports `ERR_WATER_SOURCE_NOT_FOUND` ahead of either.

A successful declaration is one of the widest cascades in the system. The basin's stage is set and its `stage_curtailment_pct` looked up, its `effective_supply_mgal` is recomputed against the new curtailment, and `last_declaration_effective_date` records the supplied effective date. Then, for every candidate site drawing on that basin whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `SCREENED` or `COMMITTED`, the site's `water_headroom_mgal` is recomputed and only afterwards its `water_feasible` flag re-evaluated — the order matters, since feasibility reads the freshly computed headroom. For any site whose feasibility flipped, the owning program's `program_status` is re-derived: a tightening declaration that renders a `COMMITTED` site infeasible drives that program to `STALLED`, and a relaxing declaration that restores feasibility can bring a program back from `STALLED` to `SITE_COMMITTED` once the committed site is whole again. The basin's own `version` is incremented by one.

### 3.4. Entering the Interconnection Queue and Filing Load Studies

An **interconnection application** is drafted by a `SiteScreeningAnalyst`, who names the site whose load it serves as `site_id`, the serving utility as `utility_id`, the nameplate `requested_mw` to be drawn from the queue, and the requested energization window as `window_start` and `window_end`. The requested megawatts must be strictly positive. The named utility must be the same utility the site itself is bound to; an application naming a different utility from its site's is rejected with `ERR_APPLICATION_SITE_UTILITY_MISMATCH`. A site may hold at most one application that is not `WITHDRAWN` — a second live application is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_APPLICATION` — and the site itself must be in `DRAFT`, `SCREENED` or `COMMITTED`, never `DISQUALIFIED`. A drafted application sits at `status` `DRAFT` with `queue_position` of 0, `reserved_mw` of 0, `study_round` of 0, `study_fee_usd` of 0, `network_upgrade_cost_usd` of 0 and `delay_exposure_usd` of 0: nothing is reserved and no fee is charged until it is submitted. Its `submitted_at` is null until it is submitted, its `diversified_peak_mw` is null until the serving utility files the first load study, its `committed_energization_date` is null until the utility commits one, and its `withdrawal_reason` is null unless and until it is withdrawn. An application's `reserved_mw` mirrors its `requested_mw` exactly while its `status` is `CAPACITY_RESERVED` or `ENERGIZATION_COMMITTED`, and is 0 in every other status.

Submission advances the application from `DRAFT` to `SUBMITTED`, and it is the point at which the site's screening becomes load-bearing: the referenced site must be in `SCREENED` status, and submitting on behalf of an unscreened site is rejected with `ERR_SITE_NOT_SCREENED`, while submitting an application that is not itself in `DRAFT` is rejected with `ERR_APPLICATION_INVALID_STATE`. On submission the current logical clock is stamped as `submitted_at`, which becomes the primary queue sort key, and the study fee is struck: `study_fee_usd` is the requested megawatts multiplied by the utility's study fee rate per megawatt, rounded to 2 decimal places. The queue is then re-ranked for the whole utility, the site's `energization_risk_tier` is recomputed and its `site_capital_commitment_usd` re-aggregated, the program's status is re-derived, and the application's `version` is incremented by one.

Queue ranking covers every application of the utility whose `status` is `SUBMITTED`, `STUDY_COMPLETE`, `CAPACITY_RESERVED` or `ENERGIZATION_COMMITTED`. Under a `FIRST_COME_FIRST_SERVED` policy those applications are ordered by `submitted_at` ascending, with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break. Under a `FIRST_READY_FIRST_SERVED` policy the utility rewards site maturity instead: applications are ordered by their site's `screening_score` descending first, then by `submitted_at` ascending, then by `created_at` ascending, and an application whose site has not been screened and therefore carries no score sorts behind every scored site. Under neither policy is the row identifier ever used as a tie-break, though insertion order settles a tie that `created_at` leaves open. The `queue_position` written back is the 1-based index in that ordering, and positions are dense and contiguous, so a withdrawal closes the gap behind it rather than leaving a hole. Applications in `DRAFT` or `WITHDRAWN` are outside the ranking entirely and carry a `queue_position` of 0.

The serving utility responds by filing **load studies**, and a study round is the `UtilityPlanner`'s work. Each round splits the campus load into its `it_load_mw` server component, its `cooling_load_mw` mechanical component and its `ancillary_load_mw` lighting and office component — each of which must be zero or greater — together with the `diversity_factor` coincidence factor the utility applies, which must be strictly greater than 0 and no greater than 1. The study is appended to its application, recording the `application_id` it belongs to and a 1-based `study_round` equal to the owning application's round count after the append, and the operator's identifier is stamped as `filed_by_user_id`. Filing a study advances the application from `SUBMITTED` to `STUDY_COMPLETE`; filing another study against an application already in `STUDY_COMPLETE` is a legitimate restudy that leaves the application in `STUDY_COMPLETE` and simply appends the next round. Filing against an application in any other status is rejected with `ERR_APPLICATION_INVALID_STATE`. Load study rows are the one place in this system with no top-level collection of their own, so listing them under their application is the only way to discover them.

Every study reports **two** views of the same load, and they must not be confused. The `nameplate_peak_mw` figure is the plain arithmetic sum of the IT, cooling and ancillary components rounded to 3 decimal places: the diversity factor and the cooling floor are ignored entirely, and this is the figure the developer originally requested capacity against. The `diversified_peak_mw` figure is the utility's coincident view: the IT component is multiplied by the diversity factor, the cooling component is multiplied by whichever is larger of the diversity factor and a cooling floor of 0.85 so that cooling load never diversifies below that floor, the ancillary component is taken undiversified at full value, the three products are added, and the total is rounded to 3 decimal places. For identical inputs the diversified figure is therefore always smaller than or equal to the nameplate figure, and it is the diversified figure — never the nameplate one — that drives upgrade determination, upgrade cost and the reservation tolerance.

Whether the utility must build network upgrades is a strict threshold. An upgrade trigger is computed as the utility's total transmission capacity multiplied by 0.50 and rounded to 3 decimal places, and `upgrade_required` is true only when the diversified peak is **strictly greater** than that trigger; a diversified peak exactly equal to the trigger does not require an upgrade. When no upgrade is required the study's `network_upgrade_cost_usd` is exactly 0.00. When one is required, the cost is the diversified peak multiplied by the utility's network upgrade rate per megawatt, multiplied by one plus a hazard uplift drawn from the owning site's hazard tier — no uplift at all for `LOW` at 0.00, an eight percent uplift for `MODERATE` at 0.08, and a twenty percent uplift for `SEVERE` at 0.20 — with the result rounded to 2 decimal places. Where the owning site has not been screened and therefore has no hazard tier, the uplift is treated as 0.00.

After each study round the application's own `diversified_peak_mw` and `network_upgrade_cost_usd` mirror the **newest** study — the one with the highest `study_round`, breaking any tie by the later `created_at` — so a restudy replaces the figures a client saw rather than adding to them. The application's `study_round` counter is incremented, and the owning site's `site_capital_commitment_usd` is recomputed because the upgrade cost feeds it.

### 3.5. Reserving Capacity, Committing Energization, Withdrawal and Capacity Revisions

Transmission capacity is allocated against overlapping *windows*, not against a single instantaneous total, and the overlap test is buffered. Two applications of the same utility are treated as concurrent when each one's `window_start` falls at or before the other's `window_end` extended forward by the utility's `commissioning_buffer_days` — that is, the buffer is applied to **both** ends of **both** windows under test, and the windows themselves are closed intervals. Only applications whose `status` is `CAPACITY_RESERVED` or `ENERGIZATION_COMMITTED` participate; applications in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED`, `STUDY_COMPLETE` or `WITHDRAWN` are excluded even when their windows overlap perfectly.

Reserving capacity moves an application from `STUDY_COMPLETE` to `CAPACITY_RESERVED` and is the `UtilityPlanner`'s decision. The application must be in exactly `STUDY_COMPLETE`: a `SUBMITTED` application has no study to reserve against and a `CAPACITY_RESERVED` one is already reserved, so either is rejected with `ERR_APPLICATION_NOT_STUDIED`. Two admission conditions then apply, in this order. First, the newest study must agree with the request: the absolute difference between `requested_mw` and `diversified_peak_mw`, divided by `requested_mw`, must be no greater than a restudy tolerance of 0.10, so a study landing more than ten percent away from the request in either direction must be restudied before capacity can be reserved, and a drift outside the tolerance is rejected with `ERR_STUDY_LOAD_MISMATCH`. Second, the aggregate must fit: the requested megawatts of every **other** reserved or energization-committed application of that utility whose buffered window overlaps this one are summed, this application's own requested megawatts are added, and that total must be less than or equal to the utility's `total_transmission_capacity_mw`. Equality is admissible; one megawatt beyond it is rejected with `ERR_TRANSMISSION_CAPACITY_EXCEEDED`, and the comparison is made at 3 decimal places. On success the application's `reserved_mw` is set to its requested megawatts, the utility's capacity ledger is recomputed, the `energization_risk_tier` of the owning site **and of every other site of that serving utility** is recomputed, the affected program's status is re-derived, and the application's `version` is incremented by one.

The utility's ledger is a peak-group figure rather than a naive sum. Taking the set of that utility's applications in `CAPACITY_RESERVED` or `ENERGIZATION_COMMITTED`, each member's group demand is its own requested megawatts plus the requested megawatts of every other member whose buffered window overlaps it. The utility's `reserved_capacity_mw` is the largest such group demand across the set, rounded to 3 decimal places, and it is exactly 0 when the set is empty. Its `available_capacity_mw` is the total transmission capacity minus the reserved figure, rounded to 3 decimal places. Because the admission test above bounds the same group demand by the same total, the available figure can never go negative, and two live applications whose buffered windows do not overlap never inflate each other's group demand.

Committing an energization date carries the application from `CAPACITY_RESERVED` to `ENERGIZATION_COMMITTED` and takes a single input, the `committed_energization_date` the utility will stand behind. Three temporal conditions must all hold: the date must fall at or after the application's own `window_start`; it must be at least the utility's `energization_lead_days` days after the current logical clock; and it must not be more than 90 days beyond the program's `required_energization_date`. A date failing any of the three is rejected with `ERR_ENERGIZATION_DEADLINE_MISSED`, while attempting the commitment from a status other than `CAPACITY_RESERVED` is rejected with `ERR_APPLICATION_INVALID_STATE`. The slip is then priced. The delay is the whole number of days from the program's required energization date to the committed date; where the committed date is earlier than or equal to the required date the delay is treated as zero, so an early or on-time commitment yields a `delay_exposure_usd` of exactly 0.00. Otherwise the exposure is the delay in days multiplied by a penalty of 250 US dollars per day per megawatt, multiplied by the application's requested megawatts, rounded to 2 decimal places — a commitment that slips the full 90-day grace on a 300 MW request therefore prices at six million, seven hundred and fifty thousand dollars of exposure. The owning site's `energization_risk_tier` is recomputed, and it becomes `BREACHED` whenever the committed date falls beyond the program's required date even though the commitment itself was admissible. The program's status and its `committed_load_mw` are re-derived and the application's `version` incremented by one.

That risk tier is a three-branch classification evaluated on the site, and the first matching branch wins. It reads the site's single application that is not `WITHDRAWN`, of which at most one exists. The tier is `BREACHED` when that application exists, sits in `ENERGIZATION_COMMITTED`, and its committed energization date is later than the program's required energization date. Otherwise the tier is `AT_RISK` when either no live application exists at all or the live application is still in `DRAFT`, `SUBMITTED` or `STUDY_COMPLETE`, **and** the serving utility's `available_capacity_mw` is strictly less than the program's `target_load_mw` — in other words, the site has not yet locked capacity and its utility no longer visibly has enough to give. In every other case the tier is `ON_TRACK`. Because the tier depends on the utility's available capacity, it is recomputed for every site of a utility whenever that utility's ledger moves, and not merely for the site whose application changed.

Withdrawal is available from any status other than `WITHDRAWN` and requires a `withdrawal_reason` to be recorded; attempting it on an application already `WITHDRAWN` is rejected with `ERR_APPLICATION_INVALID_STATE`. Withdrawing sets `status` to `WITHDRAWN`, zeroes `reserved_mw` and `queue_position`, and recomputes the utility's `reserved_capacity_mw` and `available_capacity_mw`. Withdrawing an application that had reached `CAPACITY_RESERVED` or `ENERGIZATION_COMMITTED` restores the utility's available capacity to exactly the value it held before the reservation — full compensation, no residual drift. The queue is re-ranked for the utility's remaining applications so positions stay contiguous, the owning site's `energization_risk_tier` and `site_capital_commitment_usd` are recomputed, the program's status and `committed_load_mw` are re-derived, and the application's `version` is incremented by one.

Utilities may also revise what they can deliver. A `UtilityPlanner` submits a capacity revision carrying the new `revised_total_capacity_mw` and a free-text `reason`. Unlike the strictly positive total demanded at registration, the revised figure need only be zero or greater, so a utility may revise its deliverable capacity all the way down to zero; it is carried at 3 decimal places. The utility's `total_transmission_capacity_mw` is replaced with the revised figure, and then every reservation is re-tested against the new ceiling using the same buffered overlap aggregate: any application in `CAPACITY_RESERVED` whose group demand no longer fits is walked backward to `SUBMITTED` with its `reserved_mw` zeroed, and any application in `ENERGIZATION_COMMITTED` that no longer fits is walked back to `SUBMITTED` with its `reserved_mw` zeroed, its `committed_energization_date` cleared to null and its `delay_exposure_usd` reset to 0. Seniority decides who survives, and the walk-back is iterative rather than a single sweep: the utility's live applications are considered in queue order, the least senior application whose group demand still exceeds the new ceiling is walked back, and the remaining group demands are then recomputed and re-tested, repeating until every survivor fits. Because relieving one application often brings its overlapping neighbours back inside the ceiling, no more reservations are surrendered than the revision actually requires, and the applications highest in the queue are the ones that keep theirs. In both cases the application keeps its queue position — it loses the reservation, not its place in line — so an application may legitimately travel `SUBMITTED` to `STUDY_COMPLETE` to `CAPACITY_RESERVED` and back to `SUBMITTED`, then forward again. Afterwards the utility's `reserved_capacity_mw` and `available_capacity_mw` are recomputed, `energization_risk_tier` is recomputed on every site of that utility, and the `program_status` and `committed_load_mw` of every affected program are re-derived; a revision that strands the committed site's reservation is a normal route from `SITE_COMMITTED` or `ENERGIZATION_SECURED` into `STALLED`. The utility's `version` is incremented by one.

### 3.6. Zoning Dockets, Community Engagement and Mitigation Escrow

A zoning application is filed by a `SiteScreeningAnalyst` against a site, naming the `site_id`, the `jurisdiction` — the municipality or county board that will hear it — the scheduled `hearing_date`, the ordinance's property-line noise ceiling as `noise_limit_dba`, the modelled property-line noise from the campus as `projected_noise_dba`, and the `visual_mitigation_tier` the applicant is offering, which is one of `NONE`, `BERM`, `BERM_AND_SCREENING` or `FULL_ENCLOSURE`. Both noise figures must be strictly positive. A site may hold at most one zoning application regardless of that application's status, so a second filing is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ZONING_APPLICATION`, and the site must not be `DISQUALIFIED`. A freshly filed docket sits at `status` `SUBMITTED` with `continuance_count` of 0 and `objection_count` of 0, and its acoustic and mitigation figures are already computed from the noise inputs on the assumption of zero objections. Dockets are discoverable only under their site, where they may additionally be filtered by `status`.

Community engagement is recorded one objection at a time by the `PermittingAuthority`, each carrying an `objection_type` and a `commenter_reference` identifying who raised it. Each objection increments `objection_count` by one and feeds the `weighted_opposition_score`, which is the sum of a per-type weight over all objections recorded against that docket, rounded to 2 decimal places. Water objections carry the heaviest weight at 2.0, noise objections 1.5, visual objections 1.2, and traffic objections the lightest at 1.0. Objections recorded on a sibling docket of the same site never contribute to this docket's score. Recording an objection on a docket that has already reached `APPROVED_WITH_CONDITIONS` or `DENIED` is rejected with `ERR_ZONING_INVALID_STATE`, since both are terminal. An objection is not itself an addressable resource and has no representation of its own: the operation answers 200 with the updated zoning docket rather than 201 with a new row, and the only trace of an individual objection is its contribution to the docket's `objection_count` and `weighted_opposition_score`.

Mitigation is demanded in five ordered steps, and every objection recorded — as well as every continuance granted — re-runs them. Step one is the weighted opposition score just described. Step two computes `noise_excess_dba` as the modelled noise minus the ordinance limit, floored at 0, so a campus modelled at or below the limit has no excess; the excess is reported as the exact difference of the two inputs without any further rounding of its own. Step three selects the `required_mitigation_tier` on a first-match-wins ladder in which either driver can escalate the tier on its own: the tier is `FULL_ENCLOSURE` when the noise excess is at least 10 dBA **or** the weighted opposition score is at least 12; failing that it is `BERM_AND_SCREENING` when the noise excess is at least 5 dBA **or** the score is at least 6; failing that it is `BERM` when the noise excess is strictly greater than 0 **or** the score is at least 3; and otherwise it is `NONE`. Step four prices that tier as `mitigation_cost_usd`: a tier base of 0 for `NONE`, 750,000 dollars for `BERM`, 2,100,000 dollars for `BERM_AND_SCREENING` and 5,400,000 dollars for `FULL_ENCLOSURE`, plus a per-decibel adder of 125,000 dollars applied to the **acoustic excess only** and never to the raw noise figures, with the total rounded to 2 decimal places. Step five sets the escrow the board will hold, and the crucial subtlety is that the escrow percentage is tiered on the **cost computed in step four**, not on the tier chosen in step three: the percentage is 0.00 when the cost is exactly zero, one tenth at 0.10 when the cost is strictly below 1,000,000 dollars, fifteen hundredths at 0.15 when the cost is strictly below 4,000,000 dollars, and one fifth at 0.20 otherwise. A cost of exactly 1,000,000.00 dollars therefore lands in the 0.15 band because the lowest band is a strict "below". The `conditions_escrow_usd` is the mitigation cost multiplied by that percentage, rounded to 2 decimal places.

The board's decision is a three-way fork carrying an `outcome` and an optional `decision_note`, and the docket must be in `SUBMITTED` or `CONTINUED` for any of the three; a decision on a terminal docket is rejected with `ERR_ZONING_INVALID_STATE`. All three branches record the supplied note as `decision_note`, which is null until the first decision, and all three increment the docket's `version` by one. The note is not accumulated: a decision that omits it writes null, so an approval filed without a note clears whatever note an earlier continuance recorded.

An **approval with conditions** carries the docket from `SUBMITTED` to `APPROVED_WITH_CONDITIONS`, or equally from `CONTINUED` to `APPROVED_WITH_CONDITIONS` after one or two continuances, and the two routes have identical effects. Approval has one gate: the applicant's offered mitigation must rank at least as high as the tier the analysis demands, where the ranking places `NONE` lowest, then `BERM`, then `BERM_AND_SCREENING`, then `FULL_ENCLOSURE` highest. An applicant offering a berm while the noise excess and community opposition demand a full enclosure is refused with `ERR_MITIGATION_TIER_INSUFFICIENT`; an applicant offering more mitigation than demanded is approved. On approval the `mitigation_cost_usd` and then the `conditions_escrow_usd` are finalized in that order, and the owning site's `site_capital_commitment_usd` is recomputed because both figures feed it.

A **continuance** carries the docket from `SUBMITTED` to `CONTINUED`, and a docket already in `CONTINUED` may be continued again, self-looping in place. Either route requires that `continuance_count` is still strictly below 2 continuances; the third continuance request is refused with `ERR_ZONING_CONTINUANCE_EXHAUSTED`. Both routes increment `continuance_count` by one, re-derive the `required_mitigation_tier`, the `mitigation_cost_usd` and the `conditions_escrow_usd`, and recompute the owning site's `site_capital_commitment_usd`.

A **denial** carries the docket from `SUBMITTED` or `CONTINUED` to `DENIED`, and it is the only branch that reaches outside the docket. On denial the mitigation cost and conditions escrow are left exactly at their pre-decision values rather than being re-derived — a denied campus is not priced. Instead the referenced candidate site is driven to `DISQUALIFIED` with its `disqualification_reason` set to the string `ZONING_DENIED`, and the full four-hop disqualification cascade described in §3.10 fires as though a `PortfolioDirector` had dropped the site by hand — unless that site is already `DISQUALIFIED`, in which case the denial is recorded on the docket alone and the site keeps the reason it was dropped with.

### 3.7. Incentive Negotiation, Signature and Ratification

An `IncentiveNegotiator` opens an **incentive negotiation** against a site by supplying the `site_id`, the `authority_level` — either `STATE` or `LOCAL` — and the granting authority's `counterparty_name`. A site may hold at most one negotiation per authority tier, so a second `STATE` negotiation on the same site is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_NEGOTIATION` while a `LOCAL` one alongside it is perfectly legitimate, and the site itself must be in `DRAFT`, `SCREENED` or `COMMITTED` rather than `DISQUALIFIED`, otherwise `ERR_SITE_INVALID_STATE`. A new negotiation sits at `status` `DRAFT` with `round_count` of 0 and `last_party` null, and all four of its money figures — `npv_incentive_usd`, `sales_tax_benefit_usd`, `abatement_npv_usd` and `clawback_exposure_usd` — are 0 until the first round of terms arrives, while its five standing terms — `sales_tax_exemption_pct`, `abatement_years`, `abatement_pct`, `jobs_commitment` and `capex_commitment_usd` — read null until that same first round writes them.

Rounds alternate strictly between the two sides of the table, and the negotiation's status always names the party expected to act next. A round carries a `submitted_by_party` of either `COMPANY` or `AUTHORITY` together with the five standing terms it proposes: the `sales_tax_exemption_pct` fraction of equipment sales tax to be exempted, the `abatement_years` property-tax abatement term in whole years, the `abatement_pct` fraction of annual property tax to be abated, the `jobs_commitment` count of permanent jobs the company undertakes to create, and the `capex_commitment_usd` capital investment the company undertakes to make. The exemption and abatement fractions lie between 0.0 and 1.0 inclusive, the abatement term between 1 and 30 whole years, the jobs commitment is zero or greater, and the capital commitment is strictly positive. A company round is filed by an `IncentiveNegotiator`; an authority counter is filed by a `PermittingAuthority`.

The alternation is enforced against the current status. A `COMPANY` round is expected while the negotiation is in `DRAFT` or in `COMPANY_OFFER_PENDING`, and an `AUTHORITY` round is expected while it is in `AUTHORITY_COUNTER_PENDING`; a round whose declared party is not the party the status expects is rejected with `ERR_WRONG_NEGOTIATION_PARTY`, and a round filed from any other status — from `SIGNED` or `RATIFIED` — is rejected with `ERR_NEGOTIATION_INVALID_STATE`. The opening company round drives the negotiation from `DRAFT` to `AUTHORITY_COUNTER_PENDING`, and that first round additionally requires the referenced site to be in `DRAFT` or `SCREENED` status, a site in any other status being refused with `ERR_SITE_INVALID_STATE`; that site test is evaluated ahead of both the negotiation-status test and the turn test, so an opening round against a `COMMITTED` site reports the site's state even when the declared party is also wrong. The authority's counter then drives it from `AUTHORITY_COUNTER_PENDING` to `COMPANY_OFFER_PENDING`, and a further company round drives it back from `COMPANY_OFFER_PENDING` to `AUTHORITY_COUNTER_PENDING`, so the pair of states cycles once per round pair for as long as the two sides keep talking. Every round, from either party, overwrites all five standing terms with that round's proposal, increments `round_count` by one, records `last_party` as the submitting party, flips `status` to the other party's pending state, recomputes all four money figures from the new terms, and increments the negotiation's `version` by one. Only this negotiation's own standing terms enter that computation; a sibling negotiation on the same site never contributes.

The money is valued in four steps. First, the sales-tax benefit is tiered on the capital commitment, and the tiers move *downward* as the investment grows: the applicable rate is 6.25 percent, expressed as 0.0625, when the capital commitment is strictly below 500 million dollars; 5.50 percent, expressed as 0.0550, when it is strictly below 1.5 billion dollars; and 4.75 percent, expressed as 0.0475, at 1.5 billion dollars or above. The `sales_tax_benefit_usd` is then the capital commitment multiplied by an equipment share of 0.62 — the fraction of the capital spend that is taxable equipment — multiplied by that rate, multiplied by the negotiated exemption fraction, and rounded to 2 decimal places. Second, an annual abatement base is formed as the capital commitment multiplied by an assessed ratio of 0.85, multiplied by a mill rate of 0.0225, multiplied by the negotiated abatement fraction. Third, the `abatement_npv_usd` discounts that base across the abatement term at a discount rate of 0.07 per year: for each whole year from the first through the last year of the term, the annual base is divided by one plus the discount rate raised to that year's power, **that year's figure is rounded to cents**, and only then are the per-year figures summed. This rounding order is pinned: summing the unrounded annual figures and rounding the total once at the end is incorrect and can differ from the required answer by cents. Fourth, `npv_incentive_usd` is the sales-tax benefit plus the abatement present value, rounded to 2 decimal places, and `clawback_exposure_usd` is that total multiplied by a clawback share, rounded to 2 decimal places — where the share is the gentler 0.40 when the company has committed to at least 100 permanent jobs and the harsher 0.60 when it has committed to fewer.

Signature is the `IncentiveNegotiator`'s act and only ever closes on an authority counter that is on the table: the negotiation must be in `COMPANY_OFFER_PENDING`, and it must have accumulated at least 2 rounds, so a term sheet cannot be signed before one company offer and one authority counter have both been recorded. Attempting to sign from any other status, or before the second round, is rejected with `ERR_NEGOTIATION_INVALID_STATE`. Signature moves the negotiation `COMPANY_OFFER_PENDING` to `SIGNED` and stamps the acting operator as `signed_by_user_id`, which becomes the comparand for the duty separation at ratification.

Ratification belongs to the `CapitalCommitteeChair` and moves the negotiation `SIGNED` to `RATIFIED`, stamping `ratified_by_user_id`. It requires the negotiation to be in `SIGNED` — otherwise `ERR_NEGOTIATION_INVALID_STATE` — that the acting operator is not the operator who signed, otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, and that the site's zoning docket has already reached `APPROVED_WITH_CONDITIONS`, otherwise `ERR_ZONING_NOT_APPROVED`. A ratified `STATE`-level negotiation is what later unlocks the incentives gate at site commitment. Negotiations attached to a site can be listed and filtered by `authority_level` and by `status`.

### 3.8. Phased Land Control: Options, Supersession, Exercise and Expiry

Land is assembled in phases under **option agreements** rather than bought outright. A `SiteScreeningAnalyst` signs an option by naming the `site_id`, the assessor parcel number as `parcel_apn`, the acquisition `phase_index` the parcel belongs to (a whole number of at least 1), the parcel's `acres`, the non-refundable `option_premium_usd` paid at signing, the `purchase_price_usd` that will buy the parcel on exercise, the exclusivity window as `window_start` and `window_end`, and the `expected_site_version` optimistic-concurrency token described in §2.10. Two fields are optional: `premium_credit_pct`, the fraction of the premium credited against the purchase price on exercise, which defaults to 0.5 when omitted and must lie between 0.0 and 1.0 inclusive; and `replaces_option_id`, naming a prior option on the same parcel that this one supersedes, which is null for a first-time option. Acreage and purchase price must be strictly positive and the premium must be zero or greater. A new option enters at `status` `OPTIONED`.

Exclusivity is enforced per parcel of a site, not per site. No other option sharing the same `site_id` and the same `parcel_apn`, and whose `status` is `OPTIONED` or `EXERCISED`, may have a half-open exclusivity window overlapping the new one; a collision is refused with `ERR_OPTION_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Because the windows are half-open, two options that abut exactly — the first ending on the instant the second begins — are admissible, and no commissioning-style buffer is applied here at all. The option named by `replaces_option_id` is exempt from the test, because it is released within the same request. That predecessor must itself be `OPTIONED`, otherwise `ERR_OPTION_INVALID_STATE`, and it must belong to the same site and the same parcel as the option superseding it, otherwise `ERR_APPLICATION_SITE_UTILITY_MISMATCH`. Creating an option against a `DISQUALIFIED` site is refused with `ERR_SITE_INVALID_STATE`.

Signing an option recomputes the site's land control. The acreage of every option of that site whose `status` is `OPTIONED` or `EXERCISED` is summed, multiplied by 100, divided by the site's `gross_acres`, capped at 100.0 and rounded to 2 decimal places to give `land_control_pct` — so a campus that has optioned more acreage than its gross footprint still reports 100.00 rather than more. The `land_control_status` follows directly: `FULL` when the percentage is at or above 100, `NONE` when it is exactly 0, and `PARTIAL` at any value between. The program's `full_land_control_site_count` then counts its sites whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `SCREENED` or `COMMITTED` and whose land control status is `FULL`. Signing also increments the addressed site's `version` by one — which is precisely why the next writer needs a fresh `expected_site_version` — moves the program's `option_exposure_usd` and `premium_committed_total_usd`, re-derives `program_status`, and recomputes the site's `site_capital_commitment_usd`. Where the create supersedes a predecessor, that predecessor moves `OPTIONED` to `RELEASED` in the same request and its premium shifts out of `option_exposure_usd` into `forfeited_premium_usd`.

The premium ledger is the program's four-bucket accounting of option money, and every status change moves a premium between buckets without creating or destroying value. `option_exposure_usd` totals the premiums of options still `OPTIONED`. `forfeited_premium_usd` totals the premiums of options that are `LAPSED` or `RELEASED`. `capitalized_premium_usd` totals the premiums of options that have been `EXERCISED`. `premium_committed_total_usd` totals every premium regardless of status and is therefore the conserved figure that the other three must always sum to, across every option of every site of the program regardless of those sites' own statuses.

Exercising an option is the `PortfolioDirector`'s act and moves it `OPTIONED` to `EXERCISED`. Three conditions apply: the option must be `OPTIONED`, otherwise `ERR_OPTION_INVALID_STATE`; the current logical clock must be **strictly before** `window_end`, since the window is half-open and a clock at or past that instant makes the option unexercisable, otherwise `ERR_OPTION_EXPIRED`; and the owning site's live interconnection application must already hold capacity, sitting in `CAPACITY_RESERVED` or `ENERGIZATION_COMMITTED`, otherwise `ERR_SITE_NOT_SCREENED` — the company does not buy land before the utility has reserved the power. On exercise the clock is stamped as `exercised_at`, `premium_credited_usd` becomes the option premium multiplied by the credit fraction rounded to 2 decimal places, and `net_purchase_usd` becomes the purchase price minus that credit, also rounded to 2 decimal places and not floored at zero, so a credit larger than the purchase price yields a negative net purchase. Both figures are 0 while an option is anything other than `EXERCISED`. The premium moves from `option_exposure_usd` into `capitalized_premium_usd` with `premium_committed_total_usd` unchanged; the site's `land_control_pct`, then its `land_control_status`, then its `site_capital_commitment_usd` are recomputed in that order; the program's `full_land_control_site_count` and `program_status` are re-derived; and the option's `version` is incremented by one.

Options that are never exercised do not lapse on their own. A `PortfolioDirector` runs an explicit **option-expiry sweep** across every parcel option belonging to every site of one program, and each option still in `OPTIONED` whose half-open window has closed — that is, whose `window_end` is at or before the current logical clock — is carried `OPTIONED` to `LAPSED`. The sweep is a four-hop cascade per lapsed option. The first hop lapses the option itself: `status` becomes `LAPSED`, `lapsed_at` records the current logical clock, and the premium shifts from `option_exposure_usd` into `forfeited_premium_usd` with `premium_committed_total_usd` unchanged. The second hop, because that option has left the controlled set, recomputes the owning site's `land_control_pct`. The third hop, because the percentage moved, recomputes the site's `land_control_status`, which typically falls from `FULL` back to `PARTIAL` or to `NONE`. The fourth hop, because a site's land control status moved, recomputes the program's `full_land_control_site_count` and re-derives its `program_status` — and a program whose `COMMITTED` site has just lost full land control goes from `SITE_COMMITTED` to `STALLED`. Affected sites also have their `site_capital_commitment_usd` recomputed, and each lapsed option's `version` is incremented by one. Curing the impairment later — signing fresh options that restore full land control — brings the program back from `STALLED` to `SITE_COMMITTED`. The sweep answers with the addressed siting program in `data`; a sweep that finds nothing whose window has closed mutates nothing at all, leaving every `version` and every `updated_at` in the program's neighbourhood exactly where it was. Options can be read individually and listed with filters on `site_id`, on `replaces_option_id` to trace a supersede chain, and on `status`.

### 3.9. Renewable Procurement and Load Matching

A `PortfolioDirector` procures clean energy by drafting a **power purchase agreement** against a program, supplying the `program_id`, the generator's `counterparty_name`, the generation `technology`, the `contracted_capacity_mw` nameplate under contract, the `annual_generation_mwh` expected in a year, the `hourly_matched_mwh` subset of that generation delivered in load-coincident hours, the `strike_price_usd_per_mwh` contract price, the `matching_mode` under which the agreement will be credited, and the delivery term as `delivery_start` and `delivery_end`. Technology is one of `SOLAR`, `WIND`, `STORAGE_HYBRID` or `NUCLEAR`. Matching mode is either `ANNUAL_VOLUMETRIC` or `HOURLY_MATCHED`. Contracted capacity, annual generation and strike price must all be strictly positive, while the hourly-matched volume must be zero or greater and may not exceed the annual generation, that last comparison being a request-shape rule and therefore evaluated ahead of the delivery-interval ordering test. A drafted agreement sits at `status` `DRAFT`, its `annual_cost_usd` already computed as the annual generation multiplied by the strike price rounded to 2 decimal places, and its `credited_coverage_mwh` at exactly 0 — a draft agreement never counts toward coverage.

Executing the agreement moves it `DRAFT` to `EXECUTED`; executing one that is already `EXECUTED` is refused with `ERR_PPA_INVALID_STATE`. Execution is where the two matching modes diverge sharply, and the same agreement row credits a materially different volume under each. Under `ANNUAL_VOLUMETRIC` accounting the whole contracted output counts subject to a technology curtailment haircut: `credited_coverage_mwh` is the annual generation multiplied by one minus the technology's curtailment factor, rounded to 3 decimal places, where solar is curtailed 6 percent at 0.06, wind 11 percent at 0.11, storage hybrids 4 percent at 0.04 and nuclear just 1 percent at 0.01. In this mode the `hourly_matched_mwh` figure is stored but plays no part. Under `HOURLY_MATCHED` accounting only load-coincident hours count: `credited_coverage_mwh` is the hourly-matched volume multiplied by an hourly deliverability factor of 0.98, rounded to 3 decimal places — and here it is the `annual_generation_mwh` and the technology curtailment that are ignored entirely.

Execution then re-aggregates the program's coverage. The credited volumes of every agreement of that program whose `status` is `EXECUTED` are summed — drafts contribute exactly nothing to the numerator — multiplied by 100, divided by the program's `program_annual_load_mwh`, and rounded to 2 decimal places to give `renewable_coverage_pct`. The program's `program_status` is re-derived and the agreement's `version` is incremented by one. Agreements matched to a program can be listed under it and filtered by `status`.

### 3.10. Committing a Site, Unwinding a Site, and the Program Status Rollup

Before a commitment can be evaluated the system needs a single number for what the campus will cost, and that is the site's `site_capital_commitment_usd`. It aggregates six terms, all belonging to the one site: the `study_fee_usd` and the `network_upgrade_cost_usd` of the site's interconnection application that is not `WITHDRAWN`, each contributing zero when no such application exists; the `mitigation_cost_usd` and the `conditions_escrow_usd` of the site's zoning application, each contributing zero when no docket has been filed; the sum of `net_purchase_usd` over the site's options that are `EXERCISED`; and the sum of `option_premium_usd` over the site's options that are still `OPTIONED`. The total is rounded to 2 decimal places. Premiums on `LAPSED` and `RELEASED` options are sunk money and are deliberately excluded. This figure is recomputed whenever any of its inputs moves — on application submission, on each load study round, on withdrawal, on a zoning approval or continuance, on option signature, exercise or lapse, on a disqualification, and again at commitment. It is recomputed on exactly those triggers rather than on every request, so a request that touches none of them leaves the figure as it stands even where an unrelated cascade has run.

Committing the program to a site is the `CapitalCommitteeChair`'s single most consequential act and moves the site `SCREENED` to `COMMITTED`. Beyond the state requirement — a site not in `SCREENED` is refused with `ERR_SITE_INVALID_STATE` — nine further gates are evaluated in the fixed order set out in §2.11. The acting operator must differ from the site's `screened_by_user_id`, else `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The site's `hazard_tier` must not be `SEVERE`, because a severe seismic, flood or wind exposure disqualifies a campus regardless of how well it scored, else `ERR_HAZARD_TIER_PROHIBITED`. The site's zoning docket must stand at `APPROVED_WITH_CONDITIONS`, else `ERR_ZONING_NOT_APPROVED`. No other site of the same program may already be `COMMITTED`, else `ERR_SITE_COMMITMENT_EXISTS`. The site's `land_control_pct` must be at least 100, because a partially optioned campus cannot be committed, else `ERR_LAND_CONTROL_INCOMPLETE`. The site must carry an incentive negotiation at `STATE` authority level whose status is `RATIFIED`, else `ERR_INCENTIVES_NOT_RATIFIED`. The site's `water_feasible` flag must be true, meaning its annual water demand is less than or equal to its drought-curtailed headroom with equality acceptable, else `ERR_WATER_SUPPLY_INSUFFICIENT`. The program's `renewable_coverage_pct` from executed agreements must be at least 80.0 percent, with exactly 80.00 acceptable, else `ERR_RENEWABLE_COVERAGE_INSUFFICIENT`. And last of all, the site's `site_capital_commitment_usd` must be less than or equal to the program's `capital_budget_usd`, with equality acceptable, else `ERR_CAPITAL_BUDGET_EXCEEDED`. On success the site's `status` becomes `COMMITTED`, the acting operator is stamped as `committed_by_user_id` — null until that moment — the capital commitment is recomputed and compared against the ceiling, the program moves `SCREENING` to `SITE_COMMITTED`, and the site's `version` is incremented by one.

Dropping a site is the `PortfolioDirector`'s act, requires a `disqualification_reason`, and is permitted from `DRAFT`, `SCREENED` or `COMMITTED`; a site already `DISQUALIFIED` cannot be dropped again and the attempt is refused with `ERR_SITE_INVALID_STATE`. Disqualification fires an identical four-hop cascade from all three permitted statuses; a `DRAFT` site may already carry a drafted application and signed options, and they are handled by exactly the same rules — a `DRAFT` application never entered the queue and so is left in `DRAFT`, while its `OPTIONED` options are released as below. The first hop withdraws the site's live interconnection application: any application in `SUBMITTED`, `STUDY_COMPLETE`, `CAPACITY_RESERVED` or `ENERGIZATION_COMMITTED` moves to `WITHDRAWN` with `withdrawal_reason` recorded as the string `SITE_DISQUALIFIED`, `reserved_mw` zeroed and `queue_position` zeroed. The second hop, because that application released its reservation, recomputes the serving utility's `reserved_capacity_mw` and `available_capacity_mw`. The third hop, because the utility's available capacity changed, recomputes `energization_risk_tier` on every **other** candidate site of that utility whose status is `DRAFT`, `SCREENED` or `COMMITTED`; the dropped site is skipped and keeps the tier frozen at whatever value it held when it was dropped, as does any other site that has already been disqualified. The fourth hop, because a sibling site's risk tier changed, re-derives `program_status` and recomputes `full_land_control_site_count`.

Three further effects accompany the disqualification directly rather than as part of that hop chain. Every option of the dropped site that is still `OPTIONED` moves to `RELEASED`, shifting its premium out of the program's `option_exposure_usd` and into `forfeited_premium_usd`. The serving basin's `committed_annual_demand_mgal` is recomputed and `water_feasible` re-evaluated on its remaining sites, since the dropped site no longer consumes shared headroom. And `queue_position` is recomputed for every remaining application of the serving utility so the queue stays contiguous. The site's `status` becomes `DISQUALIFIED`, its `disqualification_reason` is persisted, and its `version` is incremented by one. `DISQUALIFIED` is terminal for a candidate site.

The program's own status is never written by a request body. It is a compound projection re-derived at the end of every request that mutates any candidate site of the program, any interconnection application of one of those sites, any land parcel option of one of those sites, any power purchase agreement of the program, or a water source those sites draw on. The derivation looks at the program's single site whose `status` is `COMMITTED`, of which at most one exists, and takes the **first** matching branch of four. The status is `STALLED` when a committed site exists and that site is impaired — meaning its `land_control_status` is anything other than `FULL`, **or** its `water_feasible` flag is false, **or** its `energization_risk_tier` is `BREACHED`. Failing that, the status is `ENERGIZATION_SECURED` when a committed site exists and its application that is not `WITHDRAWN` sits in `ENERGIZATION_COMMITTED`. Failing that, the status is `SITE_COMMITTED` when a committed site exists at all. Otherwise the status is `SCREENING`. Because the impairment test is evaluated **before** the energization test, a site that is energization-committed but has lost its land control or its water reports `STALLED` rather than `ENERGIZATION_SECURED`. Disqualified sites are excluded from every branch. Alongside the status, `committed_load_mw` is re-derived as the sum of `requested_mw` over the applications of this program's sites whose status is `ENERGIZATION_COMMITTED`, and every re-derivation that changes the program increments the program's `version` by one.

Read as a lifecycle, that projection yields seven observable program transitions. A program leaves `SCREENING` for `SITE_COMMITTED` when exactly one of its sites reaches `COMMITTED` with full land control, feasible water and a risk tier that is not `BREACHED`, and that transition also refreshes `committed_load_mw`, the three premium buckets, `premium_committed_total_usd` and `full_land_control_site_count`. It moves `SITE_COMMITTED` to `ENERGIZATION_SECURED` when the committed site's live application reaches `ENERGIZATION_COMMITTED` and that site's risk tier is `ON_TRACK`. It falls from `SITE_COMMITTED` to `STALLED`, or from `ENERGIZATION_SECURED` to `STALLED`, whenever the committed site becomes impaired on any of the three counts — the usual triggers being an expiry sweep that lapses land options, a drought declaration that curtails supply, or a utility capacity revision that strands the reservation. It climbs back from `STALLED` to `SITE_COMMITTED` once the impairment is cured and the committed site's live application is not in `ENERGIZATION_COMMITTED`, typically after fresh options restore land control or a relaxed drought stage restores supply. And it returns to `SCREENING` from either `SITE_COMMITTED` or `STALLED` when a disqualification removes the program's last `COMMITTED` site, re-deriving `committed_load_mw` and `full_land_control_site_count` on the way.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Status codes follow one rule. An operation that brings a new resource into existence answers 201 Created; every other successful operation — reads, state transitions, sweeps, decisions and the clock control — answers 200 OK. Of the POSTs that address a sub-collection, only `load-studies` mints a resource of its own, so it alone answers 201 with the new study row; a capacity revision, a drought declaration, a community objection and the option-expiry sweep persist no row of their own and answer 200 carrying the updated parent — the utility, the water source, the zoning docket and the siting program respectively — while the clock control answers 200 with an empty `data` object. ## 5. Error Code Catalogue

Every error response carries its identifier in the envelope's `error_id` field, as specified in §2.2. Trigger conditions are not repeated here: each identifier's condition is stated in the §2 or §3 narrative that governs the rule it guards. Three transport-level identifiers sit outside this catalogue because they precede the domain entirely: a request to a path this API does not route answers `ERR_NOT_FOUND` with 404, a request using a verb the path does not accept answers `ERR_METHOD_NOT_ALLOWED` with 405, and an unforeseen server fault answers `ERR_INTERNAL` with 500.
