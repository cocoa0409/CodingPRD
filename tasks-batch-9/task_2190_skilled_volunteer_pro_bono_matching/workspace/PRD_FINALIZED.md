# Pro Bono Matching API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Skilled-volunteer programs fail in the middle, not at the edges. Recruiting a lawyer who will donate forty hours is comparatively easy; so is thanking her afterwards. What breaks a pro bono program is everything in between — a nonprofit whose project was never scoped tightly enough to be matchable, a volunteer who accepted terms that contradict the intellectual-property regime the deliverable was scoped under, a two-week engagement that quietly grows into a four-month one, a program manager who cannot answer the board's only question ("what was all of this worth?") because nobody agreed on how a donated hour is valued.

This document specifies the backend service that runs such a program end to end. The service holds nonprofit **partner organizations** and their **project requests**, the **scope definitions** (with their weighted deliverable sets) that make a request matchable, the roster of **volunteers** with their skill claims and availability windows, the **match offers** the selector produces, and the **engagements** that acceptance opens — each engagement carrying its negotiated IP and confidentiality agreement, its milestone check-ins, its time logs, its scope-change history, its quality review rounds and its hour valuations. Program capacity is finite and is modelled explicitly: every cycle owns a fixed number of engagement slots, every reservation and every commitment is accounted against them, and every release path must return exactly what it took.

Three properties are non-negotiable and shape nearly every rule below. First, **conservation**: cycle slots, volunteer hours and reported in-kind value are ledgered quantities, and a compensating action must restore them exactly — a terminated engagement that grew from forty to fifty-two committed hours releases fifty-two. Second, **segregation of duties**: the operator who scopes work may not be the operator who judges its quality, and no volunteer reviews her own delivery. Third, **determinism**: every threshold, band floor, rounding step, tie-break and rejection precedence in this document is fixed, because two correct implementations of this service must produce byte-identical numbers from identical inputs.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

Everything in this chapter applies to every endpoint unless a later section explicitly narrows it. The service is mounted at the root of the host; resource paths such as `/partner-orgs` and `/engagements` carry no additional prefix.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the caller, and `X-User-Role`, naming the caller's role. If either header is absent or blank, the request is rejected with `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS` (HTTP 401) — this check runs before any other, on every endpoint including reads. Exactly five roles exist, spelled precisely as follows: `ProgramManager`, `ScopingSpecialist`, `Volunteer`, `PartnerContact`, and `QualityReviewer`. A role value outside that set, or a role that is not listed among the target endpoint's authorized roles in §4, is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

A `ProgramManager` is the program operations owner: it onboards partners and volunteers, runs cycle capacity checkpoints, issues agreements, schedules and holds milestone check-ins, decides escalated scope changes, executes handoff, terminates engagements and posts hour valuations. A `ScopingSpecialist` authors and revises scope definitions with their deliverable sets, runs the matching selector, withdraws offers, and restarts scoping after an unsuccessful match. A `QualityReviewer` opens review rounds, scores each deliverable against its acceptance criteria, and commits the pass, conditional, rework or fail outcome. When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramManager`, `ScopingSpecialist` or `QualityReviewer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it where a rule says so — as `authored_by_user_id` on a scope revision drafted by a `ScopingSpecialist`, as `reviewer_user_id` on a review opened by a `QualityReviewer`, and as `decided_by_user_id` on a scope change decided by a `ProgramManager` — but never compares it to any entity's `id`.

The two remaining roles are entity-backed and therefore carry ownership semantics. When `X-User-Role` is `Volunteer`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Volunteer` row; ownership checks on any resource that references a volunteer compare that resource's volunteer reference to `X-User-ID` — the `volunteer_id` of a skill claim, availability window or match offer, and the `volunteer_id` of an engagement whose work is being started, logged against or submitted. When `X-User-Role` is `PartnerContact`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `PartnerOrg`; ownership checks compare the `partner_org_id` reached through the resource chain — directly on a project request, and transitively through the request for a scope approval, an engagement or a satisfaction survey — to `X-User-ID`. An entity-backed caller reaching for a resource that does not resolve to its own identifier is rejected with `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (HTTP 403); the `logged_by_user_id` stamped on a time log is always the submitting volunteer's own identifier, so the field is never a way around the ownership check.

Segregation of duties is an additional, independent barrier layered on top of role and ownership. A `QualityReviewer` may not decide a review whose pinned scope revision carries that same operator identifier in `authored_by_user_id`, and may not score or decide a round whose `reviewer_user_id` belongs to a different operator; opening a new review round on an engagement is likewise barred to an operator who authored the pinned scope or who owned a prior round on that engagement. Any such attempt is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

List endpoints accept two optional query parameters, `limit` and `offset`. `limit` defaults to 10, must be at least 1, and may not exceed 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and may not be negative. Both must be integers; a `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 400). Unless a specific endpoint's description in §4 states another ordering, list results are sorted by `created_at` ascending, and the `meta` block of a list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` (the number of rows matching the filter before pagination) and `has_more` (true when rows remain beyond the current page).

Filtered list endpoints combine their filter parameters conjunctively. Project requests may be narrowed by `partner_org_id`, `cycle_id` and `status`; scope revisions by `project_request_id`, `supersedes_scope_id` and `status` — supplying `supersedes_scope_id` walks the revision chain forward from any revision; match offers by `project_request_id`, `volunteer_id`, `scope_definition_id`, `availability_window_id` and `status`; engagements by `match_offer_id`, `project_request_id`, `volunteer_id`, `scope_definition_id`, `cycle_id`, `availability_window_id` and `work_status`.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Audit Stamps and Version Counters

Every resource the service persists carries an `id`, which is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix and is never supplied by a client, and a `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the row was inserted. Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, in the same timestamp format, refreshed on every mutation that touches the row, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 on creation and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row. Partner organizations, program cycles, project requests, scope definitions, volunteers, match offers, engagements, engagement agreements, milestones, scope change requests, quality reviews and valuation records — `PartnerOrg`, `ProgramCycle`, `ProjectRequest`, `ScopeDefinition`, `Volunteer`, `MatchOffer`, `Engagement`, `EngagementAgreement`, `Milestone`, `ScopeChangeRequest`, `QualityReview` and `ValuationRecord` — all carry the full set.

Five resource kinds are deliberately lighter and carry only `id` and `created_at` — they have no `updated_at` field and no `version` field at all, and callers must not expect either in their payloads: deliverables, skill profiles, availability windows, time logs and satisfaction surveys — `Deliverable`, `SkillProfile`, `AvailabilityWindow`, `TimeLog` and `SatisfactionSurvey`. A deliverable's `review_status` and `review_score` may change during a review round and an availability window's `hours_committed` moves constantly, but neither row gains a version counter or a modification stamp; the versioned parent (the scope revision, the volunteer, the engagement) is where concurrency is observed.

Version increments matter beyond bookkeeping, because they are what the stale-version guards in §2.7 read. Three writes bump a parent's counter as a declared side effect rather than as an incidental consequence of touching the row: drafting a new scope revision increments the owning project request's `version` by 1; accepting a match offer increments the project request's `version` by 1; and both posting a valuation record and issuing an engagement agreement increment the engagement's `version` by 1. Cascades bump the `version` and refresh the `updated_at` of every row they mutate, not only of the row the caller named — when a partner suspension abandons four requests and terminates two engagements, all six rows advance their counters.

### 2.5. The Logical Clock and Interval Geometry

The service reads time from a logical clock, never from wall-clock time. At startup the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it holds that instant until an operator moves it; there is no fallback to real time under any circumstance. A `ProgramManager` may advance the clock by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant to `POST /_clock/set_current_utc`. The clock only moves forward: an instant strictly earlier than the current clock reading is rejected with `ERR_CLOCK_REGRESSION` (HTTP 422), while an instant exactly equal to the current reading is accepted and leaves the clock where it is. Advancing the clock performs no sweep of its own — expired offers and overdue check-ins are only acted upon when a cycle recalculation runs, as described in §3.1.

Every deadline, expiry, buffer and containment rule in this document reads that clock. Two interval conventions apply throughout. Program cycles and availability windows are **half-open**: an interval covers instants at or after `starts_at` and strictly before `ends_at`, so a window whose `ends_at` equals another window's `starts_at` does not overlap it, and a clock reading exactly equal to `ends_at` is already outside. Both intervals require `ends_at` to be strictly after `starts_at`. The milestone spacing buffer, by contrast, is **closed**: two check-ins exactly the buffer apart are acceptable, and only a strictly smaller separation is refused.

### 2.6. Numeric Precision and Rounding

All rounding in this service is round-half-up, and rounding happens exactly at the points the calculation narratives name and nowhere else. Monetary amounts — budgets, professional rates, indemnity caps, per-line valuations, overhead and totals — carry two decimal places. Hour quantities — offered, committed, remaining, estimated, reserved, logged, approved and reported hours — likewise carry two decimals. Percentage and score fields split by purpose: satisfaction composites, the partner satisfaction index and the weighted quality score carry two decimals; creep ratios, projected creep ratios, cycle utilization and both reliability scores carry four decimals. Years of experience carries one decimal.

Where a formula names an intermediate precision, that intermediate is materially different from the final one and must be honoured: normalised deliverable weights are computed to six decimals before being multiplied out, decay weights to six decimals, and the raw match score to six decimals before the seniority multiplier and the final four-decimal rounding are applied. Band boundaries are stated explicitly in each calculation and are never approximated; where a band floor is inclusive, a value landing exactly on it belongs to the higher band.

### 2.7. Stale-Version Guards on Superseding Writes

Three write operations legitimately supersede something a client may not have seen, and each therefore demands that the client prove it read the parent's current state. In each case the guard is a required, non-optional field in the request body carrying the client's last observed `version` of the named parent; a value that does not equal the parent's current `version` is rejected, and the write is not applied. Endpoints not named here take no version field at all, and supplying one to them has no effect.

Drafting a scope revision on a project request — `POST /project-requests/{id}/scope-definitions` — requires `expected_project_request_version` to equal the target request's current `version`. A revision create supersedes the prior revision (a prior `DRAFT` sibling moves to `WITHDRAWN` immediately, and a prior `APPROVED` sibling moves to `SUPERSEDED` once the new revision is approved), so a `ScopingSpecialist` working from a stale view of the request would silently discard a revision it never saw. A mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PROJECT_REQUEST_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

Posting a valuation record — `POST /engagements/{id}/valuations` — requires `expected_engagement_version` to equal the target engagement's current `version`. A valuation post stamps `superseded_at` on the prior non-superseded record of the same `method`, so a caller holding a stale view of the engagement's `approved_hours` would supersede a figure it never read; the guard forces a re-read first. Issuing an engagement agreement — `POST /engagements/{id}/agreements` — requires the same `expected_engagement_version` field, because re-issuing terms voids the prior non-executed agreement with `void_reason` set to `TERMS_REJECTED`, and a stale caller would destroy a negotiation round, counters included, that it never observed. Both mismatches are rejected with `ERR_STALE_ENGAGEMENT_VERSION` (HTTP 412).

### 2.8. Cycle Slots as a Conserved Capacity Ledger

Program capacity is a hard ledger, not an advisory number. For every program cycle, `slots_committed` plus `slots_reserved` plus `slots_available` always equals `capacity_slot_total`. `slots_committed` counts the engagements attributed to the cycle whose `work_status` is `NOT_STARTED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `SUBMITTED` or `ACCEPTED`; `slots_reserved` counts the match offers still in `OFFERED` status whose project request belongs to the cycle; `slots_available` is `capacity_slot_total` less both of those, floored at 0 so it never reads negative. Offers in `ACCEPTED`, `DECLINED`, `EXPIRED` or `WITHDRAWN` status hold nothing, and engagements in `HANDED_OFF` or `TERMINATED` have released their slot.

The conservation requirement is behavioural, not merely arithmetical: after a forward action and its compensating action, the ledger must read exactly what it read before. Acceptance of an offer moves one unit from `slots_reserved` to `slots_committed`; terminating the resulting engagement removes that unit from `slots_committed`. Once both have run, `slots_committed` and `slots_reserved` must both read their pre-acceptance values and `slots_available` must return to `capacity_slot_total` less whatever other live holds exist. No slot may be stranded by the cascades that ride along — sibling offer withdrawals, posture recomputation, milestone waivers — and no slot may be double-released.

### 2.9. Volunteer Hour Custody

Volunteer hours are ledgered the same way, one window at a time. For every availability window, `hours_committed` equals the sum of `reserved_hours` over the match offers still in `OFFERED` status that are held against that window, plus the sum of `committed_hours` over the engagements held against that window whose `work_status` is `NOT_STARTED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `SUBMITTED` or `ACCEPTED`, the total rounded to two decimals; and `hours_remaining` equals `hours_offered` less `hours_committed`, also to two decimals. Matching adds a hold, acceptance migrates that hold from the offer to the engagement without changing the window total, and an approved scope change adds to it.

Every release path must subtract exactly the amount that was added — including hours added later by an approved scope change. An engagement whose `committed_hours` grew from 40.00 to 52.00 through an approved change releases 52.00 when it terminates or hands off, never the original 40.00. Declining an offer, the expiry sweep, termination and handoff are all release paths, and each subtracts the current held amount rather than a remembered one.

### 2.10. Valuation Conservation and Restatement

Reported in-kind value is the third ledgered quantity. A volunteer's `lifetime_valued_usd` equals the sum of `total_amount_usd` over that volunteer's valuation records whose `method` is `INDEPENDENT_SECTOR` and whose `superseded_at` is still null, rounded to two decimals; records of the `MARKET_RATE` method never enter this total, and superseded records leave it the moment they are stamped. Posting an `INDEPENDENT_SECTOR` record is the forward action; the restatement cascade that runs when an engagement terminates is its compensation, stamping `superseded_at` on the prior record and posting a replacement record computed from the same approved hours. After a post followed by its restatement, `lifetime_valued_usd` must equal the sum over non-superseded `INDEPENDENT_SECTOR` records only — the superseded amount must not remain double-counted, and the restated record must not be omitted.

### 2.11. Nested Path Resolution

Paths are validated left to right, outermost segment first. A request naming a child under a parent that does not exist fails on the parent: a nested read or write under `/partner-orgs/{id}` yields `ERR_PARTNER_ORG_NOT_FOUND`, under `/program-cycles/{id}` yields `ERR_PROGRAM_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND`, under `/project-requests/{id}` yields `ERR_PROJECT_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND`, under `/scope-definitions/{id}` yields `ERR_SCOPE_DEFINITION_NOT_FOUND`, under `/volunteers/{id}` yields `ERR_VOLUNTEER_NOT_FOUND`, under `/match-offers/{id}` yields `ERR_MATCH_OFFER_NOT_FOUND`, under `/engagements/{id}` yields `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, under `/agreements/{id}` yields `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, under `/milestones/{id}` yields `ERR_MILESTONE_NOT_FOUND`, under `/scope-change-requests/{id}` yields `ERR_SCOPE_CHANGE_NOT_FOUND`, and under `/quality-reviews/{id}` yields `ERR_QUALITY_REVIEW_NOT_FOUND`. Every one of these is HTTP 404.

A child created under a parent takes its parent reference from the resolved path segment and never from the body: a milestone, time log, scope change request, review round, agreement, valuation record or satisfaction survey created beneath `/engagements/{id}` stores that engagement as its `engagement_id`, a deliverable created beneath `/scope-definitions/{id}` stores that revision as its `scope_definition_id`, and a skill profile or availability window created beneath `/volunteers/{id}` stores that volunteer as its `volunteer_id`.

Foreign keys supplied in a request body are resolved with the same vocabulary. On a project request create, an unresolvable `partner_org_id` yields `ERR_PARTNER_ORG_NOT_FOUND` and an unresolvable `cycle_id` yields `ERR_PROGRAM_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND`; a `supersedes_scope_id` naming no existing revision yields `ERR_SCOPE_DEFINITION_NOT_FOUND`; and a deliverable identifier inside a scoring payload that does not belong to the review's pinned scope revision yields `ERR_DELIVERABLE_NOT_FOUND`.

### 2.12. Rejection Precedence

When one request violates several rules at once, exactly one error is returned, and which one is fixed rather than incidental. Checks are evaluated in the following strict order, and the first failure short-circuits the request. The ordering is global: a check with a lower number always wins over a check with a higher number on the same endpoint.

1. `X-User-Role` and `X-User-ID` are both present and non-blank, on every endpoint — otherwise `ERR_MISSING_AUTH_HEADERS` (401).
2. `X-User-Role` is one of the five named roles and is listed among the target endpoint's authorized roles, on every endpoint — otherwise `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (403).
3. The `PartnerOrg` named by a path segment exists — checked on `GET /partner-orgs/{id}`, `GET /partner-orgs/{id}/summary`, `GET /partner-orgs/{id}/satisfaction-surveys` and `PATCH /partner-orgs/{id}/status` — otherwise `ERR_PARTNER_ORG_NOT_FOUND` (404).
4. The `ProgramCycle` named by a path segment exists — on `GET /program-cycles/{id}` and `POST /program-cycles/{id}/recalculate` — otherwise `ERR_PROGRAM_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND` (404).
5. The `ProjectRequest` named by a path segment exists — on `GET /project-requests/{id}`, `POST /project-requests/{id}/submit-for-scoping`, `POST /project-requests/{id}/scope-definitions`, `POST /project-requests/{id}/match`, `POST /project-requests/{id}/rescope` and `POST /project-requests/{id}/abandon` — otherwise `ERR_PROJECT_REQUEST_NOT_FOUND` (404).
6. The `ScopeDefinition` named by a path segment exists — on `GET /scope-definitions/{id}`, `GET /scope-definitions/{id}/deliverables`, `POST /scope-definitions/{id}/deliverables` and `POST /scope-definitions/{id}/approve` — otherwise `ERR_SCOPE_DEFINITION_NOT_FOUND` (404).
7. The `Volunteer` named by a path segment exists — on `GET /volunteers/{id}`, `GET /volunteers/{id}/skill-profiles`, `GET /volunteers/{id}/availability-windows`, `POST /volunteers/{id}/vet`, `PATCH /volunteers/{id}/status`, `POST /volunteers/{id}/skill-profiles` and `POST /volunteers/{id}/availability-windows` — otherwise `ERR_VOLUNTEER_NOT_FOUND` (404).
8. The `MatchOffer` named by a path segment exists — on `GET /match-offers/{id}`, `POST /match-offers/{id}/accept`, `POST /match-offers/{id}/decline` and `POST /match-offers/{id}/withdraw` — otherwise `ERR_MATCH_OFFER_NOT_FOUND` (404).
9. The `Engagement` named by a path segment exists — on `/engagements/{id}` and every sub-path beneath it — otherwise `ERR_ENGAGEMENT_NOT_FOUND` (404).
10. The `EngagementAgreement` named by a path segment exists — on `POST /agreements/{id}/send`, `POST /agreements/{id}/counter`, `POST /agreements/{id}/execute` and `POST /agreements/{id}/void` — otherwise `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND` (404).
11. The `Milestone` named by a path segment exists — on `GET /milestones/{id}`, `GET /milestones/{id}/time-logs` and `POST /milestones/{id}/hold` — otherwise `ERR_MILESTONE_NOT_FOUND` (404).
12. The `ScopeChangeRequest` named by a path segment exists — on `POST /scope-change-requests/{id}/decide` — otherwise `ERR_SCOPE_CHANGE_NOT_FOUND` (404).
13. The `QualityReview` named by a path segment exists — on `POST /quality-reviews/{id}/score` and `POST /quality-reviews/{id}/decide` — otherwise `ERR_QUALITY_REVIEW_NOT_FOUND` (404).
14. Every foreign key supplied in the request body resolves, as described in §2.11: on `POST /project-requests` an unresolvable `partner_org_id` yields `ERR_PARTNER_ORG_NOT_FOUND` (404) and an unresolvable `cycle_id` yields `ERR_PROGRAM_CYCLE_NOT_FOUND` (404), and on `POST /quality-reviews/{id}/score` a `deliverable_id` inside `deliverable_scores` that names no deliverable of the pinned scope revision yields `ERR_DELIVERABLE_NOT_FOUND` (404).
15. For the entity-backed roles, the caller owns the target resource — `ERR_NOT_RESOURCE_OWNER` (403) — on `POST /project-requests/{id}/submit-for-scoping`, `POST /scope-definitions/{id}/approve`, `POST /match-offers/{id}/accept`, `POST /match-offers/{id}/decline`, `POST /engagements/{id}/start`, `POST /engagements/{id}/submit`, `POST /engagements/{id}/time-logs`, `POST /engagements/{id}/scope-change-requests`, `POST /engagements/{id}/satisfaction-surveys`, `POST /agreements/{id}/counter`, `POST /agreements/{id}/execute`, `POST /volunteers/{id}/skill-profiles` and `POST /volunteers/{id}/availability-windows`.
16. Segregation of duties holds — `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403) — on `POST /engagements/{id}/quality-reviews` and `POST /quality-reviews/{id}/decide`.
17. The request body conforms to the endpoint's input schema — all required fields present, enum values inside their declared sets, numbers inside their declared ranges, datetimes ISO 8601 UTC — on every write endpoint, otherwise `ERR_VALIDATION` (422).
18. Pagination parameters are integers with `limit` between 1 and 100 inclusive and `offset` at least 0 — on `GET /project-requests`, `GET /scope-definitions`, `GET /match-offers` and `GET /engagements` — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (400).
19. Quantities that must be strictly positive are strictly positive — a time log's `hours`, a revision's `estimated_hours`, a window's `hours_offered` and a deliverable's `weight_pct` — on `POST /engagements/{id}/time-logs`, `POST /project-requests/{id}/scope-definitions`, `POST /volunteers/{id}/availability-windows` and `POST /scope-definitions/{id}/deliverables`. A value of exactly 0 is refused here with `ERR_ZERO_HOURS_NOT_ALLOWED` (422), before any capacity arithmetic runs.
20. The project request's `status` is in the from-set of the requested transition — on `POST /project-requests/{id}/submit-for-scoping`, `POST /project-requests/{id}/scope-definitions`, `POST /project-requests/{id}/match`, `POST /project-requests/{id}/rescope` and `POST /project-requests/{id}/abandon` — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST_STATE` (409).
21. The scope revision's `status` is `DRAFT` — on `POST /scope-definitions/{id}/approve` and `POST /scope-definitions/{id}/deliverables` — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_SCOPE_STATE` (409).
22. The match offer's `status` is `OFFERED` — on `POST /match-offers/{id}/accept`, `POST /match-offers/{id}/decline` and `POST /match-offers/{id}/withdraw` — otherwise `ERR_OFFER_NOT_OPEN` (409).
23. The engagement's `work_status` is in the from-set of the requested transition — start requires `NOT_STARTED`, submit requires `IN_PROGRESS`, time logging and scope-change creation require `IN_PROGRESS`, opening a quality review requires `SUBMITTED`, handoff requires `ACCEPTED`, and terminate requires a non-terminal value — on `POST /engagements/{id}/start`, `POST /engagements/{id}/submit`, `POST /engagements/{id}/time-logs`, `POST /engagements/{id}/scope-change-requests`, `POST /engagements/{id}/quality-reviews`, `POST /engagements/{id}/handoff`, `POST /engagements/{id}/terminate`, `POST /engagements/{id}/satisfaction-surveys` and `POST /milestones/{id}/hold`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_WORK_STATE` (409).
24. The engagement's `valuation_status` permits the requested stage — a `PROVISIONAL` post requires `work_status` of `IN_PROGRESS` or `SUBMITTED`, a `FINAL` post requires `HANDED_OFF`, and a `RESTATED` engagement accepts no further posts — on `POST /engagements/{id}/valuations`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_VALUATION_STATE` (409).
25. The agreement's `status` is in the from-set of the requested transition — send requires `DRAFTED` or `COUNTERED`, counter requires `SENT`, execute requires `SENT`, void requires `DRAFTED`, `SENT` or `COUNTERED` — on `POST /agreements/{id}/send`, `POST /agreements/{id}/counter`, `POST /agreements/{id}/execute` and `POST /agreements/{id}/void`, otherwise `ERR_INVALID_AGREEMENT_STATE` (409).
26. The milestone's `checkin_status` is `SCHEDULED` — on `POST /milestones/{id}/hold` — otherwise `ERR_MILESTONE_NOT_SCHEDULED` (409).
27. The quality review's `status` is `OPEN` for scoring and `SCORED` for deciding — on `POST /quality-reviews/{id}/score` and `POST /quality-reviews/{id}/decide` — otherwise `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_SCORED` (409).
28. The scope change request's `status` is `PENDING` — on `POST /scope-change-requests/{id}/decide` — otherwise `ERR_SCOPE_CHANGE_NOT_PENDING` (409).
29. `expected_project_request_version` equals the request's current `version` — on `POST /project-requests/{id}/scope-definitions` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_PROJECT_REQUEST_VERSION` (412).
30. `expected_engagement_version` equals the engagement's current `version` — on `POST /engagements/{id}/valuations` and `POST /engagements/{id}/agreements` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_ENGAGEMENT_VERSION` (412).
31. The owning partner's `status` is not `SUSPENDED` — on `POST /project-requests`, `POST /project-requests/{id}/submit-for-scoping`, `POST /scope-definitions/{id}/approve`, `POST /project-requests/{id}/match` and `POST /engagements/{id}/satisfaction-surveys` — otherwise `ERR_PARTNER_SUSPENDED` (403).
32. The volunteer's `credential_verified_at` is not null — on `POST /volunteers/{id}/availability-windows` and `POST /match-offers/{id}/accept` — otherwise `ERR_CREDENTIAL_NOT_VERIFIED` (422).
33. The volunteer's `status` is `ACTIVE` — on `POST /match-offers/{id}/accept`, `POST /engagements/{id}/start`, `POST /engagements/{id}/time-logs` and `POST /engagements/{id}/submit` — otherwise `ERR_VOLUNTEER_NOT_ACTIVE` (409).
34. The handoff payload carries `confidentiality_attested` set to true and, when the pinned revision's `confidentiality_tier` is `RESTRICTED`, also a non-empty `destruction_certificate_ref` — on `POST /engagements/{id}/handoff` — otherwise `ERR_CONFIDENTIALITY_ATTESTATION_MISSING` (422).
35. The agreement's `ip_disposition` equals the pinned revision's `ip_disposition`, and the agreement's `confidentiality_tier` is not weaker than the pinned revision's, under the ordering where `STANDARD` is weakest, `ELEVATED` sits above it and `RESTRICTED` is strongest — on `POST /agreements/{id}/execute` and `POST /engagements/{id}/handoff` — otherwise `ERR_IP_TERMS_MISMATCH` (409).
36. The engagement's agreement has `status` `EXECUTED` — on `POST /engagements/{id}/start` and `POST /engagements/{id}/time-logs` — otherwise `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_EXECUTED` (409).
37. `counter_round` is below 3 and the countering party differs from `last_countered_by` — on `POST /agreements/{id}/counter` — otherwise `ERR_COUNTER_LIMIT_EXCEEDED` (409).
38. The logical clock is strictly before the offer's `expires_at` — on `POST /match-offers/{id}/accept` and `POST /match-offers/{id}/decline` — otherwise `ERR_OFFER_EXPIRED` (409).
39. A new availability window has `ends_at` strictly after `starts_at` and its whole half-open interval lies inside some program cycle's half-open interval — on `POST /volunteers/{id}/availability-windows` — otherwise `ERR_WINDOW_OUTSIDE_CYCLE` (422).
40. A new milestone's `due_at` lies inside the pinned availability window's half-open interval and strictly after the engagement's `created_at` — on `POST /engagements/{id}/milestones` — otherwise `ERR_MILESTONE_DUE_OUTSIDE_ENGAGEMENT` (422).
41. A time log's `work_date` lies inside the pinned availability window's half-open interval — on `POST /engagements/{id}/time-logs` — otherwise `ERR_WINDOW_OUTSIDE_CYCLE` (422).
42. A requested clock instant is at or after the current logical clock — on `POST /_clock/set_current_utc` — otherwise `ERR_CLOCK_REGRESSION` (422).
43. A new availability window does not overlap any existing window of the same volunteer whose `status` is not `EXPIRED` — on `POST /volunteers/{id}/availability-windows` — otherwise `ERR_AVAILABILITY_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (409).
44. A new milestone's `due_at` is at least 72 hours away from the `due_at` of every sibling milestone whose `checkin_status` is `SCHEDULED` or `HELD` — on `POST /engagements/{id}/milestones` — otherwise `ERR_MILESTONE_SPACING_VIOLATION` (409).
45. The volunteer's rolling check-in saturation is not exceeded — on `POST /engagements/{id}/milestones` — otherwise `ERR_VOLUNTEER_CHECKIN_SATURATED` (409).
46. The pair of `volunteer_id` and `skill_code` is unique across skill profiles — on `POST /volunteers/{id}/skill-profiles` — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_SKILL_PROFILE` (409).
47. A deliverable's `sequence_no` is unique within its revision, and at approval time the revision's `deliverable_weight_total_pct` equals exactly 100.00 over non-`WAIVED` deliverables with `deliverable_count` of at least 1 — on `POST /scope-definitions/{id}/deliverables` and `POST /scope-definitions/{id}/approve` — otherwise `ERR_DELIVERABLE_WEIGHT_TOTAL_INVALID` (422).
48. The `deliverable_scores` set is exactly the set of non-`WAIVED` deliverables on the pinned revision, non-empty, with no extras and no omissions — on `POST /quality-reviews/{id}/score` — otherwise `ERR_DELIVERABLE_SET_MISMATCH` (422).
49. The `ScopeDefinition` pinned on the resource still has `status` `APPROVED` — on `POST /match-offers/{id}/accept`, `POST /engagements/{id}/handoff` and `POST /agreements/{id}/send` — otherwise `ERR_SCOPE_VERSION_SUPERSEDED` (409).
50. The request's `rescope_count` is below 3 when a rescope is requested or forced — on `POST /project-requests/{id}/rescope` — otherwise `ERR_RESCOPE_LIMIT_REACHED` (409).
51. The cycle's `posture` is not `FROZEN` — on `POST /project-requests/{id}/match` and `POST /match-offers/{id}/accept` — otherwise `ERR_CYCLE_CAPACITY_FROZEN` (409).
52. When the cycle's `posture` is `RESTRICTED`, the request's `urgency` is `ELEVATED` or `CRITICAL` — on `POST /project-requests/{id}/match` — otherwise `ERR_CYCLE_RESTRICTED_URGENCY` (409).
53. The partner's `open_engagement_count` is below its `max_concurrent_engagements` — on `POST /project-requests/{id}/match` and `POST /match-offers/{id}/accept` — otherwise `ERR_PARTNER_ENGAGEMENT_LIMIT` (409).
54. The volunteer's `open_engagement_count` is below 2 — on `POST /match-offers/{id}/accept` — otherwise `ERR_VOLUNTEER_CONCURRENCY_LIMIT` (409).
55. The pinned availability window's `hours_remaining` is greater than or equal to the hours being held or logged — `reserved_hours` at match time, `additional_hours` at scope-change approval, and a log's `hours` measured against `hours_offered` less the hours already approved on that window — on `POST /project-requests/{id}/match`, `POST /engagements/{id}/time-logs` and `POST /scope-change-requests/{id}/decide`, otherwise `ERR_AVAILABILITY_HOURS_EXCEEDED` (422).
56. The engagement's `creep_escalation_level` is not `BREACH` and no child scope change request sits in `PENDING` or `ESCALATED` — on `POST /engagements/{id}/time-logs` and `POST /engagements/{id}/submit` — otherwise `ERR_SCOPE_BREACH_THRESHOLD` (409).
57. The matching selector produced a non-empty candidate set — on `POST /project-requests/{id}/match` — otherwise `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_VOLUNTEER` (422).

Three collisions are worth calling out explicitly, because each pits a plausible-looking alternative against the ordering above. A 30.00-hour time log posted to an engagement already in `SUBMITTED` fails both the work-state check at position 23 and the window-hours check at position 55; the response must be `ERR_INVALID_WORK_STATE`. A handoff posted with `confidentiality_attested` false against an engagement whose pinned revision has moved to `SUPERSEDED` fails both the attestation check at position 34 and the pinned-revision check at position 49; the response must be `ERR_CONFIDENTIALITY_ATTESTATION_MISSING`. And an availability window that both extends past every cycle's `ends_at` and overlaps an existing window of the same volunteer fails both the containment check at position 39 and the overlap check at position 43; the response must be `ERR_WINDOW_OUTSIDE_CYCLE`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Partner Enrolment and Program Capacity Posture

A `ProgramManager` enrols a nonprofit partner by supplying its legal `name` (1 to 160 characters, required), a `contact_email` that must be unique across all partners, a mission `sector` drawn from `HEALTH`, `EDUCATION`, `HOUSING`, `ENVIRONMENT`, `ARTS` and `CIVIC`, and a self-reported `annual_budget_usd` of at least zero carried to two decimals. Two fields are derived from the budget at once and never supplied by the caller. The partner's `capacity_class` is `MICRO` for a budget below 250000.00, `SMALL` from 250000.00 up to but not including 2000000.00, `MID` from 2000000.00 up to but not including 10000000.00, and `LARGE` at 10000000.00 and above — each band floor is inclusive and each ceiling exclusive. The class implies the partner's `max_concurrent_engagements` ceiling directly: one for `MICRO`, two for `SMALL`, four for `MID` and six for `LARGE`. These two derivations are re-evaluated on registration and whenever a read surfaces the class, so they never drift from the budget on file.

A newly registered partner starts with `status` `ACTIVE`, a null `probation_reason`, a null `suspended_at`, a null `satisfaction_index`, a `satisfaction_sample_size` of 0 and an `open_engagement_count` of 0. The engagement count is a live figure, not a stored tally: it counts the partner's engagements whose `work_status` is `NOT_STARTED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `SUBMITTED` or `ACCEPTED`. A dedicated computed read on the partner exposes `capacity_class`, `max_concurrent_engagements`, `open_engagement_count`, `satisfaction_index`, `satisfaction_sample_size` and the per-survey `composite_score` figures that produced the index, which is the fastest way for program staff to see why a partner sits where it does.

Partner standing moves along three values — `ACTIVE`, `PROBATION` and `SUSPENDED`, with `SUSPENDED` terminal. A `ProgramManager` moves it explicitly by supplying a `to_status` and, when placing a partner on probation by hand, a `probation_reason` of `MANUAL` (the other reasons, `LOW_SATISFACTION` and `SCOPE_CREEP_ABUSE`, are set by the system). Suspension is deliberately violent, because a suspended partner must stop consuming program capacity immediately, and it runs identically whether the partner was `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` or `PROBATION → SUSPENDED`. The transition stamps `suspended_at` from the logical clock; every project request of that partner sitting in `DRAFT`, `SCOPING`, `SCOPED`, `MATCHING` or `RESCOPING` moves to `ABANDONED`; each abandoned request in turn moves its `OFFERED` match offers to `WITHDRAWN` with `withdraw_reason` set to `PARTNER_SUSPENDED`; each such withdrawal subtracts that offer's `reserved_hours` from the holding window's `hours_committed` and forces the cycle's `slots_reserved` to be recomputed; and every engagement of that partner whose `work_status` is `NOT_STARTED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `SUBMITTED` or `ACCEPTED` moves to `TERMINATED` with `termination_reason` set to `PARTNER_SUSPENDED`. While a partner is merely on `PROBATION`, by contrast, nothing in flight is disturbed at all — only the creation of new match offers is blocked.

Capacity itself lives on the program cycle. A `ProgramManager` opens one with a `cycle_code` unique across cycles, an inclusive `starts_at`, an exclusive `ends_at` that must be strictly after it, and a `capacity_slot_total` of at least one engagement slot. The cycle then maintains five derived figures continuously: `slots_committed`, `slots_reserved` and `slots_available` as defined in §2.8, a `utilization_ratio` equal to the sum of committed and reserved slots divided by `capacity_slot_total` and rounded to four decimals, and a `posture` that gates every child operation. `posture` is `OPEN` while utilization is below 0.8000, `RESTRICTED` from 0.8000 up to but not including 1.0000, and `FROZEN` at 1.0000 and above; both band floors are inclusive, so a utilization of exactly 0.8000 is `RESTRICTED` and exactly 1.0000 is `FROZEN`. A freshly opened cycle therefore reads a `utilization_ratio` of 0.0000, a `posture` of `OPEN`, and a `slots_available` equal to its `capacity_slot_total`. `RESTRICTED` narrows the gate rather than shutting it: only requests of `ELEVATED` or `CRITICAL` urgency may take a slot, while a `ROUTINE` request attempting to match is refused with `ERR_CYCLE_RESTRICTED_URGENCY`. `FROZEN` shuts it entirely with `ERR_CYCLE_CAPACITY_FROZEN` and, as §3.5 describes, additionally sweeps out every offer the cycle was still holding. The posture calculation reruns whenever a match offer is created, whenever an offer leaves `OFFERED`, whenever an engagement enters or leaves the four live `work_status` values, and on every operations checkpoint.

That checkpoint is the program's clock-driven housekeeping run, invoked per cycle by a `ProgramManager`, and it is the only place where the passage of time produces state changes. It does three things in order. First it sweeps expiry: every match offer in the cycle still in `OFFERED` whose `expires_at` is at or before the logical clock moves to `EXPIRED`, releasing its reserved hours and its reserved slot and incrementing the owning request's `failed_match_rounds`, with each increment advancing that request to `SCOPED`, `RESCOPING` or `ABANDONED` exactly as §3.5 prescribes. Then it marks neglect: every milestone still `SCHEDULED` on a live engagement of the cycle whose `due_at` plus twenty-four hours has fallen behind the clock moves to `MISSED`, raising that engagement's `missed_checkin_count` and rerunning the volunteer's reliability figures. Finally it recomputes the capacity figures and `posture`, stamps `last_recalculated_at` from the clock, and advances `recalc_epoch` by exactly one — the epoch counter starts at 0 and exists so operators can tell one checkpoint's output from the next. A companion read lists every engagement occupying a slot in the cycle, terminal ones included.

### 3.2. The Volunteer Roster: Vetting, Skills and Availability

Volunteers are enrolled by a `ProgramManager` with a `full_name` of 1 to 120 characters, a unique `email`, a self-declared `professional_rate_usd_per_hour` that must be above zero and no greater than 2000.00, a `seniority` of `ASSOCIATE`, `SENIOR`, `PRINCIPAL` or `PARTNER`, and a `weekly_hours_cap` between 1 and 40 hours. Enrolment lands the volunteer in `status` `PENDING_VETTING` with `credential_verified_at` null, both `reliability_score` and `reliability_score_lifetime` seeded at the 0.7500 baseline, `lifetime_valued_usd` at 0.00, and `terminal_engagement_count` and `open_engagement_count` at 0. The two engagement counts are live: the terminal count covers engagements whose `work_status` is `HANDED_OFF` or `TERMINATED`, and the open count covers those in `NOT_STARTED`, `IN_PROGRESS` or `SUBMITTED`.

Vetting is a single `ProgramManager` action that stamps `credential_verified_at` from the logical clock, flips `status` from `PENDING_VETTING` to `ACTIVE`, and optionally accepts a `verify_skill_codes` list; each skill claim whose `skill_code` appears on that list has its `verified` flag set to true and its `verified_at` stamped, which is what later earns that claim a bonus in the matching score. An unvetted volunteer cannot publish availability and cannot accept an offer: both attempts are refused with `ERR_CREDENTIAL_NOT_VERIFIED`.

A volunteer declares her own skills, one claim per row, supplying a `skill_code` in upper-case letters, digits and underscores between 2 and 32 characters, a self-rated `proficiency` from 1 to 5, and `years_experience` between 0 and 60 carried to one decimal. The pair of the owning `volunteer_id` and the `skill_code` must be unique — a second claim on the same code is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_SKILL_PROFILE` — and `verified` defaults to false until a vetting run names that code.

Availability is published as half-open windows against the volunteer's own record. Each window carries `starts_at`, an `ends_at` strictly after it, a `window_kind` of `HARD` (the volunteer's guaranteed capacity) or `SOFT` (best-effort), and an `hours_offered` budget above zero and no greater than 400.00. The window's own ledger fields start at `hours_committed` of 0.00 and `hours_remaining` equal to `hours_offered`, and its `status` is derived rather than set: `OPEN` while `hours_remaining` is above zero and the clock is still before `ends_at`, `EXHAUSTED` once `hours_remaining` reaches exactly zero, and `EXPIRED` once the clock reaches or passes `ends_at`. Two geometric rules guard publication. The whole interval must sit inside some program cycle's interval, and a malformed interval or one that escapes every cycle is refused with `ERR_WINDOW_OUTSIDE_CYCLE`. And no two windows of the same volunteer whose `status` is not `EXPIRED` may overlap: overlap is judged on half-open geometry, so a new window is refused with `ERR_AVAILABILITY_WINDOW_OVERLAP` when its `starts_at` falls before an existing window's `ends_at` while that existing window's `starts_at` falls before the new one's `ends_at`. Exact adjacency — a new window starting at the precise instant an existing one ends — is permitted, and no buffer and no aggregate cap on total offered hours participate in this check.

Roster standing spans `PENDING_VETTING`, `ACTIVE`, `PAUSED` and `DEACTIVATED`, and a `ProgramManager` can move a volunteer among the last three by supplying a `to_status` with an optional `pause_reason` of `RELIABILITY_FLOOR`, `SELF_REQUESTED` or `ADMIN`. Any move into `PAUSED` or `DEACTIVATED` immediately withdraws every match offer that volunteer still holds in `OFFERED` status, each withdrawal carrying `withdraw_reason` `VOLUNTEER_PAUSED` and releasing both the window hours and the cycle slot it was holding. A volunteer who is not `ACTIVE` cannot accept an offer, start work, log hours or submit: those are refused with `ERR_VOLUNTEER_NOT_ACTIVE`.

**Reliability** is the roster's health metric, and the program keeps two versions of it that deliberately disagree. Both are computed from the same population — only the volunteer's engagements that reached `HANDED_OFF` or `TERMINATED` count, so engagements still in `NOT_STARTED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `SUBMITTED` or `ACCEPTED` are invisible to both, as are offers that merely expired. Each terminal engagement earns a point value before penalties: 1.00 when it was `HANDED_OFF` with a `quality_gate` of `PASSED`; 0.70 when it was `HANDED_OFF` with a `quality_gate` of `CONDITIONAL`; 0.00 when it was `TERMINATED` for `QUALITY_FAILURE`; 0.25 when it was `TERMINATED` for `SCOPE_BREACH` or `ADMIN`; and 0.60 when it was `TERMINATED` for `AGREEMENT_VOIDED` or `PARTNER_SUSPENDED`. From that value the program subtracts one tenth for each missed check-in the engagement recorded — the engagement's `missed_checkin_count` multiplied by 0.10 — rounds the result to four decimals, and floors it at zero so no single engagement can contribute a negative amount. Engagements are ordered most-recent-first by the instant they went terminal, which is the engagement's `handoff_at` when present and otherwise its `terminated_at`, with the engagement's own `created_at` descending as the tie-break; row identifiers never participate in the ordering.

The gating figure, `reliability_score`, uses the decay-weighted variant: it takes only the eight most recent terminal engagements in that order (or all of them when the volunteer has fewer), gives the engagement at rank zero a weight of 1, the next a weight of 0.85, the next 0.85 multiplied by itself, and so on — each rank's weight is 0.85 raised to that zero-based rank and rounded to six decimals — then divides the weighted sum of point values by the sum of the weights actually used and rounds to four decimals. Because of that decay an old failure is nearly invisible while a recent one dominates. The reporting figure, `reliability_score_lifetime`, uses the unweighted lifetime variant: it averages the point values of every terminal engagement ever, however many there are, and rounds to four decimals. The two fields therefore diverge as soon as a volunteer has more than one terminal engagement with differing outcomes. A volunteer with no terminal engagement at all reads 0.7500 in both fields. Both figures are rewritten whenever an engagement reaches `HANDED_OFF`, whenever an engagement reaches `TERMINATED`, and whenever a milestone is marked `MISSED`. One threshold consequence hangs off the decay-weighted figure alone: if `reliability_score` falls strictly below 0.4000 while the volunteer's `status` is `ACTIVE`, the volunteer moves to `PAUSED` with `pause_reason` `RELIABILITY_FLOOR`, dragging the offer-withdrawal cascade above with it. The comparison is strict — a score of exactly 0.4000 does not pause anyone.

### 3.3. From Project Request to an Approved Scope

A `PartnerContact` files a project request against a specific program cycle, supplying the owning `partner_org_id`, the `cycle_id` whose capacity the work will compete for, a `title` of 1 to 160 characters, a `discipline` drawn from `LEGAL`, `ACCOUNTING`, `TECHNOLOGY`, `MARKETING`, `HR` and `STRATEGY`, the exact `required_skill_code` a candidate volunteer must hold, a `min_proficiency` from 1 to 5 that a candidate must meet or beat, and a `requested_hours` figure above zero to two decimals expressing what the partner believes the work needs. An `urgency` may also be supplied — `ROUTINE`, `ELEVATED` or `CRITICAL` — and defaults to `ROUTINE` when omitted; urgency later drives both the offer's time-to-live and the weighting inside the matching score. The request is created in `status` `DRAFT` with `rescope_count` 0, `failed_match_rounds` 0, `active_scope_revision_no` 0 and a null `closed_at`. Filing against a suspended partner is refused with `ERR_PARTNER_SUSPENDED`.

The partner hands the work to the scoping team by moving the request `DRAFT → SCOPING`. Three things must hold: the caller must own the partner org the request belongs to, that partner's `status` must be `ACTIVE` (probation is not enough here), and the logical clock must still be strictly before the target cycle's `ends_at`. Nothing cascades from this transition beyond the request's own `updated_at` and `version`.

Scoping produces a chain of revisions rather than a single document. A `ScopingSpecialist` drafts one by supplying the mandatory `expected_project_request_version` described in §2.7, an `estimated_hours` figure above zero and no greater than 400.00, a `complexity_factor` between 1.00 and 2.50 that will multiply those hours when the commitment is made, the `confidentiality_tier` the deliverable falls under (`STANDARD`, `ELEVATED` or `RESTRICTED`), and the intended `ip_disposition` for the produced work (`ASSIGN_TO_PARTNER`, `LICENSE_PERPETUAL` or `RETAINED_BY_VOLUNTEER`). An optional `supersedes_scope_id` names the prior revision this one replaces and is null on the first revision of a request. The new row is created in `status` `DRAFT`; its `revision_no` is one greater than the highest `revision_no` among its siblings on the same request, so the first is 1; its `authored_by_user_id` records the drafting specialist's own `X-User-ID`, which is the value the segregation-of-duties barrier later reads; its `deliverable_count` and `deliverable_weight_total_pct` both start at 0. Any sibling revision still sitting in `DRAFT` moves to `WITHDRAWN` at once, and the parent request's `version` advances by one. A withdrawn revision's child deliverables remain readable forever but can never be scored again.

A revision is only meaningful once its deliverable set is complete. The same `ScopingSpecialist` adds deliverables to a `DRAFT` revision one at a time, each carrying a `title` of 1 to 120 characters, a `sequence_no` of at least 1 that must be unique within the revision and that becomes the primary sort key for quality scoring, a `weight_pct` between 0.01 and 100.00 expressing the item's share of the whole, an `hours_estimate` above zero, and an `acceptance_criteria` prose bar of 1 to 500 characters that the quality reviewer will judge against. Each deliverable begins with `review_status` `PENDING`, a null `review_score` and a null `waived_reason`; the reasons a deliverable can later be excluded from scoring are `DESCOPED_BY_CHANGE` and `PARTNER_WITHDRAWN`, and `waived_reason` stays null unless `review_status` is `WAIVED`. Every addition recomputes the revision's `deliverable_count`, a straight count of child rows, and its `deliverable_weight_total_pct`, the sum of `weight_pct` over children whose `review_status` is not `WAIVED`. An addition that would push that total past 100.00 is refused with `ERR_DELIVERABLE_WEIGHT_TOTAL_INVALID`, as is a duplicate `sequence_no` within the revision.

Approval belongs to the partner, not to the scoper — this is the program's first segregation point. A `PartnerContact` who owns the request approves a revision, and the approval requires the revision to be in `DRAFT` and to belong to that request, the request itself to be in `SCOPING`, the revision's `deliverable_count` to be at least 1, its `deliverable_weight_total_pct` to equal exactly 100.00, and the partner's `status` to be either `ACTIVE` or `PROBATION`. A weight total that is not exactly 100.00 at this moment is refused with `ERR_DELIVERABLE_WEIGHT_TOTAL_INVALID`; approving a revision that is not in `DRAFT` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_SCOPE_STATE`. On success the revision moves `DRAFT → APPROVED` with `approved_at` stamped from the logical clock and `approved_by_user_id` recording the approving contact's identifier, any sibling revision that was already `APPROVED` moves to `SUPERSEDED`, the request moves `SCOPING → SCOPED`, and the request's `active_scope_revision_no` is recomputed to the `revision_no` of the newly approved revision. That field reads 0 whenever no revision of the request is `APPROVED`.

Revisions are superseded, not deleted. A revision moves `APPROVED → SUPERSEDED` when a newer sibling reaches `APPROVED`, when the request enters `RESCOPING`, or when the request is abandoned out of `MATCHING`; each time, the request's `active_scope_revision_no` is recomputed, and any engagement already pinned to the superseded revision keeps pointing at it rather than being re-pointed at the newer one. That pinning is why a superseded revision can still block a later operation: a write that references a revision whose `status` has become `SUPERSEDED` or `WITHDRAWN`, or whose `revision_no` sits below the request's `active_scope_revision_no`, is refused with `ERR_SCOPE_VERSION_SUPERSEDED`.

Closing a request without delivery is a `ProgramManager` action carrying an optional `reason`, available while the request is in `DRAFT`, `SCOPING`, `SCOPED`, `MATCHING` or `RESCOPING`; from `CLOSED` or `ABANDONED` it is refused with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST_STATE`. In every case the request moves to `ABANDONED` and `closed_at` is stamped from the logical clock. Abandonment from `DRAFT` or `MATCHING` additionally releases any offer still in `OFFERED` exactly as an offer release does — window hours back, reserved slot back. Abandonment from `MATCHING` additionally moves the request's `APPROVED` revision, when it has one, to `SUPERSEDED`, so `active_scope_revision_no` falls back to 0; the request's `DRAFT` revisions, if any, are left where they are. Abandonment from `SCOPING` or `SCOPED` additionally moves every scope revision of the request whose `status` is `DRAFT` or `APPROVED` to `WITHDRAWN`, and when the withdrawn revision was the approved one, `active_scope_revision_no` resets to 0. Abandonment from `RESCOPING` stamps `closed_at` and nothing further, because the offers and revisions were already released when the request entered that state.

### 3.4. Matching: The Selector, the Offer and the Reservation

Matching is a `ScopingSpecialist` action on a `SCOPED` request, and it moves the request `SCOPED → MATCHING` while producing exactly one offer for exactly one volunteer. Before the selector runs, five gates are checked: the request's `active_scope_revision_no` must be at least 1, the cycle's `posture` must not be `FROZEN`, a `RESTRICTED` posture demands that the request's `urgency` be `ELEVATED` or `CRITICAL`, the partner's `status` must be `ACTIVE`, and the partner's `open_engagement_count` must be strictly below its `max_concurrent_engagements` — a partner already at its ceiling is refused with `ERR_PARTNER_ENGAGEMENT_LIMIT`.

**Eligibility.** The candidate pool is formed from pairs of a volunteer and one of that volunteer's skill claims, and every one of the following must hold for the pair to survive. The claim's `skill_code` must equal the request's `required_skill_code` exactly, as a string — no prefix, fuzzy or case-insensitive matching. The claim's `proficiency` must be greater than or equal to the request's `min_proficiency`. The volunteer's `status` must be `ACTIVE` and her `credential_verified_at` must not be null. Her `open_engagement_count` must be strictly below 2, the program-wide ceiling on concurrent engagements per volunteer. She must not appear as the `volunteer_id` of any match offer already raised on this same request whose `status` is `OFFERED`, `DECLINED`, `EXPIRED` or `WITHDRAWN` — this is the retry exclusion, and it means a later round never re-offers to someone an earlier round already approached. And she must own at least one availability window that covers the current logical clock under half-open geometry, meaning the window's `starts_at` is at or before the clock while the clock is strictly before its `ends_at`, and whose `hours_offered` minus `hours_committed` is strictly greater than zero. If nothing survives this filter, no offer is created: the request stays in `SCOPED`, its `failed_match_rounds` rises by one, and the call answers `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_VOLUNTEER`.

**Window selection.** The hours the engagement would need — call them the required hours — are the pinned revision's `estimated_hours` multiplied by its `complexity_factor`, rounded to two decimals. Each surviving candidate then contributes exactly one window: order that volunteer's eligible windows by remaining hours descending, break ties by `window_kind` with `HARD` ahead of `SOFT`, break remaining ties by `created_at` ascending, and take the first row. Row identifiers never break a tie anywhere in this selector.

**Scoring one candidate.** The skill component starts from a tiered lookup on the claim's `proficiency` — 0.20 at proficiency 1, 0.45 at 2, 0.70 at 3, 0.88 at 4 and 1.00 at 5 — plus a flat bonus of 0.05 when the claim's `verified` flag is true and nothing when it is false, with the sum capped at 1.00. The window's remaining hours are its `hours_offered` less its `hours_committed`, rounded to two decimals. The availability component is those remaining hours divided by the required hours, rounded to four decimals, and then capped at 1.00 — so a window that more than covers the work scores a full 1.00 rather than something above it. Shortfall is the required hours less the window's remaining hours, rounded to two decimals and floored at 0.00, so a covering window has no shortfall at all. The penalty is that shortfall multiplied by 0.004 per hour, rounded to four decimals, and capped at 0.30. The weight on availability is chosen by the request's `urgency` — 0.20 for `ROUTINE`, 0.30 for `ELEVATED` and 0.45 for `CRITICAL` — while the weight on reliability is always 0.25, and the weight on skill is whatever remains: 1.00 less the availability weight less 0.25, rounded to four decimals. The raw score is the skill weight times the skill component, plus the availability weight times the availability component, plus 0.25 times the volunteer's `reliability_score`, rounded to six decimals. Subtract the penalty from that raw score, floor the result at 0.0000, multiply by a seniority multiplier — 0.90 for `ASSOCIATE`, 1.00 for `SENIOR`, 1.08 for `PRINCIPAL` and 1.12 for `PARTNER` — and round to four decimals. That final figure is the candidate's score, and it therefore lives between 0.0000 and 1.2000. Rounding occurs exactly at the five points named here and nowhere else.

**Ranking.** Candidates are ranked by score descending; ties are broken first by the volunteer's `reliability_score` descending and then by the volunteer's `created_at` ascending. The top-ranked candidate wins, and her score is persisted on the resulting offer as `match_score`.

**The offer.** A winning selection writes a match offer in `status` `OFFERED` against the request, recording the winning `volunteer_id`, the `scope_definition_id` of the `APPROVED` revision pinned at this instant, the `availability_window_id` of the chosen window, and a `round_no` equal to the request's `failed_match_rounds` plus one at offer time — so the first offer on a request is round 1. Its `reserved_hours` is the required-hours figure computed above, its `offered_at` is the logical clock, and its `expires_at` is `offered_at` plus a time-to-live chosen by the request's `urgency`: five days for a `ROUTINE` request, three days for an `ELEVATED` one, and a single day for a `CRITICAL` one. `responded_at`, `decline_reason` and `withdraw_reason` all start null. Two ledger movements accompany the offer: the chosen window's `hours_committed` rises by the offer's `reserved_hours`, and the cycle's `slots_reserved` rises by one, after which the posture calculation reruns and may carry the cycle from `OPEN` to `RESTRICTED` or on to `FROZEN`. A window whose `hours_remaining` cannot cover the reservation is refused with `ERR_AVAILABILITY_HOURS_EXCEEDED`.

### 3.5. Offer Outcomes: Acceptance, Refusal and the Re-Scoping Loop

An offer has exactly four terminal outcomes — `ACCEPTED`, `DECLINED`, `EXPIRED` and `WITHDRAWN` — and their side effects deliberately differ. Any of the three volunteer- or staff-driven responses aimed at an offer that is no longer in `OFFERED` is refused with `ERR_OFFER_NOT_OPEN`.

**Acceptance.** The offered volunteer accepts, and the offer moves `OFFERED → ACCEPTED` while the request moves `MATCHING → ENGAGED`. Acceptance is the most heavily guarded operation in the program: the caller must be the volunteer named on the offer, the logical clock must be strictly before the offer's `expires_at` (an arrival at or after that instant is refused with `ERR_OFFER_EXPIRED`), the volunteer's `status` must be `ACTIVE` and her `credential_verified_at` non-null, her `open_engagement_count` must be strictly below 2, the revision pinned on the offer must still be `APPROVED`, the cycle's `posture` must not be `FROZEN`, the owning partner's `status` must be `ACTIVE` or `PROBATION`, and the partner's `open_engagement_count` must still be below its `max_concurrent_engagements`.

On success `responded_at` is stamped and an engagement is created, and it is worth walking the resulting cascade in order because each step is caused by the one before it. **Step one:** the new engagement is written with `work_status` `NOT_STARTED` and `valuation_status` `UNVALUED`, its `match_offer_id` pointing at the accepted offer, its `project_request_id`, `volunteer_id`, `cycle_id` and `availability_window_id` copied from the offer and its request, its `scope_definition_id` pinned to the offer's revision and never re-pointed afterwards, and its `committed_hours` seeded from the offer's `reserved_hours`. The remaining engagement fields start at their defaults: `approved_hours` 0.00, `scope_creep_pct` 0, `creep_escalation_level` `NONE`, `quality_gate` `PENDING`, `confidentiality_attested` false, `handoff_artifact_count` 0, `missed_checkin_count` 0, and null `handoff_at`, `terminated_at` and `termination_reason`. The request moves to `ENGAGED` and its `version` advances by one. **Step two:** because the request became `ENGAGED`, the cycle's `slots_reserved` falls by one while `slots_committed` rises by one, and the posture calculation reruns — which may set `posture` to `FROZEN`. **Step three:** because the posture became `FROZEN`, every *other* offer still in `OFFERED` whose request belongs to that same cycle moves to `WITHDRAWN` with `withdraw_reason` `CYCLE_FROZEN`. **Step four:** because those offers were withdrawn, each one's holding window has that offer's `reserved_hours` subtracted from its `hours_committed`, and each affected request's `failed_match_rounds` rises by one — which drives any request that has now used up its match rounds into `RESCOPING`. Independently of the freeze, any sibling offer still in `OFFERED` on the *same* request moves to `WITHDRAWN` with `withdraw_reason` `ADMIN` and releases its reserved hours; an `ADMIN` withdrawal caused by a sibling acceptance is the one withdrawal that does not raise `failed_match_rounds`, because the request was not failed by it.

**Refusal by the volunteer.** A decline requires a `decline_reason` of `SCOPE_TOO_LARGE`, `SCHEDULE_CONFLICT`, `OUTSIDE_EXPERTISE` or `IP_TERMS_UNACCEPTABLE`, and is only accepted while the clock is still strictly before `expires_at`. The offer moves `OFFERED → DECLINED` with `responded_at` and the reason stamped, the holding window's `hours_committed` drops by the offer's `reserved_hours`, the cycle's `slots_reserved` drops by one and the posture recomputes, and the request's `failed_match_rounds` rises by one before the request advances as described below. One reason carries an extra consequence that no other outcome shares: a decline for `IP_TERMS_UNACCEPTABLE` bars that volunteer from every later matching round on that request, over and above the ordinary retry exclusion.

**Withdrawal by staff.** A `ScopingSpecialist` may pull back an open offer with an optional `withdraw_reason`; the system sets that reason itself when the withdrawal comes from a cascade — `CYCLE_FROZEN`, `RESCOPED`, `PARTNER_SUSPENDED`, `VOLUNTEER_PAUSED` or `ADMIN`. The offer moves `OFFERED → WITHDRAWN` with `responded_at` and the reason stamped, and the same hour and slot releases run. The difference from a decline is conditional: `failed_match_rounds` rises only when `withdraw_reason` is something other than `ADMIN`.

**Expiry.** The cycle checkpoint described in §3.1 moves an offer `OFFERED → EXPIRED` once the logical clock has reached or passed its `expires_at`. `responded_at` is stamped from the clock, the window hours and the reserved slot are released, and `failed_match_rounds` rises by one. Expiry is the only terminal outcome that leaves the volunteer's reliability inputs entirely untouched — the program does not attribute an unanswered offer to the volunteer.

**Where the request goes next.** Every release of an `OFFERED` offer — decline, withdrawal or expiry — resolves the request out of `MATCHING` along exactly one of three branches, decided by the counters. If the request's `failed_match_rounds` plus one is still below 2, the request returns to `SCOPED` (`MATCHING → SCOPED`) and the specialist may run the selector again, which will now skip every volunteer already approached. If `failed_match_rounds` plus one has reached 2 while `rescope_count` is still below 3, the request moves `MATCHING → RESCOPING`: the same offer-release effects run, `failed_match_rounds` is incremented and then reset to 0, `rescope_count` rises by one, the currently `APPROVED` revision moves to `SUPERSEDED`, and `active_scope_revision_no` resets to 0. If `failed_match_rounds` plus one has reached 2 and `rescope_count` has already reached 3, there is nowhere left to go: the request moves `MATCHING → ABANDONED`, the same releases run, `closed_at` is stamped and the `APPROVED` revision moves to `SUPERSEDED`.

Re-scoping closes the loop. A `ScopingSpecialist` restarts scoping on a request sitting in `RESCOPING`, moving it `RESCOPING → SCOPING` so that a fresh revision can be drafted, approved and matched again — the full `SCOPING → SCOPED → MATCHING` sequence runs a second time on new terms. The restart is permitted only while `rescope_count` has not passed 3; a request whose `rescope_count` has exhausted the allowance is refused with `ERR_RESCOPE_LIMIT_REACHED` and must be abandoned instead. Restarting also cleans up dangling check-ins: every milestone still `SCHEDULED` on any live engagement of that request moves `SCHEDULED → WAIVED` with `waiver_reason` `RESCOPED`, and a waived check-in has no effect at all on the volunteer's reliability.

### 3.6. Negotiating IP and Confidentiality Terms

An engagement created by acceptance cannot begin work until its terms are executed. A `ProgramManager` issues the terms document against the engagement, supplying the mandatory `expected_engagement_version` from §2.7 together with an `ip_disposition`, a `confidentiality_tier` and an `indemnity_cap_usd` that must be at least 0 and no greater than 100000.00 to two decimals. The two enumerated terms are seeded from the pinned revision's own `ip_disposition` and `confidentiality_tier`, and departing from them has consequences described below. The document is created with `status` `DRAFTED`, `counter_round` 0, `terms_revision_no` 1, a null `last_countered_by`, and null `volunteer_signed_at` and `partner_signed_at`. Because re-issuing is legitimate, any prior agreement on the same engagement whose `status` is `DRAFTED`, `SENT` or `COUNTERED` moves to `VOIDED` with `void_reason` `TERMS_REJECTED`, and the engagement's `version` advances by one.

Terms circulate through a negotiation cycle. The `ProgramManager` sends the current revision for signature, moving the agreement `DRAFTED → SENT`; sending requires the engagement to still be in `NOT_STARTED` and the pinned revision to still be `APPROVED`, and it clears both `volunteer_signed_at` and `partner_signed_at` to null. Either party — the engaged `Volunteer` or the owning `PartnerContact` — may counter a `SENT` agreement, submitting replacement values for `ip_disposition`, `confidentiality_tier` and `indemnity_cap_usd`. A counter moves the agreement `SENT → COUNTERED`, raises `counter_round` by one, sets `terms_revision_no` to `counter_round` plus one, overwrites the three negotiated fields with the countered values, clears both signature timestamps to null so that nothing signed against the old revision survives, and records `last_countered_by` as `VOLUNTEER` or `PARTNER` according to who countered. Two conditions guard it: `counter_round` must be below 3, and the countering party must differ from `last_countered_by`, so the same side cannot counter twice in a row. A violation of either is refused with `ERR_COUNTER_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. The manager then re-sends, moving the agreement `COUNTERED → SENT` — permitted while `counter_round` has not passed 3 — again clearing both signature slots, and the cycle `SENT → COUNTERED → SENT` may run up to three times in total.

Exhausting the allowance ends the engagement rather than the negotiation. A counter attempted while `counter_round` already stands at 3 does not simply fail quietly: the agreement moves `COUNTERED → VOIDED` with `void_reason` `COUNTER_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, and the engagement is terminated with `termination_reason` `AGREEMENT_VOIDED`, running the full release chain described in §3.9.

Execution is a two-sided act on a `SENT` agreement. Each party calls it once for itself; the caller's own signature slot must currently be null, and the transition to `EXECUTED` only happens when the *other* party's slot is already stamped for the current `terms_revision_no`. In practice the first call stamps one slot — `volunteer_signed_at` for the volunteer, `partner_signed_at` for the partner contact — and leaves the agreement in `SENT`; the countersign by the other party stamps the second slot and completes `SENT → EXECUTED`. Execution also enforces the terms alignment that the whole negotiation exists to protect: the agreement's `confidentiality_tier` may not be weaker than the pinned revision's, under the ordering in which `STANDARD` is weakest, `ELEVATED` sits above it and `RESTRICTED` is strongest, and the agreement's `ip_disposition` must equal the pinned revision's exactly. A divergence is refused with `ERR_IP_TERMS_MISMATCH`. Attempting any of these moves from a status outside its declared from-set — sending from `EXECUTED`, countering something not `SENT`, signing something not `SENT`, voiding something already `EXECUTED` or `VOIDED` — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_AGREEMENT_STATE`.

A `ProgramManager` may also void a non-executed agreement outright with an optional `void_reason`, along `DRAFTED → VOIDED`, `SENT → VOIDED` or `COUNTERED → VOIDED`. A direct void records `void_reason` `TERMS_REJECTED` and terminates the engagement it governed with `termination_reason` `AGREEMENT_VOIDED`; a void that arrives as a cascade from an engagement termination records `void_reason` `ENGAGEMENT_TERMINATED` instead and terminates nothing further, since the engagement is already gone. An engagement whose agreement has not reached `EXECUTED` cannot be started and cannot receive time logs; both are refused with `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_EXECUTED`. Agreement rows of an engagement can be listed newest-first, which for a live engagement means at most one active document plus any voided predecessors.

### 3.7. Delivery: Check-ins, Hours and Scope Creep Control

Work begins when the engaged volunteer starts the engagement, moving its `work_status` `NOT_STARTED → IN_PROGRESS` and carrying the parent request `ENGAGED → IN_DELIVERY`. Starting requires the caller to be the engagement's own volunteer, the governing agreement to be `EXECUTED`, the volunteer's `status` to be `ACTIVE`, and the owning partner's `status` to be `ACTIVE` or `PROBATION`. The agreement gate is the point of the whole negotiation: no hour of donated work may be performed before the IP and confidentiality terms are executed.

Hours are recorded by the volunteer as individual log lines against an `IN_PROGRESS` engagement, each carrying a `work_date` naming the instant the work was performed, an `hours` figure above zero and no greater than 12.00 to two decimals, and an `activity_class` of `DELIVERY`, `ADMIN` or `TRAVEL`. A log's `work_date` must fall inside the engagement's pinned availability window under half-open geometry, and a log dated outside it is refused with `ERR_WINDOW_OUTSIDE_CYCLE`. Each line is stored with `approved` false, a null `approved_at` and a null `milestone_id`, a `rate_basis_usd` snapshotting the volunteer's `professional_rate_usd_per_hour` as it stood at log creation, and a `logged_by_user_id` recording the submitting volunteer. Logging recomputes the creep figures, though unapproved hours do not yet move them. The engagement's `approved_hours` is the sum of `hours` over its child logs whose `approved` flag is true, across every activity class without exception, rounded to two decimals.

Check-ins are scheduled by a `ProgramManager`, who supplies only a `due_at`; the milestone is created in `checkin_status` `SCHEDULED` with a `sequence_no` one greater than the highest among its siblings, and with null `held_at`, `reported_cumulative_hours`, `variance_hours` and `waiver_reason` and `escalation_raised` false. Three geometric rules guard scheduling. The `due_at` must fall inside the engagement's pinned availability window and be strictly after the engagement's own `created_at`, or the request is refused with `ERR_MILESTONE_DUE_OUTSIDE_ENGAGEMENT`. It must sit at least three days away from the `due_at` of every sibling milestone whose `checkin_status` is `SCHEDULED` or `HELD` — the buffer is 72 hours and its geometry is closed, so a separation of exactly 72 hours is accepted while anything strictly closer is refused with `ERR_MILESTONE_SPACING_VIOLATION`. And the volunteer must not be saturated across her whole portfolio: counting milestones with `checkin_status` `SCHEDULED` across *all* engagements of that volunteer whose `due_at` falls within the closed fortnight running from seven days before the new `due_at` to seven days after it, at most two may already exist before the new row is added, so the third such check-in in any rolling fourteen-day span is refused with `ERR_VOLUNTEER_CHECKIN_SATURATED`.

Holding a check-in is the program's principal accounting event and it belongs to the `ProgramManager`, who supplies the `reported_cumulative_hours` the volunteer claims as of that check-in, a figure that must be at least zero. The engagement must be `IN_PROGRESS`, the milestone must still be `SCHEDULED`, and the clock must have reached at least twenty-four hours before the `due_at` — the grace band around a check-in is 24 hours on each side, so a hold may happen a day early but not earlier. The hold moves the milestone `SCHEDULED → HELD` with `held_at` stamped, and then, in this order: every child time log of the engagement that is still unapproved and whose `work_date` is at or before the milestone's `due_at` flips `approved` to true with `approved_at` stamped and its `milestone_id` set to this milestone; the engagement's `approved_hours` is recomputed; the creep calculation reruns and may move `creep_escalation_level`; `variance_hours` is computed as the reported cumulative hours less the engagement's `approved_hours` *measured after those approvals*, which is why an early hold can show a negative variance; and `escalation_raised` is set to true when the recomputed `creep_escalation_level` is `ESCALATED` or `BREACH`. If the recomputed level is `BREACH`, the hold does not merely flag the problem — the engagement is terminated with `termination_reason` `SCOPE_BREACH` and the parent request moves `IN_DELIVERY → RESCOPING`.

A check-in that nobody holds is not forgiven. During the cycle checkpoint, a milestone still `SCHEDULED` on an `IN_PROGRESS` engagement whose `due_at` plus twenty-four hours has fallen strictly behind the logical clock moves `SCHEDULED → MISSED`, raising the engagement's `missed_checkin_count` by one and rerunning both of the volunteer's reliability figures. A second miss ends the engagement: once `missed_checkin_count` reaches 2, the engagement is terminated with `termination_reason` `ADMIN`. The third outcome, `SCHEDULED → WAIVED`, arrives only as a cascade — from a request entering `RESCOPING`, where `waiver_reason` is `RESCOPED`, or from an engagement termination, where it is `ENGAGEMENT_TERMINATED` — and a waived check-in never touches reliability.

**Creep measurement.** The engagement's `scope_creep_pct` compares what the work is consuming against what was committed. The baseline is the engagement's `committed_hours`, which is strictly positive by construction. Against it the program sets the sum of `hours` over approved child time logs of every activity class, rounded to two decimals, plus the sum of `additional_hours` over child scope change requests whose `status` is `PENDING` or `ESCALATED`, also rounded to two decimals — requests in `REJECTED`, `VOIDED` or `APPROVED` contribute nothing, because an approved change has already been folded into the baseline itself. Subtract the baseline from that total, divide by the baseline, and round to four decimals. The result drives `creep_escalation_level`: `NONE` below 0.1500, `WATCH` from 0.1500 up to but not including 0.3000, `ESCALATED` from 0.3000 up to but not including 0.5000, and `BREACH` at 0.5000 and above. The band floors are inclusive, so exactly 0.1500 is `WATCH`, exactly 0.3000 is `ESCALATED` and exactly 0.5000 is `BREACH`; the ratio may legitimately be negative when a generous scope change has raised the baseline above the hours actually burned. These figures are recomputed whenever a time log is created, whenever a check-in is held, on submission, whenever a scope change is created, and whenever a scope change is decided.

**Scope change requests.** Either the engaged `Volunteer` or the owning `PartnerContact` may ask for more room on an `IN_PROGRESS` engagement, supplying `additional_hours` between 0 and 200.00 to two decimals, an `additional_deliverable_count` between 0 and 10, and a `justification` of 1 to 500 characters. The `requested_by` field is derived from the caller's role rather than supplied, reading `VOLUNTEER` or `PARTNER`. Before the row is persisted the program projects its effect: take the engagement's `approved_hours`, add the sum of `additional_hours` over sibling change requests whose `status` is `PENDING` or `ESCALATED` — excluding the incoming row — add the incoming row's own `additional_hours`, subtract the engagement's `committed_hours` baseline, divide by that baseline and round to four decimals. That figure is stored as `projected_creep_pct`, and the `decision_tier` is chosen from it by tests applied in exactly this order: a projection at or above 0.5000 is `RESCOPE_REQUIRED`; otherwise a projection strictly below 0.1500 that also carries an `additional_deliverable_count` of exactly zero is `AUTO_APPROVABLE`; everything else is `MANAGER_REVIEW`. The order matters — a modest projection below 0.1500 that nevertheless asks for at least one extra deliverable falls to `MANAGER_REVIEW`, not to automatic approval.

The tier decides what happens next. An `AUTO_APPROVABLE` change is applied at once, moving `PENDING → APPROVED` in the same operation: the engagement's `committed_hours` rises by `additional_hours` and the holding window's `hours_committed` rises by the same amount, after which the creep calculation reruns against the *new* baseline and therefore normally reads a lower `creep_escalation_level` than before. Approval requires the holding window's `hours_remaining` to be greater than or equal to the requested `additional_hours`, or it is refused with `ERR_AVAILABILITY_HOURS_EXCEEDED`. When the approved change carried an `additional_deliverable_count` of at least one, that many deliverable rows must be added to the pinned revision before the engagement may be submitted. A `MANAGER_REVIEW` change is stored in `PENDING` with a null `decided_by_user_id` and a null `decided_at`, and its pending hours immediately raise the engagement's `scope_creep_pct` even though no work has been done against them. A `RESCOPE_REQUIRED` change moves `PENDING → ESCALATED` in the same operation and is not a request for hours at all but a declaration that the engagement has outgrown its scope: the engagement is terminated with `termination_reason` `SCOPE_BREACH`, running the full release chain, the parent request moves `IN_DELIVERY → RESCOPING`, and the request's `rescope_count` rises by one — the escalation route requires `rescope_count` to still be below 3.

A `ProgramManager` resolves a `PENDING` change by supplying a `decision` of `APPROVE` or `REJECT`; a change that is not `PENDING` is refused with `ERR_SCOPE_CHANGE_NOT_PENDING`. Approval stamps `decided_by_user_id` and `decided_at`, moves the row `PENDING → APPROVED`, and applies the same hour movements and creep recomputation as the automatic path, subject to the same window-capacity check. Rejection stamps the same two audit fields, moves the row `PENDING → REJECTED`, and lets the creep figure fall back, since the rejected hours leave the numerator. Both `PENDING → VOIDED` and `ESCALATED → VOIDED` happen only as cascades when the engagement terminates: a voided pending row stops contributing its hours to the creep numerator, and a voided escalated row later records `resulting_scope_revision_no` — the `revision_no` of the revision the forced re-scope eventually produced, which stays null when no re-scope followed.

Submission ends the delivery phase. The engaged volunteer submits, moving the engagement `IN_PROGRESS → SUBMITTED`, and five conditions must hold: the caller is the engagement's volunteer, at least one child milestone has `checkin_status` `HELD`, no child scope change request sits in `PENDING` or `ESCALATED`, the engagement's `creep_escalation_level` is not `BREACH`, and every non-`WAIVED` deliverable on the pinned revision has its `hours_estimate` accounted for by at least one time log. A submission blocked by an unresolved change request or by a breached creep level is refused with `ERR_SCOPE_BREACH_THRESHOLD` — the same code that refuses a further time log under those conditions. On success every remaining unapproved child log flips `approved` to true with `approved_at` stamped, and the engagement's `approved_hours` and creep figures are recomputed one final time before review.

### 3.8. Quality Review, Rework and Handoff

A `QualityReviewer` opens a review round on an engagement that has reached `SUBMITTED`. The new review is created in `status` `OPEN` with a `round_no` one greater than the highest among its siblings on that engagement, a `reviewer_user_id` recording the opening operator's `X-User-ID`, a `scored_deliverable_count` of 0, a null `weighted_score`, an `outcome` of `PENDING`, and null `rework_deadline_at` and `decided_at`. Opening is barred to an operator whose identifier equals the `authored_by_user_id` of the engagement's pinned revision, and to an operator other than the one who owned a prior round on the same engagement; either bar answers `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. A second round is only openable after a first round that ended in `REWORK_REQUESTED`, and opening it leaves that prior row sitting in `REWORK_REQUESTED` — the closing of the loop `OPEN → SCORED → REWORK_REQUESTED → OPEN` is what the round counter records.

Scoring is a single submission covering the whole deliverable set. The reviewer sends a `deliverable_scores` payload in which each entry names a `deliverable_id` and the `review_score` awarded on the 0-to-5 rubric. The submission must come from the operator recorded as `reviewer_user_id`, the engagement must still be `SUBMITTED`, the review must still be `OPEN`, and the scored set must be exactly the set of non-`WAIVED` deliverables on the pinned revision — non-empty, no extras, no omissions and no duplicates. A set that does not match is refused with `ERR_DELIVERABLE_SET_MISMATCH`, while an entry naming a deliverable that does not belong to the pinned revision at all is refused with `ERR_DELIVERABLE_NOT_FOUND`.

The rubric arithmetic is deliberately pinned, because the rounding order can flip a boundary case. Take the pinned revision's deliverables whose `review_status` is not `WAIVED`, processed in `sequence_no` ascending order with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break. Sum their `weight_pct` values and round to two decimals — this active weight total is the denominator, so a waived deliverable is removed from both the numerator and the denominator and the surviving weights are renormalised rather than merely shrunk. For each deliverable in the pinned order, divide its `weight_pct` by that active total and round to six decimals, then multiply that normalised weight by the deliverable's `review_score` and by a scale factor of 20, rounding each deliverable's contribution to two decimals *before* summing. The review's `weighted_score` is the sum of those per-deliverable contributions, rounded to two decimals; summing normalised weights first and rounding once at the end is not equivalent and is not permitted. The `outcome` follows from the score: `PASS` at 78.00 and above, `CONDITIONAL` from 60.00 up to but not including 78.00, and `FAIL` below 60.00. Scoring also recomputes `scored_deliverable_count`, the number of non-`WAIVED` deliverables on the pinned revision now carrying a non-null `review_score`, and moves the review `OPEN → SCORED`. Scoring a review that is not `OPEN`, or deciding one that is not `SCORED`, is refused with `ERR_REVIEW_NOT_SCORED`.

Deciding commits the outcome, and it is the operation the segregation-of-duties barrier exists for: the deciding operator must be the review's own `reviewer_user_id` and must not be the `authored_by_user_id` of the pinned revision, and the `outcome` must no longer be `PENDING`. Three branches follow, each specified in full.

A decision on a `PASS` or `CONDITIONAL` outcome without rework moves the review `SCORED → DECIDED` with `decided_at` stamped, sets the engagement's `quality_gate` to `PASSED` when the outcome was `PASS` and to `CONDITIONAL` when it was `CONDITIONAL`, moves the engagement `SUBMITTED → ACCEPTED`, and settles each deliverable: every non-`WAIVED` deliverable whose `review_score` is 3 or higher moves to `review_status` `ACCEPTED`, and every remaining one moves to `REJECTED`.

A decision carrying `request_rework` set to true moves the review `SCORED → REWORK_REQUESTED` instead, and is only available when the `outcome` is exactly `CONDITIONAL` and the review's `round_no` is still below 2 — on the second round a `CONDITIONAL` outcome can no longer ask for rework and must simply be decided. The transition stamps `decided_at` and sets `rework_deadline_at` to that instant plus one week, the rework window being 168 hours. The engagement moves back `SUBMITTED → IN_PROGRESS`, and every non-`WAIVED` deliverable has its `review_score` reset to null and its `review_status` reset to `PENDING`, so the second round scores a clean set.

A decision on a `FAIL` outcome ends the engagement, and it does so through a four-step cascade whose steps each follow from the previous one. **Step one:** the engagement's `quality_gate` becomes `FAILED` and its `work_status` moves `SUBMITTED → TERMINATED` with `termination_reason` `QUALITY_FAILURE`, releasing the cycle slot and the window hours exactly as any termination does. **Step two:** because the engagement terminated while holding a posted valuation, its `valuation_status` moves from `FINAL` or `PROVISIONAL` to `RESTATED`, a compensating `INDEPENDENT_SECTOR` valuation record carrying `variant_label` `RESTATED` is posted, and the prior record's `superseded_at` is stamped. **Step three:** because a restated record was posted, the volunteer's `lifetime_valued_usd` is recomputed from her non-superseded `INDEPENDENT_SECTOR` records and both reliability variants rerun. **Step four:** because the decay-weighted `reliability_score` has now fallen strictly below 0.4000, the volunteer moves `ACTIVE → PAUSED` with `pause_reason` `RELIABILITY_FLOOR`, and every match offer she still holds in `OFFERED` moves to `WITHDRAWN` with `withdraw_reason` `VOLUNTEER_PAUSED`, releasing their reserved hours and their cycle slots in turn.

Handoff is the happy ending, and it belongs to the `ProgramManager`. It requires the engagement to be in `ACCEPTED`, and the payload carries `confidentiality_attested`, which must be true, a `handoff_artifact_count` of at least one naming how many artifacts were transferred, and — when the pinned revision's `confidentiality_tier` is `RESTRICTED` — a non-empty `destruction_certificate_ref`. A missing attestation or a missing certificate on a restricted-tier handoff is refused with `ERR_CONFIDENTIALITY_ATTESTATION_MISSING`. Two further conditions hold: the pinned revision must still be `APPROVED`, and the governing agreement's `ip_disposition` must equal the pinned revision's `ip_disposition`, a divergence being refused with `ERR_IP_TERMS_MISMATCH`. On success `handoff_at` is stamped from the logical clock and the engagement moves `ACCEPTED → HANDED_OFF`; the parent request moves `IN_DELIVERY → CLOSED` with `closed_at` stamped; the cycle's `slots_committed` falls by one and the posture recomputes, which may release a `FROZEN` cycle back to `RESTRICTED` or `OPEN`; the holding window's `hours_committed` falls by the engagement's full current `committed_hours`, including any hours an approved scope change added; and the volunteer's `terminal_engagement_count` rises by one while both reliability variants rerun.

### 3.9. Termination, Hour Valuation and Partner Feedback

Termination is the program's universal compensating action, and it runs identically from all four live states — `NOT_STARTED → TERMINATED`, `IN_PROGRESS → TERMINATED`, `SUBMITTED → TERMINATED` and `ACCEPTED → TERMINATED`. A `ProgramManager` may invoke it directly with an optional `termination_reason`, and the system invokes it as a cascade from an agreement void, a partner suspension, a forced re-scope, a breached check-in, a second missed check-in or a failed review; the reason recorded is one of `QUALITY_FAILURE`, `SCOPE_BREACH`, `AGREEMENT_VOIDED`, `PARTNER_SUSPENDED` or `ADMIN`. Terminating from `ACCEPTED` additionally requires `handoff_at` to still be null, and terminating an engagement already in `HANDED_OFF` or `TERMINATED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_WORK_STATE`.

The release chain is the same in every case, and each of its links matters. `terminated_at` and `termination_reason` are stamped. The cycle's `slots_committed` falls by one and the posture recomputes. The holding window's `hours_committed` falls by the engagement's full current `committed_hours` — the grown figure, not the originally reserved one. Every child milestone still `SCHEDULED` moves to `WAIVED` with `waiver_reason` `ENGAGEMENT_TERMINATED`. Every child scope change request in `PENDING` or `ESCALATED` moves to `VOIDED`. The governing agreement, unless it had already reached `EXECUTED`, moves to `VOIDED` with `void_reason` `ENGAGEMENT_TERMINATED`. Any non-superseded valuation record triggers the restatement described below, moving `valuation_status` from `PROVISIONAL` or `FINAL` to `RESTATED`. And because the engagement has now reached a terminal `work_status`, the volunteer's `terminal_engagement_count` rises by one and both reliability variants rerun — which may itself pause the volunteer and withdraw her open offers. One further consequence reaches the request: when a termination for `QUALITY_FAILURE` or `AGREEMENT_VOIDED` leaves no live engagement on a request whose `rescope_count` has already reached 3, the request moves `IN_DELIVERY → ABANDONED` with `closed_at` stamped, because there is no re-scope left to attempt.

**Valuation is a second, independent dimension** on the same engagement row. `valuation_status` advances on its own operations and neither dimension's transitions constrain the other beyond the preconditions stated here. A `ProgramManager` posts a valuation record by supplying the mandatory `expected_engagement_version` and a `method` of `MARKET_RATE` or `INDEPENDENT_SECTOR`. Three forward moves exist. `UNVALUED → PROVISIONAL` requires the engagement's `work_status` to be `IN_PROGRESS` or `SUBMITTED` and at least one child time log to be `approved`. `PROVISIONAL → FINAL` requires the `work_status` to be `HANDED_OFF`. `UNVALUED → FINAL` requires the `work_status` to be `HANDED_OFF` and no prior valuation record to exist on the engagement at all. The stage a post reaches is fixed by the engagement's `work_status` rather than named by the caller — `HANDED_OFF` reaches the `FINAL` stage while `IN_PROGRESS` or `SUBMITTED` reaches the `PROVISIONAL` stage — so a post that reaches the stage the engagement already sits at is a legitimate re-post rather than a violation: `PROVISIONAL → PROVISIONAL` and `FINAL → FINAL` are both accepted, each leaving `valuation_status` where it is while still writing a new record and stamping `superseded_at` on the prior non-superseded record of the same `method`. Those two same-stage re-posts together with the three forward moves above are the complete set of legal pairings of the engagement's current `valuation_status` with the stage the post reaches; any other pairing is refused. A post attempted against a `RESTATED` engagement, or a `FINAL`-stage post while the engagement is still `NOT_STARTED` or `IN_PROGRESS`, is refused with `ERR_INVALID_VALUATION_STATE`. Each post writes a record whose `sequence_no` is one greater than the highest among sibling records *of the same method*, whose `variant_label` is `PROVISIONAL` or `FINAL` according to the stage reached, and whose `posted_at` is the logical clock; it stamps `superseded_at` on the prior non-superseded record of the same method; it recomputes the volunteer's `lifetime_valued_usd` from her non-superseded `INDEPENDENT_SECTOR` records; and it advances the engagement's `version` by one. The system also posts records itself: on termination, `PROVISIONAL → RESTATED` and `FINAL → RESTATED` both fire when at least one non-superseded record exists, each supersedeing the prior record and posting a compensating `INDEPENDENT_SECTOR` record with `variant_label` `RESTATED` computed from the same approved hours.

**What a donated hour is worth** depends entirely on which method was asked for, and the two variants are computed differently on purpose. Both look only at the engagement's child time logs whose `approved` flag is true — unapproved logs are invisible to both — processed in `work_date` ascending order with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break. Both also consult a seniority-keyed rate ceiling: 180.00 dollars an hour for an `ASSOCIATE`, 320.00 for a `SENIOR`, 480.00 for a `PRINCIPAL` and 650.00 for a `PARTNER`.

The `MARKET_RATE` variant is the donor-facing in-kind figure and it values the volunteer's own time. It admits only logs whose `activity_class` is `DELIVERY` or `ADMIN`, dropping every `TRAVEL` log from both the hours and the money. For each admitted log it multiplies the log's `hours` by the lesser of that log's `rate_basis_usd` and the seniority ceiling, then by a class factor of 1.00 for `DELIVERY` and 0.50 for `ADMIN` — travel's factor is 0.00, which is why excluding it changes nothing about the money but does change the hours — and rounds that line to two decimals. The record's `base_amount_usd` is the sum of those per-line figures, rounded to two decimals; it must be computed per line and then summed, never by summing hours first and multiplying once. `hours_included` is the sum of `hours` over the admitted logs, rounded to two decimals, and `rate_applied_usd` is the lesser of the volunteer's `professional_rate_usd_per_hour` and the seniority ceiling.

The `INDEPENDENT_SECTOR` variant is the figure that feeds `lifetime_valued_usd` and the program's IRS-style report, and it values every donated hour identically. It admits every approved log regardless of class — `DELIVERY`, `ADMIN` and `TRAVEL` alike — sets `hours_included` to the sum of their `hours` rounded to two decimals, sets `rate_applied_usd` to the flat national rate of 34.79 dollars an hour, and sets `base_amount_usd` to those hours multiplied by that rate, rounded once at the end. Seniority ceilings and personal rates play no part.

Program overhead is then layered on top, and its evaluation order resolves an apparent circularity that a careless reader gets wrong. The steps run in exactly this sequence. First, `base_amount_usd` comes from the valuation variant above. Second, the overhead tier is selected from `base_amount_usd` and never from the total: a base below 2500.00 takes a rate of 0.12, a base from 2500.00 up to but not including 10000.00 takes 0.09, and a base of 10000.00 or more takes 0.06. Third, `admin_overhead_usd` is the greater of a 45.00-dollar floor and the base multiplied by that tier rate rounded to two decimals — so even a base of 0.00 still carries 45.00 of overhead. Fourth, `total_amount_usd` is the base plus the overhead, rounded to two decimals. A base of 2380.00 therefore takes the 0.12 rate, yielding 285.60 of overhead and a total of 2665.60, even though the resulting total sits inside the 0.09 band. No sibling record participates in this arithmetic; each record is costed alone. Every record of an engagement, both methods and superseded rows included, can be listed for audit.

**Partner feedback** closes the program loop. After delivery a `PartnerContact` rates the engagement with three 1-to-5 scores — `score_expertise`, `score_responsiveness` and `score_deliverable_fit` — and a boolean `would_recommend`. The survey's `partner_org_id` is copied from the engagement rather than supplied, and its `submitted_at` is stamped from the logical clock and serves as the primary ordering key of the index window. Its `composite_score` is a weighted 0-to-100 figure: 0.40 times `score_expertise`, plus 0.35 times `score_deliverable_fit`, plus 0.25 times `score_responsiveness`, that sum multiplied by 20 and rounded to two decimals. Filing a survey against a suspended partner is refused with `ERR_PARTNER_SUSPENDED`.

The partner-level index is then rebuilt from scratch. The eligible pool is every survey of that partner whose linked engagement reached `work_status` `HANDED_OFF`; a survey filed against a terminated engagement is still stored, but with `counted_in_index` false, and it never moves the index. Order the pool by `submitted_at` descending with `created_at` descending as the tie-break, take the five most recent (or all of them when fewer exist), and set `counted_in_index` true for exactly those rows and false for every other row of the pool. The partner's `satisfaction_sample_size` is the number of rows actually used, so it ranges from 0 to 5. With no qualifying survey the `satisfaction_index` is null. Otherwise the used rows are weighted by recency from a fixed ladder — 0.34 for the newest, then 0.26, 0.18, 0.13 and 0.09 — of which only the first as many entries as there are used rows participate: sum those entries and round to four decimals, divide each entry by that sum and round to six decimals, multiply each normalised weight by its survey's `composite_score` and round each product to four decimals, then sum the products and round to two decimals.

Partner standing follows immediately from the rebuilt index, and the two bands are deliberately asymmetric so the machine cannot oscillate on a single survey. If the sample size has reached at least 3 and the index is strictly below 55.00 while the partner's `status` is `ACTIVE`, the partner moves `ACTIVE → PROBATION` with `probation_reason` `LOW_SATISFACTION`; no engagement in flight is disturbed, and only the creation of new offers is blocked while probation lasts. If the sample size has reached at least 3 and the index is 70.00 or above while the partner's `status` is `PROBATION`, the partner moves `PROBATION → ACTIVE` and `probation_reason` is cleared to null. Both evaluations run at the end of survey submission, and neither fires below a sample size of 3.
