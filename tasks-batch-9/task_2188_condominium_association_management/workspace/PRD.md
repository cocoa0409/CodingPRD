# Condominium Association Management API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Community association managers sit at the intersection of a dozen incompatible clocks. Assessments must be billed on a fiscal calendar; statutory notice periods run in fixed day counts before a ratification meeting; cure periods and appeal windows run from the instant a letter is mailed; vendor certificates of insurance expire without warning; and a title agent on a Thursday closing needs a payoff figure that will still be defensible on the following Monday. Today most associations reconcile these clocks by hand, in spreadsheets, with the result that a fine gets assessed while the owner's cure period is still open, a budget gets ratified thirteen days after notice instead of thirty, or a resale certificate quotes a monthly assessment that the board superseded the night before closing.

This document specifies the backend service that replaces that manual reconciliation. The service is the system of record for a condominium association's money, its governance, its physical plant, and its disputes: a per-unit ledger with a statutory payment waterfall and full reversal support; a budget adoption cycle with veto-style owner ratification and revision rounds; common element reserve components with two competing funding methods; vendor contracts with not-to-exceed encumbrance accounting and work orders that must not collide in the same outage window; a master insurance policy whose deductible is split between the association and affected unit owners by heated area; a rules-enforcement pipeline with tiered fines, hearings, and appeal rounds; and resale certificates and lender questionnaires that pin a point-in-time snapshot of all of the above.

Two design commitments run through every requirement below. First, **segregation of duties is structural, not advisory**: the director who reports a violation is barred from chairing its hearing, a director may not review their own earlier decision on appeal, and the manager who proposes a budget may not cast a ballot on it. Second, **every derived figure is recomputed, never trusted**: unit balances, delinquency postures, voting eligibility, reserve percent-funded, and certificate disclosures are all re-derived from the ledger and the entity graph whenever any input beneath them changes, and those recomputations cascade — sometimes five hops deep — in a single request. All endpoints described here are served under the association domain and the top-level resource mount is `/associations`.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the service must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the caller, and `X-User-Role`, naming the caller's role. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not one of the seven role names enumerated below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If both headers are present and well formed but the named role is not among the roles authorized for the target endpoint, the request is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED` (HTTP 403).

The seven roles are `CommunityManager`, `BoardDirector`, `BoardTreasurer`, `UnitOwner`, `InsuranceAdjuster`, `AssociationAttorney`, and `TitleAgent`. Their responsibilities divide as follows. A `CommunityManager` is the management agent: day-to-day operations, billing runs, ledger postings, statutory notices, work-order execution, certificate preparation, and — in test mode — the logical clock. A `BoardDirector` is a seated director, including the president: governance votes, budget revision and ratification, ballots, hearings, contract awards, and terminations. A `BoardTreasurer` is the treasurer director and holds fiscal custody: ledger reversals, special-assessment creation, installment posting, refunds, and invoice payment; the treasurer holds no hearing or award authority. A `UnitOwner` is an owner of record who pays assessments, casts weighted ballots on units they own, appeals fines on units they own, and reads their own statements. An `InsuranceAdjuster` is the master-policy adjuster who takes claims under review, delineates association versus owner responsibility, allocates the deductible, and settles. An `AssociationAttorney` is association counsel, coordinating construction-defect tender, suit filing, and resolution, and reading governance records for litigation support. A `TitleAgent` is the closing agent, who requests resale certificates and reads the lender questionnaire and holds no write authority over the ledger or governance.

Three of the seven roles are **entity-backed**, meaning `X-User-ID` names a persisted row. When `X-User-Role` is `BoardDirector`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `UnitOwner` record; ownership and segregation checks on any resource that references a `UnitOwner` compare the resource's owner-reference field to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `BoardTreasurer`, `X-User-ID` is likewise the `id` of the acting `UnitOwner` record, and the same comparison rule applies. When `X-User-Role` is `UnitOwner`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of that owner's `UnitOwner` record; an owner-scoped operation on a unit succeeds only when `X-User-ID` equals that unit's `current_owner_id`, and otherwise fails with `ERR_NOT_UNIT_OWNER` (HTTP 403). For all three entity-backed roles, if `X-User-ID` does not resolve to an existing `UnitOwner` row, the request is rejected with `ERR_ACTOR_NOT_RECOGNIZED` (HTTP 403); the same code is returned when a board role (`BoardDirector` or `BoardTreasurer`) resolves to a `UnitOwner` whose `is_seated` is not true or whose `board_role` is `NONE`.

The remaining four roles are **opaque**. When `X-User-Role` is `CommunityManager`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it but never compares it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `InsuranceAdjuster`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity, recorded but never compared to an entity `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `AssociationAttorney`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier with no entity backing. When `X-User-Role` is `TitleAgent`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque closing-agent identifier; a certificate request records `requesting_agent_id`, which is never required to equal any entity `id`.

Segregation-of-duties violations — a director recording a hearing decision on a violation they reported, a director reviewing their own earlier decision on an appeal round, or the manager who proposed a budget casting a ballot on it — are rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination

Every collection endpoint accepts two query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to 10 when omitted, may be no smaller than 1, and may be no larger than 100. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to 0 and may be no smaller than 0. A `limit` outside the range of 1 through 100 inclusive is a range violation and is rejected with `ERR_VALIDATION` (HTTP 422).

Collections are returned sorted by `created_at` in ascending order unless a subsection below states a different ordering for a specific listing. The `meta` block of a list response carries `limit` and `offset` echoing the effective values used, `total_count` giving the number of rows matching the filter before the page window was applied, and `has_more` set true when rows remain beyond the current window.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Versioning, and the Logical Clock

Every persisted resource carries an `id`, which is a server-minted UUID string with no type prefix of any kind, and a `created_at` timestamp formatted as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits. Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, formatted identically and refreshed on every mutation, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the resource is created and increases by exactly 1 on each mutation of that resource. Association records, unit owners, units, budgets, billing runs, special assessments, board meetings, common elements, vendor contracts, maintenance work orders, insurance claims, violation cases, and resale certificates all carry the full set: `id`, `created_at`, `updated_at`, and `version`.

Two resources are deliberately unversioned. A ledger entry and a vote each carry only `id` and `created_at`; **neither has an `updated_at` field nor a `version` field**, and neither ever will. The reason is economic rather than technical: a ledger row is an immutable historical assertion, so a mistake is never edited but instead answered with a compensating `REVERSAL` row, and a superseded ballot is never rewritten but simply loses at tally time. Implementations must not emit `updated_at` or `version` on ledger entries or votes.

All temporal reasoning in this service runs against a **logical clock**, never against wall-clock time. Before any client sets it, the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real time under any circumstance. Every deadline, aging computation, notice period, cure window, appeal window, docket boundary, contract term check, and expiry sweep measures against this clock. A `CommunityManager` may move the clock by supplying a `current_utc` instant to the test-driving clock capability; that operation mutates no entity, bumps no version, and returns an empty data object, but every subsequent request observes the new logical now.

### 2.5. Association Scope and Left-to-Right Path Resolution

Path parameters are resolved strictly left to right, and the first unresolvable segment determines the error. The recognized path variables are `association_id`, `unit_id`, `budget_id`, `board_meeting_id`, `special_assessment_id`, `common_element_id`, `vendor_contract_id`, `work_order_id`, `insurance_claim_id`, `violation_case_id`, `resale_certificate_id`, and `ledger_entry_id`. An unknown `association_id` yields `ERR_ASSOCIATION_NOT_FOUND`; an unknown `unit_id` yields `ERR_UNIT_NOT_FOUND`; an unknown `budget_id` yields `ERR_BUDGET_NOT_FOUND`; an unknown `board_meeting_id` yields `ERR_MEETING_NOT_FOUND`; an unknown `special_assessment_id` yields `ERR_ASSESSMENT_NOT_FOUND`; an unknown `common_element_id` yields `ERR_ELEMENT_NOT_FOUND`; an unknown `vendor_contract_id` yields `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`; an unknown `work_order_id` yields `ERR_WORK_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`; an unknown `insurance_claim_id` yields `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`; an unknown `violation_case_id` yields `ERR_CASE_NOT_FOUND`; an unknown `resale_certificate_id` yields `ERR_CERTIFICATE_NOT_FOUND`; and an unknown `ledger_entry_id` yields `ERR_LEDGER_ENTRY_NOT_FOUND`. An unknown owner reference resolves to `ERR_OWNER_NOT_FOUND`. All of these carry HTTP 404.

Foreign keys arriving in a request body are checked separately. When a body-supplied reference — `current_owner_id`, `source_budget_id`, `ratification_meeting_id`, `contract_id`, `element_id`, `claim_id`, `funding_claim_id`, `unit_id`, `voter_owner_id`, `new_owner_id`, `certificate_id`, or any member of `affected_unit_ids` — names no existing row, the request fails with `ERR_REFERENCED_ENTITY_NOT_FOUND` (HTTP 404).

Because an `Association` is the only scope root in this system, every other resource ultimately belongs to exactly one association through its `association_id`. Once all references resolve, the server verifies that they all belong to the same association as the operation's target: a unit, common element, vendor contract, board meeting, insurance claim, or budget drawn from a different association is rejected with `ERR_ASSOCIATION_SCOPE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 409). This check runs on every write endpoint that accepts a foreign key.

### 2.6. Optimistic Concurrency Control on Parent Records

Three creation endpoints in this service take a client-observed parent version and refuse to proceed if the parent has moved on. Each requires its version field as a **non-optional** input; omitting it is a field-level validation failure.

Creating a budget for a fiscal year requires `expected_association_version` in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Association`. If that value does not equal the association's current `version`, the create is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ASSOCIATION_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The rule exists because a budget creation legitimately supersedes any `DRAFT` or `PROPOSED` budget for the same `fiscal_year`; a writer holding an outdated association version would be superseding a budget it never observed. The same field and the same error apply to creating a special assessment against an association.

Creating a resale-certificate request requires `expected_unit_version` in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `Unit`. A mismatch against the unit's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_UNIT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). A new request legitimately voids any other `REQUESTED` certificate for the same unit and quotes payoff figures off that unit's ledger, so a stale reader would both supersede an unseen request and publish figures from a ledger state that has already advanced.

Creating a maintenance work order requires `expected_contract_version` in the request body, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `VendorContract`. A mismatch against the contract's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CONTRACT_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Each new order encumbers part of `contract_cap_cents`, so a writer working from a stale contract version would be computing remaining authorization from a `committed_cents` figure that an earlier encumbrance has already changed.

No other endpoint takes a version field. In particular, revising by-law tunables, revising a common element, and every action endpoint that drives a state machine take no expected-version input — but note that adjusting by-law tunables increments `Association.version`, and revising a common element increments `CommonElement.version`, either of which invalidates a version a client captured earlier.

### 2.7. Money, Basis Points, and Rounding Discipline

Every monetary quantity in this system is an **integer number of cents**. There are no decimal amounts, no floating-point money, and no currency conversion. Fields whose names end in `_cents` are integers; fields whose names end in `_bp` are integers denominated in basis points, where 10000 basis points is the whole — so an interest allocation of `interest_bp` equal to 2500 is one quarter of the association, and a threshold of 6667 basis points is two thirds plus one basis point.

Two rounding operations appear throughout, and they are not interchangeable. **Round half up** means the value is rounded to the nearest whole cent (or whole basis point) with exact halves rounded away from zero, upward for positive values. **Floor** means the value is truncated toward negative infinity to a whole unit. Where a calculation prescribes rounding per line item followed by an integer remainder distribution, the sequence is mandatory: summing first and rounding once produces a different, incorrect answer. Day counts derived from the logical clock are always whole days obtained by flooring, never fractional.

Percent-funded ratios and vote shares are computed in basis points and floored. Per-unit assessment shares are rounded half up per line and then reconciled cent by cent. Deductible allocations are floored per unit and then topped up cent by cent. Section 3 states each of these precisely at the point of use; the reason they differ is that assessments must land on a pre-pinned pool while deductible allocations must never overshoot the residue.

### 2.8. Cascade Discipline and the Conservation Invariants

Requests in this system routinely mutate several entities at once, and the specification treats those secondary mutations as part of the contract rather than as incidental bookkeeping. Whenever a cascade touches an entity, that entity's `updated_at` is refreshed and its `version` increases by exactly 1 — including entities the caller never named. A single budget ratification, for example, writes the budget, every unit of the association, several resale certificates, several ledger entries, and every currently balloting special assessment, and each of those rows (except ledger entries, which are unversioned) advances its own `version` by 1.

Three conserved quantities must hold exactly, with no tolerance, after every request and after every cascade inside a request.

**Invariant L — owner ledger cents per unit.** A unit's `balance_due_cents` always equals the signed sum of `amount_cents` over *every* ledger entry of that unit, whatever each row's `status`. Compensation is expressed by posting a `REVERSAL` entry whose `reverses_entry_id` names the original, whose `amount_cents` is the exact arithmetic negation of the original's `amount_cents`, and whose own `status` is `POSTED`, while the original's `status` becomes `REVERSED`. Because the pair nets to zero, a forward action followed by its compensation returns `balance_due_cents` to its exact pre-action value, to the cent, with no residual `applied_cents` left on the reversed row. Four forward-and-compensating pairs must satisfy this invariant: a fine assessment against its vacatur on appeal; a certificate issuance fee against a cascade void of that certificate; a special-assessment installment posting against a rescission of that assessment; and a manual charge against an explicit treasurer reversal. A cascade that voids a certificate but leaves its `RESALE_FEE` row unreversed, or a vacatur that reverses only part of the fine, is a defect.

**Invariant E — contract authorization cents per vendor contract.** A contract's `committed_cents` always equals the sum of `encumbered_cents` over that contract's work orders whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `ON_HOLD`, or `COMPLETED`; `CANCELLED` and `INVOICED` orders contribute zero. Furthermore `contract_cap_cents` is always greater than or equal to `committed_cents` plus `invoiced_cents`. Creating a work order encumbers `estimated_cost_cents`; cancelling that order, terminating the contract, or letting the contract expire each set that order's `encumbered_cents` to 0 and release exactly `estimated_cost_cents`, so a create-then-cancel round trip returns `committed_cents` to its exact pre-create value. Putting an order on hold does **not** release its encumbrance, because `ON_HOLD` remains inside the summation set. Invoicing moves `estimated_cost_cents` out of `committed_cents` and adds `final_cost_cents` to `invoiced_cents`, with no drift.

**Invariant S — restricted special-assessment cash.** The association's `restricted_sa_cash_cents` always equals the sum, over all of that association's special assessments, of `collected_cents` minus `refunded_cents`. A rescind-then-refund round trip drives it back to its pre-collection value.

### 2.9. The Validation Ladder

Requests are validated in a single fixed order, and the **first** failing check determines the response. This ordering is normative: when two checks would both fail, the lower-numbered one wins, and a test that supplies two simultaneous defects expects the earlier error identifier. The ladder runs as follows.

1. Headers present and `X-User-Role` is one of the seven declared roles — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`, on every endpoint.
2. The acting role appears in the target endpoint's authorized role list — otherwise `ERR_ROLE_NOT_PERMITTED`, on every endpoint.
3. For the entity-backed roles `UnitOwner`, `BoardDirector`, and `BoardTreasurer`, `X-User-ID` resolves to an existing owner row, and for `BoardDirector` or `BoardTreasurer` that owner's `board_role` is `DIRECTOR`, `PRESIDENT`, or `TREASURER` and `is_seated` is true — otherwise `ERR_ACTOR_NOT_RECOGNIZED`. This applies to every endpoint whose authorized roles include `UnitOwner`, `BoardDirector`, or `BoardTreasurer`.
4. Path resources exist, resolved left to right, on every endpoint that has path parameters — otherwise the matching not-found identifier from §2.5.
5. Body-supplied foreign keys exist, on every write endpoint that accepts them — otherwise `ERR_REFERENCED_ENTITY_NOT_FOUND`.
6. Field types, enum membership, and numeric ranges are valid — `interest_bp` between 1 and 10000 inclusive, `heated_sqft` at least 1, `installment_count` between 1 and 24 inclusive, `amount_cents` non-zero, a payment's `amount_cents` strictly greater than 0, `fiscal_year` between 2020 and 2100 inclusive, `limit` between 1 and 100 inclusive, `offset` at least 0 — otherwise `ERR_VALIDATION`. This applies on every endpoint.
7. Owner scoping: for an owner-scoped operation the acting owner must be the target unit's `current_owner_id` — otherwise `ERR_NOT_UNIT_OWNER`. This guards exactly three operations: appealing a fine, casting a vote or ballot, and reading a unit statement.
8. Every referenced entity belongs to the same association as the target — otherwise `ERR_ASSOCIATION_SCOPE_MISMATCH`, on every write endpoint accepting foreign keys.
9. The optimistic-concurrency guard from §2.6, on exactly three endpoints: budget creation with `expected_association_version` yielding `ERR_STALE_ASSOCIATION_VERSION`, resale-certificate request creation with `expected_unit_version` yielding `ERR_STALE_UNIT_VERSION`, and work-order creation with `expected_contract_version` yielding `ERR_STALE_CONTRACT_VERSION`. Special-assessment creation carries `expected_association_version` under the same association rule.
10. The cited violation case has not already reached a terminal enforcement outcome — `CURED`, `DISMISSED`, `FINE_UPHELD`, or `FINE_VACATED` — otherwise `ERR_CASE_ALREADY_TERMINAL`. This guards five enforcement operations: violation notice mailing, hearing docketing, hearing decision recording, cure recording, and fine appeal.
11. A case whose status is `CURED` may not be docketed for a hearing — otherwise `ERR_CASE_ALREADY_CURED`, on hearing docketing only.
12. The target resource is in a state from which the requested transition is defined — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`, on every action endpoint, except where a more specific identifier is declared: `ERR_INVALID_BUDGET_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_CONTRACT_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_WORK_ORDER_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_CLAIM_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_CERTIFICATE_STATE`, `ERR_INVALID_MEETING_STATE`, and `ERR_INVALID_BALLOT_STATE` are used for their respective entities.
13. The hosting board meeting is `IN_SESSION` when a ratification or a hearing decision is recorded — otherwise `ERR_MEETING_NOT_IN_SESSION`, on budget ratification and hearing decision recording.
14. The hosting board meeting has `quorum_met` true — otherwise `ERR_BOARD_QUORUM_NOT_MET`, on meeting convening, budget ratification, and hearing decision recording.
15. Segregation of duties, as described in §2.1 — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`, on hearing decision recording and on vote casting.
16. The vendor's certificate of insurance is still in force — otherwise `ERR_VENDOR_INSURANCE_LAPSED`, on work-order starting, contract activation, and contract reinstatement.
17. A resale certificate may not be issued for a unit whose `delinquency_stage` is `FORECLOSURE_REFERRAL` — otherwise `ERR_CERT_BLOCKED_BY_FORECLOSURE`, on certificate issuance.
18. The common element is serviceable enough for the operation — otherwise `ERR_ELEMENT_NOT_SERVICEABLE`, on work-order starting or resuming, board award recording, and contract submission.
19. A work order's `element_id` equals its contract's `covered_element_id` — otherwise `ERR_ELEMENT_CONTRACT_MISMATCH`, on work-order creation.
20. The board's award is still valid because `CommonElement.version` equals `pinned_element_version` — otherwise `ERR_ELEMENT_SPEC_CHANGED`, on contract activation and reinstatement.
21. Litigation may not advance to `IN_LITIGATION` without an approved funding assessment — otherwise `ERR_LITIGATION_FUNDING_MISSING`, on litigation-stage advancement.
22. A claim may not settle while any work order driven by it is still open — otherwise `ERR_CLAIM_WORK_OPEN`, on claim settlement.
23. Closing requires a valid issued certificate — otherwise `ERR_NO_VALID_CERTIFICATE`, on ownership transfer.
24. The certificate's pinned snapshot is still current — otherwise `ERR_CERTIFICATE_STALE`, on ownership transfer.
25. The statutory budget notice period is satisfied — otherwise `ERR_NOTICE_PERIOD_NOT_SATISFIED`, on budget notice mailing and budget ratification.
26. The cure period has expired before a hearing is docketed, and conversely a cure is not recorded after the deadline — otherwise `ERR_CURE_PERIOD_ACTIVE`, on hearing docketing and cure recording.
27. The appeal window is still open — otherwise `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_CLOSED`, on fine appeal.
28. The work window lies inside the contract term and `window_start` precedes `window_end` — otherwise `ERR_WINDOW_OUTSIDE_CONTRACT_TERM`, on work-order creation.
29. The buffered outage window does not collide with another open order on the same element — otherwise `ERR_ELEMENT_WINDOW_CONFLICT`, on work-order creation.
30. The requested half-open thirty-minute docket slot does not intersect an existing docketed slot on the same meeting — otherwise `ERR_HEARING_SLOT_TAKEN`, on hearing docketing.
31. The meeting's `hearing_slot_count` is strictly below `hearing_docket_capacity` — otherwise `ERR_HEARING_DOCKET_FULL`, on hearing docketing.
32. At most two cases for the same unit may be docketed to one meeting — otherwise `ERR_UNIT_DOCKET_LIMIT`, on hearing docketing.
33. The meeting's `agenda_kinds` contains the kind the operation needs — otherwise `ERR_AGENDA_KIND_MISSING`, on budget notice mailing, budget ratification, hearing docketing, and board award recording.
34. An appeal hearing is docketed to a different meeting than the previous round — otherwise `ERR_SAME_MEETING_REHEARING`, on hearing docketing.
35. The association's units' `interest_bp` sum to exactly 10000 — otherwise `ERR_INTEREST_SUM_INVALID`, on budget proposal, budget ratification, and ballot opening.
36. `unit_label` is unique within the association — otherwise `ERR_UNIT_LABEL_DUPLICATE`, on unit creation.
37. The billing `period` has not already been run for this association — otherwise `ERR_PERIOD_ALREADY_BILLED`, on billing-run creation.
38. The referenced budget is `ADOPTED` and its `fiscal_year` covers the relevant date — otherwise `ERR_BUDGET_NOT_ADOPTED`, on billing-run creation and certificate issuance.
39. Only one special assessment of an association may be `BALLOTING` at a time — otherwise `ERR_BALLOT_ALREADY_OPEN`, on ballot opening.
40. Closing a ballot requires `eligible_weight_bp` strictly greater than 0 after recomputation — otherwise `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_VOTERS`, on ballot closing.
41. A split adjustment requires a non-empty `affected_unit_ids` — otherwise `ERR_NO_AFFECTED_UNITS`, on claim adjustment.
42. `installments_posted` is strictly below `installment_count` — otherwise `ERR_INSTALLMENTS_EXHAUSTED`, on installment posting.
43. The reserve contribution meets the statutory minimum share of the operating total — otherwise `ERR_RESERVE_CONTRIBUTION_TOO_LOW`, on budget proposal and budget ratification.
44. The new encumbrance fits inside the contract cap — otherwise `ERR_CONTRACT_CAP_EXCEEDED`, on work-order creation.
45. The invoice sits inside the overrun tolerance and the cap — otherwise `ERR_INVOICE_EXCEEDS_TOLERANCE`, on work-order invoicing.
46. `operating_cash_cents` is at least `final_cost_cents` — otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_OPERATING_CASH`, on work-order invoicing.
47. The refund does not exceed collected-minus-already-refunded cash — otherwise `ERR_REFUND_EXCEEDS_COLLECTED`, on special-assessment refunding.
48. A reversal targets only a `POSTED` entry with a positive `amount_cents` — otherwise `ERR_ENTRY_ALREADY_REVERSED`, on ledger reversal.
49. No other budget of the association for the same `fiscal_year` is already `ADOPTED` — otherwise `ERR_DUPLICATE_ADOPTED_BUDGET`, on budget proposal and budget ratification.
50. No case docketed to the meeting is still `HEARING_SCHEDULED` at adjournment — otherwise `ERR_HEARINGS_PENDING`, on meeting adjournment.
51. No budget tied to the meeting is still `NOTICED` at adjournment — otherwise `ERR_BUDGET_NOTICE_PENDING`, on meeting adjournment.
52. No work order of the contract is `IN_PROGRESS` when terminating — otherwise `ERR_WORK_IN_PROGRESS`, on contract termination.
53. The logical clock has reached `window_start` when starting or resuming work — otherwise `ERR_WINDOW_NOT_STARTED`, on work-order starting.
54. `hearing_slot_start` is aligned to a thirty-minute boundary and the whole slot lies inside the meeting window — otherwise `ERR_SLOT_OUTSIDE_MEETING`, on hearing docketing.
55. `effective_from` precedes `effective_to` and `insurance_cert_expires_at` is at or after `effective_to` — otherwise `ERR_CONTRACT_TERM_INVALID`, on contract submission.
56. `closing_date` is not earlier than the logical clock at issuance — otherwise `ERR_CERT_CLOSING_DATE_PAST`, on certificate issuance.
57. The requested `responsibility` matches the damage split figures — otherwise `ERR_RESPONSIBILITY_MISMATCH`, on claim adjustment.
58. `appeal_round` is strictly below 2 when appealing a denied claim — otherwise `ERR_APPEAL_LIMIT_REACHED`, on claim appeal.
59. A claim names at least one of `unit_id` or `element_id` and `within_unit_boundary_cents` does not exceed `gross_damage_cents` — otherwise `ERR_CLAIM_SCOPE_INVALID`, on claim reporting and on adjuster assignment.
60. A special assessment funding a claim that is in litigation may not be rescinded — otherwise `ERR_LITIGATION_ACTIVE`, on rescission.

The practical consequences of this ordering deserve emphasis. Because owner scoping (rank 7) precedes association scope matching (rank 8), an owner acting on someone else's unit in a foreign association sees `ERR_NOT_UNIT_OWNER`, not the scope-mismatch code. Because the concurrency guard (rank 9) precedes all state and business checks, a stale parent version surfaces as a 412 even when the target is in an impossible state. Because terminal-case detection (rank 10) precedes generic state checking (rank 12), an attempt to re-decide a `DISMISSED` case returns `ERR_CASE_ALREADY_TERMINAL` rather than a generic invalid-transition code. And because meeting session (rank 13) precedes quorum (rank 14), a decision recorded at a meeting that was never convened reports `ERR_MEETING_NOT_IN_SESSION` even though quorum is also absent.

### 2.10. By-Law Tunables and Their Defaults

An association is registered with a `legal_name` between 1 and 200 characters, which is required, and an optional `fiscal_year_start_month` between 1 and 12 inclusive that defaults to 1 and drives billing periods. Every other governance parameter is optional at registration and falls back to a statutory default: `master_policy_deductible_cents` defaults to 2500000, `association_deductible_cap_cents` to 1000000, `late_fee_grace_days` to 10, `late_fee_percent_bp` to 1000, `late_fee_min_cents` to 2500, `late_fee_cap_cents` to 10000, `lien_threshold_cents` to 180000, `budget_veto_bp` to 5000, `special_assessment_approval_bp` to 6667, `board_quorum_seats` to 3, `hearing_docket_capacity` to 6, `element_window_buffer_hours` to 24, `reserve_adequacy_threshold_bp` to 7000, `reserve_contribution_min_bp` to 1000, `fine_tier_1_cents` to 5000, `fine_tier_2_cents` to 10000, `fine_tier_3_daily_cents` to 25000, `fine_cap_cents` to 250000, `repeat_lookback_days` to 365, `cure_period_days` to 14, `appeal_window_days` to 30, `budget_notice_days` to 30, `certificate_validity_days` to 30, and `resale_cert_fee_cents` to 35000.

At registration the derived and custodial figures start from zero: `total_interest_bp` is 0, `operating_cash_cents` is 0, `reserve_balance_cents` is 0, `reserve_designated_cents` is 0, `restricted_sa_cash_cents` is 0, and `version` is 1. A later revision of the tunables, available to a `CommunityManager` or a `BoardTreasurer`, accepts exactly twenty-one optional fields and no others: `master_policy_deductible_cents`, `association_deductible_cap_cents`, `late_fee_grace_days`, `late_fee_percent_bp`, `late_fee_min_cents`, `late_fee_cap_cents`, `lien_threshold_cents`, `budget_veto_bp`, `special_assessment_approval_bp`, `board_quorum_seats`, `hearing_docket_capacity`, `element_window_buffer_hours`, `reserve_adequacy_threshold_bp`, `reserve_contribution_min_bp`, `fine_cap_cents`, `cure_period_days`, `appeal_window_days`, `budget_notice_days`, `resale_cert_fee_cents`, `operating_cash_cents`, and `reserve_balance_cents`. The last two are how cash on hand is funded into the system. Every other field of the association is immutable after registration or is derived — in particular `legal_name`, `fiscal_year_start_month`, `fine_tier_1_cents`, `fine_tier_2_cents`, `fine_tier_3_daily_cents`, `repeat_lookback_days`, and `certificate_validity_days` may not be revised, and `total_interest_bp`, `reserve_designated_cents`, and `restricted_sa_cash_cents` are maintained only by the server. Any such revision increments `Association.version` by 1, which invalidates any `expected_association_version` a client captured earlier.

`total_interest_bp` is never supplied by a client: it is recomputed as the sum of `interest_bp` over all of the association's units whenever a unit is added, and it must equal exactly 10000 before any budget may be adopted. `reserve_designated_cents` on the association tracks the portion of `reserve_balance_cents` earmarked for litigation matters and approved special assessments, and that portion is not spendable on ordinary work orders. `restricted_sa_cash_cents` tracks cash collected against approved special assessments that has been neither disbursed nor refunded.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Onboarding an Association and Running Its Unit Ledger

Onboarding begins with a `CommunityManager` registering the association and its by-law tunables, as described in §2.10. Owners of record come next: each carries a `full_name` and an `email` used as the notice delivery address, and may optionally be given a board seat through `board_role`, whose permitted values are `NONE`, `DIRECTOR`, `PRESIDENT`, and `TREASURER` and which defaults to `NONE`, together with `is_seated`, a boolean defaulting to false that determines whether the seat counts toward board quorum. An owner's `owned_unit_count` is never supplied by the client; it starts at 0 and the server maintains it as units are created and transferred. Owners may be listed and filtered by `association_id` and by `board_role`.

Adding a unit requires a payload naming its owner of record through `current_owner_id`, a `unit_label` that is the door number, an `interest_bp` giving the unit's undivided common-element interest in basis points, and a `heated_sqft` giving its heated area. The label must be unique inside the association — a collision is refused with `ERR_UNIT_LABEL_DUPLICATE` — while `interest_bp` must fall between 1 and 10000 inclusive and `heated_sqft` must be at least 1. Creating a unit has two secondary effects the caller does not request: the association's `total_interest_bp` is recomputed as the sum of `interest_bp` over all of its units and `Association.version` advances by 1, and the named owner's `owned_unit_count` rises by 1 with `UnitOwner.version` advancing by 1. Units may be listed and filtered by `association_id`, by `current_owner_id`, and by `delinquency_stage`.

A newly created unit is fiscally blank: `monthly_assessment_cents` is 0 and stays 0 until a budget adoption allocates to it, `balance_due_cents` is 0, `credit_balance_cents` is 0, `oldest_unpaid_age_days` is 0, `delinquency_stage` is `CURRENT`, `voting_eligible` is true, `lien_recorded_at` is null, and `transfer_blocked` is true. That last field is derived rather than set: it is true exactly when the unit has no resale certificate in `ISSUED` status whose `expires_at` is still in the future, which is why a brand-new unit cannot be closed on.

Everything financial about a unit is expressed as ledger rows. Each row records the `unit_id` it belongs to, an `entry_type` describing its economic nature, a `bucket` used by the payment waterfall, a signed `amount_cents` where charges are positive and payments and credits negative, a `due_on` date from which aging and late fees are measured (null on payments and credits), a `posted_at` instant taken from the logical clock that is the sole chronological ordering key for replay, an `effective_period` string in `YYYY-MM` form naming the billing period the row belongs to, a `status` of either `POSTED` or `REVERSED` that defaults to `POSTED`, and a free-text `memo`. Rows also carry provenance pointers that the system fills in and the client never supplies: `billing_run_id` is set on rows produced by a billing run and is null for manual charges, payments, and reversals; `source_case_id` is set on `FINE` rows; `special_assessment_id` is set on `SPECIAL_ASSESSMENT_INSTALLMENT` and `CREDIT_REFUND` rows; `source_claim_id` is set on `INSURANCE_DEDUCTIBLE_CHARGEBACK` rows; and `reverses_entry_id` is set on `REVERSAL` rows, where it names the row being undone.

The eleven permitted `entry_type` values are `ASSESSMENT`, `SPECIAL_ASSESSMENT_INSTALLMENT`, `LATE_FEE`, `INTEREST`, `FINE`, `COLLECTION_COST`, `INSURANCE_DEDUCTIBLE_CHARGEBACK`, `RESALE_FEE`, `PAYMENT`, `CREDIT_REFUND`, and `REVERSAL`. The `bucket` is never supplied by the client; it is derived from `entry_type` by a fixed mapping: `ASSESSMENT` maps to bucket `ASSESSMENT`; `SPECIAL_ASSESSMENT_INSTALLMENT` maps to `SPECIAL_ASSESSMENT`; `LATE_FEE` maps to `LATE_FEE`; `INTEREST` maps to `INTEREST`; `FINE` maps to `FINE`; `COLLECTION_COST` maps to `COLLECTION_COST`; `INSURANCE_DEDUCTIBLE_CHARGEBACK` maps to `ASSESSMENT`, because a deductible chargeback collects like an assessment; `RESALE_FEE` maps to `COLLECTION_COST`; and `PAYMENT`, `CREDIT_REFUND`, and `REVERSAL` all map to `NONE`. The seven permitted `bucket` values are therefore `INTEREST`, `LATE_FEE`, `COLLECTION_COST`, `FINE`, `ASSESSMENT`, `SPECIAL_ASSESSMENT`, and `NONE`.

A `CommunityManager` or a `BoardTreasurer` may post a manual charge — a collection cost, an interest accrual, or a corrective assessment — by supplying `entry_type`, `amount_cents`, `due_on`, `effective_period`, and an optional `memo`. A charge amount of exactly zero is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`. Posting a charge recomputes the unit's `balance_due_cents`, re-derives its `delinquency_stage`, and advances `Unit.version` by 1.

Recording a payment is open to a `CommunityManager`, a `BoardTreasurer`, or the unit's own `UnitOwner`, and the payload carries `amount_cents`, `effective_period`, an optional `application_mode`, an optional `directed_bucket`, and an optional `memo`. The supplied `amount_cents` must be strictly greater than zero; anything else is refused with `ERR_VALIDATION`. The stored `PAYMENT` row records `amount_cents` as the arithmetic negation of the supplied figure, because a payment is a credit against the unit.

**The application waterfall.** The amount to distribute is the absolute value of the payment's `amount_cents`. The statutory bucket order is `INTEREST` first, then `LATE_FEE`, then `COLLECTION_COST`, then `FINE`, then `ASSESSMENT`, then `SPECIAL_ASSESSMENT`. Within each bucket the open charges of that unit are the rows whose `status` is `POSTED`, whose `bucket` equals the bucket under consideration, whose `amount_cents` is greater than zero, and whose remaining capacity — that is, `amount_cents` minus `applied_cents` — is greater than zero; a `REVERSED` row is closed and never receives money. Those open charges are visited in order of `due_on` ascending, then `posted_at` ascending, then `created_at` ascending. For each charge visited, the amount taken is the lesser of the amount still remaining and that charge's remaining capacity; the charge's `applied_cents` grows by the amount taken, an entry recording the `bucket`, the `target_entry_id`, and the `applied_cents` taken is appended in visit order to the payment's `applied_allocations` array, and the remaining amount falls by the same figure. As soon as the remaining amount reaches zero the walk stops immediately, across all buckets. Whatever is left over becomes the payment's `unapplied_cents`, which is held as unapplied credit and is never negative. The unit's `credit_balance_cents` is then the sum of `unapplied_cents` over its `POSTED` `PAYMENT` rows. No rounding occurs anywhere in this procedure — every step is an integer comparison.

Two variants of the waterfall exist and they produce genuinely different outcomes from the same money. The **statutory** variant applies when `application_mode` is omitted or is `STATUTORY`, and it walks the bucket order verbatim. The **owner-directed** variant applies when `application_mode` is `OWNER_DIRECTED` and `directed_bucket` names a bucket; the effective order then places `directed_bucket` first and follows it with the statutory order minus that bucket. There is one override: if the unit's `delinquency_stage` is `LIEN_FILED` or `FORECLOSURE_REFERRAL`, an owner-directed request is **silently forced onto the statutory variant** rather than rejected — collection is with counsel and the owner no longer chooses. Because the two variants target different charges, the identical payment against the identical ledger can yield different `applied_allocations`, a different `collected_cents` on a special assessment (and hence a different `funding_status`), and a different `oldest_unpaid_age_days` (and hence possibly a different `delinquency_stage`).

Corrections are the treasurer's exclusive province. A `BoardTreasurer` may reverse a ledger row, optionally supplying a `memo`; the target must have `status` `POSTED` and an `amount_cents` greater than zero, so reversing an already-`REVERSED` row or reversing a payment or credit row is refused with `ERR_ENTRY_ALREADY_REVERSED`. A successful reversal creates a `REVERSAL` row whose `reverses_entry_id` names the original and whose `amount_cents` is the exact negation of the original's, flips the original's `status` to `REVERSED`, recomputes the unit's `balance_due_cents`, re-derives its `delinquency_stage`, and advances `Unit.version` by 1.

**Balance and aging arithmetic.** After every row insertion, every reversal, and once per unit during each billing run, the server recomputes three figures. `balance_due_cents` is the signed sum of `amount_cents` over *every* ledger row of the unit, regardless of `status` — a reversed pair nets to zero, so nothing is double counted. The open set for aging consists of the rows whose `status` is `POSTED`, whose `amount_cents` is positive, and whose remaining capacity is positive; `oldest_unpaid_age_days` is 0 when that set is empty, and otherwise it is the largest whole-day count obtained by taking the difference between the logical clock and each open row's `due_on` and flooring it to whole days. An empty open set therefore yields 0, never null and never a negative number, which places a fully paid unit on the first rung of the ladder below. `credit_balance_cents` is recomputed as described above.

**The delinquency ladder.** `delinquency_stage` is a derived posture, not an operator-driven status. On every ledger mutation and on every billing run the server walks the following ladder from the top and takes the **first** matching rule, so escalation and de-escalation share one evaluation. Rule 1: if `balance_due_cents` is less than or equal to 0, the posture is `CURRENT`. Rule 2: if `oldest_unpaid_age_days` is at least 180 and `balance_due_cents` is at least twice `lien_threshold_cents`, the posture is `FORECLOSURE_REFERRAL`. Rule 3: if `oldest_unpaid_age_days` is at least 90 and `balance_due_cents` is at least `lien_threshold_cents`, the posture is `LIEN_FILED`. Rule 4: if `oldest_unpaid_age_days` is at least 30, the posture is `LATE_NOTICE`. Rule 5: if `oldest_unpaid_age_days` is strictly greater than `late_fee_grace_days`, the posture is `REMINDER`. Rule 6: otherwise the posture is `CURRENT`. With the default ten-day grace, an account exactly ten days past due is still `CURRENT`; day eleven makes it `REMINDER`.

Movement along the ladder carries side effects. A unit moving `CURRENT → REMINDER`, `REMINDER → LATE_NOTICE`, `LATE_NOTICE → CURRENT`, or `REMINDER → CURRENT` simply advances `Unit.version` by 1. Reaching `LATE_NOTICE → LIEN_FILED` additionally stamps `lien_recorded_at` with the logical clock and flips `voting_eligible` to false; because eligibility changed, every special assessment of the association whose `approval_status` is `BALLOTING` recomputes its `eligible_weight_bp` and re-derives the `counted` flag on its votes, each such assessment advancing its own `version` by 1; and when the re-derivation happened inside a billing run, that run's `escalated_unit_count` rises by 1. Reaching `LIEN_FILED → FORECLOSURE_REFERRAL` leaves `voting_eligible` false and blocks any new resale certificate for that unit, advancing `Unit.version` by 1. Falling back from `LIEN_FILED → LATE_NOTICE` (which happens when the balance is still positive but below `lien_threshold_cents` while `oldest_unpaid_age_days` is at least 30) or from `LIEN_FILED → CURRENT` (when the balance reaches zero or below) clears `lien_recorded_at` to null, sets `voting_eligible` back to true, and triggers the same `BALLOTING` eligible-weight recomputation. Falling back from `FORECLOSURE_REFERRAL → LATE_NOTICE`, which happens when a payment leaves the balance positive but below `lien_threshold_cents`, likewise clears `lien_recorded_at` and restores `voting_eligible` to true.

Ledger rows are readable by a `CommunityManager`, a `BoardTreasurer`, a `UnitOwner`, or a `TitleAgent`, and the listing may be narrowed by `unit_id`, `billing_run_id`, `source_case_id`, `special_assessment_id`, `source_claim_id`, `reverses_entry_id`, `entry_type`, `bucket`, and `status`. Separately, an owner statement capability returns the unit's per-bucket open balances together with its `oldest_unpaid_age_days`; because it is owner-scoped, a `UnitOwner` calling it for a unit they do not own is refused with `ERR_NOT_UNIT_OWNER`.

### 3.2. The Budget Adoption Cycle and Assessment Allocation

A budget begins life as a manager's draft. The creation payload carries the `fiscal_year` the budget funds, which must lie between 2020 and 2100 inclusive; an `operating_total_cents` annual operating expenditure total, which must be at least 0; a `reserve_contribution_cents` annual reserve contribution, likewise at least 0; an optional `ratification_meeting_id` naming the board meeting at which owners may veto the noticed budget; and the mandatory `expected_association_version` described in §2.6. Creation is deliberately destructive of stale work: any other budget of the same association and the same `fiscal_year` whose `status` is `DRAFT` or `PROPOSED` moves to `SUPERSEDED`, the new budget records that predecessor in its own `supersedes_budget_id`, and every vote whose `subject_type` is `BUDGET` and whose `subject_id` is the superseded budget has its `counted` flag set to false. A superseded `DRAFT` and a superseded `PROPOSED` are treated symmetrically in one respect and asymmetrically in another: both record the new budget's `supersedes_budget_id` pointer and both advance their own `version` by 1, but only the `DRAFT → SUPERSEDED` leg clears the ballot record by setting `counted` false on that budget's votes. Creation also advances `Association.version` by 1. Budgets may be listed and filtered by `association_id`, `supersedes_budget_id`, `ratification_meeting_id`, `status`, and `fiscal_year`.

Budget `status` takes one of six values — `DRAFT`, `PROPOSED`, `NOTICED`, `ADOPTED`, `REJECTED`, and `SUPERSEDED` — with `DRAFT` as the initial state and `ADOPTED` and `SUPERSEDED` as terminal. The cycle is genuinely cyclic: a board may push a budget back to `DRAFT` from `PROPOSED`, from `NOTICED`, or from `REJECTED`, and each return increments `revision_round`, which starts at 0.

Proposing a draft moves it `DRAFT → PROPOSED` and is the manager's act; the payload may carry `ratification_meeting_id` if it was not supplied at creation. Four conditions must hold. The budget must be in `DRAFT`. The association's `total_interest_bp` must equal exactly 10000, or the request fails with `ERR_INTEREST_SUM_INVALID`. The `reserve_contribution_cents` must be at least the statutory minimum, computed as `operating_total_cents` multiplied by `reserve_contribution_min_bp` divided by 10000 and rounded half up — with the default of 1000 basis points that is one tenth of the operating total — and a shortfall is refused with `ERR_RESERVE_CONTRIBUTION_TOO_LOW`. And no other budget of the association for the same `fiscal_year` may already be `ADOPTED`, or the request fails with `ERR_DUPLICATE_ADOPTED_BUDGET`. On success the server computes `assessment_total_cents` as `operating_total_cents` plus `reserve_contribution_cents`, computes `monthly_assessable_cents` as `assessment_total_cents` divided by 12 and rounded half up, and advances `Budget.version` by 1.

Mailing the statutory notice moves the budget `PROPOSED → NOTICED`. The budget must be `PROPOSED`; its `ratification_meeting_id` must be set; the referenced meeting's `status` must be `SCHEDULED` or `NOTICED`; that meeting's `agenda_kinds` must contain `BUDGET_RATIFICATION`, failing which the response is `ERR_AGENDA_KIND_MISSING`; and the meeting's `scheduled_start` must be at least `budget_notice_days` days after the logical clock, failing which the response is `ERR_NOTICE_PERIOD_NOT_SATISFIED`. With the default of thirty days, a ratification meeting scheduled twenty-nine days out cannot be noticed today. On success `notice_sent_at` is stamped with the logical clock; if the referenced meeting was `SCHEDULED` it is promoted to `NOTICED` with its own `notice_sent_at` stamped and `BoardMeeting.version` advanced by 1; and `Budget.version` advances by 1.

Returning a budget to draft is the board's prerogative and may carry an optional `reason`. The three legs differ in their side effects and must be implemented separately. From `PROPOSED → DRAFT` the only requirement is that the budget be `PROPOSED`, and the only effects are that `revision_round` rises by 1 and `Budget.version` rises by 1. From `NOTICED → DRAFT` the budget must be `NOTICED` and its ratification meeting must not be `IN_SESSION`; the effects are that `notice_sent_at` is cleared to null because the statutory notice is void and must be re-mailed, `revision_round` rises by 1, every vote whose `subject_type` is `BUDGET` and whose `subject_id` is this budget has `counted` set to false, and `Budget.version` rises by 1. From `REJECTED → DRAFT` the budget must be `REJECTED`; the effects are that `revision_round` rises by 1, `notice_sent_at` is cleared to null, `no_weight_bp` is reset to 0, every `BUDGET` vote on this budget has `counted` set to false, and `Budget.version` rises by 1. Together these three legs close the `DRAFT → PROPOSED → NOTICED → REJECTED → DRAFT` cycle.

**Veto-style ratification.** Adoption is the default outcome; owners must muster a veto to stop it. Ratification is recorded by a `BoardDirector` and requires that the budget be `NOTICED`, that the ratification meeting be `IN_SESSION` with `quorum_met` true, that the logical clock be at or after `notice_sent_at` plus `budget_notice_days` days, and — on the adoption branch — that `total_interest_bp` still equal exactly 10000 and that no other budget for the same `fiscal_year` be `ADOPTED`. The reserve-contribution minimum is re-checked here as well, because the association's `reserve_contribution_min_bp` may have changed since the proposal.

The tally that decides the branch works as follows. The candidate ballots are the votes whose `subject_type` is `BUDGET`, whose `subject_id` is this budget, whose `voter_kind` is `UNIT_OWNER`, and whose `cast_at` falls at or after the budget's `notice_sent_at` and at or before the logical clock. Among those, one ballot per `unit_id` survives: the latest by `cast_at`, breaking ties by the later `created_at`. In deliberate contrast with the special-assessment tally, **veto rights survive delinquency** — `voting_eligible` is *not* applied here, and the denominator is the whole association. `no_weight_bp` is the sum of `weight_bp` over the surviving ballots whose `choice` is `NO`, the denominator is the association's `total_interest_bp` (always 10000, never the eligible weight), and the budget is rejected when `no_weight_bp` is greater than or equal to `budget_veto_bp`. With the default veto threshold of 5000 basis points — half the association — a counted `NO` weight of exactly 5000 rejects the budget while 4999 adopts it, and a ratification meeting at which nobody voted adopts, because the no-weight is 0. Ballots cast before the notice, or before the notice was re-mailed after a return-to-draft, are excluded, because their `counted` flag was cleared.

The two branches out of `NOTICED` have deliberately disjoint side-effect sets. On `NOTICED → REJECTED`, the server writes `no_weight_bp` from the tally, does **not** touch any unit's `monthly_assessment_cents`, leaves `allocation_snapshot` empty, voids no certificate, and advances `Budget.version` by 1. On `NOTICED → ADOPTED`, the server writes `no_weight_bp` and stamps `adopted_at` with the logical clock, then runs a five-hop cascade. Hop one computes the allocation snapshot and writes every unit's `monthly_assessment_cents`, advancing each `Unit.version` by 1. Hop two: because those figures changed, every resale certificate of those units whose `status` is `ISSUED` and whose `pinned_budget_id` is not this budget becomes `VOIDED` with `requires_reissue` set to true. Hop three: because those certificates were voided by cascade rather than by an operator, a `REVERSAL` row of the negated certificate fee is posted against each certificate's `cert_fee_entry_id`, that row's `status` becomes `REVERSED`, and each affected unit's `balance_due_cents` is recomputed. Hop four: because balances fell, each affected unit's `delinquency_stage` is re-derived through the ladder, and a unit leaving `LIEN_FILED` clears `lien_recorded_at` and flips `voting_eligible` to true. Hop five: because a unit's `voting_eligible` flipped, every special assessment of the association whose `approval_status` is `BALLOTING` recomputes `eligible_weight_bp` and re-derives `counted` on its votes. Finally `Budget.version` advances by 1.

**The allocation algorithm.** Adoption is where per-unit assessments are minted, and the order of operations is pinned. First, the pool is fixed in integer cents *before* any per-unit arithmetic: for a budget adoption the pool is `assessment_total_cents` divided by 12 and rounded half up. Second, the participants are every unit of the association — with no delinquency filter, because every unit owes assessments — ordered by `interest_bp` descending and then by `created_at` ascending. Third, each participant's share is computed as the pool multiplied by that unit's `interest_bp`, divided by 10000, and rounded half up; this per-line rounding is mandatory and summing before rounding is wrong. Fourth, the rounding drift is measured as the pool minus the sum of the per-line shares. Fifth, the drift is distributed one cent at a time by cycling through the participants in the same pinned order, starting at the first participant and wrapping around as often as necessary: a positive drift adds one cent per step, a negative drift removes one cent per step, until the drift is exhausted. When the procedure ends, the sum of the shares equals the pool exactly — that post-condition must hold.

Each unit's `monthly_assessment_cents` receives its final share, and the budget's `allocation_snapshot` freezes one object per participant carrying `unit_id`, `interest_bp`, `monthly_cents` holding the final share, and `remainder_cent_awarded`, a boolean that is true exactly when the final share differs from the per-line rounded value computed in the third step. A billing run driven by this budget reports the absolute value of the drift measured in the fourth step as its `remainder_cents_allocated`. Units created after adoption are not retro-allocated and keep `monthly_assessment_cents` at 0 until the next adoption.

### 3.3. Board Meetings, Quorum, and the Vote Register

A meeting is scheduled by a `CommunityManager` with a `scheduled_start` that is inclusive, a `scheduled_end` that is exclusive, and a non-empty `agenda_kinds` array drawn from `REGULAR`, `BUDGET_RATIFICATION`, `HEARING`, `CONTRACT_AWARD`, and `SPECIAL_ASSESSMENT`. Meeting `status` runs through `SCHEDULED`, `NOTICED`, `IN_SESSION`, `ADJOURNED`, and `CANCELLED`, starting at `SCHEDULED`, with `ADJOURNED` and `CANCELLED` terminal. On creation `notice_sent_at` is null, `seated_director_count` is 0, `quorum_met` is false, and `hearing_slot_count` is 0. Meetings may be listed and filtered by `association_id` and `status`.

Mailing the meeting notice moves it `SCHEDULED → NOTICED` and requires that `scheduled_start` still lie strictly in the future relative to the logical clock; the effect is to stamp `notice_sent_at` with the clock and advance `BoardMeeting.version` by 1. Convening moves it `NOTICED → IN_SESSION` and requires that the meeting be `NOTICED`, that the logical clock have reached `scheduled_start`, and that the attendance list produce a `seated_director_count` of at least `board_quorum_seats`. Attendance is supplied as `attending_owner_ids`; the count is the number of attending owners whose `is_seated` is true and whose `board_role` is not `NONE`. On success `seated_director_count` is written, `quorum_met` is set to true, and `BoardMeeting.version` advances by 1. A short list refuses the convene with `ERR_BOARD_QUORUM_NOT_MET`; with the default of three seats, two attending seated directors are not enough.

Adjournment moves the meeting `IN_SESSION → ADJOURNED` and is guarded by two unfinished-business checks. No violation case whose `hearing_meeting_id` is this meeting may still be in `HEARING_SCHEDULED`, or the response is `ERR_HEARINGS_PENDING`; and no budget whose `ratification_meeting_id` is this meeting may still be `NOTICED`, or the response is `ERR_BUDGET_NOTICE_PENDING`. Adjournment's only effect is to advance `BoardMeeting.version` by 1.

Cancellation is the compensating action and may carry an optional `reason`. It is available from `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED` and from `NOTICED → CANCELLED`, and both legs perform the identical compensation. Every violation case docketed to this meeting whose `hearing_round` is 1 returns to `NOTICE_SENT`, with `hearing_meeting_id` and `hearing_slot_start` cleared to null and `hearing_round` returning to 0, and — importantly — with `cure_deadline_at` **not** extended. Every case docketed to this meeting whose `hearing_round` is 2 or more returns to `APPEALED`, with `hearing_meeting_id` and `hearing_slot_start` cleared to null and `hearing_round` left at its current value. In both cases the case's own `version` advances by 1. The meeting's `hearing_slot_count` becomes 0. Every budget whose `ratification_meeting_id` is this meeting and whose `status` is `NOTICED` returns to `PROPOSED` with `notice_sent_at` cleared to null and its own `version` advanced by 1. Finally `BoardMeeting.version` advances by 1.

Votes and ballots share one register. Casting is open to a `BoardDirector`, a `BoardTreasurer`, or a `UnitOwner`, and the payload carries `association_id`, a `subject_type` from `BUDGET`, `SPECIAL_ASSESSMENT`, `VENDOR_CONTRACT`, and `VIOLATION_FINE`, a `subject_id` that resolves polymorphically — to a budget when the type is `BUDGET`, to a special assessment when `SPECIAL_ASSESSMENT`, to a vendor contract when `VENDOR_CONTRACT`, and to a violation case when `VIOLATION_FINE` — a `choice` of `YES`, `NO`, or `ABSTAIN`, a `voter_owner_id` naming the natural person casting the vote, and then exactly one of two discriminators: `meeting_id` for a board vote or `unit_id` for a weighted owner ballot. From those the server derives `voter_kind`, which is `BOARD_DIRECTOR` when `meeting_id` is set and `UNIT_OWNER` when `unit_id` is set; `weight_bp`, which is the unit's `interest_bp` at cast time for owner ballots and 0 for board votes; and `counted`, initially the unit's `voting_eligible` for owner ballots and true for board votes. `cast_at` is stamped from the logical clock. An owner casting a ballot on a unit they do not own is refused with `ERR_NOT_UNIT_OWNER`, and the manager who proposed a budget may not cast a ratification ballot on it, which is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

An earlier ballot by the same unit on the same subject is never deleted or rewritten — it stays stored and simply loses at tally time, where the latest ballot per unit wins and superseded ballots have their `counted` flag set to false. When `subject_type` is `VENDOR_CONTRACT`, casting also recomputes that contract's `approval_yes_count` and advances `VendorContract.version` by 1. The register may be read by a `CommunityManager`, a `BoardDirector`, a `BoardTreasurer`, or an `AssociationAttorney`, filtered by `association_id`, `meeting_id`, `unit_id`, `voter_owner_id`, `subject_id`, and `subject_type`. Note that `counted` is recomputed at tally time as true for every board vote and, for owner ballots, as the unit's `voting_eligible` at that moment.

### 3.4. Special Assessments: Balloting, Collection, Rescission, and Refund

A special assessment is drafted by a `BoardTreasurer`, who supplies a `purpose` that will be disclosed on resale certificates, a `total_amount_cents` aggregate to be raised that must be at least 1, an `installment_count` between 1 and 24 inclusive, an optional `funding_claim_id` naming the construction-defect matter the assessment funds, and the mandatory `expected_association_version`. Creation advances `Association.version` by 1. Assessments may be listed and filtered by `association_id`, `funding_claim_id`, `approval_status`, and `funding_status`.

This entity carries **two independent state dimensions on one row**. The owner-approval dimension `approval_status` runs through `DRAFT`, `BALLOTING`, `APPROVED`, `FAILED`, and `RESCINDED`, starting at `DRAFT`. The cash-collection dimension `funding_status` runs through `UNFUNDED`, `PARTIALLY_COLLECTED`, `FULLY_COLLECTED`, `REFUNDING`, and `REFUNDED`, starting at `UNFUNDED`. Collection progresses without touching `approval_status`, and only rescission moves both. `FAILED` and `REFUNDED` are the terminal outcomes. Four derived money figures ride along: `charged_cents` is the signed sum of `amount_cents` over every `SPECIAL_ASSESSMENT_INSTALLMENT` row of this assessment plus their `REVERSAL` rows, so rescinded installments net out to zero; `collected_cents` is the sum of `applied_cents` recorded in payment allocations that targeted `SPECIAL_ASSESSMENT`-bucket rows of this assessment; `refunded_cents` is the absolute sum of `amount_cents` over this assessment's `POSTED` `CREDIT_REFUND` rows; and `installments_posted` counts the rounds already pushed to unit ledgers, starting at 0.

Opening balloting moves `approval_status` from `DRAFT` to `BALLOTING` and is a `BoardDirector` act. Four conditions apply: the assessment must be in `DRAFT`; the association's `total_interest_bp` must equal exactly 10000, or `ERR_INTEREST_SUM_INVALID` is returned; no other special assessment of the association may already be `BALLOTING`, or `ERR_BALLOT_ALREADY_OPEN` is returned; and when `funding_claim_id` is set, the referenced claim's `litigation_status` must be `TENDERED` or `IN_LITIGATION`. On success `ballot_opened_at` is stamped from the logical clock, `requires_supermajority_disclosure` is set to true exactly when `funding_claim_id` is present (and to false otherwise), forcing litigation disclosure on every certificate issued after approval, and `SpecialAssessment.version` advances by 1.

**The supermajority tally.** Closing the ballot is likewise a `BoardDirector` act and decides between two branches. The candidate ballots are the votes whose `subject_type` is `SPECIAL_ASSESSMENT`, whose `subject_id` is this assessment, whose `voter_kind` is `UNIT_OWNER`, and whose `cast_at` falls at or after `ballot_opened_at` and at or before the logical clock; board votes on the same subject are ignored entirely. One ballot per `unit_id` survives — the latest by `cast_at`, breaking ties by the later `created_at` — and superseded ballots have `counted` set to false. Of the surviving ballots, the counted ones are those whose unit has `voting_eligible` true **at tally time, not at cast time**; each surviving ballot's `counted` flag is written accordingly. `eligible_weight_bp` is the sum of `interest_bp` over every unit of the association whose `voting_eligible` is true, so a unit that never cast a ballot still sits in the denominator. `yes_weight_bp` is the sum of `weight_bp` over the counted ballots whose `choice` is `YES`; `ABSTAIN` ballots add nothing to the numerator and do not leave the denominator. If `eligible_weight_bp` is 0 — every unit having escalated to `LIEN_FILED` or `FORECLOSURE_REFERRAL` — the close is rejected with `ERR_NO_ELIGIBLE_VOTERS` rather than being treated as a failure. Otherwise the yes share in basis points is `yes_weight_bp` multiplied by 10000, divided by `eligible_weight_bp`, and floored; the assessment is approved when that share is greater than or equal to `special_assessment_approval_bp`. With the default two-thirds-plus threshold of 6667 basis points, a yes share of exactly 6667 approves and 6666 fails.

The two close-ballot branches carry symmetric bookkeeping and one asymmetric fiscal effect. On `BALLOTING → APPROVED`, the server writes `eligible_weight_bp`, `yes_weight_bp`, and `ballot_closed_at` from the logical clock, increases the association's `reserve_designated_cents` by `total_amount_cents` and advances `Association.version` by 1, leaves `funding_status` untouched at `UNFUNDED`, and advances `SpecialAssessment.version` by 1. On `BALLOTING → FAILED`, the server writes the same three fields — `eligible_weight_bp`, `yes_weight_bp`, and `ballot_closed_at` — does **not** change `reserve_designated_cents`, and advances `SpecialAssessment.version` by 1.

Posting an installment round is the treasurer's act and requires `due_on` and `effective_period` in the payload. The assessment's `approval_status` must be `APPROVED`, and `installments_posted` must be strictly below `installment_count`, or the request is refused with `ERR_INSTALLMENTS_EXHAUSTED`. Each round reuses the same allocator as budget adoption, with one difference: the pool for rounds one through `installment_count` minus one is `total_amount_cents` divided by `installment_count` and rounded half up, while the **final** round instead uses `total_amount_cents` minus the sum of the pools already posted, so that the posted installments sum to `total_amount_cents` exactly with no drift. Participants, per-line half-up rounding, drift measurement, and the cent-by-cent cycling distribution are identical to §3.2. One `SPECIAL_ASSESSMENT_INSTALLMENT` row per unit is posted with that unit's share, `installments_posted` rises by 1, `charged_cents` is recomputed, every affected unit's `balance_due_cents` is recomputed and its `delinquency_stage` re-derived with `Unit.version` advancing by 1, and `SpecialAssessment.version` advances by 1.

Collection moves the funding dimension without any operator action. When a payment waterfall credits `SPECIAL_ASSESSMENT`-bucket rows of an approved assessment and the resulting `collected_cents` is greater than 0 but strictly less than `total_amount_cents`, `funding_status` moves `UNFUNDED → PARTIALLY_COLLECTED`, the association's `restricted_sa_cash_cents` rises by the applied cents with `Association.version` advancing by 1, and `SpecialAssessment.version` advances by 1. When a later payment brings `collected_cents` to `total_amount_cents` or beyond, `funding_status` moves `PARTIALLY_COLLECTED → FULLY_COLLECTED` with the same cash and version effects.

Rescission is a `BoardDirector` act carrying an optional `reason` and moves `approval_status` from `APPROVED` to `RESCINDED`. It is blocked when `funding_claim_id` is set and that claim's `litigation_status` is `IN_LITIGATION` — a live suit cannot be defunded — in which case the response is `ERR_LITIGATION_ACTIVE`. On success the association's `reserve_designated_cents` falls by `total_amount_cents` with `Association.version` advancing by 1; every `POSTED` `SPECIAL_ASSESSMENT_INSTALLMENT` row of this assessment is answered with a `REVERSAL` row and flips to `REVERSED`, with each affected unit's `balance_due_cents` recomputed and `delinquency_stage` re-derived; `funding_status` becomes `REFUNDING` when `collected_cents` is greater than 0 and becomes `REFUNDED` directly when nothing was ever collected; and `SpecialAssessment.version` advances by 1.

Refunding collected cash is the treasurer's act, with a payload carrying `unit_id`, `amount_cents`, `effective_period`, and an optional `memo`. It is available only while `funding_status` is `REFUNDING`; attempting it otherwise yields `ERR_INVALID_BALLOT_STATE`. The requested amount may not exceed `collected_cents` minus `refunded_cents`, or the response is `ERR_REFUND_EXCEEDS_COLLECTED`. A successful refund posts a `CREDIT_REFUND` row with a negative `amount_cents` tagged with this assessment's `special_assessment_id`, raises `refunded_cents`, recomputes the unit's `balance_due_cents` and re-derives its `delinquency_stage` with `Unit.version` advancing by 1. When the posting brings `refunded_cents` up to `collected_cents`, `funding_status` moves `REFUNDING → REFUNDED`, the association's `restricted_sa_cash_cents` falls by the refunded cents, and `Association.version` advances by 1 along with `SpecialAssessment.version`. Refunds run the same allocator in reverse, over `SPECIAL_ASSESSMENT`-bucket rows only.

### 3.5. The Billing Run Checkpoint

Once a month a `CommunityManager` executes a billing run, which is the single ops-run checkpoint of this system: it posts the period's money, ages every account, and sweeps every expiry in one atomic operation. The payload carries `source_budget_id`, the `period` in `YYYY-MM` form, and the `due_on` date to be stamped on every assessment charge the run produces. A run for a period this association has already billed is refused with `ERR_PERIOD_ALREADY_BILLED`, and a `source_budget_id` that is not `ADOPTED`, or whose `fiscal_year` does not cover the `period`, is refused with `ERR_BUDGET_NOT_ADOPTED`. On completion `executed_at` is stamped from the logical clock. Runs may be listed and filtered by `association_id`, `source_budget_id`, and `period`.

The run's work happens in a fixed sequence. First, one `ASSESSMENT` row per unit is posted from the budget's `allocation_snapshot`, each carrying that unit's `monthly_cents` as its positive `amount_cents`, the run's `due_on` as its `due_on`, the run's `period` as its `effective_period`, and the run's identifier as its `billing_run_id`; the run reports `assessment_charged_cents` as the sum of those amounts, `charged_unit_count` as the number of units that received at least one charge in this run, and `remainder_cents_allocated` as the absolute rounding drift the allocator reconciled.

Second, late fees are evaluated per unit **after** that unit's assessment charge has been posted. For each of the unit's ledger rows, the aging in days is the difference between the run's `executed_at` and that row's `due_on`, floored to whole days. The delinquent principal is the sum, over the unit's rows whose `status` is `POSTED`, whose `bucket` is `ASSESSMENT`, whose `amount_cents` is positive, and whose aging in days is **strictly greater** than `late_fee_grace_days`, of `amount_cents` minus `applied_cents`. The boundary matters: a charge aged exactly `late_fee_grace_days` — ten days by default — contributes nothing, because the charge must be strictly older than the grace period. Special-assessment, fine, collection-cost, and interest balances never enter the late-fee base. If the delinquent principal is zero or less, the fee is zero. Otherwise the percentage limb is the delinquent principal multiplied by `late_fee_percent_bp`, divided by 10000, and rounded half up — one tenth of the delinquent principal at the default of 1000 basis points — and the fee is then that percentage limb raised to at least `late_fee_min_cents` and afterwards lowered to at most `late_fee_cap_cents`, applying the floor before the cap in that order. If the unit's `delinquency_stage` is `FORECLOSURE_REFERRAL`, the fee is instead exactly `late_fee_min_cents`, because collection is with counsel and only the floor accrues. Finally, if a `POSTED` `LATE_FEE` row already exists for this unit whose `effective_period` equals the run's `period`, the fee is forced to zero — there are never two late fees for one period. A positive fee is posted as a `LATE_FEE` row in bucket `LATE_FEE` with a positive `amount_cents`, the run's `due_on` as its `due_on`, and the run's `period` as its `effective_period`. The run reports `late_fees_charged_cents` as the sum of the fees it posted.

Third, every unit's `balance_due_cents`, `credit_balance_cents`, and `oldest_unpaid_age_days` are recomputed and `delinquency_stage` is re-derived through the ladder of §3.1, with `escalated_unit_count` counting the units whose posture advanced during this run. A unit crossing into `LIEN_FILED` during a run stamps `lien_recorded_at`, flips `voting_eligible` to false, and triggers the balloting eligible-weight recomputation described in §3.1.

Fourth, the run finalizes unappealed fines: every violation case whose `status` is `FINE_ASSESSED` and whose `appeal_deadline_at` the logical clock has strictly passed advances `FINE_ASSESSED → FINE_UPHELD`, the run's `fines_finalized_count` rises by 1 for each, and each case's own `version` advances by 1. The fine row itself is untouched; the money was already owed.

Fifth, the run sweeps vendor contracts. Any contract whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose `insurance_cert_expires_at` is at or before the logical clock moves `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` with `suspension_reason` set to `INSURANCE_LAPSED`, and every work order of that contract whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS` becomes `ON_HOLD` with `hold_reason` set to `CONTRACT_SUSPENDED`, encumbrances retained; the contract's `version` advances by 1. Any contract whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `SUSPENDED` and whose `effective_to` the logical clock has reached or passed moves to `EXPIRED`, every work order of that contract whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `ON_HOLD` becomes `CANCELLED` with `encumbered_cents` set to 0, `committed_cents` is recomputed as the sum of `encumbered_cents` over the surviving orders, and the contract's `version` advances by 1.

Sixth and last, the run sweeps stale resale certificates: every certificate whose `status` is `ISSUED` and whose `expires_at` the logical clock has reached or passed moves `ISSUED → EXPIRED`, the unit's `transfer_blocked` is set back to true with `Unit.version` advancing by 1, and the certificate's own `version` advances by 1.

### 3.6. Common Elements, Vendor Contracts, Work Order Execution, and Reserve Funding

Common elements are the association's physical plant and simultaneously its reserve components. Registering one requires a `name` label, an `element_class` drawn from `ROOF`, `ELEVATOR`, `FACADE`, `POOL`, `GARAGE`, `LIFE_SAFETY`, and `LANDSCAPE`, a `replacement_cost_cents` of at least 0, a `useful_life_years` of at least 1, and an `effective_age_years` of at least 0. From `element_class` the server derives `is_life_safety`, which is true exactly when the class is `ELEVATOR` or `LIFE_SAFETY`. Serviceability is tracked in `status`, which runs through `SERVICEABLE`, `IMPAIRED`, `OUT_OF_SERVICE`, and `DECOMMISSIONED` and starts at `SERVICEABLE`. A component also carries a `reserve_designated_cents` figure for reserve cash earmarked to it, starting at 0, and a `last_serviced_at` instant stamped at the completion of the most recent work order on it. Remaining life is `useful_life_years` minus `effective_age_years`. Elements may be listed and filtered by `association_id`, `element_class`, and `status`. A manager may revise a component's `replacement_cost_cents`, `useful_life_years`, `effective_age_years`, `status`, or `reserve_designated_cents`; every such revision advances `CommonElement.version` by 1, which — as §3.6 explains below — invalidates any board award that pinned the old version.

A vendor contract covers exactly one element. Drafting one requires `covered_element_id`, `vendor_name`, a `contract_cap_cents` not-to-exceed authorization of at least 1, an `insurance_cert_expires_at` expiry for the vendor's certificate of insurance, an `effective_from` term start that is inclusive, and an `effective_to` term end that is exclusive. Contract `status` runs through `DRAFT`, `PENDING_APPROVAL`, `APPROVED`, `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED`, `TERMINATED`, and `EXPIRED`, starting at `DRAFT`, with `TERMINATED` and `EXPIRED` terminal. Derived money figures are `committed_cents`, the sum of `encumbered_cents` over the contract's work orders in `SCHEDULED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `ON_HOLD`, or `COMPLETED`, and `invoiced_cents`, the sum of `final_cost_cents` over its `INVOICED` orders; both start at 0. `approval_yes_count` starts at 0, `pinned_element_version` is null until the board awards, and `suspension_reason` is an enum over `NONE`, `INSURANCE_LAPSED`, `PERFORMANCE`, and `ELEMENT_SPEC_CHANGED` defaulting to `NONE`. A manager may separately record a renewed certificate by supplying a new `insurance_cert_expires_at`, which advances `VendorContract.version` by 1. Contracts may be listed and filtered by `association_id`, `covered_element_id`, and `status`.

Submitting a draft for a board award moves it `DRAFT → PENDING_APPROVAL`. Three term conditions apply: `effective_from` must be strictly before `effective_to`, and `insurance_cert_expires_at` must be at or after `effective_to` so that the certificate covers the whole term — either failure yields `ERR_CONTRACT_TERM_INVALID` — and the covered element's `status` must not be `DECOMMISSIONED`, failing which the response is `ERR_ELEMENT_NOT_SERVICEABLE`. The only effect is to advance `VendorContract.version` by 1.

Recording the board award moves the contract `PENDING_APPROVAL → APPROVED` and is a `BoardDirector` act. The award requires that `approval_yes_count` — computed as the number of counted votes whose `subject_type` is `VENDOR_CONTRACT` and whose `subject_id` is this contract, cast at a board meeting whose `agenda_kinds` contains `CONTRACT_AWARD` and whose `status` is `IN_SESSION` or `ADJOURNED` — be at least `board_quorum_seats`, and it requires the covered element's `status` to be `SERVICEABLE` or `IMPAIRED`. A missing agenda kind yields `ERR_AGENDA_KIND_MISSING`; an unserviceable element yields `ERR_ELEMENT_NOT_SERVICEABLE`. On success the server writes `approval_yes_count`, **pins** `pinned_element_version` to the element's `version` at that instant, and advances `VendorContract.version` by 1.

Activation moves the contract `APPROVED → ACTIVE` and is a manager act. The logical clock must lie at or after `effective_from` and strictly before `effective_to`; `insurance_cert_expires_at` must be strictly after the logical clock, failing which the response is `ERR_VENDOR_INSURANCE_LAPSED`; and the element's current `version` must still equal `pinned_element_version`, because a mutated element invalidates the board's award — a mismatch yields `ERR_ELEMENT_SPEC_CHANGED`. The only effect is to advance `VendorContract.version` by 1.

Suspension and reinstatement form a cycle. A manager may suspend an `ACTIVE` contract, supplying a `suspension_reason`; every work order of that contract whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `IN_PROGRESS` becomes `ON_HOLD` with `hold_reason` set to `CONTRACT_SUSPENDED`, `encumbered_cents` is left untouched so `committed_cents` is unchanged, each such order's `version` advances by 1, and `VendorContract.version` advances by 1. The billing run's certificate-lapse sweep drives the same `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` transition with `suspension_reason` forced to `INSURANCE_LAPSED`. Reinstating moves `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` and requires that `insurance_cert_expires_at` be strictly after the logical clock, that the logical clock be strictly before `effective_to`, and that the element's `version` still equal `pinned_element_version`; the effects are that `suspension_reason` returns to `NONE`, every work order of the contract whose `status` is `ON_HOLD` **and** whose `hold_reason` is `CONTRACT_SUSPENDED` returns to `SCHEDULED` with `hold_reason` set to `NONE` and its own `version` advanced by 1, and `VendorContract.version` advances by 1. Orders held for other reasons stay held.

Termination is a `BoardDirector` act carrying an optional `reason` and is available from `ACTIVE → TERMINATED` and from `SUSPENDED → TERMINATED`. Terminating from `ACTIVE` additionally requires that no work order of the contract be `IN_PROGRESS`, or the response is `ERR_WORK_IN_PROGRESS`. Both legs perform the same compensation: every work order whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `ON_HOLD` becomes `CANCELLED` with `encumbered_cents` set to 0, `committed_cents` is recomputed to equal the sum of `encumbered_cents` over the contract's non-cancelled orders as Invariant E requires, and `VendorContract.version` advances by 1.

Scheduling maintenance work is where money gets encumbered. The payload carries `contract_id`, `element_id`, a `scope` description, a `window_start` outage window start that is inclusive, a `window_end` outage window end that is inclusive, an `estimated_cost_cents` of at least 0, an optional `is_emergency` boolean defaulting to false, an optional `claim_id` naming the insurance claim driving the repair, and the mandatory `expected_contract_version`. Emergency orders on life-safety elements skip the board award vote requirement. Four business checks apply beyond the standard ladder: the order's `element_id` must equal the contract's `covered_element_id`, or `ERR_ELEMENT_CONTRACT_MISMATCH` is returned; the window must lie inside the contract term — at or after `effective_from` and strictly before `effective_to` — with `window_start` strictly before `window_end`, or `ERR_WINDOW_OUTSIDE_CONTRACT_TERM` is returned; the buffered window must not collide with another open order on the same element; and the new encumbrance must fit inside the cap. On success `encumbered_cents` is set to `estimated_cost_cents`, the contract's `committed_cents` rises by the same amount, and `VendorContract.version` advances by 1. Work order `status` runs through `SCHEDULED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, `ON_HOLD`, `COMPLETED`, `INVOICED`, and `CANCELLED`, starting at `SCHEDULED`, with `INVOICED` and `CANCELLED` terminal; `hold_reason` is an enum over `NONE`, `CONTRACT_SUSPENDED`, `ELEMENT_UNAVAILABLE`, and `FUNDING` defaulting to `NONE`; `final_cost_cents` is supplied only at invoicing. Orders may be listed and filtered by `association_id`, `contract_id`, `element_id`, `claim_id`, and `status`.

**The outage-window buffer rule.** Two orders on the same element must be separated by a mandatory quiet buffer of `element_window_buffer_hours` — one full day at the default of 24 hours — applied to both ends. Concretely, the new order is rejected when its `window_start` is at or before an existing order's `window_end` advanced forward by the buffer **and** its `window_end` is at or after that existing order's `window_start` pushed back by the buffer. The comparison is over existing work orders on the same element whose `status` is `SCHEDULED`, `IN_PROGRESS`, or `ON_HOLD`, the intervals are closed, and **touching endpoints collide**. A rejection carries `ERR_ELEMENT_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.

**The contract cap rule.** A new order is admitted only when `estimated_cost_cents` plus the contract's current `committed_cents` plus its `invoiced_cents` is less than or equal to `contract_cap_cents`. Equality is allowed; one cent more is refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_CAP_EXCEEDED`.

Starting work moves an order `SCHEDULED → IN_PROGRESS`, and the same capability resumes a held order `ON_HOLD → IN_PROGRESS`. From `SCHEDULED` the requirements are that the contract's `status` be `ACTIVE`, that the contract's `insurance_cert_expires_at` be strictly after the order's `window_end` (a certificate that lapses mid-outage is refused with `ERR_VENDOR_INSURANCE_LAPSED`), that the element's `status` not be `DECOMMISSIONED`, and that the logical clock have reached `window_start` — an early start is refused with `ERR_WINDOW_NOT_STARTED`. From `ON_HOLD` the requirements are that the contract's `status` be `ACTIVE`, so an order held for `CONTRACT_SUSPENDED` cannot be resumed while its contract remains suspended, and that the element's `status` not be `DECOMMISSIONED`. Both legs set `hold_reason` to `NONE` and drive the element's `status` to `OUT_OF_SERVICE` when the element's `is_life_safety` is true and to `IMPAIRED` otherwise, advancing `CommonElement.version` by 1, and both advance `MaintenanceWorkOrder.version` by 1.

Holding work in progress moves it `IN_PROGRESS → ON_HOLD`, sets `hold_reason` from the request body, drives the element's `status` to `IMPAIRED` with `CommonElement.version` advancing by 1, leaves `encumbered_cents` unchanged, and advances the order's `version` by 1. Completing work moves it `IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED`, requires that the logical clock have reached `window_start`, drives the element's `status` to `SERVICEABLE`, stamps the element's `last_serviced_at` with the logical clock, and — when the payload sets the optional `resets_component` flag to true — resets the element's `effective_age_years` to 0, which changes the fully-funded basis used by the reserve computation below; both `CommonElement.version` and `MaintenanceWorkOrder.version` advance by 1. When `resets_component` is omitted it is treated as false and `effective_age_years` is left alone.

Invoicing moves a completed order `COMPLETED → INVOICED` and may be recorded by a `CommunityManager` or a `BoardTreasurer` supplying `final_cost_cents`. Two fiscal gates apply. The overrun tolerance admits a `final_cost_cents` no greater than `estimated_cost_cents` multiplied by 11 and divided by 10 with the result floored — a ten percent overrun, inclusive of the boundary — and the sum of the contract's `invoiced_cents` and `final_cost_cents` must not exceed `contract_cap_cents`; either failure yields `ERR_INVOICE_EXCEEDS_TOLERANCE`. Separately, the association's `operating_cash_cents` must be at least `final_cost_cents`, or the response is `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_OPERATING_CASH`. On success `encumbered_cents` becomes 0, the contract's `committed_cents` falls by `estimated_cost_cents` while its `invoiced_cents` rises by `final_cost_cents` with `VendorContract.version` advancing by 1, the association's `operating_cash_cents` falls by `final_cost_cents` with `Association.version` advancing by 1, and `MaintenanceWorkOrder.version` advances by 1.

Cancelling an order is available from `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED` and from `ON_HOLD → CANCELLED`, and may carry an optional `reason`. Both legs set `encumbered_cents` to 0 and reduce the contract's `committed_cents` by exactly the released encumbrance, advancing `VendorContract.version` by 1, and both advance the order's own `version` by 1. The `SCHEDULED` leg additionally sets `hold_reason` to `NONE` and returns the element's `status` to `SERVICEABLE`, but only when no other work order on that element has `status` `IN_PROGRESS` or `ON_HOLD` and no insurance claim on that element has an `adjustment_status` of `UNDER_REVIEW`, `ADJUSTED_ASSOCIATION`, or `ADJUSTED_SPLIT`.

**Reserve funding adequacy.** A computed report answers whether the association's reserves are adequate, and it accepts an optional `method` selector. The report considers every common element of the association whose `status` is not `DECOMMISSIONED`. Under the default **straight-line** method — selected when `method` is omitted or is `STRAIGHT_LINE` — each component's fully-funded basis is its `replacement_cost_cents` multiplied by the lesser of `effective_age_years` and `useful_life_years`, divided by `useful_life_years`, and rounded half up; the target accrues linearly with effective age over the whole useful life, so every non-decommissioned component contributes something. Under the **component** method — selected when `method` is `COMPONENT` — the component's remaining life is `useful_life_years` minus `effective_age_years` but never less than 0, and the fully-funded basis is 0 when that remaining life exceeds a thirty-year cash-flow horizon and is otherwise `replacement_cost_cents` multiplied by thirty minus the remaining life, divided by thirty, and rounded half up. Rounding happens per component and the results are then summed into `fully_funded_cents`. Spendable reserves, reported as `spendable_cents`, are the association's `reserve_balance_cents` minus its `reserve_designated_cents`, so litigation designations and approved special assessments depress the score. The `percent_funded_bp` figure is 10000 when `fully_funded_cents` is 0 — an association with no components reports full funding rather than dividing by zero — and is otherwise `spendable_cents` multiplied by 10000, divided by `fully_funded_cents`, and floored. The reported `posture` is `ADEQUATE` when `percent_funded_bp` is greater than or equal to `reserve_adequacy_threshold_bp` and `DEFICIENT` otherwise; a value exactly equal to the seventy-percent default threshold is `ADEQUATE`. Nothing is persisted by this report. Because the two methods weight near-term components very differently, the same reserve balance and the same component roster can report `ADEQUATE` under one method and `DEFICIENT` under the other.

### 3.7. Insurance Claims: Adjustment, the Deductible Waterfall, and Litigation

A `CommunityManager` reports a loss under the master policy by supplying `association_id`, a `loss_type` drawn from `WATER`, `FIRE`, `WIND`, `CONSTRUCTION_DEFECT`, and `LIABILITY`, the `loss_at` date of loss, a `gross_damage_cents` total of at least 1, the `within_unit_boundary_cents` portion falling inside unit boundaries per the declaration, and optionally `unit_id` for the unit where the loss originated, `element_id` for the damaged common element, and an `affected_unit_ids` array of the units that will share any deductible chargeback, defaulting to the empty array. Creation stamps `reported_at` from the logical clock. A claim naming neither `unit_id` nor `element_id`, or whose `within_unit_boundary_cents` exceeds its `gross_damage_cents`, is rejected with `ERR_CLAIM_SCOPE_INVALID`. Claims may be listed and filtered by `association_id`, `unit_id`, `element_id`, `adjustment_status`, and `litigation_status`.

Like a special assessment, a claim carries **two independent dimensions**. The `adjustment_status` dimension runs over `REPORTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, `ADJUSTED_ASSOCIATION`, `ADJUSTED_OWNER`, `ADJUSTED_SPLIT`, `DENIED`, `SETTLED`, and `APPEALED`, starting at `REPORTED`, with `SETTLED` the only terminal value. The `litigation_status` dimension runs over `NONE`, `TENDERED`, `IN_LITIGATION`, and `RESOLVED`, starting at `NONE`, and evolves entirely independently — a claim can be settled for adjustment purposes while its suit is still tendered, and a claim can be in litigation while its adjustment is denied. The claim also carries `common_element_damage_cents`, `deductible_allocation`, `association_absorbed_cents`, `owner_chargeback_cents`, `appeal_round`, and `disclosure_required`, all starting at 0, the empty array, or false as appropriate.

An `InsuranceAdjuster` takes a claim under review. From `REPORTED → UNDER_REVIEW` the requirements are that `adjustment_status` be `REPORTED` or `APPEALED` and that at least one of `unit_id` or `element_id` be set; the side effects are that `common_element_damage_cents` is computed as `gross_damage_cents` minus `within_unit_boundary_cents` and `InsuranceClaim.version` advances by 1. The same capability closes the denial cycle from `APPEALED → UNDER_REVIEW`, which requires only that `adjustment_status` be `APPEALED` and whose sole effect is to advance `InsuranceClaim.version` by 1 — `common_element_damage_cents` is **not** recomputed on the re-review leg, because the damage figures did not change.

The adjustment itself is a single adjuster capability that takes a `responsibility` selector with four values and branches into four disjoint outcomes. In every case `adjustment_status` must be `UNDER_REVIEW` first, and a `responsibility` that contradicts the claim's damage figures is refused with `ERR_RESPONSIBILITY_MISMATCH`.

- `ASSOCIATION` moves the claim to `ADJUSTED_ASSOCIATION` and requires `common_element_damage_cents` to be greater than 0. It sets `association_absorbed_cents` to the lesser of `master_policy_deductible_cents` and `association_deductible_cap_cents`, sets `owner_chargeback_cents` to 0, leaves `deductible_allocation` empty, reduces the association's `operating_cash_cents` by `association_absorbed_cents` with `Association.version` advancing by 1, and — when `element_id` is set and that element's `status` is `SERVICEABLE` — drives the element to `IMPAIRED` with `CommonElement.version` advancing by 1. `InsuranceClaim.version` advances by 1.
- `OWNER` moves the claim to `ADJUSTED_OWNER` and requires `unit_id` to be set and `within_unit_boundary_cents` to equal `gross_damage_cents` exactly, meaning no common element was damaged. It sets `association_absorbed_cents` to 0 and `owner_chargeback_cents` to the full `master_policy_deductible_cents`, posts one `INSURANCE_DEDUCTIBLE_CHARGEBACK` row in bucket `ASSESSMENT` on that unit for `owner_chargeback_cents` with `due_on` thirty days after the logical clock, recomputes that unit's `balance_due_cents` and re-derives its `delinquency_stage` with `Unit.version` advancing by 1, and records `deductible_allocation` as exactly one row for that `unit_id`. No association cash moves and no element changes state. `InsuranceClaim.version` advances by 1.
- `SPLIT` moves the claim to `ADJUSTED_SPLIT` and requires that `affected_unit_ids` be non-empty — an empty array is a rejection with `ERR_NO_AFFECTED_UNITS`, never a silent no-op — and that both `within_unit_boundary_cents` and `common_element_damage_cents` be strictly greater than 0. It runs the deductible waterfall below, posts one `INSURANCE_DEDUCTIBLE_CHARGEBACK` row per allocation row in allocation order, recomputes each affected unit's `balance_due_cents` and re-derives each `delinquency_stage` with each `Unit.version` advancing by 1, reduces the association's `operating_cash_cents` by `association_absorbed_cents` with `Association.version` advancing by 1, and advances `InsuranceClaim.version` by 1.
- `DENY` moves the claim to `DENIED`, posts no ledger entry, moves no cash, leaves `deductible_allocation` empty, and advances `InsuranceClaim.version` by 1.

**The deductible waterfall.** The tier basis is the claim's `gross_damage_cents` and **only** that figure; the owner exposure the waterfall itself produces is a reported output and is never fed back into the tier decision. The association's share in basis points is 10000 when the basis is strictly below 5000000 cents, 6000 when the basis is at or above 5000000 but strictly below 25000000 cents, and 3000 when the basis is at or above 25000000 cents; the ceilings are exclusive at the low end of each band, so a basis of exactly 5000000 lands in the sixty-percent band and exactly 25000000 lands in the thirty-percent band. The raw association slice is `master_policy_deductible_cents` multiplied by that share, divided by 10000, and rounded half up — one such rounding, on this figure only — and `association_absorbed_cents` is the lesser of that raw slice and `association_deductible_cap_cents`. The residue is `master_policy_deductible_cents` minus `association_absorbed_cents`. The participants are the units named in `affected_unit_ids` with no delinquency filter, ordered by `heated_sqft` descending and then by `created_at` ascending; the total square footage is the sum of their `heated_sqft`. Each unit's provisional allocation is the residue multiplied by that unit's `heated_sqft`, divided by the total square footage, and **floored** — a floor here, not a rounding. The drift is the residue minus the sum of the floored allocations and is at least 0 and strictly less than the number of participants; one additional cent is given to each of the first drift participants in that pinned order, at most one cent each. `owner_chargeback_cents` is then the sum of the allocations and equals the residue exactly. `deductible_allocation` is the ordered array of objects carrying `unit_id`, `heated_sqft`, and `allocated_cents` in participant order. Note that this remainder rule differs deliberately from the assessment allocator of §3.2: here allocations are floored and the drift is topped up one cent per unit in a single pass, whereas there each line is rounded half up and the drift is distributed by cycling.

A denied claim may be appealed by a `CommunityManager` or by a `UnitOwner`, with an optional `reason`, moving `adjustment_status` from `DENIED` to `APPEALED`. `appeal_round` must currently be strictly below 2; a third appeal is refused with `ERR_APPEAL_LIMIT_REACHED`. The effects are that `appeal_round` rises by 1 and `InsuranceClaim.version` advances by 1.

Settlement is an adjuster capability available from `ADJUSTED_ASSOCIATION`, `ADJUSTED_OWNER`, and `ADJUSTED_SPLIT`. All three legs require that every maintenance work order whose `claim_id` is this claim have a `status` of `INVOICED` or `CANCELLED`; an order still open refuses the settlement with `ERR_CLAIM_WORK_OPEN`. The `ADJUSTED_ASSOCIATION` and `ADJUSTED_SPLIT` legs additionally require that `litigation_status` not be `IN_LITIGATION`, and both, when `element_id` is set and every work order on that element is terminal, return the element's `status` to `SERVICEABLE` with `CommonElement.version` advancing by 1. The `ADJUSTED_OWNER` leg carries **neither** of those two extras: an owner-responsibility claim may settle while litigation is pending, and it never touches a common element, because no common element was damaged. All three legs advance `InsuranceClaim.version` by 1.

The litigation dimension is driven exclusively by the `AssociationAttorney` through a capability that takes a `stage` selector. Moving `NONE → TENDERED` requires the claim's `loss_type` to be `CONSTRUCTION_DEFECT` and its `adjustment_status` to be `DENIED`, `ADJUSTED_ASSOCIATION`, or `ADJUSTED_SPLIT`. It sets `disclosure_required` to true and then cascades: every resale certificate whose `status` is `ISSUED` and whose `unit_id` appears in `affected_unit_ids` becomes `VOIDED` with `requires_reissue` set to true, because an already-issued certificate cannot disclose a matter tendered after it was printed; each such certificate's `cert_fee_entry_id` is answered with a `REVERSAL` row, and the affected unit's `balance_due_cents` and `delinquency_stage` are recomputed with `Unit.version` advancing by 1. `InsuranceClaim.version` advances by 1. Moving `TENDERED → IN_LITIGATION` requires `litigation_status` to be `TENDERED` and requires that some special assessment exist whose `funding_claim_id` is this claim and whose `approval_status` is `APPROVED` — suit may not be filed until the owners have funded it — failing which the response is `ERR_LITIGATION_FUNDING_MISSING`; the only effect is that `InsuranceClaim.version` advances by 1. Moving `IN_LITIGATION → RESOLVED` requires `litigation_status` to be `IN_LITIGATION`, sets `disclosure_required` back to false so future certificates stop disclosing the matter, and advances `InsuranceClaim.version` by 1. `disclosure_required` is true exactly when `litigation_status` is `TENDERED` or `IN_LITIGATION`.

### 3.8. Rules Enforcement: Notices, Hearings, Fine Tiers, and Appeals

A `CommunityManager` or `BoardDirector` opens a rules-enforcement case by supplying `association_id`, the cited `unit_id`, the `rule_code` on which repeat-tier counting keys, the `reported_by_owner_id` of the director who reported the violation, and optionally `continuing_days`, the number of days the violation persisted, which must be at least 1 and defaults to 1. The case is born with `status` `OPEN`, `hearing_round` 0, `fine_tier` 0, and `fine_amount_cents` 0; `prior_case_id`, `decision_owner_id`, `hearing_meeting_id`, `notice_sent_at`, `cure_deadline_at`, `hearing_slot_start`, `fine_entry_id`, and `appeal_deadline_at` are all null. `status` runs over `OPEN`, `NOTICE_SENT`, `HEARING_SCHEDULED`, `FINE_ASSESSED`, `APPEALED`, `FINE_UPHELD`, `FINE_VACATED`, `DISMISSED`, and `CURED`, of which `CURED`, `DISMISSED`, `FINE_UPHELD`, and `FINE_VACATED` are terminal. Cases may be listed and filtered by `association_id`, `unit_id`, `prior_case_id`, `reported_by_owner_id`, `decision_owner_id`, `hearing_meeting_id`, `fine_entry_id`, `status`, and `rule_code`.

Mailing the notice is a manager act moving `OPEN → NOTICE_SENT`. It requires that the case be `OPEN` and that the cited unit exist and belong to the same association. It stamps `notice_sent_at` from the logical clock, sets `cure_deadline_at` to `notice_sent_at` advanced by `cure_period_days`, resolves `prior_case_id` to the most recent qualifying earlier case, and advances `ViolationCase.version` by 1. The predecessor is resolved as the first row among this association's cases for the same `unit_id` and the same `rule_code` whose `notice_sent_at` falls within `repeat_lookback_days` before this case's `notice_sent_at` and whose `status` is `FINE_ASSESSED`, `FINE_UPHELD`, or `CURED`, ordered by `notice_sent_at` descending and then by `created_at` descending; when no such row exists, `prior_case_id` stays null. Cases in `DISMISSED` or `FINE_VACATED` are never written as anyone's `prior_case_id`, so a dismissal or a vacated fine can never inflate a later tier.

Recording a cure is a manager act moving `NOTICE_SENT → CURED`. It requires that the case be `NOTICE_SENT` and that the logical clock be at or before `cure_deadline_at` — the deadline instant itself still cures. A cure recorded after the deadline is refused with `ERR_CURE_PERIOD_ACTIVE`. There is no ledger effect, the case stays in the repeat chain for future tier counting, and `ViolationCase.version` advances by 1.

Docketing a hearing is a `BoardDirector` act and is available from `NOTICE_SENT → HEARING_SCHEDULED` and, after an appeal, from `APPEALED → HEARING_SCHEDULED`. The payload carries `hearing_meeting_id` and `hearing_slot_start`. From `NOTICE_SENT` the requirements are: the case is `NOTICE_SENT`; the logical clock is **strictly after** `cure_deadline_at`, since the deadline instant itself still belongs to the owner and a hearing docketed at or before it is refused with `ERR_CURE_PERIOD_ACTIVE`; the target meeting's `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `NOTICED` and its `agenda_kinds` contains `HEARING`, failing which the response is `ERR_AGENDA_KIND_MISSING`; the meeting's `hearing_slot_count` is strictly below `hearing_docket_capacity`, failing which the response is `ERR_HEARING_DOCKET_FULL`; `hearing_slot_start` sits on a thirty-minute boundary and the whole thirty-minute slot lies inside the meeting's window, which is inclusive of `scheduled_start` and exclusive of `scheduled_end`, failing which the response is `ERR_SLOT_OUTSIDE_MEETING`; no other case docketed to that meeting occupies the half-open thirty-minute slot beginning at `hearing_slot_start`, failing which the response is `ERR_HEARING_SLOT_TAKEN`; and at most one other case for the same `unit_id` is already docketed to that meeting, so a third case for one unit on one docket is refused with `ERR_UNIT_DOCKET_LIMIT`. The side effects are that `hearing_meeting_id` and `hearing_slot_start` are written, `hearing_round` is set to 1, the meeting's `hearing_slot_count` rises by 1 with `BoardMeeting.version` advancing by 1, and `ViolationCase.version` advances by 1. Because the slot interval is half-open, a case starting exactly when another case's thirty-minute slot ends does **not** collide.

From `APPEALED` the requirements are: the case is `APPEALED`; the target meeting is **not** the meeting that hosted the prior hearing round, since no board may rehear an appeal in the same session it first decided, failing which the response is `ERR_SAME_MEETING_REHEARING`; the target meeting's `status` is `SCHEDULED` or `NOTICED` with `HEARING` among its `agenda_kinds`; the meeting's `hearing_slot_count` is strictly below `hearing_docket_capacity`; and no other docketed case occupies the requested half-open slot. The side effects differ from the round-one leg in exactly one respect: `hearing_round` is **incremented by 1** rather than set to 1, preserving the appeal count. `hearing_meeting_id` and `hearing_slot_start` are written, the meeting's `hearing_slot_count` rises by 1 with `BoardMeeting.version` advancing by 1, and `ViolationCase.version` advances by 1. The cure-deadline check does not apply on this leg.

The hearing decision is a `BoardDirector` act taking an `outcome` selector, and which outcomes are legal depends on the round. Every decision requires that the case be `HEARING_SCHEDULED`, that the hearing meeting's `status` be `IN_SESSION` with its `quorum_met` true — otherwise `ERR_MEETING_NOT_IN_SESSION` or `ERR_BOARD_QUORUM_NOT_MET` — and that the acting director not be the reporter: the `X-User-ID` must differ from `reported_by_owner_id`, or the response is `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`.

- `FINE`, legal only when `hearing_round` is exactly 1, moves the case to `FINE_ASSESSED`. It additionally requires that the acting owner's `board_role` not be `NONE` and that `is_seated` be true. It writes `fine_tier` and `fine_amount_cents` from the tier selection below, posts a `FINE` row in bucket `FINE` on the cited unit with a positive `amount_cents` equal to `fine_amount_cents` and `due_on` thirty days after the logical clock, stores that row's identifier in `fine_entry_id`, recomputes the unit's `balance_due_cents` and re-derives its `delinquency_stage` with `Unit.version` advancing by 1, sets `decision_owner_id` to the acting `X-User-ID`, sets `appeal_deadline_at` to the logical clock advanced by `appeal_window_days`, and advances `ViolationCase.version` by 1.
- `DISMISS`, legal only when `hearing_round` is exactly 1, moves the case to `DISMISSED`. It posts no ledger entry, leaves `fine_amount_cents` at 0 and `fine_tier` at 0, and removes the case from the repeat chain so later cases skip it when resolving `prior_case_id`. It sets `decision_owner_id` to the acting `X-User-ID` and advances `ViolationCase.version` by 1. Note the disjoint side-effect sets: the fining branch touches money, the unit, and the appeal clock, while the dismissing branch touches none of them.
- `UPHOLD`, legal only when `hearing_round` is 2 or greater, moves the case to `FINE_UPHELD`. It additionally requires that the acting `X-User-ID` differ from `decision_owner_id`, because no director may review their own decision; a self-review is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. The `FINE` row stays `POSTED`, the unit's `balance_due_cents` is unchanged, and `ViolationCase.version` advances by 1.
- `VACATE`, legal only when `hearing_round` is 2 or greater and subject to the same self-review prohibition, moves the case to `FINE_VACATED` and is the compensating action for the fine assessment: the unit ledger must return to exactly its pre-fine value. Four cascade hops follow. First, a `REVERSAL` row is posted whose `reverses_entry_id` is `fine_entry_id` and whose `amount_cents` is the negation of `fine_amount_cents`, and the original `FINE` row's `status` becomes `REVERSED`. Second, because the ledger changed, the unit's `balance_due_cents` and `oldest_unpaid_age_days` are recomputed and its `delinquency_stage` is re-derived through the ladder; a unit dropping out of `LIEN_FILED` clears `lien_recorded_at` and flips `voting_eligible` back to true, and `Unit.version` advances by 1. Third, because `voting_eligible` flipped, every special assessment of the association whose `approval_status` is `BALLOTING` recomputes `eligible_weight_bp` and re-derives `counted` on its votes, with each `SpecialAssessment.version` advancing by 1. Fourth, because `eligible_weight_bp` changed, every resale certificate of that unit whose `status` is `ISSUED` and whose `special_assessment_disclosure_cents` no longer matches the recomputed figure becomes `VOIDED` with `requires_reissue` set to true and its `RESALE_FEE` row reversed. `ViolationCase.version` advances by 1.

**Fine tier selection.** The chain walk begins at the case's `prior_case_id` and follows each predecessor's own `prior_case_id`. A link qualifies when its `status` is `FINE_ASSESSED`, `FINE_UPHELD`, or `CURED` and its `notice_sent_at` is at or after this case's `notice_sent_at` less `repeat_lookback_days`; the walk stops at the first non-qualifying link rather than skipping over it, so one stale or dismissed predecessor truncates the whole chain. `fine_tier` is 1 when the qualifying chain is empty, 2 when it holds exactly one link, and 3 when it holds two or more. The raw amount is `fine_tier_1_cents` at tier 1, `fine_tier_2_cents` at tier 2, and `fine_tier_3_daily_cents` multiplied by `continuing_days` at tier 3 — the daily rate is multiplied **before** the cap is applied. `fine_amount_cents` is then the lesser of that raw amount and `fine_cap_cents`; a raw amount exactly equal to `fine_cap_cents` is charged in full. With the defaults, one prior link yields 10000 cents and two prior links yield 25000 cents per continuing day, capped at 250000 cents, which the tier-3 rate reaches at ten continuing days.

A `UnitOwner` appeals an assessed fine with an optional `grounds`, moving `FINE_ASSESSED → APPEALED`. The requirements are that the case be `FINE_ASSESSED`, that the logical clock be at or before `appeal_deadline_at` — an appeal filed after the deadline instant is refused with `ERR_APPEAL_WINDOW_CLOSED` — and that the acting `X-User-ID` be the `current_owner_id` of the cited unit, or `ERR_NOT_UNIT_OWNER` is returned. The side effects release the docket slot: the former hearing meeting's `hearing_slot_count` falls by 1 with that `BoardMeeting.version` advancing by 1, `hearing_meeting_id` and `hearing_slot_start` are cleared to null, the `FINE` row stays `POSTED` so the fine remains owed throughout the appeal, and `ViolationCase.version` advances by 1.

Two transitions on this entity are driven by the system rather than by an operator. The billing run's unappealed-fine sweep, described in §3.5, drives `FINE_ASSESSED → FINE_UPHELD` once the logical clock is strictly past `appeal_deadline_at`. Meeting cancellation, described in §3.3, de-dockets hearings conditionally on the round: a case in `HEARING_SCHEDULED` with `hearing_round` exactly 1 whose `hearing_meeting_id` is the cancelled meeting returns to `NOTICE_SENT` with `hearing_meeting_id` and `hearing_slot_start` cleared and `hearing_round` returned to 0, and its `cure_deadline_at` is **not** extended; a case in `HEARING_SCHEDULED` with `hearing_round` of 2 or greater returns instead to `APPEALED` with `hearing_meeting_id` and `hearing_slot_start` cleared and `hearing_round` left at its current value. Both advance `ViolationCase.version` by 1.

### 3.9. Resale Certificates, Closing, and the Lender Questionnaire

A `TitleAgent` or a `CommunityManager` requests a resale certificate for a unit by supplying the `closing_date` used for proration and forward projection, an optional `payoff_basis` of `LEDGER_BALANCE` or `ESTIMATED_PAYOFF` defaulting to `LEDGER_BALANCE`, the `requesting_agent_id` opaque identifier of the closing agent, and the mandatory `expected_unit_version`. Requesting a certificate supersedes any other certificate for that unit whose `status` is `REQUESTED`, which becomes `VOIDED` with `requires_reissue` false, and advances `Unit.version` by 1. The certificate is born with `status` `REQUESTED` and with `ledger_balance_cents`, `projected_payoff_cents`, `proration_credit_cents`, `open_violation_count`, and `special_assessment_disclosure_cents` at 0, `disclosed_claim_ids` empty, `requires_reissue` false, and `pinned_budget_id`, `pinned_unit_version`, `pinned_budget_version`, `cert_fee_entry_id`, `issued_at`, and `expires_at` all null. `status` runs over `REQUESTED`, `ISSUED`, `VOIDED`, and `EXPIRED`, of which `VOIDED` and `EXPIRED` are terminal. Certificates may be listed and filtered by `unit_id`, `pinned_budget_id`, `cert_fee_entry_id`, and `status`.

Issuance is a `CommunityManager` act moving `REQUESTED → ISSUED`. Four conditions apply: the certificate must be `REQUESTED`; the unit's `delinquency_stage` must not be `FORECLOSURE_REFERRAL`, or the response is `ERR_CERT_BLOCKED_BY_FORECLOSURE`; a budget whose `status` is `ADOPTED` must exist whose `fiscal_year` contains the `closing_date`, or the response is `ERR_BUDGET_NOT_ADOPTED`; and `closing_date` must be at or after the logical clock, a past closing date being refused with `ERR_CERT_CLOSING_DATE_PAST`. Insurance claims with `disclosure_required` true never block issuance — they are disclosed on the certificate instead.

The **order** of the issuance side effects is load-bearing. First the `RESALE_FEE` row is posted on the unit for `resale_cert_fee_cents`, with its identifier stored in `cert_fee_entry_id`, and the unit's `balance_due_cents` and `delinquency_stage` are recomputed with `Unit.version` advancing by 1. Only **after** that fee posting is `pinned_unit_version` set to the unit's now-current `version`, along with `pinned_budget_id` and `pinned_budget_version` taken from the adopted budget; pinning happens last so that the certificate is never born stale against the very fee it just charged. Then `ledger_balance_cents`, `projected_payoff_cents`, and `proration_credit_cents` are written from the payoff calculation below, together with `open_violation_count`, `special_assessment_disclosure_cents`, and `disclosed_claim_ids`. Finally `issued_at` is stamped from the logical clock, `expires_at` is set to `issued_at` advanced by `certificate_validity_days`, the unit's `transfer_blocked` is set to false, and `ResaleCertificate.version` advances by 1.

**The payoff calculation.** The seller's interval is half-open over the closing month: it begins at midnight UTC on the first day of the calendar month containing `closing_date` and ends strictly before `closing_date`, so the closing day itself belongs to the buyer. The month length is the actual number of days in that calendar month, the seller's days are the difference between `closing_date` and the month start floored to whole days, and the buyer's days are the month length minus the seller's days — an actual/actual convention. Under `LEDGER_BALANCE`, `ledger_balance_cents` and `projected_payoff_cents` are both the unit's `balance_due_cents` and `proration_credit_cents` is 0: today's ledger only, with no forward projection and no seller credit. Under `ESTIMATED_PAYOFF`, `ledger_balance_cents` is still the unit's `balance_due_cents`, the count of future periods is the number of whole billing periods whose first day falls strictly after the logical clock and strictly before `closing_date` and for which the unit has no `POSTED` `ASSESSMENT` row yet, the gross figure is the unit's `balance_due_cents` plus that count multiplied by the unit's `monthly_assessment_cents` plus the unbilled special-assessment disclosure, `proration_credit_cents` is the unit's `monthly_assessment_cents` multiplied by the buyer's days, divided by the month length, and rounded half up **once at the end** — prorating each future period separately and summing is explicitly not the pinned order — and `projected_payoff_cents` is the gross figure minus `proration_credit_cents`. Because `LEDGER_BALANCE` omits both the forward periods and the seller credit, it reports a lower figure than `ESTIMATED_PAYOFF` whenever any unbilled period or approved assessment exists.

Three disclosure figures accompany the payoff. `special_assessment_disclosure_cents` is summed over every special assessment of the association whose `approval_status` is `APPROVED`, taking this unit's allocated share of that assessment's `total_amount_cents` — computed with the special-assessment allocation variant over the full `total_amount_cents` — and subtracting the sum of `amount_cents` over the `POSTED` `SPECIAL_ASSESSMENT_INSTALLMENT` rows already charged to this unit for that assessment; it is therefore the unbilled remainder the buyer will inherit. `open_violation_count` is the number of this unit's violation cases whose `status` is not one of `CURED`, `DISMISSED`, `FINE_UPHELD`, or `FINE_VACATED`. `disclosed_claim_ids` is the identifiers of the association's insurance claims whose `disclosure_required` is true, ordered by `created_at` ascending.

Voiding an issued certificate comes in two forms whose side effects diverge on exactly one point: the fee. An **operator** void, a `CommunityManager` act carrying an optional `reason`, requires only that the `status` be `ISSUED`; it sets `requires_reissue` to **false** and leaves the `RESALE_FEE` row `POSTED`, because an operator void does not refund the fee; it recomputes the unit's `transfer_blocked` with `Unit.version` advancing by 1; and it advances `ResaleCertificate.version` by 1. A **cascade** void, driven by the system when budget adoption, fine vacatur, or litigation tender invalidates the pinned snapshot — that is, when `pinned_budget_version` no longer equals the budget's `version`, or a new disclosure claim appeared, or `special_assessment_disclosure_cents` changed — sets `requires_reissue` to **true**, posts a `REVERSAL` row whose `reverses_entry_id` is `cert_fee_entry_id` and whose `amount_cents` is the negation of `resale_cert_fee_cents` so that the `RESALE_FEE` row flips to `REVERSED`, recomputes the unit's `balance_due_cents`, `delinquency_stage`, and `transfer_blocked` with `Unit.version` advancing by 1, and advances `ResaleCertificate.version` by 1. Only the cascade path refunds the fee.

Closing the sale is a `CommunityManager` act on the unit, supplying `new_owner_id` and `certificate_id`, and it consumes the certificate by moving it `ISSUED → EXPIRED`. Three conditions apply: the named certificate's `status` must be `ISSUED` and the logical clock must be strictly before its `expires_at`, or the response is `ERR_NO_VALID_CERTIFICATE`; the unit's current `version` must still equal the certificate's `pinned_unit_version`, because any ledger movement since issuance invalidates the payoff the buyer relied on, and a mismatch yields `ERR_CERTIFICATE_STALE`; and the incoming owner must exist and belong to the same association. The side effects set the unit's `current_owner_id` to the incoming owner and its `transfer_blocked` back to true with `Unit.version` advancing by 1, reduce the outgoing owner's `owned_unit_count` by 1 and raise the incoming owner's by 1 with both `UnitOwner.version` values advancing by 1, and advance `ResaleCertificate.version` by 1.

The billing run's certificate sweep, described in §3.5, drives the other `ISSUED → EXPIRED` leg once the logical clock has reached `expires_at`, setting the unit's `transfer_blocked` back to true with `Unit.version` advancing by 1 and advancing `ResaleCertificate.version` by 1. A lender questionnaire is available to a `TitleAgent`, a `CommunityManager`, or a `BoardTreasurer` as a computed read that re-runs the payoff calculation without persisting anything; its payload carries the payoff figures, the unit's delinquency posture, `open_violation_count`, `special_assessment_disclosure_cents`, and `disclosed_claim_ids`, and a certificate whose `pinned_unit_version` no longer equals the unit's current `version` is reported with `requires_reissue` true even though the stored row is untouched.

Finally, a test-driving capability lets a `CommunityManager` set the logical clock by supplying `current_utc`. It mutates no entity; subsequent requests simply observe the new logical now. Before any such call the logical clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to real wall-clock time.
