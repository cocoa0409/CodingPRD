# Health Plan Claims Adjudication API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A health insurer settles its obligations one claim at a time, and every claim is a small argument between four parties who never speak directly: the provider who billed, the member whose benefits are being spent, the network contract that fixed the price, and the payer sitting ahead of us in the coordination-of-benefits order. Today that argument is settled by a patchwork of spreadsheets, overnight batch jobs and examiner judgement, which means the same claim can settle differently depending on who touched it and in what order. The Claims Adjudication Engine described here replaces that patchwork with a deterministic, replayable pipeline: intake and duplicate screening, eligibility and benefit-accumulator application, contract pricing and fee-schedule lookup, edit and bundling logic, authorization matching and medical policy, coordination-of-benefits offsetting, pend and deny reason coding, remittance generation, and the adjustment and reprocessing cycles that follow when late information arrives.

Two properties dominate the design. The first is **determinism**: given the same configuration and the same claim, every adjudication pass must produce byte-identical money. That forces us to pin evaluation order, rounding order, and every tie-break rule explicitly — the engine never sorts by a surrogate identifier, and it never re-reads a balance in the middle of a claim that the same claim is about to move. The second is **reversibility**: money that has been remitted can be unwound. A void or an adjustment must walk the entire chain of posted effects backwards — remittance, benefit accumulator, paid service lines, consumed authorization units — leaving the system in a state indistinguishable from one in which the claim had never been finalized.

Every resource in this system is mounted under the `/adjudication` prefix. The API is consumed by six back-office operator populations and by billing providers acting for themselves, and the same engine serves all of them; the difference between them is authority, not behaviour.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. Operator Identity and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to the API — without exception, including reads — must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, naming the authority under which the principal acts. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` carries a value outside the seven roles enumerated below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` and HTTP 401. This check precedes every other check in the system. Once the caller is authenticated, the engine confirms that the named role appears in the authorized-role list for the requested endpoint (see §4); a role that is authenticated but not authorized receives `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` and HTTP 403.

The seven roles are `PlanAdmin`, `NetworkManager`, `ClaimsIntake`, `ClaimsExaminer`, `UtilizationReviewer`, `MedicalDirector` and `ProviderRep`.

- A **`PlanAdmin`** runs plan operations. Exclusively, a `PlanAdmin` configures benefit plans, enrols members, adds coverage segments to a member, terminates a coverage segment, posts manual corrections onto a benefit accumulator, and sets the server's logical clock. Shared with others, a `PlanAdmin` may register providers (with `NetworkManager`), publish fee schedule rates (with `NetworkManager`), publish edit rules (with `MedicalDirector`), and read every configuration and claim resource.
- A **`NetworkManager`** runs network contracting. Exclusively, a `NetworkManager` drafts provider contracts, activates them and terminates them. Shared, a `NetworkManager` may register providers and publish fee schedule rates, and may read every configuration resource. A `NetworkManager` is deliberately denied read access to claims, service lines, remittance advices and adjudication events; those requests are rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE`.
- A **`ClaimsIntake`** operator staffs the 837 intake desk. Exclusively, a `ClaimsIntake` posts other-payer coordination-of-benefits payments. Shared, a `ClaimsIntake` submits claims (with `ProviderRep`) and reads every configuration and claim resource.
- A **`ClaimsExaminer`** owns the adjudication desk. Exclusively, a `ClaimsExaminer` runs adjudication passes, finalizes claims into remittances, voids finalized claims and creates adjustment claims. Shared, a `ClaimsExaminer` reads every configuration and claim resource.
- A **`UtilizationReviewer`** owns prior authorization. Exclusively, a `UtilizationReviewer` issues authorizations and revokes them. Shared, a `UtilizationReviewer` reads every configuration and claim resource.
- A **`MedicalDirector`** releases or converts pended claims under medical policy. Exclusively, a `MedicalDirector` issues overrides on pended claims. Shared, a `MedicalDirector` publishes edit rules (with `PlanAdmin`) and reads every configuration and claim resource.
- A **`ProviderRep`** is a billing provider acting for itself. A `ProviderRep` has no exclusive operations; it shares claim submission with `ClaimsIntake` and may read claims, service lines, remittance advices and adjudication events — but only its own.

**Identity binding.** For six of the seven roles — `PlanAdmin`, `NetworkManager`, `ClaimsIntake`, `ClaimsExaminer`, `UtilizationReviewer` and `MedicalDirector` — `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity. The server may record it (for example, as `last_adjudicated_by_user_id` on a claim when an examiner runs an adjudication pass, or as `actor_user_id` on an adjudication event) but it never compares that value to any row's `id`. The seventh role is different: when `X-User-Role` is `ProviderRep`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Provider`, and ownership checks on any claim-side resource compare the claim's `provider_id` to `X-User-ID`.

**Provider scoping.** A `ProviderRep` that submits a claim must supply a `provider_id` equal to its own `X-User-ID`, and a `ProviderRep` that reads a claim, a list of claims, a list of service lines, a claim's remittance advices, or a claim's adjudication events may only reach rows whose owning claim has a `provider_id` equal to its `X-User-ID`. A violation of either rule is rejected with `ERR_PROVIDER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` and HTTP 403. This scope check is evaluated after path-existence and body-foreign-key resolution but before request-body shape validation.

**Segregation of duties.** The `MedicalDirector` who releases or converts a pended claim must not be the same principal whose adjudication pass produced the pend. The engine compares the overriding director's `X-User-ID` against the claim's `last_adjudicated_by_user_id`; when they are equal, the override is rejected with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` and HTTP 403.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.


### 2.3. Pagination

Every list endpoint accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` controls page size: it defaults to `10`, may not be smaller than `1`, and may not be larger than `100`. `offset` controls the starting position: it defaults to `0` and may not be negative. A `limit` outside the inclusive range from 1 to 100, or a negative `offset`, or a non-integer value for either, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` and HTTP 400.

List responses set `response_type` to `"array"` and carry four pagination fields inside `meta`: `limit` and `offset` echo the effective values used for the page, `total_count` reports the number of rows matching the filter before the page window is applied, and `has_more` is true exactly when rows remain beyond the current window.

Unless a collection declares otherwise below, list endpoints return rows sorted by `created_at` ascending. Four collections declare a different order, and that order is part of their contract:

- A member's benefit accumulators are returned newest plan year first — that is, by `plan_year` descending.
- Service lines are returned by `line_number` ascending.
- A claim's remittance advices are returned newest first.
- A claim's adjudication event log is returned oldest first, so the log reads as a chronological narrative.

### 2.4. Resource Identity, Versioning, and the Logical Clock

Every resource the API returns carries a server-minted `id`: a UUID string with no type prefix, no embedded collection name, and no client control over its value. Clients never supply an `id`.

Most resources also carry three further common properties. `created_at` is the ISO 8601 UTC timestamp at which the row was inserted, rendered with exactly three fractional-second digits. `updated_at` follows the same format and is refreshed on every mutation of the row. `version` is an integer that starts at `1` when the row is created and increases by exactly one on every mutation the server performs on that row, including mutations the row suffers as a side effect of an operation aimed at some other resource.

One resource is deliberately leaner. An adjudication event is an append-only audit record: it carries `id` and `created_at` and nothing else from the common set. It has no `updated_at` and no `version`, because it is never mutated after it is appended.

**The logical clock.** The engine does not read wall-clock time for business purposes. A single server-held logical clock supplies the value stamped into a claim's `received_at`, a remittance advice's `issued_at` and an adjudication event's `occurred_at`, and it supplies the "today" against which timely filing, coverage containment and authorization validity are judged. Before the clock has ever been set, it reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; the engine must never fall back to real wall-clock time for these comparisons. A `PlanAdmin` may advance or rewind the clock by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant. Moving the clock does not retroactively restamp any stored row; it only changes what subsequent operations observe.

### 2.5. Monetary Precision, Rounding, and Deterministic Ordering

All monetary amounts in this system are integers denominated in whole cents. There are no fractional cents in storage and none in any response. Wherever a money value is produced by multiplying by a percentage or a contract multiplier, the result is rounded to a whole number of cents using **round half up** — a value whose fractional part is exactly one half rounds away from zero, so 2516.5 becomes 2517.

**Rounding order is pinned.** When a claim's allowed amount is assembled from several service lines, each line is rounded to whole cents *first* and the rounded per-line values are then summed. The opposite order — summing un-rounded products and rounding the total once — is forbidden, and the two orders genuinely diverge. Consider three lines, each billed at one unit against a fee schedule rate of 3333 cents, priced under a contract multiplier of 0.755: each line computes 2516.415 cents and rounds to 2516, so the claim total is 7548. Summing first would give 7549.245 rounding to 7549 — a different, incorrect answer. The same discipline applies to the member cost share: each line's coinsurance is rounded before that line's liability is summed into the claim total.

**Ordering is pinned, and it is never by `id`.** Every rule in this specification that selects "the applicable row" or "the next row to process" declares an explicit sort key, and no such sort ever falls back to a surrogate identifier as a tie-break. The tie-breaks in use are `created_at` ascending for configuration rows (fee schedule rates, provider contracts, coverage segments, authorizations, edit rules and prior claims), and `line_number` ascending — or descending, where the rule says so — for service lines within a claim. Implementations that let insertion identifiers leak into an ordering will produce different money on the same inputs and are non-conforming.

### 2.6. Interval Conventions: Eligibility, Contracts, and Authorizations

Three different kinds of date window appear in this domain, and they do **not** share the same inclusivity. Getting this wrong is the single most common source of wrong-money defects, so the conventions are fixed here once.

A **coverage segment** spans a *half-open* interval: it begins on `effective_date` inclusively and ends at `term_date` exclusively. A service rendered exactly on the segment's `term_date` is therefore **not** covered by that segment, while a service rendered on the `effective_date` itself is. A `term_date` of null means the segment is open-ended and contains every date on or after its `effective_date`.

A **provider contract** spans a *closed* interval: it begins on `effective_date` inclusively and ends on `term_date` inclusively. A service rendered exactly on the contract's `term_date` is still priced by that contract.

An **authorization** likewise spans a *closed* interval: `valid_from` and `valid_to` are both inclusive, so a service on either boundary date falls inside the grant.

All dates are ISO calendar dates in `YYYY-MM-DD` form, and containment comparisons are whole-calendar-day comparisons made lexicographically on that representation. Any date field supplied in a request body that is not a well-formed `YYYY-MM-DD` string, and any interval whose end precedes its start, is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_FIELD_VALUE` and HTTP 400.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency on Supersede Postings

Two operations in this system are *supersede postings*: sending the same request twice in strict sequence is legitimate product behaviour rather than a bug, which means no state precondition can distinguish an intended second posting from a posting the client computed against a view of the data that has since moved. Both are therefore guarded by optimistic concurrency on the parent row's `version`.

The first is posting an other-payer payment onto a claim: `POST /adjudication/claims/{id}/other-payer-payment` requires a non-optional `expected_claim_version` field carrying the `version` of the target claim as the client last observed it. If that value does not equal the claim's current `version` — because some other writer advanced the claim since the client read it — the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CLAIM_VERSION` and HTTP 412, and nothing is written. A successful posting increments the claim's `version` by one.

The second is posting a manual correction onto a benefit accumulator: `POST /adjudication/accumulators/{id}/adjustments` requires a non-optional `expected_accumulator_version` field carrying the `version` of the target accumulator as the client last observed it. A mismatch against the accumulator's current `version` is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ACCUMULATOR_VERSION` and HTTP 412. A successful posting increments the accumulator's `version` by one.

No other endpoint in the system accepts a version field, and no other endpoint may reject a request for a stale version. The concurrency guard belongs to these two postings alone.

### 2.8. Request Admission Order

When a request is malformed in more than one way, exactly one error must be returned, and which one is not a matter of implementation taste. Checks are evaluated strictly in the order below, and the **first** check that fails determines the response:

1. **Authentication** — both headers present and `X-User-Role` among the seven declared roles; otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401).
2. **Role authorization** — the caller's role appears in the endpoint's authorized-role list; otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403).
3. **Path existence** — every resource identifier embedded in the URL path is resolved **left to right**, and the first identifier that resolves to nothing determines the error: `ERR_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_COVERAGE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLAIM_LINE_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_ACCUMULATOR_NOT_FOUND`, each with HTTP 404. A nested resource whose parent identifier does not exist reports the parent's not-found error, never the child's.
4. **Body foreign-key existence** — every foreign key supplied in a request body resolves to an existing row, reported as `ERR_PLAN_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND` or `ERR_PROVIDER_NOT_FOUND` with HTTP 404. This applies to member enrolment, contract drafting, authorization issuance and claim intake.
5. **Provider scoping** — the `ProviderRep` ownership rule of §2.1, reported as `ERR_PROVIDER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (403).
6. **Required fields** — every field the endpoint declares as required is present in the body; otherwise `ERR_MISSING_REQUIRED_FIELD` (400). This applies to every write endpoint.
7. **Field well-formedness** — reported as `ERR_INVALID_FIELD_VALUE` (400) on every write endpoint. A value is well formed when: enum members belong to their declared set; every units figure is at least 1; every cents figure is at least 0; every percentage lies within its declared bounds; dates are ISO `YYYY-MM-DD`; an interval's end is not earlier than its start; `contract_pct` is present when `pricing_method` is `PCT_OF_FEE_SCHEDULE` and `case_rate_cents` is present when `pricing_method` is `CASE_RATE`; `secondary_code` is present when `edit_type` is `BUNDLING` or `MUTUALLY_EXCLUSIVE`; and `max_units` is present when `edit_type` is `UNITS_LIMIT`.
8. **Pagination well-formedness** — on every list endpoint, `limit` an integer within 1 to 100 inclusive and `offset` a non-negative integer; otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (400).
9. **State admissibility** — the target resource is in a status the operation accepts, reported with HTTP 409 as `ERR_INVALID_CLAIM_STATE`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_PENDED`, `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FINALIZED`, `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_DRAFT`, `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE`, `ERR_COVERAGE_ALREADY_TERMINATED` or `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_NOT_REVOCABLE`, whichever fits the operation. The operations subject to this check are: running an adjudication pass, overriding, finalizing, voiding, creating an adjustment, posting an other-payer payment, activating a contract, terminating a contract, terminating a coverage segment, and revoking an authorization.
10. **Optimistic concurrency** — the supplied `expected_claim_version` or `expected_accumulator_version` matches the parent row's current `version`, per §2.7; otherwise `ERR_STALE_CLAIM_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_ACCUMULATOR_VERSION` with HTTP 412.
11. **Segregation of duties** — on override only, reported as `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (403).
12. **Claim has at least one service line** — on claim intake and on adjustment creation, reported as `ERR_CLAIM_NO_LINES` (422).
13. **Coverage-segment non-overlap** — on adding a coverage segment, reported as `ERR_COVERAGE_OVERLAP` (409).
14. **Contract-window separation** — on drafting a contract, reported as `ERR_CONTRACT_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (409).
15. **Timely filing** — on claim intake and on adjustment creation, reported as `ERR_TIMELY_FILING_EXCEEDED` (422).
16. **Member has coverage on file** — on claim intake, reported as `ERR_MEMBER_NO_COVERAGE` (422).
17. **Uniqueness** — reported with HTTP 409 as `ERR_DUPLICATE_MEMBER_NUMBER` on member enrolment, `ERR_DUPLICATE_CLAIM_NUMBER` on claim intake and on adjustment creation, and `ERR_DUPLICATE_FEE_RATE` on fee schedule rate publication.
18. **Other-payer amount within billed charges** — on claim intake and on other-payer posting, reported as `ERR_OTHER_PAYER_EXCEEDS_BILLED` (422).
19. **Accumulator floor** — on accumulator adjustment, reported as `ERR_ACCUMULATOR_NEGATIVE` (422).

Ordering matters in practice. A claim submitted with a duplicate `claim_number` *and* past its filing window reports the filing failure, because timely filing is checked ahead of uniqueness. An other-payer posting that is both stale and over-billed reports the stale version, because the concurrency guard sits ahead of the fiscal check. And an override attempted by the same operator who produced the pend on a claim that is not pended reports `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_PENDED`, because state admissibility precedes segregation of duties.

### 2.9. Reversal Integrity: How Unwinds Cascade

Nothing in this engine is posted in one place. When a claim is finalized it writes a remittance advice, moves the member's year-to-date balances, freezes its service lines and holds authorization units. It follows that unwinding a finalized claim must move all four back, in a fixed cascade, and that the cascade is not optional or partial: after a void, the member's accumulator, the authorization's consumed units and the derived authorization status must all read exactly as they did before the finalization. §3.9 describes the cascade hop by hop.

Two consequences deserve stating up front. First, a mutation that a row suffers as a *side effect* is a mutation like any other: it refreshes that row's `updated_at` and increases its `version`. A claim that is re-pended because someone terminated a coverage segment has been mutated, and its `version` moves. Second, an authorization's `status` is never set directly by an operator except by revocation — it is **re-derived** from `units_consumed` every single time that number changes, in either direction. That is what makes the authorization lifecycle cyclic: an exhausted grant becomes usable again when the lines that consumed it are reversed.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Plan Configuration, Fee Schedules, and Network Contracting

Nothing adjudicates until the plan is configured, and the benefit plan is the configuration object from which every pricing and cost-share constant is read. A `PlanAdmin` creates one by supplying a `plan_code` — an externally visible identifier that must be unique across all benefit plans — together with a human-readable `name`, an individual annual deductible expressed as `deductible_individual_cents`, an individual annual out-of-pocket maximum expressed as `oop_max_individual_cents`, and the member's coinsurance share as `coinsurance_member_pct` (a fraction such as 0.20, which must lie between 0 and 1 inclusive). Five further settings are optional and fall back to defaults when omitted: `plan_year_start_date`, the ISO date on which the plan year opens, defaults to `2026-01-01`; `copay_office_visit_cents`, the flat copay charged instead of a deductible draw on office-visit procedure codes, defaults to 2500 cents — twenty-five dollars; `out_of_network_penalty_pct`, the allowed-amount reduction applied to unconstrained out-of-network pricing, defaults to 0.30; `timely_filing_days`, the filing window, defaults to 90 days and must be at least 1; and `cob_method`, which selects the coordination-of-benefits arithmetic and is either `COME_OUT_WHOLE` (the default) or `NON_DUPLICATION`. Both cents figures must be zero or greater, and both percentage figures must lie between 0 and 1 inclusive.

Prices come from a fee schedule. Publishing a rate under a plan — a capability shared by `NetworkManager` and `PlanAdmin` — records the `procedure_code` the rate prices, the per-unit `allowed_amount_cents` (zero or greater), and the `effective_date` from which the rate applies to service dates. The plan the rate belongs to is taken from the URL, never from the body, and is stored as the rate's `plan_id`. A plan may hold many rates for the same procedure code with different effective dates — that is how a mid-year fee schedule revision is expressed — but the combination of plan, `procedure_code` and `effective_date` must be unique; a repeat is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_FEE_RATE`. When the rates of a plan are listed they may be narrowed by `procedure_code`.

Providers are registered by either a `PlanAdmin` or a `NetworkManager` with an `npi`, a display `name`, and a `network_status` that is either `IN_NETWORK` or `OUT_OF_NETWORK`; an optional `specialty_code` defaults to `GEN`. Network status alone does not price anything — it only decides whether the out-of-network penalty bites when no contract governs the line.

Contracting is the `NetworkManager`'s exclusive territory. Drafting a contract records the `provider_id` and `plan_id` the contract binds, the closed interval bounded by `effective_date` and `term_date`, and a `pricing_method` that is either `PCT_OF_FEE_SCHEDULE` or `CASE_RATE`. A percent-of-fee-schedule contract must carry `contract_pct`, a multiplier that must be greater than 0 and no greater than 5; a case-rate contract must carry `case_rate_cents`, a flat claim-level allowed amount of zero or more. Supplying neither companion field, or the wrong one for the chosen method, is an `ERR_INVALID_FIELD_VALUE` rejection. Contracts are always born in `DRAFT`, and a `DRAFT` contract never prices a claim.

Contract windows for the same provider-and-plan pair must be genuinely separated, not merely non-overlapping. The rule requires at least one full clear calendar day between them: a new contract is acceptable only when its `effective_date` falls at least two days after an existing contract's `term_date`, or when its own `term_date` falls at least two days before that existing contract's `effective_date`. Adjacency is therefore **rejected** here — a contract starting the very day after an existing contract ends still conflicts, which is the opposite of the coverage-segment rule in §3.2. The comparison is made against every existing contract for that provider-and-plan pair whose `status` is not `TERMINATED`, and a violation returns `ERR_CONTRACT_WINDOW_CONFLICT`.

Activation moves a contract `DRAFT → ACTIVE` and is refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_DRAFT` on a contract in any other status; the engine also confirms at activation that the method's companion field is populated — a percent-of-fee-schedule contract must have a non-null `contract_pct`, a case-rate contract a non-null `case_rate_cents`. From that moment, claims adjudicated for that provider-and-plan pair whose service dates fall inside the closed contract interval price under the `CONTRACT_PRICED` or `CASE_RATE_PRICED` variant. Termination moves a contract `ACTIVE → TERMINATED`, overwrites `term_date` with the supplied date, and is refused with `ERR_CONTRACT_NOT_ACTIVE` from any other status; claims adjudicated afterwards fall back to the `FEE_SCHEDULE_PRICED` variant, which reintroduces the out-of-network penalty for out-of-network providers. Contract listings may be narrowed by `provider_id`, by `plan_id`, or by `status`.

### 3.2. Member Enrolment, Coverage Segments, and the Benefit Accumulator

Enrolment is a `PlanAdmin` operation. A member is created with a `member_number` — unique across all members, with collisions rejected as `ERR_DUPLICATE_MEMBER_NUMBER` — and a `plan_id` naming the benefit plan whose constants will price and cost-share this member's claims. Three optional identity attributes fall back to defaults: `first_name` defaults to `Given`, `last_name` defaults to `Family`, and `date_of_birth` defaults to `1980-01-01`. A member's `status` is not client-supplied; it begins at `ACTIVE` and is either `ACTIVE` or `TERMINATED`, moving to `TERMINATED` when no coverage segment of that member contains the current logical clock date and returning to `ACTIVE` when a segment that does contain it is created. Members may be listed and narrowed by `plan_id`, by `member_number`, or by `status`.

Enrolment has one side effect worth stating explicitly: it creates a **benefit accumulator** for that member covering the calendar year of the plan's `plan_year_start_date`, with `deductible_applied_cents`, `oop_applied_cents` and `remitted_claim_count` all at zero. The accumulator's `member_id` and `plan_year` are both derived by the server. Accumulators for other calendar years are not created up front; one is created lazily the first time a claim finalizes into a year that has no row yet. A member's accumulators may be listed, narrowed by `plan_year`.

The accumulator is the ledger of year-to-date benefit consumption. `deductible_applied_cents` equals the sum of `total_deductible_applied_cents` over that member's claims whose `payment_status` is `REMITTED`, plus every manual adjustment delta ever posted; `oop_applied_cents` equals the sum of `total_member_liability_cents` over the same set of claims, plus every manual delta; and `remitted_claim_count` counts the remitted claims that posted into the row. All three must remain at zero or above.

Manual corrections exist because year-to-date balances sometimes have to be seeded from a prior carrier or repaired after an operational incident. A `PlanAdmin` posts a correction by supplying `deductible_delta_cents` and `oop_delta_cents` — signed values, added respectively to `deductible_applied_cents` and `oop_applied_cents` — an optional free-text `note`, and the `expected_accumulator_version` guard described in §2.7. A correction that would drive either balance below zero is rejected with `ERR_ACCUMULATOR_NEGATIVE` and HTTP 422, and neither balance moves. A correction that succeeds increments the accumulator's `version` by one.

Coverage is expressed as segments. A `PlanAdmin` adds a segment to a member by supplying its `effective_date` and its `cob_order` — this plan's position in the coordination-of-benefits order, one of `PRIMARY`, `SECONDARY` or `TERTIARY`. Optionally the segment carries a `term_date` (null by default, meaning open-ended), an `other_payer_name` naming the payer that sits ahead of this plan (null by default), and a `coverage_tier` of `INDIVIDUAL` (the default) or `FAMILY`. The segment's `member_id` is taken from the URL and is never read from the body. A member's coverage segments may be listed and narrowed by `status`. A new segment starts in `status` `ACTIVE`, and creating one whose half-open interval contains the current logical clock date sets the owning member's `status` back to `ACTIVE`.

Segments of the same member may not intersect. Because the interval is half-open, **adjacency is permitted**: a new segment whose `effective_date` equals an existing segment's `term_date` is accepted, since the existing segment excludes its own end date. Any genuine intersection is rejected with `ERR_COVERAGE_OVERLAP`, and an existing open-ended segment — one whose `term_date` is null — blocks every later `effective_date` outright, because it contains all of them.

Terminating a segment is where eligibility becomes expensive. The `PlanAdmin` supplies the `term_date` at which the segment closes; that date must not be earlier than the segment's `effective_date`, and an earlier one is refused as `ERR_INVALID_FIELD_VALUE`; a segment already in `TERMINATED` cannot be terminated again — that attempt returns `ERR_COVERAGE_ALREADY_TERMINATED`. When the termination succeeds, five things happen. First, the segment's `status` becomes `TERMINATED` and its `term_date` is set to the supplied date. Second, **because** the coverage terminated, every claim matched to this coverage segment whose `status` is `RECEIVED`, `PENDED` or `ADJUDICATED` and whose `statement_from_date` falls on or after the new `term_date` moves to `PENDED` with `pend_reason_code` set to `PEND_ELIG_REVIEW`. Third, **because** those claims moved to `PENDED`, each of their service lines standing at `PRICED` flips `PRICED → PENDED`, has its `edit_reason_code` stamped `PEND_ELIG_REVIEW`, has every per-line money field zeroed, and releases the units it was holding back to its linked authorization. Fourth, **because** `units_consumed` decreased on those authorizations, each authorization's `status` is re-derived — `EXHAUSTED → PARTIALLY_CONSUMED` when consumption sits strictly between zero and the grant, and `PARTIALLY_CONSUMED → ACTIVE` when consumption reaches zero. Fifth, as a separate direct effect of the termination, the member's `status` becomes `TERMINATED` when no remaining coverage segment of that member contains the current logical clock date. An adjudication event of type `ELIG_REPEND` is appended to every re-pended claim.

### 3.3. Prior Authorization: Issuance, Consumption, and Revocation

Prior authorizations are issued and revoked exclusively by a `UtilizationReviewer`. Issuing one records the `member_id` it is granted for, the `provider_id` it is granted to, the `procedure_code` it covers, the total `authorized_units` granted (at least 1), and the closed validity interval bounded by `valid_from` and `valid_to`. A freshly issued authorization begins in `status` `ACTIVE` with `units_consumed` at zero. Authorizations may be listed and narrowed by `member_id`, `provider_id`, `procedure_code` or `status`.

An authorization's status is a derived quantity, not an operator-set one. Every time `units_consumed` changes — upward when a service line prices against the grant, downward when such a line is reversed — the status is recomputed from the relationship between `units_consumed` and `authorized_units`. Consumption strictly between zero and the grant yields `PARTIALLY_CONSUMED`; consumption that reaches or exceeds the grant yields `EXHAUSTED`; consumption back at exactly zero yields `ACTIVE`. All six derived movements are reachable in practice: `ACTIVE → PARTIALLY_CONSUMED` when a first partial claim prices, `PARTIALLY_CONSUMED → EXHAUSTED` when a later claim finishes the grant, `ACTIVE → EXHAUSTED` when a single pass consumes the whole grant at once, `EXHAUSTED → PARTIALLY_CONSUMED` when some but not all of the consuming lines are reversed, `PARTIALLY_CONSUMED → ACTIVE` when all consuming lines are reversed, and `EXHAUSTED → ACTIVE` when a single operation reverses every consuming line at once. That an exhausted grant can become usable again is deliberate: reversal is a first-class outcome in claims, not an exception. Once a grant is `EXHAUSTED`, service lines that later match it pend with `PEND_AUTH_UNITS_EXCEEDED` rather than pricing.

Revocation is the one status change an operator makes directly. A `UtilizationReviewer` may revoke an authorization whose `status` is `ACTIVE` or `PARTIALLY_CONSUMED`, applying `ACTIVE → REVOKED` or `PARTIALLY_CONSUMED → REVOKED`; `REVOKED` is terminal, and attempting to revoke one that is already `EXHAUSTED` or `REVOKED` is refused with `ERR_AUTHORIZATION_NOT_REVOCABLE`. Revocation does **not** release units that have already been consumed: `units_consumed` is frozen at whatever value it held. Service lines that later match a revoked grant pend with `PEND_AUTH_REVOKED` instead of pricing.

### 3.4. Claim Intake: Duplicate Screening, Coverage Matching, and Timely Filing

Claims arrive from two directions — a `ClaimsIntake` operator keying an 837 transaction on a provider's behalf, or a `ProviderRep` submitting for itself — and both take the same shape. The submission carries the submitter-assigned control number as `claim_number`, which must be unique across every claim in the system (`ERR_DUPLICATE_CLAIM_NUMBER` otherwise), the `member_id` of the patient, the `provider_id` of the billing provider, and a `lines` array holding at least one service line. An empty or absent `lines` array is rejected with `ERR_CLAIM_NO_LINES` and HTTP 422. Three further inputs are optional: `statement_from_date` and `statement_to_date`, which bound the statement period, and `other_payer_paid_cents`, the amount already paid by the payer ahead of this plan. When `statement_from_date` is omitted it defaults to the earliest `service_date` among the submitted lines; when `statement_to_date` is omitted it defaults to the latest. When `other_payer_paid_cents` is omitted it is null, which means "not yet reported" and is materially different from zero, which means "reported as nothing".

Each element of `lines` becomes a service line. A line declares its `line_number` — a 1-based ordinal within the claim that doubles as the canonical tie-break key for every order-sensitive rule in this specification — the billed `procedure_code`, an optional `modifier_code`, the billed `units` (at least 1), the provider's charge as `billed_amount_cents` (zero or greater), and the `service_date` on which the service was rendered. Every line is persisted with its owning `claim_id` filled in by the server and with `line_status` at `PENDING`; its computed money fields — `allowed_amount_cents`, `deductible_applied_cents`, `copay_cents`, `coinsurance_cents`, `member_liability_cents` and `plan_paid_cents` — all start at zero, and `authorization_id`, `bundled_into_line_number` and `edit_reason_code` all start null. The claim's `total_billed_cents` is computed at intake as the sum of the submitted `billed_amount_cents` values, and `received_at` is stamped from the logical clock as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits. A newly received claim opens at `status` `RECEIVED`, `payment_status` `NOT_REMITTED`, `claim_type` `ORIGINAL`, `adjudication_pass` zero, `pricing_variant` `UNPRICED`, and `original_claim_id`, `duplicate_of_claim_id`, `pend_reason_code`, `deny_reason_code` and `last_adjudicated_by_user_id` all null.

Two gates run before anything else at intake. **Timely filing** measures the whole-day distance between the claim's `statement_to_date` and the logical clock date at the moment of intake, and compares it against the plan's `timely_filing_days`. A distance exactly equal to the window is **accepted**; only a strictly greater distance is refused, with `ERR_TIMELY_FILING_EXCEEDED` and HTTP 422. On a plan left at its default, that means a claim whose statement period closed 90 days ago still files, and one whose statement period closed 91 days ago does not. **Coverage on file** then requires the member to have at least one coverage segment of any status whatsoever; a member with none at all cannot have a claim opened for them, and the attempt returns `ERR_MEMBER_NO_COVERAGE` with HTTP 422. Separately, the reported `other_payer_paid_cents` may never exceed the claim's `total_billed_cents`; that fiscal ceiling is enforced both here and on every later posting, and a breach returns `ERR_OTHER_PAYER_EXCEEDS_BILLED` with HTTP 422.

**Coverage segment matching** then selects the segment the claim belongs to. The engine takes the earliest `service_date` among the claim's lines and gathers every coverage segment of the claim's member — *regardless of that segment's status*, because the half-open date arithmetic alone decides containment, so a terminated segment still covers service dates strictly before its `term_date`. A segment is a candidate when its `effective_date` falls on or before that earliest service date and either its `term_date` is null or its `term_date` falls strictly after that earliest service date. Candidates are ranked by `effective_date` descending, and ties are broken by `created_at` ascending; the first candidate wins and its identifier is written to the claim's `coverage_id`. When no candidate exists, `coverage_id` stays null. The claim's `cob_role` is then set from the winner: `PRIMARY` when no segment matched or when the matched segment's `cob_order` is `PRIMARY`, and `SECONDARY` when the matched segment's `cob_order` is either `SECONDARY` or `TERTIARY` — the tertiary position collapses into the secondary role for arithmetic purposes. This matching is redone on every subsequent adjudication pass, so a coverage change between passes moves the claim's `coverage_id` and `cob_role`.

**Duplicate screening** runs last, before any pricing. Only claims whose `claim_type` is `ORIGINAL` are screened; an `ADJUSTMENT` claim is exempt by construction, since replacing a claim necessarily reproduces its lines. The engine gathers prior claims for the same `member_id` and `provider_id` whose `claim_type` is `ORIGINAL` and whose `status` is not `VOIDED`, `ADJUSTED` or `DUPLICATE_REJECTED`, excluding the incoming claim itself. A prior claim matches when at least one of its service lines and at least one line of the incoming claim agree on all of the following: the same `procedure_code`; the same `modifier_code` after normalising a null modifier to the empty string, so that an absent modifier and an explicitly empty one are the same thing; the same `service_date`; the same `units`; and billed charges that differ by no more than five dollars — a tolerance of 500 cents, applied inclusively, so a gap of exactly 500 cents **is** a duplicate and a gap of 501 cents is not. Matching prior claims are ranked by `received_at` ascending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break, and the earliest one wins.

When a match is found, the incoming claim is still persisted — the point is that the rejection is auditable — but it moves straight `RECEIVED → DUPLICATE_REJECTED`, records the winner's identifier in `duplicate_of_claim_id`, stamps `deny_reason_code` with `DENY_DUPLICATE`, and appends an adjudication event of type `DUPLICATE_REJECT` at pass number zero. Such a claim never prices, never finalizes and never posts to any accumulator; because `DUPLICATE_REJECTED` is excluded from the prior-claim pool, it also never seeds a further duplicate of its own. When no match is found, the claim stays at `RECEIVED` and an adjudication event of type `INTAKE` is appended instead. `DUPLICATE_REJECTED` is a terminal status, alongside `VOIDED` and `ADJUSTED`.

Claim listings support narrowing by `member_id`, `provider_id`, `coverage_id`, `original_claim_id`, `duplicate_of_claim_id`, `status` and `claim_type`, which is how an operator walks the self-referential chains — from a duplicate back to the claim it duplicated, or from an adjustment back to the claim it replaced.

### 3.5. The Pricing Pass: Fee Schedules, Contracts, and Case Rates

An adjudication pass is run by a `ClaimsExaminer` and is the only operation that produces money. It begins by wiping the slate: every service line is reset to `line_status` `PENDING`, and any units a previously `PRICED` line was holding are released back to its linked authorization — with that authorization's `status` re-derived — before the pipeline runs. The pass then proceeds in a strictly fixed order: line pricing, then the case-rate allocation where applicable, then the member cost share, then the out-of-pocket cap reconciliation, then the coordination-of-benefits offset, and finally the claim-level outcome resolution. Every pass increments the claim's `adjudication_pass` by one, writes the caller's `X-User-ID` into `last_adjudicated_by_user_id`, clears both `pend_reason_code` and `deny_reason_code` before recomputing them, and appends an adjudication event of type `ADJUDICATE`. A pass may be run from `RECEIVED` or from `PENDED`; attempting one on a claim that is `FINALIZED`, `VOIDED`, `ADJUSTED` or `DUPLICATE_REJECTED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CLAIM_STATE`.

**Fee schedule lookup** comes first, per line. The engine gathers the rates published under the plan of the claim's member whose `procedure_code` equals the line's and whose `effective_date` falls on or before the line's `service_date`, ranks them by `effective_date` descending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break, and takes the first. If no rate survives that filter, the line is not priced at all: it moves `PENDING → DENIED`, its `allowed_amount_cents` is set to zero, and its `edit_reason_code` is stamped `DENY_NON_COVERED_SERVICE`. The rest of the pricing steps are skipped for that line.

**Contract lookup** comes second. The engine gathers the provider contracts for the claim's `provider_id` and the member's plan whose `status` is `ACTIVE` and whose closed interval contains the line's `service_date` — that is, the contract's `effective_date` falls on or before the service date and the contract's `term_date` falls on or after it — ranks them by `effective_date` descending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break, and takes the first. A `DRAFT` or `TERMINATED` contract is invisible to this lookup.

From there, the line's **base amount** is the selected rate's `allowed_amount_cents` multiplied by the line's `units`, and one of three pricing variants applies. The variant chosen is recorded on the claim in `pricing_variant`.

- **`CONTRACT_PRICED`** applies when the selected contract's `pricing_method` is `PCT_OF_FEE_SCHEDULE`. The allowed amount is the base amount multiplied by the contract's `contract_pct`, rounded half up to whole cents. The out-of-network penalty is **never** applied under this variant, even when the provider's `network_status` is `OUT_OF_NETWORK`, because a negotiated contract supersedes network status.
- **`CASE_RATE_PRICED`** applies when the selected contract's `pricing_method` is `CASE_RATE`. Here the claim, not the line, is priced: the claim's `total_allowed_cents` becomes the smaller of the contract's `case_rate_cents` and the claim's `total_billed_cents`, and the per-line amounts come from the pro-rata allocation described below.
- **`FEE_SCHEDULE_PRICED`** applies when no `ACTIVE` contract covers the line's service date. For an `IN_NETWORK` provider the allowed amount is simply the base amount. For an `OUT_OF_NETWORK` provider the allowed amount is the base amount multiplied by one minus the plan's `out_of_network_penalty_pct`, rounded half up to whole cents — on a plan left at its default, that is 70 percent of the base amount.

Whichever variant applied, the line's `allowed_amount_cents` is finally capped at the line's own `billed_amount_cents`: the plan never allows more than the provider charged. The claim's `total_allowed_cents` is then the sum of the already-rounded per-line `allowed_amount_cents` over the lines whose `line_status` is `PRICED` or `PAID`; lines that ended `BUNDLED`, `DENIED` or `PENDED` contribute nothing and are excluded from the total. The per-line-then-sum rounding order of §2.5 governs here and is not negotiable.

**Case-rate pro-rata allocation.** When a case-rate contract governs, the claim has a single pool of money to spread across its surviving lines. The pool is the smaller of the contract's `case_rate_cents` and the sum of `billed_amount_cents` over the lines that survived the edit pass — the pricing lines. A line that ended `BUNDLED`, `DENIED` or `PENDED` is not a pricing line: it contributes nothing to that billed total and receives nothing from the pool. Each pricing line's exact share is the pool multiplied by that line's `billed_amount_cents` divided by the total billed across pricing lines, computed as an exact rational value with no rounding. Each line first takes the whole-cent integer part of its exact share. The cents left over — the pool less the sum of those integer parts — are then handed out one cent at a time by the **largest-remainder** method: lines are ordered by the fractional part of their exact share descending, with `line_number` ascending as the tie-break, and each of the first *n* lines in that order receives one additional cent, where *n* is the leftover count. The result is that the per-line amounts sum to the pool exactly. Worked through: a `case_rate_cents` of 50000 against three lines billed 30000, 20000 and 10000 gives exact shares of 25000, 16666.666… and 8333.333…; the integer parts are 25000, 16666 and 8333, summing to 49999; the single leftover cent goes to the second line, which has the largest fractional part, producing final line amounts of 25000, 16667 and 8333.

### 3.6. Edit Rules, Bundling, and Authorization Matching

Edit and audit rules are global configuration: they are published once and apply to every adjudication pass that runs afterwards, and they are **not** filtered by plan. Publishing one is shared between `PlanAdmin` and `MedicalDirector`. A rule carries an operator-facing `rule_code`, an `edit_type` drawn from `BUNDLING`, `MUTUALLY_EXCLUSIVE`, `UNITS_LIMIT` and `AUTH_REQUIRED`, a `primary_code` that keys the rule (and, for the first two types, names the surviving code), a `severity` of `DENY`, `PEND` or `INFORM`, a `precedence_rank` of at least 1 where a lower value wins, and a `reason_code` that gets stamped onto affected lines and, when the rule wins claim-level resolution, onto the claim itself. Two fields are conditionally required: `secondary_code`, the code that is bundled away or knocked out, is required for `BUNDLING` and `MUTUALLY_EXCLUSIVE`; and `max_units`, the maximum units permitted on a single line and at least 1, is required for `UNITS_LIMIT`. Rules whose `severity` is `DENY` and whose `precedence_rank` is 10 or lower are **hard denials** — a distinction that matters enormously in §3.8, because a hard denial outranks every pend on the claim while a soft denial does not.

For each line, the applicable rules are those whose keying code matches, and they are applied in `precedence_rank` ascending with `created_at` ascending as the tie-break. The four types behave differently:

- A **`BUNDLING`** rule fires on a line when the rule's `secondary_code` equals the line's `procedure_code` *and* another line of the same claim carrying the same `service_date` has a `procedure_code` equal to the rule's `primary_code`. The line moves `PENDING → BUNDLED`: its `allowed_amount_cents`, `member_liability_cents` and `plan_paid_cents` are all set to zero, its `bundled_into_line_number` records the `line_number` of the surviving line it folded into, and its `edit_reason_code` takes the rule's `reason_code`. A bundled line consumes **no** authorization units.
- A **`MUTUALLY_EXCLUSIVE`** rule fires when the rule's `secondary_code` equals the line's `procedure_code` *and* another line of the same claim — on any service date, the same-date restriction does not apply here — has a `procedure_code` equal to the rule's `primary_code`. When such a rule carries `DENY` severity the line moves `PENDING → DENIED`: `allowed_amount_cents` becomes zero and `edit_reason_code` takes the rule's `reason_code`. Its `bundled_into_line_number` stays null, and it consumes no authorization units.
- A **`UNITS_LIMIT`** rule fires when the rule's `primary_code` equals the line's `procedure_code` and the line's `units` exceed the rule's `max_units`. Under `DENY` severity the line moves `PENDING → DENIED` on the same terms: zero allowed amount, the rule's `reason_code` in `edit_reason_code`, null `bundled_into_line_number`, no units consumed.
- An **`AUTH_REQUIRED`** rule fires when the rule's `primary_code` equals the line's `procedure_code`, and it does not decide the outcome by itself — it declares that the line needs a prior authorization, and the authorization selector below decides what happens next.

A rule whose `severity` is `INFORM` is purely advisory, whatever its `edit_type` matched. The line keeps the outcome it would otherwise have reached — normally `PRICED` — and the only visible effect is that its `edit_reason_code` is stamped with the rule's `reason_code`. Informational rules never contribute to the hard-denial test, never make a claim pend, and never count toward the all-lines-knocked-out test in §3.8.

**Authorization matching** runs for any line an `AUTH_REQUIRED` rule touched. The engine gathers the authorizations whose `member_id` equals the claim's member, whose `provider_id` equals the claim's provider, whose `procedure_code` equals the line's, and whose closed validity interval contains the line's `service_date` — meaning `valid_from` falls on or before the service date and `valid_to` falls on or after it. Candidates are ranked first by status preference, with `ACTIVE` ahead of `PARTIALLY_CONSUMED`, `PARTIALLY_CONSUMED` ahead of `EXHAUSTED`, and `EXHAUSTED` ahead of `REVOKED`; then by `valid_to` ascending, so that the grant expiring soonest is spent first; then by `created_at` ascending. The first candidate is the selected one, and there are exactly four outcomes:

1. No candidate at all — the line moves `PENDING → PENDED` with `edit_reason_code` set to `PEND_AUTH_MISSING`.
2. The selected grant's `status` is `REVOKED` — the line moves `PENDING → PENDED` with `edit_reason_code` set to `PEND_AUTH_REVOKED`.
3. The selected grant's remaining units — its `authorized_units` less its `units_consumed` — are fewer than the line's `units` — the line moves `PENDING → PENDED` with `edit_reason_code` set to `PEND_AUTH_UNITS_EXCEEDED`. Remaining units **exactly equal** to the line's `units` are sufficient and do not pend; the test is strictly fewer-than.
4. Otherwise the line moves `PENDING → PRICED`, its `authorization_id` records the selected grant, and that grant's `units_consumed` increases by the line's `units`, after which the grant's `status` is re-derived.

Note carefully that in the three pend outcomes the line's `edit_reason_code` carries the **derived** authorization code — `PEND_AUTH_MISSING`, `PEND_AUTH_REVOKED` or `PEND_AUTH_UNITS_EXCEEDED` — and **not** the `AUTH_REQUIRED` rule's own `reason_code`. A pended line also keeps `allowed_amount_cents` at zero, leaves `authorization_id` null, and consumes nothing.

### 3.7. Member Cost Share, the Out-of-Pocket Cap, and Coordination of Benefits

Once the surviving lines have allowed amounts, the member's share is computed line by line. Two accumulator readings are taken **once, at the start of the pass**, from the member's benefit accumulator for the calendar year of the earliest `service_date` on the claim, and neither is ever re-read part-way through a claim. The first is the remaining deductible: the plan's `deductible_individual_cents` less the accumulator's `deductible_applied_cents`, floored at zero. The second is the remaining out-of-pocket room, which the cap step below uses.

Lines are then processed in `service_date` ascending order, with `line_number` ascending as the tie-break — never in insertion order. Each line takes one of two branches, and the branch is decided by whether the line's `procedure_code` is one of the plan's office-visit copay codes, which are `99213` and `99214`:

- **Copay branch.** For a line whose `procedure_code` is `99213` or `99214`, the line's `copay_cents` is the smaller of the plan's `copay_office_visit_cents` and the line's `allowed_amount_cents`; its `deductible_applied_cents` is zero; and the amount left to coinsure is the line's `allowed_amount_cents` less that copay. Critically, a copay line does **not** draw down the running remaining-deductible figure at all, so an office visit never moves the member closer to meeting the deductible.
- **Deductible branch.** For every other line, `copay_cents` is zero; the line's `deductible_applied_cents` is the smaller of the running remaining deductible and the line's `allowed_amount_cents`; the running remaining deductible is then reduced by exactly that amount so the next line in order sees the reduced figure; and the amount left to coinsure is the line's `allowed_amount_cents` less its `deductible_applied_cents`.

In both branches, the line's `coinsurance_cents` is the amount left to coinsure multiplied by the plan's `coinsurance_member_pct`, rounded half up to whole cents. The line's `member_liability_cents` is then the sum of its `copay_cents`, its `deductible_applied_cents` and its `coinsurance_cents`, and its `plan_paid_cents` is its `allowed_amount_cents` less its `member_liability_cents`. Lines that ended `BUNDLED`, `DENIED` or `PENDED` are excluded from this step entirely and keep every money field at zero.

**The out-of-pocket cap reconciliation** runs immediately afterwards, and its evaluation order is pinned because the dependency looks circular: the member's liability is capped by the remaining out-of-pocket room, but that room is itself moved by the liability this very claim will post. The order that makes the value well defined is: first compute every line's provisional liability from the accumulator snapshot taken at the start of the pass; second cap the **claim-level** total against that same snapshot; third push the excess back onto the plan; and only fourth, and only at finalization, write the accumulator. Re-reading the accumulator between two lines of the same claim is forbidden.

Concretely: the remaining out-of-pocket room is the plan's `oop_max_individual_cents` less the accumulator's `oop_applied_cents`, floored at zero. The provisional total is the sum of `member_liability_cents` across the pricing lines. The excess is the provisional total less the remaining room, floored at zero. That excess is then relieved line by line in `line_number` **descending** order — the highest-numbered line gives back first — with each line giving back the smaller of its own `member_liability_cents` and the excess still outstanding. Each give-back is subtracted from that line's `member_liability_cents` and added to the same line's `plan_paid_cents`, and reduces the outstanding excess; the loop stops as soon as the excess reaches zero. Because the loop moves whole cents only, the claim's allowed total always equals the member liability plus the plan payment before any coordination-of-benefits offset. Finally the claim's totals are assembled: `total_member_liability_cents` and `total_plan_paid_cents` as the sums of the reconciled per-line values, and `total_deductible_applied_cents` as the sum of the per-line `deductible_applied_cents`.

Consider a plan with a zero deductible, a coinsurance share of 0.50 and an out-of-pocket maximum of 10000 cents, against a member whose accumulator already shows 6000 cents of out-of-pocket usage, on a claim of two lines each allowed 20000 cents. Each line provisionally owes 10000, so the provisional total is 20000; the remaining room is 4000, so the excess is 16000. Line 2 gives back first, dropping from 10000 to 0 and leaving 6000 of excess; line 1 then gives back 6000, dropping from 10000 to 4000. Final member liability is 4000 on line 1 and 0 on line 2, with plan payments of 16000 and 20000. Had the accumulator been re-read after line 1, the answer would have been 10000 and 0 — a different and wrong result.

**Coordination of benefits** is the last money step, and it only touches claims whose `cob_role` is `SECONDARY`; a `PRIMARY` claim passes through unchanged. When the claim is secondary and its `other_payer_paid_cents` is still null, no offset can be computed and the claim pends with the reason `PEND_COB_OTHER_PAYER_MISSING` instead of paying. When the amount is reported, the plan's `cob_method` selects between two genuinely different arithmetics, using as its starting point the `total_plan_paid_cents` the cap reconciliation produced — the amount this plan would have paid had it been primary:

- Under **`COME_OUT_WHOLE`**, the plan pays the smaller of that would-have-paid-as-primary figure and the claim's `total_allowed_cents` less the other payer's payment, with the latter floored at zero.
- Under **`NON_DUPLICATION`**, the plan pays the would-have-paid-as-primary figure less the other payer's payment, floored at zero.

Whichever method applied, the claim's `total_plan_paid_cents` becomes that computed payment, and the claim's `total_member_liability_cents` becomes the claim's `total_allowed_cents` less the other payer's payment less the plan's payment, floored at zero. No rounding occurs in this step; every operand is already a whole number of cents. The divergence is stark on identical inputs: with a `total_allowed_cents` of 40000, a zero deductible, a coinsurance share of 0.30 giving a would-have-paid-as-primary figure of 28000, and an `other_payer_paid_cents` of 24000, `COME_OUT_WHOLE` has the plan pay 16000 and leaves the member owing nothing, while `NON_DUPLICATION` has the plan pay 4000 and leaves the member owing 12000.

**Posting the other payer's payment** is a `ClaimsIntake` operation, guarded by the optimistic concurrency rule of §2.7: the request carries `other_payer_paid_cents` and the `expected_claim_version` the client last observed, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_STALE_CLAIM_VERSION` and HTTP 412. The posting is accepted from exactly three claim statuses — `RECEIVED`, `PENDED` and `ADJUDICATED` — and from any other status it is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CLAIM_STATE`. Its effect depends on where the claim stood. From `RECEIVED` the claim simply stays at `RECEIVED` — the self-loop `RECEIVED → RECEIVED` — and the reported figure overwrites `other_payer_paid_cents`; nothing else changes, because nothing has priced yet. From `PENDED` the claim stays `PENDED`, the figure is overwritten, and the claim waits for the next adjudication pass. From `ADJUDICATED` the claim goes **back** to `RECEIVED`, the transition `ADJUDICATED → RECEIVED`: a late other-payer payment invalidates the coordination-of-benefits arithmetic already performed, so every service line resets to `PENDING`, every per-line computed money field is zeroed, the units each `PRICED` line was holding are released back to its authorization, the claim's `total_allowed_cents`, `total_deductible_applied_cents`, `total_member_liability_cents` and `total_plan_paid_cents` are all zeroed, and `pricing_variant` returns to `UNPRICED`. In all three cases the claim's `version` increments by one and an adjudication event of type `COB_POST` is appended. The fiscal ceiling still applies at posting time: a figure exceeding the claim's `total_billed_cents` is refused with `ERR_OTHER_PAYER_EXCEEDS_BILLED`.

### 3.8. Claim-Level Outcome Resolution, Pends, and the Medical-Director Override

Once every line carries an outcome, the claim itself must be resolved into a single status with a single reason code, and the six tests below are evaluated **strictly top to bottom**. The first test that fires decides the claim and no later test runs. This ordering is the heart of the engine's determinism, and two of its consequences are counter-intuitive enough to state explicitly: a hard denial outranks every pend anywhere on the claim, whereas a *soft* denial does not outrank a pend at all.

1. **Hard denials.** Gather the lines whose `edit_reason_code` came from a rule with `severity` `DENY` and `precedence_rank` of 10 or lower. If that set is non-empty, the claim's `status` becomes `DENIED` and its `deny_reason_code` takes the `reason_code` of the hard-deny rule with the lowest `precedence_rank`, with ties broken by the affected line's `line_number` ascending. Resolution stops here — this outranks every pend, including pends raised on entirely different lines.
2. **No coverage.** If the coverage gate fails, the claim's `status` becomes `DENIED` with `deny_reason_code` set to `DENY_NO_COVERAGE`. The gate is evaluated per line using the same half-open arithmetic as intake matching: a line is covered when some coverage segment of the claim's member has an `effective_date` on or before that line's `service_date` and either a null `term_date` or a `term_date` strictly after it. The claim is covered only when **every** line is covered.
3. **Missing other-payer amount.** If the claim's `cob_role` is `SECONDARY` and its `other_payer_paid_cents` is null, the claim's `status` becomes `PENDED` with `pend_reason_code` set to `PEND_COB_OTHER_PAYER_MISSING`.
4. **Pended lines.** If any line ended at `PENDED`, the claim's `status` becomes `PENDED` and its `pend_reason_code` takes the `edit_reason_code` of the pended line whose triggering rule has the lowest `precedence_rank`, with ties broken by `line_number` ascending. This test deliberately sits **ahead** of the next one, which is why a soft denial — a `DENY`-severity rule with a `precedence_rank` above 10 — never masks a pend.
5. **Everything knocked out.** If every line ended `DENIED` or `BUNDLED`, the claim's `status` becomes `DENIED`. Its `deny_reason_code` takes the `edit_reason_code` of the denied line with the lowest `line_number`; when the claim has no denied line at all because every line was bundled away, the code is `DENY_BUNDLED_ALL` instead.
6. **Otherwise the claim adjudicates.** The claim's `status` becomes `ADJUDICATED`. In practice this is reached when every line settled at `PRICED`, `BUNDLED` or `DENIED` and at least one line is `PRICED`.

From a claim standing at `RECEIVED`, the three destinations a pass can reach are therefore `RECEIVED → ADJUDICATED`, `RECEIVED → PENDED` and `RECEIVED → DENIED`. A pass that resolves to `ADJUDICATED` writes `total_allowed_cents`, `total_deductible_applied_cents`, `total_member_liability_cents`, `total_plan_paid_cents` and `pricing_variant`, and increases `units_consumed` on the authorization of every line that reached `PRICED` with a matched grant. A pass that resolves to `PENDED` sets `pend_reason_code`, clears `deny_reason_code`, leaves `total_plan_paid_cents` at zero, and touches no benefit accumulator whatsoever. A pass that resolves to `DENIED` sets `deny_reason_code` and clears `pend_reason_code`. All three increment `adjudication_pass`, refresh `last_adjudicated_by_user_id`, and append an `ADJUDICATE` event.

**Reprocessing.** A pended claim is not stuck; it is waiting. Once the underlying cause is addressed — a missing authorization is issued, a fee schedule rate is published, a coordination-of-benefits amount is posted — an examiner runs the claim again. Every re-adjudication begins by resetting all lines: a line standing at `PRICED` moves `PRICED → PENDING`, zeroing its money fields and releasing its held units back to its linked authorization before clearing its `authorization_id`; a line standing at `PENDED` moves `PENDED → PENDING` with its `edit_reason_code` cleared. The pipeline is then re-run from scratch, and there are three destinations. `PENDED → PENDED` is a legitimate self-loop — the reprocessing cycle — in which the claim pends again, possibly with a *different* `pend_reason_code` than the previous pass produced. `PENDED → ADJUDICATED` clears `pend_reason_code` and prices the claim. `PENDED → DENIED` sets `deny_reason_code` and clears `pend_reason_code`. Each of the three increments `adjudication_pass` and appends an `ADJUDICATE` event.

**The medical-director override** is the other way out of a pend, and it is the segregation-of-duties control point. Only a `MedicalDirector` may issue one, only against a claim whose `status` is `PENDED` — any other status is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_PENDED` — and only when the acting `X-User-ID` differs from the claim's `last_adjudicated_by_user_id`; a director attempting to release a pend they themselves produced is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` and HTTP 403. The request carries a `decision` of either `APPROVE` or `DENY`, and optionally an `override_reason_code`.

An `APPROVE` decision moves the claim `PENDED → ADJUDICATED` and forces every line standing at `PENDED` through `PENDED → PRICED`, computing its allowed amount and cost share exactly as a normal pass would but **bypassing the `AUTH_REQUIRED` edit entirely**. For each forced line the authorization selector is re-run with two of its filters switched off: the `REVOKED` status exclusion is ignored, and so is the remaining-units test. If that relaxed search finds a grant, the line's `authorization_id` is set to it and that grant's `units_consumed` increases by the line's `units` — **even when doing so drives `units_consumed` above `authorized_units`**, at which point the grant re-derives to `EXHAUSTED`. If the relaxed search finds nothing, the line's `authorization_id` stays null and no units are consumed. The claim's `pend_reason_code` is cleared and its totals are recomputed. An adjudication event of type `OVERRIDE` is appended.

A `DENY` decision moves the claim `PENDED → DENIED`. Every line standing at `PENDED` moves `PENDED → DENIED` with its `edit_reason_code` set to the supplied `override_reason_code`, which defaults to `DENY_MEDICAL_POLICY` when the field is omitted; the claim's `deny_reason_code` takes the same code and its `pend_reason_code` is cleared; and the claim's `total_allowed_cents`, `total_member_liability_cents` and `total_plan_paid_cents` are all zeroed. An adjudication event of type `OVERRIDE` is appended here too.

### 3.9. Finalization, Remittance, Reversal, and the Audit Trail

Finalization is what turns an adjudication result into money on paper, and it is a `ClaimsExaminer` operation accepted from exactly two statuses. From `ADJUDICATED`, finalizing applies `ADJUDICATED → FINALIZED` and creates one remittance advice in `status` `ISSUED` that snapshots the claim's figures: its `total_allowed_cents`, `total_plan_paid_cents` and `total_member_liability_cents` are copied from the claim, its `total_other_payer_paid_cents` is copied from the claim's `other_payer_paid_cents` and is recorded as 0 when that value is null, its `claim_id` names the claim, and its `issued_at` is stamped from the logical clock. The remittance's `remit_number` is deterministic rather than random: it is formatted as `RA-` followed by the claim's `claim_number`, followed by a hyphen and the 1-based count of remittances previously issued for that claim, so the first remittance on claim `C-1001` is `RA-C-1001-1`. The claim's `payment_status` flips `NOT_REMITTED → REMITTED`. The claim's `total_deductible_applied_cents` is added to the member's `deductible_applied_cents` and its `total_member_liability_cents` to the member's `oop_applied_cents`, on the benefit accumulator for that member and the calendar year of the earliest `service_date` on the claim — creating that accumulator row if the year has none yet — and `remitted_claim_count` increases by one. Every service line standing at `PRICED` flips `PRICED → PAID`, freezing its money fields as snapshotted. An adjudication event of type `FINALIZE` is appended.

From `DENIED`, finalizing applies `DENIED → FINALIZED` and behaves differently in exactly one respect that must not be overlooked. A zero-pay remittance advice is still created, in `status` `ISSUED`, with `total_plan_paid_cents` of 0, and the claim's `payment_status` still flips `NOT_REMITTED → REMITTED`, and a `FINALIZE` event is still appended — but **no benefit accumulator field moves at all** and `remitted_claim_count` is **not** incremented. A denied claim consumes no benefits, so it posts nothing. Finalizing a claim in any status other than `ADJUDICATED` or `DENIED` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_CLAIM_STATE`.

The claim's payment dimension deserves its own note: `payment_status` evolves independently of `status`. A claim can cycle `RECEIVED → PENDED → PENDED → ADJUDICATED` any number of times while `payment_status` sits untouched at `NOT_REMITTED`; the payment dimension moves only at finalization, and thereafter only into `REVERSED`, which is terminal for that dimension.

**Voiding** unwinds a finalized claim completely, and it is refused with `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FINALIZED` from any status other than `FINALIZED`. An examiner may supply an optional `void_reason_code`. The unwind is a six-hop cascade, and each hop happens *because* the previous one did. First, the claim moves `FINALIZED → VOIDED`, which is terminal. Second, because the claim voided, its `payment_status` flips `REMITTED → REVERSED`. Third, because the payment reversed, every remittance advice of that claim standing at `ISSUED` flips `ISSUED → VOIDED`, and the accumulator deltas those remittances posted are taken back: the claim's `total_deductible_applied_cents` is subtracted from the accumulator's `deductible_applied_cents`, the claim's `total_member_liability_cents` is subtracted from its `oop_applied_cents`, and `remitted_claim_count` decreases by one. Fourth, because the remittance voided, every service line standing at `PAID` flips `PAID → REVERSED`. Fifth, because a line reversed, the authorization it was linked to has its `units_consumed` reduced by that line's `units`. Sixth, because `units_consumed` moved, the authorization's `status` is re-derived: `EXHAUSTED → PARTIALLY_CONSUMED` when consumption now sits strictly between zero and `authorized_units`, and `PARTIALLY_CONSUMED → ACTIVE` when consumption reaches zero. An adjudication event of type `VOID` is appended.

**Adjustment** is a void followed immediately by a replacement, and it is likewise refused with `ERR_CLAIM_NOT_FINALIZED` outside `FINALIZED`. It is additionally restricted to claims whose `claim_type` is `ORIGINAL`: an adjustment claim cannot itself be adjusted. The examiner supplies the replacement's `claim_number`, its `lines` array, and an optional `adjustment_reason_code`. On the original claim the cascade is identical to the void cascade in hops one through five: the original moves `FINALIZED → ADJUSTED` (terminal), `payment_status` flips `REMITTED → REVERSED`, the `ISSUED` remittance advices flip to `VOIDED` and their accumulator deltas are subtracted exactly as a void subtracts them, and the `PAID` lines flip to `REVERSED`, releasing their authorization units and re-deriving each authorization's `status`. A **new** claim is then created and returned: its `claim_type` is `ADJUSTMENT`, its `original_claim_id` names the claim it replaces, its `status` is `RECEIVED`, its `payment_status` is `NOT_REMITTED`, and it holds the submitted replacement lines. That replacement must be adjudicated from scratch like any other claim. Because it is not an `ORIGINAL` claim, duplicate screening does not apply to it — but the timely-filing window, the at-least-one-line requirement and the uniqueness of `claim_number` all do. An adjudication event of type `ADJUST` is appended on the original claim.

**The audit trail** runs alongside all of this. Every lifecycle movement of a claim appends one immutable adjudication event, and the log for a claim may be read by any role with claim visibility, filtered by `pass_number` or by `event_type`. An event records the `claim_id` it belongs to, the `pass_number` — the claim's `adjudication_pass` at the moment of appending — an `event_type` drawn from `INTAKE`, `DUPLICATE_REJECT`, `ADJUDICATE`, `OVERRIDE`, `FINALIZE`, `VOID`, `ADJUST`, `COB_POST` and `ELIG_REPEND`, the claim's `from_status` before the event and `to_status` after it, the `reason_code` the event stamped (whether a `pend_reason_code` or a `deny_reason_code`, and null when the event stamped neither), the `actor_role` which is the caller's `X-User-Role` or the literal `system` for automatic transitions, the `actor_user_id` which is the caller's `X-User-ID` or null for automatic transitions, and `occurred_at` stamped from the logical clock. Remittance advices are similarly readable per claim and may be narrowed by `status`, and individual service lines are readable both singly and as a filtered list narrowed by `claim_id`, `authorization_id` or `line_status` — the latter being how an operator finds every line a given authorization is holding units for.

## 4. Endpoint Definitions

Every path below is rooted at the `/adjudication` prefix. Endpoints that bring a brand-new resource into existence respond with HTTP 201 — creating a benefit plan, a fee schedule rate, a member, a coverage segment, a provider, a contract, an edit rule, an authorization, a claim, or an adjustment claim. Every other successful call responds with HTTP 200, including the action endpoints that move a resource through its state machine and the endpoint that adjusts an accumulator; those return the mutated resource in `data`. Successful reads return HTTP 200. The logical-clock endpoint returns HTTP 200 with an empty object in `data`.
