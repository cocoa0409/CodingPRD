# Archival Clearance API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

A feature documentary is assembled out of material the production company does not own. Newsreel shots, studio outtakes, home movies, a needle-drop of a 1960s single, a still photograph of a named individual — each fragment arrives with its own chain of title, its own rights holder, and its own price. The clearance department's job is to convert that pile of fragments into a defensible legal position: every shot either licensed, defensible as fair use, or attested as public domain; every identifiable person either released or edited out; every music cue covered on both the master recording and the underlying composition. This API is the system of record for that work.

The pain the system exists to remove is not paperwork volume but *silent drift*. A licence executed in January for a festival-only window does not cover the all-media sale negotiated in November. A most-favored-nations clause granted to a record label retroactively repriced a licence executed three weeks earlier, and nobody adjusted the budget. An opinion letter that supported a fair-use position was withdrawn by counsel, quietly turning a "cleared" asset back into an exposure the errors-and-omissions carrier was never told about. A licence term ran out two days before the distributor's delivery date. Each of these failures is invisible in a spreadsheet and expensive in litigation, so the system models rights coverage as computed state rather than as a note in a field: assets roll their own clearance status up from their live licences, productions roll a **clearance posture** up from their assets, and a sweep over expiring licences propagates the loss of coverage all the way to the delivered package and the bound insurance policy.

The service exposes a flat, graph-shaped resource model. Productions, archives, rate cards, assets, source candidates, licences, negotiation rounds, fair-use analyses, opinion letters, subject releases, clearance charts, E&O applications and delivery packages are each addressed at their own top-level collection under the service root; nested paths exist only for creating a child in its parent's context and for reading a collection back through a foreign key. All resource collections are mounted directly under `/`. The remainder of this document fixes the response envelope and the cross-cutting rules (§2), narrates the clearance workflows and their calculations (§3), and closes with the operation inventory (§4) and the error catalogue (§5).

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request to every endpoint must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting operator, and `X-User-Role`, naming the role that operator is acting in. If either header is absent, present but empty, or if `X-User-Role` names anything other than one of the five roles below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401) before any other processing occurs. A request that authenticates successfully but whose role does not appear in the target operation's authorized-role list is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The five roles are:

- **`ProductionAdmin`** — the production's own clearance producer. Opens the production file, logs assets, opens licences, papers releases, assembles delivery packages and files the E&O application. Opening a production file and driving a production to `CLEARANCE` or `ABANDONED` are reserved to this role alone.
- **`ArchiveResearcher`** — the footage researcher who identifies assets, logs source candidates at holding archives, and maintains archive rate cards. Recording a source candidate against an asset is reserved to this role alone.
- **`ClearanceSupervisor`** — the head of clearances. Suspends and reinstates archives, executes and renews licences, pins the clearance chart, records the underwriting review, and gates delivery. Renewing a licence, suspending or reinstating an archive, driving a production to `LOCKED` or `DELIVERED`, and recording the `UNDERWRITING` review are reserved to this role alone.
- **`RightsHolderRep`** — the archive's own licensing representative. Posting a `LICENSOR` negotiation round is reserved to this role alone.
- **`LegalCounsel`** — outside counsel. Authors fair-use analyses, issues and withdraws opinion letters, pins charts, and records the legal review on the E&O application. Opening a fair-use analysis, attaching an opinion letter, driving an opinion letter's status, and recording the `LEGAL` review are reserved to this role alone.

Four of the five roles carry an **opaque** identity. When `X-User-Role` is `ProductionAdmin`, `ArchiveResearcher`, `ClearanceSupervisor` or `LegalCounsel`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server never compares it to any resource's `id`. The server does record it where a workflow demands attribution — the value is stamped onto `issued_by_user_id` when a `LegalCounsel` operator attaches an opinion letter, onto `legal_reviewer_user_id` when a `LegalCounsel` operator records the legal review, and onto `underwriting_reviewer_user_id` when a `ClearanceSupervisor` operator records the underwriting review — but those stamps are attribution keys for the segregation-of-duties rule, not entity references.

`RightsHolderRep` is the exception, and it is **entity-backed**. When `X-User-Role` is `RightsHolderRep`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Archive`; ownership checks compare the addressed resource's `archive_id` foreign key to `X-User-ID`. A rep appending a negotiation round to a licence, driving a licence's status, reading a single licence, or listing an archive's rate cards may only do so where that `archive_id` equals its own `X-User-ID`; any other target is refused with `ERR_ARCHIVE_OWNERSHIP_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403).

Two further role checks are narrower than the endpoint-level list and are described where they bite. A negotiation round whose declared `party` does not match the acting role is refused with `ERR_PARTY_ROLE_MISMATCH` (§3.4), and an E&O review whose `review_type` does not match the acting role is refused with `ERR_REVIEW_TYPE_ROLE_MISMATCH` (§3.7).

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and Collection Filters

Every list-returning operation accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` defaults to 10, has a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 100. `offset` defaults to 0 and has a minimum of 0; there is no upper bound on `offset`. A `limit` below 1 or above 100, or a negative `offset`, is a malformed request and is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 400). When a list is returned, the `meta` block carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` (the number of rows matching the filters before the window is applied) and `has_more` (true when rows remain beyond the current window).

Unless a specific collection states otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Three collections deviate, and each deviation is deliberate: the clearance-chart history of a production is returned newest first, so the current chart heads the list; an archive's rate cards are returned `ACTIVE` first and then `SUPERSEDED`, each group ordered by `effective_from_date` descending; and the negotiation history of a licence is returned by `round_number` ascending, which replays the deal in the order it happened.

Filters are additive and each one narrows the result set by exact match. The asset collection accepts `production_id`, `selected_source_candidate_id`, `asset_type`, `licensing_status`, `depiction_status` and `rights_basis`. The licence collection accepts `production_id`, `asset_id`, `archive_id`, `rate_card_id`, `rights_leg`, `status`, and `term_end_on_or_before`, the last of which selects licences whose `term_end_date` falls on or before the supplied ISO date and is the natural way to find rights about to lapse. The subject-release collection accepts `production_id`, `asset_id`, `subject_person_key`, `release_type` and `status`. The E&O collection and the delivery-package collection each accept `production_id`, `clearance_chart_id` and `status`. Source-candidate collections — whether read through the owning asset or through the holding archive — accept `availability_status` and `selection_status`. The clearance-chart history, an asset's fair-use analyses, an analysis's opinion letters, and an archive's rate cards each accept `status`.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties

Every persisted resource carries a server-minted `id`: a UUID string with no type prefix and no client control over its value. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recorded when the row was written.

Most resources additionally carry `updated_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits refreshed on every mutation of that row, and `version`, an integer that starts at 1 when the resource is created and is incremented by exactly one on every mutation of the row. `version` is the currency of the concurrency rules in §2.7 and of chart staleness in §2.10, so it must move on *every* write to the row, including writes the request did not directly address — a licence execution that lifts a sibling licence's fee increments that sibling's `version` too.

`NegotiationRound` is the single exception. A negotiation round is an append-only historical record that is never edited after it lands, so a round carries only `id` and `created_at`: it has **no** `updated_at` field and **no** `version` field. What a round does carry is a copy of the parent's state at the moment it landed — `license_status_after` records the licence's `status` immediately after the round was applied, and `license_version_after` records the licence's `version` immediately after the round was applied. That second value is exactly what the next optimistically concurrent call against the licence must send.

### 2.5. Resource Addressing and Reference Resolution

All collections are top level. A nested path such as an asset created inside a production, or a rate card created inside an archive, exists to establish parenthood at creation time and to read a child collection back through its foreign key; it never becomes the child's identity. Consequently, a foreign key that is derived from the path is never accepted from the request body: `production_id` on an asset, a subject release, a clearance chart, an E&O application and a delivery package; `archive_id` on a rate card; `asset_id` on a source candidate and on a fair-use analysis; `license_id` on a negotiation round; and `fair_use_analysis_id` on an opinion letter are all taken from the URL. A licence is the one resource that names its own graph edges in the body, carrying `production_id`, `asset_id` and `archive_id` as explicit input fields.

Path parameters are resolved left to right, and the leftmost missing resource decides the error. Each entity has its own not-found code: `ERR_PRODUCTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ARCHIVE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ASSET_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SOURCE_CANDIDATE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_LICENSE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FAIR_USE_ANALYSIS_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OPINION_LETTER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SUBJECT_RELEASE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_CLEARANCE_CHART_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_EO_APPLICATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DELIVERY_PACKAGE_NOT_FOUND`, each returned with HTTP 404. `ERR_SOURCE_CANDIDATE_NOT_FOUND` is also the answer when a candidate id exists but belongs to a different asset than the one addressed.

Foreign keys supplied in a request body are resolved the same way, in the exact order the fields are declared in that operation's input list. Licence creation declares `production_id`, then `asset_id`, then `archive_id`, so a request naming a bad production and a bad archive reports `ERR_PRODUCTION_NOT_FOUND`. Body-level reference resolution applies to licence creation, source-candidate creation, E&O application creation, delivery-package creation and subject-release creation.

### 2.6. Money, Scores and Rounding Discipline

All money in this system is an integer number of cents. There are no floating-point currency fields and no fractional cents anywhere in a stored value or a response. Multipliers, weights and rates are the only non-integer quantities, and they exist purely as intermediate factors.

Wherever a monetary result must be reduced to an integer, the rounding method is **round half up**: a value whose fractional part is exactly one half rounds away from zero to the next larger magnitude. The *place* where rounding happens is as load-bearing as the method, and each calculation in §3 pins it explicitly. Two pins are worth stating up front because getting them wrong produces answers that are off by a cent or two and therefore pass casual inspection. Rate-card pricing multiplies every factor together in full precision and rounds exactly once, on the final product, before the fee floor is applied; rounding individual factors is wrong. E&O premium rating rounds the base premium once, rounds each rated asset's loading once *per asset*, and only then sums those already-rounded per-asset loadings; accumulating unrounded loadings and rounding the total once at the end is wrong.

One quantity is not money. `strength_score` on a fair-use analysis is a decimal number in the closed interval from 0 to 1, rounded half up to exactly four decimal places, and it is compared against its acceptance threshold at that precision.

Integer counters — `asset_count`, `cleared_asset_count`, `open_asset_count`, `lapse_count`, `round_count`, `revision_round_count`, `pinned_row_count`, `music_leg_gap_count`, `cue_sheet_row_count`, `rights_chart_row_count`, `sweep_epoch`, `billable_units` — are exact integers maintained incrementally, and the posture threshold in §2.8 is deliberately evaluated in integer arithmetic so that no rounding enters a gating decision.

### 2.7. Optimistic Concurrency Control

Two write operations require the client to prove it is working from a current view of a parent resource. Both are expressed as a mandatory input field carrying the client's last observed `version` of that parent, and both reject a mismatch with HTTP 412.

Pinning a clearance chart requires `expected_production_version` on every request; the field is not optional and its absence is a malformed request. Its value must equal the target `Production`'s current `version`. If any writer has advanced the production's `version` since the client last read it — a new asset logged, a licence executed, a posture recomputation, an earlier chart pinned — the pin is rejected with `ERR_STALE_PRODUCTION_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The rule exists because a fresh chart legitimately supersedes the prior `PINNED` chart: without the check, a second writer's pin would succeed on its own terms while silently discarding a chart the first writer had just pinned. The stale-version rejection forces that second writer to re-read the production before pinning.

Appending a negotiation round requires `expected_license_version` on every request; it too is mandatory. Its value must equal the target `License`'s current `version`. Where it does not, the round is rejected with `ERR_STALE_LICENSE_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Each round overwrites `current_proposed_fee_cents`, so a writer working from an outdated view of the deal would replace a counter-offer it never saw; the stale-version check makes that rejection deterministic and points the writer at `license_version_after` on the last round it did see.

No other operation takes a version field. Executing a licence, renewing a licence, driving any state machine, recording a review, selecting a source, creating an asset, creating a licence and running the rights sweep all proceed without an expected-version input.

### 2.8. The Production Clearance Posture Gate

`posture` on a production is a computed enum with three values — `OPEN`, `RESTRICTED` and `BLOCKED` — recomputed from the production's assets after every mutation that changes any of its assets' `licensing_status` or `depiction_status`, after a clearance chart is pinned, and inside the rights sweep. It is the single gate that stands between clearance work and irreversible commitments.

The computation examines every `Asset` whose `production_id` equals the production's `id`, with no status filter on that population, and derives six counts from it: the total asset count; the number of assets whose `licensing_status` is `CLEARED`; the number of *open* assets, meaning those whose `licensing_status` is `UNCLEARED`, `IN_NEGOTIATION`, `PARTIALLY_CLEARED` or `LAPSED`; the number whose `depiction_status` is `BLOCKED`; the number whose `licensing_status` is `LAPSED`; and the number whose `depiction_status` is `PENDING`. Assets belonging to other productions never contribute. The five rules below are evaluated strictly top to bottom, and the first one that matches decides the posture:

1. If the production has no assets at all, posture is `OPEN`.
2. Otherwise, if at least one asset has `depiction_status` of `BLOCKED`, or at least one asset has `licensing_status` of `LAPSED`, posture is `BLOCKED`.
3. Otherwise, if the number of open assets is zero and the number of assets with `depiction_status` of `PENDING` is also zero, posture is `OPEN`.
4. Otherwise, if the number of open assets multiplied by four is less than or equal to the total asset count, posture is `RESTRICTED`. In other words, a production stays merely restricted while at least three quarters of its assets are non-open: one open asset out of four gives four, which is less than or equal to four, so the posture is `RESTRICTED`, whereas two open assets out of four gives eight, which is not less than or equal to four, so the posture is `BLOCKED`.
5. Otherwise, posture is `BLOCKED`.

Because rule 2 sits above the ratio test, a single blocked depiction or a single lapsed licence outranks any amount of otherwise-good coverage — an eighty-asset production with seventy-nine cleared assets and one `LAPSED` asset is `BLOCKED`, not `RESTRICTED`.

Two distinct gates read this value, and confusing them is a common error. Chart pinning, E&O submission and delivery-package validation require only that posture is *not* `BLOCKED`, so a `RESTRICTED` production can still pin a chart, submit for insurance and validate a package; a `BLOCKED` production cannot, and is refused with `ERR_PRODUCTION_POSTURE_BLOCKED` (HTTP 409). Moving a validated package to `DELIVERED`, and moving a production from `CLEARANCE` to `LOCKED`, require posture to be exactly `OPEN`; a `RESTRICTED` production is refused with `ERR_PRODUCTION_POSTURE_RESTRICTED` (HTTP 409) and a `BLOCKED` one with `ERR_PRODUCTION_POSTURE_BLOCKED`. Re-binding an E&O application out of `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED` likewise demands posture `OPEN`, refusing anything else with `ERR_PRODUCTION_POSTURE_BLOCKED`.

### 2.9. Ledger Invariants

Two conserved quantities must hold after every single request, and they are the cheapest way to detect a mis-implemented cascade.

The **licence money ledger** requires that `committed_fee_cents` on a production always equals the sum of `executed_fee_cents` over every licence whose `production_id` equals that production and whose `status` is `EXECUTED`, `ACTIVE` or `EXPIRING`. Licences in `DRAFT`, `QUOTED` or `COUNTERED` have a null `executed_fee_cents` and are excluded; licences in `EXPIRED`, `TERMINATED` or `LAPSED` are excluded. The ledger is maintained incrementally rather than recomputed: executing a licence adds its `executed_fee_cents`; a most-favored-nations top-up on a peer adds the top-up amount, because that peer remains inside the summed set; a renewal adds the renewal delta; terminating a licence subtracts its `executed_fee_cents` as an exact compensating action; and expiry subtracts it likewise. Moving a licence from `EXECUTED` to `ACTIVE`, or from `ACTIVE` to `EXPIRING`, moves it *within* the summed set and therefore changes nothing. The consequence to test against is exact reversibility: executing a licence and then terminating it must return `committed_fee_cents` to its precise pre-execution value, with no residue from any rounding, including any top-up that licence had received.

The **asset counter ledger** requires that `cleared_asset_count` plus `open_asset_count` always equals `asset_count` on the same production, because `licensing_status` is total over the cleared value and the four open values with nothing in between. Logging an asset increments `asset_count` and `open_asset_count` — or `asset_count` and `cleared_asset_count`, when a public-domain attestation clears the asset on sight. Whenever an asset's `licensing_status` becomes `CLEARED`, `cleared_asset_count` rises by one and `open_asset_count` falls by one; whenever it leaves `CLEARED`, the reverse. Here too reversibility is the test: issuing an opinion letter and accepting the analysis raises `cleared_asset_count` by one, and withdrawing that letter must return both `cleared_asset_count` and `open_asset_count` to their exact pre-issue values while `asset_count` never moves at all.

### 2.10. Cascade Propagation and Chart Staleness

Clearance state is immediately consistent: every derived value a request touches is recomputed inside that request, and the response reflects the settled result. There is no deferred or eventual recomputation anywhere in the system.

Recomputation flows in one direction, from the narrow fact to the broad consequence, and every request that changes coverage walks the chain as far as it reaches. A licence status change recomputes the owning asset's rights basis and licensing status; the asset change recomputes the production's three asset counters and its posture; the posture change can suspend a delivered package; a suspended package can push a bound E&O application into remediation; and a posture that is no longer `OPEN` can knock a `LOCKED` production back to `CLEARANCE`. Each hop bumps the `version` and refreshes the `updated_at` of the row it changes, so a single request routinely advances the `version` of a licence, an asset, a production, a chart, a package and an insurance application together.

**Chart staleness** is the mechanism that makes those version bumps legally meaningful. A `ClearanceChart` records, for each asset it pinned, the exact `asset_version` that was current at pin time. Whenever the `version` of any asset named in a chart's pinned rows advances — for any reason at all — a chart in `PINNED` moves to `STALE` and its own `version` increments. Once a chart is `STALE` it can no longer back an E&O submission or a delivery validation; those requests are refused with `ERR_CHART_STALE` (HTTP 409). Pinning a fresh chart on the same production moves every earlier chart of that production, whether it was `PINNED` or already `STALE`, to `SUPERSEDED`, and a `SUPERSEDED` chart likewise cannot back a submission or a validation — that refusal carries `ERR_CHART_SUPERSEDED` (HTTP 409). One case is explicitly *not* staleness: logging a brand-new asset does not stale any chart, because a chart's rows name only the assets that existed when it was pinned and none of their versions moved.

### 2.11. The Logical Clock and Date Arithmetic

The service reads time from a logical UTC clock, never from wall-clock time. Before any caller sets it, the clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`; this startup default is mandatory and the server never falls back to the host's real time. An operator of any role may move the clock by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant, and the response reports the resulting `current_utc`. Moving the clock has no side effects of its own: it runs no sweep, expires nothing and recomputes nothing. Every subsequent date comparison — licence activation, licence expiry, the renewal window, depiction windows, rate-card effectivity — reads the new instant.

Date fields are ISO dates in `YYYY-MM-DD` form and all comparisons against them use the date part of the logical clock. Distances between dates are measured in whole days.

A licence term is a **half-open interval** running from `term_start_date` inclusive to `term_end_date` exclusive. Coverage therefore ends at the start of `term_end_date`: when the clock's date equals `term_end_date`, the licence has expired, not "expires today". The same half-open convention governs overlap between sibling licences, which means adjacency is legal — a term starting exactly on an existing term's end date does not overlap it. Depiction windows are the opposite convention: `depiction_from_date` and `depiction_to_date` are both inclusive, and a depiction window is a closed interval.

### 2.12. Validation Precedence

When a single request violates more than one rule, exactly one error is returned, and which one is determined by a fixed global precedence rather than by the order the implementation happens to evaluate checks. The ladder below is authoritative and reads top to bottom; the first violated rung wins. The domain logic behind the ordering is that a documentary must never be delivered cheaply without a legal basis, so no fiscal rule ever pre-empts a clearance-safety rule.

1. Both authentication headers are present and non-empty — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. Applies to every request.
2. `X-User-Role` names one of the five declared roles — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`. Applies to every request.
3. The acting role appears in the target operation's authorized-role list — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`. Applies to every request.
4. A `RightsHolderRep`'s `X-User-ID` equals the `archive_id` of the licence or archive addressed — otherwise `ERR_ARCHIVE_OWNERSHIP_MISMATCH`. Applies to appending a negotiation round, driving a licence's status, reading a single licence, and listing an archive's rate cards.
5. Every resource named by a path parameter exists — otherwise the entity's own not-found code. Applies to every operation with an `{id}` in its path.
6. Every foreign key supplied in the body resolves, checked left to right in declaration order — otherwise the referenced entity's not-found code. Applies to licence creation, source-candidate creation, E&O application creation, delivery-package creation and subject-release creation.
7. The body is well formed: required fields present, enum values inside their declared domain, integers non-negative, ISO dates parseable, `timecode_out_seconds` strictly greater than `timecode_in_seconds`, and `term_months` at least 1 — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. Applies to every request.
8. `expected_production_version` equals the production's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_PRODUCTION_VERSION`. Applies to chart pinning.
9. `expected_license_version` equals the licence's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_LICENSE_VERSION`. Applies to appending a negotiation round.
10. The requested transition is declared for the resource's current state — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. Applies to all eight state-driving operations (production, archive, licence, fair-use analysis, opinion letter, subject release, E&O application, delivery package), and covers any attempt to leave a terminal state. Two cases yield a more specific code instead: requesting licence execution from any status other than `QUOTED` returns `ERR_LICENSE_NOT_QUOTED` (rung 12), and requesting an E&O binding from any status other than `CLEARED_TO_BIND` or `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED` returns `ERR_REVIEWS_INCOMPLETE` (rung 44).
11. The parent production accepts new clearance work, meaning its `status` is `DEVELOPMENT` or `CLEARANCE` — otherwise `ERR_PRODUCTION_NOT_IN_CLEARANCE`. A `LOCKED`, `DELIVERED` or `ABANDONED` production accepts no new assets, licences, releases or fair-use analyses. Applies to asset creation, licence creation, subject-release creation and fair-use-analysis creation.
12. A licence is `QUOTED` before it may be executed, and is `ACTIVE` or `EXPIRING` before it may be renewed — otherwise `ERR_LICENSE_NOT_QUOTED`. Applies to licence execution and licence renewal.
13. For a `FOOTAGE` leg, the asset has exactly one source candidate in `SELECTED` and the licence's `archive_id` equals that candidate's `archive_id` — otherwise `ERR_NO_SELECTED_SOURCE`. Applies to licence creation.
14. The production's `posture` is not `BLOCKED` — otherwise `ERR_PRODUCTION_POSTURE_BLOCKED`. Applies to chart pinning, E&O submission and delivery-package validation.
15. The production's `posture` is exactly `OPEN` — otherwise `ERR_PRODUCTION_POSTURE_RESTRICTED`. Applies to moving a delivery package from `VALIDATED` to `DELIVERED` and to moving a production from `CLEARANCE` to `LOCKED`.
16. The referenced archive's `status` is `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_ARCHIVE_SUSPENDED`. A suspended holder may not receive a new licence, may not be executed against, and may not have a new rate card issued. Applies to licence creation, licence status changes to `QUOTED` or `EXECUTED`, and rate-card publication.
17. The referenced asset belongs to the referenced production, and a referenced clearance chart belongs to the production being addressed — otherwise `ERR_ASSET_PRODUCTION_MISMATCH`. Applies to licence creation and subject-release creation.
18. The named archive has exactly one rate card in `ACTIVE` whose `effective_from_date` falls on or before the licence's `term_start_date`, and that card is the one stamped onto `rate_card_id` — otherwise `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_RATE_CARD`. Applies to licence creation and licence renewal.
19. The referenced clearance chart belongs to the production of the E&O application or delivery package — otherwise `ERR_CHART_PRODUCTION_MISMATCH`. Applies to E&O application creation and delivery-package creation.
20. The referenced clearance chart has not gone `STALE` — otherwise `ERR_CHART_STALE`. Applies to E&O submission and delivery-package validation.
21. The referenced clearance chart has not been `SUPERSEDED` by a later pinning on the same production — otherwise `ERR_CHART_SUPERSEDED`. Applies to E&O submission and delivery-package validation.
22. At least one opinion letter on the analysis is in `ISSUED` — otherwise `ERR_NO_ISSUED_OPINION_LETTER`. Applies to accepting a fair-use analysis.
23. Segregation of duties: the `X-User-ID` recording the legal review is not any `issued_by_user_id` among the opinion letters pinned on the application's chart, and the legal reviewer does not also record the underwriting review — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Applies to recording an E&O review.
24. The same `review_type` has not already been recorded on this application — otherwise `ERR_REVIEW_ALREADY_RECORDED`. Applies to recording an E&O review.
25. `review_type` of `LEGAL` is recorded only by `LegalCounsel` and `UNDERWRITING` only by `ClearanceSupervisor` — otherwise `ERR_REVIEW_TYPE_ROLE_MISMATCH`. Applies to recording an E&O review.
26. The chart's `music_leg_gap_count` is zero — otherwise `ERR_CUE_SHEET_INCOMPLETE`. Applies to delivery-package validation.
27. The package's `window_class` is covered by every licence backing its pinned rows — otherwise `ERR_WINDOW_CLASS_INSUFFICIENT`. Applies to delivery-package validation.
28. The production has an E&O application in `BOUND` — otherwise `ERR_EO_NOT_BOUND`. Applies to moving a delivery package to `DELIVERED`.
29. The production has at least one delivery package in `DELIVERED` — otherwise `ERR_NO_DELIVERED_PACKAGE`. Applies to moving a production from `LOCKED` to `DELIVERED`.
30. A release's `approval_rights` is not `NONE` before an approval decision may be taken — otherwise `ERR_NO_APPROVAL_RIGHTS`. Applies to subject-release status changes.
31. No licence of the production is in `EXECUTED`, `ACTIVE` or `EXPIRING` — otherwise `ERR_LICENSES_STILL_LIVE`. Applies to abandoning a production.
32. At least one source candidate of the asset has `availability_status` of `AVAILABLE` — otherwise `ERR_NO_AVAILABLE_SOURCE`. Applies to source selection.
33. The round's `party` matches the acting role: `LICENSOR` requires `RightsHolderRep`, `PRODUCER` requires `ProductionAdmin` or `ClearanceSupervisor` — otherwise `ERR_PARTY_ROLE_MISMATCH`. Applies to appending a negotiation round.
34. The licence's `round_count` is below 6 — otherwise `ERR_NEGOTIATION_ROUND_LIMIT`. Applies to appending a negotiation round.
35. The release's `revision_round_count` is below 3 — otherwise `ERR_RELEASE_REVISION_LIMIT`. Applies to requesting a release revision.
36. A `MUSIC` asset takes a `rights_leg` of `MASTER` or `PUBLISHING`, and an `ARCHIVAL_FOOTAGE` asset takes `FOOTAGE` — otherwise `ERR_MUSIC_LEG_REQUIRED`. Applies to licence creation.
37. `term_start_date` is earlier than `term_end_date` and `term_months` is consistent with the whole-month span between them — otherwise `ERR_TERM_DATES_INVALID`. Applies to licence creation.
38. The requested term does not overlap a live sibling licence as defined in §3.3 — otherwise `ERR_LICENSE_TERM_OVERLAP`. Applies to licence creation and licence renewal.
39. The requested depiction window, widened by the quiet buffer defined in §3.6, does not intersect a live release for the same `subject_person_key` on the same production — otherwise `ERR_DEPICTION_WINDOW_CONFLICT`. Applies to subject-release creation.
40. The whole-day distance from the clock's date to `term_end_date` is between 0 and 60 inclusive — otherwise `ERR_OUTSIDE_RENEWAL_WINDOW`. Applies to licence renewal.
41. `additional_term_months` is at least 1 — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_RENEWAL_TERM`. Applies to licence renewal.
42. The analysis's `strength_score` is at least 0.6000 — otherwise `ERR_FAIR_USE_SCORE_INSUFFICIENT`. Applies to accepting a fair-use analysis.
43. `coverage_limit_cents` is at least 100000 and `retention_cents` is strictly less than `coverage_limit_cents` — otherwise `ERR_COVERAGE_LIMIT_INVALID`. Applies to E&O application creation.
44. Binding requires both `legal_review_status` and `underwriting_review_status` to be `PASSED`, which is what `CLEARED_TO_BIND` represents — otherwise `ERR_REVIEWS_INCOMPLETE`. Applies to E&O status changes to `CLEARED_TO_BIND` or `BOUND`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Opening a Production File and Logging Archival Assets

A `ProductionAdmin` opens the production file first, and nothing else in the system can be attached to a film until that file exists. The payload names the documentary's `title`, the `delivery_deadline_date` on which the distributor delivery is contractually due, and two optional facts about the release plan: `festival_premiere_date`, which is null when the film has no festival window at all, and `eo_carrier_name`, the errors-and-omissions carrier the application will eventually be filed with, which takes the literal value `UNASSIGNED` when the carrier has not been chosen. A new production is born in `DEVELOPMENT` with `posture` reading `OPEN`, with `asset_count`, `cleared_asset_count`, `open_asset_count`, `committed_fee_cents` and `sweep_epoch` all at zero, and with `version` at 1.

The production's own lifecycle runs `DEVELOPMENT → CLEARANCE → LOCKED → DELIVERED`, with `ABANDONED` available as an early exit and one system-driven reverse edge. Each requested move names the target state in `to_status` and may carry a free-text `reason`. A `ProductionAdmin` opens clearance work by moving `DEVELOPMENT → CLEARANCE`; the move has no preconditions, and it recomputes `posture` on the way through, which for a production with no assets yet yields `OPEN`. The move `CLEARANCE → LOCKED` is reserved to a `ClearanceSupervisor` and demands two things: `posture` must be exactly `OPEN` (a `RESTRICTED` production is refused with `ERR_PRODUCTION_POSTURE_RESTRICTED` and a `BLOCKED` one with `ERR_PRODUCTION_POSTURE_BLOCKED`), and at least one E&O application of the production must be in `BOUND`, failing which the answer is `ERR_EO_NOT_BOUND`. The move `LOCKED → DELIVERED`, also a `ClearanceSupervisor` move, requires at least one delivery package of the production in `DELIVERED`, failing which the answer is `ERR_NO_DELIVERED_PACKAGE`.

Abandonment behaves differently on each side of the clearance boundary, and the asymmetry is intentional. From `DEVELOPMENT`, `ABANDONED` has no preconditions at all — nothing has been committed yet. From `CLEARANCE`, abandonment first checks that no licence of the production sits in `EXECUTED`, `ACTIVE` or `EXPIRING`; if one does, the request is refused with `ERR_LICENSES_STILL_LIVE`, because a film cannot be walked away from while binding money is outstanding. Where the check passes, the abandonment additionally terminates every licence of the production still in `DRAFT`, `QUOTED` or `COUNTERED`, sweeping the open negotiations off the table. `DELIVERED` and `ABANDONED` are terminal: no transition leaves either, and an attempt to leave one is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`. The reverse edge `LOCKED → CLEARANCE` is never requestable by an operator; it is driven by the system when a rights sweep recomputes a locked production's posture to something other than `OPEN`, and it increments the production's `version`.

Assets are logged into the production by an `ArchiveResearcher` or a `ProductionAdmin` as archive research turns up usable material, and only while the production is in `DEVELOPMENT` or `CLEARANCE` — a `LOCKED`, `DELIVERED` or `ABANDONED` film refuses new assets with `ERR_PRODUCTION_NOT_IN_CLEARANCE`. Each asset declares its `asset_type`, either `ARCHIVAL_FOOTAGE` or `MUSIC`; its `title` as the shot or cue title will appear on the cue sheet; its cut points `timecode_in_seconds` and `timecode_out_seconds` in whole seconds, the in-point being zero or greater and the out-point strictly greater than the in-point; and its `usage_purpose`, either `CRITICAL_COMMENTARY` or `ILLUSTRATIVE_BACKGROUND`, which later selects the fair-use weighting variant. Two optional fields carry the legal texture: `depiction_subject_key` is a stable key for the identifiable person shown in the material and is null when nobody is identifiable, and `pd_attestation_reference` is a public-domain attestation document reference whose presence asserts the public-domain route to clearance. The server derives `duration_seconds` as the out-point minus the in-point.

A freshly logged asset is stamped with `licensing_status` of `UNCLEARED`, `lapse_count` of zero, `selected_source_candidate_id` of null, and a `rights_basis` of `UNDETERMINED` — unless `pd_attestation_reference` was supplied, in which case the rights basis reads `PUBLIC_DOMAIN` and the asset is cleared on sight. Its `depiction_status` initialises to `PENDING` when `depiction_subject_key` is non-null at creation and to `NOT_REQUIRED` otherwise. Creation then runs the clearance rollup followed by the posture recomputation, raises `asset_count` by one together with `open_asset_count` by one (or with `cleared_asset_count` by one where the attestation cleared it immediately), and increments the production's `version`. Logging a new asset never stales an existing pinned chart: a chart's rows name only the assets that existed at pin time, and none of their versions moved.

An asset carries **two independent status dimensions on one row**, and they never share a state name. Dimension A is `licensing_status`, taking `UNCLEARED`, `IN_NEGOTIATION`, `PARTIALLY_CLEARED`, `CLEARED` or `LAPSED`, and it advances on licence and fair-use events. Dimension B is `depiction_status`, taking `NOT_REQUIRED`, `PENDING`, `OBTAINED` or `BLOCKED`, and it advances only on subject-release events. Both dimensions must read favourably before the production's posture can report `OPEN`.

Dimension A is never set directly by a caller; it is the output of a **clearance rollup** that runs after every licence creation, every licence status change, every licence renewal, every fair-use analysis status change, every opinion-letter status change, every subject-release status change, and after every rights-sweep step that touched a licence of the asset. The rollup begins by establishing two leg sets. The *required* legs of a `MUSIC` asset are `MASTER` and `PUBLISHING` together; the required legs of an `ARCHIVAL_FOOTAGE` asset are the single leg `FOOTAGE`. The *covered* legs are the distinct `rights_leg` values of the asset's licences whose `status` is `EXECUTED`, `ACTIVE` or `EXPIRING`; licences in `EXPIRED`, `TERMINATED` or `LAPSED` contribute nothing to coverage.

With those two sets in hand the rollup runs three steps in a fixed order. **Step one** settles `rights_basis` by strict precedence, taking the first match: a non-null `pd_attestation_reference` yields `PUBLIC_DOMAIN`; otherwise the existence of any fair-use analysis on this asset in `ACCEPTED` yields `FAIR_USE`; otherwise a covered-leg set that includes every required leg yields `LICENSED`; otherwise the basis is `UNDETERMINED`. **Step two** performs lapse accounting, and it must run before step three because its output feeds step three: if the asset's previous `licensing_status` was `CLEARED` and the newly computed `rights_basis` is `UNDETERMINED`, then `lapse_count` is increased by one, so a single request both records the lapse and reports it. **Step three** settles `licensing_status`, again by strict first-match precedence: any `rights_basis` other than `UNDETERMINED` yields `CLEARED`; otherwise a `lapse_count` greater than zero combined with an empty covered-leg set yields `LAPSED`; otherwise a non-empty covered-leg set yields `PARTIALLY_CLEARED`; otherwise the existence of any licence on this asset in `DRAFT`, `QUOTED` or `COUNTERED` yields `IN_NEGOTIATION`; otherwise the status is `UNCLEARED`. The boundary that matters most here involves no arithmetic at all: a music asset holding only its `MASTER` leg has a non-empty but incomplete covered set and is therefore `PARTIALLY_CLEARED`, never `CLEARED`. `lapse_count` is a historical counter and is never decremented, so an asset that lapses and is then re-covered by a replacement licence moves `LAPSED → CLEARED` while keeping its lapse history intact.

### 3.2. Archive Research: Rights Holders, Rate Cards and Source Candidates

Rights holders are registered as archives before any material can be traced to them. Either an `ArchiveResearcher` or a `ProductionAdmin` registers one, supplying the holder's `name` and its `holder_type`, drawn from `FILM_ARCHIVE`, `NEWS_ARCHIVE`, `STUDIO`, `RECORD_LABEL`, `MUSIC_PUBLISHER` and `INDIVIDUAL`. The optional `mfn_policy` declares the holder's negotiating posture and takes either `NONE` or `RATE_MFN`, defaulting to `NONE`; a holder on `RATE_MFN` is one that demands most-favored-nations parity across the rights legs of a single asset. A new archive is `ACTIVE` with `version` at 1.

An archive's `status` cycles. A `ClearanceSupervisor` moves an archive `ACTIVE → SUSPENDED` — typically during a chain-of-title dispute — and back `SUSPENDED → ACTIVE` once the dispute is resolved; neither direction has preconditions and each increments the archive's `version`. Suspension is forward-looking only: licences already in `EXECUTED`, `ACTIVE` or `EXPIRING` against a suspended holder are entirely unaffected and continue to count towards coverage and towards the money ledger. What suspension blocks is new commitment — a new licence against the holder, an execution against the holder, and the publication of a new rate card for the holder are each refused with `ERR_ARCHIVE_SUSPENDED`.

Pricing lives on rate cards, and an archive has **at most one** card in `ACTIVE` at any instant, which is what makes the phrase "the archive's rate card" a deterministic single row. A card is published by an `ArchiveResearcher` or by the holder's own `RightsHolderRep`. Three money fields are mandatory: `base_rate_per_30s_cents`, the list rate for one thirty-second billable unit; `minimum_fee_cents`, the fee floor that applies to an all-media window; and `festival_minimum_fee_cents`, the fee floor that applies to a festival-only window. The mandatory `effective_from_date` is the ISO date on which the card takes effect. Everything else is optional and falls back to a declared default: the duration tiers are bounded by `tier1_max_seconds`, defaulting to 30 seconds, and `tier2_max_seconds`, defaulting to 120 seconds, with `tier2_rate_multiplier` defaulting to 1.25 and `tier3_rate_multiplier` defaulting to 1.60. The media multipliers are `media_multiplier_theatrical` at 1.40, `media_multiplier_broadcast` at 1.15, `media_multiplier_streaming` at 1.30 and `media_multiplier_all_media` at 1.75. The territory multipliers are `territory_multiplier_world` at 1.50 and `territory_multiplier_domestic` at 1.00. The term uplift `term_multiplier_per_year` defaults to 0.20 and is additive per licence year beyond the first. The window multipliers are `festival_window_multiplier` at 0.35 and `all_media_window_multiplier` at 1.00. A card's `status` is either `ACTIVE` or `SUPERSEDED`, and `archive_id` is taken from the path rather than the body.

Publishing a card resolves the single-active-card guarantee by comparing effective dates, and the two outcomes are genuinely different. When the incoming card's `effective_from_date` falls on or after the existing `ACTIVE` card's `effective_from_date`, the existing card moves `ACTIVE → SUPERSEDED` and its `version` increments, while the incoming card is created in `ACTIVE`. When the incoming card's `effective_from_date` is *earlier* than the existing `ACTIVE` card's, no supersession fires at all: the existing card stays `ACTIVE` and untouched, and the incoming card is created directly in `SUPERSEDED` — a back-dated card is filed for the record but never becomes the live price list. `SUPERSEDED` is terminal for a rate card.

Meanwhile the researcher traces each asset to the institutions that might actually hold a usable element. Recording a source candidate against an asset is the `ArchiveResearcher`'s exclusive act, and the payload names the `archive_id` of the holder claiming to control the element and the element's `availability_status`, which takes `AVAILABLE`, `ELEMENT_MISSING`, `RIGHTS_UNCLEAR` or `WITHDRAWN`. Only an `AVAILABLE` candidate is selectable. Two optional research findings ride along: `quoted_rate_per_30s_cents` is the verbal research-stage quote per thirty-second unit and serves as the selection ranking key, and `provenance_confidence` is the archivist's confidence in the chain of title as an integer from 0 to 100 inclusive. A new candidate's `selection_status` reads `NOT_SELECTED`, and its `asset_id` comes from the path.

Choosing the element the licence will be cut against is a separate, deliberate act available to an `ArchiveResearcher` or a `ClearanceSupervisor`. The request names a single `source_candidate_id`; a candidate id that does not exist, or that belongs to some other asset, is answered with `ERR_SOURCE_CANDIDATE_NOT_FOUND`. If no candidate of the asset has `availability_status` of `AVAILABLE`, the selection is refused outright with `ERR_NO_AVAILABLE_SOURCE` — an asset whose only traced elements are missing, withdrawn or of unclear provenance cannot be sourced. On success the chosen candidate's `selection_status` becomes `SELECTED`, any candidate that was previously `SELECTED` is demoted to `REJECTED`, the asset's `selected_source_candidate_id` is set to the chosen candidate, and the asset's `version` increments.

### 3.3. Opening a Licence and Pricing It Against the Rate Card

A licence covers exactly one rights leg of exactly one asset with exactly one holder, and it is opened by a `ProductionAdmin` or a `ClearanceSupervisor` while the production is still in `DEVELOPMENT` or `CLEARANCE`. Because a licence names its own graph edges, the payload carries `production_id`, `asset_id` and `archive_id` explicitly, in that declaration order, followed by the deal shape: `rights_leg` (`FOOTAGE`, `MASTER` or `PUBLISHING`), `media` (`THEATRICAL`, `BROADCAST`, `STREAMING` or `ALL_MEDIA`), `territory` (`WORLD` or `DOMESTIC`), `window_class` (`FESTIVAL_ONLY` or `ALL_MEDIA_WINDOW`), the whole-month `term_months` of at least 1, and the term boundaries `term_start_date` and `term_end_date`. The optional `mfn_requested` flag declares that the holder demands most-favored-nations parity across the sibling legs of the same asset; when the client omits it, the server sets it to true where the named archive's `mfn_policy` is `RATE_MFN`, and to false otherwise.

Licence creation is the most heavily guarded write in the system, and the guards fire in the global precedence order of §2.12. After the three body foreign keys resolve left to right, the production must still accept clearance work (`ERR_PRODUCTION_NOT_IN_CLEARANCE`). The source-selection check comes next: for a `FOOTAGE` leg the asset must have exactly one source candidate in `SELECTED`, and the licence's `archive_id` must equal that candidate's `archive_id`, since licensing footage from an institution other than the one whose print was chosen is meaningless — either failure yields `ERR_NO_SELECTED_SOURCE`. Then the archive must be `ACTIVE` (`ERR_ARCHIVE_SUSPENDED`), the asset must belong to the named production (`ERR_ASSET_PRODUCTION_MISMATCH`), and the archive must have exactly one rate card in `ACTIVE` whose `effective_from_date` falls on or before the licence's `term_start_date` (`ERR_NO_ACTIVE_RATE_CARD`). That resolved card's id is stamped onto the licence as `rate_card_id`, which is a computed field and never a client input. The leg must match the asset type — `MUSIC` takes `MASTER` or `PUBLISHING`, `ARCHIVAL_FOOTAGE` takes `FOOTAGE`, and any other pairing yields `ERR_MUSIC_LEG_REQUIRED`. The term dates must be coherent, with `term_start_date` earlier than `term_end_date` and `term_months` consistent with the whole-month span between them, failing which the answer is `ERR_TERM_DATES_INVALID`. The overlap rule is checked last.

**The overlap rule** protects against selling the same rights twice. A new licence is refused with `ERR_LICENSE_TERM_OVERLAP` when there already exists a licence on the same `asset_id`, with the same `rights_leg`, whose `status` is one of `DRAFT`, `QUOTED`, `COUNTERED`, `EXECUTED`, `ACTIVE` or `EXPIRING`, whose media overlaps the requested media, whose territory overlaps the requested territory, and whose half-open term interval intersects the requested one. Media overlap means the two `media` values are identical, or either side is `ALL_MEDIA`. Territory overlap means the two `territory` values are identical, or either side is `WORLD`. Term intersection is evaluated on the half-open convention of §2.11, so adjacency is permitted: a term starting exactly on an existing term's `term_end_date` does not overlap that term, and both licences may stand.

A newly created licence lands in `DRAFT`. The server stamps `billable_units` as the asset's `duration_seconds` divided by 30 and rounded *up* to a whole number, so a shot of any length between 1 and 30 seconds bills as one unit and a 45-second shot bills as two. It then runs the rate-card pricing described below and stamps the result onto `rate_card_fee_cents`, initialises `current_proposed_fee_cents` to that same rate-card figure so that the standing proposal before any negotiation is the list price, sets `round_count` to zero, sets `mfn_topup_cents` and `mfn_benchmark_fee_cents` to zero, and leaves `executed_fee_cents` and `termination_reason` null. Creation then runs the clearance rollup, which moves the asset's dimension A from `UNCLEARED → IN_NEGOTIATION` because a licence for the asset now exists in `DRAFT`, followed by the posture recomputation; the asset's `version` and the production's `version` each increment by one.

**Rate-card pricing** takes exactly one asset — the one at `asset_id` — and exactly one rate card — the one at `rate_card_id` — and aggregates nothing else. No other licence influences it. It proceeds as follows.

First, the billable unit count is the asset's `duration_seconds` divided by 30 seconds per billable unit and rounded up to a whole number. Second, the duration tier is selected from the asset's `duration_seconds` against the card's **inclusive** upper bounds: a duration less than or equal to `tier1_max_seconds` takes a rate multiplier of exactly 1.00, so a duration equal to the tier-1 bound stays in tier 1; a duration greater than `tier1_max_seconds` but less than or equal to `tier2_max_seconds` takes `tier2_rate_multiplier`; and a duration greater than `tier2_max_seconds` takes `tier3_rate_multiplier`. Third, the media multiplier is read from the card according to the licence's `media`: `THEATRICAL` reads `media_multiplier_theatrical`, `BROADCAST` reads `media_multiplier_broadcast`, `STREAMING` reads `media_multiplier_streaming`, and `ALL_MEDIA` reads `media_multiplier_all_media`. Fourth, the territory multiplier is `territory_multiplier_world` for a `WORLD` licence and `territory_multiplier_domestic` for a `DOMESTIC` one. Fifth, the term factor charges only for years beyond the first: the term year count is `term_months` divided by 12 months per term year and rounded *up* to a whole number, and the term factor is 1 plus `term_multiplier_per_year` multiplied by the term year count less one, with that subtraction floored at zero so that a licence of twelve months or fewer carries a term factor of exactly 1.

Sixth comes the **window branch**, which is where two otherwise identical deals diverge sharply. A licence whose `window_class` is `FESTIVAL_ONLY` — a film cleared only for festival exhibition — uses `festival_window_multiplier` as its window multiplier and `festival_minimum_fee_cents` as its fee floor. A licence whose `window_class` is `ALL_MEDIA_WINDOW` uses `all_media_window_multiplier` as its window multiplier and `minimum_fee_cents` as its fee floor.

Finally the arithmetic, and its rounding order is pinned. The raw fee is the product of six quantities held at full precision throughout: `base_rate_per_30s_cents`, the billable unit count, the duration tier's rate multiplier, the media multiplier, the territory multiplier, the term factor, and the window multiplier. That product is rounded half up to an integer number of cents exactly once, and only then is `rate_card_fee_cents` taken as the larger of that rounded product and the window's fee floor. Rounding any individual factor, or rounding before the floor comparison is applied, is wrong. To make the window divergence concrete: a 45-second shot licensed for `BROADCAST` in `WORLD` for 36 months against a card whose `base_rate_per_30s_cents` is 60000, whose `minimum_fee_cents` is 150000 and whose `festival_minimum_fee_cents` is 40000 bills two units, sits in tier 2 at 1.25, takes the broadcast multiplier of 1.15, the world multiplier of 1.50 and a term factor of 1.40 for its three term years. Under `FESTIVAL_ONLY` the window multiplier of 0.35 yields a raw product of 126787.5, which rounds half up to 126788 cents and clears the festival floor of 40000. Under `ALL_MEDIA_WINDOW` the window multiplier of 1.00 yields 362250 cents, which clears the all-media floor of 150000. The same asset, media, territory and term therefore differ by 235462 cents purely on the window sold.

### 3.4. Negotiation Rounds, Execution and Most-Favored-Nations Parity

A licence in `DRAFT` is a position, not a deal. The deal is built out of **negotiation rounds**, an append-only sequence in which the two sides alternate proposals. Each round declares the `party` putting money on the table — `LICENSOR` for the holder's side, `PRODUCER` for the production's side — the `proposed_fee_cents` that party is proposing (zero or greater), the mandatory `expected_license_version` described in §2.7, and an optional deal-memo `note`. A round whose `party` is `LICENSOR` may only be posted by a `RightsHolderRep`; a round whose `party` is `PRODUCER` may only be posted by a `ProductionAdmin` or a `ClearanceSupervisor`. Any other pairing of party and acting role is refused with `ERR_PARTY_ROLE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403). A `RightsHolderRep` posting a `LICENSOR` round must additionally be the licence's own holder, meaning its `X-User-ID` equals the licence's `archive_id`, failing which the round is refused with `ERR_ARCHIVE_OWNERSHIP_MISMATCH`.

The rounds drive the licence through a cycle. A `LICENSOR` round posted against a licence in `DRAFT` advances it `DRAFT → QUOTED`. A `PRODUCER` round posted against a licence in `QUOTED` advances it `QUOTED → COUNTERED`. A `LICENSOR` round posted against a licence in `COUNTERED` advances it `COUNTERED → QUOTED` again, and the pair `QUOTED → COUNTERED → QUOTED` repeats until one side executes or the round ceiling stops the negotiation. That ceiling is six: a round is accepted only while the licence's `round_count` is below 6, and the seventh attempt is refused with `ERR_NEGOTIATION_ROUND_LIMIT` (HTTP 409). Before that limit is consulted, the stale-version check has already run, so a client working from an outdated licence sees `ERR_STALE_LICENSE_VERSION` rather than a round-limit answer.

Every accepted round performs the same bookkeeping. The round is created with `round_number` equal to the licence's `round_count` before the round plus one, so the first round is round 1. The licence's `round_count` increases by one, its `current_proposed_fee_cents` is overwritten with this round's `proposed_fee_cents` — the standing proposal is always simply the most recent one, regardless of which side made it — and its `version` increments. The round itself is then stamped with `license_status_after`, the licence's `status` immediately after the round landed, and `license_version_after`, the licence's `version` immediately after the round landed, which is the value the next round on this licence must carry.

**Execution** is the moment the deal becomes binding, and it is only reachable from `QUOTED` — the producer accepts the holder's standing quote rather than the reverse. Requesting execution from any other status is refused with `ERR_LICENSE_NOT_QUOTED` (HTTP 409), which displaces the generic invalid-transition answer. Three further conditions are checked: the holder's archive must be `ACTIVE`, so a licence cannot be executed against a suspended holder (`ERR_ARCHIVE_SUSPENDED`); the production must be in `CLEARANCE`, and a production elsewhere in its lifecycle yields `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`; and the licence's term must not overlap a live sibling as defined in §3.3, since a term that has become conflicting since creation may not be made binding (`ERR_LICENSE_TERM_OVERLAP`).

Where those conditions hold, the licence moves `QUOTED → EXECUTED` and the **most-favored-nations settlement** runs. This calculation looks circular — a licence's binding fee depends on the highest per-unit rate among the most-favored-nations siblings on the same asset, and those siblings' fees in turn depend on this licence — so its evaluation order is pinned and the dependency is always resolved against values already persisted. The peer set is fixed once, before any writing: it consists of every other licence sharing this licence's `asset_id`, excluding the licence being executed, whose `mfn_requested` is true and whose `status` is `EXECUTED`, `ACTIVE` or `EXPIRING`. Siblings in `DRAFT`, `QUOTED`, `COUNTERED`, `EXPIRED`, `TERMINATED` or `LAPSED` are not peers.

Step one establishes this licence's own base with no most-favored-nations input whatsoever: the base fee is the larger of `current_proposed_fee_cents` and `rate_card_fee_cents`, so a negotiation that talked the holder below list price still pays list price at execution. Step two establishes the benchmark. If `mfn_requested` on this licence is false, `mfn_benchmark_fee_cents` is 0. If it is true but the peer set is empty, `mfn_benchmark_fee_cents` is likewise 0. Otherwise, the peer unit rate is taken as the greatest value, across all peers, of that peer's persisted `executed_fee_cents` divided by that peer's `billable_units`, computed at full precision with no intermediate rounding; `mfn_benchmark_fee_cents` is then that peer unit rate multiplied by *this* licence's `billable_units`, rounded half up to an integer number of cents. Peers contribute only their already-persisted `executed_fee_cents`, and no peer fee is recomputed while the benchmark is being formed, which is exactly why the apparent cycle terminates after a single pass. Step three sets this licence's `executed_fee_cents` to the larger of the step-one base fee and the step-two benchmark, and derives this licence's own unit rate as that binding fee divided by its `billable_units`, again at full precision.

Step four propagates parity backwards, and this is the retroactive part that surprises implementers. The peers are walked in ascending `created_at` order, never by id. For each peer whose own unit rate — its persisted `executed_fee_cents` divided by its `billable_units` — is strictly below the executing licence's unit rate, a target fee is computed as the executing licence's unit rate multiplied by that peer's `billable_units`, rounded half up to an integer number of cents. The difference between that target and the peer's current `executed_fee_cents` is the top-up: it is added to the peer's `mfn_topup_cents`, the peer's `executed_fee_cents` is raised to the target, the peer's `version` increments, and the production's `committed_fee_cents` rises by the same top-up amount. A peer whose unit rate already equals or exceeds the executing licence's rate is left completely untouched: parity, once granted, is never clawed back, and no downward adjustment ever occurs. Finally, the executing licence's own `executed_fee_cents` is added to `committed_fee_cents`, the clearance rollup runs for the asset — which is what drives the asset's dimension-A move and in turn the production's counters, posture and chart staleness — and the licence's `version` increments.

Execution is also the event that most often moves an asset's dimension A, and the exact landing state depends on the asset's leg arithmetic. Executing the single `FOOTAGE` leg of an archival-footage asset completes its required set and moves the asset `IN_NEGOTIATION → CLEARED`, setting `rights_basis` to `LICENSED`, raising `cleared_asset_count` by one and lowering `open_asset_count` by one, recomputing the production's posture and flipping every `PINNED` chart pinning the asset to `STALE`. Executing the first of a music asset's two legs covers a non-empty but incomplete set and moves it `IN_NEGOTIATION → PARTIALLY_CLEARED`, which bumps the asset's `version`, recomputes the posture and stales pinning charts but leaves the production's cleared and open counters untouched, since a partially cleared asset is still an open asset. Executing the remaining leg then moves it `PARTIALLY_CLEARED → CLEARED` with the full cleared-asset bookkeeping. And executing a replacement licence for an asset that had lost its coverage moves it `LAPSED → CLEARED`, again setting `rights_basis` to `LICENSED` and restoring the counters, while `lapse_count` keeps its historical value.

The two variants of this settlement are worth naming because their observable outputs differ. When no most-favored-nations peer of the asset has executed yet, the benchmark is 0, step four does nothing, and the binding fee equals the step-one base fee. When at least one peer is already `EXECUTED`, `ACTIVE` or `EXPIRING`, the binding fee can be lifted above both the negotiated proposal and the rate-card fee, and step four can retroactively raise a sibling that had already executed — with the consequence that the same two licences executed in the opposite order produce a different pair of `executed_fee_cents` values while leaving the production's ledger total the same.

Not every negotiation ends in a deal. From either `QUOTED` or `COUNTERED`, a `ClearanceSupervisor` may move the licence to `LAPSED`, which has no preconditions, recomputes the asset's `licensing_status` and increments the licence's `version`; because a lapsed licence contributes to neither the coverage set nor the negotiation set, an asset whose only licence lapses rolls back towards `UNCLEARED`. From `EXECUTED`, `ACTIVE` or `EXPIRING`, a `ClearanceSupervisor` may move the licence to `TERMINATED`; the supplied `reason` is stored as `termination_reason`, the production's `committed_fee_cents` is debited by exactly this licence's `executed_fee_cents` — which necessarily includes any `mfn_topup_cents` this licence had received, so the compensation is exact — the asset's `licensing_status` is recomputed, and the licence's `version` increments. `EXPIRED`, `TERMINATED` and `LAPSED` are terminal for a licence.

### 3.5. Fair Use Analysis, Opinion Letters and the Withdrawal Cascade

Not every fragment can be licensed. When a rights holder cannot be found, will not deal, or prices a three-second clip beyond reason, counsel builds a fair-use position instead. Opening a four-factor analysis on an asset is `LegalCounsel`'s exclusive act, permitted only while the production is in `DEVELOPMENT` or `CLEARANCE`, and the payload is four favourability scores, each an integer from 0 to 100 inclusive: `transformative_score` for the first factor, `nature_score` for the second, `amount_used_score` for the third, and `market_harm_score` for the fourth. Higher is always more favourable to fair use, which for the fourth factor means a high `market_harm_score` denotes *less* market harm. The analysis's `asset_id` is taken from the path, and a new analysis lands in `DRAFT` with a null `decision_rationale`.

The **weighting variant** is never supplied by the client; it is read from the parent asset's `usage_purpose` and stamped onto `weighting_variant`. An asset used for `CRITICAL_COMMENTARY` yields `TRANSFORMATIVE_WEIGHTED`, weighting the transformative factor at 0.45, nature at 0.10, amount used at 0.15 and market harm at 0.30. An asset used for `ILLUSTRATIVE_BACKGROUND` yields `MARKET_HARM_WEIGHTED`, weighting the transformative factor at 0.20, nature at 0.10, amount used at 0.20 and market harm at 0.50. In both variants the weights sum to one.

`strength_score` is then computed and stamped at creation. Each of the four factor scores is first divided by 100 to bring it onto a zero-to-one scale, each result is multiplied by that factor's weight from the selected variant, the four products are added together, and the sum is rounded half up to exactly four decimal places. Nothing is aggregated across analyses, and because a `REJECTED → DRAFT` revision reuses the existing row rather than creating a new one, a resubmitted analysis carries the same stored score it had before. The variant choice is decisive rather than cosmetic: factor scores of 90, 60, 40 and 50 score 0.6750 under the transformative weighting and clear the acceptance threshold, while the very same four numbers score 0.5700 under the market-harm weighting and are refused — identical inputs, opposite outcomes, decided entirely by the parent asset's `usage_purpose`.

An analysis is driven by `LegalCounsel` or a `ClearanceSupervisor` through `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`, and from `SUBMITTED` either to `ACCEPTED` or to `REJECTED`, with the supplied `decision_rationale` stored on whichever decision is taken. Submission itself has no preconditions. Acceptance has two, and their order matters: the opinion-letter requirement is checked first, so an analysis with no supporting letter is refused with `ERR_NO_ISSUED_OPINION_LETTER` (HTTP 409) even when its score is also too low, and only once at least one opinion letter on the analysis is in `ISSUED` does the score gate apply, refusing a `strength_score` below 0.6000 with `ERR_FAIR_USE_SCORE_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 422). The threshold is inclusive at the boundary: a score of exactly 0.6000 is acceptable. An unlicensed asset therefore never becomes cleared on a bare number — outside counsel must have put its name to the position.

Acceptance is what clears the asset. The asset's `rights_basis` becomes `FAIR_USE` and its `licensing_status` recomputes to `CLEARED`, whether the asset had been sitting in `UNCLEARED` with no licences at all or in `IN_NEGOTIATION` with a licence still on the table — both `UNCLEARED → CLEARED` and `IN_NEGOTIATION → CLEARED` are legitimate dimension-A moves out of an acceptance. The production's `cleared_asset_count` rises by one, its `open_asset_count` falls by one, its posture is recomputed, every `PINNED` chart pinning that asset flips to `STALE`, and the analysis's `version` increments alongside the asset's and the production's. Rejection from `SUBMITTED` stores its rationale and increments the analysis's `version` but touches nothing else, and `REJECTED → DRAFT` re-opens the analysis so counsel can rework the four-factor assessment and resubmit — a cycle with no preconditions of its own.

Opinion letters attach to a submitted analysis and are `LegalCounsel`'s exclusive province. The payload names the `counsel_firm` issuing the opinion, the firm's own `letter_reference` number, and a `risk_rating` of `LOW`, `MODERATE` or `HIGH`. The letter's `fair_use_analysis_id` comes from the path, its `status` starts at `DRAFT`, its `withdrawal_reason` starts null, and — critically for §3.7 — the acting operator's `X-User-ID` is captured as `issued_by_user_id`. That capture is the segregation-of-duties key: it is how the system later knows which lawyer's opinion is being relied upon. Moving a letter `DRAFT → ISSUED` requires the parent analysis to be in `SUBMITTED`; any other parent status is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.

Withdrawal is the most destructive single call in the system, and it is deliberately modelled as a **five-hop cascade** so that no exposure can hide behind a stale record. A letter moves `ISSUED → WITHDRAWN` with the supplied reason stored as `withdrawal_reason` and its own `version` incremented; `WITHDRAWN` is terminal. Then, hop by hop: (1) if that was the last letter in `ISSUED` on its analysis — the count of `ISSUED` letters for the analysis having dropped to zero — the analysis moves `ACCEPTED → REJECTED` with `decision_rationale` set to the exact text `AUTO: supporting opinion letter withdrawn` and its `version` incremented; (2) that rejection re-runs the clearance rollup on the asset, which no longer finds an `ACCEPTED` analysis and therefore recomputes the rights basis away from `FAIR_USE` — an asset with no covered legs at all moves `CLEARED → LAPSED` with `lapse_count` increased by one, while an asset that still holds part of its coverage lands on `PARTIALLY_CLEARED` and one that holds none and has never lapsed lands on `UNCLEARED`; (3) the production's `cleared_asset_count` and `open_asset_count` are restored to their pre-issue values and its posture is recomputed, which for a lapsed asset forces `BLOCKED`; (4) every delivery package of the production sitting in `DELIVERED` moves `DELIVERED → SUSPENDED` with its `suspension_reason` stamped; and (5) every E&O application of the production sitting in `BOUND` moves `BOUND → REMEDIATION_REQUIRED`. Every `PINNED` chart pinning the affected asset flips to `STALE` along the way, and the letter's, analysis's, asset's and production's `version` values all advance within the single request. Where another letter on the same analysis remains in `ISSUED`, hop 1 does not fire and none of the downstream hops occur at all.

### 3.6. Subject Releases, Life Rights and Negotiated Approvals

Identifiable people are the other half of clearance. A `ProductionAdmin` or `ClearanceSupervisor` papers a release against the production, choosing a `release_type` of `SUBJECT_RELEASE` for a straightforward appearance consent, `LIFE_RIGHTS` for a life-story grant, or `DEPICTION_AGREEMENT` for a negotiated portrayal. The mandatory `subject_person_key` is the stable key identifying the depicted person and is the conflict key described below. The depiction period is bounded by `depiction_from_date`, the first day of the depicted period, and `depiction_to_date`, the last day of it; both bounds are inclusive and the to-date must not precede the from-date. Two optional fields shape the deal: `asset_id` scopes the release to one specific shot or cue, and is left null when the release covers the film generally rather than a single asset — this polymorphic attachment is why the same resource can paper both a film-wide life-rights grant and a shot-specific consent. `approval_rights` grants the subject a negotiated approval over the cut, taking `NONE`, `ROUGH_CUT_APPROVAL` or `FINAL_APPROVAL`, and defaults to `NONE`. A new release lands in `DRAFT` with `revision_round_count` at zero and `cut_reference` null, and its `production_id` comes from the path. Where an `asset_id` is supplied, that asset must belong to the addressed production, failing which the request is refused with `ERR_ASSET_PRODUCTION_MISMATCH`, and on success the asset's `depiction_status` becomes `PENDING`.

**The depiction-window rule** prevents the same person's life from being papered twice under conflicting terms. Before a release is created, the requested window is widened by a quiet buffer of 30 days on each side — its from-date pushed 30 days earlier and its to-date pushed 30 days later — and the widened window is tested for intersection against the closed depiction window of every existing release on the same production carrying the same `subject_person_key` whose `status` is `SENT`, `REVISION_REQUESTED`, `SIGNED` or `APPROVAL_GRANTED`. Any intersection is refused with `ERR_DEPICTION_WINDOW_CONFLICT` (HTTP 409). Releases in `DRAFT`, `REFUSED` or `APPROVAL_WITHHELD` are not live and never block a new paper.

The release lifecycle is driven by a `ProductionAdmin` or a `ClearanceSupervisor`. `DRAFT → SENT` sends the paper out and has no preconditions. From `SENT` the release can come back four ways. `SENT → SIGNED` records consent. `SENT → REVISION_REQUESTED` records the subject's counsel asking for changes; it is permitted only while `revision_round_count` is below 3, and the fourth request is refused with `ERR_RELEASE_REVISION_LIMIT` (HTTP 409). Each accepted revision request raises `revision_round_count` by one, and `REVISION_REQUESTED → SENT` returns the revised paper to the subject, so negotiated approvals may bounce between the subject's counsel and the production for up to three revision rounds. `SENT → REFUSED` records outright refusal, and `REFUSED` is terminal.

The effect of signing on the linked asset depends on whether an approval was negotiated. Where the release's `approval_rights` is `NONE`, signing settles the depiction question immediately and the linked asset's dimension B moves `PENDING → OBTAINED`. Where the release granted `ROUGH_CUT_APPROVAL` or `FINAL_APPROVAL`, signing leaves the asset's `depiction_status` at `PENDING`, because the subject still holds a decision over the cut. A refusal instead moves the linked asset's dimension B `PENDING → BLOCKED`. In all three cases the production's posture is recomputed and the release's, asset's and production's `version` values increment; where `asset_id` is null the release moves through the same states but no asset dimension is touched.

Negotiated approvals are resolved from `SIGNED`. Both `SIGNED → APPROVAL_GRANTED` and `SIGNED → APPROVAL_WITHHELD` require `approval_rights` to be something other than `NONE` — a release granting no approval rights has no approval to give, and either request against such a release is refused with `ERR_NO_APPROVAL_RIGHTS` (HTTP 409). Both decisions store the `cut_reference` identifying the cut the decision was taken against, and both increment the release's `version`. There the symmetry ends. Granting approval sets the linked asset's `depiction_status` to `OBTAINED`. Withholding approval sets the linked asset's `depiction_status` to `BLOCKED`, moving dimension B `OBTAINED → BLOCKED` where the asset had already reached `OBTAINED`, recomputes the production's posture — where a single asset with `depiction_status` of `BLOCKED` forces the posture to `BLOCKED` outright — and flips every `PINNED` chart pinning that asset to `STALE`. `APPROVAL_WITHHELD` is terminal, so a withheld approval cannot be walked back on the same release; the remedy is a new paper or a re-cut.

### 3.7. Pinning the Clearance Chart and Placing the E&O Cover

Insurers do not underwrite a moving target. Before an errors-and-omissions carrier will look at a film, the production must hand over a **clearance chart**: a snapshot of every asset's rights position, frozen at a point in time and provably unchanged since. Pinning one is available to a `ClearanceSupervisor` or to `LegalCounsel`, and the request carries only two fields — a human `chart_label` such as "v3 pre-E&O", and the mandatory `expected_production_version` of §2.7. A production whose `version` has moved since the client read it is refused with `ERR_STALE_PRODUCTION_VERSION` (HTTP 412), and a production whose `posture` is `BLOCKED` is refused with `ERR_PRODUCTION_POSTURE_BLOCKED`; a `RESTRICTED` production may still pin.

The pin walks the production's assets in ascending `created_at` order and writes one object into `pinned_rows` per asset, recording that asset's `asset_id`, the `asset_version` current at pin time, and the asset's `rights_basis`, `licensing_status`, `depiction_status`, its `covered_legs` and the `license_ids` behind them. `pinned_row_count` is the length of that array. `pinned_opinion_letter_ids` collects the id of every opinion letter in `ISSUED` that backs a pinned row whose `rights_basis` is `FAIR_USE`, ordered by the letters' own `created_at` ascending — this is the list that later constrains who may review the application. `music_leg_gap_count` counts the pinned rows whose asset is of type `MUSIC` and whose covered legs do not include both `MASTER` and `PUBLISHING`; it is the machine-checkable statement "the cue sheet is incomplete". The new chart's `status` is `PINNED`, every earlier chart of the same production moves to `SUPERSEDED` whether it stood in `PINNED` or `STALE`, the production's posture is recomputed, and the production's `version` increments — which is why two clients cannot pin back-to-back charts without the second re-reading first.

With a chart pinned, a `ProductionAdmin` or `ClearanceSupervisor` opens the E&O application. The payload names the `clearance_chart_id` the underwriter and counsel will review, the requested per-claim `coverage_limit_cents`, and the self-insured `retention_cents`. The chart must belong to the addressed production, failing which the request is refused with `ERR_CHART_PRODUCTION_MISMATCH` (HTTP 422). The coverage figures must be commercially sane: `coverage_limit_cents` must be at least 100000 cents, and `retention_cents` must be zero or greater and strictly less than `coverage_limit_cents`; a violation of either is refused with `ERR_COVERAGE_LIMIT_INVALID` (HTTP 422). A new application lands in `DRAFT` with `legal_review_status` and `underwriting_review_status` both `PENDING`, `premium_cents` null, `policy_number` null, and `production_id` taken from the path.

Submission is where the policy is priced. A `ClearanceSupervisor` moves the application `DRAFT → SUBMITTED`; the referenced chart must still be `PINNED`, so a chart that has gone stale is refused with `ERR_CHART_STALE` and one that has been superseded with `ERR_CHART_SUPERSEDED`; the production's posture must not be `BLOCKED`, though `RESTRICTED` is acceptable here because insurance is exactly what a production with residual gaps needs; and `coverage_limit_cents` must exceed `retention_cents`, re-checked at submission time with `ERR_COVERAGE_LIMIT_INVALID`.

**Premium rating** then runs against the chart's pinned rows and stamps `premium_cents`. The base premium is the requested `coverage_limit_cents` multiplied by a base rate of 1.25 percent — that is, 0.0125 — and rounded half up to an integer number of cents. Next, the rated rows are walked in ascending asset `created_at` order, and only the rows whose `licensing_status` is `CLEARED` are rated at all; every other row is skipped. This is why assets created after the chart was pinned are never rated even though they exist on the production: rating reads the chart, not the live asset table. Each rated row contributes a risk weight chosen from its `rights_basis` — 0.010, one percent of the coverage limit, for `LICENSED`; 0.045, four and a half percent, for `FAIR_USE`; and 0.004, four tenths of one percent, for `PUBLIC_DOMAIN`. A depiction surcharge of a further 0.008 is added to that row's weight when the row's `depiction_status` is `OBTAINED` *and* there exists a subject release naming that row's asset whose `status` is `SIGNED` or `APPROVAL_GRANTED` and whose `approval_rights` is something other than `NONE`; a negotiated approval is an ongoing exposure, so it is rated. The row's loading is then the `coverage_limit_cents` multiplied by that row's final weight, rounded half up to an integer number of cents **at this point, per asset**, and the loading total is the sum of those already-rounded per-asset loadings. A retention credit is computed as `retention_cents` multiplied by 0.30 — a 30 percent credit for self-insurance — rounded half up once. Finally, `premium_cents` is the base premium plus the loading total minus the retention credit, floored at a minimum premium of 25000 cents: where the arithmetic yields less than 25000, the stored premium is 25000.

The per-asset rounding order is not a stylistic preference. Consider a `coverage_limit_cents` of 1000040 with two pinned rows, both `CLEARED` and both `LICENSED`, and a `retention_cents` of 0. The base premium is 1000040 multiplied by 0.0125, which is 12500.5 and rounds half up to 12501. Each asset's loading is 1000040 multiplied by 0.010, which is 10000.4 and rounds half up to 10000, giving a loading total of 20000. With no retention credit the premium is 32501. Accumulating the unrounded loadings instead — 10000.4 plus 10000.4, giving 20000.8, and rounding that total once — would yield 20001 and a premium of 32502, which is wrong.

Submission also **forks** the application into two independent review dimensions, both left at `PENDING`, and the application's `version` increments. The two branches are joined only at `CLEARED_TO_BIND`. Each branch is recorded by a separate call naming the `review_type` — `LEGAL` or `UNDERWRITING` — the `outcome`, which is either `PASS` or `FAIL`, and optional `notes`. Three checks guard the call, in this order. First, **segregation of duties**: the `X-User-ID` recording the legal review must differ from every `issued_by_user_id` among the opinion letters listed in the chart's `pinned_opinion_letter_ids`, because the lawyer whose opinion is being relied upon may not also sign off on relying upon it; and the operator who recorded one branch may not record the other. Either violation is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (HTTP 403). Second, the addressed dimension must still be `PENDING`; recording the same `review_type` twice is refused with `ERR_REVIEW_ALREADY_RECORDED` (HTTP 409). Third, the branch must match the acting role: `LEGAL` may only be recorded by `LegalCounsel` and `UNDERWRITING` only by a `ClearanceSupervisor`, and any other pairing is refused with `ERR_REVIEW_TYPE_ROLE_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403).

An accepted review sets its dimension to `PASSED` or `FAILED` according to the `outcome`, stamps the acting `X-User-ID` into `legal_reviewer_user_id` or `underwriting_reviewer_user_id`, and increments the application's `version`. The first review of either type also moves the application `SUBMITTED → IN_REVIEW`. From there the two join branches carry deliberately different side effects. When both `legal_review_status` and `underwriting_review_status` read `PASSED`, the application moves `IN_REVIEW → CLEARED_TO_BIND`: no premium adjustment occurs and the application remains bindable at the rated price. When either dimension reads `FAILED`, the application moves `IN_REVIEW → DECLINED` and `premium_cents` is reset to null — a declined application is not a priced one, and it forbids any delivery. The failure case has one shortcut worth stating plainly: when the very first review recorded on a `SUBMITTED` application is a `FAIL`, the single call carries the application through `SUBMITTED → IN_REVIEW → DECLINED`, and the observable end state is `DECLINED`. `DECLINED` is terminal.

Binding is a `ProductionAdmin` act. A request to bind from any status other than `CLEARED_TO_BIND` or `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED` is refused with `ERR_REVIEWS_INCOMPLETE` (HTTP 409) rather than the generic invalid-transition code, because the substantive complaint is always that both review branches have not yet passed. Binding from `CLEARED_TO_BIND` requires a `policy_number` in the body — its absence is `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` — and requires the referenced chart to still be `PINNED`, refusing a stale chart with `ERR_CHART_STALE`; the carrier's `policy_number` is stored and the application's `version` increments. Binding out of `REMEDIATION_REQUIRED` is the cycle back into cover after a lapse has been cured: it demands that the production's `posture` has returned to `OPEN`, refusing anything else with `ERR_PRODUCTION_POSTURE_BLOCKED`, and it deliberately retains the existing `premium_cents` and both review statuses rather than re-rating the policy.

### 3.8. Distributor Delivery, the Rights Sweep and Renewal

The last artefact the production owes its distributor is a delivery package: a cue sheet listing every music cue and a rights chart listing every asset, both drawn from a pinned clearance chart. A `ProductionAdmin` or `ClearanceSupervisor` assembles one by naming the `clearance_chart_id` the rows are drawn from, the `distributor_name` receiving them, and the `window_class` the distributor is buying, either `FESTIVAL_ONLY` or `ALL_MEDIA_WINDOW`. The chart must belong to the addressed production, failing which the answer is `ERR_CHART_PRODUCTION_MISMATCH`. From the chart the server derives `cue_sheet_row_count` as the number of pinned rows whose asset is of type `MUSIC`, `rights_chart_row_count` as the chart's total pinned row count across all asset types, and `music_leg_gap_count` copied straight from the chart. A new package lands in `DRAFT` with `suspension_reason` null and `production_id` taken from the path.

Validation is the compliance gate, and a `ClearanceSupervisor` drives `DRAFT → VALIDATED` through four checks in order. The production's `posture` must not be `BLOCKED` (`ERR_PRODUCTION_POSTURE_BLOCKED`) — a `RESTRICTED` production may validate a package even though it may not deliver one. The referenced chart must still be `PINNED`, so a stale chart yields `ERR_CHART_STALE` and a superseded chart yields `ERR_CHART_SUPERSEDED`. The chart's `music_leg_gap_count` must be zero, meaning every pinned `MUSIC` row carries both its `MASTER` and its `PUBLISHING` leg; a gap is refused with `ERR_CUE_SHEET_INCOMPLETE` (HTTP 409). And the window must be covered: when the package's `window_class` is `ALL_MEDIA_WINDOW`, every licence of the production whose `status` is `EXECUTED`, `ACTIVE` or `EXPIRING` must itself carry a `window_class` of `ALL_MEDIA_WINDOW`, because an all-media delivery may not rest on a festival-only licence; a shortfall is refused with `ERR_WINDOW_CLASS_INSUFFICIENT` (HTTP 409). A package buying only the `FESTIVAL_ONLY` window imposes no such requirement. On success the three row counts are re-stamped from the chart and the package's `version` increments.

Delivery is the irreversible commitment, and its gate is stricter than validation's on every axis. Moving `VALIDATED → DELIVERED` requires the production's `posture` to be exactly `OPEN`, refusing a merely restricted production with `ERR_PRODUCTION_POSTURE_RESTRICTED`; it requires the referenced chart to still be `PINNED`, refusing a stale one with `ERR_CHART_STALE`; and it requires the production to hold an E&O application in `BOUND`, refusing an uninsured delivery with `ERR_EO_NOT_BOUND`. A package may be abandoned from `DRAFT`, from `VALIDATED` or from `SUSPENDED` by moving it to `WITHDRAWN`, which has no preconditions and is terminal. The move `DELIVERED → SUSPENDED` is never requestable through the state endpoint: it is driven only by the system, either from the rights sweep or from the opinion-letter withdrawal cascade, and an operator request for it is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION`.

Which brings us to the sweep — the operation that makes expiring rights visible before they become breaches. A `ClearanceSupervisor` or `ProductionAdmin` runs the rights sweep against one production; it takes no request body, reads the date part of the logical clock, and considers only licences whose `production_id` equals the swept production. It first increases the production's `sweep_epoch` by one, then runs four steps in exactly the numbered order below, processing rows within each step in ascending `created_at` order and never by id.

**Step one — activation.** Every licence of the production in `EXECUTED` whose `term_start_date` falls on or before the clock's date moves `EXECUTED → ACTIVE`, incrementing its `version`. Because both `EXECUTED` and `ACTIVE` are inside the summed set of §2.9, this step moves no money.

**Step two — expiry.** Every licence in `ACTIVE` or `EXPIRING` whose `term_end_date` is on or before the clock's date — recall the half-open term of §2.11, so a clock date equal to `term_end_date` has already expired — moves to `EXPIRED`, the production's `committed_fee_cents` is debited by that licence's `executed_fee_cents`, and its `version` increments. This step re-reads the status set that step one has just produced, so a licence whose entire term has already elapsed can activate and expire inside a single sweep.

**Step three — expiry warning.** Every licence still in `ACTIVE` whose `term_end_date` lies between 0 and 60 whole days ahead of the clock's date, inclusive at both ends, moves `ACTIVE → EXPIRING` and increments its `version`. The boundary is exact: a licence exactly 60 whole days from its end date is flagged `EXPIRING`, and one 61 days out is not.

**Step four — cascade**, recomputed in this exact order. (a) The clearance rollup runs for every asset touched by steps one through three, which is where a licence's expiry becomes an asset's loss of coverage: an asset whose covered-leg set has emptied moves `CLEARED → LAPSED` with `lapse_count` raised by one, while a music asset that lost one leg while the other stayed live moves `CLEARED → PARTIALLY_CLEARED`, also raising `lapse_count` by one and resetting `rights_basis` to `UNDETERMINED`; both moves adjust `cleared_asset_count` down and `open_asset_count` up and flip pinning charts to `STALE`. (b) The production's posture is recomputed. (c) If the recomputed posture is `BLOCKED`, every delivery package of the production sitting in `DELIVERED` moves `DELIVERED → SUSPENDED`, in ascending `created_at` order, each stamping `suspension_reason` with the text `AUTO: production posture BLOCKED at sweep epoch <n>` where the placeholder is the sweep epoch just reached. (d) For every package just suspended, every E&O application of the production sitting in `BOUND` moves `BOUND → REMEDIATION_REQUIRED`. (e) If the production's `status` is `LOCKED` and the recomputed posture is anything other than `OPEN`, the production moves `LOCKED → CLEARANCE`, incrementing its own `version` — a locked film that has lost coverage is pushed back into clearance work rather than being allowed to ship.

The sweep answers with a report of exactly what it did: `sweep_epoch` for the epoch just reached, `activated_license_ids`, `expired_license_ids` and `expiring_license_ids` for the three licence steps, `lapsed_asset_ids` for the assets that lost coverage, `suspended_delivery_package_ids` and `remediated_eo_application_ids` for hops (c) and (d), plus the resulting `production_posture` and `committed_fee_cents`. Apart from `sweep_epoch`, which advances on every call, a sweep is idempotent at a fixed clock instant: running it twice without moving the clock produces no further state change and empty id lists on the second run.

The whole point of flagging a licence `EXPIRING` is to give the clearance department time to act, and the action available is **renewal** — a `ClearanceSupervisor`'s exclusive right. The request carries a single field, `additional_term_months`. The checks run in the global order: the licence must be in `ACTIVE` or `EXPIRING`, and any other status is refused with `ERR_LICENSE_NOT_QUOTED`; the archive must still have exactly one rate card in `ACTIVE` effective on or before the term start, failing which the answer is `ERR_NO_ACTIVE_RATE_CARD`; the extended term must not overlap a live sibling licence under the rule of §3.3, failing which the answer is `ERR_LICENSE_TERM_OVERLAP`; the renewal window must be open, meaning the whole-day distance from the clock's date to `term_end_date` is between 0 and 60 inclusive, and a licence more than 60 whole days from its end date is refused with `ERR_OUTSIDE_RENEWAL_WINDOW` (HTTP 409); and `additional_term_months` must be at least 1, with zero, a negative value or an absent field refused as `ERR_INVALID_RENEWAL_TERM` (HTTP 422).

A successful renewal extends `term_months` by `additional_term_months` and advances `term_end_date` by that many calendar months. Rate-card pricing is then re-run against the *extended* `term_months`, which typically lifts the term factor into another term year, and the resulting figure replaces `rate_card_fee_cents`. The renewal delta, `renewal_delta_cents`, is the new `rate_card_fee_cents` less the old one, floored at zero so that a renewal never refunds: that delta is added to the licence's `executed_fee_cents` and to the production's `committed_fee_cents`, keeping the money ledger of §2.9 exact. A licence renewed out of `EXPIRING` moves `EXPIRING → ACTIVE`, back into the live band, and may cycle through `ACTIVE → EXPIRING → ACTIVE` as many times as the department keeps renewing it. The licence's and the production's `version` values each increment, and because the asset's coverage set is unchanged the asset's clearance status is simply reconfirmed.
