# Municipal Bond Underwriting API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

Bringing a municipal bond issue to market is not a single transaction but a choreography among six professional parties who are legally forbidden from occupying each other's seats. A governmental issuer authorises a par amount; an independent municipal advisor structures the maturity schedule and drives the disclosure document; a broker-dealer syndicate buys the bonds and resells them; bond counsel drafts the official statement and the tax opinion; rating agency analysts run a credit review; and a compliance function reconciles the arbitrage yield and hands the issue to post-issuance monitoring. Every one of those handoffs is governed by an MSRB or SEC rule that the current, spreadsheet-driven process enforces only by convention — which is exactly why late continuing-disclosure filings, misdisclosed underwriter compensation, and municipal-advisor conflicts remain the most frequently cited deficiencies in the market.

This document specifies the Municipal Bond Underwriting API, a system of record that makes those rules structural rather than advisory. All endpoints are mounted under the `/bond-offerings` prefix. The API models the transaction as a deal that moves through structuring, a sale-method fork into either a competitive or a negotiated pricing branch, pricing, award, and closing, with the official statement, the continuing disclosure undertaking, the rating engagements, the credit enhancement, the syndicate, and the order book all hanging off that spine as independently governed sub-processes. Money is conserved: the aggregate reoffering proceeds of an issue always decompose exactly into net proceeds to the issuer, disclosed underwriter compensation, and any credit enhancement premium. Par is conserved: every dollar of a maturity's principal is either allotted to an order or retained unsold by the syndicate, before and after any compensating action.

Two design commitments shape almost every rule that follows. First, **segregation of duties is enforced by identity, not by policy documentation**: the firm engaged as a deal's municipal advisor may never sit on that deal's underwriting syndicate (MSRB Rule G-23), and the bond counsel user who authored a section of the official statement may never be the user who approves it (MSRB Rule G-17 fair dealing). Second, **disclosure adequacy gates the money**: a deal cannot be priced until its preliminary official statement is posted with every mandatory disclosure section approved, and a revision request anywhere in the drafting loop propagates outward to block pricing clearance and to reopen the rating agencies' credit review. Implementers should expect cascades to be the norm rather than the exception, and should expect each cascade to be specified hop by hop.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the rules that hold across every endpoint: identity, envelope shape, pagination, precision, validation ordering, concurrency guarding, and the invariants that cascades must preserve.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which it acts. If either header is absent or blank, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (HTTP 401). If both headers are present but the declared role is not among the roles authorised for the target endpoint in section 4, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED` (HTTP 403).

The system recognises exactly six roles: `IssuerOfficial`, `MunicipalAdvisor`, `Underwriter`, `BondCounsel`, `RatingAnalyst`, and `ComplianceOfficer`. An `IssuerOfficial` is the finance officer of the governmental issuer; it owns the decisions to insure, to award, and to cancel, and it signs the tax certificate and the continuing disclosure agreement. A `MunicipalAdvisor` is the independent registered advisor to the issuer; it structures the maturity schedule, drives the official statement review, and posts both statements. An `Underwriter` is the broker-dealer that buys and resells the bonds; it runs the order book, prices the issue, and discloses its own compensation. A `BondCounsel` user drafts the official statement, the undertaking, and the bond opinion. A `RatingAnalyst` is a rating agency analyst. A `ComplianceOfficer` registers firms, waives non-opinion closing documents, reopens a closed order book, runs the arbitrage analysis, performs the post-issuance handoff, and closes the deal.

Three of the six roles are **entity-backed**, meaning `X-User-ID` must resolve to a persisted row before any business rule is evaluated. When `X-User-Role` is `IssuerOfficial`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Issuer`; ownership checks on any resource that references an `Issuer` compare the resource's foreign-key field to `X-User-ID`. When `X-User-Role` is `MunicipalAdvisor`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `Firm`, and that firm's `firm_type` must be either `MUNICIPAL_ADVISOR` or `DUAL_REGISTERED`. When `X-User-Role` is `Underwriter`, `X-User-ID` is likewise the `id` of the acting `Firm`, and that firm's `firm_type` must be either `UNDERWRITER` or `DUAL_REGISTERED`. In all three cases, a `X-User-ID` that names no such row, or that names a row whose `firm_type` does not admit the declared role, is rejected with `ERR_ROLE_IDENTITY_MISMATCH` (HTTP 403).

The remaining three roles are **opaque**. When `X-User-Role` is `BondCounsel`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier not tied to any persisted entity; the server records it (as `authored_by_user_id` when a section is created and as `approved_by_user_id` when a section is approved) but never compares it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `RatingAnalyst`, `X-User-ID` is likewise an opaque operator identifier; the server records it as `assigned_analyst_user_id` when an engagement is acknowledged and afterwards compares subsequent requests on that engagement against the recorded value, but it does not resolve it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `ComplianceOfficer`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that the server neither persists nor compares to any entity's `id`.

Ownership of deal-scoped work is checked separately from role membership and also reports `ERR_ROLE_IDENTITY_MISMATCH`. An `IssuerOfficial` may act only on a deal whose `issuer_id` equals `X-User-ID`. A `MunicipalAdvisor` may act only on a deal whose `municipal_advisor_firm_id` equals `X-User-ID`. An `Underwriter` may act only on a deal on which the firm named by `X-User-ID` holds a `SyndicateMember` row with `status` `ACTIVE`, with two carve-outs: on a deal whose `sale_method` is `COMPETITIVE`, any firm that is not suspended and whose `firm_type` admits the `Underwriter` role may act, because a competitive sale has no pre-sale syndicate; and when adding a syndicate member, an as-yet unseated `Underwriter` may add its own firm, that is, a request whose `firm_id` equals `X-User-ID`.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_`.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Ordering

Every endpoint that returns a collection accepts two optional query parameters. `limit` bounds the number of rows returned; it defaults to 10 when omitted, may not be smaller than 1, and may not be larger than 100. `offset` is the number of rows to skip before the returned window begins; it defaults to 0 and may not be negative. A `limit` outside the inclusive range 1 through 100, a non-integer `limit`, a negative `offset`, or a non-integer `offset` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION` (HTTP 422).

List responses populate `limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, and `has_more` in the `meta` object of the envelope. `total_count` reports the number of rows matching the request's filters before the pagination window is applied, and `has_more` is true exactly when rows matching the filters remain beyond the returned window.

Unless a specific collection states otherwise, list endpoints sort by `created_at` ascending. Four collections deviate, and the deviation is part of their contract: a deal's maturity schedule is returned in ascending `sequence_number`; a deal's syndicate seats are returned in ascending `priority_rank`; the orders of an order period are returned in ascending `submitted_at`; and a deal's official statements are returned newest first, that is, in descending `created_at`.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties, Identifiers and the Logical Clock

Every persisted resource in this system — issuers, firms, deals, maturity tranches, credit enhancements, official statements, official statement sections, continuing disclosure undertakings, rating engagements, rating presentations, syndicate members, order periods, orders, and closing documents — carries the same four server-managed properties. `id` is a server-minted UUID with no type prefix. `created_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the row was first written. `updated_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording the most recent mutation of the row. `version` is an integer that starts at 1 when the row is created and increases by exactly 1 on every mutation of that row, including mutations that only recompute a derived field. No entity in this system omits `updated_at` or `version`.

Because a single request routinely mutates several rows, the rule is worth stating plainly: whenever a cascade recomputes a field on a parent or a sibling, that row's own `updated_at` is refreshed and its own `version` is incremented. A request that touches a deal, four of its tranches, and two of its syndicate members advances seven independent version counters.

Time in this system is a **logical clock**, never the host's wall clock. At startup the clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it changes only when a client sets it explicitly by supplying a `current_utc` instant to the clock capability described in section 3. Every system-assigned timestamp — an order's `submitted_at`, a statement's `posted_preliminary_at` and `posted_final_at`, a closing document's `executed_at` — is read from this clock, and every window test on an order period reads it too. The clock capability responds with the new `current_utc` together with `held_presentation_ids`, the identifiers of the rating presentations that the advance promoted.

### 2.5. Validation Order and the First-Violation Rule

Requests are validated in a fixed sixteen-tier ladder, and **only the first violated tier is reported**. When two conditions in a single request are both unsatisfied, the response always carries the error of the lower-numbered tier; a caller cannot observe the later failure until the earlier one is fixed. Within a single tier, rules are mutually exclusive in practice, so the ordering among them is immaterial. The ladder is:

1. **Authentication.** `X-User-ID` and `X-User-Role` are both present and non-blank, on every endpoint without exception. Otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED`.
2. **Role authorisation.** The declared role appears in the endpoint's authorised-role list, on every authenticated endpoint. Otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHORIZED`.
3. **Identity resolution.** On every endpoint whose authorised-role list contains an entity-backed role, `X-User-ID` resolves to an existing `Issuer` (for `IssuerOfficial`) or to an existing `Firm` whose `firm_type` admits the declared role (for `MunicipalAdvisor` and `Underwriter`). Otherwise `ERR_ROLE_IDENTITY_MISMATCH`.
4. **Path existence.** On every endpoint with a path parameter, the identifier in the path names an existing row of the expected entity. Otherwise the entity's own not-found error, all at HTTP 404: `ERR_ISSUER_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_FIRM_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_DEAL_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_TRANCHE_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_OFFICIAL_STATEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_SECTION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_UNDERTAKING_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ENHANCEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_RATING_ENGAGEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_PRESENTATION_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ORDER_PERIOD_NOT_FOUND`, `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, or `ERR_CLOSING_DOCUMENT_NOT_FOUND`.
5. **Request shape.** On every endpoint that accepts a body: every required field is present and non-null, every field matches its declared type, every enum field carries a listed member, every string satisfies its length bounds, every number satisfies its range bounds, every datetime is a valid ISO 8601 UTC instant, and no unknown top-level field is present. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (HTTP 422).
6. **Pagination shape.** On every list endpoint, `limit` is an integer between 1 and 100 inclusive and `offset` is a non-negative integer. Otherwise `ERR_INVALID_PAGINATION`.
7. **Body reference existence.** Every foreign key supplied in a body names an existing row — `issuer_id`, `municipal_advisor_firm_id`, `firm_id`, `maturity_tranche_id`, `syndicate_member_id`, `refunded_tranche_id`, `parent_section_id`, and every member of `insured_tranche_ids`. The reported error is the referenced entity's not-found error from tier 4. This tier guards six operations: opening a deal, adding a maturity tranche, proposing a credit enhancement, adding a syndicate member, entering an order, and adding an official statement section.
8. **Actor ownership.** The deal-scoped ownership rules of section 2.1 hold, on every mutating deal-scoped operation. Otherwise `ERR_ROLE_IDENTITY_MISMATCH`. A resource addressed by its own identifier rather than through its deal is still deal-scoped work: scheduling, opening and closing an order period, and entering and cancelling an order, are each checked against the deal that owns the order period, so an `Underwriter` holding no `ACTIVE` `SyndicateMember` seat on a deal whose `sale_method` is not `COMPETITIVE` is refused at this tier before any state or window guard of section 3.7 is reached.
9. **Single-deal referential integrity.** Entities referenced together in one request belong to the same deal. Otherwise `ERR_CROSS_DEAL_REFERENCE` (HTTP 422). Concretely: an order's `maturity_tranche_id` and `syndicate_member_id` must both belong to the deal that owns the order period; every member of `insured_tranche_ids` must belong to the enhancement's own deal; a section's `parent_section_id` must belong to the same official statement as the section being created; and a tranche's `refunded_tranche_id` must belong to a *different* deal of the *same* issuer, because a refunding maturity never refunds a maturity of its own series. This tier guards four operations: entering an order, proposing a credit enhancement, adding a maturity tranche, and adding an official statement section.
10. **Optimistic concurrency.** The supplied expected-version token equals the guarded parent's current `version`, on the two version-guarded operations described in section 2.7. Otherwise `ERR_STALE_DEAL_VERSION` or `ERR_STALE_ORDER_PERIOD_VERSION`, both HTTP 412.
11. **State legality.** The targeted entity's current state admits the requested transition, on every action operation and on every child-creation operation whose parent must be in a permitting state. The reported error names whichever entity is being transitioned, all at HTTP 409: `ERR_DEAL_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_OS_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_SECTION_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_UNDERTAKING_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_ENHANCEMENT_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_RATING_ENGAGEMENT_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_PRESENTATION_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_ORDER_PERIOD_INVALID_STATE`, `ERR_ORDER_INVALID_STATE`, or `ERR_CLOSING_DOC_ALREADY_EXECUTED`.
12. **MSRB conduct.** The seven segregation-of-duties and standing rules of section 2.8, all reporting at HTTP 422: `ERR_MA_UNDERWRITER_CONFLICT`, `ERR_FIRM_SUSPENDED`, `ERR_MA_ENGAGEMENT_REQUIRED`, `ERR_SENIOR_MANAGER_REQUIRED`, `ERR_SALE_METHOD_CONFLICT`, `ERR_SECTION_SELF_APPROVAL`, and `ERR_SIGNATORY_ROLE_INVALID`.
13. **Disclosure adequacy.** The eight disclosure and readiness gates of sections 3.4, 3.5, and 3.8, all at HTTP 422: `ERR_MANDATORY_SECTION_NOT_APPROVED`, `ERR_DISCLOSURE_RISK_SECTION_REQUIRED`, `ERR_UNDERTAKING_NOT_ACTIVE`, `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, `ERR_OS_NOT_FINAL_POSTED`, `ERR_PRESENTATION_REQUIRED`, `ERR_DISCLOSURE_NOT_CLEARED`, and `ERR_PRICING_PRECONDITION_UNMET`.
14. **Temporal rules.** The four calendar and window rules, all at HTTP 422: `ERR_ORDER_PERIOD_OVERLAP`, `ERR_PRESENTATION_BUFFER_CONFLICT`, `ERR_ORDER_PERIOD_WINDOW_CLOSED`, and `ERR_MATURITY_DATE_UNALIGNED`.
15. **Structural constraints.** The nine shape-of-the-deal rules, all at HTTP 422: `ERR_CALL_FEATURE_INVALID`, `ERR_DENOMINATION_INVALID`, `ERR_PAR_SCHEDULE_MISMATCH`, `ERR_TRANCHE_SEQUENCE_CONFLICT`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_SYNDICATE_MEMBER`, `ERR_DUPLICATE_RATING_ENGAGEMENT`, `ERR_REFUNDED_TRANCHE_INVALID`, `ERR_ORDER_TYPE_NOT_PERMITTED`, and the natural-key rules reporting `ERR_DUPLICATE_MSRB_REGISTRATION` on firm registration and `ERR_DUPLICATE_SERIES_DESIGNATION` on deal creation.
16. **Fiscal rules.** The six money rules, all at HTTP 422: `ERR_LIABILITY_PCT_SUM_INVALID`, `ERR_COMPENSATION_INPUT_MISSING`, `ERR_PREMIUM_EXCEEDS_PROCEEDS`, `ERR_ENHANCEMENT_ALREADY_COMMITTED`, `ERR_CLOSING_DOCS_INCOMPLETE`, and `ERR_POST_ISSUANCE_HANDOFF_INCOMPLETE`.

The practical consequence of this ladder deserves emphasis. A pricing request submitted by an `Underwriter` whose firm has been suspended *and* whose deal has an order period still open fails with `ERR_FIRM_SUSPENDED`, because MSRB conduct at tier 12 precedes disclosure adequacy at tier 13. A request to add a syndicate member that is simultaneously the deal's municipal advisor *and* a duplicate seat fails with `ERR_MA_UNDERWRITER_CONFLICT`, because tier 12 precedes the structural constraints at tier 15. And a stale expected-version token surfaces before any state check: creating an official statement against a deal whose `status` is `CLOSED` with a stale token reports `ERR_STALE_DEAL_VERSION`, not `ERR_DEAL_INVALID_STATE`.

### 2.6. Precision, Rounding and Denomination Conventions

Every rounding in this system is **round half up**, and the number of decimal places depends on what is being measured. Prices expressed as a percent of par — `price_to_maturity_pct`, `price_to_call_pct`, and `reoffering_price_pct` — carry three decimal places. So does `gross_spread_per_bond_usd`, which is a per-bond dollar figure quoted to the same precision the market quotes prices. Dollar amounts — `issue_price_total_usd`, `gross_spread_total_usd`, `credit_enhancement_premium_usd`, `net_proceeds_to_issuer_usd`, `insured_debt_service_usd`, `premium_usd`, `liability_par_usd`, and `takedown_credit_usd` — carry two decimal places. Yields used in the arbitrage test — `arbitrage_yield_pct` and the derived permitted yield — carry four decimal places. The disclosure risk score is pure integer arithmetic and no rounding is possible on it. Par amounts are whole dollars.

Where a total is built from parts, **the order of rounding is pinned and is part of the contract**. Every aggregation in this document rounds each component first and then sums the rounded components; it never sums exact components and rounds once at the end. The two orders differ whenever a per-unit input carries more decimals than the output precision, and the per-component order is always the correct one here.

Two denomination conventions run through the whole system. A **bond** is USD 1,000 of par, so any figure quoted "per bond" is a figure per USD 1,000 of par, and the number of bonds in a maturity is its principal divided by one thousand. The **minimum denomination** in which par may be requested or allotted is USD 5,000: a maturity's `principal_amount_usd` and an order's `par_requested_usd` must each be a positive whole multiple of five thousand dollars, as must a deal's `expected_par_amount_usd`, and every allotted amount the waterfall produces is likewise an exact multiple of five thousand. A violation on a tranche principal or an order's requested par is reported as `ERR_DENOMINATION_INVALID`.

The bond calendar is semiannual: there are two coupon periods in a year, coupons are paid semiannually, and every date the system cares about is expressed as a whole number of semiannual periods from the deal's `dated_date`.

### 2.7. Version Guarding on Two Otherwise-Unconditional Writes

Most operations in this system are guarded by a state machine: if the target is in the wrong state, the request is refused. Two writes have no such natural guard, and both are therefore protected by an explicit optimistic-concurrency token that the client must have read from the parent resource.

Starting a new official statement on a deal requires `expected_deal_version` as a mandatory body field carrying the `version` the client last observed on that `Deal`. If it does not equal the deal's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_DEAL_VERSION` (HTTP 412). The reason this guard exists is substantive rather than defensive: creating an official statement legitimately supersedes the deal's prior non-terminal statement, so a writer holding a stale view of the deal would silently discard a drafting round it never saw. The stale-version check turns that silent discard into an explicit rejection.

Entering an order into an order period requires `expected_order_period_version` as a mandatory body field carrying the `version` the client last observed on that `OrderPeriod`. If it does not equal the period's current `version`, the request is rejected with `ERR_STALE_ORDER_PERIOD_VERSION` (HTTP 412). Order entry is legitimately repeatable, so nothing in the order period's own state machine would refuse the write; the stale version is the only available signal that the period was closed, reopened with its allotments voided, or otherwise re-shaped since the client last looked. Note that each accepted order itself increments the order period's `version`, so a client entering a run of orders must re-read the period's `version` between writes.

No other endpoint accepts a version field. Requests to any other endpoint that carry one are rejected as unknown top-level fields under tier 5 of the validation ladder.

### 2.8. Segregation of Duties and Firm Standing

MSRB Rule G-23 forbids a firm from advising an issuer and underwriting the same issuer's bonds. The system enforces the prohibition symmetrically in both directions: a firm named as a deal's `municipal_advisor_firm_id` may not be added as a `SyndicateMember` of that deal in any `member_role`, and a deal may not be opened naming as its municipal advisor a firm that already holds a `SyndicateMember` row on that deal. Either direction is rejected with `ERR_MA_UNDERWRITER_CONFLICT`.

A firm whose `is_suspended` is true is barred from new business entirely. It may not be engaged as a deal's municipal advisor when the deal is opened, it may not be added to a syndicate, it may not be the advisor of record when a sale method is designated, and it may not be the acting underwriter on a pricing request. All four rejections carry `ERR_FIRM_SUSPENDED`.

MSRB Rule G-17 fair dealing requires that disclosure be reviewed by someone other than its author. When a section of an official statement is approved, the approving `X-User-ID` must differ from the `authored_by_user_id` recorded on that section when it was created. A user attempting to approve their own prose is rejected with `ERR_SECTION_SELF_APPROVAL`.

Closing documents carry a `signatory_role` naming the single role entitled to execute them. On execution, `X-User-Role` must equal that `signatory_role`; a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_SIGNATORY_ROLE_INVALID`. The same error also covers the one document class that may never be waived: a `BOND_OPINION` and a `TAX_CERTIFICATE` must be executed and can never be discharged by waiver.

### 2.9. Conserved Identities and Cascade Discipline

Two arithmetic identities must hold after every write that touches any of their terms, and they are the fastest way to detect a partially applied cascade.

The **money identity** states that a priced deal's aggregate reoffering proceeds decompose exactly: `issue_price_total_usd` equals `net_proceeds_to_issuer_usd` plus `gross_spread_total_usd` plus `credit_enhancement_premium_usd`. Committing a credit enhancement, withdrawing one, and pricing the deal all disturb terms of this identity, and each must leave it exactly satisfied, with no drift attributable to rounding order.

The **par identity** states that for every maturity tranche, `par_allotted_usd` plus `par_unsold_usd` equals `principal_amount_usd`, and that `Deal.par_allotted_total_usd` equals the sum of `par_allotted_usd` across the deal's tranches. Running an allotment, reopening a closed order period, cancelling an order, and cancelling the deal all disturb these terms; each must restore the identity exactly, with zero drift.

Cascades in this system are **immediately consistent and hop-ordered**. Where a transition's effects are described as hops, each hop is caused by the hop before it, and every hop is applied inside the same request that triggered the first one. A client never observes an intermediate state in which hop two has been applied but hop four has not. Derived fields — `Deal.disclosure_clearance`, `OfficialStatement.mandatory_sections_approved`, `MaturityTranche.par_allotted_usd`, `MaturityTranche.par_unsold_usd`, `Deal.par_allotted_total_usd`, `SyndicateMember.liability_par_usd`, `SyndicateMember.takedown_credit_usd`, and `OrderPeriod.orders_received_count`, `OrderPeriod.allotted_order_count` — are recomputed from their sources rather than incrementally adjusted, so a recomputation always agrees with a full rebuild from the underlying rows.

`Deal.disclosure_clearance` deserves its own statement because so many gates read it. It is recomputed on every mutation of an official statement or of any of its sections, and it takes one of three values. It is `CLEARED` while the deal's governing official statement has `mandatory_sections_approved` true and a `status` among `IN_REVIEW`, `PRELIMINARY_POSTED`, and `FINAL_POSTED`. It is `BLOCKED` while that statement has `mandatory_sections_approved` false and a `status` of either `REVISION_REQUESTED` or `WITHDRAWN`. In every other combination it is `PENDING`. The governing official statement of a deal is its most recently created one; earlier non-terminal statements are superseded when a new one is created.

### 2.10. Nested Resource Retrieval

Several paths address a child resource through its parent, and several address a child directly by its own identifier. Where a path carries more than one identifier, identifiers are resolved strictly left to right, and the first identifier that names no existing row determines the 404 that is returned — the parent's not-found error always wins over the child's. A request for the sections of a non-existent official statement therefore reports `ERR_OFFICIAL_STATEMENT_NOT_FOUND` and never `ERR_SECTION_NOT_FOUND`. The same precedence governs creation: when a child is created under a parent, the parent's existence is established before any field of the child is examined, and before any business rule of the child is evaluated.

Path nesting in this system is never deeper than one level. Sections are reached either through their official statement or directly; orders are reached either through their order period or directly; tranches, enhancements, undertakings, engagements, presentations, order periods, and closing documents are all reached through their deal or engagement for creation and listing, and directly by identifier for reading and for action. Closing documents are the one entity with no creation endpoint at all: they exist only as a side effect of award, so listing them under their deal is their only discovery channel.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Participants: Issuers, Firms and Continuing-Disclosure Risk

Before any deal exists, the two classes of participant must be on file. Registering a governmental issuer is open to an `IssuerOfficial`, a `MunicipalAdvisor`, or a `ComplianceOfficer`, and takes the issuer's legal `name` (one to one hundred sixty characters, not blank), its two-letter `state_code` (exactly two uppercase letters), and its `entity_type`, which is one of `CITY`, `COUNTY`, `SCHOOL_DISTRICT`, `AUTHORITY`, or `STATE`. Four further inputs describe the issuer's compliance history and are all optional. `prior_late_annual_filings` counts annual financial reports filed after the undertaking deadline in the last five years; it is an integer from 0 through 20 and defaults to 0. `prior_late_event_notices` counts Rule 15c2-12 material event notices filed after their deadline in the same window, likewise an integer from 0 through 20 defaulting to 0. `has_prior_material_event` records whether a reportable material event such as a default or a draw on a reserve occurred in that window, defaulting to false. `is_bank_qualified_eligible` records whether the issuer expects to stay inside the ten-million-dollar bank-qualified limit for the year, defaulting to false; it is carried for disclosure purposes and is not consumed by any gate. Issuers can be listed with an optional `state_code` filter and an optional `entity_type` filter, and read individually.

At registration the server scores the issuer's continuing-disclosure risk once and stores the result in `disclosure_risk_score`, an integer confined to the range 0 through 100. The score starts from a raw total assembled from four contributions. Each late annual filing contributes twelve points, so `prior_late_annual_filings` is multiplied by twelve. Each late event notice contributes eight points, so `prior_late_event_notices` is multiplied by eight. A prior material event contributes a flat twenty-five points when `has_prior_material_event` is true and nothing when it is false. Finally the issuer's governmental character contributes a surcharge determined by `entity_type`: a `STATE` adds nothing, a `CITY` or a `COUNTY` adds five, a `SCHOOL_DISTRICT` adds ten, and an `AUTHORITY` adds fifteen. Those four contributions are added together, and the resulting raw total is then clamped into the permitted range: anything below zero becomes zero and anything above one hundred becomes one hundred. All of this is integer arithmetic, so no rounding arises. An `AUTHORITY` with three late event notices and no material event therefore scores thirty-nine — fifteen from its character plus twenty-four from the notices — while an `AUTHORITY` with a material event and a clean filing record scores exactly forty.

Forty is the threshold that matters. A score **at or above forty** puts the issuer inside the high-risk gate, and a deal for that issuer cannot post a preliminary official statement until an approved material continuing-disclosure risk factor is in the document; a score of thirty-nine does not. The boundary is inclusive on the gate side, and the score is never recomputed after registration, so the risk posture of a deal is fixed by the issuer record it was opened against.

Registering a firm is reserved to a `ComplianceOfficer` and captures the firm's registered `name` (one to one hundred sixty characters, not blank), its `firm_type` — one of `UNDERWRITER`, `MUNICIPAL_ADVISOR`, or `DUAL_REGISTERED` — and its `msrb_registration_id`, exactly six uppercase alphanumeric characters that must be unique across every firm in the system. A collision on that identifier is rejected with `ERR_DUPLICATE_MSRB_REGISTRATION`. The optional `is_suspended` flag defaults to false; a suspended firm remains readable and listable but is barred from every new engagement as described in section 2.8. Firms can be listed with optional `firm_type` and `is_suspended` filters and read individually, and a reverse lookup returns every syndicate seat a given firm holds across all deals, narrowable by seat `status`.

### 3.2. Structuring the Series: the Maturity Schedule and Yield-to-Worst Pricing

A deal is opened by an `IssuerOfficial` or a `MunicipalAdvisor` and represents one series of bonds. Creation takes the `issuer_id` of the governmental issuer, a `series_designation` such as `Series 2026A` (one to forty characters, not blank, and unique among the deals of that same issuer — a collision reports `ERR_DUPLICATE_SERIES_DESIGNATION`), a `tax_status` of `TAX_EXEMPT`, `TAXABLE`, or `AMT`, a `dated_date` given as an ISO 8601 UTC instant with three fractional digits, and an `expected_par_amount_usd` that must be strictly positive and a whole multiple of five thousand dollars. Two inputs are optional at this point: `municipal_advisor_firm_id`, which engages an independent advisor and defaults to null when no advisor is engaged, and `record_retention_years`, which pre-populates the record-retention commitment and otherwise defaults to null.

Everything else on a freshly created deal is server-assigned. Its `status` is `DRAFT`, its `sale_method` and its `gross_spread_basis` are both `UNDETERMINED`, its `disclosure_clearance` is `PENDING`, its `post_issuance_handoff_status` is `NOT_STARTED`, and its `version` is 1. Three counters start at zero: `par_amount_usd`, `credit_enhancement_premium_usd`, and `par_allotted_total_usd`. Both branch flags, `requires_g17_letter` and `requires_notice_of_sale`, start false. Every field awaiting the pricing event starts null: `takedown_per_bond_usd`, `management_fee_per_bond_usd`, `expenses_per_bond_usd`, `winning_bid_price_pct`, `issue_price_total_usd`, `gross_spread_total_usd`, `gross_spread_per_bond_usd`, `net_proceeds_to_issuer_usd`, and `arbitrage_yield_pct`. `cancellation_reason` starts null as well. Deals can be listed with optional `issuer_id`, `municipal_advisor_firm_id`, `status`, and `sale_method` filters, and read individually — the individual read is where a client obtains the deal's `version` before starting an official statement.

Only a `MunicipalAdvisor` may add maturities. Each maturity carries a `sequence_number` from 1 through 40 that must be unique within the deal, and the schedule must be monotone: `maturity_date` has to increase strictly as `sequence_number` increases. A reused sequence number or an out-of-order maturity date is rejected with `ERR_TRANCHE_SEQUENCE_CONFLICT`. The `maturity_date` itself must be strictly after the deal's `dated_date` and must land exactly on a semiannual anniversary of it — that is, a whole number of six-month periods after the dated date, never a fraction of one; a date that misses the anniversary is rejected with `ERR_MATURITY_DATE_UNALIGNED`. The `principal_amount_usd` maturing on that date must be strictly positive and a whole multiple of the five-thousand-dollar minimum denomination. `coupon_rate_pct` is the annual coupon in percent, paid semiannually, and lies between 0 and 12 inclusive with at most three decimal places. `yield_pct` is the reoffering yield in percent, strictly greater than 0 and at most 12, likewise with at most three decimal places.

Call features are optional and must be internally coherent. `is_callable` defaults to false. When it is true, both `first_call_date` and `call_price_pct` become required, `first_call_date` must land on a semiannual anniversary of the deal's `dated_date` and must fall strictly before `maturity_date`, and `call_price_pct` must lie between 100.000 and 103.000 inclusive. When `is_callable` is false, both `first_call_date` and `call_price_pct` must be absent, and both remain null on the stored row. Any breach of that symmetry — a missing call field on a callable maturity, a stray call field on a non-callable one, a call date not before maturity, or a call price outside the permitted band — is rejected with `ERR_CALL_FEATURE_INVALID`, while a misaligned `first_call_date` is reported as `ERR_MATURITY_DATE_UNALIGNED` under the earlier temporal tier.

An advance-refunding maturity may name the prior-issue maturity it refunds through the optional `refunded_tranche_id`, which defaults to null for new-money maturities. The referenced maturity must belong to a deal whose `status` is `CLOSED`, must not already be named as the `refunded_tranche_id` of some other maturity, and must not be the maturity itself; any of those three failures reports `ERR_REFUNDED_TRANCHE_INVALID`. Separately, and at the earlier integrity tier, the referenced maturity must sit on a *different* deal belonging to the *same* issuer, since a series never refunds itself; a reference that crosses issuers or stays inside the same deal reports `ERR_CROSS_DEAL_REFERENCE`.

Adding a maturity prices it immediately, on a semiannual actuarial basis, per one hundred dollars of principal, under the **yield-to-worst** convention. Two intermediate period counts are stored: `periods_to_maturity` is the whole number of semiannual periods from the deal's `dated_date` to the maturity's `maturity_date`, and `periods_to_call` is the same count taken to `first_call_date`, or null when the maturity is not callable. Both price variants share three quantities. The semiannual yield is `yield_pct` divided by two hundred, which converts an annual percentage into a semiannual decimal rate. The semiannual coupon per one hundred of par is `coupon_rate_pct` divided by two. The discount factor is one plus the semiannual yield, raised to the power of the negative of the relevant period count; and the annuity factor is one minus that discount factor, divided by the semiannual yield.

`price_to_maturity_pct` is always computed, for callable and non-callable maturities alike. It is the semiannual coupon multiplied by the annuity factor taken over `periods_to_maturity`, plus a redemption value of 100.000 percent of par multiplied by the discount factor taken over `periods_to_maturity`, with the sum rounded half up to three decimal places. `price_to_call_pct` is computed only when `is_callable` is true and is null otherwise. It is the semiannual coupon multiplied by the annuity factor taken over `periods_to_call`, plus `call_price_pct` multiplied by the discount factor taken over `periods_to_call`, with the sum rounded half up to three decimal places. Each variant is rounded independently, and the rounded figures are what get compared.

Selection then follows a strictly-lower test. When `is_callable` is false, `reoffering_price_pct` takes the value of `price_to_maturity_pct` and `is_priced_to_call` is false. When `is_callable` is true and `price_to_call_pct` is strictly less than `price_to_maturity_pct`, `reoffering_price_pct` takes the value of `price_to_call_pct` and `is_priced_to_call` becomes true. In every other case — including an exact tie between the two rounded prices — `reoffering_price_pct` takes the value of `price_to_maturity_pct` and `is_priced_to_call` stays false. A five percent coupon reoffered at a four and a quarter percent yield over twenty semiannual periods without a call prices at 106.058 to maturity. The same coupon at a four percent yield over forty periods, callable at par after twenty, prices at 113.678 to maturity and 108.176 to the call, so the maturity is marketed at 108.176 and `is_priced_to_call` is true. A three percent coupon at the same yield and the same call structure prices at 86.322 to maturity against 91.824 to the call, so the discount maturity stays priced to maturity. A four percent coupon at a four percent yield prices at exactly 100.000 on both variants, and the tie resolves to the to-maturity variant with `is_priced_to_call` false.

Three further maturity fields start at their defaults and are moved only by later events. `is_insured` starts false and flips with credit-enhancement commitment and withdrawal. `rating_basis` starts `UNDERLYING` and becomes `ENHANCED` on insured maturities. `par_allotted_usd` and `par_unsold_usd` both start at zero. Maturities can be listed under their deal in schedule order with optional `is_callable` and `is_insured` filters, listed across deals with optional `deal_id` and `refunded_tranche_id` filters, and read individually with both price variants and their allotment counters exposed.

Submitting the structure is the `MunicipalAdvisor`'s act of freezing the schedule, and it advances the deal from `DRAFT` to `STRUCTURING`. It requires at least one maturity on the deal, and it requires the schedule to foot to the authorisation exactly: the sum of `principal_amount_usd` over the deal's maturities must equal `expected_par_amount_usd` to the dollar, and any discrepancy in either direction is rejected with `ERR_PAR_SCHEDULE_MISMATCH`. Every maturity's `periods_to_maturity` must be a positive whole number, which the alignment rule already guarantees at creation time. On success, three things are written. `Deal.par_amount_usd` is set to the sum of the maturities' `principal_amount_usd`. Every maturity of the deal has its `par_unsold_usd` initialised to its own `principal_amount_usd` and its `par_allotted_usd` initialised to zero, so the par identity holds from the outset. And every syndicate member of the deal whose `status` is `ACTIVE` has its `liability_par_usd` set to its `liability_pct` divided by one hundred, multiplied by the deal's newly known `par_amount_usd`, rounded half up to two decimal places. The deal's `version` increments by one.

### 3.3. Credit Enhancement: Commitment and Compensating Withdrawal

An enhancement proposal is recorded by a `MunicipalAdvisor` or an `IssuerOfficial` and describes an offer from an insurer, a bank, or a state intercept program. It captures the `provider_name` (one to one hundred twenty characters, not blank), the `enhancement_type` — one of `BOND_INSURANCE`, `LETTER_OF_CREDIT`, or `STATE_INTERCEPT` — the `enhanced_rating` the enhancement would carry (one to six characters), the `premium_bps` charged in basis points (strictly greater than zero, no more than two hundred, with at most two decimal places), and `insured_tranche_ids`, the list of maturities the enhancement would cover. That list must hold between one and forty distinct maturity identifiers, and every identifier in it must belong to the same deal as the enhancement; a duplicated identifier, an empty list, or a `premium_bps` outside the range of one through two hundred reports `ERR_ENHANCEMENT_ALREADY_COMMITTED`, while an identifier belonging to another deal reports `ERR_CROSS_DEAL_REFERENCE` at the earlier integrity tier. A new proposal is created with `status` `PROPOSED`, with `insured_debt_service_usd`, `premium_usd`, and `withdrawal_reason` all null. Proposals can be listed under their deal with an optional `status` filter.

Committing is the `IssuerOfficial`'s decision to buy the enhancement, and it moves the proposal from `PROPOSED` to `COMMITTED`. A proposal not in `PROPOSED` is refused with `ERR_ENHANCEMENT_INVALID_STATE`. The deal must be in `STRUCTURING`, `PRICING_COMPETITIVE`, or `PRICING_NEGOTIATED`, and any other deal status is refused with `ERR_DEAL_INVALID_STATE`. At most one enhancement per deal may hold `COMMITTED` at a time, so a second commitment while one is live is refused with `ERR_ENHANCEMENT_ALREADY_COMMITTED`.

Commitment does four things. Every maturity whose identifier appears in `insured_tranche_ids` has its `is_insured` set to true and its `rating_basis` moved from `UNDERLYING` to `ENHANCED`, which is what tells the marketing documents that those maturities carry the insurer's rating rather than the issuer's own. The enhancement's `insured_debt_service_usd` is computed as the aggregate debt service of the insured set: taking the insured maturities in ascending `sequence_number`, each maturity's debt service is its `principal_amount_usd` plus the semiannual interest it will pay over its whole life, where that interest is `principal_amount_usd` multiplied by `coupon_rate_pct` divided by one hundred, divided by the two semiannual periods in a year, and multiplied by `periods_to_maturity`; each maturity's figure is rounded half up to two decimal places and the rounded figures are then summed. The enhancement's `premium_usd` is `insured_debt_service_usd` multiplied by `premium_bps` and divided by ten thousand, because a basis point is one ten-thousandth, rounded half up to two decimal places. Finally `Deal.credit_enhancement_premium_usd` is set to that `premium_usd`, and if the deal has already been priced its `net_proceeds_to_issuer_usd` is recomputed so the money identity continues to hold. Both the enhancement's `version` and the deal's `version` increment. A five-million-dollar maturity carrying a five percent coupon over twenty semiannual periods has aggregate debt service of 7,500,000.00, and at forty-five basis points the premium is 33,750.00; the same principal and coupon over forty periods has debt service of 10,000,000.00 and a premium of 45,000.00.

Withdrawal is the compensating action, available to an `IssuerOfficial` on an enhancement whose `status` is `COMMITTED` — any other status reports `ERR_ENHANCEMENT_INVALID_STATE` — while the deal is in `STRUCTURING`, `PRICING_COMPETITIVE`, `PRICING_NEGOTIATED`, or `PRICED`; any other deal status reports `ERR_DEAL_INVALID_STATE`. It takes a `withdrawal_reason` and unwinds the commitment in four causally ordered hops. In the first hop the enhancement's `status` becomes `WITHDRAWN`, its `enhanced_rating` is cleared to null, and `withdrawal_reason` is stored. In the second hop, caused by the first, every maturity named in `insured_tranche_ids` whose `is_insured` is true has that flag set back to false and its `rating_basis` returned to `UNDERLYING`. In the third hop, caused by the second, the deal now has no insured maturity, so `Deal.credit_enhancement_premium_usd` becomes 0.00 and — when the deal's `status` is already `PRICED` — `net_proceeds_to_issuer_usd` is recomputed as `issue_price_total_usd` less `gross_spread_total_usd` less a zero premium, rounded half up to two decimal places. In the fourth hop, caused by the third, the disclosed proceeds figure has changed, so every section of the deal's governing official statement whose `references_credit_enhancement` is true and whose `review_status` is `APPROVED` is moved to `NEEDS_REVISION`, that statement's `mandatory_sections_approved` is recomputed, and `Deal.disclosure_clearance` is recomputed — becoming `BLOCKED` when the governing statement's `status` is `REVISION_REQUESTED` or `WITHDRAWN` and `PENDING` otherwise. Alongside the disclosure reopening, every rating presentation on the deal whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` is moved to `CANCELLED` with its `cancellation_reason` recorded as `DISCLOSURE_BLOCKED`. Both the enhancement's `version` and the deal's `version` increment. Where a deal was priced with a premium of 33,750.00 against proceeds of 10,711,700.00 and a spread of 87,500.00, withdrawal restores net proceeds of 10,624,200.00 — the money identity holds before and after with no drift.

An enhancement still sitting in `PROPOSED` when the deal is cancelled is moved to `DECLINED` rather than `WITHDRAWN`; a `COMMITTED` one is moved to `WITHDRAWN`. Both `WITHDRAWN` and `DECLINED` are terminal.

### 3.4. Disclosure: Drafting the Official Statement, Reviewing Sections, and Posting

Starting an official statement is available to a `BondCounsel` user or a `MunicipalAdvisor` and is the one creation in the system that is version-guarded. The request supplies a `version_label` such as `POS v1` (one to forty characters, not blank), an `is_preliminary` boolean stating whether the document is being drafted as a preliminary official statement, and the `expected_deal_version` token described in section 2.7. The new statement begins with `status` `DRAFTING`, `draft_round` 1, `mandatory_sections_approved` false, and `posted_preliminary_at`, `posted_final_at`, and `revision_reason` all null. Creation also supersedes the predecessor: any earlier statement on the same deal whose `status` is `DRAFTING`, `IN_REVIEW`, or `REVISION_REQUESTED` is moved to the terminal `SUPERSEDED` state with its own `version` incremented. `Deal.disclosure_clearance` is then recomputed against the newly created statement, and the deal's `version` increments. Statements can be listed under their deal newest first with an optional `status` filter, and read individually to obtain `mandatory_sections_approved`, `draft_round`, and `version`.

Only a `BondCounsel` user writes sections. A section supplies a `section_code` such as `RISK-03` (one to sixteen characters, unique within its official statement), a `title` (one to one hundred sixty characters, not blank), the `body_text` prose (one to eight thousand characters, not blank), and a `section_type` drawn from `RISK_FACTOR`, `TAX_MATTERS`, `CONTINUING_DISCLOSURE`, `UNDERWRITING`, and `GENERAL`. Three inputs are optional: `is_material_risk`, defaulting to false, marks the section as disclosing a material risk factor; `references_credit_enhancement`, defaulting to false, marks the section's prose as depending on a committed enhancement and is what subscribes it to the reopening cascade of section 3.3; and `parent_section_id`, defaulting to null, nests the section inside an enclosing one and must name a section of the very same official statement. The server sets `review_status` to `DRAFT`, records `authored_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID`, leaves `approved_by_user_id` null, and computes `word_count` as the number of whitespace-delimited tokens in `body_text`, recomputing it on every write that changes the prose. Sections can be listed under their statement with optional `section_type` and `review_status` filters, listed across statements with optional `official_statement_id`, `parent_section_id`, and `review_status` filters, and read individually.

Approving a section is also a `BondCounsel` act, and it is where MSRB Rule G-17 bites. The section's `review_status` must be either `DRAFT` or `NEEDS_REVISION`; a section already `APPROVED` cannot be approved again and reports `ERR_SECTION_INVALID_STATE`. The owning statement's `status` must be `DRAFTING` or `IN_REVIEW`; any other status reports `ERR_OS_INVALID_STATE`. And the approving `X-User-ID` must differ from the section's `authored_by_user_id`, or the request is refused with `ERR_SECTION_SELF_APPROVAL`. Both entry paths into approval behave identically: a section moving from `DRAFT` to `APPROVED` and a section moving from `NEEDS_REVISION` to `APPROVED` each record `approved_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID`, each trigger a recomputation of the statement's `mandatory_sections_approved` and of `Deal.disclosure_clearance`, and each increment both the section's `version` and the statement's `version` by one.

`mandatory_sections_approved` is a derived flag with a precise definition: it is true only when the statement carries at least one section with `review_status` `APPROVED` for each of the four mandatory disclosure categories — `RISK_FACTOR`, `TAX_MATTERS`, `CONTINUING_DISCLOSURE`, and `UNDERWRITING` — and at least one of those approved `RISK_FACTOR` sections has `is_material_risk` true. `GENERAL` sections never contribute to the flag. Because the flag is recomputed rather than latched, the removal of any single contributing approval turns it false again.

The review loop runs `DRAFTING` → `IN_REVIEW` → `REVISION_REQUESTED` → `DRAFTING`, and it is bounded. Submitting for review is a `BondCounsel` act that requires at least one section to exist on the statement — an empty statement reports `ERR_OS_INVALID_STATE` — and requires the owning deal not to be in `CLOSED` or `CANCELLED`, which otherwise reports `ERR_DEAL_INVALID_STATE`. It recomputes `mandatory_sections_approved` and `Deal.disclosure_clearance` and increments the statement's `version`.

Requesting a revision is the `MunicipalAdvisor`'s rejection of a statement in `IN_REVIEW`, it carries a `revision_reason`, and it is the widest cascade in the system. It is permitted only while `draft_round` is strictly less than four; a fourth request when `draft_round` has already reached four is refused with `ERR_REVISION_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, which is the hard cap on drafting rounds. When it is permitted, five hops fire in order. Hop one sets the statement's `status` to `REVISION_REQUESTED`, adds one to `draft_round`, and stores `revision_reason`. Hop two, caused by hop one, moves every section on that statement whose `section_type` is `RISK_FACTOR` and whose `review_status` is `APPROVED` to `NEEDS_REVISION`, clearing each one's `approved_by_user_id` back to null. Hop three, caused by hop two, recomputes `mandatory_sections_approved`, which necessarily becomes false because no approved `RISK_FACTOR` section survives. Hop four, caused by hop three, recomputes `Deal.disclosure_clearance`, which becomes `BLOCKED` because the governing statement now has `mandatory_sections_approved` false and `status` `REVISION_REQUESTED`. Hop five, caused by hop four, reaches into the credit review: every rating presentation with `status` `SCHEDULED` belonging to any rating engagement of that deal is moved to `CANCELLED` with `cancellation_reason` set to `DISCLOSURE_BLOCKED`, and every rating engagement of that deal whose `status` is `UNDER_REVIEW` is moved to `INFO_REQUESTED` with one added to its `info_request_count` and its `latest_information_request` set to `DISCLOSURE_BLOCKED_PENDING_OS_REVISION`. Both the statement's `version` and the deal's `version` increment.

Resuming drafting closes the loop. A `BondCounsel` user returns a statement from `REVISION_REQUESTED` to `DRAFTING` provided the owning deal is not `CLOSED` or `CANCELLED`, which otherwise reports `ERR_DEAL_INVALID_STATE`. `Deal.disclosure_clearance` is recomputed and, because the statement is now back in `DRAFTING` with its mandatory flag false, lands on `PENDING`. Both the statement's `version` and the deal's `version` increment. Note that resuming drafting does *not* undo hop five: presentations that were cancelled stay cancelled and engagements that were pushed to `INFO_REQUESTED` stay there until the deal team answers.

The continuing disclosure undertaking is the Rule 15c2-12 promise that makes posting possible, and it is drafted by a `BondCounsel` user or a `MunicipalAdvisor`. It captures `annual_report_due_days`, the number of days after each fiscal year end by which the annual report must be filed, which must be at least 120 and at most 270; `material_event_notice_days`, the number of days after a reportable event by which the notice must be filed, which must be at least 1 and at most 10, since Rule 15c2-12 caps material event notices at ten business days; `dissemination_agent`, the name of the agent responsible for filings with EMMA (one to one hundred twenty characters, not blank); and the optional `audited_financials_included`, which defaults to true and states whether audited financial statements form part of the annual report commitment. A new undertaking has `status` `DRAFT`. Undertakings can be listed under their deal with an optional `status` filter. Activating one is an `IssuerOfficial` act: the undertaking must be in `DRAFT`, or the request is refused with `ERR_UNDERTAKING_INVALID_STATE`, and the deal must not be `CLOSED` or `CANCELLED`, or it is refused with `ERR_DEAL_INVALID_STATE`. Activation moves the undertaking to `ACTIVE` and moves every other undertaking of the same deal that was `ACTIVE` to the terminal `SUPERSEDED`, incrementing each affected row's `version`. Exactly one undertaking is therefore `ACTIVE` on a deal at any moment.

Posting the preliminary official statement is the `MunicipalAdvisor`'s act that clears the deal for pricing, and it takes a statement from `IN_REVIEW` to `PRELIMINARY_POSTED`. The deal must already be on one of the two pricing branches, `PRICING_COMPETITIVE` or `PRICING_NEGOTIATED`; any other deal status reports `ERR_DEAL_INVALID_STATE`, and because state legality sits above disclosure adequacy in the ladder of section 2.5, that refusal precedes the three disclosure gates. Those three gates are then evaluated in this order. First, `mandatory_sections_approved` must be true, or the request is refused with `ERR_MANDATORY_SECTION_NOT_APPROVED`. Second, the deal must carry a continuing disclosure undertaking whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, or it is refused with `ERR_UNDERTAKING_NOT_ACTIVE`. Third, the high-risk-issuer gate applies: when the deal's issuer has a `disclosure_risk_score` at or above forty, the statement must carry at least one section whose `section_type` is `CONTINUING_DISCLOSURE`, whose `is_material_risk` is true, and whose `review_status` is `APPROVED`; a statement without such a section is refused with `ERR_DISCLOSURE_RISK_SECTION_REQUIRED`. On success, `posted_preliminary_at` is stamped from the logical clock, `Deal.disclosure_clearance` is recomputed and becomes `CLEARED`, and both the statement's `version` and the deal's `version` increment.

Posting the final official statement happens after award and is likewise a `MunicipalAdvisor` act, taking the statement from `PRELIMINARY_POSTED` to the terminal `FINAL_POSTED`. It requires the deal's `status` to be exactly `AWARDED`, refusing anything else with `ERR_DEAL_INVALID_STATE`, and it raises the disclosure bar: *every* section on the statement must have `review_status` `APPROVED`, including `GENERAL` sections that never counted toward `mandatory_sections_approved`. A single unapproved section refuses the posting with `ERR_MANDATORY_SECTION_NOT_APPROVED`. On success, `posted_final_at` is stamped from the logical clock, `Deal.disclosure_clearance` stays `CLEARED`, and the statement's `version` increments.

Two more statement transitions are driven by events elsewhere. A statement in `DRAFTING`, `IN_REVIEW`, or `REVISION_REQUESTED` becomes `SUPERSEDED` when a newer statement is created on the same deal. A statement in `DRAFTING`, `IN_REVIEW`, `REVISION_REQUESTED`, or `PRELIMINARY_POSTED` becomes the terminal `WITHDRAWN` when the owning deal is cancelled, and in that case `Deal.disclosure_clearance` is recomputed to `BLOCKED` and the statement's `version` increments.

### 3.5. Rating Agency Coordination and the Credit Review Loop

A credit rating is requested by a `MunicipalAdvisor` or an `IssuerOfficial` and names a single `agency` from `MOODYS`, `SP`, `FITCH`, and `KBRA`. A deal may hold at most one engagement per agency that is not `WITHDRAWN`, so a second live request to the same agency is refused with `ERR_DUPLICATE_RATING_ENGAGEMENT`; once an engagement has been withdrawn, that agency may be re-engaged. A new engagement starts with `status` `REQUESTED`, `assigned_analyst_user_id` null, `info_request_count` at zero, and `latest_information_request`, `assigned_rating`, and `outlook` all null. Engagements can be listed under their deal with optional `agency` and `status` filters, and read individually to see the pinned analyst and the information-request counter.

Acknowledgement is how an analyst takes ownership. A `RatingAnalyst` acknowledges an engagement whose `status` is `REQUESTED` — any other status reports `ERR_RATING_ENGAGEMENT_INVALID_STATE` — which advances it from `REQUESTED` to `UNDER_REVIEW` and writes the acting `X-User-ID` into `assigned_analyst_user_id`. From that moment the engagement is **pinned**: only the analyst whose identifier matches `assigned_analyst_user_id` may request information on it or assign its rating, and any other `RatingAnalyst` attempting either is refused with `ERR_ROLE_IDENTITY_MISMATCH` even though the role itself is authorised.

The credit review then runs an unbounded loop between `UNDER_REVIEW` and `INFO_REQUESTED`. The pinned analyst suspends the review by supplying an `information_request`, which moves the engagement from `UNDER_REVIEW` to `INFO_REQUESTED`, adds one to `info_request_count`, and stores the text in `latest_information_request`. The deal team answers through a `MunicipalAdvisor` who supplies a `response_summary`; that returns the engagement from `INFO_REQUESTED` to `UNDER_REVIEW`, and an engagement not in `INFO_REQUESTED` reports `ERR_RATING_ENGAGEMENT_INVALID_STATE`. There is no cap on how many times the loop may run, and `info_request_count` is a cumulative counter that is never reset — it counts every information request the engagement has ever received, whether raised by the analyst directly or injected by the disclosure-block cascade of section 3.4.

Assigning the rating closes the engagement into the terminal `RATED` state. The pinned analyst supplies an `assigned_rating` and an `outlook` chosen from `POSITIVE`, `STABLE`, `NEGATIVE`, and `DEVELOPING`, both of which are stored on the engagement. The transition is available only from `UNDER_REVIEW`, and it carries one substantive precondition: at least one rating presentation on that engagement must have `status` `HELD`. An analyst who tries to rate an issue on which no presentation has actually taken place is refused with `ERR_PRESENTATION_REQUIRED`. An engagement in `REQUESTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, or `INFO_REQUESTED` is instead moved to the terminal `WITHDRAWN` if the owning deal is cancelled.

Presentations are the scheduled windows in which the deal team walks the agency through the credit, and either a `MunicipalAdvisor` or an `Underwriter` may schedule one on an engagement. A presentation records the engagement it belongs to in its `rating_engagement_id` and supplies `starts_at` and `ends_at` as ISO 8601 UTC instants with three fractional digits, and an `attendee_count` of issuer and advisor attendees that must be at least 1 and at most 25. `ends_at` must fall strictly after `starts_at`, and the window may run no longer than three hours — that is, `ends_at` may be at most one hundred eighty minutes after `starts_at`. A presentation may not be scheduled on an engagement that has already reached `RATED` or `WITHDRAWN`; such an attempt is refused with `ERR_PRESENTATION_INVALID_STATE`.

Agencies will not tolerate back-to-back sessions, so a **buffer of one clear hour** guards each agency's schedule on a deal. Take the new presentation's closed interval from `starts_at` through `ends_at` and expand it by sixty minutes at each end. If that expanded interval contains any instant belonging to an existing presentation that is not `CANCELLED` and that belongs to a rating engagement of the *same* deal with the *same* `agency`, the request is refused with `ERR_PRESENTATION_BUFFER_CONFLICT`. The boundary is exact: a gap of precisely sixty minutes between one presentation's `ends_at` and the next presentation's `starts_at` is permitted, while a gap of fifty-nine minutes is not. Presentations for *different* agencies on the same deal are free to overlap without restriction, since each agency sits in its own room. Because the test ignores cancelled presentations, cancelling a slot genuinely frees its window for rescheduling. A new presentation begins with `status` `SCHEDULED` and `cancellation_reason` null; presentations can be listed under their engagement with an optional `status` filter and read individually.

A presentation leaves `SCHEDULED` by three routes. The ordinary route is the logical clock: whenever the clock is set to an instant at or after a `SCHEDULED` presentation's `ends_at`, that presentation is promoted to the terminal `HELD` and its `version` increments, and where a single clock advance sweeps past several presentations they are promoted in ascending order of `ends_at`, ties broken by ascending `created_at`. The second route is a deliberate cancellation by a `MunicipalAdvisor` or an `Underwriter`, which supplies a `cancellation_reason`, moves a `SCHEDULED` presentation to the terminal `CANCELLED`, and increments its `version`; a presentation not in `SCHEDULED` reports `ERR_PRESENTATION_INVALID_STATE`. The third route is a cascade: the disclosure-block cascade and the enhancement-withdrawal cascade both cancel `SCHEDULED` presentations with `cancellation_reason` recorded as `DISCLOSURE_BLOCKED`, and deal cancellation cancels them with `cancellation_reason` recorded as `DEAL_CANCELLED`.

### 3.6. Syndicate Formation and the Sale-Method Fork

Seating an underwriting firm on a deal is available to an `Underwriter` or a `MunicipalAdvisor` and supplies the `firm_id` of the firm being seated, a `member_role` of `SENIOR_MANAGER`, `CO_MANAGER`, or `SELLING_GROUP`, a `liability_pct` share of the underwriting liability, a `takedown_share_pct` share of the syndicate takedown pot, and a `priority_rank` used only for reporting order. Both percentage shares must be strictly greater than zero, no greater than 100, and carry at most four decimal places. `priority_rank` is an integer from 1 through 40 and must be unique among the deal's `ACTIVE` members. A new seat has `status` `ACTIVE` and `takedown_credit_usd` zero, and its `liability_par_usd` is set immediately when the deal's `par_amount_usd` is already known — that is, once the structure has been submitted; otherwise it starts at zero and is filled in when the structure is submitted. The alternative membership state is `RESIGNED`.

Four rejections guard the seat. MSRB Rule G-23 refuses a firm that is the deal's `municipal_advisor_firm_id` with `ERR_MA_UNDERWRITER_CONFLICT`. A firm whose `is_suspended` is true is refused with `ERR_FIRM_SUSPENDED`. Uniqueness is enforced three ways — a firm may not hold more than one seat on the deal that is not `RESIGNED`, a deal may have at most one `SENIOR_MANAGER`, and `priority_rank` may not collide among `ACTIVE` members — and all three report `ERR_DUPLICATE_SYNDICATE_MEMBER`. Finally the fiscal bound: the sum of `liability_pct` over the deal's `ACTIVE` members may never exceed 100.0000 once the new seat is counted, and the identical bound applies to the sum of `takedown_share_pct`; a breach of either reports `ERR_LIABILITY_PCT_SUM_INVALID`. Note the asymmetry between the two moments at which liability shares are examined: adding a member merely requires the running total not to *exceed* the whole, whereas pricing a negotiated deal requires it to *equal* the whole exactly. Seats can be listed under their deal in `priority_rank` order with optional `member_role` and `status` filters, and a firm's seats across all deals can be listed from the firm side.

Designating the sale method is the branching decision of the whole transaction and is taken by a `MunicipalAdvisor` or an `IssuerOfficial` on a deal in `STRUCTURING`. The request supplies a single `sale_method` value, and the two branches diverge in preconditions, in flags, and — much later, at award — in the set of closing documents they generate. A deal whose `sale_method` is already `COMPETITIVE` or `NEGOTIATED` may not be re-designated; a second designation is refused with `ERR_SALE_METHOD_CONFLICT`.

Choosing `COMPETITIVE` moves the deal from `STRUCTURING` to `PRICING_COMPETITIVE` and models a public bid: the issuer publishes a notice of sale, bidders bid, and there is no pre-formed syndicate. It therefore requires a genuinely independent advisor. The deal's `municipal_advisor_firm_id` must be non-null and the firm it names must have a `firm_type` of `MUNICIPAL_ADVISOR` or `DUAL_REGISTERED`; a missing or ineligible advisor is refused with `ERR_MA_ENGAGEMENT_REQUIRED`. That advisor firm must also have `is_suspended` false, or the request is refused with `ERR_FIRM_SUSPENDED`. And no `SyndicateMember` row may exist on the deal at all, since a pre-arranged syndicate is incompatible with a competitive bid; an existing seat is refused with `ERR_SALE_METHOD_CONFLICT`. On success, `sale_method` and `gross_spread_basis` are both set to `COMPETITIVE`, `requires_notice_of_sale` is set to true, `requires_g17_letter` remains false, and the deal's `version` increments. A competitive deal runs a single institutional book: creating an order period whose `period_type` is `RETAIL` on a `COMPETITIVE` deal is refused with `ERR_SALE_METHOD_CONFLICT`.

Choosing `NEGOTIATED` moves the deal from `STRUCTURING` to `PRICING_NEGOTIATED` and models a syndicate purchase at a negotiated price. It therefore requires the syndicate to have a head: exactly one `SyndicateMember` on the deal with `member_role` `SENIOR_MANAGER` and `status` `ACTIVE` must exist, and neither zero nor more than one satisfies the rule; a failure is refused with `ERR_SENIOR_MANAGER_REQUIRED`. The firm holding that senior manager seat must have `is_suspended` false, or the request is refused with `ERR_FIRM_SUSPENDED`. On success, `sale_method` and `gross_spread_basis` are both set to `NEGOTIATED`, `requires_g17_letter` is set to true, `requires_notice_of_sale` remains false, and the deal's `version` increments. A negotiated deal may run both `RETAIL` and `INSTITUTIONAL` order periods.

The two branches rejoin at `PRICED`, but the flags they set travel forward untouched and are read again at award. `gross_spread_basis` in particular is frozen at designation time and selects which compensation formula applies when the deal is priced.

### 3.7. The Order Book: Periods, Order Entry, Allotment and Reopening

Orders are collected inside **order periods**, each of which is a window on a single deal for a single investor class. Either an `Underwriter` or a `MunicipalAdvisor` schedules a period by supplying a `period_type` of `RETAIL` or `INSTITUTIONAL`, an `opens_at`, a `closes_at` that must fall strictly after `opens_at`, and an optional `priority_policy` of `STANDARD_PRIORITY` or `RETAIL_ONLY`; when `priority_policy` is omitted the period is treated as `STANDARD_PRIORITY`, which admits every order type, whereas `RETAIL_ONLY` narrows the book to retail demand. A new period has `status` `SCHEDULED`, `allotment_run_count` zero, `orders_received_count` and `allotted_order_count` zero, and `reopen_reason` null. Scheduling imposes no deal-state precondition of its own — the deal-state gate arrives when the book is opened — but a `RETAIL` period may not be scheduled on a deal whose `sale_method` is `COMPETITIVE`, and such an attempt is refused with `ERR_SALE_METHOD_CONFLICT`.

The windows of a single deal may not overlap. Each period occupies the half-open interval from `opens_at` inclusive to `closes_at` exclusive, and a new period's interval must not overlap the interval of any existing period of the same deal that is not `CANCELLED`; a violation is refused with `ERR_ORDER_PERIOD_OVERLAP`. Because the interval is half-open, **abutting** windows do not overlap: a retail period closing at a given instant and an institutional period opening at exactly that same instant are both permitted, and this is the normal way to run a retail order period immediately ahead of the institutional book. Periods can be listed under their deal with optional `period_type` and `status` filters, and read individually — the individual read is how a client obtains the `version` that order entry must echo.

Opening the book moves a period from `SCHEDULED` to `OPEN` at the hand of an `Underwriter` and carries three preconditions. The deal must be in `PRICING_COMPETITIVE` or `PRICING_NEGOTIATED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_DEAL_INVALID_STATE`; a deal still in `STRUCTURING` has not yet chosen how it will sell its bonds and cannot take orders. No other period of the same deal may currently be `OPEN`, since only one book runs at a time, and a violation reports `ERR_ORDER_PERIOD_INVALID_STATE`. And the logical clock must lie inside the window — at or after `opens_at` and strictly before `closes_at` — or the request is refused with `ERR_ORDER_PERIOD_WINDOW_CLOSED`. A period whose window has already elapsed on the logical clock can therefore never be opened at all.

An order is entered by an `Underwriter` into an open book. The request supplies the `maturity_tranche_id` being ordered, the `syndicate_member_id` entering the order, an `order_type` of `RETAIL`, `INSTITUTIONAL_PRIORITY`, `MEMBER`, or `STOCK`, a `par_requested_usd`, a `price_or_yield_pct` that must be greater than zero, no greater than 130, and carry at most three decimal places, and the `expected_order_period_version` described in section 2.7. Both referenced entities must belong to the same deal as the order period, or the request is refused with `ERR_CROSS_DEAL_REFERENCE`. `par_requested_usd` must be a positive whole multiple of five thousand United States dollars, or the request is refused with `ERR_DENOMINATION_INVALID`. Three order-type placement rules apply, all reported as `ERR_ORDER_TYPE_NOT_PERMITTED`: an order of `order_type` `RETAIL` may only be placed into a period whose `period_type` is `RETAIL`; when the period's `priority_policy` is `RETAIL_ONLY`, only `order_type` `RETAIL` is accepted at all; and an order of `order_type` `MEMBER` or `STOCK` requires the referenced syndicate member to have `member_role` `SENIOR_MANAGER` or `CO_MANAGER`, because those classes are the syndicate's own demand. The period must be `OPEN`, or `ERR_ORDER_PERIOD_INVALID_STATE` is reported, and the logical clock must be strictly before `closes_at`, or `ERR_ORDER_PERIOD_WINDOW_CLOSED` is reported — an open period whose window has expired on the clock accepts nothing further even though its `status` still reads `OPEN`. On success the order is created with `status` `PENDING`, `par_allotted_usd` zero, `cancellation_reason` null, and `submitted_at` set to the current logical clock instant; the period's `orders_received_count` is recomputed as the count of its orders that are not `CANCELLED`, and the period's `version` increments. An `Underwriter` may withdraw a `PENDING` order while the book is still `OPEN` and the clock is still before `closes_at` by supplying a `cancellation_reason`; the order becomes the terminal `CANCELLED` with `par_allotted_usd` zero, `orders_received_count` is recomputed, and both the order's and the period's `version` increment. A cancelled order is excluded from every later allotment run, so cancelling genuinely removes demand from the book. Orders can be listed under their period in `submitted_at` order with optional `order_type` and `status` filters, listed across periods with optional `maturity_tranche_id`, `syndicate_member_id`, `order_period_id`, and `status` filters, and read individually.

Closing the book is where the bonds are actually distributed. An `Underwriter` closes an `OPEN` period — any other status reports `ERR_ORDER_PERIOD_INVALID_STATE` — and the close runs the **priority-of-orders waterfall** once per maturity tranche of the deal, independently for each tranche. For a given tranche the principal available to this run is the tranche's `principal_amount_usd` less the par already allotted to that tranche by *other* order periods of the same deal, counted as the sum of `par_allotted_usd` over orders on that tranche whose `status` is `ALLOTTED` or `PARTIALLY_ALLOTTED` and which belong to other periods. The candidates are the orders in the closing period on that tranche whose `status` is `PENDING`; cancelled orders and orders from other periods are never candidates. Candidates are grouped into four priority tiers by `order_type` — `RETAIL` is tier one, `INSTITUTIONAL_PRIORITY` is tier two, `MEMBER` is tier three, and `STOCK` is tier four — and within a tier they are ordered by ascending `submitted_at`, ties broken by ascending `created_at`.

The tiers are then satisfied strictly in order, one through four. If a tier's total demand, the sum of `par_requested_usd` over its candidates, is less than or equal to the principal still available, every candidate in that tier receives its full `par_requested_usd` and becomes `ALLOTTED`, and the available principal is reduced by the tier's demand. If a tier's demand exceeds what remains, that tier is pro-rated and every lower tier receives nothing. Pro-ration proceeds in two stages. First, each candidate's raw share is the available principal multiplied by that candidate's `par_requested_usd` and divided by the tier's total demand, and that raw share is floored down to a whole multiple of five thousand United States dollars. Second, the remainder — the available principal less the sum of the floored allotments — is distributed in whole five-thousand-dollar increments, one increment per candidate per pass, walking the tier in its `submitted_at` then `created_at` order and skipping any candidate already standing at its full `par_requested_usd`, until the remainder is less than five thousand dollars. After a pro-rated tier the available principal is zero. Each candidate's outcome state follows from the arithmetic: `ALLOTTED` when `par_allotted_usd` equals `par_requested_usd`, `PARTIALLY_ALLOTTED` when it lies strictly between zero and `par_requested_usd`, and `UNFILLED` when it is zero.

For a worked case, take a tranche with `principal_amount_usd` of 5,000,000 and four orders: O1 is `RETAIL` for 2,005,000 and was submitted first, O2 is `INSTITUTIONAL_PRIORITY` for 2,000,000, O3 is `INSTITUTIONAL_PRIORITY` for 1,500,000 submitted after O2, and O4 is `MEMBER` for 1,000,000. Tier one demands 2,005,000 against 5,000,000 available, so O1 is allotted 2,005,000 in full and becomes `ALLOTTED`, leaving 2,995,000. Tier two demands 3,500,000, which exceeds 2,995,000, so it is pro-rated: O2's raw share is 1,711,428.571… and O3's is 1,283,571.428…, which floor to 1,710,000 and 1,280,000 respectively and leave a remainder of 5,000. That single increment goes to O2 as the earlier `submitted_at`, so O2 finishes at 1,715,000 `PARTIALLY_ALLOTTED` and O3 at 1,280,000 `PARTIALLY_ALLOTTED`. Tier three is starved, so O4 is allotted zero and becomes `UNFILLED`. The tranche ends with `par_allotted_usd` 5,000,000 and `par_unsold_usd` zero.

Beyond the per-order writes, closing adds one to the period's `allotment_run_count`, recomputes `allotted_order_count` as the count of orders on the period whose `status` is `ALLOTTED` or `PARTIALLY_ALLOTTED` after this run, recomputes every touched tranche's `par_allotted_usd` and its `par_unsold_usd` as `principal_amount_usd` less `par_allotted_usd`, sets `Deal.par_allotted_total_usd` to the sum of `par_allotted_usd` across the deal's tranches, and increments both the period's and the deal's `version`. Closing a period that holds no `PENDING` orders at all is entirely legal: `allotment_run_count` still becomes one, `allotted_order_count` is zero, and every tranche keeps `par_unsold_usd` equal to its `principal_amount_usd`.

Reopening is the compensating action for a completed allotment run, and it is reserved to a `ComplianceOfficer`. The period must be `CLOSED`, or `ERR_ORDER_PERIOD_INVALID_STATE` is reported; the deal must still be in `PRICING_COMPETITIVE` or `PRICING_NEGOTIATED`, or `ERR_DEAL_INVALID_STATE` is reported; and the logical clock must still be strictly before `closes_at`, or `ERR_ORDER_PERIOD_WINDOW_CLOSED` is reported — a book whose window has elapsed cannot be reopened, only settled or cancelled. The request supplies a `reopen_reason`, and the cascade runs in four ordered hops. Hop one sets the period's `status` back to `OPEN` and stores the supplied reason in `reopen_reason`. Hop two, caused by hop one, returns every order in this period whose `status` is `ALLOTTED`, `PARTIALLY_ALLOTTED`, or `UNFILLED` to `PENDING` with `par_allotted_usd` reset to zero and increments each such order's `version`. Hop three, caused by hop two, recomputes for every maturity tranche referenced by those orders its `par_allotted_usd` from the allotted orders that survive — that is, the allotments belonging to other periods, which are untouched — and its `par_unsold_usd` as `principal_amount_usd` less `par_allotted_usd`. Hop four, caused by hop three, recomputes `Deal.par_allotted_total_usd` and recomputes every active syndicate member's `takedown_credit_usd`. The period's and the deal's `version` each increment. The par identity is the acceptance test for this cascade: for every tranche, `par_allotted_usd` plus `par_unsold_usd` equals `principal_amount_usd` exactly, both before the reopen and after it, with no drift of a single dollar. Note that `allotment_run_count` is a cumulative counter and is *not* decremented by a reopen — it records that a run happened, which is what the pricing precondition looks for.

Two system transitions retire a period. When the deal is priced, every `CLOSED` period of that deal moves to the terminal `SETTLED` and its `version` increments. When the deal is cancelled, every period in `SCHEDULED`, `OPEN`, or `CLOSED` moves to the terminal `CANCELLED` and its `version` increments.

### 3.8. Pricing, Compensation Disclosure and Arbitrage Yield Restriction

Pricing is the single largest write in the system: it is taken by an `Underwriter`, it is the point at which underwriter compensation becomes a disclosed number under MSRB Rule G-17, and it runs five calculations in a pinned order. It is reached from either pricing branch, and its two variants — from `PRICING_COMPETITIVE` and from `PRICING_NEGOTIATED`, both to `PRICED` — differ only in which compensation inputs they demand and which spread formula they select. A request against a deal in any other state reports `ERR_DEAL_INVALID_STATE`.

The compensation inputs are branch-exclusive, and supplying the wrong set is refused with `ERR_COMPENSATION_INPUT_MISSING`. Pricing a `NEGOTIATED` deal requires `takedown_per_bond_usd`, `management_fee_per_bond_usd`, and `expenses_per_bond_usd` all to be present and forbids `winning_bid_price_pct`. Pricing a `COMPETITIVE` deal requires `winning_bid_price_pct` to be present and forbids all three per-bond components. Presence of a forbidden input is as much a violation as absence of a required one, so a competitive pricing that helpfully includes a takedown is rejected rather than ignored. On the negotiated branch there is one additional fiscal precondition: the sum of `liability_pct` over the deal's `ACTIVE` syndicate members must equal exactly 100.0000, not merely not exceed it, or the request is refused with `ERR_LIABILITY_PCT_SUM_INVALID`. The competitive branch has no such requirement, since a competitive deal has no syndicate at all. Two order-book preconditions apply to both branches and both report `ERR_PRICING_PRECONDITION_UNMET`: no order period of the deal may currently be `OPEN`, and at least one order period of the deal must be `CLOSED` with `allotment_run_count` of one or more. Together these mean the bonds must have been distributed at least once and no book may still be live. Two disclosure preconditions also apply to both branches and both report `ERR_DISCLOSURE_NOT_CLEARED`: the deal's governing official statement must have `status` `PRELIMINARY_POSTED`, and `Deal.disclosure_clearance` must read `CLEARED`. This is the load-bearing expression of the disclosure-gates-the-money commitment — no municipal issue may be priced against an unposted or blocked preliminary official statement. The relative order in which these are observed follows the ladder of section 2.5: the deal-state check precedes the acting firm's suspension check, which precedes the order-book and disclosure checks, which precede the compensation, liability-sum, and net-proceeds checks.

Pricing then computes, in this order. First the **issue price total**. For each maturity tranche of the deal, taken in ascending `sequence_number` and with no state filter at all, that tranche's proceeds are its `principal_amount_usd` divided by one hundred and multiplied by its `reoffering_price_pct`, rounded to two decimal places; `Deal.issue_price_total_usd` is the sum of those per-tranche figures. The rounding order is pinned: round each tranche and then sum, never sum and then round. A two-tranche schedule with 5,000,000 of principal at a `reoffering_price_pct` of 106.058 and 5,000,000 at 108.176 yields tranche proceeds of 5,302,900.00 and 5,408,800.00 and an `issue_price_total_usd` of 10,711,700.00.

Second the **gross spread disclosure**, whose variant is selected by `Deal.gross_spread_basis` as frozen at designation. On the negotiated basis, the per-bond component total is `takedown_per_bond_usd` plus `management_fee_per_bond_usd` plus `expenses_per_bond_usd`; for each tranche in ascending `sequence_number`, the tranche's bond count is its `principal_amount_usd` divided by one thousand and its spread is that bond count multiplied by the component total, rounded to two decimal places; `Deal.gross_spread_total_usd` is the sum of those rounded per-tranche spreads. Here too the rounding order is pinned — summing bond counts across tranches and rounding once at the end is a different and wrong answer whenever a per-bond component carries more than two significant decimals. On the competitive basis, bid proceeds are `Deal.par_amount_usd` multiplied by `winning_bid_price_pct` divided by one hundred, rounded to two decimal places, and `Deal.gross_spread_total_usd` is `Deal.issue_price_total_usd` less those bid proceeds, rounded to two decimal places — the spread is what the underwriter keeps between the reoffering scale and the price it paid the issuer. Both variants then close with the identical final step: `Deal.gross_spread_per_bond_usd` is `Deal.gross_spread_total_usd` divided by the deal's bond count, which is `Deal.par_amount_usd` divided by one thousand, rounded to three decimal places, and it is always derived from the already-rounded total, never recomputed from the components. A negotiated deal with a takedown of 5.000, a management fee of 2.500, and expenses of 1.250 across two 5,000,000 tranches has a component total of 8.750, per-tranche spreads of 43,750.00 and 43,750.00, a `gross_spread_total_usd` of 87,500.00, and a `gross_spread_per_bond_usd` of 8.750. A competitive deal with `par_amount_usd` of 10,000,000, a `winning_bid_price_pct` of 106.000, and an `issue_price_total_usd` of 10,711,700.00 has bid proceeds of 10,600,000.00, a `gross_spread_total_usd` of 111,700.00, and a `gross_spread_per_bond_usd` of 11.170.

Third the **premium and net proceeds**, computed exactly as set out in section 3.3 and single-pass by construction: the premium is charged against insured debt service, never against proceeds, and net proceeds are never grossed up and re-fed into the premium. `Deal.net_proceeds_to_issuer_usd` must come out strictly greater than zero, or the whole pricing is refused with `ERR_PREMIUM_EXCEEDS_PROCEEDS`. The negotiated deal above with one 5,000,000 tranche at a 5.000 coupon and twenty periods insured at 45 basis points has insured debt service of 7,500,000.00, a premium of 33,750.00, and net proceeds of 10,590,450.00; the competitive uninsured deal above has a `credit_enhancement_premium_usd` of 0.00 and net proceeds of 10,600,000.00. After every write that touches these fields the money identity must hold: `issue_price_total_usd` equals `net_proceeds_to_issuer_usd` plus `gross_spread_total_usd` plus `credit_enhancement_premium_usd`.

Fourth the **arbitrage yield**. Each maturity tranche contributes bond years equal to its `principal_amount_usd` divided by one thousand, multiplied by its `periods_to_maturity`, divided by two. `Deal.arbitrage_yield_pct` is the bond-year-weighted average of the tranches' `yield_pct` — the sum over tranches of `yield_pct` multiplied by that tranche's bond years, divided by the total bond years — rounded to four decimal places. Fifth the **takedown reconciliation**. For each syndicate member of the deal whose `status` is `ACTIVE`, and for each of that member's orders whose `status` is `ALLOTTED` or `PARTIALLY_ALLOTTED` taken in ascending `submitted_at` then ascending `created_at`, the order's credit is its `par_allotted_usd` divided by one thousand and multiplied by `Deal.takedown_per_bond_usd`, rounded to two decimal places; the member's `takedown_credit_usd` is the sum of those per-order credits, rounded per order and then summed. Orders that are `PENDING`, `UNFILLED`, or `CANCELLED` contribute zero. The same routine also sets each member's `liability_par_usd` to `liability_pct` divided by one hundred and multiplied by `Deal.par_amount_usd`, rounded to two decimal places. With a `takedown_per_bond_usd` of 5.000, a member holding an allotted 2,005,000 and an allotted 1,715,000 earns 18,600.00 while a member holding 1,280,000 and one unfilled order earns 6,400.00, a total of 25,000.00.

The remaining writes differ slightly by branch. On the competitive branch `Deal.winning_bid_price_pct` is stored and `Deal.takedown_per_bond_usd` is set to zero, which is precisely why every syndicate member's `takedown_credit_usd` comes out as 0.00 on that branch even though the reconciliation still runs. On the negotiated branch `Deal.takedown_per_bond_usd`, `Deal.management_fee_per_bond_usd`, and `Deal.expenses_per_bond_usd` are stored as supplied. On both branches every `CLOSED` order period of the deal moves to `SETTLED` and the deal's `version` increments.

The yield-restriction analysis is a separate, read-only projection available to a `ComplianceOfficer` or a `MunicipalAdvisor`. It supplies a `projected_investment_yield_pct`, an `unspent_proceeds_usd`, and a number of `investment_days`, and it returns the deal's identifier, the `arbitrage_yield_pct` computed from the current maturity schedule, a `permitted_investment_yield_pct`, the `projected_investment_yield_pct` as supplied, a `yield_restriction_applies` flag, and an `excess_earnings_usd` figure. It stores nothing whatsoever. The permitted yield is `arbitrage_yield_pct` plus the one-eighth of one percent allowance — that is, plus 0.125 — rounded to four decimal places. The restriction test is **strict**: `yield_restriction_applies` is true only when `projected_investment_yield_pct` is strictly greater than `permitted_investment_yield_pct`, so a projected yield exactly equal to the permitted yield does not trigger restriction. When the restriction applies, `excess_earnings_usd` is `unspent_proceeds_usd` multiplied by the difference between the projected and permitted yields, divided by one hundred, multiplied by `investment_days`, divided by three hundred sixty, rounded to two decimal places; when it does not apply, `excess_earnings_usd` is 0.00. For the two-tranche issue with a `yield_pct` of 4.250 on 5,000,000 over twenty periods and 4.000 on 5,000,000 over forty periods, bond years are 50,000 and 100,000, `arbitrage_yield_pct` is 4.0833 and `permitted_investment_yield_pct` is 4.2083; a `projected_investment_yield_pct` of 4.600 with 5,000,000 of unspent proceeds over 180 `investment_days` gives `yield_restriction_applies` true and `excess_earnings_usd` of 9,792.50, while a `projected_investment_yield_pct` of exactly 4.2083 on the same issue gives `yield_restriction_applies` false and `excess_earnings_usd` of 0.00.

### 3.9. Award, Closing Documents, Post-Issuance Handoff and Cancellation

Award is taken by an `IssuerOfficial` and moves the deal from `PRICED` to `AWARDED`. It has two preconditions. `Deal.net_proceeds_to_issuer_usd` must be strictly greater than zero, or the request is refused with `ERR_PREMIUM_EXCEEDS_PROCEEDS` — the same fiscal floor as pricing, re-checked here because an enhancement commitment or withdrawal may have moved the number in between. And the deal's governing official statement must have `status` `PRELIMINARY_POSTED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_OS_INVALID_STATE`; note that award reports the state error while pricing reports `ERR_DISCLOSURE_NOT_CLEARED` for the same underlying condition, because at award the posting is a state fact about the document rather than a clearance gate on the money.

Award is also the only way closing documents come into existence: they have no creation endpoint of any kind, so the only way to discover them is to list them under their deal. Every award creates five documents with `execution_status` `PENDING` and `is_required` true — a `BOND_OPINION` whose `signatory_role` is `BondCounsel`, a `TAX_CERTIFICATE` whose `signatory_role` is `IssuerOfficial`, an `ISSUE_PRICE_CERTIFICATE` whose `signatory_role` is `Underwriter`, a `CONTINUING_DISCLOSURE_AGREEMENT` whose `signatory_role` is `IssuerOfficial`, and an `UNDERWRITER_RECEIPT` whose `signatory_role` is `Underwriter`. Then the branch flags are read. When `Deal.requires_g17_letter` is true — which is to say on a negotiated deal — award additionally creates a `G17_DISCLOSURE_LETTER` with `signatory_role` `Underwriter` and `is_required` true. When `Deal.requires_notice_of_sale` is true — which is to say on a competitive deal — award additionally creates a `NOTICE_OF_SALE_CERTIFICATE` with `signatory_role` `MunicipalAdvisor` and `is_required` true. The two branches therefore rejoin at the same *count* of six required documents but with different compositions, and the deal's `version` increments. Each new document has `signatory_name`, `executed_at`, and `waiver_reason` all null. Documents can be listed under their deal with optional `doc_type` and `execution_status` filters and read individually.

Executing a document is done by whichever of `BondCounsel`, `IssuerOfficial`, `Underwriter`, or `MunicipalAdvisor` the document names, and the request supplies a `signatory_name`. The document must have `execution_status` `PENDING`, or the request is refused with `ERR_CLOSING_DOC_ALREADY_EXECUTED`. The acting role must equal the document's `signatory_role` exactly, or the request is refused with `ERR_SIGNATORY_ROLE_INVALID` — a `MunicipalAdvisor` may not sign a bond opinion however senior. The deal must be in `AWARDED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_DEAL_INVALID_STATE`. Three further gates then apply by `doc_type`. A `BOND_OPINION` requires the deal's governing official statement to have `status` `FINAL_POSTED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_OS_NOT_FINAL_POSTED`: counsel will not opine until the final disclosure document is on the record. A `TAX_CERTIFICATE` requires `Deal.arbitrage_yield_pct` to be non-null and `Deal.post_issuance_handoff_status` to be `READY`, or the request is refused with `ERR_POST_ISSUANCE_HANDOFF_INCOMPLETE`. A `CONTINUING_DISCLOSURE_AGREEMENT` requires the deal to have a continuing-disclosure undertaking with `status` `ACTIVE`, or the request is refused with `ERR_UNDERTAKING_NOT_ACTIVE`. On success the document becomes the terminal `EXECUTED` with the supplied `signatory_name` stored, `executed_at` set to the current logical clock instant, and its `version` incremented.

Waiving is reserved to a `ComplianceOfficer` and supplies a `waiver_reason`. The document must be `PENDING`, or the request is refused with `ERR_CLOSING_DOC_ALREADY_EXECUTED`, and the deal must be in `AWARDED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_DEAL_INVALID_STATE`. Two document types can never be waived: a `BOND_OPINION` and a `TAX_CERTIFICATE` are the never-waivable pair of section 2.8, and an attempt to waive either is refused with `ERR_SIGNATORY_ROLE_INVALID`. On success the document becomes the terminal `WAIVED` with the supplied reason stored in `waiver_reason` and its `version` incremented. Because closing counts `EXECUTED` and `WAIVED` alike, a waiver is a genuine substitute for execution on the five waivable types and not on the other two.

The post-issuance handoff is a status on the deal rather than a state transition of it, and it is taken by a `ComplianceOfficer` supplying a `record_retention_years` commitment. Four conditions must all hold, and any failure is reported as `ERR_POST_ISSUANCE_HANDOFF_INCOMPLETE`: the deal's `status` must be `PRICED` or `AWARDED`, `Deal.arbitrage_yield_pct` must be non-null, the deal must have a continuing-disclosure undertaking with `status` `ACTIVE`, and `record_retention_years` must be at least six. On success `Deal.post_issuance_handoff_status` moves from `NOT_STARTED` to `READY`, the supplied `record_retention_years` is stored on the deal, and the deal's `version` increments. Because the handoff is available from `PRICED`, it may be completed either before or after award, but it must be completed before the tax certificate can be executed and before the deal can close.

Closing is taken by a `ComplianceOfficer` and moves the deal from `AWARDED` to the terminal `CLOSED`. Three preconditions apply. Every closing document of the deal whose `is_required` is true must have `execution_status` `EXECUTED` or `WAIVED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_CLOSING_DOCS_INCOMPLETE`. The deal's governing official statement must have `status` `FINAL_POSTED`, or the request is refused with `ERR_OS_NOT_FINAL_POSTED`. And `Deal.post_issuance_handoff_status` must be `READY`, or the request is refused with `ERR_POST_ISSUANCE_HANDOFF_INCOMPLETE`. On success the deal's `version` increments and nothing further about the deal may change. A closed deal is not inert to the rest of the system, however: its maturity tranches are exactly the population eligible to be named as a `refunded_tranche_id` by a later refunding series of the same issuer, as described in section 3.2.

Cancellation is taken by an `IssuerOfficial` supplying a `cancellation_reason`, and it is available from `DRAFT`, `STRUCTURING`, `PRICING_COMPETITIVE`, `PRICING_NEGOTIATED`, and `PRICED`. A deal already in `CLOSED` or `CANCELLED` is refused with `ERR_DEAL_INVALID_STATE`; note that an `AWARDED` deal is likewise not cancellable, since the bonds have been awarded to their purchasers. On success the deal moves to the terminal `CANCELLED`, the supplied reason is stored in `Deal.cancellation_reason`, the deal's `version` increments, and a full unwinding cascade runs over every child record. Every official statement of the deal in `DRAFTING`, `IN_REVIEW`, `REVISION_REQUESTED`, or `PRELIMINARY_POSTED` becomes `WITHDRAWN`. Every order of the deal in `PENDING`, `ALLOTTED`, `PARTIALLY_ALLOTTED`, or `UNFILLED` becomes `CANCELLED` with `par_allotted_usd` set to zero and `cancellation_reason` recorded as `DEAL_CANCELLED`. Every order period of the deal in `SCHEDULED`, `OPEN`, or `CLOSED` becomes `CANCELLED`. Every maturity tranche of the deal recomputes `par_allotted_usd` to zero and `par_unsold_usd` to its `principal_amount_usd`, and `Deal.par_allotted_total_usd` becomes zero — the par identity holds across the cancellation exactly as it holds across a reopen. Every rating engagement of the deal in `REQUESTED`, `UNDER_REVIEW`, or `INFO_REQUESTED` becomes `WITHDRAWN`, and every rating presentation with `status` `SCHEDULED` on those engagements becomes `CANCELLED` with `cancellation_reason` recorded as `DEAL_CANCELLED`. Every credit enhancement of the deal with `status` `PROPOSED` becomes `DECLINED`, and one with `status` `COMMITTED` becomes `WITHDRAWN` with `withdrawal_reason` recorded as `DEAL_CANCELLED`. Every entity touched by the cascade has its own `version` incremented.
