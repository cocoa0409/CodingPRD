# Airline Loyalty Partner & Co-Brand Program Economics API — Product Requirements Document

## 1. Introduction

For a modern carrier, the frequent-flyer programme is not a marketing accessory but a balance-sheet business. Miles are manufactured by the airline and sold in bulk to a co-brand card issuer under a negotiated volume-tier price schedule; the cash arrives long before the miles are flown, so every mile sold is simultaneously revenue received and a liability owed to a member who may redeem it years later. This API is the system of record for that business. It has to keep four tightly coupled ledgers mutually consistent at all times: the **mile-sale economics** ledger (agreement tier schedules, provisional order pricing, per-slice final invoicing, and the deferred-revenue balance in cents), the **member mile liability** ledger (earning across flight, card spend and partner activity, promotional stacking, redemption and breakage recognition), the **award seat inventory** ledger (authorised seats, holds, confirmations, load factor and the demand multiplier that determines the price the next booking is quoted at), and the **governance** ledger (negotiation rounds, segregation of duties, programme-change reaction risk, fraud adjudication, and an accounting-period lock that freezes postings).

The commercial pain the system addresses is that these four ledgers are normally maintained in separate spreadsheets by separate departments, and they drift. A partner manager draws a mile purchase order priced off a volume tier that a colleague's order has already exhausted. A revenue accountant recognises breakage against a deferred-revenue balance that a mile posting has already moved. A member holds award seats at a price the window no longer offers because the load factor climbed between quote and confirmation. A fraud analyst freezes an account whose held seats stay stranded in inventory. Every requirement in this document exists to make one of those drifts structurally impossible: derived counters are recomputed at the moment of every event, cross-entity cascades are specified hop by hop, and the three writes that are priced off a parent's live counters demand that the caller prove which version of that parent it read.

Two technical constraints shape the whole surface. First, every temporal decision — hold expiry, campaign windows, trailing accounting windows, notice lead time, agreement terms, clawback lookbacks — reads a **logical clock** that the test harness positions explicitly; the server never consults wall-clock time. Second, all monetary and mileage quantities are integers. Cents, hundredths of a cent ("centicents") and basis points are the only units in the system, and every division is closed by an explicit rounding rule at an explicitly specified point in the calculation, because the difference between rounding per slice and rounding once at the end is a real cent of real revenue.

## 2. Global Specifications & System-Wide Principles

This section defines the contract-level rules that hold across the entire surface. Where a rule here is repeated inside a §3 workflow narrative, the two statements reinforce each other; they never conflict.

### 2.1. User and Role-Based Access Control

Every request, without exception, must carry two headers: `X-User-ID`, identifying the acting principal, and `X-User-Role`, declaring the capacity in which that principal is acting. If either header is absent, or if `X-User-Role` is not exactly one of the seven role names below, the request is rejected with `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). This applies to the logical-clock and epoch-runner utilities as well as to every business collection. Once authenticated, the caller's role must appear in the authorised-role list for the operation being invoked, which §4 tabulates; a role that is authenticated but not authorised is rejected with `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403).

The seven recognised roles are `ProgramAdmin`, `PartnerManager`, `ContractsOfficer`, `RevenueAccountant`, `FraudAnalyst`, `IssuerRepresentative`, and `Member`.

- A `ProgramAdmin` is the loyalty programme operator: it owns programme setup, member lifecycle, promotional campaigns, award inventory, programme-change communication, and the epoch runner.
- A `PartnerManager` is the commercial owner of the co-brand relationship: it drafts, submits and revises mile sale agreements and draws mile purchase orders against them, but it may never ratify what it negotiated.
- A `ContractsOfficer` is the independent contract authority: it ratifies and activates agreements, and exists precisely as the counterparty control to `PartnerManager`.
- A `RevenueAccountant` owns the accounting period, order invoicing and cash settlement, partner settlement runs, and breakage recognition.
- A `FraudAnalyst` opens, investigates and adjudicates fraud cases, subject to the rule that the opener of a case may not be its adjudicator.
- An `IssuerRepresentative` is the co-brand card issuer acting on its own agreements.
- A `Member` is a loyalty programme member acting on their own account.

Caller identity binds to persisted rows differently per role, and the distinction is load-bearing. When `X-User-Role` is `ProgramAdmin`, `PartnerManager`, `ContractsOfficer`, `RevenueAccountant`, or `FraudAnalyst`, `X-User-ID` is an opaque operator identifier that is not tied to any persisted entity; the server may record it — as `negotiated_by_user_id` and `ratified_by_user_id` on a mile sale agreement, and as `opened_by_user_id` and `adjudicated_by_user_id` on a fraud case — but it never compares it to any entity's `id`. When `X-User-Role` is `IssuerRepresentative`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `CoBrandIssuer`; ownership checks on any resource that references a `CoBrandIssuer` compare that resource's `issuer_id` to `X-User-ID`, and a mismatch is rejected with `ERR_ISSUER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (403). When `X-User-Role` is `Member`, `X-User-ID` is the `id` of the acting `MemberAccount`; a `Member` may read or act only on the member account whose `id` equals `X-User-ID`, and on the award bookings, mile ledger rows and fraud cases whose `member_id` equals `X-User-ID`. Any attempt to reach another member's rows is rejected with `ERR_MEMBER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (403). On list endpoints both of these scopes narrow rather than reject: a `Member` listing member accounts, mile ledger rows, award bookings or fraud cases silently sees only its own rows, and an `IssuerRepresentative` listing co-brand issuers, mile sale agreements or mile purchase orders silently sees only those belonging to its own issuer — though a `member_id` filter naming a different member is still refused with `ERR_MEMBER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`. The promotion campaign, award inventory window, partner airline and programme change notice collections carry no per-member ownership and are therefore not narrowed for a `Member` caller at all.

### 2.2. API Response Structure

#### Standard Success Response Format

All successful API responses (those with 2xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format in their JSON body:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z",
    "limit": 10,
    "offset": 0,
    "total_count": 100,
    "has_more": true
  },
  "response_type": "object" | "array",
  "data": { }
}
```

- The `api_request_id` is a server-generated unique identifier for the request that starts with `req_` followed by exactly twenty characters drawn from the upper-case letters and the digits.
- The `api_request_timestamp` is a server-generated ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits. It is read from wall-clock time rather than from the logical clock of §2.6, so it is never expected to agree with `current_utc`.
- The `response_type` is either "object" for a single resource or "array" for a list of resources.
- The `data` field contains the resource object or an array of resource objects. For 2xx responses with no data, it contains an empty object. Unless otherwise noted, operations that create or modify a resource will return the created or updated resource object in the data field.
- Pagination-related fields in `meta` (`limit`, `offset`, `total_count`, `has_more`) are included for list endpoints.

#### Standard Error Response Format

All error API responses (4xx status codes) follow this standard envelope format:

```json
{
  "meta": {
    "api_request_id": "req_RANDOMALPHANUM123456",
    "api_request_timestamp": "2025-05-24T10:30:00.123Z"
  },
  "response_type": "error",
  "data": {
    "error_id": "UNIQUE_ERROR_IDENTIFIER_STRING",
    "message": "A human-readable explanation of the error."
  }
}
```

- The `meta` object for error responses contains only `api_request_id` and `api_request_timestamp`.
- The `error_id` is a unique, machine-readable string that identifies the specific error.
- The `message` provides a human-readable explanation of what went wrong.

### 2.3. Pagination and List Filtering

List endpoints are paginated through two query parameters. `limit` caps the number of rows returned; when omitted it defaults to 10, its minimum accepted value is 1 and its maximum is 100. `offset` skips rows from the front of the result set and defaults to 0. A `limit` or `offset` that is not an integer, a `limit` outside 1 through 100, or a negative `offset` is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400); an empty-string value for either is treated as though the parameter had been omitted. Unless a specific endpoint says otherwise, list results are ordered by `created_at` ascending. Every list response carries `limit`, `offset`, `total_count` and `has_more` inside the `meta` object of the envelope, where `total_count` counts the rows matching the filters before pagination is applied and `has_more` states whether any matching row remains beyond the current page.

Beyond pagination, each collection accepts filters named exactly for the fields they narrow, and all of them are optional. Loyalty programmes accept no filter beyond pagination. Co-brand issuers filter by `program_id` and `status`. Mile sale agreements filter by `issuer_id`, `supersedes_agreement_id` and `status`. Mile purchase orders filter by `agreement_id`, `fulfillment_status` and `settlement_status`. Member accounts filter by `program_id`, `elite_tier` and `security_state`. Mile ledger rows filter by `member_id`, `promotion_id`, `award_booking_id`, `reverses_transaction_id` and `txn_type`. Promotion campaigns filter by `program_id`, `earn_channel` and `status`. Award inventory windows filter by `program_id`, `route_code`, `cabin` and `capacity_control_state`. Award bookings filter by `member_id`, `window_id`, `partner_airline_id` and `status`. Partner airlines filter by `program_id` and `reciprocity_mode`. Programme change notices filter by `program_id` and `status`. Fraud cases filter by `member_id`, `status` and `case_type`. Deferred revenue postings filter by `program_id` and `period`. Every filter is matched as an exact string and is not validated against the enumeration it narrows, so a value that matches nothing yields an empty page with a `total_count` of 0 rather than an error, and an empty-string value is treated as absent; filtering happens before the caller's own scope narrowing described in §2.1.

### 2.4. Common Resource Properties and Immutable Ledger Rows

Every resource in the system carries an `id`: a server-minted UUID with no type prefix and no client control over its value. Every resource also carries `created_at`, an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp with three fractional digits recording when the row was inserted. That stamp, and the `updated_at` stamp described below, are read from wall-clock time rather than from the logical clock of §2.6, so repositioning the clock neither changes them nor reorders a `created_at` ascending listing.

That rendering is the system-wide serialisation form for instants, not a peculiarity of `created_at`. Every field whose value is an instant rather than a calendar date — `created_at` and `updated_at`, a booking's `hold_expires_at`, a campaign's `window_start` and `window_end`, a notice's `announced_at`, `effective_at` and `grandfather_until`, a member's `status_match_expires_at`, a partner's `last_settlement_run_at`, and the `current_utc` and `epoch_at` reported by the two test-driving utilities — is emitted as ISO 8601 UTC with exactly three fractional digits and a trailing `Z`, including when the fractional part is zero, so an instant on a whole second renders as `2026-08-04T00:00:00.000Z` and never as `2026-08-04T00:00:00Z`. An instant supplied by the client is accepted in ISO 8601 form with or without an explicit offset — a bare instant carrying no offset is read as UTC, and one carrying an offset is converted to UTC — and is thereafter stored and echoed back in that same normalised three-digit form; an instant-valued field that has no value is rendered as `null`. Fields that are calendar dates (`term_start_date`, `term_end_date`, `window_start_date`, `window_end_date`, `activity_date`, `travel_date`) stay plain `YYYY-MM-DD` strings, and accounting periods (`current_period`, `period`, `posted_period`) stay `YYYY-MM` strings; neither is ever widened into an instant.

Most resources additionally carry two mutation-tracking fields. `updated_at` is an ISO 8601 UTC timestamp, again with three fractional digits, refreshed on every change to the row — including changes to purely derived counters such as `drawn_miles`, `mile_balance`, `load_factor_bps` or `deferred_revenue_cents`. `version` is an integer that increments by exactly one on every such change. The unit of change is the request: a row that one request modifies several times — an agreement that is both ratified and credited with a swept prepayment balance, say — still advances its `version` by exactly one and carries a single refreshed `updated_at`. Both fields are present on `LoyaltyProgram`, `CoBrandIssuer`, `MileSaleAgreement`, `MilePurchaseOrder`, `MemberAccount`, `PromotionCampaign`, `AwardInventoryWindow`, `AwardBooking`, `PartnerAirline`, `ProgramChangeNotice` and `FraudCase`.

Two resources are append-only ledger rows and are never modified after insertion: a mile ledger row (`MileTransaction`) and a deferred revenue posting (`DeferredRevenuePosting`). Both carry only `id` and `created_at`; on these two resources `updated_at` and `version` are absent entirely, and no operation exists that would edit them. Corrections to the mile ledger are made by inserting a further row — a reversal or an adjustment — never by rewriting history.

### 2.5. Units, Precision and Rounding

Three integer units carry all quantitative meaning in this system, and no field is ever a decimal number.

- **Cents.** Every cash amount is a whole number of cents in the programme's `currency_code`, which defaults to `USD` and must be a three-letter ISO-4217 code — the check is structural, so any three ASCII letters are accepted and stored upper-cased, without validation against the real ISO register. Field names ending in `_cents` are cash.
- **Centicents.** Mile prices and partner settlement rates are quoted in hundredths of a cent per mile, because a mile costs on the order of one and a half cents and whole cents would be uselessly coarse. One cent is 100 centicents, so a `price_per_mile_centicents` of 160 means 1.60 cents per mile. Field names ending in `_centicents` or `_centicents_per_mile` are centicent-denominated.
- **Basis points.** Every rate, share, multiplier, discount and uplift is expressed in basis points, where 10000 basis points is exactly 1.0 (100%), 2500 is 0.25, and 16000 is 1.6. Field names ending in `_bps` are basis points.

Wherever a division occurs, the result is rounded **half up** to the whole unit named in the surrounding rule — whole cents for cash, whole miles for mileage, whole basis points for rates, and whole points for the two 0-to-100 index scores. Half up means that a value exactly on the half boundary rounds away from zero to the larger magnitude. The **position** of the rounding step is itself part of the specification and is never at the implementer's discretion: mile purchase order invoicing rounds once per tier slice and only then sums; award pricing rounds the per-seat price before multiplying by the seat count; partner settlement rounds once per booking before accumulating into the running balance; the promotional multiplier's `MULTIPLICATIVE` mode rounds the tier-boosted subtotal before applying the promotional factor. Each of these is restated at the point of use in §3, and summing exact fractional products and rounding once at the end is explicitly **not** the rule in any of them.

### 2.6. The Logical Clock

The server maintains a logical clock that is the sole source of "now" for the entire system. At startup the clock reads exactly `2026-08-01T00:00:00Z`, and it never falls back to wall-clock time. An operator with any role other than `Member` or `IssuerRepresentative` may reposition it by supplying a `to_iso8601` instant, and the response reports the resulting `current_utc`. Repositioning the clock mutates no business entity by itself; time-driven state changes happen only when the epoch runner is invoked, as described in §3.1.

Every temporal comparison in this document — a hold expiring, a campaign window opening or closing, a status-match trial lapsing, an agreement term ending, a notice becoming effective, a trailing accounting window, a clawback lookback, a settlement eligibility date — reads this logical clock. Where a rule compares an instant to a calendar date, the date is interpreted at `00:00:00Z`.

### 2.7. The Accounting Period and the Posting Lock

Each loyalty programme posts into exactly one accounting period at a time, named in `current_period` as a `YYYY-MM` string and defaulting to `2026-08`. The programme's `revenue_recognition_state` describes its posture within that period and cycles through three values: `OPEN`, `CLOSING` and `LOCKED`. In `OPEN` the ledger accepts mile postings freely but breakage may not be recognised — an attempt to create a deferred revenue posting while the programme is `OPEN` is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409) at rung 8 of §2.9's ladder. In `CLOSING` the period is being finalised and a deferred revenue posting becomes permitted. In `LOCKED` the period's books are shut: every ledger-moving operation is refused with `ERR_PERIOD_LOCKED` (409). Precisely six operations are ledger-moving for this purpose — posting a mile ledger row, invoicing a mile purchase order, ticketing an award booking, refunding an award booking, creating a deferred revenue posting, and substantiating a fraud case. Reads, holds, cancellations, agreement negotiation and settlement runs are unaffected by the lock.

Only a `RevenueAccountant` may move the posture, and only along the cycle described in §3.9. The transition out of `LOCKED` back to `OPEN` is also the only thing that advances `current_period`, which it does by exactly one calendar month.

### 2.8. Stale-Version Protection on Ledger-Anchored Writes

Four create operations are priced or measured off a parent row's live counters, and each therefore requires the client to prove which version of that parent it last read. The requirement is a non-optional input field on the request body, and the field name states which parent it refers to.

- Creating a mile purchase order requires `expected_agreement_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `MileSaleAgreement`.
- Creating an award booking hold requires `expected_window_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `AwardInventoryWindow`.
- Creating a deferred revenue posting requires `expected_program_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `LoyaltyProgram`.
- Creating a programme change notice requires `expected_program_version`, carrying the client's last observed `version` of the target `LoyaltyProgram`.

In each case, if the supplied value does not equal the parent's current `version`, the write is rejected with `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412) and nothing is mutated. The rationale is specific to each parent. Every issued order moves the agreement's `drawn_miles`, which is the anchor for the tier price the next order will be quoted at, so a client working from a stale agreement would be priced off a tier that no longer applies. An award window's `load_factor_bps` and `demand_multiplier_bps` move on every seat event and on every capacity-control change, so a client working from a stale window would lock in a price the window no longer offers. A deferred revenue posting snapshots `outstanding_miles` and `deferred_revenue_cents`, both of which move on mile postings, order issuance and prior postings, so a client working from a stale programme would recognise breakage against a balance that no longer exists. A programme change notice is announced against a programme-wide member exposure count, so its creation is likewise anchored to a programme version — and creating the notice itself increments `LoyaltyProgram.version` by one.

A second order on the same agreement, a second hold on the same window, and a posting for a different period are all perfectly legitimate acts in themselves. That is exactly why these conflicts are resolved by a stale-version check rather than by a state precondition. No other endpoint accepts a version field of any kind, and none may be required to.

### 2.9. The Validation Ladder

Every request is validated against an ordered ladder. Checks are evaluated in ascending order, and **the first check that fails is the response** — no later check runs, and no partial mutation is retained. This ordering is normative: when two conditions on a request are both violated, the reported `error_id` is the one belonging to the lower-numbered rung. The ladder is:

1. `X-User-ID` is present, `X-User-Role` is present, and `X-User-Role` is one of the seven declared roles — otherwise `ERR_UNAUTHENTICATED` (401). Applies to every endpoint, including the logical-clock and epoch utilities.
2. The caller's role is among the endpoint's authorised roles — otherwise `ERR_FORBIDDEN_ROLE` (403). Applies to every endpoint.
3. A `Member` caller acts only within its own scope, as defined in §2.1 — otherwise `ERR_MEMBER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (403). A `memberId` path parameter, a body-supplied `member_id` and a `member_id` list filter are all judged here, before they are resolved, so a `Member` naming a different identifier is refused with the scope violation even when no such row exists. The ownership of an award booking, a mile ledger row or a fraud case can only be read off the row itself, so for those three the not-found check at rung 5 necessarily runs first and a `Member` naming a row that does not exist receives that row's 404.
4. An `IssuerRepresentative` caller acts only on mile sale agreements whose `issuer_id` equals `X-User-ID` — otherwise `ERR_ISSUER_SCOPE_VIOLATION` (403). Applies to countering an agreement and to issuer-scoped reads, which are reading a co-brand issuer, reading a mile sale agreement, and reading a mile purchase order (compared through its agreement's `issuer_id`).
5. Every path-parameter identifier resolves to an existing row, checked left to right along the path — otherwise the 404 not-found code for the first identifier that fails to resolve.
6. Every foreign key supplied in the request body resolves to an existing row, checked in the order the fields are declared for that operation — otherwise the corresponding 404 not-found code.
7. The request body is well formed: required fields present, enumerated fields within their declared value sets, integers within their declared bounds, dates and instants in ISO 8601 form, and `tier_schedule` shaped as an array of objects each carrying `threshold_miles` and `price_per_mile_centicents` whose first entry has a `threshold_miles` of 0, whose `threshold_miles` values strictly increase, and whose `price_per_mile_centicents` values strictly decrease. Violations yield `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST` (400). Two bounds are deliberately excluded from this rung because dedicated rungs further down own them: an out-of-range `seats` value on an award booking must surface as `ERR_SEAT_COUNT_INVALID` at rung 26, and an inverted date interval must surface as `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL` at rung 14. Neither may ever be reported as `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`.
8. The target row is in a state from which the requested transition exists, per the state machines described throughout §3 — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409). This rung also carries the parent-state preconditions of create operations: the issuer must be `ACTIVE`, the agreement must be `ACTIVE` before an order may be drawn against it, a referenced campaign must be `LIVE`, a booking must be `HELD` before it is ticketed or cancelled, and a fraud case must be in the status its transition demands.
9. The supplied `expected_agreement_version`, `expected_window_version` or `expected_program_version` equals the parent's current `version` — otherwise `ERR_STALE_VERSION` (412), as specified in §2.8.
10. The ratifying `ContractsOfficer`'s `X-User-ID` differs from the agreement's `negotiated_by_user_id` — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (409).
11. The adjudicating `FraudAnalyst`'s `X-User-ID` differs from the case's `opened_by_user_id` — otherwise `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES` (409). Applies to both substantiating and dismissing a case.
12. The member's `security_state` is `NORMAL` — otherwise `ERR_MEMBER_FROZEN` (409). Applies to exactly four operations: posting a mile ledger row, creating an award booking hold, ticketing an award booking, and granting a status match.
13. The programme's `revenue_recognition_state` is not `LOCKED` — otherwise `ERR_PERIOD_LOCKED` (409). Applies to exactly six operations: posting a mile ledger row, invoicing a mile purchase order, ticketing an award booking, refunding an award booking, creating a deferred revenue posting, and substantiating a fraud case.
14. Every date interval is correctly ordered: `term_start_date` is not after `term_end_date`, `window_start_date` is not after `window_end_date`, and `window_start` is strictly before `window_end` — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_INTERVAL` (422). Applies to creating a mile sale agreement, a promotion campaign, and an award inventory window.
15. A newly created promotion campaign or award inventory window does not open in the past: its `window_start`, or its `window_start_date` read at `00:00:00Z`, is at or after the logical clock's current instant — otherwise `ERR_WINDOW_IN_PAST` (422).
16. A programme change notice is schedulable: `effective_at` is strictly after the logical clock's now, and `grandfather_until`, when supplied, is at or after `effective_at` — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_NOTICE_SCHEDULE` (422). Applies both to creating a notice and to announcing one.
17. An agreement term is at least 90 whole days long, measured from `term_start_date` to `term_end_date`, and `term_start_date` is not earlier than the logical clock's calendar date — otherwise `ERR_INVALID_TERM` (422).
18. An award booking's `travel_date` falls inside the window's closed date interval, meaning at or after `window_start_date` and at or before `window_end_date` — otherwise `ERR_TRAVEL_DATE_OUT_OF_WINDOW` (422).
19. A new campaign's window does not overlap an existing `SCHEDULED` or `LIVE` campaign sharing the same `program_id` and `earn_channel` — otherwise `ERR_PROMOTION_WINDOW_OVERLAP` (409).
20. A new award inventory window leaves at least two clear days between its closed date interval and that of every existing window sharing the same `program_id`, `route_code` and `cabin` — otherwise `ERR_AWARD_WINDOW_BUFFER_VIOLATION` (409).
21. A new agreement's closed term does not overlap the term of any agreement of the same issuer whose `status` is `DRAFT`, `ISSUER_REVIEW`, `COUNTERED`, `RATIFIED` or `ACTIVE`, unless the new agreement supersedes that very agreement — otherwise `ERR_AGREEMENT_TERM_OVERLAP` (409).
22. `supersedes_agreement_id`, when supplied, names an agreement of the same issuer whose `status` is `RATIFIED` or `ACTIVE`, which is not already superseded by another agreement and is not the new agreement itself — otherwise `ERR_SUPERSEDE_TARGET_INVALID` (422).
23. A referenced campaign's `earn_channel` corresponds to the ledger row's `txn_type`, and the campaign's window contains the row's `activity_date` — otherwise `ERR_PROMOTION_CHANNEL_MISMATCH` (422).
24. The partner airline's `reciprocity_mode` is not `SUSPENDED` — otherwise `ERR_RECIPROCITY_SUSPENDED` (409). Applies both to requesting a settlement run and to creating an award booking that names that airline.
25. The award window's `capacity_control_state` is not `CLOSED` — otherwise `ERR_AWARD_WINDOW_CLOSED` (409).
26. `seats` is at least 1 and at most 9 — otherwise `ERR_SEAT_COUNT_INVALID` (422).
27. Every cross-entity reference in one request belongs to the same loyalty programme — the member, the window, the partner airline, the campaign, the notice and the posting must all share one `program_id` — otherwise `ERR_CROSS_PROGRAM_REFERENCE` (422). Applies to creating an award booking and to posting a mile ledger row.
28. The window has raw capacity: `seats_held` plus `seats_confirmed` plus the requested `seats` is at most `authorized_seats` — otherwise `ERR_AWARD_SEATS_UNAVAILABLE` (409).
29. For a `SAVER` booking, `saver_seats_consumed` plus the requested `seats` is at most the window's saver allotment, which is `authorized_seats` multiplied by `saver_seat_share_bps` divided by 10000 and rounded half up — otherwise `ERR_SAVER_ALLOTMENT_EXHAUSTED` (409).
30. The booking's hold is still live: `hold_expires_at` is strictly after the logical clock's now — otherwise `ERR_HOLD_EXPIRED` (409). Applies to ticketing.
31. The member's `mile_balance` is at least the miles being debited — `quoted_miles` when ticketing, the supplied `base_miles` magnitude on a `REDEEM`, `EXPIRY` or `ADJUSTMENT` ledger row — otherwise `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_MILES` (402).
32. The agreement's `drawn_miles` plus the requested `order_miles` is at most 110% of `committed_volume_miles`, computed as `committed_volume_miles` multiplied by 11000 divided by 10000 and rounded half up — otherwise `ERR_VOLUME_COMMITMENT_EXCEEDED` (409).
33. The issuer's `status` is `ACTIVE` — otherwise `ERR_ISSUER_SUSPENDED` (409). Applies to submitting an agreement, activating an agreement, and creating a mile purchase order.
34. At ratification, the issuer's `aggregate_committed_miles` net of any agreement this one supersedes, plus this agreement's `committed_volume_miles`, is at most `annual_mile_purchase_cap_miles` — otherwise `ERR_ISSUER_CAP_EXCEEDED` (409).
35. A notice being announced has a `member_reaction_index` below 70, or else has a non-null `grandfather_until` — otherwise `ERR_REACTION_RISK_EXCEEDED` (409).
36. At most one deferred revenue posting exists per `program_id` and `period` pair — otherwise `ERR_PERIOD_ALREADY_POSTED` (409).

### 2.10. Cascade Propagation and Derived-Counter Recomputation

No derived counter in this system is ever left to a batch job. Aggregates are recomputed synchronously, inside the request that caused them to change, and every entity whose row is touched has its `updated_at` refreshed and its `version` incremented by one. A recomputation that lands on the value a counter already held is not a change: adding zero miles or zero cents to a counter, or recomputing an issuer aggregate that has not moved, leaves that row's `updated_at` and `version` alone. A single request therefore commonly mutates three or four rows: ticketing an award booking writes the booking, inserts a mile ledger row, debits the member, debits the programme and re-postures the window.

Cascades propagate hop by hop, and each hop is triggered by the hop before it rather than by the original request. The two deepest chains are ratifying a superseding agreement (which terminates the superseded agreement, sweeps its prepayment balance, recomputes the issuer aggregate, and may suspend the issuer) and substantiating a fraud case (which claws back miles, inserts an adjustment row, freezes or closes the member, cancels that member's held bookings, releases the seats, re-postures each affected window, and reduces programme-wide outstanding miles). Both chains are specified hop by hop in §3, and every hop is mandatory: an implementation that performs the primary effect and skips a secondary one leaves the four ledgers inconsistent.

Where a rule refers to an entity's aggregate — the issuer's `aggregate_committed_miles`, the agreement's `drawn_miles` and `prepayment_balance_miles`, the member's `mile_balance`, `lifetime_miles`, `qualifying_miles_ytd`, `qualifying_segments_ytd` and `qualifying_spend_cents_ytd`, the campaign's `accrued_bonus_miles`, the window's `seats_held`, `seats_confirmed`, `saver_seats_consumed` and `load_factor_bps`, the partner's `net_settlement_balance_cents` and `settled_booking_count`, or the programme's `outstanding_miles`, `deferred_revenue_cents`, `last_breakage_rate_bps` and `lifetime_breakage_income_cents` — that aggregate is defined by the rows beneath it and must equal that definition after every request.

### 2.11. Path Resolution, Foreign Keys and Programme Boundaries

Path identifiers are resolved strictly left to right, and the first identifier that does not resolve determines the error. Each identifier has its own not-found code: `programId` yields `ERR_PROGRAM_NOT_FOUND`, `issuerId` yields `ERR_ISSUER_NOT_FOUND`, `agreementId` yields `ERR_AGREEMENT_NOT_FOUND`, `orderId` yields `ERR_ORDER_NOT_FOUND`, `memberId` yields `ERR_MEMBER_NOT_FOUND`, `transactionId` yields `ERR_TRANSACTION_NOT_FOUND`, `campaignId` yields `ERR_PROMOTION_NOT_FOUND`, `windowId` yields `ERR_AWARD_WINDOW_NOT_FOUND`, `bookingId` yields `ERR_BOOKING_NOT_FOUND`, `airlineId` yields `ERR_PARTNER_NOT_FOUND`, `noticeId` yields `ERR_NOTICE_NOT_FOUND`, `caseId` yields `ERR_FRAUD_CASE_NOT_FOUND`, and `postingId` yields `ERR_POSTING_NOT_FOUND`.

The same codes serve body-supplied foreign keys, which are resolved in the order the fields are declared for the operation: `program_id` resolves to a programme, `issuer_id` to an issuer, `agreement_id` and `supersedes_agreement_id` to agreements, `member_id` to a member account, `reverses_transaction_id` to a mile ledger row, `promotion_id` to a campaign, `window_id` to an award inventory window, `award_booking_id` to an award booking, and `partner_airline_id` to a partner airline.

Programme boundaries are hard. The system is multi-tenant by programme, and no request may combine rows belonging to two different programmes — a booking whose member and window sit in different programmes, or a ledger row whose member and campaign do, is rejected with `ERR_CROSS_PROGRAM_REFERENCE`.

## 3. Business Logic & Workflows

### 3.1. Programme Foundation, Counterparties and Epoch Advancement

A `ProgramAdmin` opens the system by creating a **loyalty programme**, supplying a `name` of at most 120 characters and, optionally, four settings that shape everything downstream: the settlement `currency_code`, the starting `current_period`, the `breakage_policy_mode` that will be used whenever a deferred revenue posting omits its own method, and `saver_share_default_bps`, the default share of authorised award seats reservable at the cheaper fare. When omitted, `currency_code` is `USD`, `current_period` is `2026-08`, `breakage_policy_mode` is `HISTORICAL_ATTRITION` (the alternative being `FORWARD_EXPECTATION`), and `saver_share_default_bps` is 3000 — thirty percent of authorised seats — which must lie between 0 and 10000 inclusive. A fresh programme starts with `revenue_recognition_state` at `OPEN` and all four of its ledger counters at zero: `outstanding_miles`, `deferred_revenue_cents`, `last_breakage_rate_bps` and `lifetime_breakage_income_cents`.

Those four counters are the programme's whole economic picture and each has a precise definition. `outstanding_miles` is the member-held mile liability: the sum of `total_miles` across every mile ledger row belonging to a member of this programme, taking every `txn_type` and respecting the sign of each row, recomputed on every insert. `deferred_revenue_cents` is unrecognised cash received from co-brand mile sales; it is credited when a mile purchase order is issued, adjusted by the volume true-up when that order is invoiced, and debited when breakage is recognised. `last_breakage_rate_bps` echoes the `breakage_rate_bps` of the most recent deferred revenue posting for the programme, ordered by `created_at` ascending so that the newest row wins. `lifetime_breakage_income_cents` is the running sum of `breakage_income_cents` across every posting the programme has ever made.

Two kinds of counterparty are registered under a programme. A **co-brand issuer** is created by a `ProgramAdmin` or a `PartnerManager` with a `program_id`, a non-empty legal `name`, and an `annual_mile_purchase_cap_miles` of at least one mile, which is the hard ceiling on miles the issuer may commit to across all its ratified and active agreements. Its `aggregate_committed_miles` starts at zero and is thereafter defined as the sum of `committed_volume_miles` over this issuer's agreements whose `status` is `RATIFIED` or `ACTIVE`, recomputed whenever an agreement of that issuer is ratified, activated, terminated or expired. Its trading posture lives in `status`, which starts `ACTIVE` and may become `SUSPENDED`, paired with a `suspension_reason` that starts `NONE` and becomes `CAP_BREACH` when suspension is automatic. A `SUSPENDED` issuer blocks three things: submitting a new agreement, activating an agreement, and drawing a new mile purchase order — all with `ERR_ISSUER_SUSPENDED`.

A **partner airline** is likewise registered by a `ProgramAdmin` or a `PartnerManager` under a `program_id`, with an `iata_code` — conventionally the two-letter IATA designator, though the server checks only that it is a non-empty string and does not enforce uniqueness within the programme — and a `settlement_rate_centicents_per_mile` of at least one, being the cash the programme owes the partner for each redeemed mile. Two optional enumerations govern the relationship. `reciprocity_mode` defaults to `FULL` and may also be `INBOUND_ONLY`, `OUTBOUND_ONLY` or `SUSPENDED`; it sets the netting factor applied during settlement and, when `SUSPENDED`, blocks both settlement runs and new bookings naming the partner. `award_pricing_mode` defaults to `FIXED_CHART` and may also be `DISTANCE_BAND` or `DYNAMIC_PASSTHROUGH`; it determines how partner award miles are uplifted over the own-metal chart. A newly registered partner has `net_settlement_balance_cents` and `settled_booking_count` at zero and a null `last_settlement_run_at`.

Time-driven state changes are gathered into a single, explicitly invoked sweep. A `ProgramAdmin` names a `program_id` and the system advances every clock-driven transition for that programme at the current logical-clock instant, reporting what it did: the `epoch_at` instant it ran at, plus counts named `holds_expired`, `campaigns_launched`, `campaigns_ended`, `status_matches_lapsed`, `notices_advanced`, `agreements_expired` and `windows_recomputed`. Within one sweep, award booking holds whose `hold_expires_at` is at or before now move `HELD → EXPIRED` and release their seats; campaigns whose `window_start` has arrived move `SCHEDULED → LIVE` and campaigns whose `window_end` has arrived move `LIVE → ENDED`; member accounts whose `status_match_expires_at` is at or before now move `TRIAL → LAPSED`; programme change notices advance `ANNOUNCED → IN_GRANDFATHER`, `ANNOUNCED → EFFECTIVE` or `IN_GRANDFATHER → EFFECTIVE` as their dates dictate; active agreements whose `term_end_date` now lies before the clock's calendar date move `ACTIVE → EXPIRED`, which drops them out of the ratified-and-active set and therefore recomputes the issuer's `aggregate_committed_miles`; and every award inventory window recomputes `load_factor_bps`, `demand_multiplier_bps` and `capacity_control_state`. Two counting details follow from that. `windows_recomputed` counts every award window of the programme the sweep visited, whether or not any of the three values actually moved, while a window's `version` advances only when one of them did. And a campaign whose entire window already lies in the past both launches and ends inside the same sweep, counting once in `campaigns_launched` and once in `campaigns_ended`.

### 3.2. Negotiating and Ratifying a Mile Sale Agreement

The commercial heart of the co-brand relationship is the **mile sale agreement**, and it is negotiated over a cycle rather than approved in one shot. A `PartnerManager` drafts one against an `issuer_id`, giving the term as `term_start_date` and `term_end_date` (inclusive calendar dates), the `committed_volume_miles` the issuer undertakes to buy over that term (at least one mile), and a `tier_schedule` of volume price tiers. Optionally it also names a `supersedes_agreement_id`, which is null for a first-generation agreement and otherwise identifies the predecessor this contract will replace. The schedule is an array of at least one entry, each entry carrying a non-negative integer `threshold_miles` and a non-negative integer `price_per_mile_centicents`; the first entry's `threshold_miles` must be 0, thresholds must strictly increase down the array, and prices must strictly decrease — the issuer buys more cheaply the deeper it draws. A drafted agreement starts in `DRAFT` with `negotiation_round` at 1, both counter fields (`countered_price_per_mile_centicents` and `countered_committed_volume_miles`) null, `negotiated_by_user_id` already stamped with the drafting `PartnerManager`'s `X-User-ID` rather than left null, `ratified_by_user_id` null, and `drawn_miles` and `prepayment_balance_miles` at zero. Crucially, a `DRAFT` agreement does not yet count toward the issuer's aggregate.

Three temporal and structural guards fire at drafting. The term must be correctly ordered and at least 90 whole days long, and it must not begin before today's date on the logical clock — a short or backdated term is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_TERM`. The term must not overlap the term of any other agreement of the same issuer that is still alive in `DRAFT`, `ISSUER_REVIEW`, `COUNTERED`, `RATIFIED` or `ACTIVE`, the sole exception being the very agreement this one supersedes; an overlap raises `ERR_AGREEMENT_TERM_OVERLAP`. And a supplied `supersedes_agreement_id` must point at an agreement of the same issuer that is currently `RATIFIED` or `ACTIVE`, that no other agreement already supersedes, and that is not this agreement itself; anything else raises `ERR_SUPERSEDE_TARGET_INVALID`.

Submission advances `DRAFT → ISSUER_REVIEW`, is performed by a `PartnerManager`, and requires the issuer to be `ACTIVE`. It re-stamps `negotiated_by_user_id` with the submitting operator's `X-User-ID`, overwriting whatever the drafter left there — the value against which segregation of duties will later be measured. The issuer's own representative may then push back, advancing `ISSUER_REVIEW → COUNTERED` and stamping `countered_price_per_mile_centicents` and `countered_committed_volume_miles` from the request's `counter_price_per_mile_centicents` and `counter_committed_volume_miles`, each an integer of at least zero, with an optional free-text `note` that is accepted and validated but persisted nowhere on the agreement. Only the issuer named on the agreement may do this: an `IssuerRepresentative` whose `X-User-ID` does not equal the agreement's `issuer_id` is refused with `ERR_ISSUER_SCOPE_VIOLATION`.

The airline answers a counter by revising, which returns the agreement `COUNTERED → DRAFT`, replaces `tier_schedule` and `committed_volume_miles` wholesale from the request, increases `negotiation_round` by exactly one, and re-stamps `negotiated_by_user_id` with the revising `PartnerManager`. The two `countered_*` fields are deliberately left standing at the values the issuer wrote, so the last counter stays visible through the next lap. Each lap of `DRAFT → ISSUER_REVIEW → COUNTERED → DRAFT` therefore leaves an audit trail in `negotiation_round`, and the round counter never resets.

Ratification is where the segregation of duties bites and where the deepest cascade in the contract ledger fires. A `ContractsOfficer` ratifies an agreement sitting in `ISSUER_REVIEW`, advancing it `ISSUER_REVIEW → RATIFIED`. Two preconditions guard the act. First, the ratifier's `X-User-ID` must differ from `negotiated_by_user_id`; whoever negotiated the deal may not approve it, and an attempt to do so is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Second, the issuer's `aggregate_committed_miles` — computed net of any agreement this one supersedes, so a straight renewal does not double-count — plus this agreement's `committed_volume_miles` must not exceed `annual_mile_purchase_cap_miles`, or the ratification is refused with `ERR_ISSUER_CAP_EXCEEDED`.

When ratification proceeds it stamps `ratified_by_user_id` from `X-User-ID` and then propagates in three hops. The first hop applies only when `supersedes_agreement_id` is set: the superseded agreement is terminated, `ACTIVE → TERMINATED` for a predecessor already in service and equally `RATIFIED → TERMINATED` for one that was ratified but never activated. The second hop, caused by the first, moves the terminated agreement's entire `prepayment_balance_miles` onto the ratifying agreement and sets the terminated agreement's balance to zero — miles the issuer has already paid for survive the contract change — and recomputes the issuer's `aggregate_committed_miles` over agreements whose `status` is `RATIFIED` or `ACTIVE`. The third hop, caused by the second, tests the newly recomputed aggregate against a utilisation alarm set at 9000 basis points of the cap, that is at ninety percent of `annual_mile_purchase_cap_miles` after rounding half up, with the boundary inclusive: when the recomputed `aggregate_committed_miles` reaches or exceeds that figure, the issuer's `status` becomes `SUSPENDED` and its `suspension_reason` becomes `CAP_BREACH`. An issuer can therefore be tipped into suspension by the very ratification that filled its book, which immediately blocks further submissions, activations and draws.

Activation is the last governed step: a `ContractsOfficer` moves a `RATIFIED` agreement to `ACTIVE`, which is the state in which mile purchase orders become creatable, and this too requires the issuer to be `ACTIVE`. Beyond that, agreements leave the live set in one of two ways. The epoch sweep expires an `ACTIVE` agreement once its `term_end_date` falls before the clock's calendar date, moving it `ACTIVE → EXPIRED`. Or a superseding ratification terminates it, moving it `ACTIVE → TERMINATED`. `EXPIRED` and `TERMINATED` are both terminal, and both recompute the issuer's aggregate on the way out.

### 3.3. Drawing, Invoicing and Reversing Mile Purchase Orders

A **mile purchase order** is the issuer's actual draw against a live agreement, and it is the one place in the system where the same economic event is priced twice by two deliberately different rules. A `PartnerManager` creates an order naming the `agreement_id`, the `order_miles` being bought (at least one mile), and `expected_agreement_version` as described in §2.8. Three guards precede pricing: the agreement must be `ACTIVE`, the issuer must not be `SUSPENDED`, and the cumulative draw must stay within tolerance — the agreement's `drawn_miles` plus the requested `order_miles` may not exceed 110% of `committed_volume_miles`, computed as `committed_volume_miles` multiplied by 11000 divided by 10000 and rounded half up. Breaching that ceiling raises `ERR_VOLUME_COMMITMENT_EXCEEDED` and the pricing calculation never runs at all.

At creation the order snapshots the agreement's live `drawn_miles` into `drawn_miles_before`. Both pricing variants anchor on that snapshot and never on the live value, which is what makes an order's price reproducible after the fact. The **provisional** variant then runs. It selects a single tier: the last entry of the agreement's `tier_schedule` whose `threshold_miles` is at or below `drawn_miles_before`. Because thresholds strictly increase and the first is 0, such a tier always exists. That entry's `price_per_mile_centicents` is stamped as `provisional_tier_price_centicents`, and `gross_amount_cents` is the `order_miles` multiplied by that centicent price, divided by 100 to convert centicents to cents, rounded half up. One tier price is applied to the whole order, chosen purely by where the order **starts**; under this variant an order never straddles tiers. The order lands with `fulfillment_status` at `PENDING` and `settlement_status` at `PENDING`, with `final_amount_cents`, `net_amount_cents`, `posted_period` and `reversal_reason` all null and `volume_true_up_cents` at zero. Creating the order also increments the agreement's `version` by one, which is why a client wanting to place a second order must re-read the agreement first.

The two status dimensions on an order then evolve independently: `fulfillment_status` tracks mile delivery through `PENDING`, `ISSUED` and `REVERSED`, while `settlement_status` tracks cash through `PENDING`, `INVOICED`, `SETTLED` and `WRITTEN_OFF`. Issuing is the `PartnerManager`'s act of actually delivering the miles; it requires `fulfillment_status` to be `PENDING` and the agreement to be `ACTIVE`, and it moves the fulfilment dimension `PENDING → ISSUED`. Its effects reach three rows: the agreement's `drawn_miles` increases by `order_miles`, the agreement's `prepayment_balance_miles` increases by the same `order_miles` (these are miles bought but not yet distributed to members), and the programme's `deferred_revenue_cents` increases by `gross_amount_cents`. Both the order's `version` and the agreement's `version` increment by one.

Invoicing is the `RevenueAccountant`'s act, requires `settlement_status` to be `PENDING` and `fulfillment_status` to be `ISSUED`, and is refused while the programme is `LOCKED`. It moves the settlement dimension `PENDING → INVOICED` and runs the **final** pricing variant, which reprices the same order progressively rather than at a single tier. Conceptually the order occupies the half-open mile interval that begins at `drawn_miles_before` and ends just before `drawn_miles_before` plus `order_miles`. That interval is cut at every `threshold_miles` in the schedule, producing one slice for each tier the order actually traverses; each slice has a mile count and takes the `price_per_mile_centicents` of the tier whose threshold opens it, thresholds being inclusive lower bounds so an interval starting exactly on a threshold opens at that tier. For each slice, the slice's mile count is multiplied by that slice's centicent price, divided by 100, and **rounded half up to whole cents individually**; `final_amount_cents` is then the sum of those already-rounded slice amounts. Summing the exact centicent products first and rounding once at the end is not the rule and can differ by a cent. From there, `volume_true_up_cents` is `final_amount_cents` minus `gross_amount_cents` — signed, and negative whenever marginal slicing beat the provisional single-tier quote — and `net_amount_cents` is set equal to `final_amount_cents`. Invoicing then adds `volume_true_up_cents` to the programme's `deferred_revenue_cents`, which may therefore reduce it, and stamps `posted_period` from the programme's `current_period`. An order that stays entirely inside its starting tier produces exactly one slice, so `final_amount_cents` equals `gross_amount_cents` and `volume_true_up_cents` is zero.

Settling is a bookkeeping acknowledgement by a `RevenueAccountant` that the cash arrived: it requires `settlement_status` to be `INVOICED` and moves it `INVOICED → SETTLED`. Reversal is the compensating path, available to a `PartnerManager` while `fulfillment_status` is `ISSUED`, `settlement_status` is anything other than `SETTLED`, and the agreement still holds at least `order_miles` in `prepayment_balance_miles` — miles that have already been distributed to members cannot be un-bought. Reversal moves the fulfilment dimension `ISSUED → REVERSED` and forces the settlement dimension to `WRITTEN_OFF`, stamps the supplied `reason` into `reversal_reason`, reduces the agreement's `drawn_miles` and `prepayment_balance_miles` by `order_miles` each, and debits the programme's `deferred_revenue_cents` by exactly the cents that were credited: `net_amount_cents` when the order had already reached `INVOICED`, otherwise `gross_amount_cents`. Reversal un-rounds nothing. As with issuance, both the order's `version` and the agreement's `version` increment.

### 3.4. Member Accounts, Mile Ledger Postings and Promotional Campaigns

A `ProgramAdmin` opens a **member account** under a `program_id`, supplying the airline-side `external_member_ref` (expected to be unique within the programme, though the server does not enforce that and will accept a duplicate) and the member's `home_region`, which is one of `AMER`, `EMEA` or `APAC`. Two optional inputs seed the qualification cycle: `cycle_year`, the elite qualification cycle currently accumulating, which defaults to 2026 and is accepted as any integer with no range check, and `rollover_carried_in`, qualifying miles banked from the prior cycle, which defaults to 0 and counts toward tier evaluation. A new account opens at `elite_tier` `BASE`, `security_state` `NORMAL`, `status_match_state` `NONE` and `qualification_state` `ACCUMULATING`, with `status_match_tier` and `status_match_expires_at` null, `awarded_tier_at_close` null, and `qualifying_miles_ytd`, `qualifying_segments_ytd`, `qualifying_spend_cents_ytd`, `rollover_carried_out`, `mile_balance`, `lifetime_miles` and `risk_score` all at zero. `elite_tier` progresses through `BASE`, `SILVER`, `GOLD` and `PLATINUM` in that order of seniority, and drives both earn bonus and award mileage discount. The three status dimensions — `security_state` (`NORMAL`, `WATCH`, `FROZEN`, `CLOSED`), `status_match_state` (`NONE`, `TRIAL`, `CONFIRMED`, `LAPSED`) and `qualification_state` (`ACCUMULATING`, `CLOSED`, `RESTATED`) — evolve independently on the same row; none constrains the others' transitions except where a rule says so explicitly.

The **mile ledger** is the append-only record beneath every account. A `ProgramAdmin` or a `RevenueAccountant` posts a row naming the `member_id`, the `txn_type`, and the `activity_date` on which the underlying activity occurred. Depending on type it also supplies `base_miles`, `qualifying_spend_cents` and `segments` (each defaulting to 0 and each required to be non-negative), and optionally `promotion_id`, `award_booking_id` and `reverses_transaction_id`. Seven `txn_type` values exist: `FLIGHT_EARN`, `CARD_SPEND_EARN`, `PARTNER_EARN`, `REDEEM`, `REVERSAL`, `EXPIRY` and `ADJUSTMENT`. Every row records `elite_tier_at_earn`, a snapshot of the member's `elite_tier` at insert time, and `posted_period`, a snapshot of the programme's `current_period`. Posting is refused when the member's `security_state` is not `NORMAL` (`ERR_MEMBER_FROZEN`) and when the programme is `LOCKED` (`ERR_PERIOD_LOCKED`), and the member and any referenced campaign must belong to the same programme.

For the three earning types the ledger row is computed in seven ordered steps. **First**, base miles are resolved by type: a `FLIGHT_EARN` row takes the supplied `base_miles` and the supplied `segments`, which — like `base_miles` and `qualifying_spend_cents` on every type — simply defaults to 0 when omitted rather than being demanded; a `PARTNER_EARN` row takes the supplied `base_miles`; a `CARD_SPEND_EARN` row ignores `base_miles` entirely and derives its base as the supplied `qualifying_spend_cents` divided by 100 and rounded **down** to a whole mile, which is one base mile per dollar of card spend. The stored row's own `base_miles` field always echoes whatever the caller supplied, so on a `CARD_SPEND_EARN` row it is the ignored input rather than the derived base, and the derived base surfaces only through `total_miles` and `qualifying_miles_credited`. **Second**, the elite tier bonus is computed: the tier bonus rate is 0 basis points at `BASE`, 2500 at `SILVER`, 5000 at `GOLD` and 10000 at `PLATINUM`, so a platinum member's bonus doubles their base, and `tier_bonus_miles` is the base multiplied by that rate divided by 10000, rounded half up. **Third**, the promotional multiplier is resolved: when no `promotion_id` is supplied, `promo_multiplier_bps` is 10000 (a neutral one-times factor) and `stacking_mode_applied` is null; when one is supplied, `promo_multiplier_bps` takes the campaign's `multiplier_bps` and `stacking_mode_applied` takes the campaign's `stacking_mode`.

**Fourth**, a gross total is formed according to the stacking mode, and the four cases genuinely differ. With no promotion at all, the gross total is simply the base plus `tier_bonus_miles`. Under `EXCLUSIVE`, the gross total is the base multiplied by the promotional multiplier divided by 10000 and rounded half up, and `tier_bonus_miles` is forced to zero — the promotion replaces the elite bonus rather than adding to it. Under `ADDITIVE`, the tier rate and the promotional lift are treated as independent percentage adders: the gross total is the base multiplied by the quantity (10000 plus the tier rate plus the amount by which the promotional multiplier exceeds 10000), divided by 10000, rounded half up. Under `MULTIPLICATIVE`, the promotion multiplies an already tier-boosted subtotal, and the rounding happens twice: first the base multiplied by the quantity (10000 plus the tier rate), divided by 10000, rounded half up to whole miles; then that rounded subtotal multiplied by the promotional multiplier, divided by 10000, rounded half up again. The double rounding is deliberate, and it means `MULTIPLICATIVE` can diverge from `ADDITIVE` by more than the pure arithmetic difference between the two formulas.

**Fifth**, the promotional bonus is isolated and capped. The raw bonus is the gross total minus the base minus `tier_bonus_miles`, floored at zero. The campaign's remaining room is its `bonus_cap_miles` minus its `accrued_bonus_miles`, floored at zero, and is treated as unbounded when `bonus_cap_miles` is null. The stamped `bonus_miles` is the smallest of the raw bonus, the campaign's remaining room, and a per-transaction ceiling of 25000 miles per member. **Sixth**, `total_miles` is the base plus `tier_bonus_miles` plus `bonus_miles`. **Seventh**, elite-qualifying credit is computed entirely independently of every multiplier above: a `FLIGHT_EARN` row credits `qualifying_miles_credited` equal to its base and adds the supplied `segments` to the member's `qualifying_segments_ytd`; a `CARD_SPEND_EARN` row credits the lesser of its base and a per-transaction cap of 15000 qualifying miles, and adds the supplied `qualifying_spend_cents` to the member's `qualifying_spend_cents_ytd`; a `PARTNER_EARN` row credits zero qualifying miles. In all three cases `qualifying_miles_credited` is added to the member's `qualifying_miles_ytd`. The insert then adds `total_miles` to the member's `mile_balance` and `lifetime_miles`, adds `total_miles` to the programme's `outstanding_miles`, and adds `bonus_miles` to the campaign's `accrued_bonus_miles`. Note that `lifetime_miles` counts only the three earning types, whereas `mile_balance` and `outstanding_miles` sum every type with its sign, and `mile_balance` is never negative.

Several boundary behaviours follow from the above and are intended. A campaign whose room is already exhausted — `accrued_bonus_miles` equal to `bonus_cap_miles` — yields a `bonus_miles` of zero and the transaction is still created successfully; an earn is never rejected for a spent campaign. A campaign with less room than the raw bonus clips the bonus to exactly the room remaining, driving `accrued_bonus_miles` to precisely `bonus_cap_miles`. And an `EXCLUSIVE` campaign at a neutral multiplier of 10000 basis points produces a gross total equal to the base with `tier_bonus_miles` and `bonus_miles` both zero, which leaves a `PLATINUM` member strictly worse off than having applied no promotion at all.

The four non-earning types bypass all seven steps. Their `total_miles` is taken straight from the caller-supplied `base_miles` magnitude, which is always supplied non-negative, with the sign the type dictates: negative for `REDEEM`, `EXPIRY` and `ADJUSTMENT`, positive for `REVERSAL`. On these rows `tier_bonus_miles`, `bonus_miles` and `qualifying_miles_credited` are all zero, `promo_multiplier_bps` stays at 10000 and `stacking_mode_applied` stays null; any `qualifying_spend_cents` or `segments` the caller happens to supply is echoed on the row but moves no member counter. A `REDEEM`, `EXPIRY` or `ADJUSTMENT` row still cannot drive the balance below zero: if the member's `mile_balance` is below the magnitude being debited the posting is refused with `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_MILES`. `REDEEM` rows created by ticketing and `REVERSAL` rows created by refunding carry `award_booking_id`, and a `REVERSAL` row additionally carries `reverses_transaction_id` naming the row it undoes.

A **promotion campaign** raises earning on exactly one channel for exactly one window. A `ProgramAdmin` schedules one under a `program_id` with a `code` that is expected to be unique within that programme but whose uniqueness the server does not enforce, an `earn_channel` of `FLIGHT`, `CARD_SPEND` or `PARTNER`, a `multiplier_bps` of at least 10000 and at most 50000 (from a neutral one-times factor up to five times), a `stacking_mode` of `EXCLUSIVE`, `ADDITIVE` or `MULTIPLICATIVE`, and the window as `window_start` (inclusive) and `window_end` (exclusive). An optional `bonus_cap_miles` of at least zero caps the programme-wide bonus the campaign may ever award; when null the campaign is unbounded. `accrued_bonus_miles` starts at zero and is thereafter the sum of `bonus_miles` across every ledger row whose `promotion_id` names this campaign.

Two scheduling guards apply at creation. The window must be correctly ordered, with `window_start` strictly before `window_end`, and it must not open before the current logical-clock instant, else `ERR_WINDOW_IN_PAST`. And the half-open window must not overlap that of any `SCHEDULED` or `LIVE` campaign sharing the same `program_id` and `earn_channel`; because the window is half-open, exact adjacency — one campaign's `window_end` equal to the next campaign's `window_start` — is explicitly allowed, while any genuine overlap raises `ERR_PROMOTION_WINDOW_OVERLAP`.

A campaign's `status` begins at `SCHEDULED`. It is the epoch sweep, not the create call, that moves it `SCHEDULED → LIVE` once `window_start` is at or before the clock's now, and `LIVE → ENDED` once `window_end` is at or before now; only a `LIVE` campaign may be referenced as the `promotion_id` on an earning row. A `ProgramAdmin` may also cancel a campaign from either `SCHEDULED` or `LIVE`, stamping the supplied `reason` into `cancellation_reason` and moving `status` to `CANCELLED`. `ENDED` and `CANCELLED` are both terminal, and cancellation deliberately does not claw back bonus miles already accrued.

When an earning row names a campaign, two eligibility checks run beyond the state check. The campaign's `earn_channel` must correspond to the row's `txn_type` — `FLIGHT` with `FLIGHT_EARN`, `CARD_SPEND` with `CARD_SPEND_EARN`, `PARTNER` with `PARTNER_EARN` — and the campaign's half-open window must contain the row's `activity_date`; a channel or window mismatch is rejected with `ERR_PROMOTION_CHANNEL_MISMATCH`. A campaign that is not `LIVE` is instead rejected with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`.

### 3.5. Award Seat Inventory, Dynamic Award Pricing and the Booking Lifecycle

Award availability is authorised in windows. A `ProgramAdmin` creates an **award inventory window** under a `program_id` for one non-empty `route_code` such as JFK-LHR and one `cabin` from `ECONOMY`, `PREMIUM`, `BUSINESS` and `FIRST`, covering the closed travel-date interval from `window_start_date` through `window_end_date` inclusive, with `authorized_seats` (zero or more) being the total award seats released across all fare classes. An optional `saver_seat_share_bps` between 0 and 10000 sets the share of those seats reservable at the cheaper fare; when omitted it falls back to the programme's `saver_share_default_bps`. Beyond correct interval ordering and the no-opening-in-the-past rule, a new window must leave at least two clear days between its closed interval and that of every existing window sharing the same `program_id`, `route_code` and `cabin`; an overlap or an insufficient gap raises `ERR_AWARD_WINDOW_BUFFER_VIOLATION`. A fresh window starts with `seats_held`, `seats_confirmed` and `saver_seats_consumed` at zero, `load_factor_bps` at zero, `capacity_control_state` at `OPEN`, and `demand_multiplier_bps` seeded at 9000 — the rung an empty window occupies on the demand ladder.

Two derived quantities give a window its posture. `load_factor_bps` is `seats_held` plus `seats_confirmed`, multiplied by 10000, divided by `authorized_seats`, rounded half up, and defined as zero when `authorized_seats` is zero. `capacity_control_state` then follows from it against two inclusive lower bounds: a load factor below 7500 basis points (75% sold) is `OPEN`, a load factor from 7500 through 9999 is `CONSTRAINED`, and a load factor of 10000 or more is `CLOSED`. The system recomputes both after every seat movement — a hold being created, ticketed, cancelled, refunded or expired — and again on every epoch sweep, refreshing `demand_multiplier_bps` at the same time and incrementing the window's `version`. The reachable transitions are `OPEN → CONSTRAINED`, `CONSTRAINED → CLOSED`, `CONSTRAINED → OPEN` on seat release, and `CLOSED → CONSTRAINED` on seat release; because the recompute simply writes whichever state the fresh load factor names, one large seat movement may also step `OPEN → CLOSED` outright, or `CLOSED → OPEN` outright on release. A `ProgramAdmin` may additionally pin the posture by hand, naming the target in a `capacity_control_state` field: pinning to `CLOSED` is permitted from `OPEN` or `CONSTRAINED` whenever the state is not already `CLOSED`, and reopening a pinned window to `OPEN` requires both that it currently be `CLOSED` and that its `load_factor_bps` already sit below 7500. Pinning to `CONSTRAINED` is never permitted, and it — like any pin the two rules above disallow — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`. A successful pin writes `capacity_control_state` alone, leaving `load_factor_bps` and `demand_multiplier_bps` exactly as they were while still incrementing the window's `version`. A manual pin is advisory only, in the specific sense that the next system recompute overwrites it. While a window is `CLOSED`, new holds are refused with `ERR_AWARD_WINDOW_CLOSED`.

An **award booking** begins as a hold. A `Member` or a `ProgramAdmin` creates one naming the `member_id`, the `window_id`, the `travel_date`, the `fare_class` (`SAVER` or `FLEX`), and `expected_window_version` per §2.8; `seats` and the optional `partner_airline_id` are both discussed below. The hold survives 72 hours: `hold_expires_at` is stamped as the logical clock's now plus 72 hours, and the booking lands with `status` `HELD` and `partner_settled` false. Before any of that, the request must clear the guards at rungs 18, 24, 25, 26, 27, 28 and 29 of the ladder — `travel_date` inside the window's closed interval, the named partner not `SUSPENDED`, the window not `CLOSED`, `seats` between 1 and 9 inclusive, member and window in the same programme, raw seats available, and, for a `SAVER` request, saver allotment remaining. Note that raw availability and saver allotment are separate ceilings: a `SAVER` request can be refused with `ERR_SAVER_ALLOTMENT_EXHAUSTED` while ordinary seats plainly remain.

Pricing then runs in **exactly seven ordered steps**, each consuming the previous step's output, and no step is ever recomputed after a later step changes something.

1. **Pre-booking load factor.** The window's occupancy is measured *before* the requested seats are added: `seats_held` plus `seats_confirmed`, multiplied by 10000, divided by `authorized_seats`, rounded half up — and zero when `authorized_seats` is zero, which short-circuits rather than dividing by zero.
2. **Demand multiplier.** That pre-booking load factor selects `demand_multiplier_bps_applied` from a four-rung ladder whose lower bounds are inclusive: 9000 basis points below 5000, 10000 from 5000 through 7499, 12500 from 7500 through 8999, and 16000 at 9000 and above. A load factor of exactly 5000, 7500 or 9000 therefore takes the higher rung.
3. **Chart miles.** `chart_miles_per_seat` is read from the award chart by the window's `cabin` and the requested `fare_class`: `ECONOMY` charges 12500 at `SAVER` and 25000 at `FLEX`; `PREMIUM` charges 25000 and 45000; `BUSINESS` charges 50000 and 85000; `FIRST` charges 90000 and 140000.
4. **Partner uplift.** `partner_uplift_bps_applied` is 10000 for own-metal awards, meaning a null `partner_airline_id`. When a partner is named, it is 10000 under `FIXED_CHART`, 11500 under `DISTANCE_BAND`, and — under `DYNAMIC_PASSTHROUGH` — the very `demand_multiplier_bps_applied` from step 2, so the demand multiplier is charged a second time.
5. **Gross per seat.** The gross award miles per seat are `chart_miles_per_seat` multiplied by `demand_multiplier_bps_applied` and by `partner_uplift_bps_applied`, divided by 100000000 (that is, both basis-point factors converted to plain multipliers), rounded half up. A `DYNAMIC_PASSTHROUGH` partner on a window loaded at 9000 basis points or more therefore compounds 16000 basis points twice, at 2.56 times chart.
6. **Surcharge band.** `surcharge_band` is keyed by that gross per-seat figure, pre-discount, against inclusive lower bounds: `SURCHARGE_LOW` below 25000 gross miles, `SURCHARGE_MID` from 25000 through 74999, and `SURCHARGE_HIGH` at 75000 and above, so a gross of exactly 25000 or exactly 75000 takes the higher band. The band also fixes the cash side: `cash_surcharge_cents` is 560 cents per seat at `SURCHARGE_LOW`, 1120 at `SURCHARGE_MID` and 2240 at `SURCHARGE_HIGH`, multiplied by `seats`.
7. **Elite discount.** `elite_discount_bps_applied` is looked up by the member's `elite_tier` and the band just fixed. A `BASE` member receives no discount in any band. A `SILVER` member receives 0 basis points at `SURCHARGE_LOW`, 250 at `SURCHARGE_MID` and 500 at `SURCHARGE_HIGH`. A `GOLD` member receives 250, 500 and 1000 respectively. A `PLATINUM` member receives 500, 1000 and 1500. `quoted_miles` is then the gross per-seat figure multiplied by the quantity (10000 minus `elite_discount_bps_applied`), divided by 10000, rounded half up **per seat**, and only then multiplied by `seats`. Because the band was frozen at step 6, a generous `PLATINUM` discount can pull the per-seat price below the band's own threshold without moving either the band or `cash_surcharge_cents`.

Creating the hold also stamps the partner settlement basis and moves the window. `partner_settlement_cents` is zero for own metal and otherwise `quoted_miles` multiplied by the partner's `settlement_rate_centicents_per_mile`, divided by 100, rounded half up. The window's `seats_held` increases by `seats`, and for a `SAVER` booking `saver_seats_consumed` increases by `seats` as well; `load_factor_bps`, `demand_multiplier_bps` and `capacity_control_state` are then recomputed and the window's `version` increments. A window with `authorized_seats` of zero deserves special mention: its pre-booking load factor is defined as zero rather than raising an arithmetic error, but every booking against it fails the raw-availability check with `ERR_AWARD_SEATS_UNAVAILABLE` regardless.

A hold is not a redemption. **Ticketing** is what converts miles into a flown award, and it is available to a `Member` (for their own booking) or a `ProgramAdmin` while `status` is `HELD`. Four conditions guard it: the hold must still be live, with `hold_expires_at` strictly after the clock's now, else `ERR_HOLD_EXPIRED`; the member's `security_state` must be `NORMAL`; the member's `mile_balance` must be at least `quoted_miles`, else `ERR_INSUFFICIENT_MILES`; and the programme must not be `LOCKED`. Ticketing then moves `status` `HELD → TICKETED` and touches four rows. A mile ledger row of `txn_type` `REDEEM` is inserted with `total_miles` equal to the negative of `quoted_miles` and `award_booking_id` naming this booking; that row's `activity_date` is the logical clock's calendar date, its `base_miles` echoes `quoted_miles` as a positive magnitude, and its `posted_period` snapshots the programme's `current_period` like any other ledger row. The member's `mile_balance` falls by `quoted_miles` and the programme's `outstanding_miles` falls by the same amount — the liability is discharged. On the window, `seats_held` falls by `seats` while `seats_confirmed` rises by `seats`, after which `load_factor_bps`, `demand_multiplier_bps` and `capacity_control_state` are recomputed; note that this movement leaves total occupancy unchanged, so a ticketing event by itself does not change the posture. Both the booking's `version` and the window's `version` increment.

Three paths release seats instead. **Cancelling** a hold is available to a `Member`, a `ProgramAdmin`, or the fraud security cascade, and requires `status` to be `HELD`; it moves `HELD → CANCELLED`, decreases `seats_held` by `seats`, decreases `saver_seats_consumed` by `seats` when `fare_class` is `SAVER`, and recomputes the window's three derived posture fields. **Expiry** is the epoch sweep's equivalent for a hold whose `hold_expires_at` has passed: it moves `HELD → EXPIRED` with the identical seat release and recompute, and it explicitly leaves `mile_balance` untouched, because a hold never debited any miles in the first place. **Refunding** is the compensating transition for a booking that was already ticketed. Only a `ProgramAdmin` may refund, only while `status` is `TICKETED`, only while `partner_settled` is false, and never while the programme is `LOCKED`. A refund inserts a mile ledger row of `txn_type` `REVERSAL` with `total_miles` equal to positive `quoted_miles`, `award_booking_id` naming this booking and `reverses_transaction_id` naming the `REDEEM` row it undoes — the most recently created such row by `created_at` should more than one exist, and null if none does — with its `activity_date` again the clock's calendar date and its `base_miles` again `quoted_miles`; restores the member's `mile_balance` and the programme's `outstanding_miles` by exactly `quoted_miles`, returning both to their pre-ticket values; decreases the window's `seats_confirmed` by `seats` and, for a `SAVER` booking, `saver_seats_consumed` by `seats`; recomputes the window's posture; and moves `status` `TICKETED → REFUNDED`. `CANCELLED`, `EXPIRED` and `REFUNDED` are all terminal.

Awards flown on a partner's metal create a cash obligation, which is discharged by a **settlement run** requested by a `RevenueAccountant` for one partner airline. The eligible set is the partner's bookings whose `status` is `TICKETED`, whose `partner_settled` is false, and whose `travel_date` is at or before the logical clock's calendar date — the date bound being inclusive, so a booking travelling today is eligible. Eligible bookings are processed strictly in `created_at` ascending order. Before anything is processed, the partner's `reciprocity_mode` is checked: a `SUSPENDED` partner has its whole run rejected with `ERR_RECIPROCITY_SUSPENDED`, no booking is settled, and `net_settlement_balance_cents` does not move. Otherwise a reciprocity factor is selected — 10000 basis points under `FULL`, 10000 under `INBOUND_ONLY`, and 5000 under `OUTBOUND_ONLY`, which halves the partner's take. For each eligible booking in order, an amount equal to that booking's `partner_settlement_cents` multiplied by the factor, divided by 10000, **rounded half up per booking**, is added to `net_settlement_balance_cents`; `settled_booking_count` increases by one; and the booking's `partner_settled` is set true, which makes it permanently ineligible for any later run. Finally `last_settlement_run_at` is stamped with the clock's now and the partner's `version` increments; the run's response body is the updated partner airline row itself rather than any summary of the run. Because rounding is per booking, two bookings of 1121 cents each under `OUTBOUND_ONLY` settle to 561 plus 561, totalling 1122 — not the 1121 that halving the combined 2242 would give. A run that finds no eligible booking succeeds, changes nothing but `last_settlement_run_at`, and is not an error; a `HELD` or `CANCELLED` booking is never eligible however old its travel date; and refunding a ticketed booking before settlement removes it from the eligible set, which is exactly why refund is blocked once `partner_settled` is true.

### 3.6. Elite Status: Qualification, Rollover and Status Match

The qualification cycle is closed explicitly rather than on a calendar. A `ProgramAdmin` closes a member's cycle while `qualification_state` is `ACCUMULATING` (or `RESTATED`, as below), moving it to `CLOSED` and running the tier waterfall.

The waterfall works on integer arithmetic with no rounding anywhere. Effective miles are the member's `qualifying_miles_ytd` plus `rollover_carried_in`. A tier is earned when **either** of two arms holds. The miles arm holds when effective miles are at least the tier's mileage threshold. The segment arm is a conjunction and holds only when `qualifying_segments_ytd` is at least the tier's segment threshold **and** `qualifying_spend_cents_ytd` is at least the tier's spend threshold — segments alone never earn a tier. The thresholds are 25000 miles, or 30 segments together with 300000 cents of spend, for `SILVER`; 75000 miles, or 75 segments with 900000 cents, for `GOLD`; and 150000 miles, or 120 segments with 1500000 cents, for `PLATINUM`. Tiers are evaluated strictly highest first — `PLATINUM`, then `GOLD`, then `SILVER`, then `BASE` — and evaluation stops at the first tier earned; `BASE` is the fallthrough and is always earned. That tier is stamped as `awarded_tier_at_close`. A member sitting exactly on a threshold earns that tier, the thresholds being inclusive.

Rollover then banks only what the awarded tier did not consume, and only from the miles arm; `rollover_carried_in` counts toward the miles arm and never toward segments or spend. Consumption is the mileage threshold of `awarded_tier_at_close`, or zero when the awarded tier is `BASE`. `rollover_carried_out` is the effective miles minus that consumption, floored at zero, and then capped at 40000 miles. Two consequences are worth naming. A member one mile short of `GOLD` is awarded `SILVER` and rolls the entire surplus above 25000 forward, up to the 40000 cap — so a near miss can bank more rollover than a clean qualification. And a member who qualified through the segment arm still computes rollover against the miles arm, so a low-mileage segment qualifier may carry out nothing at all.

Closing the cycle also sets `elite_tier` to `awarded_tier_at_close` — unless `status_match_state` is `TRIAL` or `CONFIRMED` and `status_match_tier` outranks the awarded tier, in which case `elite_tier` is retained at `status_match_tier`. A `ProgramAdmin` may reopen a closed cycle for **restatement**, supplying a `reason`; that moves `qualification_state` `CLOSED → RESTATED`, clears `awarded_tier_at_close` to null and `rollover_carried_out` to zero, and reverts `elite_tier` to `BASE` unless a status match holds a higher tier. Closing again from `RESTATED` re-runs the identical waterfall on the restated ledger and moves `qualification_state` back to `CLOSED`.

**Status match** is the competitive-acquisition lever and runs on its own dimension. A `ProgramAdmin` grants one while `status_match_state` is `NONE`, supplying a `matched_tier` drawn from `SILVER`, `GOLD` and `PLATINUM` and a `trial_days` of at least zero. Two preconditions apply beyond the state check: the member's `security_state` must be `NORMAL`, and `matched_tier` must be strictly higher than the member's current `elite_tier` in the order `BASE`, `SILVER`, `GOLD`, `PLATINUM` — a sideways or downward match is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE`, except that a `matched_tier` of `BASE` is not in the accepted value set at all and is therefore refused earlier, at rung 7, with `ERR_INVALID_REQUEST`. The grant moves `status_match_state` `NONE → TRIAL`, sets both `elite_tier` and `status_match_tier` to `matched_tier`, and sets `status_match_expires_at` to the logical clock's now plus `trial_days` days.

A trial resolves in one of two directions, and a `ProgramAdmin` supplies the `outcome` to choose. An outcome of `CONFIRM` moves `TRIAL → CONFIRMED` and retains `elite_tier` at `status_match_tier` permanently. An outcome of `LAPSE` moves `TRIAL → LAPSED` and reverts `elite_tier` to `awarded_tier_at_close` when that field is not null, and to `BASE` otherwise. The epoch sweep produces the identical `TRIAL → LAPSED` transition, with the identical tier reversion, for any trial whose `status_match_expires_at` is at or before the clock's now.

### 3.7. Fraud Detection, Adjudication and Clawback

Fraud enters the system as a **case**, opened by a `FraudAnalyst` against a `member_id` with a `case_type` of `ACCOUNT_TAKEOVER`, `MILE_BROKERING` or `VELOCITY_ANOMALY`, and four non-negative signal counts: `login_geo_velocity` (impossible-travel login events in the detection sample), `redemption_velocity_24h` (redemptions in the trailing 24 hours of the sample), `brokering_pattern_hits` (third-party-beneficiary patterns matched) and `dormancy_days` (days of dormancy before the burst of activity). The opener's `X-User-ID` is stamped into `opened_by_user_id`, `adjudicated_by_user_id` starts null, `clawback_miles` starts at zero, `freeze_applied` starts false, and `status` starts `OPEN`.

The case is scored the instant it is opened. Each signal is first converted to a 0-to-100 sub-score and clamped at 100 **before** weighting, so that no single signal can dominate the result: the geo-velocity sub-score is `login_geo_velocity` times 10, capped at 100; the redemption sub-score is `redemption_velocity_24h` times 20, capped at 100; the brokering sub-score is `brokering_pattern_hits` times 25, capped at 100; and the dormancy sub-score is `dormancy_days` divided by 4 and rounded down, capped at 100. `detection_score` is then the weighted blend of those four — 3500 basis points on geo velocity, 3000 on redemption velocity, 2500 on brokering pattern and 1000 on dormancy, meaning 35%, 30%, 25% and 10% — with the weighted sum divided by 10000, rounded half up, and capped at 100.

Opening the case applies a security posture to the member immediately, against two inclusive thresholds. A `detection_score` of 70 or more drives `security_state` to `FROZEN` and sets the case's `freeze_applied` true, but only when the member currently sits at `NORMAL` or `WATCH`: a member already `FROZEN` or `CLOSED` is left exactly where it is and `freeze_applied` stays false. A score from 40 through 69 drives `security_state` to `WATCH`, and only from `NORMAL` — it never pulls a `FROZEN` or `CLOSED` member back up. A score below 40 leaves `security_state` where it was. In every case, including the last, the member's `risk_score` is set to the new `detection_score`; `risk_score` is by definition the `detection_score` of the member's most recent case, ordered by `created_at` ascending so the newest wins. Whenever this opening is itself what freezes the member — that is, whenever `freeze_applied` came out true — every one of that member's bookings still in `HELD` transitions `HELD → CANCELLED`, releasing its seats from `seats_held` and, for `SAVER` bookings, from `saver_seats_consumed`, with each affected window's `load_factor_bps`, `demand_multiplier_bps` and `capacity_control_state` recomputed.

A `FraudAnalyst` moves an `OPEN` case to `INVESTIGATING`, and from there adjudicates it in one of two directions. Both directions are governed by segregation of duties: the adjudicating analyst's `X-User-ID` must differ from `opened_by_user_id`, or the request is refused with `ERR_SEGREGATION_OF_DUTIES`. Both stamp `adjudicated_by_user_id`, and both are terminal.

**Dismissal** moves `INVESTIGATING → DISMISSED` with an optional `note`, which — like the counter-offer note in §3.2 — is accepted and validated as a string but persisted nowhere on the case. It returns the member's `security_state` to `NORMAL` from `WATCH` or `FROZEN` and resets `risk_score` to zero — but only when no other case for that member remains in `OPEN`, `INVESTIGATING` or `SUBSTANTIATED`. A member under two live cases is not released by clearing one of them.

**Substantiation** moves `INVESTIGATING → SUBSTANTIATED`, is refused while the programme is `LOCKED`, and is the deepest cascade in the system — five hops, each caused by the one before it. The first hop stamps `adjudicated_by_user_id`, computes `clawback_miles`, and sets the case's `freeze_applied` true whatever it was before — substantiation always records that a freeze was applied, even for a case that opened below the freeze threshold or against a member who was already frozen. The clawback basis is the sum of `total_miles` over the member's ledger rows whose `txn_type` is `FLIGHT_EARN`, `CARD_SPEND_EARN` or `PARTNER_EARN` and whose `activity_date` falls within the 90 days before the logical clock's calendar date, inclusive. From that basis, a `MILE_BROKERING` case claws back the lesser of the member's current `mile_balance` and the full basis; a `VELOCITY_ANOMALY` case claws back the lesser of the member's `mile_balance` and half the basis rounded down; and an `ACCOUNT_TAKEOVER` case claws back nothing at all, because those miles were stolen *from* the member rather than earned by them, and the balance is preserved for reissue. The second hop, caused by the first, applies the outcome: when `clawback_miles` is greater than zero a mile ledger row of `txn_type` `ADJUSTMENT` with `total_miles` equal to the negative of `clawback_miles` is inserted — its `activity_date` being the logical clock's calendar date and its `base_miles` the positive `clawback_miles` magnitude — and the member's `mile_balance` is debited accordingly, and the member's `security_state` moves to `CLOSED` for an `ACCOUNT_TAKEOVER` case and to `FROZEN` for either other case type. The third hop, caused by the second hop's security transition, cancels every one of the member's bookings still in `HELD`, moving each `HELD → CANCELLED` and releasing its seats from `seats_held` and, for `SAVER` bookings, `saver_seats_consumed`. The fourth hop, caused by the third hop's seat release, recomputes `load_factor_bps`, `demand_multiplier_bps` and `capacity_control_state` on each affected window. The fifth hop, caused by the second hop's adjustment insert, reduces the programme's `outstanding_miles` by `clawback_miles`. The case's `version` increments alongside every entity the cascade touched.

Three boundaries are deliberate. A score of exactly 40 sets `WATCH` and a score of exactly 70 sets `FROZEN`, both bounds being inclusive. A low-signal case scoring below 40 still opens and can still be substantiated — substantiation freezes or closes the member regardless of the original score. And because clawback is capped by the member's *current* `mile_balance`, a member who has already redeemed everything is clawed back zero and the `ADJUSTMENT` row is skipped entirely; earn rows older than the 90-day lookback are excluded from the basis even though their miles are still sitting in the balance. `security_state` `CLOSED` is reachable only through substantiating an `ACCOUNT_TAKEOVER` case.

### 3.8. Programme Change Communication and Reaction Risk

Devaluations are the loyalty industry's reputational third rail, so the system forces a change to be drafted, scored for member reaction, and announced with lead time before it can take effect. A `ProgramAdmin` drafts a **programme change notice** under a `program_id` with a `change_type` of `EARN_STRUCTURE`, `AWARD_PRICING`, `ELITE_THRESHOLD` or `EXPIRY_POLICY`, an `effective_at` instant, and `expected_program_version` per §2.8. Two optional grandfathering terms soften the change: `grandfather_until`, the instant grandfathering lapses (null meaning none is offered), and `grandfathered_tier_floor`, the minimum elite tier protected while grandfathering runs, drawn from `BASE`, `SILVER`, `GOLD` and `PLATINUM` and null when no floor is offered. The draft is rejected with `ERR_INVALID_NOTICE_SCHEDULE` unless `effective_at` is strictly in the future on the logical clock and any supplied `grandfather_until` is at or after `effective_at`. A drafted notice starts in `DRAFT` with `announced_at` null, `notice_days`, `exposed_member_count` and `member_reaction_index` all at zero, and `rollback_reason` null.

Announcement is what computes the reaction risk. It requires `status` to be `DRAFT` and `effective_at` still to be in the future, stamps `announced_at` with the clock's now, and computes three derived figures. `notice_days` is the whole number of days from `announced_at` to `effective_at`, taking the floor of the difference. `exposed_member_count` is the number of member accounts in the programme whose `elite_tier` is anything other than `BASE` and whose `security_state` is either `NORMAL` or `WATCH`, snapshotted at announcement. `member_reaction_index` is then a 0-to-100 score assembled from four ingredients.

The first ingredient is severity, taken from `change_type`: 40 points for `EARN_STRUCTURE`, 50 for `AWARD_PRICING`, 30 for `ELITE_THRESHOLD` and 20 for `EXPIRY_POLICY` — repricing awards angers members more than tightening expiry. The second is a lead-time factor keyed by `notice_days` against inclusive lower bounds: 10000 basis points at 180 days or more, 12500 from 90 through 179 days, 15000 from 30 through 89 days, and 20000 below 30 days, so exactly 180, 90 or 30 days each take the kinder factor. The third is grandfathering relief: 30 points when `grandfather_until` is at or after `effective_at` plus 365 days, 15 points when `grandfather_until` is at or after `effective_at` but earlier than that full year (so a `grandfather_until` exactly equal to `effective_at` still earns the partial 15), and 0 points when `grandfather_until` is null. The fourth is exposure: an exposure factor of 10000 basis points plus 100 basis points for each exposed member, with that uplift capped at 5000 basis points — the cap saturating at 50 exposed members, beyond which the fifty-first exposed member does not move the index at all.

Those combine in a fixed order. Severity is multiplied by the lead-time factor, divided by 10000, and rounded half up. Relief points are subtracted from that product and the result is floored at zero — relief can therefore drive the pre-exposure term to zero, in which case the index is zero no matter how many members are exposed. That floored term is then multiplied by the exposure factor, divided by 10000, rounded half up, and finally capped at 100 to give `member_reaction_index`.

The gate follows immediately: announcement is refused with `ERR_REACTION_RISK_EXCEEDED` when `member_reaction_index` is 70 or more **and** `grandfather_until` is null. The threshold is inclusive, so an index of exactly 70 without grandfathering is blocked while the same index with grandfathering attached announces successfully. A notice can therefore always be pushed through by attaching grandfathering — which is the intended incentive, since grandfathering also lowers the index by its relief points. A successful announcement moves `status` `DRAFT → ANNOUNCED`.

From `ANNOUNCED` the epoch sweep carries the notice forward. Once the clock reaches `effective_at`, a notice with a non-null `grandfather_until` still in the future moves `ANNOUNCED → IN_GRANDFATHER`, while a notice whose `grandfather_until` is null or already at or before now moves straight `ANNOUNCED → EFFECTIVE`. A notice already `IN_GRANDFATHER` moves `IN_GRANDFATHER → EFFECTIVE` once `grandfather_until` is at or before the clock's now. At any point from `ANNOUNCED`, `IN_GRANDFATHER` or `EFFECTIVE`, a `ProgramAdmin` may roll the change back with a supplied `reason`, which is stamped into `rollback_reason` and moves `status` to the terminal `ROLLED_BACK`. Rollback carries one cross-entity consequence: when the rolled-back notice's `change_type` is `EARN_STRUCTURE`, every promotion campaign in the same programme whose `status` is `SCHEDULED` and whose `earn_channel` is `CARD_SPEND` is itself cancelled, moving `SCHEDULED → CANCELLED`, because those campaigns were priced against the earn structure that is no longer changing. A campaign cancelled by this cascade has its `version` incremented but its `cancellation_reason` left null — only the explicit cancel endpoint of §3.4 stamps a reason — and `LIVE` campaigns, campaigns on other earn channels and campaigns in other programmes are all left alone.

### 3.9. Closing the Period: Breakage and Deferred Revenue

The accounting cycle is driven by a `RevenueAccountant` supplying a `target_state`, and it is a genuine cycle rather than a one-way street. From `OPEN` the posture may move to `CLOSING`, which is what makes a deferred revenue posting permitted. From `CLOSING` it may move to `LOCKED` — but only once a deferred revenue posting already exists for this programme and its `current_period`, so a period cannot be locked without being posted. From `LOCKED` it returns to `OPEN`, and that transition alone advances `current_period` by exactly one calendar month. A close may also be abandoned: from `CLOSING` the posture may return directly to `OPEN`, leaving `current_period` unchanged. Every one of these four transitions increments the programme's `version` by one, and each requires the posture to be exactly the state the transition leaves from. Those four ordered pairs are the whole of the permitted set: any other combination of current posture and `target_state` — `OPEN → LOCKED`, `LOCKED → CLOSING`, and every no-op such as `OPEN → OPEN` or `LOCKED → LOCKED` alike — is refused with `ERR_INVALID_STATE` (409) and leaves the programme, its `version` and its `current_period` untouched. The lock refused for want of a posting is likewise `ERR_INVALID_STATE` rather than any period-specific error.

A **deferred revenue posting** recognises breakage — the portion of sold miles the programme expects never to have to honour — for exactly one period. A `RevenueAccountant` creates one naming the `program_id`, the `period` being posted as a `YYYY-MM` string, `expected_program_version` per §2.8, and optionally a `method`. When `method` is omitted it defaults to the programme's `breakage_policy_mode`, and the variant actually used is echoed on the row as `method_applied`; the row also carries a `method` field holding the request's own value verbatim, which is therefore `null` exactly when the caller omitted it. The `period` being posted is not constrained to the programme's `current_period` — any well-formed `YYYY-MM` string is accepted, so a programme may post a period it has already advanced past — though only a posting whose `period` equals `current_period` satisfies the lock precondition in the paragraph above. Only one posting may ever exist per `program_id` and `period` pair; a second attempt is rejected with `ERR_PERIOD_ALREADY_POSTED`. The posting is an immutable ledger row, and it is refused while the programme is `LOCKED`.

Three trailing buckets are measured first, over the mile ledger rows belonging to members of the programme whose `activity_date` falls inside the **trailing window**: the 24 calendar months ending on the last day of `period`, inclusive at both ends. `expired_miles_trailing` is the absolute miles expired in that window, computed as the sum of the negated `total_miles` of rows whose `txn_type` is `EXPIRY`. `redeemed_miles_trailing` is likewise the sum of the negated `total_miles` of rows whose `txn_type` is `REDEEM`. `issued_miles_trailing` is the sum of `total_miles` over rows whose `txn_type` is `FLIGHT_EARN`, `CARD_SPEND_EARN` or `PARTNER_EARN`. `REVERSAL` and `ADJUSTMENT` rows are excluded from all three buckets, because they are corrections rather than programme activity — but they do move `outstanding_miles`, and therefore they do affect the snapshot below.

The rate itself is produced by whichever of two variants applies, and on identical data the two generally disagree. The **historical attrition** variant looks backward and asks what share of the miles that left the programme left by expiring rather than by being redeemed: its denominator is `expired_miles_trailing` plus `redeemed_miles_trailing`, and the raw rate is `expired_miles_trailing` multiplied by 10000 and divided by that denominator, rounded half up to whole basis points — or exactly zero when the denominator is zero, in which case the posting is still created with a `breakage_income_cents` of zero. That raw rate is then clamped between a floor of 0 basis points and a ceiling of 3500. The **forward expectation** variant looks forward and asks what share of the miles issued is expected never to be redeemed: its raw rate is 10000 multiplied by the quantity (`issued_miles_trailing` minus `redeemed_miles_trailing`), divided by `issued_miles_trailing`, rounded half up — or the ceiling of 3500 basis points outright when `issued_miles_trailing` is zero, which is a defined answer rather than a division error. That raw rate is clamped between a floor of 200 basis points and the same ceiling of 3500. The forward variant ignores expiries entirely and carries a non-zero floor, which is precisely why the two variants diverge. In both variants the clamp is applied **after** rounding, so a rate that rounds to more than 3500 basis points is clamped to exactly 3500.

A common tail then runs whichever variant produced the rate. `outstanding_miles_snapshot` captures the programme's `outstanding_miles` at posting time and `deferred_balance_before_cents` captures its `deferred_revenue_cents` at the same instant. `breakage_income_cents` is `deferred_balance_before_cents` multiplied by `breakage_rate_bps`, divided by 10000, rounded half up to whole cents — so a `deferred_balance_before_cents` of zero yields zero income regardless of the rate. `deferred_balance_after_cents` is `deferred_balance_before_cents` minus `breakage_income_cents`. The posting then writes back to the programme: `deferred_revenue_cents` becomes `deferred_balance_after_cents`, `last_breakage_rate_bps` becomes this posting's `breakage_rate_bps`, `lifetime_breakage_income_cents` increases by this posting's `breakage_income_cents`, and the programme's `version` increments by one.
